## Problem

Existing deep clustering methods treat all samples equally, causing models to overfit simple, redundant feature patterns in high-density regions while under-learning complex, diverse patterns in low-density regions. This imbalance biases cluster prototypes toward redundant samples and wastes training capacity on already-mastered examples, limiting discriminative power on harder samples.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a plug-in on top of existing deep clustering architectures, consuming unlabeled RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 96×96 for STL-10, 224×224 for ImageNet subsets, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) through a MoCo-v2-pretrained ResNet-34 encoder. It produces cluster assignments via a clustering head whose weights are initialized with density-weighted prototypes, and during training it dynamically prunes the active sample set, reducing the number of samples involved in gradient updates by up to ~50% in later epochs.

## Method

The approach has two components. First, a density-aware clustering head initialization (DACHI) computes per-sample weights as the exponential of the average k-NN distance within each K-Means-assigned cluster, then re-estimates prototypes via weighted averaging so that low-density (complex) samples contribute more and high-density (redundant) samples contribute less. Second, a dynamic sample selection (DSS) strategy evaluates each sample's learning state by measuring prediction consistency (cosine similarity between weak and strong augmented view outputs) and tracking its second-order difference across three consecutive epochs; samples whose consistency is stable (|Δ²S| < ε) and whose pseudo-labels have not changed are temporarily excluded from the training batch, reallocating capacity to unstable, under-learned samples. The entire module is plug-and-play, requiring no architectural changes to the base clustering method.

## Results

The proposed plug-in improves average clustering accuracy by up to 6.1% when integrated with CC (60.8% → 66.9%), 5.3% with TCL (62.7% → 68.0%), 2.3% with SCAN (68.7% → 71.0%), and 1.1% with the state-of-the-art CDC (72.7% → 73.8%) across six benchmarks. On ImageNet-Dogs, CDC+Ours achieves 84.3% ACC, surpassing the previous best (CoNR at 79.4%) by 4.9%. The method also improves training efficiency by ~1.3× by dynamically pruning well-learned samples, with SCAN+Ours retaining only ~60% of samples on average while maintaining or improving accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper integrates its plug-in into four deep clustering baselines (CC, TCL, SCAN, CDC) and compares against nine additional methods (BYOL, NNM, GCC, IDFD, TCC, SPICE, ProPos, SeCu, CoNR). CDC+Ours achieves the highest average ACC (73.8%), surpassing CoNR (71.6%) and ProPos (70.3%), and wins on 5 of 6 datasets; SCAN+Ours achieves the best single-dataset result on ImageNet-10 (97.8% ACC). The largest absolute gains appear on harder datasets: ImageNet-Dogs improves by 4.8% (CDC) and 4.9% (SCAN), while Tiny-ImageNet improves by 5.2% (CC) and 4.9% (TCL). On CIFAR-10, CDC+Ours (94.7%) edges out ProPos (94.3%) and CoNR (93.2%), confirming gains even on the easiest benchmark. The method consistently outperforms all 13 baselines on at least one metric across all six primary datasets, with the most pronounced improvements on medium- and low-density samples (e.g., +2.4% ACC on low-density STL-10 samples vs. CDC).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prototype-based clustering head initialization introduced by CDC (Jia et al., 2025) by replacing uniform averaging with density-weighted aggregation, directly addressing the bias toward high-density regions that CDC's approach inherits. It also adapts the dynamic sample selection paradigm from supervised learning (importance sampling, active learning) to the unsupervised clustering setting, where the absence of ground-truth labels necessitates novel stability criteria based on prediction consistency between augmented views and pseudo-label temporal stability. The method is positioned as a plug-in orthogonal to the underlying clustering architecture, building on the self-labeling and contrastive learning paradigms used by SCAN, CC, TCL, and CDC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in design makes the technique broadly applicable across existing deep clustering methods without architectural modification, and the 1.3× training efficiency gain has practical value for large-scale clustering pipelines. The density-aware initialization and consistency-based sample filtering are simple to implement and add only two hyperparameters (α, ε) that the authors show are not highly sensitive. Open questions include whether the k-NN-based density estimation scales efficiently to very high-dimensional or very large datasets, whether the approach generalizes to settings with highly imbalanced cluster sizes, and whether the prediction-consistency criterion remains reliable when the feature encoder is weakly pre-trained.
