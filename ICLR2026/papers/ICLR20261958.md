## Problem

The cold-start initialization stage in training Multimodal Large Reasoning Models (MLRMs) is critical yet poorly understood. A counter-intuitive phenomenon shows that text-only cold-start outperforms multimodal cold-start for subsequent RL tuning, but the underlying mechanism remains unexplained. This paper identifies that multimodal cold-start fails to increase attention to visual tokens (termed Lazy Attention Localization), leaving models with weak visual grounding that limits reasoning performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261958/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image paired with a text question as input and produces a structured reasoning chain with explicit visual anchors (e.g., 'look back at the triangle', 'check the image again') followed by a final answer in \boxed{} format. Training data is synthesized via a three-stage pipeline: Gemini 2.5-Pro generates high-fidelity visual descriptions, Qwen3-235B-A22B produces reflection-enhanced reasoning chains over those descriptions, and Qwen3-32B inserts explicit visual anchor references throughout. Cold-start training uses 30.6K synthesized samples; the RL stage uses 17.9K public samples with difficulty filtering (accuracy between 0.25–0.75 over 8 rollouts).

## Method

AVAR (Attention-Guided Visual Anchoring and Reflection) reshapes attention allocation during cold-start through three synergistic components. First, Visual-Anchored Reflection Data Synthesis (VARD) embeds visual anchors directly into reasoning chains rather than using caption-then-reason pipelines. Second, Attention-Guided Training Objectives (AGTO) add two auxiliary losses to the standard LM loss: an image-enhancement loss that maximizes attention to visual tokens and a system-suppression loss that minimizes attention to system tokens, applied across all layers. Third, Visual-Anchored Reward Shaping (VARS) introduces a visual attention ratio reward during GRPO-based RL that incentivizes sustained visual grounding beyond answer correctness. Applied to Qwen2.5-VL-7B, the framework raises VAS from 7.5 to 18.9 and achieves a 7.0% average gain across 7 multimodal reasoning benchmarks.

## Results

AVAR-Thinker (built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B) achieves a 56.1% average across 7 multimodal reasoning benchmarks, a +7.0% gain over the base model. The largest improvements are on MathVision (+12.2%, 37.4%) for multi-step geometric reasoning and HallusionBench (+8.8%, 59.5%) for hallucination robustness. On math reasoning, AVAR-Thinker scores 74.7% on MathVista, 50.4% on MathVerse-VO, and 63.8% on MMMU-VAL, establishing a new state of the art among 7B open-source models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AVAR-Thinker outperforms all compared 7B multimodal reasoning models on average. Against the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (49.1%), it gains +7.0% overall, with the most dramatic improvement on MathVision (+12.2%) and HallusionBench (+8.8%). It surpasses ThinkLite-VL (53.1%) by 3.0% and MM-Eureka-7B (51.2%) by 4.9%, and beats models initialized with multimodal cold-start data—R1-OneVision (44.8%) and OpenVLThinker (50.2%)—by 11.3% and 5.9% respectively, underscoring the critical role of attention reshaping. On MathVista, AVAR-Thinker (74.7%) slightly trails ThinkLite-VL (75.1%) but leads on every other benchmark. Compared to Vision-R1 (which omits MathVision due to training overlap), AVAR-Thinker wins on MathVista (+1.2), MathVerse-VO (+2.7), MMMU-VAL (+7.5), MMMU-Pro (+3.3), and HallusionBench (+7.6), while Vision-R1 edges ahead on MMStar (64.8 vs. 64.1). The generalization experiment on Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct confirms consistent gains (+9.5% average), validating that AVAR's benefits are not model-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active threads: (1) the MLRM training pipeline (cold-start SFT followed by RL, as in OVR, Revisual-R1, MiMo-VL, and DeepSeek-R1-style GRPO) and (2) attention-allocation analysis in MLLMs (Yin et al. 2025, Tang et al. 2025, Liu et al. 2025), which identified insufficient visual attention as a bottleneck but addressed it only at inference time. The key departure is shifting the intervention point from inference-time reweighting to the cold-start training stage, arguing that attention allocation can be reshaped during initialization rather than patched at test time. The VAS metric and the 'Lazy Attention Localization' diagnosis reframe the well-known observation that text-only cold-start outperforms multimodal cold-start (Wei et al. 2025a,b; Yue et al. 2025b) from an empirical curiosity into a mechanistic explanation grounded in attention distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first quantitative, attention-level explanation for why multimodal cold-start underperforms text-only cold-start in MLRMs, which has been a practical puzzle for the community. The AVAR framework is modular (data synthesis, loss design, reward shaping) and demonstrated on two base architectures (Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Llama-3.2-11B), suggesting reasonable transferability. However, the VAS–performance correlation rests on only ~11 models, the primary experiments are confined to 7B-scale models, and the 7% average gain, while solid, is achieved on a relatively narrow set of math-heavy benchmarks; whether the attention-reshaping principle generalizes to larger models, non-mathematical multimodal tasks, or longer reasoning chains remains open. The training-free intervention yielding only 1–2% gains also suggests that the training-based components are essential, limiting the framework's applicability to settings where retraining is feasible.
