## Problem

After structured pruning of LLMs, recovery post-training with full instruction tuning datasets is computationally expensive, can introduce negative effects from irrelevant or conflicting samples, and fails to account for the uneven degradation of different model capabilities. Existing data selection methods (IFD, Nuggets, Instruction Mining) are designed for general instruction tuning quality rather than targeting the specific capabilities most severely compromised by pruning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260493/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two model checkpoints (original M_o and pruned M_p), an instruction tuning dataset D of (instruction, response) pairs, a sample-count budget B, and a computational cost budget U. It produces a selected subset S ⊂ D of at most B samples whose total quadratic sequence-length cost does not exceed U. The selection is guided by token-level output probability distributions from both models, making it a model-aware procedure rather than a purely data-driven one.

## Method

PASER operates in three stages. First, S²RIC clusters instructions by capability using SentenceBERT embeddings transformed via a diffusion kernel on the graph Laplacian (with diffusion time chosen by the spectral gap method), followed by NMF-based spectral clustering with adaptive K selection. Second, CDAIS computes a Capability Degradation Score (CDS) per cluster using Jensen-Shannon divergence between the original and pruned models' token-level output distributions, allocates the data budget proportionally to CDS across clusters, and within each cluster selects samples by an Individual Efficiency Score that divides per-sample JSD by the log of quadratic sequence-length computational cost. Third, NTEM maintains a Concept Consistency Graph over extracted semantic concepts to reject samples that introduce conflicting or irrelevant information, with lightweight enhancements including semantic normalization, soft down-weighting of low-confidence conflicts, and an optional NLI reranker for top-risk pairs. The paper provides a time complexity bound of O(N log N + NC²) and a theoretical error bound showing PASER's solution is within (1−βγ)·C of the optimal recovery subset.

## Results

On LLaMA2-7B with Alpaca (52K samples), PASER recovers average reasoning performance to 61.10 under LLM-Pruner (25%), 64.31 under SliceGPT (25%), 62.02 under Wanda (2:4), and 62.78 under SparseGPT (50%), compared to 62.91 for the unpruned model. Notably, under SliceGPT, PASER exceeds the unpruned model's average reasoning score (64.31 vs. 62.91) while using only a fraction of the full dataset. PASER also achieves the lowest zero-shot perplexity (e.g., 12.24 on WikiText2 and 21.53 on PTB under SliceGPT) among all recovery methods, surpassing even the unpruned model (12.62/22.14). Across LLaMA2-13B/70B, LLaMA3-8B, and Baichuan2-7B/13B, PASER consistently outperforms all baselines, reducing reasoning degradation to under 3% for LLaMA2-70B at 50% pruning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PASER consistently outperforms all three general instruction-tuning data selection baselines (Instruction Mining, IFD, and Nuggets) across every pruning scheme and target model. Under LLM-Pruner (25%) on LLaMA2-7B, PASER achieves 61.10 average reasoning vs. 57.65 (IFD) and 58.69 (Nuggets), a gap of 3.45 and 2.41 points respectively. The advantage is most dramatic under SliceGPT, where PASER reaches 64.31 versus 59.39 (Nuggets, the strongest baseline) and 56.39 (IFD), a 4.92-point margin. Full-data training, which uses 100% of the dataset, actually performs worse than PASER in most settings (e.g., 35.64 vs. 61.10 under LLM-Pruner), confirming that indiscriminate use of all instructions introduces harmful conflicting signals. On the larger LaMini dataset (2.58M samples), PASER maintains its lead (63.79 vs. 60.99 for Nuggets under LLM-Pruner), and on mathematical (GSM8K: 49.4 vs. 47.1) and code generation (HumanEval P@1: 14.4 vs. 7.8 for Full Data) tasks, the same pattern holds. PASER achieves these gains while using only 4%–20% of the original post-training data, also consuming the least training time among all methods tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of LLM pruning (LLM-Pruner, SliceGPT, Wanda, SparseGPT) and instruction tuning data selection (IFD, Nuggets, Instruction Mining), but explicitly departs from both lines by formulating data selection specifically for post-pruning capability recovery rather than general instruction tuning quality. It repurposes manifold learning (diffusion kernel + NMF spectral clustering) from general-purpose clustering to identify capability-specific instruction groups, and introduces Jensen-Shannon divergence between original and pruned model outputs as a degradation signal that prior data selection methods do not exploit. The concept consistency graph for filtering conflicting samples is a novel lightweight mechanism not present in prior selection baselines, which focus on quality scoring rather than semantic conflict detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for LLM deployment: reducing recovery training data from 100% to 4–20% while simultaneously improving performance addresses a real cost bottleneck in industrial model compression pipelines. The finding that full-dataset recovery training can be worse than no training at all (negative tuning effects) challenges the default assumption that more data is always better in post-compression fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the capability clustering truly captures distinct functional modules or merely semantic topic groupings, whether the JSD-based degradation signal generalizes to non-structured compression (quantization, distillation) as the authors briefly claim, and whether the relatively simple co-occurrence-based concept consistency graph scales to larger, more heterogeneous instruction corpora where subtle conflicts are harder to detect.
