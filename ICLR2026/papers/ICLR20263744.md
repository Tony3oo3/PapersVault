## Problem

Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of LLMs is efficient but tends to memorize surface patterns, while reinforcement learning generalizes better at much higher computational cost. Dynamic Fine-Tuning (DFT) was proposed as a middle ground via probability-based reweighting, but it exhibits severe instability in knowledge-intensive domains and lacks a principled theoretical explanation for its domain-specific behavior. This paper identifies the root cause—distributional drift in DFT's implicit auxiliary distribution—and proposes a fix that preserves DFT's tighter RL bounds while restoring training stability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes instruction-response pairs (x, y*) from curated datasets (e.g., NuminaMath CoT, MedMCQA, Magicoder-Evol-Instruct-110K) and produces a fine-tuned autoregressive language model that generates text responses. Training operates at the token level, distributing sequence-level importance weights across positions via normalized per-token allocation. A key practical constraint is that the full-parameter ASFT variant requires simultaneously loading both the training model and a frozen reference model (doubling GPU memory from ~39 GB to ~88 GB for a 7B model), though the ASFT-LoRA variant reduces this to ~41 GB by exploiting LoRA's parameter decomposition to compute the KL term with a single model instance.

## Method

The paper first shows within a reward-weighted regression (RWR) framework that DFT's stop-gradient reweighting is mathematically equivalent to choosing an auxiliary distribution q(τ) ∝ π_ref(τ|D⁺)·sg[p_θ(τ)], which provably yields a strictly tighter lower bound on the RL objective than standard SFT whenever Var(p_θ(τ)) > 0. However, this same construction causes progressive distributional drift: as training proceeds, q concentrates on high-probability trajectories, making the bound increasingly loose and importance weights high-variance. ASFT resolves this by augmenting DFT's objective with a forward KL divergence penalty λ·E_s[KL(π_base(·|s) ‖ π_θ(·|s))], which creates a trust region around the reference policy without altering the lower-bound structure. The forward (rather than reverse) KL is chosen for its mode-covering property, preventing the policy from collapsing onto a narrow subset of sequences. An ASFT-LoRA variant further reduces memory overhead to ~13% above standard SFT while retaining 94% of full-parameter ASFT's performance.

## Results

On medical knowledge tasks (LLaMA-2-7B, 10k samples), ASFT achieves 42.03% average accuracy across MedQA, MMLU, and MedMCQA, outperforming SFT (33.37%), DFT (29.19%), and the base model (31.38%) by +8.66, +12.84, and +10.65 points respectively. On mathematical reasoning (Qwen2.5-7B, 100k samples), ASFT reaches 30.50% average across AIME24, Math500, Minerva, OlympiadBench, and AMC23, surpassing DFT (26.04%) by +4.46 points and the base model (12.61%) by +17.89 points. ASFT also achieves the best code generation average (27.0% on HumanEval/HumanEval+/MBPP/MBPP+ with LLaMA-2-7B), exceeding SFT (26.4%) and DFT (19.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DFT (Wu et al., 2025a), ASFT wins decisively on medical knowledge (42.03% vs 29.19% avg at 10k; DFT actually degrades below the base model) and on math reasoning (30.50% vs 26.04% avg at 100k), confirming that DFT's distributional drift undermines performance in knowledge-intensive domains. Against iw-SFT (Qin & Springenberg, 2025), ASFT achieves 42.03% vs 33.04% average on medical benchmarks, a +8.99 point gap, while also outperforming it on code generation (27.0% vs 25.7% avg). Against GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), ASFT substantially outperforms (42.03% vs 32.53% on medical avg) at a fraction of the computational cost (0.648h vs 51.24h training time). Against DAPO, ASFT is slightly below on standalone medical performance (42.03% vs 42.25%), but when used as an RL initialization (ASFT+DAPO), it surpasses standalone DAPO by +1.85 points (44.10% vs 42.25%), demonstrating that KL-anchored fine-tuning provides a superior starting point for subsequent RL optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of reward-weighted regression (RWR) theory and practical LLM fine-tuning, building directly on Dynamic Fine-Tuning (DFT, Wu et al. 2025a) and the importance-weighted SFT framework of Qin & Springenberg (2025). It provides the first rigorous RWR characterization of DFT, showing it corresponds to a specific auxiliary distribution that yields a provably tighter RL lower bound than standard SFT, while simultaneously revealing DFT's critical flaw: uncontrolled distributional drift. The proposed fix—adding forward KL anchoring to the DFT objective—draws on trust-region ideas from TRPO/PPO and proximal supervised fine-tuning (Zhu et al. 2025), but applies them at the auxiliary-distribution level rather than the policy-update level, which is the key conceptual departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ASFT offers a practically attractive middle ground: RL-comparable generalization (within ~0.2 points of DAPO on medical tasks) at SFT-level compute cost, with the added benefit of serving as a superior RL initialization (ASFT+DAPO outperforms SFT+DAPO by +3.86 points). The theoretical framework provides a systematic lens for understanding why reweighting-based SFT variants succeed or fail across domains, which should guide future method design. However, the method's reliance on base-model priors limits its applicability to knowledge-intensive settings requiring large distributional shifts, the KL computation doubles GPU memory (mitigated by the ASFT-LoRA variant at some accuracy cost), and evaluation is confined to three domains—broader validation and domain-adaptive anchoring strategies remain open.
