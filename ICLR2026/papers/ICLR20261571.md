## Problem

Approximate machine unlearning, which finetunes a model to erase the influence of a forget-set, inadvertently creates a privacy attack surface: the parameter difference between the original and unlearned models approximates the forget-set gradient, enabling membership inference and data reconstruction by an adversary with access to both models. The paper identifies two root causes—large per-sample gradient norms on the forget-set and the small parameter displacement inherent to utility-preserving unlearning—and shows that state-of-the-art methods (NGP, SCRUB, etc.) remain vulnerable under both black-box and white-box auditing.

## Input / Output

WARP is a plug-and-play module that takes a pretrained model (ResNet-18 or ViT-B/16), a forget-set (~1% of training data per class), and a retain-set (the remainder), and outputs unlearned model parameters. It requires no training-time per-sample gradients or stored statistics; at each teleportation step it only needs a retain minibatch to compute per-layer null-space projectors via SVD. The module is interleaved with any existing gradient-based unlearning algorithm at configurable intervals (every S steps or when forget-set gradient norm exceeds a threshold), and a safeguard backtracks the teleportation step if retain-set loss drifts beyond a tolerance ε.

## Method

WARP exploits loss-invariant symmetries of deep networks to simultaneously reduce forget-set gradient norms and displace parameters away from the original model, making the parameter difference Δθ harder to invert. The primary instantiation uses retain-null-space projection: for each layer, the retain-set representation matrix is decomposed via thin SVD, and teleportation updates are projected onto the orthogonal complement of the top-k retain subspace, confining motion to directions that preserve retain-set predictions while suppressing forget-set gradient energy. The teleportation loss minimizes the sum of squared forget-set gradient norms minus a dispersion term (β‖θ−θ^org‖²), subject to a retain-loss constraint. An alternative SVD-free instantiation uses change-of-basis rescaling symmetries (Armenta et al., 2023). The paper also provides information-theoretic bounds showing that symmetry-induced noise expands the orbit of equivalent parameters and provably increases expected reconstruction error for white-box attackers.

## Results

Across six unlearning algorithms on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), WARP reduces black-box membership inference AUC by up to 64.4% (NGP: 0.545→0.516) and white-box AUC by up to 92.9% (PGU: 0.659→0.533), while preserving or slightly improving test accuracy (e.g., PGU: 0.804→0.808, BT: 0.816→0.818). On the most-memorized 1% slice, gains are even sharper: SF's AUC drops to near-random (0.518→0.501) and NGP's TPR@1 nearly halves (0.157→0.082). For reconstruction on ImageNet-1K (ResNet-18, NGP), teleportation degrades PSNR from 10.74 to 7.38 dB (−45.5%) and SSIM from 0.12 to 0.08 (−31.6%), rendering recovered images semantically meaningless. Runtime overhead averages +27% with full SVD but drops to near-zero with the FASTWARP low-rank approximation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WARP is evaluated as a plug-in on top of six unlearning baselines: NGP and SCRUB (Kurmanji et al., 2023), SalUn (Fan et al., 2023), PGU (Hoang et al., 2024), BadTeacher (Chundawat et al., 2023), and SRF-ON (Huang et al., 2024). The largest black-box gains appear for NGP (64.4% AUC reduction) and SF (33.3%), while PGU shows the most dramatic white-box improvement (92.9% AUC reduction, 0.659→0.533), suggesting PGU's projection-based updates leave strong gradient-difference signals that teleportation effectively disrupts. BadTeacher, the most vulnerable baseline (AUC 0.725 black-box, 0.938 white-box), still retains elevated risk after WARP (0.661/0.907) but sees consistent 28–49% reductions. For reconstruction, the paper's subspace-filtered attack outperforms the GIFD baseline (Fang et al., 2023) by 29.7% in PSNR (10.74 vs. 8.28 dB) without WARP, but WARP collapses reconstruction quality to near-random (PSNR 7.38 dB), confirming that symmetry-based randomization is more effective than the generative prior alone. Compared to DP-Langevin unlearning (Chien et al., 2024), WARP achieves comparable or better privacy at lower noise levels without requiring DP calibration, though the Langevin comparison is limited to NGP on CIFAR-10 (Appendix M). The only notable accuracy cost is ~1 percentage point for NGP; all other methods maintain or slightly improve test accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes neural network teleportation—originally developed for optimization acceleration (Armenta et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2025)—as a privacy defense for approximate machine unlearning. It builds on prior work identifying that unlearning leaks forgotten data through parameter differences (Hu et al., 2024; Bertran et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2021), but extends those findings from toy models to realistic multi-step methods (NGP, SCRUB, SALUN, PGU, BADTEACHER, SRF-ON) and proposes the first systematic symmetry-based countermeasure. The defense is positioned as complementary to, rather than a replacement for, differential-privacy-based unlearning (Chien et al., 2024b), which requires training-time modifications and certified guarantees that post-hoc unlearning cannot provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that state-of-the-art approximate unlearning methods remain substantially vulnerable to both membership inference and gradient-inversion reconstruction when an adversary has access to pre- and post-unlearning models, and that a lightweight symmetry-preserving transformation can reduce white-box attack advantage by up to 92% while preserving retain-set accuracy. Open questions include whether teleportation scales to LLM-scale architectures (the paper defers this to future work), whether adaptive symmetry-aware attacks can recover the obfuscated signal, and how WARP interacts with other defenses like quantization or re-unlearning. The ~27% runtime overhead of the SVD-based instantiation (mitigated to near-zero by FastWARP) and the narrow corner case where SCRUB's distillation interacts adversely with symmetry moves at very low FPR are practical limitations to monitor.
