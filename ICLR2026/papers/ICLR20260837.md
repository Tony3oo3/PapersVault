## Problem

Prior audio language models treat understanding and text-to-audio generation as separate tasks with divergent paradigms—autoregressive LLMs for understanding and diffusion models for generation—while reasoning in the audio domain remains confined to text-only trajectories serving understanding tasks. No existing model unifies all three capabilities (understanding, generation, and multimodal generative reasoning) within a single architecture, leaving a gap toward the tightly integrated perception-production-reflection loop that characterizes human auditory intelligence.

## Input / Output

For audio input, UALM consumes 16 kHz monophonic waveforms processed by a 25 Hz acoustic encoder with a 30-second sliding window, connected to the LLM via a single-layer MLP adapter. For audio output, the model predicts discrete X-codec tokens at 50 Hz (8 RVQ tokens per frame using the delay pattern), which are decoded to 16 kHz mono and then upscaled to 48 kHz stereo by a dedicated enhancement VAE. Text prompts are processed as standard BPE tokens within the LLM vocabulary—no external text encoder (e.g., T5) is used—making the architecture fully self-contained for both text and audio modalities.

## Method

UALM extends a Qwen2.5-7B decoder-only LLM with an audio encoder-adapter for input and X-codec token prediction with delay pattern for output, trained via a three-stage recipe: (1) a modality alignment stage that freezes the transformer and trains only the MLP adapter and audio embeddings with large batch sizes, (2) full multimodal pre-training with carefully blended data (30M audio generation pairs up-sampled 2×, audio understanding data, and 21M text reasoning samples) using sequence packing, and (3) post-training with interleaved SFT-DPO rounds that instill multimodal reasoning patterns (enrichment, dialogue, self-reflection) via rich captions as intermediate blueprints. Key empirical findings for LM-based generation include the necessity of ~10× more training data than diffusion baselines, classifier-free guidance (λ=3.0) at inference, and a self-adaptation step before DPO to prevent out-of-domain divergence. The enhancement VAE (153M encoder + 489M decoder with SnakeBeta activations) is trained with stereo MR-STFT, log-mel, adversarial, and feature-matching losses to restore perceptual quality from the lossy codec output.

## Results

On SongDescriber, UALM-Gen achieves FD=74.43, CL=0.54, AES=7.36, and OVL=4.07, outperforming the strongest diffusion baseline ETTA (FD=95.66, CL=0.44, AES=6.71, OVL=3.92) and all prior LM-based models. On AudioCaps, UALM-Gen reaches FD=75.14, CL=0.65, AES=5.08, and OVL=3.79, again surpassing ETTA (FD=80.13, CL=0.54, AES=4.51, OVL=3.73). The unified UALM model retains competitive generation quality (FD=83.69 on SongDescriber, FD=65.87 on AudioCaps) while matching state-of-the-art audio understanding (MMAU mean 74.1%, surpassing Audio Flamingo 3's 72.3%) and text reasoning (GSM8K 92.1%, HumanEval 81.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UALM-Gen decisively outperforms all prior LM-based text-to-audio models (MusicGen, AudioGen, MAGNeT) on every metric, with FD dropping from 191–229 to 74.43 on SongDescriber and from 149 to 75.14 on AudioCaps. Against the strongest diffusion baselines, UALM-Gen beats ETTA on FD (74.43 vs. 95.66), CLAP (0.54 vs. 0.44), AES (7.36 vs. 6.71), and OVL (4.07 vs. 3.92) on SongDescriber, and on FD (75.14 vs. 80.13), CLAP (0.65 vs. 0.54), and AES (5.08 vs. 4.51) on AudioCaps, though it trails ETTA slightly on Inception Score (1.87 vs. 2.15 on SongDescriber). The unified UALM model retains most of this advantage while adding audio understanding and text reasoning: it achieves MMAU mean 74.1% (vs. Audio Flamingo 3's 72.3% and Qwen2.5-Omni's 71.0%) but falls short on MMAR (55.2% vs. 58.5% for AF3). On text benchmarks, UALM shows only marginal degradation from its Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct initialization (mean 81.6 vs. 83.6), a clear improvement over prior unified vision models like Liquid-7B (56.0 MMLU) and Chameleon-7B (52.1 MMLU). For reasoning-oriented generation, UALM-Reason improves subjective scores over base UALM across all three paradigms (Enrichment 4.01 vs. 3.77, Dialogue 4.02 vs. 3.92, Self-Reflection 4.04 vs. 3.82).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Encoder-Adapter-LLM architecture from Audio Flamingo 3 and the delay-pattern RVQ tokenization from MusicGen, but departs from the prevailing paradigm by replacing diffusion-based generation with a pure autoregressive LM and removing the external T5 text encoder in favor of native BPE tokens. It builds on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) and classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) — both previously applied to diffusion audio models — and adapts them to the LM setting. The unification of understanding, generation, and reasoning in a single model extends prior unified vision/speech models (Janus, Liquid, OpusLM) to the broader audio domain, while the multimodal generative reasoning (enrichment, dialogue, self-reflection) carves new ground beyond the text-only reasoning trajectories explored in Audio Reasoner and SoundMind.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes that a single autoregressive LM can simultaneously match frontier diffusion models on text-to-audio generation, state-of-the-art models on audio understanding, and retain strong text reasoning — a unification that was previously thought to require separate specialized architectures. The key practical finding that LM-based audio generation requires roughly an order of magnitude more training data than diffusion (30M vs ~1-2M samples) provides important guidance for future resource allocation. Open questions remain around unified audio representations (currently using continuous encoder for input and discrete codec for output), scalable quality assessment of synthetic captions, and developing audio-specific evaluation metrics adequate for complex generative reasoning chains.
