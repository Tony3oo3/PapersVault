## Problem

Concept erasure in diffusion models faces a persistent robustness-retention trade-off: methods that harden erased concepts against adversarial prompt attacks (APAs) degrade generation quality for unrelated concepts, and vice versa. The paper identifies the root cause as an inappropriately chosen erasure target—mapping a single instance prompt to a fixed safe target leaves class-level remnants near the semantic center of the concept cluster, which APAs can exploit to reactivate the erased concept.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261759/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

AEGIS requires only the erased concept prompt (e.g., 'nudity') and the original U-Net noise predictor; no auxiliary retention dataset is needed. The AET is a learnable prompt embedding with a single learnable dimension (m'=1) initialized randomly, and the adversarial prompt c* is also auto-generated per epoch. The output is a fine-tuned U-Net that suppresses the target concept while preserving generation quality on all other concepts, evaluated via ASR under three APAs (P4D, UnlearnDiffAtk, Ring-A-Bell) and FID/CLIP on held-out prompts.

## Method

AEGIS introduces two components. First, the Adversarial Erasure Target (AET) is an optimizable prompt embedding updated via sign-based single-step gradients to maximize the predicted-noise distance from both the fine-tuned model's output on the erased concept and the original model's output on the erased concept, effectively approximating the semantic center of the concept class and pushing the erasure direction to cover the full cluster rather than a single instance. Second, Gradient Regularization Projection (GRP) replaces data-dependent retention losses with a parameter L2 penalty (θ vs θ₀) and a Directional Gradient Rectification step: when the erasure gradient g_e and retention gradient g_r have negative cosine similarity, the retention gradient is orthogonally projected to remove its component opposing g_e, scaled by a dynamically updated weight ω that starts at 0 (prioritizing erasure) and grows as conflict emerges. Theoretical analysis (Thms. 4.1–4.2) certifies that GRP preserves local descent on the erasing loss and improves retention under curvature conditions.

## Results

On the nudity concept (SD v1.4), AEGIS achieves ASR of 12.06% (P4D), 8.45% (UnlearnDiffAtk), and 3.52% (Ring-A-Bell), substantially outperforming the prior best SalUn (45.77%, 14.08%, 7.04%) and AdvUnlearn (19.86%, 11.27%, 7.04%). AEGIS also maintains strong retention with FID 17.43 and CLIP 0.303, compared to AdvUnlearn's severely degraded FID of 33.62. On Van Gogh style erasure, AEGIS reduces ASR to 36%/12%/10% across the three attacks, and on Church object erasure achieves 28%/6%/8%, while keeping FID within ~2.5 of the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AEGIS outperforms all 12 baselines on at least two of the three ASR metrics for nudity erasure. Against the closest robustness competitor SalUn, AEGIS reduces P4D ASR from 45.77% to 12.06% (−33.71 pp) and UnlearnDiffAtk ASR from 14.08% to 8.45% (−5.63 pp), while also improving FID (17.43 vs 18.27) and CLIP (0.303 vs 0.286). Against AdvUnlearn, AEGIS achieves comparable or better ASR (12.06% vs 19.86% on P4D) but dramatically better retention (FID 17.43 vs 33.62), showing that GRP's data-free parameter regularization avoids the severe quality collapse of data-based retention. On Van Gogh style, AEGIS beats STEREO (the prior best) by 12 pp on P4D and 18 pp on UnlearnDiffAtk while improving FID from 20.42 to 17.25. On Church object, AEGIS ties SH on UnlearnDiffAtk ASR (6%) but improves FID from 68.02 to 19.06, and beats SH on P4D (28% vs 32%). Attention-based methods (FMN, UCE, AC, SPM) and output-based methods (ESD, AGE, RECE, RECELER) all show ASR above 50% on at least one attack, confirming that AEGIS's AET + GRP combination addresses a fundamental limitation of fixed-target erasure.

## Relation to Prior Work

AEGIS extends the ESD (Gandikota et al., 2023) output-based erasure paradigm by replacing its fixed, handpicked mapping target with an Adversarial Erasure Target (AET) that is iteratively optimized to approximate the semantic center of the erased concept class, drawing on fast adversarial training strategies (Jia et al., 2022) for efficient single-step updates. It also builds on AdvUnlearn (Zhang et al., 2024b), which introduced adversarial prompts into the erasure objective, but departs from it by (i) targeting the concept-class center rather than a single instance and (ii) replacing dataset-dependent retention losses with a data-free Gradient Regularization Projection (GRP) that combines parameter regularization with conflict-aware directional gradient rectification. The key conceptual departure is the diagnosis that incomplete erasure stems from the target lying too far from the semantic center of the concept cluster, leaving class-level remnants exploitable by adversarial prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AEGIS addresses the long-standing robustness-retention trade-off in concept erasure without requiring auxiliary retention datasets, which is practically significant given that retention quality is highly sensitive to dataset selection and small-scale datasets cannot cover all preserved concepts. The data-free GRP mechanism also eliminates the risk of inadvertently relearning erased concepts through retention data. However, the approach is validated only on Stable Diffusion v1.4 and v2.1 with U-Net architectures, and performance degrades on v2.1 due to more entangled concept representations from different text encoders and training data. Open questions include whether the AET+GRP framework generalizes to newer architectures (e.g., DiT-based models like SD3 or FLUX), whether the convexity assumptions in the theoretical analysis hold in practice for non-convex U-Net fine-tuning, and whether the single-step AET generation remains effective for highly polysemous concepts with large semantic spread.
