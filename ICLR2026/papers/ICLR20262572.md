## Problem

Machine unlearning methods remain fragile to 'benign relearning,' where forgotten content reemerges after fine-tuning on seemingly unrelated data. The prevailing explanation attributes this to topical relevance between the relearn and target sets, but the paper demonstrates this account is confounded by dataset-size and evaluation-step artifacts, and that syntactic similarity—surface structural overlap rather than shared entities—is the more consistent and primary driver of recovery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262572/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a fine-tuned LLM (Llama-2-7b-chat, Llama-3-8B, or Phi-1.5B) along with a forget set of QA pairs (e.g., 4,000 synthetic author-biography pairs from TOFU) and a retain set, and produces an unlearned model plus a diversified forget set in which each target query is paraphrased into multiple syntactically heterogeneous variants via GPT-4o (or Llama-3-8B). Relearn sets are constructed in two controlled forms: topically relevant (same entities, different question types) and syntactically similar (same surface template, different entities), with similarity quantified by normalized Levenshtein distance at the sentence level. Evaluation uses a keyword-based Relearn Success Rate (exact full-name match), ROUGE-L against the base model, and LLM-as-judge scoring, all reported across unlearning and relearning steps.

## Method

The paper's core insight is that unlearning on syntactically homogeneous forget sets disproportionately suppresses template tokens (repeated phrasing like 'The full name of the author born in...') rather than keyword tokens (the actual names to forget), as evidenced by a steadily rising template-to-keyword loss ratio during training. This template-dominant suppression creates a structural pathway: fine-tuning on syntactically similar data quickly restores the suppressed query-answer patterns, lowering loss and allowing forgotten keywords to reemerge even without topical overlap. To break this rigidity, the paper proposes syntactic diversification: before unlearning, each target query is paraphrased by GPT-4o into multiple distinct surface forms (varying word order, phrasing, and structure) while preserving semantics, and variants with high mutual syntactic similarity are filtered out. The diversified forget set is then used for standard unlearning (GA, NPO, or SCRUB), which forces balanced suppression of both templates and keywords, consistently suppresses benign relearning, accelerates forgetting, and improves model utility on retain and world-knowledge sets.

## Results

On the TOFU benchmark, syntactically similar relearn sets (D_relearn^syntactic, Levenshtein similarity 0.4513) consistently trigger far stronger recovery than topically relevant sets (D_relearn^topic, similarity 0.2349): at 50 unlearning steps and 47 relearning steps, the syntactic set achieves 0.7 keyword-based Relearn Success Rate and 0.7254 cosine similarity, versus 0.0 and 0.0251 for the topical set. Syntactic diversification (D'_forget) reduces the syntactic similarity between the forget set and the syntactic relearn set from 0.4513 to 0.2241, eliminating relearning entirely (0% recovery at all steps) while improving model utility: Retain-set average score rises from 0.1607 to 0.3128, and Real Authors average from 0.4014 to 0.4852.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates three parameter-optimization unlearning methods—Gradient Ascent (GA, Jang et al. 2023), Negative Preference Optimization (NPO, Zhang et al. 2024), and SCRUB (Kurmanji et al. 2023)—as the base unlearning algorithms onto which syntactic diversification is applied. Across all three, the core finding is consistent: syntactically similar relearn data recovers forgotten content far more effectively than topically relevant data, with SCRUB being the most vulnerable (full recovery under syntactic relearning) despite suppressing keywords earliest during unlearning. The proposed diversification strategy is applied on top of these methods and eliminates relearning under all three, with the effect most dramatic under GA where the standard D_forget shows rapid keyword recovery but D'_forget shows zero recovery even after 50 unlearning steps. In Appendix E, safety training methods (DPO, IDK) are compared against unlearning methods under syntactic relearning: DPO and IDK suppress target keywords earlier but exhibit near-complete recovery within a few relearning steps, whereas GA and NPO maintain substantially lower relearn success rates, confirming that unlearning methods are more robust than safety training against syntactic relearning attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly challenges the BLUR benchmark (Hu et al., 2025b), which established topical relevance as the dominant driver of benign relearning in unlearned LLMs. By identifying confounds in BLUR's evaluation protocol (unequal dataset sizes, non-monotonic recovery trajectories), the authors show that syntactic similarity—measured via Levenshtein distance, template mining, and parse-tree overlap—better explains recovery across WMDP, WHP, and RWKU. The mechanistic analysis (representation/gradient alignment, template-vs-keyword loss ratio) builds on the unlearning methods of GA, NPO, and SCRUB, while the proposed remedy (syntactic diversification via GPT-4o paraphrasing) repurposes standard data augmentation ideas from the broader fine-tuning literature into the unlearning context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that surface syntactic structure, not topical content, is the primary pathway for forgotten knowledge to resurface has immediate deployment implications: fine-tuning service providers that filter only for topical overlap will miss syntactically similar but semantically benign requests that can restore unlearned content. The simple remedy—paraphrasing the forget set before unlearning—reduces the forget-efficacy/utility trade-off and is model-agnostic (validated on Llama-2, Llama-3, Zephyr, and Phi-1.5B). Open questions include whether the effect generalizes beyond QA-style unlearning to long-form or multi-turn settings, whether more principled structural diversification (beyond LLM paraphrasing) can further improve robustness, and how this interacts with parameter-efficient unlearning (LoRA) at scale.
