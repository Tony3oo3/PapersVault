## Problem

Clustering accuracy in single-cell RNA-seq data is undermined by measurement noise and biological variability, which cause distinct cell types to project close together in standard latent spaces like PCA. Existing denoising approaches either impose restrictive parametric generative assumptions (VAEs, empirical Bayes with Gaussian noise models) or operate purely in compressed latent spaces where the full geometric structure needed to separate cell populations is lost. The paper addresses the gap of performing principled, likelihood-free denoising that preserves the high-dimensional relational structure essential for accurate clustering.

## Input / Output

The method takes log1p-transformed gene expression profiles (after standard QC and normalization) as input, along with a factor loading matrix V estimated via SVD from a high-quality reference dataset and a diffusion model trained on the reference's PCA-projected latent embeddings. It produces k-dimensional denoised embeddings for each query cell (k chosen via the elbow of the singular-value spectrum, e.g., 15–25 in practice), and by running the Gibbs sampler multiple times, yields confidence sets that quantify uncertainty in each cell's cluster assignment. The critical design constraint is that the likelihood step operates in the original d-dimensional observation space (e.g., 2000–3000 genes) while the prior step operates in the k-dimensional latent space.

## Method

DICE (Diffusion Induced Cell Embeddings) introduces a latent plug-and-play diffusion framework that separates the observation and denoising spaces through a split Gibbs sampler with an auxiliary variable Z. During training, a factor loading matrix V is estimated via SVD on a high-quality reference dataset, and a TabularDiffusionMLP (residual MLP with sinusoidal time embeddings) is trained to learn the prior over k-dimensional latent embeddings. At inference, the Gibbs sampler alternates between a likelihood alignment step that reintroduces noise in the original d-dimensional gene expression space (admitting a closed-form Gaussian update under standard noise assumptions) and a prior alignment step that applies the reverse diffusion update in the k-dimensional latent space. The tunable parameter ρ controls the coupling strength between the two steps, balancing data fidelity against prior guidance, and an annealing schedule over T Gibbs iterations progressively tightens this coupling. This 'input-space steering'—reintroducing noise in the full observation space rather than the compressed latent space—is the key mechanism that prevents the latent-space collapsing problem inherent to PCA-based methods and preserves the geometric relationships needed for biologically meaningful cluster separation.

## Results

On the CITE-seq dataset (1,000 held-out PBMCs, ~30 immune subtypes), DICE achieves an ARI of 0.805, NMI of 0.740, and V-measure of 0.813, outperforming PCA (ARI 0.745) and all denoising baselines. On the human fetal neocortex dataset (cross-dataset transfer from Nowakowski to Polioudakis), DICE reaches ARI 0.393, NMI 0.553, and V-measure 0.559, again leading all competitors. On synthetic data across four noise/distribution-shift configurations, DICE consistently improves silhouette score over PCA (e.g., 0.37 vs. 0.25 in the matched setting) and reduces cLISI (1.17 vs. 1.27), demonstrating robustness to signal-strength shifts, heavy-tailed noise, and latent-prior mismatches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DICE outperforms all five denoising baselines (MAGIC, ALRA, kNN smoothing, NMF, scVI) on ARI, NMI, and V-measure across both real datasets. The largest margins are against NMF (ARI 0.805 vs. 0.448 on CITE-seq; 0.393 vs. 0.209 on Neo-Cortex) and kNN smoothing (ARI 0.805 vs. 0.713–0.735 on CITE-seq; 0.393 vs. 0.268–0.285 on Neo-Cortex). Against the strongest baseline, PCA, DICE gains +0.060 ARI and +0.051 NMI on CITE-seq, and +0.046 ARI and +0.057 NMI on Neo-Cortex. On cLISI (lower is better), DICE is marginally worse than PCA on CITE-seq (1.344 vs. 1.334) but clearly better on Neo-Cortex (1.406 vs. 1.496), suggesting the advantage is most pronounced under cross-dataset distribution shift. ALRA is the closest competitor on NMI for CITE-seq (0.713 vs. 0.740) but trails DICE by a wider margin on Neo-Cortex (0.474 vs. 0.553). scVI is only evaluated on CITE-seq (raw counts unavailable for Neo-Cortex) and underperforms DICE on all four metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the plug-and-play (PnP) diffusion framework from image restoration (Xu & Chi, 2024; Vono et al., 2019) to single-cell genomics, introducing a critical architectural departure: the likelihood step operates in the original high-dimensional observation space rather than the latent space, preventing the cell-type collapse that plagues PCA-based denoising. It builds on the DDPM reverse-diffusion update (Ho et al., 2020) as the prior step within a split Gibbs sampler, but replaces the generic image denoiser with a TabularDiffusionMLP trained on low-rank PCA projections of a reference dataset. The approach diverges from VAE-based single-cell methods (scVI, scVAE) by avoiding restrictive parametric likelihoods, and from approximate message passing methods (Zhong et al., 2022) by operating without explicit noise models, instead learning the prior directly from data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DICE provides the first principled uncertainty quantification for single-cell cluster assignments via confidence sets, which is critical for clinical annotation reliability. The transfer-learning capability—training on high-signal reference data (e.g., SMART-seq2) to denoise noisier droplet-based data—addresses a practical pain point in multi-lab atlas construction. However, the method is constrained by a linear low-rank factor model and i.i.d. noise assumption, limiting applicability to nonlinear biological manifolds and correlated noise structures. Open questions include extending to multimodal (RNA + protein + spatial) data, improving Gibbs sampling efficiency for larger datasets, and validating on clinically meaningful downstream tasks beyond clustering metrics.
