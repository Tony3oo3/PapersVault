## Problem

Unsupervised RL agents that learn from self-imposed goals have typically been evaluated on fixed or known goal distributions, with goal difficulty assessed by immediate performance rather than post-adaptation success. This misaligns pre-training with the realistic setting where downstream tasks are unknown, partially observable, and require multi-episode adaptation before the agent can solve them. The paper asks how to generate, select, and learn from self-imposed goals so that the resulting policy generalizes to novel objectives, dynamics, and map structures under limited adaptation budgets.

## Input / Output

The pre-trained policy is unconditioned (not goal-conditioned) and consumes 5×5 symbolic grid observations (unoccluded by walls) concatenated with a per-step reset flag, previous action, and previous reward, processed by a Gated Transformer-XL (2 blocks, hidden 192, 4 heads, 128-step cached context) to emit one of 6 discrete primitive actions. During pre-training, the goal-generation subsystem and goal-conditioned reward function receive privileged access to full environment state, while the policy itself operates only on observations. The output is a single deployable policy that can be evaluated zero-shot, fine-tuned on fixed tasks, or used as initialization for supervised meta-learning.

## Method

ULEE meta-learns an in-context policy over multi-episode lifetimes (H episodes per environment) using a black-box RL²-style objective, so the agent explicitly optimizes for efficient adaptation rather than single-episode performance. A Goal-search Policy is trained adversarially to visit states whose mapped goals are difficult for the current policy, with rewards equal to predicted post-adaptation difficulty (1 − success rate over the last K episodes, not immediate success). A Difficulty Predictor MLP estimates this post-adaptation difficulty for unseen goals via supervised L2 regression on a rolling buffer of recent empirical estimates, enabling goal selection without additional environment rollouts. Goals are then sampled uniformly from candidates whose predicted difficulty falls within a bounded range [0.1, 0.9], maintaining the curriculum at the frontier of the agent's capabilities and avoiding both trivially easy and unsolvable extremes. The key distinction from prior adversarial goal-generation work (e.g., GoalGAN, ASP) is that difficulty is measured after an adaptation budget, directly matching the evaluation regime where the policy must face novel tasks it has not previously encountered.

## Results

On XLand-MiniGrid benchmarks, ULEE's pre-trained policy reaches more than twice as many evaluation goals at the 20-episode mark compared to DIAYN and random baselines across all three benchmarks (4Rooms-Trivial, 4Rooms-Small, 6Rooms-Small). In few-shot adaptation over 30-episode lifetimes, ULEE achieves up to a 3× increase in mean return by the 30th episode, while DIAYN shows only a single discrete jump. On classical MiniGrid tasks (Table 1), ULEE (f_counts) attains non-zero return on all 14 environments, with standout results such as 0.43 on BlockedUnlockPickUp (vs. 0.02 for DIAYN) and 0.75 on Unlock (vs. 0.03 for random).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ULEE consistently outperforms DIAYN (Eysenbach et al., 2018) across all evaluation axes: exploration (2× more goals reached at 20 episodes), few-shot adaptation (steady improvement vs. DIAYN's single discrete jump), fine-tuning on fixed tasks (higher mean and percentile returns up to 1B steps), and MiniGrid generalization (non-zero return on all 14 tasks vs. DIAYN's near-zero on most). Against PPO and RND trained from scratch on fixed extrinsic tasks, ULEE's pre-trained initialization yields substantially higher returns at all budget levels up to 1 billion steps, with DIAYN offering only a brief initial advantage before stagnating. In the supervised meta-learning fine-tuning setting, ULEE initialization outperforms RL² trained from scratch on mean, 40th, and 20th percentile returns throughout up to 5 billion steps of meta-learning. The one area where ULEE (f_counts) does not dominate is on simple Empty and EmptyRandom MiniGrid tasks, where DIAYN (f_grid) achieves slightly higher returns (e.g., 0.72 vs. 0.64 on Empty-8x8), suggesting the goal-mapping choice trades off generalization to structural novelty against performance on simpler spatial tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

ULEE sits at the intersection of three research threads: adversarial goal generation for curriculum learning (GoalGAN, ASP, AMIGo), unsupervised meta-learning (Gupta et al. 2018, Jabri et al. 2019), and in-context meta-RL (RL²). Its key departure from prior work is the post-adaptation difficulty metric: whereas GoalGAN and related methods select goals based on the agent's immediate success rate, ULEE measures difficulty after a multi-episode adaptation budget, aligning the curriculum with the evaluation regime where the policy must adapt to novel tasks. The method also trains an unconditioned (rather than goal-conditioned) in-context policy, making it directly deployable when goals are unknown or unencodable at test time, in contrast to hierarchical approaches that require a high-level controller.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ULEE provides a concrete recipe for what an unsupervised RL agent should seek and learn from, addressing a core open question in the field. The consistent outperformance over DIAYN, PPO-from-scratch, RND, and RL² across exploration, few-shot adaptation, fixed-task fine-tuning, and meta-learning fine-tuning suggests that post-adaptation difficulty is a meaningfully better curriculum signal than immediate performance. However, all experiments are confined to symbolic 13×13 grid worlds with 5×5 observations; generalization to visual, continuous, or higher-dimensional domains remains untested. The paper itself notes that 60% of the hardest out-of-distribution tasks yield zero return even after adaptation, and suggests hierarchical policies and VLM-based goal specification as natural next steps.
