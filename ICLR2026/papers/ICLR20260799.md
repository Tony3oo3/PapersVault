## Problem

Existing GraphRAG methods for textual graph question answering operate exclusively on low-dimensional structures—treating nodes as 0-cells and edges/paths as 1-cells—while overlooking cycles (2-cells) that encode closed-loop relational dependencies. This limitation prevents effective reasoning over queries that require cyclic inference among multiple entities, such as spatial loops or material consistency constraints, resulting in incomplete contextual grounding and restricted reasoning capability.

## Input / Output

The method takes a textual graph where each node and edge carries free-text attributes, along with a natural language query, and produces a natural language answer. The textual graph is first lifted into a regular cell complex (0-cells, 1-cells, and 2-cells), and the LLM (Llama-2-7B) generates the answer autoregressively conditioned on both a projected subcomplex embedding (soft prompt) and a textualized rendering of the retrieved subcomplex that preserves structural hierarchy. Maximum input length is 512 tokens with up to 32 generated tokens.

## Method

TopoRAG lifts textual graphs into regular cell complexes by fixing a spanning tree, identifying fundamental cycles from non-tree edges, and attaching 2-cells via boundary maps, thereby explicitly encoding higher-dimensional topological structure. A topology-aware subcomplex retrieval module generalizes the Prize-Collecting Steiner Tree formulation to multi-dimensional cell complexes, assigning descending prizes to top-k 0/1-cells by query similarity and computing 2-cell prizes inductively from boundary cells minus a size penalty, then solving a near-linear-time approximation to extract a connected subcomplex. A two-stage multi-dimensional message-passing mechanism propagates information along the 1-skeleton (faces and cofaces) over L hops, then exchanges messages across all dimensions via shared cofaces, producing a pooled subcomplex embedding. This embedding is projected into the LLM's hidden space via an MLP to serve as a soft prompt, while the textualized subcomplex is concatenated with the query as token embeddings; only the MLP and subcomplex encoder parameters are trained (or LoRA adapters on the LLM), keeping the backbone frozen.

## Results

TopoRAG achieves the best performance across all three datasets in both frozen and tuned LLM settings. In the frozen LLM setting, TopoRAG reaches 88.99% Accuracy on ExplaGraphs, 83.62% on SceneGraphs, and 87.10 Hit on WebQSP. In the tuned (LoRA) setting, TopoRAG w/LoRA improves to 91.51% on ExplaGraphs, 87.68% on SceneGraphs, and 90.66 Hit on WebQSP. The paper reports improvements of up to 5.12% (ExplaGraphs), 0.98% (SceneGraphs), and 4.67% (WebQSP) over the strongest baselines, with the largest WebQSP gain (16.87%) observed in the tuned setting over G-Retriever w/LoRA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopoRAG outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and setting. Among inference-only methods, KAPING (52.64 Hit on WebQSP) and Zero-CoT (51.30) are the strongest, yet TopoRAG exceeds them by over 34 points on WebQSP, demonstrating the critical role of structured retrieval. In the frozen LLM setting, SubgraphRAG is the closest competitor (86.61 Hit on WebQSP, 0.8535 on ExplaGraphs), but TopoRAG surpasses it by 0.49 Hit and 3.64% Accuracy respectively; G-Retriever trails further behind at 70.49 Hit on WebQSP. In the tuned LLM setting, G-Retriever w/LoRA (73.79 Hit) and GNN-RAG (85.70 Hit) are the strongest baselines on WebQSP, with TopoRAG w/LoRA achieving 90.66 Hit—a 16.87-point margin over G-Retriever w/LoRA. On SceneGraphs, G-Retriever w/LoRA (0.8683) is the nearest tuned baseline, and TopoRAG w/LoRA edges ahead by 0.85%. GraphToken and plain LoRA underperform substantially on SceneGraphs and WebQSP, confirming that topology-aware retrieval and multi-dimensional reasoning provide consistent gains over generic prompt or fine-tuning strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

TopoRAG extends the GraphRAG lineage (G-Retriever, GNN-RAG, SubgraphRAG) by lifting textual graphs into regular cell complexes, explicitly encoding 2-cells (fundamental cycles) that prior methods treat as implicit or ignore entirely. The retrieval mechanism generalizes the Prize-Collecting Steiner Tree formulation used by G-Retriever to a higher-dimensional PCST over cell complexes with multi-dimensional prizes and boundary-consistency constraints. The reasoning module draws on topological deep learning (CW networks, simplicial message passing) but adapts it to a retrieval-augmented LLM setting rather than static graph classification, coupling cellular message passing with soft-prompt-guided generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled topological justification for why cycles matter in graph QA—closed-loop relational dependencies (spatial consistency, material loops) cannot be resolved by node-edge retrieval alone—and demonstrates this empirically across three datasets. Limitations include the restriction to 2-cells (no 3+ dimensional structures), reliance on a single LLM backbone (Llama-2-7B), and moderate gains over strong baselines (≤5% absolute), suggesting the topological signal may be more impactful on datasets with denser cyclic structure. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional complexes, larger LLMs, and whether the spanning-tree-dependent cycle basis introduces task-specific biases that the homology invariance argument does not fully address in practice.
