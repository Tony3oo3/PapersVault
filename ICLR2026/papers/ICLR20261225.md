## Problem

Diffusion Transformers still rely on the original SD-VAE, whose convolutional backbone, aggressive channel compression, and reconstruction-only training produce low-capacity latents that lack global semantic structure. Two long-standing assumptions further block progress: that semantic encoders cannot faithfully reconstruct images, and that diffusion models perform poorly in high-dimensional latent spaces. This paper challenges both assumptions and shows that frozen representation encoders can serve as superior autoencoders for generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261225/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label (ImageNet-1K) as conditioning and generates 256×256 or 512×512 RGB images. Unlike VAE-based DiTs that operate on 4-channel 32×32 latents, the diffusion process runs on 256 tokens of 768 dimensions (from DINOv2-B with patch size 1), keeping the sequence length identical to prior VAE-based DiTs so that backbone FLOPs are unchanged. For 512×512 synthesis, the same 256 tokens are decoded with an upsampling decoder (p_d = 2·p_e) rather than quadrupling the token count, enabling reuse of 256-resolution diffusion checkpoints.

## Method

The paper introduces Representation Autoencoders (RAEs), which pair a frozen pretrained representation encoder (DINOv2, SigLIP2, or MAE) with a trained ViT decoder using L1+LPIPS+GAN losses, eliminating compression and quantization entirely. To make diffusion transformers work in these high-dimensional latents, three adaptations are required: (1) the DiT width must match or exceed the token dimension, justified by a theoretical lower bound showing that width-constrained models cannot capture the full-rank noise-injected distribution; (2) a dimension-dependent noise schedule shift generalizing prior resolution-based strategies to effective data dimension; and (3) noise-augmented decoder training that smooths the discrete latent distribution to handle imperfect diffusion outputs. On top of this, DiT^DH attaches a lightweight, wide (2048-dim), shallow (2-layer) DDT head to the standard DiT backbone, increasing effective denoising width without quadratic FLOP growth, achieving 1.51 FID at 256×256 without guidance and 1.13 with AutoGuidance at both 256×256 and 512×512.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, DiT^DH-XL with DINOv2-B (RAE) achieves a gFID of 1.51 without guidance and 1.13 with AutoGuidance after 800 training epochs, outperforming all prior diffusion and autoregressive methods by a large margin. At 512×512 with 400-epoch training, the same model reaches a gFID of 1.13 with guidance, surpassing the previous best (EDM2 at 1.25). The method also demonstrates substantially faster convergence: at just 80 epochs it already achieves gFID 2.16, surpassing REPA-XL and SiT-XL at their full 800-epoch budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiT^DH-XL with RAE (DINOv2-B) achieves the best gFID among all 21 compared baselines on both 256×256 and 512×512 ImageNet. Without guidance at 256×256, it reaches 1.51 FID, beating the closest competitor REPA-E (1.70) by 0.19 and VAR (1.92) by 0.41. With guidance at 256×256, it achieves 1.13 FID, outperforming xAR (1.24), DDT (1.26), REPA (1.29), and REPA-E (1.15). At 512×512 with guidance, it reaches 1.13 FID, surpassing EDM2 (1.25), DDT (1.28), and SiD2 (1.50). The convergence advantage is particularly striking: at 80 epochs, DiT^DH-XL already achieves 2.16 FID, surpassing REPA-XL (5.78) and SiT-XL (8.61) at their full 800-epoch training, representing a 16× and 47× training speedup respectively. Notably, the method uses only 839M parameters (vs. 675M for most VAE-based baselines) due to the wide DDT head, yet still outperforms them decisively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DiT/LightningDiT architecture and the flow-matching training objective, but fundamentally rethinks the autoencoder that defines the latent space. Rather than the standard SD-VAE (a convolutional, reconstruction-trained, heavily compressed encoder), it replaces the encoder with frozen pretrained representation models (DINOv2, SigLIP2, MAE) paired with a lightweight ViT decoder, forming a Representation Autoencoder (RAE). The DiT^DH architecture draws inspiration from DDT's decoupled wide-head design but is motivated by a distinct theoretical argument: the diffusion model's width must match the token dimensionality of the latent space. The paper also generalizes the resolution-dependent noise schedule shift from Esser et al. (2024) to account for channel dimensionality, and introduces noise-augmented decoder training to address the distribution mismatch between clean RAE latents and noisy diffusion outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges two widely-held assumptions in latent diffusion: that semantic encoders cannot faithfully reconstruct images, and that high-dimensional latents are detrimental to diffusion training. By showing both are wrong (with theoretical backing via Theorem 1), it reframes autoencoding from a compression mechanism into a representation foundation, potentially making RAE the new default for diffusion transformer training. The 47× training speedup over SiT-XL and SOTA FID scores (1.51 unguided, 1.13 guided at 256×256) are compelling, though results are limited to ImageNet class-conditional generation. Open questions include whether RAE generalizes to text-to-image, video, and other modalities, and whether the width-matching requirement can be relaxed with alternative architectures.
