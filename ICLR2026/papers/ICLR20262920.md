## Problem

Prior self-supervised learning methods for visual representations have focused on either object recognition or motion understanding, but not both in tandem. Image-based SSL methods learn semantic features but lack temporal information, while video-based methods either ignore motion transformations or depend on external supervised optical flow networks. The paper asks how to jointly learn rich image-level representations for both recognition and motion solely from natural videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262920/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes pairs of source and target video frames sampled 0.5–1 seconds apart, cropped at 224×224 resolution with larger crops (area 0.2–0.4) for the dense prediction objective and smaller crops (area 0.05–0.2) for the DINO invariance loss. It produces dense feature predictions at three hierarchical levels (3, 6, 9 of 12 ViT levels), 10 learnable motion latent tokens, and backward-refined features usable for downstream segmentation (via UperNet or linear readout) and optical flow (via a DPT head fed with multi-level dense tokens).

## Method

Midway Network extends latent dynamics modeling to natural videos by introducing a midway top-down path: a transformer-based inverse dynamics model infers motion latents between source and target frames, which condition a forward dynamics transformer that predicts dense target features. Two key design choices handle multi-object natural video scenes: (1) the forward prediction objective operates over dense spatial features rather than global embeddings, and (2) a hierarchical architecture with cross-attention backward layers refines features top-down across levels, with motion latents accumulating and being refined using higher-level predictions (inspired by PWC-Net's iterative flow refinement). Learnable gating units on the forward transformer's residual connections allow the model to suppress identity propagation for moved objects. Training combines the dense forward prediction MSE loss (averaged over levels) with a DINO joint-embedding invariance objective on smaller crops, using an EMA target network.

## Results

After pretraining on BDD100K (ViT-S, 300 epochs), Midway Network achieves 50.4 mIoU on BDD100K UperNet segmentation and 58.5 mIoU on CityScapes UperNet, while simultaneously attaining 4.1 EPE on MPI-Sintel and 7.3 EPE on FlyingThings optical flow. It is the only method to achieve strong performance on both semantic segmentation and optical flow tasks; prior methods excel at one but not the other. Scaling to ViT-B further improves results to 55.2 mIoU (BDD100K UperNet) and 4.1 EPE (MPI-Sintel), with CroCo v2 remaining slightly ahead on optical flow (3.0 EPE) but suffering a large segmentation drop (26.5 mIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Midway Network outperforms all baselines on BDD100K semantic segmentation (50.4 mIoU UperNet vs. DINO's 49.3, DynaMo's 47.4, PooDLe's 47.4) and achieves the best optical flow on MPI-Sintel (4.1 EPE vs. CroCo v2's 5.1, VideoMAE's 7.2, DINO's 11.5). On CityScapes, Midway (58.5 mIoU) is competitive with PooDLe (59.2) and DINO (57.9), though PooDLe relies on an external supervised optical flow network. CroCo v2, the strongest optical-flow-only baseline, achieves 31.9 mIoU on BDD100K UperNet—nearly 19 points below Midway—demonstrating the tradeoff of focusing solely on motion. DoRA and iBOT are outperformed on both tasks by large margins (e.g., DoRA: 40.8 mIoU / 11.5 EPE vs. Midway's 50.4 / 4.1). The key finding is that Midway is the only method to simultaneously achieve top-tier performance on both recognition and motion, whereas every baseline is strong in at most one domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends latent dynamics modeling from the robotic control domain (DynaMo, Cui et al. 2024) to natural video self-supervised learning, combining inverse and forward dynamics with a hierarchical architecture inspired by optical flow networks (PWC-Net). It also builds on PooDLe's (Wang et al. 2025) backward-layer design and DINO-style invariance objective for learning from naturalistic videos. The key departure from prior video SSL methods (DoRA, CroCo v2, PooDLe) is the joint learning of both semantic and motion representations through a dense forward prediction objective conditioned on inferred motion latents, rather than relying on external flow networks or treating motion only as a data augmentation signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first architecture to simultaneously achieve competitive performance on both semantic segmentation and optical flow from natural video pretraining, addressing a gap where prior methods excelled at one task but not the other. The forwarded feature perturbation analysis provides a novel, interpretable diagnostic for probing what dynamics a model has learned, which could become a useful tool for evaluating latent dynamics models more broadly. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger and more diverse video corpora, how the learned dynamics can be leveraged for downstream planning or world-modeling tasks, and whether the hierarchical refinement is necessary at larger model scales.
