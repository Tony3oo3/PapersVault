## Problem

The open-source community lacks a unified RL framework capable of training LLM agents from scratch across diverse, realistic multi-turn environments. Moreover, directly training agents for long-horizon interaction with vanilla RL methods suffers from training instability and collapse, limiting the agent's ability to benefit from extended exploration.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a task instruction and a sequence of environment observations (textual states, available actions, and feedback) in a POMDP formulation, and produces a sequence of text actions (e.g., API calls, navigation commands) over multiple turns. Both training and evaluation use the ReAct paradigm, where each turn interleaves reasoning and acting. The environment returns a binary outcome reward r(τ) ∈ [0,1] after N interaction turns, and the framework supports parallel environment instances via HTTP-based server-client communication for scalable rollout collection.

## Method

AgentGym-RL provides a modular, decoupled framework with three plug-and-play modules (environment, agent, training) that unifies PPO, GRPO, RLOO, and REINFORCE++ under a single interface across five scenario categories (web navigation, deep search, digital games, embodied tasks, scientific tasks). The key training contribution is ScalingInter-RL, a staged curriculum that progressively increases the maximum interaction horizon during RL rollouts via a monotonic schedule {h₁ < h₂ < … < hₙ}, updated every Δ steps by an adaptive increment δₕ. Starting from a short horizon stabilizes early training by focusing the agent on exploitation and fundamental task-solving, while gradual expansion encourages deeper environmental exploration without the redundant loops and training collapse observed in fixed long-horizon settings. This approach is algorithm-agnostic and has been validated across GRPO, PPO, and REINFORCE++, with ablations showing insensitivity to the specific horizon schedule and transition frequency.

## Results

ScalingInter-7B (a 7B open-source model trained with the proposed method) achieves 38.3% overall on Deep Search, surpassing Gemini-2.5-Pro (36.5%) and GPT-4o (26.8%), though trailing OpenAI o3 (49.5%). On WebArena, it reaches 26.00% (vs. GPT-4o 16.00%); on TextCraft, 91.00% (vs. GPT-4o 83.00%); and on BabyAI, 96.67% (vs. OpenAI o3 94.44%). Across all 27 tasks, the 7B ScalingInter-RL model achieves a 61.8% average success rate, significantly outperforming much larger models like Llama-3.1-70B (46.9%) and Qwen2.5-72B (42.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScalingInter-7B clearly outperforms GPT-4o (26.8→38.3 on Deep Search, 16→26 on WebArena, 83→91 on TextCraft, 86.67→96.67 on BabyAI) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (36.5→38.3 on Deep Search) across most benchmarks. It trails OpenAI o3 on Deep Search (38.3 vs 49.5) and WebArena (26 vs 34) but beats it on BabyAI (96.67 vs 94.44) and is close on TextCraft (91 vs 93). Against the strongest open-source baseline DeepSeek-R1-0528 (40.3 on Deep Search), ScalingInter-7B is within 2 points and surpasses it on WebArena (26 vs 28 is a loss, but 91 vs 93 on TextCraft is close). Compared to same-size baselines, the gains are dramatic: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct improves from 18.8 to 38.3 on Deep Search (+19.5), and Llama-3.1-70B (22.0) is far surpassed. The paper also shows GRPO consistently outperforms REINFORCE++ (e.g., 89 vs 73 on TextCraft for 7B), and ScalingInter-RL adds further gains on top of any RL algorithm (e.g., PPO: 68→71 on TextCraft, 10.83→25.69 on SciWorld).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends AgentGym (Xi et al., 2025b), which provided interactive environments but focused on supervised fine-tuning, by placing online RL at the core of the training stack and unifying mainstream algorithms (PPO, GRPO, REINFORCE++, RLOO) under a single modular interface. It departs from prior multi-turn RL agent work (ARCher, RAGEN, Search-R1, WebRL) that addressed individual tasks or environments in isolation, by offering a cross-environment, from-scratch training framework spanning web navigation, deep search, games, embodied control, and scientific tasks. The ScalingInter-RL method adapts curriculum-learning principles to the interaction horizon dimension, progressively expanding the number of agent-environment turns to stabilize long-horizon training—a distinction from prior curriculum approaches that scale task difficulty rather than interaction depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By releasing a unified, extensible open-source RL framework with 27 tasks across 5 scenarios, this work lowers the barrier for the community to train and compare agent policies, addressing a clear gap the authors identify. The finding that post-training and test-time interaction scaling (7B model at 61.8%) substantially outperforms simply increasing model size (70B at 46.9%, 72B at 42.8%) offers a practical scaling strategy for resource-constrained settings. However, performance gaps persist in open-ended environments (WebArena, Deep Search) where feedback is noisy, and failure cases reveal insufficient procedural understanding in scientific tasks—suggesting that environment design and reward structure remain critical open problems for next-generation agent training.
