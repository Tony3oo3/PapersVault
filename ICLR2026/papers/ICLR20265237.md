## Problem

The dominant VAE+Diffusion paradigm for image generation suffers from slow training (millions of steps), expensive inference (250+ sampling steps), and poor transferability to perception tasks, all stemming from the weak semantic discriminability of VAE latent spaces. The paper identifies that semantic entanglement in VAE latents makes the velocity field ambiguous during diffusion, fundamentally limiting both training efficiency and few-step sampling quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265237/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label (ImageNet-1K) and Gaussian noise in a 16×16×384 SVG feature space as input, producing a 256×256 RGB image (also demonstrated at 512×512 and 1024×1024). Unlike standard VAE-based LDMs that operate on 16×16×4 latents, SVG operates in a much higher-dimensional space (384 channels) yet requires only 25 Euler steps for high-quality generation versus 250 for baselines. The same SVG feature space also supports zero-shot class-conditioned editing via SDEdit-style inversion and latent interpolation.

## Method

SVG replaces the VAE with a frozen DINOv3 encoder augmented by a lightweight ViT-based Residual Encoder that captures fine-grained perceptual details missing from DINO features. The residual outputs are concatenated with DINO features along the channel dimension, then distribution-aligned (normalized to match DINO batch statistics) to preserve semantic discriminability. A VAE-style decoder maps the combined SVG features back to pixel space. The diffusion model is trained with a flow matching objective directly on this semantically structured latent space, following SiT's architecture with QK-Norm. The key insight is that clear semantic separation in the latent space makes the mean velocity field consistent within classes and divergent across classes, dramatically simplifying the optimization landscape and enabling stable training in high-dimensional spaces with far fewer sampling steps.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, SVG-XL achieves a gFID of 3.54 (w/ CFG) and 6.57 (w/o CFG) using only 25 sampling steps and 80 training epochs, substantially outperforming SiT-XL with SD-VAE under the same few-step setting (gFID 6.06 w/ CFG, 22.58 w/o CFG). With extended training (500 epochs, still 25 steps), SVG-XL reaches gFID 2.10 (w/ CFG) and 3.94 (w/o CFG), surpassing SiT-XL and DiT-XL which require 1400 epochs and 250 steps to reach gFID 2.15 and 2.27 (w/ CFG) respectively. At 1400 epochs, SVG-XL further improves to gFID 1.92 (w/ CFG) and 3.36 (w/o CFG), while maintaining the 25-step inference budget. The reconstruction fidelity (rFID = 0.65) confirms that the SVG tokenizer preserves image quality comparable to SD-VAE (rFID = 0.61).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SiT-XL with SD-VAE (the standard LDM baseline), SVG-XL at 80 epochs and 25 steps achieves gFID 6.57 (w/o CFG) versus SiT-XL's 22.58 under identical few-step conditions — a 3.4× improvement — and at 500 epochs/25 steps reaches gFID 3.94 (w/o CFG), beating SiT-XL's 9.35 at 1400 epochs/250 steps. Against DiT-XL (1400 epochs, 250 steps, gFID 2.27 w/ CFG), SVG-XL at 500 epochs/25 steps matches or exceeds it (gFID 2.10 w/ CFG) with 10× fewer sampling steps and ~3× fewer training epochs. Against REPA-XL (800 epochs, 250 steps), SVG wins on w/o CFG gFID (3.94 vs 5.90 at 500 epochs) but trails on w/ CFG gFID (2.10 vs 1.42), indicating REPA's representation alignment still provides a slight edge under strong guidance. Against SiT-XL with VA-VAE (80 epochs, 25 steps), SVG-XL improves both w/o CFG (6.57 vs 7.29) and w/ CFG (3.54 vs 4.13) gFID, confirming that replacing the VAE entirely with DINOv3-based features is more effective than merely aligning VAE latents to VFM features. Against LlamaGen (300 epochs, 256 steps, gFID 2.18 w/ CFG) and MaskDiT-XL (1600 epochs, 250 steps, gFID 2.28 w/ CFG), SVG-XL at 500 epochs/25 steps achieves gFID 2.10 w/ CFG, matching or exceeding both with far fewer steps and epochs.

## Relation to Prior Work

SVG departs fundamentally from the dominant VAE+Diffusion paradigm (LDM, DiT, SiT) by eliminating the VAE entirely and training diffusion directly on frozen DINOv3 self-supervised features augmented with a lightweight residual encoder. While prior work (REPA, VA-VAE) attempted to improve VAE latent spaces by aligning them with vision foundation model features, SVG argues these are ad hoc fixes that don't address the root cause—weak semantic discriminability in VAE latents—and instead constructs the latent space from self-supervised representations from the ground up. The diffusion backbone follows SiT's flow-matching setup, and the decoder architecture is inherited from the original LDM, but the feature space itself is a novel contribution that unifies generation with perception and understanding in a single representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work challenges a foundational assumption in generative modeling—that VAEs are necessary to create a suitable latent space for diffusion—and provides evidence that semantically structured self-supervised features are actually superior for both training efficiency (80 vs. 1400+ epochs) and inference speed (25 vs. 250 steps). The broader vision of a single unified feature space supporting generation, classification, segmentation, and depth estimation is compelling for multimodal systems, though the paper acknowledges that CFG is less effective in this framework, the feature dimensionality (384 vs. 4 channels) remains a concern, and scaling to text-to-image/video and higher resolutions is still unexplored. Whether this paradigm generalizes beyond class-conditional ImageNet generation to open-ended T2I tasks is the critical open question.
