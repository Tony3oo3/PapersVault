## Problem

Real-world multivariate time series forecasting faces three simultaneous challenges—complex inter-channel dependencies, channel-wise asynchronous sampling at different fixed periods, and block-wise missing intervals at test time—that existing architectures address only in isolation or under simplifying assumptions of uniform sampling and complete data. No prior framework handles all three jointly without resorting to interpolation, which introduces spectral distortion and signal degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265207/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model ingests multivariate time series where each channel has a distinct fixed sampling period (e.g., 1-hour for load, 15-minute for temperature), so channels carry different numbers of points over the same temporal window. At test time, contiguous missing blocks may remove entire patches from a channel's input; these are handled by removing the corresponding local tokens rather than zero-filling. Outputs are per-channel future forecasts at each channel's native resolution, evaluated via Channel-aggregated MSE (CMSE) computed only at valid observable positions, with no interpolation applied to either input or target.

## Method

ChannelTokenFormer (CTF) is a Transformer-based framework built on three coupled mechanisms. First, channel-wise frequency-based dynamic patching uses FFT to detect each channel's dominant period and sets patch lengths accordingly, preserving natural sampling resolutions without resampling. Second, each channel contributes local patch tokens plus a small set of learnable channel tokens, which are processed through a unified mask-guided self-attention layer: local tokens attend only within their own channel (intra-temporal modeling), while channel tokens attend to their own local tokens and to channel tokens from other channels (but never to themselves), acting as read-only global anchors that aggregate cross-channel information. Third, random patch masking during training serves as a proxy for test-time block-wise missingness, and at inference fully unobserved patches are simply removed from the token sequence. Predictions are decoded from the channel tokens via per-sampling-period shared decoders, making the architecture robust to variable input lengths and heterogeneous channel counts.

## Results

On the channel-wise asynchronous forecasting benchmark (Case 1), ChannelTokenFormer (CTF) achieves the best or second-best average CMSE across all six datasets: 0.399 on ETT1, 0.377 on ETT2, 0.403 on SolarWind, 0.275 on Weather, 0.776 on EPA, and 0.285 on CHS. Under the more challenging setting with block-wise test-time missingness (Case 2, SolarWind), CTF maintains its lead even at a 50% missing ratio (CMSE 0.475), outperforming the next-best baseline TimeFilter (0.522) by nearly 10%. The ablation study confirms that all three components—channel dependency, dynamic patching, and patch masking—contribute to performance, with removing dynamic patching causing the largest degradation (CMSE 0.452 → 0.494 at 37.5% missingness).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CTF outperforms all channel-dependent baselines on 5 of 6 datasets in CMSE, with the largest margins on EPA (0.776 vs. 0.782 for DUET, 0.863 for TimeFilter) and CHS (0.285 vs. 0.294 for t-PatchGNN). The only dataset where CTF is not the top performer is Weather, where TimeFilter edges it out by 0.002 CMSE (0.273 vs. 0.275). Against channel-independent methods, CTF shows consistent advantages: on ETT2, PatchTST degrades severely (CMSE 0.455 vs. CTF's 0.377), and on EPA, DLinear reaches 0.808 versus CTF's 0.776. Irregular-sampling baselines (Hi-Patch, t-PatchGNN) are competitive on CHS (0.294) but trail CTF on all other datasets, confirming that point-wise inter-channel modeling is insufficient for structured multi-source asynchrony. Under block-wise test-time missingness (Case 2), CTF's advantage widens with increasing missing ratio: at m=0.5, CTF achieves CMSE 0.475 while the next-best TimeFilter reaches 0.522 and BiTGraph (the only missingness-aware baseline) reaches 0.478, showing CTF's patch-masking strategy provides a clear structural advantage over interpolation-based or imputation-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

ChannelTokenFormer repurposes channel-level summary tokens from iTransformer and TimeXer, frequency-guided patching from Moirai and LightGTS, and random patch dropout from PatchDropout, but recombines them under a unified mask-guided attention mechanism that simultaneously handles cross-channel dependencies, channel-wise asynchronous sampling, and block-wise test-time missingness. The key conceptual departure is that prior work addresses these three challenges in isolation—channel-dependent models assume aligned sampling, channel-independent models lose cross-channel structure, and missing-value methods ignore asynchrony—whereas this paper treats them as a single coupled problem requiring a joint architectural solution. The read-only attention masking strategy, inspired by prompt-optimization work in vision-language models, is adapted to enforce a strict information flow where channel tokens aggregate but local tokens remain channel-local.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap that is increasingly relevant as industrial and environmental monitoring systems deploy heterogeneous sensor networks with genuinely different sampling rates and intermittent outages. The interpolation-free design, supported by a rigorous spectral distortion analysis (Appendix B), provides a principled argument for why avoiding resampling matters beyond just convenience. However, the quadratic attention complexity limits scalability to ultra-high-dimensional settings (e.g., thousands of traffic channels), and the gains over strong baselines like TimeFilter are modest (1–3% CMSE), suggesting the unified framework's advantage is most pronounced under severe missingness or extreme asynchrony rather than in mild practical settings. Open questions include whether the frequency-based patching generalizes to non-stationary signals with drifting dominant periods and whether the approach extends to multimodal or streaming settings.
