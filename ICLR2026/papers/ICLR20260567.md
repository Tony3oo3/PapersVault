## Problem

Existing self-improvement methods for neural combinatorial optimization supervise policies on single-action trajectories, which is fundamentally misaligned with multi-agent problems (e.g., vehicle routing, job-shop scheduling) where agent-permutation symmetries mean many distinct action sequences yield identical solutions. This forces the model to treat symmetric alternatives as errors, reducing sample efficiency and hindering the learning of coordinated multi-agent behavior. Additionally, the sequential single-action generation makes solution construction computationally expensive, requiring one expensive encoder forward pass per agent decision.

## Input / Output

The method takes a combinatorial optimization instance encoded as a bipartite graph with agent nodes (machines, vehicles) and task nodes (jobs, customers), where edges carry problem-specific costs (processing times, travel distances). At each decision step, the policy produces a joint logit matrix L ∈ R^{M×N} in a single forward pass, from which M agent-task pairs are sampled autoregressively without replacement to form a valid matching. A learnable skip token is appended to the task set, allowing any agent to defer its assignment to a later step (with a small annealed penalty), and at least one agent must select a real task per step. The final output is a complete solution (schedule or set of routes) with an associated objective value.

## Method

MACSIM replaces the single next-token prediction of standard self-improvement with a multi-agent policy that emits a full joint agent-task logit matrix via a MatNet-style cross-attention encoder over the bipartite state graph. A key architectural insight is that the expensive neural forward pass is amortized over M agent decisions: the logit matrix is computed once, and M valid assignments are extracted through a fast autoregressive sampling procedure (Algorithm 1) that sequentially draws agent-task pairs from the joint softmax without replacement, guaranteeing conflict-free matchings by construction. For training, MACSIM introduces a permutation-invariant set-based cross-entropy loss (L_CE) that treats each agent-task pair in the expert matching as an independent supervised instance, avoiding the gradient conflicts inherent in sequential multi-action NLL and the sensitivity to agent ordering in Plackett-Luce losses. The overall training follows a two-stage self-improvement loop: the current policy generates β solutions per instance, the best trajectory is selected, and the policy is updated via imitation on the resulting state–multi-action pairs. A skip token with exponentially annealed penalty further enables agents to wait for more favorable assignments, improving solution quality on larger instances.

## Results

On FFSP, MACSIM with sampling achieves a 0.00% gap to the best-known solution across all instance sizes (20×4×3, 50×4×3, 100×4×3), outperforming SLIM (0.82–2.61% gap) and all other neural baselines. On FJSP 20×5, MACSIM(s.) reaches 188.10 (0.00% gap), matching OR-Tools (188.15, 0.03% gap) while being ~800× faster. On HCVRP, MACSIM(s.) achieves gaps of 2.89–3.69%, consistently beating SLIM(s.) (4.69–5.05%) and all other neural methods. Inference latency is reduced by 9–10× compared to SLIM (e.g., 0.91 s vs. 10.21 s on FFSP 50×4×3 with sampling).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MACSIM consistently outperforms the primary self-improvement baseline SLIM across all three problem families: on FFSP it closes the gap from 0.82–2.61% (SLIM) to 0.00%, on FJSP it reduces the gap from 0.52–3.82% to 0.00–1.69%, and on HCVRP from 4.69–5.05% to 2.89–3.69%. Against exact/heuristic solvers, MACSIM(s.) matches OR-Tools on FJSP 20×5 (188.10 vs. 188.15) while being ~800× faster, and on FFSP it surpasses Gurobi (which only solves the smallest instance within 600 s) by a wide margin. Among neural baselines, MACSIM dominates: on FFSP it beats PARCO(s.) by 0.31–2.28 objective units, MatNet(s.) by 1.72–2.50, and PolyNet(s.) by 1.27–1.99. On HCVRP, MACSIM(s.) outperforms 2D-Ptr(s.) (best prior neural method) by 0.06–0.04 in objective value. The advantage over SLIM grows with the number of agents (Figure 3b), and inference time is reduced by 9–10× (e.g., 0.91 s vs. 10.21 s on FFSP 50×4×3), confirming that the multi-action generation and set-based loss yield both quality and efficiency gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

MACSIM extends the self-improvement paradigm for NCO (Pirnay & Grimm, 2024; Corsini et al., 2024) from single-action next-token prediction to joint multi-agent action generation, addressing a structural limitation when multiple agents must coordinate. Architecturally, it adapts the MatNet-style cross-attention encoder (Kwon et al., 2021) and the joint-logit scoring mechanism from PARCO (Berto et al., 2024), but departs from PARCO's independent-agent sampling and post-hoc conflict resolution by introducing autoregressive sampling without replacement from a shared joint distribution. The permutation-invariant set-prediction loss draws conceptual parallels to bipartite matching in object detection (Carion et al., 2020) and to DPN's (Zheng et al., 2024) use of agent-permutation symmetry, but applies it to imitation learning rather than RL baseline computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and resolves a concrete failure mode of self-improvement in multi-agent CO: single-action supervision implicitly enforces an arbitrary agent ordering and treats symmetric alternatives as errors, degrading both sample efficiency and generalization. The practical impact is significant—MACSIM reduces solution generation time by roughly an order of magnitude versus SLIM (with the gap widening as agent count grows) and maintains stable training where SLIM oscillates on larger instances. However, the reliance on step-wise graph re-encoding still makes MACSIM slower than lightweight encode-once solvers, and the gap to exact solvers (OR-Tools, Gurobi) remains substantial on harder instances. Future work on incremental state updates or cached encodings could close this efficiency gap while preserving the symmetry-aware training signal.
