## Problem

Majority voting, a proven self-improvement signal for verifiable tasks, relies on exact matching and thus cannot handle open-ended responses like translations or summaries. Existing alternatives for unverifiable tasks—self-judging and entropy minimization—require the LLM to evaluate its own outputs, incurring high computational overhead and introducing overconfidence biases from the model's intrinsic self-preference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261254/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unsupervised dataset of questions (no reference answers) and a base LLM. For each question, it samples N=64 stochastic responses (temperature 0.7, top_p 0.9), encodes them with a lightweight sentence embedding model (SimCSE), and outputs DPO-style preference pairs consisting of the highest- and lowest-scoring responses. The final output is a DPO-finetuned model; the embedding model and clustering step add negligible overhead (0.1%–5% of self-evaluation cost) and do not scale with base model size.

## Method

Semantic voting replaces exact-match majority voting with soft matching via cosine similarity of sentence embeddings. For each question, N stochastic responses are first filtered by HDBSCAN density-based clustering on their embeddings, retaining only the largest cluster to remove outliers. Within this cluster, each response's voting score is the mean cosine similarity to all other cluster members, identifying the response most semantically aligned with the consensus. The highest- and lowest-scoring responses form a preference pair, and the model is trained with DPO, which provides provable robustness to noisy pseudo-labels. This design eliminates any need for the LLM to judge its own outputs, replacing a costly and biased self-evaluation step with a lightweight embedding-based similarity computation.

## Results

SVSI consistently improves over base models across all 6 model–dataset configurations tested (Qwen-1.5B/3B/7B, Llama-1B/3B/8B on 4 translation + 2 summarization tasks). On the strongest model (Qwen-7B), SVSI achieves 20.19 BLEU on wmt24pp_de (vs. 16.42 base), 21.76 on wmt24pp_fr (vs. 19.71), and 20.69 RougeL on cnn_dailymail (vs. 16.44). For the weakest model (Llama-1B), SVSI more than doubles BLEU on wmt24pp_de (5.28 vs. 2.91 base). Computational overhead for building preference pairs is orders of magnitude lower than self-evaluation baselines, ranging from 0.1%–5% of the time required by SJ or EM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Self-Judging (SJ; Yuan et al., ICML 2024), SVSI is substantially more stable: SJ causes catastrophic degradation in many settings (e.g., Qwen-1.5B drops from 16.12 to 3.45 BLEU on wmt24pp_de; Llama-8B crashes from 24.32 to 11.46 on wmt24pp_fr and from 20.68 to 0.08 RougeL on cnn_dailymail), whereas SVSI never degrades below the base model. Against Entropy Minimization (EM; Agarwal et al., 2025), SVSI also wins on stability—EM produces zeros or near-zeros in several cells (e.g., Qwen-7B drops to 7.32 BLEU on wmt24pp_de; Llama-8B drops to 0 on wmt24pp_de)—while SVSI maintains consistent gains. On the GRPO variant (SVSI-G vs. EMPO; Zhang et al., 2025b), SVSI-G is competitive in average performance but less stable than EMPO, which uses discrete cluster-size rewards; the authors attribute this to the high-cardinality continuous ranking in SVSI-G introducing noisy preference pairs. Overall, SVSI matches or exceeds both self-evaluation baselines in the majority of model–task configurations, with its clearest advantage being the absence of severe performance collapses and a 10–100× reduction in preference-pair construction time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the majority voting paradigm (Wang et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2023) from hard exact matching to soft semantic matching via sentence embeddings, thereby making the consensus mechanism applicable to open-ended unverifiable tasks where exact matching is infeasible. It explicitly departs from the two dominant self-evaluation strategies—self-judging (Yuan et al., 2024) and entropy minimization (Agarwal et al., 2025)—arguing that their computational overhead and intrinsic overconfidence biases are unnecessary given the robustness of DPO to noisy labels (Chowdhury et al., 2024). The training framework borrows DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) and the filtering step draws on density-based clustering (HDBSCAN), but the core contribution is the semantic voting score itself as a lightweight, bias-free pseudo-labeling signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: semantic voting reduces the cost of constructing preference pairs by orders of magnitude (0.1%–5% of self-evaluation time) while matching or exceeding their quality, making self-improvement on open-ended tasks far more accessible for resource-constrained settings. Key limitations include a measurable reduction in linguistic diversity (a precursor to model collapse in iterative self-training), instability when integrated with GRPO due to continuous reward noise, and the finding that the method's benefit is most pronounced for weaker models that produce noisier self-generated candidates. Open questions include whether hybrid discrete-continuous reward schemes can stabilize GRPO-based variants, how the approach scales to longer-form generation where embedding quality may degrade, and whether the diversity loss becomes problematic in multi-turn or iterative self-training regimes.
