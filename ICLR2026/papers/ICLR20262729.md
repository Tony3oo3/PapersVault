## Problem

Existing training paradigms for equipping VLMs with thinking capabilities—SFT on chain-of-thought data and RL with verifiable rewards (RLVR)—are designed for large VLMs and fail on small VLMs (SVLMs, <1B parameters) due to their limited capacity and weak instruction adherence. SFT forces SVLMs to memorize verbose pseudo-thinking traces, while RLVR suffers from advantage collapse when the model cannot reliably produce structured outputs. The central challenge is that the trade-off between memorization and exploration, which static hybrid methods handle via fixed hyperparameters, has an extremely narrow success window for SVLMs, making empirical tuning nearly impossible.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image paired with a text instruction (e.g., a chart question or medical VQA prompt) and produces a structured thinking trace followed by a final answer enclosed in tags. The thinking trace follows a domain-specific template with explicit sections (Extraction, Calculation, Conclusion) that must reference concrete visual facts (objects, attributes, values) extracted from the image. The structured format is critical: it enables rule-based answer verification for RL rewards and token-level F1 scoring of thinking traces, and it constrains the SVLM's limited capacity toward grounded, concise reasoning rather than open-ended generation.

## Method

DyME (Dynamic Memorize-Explore) integrates SFT and GRPO through a per-step binary switching rule: at each optimization step, the policy samples K responses; if at least one passes rule-based answer verification, training proceeds in GRPO exploration mode (with a modified objective that drops KL penalty and clipping, and adds an auxiliary thinking-trace reward), otherwise it falls back to SFT memorization mode. This state-driven switch is parameter-free and ensures that every gradient update is well-conditioned—SFT provides low-variance ground-truth guidance when exploration is uninformative, while GRPO exploits relative advantages only when the policy has already found a feasible solution. Complementing the switching mechanism, a Visual Supervision module (implemented via LLM prompt engineering with Qwen2.5-14B) introduces a visual checker that scores self-generated thinking traces against extracted visual facts, and a visual refiner that dynamically restructures SFT ground-truth by incorporating visual elements from high-scoring explorations, creating a synergistic feedback loop between the two modes.

## Results

On the three-domain benchmark (Medical VQA, Chart Understanding, Geometry), DyME consistently improves SVLMs: SmolVLM-500M rises from 49.9 to 55.6 average (+5.7%), LLaVA-OV-S from 50.7 to 55.4 (+4.7%), and InternVL2-S from 56.3 to 58.1 (+1.8%). In contrast, existing paradigms degrade performance (e.g., SFT drops SmolVLM to 44.1, GRPO to 44.0, Two-stage to 45.4). In the controlled ChartQA experiment (Part I), Pure DyME achieves 64.9% relaxed correctness with medium-quality CoT data, outperforming SFT (57.8%), Two-stage (59.9%), and Two-stage w/ KL (60.8%). DyME-trained SVLMs surpass the LVLM MoVA (54.2 avg) on these specialized domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyME decisively outperforms the Two-stage SFT→RL baseline (Chu et al.) across all three SVLMs and all three domains: e.g., on SmolVLM, DyME achieves 55.6 avg vs. Two-stage's 45.4, and on LLaVA-OV-S, 55.4 vs. 48.0. Against pure GRPO (Shao et al.), DyME wins by a wide margin (55.6 vs. 44.0 on SmolVLM), confirming that unguided exploration causes advantage collapse in SVLMs. In the ChartQA ablation (Table 1b), DyME's binary switch (64.9%) beats SFT Annealing/CHORD (Zhang et al., 64.0% at +25% compute cost) and SFT Budget/hard-mining (59.6%), while LUFFY (Yan et al.) is shown in Fig. S2 to have a less adaptive off-policy decay profile. DyME-trained SVLMs surpass the LVLM MoVA (Zong et al., 54.2 avg) with SmolVLM reaching 55.6 and LLaVA-OV-S 55.4, though they remain below the strongest LVLMs like InternVL2-L (66.5) and LLaVA-OV-L (60.4). The remaining LVLM references (LLaVA-Med, Cambrian-1, LLaVA-1.5/1.6) serve as context for domain-specific performance levels rather than direct training-paradigm comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for its exploration mode and standard SFT for its memorization mode, but departs from all prior hybrid SFT+RL paradigms (two-stage training, CHORD's annealed SFT loss, LUFFY's policy shaping) by replacing their static, hyperparameter-governed trade-off with a state-driven binary switch that selects the training mode per-sample based on whether any generated response passes rule-based verification. The visual supervision module (checker + refiner) is a novel LLM-prompt-engineering layer that dynamically restructures ground-truth and scores thinking traces using extracted visual facts, a mechanism absent from prior RLVR-for-VLMs work. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as the first training paradigm explicitly designed for sub-1B VLMs, arguing that the capacity constraints of SVLMs make the static balance window in existing hybrid methods too narrow to succeed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for edge deployment: a 0.5B model trained with DyME on a few thousand samples matches or surpasses 7B LVLMs on specialized domains (medical VQA, chart QA, geometry), and the visual supervision pipeline eliminates dependence on expensive proprietary annotation (GPT-4o) by matching its quality with open-source Qwen2.5-14B. A key open limitation is that the Visual Supervision module requires extractable, discrete Visual Facts, which constrains applicability to structured/semi-structured domains and may fail on abstract semantics (memes, irony) or unstructured perception (dense crowds). Whether the dynamic switching mechanism generalizes to truly open-ended, multi-step reasoning beyond template-structured tasks remains an open question, as the paper's strongest results rely on relatively fixed output formats.
