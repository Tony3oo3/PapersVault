## Problem

Safety alignment in LLMs is brittle: even fine-tuning on entirely benign data or using low-rank adapters like LoRA can degrade refusal behaviors and produce harmful outputs. Existing fine-tuning-stage defenses do not explicitly identify which weight components encode safety or which update directions are harmful, so they cannot prevent training conflicts between safety preservation and downstream task utility in a targeted manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264556/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three inputs: the pre-trained safety-aligned LLM weights, a set of safety-triggering harmful prompts (typically 520 from AdvBench for subspace estimation and 520 from RealToxicityPrompts for projector construction), and a downstream task dataset (e.g., GSM8K, SST-2). It produces a fine-tuned model whose per-layer linear weights are split into a frozen safety-relevant component W' and a learnable low-rank adapter pair (A*, B*) constrained by a fixed null-space projector P. The projector is computed once before training and never updated, so the only trainable parameters are the adapter matrices, keeping inference overhead identical to standard LoRA.

## Method

GuardSpace has two coupled components. First, for each linear layer it computes the covariance C = XX^T from harmful-prompt activations and performs a right-preconditioned SVD on WC = UΣV^T; the leading components (large singular values) are identified as safety-relevant and frozen, while the trailing r components (smallest singular values) are used to initialize the LoRA adapters A and B, so that BA equals the safety-irrelevant portion of the original weight. Second, it performs an eigendecomposition of C and constructs a fixed projector P = Q̂Q̂^T from the zero-eigenvalue eigenvectors; during fine-tuning the forward pass uses (W' + BAP)X where W' = W − BAP, guaranteeing by Lemma 2 that for any harmful input X the adapter update is nullified (PX = 0), so the output on harmful prompts remains exactly W'X regardless of how A and B evolve. This means the model is safe at initialization (by construction) and remains safe throughout training (by the null-space constraint), while the safety-irrelevant initialization gives a head start over zero-initialized LoRA for downstream task performance.

## Results

On Llama-2-7B-Chat fine-tuned across four downstream tasks (SST-2, AGNEWS, GSM8K, Dialog Summary), GuardSpace achieves an average Harmfulness Score (HS↓) of 2.70% and average Fine-tuning Accuracy (FA↑) of 64.36%, outperforming the state-of-the-art AsFT (HS 8.10%, FA 62.78%) and the base model (HS 4.40%, FA 34.62%). On GSM8K specifically, GuardSpace reduces HS from 14.40% (AsFT) to 3.60% while improving FA from 26.00% to 28.00%. Across five model architectures on GSM8K, GuardSpace achieves the lowest average HS (7.60%) and highest average FA (64.60%), and maintains near-floor HS (≤3.60%) even with up to 20% poisoned training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GuardSpace dominates all seven baselines on both safety (HS↓) and utility (FA↑) in the main Llama-2-7B-Chat comparison. Against the strongest prior method AsFT, GuardSpace reduces average HS from 8.10% to 2.70% (a 3× improvement) while increasing average FA from 62.78% to 64.36%. Against standard LoRA, GuardSpace cuts HS from 43.10% to 2.70% (a 16× reduction) with a modest FA gain of +1.66%. Among the remaining baselines, SABT (HS 7.80%) and Safe LoRA (HS 17.80%) are the closest competitors on safety, but both trail GuardSpace by 5.1 and 15.1 percentage points respectively, while also underperforming on FA (60.66% and 60.35% vs. 64.36%). Notably, GuardSpace even surpasses the un-fine-tuned base model on HS (2.70% vs. 4.40%), which the authors attribute to the fixed null-space projector limiting first-order adapter effects on harmful inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

GuardSpace builds directly on LoRA's low-rank adaptation framework and the growing literature on safety preservation during fine-tuning (AsFT, SafeLoRA, Lisa, SafeInstr, BEA, SABT). Its key departure is a two-stage decomposition: (1) a covariance-preconditioned SVD (drawing on ASVD and CoRDA) that explicitly separates safety-relevant from safety-irrelevant weight components at initialization, and (2) a fixed null-space projector derived from the same harmful-prompt covariance that constrains all subsequent adapter updates. Unlike prior fine-tuning-stage defenses that rely on learned regularization, data augmentation, or post-hoc projection, GuardSpace makes the safety constraint structural and exact (via the null space identity) rather than approximate, and it initializes adapters from the pre-trained weights' safety-irrelevant tail rather than from zero.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is high: safety degradation during routine fine-tuning is a real deployment risk, and GuardSpace offers a drop-in modification to LoRA that requires only a one-time ~20-minute SVD pre-computation with no additional inference overhead. The OOD generalization result (matching base-model HS on semantically disjoint harmful categories) is encouraging but rests on a single model and a single OOD split, leaving open whether the fixed null space generalizes to truly novel attack paradigms (e.g., multi-turn jailbreaks, code-based exploits) or to models aligned via RLHF rather than SFT. Scalability to 70B+ models and the interaction with iterative alignment (e.g., DPO rounds) remain unaddressed.
