## Problem

Video diffusion transformers trained on a fixed maximum sequence length fail to generate longer videos, exhibiting two failure modes: model-specific periodic content repetition and a universal quality degradation (blurred details, frozen motion). Prior work such as RIFLEx addresses only repetition through positional encoding modifications, overlooking quality degradation and achieving limited extrapolation. The paper identifies that both failures share a single root cause—attention dispersion, where tokens beyond the training window dilute learned attention patterns—and that repetition is a special case when RoPE frequency harmonics organize this dispersion into periodic attention patterns.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pre-trained video diffusion transformer (HunyuanVideo, Wan2.1-1.3B, or CogVideoX-5B) and takes a text prompt (or reference image + text, or pose + text for downstream tasks) as input, producing a generated video at an extrapolated length up to 4× the training duration (e.g., 129 frames for HunyuanVideo trained at 43 frames). The method is entirely training-free and plug-and-play: it modifies attention logits during the denoising inference loop without any weight updates, and integrates into existing efficient attention kernels (FlashAttention, SageAttention) to handle sequences of ~200K tokens without out-of-memory errors.

## Method

UltraViCo suppresses attention scores for token pairs whose relative distance exceeds the training window L/2 by multiplying positive logits by a constant decay factor α (<1), while leaving in-window pairs and negative logits unchanged. For models exhibiting periodic repetition (e.g., HunyuanVideo), a stronger decay β (<α) is additionally applied to positions near harmonic alignment points mT, where RoPE frequency harmonics cause constructive amplitude accumulation and spurious periodic attention. The key theoretical insight is that the statistical row-wise attention pattern decomposes into a sum of cosines at RoPE frequencies (Eq. 4), and when these frequencies form harmonics, the composite pattern becomes strongly periodic, directly causing repeated outputs. To make this practical for long video sequences (~200K tokens), the authors implement the decay within a Triton-based FlashAttention kernel using the online-softmax formulation, avoiding explicit materialization of the full L'×L' attention matrix that would consume >80GB in bf16.

## Results

On VBench, UltraViCo consistently outperforms all six baselines across HunyuanVideo, Wan2.1-1.3B, and CogVideoX at 2×–5× extrapolation. At 4× extrapolation on HunyuanVideo, UltraViCo achieves a Dynamic Degree of 42 and Imaging Quality of 66.54, improving these by 233% and 40.5% over the previous best method (RIFLEx: 11 and 41.02), while also achieving a NoRepeat Score of 99.87 vs. 52.84. On Wan at 3× extrapolation, where all baselines collapse to near-static videos (Dynamic Degree ≤ 12), UltraViCo restores fluid motion with a Dynamic Degree of 46 and Overall score of 23.21, effectively extending the practical extrapolation limit from 2× to 4×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UltraViCo wins decisively over all six baselines (PE, PI, NTK, YaRN, TASR, RIFLEx) on every metric, model, and extrapolation ratio tested. The most dramatic gap is in Dynamic Degree: at 4× on HunyuanVideo, UltraViCo scores 42 while the best baseline (RIFLEx) scores only 11, a 233% improvement; on Wan at 3×, UltraViCo reaches 46 versus ≤12 for all baselines, which produce near-static videos. For content repetition (NoRepeat Score), UltraViCo achieves 100.0 at 3× and 99.87 at 4× on HunyuanVideo, compared to RIFLEx's 73.97 and 52.84 respectively, confirming that RIFLEx's positional-encoding fix is insufficient. RIFLEx is the strongest baseline overall but still lags UltraViCo by 4–5 points in Overall score and 15–25 points in Dynamic Degree. NTK and TASR are the next-best baselines on quality metrics but remain far behind in dynamics and repetition. The consistent margin across all settings indicates that attention-level intervention addresses failure modes that positional-encoding modifications fundamentally cannot.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of length-extrapolation work (PI, NTK, YaRN, TASR) that modifies positional encodings, and from the most direct video-specific prior work RIFLEx, which identifies RoPE frequency harmonics as the cause of content repetition but ignores the universal quality degradation. The key conceptual shift is moving from the indirect level of positional encodings to the direct level of attention maps, where the authors show both failure modes reduce to a single mechanism—attention dispersion—and that periodic repetition is merely a structured special case. The method itself (a constant decay on out-of-window attention logits) is a simple attention-level intervention, distinct from all prior positional-encoding-based approaches, and is implemented via a custom FlashAttention/SageAttention kernel to avoid materializing the full L×L mask.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By pushing the practical extrapolation limit from 2× to 4× in a training-free, plug-and-play manner, UltraViCo offers an immediately deployable improvement for existing video DiT pipelines without retraining. The unified attention-dispersion explanation provides a diagnostic lens that could inform future architectural or training-time fixes for long-sequence generalization in DiTs more broadly. Open questions include whether the approach extends beyond 5× extrapolation, whether it transfers to non-RoPE positional schemes, and whether the model-specific hyperparameters (α, β, γ) can be predicted from model architecture rather than tuned per model. The method is also orthogonal to and composable with existing long-video generation techniques (FreeNoise, FIFO-Diffusion, sliding window), broadening its practical utility.
