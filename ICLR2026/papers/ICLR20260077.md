## Problem

Vision-Language Models are vulnerable to malicious prompts that elicit harmful content, yet existing defenses rely on easily circumvented blacklist filters or computationally expensive classifier-based systems that remain fragile under embedding-level adversarial attacks. There is a lack of lightweight, robust, and interpretable mechanisms that can both detect and neutralize malicious intent before it reaches the generative pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260077/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form text prompt, tokenized into 77 subtokens by a fixed tokenizer, and processes it through a frozen hyperbolic text encoder (HySAC) to produce a point in the Lorentz model. Detection outputs a binary label by comparing the geodesic distance from the embedding to the Lorentz origin against a single learned radius. For prompts flagged as harmful, HyPS outputs a modified prompt where specific words are removed or replaced, preserving the remaining semantic content for downstream T2I generation or image retrieval.

## Method

HyPE extends Support Vector Data Description to hyperbolic geometry (HSVDD) by fixing the hypersphere center at the Lorentz origin and learning only the radius R as a single parameter, trained exclusively on benign prompts via geodesic-distance-based margin optimization. The key insight is that the hyperbolic encoder (inherited from HySAC) naturally separates benign prompts into compact clusters near the origin while pushing semantically deviant harmful prompts to larger geodesic distances, making one-class anomaly detection particularly effective. HyPS then applies Layer Integrated Gradients on the first-layer token embeddings of the HySAC encoder to compute word-level attribution scores, identifying which specific words drive the harmful classification. Sanitization proceeds via three escalating strategies: direct word removal, thesaurus-based antonym substitution (selected by CLIP similarity), or LLM-generated safe replacements (Qwen3-14B) when no antonym exists, balancing neutralization rate against semantic preservation.

## Results

HyPE achieves the highest F1 score across all paired and adversarial datasets: 0.98 on ViSU, 0.95 on MMA, 0.78 on SneakyPrompt, 0.96 on adv-MMA, and 0.80 on adv-ViSU. On single-class datasets, it reaches 0.99 accuracy on both COCO (benign) and NSFW56K (harmful), and 0.66 on I2P*. Notably, HyPE is trained exclusively on benign ViSU prompts yet generalizes to unseen harmful datasets and adversarially crafted prompts, consistently outperforming or matching all five baselines on the F1 metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyPE outperforms all five baselines on F1 across every paired and adversarial dataset. Against NSFW-Classifier (Li, 2025), HyPE gains +0.23 F1 on ViSU (0.98 vs 0.75) and +0.20 on MMA (0.95 vs 0.75), while tying on SneakyPrompt (0.78) but with higher precision (0.68 vs 0.67). Against Detoxify (Hanu, 2020), HyPE leads by +0.58 F1 on ViSU (0.98 vs 0.40) and +0.34 on adv-ViSU (0.80 vs 0.13), though Detoxify achieves slightly higher F1 on MMA (0.92 vs 0.95) due to its extreme precision-recall trade-off (0.98 precision, 0.26 recall on ViSU). Against LatentGuard (Liu, 2024), HyPE improves F1 by +0.35 on ViSU (0.98 vs 0.63) and +0.53 on adv-ViSU (0.80 vs 0.27). Against GuardT2I (Yang, 2024b), HyPE gains +0.39 F1 on ViSU (0.98 vs 0.59) and +0.27 on adv-ViSU (0.80 vs 0.53), though GuardT2I achieves a perfect 1.00 precision on adv-MMA at the cost of only 0.10 recall. DiffGuard (Khader, 2025) is the weakest baseline overall, with F1 scores below 0.65 on all paired datasets and 0.65 on adv-ViSU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Support Vector Data Description (SVDD) framework from Euclidean to hyperbolic geometry, introducing HSVDD as a one-class anomaly detector that requires training only a radius parameter. It builds directly on the hyperbolic safety-aware VLM encoder from HySAC (Poppi et al., 2025) and the broader line of work on hyperbolic embeddings for hierarchical structure (Nickel & Kiela, 2018; Ganea et al., 2018), but departs from prior harmful-prompt defenses by reframing detection as outlier detection in a structured geometric space rather than supervised binary classification. The sanitization module (HyPS) repurposes Layer Integrated Gradients for word-level attribution, connecting the detection and remediation steps in a way that prior blacklist or classifier-based systems do not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-parameter, benign-only training requirement makes HyPE a lightweight, deployable safety layer that avoids the data-hunger and opacity of classifier-based filters, and its robustness across MMA, SneakyPrompt, and a custom white-box adaptive attack addresses a real gap where prior defenses collapse. The adaptive-attack trade-off finding—that evading detection requires substantially degrading the prompt's harmful intent—provides a useful security guarantee, though it is bounded by the specific hyperbolic encoder used. Open questions include whether the fixed-origin HSVDD generalizes to multi-domain or multi-modal safety, how the approach scales to increasingly sophisticated jailbreak taxonomies, and whether the hyperbolic encoder's own vulnerability to embedding-level attacks (CCA) can be addressed without the detection layer.
