## Problem

Group-based policy optimization methods like GRPO suffer from two fundamental flaws: degenerate groups (where all responses share the same outcome) erase learning signals and waste computation, and the group synchronization barrier forces the entire group to wait for its slowest member, severely hindering throughput in agentic or long-horizon settings with highly variable generation times. These issues have been patched with engineering heuristics like dynamic sampling, but the underlying architectural constraints remain.

## Input / Output

Each training sample is a single (prompt, response) pair with a binary outcome reward (+1 correct, 0 incorrect), rather than a group of G responses per prompt. The method maintains per-prompt state in the form of Beta distribution parameters (α, β) that track the success probability over time, and a prioritized sampling mechanism selects which prompts to process at each iteration. The output is updated policy parameters via PPO-Clip, with the value tracker state persisting across iterations to provide a running baseline for the next step.

## Method

SPO replaces GRPO's noisy per-group baseline with three synergistic components: (1) a KL-adaptive Bayesian value tracker that maintains a persistent Beta-distribution estimate of each prompt's success probability, where the discount factor ρ(x) = 2^(−D(x)/D_half) causes faster forgetting as the policy drifts (measured by KL divergence), making it equivalent to an adaptive exponential moving average; (2) global advantage normalization across the entire batch rather than per-group, which stabilizes the learning signal; and (3) prioritized prompt sampling weighted by the Bernoulli standard deviation √(v̂(1−v̂))/N_eff^γ + ε, creating an adaptive curriculum that focuses computation on prompts with high learning potential. The policy is updated with standard PPO-Clip using the tracker's pre-update estimate as an action-independent baseline, preserving gradient unbiasedness while achieving ~50% variance reduction over raw rewards. Being group-free, SPO eliminates synchronization barriers, enabling a 4.35× throughput speedup in simulated agentic settings with long-tail generation times.

## Results

On five hard math benchmarks with Qwen3-8B, SPO achieves an average maj@32 of 63.8, improving +3.4 pp over GRPO (60.4) and +6.7 pp over the base model (57.1). The largest gains appear on the most challenging and least-contaminated datasets: +7.3 pp on BRUMO 25 (64.0 vs. 56.7), +4.4 pp on AIME 25 (76.5 vs. 72.1), and +3.3 pp on HMMT 25 (47.5 vs. 44.2). SPO also outperforms GRPO on pass@k for all evaluated k values, with an average improvement of approximately 2.4 pp, and achieves a 4.35× training throughput speedup in simulated agentic settings by eliminating group synchronization barriers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPO outperforms GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) on maj@32 across all five benchmarks, with the most substantial margin on BRUMO 25 (+7.3 pp) and AIME 25 (+4.4 pp), while avg@32 is more competitive (56.0 vs. 55.7). Against RLOO (Ahmadian et al., 2024), SPO leads by +4.3 pp on AIME 24 and +3.5 pp on AIME 25 (maj@16: 67.20 vs. 62.91 and 55.23 vs. 51.70). SPO also surpasses A*-PO (Brantley et al., 2025), whose static offline-computed value baseline cannot adapt as the policy drifts during tool-integrated training, confirming that an adaptive, KL-aware tracker is critical. Compared to the untrained Qwen3-8B base model, SPO gains +6.7 pp in average maj@32, demonstrating that the single-stream RL procedure itself drives the improvement. On the smaller Qwen3-4B model, SPO still outperforms GRPO by +3.3 pp (AIME 24) and +1.5 pp (AIME 25), indicating the advantage is not scale-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPO departs from the dominant group-based paradigm (GRPO, RLOO, DAPO) that constructs baselines from concurrently generated response groups, instead returning to the classic single-stream policy gradient formulation. It builds on PPO-Clip for the policy update, REINFORCE for the gradient structure, and prioritized experience replay for curriculum sampling, but introduces a KL-adaptive Beta-distribution value tracker as a persistent, temporally-informed baseline that replaces the noisy per-group mean. The paper explicitly positions itself against engineering workarounds like dynamic sampling (DAPO) and static offline value estimation (A*-PO), arguing that a well-designed adaptive tracker with global batch normalization subsumes the need for group synchronization and degenerate-group handling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPO's group-free design eliminates the synchronization bottleneck that cripples group-based methods in agentic and long-horizon settings, with a simulated 4.35× throughput speedup, and removes the 60–80% signal loss from degenerate groups. The finding that GRPO's effective advantage (computed only on non-degenerate samples) actually has *higher* variance than raw no-baseline REINFORCE is a pointed critique of the group baseline's quality. Open questions include whether the gains persist at larger model scales (only Qwen3-8B is tested), whether the Beta tracker generalizes cleanly to non-binary or dense reward settings, and whether the simulated agentic speedup translates to real multi-turn tool-use training. The paper's broader message—that fundamental RL principles can outperform architectural complexity—may influence how the community approaches RLVR algorithm design going forward.
