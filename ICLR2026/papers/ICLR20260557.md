## Problem

Neural operators trained on complex PDEs exhibit correlated yet significantly worse performance on the fundamental physical terms (e.g., diffusion, advection) that underpin those equations, revealing a gap in their understanding of basic physics. This leads to high data demands, physical inconsistency in long-term rollouts, and poor out-of-distribution generalization when physical parameters shift. The paper asks whether explicitly incorporating fundamental physics knowledge into training can address all three limitations simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model consumes ℓ=10 past spatiotemporal solution frames (e.g., 128×128×2 for 2D Diffusion-Reaction, 256×256×3 for 2D Navier-Stokes, 50×50×89×4 for 3D Navier-Stokes, 512×1 for 1D Kuramoto-Sivashinsky) and predicts the next solution frame. During training, the model simultaneously processes both original PDE simulations and their decomposed basic-form simulations at a cost-matched sample mixture ratio (e.g., 1:24 for 2D Navier-Stokes), while all evaluation is conducted exclusively on original PDE data.

## Method

The method decomposes each target PDE into a physically meaningful basic form by retaining dominant dynamics terms and removing stiff or computationally expensive terms (e.g., pure diffusion for Diffusion-Reaction, inviscid advection for Navier-Stokes, linear anti-diffusion+diffusion for Kuramoto-Sivashinsky). A multi-task training formulation then jointly optimizes the neural operator on both the original PDE and its basic form, sharing the entire backbone architecture while using separate final prediction layers for each task. The sample mixture ratio is derived from the relative simulation costs so that replacing original PDE data with cheaper basic-form data maintains a comparable or reduced total simulation budget. The basic form acts as a physically motivated auxiliary task inspired by curriculum learning, providing inductive bias for fundamental transport mechanisms. The approach is architecture-agnostic, demonstrated across FNO, Transformer, and the DPOT foundation model, with a fixed auxiliary loss weight of 0.7 shown to be robust across the 0.5–1.0 range.

## Results

Across four PDE systems (2D Diffusion-Reaction, 2D/3D Navier-Stokes, 1D Kuramoto-Sivashinsky), joint training on the original PDE and its decomposed basic form consistently reduces nRMSE compared to training on the original PDE alone. The largest gains appear in 2D Navier-Stokes, where source nRMSE drops from 0.0487 to 0.0175 (64% reduction) and OOD target nRMSE drops from 0.0825 to 0.0222 (ν=0.05) and 0.0369 to 0.0125 (ν=0.0001). In 3D Navier-Stokes, source nRMSE improves from 0.0675 to 0.0481, and OOD errors at ν=0.0001 drop from 0.0836 to 0.0602. The method also achieves lower nRMSE at every simulation-cost budget (Figure 5), demonstrating that fewer expensive PDE simulations are needed to reach a given accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is a neural operator (FNO or Transformer) trained exclusively on simulations of the original PDE. The proposed joint-training method outperforms this baseline on every PDE and every OOD target setting in Table 2, with the most dramatic improvement in 2D Navier-Stokes (nRMSE reduced by up to 73% at ν=0.0001: 0.0369→0.0125) and 2D Diffusion-Reaction (nRMSE reduced by 30% at Dv/Du=100: 0.0770→0.0538). In the appendix comparison against Lie point symmetry data augmentation (Brandstetter et al., ICML 2022; Mialon et al., 2023), the Lie-transform augmentation alone only marginally improves the baseline on 2D Navier-Stokes, whereas the proposed method achieves substantially lower nRMSE at every simulation-cost budget (Figure 17). Combining Lie-transform augmentation with the proposed joint training yields performance comparable to the proposed method alone, confirming the two techniques are orthogonal and that the fundamental-physics auxiliary task is the dominant source of improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the multiphysics pretraining paradigm of SciML foundation models (MPP, DPOT), which aggregate diverse or weakly related PDE systems, by instead decomposing a single PDE into its fundamental physical terms (e.g., pure diffusion, pure advection) and jointly training on both the full equation and its simplified basic form as a multi-task learning problem. It builds on the neural operator framework (FNO, transformers) and draws conceptual inspiration from curriculum learning and auxiliary task learning, but the specific strategy of using physically decomposed PDE components as cheaper auxiliary training signals is novel. The paper explicitly contrasts with PINNs (which embed PDEs in loss functions) and with Lie symmetry augmentation (which generates new training examples via group transformations), positioning its approach as orthogonal and complementary to both.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for SciML practitioners: the method reduces simulation costs (basic forms are 3–24× cheaper to simulate), improves OOD generalization under parameter shifts, and enhances long-term rollout consistency, all with a simple architecture-agnostic multi-task setup. An important open question is how to systematically and automatically determine which PDE terms constitute the 'fundamental' basic form for arbitrary equations—the paper provides a qualitative procedure and a quantitative RMSE criterion but the choice remains somewhat manual. Whether the benefits persist for highly coupled multi-physics systems (beyond the four PDEs studied) or at substantially larger spatial/temporal scales remains to be explored.
