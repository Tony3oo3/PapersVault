## Problem

Current video foundation models, despite strong imaginative priors from large-scale training, lack explicit 3D grounding, making them unable to preserve real-world spatial structure or directly support downstream 3D reasoning tasks. Existing attempts to couple video generation with 3D perception either require expensive per-scene optimization (NeRF/3DGS), operate the two processes largely independently at inference, or fine-tune the video backbone with a frozen 3D model at substantial computational cost while risking the model's generative capacity.

## Input / Output

The model takes a reference image, an optional text prompt, and a target camera trajectory as input, producing 81-frame videos at 592×336 (or 336×592) in a single forward pass. Alongside the video, the geometry branch outputs task-agnostic 3D features decoded by a custom 3D DPT head into per-frame depth maps, 3D point maps with confidence, and 9D camera pose vectors (rotation, translation, FOV). The DPT head performs 4× temporal upsampling via causal 3D convolutions, expanding 21 latent frames to 81 output frames aligned with the WanVAE temporal resolution.

## Method

FANTASYWORLD splits the frozen Wan2.1 backbone into Preconditioning Blocks (first 16 layers, partially denoising latents to provide geometry-relevant cues) and 24 Integrated Reconstruction and Generation (IRG) Blocks, each containing an asymmetric dual-branch design: an Imagination Prior Branch propagating appearance features and a Geometry-Consistent Branch projecting them into a geometry-aligned latent space. The two branches are coupled via bidirectional cross-attention adapters (MM-BiCrossAttn) with learnable gates, so geometry cues regularize video features for multi-view coherence while video priors inject imaginative signals to complete occluded regions in the 3D field. Training proceeds in two stages: first bridging the geometry branch to Wan hidden features with a lightweight transformer adapter (frozen backbone), then inserting cross-attention adapters for joint co-optimization while keeping all core backbones frozen. A key architectural choice is inverting the conventional DPT reassembly strategy—sourcing fine spatial features from late diffusion blocks where denoising is mature rather than early layers dominated by noise—and inferring geometry directly from video latents rather than RGB, ensuring the 3D pathway operates in the same feature domain as video synthesis.

## Results

On the WorldScore static benchmark (Small camera motion, 1,000 photorealistic samples), FANTASYWORLD achieves the highest scores on all three consistency metrics: 3D Consistency 83.31±14.24, Photo Consistency 86.11±7.97, and Style Consistency 94.22±9.11, while also yielding the lowest standard deviation across samples. Under the more challenging Large camera motion setting (wide orbital/panning trajectories up to 90°), FANTASYWORLD maintains 74.83 3D Consistency and 82.02 Style Consistency, substantially outperforming all baselines. For geometric fidelity, 3DGS reconstruction on RealEstate10K shows PSNR 28.24, SSIM 0.86, and LPIPS 0.14 with the full model (VGGT initialization), versus 26.89/0.84/0.17 without the geometry branch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FANTASYWORLD outperforms all four baselines on the three core consistency metrics under both Small and Large camera motion settings. Against WonderWorld (the closest competitor on 3D Consistency at 82.85), FANTASYWORLD leads by 0.46 points but with a markedly lower variance (±14.24 vs. ±19.69), and dominates on Photo Consistency (86.11 vs. 67.86) and Style Consistency (94.22 vs. 55.79). Uni3C is competitive on Photo Consistency (85.48) and Style Consistency (88.32) but trails on 3D Consistency (78.59 vs. 83.31) and shows much higher variance. Voyager is the weakest baseline, scoring only 56.00 on 3D Consistency and collapsing to 13.82 under Large motion, attributed to its first-frame point-cloud prior falling out of view. FANTASYWORLD intentionally trades off Camera Control (57.05 vs. WonderWorld's 92.32) and Object Control, as its design prioritizes geometric consistency over explicit camera manipulation; the authors show in Appendix A.6 that a post-hoc 3DGS reconstruction step can recover 90.71 Camera Control while maintaining 88.88 3D Consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

FANTASYWORLD sits at the intersection of camera-guided video diffusion (Wan2.1) and feed-forward 3D reconstruction (VGGT), but departs from both lines in a key architectural way: rather than fine-tuning the video backbone to absorb geometric supervision (as Geometry Forcing does) or running a separate 3D reconstruction pipeline after generation (as Voyager and AETHER do), it keeps the video model entirely frozen and attaches a trainable geometry branch coupled via bidirectional cross-attention. The Preconditioning Blocks concept—reusing early frozen denoiser layers to partially denoise latents before geometry processing—and the inverted DPT head that sources features from deeper (less noisy) diffusion blocks rather than shallower ones are novel departures from standard DPT and VGGT designs. The depth loss adapts Video Depth Anything's temporal gradient matching, and the camera/point-map losses follow VGGT's formulation, making the geometry branch a direct descendant of the VGGT family while operating in the Wan latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frozen-backbone-plus-lightweight-geometry-branch design offers a practical path to 3D-consistent world generation without the compute cost of fine-tuning large video models or the per-scene optimization overhead of NeRF/3DGS, which is significant for scaling world models to embodied AI applications. However, the current instantiation is limited to fixed-length clips (81 frames) and quasi-static scenes, leaving long-horizon synthesis and fully dynamic 4D environments as open problems that will require caching/streaming mechanisms and time-conditioned geometry fields. The paper also leaves open whether the implicit 3D features truly generalize to downstream tasks (novel view synthesis, navigation) without per-task adaptation, a claim supported only indirectly by reference to AnySplat rather than by direct experiments.
