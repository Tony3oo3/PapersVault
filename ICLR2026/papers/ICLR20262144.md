## Problem

Existing deep learning weather forecasters predominantly treat atmospheric states as 2D spatial-temporal videos, failing to explicitly model pressure-level-aware 3D dynamics (e.g., vertical coupling, inter-level energy transfer) and lacking explicit incorporation of fundamental physical constraints such as momentum and mass conservation. This limits their ability to capture short-term weather evolutions driven by convective processes and 3D atmospheric motion governed by the Navier-Stokes equations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262144/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes three consecutive timesteps of 3D atmospheric fields (surface variables at 1 level plus upper-air variables at P pressure levels, e.g., 7 or 13 levels), along with static variables (land-sea mask, orography, latitude) and spatial-temporal embeddings encoding day/season and longitude/latitude. It produces forecasts at N future timesteps (Δt = 1 h per ODE step) for all prognostic variables—geopotential, temperature, wind components, specific humidity, and relative humidity—across all pressure levels, evaluated at 5.625°, 1.40625°, and 0.25° ERA5 resolutions. The continuous-time ODE formulation allows prediction at arbitrary lead times rather than fixed intervals.

## Method

DeepPrim formulates weather evolution as a Neural ODE system where an intermediate motion field v* drives advection of all prognostic variables, and a force network learns the Navier-Stokes force term. The force network (3D-BiViT) uses a two-stage attention mechanism: intra-pressure-level self-attention models horizontal dynamics within each pressure level, while cross-pressure-level self-attention models vertical coupling across levels, with separate query/key/value projections for surface and upper-air tokens to handle their distinct physical characteristics. An initialization network (ResNet) estimates v*(t₀) from temporal variations and 3D spatial gradients of the input, and a source-sink network (ResNet) corrects the integrated ODE solutions by modeling the total resolved and unresolved physical processes (radiation, condensation, turbulent mixing) as a learnable residual rather than fixed parameterization schemes. This design replaces empirical parameterization with data-driven learning while preserving the structural constraints of the primitive equations.

## Results

On the 5.625° ERA5 dataset, DeepPrim achieves the best latitude-weighted RMSE across all five target variables (z500, t850, t2m, u10, v10) at lead times of 6–24 h, outperforming ClimODE by 38.6% and pre-trained ClimaX by 10.7% on average. At 1.40625° resolution, DeepPrim surpasses IFS on t850, t2m, and u10, and outperforms pre-trained ClimaX by 36.11% on average while using only 1/20 the parameters of WeatherGFT. On the 0.25° dataset, DeepPrim achieves the best overall performance, particularly in wind speed prediction (u10: 0.76 vs. GraphCast 0.81, Pangu 0.91 at 24 h). In regional forecasting, DeepPrim suppresses the second-best baseline by 35.8% on average across North America, South America, and Australia.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the gold-standard NWP system IFS (ECMWF 2023), DeepPrim is broadly comparable at 5.625° but outperforms IFS on t2m and v10; at 1.40625°, DeepPrim surpasses IFS on t850, t2m, and u10. DeepPrim outperforms ClimODE (Verma et al., 2024) by 38.6% on average at 5.625°, the closest physics-informed Neural ODE baseline. Against pre-trained ClimaX (Nguyen et al., 2023), DeepPrim achieves 10.7% improvement at 5.625° and 36.11% at 1.40625°. At 0.25° resolution, DeepPrim outperforms both Pangu (Bi et al., 2023) and GraphCast (Lam et al., 2023) overall, though GraphCast retains an edge on z500 due to its graph-based global interaction modeling. At 1.40625°, DeepPrim matches or exceeds WeatherGFT (Xu et al., 2024) with only 23M vs. 470M parameters, and achieves competitive performance with NeuralGCM (Kochkov et al., 2024) while using a more explicit 3D pressure-level attention design. FourCastNet (Pathak et al., 2022) is substantially outperformed across all variables and resolutions, reflecting the advantage of physics-informed continuous-time modeling over fixed-lead-time Fourier operators.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeepPrim extends the physics-informed Neural ODE paradigm introduced by ClimODE (Verma et al., 2024) by replacing its continuity-equation-inspired advection with an explicit learning of the full Navier-Stokes force term via a novel 3D-BiViT architecture. It departs from prior 3D weather models like Pangu (unified attention treating pressure as geometric height) and NeuralGCM (local per-column networks) by explicitly separating intra-pressure-level horizontal dynamics from cross-pressure-level vertical coupling through dedicated attention stages. The source-sink network further distinguishes DeepPrim from both NWP parameterization schemes and purely data-driven forecasters by learning resolved and unresolved processes jointly as a learnable correction to the ODE solution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly encoding primitive equations (rather than relying on statistical correlations) yields substantial gains in short-term forecasting, with the 3D pressure-level-aware modeling contributing a verified 12.3% performance improvement. Practical impact is underscored by deployment in the operational Baguan system and open-source availability. However, the approach is limited to short-term (≤24h) forecasting, the source-sink network remains a learned correction without explicit conservation guarantees, and at 0.25° resolution DeepPrim does not surpass GraphCast on geopotential, suggesting the physics-informed advantage may diminish at higher spatial fidelity. Open questions include whether the ODE framework can be extended to medium-range forecasting with rolling inference, and whether rigorous stability and conservation analyses can be incorporated.
