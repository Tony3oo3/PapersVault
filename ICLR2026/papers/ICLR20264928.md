## Problem

Safety alignment in LLMs is brittle: benign fine-tuning on new tasks causes safety guardrails to collapse, and improving safety incurs an alignment tax that degrades utility. Prior work treated safety alignment as a subset of general instruction-following, obscuring its distinct mechanism and making it difficult to explain why so few parameters appear sufficient for safety or why those parameters are so easily overwritten during adaptation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a safety-aligned LLM (LLaMA2-7B-Chat or LLaMA3-8B-Instruct), a calibration corpus split into a safety subset (420 harmful prompts from AdvBench_attr) and a utility subset (Alpaca-Cleaned with safety pairs filtered out), and a downstream fine-tuning dataset (Alpaca, Dolly, or GSM8K). It produces a per-neuron/channel categorization into four attribute groups (SCU, UCU, CU, RU) at the granularity of inner channels in depth-2 modules f(X)=Bσ(AX), a modified model with selected units frozen for downstream adaptation, and paired safety (ASR on AdvBench_eval, HEx-PHI harm scores) and utility (zero-shot accuracy on six LM-Harness tasks) metrics.

## Method

The paper proposes the Superficial Safety Alignment Hypothesis (SSAH), which reframes safety alignment as an implicit binary classification task—teaching an otherwise unsafe model to choose the correct reasoning direction (fulfill vs. refuse) plus a standardized refusal mechanism—rather than as a general instruction-following problem. To validate this, the authors design cosine-distance probing experiments that compare hidden-state trajectories for clean malicious queries, query+benign tokens, and query+malicious tokens across all Transformer blocks, showing aligned models exhibit a consistent safe-reasoning preference from early layers onward. For the 'less is more' implication, they compute per-channel importance scores I_U and I_S via activation variance over the calibration sets, then systematically prune units ranked by I_U+I_S (to find RUs) and by I_S−I_U (to separate SCUs from UCUs), discovering that only ~1.3–1.4% of neurons are exclusively safety-critical. The practical intervention freezes all SCUs plus the top 6% of CUs during downstream fine-tuning, which significantly reduces attack success rate degradation without hurting utility. Finally, they demonstrate that repurposing the ~20% redundant units as an 'alignment budget'—fine-tuning only RUs while freezing everything else—achieves comparable alignment quality with zero alignment tax, particularly on mathematical reasoning.

## Results

The paper identifies that Safety Critical Units (SCU) account for only 1.3–1.4% of total computing units in Llama2-7B-Chat and Llama3-8B-Instruct, yet pruning them causes safety ASR to jump from ~10% to 66–87% while utility degrades minimally (−1.3 to −3.6 avg accuracy). Freezing all SCU plus the top 6% of Complex Units during fine-tuning reduces AdvBench keyword ASR from 5.3% (full fine-tuned) to 2.96% on Llama2-7B-Chat (Alpaca) and from 61.15% to 51.38% on Llama3-8B-Instruct (Dolly), with HEx-PHI GPT-4 harm scores dropping from 1.79 to 1.39 and 2.13 to 2.0 respectively. Repurposing only the 20% Redundant Units for SFT on Alpaca achieves comparable alignment (MT-Bench 3.5 vs. 2.83 first-turn) while *improving* GSM8K from 9.24 to 13.4 (+4.16), eliminating the alignment tax that full-parameter fine-tuning incurs (−0.44).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed freeze-SCU+CU strategy consistently outperforms all five baselines on safety preservation. Against PEFT methods (Table 5), the method achieves a harm score of 1.26 vs. LoRA's 2.18, LLaMA-Adapter's 2.38, and Prefix Tuning's 2.20 on Alpaca (HEx-PHI GPT-4, 1–5 scale), with high-harmfulness rates of 4.5% vs. 25.2%, 26.4%, and 24.8% respectively—roughly a 5–6× reduction in safety degradation. Against the neuron-level safety-freezing baseline of Zhao et al. (2025) (Tables 7–8), the method achieves 2.96% AdvBench keyword ASR vs. Zhao's 5.19% on Alpaca and 1.92% vs. 3.85% on GSM8K, with the gap widening when Zhao's budget is scaled to 6.3% (4.42% and 2.88%). Against the layer-level baseline of Li et al. (2024b) under a matched 28.1% parameter budget (Tables 9–10), the method achieves 2.30% vs. 3.85% ASR on Alpaca and 1.73% vs. 2.50% on GSM8K, with HEx-PHI GPT-4 scores of 1.26 vs. 1.41 and 1.31 vs. 1.43. The paper attributes its advantage to (i) a maximal-margin identification criterion on I_safety − I_utility (vs. Zhao's minimal knockout set) and (ii) finer neuron/channel-level granularity (vs. Li's coarse block-level over-rejection proxy). Utility is preserved: Table 3 shows the freezing method matches or slightly exceeds full fine-tuning on all 10 downstream tasks (e.g., MMLU 47.94 vs. 46.82 for Llama2-7B-Chat).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Superficial Alignment Hypothesis (SAH) from Zhou et al. (2024, LIMA) from general alignment to the safety-specific domain, proposing that safety alignment reduces to an implicit binary classification task (fulfill vs. refuse) rather than teaching new knowledge. Methodologically, it builds on structured pruning techniques from An et al. (2024) and Li et al. (2024a) but repurposes them as a diagnostic tool for identifying neuron-level safety-critical units, moving beyond the weight-level granularity of Wei et al. (2024). The attribute-based categorization framework (SCU/UCU/CU/RU) and the freeze-SCU/fine-tune-RU intervention distinguish this work from prior safety-neuron identification efforts (Zhao et al. 2025, Li et al. 2024b) by using a maximal-margin criterion on I_safety − I_utility rather than minimal knockout sets or layer-level proxies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic account of why safety is brittle: fine-tuning converts safety-critical units into utility-critical units, and the practical remedy (freezing ~1.3% SCUs plus top 6% CUs) is simple and effective across multiple attack benchmarks. The counterintuitive finding that PEFT methods (LoRA, Prefix Tuning) degrade safety more than full fine-tuning challenges the assumption that updating fewer parameters is inherently safer. Key open questions include whether the 'superficial' framing adequately explains jailbreak resistance at each token step (the authors propose a non-superficial extension but do not fully validate it), whether the neuron-level identification generalizes beyond the LLaMA 7B–8B family, and whether the RU-as-alignment-budget strategy scales to larger models or more complex alignment objectives.
