## Problem

Test-time scaling via soft best-of-n sampling from a large target model is prohibitively expensive, while existing reward-guided speculative decoding (RSD) provides no guarantees on the induced policy distribution. The paper asks whether a small draft model can be used to efficiently approximate the optimal reward-tilted policy of a large target model with provable distributional fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262543/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates stepwise on chain-of-thought reasoning steps (delimited by double line breaks) rather than individual tokens, with a process reward model scoring each step in [0,1]. At each step, n draft reasoning steps are generated in parallel from π_S and verified against π_B via a single forward pass for log-likelihood computation, avoiding autoregressive generation from the target. Generation continues until an EOS token is produced, yielding a complete reasoning trace and a final answer extracted via \boxed{}.

## Method

The key insight is rewriting the optimal tilted distribution π_{β,B}(y|x) ∝ π_B(y|x)exp(βr(x,y)) as a distribution over the draft model π_S tilted by modified rewards r̃(x,y) = r(x,y) + (1/β)log(π_B(y|x)/π_S(y|x)), enabling soft best-of-n sampling from the cheap draft model to approximate the target's optimal policy. The log-likelihood ratio is computed via a single parallel forward pass of π_B rather than autoregressive generation, preserving full parallelizability. A rejection threshold u on the tilted reward triggers fallback to soft best-of-n from π_B when the best draft sample falls below it, improving empirical accuracy. The paper proves a KL bound (Theorem 1) showing the approximation error vanishes as n grows under a coverage assumption, and a reward convergence guarantee (Theorem 2) at rate O(1/√n), making GSI the first speculative test-time scaling method with distributional guarantees to the optimal tilted policy.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Math (1.5B/7B), GSI achieves 60.0% average accuracy at n=16 across five reasoning benchmarks, outperforming RSD (58.7%) and S-BoN with the draft model (57.8%), while approaching S-BoN with the target model (62.1%). On Qwen3 (1.7B/14B), the gap widens: GSI reaches 66.9% average at n=16 versus RSD (60.9%) and S-BoN-small (58.5%), closing most of the gap to S-BoN-base (69.4%). In terms of latency, GSI generates up to 51% more reasoning steps per second than S-BoN with the target model (Qwen3, n=16), translating to up to 28% reduced end-to-end latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSI consistently outperforms RSD (Liao et al., 2025) across all five benchmarks and both model families. On Qwen3 at n=16, GSI achieves 66.9% average accuracy versus RSD's 60.9% (a 6-point gap), with the largest individual gap on MATH500 (84.3% vs 78.5%). The paper attributes RSD's weaker performance to its near-100% acceptance rate, which causes it to effectively collapse into S-BoN with the draft model. Against S-BoN with the small model, GSI's advantage is even more pronounced: 66.9% vs 58.5% on Qwen3 (n=16), a gap of over 8 points, driven by the tilted-reward mechanism that corrects draft model errors. Against S-BoN with the target model (the theoretical upper bound), GSI narrows the gap to 2.5 points on Qwen3 (n=16) and 2.1 points on Qwen2.5-Math (n=16), and on some individual datasets (e.g., MATH500 at n=256) GSI matches or slightly exceeds the target-model baseline. In terms of latency, GSI is significantly faster than S-BoN with the target model (0.83 vs 0.55 steps/s on Qwen3, n=16) while maintaining most of the accuracy, making it a practical speed-accuracy trade-off that neither RSD nor S-BoN-small can match.

## Relation to Prior Work

GSI sits at the intersection of speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023), soft best-of-n sampling (Verdun et al., 2025), and reward-guided speculative decoding (Liao et al., 2025). The key conceptual departure is the 'reward-likelihood tilt': by rewriting the target model's tilted distribution π_{β,B} as a distribution over the draft model π_S with modified rewards r̃ = r + (1/β)log(π_B/π_S), GSI makes soft best-of-n over draft samples provably approximate the optimal tilted policy. Unlike RSD, which only guarantees expected reward improvement, and SPECS, whose KL bound requires asymptotic assumptions on block size and random thresholds, GSI provides a finite-n KL bound to the tilted distribution under a standard coverage assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GSI is the first speculative test-time scaling method with a distributional guarantee to the optimal tilted policy, bridging a gap between the theoretical ideal (sampling from π_{β,B}) and practical latency constraints. The empirical finding that a 1.5B/1.7B draft model can match or occasionally exceed the 7B/14B target model's own S-BoN accuracy while reducing latency by up to 28% makes this practically relevant for deployment. Open questions include whether adaptive threshold schedules {u_n} can further improve the accuracy-latency trade-off, whether the approach extends to one-shot generation beyond stepwise reasoning, and how to reduce the significant runtime cost of the PRM, which currently dominates end-to-end latency.
