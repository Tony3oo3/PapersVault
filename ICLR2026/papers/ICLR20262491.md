## Problem

Test-time compute scaling via Best-of-N requires reliable verification of candidate solutions, but small language models (sLMs) fail at verification steps that demand memorization—multi-step arithmetic, fact-checking—because their limited parameter capacity cannot encode all necessary calculation facts. Prior work sidestepped this by using a larger model as the verifier, which undermines the deployment efficiency that motivates sLMs in the first place. The paper asks whether sLMs can verify their own outputs reliably, and if not, what mechanism can close the gap without scaling up parameters.

## Input / Output

Given a problem and N=64 candidate solutions sampled from an sLM policy, the method outputs a single selected solution whose score is the product of a binary tool-based filter (0 or 1) and a scalar reward-model score. For mathematical reasoning, the sLM generates a Python verification script that a code interpreter executes to produce the binary signal; for knowledge-intensive tasks, BM25 retrieves three Wikipedia abstracts that the sLM uses to check factual consistency of claims in the solution. The final selection is argmax over candidates of f(x,y;T,θ) × r(x,y;θ), where f is the tool filter and r is the PRM or GenRM score.

## Method

T1 is a two-stage verification framework: an external tool first acts as a hard rejection filter (ToolV), and a distilled reward model then scores the surviving candidates. In the ToolV stage, the sLM generates a tool-calling query (Python code for math, or the problem-solution pair for knowledge retrieval), the tool executes, and the sLM extracts a binary pass/fail verdict; in the RM stage, a distilled PRM or GenRM assigns a continuous score to the remaining candidates. Both stages are trained via knowledge distillation from GPT-4o-mini (or Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B for PRM) using separate LoRA adapters per verifier role, so the same base sLM serves as generator, tool caller, and reward model. The paper proves that tool integration reduces the required memorization from Ω(M³) bits of mutual information to 0, and that the two-stage design monotonically increases Best-of-N accuracy by improving the effective verifier noise level, as formalized in Theorem 5.3.

## Results

On MATH500 (N=64, weighted Best-of-N), ToolV+PRM with Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct achieves 53.2% accuracy, outperforming the Llama-3.1-8B model (reported at N=1 greedy decoding) and surpassing distilled PRM alone (47.4%) by 5.8 points. With GenRM, the same 1B model reaches 50.6% (vs. 45.4% for distilled GenRM alone). On GSM8K, ToolV+GenRM with Llama-3.2-1B achieves 50.6% at N=64, and the 0.5B Qwen2.5 model matches the 1.5B model's performance using only 16 solutions. The compute overhead of ToolV is modest: the extra code-generation tokens correspond to only a 1.14× increase in verifier compute, and performance gains persist even after normalizing the x-axis for this budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ToolV+PRM (53.2%) outperforms distilled PRM alone (47.4%) by 5.8 points and majority voting (45.8%) by 7.4 points on MATH500 at N=64 with Llama-3.2-1B. Against Themis (Li et al., 2024), a unified tool-augmented reward model, both ToolV+GenRM and ToolV+PRM achieve higher weighted Best-of-N accuracy (Figure 9), confirming that the two-stage design is better suited for math reasoning with small models. The headline result is that Llama-3.2-1B with ToolV surpasses Llama-3.1-8B (N=1 greedy), a 8× larger model, on MATH500. On GSM8K, gains are smaller (e.g., 50.6% vs. 45.4% for Llama-3.2-1B with GenRM) because the task is simpler and existing verifiers already perform more reliably. Zero-shot GenRM without distillation performs nearly identically to majority voting, confirming that sLMs cannot verify without fine-tuning, and that ToolV's gains are not merely from additional compute (a two-stage GenRM without tools in Table 5 yields only 46.0% vs. 50.6% with ToolV).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of test-time compute scaling (best-of-N, Cobbe et al. 2021), tool-augmented language models (PAL, Gao et al. 2023; TORA, Gou et al. 2024b), and reward modeling (PRM, Wang et al. 2024a; GenRM, Zhang et al. 2024). The key departure from prior work is the explicit identification of memorization as the bottleneck preventing sLMs from serving as reliable verifiers, and the two-stage design that offloads memorization-heavy steps (arithmetic, fact-checking) to external tools before the sLM performs final scoring. Unlike Themis (Li et al. 2024), which integrates tools into a unified reward model for 7B+ models, T1 targets sub-1B models and separates tool-based filtering from reward-model scoring, enabling compatibility with both PRM and GenRM architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant for resource-constrained deployment: a 1B model with tool-integrated verification can match or exceed an 8B model on MATH500, suggesting that tool integration may be a more parameter-efficient path to reliable verification than simply scaling the verifier. However, ToolV functions only as a rejection filter and cannot recover from false negatives (correct solutions wrongly rejected), and the evaluation is limited to best-of-N parallel scaling on math and a few knowledge domains. Open questions include whether the two-stage design extends to sequential test-time scaling, step-level search, or domains where the relevant 'tool' is less clearly defined than a code interpreter.
