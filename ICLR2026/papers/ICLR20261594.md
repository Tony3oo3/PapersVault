## Problem

Existing task-aware context compression methods select tokens based solely on query relevance, ignoring the high semantic redundancy among top-relevant tokens (with pairwise similarity exceeding 0.6). This over-similarity misleads LLMs into producing erroneous outputs, and existing dynamic compression-rate allocation mechanisms fail to account for redundancy among compression units, leading to repeated retention of similar information and degraded performance under high compression rates.

## Input / Output

COMI takes a long context (up to 32K tokens) and a natural-language query as input, and produces a compressed soft-token representation at a specified compression ratio (16× or 32×), which is then decoded into a generated answer or summary. The query is first average-pooled into a single vector used throughout compression, and the compressed representation is passed through a Layer Semantic Alignment (LSA) module before decoding. Training samples are drawn from five QA and summarization datasets with token lengths capped at 32K.

## Method

COMI introduces Marginal Information Gain (MIG), defined as the cosine similarity between a unit and the query minus its maximum cosine similarity to any other unit, jointly capturing relevance and semantic uniqueness. The framework operates in two stages: (1) Coarse-Grained Group Reallocation, where the encoded context is split into equal-length segments, inter-group MIG is computed, and compression budgets are dynamically reallocated via a softmax of negative MIG so that high-information segments receive lower compression rates; (2) Fine-Grained Token Merging, where tokens within each segment are fused into a single compressed token using softmax-weighted averaging by intra-group MIG, ensuring key semantics are preserved while redundant content is suppressed. Training fine-tunes the encoder, LSA, and the decoder's W_Q, W_K, W_V, W_O matrices end-to-end with cross-entropy loss on the compressed representation, using randomly sampled compression rates per training sample.

## Results

Under 32× compression with Qwen2-7B-Instruct, COMI achieves 49.15 EM on NaturalQuestions, 48.89 EM on 2WikiMQA, 40.46 EM on HotpotQA, and 11.18 EM on NarrativeQA, outperforming the next-best baseline (GMSA) by approximately 25 points on NaturalQuestions. Under 16× compression with the same backbone, COMI reaches 56.31 EM on NaturalQuestions and 52.13 EM on 2WikiMQA. On MultiNews summarization, COMI attains 33.60 F1 at 32× compression, surpassing the original prompt (31.42 F1). COMI also achieves >2× end-to-end speedup over the original prompt at 32× compression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COMI outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and compression rate. Against the closest competitor GMSA (same encoder-decoder family), COMI gains ~25 EM points on NaturalQuestions and ~20 on 2WikiMQA at 32× with Qwen2-7B, demonstrating that MIG-guided reallocation and token merging substantially improve over uniform group merging. Against Activation Beacon (the strongest KV-cache baseline), COMI leads by ~40 EM on NaturalQuestions at 32×, though Activation Beacon remains competitive on 2WikiMQA (38.79 vs. 48.89 EM). Hard prompt compression methods (LongLLMLingua, LLMLingua-2-large) and soft prompt compression (ICAE) all fall well behind COMI, with LLMLingua-2-large particularly weak on HotpotQA (2.26 EM vs. COMI's 40.46). KV-cache methods (StreamLLM, SnapKV) show the largest gaps, with SnapKV scoring 0.00 EM on NarrativeQA at 32×. Notably, COMI even surpasses the original (uncompressed) prompt on MultiNews summarization (33.60 vs. 31.42 F1), indicating that redundancy removal can improve generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

COMI extends the GMSA encoder-decoder framework (Tang et al., 2025b) by replacing its uniform group-merging strategy with a two-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline guided by a new Marginal Information Gain (MIG) metric. The MIG formulation directly adapts the Maximal Marginal Relevance (MMR) principle from information retrieval to the token-compression setting, combining query relevance with a redundancy penalty that prior task-aware methods (LongLLMLingua, Activation Beacon) and task-agnostic methods (ICAE, LLMLingua-2) both lack. The training paradigm follows RADIT's joint instruction-tuning approach, but applied to compressed soft-token representations rather than raw text.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~25-point EM gain over GMSA under 32× compression and the finding that COMI outperforms the full original prompt even on a native 256K model (Qwen3-4B) suggest that redundancy-aware compression can be strictly beneficial rather than merely a cost-saving tradeoff. Open questions include whether MIG can be applied training-free or within KV-cache compression (which would broaden applicability), whether the encoder-decoder bottleneck is necessary versus in-place token merging, and how the method scales beyond 64K contexts. A practical limitation is that COMI requires a dedicated fine-tuning run per backbone, unlike training-free baselines such as SnapKV or LongLLMLingua.
