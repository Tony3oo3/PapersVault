## Problem

In the metric distortion framework for voting, the best achievable distortion for any deterministic single-winner mechanism is 3, and even randomized mechanisms cannot go below ~2.112. This paper asks whether selecting a committee of k > 1 candidates—where each voter's cost is the distance to their closest committee member—can beat the optimal single-candidate benchmark, and introduces the notion of 1-distortion (committee cost divided by optimal single-candidate cost) to formalize this bi-criteria comparison.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264606/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The mechanism receives only ordinal preference profiles (each voter's ranking of all m candidates) with no access to the underlying metric distances, and must output a committee of k candidates. The 1-distortion guarantee is the worst-case ratio of the committee's social cost (sum or max of voter-to-nearest-committee-member distances) to the cost of the single best candidate, taken over all metric realizations consistent with the given rankings. The paper distinguishes results by metric space type (line, 2D Euclidean, tree) and committee size k, with the key structural constraint that the algorithm must be deterministic and ranking-based.

## Method

For the line metric, the paper first develops an ordering algorithm (Section A) that recovers the relative positions of voters and candidates from ordinal preferences alone, using a pivot-based SplitLine procedure that iteratively determines which side of a midpoint each candidate lies on, then sorts candidates and voters lexicographically. For the sum-cost objective, selecting the two candidates immediately adjacent to the median voter guarantees the committee contains an optimal candidate (1-distortion = 1), proven via a median-sliding argument and a triangle-inequality comparison showing one extreme candidate is never strictly better than the middle. For the max-cost objective, the leftmost and rightmost voters serve as anchors: selecting their closest candidates gives 1-distortion 2, adding one inward candidate gives 3/2, and adding one on each side gives 1. For general metrics, a simple rule selecting all but the least popular top-choice candidate achieves 1-distortion 1+2/(m-1), and matching lower-bound constructions in 2D Euclidean and tree metrics (using m+1 indistinguishable instances with shifted voter-candidate pairs) prove this is tight, establishing a stark separation between the line and higher-dimensional settings.

## Results

On the line metric, a 2-candidate committee achieves 1-distortion of 1 for the sum-cost (utilitarian) objective, matching the optimal single-candidate cost exactly, while a 4-candidate committee achieves 1-distortion of 1 for the max-cost (egalitarian) objective. For the max-cost on the line, a smooth trade-off is established: 2 candidates give distortion 2, 3 candidates give 1.5, and 4 candidates give 1. For general metrics (2D Euclidean or tree), selecting m−1 out of m candidates achieves a tight 1-distortion of 1+2/(m−1) for sum-cost, and no algorithm can beat distortion 3 for max-cost even with m−1 candidates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the classical single-winner (k=1) metric distortion bounds. Anshelevich et al. (2018) established a lower bound of 3 for any deterministic mechanism on the line metric, and Gkatzelis et al. (FOCS'20) proved that 3 is also the tight upper bound for any metric. This paper shows that by moving to a bi-criteria setting (selecting k>1 candidates but benchmarking against the best single candidate), the line metric admits a 1-distortion of 1 with just 2 candidates for sum-cost and 4 candidates for max-cost, strictly improving upon the inescapable factor of 3 for single-winner elections. For the 2D Euclidean and tree metrics, the paper proves that even selecting m−1 out of m candidates cannot beat 1+2/(m−1) for sum-cost or 3 for max-cost, establishing a stark separation between the line metric and higher-dimensional metrics that does not exist in the single-winner setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces a bi-criteria perspective to the metric distortion framework, where a mechanism selects k candidates but is benchmarked against the optimal single candidate rather than the optimal k-candidate committee. It builds on Elkind & Faliszewski (2014) for recovering voter/candidate orderings from ordinal preferences on a line, and on Anshelevich & Postl (2017) for the median-voter characterization of optimal single-winner candidates. The formulation deliberately departs from Caragiannis et al. (2022), who showed unbounded distortion when comparing k-committee cost to optimal k-committee cost, by instead using the single-candidate optimum as the baseline, which yields meaningful finite guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a sharp structural separation: on the line metric, O(1) candidates achieve distortion 1 for both utilitarian and egalitarian objectives, while in 2D Euclidean or tree metrics even selecting m−1 of m candidates cannot beat distortion 1+2/(m−1) (sum) or 3 (max). This reveals that the line metric is fundamentally easier for bi-criteria committee selection than any higher-dimensional or tree-structured space. Open questions include characterizing which metric spaces admit constant-size optimal committees, whether randomization can improve the 2D/tree lower bounds, and whether limited cardinal information (beyond pure ordinal rankings) can bridge the gap.
