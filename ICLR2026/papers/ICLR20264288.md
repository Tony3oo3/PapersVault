## Problem

Forecasting how objects in a scene will move from a single image is highly ambiguous, yet prior approaches either regress a single trajectory (ignoring uncertainty) or rely on video generators that struggle with physical consistency even in simple scenarios like falling blocks. The paper asks whether directly generating point trajectories—rather than generating pixels and extracting motion—can more effectively capture the distribution of plausible futures while respecting physical invariants like rigidity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264288/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (H×W×3) and produces a quasi-dense grid of 2D point trajectories over T timesteps (T ∈ {16, 24, 30}), with points sampled at stride s (e.g., every other pixel), yielding a tensor of shape (H/s)×(W/s)×T×2. At inference, multiple samples are drawn from the conditional distribution p(X|I) to represent forecasting uncertainty. The image also serves as auxiliary input to the trajectory VAE encoder and decoder to help reason about object boundaries and geometry, and DINOv2 patch features from the image condition the denoiser via cross-attention at every block.

## Method

The approach is a two-stage latent generative model: (1) a trajectory VAE with a spatio-temporal transformer encoder/decoder maps dense trajectory grids to a compact latent code (spatially downsampled by factor r, with latent dimension D), trained with a β-VAE objective using Huber reconstruction loss; (2) a rectified flow denoiser learns the conditional distribution p(Z|I) by predicting velocity fields along straight paths from noise to data in the latent space. Both components share a Latte-derived architecture that alternates spatial and temporal attention blocks, extended with learnable gated cross-attention to DINOv2 image tokens at every block. The key design insight is that generating trajectories directly injects inductive biases for object permanence and temporal coherence, avoiding the capacity overhead of RGB synthesis; ablations confirm that without the VAE, the denoiser collapses to a single mode, and switching the output modality from RGB to trajectories at matched latent dimensionality substantially improves both distributional fidelity and rigidity.

## Results

On Kubric (in-distribution), the proposed method (Ours-L) achieves FVMD 13,745, FVMD(S) 17,838, Best-of-K MSE 127.0, and LRTL 14.1, outperforming all video generators (WAN 14B: FVMD 34,573; SVD: 30,173) and the trajectory generator Track2Act (FVMD 16,735, Best-of-K 250.8). On LIBERO-10, the method reduces MSE from 69.96 (ATM) to 35.93 (Min, k=8) on the effector view. On Cityscapes, Ours(L) achieves MeanT MSE of 1,475.68 versus 2,650.59 (WAN 1.3B) and 7,037.88 (Track2Act). On Physics101, the method achieves overall MSE 28.62 versus 30.08 for fine-tuned WAN 1.3B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all eight baselines across every metric on Kubric. Against the closest trajectory generator Track2Act, Ours(L) nearly halves the Best-of-K MSE (127.0 vs. 250.8) and improves FVMD by ~18%, attributed to spatio-temporal cross-attention with DINOv2 patch tokens versus Track2Act's single-vector ResNet18 conditioning. Against the strongest video generator WAN 14B (no fine-tuning), the method achieves 2.5× lower FVMD (13,745 vs. 34,573) and 2.5× lower LRTL (14.1 vs. 35.1), indicating far better physical rigidity. Even after fine-tuning video generators on Kubric (SVD†, WAN 1.3B†), the method still wins on all four metrics, with the largest gap in LRTL (14.1 vs. 26.6–30.1), confirming that RGB generation introduces inherent motion overhead. On LIBERO, the method beats both regression baselines ATM and Tra-MoE by 30–50% MSE in the best-case (Min) setting. On Cityscapes, Ours(L) reduces MeanT MSE by 44% over fine-tuned WAN 1.3B and by 79% over Track2Act, with WAN frequently hallucinating during turns while Track2Act ignores distant scene objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on image-based motion forecasting (ATM, Tra-MoE, Track2Act) by shifting from sparse, regression-based prediction of a few active points to dense, generative prediction of a full trajectory grid. Architecturally, it adapts the spatio-temporal transformer design from Latte (a video diffusion model) and the rectified flow / flow matching framework from Lipman et al. and Liu et al., but repurposes them to generate point coordinates rather than pixel values. The key conceptual departure from prior trajectory predictors is the use of a VAE latent space plus conditional flow matching to model the distribution over possible futures, and the key departure from video generators is the argument that trajectories are a more appropriate output modality than RGB pixels for capturing motion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the widely held assumption that large-scale video generators serve as effective world models for physical reasoning, showing that even billion-parameter models fine-tuned on a domain still produce physically implausible motion (object splitting, shape distortion) in simple scenarios. The clean modality ablation (same architecture, RGB vs. trajectories) provides strong evidence that the limitation is representational rather than computational, suggesting that future world models should consider motion-native representations. Open questions include whether the approach scales to complex, long-horizon real-world dynamics beyond simple rigid-body physics, and whether the dense-grid formulation remains practical at higher resolutions or longer horizons.
