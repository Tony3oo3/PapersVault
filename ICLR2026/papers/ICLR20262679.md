## Problem

Existing 3D RNA inverse design methods optimize and evaluate using native sequence recovery (NSR), which is a limited surrogate for structural fidelity because the RNA sequence-structure relationship is highly degenerate—multiple distinct sequences can fold into similar structures, and high NSR does not guarantee correct folding. This paper addresses the gap by directly optimizing for 3D structural similarity rather than relying on sequence recovery as a proxy objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262679/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RNA 3D backbone structure as input, represented as a geometric graph where each nucleotide node is characterized by the centroid of its P, C4', and N1/N9 backbone atoms, with edges connecting each node to its 32 nearest spatial neighbors. The output is a discrete RNA nucleotide sequence (one of four bases per position) generated by applying argmax over the denoised one-hot representation. The structural input is consumed through a GVP-GNN encoder that produces E(3)-equivariant node-level embeddings serving as conditioning context for the diffusion process.

## Method

RIDER is a two-stage framework. First, RIDE pre-trains a 5-layer GVP-GNN conditional diffusion model (variance-preserving SDE, 10.2M parameters) to predict noise added to one-hot encoded sequences given structural embeddings, achieving 61% NSR. Second, the denoising process is framed as an MDP and fine-tuned via policy gradient with three key modifications to DDPO: (1) a batch-mean baseline with exponential moving average for advantage estimation to reduce gradient variance, (2) PPO-style clipping on the importance sampling ratio, and (3) four reward functions based on 3D self-consistency metrics (GDT_TS, TM-score, RMSD, and a composite GDT_TS+RMSD) computed by folding the generated sequence with RhoFold and comparing to the target structure. A bonus reward component further incentivizes sequences exceeding designability thresholds (GDT_TS > 0.5 or RMSD < 2.0 Å). This yields over 100% improvement in structural similarity across all metrics compared to SOTA baselines.

## Results

On the RNASolo test set (100 structures), RIDER with the composite R^{gdt-rmsd} reward achieves GDT_TS of 0.62±0.02, RMSD of 3.35±0.44 Å, and TM-score of 0.56±0.03, representing over 100% improvement over the best baseline (gRNAde) on all three 3D self-consistency metrics. 72% of RIDER designs exceed the GDT_TS ≥ 0.5 designability threshold (vs. 27% for gRNAde and 5% for RiboDiffusion), and 33% achieve RMSD ≤ 2 Å (vs. 3% for gRNAde). The pre-trained RIDE model alone achieves 61% NSR, surpassing RiboDiffusion (56%) and gRNAde (55%), but its structural fidelity (GDT_TS 0.33) is substantially lower than after RL fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RIDER substantially outperforms both baselines on all three 3D structural similarity metrics. Against gRNAde (ICLR 2025), RIDER improves GDT_TS from 0.28 to 0.62 (+121%), RMSD from 10.89 to 3.35 Å (−69%), and TM-score from 0.30 to 0.56 (+87%). Against RiboDiffusion (Bioinformatics 2024), the gains are even larger: GDT_TS from 0.13 to 0.62 (+377%), RMSD from 15.68 to 3.35 Å (−79%), and TM-score from 0.14 to 0.56 (+300%). However, RIDER trades off native sequence recovery: its NSR drops to 0.45–0.47 after RL fine-tuning, below RIDE's 0.61 and both baselines (~0.55–0.56). The authors argue this trade-off is desirable, as their experiments (Figure 3B) show no clear correlation between NSR and structural fidelity, and RIDER discovers high-GDT_TS designs that are distinct from native sequences. Cross-oracle validation with AlphaFold3 (Table 2) confirms RIDER's superiority generalizes beyond the RhoFold predictor used during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DDPO framework (Black et al., 2024) for RL fine-tuning of diffusion models from image generation to RNA sequence design, adapting it with advantage estimation via a moving-average baseline and PPO-style clipping to stabilize training in the discrete, high-variance RNA setting. The generative backbone builds on GVP-GNN (Jing et al., 2021) for E(3)-equivariant encoding of RNA tertiary structures and variance-preserving diffusion (Ho et al., 2020) for sequence generation. Conceptually, the key departure from prior 3D RNA inverse design methods (RiboDiffusion, gRNAde, RDesign, RhoDesign) is the rejection of native sequence recovery as the optimization target: those methods are trained and evaluated on NSR, whereas RIDER directly optimizes 3D structural similarity via RL rewards computed from a structure prediction oracle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical demonstration that NSR and 3D structural similarity are largely uncorrelated (Figure 3B) challenges a widely-used evaluation paradigm in RNA design and motivates direct structural optimization. The >100% improvement in GDT_TS, RMSD, and TM-score over baselines, validated across two independent oracles (RhoFold and AlphaFold3), suggests the RL fine-tuning captures generalizable design principles rather than overfitting a single predictor. Key limitations include reliance on in silico evaluation via structure prediction models (introducing circularity), a single dataset (RNASolo, ~4K structures), and the absence of experimental validation. Open questions include whether the framework extends to multi-objective rewards (thermodynamic stability, functional motifs), dynamic RNA conformations, and larger-scale structure libraries.
