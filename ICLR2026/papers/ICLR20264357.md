## Problem

Time Series Foundation Models (TFMs) have achieved strong forecasting performance but their application to reconstruction-based anomaly detection is hampered by two underexplored failure modes: overgeneralization, where the model reconstructs both normal and anomalous data with similar accuracy (especially on low-complexity, low-frequency-dominated data), and overstationarization, where instance normalization layers discard the mean and variance that are critical for detecting statistical anomalies. Prior work attributed overgeneralization primarily to model capacity without accounting for data complexity, and the overstationarization problem had not been addressed for anomaly detection without costly retraining.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a univariate or multivariate time series window of length 256 (with instance normalization fixing ‖x‖₂² = T) and, for MOMENT-Stat, the per-instance mean μ and standard deviation σ computed from each window. The TFM produces a reconstructed series, from which a per-time-point reconstruction error is extracted as the anomaly score. The final CAE score is a convex combination s^CAE = α·s^TFM + (1−α)·s^stat, where α ∈ [0,1] is a quantile-transformed complexity metric computed once per dataset from the normalized gap between imputation error (30% random masking) and reconstruction error (minimal masking).

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary mechanisms. First, the Complexity-Aware Ensemble (CAE) defines a raw complexity score w(x) = (L_imp − L_rec)/‖x‖₂², quantile-transforms it to α ∈ [0,1], and uses α to adaptively weight the TFM reconstruction score against a statistical model score (e.g., Sub-PCA, SR, IForest). Theoretically, Theorem 1 shows α is a weighted sum of Haar wavelet scale energies with non-decreasing weights toward high frequencies, and Theorem 2 proves that larger α yields a strictly larger lower bound on the anomaly gap growth during optimization. Second, MOMENT-Stat resolves overstationarization by concatenating the instance statistics [μ, σ] to the encoder feature vector before the linear decoder, so that the reconstruction error becomes sensitive to mean/variance shifts without any retraining of the pre-trained TFM encoder. The two mechanisms are orthogonal: MOMENT-Stat fixes the TFM's blind spot to distributional anomalies, while CAE compensates for the TFM's overgeneralization on simple data by delegating to statistical models when α is low.

## Results

On the TSB-AD-U univariate benchmark (23 datasets), the proposed Complexity-Aware Ensemble (CAE) with Sub-PCA achieves a VUS-PR of 0.4679, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art Sub-PCA (0.4233) and vanilla MOMENT (0.3857). MOMENT-Stat (instance statistics augmentation) attains VUS-PR 0.3913, outperforming fine-tuned MOMENT (0.3857) and zero-shot MOMENT (0.3790). On the multivariate TSB-AD-M benchmark (17 datasets), CAE improves VUS-PR for HBOS from 0.1751 to 0.2535 and LOF from 0.1091 to 0.2122 over the naive ensemble. Among forecasting-based TFMs, Chronos-bolt-small achieves the best VUS-PR of 0.4172 with the lowest sMAPE (0.1073).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAE with Sub-PCA (VUS-PR 0.4679) outperforms all 25 baselines in the TSB-AD-U benchmark, including the previous best Sub-PCA (0.4233), KShapeAD (0.4007), and MOMENT-FT (0.3857). SR jumps from rank 14 to rank 1 (0.3237→0.4596) and Sub-IForest from rank 29 to rank 2 (0.2230→0.4318) under CAE, demonstrating that the ensemble rescues weak statistical backbones. MOMENT-Stat (0.3913) beats both fine-tuned MOMENT (0.3857) and zero-shot MOMENT (0.3790) on VUS-PR, confirming that re-injecting instance mean/variance helps without retraining. On multivariate data, CAE consistently outperforms the naive 50/50 ensemble (e.g., PCA: 0.3433 vs. 0.3132; LOF: 0.2122 vs. 0.1091), validating adaptive weighting over fixed blending. Among forecasting-based TFMs, Chronos variants (VUS-PR 0.4160–0.4172) substantially outperform Moirai (0.2775–0.2869) and TimeMoE (0.3954–0.3986), with a clear inverse correlation between sMAPE and detection performance. Purely statistical ensembles (best: PCA+SR at 0.4398) underperform the TFM-based CAE (0.4679), confirming the TFM contributes complementary high-level pattern information.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the reconstruction-based anomaly detection paradigm and the growing family of Time Series Foundation Models (MOMENT, Chronos, Moirai, TimeMoE), which have been primarily developed for forecasting. It departs from prior overgeneralization mitigations (decoder capacity reduction, memory-augmented models, contrastive learning) by reframing the problem as a data-complexity issue and proposing a complexity metric α derived directly from the TFM's own imputation-vs-reconstruction error gap, grounded in Haar wavelet spectral analysis. The instance statistics augmentation (MOMENT-Stat) extends the RevIN re-injection idea from forecasting (Kim et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2022) to anomaly detection without retraining, and the Complexity-Aware Ensemble (CAE) introduces an adaptive weighting mechanism between TFMs and classical statistical detectors (PCA, IForest, HBOS, SR, POLY) that had not been previously combined in this way.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As TFMs become the default architecture for time-series tasks, understanding when they fail at anomaly detection—and how to cheaply fix those failures—is practically important for industrial monitoring, healthcare, and finance. The retraining-free statistics augmentation and the complexity-guided ensemble offer low-cost, drop-in improvements that practitioners can adopt immediately. Key limitations include the restriction to O(N log N) statistical backbones, the focus on univariate reconstruction-based detection, and the channel-independence assumption of MOMENT in multivariate settings, where cross-channel modeling remains an open challenge. The finding that forecasting accuracy correlates with anomaly detection performance for forecasting-based TFMs opens the question of whether further improvements in TFM forecasting will continue to translate into better detection.
