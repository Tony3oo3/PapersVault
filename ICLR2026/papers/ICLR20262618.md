## Problem

Squared probabilistic circuits (squared PCs) enable expressive density estimation via modulus squaring, but computing the partition function or any marginal incurs a quadratic O(|c|²) overhead from materializing the squared circuit as a decomposable one. Canonical forms in tensor networks (e.g., MPS, TTNs) eliminate this overhead for specific factorizations, yet they cannot be applied to general circuits whose structure does not map to a known TN. The paper asks: how can one parameterize squared circuits to achieve linear-time marginalization without restricting to structured-decomposable architectures?

## Input / Output

The method takes a tensorized circuit architecture (layers of inputs, Hadamard/Kronecker products, and matrix-parameterized sums) and grayscale image data (MNIST 28×28, FashionMNIST 28×28) as training input. It produces an already-normalized probability distribution (Z=1 by construction) from which probability values, arbitrary variable marginals, and log-likelihoods are computed in a single forward pass without explicitly materializing the squared circuit. The unusual constraint is that sum-layer weights must lie on the Stiefel manifold (semi-unitary matrices) and input functions must be orthonormal, which is enforced during training via a Riemannian optimizer.

## Method

The paper introduces orthogonality (Def. 5) as a relaxation of determinism: sum units receive inputs encoding mutually orthogonal functions, so cross-terms in |c(x)|² vanish upon integration, reducing marginalization from O(|c|²) to O(|c|) (Thm. 1). For tensorized circuits with dense layers that are not basis-decomposable, the authors define unitarity conditions (U1–U4) at the layer level: orthonormal input functions, (semi-)unitary sum-layer matrices, and disjoint input-layer dependencies across sum-layer inputs. Theorem 2 proves that unitary circuits encode already-normalized distributions, and Theorem 3 yields a tighter marginalization complexity of O(|φ_Y∖φ_Z|·S_max + |φ_Y∩φ_Z|·S_max²) versus the previous O(L²·S_max²). Training proceeds by maximum likelihood with the negative log-likelihood L = |B|·log Z − Σ 2log|c(x)|, where Z=1 under unitarity, and the Stiefel constraint is handled by adapting the LandingSGD Riemannian optimizer. The framework generalizes to non-structured-decomposable circuits, enabling efficient marginalization in architectures that would otherwise be #P-hard to square.

## Results

On MNIST and FashionMNIST, squared unitary PCs (⊥²_ℂ with Kronecker product layers) match the bits-per-dimension (bpd) performance of baseline squared PCs (±²_ℂ with Hadamard product layers) across model sizes up to 1B parameters, while requiring significantly less memory and time per training step. At 357M parameters, the squared unitary PC uses 12 GiB of GPU memory and 0.29 ms per iteration, compared to 18 GiB and 0.52 ms for the Hadamard-based baseline. Non-structured-decomposable squared unitary PCs (gray lines in Fig. 5b) are competitive with their structured-decomposable counterparts, especially at large scales, though they may be harder to train.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares squared unitary PCs (⊥²_ℂ, Kronecker product layers, unitary parameterization) against the baseline squared PCs from Loconte et al. (2024) (±²_ℂ, Hadamard product layers, no unitarity constraint) on MNIST and FashionMNIST distribution estimation. On bits-per-dimension, the two methods match in performance across all model sizes tested, confirming that the unitarity constraint (U1–U4) introduces no expressiveness loss. The key advantage is computational: the unitary parameterization avoids materializing the squared circuit as a separate decomposable circuit to compute the partition function Z, yielding ~33% less GPU memory (12 vs. 18 GiB) and ~44% faster iterations (0.29 vs. 0.52 ms) at 357M parameters. Additionally, the paper demonstrates for the first time that non-structured-decomposable squared PCs can be trained efficiently under the unitarity conditions, a setting where explicit Z computation is generally intractable (#P-hard), and these models are competitive with structured-decomposable counterparts at large scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: canonical forms in tensor networks (which use (semi-)unitary matrices to ensure normalized distributions and efficient marginals) and structural properties of probabilistic circuits (smoothness, decomposability, determinism). It builds directly on the squared PC framework of Loconte et al. (ICLR 2024) and the tensorized circuit formalism of Loconte et al. (TMLR 2025), but departs from both by showing that TN canonical forms cannot be naively transplanted to circuits that don't correspond to known TN factorizations. The key conceptual move is identifying orthogonality as a strict relaxation of determinism in circuits, and then showing that (semi-)unitary layer parameterizations generalize TTN upper-canonical forms to a strictly larger class of non-structured-decomposable architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that squared PCs can now be trained and queried with linear-time marginalization (vs. the previous quadratic overhead), and—more importantly—non-structured-decomposable architectures, which were previously intractable for exact inference, become usable. This opens a path toward more expressive factorizations that go beyond the single hierarchical partitioning implicit in MPS and TTNs. Limitations include that the empirical validation is confined to MNIST/FashionMNIST at up to ~1B parameters, and that non-structured-decomposable unitary PCs are noted to be 'harder to learn,' suggesting the optimization landscape under these constraints is not yet well understood. Open questions include whether the expressiveness gains of non-structured-decomposable orthogonal circuits can be realized in practice, and whether orthogonality can be extended as a pairwise property between circuits (analogous to how determinism was generalized in Wang et al. 2024) to simplify compositional operations.
