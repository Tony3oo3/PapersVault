## Problem

Classical matching for counterfactual prediction restricts neighbor search to the target treatment group, but limited observational data and confounding-induced distribution shifts between groups often leave regions where the target group is sparse or absent. This forces large matching distances, which are unreliable on the intrinsic data manifold and degrade counterfactual prediction. The paper asks whether removing the group barrier—searching for neighbors across all treatment groups—can yield closer matches and tighter estimation error bounds.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular observational data: covariate vectors x_i ∈ R^d, scalar factual outcomes y_i, and discrete treatment labels t_i ∈ {0,1,…,T}. It outputs a full potential outcome matrix Ŷ ∈ R^{n×(T+1)} (one predicted outcome per sample per treatment) from which pairwise heterogeneous treatment effects τ_{t,t';i} are derived. The distance-learning component additionally produces an orthogonal projection matrix P ∈ R^{d×d'} that defines the learned transport cost, and the matching step produces a doubly stochastic transport plan γ ∈ R^{n×n} with zero diagonal, which doubles as the transition probability matrix for the propagation step.

## Method

MOGA formulates matching as a self-optimal transport problem: it transports mass from the empirical distribution of all n samples to itself (excluding self-transport via a large diagonal penalty), with a Frobenius-norm regularizer and entropic regularization solved by Sinkhorn iterations. The resulting transport plan γ is scaled to a transition probability matrix W = nγ, and counterfactual outcomes are predicted by iterative random-walk label propagation (S = ρW + (1−ρ)I) that diffuses known factual outcomes along the learned graph while pinning observed entries at each step. For the transport cost, the paper learns a projection φ(x) = P^⊤x by jointly optimizing a coupled self-OT objective over each treatment group that aligns the covariate-based cost C_t^φ with the factual-outcome cost C_t^Y; the P-update has a closed-form solution via eigendecomposition of a weighted Laplacian-like matrix Θ_t, and the γ-update is a standard Sinkhorn problem with combined cost C_t^φ + λ_y C_t^Y. Theoretical analysis provides an upper bound on the outcome estimation error in terms of weighted pairwise distances and a variance term, and shows the mPEHE is bounded by the sum of per-treatment outcome errors.

## Results

On the semi-synthetic News-4 dataset (4 treatments), MOGA achieves √ε_MPEHE = 5.960, ε_mATE = 1.155, and √AMSE = 4.420, substantially outperforming the next-best methods (e.g., CFR at 9.746/3.386/8.194 and MitNet at 9.910/3.598/7.315). On the high-dimensional TCGA dataset (3 treatments), MOGA reaches √ε_MPEHE = 10.597 and ε_mATE = 2.785, beating GOM (10.545/2.706) and BART (10.628/2.812) on AMSE (7.751 vs 7.887/7.839). On simulation data with increasing confounding bias, MOGA consistently achieves the lowest ε_ATE (0.043–0.064) and √AMSE (1.063–1.082) across all four bias settings, while competitors like CFR and CP show larger degradation as group separation increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOGA achieves the best or near-best results across all four semi-synthetic datasets and all simulation settings. On News-4 (4 treatments), MOGA's √ε_MPEHE of 5.960 beats CFR (9.746), MitNet (9.910), and GOM (17.379) by 39–66%, with ε_mATE of 1.155 versus CFR's 3.386 and GOM's 26.188. On the high-dimensional TCGA dataset, MOGA (√ε_MPEHE = 10.597, √AMSE = 7.751) edges out GOM (10.545, 7.887) and BART (10.628, 7.839) on AMSE while matching them on PEHE. On News-2 (binary treatment), GOM slightly outperforms MOGA on √ε_PEHE (6.451 vs 7.501), but MOGA dominates on ε_ATE (0.449 vs 1.532) and √AMSE (3.591 vs 10.451), indicating more stable individual-level predictions. Against traditional matching baselines (k-NN, PSM, PM, CEM), MOGA consistently improves by 20–40% on √ε_MPEHE, attributed to removing the group barrier and leveraging all samples for neighbor selection. On simulation data with increasing confounding bias, MOGA maintains ε_ATE below 0.065 across all settings while CFR degrades from 0.084 to 0.105 and PSM from 0.578 to 0.459, demonstrating superior robustness to distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOGA departs from classical matching methods (Kallus 2020, Schwab 2018, Li & Fu 2017) that restrict neighbor search to the target treatment group, instead formulating matching as a self-optimal transport problem over all samples regardless of treatment assignment. The self-OT framework is directly inherited from Yan et al. (AAAI 2024), which introduced self-OT for subspace and spectral clustering, and the counterfactual prediction mechanism adapts random-walk label propagation (Xia et al. 2019; Zhu & Ghahramani 2002) to the causal setting. Unlike prior OT-based causal methods (Gunsilius & Xu 2021; Wang et al. 2024; Dunipace 2021) that use OT to reduce distributional discrepancy between treated and control groups, MOGA uses self-OT to define a matching structure across all groups simultaneously, with the transport plan serving as a transition matrix for outcome propagation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical limitation of matching—scarce or absent samples in the target group under confounding bias—by expanding the candidate pool to all samples, with a clean theoretical justification via error bounds that naturally decompose into transport cost and Frobenius norm terms. The outcome propagation mechanism elegantly handles the challenge of unknown potential outcomes for cross-group matched samples, and the distance learning component that supervises the transport cost with factual outcomes provides a principled way to incorporate outcome information. Limitations include reliance on standard causal assumptions (unconfoundedness, overlap, Lipschitz continuity), O(N²) space complexity that limits scalability to very large datasets, and evaluation primarily on semi-synthetic and simulation data with limited real-world validation (only LaLonde). Open questions include performance under unobserved confounding and whether the self-OT formulation extends to high-dimensional or non-Euclidean covariate spaces.
