## Problem

Mean-based RLVR methods like GRPO suffer from entropy collapse and limited reasoning gains because the mean objective overemphasizes high-probability generation paths while neglecting rare but informative reasoning trajectories. When all sampled responses to a question are incorrect, the GRPO advantage collapses to zero, leaving no learning signal precisely where the model is weakest. This causes premature convergence and prevents genuine expansion of the model's reasoning boundary.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts (math problems, code problems, or geometry problems) and produces text responses evaluated by a rule-based binary verifier (correct/incorrect). A key structural detail is that multiple questions are grouped into bundles of size B (default 5), and G responses per question are sampled and permuted across bundles so that every response is used exactly once without replacement. The reward signal is sequence-level only (no intermediate process feedback), and quantile thresholds are tracked online during training.

## Method

RiskPO replaces GRPO's mean-based objective with a Mixed Value-at-Risk (MVaR) objective that applies weighted attention over multiple quantile regions of the reward distribution, amplifying gradient signals on low-reward (hard) instances while excluding high-performance samples from the current training step. To overcome the sparsity of binary per-question rewards, the method bundles B questions together and computes advantages on the summed bundle score, transforming coarse binary feedback into a richer distribution that avoids zero-advantage on difficult questions. The gradient is derived via a score-function transformation of the RVaR functional, and the final loss uses a trust-region clipped objective with sequence-level importance sampling ratios for stable multi-update training. Theoretically, the paper proves that under a natural gradient step, the MVaR advantage has smaller covariance with log-probabilities than the mean advantage, which directly implies higher policy entropy and mitigates entropy collapse. The algorithm is implemented as a two-timescale stochastic approximation where quantile trackers are updated at a faster rate than the policy parameters.

## Results

On hard-level mathematical reasoning (Table 1), RiskPO-1.5B achieves an average Pass@1 of 46.65 across six benchmarks, surpassing the strongest baseline DAPO (43.87) by +2.78 and vanilla GRPO (40.41) by +6.24. The gains are most pronounced on AIME24/AIME25, where RiskPO reaches 33.3 vs. DAPO's 26.6/30.0. On easier math and multi-modal/coding tasks (Table 2), RiskPO maintains consistent advantages: 56.2 on MATH, 26.8 on LiveCodeBench, and 54.5 on Geometry3K, all leading GRPO and DAPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RiskPO outperforms all seven baselines on every hard-level math benchmark. Against the strongest competitor DAPO, RiskPO gains +3.3 on AIME25, +6.7 on AIME24, +2.2 on AMC, +3.6 on MATH500, +0.3 on Minerva, and +0.6 on OlympiadBench. Compared to vanilla GRPO, the average improvement is +6.24, with the largest gap on AIME24 (+13.3) and AIME25 (+13.3). GMPO and GPG trail RiskPO by 5.75 and 7.75 points on average respectively, while Dr.GRPO lags by 6.68. On the easier MATH/GSM8K and multi-modal/coding tasks, RiskPO leads DAPO by +0.5 (MATH) and +0.6 (LCB), and GRPO by +1.9 (MATH) and +1.0 (LCB), confirming consistent but smaller margins on less challenging settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

RiskPO builds directly on the GRPO framework (Shao et al., 2024) and its sequence-level importance sampling variant GSPO (Zheng et al., 2025), retaining the group-sampling and clipped trust-region structure while replacing the mean-based advantage with a Mixed Value-at-Risk (MVaR) objective. The risk-sensitive RL literature (CVaR, RVaR, distortion risk measures) provides the mathematical machinery, but this is the first work to transplant these distributional objectives into the LLM post-training setting with verifiable reward. The bundling scheme—aggregating multiple binary-reward questions into a composite score—is a novel practical device that addresses the sparsity of per-question feedback and enables meaningful quantile tracking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles entropy collapse, a widely recognized failure mode in RLVR that limits genuine reasoning gains, and provides both a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 2: risk-averse updates reduce the log-prob/advantage covariance) and empirical evidence (sustained higher entropy, widening Pass@k gaps) that the fix works. The Pass@k widening—where RiskPO solves instances GRPO cannot solve even at k=16—is a compelling signal of reasoning-boundary expansion rather than mere sampling efficiency. Limitations include evaluation only on 1.5B–3B models, sensitivity to hyperparameters (α, β, ω, B all require tuning), and the assumption that the base model's log-probability is monotone in reward tails (Assumption 1), which may not hold for all architectures or training stages. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, how it composes with other RLVR improvements (e.g., DAPO's dynamic sampling), and whether the bundling overhead becomes prohibitive at larger batch sizes.
