## Problem

Existing video deraining methods rely on optical flow or heuristic alignment for cross-frame consistency, but optical flow is unreliable under rain (brightness constancy breaks) and computationally expensive, while implicit attention-based approaches lack explicit geometric constraints and struggle under camera pose variations. The paper addresses how to inject physically meaningful, geometry-consistent motion priors directly into the attention mechanism to guide reliable spatiotemporal feature aggregation without depending on fragile flow estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262521/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a window of T=5 consecutive rainy video frames, which are patchified into token embeddings. It outputs the restored clean center frame of the window. Training uses an L1 reconstruction loss on the center frame, with the model operating on 8×NVIDIA 3090 GPUs at batch size 64 over 5000 epochs with cosine annealing.

## Method

DeLiVR injects two Lie-group-derived biases into a Transformer backbone's self-attention logits. First, a lightweight SO(2) head predicts per-frame in-plane rotations via the exponential map from the Lie algebra (bounded by tanh), and a spatial bias is computed as the inner product of rotated normalized 3D coordinate embeddings between token pairs across frames. Second, a differential group displacement computes angular differences between frames via the logarithm map on relative rotation matrices, yielding a temporal bias that penalizes large pose discrepancies. These biases are fused with an exponential temporal decay matrix and a banded attention mask into a unified spatiotemporal bias added to the attention scores. The model is trained end-to-end with L1 reconstruction loss plus rotation-magnitude and Lie-velocity regularizers (both weighted at 0.02), requiring only 2.64M parameters and 82.52 ms/frame inference.

## Results

On the real-world WeatherBench dataset, DeLiVR achieves the best PSNR of 26.56 and SSIM of 0.781, outperforming the strongest competitor VDMamba (CVPR 2025) by 2.65 dB in PSNR. On synthetic benchmarks, DeLiVR leads on Rain-Syn-Light (PSNR 30.53) and Rain-Syn-Complex (PSNR 24.68), while ranking second on NTURain (PSNR 34.06) behind VDMamba (36.29). The method also uses only 2.64M parameters and runs at 82.52 ms/frame, substantially lighter than most Transformer-based baselines (29–58M params).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeLiVR outperforms all eight main baselines (S2VD, MFGAN, ESTINe, ViMP-Net, ViWS-Net, RainMamba, Turtle, and VDMamba) on three of four datasets, losing only on NTURain to VDMamba (34.06 vs. 36.29 PSNR). The most striking gap is on the real-world WeatherBench, where DeLiVR leads VDMamba by 2.65 dB (26.56 vs. 23.91 PSNR) and ESTINe by 1.34 dB, indicating substantially better generalization to authentic rain. On the challenging Rain-Syn-Complex benchmark, DeLiVR surpasses the next-best MFGAN by 3.63 dB (24.68 vs. 21.05 PSNR). Against the two optical-flow-based methods evaluated on WeatherBench (Yang et al. 2019 at 23.33 PSNR and Fang & Zhan 2022 at 24.10 PSNR), DeLiVR gains 3.23 and 2.46 dB respectively, confirming that the SO(2) Lie-group bias provides a stronger geometric prior than unconstrained flow fields. In a controlled head-to-head with a flow-bias variant sharing the same 12-layer Transformer backbone, DeLiVR improves PSNR by +2.43 dB on NTURain, isolating the benefit of the rotation-manifold parameterization.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeLiVR departs from the dominant optical-flow-based alignment paradigm in video deraining (MFGAN, S2VD, ESTINe) and from implicit attention-based alignment (ViWS-Net, VDMamba) by injecting explicit SO(2) Lie-group rotation priors directly into Transformer attention scores. Conceptually, it repurposes Lie-group formulations previously explored in equivariant networks (LieTransformer, Hutchinson et al. 2021) and relative positional encodings, but applies them as lightweight per-frame rotation biases rather than full equivariant architectures. The paper is the first to frame video deraining alignment as a problem on the SO(2) manifold, replacing dense 2D flow fields with a single scalar rotation angle per frame predicted by a compact head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single in-plane rotation per frame outperforms dense optical flow for cross-frame alignment in rainy video challenges the field's working assumption that alignment requires full displacement fields, and the ~2.6 dB real-world gap on WeatherBench over the strongest baseline (VDMamba) suggests geometric priors may generalize better than data-driven alignment to unseen conditions. However, the method is limited to SO(2) (in-plane rotation only), so it cannot capture 3D camera motion, non-rigid scene dynamics, or translational shifts, which are common in real driving scenarios. Extending the framework to SE(2) or SE(3), and validating on longer sequences with more complex motion, are natural open questions that would determine whether this geometric-prior approach scales beyond the controlled settings tested here.
