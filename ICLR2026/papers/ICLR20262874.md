## Problem

Diffusion-based object removal methods fail to recover fine structural and textural details of small objects because the VAE encoder's fixed downsampling compresses small masked regions, discarding high-frequency information that the decoder cannot restore. Additionally, simply increasing input resolution to mitigate this loss introduces color shifts and visible seams when the magnified inpainted region is reintegrated into the original image due to VAE encoder-decoder asymmetry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262874/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (960×540 from RORD) and a binary object mask as input, and produces a full-resolution object-removed image where the masked region is filled with contextually consistent content and any residual shadows of the removed object are automatically corrected. The input image is first processed through GeoCalib to estimate camera intrinsics (focal length, roll, pitch, vFoV), which determines whether a camera-adaptive zoom-in transformation is applied before inpainting.

## Method

ReFocusEraser is a two-stage framework built on FLUX.1-dev. Stage I (Camera-Adaptive Refocus Inpainter) estimates focal length via GeoCalib, applies central FoV cropping and rescaling to simulate 2×/3×/4× zoom on far-field scenes, then fine-tunes the DiT backbone with LoRA (rank 32) using a flow-matching loss conditioned on the zoomed masked foreground, visible background, and binary mask. Stage II (Seam- and Shadow-Aware Repair) composites the inpainted region back via mask-based stitching (pasting only masked pixels to preserve surrounding context), then fine-tunes only the VAE decoder—keeping the encoder frozen—with a color-shadow consistency loss that applies L1 to the foreground for pixel-level color accuracy and LPIPS to the background for perceptual detail preservation, simultaneously correcting color shifts and removing unmasked object shadows.

## Results

On RORD-val (small-object removal), ReFocusEraser achieves PSNR 31.256, SSIM 0.924, LPIPS 0.041, FID 21.378, and CMMD 0.263, substantially outperforming the previous best region-level method Flux-Fill (PSNR 25.025, SSIM 0.794, LPIPS 0.092) and the previous best overall-quality method AttentiveEraser (FID 25.393, CMMD 0.276). On RemovalBench (normal-object removal), ReFocusEraser reaches PSNR 30.495, SSIM 0.841, LPIPS 0.223, FID 38.115, and CMMD 0.117, surpassing AttentiveEraser (PSNR 25.265, SSIM 0.751, LPIPS 0.284, FID 65.326, CMMD 0.169) by large margins on every metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReFocusEraser outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across both benchmarks. On RORD-val, it improves PSNR by +6.23 over Flux-Fill (the prior best region-level method) and by +7.19 over AttentiveEraser (the prior best overall-quality method), while reducing FID from 65.215 to 21.378. On RemovalBench, the gains over AttentiveEraser are even more pronounced: PSNR +5.23, SSIM +0.09, FID −27.21, and CMMD −0.052. General inpainting methods (RePaint, PixelHacker, Flux-Kontext) perform poorly on object removal, with RePaint and Flux-Kontext scoring below PSNR 16 on RORD-val, indicating they struggle to produce semantically coherent backgrounds. Specialized removal methods (CLIPAway, PowerPaint, OmniEraser) achieve moderate results but fail to match ReFocusEraser's detail preservation, with OmniEraser (the closest specialized competitor on RemovalBench) still trailing by PSNR 5.87 and FID 28.76. Flux-Fill, while competitive on RORD-val region-level metrics, degrades sharply on RemovalBench (PSNR 21.526, FID 177.571), suggesting limited generalization beyond small-object scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on FLUX.1-dev's DiT-based diffusion architecture and OmniEraser's multi-channel conditioning design (masked foreground, visible background, binary mask), but departs from the fixed-resolution paradigm that all prior diffusion-based inpainting methods share. The key conceptual departure is the camera-adaptive refocus mechanism, which simulates focal-length zoom to enlarge small masked regions before VAE encoding, directly addressing the information-loss bottleneck that prior methods (RePaint, CLIPAway, PowerPaint, Flux-Fill, AttentiveEraser) accept as inherent. The two-stage pipeline—LoRA-tuned DiT inpainting followed by a fine-tuned VAE decoder for seam/shadow repair—repurposes GeoCalib's single-image camera calibration in a novel generative context, and the color-shadow consistency loss (L1 on foreground + LPIPS on background) is a targeted departure from the uniform reconstruction losses used in prior VAE fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically validates a concrete bottleneck in latent diffusion inpainting—VAE downsampling destroys high-frequency detail in small masked regions—that prior work has acknowledged but not systematically addressed. The camera-zoom analogy is a practical, interpretable solution that could transfer to other tasks requiring cross-scale detail preservation (e.g., super-resolution-guided inpainting, multi-scale editing). However, the approach is constrained to far-field scenes (focal length < 500 px), requires two separate training stages with substantial GPU resources (~3.5 days on H200s), and the shadow-removal capability, while impressive, is somewhat incidental to the main inpainting pipeline. Open questions include whether the refocus strategy generalizes to video inpainting, whether the 500 px threshold and 3× zoom factor are robust across diverse camera systems, and whether the seam/shadow decoder can be distilled into a single-pass model.
