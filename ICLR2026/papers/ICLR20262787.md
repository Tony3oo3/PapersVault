## Problem

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle to form coherent spatial mental models from limited, partially occluded views, performing near-randomly on tasks requiring reasoning about unseen objects, cross-view consistency, and 'what-if' mental simulation. No existing benchmark systematically evaluates this capability, and it remains unclear whether structured intermediate representations (cognitive maps, interpolated views, reasoning chains) can scaffold spatial mental modeling in VLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262787/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives 2–4 orthogonal multi-view RGB images (organized into Rotation, Around, or Among camera-movement settings) paired with a multiple-choice spatial reasoning question that references objects not visible in the query view. In the trained 'map-then-reason' configuration, the model first generates a 2D cognitive map on a 10×10 grid encoding object positions, facing directions, and camera viewpoints, then produces a free-form natural-language reasoning chain before outputting the final answer. The MINDCUBE-TINY diagnostic subset (1,050 questions) is used for all training and ablation experiments.

## Method

The paper introduces MINDCUBE (21,154 questions, 3,268 images, 976 multi-view groups) and then investigates three cognitive scaffolds—view interpolation, augmented/plain cognitive maps, and free-form reasoning—under frozen, SFT, and RL regimes using Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct. The key finding is that passive structures (interpolated views, externally supplied maps) provide little or negative benefit, while actively generating a cognitive map and then reasoning over it ('map-then-reason') yields the strongest gains: SFT on 10,000 grounded reasoning chains plus cognitive maps raises accuracy from 37.8% to 57.8%, and subsequent RL via GRPO (VAGEN framework) warm-started from the SFT checkpoint pushes it to 61.3%. The synergy arises because the joint map-generation and reasoning objective forces the model to build a functionally effective spatial representation rather than merely replicating map structure, as evidenced by the fact that map-only models learn structure rapidly but plateau in QA accuracy while the joint model learns maps more slowly yet continues improving on the end task.

## Results

On the MINDCUBE benchmark (21,154 questions, 3,268 images), the best existing VLM (DeepSeek-VL2-Small) achieves only 47.62% overall accuracy, barely above the 32.35% random-chance baseline, while human annotators reach 94.55%. The paper's 'map-then-reason' approach (SFT with Plain-CGMap-FFR-Out) boosts Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct from 37.81% to 57.81% (+20.0%) on MINDCUBE-TINY, and adding GRPO-based RL from the SFT checkpoint further raises accuracy to 61.33% (+23.5% over the frozen baseline). Notably, view interpolation provides no meaningful gain (+0.09%), and directly feeding ground-truth cognitive maps as input actually degrades performance (−5.81%), underscoring that active reasoning over self-generated maps is essential.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All 17 evaluated VLMs perform near-random on MINDCUBE: the best open-weight model (DeepSeek-VL2-Small, 47.62%) and best proprietary model (GPT-5, 47.59%) are within 0.03% of each other, and both fall far short of the 94.55% human average. Spatially fine-tuned models (SpaceMantis 22.81%, Spatial-MLLM 32.06%, Space-Qwen 33.28%, RoboBrain 37.38%) do not consistently outperform general-purpose VLMs, indicating that spatial fine-tuning alone is insufficient. GPT-5 excels in ROTATION (93.33%) but drops to 34.17% on AMONG, while Mantis-8B is strongest in AROUND (50.99%) but weak overall (41.05%), showing no model generalizes across all three settings. The paper's method (RL-Plain-CGMap-FFR-Out from SFT) achieves 61.33% on MINDCUBE-TINY, surpassing the frozen Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline (37.81%) by 23.5 points and exceeding the best full-MINDCUBE model (DeepSeek-VL2-Small, 47.62%) by a wide margin. Among the paper's own ablations, free-form reasoning alone (FFR, 55.43%) and map generation alone (Plain-CGMap-Out, 54.29%) both underperform the joint map-then-reason configuration (57.81%), confirming the synergy between structured spatial representation and natural-language inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper grounds its approach in cognitive science literature on spatial mental models (Johnson-Laird, Tversky) and repurposes the VAGEN/GRPO framework for VLM policy optimization to refine spatial reasoning. It departs from prior spatial reasoning benchmarks (SpatialVLM, SpaceMantis, Spatial-MLLM, RoboBrain) by explicitly targeting internal representation formation under partial observability—occlusion, cross-view consistency, and 'what-if' mental simulation—rather than direct spatial QA. The 'map-then-reason' training recipe is a novel combination of structured cognitive map generation with free-form chain-of-thought, positioned against prior work that treats these as separate or competing strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MINDCUBE exposes a stark capability gap: the best evaluated model (DeepSeek-VL2-Small) achieves only 47.62% versus 94.55% for human annotators, and even GPT-5 scores just 47.59% overall. The counterintuitive finding that adding interpolated views or providing ground-truth cognitive maps as input fails to help (and can hurt) challenges the assumption that more spatial evidence automatically improves spatial reasoning, pointing instead to the necessity of active internal representation construction. Open questions include whether the map-then-reason recipe generalizes to larger models and more complex 3D environments, and whether the persistently low isomorphism rates (~35% after training) indicate a fundamental architectural limitation in VLMs' spatial representation capacity.
