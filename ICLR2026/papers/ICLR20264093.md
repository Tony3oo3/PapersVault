## Problem

Existing intervention-based decoding methods (DoLa, SLED) mitigate LLM hallucination by amplifying latent factual signals from early layers, but they do not explicitly identify and suppress the hallucination-prone directions that cause the model to deviate from truth. This paper asks whether one can instead elicit a controllable hallucination signal and use it as a negative direction to subtract probability mass from spurious trajectories during decoding, without any retraining or external knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264093/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard text prompt and the pre-computed per-head importance scores from Wu et al. (2024), then performs two forward passes through the LLM: one unmasked (yielding original logits at every layer) and one with the top-x heads masked in the K lowest-entropy layers (yielding the hallucination signal logits). It outputs a single set of adjusted final-layer logits, from which tokens are decoded as usual; the adjustment is a single gradient-descent step scaled by a hyperparameter α (default 2) and a fixed number I (default 10) of top-probability 'correctness directions' drawn from the final-layer softmax.

## Method

NDAD first generates a hallucination signal by masking the most important attention heads (ranked by Wu et al.'s retrieval-head scores) in the K layers whose head-importance distributions have the lowest entropy, producing logits_l^mask at each layer. Two complementary weights then regulate this signal: a global weight (cosine similarity between logits_l^mask and the original logits_l, mapped to [0,1]) quantifies how aligned the hallucination direction is with the layer's native activations, and a local weight (cosine similarity between the premature-to-mature trajectory logits_l^mask − logits_L and the tail-token evolution P_tail − P_ei for each of the top-I correctness one-hot vectors) estimates whether low-probability tokens are likely to evolve into the final output. After squaring and normalizing these weights across correctness directions and layers, NDAD forms a latent hallucination distribution P_NDAD and applies one gradient-descent step, logits_L^new = logits_L − α ∇KL(P_NDAD, P_logits_L), to subtract probability mass from hallucination-prone regions while preserving high-confidence predictions.

## Results

On Llama2-7B-base, NDAD achieves 43.00% average on TruthfulQA (MC), 67.30% on FACTOR Wiki, 61.57% on StrategyQA, and 14.86% on GSM8K, consistently outperforming the strongest baseline SLED (42.87%, 67.00%, 61.27%, 14.63%). The same pattern holds across Llama2-13B-chat, where NDAD reaches 44.74% (TruthfulQA), 67.74% (FACTOR), 69.96% (StrategyQA), and 37.30% (GSM8K) versus SLED's 44.62%, 67.50%, 69.74%, and 37.15%. On open-ended generation (Table 2), NDAD improves PopQA from 25.86% (SLED) to 26.00% and NQ-Open from 25.96% to 26.26% on Llama2-7B-base. Runtime overhead is modest: 1.34s vs. 1.11s for greedy decoding on Llama2-7B-base, with memory increasing from 13.5 GB to 17.8 GB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NDAD consistently outperforms SLED, the strongest baseline, across all four Llama2 model variants and all four benchmark tasks, with margins of 0.1–0.5 points on TruthfulQA/FACTOR/StrategyQA and up to 0.46 points on GSM8K (e.g., 14.86% vs. 14.63% on Llama2-7B-base). Against DoLa (both low and high variants), NDAD achieves substantially larger gains on FACTOR Wiki (e.g., 67.30% vs. 63.36% for DoLa-low on 7B-base) and GSM8K, while DoLa-high sometimes degrades below greedy on GSM8K (11.90% vs. 28.81% on 13B-base). AD (Activation Decoding) performs catastrophically on CoT reasoning tasks, dropping to near-zero on GSM8K (2.12% on 7B-base, 0.00% on 13B-base) and StrategyQA (1.97%, 2.01%), because its entropy-based constraint misclassifies logically-dependent intermediate tokens as hallucinations. On open-ended generation (Table 2), NDAD edges out SLED on PopQA (26.00% vs. 25.86%) and NQ-Open (26.26% vs. 25.96%) for Llama2-7B-base, and on TriviaQA (71.66% vs. 71.47%) and NQ-Open (32.88% vs. 32.52%) for 13B-base. Across diverse architectures (Qwen2.5-7B, Mistral-7B, Llama3-8B in Table 3), NDAD maintains its lead over SLED, with the largest relative gain on GSM8K (e.g., 85.14% vs. 84.91% for Qwen2.5-7B, 77.18% vs. 75.82% for Llama3-8B).

## Relation to Prior Work

NDAD extends the intervention-decoding lineage initiated by DoLa (contrasting early vs. late layer logits) and SLED (gradient-descent logit adjustment using early-layer signals), but inverts the contrastive direction: rather than amplifying factual signals from early layers, it explicitly elicits hallucination-prone signals by masking important attention heads (following Wu et al., 2024) and uses them as a repulsive negative direction. The global and local weighting mechanisms borrow conceptual tools from ITI (directional consistency) and SLED (evolution-trajectory tracking), but apply them to the hallucination signal rather than to factual evidence. This shifts the calibration paradigm from 'boost what is true' to 'subtract what is likely false,' a framing the authors argue prevents probability mass from accumulating on speculative trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NDAD offers a training-free, plug-and-play decoding strategy that consistently (if modestly) outperforms SLED across five model families and seven benchmarks, including complex CoT reasoning where prior methods like AD collapse. The negative-direction framing provides a complementary perspective to the dominant positive-signal approach, and the head-masking mechanism for hallucination induction is a reusable primitive. However, the gains over SLED are typically under 1 point on most benchmarks, the method adds ~20% runtime and ~30% memory overhead, and the ablation reveals that random head/layer selection sometimes matches the guided strategy, suggesting the importance-guided masking may not be as critical as claimed. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 100B+ models, whether the fixed [6,13] masking range generalizes across architectures, and whether the KL-divergence penalty can be replaced by lighter-weight alternatives.
