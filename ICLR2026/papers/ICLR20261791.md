## Problem

Sequential social dilemma environments like Melting Pot 2.0 require over 1000 CPUs for training, making experiments prohibitively expensive, and their baseline algorithms (A3C, V-MPO, OPRE) are not publicly released, hindering reproducibility. No existing JAX-based MARL framework provides a dedicated suite of sequential social dilemma environments with standardized evaluation protocols, leaving a gap between the computational demands of these environments and the hardware acceleration available through GPU/TPU vectorization.

## Input / Output

Agents observe 11×11 grid matrices where objects are encoded as distinct numerical values (not pixel images as in Melting Pot 2.0), and select from discrete action spaces. Beyond standard training returns, each environment defines a tailored cooperation metric (e.g., apples remaining in Harvest, cleaned river tiles in Clean Up, gold ore mined in Coop Mining, blue mushrooms consumed in Mushrooms) to quantify prosocial versus antisocial behavior. The suite also produces Schelling diagrams mapping cooperator/defector payoffs as a function of the number of cooperating co-players, and throughput benchmarks at scales from 1 to 4096 parallel environments.

## Method

SocialJax re-implements nine sequential social dilemma environments (derived from Melting Pot 2.0 but using grid-level rather than pixel-level observations) in JAX, enabling GPU vectorization of up to 4096 parallel environments via JIT compilation and automatic parallelization. The training pipeline includes JAX implementations of IPPO (under common and individual reward settings, with and without parameter sharing), MAPPO, VDN, SVO (with sweepable social value orientation angle θ ∈ [0°, 90°]), and IPPO-RE (reward exchange with ratios 0.25, 0.5, 0.75). This yields a 50–400× real-time speedup over Melting Pot 2.0's RLlib baselines on a single A100 GPU with 14 CPUs. Social dilemma properties are validated by constructing Schelling diagrams from policies trained under common reward (cooperators) versus individual reward (defectors), confirming fear or greed conditions per environment. Environment-specific cooperation metrics and SVO angle sweeps provide finer-grained behavioral analysis beyond scalar return.

## Results

SocialJax achieves a 50–400× speed-up over Melting Pot 2.0 (RLlib) on Coins and 50–140× on Clean Up, completing 10⁹ timesteps in ~3 hours on a single A100 GPU versus ~150 hours (RLlib) or ~1,300 hours (Stable-Baselines3). Across the nine environments, IPPO with common reward (IPPO-CR) and SVO at θ=90° (altruistic) consistently achieve the highest cooperation metrics, while IPPO with individual reward (IPPO-IR) yields the lowest. In Clean Up, IPPO-RE at reward ratio 0.5 reaches 1780±340 return, outperforming the common-reward baseline (970±90), demonstrating that partial reward sharing can beat full alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPPO-CR dominates IPPO-IR across all nine environments, confirming that common reward alignment is essential for cooperation (e.g., Coins: 164.07 vs. 100.83; Clean Up: 109.54 vs. 9.28). MAPPO, as a centralized critic, achieves the highest cooperation metric in Clean Up (133.99), Coop Mining (571.25), Mushrooms (22.96), and Prisoner's Dilemma (119.63), but underperforms IPPO-CR in Gift Refinement (20.24 vs. 28.37) and Harvest: Partnership (15.56 vs. 17.75) due to unstable training dynamics. VDN outperforms IPPO-IR in most environments but is severely limited by its linear Q-decomposition, scoring only 15.06 on Coins and 0.01 on Gift Refinement. SVO at θ=90° (altruistic) achieves the best or near-best cooperation metric in 6 of 9 environments, with returns scaling monotonically from θ=0° to θ=90° (e.g., Clean Up: 0.02 → 1410.53). IPPO-RE substantially improves over IPPO-CR in Clean Up (1780 vs. 970 return at ratio 0.5) but performs worse in Gift Refinement (12.20 vs. 28.37), indicating that free-riding incentives are particularly strong in trust-based settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Melting Pot 2.0 framework (Agapiou et al., 2022; Leibo et al., 2021), which established the first standardized benchmark for evaluating MARL in sequential social dilemmas, but departs from it in two key ways: (1) reimplementing environments in JAX to exploit GPU parallelism rather than CPU-based parallelism, and (2) providing open-source implementations of baseline algorithms (IPPO, MAPPO, VDN, SVO, IPPO-RE) that were previously unavailable in Melting Pot. It also extends the JAX-based MARL ecosystem (JaxMARL, PureJaxRL) by adding dedicated sequential social dilemma environments and Schelling diagram validation, filling a gap where prior JAX frameworks focused on cooperative or single-agent tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50–400× speedup over Melting Pot 2.0 makes sequential social dilemma research accessible to labs without 1000+ CPU clusters, substantially lowering the barrier to entry for studying cooperation dynamics. The environment-specific cooperation metrics and Schelling diagram validation provide a more nuanced evaluation than return alone, enabling researchers to distinguish fear- vs. greed-driven dilemmas. Open questions include whether findings generalize beyond grid-based, partially observable settings to continuous or larger-scale environments, and whether the reward-exchange ratios identified here transfer across agent counts and environment scales. The paper also leaves formal estimation of self-interest levels in these environments to future work.
