## Problem

Deploying LLMs on long inputs is bottlenecked by the linearly growing KV cache, which creates severe memory and bandwidth constraints during autoregressive decoding. Existing token-eviction methods are either static (risking irreversible information loss), heuristic-based (failing to capture the query-dependent nature of token importance), or learning-based (suffering poor cross-dataset generalization). The paper asks whether a query-aware token importance predictor can be achieved without costly training.

## Input / Output

At each decoding step, FASA consumes the current query vector, the full past KV cache, and a pre-calibrated set of dominant frequency-chunk (FC) indices (head-specific but task-agnostic, identified via a one-time offline calibration on a single sample). It produces a reduced set of top-N_fac token indices and the corresponding full-dimensional attention output. The method operates exclusively during the decode phase (not prefill), and two hardware-aware variants exist: FASA-M offloads non-dominant keys and all values to CPU memory, while FASA-C retains the full cache on-GPU but accesses only a sparse subset, trading memory for speed.

## Method

FASA is grounded in a novel empirical discovery: RoPE induces functional sparsity at the frequency-chunk level, where a tiny subset of dominant FCs (<1% of all 64 FCs per head) consistently achieves high Contextual Agreement (CA) with the full attention head, while the remaining FCs primarily encode positional patterns. The framework is a training-free, two-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline: (1) the Token Importance Prediction (TIP) stage computes approximate scores using only the 2D dominant FC subspaces (complexity O(2t·N_tip) vs. O(td) for full attention) and selects the top-N_fac most salient tokens; (2) the Focused Attention Computation (FAC) stage performs full-dimensional attention exclusively on this reduced subset (complexity O(N_fac·d)), preserving original absolute positions to maintain positional encoding integrity. Dominant FCs are identified offline by maximizing the expected CA score over a small calibration set, and are empirically shown to be universal across model scales, architectures, and tasks (>70% cross-task overlap). A key design constraint is that FCs are indivisible functional units—splitting them into individual dimensions destroys the RoPE rotation structure and causes catastrophic failure—and the FC-based scores serve strictly as a ranking proxy, not a substitute for attention weights.

## Results

On LongBench-V1 with a 256-token budget and 25% FCs, FASA achieves 41.5% average on Llama-3.2-3B (vs. 42.2% FKV), 47.9% on Qwen2.5-7B (vs. 47.8% FKV), and 47.9% on Mistral-7B (vs. 47.4% FKV), staying within 0.7% of full-KV performance while using only 18.9% of the cache. On Long-CoT reasoning (AIME24), FASA achieves 2.56× speedup with 86.4% accuracy at 10% context budget, narrowly trailing the 92.6% FKV upper bound. FASA-M provides 8× KV cache compression, and FASA-C delivers 2.6× inference speedup with 25% FCs selected.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FASA consistently outperforms all token-eviction baselines across every model and task. Against SnapKV (the strongest baseline), FASA gains +4.5 points on Llama-3.2-3B (41.5 vs. 37.0), +5.3 on Qwen2.5-7B (47.9 vs. 42.6), +3.8 on Mistral-7B (47.8 vs. 44.0), and +3.3 on Qwen2.5-14B-1M (49.2 vs. 45.9). Quest and Stream suffer catastrophic degradation under tight budgets (e.g., Quest drops to 25.5 on Llama-3.2-3B, a 16.7-point gap vs. FASA), with Quest's page-level granularity and Stream's fixed recency window failing to capture query-dependent token importance. Remarkably, FASA occasionally exceeds the FKV upper bound (e.g., 47.9 vs. 47.4 on Mistral-7B; 47.9 vs. 47.8 on Qwen2.5-7B), which the authors attribute to mitigating attentional distraction from irrelevant tokens. On Long-CoT reasoning (AIME24), FASA achieves 86.4% at 10% context budget, surpassing R-KV (a specialized CoT compression method) and dramatically outperforming SnapKV, which collapses to 21.6% vs. FKV's 72.4% on R1-Llama. FASA also demonstrates compatibility with PyramidKV, yielding consistent +0.1 to +1.1 point gains when combined.

## Relation to Prior Work

FASA builds directly on RoPE's frequency-chunk decomposition, revealing that a tiny subset of frequency chunks (<1%) carries most of the contextual information in attention scores — a structural property of RoPE not previously characterized at this granularity. It departs from prior token-eviction methods (Stream, SnapKV, Quest, H2O) that rely on static heuristics or one-time prefill-stage filtering, and from low-rank compression approaches (SparQ, LoKi) that project into learned or magnitude-selected subspaces. The key conceptual departure is that FASA uses the inherent rotational structure of RoPE itself as a training-free, query-aware proxy for token importance, requiring no learned predictor and no auxiliary projection matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that functional sparsity at the FC level is universal across architectures (Llama, Mistral, Qwen, R1-Distill), scales (3B–32B), and tasks (QA, summarization, code, reasoning) suggests a fundamental, previously uncharacterized property of RoPE that could inform broader efficiency strategies beyond KV cache compression. Practically, FASA achieves 8× memory reduction and 2.56× speedup with <0.7% performance loss, and its orthogonality to other compression paradigms (demonstrated via PyramidKV integration) makes it a composable building block. Open questions include whether the one-time calibration truly generalizes to unseen model families or fine-tuning regimes, how the method behaves under extreme compression budgets (<64 tokens), and whether the FC-level sparsity insight transfers to non-attention components (FFN, embeddings).
