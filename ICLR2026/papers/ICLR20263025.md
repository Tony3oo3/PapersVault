## Problem

Omni-modal Large Language Models (OLLMs) can perceive diverse modalities but lack the complex reasoning abilities of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs), while existing approaches to enhance OLLM reasoning require expensive supervised or reinforcement fine-tuning, task-specific adaptation, and large curated datasets. The paper asks whether textual reasoning capabilities can be lifted to omni-modal scenarios in a training-free manner without modality-specific expert models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263025/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes omni-modal content (images, audio, video) and a text prompt as input to the OLLM, while the LRM receives only the textual prefix. A critical constraint is that the OLLM and LRM must share the same token vocabulary for logit-level fusion. Each decoding step requires three forward passes (OLLM with full omni-modal input, OLLM with text-only input, and LRM with text-only input), and the output is a standard autoregressive text response generated token-by-token from the fused logit distribution.

## Method

THINKOMNI is a training-free inference-time framework with two components. First, LRM-as-a-Guide performs contrastive decoding by mixing logits from three sources: the OLLM with full omni-modal input (base), the OLLM with text-only input (negative), and an off-the-shelf LRM on the same text prefix (positive), following the formula P̂ = Softmax[z_base + α·(z_LRM − z_OLLM_text)]. Second, Stepwise Contrastive Scaling replaces the fixed guidance weight α with per-step adaptive weights α_t^r and α_t^p, computed from Jensen-Shannon divergences between the three probability distributions to measure how much each step is dominated by reasoning versus perception, with the constraint α_t^r + α_t^p = 1. This allows the decoder to dynamically emphasize omni-modal cues when perception is critical (e.g., reading a label) and lean on LRM logical guidance when abstract reasoning is needed (e.g., algebraic manipulation), without any manual hyperparameter tuning.

## Results

With Qwen2.5-Omni-7B as the base OLLM and Qwen3-8B as the LRM guide, THINKOMNI achieves 70.2% on MathVista test-mini, 32.9% on MathVision test, 45.1% on MathVerse test-mini, 75.5% on MMAU test-mini, 59.5% on DailyOmni test, and 43.6% on OmniBench test. These represent improvements of +3.4, +7.9, +4.9, +4.0, +1.6, and +1.5 percentage points over the unmodified Qwen2.5-Omni-7B baseline, respectively, all without any additional training. The method also improves Omni-R1-7B (an RFT-trained model) by up to +6.6 points on MathVista when guided by Qwen3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RFT-trained omni models, THINKOMNI-Qwen2.5-Omni-7B+Qwen3 consistently outperforms both Omni-R1 (e.g., 70.2 vs. 64.7 on MathVista, 32.9 vs. 25.4 on MathVision) and HumanOmniV2 (70.2 vs. 68.8 on MathVista, 75.5 vs. 75.3 on MMAU) across all six benchmarks, despite requiring no additional training. Compared to open-source omni models Baichuan-Omni-1.5 and Ola, THINKOMNI leads by 6.6 and 1.8 points on MathVista, and by 9.3 and 5.2 points on MMAU, respectively. Against closed-source models, THINKOMNI surpasses GPT-4o on MathVista (70.2 vs. 63.8), MathVision (32.9 vs. 30.4), MMAU (75.5 vs. 62.5), and DailyOmni (59.5 vs. 56.5), but trails Gemini-2.0-Flash on MathVista (70.2 vs. 73.1), MathVision (32.9 vs. 41.3), MathVerse (45.1 vs. 59.3), and DailyOmni (59.5 vs. 67.8). Among training-free baselines (Table 2), THINKOMNI outperforms VCD by +2.3 on MathVista and +1.6 on MMAU, while Average Logits Fusion and Caption-then-Answer degrade performance substantially (e.g., 55.0 and 61.0 vs. 68.8 on MathVista), confirming that naive logit averaging or one-way captioning is inferior to the adaptive contrastive mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the guidance decoding lineage (Contrastive Decoding, Visual Contrastive Decoding, ProxyTuning) by introducing a fundamentally cross-modal contrastive pair: a text-only LRM guides an omni-modal OLLM, rather than requiring both models to share input modalities. The core logit-mixing formula is inherited from Contrastive Decoding, the perceptual contrastive term adapts Visual Contrastive Decoding (replacing noise perturbation with direct modality removal), and the Jensen-Shannon divergence metric for measuring distributional disagreement is borrowed from DoLa. The key departure is the Stepwise Contrastive Scaling module, which replaces the fixed guidance weight α used in all prior contrastive decoding methods with a per-token adaptive weight determined online from the relative magnitudes of reasoning and perception divergences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that as new LRMs emerge faster than their multimodal counterparts, this training-free framework can immediately transfer improved reasoning to existing OLLMs without retraining, data annotation, or task-specific adaptation. However, the method requires shared token vocabularies between OLLM and LRM, incurs roughly 2.9× generation latency from three forward passes per step, and the LRM's inability to access omni-modal content limits its guidance to what is already in the textual trace. Open questions include what aspects of textual reasoning genuinely transfer across modalities, whether the approach extends to modalities beyond image/audio/video (e.g., 3D point clouds, protein structures), and whether the adaptive scaling principle generalizes to other inference-time control problems.
