## Problem

Generating realistic synthetic hypergraphs with hyperlink attributes is a critical but underexplored task: existing generative models target continuous data or pairwise graphs, and those that handle discrete structures ignore the multi-way, sparse, mixed-type nature of hypergraphs. No principled framework with theoretical guarantees exists for jointly modeling hyperlinks and their attributes in a generative setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261117/cropped_page9_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed set of n nodes, m observed hyperlinks (each a subset of [n]), and p-dimensional mixed-type attributes (continuous, binary) per hyperlink. It produces m̃ synthetic hyperlinks (new node subsets over the same fixed node set) with corresponding synthetic attributes, where m̃ is user-specified. Unlike methods that generate new nodes, ReLaSH operates on a fixed node vocabulary, making it suitable for tasks like generating new patient profiles over a fixed disease/symptom code set.

## Method

ReLaSH is a three-stage pipeline: (1) a likelihood-based joint embedding model maps each hyperlink and its attributes into a shared latent space partitioned into three blocks (k₁ for attributes-only, k₂ for shared, k₃ for hyperlinks-only), using a sigmoid factor model for hyperlink membership and a linear factor model for attributes, trained via projected gradient descent with identifiability constraints and SVD-based initialization; (2) a score-based diffusion generator (forward/reverse SDEs with denoising score matching) is trained on the learned embeddings to reconstruct the joint latent distribution; (3) new samples are drawn from the generator and decoded stochastically through the fitted likelihood model to yield synthetic hyperlinks and attributes. The key insight is that by reducing generation to a low-dimensional continuous latent space, the method circumvents the curse of dimensionality of the original high-dimensional discrete hypergraph space, and the paper proves a KL-divergence decomposition showing the overall generative error is dominated by the low-dimensional latent reconstruction rather than the ambient dimension.

## Results

Across three real-world hypergraph generation tasks, ReLaSH achieves the best or near-best performance on all five evaluation metrics (ΔHv, ΔXm, ΔXv, FED, a-FED). On the MIMIC-III patient-profile task, ReLaSHc-(7,0,2) attains the lowest FED (0.013×10⁻²) and a-FED (0.193×10⁻¹), outperforming the next-best VAE (FED 9.374×10⁻²) by over 700×. On the ERRN recipe task, ReLaSH-(5,0,6) achieves an exceptional FED of 0.003×10⁻¹ and a-FED of 0.048×10⁻¹, roughly 100× better than the strongest baseline TabPFGen (FED 0.297×10⁻¹). On the MADStat co-citation task, ReLaSHc-(2,7,8) records the best FED (0.947×10⁻¹) and is within 0.006×10⁻¹ of WGAN on a-FED (0.706 vs. 0.700).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReLaSH decisively outperforms all nine baselines on the Fréchet Embedding Distance (FED) and its bias-corrected variant (a-FED) across all three tasks. On MIMIC-III, ReLaSHc-(7,0,2) achieves FED 0.013×10⁻² versus Gau-Diff (39.731×10⁻²), RealNVP (27.685×10⁻²), WGAN (21.053×10⁻²), and VAE (9.374×10⁻²) — a 700×–3000× improvement. On the recipe task, ReLaSH-(5,0,6) records FED 0.003×10⁻¹, roughly 100× lower than the best baseline TabPFGen (0.297×10⁻¹) and ~3800× lower than VAE (11.553×10⁻¹). On co-citation, ReLaSHc-(2,7,8) attains the best FED (0.947×10⁻¹), narrowly beating WGAN (1.253×10⁻¹) and Gau-Diff (5.060×10⁻¹). WGAN is the closest competitor on a-FED for co-citation (0.700 vs. ReLaSHc's 0.706), and TabPFGen edges out ReLaSH on ΔHv for recipes (1.565 vs. 1.847×10⁻³), but ReLaSH dominates on the distributional metrics that capture joint structure. The tabular-data baselines (CTGAN, CTAB-GAN, CTAB-GAN+, ForestDiffusion, TabPFGen) are only evaluated on the recipe task because they do not scale to the larger MIMIC-III and co-citation datasets; among them, VAE and CTGAN perform worst on all metrics, while ForestDiffusion and TabPFGen are the strongest tabular baselines but still trail ReLaSH substantially on FED and a-FED.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReLaSH synthesizes three distinct research threads: (1) likelihood-based latent embedding models for sparse hypergraphs (Wu et al., 2024), (2) joint latent space models for network data with high-dimensional attributes (Zhang et al., 2022; Li et al., 2025), and (3) score-based generative modeling via SDEs (Song et al., 2020). The key departure from prior work is the explicit joint modeling of hyperlinks and their attributes within a single decomposed latent space (k₁, k₂, k₃), where the shared block k₂ captures dependence between structure and attributes. Unlike graph generative models (You et al., 2018) that handle only pairwise edges, or hypergraph generators (Wu et al., 2025; Chun et al., 2025) that omit hyperlink attributes, ReLaSH treats the discrete, sparse, mixed-type nature of attributed hypergraphs as a first-class design constraint, using a likelihood model for interpretability and a distribution-free generator for flexibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first generative framework with theoretical guarantees (KL-divergence decomposition, F-consistent estimation) for producing synthetic hypergraphs with hyperlink attributes, enabling applications in privacy-preserving medical data sharing, recipe creation, and academic citation simulation. The central theoretical insight—that the generative error is dominated by the low-dimensional latent space rather than the ambient hypergraph dimensionality—provides a principled justification for the two-stage architecture and circumvents the curse of dimensionality. Open questions include extending to dynamic/temporal hypergraphs, conditional generation, and uncertainty quantification for generated structures. A practical limitation is that the method requires a fixed node set and identifiability constraints, and the score-based generator is trained on estimated (not true) embeddings, introducing an additional error source that is analyzed but not fully characterized.
