## Problem

Existing debiasing methods for vision-language models modify internal encoder or decoder representations, which can degrade overall model utility and remain vulnerable to bias reintroduction from external sources such as RAG. No prior post-hoc approach operates directly on token-level logits during autoregressive generation to selectively suppress biased words while preserving essential contextual information.

## Input / Output

At each autoregressive decoding step, ALA consumes the model's raw logit vector, a frozen image-encoder representation (processed by a lightweight logistic-regression classifier to yield a target bias score s ∈ [-1,1]), and the partially generated text (scored by a small transformer-based text classifier to yield a current bias score α ∈ [-1,1]). The output is the same autoregressive text stream, but with token probabilities selectively perturbed only when the argmax token's precomputed bias-importance score |β| exceeds a threshold τ=0.1, yielding either a bias-aligned caption (matching the image's ground-truth attribute) or a neutralized one (sensitive attributes removed).

## Method

ALA derives a gradient-descent-like logit update via first-order Taylor expansion: Δz_i = -λ(α(z^t) − s)·∂α/∂z_i, which provably reduces the discrepancy between the text's bias score and the image's target bias at every step. Because the true gradient is intractable during decoding, the paper precomputes a fixed vocabulary-level importance vector β using Integrated Gradients on the text classifier, yielding the practical update z_i' = z_i − λ(α(z^t) − s)·β_i. Adjustment is applied selectively—only when the selected token's |β| ≥ τ—avoiding unnecessary perturbation of bias-irrelevant tokens. For neutralization, the target is set to s=0; for alignment, s is the image classifier's output; and for intersectional debiasing, adjustments from multiple attribute classifiers (e.g., gender and race) are summed into a single logit correction. The entire procedure is model-agnostic, requires no retraining of the VLM/LMM, and adds only ~1.2% inference overhead.

## Results

On image captioning (COCO), ALA-BA reduces the Composite Misclassification Rate (MR_C) from 2.91 to 2.34 on CLIP-CAP while preserving MaxMETEOR at 34.37 (vs. 34.51 baseline), and ALA-N reduces MR_C from 2.52 to 1.69 on BLIP with MaxMETEOR 25.56. On VQA-Task-1 (FACET), ALA-N cuts MR_C from 5.72 to 3.50 on PaliGemma and from 6.91 to 5.96 on LLaVA-1.5. On VQA-Task-2 (SocialCounterfactuals), ALA-N reduces the mean toxicity disparity D_mean from 6.08 to 3.81 on PaliGemma and from 0.70 to 0.60 on LLaVA-1.5, while the VQA-as-judge task (Task-3) shows near-zero accuracy degradation (D_WCA = −0.04) for ALA-BA on both models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALA outperforms all four baselines on the fairness-utility trade-off. DeAR is the weakest competitor: it worsens MR_C to 3.52 on CLIP-CAP and a catastrophic 8.51 on BLIP (vs. 2.52 baseline), indicating its adversarial residual approach actively introduces bias. CLIP-clip achieves the lowest |MR_M−MR_F| gap (0.37) on CLIP-CAP but at a significant quality cost (MaxMETEOR drops from 34.51 to 31.95), and it actually degrades MR_C on BLIP (3.04 vs. 2.52 baseline). SFID performs competitively on BLIP (MR_C 1.65) but sacrifices caption quality (MaxMETEOR 24.11 vs. 25.84). VDD, the only other logit-based method, achieves moderate fairness (MR_C 2.70 on CLIP-CAP, 2.04 on BLIP) but with a 101.5% inference time overhead. ALA-BA and ALA-N consistently match or beat the best baseline on MR_C while retaining within 0.5 points of baseline MaxMETEOR, and on the VQA-as-judge task (Table 8) ALA-BA shows D_WCA = −0.04, meaning zero measurable accuracy loss, whereas CLIP-clip, DeAR, and VDD all degrade accuracy by 0.37–2.23 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

ALA departs from the dominant line of post-hoc debiasing methods (CLIP-clip, DeAR, SFID, model steering) that modify encoder or decoder representations, arguing that such interventions distort essential information and degrade utility. It also distinguishes itself from VDD, which applies logit subtraction to cancel unconditional output biases for hallucination reduction, by introducing a dynamic, classifier-guided adjustment specifically targeting social bias misalignment. The method repurposes Integrated Gradients (Sundararajan et al., 2017) not for model interpretability but as a precomputed token-importance dictionary that drives selective logit steering, bridging the gradient-explanation literature with fair generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ALA offers a practical, model-agnostic debiasing tool that operates at inference time with only ~1.2% overhead, making it deployable on frozen LMMs without retraining and compatible with RAG pipelines where external knowledge can re-introduce bias. Its key limitation is dependence on external classifier accuracy, though the neutralization variant (s=0) provides a robust fallback for ambiguous inputs. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer-form generation, more nuanced intersectional biases beyond the tested gender/race/toxicity axes, and whether the precomputed β dictionary remains valid as base models are updated.
