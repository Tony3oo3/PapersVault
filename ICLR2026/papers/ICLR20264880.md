## Problem

The Orienteering Problem with Time Windows and Variable Profits (OPTWVP) requires jointly optimizing discrete routing decisions and continuous service-time allocations under a fixed time budget, but existing neural combinatorial optimization methods handle only routing and cannot capture the tight bidirectional coupling between path selection and service-time allocation. Heuristic and metaheuristic solvers rely on manually designed heuristics and exhaustive local search, limiting scalability and generalization. This paper tackles the gap by proposing a learning-based framework that explicitly decouples the two decision types while maintaining efficient, learnable coordination between them.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a complete undirected graph where each node carries spatial coordinates, a time window [s⁻, s⁺], a unit profit pᵢ, and a maximum service time dᵢ_max, while edges encode pairwise travel times. The output is a feasible visit sequence (path) together with a per-node continuous service-time allocation, jointly maximizing total collected reward Σ pᵢdᵢ. During generation, the service time is parameterized as a normalized ratio δdᵢ ∈ [0,1] (i.e., dᵢ/dᵢ_max), and feasibility masking dynamically prunes candidate nodes that would violate time-window or time-limit constraints at each decoding step.

## Method

DeCoST is a two-stage framework that decouples discrete routing from continuous service-time allocation. In Stage 1, a parallel decoder pairs a routing decoder (selecting the next node via softmax attention) with a Service Time Decoder (STD) that simultaneously predicts the normalized service-time ratio; the encoder uses Graphormer-style spatial encoding to inject edge-distance bias into attention scores, and feasibility masking enforces time-window and time-limit constraints during autoregressive construction. In Stage 2, given the fixed path, service-time allocation is cast as a linear program and solved by the STO algorithm, which greedily assigns service times in descending profit order while respecting downstream time-window constraints; Theorem 4.1 proves STO returns the global LP optimum via KKT conditions. A cross-stage supervisory signal, the profit-weighted time allocation ratio (pTAR) loss, compares the STD's initial prediction against the LP-optimal allocation to prevent premature convergence and encourage broader exploration of the service-time policy. The overall policy is trained with REINFORCE using a combined loss of the reward-based objective and the pTAR supervisory term.

## Results

On OPTWVP (n=50, TW=100), DeCoST achieves a score of 15.1 with a 1.06% optimality gap in 94 ms, closely approaching the exact B&C optimum (15.2) while being 2.1× faster. At n=100, TW=100, DeCoST scores 25.6 (1.97% gap) in 158 ms, outperforming ILS (24.9, 4.2%, 7122 ms) by 45× in runtime. On the large-scale n=500 setting, DeCoST reaches 79.6 (3.31% gap) in 1329 ms versus ILS's 78.2 (4.98%) in 8803 ms. On Solomon100, DeCoST achieves a score of 10680.3 in 0.29 s, surpassing ILS at both 10 s (9846.3) and 20 s (10616.9) time limits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeCoST outperforms all four baseline categories across every tested setting. Against the exact Gurobi B&C solver, DeCoST achieves near-optimal gaps (1.06% at n=50, 1.97% at n=100) while running 2–6× faster, and the gap widens dramatically at n=500 where B&C takes 68.4 s versus DeCoST's 1.3 s. Against the metaheuristic ILS, DeCoST wins on both score and runtime in every configuration (e.g., 15.1 vs 14.5 at n=50 with 94 ms vs 2109 ms; 79.6 vs 78.2 at n=500 with 1329 ms vs 8803 ms), and even ILS extended to 200 s (Appendix B.1) cannot close the gap. Against GFACS, DeCoST improves the score from 12.3 to 15.1 (n=50) and from 25.2 to 25.6 (n=100) while reducing runtime by 45–46×. Against POMO, the improvement is most dramatic: DeCoST raises the score from 11.3 to 15.1 (n=50) and from 11.5 to 25.6 (n=100), reducing the optimality gap from 25.3%/55.7% to 1.06%/1.97%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NCO line of work (POMO, GFACS) by introducing a parallel decoder that jointly predicts discrete routing decisions and continuous service-time allocations, a capability absent from prior constructive solvers that focus solely on routing. It draws on Graphormer-style spatial encoding for edge-aware attention and Bi et al.'s feasibility masking for constraint handling, but departs from both by adding a second-stage linear programming solver (STO) with a proven global optimality guarantee and a cross-stage pTAR supervisory loss that feeds optimal service times back into the first-stage policy. The two-stage decoupling strategy is conceptually related to predict-and-optimize frameworks for MIPs, but is tailored to the specific discrete-continuous coupling structure of OPTWVP rather than general mixed-integer settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OPTWVP captures realistic industrial scenarios (robotic scheduling, logistics with time-dependent rewards) where prior NCO methods are inapplicable due to their routing-only focus, and where metaheuristics like ILS require 10+ seconds per instance. The proven global optimality of the second-stage STO and the 20–45× inference speedup over ILS with superior solution quality make this a practically relevant solver for time-constrained routing with variable profits. Key limitations include the reduced batch parallelism when integrating with non-autoregressive solvers (GFACS), the single-vehicle focus (team extension is preliminary), and the fact that the STO optimality proof is specific to the LP structure of OPTWVP and may not generalize to more complex service-time functions. Open questions include extending the framework to multi-vehicle settings, non-linear profit functions, and dynamic/stochastic environments.
