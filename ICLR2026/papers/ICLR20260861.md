## Problem

Video understanding in multimodal LLMs is bottlenecked by context length: models miss key transition frames and lose coherence over long time scales. Token compression methods degrade on complex reasoning tasks due to irreversible information loss, while existing training-free sparse attention methods are not hardware-aligned or tailored to the spatio-temporal redundancy structure of video. No learnable, hardware-aware sparse attention framework existed that could scale video-language models to ultra-long (128K+) vision-text contexts.

## Input / Output

The model consumes video sampled at 4 fps (350–550 frames, max 50,176 pixels per frame) alongside a text question, with each frame encoded into 64 vision tokens forming frame-level KV blocks. At inference it supports up to 128K total context tokens, far exceeding the 36K training budget, and produces a free-form text answer. The hybrid design routes video tokens through sparse attention while text tokens retain standard grouped-query attention to preserve instruction-following fidelity.

## Method

VideoNSA builds on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and introduces a modality-split attention mechanism: video tokens use Native Sparse Attention (NSA) with three complementary branches—block compression (learnable MLP averaging over 64-token frames), top-k block selection (importance-scored salient blocks), and a 256-token sliding window—while text tokens use standard GQA. A two-layer MLP gate with sigmoid activation dynamically weights the three branch outputs per head and per timestep, enabling task-adaptive allocation of global vs. local attention. The model is trained end-to-end on 216K filtered video instruction pairs from LLaVA-Video-178K (4 fps, 350–550 frames, 36K max context) using AdamW with cosine schedule, requiring 4600 H100 GPU-hours. This learnable sparsity preserves all tokens (unlike compression) while restricting each query to only ~3.6% of the full attention budget at 128K context, achieving near-linear scaling in both time and memory.

## Results

VideoNSA achieves 60.0 on LongVideoBench, 51.8 on MLVU, 83.7 on TimeScope, 44.4 on LongTimeScope, 26.5 on Tomato, and 36.1 on VSIBench, outperforming the Qwen2.5-VL-7B dense baseline (58.7/51.2/81.0/40.7/22.6/29.7) across all six benchmarks. Notably, VideoNSA attains these results while using only 3.6% of the full attention budget at 128K context length. On the ultra-long LongTimeScope benchmark (10-hour videos), VideoNSA ties MInference at 44.4 and substantially outperforms all token compression methods (best: VScan at 31.1). On temporal reasoning (Tomato), VideoNSA leads all methods by a clear margin (26.5 vs. 23.7 for the next-best FlexPrefill).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three token compression baselines (FastV, VScan, VisionZip), VideoNSA wins on every benchmark, with the largest gaps on TimeScope (83.7 vs. 46.5 for FastV) and LongTimeScope (44.4 vs. 31.1 for VScan), confirming that irreversible token removal severely degrades temporal reasoning. Among the four training-free sparse attention baselines, VideoNSA outperforms Tri-Shape (59.5/49.2/82.7/28.4/22.1/34.9) on all six metrics, beats FlexPrefill (58.4/46.0/83.0/39.1/23.7/34.0) on all six, and surpasses XAttention (59.1/50.2/83.1/41.1/21.4/36.6) on LVB, MLVU, TimeScope, LTS, and Tomato while trailing slightly on VSIBench (36.1 vs. 36.6). VideoNSA ties MInference (59.2/49.2/82.7/44.4/23.0/36.5) on LongTimeScope (44.4) but leads on LVB (+0.8), MLVU (+2.6), TimeScope (+1.0), and Tomato (+3.5), while MInference edges ahead on VSIBench (36.5 vs. 36.1). Against other video LLMs (LLaVA-OneVision-7B, LLaVA-Video-7B, VideoLLaMA3-8B, InternVL2.5-8B, Video-XL-2), VideoNSA achieves the highest scores on all reported benchmarks. On the additional LSDBench and VideoEvalPro benchmarks, VideoNSA also leads all sparse attention baselines (55.2 vs. 52.3 for FlexPrefill on LSDBench; 29.4 vs. 28.9 for XAttention on VideoEvalPro).

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoNSA adapts Native Sparse Attention (NSA), a learnable hardware-aware sparse attention mechanism originally designed for long-context LLMs, to the video-language setting by applying it selectively to vision tokens while preserving grouped-query attention for text. This departs from two dominant prior lines: token compression methods (FastV, VisionZip, VScan) that irreversibly discard visual information, and training-free sparse attention methods (MInference, Tri-Shape, XAttention) that impose static, data-independent sparsity patterns. The hybrid modality-specific design—NSA for video, GQA for text—is the paper's key architectural departure, positioning it as the first end-to-end trainable sparse attention framework purpose-built for ultra-long video contexts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VideoNSA demonstrates that 128K-token video understanding is achievable with only 3.6% of the full attention budget, offering a practical path toward hour-scale video processing on consumer hardware. The compression branch emerging as the dominant latency bottleneck (despite being simple token averaging) highlights an open systems-level challenge for further kernel optimization. The finding that sparse-trained QKV weights transfer beneficially to dense attention suggests the training process itself encodes useful inductive biases beyond the sparsity pattern, a direction worth exploring. Limitations include evaluation only at 7B scale, a single training dataset (216K pairs from LLaVA-Video-178K), and modest absolute gains over the strongest training-free sparse baselines (1–3 points on most benchmarks), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger backbones or benefits from more diverse training data.
