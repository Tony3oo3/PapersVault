## Problem

Existing multimodal RAG benchmarks focus on simplified 1–2 hop QA with fixed retrieve-then-generate pipelines, leaving adaptive planning, long multi-hop reasoning, and cross-modal evidence integration largely unexplored. No prior dataset provides step-wise annotated reasoning chains with explicit retrieval modalities and verified hop necessity, making it impossible to diagnose whether MLLMs genuinely perform structured multimodal search-enhanced reasoning or merely exploit shortcuts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260275/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each example takes a complex natural-language question (avg. 33 tokens) optionally accompanied by an input image, and a local multimodal knowledge base of ~390K images and ~784K Wikipedia-derived documents. The system produces a final text answer (avg. 47 tokens, ~8 entities) together with a full step-wise reasoning chain where each hop specifies a sub-question, a retrieval modality (text search, image search by text query, or image search by input image), the top-1 retrieved evidence, and an intermediate answer. Chains average 3.7 hops and are constrained to five predefined reasoning topologies, with uniqueness enforced so no unused KB item can independently resolve any sub-question.

## Method

MC-SEARCH is constructed by clustering Wikipedia text and images, prompting Gemini-2.5-Flash to synthesize multi-hop questions with reasoning chains aligned to five topologies (Image-Initiated Chain, Text-Initiated Chain, Parallel Image-Text Fork, Multi-Images Fork, Text-Only Chain), then filtering via HAVE (Hop-wise Attribution and Verification of Evidence), which scores each hop's context utility (F1 drop upon removal) and navigational role (entity overlap with downstream sub-questions) to eliminate hallucinated or redundant steps, yielding 3,333 verified examples. For evaluation, the paper introduces process-level metrics—Hit per Step (HPS) for per-step retrieval fidelity via maximum-weight bipartite matching, Rollout Deviation (RD) for structural length alignment, and LLM-as-a-Judge for open-ended reasoning quality—alongside standard answer accuracy (F1, ΔF1, Golden F1). A unified agentic MM-RAG pipeline iteratively generates sub-queries, selects modality-aware retrieval actions, and reasons over top-1 evidence until the agent decides to terminate. SEARCH-ALIGN converts HAVE-verified chains into conversation-style traces (assistant poses sub-questions and reasons; user executes retrieval and returns results) and applies supervised fine-tuning on InternVL3.5-8B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B, providing process-level supervision that improves planning and retrieval fidelity by +13.7 F1 and +16.0 HPS on average for Qwen2.5-VL-7B.

## Results

On the MC-SEARCH benchmark (3,333 examples, avg. 3.79 hops), Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves the highest F1 across most reasoning topologies (e.g., 47.61 on Image-Initiated Chain, 45.30 on Text-Initiated Chain), while Qwen2.5-VL-7B lags significantly (26.30 F1 on Image-Initiated Chain). After SEARCH-ALIGN fine-tuning, Qwen2.5-VL-7B gains +19.4 F1 (to 45.70) and +17.1 HPS (to 33.59), while InternVL3.5-8B gains +3.2 F1 (to 42.27) and +9.9 HPS (to 32.49). Rollout Deviation drops sharply for both open-source models (QwenVL: 4.04→0.70; InternVL: 1.58→0.94), indicating that the primary weakness of open-source MLLMs lies in retrieval planning and step-level alignment rather than basic perception.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among closed-source models, Gemini-2.5-Pro leads on F1 for four of five topologies (e.g., 47.61 on Image-Initiated Chain vs. 44.10 for Gemini-2.5-Flash and 37.80 for Claude-3.7-Sonnet), though it is not uniformly best on HPS (25.90 vs. 31.46 for Flash). GPT-4o-Mini and Claude-3.7-Sonnet trail Gemini-2.5-Pro by roughly 10 F1 points on most topologies. Among open-source models, InternVL3.5-8B consistently outperforms Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 8–13 F1 points before alignment. SEARCH-ALIGN closes much of this gap: Qwen2.5-VL-7B jumps from 26.30 to 45.70 F1 on Image-Initiated Chain (nearly matching Gemini-2.5-Pro's 47.61), and on Text-Initiated Chain it reaches 45.07 F1, essentially tying Gemini-2.5-Pro (45.30). The HPS gains (+17.1 for QwenVL, +9.9 for InternVL) and RD reductions (−3.34 and −0.64 respectively) confirm that the alignment primarily fixes retrieval planning and step-coverage errors rather than improving raw perception.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: multimodal RAG benchmarks (OK-VQA, MMSearch, Dyn-VQA, MRAG, M2RAG) that evaluate visual evidence retrieval but restrict reasoning to 1–2 hops with fixed retrieve-then-generate pipelines, and agentic RAG systems (Search-o1, Search-R1, CoRAG) that model retrieval as iterative sequential decision-making but remain text-only. MC-SEARCH departs from both by introducing long (avg. 3.7 hops), step-wise annotated multimodal reasoning chains across five distinct topologies, verified by the HAVE procedure for hop necessity, and by proposing process-level metrics (Hit per Step, Rollout Deviation, LLM-as-a-Judge) that go beyond final-answer accuracy. The SEARCH-ALIGN framework further extends supervised fine-tuning from answer-level to trajectory-level supervision, building on the observation that open-source MLLMs' bottleneck is retrieval planning rather than perceptual understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MC-SEARCH fills a clear gap: no prior benchmark simultaneously provides long structured reasoning chains, multimodal evidence, and step-wise annotations needed to diagnose *where* in a multi-hop agentic pipeline a model fails. The finding that image-retrieval coverage collapses to near zero (0.66% for InternVL) without explicit image input exposes a critical modality-planning weakness that answer-level metrics would mask. SEARCH-ALIGN's large gains (+13.7 F1 for Qwen2.5-VL-7B) demonstrate that process-supervised training on verified chains is a practical path to closing the open-source gap. Open questions include whether these findings generalize beyond Wikipedia-sourced knowledge, whether the top-1 retrieval constraint is too restrictive for real-world deployment, and whether the five topologies capture the full space of agentic multimodal reasoning patterns.
