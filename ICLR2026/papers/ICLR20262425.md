## Problem

PPO-based RLHF consistently narrows the policy's output distribution during on-policy optimization—entropy falls, repetition rises, and many plausible SFT completions receive vanishing probability—yet this 'policy contraction' lacked a clean, decoding-agnostic diagnostic and a principled training-time remedy. Standard PPO treats entropy and KL as secondary scalarized terms with fixed weights, making the optimization brittle to reward scale and critic noise, so once entropy collapses below a threshold, exploration becomes ineffective and the policy locks onto a narrow set of high-reward modes.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RLHF pipelines: text prompts are fed to the policy to generate completions, which are scored by a preference-trained reward model. A key diagnostic input is the set of SFT completions sampled from the frozen reference policy, used to compute the Support Retention Ratio (SRR)—the fraction of reference completions whose length-normalized log-likelihood under the trained policy exceeds a percentile threshold. Outputs are text completions evaluated on preference win rate, diversity metrics (Self-BLEU, Distinct-2), and SRR across decoding settings (temperature 0.7–1.0, top-p 0.8–0.95).

## Method

CaPPO replaces PPO's scalarized loss with a minimum-norm multi-gradient update that treats reward (PPO clipped surrogate), token entropy, and KL-to-reference as three peer maximization objectives. At each step, it computes the three gradients, preconditions them with a diagonal metric, and solves a small 3-variable quadratic program over the simplex to find the minimum-norm convex combination, yielding a Pareto-improving direction that avoids any single objective dominating. A second component, an entropy-scheduling controller, tracks an EMA of per-token entropy and adaptively adjusts the entropy coefficient β via a clipped proportional update toward a target, injecting exploration pressure when entropy collapses and relaxing it when sufficient. A guarded line search enforces KL and entropy floors on the step size, and the entire procedure remains compatible with PPO's clipped surrogate, adding only ~3% peak memory and ~7% throughput cost over standard PPO.

## Results

On a macro-average across HH-RLHF, Summarize-from-Feedback, and UltraFeedback with Qwen2-7B, CaPPO achieves 71.2% preference win rate (vs. 50.0% SFT), 0.27 Distinct-2, 0.33 Self-BLEU, and 0.82 SRR. Across all four backbones (Qwen2-7B, Qwen2.5-14B, Mistral-7B-Instruct, Llama-3-8B-Instruct) and three datasets, CaPPO delivers 2–4 points higher win rate than PPO (e.g., 69.0% vs. 65.1% for Qwen2.5-14B on HH-RLHF) while improving SRR by 0.20–0.30 and Distinct-2 by 0.06–0.10. Throughput is 92–94% of PPO with ~3% additional peak memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CaPPO achieves the highest win rate (71.2%) among all methods, edging out GRPO (71.0%) by 0.2 points while substantially outperforming PPO (67.4%) by 3.8 points and VinePPO (68.6%) by 2.6 points. On diversity and support metrics, CaPPO dominates all on-policy baselines: it reduces Self-BLEU from 0.48 (PPO) to 0.33, raises Distinct-2 from 0.17 to 0.27, and lifts SRR from 0.55 to 0.82. Against off-policy methods (DPO, IPO, ORPO, KTO, RRHF), CaPPO wins decisively on win rate (71.2% vs. 61.9–64.7%) and Self-BLEU, but trails on SRR (0.82 vs. 0.85–0.92), reflecting that off-policy methods inherently preserve more of the SFT distribution since they never perform on-policy updates. CaPPO also outperforms the simpler PPO+Entropy baseline (68.8% win rate, 0.66 SRR) on every metric, confirming that the Pareto multi-gradient update provides gains beyond entropy scheduling alone. The gains are consistent across all four model backbones, with CaPPO improving win rate by 2–4 points over PPO and SRR by 0.20–0.30 regardless of architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

CaPPO extends standard PPO-based RLHF by reframing the entropy and KL regularization terms from fixed-weight auxiliary penalties into peer objectives optimized via minimum-norm multi-gradient descent, drawing on Pareto-stationary multi-objective optimization (Parisi et al., 2014; Momma et al., 2022; Liu & Vicente, 2024). It departs from the scalarization paradigm that dominates PPO practice and from RL-free methods (DPO, KTO, ORPO) that avoid on-policy drift but cannot directly control it. The paper also builds on recent analyses showing RL reweights rather than expands support (Yue et al., 2025) and on degeneration/diversity literature (Holtzman et al., 2019; Li et al., 2015), but introduces SRR as a decoding-agnostic diagnostic that prior work lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Policy contraction is a widely observed but poorly quantified side effect of RLHF; SRR provides a clean, decoding-independent metric, and CaPPO offers a drop-in, principled remedy that simultaneously improves win rate and diversity across four model families and three datasets. The modest computational overhead (~7% throughput loss) makes it practical for production pipelines. Open questions include formal convergence guarantees for Pareto updates under trust-region constraints, interaction with inference-time diversity controllers, and whether the approach transfers to verifiable-reward reasoning settings where contraction may manifest differently.
