## Problem

Instruction-based face editing models struggle with precise attribute control and identity preservation, particularly under large expression or pose changes. Existing landmark-based DiT editors rasterize landmarks into dense 2D images and encode them via VAE, which encourages pixel-level template copying rather than geometric reasoning, causing identity drift when target landmarks deviate substantially from the source. The paper addresses this by replacing dense pixel-wise landmark conditioning with compact discrete tokens that decouple geometry from appearance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source face image, a natural-language editing instruction (e.g., 'make her smile gently and turn her head 30° left'), and 68 target facial landmark coordinates as input. The landmarks can be supplied directly or inferred by a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-3B predictor that outputs a machine-parsable list of (X, Y) pairs on a normalized 512×512 canvas via structured chain-of-thought reasoning. The output is a photorealistic edited face image with preserved identity; the landmark predictor additionally outputs the predicted target landmarks for user verification and interactive control.

## Method

LaTo quantizes raw 68-point landmark coordinates into discrete facial tokens via a VQ-VAE-style codebook (8,192 entries, 3,072-dim codes), producing compact embeddings aligned with image-token dimensionality while preserving geometric structure. A location-mapped positional encoding anchors each landmark token to its physical (x, y) position in the latent grid using modified 3D RoPE, ensuring spatially discontinuous tokens guide the correct facial region. All modal tokens (text, source image, landmark, noisy image) are concatenated into a unified sequence and processed through DiT blocks with multi-modal attention, where the 68 landmark tokens interact with 1,024 noisy image tokens at comparable computational cost. A landmark-aware classifier-free guidance scheme replaces zeroed landmark coordinates with learnable unconditional tokens during unconditional passes, preventing reference-face copying at high guidance weights. The system is fine-tuned from Step1X-Edit with LoRA (rank 64) on HFL-150K, a 150K+ face-pair dataset with fine-grained expression and pose instructions, and a lightweight VLM landmark predictor bridges user instructions to precise geometric targets.

## Results

On HFL-150K, LaTo achieves SC=0.832, VQ=0.749, NA=0.805, and IP=0.634, outperforming the strongest fine-tuned baseline (FLUX.1-Kontext†) by 4.6% in semantic consistency and 7.8% in identity preservation. On the GEdit-Bench/ICE-Bench face-attribute subset, LaTo reaches SC=0.829 and IP=0.651, again leading all competitors. The landmark predictor (fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-3B) achieves 73.0% human-verified accuracy, surpassing Gemini 2.5 Pro (61.3%) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (59.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaTo outperforms all eight baselines on semantic consistency (SC) and identity preservation (IP) across both evaluation sets. Against the strongest fine-tuned competitor FLUX.1-Kontext†, LaTo gains +4.6% SC (0.832 vs 0.786) and +7.8% IP (0.634 vs 0.593) on HFL-150K, though FLUX.1-Kontext† retains a slight edge on natural appearance (NA: 0.816 vs 0.805). Compared to Step1X-Edit† (the base architecture fine-tuned on the same HFL-150K data), LaTo improves by +2.8% SC, +2.4% VQ, and +6.3% IP, confirming the benefit of landmark tokenization over the baseline's rendered-image conditioning. On the GEdit-Bench/ICE-Bench subset, LaTo leads Bagel by +3.2% SC and +7.2% IP, and surpasses OmniGen by +9.2% SC and +14.8% IP. For the landmark predictor sub-task, the fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-3B (73.0% accuracy) beats Gemini 2.5 Pro (61.3%) by 11.7 points and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (59.7%) by 13.3 points under unified prompting, demonstrating that task-specific fine-tuning with structured CoT supervision substantially narrows the gap to much larger general-purpose VLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaTo builds directly on the Step1X-Edit DiT framework (unified multi-modal token processing, 3D RoPE) and repurposes VQ-VAE-style discrete tokenization for a new input modality: raw 2D landmark coordinates. It departs from prior landmark-conditioned editors (OminiControl, OmniGen) that rasterize landmarks into dense pixel images and encode them via a visual VAE, arguing that this pixel-wise conditioning encourages template copying and identity drift under large geometric changes. The paper also extends the instruction-based editing lineage (InstructPix2Pix, AnyEdit, BAGEL, FLUX.1-Kontext) by introducing an explicit geometric control channel and a VLM-based landmark predictor that bridges natural-language instructions to precise coordinate-level guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HFL-150K dataset (302K samples, real + synthetic, with fine-grained instructions and landmarks) fills a notable gap in face-editing benchmarks and will likely become a standard training/evaluation resource. The landmark-tokenizer-plus-location-mapped-PE recipe offers a lightweight, efficient alternative to dense image-based conditioning that could transfer to other structured-geometry tasks in DiT pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond 68-point 2D landmarks to 3D morphable models or multi-person scenes, and whether the VLM-based landmark predictor's accuracy is sufficient for production use without manual correction.
