## Problem

While positional encodings in Transformers are understood to provide distance attenuation and translation invariance, the mechanistic differences between additive (Absolute, T5, ALiBi) and multiplicative (RoPE) schemes in how they couple token content with position remain unexplained. This gap is highlighted by RoPE's paradoxical behavior: despite strong theoretical properties supporting length generalization, it can underperform simpler relative PEs or even no-PE models on certain tasks, and no prior work has identified the underlying training dynamics responsible.

## Input / Output

The method operates on random token sequences of length up to 128 drawn from a 574-token vocabulary, processed by a 6-layer Transformer decoder (d_model=256, 16 heads). Task 1 presents sequences containing two unique 'trigger' words and requires the model to classify their relative distance into binned categories; Task 2 presents sequences with up to 20 predefined trigger words and requires predicting the total count, making position a nuisance variable. Both tasks are fully synthetic with 100K and 50K training examples respectively, enabling precise isolation of positional reasoning from linguistic structure.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified Toeplitz-matrix framework that decomposes attention logits into four Gram-matrix terms (content-content, content-position, position-content, position-position) plus an optional bias, showing that additive PEs contribute a Toeplitz component via G_{q^p,k^p} or an explicit bias B, while RoPE applies a Toeplitz kernel G_e multiplicatively via a Hadamard product over the entire logit matrix. This formal distinction reveals that RoPE's multiplicative coupling enables content-conditioned positional reasoning (expressive advantage) but introduces a strong inductive bias that concentrates positional learning into a single shallow-layer attention head — the 'single-head deposit pattern.' The paper validates this through two synthetic tasks, head-wise causal ablation, and three targeted ablations (injecting absolute PE, reducing RoPE-enabled heads, and testing MLA architecture), then provides a theoretical proof via gradient seed analysis and Szegö's theorem showing the deposit pattern is an inherent consequence of RoPE's multiplicative structure, with exponential amplification of the dominant head's gradient signal during backpropagation.

## Results

On the Position-Sensitive task (Task 1), RoPE achieves 92.64% test accuracy, decisively outperforming all other PE methods and converging fastest with a minimal generalization gap. On the Position-Agnostic task (Task 2), RoPE scores 69.43%, underperforming NoPE (77.69%) and Relative PE, while ALiBi suffers the most severe degradation due to its rigid, non-adaptive bias. The MLA hybrid architecture achieves 88.34% on Task 1 and 97.41% on Task 2, nearly matching RoPE on the position-sensitive task while dramatically improving on the position-agnostic one. Ablating a single head in Layer 1 of the RoPE model causes a ~60% accuracy drop on Task 1, confirming the single-head deposit pattern.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoPE dominates on the Position-Sensitive task (92.64%), outperforming Absolute PE (second best), T5 Relative Bias, ALiBi, NoPE (3.51%), and the Random embedding baseline, which overfits without generalizing. On the Position-Agnostic task, the ranking inverts: NoPE (77.69%) and Relative PE (which learns to attenuate its bias toward zero) perform best, while ALiBi suffers the most severe degradation due to its fixed, data-agnostic Toeplitz slope. RoPE (69.43%) underperforms NoPE on Task 2, revealing that its multiplicative positional coupling, while powerful for content-position reasoning, introduces a harmful inductive bias when position is irrelevant. The MLA hybrid (88.34% / 97.41%) nearly matches RoPE on Task 1 while dramatically outperforming it on Task 2, demonstrating that preventing over-specialization via parallel NoPE+RoPE pathways yields a more robust balance. The Random embedding baseline overfits on both tasks, confirming that non-Toeplitz structure lacks the translation invariance needed to learn generalizable positional rules.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on RoPE (Su et al., 2024) as its primary analytical subject but departs from prior PE research by introducing a unified Toeplitz-matrix framework that categorizes all positional encodings into additive versus multiplicative mechanisms, going beyond the two previously recognized properties (distance attenuation and translation invariance). It extends the mechanistic interpretability line of work (Zuo et al., 2025; Kazemnejad et al., 2023; Haviv et al., 2022) that showed models can learn positional information without explicit PE, by explaining *why* RoPE suppresses such latent representations through its multiplicative coupling. The spectral-theoretic analysis (Szegö's theorem, Hadamard product bounds) and the gradient-flow proof of the deposit pattern represent a novel theoretical angle not present in prior empirical PE studies, while the MLA comparison connects to the DeepSeek-V3 architecture (Liu et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-head deposit pattern provides a concrete mechanistic explanation for RoPE's well-documented performance paradox—strong theoretical properties yet inconsistent practical advantages—and suggests that the multiplicative structure creates a 'winner-takes-all' training dynamic that compresses positional reasoning into a fragile, low-rank representation. The practical implication is that hybrid architectures (MLA-style) that explicitly separate position-sensitive and position-agnostic pathways can mitigate over-specialization, offering a design principle for future PE schemes. Key open questions remain: whether the deposit pattern persists in large-scale LLMs, its direct causal link to length extrapolation failures (hypothesized but not empirically validated here), and whether the synthetic-task findings generalize to compositional tasks requiring multiple simultaneous positional relations.
