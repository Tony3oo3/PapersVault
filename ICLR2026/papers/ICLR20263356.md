## Problem

Despite promising empirical performance of feedback-based quantum reservoir computing, no quantitative approximation error bounds or universality guarantees existed for recurrent quantum neural networks (RQNNs) with linear readouts. Previous QRC universality results relied on polynomial output layers and the Stone-Weierstrass theorem, which are impractical for real-time applications. This paper asks whether RQNNs can universally approximate arbitrary fading memory filters with linear readouts, and precisely how many qubits and circuit parameters are needed for a given accuracy.

## Input / Output

At each discrete time step t, the RQNN consumes a d-dimensional input vector z_t ∈ R^d together with the previous N-dimensional reservoir state x_{t-1} ∈ R^N, which is fed back as the circuit's state input. The quantum circuit is re-initialized to |0⟩^⊗n at every step (feedback protocol), producing a new reservoir state x_t ∈ R^N whose components are computed from measurement probabilities of the circuit. A fixed linear readout y_t = W x_t ∈ R^m then maps the reservoir state to the final output. The sequential, real-time nature means only one time step of circuit execution is needed per output, unlike restarting or rewinding protocols that replay past inputs.

## Method

The RQNN architecture uses uniformly controlled quantum gates: n parallel rotation blocks (each a product of Z-rotations encoding the state and input, followed by a fixed Y-rotation) are selected by log₂(n) control qubits, requiring only O(log(1/ε)) qubits for accuracy ε. Proposition 4.1 shows the circuit output reduces to a sum of n cosines with learnable parameters, making the state map a variational quantum circuit. The key technical contribution is proving approximation bounds for the state map jointly with its first derivatives (Propositions 4.2, 4.4), which is essential for controlling error propagation through the feedback loop. Using Fourier-based random feature constructions under Barron-type integrability conditions, the paper shows the L∞ approximation error decays as 1/√n independent of input and state dimensions. Theorem 4.6 then lifts this to filter-level bounds for contracting state-space systems, and Theorem 4.8 extends universality to all fading memory, causal, time-invariant filters by adding linear preprocessing maps that enforce the echo state property without requiring contraction.

## Results

The paper's main quantitative result (Theorem 4.6) shows that RQNNs uniformly approximate the filters induced by any contracting Barron-type state-space system with error bounded by (1/(1−λ))·(√N · max_j C_j^∞)/√n, where λ is the contraction constant. The approximation rate of 1/√n is free from the curse of dimensionality, being independent of both the input dimension d and the state-space dimension N. The required number of qubits scales only as O(⌈log₂(ε⁻¹)⌉) and the number of circuit weights as O(ε⁻²) to achieve approximation error ε. Theorem 4.8 further establishes qualitative universality: RQNNs with linear readouts can uniformly approximate any causal, time-invariant, fading-memory filter to arbitrary accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include experimental comparisons or benchmark tables, as it is a purely theoretical work. The closest comparison is with classical RNN approximation bounds from Gonon et al. (2023, Theorem 3), which also achieve a 1/√n rate for state-space systems driven by Barron-type functions. However, the QRNN result requires a strictly weaker Fourier integrability condition: for Sobolev functions F ∈ H^s(R^{N+d}), the QRNN bound holds for any s > (N+d)/2 + 4, whereas the classical RNN bound requires the stronger condition s > N + d + 3. This means the quantum architecture can approximate a broader class of target dynamics with the same convergence rate. Additionally, the RQNN universality result (Theorem 4.8) extends to all fading-memory filters without requiring contractivity or Barron-type conditions on the target, matching the scope of classical echo state network universality results while using only a linear readout.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feedforward QNN architecture of Gonon & Jacquier (2025) to a recurrent setting by introducing a feedback loop, and adopts the feedback protocol from Kobayashi et al. (2024) where measured observables are reinjected as circuit parameters at each time step. It departs from prior QRC universality results (Chen & Nurdin, 2019; Nokkala et al., 2021; Sannia et al., 2024), which relied on polynomial output layers and the Stone-Weierstrass theorem, by proving universality with linear readouts. The analysis also builds on the classical reservoir computing universality framework of Grigoryeva & Ortega (2018) and Gonon & Ortega (2020; 2021), but requires novel techniques to handle the feedback loop, specifically approximating functions jointly with their derivatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide quantitative approximation error bounds and prove universality for recurrent quantum neural networks, establishing that RQNNs can approximate arbitrary fading memory filters with linear readouts and without the curse of dimensionality (qubit count grows only logarithmically in 1/ε). The results are significant because linear readouts are what is actually used in experimental implementations, making the theory directly relevant to NISQ-era quantum reservoir computing. Open questions include whether the variational training approach avoids barren plateaus at scale, how the theory extends to non-contractive or high-roughness dynamics, and whether randomizing the recurrent layer (as in classical reservoir computing) preserves the approximation guarantees.
