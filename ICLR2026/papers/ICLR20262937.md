## Problem

Contrastive multi-view clustering relies on a cross-view consistency prior that is frequently violated in web-collected data, where noisy correspondence (NC) manifests as category-level mismatch (same-class samples treated as negatives) and sample-level mismatch (misaligned or unalignable pairs). Existing reweighting and realignment strategies address only instance-level errors while overlooking category-level semantics and samples that lack any valid counterpart, leading to unreliable cluster structures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted multi-view feature vectors (e.g., PHOG+GIST, DECAF+VGG19, or ViT+BERT embeddings) with no requirement for verified cross-view pairings; it tolerates up to 80% mismatch ratio and 50% corruption ratio in the input. Output is a shared embedding space from which cluster assignments are obtained via K-means, along with a soft many-to-many correspondence matrix between views that reveals latent category-level relationships beyond the given (potentially noisy) pairs.

## Method

CorreGen formulates noisy correspondence learning as maximum likelihood estimation over latent cross-view correspondences, solved via an EM algorithm. In the E-step, GMM-guided marginals (Mahalanobis distance mapped through an exponential kernel and curve-shaping function) determine per-sample alignment mass, while an entropy-regularized optimal transport problem with virtual samples (absorbing unalignable outliers) yields a soft joint distribution via Sinkhorn iterations. In the M-step, the embedding network is updated to maximize the expected log-likelihood under the estimated posterior, using a softmax-normalized similarity parameterization. The paper proves that standard InfoNCE is a special case when marginals are uniform and the posterior degenerates to the identity, and the framework is implemented on top of DIVIDE's feature extraction with a warmup phase before EM activation.

## Results

CorreGen consistently achieves the best clustering accuracy across all four datasets (Scene15, LandUse21, Caltech101, UMPC-Food101) at every mismatch ratio from 0% to 80%. On the clean setting (MR=0%), it reaches 50.25% ACC on Scene15, 68.52% ACC on Caltech101, and 49.77% ACC on UMPC-Food101. Under severe noise (MR=80%), it maintains 40.96% ACC on Scene15 and 43.00% ACC on UMPC-Food101, outperforming the next-best method (CANDY) by 12.69 and 15.41 percentage points respectively. The paper reports up to ~10% absolute accuracy improvement on the challenging UMPC-Food101 dataset compared to prior state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CorreGen outperforms all seven baselines (DCP, SURE, GCFAgg, CGCN, DIVIDE, CANDY, ROLL) on every dataset and every mismatch ratio tested. The most dramatic gains appear on UMPC-Food101, where the generative objective yields 13.57 pp over DIVIDE (49.77 vs. 36.20 ACC at MR=0%) and 15.41 pp over CANDY (43.00 vs. 27.59 at MR=80%), confirming the paper's claim of ~10% improvement on this noisy real-world benchmark. On Caltech101, CorreGen leads by 0.88 pp over CANDY at MR=0% and by 10.57 pp at MR=80%, showing that the advantage grows with noise. ROLL, the most recent noisy-correspondence baseline, degrades sharply on Caltech101 (17.83→20.83 ACC from MR=0% to 80%) while CorreGen remains stable (68.52→64.74), highlighting the robustness of the EM-based correspondence discovery. On Scene15 and LandUse21, CorreGen also leads consistently, with margins of 2.64 pp over ROLL on Scene15 at MR=0% and 3.30 pp over CANDY on LandUse21 at MR=80%. The ablation (Table 3) further confirms that replacing the generative objective with vanilla InfoNCE causes a 3.42 pp drop on Scene15 and 2.13 pp on UMPC-Food101 under noisy conditions (MR=0.2, CR=0.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

CorreGen departs from the dominant discriminative paradigm in contrastive multi-view clustering, where noisy correspondence is handled via pairwise reweighting or realignment of given positive/negative pairs. Instead, it reframes the problem as maximum likelihood estimation over latent cross-view correspondences, solved through an EM algorithm whose E-step combines GMM-guided marginal estimation with entropy-regularized optimal transport (Sinkhorn iterations) to produce soft many-to-many assignments. The framework builds on the DIVIDE contrastive architecture as its backbone and draws on recent OT-based clustering work (Deng et al., 2025; Fu et al., 2025) for the joint distribution estimation, while proving that standard InfoNCE emerges as a degenerate special case when marginals are uniform and posteriors collapse to identity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The generative formulation offers a principled way to handle both category-level and sample-level noisy correspondence simultaneously, which is critical for web-collected multi-view data where pairwise noise ratios can exceed 20% and manual cleaning is infeasible. The virtual-sample mechanism for unalignable outliers and the GMM-based down-weighting of low-confidence samples provide practical tools that prior reweighting/realignment methods lack. Limitations include reliance on the GMM assumption for cluster structure (which may fail for highly non-Gaussian or overlapping clusters), testing primarily on two-view settings, and the method being validated on top of a single base architecture (DIVIDE). Open questions include whether the EM framework scales to many-view or streaming settings, and whether the Sinkhorn-based E-step can be replaced by learned transport plans for greater flexibility.
