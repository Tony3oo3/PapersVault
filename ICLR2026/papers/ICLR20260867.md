## Problem

Policy gradient methods for LLM reasoning (e.g., GRPO, PPO) suffer from optimization instabilities—catastrophic updates and policy collapse—driven by the nonstationary RL objective and high-variance gradient estimates. Current implementations resort to conservative hyperparameters (very low learning rates, thousands of generations per update) to maintain stability, which is computationally expensive and sample-inefficient. The optimization dynamics of RL at LLM scale, particularly the role of second-order geometry in causing or preventing instability, remain largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes math reasoning prompts, LLM-generated trajectories (token sequences), and accuracy-based reward signals, operating on the Qwen2.5-Math-7B model. It produces updated policy parameters and a per-token binary acceptance/rejection mask that determines which tokens contribute to the gradient update. The curvature computation exploits the sparsity of selective sampling (top-k/nucleus), where only k < 100 tokens have non-zero probability per step, reducing the effective parameter subspace from K×d_i to k×d_i and keeping memory and compute at O(k·d_i) per sample.

## Method

CAPO introduces a last-layer computational model that approximates the Hessian and Fisher Information Matrix directional curvatures (m_H and m_F) without materializing full d×d matrices, using Kronecker-product structure and sparse gradient representations. Given a batch of trajectories, it partitions them into subsets, computes a proposed Adam-style update step for each subset, and evaluates the predicted objective shift and policy KL shift under the model. Subsets violating trust-region thresholds (δ_H ≤ m_H ≤ δ_H^high, m_F ≤ δ_F) are rejected, effectively masking out tokens that would induce abrupt policy or objective changes. Theoretically, the paper proves monotonic policy improvement under bounded curvature and step-norm assumptions, and empirically shows that rejecting fewer than 8% of tokens prevents policy collapse under aggressive learning regimes, yielding up to 30× sample efficiency gains over standard GRPO.

## Results

CAPO achieves up to 30× greater sample efficiency on MATH and 9× fewer completions on the TEST benchmark (average of GSM8K, MATH500, OlympiadBench, MinervaMath, GPQA:Diamond, AMC23, AIME24, AIME25) compared to standard conservative GRPO. Under an aggressive update regime (5× higher learning rate, 12× smaller batch size), all baseline methods (GRPO-A, Dr.GRPO, REINFORCE) suffer policy collapse, while CAPO maintains stable performance throughout training. The intervention is minimal: token rejection peaks at ~8% early in training and drops below 2% for the remainder.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard conservative GRPO, CAPO requires 30× fewer training completions to reach comparable accuracy on MATH and 9× fewer on the TEST benchmark, demonstrating that curvature-aware token masking enables aggressive hyperparameters without sacrificing stability. Against GRPO (A) (aggressive regime), CAPO is the only method that avoids policy collapse; GRPO (A) degrades below the base model's performance and cannot recover. Dr.GRPO and REINFORCE also collapse under the same aggressive regime, but when augmented with CAPO's curvature-aware selection (Dr.CAPO, ReinCAPO), both methods are stabilized and outperform their unmodified counterparts. The paper's results are presented as accuracy-vs-completions curves (Figures 1–2) rather than a single numerical table, so exact per-benchmark accuracy values are not tabulated; the headline claim is the 30× sample-efficiency gap on MATH under aggressive updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) by introducing a second-order geometric model of the optimization landscape—specifically, last-layer approximations of the Hessian and Fisher Information Matrix—that is tractable at LLM scale. Conceptually, it draws on trust-region principles from TRPO (Schulman et al., 2015) for its acceptance criteria, but departs from both TRPO's explicit trust-region constraints and from Castanyer et al. (2025), who apply K-FAC natural gradients in general deep RL, by instead using curvature information to perform token-level data selection (masking). Unlike heuristic stabilizers (PPO clipping, KL regularization, entropy control), CAPO provides a unified second-order framework that explains and addresses instabilities rather than patching individual symptoms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30× sample-efficiency gain over conservative GRPO, achieved with under 8% token rejection, is practically significant for reducing the computational cost of LLM post-training, where generation dominates expense. However, the evidence is limited to a single 7B model (Qwen2.5-Math) on math reasoning, and the headline comparison is against a deliberately conservative baseline rather than the aggressive regime where CAPO's true advantage (stability) is demonstrated. Open questions include whether the last-layer approximation remains informative for deeper layers or larger models, whether thresholds transfer across tasks and base policies without retuning, and whether the framework extends to other intervention mechanisms beyond hard token masking.
