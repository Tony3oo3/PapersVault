## Problem

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for autonomous driving suffer from a 'supervision deficit': their large model capacity is supervised only by sparse, low-dimensional action signals (e.g., a few waypoints), leaving most representational power underutilized. This deficit cannot be overcome by simply scaling action-only data, and in fact large VLA models can underperform smaller specialized BEV models without sufficient supervision. The paper asks whether dense, self-supervised world modeling can remedy this bottleneck and unlock true data scaling.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a deeply interleaved multimodal sequence of language instructions, front-view images (256×144), and FAST-tokenized past action trajectories over a history window (up to 6 vision-action pairs for pretraining, 2 for fine-tuning). It outputs a continuous 6-waypoint trajectory over 3 seconds at 2 Hz (decoded from discrete action tokens via the FAST detokenizer) and, during training, a predicted future image (either as discrete visual tokens via MoVQGAN or as denoised latents via a diffusion decoder). Two architectural variants exist: a VQ variant using Emu3 (8B) with discrete visual tokens, and a ViT variant using Qwen2.5-VL (7B) with continuous features.

## Method

DriveVLA-W0 augments standard action prediction with a dense world-modeling objective that forces the VLA backbone to predict future visual scenes, providing self-supervised signal at every timestep. For the discrete-token (VQ) variant, this is an autoregressive next-token prediction over visual tokens; for the continuous-feature (ViT) variant, a latent diffusion model denoises the future frame conditioned on the VLA's current visual and action features. Training follows a two-stage paradigm: first, the backbone is pretrained on a 6VA interleaved sequence with a joint action + world-model loss; second, a lightweight 500M MoE-based Action Expert is integrated via Joint Attention, supervised only by the action loss on a 2VA sequence, reducing inference latency to 63.1% of the full backbone. The key empirical finding is that world modeling amplifies the data scaling law: on a 70M-frame in-house dataset, adding world modeling improves ADE by 28.8% (VQ) and collision rate by 15.9% (ViT) over action-only baselines, a gap that widens with data scale rather than saturating.

## Results

On NAVSIM v1, DriveVLA-W0 achieves a PDMS of 93.0 with the AR action expert (best-of-N=6), surpassing the previous best VLA method AutoVLA† (92.1) and all BEV-based methods (WoTE: 88.3, DiffusionDrive: 88.1). With the query-based action expert, DriveVLA-W0 already reaches 88.4 PDMS using only a single front-view camera, matching or exceeding multi-camera + LiDAR baselines. On NAVSIM v2, DriveVLA-W0 achieves an EPDMS of 86.1, outperforming DiffusionDrive (84.5) and DriveSuprem (83.1). On the 70M-frame in-house dataset, adding world modeling improves ADE by 28.8% (VQ) and collision rate by 15.9% (ViT) over action-only baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DriveVLA-W0 outperforms all 11 compared baselines on NAVSIM v1. Against the strongest BEV methods, it surpasses WoTE (88.3) and DiffusionDrive (88.1) by 0.1 and 0.3 PDMS with the query-based expert, and by 4.7 and 4.9 PDMS with the AR best-of-N expert (93.0). Against the top VLA competitors, it beats ReCogDrive (89.6) by 3.4 PDMS and AutoVLA (89.1) by 3.9 PDMS in standard settings; with best-of-N, it edges out AutoVLA† (92.1) by 0.9 PDMS. Notably, DriveVLA-W0 achieves these results using only a single front-view camera, while most baselines rely on 3–6 cameras plus LiDAR. On NAVSIM v2, DriveVLA-W0 (EPDMS 86.1) leads DiffusionDrive (84.5), DriveSuprem (83.1), and ARTEMIS (83.1). On the in-house 70M-frame dataset, the world-model-augmented VLA (VQ) reduces ADE from 1.4829 m to 1.0563 m (28.8% improvement) and collision rate from 4.88% to 3.92% (19.7% improvement) over its action-only baseline, while TransFuser-7B (1.2244 m ADE) remains inferior despite its larger parameter count.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLA-for-driving line (UniVLA, WorldVLA, AutoVLA, ReCogDrive) by identifying a 'supervision deficit'—sparse action labels underutilize large VLM capacity—and addressing it with world modeling as a dense self-supervised objective. It departs from prior driving world models (LAW's latent prediction, GAIA-1/DrivingGPT's data synthesis) by supervising the VLA backbone to predict future images directly, tailored to both discrete-token (Emu3) and continuous-feature (Qwen2.5-VL) backbones. The MoE-based Action Expert draws on the π0/π0.5 architecture for efficient inference, while the action decoding comparison (query, AR, flow-matching) builds on the FAST tokenizer and rectified-flow formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that world modeling amplifies the data scaling law—where the performance gap over action-only baselines widens from ~4% at 700k frames to ~29% at 70M frames—provides a concrete recipe for making large VLA models actually benefit from data scale, a problem that has limited prior solutions. The decoder reversal (autoregressive surpassing flow-matching at massive scale) challenges the prevailing assumption that continuous decoders are inherently superior. Key limitations include reliance on a non-public 70M-frame in-house dataset for the most dramatic scaling results, single front-view camera input, and the fact that the world model is bypassed at inference, leaving open whether closed-loop reactive simulation (where the world model is actively used) can further improve planning.
