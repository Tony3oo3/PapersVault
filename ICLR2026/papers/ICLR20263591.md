## Problem

Frontier LLMs' reasoning benchmark scores are inflated because models can bypass genuine reasoning by relying on stored linguistic knowledge or memorised training-set problems. Existing perturbations (synthetic test cases, variable reassignment) are too small-scale to prevent models from matching problems to prior exposure, leaving a gap in measuring true inductive reasoning ability on novel linguistic tasks.

## Input / Output

Each prompt contains a full linguistics olympiad problem sheet (preamble, context with Problemese-Solverese word pairs, all questions in the problem, and the specific sub-question to answer), with the model expected to return a JSON-formatted answer. Answers are extremely short (14% are single digits, 14% single characters, 71.6% 'other'), so scoring uses strict exact match with no partial credit. Each of the 82 problems is evaluated in 7 versions (1 original + 6 grapheme permutations), yielding 6,995 total (sub-question, answer) pairs scored as M_og, M_obf, and M_rob.

## Method

The core mechanism is a reasoning-equivariant grapheme permutation: for each of 82 UKLO problems, experts manually construct a problem-specific ruleset that partitions the Problemese graphemes into four structured collections (sets, tables, free-tables, and a fixed set), encoding which phonological relationships (e.g., vowel harmony pairs, sibilant consonant groups, voiced/voiceless pairings) must be preserved for the problem to remain solvable. A valid permutation is a structure-preserving bijection over these collections, ensuring the underlying solution logic is intact while the surface orthography becomes unrecognisable to any model's prior knowledge. Cognates, loanwords, and proper names are explicitly fixed to avoid altering problem difficulty. Up to 6 permutations are randomly sampled per problem, generating 6,995 (sub-question, answer) pairs from 1,005 original pairs. This approach is substantially more severe than standard Linguistics Olympiad obfuscation (which only removes metadata like language names), as it rewrites the Problemese text itself into a novel orthography with minimal chance of appearing in any training corpus.

## Results

On LINGOLY-TOO (1,203 questions, 6,995 sub-questions), the best model GPT-5 achieves M_og ≈ 0.59 on original problems but drops to M_obf = 0.48 after orthographic obfuscation, and only 0.29 under the robust metric M_rob (minimum across permutations). No model exceeds 0.5 on M_obf, and at the highest difficulty (Round 2), even GPT-5 and Claude 3.7 Sonnet score below 0.31. Reasoning models consistently outperform general-purpose models: Claude 3.7 (thinking) scores 0.44 vs. 0.30 for Claude 3.7 (no thinking), and o3-mini (high) scores 0.31 vs. 0.13 for o3-mini (low).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between the unobfuscated LINGOLY benchmark (M_og) and the obfuscated LINGOLY-TOO (M_obf) for the same 15 models. The key finding is that M_og systematically overestimates reasoning ability: frontier models score ~0.59 on original problems but only 0.48 after obfuscation, a gap of ~0.11 attributed to knowledge shortcuts. This gap is correlated with language resourcedness—problems in high-resource languages (Japanese, Finnish, Italian) show the largest negative Δ_obf values (−0.57 to −0.59), confirming that models exploit prior linguistic knowledge rather than reasoning. In the no-context setting (where reasoning is impossible), obfuscated problems yield near-zero scores (M_obf ≈ 0.01–0.03) while unobfuscated problems still yield M_og ≈ 0.07 for GPT-4o through direct translation, further validating that obfuscation removes knowledge shortcuts. On unpublished UKLO 2025 problems (not in any training data), the performance drop between unobfuscated and obfuscated versions persists (e.g., Claude 3.7 thinking: 0.25 → 0.20), confirming the gap is not solely due to memorisation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LINGOLY (Bean et al., 2024), a benchmark of olympiad-level linguistic reasoning puzzles, by introducing expert-designed grapheme-level permutations that preserve solution logic while rendering the text unrecognizable to models. Conceptually, it builds on the symbolic perturbation approach of GSM-Symbolic (Mirzadeh et al., 2024) for math problems, but departs from it by operating at the orthographic level rather than permuting variable values, which the authors argue is more effective at preventing knowledge-based shortcuts. The work also sits within the broader construct-validity and contamination literature (Reuel-Lamparth et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2023), positioning obfuscation as a principled intervention to disentangle reasoning from memorisation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LINGOLY-TOO provides a practical, scalable method for generating uncontaminated reasoning problems, addressing the saturation and contamination crises in LLM benchmarks. The finding that language resourcedness systematically inflates scores (with high-resource languages showing the largest obfuscation gaps) offers a diagnostic tool for identifying knowledge shortcuts in future evaluations. Open questions include whether the exact-match metric is too punitive for partially correct reasoning, whether the approach generalises beyond linguistic tasks, and whether the benchmark will remain unsaturated as reasoning models improve. The human RCT showing only a 5.7% performance drop (vs. ~19% for the best LLM) suggests the gap is primarily about knowledge access rather than cognitive difficulty, raising questions about what 'reasoning' actually measures in these settings.
