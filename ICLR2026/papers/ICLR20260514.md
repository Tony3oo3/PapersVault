## Problem

Standard SFT-based cold start for multimodal RLVR jointly optimizes reasoning content and output format, inducing instruction-style overfitting that weakens out-of-distribution generalization and constrains exploration during subsequent RL. The paper asks whether a preference-based cold start that decouples shallow format learning from deep reasoning can provide a superior initialization for RL, and introduces the Generalization Factor (GF) to quantify this effect.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images paired with text questions spanning math, science, coding, and general knowledge tasks. Outputs follow a strict two-part structure: a reasoning chain enclosed in  tags, followed by a final answer wrapped in <answer> tags with LaTeX-formatted results (e.g., \boxed{...}). During cold start, the preference data is deliberately constructed so that both chosen and rejected responses contain the correct final answer but differ only in format compliance, isolating the format-learning signal from content reasoning.

## Method

SPECS is a three-stage pipeline that decouples format learning from reasoning. Stage 1 (Self-Distillation): a brief GRPO pass on the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B yields an exploratory π_GRPO-zero model, which is then prompted alongside the base model to generate response pairs where both answers are correct but the rejected response has one of five types of format corruption (removed tags, misplaced tags, etc.); a Gemini-2.5-flash evaluator filters chosen responses for reasoning-answer consistency. Stage 2 (DPO Pre-Alignment): the base model is trained on 9K self-distilled preference pairs using a hybrid loss L_DPO + λ·L_SFT (λ=1), which teaches the model to prefer structurally correct outputs without memorizing specific reasoning content. Stage 3 (Final GRPO): the DPO-pre-aligned model serves as the RL initialization, with a composite reward (0.5 format + 1.0 accuracy) allowing credit assignment to focus on reasoning quality rather than basic structural compliance. The key empirical finding is that DPO cold start produces higher GF (better OOD generalization), higher rollout branching factor (more exploration), smoother policy loss curves, and a higher final performance ceiling than SFT cold start, improving MEGA-Bench by 4.1% and MathVista by 12.2% over the backbone.

## Results

On MEGA-Bench Core, SPECS (Ours-7B) achieves 39.17, outperforming the Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone (35.07) by +4.1 points and the strongest reasoning baseline Orsta-7B (38.31) by 0.86 points. Across five benchmarks (MMMU, MathVision, MathVista, MathVerse, MEGA-Bench), the overall average reaches 52.73, a +7.3 improvement over the backbone and +0.48 over the closest competitor VL-Rethinker-7B (52.25). The largest single-benchmark gain is on MathVista, where SPECS scores 75.90 versus the backbone's 63.70 (+12.2). On MMMU, SPECS achieves 56.78, matching VL-Rethinker-7B (56.7) and exceeding all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPECS outperforms all nine baselines on the overall multi-benchmark average. Against the closest reasoning-model competitor VL-Rethinker-7B (Wang et al., 2025), SPECS leads by +0.48 overall (52.73 vs. 52.25), with the largest margin on MathVista (+2.3) and MathVerse (−0.25, a slight loss). Against Orsta-7B (Ma et al., 2025), SPECS gains +7.13 overall, driven primarily by MathVerse (48.73 vs. 32.10) and MathVista (75.90 vs. 70.20). MM-Eureka-7B (Meng et al., 2025) is outperformed by +1.97 overall, with SPECS leading on MathVista (+2.9) and MathVerse (+1.15). R1-OneVision (Yang et al., 2025) and VLAA-Thinking (Chen et al., 2025) trail by +6.81 and +3.91 overall respectively, with R1-OneVision notably underperforming even the untrained backbone on MMMU (49.67 vs. 54.2). Among general-purpose VLMs (QwenVL-2-7B, InternVL2/2.5/3-8B, Kimi-VL-A3B, DeepSeek-VL-7B), SPECS leads the MEGA-Bench Core score by 3.15 points over the best general model (InternVL3-8B at 36.02). The DPO-based cold start also yields a higher Pass@32 on MMMU and a higher Rollout Branching Factor than the SFT-based cold start, confirming broader exploration capacity that translates into the final RL gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits squarely in the MLLM-r1 wave that adapts RLVR to vision-language models, building directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) for preference-based alignment and GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for the final RL stage. Its key departure is from the dominant SFT-based cold-start paradigm used by R1-OneVision, VLAA-Thinking, and most prior MLLM-r1 systems, replacing it with a preference-based (DPO) cold start that decouples shallow format learning from deep reasoning. The self-distillation mechanism for preference data generation also departs from the teacher-student distillation approach common in synthetic data construction, instead leveraging a briefly RL-tuned version of the base model itself to avoid the capacity-gap problem identified in prior distillation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, low-cost recipe (self-distill → DPO → GRPO) that practitioners can adopt to improve multimodal RL pipelines without requiring larger teacher models or additional annotation. The Generalization Factor metric provides a useful diagnostic for comparing cold-start strategies, and the decoupling insight—letting cold start handle format while RL handles reasoning—gives a clean conceptual framework for structuring multi-stage training. However, the evaluation is limited to a single 7B backbone (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and a narrow set of OOD benchmarks, leaving open whether the GF advantage and performance gains persist at larger scales, across architectures, or in text-only reasoning settings.
