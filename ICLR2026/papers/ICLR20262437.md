## Problem

Existing knowledge distillation methods for Multimodal LLMs effectively transfer visual recognition ability (e.g., VQA) from teacher to student but fail to transfer visual perception ability (e.g., compositional reasoning), leaving student models at SFT-level performance on perception tasks. The paper identifies the root cause as visual attention misalignment in the intermediate 'visual understanding layers,' where the student's attention over visual tokens does not align with the teacher's, a bottleneck previously overlooked in KD research.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262437/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed at 384×384 resolution, yielding 729 visual patches via SigLIP) and a text query, producing a free-form text answer. A notable design choice is that the student's visual tokens are generated by passing the vision encoder output through the teacher's frozen pretrained adapter followed by a lightweight trainable MLP for dimensional alignment, rather than training a standalone adapter from scratch. The LLM processes a combined sequence of visual and text tokens with a maximum sequence length of 2048.

## Method

CompoDistill introduces two modules to address visual attention misalignment. The Visual ATtention alignment (VAT) module computes a cosine-similarity loss between the student's and teacher's attention sub-matrices over visual tokens in the intermediate layers, using a Group Layer Matching strategy where each student layer is aligned with a sliding window of consecutive teacher layers (one-to-many) to handle depth differences. The Teacher Adapter Fetch (TAF) module reuses the teacher's frozen adapter and adds only a small trainable MLP for dimension projection, ensuring the student processes visual features in the same space where the teacher's attention was optimized. Training follows a three-stage recipe: Distilled Pre-Training (freeze encoder and LLM, train only the adapter with LM + KL losses), Distilled Fine-Tuning (co-optimize LLM and adapter adding the attention distillation loss), and Supervised Fine-Tuning (consolidate knowledge with standard LM loss to restore instruction-following).

## Results

CompoDistill-2B achieves a CR average of 66.7, substantially outperforming the best prior KD method LLaVA-KD-2B (61.5) by +5.2 points, while maintaining a competitive VQA average of 61.9 (vs. 61.6 for LLaVA-KD-2B). This is achieved with only 1.2M training samples, compared to 5.0M for LLaVA-MoD-2B. On the SugarCrepe benchmark specifically, CompoDistill-2B scores 82.9 vs. 75.3 for LLaVA-KD-2B and 76.9 for LLaVA-MoD-2B, approaching the 4B teacher's 83.0. The method also reduces relational hallucination, scoring 78.6 F1 on R-Bench and 67.9 on Reefknot, nearly matching the teacher (79.1 and 67.9 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three main KD baselines distilled from the same LLaVA-4B teacher, CompoDistill-2B wins decisively on all four CR benchmarks: SugarCrepe (82.9 vs. 75.3/76.9/76.9), SADE (69.4 vs. 63.6/61.5/63.8), BiVLC (63.3 vs. 57.9/54.1/60.3), and Winoground (51.2 vs. 49.3/48.4/49.4). On VQA, CompoDistill matches or slightly exceeds LLaVA-KD-2B (61.9 vs. 61.9 VQA Avg) and clearly outperforms LLaVADI-2B (61.9 vs. 56.6) and LLaVA-MoD-2B (61.9 vs. 58.9), despite using 4x less training data than LLaVA-MoD. Compared to the 4B teacher, CompoDistill-2B closes the CR gap from 8.8 points (LLaVA-KD) to just 3.6 points, while the VQA gap shrinks from 1.0 to 0.7 points. On relational hallucination benchmarks, CompoDistill-2B (78.6 R-Bench F1, 67.9 Reefknot) nearly matches the teacher (79.1, 67.9) and outperforms LLaVA-KD-2B (76.5, 60.3) and LLaVA-MoD-2B (76.2, 63.4). Against non-KD 2B models (IMP-2B, Bunny-2B, MoE-LLaVA-2B), CompoDistill leads on CR by 3.8–6.6 points while remaining competitive on VQA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM knowledge distillation line (LLaVA-KD, LLaVADI, LLaVA-MoD) by identifying a specific failure mode—visual attention misalignment—that prior logit-level KD methods overlook. It repurposes attention distillation from NLP (MiniLM) and dataset distillation to the multimodal setting, but introduces the Teacher Adapter Fetch module to resolve the feature-space mismatch that makes naive attention transfer ineffective in MLLMs. The group layer matching strategy departs from both uniform sampling and the adaptive KL-distance pairing used in VLSI (Lee et al., 2025b), offering a simpler one-to-many sliding-window alternative. Conceptually, the paper draws on recent interpretability work on intermediate 'visual understanding layers' (Kaduri et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2025b) to target distillation at the right depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that existing MLLM distillation methods silently fail to transfer visual perception (relational/attribute understanding) while successfully transferring visual recognition—a distinction that had been conflated in prior KD evaluations. This diagnostic insight, combined with a practical three-stage framework, gives practitioners a clear recipe for closing the perception gap without sacrificing recognition performance. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or 3D modalities, whether the three-stage schedule is strictly necessary versus a single joint stage, and how the method behaves when teacher and student use entirely different vision encoders (not just different LLM depths). The paper also notes that the TAF module, while effective, couples the student to the teacher's adapter architecture, potentially limiting flexibility.
