## Problem

Existing guardian models are locked into static, pre-defined harm taxonomies (violence, NSFW, self-harm, etc.) and fail catastrophically on application-specific policies such as 'do not offer refunds' or 'redact HIPAA identifiers,' with LlamaGuard3 achieving only 13.1% F1 on user-defined rules. No prior model simultaneously supports dynamic user-defined policies, interpretable violation explanations, fast token-efficient inference, and open weights for on-premises deployment.

## Input / Output

The model receives a numbered list of policy rules wrapped in <rules> tags and a multi-turn user-agent dialogue in <transcript> tags. At runtime, prepending a <think> tag elicits a full chain-of-thought reasoning trace before the binary PASS/FAIL label in <answer> tags, while prepending <answer> directly yields the label followed by an optional <explanation> tag containing a brief, actionable justification. This dual-mode design lets operators choose between latency-sensitive fast inference and detailed reasoning without retraining.

## Method

The paper introduces DynaBench, a 61.5K-sample training set built by composing 5,000 hand-seeded rules into 40,000 unique policies (median 3 rules, max 86) and generating multi-turn dialogues with LLMs, plus a 543-example handcrafted test set spanning 12 business-impact categories and 16 failure modes. Labels are produced by decomposing each policy into individual rules and having GPT-4o judge compliance per rule, then aggregating to a single PASS/FAIL per turn. DynaGuard is trained by fine-tuning Qwen3 instruction models (1.7B/4B/8B) in two stages: supervised fine-tuning on a 50/50 mixture of DynaBench and four safety datasets (WildGuard, BeaverTails, ToxicChat, Aegis 2.0), followed by GRPO reinforcement learning on 11K samples. One-third of SFT examples include chain-of-thought traces wrapped in <think> tags, enabling the model to produce interpretable explanations that can be fed back to a generating LLM for self-correction, as demonstrated on IFEval where DynaGuard-guided revision improves Ministral-8B accuracy from 57.3% to 63.8%.

## Results

DynaGuard-8B with CoT achieves the best overall F1 score of 79.7% across all seven benchmarks (Safety Avg + DynaBench), surpassing GPT-4o-mini (75.8%) by nearly 4 points. On the novel DynaBench test set specifically, DynaGuard-8B scores 73.1% F1, dramatically outperforming existing guardian models: WildGuard (20.9%), LlamaGuard3 (13.1%), and NemoGuard (23.7%). Even in fast-inference mode without CoT, DynaGuard-8B (78.4% All Tasks Avg) exceeds GPT-4o-mini, and the 1.7B variant (77.4%) outperforms all dedicated guardian models, demonstrating that the training recipe scales effectively across model sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynaGuard-8B (CoT) achieves the highest All Tasks Avg F1 (79.7%), beating GPT-4o-mini (75.8%) by 3.9 points and the base Qwen3-8B (67.5%) by 12.2 points. On the critical DynaBench benchmark for dynamic policies, DynaGuard-8B (73.1%) substantially outperforms all existing guardian models—WildGuard (20.9%), LlamaGuard3 (13.1%), NemoGuard (23.7%), and GuardReasoner-8B (22.0% with CoT)—by 40–60 points, confirming that prior models cannot generalize to user-defined policies. GuardReasoner-8B slightly edges DynaGuard on Safety Avg (81.5% vs 81.1%) but trails by 8.1 points on All Tasks Avg, reflecting its weakness on unseen policies. Even the smallest DynaGuard-1.7B (77.4% All Tasks Avg) surpasses every dedicated guardian model, and DynaGuard-8B without CoT (78.4%) still exceeds GPT-4o-mini, validating the fast-inference capability. On IFEval, pairing Ministral-8B with DynaGuard improves instruction-following accuracy from 57.3% to 63.8%, while other guardians (GuardReasoner, LlamaGuard3, NemoGuard) provide no improvement or slight degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of guardian models (LlamaGuard, WildGuard, NemoGuard, ShieldGemma) that classify responses against fixed, pre-defined harm taxonomies, instead framing compliance evaluation as an open-ended task over arbitrary user-defined policies. Methodologically, it builds on the SFT + RL (GRPO) training recipe applied to instruction-tuned LLMs (Qwen3), and on chain-of-thought reasoning for interpretability (as in GuardReasoner), but combines these in a dual-mode architecture that supports both fast token-efficient classification and optional CoT explanation. The DynaBench dataset itself extends the compliance-related dataset lineage (BeaverTails, WildGuard, ToxicChat, Aegis 2.0) by replacing fixed harm categories with 40K bespoke policies spanning business-specific domains like transactions, regulatory compliance, and task routing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and growing practical gap: production LLM deployments require application-specific guardrails (e.g., 'do not offer refunds,' 'redact HIPAA identifiers') that no static harm taxonomy covers, as illustrated by the Air Canada chatbot incident. DynaBench provides a much-needed benchmark for this setting, and the finding that LlamaGuard3 scores only 13.1% F1 on it quantifies how poorly existing tools handle dynamic policies. Open questions remain around how DynaGuard's explanations best integrate into multi-agent recovery loops, how ambiguity in policy language should be handled by design, and whether the approach generalizes to multimodal or non-English settings. The models' open weights and small size (1.7B–8B) make them deployable in latency-sensitive, on-premises settings that API-based alternatives cannot serve.
