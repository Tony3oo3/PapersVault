## Problem

Standard normalization layers (BatchNorm, LayerNorm, InstanceNorm) treat time samples in intermediate feature maps independently, ignoring the temporal autocorrelation and spectral structure inherent to physiological signals like EEG. This limitation becomes critical in sleep staging, where data collected across different subjects, institutions, and recording devices exhibits significant distribution shift in the power spectral density. While Temporal Monge Alignment (TMA) addresses spectral shifts via optimal transport, it is restricted to a preprocessing step and cannot be integrated as a learnable layer within a network.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 2-channel bipolar EEG (e.g., C3-A2 and C4-A1) resampled to 100 Hz, processed in 17.5-minute windows with a 10.5-minute stride along full-night recordings. It outputs a 5-class sleep stage label (Wake, N1, N2, N3, REM) for each 30-second epoch, with performance evaluated on the central 10.5 minutes of each prediction window using balanced accuracy. The PSDNorm layer itself operates on intermediate feature maps of shape (N, c, ℓ) — batch, channels, temporal length — and is inserted at every encoder/decoder block in place of standard normalization.

## Method

PSDNorm is a drop-in normalization layer that aligns the Power Spectral Density of each feature map to a running Riemannian barycenter via f-Monge mapping. At each forward pass it (1) estimates the per-sample PSD using the Welch estimator over overlapping windows of size f, (2) updates a running barycenter by interpolating along the Bures-Wasserstein geodesic between the current running barycenter and the batch-level Wasserstein barycenter, and (3) applies a circular convolution with a frequency-domain filter H = (P̄ ⊘ P̂)^(⊙1/2) F_f* to re-color each feature map's spectrum toward the barycenter. The hyperparameter f (typically 5–11) controls the number of frequency bins considered, thereby adjusting the strength of temporal normalization; f=1 with a uniform barycenter recovers InstanceNorm. The layer is fully differentiable, uses a stop-gradient on the running barycenter, and adds less than 10% training overhead over BatchNorm, making it a practical plug-and-play replacement in both U-Net-style CNNs and transformer-based architectures.

## Results

On the full 10-dataset LODO evaluation (~10K subjects), PSDNorm achieves a mean subject-level BACC of 79.51% with U-Sleep, outperforming InstanceNorm (79.26%), TMA (78.77%), BatchNorm (78.14%), and LayerNorm (76.78%). In the data-limited balanced@400 setting (~2.5K subjects), PSDNorm reaches 78.85% subject-level BACC, a +0.68% gain over the best baseline (InstanceNorm at 78.17%), compared to only +0.25% in the full-data setting. On the most challenging CHAT dataset (pediatric, strong domain shift), PSDNorm scores 70.57% (all subjects) and 70.28% (balanced@400), exceeding all other methods by more than 1 percentage point. The method also requires 4× fewer labeled subjects to match the accuracy of the best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSDNorm consistently outperforms all four baselines on mean subject-level BACC across both the full-data and balanced@400 settings. Against BatchNorm, PSDNorm gains +1.37% (all subjects) and +1.63% (balanced@400), with the largest per-dataset advantage on CHAT (+5.85% all subjects). Against LayerNorm, the margin is even larger at +2.73% (all subjects) and +3.81% (balanced@400), confirming LayerNorm's poor suitability for temporal signals. Against InstanceNorm (the strongest baseline), PSDNorm leads by +0.25% on all subjects and +0.68% on balanced@400, with the gap widening under data scarcity. Against TMA (the same Monge-mapping idea applied as a fixed preprocessing step), PSDNorm improves by +0.74% (all subjects) and +1.11% (balanced@400), demonstrating that integrating the alignment as a learnable layer inside the network yields better adaptation than a one-shot preprocessing. Critical difference tests confirm that PSDNorm's advantage over all baselines is statistically significant in the balanced@400 regime for both U-Sleep and CNNTransformer architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

PSDNorm converts the Temporal Monge Alignment (TMA) preprocessing step from Gnassounou et al. (NeurIPS 2023) into a differentiable, in-network normalization layer, leveraging the same f-Monge mapping and Gaussian Wasserstein barycenter (Agueh & Carlier, 2011) but updated via a running Riemannian barycenter along the Bures-Wasserstein geodesic (Bhatia et al., 2019). Conceptually, it generalizes InstanceNorm (recovered at f=1 with a uniform target PSD) by replacing the i.i.d. whitening assumption with explicit temporal autocorrelation alignment in the frequency domain. The paper departs from standard normalization layers (BatchNorm, LayerNorm, InstanceNorm) that treat time samples independently, and from TMA which is limited to raw-signal preprocessing, by embedding spectral alignment as a learnable layer applicable at any depth of a CNN or transformer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PSDNorm offers a practical, plug-and-play normalization layer that consistently improves domain generalization in sleep staging with minimal computational overhead (<10% training time), and the 4× data-efficiency gain is particularly relevant for clinical settings where labeled recordings are scarce. The large-scale LODO evaluation across 10K subjects and 10 datasets provides the most systematic comparison of normalization strategies for sleep staging to date. Open questions include whether the filter size f can be learned or adapted per-layer, whether the Gaussian OT approximation remains valid for highly non-Gaussian intermediate features in deeper networks, and whether the approach transfers to other signal modalities (audio, ECG) as the authors note as future work.
