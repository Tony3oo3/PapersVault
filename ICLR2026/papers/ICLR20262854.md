## Problem

Existing methods for decomposing visual scenes into interpretable concepts and governing rules rely on hand-crafted inductive biases, predefined attribute taxonomies, or task-specific rule templates, limiting their adaptability across diverse visual domains. Meanwhile, Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) process images holistically and lack explicit mechanisms for concept-rule decomposition, resulting in weak compositional understanding and poor performance on abstract visual reasoning. This paper asks whether LVLMs can serve as data-driven priors to automatically discover both the relevant visual concepts and the spatial rules governing their variation, without manual rule specification.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (natural scene, 3×3 matrix reasoning problem, or spatial reasoning image with a question) and splits it into N non-overlapping patches in raster-scan order (default 2×2 grid, N=4). A pretrained LVLM is queried to propose candidate meta-attributes and extract per-patch concept values for each attribute. The output is a Visual Concept Set of K selected meta-attributes, a Concept Rule Function (a GP mapping spatial positions to concept values) for each retained attribute, and—on reasoning tasks—a final answer selected by comparing CRF marginal-likelihood scores across candidate completions.

## Method

CRD operates in two stages. In the CRF learning stage, the LVLM extracts patch-wise concept values, and a Gaussian Process with a deep kernel (a neural network mapping positions to high-dimensional representations) is trained by minimizing the negative log marginal likelihood, learning a function space that captures how each concept's values vary spatially. The log marginal likelihood of each concept's values under the GP defines its rule score θ_i, linking concepts to rules: concepts whose values follow coherent spatial patterns receive higher scores. In the VCS sampling stage, a Metropolis-Hastings procedure (LVLM-MH) iteratively replaces one concept in the current set with a candidate proposed by the LVLM; the acceptance probability combines the ratio of CRF scores (e^{θ_j − θ_i}) with the LVLM proposal ratio, so that rule-consistent and semantically relevant concepts are preferentially retained. This design replaces hand-crafted rule templates with a learned GP prior and replaces fixed attribute taxonomies with LVLM-driven concept proposals, yielding a model-agnostic decomposition that can be plugged into any pretrained LVLM.

## Results

On the abstract visual reasoning benchmarks RAVEN and I-RAVEN, Qwen-VL-CRD achieves 89.4% and 89.3% average accuracy, dramatically surpassing the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (59.7%/15.0%) and all deep-learning baselines (best: LEN at 72.4%/15.0%). On the VSB-MA meta-attribute extraction task, CRD consistently improves F1 across every model scale, e.g., InternVL-3.5-8B rises from 61.1 to 64.7 and Qwen2.5-VL-7B from 50.2 to 56.1. On SpatialEval, CRD-full lifts the overall score of Qwen2.5-VL-7B from 60.11 to 63.37, with the largest gains in SpatialReal (91.11→97.04) and MazeNav (28.93→33.33).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRD (instantiated with Qwen2.5-VL-7B) achieves 89.4%/89.3% on RAVEN/I-RAVEN, outperforming the strongest deep-learning baseline SRAN (56.2%/61.0%) by over 33 points on RAVEN and 28 points on I-RAVEN, and surpassing LEN (72.4%/15.0%) which collapses to near-random on I-RAVEN. Among LVLM baselines, Qwen-VL-CRD exceeds GPT-4o (11.6%/12.1%), LLaVA-NeXT (12.7%/13.1%), DeepSeek-VL2 (17.0%/12.4%), and InternVL3-8B (11.6%/13.9%) by a wide margin on both datasets. On VSB-MA, CRD improves every LVLM baseline it is paired with: InternVL-3.5-8B F1 rises from 61.1 to 64.7, Qwen2.5-VL-7B from 50.2 to 56.1, and DeepSeek-VL2-Tiny from 27.4 to 30.6. On SpatialEval, CRD-full outperforms the dedicated SpatialVLM (50.27 overall) by 13.1 points (63.37) using Qwen2.5-VL-7B, while also improving the base model's own score. The InternVL-CRD variant (31.6%/33.6%) also surpasses all LVLM baselines on I-RAVEN, though it trails Qwen-VL-CRD, suggesting the LVLM backbone's quality significantly affects CRD's ceiling.

## Relation to Prior Work

CRD repurposes the authors' own line of GP-based rule modeling for abstract reasoning (LGPP, CLAP-NP) by replacing learned latent representations with LVLM-extracted concept values, and introduces a Metropolis-Hastings sampler with an LVLM-informed proposal distribution for concept selection. It departs from earlier hierarchical Bayesian and compositional character-decomposition approaches (Kemp & Tenenbaum; Lake et al.) that relied on hand-crafted inductive biases or predefined attribute taxonomies, instead treating the LVLM as a data-driven prior for both concept discovery and rule induction. The framework is explicitly model-agnostic, plugging into any pretrained LVLM without architectural modification, which distinguishes it from task-specific deep learning baselines like SRAN or PrAE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CRD offers a practical, interpretable decomposition layer that can be attached to existing LVLMs to improve abstract and spatial reasoning without retraining, and the newly curated VSB-MA benchmark provides a standardized reference for evaluating meta-attribute extraction in real-world scenes. Key limitations include the small patch-grid sizes (N ≤ 16) that keep GP inference tractable but limit spatial granularity, the unresolved Qwen2.5-VL data-contamination concern on RAVEN, and the fact that the framework has not yet been extended to temporal or video settings. Open questions include whether richer rule spaces (beyond GP with a deep kernel) or alternative samplers could handle more complex compositional structures, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the relatively structured domains tested here.
