## Problem

Existing depth completion methods treat the task as pixel-wise depth restoration or interpolation, lacking explicit 3D geometric reasoning. This paradigm fails under severe domain shifts and highly irregular sparse observations (e.g., Structure-from-Motion points with non-uniform density and large missing regions), limiting real-world applicability. The paper asks whether reformulating both the input preprocessing and the training objective can yield a simple, robust model that generalizes zero-shot across diverse sparse patterns and unseen environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264053/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (H×W×3) and a sparse depth map (H×W) as input, where sparsity can range from 1–10% random sampling, SIFT/ORB keypoint-based extraction, to LiDAR-simulated scans with 64/32/16/8 vertical lines. It outputs a metric 3D point map (H×W×3) in camera space; the dense depth map is simply the z-channel of this point map. Notably, no camera intrinsics are required at inference, enabling deployment in uncalibrated environments. Evaluation is conducted at 480×640 resolution on six held-out benchmarks.

## Method

LDCM is a two-stage ViT-B (DINOv2-pretrained) framework. Stage 1 performs Poisson-based coarse depth alignment: a monocular foundation model (DepthAnythingV2-S) provides relative depth cues, which are combined with sparse observations via a target log-gradient field G = ∇log(D_r + γ) (where γ = β/α from a global affine fit), then solved with conjugate gradient to produce a metric-anchored coarse depth map. Stage 2 feeds the RGB image and coarse depth through dual encoders with a prompt fusion block, and replaces the conventional depth regression head with a point map head that directly regresses per-pixel 3D coordinates. Training uses three complementary losses on the point map: a global L1 loss weighted by inverse depth, a local 3D-neighborhood L1 loss (spherical regions around anchor points), and a surface-normal alignment loss. The model is trained for 200K iterations on ~2.7M samples from 11 RGB-D datasets covering indoor, outdoor, driving, and in-the-wild scenes.

## Results

LDCM achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot depth completion across all six benchmarks, with an average REL of 0.017 and δ₁ of 0.987, outperforming the closest competitor SPNet (REL 0.030, δ₁ 0.970) by a wide margin. On KITTI, LDCM attains RMSE 1.911 and MAE 0.537, roughly 20% lower than the next-best method (SPNet: RMSE 2.365, MAE 0.757). For point map estimation (Table 2), LDCM achieves an average REL^p of 0.042 and δ₁^p of 0.991, again ranking first across all datasets. The model also leads in affine-invariant point map estimation (Table 3), with an average REL^p of 0.037, surpassing MoGe V2 (0.064) and VGGT (0.114).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LDCM outperforms all 17 compared baselines on every dataset and metric in both depth completion and point map estimation. The closest competitors are SPNet (avg. REL 0.030), OMNI-DC (0.029), and PriorDA (0.028), all of which trail LDCM (0.017) by roughly 40–75% in relative error. Among monocular depth foundation models, DepthAnythingV2 (REL 0.071) and VGGT (0.121) are significantly outperformed, while MoGe V1 (0.142) and MoGe V2 (0.180) show larger gaps. Prompt-based depth completion methods PromptDA (0.053) and PriorDA (0.028) are also clearly surpassed, as are diffusion-based approaches Marigold-DC and DepthLab (Table 8, where LDCM achieves REL 0.020 vs. 0.042 and 0.052 respectively on the averaged KITTI setting). For point map estimation, LDCM's average REL^p of 0.042 beats the next-best SPNet (0.075) and OMNI-DC (0.075) by nearly 45%, and for affine-invariant point maps, LDCM (0.037) leads MoGe V2 (0.064) and VGGT (0.114) by substantial margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

LDCM departs from the dominant depth-completion paradigm that treats the task as pixel-wise depth restoration (interpolation/denoising conditioned on sparse observations), as pursued by SPNet, PromptDA, and OMNI-DC. Instead, it reformulates the problem as 3D point map regression, borrowing the point-map loss design from MoGe (Wang et al., 2025e) and the prompt-fusion architecture from PromptDA (Lin et al., 2025). The Poisson-based coarse depth alignment is a novel preprocessing step that repurposes monocular depth foundation models (DepthAnythingV2) in a gradient-domain optimization, contrasting with the local weighted linear regression (LWLR) used by PriorDA or the global affine alignment used by other methods. Conceptually, the paper bridges the depth-completion and 3D-geometry-estimation literatures by showing that learning explicit 3D structure rather than 2.5D depth yields superior metric accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LDCM demonstrates that a simple ViT-B architecture with a point map head and a Poisson-based initialization can outperform substantially more complex methods (diffusion-based, multi-stage) by large margins across six benchmarks, suggesting that the reformulation of the training objective matters more than architectural complexity for depth completion. The elimination of camera intrinsics via the point map formulation has practical implications for deployment in uncalibrated environments. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video sequences with temporal consistency, and how it handles challenging materials (transparent, reflective surfaces) where the current method fails. The Poisson initialization's effectiveness over LWLR also raises the question of whether other gradient-domain formulations could further improve robustness under noisy sparse inputs.
