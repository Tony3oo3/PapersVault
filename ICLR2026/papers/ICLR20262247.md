## Problem

Despite growing research on network alignment (NA), no comprehensive library exists that provides standardized, multi-dimensional evaluation of NA methods across diverse domains. Existing benchmarks are limited to single domains (e.g., biological or social networks), single method categories (e.g., only consistency-based or only embedding-based), and single evaluation aspects (e.g., only effectiveness), leaving the true performance, scalability, and robustness of NA methods largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262247/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The library takes two input networks (plain or attributed, with node and/or edge attribute matrices) and an optional set of anchor node pairs for semi-supervised settings, and produces an alignment matrix S where S(x,y) encodes the likelihood of node x in G₁ corresponding to node y in G₂, along with intermediate node embeddings. Evaluation outputs include Hits@K and MRR averaged over both alignment directions, plus runtime and peak memory for scalability assessment, with all splits controlled by a user-defined random seed for reproducibility.

## Method

PLANETALIGN is a PyTorch-based library that unifies 18 datasets across 6 domains and 14 NA methods spanning three categories—consistency-based (IsoRank, FINAL), embedding-based (IONE, REGAL, CrossMNA, NetTrans, WAlign, BRIGHT, NeXtAlign, WLAlign), and optimal-transport-based (SLOTAlign, PARROT, HOT, JOENA)—under a single API with base classes for extensible dataset and method integration. The standardized evaluation pipeline covers effectiveness (Hits@K, MRR under configurable training ratios), scalability (runtime and memory on ER graphs up to 100K nodes), robustness (edge, attribute, and supervision noise injection), and sensitivity to supervision levels. Key empirical findings reveal that OT-based methods (especially PARROT and JOENA) consistently achieve state-of-the-art alignment by solving globally constrained optimization with informative transport costs, while embedding-based methods face a two-out-of-three trade-off among effectiveness, time, and memory efficiency. The library also demonstrates up to 3× speedup over official implementations while maintaining comparable accuracy, validating its correctness and efficiency.

## Results

Across 18 datasets spanning 6 domains, OT-based methods JOENA and PARROT consistently achieve the highest MRR in every category, with JOENA reaching 80.2% MRR on Publication networks and 74.3% on Communication networks, while PARROT follows closely (74.4% and 71.3% respectively). The best embedding-based methods (BRIGHT, NetTrans) achieve MRR in the 40–55% range on Publication and Knowledge graphs but drop to 9–12% on Social networks, while the best consistency-based method (FINAL) peaks at only 37.3% MRR on Publication. In terms of efficiency, PLANETALIGN's reimplementation of 10 NA algorithms matches or slightly exceeds official MRR (Δ ≤ 0.006) while achieving up to 3.15× speedup (WLAlign: 4018s → 1276s). Scalability experiments on ER graphs show that most methods cannot scale beyond 100K nodes, with WAlign, NeXtAlign, WLAlign, SLOTAlign, and JOENA hitting OOM at that scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JOENA (OT-based, Yu et al. 2025) achieves the best MRR in all 6 dataset categories, outperforming the second-best PARROT (Zeng et al. 2023) by 5–10 MRR points (e.g., 80.2 vs. 74.4 on Publication; 74.3 vs. 71.3 on Communication). Among embedding-based methods, BRIGHT (Yan et al. 2021) and NetTrans (Zhang et al. 2020) are the strongest, reaching 51.8–54.7 MRR on Publication and 39.7–40.9 on Knowledge, but they fall to 9–12 MRR on Social networks where JOENA reaches 24.4. Consistency-based methods (IsoRank, FINAL) are uniformly outperformed by both embedding and OT methods in every category, with FINAL's best result (37.3 MRR on Publication) trailing JOENA by over 40 points. Among OT-based methods, SLOTAlign (Tang et al. 2023) and HOT (Zeng et al. 2024) are substantially weaker than PARROT and JOENA, with SLOTAlign dropping to 2.2 MRR on Social and 3.1 on Knowledge, suggesting that the specific OT formulation and cost design matter critically. PLANETALIGN's reimplementation matches official MRR within ±0.006 for all 10 compared algorithms while delivering 1.02×–3.15× speedup, with the largest gain on WLAlign (3.15×) and REGAL (1.43×).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a comprehensive successor to five prior NA benchmarks (SGAPBSA, CAPABN, ASNets, NAB, OpenEA), each of which was limited to a single domain, a single method category, or a single evaluation dimension. It unifies the three major NA method families—consistency-based (IsoRank, FINAL), embedding-based (IONE through WLAlign), and OT-based (SLOTAlign, PARROT, HOT, JOENA)—into a single PyTorch library, with the OT-based category being entirely absent from all prior benchmarks. Conceptually, it repurposes the benchmarking paradigm from NAB (which covered effectiveness, scalability, robustness for social networks only) and extends it to six domains and all three method categories simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PLANETALIGN becomes the de facto standard for NA benchmarking by providing the first unified, reproducible evaluation across 18 datasets, 14 methods, and three evaluation dimensions (effectiveness, scalability, robustness). Key open questions it surfaces include: (1) whether self-supervised or active alignment can reduce dependence on anchor quality, (2) how to integrate consistency, embedding, and OT principles to improve robustness against heterogeneous noise, and (3) how to scale NA to million-node networks where most current methods hit OOM. The paper also explicitly notes gaps in multi-network simultaneous alignment and dynamic network alignment as underexplored settings.
