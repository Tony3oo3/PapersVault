## Problem

Controllable video generation systems that animate multiple actors simultaneously suffer from severe identity drift, appearance bleeding, and loss of interaction detail when subjects exchange positions or heavily occlude each other. Existing methods are predominantly trained on single-person dance data and rely on frame-wise synthesis with post-hoc temporal smoothing, which cannot maintain persistent identity-to-motion binding across hundreds of frames in multi-actor scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261042/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single 512×512 reference image (containing all actors) plus independent per-person 133-keypoint DWPose heatmaps (3×512×512) and SAMURAI-derived binary tracking masks (1×512×512) as control signals, and produces 16-frame photorealistic interaction clips at 512×512 resolution. All inputs are encoded into a 64×64 latent space via a frozen SVD VAE before conditioning the UNet, and the pose/mask streams are processed per-person through shared-weight branches that scale to arbitrary actor counts via adaptive branch activation.

## Method

DanceTogether builds on the StableAnimator SVD-based 16-frame latent UNet and introduces two key modules. The MaskPoseAdapter fuses per-person pose features (320-channel, from a shared 8-layer PoseNet) with lightweight 3-channel mask features through learned per-pixel gates (λ≈0.8 bias toward pose), then applies cross-person attention with a learnable temperature-scaled softmax to dynamically reweight person importance during occlusions, and integrates via a 0.95/0.05 attention-weighted sum plus residual mean. The MultiFace Encoder projects each ArcFace 512-d embedding into K=4 tokens of width 768 via a two-layer MLP, refines them through 4 Perceiver-IO cross-attention layers with CLIP embeddings as memory, and concatenates all N persons' tokens (NK×768) to modulate every cross-attention layer with shared weights. A distribution-aware ID Adapter aligns face and image feature moments before fusion, and a masked reconstruction loss (face weight 2, body weight 1) focuses capacity on identity-critical regions. Training uses transfer learning from StableAnimator weights, full fine-tuning on multi-person datasets including the newly curated PairFS-4K (26 h, 7,000+ IDs) for 20 epochs on 8 A100 GPUs at lr=1e-5.

## Results

On DanceTogEval-100, DanceTogether (full data + PairFS-4K) achieves 83.94 HOTA, 89.59 IDF1, and 79.80 MOTA for identity consistency, surpassing the strongest baseline (StableAnimator fine-tuned on Swing Dance) by +12.6 HOTA, +7.1 IDF1, and +5.9 MOTA. For interaction coherence, it reduces MPJPE-2D by 69% (1555→492 px) and attains the highest OKS (0.83) and PoseSSIM (0.93). On video quality, masked-region FVD drops from 29.0 to 17.1 and FID from 66.7 to 48.0, while full-frame FVD/FID reach 76.3/75.1 with the best CLIP score (0.95).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DanceTogether outperforms all eight baselines on every reported metric across all three evaluation tracks. The closest competitor is StableAnimator fine-tuned on Swing Dance data (HOTA 71.35, IDF1 82.53, MPJPE 1555 px, masked FVD 29.0); DanceTogether leads it by +12.6 HOTA, +7.1 IDF1, a 69% reduction in MPJPE-2D, and a 41% reduction in masked FVD. Among the original (non-fine-tuned) baselines, HumanVid (NeurIPS'24) is the strongest at HOTA 56.12 / IDF1 68.84, still trailing DanceTogether by 27.8 HOTA points. The remaining baselines—Animate Anyone, Champ, MimicMotion, UniAnimate, UniAnimate-DiT, and DisPose—all score below HOTA 50 and exhibit severe identity drift or missing subjects during position exchanges, with MPJPE-2D values ranging from 2185 to 5543 px versus DanceTogether's 492 px. Notably, even the DiT-based UniAnimate-DiT (HOTA 35.02) and the ICLR'25 DisPose (HOTA 20.68) are far behind, confirming that architectural scale alone does not solve the multi-person identity-binding problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

DanceTogether extends the StableAnimator backbone (a 16-frame latent UNet from SVD) from single-person to multi-person interaction video generation, introducing two key architectural departures: a MaskPoseAdapter that fuses per-person tracking masks with pose heatmaps via gated fusion and cross-person attention, and a MultiFaceEncoder that projects multiple ArcFace embeddings into shared Perceiver-IO tokens. This contrasts with prior multi-person animation works (Follow-your-multipose, Multi-HumanVid, EverybodyDance, ReMask-Animate) that handle fixed relative positions but lack explicit identity-action binding, and with single-person pose-conditioned methods (AnimateAnyone, Champ, DisPose, MimicMotion) that cannot disambiguate overlapping subjects. The paper also carves new ground on the data and evaluation side by introducing PairFS-4K (the first large-scale two-person figure skating dataset) and TogetherVideoBench, a three-track benchmark absent from prior controllable video generation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identity-action binding principle—fusing stable masks with noisy poses at every denoising step—offers a general recipe for any multi-subject generation task where identity confusion arises from occlusion or position exchange, and the one-hour HumanRob-300 fine-tune suggests the framework transfers to embodied-AI and HRI simulation. However, the method remains limited to 16-frame clips at 512×512 resolution, requires per-person pose and mask inputs (precluding text-only or single-image conditioning for multi-person scenes), and has not been validated beyond two actors despite the claimed N-person scalability. Open questions include whether the cross-person attention mechanism degrades gracefully at higher person counts, whether the approach extends to 3D-consistent generation, and whether the benchmark's reliance on DWPose/SAMURAI for evaluation introduces circularity when the same tools are used for training annotation.
