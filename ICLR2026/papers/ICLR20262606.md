## Problem

Standard linear probing relies on a single global token (e.g., [CLS]) and underestimates the representational capacity of models trained with local objectives such as masked image modeling, autoregressive, or diffusion pre-training, where discriminative information is distributed across patch tokens. Although attentive probing was proposed to address this gap, existing methods are overparameterized, computationally inefficient, and have never been systematically benchmarked against each other under a unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262606/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the N = W×H patch-level feature tokens (each of dimension D_i) extracted from a frozen ViT backbone, with no [CLS] token required. It produces a single global image-level descriptor y ∈ R^{D_o} (where D_o can be set to D_i, D_i/2, D_i/4, or D_i/8 for parameter control) that feeds a C-way linear classifier, along with M spatial attention maps (each W×H) as interpretable byproducts. The backbone remains entirely frozen; only the M learnable query vectors and the value projection W_V are trained.

## Method

Efficient Probing (EP) is a multi-query cross-attention mechanism that replaces the standard key projection matrices W_K with M directly learnable query vectors u_j ∈ R^{D_i} in the full input feature space, computing attention as a_j = softmax(X^T u_j) and pooling as y_j = W_{V_j} X a_j. This eliminates all attention-side projection matrices, reducing the attention parameter count from D_a(D_i+1) to D_i·M (typically 1–2 orders of magnitude fewer) while remaining mathematically equivalent to multi-head cross-attention with learnable queries. The value projection W_V is retained, as ablations confirm it is critical for performance. EP is a lightweight single-iteration analogue of slot attention (no GRU, MLP, or LayerNorm; learnable rather than randomly initialized slots), and its M queries naturally specialize in complementary object regions, yielding interpretable and diverse attention maps that correlate with per-predictor classification contribution.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, EP consistently outperforms linear probing across all pre-training paradigms: MAE ViT-B improves from 67.7% (LP) to 75.6% (EP_64) with only 1.4M trainable parameters, BEiTv2 ViT-B from 79.0% to 81.7%, and CAPI ViT-L from 81.5% to 83.6%. The largest gains occur for models with patch-distributed representations: SimMIM ViT-B gains +13.6% and DiT-XL gains +24.3% over LP. A hybrid LoRA+EP configuration with 850K parameters reaches 76.99% top-1, strictly dominating both pure EP (75.58% at 1.38M) and pure all-layer LoRA (76.72% at 1.95M). EP also converges rapidly, matching or surpassing LP@90 after only 10 training epochs across 12 models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EP dominates the accuracy-parameter Pareto frontier over all attentive probing baselines: compared to AIM (75.1% at 1.95M params), EP_12 achieves identical 75.1% with only 1.36M params; compared to V-JEPA and CAE, EP matches or exceeds accuracy while using 3–10× fewer parameters. Against the lightweight baselines [CLS] (67.7%) and GAP (66.7%) on MAE ViT-B, EP_64 gains +7.9% and +8.9% respectively with modest parameter overhead. In the PEFT comparison (Figure 4), EP strictly dominates single-layer LoRA, BitFit, and LayerNorm tuning in the accuracy-parameter plane; only all-layer LoRA approaches EP's accuracy (76.7% vs 75.6%) but at 1.95M vs 1.4M parameters, and the hybrid LoRA+EP configuration (76.99% at 850K) outperforms both. EP also changes the relative ranking of pre-training methods: MAE surpasses BYOL and CAPI outperforms CLIP under EP, whereas LP and k-NN rank them differently. On fine-grained datasets, EP_24 on FGVC-Aircraft achieves 61.2% (+19.5% over LP at 41.7%) with fewer parameters than LP itself, and EP_64 on CUB-200 reaches 75.9% matching SigLIP (77.8%) with ~7M fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies a fragmented landscape of attentive pooling/probing methods (AIM, CAE, V-JEPA, CAPI, AbMILP, SimPool, DELF, CLIP, SigLIP, CoCa, CaiT) under a single multi-head cross-attention (MHCA) framework, showing they are all instances of the same template with different query sources, key/value transforms, and pooling operations. The proposed Efficient Probing (EP) is derived by absorbing the key projection into learnable query vectors in the full input feature space, making it mathematically equivalent to MHCA with learnable queries but with far fewer parameters. Conceptually, EP is positioned as a single-iteration, parameter-light counterpart of slot attention (Locatello et al., 2020), where the absence of iterative interactions is compensated by learned query features. The paper also extends the evaluation axis by comparing probing against parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, BitFit, LayerNorm tuning), a comparison not previously made in the attentive probing literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As full fine-tuning becomes increasingly impractical at scale, probing is consolidating as the standard evaluation protocol, yet the attentive probing subfield lacked a systematic benchmark and a clear accuracy-efficiency Pareto analysis. EP provides a drop-in, lightweight mechanism that consistently improves over linear probing across MIM, JEA, hybrid, VLM, and generative backbones, and the finding that hybrid LoRA+EP configurations strictly dominate both pure probing and pure LoRA suggests the two are complementary rather than redundant. The emergent property of complementary, interpretable attention maps (with +9.8% unsupervised localization gains out-of-the-box) opens a direction for leveraging probing beyond classification evaluation. Open questions include whether the complementarity structure can be explicitly optimized for downstream tasks, whether EP generalizes to video or 3D modalities, and whether the Matryoshka-style multi-scale probing explored in the appendix can be made fully practical without per-dimensionality training.
