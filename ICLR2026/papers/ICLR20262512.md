## Problem

LLM-driven evolutionary program optimization frameworks, such as AlphaEvolve, suffer from severe sample inefficiency, typically requiring thousands of evaluations to discover effective solutions. This makes them computationally expensive and impractical for expensive evaluation domains like LLM pretraining. SHINKAEVOLVE targets this gap by achieving state-of-the-art results with orders of magnitude fewer samples, e.g., discovering a superior circle-packing solution in only 150 evaluations versus thousands for prior methods.

## Input / Output

The framework takes an initial seed program with mutable regions delimited by EVOLVE-BLOCK-START/END markers, a user-defined evaluation script that returns a scalar fitness score plus public metrics and free-text feedback, and access to a pool of LLM APIs (GPT, Gemini, Claude, DeepSeek). The output is an evolved program that preserves all immutable code while optimizing only the mutable blocks, along with its fitness trajectory and the discovered algorithm or loss function. The textual feedback from each evaluation is fed back into subsequent mutation prompts, and the multi-objective assessment (scalar fitness + public metrics + text) is stored in the archive to inform future generations.

## Method

SHINKAEVOLVE introduces three synergistic algorithmic innovations on top of an island-model evolutionary loop. First, a weighted parent sampling strategy combines sigmoid-scaled fitness (relative to the island median) with an inverse-offspring-count novelty term, balancing exploitation of high-performing programs against exploration of underutilized ones. Second, code novelty rejection sampling embeds mutable code segments, computes cosine similarity across the island subpopulation, and rejects proposals exceeding a similarity threshold (default 0.95), with an optional LLM-as-a-novelty-judge for borderline cases to prevent near-duplicate proliferation. Third, a UCB1-based bandit algorithm dynamically adapts LLM ensemble selection probabilities each generation, using a relative-improvement reward (exponentiated difference from the parent/initial baseline) that favors bold, high-risk mutations over safe incremental edits. The framework also employs three mutation operators (diff-based SEARCH/REPLACE edits, full rewrites, and crossover), a meta-scratchpad that periodically synthesizes actionable design insights from successful solutions into the mutation prompt, and island migration to diffuse knowledge across subpopulations while protecting each island's best program.

## Results

On the circle-packing benchmark, SHINKAEVOLVE discovers a new state-of-the-art solution with a sum of radii of 2.635983 using only 150 evaluations, orders of magnitude fewer than AlphaEvolve's thousands. On ALE-Bench LITE, SHINKAEVOLVE improves ALE-Agent's solutions by ~2.3% on average across 10 tasks, and on task ahc039 elevates the solution from 5th to 2nd place on the AtCoder leaderboard (2880 → 3140). For MoE load balancing loss design, the discovered LBL achieves a mean downstream accuracy of 0.368 vs. 0.362 for the global-batch LBL baseline (λ=0.01), with the gap widening at higher λ (0.366 vs. 0.357 at λ=0.1). On AIME 2024, the evolved 7-call agent scaffold significantly outperforms single-query and majority-voting baselines using gpt-4.1-nano.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AlphaEvolve (Novikov et al., 2025), SHINKAEVOLVE achieves a superior circle-packing sum of radii (2.635983 vs. AlphaEvolve's reported value) while requiring only 150 evaluations compared to AlphaEvolve's thousands, representing an orders-of-magnitude sample-efficiency gain. Against OpenEvolve (Sharma, 2025) and LLM4AD (Liu et al., 2024), SHINKAEVOLVE also outperforms on the circle-packing task, with the paper noting that OpenEvolve's verification script was used for evaluation but SHINKAEVOLVE's solution exceeds their reported scores. On ALE-Bench LITE, SHINKAEVOLVE improves upon ALE-Agent's (Imajuku et al., 2025) initial solutions by ~2.3% on average, with the most dramatic gain on ahc039 where the solution jumps from 5th to 2nd place on the AtCoder leaderboard. For MoE load balancing, SHINKAEVOLVE's discovered LBL consistently outperforms the global-batch LBL (Shazeer et al., 2017) used in Qwen3 and other flagship models: it achieves lower CE loss and higher mean downstream accuracy across all three tested λ values (0.001, 0.01, 0.1), with the advantage growing as λ increases, indicating the new regularization term is most beneficial under stronger load-balancing pressure.

## Relation to Prior Work

SHINKAEVOLVE is a direct successor to AlphaEvolve (Novikov et al., 2025) and OpenEvolve (Sharma, 2025), adopting their island-model population architecture, SEARCH/REPLACE diff edits, and EVOLVE-BLOCK code delimiters. Its three core departures are: (1) a weighted parent-sampling strategy that combines sigmoid-scaled fitness with an offspring-count novelty term, replacing AlphaEvolve's simpler selection; (2) code-embedding-based novelty rejection sampling (with an optional LLM-as-judge) to prevent near-duplicate mutations from wasting evaluations; and (3) a UCB1 bandit (Auer et al., 2002) adapted with a relative-improvement reward to dynamically prioritize which LLM in an ensemble proposes the next mutation. The meta-scratchpad mechanism for periodic knowledge synthesis draws on the broader open-ended agentic discovery literature (Zhang et al., 2025; Hu et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's headline claim—achieving state-of-the-art circle packing in ~150 evaluations versus thousands for AlphaEvolve—directly addresses the cost barrier that has limited adoption of LLM-driven evolutionary search in practice. More broadly, the discovery of a new MoE load-balancing loss that targets under-utilized experts (closing a blind spot in the global-batch LBL used by Qwen3 and other flagship models) demonstrates that evolutionary code search can contribute to core LLM training infrastructure, not just optimization benchmarks. Key limitations remain: the framework still requires human-specified objective functions and is restricted to problems with well-defined numerical fitness, and the ALE-Bench results hint at overfitting to the initialization. Open questions include whether automated task specification and true open-endedness (self-generated objectives) can be achieved, and whether the MoE LBL gains persist at much larger training scales.
