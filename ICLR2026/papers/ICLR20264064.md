## Problem

Existing medical vision-language models lack tumor-centric specialization, reliable multi-step reasoning for 3D CT volumes, and large-scale tumor-specific datasets with traceable reasoning chains. They fail to connect radiologic findings to pathology-level endpoints (TNM stage, nodal metastasis, risk stratification), and single-step reasoning is inadequate for the structural complexity of volumetric CT in oncologic decision-making.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264064/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 3D CT volumes (preprocessed to 256×256×32 in .nii.gz format with soft-tissue window normalization) and a task prompt text as input. It produces an interleaved multimodal CoT reasoning chain that progresses through three hierarchical levels—Finding Chain (objective observations), Impression Chain (intermediate clinical impressions), and Long Reasoning Chain (high-level diagnostic conclusions)—covering four task types: organ/tumor localization, lesion attribute analysis, TNM stage prediction, and structured CoT report generation. The reasoning chain is structured as subject-relation-object triplets at each level, enabling stepwise evaluation of both factual accuracy and logical coherence.

## Method

TumorChain is a hybrid-model interleaved reasoning framework integrating five modules: a 3D vision encoder (M3D), an organ segmentation expert (TotalSegmentator), an auxiliary binary classification head (normal vs. abnormal per organ), an MLP projector, and a Qwen2.5-VL LLM backbone. The core mechanism is Organ-Guided Iterative Interleaved Reasoning (IIR): the LLM first reasons over global CT tokens to identify candidate organs, the segmentation expert extracts ROI masks and local vision tokens for those organs, and the augmented tokens plus a targeted prompt are fed back to the LLM for the next round—repeating until no new ROIs emerge, yielding a progressively refined CoT chain that fuses global context with local evidence. Training uses a Hybrid-Model Collaborative Optimization (HCO) strategy with a joint loss combining the LLM's autoregressive cross-entropy and the auxiliary classifier's binary cross-entropy (weighted by α), ensuring the visual encoder learns discriminative organ-level features while the LLM performs high-level clinical reasoning. The framework is trained on TumorCoT-1.5M, a 1.5M-sample CoT-annotated VQA dataset constructed via a multi-agent, knowledge-graph-guided data engine that converts radiology and pathology reports into four task types with step-aligned rationales.

## Results

On the TumorCoT-1.5M benchmark, TumorChain-7B achieves a mean accuracy of 84.41% across all four task groups (Position, Lesion Attributes, TNM Prediction, CoT-Report), substantially outperforming the strongest commercial baseline GPT-5-Mini (51.59%) and the best open-source model MiniCPM-V4.5-9B (45.98%). On the CoT reasoning metric (CoT_e), TumorChain-7B scores 58.33, trailing only GPT-5-Mini (61.23) while surpassing all other models. On the held-out DeepTumorVQA benchmark, TumorChain-7B reaches 57.51% average accuracy, exceeding the second-best model MedVLM-R1-2B (42.67%) by 14.84 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TumorChain-7B (84.41% avg.) dominates all 12 baselines on the TumorCoT-1.5M benchmark by a wide margin: the closest commercial model GPT-5-Mini scores 51.59% (a 32.8-point gap), the best generalist MiniCPM-V4.5-9B reaches 45.98%, and the strongest dedicated medical models (Lingshu-7B at 45.10%, M3D-Phi-3-4B at 32.84%) trail by 39+ points. Notably, 3D medical models (RadFM, M3D-Phi-3-4B) underperform even 2D medical and generalist models, revealing a critical bottleneck in spatial understanding of volumetric CT. On the CoT reasoning metric (CoT_e), TumorChain-7B (58.33) is the best open-source model but is slightly outperformed by GPT-5-Mini (61.23), while all other baselines fall below 55. On the external DeepTumorVQA benchmark, TumorChain-7B (57.51% avg.) surpasses the second-best MedVLM-R1-2B (42.67%) by 14.84 points and GPT-5-mini (38.24%) by 19.27 points, confirming strong zero-shot generalization to unseen tumor data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three research threads—medical vision-language models (M3D, Lingshu, HealthGPT), chain-of-thought reasoning in clinical contexts (MedVLM-R1, MedVLThinker, ReasonMed), and interleaved/iterative reasoning (IRCoT, Chain-of-Focus, CX-Mind)—into a unified framework specifically for 3D CT tumor analysis. It departs from prior Med-LVLMs by introducing an organ-guided iterative interleaved reasoning loop that couples a segmentation expert (TotalSegmentator), an auxiliary abnormality classifier, and an LLM in a hybrid collaborative optimization, rather than relying on a single monolithic model. The formulation of the full clinical pipeline (findings → impressions → pathology) as a structured, traceable CoT task with stepwise evaluation is a deliberate extension beyond the study-level report generation that dominates existing medical VQA benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1.5M-sample TumorCoT-1.5M dataset and the TumorChain-Eval stepwise reasoning metric address a critical gap: existing medical benchmarks lack tumor-specific, multi-granular CoT annotations that connect imaging findings to pathology-level endpoints (TNM staging, metastasis). The organ-guided IIR mechanism offers a practical template for grounding LLM reasoning in spatial evidence for 3D volumetric data, potentially transferable to other organ systems. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the five digestive organs, whether the heavy reliance on TotalSegmentator masks limits robustness to atypical anatomy, and whether the CoT_e scores (still below GPT-5-mini) indicate a fundamental ceiling for open-source models in complex clinical reasoning.
