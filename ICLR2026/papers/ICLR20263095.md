## Problem

Existing diffusion and flow-based super-resolution methods either require many iterative sampling steps for high perceptual quality or rely on distillation that fixes the fidelity-realism trade-off at a single operating point. This lack of flexibility limits applicability in domains like medical imaging, remote sensing, and film upscaling where different tasks demand varying balances between reconstruction fidelity and perceptual realism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263095/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a low-resolution image x_LR and a scalar trade-off parameter t ∈ [0,1] as input. Internally, x_LR is noise-augmented via VP noising (x_0 = √(1−σ_p²)·x_LR + σ_p·ε) to form the initial state, and both x_0 and x_LR are channel-concatenated as the network input. A single forward pass of the student network produces the high-resolution output x_1^t = x_0 + v_φ(x_0, x_LR, t), where t≈0 yields high-fidelity (high PSNR, blurry) reconstructions and t≈1 yields high-realism (low LPIPS, detailed) reconstructions.

## Method

OFTSR uses a two-stage pipeline. In stage one, a noise-augmented conditional rectified flow is trained from scratch: the initial distribution is noise-perturbed LR images (expanding support to enable diverse HR reconstructions), while the clean LR is channel-concatenated as a condition to compensate for information loss from perturbation. In stage two, a one-step student is distilled from the teacher by enforcing that the student's implicit intermediate states at two timesteps t and s (s = t + dt) lie on the same PF-ODE trajectory of the teacher, i.e., x_s = x_t + (s−t)·v_θ(x_t, t). This constraint yields a distillation loss (Eq. 9) that aligns the student's velocity predictions without requiring full ODE trajectory simulation, supplemented by an alignment loss and a boundary loss at t=0. The key insight is that single-step estimates x_1^t from different timesteps naturally trace the fidelity-realism curve, so preserving the ODE trajectory structure in the student preserves this tunability at inference time with only one NFE.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 4× SR, OFTSR's one-step distilled model (t=1) achieves PSNR 24.20 dB, LPIPS 0.135, and FID 52.69 with only 1 NFE, surpassing SinSR (PSNR 22.25, LPIPS 0.207, FID 94.90) and DCD (PSNR 24.67, LPIPS 0.156, FID 62.06 at 5 NFEs). The tunable parameter t enables a continuous fidelity-realism trade-off: at t=0 the model reaches PSNR 26.18 (highest fidelity) while at t=1 it achieves the best perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.135). On real-world SR (RealLQ250), OFTSR distilled from DiT4SR achieves the best MUSIQ (72.60), MANIQA (0.5904), CLIPIQA (0.7252), and LIQE (4.1122) among all one-step methods, with competitive NIQE (3.78 vs. TSDSR's 3.49).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OFTSR's one-step distilled model (t=1) outperforms all prior one-step SR methods: it beats SinSR by +1.95 dB PSNR and −0.072 LPIPS, and DCD (5 NFEs) by −0.021 LPIPS while using 5× fewer NFEs. Against multi-step training-free methods (DPS, DDRM, DDNM, DiffPIR, SITCOM, CDBD), OFTSR achieves substantially better LPIPS (0.135 vs. 0.191–0.438) and FID (52.69 vs. 58.25–164.99) with 1–1000× fewer NFEs. On real-world SR (Table 6), OFTSR distilled from DiT4SR achieves the best no-reference IQA scores (MUSIQ 72.60, MANIQA 0.5904, CLIPIQA 0.7252, LIQE 4.1122) among all one-step methods, narrowly trailing TSDSR only on NIQE (3.78 vs. 3.49). The tunable t parameter is unique among all compared methods: at t=0, OFTSR achieves the highest PSNR (26.18) among one-step methods, while at t=1 it achieves the best LPIPS (0.135), a flexibility no other baseline offers. When distilled from the ResShift teacher, OFTSR matches or exceeds SinSR (which also distills ResShift) in FID (60.64 vs. 94.90) with 6× less training compute (5k vs. 30k iterations).

## Relation to Prior Work

OFTSR builds on the rectified flow framework (Liu et al., 2022) for the generative backbone and adapts the BOOT distillation paradigm (Gu et al., 2023) into a flow-based setting. Its noise-augmented conditional flow training stage generalizes both InDI (Delbracio & Milanfar, 2023) and SR3 (Saharia et al., 2022) as special cases of a unified VP-noising formulation. The key departure from prior one-step SR distillation methods (SinSR, DAVI, OSEDiff, AddSR) is the ODE-trajectory constraint: rather than directly matching teacher outputs or using adversarial/score-distillation losses, OFTSR forces the student's single-step predictions at different timesteps to lie on the same teacher PF-ODE trajectory, which is what preserves the fidelity-realism curve. The authors further show their loss is a discrete-time counterpart of the forward distillation loss from MeanFlow (Geng et al., 2025) and AlignYourFlow (Sabour et al., 2025), situating it within a broader theoretical family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

One-step SR with a user-controllable fidelity-realism knob is practically important for domains like medical imaging, remote sensing, and film upscaling where different applications demand different trade-offs, yet prior one-step distillation methods locked in a fixed operating point. The distillation loss is model-agnostic (demonstrated on self-trained flows, ResShift, and SD-based DiT4SR) and requires only ~5k training iterations, making it a low-cost plug-in for any pre-trained conditional flow model. Limitations include a slight deviation from the teacher's perception-distortion frontier (attributed to finite step size) and performance ultimately bounded by the teacher; the authors note that incorporating ground-truth regression or adversarial supervision could close this gap. An open question is whether the ODE-trajectory constraint generalizes beyond SR to other inverse problems where a tunable trade-off is desired.
