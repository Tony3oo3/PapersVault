## Problem

Existing differential privacy mechanisms for Riemannian manifolds rely on computationally expensive MCMC sampling and are restricted to specific privacy notions or specific manifolds (e.g., (ε,δ)-DP only on SPDM with the flat Log-Euclidean metric, GDP only on constant-curvature spaces). There is no unified, scalable framework that extends multiple DP notions—ε-DP, (ε,δ)-DP, GDP, and RDP—to general Hadamard manifolds while remaining practical in high dimensions.

## Input / Output

The mechanism takes a dataset of manifold-valued points (SPDM matrices under Log-Cholesky, Log-Euclidean, or affine-invariant metrics, or points in hyperbolic space) assumed to lie within a geodesic ball B_r(m₀) for finite sensitivity, a privacy budget (μ for GDP, ε for ε-DP, or (α,ε) for RDP), and a fixed footpoint p₀ that must not depend on the private data. It outputs a single privatized Fréchet mean on the manifold, sampled by drawing noise from a standard tangent-space distribution (Gaussian or Laplace) and applying the exponential map at p₀. The footpoint choice directly affects utility through the term d(p₀, x̄) in the theoretical bounds, but its influence diminishes in high dimensions.

## Method

The core construction is the Exponential-Wrapped distribution: a probability on the manifold defined as the push-forward of a tangent-space density via the exponential map Exp_{p₀}, with a volume-distortion factor J_{p₀} in the resulting density. For the Laplace mechanism, sampling u from a tangent-space Laplace centered at Log_{p₀}(f(D)) with rate σ=Δ/ε and returning Exp_{p₀}(u) achieves ε-DP, improving on the Riemannian Laplace mechanism's required rate of 2Δ/ε on non-homogeneous manifolds. For the Gaussian mechanism, sampling u from N(Log_{p₀}(f(D)), σ²I) on the tangent space and returning Exp_{p₀}(u) achieves (ε,δ)-DP (via the analytic Gaussian inequality with Δ_{p₀}), μ-GDP with the closed-form calibration σ=Δ/μ, and (α,ε)-RDP with σ=Δ/√(2ε/α). The Cartan-Hadamard theorem guarantees that Log_{p₀} is globally defined and a contraction, which is what makes the DP proofs reduce to standard Euclidean arguments. Utility bounds for the privatized Fréchet mean are derived in terms of dimension, curvature lower bound K, and footpoint distance, showing that in flat manifolds the footpoint drops out entirely and in high dimensions its effect becomes negligible.

## Results

The Exponential-Wrapped Gaussian (EWG) mechanism consistently achieves lower Riemannian distance to the true Fréchet mean than the Riemannian Laplace (RL) baseline across SPDM spaces under Log-Cholesky and Log-Euclidean metrics, hyperbolic space, and the real-world OCTMNIST dataset, for all tested dimensions (d ∈ {3, 10, 15}) and privacy budgets (μ ∈ [0.1, 2]). Under the affine-invariant metric, EWG degrades slightly at moderate-to-high privacy budgets (μ ∈ [0.7, 2]) when d = 3 due to footpoint misalignment, but outperforms RL for d = 10 and 15. Computationally, EWG is orders of magnitude faster: at m = 30 (d = 465), EWG takes 0.007 s on average versus 6.85 s for RL (~966× speedup), with the gap widening as dimension increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole experimental baseline is the Riemannian Laplace (RL) mechanism of Reimherr et al. (NeurIPS 2021), which requires MCMC sampling with a 10,000-iteration burn-in. EWG wins on utility (lower Riemannian distance to the true Fréchet mean) in nearly all settings: consistently under Log-Cholesky and Log-Euclidean metrics for all dimensions and privacy budgets, consistently on hyperbolic space, and consistently on the OCTMNIST real-data experiment across all four classes. The one exception is the affine-invariant metric at d = 3 with μ ∈ [0.7, 2], where RL slightly outperforms EWG due to footpoint misalignment effects that are negligible in higher dimensions. On runtime, EWG dominates by roughly three orders of magnitude (e.g., 0.002 s vs. 1.0 s at m = 2; 0.007 s vs. 6.85 s at m = 30), making it the only practical option for high-dimensional SPDM spaces. The paper also notes that EWG generalizes the tangent Gaussian mechanism of Utpala et al. (2023b) for the Log-Euclidean case, but no direct head-to-head comparison with that method is provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends the line of differential privacy mechanisms on Riemannian manifolds initiated by Reimherr et al. (2021), who introduced the Riemannian Laplace mechanism requiring MCMC sampling, and Jiang et al. (2023), who extended GDP to manifolds but only for constant-curvature spaces with expensive calibration. The core technical tool is the Exponential-Wrapped distribution framework from Chevallier et al. (2022), which the authors repurpose to push forward simple tangent-space noise (Laplace or Gaussian) via the exponential map, thereby eliminating MCMC entirely. The (ε,δ)-DP calibration directly adapts the analytic Gaussian mechanism of Balle and Wang (2018) by substituting a footpoint-dependent sensitivity Δ_{p₀} for the standard sensitivity, while the GDP and RDP results reduce to closed-form rate parameters (σ = Δ/μ and σ = Δ/√(2ε/α) respectively), in contrast to the integral-inequality calibration required by prior Riemannian Gaussian mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to simultaneously support ε-DP, (ε,δ)-DP, GDP, and RDP on general Hadamard manifolds with a single, MCMC-free sampling procedure, making privacy-preserving Fréchet mean estimation practical in high-dimensional settings (e.g., 15-dimensional SPDM for medical imaging). The footpoint p₀ introduces a new design parameter whose optimal selection in non-constant-curvature spaces remains open, and the authors note that in low-dimensional curved spaces (d=3, affine-invariant metric) the footpoint misalignment can degrade utility at moderate privacy budgets. Extending the framework to manifolds with non-negative curvature (e.g., spheres) and to tasks beyond Fréchet mean estimation (principal geodesic analysis, regression) are natural next steps that the paper leaves for future work.
