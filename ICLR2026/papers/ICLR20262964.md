## Problem

While it has been empirically observed that Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) produces better-calibrated models than standard optimizers like SGD, the theoretical mechanism behind this calibration benefit remained unexplained. This paper formally investigates why SAM counteracts overconfidence and proposes a targeted variant to further reduce calibration error without sacrificing accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262964/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a drop-in optimizer replacement (for SGD or AdamW) on standard RGB image classification tasks, consuming images at their native resolutions (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K). The output is a softmax probability vector over K classes whose maximum serves as the predicted label; the key property is that this probability vector is well-calibrated, meaning the predicted confidence closely matches the empirical correctness rate across confidence bins.

## Method

The paper proves that SAM's two-step perturbation procedure implicitly regularizes the negative entropy of the predictive distribution: minimizing the perturbed loss ℓ_θ̃(z) is lower-bounded by ℓ_θ(z) − λH(p_y) + H(p̃_y) with λ > 1, so the optimizer inherently pushes p_y away from 1 (reducing overconfidence) and toward 0 (reducing underconfidence). Building on this, CSAM modifies the per-example loss in SAM's outer loop by reweighting over-confident examples (p̃_y > 1/2) with a factor (1+p̃_y)^(−γ), which strengthens the implicit entropy penalty specifically where overconfidence is most harmful. Theoretically, this introduces a coefficient (1−γ/2) before H(p̃_y), making the net penalty on overconfidence strictly stronger than vanilla SAM while preserving the descent direction. The full procedure retains SAM's two backpropagation steps per iteration, with the only change being the modified loss used to compute the perturbed gradient in the second step.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (WideResNet-20-10), CSAM achieves 96.97% test accuracy with 0.50% ECE, outperforming SAM (96.91% acc, 0.86% ECE) and all 15 calibration-focused baselines. On ImageNet-1K, CSAM with ResNet-50 reaches 77.95% accuracy and 1.18% ECE (vs. 76.97%/3.39% for SGD), while with ViT-S/16 it achieves 75.91% accuracy and 1.58% ECE (vs. 71.35%/9.72% for AdamW). Under distribution shift (ImageNet-C), CSAM reduces OOD ECE by 40–60% relative to the base optimizer, e.g., 3.28% vs. 7.97% for ResNet-50 on motion blur.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSAM dominates all 15 baselines on ECE (0.50%) while matching or exceeding their accuracy; the closest competitor is MIMO (0.88% ECE, 95.96% acc), which CSAM beats by 0.38% ECE and 1.01% accuracy. SAM itself already outperforms focal-loss variants (FL: 1.16%, AdaFocal: 0.91%), probabilistic methods (Rank1-BNN: 1.92%, VI: 3.14%), and label-smoothing approaches (ACLS: 2.48%) on ECE, while also achieving the highest accuracy (96.91%). On ImageNet-1K, CSAM consistently improves over SAM by 0.18–0.34% ECE (e.g., ResNet-50: 1.18% vs. 1.52%) and 0.3–0.6% accuracy, and both substantially outperform the base optimizers (SGD: 3.39% ECE; AdamW on ViT-S/16: 9.72% ECE). Under OOD shift (ImageNet-C), CSAM reduces ECE by ~40% relative to SGD (3.28% vs. 7.97% on motion blur for ResNet-50), and the gap widens for ViTs (3.01% vs. 14.14% for ViT-S/16). The Bayesian variant bSAM (1.82% ECE) underperforms both SAM and CSAM, attributed to additional hyperparameters that complicate tuning. On CIFAR-100, CSAM achieves 83.07% accuracy and 1.93% ECE, beating SAM (82.93%/2.11%) and all baselines including MIMO (80.75%/2.38%) and AdaFocal (80.70%/2.58%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SAM (Foret et al., 2021) by providing the first formal theoretical explanation for SAM's empirically observed calibration benefits, showing that its perturbation-based optimization implicitly maximizes the entropy of the predictive distribution—an effect analogous to focal loss (Mukhoti et al., 2020) but achieved through the optimizer rather than the loss function. It positions itself at the intersection of the sharpness/generalization literature (Wen et al., 2022; Bartlett et al., 2023) and the calibration literature (Guo et al., 2017; Minderer et al., 2021), bridging two communities that had previously treated flat minima and overconfidence as separate concerns. The proposed CSAM variant modifies SAM's outer-loop loss to more aggressively suppress over-confident examples, extending the implicit entropy regularization with an explicit confidence-dependent penalty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result gives practitioners a principled reason to prefer SAM over SGD when calibrated predictions matter (e.g., safety-critical applications), and CSAM offers a drop-in improvement with minimal hyperparameter overhead. However, the analysis relies on a boundedness assumption on the Hessian's smallest eigenvalue that, while empirically validated, is non-trivial and may not hold universally; the multi-class setting is reduced to a binary entropy argument via geometric means, which may obscure class-specific calibration failures. Open questions include whether the calibration benefit is purely attributable to the entropy regularization or also to SAM's other optimization properties (e.g., gradient norm penalization), whether CSAM's advantage persists at very large scale or with non-CE losses in a systematic way, and why MC-Dropout unexpectedly degrades SAM's OOD performance.
