## Problem

Existing efficiency-oriented conformal prediction methods optimize average prediction set size, but in practice the most valuable outcome is a singleton (unambiguous) prediction, since multi-label sets trigger costly downstream interventions. The singleton objective (minimizing P(|C(X)|>1), or M-criterion) was conceptualized by Vovk et al. (2005) but no practical conformal prediction method had been developed to optimize it. This paper bridges that gap by deriving a nonconformity score that explicitly targets singleton frequency while maintaining marginal coverage.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the probability vector p̂(·|x) from any pre-trained K-class classifier (no retraining required), a pre-computed calibration quantile q̂ from n calibration points, and a scalar regularization parameter λ. It outputs a prediction set C(x) that is always a prefix of the probability-sorted label ordering (i.e., the top-k most probable labels for some k), with the marginal coverage guarantee P(Y∈C(X))≥1−α. The nonconformity score r(x,yᵢ) is the smallest Lagrange multiplier τ at which label yᵢ enters the optimal nested set, computed in O(K) time per instance via a lower convex hull construction.

## Method

Starting from a constrained optimization over prediction sets that minimizes a linear combination of non-singleton probability and expected length subject to coverage, the paper takes the Lagrangian and exploits its separability across x to define a per-instance cost. Two structural lemmas show that optimal prediction sets are always top-k labels and are nested as the Lagrange multiplier η increases, enabling a nested conformal prediction formulation. The key computational insight is a geometric reformulation: for a probability vector γ, the optimal index κ(η;γ) is characterized by the vertices and edge slopes of the lower convex hull of K+1 points P_k=(Γ_k, g_k) in ℝ², where Γ_k is the cumulative probability and g_k=I(k>1)+λk. Theorem 2.5 proves that κ jumps exactly at the hull edge slopes, so the nonconformity score and final prediction set are found by a single O(K) monotone-chain pass over the hull. This yields the SOCOP algorithm: sort probabilities, compute the lower convex hull, and select the largest hull vertex whose incoming edge slope does not exceed the calibration quantile.

## Results

On ImageNet-Val with ResNet152-v2, SOCOP achieves P(size>1) = 0.370 with Avg Size 2.477, reducing the non-singleton probability by ~20% relative to Least Ambiguous Sets (0.466) while only increasing average set size by 0.20. For ViT-h-14, SOCOP achieves P(size>1) = 0.175 vs. 0.224 for Least Ambiguous Sets at nearly identical Avg Size (1.356 vs. 1.291). On TissueMNIST, SOCOP reduces P(size>1) from 0.788 to 0.638 (~15% reduction), and on MMLU from 0.675 to 0.587 (~10% reduction), in both cases with negligible increase in average set size. All methods maintain the target 0.95 marginal coverage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOCOP outperforms RAPS (Angelopoulos et al., 2021) on both Avg Size and P(size>1) across all models and datasets; for example, on ResNet152-v2/ImageNet-Val, SOCOP achieves Avg Size 2.477 vs. 3.158 and P(size>1) 0.370 vs. 0.603. Against Least Ambiguous Sets (Sadinle et al., 2019), SOCOP trades a small increase in Avg Size (e.g., 2.477 vs. 2.274 for ResNet152-v2) for a substantial reduction in P(size>1) (0.370 vs. 0.466, a ~20% relative decrease); this trade-off is consistent across all five ImageNet models, TissueMNIST (~15% reduction), and MMLU (~10% reduction). The Pure Singleton limit (λ=0) achieves the lowest P(size>1) but at the cost of enormous average set sizes (e.g., 249 for ResNet152-v2), confirming that the intermediate λ values used by SOCOP are essential for practical utility. The CPL method (Kiyani et al., 2024), which optimizes length via a training procedure, is evaluated in the appendix and shows comparable or worse P(size>1) than SOCOP at similar average sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conformal prediction framework by introducing a new nonconformity score derived from the singleton objective (M-criterion) first conceptualized by Vovk et al. (2005), for which no practical method previously existed. It builds directly on split conformal prediction (Papadopoulos et al., 2002) and nested conformal prediction (Gupta et al., 2022) as the computational backbone, but departs from prior efficiency-oriented scores (RAPS, Least Ambiguous Sets) that optimize only average set length. The key methodological departure is a Lagrangian relaxation of a non-convex constrained optimization over prediction sets, whose geometric reformulation as a lower convex hull problem yields an O(K) per-instance algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: singleton predictions avoid costly human intervention in downstream workflows, and the ~20% reduction in non-singleton probability at near-identical average set size is a meaningful operational improvement across ImageNet, medical imaging, and LLM benchmarks. The O(K) algorithm makes the method scalable to large-class problems (1000-class ImageNet) without approximation. Open questions include extending the score to conditional or Mondrian conformal prediction, designing intrinsically conditional-coverage-aware variants, and eliminating the separate tuning set for λ selection to improve data efficiency.
