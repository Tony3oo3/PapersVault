## Problem

Existing humanoid whole-body control policies are task-specific, non-adaptive, and lack a unified interface for goal specification, typically relying on on-policy RL (PPO) with explicit tracking rewards. No prior work has demonstrated that off-policy unsupervised RL can handle the sim-to-real gap and dynamic disturbances robustly enough to deploy a promptable, multi-task behavioral foundation model on a real humanoid robot.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262692/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a proprioceptive history of H=4 steps (joint positions normalized to nominal, joint velocities, root angular velocity, and projected gravity, totaling 93·H+64 dimensions) plus a 256-dimensional latent prompt vector z, and outputs 29 DoF PD controller targets. At inference, the latent prompt is computed differently per task: for reward optimization, z_r is a sample-based estimate from the replay buffer; for goal reaching, z_g = B(s_g) is a single backward-map evaluation; for tracking, a look-ahead sequence z_t = Σ B(s_{t'}) is used. The privileged state (463D: root height, body pose, rotation, linear/angular velocities) is available only to critics during training, not to the deployed policy.

## Method

BFM-Zero trains an off-policy actor-critic using Forward-Backward (FB) successor-feature representations, where a forward map F and backward map B jointly approximate long-term policy dynamics, inducing a smooth, task-centric latent space Z. A latent-conditioned discriminator trained with a GAN-style objective on motion-capture data (LAFAN1) regularizes the policy toward human-like behaviors, while an auxiliary critic enforces safety constraints (joint limits, action rate, self-contact, feet orientation). Critical sim-to-real design choices include asymmetric history-dependent training (policy sees only proprioceptive history; critics see privileged state), domain randomization of physical parameters, and massively parallel environments (1024 envs, ~192M training steps) following the FastTD3 recipe. Zero-shot inference embeds arbitrary rewards, goals, or motions into Z without retraining, and few-shot adaptation refines the latent prompt via CEM or dual-annealing trajectory optimization in Z, avoiding any network weight updates.

## Results

BFM-Zero achieves a tracking error (E_mpjpe) of 1.0789 on LAFAN1 and 1.0342 on out-of-distribution AMASS motions in MuJoCo with domain randomization, while attaining an average reward return of 207.3 and a goal-reaching error of 1.1041 (LAFAN1) / 1.4735 (AMASS). In real-world deployment on a Unitree G1, the average tracking error is 1.1408 E_mpjpe, only 0.34 rad higher than the domain-randomized simulation (0.8041). Few-shot CEM adaptation reduces trajectory tracking error by ~29.1% and enables stable single-leg standing under an extra 4 kg payload for over 15 s (vs. destabilization within 5 s without adaptation).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole direct baseline is GMT (Chen et al., 2025), a state-of-the-art general motion tracking controller. BFM-Zero outperforms GMT by a wide margin on tracking error: 1.0789 vs. 2.2425 on LAFAN1 (52% lower) and 1.0342 vs. 1.9064 on AMASS (46% lower), while using only 200 M environment steps versus GMT's 6,800 M (34× fewer). GMT also struggles with get-up and on-ground motions, causing episode terminations that further inflate its tracking error. Beyond tracking, BFM-Zero uniquely supports zero-shot reward optimization, goal reaching, and latent-space interpolation—capabilities that GMT does not provide—making the comparison partially asymmetric in task scope. In real-world validation, BFM-Zero's tracking error (1.1408) remains close to its domain-randomized simulation counterpart (0.8041), confirming robust sim-to-real transfer that GMT's evaluation does not address.

## Relation to Prior Work

BFM-Zero extends the Forward-Backward (FB) representation framework (Touati & Ollivier, 2021) and its online, motion-regularized variant FB-CPR (Tirinzoni et al., 2025), which had previously been demonstrated only on simulated virtual characters. The key departure is from the dominant on-policy PPO paradigm used in virtually all prior real-world humanoid control work (HOVER, GMT, OmniH2O, ASAP, etc.), which relies on explicit tracking rewards and either single-task training or two-stage distillation into a multi-task policy. BFM-Zero instead trains a single off-policy, unsupervised policy with a structured latent space that supports zero-shot reward optimization, goal reaching, and motion tracking without task-specific retraining, and adds domain randomization, asymmetric history-dependent training, and auxiliary reward regularization to bridge the sim-to-real gap for the first time in this algorithmic family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that off-policy unsupervised RL can produce a deployable, promptable whole-body controller for a real humanoid, challenging the field's implicit assumption that on-policy policy gradient methods are necessary for sim-to-real transfer. The unified latent space enables intuitive task specification (linear reward combinations, single-frame goals, motion sequences) and smooth skill interpolation without retraining, opening a path toward scalable behavioral foundation models. Key open questions include: how behavior quality and coverage scale with motion dataset size (scaling laws), whether the current adaptation mechanisms (CEM, trajectory optimization) suffice for genuinely novel skills, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the 29-DoF Unitree G1 to more complex morphologies or manipulation tasks.
