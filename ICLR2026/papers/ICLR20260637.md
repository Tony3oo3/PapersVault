## Problem

Safety-aligned LLMs frequently reject harmless instructions that merely contain superficially risky vocabulary (over-refusal), undermining usability. The paper identifies the root cause as a cognitive bias in the model's internal representation space: pseudo-harmful instructions are over-encoded with harmful features in hidden states, shifting the internal decision boundary. Existing mitigations either require costly retraining or add inference-time overhead without correcting this underlying parameter-level bias.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three small instruction sets (200 examples each from HEx-PHI, Alpaca-Cleaned, and OR-Bench) to extract activation vectors from every submodule weight matrix, plus the full set of LLM weight matrices. It produces a single set of modified weight matrices (W' = (I − λΩ)W) applied to a small number of selected middle layers, yielding a fully standalone model with no auxiliary vectors, steering files, or inference-time modifications required. The pruning rank r=16 and strength λ∈[0,1] are the only tunable hyperparameters, and the theoretical energy bound guarantees that less than ~8% of total parameter energy is removed.

## Method

ProSafePrune constructs three subspaces via truncated SVD on mean-pooled layer activations (safe, harmful, pseudo-harmful), then defines an overlap operator Ω = (I − Π^(s))Π^(u)Π^(p) that isolates precisely the harmful components embedded within pseudo-harmful directions while excluding safe-aligned components. This operator is applied as a left-multiplication on selected weight matrices (W' = (I − λΩ)W), effectively carving out low-rank row-space directions that push pseudo-harmful representations toward harmful outputs. Target layers are identified via t-SNE visualization and silhouette scores, which reveal that middle layers exhibit the strongest feature separability. Two theoretical results underpin the approach: the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem guarantees SVD subspaces are optimal low-rank approximations, and a stable-rank energy bound (‖ΩW‖²_F / ‖W‖²_F ≤ r/sr(W)) proves the pruned energy is negligible relative to total model capacity, explaining why general task performance is preserved or slightly improved.

## Results

On LLaMA-2-7B, ProSafePrune achieves an average compliance rate (C.R.) of 84.5% and tradeoff score (T.S.) of 88.4, outperforming the strongest baseline (Surgical, T.S. 82.4) by +6.0 points. On LLaMA-2-13B, the gap widens dramatically: ProSafePrune reaches T.S. 82.5 versus Surgical's 72.1 and SCAN's 55.1. On LLaMA-3-8B (which has milder over-refusal), ProSafePrune still leads with T.S. 89.4 vs. Surgical's 87.2. Safety scores on AdvBench and JailbreakBench remain within 1–2 points of the default model, and general capabilities improve (e.g., MMLU 37.1→39.6, CommonsenseQA 49.0→53.0 on LLaMA-2-7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProSafePrune outperforms all three baselines on the average tradeoff score (T.S.) across every evaluated LLaMA model. Against Self-CD (Shi et al., ACL 2024), which decodes prompts twice and suppresses refusal tokens via probability comparison, ProSafePrune gains +9.2 T.S. on LLaMA-2-7B (88.4 vs. 79.2) and +15.4 on LLaMA-2-13B (82.5 vs. 77.1); Self-CD also suffers notable safety degradation on LLaMA-3-8B (JBB drops to 73.0). Against SCAN (Cao et al., AAAI 2025), which uses cosine-similarity-based activation steering, ProSafePrune leads by +6.1 T.S. on LLaMA-2-7B and a striking +27.4 on LLaMA-2-13B, where SCAN's compliance collapses to 32.9% (OR-Bench 4.0%). Against Surgical (Wang et al., ICLR 2025), the closest competitor using single-vector ablation, ProSafePrune still wins by +6.0 T.S. on LLaMA-2-7B and +2.2 on LLaMA-3-8B, while maintaining comparable safety scores (within 1–2 points on AdvBench and JBB). The advantage is most pronounced on models with severe over-refusal (LLaMA-2 family), where the multi-dimensional subspace pruning captures richer refusal structure than single-vector or activation-level interventions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both training-based over-refusal mitigation (Safety Patching, targeted SFT, Dabas et al.'s refusal-direction fine-tuning) and training-free inference-time interventions (Self-CD, SCAN, Surgical) by operating directly in parameter space via low-rank pruning. Conceptually, it builds on Wei et al. (2024)'s observation that safety alignment is carried by low-rank structures, and on Dabas et al. (2025)'s layer-selection methodology (t-SNE + silhouette scores), but introduces a novel subspace-overlap operator that selectively removes harmful amplification within pseudo-harmful directions while preserving safe encoding. The key reframing is treating over-refusal not as an output-level alignment failure but as an internal 'over-harmful encoding' bias in hidden-state geometry, grounded in SVD-based subspace decomposition with an Eckart-Young-Mirsky optimality guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic account linking over-refusal to the broader alignment tax, suggesting that the same low-rank harmful-encoding directions that cause false rejections also suppress general capability — a connection that, if validated more broadly, could reshape how alignment objectives are designed. Practical advantages (zero inference overhead, standalone pruned model, no auxiliary vectors to store) make it more deployable than activation-steering baselines. However, the method requires white-box parameter access, relies on static subspaces from fixed datasets, and shows diminishing returns on already well-aligned models (Qwen series), leaving open questions about dynamic adaptation, black-box applicability, and multi-turn safety contexts.
