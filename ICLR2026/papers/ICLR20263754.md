## Problem

Diagnosing vulnerabilities in robot manipulation policies is hindered by two coupled challenges: the relevant environmental variations to test are often unknown a priori, and direct physical testing is slow, expensive, and potentially unsafe. Existing approaches—static VLM queries, heuristic trial-and-error, or OOD detection—either fail to iteratively interact with the policy, cannot generalize beyond a fixed set of known failures, or conflate out-of-distribution inputs with actual policy failures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes M labeled success-failure rollouts, each consisting of an RGB image (the raw visual input typically fed to manipulation policies) and an auto-generated short textual description of the applied environmental variation (e.g., object color, lighting level). The trained RoboMD policy outputs a Gaussian density over a 512-dimensional embedding space, which can be queried to produce a ranked vulnerability-likelihood map for both seen and previously unseen variations, or a categorical probability mass function over a discrete candidate set. These likelihoods directly drive targeted fine-tuning of the manipulation policy, requiring only 1.3 GB of failure data versus 9.0 GB for exhaustive fine-tuning.

## Method

RoboMD trains a PPO-based deep RL agent (π_MD) to actively search for failure-inducing conditions within a continuous vision-language embedding space, rather than over discrete hand-specified variations. The embedding is constructed by a dual backbone—ViT-B/16 for visual features and a CLIP text encoder for semantic descriptions—projected through a 512-unit MLP and trained jointly with binary cross-entropy and a contrastive loss that enforces local smoothness, effectively turning the space into a potential field where the agent is attracted toward failure clusters and repelled from success clusters. During RL training, the agent samples a 512-dim action vector, snaps it to the nearest known embedding to infer success/failure without physical rollouts, and receives a reward shaped by potential differences, distance penalties, and a frequency penalty to encourage exploration. Three theorems provide theoretical grounding: potential-based reward shaping preserves optimality and advantage invariance, entropy-driven exploration concentrates near the success/failure boundary with polynomial sample complexity, and the shaped critic converges faster than the unshaped one. This architecture-agnostic diagnostic agent can be queried at any time to predict failure likelihood for novel environmental conditions, enabling scalable pre-deployment vulnerability analysis.

## Results

RoboMD (PPO-based) achieves 80.7% average ranking accuracy across Lift, Square, and Pick & Place tasks, outperforming the best RL baseline A2C (75.0%) and all VLM baselines (GPT-4o-ICL at 54.3%, Gemini 1.5 Pro at 44.3%, Qwen2-VL at 38.0%). The framework uncovers up to 23% more unique vulnerabilities than state-of-the-art vision-language baselines. Fine-tuning the manipulation policy with RoboMD-selected failures achieves 92.83% accuracy using only 1.29 GB of targeted data, compared to 85.48% when fine-tuning on all 9.0 GB of known failures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoboMD (PPO) consistently outperforms all baselines across every task. Against the strongest RL baseline A2C, RoboMD gains +5.7 points average (82.3% vs 74.2% on Lift, 84.0% vs 79.0% on Square, 76.0% vs 72.0% on Pick Place). Against SAC, the margin is far larger at +28.5 points average, with SAC struggling below 55% on all tasks. All VLM baselines (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Qwen2-VL, and GPT-4o with 5-shot ICL) remain below 60% on every task, with GPT-4o-ICL being the closest at 54.3% average — still 26.4 points below RoboMD. The lightweight CNN and ResNet baselines also fall well short (50.6% and 48.3% average), confirming that standalone visual encoders cannot disentangle subtle failure cues. The paper attributes PPO's advantage to its higher action-space entropy (2.8839 vs A2C's lower value), enabling broader exploration of the failure landscape.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes potential-based reward shaping theory (Ng et al., 1999) not for its original purpose of reward design, but to structure a learned vision-language embedding as a continuous potential field that guides RL exploration toward failure regions. It departs from prior robot failure-detection work—which has focused on OOD detection, runtime monitoring, or static VLM queries—by formulating vulnerability discovery as an active sequential search problem solved by PPO in a semantically rich embedding space. The dual ViT-B/16 + CLIP backbone with contrastive loss is a new architectural combination for this diagnostic purpose, and the framework is explicitly positioned as complementary to (rather than a replacement for) generalization-focused training methods like domain randomization or curriculum learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RoboMD provides a policy-agnostic, scalable diagnostic tool that can identify manipulation vulnerabilities without expensive or unsafe physical trials, and the finding that targeted fine-tuning on 1.3 GB of RoboMD-selected failures (92.83% accuracy) outperforms fine-tuning on all 9 GB of known failures (85.48%) offers a practical data-efficiency win for deployment pipelines. Key limitations include the need for initial success-failure rollouts to seed the embedding, evaluation primarily on single-task manipulation (lift, stack, threading, pick-and-place), and absolute diagnostic accuracy (~81%) that leaves substantial room for improvement. Open questions include whether the framework scales to multi-task generalist policies, truly novel failure modes outside the semantic manifold, and integration into continuous regression-testing pipelines as policies evolve.
