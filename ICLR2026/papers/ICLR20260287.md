## Problem

Visual understanding and generation demand fundamentally different representation spaces: reconstruction-trained tokenizers capture low-level appearance but lack language-aligned semantics, while CLIP/SigLIP encoders encode high-level semantics but cannot decode back to pixel space. Directly combining reconstruction and semantic objectives in a single tokenizer creates an inherent conflict that degrades both reconstruction fidelity and semantic accuracy, as demonstrated by VILA-U's distorted outputs and dropped zero-shot performance. The paper asks whether a single unified tokenizer can simultaneously serve both tasks without this degradation.

## Input / Output

For understanding, the model accepts an RGB image (256×256 or 384×384) plus a text question and produces a free-form text response. For generation, it takes a text prompt and autoregressively produces an RGB image at the corresponding resolution. Visually, each image is encoded into a 16×16 (or 27×27) grid of positions, where each position carries two sets of residual codes (pixel and semantic) with depth D=4 or D=8. Critically, the two token types are concatenated along the embedding dimension rather than the sequence dimension, so the LLM sees the same number of visual positions as a single-token design, keeping sequence length and LLM compute unchanged.

## Method

DualToken partitions a pretrained SigLIP ViT into shallow (layers 1–6) and deep (layer 26) regions based on inter-layer cosine similarity, then applies reconstruction loss (L2 + LPIPS + adversarial) to supervise the shallow layers and a semantic preservation loss (negative cosine similarity + L2 distance to the frozen initial SigLIP features) to the deep layer. Shallow and deep features are independently discretized via residual vector quantization into a pixel codebook and a semantic codebook, respectively, with a VQ commitment loss ensuring encoder outputs align with codebook entries. In the unified MLLM (Qwen-2.5 backbone), both token types pass through a 2-layer MLP projector, are concatenated along the embedding dimension, and are interleaved with text tokens for next-token prediction. For image generation, separate pixel and semantic visual heads—each a 3-layer depth transformer with per-depth classification heads—autoregressively predict the residual codes at every visual position. This hierarchical decoupling resolves the objective conflict: the model achieves 0.25 rFID and 82.0% zero-shot accuracy on ImageNet, surpassing both dedicated reconstruction and dedicated semantic models, while the dual tokens mutually reinforce each other (pixel tokens aid understanding via fine-grained detail; semantic tokens guide generation toward text-aligned outputs).

## Results

DualToken (SigLIP-So/14-384) achieves 0.25 rFID and 82.0% zero-shot classification accuracy on ImageNet-1K, simultaneously surpassing dedicated reconstruction models (e.g., SBER-MoVQGAN: 0.68 rFID) and dedicated semantic models (e.g., SigLIP-So/14-384: 83.2% zero-shot). In the unified MLLM setting, DualToken-7B (384px) attains 80.0 MMBench, 72.5 SEED, 47.4 MMMU, 44.3 MMVet, and 1625.0 MME, outperforming VILA-U by 5.8 points on average across ten understanding benchmarks. On GenAI-Bench, DualToken-3B (256px) achieves 0.68 overall VQAScore, a 13% improvement over VILA-U (0.64), and on MJHQ-30K the DualTower 3B model reaches FID 7.88 versus VILA-U 7B's 12.81.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualToken decisively outperforms VILA-U, the closest concurrent unified tokenizer: with SigLIP-So/14-384, DualToken achieves 82.0% zero-shot vs. VILA-U's 78.0% and 0.25 rFID vs. 1.25 rFID, while also recovering the T2I/I2T retrieval scores (21.5/21.6) that VILA-U degrades to 10.0/11.2. Against UniTok, DualToken leads on zero-shot accuracy (82.0% vs. 78.6%) and rFID (0.25 vs. 0.38), and against TokenFlow (384px) it improves rFID from 0.63 to 0.25. In the unified MLLM setting, DualToken-7B (384px) surpasses VILA-U by 5.8 points on average across ten understanding benchmarks (e.g., MMBench 80.0 vs. 72.1 for MUSE-VL, MMMU 47.4 vs. 39.7) and achieves 0.68 overall GenAI-Bench VQAScore versus VILA-U's 0.64. On MJHQ-30K, the DualTower 3B model (FID 7.88) substantially outperforms VILA-U 7B (FID 12.81) and approaches diffusion-based methods like PixArt (FID 6.14). The paper also shows that a naive dual-encoder baseline (SBER-MoVQGAN + frozen SigLIP) yields FID 17.55 on MJHQ-30K, far worse than DualToken's unified 7.88, confirming that learning both codebooks within a single ViT is superior to combining heterogeneous encoders.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of unified autoregressive multimodal models (Chameleon, Emu3, Show-o, Janus) by addressing a core bottleneck: the visual tokenizer. Prior unified tokenizers like VILA-U attempt to serve both reconstruction and semantic objectives through a single codebook, while dual-encoder approaches (Janus, ILLUME+) combine a VQ-VAE pixel encoder with a CLIP semantic encoder as separate modules. DualToken departs from both by exploiting the hierarchical structure of a single ViT backbone—extracting shallow-layer features for a pixel codebook and deep-layer features for a semantic codebook—thereby unifying both vocabularies within one architecture. The depth-transformer prediction mechanism is inherited from RQ-VAE (Lee et al., 2022), and the semantic preservation strategy builds on SigLIP's pretrained text-image alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, layer-based recipe for resolving the reconstruction-vs-semantics conflict that has limited all prior unified visual tokenizers, and demonstrates that the resulting dual tokens mutually enhance each other (pixel tokens improve understanding; semantic tokens improve generation). This offers a practical, drop-in architectural pattern for anyone building discrete autoregressive unified MLLMs, with the added advantage of requiring only ~10% of VILA-U's pretraining data. Open questions include whether the first-quarter layer partitioning generalizes to non-ViT or video architectures, whether combining with more advanced quantizers (e.g., MCQ from UniTok) yields further gains, and whether the approach scales to higher resolutions and longer sequences without the dual-head overhead becoming prohibitive.
