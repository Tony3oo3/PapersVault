## Problem

Current 3D editing pipelines rely on labor-intensive per-scene fine-tuning—either to enforce multi-view consistency across edited views or to generate sufficient consistent inputs for downstream 3DGS optimization—and most still depend on older U-Net-based diffusion architectures rather than the latest DiT/flow-matching models. A key bottleneck is the absence of large-scale multi-view consistent editing datasets, which prevents foundation editing models from learning to propagate edits coherently across viewpoints without scene-specific training.

## Input / Output

TINKER takes an existing 3DGS scene and a text editing prompt, requiring only one (one-shot) or a few (few-shot) rendered views as the editing reference. It renders sparse views from the 3DGS, edits them, estimates per-frame depth via Video Depth Anything, and feeds these into a scene completion model that generates a dense set of view-consistent edited images with known camera poses. The output is a fully edited 3DGS optimized via NeRFStudio, with no per-scene fine-tuning of the editing or completion models. The entire pipeline runs on a single 24 GB consumer GPU in approximately 15 minutes.

## Method

TINKER comprises two components. First, a multi-view consistent editor fine-tunes Flux Kontext with LoRA (rank 128) on a 250K-sample synthesized dataset: two views of the same scene are concatenated and jointly edited, filtered via DINOv2 similarity thresholds (τ_noedit=0.95, τ_mv=0.9), then re-paired as (unedited view, edited reference view) to teach the model reference-driven cross-view editing under a flow-matching loss. Second, a scene completion model built on Wan2.1 1.3B is trained in two stages—pre-training on OpenVid-1M followed by fine-tuning on 3D datasets (DL3DV, Re10k, ArkitScenes, WildRGBD, uCO3D)—to reconstruct dense video from sparse reference views and depth maps. Depth is tokenized identically to RGB frames, reference views share positional embeddings with their target frames, and the text embedding is fixed to a constant so the model focuses purely on depth-guided generation. This reframes editing as reconstruction: the model generalizes from reconstructing original scenes to reconstructing edited scenes from edited sparse views, enabling dense multi-view consistent outputs without per-scene optimization.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF-360 and IN2N, TINKER-few-shot achieves the best CLIP directional similarity (0.157), DINO cross-view consistency (0.959), and aesthetic score (6.338) among all compared methods, while TINKER-one-shot also leads in CLIP-dir (0.143) and aesthetic (6.214). Both settings run on a single 24 GB consumer GPU with an average editing time of 15 minutes, whereas GaussCtrl and TIP-Editor cannot fit on 24 GB GPUs and TIP-Editor requires 35 minutes. In the scene completion ablation (Table S1), TINKER's depth-conditioned model outperforms VACE (DINO 0.978 vs. 0.916) and Framer (DINO 0.978 vs. 0.973) on both geometric consistency and visual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TINKER outperforms all four 3D editing baselines on the primary metrics. Against DGE (Chen et al., ECCV 2024), TINKER-few-shot improves CLIP-dir by 54% (0.157 vs. 0.102) and aesthetic score by 10% (6.338 vs. 5.747) while matching DINO consistency (0.959 vs. 0.948). Against GaussCtrl (Wu et al., ECCV 2024), TINKER gains 27% in CLIP-dir (0.157 vs. 0.123) and 13% in aesthetic (6.338 vs. 5.624), and additionally runs on consumer 24 GB hardware where GaussCtrl cannot. Against TIP-Editor (Zhuang et al., ACM TOG 2024), TINKER achieves the largest margin: 87% higher CLIP-dir (0.157 vs. 0.084), 9.5% higher DINO (0.959 vs. 0.875), and 17% higher aesthetic (6.338 vs. 5.397), while being more than 2× faster (15 min vs. 35 min). Against EditSplat (Lee et al., CVPR 2025), TINKER leads in CLIP-dir (0.157 vs. 0.102) and aesthetic (6.338 vs. 5.661) with comparable DINO scores. In the scene completion comparison (Table S1), TINKER's depth-conditioned model surpasses VACE (Jiang et al., 2025) on all three metrics (DINO 0.978 vs. 0.916, Aesthetic 6.586 vs. 5.833) and Framer (Wang et al., 2024) on text-image similarity (0.821 vs. 0.773) and aesthetic (6.586 vs. 6.227), confirming that explicit depth constraints yield tighter geometric consistency than ray-map or frame-interpolation approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

TINKER builds on two recent foundation models—Flux Kontext for image editing and Wan2.1 for video generation—but departs fundamentally from the prevailing 3D editing paradigm that chains U-Net-based 2D editors with per-scene 3DGS/NeRF fine-tuning. Rather than inheriting the attention-alignment or inversion-based mechanisms of prior methods (DGE, GaussCtrl, TIP-Editor), TINKER repurposes the latent multi-view awareness already present in large DiT-based editing models, amplifying it through a small LoRA fine-tune on a newly curated reference-based editing dataset. The scene completion component reframes 3D editing as a depth-conditioned video reconstruction problem, drawing on video diffusion priors rather than the ray-map or frame-interpolation strategies used by earlier novel-view synthesis approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating per-scene optimization entirely, TINKER substantially lowers the practical barrier to 3D and 4D editing, making it feasible on a single consumer GPU in ~15 minutes. The released multi-view consistent editing dataset (250K samples) and data pipeline address a recognized bottleneck that has slowed progress in view-consistent editing. Key limitations remain: the depth-conditioned scene completion model cannot handle edits involving large geometric deformations, and the synthesized training data occasionally introduces fine-detail inconsistencies. Open questions include whether the latent 3D awareness in 2D foundation models can be further exploited for more complex structural edits, and whether the reconstruction-based scene completion generalizes beyond the camera trajectories seen in training.
