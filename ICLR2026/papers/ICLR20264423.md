## Problem

Memory-based video object segmentation methods rely on pixel-level appearance matching, which degrades severely under occlusions, viewpoint shifts, and multi-shot scene transitions because they lack the conceptual reasoning needed to maintain object identity across drastic visual changes. Existing VOS benchmarks also fail to stress-test this semantic-level reasoning, as they contain mostly single-scene videos with low discontinuity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264423/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates in a semi-supervised setting: the first frame's ground-truth mask is provided, and the model produces per-frame binary segmentation masks for the target object. For LVLM-based concept extraction, a sparse keyframe bank (representative past frames with a green contour drawn around the target) plus the current query frame are resized to 448×448 and fed to InternVL 2.5; the LVLM outputs a single hidden-state embedding from a learnable <SEG> token rather than any generated text. The LVLM is invoked only when an HSV-histogram Bhattacharyya distance between consecutive frames exceeds a threshold (0.35), keeping the concept-guidance ratio below ~10% of frames in practice.

## Method

SeC builds on the frozen SAM 2.1-large image encoder and mask decoder, adding two modules: (1) a pixel-level association memory that extends SAM 2's memory attention to a 22-frame temporal window with object-aware filtering, and (2) a concept-guidance module that uses InternVL 2.5-4B to distill an object-level concept into a single learnable <SEG> token embedding, which is fused into query-frame features via a lightweight cross-attention layer. A scene-adaptive activation strategy (HSV histogram + Bhattacharyya distance) gates LVLM invocation to scene-change frames only, falling back to pure memory-based matching for temporally coherent frames. Training is two-stage: first the memory module is trained on 2k SA-V videos with scene transitions, then InternVL 2.5 is LoRA-fine-tuned on ~190k object instances with 1–7 reference frames (including 0–2 distractor frames) and one query frame. The key insight is progressive concept construction: the concept representation improves as more diverse frames are observed, and an offline re-segmentation pass using the finalized concept yields further gains over the online setting.

## Results

On the newly introduced SeCVOS benchmark, SeC achieves 70.0 J&F overall, surpassing SAM 2.1 (58.2) by 11.8 points and SAM2.1Long (62.3) by 7.7 points. The gap widens with scene complexity: in multi-scene-change videos, SeC scores 67.5 J&F versus 52.4 for SAM 2.1 (+15.1). Across 7 standard VOS benchmarks, SeC sets new state-of-the-art on SA-V (82.7 val / 81.7 test), LVOS v2 (86.5), MOSE v2 (53.8), and M³-VOS (67.2 J), while matching or slightly trailing SAM 2.1 on DAVIS (91.3 vs 90.6) and YouTube-VOS (88.6 vs 88.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeC outperforms all 22 compared baselines on the SeCVOS benchmark, with the largest margins over SAM 2.1 (+11.8 J&F overall, +15.1 in multi-scene videos) and Cutie-base (+17.3). On standard benchmarks, SeC beats SAM 2.1 on 7 of 8 datasets (SA-V val +4.1, MOSE v2 +4.3, LVOS v2 +2.4, M³-VOS +2.3, SA-V test +2.1, MOSE v1 +0.8, DAVIS +0.7) but trails by 0.1 on YouTube-VOS. Against the strongest recent variants, SeC leads SAM2.1Long by +1.6 on SA-V val and +2.3 on MOSE v2, and surpasses SAMURAI by +2.9 on SA-V val and +2.7 on MOSE v2. On the RefVOS extension of SeCVOS, all evaluated methods (VISA, GLUS-A, Sa2VA, Grounded SAM 2, SAMWISE, VideoLISA) score below 60 J&F, confirming the benchmark's difficulty. Traditional memory-based methods (XMem, DEVA, STCN, AOT, DeAOT, RDE, SimVOS) all score below 55 J&F on SeCVOS, highlighting their inability to handle multi-shot semantic discontinuities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAM 2 memory-based VOS paradigm by injecting LVLM-derived concept-level features via a single learnable <SEG> token, drawing on LISA's token-based segmentation idea but repurposing it for implicit (text-free) object concept distillation rather than explicit mask decoding. It builds on SAM2Long's object-aware memory filtering and extends the temporal window, while departing from prior LVLM-segmentation work (VISA, UFO, GLUS) that relies on explicit textual reasoning chains. The scene-adaptive activation strategy—invoking the LVLM only at detected scene transitions via a lightweight HSV histogram detector—is a novel efficiency mechanism not present in prior memory-based or LVLM-based VOS methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SeCVOS benchmark (160 multi-shot videos, 4.26 scenes/video, 30.2% disappearance rate) fills a clear gap: existing VOS benchmarks are saturated (>90 J&F) and lack multi-shot semantic discontinuity, making it difficult to assess whether models truly understand object identity beyond appearance. The finding that <10% concept-guidance activation suffices for most of the gain is practically important for deployment, and the 11.8-point gap over SAM 2.1 on SeCVOS underscores how poorly current methods handle scene transitions. Open questions include whether the lightweight HSV scene detector generalizes to all transition types, whether concept representations degrade under extreme viewpoint shifts (acknowledged failure mode), and how this approach scales to longer videos or temporal prompts like 'a child who is running.'
