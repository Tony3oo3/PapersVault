## Problem

Test-time reinforcement learning (TTRL) relies on pseudo-labels to guide policy optimization, but the standard majority-voting mechanism amplifies systematic reasoning errors: when a model consistently produces the same wrong answer, majority voting reinforces that error with high confidence. The paper addresses how to construct reliable, label-free learning signals that are robust to the model's own biases without requiring external verifiers or ground-truth labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262807/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled text questions (no correctness labels at any stage) and produces adapted model parameters after T training steps. Each question is processed in a dual-view fashion: the original question and a self-generated paraphrase, with 16 rollouts per view (32 total). The pseudo-label is selected by maximizing the harmonic mean of answer frequencies across both views, and the final output is a policy that answers questions with improved reasoning accuracy. All baselines are allocated the same 2N_r rollout budget for fair comparison.

## Method

Self-Harmony uses a single model in two cooperative roles—Solver (generates answers) and Reframer (paraphrases the question)—to create two distributional views of each problem. Instead of majority voting, the pseudo-label is selected via the Harmonic Mean Score (HMS), which the paper proves is the second-order Taylor approximation of a View-Invariant Infomax objective J₂(a) = I(Z_a;A) − 2I(Z_a;X), making it a principled regularizer that penalizes view-dependent spurious answers. The Reframer is trained with a multiplicative reward that gates on answer correctness and applies format and diversity penalties (Jensen-Shannon divergence between answer distributions), ensuring paraphrases are both well-formed and meaningfully different. For computational efficiency, the reframing and second solving steps are fused into a single generative action, reducing the pipeline to two model calls per question per training step.

## Results

Across 30 configurations (5 models × 6 benchmarks), Self-Harmony ranks first in 28 cases and second in the remaining 2, using only 16 original + 16 reframed rollouts per question. Headline improvements include Llama-3.1-8B on GSM8K (60.5% → 91.6%), Qwen3-4B on MATH500 (60.2% → 78.5%), and Qwen3-1.7B on MATH500 (42.7% → 69.6%, nearly matching the oracle GT-Reward score of 71.8%). The method also demonstrates zero training failures across all experiments, whereas baselines like Intuitor and Rent show significant performance degradation (marked with asterisks) after their peak.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Self-Harmony outperforms all four label-free baselines in 28 of 30 model–benchmark configurations. Against Co-Reward (Zhang et al., 2025), the closest competitor, Self-Harmony leads by 2.1–5.0 points on MATH500 (e.g., 78.50 vs. 76.54 on Qwen3-4B) and by up to 11.2 points on GPQA (29.95 vs. 19.79 on Llama-3.2-3B), where Co-Reward's contrastive-agreement signal appears to degrade. Against TTRL/Majority-Voting (Zuo et al., 2025), Self-Harmony gains 1.0–5.0 points on MATH500 and up to 2.8 points on GSM8K (91.59 vs. 88.78 on Llama-3.1-8B); the two methods tie on AIME 2024 for Qwen3-4B (both 20.00), and Majority-Voting narrowly edges Self-Harmony on AIME for Qwen3-8B (24.16 vs. 23.33). Against Intuitor (Zhao et al., 2025b) and Rent (Prabhudesai et al., 2025), Self-Harmony shows the largest margins—up to 22.8 points on MATH500 (69.60 vs. 46.78 for Intuitor on Llama-3.1-8B) and 72.9 points on GSM8K (89.55 vs. 16.67 for Rent on Llama-3.2-3B)—and critically, Intuitor and Rent suffer severe training collapse (asterisked scores) on Llama models, while Self-Harmony maintains stable performance throughout training with zero failures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time reinforcement learning (TTRL) paradigm introduced by Zuo et al. (2025) and the dual-view self-supervised RL approach of Co-Reward (Zhang et al., 2025), but departs from both by replacing majority voting with a harmonic-mean-based pseudo-label selector derived from a View-Invariant Infomax objective (building on Hjelm et al., 2019). Unlike prior adversarial self-play methods for LLMs, Self-Harmony introduces a cooperative self-play structure where a single model acts as both Solver and Reframer, generating a semantically equivalent but stylistically distinct second view. The key conceptual departure is the theoretical argument that the harmonic mean is not an ad hoc heuristic but the principled second-order approximation of an information-theoretic view-invariance criterion, connecting the method to Invariant Risk Minimization and Group-Invariant Learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-identified failure mode of TTRL—majority voting amplifying systematic reasoning errors—and provides both a theoretical fix and a practical algorithm with zero training failures across 30 model-benchmark configurations, a stability property that prior label-free methods (Intuitor, Rent) do not achieve. The finding that even a 0.6B Reframer suffices for effective paraphrasing (Table 5) suggests the method is robust to paraphrase quality, lowering the practical barrier to adoption. Open questions include whether the dual-view overhead (2× rollouts) remains justified at larger model scales where single-view majority voting is already highly reliable, and whether the approach transfers to non-mathematical or open-ended reasoning tasks where answer extraction is less well-defined.
