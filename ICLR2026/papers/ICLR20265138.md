## Problem

Vision-Language-Action agents executing long-horizon embodied tasks suffer from cascading failures: once an intermediate step is mis-specified, local errors propagate through subsequent steps and accumulate into task failure. Existing approaches either apply post-hoc correction after errors have already compounded, or rely on fixed task decompositions that cannot adapt when the initial plan is flawed, and they typically operate at only a single level of abstraction, leaving propagation at other levels unchecked.

## Input / Output

The system consumes RGB environmental observations, a natural-language task prompt, and historical state-action trajectories, with GPT-4o-mini providing task decompositions and subgoal completion markers at inference. Text and vision inputs are encoded by frozen Nomic encoders (nomic-embed-text-v1.5 and nomic-embed-vision-v1.5) into fixed-dimensional embeddings. Output is a hierarchical decision: at the action level, Top-K candidate actions are scored against the current subgoal with threshold-based acceptance; at the subgoal level, a sliding-window representation triggers transitions between subgoals; at the trajectory level, each candidate is re-evaluated via Sinkhorn prompt-trajectory consistency before final selection.

## Method

ReCAPA introduces Hierarchical Predictive Correction (HPCC), which organizes trajectory representations into three levels (action, subgoal, trajectory) where each lower level predicts the next higher level's embedding via a Transformer-based predictor, and the prediction discrepancy is regularized with an InfoNCE contrastive loss whose negatives are LLM-generated plausible-but-misaligned trajectory segments. Two complementary alignment modules enforce prompt-trajectory consistency: a Sinkhorn-based module computes an entropy-regularized optimal transport divergence between the trajectory distribution and the prompt distribution for global alignment, while a Score-field module (an MLP denoising score network) provides step-wise corrective gradients that push deviant state representations toward the prompt-defined high-density region. During training, the total loss jointly optimizes hierarchical encoders, predictors, and alignment modules end-to-end, with higher-level prediction targets detached to prevent gradient leakage. At inference, the three-tier correction mechanism resamples actions, adjusts subtask boundaries, and re-scores candidates against the full trajectory, enabling early cross-level corrections that prevent small deviations from escalating into irreversible drift.

## Results

On VisualAgentBench, ReCAPA achieves an average score of 58.65, surpassing GPT-4o mini (54.15), Gemini 2.5 Flash (53.00), and Claude-4-Sonnet (50.25) by +4.5, +5.65, and +8.4 points respectively. On AI2-THOR, ReCAPA attains the highest Success Rate (0.75), Transport Rate (0.93), and Balance (0.93), outperforming LLaMAR (SR 0.68) and GPT-4V (SR 0.66). On MineDojo, ReCAPA leads in 8 out of 10 long-horizon tasks, and achieves the lowest Error Propagation Rate (EPR₁₀ = 0.082 on OmniGibson) compared to GPT-4o-mini (~0.3) and Claude-4-Sonnet (>0.453), indicating substantially faster error recovery.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReCAPA outperforms all proprietary LMM baselines on VisualAgentBench, beating GPT-4o mini (54.15) by +4.5 and Gemini 2.5 Flash (53.00) by +5.65 in AVG score, with the largest gap over Claude-4-Sonnet (+8.4). On AI2-THOR, ReCAPA achieves SR 0.75 vs. LLaMAR's 0.68 and GPT-4V's 0.66, though it trails GPT-4V slightly on Coverage (0.95 vs. 0.97) due to its more conservative exploration strategy. On MineDojo, ReCAPA leads in 8 of 10 tasks, surpassing RL-GPT (the strongest prior method) on multi-step synthesis tasks (e.g., 0.95 vs. 0.85 on one task). On AlfWorld, ReCAPA (96% avg SR) trails WALL-E 2.0 (98%) by 2 points but exceeds RAFA (95%) and AdaPlanner (91%), with the gap attributed to WALL-E's dense-reward RL fine-tuning. On EmbodiedAgentInterface (Behavior), ReCAPA (72.2 Perf.) trails o1-preview (74.9) by 2.7 points on overall performance but leads on Goal F1 (84.8 vs. 81.6), indicating stronger goal-level reasoning despite slightly lower action-sequence accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReCAPA extends the line of hierarchical embodied planning (HIRO, LLaMAR, CityNavAgent) by replacing fixed, post-hoc correction with a *predictive* mechanism: lower-level representations (actions, subgoals) forecast higher-level embeddings, and mismatches trigger top-down corrective signals. It repurposes two established tools—Sinkhorn-based optimal transport (Cuturi, 2013) for global prompt-trajectory distributional alignment and score-based denoising (Song et al., 2020) for step-level corrective gradients—into a unified cross-level alignment objective, departing from prior integration attempts (TrajPrompt, HiP) that align only substeps without cross-level feedback. The introduction of EPR and PAC as diagnostic metrics for error propagation and recovery is a novel evaluation contribution not present in prior embodied-agent benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EPR and PAC metrics fill a genuine gap: standard success-rate metrics conflate error prevention with error recovery, masking whether an agent's failures stem from early error amplification or poor recovery. ReCAPA's limitations—discrete hierarchical scoring without continuous intermediate feedback, and deterministic next-layer mappings that cannot represent alternative continuations under uncertainty—point toward open questions about how to inject richer, uncertainty-aware correction into long-horizon embodied agents. The observed trade-off between exploration-driven coverage and consistency-driven stability suggests that future systems may need adaptive mechanisms that shift between these regimes depending on task phase.
