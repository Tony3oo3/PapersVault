## Problem

Standard RLHF regularizes the learned policy against a reference model using KL divergence or its f-divergence variants, which compare token probabilities only at identical vocabulary indices. This ignores semantic relationships between tokens (e.g., 'cat' vs. 'kitten' vs. 'table'), making the regularization blind to whether probability mass shifts toward semantically related or unrelated tokens. The paper asks whether a distance metric that incorporates the geometry of token space can provide a more meaningful and effective policy regularizer for alignment.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt x and autoregressively generates a response y, token by token. At each decoding step n, the regularization penalty is computed between the learned policy π_θ(·|x, y_{1:n-1}) and the reference policy π_ref(·|x, y_{1:n-1}) over the full vocabulary, using a pre-computed cost matrix C built from Euclidean distances in the SFT model's frozen token embedding space. The penalty is applied as a token-wise adjustment to the reward signal, so the output structure is identical to standard RLHF: a single generated text response per prompt.

## Method

The paper replaces the KL divergence term in the RLHF objective with the entropy-regularized Wasserstein (Sinkhorn) distance between the learned and reference token distributions at each decoding step. The key theoretical contribution is showing that, via the dual formulation of the entropic optimal transport problem, the Sinkhorn distance decomposes into a token-wise penalty φ*_{y_n}(x, y_{1:n-1}) plus a policy-independent constant, making the objective directly compatible with PPO and other standard RL algorithms. The optimal dual variables φ* are computed by the Sinkhorn-Knopp matrix-scaling algorithm, with two practical truncation strategies—nearest-k₁ sparsification of the cost matrix and top-k₂ truncation of the token distributions—that reduce complexity from O(d²) to O(k₂²) and add only ~2.5% overhead per training step. The cost function is user-defined (Euclidean or cosine distance in embedding space), enabling flexible encoding of token-level semantic similarity.

## Results

On TL;DR summarization with Gemma-2B, Wasserstein Policy Regularization (WPR) achieves a GPT-4 win rate of 0.924±0.019 vs. SFT and 0.608±0.029 vs. RKL, outperforming all f-divergence baselines (best f-divergence: χ² at 0.904/0.540). On HH-RLHF dialogue generation, WPR reaches 0.852±0.027 vs. SFT and 0.616±0.039 vs. RKL, again the best among all methods. On MT-Bench, WPR scores 4.272, the highest among all regularization variants (RKL: 4.000). On the APPS code generation benchmark with CodeGemma-7B, WPR improves pass@1 from 8.32 (RKL) to 8.79 overall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard RKL-regularized PPO baseline (Ouyang et al., 2022), WPR consistently wins on both primary tasks: 0.608 vs. 0.540 (χ², the best f-divergence) on TL;DR vs. RKL, and 0.616 vs. 0.564 (FKL, the best f-divergence) on HH-RLHF vs. RKL. Against the f-divergence framework of Wang et al. (2024), WPR outperforms all five tested f-divergences (FKL, JS, α, TV, χ²) on both datasets, with the largest margins over FKL and TV on TL;DR (0.608 vs. 0.040 and 0.052 respectively), where those methods suffer from exploding probability ratios and unstable training. The advantage generalizes across model scales: on Gemma-7B TL;DR, WPR achieves 0.712 vs. RKL-7B (vs. 0.668 for RKL-2B vs. RKL-7B), and on Qwen-1.5-1.8B-Chat HH-RLHF, WPR reaches 0.560 vs. RKL. On MT-Bench, WPR (4.272) edges out the best f-divergence α (4.256) and substantially outperforms RKL (4.000). On APPS code generation, WPR improves pass@1 over RKL at every difficulty level (e.g., 24.78 vs. 24.00 Introductory, 5.75 vs. 5.28 Interview, 1.92 vs. 1.76 Competition).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLHF regularization framework by replacing the standard KL (or f-divergence) penalty with an entropy-regularized Wasserstein (Sinkhorn) distance over the token space. It builds directly on the f-divergence generalization line (f-DPO, χPO) that identified limitations of KL but remained confined to index-wise probability comparisons, and on the broader optimal-transport-in-ML literature (Wasserstein GANs, Sinkhorn-based trust regions in RL) that had not previously been applied to LLM policy regularization. The key theoretical departure is showing that the dual variables of the entropic OT problem yield a token-wise penalty φ*_{y_n} that slots directly into PPO's reward, making the Wasserstein regularizer as tractable as the log-ratio penalty of KL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WPR offers a principled, drop-in replacement for KL regularization in RLHF that encodes semantic structure in the token space, with only ~2.5% additional training overhead and consistent gains across summarization, dialogue, and code generation tasks on multiple model families. The dual-variable formulation is elegant and general: any user-defined cost matrix (embedding distance, task-specific similarity) can be plugged in without modifying the RL algorithm. Open questions include the persistent dependence on the β hyperparameter, the need for same-tokenizer embeddings (limiting cross-architecture cost matrices), and whether the approach extends naturally to implicit alignment methods like DPO where the policy is never explicitly sampled.
