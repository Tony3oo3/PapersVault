## Problem

Most 3D Gaussian Splatting pipelines are deterministic and do not explicitly encode structural sparsity in per-splat covariance matrices, leaving noise-dominated axis-wise variances and spurious cross-axis couplings insufficiently regularized. This limits both the trustworthiness of rendered views under sparse or occluded conditions and the ability to produce calibrated, pixel-wise uncertainty estimates that flag unreliable regions for downstream tasks such as active view selection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260032/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed RGB training images, their camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and a sparse SfM point cloud for Gaussian initialization, following the standard 3DGS protocol. At inference it produces a rendered novel-view RGB image, a per-pixel posterior uncertainty map (mean and variance over M=10 Monte Carlo covariance samples), and a depth map. The uncertainty is structured at the level of individual Gaussian scale parameters rather than at the network-weight or ray level, so it directly reflects geometric anisotropy confidence in each splat's elliptical footprint.

## Method

Horseshoe Splatting places a global-local Horseshoe prior on the diagonal scale entries s_ij of each splat's covariance Σ_i = R_i diag(s_i1,s_i2,s_i3) R_i^T, using a half-Cauchy hierarchy (λ_ij local, θ_j global per axis) expressed via inverse-Gamma augmentation. A factorized mean-field variational posterior mirrors this IG-IG structure, and training minimizes a combined loss of expected reconstruction NLL (sampled from the posterior) plus an analytic KL divergence between the variational and prior IG factors. At test time, ancestral sampling through the learned hierarchy yields M covariance draws, each rendered by the standard differentiable 3DGS rasterizer, from which per-pixel predictive mean and variance are computed. The paper further proves near-minimax posterior contraction rates for the sparse scale vector under a nonlinear observation model and transfers them to image space via a local Lipschitz bound on the renderer, certifying that both estimation error and predictive uncertainty diminish with data.

## Results

On the LF dataset, Horseshoe Splatting achieves a PSNR of 30.05 dB, SSIM of 0.947, and LPIPS of 0.064 for novel view synthesis, while delivering the best uncertainty metrics with AUSE of 0.25 and NLL of −0.74. On the more challenging LLFF dataset, it leads with PSNR 25.86, SSIM 0.864, LPIPS 0.110, AUSE 0.31, and NLL 0.14. For depth uncertainty on LF, the method achieves a state-of-the-art average AUSE of 0.18 (0.10 on the basket scene, a 23% improvement over the next best). Inference runs at 0.03 s per view, approximately 9× faster than Ensemble GS (0.27 s) and 4× faster than Variational 3DGS (0.06 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Horseshoe Splatting outperforms all five baselines on rendering quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across both LF and LLFF datasets. Against the closest 3DGS-based competitor, Variational 3DGS (Li & Cheung, 2024), it gains +2.66 dB PSNR on LF (30.05 vs. 27.39) and +1.89 dB on LLFF (25.86 vs. 23.97), while also improving AUSE (0.25 vs. 0.26 on LF) and NLL (−0.74 vs. −0.30 on LF). FisherRF (Jiang et al., 2024) achieves competitive PSNR (29.13 on LF) but its Hessian-based uncertainty is poorly calibrated, yielding NLL of 7.02 on LF and 7.05 on LLFF versus −0.74 and 0.14 for Horseshoe Splatting. The NeRF-based baselines CF-NeRF and S-NeRF are substantially outperformed on both fidelity and uncertainty (e.g., PSNR 30.05 vs. 24.32 for CF-NeRF on LF). Ensemble GS (×10) is the strongest uncertainty baseline on LLFF AUSE (0.30 vs. 0.31, a near-tie) but is 9× slower at inference (0.27 s vs. 0.03 s per view) and trails on all rendering metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) by importing the global-local Horseshoe shrinkage prior from Bayesian high-dimensional statistics (Carvalho et al., 2009; Piironen and Vehtari, 2017) and placing it directly on per-splat covariance scales, a structural level that prior uncertainty-aware 3DGS methods (FisherRF, Variational 3DGS) leave unregularized. It departs from existing Bayesian NeRF/3DGS work (S-NeRF, CF-NeRF, Bayes' Ray) by targeting the anisotropic covariance geometry itself rather than radiance values or ray coordinates, and from post-hoc pruning approaches by providing a principled hierarchical prior with a tractable factorized variational posterior that mirrors the Horseshoe's inverse-Gamma augmentation. The theoretical contribution—posterior contraction rates for sparse scales under a nonlinear observation model, transferred to image space via a local Lipschitz renderer—follows the standard Ghosal–van der Vaart testing-plus-prior-mass program but is a novel application to differentiable rasterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a statistically principled shrinkage prior can simultaneously induce structural sparsity in Gaussian footprints and produce well-calibrated pixel-wise uncertainty at near-deterministic 3DGS speed (33 FPS vs. 8 FPS for FisherRF), making uncertainty-aware rendering practical for active view selection and OOD detection without ensemble overhead. The dramatic NLL gap (−0.74 vs. 9.15 for a Gaussian prior on LF) suggests that the heavy-tailed, spike-at-zero structure of the Horseshoe is specifically critical for calibration, not just any sparsity-inducing prior. Open questions include whether the diagonal-scale-only parameterization captures meaningful off-diagonal covariance structure, whether the mean-field variational approximation remains tight as splat count grows, and whether the approach extends to dynamic or unbounded scenes where the local Lipschitz renderer assumption may break down.
