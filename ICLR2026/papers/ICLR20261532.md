## Problem

Current text-to-image benchmarks rely on short, information-sparse prompts that only verify object presence, attributes, or simple binary relations, leaving complex spatial intelligence—perception of arrangement, reasoning about relative size/occlusion, and causal interaction—largely unmeasured. This paper identifies that even SOTA T2I models fail on fine-grained spatial constraints (misplacing objects, ignoring occlusion, rendering incorrect causal interactions) but existing evaluation protocols are too coarse to expose these failures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261532/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes 1,230 long, information-dense prompts (46–97 words, ~66 average) spanning 25 real-world scenes, each integrating all 10 spatial sub-domains, and pairs them with 10 multiple-choice questions (5 options including a 'None' refusal). The MLLM evaluator (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) receives only the generated image and questions—never the original prompt—to prevent answer leakage, and uses a 5-round majority-voting scheme. Beyond evaluation, the SpatialT2I dataset (15,400 text-image pairs from 14 models, with MLLM-rewritten prompts for alignment) serves as SFT input, producing fine-tuned T2I models with measurable spatial gains.

## Method

SpatialGenEval decomposes spatial intelligence into a 4-domain, 10-sub-domain hierarchy (Foundation → Perception → Reasoning → Interaction) and constructs prompts via Gemini 2.5 Pro with human-in-the-loop refinement to ensure logical consistency, vocabulary simplicity, and full sub-domain coverage. Evaluation is omni-dimensional: each prompt yields 10 targeted multiple-choice questions, and an MLLM judge selects the best option from five choices (A–D plus 'E: None') under a no-external-knowledge instruction, with correctness requiring ≥4/5 voting rounds. For the data-centric contribution, SpatialT2I is built by generating images from 14 top-scoring models, then using Gemini 2.5 Pro to rewrite prompts to match actual image content while preserving spatial information density. Fine-tuning SD-XL, UniWorld-V1, and OmniGen2 on this dataset yields consistent +4.2%, +5.7%, and +4.4% overall gains, with the largest improvements in spatial reasoning sub-domains (Comparison, Occlusion), confirming that information-dense spatial data is an effective lever for closing the reasoning gap.

## Results

On SpatialGenEval (1,230 information-dense prompts, 10 spatial sub-domains, evaluated via Qwen2.5-VL-72B multi-choice VQA), the best overall score is 62.7% (Seed Dream 4.0), with the top open-source model Qwen-Image reaching 60.6%. A stark gap exists between Spatial Foundation (Object ~60%, Attribute ~77% for top models) and Spatial Reasoning (Comparison ~29%, Occlusion ~31%), which remains the primary bottleneck across all 23 models. Fine-tuning on the SpatialT2I dataset yields consistent gains: +4.2% on SD-XL (41.2→45.4), +5.7% on UniWorld-V1 (54.2→59.9), and +4.4% on OmniGen2 (56.4→60.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among 23 models spanning diffusion, autoregressive, unified, and closed-source architectures, Seed Dream 4.0 (62.7%) leads overall, followed by Nano Banana (61.7%), Qwen-Image (60.6%), and GPT-Image-1 (60.5%). The best open-source model, Qwen-Image (20B, LLM encoder), trails the top closed-source model by only 2.1%, indicating a narrowing gap. Older CLIP-encoder diffusion models (SD-1.5 at 28.5%, SD-XL at 41.2%) are substantially outperformed by LLM-encoder models (FLUX.1-krea 58.5%, Qwen-Image 60.6%), confirming text-encoder strength as a key determinant. Unified models show parameter efficiency: 7B Bagel (57.0%) approaches 12B FLUX.1-krea (58.5%), while 4B OmniGen2 (56.4%) rivals 14B NextStep-1 (55.0%). Across all models, Spatial Reasoning sub-domains (Comparison ~26–32%, Occlusion ~27–34%) are the universal bottleneck, scoring only 6–12 points above random (20%), whereas Spatial Interaction (Motion ~70–83%, Causal ~72–84%) is relatively well handled. Fine-tuning on SpatialT2I consistently improves all three tested models (SD-XL +4.2%, UniWorld-V1 +5.7%, OmniGen2 +4.4%), with the largest gains in Spatial Reasoning (e.g., UniWorld-V1 Comparison: 26.1→34.6%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from existing T2I benchmarks (GenEval, DPG-Bench, TIIF-Bench, OneIG-Bench) that rely on short, information-sparse prompts and coarse-grained metrics (detection, yes/no, scoring) by introducing a hierarchical 4-domain, 10-sub-domain framework for spatial intelligence drawn from cognitive science literature (Malanchini et al., 2020; Ruan et al., 2025). It repurposes the MLLM-as-judge paradigm (common in VLM evaluation) for T2I assessment via multi-choice VQA, and extends the data-centric fine-tuning approach of SPRIGHT (Chatterjee et al., 2024) from spatial consistency to full spatial reasoning and interaction. The paper does not build on a single prior method but synthesizes the spatial cognition taxonomy, information-dense prompt design, and MLLM evaluation into a unified benchmark and training dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that spatial reasoning (comparison, occlusion) remains near random-guess levels (~20-32%) across all 23 SOTA models, identifying a fundamental architectural bottleneck that no amount of scale or better text encoders has resolved. The finding that LLM-based text encoders are a key determinant of spatial performance, and that prompt rewriting helps explicit relations but not implicit 3D reasoning, provides actionable direction for the field. Open questions include whether the data-centric approach (SpatialT2I) scales beyond 15K pairs, whether unified architectures can close the spatial reasoning gap without massive parameter increases, and whether the 10-sub-domain framework captures the full breadth of spatial intelligence needed for embodied or interactive applications.
