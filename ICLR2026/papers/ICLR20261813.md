## Problem

Existing safety alignment methods for Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) focus on making final responses safe, yet harmful content frequently persists in intermediate chain-of-thought reasoning steps, creating exploitable vulnerabilities for malicious users. Simply applying reinforcement learning (e.g., GRPO) with safety rewards to supervise the reasoning process proves insufficient because low rollout diversity yields weak or uninformative training signals, making effective process-level safety supervision a non-trivial challenge.

## Input / Output

The method takes harmful or adversarial text prompts (e.g., jailbreak attempts) as input and produces a full LRM output consisting of a reasoning trajectory (bounded by <think>/ tokens) followed by a final response, with both components evaluated for safety. During training, preference pairs are constructed so that the chosen and rejected trajectories share an identical prefix up to a safety-critical token index h, diverging only at the point where a compliance cue is replaced by a safety trigger, enabling localized contrastive supervision. The training data is built from the STAR-1 dataset (1,000 harmful prompts plus 915 benign prompts for over-refusal mitigation), with GPT-4o serving as both the compliance-cue detector and the safety evaluator.

## Method

Intervened Preference Optimization (IPO) identifies three empirical patterns in LRM reasoning: (1) safe trajectories are consolidated by a few critical 'safety trigger' steps after which continuation safety approaches 100%; (2) 'compliance cues'—sentences expressing willingness to fulfill a malicious request—strongly correlate (Pearson r=0.85) with the onset of unsafe continuations; and (3) replacing a compliance cue with a safety trigger reliably steers the trajectory toward safe completion. Concretely, IPO detects the first compliance cue in a sampled unsafe trajectory via GPT-4o, substitutes it with a trigger sampled from a curated pool of six safety sentences, and continues generation to produce a corrected safe trajectory. The original and corrected trajectories form a preference pair that shares the same prefix but diverges at the intervention index h, and the model is trained with a partial DPO objective applied only from the divergence point onward, concentrating the learning signal at safety-critical steps. This design overcomes the rollout-diversity bottleneck of GRPO by artificially introducing safe trajectories through corrective intervention, requiring at most 14 generations per prompt versus 40+ for GRPO, while achieving over 30% relative reduction in harmfulness across reasoning and responses.

## Results

On DS-8B, IPO reduces average reasoning harmfulness to 15.3% (vs. 71.5% base, 22.6% STAR, 18.5% GRPO) and average response harmfulness to 6.9% (vs. 56.1% base, 12.5% STAR, 17.8% GRPO) across JailbreakBench, StrongReject, and WildJailbreak. On WildJailbreak specifically, DS-8B reasoning harmfulness drops from 82.4% (base) to 23.4% (IPO), a 71% relative reduction. IPO also preserves and slightly enhances reasoning capabilities: DS-8B average reasoning accuracy rises from 66.7% to 68.5%, DS-7B from 69.0% to 71.5%, and Qwen3-8B from 79.5% to 80.2% across AIME, MATH-500, GPQA-Diamond, and HumanEval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPO outperforms all SFT-based baselines (SafeChain, RealSafe, STAR, SafeKey) on reasoning safety by a wide margin: on DS-8B, IPO achieves 15.3% average reasoning harmfulness vs. 59.4% (SafeChain), 34.2% (RealSafe), 22.6% (STAR), and 30.9% (SafeKey). Against the RL-based GRPO baseline, IPO reduces reasoning harmfulness from 18.5% to 15.3% and response harmfulness from 17.8% to 6.9% on DS-8B, while requiring only ~14 generations per prompt versus ≥40 for GRPO. RealSafe achieves the lowest response harmfulness (2.7%) but at the cost of severe over-refusal (XsTest compliance drops to 47.5% vs. IPO's 80.0%), indicating it sacrifices utility for safety. IPO also surpasses TARS (18.6% vs. 16.7% on StrongReject reasoning) and BackTrack (27.3% vs. 16.7%) in reasoning safety, confirming that localized process-level preference optimization at safety-critical steps is more effective than trajectory-level or token-level interventions. Notably, IPO is the only method that simultaneously achieves the best reasoning safety, competitive response safety, and the highest average reasoning accuracy across all three models, demonstrating a favorable safety-utility trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to the reasoning-safety domain by constructing preference pairs that diverge only at safety-critical steps, rather than comparing full trajectories. It departs from SFT-based safety alignment (SafeChain, RealSafe, STAR, SafeKey), which train on curated safe CoT data but leave harmful content in intermediate reasoning, and from RL-based approaches (GRPO, TARS), which suffer from low rollout diversity that limits training signals for process-level safety. The key conceptual departure is the identification of localized 'safety triggers' and 'compliance cues' as the critical junctures where reasoning safety is determined, enabling targeted intervention rather than global reward shaping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper elevates reasoning-level safety from an afterthought to a primary alignment objective, showing that safe reasoning naturally propagates to safe responses and that a handful of critical steps determine the safety of entire trajectories. Practically, IPO is computationally efficient (~40 min vs. 2+ hours for GRPO) and requires only ~1,400 preference pairs, making it accessible for practitioners aligning open-source LRMs. Open questions include whether the hand-crafted trigger pool generalizes across domains, whether the approach extends to multi-turn and agentic settings where reasoning drives tool use, and whether reliance on GPT-4o for compliance-cue detection creates a circular dependency as models improve.
