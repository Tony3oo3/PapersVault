## Problem

State-of-the-art text-to-video models produce physically implausible motions (e.g., objects falling upward, abrupt velocity changes) because they learn appearance-level motion distributions without understanding underlying physical laws. Moreover, these models lack precise parameter control, making it impossible to generate physically consistent dynamics under user-specified initial conditions. The paper argues this stems from a fundamental architectural gap: no explicit dynamical constraints are injected into the generation process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260438/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt describing the scene and initial physical conditions (position, velocity, angular velocity, object dimensions) as input, which are parsed into a 9-dimensional latent physical state vector Z₀ = [x, y, vₓ, v_y, θ, ω, s, l, a]. The output is a 1–2 second video (velocities typically 0–15 m/s) with physically consistent motion across 12 supported dynamics (uniform motion, parabolic, circular, damped oscillation, deformation, etc.). The physical states are converted to pixel-level optical flow, which is temporally and spatially downsampled to match the latent resolution of the Go-with-the-Flow video generator before sampling.

## Method

NewtonGen is a two-stage framework. In stage 1, a Neural Newtonian Dynamics (NND) module is trained on a small set of physics-clean simulated videos (100 per motion type) using an encoder-only architecture: SAM2 segments dynamic regions, morphological analysis extracts physical states, and a second-order physics-informed linear neural ODE (a_z·z̈ + b_z·ż + c_z·z + d_z) augmented with a residual 3-layer MLP (R⁹→R⁶, initialized to zero) learns the latent dynamics in a unified form across all motion types. Training minimizes L2 loss between predicted and encoder-extracted states over 20,000 epochs (~2 hours on one A100). In stage 2 (training-free), the trained NND predicts future physical states from user-specified Z₀ via the odeint solver, these states are converted to approximate optical flow using scene geometry, and the flow is injected as structured warped noise into Go-with-the-Flow to produce the final video. The key insight is decoupling dynamics reasoning (lightweight, interpretable ODE) from visual synthesis (heavy diffusion model), enabling white-box parameter control without retraining the video generator.

## Results

NewtonGen achieves the highest Physical Invariance Score (PIS) across all 12 evaluated motion types, with scores closely matching the simulated reference (e.g., PIS-v = 0.9830 for uniform motion vs. reference 0.9972; PIS-vx = 0.9803 for parabolic motion vs. reference 0.9988; PIS-ω = 0.9788 for circular motion vs. reference 0.9933). Background Consistency (BC) and Motion Smoothness (MS) are competitive across all methods (typically >0.93), with NewtonGen achieving 0.9694 BC and 0.9962 MS on uniform motion. The ablation study confirms that the residual MLP in NND is critical for nonlinear dynamics (e.g., reducing circular motion error from 0.5388 to 0.0255), and that 100 physics-clean training samples per motion type suffice for accurate dynamics learning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NewtonGen decisively outperforms all five baselines on the primary physical consistency metric (PIS) across nearly every motion type. The closest competitor is Veo3, which achieves PIS-v = 0.9784 on uniform motion (vs. NewtonGen's 0.9830) and PIS-ω = 0.8932 on circular motion (vs. 0.9788), but falls further behind on complex dynamics like parabolic-with-rotation (PIS-ω: 0.9019 vs. 0.9289) and deformation (PIS-Δl: 0.3466 vs. 0.5492). Sora and CogVideoX-5B show substantially lower PIS scores, often below 0.70, indicating frequent violations of physical invariants. The physics-aware baseline PhyT2V, which uses LLM-guided self-refinement, is also consistently outperformed (e.g., PIS-vx for parabolic motion: 0.6370 vs. 0.9803), suggesting that implicit physical reasoning from large models is insufficient compared to explicit dynamics modeling. On secondary metrics (BC, MS), all methods are tightly clustered (0.93–0.99), with NewtonGen competitive but not always the best, confirming that its advantage is specifically in physical trajectory consistency rather than visual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

NewtonGen sits within the 'Generation with Learned Physics Priors' paradigm but departs from prior methods (e.g., PhyT2V, WISA) that rely on large pretrained LLMs/VLMs for implicit physics reasoning. Instead, it introduces a lightweight, trainable Neural ODE (building on Chen et al., 2018) that explicitly learns Newtonian dynamics from a small set of physics-clean videos, and couples this with Go-with-the-Flow (Burgert et al., 2025) for optical-flow-based motion control in video generation. This contrasts with 'Physical Simulation then Generation' approaches (e.g., PhysGen) that require hand-specified simulation parameters, and with 'Generation then Simulation' pipelines that demand manual 3D setup. The key conceptual move is treating dynamics learning as a separate, interpretable, and data-efficient sub-problem decoupled from the visual generation stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that physically consistent and parameter-controllable video generation is achievable with only ~100 clean training videos per motion type, suggesting that explicit dynamics modeling can substitute for the massive data scaling that current video generators rely on. The training-free second stage (injecting predicted optical flow into a frozen video model) makes the approach practical and composable with existing generators. However, the framework is limited to continuous single-object dynamics and cannot handle collisions, rebounds, or multi-object interactions, and real-world video training yields notably lower PIS scores than simulated data, leaving open the question of how to scale this to complex, noisy, multi-body physical scenes.
