## Problem

Standard GNN pooling layers operate only on the final layer's node features, discarding the rich multi-scale information encoded in intermediate activations produced during message passing. This under-utilization is especially harmful in deep GNNs, where over-smoothing causes later-layer representations to become indistinguishable while early layers retain discriminative local structure. The paper asks whether explicitly modeling the full trajectory of node embeddings across layers can yield stronger, more robust graph representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the stacked historical activations from all L GNN layers as an N×L×D tensor (projected to a common hidden dimension via per-layer linear maps), along with sinusoidal positional encodings to mark layer order. It outputs a fixed-size graph-level embedding vector of dimension D, obtained by averaging the node-wise attention outputs, which then feeds a standard MLP prediction head. The same architecture can also produce per-node embeddings for node-classification or link-prediction tasks, and it can be applied as a frozen post-processing head on a pretrained backbone without retraining the encoder.

## Method

HISTOGRAPH is a two-stage attention-based pooling operator that first applies layer-wise attention, where each node's final-layer embedding serves as a query attending over its own sequence of L historical activations (keys and values), with attention scores averaged across nodes and normalized by division by their sum rather than softmax—permitting signed (negative) weights that enable high-pass and low-pass filtering over the layer trajectory. The resulting cross-layer pooled node embeddings H are then refined by a single multi-head self-attention over all N nodes to capture spatial interactions, followed by mean pooling to yield the graph representation. This two-stage decomposition (layers first at O(LD) per node, then nodes at O(N²D)) avoids the prohibitive O(L²N²D) cost of joint node-layer attention, and the signed normalization is shown theoretically to mitigate over-smoothing by retaining early-layer discriminative information. The method supports both end-to-end joint training and lightweight frozen-backbone fine-tuning, where only the HISTOGRAPH head is trained on cached activations.

## Results

On TU Datasets (5-layer GIN backbone), HISTOGRAPH achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on 5 of 7 benchmarks, with the largest gain on PROTEINS (97.8% vs. 81.2% for DKEPool, +16.6%) and strong improvements on IMDB-B (87.2% vs. 80.9%) and IMDB-M (61.9% vs. 56.3%). On OGB molecular property prediction (3-layer GCN backbone), HISTOGRAPH attains the top ROC-AUC on 3 of 4 datasets: MOLBBBP 72.02%, MOLTOX21 77.49%, and TOXCAST 66.35%. For deep GNNs (64 layers), HISTOGRAPH dramatically mitigates over-smoothing, maintaining 77.5% on Cora and 79.3% on Pubmed where standard GCN collapses to 28.7% and 35.3% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HISTOGRAPH outperforms the strongest pooling baseline DKEPool on 5 of 7 TU datasets, with the most dramatic margin on PROTEINS (+16.6%) and substantial gains on IMDB-B (+6.3%) and IMDB-M (+5.6%); it trails DKEPool slightly on PTC (79.1% vs. 79.6%) and RDT-B (93.4% vs. 95.0%). On OGB, HISTOGRAPH beats DKEPool on MOLBBBP (+2.29% ROC-AUC) and GMT on TOXCAST (+0.91%) and MOLTOX21 (+0.19%), while DKEPool leads on MOLHIV (78.65% vs. 77.81%). Against attention-based pooling methods (GMT, SOPool, HAP, PAS), HISTOGRAPH consistently achieves the highest or near-highest accuracy across all TU benchmarks. On deep node classification (64 layers), GCN+HISTOGRAPH surpasses both standard GCN and JKNet on all three citation datasets (Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed), with gains up to +49.8% on Cora. The five graph kernel baselines (GK, RW, WL, DGK, AWE) are all outperformed on TU datasets, confirming that learned historical-activation pooling captures richer structure than fixed kernel similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

HISTOGRAPH extends the line of work on multi-scale GNN readout initiated by Jumping Knowledge (Xu et al., 2018), which concatenates or sums layer representations, by replacing fixed aggregation with a learned two-stage attention mechanism that explicitly models the temporal evolution of node embeddings across layers and their spatial interactions. It departs from residual-connection approaches (GCNII, APPNP) that operate within the message-passing loop by applying historical aggregation only at readout, and from state-space/ARMA graph models (Ceni et al., 2025; Eliasof et al., 2025) that focus on training stability rather than exploiting the computational trajectory as a predictive signal. The signed normalization (division-by-sum rather than softmax) is a distinctive design choice absent from prior attention-based pooling methods like Set2Set, GMT, or SAGPool, enabling the architecture to implement subtractive (high-pass) filtering over the layer trajectory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HISTOGRAPH offers a practical, drop-in pooling layer that consistently improves graph classification, node classification, and link prediction across TU and OGB benchmarks, with the frozen-backbone post-processing mode providing a low-cost way to upgrade existing pretrained GNNs without retraining. The demonstrated robustness to depth (maintaining ~80% accuracy at 64 layers on Cora where GCN collapses to 28.7%) addresses a well-known limitation of deep GNNs. Key open questions include scalability to large graphs (the O(N²D) node-wise attention and O(NLD) activation storage become bottlenecks), whether the gains persist on substantially larger or more heterogeneous benchmarks, and whether the signed normalization's criticality (74.45% vs. 97.8% on PROTEINS) generalizes beyond the evaluated settings.
