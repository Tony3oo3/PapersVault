## Problem

Scene Graph Generation methods across all paradigms (one-stage, two-stage, open-vocabulary) rely on frozen, static semantic embeddings from pre-trained language models that cannot adapt to visual context, fine-grained relational nuance, or role-specific semantics. The paper argues this representational rigidity—not architectural choice—is the fundamental bottleneck, as evidenced by t-SNE visualizations showing collapsed semantic spaces versus naturally separated visual feature spaces, and by consistent performance gaps when models are constrained to fixed embeddings.

## Input / Output

APT operates as a plug-in module on top of existing SGG backbones, consuming standard RGB images and producing scene graph triplets (subject, predicate, object) with bounding boxes in the same format as the host architecture. Internally, it intercepts the frozen semantic embedding lookup for each detected object or predicate class and replaces it with a dynamically modulated representation conditioned on the corresponding visual feature vector. The module adds fewer than 0.5M parameters (<1.5% overhead) and requires no changes to the input pipeline or output head of the base model.

## Method

APT introduces lightweight learnable prompt vectors (P_d for detection, P_r for relations, P_ur for one-stage) that act as conditional adapters: each prompt is concatenated with the frozen semantic embedding and a projected visual feature, then passed through a small MLP fusion network to produce a context-aware representation, with only the prompts, visual projectors, and fusion MLPs being trained. For open-vocabulary generalization, a Compositional Generalization Prompter (CGP) synthesizes novel prompts via three submodules—Relational Context Gating (visual-conditioned softmax weights over basis prompts), Basis Prompt Synthesis (weighted combination of N learnable basis vectors), and Feature Refinement & Fusion (non-linear integration of synthesized prompt, static embedding, and visual feature). The training objective augments the standard classification loss with Frobenius-norm regularizers on prompts, an orthogonality penalty on basis prompts, a KL/entropy term on gating weights, and a residual anchor term pulling adapted features toward the static embedding. The theoretical justification draws on the Information Bottleneck principle: the prompts learn to compress irrelevant semantic dimensions while amplifying those predictive of the target predicate, yielding representations that are simultaneously more sufficient and more minimal than frozen embeddings.

## Results

On Visual Genome, APT consistently improves all 10 base SGG models across PredCls, SGCls, and SGDet. The headline gains include +2.7 mR@100 on PredCls (e.g., HQSG 34.6→37.3), +3.6 F@100 on PredCls (e.g., SGTR 42.8→45.9), and up to +6.0 mR@50 on the Novel split in open-vocabulary settings (e.g., SDSGG 25.2→31.2). These improvements are achieved with fewer than 0.5M additional parameters (<1.5% overhead) and a 7.8%–25% reduction in per-epoch training time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APT is a plug-in module, so every comparison is a base model vs. base model + APT; the method wins on every single baseline across all three datasets (VG, OI-V6, GQA) and all three sub-tasks. Among two-stage methods, DRM+APT achieves the largest PredCls mR@100 gain (+2.7, 19.2→21.9 on VG), while MOTIFS+APT shows the largest SGDet F@100 gain (+2.5, 12.6→15.1). Among one-stage methods, LLM4SGG+APT reaches the highest absolute PredCls F@100 (50.3 on VG), and EGTR+APT posts the best PredCls mR@100 (40.1). In open-vocabulary settings, APT's Compositional Generalization Prompter (CGP) yields the most dramatic Novel-split improvements: SDSGG+APT gains +6.0 mR@50 (25.2→31.2 on VG) and +3.9 mR@50 (18.7→22.1 on GQA), while SGTR+RAHP+APT improves Novel F@50 by +0.8 on VG and +1.3 on GQA. No baseline shows a regression after APT integration, and the efficiency analysis (Table 6) confirms that APT simultaneously reduces training time by 7.8%–25% while adding <0.5M parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the continuous prompt learning paradigm from Lester et al. (2021) to the multimodal, structured-prediction setting of Scene Graph Generation, repurposing learnable prompt vectors as feature modulators rather than language-model prefixes. It deliberately positions itself outside the one-stage vs. two-stage architectural debate, arguing that the shared reliance on frozen semantic embeddings (GloVe, BERT, CLIP-text) is the true bottleneck, and grounds its design in the Information Bottleneck principle. The Compositional Generalization Prompter (CGP) extends this idea to open-vocabulary SGG by synthesizing novel prompts from a finite basis set, building on prior OV-SGG work (SDSGG, OvSGTR, RAHP) that still depends on frozen vision-language backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

APT offers a genuinely plug-and-play mechanism that can be dropped into any existing SGG pipeline with <0.5M parameters, which is practically valuable for the community iterating on diverse architectures. The consistent mR@K gains (especially on tail predicates) and the more substantial open-vocabulary improvements (+6.0 mR@50 on novel splits) suggest that adaptive semantic modulation is an underexplored axis of improvement. However, the Information Bottleneck analysis is largely post-hoc and the efficiency-gain mechanism (faster convergence due to 'more informative' prompts) is asserted rather than rigorously demonstrated; the paper also does not ablate prompt length or basis-set size in depth, leaving open how much of the gain is attributable to the specific prompt architecture versus simply adding a small learnable adapter.
