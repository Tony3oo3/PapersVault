## Problem

Automated agent design frameworks face a fundamental trade-off: task-level architecture search (e.g., ADAS, AFlow) produces a single static workflow that cannot adapt to individual queries, while query-level adaptation (e.g., MaAS, FlowReasoner) regenerates a bespoke architecture per query, incurring repeated 'rediscovery' costs and preventing individual agents from accumulating deep, persistent expertise. No existing framework reconciles these paradigms by creating stateful specialist agents that both accumulate knowledge over time and can be reconfigured for novel tasks entirely without human intervention.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a text query and produces a task answer (e.g., a multiple-choice selection for GPQA, a numerical solution for MATH, or executable code for HumanEval/SciCode). At inference, the query is first encoded via MiniLM into a fixed-length embedding that, together with an attention-weighted 'bag-of-operators' embedding of the current architecture graph, forms the state for the meta-controller's retain/resample decision. If resampling is triggered, the Architect LLM receives the query, a sliding window of the last m=10 execution experiences, the current operator pool, and the prior architecture graph, and outputs a new DAG of agentic operators (each a tuple of LLM backbone, prompt, and tools) that is then executed to produce the final answer.

## Method

ASPEC manages the full lifecycle of specialist agents through an offline two-stage process and an online adaptation loop. In the Discovery stage, an LLM Architect evolves a pool of specialist operators via creation (instantiating base operators like CoT or LLM-Debate with structured identity-directive prompts, using multi-variant synthesis with LLM adjudication) and crossover (combining two parent specialists into a child), with a dynamic pool-size cap of 2k to force consolidation; top-k specialists are then selected via a multi-objective optimization balancing performance and embedding-space diversity. In the Cultivation stage, each selected specialist independently accumulates structured episodic memory (problem patterns, failure modes, general rules) through post-execution reflection on a training corpus, with semantic retrieval injecting relevant memory chunks at inference. The online loop is governed by a lightweight meta-controller trained with REINFORCE, which learns a 'retain-then-escalate' policy: it defaults to reusing the current stateful agent team (leveraging accumulated expertise at minimal cost) and only escalates to the expensive Architect call when the query-architecture mismatch warrants structural change. This hierarchical design—evolutionary discovery, experiential cultivation, and RL-based gating—distinguishes ASPEC from both static task-level search and stateless per-query regeneration.

## Results

ASPEC achieves 62.8% accuracy on GPQA, a 6.5-point improvement over the vanilla Gemini 2.0 Flash baseline (56.3%) and outperforming the strongest automated agent design framework AFlow (61.3%) by 1.5 points. On the broader average across five benchmarks, ASPEC scores 69.6%, leading AFlow (68.4%) and EvoAgent (68.1%). ASPEC also leads on SciCode (26.6% vs. 25.6% for MaAS) and MATH (77.3% vs. 76.5% for AFlow), while trailing slightly on HumanEval (91.4% vs. 91.6% for MaAS) and MMLU (90.0% vs. 90.5% for AFlow).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASPEC outperforms all 13 baselines on GPQA (62.8%), the expert-level scientific QA benchmark, beating the closest competitors EvoAgent (61.5%) and AFlow/DyLAN (61.3%) by 1.3–1.5 points. On MATH, ASPEC leads AFlow (76.5%) by 0.8 points, and on SciCode it surpasses MaAS (25.6%) by 1.0 points. ASPEC trails slightly on HumanEval (91.4% vs. MaAS's 91.6%) and MMLU (90.0% vs. AFlow's 90.5%), but its average score of 69.6% is the highest overall. Among hand-designed single-agent baselines (CoT, Self-Refine, Reflexion, CoT-SC), ASPEC leads on every benchmark, with the largest margin on GPQA (+4.6 to +5.7 points). Among automated agent design frameworks (ADAS, AFlow, MaAS), ASPEC wins on MATH, GPQA, and SciCode while matching or narrowly losing on MMLU and HumanEval, demonstrating that the stateful specialist approach provides consistent gains particularly on expert-level tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

ASPEC builds directly on MaAS's formal framework for agentic operators and multi-agent system representation as directed acyclic graphs, but departs from both major paradigms in automated agent design: task-level architecture search (ADAS, AFlow, AgentSquare) which produces a single static workflow, and query-level adaptation (MaAS, FlowReasoner, MAS-Zero) which regenerates architecture per query without persistent state. The paper integrates mechanisms from self-evolving agents (Reflexion, ExpeL, Agent Workflow Memory) for the cultivation phase and evolutionary search (EvoAgent, PromptBreeder) for the discovery phase, unifying them under a two-stage lifecycle with a hierarchical RL meta-controller that governs when to retain versus resample. The key conceptual departure is treating specialist agents as persistent, cultivable entities whose expertise accumulates across queries, rather than as transient components regenerated or resampled for each task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'retain-then-escalate' policy offers a practical middle ground for deploying multi-agent systems at scale, achieving the best accuracy on GPQA (62.8%) at the lowest inference cost (0.88 USD) among all compared methods, with training cost an order of magnitude below AFlow. The finding that domain-specific specialists transfer effectively across benchmarks (MATH-trained specialists matching full-system performance on HumanEval) suggests that cultivated reasoning strategies are more general than their training domain implies, though this is supported by a single ablation configuration. Open questions include whether the lightweight meta-controller's state representation can scale to more complex architectural spaces, how the co-evolutionary dynamics between controller policy, architect choices, and memory accumulation affect long-term stability, and whether memory cultivation amplifies training biases in ways that compound over time.
