## Problem

Reinforcement learning post-training of LLMs substantially improves task quality but sharply reduces output diversity, causing models to converge on narrow canonical responses. Existing diversity-promoting methods are limited to inference-time sampling tricks or lexical-level measures (token entropy, word counts) that fail to capture meaningful semantic diversity. The paper addresses the gap of a principled training-time objective that jointly optimizes for semantic diversity and response quality.

## Input / Output

During training, the method takes a text prompt and samples k responses (typically k=4) from the current policy. Each response is mapped into a fixed-dimensional semantic space via a pretrained sentence encoder (e.g., all-MiniLM-L6-v2, 384-dim), and a kernel similarity matrix is constructed from these embeddings. At inference, the trained model produces diverse, high-quality text responses for a given prompt, evaluated across instruction-following, summarization, story generation, and mathematical reasoning tasks.

## Method

DQO (Diversity Quality Optimization) defines the diversity of a group of k responses as the determinant of their kernel-based similarity matrix in embedding space, which geometrically equals the squared volume of the parallelepiped spanned by the response embeddings. The training objective augments the standard RL reward with α·log det(L_φ(y_{1:k})), encouraging responses to span the embedding space broadly while maintaining high reward. Two practical stabilizations are introduced: replacing det(L) with det(L + I_k) to bound the diversity term, and a leave-one-out gradient estimator that subtracts the log-determinant of the (k−1)-response submatrix to reduce variance. The optimal policy admits a closed-form interpretation as a DPP over reward-augmented embeddings, where the reward scales the norm of each embedding vector, providing a clear geometric trade-off between quality (vector length) and diversity (angular separation). DQO is model-agnostic and layers directly on top of existing RL algorithms such as PPO and GRPO, with negligible additional computational cost (O(k³) determinant computation per prompt).

## Results

On the Dolly instruction-following task, DQO achieves the best pass@10 (8.74) and pass@1 (5.92) while also improving all four diversity metrics over the PPO baseline (e.g., self-BLEU 0.46 vs. 0.41, self-ROUGE 0.54 vs. 0.49). On GSM8K, DQO maintains near-baseline quality (pass@1 = 76.3 vs. GRPO's 76.8) while improving diversity across all metrics (e.g., self-ROUGE 0.31 vs. 0.21, distinct-4 0.42 vs. 0.32). DQO is the only method that simultaneously achieves high quality and high diversity on both tasks, whereas GRPO-likelihood sacrifices quality on GSM8K (pass@1 drops to 50.9) and GRPO-entropy sacrifices quality on Dolly (pass@1 drops to 4.71).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DQO outperforms the PPO/GRPO reward-only baselines on all diversity metrics while matching or exceeding their quality: on Dolly, pass@1 improves from 5.65 to 5.92 and pass@10 from 8.39 to 8.74; on GSM8K, pass@1 is nearly identical (76.3 vs. 76.8) while pass@10 improves from 87.9 to 91.2. Against GRPO-likelihood (He et al., 2025), DQO achieves comparable diversity on Dolly but far superior quality on GSM8K (pass@1 76.3 vs. 50.9), where GRPO-likelihood's diversity objective severely degrades reasoning accuracy. Against GRPO-entropy (Yao et al., 2025), DQO achieves higher diversity on GSM8K (self-ROUGE 0.31 vs. 0.25, distinct-4 0.42 vs. 0.38) while maintaining comparable quality (pass@1 76.3 vs. 77.0); on Dolly, DQO trades slightly lower lexical diversity (distinct-1 0.28 vs. 0.36) for substantially better quality (pass@1 5.92 vs. 4.71). Overall, DQO is the only method that avoids the quality-diversity trade-off, achieving strong scores on both axes across both task types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPP-based diversity framework from population-based RL (Parker-Holder et al., 2020) to LLM post-training, repurposing the determinant of a kernel similarity matrix as a semantic diversity objective within standard RL algorithms (GRPO, PPO). It departs from prior diversity-promoting methods that operate at the lexical level (token entropy in Yao et al., 2025; word-count-based selection in Lanchantin et al., 2025) or at inference time (temperature, top-k sampling) by defining diversity over embedded response groups during training. The closest prior work, Chung et al. (2025), uses pairwise embedding distances in a DPO variant, but DQO's determinant-based group metric avoids the degenerate clustering solutions that pairwise distances are vulnerable to, and the connection to D-optimal design provides a principled interpretation of the quality-diversity trade-off as volume maximization in a reward-augmented embedding space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DQO offers a practical, modular solution to the well-documented diversity collapse in RL post-training that can be layered on top of existing algorithms without architectural changes, and the negligible computational overhead (Table 10) makes it broadly deployable. However, two limitations constrain its impact: the method requires a reward model rather than outcome rewards (due to reward hacking where the model appends irrelevant content to inflate diversity), and its performance depends on the quality of the external embedding model. Open questions include whether adaptive or task-specific diversity metrics could replace the fixed embedding-based formulation, and whether the reward-hacking vulnerability can be resolved for outcome-reward settings that dominate reasoning benchmarks.
