## Problem

Existing GRPO-based reinforcement learning methods for subject-driven generation apply uniform optimization across all denoising timesteps, ignoring that textual and visual conditioning signals exert different influence at different stages of generation. This temporal blindness and entangled feedback prevent targeted credit assignment, forcing a problematic trade-off between subject fidelity and semantic alignment that is especially severe in multi-subject scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260011/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (tokenized via CLIP, 77-token context) and one or more reference images (resized to 512×512 with center crop, normalized to [-1,1]) as conditioning signals, combined with a noised latent initialized from Gaussian noise. It produces generated images (via UNO for single/multi-subject R2I) or videos (via VACE-1.3B for R2V) through SDE-based iterative denoising. For video evaluation, DINOv3 processes sampled frames (initial, middle, final) for efficient identity scoring, and temporal consistency is measured via consecutive CLIP embedding similarities across frames.

## Method

Consis-GCPO reformulates multi-condition guided generation as a discrete-time structural causal model where each denoising step's output is causally determined by the current latent, text prompt, reference image, and noise. It introduces step-wise causal interventions (do-operations) that selectively ablate either the prompt or reference at a single timestep while keeping all other steps standard, generating three trajectory types (main, prompt-intervention, reference-intervention) per initial noise. Instantaneous causal effects are quantified by measuring reward degradation from each ablation, then converted into temporally-weighted advantages via softmax normalization over timesteps. These weighted advantages feed into a PPO-style loss that upweights gradients precisely at timesteps where each modality demonstrates high causal influence, revealing a 'Coarse-to-Fine' pattern where text dominates early (structural) steps and reference dominates late (identity-detail) steps. Joint optimization of both modalities in a single backward pass avoids the gradient oscillation and catastrophic forgetting seen in alternating or sequential strategies.

## Results

On DreamBench (image), Consis-GCPO achieves CLIP-T 0.325, CLIP-I 0.848, and DINO 0.781 for single-subject generation, and CLIP-T 0.331, CLIP-I 0.772, DINO 0.572 for multi-subject generation, outperforming all baselines with statistical significance (p<0.05). On Dream-VBench (video), it reaches CLIP-T 0.305, CLIP-I 0.790, DINO-I 0.746, and temporal Consistency 0.984 for single-subject, and CLIP-T 0.300, CLIP-I 0.674, DINO-I 0.608, Consistency 0.981 for multi-subject. The method also converges ~11.5× faster than Flow-GRPO (≈1,300 vs. ≈15,000 steps) and achieves a 1.4× wall-clock speedup in reaching target evaluation reward.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Consis-GCPO outperforms all ten baselines on every reported metric in both image and video generation. In single-subject image generation, it surpasses the strongest GRPO baseline (UNO+Dance-GRPO: CLIP-I 0.841, DINO 0.772) by +0.007 CLIP-I and +0.009 DINO, while also leading in text alignment (CLIP-T 0.325 vs. 0.314 for Flow-GRPO). In the more challenging multi-subject setting, the gap widens: Consis-GCPO gains +0.022 CLIP-I and +0.011 DINO over the best baseline (UNO+Dance-GRPO), confirming that causal temporal re-weighting is especially beneficial when multiple references compete for attention. For video generation on Dream-VBench, Consis-GCPO beats VACE+DanceGRPO (the strongest video baseline) by +0.018 CLIP-T (0.305 vs. 0.287), +0.035 CLIP-I (0.790 vs. 0.755), +0.014 DINO-I (0.746 vs. 0.732), and +0.003 temporal Consistency (0.984 vs. 0.981). Non-RL baselines like VideoBooth, MAGREF, and Phantom trail significantly, with VideoBooth scoring only 0.267 CLIP-T and 0.523 CLIP-I in single-subject video, highlighting the advantage of RL-based optimization over purely architectural approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Flow-GRPO's formulation of denoising as a sequential decision problem optimized via PPO-style policy gradients, and on DanceGRPO's extension of GRPO to visual generation. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a discrete-time structural causal model (SCM) with step-wise do-interventions to decompose the instantaneous causal contribution of text versus visual reference at each denoising timestep, replacing the uniform advantage signal in standard GRPO with temporally-weighted, modality-specific advantages. This positions the work at the intersection of causal inference and RL-based generative optimization, a combination not previously explored in the subject-driven generation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-motivated practical failure mode in personalized generation—the trade-off between subject fidelity and text alignment—by providing a principled mechanism for temporal credit assignment that is both interpretable (the learned ω_P(t) and ω_Ir(t) curves reveal when each modality matters) and efficient (11.5× fewer training steps than Flow-GRPO). The 'coarse-to-fine' finding that text dominates early denoising while visual references anchor late-stage details, while somewhat intuitive, is now empirically quantified and leveraged for optimization. Open questions include whether the causal framework generalizes beyond two conditioning modalities, whether the reward model choices (ImageReward, DINOv3) are the binding constraint on further gains, and how the method behaves under extreme semantic conflicts between prompt and reference, where the authors acknowledge failure.
