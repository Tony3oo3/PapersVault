## Problem

Personalizing LLMs to individual user preferences remains challenging: tune-free methods (RAG, PAG) lack deep personalization, while parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) requires millions of parameters per user, creating prohibitive communication and storage costs in edge-cloud deployments. Moreover, the mechanisms by which personalized information is encoded in LLM hidden representations have remained largely unexplored, leaving no principled basis for lightweight personalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262553/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PerFit takes a standard text query from a given user and produces either a class label (for classification tasks like news categorization, movie tagging, product rating) or a generated text response (for headline generation, scholarly title generation, tweet paraphrasing). The personalization signal is implicit, derived from the user's interaction history rather than explicit instructions. Interventions are applied to the residual stream hidden state at specific token positions (prefix and/or suffix tokens) in selected middle layers (typically layers 14–16) of a decoder-only transformer, with the base model (Llama2-7B) remaining frozen throughout.

## Method

PerFit is a two-stage fine-tuning method that directly intervenes in the hidden representation space rather than modifying model parameters. The key empirical finding is that user-specific information (captured as δ-vectors via difference-in-means of personalized vs. original inputs) lies in a low-rank orthogonal subspace and exhibits a collective shift shared across all users plus a personalized shift unique to each user. The intervention function φ_ΔΘ(x) = x + R^T(Wx + b − Rx) uses an orthogonal projection matrix R (r×d, r≪d) to map into and out of this low-rank subspace, learning the intervention vector end-to-end. Stage 1 trains a shared collective shift on all users' data, and Stage 2 fine-tunes a per-user personalized shift on top of the Stage-1 intervened representation. Theoretically, the paper proves that LoRA-based methods cannot in general produce input-independent additive shifts decomposable into collective and personalized components, whereas PerFit's low-rank additive structure naturally supports this decomposition, yielding a 92.3% average parameter reduction over LoRA-based baselines while matching or exceeding their performance.

## Results

On the LaMP benchmark, PerFit achieves state-of-the-art or competitive performance across all six tasks while reducing trainable parameters by 81.25%–98.44% compared to OPPU. On classification, PerFit attains 81.8% accuracy on LaMP-2N (vs. 81.0% for OPPU) and 63.0% accuracy / 51.8% F1 on LaMP-2M (vs. 60.0% / 49.3% for OPPU). On generation, PerFit reaches 52.1% ROUGE-1 on LaMP-5 (vs. 51.9% for OPPU) and 20.7% ROUGE-1 on LaMP-4 (vs. 19.1% for OPPU), while also cutting training time by 17.0%–35.8% relative to fine-tuning baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PerFit outperforms the strongest tuned baseline OPPU on LaMP-2N (81.8% vs. 81.0% Acc), LaMP-2M (63.0% vs. 60.0% Acc), LaMP-4 (20.7% vs. 19.1% R-1), and LaMP-5 (52.1% vs. 51.9% R-1), while tying on LaMP-3 (0.179 MAE) and narrowly trailing on LaMP-7 (52.5% vs. 53.9% R-1). Against LoRA-C and LoRA-P, PerFit achieves substantially higher scores on nearly all generation tasks (e.g., 52.1% vs. 47.6%/48.9% R-1 on LaMP-5) and classification tasks (e.g., 63.0% vs. 47.8%/52.8% Acc on LaMP-2M). LoFiT times out (TLE) on LaMP-3 and LaMP-5, and underperforms PerFit on LaMP-7 (27.2% vs. 52.5% R-1). All non-tuned methods (PAG, RAG, StyleVector) are far behind tuned methods, with the best RAG (k=4) reaching only 16.9% R-1 on LaMP-5 versus PerFit's 52.1%. The key differentiator is parameter efficiency: PerFit uses only 0.001%–0.012% of total model parameters per stage, achieving 81–98% fewer parameters than OPPU while matching or exceeding its accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

PerFit sits at the intersection of two research threads: the representation fine-tuning paradigm (ReFT, Wu et al. 2024), which adds learned intervention vectors to hidden states rather than modifying weight matrices, and the two-stage personalized PEFT framework (OPPU, Tan et al. 2024b), which separates collective and user-specific LoRA training. The key departure is that PerFit transplants OPPU's collective-then-personalized structure into the hidden representation space, guided by empirical observations that δ-vectors (difference-in-means personalization vectors) occupy a low-rank orthogonal subspace with a shared collective shift plus user-specific dispersion. A theoretical analysis further distinguishes PerFit from LoRA-based methods by showing that LoRA-induced shifts are input-dependent and piecewise-affine, and thus cannot in general realize the clean additive constant-vector decomposition that PerFit's orthogonal projection enforces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 92.3% average parameter reduction relative to OPPU while maintaining competitive (and sometimes superior) performance across six LaMP tasks is practically significant for edge-cloud personalization, where per-user parameter storage and communication costs are the binding constraint. The empirical discovery that personalized information is concentrated in a very low-rank subspace (as few as 1–4 dimensions explaining 80% of variance for several tasks) offers a new lens for understanding how LLMs encode user-specific style, and the layer-wise analysis showing that early-middle layers are the appropriate intervention point (contrary to knowledge-editing practice) is a useful practical guideline. Open questions include whether the collective shift transfers across tasks and user populations, how the method behaves in cold-start settings with limited user history, and whether the low-rank structure persists for more complex or multi-faceted personalization beyond the LaMP benchmark's implicit style tasks.
