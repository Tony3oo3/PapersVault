## Problem

In arbitrary-order language models, it remains an open question how to sample tokens in parallel while preserving fidelity to the learned joint distribution. Discrete diffusion models can generate multiple tokens simultaneously but rely on a conditional independence assumption that only holds for infinitesimally small timesteps, degrading output quality as parallelism increases. No prior method provided a provably lossless parallel sampling scheme for the O(2^N) arbitrary infilling tasks supported by any-order autoregressive models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partially masked text sequence where prompt tokens can occupy any arbitrary subset of positions, supporting O(2^N) infilling configurations (exponentially more than the O(N) prefix-only patterns of standard autoregressive models). It produces the missing tokens sampled exactly from the joint conditional distribution p(x_{σ(≥m)} | x_{σ(<m)}), with total neural network calls upper-bounded by the number of generated tokens. Internally, the model uses two distinct attention masks: a parallel sampling mask where all masked tokens attend only to prompt tokens (for fast drafting), and a causal-like mask where each token attends only to preceding tokens in the generation order (for joint density estimation in a single O(S) forward pass).

## Method

The core contribution is Any-Subset Speculative Decoding (ASSD), which combines speculative decoding with Any-Subset Autoregressive Models (AS-ARMs) that use recursive binary lattice mask decomposition to reduce learned factorization paths from N! to 2^N. The model serves as its own draft by sampling all masked tokens in parallel under a conditional independence assumption, then corrects these drafts via rejection sampling against the joint density estimate obtained from a causal-like attention mask in a single forward pass. A key theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) is that total function evaluations never exceed the number of tokens generated, unlike vanilla speculative decoding which can theoretically increase runtime. Training uses a teacher-forced joint loss maximizing the joint conditional probability with expectations over both token orderings and prompt lengths, finetuned from pretrained XLNet weights with a masking schedule that progressively increases the fraction of masked tokens from 15% to 99%.

## Results

On ROCStories infilling (Table 3), AS-ARM-PT (110M params, 2T pretrained tokens) achieves the best ROUGE-1/2/L of 14.4/1.7/13.1 on Infill 1/5, outperforming GPT2-S (9.5/0.4/8.7), SEDD-S (11.6/0.8/10.7), MDLM (11.6/1.1/10.7), and DiffuGPT-S (14.0/1.5/13.0). On Infill 3/5, AS-ARM-FT (110M, +12B finetuning tokens) achieves the best ROUGE-1 of 18.0, surpassing all baselines. On HumanEval code infilling, the 110M AS-ARM achieves 38.59 pass@1, nearly matching DiffuLLaMA (6738M) at 40.68, while using ~50× fewer parameters and fewer finetuning tokens. ASSD provides a ~9% wall-clock speedup over sequential decoding with statistically identical output distributions (PPL 107.6 vs 107.9, entropy 7.64 vs 7.65).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT2-S (autoregressive, 127M), AS-ARM-PT wins decisively on Infill 1/5 ROUGE-1 (14.4 vs 9.5) and ROUGE-2 (1.7 vs 0.4), while AS-ARM-FT wins on Infill 3/5 ROUGE-1 (18.0 vs 13.5). Against SEDD-S (discrete diffusion, 170M, 210B tokens), AS-ARM-PT leads on Infill 1/5 ROUGE-1 (14.4 vs 11.6) and ROUGE-2 (1.7 vs 0.8), and AS-ARM-FT leads on Infill 3/5 ROUGE-1 (18.0 vs 16.2). Against MDLM (discrete diffusion, 130M, 262B tokens), AS-ARM-PT wins on Infill 1/5 ROUGE-1 (14.4 vs 11.6) and ROUGE-2 (1.7 vs 1.1). Against DiffuGPT-S (discrete diffusion, 127M, +130B tokens), AS-ARM-PT edges ahead on Infill 1/5 ROUGE-1 (14.4 vs 14.0) and ROUGE-2 (1.7 vs 1.5), while DiffuGPT-S retains a slight edge on Infill 3/5 ROUGE-2 (2.0 vs 1.4) and ROUGE-L (14.2 vs 13.2). On HumanEval code infilling, the 110M AS-ARM (38.59 pass@1) nearly matches the 6738M DiffuLLaMA (40.68 pass@1) despite using ~50× fewer parameters and 15B vs 65B total training tokens. Overall, AS-ARMs are best on 4 out of 6 ROCStories metrics, using the fewest parameters and finetuning resources among all compared models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: any-order/any-subset autoregressive models (XLNet, MAC) and speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., Chen et al.). While speculative decoding was developed exclusively for left-to-right autoregressive models, and AO-ARMs had no fast sampling scheme, the paper shows that AS-ARMs can serve as their own draft model and oracle simultaneously, enabling lossless parallel generation. It explicitly departs from discrete diffusion models, whose parallel sampling relies on a conditional independence assumption that only holds at infinitesimal timesteps, and from σ-GPT, which the authors show violates both the NFE bound and distributional correctness guarantees. The recursive binary lattice mask decomposition from MAC is adopted to reduce the number of learned factorization paths from N! to 2^N, which is critical for the algorithm's correctness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretically guaranteed lossless parallel decoding scheme for any-order models, with a clean NFE upper bound that vanilla speculative decoding lacks. The competitive performance of a 110M AS-ARM against 6.7B diffusion models on code generation (38.59 vs 40.68 pass@1) and SOTA infilling among sub-200M models suggests that the any-subset AR paradigm, largely abandoned in favor of discrete diffusion, may be worth revisiting. However, all experiments are at 110M parameters with a single architecture (XLNet), and the practical speedup is modest (~9–11%), leaving open whether the approach scales to billion-parameter models and whether the speedup remains meaningful at larger sequence lengths and batch sizes where other optimizations (KV caching, FlashAttention) dominate.
