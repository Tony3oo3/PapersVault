## Problem

Score-based data assimilation methods like EnSF fail under sparse observations because the likelihood score vanishes in unobserved state components, and the latent-space fix (Latent-EnSF) still requires expensive full-space forward simulations at every assimilation step, making real-time deployment infeasible. Additionally, existing surrogate models (VAE-based latent dynamics) produce oscillatory latent trajectories that are difficult to evolve stably over time, and standard LDNets cannot handle varying initial conditions or high-frequency dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of sparse noisy observations y_{1:t} sampled at as few as 0.1% of spatial grid points and 0.2% of time steps, with observation locations that can be irregularly/randomly placed rather than restricted to a regular grid. It also takes an initial ensemble of latent state-parameter pairs {s_{-1}, u_0} drawn from a prior distribution. The output is a posterior ensemble in a very low-dimensional latent space (10–52 dimensions), which is decoded via a reconstruction network to recover full system states at arbitrary spatial query points and continuous time points (not just observation times), along with joint estimates of uncertain system parameters (e.g., Reynolds number, initial condition location, forcing amplitude).

## Method

LD-EnSF replaces the VAE + full-space dynamics pipeline of Latent-EnSF with two co-designed components operating entirely in a compact latent space: (1) an improved Latent Dynamics Network (LDNet) that learns smooth latent trajectories via a dynamics MLP and a ResNet-based reconstruction network with trainable Fourier encoding, using a shifted initialization (s_{-1}=0) and a two-stage training strategy (joint training followed by reconstruction fine-tuning) to handle varying initial conditions and high-frequency content; and (2) a single-layer LSTM observation encoder that maps the full observation history y_{1:t} to a latent state-parameter pair (ŝ_t, û_t), exploiting temporal correlations to handle both irregular spatial sampling and sparse temporal coverage. The Ensemble Score Filter is then applied to the augmented latent state κ_t = (s_t, u_t) using the LDNet as a surrogate forward model and the LSTM-encoded observations as the likelihood, eliminating all full-space simulation during assimilation. The smoothness of LDNet latent trajectories (in contrast to oscillatory VAE latents) makes the observation-to-latent alignment significantly easier, improving assimilation accuracy while the 10–52 dimensional latent space yields speedups of 10³–10⁵× over full-space methods.

## Results

LD-EnSF achieves the lowest assimilation relative RMSE across all three benchmarks with 10% observation noise, reaching approximately 5% relative RMSE on the atmospheric modeling task despite extreme sparsity (0.1% spatial, 0.2% temporal). In terms of computational efficiency, evolving latent dynamics via LD-EnSF yields speedups of 2×10⁵, 4×10³, and 5×10⁵ times over full-space dynamics for Kolmogorov flow, tsunami, and atmospheric modeling respectively, while reducing the assimilation dimension from 45,000/67,500/393,216 (full-space) or 400/400/512 (Latent-EnSF) to just 10/12/52. The improved LDNet surrogate achieves relative RMSE of 0.0123, 0.0168, and 0.0656 on the three tasks, outperforming the original LDNet (0.0349, 0.1837, 0.1042) and VAE-based alternatives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LD-EnSF outperforms all five baselines on both accuracy and efficiency. Against EnSF, LD-EnSF wins decisively: EnSF fails under sparse observations due to vanishing likelihood gradients in unobserved state components, while LD-EnSF maintains ~5% relative RMSE even at 0.1% spatial sparsity. Against Latent-EnSF (VAE-based), LD-EnSF achieves lower assimilation error (attributed to smoother latent trajectories) and eliminates the need for full-space forward propagation, reducing dynamics time from 10,829 s to 0.049 s (Kolmogorov) and cutting the latent dimension from 400 to 10. Against LETKF, LD-EnSF is more accurate and far faster; notably, LETKF diverges entirely in the atmospheric modeling case due to CFL-condition instability. Against the original LDNet (Regazzoni et al., 2024), the improved LDNet reduces surrogate error by 65% on Kolmogorov flow (0.0123 vs 0.0349) and 91% on tsunami (0.0168 vs 0.1837), primarily through the new initialization scheme, two-stage training, and Fourier-encoded ResNet reconstruction. Against 4DEnVar (tested only on tsunami), LD-EnSF achieves lower assimilation error while being orders of magnitude faster, since 4DEnVar requires repeated forward simulations and complex optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Ensemble Score Filter (EnSF) and its latent-space extension Latent-EnSF, which addressed sparse observations by projecting states and observations into a VAE latent space. The key departure is replacing the disconnected VAE encoder plus full-space forward propagation with a unified Latent Dynamics Network (LDNet) surrogate that evolves dynamics entirely in a compact latent space, eliminating the costly full-model simulation during assimilation. An LSTM-based observation encoder further extends the VAE encoder from Latent-EnSF by incorporating temporal history and handling irregularly spaced observations, enabling joint state and parameter estimation in a single latent-space filtering loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LD-EnSF achieves speedups of 10^3–10^5 over full-dynamics methods while maintaining high accuracy under extreme sparsity (0.1% spatial, 0.2% temporal), making real-time data assimilation feasible for large-scale systems like atmospheric and tsunami modeling. The finding that LDNet latent trajectories are substantially smoother than VAE latents—and that this smoothness is critical for the observation encoder to align with predicted states—provides a useful design principle for latent-space DA. Open questions include extending the framework to high-dimensional stochastic or spatially varying parameters, handling long-time-horizon dynamics where latent surrogates degrade, and improving out-of-distribution generalization when initial conditions shift beyond the training region.
