## Problem

Existing skeleton-based temporal action segmentation (STAS) methods decouple action classification and boundary localization into separate decoder heads atop a shared encoder, creating information silos that prevent the semantic complementarity between the two sub-tasks from being exploited. This decoupled paradigm forces a single compromised feature representation to serve both temporally-invariant classification and temporally-sensitive localization, artificially limiting performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262516/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes skeleton sequences F_s ∈ R^{3×T×V} (3D joint coordinates over T frames and V joints) from motion capture or pose estimation. It produces per-frame action class labels (C classes) and per-frame binary boundary predictions. Curvature is computed on globally pooled frame-level classification features (post spatial pooling in the encoder), not per-joint, and the boundary-change metric is derived by inverting min-max-normalized smoothed curvature values.

## Method

CurvSeg introduces two coupled mechanisms. First, Curvature-Guided Synergy (CGS) exploits the geometric property that well-learned classification features confine intra-segment trajectories within compact class-specific hyper-spheres, producing high curvature within segments and low-curvature valleys at transitions (formally proven in Appendix B via the inverse relationship between mean curvature and confining radius). A bidirectional MSE consistency loss with stop-gradient operators aligns boundary head predictions with the curvature-derived boundary metric in both directions, creating a self-reinforcing training loop. Second, Expert-Driven Decoupling (EDD) deploys task-specific experts within a Mixture-of-Experts framework: spatially via SE-style joint attention plus DSTI layers, and temporally via Gaussian experts operating on uniformly partitioned segments (M=64) with soft routing, so that classification and localization heads receive optimally tailored features rather than a single shared representation. The overall loss combines standard classification cross-entropy, boundary logistic loss, and the curvature synergy term weighted by λ.

## Results

On PKU-MMD (X-sub), CurvSeg achieves 74.3% Acc, 74.4% Edit, and 79.3/76.3/65.5 F1@{10,25,50}, outperforming the strongest baseline LaSA (73.5/73.4/78.3/74.8/63.6) by +0.8 Acc and +1.0 Edit. On LARa, it reaches 76.6% Acc and 66.2% Edit (vs. LaSA's 75.3/65.7), with F1@50 improving from 57.9 to 59.0. On MCFS-22, CurvSeg attains 81.2% Acc and 87.7% Edit, surpassing LaSA (80.8/86.7). The most consistent gains appear in segmental F1 scores, confirming improved boundary precision across all four datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CurvSeg outperforms all 10 compared baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest skeleton-based baseline LaSA (Ji et al., 2024), gains are +0.8 Acc / +1.0 Edit on PKU-MMD X-sub and +1.3 Acc / +0.5 Edit on LARa, with the largest relative improvement in F1@50 (+1.9 on PKU X-sub, +1.1 on LARa). Against ME-ST (Ji et al., 2025), the most recent competitive method, CurvSeg leads by +0.2 Acc but +3.9 Edit on PKU-MMD X-sub and +2.4 Acc on LARa. Video-based methods (MS-TCN++, ETSN, ASRF) are substantially outperformed (e.g., +8.3 Acc over MS-TCN++ on PKU X-sub), reflecting the inherent advantage of skeleton modality. The DeST-TCN baseline (Li et al., 2023d) is surpassed by +6.7 Acc and +8.1 Edit on PKU-MMD X-sub, confirming that the curvature-guided synergy and expert decoupling add substantial value beyond the decoupled spatio-temporal backbone alone. Notably, the gains are most pronounced in segmental F1 scores across all comparisons, directly validating the method's focus on boundary precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DeST (Li et al., 2023d) and LaSA (Ji et al., 2024) backbone architectures for skeleton-based temporal action segmentation, which employ decoupled spatio-temporal modeling with separate classification and localization heads. Its key conceptual departure is the argument that this decoupling paradigm, while standard, creates 'information silos' that prevent beneficial cross-task collaboration. The curvature-based boundary prior is adapted from representation learning work (Shin et al., 2024) on exploiting feature-space geometry for time-series boundary detection, and the theoretical grounding draws on Diao et al. (2013) for the inverse relationship between confinement radius and mean curvature. The dual-expert MoE mechanism for task-adaptive feature extraction is inspired by multi-modal perception (Kim et al., 2025) but repurposed here to resolve the shared-encoder compromise between classification and localization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine structural limitation in STAS methods—the tension between temporally invariant features for classification and temporally sensitive features for boundary detection—by proposing a geometric bridge (curvature) that couples the two tasks without requiring architectural unification. The finding that low-dynamic actions benefit more in relative terms (+6.02% Edit vs. +3.17% for high-dynamic) suggests the geometric prior is most valuable precisely where semantic baselines struggle, which is practically useful. However, limitations remain for extremely noisy skeleton data, multi-person scenarios, and the curvature signal's sensitivity to estimation jitter. Open questions include whether the geometric synergy generalizes to video-based TAS or other sequential labeling tasks, and whether the MoE overhead is justified at scale.
