## Problem

Existing image customization methods treat position-aware (mask-specified insertion) and position-free (text-guided identity-consistent generation) as separate tasks requiring distinct models, and they provide limited support for diverse mask types, often confusing coarse user-drawn regions with precise boundaries. This fragmentation hinders the development of a unified framework capable of flexibly handling diverse industrial customization requirements within a single architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260083/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a width-wise diptych concatenation of a reference identity image and a fill-in image (partially or fully masked), a text prompt with [REF-SCENE] and [TARGET-SCENE] placeholders describing both images, and optional Redux embeddings. Position-free customization is handled as a special case where the fill-in image and mask are set to zero. Inputs span resolutions from 800×800 to 3000×3664 pixels, and the output is a single identity-consistent customized image at the fill-in region's resolution, with unmasked regions preserved in position-aware mode.

## Method

IC-Custom unifies both customization paradigms under a single in-context diptych formulation p(X̂ | C_I, C_I′, M, C_T), where the reference and fill-in images are concatenated width-wise and jointly encoded as tokens. The core architectural contribution is the In-Context Multi-Modal Attention (ICMA) module, which replaces the standard MM-DiT attention layers with two innovations: (1) three learnable task-oriented register tokens that explicitly signal whether the task uses a precise mask, a user-drawn mask, or is position-free, and (2) boundary-aware positional embeddings (Reference Embeddings and Fill Embeddings) added to image tokens to delineate the diptych boundary and prevent edge artifacts. Training uses Rectified Flow with a Conditional Flow Matching loss, applying LoRA (rank 64) to the first 10 layers of both single and double blocks plus fine-tuning of input layers, totaling only 49.26M trainable parameters (0.4% of the 12B FLUX.1-Fill base). The model is trained on a curated 12K-sample dataset (8K real-world e-commerce pairs + 4K filtered synthetic pairs from SynCD) with both precise SAM masks and randomly generated user-drawn masks, enabling a single model to handle all customization modes without separate adapters.

## Results

On ProductBench (position-aware), IC-Custom achieves the best identity consistency (DINO-I 63.14 precise / 63.28 user-drawn) and text alignment (CLIP-T 31.75 / 31.80) across all mask types. On DreamBench (position-free), IC-Custom substantially outperforms all baselines with DINO 65.67, CLIP 83.19, and CLIP-T 36.88, surpassing the next-best DreamO (DINO 57.69) by nearly 8 points. In the human study, IC-Custom receives 78.5% preference for identity consistency, 75.4% for harmony, and 62.2% for text alignment—dramatically exceeding the runner-up GPT-4o (4.6%, 7.5%, 21.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IC-Custom outperforms all six baselines on every metric where they overlap. Against the position-aware specialists (FLUX.1 workflow, Insert Anything), IC-Custom leads by 0.88–1.07 DINO-I and 0.26–0.36 CLIP-T on ProductBench, while also being the only method to handle both precise and user-drawn masks natively. Against the position-free specialists (OminiCtrl, DreamO), the gap widens dramatically on DreamBench: IC-Custom scores DINO 65.67 vs. DreamO's 57.69 (+7.98) and CLIP 83.19 vs. OminiCtrl's 75.85 (+7.34). GPT-4o, the only unified baseline, is beaten on all nine metrics—most notably on DreamBench DINO (65.67 vs. 54.31, +11.36) and CLIP (83.19 vs. 77.38, +5.81). The human study confirms these quantitative gaps: IC-Custom earns 78.5% preference for identity consistency versus GPT-4o's 4.6%, a roughly 17× margin. Notably, IC-Custom achieves all of this while training only 49.26M parameters (0.4% of FLUX's 12B), compared to DreamO's 707M LoRA parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the FLUX.1-Fill MM-DiT architecture and its multi-modal attention mechanism, extending the in-context conditioning paradigm introduced by recent DiT-based customization methods (ACE++, Insert Anything, DreamO, OminiControl). The key departure is unifying position-aware (mask-guided image filling) and position-free (text-guided identity-consistent generation) customization—previously treated as separate tasks requiring distinct models or LoRA adapters—into a single diptych-based framework. The proposed ICMA module introduces task-oriented register tokens and boundary-aware positional embeddings to disambiguate customization types and input regions, addressing failure modes (mask-type confusion, boundary artifacts) that prior in-context methods do not explicitly handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of two previously separate customization paradigms into one model with only 0.4% trainable parameters has direct practical value for industrial media production (try-on, product insertion, IP customization), where maintaining a single pipeline across diverse user workflows is essential. The curated 12K real-world-plus-synthetic dataset addresses a known gap where prior methods over-rely on synthetic data, producing overly glossy results. Open questions remain around explicit 3D geometric control (viewpoint, lighting), robustness to ambiguous or extremely small masks, and faithful reconstruction of high-frequency textures on complex subjects—limitations the authors acknowledge but leave for future work.
