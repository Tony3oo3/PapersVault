## Problem

Existing LLM-based Knowledge Graph Foundation Models (KGFMs) suffer from knowledge distortion: sparse structural context extracted from KGs can override the dense intrinsic knowledge in LLMs when injected as explicit prompts, causing irreversible damage to reasoning quality. Additionally, these methods lack effective constraints on generative hallucinations, producing out-of-scope entity predictions that undermine evaluation fairness. The core gap is the absence of a unified mechanism to coordinate LLM internal knowledge with external KG structural representations throughout the entire inductive KGR pipeline.

## Input / Output

Given a query triplet ⟨e_h, r_q, ?⟩ and the full KG (including entity/relation text descriptions), KRLM constructs a Knowledge Reasoning Language (KRL) instruction that interleaves word-level entity/relation tokens (via Principal Attribute Aggregation), GNN-derived structural embeddings, and standard LLM text tokens into a single sequence of ~119 tokens on average. The output is a ranked score over all entities in the KG's entity vocabulary (not free-text generation), with the top-1 entity as the predicted tail. This entity-space constraint guarantees predictions always fall within the evaluable KG domain, unlike prior LLM-based methods that generate entity names as token sequences from a 32k-token vocabulary.

## Method

KRLM unifies LLM knowledge and KG structure through three coupled components. First, a KRL tokenizer encodes entities/relations via Principal Attribute Aggregation (PAA) of pre-trained token embeddings (mean, max, min, std pooling + fusion MLP) for word-level representations, and via a 6-layer NBFNet-based GNN for structural knowledge representations, all projected into the LLM's 4096-dim hidden space without expanding the vocabulary table. Second, a KRL attention layer extends standard Llama2-7b self-attention with a dynamic knowledge memory mechanism: top-K (K=50) structurally relevant entity embeddings are injected as additional key-value pairs in the attention computation, allowing the last-token hidden state to simultaneously attend to in-context textual tokens and external KG structural context. Third, a structure-aware next-entity predictor replaces the LLM's projection head with a GNN-decoded entity-specific scoring matrix, constraining predictions to the KG entity set. Training employs a mutual knowledge distillation objective combining structural scoring loss, KRL scoring loss, and bidirectional KL divergence terms to dynamically align textual and structural knowledge representations.

## Results

On 12 Inductive Entity (IndE) datasets, KRLM(FT) achieves an average Hit@10 of 0.751 and MRR of 0.590, outperforming the best zero-shot KGFM (TRIX-PT: 0.732/0.579) and the best fine-tuned KGFM (TRIX-FT: 0.734/0.583). On 13 Inductive Entity-and-Relation (IndER) datasets, KRLM(FT) reaches Hit@10 0.556 and MRR 0.367, surpassing ULTRA(FT) (0.542/0.350) and PROLINK (0.542/0.354). Notably, KRLM in zero-shot (PT) mode already outperforms 87% of all baselines and even exceeds some fine-tuned KGFMs, demonstrating strong transfer capability without dataset-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KRLM(FT) achieves the best average Hit@10 and MRR across all 25 inductive datasets, beating the strongest KGFM baselines ULTRA (0.724→0.751 Hit@10 on IndE), MOTIF (0.740→0.751), and TRIX (0.734→0.751) by 1.7–2.7 percentage points. Among LLM-based KGFMs, KRLM outperforms PROLINK (0.733→0.751 on IndE; 0.542→0.556 on IndER) and MKGL (0.726→0.751 on IndE), with MKGL being entirely inapplicable to IndER due to its fixed relation vocabulary. In zero-shot (PT) mode, KRLM already surpasses TRIX(FT) on IndE MRR (0.583 vs. 0.583, tied) and ULTRA(FT) on IndER Hit@10 (0.546 vs. 0.542), confirming that the LLM's intrinsic knowledge provides a meaningful complement to structural invariance. On transductive benchmarks, KRLM(E2E) trails GPT-4 (0.565 Hit@10 on FB15k-237) and MKGL (0.591), but the paper notes no significant correlation between closed-domain and open-domain performance, suggesting transductive results are less indicative of inductive generalization. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that removing the knowledge encoder (−KEn) causes the largest drop (e.g., FB-V1: 0.705→0.614 Hit@10), while removing the knowledge memory (−KMe) or knowledge decoder (−KDe) yields smaller but consistent degradations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Knowledge Graph Foundation Models (KGFMs) like ULTRA, MOTIF, and TRIX—which learn structural invariance across KGs for zero-shot inductive reasoning—and LLM-based KGR methods like MKGL and PROLINK that leverage pre-trained language knowledge. It departs from prior LLM-based KGFMs by identifying and addressing 'knowledge distortion,' where sparse KG context in explicit prompts overrides the dense intrinsic knowledge of LLMs. The architectural lineage draws on NBFNet for structural encoding, ULTRA's relational graph construction for cross-KG invariance, and MKGL's Principal Attribute Aggregation for parameter-efficient tokenization, but reorganizes these into a unified KRL instruction format with a dynamic knowledge memory mechanism in the attention layer and a structure-constrained next-entity predictor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and underexplored failure mode in LLM-based KGR—knowledge distortion—providing a principled coordination mechanism between LLM parametric knowledge and external KG structure that yields competitive zero-shot performance (outperforming 87% of baselines without fine-tuning). The structure-aware predictor's constraint of predictions to the entity vocabulary is a straightforward but important fix for evaluation fairness in LLM-based KGR. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger LLMs or multi-hop reasoning, whether the knowledge memory mechanism generalizes beyond single-triplet queries, and whether the mutual distillation objective remains effective when the LLM backbone is significantly stronger than Llama2-7b.
