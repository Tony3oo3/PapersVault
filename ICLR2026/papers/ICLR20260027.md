## Problem

Identifying optimal data mixtures for large-model pre-training and post-training remains a major open problem. Existing proxy-based search methods (RegMix, CLIMB) require training hundreds of proxy models under candidate mixtures, incurring prohibitive computational costs (720+ GPU-hours) that scale poorly as models and datasets grow. The central question is how to efficiently discover effective data mixtures without this massive search overhead.

## Input / Output

The method takes a collection of k data sources (e.g., 17 Pile subsets for pre-training or 8 SFT domains for post-training), a single proxy model (1M parameters for pre-training, ~1B for post-training), and a held-out validation set defining the search target. It produces a vector of optimized mixture weights α on the probability simplex (α_i ≥ 0, Σα_i = 1). A key design choice is that data is sampled uniformly across sources during training, with the mixture weights acting as per-source loss multipliers rather than stochastic sampling probabilities, which is what makes the weights differentiable.

## Method

FASTMIX reformulates data mixture selection as a bilevel optimization problem and applies a reparameterization that makes it fully differentiable: instead of treating mixture ratios as non-differentiable categorical sampling probabilities, it reinterprets them as continuous per-source loss weights under uniform sampling, so the expected training loss becomes a convex combination Σα_i·L_train(D_i, w). The algorithm alternates between an inner loop (SGD updates on model parameters w to minimize the weighted training loss) and an outer loop (gradient-based updates on mixture weights α to minimize a combined validation + training + entropy objective). When the outer-loop horizon n₂=1, the gradient of the validation loss with respect to α_i admits a closed form proportional to −η_w · ∇_w ℓ_val · ∇_w L_train(D_i, w), meaning sources whose training gradients align with the validation gradient are automatically upweighted. Entropy regularization (λ≈10⁻⁵) and an auxiliary training-loss term (β≈0.1) are added to the search target to prevent mixture collapse and improve generalization beyond the validation set.

## Results

In pre-training on the Pile dataset (17 subsets, 1B-token proxy), FASTMIX achieves an average score of 48.2 across 14 downstream benchmarks (best on 9), with a rank-1 average, using only 1.3 GPU-hours of search time. In post-training (SFT on Qwen2.5-Math-Instruct 7B with 8 data domains), FASTMIX reaches an average of 65.4 across math, coding, and STEM-QA benchmarks, completing search in 2.2 GPU-hours. The method is up to 550× faster than RegMix (720.5 GPU-hours) and 55× faster than CLIMB (71.9 GPU-hours) in pre-training, and ~52× faster than both in post-training (~115–117 GPU-hours).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FASTMIX outperforms all three automated baselines on both pre-training and post-training. Against RegMix (Liu et al., 2024), which trains 512 proxy models and fits a regression model, FASTMIX gains +1.0 average points in pre-training (48.2 vs. 47.2) while reducing search cost from 720.5 to 1.3 GPU-hours (×550 speedup); in post-training, RegMix required 115.9 GPU-hours versus FASTMIX's 2.2. Against CLIMB (Diao et al., 2025), which uses 64 proxy models with iterative region refinement, FASTMIX achieves +0.7 in pre-training (48.2 vs. 47.5) with a ×55 speedup (1.3 vs. 71.9 GPU-hours), and a decisive +5.5 in post-training (65.4 vs. 59.9) with a ×53 speedup (2.2 vs. 117.4 GPU-hours). DoReMi (Xie et al., 2024) is listed as a compared method in the experimental setup but specific numerical results are not reported in the main text; it is positioned as an earlier proxy-based approach that FASTMIX supersedes in both efficiency and accuracy. Notably, FASTMIX's post-training mixture, optimized solely on math benchmarks (GSM8K + Gaokao2023), generalizes to coding (LiveCodeBench-v2) and STEM QA (GPQA-Diamond), where it also achieves the best scores, indicating the discovered mixture avoids overfitting to the optimization signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

FASTMIX builds on the bilevel optimization framework established for hyperparameter optimization (Maclaurin et al., 2015; Franceschi et al., 2018) and repurposes it for data mixture selection. It departs fundamentally from the dominant proxy-based paradigm (DoReMi, RegMix, CLIMB), which trains hundreds of small models under candidate mixtures and fits a regression or iterative search over the resulting performance landscape. The key conceptual departure is a reparameterization showing that optimizing sampling ratios is mathematically equivalent to learning per-source loss weights under uniform sampling, thereby making mixture coefficients differentiable and amenable to standard gradient descent with a single proxy model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Data mixture optimization is a recognized bottleneck in LLM training, and the 550× reduction in search cost (1.3 GPU-hours vs. 720.5 for RegMix) makes automated mixture discovery practical for settings where training hundreds of proxy models is infeasible, such as post-training with 1B+ parameter models. The paper's limitation to a static mixture (fixed throughout training) and the requirement that n₂=1 for a closed-form gradient leave open the question of whether dynamic, curriculum-style mixtures can be optimized with the same framework. Generalization beyond the Qwen2.5 family and to modalities beyond text also remain untested.
