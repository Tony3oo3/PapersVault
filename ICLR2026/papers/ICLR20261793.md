## Problem

Single-cell RNA-seq is a destructive process, making perturbation data inherently unpaired—no cell can be measured both before and after treatment. Existing methods either force unrealistic pairings between control and perturbed cells or ignore the unpaired structure entirely, while the high dimensionality and extreme sparsity of expression matrices cause generative models to overfit zeros and collapse diversity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261793/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a real control cell expression vector (not a group mean), a cell type label, and a perturbation condition (a specific gene knockout identified via GAT embeddings on a gene regulatory network, or a drug–cell line–dosage triple encoded with Uni-Mol molecular embeddings). It produces a continuous gene expression vector that is element-wise multiplied by a predicted binary activation mask and rescaled to original units, yielding a sparse perturbed profile. The mask model operates independently, predicting per-gene activation probabilities conditioned only on the perturbation covariates.

## Method

Doloris employs two conditional diffusion models—a source model for the control distribution and a target model for the perturbed distribution—that share a Gaussian latent space, implementing the DDIB framework to implicitly bridge the two distributions without requiring cell-level pairing. The target model is additionally conditioned on a noisy version of the unperturbed group mean (μ_ct + σ_ct·ε) to inject heterogeneity without assuming Gaussian expression. A separate sparsity masking strategy trains a lightweight mask model to predict zero-valued genes via cross-entropy, and the diffusion loss is computed only over expressed genes, steering the model away from fitting the dominant zeros. Inference proceeds by DDIM-forwarding a real control cell into the shared latent space, then DDIM-reversing under the perturbation condition to obtain continuous expression, followed by applying the predicted binary mask and rescaling. The perturbation embedding uses a GAT over a gene regulatory network for genetic knockouts (supporting multi-gene combinations) and pre-trained Uni-Mol representations for molecular perturbations.

## Results

On the Adamson dataset (unseen single-gene CRISPR knockouts), Doloris achieves RMSE 0.0336, E-distance 0.4682, and EMD 0.0348 on all genes, outperforming the next-best method (linear baseline) by ~29% on RMSE and ~47% on E-distance. On sci-Plex3 (drug perturbations), Doloris achieves RMSE 0.0287, E-distance 0.4055, and EMD 0.0265, surpassing the strongest competitor (BioLord) by 30% on RMSE and 68% on E-distance. On the Norman double-gene knockout task, Doloris leads with RMSE 0.0385 and EMD 0.0179, and on OOD drug perturbations it achieves RMSE 0.0547 and EMD 0.0295, substantially ahead of chemCPA (RMSE 0.0689, EMD 0.0959) and GraphVCI (RMSE 0.5431, EMD 0.0986).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Doloris outperforms all nine baselines across every metric on both datasets. Against the linear baseline (Ahlmann-Eltze et al., 2025), which is the strongest competitor on Adamson, Doloris reduces RMSE from 0.0473 to 0.0336 (−29%) and E-distance from 0.8658 to 0.4682 (−46%), while EMD is comparable (0.0348 vs. 0.0373). Among deep learning methods, scGPT (Cui et al., 2024) performs worst on Adamson (RMSE 0.5372), while scLambda (Wang et al., 2024), GRAPE (Chi et al., 2025), and GEARS (Roohani et al., 2022) cluster around RMSE 0.05–0.055, all substantially behind Doloris. On sci-Plex3 drug perturbations, BioLord (Piran et al., 2024) is the closest competitor (RMSE 0.0409 vs. 0.0287), while chemCPA (Hetzel et al., 2022) and CPA (Lotfollahi et al., 2019) trail further behind, and GraphVCI (Wu et al., 2022) is an order of magnitude worse on RMSE (0.6212). On the Norman double-gene task, Doloris leads the linear baseline by a narrow margin on RMSE (0.0385 vs. 0.0405) but pulls ahead more clearly on E-distance (0.6819 vs. 0.7886) and EMD (0.0179 vs. 0.0190), with GRAPE and GEARS lagging by 30–40% on RMSE. On OOD drug perturbations, Doloris achieves a 21% RMSE improvement over chemCPA and a 10× improvement over GraphVCI, confirming superior generalization to unseen molecules.

## Relation to Prior Work

Doloris repurposes the Dual Diffusion Implicit Bridges (DDIB) framework from image-to-image translation (Su et al., 2022) to model transitions between unperturbed and perturbed single-cell distributions, adapting it to the fundamentally unpaired nature of scRNA-seq perturbation data. It departs from prior perturbation prediction methods (GEARS, scGPT, BioLord, GRAPE, scLambda, CPA, chemCPA, GraphVCI) that either enforce explicit cell pairing or ignore the control-perturbed relationship entirely, instead learning separate conditional diffusion models that share a Gaussian latent space. The sparsity masking strategy, interpretable as a single-cell-level hurdle model, is a novel addition that addresses the zero-inflation problem specific to high-dimensional transcriptomics, complementing the diffusion backbone with a dedicated gene-activation predictor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly modeling the unpaired structure of single-cell perturbation data without forcing unrealistic sample correspondences, Doloris offers a more principled framework that could become a standard approach for perturbation response prediction. The sparsity masking strategy is a practical, transferable technique that any diffusion-based single-cell model could adopt to avoid collapsing onto zero-dominant solutions. However, the paper acknowledges that a simple linear baseline remains competitive on some metrics, and the approach's scalability to very large perturbation screens (thousands of conditions) or to multi-omics settings remains untested. Open questions include whether the DDIB latent-space alignment generalizes to temporal perturbation trajectories and whether the mask model's coherent sampling strategy (Appendix A.11) degrades gracefully with increasing gene dimensionality.
