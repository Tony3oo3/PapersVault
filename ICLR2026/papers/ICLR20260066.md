## Problem

Existing DDI prediction methods inadequately model pairwise intermolecular interactions at the atomic level, treating each drug's substructure in isolation rather than capturing how critical substructures shift when two molecules co-administer. Additionally, prior OOD generalization strategies rely on arbitrary noise injection that distorts chemical semantics and fails to reflect authentic environmental distributions in chemical space, limiting real-world applicability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260066/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of drug molecules as SMILES strings, converted via RDKit into molecular graphs with 10 atomic features (species, valence, aromaticity, partial charge, etc.), 4 bond features, and 3 global features. These two graphs are merged into a single bipartite-connected graph where every atom in drug A is linked to every atom in drug B via a learned relation edge, which is then pruned to the top 20% by attention score. The output is a binary DDI interaction class label (interaction present/absent) produced by a classification head over the pooled rationale representation.

## Method

I2Mole constructs a merged molecular graph connecting all intermolecular atom pairs, then performs intra-molecular message passing (3-layer GatedConv) followed by intermolecular message passing (3-layer GAT) that computes attention weights on relation edges and retains only the top-x% to reduce complexity. Core substructure extraction uses an improved Graph Information Bottleneck adapted for merged graphs: noise is injected into both node representations and relation edges via learned Bernoulli probabilities with concrete relaxation, and the compression term is upper-bounded by a Gaussian variational objective. The irrelevant substructures (nodes masked by GIB) are pooled via set2set and quantized into a discrete environment codebook of M vectors using VQ-VAE-style nearest-neighbor lookup with a commitment loss. Invariant learning is achieved by concatenating the rationale embedding with each codebook vector and minimizing a weighted cross-entropy across all M environments, ensuring the rationale's prediction is independent of the specific environmental context.

## Results

In the transductive setting, I2Mole achieves 88.64% ACC, 95.12% AUROC, and 85.87% F1 on ZhangDDI; 95.34% ACC, 98.84% AUROC, and 96.21% F1 on ChChMiner; and 96.51% ACC, 99.04% AUROC, and 97.53% F1 on DeepDDI, outperforming the second-best model by 0.56%, 0.42%, and 0.66% in ACC respectively. In the more challenging domain generalization setting (trained on ZhangDDI, tested on other datasets), I2Mole reaches 59.25% ACC and 69.22% AUROC on ChChMiner, and 58.12% ACC and 68.72% AUROC on DeepDDI, surpassing the best baseline (IGIB-ISE) by 2.19% and 0.91% in ACC respectively. All improvements are statistically significant with p-values below 0.05.

## Baselines & Comparisons

I2Mole outperforms all eight baselines across every evaluation setting. In the transductive setting, the closest competitor is IGIB-ISE (iterative substructure extraction with interactive GIB), which I2Mole surpasses by 0.56% ACC on ZhangDDI, 0.42% on ChChMiner, and 0.66% on DeepDDI. In the domain generalization setting, the gap widens substantially: I2Mole beats IGIB-ISE by 2.19% ACC on ChChMiner and 0.91% on DeepDDI, and outperforms CMRL (the second-closest) by 3.49% and 1.69% respectively. The older baselines (DeepDDI, SSI-DDI, MDF-SA-DDI) show the largest performance gaps, with MDF-SA-DDI dropping to 33.54% ACC on ChChMiner in domain transfer. The ablation study confirms that the intermolecular interaction module (Δ), the GIB substructure extraction, and the VQ environment codebook each contribute meaningfully, with removing VQ causing the largest single-component drop (from 88.64% to 74.52% ACC on ZhangDDI).

## Relation to Prior Work

I2Mole extends the variational Graph Information Bottleneck (Yu et al., CVPR 2022) from single-molecule subgraph extraction to a merged pairwise molecular graph, where intermolecular atomic interactions are explicitly modeled via GAT-based attention over all atom pairs. It departs from prior DDI methods (CGIB, CMRL, SSI-DDI) that treat the second molecule as a conditioning signal or use co-attention at the substructure level, instead constructing a fully connected merged graph with dynamic weighted relational edges and iterative truncation. The environment codebook, built via vector quantization (van den Oord et al., NIPS 2017) over irrelevant subgraph embeddings, replaces the random or instance-dependent noise injection used in MoleOOD (Yang et al., NeurIPS 2022) and DIR (Wu et al., ICLR 2022), providing a structured, learnable set of discrete environmental contexts for invariant learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VQ-based environment codebook offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc noise injection for OOD generalization in molecular relational learning, and the ablation showing a ~14-point ACC drop when VQ is removed underscores its practical importance. However, the computational cost is substantially higher than baselines (49M parameters, ~22 hours training vs. 10–11M parameters, 1–4 hours for CGIB/CMRL), limiting scalability to very large molecular pairs or multi-drug settings. Open questions include whether the merged-graph paradigm extends naturally to ternary or higher-order drug interactions, whether 3D structural information could replace the purely topological pairwise edges, and whether the approach generalizes to regression tasks (solvation free energy) without the modest performance gains the authors report.
