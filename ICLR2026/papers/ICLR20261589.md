## Problem

Existing Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) methods for Vision-Language Models are designed and evaluated on (nearly) balanced datasets, yet real-world test streams exhibit long-tailed class distributions where head classes dominate decision boundaries. The paper identifies two failure modes unique to VLM-based long-tailed TTA: text-induced tail erosion (pre-trained text embeddings carry class biases that intensify when rich classes coincide with head classes) and modality-bias amplification (applying unimodal LT-TTA to VLMs progressively worsens visual-textual misalignment). This is the first systematic study of TTA under long-tailed settings for VLMs.

## Input / Output

At each step of a sequential unlabeled datastream, the method receives a single test image, generates 15 augmented views via random resized cropping, and computes visual embeddings through a frozen CLIP encoder (ViT-B/16 or ResNet-50 by default). Class text prompts (e.g., 'a photo of a [CLASS].') are pre-encoded and kept frozen throughout adaptation. The output is a per-class probability vector combining cosine similarity with text embeddings plus affinity-scaled contributions from both Deterministic and Exclusionary Prototypes, from which the argmax class label is extracted. The class prior vector is continually updated online from predicted pseudolabels, making the method fully unsupervised and label-free at test time.

## Method

L-TTA comprises three co-designed mechanisms. (1) Synergistic Prototypes (SyPs) maintain two complementary caches: Deterministic Prototypes (DPs) updated via EMA only from high-confidence views (entropy below a dynamic threshold), and Exclusionary Prototypes (EPs) updated from every view weighted by an exclusion factor φ_c that measures how improbable each class is for that view, thereby capturing fine-grained inter-class associations and enriching tail representations that DPs alone would leave under-initialized. (2) Rebalancing Shortcuts (RSs) apply cross-attention with K shared hyperclass vectors to both DPs and EPs, optimized by a Class Re-Allocation (CRA) loss borrowed from MoE load-balancing that penalizes the dot product between expert activation and top-1 assignment counts, enforcing uniform attention distribution across hyperclasses and promoting discernable feature clustering. (3) Balanced Entropy Minimization (BEM) modifies standard EM by subtracting a penalty term (1−P̃)^β · log(π/Σπ_i) from the logits before softmax, which drastically reduces the gradient contribution of confident (typically head) classes while amplifying adaptation for rare and uncertain tail classes; two propositions formally prove that BEM narrows the head-tail optimization gap that vanilla EM widens. The final loss is L_BEM + η·L_CRA, optimized with AdamW only on the shortcut parameters (no gradient flows through the backbone), yielding a 1.45-hour runtime on a single A100.

## Results

On the Long-tailed OOD Benchmark (imb=10), L-TTA achieves 65.97% accuracy and 61.18% macro-F1 on the OOD Average, outperforming the strongest baseline DPE (64.50%/60.15%) by 1.47%/1.03%. On ImageNet specifically, L-TTA reaches 71.30% accuracy and 65.83% macro-F1. On the Cross-Domain Benchmark (averaged over imb={10,20,50}), L-TTA attains 68.77% accuracy and 63.44% macro-F1, surpassing DPE (67.75%/61.24%) by 1.02%/2.20%. Under the Corruption Benchmark (imb=10), L-TTA leads with 43.31% accuracy and 39.97% macro-F1, a 2.87%/2.64% margin over the best competitor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L-TTA outperforms all 12 baselines on every benchmark and imbalance ratio tested. The closest competitor is DPE (dual prototype evolving), which L-TTA surpasses by 1.47% accuracy and 1.03% macro-F1 on the OOD Average (imb=10), and by 1.02%/2.20% on the Cross-Domain Average; the macro-F1 gap is notably larger, confirming L-TTA's superior class-balancing. TDA (prototype-based, training-free) ranks second on OOD (63.75%/59.65%) but degrades sharply under higher imbalance (imb=50: 62.37%/54.79%), while L-TTA's macro-F1 drops only 1.29% across imb 10→50. Under corruption (Table 3), the advantage widens: L-TTA leads DPE by 2.87% accuracy and 2.64% macro-F1, whereas prototype-based methods like TDA nearly collapse to TPT-level performance. Training-free methods (ZERO, MTA) and prompt-only methods (TPT, C-TPT, O-TPT) consistently trail by 3–8% in accuracy, confirming that accumulated prototype knowledge and active rebalancing are essential under long-tailed TTA. On efficiency, L-TTA (1.45h, 1.89 GB) is comparable to DPE (1.38h, 1.81 GB) and far cheaper than WATT (27.7h) or RLCF (8.3h), while achieving the highest harmonic mean (67.20 on LT-CDB, 46.08 on LT-CB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TTA-for-VLMs line initiated by TPT (Shu et al., 2022) and its prototype-based successors (TDA, DPE) by addressing a previously unstudied regime: long-tailed test distributions. It departs from prior TTA work in two key ways: (1) it identifies two VLM-specific failure modes under imbalance—text-induced tail erosion (where pre-trained text embeddings carry class biases that compound with data imbalance) and modality-bias amplification (where unimodal LT strategies worsen image-text misalignment)—and (2) it co-designs three mechanisms (Synergistic Prototypes, Rebalancing Shortcuts, Balanced Entropy Minimization) rather than bolting a standard LT reweighting onto an existing TTA pipeline. The affinity function in the final prediction is borrowed from CLIP-Adapter, and the cross-attention-based Rebalancing Shortcuts draw inspiration from mixture-of-experts load-balancing losses in LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of long-tailed TTA for VLMs, a setting that is arguably more realistic than the balanced-distribution assumption underlying all prior TTA benchmarks. The theoretical propositions formalizing why standard entropy minimization specifically degrades tail-class decision boundaries provide a principled justification for BEM rather than ad-hoc reweighting. Open questions include: whether the class-prior estimation from noisy pseudolabels remains reliable under extreme imbalance or distribution shift, whether the approach extends to open-vocabulary or multi-label settings, and whether the computational overhead of maintaining dual prototypes plus learnable shortcuts becomes prohibitive at very large class counts. The paper's reliance on CLIP-style frozen encoders also leaves open how these ideas transfer to end-to-end VLMs (e.g., LLaVA, GPT-4V).
