## Problem

Prior SAM models (SAM 1, SAM 2) addressed Promptable Visual Segmentation (PVS), segmenting a single object per geometric prompt (point, box, mask). They did not solve the general task of finding and segmenting all instances of a visual concept (e.g., every 'cat' in a video) given a text phrase or image exemplar. SAM 3 introduces Promptable Concept Segmentation (PCS), which requires open-vocabulary recognition, exhaustive multi-instance localization, and identity-preserving tracking simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264706/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts short noun phrases (e.g., 'yellow school bus'), image exemplars (a bounding box with a positive or negative label), or both, applied to RGB images or short videos (≤30 s). Text prompts are global across all frames, while exemplars and visual prompts (points, boxes, masks) can be added per-frame for interactive refinement. Outputs include per-instance segmentation masks with unique tracking IDs, bounding boxes, a binary semantic mask, and a global presence score; the model runs at 30 ms per image with 100+ detections on an H200 GPU and sustains near-real-time video tracking for ~5 concurrent objects.

## Method

SAM 3 is a dual encoder-decoder transformer (~850 M params) in which a DETR-based image detector and a SAM 2-style memory-based video tracker share a single Perception Encoder (PE) backbone that produces aligned vision and text embeddings. The key architectural insight is a learned global presence token that decouples recognition from localization: the presence token predicts whether the concept exists in the frame, while each of 200 object queries only solves the local matching problem, with the final score being the product of the two. For video, the tracker propagates masklets via a memory bank and matches them to per-frame detections using IoU, with temporal disambiguation via a masklet detection score and periodic re-prompting from high-confidence detections. A mixture-of-experts ambiguity head (K=2, winner-takes-all) resolves polysemous phrases. Training proceeds in four stages (PE pre-training → detector pre-training → detector fine-tuning with hard negatives → tracker training with frozen backbone), and a human-plus-AI data engine using fine-tuned Llama 3.2 verifiers for mask quality and exhaustivity checks produces 4 M unique concept labels and 52 M masks, doubling annotation throughput over a human-only pipeline.

## Results

On the SA-Co/Gold benchmark (open-vocabulary concept segmentation), SAM 3 achieves 54.1 cgF1, more than doubling the strongest baseline OWLv2* (24.6) and reaching 74% of estimated human performance (72.8). On LVIS instance segmentation, SAM 3 reaches 48.5 AP vs. DINO-X's 38.5 and OWLv2*'s 43.4. For video PCS on SA-Co/VEval, SAM 3 achieves 30.3 cgF1 on SA-V and 50.8 on YT-Temporal-1B, vastly outperforming GLEE (0.1 and 1.6 respectively) and reaching over 80% of human pHOTA. On VOS, SAM 3 improves over SAM 2 by 6.5 points on MOSEv2 (60.3 vs. 53.8 J&F) and on interactive image segmentation achieves 85.1 mIoU at 5 clicks (vs. SAM 2.1L's 84.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAM 3 decisively outperforms all open-vocabulary detection/segmentation baselines: on SA-Co/Gold it achieves 54.1 cgF1 vs. OWLv2*'s 24.6 (2.2×), DINO-X's 21.3, and Gemini 2.5's 13.0; on LVIS masks it reaches 48.5 AP vs. DINO-X's 38.5 and OWLv2*'s 43.4. For exemplar-based detection (Table 3), SAM 3 surpasses T-Rex2 by +18.3 AP on COCO, +10.3 on LVIS, and +20.5 on ODinW13. In video PCS, SAM 3 dominates GLEE by a wide margin (30.3 vs. 0.1 cgF1 on SA-V; 50.8 vs. 1.6 on YT-Temporal-1B) and also outperforms the hybrid LLMDet+SAM3Tracker (2.3 and 8.0 respectively). Against SAM 2, SAM 3 improves VOS J&F on MOSEv2 from 53.8 (SeC) / 47.9 (SAM2.1L) to 60.3, and interactive image segmentation mIoU from 84.3 (SAM 2.1L) to 85.1 at 5 clicks. On object counting, SAM 3 achieves 93.8% accuracy and 0.12 MAE on CountBench, beating Gemini 2.5 Pro (92.4%, 0.24 MAE) and Molmo-72B (92.4%, 0.27 MAE), while also providing segmentation masks that MLLMs cannot. The SAM 3 Agent (with Gemini 2.5 Pro) reaches 77.0 gIoU on ReasonSeg, surpassing the overall trained SOTA of 65.0 without any task-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAM 3 extends the SAM series (SAM 1, SAM 2) from single-object promptable visual segmentation (PVS) to the new Promptable Concept Segmentation (PCS) task, where a noun phrase or image exemplar prompts segmentation of all matching instances. Architecturally, it couples a DETR/MDETR-style detector (with a novel presence head that decouples recognition from localization) with a SAM 2-style memory-based video tracker, both sharing a Perception Encoder backbone for aligned vision-language features. The data engine departs from prior annotation pipelines by fine-tuning MLLMs (Llama 3.2) as AI verifiers that achieve near-human accuracy, doubling throughput and enabling scalable collection of 4M unique concept labels with hard negatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAM 3 and the SA-Co benchmark (207K concepts, >50× more than prior benchmarks) establish a new standard for open-vocabulary, interactive, multi-instance segmentation in images and videos, with practical implications for robotics, content creation, and data annotation. Key limitations include constrained support for simple noun phrases only (no complex referring expressions without an MLLM wrapper), linear scaling of video inference with object count, and difficulty generalizing to fine-grained out-of-domain concepts zero-shot. The finding that AI-verified synthetic data matches human-annotated data in scaling behavior for domain adaptation suggests a path toward self-improving segmentation systems without human annotation, though the gap to human performance (54 vs. 73 cgF1) remains substantial.
