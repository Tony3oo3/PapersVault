## Problem

Existing conformal-prediction methods for generative modeling require an experimental oracle to label newly generated samples, assume exchangeability between calibration and test data, and do not address budget constraints. In resource-limited drug discovery, one cannot synthesize and validate every generated candidate, and the distribution of generated molecules typically differs from historical assay data. CONFHIT addresses the question of how to certify, with finite-sample statistical guarantees, that a batch of generated molecules contains at least one hit—without oracle access and under covariate shift.

## Input / Output

The method consumes inactive calibration molecules (those with known negative labels), a batch of N generated candidates from any generative model, a pre-trained property predictor μ̂, and a kernel-density-estimated density ratio ŵ between historical and generated samples. It outputs a monotonized sequence of conformal p-values for each nested prefix of the candidate batch, a stopping index N̂ (the smallest prefix whose p-value falls below α), and the corresponding certified subset—or an explicit 'not confident enough' flag when even the full batch cannot be certified. The density ratio is estimated via Gaussian KDE on penultimate-layer features of the property model, following the CODRUG pipeline.

## Method

CONFHIT constructs a multiple-sample weighted conformal p-value that tests the global null hypothesis (no hits in the batch) by exploiting weighted exchangeability between inactive calibration data and generated samples under covariate shift. The p-value is computed via Monte Carlo permutation: for each of B random permutations of calibration and test indices, it evaluates a conformity score V (e.g., max-pooling of μ̂ predictions) and weights each permutation by the joint density ratio of the test points, yielding a weighted rank statistic. For the design problem, CONFHIT computes this p-value for every nested prefix {X_{n+1},…,X_{n+k}}, monotonizes the sequence (p_k = max_{k'≥k} p_{k'}), and stops at the first k where p_k ≤ α. The key theoretical insight is that the nested structure of the null hypotheses (H_k true implies H_ℓ true for all ℓ≤k) guarantees finite-sample error control without any multiplicity correction. The framework is fully model-agnostic: the generative model, property predictor, and score function can be arbitrary, affecting only power rather than validity.

## Results

CONFHIT achieves valid coverage (realized error ≤ target α) across all tested generative models (HGraph2Graph, Self-Edit, TargetDiff, DecompDiff, MolCRAFT) and both tasks (CMO and SBDD) at multiple confidence levels. In the design problem, the nested testing procedure prunes certified sets to 2–5 molecules (30–50% of the full budget N=15) in SBDD at α=0.1, reducing mean set size to ~4 molecules while maintaining error control. The max-pooling conformity score achieves the highest power, certifying 73% of active seeds at α=0.1 (SBDD, TargetDiff, N=5), while rank-sum is most conservative. Without distribution shift correction (w≡1), coverage guarantees are violated for TargetDiff (SBDD) and Self-Edit (CMO) at stringent α levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CONFHIT is compared against three baselines. Against Bonferroni correction (thresholding single-sample p-values at α/N), CONFHIT substantially outperforms: at α=0.1 in SBDD, Bonferroni yields empty sets for nearly 100% of inputs while CONFHIT's empty-set frequency is as low as 16% (N=15); when Bonferroni does certify, it typically exhausts the full budget, whereas CONFHIT prunes to 2–5 molecules. Against the certification-only baseline (no pruning), the nested procedure halves the certified set size in CMO and reduces SBDD mean set size to ~4 molecules at α=0.1, N=15, with tighter agreement between realized and nominal error. Against the oracle baseline from Quach et al. (2023) — which unrealistically labels generated samples but ignores covariate shift — CONFHIT achieves lower error rates and fewer empty sets, demonstrating that distribution shift correction is essential even when oracle access is available.

## Relation to Prior Work

CONFHIT extends the conformal prediction lineage for generative modeling (Quach et al., 2023; Kladny et al., 2024; Shahrokhi et al., 2025) by eliminating the oracle-access assumption that all prior methods require, and by correcting for covariate shift between historical calibration data and newly generated samples via density-ratio-weighted exchangeability (Tibshirani et al., 2019; Jin & Candès, 2023a). The density ratio estimation strategy follows CODRUG (Laghuvarapu et al., 2023), while the multi-test-point weighted p-value builds on the two-sample conditional distribution test of Hu & Lei (2024). The key conceptual departure is the nested testing framework, which converts individual certification p-values into a compact design procedure without multiplicity correction, a step absent from prior conformal generative work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CONFHIT is the first framework to provide finite-sample validity guarantees for generative design without requiring experimental oracle access, making conformal guarantees practically deployable in resource-constrained drug discovery where wet-lab validation is the bottleneck. The nested testing result—that no multiplicity correction is needed due to the nested hypothesis structure—is an elegant theoretical contribution that simplifies the design problem. Key limitations include reliance on density ratio estimation quality (which degrades with small calibration sets), focus on small molecules only, and validation via in-silico oracles rather than wet-lab confirmation. Extending the framework to macromolecular design (proteins, materials) and establishing transferability under real experimental conditions remain open.
