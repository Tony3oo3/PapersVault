## Problem

Arena-based LLM evaluation (e.g., Chatbot Arena) relies on exhaustive or random pairwise annotations to produce reliable ELO rankings, resulting in redundant battles, long evaluation cycles, and poor scalability as the number of models grows. Existing efficiency methods target static QA benchmarks and are incompatible with the dynamic, iterative nature of arena evaluation, while current arena methods focus solely on ranking accuracy and ignore annotation efficiency.

## Input / Output

At each iteration t, the method consumes the accumulated battle records S_{t-1} (each a triple (i, j, w_{ij}) with w ∈ {0, 0.5, 1}), the current MLE-based ability estimates r̂^{t-1}, and the set of N candidate models to produce the next model pair (i_t, j_t) for annotation. The output also includes the updated ability vector and the (N-1)×(N-1) Fisher information matrix, which is incrementally updated by adding the rank-1 contribution of the new battle rather than recomputed from scratch. The method requires only the minimal connectivity condition q_N = ω(log N / N) on the underlying Erdős-Rényi graph, far less than Ada-Pair's requirement of q_N = 1.

## Method

The paper models arena battles via the Bradley-Terry logistic win-probability and shows (Lemma 1) that under sparse E-R graph conditions, MLE-based ELO estimates are asymptotically normal with covariance governed by the inverse Fisher information matrix. This reduces the efficiency problem to selecting pairs that most rapidly shrink the inverse information matrix. Two optimal-design criteria are applied: A-optimality minimizes tr[I^{-1}] (sum of per-model variances) and D-optimality maximizes det[I] (overall uncertainty volume), both evaluated by approximating true abilities with current estimates. D-optimality is preferred in practice because it has lower per-step complexity O(N^{β+2}) versus O(N^{β+3}) for A-optimality and is minimally affected by the ELO constraint that fixes one model's ability to zero. A time-improved variant selects the top-K highest-information-gain pairs per user request to handle system concurrency, and Theorem 2 guarantees that both A-Info and D-Info grow at most linearly in sample count, preventing numerical overflow.

## Results

The primary metric is the Pairwise index (consistency between estimated and true model abilities, where 1.0 is perfect). On the main 20-model datasets (Chatbot Arena and PPE), D-optimality achieves the highest total pairwise score of 0.8531 (Original) and 0.8581 (Improved with Top-K), outperforming the Random baseline (0.8409) by ~1.2% and the official Chatbot Arena strategy Ada-Pair (0.8437) by ~0.9%. On the larger Arena-100k dataset (55 models), D-optimality again leads with a total of 0.8346 versus 0.8249 for Random and 0.8312 for Ada-Pair. The Improved D-optimality variant (selecting Top-10 pairs per round) further gains ~0.5% over the Original version, confirming that prioritizing high-information-gain pairs is effective.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three main baselines: (1) **Random** selection (the standard baseline), (2) **Ada-Pair** (the official Chatbot Arena strategy that maximizes confidence-interval reduction), and (3) **Nearest** (pairing models with closest current ELO ratings, common in competitive games). D-optimality consistently outperforms all three across every dataset and ELO variant: it beats Random by +1.22% (0.8531 vs 0.8409), Ada-Pair by +0.94% (0.8531 vs 0.8437), and Nearest by +1.21% (0.8531 vs 0.8410) in total pairwise score. The paper's own A-optimality method is intermediate (0.8456), confirming that D-optimality's determinant-based criterion is more effective than trace-based variance minimization for this task. On the larger Arena-100k dataset (55 models), D-optimality maintains its lead (0.8346 vs 0.8249 Random, 0.8312 Ada-Pair), while A-optimality actually underperforms Random (0.8243 vs 0.8249), likely due to the fixed-anchor bias of the ELO system. The Improved (Top-K) variant of D-optimality further extends its advantage to 0.8581, suggesting that batching high-information pairs per user request is practically beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected literatures: optimal experimental design (A-optimality and D-optimality from statistics) and arena-based LLM evaluation (Bradley-Terry/ELO frameworks). While prior arena work (Chatbot Arena, Ada-Pair) focuses on ranking accuracy and prior efficiency work (TinyBenchmarks, MetaBench) targets static QA benchmarks, this paper is the first to formalize annotation efficiency as a first-class objective in dynamic pairwise evaluation. It departs from the ELO tradition by leveraging the asymptotic normality of MLE-based ability estimates under sparse Erdős-Rényi graphs to derive principled pair-selection criteria, rather than relying on heuristics like nearest-ability matching or confidence-interval reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For platforms hosting 190+ models like Chatbot Arena, reducing redundant battles directly lowers annotation costs and shortens evaluation cycles, making the framework practically deployable. The theoretical guarantees (positive definiteness of the Fisher information matrix, linear growth bounds) provide a rigorous foundation that prior heuristic methods lack. Open questions include incorporating instance-level difficulty and annotator effects into the selection loop, and whether the O(N^(β+2)) computational overhead remains acceptable as model counts grow into the thousands. The finding that D-optimality is less affected by the ELO anchor-model constraint than A-optimality suggests the optimal criterion may depend on the specific rating system in use.
