## Problem

Standard generative modeling requires clean data samples, but in many scientific and engineering domains only corrupted observations are available through a noisy, ill-conditioned forward channel. Existing approaches to inverse generative modeling (e.g., Ambient Diffusion, EM-based methods) require the forward model to be explicitly linear, differentiable, or to have a tractable likelihood, severely limiting their applicability to real-world corruption processes like motion blur, JPEG compression, or Poisson noise.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset of corrupted observations y ~ μ and black-box access to the forward map F (which may be nonlinear, non-differentiable, and stochastic), plus optional latent variables (e.g., random mask, blur direction, JPEG quality factor) that are appended to the observation and used to condition the transport. It produces a learned backward transport map Φ_Θ that pushes μ to an estimate of the clean distribution π, yielding a restored dataset {Φ_Θ(y_i)}. To obtain a full generative model, the restored samples are used to train a standard diffusion model, or a generative model for μ is trained first and its outputs are restored via Φ_Θ. The method assumes the observation space embeds into the data space (or uses a lifting construction to a product space).

## Method

SCSI constructs a self-consistent stochastic interpolant via a bi-level iterative scheme: the outer loop updates a transport map Θ by building a linear SI between the current transported samples Φ_Θ(y) and their forward-model images F(Φ_Θ(y)), while the inner loop trains the velocity and denoiser fields via standard SI least-squares regression. The key insight is that the fixed point of this iteration satisfies the self-consistency condition KΦ_#μ = μ, meaning the forward model applied to the transported observations reproduces the original observation distribution, thereby inverting the corruption channel at the distribution level without ever needing clean samples, gradients of F, or explicit likelihood evaluation. The velocity field is parameterized by a U-Net (~32M parameters), and the method supports both ODE (probability flow) and SDE (diffusion) inference. Theoretical analysis establishes linear convergence in Wasserstein distance under a Lipschitz stability assumption and in KL divergence under a condition number bound, with the ODE setting yielding the fastest contraction rate.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 restoration tasks, SCSI achieves LPIPS of 0.005 on Gaussian blur (σn=0.1), outperforming DPS (0.009) by 44%, and 0.0064 on random masking (σn=0.1) vs. DPS's 0.0072. For generative model quality on random masking, SCSI achieves FID of 5.38 (ρ=0.25) and 6.74 (ρ=0.50), substantially outperforming Ambient Diffusion (11.70 and 18.85) and matching EM Posterior (5.88 and 6.76) while requiring only ~86 GPU hours vs. 512 for EM. On the SDSS quasar spectra task, SCSI recovers peak heights and locations more faithfully than Wiener filter restoration across both high-noise and low-resolution regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCSI outperforms DPS (Chung et al., 2022) on Gaussian blur restoration (LPIPS 0.005 vs. 0.009 at σn=0.1; 0.015 vs. 0.025 at σn=0.25) and on random masking with higher noise (0.0064 vs. 0.0072), while DPS wins on motion blur at low noise (0.0026 vs. 0.0069). Crucially, DPS requires clean pre-training data, forward-map gradients, a 2× larger network, and task-specific hyperparameter search, whereas SCSI uses only black-box access to F. For generative model quality (FID), SCSI (5.38 at ρ=0.25) substantially outperforms Ambient Diffusion (Daras et al., 2023) at 11.70, and slightly beats EM Posterior (Rozet et al., 2024) at 5.88, while being ~6× more computationally efficient (86 vs. 512 GPU hours). The SI-Oracle (Liu et al., 2023a), trained with paired clean-corrupted data, serves as an upper bound; SCSI approaches it on most settings but lags on motion blur (0.0069 vs. 0.003) and high-noise Gaussian blur (0.015 vs. 0.0011). Unlike all prior baselines, SCSI handles nonlinear and non-differentiable forward models (motion blur, JPEG compression) with only black-box access, a capability none of the compared methods possess.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Stochastic Interpolant framework (Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden, 2023; Albergo et al., 2023a) and repurposes it for the inverse generative modeling problem previously addressed by Empirical Bayes / EM-based methods (Rozet et al., 2024; Bai et al., 2024) and Ambient Diffusion (Daras et al., 2023). The key departure is replacing the explicit likelihood evaluation and posterior sampling required by those approaches with a self-consistent bi-level fixed-point iteration that only needs black-box access to the forward map, thereby extending applicability to nonlinear, non-differentiable, and non-Gaussian corruption channels (motion blur, JPEG compression, Poisson noise). The theoretical analysis introduces a condition-number framework for regularized inverse problems in measure space, complementing the stability and gradient-flow analyses of Li et al. (2024, 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The black-box requirement substantially broadens the class of forward models to which inverse generative modeling applies, removing the linearity and differentiability assumptions that have limited prior work. The finding that ODE-based transport outperforms SDE for moderate corruption (and even beats the EM algorithm's convergence rate in the Gaussian AWGN case) provides practical guidance for method selection. Open questions include extending the framework to discrete data, quantifying the approximation-estimation tradeoff in the condition number, and comparing marginal vs. posterior transport more rigorously. The current KL contraction results do not cover the ε→0 (pure ODE) limit, leaving a gap between theory and the empirically preferred ODE setting.
