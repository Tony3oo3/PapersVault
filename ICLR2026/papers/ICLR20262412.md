## Problem

Existing adversarial defense methods for Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) rely on adversarial fine-tuning that significantly degrades clean-input performance, creating a persistent robustness–accuracy trade-off. No prior work has reframed adversarial training as a preference optimization problem, which would naturally balance the model's preference for correct outputs on clean images against rejection of misleading outputs on adversarial images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262412/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images (trained on ImageNet without category labels) and a text prompt (e.g., 'What is the content of the image?'), producing free-form image captions or VQA answers. Adversarial images are generated online via 10-step PGD under an ℓ∞ budget of ε=2/255, and only the CLIP ViT-L/14 image encoder parameters are updated during training. The trained encoder is then transferred to larger LVLMs (LLaVA-1.5-7B, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3.5, BLIP-2) for evaluation, where the model outputs CIDEr-scored captions or VQA accuracy scores.

## Method

AdPO reframes adversarial training as a preference optimization problem with two jointly optimized components. Preferred Image Optimization (PIO) extends DPO to an online setting: the policy model generates its own interpretations for both clean and adversarial images, which serve as positive and negative samples, eliminating the need for image annotations or the CLIP text encoder. Adversarial Image Optimization (AIO) addresses the limitation that PIO alone does not explicitly encourage correct outputs under adversarial perturbations; it applies dynamic fine-tuning with confidence-weighted token-level loss (stop-gradient on the model's own probability) to boost adversarial robustness while avoiding the overfitting that plain SFT causes. Training is performed on TinyLLaVA (CLIP ViT-L/14 + OpenELM-450M-Instruct) for computational efficiency, and the resulting image encoder is transferred to larger models. The final objective is L_AdPO = L_PIO + λ·L_AIO with λ=1, and the approach is model-agnostic, validated across CLIP-based and non-CLIP-based LVLMs.

## Results

On the four main evaluation tasks (COCO, Flickr30k, TextVQA, VQAv2), AdPO achieves near-original clean performance (e.g., 115.3 CIDEr on COCO vs. 115.5 for untrained CLIP) while substantially outperforming all baselines on adversarial robustness. Under untargeted APGD attacks at ε=2/255, AdPO scores 68.9 on COCO, 38.6 on Flickr30k, 24.2 on TextVQA, and 52.3 on VQAv2, exceeding the best prior method (AGH-MAT or AdvSimplex) by 11–14 points. At the larger ε=4/255 budget, the gap widens further (e.g., 47.6 vs. 32.6 on COCO). AdPO also achieves 0% attack success rate on targeted attacks, compared to 3–5% for the strongest baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdPO outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across all four tasks. Against the strongest prior methods, the margins are substantial: on COCO at ε=2/255, AdPO (68.9) exceeds AGH-MAT (57.2) by 11.7 points and AdvSimplex (55.8) by 13.1 points; at ε=4/255, AdPO (47.6) leads AdvSimplex (32.6) by 15.0 points. On VQAv2, the gap is even wider—AdPO scores 52.3 at ε=2/255 versus 38.4 for AdvSimplex, a 13.9-point margin. On clean performance, AdPO (115.3 COCO, 75.9 Flickr30k, 35.5 TextVQA, 73.6 VQAv2) nearly matches or exceeds the untrained CLIP baseline, whereas TeCoA drops 17.1 points on COCO and 20.4 on Flickr30k, and FARE drops 5.6 and 6.4 points respectively. On targeted attacks (Table 2), AdPO achieves 0% ASR across all five target strings, while the best baselines (FARE, AGH-MAT, AdvSimplex) still allow 3% ASR. The consistent superiority across untargeted, targeted, and jailbreak attack settings confirms AdPO as a clear state-of-the-art.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdPO builds on two distinct research threads: (1) CLIP-based adversarial fine-tuning methods (FARE, TeCoA, TGA-ZSR, PMG-AFT, AGH-MAT, AdvSimplex) that train the image encoder for robustness but typically sacrifice clean accuracy, and (2) Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and its variants from the LLM alignment literature. The key conceptual departure is reframing adversarial training as a preference optimization problem—using DPO to preserve clean-output preference while rejecting adversarial misleading outputs, combined with dynamic fine-tuning to explicitly encourage correct outputs under adversarial inputs. The paper also introduces a practical transfer strategy (train on TinyLLaVA with OpenELM-450M, transfer the CLIP ViT to LLaVA-7B) that avoids the computational cost of full LVLM adversarial training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is that AdPO breaks the typical robustness–clean-accuracy trade-off: it maintains near-identical clean performance to the original CLIP while substantially outperforming all baselines on adversarial robustness across captioning, VQA, and targeted-attack settings. This makes adversarial defense more deployable in real-world LVLM applications where clean performance cannot be sacrificed. Open questions include whether the preference-optimization framing extends to text-side adversarial attacks, whether it generalizes to video or audio modalities, and whether the identified failure mode (semantically close adversarial targets) can be addressed. The paper also notes that jailbreak attack evaluation may overestimate performance given the rapid evolution of that area.
