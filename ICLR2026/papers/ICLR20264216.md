## Problem

Deploying LLMs for rating prediction faces two practical bottlenecks: (1) raw user histories are long and noisy, and truncating to recent items discards long-term preference signals while LLM summarization still incurs high token cost; (2) explicit chain-of-thought decoding in decoder-only architectures requires autoregressively generating hundreds of rationale tokens, inflating per-sample latency by over 100× compared to answer-only inference. Prior approaches either sacrifice long-term signals, add expensive summarization steps, or still emit intermediate reasoning tokens at inference time.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a compact, token-bounded interest sketch (a short list of corpus-level topic names partitioned into likes and dislikes based on per-topic average rating threshold θ=4), the N most recent raw interactions (N=30 for Books, N=10 for Movies), and the candidate item's textual description. At inference, the LLM generates a single numeric rating in [1.0, 5.0] as one answer token with no rationale tokens, achieving ~0.013 s/sample latency on a single H20 GPU via vLLM. The sketch reduces average input tokens by 48.7% relative to raw-history prompting while preserving persistent preference structure.

## Method

SIREN combines two components. First, Long-Term Interest Sketching encodes all item descriptions with BGE-M3, applies K-means (K=20) to discover corpus-level semantic topics once, names each topic via an LLM prompt, then aggregates each user's full history into a small like/dislike topic list by thresholding per-topic average ratings; this sketch is concatenated with recent interactions and the candidate description to form the prompt. Second, Internalized Reasoning uses a two-stage training paradigm: Stage 1 fine-tunes the LLM (Qwen3-4B) with GRPO using a composite reward (format compliance + linear rating-regression reward in [−2,2]) to learn explicit CoT reasoning without ground-truth rationales; Stage 2 applies a hidden-alignment loss that minimizes 1−cosine similarity between the <answer>-token hidden states under answer-only and CoT decoding across all layers, implemented via LoRA (r=8) on key and value projections only. A first-order linearized attention analysis (Theorem 1) shows that KV updates can exactly reproduce the CoT-induced hidden-state shift, justifying the KV-targeted adaptation and enabling near-CoT accuracy at answer-only latency.

## Results

On the Books dataset, SIREN achieves MAE 0.3510 and RMSE 0.6887, improving over the strongest LLM baseline EXP3RT (MAE 0.4001, RMSE 0.7567) by 12.45% and 8.99% respectively. On Movies, SIREN reaches MAE 0.7603 and RMSE 1.2924, outperforming the best prior method LLM4Rate-Qwen3-4B (MAE 0.7995) by 4.9%. SIREN also reduces average input tokens by 48.7% versus raw-history prompting and delivers over 100× lower per-sample inference latency (~0.013 s) compared to CoT-based decoding (~2.2 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIREN outperforms all five baselines on both datasets and both metrics. Against the strongest LLM-based competitor EXP3RT (Kim et al., 2025), SIREN reduces Books MAE from 0.4001 to 0.3510 (−12.45%) and RMSE from 0.7567 to 0.6887 (−8.99%), and Movies MAE from 0.9521 to 0.7603 (−20.1%). Versus LLM4Rate (Kang et al., 2023) with Qwen3-4B, SIREN improves Movies MAE from 0.7995 to 0.7603 and Books MAE from 0.4730 to 0.3510. Rec-SAVOR (Tsai et al., 2024b) and P5 (Geng et al., 2022) trail SIREN by a wider margin, with Rec-SAVOR posting the worst LLM-based MAE on Movies (1.0884). MF (Rendle et al., 2009) is competitive on RMSE for Books (0.7259 vs. SIREN 0.6887) but lags substantially on MAE (0.5740 vs. 0.3510). On the secondary IMDB reranking task, SIREN also beats EXP3RT by 14.7% in Recall@5 (0.0663 vs. 0.0578) while running 52× faster (0.03 s vs. 1.56 s per query).

## Relation to Prior Work

SIREN builds on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework (Shao et al., 2024) to train explicit CoT reasoning in LLMs, but departs from prior LLM-based recommenders (LLM4Rate, Rec-SAVOR, EXP3RT) in two fundamental ways. For user representation, it replaces both raw-history prompting and free-form LLM-generated profiles with a fixed corpus-level K-means topic vocabulary, aggregating each user's history into a compact like/dislike sketch under a strict token budget. For inference efficiency, rather than distilling to smaller models or employing speculative/latent decoding (which still generate intermediate tokens), it introduces hidden-state alignment that transfers CoT effects into KV-projection parameters via LoRA, supported by a first-order linearized attention analysis showing KV updates are a sufficient locus to absorb the CoT residual.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× inference latency reduction while retaining near-CoT accuracy addresses a critical deployment bottleneck for LLM-based recommenders, making real-time rating prediction feasible at production scale. The hidden-alignment technique is potentially generalizable beyond recommendation to any setting where explicit reasoning improves quality but autoregressive generation is too costly. Limitations include evaluation only on 4B-scale backbones and two Amazon subcategories, a first-order theoretical justification that may not hold for deeper or more nonlinear interactions, and the interest sketch's reliance on a fixed topic count K that requires per-dataset tuning. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, whether the sketch generalizes to sequential recommendation or retrieval tasks, and whether the KV-only LoRA target remains optimal as model architecture changes.
