## Problem

Text-conditioned hand motion generation has been largely confined to studio-captured mocap datasets (GRAB, ARCTIC, H2O) that are expensive to collect and cover only narrow action ranges, leaving 'in-the-wild' hand motions—piano playing, cooking, knitting, writing—unmodelled. Additionally, existing LLM-based motion models suffer from poor tokenizer generalization and geometric inaccuracies in generated motions, as token-level cross-entropy loss does not guarantee realistic hand kinematics.

## Input / Output

For text-to-motion, the model takes a natural language prompt and autoregressively generates an interleaved sequence of discrete trajectory and pose tokens (4 per timestep: left/right × trajectory/pose), which are decoded via the SHIFT VQ-VAE into MANO parameters (9D trajectory: 6D rotation + 3D translation; 90D pose: 15 joints × 6D rotation per hand). For motion-to-text, it takes a 3D hand motion sequence and produces a text caption. The VQ-VAE compresses 8 temporal frames into a single token, and the full motion vocabulary stacks trajectory and pose codebooks (4096 entries × 64 dims each) into a unified token space alongside the text vocabulary.

## Method

CLUTCH combines three key components. First, the 3D-HIW dataset (32K sequences, 5000 minutes) is built from Ego4D/EgoVid5M egocentric videos using HaWor for 3D hand reconstruction and a two-stage VLM annotation pipeline: Parallel Chain-of-Thought prompting decomposes reasoning into atomic sub-prompts (hand role, action-object relations, state transitions, intent) aggregated by a summarization LLM, followed by closed-vocabulary grounding against a curated object-action vocabulary. Second, the SHIFT tokenizer is a part-modality decomposed VQ-VAE that uses separate encoders/decoders for trajectory and pose, and disentangles left and right hands, yielding substantially better reconstruction (MPJPE 45.9 vs. ~93 for baselines) and robustness to temporal compression. Third, the LLM (Flan-T5-Base, 220M) is trained in three stages: pre-training with standard cross-entropy next-token prediction, a geometric refinement stage that applies Gumbel-Softmax sampling to decode tokens into continuous motion space and backpropagates an MSE reconstruction loss directly on MANO parameters (avoiding the conflict between soft-blending regression and CE that plagues EgoLM), and instruction fine-tuning for multi-task generalization.

## Results

On the 3D-HIW dataset, CLUTCH achieves state-of-the-art text-to-motion generation with RP3 of 0.721, MMD of 1.765, and KID of 0.216, outperforming all baselines across every metric. For motion-to-text captioning, CLUTCH reaches RP3 of 0.571, BLEU-4 of 0.181, and ROUGE-L of 0.472, substantially exceeding MotionGPT (RP3 0.407) and TM2T (RP3 0.385). On the controlled ARCTIC+GRAB benchmark, CLUTCH also leads with RP3 of 0.492 and MMD of 3.008 for T2M, and RP3 of 0.460 and BLEU-4 of 0.234 for M2T.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLUTCH outperforms HumanMDM (Tevet et al., 2023) on all T2M metrics, with the largest gap in KID (0.216 vs. 0.344) and MMD (1.765 vs. 1.971), while also matching or exceeding its diversity. Against MotionGPT (Jiang et al., 2024), CLUTCH achieves a 25% relative improvement in RP3 (0.721 vs. 0.573) and a 3.5× reduction in KID (0.216 vs. 0.756), and similarly dominates on M2T (RP3 0.571 vs. 0.407). T2M-GPT (Zhang et al., 2023) is the closest T2M competitor, but CLUTCH still leads on RP3 (0.721 vs. 0.683) and KID (0.216 vs. 0.431). For M2T, CLUTCH surpasses TM2T (Guo et al., 2022) by a wide margin on every metric (e.g., BLEU-4: 0.181 vs. 0.122). In the annotation quality comparison, CLUTCH's two-stage pipeline achieves a GPT-Score of 6.9, beating LaViLa (Zhao et al., 2023) at 4.9 and EgoHOD (Pei et al., 2025) at 6.1. Finally, against EgoLM's (Hong et al., 2024) soft-blending reconstruction approach, CLUTCH's geometric refinement stage yields better T2M KID (0.216 vs. 0.263) and RP3 (0.721 vs. 0.705), with M2T performance essentially tied (0.571 vs. 0.570).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-for-motion paradigm established by MotionGPT and HOIGPT, which treat discretized motion tokens as foreign-language symbols for a pretrained language model. It departs from prior work in three key ways: (1) replacing studio-captured mocap datasets (GRAB, ARCTIC) with a large-scale in-the-wild dataset (3D-HIW) built from egocentric video via VLM-based annotation; (2) introducing the SHIFT tokenizer, which decomposes VQ-VAE encoding by both body part (left/right hand) and modality (trajectory/pose), going beyond the single-codebook or part-only decompositions in MotionGPT and HOIGPT; and (3) adding a geometric refinement stage using Gumbel-Softmax sampling to apply reconstruction loss directly in motion space, addressing the CE-vs-regression conflict identified in EgoLM's soft-blending approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CLUTCH establishes the first benchmark for scalable in-the-wild hand motion modelling, with the 3D-HIW dataset (32K sequences, 1355 objects, 1045 verbs) being roughly 10× larger than GRAB/ARCTIC and 2× larger than Gigahands. The practical compute efficiency (4 A100s vs. 64 V100s for MotionGPT) makes in-the-wild hand motion modelling accessible to smaller labs. Key limitations include the absence of hand-object interaction modelling, reliance on automated VLM annotations that may introduce systematic biases, and the focus on hand motions alone rather than full upper-body dynamics. Open questions include whether the in-the-wild distribution can be combined with controlled mocap for better fine-grained expressiveness, and how to handle temporal segmentation of overlapping multi-action sequences.
