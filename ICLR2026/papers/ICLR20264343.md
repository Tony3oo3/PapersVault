## Problem

Existing image editing methods either rely on 2D drag-based interfaces that lack geometric consistency (objects change shape, scale incorrectly) or require full 3D mesh reconstruction that is bottlenecked by reconstruction quality. There is no compact, editable 3D representation that simultaneously supports camera moves, object-level manipulation, and texture/identity preservation in a single streamlined pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264343/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (depth estimated via DepthAnythingV2), a text prompt, and user-specified edits to 3D convex primitives (translation, rotation, scaling) and/or camera pose. It produces a variable-scale set of convex polytopes (K=6–10+), a ray-marched depth map, a texture hint image with a per-pixel confidence mask, and a final 512×512 generated image via FLUX. The same scene can be decomposed at different primitive counts, enabling both coarse (whole-object) and fine (part-level) edits from the same input.

## Method

The pipeline decomposes the scene into CVXNet-style convex polytopes using a ResNet-18 encoder–FC decoder trained on 1.8M LAION images with DepthAnythingV2 depth supervision, then renders a depth map by ray-marching the primitive SDFs. A key contribution is a projection-based texture hint: 3D point-cloud correspondences between the original and edited views are computed to warp source pixels into the new view, producing a rough hint image and confidence mask that are injected into FLUX's denoising process at timesteps [t_end, t_start] via a masked latent blend. This avoids the need for fine-tuning the depth-conditional FLUX model (unlike LooseControl's LoRA) and preserves object identity far better than key-value transfer methods. The diffusion model then refines the hint, filling gaps and sharpening aliased textures, while the depth conditioning enforces the edited geometry.

## Results

On 48 test images with random camera moves (K=10 primitives), Generative Blocks World achieves AbsRel_src of 0.072 and AbsRel_dst of 0.076 for geometric consistency, and PSNR of 18.7 dB with SSIM of 0.874 for texture consistency. These numbers are evaluated by reprojecting the edited image back to the original viewpoint and comparing against the source. The method requires no fine-tuning of the underlying FLUX depth-to-image model, using only a texture hint and confidence mask injected during selected denoising timesteps.

**Table 1: Comparison of image reconstruction and generation metrics between Generative Blocks World and LooseControl (48 test images, random camera moves, K=10 parts).**

| **Ours** | **0.072** | **0.076** | **18.7** | **0.874** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LooseControl (Bhat et al., 2024), Generative Blocks World wins decisively on all four metrics: geometric error (AbsRel) is roughly halved (0.072 vs. 0.143 source; 0.076 vs. 0.146 destination), and texture fidelity improves dramatically (PSNR 18.7 vs. 6.65 dB; SSIM 0.874 vs. 0.670). Visually (Fig. 5), LooseControl introduces object count changes, altered water levels, and spurious objects during camera moves, while the proposed method preserves scene identity. Against DragDiffusion (Shi et al., 2024), the comparison is qualitative (Fig. 4): DragDiffusion's 2D drag handles produce ambiguous translations vs. scalings, shape distortions (e.g., a clock changing shape), and lost fine details, whereas primitive-based manipulation yields geometrically consistent results. Against StableFlow (Avrahami et al., 2025), which uses vital-layer key-value injection for style preservation, the paper's projection-based texture hints better maintain object identity and fine texture after edits (Fig. 6), though combining both approaches can recover some additional fine detail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lineages: (1) convex primitive decomposition for scene representation, building directly on CVXNet's differentiable polytope formulation and the authors' own CDIS pipeline for fitting primitives to real-world RGB-D scenes, and (2) depth-conditioned image synthesis via rectified flow transformers (FLUX). It departs from prior 3D-aware editing methods in two key ways: rather than reconstructing full meshes (Image Sculpting, OMG3D) or using coarse box primitives requiring LoRA fine-tuning (LooseControl), it uses multi-scale convex polytopes as a directly editable intermediate; and rather than relying on learned key-value transfer for texture preservation (StableFlow), it introduces a geometric point-cloud-correspondence-based texture hint that explicitly tracks how surface points move under primitive and camera transforms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, training-free (no fine-tuning of the generator) 3D editing interface that outperforms box-based and drag-based alternatives in both geometric fidelity and texture preservation, making it a potentially useful tool for interactive image editing. However, several limitations remain open: non-convex geometry (mug handles, chair undersides), view-dependent lighting and reflections, large rotations, and temporal consistency for video are all acknowledged failure modes. The paper also highlights an unresolved tension between text prompts and geometric edits (e.g., moving an object contrary to the prompt), and suggests that applying point correspondences within network layers rather than in pixel space could yield more robust solutions.
