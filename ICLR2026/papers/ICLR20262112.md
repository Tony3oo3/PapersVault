## Problem

Previous work demonstrated that a two-layer transformer with chain of continuous thought (COCONUT) can solve directed graph reachability by maintaining a superposition of multiple reasoning traces in latent space, but it remained unclear whether gradient-based training naturally produces this superposition mechanism. This paper asks: do standard training methods lead to the superposition construction, and what training dynamics explain its emergence?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262112/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a directed graph encoded as a token sequence of m edges (source node, target node, <e> marker) followed by a query specifying a root node r and two candidate destination nodes c1, c2. The model autoregressively generates C continuous thought vectors (d-dimensional latent vectors appended directly to the input sequence without projection to discrete tokens) that progressively expand the reachable frontier from r, then appends a special <A> token to trigger a final prediction of which candidate node is reachable. The continuous thoughts are the key structural difference from discrete CoT: they remain in continuous space, enabling superposition of multiple partial paths.

## Method

The paper analyzes the training dynamics of a two-layer transformer with linear attention and an index-matching reparameterization (W = Σ μ_v E(v)E_s(v)^T) on the graph reachability task, using curriculum learning where at stage c the model is supervised only on the (c+1)-th continuous thought. The central finding is that under the COCONUT loss (single-path demonstration), the index-matching logit μ — quantifying the strength of local search — converges to a finite fixed point μ*, in contrast to discrete CoT settings where attention logits diverge logarithmically. This bounded μ balances exploration and exploitation: it is large enough to exploit local graph structure (distinguishing frontier nodes by in-degree) but small enough to assign comparable weights to multiple plausible paths, naturally producing superposition. In the prediction stage, two signals — a residual carryover (μ_A) bringing explored nodes into the answer token and a candidate lift (μ_R) boosting the two candidates — grow at comparable rates so that the reachable node attains the highest logit. Experiments with a GPT-2-style model (d=768, 8 heads) on ProsQA confirm the theory: frontier-edge attention logits saturate under COCONUT but keep increasing under a BFS variant, and the model achieves 96.2% test accuracy.

## Results

The two-layer GPT-2-style transformer (d_model=768, n_heads=8) trained on a subset of ProsQA achieves 96.2% test accuracy on the graph reachability task after 350 epochs of multi-stage curriculum training. The key empirical validation is that under the COCONUT loss (single-path supervision), the index-matching logit difference between frontier and non-frontier edges saturates at approximately 60 after ~125 epochs in Stage 1, confirming the theoretical prediction of bounded attention logits. In contrast, under the COCONUT-BFS variant, the logit difference continues to grow without saturating, consistent with the theoretical prediction of logarithmic divergence. In the prediction stage, both the residual carryover (μ_A) and candidate lift (μ_R) logits increase rapidly and stabilize after roughly 5 epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is primarily a theoretical analysis with validation experiments rather than a method comparison study, so there are no traditional baseline methods to compare against. The central empirical comparison is between two training objectives: COCONUT (single-path supervision, Eq. 6) versus COCONUT-BFS (set-based supervision, Eq. 5). Under COCONUT, the index-matching logit grows and then saturates at a bounded value (~60), enabling the balanced exploration-exploitation tradeoff that produces superposition. Under COCONUT-BFS, the logit grows unboundedly (logarithmically), leading to over-confident commitment to a single path and loss of the superposition mechanism. This contrast directly validates Theorem 1, which proves bounded convergence under COCONUT (when d_star < d_max) versus logarithmic divergence under COCONUT-BFS. The paper also demonstrates 'length generalization': once superposition emerges in Stages 1–2, the model quickly generalizes to Stages 3 and 4 without explicit training at those depths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the COCONUT framework (Hao et al., 2024) and its theoretical expressivity analysis (Zhu et al., 2025) by answering the natural follow-up question: can the superposition-based solution be learned via gradient descent? It adopts the linear-attention reparameterization and index-matching framework from Nguyen & Nguyen-Tang (2025) but applies it to a continuous-thought setting, where the key departure from prior transformer training dynamics analyses (Tian et al., 2023; Nichani et al., 2024) is that attention logits remain bounded rather than diverging logarithmically. The paper positions itself as bridging the gap between a constructive existence proof and a mechanistic understanding of how gradient-based training naturally produces the superposition mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central insight—that a single-demonstration COCONUT loss naturally produces bounded index-matching logits, which in turn balance exploration and exploitation to yield superposition—provides a principled explanation for why continuous CoT enables parallel reasoning without explicit multi-path supervision. This could guide the design of more efficient training curricula and loss functions for scaling continuous CoT to harder tasks. However, the analysis is restricted to a simplified two-layer transformer with linear attention and a specific reparameterization, and the prediction-stage theory predicts unbounded logit growth while experiments show a plateau, leaving a gap between theory and practice. Open questions include whether the bounded-logit phenomenon persists in deeper, multi-head architectures with nonlinear attention, and how the mechanism interacts with the thought-generation stage during joint training.
