## Problem

Classical federated learning requires many communication rounds of model/gradient exchange, incurring high bandwidth costs and repeated privacy exposure, while existing one-shot FL methods either depend on public/proxy datasets, assume homogeneous client models, or require uploading full model parameters or activations. The paper addresses the gap of a strictly data-free, single-round federated scheme that handles strong non-IID heterogeneity while transmitting only minimal per-class statistics from clients.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260285/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Clients hold local images (32×32 CIFAR, ~73k SVHN) or text sequences, which are encoded by a frozen pretrained backbone (ResNet-18/50, MobileNetV2, EfficientNet-B0, VGG-16 for vision; DistilBERT for NLP) into fixed-dimension embeddings (d=512 or 768). These embeddings are projected via a shared public random-projection matrix R ∈ R^{d×k} (k≪d) before clients upload only additive sufficient statistics: per-class counts N_c, first moments A_c, and optionally second moments B, S_c, or diagonal sums D_c. The server outputs class labels through closed-form Gaussian discriminants (NB-diag, LDA, QDA) or lightweight trained heads (FisherMix, Proto-Hyper), with no raw data, gradients, or model parameters ever leaving the clients.

## Method

GH-OFL exploits the approximate class-conditional Gaussianity of pretrained embeddings to build classification heads entirely from aggregated per-class moments, eliminating the need for multi-round training or public data. The server instantiates three head families: (i) closed-form Gaussian discriminants (NB-diag with class-diagonal covariances, LDA with shared pooled covariance, QDA with class-specific full covariances) computed in closed form from the received statistics with shrinkage regularization; (ii) FisherMix, a linear classifier trained on synthetic class-conditional samples drawn in a low-dimensional Fisher subspace (top generalized eigenvectors of between/within scatter); and (iii) Proto-Hyper, a compact low-rank residual head h(z_f)=V₂U₁z_f that corrects systematic bias of a Gaussian base head via temperature-scaled knowledge distillation on the same synthetic samples. A public random projection compresses the embedding space before statistics are computed, reducing per-client upload to O(Ck+k²) and strengthening privacy, while secure aggregation ensures only global sums are revealed. The entire pipeline is partition-invariant: global moments, the Fisher subspace, and head parameters are independent of the Dirichlet non-IID parameter α and the number of clients, so accuracy remains stable under strong label skew without any additional communication.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, GH-LDA achieves 86.05% top-1 accuracy in a single communication round, matching or exceeding 50-round FedAvg (91.52% at α=0.50) and FedProx (91.74% at α=0.50) while requiring only one round. On CIFAR-100, FisherMix reaches 66.99% and GH-QDAfull 66.52%, surpassing 50-round FedAvg (68.55% at α=0.50) and all OFL baselines by 25+ points. On SVHN, GH-LDA attains 62.16%, outperforming all OFL baselines (best: Co-Boosting at 84.65% only at α=0.50, DENSE at 77.44% at α=0.50) but trailing 50-round FL methods (~92%). A key property is partition invariance: all GH-OFL heads report identical accuracy across Dirichlet α ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.50}, confirming robustness to non-IID skew without any client-side adaptation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest OFL baselines, GH-OFL methods dominate by wide margins: on CIFAR-10 (α=0.05), GH-LDA (86.05%) exceeds FedCGS (63.95%) by 22 points and Co-Boosting (44.37%) by 42 points; on CIFAR-100 (α=0.05), FisherMix (66.99%) surpasses FedCGS (39.95%) by 27 points and DENSE (14.31%) by 53 points. Against multi-round FL, GH-OFL achieves competitive or superior accuracy in a single round: on CIFAR-10 at α=0.50, GH-LDA (86.05%) is within 5.5 points of 50-round FedProx (91.74%) while using 1/50th the communication; on CIFAR-100 at α=0.50, FisherMix (66.99%) is within 1.6 points of 50-round FedAvg (68.55%). On SVHN, GH-OFL methods trail 50-round FL baselines by roughly 30 points (GH-LDA 62.16% vs. FedProx 92.17%), reflecting the dataset's near-linear separability where full-model fine-tuning retains an advantage. The partition invariance of GH-OFL (identical accuracy across all α) contrasts sharply with multi-round FL, where FedAvg drops from 91.52% to 77.42% on CIFAR-10 as α decreases from 0.50 to 0.05, and with OFL baselines like DENSE that degrade from 62.42% to 31.26% under the same shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-shot federated learning (OFL) paradigm initiated by Guan et al. (2025), who first proposed capturing global feature statistics for single-round FL, by developing a full family of Gaussian-head methods (GH-OFL) that go beyond simple moment aggregation. It departs from KD-based OFL (FedMD, FedDF) that require public or proxy datasets, from parameter/meta-learning OFL (Guha et al., Fallah et al.) that upload full model weights, and from generative OFL (DENSE, Co-Boosting) that synthesize surrogates, by instead assuming class-conditional Gaussianity of frozen encoder embeddings and building both closed-form discriminants (NB/LDA/QDA) and lightweight trainable heads (FisherMix, Proto-Hyper) entirely from per-class sufficient statistics. The Fisher-subspace synthesis and low-rank residual correction are novel architectural choices within this probabilistic, data-free framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GH-OFL offers a practically attractive one-shot FL protocol: communication reduces to O(Ck + k²) per client (independent of local sample size), the method is strictly data-free, and a clean partition-invariance theorem guarantees that accuracy is insensitive to both the number of clients and the Dirichlet non-IID parameter α. The striking NLP results—where standard OFL baselines collapse to near-random under strong non-IID (α=0.01) while GH-OFL maintains 88–89%—highlight a robustness advantage that multi-round FL does not enjoy in a single exchange. Open questions include whether the Gaussian assumption holds for highly multimodal or heavy-tailed per-class distributions, whether the approach extends to sequential or multimodal prediction tasks, and how performance scales when the frozen encoder is itself domain-mismatched (the Places365 experiments show QDA becomes critical under strong corruption).
