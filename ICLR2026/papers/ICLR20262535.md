## Problem

Existing training-free attention tuning methods (PASTA, Auto-PASTA, ACT) improve frozen LLMs by identifying and re-weighting task-specific 'important' tokens, but this reliance on external heuristics introduces bias, fails when token importance is ambiguous, and breaks under optimized attention kernels (FlashAttention, SDPA) that hide attention maps. The paper asks whether a single universal, task-agnostic token can serve as an effective control point instead.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard text prompt and, in supervised mode, a small labeled validation set (~500 samples) for calibrating the scaling factor and head selection; in unsupervised mode, it uses only unlabeled inputs to minimize average next-token prediction entropy. Output is identical to standard LLM inference (generated text, class labels, multiple-choice answers) with no changes to the decoding strategy or KV-cache. Implementation requires only four lines of modification to standard LlamaAttention code and is kernel-agnostic, working with both eager attention and optimized SDPA/FlashAttention backends.

## Method

ZeroTuning scales the attention logit of the initial token (e.g., <BOS>) by a factor γ and re-normalizes, which theoretically flattens (γ>1) or sharpens (γ<1) the attention distribution over all subsequent tokens while preserving their relative proportions. The paper proves the effect magnitude is monotonically increasing in the initial token's own attention weight, explaining why the attention-sink BOS token is an ideal lever. Rather than applying uniform scaling, the method profiles each attention head as 'up-effective' or 'down-effective' and applies head-specific γ values, which consistently outperforms uniform scaling. For optimized kernels where direct attention-score modification is infeasible, scaling is applied to the key state of the initial token, which the paper shows produces a similar (though more sensitive) effect. Two calibration variants are provided: supervised (grid search over γ maximizing validation accuracy) and unsupervised (selecting γ that minimizes average output entropy over unlabeled inputs), both requiring no parameter updates.

## Results

On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, ZeroTuning achieves an average classification accuracy of 71.44%, a +11.71% absolute gain over vanilla (59.59%), with peaks of +22.00% on SUBJ and +18.40% on SST-2. On multiple-choice tasks, it improves average accuracy by +2.64% (61.48% vs. 58.84%), with a notable +3.40% on LogiQA. On MT-Bench multi-round conversation, ZeroTuning raises Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct from 7.804 to 7.966 (+0.162) and Llama-2-13B-Chat from 6.650 to 6.916 (+0.266). Gains are consistent across all four tested models (Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-2-13B, Qwen-2-7B, DeepSeek-R1-14B) and 15 datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZeroTuning outperforms both baselines across all models and task categories. Against ACT (Yu et al., 2024), which down-weights non-initial sink tokens, ZeroTuning gains +11.33% average on Llama-3.1-8B classification (71.44 vs. 60.11) and +2.33% on multiple-choice (61.48 vs. 59.15); ACT is only evaluated on Llama-3.1-8B since it requires explicit attention maps unavailable under SDPA/FlashAttention. Against Auto-PASTA (Zhang et al., 2024a), which uses an LLM to identify and up-weight key tokens, ZeroTuning leads by +7.71% on Llama-3.1-8B classification (71.44 vs. 63.73), +1.30% on multiple-choice (61.48 vs. 60.18), and also wins on Qwen-2-7B (+2.62% classification, +0.83% MC) and DeepSeek-R1-14B (+2.83% classification, +1.89% MC). The largest single-dataset margin is +22.00% on SUBJ (Llama-3.1-8B), where ZeroTuning reaches 66.60% versus Auto-PASTA's 45.40% and ACT's 44.60%. Notably, ZeroTuning achieves these gains while tuning only one token (the initial/BOS token) per head, whereas both baselines require identifying and modifying multiple task-specific tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the token-level attention tuning lineage (PASTA, Auto-PASTA, ACT) by eliminating the need for task-specific token identification, instead targeting only the universal initial token (BOS). It repurposes the well-documented attention-sink phenomenon—previously studied as a structural artifact (Xiao et al., 2023; Barbero et al., 2025; Gu et al., 2024)—from a passive observation into an active, head-specific control point for inference-time performance. The theoretical framing (showing that the initial token's attention weight governs the magnitude of downstream redistribution) and the head-heterogeneity analysis (up-effective vs. down-effective heads) are novel contributions that bridge the mechanistic-interpretability literature with practical tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ZeroTuning offers an extremely low-cost (four lines of code, kernel-agnostic, no KV-cache changes) inference-time improvement that is broadly applicable across models and tasks, making it a practical tool for deployment settings where fine-tuning is infeasible. The unsupervised entropy-minimization variant further removes the dependency on labeled calibration data, opening the door to test-time adaptation. Key limitations include an inability to correct high-confidence factual errors and performance degradation under extreme scaling, positioning it as a complement to rather than replacement for fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the U-shaped entropy landscape enables more efficient search (e.g., Bayesian optimization), how the method interacts with stronger SFT/RLHF pipelines, and whether the head-specific structure generalizes across architectures.
