## Problem

Long video understanding is bottlenecked by the explosive growth of visual tokens, making full-token processing infeasible within LLM context limits (4K–32K tokens). Existing compression strategies—uniform sampling, clustering, and query-aware pruning—either discard rare but critical visual cues (e.g., small objects appearing in few frames) or incur high computational overhead, failing to simultaneously guarantee representativeness and diversity under a fixed token budget.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263495/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method receives visual token embeddings (cosine-normalized feature vectors) extracted by a frozen visual encoder from video frames, partitioned into temporal blocks of length T (default T=32), along with a user text prompt. It outputs a compressed subset of at most K tokens per block (compression ratios of 1/8, 1/16, or 1/32 of the original count), which is concatenated with the text prompt and fed into a video-LMM. The entire pipeline is training-free, model-agnostic, and query-agnostic, requiring no re-compression when the text query changes.

## Method

FLoC formulates token selection as maximizing the facility location submodular function f(S) = Σ_{v∈V} max_{u∈S} sim(v,u), which inherently balances coverage of all original tokens (representativeness) against penalizing redundant selections (diversity). To solve this NP-hard maximization efficiently, the paper employs the lazy greedy algorithm (Minoux, 1978), which exploits the diminishing-returns property of submodularity: previously computed marginal gains serve as valid upper bounds, so a priority queue allows skipping recomputation for candidates whose upper bound is already dominated. This yields O(nK) time complexity with a (1−1/e) ≈ 0.632 approximation guarantee, achieving roughly 10× speedup over clustering baselines like k-means and spectral clustering. The algorithm operates independently on each temporal block, enabling plug-and-play integration with any video-LMM (validated on Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3, Qwen2-VL, and LLaVA-Next Video) without architectural modification or retraining.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, FLoC achieves average accuracy of 62.57% at 1/8 compression, 60.09% at 1/16, and 57.45% at 1/32, consistently outperforming all 8 baselines across Video-MME, MLVU, LongVideoBench, and EgoSchema. On InternVL3-8B, FLoC reaches 65.65%, 63.79%, and 61.44% at the same compression ratios, again leading all competitors. In the extended temporal experiment (7200 frames compressed to 24,576 tokens), FLoC improves Qwen2.5-VL-7B by +1.21 points and Qwen2.5-VL-32B by +2.44 points over the 768-frame baseline. FLoC also achieves the lowest compression time (0.010–0.527 s) among all methods tested, with O(n·K) complexity versus O(n³) for spectral clustering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLoC outperforms all 8 baselines (TS-LLaVA, LongVU, DivPrune, Random, DyCoke, PruneVid, STTM, Scissor, and FastVID) across every compression ratio and both backbone models. The closest competitors are DyCoke and DivPrune: at 1/8 compression on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, FLoC leads DyCoke by +1.48 avg points (62.57 vs 61.09) and DivPrune by +1.63 (62.57 vs 60.94); on InternVL3-8B the gap over LongVU is +0.96 (65.65 vs 64.69). At the more aggressive 1/32 ratio, FLoC's advantage widens—beating DivPrune by +1.56 on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (57.45 vs 55.89) and +1.79 on InternVL3-8B (61.44 vs 59.65). PruneVid and STTM, which rely on graph-based token merging, show the largest gaps (up to 5+ points at 1/32), attributed to irreversible merging of dissimilar tokens. In terms of efficiency, FLoC (O(n·K)) is roughly 10× faster than K-Means and Spectral Clustering at T=32 (0.527 s vs 218 s and 21.1 s respectively), while maintaining higher accuracy than all clustering baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLoC repurposes the facility location function—a submodular objective originally developed for extractive document summarization (Lin & Bilmes, 2011; Lin et al., 2009)—to the problem of visual token selection for video LMMs, combined with the lazy greedy algorithm (Minoux, 1978) for efficient near-optimal subset selection. This departs from the dominant paradigms in the video token compression literature: clustering-based methods (k-means, spectral clustering) that bias toward dense feature regions, graph-based merging approaches (STTM, LLaVA-Scissor) vulnerable to the 'weak connection' problem, and query-aware or learned compression methods that sacrifice model-agnosticism or require retraining. The key conceptual contribution is framing token selection as a global coverage problem rather than a local similarity or density problem, ensuring both representativeness and diversity in a single principled objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, query-agnostic, and model-agnostic nature of FLoC makes it a practical plug-and-play component for deploying video LMMs in resource-constrained settings (surveillance, smart glasses, mobile robots) where per-query recompression or model-specific retraining is infeasible. The one-time compression paradigm—storing only compressed tokens rather than retaining all uncompressed tokens for future queries—yields significant memory savings in streaming or multi-query scenarios. A key open question is the content-dependent block length T: the paper acknowledges that a fixed T is a heuristic simplification, and developing adaptive segmentation (e.g., via scene detection) to automatically determine T remains future work. Additionally, whether the facility location objective generalizes to other token compression settings beyond video (e.g., high-resolution image tokens) is unexplored.
