## Problem

Current unsupervised SNNs rely on shallow architectures or localized plasticity rules, limiting their capacity to model long-range temporal dependencies and maintain temporal feature consistency across timesteps. This produces semantically unstable representations that impede the development of deep unsupervised SNNs for large-scale video data, and no systematic benchmarks exist for SNN self-supervised learning on medium-to-large video datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264085/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips as sequential frame sequences (UCF101/HMDB51: 128×128 crops, 16 frames at stride 2; MiniKinetics: 112×112 crops, 8 frames at stride 8), which are augmented into two views and processed frame-by-frame through a SEW ResNet SNN backbone. The SNN emits per-timestep features from LIF neurons that are temporally averaged into a single representation; downstream, these features support action classification (top-1/top-5 accuracy) and nearest-neighbor video retrieval (Recall@K).

## Method

PredNext is a plug-and-play auxiliary module that adds two temporal prediction heads to any existing self-supervised SNN framework: a Step Predictor (P_T) that maps current-timestep features to the next timestep's features, and a Clip Predictor (P_C) that maps temporally aggregated features to those of a subsequently sampled clip. Both predictors are 2-layer MLPs (512-dim hidden layer) and operate in a cross-view fashion—features from view i predict future features of view j with stop-gradient on the target—forcing the network to discard view-specific noise and retain semantically stable representations. The total loss is L = (1−α)·L_ssl + α·L_pred with α=0.5, where L_pred combines symmetric cosine-similarity losses over step and clip predictions. The key insight, grounded in predictive coding theory, is that semantically rich features naturally predict their future counterparts while low-level dynamic features cannot, so explicit temporal prediction implicitly enforces cross-temporal consistency without the performance degradation caused by direct consistency constraints.

## Results

On UCF101 (pretrained and fine-tuned), PredNext consistently improves all five self-supervised baselines: PredNextSimCLR reaches 59.47% top-1 (vs. 57.04% SimCLR), PredNextBYOL 58.58% (vs. 56.41%), PredNextBarlowTwins 59.76% (vs. 56.15%), and PredNextSimSiam 54.93% (vs. 50.81%). With ImageNet pretraining, PredNextSimSiam achieves 72.24% top-1 / 91.81% top-5 on UCF101, surpassing the supervised ImageNet-initialized baseline (64.42%). On MiniKinetics, PredNextBarlowTwins attains 62.75% top-1 (vs. 58.04% baseline), and on HMDB51, PredNextSimCLR reaches 31.58% top-1 (vs. 30.59%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PredNext outperforms all five SNN self-supervised baselines (SimCLR, MoCo, BYOL, BarlowTwins, SimSiam) by 2–5 points in top-1 accuracy on UCF101, with the largest gain over SimSiam (+4.12 pts) and MoCo (+5.28 pts). Against the video-specific baselines ρSimSiam and CVRL, PredNextSimSiam also leads by 2.88 and 2.12 points respectively. In the broader SNN/ANN comparison (Table 9), PredNextSimSiam(ImageNet) at 72.24% top-1 surpasses LSM+STDP (70.2%) and STS ResNet (42.1%), but falls short of the larger supervised SNNs ReSpike (77.5%) and SVFormer (80.2%), which benefit from stronger ImageNet pretraining (73.2% and 82.9% ImageNet accuracy vs. 63.2% for the SNN backbone). Notably, without any ImageNet pretraining, PredNextSimCLR (59.5%) already exceeds the ANN SimSiam baseline (49.3%) on UCF101, demonstrating the advantage of SNN temporal dynamics in self-supervised video learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two underdeveloped areas: deep SNN training and video self-supervised learning. Prior SNN unsupervised work (Diehl & Cook 2015, Kheradpisheh 2018, Dong 2023) relied on shallow architectures or local STDP plasticity rules on small DVS datasets, while prior video self-supervised methods (DPC, MemDPC, CPC-like approaches) required complex auxiliary temporal aggregator networks. PredNext departs from both lines by introducing a lightweight, plug-and-play cross-view temporal prediction module (inspired by predictive coding theory) that integrates with standard SSL objectives (SimCLR, MoCo, BYOL, SimSiam, BarlowTwins) without modifying their core architecture, and by establishing the first systematic benchmarks for deep SNN self-supervised learning on medium-to-large video datasets (UCF101, HMDB51, MiniKinetics) rather than conventional DVS data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive benchmark suite for deep SNN self-supervised video learning, filling a gap that has limited progress in this area. The finding that explicitly forcing temporal consistency via a direct loss term degrades downstream performance—while the indirect prediction-based approach improves both consistency and accuracy—challenges the intuitive assumption that consistency should be directly constrained. Limitations include evaluation only on medium-scale datasets (up to 80K clips), exclusion of optical flow and other modalities, and the observation that SNN pretrained weights still lag behind ANN counterparts (63.2% vs. 73.2% on ImageNet), leaving open whether the approach scales to truly large-scale video (e.g., full Kinetics-400/600) and whether the plug-and-play design transfers to non-ResNet SNN backbones or event-based data.
