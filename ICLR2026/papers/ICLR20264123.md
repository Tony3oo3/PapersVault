## Problem

Existing provable analyses of multimodal learning from the generalization error perspective are almost exclusively derived under the late fusion (LF) framework, leaving intermediate fusion (IF) theoretically underexplored despite its empirical superiority. This paper asks whether a comprehensive MML approach can be designed with sufficient theoretical justification spanning both fusion types, and what specific constraints and mechanisms are needed to make IF provably advantageous.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264123/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes modality-specific latent features (e.g., image embeddings from CNN/Transformer encoders, text embeddings from BERT, depth features, or knowledge graph entity embeddings) that are projected to a shared dimension d via linear transforms. Fusion occurs in the latent space via weighted summation z = Σ w^m z_m, followed by a single shared linear target mapping g_θ(z) that produces class logits, entity ranking scores, or scene labels. Fusion weights w^m can be static constants or dynamic functions of the input, and the method is designed as a plug-and-play module that can be grafted onto existing IF-based architectures.

## Method

IID (Intermediate Fusion with Informatic Constraint and Distribution Coherence) is built on two theoretical pillars: (1) a fine-grained dimensional decomposition proving that IF with a linear target mapping parameterized by θ ∈ Λ achieves lower prediction and generalization error than LF (Theorems 1–2), and (2) a K-Lipschitz-based generalization bound (Theorem 3) identifying distribution incoherence between unimodal and fused feature distributions as a reducible error term. Practically, the informatic constraint implements an information-bottleneck objective that maximizes I(z;y) − Σ I(z_m;y) via variational approximation with KL regularization, steering the shared linear mapping toward the theoretically optimal θ*. The distribution cohering module minimizes pairwise Wasserstein distances between modality features, computed efficiently by first applying FFT to exploit frequency-domain sparsity, then projecting through a RIP-preserving Gaussian random matrix to reduce dimensionality, and finally solving a Lagrange-relaxed Sinkhorn problem that replaces hard marginal constraints with KL penalties to tolerate semantic loss from dimensionality reduction.

## Results

On four vision-language datasets, IID-P (dynamic fusion with PDF's weights) achieves the best average accuracy: 81.13% on MVSA-Single, 71.23% on MVSA-Multiple, 86.88% on HFM, and 93.73% on Food101, outperforming the strongest LF baseline PDF by 1.19, 1.69, 0.85, and 0.41 percentage points respectively. On scene recognition, IID-P reaches 72.04% on NYU Depth V2 and 62.99% on SUN RGB-D. For link prediction on multimodal knowledge graphs, integrating IID into OTKGE yields SOTA MRR of 0.855 (FB-IMG) and 0.932 (WN9-IMG). All improvements are statistically significant (p < 0.05 via Student t-test).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IID-P consistently outperforms PDF (its LF counterpart with identical fusion weights) across all four vision-language datasets, with the largest gap on MVSA-Multiple (+1.69%) and MVSA-Single (+1.19%), confirming that the IF framework with informatic constraint and distribution cohering yields genuine gains beyond fusion-weight design. Similarly, IID-Q surpasses QMF by 1.95% on MVSA-Multiple and 1.95% on MVSA-Single. The static-fusion variant IID-L trails its LF counterpart (Late-fusion) on MVSA-Single (77.78 vs. 76.88) but the gap narrows or reverses on other datasets, suggesting the informatic constraint and distribution cohering modules are most beneficial under dynamic fusion. On scene recognition (Table 3), IID-P beats PDF by 0.67% on NYU Depth V2 and 0.65% on SUN RGB-D. For link prediction, integrating IID into OTKGE improves MRR from 0.843 to 0.855 on FB-IMG and from 0.923 to 0.932 on WN9-IMG, establishing SOTA on both multimodal knowledge graph benchmarks. All reported improvements are statistically significant (p < 0.05), ruling out random variation as the source of gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the provable multimodal learning (MML) line initiated by QMF (Zhang et al., 2023b) and PDF (Cao et al., 2024), which derived generalization error bounds exclusively for the late fusion (LF) framework. The key departure is providing the first theoretical justification for intermediate fusion (IF) superiority over LF under a specific informatic constraint on the linear target mapping, drawing on the information bottleneck framework (Tishby et al., 2000) to operationalize this constraint. For the distribution cohering component, the paper repurposes the Sinkhorn algorithm (Cuturi, 2013) for Wasserstein distance computation but addresses its prohibitive cost on high-dimensional features via a novel FFT + Restricted Isometry Property (RIP) dimensionality reduction pipeline inspired by Wright & Ma (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a notable theoretical gap: IF has been empirically dominant in multimodal fusion but lacked the kind of generalization-error guarantees that LF-based methods (QMF, PDF) already enjoyed. The practical contribution of an efficient Wasserstein distance estimator via FFT+RIP is broadly applicable beyond MML. However, the theoretical results are restricted to linear target mappings and a specific parameter set Λ, and the informatic constraint is acknowledged as a necessary-but-not-sufficient condition, leaving open whether the IF superiority claim holds for non-linear heads or in the absence of the constraint. The performance gains over strong baselines (PDF, QMF) are modest (≈1–2 points), and the plug-and-play integration into existing IF models (OTKGE, MMKRL) suggests the method's value is more as a regularization module than a standalone architecture.
