## Problem

Vision foundation models achieve strong results on standard benchmarks, but it remains unclear whether they genuinely understand 3D geometric properties such as symmetry, convexity, and shape complexity, or merely exploit 2D texture patterns. Existing 3D evaluation datasets (Objaverse, GSO, NAVI) contain arbitrary objects without the precise, unambiguous ground-truth annotations needed to probe fine-grained geometric reasoning. GIQ fills this gap by providing a structured polyhedron-based benchmark where every shape has exact, mathematically defined symmetry groups, face types, and topological properties.

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides 224 unique polyhedra rendered synthetically at 256×256 (Mitsuba PBR, 20 viewpoints each) and photographed in the wild at 6000×4000 (Nikon D3500, ~40 views per shape across indoor and outdoor conditions). For reconstruction, a single preprocessed image (background removed, centered) yields a full 3D mesh; for symmetry detection, a single image yields binary labels for central point reflection, 4-fold, and 5-fold rotational symmetry; for mental rotation, a synthetic-wild image pair yields a same/different judgment; and for zero-shot classification, an image plus the prompt 'What is the name of this polyhedron?' yields a shape name. Wild images retain natural backgrounds and lighting for all tasks except reconstruction, which requires clean inputs to match baseline assumptions.

## Method

GIQ is a benchmark and empirical study rather than a new architecture. The dataset spans eight polyhedral families (Platonic, Archimedean, Catalan, Johnson, Stellations, Kepler-Poinsot, Compounds, Uniform non-convex) with 224 total shapes, rendered via Mitsuba's physically-based renderer (two-sided diffuse BRDF, 1024 low-discrepancy samples, direct integrator for sharp face contrast) and captured as hand-assembled paper models in diverse real-world settings. Four evaluation protocols probe distinct dimensions of geometric intelligence: (1) zero-shot monocular 3D reconstruction with Shap-E, Stable Fast 3D, and OpenLRM; (2) 3D symmetry detection via linear and nonlinear probes on 12 image encoders (CLIP, DINOv2, SigLIP, MAE, DUSt3R, MASt3R, VGGT, etc.) trained on synthetic embeddings and tested on wild images with 5-fold cross-validation over shape identities; (3) a Mental Rotation Test using absolute-difference embedding combinations with nonlinear probes, evaluated on both trivial and hard (geometrically similar) shape pairs; and (4) zero-shot polyhedron classification by frontier VLMs (ChatGPT o3/o4-mini, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Claude 3.7 Sonnet) and geometry-native 3D models (LLaVA-3D, ShapeLLM, PointBind/PointLLM), with ablations for Chain-of-Thought prompting and multi-view inputs. A 42-participant human study establishes a behavioral baseline for the mental rotation task.

## Results

On 3D symmetry detection (linear probing, trained on synthetic, evaluated on wild images), DINOv2 achieves up to 93% balanced accuracy for 4-fold rotational symmetry, the strongest result across all 12 tested encoders. On the hard Mental Rotation Test (synthetic-wild pairs), SigLIP with a non-linear probe reaches 69% accuracy and DINOv2 reaches 67%, matching the human average of 68.05%, though 68% of individual human participants outperformed the best model. In zero-shot polyhedron classification, all frontier VLMs (ChatGPT o3, o4-mini-high, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Claude 3.7 Sonnet) fall below 20% accuracy on Catalan, Johnson, and non-convex uniform solids, while monocular 3D reconstruction methods (Shap-E, Stable Fast 3D, OpenLRM) all fail to recover basic geometric properties even for a cube on wild images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This benchmark paper evaluates 21+ models across four geometric reasoning tasks rather than proposing a new method. In 3D symmetry detection, DINOv2 (self-supervised) outperforms all supervised encoders (CLIP, SigLIP, DeiT III, ConvNext, SAM) and multi-view geometry models (VGGT, DUSt3R, MASt3R), reaching 93% on 4-fold rotation with wild images despite training only on synthetic data. In the Mental Rotation Test, SigLIP and DINOv2 with non-linear probes (69% and 67%) match the human average (68.05%), but the majority of other encoders fall below 60%, and 68% of human participants beat the best model. For zero-shot classification, geometry-native 3D models (LLaVA-3D, ShapeLLM, PointBind/PointLLM) do not outperform generalist frontier VLMs (ChatGPT o3, o4-mini-high, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Claude 3.7 Sonnet), all of which struggle below 20% on complex polyhedra. In monocular 3D reconstruction, Shap-E, Stable Fast 3D, and OpenLRM all fail qualitatively on wild images and complex shapes, with no method reliably recovering even a cube's axis-aligned faces and uniform edge lengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mental rotation evaluation framework of Ramakrishnan et al. (2024), which used Lego-like blocks, to polyhedral shapes with well-defined symmetry groups and geometric complexity hierarchies. It also builds on the line of work probing 3D awareness in foundation models (El Banani et al., 2024; Man et al., 2024), but shifts focus from depth estimation and scene registration to intrinsic geometric properties—symmetry, convexity, and topological regularity—where unambiguous ground truth is available. Unlike prior large-scale 3D datasets (Objaverse, GSO, OmniObject3D) that lack fine-grained geometric annotations, GIQ provides precise symmetry group labels and structured complexity categories, enabling targeted evaluation of geometric reasoning rather than general 3D perception.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GIQ provides the first structured, polyhedron-based diagnostic for geometric intelligence, revealing that implicit geometric encoding (e.g., DINOv2 capturing 4-fold symmetry via linear probes at 93%) does not transfer to explicit geometric reasoning (mental rotation, shape classification), where even frontier VLMs fall below 20% on complex classes. The finding that monocular 3D reconstruction fails on basic Platonic solids despite training on millions of 3D assets suggests that scale alone without mathematically exact training data is insufficient for geometric fidelity. Open questions include whether geometry-aware training objectives or explicit 3D constraint enforcement can close the gap, and whether the polyhedral findings generalize to organic shapes. The benchmark's structured ontology also enables automatic generation of Chain-of-Thought training data for future geometry-aware model development.
