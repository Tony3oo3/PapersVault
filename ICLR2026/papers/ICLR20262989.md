## Problem

Existing tools for visual assessment and creative assistance in art and design rely on large-scale annotations and black-box deep representations, making it difficult to trace why an image is compositionally good or to produce actionable, task-specific improvement suggestions. The paper addresses this by seeking a recomposable, interpretable structured representation that captures how visual elements are organized (parts, relations, geometry) and can yield both a unified quality score and executable structural guidance with few-shot data efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (professional photographs as labeled seeds and an unlabeled pool for semi-supervised expansion) after YOLOv11 object detection and segmentation, producing a response matrix R ∈ [0,1]^{N×D} of feature responses. At test time, given a target image and a learned theme template, it outputs a decomposable consistency score (log-likelihood gain), a parse tree marking activated terminals and satisfied/violated AND/OR constraints, a geometric configuration (positions, scales, orientations), and textual-plus-visual prescriptive edits. The same template also serves as a lightweight structural conditioning signal for AIGC generators (e.g., Midjourney) and as a scoring objective for layout optimization.

## Method

The core representation is a two-level AND-OR template regulated by a stochastic context-free grammar: at the object level, AND nodes encode jointly-satisfied relational/geometric constraints, OR nodes enumerate structural variants, and terminals are Photography Art Templates (PATs) of image primitives and histogram descriptors with position/scale/orientation attributes; at the scene level, learned object templates are reused as scene terminals to compose inter-object constraints without pixel-level combinatorial blow-up. Under a maximum-entropy log-linear model, a unified consistency score is defined as the log-likelihood gain of the image under the template relative to a reference distribution, decomposing additively into per-terminal (object, relation, geometry) contributions that map directly to evidence-to-prescription adjustments. Learning proceeds via an EM-type block-pursuit: the E-step performs terminal matching, relation instantiation, and geometric evaluation within local windows, while the M-step adds OR branches or refines AND constraints by maximizing penalized marginal gain with sparsity and local mutual-exclusivity regularization to suppress combinatorial explosion. A semi-supervised structural expansion uses a dual threshold (matching gain plus structural consistency) to bootstrap new branches from unlabeled high-quality images, with conflict resolution and early stopping for robustness.

## Results

On AVA (14 themes, 280 images per theme for training), the compositional AND-OR template achieves 85.65% accuracy with only 2.3×10³ parameters and 6.7×10⁴ FLOPs, while attaining the highest SRCC of 0.8419 with human annotations—outperforming VGG19 (0.7506), ResNet50 (0.7842), and ViT-B/16 (0.7590). On a 10-class Places365 subset, the template method records 23.44% Top-1 error and 8.03% Top-5 error, matching or exceeding deep baselines (Places-365-CNN: 23.27%/8.48%; ResNet: 23.35%/8.61%; SENet: 23.67%/8.26%) at orders-of-magnitude lower parameter and data costs. Late fusion of template features with deep features further lifts ViT-B/16 to 86.20% accuracy and ResNet50 to 85.87% on AVA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VGG19, the template method gains +7.57% accuracy and +0.0913 SRCC while using roughly 60× fewer parameters (2.3×10³ vs. 1.39×10⁸). Against ResNet50, accuracy is essentially tied (85.65% vs. 85.71%), but the template's SRCC is substantially higher (0.8419 vs. 0.7842), indicating stronger alignment with human aesthetic ratings, and it requires ~10× fewer parameters. Against ViT-B/16, the template wins on both accuracy (+0.27%) and SRCC (+0.0829) with ~37× fewer parameters. Late fusion (deep features + template configuration features) consistently improves all three deep backlines, confirming complementarity. On Places365, the template achieves the best Top-5 error (8.03%) among all methods, while its Top-1 error (23.44%) is within 0.17% of the best deep baseline (Places-365-CNN at 23.27%), all without pretraining on large-scale data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the AND-OR Template (AOT) framework of Si & Zhu (2013) from the object level to the scene level, reusing learned object templates as scene terminals composed under inter-object AND/OR constraints. The unified log-linear consistency score inherits its theoretical grounding from the maximum-entropy FRAME formulation (Zhu et al., 1998) and the stochastic image grammar tradition (Zhu et al., 2007), while the terminal representation (Photography Art Templates) descends from Hybrid Image Templates (Si & Zhu, 2011). The key departure from prior AOT/HIT work is the explicit scene-level compositionality, the decomposable evidence-to-prescription scoring, and a semi-supervised structural expansion mechanism that bootstraps new branches from unlabeled data—none of which were present in the original object-recognition AOT setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a compelling data/parameter-efficiency story (≈2.3×10³ parameters versus 10⁷–10⁸ for deep baselines) while achieving comparable accuracy and notably higher SRCC with human aesthetic ratings, suggesting that explicit part-relation-geometry structure captures what humans actually attend to in composition. The term-wise score decomposition provides an auditable evidence-to-prescription pathway that black-box predictors cannot match, making it attractive for creative-assistance and layout-design applications. However, the evidence base is narrow: a 750-image curated dataset, a 10-class Places365 subset, and reliance on expert curation limit generalizability claims. Open questions include whether the approach scales to open-vocabulary or cross-cultural settings, how sensitive the dual-threshold semi-supervised stage is to hyperparameters, and whether the structural prior degrades when lighting, material, or portrait factors dominate aesthetic judgment.
