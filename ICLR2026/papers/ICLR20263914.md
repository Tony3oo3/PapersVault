## Problem

Existing VQA benchmarks evaluate models on clear, unambiguous image-question pairs, leaving unexplored the ability to handle the varying degrees of ambiguity encountered in real-world visual queries. Prior work on ambiguous VQA adopts a rigid binary answer-or-ask strategy, failing to capture how humans adaptively choose among multiple response strategies—direct answering, contextual inference, enumerating alternatives, or requesting clarification—depending on the nature and degree of ambiguity.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (sourced from COCO for training/evaluation and Open Images V7 for generalization testing) paired with a visual question that may contain ambiguous referential terms (e.g., 'this', 'that', 'it'). The output is a single strategy-aware text response whose form is determined by the ambiguity level: a definitive answer (Level 0), a context-resolved answer that first identifies the referent (Level 1), a natural-language enumeration of 2–3 plausible targets (Level 2), or a polite clarification request (Level 3). The AQUA dataset contains 7.2K image-question-answer triplets (3.6K train / 3.6K eval), evenly balanced at 0.9K per level, with the evaluation split validated by dual-annotator human review on MTurk.

## Method

AQUA is constructed from COCO images using bounding-box annotations to control ambiguity: object saliency is scored as a weighted combination of area ratio (0.7) and normalized center distance (0.3), and images are assigned to levels based on the count of objects exceeding a 0.6 saliency threshold (0, 1, 2–3, or ≥5 salient objects). GPT-5 generates level-specific Q-A pairs, which pass through a three-stage GPT-5-mini filtering pipeline (level consistency, best-fit validation, real-world quality check). For training, the paper fine-tunes Qwen2.5-VL-3B and InternVL3-2B in two stages: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on the full training split to teach the strategy space, followed by Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with an LLM-as-a-judge reward that assigns 1 for correct strategy with no factual distortion, 1−λ (λ=0.3) for correct strategy with detected hallucination, and 0 otherwise. This two-stage pipeline enables small open-source VLMs to reach ~80–86% overall strategic accuracy, substantially outperforming larger open-source and closed-source baselines (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Flash) that top out around 25–42%.

## Results

On the AQUA benchmark, the proposed Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Tuned (SFT+GRPO) achieves 86.28% overall strategic accuracy with 81.06% factual groundedness, while InternVL3-2B-Tuned reaches 79.11% overall strategic accuracy with 80.44% groundedness. These small tuned models substantially outperform all zero-shot, CoT, and strategy-prompting baselines, whose overall strategic accuracy ranges from 22.86% (GPT-5 zero-shot) to 42.25% (GPT-5 with strategy prompting). On the generalization test with Open Images V7, Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Tuned maintains 83.81% overall strategic accuracy, confirming robustness beyond the COCO-based training distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against Qwen2.5-VL (3B, 7B, 72B) and InternVL3 (2B, 8B, 78B) open-source models as well as GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Flash closed-source models under zero-shot, CoT, and strategy-prompting settings. The 3B Qwen2.5-VL tuned on AQUA achieves 86.28% overall strategic accuracy, surpassing the 72B Qwen2.5-VL with strategy prompting (42.22%) by over 44 points, and GPT-5 with strategy prompting (42.25%) by over 44 points. Similarly, the 2B InternVL3 tuned model (79.11%) outperforms the 78B InternVL3 with strategy prompting (30.61%) by nearly 49 points. Notably, all baseline models—regardless of scale—show a strong bias toward Level 0 (direct answering) predictions, with Level 1–3 strategic accuracy consistently below 10% in zero-shot settings, whereas the tuned models achieve 59–86% across all ambiguity levels. Factual groundedness remains high across all models (76–99%), confirming that the primary failure mode is strategic rather than perceptual.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the emerging line of work on ambiguity in visual question answering (ClearVQA, VAGUE, Focus Ambiguity) by replacing the binary answer-or-ask paradigm with a four-level strategy taxonomy (direct answer, context-inferred answer, enumerate alternatives, request clarification). The training pipeline borrows GRPO from the DeepSeekMath line of work, applying it not to mathematical reasoning but to strategy selection in VLMs. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against prior uncertainty-handling approaches (UNK-VQA, abstention training) that treat the problem as a single threshold decision, arguing instead that humans deploy a spectrum of strategies depending on the degree and nature of ambiguity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Flash achieve only ~23–27% strategic accuracy while 3B/2B models fine-tuned on 3.6K samples reach ~80–86% suggests that strategic ambiguity handling is a distinct capability not captured by scale or general instruction tuning. However, the evaluation rests on a GPT-5-generated synthetic dataset judged by GPT-5-mini, which limits confidence in the absolute numbers and raises questions about whether the four-level taxonomy captures the full complexity of real-world ambiguity. Open questions include whether the strategy framework generalizes to multi-turn dialogue, whether the level boundaries (especially between Level 2 and 3) are stable across annotators, and whether the approach transfers to non-COCO visual domains without retraining.
