## Problem

Most symbolic regression methods search in a discrete equation space where structural edits (measured by edit distance) bear no reliable relationship to numerical behavior, leaving fitting-error feedback too noisy to guide exploration. This forces search to rely on random mutations and backtracking, producing unstable trajectories and high time complexity. The paper asks whether a continuous, well-organized latent space can provide meaningful directional signals for equation discovery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263014/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes m numerical (x, y) sample pairs, tokenized into base-10 floating-point components (sign, mantissa 0-9999, exponent E-100 to E+100) and embedded into a fixed-length sequence. The prior branch encodes these into a Gaussian distribution N(mu_0, sigma_0^2 I) in a 512-dimensional latent space, which is then refined by CMA-ES; the final output is a decoded symbolic expression whose constants are further polished by BFGS. During training, the posterior branch additionally consumes the ground-truth equation (linearized in prefix order, padded to match the numerical sequence length) to supervise reconstruction.

## Method

GenSR pretrains a dual-branch Conditional VAE with a shared 8-layer Transformer encoder-decoder: the posterior branch (input: numerical samples X + equation F) learns q(z|X,F), while the prior branch (input: X only) learns p(z|X). Joint optimization of cross-entropy reconstruction and KL divergence between the two branches yields a generative latent space that is globally continuous in symbolic structure (structurally similar equations cluster adjacently) and locally smooth in numerical features (nearby latent points decode to equations with similar numerical behavior). At inference, the prior branch provides a coarse localization distribution, and a modified CMA-ES (diagonal covariance assumption + top-k variance update) contracts this distribution toward the optimum, exploiting the latent space's numerical smoothness for directional search. From a Bayesian perspective, the entire pipeline corresponds to maximizing the ELBO of p(Equation|Numerical), with CVAE training optimizing the bound and CMA-ES performing approximate posterior refinement.

## Results

On the noise-free Strogatz dataset, GenSR achieves R²=0.9918 with only 33 s runtime and equation complexity of 20.53 tokens, outperforming all baselines on the time-complexity axis while matching or exceeding their accuracy. On the noise-free Feynman dataset, GenSR reaches R²=0.9872 in 192 s with the lowest complexity (22.94 tokens), compared to GP-GOMEA (R²=0.9956, 5030 s, 34.57 tokens) and SNIP (R²=0.985, 1204 s, 31.71 tokens). On the Black-box dataset, GenSR attains the highest R² (0.8416) with the lowest complexity (35.05 tokens) among all 24 baselines. GenSR consistently lies on the rank-1 Pareto front across all three datasets, jointly optimizing accuracy, simplicity, and speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenSR dominates on the time-complexity Pareto front: on Strogatz it is 15× faster than SNIP (33 s vs. 509 s) at comparable R² (0.9918 vs. 0.9952), and 84× faster than SBP-GP (33 s vs. 17591 s) while achieving higher R². On Feynman, GenSR is 6.3× faster than SNIP (192 s vs. 1204 s) with slightly lower R² (0.9872 vs. 0.9850) but substantially lower complexity (22.94 vs. 31.71 tokens). Among evolutionary methods, GenSR outperforms GP-GOMEA in runtime (192 s vs. 5030 s on Feynman) and complexity (22.94 vs. 34.57), though GP-GOMEA achieves marginally higher R² (0.9956 vs. 0.9872). On the Black-box dataset, GenSR achieves the best R² (0.8416) and lowest complexity (35.05) among all 24 baselines, surpassing Operon (R²=0.7945, Complx=65.69) by 4.7% in accuracy and 47% in simplicity. Neural baselines E2ESR and SNIP are fast but sacrifice accuracy on Strogatz (E2ESR R²=0.5341) or require much longer runtimes (SNIP 509 s). The paper's key claim is that GenSR is the only method on the rank-1 Pareto front across all three metrics simultaneously, whereas each baseline excels on at most one axis.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenSR departs from the dominant paradigm of searching discrete symbolic spaces via genetic programming or MCTS by reparameterizing equations into a continuous generative latent space. It builds on the dual-branch CVAE architecture of Sohn et al. (2015) and the numerical tokenization scheme of E2ESR (Kamienny et al., 2022), but introduces a critical distinction: the latent space is explicitly *generative* (all points decode to valid equations) rather than *discriminative* (as in SNIP's contrastive embeddings), and is jointly shaped for symbolic continuity and local numerical smoothness. The search stage repurposes CMA-ES (Hansen, 2016) with diagonal-covariance and top-k variance modifications, and the entire pipeline is unified under a Bayesian ELBO objective that reframes SR as maximizing p(Equation | Numerical data).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The generative-vs-discriminative latent space distinction provides a clear design principle for continuous search over combinatorial objects, potentially transferable beyond symbolic regression to other structured prediction tasks. The Bayesian ELBO formulation gives theoretical grounding that prior heuristic SR methods lack, and the latent space's demonstrated transferability to function-family classification suggests broader utility. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional inputs (beyond D≤10), whether richer generative priors (diffusion models, flow matching) could replace the Gaussian CVAE, and how to incorporate domain-specific physical constraints directly into the latent geometry.
