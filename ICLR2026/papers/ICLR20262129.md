## Problem

Existing feature caching methods for Diffusion Transformers apply a uniform prediction strategy across all hidden dimensions, implicitly assuming a single unified temporal evolution. However, DiT feature spaces are high-dimensional and heterogeneous: some dimensions exhibit oscillatory or stiff dynamics while others evolve smoothly, making a one-size-fits-all solver suboptimal and unstable under aggressive acceleration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262129/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

HyCa is a training-free, inference-time acceleration layer that wraps around any pre-trained DiT (FLUX.1-dev at 1024×1024, Qwen-Image at 1328×1328, HunyuanVideo at 480×640×65 frames, Qwen-Image-Edit at variable resolution). It takes the same inputs as the base model (text prompts, or reference image + text prompt for editing) and produces the same outputs (images, videos, edited images), but skips redundant transformer block computations at designated timesteps by predicting residual features from cached values. The only additional requirement is a one-time offline probing pass (~1 second) per model to determine solver assignments.

## Method

HyCa reframes feature caching as hybrid ODE solving: it models the temporal evolution of each hidden feature dimension as a trajectory governed by an implicit ODE, then groups dimensions into clusters via k-means on dynamic descriptors (jerk ratio, curvature ratio, direction consistency, spectral flatness, etc.). For each cluster, it selects the optimal numerical solver from a pool of five methods (Runge-Kutta, Adams-Bashforth, Taylor extrapolation, BDF, Adams-Moulton) by minimizing next-step prediction error during a single offline probe. A critical empirical finding is that cluster assignments are input-invariant—stable across prompts, timesteps, resolutions, and even LoRA fine-tuning (ARI > 0.8)—enabling 'One-Time Choosing, All-Time Solving' with zero runtime overhead. At inference, each cluster's assigned solver predicts the next residual from cached features dimension-wise, bypassing expensive block computations while the outer diffusion sampler and residual pipeline remain unchanged.

## Results

HyCa achieves near-lossless acceleration across four diffusion transformer models without retraining: 5.55× FLOPs speedup on FLUX.1-dev (ImageReward 0.9895 vs. original 0.9898), 6.25× on Qwen-Image (ImageReward 1.0811), 5.56× on HunyuanVideo (VBench 80.25 vs. 80.66), and 6.24× on Qwen-Image-Edit (GEdit-EN OS 7.42). On distilled models, HyCa reaches 24.4× speedup on FLUX.1-schnell (ImageReward 0.9592, PSNR 34.37) and 12.2× on Qwen-Image-Lightning (ImageReward 1.2201, CLIP 35.07), confirming full compatibility with step distillation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At the ~5.5× FLOPs speedup level on FLUX.1-dev, HyCa (ImageReward 0.9895) nearly matches the original model (0.9898) while all baselines degrade substantially: TaylorSeer drops to 0.9698 (−2.02%), DuCa to 0.8382 (−15.33%), TeaCache to 0.8379 (−15.36%), FORA to 0.7760 (−21.62%), and ToCa to 0.7155 (−27.70%). On Qwen-Image at 6.25× speedup, HyCa achieves ImageReward 1.0811 versus TaylorSeer's 0.9113 and FORA's 0.4781, a gap of 17–56%. On HunyuanVideo at 5.56× FLOPs speedup, HyCa scores 80.25 on VBench compared to TaylorSeer's 79.93 (at only 5.00×) and TeaCache's 79.36. On Qwen-Image-Edit at 6.24× speedup, HyCa attains GEdit-EN Overall Score 7.42, exceeding the original model (7.54) by a small margin while TaylorSeer drops to 6.31 and FORA to 7.06. The consistent pattern across all four tasks and both image and video modalities confirms that dimension-wise hybrid solver assignment substantially outperforms uniform caching strategies (FORA, TaylorSeer) and token-wise dynamic caching (ToCa, DuCa).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature caching line of work for diffusion transformers (DeepCache, FORA, ToCa, TaylorSeer, FoCa) by reframing the problem through the lens of hybrid ODE solvers from numerical analysis. While prior methods like TaylorSeer and FoCa already cast feature caching as ODE solving, they apply a single uniform solver across all feature dimensions. HyCa departs from this by modeling hidden feature evolution as a mixture of ODEs—clustering dimensions by their temporal dynamics (via k-means on descriptors like curvature ratio and jerk ratio) and assigning a distinct solver (RK, AB, BDF, AM, Taylor) to each cluster, drawing directly on the hybrid solver literature from computational mathematics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: HyCa is training-free, requires only a ~1-second offline solver-selection pass, and achieves near-lossless 5–6× speedup across four diverse architectures (FLUX, HunyuanVideo, Qwen-Image, Qwen-Image-Edit) while remaining compatible with both distillation (up to 24×) and LoRA fine-tuning—settings where prior caching methods degrade. The key open question is whether the mixture-of-ODE perspective generalizes beyond DiTs to other generative architectures (e.g., autoregressive or flow-matching models), and whether learning-based clustering or solver selection could further reduce the residual quality gap at extreme compression ratios. A limitation is that the solver pool is fixed and hand-designed; adaptive or learned solver composition remains unexplored.
