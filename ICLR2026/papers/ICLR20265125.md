## Problem

Recent spiking transformers (e.g., SDT-V3 with 173M parameters and 28.4B synaptic ops/s) achieve strong accuracy but demand substantial computational resources, limiting edge deployment. Existing token pruning methods for SNNs either modify the original architecture (adding anchor tokens, trainable halting modules) or require full retraining, increasing application costs and reducing generalizability across architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265125/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on spiking feature maps of shape T×H×W×D (time steps × spatial grid × channel dimension) produced by a pre-trained spiking transformer, pruning tokens at each of the L transformer blocks during forward propagation. It accepts standard inputs—224×224 RGB images, 32×32 CIFAR images, event streams, or text sequences—and produces the same outputs as the unpruned model (class labels, pixel-wise masks, bounding boxes, tracking boxes, or text scores) with reduced block-level operations and power. A key constraint is that the spatial structure of the feature map must be preserved at every block boundary, which is critical for feature-variant architectures with embedded convolutions.

## Method

TP-Spikformer combines two components: (1) IRToP, a heuristic spatiotemporal scoring criterion that assigns each token a retention score by summing a spatial saliency term (1 minus cosine similarity between the token and the mean of its k×k neighborhood) and a temporal variation term (L1 norm of the difference between consecutive time steps), then selecting the top-K scoring tokens as informative; and (2) IR-Arc, a block-level early stopping architecture where informative tokens undergo full spiking self-attention and MLP computation while uninformative tokens are passed through unchanged and reassembled into their original spatial positions. This early-stopping-and-reassembly strategy, rather than direct token removal, preserves the square feature map structure required by convolutional layers in hierarchical architectures like QKFormer and SDT-V3. The method is entirely training-free—no new parameters, no architectural modification, no retraining—and requires only a per-block pruning ratio schedule (determined by a lightweight grid search or manual assignment) to achieve up to 48% reduction in block operations with minimal accuracy loss.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, TP-Spikformer achieves 74.79% top-1 accuracy on SDT-V1-8-768 at 51% token retention (vs. 76.32% unpruned), reducing block OPs by 48% and power by 38% while increasing throughput by 29%. On QKFormer-10-768 at 53% retention, it reaches 82.53% accuracy with 47% OPs reduction and 41% throughput gain. On CIFAR-10, retaining only 20% of tokens yields 95.12% accuracy (0.07% drop from the 95.19% baseline). For downstream tasks, TP-Spikformer with SDT-V3 at 56% retention achieves 40.0% mIoU on ADE20K (1.7× throughput) and 54.4% mAP@0.5 on COCO (1.4× throughput), while matching SDTrack on event-based tracking (58.4% AUC on FE108) at lower power.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TP-Spikformer outperforms all existing SNN token pruning methods (SparseSpikformer, AT-SNN, STATA) on CIFAR-10/100 and DVS-CIFAR10 while being training-free: at 20% token retention it achieves 95.12% on CIFAR-10 vs. 94.88% (AT-SNN at 21%) and 94.77% (SparseSpikformer at 63%), and even surpasses the unpruned Spikformer baseline (95.19%) on CIFAR-100 (78.48% vs. 78.21%). On ImageNet, STATA achieves 74.03% on Spikformer-8-768 at ~50% retention but requires full retraining, whereas TP-Spikformer reaches 74.79% on SDT-V1 at 51% retention in a training-free manner. For downstream tasks, TP-Spikformer with SDT-V3 at 78% retention achieves 55.4% mAP@0.5 on COCO (vs. 58.8% unpruned SDT-V3) and 40.2% mIoU on ADE20K (vs. 40.1% unpruned), maintaining competitiveness with 1.4–1.7× throughput gains. On event-based tracking, TP-Spikformer at 56% retention matches SDTrack on FE108 (58.4% vs. 59.0% AUC) at lower power (6.51 vs. 8.16 mJ), and surpasses all RGB-based trackers. On the GLUE NLP benchmark, TP-Spikformer applied to SpikeLM achieves 75.9% average (vs. 76.5% unpruned), confirming cross-domain generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of the spiking transformer lineage (Spikformer, SDT-V1/V2/V3, QKFormer) and the token pruning literature from both ANN (DynamicViT, A-ViT) and SNN (SparseSpikformer, AT-SNN, STATA) communities. It departs from prior SNN token pruning methods in two key ways: (1) it requires no architectural modification or retraining, unlike STATA's anchor tokens or AT-SNN's ACT mechanism, and (2) it replaces direct token removal with a block-level early stopping strategy, which is necessary for compatibility with feature-variant architectures like QKFormer and SDT-V3 that embed convolutional layers requiring structured feature maps. The spatiotemporal scoring criterion draws on neuroscience-inspired saliency and change-detection principles, repurposing them as a heuristic for token importance in the spiking domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TP-Spikformer offers a practical, training-free compression path for deploying spiking transformers on edge devices, addressing a real bottleneck as SNN transformer models grow to 100M+ parameters. The block-level early stopping insight—that preserving spatial structure matters more than removing tokens for feature-variant architectures—opens the door to applying token pruning to a broader class of SNN backbones that prior methods could not handle. Limitations include the heuristic (non-learned) nature of the scoring criterion, the need for per-block ratio search (even if coarse), and the observation that the temporal scorer underperforms at the first time step. Open questions include whether learned or data-driven pruning criteria could outperform the neuroscience-inspired heuristic, and whether the approach composes well with quantization and architecture search at scale.
