## Problem

Existing hand-object interaction (HOI) generation is confined to a grasp-centric paradigm where control signals—whether force-closure constraints or coarse verb-noun instructions—impose a strong inductive bias toward stable grasps, failing to capture the rich diversity of daily non-grasping interactions like pushing, poking, and rotating. This limitation is compounded by the absence of large-scale, in-the-wild 3D HOI datasets that include diverse non-grasping motions, as prior datasets are restricted to lab-based grasping with limited object and action variety.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D object mesh (sampled as a 3000-point cloud) and a two-level text prompt: a Short Synthetic Caption (SSC, e.g., 'picking something up') providing coarse intent, and a Descriptive Synthetic Caption (DSC) specifying fine-grained contact geometry (e.g., 'applying index pad, middle pad to exert gentle force on the body of spray bottle'). It outputs MANO hand pose parameters (shape β, pose Θ, global rotation, translation) along with binary contact maps on both the hand surface (778 points) and object surface (3000 points), where the hand is segmented into 17 anatomical parts (finger pads, nails, knuckles, palmar, dorsal).

## Method

TOUCH is a three-stage framework. First, two independent CVAEs predict binary contact maps for the hand and object separately, conditioning on PointNet-extracted geometry and Qwen-7B text features, with the hand branch additionally receiving a part-level mask derived from the DSC to emphasize interaction-relevant regions. Second, an 8-block Transformer-based DDPM synthesizes MANO pose parameters using a hierarchical coarse-to-fine injection strategy: early blocks (i<4) receive global object/hand geometry plus SSC text via FiLM modulation, while later blocks (i≥4) switch to DSC text and adaptively selected local contact-point features (128 object + 64 hand points) via cross-attention, decoupling global context from local geometric refinement. Third, a refiner network (same Transformer architecture) performs one forward pass for global pose correction followed by 500 iterations of test-time optimization guided by a self-supervised cycle-consistency loss that enforces bidirectional hand-to-object and object-to-hand contact mapping, combined with penetration, contact, anatomy, and self-penetration constraints.

## Results

On the WildO2 test set, TOUCH achieves the best performance across nearly all metrics: P-IoU 0.776, P-F1 0.844 for contact accuracy; MPVPE 2.97 mm, PD 0.932 cm, PV 2.67 cm³ for physical plausibility; entropy 2.93 and cluster size 5.40 for diversity; and P-FID 4.13, VLM score 7.1, perceptual score 8.8 for semantic consistency. The most striking gap is in P-FID, where TOUCH (4.13) substantially outperforms Text2HOI (15.72), indicating far better semantic alignment with text prompts. On the OakInk dataset, TOUCH also leads with P-IoU 0.812, P-F1 0.882, MPVPE 4.49 mm, and P-FID 3.21.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TOUCH outperforms both baselines on the majority of metrics. Against ContactGen (a CVAE-based object-conditioned method), TOUCH improves P-IoU by 0.156 (0.776 vs. 0.620), reduces MPVPE by 46% (2.97 vs. 5.46 mm), and lowers penetration volume by 64% (2.67 vs. 7.37 cm³). Against Text2HOI (a transformer-based conditional diffusion model), TOUCH gains 0.065 in P-IoU (0.776 vs. 0.711), reduces MPVPE by 37% (2.97 vs. 4.69 mm), and achieves a dramatically lower P-FID (4.13 vs. 15.72), indicating that Text2HOI's coarse text conditioning fails to produce semantically faithful hand poses. Notably, Text2HOI's P-FID is nearly 4× worse than TOUCH's, suggesting its generation is poorly aligned with the input text despite using a similar diffusion backbone. Both baselines exhibit noticeable overall hand drift in free-form (non-grasping) settings, which the authors mitigate with an optimization-based post-processing module for fair comparison. On the OakInk dataset, TOUCH similarly leads all baselines, confirming generalizability beyond WildO2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant grasp-centric paradigm in HOI generation (ContactGen, Text2HOI, SemGrasp, etc.), which imposes strong inductive biases toward stable force-closure grasps, by reframing the problem as free-form interaction synthesis conditioned on fine-grained text. Methodologically, it combines a DDPM-based Transformer for pose generation with CVAE-based contact map prediction and a self-supervised cycle-consistency refiner, drawing on PointNet for geometric encoding and Qwen-7B for semantic feature extraction. The data pipeline is a notable departure from prior lab-based or simulation-based HOI datasets, leveraging image-to-3D reconstruction (InstantMesh) and dense matching (RoMa) to automatically recover 3D interactions from in-the-wild video, a direction only recently explored by a handful of works (EasyHOI, Web2Grasp) but not yet validated at scale with full semantic annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WildO2 is the first large-scale in-the-wild 3D HOI dataset covering non-grasping interactions (pushing, poking, rotating, etc.) across 92 intents and 610 object categories, filling a critical gap that has constrained the field to laboratory grasping scenarios. The emergent finding that the model associates force-related language ('firmly' vs. 'gently') with measurably different contact geometries (22–25% area difference) without explicit force modeling suggests that contact geometry alone can encode physical intent, a potentially useful inductive prior. Key limitations include the static-snapshot scope (no temporal dynamics), single-hand focus, modest dataset size (4.4k samples), and reliance on a single source video dataset (Something-Something V2), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to bimanual, dynamic, or deformable-object interactions at scale.
