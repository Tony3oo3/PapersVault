## Problem

While model compression and adversarial robustness have each been studied extensively, a principled understanding of how structured compressibility (neuron-level sparsity, spectral low-rankness) shapes adversarial vulnerability remains elusive. Prior work hints at regime-dependent effects but lacks a unified, source-agnostic theoretical framework connecting compressibility to robustness bounds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264689/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a neural network's layer weight matrices and characterizes their structured compressibility via (q,k,ε) parameters applied to row ℓ1-norms (neuron compressibility) or singular values (spectral compressibility), along with an attack budget δ. It produces adversarial robustness gap upper bounds that decompose into analytically interpretable terms: a compressibility-dependent factor (involving k, ε, and spread β), a scale factor (Frobenius norm), and interlayer alignment corrections. The bounds are norm-specific—ℓ∞ Lipschitz bounds for neuron compressibility and ℓ2 Lipschitz bounds for spectral compressibility—with cross-norm transfer via standard norm inequalities.

## Method

The paper introduces a 'structure vs. scale' decomposition: since (q,k,ε)-compressibility is scale-invariant, it bounds operator norms as (compressibility factor) × (Frobenius norm), isolating how concentrating parameter energy on few dominant rows or singular values inflates ℓ∞ or ℓ2 operator norms. Theorem 3.1 establishes these per-layer bounds, and Theorem 3.2 composes them across layers using interlayer alignment terms A_p that correct for the overly pessimistic assumption of perfect direction alignment between consecutive layers, yielding tight global Lipschitz constant bounds for the encoder. Corollary 3.3 then converts these into adversarial robustness gap bounds under logistic loss. Empirically, the authors validate predictions across FCN, ResNet18, VGG16, WideResNet-101, ViT, and CLIP architectures using group lasso, nuclear norm regularization, and low-rank factorization as compressibility-inducing interventions, confirming that vulnerabilities persist under adversarial training, transfer learning, and contribute to universal adversarial examples.

## Results

The paper's central quantitative finding is a strong correlation (ρ = 0.947) between the theoretical Lipschitz-based robustness bound (Corollary 3.3) and the empirically measured adversarial robustness gap across varying levels of spectral compressibility. Empirically, increasing neuron or spectral compressibility (via group lasso, nuclear norm regularization, or low-rank factorization) consistently degrades adversarial accuracy across FCN, ResNet-18, VGG-16, WideResNet-101, ViT, and CLIP architectures on MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, and ImageNet-1k. Critically, the robustness degradation persists under adversarial training (PGD-based) and transfer learning, and only compressibility (not merely increased Frobenius norm) induces universal adversarial examples. Simple interventions inspired by the bounds—controlling the spread β of dominant terms and using ε-based per-layer pruning targets—yield tangible improvements in robust accuracy retention under pruning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not propose a new method to compare against competing baselines; instead, it validates a theoretical framework by comparing uncompressed models against models with varying degrees of structured compressibility. The primary 'baseline' is the standard (uncompressed) model, and the key comparison is how adversarial robustness degrades as compressibility increases. Across all tested architectures (FCN, ResNet-18, VGG-16, WideResNet-101, ViT, CLIP) and datasets, the uncompressed model consistently outperforms its compressed counterparts in adversarial accuracy, with the gap widening as compressibility increases. Under adversarial training, both compressed and uncompressed models improve in absolute robustness, but the relative penalty from compressibility remains unchanged. In the pruning experiments (Figure 8), the compressed model retains performance better than the baseline under aggressive pruning ratios, illustrating the fundamental tension: compressibility aids practical compression but inherently sacrifices adversarial robustness. The paper also shows that increasing Frobenius norm alone (without compressibility) reduces standard adversarial robustness but does not produce universal adversarial examples, isolating the structural mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends several threads: the compressible vector framework from compressed sensing (Gribonval et al., 2012), Lipschitz-based robustness analysis with interlayer alignment terms (Scaman & Virmaux, 2018), and adversarial robustness bounds for linear classifiers (Ribeiro et al., 2023). It departs from prior work on low-rank training (Savostianova et al., 2023) and sparsity-condition number connections (Feng et al., 2025) by providing a source-agnostic, decomposable bound that covers both neuron-level and spectral compressibility under a single (q,k,ε) framework, rather than tying vulnerability to a specific compression procedure. The interlayer alignment terms generalize Scaman & Virmaux's approach to the structured-compressibility setting, and the corollary connecting encoder Lipschitz bounds to adversarial risk follows the linear-regression robustness template of Ribeiro et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a fundamental tension: the very structural properties that make networks compressible (concentrated parameter energy in few rows or singular values) create globally sensitive directions that adversaries can exploit, and this vulnerability persists under adversarial training and transfer learning. The mechanistic distinction that compressibility (not merely scale) drives universal adversarial examples offers a concrete diagnostic for practitioners. Open questions include how composite compression pipelines (pruning + quantization + distillation) interact with these effects, whether tighter local bounds can close the gap to empirical robustness, and whether the proposed interventions (spread regularization, alignment-aware pruning) scale to large pretrained models.
