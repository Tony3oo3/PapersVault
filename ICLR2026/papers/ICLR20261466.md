## Problem

Existing multi-LLM systems communicate exclusively through text, which acts as a low-bandwidth, ambiguous medium that compresses rich internal representations into linear token sequences, losing semantic information and incurring sequential decoding latency. This paper asks whether LLMs can communicate beyond text by directly transferring internal representations, specifically KV-Cache, between models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261466/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Both the Sharer and Receiver models independently prefill the same input text sequence, producing per-token KV-Cache vectors (dimension d, flattened across all layers). The C2C fuser operates at the prefill stage, consuming both models' KV-Caches and producing a single fused KV-Cache for the Receiver. Decoding then proceeds autoregressively using only the fused cache, so the Sharer contributes zero output tokens—eliminating the sequential text-generation bottleneck entirely. Token alignment between different tokenizers is handled by decoding Receiver tokens to strings and re-encoding with the Sharer's tokenizer.

## Method

C2C projects the Sharer's KV-Cache into the Receiver's representation space and merges them via a per-layer neural fuser with residual connection, so the Receiver's original cache is preserved rather than overwritten. The fuser comprises three modules: a projection layer that concatenates and fuses both caches, an input-aware dynamic weighting layer for per-query modulation, and a learnable per-layer gate using Gumbel-sigmoid with temperature annealing (differentiable during training, binary at inference) to select which layers benefit from cache injection. Layer alignment uses a terminal strategy (aligning last layers first, then working backwards), and both LLMs remain frozen during training—only the fuser is optimized with standard next-token prediction loss on the Receiver's response. Oracle experiments first validate that KV-Cache enrichment improves accuracy at fixed cache length, that caches are convertible across models via a simple MLP, and that different models encode complementary semantic understandings of the same input.

## Results

C2C achieves 6.4–14.2% higher average accuracy than individual models across four benchmarks (MMLU-Redux, OpenBookQA, ARC-Challenge, C-Eval) with three different Sharer–Receiver pairs. Compared to text-to-text (T2T) communication, C2C gains an additional 3.1–5.4% average accuracy while delivering a 2.5× average latency speedup (ranging from 1.51× to 14.41× depending on the Sharer). For example, with Qwen2.5-0.5B as Sharer and Qwen3-0.6B as Receiver, C2C reaches 42.92% on MMLU-Redux (vs. 41.03% T2T, 35.53% Receiver-only) in 0.40 s (vs. 1.52 s for T2T).

## Baselines & Comparisons

C2C is compared against three baselines: (1) Text-to-Text (T2T) communication, where the Sharer generates an analytical text that is concatenated with the question for the Receiver; (2) Query-level routing (RouteLLM, Ong et al. 2024), which assigns each query to the stronger or weaker model based on a difficulty score; and (3) individual model performance (Receiver-only or Sharer-only). C2C outperforms T2T on all 12 benchmark–Sharer combinations by an average of 3.1–5.4% accuracy, while reducing inference time by 2.5× on average (e.g., 0.40 s vs. 1.52 s on MMLU-Redux with Qwen2.5-0.5B Sharer). Against query-level routing, C2C achieves substantially higher accuracy (e.g., 42.92% vs. 35.58% on MMLU-Redux), since routing is capped at the better individual model's performance. Notably, when the Sharer is a base (non-instruction-tuned) model like Qwen3-4B-Base that cannot follow instructions (1.03% standalone accuracy), C2C still achieves 43.95% on MMLU-Redux, matching T2T's 43.87% while being 17× faster (0.45 s vs. 7.54 s), demonstrating that C2C can extract useful knowledge even from models that fail at text-based communication.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two distinct lines of work. On one hand, KV-Cache sharing and reuse methods (intra-model layer sharing, cross-query prefix caching, DroidSpeak) treat the cache as a computational artifact for efficiency, whereas C2C repurposes it as a semantic communication medium across heterogeneous models. On the other hand, collaborative multi-LLM systems (CAMEL, MetaGPT, Mixture-of-Agents, DyLAN) and routing-based inference systems (RouteLLM, token-level routing) all rely on text-to-text interfaces or passive context inheritance; C2C introduces a fundamentally different channel—direct projection and fusion of internal KV-Cache representations—enabling cross-family, cross-size semantic transfer without intermediate text generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

C2C establishes KV-Cache as a viable, faster, and richer communication primitive for multi-LLM systems, with the practical consequence that a weaker instruction-tuned model can leverage a stronger base model's knowledge even when the base model cannot follow instructions. Key open questions include scaling beyond pairwise communication (the paper notes O(N) training cost remains unresolved), handling noisy information from much weaker Sharers, and extending the paradigm to cross-modal (VLM/VLA) settings. The finding that layer-specific gating is critical—some layers benefit from enrichment while others degrade—suggests that future cache-communication designs will need fine-grained, model-aware fusion strategies rather than uniform injection.
