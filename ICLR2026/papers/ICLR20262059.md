## Problem

Multi-agent LLM reasoning frameworks such as ReMA suffer from a critical 'lazy agent' failure mode in which the reasoning agent contributes only trivially—producing empty outputs or copying the meta-thinking agent—collapsing the two-agent system into an ineffective single agent. The paper traces this to a structural bias in multi-turn GRPO's turn-level normalization (1/T_i) that implicitly incentivizes fewer interaction turns, and demonstrates that prompt engineering alone cannot resolve the issue during online reinforcement learning.

## Input / Output

The system takes a math reasoning problem as input and produces a final boxed answer through an alternating multi-turn dialogue between a meta-thinking agent (high-level planning, decomposition) and a reasoning agent (step-by-step computation), both sharing the same model weights but distinguished by role-specific system prompts. Uniquely, the reasoning agent can emit a <restart> control token to discard all prior reasoning outputs, consolidate accumulated instructions, and begin a fresh attempt, making the output a potentially non-monotonic reasoning trace where earlier steps may be explicitly invalidated.

## Method

Dr. MAMR makes three key modifications to the multi-turn GRPO training objective. First, it removes the 1/T_i turn normalization, which Theorem 1 formally shows biases gradient updates toward shorter trajectories and thus toward lazy, minimal-contribution behaviors. Second, it introduces a Shapley-inspired causal influence measure that groups semantically similar steps across rollouts (cosine similarity ≥ 0.9 via Qwen2.5-0.5B embeddings) and averages their one-step log-probability impact under attention masking, yielding robust step-level credit without expensive resampling. Third, it designs a verifiable restart reward that checks whether masking all prior reasoning outputs increases or decreases the model's confidence in the final answer, rewarding restarts only when they genuinely improve the trajectory. These three signals—normalized outcome advantage, causal influence, and restart reward—are combined into a weighted step-level advantage that replaces the uniform trajectory-level advantage, and the model is trained via RL on DeepScaleR with a brief SFT cold-start to seed restart behavior.

## Results

On the 7B model (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), Dr. MAMR achieves an average pass@1 of 58.43 across seven math benchmarks, outperforming single-agent GRPO (55.08) and the multi-agent ReMA baseline (51.97). On the 14B model, Dr. MAMR reaches 62.49 average, again surpassing GRPO (58.05) and ReMA (57.24). The largest gains appear on harder benchmarks: AIME24 improves from 16.67 (GRPO) to 20.00 (7B) and 26.67 (14B), and OlympiadBench improves from 48.60 to 52.34 (7B) and 57.03 (14B). Notably, ReMA consistently underperforms single-agent GRPO, confirming the severity of the lazy-agent problem.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dr. MAMR outperforms single-agent GRPO on all seven benchmarks for both 7B and 14B models, with average gains of +3.35 (7B) and +4.44 (14B), and the gap widens with model scale. Against the multi-agent ReMA baseline, Dr. MAMR wins on every benchmark by a substantial margin (e.g., +6.63 average on 7B, +5.25 on 14B), while ReMA itself underperforms single-agent GRPO—directly evidencing the lazy-agent collapse. The VRP(CoT) prompting baseline (no RL training) is consistently weaker than both GRPO and Dr. MAMR, confirming that RL training is necessary for effective multi-agent collaboration. In the extended comparison (Appendix Table 7), Dr. MAMR also surpasses stronger single-agent RL baselines including DAPO (55.89), Dr.GRPO (54.67), and GSPO (56.49) on the 7B average, further validating that the multi-agent framework, once the lazy-agent issue is resolved, provides genuine gains over single-agent approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends ReMA (Wan et al., 2025), a two-agent meta-reasoning framework trained with multi-turn GRPO, by diagnosing a structural bias in its loss that causes one agent to become lazy. The theoretical analysis builds on the GRPO objective (Shao et al., 2024) and multi-turn GRPO variants (Wei et al., 2025; Shi et al., 2025), while the causal influence mechanism draws on Shapley counterfactual credits (Li et al., 2021) and attention-suppression methods (Bogdan et al., 2025). The paper departs from prior multi-agent RL credit-assignment work (COMA, VDN, Dr.Reinforce) by addressing laziness in *sequential* rather than simultaneous agent settings, and from Dr.GRPO (Liu et al., 2025b) by targeting turn-level rather than token-level normalization bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a multi-agent framework can underperform a single-agent baseline due to a specific normalization term is a cautionary result for the growing multi-agent LLM reasoning literature, and the clean theoretical explanation (Theorem 1) gives practitioners a concrete diagnostic. The Shapley-inspired grouping and verifiable restart reward are practical, low-overhead additions that could be adopted in other multi-turn RL settings. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond two-agent math reasoning to more agents, non-math domains, or asynchronous settings, and whether the SFT cold-start requirement for restart behavior limits deployment. The paper is limited to Qwen2.5 and Llama-3 families at 3B–14B scale, so scaling behavior remains untested.
