## Problem

Successor Measures (SM) provide a principled way to model discounted future state occupancy under a policy, but existing SM estimators (GHM, γ-model, TDFlow) are designed for offline policy evaluation and transfer, not for compact representations that can be jointly trained with value functions in online RL. The paper addresses the missing bridge between the generative, distributional power of SM and the fast-adapting, policy-aware features needed by off-policy algorithms like TD3 and SAC.

## Input / Output

The method consumes transition tuples (s, a, s') from a replay buffer along with a next action a' sampled from the current policy, and produces a fixed-dimensional state-action feature vector ψ(s,a) ∈ R^d that serves as the sole input to the Q-network. The same ψ, combined with a time-dependent projection ζ(s',k), also parameterizes a flow-matching vector field from which successor measure samples can be drawn via a short ODE integration (default 2 function evaluations). For image-based environments (MinAtar), an autoencoder compresses observations into a latent space where the flow operates, while for DMC tasks the raw state vector is used directly.

## Method

SF² decomposes the conditional flow-matching vector field as a linear projection u(s',k,s,a) = ζ(s',k)ᵀ ψ(s,a), where ψ is a time-invariant embedding of (s,a) and ζ is a time-conditioned matrix field over future states. The training objective explicitly exploits the SM's Bellman mixture structure: L_flow = (1−γ)·L_P + γ·L_bootstrapping, where the bootstrapping term aligns vector fields conditioned on (s,a) and (s',a') at the same noise level (following TD²-CFM) rather than generating full successor states, avoiding expensive ODE integration. A value-alignment loss L_value is added to anchor ψ to the Q-function's TD target, and EMA target networks (ψ', ζ') stabilize bootstrapping. In the limit k→0, the gradient on ψ reduces to a Bellman-like TD recursion ψ(s,a) ← (1−γ)·(immediate feature) + γ·ψ(s',a'), recovering a Successor Representation structure. The resulting ψ is plugged directly into standard TD3 or SAC Q-networks, making the representation jointly optimized with policy learning.

## Results

On seven DeepMind Control Suite tasks (AcrobotSwingup, CartpoleBalanceSparse, CartpoleSwingupSparse, CheetahRun, FishSwim, HopperHop, PendulumSwingup), SF² with the full successor horizon (γ=0.99) consistently achieves the highest IQM AUC across both TD3 and SAC variants, outperforming vanilla baselines, SF-based methods (Sim, SimLap), and SPR. Gains are more pronounced for TD3 than SAC, and the successor variant (γ=0.99) clearly surpasses the transition-only variant (γ=0.0), confirming the value of long-horizon modeling. At a reduced 1M-timestep budget, SF² still maintains a clear margin over all baselines. On MinAtar (discrete actions, image input) with PPO, SF² (both with and without autoencoder) also improves over vanilla PPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SF² outperforms vanilla TD3 and SAC across most of the seven DMC environments, with the largest relative gains observed for TD3, suggesting the representation is particularly beneficial for algorithms that struggle with exploration. Against the strong SF baseline from Chua et al. (2024) (TD3Sim/SACSim), SF² wins consistently, and the advantage is especially notable on sparse-reward tasks (CartpoleBalanceSparse, CartpoleSwingupSparse) where SF-based methods degrade because most transitions yield zero reward, making it difficult to learn the task weight. The Laplacian-regularized variant (TD3SimLap/SACSimLap) is also outperformed. SPR (Schwarzer et al., 2021), configured with K=1 and Gaussian noise augmentation (σ=0.05), is likewise surpassed by SF². Finally, the ablation comparing γ=0.0 (transition-only) versus γ=0.99 (full successor) shows that incorporating the discounted future horizon is critical: the successor variant achieves higher AUC, validating that long-horizon state-occupancy information improves representation quality beyond short-term transition prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of successor measure estimation (Blier et al., 2021; Thakoor et al., 2022; Janner et al., 2020) and flow-matching generative modeling (Lipman et al., 2023; Farebrother et al., 2025), but departs from prior SM work by making the learned representation directly consumable by online policy optimizers (TD3, SAC) rather than serving only policy evaluation or transfer. The structured linear decomposition u = ζ(s',k)ᵀψ(s,a) draws on the spectral-projection architecture of Diffusion Spectral Representation (Shribak et al., 2024) and the TD²-CFM bootstrapping trick from TDFlow, but repurposes them to yield a time-invariant feature ψ that can be jointly trained with Q-networks. The authors position SF² as the first framework to explicitly close the loop between successor measure estimation and online policy optimization, whereas prior SM methods (GHM, γ-model, TDFlow) were designed for offline evaluation or zero-shot reward transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core contribution is a clean architectural recipe—time-invariant embedding plus time-dependent projection—that makes successor-measure-based representations plug-and-play with standard off-policy algorithms, potentially opening a path toward reward-agnostic, dynamics-driven representation learning in continuous control. However, the paper is explicitly preliminary: results cover only seven DMC tasks, the ~2× compute overhead is non-trivial, no theoretical guarantees are provided, and the k→0 SR connection is an approximation rather than a proof. Key open questions include how the representation behaves at intermediate k values, whether the approach scales to high-dimensional or partially observable settings, and whether the gains persist under tighter sample budgets or more diverse task suites.
