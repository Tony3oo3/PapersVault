## Problem

Existing reference-based super-resolution (RefSR) datasets and methods are designed for rigid, structured scenes (human activities, architecture) and fail to handle the irregular structures, fine-grained textures, frequent defocused backgrounds, and significant shape deformations characteristic of plant imagery. No large-scale, plant-specific RefSR benchmark exists, and current matching and fusion strategies are inefficient or inaccurate for the complex botanical textures encountered in real-world plant photography.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264737/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 4× bicubic-downscaled low-resolution (LR) image and a high-quality reference (Ref) image as input, both in RGB format, and produces a single super-resolved (SR) image with texture-rich details. Training operates on 160×160 patch pairs (with a 300×300 variant also provided), while the underlying dataset spans 2K–8K resolutions with over 80% of images above 4K. The Ref image may differ from the LR in color, scale, rotation, deformation, and background, reflecting real-world capture variability.

## Method

The framework combines two novel modules. First, Selective Key-Region Matching (SKRM) computes a binary key-detail mask via the absolute difference between a feature map and its bilinear downsample-upsample reconstruction, then performs cosine-similarity matching only on the selected textured regions, reducing matching cost by over 150× versus exhaustive search; matched Ref features are warped to the LR frame using deformable convolution at multiple scales. Second, the Texture-Guided Diffusion Module (TGDM) treats the LR feature as the starting point of a 4-step conditional reverse diffusion process, where the Denoising Network (three ResBlocks with AdaLN modulated by timestep embeddings and matched Ref features) progressively denoises the latent; the refined latent is further enhanced by a Residual State Space Block and fused back via residual addition and sub-pixel upsampling. The LR and Ref feature extractors are trained with contrastive learning and knowledge distillation (following C2-Matching), and the full network is optimized with L1 loss over 400 epochs.

## Results

On the PlantRSR test set (trained on PlantRSR), the proposed method achieves 38.62 dB PSNR, 31.53 dB M-PSNR, 0.9538 SSIM, 0.1288 LPIPS, and 0.0826 DISTS with only 11.1M parameters, outperforming the strongest competitor SSMTF (38.49 dB PSNR, 31.25 dB M-PSNR) by 0.13 dB in PSNR and 0.28 dB in M-PSNR. On the CUFED5 test set, the method reaches 28.95 dB PSNR / 0.8602 SSIM / 0.2184 LPIPS, surpassing SSMTF (28.86/0.8595/0.2202) and RRSR (28.83/0.8563/0.2241). On WR-SR, it achieves 28.51 dB PSNR / 0.8062 SSIM / 0.2813 LPIPS, leading all baselines. The method also attains the best results on Sun80 (30.41/0.824/0.284) and DRefSR test (31.77/0.871/0.272).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 13 baselines across every evaluated dataset and metric. On the primary PlantRSR test set (trained on PlantRSR), it leads SSMTF (the closest competitor) by 0.13 dB PSNR and 0.28 dB M-PSNR, while using fewer parameters (11.1M vs. 13.9M). Against heavier models like RRSR (21.5M) and MRefSR (23.7M), the gains are larger: +0.18 dB PSNR over RRSR and +0.20 dB over MRefSR. On CUFED5 test, the method achieves 28.95 dB PSNR, beating SSMTF (28.86) and RRSR (28.83); on WR-SR it reaches 28.51 dB, ahead of SSMTF (28.42) and RRSR (28.41). On Sun80 and DRefSR test sets, margins are smaller but consistent (e.g., 30.41 vs. 30.38 PSNR on Sun80; 31.77 vs. 31.75 on DRefSR). HiTSR is the weakest baseline across all settings, trailing the proposed method by over 0.5 dB PSNR on PlantRSR and ~1.9 dB on CUFED5, indicating that hierarchical transformer approaches struggle most with complex plant textures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on C2-Matching (Jiang et al., 2021) for correspondence learning via contrastive learning and knowledge distillation, and on DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) for the conditional denoising process in its Texture-Guided Diffusion Module. It departs from the broader RefSR literature—which has focused on rigid-structure scenes (CUFED5, LMR)—by arguing that plant imagery with its irregular shapes, subtle textures, and frequent defocus requires both a purpose-built dataset and domain-specific matching/refinement strategies. The SKRM module selectively matches only key textured regions (reducing computation by ~150× over exhaustive matching), while TGDM introduces a lightweight diffusion refinement conditioned on matched Ref textures, combining ideas from deformable convolutions (SSEN, DATSR) and state-space models (MambaIR's RSSB) in a new configuration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PlantRSR dataset (16,585 manually annotated HR-Ref pairs, 2K–8K resolution) provides the first dedicated RefSR benchmark for plant imagery, addressing a clear gap for precision agriculture and plant phenotyping applications. The paper demonstrates that domain-specific data and selective matching yield consistent gains over general-purpose RefSR methods, but acknowledges two important limitations: hardware constraints prevent full exploitation of the 4K–8K source images, and performance degrades with low-similarity reference images. Open questions include whether the selective matching and diffusion refinement strategies generalize beyond plant scenes to other complex natural textures, and whether more adaptive Ref utilization could handle the dissimilar-reference case.
