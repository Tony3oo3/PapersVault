## Problem

Developing biologically plausible learning algorithms that match backpropagation performance remains a longstanding challenge. Existing approaches either avoid spikes entirely for error propagation or rely on signed (positive and negative) learning signals, leaving unresolved how discrete spikes can represent negative values. The paper identifies that prior work fails to satisfy two additional biological plausibility criteria—discrete spiking communication and unsigned error signaling—while also underperforming on standard benchmarks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw inputs (28×28 grayscale or 32×32 color images, character sequences, or multivariate time series) which are encoded into spike trains via LIF neurons, and produces class labels, next-character predictions, future time-series values, or reconstructed images. Unusually, the supervision target is also spike-encoded: for classification, a one-hot binary vector is repeated along the temporal dimension and delivered to top-layer Type 2 neurons, so both the stimulus and the concept are represented as discrete spike sequences rather than continuous activations.

## Method

BSD jointly trains a feedforward spiking network (Type 1 pyramidal neurons mapping stimuli to concepts) and a backward spiking network (Type 2 pyramidal neurons reconstructing stimuli from concepts) using independent, asymmetric weight matrices W and Θ. Each pyramidal neuron has three compartments—soma, basal dendrites (receiving feedforward input), and apical dendrites (receiving feedback signal)—and the training objective is to align the basal-received voltage v with the apical-received voltage v̂ within each neuron. This alignment is achieved via the Relaxed Contrastive (ReCo) loss, an unsigned contrastive objective that penalizes misalignment of corresponding samples while only softly penalizing positive cross-sample correlations, avoiding the need for signed error signals. Gradients are computed locally per layer with detach() operations truncating cross-layer propagation, and both pathways update simultaneously in a single pass, eliminating the two-stage forward-backward separation of backpropagation. For generation tasks, an FFT-based frequency decomposition is applied to adaptively weight the ReCo loss, assigning larger penalties to high-frequency components to preserve edge detail.

## Results

On image classification with CNNs, BSD achieves 99.44% on MNIST, 91.05% on FashionMNIST, 90.81% on SVHN, 84.13% on CIFAR-10, and 53.48% on CIFAR-100 (average 83.78%), within 1–4% of backpropagation on ANNs (86.29% average). On sequential regression, BSD-trained RNNs outperform backpropagation on Electricity (MSE 0.165 vs. 0.175) and Metr-la (MSE 0.125 vs. 0.131), while trailing on Pems-bay (0.174 vs. 0.164) and Harry Potter character prediction (41.8% vs. 51.9%). For image generation, BSD-trained autoencoders achieve FID 168.12 on CIFAR-10 and 72.39 on MNIST, competitive with the fully spiking FSVAE baseline (175.5 and 97.06) but below BP-trained ANNs (127.34 and 49.56).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against backpropagation on ANNs (the strongest baseline), BSD CNNs trail by 0.12% on MNIST, 1.63% on FashionMNIST, 3.55% on SVHN, 2.99% on CIFAR-10, and 4.27% on CIFAR-100, while BSD MLPs show larger gaps (up to 8.75% on CIFAR-10). Compared to other biologically plausible methods, BSD CNNs substantially outperform DLL (e.g., 84.13% vs. 70.89% on CIFAR-10; 53.48% vs. 38.60% on CIFAR-100) and R-STDP (84.13% vs. 33.19% on CIFAR-10), and beat CCL on MNIST (99.44% vs. 96.30%) and SVHN (90.81% vs. 88.78%) though CCL edges ahead on CIFAR-100 (56.29% vs. 53.48%). BSD also surpasses Predictive Coding on CIFAR-10 (84.13% vs. 72.94%) but is slightly behind on SVHN (90.81% vs. 94.53%). On sequential regression, BSD beats Predictive Coding and DLL across all four tasks and even outperforms BP on Electricity and Metr-la MSE, though it lags BP on Harry Potter (41.8% vs. 51.9%) and Pems-bay (0.174 vs. 0.164). For image generation, BSD's FID (168.12 on CIFAR-10) is worse than BP (127.34) but better than FSVAE (175.5), confirming competitive generative quality under full spiking constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the biologically plausible learning line initiated by Lv et al. (2025) (Dendritic Localized Learning), which established three criteria (asymmetric weights, local error, non-two-stage training), by adding two further criteria: spiking neuron models and unsigned error signals. Conceptually, it draws on the pyramidal neuron architecture of Sacramento et al. (2018) and the bidirectional dual-network design of Counter-Current Learning (Kao & Hariharan, 2024), but departs from both by replacing their signed floating-point interlayer signals with discrete binary spikes and by using a relaxed contrastive (ReCo) loss rather than MSE or InfoNCE for local alignment. The mutual distillation framing—where a feedforward perception network and a backward recall network are jointly trained by aligning their spiking feature representations—synthesizes ideas from target propagation, predictive coding, and multimodal contrastive learning into a unified spike-based framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BSD is the first algorithm to simultaneously satisfy all five biological plausibility criteria while reaching within a few percentage points of backpropagation on standard benchmarks, suggesting that the field's assumption that signed or continuous signals are necessary for effective deep learning may be overly conservative. However, meaningful gaps remain on harder datasets (e.g., ~4–6% on CIFAR-100), the method still relies on gradient descent rather than truly local synaptic plasticity rules, and image generation quality (FID) lags behind backpropagation-trained autoencoders. Open questions include whether the approach scales to ImageNet-level tasks, whether the ReCo loss and FFT-based frequency decomposition generalize to other generative architectures, and whether the unsigned-spike constraint can be relaxed or further tightened without sacrificing convergence.
