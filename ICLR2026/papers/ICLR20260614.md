## Problem

Existing sequential pricing algorithms for competitive markets either assume linear demand or restrict to a fixed parametric family, limiting applicability to realistic nonlinear demand. This paper asks whether a tuning-free pricing policy can be designed for unknown, nonlinear demand that simultaneously guarantees convergence to the Nash equilibrium and sublinear regret, without the seller needing to know the functional form of the demand curve.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260614/cropped_page50_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

Each seller observes only their own realized demand (private) and competitors' posted prices (public) over T periods, along with known price bounds and the shape-concavity parameter s_i. The method outputs a periodic price p_i^(t) per period, where during exploitation the price is the argmax of the estimated revenue function over the price domain. Unusually, the demand model is a monotone single-index ψ_i(⟨θ_i, p⟩) in which both the index direction θ_i and the link function ψ_i are unknown, and the exploration phase requires joint prices to follow an elliptically symmetric distribution to ensure consistency of the linear estimator for θ_i.

## Method

The SPE-BR algorithm has two phases: an exploration phase of length τ ∝ T^ξ where all sellers sample prices from a common elliptically symmetric distribution, followed by an exploitation phase where each seller plays the best response computed from their estimated demand model. During exploration, seller i first estimates the index direction θ_i via normalized linear least squares on a fraction κ_i of the data, then estimates the link function ψ_i via shape-constrained least squares under s_i-concavity (minimizing squared loss over concave functions φ with ψ_i = h_{s_i} ∘ φ). The key theoretical insight (Proposition 3.5) shows that the virtual valuation φ_i(u) = u + ψ_i(u)/ψ_i'(u) is increasing with bounded derivative if and only if ψ_i is (c_i−1)-concave, unifying log-concavity and concavity under a single shape constraint that guarantees both Nash equilibrium existence and contraction of the best-response operator. This yields a regret bound of Õ(N^{3/2} T^{5/7}) and NE convergence at Õ(N^{3/4} T^{-1/7}), with the optimal exploration fraction ξ* = 5/7 balancing estimation error against exploitation loss.

## Results

The paper establishes that under s-concave demand with optimal exploration fraction ξ=5/7, each seller incurs a total expected regret of Õ(N^{3/2} T^{5/7}) and the joint prices converge to the unique Nash equilibrium at rate Õ(N^{3/4} T^{-1/7}). The estimation component achieves a parametric rate of O((N log n / n)^{1/2}) for the direction vector θ_i and a nonparametric supremum-norm rate of O((log n / n)^{2/5}) for the s-concave link function ψ_i. In numerical experiments with N∈{2,4,6} sellers and T up to 3200 periods, the observed convergence slopes on log-log axes are consistently faster (smaller) than the theoretical upper bounds across all contraction constant regimes (L_Γ ≈ 0, 0.5, 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper with no direct empirical baselines; the `compares_to` list is empty. The paper's contributions are framed as strict generalizations of prior work: setting ψ_i(u)=u+α_i recovers the linear demand model of Li et al. (2024), which achieves √T regret, while the present framework handles unknown nonlinear s-concave demand and yields the slower T^{5/7} rate inherent to nonparametric estimation. For the monopolistic case (N=1), the regret bound matches that of Fan et al. (2024), who use a kernel-based estimator for binary outcomes; the present method offers the practical advantage of being fully data-driven with no bandwidth selection. The contraction assumption (Assumption 3.6) generalizes the corresponding condition in Li et al. (2024) and Kachani et al. (2007), and the s-concavity framework subsumes log-concavity (s=0) and concavity (s=1) as special cases, unifying assumptions scattered across the N=1 dynamic pricing literature (Chen & Farias 2018, Cole & Roughgarden 2014, Golrezaei et al. 2019, Javanmard 2017).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sequential price competition framework of Li et al. (2024), which achieves optimal √T regret under linear demand, to a semiparametric single-index model with unknown nonlinear link functions. The estimation strategy draws on the monotone single-index literature (Balabdaoui et al., 2019) for the parametric component and on shape-constrained least squares techniques (Mösching & Dümbgen, 2020; Dumbgen et al., 2004) for the nonparametric component, but introduces a new supremum-norm concentration inequality for s-concave regression. The key conceptual departure is Proposition 3.5, which shows that the Nash equilibrium existence condition (monotone virtual valuation) is equivalent to s-concavity of the demand link, thereby unifying log-concavity (s=0) and concavity (s=1) as special cases within a single shape-constrained estimation framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a fully data-driven, tuning-parameter-free algorithm for competition-aware dynamic pricing that handles nonlinear demand, which is more realistic than the linear models dominating prior work. The regret bound of Õ(T^{5/7}) is sublinear but strictly worse than the √T rate for linear demand, reflecting the fundamental cost of nonparametric flexibility. Open questions include estimating the s-concavity parameter s_i from data (currently assumed known), developing joint estimators for (θ_i, ψ_i) to avoid the two-phase split, and designing algorithms that bypass the exploration-exploitation trade-off entirely. The framework is directly applicable to platform-mediated markets (e.g., hotel pricing services) where multiple sellers use the same class of algorithmic tools.
