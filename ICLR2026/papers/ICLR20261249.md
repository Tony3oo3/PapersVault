## Problem

Sim-to-real transfer in RL typically requires expensive retraining of task-specific policies for each new environment, coupling skill acquisition with domain adaptation. This limits flexible deployment in ever-changing real-world settings. The paper asks whether a pre-trained, task-agnostic foundation policy can be adapted to new dynamics purely in latent space, without any policy retraining.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two data sources: (1) a large offline dataset of unlabeled simulator trajectories (2M episodes, 1M-transition replay buffer) used to pretrain the Hilbert-space encoder and latent-conditioned DDPG policy, and (2) a small target-domain dataset of ~5,000 interaction rounds collected in the perturbed environment. The output is a single refined latent vector z_final ∈ R^D (normalized to unit norm) that conditions the frozen policy π(a|s, z_final) for action generation. All observations are state vectors (not images), and the adapter operates on a few hundred gradient steps, yielding adaptation in ~6 seconds versus hour-scale for deployment-adaptation baselines.

## Method

The framework pretrains a foundation policy consisting of a Hilbert-space state encoder φ and a latent-conditioned policy π(a|s,z) using intrinsic rewards on offline simulator data, so that varying z spans a family of reusable skills. At deployment, a three-step latent adaptation procedure replaces policy retraining: (A) a weighted joint least-squares regression over both simulator and target transitions yields an initial latent z_src that partially corrects first-order dynamics mismatch; (B) a frozen permutation-invariant DeepSet network (MetaDynamic) encodes the unordered set of target-state embeddings into a 64-dim dynamics signature η capturing higher-order distributional structure; (C) a small adapter MLP g_θ, initialized near identity, is fine-tuned for a few hundred steps on target data to map (z_src, η) → z_final, reconciling the alignment solution with the dynamics signature. The policy π and encoder φ remain entirely frozen throughout adaptation, making the approach task-agnostic and parameter-efficient, with the only learned component at deployment being the lightweight adapter.

## Results

On the OGBench Walker environment under gravity perturbations (G1–G4), Found-adapt consistently achieves the top-1 or top-2 average return across both Stand and Walk tasks. Under the easiest perturbation (G1, gravity −9.8→−15), Found-adapt reaches 562.72 (Stand) and 472.25 (Walk), reducing the sim-to-real gap to −202.27 and −292.74 respectively, compared to Direct-Transfer's −393.37 and −446.06. Under the hardest perturbation (G4, gravity −9.8→−44), Found-adapt still outperforms all GAT/UGAT baselines on Walk (42.57 vs. 25.42/14.51) and remains competitive with PAD on Stand (71.70 vs. 78.69). Online adaptation time is ~6.1 s, comparable to Direct-Transfer (~5.2 s) and substantially faster than UGAT (~9.1 s) or PAD (hour-scale).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Found-adapt is compared against three families of baselines: (1) Direct-Transfer (no adaptation), (2) Grounded Action Transformation (Vanilla-GAT and UGAT), and (3) Policy Adaptation during Deployment (PAD). Against Direct-Transfer, Found-adapt consistently reduces the sim-to-real gap by 30–50% across all gravity levels (e.g., G1 Stand gap shrinks from −393 to −202). Against Vanilla-GAT and UGAT, Found-adapt dominates on nearly every setting: on G1 Walk it achieves 472.25 vs. 185.66 (GAT) and 326.49 (UGAT), and on G3 Stand it reaches 322.06 vs. 32.28 (GAT) and 72.30 (UGAT). Against PAD, the comparison is mixed: Found-adapt wins on G1 (both tasks), G3 Stand, and G4 Walk, but trails PAD slightly on G2 (both tasks) and G4 Stand. The paper notes that PAD requires task-specific pretraining and hour-scale adaptation, whereas Found-adapt is task-agnostic and completes adaptation in ~6 s, making it more practical for multi-task deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Park et al. (2024b) 'Foundation policies with Hilbert representations,' adopting their Hilbert-space encoder, latent-conditioned policy, and least-squares task solving as the foundation policy backbone. It departs from the traditional sim-to-real literature (domain randomization, grounded action transformation, deployment adaptation like PAD) by reframing the problem: instead of retraining or post-training the policy network to close the dynamics gap, it keeps the policy frozen and adapts only a latent vector z through a lightweight adapter network. The MetaDynamic component draws on DeepSet-style permutation-invariant set encoders and contrastive (NT-Xent) pre-training, while the overall 'skill reuse + context adjustment' framing is inspired by the evolved apprentice analogy from cognitive science (Sterelny, 2012).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a parameter-efficient paradigm for sim-to-real transfer that decouples skill acquisition (offline foundation policy training) from environment adaptation (few-shot latent refinement), potentially reducing deployment cost from hours of policy retraining to seconds of adapter tuning. However, all experiments are sim-to-sim with controlled gravity/friction perturbations on Walker locomotion tasks, leaving real-hardware validation and vision-based generalization as open questions. The authors acknowledge that mitigation degree varies across environments and that hyperparameter sensitivity (e.g., the weighting λ, adapter capacity) may require per-task tuning, limiting immediate plug-and-play applicability. Broader impact hinges on whether the Hilbert-space foundation policy assumption (linear reward structure in latent space) holds for more complex, non-locomotion domains.
