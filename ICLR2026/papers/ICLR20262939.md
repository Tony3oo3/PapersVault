## Problem

Prior LLM unlearning work assumes the trained model and unlearning targets are fixed, focusing solely on improving unlearning algorithms. However, the way knowledge is textually encoded during training—such as whether it appears in single or paraphrased descriptions, or whether multiple facts are entangled within the same text chunk—may fundamentally determine how difficult unlearning is. No prior work has systematically studied how learning-time knowledge encoding shapes the unlearning process in LLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262939/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The study takes pre-trained LLMs (Llama2-7B, Gemma2-2B, Qwen3-4B), fine-tunes them on synthetic biographical or QA knowledge (Eval-DU+, TOFU+) under controlled encoding regimes, then applies unlearning algorithms (Gradient Ascent, Task Vector) with 1 or 3 unseen textual descriptions per forget-set knowledge piece. The output is a family of unlearned model checkpoints parameterized by unlearning strength (steps t for GA, scaling α for TV), evaluated via normalized area-under-curve (Norm-AUC) trade-off curves and an absolute Retain@Unlearn τ metric that measures retention at a fixed forgetting threshold.

## Method

The paper constructs two augmented datasets (Eval-DU+ and TOFU+) by generating multiple paraphrased descriptions per knowledge piece and paraphrased text chunks per knowledge group using ChatGPT-4o, enabling controlled variation of training-time encoding. Six fine-tuning modes are defined—FT-Single, FT-Unlearn-Mul, FT-Retain-Mul, FT-Mul, FT-Mul-Chunk, and FT-Mul-Chunk-ISO—that systematically vary whether paraphrasing applies to the forget set, retain set, or both, and whether knowledge is isolated per sample or entangled within multi-fact paragraphs. Unlearning is then performed via Gradient Ascent (ascending loss on forget data) and Task Vector (subtracting an overfit direction), with the unlearn-retain trade-off quantified by Norm-AUC across a sweep of strength parameters. The key empirical findings are: (1) paraphrasing the forget set makes unlearning harder while paraphrasing the retain set makes it easier, yet paraphrasing the entire corpus improves overall unlearning; (2) unlearning individual facts entangled within the same chunk as retain knowledge is nearly ineffective (Norm-AUC ≈ 0.5); and (3) aligning the forget set with chunk boundaries or isolating facts into separate sentences substantially recovers unlearning effectiveness.

## Results

The paper reports Norm-AUC (higher is better, 0.5 = no unlearning benefit) as the primary metric across all settings. For Llama2-7B on Eval-DU+, FT-Mul achieves Norm-AUC of 0.63–0.72 (GA/TV), outperforming FT-Single (0.62–0.69), while FT-Unlearn-Mul drops to 0.56–0.65 and FT-Retain-Mul rises to 0.62–0.71. The most striking result is that FT-Mul-Chunk collapses to Norm-AUC ≈ 0.49–0.56 (near the 0.5 random baseline), showing that entangled multi-fact chunks make individual-fact unlearning nearly impossible, whereas FT-Mul-Chunk-ISO recovers to 0.60–0.69. Task Vector consistently outperforms Gradient Ascent by 0.02–0.08 Norm-AUC across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper applies two standard unlearning algorithms—Gradient Ascent (GA, Jang et al. 2022) and Task Vector (TV, Ilharco et al. 2022)—as baselines across all training-encoding conditions. TV consistently outperforms GA by 0.02–0.08 Norm-AUC in nearly every setting (e.g., 0.72 vs. 0.64 for FT-Mul on Llama2-7B/Eval-DU+), confirming TV as the stronger unlearning method. However, the paper's central contribution is not algorithmic: both GA and TV exhibit identical qualitative patterns, showing that training-time encoding dominates unlearning difficulty regardless of algorithm choice. Specifically, paraphrasing the forget set (FT-Unlearn-Mul) degrades Norm-AUC by 0.04–0.11 relative to FT-Single, while paraphrasing the retain set (FT-Retain-Mul) improves it by 0.02–0.12. The most dramatic comparison is FT-Mul-Chunk vs. FT-Mul: Norm-AUC drops from ~0.63–0.72 to ~0.49–0.56, a 0.13–0.22 collapse, demonstrating that chunk-level entanglement is far more damaging than any algorithmic limitation. These findings hold across Llama2-7B, Gemma2-2B, and Qwen3-4B, and are further validated with a third method (Gradient Difference) and an alternative metric (Retain@Unlearn τ) in the appendix.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM knowledge unlearning literature, which has primarily focused on algorithm design (gradient ascent, task vectors, NPO, etc.) under the assumption that the training process and target model are fixed. It builds conceptually on Zhao et al. (2024), who showed that entanglement between forget and retain sets increases unlearning difficulty at the data level, and on Allen-Zhu & Li (2024), who demonstrated that paraphrased training data leads to more structured internal knowledge representations. The key departure is treating learning-time encoding choices—paraphrasing frequency and text chunk structure—as first-class variables that shape unlearning difficulty, a dimension the authors argue has been systematically overlooked in prior unlearning benchmarks and algorithmic studies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, data-curation perspective on unlearning that is orthogonal to algorithmic improvements: practitioners can make future unlearning easier by paraphrasing training descriptions and structuring text to isolate facts that may later need removal. This reframes some apparent algorithmic failures (AUC ≈ 0.5) as consequences of training data structure rather than method weakness, offering a new diagnostic lens for interpreting cross-benchmark and cross-model variance. Key limitations include the restriction to fine-tuning on synthetic biographies (not pre-training), only two unlearning algorithms in the main analysis, and the use of fictional characters that may not capture the complexity of real-world knowledge entanglement. Whether these encoding effects generalize to pre-trained models and to more natural, less controlled corpora remains an open question.
