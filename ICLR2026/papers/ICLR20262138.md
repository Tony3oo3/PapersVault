## Problem

Transformer attention weights (W_Q, W_K, W_V) are typically initialized from random Gaussian or uniform distributions without regard to the spectral structure of the attention mechanism. The paper argues that this random initialization introduces an optimization bias that degrades the conditioning of the attention Jacobian, leading to slower convergence and less stable training, yet principled initialization schemes targeting this property have been largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes only the architectural hyperparameters of a Transformer layer (embedding dimension D, per-head dimension d, and number of heads h) and produces initialized W_Q, W_K, and W_V matrices for each head. W_V is set to the rectangular identity I_{D×d}, while W_Q and W_K are constructed as semi-orthogonal matrices (W^T W = I_d) via truncated SVD of random D×d matrices. No changes to the forward pass, loss function, or training loop are required; the initialization is a one-time substitution at model construction.

## Method

The paper derives a theoretical upper bound on the condition number of the self-attention Jacobian (Theorem 3.1) showing it is controlled by the condition numbers of W_Q, W_K, and W_V. Motivated by this bound, conditioned initialization sets W_V to the rectangular identity (preserving input scale and keeping κ(W_V)=1) and initializes W_Q and W_K as independent semi-orthogonal projections obtained from truncated SVD of random matrices, yielding near-isometric embeddings that diversify per-head attention logits. The key design distinction is that Q and K receive semi-orthogonal rather than identity initialization because their bilinear interaction through S = XW_Q W_K^T X^T would produce anisotropic, coordinate-biased logits under identity initialization. The scheme is architecture-agnostic, compatible with modern normalization layers (RMSNorm, QKNorm), and requires no modification to the training procedure beyond replacing the default weight initialization call.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k, conditioned initialization improves top-1 accuracy across all five Vision Transformer architectures by 1.0–1.2 points over both default and mimetic initialization (e.g., ViT-B: 81.5% vs. 80.3%/80.5%; DaViT-B: 85.3% vs. 84.3%/84.4%). On COCO 2017, it raises bounding-box AP from 44.9 to 45.5 and mask AP from 40.1 to 40.6. On the GLUE benchmark, Crammed BERT with conditioned initialization achieves 79.6 average score versus 78.6 (default) and 78.9 (mimetic), and on GPT-2 TinyStories it reduces perplexity from 2.47 to 2.29. Convergence is accelerated by 20–30% across all tested architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against mimetic initialization (Trockman & Kolter, ICML 2023), conditioned initialization wins on every reported benchmark: +1.0 to +1.2 points top-1 on all five ImageNet-1k ViT variants, +0.6 AP^b on COCO detection, +0.8 to +1.1 points across all five LRA tasks, +0.7 average GLUE score, and −0.18 perplexity on GPT-2 TinyStories. Against weight selection (Xu et al., 2023), the comparison is more nuanced: when both methods use ImageNet-1K-pretrained ViT-S as the source, conditioned initialization (87.3/97.1/81.0 on Food-101/CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100) matches or slightly exceeds the ImageNet-21K + default weight-selection baseline (87.1/97.1/81.1), suggesting that spectral conditioning at initialization can substitute for the extra pretraining data that weight selection typically requires. Overall, conditioned initialization is the strongest method in every head-to-head comparison reported in the paper.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weight-conditioning framework of Saratchandran et al. (2025), which showed that better-conditioned weights improve optimization and proposed during-training matrix preconditioning, by moving the conditioning intervention to initialization time and specializing it to the spectral structure of attention projections. It departs from recent heuristic initialization methods—mimetic initialization (Trockman & Kolter, 2023) and weight selection (Xu et al., 2023)—by grounding the choice of initial weights in an explicit upper bound on the attention Jacobian's condition number (Theorem 3.1), rather than in statistical pattern transfer or teacher-model reuse. The optimization motivation draws on NTK theory (Liu et al., 2022; Yang, 2020), which links Jacobian conditioning to convergence speed, but the paper's specific contribution is the derivation of how W_Q, W_K, W_V condition numbers propagate into the attention Jacobian bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Conditioned initialization is a drop-in, architecture-agnostic technique that consistently yields 0.5–1.5% accuracy gains and 20–30% faster convergence across five vision Transformer families, NLP, detection, and long-range sequence tasks, making it practically attractive for practitioners who want a zero-cost improvement. The main limitation is that the method optimizes a surrogate upper bound on the Jacobian condition number rather than the quantity itself, so the theoretical guarantee is indirect; developing methods to estimate and control the exact Jacobian conditioning during training is identified as a key open direction. Broader implications include the possibility that principled spectral initialization could complement (rather than replace) normalization layers, and that similar conditioning arguments might extend to other parameterized sub-layers beyond attention.
