## Problem

Diffusion-based world models offer high generative fidelity for RL but face a critical efficiency bottleneck in on-policy imagination: each observation requires multiple costly denoising steps, and the standard sequential rollout must interleave policy decisions with world model predictions one frame at a time. This makes training lightweight, deployable policies via imagination computationally prohibitive, especially as diffusion architectures grow larger.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265173/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The world model denoiser operates on sequences of 16×8×8 latent representations (encoded from 64×64 RGB frames via a continuous autoencoder with tanh-constrained outputs in [-1,1]), conditioned on discrete actions and per-observation denoising times via AdaLN. Unlike standard autoregressive generation, the policy is queried at every denoising step (not just on clean observations), producing action distributions over noisy intermediate latents. The full agent outputs a 32-frame imagined trajectory of clean latents, the corresponding discrete actions, and per-step rewards and termination signals from a lightweight RNN predictor.

## Method

Horizon Imagination (HI) denoises h future observations in parallel within a single forward pass of a causal DiT denoiser (rectified flow), rather than sequentially one frame at a time. Two key mechanisms make this stable: (1) a stable discrete action sampling scheme that draws a fixed uniform vector ω and permutation ρ once, then maps them to actions under the evolving policy distribution at each denoising step via a conditional-probability scan (Eq. 2), provably minimizing action changes to at most the total variation distance between successive policy distributions; and (2) the Horizon schedule, a linear-ramp matrix K that decouples the denoising budget B from the decay horizon ν, enabling sub-step budgets (B < h, e.g., 0.5 steps per frame) and independent tuning of both parameters. The actor-critic controller is trained via REINFORCE across all noise levels, with the critic operating only on fully denoised outputs, and advantage scaling via EMA of return quantiles in symlog space.

## Results

On Atari 100K and Craftium, Horizon Imagination with a sub-step budget (ν=4, B=16, i.e., 0.5 denoising steps per frame) maintains control performance comparable to the autoregressive baseline (ν=1, B=32, one step per frame) across all eight environments, while reducing training time from ~27 h to ~19 h per run. In generation-quality evaluation (FVD/MSE over 512 segments of 32 frames), parallel configurations (4≤ν≤16) with sub-step budgets achieve FVD comparable to autoregressive baselines using up to 16× more denoising steps. At extreme budgets (B≥128), the autoregressive baseline (ν=1) still ranks slightly best on FVD, but the gap is small and MSE actually worsens with larger budgets, suggesting perceptual quality plateaus while pixel-level drift increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the autoregressive (sequential) imagination baseline (ν=1, B=32). Both parallel variants (ν=4, B=16 and ν=4, B=32) match or exceed the autoregressive baseline's episodic return across all Atari and Craftium environments, with the B=16 sub-step configuration achieving this at roughly half the denoising cost. Against the Pyramidal schedule of Chen et al. (2024) (Diffusion Forcing), the proposed Horizon schedule shows a decisive advantage: while the Pyramidal schedule suffers a pronounced collapse in FVD and MSE as the denoising budget grows beyond B≈32 (due to the entanglement of budget and decay horizon), the Horizon schedule maintains stable or improving generation quality across all tested budgets up to B=256. In the ablation comparing stable action sampling to naive per-step resampling, the naive baseline shows a substantial drop in episodic return, with particularly severe collapse on Atari Boxing and Gopher, confirming that the stabilization mechanism is critical for parallel imagination to function.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-step parallel generation framework of Diffusion Forcing (Chen et al., 2024) by decoupling the denoising budget from the decay horizon via the proposed Horizon schedule, enabling sub-step budgets (fractional denoising steps per frame) that were inexpressible under the Pyramidal schedule. It departs from sequential imagination approaches (Alonso et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024) that interleave policy and world model calls one observation at a time, and from policy-guided diffusion methods (Rigter et al., 2024; Jackson et al., 2024) that are restricted to continuous action spaces, by introducing a stable discrete action sampling mechanism grounded in a single uniform draw mapped through evolving policy distributions. The actor-critic training objective builds on the symlog-based value estimation of Hafner et al. (2025) and Cohen et al. (2025), while the DiT denoiser architecture and tokenizer follow the Cosmos implementation (Agarwal et al., 2025) adapted to rectified flow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: halving the denoising budget (0.5 steps per frame) while maintaining control performance directly addresses the dominant computational bottleneck in diffusion world model training, making on-policy imagination feasible on modest hardware. The Horizon schedule's clean separation of budget and decay horizon is a generalizable design principle that could transfer to other multi-step diffusion generation settings. Key limitations include evaluation on only eight environments with discrete actions, exploration of a single decay horizon (ν=4) for control, and the absence of comparison against external SOTA world model agents. Open questions include extension to continuous action spaces (sketched in Appendix F), scaling to larger models and longer horizons, and whether the sub-step budget advantage persists in more visually complex or higher-dimensional settings.
