## Problem

Existing LLM-based time series methods perform cross-modality alignment at a single scale, overlooking the multi-scale structures inherent in both natural language (words, phrases, sentences) and time series (daily, weekly, seasonal patterns). This leads to two critical gaps: the semantic space disparity between rich natural language and sparse time series representations, and the inability of frozen LLMs to reason over multi-scale temporal patterns without appropriate contextual guidance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multivariate time series of shape T×D (normalized via reversible instance normalization) and frozen LLM word token embeddings of shape V×P (e.g., from LLaMA-7B or GPT-2). The time series is hierarchically aggregated into S=3 scales via 1D convolutions, while word embeddings are linearly mapped into progressively smaller sets of text prototypes at each scale. Outputs are produced through a linear projection followed by instance denormalization, yielding forecasts (H×D), class labels, or imputed values depending on the downstream task.

## Method

MSH-LLM introduces three key mechanisms to perform multi-scale cross-modality alignment with a frozen LLM. First, a hyperedging mechanism constructs learnable hypergraph structures at each scale: node and hyperedge embeddings are transformed via tanh, their similarity forms a sparse incidence matrix (TopK thresholding), and hyperedge features are computed by averaging connected node features, capturing implicit group-wise interactions in a data-driven manner. Second, a Cross-Modality Alignment (CMA) module applies multi-head cross-attention at each scale, using hyperedge features as queries and text prototypes as keys/values to align the two modalities. Third, a Mixture of Prompts (MoP) mechanism concatenates three prompt types—scale-specific learnable prompts, data-correlated prompts (description, task instruction, statistics), and capability-enhancing prompts (logical thinking, emotional manipulation, time-series reasoning cues)—with the aligned features before feeding them into the frozen LLM, thereby activating its reasoning over multi-scale temporal patterns.

## Results

On long-term forecasting (input length 512, horizons 96/192/336/720), MSH-LLM achieves the best MSE and MAE across all 7 datasets, with headline values of 0.217/0.254 (Weather), 0.159/0.253 (Electricity), 0.381/0.283 (Traffic), and 0.402/0.420 (ETTh1). The paper reports average error reductions of 4.10% (MSE) and 3.72% (MAE) over LLM4TS baselines, 8.54% and 6.45% over Transformer-based methods, and 7.48% and 5.58% over linear-based methods. On short-term M4 forecasting, MSH-LLM achieves 11.659 SMAPE, 1.557 MASE, and 0.837 OWA, outperforming AutoTimes (11.831 SMAPE) and all other baselines. For zero-shot cross-dataset transfer (M3↔M4), MSH-LLM achieves 12.469 and 12.968 SMAPE respectively, yielding a 10.23% average SMAPE reduction over all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSH-LLM achieves the best MSE and MAE on all 7 long-term forecasting datasets, outperforming every baseline without exception. Among LLM4TS methods, the closest competitor is S²IP-LLM (ICML 2024), which trails by 0.004–0.020 MSE depending on dataset; Time-LLM (ICLR 2024) and AutoTimes (NeurIPS 2024) are further behind, with AutoTimes showing the largest gap on ETTm2 (0.252 vs. 0.258 MSE). Against non-LLM baselines, the gap widens substantially: iTransformer (ICLR 2024) lags by 0.024–0.049 MSE, MSHyper (arXiv 2024) by 0.004–0.025 MSE, and DLinear (AAAI 2023) by 0.007–0.160 MSE, with the largest advantage on ETTh2 (0.342 vs. 0.502 MSE). On short-term M4 forecasting, MSH-LLM edges out AutoTimes by 1.45% SMAPE and S²IP-LLM by 3.01% SMAPE, while leading Time-LLM and FPT by 6.68% and 8.13% respectively. In few-shot (5% data) and zero-shot (cross-dataset) settings, MSH-LLM maintains its lead over all 11 and 9 baselines respectively, with the zero-shot advantage reaching 10.23% average SMAPE reduction, confirming that the multi-scale hypergraph alignment and MoP mechanism generalize well under data scarcity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own line of multi-scale hypergraph work (MSHyper, Ada-MSHyper) by replacing pre-defined hypergraph structures with learnable hyperedges and, critically, by coupling the multi-scale temporal features with frozen LLMs via a cross-modality alignment module. It departs from prior LLM4TS methods (Time-LLM, S2IP-LLM, AutoTimes) that perform single-scale alignment between time series patches and text prototypes, arguing that both natural language and time series inherently possess multi-scale structures that should be aligned jointly. The mixture-of-prompts mechanism also extends the single-prompt or prefix-prompt strategies in prior work by combining learnable, data-correlated, and capability-enhancing prompts into a unified input to the LLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being the first to perform multi-scale cross-modality alignment between time series and LLMs, the paper opens a path toward more principled integration of the rich hierarchical semantics in language with the multi-scale temporal patterns in time series, potentially reducing the semantic space disparity that has limited LLM-based time series methods. The MoP mechanism, particularly the capability-enhancing prompts (logical thinking, emotional manipulation, time-series reasoning), offers a reusable recipe for activating LLM reasoning in non-linguistic domains, though the 'emotional manipulation' component remains somewhat ad-hoc and its generalizability is untested. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger LLMs (beyond LLaMA-7B), whether the learned text prototypes develop stable semantic roles across domains, and whether the hyperedging mechanism can be extended to other structured modalities beyond time series.
