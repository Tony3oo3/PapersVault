## Problem

Latent diffusion, while highly effective for image generation, suffers from posterior collapse when applied to time series: the latent variable's influence on the autoregressive decoder vanishes exponentially over time, reducing the model to a weak VAE. This paper provides a rigorous analysis showing that a collapsed posterior makes latent diffusion no more expressive than a vanilla VAE, and introduces dependency measures to empirically confirm and quantify this degradation on real time-series data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261929/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model samples a latent variable z from a reverse diffusion process (L=1000 steps, U-Net parameterized) starting from standard Gaussian noise, then feeds z into an autoregressive decoder (LSTM or Transformer) that sequentially generates D-dimensional observations x_1, ..., x_T conditioned on z and all prior observations. At training, raw multivariate time series X ∈ R^{TD} (e.g., 12-hour clinical windows from MIMIC/WARDS with top-5 variance features) are encoded into a 128-dimensional latent space, with the conditional generation distribution p^gen(X|z) parameterized as a Gaussian with learnable mean and diagonal covariance.

## Method

The proposed posterior-stable latent diffusion reinterprets the forward diffusion process as variational inference: instead of the VAE reparameterization trick, the encoder output v is converted to latent variable z by applying N early diffusion steps (i ~ U{0,N}), which adds controlled Gaussian noise without any KL-divergence term. A second key mechanism is a collapse-simulation loss L^CS that applies late diffusion steps (i ~ U{M,L}) to produce highly noisy, non-informative latent variables, then penalizes the decoder for assigning high conditional density p^gen(X|z) to such variables—directly countering dependency illusion where the decoder relies on prefix observations rather than z. Training jointly optimizes three losses: a weighted likelihood loss L^VI (with weight α̅^{γi} to down-weight noisy z), the standard diffusion denoising loss L^DM, and the collapse penalty L^CS. The framework permits unrestricted prior distributions for z (sampled via the reverse diffusion process at test time) and requires no KL annealing, variable masking, or skip connections to maintain posterior stability.

## Results

Using Wasserstein distance (lower is better) as the primary metric, the proposed posterior-stable latent diffusion achieves 2.13 on MIMIC, 3.01 on WARDS, and 2.49 on Earthquakes with a Transformer backbone, substantially outperforming vanilla latent diffusion (5.02, 7.46, 5.91 respectively). With an LSTM backbone, the framework scores 2.29, 3.16, and 2.67 on the same datasets. On additional datasets (Retail, Energy) evaluated with MMD, the framework achieves 0.025 and 0.031 with Transformer, again the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed framework decisively outperforms all baselines across every dataset and backbone. Against vanilla latent diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022), it reduces Wasserstein distance by roughly 57% on MIMIC (2.13 vs 5.02) and 60% on WARDS (3.01 vs 7.46) with Transformer. Against the most recent time-series generative baselines in Table 2, it beats Neural STPP (Chen et al., 2021) by 2.00 on MIMIC (2.13 vs 5.13) and 3.33 on Earthquakes (2.49 vs 5.82), Neural Latent Dynamic (Li et al., 2024) by 2.18 on MIMIC (2.13 vs 4.31), and Frequency Diffusion (Crabbé et al., 2024) by 2.43 on MIMIC (2.13 vs 4.56). Among the posterior-collapse mitigation strategies (KL annealing, variable masking, skip connections), the framework still leads by 1.62–2.53 points in Wasserstein distance, indicating that simply patching the KL term or decoder is insufficient compared to the principled re-interpretation of diffusion as variational inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends latent diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022) to time-series generation by reinterpreting its VAE autoencoder component through the lens of posterior collapse. It departs from the standard VAE formulation by eliminating the KL-divergence term entirely—arguing that the diffusion model already handles complex prior sampling—while introducing a novel 'collapse simulation' loss that penalizes decoder insensitivity to the latent variable. The dependency measure, inspired by integrated gradients (Sundararajan et al., 2017), provides a principled diagnostic that goes beyond the ad-hoc mitigations (KL annealing, variable masking, skip connections) previously proposed for sequence autoencoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical result that posterior collapse reduces latent diffusion to a vanilla VAE (Proposition 3.1) reframes a practical training pathology as a fundamental expressiveness limitation, motivating the removal of KL regularization rather than its tuning. The dependency measure offers a broadly applicable diagnostic for any autoregressive conditional generator, not just diffusion models. Open questions include whether the framework scales to very long sequences (beyond the 12-hour windows tested), how it interacts with more complex decoder architectures, and whether the collapse-simulation loss generalizes to non-Gaussian conditional distributions. The modest improvements on image data (Table 7) confirm the problem is specific to autoregressive decoders, narrowing the scope of applicability.
