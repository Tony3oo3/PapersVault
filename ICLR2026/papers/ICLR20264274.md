## Problem

PU learning algorithms have been evaluated under highly inconsistent experimental settings, making it impossible to determine which method is truly superior. Two critical issues are overlooked: (1) many algorithms rely on validation sets containing negative labels, contradicting the core PU assumption that negatives are unavailable, and (2) the internal label shift (ILS) between one-sample and two-sample data generation settings causes TS algorithms to be unfairly penalized when evaluated under OS protocols.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes positive training sets D_P and unlabeled training sets D_U (images at 32×32 for CIFAR-10, 128×128 for ImageNette, or tabular feature vectors for USPS/Letter) and produces a binary classifier scoring each instance. Unusually, model selection must be performed using only PU validation data (no negative labels), and the proposed calibration step augments the unlabeled mini-batch with the positive mini-batch during training so that the combined set is marginally unbiased with respect to the true data distribution.

## Method

The paper proposes three interlocking contributions. First, it defines two PU-compatible model selection criteria—proxy accuracy (PA) and proxy AUC (PAUC)—with theoretical guarantees (Propositions 1–2) that the classifier maximizing the expected proxy metric also maximizes the true metric, eliminating the need for labeled validation data. Second, it formally identifies the ILS problem: under the OS setting the unlabeled data density has a shifted class prior π̄=(1−c)π/(1−cπ), breaking the unbiasedness of TS risk estimators. Third, it derives a calibrated risk estimator (Theorem 1) and a simple calibration procedure (Algorithm 1) that augments D_U with D_P when computing the unlabeled loss term, restoring marginal unbiasedness and yielding a consistency guarantee (Theorem 2) that the calibrated minimizer converges to the Bayes-optimal classifier. The full benchmark unifies 17 PU algorithms across four datasets with standardized hyperparameter search, data splits, and evaluation metrics.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (Case 1, 30% positive rate), the calibrated Dist-PU (Dist-PU-c) achieves the best accuracy of 87.06±0.45% and AUC of 94.55±0.21 under PA/PAUC validation, outperforming all 17 baselines. The proposed calibration approach consistently improves TS algorithms under the OS setting: e.g., uPU improves from 80.24% to 85.89% accuracy, VPU from 84.93% to 86.41%, and Dist-PU from 81.64% to 87.06%. No single algorithm dominates across all four datasets and five test metrics; some early methods (PUbN, P3MIX-C) remain competitive, while Holistic-PU and P3MIX-E degrade to near-random performance (≈50% accuracy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark compares 17 PU learning algorithms across cost-sensitive (uPU, nnPU, nnPU-GA, PUSB, VPU, Dist-PU), sample-selection (Robust-PU, Holistic-PU, P3MIX-E/C), and biased (PUe, LBE, GLWS, PUbN, PAN, CVIR, Count Loss) families. The calibration approach (Algorithm 1) consistently improves all TS cost-sensitive methods under the OS setting: Dist-PU gains +5.42% accuracy, uPU gains +5.65%, VPU gains +1.48%, and nnPU gains +3.49% on CIFAR-10 Case 1. Among uncalibrated methods, PUbN (86.46%) and P3MIX-C (86.36%) are the strongest baselines on CIFAR-10 Case 1 accuracy, but both are outperformed by Dist-PU-c (87.06%). On ImageNette Case 1, CVIR (78.78% acc) and P3MIX-C (78.81%) lead among uncalibrated methods, while VPU-c (77.48%) and P3MIX-C remain competitive after calibration. Holistic-PU and P3MIX-E fail catastrophically (≈50% accuracy) under PA/PAUC validation across all datasets, indicating severe overfitting or degenerate solutions. The model selection criterion matters: PAUC-selected models generally achieve better AUC than OA-selected ones, while PA-selected models tend to yield better accuracy, confirming that the validation metric should match the target test metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the foundational PU learning literature, particularly the unbiased risk estimator framework of du Plessis et al. (2015) and the theoretical analysis of du Plessis et al. (2014), to create the first unified benchmark for systematically comparing seventeen PU learning algorithms. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it identifies the internal label shift (ILS) problem, where the class prior of unlabeled data under the one-sample setting differs from the test distribution, breaking the theoretical guarantees of two-sample algorithms—a mismatch that existing evaluation protocols had overlooked; and (2) it replaces the unrealistic assumption of a fully labeled validation set with PU-only model selection criteria (proxy accuracy and proxy AUC), grounded in theoretical propositions. The paper positions itself as a methodological correction to the field's inconsistent experimental practices rather than proposing a new algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of ILS as a systematic evaluation pitfall is significant because it means that published comparisons of TS algorithms under OS protocols have been unfairly penalizing them, potentially misleading the field about which methods are truly competitive. The proposed calibration (Algorithm 1) is remarkably simple—augmenting the unlabeled set with the positive set when computing the unlabeled loss—yet it restores theoretical consistency and consistently improves performance across six TS algorithms and multiple datasets. Limitations include the use of relatively small benchmark datasets (CIFAR-10, ImageNette, USPS, Letter) and the requirement of knowing or estimating the class prior π. Open questions include whether the ILS phenomenon and calibration generalize to large-scale, real-world PU datasets, and whether the model selection findings (PA vs. PAUC vs. OA) hold under different test metrics and data regimes.
