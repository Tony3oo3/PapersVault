## Problem

Variance-dependent regret upper bounds for linear contextual bandits have been well studied, but matching lower bounds remain largely absent. The only prior lower bound (Jia et al., 2024) suffers from a √d gap relative to known upper bounds and applies only to a fixed total variance budget rather than arbitrary variance sequences. This paper asks whether tight variance-dependent lower bounds can be established for general (prefixed or adaptive) variance sequences.

## Input / Output

The construction takes a variance sequence {σ₁,…,σ_K} (either fully revealed upfront or adaptively chosen by an adversary based on past observations), a dimension d, and a horizon K, and produces a hard linear contextual bandit instance with orthogonal decision sets D_k per round. The output is a regret lower bound of Ω(d√(Σσ_k²)/log K) for prefixed sequences and Ω(d√(Σσ_k²)/log⁶(dK)) for adaptive sequences with a weak adversary, both matching the SAVE upper bound up to logarithmic factors. A key constraint is that total variance must be at least Ω(d²) for the bound to be non-trivial, and the strong-adversary setting (variance chosen after seeing D_k) is shown to admit no variance-dependent lower bound at all.

## Method

The core technique is a peeling construction that partitions rounds into L = ⌈log₂K⌉+1 groups by variance magnitude (σ_k in (2^{i-1}/K, 2^i/K]) and assigns each group an orthogonal sub-instance of dimension d/L, so that information from one group cannot transfer to another. For prefixed sequences, each sub-instance inherits the standard hard-instance lower bound scaled by its variance threshold, and the total regret decomposes as a sum over groups. For adaptive sequences where group sizes are unknown, a multi-instance framework creates L instances per group (one per possible round-count interval 2^{j-1} ≤ |K_i| < 2^j) and cycles the learner through them, guaranteeing one instance matches the true interval. To handle the adaptive setting's need for high-probability guarantees, an auxiliary algorithm detects when cumulative regret exceeds a threshold and switches to OFUL, converting the expected lower bound to a constant-probability one, which is then boosted to 1−1/K via Ω(log²(dK)) independent copies. Finally, a counter-example with a strong adversary (choosing σ_k after seeing D_k) demonstrates that the learner and adversary can cooperate to achieve O(d) regret even with total variance Ω(K), proving no variance-dependent lower bound is possible in that regime.

## Results

The paper establishes variance-dependent regret lower bounds for linear contextual bandits that match the SAVE upper bound Õ(d√(Σσ_k²)) up to logarithmic factors. For prefixed variance sequences (Theorem 4.1), the expected regret lower bound is Ω(d√(Σσ_k²)/log K). For adaptive variance sequences with a weak adversary (Theorem 5.2), a high-probability lower bound of Ω(d√(Σσ_k²)/log⁶(dK)) is achieved — the first high-probability lower bound for linear contextual bandits. For a strong adversary that observes the decision set before choosing variance, the paper shows no variance-dependent lower bound is possible, as a cooperating adversary-learner pair achieves O(d) regret with total variance Ω(K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's experiments (Appendix A) evaluate OFUL (Abbasi-Yadkori et al. 2011) and Weighted OFUL (Zhou et al. 2021) on constructed hard instances with D=10 dimensions, K=4000 rounds, and a piecewise variance shift from σ=0.1 to σ=1.0 at round 2000. OFUL, being variance-agnostic, shows comparable cumulative regret in both the low-variance and high-variance periods, confirming it cannot exploit heteroscedastic structure. Weighted OFUL, which incorporates variance information, achieves significantly lower regret during the initial low-variance phase under piecewise assignment but incurs substantially higher regret in the high-variance phase because information from the orthogonal sub-instance does not transfer. Under alternating assignment (switching sub-instances every round), Weighted OFUL performs best overall, as each sub-instance receives balanced exposure to both variance levels, enabling tighter confidence sets throughout. These results empirically validate the paper's theoretical construction: the orthogonal peeling strategy that assigns different decision sets based on variance magnitude is necessary to prevent early low-variance exploration from being wasted on a sub-instance that later faces high-variance exploitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the variance-dependent lower bound of Jia et al. (2024), which only handled a fixed total variance budget Λ and suffered a √d gap from known upper bounds, to arbitrary variance sequences in both prefixed and adaptive settings. The core construction builds directly on the hard-instance framework of Zhou et al. (2021) for standard linear bandits, scaling it by a variance parameter σ and combining multiple such instances via an orthogonal peeling decomposition that groups rounds by variance magnitude. The resulting lower bounds match the SAVE algorithm's upper bound (Zhao et al., 2023) up to logarithmic factors, establishing near-minimax optimality for the first time in the variance-dependent regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a significant gap in the theory of heteroscedastic linear bandits by showing the SAVE upper bound is essentially tight, and introduces a peeling technique that may be reusable for other variance-aware lower bound constructions. The strong-adversary impossibility result is a fundamental negative finding: if the environment can set variance after observing the decision set, no variance-dependent lower bound can exist, which delineates the precise boundary of when variance-aware guarantees are meaningful. The first high-probability lower bound for linear contextual bandits and the multi-instance framework for handling unknown group sizes are techniques of independent interest. The main open question is extending these results beyond linear bandits to general function approximation with finite eluder dimension.
