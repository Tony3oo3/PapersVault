## Problem

Evaluating robot control policies is expensive and risky: real-world testing is costly and can cause physical damage, while handcrafted simulators require extensive manual engineering and suffer from sim-to-real gaps. The paper asks whether a single video world model, trained on diverse robot data, can serve as a universal proxy environment for evaluating any policy on any task, leveraging the observation that while tasks and policies are numerous, the underlying physical world is singular.

## Input / Output

WorldGym requires only a single 256×256 RGB initial frame from a real robot (no full video context), a 7-dimensional action sequence (6D end-effector position + binary gripper) predicted by a VLA policy, and a natural-language task instruction. At inference, the world model autoregressively generates one frame per action step, with the parallel denoising horizon dynamically set to match the policy's action chunk size. The final generated video is scored by GPT-4o as a binary (or 0/0.5/1) success reward, yielding a Monte Carlo estimate of policy value.

## Method

The world model is a 609M-parameter latent Diffusion Transformer (16 layers, 1024 hidden dims) trained with Diffusion Forcing for autoregressive frame generation, using the Stable Diffusion 3 VAE to encode 256×256 frames. Per-frame 7-dim robot actions are linearly projected and added elementwise to diffusion timestep embeddings, then injected via AdaLN-Zero modulation; classifier-free guidance with random action dropout ensures strong action adherence. Causal temporal attention blocks interleaved with spatial attention enable conditioning on a sliding window of the last 20 frames. The key architectural contribution is flexibly setting the parallel denoising horizon equal to the policy's action chunk size at inference, avoiding the fixed 16-frame constraint of prior diffusion world models like Cosmos and enabling efficient long-horizon rollouts across policies with heterogeneous chunk sizes. The evaluation loop iterates: policy predicts an action chunk → world model generates corresponding frames → latest frame is fed back as the policy's next observation, repeating until the rollout horizon is reached, after which GPT-4o scores task completion from the full video.

## Results

WorldGym achieves a Pearson correlation of r=0.78 between per-task policy success rates in the world model and in the real world across 17 Bridge evaluation tasks. Mean success rates differ by only 3.3% on average: RT-1-X scores 15.5% in WorldGym vs. 18.5% real-world, Octo scores 23.82% vs. 20.0%, and OpenVLA scores 67.4% vs. 70.6%. Policy rankings are preserved across model versions (Octo-Small < Octo-Base; OpenVLA v0.1 < OpenVLA 7B) and training checkpoints (success rate increases monotonically with training steps for both UniPi and DexVLA policies). On OOD distractor tasks, OpenVLA is most robust with a 41.5% performance drop, compared to 51% for RT-1-X and 83% for Octo.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates three VLA-based robot policies—RT-1-X, Octo, and OpenVLA—both in the real world and within WorldGym, using the OpenVLA Bridge evaluation suite of 17 tasks. WorldGym preserves the known real-world ranking (OpenVLA > Octo > RT-1-X) with mean success rates of 67.4%, 23.82%, and 15.5% respectively, compared to 70.6%, 20.0%, and 18.5% in the real world. Across model versions, WorldGym correctly assigns higher scores to larger and more recent models (Octo-Base 1.5 > Octo-Small 1.5; OpenVLA 7B > OpenVLA v0.1 7B), consistent with published real-world results. Across training checkpoints, WorldGym success rates increase monotonically with training steps for both UniPi (video prediction) and DexVLA (diffusion) policies, matching their decreasing validation MSE. On OOD language tasks, OpenVLA again leads (e.g., 7/10 on 'Move Pot Into Drying Rack' vs. 3/10 for RT-1-X and 0/10 for Octo), attributed to its stronger VLM backbone. On OOD image tasks with distractors, all policies degrade significantly, but OpenVLA is most robust (39.4% mean success vs. 7.6% for RT-1-X and 4.1% for Octo).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model-based offline policy evaluation lineage (Hafner et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2021) by learning a single action-conditioned video world model across diverse robot morphologies and tasks, rather than task-specific dynamics models. Architecturally, it builds on Diffusion Forcing (Chen et al., 2024) for autoregressive generation and causal temporal attention from Genie (Bruce et al., 2024) and LATTE (Ma et al., 2025), while introducing flexible horizon alignment with variable-length policy action chunks as a practical departure from fixed-context approaches like Cosmos. It positions itself against SIMPLER (Li et al., 2024), which constructs software simulators from images but struggles with OOD inputs, and WorldEval (Li et al., 2025), which is limited to a single bi-manual setup, by offering a unified generative environment that supports arbitrary initial frames and language instructions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WorldGym provides a practical, GPU-only pipeline for pre-deployment policy evaluation that avoids the cost and risk of real-robot testing, with the encouraging result that simulated success rates correlate at r=0.78 with real-world outcomes and preserve relative policy rankings. However, the 3.3% average gap in mean success rates, per-task discrepancies (e.g., OpenVLA scoring 4/10 vs. 8/10 on 'Put Carrot on Plate'), and acknowledged difficulty in generating realistic object interactions limit its fidelity for fine-grained policy comparison. Open questions include whether compounding error degrades evaluation quality over longer horizons, whether the world model can be improved for deformable/soft-object manipulation, and whether the VLM-as-reward approach generalizes beyond the sparse binary reward setting. The OOD findings—that modern VLAs cannot reliably distinguish object shapes and are distracted by 2D facades—offer concrete targets for future policy research.
