## Problem

Inverted bottleneck structures in lightweight CNNs rely on expensive pointwise convolutions for channel expansion, where GhostNet showed many expanded channels are linearly redundant. However, existing feature-reuse methods (GhostNet, FasterNet) operate through linear transformations or partial convolutions, leaving the intrinsic nonlinear representational capacity of Hadamard products unexploited as a dedicated, parameter-free channel expansion operator in resource-constrained vision models.

## Input / Output

The network consumes standard RGB images at 224×224 (ImageNet-1K), 32×32 (CIFAR-100), or 320×320 (COCO detection) and outputs class probabilities or bounding boxes with class labels. Internally, the ACH module operates on intermediate feature maps of shape C×H×W, expanding the channel dimension from C to C + C_s(C_s−1)/2 by computing cross-Hadamard products over a learned subset of C_s channel pairs, then concatenating the results with the original features.

## Method

The Adaptive Cross-Hadamard (ACH) module replaces the expensive expansion convolution in inverted bottlenecks with a parameter-free nonlinear operation: an ECA module scores all input channels, Gumbel-TopK sampling with gradient-norm-adaptive temperature selects C_s channels differentiably (using a straight-through estimator for backpropagation), and pairwise cross-Hadamard products of the selected channels generate new feature maps. Outputs are stabilized by DySoft normalization (a bounded softsign activation with learnable affine parameters) rather than BatchNorm, which the authors prove is necessary to prevent multiplicative variance explosion from cross-channel coupling. The module is integrated into Hadaptive-Net via differentiable NAS (GDAS) over a search space that co-optimizes ACH versus Ghost modules per layer, revealing that ACH is preferentially selected in high-dimensional late-stage layers where its O(m²) expansion cost becomes advantageous over O(mn) pointwise convolutions. Custom CUDA kernels (Direct-Indexing and Parity-Balanced) further reduce GPU latency by ~42% over naive decomposition.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, Hadaptive-Net-L (6.11M params, 669M FLOPs) achieves 80.79% top-1 accuracy, outperforming StarNet-S4 (7.22M, 1050M FLOPs) by 2.67% and MobileNetV4-M (8.56M, 827M FLOPs) by 0.91% while using fewer FLOPs. On CIFAR-100, Hadaptive-Net-S (2.10M, 131M FLOPs) reaches 73.57%, surpassing MobileOne-S0 (2.10M, 275M FLOPs) by 3.87% with half the FLOPs. For object detection on COCO, Hadaptive-Net-L achieves 23.2 mAP@0.5:0.95 with SSD, exceeding GhostNetV3-1.0 (22.7) and MobileNetV3-S (21.7). The ACH module also improves MobileViT-small by 0.72% on CIFAR-100 without increasing parameters or FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hadaptive-Net-S (2.10M params) decisively outperforms MobileOne-S0 (same 2.10M params) by 3.87% on CIFAR-100 and 2.54% on ImageNet-1K while using less than half the FLOPs (131M vs 275M). At the mid-scale, Hadaptive-Net-M (3.09M) beats StarNet-S2 (3.43M) by 6.40% on CIFAR-100 and 3.29% on ImageNet-1K with 38% fewer FLOPs, and surpasses MobileNetV4-S (2.62M) by 0.95% on CIFAR-100 and 4.27% on ImageNet-1K. At the large scale, Hadaptive-Net-L (6.11M) leads StarNet-S4 (7.22M) by 5.76% on CIFAR-100 and 2.67% on ImageNet-1K with 36% fewer FLOPs, and edges out MobileNetV4-M (8.56M) by 0.07% on CIFAR-100 and 0.91% on ImageNet-1K with 19% fewer FLOPs. The only baseline that surpasses Hadaptive-Net-L on ImageNet-1K is MobileNetV4-L (82.92% vs 80.79%), but at 5.1× the parameters and 3.2× the FLOPs. For object detection, Hadaptive-Net-L achieves 23.2 mAP, beating GhostNetV3-1.0 (22.7) and MobileNetV3-S (21.7). On NLP (BERT), ACH underperforms the standard FFN baseline (76.2 vs 80.3 on SST-2 for BERT-6), indicating the module's advantage is most pronounced in CV channel-expansion contexts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature-reuse paradigm initiated by GhostNet (which replaces redundant convolutional channels with cheap linear transformations) by introducing a nonlinear alternative: the Adaptive Cross-Hadamard (ACH) module, which generates expanded channels via parameter-free element-wise products of selected input channels. It draws on the theoretical observation that stacked Hadamard products induce implicit high-dimensional nonlinear mappings (Ma et al., 2024), but departs from prior Hadamard applications (gating in LSTMs, bilinear pooling, attention shortcuts) by positioning the product as a primary channel-expansion operator rather than an auxiliary modulation. The differentiable discrete sampling mechanism (Gumbel-TopK + STE) and the DySoft normalization are novel engineering contributions that make the Hadamard-based expansion trainable and stable, while the gradient-based NAS framework (Dong & Yang, 2019) is repurposed not to search a new architecture from scratch but to discover optimal ACH integration points within a fixed backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes Hadamard products as a viable, parameter-free alternative to pointwise convolutions for channel expansion, with the plug-and-play experiments (replacing only the last two layers of ShuffleNetV2, MobileNetV3, StarNet) demonstrating broad architectural compatibility. However, the MobileNetV4 degradation (73.15→72.19%) and the modest NLP results (BERT-6 ACH at 76.2 vs. standard FFN at 80.3 on SST-2) suggest the module's benefits are architecture-dependent and not universally transferable. Open questions include whether ACH can be co-designed into multi-scale fusion structures like FPNs (as the authors themselves suggest), whether the NAS-discovered depth-dependency principle generalizes to larger backbones, and whether the custom CUDA kernels can be integrated into mainstream inference frameworks without the current ONNX Runtime limitations.
