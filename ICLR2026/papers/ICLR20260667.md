## Problem

Existing multimodal benchmarks evaluate general cross-modal question-answering ability but do not measure whether omni-modal language models (OLLMs) reason in a truly modality-invariant manner or exhibit systematic biases tied to specific input modalities. It remains unclear whether models converge on the same answer when semantically identical content is presented as spoken audio, written text, or visual images, and no prior benchmark systematically covers all six cross-modal directions among audio, vision, and text.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260667/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each question is a four-choice multiple-choice item consisting of a context (question stem) and four candidates (answer options), where the context and candidates are each drawn from a different modality (text, vision, or audio). The same semantic instance is instantiated in all six modality configurations (A→T, A→V, T→A, T→V, V→A, V→T), producing 61,320 QA pairs from 10,220 unique instances. Beyond raw accuracy, the evaluation produces modality disparity scores (paired subtractions isolating the effect of modality substitution) and directional imbalance scores (accuracy gaps between inverse context-candidate configurations).

## Method

XModBench's core design is a modality-balanced permutation scheme: for every question, text (T), vision (V), and audio (A) are systematically swapped across the context and candidate slots to generate six configurations of semantically identical questions, ensuring no single modality is privileged. This balanced design supports three diagnostic axes: (1) task competence via averaging accuracy across all six configurations, (2) modality disparity via paired comparisons that differ only by substituting one modality for another (e.g., T→A vs. T→V), and (3) directional imbalance via comparing inverse settings (e.g., V→T vs. T→V). The benchmark spans five task families (perception, spatial reasoning, temporal reasoning, linguistic understanding, external knowledge) with 17 subtasks, built from re-annotated datasets (VGG-Sound, STARSS23), synthetic content (FireRedTTS speech, rendered text images), and targeted web collection, all verified through LLM filtering and human-in-the-loop annotation.

## Results

On XModBench (61,320 multiple-choice QA pairs across 5 task families and 6 modality configurations), Gemini 2.5 Pro achieves the highest average accuracy of 70.6% (Std 11.7), followed by Gemini 2.5 Flash (63.7%) and Gemini 2.0 Flash (61.2%). Among open-source models, EchoInk-R1 (59.2%) and Qwen2.5-Omni (58.6%, Std 10.1) lead, surpassing Gemini 1.5 Pro (55.0%). Spatial reasoning remains the weakest task family even for the best model (50.1% for Gemini 2.5 Pro), while perception and linguistic understanding reach 75.9% and 76.8% respectively; human performance is 91.5%, leaving a ~21-point gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini 2.5 Pro (70.6% avg) outperforms all other models by a wide margin, beating Gemini 2.5 Flash (63.7%) by 6.9 points and the strongest open-source model EchoInk-R1 (59.2%) by 11.4 points. Among open-source OLLMs, Qwen2.5-Omni (58.6%) and EchoInk-R1 (59.2%) are the top performers, both surpassing Gemini 1.5 Pro (55.0%) by 3.6 and 4.2 points respectively, and exceeding Baichuan Omni 1.5 (48.7%) by roughly 10 points. Older or smaller omni-modal models—VideoLLaMA 2 (36.5%), VITA (35.4%), Unified-IO 2 XXL (33.0%), and PandaGPT (24.7%)—perform near or below the 25% random-guess baseline, indicating limited cross-modal competence. In terms of modality robustness (Std. across six configurations), Qwen2.5-Omni is the most consistent competitive model (Std 10.1), while Gemini 1.5 Pro (Std 16.7) and VideoLLaMA 2 (Std 17.4) show the greatest sensitivity to modality swaps. Notably, even the best model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) trails human performance (91.5%) by 20.9 points, with the largest gaps in spatial reasoning (50.1% vs. 89.7%) and temporal reasoning (60.8% vs. 88.9%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-modal consistency concept introduced by Zhang et al. (2024) for vision-text pairs and Park et al. (2025) for video QA to the full tri-modal setting (audio, vision, text), systematically covering all six directional modality configurations. It departs from existing multimodal QA benchmarks (AVQA, OmniBench, MME, etc.) that evaluate cross-modal understanding but fix context or candidate to a single modality, by permuting both context and candidate across modalities to isolate consistency. The data curation pipeline repurposes existing corpora (VGG-Sound, STARSS23) and synthetic generation (FireRedTTS) into a balanced multiple-choice format, but the core contribution is the modality-balanced evaluation design rather than new data sources.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

XModBench provides the first large-scale diagnostic tool for measuring whether OLLMs achieve modality-invariant reasoning, revealing that even the strongest model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) shows a 49-point audio-text disparity and systematic directional imbalance. The finding that post-training on non-interleaved data (EchoInk-R1) can erode cross-modal alignment inherited from pre-training is a practical warning for model developers. Open questions include whether interleaved training at scale can close the audio gap, whether the directional imbalance reflects fundamental architectural limitations or is addressable through training data composition, and how these findings generalize to real-world scenarios where modalities arrive jointly rather than in isolation.
