## Problem

Training-free video motion transfer methods built on Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbones suffer from two structural sources of computational inefficiency: motion redundancy, where global token-to-token attention similarities are computed despite frame-to-frame motion being small and locally smooth, and gradient redundancy, where gradients are recomputed at every inner optimization step even though they change slowly along the diffusion trajectory. Prior acceleration efforts target generic DiT computation but do not address these task-specific redundancies.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference video (81 frames at 480×832, encoded to latents of shape (1,16,21,60,104) via a 3D VAE) and a text prompt describing the target content, and produces a motion-transferred video of the same length and resolution that preserves the reference's camera trajectory and object dynamics while matching the prompt's appearance. Sliding-window AMF guidance is applied only during the first 20% of 50 outer denoising steps, with a 10-step latent-only inner optimization (AdamW, LR 0.003→0.002) at each guided step, extracting queries/keys from the 15th DiT block.

## Method

FastVMT introduces two efficiency mechanisms on top of the Wan-2.1 DiT backbone. First, a sliding-window motion extraction strategy partitions the spatial token grid into 3×4 tiles, uses representative center queries to estimate per-tile window centers via argmax over downsampled attention maps, then constrains Attention Motion Flow (AMF) computation to a local l×l spatial window (l=21) and temporal span s_f=5, reducing temporal complexity from O(F²) to O(F); a corresponding-window loss penalizes first-order temporal variation of window-averaged key features to enforce temporal stability. Second, a step-skipping gradient optimization computes the full gradient only every Δ=3 inner optimization steps and reuses the cached gradient on intermediate steps, cutting gradient computations from J to ⌈J/Δ⌉ per guidance step. Together these yield a 3.43× average speedup (184 s vs. 627 s for DiTFlow) while improving motion fidelity (0.7471) and temporal consistency (0.9865) over all baselines.

## Results

On 50 DAVIS videos (830×480, 32 frames), FastVMT achieves the best scores across all metrics: Text Similarity 0.2422, Motion Fidelity 0.7471, Temporal Consistency 0.9865, Subject Consistency 0.9809, Background Consistency 0.9684, Aesthetic Quality 0.5778, and Motion Smoothness 0.9891. Inference time is 184.18 s, yielding a 3.43× average speedup over the original training-free pipeline and up to 14.91× lower latency versus the slowest baseline (DeT, 2745.60 s). On the VBench evaluation (40 real-world + 40 generated videos, 5 prompts each), FastVMT also leads on all four VBench sub-metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FastVMT outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric. Among training-free methods, the closest competitor is MotionClone (Motion Fid. 0.7315 vs. 0.7471; Temp. Cons. 0.9722 vs. 0.9865), but FastVMT is also 2.15× faster (184 s vs. 397 s). DiTFlow, the direct architectural predecessor, trails substantially on Motion Fidelity (0.4062 vs. 0.7471) and Text Similarity (0.2091 vs. 0.2422) while being 3.4× slower. SMM achieves the second-best Aesthetic Quality (0.5770) but has the worst Temporal Consistency (0.9366) and Subject Consistency (0.8907) among training-free methods. Among training-based methods, DeT is the strongest on Subject Consistency (0.9787) but requires 2745.60 s—nearly 15× longer than FastVMT—and still scores lower on Motion Fidelity (0.6116) and Aesthetic Quality (0.3559). In the user study (20 participants, 8 methods ranked 1–8), FastVMT achieves the best average rank on all four criteria (Motion Preservation 1.712, Generation Quality 2.562, Text Alignment 2.400, Overall 2.225), with the next-best method (DeT) at 3.783 overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DiTFlow (Pondaven et al., 2025), which established the training-free attention-motion-flow (AMF) pipeline for video motion transfer using DiT backbones. It departs from that line by identifying two structural redundancies—global token-by-token attention for motion extraction and per-step gradient recomputation during denoising—and replacing them with a sliding-window local search and a step-skipping gradient cache, respectively. The corresponding-window loss is a new auxiliary objective not present in prior AMF-based methods, designed to enforce temporal smoothness of key representations within the constrained window. The work sits in the broader training-free motion transfer family (MOFT, MotionClone, SMM) but explicitly targets the DiT-based subset where these redundancies are most pronounced.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.43× average speedup with near-lossless quality makes training-free DiT-based motion transfer substantially more practical for interactive or near-real-time applications, narrowing the gap with training-based methods that require per-video fine-tuning. The 'stable gradient optimization' observation, while intuitive given the smooth diffusion trajectory, provides a principled justification for gradient caching that could inform efficiency improvements in other iterative diffusion-guided tasks. Key limitations remain: the method is still bounded by the pre-trained Wan-2.1 backbone's capacity, struggles with highly complex actions (e.g., Thomas Flair) and occlusions, and the pairwise AMF design introduces higher memory consumption that may hinder scaling to long videos. The authors envision an agent-based multi-module architecture as a future direction to address these compositional challenges.
