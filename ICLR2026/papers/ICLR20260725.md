## Problem

Existing reasoning segmentation models fail in clinical pathology for three reasons: their general-domain vision encoders lack pathology-specific morphological knowledge and robustness to staining variations, their fixed <SEG> token insertion prevents autonomous determination of when sufficient semantic context has been gathered to trigger segmentation, and no reasoning segmentation benchmark exists for pathology. This paper addresses all three barriers simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260725/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a pathology image (whole-slide or intraoperative frozen section at 5×–40× magnification) and a natural-language text query describing the target region; in one-shot mode, a single annotated reference image is additionally provided to supply morphological context for novel pathologies. The output is a pixel-level segmentation mask plus an explicit reasoning chain that articulates diagnostic logic in pathology terminology, with the <SEG> token autonomously placed at the reasoning step where spatial-semantic context is deemed sufficient for mask generation.

## Method

PathChat-SegR1 combines a Qwen2.5-VL backbone with a RuiPath pathology-specific vision encoder for reasoning, a MedSAM encoder for fine-grained spatial features, and an adapter bridging the <SEG> token representation to a mask decoder. The MedSAM encoder is pre-trained via stain-invariant self-distillation that pairs LAB color-space augmented views with masked auto-encoding, weighting feature consistency by stain-template cosine distance. The key RL contribution, SO-GRPO, replaces standard GRPO's trajectory-level rewards with GAE-based per-step credit assignment to learn optimal <SEG> emission timing, a differentiable soft-Dice reward that enables gradient flow from segmentation quality back to the <SEG> token embedding, and a sparsity-aware reward that penalizes <SEG> generation at non-spatial reasoning states. Training proceeds in three stages: stain-invariant self-supervised pre-training, supervised fine-tuning with LoRA on the LLM and adapters on the vision encoders, and SO-GRPO reinforcement learning with adaptive learning-rate scheduling and KL regularization for convergence.

## Results

On the in-domain public benchmarks (Table 2), PathChat-SegR1 achieves Dice scores of 0.76 (Camelyon16), 0.87 (GlaS), and 0.92 (CRAG) in a zero-shot unified-training setting, approaching or matching dataset-specific nnU-Net while outperforming the strongest reasoning segmentation baseline MMR-7B by up to 33% relative improvement. On out-of-domain evaluation (Table 3), PathChat-SegR1 achieves 0.58 Dice on PMBT (pulmonary metastasis from bone tumors) and 0.53 Dice on rare diseases under zero-shot conditions, representing a 61% relative improvement over MMR-7B. With a single annotated reference (one-shot), performance on rare diseases jumps to 0.72 Dice, a 53% improvement over MMR-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PathChat-SegR1 outperforms all nine baselines across the majority of benchmarks. Against the strongest reasoning segmentation baseline MMR-7B, it achieves 33% relative Dice improvement on Camelyon16 (0.76 vs. 0.57) and 25% on GlaS (0.87 vs. 0.72), while on CRAG it reaches 0.92 vs. MMR-7B's 0.74. Compared to the best closed-set method nnU-Net (trained per-dataset), PathChat-SegR1 is competitive on most benchmarks (e.g., 0.76 vs. 0.74 on Cam16, 0.87 vs. 0.91 on GlaS) but trails on CRAG (0.92 vs. 0.94) and WSSS4LUAD (0.78 vs. 0.84), though it does so with a single unified model rather than per-dataset training. Against weaker reasoning baselines, the margins are large: LISA-7B scores only 0.48 on Cam16 and 0.39 on FS-Mic, while OVSeg is the weakest overall (0.41 on Cam16, 0.31 on FS-Mic). On the out-of-domain PMBT dataset, PathChat-SegR1 achieves 0.58 Dice zero-shot versus MMR-7B's 0.36 (61% relative gain) and Seg-Zero's 0.39, and with one-shot in-context learning reaches 0.72 on rare diseases versus MMR-7B's 0.47.

## Relation to Prior Work

PathChat-SegR1 extends the reasoning segmentation paradigm introduced by LISA (which inserts <SEG> tokens at fixed positions) and Seg-Zero/MMR (which use GRPO for joint reasoning-segmentation optimization) into the pathology domain, where no prior reasoning segmentation model or benchmark existed. The key architectural departure is replacing LISA's predetermined <SEG> insertion with autonomous token generation, and the key algorithmic departure is extending standard GRPO into SO-GRPO via GAE-based temporal credit assignment, differentiable segmentation rewards, and sparsity-aware penalties. On the vision side, the model combines RuiPath (a DINOv2-based pathology foundation model) for semantic reasoning with a MedSAM encoder trained via stain-invariant self-distillation for spatial mask generation, bridging two previously separate pathways.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 118,667-triplet PathChat-SegR1 benchmark, including 43,847 private intraoperative frozen sections with real preparation artifacts, fills a critical gap for evaluating reasoning segmentation under clinically authentic conditions. The one-shot in-context adaptation (0.53→0.72 Dice on rare diseases) offers a practical pathway for deploying segmentation to novel pathologies without retraining, which is directly relevant to clinical workflows where annotated data for rare conditions is scarce. Open questions include whether the SO-GRPO timing mechanism generalizes to other domains requiring special-token generation, whether the approach scales to full whole-slide images (experiments use patches), and whether the RL-trained segmentation timing is robust to distribution shifts beyond the tested stain variations.
