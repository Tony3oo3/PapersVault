## Problem

Existing text-to-image diffusion models excel at concrete concepts but collapse on abstract emotions, and the prior EICG-specific method EmoGen relies on word-level attribute labels that lack contextual coherence, introduce emotional ambiguity, and suffer from missing annotations that limit scalability. CoEmoGen addresses these three limitations by replacing isolated attribute labels with context-rich sentence-level captions and designing a hierarchical adaptation module grounded in psychological observations about emotion structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260225/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single emotion category from the eight-class Mikels model (amusement, awe, contentment, excitement, anger, disgust, fear, sadness) as a one-hot vector and produces a 512×512 RGB image that evokes that emotion. At inference, visual embeddings are sampled from pre-constructed per-emotion Gaussian clusters to ensure diversity, and the one-hot vector is mapped through a learnable Neuro-symbolic Mapper rather than being fed directly as text or a fixed embedding, enabling flexible latent-space exploration while preserving semantic consistency.

## Method

CoEmoGen builds on Stable Diffusion v1.5 with CLIP ViT L/14 encoders and introduces two key components. First, MLLMs generate concise one-sentence captions focused on emotion-triggering visual attributes (brightness, colorfulness, scene type, objects, expressions, actions) for each training image, with the bottom 20% filtered out via CLIP image-caption similarity to mitigate hallucination noise. Second, a Hierarchical LoRA (HiLoRA) module decomposes adaptation into two polarity-shared LoRAs (positive/negative) that capture common low-level features like brightness and colorfulness, plus eight emotion-specific LoRAs that learn fine-grained high-level semantics; only the relevant polarity and emotion LoRAs are activated per sample. The one-hot emotion vector passes through a Neuro-symbolic Mapper and a cross-attention Visual-Perception Encoder fused with the CLIP image embedding before being encoded by the CLIP text encoder to produce the conditioning signal for the U-Net. Training uses a dual loss combining standard LDM denoising loss with a cosine-similarity semantic loss that aligns the emotion condition with the CLIP encoding of the sentence-level caption, preventing collapse into narrow pixel-level patterns while preserving semantic diversity.

## Results

On the EmoSet-based EICG benchmark, CoEmoGen achieves the best scores across all five metrics: FID 40.66, LPIPS 0.732, Emo-A 80.15%, Sem-C 0.641, and Sem-D 0.0349. The most notable gain is in Emotion Accuracy (Emo-A), where CoEmoGen improves over the closest EICG-specific baseline EmoGen by 3.9 percentage points (80.15% vs. 76.25%) and over general-purpose Stable Diffusion by 9.4 points. A user study with 17 participants further confirms these gains, with CoEmoGen receiving a 78.67% average pairwise preference rate and 88.42% on semantic coherence specifically.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoEmoGen outperforms all four baselines on every metric. Against the most directly comparable EICG method, EmoGen (Yang et al., CVPR 2024), CoEmoGen improves Emo-A by 3.9 pp (80.15% vs. 76.25%), reduces FID by 0.94 (40.66 vs. 41.60), and gains small but consistent margins on LPIPS (+0.015), Sem-C (+0.008), and Sem-D (+0.0014). Against general-purpose Stable Diffusion, the Emo-A gap widens to 9.4 pp and FID improves by 3.4 points, while Textual Inversion and DreamBooth trail even further (Emo-A 74.87% and 70.50% respectively; FID 50.51 and 46.89). The qualitative analysis highlights that EmoGen's word-level attribute labels produce semantically incoherent composites (e.g., a 'flaming tiger' for anger), whereas CoEmoGen's sentence-level captions yield more natural, contextually grounded images. In the user study, CoEmoGen secures a 78.67% average preference over EmoGen across four criteria, with semantic coherence being the most decisive dimension at 88.42%.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoEmoGen directly extends EmoGen (Yang et al., CVPR 2024), which first formalized the Emotional Image Content Generation (EICG) task and aligned emotion categories with CLIP space using word-level attribute labels from EmoSet. The key conceptual departure is replacing these isolated word-level labels with MLLM-generated sentence-level captions that preserve contextual and semantic coherence, and introducing a Hierarchical LoRA (HiLoRA) module that decomposes LoRA's low-rank adaptation into polarity-shared and emotion-specific components, inspired by psychological observations about shared low-level features within polarity. The pipeline is built on Stable Diffusion v1.5 and CLIP ViT-L/14, following the standard latent diffusion + cross-attention conditioning paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary practical contribution is scalability: by leveraging MLLMs for caption generation, CoEmoGen removes the bottleneck of manually annotated word-level attribute labels that constrained EmoGen's training corpus, enabling straightforward extension to new domains (demonstrated via the 13,633-image EmoArt dataset). However, the work remains limited to the 8-category Mikels model, the user study involves only 17 participants, and the MLLM hallucination filtering (discarding bottom 20% by CLIP similarity) is a heuristic rather than a principled guarantee. Open questions include whether the HiLoRA hierarchy generalizes to finer-grained or non-Mikels emotion taxonomies, and how the denoising dynamics interact with emotion-specific attention across timesteps (flagged as future work).
