## Problem

Existing MNL contextual bandits model relevance-driven choice but ignore within-assortment diversity, while submodular/combinatorial bandits encode diversity in set rewards but lack a structured discrete choice model. No prior framework captures the relevance-diversity trade-off within a single probabilistic choice model for sequential assortment selection, leaving a modeling gap between diversity-aware set selection and choice-model-driven dynamic assortment learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264200/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each round the agent receives N context feature vectors x_ti ∈ R^d (possibly adversarial) and a known monotone submodular diversity function g_t, and must select an assortment S_t of at most K items. The critical structural detail is that the diversity-augmented feature z_ti(S) = [x_ti, g_t(S)] depends on the entire assortment S rather than individual items, so the optimistic reward cannot be decomposed per-item. The agent observes only a single binary choice (selected item or outside option) per round, with no intermediate marginal-gain feedback during assortment construction.

## Method

The DMNL model augments MNL choice probabilities by scaling the outside option's attraction with exp(−λ*·g_t(S)), so higher-diversity assortments reduce the probability of no selection. OFU-DMNL estimates the joint parameter w* = [θ*, λ*] via online mirror descent on the MNL log-loss using diversity-augmented features, then constructs assortments through an item-wise optimistic greedy procedure that adds the item maximizing the optimistic reward at each step. A key theoretical contribution is proving that this greedy construction achieves a (1−1/(e+1))-approximation to the optimal assortment—strictly improving on the (1−1/e) rate for general submodular functions—by exploiting the specific LogSumExp structure of the MNL reward. The strict submodularity assumption on g_t (ω-strict) is shown to be sufficient to recover submodularity of the optimistic reward function despite the absence of intermediate feedback, enabling a Õ(d√(T/K)) γ-approximate regret bound that matches the minimax rate for standard MNL bandits.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is a $(1-1/(e+1))$-approximate cumulative regret bound of $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(d\sqrt{T/K})$ for OFU-DMNL (Theorem 3), which matches the minimax lower bound $\Omega(d\sqrt{T/K})$ (Theorem 2) up to logarithmic factors. The item-wise greedy construction achieves an approximation rate of at least $1-1/(e+1) \approx 0.632$, strictly improving over the standard $(1-1/e) \approx 0.632$ rate for general submodular maximization (Theorem 1). Computationally, each round requires only $\mathcal{O}(NK)$ operations, compared to $\mathcal{O}((eN/K)^K)$ for exhaustive enumeration over all $\binom{N}{K}$ assortments. In experiments (Figure 2), OFU-DMNL achieves cumulative regret comparable to the exhaustive-search variant OFU-DMNL-FULL while reducing runtime by orders of magnitude.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OFU-DMNL outperforms all three MNL baselines (UCB-MNL, TS-MNL, OFU-MNL+) in cumulative regret across all tested configurations ($N=10, 20$; various $\lambda^*$ values), because these baselines ignore within-assortment diversity and simply select the top-$K$ most relevant items. Against OFU-MNL-DR, which adds a manually tuned diversity term $\lambda g(S)$ to the MNL reward, OFU-DMNL achieves lower regret because it jointly learns the diversity parameter $\lambda^*$ from data rather than requiring a fixed hyperparameter. Relative to OFU-DMNL-FULL (exhaustive search over all $\binom{N}{K}$ assortments), OFU-DMNL achieves comparable cumulative regret while reducing per-round computation from $\mathcal{O}((eN/K)^K)$ to $\mathcal{O}(NK)$, yielding a dramatic runtime advantage (visible in the rightmost panel of Figure 2 at $T=10{,}000$). The regret scaling $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(d\sqrt{T/K})$ matches that of the nearly minimax-optimal OFU-MNL+ for standard MNL bandits, despite the additional challenge of estimating the diversity parameter $\lambda^*$.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate literatures: MNL contextual bandits (Oh & Iyengar, 2019/2021; Lee & Oh, 2024), which model relevance-driven choice but ignore within-assortment diversity, and submodular/combinatorial bandits (Yue & Guestrin, 2011; Chen et al., 2013/2016), which encode diversity in set-function rewards but lack a structured discrete-choice probability model. The DMNL model embeds a monotone submodular diversity function directly into MNL choice probabilities, and the OFU-DMNL algorithm adapts the online mirror descent parameter estimation from Lee & Oh (2024) to a diversity-augmented feature vector. A key departure from combinatorial bandit approaches is the avoidance of black-box optimization oracles: the item-wise greedy construction is shown to achieve an improved (1−1/(e+1)) approximation ratio by exploiting the specific LogSumExp structure of the MNL reward, surpassing the standard (1−1/e) Nemhauser bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first model to formalize the relevance-diversity trade-off within a single probabilistic choice framework, and the matching nearly-minimax regret bound (despite jointly learning a diversity parameter) demonstrates that diversity-aware assortment learning does not incur additional statistical cost. The strict submodularity requirement (Assumption 3) is stronger than standard submodularity and is needed to recover submodularity of the optimistic reward without intermediate marginal-gain feedback—a limitation that may restrict applicability to certain diversity measures. Open questions include extending to non-uniform revenues (where diversity may conflict with reward maximization), relaxing the strict submodularity condition, and whether the improved approximation ratio can be tightened further or made instance-dependent.
