## Problem

Rare-event forecasting in multivariate time series must simultaneously handle severe class imbalance, long-range temporal dependencies that dilute early precursors, and the need for calibrated probabilities with explicit tail-risk assessment for operational thresholding. Prior architectures improve sequence aggregation or calibration in isolation, but rarely address calibration and extreme-tail sensitivity jointly within a compact, deployable model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260228/cropped_page7_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes fixed-length multivariate windows X ∈ R^{T×F} (e.g., 10 steps × 9 SHARP solar-magnetogram parameters at 12-minute cadence, or 24 steps × 32 raw+diff channels for SKAB) and produces a single binary event probability p̂ = σ(l) for thresholded alert decisions. During training it additionally emits Normal-Inverse-Gamma parameters (μ, v, α, β) over the logit for closed-form aleatoric/epistemic decomposition and Generalized Pareto parameters (ξ, σ) for exceedances above a batchwise 90th-percentile quantile, but at inference only the classification head is active, incurring zero auxiliary overhead.

## Method

EVEREST is a 6-layer Transformer encoder (d=128, 4 heads) whose final hidden states are pooled by a single-query attention bottleneck—a learned scorer w ∈ R^d that computes one softmax distribution over time and aggregates into a single latent z, adding only +d parameters versus full self-attention. From a shared 128-d MLP, four parallel heads branch: a primary classification logit (the sole inference output), an evidential NIG head that regularises logit-level calibration via a closed-form Normal-Inverse-Gamma objective, an EVT head that fits a Generalized Pareto distribution to logit exceedances above a high quantile to reallocate gradient mass toward the risky tail, and a lightweight precursor head providing anticipatory binary cross-entropy supervision. The composite training loss combines focal loss (γ annealed 0→2 over 50 epochs), evidential NLL, EVT GPD log-likelihood, and precursor BCE with weights (0.8, 0.1, 0.1, 0.05), inspired by an information-bottleneck view where each term targets a distinct aspect of the latent–label mutual information. All three auxiliary heads are discarded at deployment, leaving a single 814k-parameter, 16.6M-FLOP classifier with no inference-time overhead.

## Results

On the SHARP-GOES solar-flare benchmark, EVEREST achieves state-of-the-art True Skill Statistic (TSS) of 0.973/0.970/0.966 at 24/48/72-hour horizons for ≥C-class flares, 0.898/0.920/0.906 for ≥M, and 0.907/0.936/0.966 for the rarest ≥M5 events, with strong calibration (M5-72h ECE = 0.016). On the SKAB industrial anomaly benchmark, EVEREST reaches F1 = 98.2% and TSS = 0.964 without architectural changes. The model is compact at ~814k parameters with 16.6M FLOPs per window, and all nine SHARP-GOES tasks show statistically significant improvements (p < 0.001) over the strongest prior baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVEREST outperforms all three SHARP-GOES baselines across all nine tasks. Against the strongest prior method, SolarFlareNet (Abduallah et al., 2023), EVEREST gains +0.138 to +0.264 TSS for ≥C, +0.059 to +0.192 for ≥M, and +0.089 to +0.237 for ≥M5, with the largest absolute gains at longer horizons (e.g., +0.264 TSS for C-72h, +0.237 for M5-72h). The LSTM baseline (Liu et al., 2019) and 3D-CNN (Sun et al., 2022) are substantially outperformed, with EVEREST exceeding them by 0.21–0.36 TSS at 24h. On SKAB, EVEREST (F1 = 98.2%) surpasses the strongest published baseline TranAD (F1 = 91–96%) by roughly 2–7 F1 points, and outperforms all other listed anomaly detectors (USAD, OmniAnomaly, LSTM-VAE, DeepLog, TAnoGAN, and classical methods) by 3–33 F1 points. All improvements over SolarFlareNet are statistically significant at p < 0.001 via bootstrap testing.

## Relation to Prior Work

EVEREST sits at the intersection of three research threads: (i) transformer-based time-series encoders (PatchTST, FEDformer, iTransformer, ModernTCN), from which it borrows the self-attention backbone but replaces full sequence-level attention with a single-query attention bottleneck closer to attention-pooling mechanisms (Ilse et al., 2018; Lee et al., 2019); (ii) evidential deep learning (Amini et al., 2020; Sensoy et al., 2018), where the Normal-Inverse-Gamma head is repurposed not as a regression output but as a training-time logit-calibration regulariser; and (iii) extreme-value theory (Coles, 2001; Kozerawski et al., 2022), where a Generalised Pareto penalty is applied to logit exceedances rather than to raw signal residuals. The key departure from prior solar-flare forecasters (Liu et al., 2019; Sun et al., 2022; Abduallah et al., 2023) is that all three auxiliary heads—evidential, EVT, and precursor—are discarded at inference, so the deployed model is a standard single-head transformer with no additional latency, while the multi-task training recipe (focal + evidential NLL + EVT + precursor BCE) jointly shapes the shared representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that calibration, tail-risk sensitivity, and rare-event discrimination can be addressed simultaneously in a compact (814k-parameter) model whose training-only auxiliaries impose zero inference cost, making it practical for operational space-weather and industrial-monitoring pipelines. The cross-domain transfer to SKAB without architectural changes and the prospective X9.3 flare replay strengthen the case for generalisability. Open questions remain around fixed-length context windows (potentially missing very slow precursors), the extreme scarcity of X-class events limiting tail fitting, and the absence of image/radio modalities; extending to streaming, multimodal, and cross-cycle-drift-robust settings is the natural next step.
