## Problem

Autoregressive video diffusion models require multiple denoising steps per chunk, making real-time streaming infeasible, while existing distillation methods adapted from image generation neglect temporal dependencies and suffer from error accumulation, motion degradation, and oversaturation in long sequences. The paper identifies two root causes: insufficient exploitation of temporal context across denoising steps during step reduction, and implicit next-noise-level prediction (exposure bias) inherent in next-chunk conditioning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a streaming video at 832×480 resolution and 16 FPS, generated sequentially in chunks of 3 frames using a rolling KV cache that retains context from the most recent 4 chunks (17.5 GB memory footprint). A 5-second video (7 chunks) is generated in 2.61 seconds at up to 31 FPS on a single H100 GPU, with a first-frame latency of 0.37 s. The system also supports dynamic prompting, allowing new text descriptions to be injected at arbitrary time points during long-video generation (validated up to 45 s).

## Method

Diagonal Distillation builds on the Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B Flow Matching DiT and extends Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) with three coupled mechanisms. First, Diagonal Denoising allocates denoising steps asymmetrically across chunks (e.g., 4-3-2-2-2-2-2 for a 5 s video), so early chunks establish structural and appearance priors with more steps while later chunks inherit that context and use only two steps. Second, Diagonal Forcing conditions each new chunk on the noised final latent of the previous chunk (injected as KV cache at a specific noise level, optimally 100 timesteps), explicitly simulating the diagonal denoising trajectory during training to align train-time and inference-time conditioning and mitigate error accumulation. Third, Flow Distribution Matching adds a temporal loss that aligns the student's and teacher's motion distributions via a lightweight learnable extractor (convolution on latent frame differences followed by an MLP, with EMA-updated teacher), preventing motion amplitude attenuation under the reduced step budget. The total objective combines spatial DMD gradient loss, a regression loss, and the flow-matching terms weighted by γ.

## Results

DiagDistill achieves a 277.3× speedup over the Wan2.1 baseline while maintaining a VBench total score of 84.48 (vs. 84.26 for Wan2.1) and a quality score of 85.26 (vs. 85.30). It reaches 31 FPS throughput with a first-frame latency of 0.37 s on a single H100 GPU, generating a 5-second video in 2.61 seconds. The method also attains the highest semantic alignment score (81.73) among all compared methods, surpassing the previous fastest baseline Self-Forcing (81.28) by 0.45 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the non-autoregressive baselines (Wan2.1, SkyReels-V2, MAGI-1), DiagDistill delivers a 277.3× speedup while matching or exceeding their VBench quality scores (85.26 vs. 85.30/84.70/82.04) and substantially outperforming them on semantic alignment (81.73 vs. 80.09/74.53/67.74). Compared to the two autoregressive distillation baselines CausVid and Self-Forcing (both at 17 FPS, 0.69 s latency), DiagDistill nearly doubles throughput to 31 FPS and halves first-frame latency to 0.37 s, while also improving total VBench score (84.48 vs. 81.20/84.31) and semantic alignment (81.73 vs. 69.80/81.28). The closest competitor, Self-Forcing, achieves a marginally higher quality sub-score (85.07 vs. 85.26 — actually DiagDistill is slightly higher), but DiagDistill wins on total score, semantic alignment, and all speed metrics. In long-video (45 s) generation, DiagDistill maintains stable quality over time whereas CausVid and Self-Forcing exhibit progressive saturation distortion and quality decay, confirmed by a user study (93 participants) where DiagDistill was preferred 59.3% of the time over Self-Forcing and 66.1% over CausVid.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) framework from image generation to autoregressive video generation, building directly on CausVid's next-chunk prediction paradigm and Self-Forcing's rolling KV cache mechanism for exposure bias mitigation. The key departure is the 'diagonal' reframing: the authors identify that next-chunk prediction in autoregressive video models implicitly predicts the next noise level, and exploit this by allocating more denoising steps to early chunks and progressively fewer to later ones, rather than the uniform step allocation used by prior video distillation methods. They further introduce Diagonal Forcing (training with noisy KV cache in a diagonal temporal pattern) and Flow Distribution Matching (a temporal loss aligning optical flow distributions), both of which are absent from prior image-centric distillation approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 277.3× speedup (31 FPS on a single H100) makes real-time streaming video generation practically viable, and the diagonal step-allocation strategy offers a simple, generalizable principle for any autoregressive diffusion pipeline. However, the method is currently tied to the Wan2.1 architecture and a specific step configuration (4322222), and the 1.53× improvement over Self-Forcing, while meaningful, does not constitute a paradigm shift. Open questions include whether the diagonal strategy generalizes to other base models or resolutions, how it scales beyond 45-second videos, and whether the flow distribution matching component can be replaced by cheaper temporal regularizers.
