## Problem

Large Reasoning Models improve accuracy by generating long reasoning traces, but this inflates inference latency and cost since output tokens are priced 2–5× higher than input tokens and must be decoded sequentially. Prior retrieval-based approaches (BoT, SuperCorrect) reuse static, pre-distilled templates that cannot adapt or compose new reasoning pathways on the fly, leaving a gap between the flexible, recombining memory humans use and what LLMs can exploit at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263364/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reasoning query (e.g., an AIME/AMC math problem), a pre-constructed thought graph of 3.34k templates from ReasonFlux-v2 (each node carrying metadata tags, reasoning text, and a pre-computed jina-embeddings-v2-small-en vector), and a reasoning model (Qwen3 0.6B–14B, DLER-7B, or DeepSeek-R1-Llama8B). It produces an assembled reasoning template of at most 8 thought steps, which is inserted either directly into the prompt (RoT) or inside  tags to steer the model's internal deliberation (RoT+TI), followed by the model's final solution. The thought graph is stored once and shared across all queries, adding only ~1.7 GB of GPU memory overhead.

## Method

RoT organizes reasoning steps into a directed, weighted multi-graph with two edge types: sequential edges preserving within-template ordering, and bidirectional semantic edges connecting analogous steps across templates (cosine similarity ≥ 0.85 on L2-normalized embeddings). At inference, a three-step pipeline assembles a query-specific template: (1) metadata and knowledge-tag filtering narrows candidates, then a scoring function R_Initial = α·R_Q + (1−α)·R_S (α=0.8) selects the entry node by balancing query-semantic similarity against a structural bias toward step-0 nodes; (2) iterative reward-guided traversal expands the template by selecting neighbors that maximize R = R_Q + R_F, where R_F rewards following sequential edges, halting when relevance drops below threshold, template length reaches 8, or no candidates remain; (3) the assembled template is injected into the prompt or wrapped in  tags following the Thinking Intervention mechanism, anchoring the model to a coherent reasoning path and reducing redundant exploration. The key distinction from prior work is that templates are decomposed into atomic, reusable thought steps and dynamically recombined per query via graph traversal, rather than retrieved as fixed, monolithic scaffolds.

## Results

Across Qwen3 models (0.6B–14B) on AIME 2023/2024/2025 and AMC 2023, RoT+TI achieves comparable or higher accuracy than CoT while reducing output tokens by 20–40% (e.g., 72% vs. 74% accuracy on Qwen3-4B with ~800 fewer tokens; 83% vs. 80% on Qwen3-8B vs. RAG). Inference cost drops by up to 59% (Qwen3-0.6B) and 39.3% (Qwen3-1.7B) relative to CoT, and end-to-end latency decreases by up to 82% (Qwen3-0.6B) and 29.7% (Qwen3-1.7B). Path switching is reduced by up to 81.8%, confirming that template-guided reasoning anchors the model to fewer detours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CoT, RoT+TI trades a small accuracy drop (1–2 pp on 1.7B/4B) for a 20–40% reduction in output tokens, making it strictly more cost-efficient; on Qwen3-8B it actually exceeds CoT accuracy (83% vs. 80% on AMC'23) while using fewer tokens. Against CoT-SC, RoT+TI achieves comparable accuracy (e.g., 66.67% vs. 80% on AIME'25 for 4B) but with roughly 4× fewer output tokens (9.4k vs. 42k), since self-consistency multiplies generation cost. Against RAG (static template retrieval), RoT+TI matches or beats accuracy on 8B (83% vs. 80% on AMC'23) with ~500 fewer tokens, demonstrating the advantage of dynamic over static template assembly. Against BoT, RoT+TI is dramatically more token-efficient: BoT consumes 3–5× more output tokens (e.g., 50k vs. 9.4k on Qwen3-4B) while often achieving lower accuracy, confirming that whole-template retrieval is far less efficient than fine-grained thought-step composition.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoT extends the retrieval-based reasoning line initiated by Buffer of Thought (BoT), SuperCorrect, and RAT, which retrieve static, pre-distilled thought templates to guide smaller LLMs. The key departure is decomposing templates into atomic reasoning steps organized in a directed thought graph (with sequential and semantic edges) and dynamically assembling problem-specific templates at inference time via reward-guided traversal, rather than retrieving a fixed scaffold. For template integration into the model's internal deliberation, RoT+TI directly adopts the Thinking Intervention (TI) mechanism of Wu et al. (2025), placing retrieved steps inside  tags without any fine-tuning. The thought graph is constructed from the ReasonFlux-v2 template collection, positioning RoT as a dynamic, fine-grained successor to the static-template paradigm of prior retrieval-augmented reasoning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: RoT+TI cuts output tokens by up to 40%, end-to-end latency by up to 82%, and inference cost by up to 59% while maintaining comparable accuracy on AIME/AMC benchmarks, addressing the core cost bottleneck of reasoning-heavy LLM deployment. Scalability is demonstrated (larger thought graphs yield up to +17% accuracy on 8B models), suggesting that serving platforms accumulating user reasoning traces can compound gains over time. However, several limitations temper the picture: metadata annotation is manual, accuracy sometimes drops with RoT+TI on larger models (e.g., Qwen3-14B on AIME 2025), and the GPQA results show that aggressive token savings come at a meaningful accuracy cost in less formulaic domains. Open questions include automating the metadata/knowledge-tag pipeline, extending benefits to larger models whose RL training erodes instruction-following, and validating the approach on truly open-ended reasoning tasks beyond structured math and multiple-choice science.
