## Problem

Existing transfer-based adversarial attacks on large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) rely on a single fixed alignment objective on a surrogate model, which overfits to unnatural feature directions and fails to transfer to black-box models. Even when transfer succeeds, the resulting perturbations are often perceptible to humans or detectable by the VLM itself, triggering adversarial warnings. The paper identifies that naturalness—adherence to the natural image distribution—is a critical but unaddressed factor for both transferability and stealthiness.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source image and a target image (both RGB, from MS-COCO validation set) and produces a single adversarial image that, when fed to a black-box VLM with the prompt 'Describe this image in 30 words,' elicits a caption semantically similar to the target image's caption while remaining visually close to the source. Unlike prior ℓ∞-bounded attacks, PSI generates unrestricted perturbations via a diffusion model (stable-diffusion-2-1), with no explicit pixel-level norm constraint. The surrogate models are CLIP variants (ViT-B/16, ViT-B/32, ViT-g-14) used only for computing alignment gradients during optimization.

## Method

PSI (Progressive Semantic Infusion) formulates a joint objective combining surrogate-model alignment and naturalness (adherence to the natural image distribution), then optimizes it through a diffusion-based denoising trajectory. Starting from a DDPM-inverted latent of the source image at timestep t*, the method denoises step-by-step to timestep 0, injecting a small alignment-guided perturbation at each step; each denoising step implicitly optimizes the naturalness term via the diffusion score. To avoid overfitting to a single fixed objective, PSI replaces it with a sequence of progressive localized alignment objectives: at each timestep, a source region is selected from the diffusion model's predicted mean (via co-evolving selection that maximizes feature similarity to the target region), and a target region is interpolated between a salient object and the full target image, creating spatially diverse yet semantically consistent supervision. Source-aware denoising embeds source-image cues into the noise terms through DDPM inversion, ensuring the final adversarial example preserves visual fidelity to the source without introducing conspicuous artifacts.

## Results

PSI achieves the highest attack success rate (ASR) and stealthy attack success rate (S-ASR) across all six victim models. On GPT-5, PSI reaches 78.6% ASR and 62.8% S-ASR, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art FOA (75.8% / 56.5%). On the adversarially robust FARE4 model, PSI achieves 64.3% ASR versus FOA's 54.4%, and on Grok-4 it reaches 81.4% ASR / 75.0% S-ASR. In terms of imperceptibility, PSI attains the best BRISQUE score (22.14) and LPIPS score (0.192), substantially outperforming ℓ∞-bounded methods like AnyAttack (BRISQUE 68.32, LPIPS 0.478).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSI outperforms all seven baselines on both ASR and S-ASR across every victim model. Against the strongest baseline FOA, PSI gains +2.8 pp ASR and +6.3 pp S-ASR on GPT-5, +9.9 pp ASR on FARE4 (adversarially robust), and +7.2 pp ASR on Claude-3.5. Compared to M-Attack, PSI improves S-ASR by 8.3 pp on GPT-5 (62.8 vs 54.5) and 8.2 pp on FARE4 (63.5 vs 49.5), indicating better stealthiness under adversarial training. AnyAttack achieves competitive ASR on Gemini-2.5 (31.5%) but suffers a severe S-ASR drop (11.2% on GPT-5) and the worst imperceptibility (BRISQUE 68.32, LPIPS 0.478), confirming that its ℓ∞-bounded perturbations are easily detected. AdvDiffVLM matches PSI on BRISQUE (22.59 vs 22.14) but trails by a wide margin on transferability (13.1% vs 78.6% ASR on GPT-5), illustrating the trade-off it makes between stealth and attack effectiveness. The older fixed-alignment methods (AttackVLM, SSA-CWA, CoA) remain far behind, with ASR below 14% on all commercial models, validating the paper's argument that a joint alignment-plus-naturalness objective is essential for transferability.

## Relation to Prior Work

PSI extends the transfer-based adversarial attack paradigm for VLMs (AttackVLM, CoA, M-Attack, FOA) by replacing the single fixed alignment objective with a joint objective that explicitly incorporates naturalness (adherence to the natural image distribution) alongside feature alignment. It builds on diffusion-based attack methods like AdvDiffVLM and ACA, which also leverage diffusion priors, but departs from them in two key ways: (1) progressive alignment objectives with co-evolving region selection replace label-dependent GradCAM masking or single-step perturbation injection, and (2) source-aware DDPM inversion embeds source-image cues into the noise schedule to preserve visual fidelity. The paper positions random cropping (M-Attack, FOA) as a degenerate case of its progressive alignment, and the Cauchy-Schwarz argument in Appendix A.3 provides a theoretical justification for distributing perturbations uniformly across timesteps rather than concentrating them at a single step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that commercial VLMs (GPT-5, Grok-4, Gemini-2.5) remain vulnerable to unrestricted, stealthy adversarial perturbations that evade both human perception and model-level adversarial detection, raising concrete safety concerns for content moderation and safety-critical deployments. A key open question the paper surfaces is the tension between pixel-level imperceptibility and scene-level semantic consistency: even PSI's best examples are flagged by LLM judges due to unnatural object relationships or perspective mismatches, suggesting that truly undetectable attacks require solving a harder joint problem. The finding that the most robust model (Claude-3.5) simultaneously fails to detect clearly perceptible adversarial noise highlights an unresolved trade-off between adversarial awareness and semantic robustness that the field has not yet addressed.
