## Problem

Existing video captioning benchmarks evaluate descriptive comprehensiveness but largely overlook whether models can follow specific user instructions with format, length, and content constraints. While MLLMs excel at general video description, they struggle to adhere to fine-grained, compositional directives that impose multiple simultaneous constraints, and no prior benchmark systematically measures this instruction-following fidelity in the video captioning domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260791/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation sample is a Video-Instruction-Checklist triplet: a 2–60 second video (≥480p) paired with a text instruction averaging 6 constraints drawn from 27 distinct types (e.g., JSON structure, word/sentence limits, keyword exclusion, camera-movement description, element focus). The model must produce a single caption that simultaneously satisfies all format and content constraints; the checklist then decomposes verification into rule-based items (checked by LLM-extracted content passed to deterministic scripts) and open-ended QA items (true/false and multiple-choice questions answered by an LLM judge using the caption as context).

## Method

IF-VidCap introduces a two-dimensional evaluation protocol combining Constraint Satisfaction Rate (CSR) and Instruction Satisfaction Rate (ISR), each further split into rule-based (format) and open-ended (content) sub-metrics. For rule-checkable constraints, an LLM extracts relevant content from the model's response and deterministic scripts verify it; for open-ended constraints, retrieval-based QA pairs probe semantic fidelity against ground-truth annotations. To demonstrate a training path, the authors construct 46K video-instruction-response triplets via a 'response-to-instruction' pipeline (using DeepSeek-V3.1 to synthesize diverse instructions from 11K existing captions) and fine-tune Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, yielding IF-Captioner-Qwen which substantially outperforms the base model on both CSR and ISR. The benchmark is validated by 96.33% agreement with human annotations using GPT-5-mini as the automated judge.

## Results

On IF-VidCap (1,400 samples, 27 constraint types, avg. 6 constraints per instruction), Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves the highest overall ISR of 27.83% and CSR of 74.53%, followed by Gemini-2.5-Flash (25.50% / 72.63%) and GPT-4o (22.90% / 70.74%). Among open-source models, Qwen3-VL-Instruct-235B reaches 26.41% ISR and 71.65% CSR, nearly matching the top closed-source model. The authors' fine-tuned IF-Captioner-Qwen (7B) improves over the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct from 10.92% to 14.63% ISR and from 58.12% to 62.82% CSR, while the human baseline achieves 31.89% ISR and 75.57% CSR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among closed-source models, Gemini-2.5-Pro leads all 26 evaluated models with 27.83% ISR and 74.53% CSR, outperforming GPT-4o by ~5 points in ISR and ~4 points in CSR. The performance gap between top open-source and closed-source models is narrowing: Qwen3-VL-Instruct-235B (26.41% ISR) trails Gemini-2.5-Pro by only 1.4 points, and InternVL-3.5-241B (24.20% ISR) is within 3.6 points. The authors' IF-Captioner-Qwen (7B) substantially outperforms its base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, gaining +3.71 points in ISR (14.63 vs. 10.92) and +4.70 points in CSR (62.82 vs. 58.12), demonstrating that targeted instruction-following fine-tuning is effective. A striking finding is that specialized dense-captioning models (Tarsier2 at 1.40% ISR, ARC-Hunyuan-Video at 2.32% ISR) dramatically underperform even small general-purpose MLLMs like Qwen2.5-VL-7B (10.92% ISR), indicating that descriptive richness does not transfer to instruction-following fidelity. Thinking-mode variants (e.g., InternVL-3.5-38B: 20.71% vs. 15.43% ISR) consistently outperform their non-thinking counterparts, confirming that explicit reasoning helps satisfy compositional constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate evaluation lineages: text-only instruction-following benchmarks (IFEval, CFBench, ComplexBench) that assess compositional constraint adherence, and video captioning benchmarks (Dream-1K, CaReBench, VidCapBench) that evaluate descriptive quality against fixed rubrics. IF-VidCap transplants the constraint-satisfaction paradigm into the video modality, introducing 27 constraint types and a hybrid rule-based + LLM-as-judge evaluation protocol. For the model contribution, it fine-tunes Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct on a synthetic instruction-following dataset, positioning IF-Captioner-Qwen as a lightweight demonstration that targeted SFT can improve constrained captioning without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a counterintuitive finding: models specialized for dense video captioning (e.g., Tarsier2) substantially underperform general-purpose MLLMs on instruction-following, suggesting that descriptive richness and constrained generation are not complementary but potentially competing objectives. This reframes how the community should think about training video captioning models—simply scaling up description quality does not yield better controllability. Open questions include whether the gap persists on longer videos, whether real user instructions (vs. synthetic ones) shift the difficulty landscape, and whether the format-vs-content asymmetry (models handle JSON/length constraints far better than semantic focus constraints) can be closed through targeted training.
