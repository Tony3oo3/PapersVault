## Problem

LLMs, especially smaller ones, suffer significant performance drops on GSM reasoning when facing distribution shifts such as numerical/nominal variable changes or insertion of distracting conditions, despite solving the original problems correctly. While data augmentation with synthetic variants is one strategy, it is computationally expensive; this paper instead asks whether teaching LLMs to learn the underlying symbolic abstraction of each problem can make reasoning invariant to surface-form perturbations without scaling training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263965/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a GSM math word problem as text input and first parses it into an abstract question where concrete values are replaced with indexed symbols (in0, in1, …) alongside a conditions list. The model generates a GranulAR-format abstract answer that decomposes the problem into sub-questions, answers each with chain-of-thought reasoning over abstract symbols, and encodes each derivation in double angle brackets (e.g., <<in0*in2=out0>>). A regex script extracts these bracketed derivations as the de-contextualized abstraction, which is then solved by SymPy together with the conditions to produce the final numerical answer.

## Method

AbstRaL decomposes GSM solving into a four-step pipeline—condition recognition, abstract reasoning, abstraction retrieval, and symbolic derivation—and trains the core abstract reasoning step via SFT on GranulAR data (socratic CoT rewritten with abstract symbols and sub-question decomposition) followed by GRPO reinforcement learning. The RL uses two model-free rewards: an answer-correctness reward that checks whether the generated abstraction, combined with gold conditions, yields the correct answer via SymPy, and a symbolic-distance reward computed as normalized Levenshtein distance between the generated and gold abstraction token sequences. The key finding is that SFT alone fails to produce faithful abstractions because its autoregressive objective overfits to specific training contexts, while RL with the milestone-style symbolic reward provides fine-grained learning signal that drives the model toward context-invariant reasoning. The granular sub-question decomposition in GranulAR is critical for distractor robustness, as it forces the model to plan reasoning steps and re-verify which input conditions are relevant at each step, effectively isolating irrelevant information.

## Results

On GSM-Symbolic (Vary Both), AbstRaL achieves 44.6% accuracy for Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct (vs. 34.0% for CoT-8S) and 90.2% for Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct (vs. 87.7% for CoT-8S), with a performance drop (Δ) of only −1.27% and 0.86% respectively—nearly eliminating the degradation from distribution shifts. On GSM-Plus Distract, AbstRaL scores 36.5% (0.5B) and 82.3% (7B), substantially outperforming all baselines (e.g., 22.7% and 76.3% for CoT-8S). Zero-shot OOD evaluation on 11 math datasets and 4 general reasoning benchmarks (MMLU, BBH, ARC-Challenge, OpenBookQA) shows consistent improvements, e.g., BBH rises from 43.7% to 54.8% for the 7B model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AbstRaL outperforms all five baselines on both GSM-Symbolic Vary Both and GSM-Plus Distract across all tested model sizes (0.5B–8B). Against CoT-8S (Wei et al., 2022), AbstRaL gains +10.6 pp on Vary Both (44.6 vs. 34.0) and +13.8 pp on Distract (36.5 vs. 22.7) for the 0.5B model; for the 7B model the gains are +2.5 pp and +6.0 pp respectively. Against PoT-8S (Chen et al., 2022), AbstRaL leads by +17.6 pp on Vary Both and +17.1 pp on Distract (0.5B). Against the closest symbolic-reasoning baseline SyReLM (Dutta et al., 2024), AbstRaL improves Vary Both by +7.8 pp (0.5B) and +2.5 pp (7B), and Distract by +15.4 pp (0.5B) and +6.3 pp (7B). The natural-language abstraction baselines CoA (Gao et al., 2025) and AoT (Hong et al., 2024) suffer the largest drops on Distract (12.7% and 12.7% for 0.5B; 57.1% and 56.6% for 7B), while AbstRaL maintains 36.5% and 82.3% respectively—demonstrating that the granularly-decomposed GranulAR format and RL training are critical for distractor robustness. On OOD generalization, AbstRaL consistently beats CoT-RL and CoA across all 15 OOD benchmarks, whereas CoT-RL and CoA often underperform the original SFT checkpoint.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant data-augmentation strategy for improving reasoning robustness (Boix-Adsera et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2024) by instead teaching LLMs to explicitly construct de-contextualized symbolic abstractions of problems. It builds on prior abstract-reasoning methods (AoT, CoA, SyReLM) but distinguishes itself by (1) integrating socratic chain-of-thought decomposition with abstract symbols in a granular format (GranulAR), (2) using GRPO-based reinforcement learning with novel model-free rewards (answer correctness + symbolic edit distance) rather than plain SFT, and (3) connecting the learned abstraction to a SymPy equation solver for final derivation. The RL formulation directly extends DeepSeekMath's GRPO framework (Shao et al., 2024) with task-specific abstraction rewards that require no pre-trained reward model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most notable finding is that abstract thinking acquired in the narrow GSM domain implicitly improves zero-shot performance on OOD math (AIME, MATH) and general reasoning (BBH, MMLU) benchmarks, suggesting that reinforced abstraction learning cultivates a transferable reasoning skill rather than domain-specific memorization. Key limitations include scope to grade-school math in English, full-model tuning (no LoRA), greedy decoding only, and perturbations that preserve the underlying abstraction (no testing of altered reasoning schemas). Open questions include formalizing how abstraction reduces sample complexity and distribution gap, extending to competition math and multilingual settings, and whether the approach generalizes to non-mathematical reasoning domains.
