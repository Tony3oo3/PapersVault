## Problem

The state-of-the-art task-agnostic model fingerprinting method, MetaV, relies on large, diverse model sets to prevent overfitting, but constructing such sets demands prohibitive computational resources. When model sets are reduced to cut costs, MetaV's entangled joint training of all fingerprints with a single global verifier causes severe overfitting, degrading generalization to unseen models. The paper asks how to maintain strong generalization in task-agnostic fingerprinting while drastically reducing the number of trained models required.

## Input / Output

During fingerprint generation, the method takes a protected model and a small set of independently-trained models (as few as one per set, augmented with training checkpoints) and produces N optimized fingerprint inputs paired with lightweight linear verifiers. During verification, each fingerprint is fed as a standard forward-pass query to a black-box suspect model; the model's output (dimension O, task-dependent) is scored by its paired verifier via a single Frobenius inner product and sigmoid, yielding a confidence in (0,1). The final ownership decision is the arithmetic mean of all N confidence scores compared against a threshold τ, making the query budget flexibly adjustable without retraining.

## Method

LiteGuard introduces two key mechanisms to decouple parameter entanglement and augment model diversity. First, checkpoint-based model set augmentation samples intermediate training snapshots at uniform intervals (every l epochs starting from epoch e_s) from each model in the piracy and independence sets, enriching behavioral diversity at zero additional training cost. Second, a local verifier architecture replaces MetaV's single global MLP with N independent lightweight verifiers—each a single linear layer plus sigmoid—so that each fingerprint-verifier pair is trained in isolation via batch-wise binary cross-entropy over randomly sampled mini-batches of pirated and independent models. This reduces the jointly trained parameter complexity from O(N(F+OH)+H²L) in MetaV to a constant O(F+O) per pair, eliminating the overfitting risk that scales with fingerprint count. The paper further provides a parameter complexity analysis and a unified verifier framework showing that partial grouping of fingerprints under shared verifiers monotonically degrades AUC as group size increases, confirming that full decoupling is optimal under limited model pools.

## Results

LiteGuard achieves state-of-the-art AUC across all five tasks, evaluated under an extreme setting where only one trained model per set is used during fingerprint training. Headline AUCs are 0.936 (CNN/CIFAR-100), 0.902 (MLP/CASP), 0.977 (AE/California Housing), 0.803 (GNN/QM9), and 0.971 (RNN/Weather). These results are obtained with N=100 fingerprints, batch size K=1, and 1000 training iterations using the Adam optimizer at lr=0.001. The method also achieves 5–10× reduction in model training cost compared to MetaV while matching or exceeding its AUC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiteGuard outperforms the task-agnostic baseline MetaV on all five tasks, with AUC improvements of 34.5% (CNN/CIFAR-100: 0.936 vs 0.676), 9.5% (MLP/CASP: 0.902 vs 0.824), 24.9% (AE/CH: 0.977 vs 0.783), 34.3% (GNN/QM9: 0.803 vs 0.598), and 13.7% (RNN/Weather: 0.971 vs 0.854). Against task-specific baselines on CNN/CIFAR-100, LiteGuard (0.936) surpasses ADVTRA (0.845), UAP (0.752), and IPGuard (0.708). On GNN/QM9, LiteGuard (0.803) substantially outperforms the GNN-specific method GNNFingers (0.608±0.165). Notably, LiteGuard achieves these gains while using only 1 trained model per set during fingerprint training, whereas MetaV requires 10–30 models to reach comparable AUC, yielding 5–10× computational savings. Under ownership obfuscation (Table 3), LiteGuard maintains AUCs above 0.95 for most techniques on CNN/CIFAR-100, while MetaV drops to 0.511 under adversarial fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

LiteGuard builds directly on MetaV (Yang et al., 2022), the only fully task-agnostic model fingerprinting method, which jointly trains all fingerprints with a single global verifier using large piracy and independence model sets. The paper departs from MetaV along two axes: it replaces the global verifier with per-fingerprint local verifiers to reduce parameter entanglement, and it introduces checkpoint-based model set augmentation (drawing from checkpointing literature in Eisenman et al., 2022 and Wang et al., 2021a) to substitute for the large number of independently trained models that MetaV requires. The work also positions itself against task-specific fingerprinting methods (IPGuard, UAP, ADVTRA, GNNFingers), which exploit task-specific decision-boundary or structural properties but cannot transfer across architectures or tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: by reducing the required number of trained models from 10–30 to 1–5 per set while matching or exceeding MetaV's AUC, LiteGuard makes task-agnostic fingerprinting feasible in domains where publicly available models are scarce and training budgets are limited. The finding that a task-agnostic method can outperform task-specific baselines on their own target tasks suggests that the decoupled local-verifier design captures model-specific behavioral signatures more effectively than task-tailored adversarial constructions. Open questions include whether the unified verifier spectrum (Section 6) can be further exploited for intermediate coupling levels, how performance holds under more aggressive obfuscation (KD remains the weakest case), and whether the checkpoint augmentation strategy transfers to settings where training checkpoints are not available (e.g., pre-trained foundation models).
