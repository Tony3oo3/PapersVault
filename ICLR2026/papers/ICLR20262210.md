## Problem

Expert offloading systems for MoE LLMs exploit the locality of expert activations, yet the degree of this local routing consistency varies substantially across models and has been studied only on a single model (Mixtral) over 2-token spans. It remains unclear which architectural factors—shared experts, load balance, expert combination space, specialization type—determine whether a given MoE model is well-suited for segment-based expert caching.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262210/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis consumes per-token routing decisions from 20 MoE LLMs (3B–57B parameters) on 22,528 sequences of 512 tokens drawn from 11 domains, parameterized by segment length m ∈ {4, 16, 64, 256} and cache ratio ρ. SRP is reported per expert and per layer as a parameter-free upper-bound F1 score (plus a supplementary size ratio ρ̂), while SCH is reported per layer as a hit rate under a hard cache limit of ρ × active experts. Toy OLMoE-like models (1.43B params) pre-trained from scratch with single-factor architectural modifications serve as controlled validation.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary metrics: SRP (Segment Routing Best Performance) defines the upper-bound F1 score of a segment-based router that must predict a single active/inactive decision for all m tokens in a segment, proven to depend only on the expert's activation frequency distribution; SCH (Segment Cache Best Hit Rate) defines the hit rate of an oracle cache that evicts the experts least activated in the next m tokens under a fixed cache size limit, bridging SRP to practical offloading. The authors validate architectural factors by pre-training toy models with controlled changes to shared experts, load-balance loss coefficient, expert count, and activation count, revealing a strong trade-off between local routing consistency and local load balance, that shared experts and reduced expert combination space degrade consistency, and that domain-specialized experts (not vocabulary-specialized ones) drive both local consistency and global load balance. They further demonstrate that SCH correlates with LRU/LFU hit rates (r > 0.81) and that a cache size of approximately 2× the number of active experts achieves the best effectiveness–efficiency balance across most models.

## Results

Across 20 MoE LLMs (3B–57B parameters), SRP at m=16 ranges from 78.16 (LLaMA-MoE-v2) down to 19.27 (SwitchTransformers decoder), with most models clustering between 36–55. The paper identifies four distinct groups: Group 1 (LLaMA-MoE-v2, Yuan2.0, PowerMoE, Qwen3, Phi-3.5-MoE, OLMoE, GRIN-MoE) achieves SRP > 50 with low size ratio ρ̂ ≈ 1.25; Group 4 (NLLB-MoE, SwitchTransformers, OpenMoE, Qwen1.5-MoE) falls below 31. For expert offloading, SCH analysis shows that a cache ratio ρ = 2 (cache size ≈ 2× active experts) yields the best balance between hit rate and memory efficiency, with SCH(m=16, ρ=2) reaching 90.55% on the Baseline toy model versus 80.97% at ρ=1. SCH correlates strongly with practical cache algorithms: LRU achieves 90.43% correlation and LFU 88.70% at m=16 across all REAL models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an empirical analysis paper rather than a method-vs-baseline comparison, so the key contrasts are architectural and metric-level. Among the 20 REAL models, those without shared experts (Groups 1–2: LLaMA-MoE-v2, Mixtral, OLMoE, GRIN-MoE, etc.) consistently outperform those with shared experts (Groups 3–4: DeepSeekMoE, Qwen2, Qwen1.5-MoE, OpenMoE) by 10–30 SRP points at m=16, confirming that shared experts reduce expert combination space and harm local routing consistency. Toy model ablations verify this: Share1/Share2 drop SRP from 43.56 (Baseline) to 41.38/38.79, while NoLB (no load-balance loss) reaches 56.42 SRP but with poor load balance (SD=13.21 vs. 4.02 for Baseline), and OverLB drops to 36.42. On the cache side, SCH(m=16) at ρ=2 achieves 90.55% hit rate on the Baseline toy model, closely approaching the clairvoyant optimal (100%), while LRU and LFU reach only 67.04% and 70.87% respectively—yet all three are highly correlated (r > 0.88) with SCH across models, validating SCH as a practical upper bound. Domain-specialized experts (e.g., in Qwen3, OLMoE, GRIN-MoE) yield SRP 10%+ higher on ArXiv/GitHub/StackExchange than the global average, whereas vocabulary specialization shows negligible or negative correlation with SRP, establishing domain specialization as the dominant factor for routing consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the line of expert offloading work (Eliseev & Mazur, 2023; Hwang et al., 2024; Xue et al., 2024b) that observed locality in expert activations, but departs from prior single-model, short-span observations (e.g., Jiang et al., 2024 examining only 2 consecutive tokens in Mixtral) by formalizing local routing consistency as a measurable, model-level property with two parameter-free metrics (SRP and SCH). It also extends the expert specialization analysis of Muennighoff et al. (2025) by connecting domain specialization directly to routing consistency and global load balance, and uses toy models to isolate architectural factors (shared experts, expert combination space, load balance loss) that prior work had not systematically controlled for.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic, cross-model quantification of how 'offloading-friendly' different MoE architectures are, yielding actionable design guidance: avoid shared experts, preserve expert combination space, and accept a local load balance trade-off. The practical recommendation that a cache size of ~2× active experts balances effectiveness and efficiency gives system designers a concrete starting point. Open questions remain on whether training objectives can be explicitly shaped to improve long-term (m≥16) routing consistency without quality loss, and whether the findings generalize to MoE variants beyond FFN replacement (e.g., mixture-of-attention). A limitation is that the analysis is purely observational on routing patterns rather than end-to-end offloading system benchmarks.
