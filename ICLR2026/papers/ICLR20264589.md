## Problem

Inversion-based watermarking for diffusion models requires reconstructing the initial noise from a generated image, but existing inversion methods are confined to high-NFE (50+ steps) regimes because they over-optimize internal discretization error. In the watermarking setting, however, external distortions (JPEG, blur, cropping) dominate the error budget, making high-NFE both unnecessary and counterproductive—it also renders end-to-end adversarial training computationally infeasible due to the prohibitive cost of backpropagating through long iterative trajectories.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB image that has been subjected to a distortion (JPEG compression, random crop/drop, resize, Gaussian/median blur, Gaussian/salt-and-pepper noise, or brightness shift), VAE-encodes it into a latent z_0^D, and produces a single reconstructed noise vector ẑ_T in the same latent space. The inversion is performed unconditionally (guidance scale 1.0, null prompt) with the denoiser queried at timestep t=0 rather than t=T, which empirically yields a better initialization for the single-step prediction. The output noise is then fed to a downstream watermark extractor (Tree-Ring or Gaussian Shading) to recover the embedded bits.

## Method

FARI exploits a geometric asymmetry: the DDIM inversion trajectory has substantially lower curvature than the generation trajectory (because accumulated inversion error retains low-frequency semantic information that stabilizes the path direction), making it highly compressible to a single step. The method fine-tunes the denoiser via LoRA (rank 8, attention modules only) with a unified objective that simultaneously performs step distillation and adversarial robustness training: each iteration samples a ground-truth noise z_T, generates an image via 20-step DDIM (LoRA deactivated), applies a random distortion, VAE-encodes the result, and trains the LoRA branch to predict z_T in one forward pass via ẑ_T = √(ᾱ_T)·z_0^D + √(1−ᾱ_T)·ε_θ(z_0^D, 0, ∅; ψ). Because the computational graph is a single denoiser call, end-to-end adversarial training over 9 distortion types becomes feasible in ~20 minutes on one RTX A6000 GPU. The LoRA adapter is deactivated during generation, preserving the original model's output quality while providing a plug-and-play robust inverter for watermark verification.

## Results

FARI achieves 1-step (NFE=1) inversion that surpasses the 50-step DDIM baseline on nearly all distortion types for both downstream watermarking tasks. On SD v2.1 with Gaussian Shading, FARI reaches 0.9824 bit accuracy under adversarial distortion (vs. 0.9755 for DDIM-50) and 0.9659 under salt-and-pepper noise (vs. 0.9373). For Tree-Ring on SD v2.1, FARI achieves TPR@1e-3 of 0.997 under adversarial distortion (vs. 0.962 for DDIM-50) and 0.979 under Gaussian noise (vs. 0.726). Training takes only ~20 minutes on a single RTX A6000 to surpass the 50-step baseline, with total training of ~70 minutes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FARI (1 NFE) outperforms the 50-step DDIM baseline on virtually every distortion for both watermarking tasks, e.g., +0.7% GS bit accuracy under adversarial distortion and +25.3% TR TPR under Gaussian noise on SD v2.1. Against EDICT (50 NFE), FARI wins on all distortions except clean images, with the largest gap on Gaussian noise (+4.2% GS, +29.8% TR). BELM (50 NFE) is the weakest baseline, collapsing to near-zero TR TPR under random crop/drop (0.001/0.000) while FARI maintains 1.000. AMED-Solver (2 NFE, adversarially retrained) is the closest competitor but still trails FARI by 1.6–2.3% GS and 7–32% TR across distortions. Distillation-based methods LCM-LoRA (2 NFE) and DMD2 (1 NFE) underperform FARI substantially, confirming that generation-oriented distillation does not transfer to the inversion task. ExactDPM (>150 NFE) is the slowest baseline and, despite its gradient-descent optimization, is outperformed by FARI on most distortions while requiring orders of magnitude more computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FARI repurposes the distillation paradigm from diffusion generation acceleration (LCM-LoRA, DMD2, AMED-Solver) to the inversion direction, but with a critical conceptual departure: rather than mimicking a 50-step DDIM teacher, it learns a direct one-step mapping from a generated image back to its ground-truth initial noise. This is enabled by the paper's geometric observation that inversion trajectories exhibit substantially lower curvature than generation trajectories, making them far more compressible. The method also departs from prior inversion work (EDICT, BELM, ExactDPM) that over-optimizes internal truncation error, instead reframing the bottleneck as external distortion and using the resulting low-NFE regime to unlock end-to-end adversarial LoRA fine-tuning that was previously computationally infeasible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FARI delivers a 50× reduction in inversion NFE while simultaneously improving robustness over the 50-step DDIM baseline, trainable in ~20 minutes on a single GPU—a practical recipe likely to be widely adopted for large-scale watermark verification. The LoRA plug-and-play design preserves original generation quality, making deployment straightforward. Key limitations remain: the method is restricted to ODE-based samplers (failing with SDE samplers), is unsuitable for image editing due to sacrificed clean-inversion precision, and its robustness ceiling is still governed by the underlying watermark design. Open questions include whether the one-step formulation can be leveraged to make adversarial watermark-removal attacks more tractable, and how the approach generalizes to video diffusion or flow-matching architectures.
