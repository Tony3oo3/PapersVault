## Problem

Fibring of neural networks was introduced as a neurosymbolic framework inspired by fibring of modal logics, but the exact formal correspondence between the two was never established. This gap prevented fibring from being used as a rigorous formalism for deriving logical expressiveness results for modern architectures such as GNNs, GATs, and Transformer encoders, leaving no unified logical characterization across these architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261142/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained GAT (or GNN/Transformer encoder) with rational weights, a graph G (or complete graph with positional encodings for Transformers), a target node u, and Boolean node feature vectors x_u ∈ {0,1}^n. It produces a fibred modal logic formula φ̃^τ in the language L_{F^τ} such that its truth value at the world corresponding to x_u in any compatible fibred model equals the network's classification output (true/false). Crucially, the formula is non-uniform: for a fixed (G, u) the fibring architecture F^τ is fixed, but the fibring functions vary with the node features x, yielding a countable family of formulas indexed by inputs.

## Method

The paper first generalizes the original fibring definition to arbitrary directed trees of neural architectures, where each edge specifies a layer and a set of positions at which a child network's output is spliced back into the parent. It then defines compatible fibred models — Kripke models whose accessibility relations mirror network equivalence on inputs and whose logical fibring functions mirror the delegation of computation to child networks — and proves this class is non-empty and closed under isomorphism, establishing a valid fibred logic L_F. The key theorem (Theorem 4.5) constructs, for any root network instance, a propositional formula whose truth in the fibred logic exactly matches the fibred network's classification, via induction on the fibring tree depth. For GATs, the fibring architecture is built from the lazy unraveling tree of the target node at the GAT's depth, with fibring functions encoding neighbor features and layer weights; the attention mechanism is captured by additional nodes in the tree. This yields non-uniform expressiveness results: each GAT instance is characterized by a countable family of fibred logic formulas, one per input, with the formula structure depending only on (G, u) and not on the node features.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or quantitative metrics. The main results are three theorems: (1) Theorem 4.5 establishes an exact correspondence between fibred neural networks and a fragment of fibred modal logic, showing that for every network instance there exists a propositional formula whose truth value in any compatible fibred model coincides with the network's classification output; (2) Theorem 5.1 shows that for every GAT (and by extension GNN and Transformer encoder) instance applied to a specific graph and node, there exists a fibred neural network whose computation exactly reproduces the GAT's output; (3) Theorem 5.2 derives a non-uniform expressiveness result, showing that for each GAT instance there exists a formula in the fibred logic whose truth value at the appropriate world matches the GAT's classification for all possible node feature assignments. The fibring architecture is fixed per (GAT, graph, node) tuple, and only the fibring functions vary with the input features.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not perform empirical comparisons against baselines. Instead, it positions its non-uniform expressiveness results relative to existing logical characterizations in the literature. The discriminative power of standard GNNs is known to be upper-bounded by the Weisfeiler-Leman (WL) test (Barcelo et al., 2020), while Grohe (2023) established non-uniform expressiveness in terms of Boolean circuits. For uniform expressiveness, Benedikt et al. (2024) derived Presburger logic characterizations for broad GNN classes, and Cuenca Grau et al. (2025) showed bounded GNNs correspond to fragments of first-order logic. For Transformer encoders, Hao et al. (2022) mapped Unique Hard Attention Transformers to AC^0 fragments, and Barcelo et al. (2024) established a uniform lower bound via LTL(C,+). The present paper's contribution is orthogonal: it provides a *non-uniform* fibred-logic characterization that unifies GNNs, GATs, and Transformer encoders under a single formalism, whereas prior results treat these architectures separately and use different logical frameworks (Presburger logic, first-order logic, AC^0, LTL). The paper explicitly notes that bridging its non-uniform results to uniform expressiveness (e.g., collapsing the countable family of fibred formulas into a single Presburger formula) remains open.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper formalizes a correspondence that was conceptually anticipated but never rigorously established: it bridges Garcez & Gabbay's (2004) fibring of neural networks with Gabbay's (1999) fibring of modal logics by defining compatible fibred models and proving an exact equivalence between fibred network computation and satisfaction in a fibred modal logic. It positions itself within the broader neurosymbolic AI program of characterizing neural architectures via logical expressiveness, building on the discriminative-power bounds of Barcelo et al. (2020), the descriptive-complexity results of Grohe (2023), the Presburger-logic uniform expressiveness of Benedikt et al. (2024) for GNNs, and the LTL(C,+) lower bounds of Barcelo et al. (2024) for Transformer encoders. The key departure is providing a single fibred-logic formalism that simultaneously covers GNNs, GATs, and Transformer encoders, rather than treating each architecture with a bespoke logical characterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper opens a path toward unifying the logical expressiveness of GNNs and Transformers under one formalism, which could eventually yield uniform (single-formula) characterizations for GATs and Transformer encoders where only non-uniform or lower-bound results currently exist. The non-uniform results are a stepping stone: the authors argue that the recursive structure of fibred formulas mirrors the ℓ-spectra used in Benedikt et al.'s uniform GNN results, suggesting a route to collapse the countable formula families into single Presburger (or richer) formulas. A significant open question is whether the fibring framework can actually deliver uniform expressiveness for GATs and Transformers, and whether the resulting fibred logics inherit useful decidability/complexity properties from known results on modal and fibred logics (e.g., Wu et al., 2015). The longer-term vision of reverse-engineering interpretable logical theories from trained networks via fibred formulas is ambitious but currently speculative.
