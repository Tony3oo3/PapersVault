## Problem

While VLMs can translate visual inputs into PDDL problem files, accurately generating PDDL domain files—which encode the planning rules, action preconditions, and effects—remains a critical bottleneck that typically requires human expertise or direct environment interaction. Existing approaches either assume access to ground-truth domain PDDL, rely on textual scenario descriptions, or need environment feedback, none of which are available when the only inputs are a natural language rule description and a single image of the initial state.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a natural language domain description (game rules, available actions, planning objective) and a single RGB problem-setup image showing the initial spatial configuration. It produces two PDDL files: a domain file (defining predicates, actions with preconditions and effects) that is reusable across all instances of the same domain, and a problem file (specifying objects, initial state, and goal) that is instantiated per scene. The PDDL planner then solves the problem to yield an action sequence. The pipeline is dimensionality-agnostic, operating identically on 2D grid-world images and multi-view 3D scene renderings with partial observability.

## Method

VLMFP employs a dual-VLM architecture: a fine-tuned Qwen2-VL-7B (SimVLM) handles spatial perception and action simulation, while a large API-based VLM (GPT-4o, GenVLM) performs symbolic reasoning and PDDL generation. SimVLM is trained on 430k action-sequence simulations across six grid-world domains with varied map sizes, appearances, and rules, learning to describe scenes, predict step-by-step action outcomes, and judge goal achievement. The iterative refinement loop works in four steps: (1) SimVLM describes the scene and GenVLM generates candidate PDDL files; (2) prescreening checks syntactic and semantic validity; (3) random action sequences are executed in both the PDDL environment and SimVLM, with discrepancies quantified via a bidirectional Exploration Walk (EW) score; (4) GenVLM receives natural-language feedback on mismatches and refines the PDDL files. This loop repeats until consistency is achieved, eliminating the need for pre-defined domain files, human corrections, or environment access.

## Results

On six grid-world domains, VLMFP (with GPT-4o as GenVLM) achieves an average planning success rate of 70.0% on unseen instances in seen appearances and 54.1% in unseen appearances, outperforming the strongest baseline CodePDDL (30.7% / 32.3%) by 39.3 and 21.8 percentage points respectively. SimVLM (fine-tuned Qwen2-VL-7B) achieves 87.3% and 86.0% average string-matching accuracy for scenario understanding and action simulation on seen and unseen appearances. On complex 3D tasks (Assembly and MultiRob), VLMFP reaches 86.4% and 79.8% average success rates for unseen instances in seen and unseen appearances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLMFP substantially outperforms all baselines across all six grid-world domains. Against the strongest baseline CodePDDL (GPT-4o), which has access to SimVLM-generated scenario descriptions but lacks the iterative feedback-and-update loop, VLMFP gains +39.3 pp (seen) and +21.8 pp (unseen) in average success rate. The gap is most dramatic in complex domains: on Sokoban (unseen), VLMFP achieves 25.1% vs. 0.4% for CodePDDL, and on Printer (unseen), 61.0% vs. 22.1%. Direct plan generation and Chain-of-Thought prompting (Wei et al., 2022) perform very poorly (≤12.7% average even with GPT-5), confirming that without explicit PDDL grounding, LLMs cannot maintain consistency in long-horizon planning. Notably, VLMFP's advantage over CodePDDL grows with domain complexity, validating that the simulation-consistency feedback and iterative PDDL refinement steps are critical for capturing multi-object interactions and complex action preconditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM+PDDL planning line (Shirai et al. 2024; Dang et al. 2025) that translates visual observations into PDDL problem files, but departs from the universal assumption in prior work that a ground-truth domain PDDL file is provided. It also builds on Mahdavi et al. (2024), who used environment interaction to generate both PDDL files, by replacing direct environment access with a fine-tuned SimVLM that simulates action consequences as a proxy oracle. The key architectural departure is the Dual-VLM split: a small fine-tuned model (Qwen2-VL-7B) handles spatial perception and action simulation, while a large API model (GPT-4o) handles PDDL generation and iterative refinement, with the Exploration Walk score from Mahdavi et al. serving as the consistency metric between the two.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VLMFP removes the expert-knowledge bottleneck that has limited PDDL planner adoption: a non-expert user can now obtain a working formal plan from a single image and a natural-language rule description. The framework's multi-level generalization (reusable domain files, in-context problem instantiation, transfer to unseen appearances and modified rules) makes it a practical template for deploying symbolic planners in visually grounded settings. Open questions remain: performance still degrades substantially on complex multi-object domains (Sokoban drops to 25.1% unseen), entirely novel dynamics (Rule 5) break execution simulation despite correct reasoning, and the 3D evaluation, while promising, covers only two tasks with limited visual diversity. Scaling to open-ended real-world scenes with continuous state spaces and temporal dynamics is the natural next step.
