## Problem

Linear models are surprisingly competitive for long-term time series forecasting, yet the theoretical reasons for their effectiveness remain underexplored, and their performance degrades under noise due to the learning of spurious characteristic roots. The paper identifies a fundamental data-scaling limitation—MSE-based linear training converges only at O(1/√T) under noise—motivating structural regularization to recover the low-dimensional latent dynamics that govern true temporal behavior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260743/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a multi-channel time series segmented into overlapping history windows of length L (typically 720) and produces direct multi-horizon forecasts for H steps ahead (H ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720}) via a single linear weight matrix W ∈ R^{L×H}. Inputs are preprocessed with instance normalization (centering and scaling each segment) and modeled channel-independently, so each channel shares the same W. In the frequency-domain variant, the linear map is applied as F⁻¹ ∘ W_F ∘ F, effectively performing a learned frequency filter.

## Method

The paper provides a characteristic-root-based theoretical framework showing that a linear model's expressivity is determined by whether the data's roots are a subset of the model's roots (Fact 1), and that higher-horizon or longer-lookback models preserve the fundamental roots (Fact 2). To combat noise-induced spurious roots, two complementary regularization strategies are proposed: (1) Rank Reduction via Reduced-Rank Regression (projecting OLS outputs onto a truncated-SVD subspace) or Direct Weight Rank Reduction (truncating the SVD of W post-hoc), which enforces a low-rank bottleneck; and (2) Root Purge, a novel adaptive training loss that adds a regularizer λ‖G_W ∘ P(residual)‖²_F, re-applying the model to its own prediction error to drive noise into the learned null space. Root Purge dynamically adjusts the effective rank through the rank-nullity tradeoff during optimization, balancing signal recovery (root-seeking) against noise suppression (root-purging) without requiring a fixed rank specification.

## Results

On the primary benchmark (Table 1, MSE, lower is better, L=720, H∈{96,192,336,720}), Root Purge achieves 13 first-place results out of 32 dataset-horizon combinations, while RRR achieves 9, and all other baselines (FEDformer, FilterNet, TSLANet, TimesNet, PatchTST, DLinear, SparseTSF, FITS) achieve at most 2 (PatchTST). Representative numbers: on ETTh1 H=96, Root Purge reaches 0.359 MSE vs. 0.362 (SparseTSF, best baseline); on Exchange H=96, Root Purge reaches 0.082 vs. 0.088 (DLinear); on Weather H=720, RRR reaches 0.304 vs. 0.309 (FITS). The gains are most pronounced on smaller datasets (ETT, Exchange, Weather), consistent with the paper's data-scaling theory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Both proposed methods (RRR and Root Purge) consistently outperform all eight baselines across the six main-text datasets. Root Purge secures 13 of 32 first-place MSE results, RRR secures 9, while the strongest baseline (PatchTST) wins only 2 (on ETTh2 H=96 and ETTm2 H=96). Among linear baselines, SparseTSF and FITS are the closest competitors, but Root Purge still edges them out by 0.001–0.007 MSE on most settings (e.g., ETTh1 H=96: 0.359 vs. 0.362; Exchange H=96: 0.082 vs. 0.086). Against transformer-based models (FEDformer, PatchTST, TSLANet) and convolution-based models (TimesNet, FilterNet), the gap is larger—typically 0.02–0.08 MSE—especially at longer horizons (H=336, 720) where recursive or attention-based errors accumulate. The advantage is most pronounced on smaller datasets (ETT, Exchange, Weather), aligning with the paper's theoretical claim that structural regularization compensates for limited data, whereas on larger datasets (Traffic, Electricity, reported in Appendix E.4) the margin narrows as data scaling alone becomes sufficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the recent wave of work showing that simple linear models (DLinear, FITS, SparseTSF) are competitive with or superior to complex transformer-based architectures for long-term time series forecasting, and on classical linear systems theory (characteristic roots, companion matrices, difference equations). It departs from prior work by providing a unified theoretical lens—characteristic root analysis—that simultaneously explains why common design choices (instance normalization, channel-independent modeling, independent per-horizon regression) are principled, and by identifying a concrete data-scaling bottleneck (O(1/√T) noise convergence) that motivates structural regularization. The proposed Root Purge loss is a novel adaptive regularizer that connects to rank-nullity theory, while the rank reduction methods (RRR, DWRR) repurpose classical Reduced-Rank Regression (Izenman, 1975) and truncated SVD in a time-series-specific context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers practitioners a theoretically grounded, interpretable alternative to black-box deep forecasting models, with Root Purge providing a simple, single-hyperparameter regularizer that works in both time and frequency domains. The data-scaling property (noise effects decay only as O(1/√T)) is a practical warning: in high-noise, data-limited regimes, simply collecting more data is inefficient without structural constraints. Limitations include the restriction to linear dynamics and single-channel analysis in the theory, the need to select rank ρ for RRR, and modest empirical margins over strong linear baselines. Open questions include whether the characteristic-root framework extends meaningfully to nonlinear or non-stationary dynamics, and whether Root Purge's null-space learning generalizes beyond the linear setting.
