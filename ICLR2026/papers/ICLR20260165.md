## Problem

Discrete latent variables in VAEs cannot be exactly reparameterized, forcing reliance on approximate techniques (Gumbel-Softmax, straight-through estimation) or high-variance gradient-free estimators (REINFORCE). Gumbel-Softmax is temperature-sensitive and suffers from vanishing gradients in autoregressive settings, while vector-quantization methods (VQ-VAE, FSQ) cannot compute the true ELBO and lack explicit entropy control. The paper asks whether policy-search techniques from reinforcement learning—specifically trust-region and natural-gradient methods—can train discrete VAEs stably and at scale without any reparameterization trick.

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images (MNIST 28×28, CIFAR-10 32×32, ImageNet-256 256×256) or retargeted robot motion trajectories (LAFAN, 32×121 tensors of relative joint poses for the Unitree H1) and produces a reconstructed output plus a discrete latent code organized as an autoregressive sequence of B blocks, each drawn from a vocabulary of V classes (e.g., 8×256 for MNIST, 64×512 for CIFAR-10, 1024×1024 for ImageNet-256). The autoregressive structure means each latent block is conditioned on all previously sampled blocks, enabling multimodal latent generation. For the robotics downstream task, the discrete latents serve as a compact command space consumed by a low-level RL policy that outputs 19-dimensional torque vectors at 100 Hz.

## Method

DAPS reformulates the discrete VAE encoder update as a KL-regularized policy-search problem: it constructs a closed-form nonparametric target distribution q* (derived from REPS) that balances the reconstruction return against a trust-region constraint to the current parametric encoder q_θ, then updates q_θ via self-normalized importance-weighted maximum likelihood, avoiding backpropagation through the autoregressive sampling path entirely. A soft-maximum of K sampled returns serves as a low-variance baseline for the advantage estimate. The trust-region multiplier η is adapted automatically by matching the minibatch effective sample size (ESS) to a target level, yielding stable, task-agnostic step sizes that decay smoothly over training. The decoder is updated separately via standard reconstruction loss (coordinate descent), and the overall framework uses a vision transformer for autoregressive encoding and a ResNet (or transformer for trajectories) for generation, scaling to ImageNet-256 with 1,024-block sequences.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (576-bit bottleneck), DAPS achieves PSNR 25.21 and FID 157.27, outperforming FSQ (PSNR 24.19, FID 163.00) and VQ-VAE (PSNR 24.19, FID 164.30). On ImageNet-256 (10,240-bit bottleneck), DAPS reaches PSNR 24.66 and FID 48.65, beating FSQ (PSNR 24.24, FID 54.54) and VQ-VAE (PSNR 23.83, FID 65.01). On the LAFAN motion dataset (640-bit bottleneck), DAPS achieves PSNR 36.81, surpassing FSQ (36.19) and VQ-VAE (31.04). On MNIST (64-bit bottleneck), DAPS is competitive with VQ-VAE and FSQ (PSNR 18.23 vs. 18.45/18.42) while also producing high-quality samples from the latent prior, a capability VQ-based methods lack.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FSQ, DAPS wins on CIFAR-10 (PSNR 25.21 vs. 24.19; FID 157.27 vs. 163.00), ImageNet (PSNR 24.66 vs. 24.24; FID 48.65 vs. 54.54), and LAFAN (PSNR 36.81 vs. 36.19), while FSQ edges out DAPS on MNIST PSNR (18.42 vs. 18.23). Against VQ-VAE, DAPS achieves substantially larger margins on ImageNet (FID 48.65 vs. 65.01) and LAFAN (PSNR 36.81 vs. 31.04), with moderate gains on CIFAR-10 (PSNR 25.21 vs. 24.19). Against Gumbel-Softmax and its Rao-Blackwellized variant GR-MCK, DAPS dominates across all four datasets: on CIFAR-10, DAPS reaches PSNR 25.21 versus 23.74 (Gumbel) and 22.69 (GR-MCK), and on ImageNet, DAPS achieves FID 48.65 versus 51.66 (Gumbel-NA) and 73.21 (GR-MCK). The autoregressive Gumbel-Softmax baseline is excluded from ImageNet entirely due to training instability at the base learning rate, whereas DAPS trains stably with a single set of hyperparameters. Overall, DAPS is the only method that simultaneously achieves the best or near-best PSNR, FID, and β-ELBO across all four datasets, with the sole exception being MNIST PSNR where VQ-VAE leads by 0.22 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAPS reframes the discrete VAE encoder update as a KL-regularized policy-search problem, directly building on the closed-form optimal policy from REPS (Peters et al., 2010) and the ESS-based trust-region adaptation from LBPS (Watson & Peters, 2023). It departs from the two dominant paradigms for discrete latent VAEs—approximate reparameterization (Gumbel-Softmax, GR-MCK) and vector quantization with straight-through estimation (VQ-VAE, FSQ)—by eliminating both the temperature hyperparameter and the non-differentiable quantization step. The weighted maximum likelihood update is conceptually similar to V-MPO (Song et al., 2019) and SPU (Vuong et al., 2018), but those methods target continuous or general RL policies, whereas DAPS is the first to apply this recipe to autoregressive discrete encoders in a VAE setting with an explicit β-ELBO objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAPS provides a practical, stable training recipe for discrete VAEs that scales to ImageNet-256 with 1,024-block autoregressive latents—a regime where Gumbel-Softmax is unstable and GR-MCK is memory-prohibitive—while offering explicit entropy/bit-rate control via β that quantization-based methods lack. The downstream robotics experiment (goal-conditioned whole-body motion on Unitree H1) demonstrates that the learned discrete latent space is not merely a compression artifact but a usable command space for hierarchical control. Open questions include whether the autoregressive sampling bottleneck can be further reduced (e.g., via speculative decoding or parallel sampling), whether the approach extends to continuous or mixed discrete-continuous latents, and how it compares to emerging discrete diffusion or flow-matching objectives at even larger scales.
