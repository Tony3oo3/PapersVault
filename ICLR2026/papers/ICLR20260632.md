## Problem

Existing constrained RL methods (CPO, CUP, PCPO) rely on greedy projection or recovery onto the feasible set, which can trap the policy in safe-but-suboptimal local optima, especially during early training when the policy has no knowledge of constraint boundaries. The intersection of feasible policies and KL-ball-constrained policies may be empty, making standard projection-based updates infeasible or requiring carefully tuned cost-bound decay schedules. The paper asks whether one can guarantee monotonic safety improvement at every iteration without overcommitting to any single cost estimate.

## Input / Output

The method consumes continuous environment states and actions sampled from an off-policy replay buffer (batches of 1024, 20 batches per update) in continuous state-action spaces (differential-drive cars or point agents in 3D safety-gymnasium environments). It produces a Gaussian policy network π(a|s) parameterized by mean and covariance, updated via a split KL-constrained cloning step. The nonparametric surrogate q* is computed in closed form from the batch, and Q/C value functions are maintained with the RETRACE off-policy estimator.

## Method

SafeMPO extends MPO's EM-style update to CMDPs by introducing a joint 'optimal and safe' event, where safety likelihood is a truncated exponential in the cost (equal for all feasible policies, decaying exponentially beyond the budget B). The E-step solves a nonparametric surrogate maximizing expected Q plus a logarithmic barrier κ·log(x/κ) on the safety improvement constraint K(q,π)≥x, with a KL-divergence constraint to the current policy. This interior-point-style barrier guarantees strictly positive safety improvement at every iteration regardless of κ, and the dual problem has only two variables (λ, ν) solvable by SLSQP. The M-step clones the resulting q* into the policy network via a split KL-constrained objective. The paper proves that the naive improvement constraint is insufficient for contraction, but the log-barrier variant yields geometric convergence to the feasible set under mild assumptions (bounded Q, non-constant G, fully supported π, concave K).

## Results

On the Safety-Gymnasium benchmark (10M steps, cost budget B=25), SafeMPO achieves reward 21.43±5.10 with cost 32.23±7.43 on SafetyCarGoal, 0.67±0.61 with cost 30.87±4.47 on SafetyCarButton, and 13.09±2.79 with cost 32.92±3.43 on SafetyPointGoal. With a tightened budget B=20, SafeMPO produces feasible policies (cost 25.68±2.97) at near-SOTA reward (20.24±2.24) on SafetyCarGoal and (18.36±3.41, cost 23.00±1.56) on SafetyPointGoal. SafeMPO consistently exhibits the lowest run-to-run cost variance among all compared methods, and requires only a single hyperparameter (κ=10) that does not affect asymptotic behavior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On SafetyCarGoal, CPO achieves the highest reward (25.52) but at a substantially higher cost (43.32 vs. SafeMPO's 32.23); SafeMPO outperforms CUP (6.14 R) and CPPOPID (10.60 R) by a wide margin while maintaining lower cost than PCPO (38.42) and CPO (43.32). On SafetyCarButton, SafeMPO (0.67 R, 30.87 C) beats RCPO (−2.04 R, 43.48 C) and CPPOPID (−1.36 R, 14.62 C) on reward, and achieves lower cost than CPO (37.86), PCPO (40.52), and CUP (103.24), though CPO edges it slightly on reward (0.82 vs. 0.67). On SafetyPointGoal, CPO (20.46 R, 28.84 C) and PCPO (18.57 R, 22.98 C) outperform SafeMPO (13.09 R, 32.92 C), making this the weakest benchmark for the proposed method; however, SafeMPO@B=20 recovers to 18.36 reward with a feasible cost of 23.00. Across all three environments, SafeMPO consistently shows the lowest cost standard deviation (≤7.43) compared to baselines that range up to 123.12 (CUP on CarButton), indicating substantially more stable training. The paper notes that no single baseline dominates across all environments, and that SafeMPO's single untuned hyperparameter (κ=10) contrasts with the highly tuned Omnisafe hyperparameters used for all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MPO (Abdolmaleki et al., 2018) nonparametric surrogate + cloning framework to constrained MDPs by introducing a joint 'optimal and safe' event and solving the resulting EM problem with a logarithmic barrier on a safety-order-preserving improvement constraint. It departs from the dominant CRL paradigm of projection-based recovery (CPO, CUP, PCPO) or Lagrangian dual updates (RCPO, CPPOPID) by instead guaranteeing monotonic safety improvement per iteration, drawing on interior-point methods to ensure the improvement is strictly positive. The conceptual novelty lies in the safety-order-preserving function abstraction and the proof that naive expectation-based improvement bounds fail to contract, while the log-barrier formulation provably does.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical failure mode of projection-based CRL—premature convergence to safe-but-suboptimal local optima (illustrated by the ball-throwing example)—and provides the first general convergence guarantees for incremental safety improvement in CRL. The single hyperparameter κ that does not affect asymptotic behavior is a practical advantage over methods requiring carefully tuned Lagrangian schedules or PID gains. However, the truncated exponential likelihood function causes costs to hover above the budget (≈30 vs B=25), and the method underperforms on the easiest benchmark (SafetyPointGoal), leaving open the question of whether alternative safety likelihoods (e.g., CVaR) or different safety-order-preserving functions can close this gap.
