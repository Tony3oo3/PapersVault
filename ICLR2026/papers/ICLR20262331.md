## Problem

Pre-trained vision-language models assume complete multimodal inputs, but real-world deployment often encounters missing modalities due to privacy, collection, or resource constraints. Existing prompt-based methods (MMP, DCP) handle missing modalities by inserting learnable tokens, which fail to explicitly model cross-modal relationships and introduce unavoidable inference overhead. The paper addresses how to fine-tune multimodal models for missing-modality scenarios while preserving inter-modal alignment and enabling modality-specific adaptation, without adding any inference latency.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images and text descriptions, where a missing image is replaced by an all-1 matrix and a missing text by an empty string; the missing ratio η (50%, 70%, 90%) controls the fraction of incomplete samples during both training and testing. For classification, it outputs a class label (evaluated via F1-Macro, top-1 accuracy, or AUROC depending on the dataset). For the composed image retrieval extension, it takes a reference image plus optional text modification and outputs a ranked list of target images on MS-COCO.

## Method

MoRA decomposes each encoder's weight update into a modality-specific component (standard LoRA-style BA matrices) and a modality-shared component, both expressed in DoRA's magnitude-direction form. The key mechanism for cross-modal transfer is the use of Gram matrices: shared parameters S^v and S^t are projected into a common r×r rank space via G^v = S^v S^{vT} and G^t = S^t S^{tT}, then applied cross-modally as S^{vT} G^t S^v (for vision) and S^{tT} G^v S^t (for text), elegantly resolving the dimension mismatch between 768-d vision and 512-d text encoders without projection layers. This dual structure enforces that both modalities move in a shared direction (preserving the CLIP alignment gap) while retaining independent adaptation directions for downstream tasks. All learnable parameters (~0.11% of total) are absorbed into the frozen pretrained weights before inference, yielding zero additional inference latency compared to the base model.

## Results

On the three main benchmarks (MM-IMDb, Food101, Hateful Memes) at 70% missing ratio, MoRA achieves average F1/Accuracy/AUROC of 53.97, 84.21, and 70.09 respectively, outperforming the strongest baseline DCP by 2.94, 1.52, and 5.75 points. Across all missing ratios (50%, 70%, 90%), MoRA delivers average improvements of 5.30%, 1.91%, and 8.51% over DCP on the three datasets. MoRA requires only 0.11% of trainable parameters versus full fine-tuning and uses 25.90% of the inference time of DCP, since all learnable parameters are absorbed into pre-trained weights at inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoRA outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and missing-ratio setting. Against the strongest missing-modality baseline DCP (Hu et al., NeurIPS 2024), MoRA gains +3.12, +1.42, and +6.34 average points on MM-IMDb, Food101, and Hateful Memes at 50% missing ratio, with the gap widening at 90% missing (e.g., +4.32 on Hateful Memes). Prompt-based methods CoOp (Zhou, IJCV 2022), MMP (Lee, CVPR 2023), MaPLe (Khattak, CVPR 2023), and DePT (Zhang, CVPR 2024) all trail MoRA by 3–8 points on average, and additionally incur inference overhead that MoRA avoids entirely. Among PEFT baselines (Table 4), MoRA beats LoRA (Hu, ICLR 2022) by +1.62 average, DoRA (Liu, ICML 2024) by +1.08, and BitFit (Zaken, ACL 2022) by +4.40, while full fine-tuning (FFT) catastrophically overfits (e.g., 3.01 on MM-IMDb vs. MoRA's 52.97). On the retrieval extension (Table 6), CLIP4CIR+MoRA achieves Recall@1 of 60.50 on MS-COCO versus 43.34 for the original CLIP4CIR (Baldrati, ACM TOMM 2024), a +17.16 gain, confirming transferability beyond classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoRA extends the LoRA/DoRA line of parameter-efficient fine-tuning by decomposing weight updates into magnitude and direction components, then introducing shared cross-modal parameters that enable bidirectional knowledge transfer between vision and text encoders. It departs from the dominant prompt-based missing-modality approaches (MMP, DCP, MaPLe, DePT) by operating entirely within the weight space, thereby eliminating the inference latency that prompts inherently introduce. The Gram-matrix formulation in rank space is a novel architectural device that resolves the dimension mismatch between encoders (e.g., 768-d ViT vs. 512-d BERT) without requiring projection layers, drawing on the modality-gap observation from Liang et al. (2022) as its core motivation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-inference-overhead property (all learnable parameters merge into frozen weights) combined with only 0.11% trainable parameters makes MoRA practically attractive for deploying VLMs in resource-constrained or latency-sensitive settings where prompt-based methods are prohibitive. However, the method is currently limited to two modalities (vision and text) and dual-encoder architectures; extending the Gram-matrix cross-modal mechanism to three or more modalities, to single-encoder or cross-attention architectures, and to generative tasks remains open. The consistently smaller gains when text is missing versus when image is missing suggest the bidirectional transfer is asymmetric in practice, an imbalance that future work could target.
