## Problem

Existing opponent shaping algorithms (LOLA, M-FOS, SHAPER) rely on higher-order derivatives, dual-agent architectures, or RNN-specific memory mechanisms that are incompatible with transformer-based LLM agents. It remains entirely unclear whether LLM agents can strategically influence co-players' learning dynamics through interaction alone, a critical question as LLM agents are increasingly deployed in multi-agent settings where they may be exploited or used to foster cooperation.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a single structured natural language prompt per round containing the game description with payoff matrix, the most recent joint action (intra-episode history), and cumulative state visitation counts across all prior episodes in the current trial (inter-episode context, e.g., 'CC:1, CD:1, DC:2, DD:3'). Generation is restricted to one token; any output not matching the two designated action tokens is mapped to a null action incurring a fixed penalty, and the transition is excluded from both players' histories. The only learnable parameters are rank-2 LoRA adapters on query/value projections and a value head, fine-tuned via QLoRA at 4-bit precision.

## Method

ShapeLLM adapts model-free opponent shaping (M-FOS/SHAPER) to transformers by replacing the RNN hidden state with structured natural language prompts that encode both history and context in a single information stream. The key architectural distinction is an asymmetric parameter update schedule: the shaper's LoRA weights are updated only at trial completion (after observing E opponent PPO updates across E episodes), while the opponent updates at every episode boundary, enabling the shaper to indirectly track and respond to the opponent's evolving policy. Training uses PPO with a KL penalty against the pre-trained distribution, and the shaper's value function must predict trial-level cumulative returns rather than episode-level returns, requiring a reduced value-function coefficient to avoid training instability. The framework is formalized as a POMDP over all agents' parameters and conditioning prompts, and is evaluated across five 2×2 repeated matrix games (IPD, IMP, ICG, ISH, C-IPD) using gemma-2-2b-it as the base model on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

In exploitative settings, the ShapeLLM shaper achieves average per-step rewards of 3.96 (vs. 1.00 baseline) in the IPD, 0.99 (vs. ~0.00 baseline) in the IMP, and 2.98 (vs. 2.00 baseline) in the ICG, while limiting the naive-learner opponent to 0.10, −0.99, and 1.01 respectively. In cooperative settings, the shaper guides both agents to the Pareto-optimal equilibrium in the ISH (3.96 each vs. 1.30 baseline) and achieves mutual cooperation in the C-IPD (5.88 for shaper, 2.86 for opponent vs. 1.00/1.00 baseline). Ablations confirm that inter-episode context (not merely enriched observations) is essential for shaping to succeed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against prior opponent-shaping algorithms (LOLA, M-FOS, SHAPER) because those methods rely on higher-order derivatives, RNN hidden states, or dual-agent architectures incompatible with transformers. Instead, the sole baseline is two independently trained naive learners (PPO + QLoRA on gemma-2-2b-it) that treat the opponent as a stationary environment component. The ShapeLLM shaper dominates this baseline in every game: in the IPD it lifts its own reward from 1.00 to 3.96 while suppressing the opponent below the mutual-defection payoff (0.10 vs. 1.00); in the IMP it converts a near-zero-sum oscillation into near-perfect exploitation (0.99 vs. −0.99); and in the ICG it forces convergence to its preferred Nash equilibrium (2.98 vs. 1.01). In cooperative settings the shaper resolves coordination failures that the baseline cannot: ISH baseline runs converge 90% of the time to the Pareto-inferior Hare–Hare equilibrium (1.30 each), whereas the shaper drives both agents to Stag–Stag (3.96 each). Ablation experiments (Appendix A.4, A.8) further show that simply giving one naive learner enriched intra-episode observations does not reproduce the shaping effect, confirming that the inter-episode context and asymmetric trial-level updates are the operative mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts model-free opponent shaping from MARL to transformer-based LLM agents, drawing on the meta-learning formulation of M-FOS (Lu et al., 2022) and the single-network history/context separation of SHAPER (Khan et al., 2024). The key architectural departure is replacing SHAPER's RNN hidden state (which encodes inter-episode context) with structured natural language prompts containing cumulative state visitation counts, and replacing M-FOS's dual-agent bi-level architecture with a single transformer whose parameters are updated only at trial boundaries. The paper explicitly positions against LOLA and its variants, which require higher-order derivatives and knowledge of the opponent's learning rule—components absent or intractable in transformer architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that LLM agents can both shape and be shaped through interaction alone, with direct safety implications as LLM agents are increasingly deployed in multi-agent settings where they may be vulnerable to strategic exploitation by adversaries. The dual finding—that the same mechanism can be harnessed to resolve coordination failures (ISH, C-IPD) and to extract unilateral advantage (IPD, IMP, ICG)—frames opponent shaping as a critical dimension of multi-agent LLM research. Key open questions include whether shaping generalizes to larger models, whether LLM shapers can influence non-LLM agents, and how natural language communication (beyond fixed action tokens) would alter shaping dynamics.
