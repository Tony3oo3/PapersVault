## Problem

Existing diffusion-based ultra-low bitrate image compression methods suffer from three critical limitations: they require separate models per bitrate (limiting adaptability in dynamic transmission), incur high encoding/decoding complexity from iterative denoising, and some progressive variants (e.g., DiffC) require infinite shared randomness between sender and receiver. The potential of Visual Autoregressive (VAR) models, with their natural coarse-to-fine next-scale prediction, for progressive image compression had remained unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 1024×1024 RGB image and a BLIP-2-generated text caption as input, and produces a variable-length compressed bitstream consisting of arithmetically coded binary token indices from the first k of K=13 multi-scale residual maps. The receiver reconstructs the image by losslessly decoding the k transmitted scales and autoregressively generating the remaining K−k scales, with the choice of k directly controlling the bitrate (ranging from ~7×10⁻⁶ bpp at k=1 to ~0.108 bpp at k=13). The text caption is transmitted alongside the bitstream as global semantic context for the generative reconstruction.

## Method

ARPC encodes images into K=13 hierarchical scale tokens using a group-masked bitwise multi-scale residual quantizer (GM-BMSRQ), which adaptively allocates fewer channels (8, 12, 16) to three groups of scales (4, 5, 4) based on their information density, and applies scale random dropout during training to push semantic content into earlier scales. Only the first k scales are transmitted; the remaining K−k scales are generated autoregressively by a fine-tuned Infinity-2B VAR model, making the decompression process identical to VAR's native generation. The VAR simultaneously serves as a probability estimator for arithmetic coding, enabling lossless re-encoding of the transmitted token indices and reducing bitrate by ~30% without quality loss. A theoretical bound (Theorem 3.1) decomposes reconstruction distortion into a VAE reconstruction term and a KL-divergence term between true and predicted scale-token distributions, guiding the two-stage training: first optimizing the VAE encoder/decoder/quantizer, then fine-tuning the VAR to minimize the distribution mismatch.

## Results

On CLIC2020 (1024×1024), ARPC achieves 0% BD-rate (as reference) on FID, DISTS, and PIEAPP, while the closest competitor StableCodec requires 0.15% more bits for FID but 6.99% more for DISTS and 254.42% more for PIEAPP. ARPC decodes in 5.39 s, which is 2–6× faster than DiffC (13.63–37.25 s) and 2× faster than PerCo (10.25 s). Removing lossless re-encoding (LRE) increases BD-rate by ~34% across all three metrics, confirming the importance of VAR-based arithmetic coding. On DIV2K and CLIC2020, ARPC consistently outperforms all 13 baselines across LPIPS, DISTS, PIEAPP, CLIP Score, FID, and KID at ultra-low bitrates (<0.01 bpp).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARPC outperforms all 13 baselines on FID and KID across DIV2K and CLIC2020, with the largest margins over token-based methods (PerCo: +1167% BD-rate on FID; VQGAN, GLC, DLF) and diffusion-based methods (DiffEIC: +682% FID; RDEIC: +524% FID). The closest competitor is StableCodec (one-step diffusion), which is nearly matched on FID (+0.15% BD-rate) but trails ARPC by 6.99% on DISTS and 254.42% on PIEAPP, indicating ARPC preserves perceptual and human-preference quality better. Against the progressive diffusion codec DiffC, ARPC wins on all three BD-rate metrics (FID +674%, DISTS +60%, PIEAPP +117%) while also eliminating the need for shared randomness and offering deterministic decoding time (5.39 s vs. DiffC's variable 13.63–37.25 s). DiffPC, the strongest non-progressive diffusion baseline, still requires 93.9% more bits than ARPC for equivalent FID. On the Kodak dataset (Appendix A.4), ARPC maintains its lead, and at the highest bitrate (all K scales transmitted), ARPC shows a slight drop in PSNR/MS-SSIM relative to DiffPC due to the discrete VAE's lower reconstruction ceiling, yet still achieves SOTA FID and KID.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Visual Autoregressive (VAR) model's next-scale prediction paradigm—originally designed for image generation—as the backbone of a progressive image compression framework. It departs from the dominant diffusion-based compression line (DiffC, DiffPC, StableCodec, OSCAR) by replacing iterative denoising with autoregressive scale-by-scale generation, eliminating the need for shared randomness and multi-step inference. The bitwise multi-scale residual quantizer is inherited from Infinity, and the group-masked variant (GM-BMSRQ) is a novel extension inspired by product quantization that adaptively allocates fewer bits to coarse scales. The use of VAR simultaneously as a generative model for missing scales and as a probability estimator for arithmetic coding is the paper's key architectural unification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ARPC addresses three concrete limitations of diffusion-based ultra-low bitrate compression—fixed-rate training, high decoding latency, and the shared-randomness requirement of reverse channel coding—within a single model that supports arbitrary bitrates via scale selection. The 2–6× decoding speedup over diffusion baselines while matching or exceeding perceptual quality makes this practically relevant for bandwidth-constrained or latency-sensitive deployment. Open questions include the growing encoding time at higher bitrates (up to 6.2 s for 1024×1024), the dependence on a 2.2B-parameter VAR at the sender for lossless re-encoding (mitigated but not eliminated by the lightweight w/o-LRE variant), and whether the approach extends naturally to video or higher resolutions beyond 1024×1024.
