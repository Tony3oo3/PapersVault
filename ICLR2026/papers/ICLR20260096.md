## Problem

Continuous-time consistency models (sCM) are theoretically elegant for few-step diffusion sampling but have never been validated at application scale (10B+ parameters, video tasks) due to infrastructure barriers in Jacobian-vector product computation under FlashAttention, FSDP, and context parallelism. Moreover, sCM's forward-divergence objective exhibits error accumulation that degrades fine-detail quality in text-to-image and text-to-video generation, a limitation invisible on weakly-conditioned ImageNet benchmarks.

## Input / Output

The distilled models accept text prompts (and optionally a reference image for I2V) and produce images at 1360×768 (Cosmos-Predict2) or 1024×1024 (SDXL), and videos at 832×480×81 frames (Wan2.1) or 1280×704×93 frames (Cosmos 720p). Generation requires only 1–4 function evaluations versus 35–50 for the teacher, yielding 15×–50× speedup. Internally, the student operates in VAE latent space under a TrigFlow noise schedule, with the teacher's original schedule (e.g., rectified flow) mapped analytically via signal-to-noise ratio matching.

## Method

rCM combines two complementary supervision signals: (1) the sCM consistency loss, a forward-divergence objective that enforces instantaneous self-consistency along the teacher ODE via a JVP-computed time derivative, and (2) a DMD score-distillation loss on self-generated (on-policy) samples that acts as a reverse-divergence long-skip regularizer, with a fixed balancing weight λ=0.01. The key infrastructure contribution is a Triton-based FlashAttention-2 JVP kernel that integrates forward-mode AD into the block-wise tiling of attention, made compatible with FSDP (by exposing per-layer tangent interfaces) and Ulysses context parallelism (by distributing QKV tangents alongside primals). For training stability at 10B+ scale, the paper introduces semi-continuous time (finite-difference approximation of ∂t F) for smaller models and FP32 time-embedding layers for larger ones, preventing JVP-induced collapse. The rollout strategy randomly samples the number of simulation steps and draws timesteps stochastically from a distribution, ensuring the student explores the full time range rather than fixed checkpoints.

## Results

On GenEval (T2I), Cosmos-Predict2 14B + rCM achieves an overall score of 0.83 in 4 steps (0.82 in 1 step), closely matching the 35-step teacher (0.84) and surpassing all other few-step models including FLUX.1-schnell (0.69) and SANA-Sprint (0.77). On VBench (T2V), Wan2.1 14B + rCM reaches a total score of 84.92 (4-step) and 85.05 (2-step), exceeding the 50-step teacher (83.58) and DMD2 on the 1.3B model (84.56). The distilled models deliver 15×–50× inference speedup, with the 14B video model achieving 4.5 FPS (4-step) versus 0.18 FPS for the teacher.

## Baselines & Comparisons

rCM matches or slightly exceeds DMD2 on quality metrics: on Cosmos-Predict2 2B (4-step), rCM scores 0.81 vs. DMD2's 0.80 on GenEval overall, and on Wan2.1 1.3B (4-step), rCM achieves 84.43 vs. DMD2's 84.56 on VBench total (nearly tied) while scoring higher on semantic alignment (80.63 vs. 80.50). The paper's key qualitative finding is that rCM preserves the generation diversity of sCM (forward divergence) while DMD2 exhibits mode collapse with objects converging to similar positions and orientations. Against other few-step baselines, rCM substantially outperforms SDXL-LCM (0.50), SDXL-Lightning (0.53), and Hyper-SDXL (0.58) on GenEval, and even surpasses the 12B FLUX.1-schnell (0.69) by a wide margin. On the 14B Cosmos model, rCM at 1 step (0.82) nearly matches the 35-step teacher (0.84), demonstrating that the score-regularized consistency objective is robust to extreme step reduction. For video, rCM on Wan2.1 14B at 2 steps (85.05 VBench total) actually exceeds the 50-step teacher (83.58), while DMD2 was only evaluated at 1.3B scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends continuous-time consistency models (sCM, Lu & Song 2024) by identifying their quality limitations at scale—attributed to error accumulation through JVP self-feedback and the mode-covering nature of forward divergence—and remedies them by integrating distribution matching distillation (DMD, Yin et al. 2024) as a long-skip regularizer. The resulting rCM framework unifies forward-divergence (consistency) and reverse-divergence (score) objectives, echoing a broader philosophy seen in DDO, DDRL, and DiffusionNFT. On the infrastructure side, the paper is the first to make JVP-based distillation compatible with FlashAttention-2, FSDP, and context parallelism, enabling sCM training on 10B+ parameter models and high-dimensional video data—something prior sCM work (limited to 1.5B ImageNet) could not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

rCM provides a practical, theoretically grounded path to 15–50× diffusion sampling acceleration on application-level models (up to 14B params, 5-second video) without GAN tuning or multi-stage training, matching DMD2 quality while offering clear diversity advantages. The clean forward+reverse divergence framing suggests a unifying paradigm for future distillation and post-training work. Open questions include whether the approach extends to autoregressive video diffusion (the paper hints at complementing Self-Forcing with teacher-forced forward divergence), whether the λ=0.01 sweet spot holds for even larger or differently-architected models, and whether the JVP numerical fragility under BF16 can be further mitigated for future hardware.
