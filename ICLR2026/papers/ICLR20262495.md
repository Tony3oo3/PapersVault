## Problem

Multi-view diffusion models for single-view 3D human reconstruction produce unnatural postures on dynamic or acrobatic poses, a limitation the authors attribute to the small scale and narrow pose distribution of publicly available 3D human scan datasets. The paper addresses this by enabling post-training of the diffusion model on diverse poses without requiring expensive multi-view 3D human assets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262495/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a segmented single-view RGB human image as input and produces a textured 3D human mesh via explicit carving. Internally, the I2MV diffusion model generates six 768×768 normal maps and RGB images (for the PSHuman base), which are then lifted to 3D through SMPL-X initialization, differentiable remeshing, and appearance fusion. During training, each input image is paired with a ground-truth SMPL-X pose parameter θ used to compute the differentiable PoseScore reward.

## Method

DrPose post-trains an I2MV diffusion model by maximizing PoseScore, a differentiable reward that measures consistency between generated multi-view latent images and a ground-truth 3D pose. PoseScore works by predicting per-joint skeletal images from the latent via a pretrained U-Net (g_skel) and comparing them to skeletal images rendered by projecting the 3D joints J(θ) into the corresponding viewpoints, using L1 distance as the reward. Training follows the DRTune paradigm: a random subset of denoising steps is sampled for gradient computation, and a KL-divergence regularization term against a frozen reference U-Net prevents reward hacking. The method is trained on DrPose15K, a dataset of 15K pose-image pairs constructed by sampling 1.5K diverse poses from Motion-X (AIST subset) via farthest-point sampling, extending each to a 10-frame sequence, and generating single-view images with the MIMO pose-conditioned video model. The approach is applied as a drop-in post-training step to both PSHuman and Era3D base models.

## Results

On the proposed MixamoRP benchmark (challenging dynamic poses), DrPose (PSHuman-based) achieves the best Chamfer Distance of 126.53 and f-Score of 8.82, outperforming the PSHuman baseline (CD 137.28, f-Score 8.21) and Era3D* (CD 150.01, f-Score 7.35). On THuman2.1-test, the method reduces CD from 52.96 (PSHuman) to 42.05 and improves f-Score from 18.63 to 19.68. For appearance, DrPose achieves the highest PSNR of 20.86 on THuman2.1-test (vs. 18.39 for PSHuman) and 17.66 on MixamoRP, with the lowest LPIPS of 0.1063 on THuman2.1-test.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DrPose (PSHuman-based) consistently outperforms all five baselines on geometry metrics across all three benchmarks. Against the strongest baseline PSHuman, DrPose reduces Chamfer Distance by ~10.9 on THuman2.1 (42.05 vs. 52.96), ~8.1 on CustomHumans (44.13 vs. 52.22), and ~10.8 on MixamoRP (126.53 vs. 137.28), while improving f-Score on all three. Against Era3D* (fine-tuned with standard DDPM loss), the gains are even larger: CD drops from 55.41 to 42.05 on THuman2.1 and from 150.01 to 126.53 on MixamoRP. On appearance metrics (Table 2), DrPose achieves the best PSNR on THuman2.1 (20.86 vs. 18.39 for PSHuman, +2.47 dB) and the lowest LPIPS (0.1063), while gains on CustomHumans and MixamoRP are more modest (PSNR 19.19 vs. 18.91 and 17.66 vs. 17.59 respectively). The largest relative improvement appears on MixamoRP, the benchmark specifically designed for extreme poses (breakdancing, bat swinging, acrobatic movements), confirming that DrPose's pose-alignment objective is most impactful where prior models struggle most.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the direct reward fine-tuning paradigm for diffusion models (DRTune, Wu et al. 2024) from text-to-image alignment to the specific problem of pose consistency in multi-view human reconstruction. It departs from prior single-view 3D human reconstruction methods (PIFu, ECON, SiTH, PSHuman, Era3D) that rely on limited 3D scan datasets by instead post-training an I2MV diffusion model on a synthetic dataset (DrPose15K) constructed from motion capture sequences and a pose-conditioned video generator (MIMO), thereby bypassing the need for expensive multi-view 3D human captures. The differentiable PoseScore reward, which compares predicted skeletal images against projected ground-truth joints, is a novel application of reward-based optimization to the 3D human domain, building on the broader trend of using differentiable rewards to steer diffusion model outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in 3D human reconstruction: multi-view diffusion models produce visibly unnatural postures on dynamic or acrobatic poses due to training data scarcity, and the proposed reward-based post-training offers a data-efficient fix that does not require new 3D scans. The MixamoRP benchmark fills a gap in evaluation by targeting extreme poses (breakdancing, bat swinging, crouched landings) absent from THuman2.1 and CustomHumans. However, the method still requires segmented input, struggles with fine details like hands, and incurs substantial GPU memory overhead from generating 24 high-resolution images per iteration; whether the reward-hacking mitigation via KL regularization scales to more aggressive pose distributions remains open.
