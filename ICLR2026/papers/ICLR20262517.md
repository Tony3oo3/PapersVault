## Problem

Mamba-based multimodal large language models reduce decoding cost via linear-time state space models, but the prefill stage remains dominated by redundant visual tokens that far outnumber text tokens. Existing visual token pruning methods rely on Transformer attention scores to rank token importance, making them inapplicable to Mamba architectures that lack explicit self-attention. This paper addresses the gap of how to derive a principled, architecture-native token importance signal and determine optimal pruning locations for Mamba-based MLLMs.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard Mamba-based MLLM inference: an RGB image is encoded into a fixed set of visual tokens (729 for Cobra via DINOv2/SigLIP, 256 for RoboMamba via CLIP) concatenated with a text question, and the model produces a free-form text answer. Pruning is applied purely at inference time with no retraining or architectural modification; visual tokens are progressively removed at two specific Mamba layers while text tokens are always retained. The keep ratio (e.g., 0.5 or 0.9) controls how many visual tokens survive the early selective pruning stage.

## Method

DTP derives token importance directly from Mamba's input-dependent parameter Δ_t, which controls the discretized state transition and input mapping in the selective SSM; tokens with larger Δ_t induce stronger hidden-state updates and are deemed more informative. The importance score for each token is the mean of Δ_t across its channel dimension, and a top-k selection retains the most important visual tokens. Pruning is applied at two stages: selective top-k pruning at an early layer (the layer with minimum standard deviation of importance scores within the first 25% of depth) and complete removal of all visual tokens at a late layer (the layer with maximum inter-layer change in standard deviation within the final 30% of depth). Layer selection is further validated by analyzing Mamba's implicit attention matrices, which show coherent token-token interaction patterns at the chosen early layer and a collapse of structure at the late layer, confirming that partial pruning becomes unreliable beyond that point.

## Results

On the Cobra model at ~50% FLOPs, DTP (r=0.5) achieves an average score of 64.9 across six benchmarks, compared to 64.7 (FastV), 54.0 (VTW), and 64.3 (DART), while reducing FLOPs from 2.01T to 0.97T. At the ~70% FLOPs level, DTP (r=0.9) exactly matches the unpruned baseline average of 65.8, outperforming FastV (65.3), VTW (65.7), and DART (65.2). On RoboMamba at ~50% FLOPs, DTP achieves 65.9 average versus 65.6 (FastV), 53.4 (VTW), and 65.4 (DART). In wall-clock terms, DTP reduces mean prefill latency by 37% (98.04 ms → 61.54 ms) and total inference time from 16 min 05 s to 10 min 35 s on the POPE benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTP outperforms all three baselines at both FLOPs reduction levels on Cobra. At ~50% FLOPs, DTP (64.9 avg) beats FastV (64.7) by 0.2, DART (64.3) by 0.6, and VTW (54.0) by a dramatic 10.9 points; VTW's collapse is due to its single-layer withdrawal strategy being ill-suited to aggressive pruning. At the milder ~70% FLOPs level, DTP exactly matches the unpruned baseline (65.8) while FastV drops 0.5, DART drops 0.6, and VTW drops only 0.1. On RoboMamba at ~50% FLOPs, DTP (65.9) again leads FastV (65.6) by 0.3 and DART (65.4) by 0.5, while VTW suffers a 13.6-point average drop. The consistent advantage of DTP stems from its two-stage design: selective top-k pruning at an early layer (k=15) preserves discriminative visual tokens, while complete pruning at a late layer (k=45) removes tokens that no longer carry useful signal, a strategy that neither single-layer FastV/VTW nor similarity-based DART replicates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual token pruning paradigm from Transformer-based MLLMs (FastV, VTW, DART) to Mamba-based MLLMs by replacing attention-score-based importance with the input-dependent parameter Δ_t from Mamba's selective SSM. It builds directly on the implicit attention matrix formulation of Ali et al. (2024) to analyze token-token interaction patterns across layers, which informs the two-stage pruning design (selective pruning at an early layer, complete pruning at a late layer). Prior Mamba token-reduction work (Zhan et al. 2024, Shen et al. 2024) addressed unimodal vision tasks only; this is the first method targeting multimodal MLLMs specifically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DTP provides the first training-free, architecture-native pruning strategy for Mamba-based MLLMs, achieving ~50% FLOPs reduction and 37% prefill latency reduction with minimal accuracy loss, which is practically significant as prefill dominates inference cost in multimodal settings. The analysis of Δ_t distributions and implicit attention patterns across layers offers new interpretive insight into how visual tokens propagate through SSM layers, a dimension previously unexplored. Limitations include evaluation on only two 64-layer models (Cobra, RoboMamba) and a preliminary analysis step (sampling 50 examples) needed to select pruning layers, raising open questions about generalization to other SSM variants (Mamba-2, hybrid architectures) and to models with different depths or token counts.
