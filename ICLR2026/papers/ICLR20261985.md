## Problem

Existing proof autoformalization methods translate natural language proofs into formal code using low-level tactics, which frequently fail to preserve the logical structure and step-by-step reasoning of the original human argument. Prior approaches either process the entire proof monolithically or handle steps independently with all preceding steps as premises, both of which allow the prover to take logical shortcuts that diverge from the author's intended proof path. Additionally, no existing evaluation metric simultaneously captures structural fidelity, semantic faithfulness, and syntactic correctness of a formalized proof.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a natural language theorem statement and its accompanying proof as input. It produces a dependency DAG whose nodes are partitioned into Theorem Conditions, Definitions, Lemmas, and Theorem Solutions, with each node carrying a self-contained NL statement and explicit prerequisite edges. The final output is structured Lean 4 code where each proof step is a named lemma with its dependencies declared in the header, completed with tactics, accompanied by an interactive graph visualization and a per-node PROOFSCORE.

## Method

PROOFFLOW is a three-stage LLM pipeline: (1) a Graph Builder parses the NL proof into a DAG G=(V,E) where each edge encodes a prerequisite relationship, validated for acyclicity and completeness; (2) a Formalizer translates each node's self-contained NL statement into Lean 4 lemma code with a 'by sorry' placeholder, iteratively correcting compilation errors; (3) a Tactic Completer replaces placeholders with valid Lean 4 tactics. The key architectural choice is the lemma-based approach—each step becomes a standalone lemma whose header explicitly lists only its DAG-specified dependencies—rather than a flat tactic sequence, which prevents the prover from using unintended premises or skipping intermediate steps. For evaluation, PROOFSCORE is defined as the mean over all nodes of the product of semantic faithfulness (via an adapted LeanScore using component-wise LLM-as-judge and Sugeno Integral aggregation), syntactic correctness (binary Lean compilation check), and structural fidelity (DAG dependency match against ground truth), yielding a single [0,1] score.

## Results

On the 184-problem PROOFFLOWBENCH benchmark (Pass@5, thinking mode), PROOFFLOW DAG achieves a PROOFSCORE of 0.545, nearly doubling the FULL PROOF baseline (0.279) and substantially exceeding STEP PROOF (0.029). At the step level, PROOFFLOW DAG reaches 0.939 formalizer accuracy and 0.742 tactic accuracy, with 0.375 proof-level correct syntax. On the easier miniF2F benchmark (50 problems), PROOFFLOW DAG (thinking) improves to a PROOFSCORE of 0.63 and 0.58 correct syntax, confirming the relative ordering is preserved.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PROOFFLOW DAG (thinking mode) achieves a PROOFSCORE of 0.545, nearly 2× the FULL PROOF baseline (0.279) and ~19× the STEP PROOF baseline (0.029). Interestingly, FULL PROOF attains higher proof-level correct syntax (0.571 vs. 0.375), but its generated proofs frequently fail to preserve the logical structure and semantic intent of the original natural language argument, which PROOFSCORE penalizes. STEP PROOF performs worst across all metrics, with only 0.119 proof-level correct syntax in thinking mode, largely because the LLM struggles to maintain consistent Lean 4 indentation across independently formalized steps, causing cascading failures. The DAG ablation (PROOFFLOW noDAG, 0.417 PROOFSCORE) confirms that explicit dependency enforcement contributes a meaningful ~13-point gain in structural fidelity over providing all prior lemmas as context. On the easier miniF2F benchmark, the same ordering holds: PROOFFLOW DAG (0.63) > FULL PROOF (0.38) > STEP PROOF (0.01) in PROOFSCORE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant tactic-based autoformalization paradigm (Lu et al. 2024, Hu et al. 2025) by introducing a lemma-based approach that explicitly models proof dependencies as a DAG, rather than treating the proof as a monolithic block or a linear sequence where all prior steps are available as premises. It adapts the LeanScore semantic faithfulness metric from Yu et al. (2025a, Mathesis) and extends it into a composite PROOFSCORE that also captures structural fidelity. The pipeline leverages recent LLM-based provers (Goedel-Prover-V2, DeepSeek-Prover-V2) as components but restructures their usage around a graph-driven workflow rather than end-to-end generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in the formal mathematics ecosystem: existing autoformalizers produce syntactically valid but structurally unfaithful proofs, making it impossible to verify that the formal code mirrors the mathematician's intended reasoning. The 37.5% proof-level syntactic accuracy (vs. 6.10% from prior step-level work) and the error-detection capability that flags ambiguities in the original NL proof are practically significant for mathematicians. Key limitations include the restriction to undergraduate-level proofs, the Formalizer stage remaining the primary bottleneck (32–47% of failures are semantic), and the lack of parallelization despite the DAG architecture naturally supporting it. Open questions include whether the approach scales to research-level proofs and whether the semantic preservation gap can be closed through better formalizer models or training data curation.
