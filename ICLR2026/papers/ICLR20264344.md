## Problem

When the objective in Bayesian Optimization is invariant under a group action, the standard orbit-averaged kernel (k_avg) dilutes the similarity signal by averaging over all group transformations, often underperforming even the non-invariant base kernel for large or continuous groups. The max-alignment kernel (k_max), which retains only the best alignment over orbits and captures the correct invariance geometry, has been unusable in BO because it is not positive semidefinite. This paper closes that gap by constructing a valid PSD surrogate of k_max that preserves its geometric advantages.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a user-specified base kernel (RBF or Matérn-5/2), a symmetry group G (finite permutations/sign-flips or continuous rotations/rescalings), and the running design set D of all past query points. At each BO iteration it produces a PSD, G-invariant kernel k_+^(D) defined on the full search space via a Nyström extension of the PSD-projected Gram matrix, which is then plugged into standard GP-UCB to yield posterior mean, posterior covariance, and the next acquisition-maximizing query. The per-iteration asymptotic cost matches that of orbit-averaged kernels (O((mn+n²)|G|*+n³)), with the only extra overhead being the SVD-based PSD projection.

## Method

The core construction defines k_max(x,x') = max_{g,g'∈G} k_b(gx, g'x'), showing it is the exact covariance of a natural class of G-invariant GPs (Proposition 1) and that k_avg can never reproduce its geometry except in degenerate cases (Lemma 2). To make k_max usable in GP-BO, the paper projects its Gram matrix on the current design set D onto the PSD cone via eigenvalue clipping (K_+ = Q max(0,Λ) Q^⊤) and extends to new points through a Nyström formula k_+^(D)(x,x') = k_max(x,D) K_+^† k_max(D,x'), yielding a kernel that is PSD, G-invariant, coincides with k_max on D when k_max is already PSD, and has the same asymptotic per-iteration cost as k_avg. Empirically, k_+^(D) achieves up to 50% lower cumulative regret than k_avg across synthetic and real-world benchmarks, with gains growing as |G| increases, while k_avg can even underperform the non-invariant base kernel for continuous groups. A spectral analysis reveals that k_avg often has faster eigendecay than k_+^(D), contradicting standard eigendecay-based regret theory and pointing to geometric alignment of kernel eigenvectors and RKHS approximation hardness as the true drivers of the performance gap.

## Results

Across all seven benchmarks, k_+^(D) achieves the best cumulative regret (synthetic) or negated simple reward (real-world) in every case. The largest gains appear at large group sizes: on Rastrigin5d (|G|=3,840), k_+^(D) achieves 813.4±70.6 cumulative regret versus 1,583.5±341.9 for k_avg (49% lower) and 3,568.5±91.3 for k_b. On the continuous-group Scaling2d benchmark, k_+^(D) reaches 25.4±6.4 while k_avg degrades to 3,361.8±742.9 (worse than k_b's 1,820.6). On real-world tasks, k_+^(D) finds the best parameter configurations: −74.4±0.7 on WLAN8d and −0.92±0.10 on PartPack6d, outperforming both baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares k_+^(D) against two baselines: the non-invariant base kernel k_b (no symmetry handling) and the orbit-averaged kernel k_avg (Brown et al., NeurIPS 2024). k_+^(D) wins on all seven benchmarks. Against k_avg, the gap widens with group size: at |G|=8 (Ackley2d) the two are essentially tied (126.4 vs 128.2), but at |G|=3,840 (Rastrigin5d) k_+^(D) achieves 49% lower regret (813.4 vs 1,583.5), and on continuous groups k_avg actually underperforms k_b (e.g., 3,361.8 vs 1,820.6 on Scaling2d) while k_+^(D) remains far ahead. Against k_b, k_+^(D) consistently dominates, with the most dramatic improvement on Scaling2d (25.4 vs 1,820.6, a ~70× reduction). The key finding is that k_avg's performance degrades as |G| grows due to dilution of informative similarities across many group elements, whereas k_+^(D) preserves high-contrast orbit alignments and remains stable regardless of group size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard orbit-averaging construction for invariant GP kernels (Kondor 2008; Brown et al. 2024) by replacing the average over group actions with a maximum (best-alignment), drawing on max-similarity kernels from structured-data learning (Gärtner 2003; Vishwanathan 2003). Since the max kernel is not PSD, the authors introduce a Frobenius-nearest PSD projection combined with a Nyström extension to obtain a valid GP kernel, borrowing techniques from indefinite-kernel SVMs (Luss & D'Aspremont 2007) and kernel approximation (Williams & Seeger 2000). The construction is positioned as a drop-in replacement for k_avg in the invariant-kernel BO framework of Brown et al. (2024), with matching asymptotic per-iteration cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals a concrete mismatch between standard eigendecay-based regret theory and empirical BO performance: k_avg often decays faster spectrally yet achieves higher regret, suggesting that geometric alignment of kernel eigenvectors with optimization-relevant directions and RKHS approximation hardness matter beyond pure spectral rates. This opens a theoretical gap—developing regret bounds that capture approximation difficulty rather than mere RKHS membership—that the authors flag as essential future work. Practically, the method is a simple, low-overhead modification (one SVD + Nyström) that yields up to 50% regret reduction on symmetric benchmarks, making it immediately adoptable for BO practitioners with known invariances.
