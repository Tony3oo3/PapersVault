## Problem

Extending zero-shot image inverse solvers based on latent diffusion models to high-definition video restoration is challenging: applying image priors frame-by-frame produces temporally inconsistent reconstructions with flickering and incoherent dynamics, while using video diffusion models directly requires backpropagation through the model, incurring prohibitive memory costs. No prior method combines the temporal coherence of video generative models with the computational efficiency of few-step consistency models in a gradient-free, plug-and-play video restoration framework.

## Input / Output

LVTINO takes a degraded video (e.g., temporally downsampled by ×4 or ×8, spatially downsampled by ×4 or ×8, or motion-blurred), a known linear or non-linear degradation operator A, and a generic text prompt, and produces a restored high-definition video at 1280×768 resolution over 25 frames. The method is fully zero-shot: no task-specific training is required, and the degradation operator only needs forward and adjoint evaluations. For the hardest problem (temporal SR×8 + spatial SR×8), a warm-start from a pre-trained interpolation network reduces the required NFEs from 9 to 7.

## Method

LVTINO is a zero-shot Langevin posterior sampler that draws samples from p(x|y,c,λ) using a product-of-experts prior combining a Video Consistency Model (CausVid/Wan, distilled via DMD) for spatiotemporal coherence, a frame-wise Image Consistency Model (DMD2/SDXL) for fine spatial detail, and a 3D total-variation regularizer for smooth temporal transitions. Each iteration of the splitting scheme (Eq. 7) alternates between two stochastic auto-encoding (SAE) steps — one contracting toward the VCM prior and one toward the ICM prior — and two implicit Euler (proximal) likelihood half-steps that enforce measurement consistency. The VCM SAE step encodes the current iterate into the video latent space, adds noise at a scheduled timestep, applies the consistency function, and decodes back; the ICM step does the same frame-by-frame. The proximal steps are solved without automatic differentiation: the quadratic data-fidelity term uses warm-started conjugate gradient (~10 iterations), while the TV-regularized variant uses Chambolle-Pock (PDHG) or Adam. The non-smooth TV term is handled via Moreau-Yosida regularization, ensuring log-concavity and Lipschitz differentiability of the smoothed factor. The full pipeline requires only 9 NFEs (5 VCM + 4 ICM) and no backpropagation through the generative models, achieving state-of-the-art FVMD and LPIPS scores with significantly lower memory than gradient-based video diffusion posterior sampling.

## Results

On Adobe240, LVTINO achieves the best FVMD (temporal consistency) across all three problems: 371.1 (Problem A), 42.65 (Problem B), and 602.5 (Problem C), substantially outperforming VISION-XL (1141, 82.92, 1604 respectively). LVTINO also leads in LPIPS (perceptual quality) on all problems (0.249, 0.370, 0.411 vs. 0.439, 0.468, 0.520 for VISION-XL) and in PSNR on Problem A (27.25 dB vs. 26.03 dB). On GoPro240, LVTINO similarly dominates FVMD (189.4, 46.20, 232.6) and LPIPS, while VISION-XL retains a slight PSNR edge on Problems B and C. The method requires only 9 NFEs (7 for Problem C with warm-starting), comparable to VISION-XL's 8 NFEs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VISION-XL (Kwon & Ye, 2025b), the primary SOTA zero-shot video inverse solver using image LDMs frame-by-frame, LVTINO achieves substantially lower FVMD (3–3× better temporal consistency) and lower LPIPS (15–20% better perceptual quality) across all three problems on both Adobe240 and GoPro240. LVTINO also wins on PSNR for Problem A (27.25 vs. 26.03 dB on Adobe240) and SSIM for Problems A and C, but VISION-XL retains a modest PSNR advantage on Problems B (26.18 vs. 24.91 dB) and C (23.38 vs. 23.11 dB), likely due to its stronger per-frame spatial detail from the image diffusion prior. Against VIDUE (Shang et al., 2023), a task-specific trained network for joint deblurring and interpolation, LVTINO achieves higher PSNR (23.11 vs. 21.78 dB) and SSIM (0.697 vs. 0.624) on Problem C while using only 7 NFEs versus VIDUE's single forward pass; however, VIDUE reports a lower FVMD (142.5 vs. 602.5) because it operates natively at lower resolution and does not perform super-resolution. Against the classical ADMM-TV optimization baseline, LVTINO dominates on all perceptual and fidelity metrics (e.g., 27.25 vs. 18.04 dB PSNR on Problem A), confirming the advantage of generative priors over hand-crafted regularizers for high-definition video restoration.

## Relation to Prior Work

LVTINO extends the LATINO framework (Spagnoletti et al., 2025), which embeds Latent Consistency Models as priors in a Langevin diffusion for image inverse problems, to the video domain by introducing a product-of-experts prior combining a Video Consistency Model (CausVid/Wan) for temporal causality, a frame-wise Image Consistency Model (DMD2) for spatial detail, and a 3D total-variation regularizer. The key conceptual departure from prior video restoration methods like VISION-XL (Kwon & Ye, 2025b) is the use of a video-native CM that jointly models inter-frame dependencies, rather than applying image LDMs frame-by-frame with external temporal constraints. The gradient-free SAE + proximal splitting conditioning mechanism avoids the prohibitive memory cost of backpropagating through video diffusion transformers (as required by DPS-style methods like VDPS), enabling scalability to high-resolution sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first zero-shot inverse solver for HD video restoration that leverages VCMs as Bayesian priors, achieving strong temporal coherence (FVMD) and spatial quality with only 9 NFEs and no automatic differentiation. The practical impact is significant for settings where memory-constrained GPUs cannot support gradient-based video diffusion guidance, and the modular design (VCM + ICM + TV) allows practitioners to trade off quality and speed (e.g., LVTINO-V with 5 NFEs). Open questions include extending to long videos via autoregressive strategies (where generative drift remains a challenge), incorporating prompt optimization, and whether the VCM prior can eventually replace the ICM entirely to simplify the pipeline.
