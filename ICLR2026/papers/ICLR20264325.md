## Problem

While near-optimal sample complexity bounds exist for unconstrained average-reward MDPs and for discounted constrained MDPs, no such bounds were known for constrained average-reward MDPs (CAMDPs), where policies must maximize long-run average reward while satisfying an average cost constraint. This paper closes that gap by establishing the first minimax-optimal sample complexity characterization for CAMDPs under both relaxed and strict feasibility regimes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264325/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm queries a generative model (simulator) that returns i.i.d. next-state samples for any state-action pair (s,a), with known reward r and constraint c functions bounded in [0,1]. It requires the Slater constant ζ (feasibility margin) and a target accuracy ε, and outputs a stationary mixture policy π̂ that is ε-optimal in reward while either violating the constraint by at most ε (relaxed) or satisfying it exactly (strict). The sample complexity scales as Õ(SA(B+H)/ε²) for relaxed feasibility and Õ(SA(B+H)/(ε²ζ²)) for strict feasibility, where H is the bias span and B is the transient time bound.

## Method

The algorithm is a model-based primal-dual procedure that reduces the average-reward problem to a sequence of discounted MDPs by setting γ = 1 − ε_opt/(4(B+H)) and adding uniform reward perturbations of magnitude ω to break ties. At each iteration, the primal step solves an unconstrained AMDP with combined reward r_p + λ_t·c using a black-box planner (e.g., policy iteration), while the dual step performs projected gradient descent on the Lagrange multiplier over an ε_1-resolution epsilon-net within [0, U]. The final policy is the uniform mixture over all T primal iterates. A key technical ingredient is bounding the optimal dual variable |λ*| via the Slater constant ζ (for strict feasibility) or the constraint slack (for relaxed feasibility), which controls the number of primal-dual iterations and the required sample size per state-action pair. Matching lower bounds are established via Fano's inequality on carefully constructed hard CAMDP instances with tree-structured state spaces.

## Results

The paper establishes the first near-minimax-optimal sample complexity bounds for learning ε-optimal policies in constrained average-reward MDPs (CAMDPs) under a generative model. Under relaxed feasibility (allowing constraint violation up to ε), the algorithm requires Õ(SA(B+H)/ε²) samples. Under strict feasibility (zero constraint violation), the sample complexity increases to Õ(SA(B+H)/(ε²ζ²)), where ζ is the Slater constant measuring the feasibility margin. A matching lower bound of Ω̃(SA(B+H)/(ε²ζ²)) is proven for the strict feasibility case, confirming minimax optimality in S, A, B, H, ε, and ζ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are against prior theoretical results. For unconstrained average-reward MDPs, Zurek & Chen (2024) established Õ(SA(B+H)/ε²) samples, and this paper shows that the relaxed-feasibility CAMDP bound matches that rate exactly, meaning the constraint adds no additional sample cost when small violations are permitted. For the strict feasibility regime, the paper introduces a necessary 1/ζ² dependence absent in the unconstrained setting, and proves via Fano's inequality that this dependence is information-theoretically unavoidable. The primal-dual algorithm reuses concentration lemmas from Vaswani et al. (2022) for discounted CMDPs, but the key technical contribution is extending the analysis to the average-reward setting where Bellman contraction fails, requiring the bias span H and transient time B parameters. The lower bound construction for general CAMDPs (Theorem 5) combines a diameter-based hard instance (analogous to Zurek & Chen's construction) with a transient-state amplification argument to capture the B-dependent term.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: the primal-dual framework for discounted constrained MDPs (Vaswani et al., 2022) and the span-based sample complexity analysis for unconstrained average-reward MDPs (Zurek & Chen, 2024). The key technical departure is that average-reward MDPs lack the Bellman contraction property that underpins all prior discounted CMDP analyses, forcing the authors to work with bias spans, transient times, and Blackwell-optimal policies instead. The primal-dual algorithm is adapted from Vaswani et al.'s dual-descent method, but the concentration analysis and the reduction from average-reward to discounted MDPs (via a carefully chosen γ) are new contributions specific to the average-reward setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish minimax-optimal sample complexity bounds for CAMDPs, resolving a gap that had persisted since the unconstrained average-reward case was settled. The provable separation between relaxed and strict feasibility (the additional ζ² factor) provides a fundamental characterization of how constraint tightness affects learning difficulty in long-horizon settings. Open questions include extending these bounds to model-free or online settings, handling multiple constraints simultaneously, and understanding whether the generative-model assumption can be relaxed to tabular or function-approximation settings. The results are purely theoretical with no empirical validation, limiting immediate practical impact.
