## Problem

Existing vertical federated learning (VFL) methods impose restrictive assumptions: they typically support only two parties, require fully aligned training data, or cannot exploit unlabeled samples. No prior framework simultaneously handles arbitrary data alignment (partially or fully unaligned samples), unlabeled data, and multi-party collaboration under diverse missingness mechanisms (MCAR, MAR, MNAR). This paper reinterprets VFL alignment gaps as a missing-data problem to unify these challenges.

## Input / Output

Each of K parties holds a local feature vector x_i^k ∈ R^{d_k} for sample i, along with a binary missingness indicator m_i^k; the active party additionally holds labels y_i that may themselves be absent (u_i=1). At inference time, any arbitrary subset of parties may be present, so the method must predict from whatever partial observation x_i^obs is available without requiring all K parties. The output is a class label (categorical) or continuous regression value, produced via self-normalized importance sampling over L latent samples.

## Method

FALSE-VFL builds a deep latent variable model where each party k has a local encoder γ_c^k and decoder θ_c^k, while the active party adds a global encoder γ_s, global decoder θ_s, and a discriminator φ. The variational posterior q(h|x^obs) aggregates party contributions through a precision-weighted combination: the mean is the average of observed-party encoder means and the covariance is the inverse of the sum of their precision matrices, so missing parties are implicitly treated as infinite-variance. Training is two-stage: (1) pretraining maximizes the marginal likelihood p(x^obs) over all data (labeled and unlabeled) using a κ-sample importance-weighted bound, learning a robust generative representation; (2) training freezes the generative parameters Θ_g and maximizes the conditional likelihood p(y|x^obs) on labeled data only, which is equivalent to maximizing the joint likelihood once Θ_g is fixed. This two-stage design avoids the implicit modeling bias where the high-dimensional marginal term would otherwise dominate the conditional signal. For MNAR missingness, an additional per-party mask model p_ψ(m^k|x^k) is introduced and jointly optimized in the pretraining stage.

## Results

Across 168 configurations (4 datasets × 6 training missingness mechanisms × 7 test missingness patterns), FALSE-VFL outperforms all baselines in 160 cases with an average accuracy gap of 9.6 percentage points over the next-best competitor. Per-dataset average gaps are 15.7% (Isolet), 7.3% (HAPT), 4.5% (FashionMNIST), and 9.1% (ModelNet10). The gap widens with test-time missingness: from 1.8 pp under fully aligned test data (MCAR 0) to 13.1 pp under the most severe MNAR 9 test condition. Under MCAR 2 training, FALSE-VFL-I achieves 86.3% on Isolet, 89.0% on HAPT, 82.9% on FashionMNIST, and 86.6% on ModelNet10 (MCAR 0 test), consistently leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FALSE-VFL dominates all three primary baselines across nearly every configuration. Against LASER-VFL (Valdeira et al., 2024), which handles unaligned data but ignores unlabeled samples, FALSE-VFL leads by 10–30 pp under high-missingness test conditions (e.g., 65.2% vs. 51.0% on Isolet MCAR 5 test; 78.5% vs. 68.6% on ModelNet10 MCAR 5 test). Against PlugVFL (Sun et al., 2024), which uses zero-imputation for missing parties, the gap is particularly stark under MNAR test conditions (e.g., 70.4% vs. 51.8% on HAPT MNAR 9; 78.9% vs. 51.1% on ModelNet10 MNAR 9). Against FedHSSL (He et al., 2024), the closest competitor under low-missingness settings, FALSE-VFL still leads by 2–6 pp (e.g., 82.9% vs. 78.5% on FashionMNIST MCAR 0; 89.0% vs. 85.3% on HAPT MCAR 0) and the gap expands to 10–12 pp under severe missingness (e.g., 70.4% vs. 59.0% on HAPT MNAR 9). The only near-tie is ModelNet10 under MCAR 0 test where FedHSSL scores 86.9% vs. FALSE-VFL-I's 86.6%, but FALSE-VFL-II recovers the lead at 86.3% and pulls ahead decisively as test missingness increases. Overall, FALSE-VFL wins 160 of 168 configurations, with the 8 losses being marginal (<1 pp) and concentrated in the easiest test setting (MCAR 0) on ModelNet10.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the two-stage importance-weighted variational framework of Ipsen et al. (2022, supMIWAE) and the IWAE estimator of Burda et al. (2016) for the vertical federated learning setting, with the key conceptual move of reinterpreting cross-party alignment gaps as blockwise missing data. Prior VFL methods (LASER-VFL, FedHSSL, PlugVFL, VFedTrans) each handle a subset of the challenges—unaligned training, unlabeled data, or unaligned inference—but none jointly address all three under arbitrary missingness mechanisms in a multi-party setting. The paper also draws on the classical missing-data taxonomy (MCAR/MAR/MNAR) to formalize alignment patterns, a connection not previously made in the VFL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Given that a recent survey reports only 0.2% of potential VFL pairs are fully alignable, a framework that natively exploits unaligned and unlabeled data has direct practical relevance for healthcare, banking, and insurance collaborations. The 9.6-percentage-point average gap over the strongest baseline across 168 configurations is substantial, though partly attributable to baselines that cannot handle unaligned data at all. Open questions include whether the two-stage (freeze-then-finetune) protocol is strictly necessary versus joint optimization, how communication overhead scales with the number of importance samples κ and parties K, and whether the approach extends to the 'potentially alignable but unlinked' record-linkage case the authors explicitly defer.
