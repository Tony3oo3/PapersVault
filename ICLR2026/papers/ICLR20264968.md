## Problem

Current LLM-as-a-judge frameworks suffer from two fundamental inconsistencies that have not been systematically addressed together: Score-Comparison Inconsistency, where responses with lower absolute scores win pairwise comparisons, and Pairwise Transitivity Inconsistency, manifested as circular preference cycles and equivalence contradictions. The paper identifies the root causes as information loss in coarse discrete scoring (e.g., 5-point scales compressing distinct quality levels into identical scores) and ambiguous tie judgments during pairwise evaluation, and provides the first unified theoretical and practical resolution.

## Input / Output

For single-score evaluation, the method consumes a model response and the judge model's token-level log-probabilities over an expanded score vocabulary (e.g., 100 discrete tokens), producing a continuous expected score rescaled to the original range (e.g., 1–5). For pairwise comparison, it takes two model responses and the judge's probability vectors for win/tie/lose under both presentation orders, outputting a single preference judgment. A tolerance hyperparameter δ controls tie granularity: if the score difference, PPL gap, or probability margin falls below δ, the pair is declared a tie without retraining.

## Method

TrustJudge is a training-free probabilistic framework with two components. First, distribution-sensitive scoring replaces the argmax (mode) of the judge's score distribution with its expected value over a fine-grained expanded scale (e.g., 100-point), applying proper softmax normalization to the raw token probabilities before computing the expectation and rescaling to the original range—this preserves the full entropy of the judge's assessment and avoids the non-injectivity of discrete scoring. Second, likelihood-aware aggregation resolves pairwise transitivity violations via two options: (A) a PPL-based method that selects the presentation order with lower perplexity to break ambiguous ties, and (B) a bidirectional probability aggregation that sums the win/tie/lose probabilities from both orderings (flipping the sign for the reversed direction) and selects the argmax outcome. The paper proves that the expectation-based score is injective where the mode is not, and that the PPL-derived confidence distribution has strictly lower Shannon entropy than the original ambiguous outcome distribution, formalizing why both mechanisms reduce inconsistency.

## Results

Using Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct as judge, TrustJudge reduces Score-Comparison Inconsistency (Conflict Ratio) by 8.43 percentage points (23.32% → 14.89%) and Pairwise Transitivity Inconsistency (NTR at k=5) by 10.82 percentage points (15.22% → 4.40%). The largest absolute gains appear for smaller models: Llama-3.2-3B drops NTR_k=5 from 54.69% to 17.76% and CR from 36.65% to 29.15%. Pairwise Exact Match improves by 1.19–6.85% over the baseline across all tested models, with the 6.85% gain for Llama-3.2-3B being the most pronounced.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TrustJudge is compared against two baselines: (1) the raw 5-point scoring baseline from MT-Bench (Zheng et al., 2023), which directly outputs integer scores and uses two-pass swap-order for pairwise comparisons, and (2) G-Eval-style probability summation (Liu et al., 2023), which aggregates token-level probabilities over score tokens without softmax normalization. On Score-Comparison Inconsistency, TrustJudge outperforms the raw baseline by 4.78–8.43 percentage points in CR across all four judge models, and consistently beats G-Eval by approximately 1–2 percentage points (e.g., 14.89% vs. 15.77% for Llama-3.1-70B). On Pairwise Transitivity Inconsistency, TrustJudge's likelihood-aware aggregation dramatically outperforms the two-pass swap-order baseline, reducing NTR_k=5 by 10.82–36.93 percentage points (e.g., 4.40% vs. 15.22% for Llama-3.1-70B; 17.76% vs. 54.69% for Llama-3.2-3B). In terms of accuracy, TrustJudge improves Pairwise Exact Match over the baseline by 1.19–6.85% and achieves higher Win Rates on both 5-point and 100-point scales against both baselines, confirming that the consistency gains do not come at the cost of evaluation precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

TrustJudge extends the probabilistic evaluation line initiated by G-Eval (Liu et al., 2023) and the judgment-distribution extraction of Wang et al. (2025b), but repurposes these mechanisms from improving human-judge alignment to resolving internal inconsistencies within the LLM-as-a-judge framework itself. Where prior work addressed position bias via swap-order evaluation or improved correlation with human preferences, this paper formalizes two previously unaddressed failure modes—Score-Comparison Inconsistency and Pairwise Transitivity Inconsistency—and provides a unified, training-free probabilistic resolution grounded in information-theoretic arguments about entropy loss in discrete scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLM-as-a-judge becomes the default evaluation and reward signal for alignment training (GRPO, DPO, self-rewarding), unaddressed inconsistencies in scoring and pairwise comparison undermine the reliability of the entire pipeline. TrustJudge's training-free design makes it immediately deployable, and the GRPO experiment demonstrates that more consistent reward signals translate into better downstream model performance. Key limitations include dependence on the judge model's instruction-following quality (smaller models may fail to produce valid scores) and the open question of whether the theoretical guarantees (entropy preservation, symmetry) hold robustly in multimodal or multi-turn evaluation settings.
