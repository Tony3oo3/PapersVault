## Problem

Convolutional sequence models (e.g., STUs, Hyena) achieve near-linear training via FFT but suffer from quadratic O(L²) auto-regressive generation at inference, matching the bottleneck they were designed to avoid. This limits their practical advantage over attention-based models for long-sequence generation. The paper asks whether exact (non-approximate) generation from convolutional models can be made quasilinear in sequence length while also reducing the required cache size.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a known convolutional filter φ ∈ R^L and a streaming input sequence where token u_t arrives at round t and the output ŷ_t must be produced immediately (online/auto-regressive setting). For prompt-based generation, a prompt vector p ∈ R^L is ingested in a prefill stage, after which K tokens are decoded using only an O(K)-sized cache rather than the O(L+K) cache required by naive convolution or standard KV-cache approaches. The output is the exact auto-regressively generated token sequence with no approximation error.

## Method

The core primitive is FutureFill(v, w), which computes the contribution of an already-known input segment v to future (not-yet-generated) positions of the convolution v * w, computable in O((t₁+t₂) log(t₁+t₂)) via FFT. Two algorithms exploit this: (1) Epoched-FutureFill recomputes the FutureFill cache every K steps, yielding O(L^{3/2}√(log L)) total time with O(√(L log L)) memory at the optimal K = √(L log L); (2) Continuous-FutureFill uses a divide-and-conquer decomposition of the convolution, achieving O(L log²L) total time with O(L) memory. For prompt-based generation, the prompt's contribution to all future decode positions is computed once via a single FutureFill call, so the decode phase only tracks O(K) state for the generated tokens, reducing cache from O(L+K) to O(K). All results are exact—no approximation or distillation is involved—and apply to any convolutional model regardless of training procedure.

## Results

FutureFill reduces auto-regressive generation time from O(L²) to O(L log²L) (Continuous-FutureFill) or O(L^{3/2}√log L) (Epoched-FutureFill) for convolutional sequence models, while also reducing the generation cache from O(L+K) to O(K) when generating K tokens from a prompt of length L. Empirically, on a 670.75M-parameter STU-only model, Epoched-FutureFill achieves a 1.7× speedup over naive convolution at L_gen=126,976 (1009s vs 1732s), and a 2× speedup with a 32,768-token prefill at L_gen=16,384 (75.7s vs 165.1s). On downstream tasks (WinoGrande, HellaSwag, PiQA), FutureFill matches the naive convolution baseline accuracy (e.g., 0.5138 vs 0.5160 on WinoGrande), confirming no quality loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is naive online convolution, which stores all past inputs and recomputes the full convolution at every generation step (O(L²) total time, O(L+K) cache). Epoched-FutureFill consistently outperforms this baseline across all model sizes and generation lengths: at the largest generation length (126,976 tokens, no prefill), it achieves a 1.7× speedup on the 670.75M STU-only model (1009s vs 1732s) and 1.5× on the 689.48M hybrid model (669s vs 968s). With a 32,768-token prefill, the speedup grows to 2× at L_gen=16,384 (75.7s vs 165.1s for the 670.75M model), because the baseline must recompute the full prompt convolution at every step while FutureFill only needs an O(K) cache. The paper also notes that standard attention with KV cache has the same O(L²) generation time and O(L+K) cache as naive convolution, so FutureFill provides a strict improvement over both. On downstream accuracy (WinoGrande, HellaSwag, PiQA), FutureFill matches the naive convolution baseline within 0.002–0.003, confirming the speedup comes with no quality degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the family of convolutional sequence prediction models (Hyena, Spectral Transform Units, LongConv, SGConv) that leverage FFT for near-linear training but suffer from quadratic O(L²) auto-regressive generation at inference. It departs from the SSM-distillation approach of Massaroli et al. (2024), which approximates convolutional models with recurrent equivalents, by providing exact generation with no quality loss. The core FutureFill primitive is a simple algebraic decomposition of convolution into a 'past-to-future contribution' term and a local convolution, enabling a divide-and-conquer online algorithm. Independent concurrent work (Oncescu et al., 2024) achieves the same O(L log²L) bound via relaxed polynomial interpolation, but FutureFill's framing yields a practical spectrum of runtime/memory trade-offs (Epoched vs. Continuous variants) that the polynomial-interpolation approach does not naturally provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction of generation time from O(L²) to O(L log²L) and cache size from O(L+K) to O(K) is practically significant for long-context inference with convolutional models, where the quadratic bottleneck has been a key limitation compared to SSMs and attention-based models. The method is model-agnostic (applies to any convolutional architecture regardless of training) and exact, avoiding the approximation gaps of distillation-based approaches. Open questions include whether the O(L log²L) bound is tight (the paper notes it is optimal up to poly-log factors), whether the approach extends to non-causal or multi-scale convolutions, and how it interacts with emerging hardware-specific optimizations. The moderate 1.5–1.7× empirical speedup suggests that for very long sequences the asymptotic gains will become more pronounced, but at current practical lengths the constant factors of FFT and memory access still limit the realized speedup.
