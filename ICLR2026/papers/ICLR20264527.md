## Problem

Reinforcement fine-tuning of LLMs suffers from reward over-optimization, where the policy exploits proxy reward inaccuracies to score high while true quality degrades. While this phenomenon is well-documented empirically, it remains unclear *where* in the reward distribution misspecification is most damaging, and how to practically construct reward models that are accurate precisely in that critical region—especially since high-reward examples are rare under the base policy and naively using off-policy exemplars introduces superficial artifacts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a pool of off-policy candidate responses (sampled from up to 16 frontier LLMs such as Gemini-2.5-Pro, GPT-5, Claude-Sonnet-4, etc.), and an initial set of rubric criteria as input. It produces a refined rubric encoded as a JSON array of binary (yes/no) criteria each with an integer weight from 1 to 3, which a verifier LLM (GPT-4.1-mini) uses to compute a normalized weighted-sum reward score for any response. The final output is a fine-tuned Qwen3-8B-Base policy trained via GRPO, evaluated by head-to-head win rate against Qwen3-8B and by domain-specific benchmarks (HealthBench, PRBench-Finance).

## Method

The paper first proves a theoretical result: under a misspecification mapping f from gold to proxy rewards, the KL divergence between the RFT solution and the base model is invariant to f, while the expected reward depends on f^{-1} weighted by an exponential e^{u/β}, meaning errors in the high-reward tail are exponentially amplified relative to errors elsewhere. This motivates a practical method called Refinement-through-Differentiation (RTD): starting from off-policy candidate responses, the workflow iteratively scores all candidates with the current rubric, selects the top two responses, and prompts a proposer LLM (GPT-4.1) to identify the distinguishing features between them and encode those distinctions as new or refined rubric criteria. Two guiding principles drive the design: (1) rubrics must distinguish excellent responses from merely great ones (not just good from bad), and (2) rubrics must be refined against a diverse set of great responses from multiple frontier models to avoid overfitting to a single model's style. The iterative loop progressively concentrates rubric discovery on the performance frontier, and the resulting rubric-based reward is shown to substantially delay the onset of reward over-optimization during extended GRPO training.

## Results

On the generalist domain (Filtered Set / LMArena), the proposed method (4 great & Diverse Pairs) achieves 39.7% / 35.1% win rate against Qwen3-8B, compared to 35.9% / 29.6% for SFT and 31.3% / 29.7% for initial rubrics alone. On the health domain, it reaches 34.4% win rate on Medical-o1 and 0.3513 on HealthBench, versus 25.8% / 0.2999 for SFT. On the finance domain, it achieves 49.6% win rate and 0.3018 on PRBench-Finance, versus 26.0% / 0.2218 for SFT. The method also substantially delays reward over-optimization during extended RL training, maintaining higher win rates and benchmark scores for longer than all other rubric construction strategies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its iterative rubric refinement method (4 great & Diverse Pairs) against six baselines: the untrained base policy, SFT, initial prompt-only rubrics, and three levels of rubric refinement (1 good pair, 1 great pair, 4 great pairs). The proposed method wins across all three domains and all metrics. Against SFT, it gains +3.8 pp win rate on the Filtered Set, +8.6 pp on Medical-o1, and +0.051 on HealthBench. Against initial rubrics (no refinement), the gains are larger: +8.4 pp on Filtered Set, +12.7 pp on Medical-o1, and +0.051 on HealthBench, confirming that rubric refinement is essential. The progression from 1 good pair → 1 great pair → 4 great pairs → 4 great & diverse pairs shows monotonic improvement, validating both principles (distinguishing great responses, and using diverse candidates). Notably, in extended RL training (Figure 4), the 4 great & Diverse Pairs method sustains high performance far longer before over-optimization sets in, whereas initial rubrics and single-pair refinements peak early and then degrade rapidly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rubric-based reward framework (RLRR) introduced by Gunjal et al. (2025) by addressing a specific gap: how to elicit rubrics that capture the high-reward tail where over-optimization originates. It builds on the reward over-optimization literature (Gao et al., 2023) by providing a theoretical characterization showing that misspecification in the high-reward region dominates the utility-KL tradeoff, and by proposing Refinement-through-Differentiation (RTD) as a concrete workflow to sharpen rubrics in that region. The paper also positions itself against Bradley-Terry reward models, demonstrating empirically that off-policy data fails to improve BT models at moderate scale, whereas rubric-based rewards naturally absorb off-policy exemplars without learning superficial artifacts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a small number of well-chosen comparisons among strong, diverse off-policy responses (as few as 4 pairs) can substantially delay reward over-optimization and improve downstream RL performance across domains, offering a scalable alternative to expensive online RLHF. The theoretical result that KL divergence is invariant to the misspecification function while expected reward is exponentially sensitive to high-tail errors provides a clean diagnostic for reward model design. Open questions include the optimal score aggregation strategy (the paper deliberately uses a simple weighted average), whether the approach generalizes to domains without strong off-policy models, and how to reduce dependence on a single powerful LLM (GPT-4.1) for both rubric generation and verification.
