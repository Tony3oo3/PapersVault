## Problem

Existing Reasoning Video Object Segmentation methods rely on fine-tuning MLLMs with segmentation tokens and still fail on temporally-sensitive queries (e.g., 'which player makes a three-point shot?') because they lack the ability to integrate temporal and spatial reasoning over complex, implicit text queries. No prior work leverages zero-shot Chain-of-Thought reasoning from pre-trained MLLMs to resolve temporal-semantic correlations across video frames for segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261998/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video (T frames of H×W×3) and a complex or implicit text query as input, and outputs a list of k non-overlapping binary mask sequences (each T×H×W), one per target instance satisfying the query. In offline mode, keyframe candidates are uniformly sampled (at most 8) and concatenated into a single grid image for the MLLM; in online mode, frames arrive sequentially and the model makes predictions only from previously seen frames, periodically re-evaluating keyframe selection every ξ frames. The framework is training-free and compatible with both closed-source (GPT-4o) and open-source (Gemma3-12B, LLaVA1.5-7B) MLLMs.

## Method

CoT-RVS is a modular, training-free multi-agent framework with three components: an MLLM keyframe selector (F_key), a reasoning image segmentation model (F_seg, e.g., Seg-Zero), and a video processor (F_vid, e.g., SAM2). The keyframe selector receives a grid of sampled keyframe candidates plus the text query and performs zero-shot CoT reasoning via a progressive self-Q&A prompt—starting from 'what can be seen in each frame?' to 'is this a better keyframe?' to 'are there new objects meeting the query?'—to identify temporal-semantic correlations and output, for each target instance, a keyframe index and a spatial description. The segmentation model then generates a key mask per instance from the selected keyframe and description, and SAM2 propagates each key mask into a full video mask sequence. An optional post-processing step enforces non-overlap across instances. The online variant periodically queries the MLLM for a binary keyframe selectivity decision, updating the tracked target when a better-matching object emerges mid-stream.

## Results

On the ReasonVOS benchmark, CoT-RVS with GPT-4o achieves 65.5 J&F (J=62.4, F=68.7), surpassing the previous best offline method GLUS (49.9) by 15.6 points. On Refer-DAVIS-17, CoT-RVS-GPT-4o reaches 79.1 J&F, outperforming HyperSeg (71.2) by 7.9 points. On the temporally sensitive T-ReasonVOS subset, CoT-RVS-GPT-4o achieves 55.5 J&F versus 37.4 for VideoLISA, an 18.1-point gap that highlights the critical role of temporal CoT reasoning. The online variant (LLaVA1.5-7B) also surpasses offline baselines in most settings, e.g., 52.0 J&F on ReasonVOS vs. 47.5 for VideoLISA-Po.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoT-RVS-GPT-4o decisively outperforms all prior methods on ReasonVOS (65.5 vs. 49.9 J&F for GLUS, 47.5 for VideoLISA-Po) and Refer-DAVIS-17 (79.1 vs. 71.2 for HyperSeg, 70.6 for SAMWISE), with the largest margins on reasoning-heavy benchmarks. On ReVOS, the advantage narrows to 0.2 points over HyperSeg (55.9 vs. 55.7), suggesting the CoT benefit is most pronounced when temporal sensitivity is high. On the purpose-built T-ReasonVOS subset of temporally sensitive queries, CoT-RVS-GPT-4o (55.5) leads VideoLISA (37.4) by 18.1 points and ThinkFirst+SAM2 (33.6) by 21.9 points, confirming that the CoT keyframe selection mechanism is the primary source of the gain. The online variant using LLaVA1.5-7B (52.0 on ReasonVOS, 73.9 on Refer-DAVIS-17) also surpasses offline baselines like VideoLISA-Po and VISA-13B despite using a smaller, open-source MLLM, demonstrating the framework's robustness to model scale. Gemma3-12B performs slightly below GPT-4o across all benchmarks (e.g., 50.7 vs. 65.5 on ReasonVOS), indicating that the quality of the MLLM's temporal-semantic reasoning remains a bottleneck for smaller models.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoT-RVS departs from the dominant paradigm in Reasoning VOS (VISA, VideoLISA, HyperSeg) that finetunes MLLMs to emit segmentation tokens, instead adopting a fully training-free, modular pipeline where a pre-trained MLLM (GPT-4o or Gemma3) performs zero-shot Chain-of-Thought keyframe selection, a separate image segmentation model (Seg-Zero) generates per-instance key masks, and SAM2 tracks them across frames. Conceptually, it extends the image-domain CoT reasoning segmentation line (ThinkFirst, Seg-Zero) into the temporal dimension by prompting the MLLM to reason over multiple keyframe candidates simultaneously, and it generalizes the single-object Reasoning VOS formulation to multi-instance Reasoning VIS and to online streaming settings. The framework is explicitly positioned as compatible with closed-source MLLMs, a practical distinction from prior finetuning-based approaches that require open weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, modular design makes CoT-RVS immediately deployable with any capable MLLM (including API-only models like GPT-4o) without the cost and data requirements of finetuning, and the T-ReasonVOS benchmark isolates temporally sensitive queries where prior methods show the largest failures. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos (the 8-keyframe grid limits temporal resolution), whether fine-tuning MLLMs specifically for temporal-semantic CoT could close the remaining gap, and whether the strong GPT-4o results translate to open-source models as they improve. The robustness finding that smaller open-source models (LLaVA1.5-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-3B) produce 0% valid CoT outputs on the offline prompt highlights a practical bottleneck for democratizing the method.
