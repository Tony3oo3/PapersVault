## Problem

In work-conserving (WC) multi-GPU systems, operations are scheduled asynchronously as soon as dependencies are met, making execution order non-deterministic and load balancing inherently temporal. Prior learning-based device placement methods either assume bulk-synchronous execution, learn a single placement policy that ignores traversal dynamics, or stop optimizing after pre-training, leaving them unable to capture the stochastic interplay between scheduling order and hardware contention that dominates WC performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262156/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a static dataflow graph (vertices are sharded computation kernels, edges are data dependencies) annotated with per-node features (FLOPs, critical-path lengths, communication costs) and a set of devices with dynamic features (cumulative load, earliest/latest input arrival times). It produces a complete device assignment by sequentially selecting and placing each vertex over |V| MDP steps; the assignment is then executed by a WC runtime (or simulator) whose stochastic execution time serves as the reward signal. Graphs are typically generated by sharding high-level operations (e.g., via Alpa) for N-GPU parallelism.

## Method

DOPPLER formulates device assignment as an episodic MDP with two learned policies: SEL_θ selects the next node to assign (approximating the non-deterministic temporal flow of a WC scheduler) and PLC_θ places that node on a device, both encoded by a message-passing GNN over the dataflow graph and decoded by FFNNs with ε-greedy action selection. Training proceeds in three stages: (I) imitation learning from a critical-path list-scheduling heuristic, (II) simulation-based policy-gradient RL using a digital-twin WC simulator, and (III) lightweight online RL on the real system where execution times are observed 'for free' during production serving. A key efficiency contribution is performing GNN message passing once per episode rather than per MDP step, reducing training cost by over 30× with negligible impact on final assignment quality.

## Results

On four NVIDIA Tesla P100 GPUs, DOPPLER-SYS (full three-stage training) achieves the lowest execution time across all four dataflow graphs: 123.4 ms (CHAINMM), 47.4 ms (FFNN), 160.3 ms (LLAMA-BLOCK), and 150.6 ms (LLAMA-LAYER). DOPPLER-SYS reduces execution time by up to 52.7% over the best prior baseline (GDP on FFNN) and by up to 13.8% over the authors' own ENUMERATIVEOPTIMIZER (on LLAMA-LAYER). The simulation-only variant DOPPLER-SIM (Stages I+II) is competitive, often within 1–2 ms of the full system, confirming that the real-system RL stage (Stage III) provides incremental but consistent gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOPPLER-SYS outperforms all three external baselines on every model. Against CRITICAL PATH (Kwok & Ahmad, 1999), a classical list-scheduling heuristic, DOPPLER-SYS achieves up to 78.2% lower execution time (FFNN: 47.4 ms vs. 217.8 ms). Against PLACETO (Addanki et al., 2019), a GNN-based single-policy RL method, DOPPLER-SYS wins by up to 62.5% (FFNN: 47.4 ms vs. 126.3 ms); PLACETO is particularly weak on LLAMA-BLOCK (411.5 ms), where it is slower even than the non-learning CRITICAL PATH heuristic. Against GDP (Zhou et al., 2019), a graph-embedding RL method with sequential attention, DOPPLER-SYS achieves up to 52.7% lower execution time (FFNN: 47.4 ms vs. 100.3 ms). DOPPLER also surpasses the authors' own ENUMERATIVEOPTIMIZER—a greedy, level-by-level exhaustive search over shard assignments—by up to 13.8% on LLAMA-LAYER (150.6 ms vs. 174.8 ms), demonstrating that the learned dual-policy approach captures temporal load-balancing dynamics that the lock-step-assuming enumerative method cannot.

## Relation to Prior Work

DOPPLER extends the RL-based device placement line (Mirhoseini et al. 2017, PLACETO, GDP) by replacing the single placement policy with a dual-policy (select-then-place) decomposition that mirrors the classical list-scheduling heuristic of Kwok & Ahmad (1999), but learned end-to-end via an episodic MDP. It departs from prior work in two structural ways: (1) the target execution model is a work-conserving (asynchronous) scheduler rather than bulk-synchronous all-reduce, which makes execution order stochastic and load balancing inherently temporal; and (2) training proceeds in three stages—imitation learning, simulation-based RL, and online real-system RL—whereas prior methods train only offline. The GNN message-passing encoder follows the standard MPNN formulation (Gilmer et al. 2017) used in combinatorial optimization, with the key efficiency modification of performing message passing once per episode rather than per step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work-conserving setting is the regime in which modern multi-GPU runtimes (e.g., JAX, custom CUDA event loops) actually operate, yet prior learned placement methods were validated only under bulk-synchronous assumptions where execution order is fixed. DOPPLER's three-stage training pipeline is practically important because it avoids the long, unstable exploration phase that pure online RL would impose in production, and the 'reward for free' property (observing real execution times during serving) enables continuous improvement without additional overhead. Open questions include scaling to graphs with thousands of nodes and dozens of GPUs (the paper discusses but does not test this), whether the dual-policy decomposition generalizes to heterogeneous device types beyond homogeneous GPU clusters, and how the approach interacts with dynamic graph structures (e.g., MoE routing) that violate the static-graph assumption.
