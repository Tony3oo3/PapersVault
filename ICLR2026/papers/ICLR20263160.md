## Problem

Vision-language models align images and text in a shared embedding space, yet the geometric structure of this space remains poorly understood. When sparse autoencoders are applied to VLM activations, the resulting concept dictionaries segregate by modality—a concept-level manifestation of the modality gap—but prior geometric accounts (cone effects, contrastive-loss dynamics) do not explain what this separation means for shared concepts or how to exploit it. The paper asks how VLMs internally organize cross-modal alignment and whether a principled inductive bias can make this structure both interpretable and actionable.

## Input / Output

The method operates on frozen embeddings (not raw pixels or tokens) from dual-encoder VLMs—CLIP ViT-B/32, CLIP ViT-L/14, OpenCLIP, OpenCLIP-L, SigLIP, and SigLIP2—using 1 million randomly sampled LAION image-text pairs. It produces an overcomplete sparse dictionary (expansion ratio 8, target ℓ₀ = 20) whose atoms are partitioned into bimodal and unimodal groups via a modality-score threshold. Downstream, the dictionary enables two interventions: (i) masking unimodal atoms to reconstruct gap-free embeddings, and (ii) restricting semantic edit vectors to the bimodal subspace to generate in-distribution retrieval queries on FashionIQ.

## Method

The core contribution is the Iso-Energy Assumption: a concept genuinely shared across modalities should exhibit invariant average squared activation (second moment) in both image and text domains. This is operationalized by augmenting a Matching Pursuit sparse autoencoder with a soft alignment regularizer L_align = −(1/b)·Tr(Z^(d) Z^(d')ᵀ), which maximizes cosine similarity of ℓ₂-normalized codes from paired modalities, thereby encouraging energy consistency without compromising reconstruction (β ≈ 10⁻⁴). The resulting Aligned SAE (SAE-A) reveals a clean geometric decomposition invisible to standard SAEs: a small set of high-energy unimodal atoms acts as modality-specific biases that fully account for the modality gap, while a compact set of sparse bimodal atoms carries the entire cross-modal alignment signal. This decomposition is validated through four complementary metrics (probing accuracy, functional alignment ρ, Functional and Distributional Agreement, and interventional robustness δ_r) and confirmed on synthetic data with known ground truth. The structure is then made actionable: removing unimodal atoms collapses the modality gap while preserving retrieval, and restricting vector arithmetic to the bimodal subspace yields in-distribution semantic edits that outperform classical embedding-space arithmetic.

## Results

Across six VLMs (CLIP, CLIP-L, OpenCLIP, OpenCLIP-L, SigLIP, SigLIP2), SAE-A matches SAE's reconstruction fidelity (MSE and R² nearly identical, e.g., R² = 0.859 vs 0.837 on CLIP) while dramatically improving all multimodality-sensitive metrics. Functional alignment ρ increases by more than an order of magnitude (e.g., 0.327 → 4.232 on CLIP; 0.713 → 1.475 on SigLIP2), and FDA more than doubles (e.g., 2.630 → 4.559 on CLIP; 8.246 → 18.24 on SigLIP2). Interventional robustness δ_r remains near zero (≤ 0.007) for SAE-A, confirming that unimodal atoms can be ablated without harming retrieval. On FashionIQ semantic vector arithmetic, restricting to bimodal atoms reduces OOD scores by 20–38% (e.g., 0.99 → 0.61 on SigLIP2) while preserving retrieval performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between the standard Matching Pursuit SAE and the proposed Aligned SAE (SAE-A) trained with the Iso-Energy regularizer. SAE-A matches SAE on reconstruction (MSE, R²) but wins decisively on every multimodality-sensitive metric: ρ improves by 10–23×, FDA by 1.7–4×, and δ_r drops to near zero, indicating that unimodal atoms carry no cross-modal signal. Against the embedding-shift baseline of Liang et al. (2022), which translates each modality's mean toward the midpoint, the proposed unimodal-atom filtering fully merges the image and text distributions (closing the modality gap) while preserving retrieval, whereas the shift method leaves the distributions well separated and degrades performance. The paper also contrasts with Schrodi et al. (2025), whose projection onto a small set of canonical directions was shown to be detrimental to cross-modal tasks and to leave the gap wide open, confirming that those directions do not capture the full modality-specific subspace Ω_I ⊕ Ω_T.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Matching Pursuit sparse autoencoder (Costa et al., 2025; Mallat & Zhang, 1993) as its base architecture and extends the SAE-based interpretability line (Bricken et al., 2023; Cunningham et al., 2023; Elhage et al., 2022) to the multimodal setting. It departs from prior modality-gap analyses (Liang et al., 2022; Schrodi et al., 2025; Fahim et al., 2024), which described the phenomenon geometrically (cones, ellipsoids) or through contrastive training dynamics, by providing a concept-level explanation: the gap is fully accounted for by a small set of high-energy unimodal atoms acting as modality biases. It also contrasts with Papadimitriou et al. (2025), who found idiosyncratic (modality-specific) atoms in VLM SAEs, by showing that cross-modal information is carried by shared bimodal atoms rather than idiosyncratic ones, and with Schrodi et al. (2025), whose projection onto canonical directions was harmful, by identifying the correct directions (unimodal SAE atoms) whose removal is benign.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, actionable decomposition of VLM embedding geometry: a compact bimodal subspace that fully sustains cross-modal alignment, and a small set of unimodal bias atoms that explain the modality gap. This enables practical interventions—closing the gap without performance loss and performing in-distribution semantic arithmetic—that were previously inaccessible. Key limitations include sensitivity to the alignment weight β, analysis restricted to SAE reconstructions rather than raw embeddings, and evaluation limited to dual-encoder architectures; whether the same structural invariants hold in cross-attention or generative VLMs remains open. A more principled calibration of β and extension to encoder-decoder VLMs are natural next steps.
