## Problem

Conditional image generation models fail to simultaneously satisfy text prompts and conditioning inputs when the two sources of guidance conflict, either at the input level (condition semantics contradict the prompt) or due to model bias (learned priors override compatible prompts). Existing post-training approaches—supervised fine-tuning and naive DPO—suffer from gradient entanglement that causes the model to collapse toward one constraint while neglecting the other, and no disentangled, conflict-aware preference dataset exists for multi-constraint conditional generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a conditioning image (depth map, Canny edge map, soft edge, or style reference) and produces a single generated image that adheres to both constraints. Conditioning is injected via FLUX-based architectures (FLUX-Depth, FLUX-Canny, Union-Pro2, or IP-Adapter for style), where the condition is either concatenated at the input channel or injected through an auxiliary adapter network. The key operational detail is that the text prompt and condition are deliberately allowed to conflict during training, and the model must learn case-by-case trade-offs rather than a fixed priority.

## Method

BideDPO replaces the single coupled preference pair of vanilla DPO with two decoupled pairs per sample—one isolating text alignment (both samples share similar condition adherence, differing only in prompt fidelity) and one isolating condition alignment (both samples follow the prompt, differing only in structural fidelity)—so that each objective receives an independent gradient signal free from the other's interference. An Adaptive Loss Balancing (ALB) module computes detached weights from the instantaneous loss magnitudes of the two branches, preventing the stronger objective from swallowing the weaker one during backpropagation. Training data is generated by an automated pipeline that uses an LLM to create conflicting prompt-condition pairs, samples from the current model, and applies VLM-based verification to label positive/negative examples for each branch. The entire process is wrapped in an iterative loop where the updated model regenerates higher-quality preference data for the next round, progressively sharpening both text and condition adherence.

## Results

On the DualAlign depth-conditioned benchmark, BideDPO applied to FLUX-Depth achieves a Success Ratio of 0.91 (vs. 0.76 baseline, +15%) and reduces MSE from 233.6 to 145.9 (−87.7 points), while SGMSE drops from 282.8 to 164.4. On Union-Pro2 (depth), the method boosts SR from 0.49 to 0.84 (+35%) and on FLUX-Canny it raises SR from 0.33 to 0.73 (+40%), even surpassing the unconstrained FLUX text-to-image model in text alignment. On the standard COCO benchmark (unseen during training), BideDPO still improves depth-conditioned SR from 0.83 to 0.91 and canny-conditioned F1 from 0.416 to 0.497, confirming robustness without iterative optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BideDPO consistently outperforms all structural-conditioning baselines. Against LooseControl (SR 0.43, MSE 791.1) and ControlNet++ (SR 0.49, MSE 331.9), the method achieves substantially higher text success and lower condition error (SR 0.91, MSE 145.9 on FLUX-Depth). Compared to Union-Pro2 (SR 0.49, MSE 177.0), BideDPO yields a 35-point SR gain and a 13-point MSE reduction. Against the two post-training baselines, BideDPO beats SFT (SR 0.79, MSE 162.2) by 12 points in SR and 16.3 in MSE, and beats naive DPO (SR 0.89, MSE 171.9) by 2 points in SR and 26 in MSE, confirming that the decoupled gradient formulation is critical. On the COCO robustness benchmark, BideDPO also surpasses Union-Pro2 (SR 0.83→0.91 depth; F1 0.416→0.497 canny) and both SFT and DPO variants, demonstrating that gains transfer to standard prompts without conflict.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO-for-diffusion line of work (Diffusion-DPO, RankDPO, SPO) to the multi-constraint conditional generation setting, where a text prompt and a structural/style condition must be jointly satisfied. Its key departure from vanilla DPO is the diagnosis of gradient entanglement when a single coupled preference pair must simultaneously improve two competing objectives, and the solution of constructing two decoupled preference pairs (one per objective) with an adaptive loss-balancing mechanism. The automated VLM-based data pipeline and iterative self-refinement loop are novel engineering contributions that address the previously absent conflict-aware DPO dataset for conditional generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formally identifying the input-level and model-bias conflicts that degrade controllability in conditional image generation, the paper gives the community a concrete problem formulation and a new benchmark (DualAlign) to measure progress. The decoupled DPO recipe is lightweight (LoRA, ~2k steps on top of SFT) and plugs into existing FLUX/ControlNet/IP-Adapter pipelines, making it practically adoptable. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond two constraints (e.g., depth + canny + style simultaneously), whether the iterative loop's degradation after iteration 3 signals a fundamental ceiling, and whether the VLM-based preference labeling introduces systematic biases that limit real-world deployment.
