## Problem

Equivariant MLIPs achieve state-of-the-art accuracy on materials benchmarks but rely on computationally expensive tensor products and high-degree irreducible representations, requiring hundreds of GPU-days for training. As QM-based datasets continue to expand, the paper asks whether strict equivariance remains indispensable and whether a more compact invariant architecture can fully exploit the high-dimensional atomic interactions encoded in these datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262962/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a crystalline system defined by atomic numbers, 3D Cartesian coordinates, and a 3×3 lattice matrix, then constructs a dual graph representation: an Atom Graph (atoms as nodes, bonds as edges) and a Line Graph (bonds as nodes, angles as edges) via radius-cutoff construction with periodic boundary conditions. Outputs include per-atom energies (summed to total energy), magnetic moments, and forces/stress computed via automatic differentiation of the energy with respect to coordinates and strain, rather than being directly regressed.

## Method

MatRIS is an invariant GNN that explicitly models three-body interactions through a Line Graph, where each node corresponds to a bond in the Atom Graph and edges encode angular (three-body) information. The key architectural innovation is a separable attention mechanism with O(N) complexity: Dim-wise Softmax computes attention weights independently per feature dimension (rather than sharing a single weight across all dimensions), and Separable Attention introduces two independent weight sets—one for source-to-target and one for target-to-source information flow—capturing asymmetric interatomic interactions. The Line Graph is updated first via attention, and its refined edge features are propagated back to the Atom Graph for node updates. Training combines supervised learning on energies, forces, and magnetic moments with a denoising pretraining stage (projecting forces onto edges to scalarize them for non-equivariant networks) and a graph-level loss reduction strategy to prevent large structures from dominating the force loss.

## Results

On the compliant Matbench-Discovery benchmark, MatRIS-L (10.4M params) achieves SOTA across all metrics: F1=0.847, MAE=0.031 meV/atom, and RMSD=0.0717 Å, outperforming the previous best eSEN-30M-MP (F1=0.831, 30.1M params) and eqV2 S DeNS (F1=0.815, 31.2M params). MatRIS-M (6.3M) matches eSEN-30M-MP on F1 (0.833 vs 0.831) and MAE (0.033), while MatRIS-S (4.3M) matches eqV2 S DeNS on MAE (0.036). Training efficiency improves by 13.0× over eqV2 S DeNS and 6.4× over eSEN-30M-MP, with MatRIS-L requiring far fewer GPU days than the 228 and 335 GPU days needed by the equivariant baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MatRIS-L (10.4M params) achieves SOTA F1 of 0.847 on Matbench-Discovery, surpassing eSEN-30M-MP (0.831, 30.1M) by 1.6 points and eqV2 S DeNS (0.815, 31.2M) by 3.2 points, while using roughly 3× fewer parameters. MatRIS-M (6.3M) matches eSEN-30M-MP on F1 (0.833 vs 0.831) and MAE (0.033), and MatRIS-S (4.3M) ties eqV2 S DeNS on MAE (0.036) with a slightly lower F1 (0.811 vs 0.815). Against smaller invariant models like CHGNet (0.613 F1) and DPA-3.1-MPTrj (0.803 F1), MatRIS-L leads by 23.4 and 4.4 F1 points respectively. On the non-compliant benchmark (OMat24+sAlex+MPTrj), MatRIS-10M-OAM achieves F1=0.903 and RMSD=0.060, marginally beating eSEN-30M-OAM (F1=0.902, RMSD=0.061) with ~3× fewer parameters. On the MatCalc benchmark, MatRIS-M (MPTrj-trained) attains SOTA or near-SOTA on 83% of metrics, and MatRIS-10M-OAM achieves the best overall performance. On the molecular zero-shot benchmark, MatRIS-M reduces TorsionNet-500 energy MAE to 0.04 kcal/mol (vs 0.06 for DPA3-L24) and ranks first in 5 of 6 metrics across MD22, ANI-1x, and AIMD-Chig.

## Relation to Prior Work

MatRIS extends the invariant MLIP lineage (CHGNet, DimeNet, GemNet) by introducing an explicit line-graph construction for three-body interactions updated via a novel O(N) separable attention mechanism with dim-wise softmax. It departs from the dominant equivariant paradigm (eSEN, eqV2, MACE, NequIP) that relies on tensor products and high-degree irreps, arguing that with sufficiently large QM-based datasets, invariant architectures can match or exceed equivariant accuracy at a fraction of the cost. The line-graph approach for encoding angles has precedent in ALIGNN and DPA3, but MatRIS is the first to use an O(N) attention mechanism to explicitly encode and update three-body features, rather than implicitly refining them through message passing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work challenges the prevailing assumption that equivariance is indispensable for SOTA MLIP accuracy, demonstrating that a carefully designed invariant model with attention-based three-body modeling can achieve F1 of 0.847 on Matbench-Discovery (vs. 0.831 for eSEN-30M-MP) while reducing training cost by 13×. As QM-based datasets continue to grow (OMat24, OMC25, OMoL25), this opens a path toward more compact and efficient foundation models for materials science. Open questions include whether the invariant approach maintains its advantage in data-limited regimes, how it scales to even larger datasets, and whether reliable distillation strategies can produce lightweight student models. The authors also note the need to incorporate long-range electrostatics for broader applicability.
