## Problem

Existing reward methods for GUI agents are either rule-based (requiring manually crafted unit tests per task, limiting scalability) or passive LLM-as-a-judge approaches that evaluate static trajectories. Both fail because GUI task states are often incompletely observable from screenshots alone, and general-purpose LLMs lack the domain-specific GUI expertise needed to interpret complex UI logic and verify task completion accurately.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262434/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a task instruction and the policy agent's execution trajectory (screenshots, action history, and HTML states) as input, but unlike passive judges, it also receives access to the live environment where evaluator agents can actively navigate and probe additional UI states. The output is a binary outcome reward (success/failure), produced through a structured chain-of-claims comparison between policy-agent claims and evaluator-agent claims, with each claim tied to specific trajectory steps and grounded in observed UI evidence.

## Method

PRORE introduces a reasoner-actor collaboration architecture where a general-purpose LLM reasoner (Gemini-2.5-Pro) schedules targeted state probing tasks based on the original task's expected outcomes, and domain-specific evaluator agents (e.g., V-Droid) execute these tasks by actively interacting with the environment to collect key state observations. Both the policy trajectory and probed states are summarized into structured claims, which the reasoner then compares via chain-of-claims reasoning (classifying relationships as confirming, contradicting, complementary, or unrelated) to produce the final reward. The key insight is the execution-probing gap: probing tasks (navigating to verify a state) are substantially easier than execution tasks (performing multi-step modifications), making the reward system more generalizable than the policy agent it evaluates. Test-time scaling via parallel and iterative state probing further refines reward accuracy, and a claim filter eliminates irrelevant or misleading evidence before the final judgment.

## Results

PRORE achieves an average reward accuracy of 93.7% and F1 of 83.0% across three mobile benchmarks (AndroidWorld, AndroidLab, MobileAgentBench) and three policy agents (V-Droid, M3A, UI-TARS-7B), surpassing the best baseline (StepCritic) by 5.3% in accuracy and 19.4% in F1. On PC/web tasks (OSWorld and OSWorld-Chrome), PRORE reaches 92.0% and 93.5% accuracy respectively, outperforming the best baselines by 4.0% and 6.5%. When used to guide test-time scaling, PRORE improves V-Droid's success rate from 56.5% to 67.2% and M3A's success rate by 22.4%, with gains 3.9–4.3% higher than other reward methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRORE outperforms all four baselines on every metric across all three mobile benchmarks. Against the strongest baseline, StepCritic (Lai et al., 2025), PRORE gains 5.3% in average accuracy (93.7% vs. 88.4%) and 19.4% in average F1 (83.0% vs. 63.6%). The gap is most pronounced on UI-TARS-7B trajectories, where baselines suffer F1 scores of only 10–25% due to the agent's low 7.9% success rate, while PRORE reaches 66.7% F1 by proactively probing key states. Compared to DigiRL (Bai et al., NeurIPS 2024), DistRL (Wang et al., 2024), and WebRL (Qi et al., 2024), PRORE improves average accuracy by 9.1%, 7.6%, and 6.8% respectively, with F1 gains of 23.1%, 22.1%, and 20.2%. On the PC/web extension (OSWorld, OSWorld-Chrome), PRORE leads the best baseline by 4.0% and 6.5% in accuracy, confirming that the reasoner–actor decoupling generalizes beyond mobile GUIs.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRORE departs from the dominant LLM-as-a-judge paradigm for GUI agent rewards (DigiRL, DistRL, WebRL, StepCritic), which passively evaluates static trajectories, by introducing proactive state probing where domain-specific evaluator agents interact with the environment to collect additional evidence. It also extends the broader agent-as-a-judge paradigm (Zhuge et al. 2024; Yu 2025) by decoupling a general-purpose reasoner (which schedules probing tasks and performs chain-of-claims reasoning) from domain-specific actors (which execute GUI navigation), rather than relying on a single LLM to handle both high-level logic and low-level UI interpretation. The execution-probing gap—demonstrating that verification tasks are substantially easier than execution tasks—provides a principled justification for why this decoupling is effective and generalizable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRORE is the first reward system to surpass 90% accuracy for GUI agents, and its reasoner-actor architecture offers a reusable pattern for any domain where verification requires domain-specific interaction with an environment. The co-evolution dynamic between policy and evaluator agents (both potentially instantiated from the same model) opens a path toward self-improving agent systems, though the paper defers full online RL integration to future work. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-device or cross-platform tasks, how the cost overhead (~$0.06/task) compares as evaluator agents grow more capable, and whether the execution-probing gap holds for fundamentally different interaction modalities (e.g., voice, haptic).
