## Problem

Video reasoning segmentation requires localizing objects based on complex textual queries that demand world knowledge and temporal reasoning, yet existing approaches either jointly train MLLMs with segmentation models (incurring high computation and limited generalization) or extract raw attention maps that are dominated by visual attention sinks and irrelevant activations. No prior training-free method reliably converts MLLM attention into accurate segmentation masks across diverse MLLM architectures and reasoning-intensive benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260968/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video (16 uniformly sampled frames) and a free-form text expression as input, and produces two outputs: coarse segmentation masks at the visual token grid resolution (H_p × W_p) obtained by thresholding fused attention maps, and dense full-resolution video object masks (T × H × W) generated by SAM2 prompted with point queries derived from the attention maps. The attention maps operate at a much coarser spatial resolution than the video frames, and the SAM2 pipeline uses full-resolution frames for propagation, bridging this gap.

## Method

DecAF refines noisy attention rollout maps through two decomposed fusion mechanisms: (1) contrastive object-background fusion, which subtracts a background attention map (obtained by prompting the MLLM to describe the scene while explicitly excluding the identified target category) from an object attention map, thereby suppressing visual attention sinks; and (2) complementary video-frame fusion, which averages video-level attention (capturing temporal context but at coarse granularity) with frame-level attention (providing fine-grained spatial detail) processed at higher resolution via multi-scale tiling. The attention rollout itself is modified with a vision-aware head-weighted normalization (V-Max) that weights heads by their maximum visual-token attention to reduce noise. For dense masks, point queries are extracted from thresholded attention maps and fed to SAM2, with an attention consistency score (measuring overlap between predicted masks and high-attention regions across frames) used to filter spurious tracklets before final propagation.

## Results

DecAF with Qwen2.5VL-7B achieves 75.2 J&F on Ref-DAVIS, 63.9 J&F on ReasonVOS, and 54.2 J&F on ReVOS (Overall), all without any training. On ReasonVOS, DecAF surpasses all training-based baselines, including Veason-R1 (59.9) which uses the same MLLM with RL-based fine-tuning, and VRS-HQ (54.9). On Ref-DAVIS, DecAF outperforms VISA (69.4) and VideoLISA (68.8) by 5.8 and 6.4 J&F respectively, while remaining close to VRS-HQ (76.0). The method also generalizes across MLLM families, with InternVL3-8B reaching 58.9 J&F on ReasonVOS and Qwen2VL-7B reaching 52.5 J&F.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest training-based baseline VRS-HQ (ChatUniVi-7B), DecAF with Qwen2.5VL-7B is slightly lower on Ref-DAVIS (75.2 vs. 76.0) and ReVOS Overall (54.2 vs. 59.1) but substantially higher on ReasonVOS (63.9 vs. 54.9, +9.0 J&F). DecAF also surpasses Veason-R1 (59.9) on ReasonVOS by 4.0 J&F despite Veason-R1 using RL-based fine-tuning of the same Qwen2.5VL backbone. Compared to other training-based methods, DecAF outperforms VISA by 5.8 J&F on Ref-DAVIS, VideoLISA by 6.4 J&F on Ref-DAVIS, and GLUS by 14.0 J&F on ReasonVOS. Among training-free methods, DecAF consistently outperforms Loc-Head across all MLLMs and datasets, with the largest gap on ReasonVOS (e.g., 63.9 vs. 41.1 with Qwen2.5VL-7B, +22.8 J&F). The only case where Loc-Head edges out DecAF is Ref-DAVIS with InternVL3-8B (66.3 vs. 62.8), but DecAF recovers strongly on ReasonVOS (58.9 vs. 44.3). TAM (Li et al., 2025) performs poorly (2.8–4.0 J&F) due to its dependence on predicted word tokens, making it an unreliable baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the attention rollout mechanism (Abnar & Zuidema, 2020) for extracting token-level attention from MLLMs, extending it with a vision-aware head-weighted normalization (V-Max) that upweights heads with strong visual attention. It departs from prior training-free grounding methods—Loc-Head, which selects a small set of localization heads via spatial entropy heuristics, and TAM, which relies on predicted word tokens—by instead using all heads with contrastive object-background subtraction and complementary video-frame averaging to produce cleaner attention maps. Unlike training-based approaches (LISA, VISA, GLUS, VRS-HQ, Veason-R1) that jointly fine-tune MLLMs with SAM via LoRA or RL, DecAF operates entirely without retraining, using only prompt engineering and attention-map post-processing to guide SAM2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a purely training-free pipeline (attention rollout + subtraction + averaging + SAM2 prompting) matches or exceeds training-based methods on ReasonVOS (63.9 J&F vs. Veason-R1's 59.9 on the same Qwen2.5VL backbone) suggests that MLLMs' reasoning-driven attention already encodes sufficient localization signal, reducing the need for expensive joint optimization. Open questions include part-level segmentation (where SAM2 struggles with single-point prompts on sub-object regions), very small or elongated objects where attention resolution is insufficient, and whether the approach extends to audio-visual or other omni-modal settings. The method's MLLM-agnostic design and lack of model-specific heuristics make it a practical baseline for future video grounding research.
