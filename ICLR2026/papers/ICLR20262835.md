## Problem

Existing audio-visual benchmarks for evaluating Omni-LLMs suffer from three critical gaps: they lack strict audio-visual correlation (many questions are answerable from a single modality), they cover only a limited range of audio signal types, and they largely neglect multi-scene reasoning that is central to human video understanding. This makes it impossible to rigorously assess whether models truly integrate visual and auditory information across diverse scenes and audio categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262835/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmark item consists of a short-film video clip (average 97.2s, up to ~10 min for full-scene tasks) sampled at 32 frames, its full audio track, and a four-option multiple-choice question. The 2,853 questions span 15 tasks organized along three axes: five cognitive dimensions (temporal, spatial, emotion, plot, long-form), four audio signal types (speech, vocal traits, sound event, music), and three scene spans (single-, cross-, and full-scene). The output is a single letter (A–D) selected by the model, evaluated by accuracy.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-stage semi-automated pipeline that generates QA pairs with enforced strict audio-visual correlation. Stage 1 produces omni-modal captions: PySceneDetect splits videos into semantically consistent scenes, Qwen2.5-VL generates visual captions at 1 fps, Qwen2.5-Omni generates separate audio captions for vocal traits, sound events, and music (with post-processing to separate the latter two), and Whisper-v3 transcribes speech with timestamps. Stage 2 creates QA pairs using Qwen2.5 with task-specific templates, critically feeding only the modality descriptions from the designated scenes required by each task (e.g., video caption + vocal traits from one scene for Speaker Spatial Localization) to eliminate cross-modal leakage. Stage 3 applies a general-to-specific quality control: modality, format, content, and speculation checks followed by task-specific sequence, ambiguity, and audio-signal-type checks, then generates three plausible distractors. Human annotators verify the 3,974 auto-generated MCQs, retaining 2,853 (71.8%) after filtering for answer correctness, information accuracy, audio-visual dependency, and difficulty.

## Results

On JointAVBench (2,853 MCQs across 15 tasks), the best-performing Omni-LLM, Gemini 2.5-Pro, achieves only 65.3% average accuracy, while Qwen3-Omni (30B) follows at 63.6%. The best Video-LLM (InternVL-2.5, 8B) reaches 51.7% and the best Audio-LLM (Kimi-Audio, 7B) reaches 45.6%, confirming a substantial gap between joint audio-visual reasoning and single-modal performance. Performance degrades sharply on multi-scene tasks (e.g., MPO, PTG) and on tasks requiring vocal-traits understanding (SPL, SPER), where even the top model scores below 50%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among Omni-LLMs, Gemini 2.5-Pro (65.3%) leads by a clear margin over Qwen3-Omni (63.6%) and Gemini 2.5-Flash (58.0%), with the open-source Qwen2.5-Omni (7B) at 56.5% remaining competitive. All Omni-LLMs substantially outperform the best Video-LLM (InternVL-2.5 at 51.7%) and the best Audio-LLM (Kimi-Audio at 45.6%), confirming that native audio-visual fusion is critical. Notably, GPT-4o (45.0%), despite being a strong general-purpose model, underperforms even 7B Video-LLMs on this benchmark, likely because it receives only video frames without audio. Within the Omni-LLM group, early models like AVicuna (31.0%) and v-SALMONN (35.8%) lag far behind the top performers, highlighting rapid progress in the field. A key finding is that Omni-LLMs paradoxically underperform single-modal models on emotion-based tasks (SPER, MESI) and spatial tasks (SOOG, SOER), suggesting that additional modalities can introduce interference rather than complementary signal in these specific settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the audio-visual benchmarking space by unifying three dimensions that prior benchmarks each address only partially: strict audio-visual correlation (93.5% vs. 56–99% for existing datasets), multi-scene reasoning (single/cross/full), and diverse audio signal types (speech, vocal traits, sound events, music). It departs from earlier audio-visual QA datasets (AVQA, Music-AVQA, OmniBench, AV-Odyssey, WorldSense) that either lack rigorous correlation controls, focus on static images, or cover only 1–3 audio types. Methodologically, the semi-automated three-stage pipeline (omni-caption → QA synthesis → multi-level quality control) repurposes existing vision-LLMs, audio-LLMs, and general LLMs as annotation tools, reducing the manual cost that has limited prior benchmark scale and diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

JointAVBench provides the first systematic, multi-dimensional evaluation framework for Omni-LLMs' joint audio-visual reasoning, and its results (best model at 65.3%) expose concrete failure modes—vocal-traits alignment, cross-scene temporal reasoning, and modality interference on emotion/spatial tasks—that should guide future model development. The finding that Omni-LLMs underperform single-modal baselines on emotion and spatial tasks challenges the assumption that adding modalities monotonically improves performance, suggesting current architectures may suffer from cross-modal interference rather than true fusion. Open questions include whether the SF20K-only source introduces domain bias, whether the automatic pipeline's 71.8% retention rate hides subtle factual errors, and whether the 15-task taxonomy captures the full breadth of human audio-visual cognition.
