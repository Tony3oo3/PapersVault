## Problem

Existing next-token prediction (NTP) traffic simulators rely on supervised learning (behavior cloning or closed-loop SFT) to match logged data distributions, but their training objectives are not explicitly aligned with outcome-level evaluation metrics such as collision rate, off-road rate, and traffic light violations. These metrics are scalar, sparse, and non-differentiable, making them unsuitable as direct gradient-based losses, so purely imitative training cannot optimize them. The paper asks whether R1-style reinforcement fine-tuning, borrowed from large reasoning models, can bridge this gap for multi-agent traffic simulation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 10-step historical agent tracks, the current timestep state, and HD map polylines, all discretized into a shared motion-token vocabulary via K-disks clustering. It autoregressively generates 80-step (8-second at 10 Hz) joint future trajectories for up to 128 agents in a closed-loop rollout, where each step's predicted tokens become the next step's context. The output is a sequence of categorical token indices per agent per timestep, which are decoded back into continuous centroid coordinates and heading angles for evaluation under the WOSAC Realism Meta protocol.

## Method

SMART-R1 builds on the SMART-tiny (7M) autoregressive NTP backbone and introduces a three-stage post-training pipeline: (1) closed-loop SFT with CAT-K rollouts to mitigate covariate shift, (2) RFT via a proposed Metric-oriented Policy Optimization (MPO) algorithm, and (3) a second closed-loop SFT pass to restore distributional fidelity. MPO formulates simulation as an MDP over motion tokens and computes a simplified advantage A = r − α, where r is the Realism Meta score and α is an empirical threshold (0.77), avoiding the group-sampling bias of GRPO by exploiting the relatively predictable reward expectation in traffic simulation. A per-token KL penalty (β = 0.04) against a frozen reference model prevents the policy from drifting too far from the SFT-learned distribution. The iterative SFT-RFT-SFT schedule, inspired by DeepSeek-R1, balances metric-driven optimization with preservation of the logged-data distribution, yielding state-of-the-art results (Realism Meta 0.7858) on the 2025 WOSAC leaderboard.

## Results

On the 2025 Waymo Open Sim Agents Challenge (WOSAC), SMART-R1 achieves a state-of-the-art Realism Meta score of 0.7858, ranking first on the leaderboard at submission time. It also attains the best kinematic sub-metric score (0.4944) and the lowest minADE (1.2885 m) among all compared methods, while using only 7M parameters. The gains over the base SMART model (0.7814) and the closed-loop SFT variant CLSFT (0.7846) are driven primarily by improvements in safety-critical sub-metrics such as collision rate, off-road rate, and traffic light violations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMART-R1 outperforms all seven baselines on the primary Realism Meta metric, edging out the closest competitor TrajTok (0.7852) by 0.0006 and the prior best NTP-based method CLSFT (0.7846) by 0.0012. It also leads on kinematic metrics (0.4944 vs. 0.4931 for CLSFT) and minADE (1.2885 m vs. 1.3065 m for CLSFT), indicating superior motion fidelity and open-loop prediction accuracy. However, SMART-R1 trails RLFTSim (0.8128) and TrajTok (0.8116) on the interactive sub-metric, and falls slightly behind TrajTok on map-based metrics (0.9201 vs. 0.9207), suggesting that rule-based priors in TrajTok's tokenizer give it a marginal edge on map adherence. Against the base SMART model, SMART-R1 improves Realism Meta by 0.0044 and reduces minADE by 0.1046 m, all without adding parameters. The comparison with RLFTSim is particularly notable: both use 7M parameters and RL-based fine-tuning, yet SMART-R1's MPO algorithm and SFT-RFT-SFT pipeline yield a 0.0014 Realism Meta advantage, validating the proposed metric-oriented optimization over generic RL fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SMART next-token prediction architecture (Wu et al., 2024) and the CAT-K closed-loop SFT strategy (Zhang et al., 2025) by introducing an R1-style reinforcement fine-tuning stage directly into the traffic simulation training pipeline. The SFT-RFT-SFT iterative paradigm is borrowed from DeepSeek-R1's LLM post-training recipe, but the key methodological departure is the proposed Metric-oriented Policy Optimization (MPO), which replaces GRPO's group-relative advantage estimation with a simple threshold-based advantage (A = r − α), justified by the relatively predictable reward landscape in simulation. This positions the work at the intersection of LLM alignment techniques and autoregressive traffic modeling, where prior efforts (RLFTSim, Peng et al.) applied RL fine-tuning to driving but not within the NTP tokenized simulation framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly aligning a traffic simulator with evaluation metrics via RFT yields consistent gains on safety-critical sub-metrics (collision, off-road, traffic-light violation) that are inherently non-differentiable and thus inaccessible to pure supervised learning. However, the overall Realism Meta improvement over the strongest baselines is modest (0.7858 vs. 0.7852), and all experiments are conducted on a single 7M-parameter model trained on WOMD, leaving open whether the MPO strategy and SFT-RFT-SFT schedule transfer to larger architectures, other datasets, or more diverse driving environments. A practical limitation is that the reward signal is a single scalar (Realism Meta), so the approach may not generalize cleanly to multi-objective or preference-conditioned simulation settings without further reward engineering.
