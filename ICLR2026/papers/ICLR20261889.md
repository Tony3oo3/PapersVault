## Problem

Most hallucination detection work frames the task as binary (hallucinated or not), but real-world applications require identifying which specific spans in a generated response are unsupported. This paper asks whether explicit chain-of-thought reasoning, learned via reinforcement learning with span-level rewards, can effectively perform this fine-grained multi-step detection task, and whether in-domain reasoning is necessary compared to general-purpose reasoning models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261889/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a context (source document, question with retrieved passages, or structured JSON data) and a generated response as input, and produces a CoT reasoning trace followed by a JSON list of hallucinated text segments. Each predicted segment is mapped to start and end character positions in the response by string matching, and performance is measured by dataset-level span-F1 (character-level overlap between predicted and ground-truth span unions). The model is generative, directly outputting hallucinated text rather than performing token-level classification.

## Method

RL4HS trains an LLM (Qwen2.5-7B/14B-Instruct) using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a verifiable span-F1 reward that scores the overlap between predicted and ground-truth hallucination spans. The key technical contribution is Class-Aware Policy Optimization (CAPO), which addresses a reward imbalance where non-hallucination predictions systematically receive higher advantages because predicting an empty span list is trivially easy while precisely localizing hallucinated spans is hard. CAPO scales the normalized advantage for non-hallucination samples by a factor α=0.5, preventing the policy from exploiting this asymmetry (reward hacking) and achieving a better precision-recall balance. The model is trained directly from the instruct checkpoint without a prior SFT stage, using group size 16 and 5 epochs on the RAGTruth dataset.

## Results

On the RAGTruth benchmark, RL4HS-7B (with CAPO) achieves an average span-F1 of 55.9 (precision 62.9, recall 51.2), outperforming SFT-7B (50.1 F1) and all pretrained reasoning models. At 14B scale, RL4HS-14B reaches 58.3 average span-F1, surpassing the strongest proprietary model o3 (51.2 F1) by 7.1 points. The CAPO variant improves recall over vanilla GRPO (51.2 vs. 47.3) while maintaining comparable precision, yielding a 1.7-point F1 gain. On the PsiloQA dataset, RL4HS achieves 75.5 span-F1 versus 73.0 for SFT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RL4HS-7B (55.9 avg F1) outperforms SFT-7B (50.1) by 5.8 points and SFT-14B (55.4) by 0.5 points, demonstrating that RL with span-level rewards is more effective than supervised fine-tuning even at the same model size. Against proprietary reasoning models, RL4HS-7B surpasses o3 (51.2), GPT-5-mini (44.5), GPT-5 (42.2), and GPT-4o-mini (33.1) by 4.7–22.8 F1 points, despite being a 7B open model versus much larger proprietary systems. Among open reasoning models, RL4HS-7B beats Qwen3-14B (33.7) by 22.2 points and QwQ-32B (15.3) by 40.6 points, showing that in-domain RL training far exceeds general-purpose reasoning capability. Compared to the token-level Multi-View Attention detector (49.1 F1), RL4HS-7B gains 6.8 F1 points while also producing interpretable reasoning traces. The CAPO variant improves over vanilla GRPO (54.2) by 1.7 F1 points, primarily through a 3.9-point recall gain (51.2 vs. 47.3) that corrects the reward-hacking bias toward non-hallucination predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its RL backbone and on the hallucination span detection formulation from Wu et al. (2023) and Ogasa & Arase (2025), but departs from prior work in two key ways: it is the first to train a CoT reasoning model for span-level hallucination detection via RL with a span-F1 reward, and it introduces Class-Aware Policy Optimization (CAPO) to fix a reward-imbalance pathology specific to GRPO's group normalization. Unlike earlier CoT-for-hallucination-detection work (Luo et al., 2023; Eliav et al., 2025) that only explored prompting for binary detection, this paper targets the harder fine-grained span localization task and learns the reasoning process end-to-end rather than relying on prompt engineering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that RL with task-specific span-level rewards is not merely helpful but necessary for hallucination span detection, outperforming both SFT and general-purpose reasoning models (QwQ-32B, GPT-5, o3) by large margins. The identification of reward hacking in GRPO for asymmetric detection tasks and the CAPO fix are practically useful for anyone applying GRPO to imbalanced classification-like rewards. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the three RAGTruth task types, whether it scales to longer documents or multilingual settings, and whether the learned reasoning traces remain faithful under distribution shift.
