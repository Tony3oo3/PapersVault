## Problem

Current SpeechLLMs inherit the conventional SER paradigm of treating emotion understanding as a categorical classification task, which limits interpretability and leaves the expressive and reasoning capabilities of multimodal LLMs underutilized. Three specific gaps hinder progress: existing emotion corpora lack fine-grained acoustic annotations for reasoning supervision, foundation models exhibit weak prosody perception (the core carrier of emotional intent), and standard rule-based RL rewards (e.g., outcome accuracy alone) are insufficient to supervise intermediate reasoning quality, risking shortcut reward hacking.

## Input / Output

The model takes a raw speech audio clip as input and produces a structured two-part output: a chain-of-thought reasoning trace enclosed in <think> tags that integrates speaker traits (gender, age group), fine-grained prosodic cues (pitch level, energy, speech rate, word-level stress, intonation contour), and semantic content, followed by a final emotion label from nine categories (Neutral, Happy, Sad, Angry, Contempt/Disgust, Confused, Whisper, Surprise, Fear) in <answer> tags. The reasoning is expected to explicitly link each acoustic observation to its emotional significance rather than providing surface-level description.

## Method

EmotionThinker is a three-stage framework built on Qwen2.5-Omni-7B. Stage 1 constructs EmotionCoT-35K (~200 hours, 35K samples) via an automated pipeline that extracts prosodic features (pitch, energy, speed via DSP; stress via WhiStress; intonation contours via Savitzky-Golay smoothing) and speaker attributes (via wav2vec2.0 classifiers), then uses GPT-4o to generate step-wise CoT reasoning traces. Stage 2 builds EmotionThinker-Base through prosody-centric SFT (~500 hours) combining word-level stress perception, prosodic attribute classification, comparative prosodic augmentation (systematically modified pitch/energy/speed sequences), and cold-start CoT training, jointly optimizing the audio encoder, adapter, and LLM backbone. Stage 3 applies GRPO-PTR, which augments standard GRPO with a trained reasoning reward model (Qwen2.5-Omni-3B fine-tuned on 101,400 quality-graded tuples) that scores reasoning across four dimensions (factual alignment, interpretative quality, caption completeness, fluency), combined with a trustworthiness weight τ that computes group-level separation between mean reasoning rewards of correct vs. incorrect responses and exponentially down-weights the reasoning signal when this separation is negative, all under a progressive scheduling strategy that introduces the reasoning reward only after outcome accuracy stabilizes above a threshold.

## Results

EmotionThinker achieves a state-of-the-art average emotion recognition accuracy of 68.89% across four benchmarks (IEMOCAP 77.68%, MELD 59.71%, RAVDESS 71.56%, SAVEE 73.96%), surpassing the second-best model BLSP-Emo (65.41%) by approximately 3.5 percentage points. On reasoning quality, EmotionThinker attains the highest average score of 3.98/5 across four dimensions (Factual Alignment 3.54, Interpretative Quality 4.01, Caption Completeness 3.96, Fluency & Structure 4.42), outperforming the next-best model MiniCPM-O (3.01) by nearly a full point. Human evaluation on 100 samples confirms these rankings, with EmotionThinker scoring 4.4/5 versus 3.5/5 for Qwen2.5-Omni-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EmotionThinker outperforms all 16 baselines on both emotion accuracy and reasoning quality. In accuracy, it leads BLSP-Emo (65.41%) by 3.5 points and Kimi-Audio (58.83%) by 10 points on average; the gap is largest on IEMOCAP (77.68% vs. 76.00% for BLSP-Emo) and SAVEE (73.96% vs. 63.73% for BLSP-Emo). Notably, general-purpose models like Kimi-Audio and Qwen-Audio-Chat (54.86%) substantially outperform dedicated emotion-focused models SECap (32.64%) and OSUM-EChat (44.04%), highlighting the underdevelopment of emotion-specialized SpeechLLMs. On reasoning quality, EmotionThinker (3.98) surpasses the strongest baseline MiniCPM-O (3.01) by 0.97 points and its own backbone Qwen2.5-Omni-7B (2.87) by 1.11 points, with the largest margin on Caption Completeness (3.96 vs. 2.71 for MiniCPM-O). Human evaluation corroborates these rankings, confirming EmotionThinker's superiority in Factual Alignment (3.7 vs. 2.6 for Kimi-Audio) and Caption Completeness (4.7 vs. 3.0 for Qwen2.5-Omni).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO reinforcement learning paradigm from DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024) and DeepSeek-R1 into the speech emotion domain, where prior RL work has been largely unexplored. It departs from the dominant SER paradigm—treating emotion as a discrete classification label—by reformulating it as an open-ended reasoning task, and from prior explainable-SER efforts (SECap, OSUM-EChat, BLSP-Emo) that rely on supervised fine-tuning for caption generation, by introducing a dedicated RL stage with a trained reward model. The prosody-centric SFT stage builds on Qwen2.5-Omni-7B but addresses a specific gap: the backbone's near-chance-level performance on fine-grained prosodic perception tasks (pitch, stress, intonation), which the paper identifies as a prerequisite for reliable emotion reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EmotionThinker provides the first end-to-end framework that combines a prosody-aware CoT dataset (EmotionCoT-35K), a prosody-enhanced base model, and a trust-aware RL reward scheme, offering a reproducible recipe for explainable SER that goes beyond label prediction. The finding that a 7B omni model scores only ~25–30% on basic prosody perception tasks (vs. 60–75% after targeted SFT) highlights a fundamental perceptual gap in current SpeechLLMs that likely affects many paralinguistic tasks beyond emotion. Open questions include whether the GPT-4o-synthesized CoT annotations faithfully capture human perceptual reasoning, whether the 9-emotion taxonomy generalizes to continuous or culture-specific affect, and whether the GRPO-PTR trust mechanism scales to longer, multi-step reasoning chains without additional reward-model overhead.
