## Problem

Existing diffusion unlearning methods suppress not only the target harmful concept but also benign concepts that naturally co-occur with it in training data (e.g., erasing 'nudity' also suppresses 'person'). No prior work has formally identified this failure mode, provided a metric to measure it, or designed a training mechanism to prevent it, leaving a critical gap between robust erasure and faithful preservation of associated benign semantics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264169/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The unlearning pipeline takes 500 target-concept images (generated from a fixed prompt like 'A photo of a nude person') to automatically extract a CARE-set of benign co-occurring tokens, then fine-tunes a pre-trained Stable Diffusion v1.4 model. At inference, the unlearned model accepts standard text prompts and produces 512×512 RGB images, with the constraint that the erased concept is absent while CARE concepts (e.g., person, stars, freshwater) remain generable. Evaluation uses adversarial prompt attacks (UD, RAB, CCE) for robustness, COCO-30K for utility (FID, CLIP score), and a dedicated CARE score based on CLIP R-Precision@1 against 80 unrelated COCO tokens.

## Method

ReCARE constructs a CARE-set through two refinement stages: (1) global clustering projects candidate tokens (top-N frequent CLIP matches from target images) into 2D via t-SNE, clusters them with k-means, and discards the cluster most aligned with the target (harmful co-occurrences) and the most orthogonal cluster (irrelevant tokens); (2) intra-cluster refinement prunes individual tokens whose removal would reduce the cluster's concept-orthogonal residual, filtering out subtle target-aligned words like 'stripped' or 'body'. During training, the CARE-set serves dual roles: a Retain Loss constrains the unlearned model's noise predictions on CARE prompts to match the original model, while an Erase Loss uses STE textual-inversion embeddings to compute a harmful erase direction and trains the model to map harmful tokens toward (CARE representation minus erase direction), thereby disentangling the target from its benign co-occurrences. The overall objective is L_ReCARE = λ·L_Retain + L_Erase with λ=1.0, optimized via AdamW at lr 2×10⁻⁵ for ~28 minutes on a single H100.

## Results

ReCARE achieves the highest RATIO (normalized radar-chart area over Robustness, Utility, and CARE preservation) across all three unlearning tasks: 0.76 on Nudity, 0.81 on Van Gogh style, and 0.85 on Tench object. On the Nudity task, ReCARE attains CCE ASR of 11.14% (vs. 27.27% for AGE and 19.55% for STEREO) while maintaining a CARE score of 0.94 (vs. 0.79 for AGE and 0.11 for STEREO) and competitive utility (CLIP 0.3053, FID 13.85). The CARE score, a novel metric measuring retention of benign co-occurring concepts, shows that existing methods systematically suppress these concepts—e.g., STEREO drops to 0.11 on 'person' after nudity erasure—whereas ReCARE preserves them at 0.94.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReCARE outperforms all 11 baselines on the RATIO metric across all three tasks, with the largest margins over STEREO (0.76 vs. 0.21 on Nudity; 0.85 vs. 0.56 on Tench) and AdvUnlearn (0.76 vs. 0.18 on Nudity). STEREO achieves the strongest adversarial robustness (CCE ASR 19.55% Nudity, 4.00% Van Gogh, 3.60% Tench) but catastrophically fails CARE preservation (0.11, 0.31, 0.62) and degrades utility (FID 17.83–18.17), because its anchor-based erasure over-suppresses benign co-occurring concepts. AGE is the closest competitor on Nudity (RATIO 0.56) with good robustness (CCE 27.27%) and the best FID (11.25), but its CARE score (0.79) lags ReCARE (0.94) by 15 points, indicating it still partially erases 'person.' AdvUnlearn is the weakest overall: it is highly vulnerable to CCE attacks (65.45% ASR on Nudity) and shows the lowest CARE score among non-STEREO methods (0.36), confirming that its generic ImageNet/LLM anchors fail to protect contextually relevant co-occurring concepts. On the Tench task, ReCARE achieves near-perfect erasure (CCE 0.40%) while maintaining the highest CARE score (0.97), whereas methods like SalUn and EraseDiff preserve CARE well (0.96, 0.93) but fail at robustness (CCE 91.60%, 87.00%), illustrating the robustness–CARE trade-off that ReCARE resolves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the post-hoc concept erasure line for diffusion models (ESD, AdvUnlearn, AGE, STEREO) by identifying a previously unacknowledged failure mode: the collateral suppression of benign co-occurring concepts (CARE) that naturally appear alongside the erase target. Prior methods preserve utility via generic anchors (ImageNet labels, LLM-generated prompts, Oxford-3K), but these do not specifically protect the tightly entangled co-occurring tokens most vulnerable to erasure. ReCARE departs from this by automatically constructing a task-specific CARE-set from target images through CLIP-based extraction, t-SNE clustering, and intra-cluster refinement, then integrating it into both a Retain Loss and a CARE-aligned Erase Loss that borrows the textual-inversion-based STE procedure from STEREO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CARE score provides a principled, encoder-agnostic metric (validated at r=0.905 against human annotation) that is orthogonal to existing robustness and utility evaluations, filling a gap that could become a standard axis in unlearning benchmarks. The CARE-set construction is lightweight (1.78 min) and requires no additional data beyond the target images already used for textual inversion, making it practical to adopt. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video diffusion models, multi-concept simultaneous erasure, and whether the CLIP/SigLIP-based scoring inherits biases from the vision-language encoder. The paper also leaves open whether the entanglement in text-encoder embedding space (identified as the root cause) can be addressed at the encoder level rather than through post-hoc training objectives.
