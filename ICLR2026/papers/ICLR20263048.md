## Problem

Automated interpretability of LLMs currently relies on collecting maximally activating samples and passing them to an explainer LLM, making it heavily dependent on dataset quality, prompt design, and the reliability of the external LLM. Activation-based analysis also misses circuit-level interactions between features and struggles with context-dependent or polysemantic features that do not activate on stable token patterns. This paper addresses these gaps by developing methods that interpret features directly from model weights and circuit structure, reducing dependence on large datasets and external LLMs.

## Input / Output

WeightLens consumes only transcoder encoder/decoder weight vectors, the model's embedding and unembedding matrices, and attention head parameters—no dataset or explainer LLM is required. CircuitLens additionally takes text samples (24M–2.3B tokens from The Pile) and computes per-token attributions via forward/backward passes through the transcoder. Outputs are structured as token-based feature descriptions (WeightLens), circuit-level input patterns identifying which attention heads and tokens triggered activation, output patterns showing which generated tokens the feature influenced, and DBSCAN cluster assignments that decompose polysemantic features into monosemantic sub-concepts.

## Method

The paper builds on transcoder architectures, which decompose feature attributions into an input-dependent scalar activation and an input-invariant weight-based connectivity term (Eq. 1–5). WeightLens exploits the input-invariant component by projecting encoder vectors into embedding space and decoder vectors into logit space, extracting outlier tokens via z-score thresholds, tracing strong weight connections to earlier-layer features, and validating candidate tokens with a single forward pass—eliminating the need for any dataset or LLM. CircuitLens uses Jacobian-augmented attribution (Eq. 2, 4, 5) to identify which (attention head, token) pairs drive a feature's activation and which output tokens the feature influences, then masks inputs to isolate interpretable patterns. It applies inverse-frequency quantile sampling across the full activation distribution (rather than top-k only) and performs circuit-based clustering using Jaccard similarity over contributing feature/head sets with DBSCAN, enabling decomposition of polysemantic features into coherent sub-circuits that are individually described by an explainer LLM.

## Results

On Gemma-2-2B transcoder features evaluated via the FADE framework, WeightLens (WL) achieves Clarity scores of 0.65–0.80 across layers, outperforming Neuronpedia (0.28–0.61) and MaxAct* (0.33–0.62) in most layers, with the largest gap at layer 18 (0.80 vs. 0.58/0.62). The combined WL+CircuitLens-Full method reaches Clarity of 0.68 (layer 0), 0.66 (layer 15), and 0.65 (layer 23), consistently exceeding both baselines. However, activation-based methods retain higher Purity (e.g., MaxAct* 0.73–0.74 vs. WL 0.63–0.70 at layers 15–21), indicating that many features are context-dependent and not captured by weight-only analysis. Faithfulness remains uniformly low (<0.15) across all methods and layers, attributed to the transcoder architecture's high feature redundancy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Neuronpedia, WeightLens and WL+CircuitLens-Full consistently outperform on Clarity across nearly all layers (e.g., 0.80 vs. 0.58 at layer 18; 0.68 vs. 0.57 at layer 0), while Neuronpedia retains an advantage on Purity (e.g., 0.75 vs. 0.70 at layer 15), reflecting that activation-based descriptions capture more of the full activation distribution. Against MaxAct*, the pattern is similar: WL+CL-Full wins on Clarity in most layers (0.66 vs. 0.49 at layer 15; 0.65 vs. 0.54 at layer 23) but trails on Purity (0.74 vs. 0.73 at layer 15 is a near-tie, while at layer 0 it is 0.38 vs. 0.60). The CircuitLens variants sampled from the full activation distribution (small 24M-token dataset) remain competitive with and sometimes outperform baselines generated on a 23× larger dataset, demonstrating reduced dataset sensitivity. The (top) variants, which sample from the top of the activation distribution on the large dataset, achieve the strongest absolute scores (Clarity up to 0.82, Purity up to 0.84), confirming that dataset size still matters but that the circuit-based approach narrows the gap substantially. Faithfulness is uniformly low (<0.15) for all methods including baselines, which the authors attribute to transcoder architectural redundancy rather than a deficiency of the proposed methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the automated interpretability pipeline initiated by Bills et al. (2023) and refined by subsequent work (Choi et al., 2024; Puri et al., 2025; Gur-Arieh et al., 2025), which relies on collecting activating samples and prompting an explainer LLM. It builds directly on the transcoder architecture (Dunefsky et al., 2024; Ge et al., 2024) and its attribution decomposition into input-dependent and input-invariant components, as well as on circuit-tracing methods (Ameisen et al., 2025). The key departure is shifting from activation-centric analysis to weight-based (WeightLens) and circuit-structure-based (CircuitLens) interpretation, reducing or eliminating dependence on large datasets and explainer LLMs for a substantial fraction of features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that weight-based analysis alone can match or exceed activation-based baselines on Clarity and Responsiveness for up to 58.8% of features without any dataset or LLM, the paper offers a more scalable and robust path to automated interpretability. However, faithfulness scores remain uniformly low across all methods (including baselines), reflecting the architectural redundancy of transcoders and suggesting that single-feature steering is an inadequate evaluation paradigm. Open questions include extending these methods to SAEs and crosscoders, developing group-level or circuit-level faithfulness metrics, and optimizing clustering hyperparameters to balance polysemanticity capture against over-fragmentation.
