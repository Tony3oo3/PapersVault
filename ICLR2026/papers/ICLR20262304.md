## Problem

Batched LLM inference wastes significant computation on padding shorter sequences to match the longest in a batch, a problem exacerbated by long-tailed length distributions and made intractable in stochastic RL sampling where a single prompt yields multiple responses of widely varying lengths. Existing predictors rely on separate auxiliary models (e.g., DistilBERT) that incur deployment overhead, generalize poorly to long sequences and complex reasoning, and cannot handle the 'one-to-many' dynamic of RL sampling. No comprehensive benchmark covering long-sequence, Chain-of-Thought, and RL data existed for evaluating such predictors.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the main LLM's own final-layer hidden states (already computed during the prefill forward pass) along with per-token entropy values derived from the next-token probability distribution, requiring no separate model forward pass. For static prediction (EGTP), it outputs a single continuous length estimate computed as the expected value of a 20-bin soft probability distribution. For dynamic prediction (PLP), at each decoding step t it outputs the predicted remaining token count, with the input representation updated by concatenating the prompt feature vector with hidden states of all tokens generated so far. The entire predictor is a lightweight linear head consuming only 5–7 MB of VRAM.

## Method

The framework has two components. EGTP pools the prompt's hidden states using attention weights derived from per-token entropy (softmax over entropies scaled by a temperature parameter), motivated by an empirical finding that token entropy correlates positively (r=0.451) with gradient-based importance for length prediction. The pooled vector feeds a prediction head that produces a K=20 bin probability distribution, trained with a joint cross-entropy and MSE loss (λ=0.95) against a soft label distribution where target probability decays exponentially with bin distance, making the loss robust to the heavy-tailed length distribution while preserving distance awareness. PLP extends this to stochastic generation by re-running the same prediction head at every decoding step on a representation that concatenates the static prompt feature with the hidden states of all previously generated tokens, iteratively refining the remaining-length estimate and training by averaging the joint loss across all timesteps. This design eliminates the need for any auxiliary model, reusing activations the main LLM has already computed.

## Results

On the LMSYS benchmark, EGTP achieves the lowest MAE of 87.32 for GPT-4 and 68.33 for Claude-2, outperforming the next-best baselines by 9.1% and 11.3% respectively. On the more challenging ForeLen benchmark (Long-Sequence, Reasoning, and RL scenarios), EGTP reduces average MAE by 29.16% over the strongest baseline (TRAIL) and 55.09% over SSJF-Reg, with per-model improvements ranging from 24.4% (Qwen2.5-3B) to 35.7% (Llama3.2-3B). In end-to-end system evaluation with a length-aware SJF scheduler, EGTP reduces the padding ratio to 0.18 (Long Sequence) and 0.09 (Reasoning), yielding the highest throughput (131.05 and 194.21 tokens/s) and lowest average JCT (4.20s and 8.21s) among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EGTP outperforms all seven baselines across every model and scenario in the main comparison. The closest competitor is TRAIL (embedding-based MLP on Llama internals), which EGTP beats by 24–36% in average MAE on ForeLen; TRAIL is the only baseline that occasionally approaches EGTP on individual sub-tasks (e.g., Llama3.2-3B RL: 152.85 vs. 114.53). LTR-C (OPT-based ranking) is the second-strongest baseline on LMSYS (77.03 for Claude-2) but degrades sharply on ForeLen's long-sequence and RL scenarios. SSJF-Reg and SSJF-MC (fine-tuned BERT regression/classification) are consistently the weakest, with SSJF-MC reaching MAE >700 on long-sequence tasks. S3 (DistilBERT 10-bucket classification) and PiA (Vicuna self-prediction) show moderate performance but are outperformed by EGTP by 20–50% on ForeLen. TPV (linear progress regressor for DeepSeek-R1) is highly unstable, with MAE exceeding 700 on some Llama3.2-3B long-sequence samples, making it unreliable outside its narrow training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of training separate auxiliary predictors (DistilBERT, OPT, Vicuna) for LLM output length estimation by instead reusing the main model's own final-layer hidden states, eliminating the need for an additional forward pass. The entropy-guided pooling mechanism (EGTP) repurposes the next-token probability distribution's entropy as a soft attention signal over token positions, drawing on the observation that high-uncertainty tokens carry more predictive signal for generation length. The Progressive Length Prediction (PLP) component extends the task from a single static forecast to an autoregressive, step-by-step refinement, addressing the stochastic 'one-to-many' sampling regime in RL training that prior prompt-only predictors cannot handle. The soft-label-distribution regression loss is a novel training strategy that blends classification robustness with regression distance-awareness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ForeLen benchmark fills a genuine gap: prior length-prediction evaluations relied on LMSYS, which contains few long sequences and little complex reasoning, making it unclear whether predictors generalize to realistic workloads. The practical efficiency gains are significant for serving infrastructure—EGTP adds only ~0.7 ms and 5–7 MB VRAM while cutting padding ratios by roughly 3×, directly translating to throughput improvements in vLLM-based deployments. However, the PLP component's gains over static EGTP are modest (e.g., 95→81 MAE on RL), and the framework has only been validated on Qwen2.5 and Llama3.2 families; generalization to MoE architectures, multi-turn conversations, or very long-context models (>32k) remains untested. An open question is whether the entropy signal degrades as models grow larger or as reasoning chains become more structured, potentially reducing the discriminative power of the pooling weights.
