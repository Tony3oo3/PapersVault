## Problem

A significant performance gap persists between open-source web agents and proprietary deep-research systems (e.g., OpenAI DeepResearch), stemming from two fundamental bottlenecks: (1) training data constructed via linear or tree-like expansion lacks the cyclic, interdependent logical structures needed for sophisticated multi-step reasoning, and (2) real-world RL environments suffer from API volatility, latency, and inconsistent outputs that contaminate training signals and destabilize policy learning.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a complex, multi-faceted research question (e.g., identifying a company from a set of obfuscated financial, legal, and corporate clues) and iteratively invokes four tools—web search, page visitation, Google Scholar, and a Python interpreter—within a 128k context window and 100-tool-call budget. The output is a final answer with supporting citations and structured evidence, produced through the ReAct thought-action-observation loop. Evaluation uses pass@1 with LLM-as-judge scoring against ground-truth answers.

## Method

WebSailor-V2 introduces two core innovations. First, SailorFog-QA-V2 constructs densely interconnected knowledge graphs (avg. ~30 nodes, degree 2.5) with intentional cyclic structures and cut vertices, then samples subgraphs via random walks (verified non-isomorphic by Weisfeiler-Leman) and generates QA pairs with multiple uncertainty types—semantic ambiguity, distractor noise, and structural constraints—beyond simple obfuscation. Second, a symbiotic dual-environment RL framework decouples algorithmic iteration in a high-fidelity offline Wikipedia simulator (the 'wind tunnel') from final policy training in a managed real-world environment with a unified tool-execution interface providing QPS constraints, caching, retry protocols, and fault tolerance. Training proceeds in two stages: SFT cold-start on 30k+ synthetic trajectories from high-performing open-source models, followed by GRPO-based RL with token-level policy gradients, leave-one-out advantage estimation, and targeted negative-sample filtering to prevent format collapse. Built on Qwen3-30B-A3B (MoE) with 128k context, the agent achieves 35.3 on BrowseComp-EN and 30.6 on HLE, outperforming the 671B DeepSeek-V3.1.

## Results

WebSailor-V2-30B-A3B (RL) achieves 35.3 on BrowseComp-EN, 44.1 on BrowseComp-ZH, 73.7 on xbench-DeepSearch, 74.1 on GAIA, and 30.6 on HLE, setting new state-of-the-art results among all open-source web agents. Notably, the 30B-A3B MoE model outperforms the 671B DeepSeek-V3.1 on BrowseComp-EN (35.3 vs. 30.0), xbench-DeepSearch (73.7 vs. 71.2), GAIA (74.1 vs. 63.1), and HLE (30.6 vs. 29.8). On DeepResearch Bench, WebSailor-V2 scores 47.7, ranking second only to Gemini-2.5-pro-DeepResearch (49.7) among proprietary agents.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WebSailor-V2 decisively outperforms all open-source baselines across every benchmark: it leads GLM-4.5-355B by 8.9 points on BrowseComp-EN (35.3 vs. 26.4) and 9.4 on HLE (30.6 vs. 21.2), and surpasses DeepSeek-V3.1-671B on four of five benchmarks (BrowseComp-EN +5.3, xbench-DeepSearch +2.5, GAIA +11.0, HLE +0.8), though it trails on BrowseComp-ZH (44.1 vs. 49.2). Against proprietary agents, WebSailor-V2 beats OpenAI-o3 on xbench-DeepSearch (73.7 vs. 66.7), GAIA (74.1 vs. 70.5), and HLE (30.6 vs. 20.2), and surpasses OpenAI DeepResearch on BrowseComp-ZH (44.1 vs. 42.9), GAIA (74.1 vs. 67.4), and HLE (30.6 vs. 26.6), while remaining behind on BrowseComp-EN (35.3 vs. 51.5). The SFT-only checkpoint already exceeds several fully-trained open-source agents (e.g., 24.4 on BrowseComp-EN vs. WebSailor-72B's 12.0), underscoring the importance of the synthetic data pipeline. On DeepResearch Bench, WebSailor-V2 (47.7) ranks second to Gemini-2.5-pro-DeepResearch (49.7), with the gap attributed to report presentation rather than retrieval quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the direct successor to WebSailor (Li et al., 2025b), extending its SailorFog-QA graph-based data synthesis from tree-like/linear expansion to densely interconnected cyclic knowledge graphs, and introducing a wider taxonomy of uncertainty types (semantic ambiguity, distractor noise, structural constraints) beyond the obfuscation used in prior work like WebShaper, ASearcher, and WebExplorer. The RL component adapts GRPO with token-level policy gradients following DAPO and DeepSWE, but the paper's central methodological departure is the dual-environment training framework—using an offline Wikipedia-based simulator as an algorithmic 'wind tunnel' before final training in a managed real-world environment—a strategy borrowed from sim-to-real transfer in robotics but not previously applied to web-agent RL. The agent architecture deliberately remains the vanilla ReAct loop, explicitly rejecting more complex multi-agent or context-engineering paradigms in the spirit of Sutton's 'Bitter Lesson.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most consequential finding is that a 30B-A3B MoE agent, trained with the proposed pipeline, outperforms the 671B DeepSeek-V3.1 on BrowseComp-EN (35.3 vs. 30.0) and HLE (30.6 vs. 29.8), suggesting that data topology and training-environment stability can partially substitute for raw model scale in agentic tasks. The dual-environment RL framework addresses a widely acknowledged but under-addressed practical bottleneck: real-world API volatility corrupting on-policy training signals. Open questions include whether the gap to top proprietary systems (OpenAI DeepResearch at 51.5 on BrowseComp-EN) can be closed further, whether the dense-graph data strategy transfers to non-web domains, and whether the 'data > algorithm' claim holds under more controlled ablation. The paper also acknowledges that its DeepResearch Bench score (47.7) trails Gemini-2.5-pro-DeepResearch (49.7) due to report-quality rather than retrieval capability, pointing to a clear next frontier.
