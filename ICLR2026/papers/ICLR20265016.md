## Problem

Under matched compute, recursive transformers with fewer parameters consistently lag behind non-recursive counterparts, yet the root cause has not been clearly identified. This paper traces the gap to two architectural pathologies: undifferentiated computation, where the shared core is forced into a near-identical transformation at every iteration, and information overload, where a single hidden state must simultaneously preserve long-term context and serve as a high-plasticity workspace for transient computation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a text sequence of up to 4096 tokens (GPT-NeoX tokenizer, vocabulary 50,257) and produces next-token predictions via a standard language modeling objective. The key structural detail is that the memory buffer operates entirely within a single forward pass over one fixed-length input: it stores and retrieves intermediate hidden states generated across successive recursive iterations of that same sequence, rather than spanning multiple input segments or extending context length.

## Method

MeSH replaces the rigid additive state-passing of naive recursion with an explicit B-slot memory buffer and lightweight, step-wise read/write routers. At each iteration t, the core block's output is distributed across buffer slots via learned write weights (a single linear projection + softmax over slots), and the next hidden state is synthesized by a weighted read from the updated buffer; each iteration t has its own unique router parameters, breaking the symmetry that causes undifferentiated computation. The buffer is initialized with raw token embeddings in slot 0 (serving as a persistent anchor) and zeros elsewhere, with B = N_loop + 3 slots. This design decouples persistent memory (stored in the buffer) from transient computation (the hidden state), and the dynamic routing generalizes fixed heuristics like residual and anchor connections into a learnable, state-dependent composition of all historical core outputs.

## Results

On the Pythia suite (160M–1.4B), MeSH-enhanced recursive transformers (+mesh) consistently achieve the lowest perplexity and highest downstream accuracy among all recursive variants. At the 1.4B scale, +mesh (4+8R2+4, 33% fewer non-embedding params) reaches 7.39 Pile PPL, 15.84 Wiki PPL, 50.56% 0-shot, and 52.79% 5-shot average accuracy, surpassing the full 24-layer Vanilla model (7.44, 15.97, 49.50%, 51.93%) by +1.06% and +0.86% in 0-shot and 5-shot accuracy respectively. The advantage scales with model size: at 1B, +mesh achieves 7.90 Pile PPL and 47.53% 0-shot vs. Vanilla's 7.96 and 46.95%, and at 410M it reaches 9.09 Pile PPL and 44.12% 0-shot vs. Vanilla's 9.07 and 43.87%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the non-recursive Vanilla Pythia model with unique parameters per layer. Naive recursive (base) models consistently underperform Vanilla across all scales (e.g., at 1.4B: 48.89% vs. 49.50% 0-shot), confirming the performance gap from undifferentiated computation. Heuristic fixes (+residual, +anchor, +anchor*) partially close the gap but never match Vanilla on perplexity; at 1.4B, the best heuristic (+anchor*) reaches 49.29% 0-shot, still below Vanilla's 49.50%. MeSH (+mesh) is the only recursive variant that surpasses Vanilla at the 1.4B scale, achieving 50.56% 0-shot and 52.79% 5-shot (+1.06% and +0.86% over Vanilla) while using 33% fewer non-embedding parameters. At 1B, +mesh also beats Vanilla on both perplexity (7.90 vs. 7.96 Pile) and accuracy (47.53% vs. 46.95% 0-shot). The paper further shows that combining heuristics (e.g., +residual+anchor) degrades performance, and even learnable linear combinations of supplementary states fail to match MeSH's dynamic routing (9.17 vs. 9.09 Pile PPL at 410M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Prelude-Recurrent-Coda recursive transformer architecture (Geiping et al., 2025) and the broader line of looped/recursive transformers descending from Universal Transformers (Dehghani et al., 2018). It departs from prior parameter-sharing strategies (per-iteration LoRA, loop-index embeddings, MoE-in-loop) by keeping the core block purely weight-shared and instead externalizing functional specialization into a dynamic read-write memory buffer with step-wise routers. The mechanism is conceptually related to memory-augmented networks (Neural Turing Machines, Memformer) and dense connectivity (Hyper-connections, MUDDformer), but operates on a fundamentally different axis: managing intermediate hidden states across computational iterations within a single forward pass rather than extending context length or wiring layers together.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a quantified mechanistic diagnosis (via CKA similarity and singular value spectrum analysis) of why naive recursion underperforms, identifying information overload on the hidden state as the root cause of representational collapse. The MeSH solution is remarkably lightweight (~61K extra parameters at 1.4B scale) yet consistently closes the recursive-vs-vanilla gap and even surpasses the larger non-recursive baseline, suggesting that state management—not core capacity—is the binding constraint in recursive architectures. Open questions include whether the buffer-size heuristic (B = N_loop + 3) generalizes to much deeper loops, whether MeSH composes with adaptive computation time or mixture-of-recursions, and whether the approach retains its advantage at the 70B+ scale where communication overheads dominate.
