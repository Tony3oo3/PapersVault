## Problem

Relative over-generalization (RO) in cooperative MARL causes agents to become risk-averse and converge to sub-optimal joint policies, yet no prior work formally defines RO or provides a method that provably avoids it. Existing reasoning-based and credit-assignment approaches mitigate RO empirically but lack theoretical guarantees, leaving open whether RO can be provably eliminated and how to design such a method.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265013/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent receives the shared environment state s and a synchronized Gaussian noise vector ξ_i (generated via common PRNG seeds, serving as an Aumann correlation device rather than a communication channel). The output is a joint action u = (u_1, ..., u_N) produced by an autoregressive conditional factorization u_i = f_{ψ_i}(ξ_i; ξ_{C_i}, s) with strictly nested coordination sets C_i = {1,...,i}, so that a single forward pass per agent yields the negotiated agreement without iterative inner-loop optimization at execution time.

## Method

SVNR introduces a Negotiated Reasoning framework where agents iteratively perturb their action beliefs via negotiation policies f_i derived from Message-Passing Stein Variational Gradient Descent (MPSVGD), which provides the steepest-descent direction in an RKHS to minimize KL divergence between the perceived and optimal joint policy. A strictly nested negotiation topology (C_i = {1,...,i}) guarantees that the iterative particle updates converge to the identity map and the perceived joint policy matches the Boltzmann-optimal policy, satisfying the paper's 'consistent reasoning' condition that provably eliminates both Perceived RO (training-time) and Executed RO (execution-time, via α→0 annealing). The full algorithm is embedded in maximum-entropy policy iteration: a soft Bellman operator evaluates the joint policy, and the SVGD-based update improves it monotonically. For practical deployment, Amortized MPSVGD parameterizes the negotiation with neural networks that distill the multi-round negotiation dynamics into their weights, reducing inference to a single forward pass (K=1) while preserving the theoretical convergence guarantees up to a bounded approximation error.

## Results

On the RO-challenged Max of Three differential game, SVNR achieves near-optimal returns (9.60±0.30, 9.64±0.17, 9.71±0.20) across all coverage settings (s₂ = 3.0, 2.0, 1.5), while baselines collapse as the reward basin narrows (e.g., MASQL drops from 8.92 to −0.34; MMQ from 9.54 to −0.07). On Particle Gather, SVNR attains 4.76±0.20, the only method achieving positive coordinated returns, while all baselines converge to zero or negative values. On MaMuJoCo, SVNR reports the highest returns in 3 of 4 tasks (e.g., 1678±275 on Walker2d-2x3 vs. 1422±79 for PR2; 536±31 on Ant-2x4 vs. 354±58 for PR2), with the HalfCheetah-2x3 entry (853±212) appearing inconsistent with the paper's stated claim of superiority across all tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SVNR decisively outperforms all reasoning-based RO-targeting baselines (MADDPG, MASQL, PR2, ROMMEO, MMQ) on the RO-challenged tasks: at the hardest setting (s₂=1.5), SVNR scores 9.71 while the next-best (MMQ) is at −0.07, a gap of nearly 10 reward units. The advantage is most dramatic as the reward basin narrows—MASQL and MMQ, which are competitive at s₂=3.0 (8.92 and 9.54 respectively), collapse to negative returns by s₂=2.0, whereas SVNR remains stable. On Particle Gather, SVNR is the only method to achieve positive returns (4.76), while PR2 and MADDPG stall at 0.00 and MASQL/ROMMEO/MMQ go negative, indicating failure to coordinate simultaneous landmark arrival. On MaMuJoCo, SVNR beats PR2, ROMMEO, and all value-decomposition baselines (MAPPO, QMIX, FACMAC) on HalfCheetah-1p1, Ant-2x4, and Walker2d-2x3, with margins of 42–256 reward units over the strongest competitor; the HalfCheetah-2x3 result (853 vs. PR2's 8662) appears to be a reporting anomaly given the paper's stated claim of universal superiority. General-purpose baselines (MAPPO, QMIX, FACMAC) perform poorly on RO-challenged tasks (all below 3.0 at s₂=3.0), confirming that standard value-decomposition lacks explicit mechanisms to prevent PRO/ERO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of variational inference (specifically MPSVGD for graphical model inference) and maximum-entropy MARL, repurposing Stein variational gradient descent as a mechanism for inter-agent negotiation rather than Bayesian posterior approximation. It departs from prior reasoning-based MARL methods (PR2, ROMMEO, MASQL) that address relative over-generalization empirically through recursive or opponent-modeling heuristics, by introducing a formal theoretical framework (PRO, ERO, consistent reasoning) and proving that RO can be provably eliminated under a consistent reasoning condition. The maximum-entropy policy iteration backbone is inherited from Soft Q-Learning, and the practical amortized implementation follows the amortized SVGD paradigm, but the strict nested negotiation topology and the proof that it yields exact joint-policy representability (via the Gelman–Speed conditional theorem) are new contributions that distinguish SVNR from both credit-assignment and reasoning-based RO mitigations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first MARL method with a formal proof of RO-freedom, providing the field with a rigorous diagnostic (PRO/ERO decomposition) and a constructive recipe (negotiated reasoning with SVGD) that prior heuristic approaches lacked. The theoretical framework reframes RO as a variational bias (training) plus a factorization loss (execution), which could inform the design of future coordination mechanisms beyond SVGD. Limitations include the CTDE assumption, the need for synchronized pseudo-random noise at execution (a correlation device rather than communication), and proofs that are cleanest for discrete action spaces (with continuous extensions deferred to appendices). Open questions include whether the guarantees hold under partial observability, how the method scales to large teams with non-nested topologies, and whether the amortized approximation preserves the provable RO-free property in practice.
