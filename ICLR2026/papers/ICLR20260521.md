## Problem

Existing time-series anomaly detection methods—whether prediction-based (monitoring forecast errors) or distance-based (tracking embedding shifts)—primarily capture sudden, instantaneous stress at individual timesteps rather than modeling how stresses accumulate over time toward a critical transition. This limitation is often masked by window-level evaluation but causes substantial performance degradation under stricter point-wise protocols that demand precise localization at every timestep. FOLD addresses this gap by reframing anomaly detection as tracking a system's proximity to a tipping point via fold-bifurcation dynamics, unifying both gradual drifts and abrupt spikes within a single mechanistic framework.

## Input / Output

FOLD takes a time series window X ∈ R^{L×d} and a frozen forecasting model f_θ (trained on normal data only) as input. It produces a continuous risk-state trajectory z(t) ∈ R^{L×d} (aggregated to z_sys(t) ∈ R^L for multivariate settings) and a binary point-wise anomaly mask ŷ(t) = 1{z_sys(t) > Z_thr} at each timestep. Notably, no anomaly labels or additional detector training are required; the detection threshold Z_thr is calibrated solely from the high quantile of risk maxima observed on normal training sequences, and the ODE coefficients (α, β, γ) are fixed hyperparameters selected from data statistics rather than learned.

## Method

FOLD computes a time-varying stress signal S(t) by masking N patches in the input, feeding each perturbed sequence through the frozen forecaster with MC dropout, and combining a sensitivity term (distance between baseline and perturbed predictions) with an uncertainty term (variance difference across stochastic forward passes). This stress signal drives a fold-bifurcation inspired ODE: dz/dt = αS(t) − βz² − γz, where α scales stress injection, γ provides resilience (pulling the system back toward stability), and β induces nonlinear escalation that amplifies accumulated risk near a tipping point. The ODE is solved numerically (e.g., Runge-Kutta) to produce the risk trajectory, and anomalies are flagged when z(t) exceeds a threshold calibrated as a high quantile of normal-data risk maxima. The key theoretical insight is that the same ODE naturally handles both anomaly types through time-scale separation: under slow-varying stress (gradual drifts), the system tracks a moving equilibrium and triggers via saddle-node bifurcation, while under impulsive stress (abrupt spikes), the forcing term dominates and z jumps proportionally to the accumulated spike energy. This makes FOLD a training-free, parameter-light extension of any pre-trained forecaster, with linear O(L) complexity and minimal memory footprint.

## Results

On the TSB-AD benchmark (40 datasets, 1,070 time series), FOLD achieves the best average VUS-PR rank of 3.86 across 23 univariate datasets and 3.11 across 17 multivariate datasets, substantially outperforming the runner-up TSPulse (rank 8.65 univariate, 9.88 multivariate). Under the stricter point-wise F1 protocol, FOLD(DLinear) reaches 0.6937 on SMAP, 0.8112 on SWaT, and 0.4655 on NAB, improving over the strongest baseline by 170.1% and 216.4% respectively. Integrating FOLD with the Chronos foundation model in a zero-shot setting yields an even superior univariate rank of 2.95, demonstrating the framework's model-agnostic nature.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOLD dominates across all 34 baselines on the primary VUS-PR metric: it achieves average rank 3.86 (univariate) vs. TSPulse at 8.65, CNN at 7.52, and OmniAnomaly at 11.17 (multivariate). On point-wise F1, FOLD(DLinear) outperforms the strongest point-wise baseline NRDetector by large margins—0.6937 vs. 0.2568 on SMAP (+170%) and 0.4655 vs. 0.0539 on NAB (+216%)—while also surpassing window-based methods like TranAD (0.1540 on SMAP) and LSTM-AD (0.1706 on SMAP). On SWaT, FOLD(DLinear) reaches 0.8112 F1, beating OmniAnomaly (0.7779) and MTAD-GAT (0.7570), which are the strongest non-FOLD methods on that dataset. Notably, FOLD's performance is backbone-agnostic: DLinear, PatchTST, and TimeMixer all achieve competitive F1 scores, and the zero-shot Chronos variant achieves the best univariate rank (2.95), confirming that the fold-bifurcation ODE mechanism—not forecaster sophistication—drives detection quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

FOLD departs from the two dominant paradigms in time-series anomaly detection—prediction-error monitoring and embedding-distance scoring—by reframing detection as tracking a system's proximity to a critical transition via a fold-bifurcation ODE. It repurposes bifurcation theory from ecology and climate science (Scheffer 2009; Williamson & Lenton 2015) and uncertainty estimation from Bayesian deep learning (Gal & Ghahramani 2016; Lakshminarayanan et al. 2017) into a unified stress-accumulation mechanism, where forecasting-model-derived sensitivity and uncertainty signals serve as the time-varying control parameter rather than direct anomaly scores. Unlike prior methods that flag instantaneous deviations, FOLD models how small stresses compound into a tipping point, unifying sudden spikes and gradual drifts under a single dynamical framework without requiring a separately trained detector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The emphasis on strict point-wise evaluation (no dilation, no window-level credit) exposes a gap that window-level metrics mask, and FOLD's training-free, linear-complexity design makes it practical for real-time industrial deployment. However, the threshold calibration is sensitive to contamination (F1 drops ~53% at 10% anomalous calibration data), and the ODE coefficients, while robust over moderate ranges, are still hand-set rather than data-adaptive. Open questions include extending to streaming settings with concept drift, adapting the ODE parameters automatically from normal data statistics, and whether the fold-bifurcation form is truly optimal compared to other bifurcation types for specific anomaly morphologies.
