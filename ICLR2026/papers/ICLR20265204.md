## Problem

LLMs suffer from proactive interference, where irrelevant information in earlier context positions disrupts reasoning and memory recall on long-context tasks. Existing solutions—expanding context windows, external memory systems, and RAG—address information capacity but not the fundamental inability of LLMs to actively curate their internal working memory during a single reasoning session, leaving them passively overwhelmed by accumulated contextual noise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265204/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a long-context conversation (up to 128K tokens) with a task query, and the LLM performs multi-step tool calls (capped at 20 per turn) to reorganize its visible context before emitting a final answer. Read-only tools (search_context, get_search_detail) preserve KV-cache prefix relationships for efficient inference, while context-modifying tools (fold, summarize, fragment) break the prefix but are fully reversible via restore_fragment, guaranteeing no information is permanently lost and the framework degrades gracefully to baseline behavior if no tools are invoked.

## Method

Sculptor equips LLMs with six deterministic, self-contained tools in three categories: context fragmentation (segmenting into 6-character-ID-tagged pieces), compression/restoration (summarize, fold, restore), and precise search (exact keyword matching with configurable context windows). The key training innovation is a dynamic context-aware GSPO approach that handles the non-monotonic context evolution caused by tool calls: conditional trajectory collection creates separate training instances at each context-modification point, while incremental loss assignment ensures each completion receives gradient signal exactly once across all trajectory snapshots, preventing training collapse from redundant learning. This is preceded by SFT on tool-usage trajectories collected via prompt-engineered frontier models (Claude-4-Sonnet) on BABILong and GSM-Infinite, followed by RL with a reward that gives +1 for correct answers, −1 for format errors or exceeding 20 tool calls / 128K tokens, and 0 otherwise, enabling the policy to autonomously discover optimal compression-and-restore strategies.

## Results

On the M3 (13B) model, Sculptor-M3-RL achieves 99.4% on PI-LLM, 84.8% on NeedleBench-M-RS, 85.7% on MRCR, and a 73.8% normalized average across five long-context benchmarks, compared to 39.4% for the unmodified baseline. On the open-source GLM-4.5-air model, Sculptor-GLM-4.5-air-RL reaches 86.0% PI-LLM, 84.0% NeedleBench, 99.0% MRCR, and a 79.8% normalized average versus 44.0% baseline. In zero-shot settings with frontier models, ACM tools yield +27.0 points on NeedleBench for Claude-4-Sonnet and +23.0 for GPT-4.1, with PI-LLM gains of +5.90 and +6.58 respectively. Sculptor also achieves dramatic context reductions: 89.0% on PI-LLM (72.4K→8.0K tokens) and 76.2% on NeedleBench (85.2K→20.3K tokens).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sculpor-M3-RL decisively outperforms both Mem0 and MemAgent across all five benchmarks. On the M3 model, Sculptor-M3-RL achieves 73.8% normalized average versus 29.8% for Mem0 and 35.7% for MemAgent; on GLM-4.5-air, the gap widens to 79.8% vs. 26.1% (Mem0) and 29.9% (MemAgent). Critically, both Mem0 and MemAgent actually underperform their respective full-attention baselines (39.4% and 44.0%), whereas Sculptor with RL surpasses the baseline on every benchmark. The paper attributes Mem0's weakness to its cross-session design being mismatched to single-session long-context tasks, and MemAgent's to irreversible query-dependent memory accumulation that discards information needed for multi-hop reasoning. RAG baselines (BM25 and Qwen3-Emb) perform even worse than Mem0/MemAgent, dropping to 19.3–24.2% average, confirming that irreversible retrieval-based filtering is fundamentally inferior to reversible context management. The only benchmark where Sculptor-RL does not clearly dominate is LongBenchV2 (34.5% for M3-RL vs. 33.0% baseline), which the authors acknowledge as a remaining challenge.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sculptor carves a distinct niche between two well-studied lines of work: external memory systems (Mem0, MemAgent, MemGPT) that store and retrieve information outside the model's context, and context compression methods (LLMLingua, SnapKV, Scissorhands) that apply fixed or externally-driven heuristics to reduce token counts. The paper departs from both by making context-management decisions internal to the LLM's reasoning loop via a suite of reversible tools (fragment, fold, summarize, restore, search), drawing conceptual inspiration from human working-memory strategies and from the proactive-interference diagnosis of Wang & Sun (2025). On the training side, it adapts GSPO (Zheng et al., 2025) with a novel conditional-trajectory-collection and incremental-loss-assignment mechanism specifically designed for the non-monotonic context evolution that context-modifying tools introduce, a setting absent from standard multi-step RL formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides empirical evidence that irreversible filtering (RAG, external memory) can actually degrade performance below the full-attention baseline, and that even well-pretrained attention mechanisms do not naturally suppress irrelevant content—93.5% of key-value pairs show improved attention after explicit folding. This reframes the long-context problem from one of capacity to one of active curation, suggesting that explicit, reversible context-control tools may be a more robust path than ever-larger token windows. Open questions remain: results on LongBenchV2 and FRAMES are still modest, the approach requires dedicated RL training infrastructure (128 H800 GPUs), and generalization beyond long-context retrieval to domains like mathematical reasoning or coding is left as future work.
