## Problem

Sampling from diffusion and flow models requires costly numerical integration of the probability flow ODE, motivating methods that learn the flow map directly. However, existing consistency and map-matching approaches suffer from practical and theoretical limitations: they require explicit model inverses, backpropagation through nested model evaluations, adversarial training, or fail to prove that the true flow map is a stationary point of the loss. SGFlow addresses all of these simultaneously with a new training objective.

## Input / Output

The model is a two-time map f(t, u, x) parameterized as x + (u−t) f̃_θ(t, u, x), which maps an interpolant sample X_t = α_t X_0 + σ_t X_1 (with α_t = 1−t, σ_t = t) forward to time u along the probability flow ODE. At inference, starting from Gaussian noise at t=0, the map is evaluated with any number of intermediate steps to produce a 32×32 RGB image on CIFAR-10. The same network also outputs the implied velocity via −∂_t f(t,t,·), enabling both flow-map-based sampling and velocity-based integration from a single model.

## Method

SGFlow enforces the flow-map PDE ∂_t f + (∂_x f) v = 0 by squaring the residual and taking expectations over interpolant samples, but replaces the unknown velocity v with stopgrad[f̃_θ(t,t,x)]—the model's own velocity estimate at the diagonal t=u. This stopgrad placement creates non-conservative (nongradient) optimization dynamics: the update field has nonzero curl and cannot be derived from any single scalar objective, yet Theorem 1 proves the true flow map remains the unique stationary point. Both loss terms are computed as squared norms of Jacobian-vector products via forward-mode autodifferentiation, avoiding explicit Jacobian materialization and nested model calls. The parameterization f = x + (u−t)f̃ ensures that at t=u the loss reduces to standard flow matching, so the velocity is learned from scratch without a teacher model, while the t≠u terms enforce the full two-time flow-map structure.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (unconditional, 50K EMA samples after 200K training steps), SGFlow achieves FID of 12.26 at 10 sampling steps, 2.88 at 50 steps, and 2.81 at 100 steps — the best FID at every step count among all compared methods. SGFlow outperforms MeanFlow (the closest competitor) by a wide margin at 10 steps (12.26 vs. 37.32) and maintains a consistent advantage at 50 and 100 steps (2.88 vs. 4.54; 2.81 vs. 4.23). In terms of GPU memory, SGFlow uses 43.2 Gb during backward pass, sitting between MeanFlow (14.2 Gb) and the map-matching losses (54–70 Gb).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGFlow outperforms all four baseline families on CIFAR-10 FID at every tested step count. Against MeanFlow (Geng et al., 2025), the closest competitor, SGFlow achieves substantially lower FID: 12.26 vs. 37.32 at 10 steps, 2.88 vs. 4.54 at 50 steps, and 2.81 vs. 4.23 at 100 steps; the paper attributes this to SGFlow's differentiated loss terms that train model derivatives toward the true flow map, whereas MeanFlow stopgrads all derivatives. Against Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2022), SGFlow wins at all step counts (12.26 vs. 24.87 at 10 steps; 2.88 vs. 3.53 at 50 steps; 2.81 vs. 3.05 at 100 steps), confirming that learning the flow map directly yields better few-step sampling than integrating the velocity field. The Lagrangian, Eulerian, and Progressive map-matching losses from Boffi et al. (2024, 2025) perform far worse (FID 39–337), likely due to their reliance on nested model evaluations or product-rule expansions that complicate optimization. SGFlow also has a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) that the true flow map is the unique stationary point, a property MeanFlow lacks and the map-matching methods share but at higher computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

SGFlow sits at the intersection of flow matching, consistency models, and flow map matching, inheriting the two-time map parameterization f_θ = x + (u−t)f̃_θ from MeanFlow and the PDE characterization of flow maps from Flow Map Matching. Its key departure is the placement of stopgrad: rather than detaching all model derivatives (as MeanFlow does) or requiring model inverses (as Lagrangian map matching does), SGFlow stops only the velocity estimate f̃_θ(t,t,x) while still backpropagating through the Jacobian-vector products in the loss. This yields a provable stationary-point guarantee (Theorem 1) that MeanFlow lacks, while avoiding the nested model evaluations that make Lagrangian and Progressive losses memory-intensive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical guarantee that the true flow map is the unique stationary point—without requiring invertibility constraints, adversarial training, or model inverses—gives practitioners a principled loss for few-step flow map learning with a clear FID-vs-steps trade-off. However, the evaluation is limited to CIFAR-10 with a single training configuration, and the 3× memory overhead over MeanFlow (43.2 Gb vs 14.2 Gb) raises practical concerns for scaling to larger models or datasets. Open questions include whether the non-conservative optimization dynamics (formally a two-player game) help or hinder convergence in practice, whether purpose-built architectures for flow maps (rather than repurposed diffusion U-Nets) close the remaining gap to flow matching at high step counts, and whether the method extends to conditional or high-resolution generation.
