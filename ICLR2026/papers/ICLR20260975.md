## Problem

InfoNCE, the dominant objective in contrastive representation learning, is widely used as a mutual information estimator but is not one: it is a variational lower bound of a K-way Jensen-Shannon divergence, not the KL divergence underlying MI, and its learned critic only recovers the density ratio p(x,y)/(p(x)p(y)) up to an arbitrary function C(y), precluding use in a plug-in estimator. This paper provides a sharp characterization of the gap between InfoNCE and true MI, and introduces a minimal modification that resolves the identifiability issue.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a minibatch of B paired samples (x_b, y_b) from the joint p(x,y), treating each (x_b, y_b) as a positive and the remaining B−1 x-values as negatives drawn from the marginal p(x). The anchor variant additionally introduces a synthetic 'all-negative' class where all K samples come from p(x), weighted by a hyperparameter ν (default ν=1). The trained critic c_θ(x,y) = log r_θ(x,y) directly estimates the log-density ratio, and MI is computed as the plug-in estimate E_{p̂(x,y)}[c_θ(x,y)] in bits. For the protein interaction task, inputs are pretrained ProtTrans embeddings of kinase–target and ligand–receptor pairs, and the output is a PMI score thresholded to predict interaction.

## Method

InfoNCE-anchor augments the standard K-way InfoNCE classification with an anchor class (class 0) in which all K samples are drawn from the noise distribution q_0, with prior weight ν/(K+ν). This single addition makes the MLE objective Fisher-consistent: the global minimizer of the critic r_θ(x) exactly equals q_1(x)/q_0(x) (rather than C·q_1(x)/q_0(x) as in vanilla InfoNCE), enabling a consistent plug-in MI estimator that retains InfoNCE's low variance while eliminating its systematic bias. The paper further unifies the framework via proper scoring rules from statistical decision theory, showing that InfoNCE-anchor is the log-score instance, that NCE (K=1, ν>0) and ranking NCE (K=2, ν=0) are special cases, and that the objective monotonically interpolates between the DV and NWJ bounds as K→∞. Empirically, the log-score variant achieves state-of-the-art MI estimation across Gaussian, MNIST, and text benchmarks, yet the anchor does not improve downstream linear-probing accuracy in self-supervised representation learning, indicating that contrastive learning benefits from structured density-ratio geometry rather than accurate MI magnitude.

## Results

On the protein interaction prediction task (OmniPath database), InfoNCE-anchor achieves AUROC of 0.80±0.06 on Kinase and 0.97±0.01 on Ligand benchmarks, outperforming all baselines. On the MI estimation benchmark (Gaussian, MNIST, IMDB texts), InfoNCE-anchor consistently achieves the lowest bias with comparable variance across all three domains. In self-supervised representation learning on CIFAR-100 (ResNet-18, linear probing), InfoNCE-anchor achieves 65.74% top-1 accuracy, slightly below standard InfoNCE (65.98%), confirming that the anchor does not improve downstream SSL performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfoNCE-anchor wins on all protein interaction prediction comparisons: it outperforms LMI (0.74→0.80 Kinase, 0.87→0.97 Ligand), χ²/DRF (0.76→0.80, 0.92→0.97), JS (0.77→0.80, 0.95→0.97), and Spherical (0.73→0.80, 0.87→0.97) in AUROC. On the MI estimation benchmark (Figure 1), InfoNCE-anchor achieves the lowest bias across Gaussian, MNIST, and text domains, while JS_plugin (equivalent to K=1, ν=1) performs comparably on Gaussians but deteriorates on higher-dimensional tasks. In SSL on CIFAR-100, InfoNCE-anchor (65.74% top-1) is essentially tied with standard InfoNCE (65.98%), while JS (61.69%) and χ² (65.59%) are slightly worse, and Spherical collapses entirely (4.33%). The paper also shows that α-InfoNCE's claimed tightness bound is invalid (Proposition 8), and that MLInfoNCE cannot be derived from a proper classification setup, positioning InfoNCE-anchor as the principled fix.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends InfoNCE (van den Oord et al., 2018) by adding a single anchor class to the classification formulation, and unifies it with NCE (Gutmann & Hyvärinen, 2012) under a proper scoring rule framework. It departs from the standard interpretation of InfoNCE as a variational lower bound on KL divergence (and hence MI), showing instead that it bounds a K-way Jensen-Shannon divergence and can never equal the KL divergence for any finite batch size. The proper scoring rule generalization subsumes NCE, InfoNCE, f-DIME, and related objectives as special cases, while also correcting a proof error in Lee & Shin (2022) regarding α-InfoNCE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing ambiguity in the contrastive learning literature by showing that InfoNCE's critic estimates the density ratio only up to an arbitrary function C(y), making it unsuitable for plug-in MI estimation. The key negative result—that fixing this identifiability issue via the anchor does not improve self-supervised representation quality (CKA ≈ 0.88 between InfoNCE and InfoNCE-anchor features)—challenges the 'maximize MI' narrative and suggests contrastive learning benefits from structured density ratios rather than accurate MI values. Open questions include why the log score succeeds while other Fisher-consistent scoring rules (e.g., spherical) collapse in practice, and whether the optimization landscape differences can be characterized more precisely.
