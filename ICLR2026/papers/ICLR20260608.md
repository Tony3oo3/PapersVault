## Problem

Vector quantization in deep models uses hard nearest-neighbor assignments that are non-differentiable, blocking gradient flow to encoders and codebooks during backpropagation. Existing remedies (STE, EMA, Rotation Trick, Gumbel-Softmax, NSVQ) each introduce auxiliary losses, hyperparameter schedules, train-test mismatch, biased gradients, or codebook collapse. The paper asks whether one can make VQ differentiable while preserving exact hard assignments in the forward pass, without any of these compromises.

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous latent vector z ∈ ℝ^D produced by an encoder (e.g., 512-dim embeddings on a 16×16 spatial grid for 256×256 images in VQ-VAE, or 512-dim frame-level vectors in speech coding) and outputs a differentiable quantized vector z_q that, in the small-variance limit, equals the nearest codeword. During inference, standard hard argmin VQ is used for DiVeQ, while SF-DiVeQ maps to the nearest point on a space-filling curve of line segments connecting codewords. The codebook size ranges from 2^4 to 2^13 codewords depending on the task.

## Method

DiVeQ reparameterizes the quantization error by defining a directional noise v_d = v + (c_i* − z) with v ~ N(0, σ²I), then computing z_q = z + ‖c_i* − z‖₂ · sg[v_d / ‖v_d‖₂], where sg[·] is stop-gradient. As σ² → 0, z_q converges exactly to c_i*, preserving hard assignments, while gradients flow to both z and c_i* through the un-stopped norm term. SF-DiVeQ extends this by quantizing along the line segment between two neighboring codewords c_i* and c_{i*+1} using a random interpolation factor λ ~ U(0,1), producing z_q as a weighted sum of two directional error terms. This space-filling construction pulls codeword connections inside the latent distribution, ensuring full codebook utilization and preventing codebook-latent misalignment without any codebook replacement heuristics, auxiliary losses, or temperature schedules.

## Results

On VQ-VAE image compression (AFHQ, 11-bit codebook), DiVeQ and SF-DiVeQ achieve the highest SSIM and PSNR and lowest LPIPS across all codebook sizes (4–11 bits), with the gap over STE and ST-GS growing as codebook size increases. On VQGAN generation (CELEBA-HQ, 8-bit codebook, HP1), SF-DiVeQ achieves FID 6.24 vs. STE 6.64, EMA 6.86, RT 9.32, ST-GS 8.47, and NSVQ 81.5. On DAC speech coding (VCTK, 12-bit codebook, batch size 64), SF-DiVeQ achieves the best LSD (1.01), MFCC distance (68.3), PESQ (1.62), and STOI (0.85), while STE, EMA, and ST-GS exhibit codebook-latent misalignment in several configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiVeQ and SF-DiVeQ consistently outperform all five baselines (STE, EMA, RT, ST-GS, NSVQ) across all three tasks and all codebook sizes. Against STE and EMA, the proposed methods achieve lower FID at small codebooks (8–9 bits) where generation is most challenging, and the compression gap (SSIM/PSNR/LPIPS) widens with increasing codebook size. RT performs poorly at small codebook sizes in both compression and generation (e.g., FID 9.32 at 8-bit vs. 5.90 for DiVeQ), while ST-GS suffers severe codebook-latent misalignment under HP2, producing FID values exceeding 300 in multiple configurations. NSVQ is dramatically outperformed, with FID values 10–15× higher than DiVeQ/SF-DiVeQ (e.g., 81.5 vs. 5.90 at 8-bit CELEBA-HQ) and consistently worse speech metrics. SF-DiVeQ uniquely avoids all observed misalignment cases and does not require codebook replacement, whereas every other method (including DiVeQ) still exhibits at least mild misalignment under aggressive hyperparameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiVeQ directly extends the noise-substitution formulation of NSVQ (Vali & Bäckström, 2022) by replacing its isotropic Gaussian noise with a directional reparameterization (inspired by Kingma & Welling's reparameterization trick) that aligns the simulated quantization error with the nearest codeword, eliminating the ~67% overshoot probability that plagues NSVQ. SF-DiVeQ further builds on the space-filling curve construction from Vali & Bäckström (2023a), extending quantization from discrete codewords to continuous line segments between neighboring codewords. The paper positions itself against the full landscape of VQ gradient solutions—STE, EMA, Rotation Trick, Gumbel-Softmax, and NSVQ—arguing that all suffer from auxiliary losses, hyperparameter sensitivity, train-test mismatch, codebook collapse, or latent-codebook misalignment, which DiVeQ and SF-DiVeQ avoid simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that DiVeQ and SF-DiVeQ serve as drop-in replacements for standard VQ layers requiring no auxiliary losses, temperature schedules, or codebook replacement heuristics, lowering the barrier to effective VQ training across image compression, generation, and speech coding. The misalignment analysis (showing that rate-distortion theory breaks down when codebook and latent representations diverge under high learning rates) provides a useful diagnostic for practitioners debugging VQ training failures. Open questions include whether the space-filling property generalizes to structured or hierarchical codebooks (e.g., multi-level RVQ at scale), whether the small-variance regime truly requires no tuning in all architectural settings, and whether the approach extends naturally to continuous-valued or learned codebook topologies beyond the fixed sequential ordering assumed by SF-DiVeQ.
