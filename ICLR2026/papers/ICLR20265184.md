## Problem

Existing generative models for MOFs produce structures that are geometrically plausible but physically unstable, exhibiting a large 'energy gap' relative to real synthesizable materials. A natural fix—applying reinforcement learning with a physics-based reward—introduces a second failure mode: standard Pass@1 policy optimization rapidly collapses structural diversity as the generator exploits a few 'safe' regions of chemical space, defeating the goal of discovering novel materials.

## Input / Output

The method takes a scalar or vector target property condition (e.g., maximize CO₂ working capacity, hit a pore-limiting diameter of 6.0 ± 0.2 Å, or minimize formation energy) and produces a full 3D MOF structure represented as a tuple of building-block atom types/coordinates, rigid-body rotations and translations, internal torsion angles, and global lattice parameters. The chemical composition is emitted as a canonicalized SMILES sequence (metal clusters followed by organic linkers, sorted by molecular weight), while the geometric assembly is a continuous parameter vector generated by a flow-matching model. All methods are evaluated under an identical budget of 10,000 UMA energy evaluations.

## Method

PRO-MOF formulates MOF generation as a two-level hierarchical RL problem: a high-level autoregressive Transformer policy proposes building-block SMILES sequences, and a low-level conditional flow-matching policy assembles their 3D geometry. The key technical innovation is converting the deterministic flow-matching ODE into a reverse-time SDE (via a rectified-flow score-velocity identity), which makes the geometric policy stochastic and amenable to online RL. A Pass@K variant of GRPO is applied to the geometric policy: k structures are sampled per chemical proposal, and group-relative advantages reward in-group diversity, directly countering mode collapse. The high-level policy receives a max-over-k reward for credit assignment. A pre-trained universal atomistic model (UMA) serves as the high-fidelity reward environment, and a linear reward-annealing schedule stabilizes early training when the policy still produces out-of-distribution structures. Both policies are further regularized with KL penalties against frozen reference copies.

## Results

PRO-MOF (Pass@3) achieves the highest success rate across all three inverse design tasks: 10.3% for maximizing CO₂ working capacity, 7.8% for targeting a 6.0 ± 0.2 Å pore limiting diameter, and 12.4% for minimizing formation energy, all within a fixed budget of 10,000 UMA evaluations. On Top-1 performance, it reaches 5.9 mmol/g CO₂ capacity, 6.0 Å PLD (exactly on target), and −1.35 eV formation energy. The Pass@K strategy is critical: the Pass@1 ablation drops success rates to 8.1%, 3.1%, and 7.2% respectively, confirming that in-group diversity rewards are essential for avoiding mode collapse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRO-MOF (Pass@3) dominates all baselines on every task and metric. Against MOFDiff (Latent Opt.) [Fu et al., 2023], it improves success rate by roughly 5× (10.3% vs. 2.1% on CO₂ capacity; 7.8% vs. 0.8% on PLD targeting) and finds materially better Top-1 values (5.9 vs. 4.9 mmol/g; −1.35 vs. −0.95 eV). Against MOFFlow-2 (Release) [Kim et al., 2025a], the gains are 2.6× on CO₂ success rate (10.3% vs. 4.0%), 3.5× on PLD success rate (7.8% vs. 2.2%), and 3.4× on minimum-energy success rate (12.4% vs. 3.6%). The strongest non-RL baseline, GA+UMA, is outperformed by 1.7–2.3× on success rate across tasks, with a notable 0.2 eV improvement in minimum formation energy (−1.35 vs. −1.15 eV). The internal ablation (Pass@1 vs. Pass@3) shows the Pass@K diversity mechanism contributes an additional 27–150% relative improvement in success rate, confirming that exploration incentives—not just the RL loop itself—are the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRO-MOF builds directly on MOFFlow-2's torsion-aware flow matching architecture for MOF structure generation and on GRPO's group-relative advantage estimation for policy optimization. Its key departure is the hierarchical decomposition into a discrete chemical policy (autoregressive Transformer over SMILES) and a continuous geometric policy (flow matching over 3D assembly parameters), trained jointly via a closed-loop RL scheme where a universal atomistic model (UMA) serves as the reward environment. The Pass@K reward strategy, borrowed from LLM reasoning training (Chen et al., 2025), is repurposed here to combat mode collapse in materials generation, and the ODE-to-SDE conversion follows Flow-GRPO (Liu et al., 2025) to enable stochastic exploration from a deterministic flow model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that online RL with a high-fidelity MLIP surrogate can close the 'physical reality gap' between generated and synthesizable MOFs, reducing a multi-year DFT screening problem to a ~1.5-day GPU computation. The Pass@K mechanism offers a general, lightweight fix for diversity collapse that could transfer to other generative RL settings beyond MOFs. However, the framework's efficacy is bounded by UMA's accuracy and domain coverage, and the current design still relies on a pre-existing library of building blocks rather than truly end-to-end chemical discovery. Notably, several supporting ablations (Tables 6–7) are labeled 'hypothesized results,' which tempers confidence in the diversity and annealing claims.
