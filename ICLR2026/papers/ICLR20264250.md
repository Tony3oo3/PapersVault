## Problem

Existing multi-marginal flow matching methods (piecewise linear, cubic splines, metric flow matching) enforce that interpolants pass through intermediate marginal samples pointwise, producing non-smooth, piecewise trajectories that are difficult to marginalise accurately and that fail when the data geometry changes over time or when K is large. This paper asks whether one can instead learn smooth interpolants that match intermediate marginals in distribution, avoiding the brittleness of pointwise constraints while retaining the ability to recover a well-defined vector field.

## Input / Output

The method consumes K unpaired snapshots (marginal distributions q_{t_i} at discrete time stamps 0=t_1<...<t_K=1) together with a coupling π between the end marginals q_0 and q_1 (either independent or minibatch OT). It outputs a time-dependent vector field u_t^θ parameterised by a neural network, whose ODE integration map pushes q_0 to q_1 while matching all intermediate marginals; the intermediate artefact is a set of smooth adversarially learnt interpolant curves G_φ(x_0,x_1,t) that are provably unique under mild absolute-continuity conditions. Unlike prior methods that require interpolants to hit individual samples, ALI-CFM only requires distributional agreement, making it robust to noisy or sparse snapshots.

## Method

ALI-CFM learns interpolants G_φ(x_0,x_1,t)=(1-t)x_0+tx_1+t(1-t)f_φ(x_0,x_1,t) via a GAN-like min-max objective: a discriminator D_γ(x_t,t) is trained to distinguish true marginal samples x_{t_i}~q_{t_i} from interpolant samples G_φ(x_0,x_1,t_i) with (x_0,x_1)~π, while the generator f_φ is trained to fool it. Under an optimal discriminator this is equivalent to minimising the Jensen-Shannon divergence between q_{t_i} and the pushforward (G_φ(·,·,t_i))_#π, so the interpolants match intermediate marginals in distribution rather than pointwise. Three regularisers (linear reference, piecewise-linear reference via Markov-chained OT coupling, and an integral-of-second-derivative norm) are added to guarantee uniqueness of the interpolant and enforce smoothness; the first two come with formal uniqueness theorems. Once ALIs are trained, they are marginalised through the standard conditional flow matching objective (Eq. 4) to yield the final vector field u_t^θ, completing the two-stage ALI-CFM pipeline.

## Results

On the breast cancer spatial transcriptomics task (K=4 Visium sections, 2D tumour coordinates), OT-ALI-CFM achieves an EMD of 98.91±2.03, outperforming OT-CFM (109.76±9.98), OT-MMFM (109.17±9.82), and OT-MFM (183.88±53.92). On 5D PCA scRNA-seq trajectory inference, OT-ALI-CFM achieves EMDs of 0.910, 0.742, and 0.925 on Cite, EB, and Multiome datasets respectively, performing on par with OT-MFM (0.793, 0.711, 0.890) while substantially outperforming spline-based OT-MMFM (1.099, 3.530, 1.807). On the synthetic knot distribution with K=1200 marginals, ALI-CFM is the only method producing smooth trajectories that accurately capture the time-dependent geometry, whereas piecewise linear, cubic spline, and MFM interpolants all exhibit non-smoothness causing vector field divergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OT-CFM (Tong et al., 2024), OT-ALI-CFM wins on spatial transcriptomics (98.91 vs. 109.76 EMD) and on all three 5D scRNA-seq datasets (e.g., 0.742 vs. 0.809 on EB); on the cell tracking experiment, OT-CFM trajectories diverge during integration while ALI-CFM trajectories remain stable. Against OT-MFM (Kapuśniak et al., 2024), the results are mixed: OT-MFM achieves slightly lower EMD on 5D scRNA-seq (0.793 vs. 0.910 on Cite) and on 50D/100D data, but OT-ALI-CFM decisively wins on spatial transcriptomics (98.91 vs. 183.88) and on cell tracking where time-independent OT-MFM fails to capture temporal dynamics. Against OT-MMFM with cubic splines (Rohbeck et al., 2025), OT-ALI-CFM wins on spatial transcriptomics (98.91 vs. 109.17) and on all 5D scRNA-seq datasets (e.g., 0.742 vs. 3.530 on EB), with the spline methods degrading severely as dimensionality or K increases. Against I-CFM (Liu et al., 2023), OT-ALI-CFM wins across all evaluated datasets, confirming the benefit of OT coupling combined with adversarial interpolant learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

ALI-CFM sits at the intersection of conditional flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023; Tong et al., 2024), neural interpolant parameterisations from metric flow matching (Kapuśniak et al., 2024; Neklyudov et al., 2024), and GAN-style adversarial training (Goodfellow et al., 2014). The key conceptual departure from all prior multi-marginal flow matching methods (Tong et al., 2024; Rohbeck et al., 2025; Kapuśniak et al., 2024) is that ALIs match intermediate marginals in a distributional sense via a Jensen-Shannon divergence objective, rather than forcing interpolants to pass through individual samples pointwise. This distributional matching, combined with provably unique regularisers (linear or piecewise-linear reference), yields smooth interpolants that are more amenable to marginalisation into a stable vector field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the pointwise-vs-distributional distinction is not merely theoretical: on cell tracking data, piecewise-linear and MFM interpolants produce vector fields whose trajectories diverge, while ALI-CFM's smooth interpolants integrate stably. The spatial transcriptomics tumour coordinate inference task is, to the authors' knowledge, new, and ALI-CFM achieves a clear EMD advantage (98.9 vs. 109.8 for OT-CFM and 183.9 for OT-MFM). However, on the more established scRNA-seq trajectory inference benchmarks, ALI-CFM is competitive with but does not clearly surpass OT-MFM, and the authors acknowledge that adversarial distributional matching may lose to pointwise overfitting on pointwise metrics. Open questions include whether the distributional-matching advantage persists in very high dimensions, how sensitive the method is to the choice of GAN objective (R3GAN was used in practice), and whether the uniqueness theorems translate into practical training stability guarantees beyond the empirical regularisation.
