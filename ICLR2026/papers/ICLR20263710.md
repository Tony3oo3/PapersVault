## Problem

Existing data-oriented graph domain adaptation methods rely on discrete intermediate graphs or fixed-step alignment to bridge source and target domains, but real-world graphs evolve continuously and nonlinearly under domain-specific dynamics (e.g., citation growth, social dynamics), making rigid step-by-step alignment inadequate. This paper asks whether GDA can be reformulated as a continuous-time generative process that jointly models structural and semantic transitions without assuming a fixed number of alignment steps.

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled source graph (node feature matrix X^S ∈ R^{N_S×F}, binary adjacency A^S, one-hot label matrix Y^S) and an unlabeled target graph (X^T, A^T) sharing the same feature dimension F and C-class label space. Before diffusion, source features and labels are concatenated along the channel dimension to form augmented features X̃^S = [X^S || Y^S] ∈ R^{N_S×(F+C)}, so the diffusion process operates jointly over the feature-label space and the adjacency matrix. A targeted stochastic diffusion strategy perturbs only a fraction α of nodes to balance computational cost and information preservation. The output is a generated intermediate graph G' = (X', A', Y') with reconstructed labels, which is then used to train a GNN that predicts node labels on the target graph.

## Method

DiffGDA formulates GDA as a continuous-time SDE-based diffusion process: a forward VE-SDE progressively corrupts the augmented source graph (features+labels and adjacency) into Gaussian noise, while a reverse SDE reconstructs a graph conforming to the target distribution. The reverse process is guided by two learned components: a score network P(ℓ) decomposed into a feature-score branch (GNN layers + MLP) and an adjacency-score branch (GNN + graph multi-head attention blocks), and a domain-aware guidance network Q(δ) that estimates the log density ratio q(G₀^T)/p(G₀^S) via a GNN domain classifier and Monte Carlo sampling. Theorem 1 proves that the optimal reverse score equals the source score plus the gradient of this density ratio, providing a principled target for the guidance network. The full system is trained end-to-end: the diffusion loss (score matching + guidance matching) is jointly optimized with a GNN classification loss (cross-entropy on the generated graph plus MMD alignment between generated and target representations), yielding a single unified objective over all parameters.

## Results

On the Citation domain (ACMv9, Citationv1, DBLPv7), DiffGDA achieves an average Mi-F1 of 77.58%, outperforming the strongest baselines A2GNN (75.97%) and TDSS (75.72%) by 1.6–1.9 points across all six transfer tasks. On the Airport domain (USA, Brazil, Europe), DiffGDA reaches 60.75% average Mi-F1, surpassing the next-best GraphAlign (56.39%) and AdaGCN (56.17%) by over 4 points. Across all 14 transfer tasks spanning three domains, DiffGDA consistently achieves the highest Mi-F1 and Ma-F1 scores, with the largest gains observed on structurally heterogeneous transfers (e.g., D→A: 74.55% vs. 73.21% for A2GNN; E→B: 74.81% vs. 71.02% for GraphAlign).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffGDA outperforms all 15 baselines on every one of the 14 transfer tasks. Among source-only GNNs (GAT, GIN, GCN), DiffGDA leads by 13–48 Mi-F1 points, confirming that direct transfer without domain adaptation is insufficient. Among GDA methods, the closest competitors are A2GNN (75.97% avg on Citation) and TDSS (75.72%), which DiffGDA surpasses by 1.6 and 1.9 points respectively; on the more challenging Airport domain, DiffGDA leads GraphAlign (56.39%) and AdaGCN (56.17%) by 4.4 and 4.6 points. Data-oriented baselines that construct discrete intermediate graphs (GraphAlign, GGDA, PairAlign) are consistently outperformed, supporting the paper's claim that continuous diffusion-based evolution better captures nonlinear structural shifts. Model-oriented adversarial methods (UDAGCN, AdaGCN, DANE) show moderate performance on Citation but degrade sharply on Airport (e.g., DANE drops to 38.36%), while DiffGDA maintains robustness across domains. The largest absolute gains appear on structurally heterogeneous transfers such as D→A (74.55 vs. 73.21 for A2GNN) and E→B (74.81 vs. 71.02 for GraphAlign), where discrete alignment strategies struggle most.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffGDA builds directly on two threads: the score-based graph generation framework of Jo et al. (ICML 2022), which provides the SDE decomposition for jointly modeling node features and adjacency matrices, and the transfer-learning-for-diffusion theory of Ouyang et al. (NeurIPS 2024), from which the density-ratio guidance mechanism and Theorem 1 are derived. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant data-oriented GDA paradigm (GraphAlign, GGDA) that constructs discrete intermediate graphs via heuristic alignment, reframing adaptation as a continuous-time stochastic process governed by SDEs. It also moves beyond model-oriented GDA (adversarial, MMD-based) by learning an explicit generative trajectory rather than aligning fixed representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first diffusion-based GDA method, DiffGDA opens a new methodological axis for graph transfer and provides a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) that the guided reverse process converges to the optimal adaptation trajectory in latent space. However, practical limitations remain: the density-ratio estimation relies on a domain classifier and Monte Carlo sampling, the consistent-diffusion assumption (same VE-SDE for both domains) may not hold for highly heterogeneous graphs, and the stochastic sampling ratio α must be tuned per task. Open questions include whether the continuous formulation scales to very large graphs (tens of thousands of nodes), whether the guidance network can be replaced by lighter-weight alternatives, and whether the theoretical convergence guarantee translates to tighter empirical bounds under finite-sample conditions.
