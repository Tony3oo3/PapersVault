## Problem

Non-human primate (NHP) behavior research lacks accurate 3D body surface and pose representations, with existing macaque datasets restricted to sparse 2D keypoints or 3D positions that cannot capture the richness of action dynamics and social interactions. No prior dataset combines realistic, subject-specific 3D surface-based pose reconstructions with action recognition labels for NHPs, leaving a gap between the detailed body modeling available for humans and the behavioral tracking needs of primate neuroscience and ethology.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes 16 synchronized camera views at 40 FPS (2464×2056 px) of 8 male rhesus macaques, along with per-frame 2D keypoints (20 landmarks) from HRNet-W48, segmentation masks from SAM 2, and bounding boxes from YOLOv8, all aligned to a 3,625-vertex low-poly macaque template mesh with 115-joint rig. Outputs include per-frame axis-angle joint rotation vectors (θ ∈ ℝ^{3×115}), subject-specific textured meshes with per-vertex RGB colors, ethogram-aligned multi-label action categories, and the full annotation stack (keypoints, masks, bboxes). The derived BigMaQ500 benchmark further provides 8,176 multi-view videos with complete video-to-3D-pose correspondences sampled at 10 Hz for action recognition evaluation.

## Method

The core method fits a parametric macaque mesh (deformed via linear blend skinning) to multi-view video using differentiable rendering, with subject-specific adaptation through learnable bone lengths, sparse vertex offsets (exploiting bilateral symmetry to reduce parameters to 1,305×3 in the low-poly mesh), and per-vertex colors optimized via a masked photometric loss. Three key technical contributions enable scaling to 763 actions: (1) a symmetric temporal loss that penalizes angular velocities in axis-angle space for rotations and Euclidean differences for translations, enforcing smoothness across 80-frame batches with 10-frame overlap; (2) cropped differential rendering at 100-pixel maximum side length to reduce memory, processing cameras sequentially in batched format; and (3) integration of texture optimization into the same pipeline. For action recognition, the paper demonstrates that representing pose as 3D rotation matrices (rather than 2D/3D keypoints or surface vertex positions) consistently outperforms alternative descriptors when combined with video features from foundation models (ViT, DINOv2, TimeSformer, VideoPrism), achieving up to 44.0 mAP versus 43.5 for pose-only, showing that generative 3D structure encodes behavioral information beyond raw 3D geometry.

## Results

On single-frame surface reconstruction across all single-subject actions, BigMaQ achieves a mean IoU of 0.844, substantially outperforming MAMMAL (0.714) and AniMer+ (0.591). At the sequence level, BigMaQ reduces MPJPE to 26.9 mm (vs. 31.7 mm for MAMMAL) and MPJTD to 8.5 mm/frame (vs. 12.4 mm/frame for MAMMAL) across evaluated actions. On the BigMaQ500 action recognition benchmark, combining visual features with 3D rotation-matrix pose vectors yields a peak mAP of 44.0 (ResNet50, ViT-base-cls), compared to 43.5 for pose-only and 34.3–40.4 for visual-only baselines. Among pose representations, 3D rotation matrices (43.5 mAP pose-only) consistently outperform 2D keypoints (35.6), 3D keypoints (40.8), and mesh vertex positions (35.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BigMaQ decisively outperforms both baselines on surface reconstruction quality. Against MAMMAL (An et al., 2023), BigMaQ improves mean single-frame IoU from 0.714 to 0.844 (+13.0 points) and reduces sequence-level MPJPE from 31.7 mm to 26.9 mm, with similar gains in temporal smoothness (MPJTD). Against AniMer+ (Lyu et al., 2025), the gap is even larger: IoU drops to 0.591, and qualitatively AniMer+ produces shapes resembling entirely different animal species (e.g., lion-like silhouettes in side view). In the action recognition benchmark (BigMaQ500), the paper's 3D rotation-matrix pose vectors outperform all alternative pose descriptors—2D keypoints, 3D keypoints, and 3D mesh vertex positions—across every visual backbone tested (ViT-base-cls, DINOv2-base, VideoPrism-base), confirming that generative pose parameters carry more discriminative information than positional descriptions. Social interaction mAP remains the weakest category (~29–30) across all models, highlighting the continued challenge of multi-individual behavior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MAMMAL framework for multi-view surface-based animal tracking (An et al., 2023) from quadrupeds to primates, incorporating subject-specific shape adaptation via learnable bone lengths and vertex offsets (Badger et al., 2020) and silhouette loss gating (Ruegg et al., 2022). The key departure is the addition of a symmetric angular-velocity temporal loss, cropped differential rendering for computational feasibility at scale, and per-vertex texture optimization, enabling realistic avatars for 8 individual macaques. For action recognition, the paper adapts the human finding that 3D pose vectors improve recognition (Rajasegaran et al., 2023) to the NHP domain, where prior work relied exclusively on 2D keypoints or 3D positions without full surface models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BigMaQ fills a critical gap: no prior NHP dataset combined realistic 3D surface pose with action labels, forcing neuroscientists to rely on human body models or sparse keypoints. The finding that generative rotation parameters consistently outperform 3D keypoints and surface vertex positions for action recognition (Table 5) provides empirical support for inverse-graphics-inspired representations in behavioral neuroscience. Limitations include the small cohort (8 captive males), lab-only setting, and two-annotator ethogram, raising open questions about generalization to wild populations, other NHP species, and single-view reconstruction. A natural next step identified by the authors is deriving a pose prior from this data to regularize in-the-wild single-view methods.
