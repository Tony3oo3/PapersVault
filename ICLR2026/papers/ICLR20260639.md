## Problem

Self-supervised learning discovers semantic structures through self-generated objectives but does not specify the true class space, so emergent clusters are not guaranteed to align with human-defined categories. This paper introduces the One-Shot Exemplar SSL (OSESSL) setting, which provides minimal class grounding by requiring only a single annotated instance per class, and asks how to effectively propagate such extremely sparse supervision to large-scale unlabeled data without overfitting or representation collapse.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260639/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a large unlabeled image dataset (e.g., CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-1K) together with exactly one labeled exemplar image per class, where the number of exemplars scales with the number of classes rather than the sample size. During training, exemplars receive only weak augmentation while unlabeled images receive both weak and strong augmentations; a FIFO memory bank of 65,536 (ImageNet) or 4,096 (CIFAR) historical embeddings is maintained for neighbor selection. The output is a pretrained encoder (ResNet-18/50 or ViT-S/16) whose frozen features support linear probing, k-NN classification, and fine-tuning for detection and segmentation.

## Method

The framework builds exemplar-guided prototypes by selecting k discriminative neighbors from a memory bank for each exemplar using a score that balances intra-class similarity against inter-class differentiability (weighted by α=0.75), then computes soft-weighted prototype vectors. An alignment loss enforces consistency between prototype assignment distributions of two augmented views, effectively pulling unlabeled embeddings toward the exemplar-anchored prototype barycenter, while a dispersion regularization term repels prototypes from each other to prevent collapse. To extend guidance into ambiguous decision-boundary regions, an exemplar-guided interpolation consistency loss applies mixup-style linear interpolation of views and enforces both prototype-level distribution matching and instance-level similarity consistency on the mixed embeddings. The total training objective combines a base clustering loss (following the ReSA baseline), the prototype learning loss, and the interpolation consistency loss with tunable weights, and is trained with a momentum teacher network and a three-layer MLP projector.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, the method achieves 75.47% linear and 69.89% k-NN accuracy (ResNet-18, 1000 epochs), surpassing the strongest baseline ReSA by 3.26% and 3.06% respectively. On ImageNet-100, it reaches 83.88% linear and 80.42% k-NN, a 5.86% k-NN gain over ReSA. On ImageNet-1K (ResNet-50, 800 epochs), the method attains 76.4% top-1 linear accuracy, outperforming all self-supervised baselines and even semi-supervised methods that use 1% labels (12,811 samples) versus only 1,000 exemplars. On COCO detection (Mask R-CNN, ResNet-50), it achieves 41.3 AP, the best among all compared SSL and semi-supervised approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 23 compared baselines across every dataset and evaluation protocol. Against the strongest self-supervised baseline ReSA (Weng et al., 2025), it gains +1.66% linear and +1.18% k-NN on CIFAR-10, +3.26% linear and +3.06% k-NN on CIFAR-100, and +1.64% linear and +5.86% k-NN on ImageNet-100. On ImageNet-1K (ResNet-50, 800 epochs), it reaches 76.4% top-1, exceeding ReSA (75.2%), INTL (73.3%), and MoCoV3 (73.8%), and also surpassing semi-supervised methods PAWS (73.5%) and Suave (75.3%) that use 12,811 labels versus only 1,000 exemplars. In semi-supervised fine-tuning (1% labels), it achieves 61.3% top-1, beating SsCL (60.2%), Suave (60.4%), and PAWS (59.2%). For transfer to fine-grained datasets (k-NN, k=20), it leads on Food-101 (64.2% vs. ReSA 61.3%), CUB-200-2011 (60.5% vs. 59.9%), and Oxford Pets (88.3% vs. 87.5%). On COCO detection, it achieves 41.3 AP vs. ReSA's 41.0 and Suave's 40.6, confirming consistent gains in dense prediction tasks as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on clustering-based SSL methods (SwAV, DINO, ReSA) as its architectural and training backbone, but departs from them by introducing explicit class grounding through one labeled exemplar per class. It also draws on mixup-style interpolation consistency (Zhang et al., 2018) to regularize decision boundaries in ambiguous regions. Conceptually, OSESSL carves a middle ground between pure SSL (no labels, emergent clusters) and conventional semi-supervised learning (labels proportional to sample size), positioning the one-shot exemplar as a semantic anchor that exposes the true class space at O(1) annotation cost relative to dataset scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(1)-per-class annotation requirement makes class-grounded SSL practically viable for large-scale datasets where even 1% labeling is prohibitive, and the consistent 3–6% k-NN gains across CIFAR-100, ImageNet-100, and ImageNet-1K demonstrate that minimal supervision can substantially improve representation quality. A key open question is robustness to noisy or mislabeled exemplars, which the authors acknowledge but do not address; extending the framework to handle annotation noise or to multi-shot regimes would be a natural next step. The approach also raises the broader question of how much class structure is truly necessary to overcome the no-free-lunch limitation of self-supervision.
