## Problem

Protein language models (PLMs) frequently collapse into pathological repetition—redundant motifs or long homopolymers—during generation, undermining structural confidence and functional viability. Unlike in NLP where repetition merely hurts readability, in proteins it directly destabilizes folds, yet this failure mode has been largely overlooked in the protein modeling community. The paper addresses the lack of principled metrics to quantify this degeneracy and the absence of methods that can reduce repetition without degrading AlphaFold-based structural confidence.

## Input / Output

The method operates at inference time on a pretrained PLM (ESM-3 for masked LM or ProtGPT2 for autoregressive LM), taking as input an optional 10-residue prefix (for conditional generation) or a special start token (for unconditional generation), with target sequence lengths uniformly sampled between 50 and 512 residues. A precomputed steering vector v^L (one per selected layer) is injected into hidden activations at each decoding step, requiring no retraining or modification of the model weights. The output is a generated protein sequence evaluated by a composite repetition score R(x) (aggregating entropy, Distinct-2/3, and homopolymer diversity) and a utility score U(x) (mean of scaled pLDDT and pTM from AlphaFold).

## Method

UCCS (Utility-Controlled Contrastive Steering) disentangles repetition from structural utility by constructing contrastive datasets D+ (low repetition) and D− (high repetition) that are matched in biological utility: sequences are selected to maximize ΔR subject to ΔU ≤ ε, using Pareto-frontier or composite-score ranking within length-utility bins. The steering vector v^L is computed as the difference in mean sequence-level hidden representations (mean-pooled across tokens for MLMs, last-token embedding for AR-LMs) between D+ and D−, capturing a latent direction specific to pathological repetition rather than foldability artifacts. At inference, hidden activations at chosen layers are updated as h̃ = h + α·v^L (α=1 by default), steering generation away from degenerate patterns without retraining. The key design choice is the utility-controlled contrastive selection, which prevents the steering vector from conflating repetition with structural quality—a confound that makes naive high-vs-low repetition contrasts ineffective. Ablations show that structured dataset construction (Pareto/Composite) outperforms random sampling, moderate steering strength (α≈1–2) yields the best trade-off, and later layers are most effective for injection.

## Results

On ProtGPT2 unconditional generation (CATH), UCCS achieves R=0.845 and U=0.711, improving repetition by +16% over the original model (R=0.728) while simultaneously boosting utility by +14% (U=0.621). On ESM-3 unconditional generation (CATH), UCCS raises R from 0.423 to 0.645 (+52%) while increasing U from 0.576 to 0.652 (+13%). Across all three datasets and both generation settings, UCCS is the only method that consistently satisfies the utility constraint (U ≥ U_Original) while achieving the highest repetition score, outperforming all decoding heuristics and mechanism-level baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UCCS decisively outperforms all four baseline categories on both R and U simultaneously. Against temperature/top-p sampling (Holtzman et al., 2020), UCCS achieves +16% higher R (0.845 vs. 0.756 on CATH) while those methods sacrifice U (0.612 vs. 0.621 original); UCCS is the only method that improves both metrics. Against n-gram blocking (Kulikov et al., 2019), UCCS gains +15% in R (0.845 vs. 0.736) and +16% in U (0.711 vs. 0.613), whereas n-gram blocking provides only marginal R improvement at the cost of U. Against neuron deactivation (Radford et al., 2017), UCCS leads by +17% in R and +16% in U; neuron deactivation is unstable, sometimes improving R but consistently degrading U below the original model. Against probe steering (Elazar et al., 2021), UCCS achieves +17% in R and +17% in U; probe steering fails to improve R meaningfully (0.722 vs. 0.728 original) and often reduces U. The critical distinction is that UCCS is the only method that satisfies the utility constraint (U ≥ U_Original) across all datasets and tasks while delivering the largest R gains, confirming that dataset-guided contrastive steering disentangles repetition from foldability in a way that decoding heuristics and isolated mechanism interventions cannot.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the contrastive activation addition framework (Rimsky et al., 2024; Turner et al., 2024) from NLP to protein language models, but introduces a critical departure: the contrastive sets are constructed under a utility-matching constraint so that the extracted steering vector encodes repetition rather than a confound of foldability. The repetition metrics adapt Distinct-n (Li et al., 2016) and add a novel homopolymer diversity score motivated by the small 20-residue alphabet. Unlike prior PLM work that focuses on generation quality or structure prediction, this is the first to formally define and systematically study pathological repetition as a distinct failure mode in protein generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a previously overlooked failure mode in PLMs and provides a plug-and-play, retraining-free remedy that practitioners can immediately adopt. The key open question is whether the utility-controlled disentanglement generalizes beyond the two tested architectures (ESM-3, ProtGPT2) and to longer sequences or other failure modes (e.g., structural motif collapse). Biological validation relies entirely on AlphaFold confidence scores (pLDDT, pTM) as proxies; wet-lab confirmation of foldability and function for UCCS-steered sequences remains future work. The finding that standard NLP decoding heuristics actively harm protein foldability also cautions against blind cross-domain transfer of generation techniques.
