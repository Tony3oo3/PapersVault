## Problem

Multimodal spatiotemporal learning on real-world scientific data faces two coupled challenges: within-modality measurements are sparse, irregular, and noisy with sensor-specific QA/QC artifacts, while the set of available modalities varies across space and time. Existing approaches either preprocess data via gridding or imputation (introducing smoothing bias and uncertainty collapse) or assume fixed observation operators and shared sampling indices across modalities, yielding likelihood misspecification when sensor supports and noise profiles differ. No prior framework simultaneously handles both challenges in a single continuity-aware model without surrogate preprocessing.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a context set C of finite, irregular point clouds per available modality (each modality observed at its own partially overlapping sensor locations, e.g., 432 of 21,600 grid points on ClimSim) together with arbitrary query space-time coordinates (x, t). It outputs per-modality scalar field values at those queried coordinates, supporting reconstruction, spatial interpolation, forecasting (Δt up to 48 h), and cross-modal prediction (predicting modalities absent from the input). Absent modalities are handled via zero-gating and attention masking rather than imputation, so the same trained model operates on any input subset without retraining.

## Method

OmniField is an encoder–processor–decoder conditioned neural field (CNF) that maps irregular multimodal observations to a continuous spatiotemporal field. Three mechanisms address the data and modality challenges: (1) Gaussian Fourier Features (GFF) with sinusoidal initialization of learnable query tokens replace fixed sinusoidal encodings to combat low-frequency spectral bias; (2) a Multimodal Crosstalk (MCT) block concatenates per-modality CNF tokens and conditions the processor with a lightweight global multimodal code z, enabling cross-modal information exchange before decoding; and (3) Iterative Cross-Modal Refinement (ICMR) revisits the MCT fusion over L processor steps, updating z as a communication bridge that progressively aligns signals across mismatched supports, scales, and noise profiles. Fleximodal fusion with per-modality presence masks gates absent channels at the encoder, cross-attention, and loss levels, allowing one model to handle arbitrary modality subsets. The encoder builds query-local, permutation-invariant summaries; the processor fuses multi-resolution GFF coordinate encodings with the context summary; and lightweight per-modality decoders produce final predictions.

## Results

On ClimSim-THW (sparse multimodal climate forecasting at 3.87% sampling rate), OmniField achieves the best Mid-Fusion RMSE across all three modalities: Temperature 1.07 K, Humidity 0.66×10⁻³ kg/kg, and Wind 4.86 m/s, yielding a 22.4% average relative error reduction over the strongest baseline (SCENT: 1.52/0.99/5.07). On the naturalistic EPA-AQS air-quality dataset (6 pollutants, irregular stations), OmniField leads at every modality-count level (M2→M4→M6) and attains the overall state of the art across all six pollutants. Under heavy simulated sensor noise (σ up to 2.0×), ICMR remains near clean-input accuracy while Mid-Fusion baselines degrade substantially.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniField outperforms all eight primary baselines on ClimSim-THW under the Mid-Fusion protocol. Against the closest competitor SCENT (the prior CNF backbone), OmniField reduces RMSE by 29.6% on Temperature (1.07 vs 1.52 K), 33.3% on Humidity (0.66 vs 0.99), and 4.1% on Wind (4.86 vs 5.07 m/s). Among operator-learning baselines, FNO is the strongest on Temperature (3.36 K) but OmniField still leads by 3.2×; CORAL and OFormer are substantially weaker (T RMSE 13.12 and 11.20 respectively). Natively multimodal models PROSE-FD (T=5.20) and MIA (T=4.43) benefit from multi-channel fusion but trail OmniField, confirming that iterative cross-modal refinement (ICMR) adds gains beyond simple multi-channel conditioning. On EPA-AQS, OmniField (4.3M params) beats SCENT (29.3M) on O₃ (4.4 vs 5.5) and PM₂.₅ (14.9 vs 15.8) at 6-modality training, and outperforms all baselines on every pollutant. On the RainNet nowcasting task, OmniField surpasses both SCENT and the original RainNet CNN baseline. For downstream transfer, OmniField achieves MSE 0.755 on CESNET multivariate forecasting versus TimeMixer's 0.859, and 71.8% accuracy on 20%-sparse CIFAR-10 versus UNet's 55.0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniField extends the conditioned neural field (CNF) lineage—particularly SCENT (Park et al., 2025), which established a scalable encoder-processor-decoder CNF for unimodal spatiotemporal scientific data—by introducing a multimodal fusion layer (MCT + ICMR) that iteratively aligns per-modality tokens before conditioning the shared field. Architecturally it inherits the Perceiver IO scaffold (Jaegle et al., 2022) and the operator-learning framing of FNO/OFormer/CORAL, but departs from them by operating on irregular, partially co-located point clouds without gridding. The fleximodal masking mechanism distinguishes OmniField from prior multimodal CNFs (PROSE-FD, MIA) that assume a fixed modality set, and from training-time dropout (ModDrop) by handling truly absent channels at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, unified framework for the two most common failure modes in real sensor networks—sparse/noisy measurements and variable modality availability—without requiring gridding, imputation, or per-instance retraining, which is directly relevant to climate, air-quality, and materials-science deployments. The noise-robustness finding (ICMR routing through cleaner channels) suggests iterative cross-modal refinement is a lightweight alternative to explicit uncertainty modeling. Open questions include calibrated uncertainty quantification (current decoder gives point estimates), domain-shift generalization (seasonal changes, sensor re-sitings), and whether the token-scaling cost limits deployment at extreme spatial scales.
