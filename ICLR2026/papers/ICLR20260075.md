## Problem

Existing geometric image editing methods face two persistent limitations: they struggle to achieve accurate object transformations (translation, rotation, scaling) especially under large displacements or complex scenes, and they fail to model physically consistent lighting and shadow effects, producing unrealistic composites. No prior approach simultaneously delivers high-fidelity geometric control and photorealistic visual effects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260075/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 1024×1024 RGB image, a source mask, and geometric transformation parameters (translation vector, rotation angle, or scale factor). A Geometric Transformation module precomputes the transformed object appearance and target mask—using 3D mesh reconstruction via Hunyuan3D-2.1 for rotation, direct mask copying for translation, and uniform scaling for depth cues—which together with the original image form an in-context inpainting prompt. The output is a single edited image where the object has been repositioned/reoriented/resized with physically plausible shadows and illumination.

## Method

GeoEdit builds on FLUX.1 Fill (a DiT-based inpainting model), replacing the T5 text encoder with a SigLIP image encoder and fine-tuning via LoRA (rank 256) under a flow-matching objective. The Geometric Transformation module lifts objects into 3D space via textured mesh reconstruction to execute precise parametric transformations, producing appearance references and paired masks that constrain in-context generation. The key architectural contribution is Effects-Sensitive Attention (ESA), which adds a soft bias δ = α·std(S_ij) to attention logits for edit-region queries, increasing focus on object tokens while preserving cross-region interactions needed for shadow and lighting synthesis; Theorem 3.1 proves ESA reduces KL divergence to an ideal attention distribution relative to both standard and hard-modulated attention. Training uses the newly constructed RS-Objects dataset (120K+ image-mask pairs from Blender rendering and LoRA-based synthesis with human-in-the-loop filtering), and two separate α values (α₁=0.1 for insertion, α₂=1.0 for background restoration) tune attention strength per editing sub-task.

## Results

On GeoBench 2D-edits, GeoEdit achieves the best scores across all seven metrics: FID 25.07, DINOv2 distance 90.66, Kernel Distance 0.051, Subject Consistency 0.910, Background Consistency 0.977, Warp Error 0.054, and Mean Distance 9.23. On 3D-edits, GeoEdit again leads with FID 64.30, DINOv2 350.69, Warp Error 0.051, and Mean Distance 18.08. On the ObjMove-A benchmark, GeoEdit achieves the best PSNR (26.18), SSIM (0.891), CLIP-Score (92.90), FID (23.65), LPIPS (0.180), and DreamSim (0.023), while tying ObjectMover on ReMOVE (0.874).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoEdit outperforms all 12 baselines on every metric for both 2D-edits and 3D-edits. The closest competitor is FreeFine (Zhu et al., 2025), which trails GeoEdit by 2.41 FID (27.48 vs. 25.07) and 18.57 DINOv2 (109.23 vs. 90.66) on 2D-edits, and by 1.64 FID (65.94 vs. 64.30) and 15.70 DINOv2 (366.39 vs. 350.69) on 3D-edits. Magic Fixup (Alzayer et al., 2025) and DesignEdit (Jia et al., 2024) are the next-strongest 2D baselines but fall 2.25 and 7.48 FID behind GeoEdit respectively. For 3D-edits, GeoDiffuser (Sajnani et al., 2025) and Diffusion Handles (Pandey et al., 2024) are substantially weaker, with FID gaps of 13.04 and 61.94 over GeoEdit. On the ObjMove-A benchmark, GeoEdit surpasses ObjectMover (Yu et al., 2025) by 2.12 PSNR and 13.19 FID, and AnyDoor (Chen et al., 2024) by 2.76 PSNR and 17.46 FID, while tying ObjectMover on ReMOVE (0.874). The user study (33 annotators, 3,960 votes) confirms GeoEdit's dominance, receiving 1,732 total votes versus 778 for the runner-up FreeFine.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoEdit sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: diffusion-based geometric editing (DragDiffusion, Diffusion Handles, GeoDiffuser, FreeFine) that achieves precise object manipulation but produces physically inconsistent lighting, and video/3D-prior-based methods (Magic Fixup, ObjectMover, 3D-Fixup) that learn realistic illumination but lack fine-grained geometric control. The paper builds directly on the in-context inpainting paradigm of Insert Anything (Song et al., 2025) and the FLUX.1 Fill DiT backbone, introducing a Geometric Transformation module (leveraging Hunyuan3D for 3D mesh rotation) and Effects-Sensitive Attention as task-specific modifications. The key conceptual departure is the theoretical framing of attention modulation: rather than hard-gating (which the paper shows destroys shadow/lighting generation), ESA applies a soft logit bias whose effect on KL divergence to an ideal attention distribution is formally characterized in Theorem 3.1.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a practical gap that has persisted in geometric editing: no prior method simultaneously achieves pixel-accurate translation/rotation/scaling AND physically plausible shadow and lighting transfer. The RS-Objects dataset (120K pairs with controlled geometry and realistic effects) is a valuable resource that the authors argue existing datasets cannot provide. However, the approach still depends on external 3D reconstruction (Hunyuan3D) for rotation, limiting robustness to objects that are hard to reconstruct, and the authors acknowledge failure on dynamic particle-based effects (sand, dust). Open questions include whether the ESA mechanism generalizes beyond geometric editing to other attention-guided tasks, and whether the 3D-reconstruction dependency can be eliminated.
