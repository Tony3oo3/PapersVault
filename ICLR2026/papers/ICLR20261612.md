## Problem

Standard RLVR methods for LLMs suffer from an exploration dilemma: they sharpen the already-peaked pretrained policy around a narrow set of homogeneous solutions, improving pass@1 while suppressing solution diversity and causing pass@k to stagnate or degrade. The root cause is a mismatch between the sharply peaked initial policy of pretrained LLMs and the dynamics of mean-reward policy gradient, which traps optimization in suboptimal local modes rather than discovering novel reasoning strategies.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (e.g., a math word problem) and generates N=16 candidate responses per prompt during training, scored by a binary verifiable reward (Math_Verify: 1 for correct, 0 otherwise). The output is a single text response containing step-by-step reasoning with a final answer in \boxed{}. During evaluation, up to 1024 solutions are sampled per problem to compute pass@k metrics. The method operates as a drop-in replacement for the advantage computation in existing GRPO pipelines, requiring no changes to the policy architecture, sampling procedure, or reward model.

## Method

RS-GRPO replaces the standard risk-neutral advantage (reward minus batch mean) with a risk-sensitive advantage derived from the exponential-utility objective J_RS = (1/β) log E[e^{βr(y)}], which smoothly interpolates between mean reward (β→0) and maximum reward (β→∞). The resulting advantage A_β(y_i) = (1/β)(e^{βr(y_i)} / (1/N)Σ_j e^{βr(y_j)} − 1) dynamically re-weights optimization: as β increases, it amplifies learning signals on hard prompts (low accuracy) while suppressing them on easy ones, driving the policy to explore under-explored regions of the solution space. Theoretically, the paper proves that standard policy gradient can decrease the probability of the optimal action when a suboptimal-but-above-average action exists (Lemma 2), whereas risk-sensitive gradient guarantees an increase in optimal-action probability for sufficiently large β (Lemma 3), with diminishing returns beyond a threshold (Lemma 4). Empirically, β=2 provides an effective trade-off, consistently improving pass@32 by ~4–5% over GRPO while maintaining or improving pass@1 across five LLMs and six mathematical reasoning benchmarks.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Math-7B trained on DeepMath-103K, RS-GRPO achieves 28.6% average pass@1 and 48.3% average pass@32 across six math benchmarks, improving over GRPO (26.6% / 45.3%) by +2.0% and +3.0% respectively. On Qwen3-4B-Base (math12k), the gains are larger: 30.3% pass@1 (+2.6%) and 53.1% pass@32 (+5.0%) over GRPO. On out-of-distribution tasks, RS-GRPO reaches 88.9% pass@32 on GPQA-Diamond (vs. 84.8% for GRPO) and 39.1% pass@32 on LiveCodeBench v5 (vs. 34.3% for GRPO), confirming the exploration benefit generalizes beyond math.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary GRPO baseline, RS-GRPO consistently improves pass@32 by ~3–5% across all five models while maintaining or improving pass@1 by ~1–3%, demonstrating a superior exploration–exploitation trade-off. Compared to the pass@k-specific baselines (Walder & Karkhanis, Mahdavi et al., Chen et al.), RS-GRPO achieves comparable or slightly lower pass@32 but substantially higher pass@1 (e.g., 28.6% vs. 26.2–26.3% average on Qwen2.5-Math-7B), which the authors attribute to its denser advantage signal that remains non-zero even at high prompt accuracy. Walder & Karkhanis performs worst overall, with strictly positive advantages causing rapid entropy collapse and below-GRPO pass@1. On the Qwen3-4B-Base model, RS-GRPO's advantage is most pronounced, gaining +5.0% pass@32 and +2.6% pass@1 over GRPO, while the pass@k baselines are not reported for this model. On OOD tasks (GPQA-Diamond, LiveCodeBench v5), RS-GRPO outperforms GRPO at every k value, with the largest gap at pass@32 (+4.1% and +4.8% respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RLVR/GRPO paradigm for LLM reasoning and the classical risk-sensitive RL literature (Howard & Matheson, 1972; Mihatsch & Neuneier, 2002), repurposing the exponential-utility objective to address a specific failure mode in LLM fine-tuning: the exploration dilemma where sharply peaked pretrained policies trap standard mean-reward optimization in local optima. It departs from concurrent pass@k optimization methods (Tang et al., 2025; Walder & Karkhanis, 2025; Mahdavi et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025) by providing a formulation that naturally handles continuous rewards and yields denser advantage signals that do not vanish at high prompt accuracy. The theoretical contribution (showing standard PG can decrease the optimal action's probability) formalizes a previously observed but unexplained phenomenon in the RLVR literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The drop-in advantage replacement makes RS-GRPO immediately deployable in existing GRPO pipelines (VeRL, DAPO), and the consistent pass@k gains across five models and six benchmarks with maintained pass@1 make it a practical upgrade for RLVR practitioners. Key limitations include the restriction to bandit settings (no credit assignment for multi-step MDPs), the use of a fixed β (dynamic scheduling was attempted but did not improve results), and the observation that for some models the optimal policy is too distant from the initial distribution for even risk-sensitive RL to escape. Open questions include extending risk-sensitive objectives to general MDPs with learned value functions, and whether the exploration benefits transfer to non-mathematical reasoning domains beyond the GPQA and coding results shown.
