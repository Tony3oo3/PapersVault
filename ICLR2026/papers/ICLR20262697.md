## Problem

While linear layers in LLMs have been extensively optimized for low-bit computation, nonlinear operations (SiLU, RMSNorm, Softmax) still depend on high-precision floating-point arithmetic, creating a severe efficiency bottleneck. Existing approximation methods such as NN-LUT were validated only on narrow input ranges (e.g., [-5,5]) and require data calibration, causing them to collapse when confronted with the extreme activation outliers (exceeding ±100) characteristic of modern LLMs, producing perplexity surges up to 7×10⁴.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single FP16 scalar activation and the identity of the target nonlinear operator (SiLU, Softmax, rsqrt, exp, etc.), then produces an FP16 approximated output via a 259-entry lookup table with linear interpolation. The LUT is constructed offline over the full finite FP16 grid (65,536 values) and is calibration-free—depending only on the target function and numeric settings—so the same table is reusable across all layers and models. Inputs outside the table's domain are clamped to the nearest boundary endpoint, guaranteeing numerical stability for extreme values.

## Method

NLI formulates the placement of M cutpoints on the sorted FP16 grid as a discrete dynamic program that minimizes mean relative interpolation error with endpoint clamping; the Bellman recursion exploits optimal substructure to find the globally optimal partition in O(M·N²) time. A hardware-consistent layout constrains the search to 11 macro-interval endpoints (first and last unsplit, middle eight uniformly subdivided into 32 bins each), yielding 2+8×32+1=259 total cutpoints and reducing DP search time by ~26× versus a naive 259-point search. On the hardware side, a two-level address translation replaces 259 parallel FP16 comparators with just 10: a first level selects the macro-interval, and a second level computes the micro-bin index via a preloaded per-interval scale factor followed by a floor operation. The full NLI Engine is a four-stage pipeline (interval select, micro-address generation, dual-port SRAM read with slope computation, and fused multiply-add interpolation) synthesized in SMIC 28nm, achieving 4× higher throughput-per-area-per-power than NN-LUT and RI-LUT baselines.

## Results

On LLMs (Llama3-8B/70B, Qwen2.5-7B/32B, Qwen1.5-110B, Qwen3-8B/30B-A3B), NLI matches FP32 accuracy to within 0.1–0.5 points on MMLU, GSM8k, HumanEval, and zero-shot average, with identical Wikitext-2 perplexity (e.g., 6.14 for Llama3-8B, 5.32 for Qwen2.5-32B). In hardware (SMIC 28 nm, 1 GHz), the NLI Engine achieves 3.78 G/(μm²·mW) efficiency—4.02× NN-LUT and 4.29× RI-LUT—while using only 7,787 μm² (68–69% less area) and 34 mW power.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NN-LUT (Yu et al., DAC 2022), NLI is a decisive win: on Qwen2.5-7B, NN-LUT's Wikitext-2 perplexity explodes to 28,194 (vs. 7.46 for FP32/NLI) and MMLU collapses to 25.51 (vs. 70.67 for NLI); the same catastrophic failure occurs on Qwen2.5-32B (PPL 70,360) and Qwen3-8B (PPL 825). On Llama models where NN-LUT partially functions, it still lags NLI by 1–4 points on MMLU and 2–3.6 points on zero-shot average. Against RI-LUT (Kim et al., DAC 2023), the comparison is hardware-only: NLI uses 67% less area (7,787 vs. 23,647 μm²) and 29% less power (34 vs. 48 mW) at the same 1 GHz / 1 G throughput, yielding 4.29× higher efficiency (3.78 vs. 0.88 G/(μm²·mW)). The area savings stem from replacing 256 FP16 comparators with 10 (two-level address translation) and storing 259 LUT entries instead of 512 (k,b) pairs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the calibration-based LUT fitting paradigm (NN-LUT, RI-LUT) and operator-specific hardware approximations (Softermax, I-BERT) by recasting the classical piecewise-linear-interpolation problem as a dynamic-programming optimization over the FP16 grid. Rather than learning LUT parameters from data or hand-tuning ranges for a specific model family, NLI derives cutpoints purely from the target function's curvature via Bellman's optimality principle, yielding a calibration-free, model-agnostic lookup table. The two-level address translation (10 comparators + per-interval scale) is a hardware co-design contribution that has no direct analogue in prior LUT-based nonlinear units, which typically require O(N) parallel comparators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NLI addresses a concrete and growing bottleneck: as linear layers are increasingly quantized to INT8/INT4, nonlinear operations (SiLU, Softmax, RMSNorm) remain the dominant FP32 cost in LLM inference, and prior hardware approximations were validated only on BERT-scale activation ranges. The calibration-free, DP-optimal formulation means a single LUT per operator is reusable across all models and layers without re-tuning, which simplifies NPU firmware and deployment. Open questions include interaction with aggressive weight/activation quantization (e.g., W4A4), applicability during training (where gradients through the piecewise-linear approximation may be noisy), and whether the O(MN²) DP search remains tractable if the FP16 grid is extended to cover even wider dynamic ranges in future architectures.
