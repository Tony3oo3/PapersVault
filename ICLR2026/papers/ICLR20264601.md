## Problem

While empirical evidence shows that LLM representations encode human-interpretable concepts (sentiment, style, truthfulness), the mechanism by which next-token prediction gives rise to these representations remains unexplained. Existing latent variable models for text either assume continuous variables (inappropriate for discrete language), require invertible latent-to-observed mappings (too restrictive for real text), or impose specific graph structures, leaving a gap in formally linking the next-token prediction objective to latent concept recovery under realistic conditions.

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework takes a text context x and a target token y as input, with the LLM's final-layer representation f_x(x) and output embedding f_y(y) as the objects of analysis. The key output is the identifiability result: f_x(x) ≈ A[log p(c=c_i|x)]_i + b, where A = (L̂^T)^(-1)L is determined by the diversity condition's anchor tokens and the posterior differences across them. For the SAE evaluation, the method consumes counterfactual text pairs (27 pairs from the Big Analogy Test differing in a single binary concept) and produces Pearson correlation scores between SAE features and linear-probe logits, alongside reconstruction MSE.

## Method

The paper introduces a discrete latent variable model p(x,y) = Σ_c p(x|c)p(y|c)p(c) where concepts c form an arbitrary DAG and the latent-to-observed mapping g is explicitly non-invertible (quantified by an error ε). The core proof equates the softmax next-token prediction with the Bayes-rule conditional, takes logarithms, and under a diversity condition (invertibility of a matrix of f_y difference vectors), a TV condition (slow posterior variation across anchor tokens), and a coverage condition (bounded log-posterior differences), solves for f_x(x) as a linear transformation of log p(c|x) plus a vanishing remainder. This yields a unified explanation of the linear representation hypothesis: the same matrix A underlies concepts-as-directions, steering vectors, and linear probing. For practical interpretability, the authors propose structured SAEs that augment the standard p-annealing sparsity objective with a nuclear-norm penalty on a low-rank component R (z = S + R), motivated by the inter-concept dependencies encoded in the latent DAG, and introduce a new evaluation metric measuring Pearson correlation between individual SAE features and supervised linear-probe logits as a proxy for monosemantic concept recovery.

## Results

On the SAE evaluation using 27 counterfactual pairs from the Big Analogy Test dataset, the proposed Structured SAE achieves the highest average Pearson correlation with the true concept posteriors and the lowest reconstruction MSE across Pythia-70m, Pythia-1.4b, and Pythia-2.8b, outperforming Top-k SAE, Batch-top-k SAE, and P-annealing SAE on both metrics. All four SAE variants achieve Pearson correlations below 0.8 (where 1.0 indicates perfect concept recovery), confirming the difficulty of the benchmark. On the identifiability validation, the product A_s × W_s (representation difference matrix times linear classifier weight matrix) approximates the identity matrix across LLaMA-2 and Pythia model families, supporting Corollary 4.3's prediction of linear separability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed Structured SAE against three established sparse autoencoder variants: Top-k SAE (Gao et al., 2025), Batch-top-k SAE (Bussmann et al., 2024), and P-annealing SAE (Karvonen et al., 2024). The Structured SAE, which adds a nuclear-norm (low-rank) regularization term to the standard sparsity penalty, consistently outperforms all three baselines on both the proposed Pearson correlation metric (measuring alignment with true concept posteriors) and standard reconstruction MSE, across Pythia-70m, Pythia-1.4b, and Pythia-2.8b. The advantage is attributed to the structured regularization capturing interdependencies among latent concepts, which pure sparsity regularization fails to model. The paper notes that even the best SAE (Structured SAE) achieves Pearson correlations below 0.8, indicating that the 27-pair counterfactual benchmark is challenging and that no current SAE fully recovers monosemantic concepts. The evaluation framework itself is validated by showing that its rankings are consistent with conventional MSE-based reconstruction metrics, reinforcing its reliability as a concept-alignment measure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the causal representation learning and identifiability literature (Schölkopf et al., 2021; Hyvarinen & Morioka, 2016) to the specific setting of LLM next-token prediction, departing from prior LLM identifiability work (Park et al., 2023; Marconato et al., 2024; Jiang et al., 2024; Rajendran et al., 2024) in three key ways: it models both latent concepts and observed tokens as discrete variables, it does not require invertibility of the latent-to-observed mapping, and it directly connects the identifiability result to the softmax-based next-token prediction objective. For the SAE component, it builds on existing sparse autoencoder variants (top-k, batch-top-k, p-annealing) by adding a low-rank nuclear-norm regularization term, drawing on classical structured sparsity and robust PCA literature (Chandrasekaran et al., 2009; Candès et al., 2011).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identifiability theorem provides the first rigorous theoretical explanation for why LLM representations exhibit the linear structure observed empirically (steering vectors, linear probing, concept directions), unifying these phenomena under a single matrix A derived from the data diversity condition. This opens a principled path toward evaluating SAEs beyond reconstruction loss by measuring alignment with concept posteriors, though the current evaluation is limited to 27 binary counterfactual pairs and the last layer only. Key open questions include extending the analysis to intermediate layers (where linearity often first emerges), relaxing the TV condition which is difficult to verify in practice, and developing methods to linearly unmix representations to directly extract individual concept probabilities.
