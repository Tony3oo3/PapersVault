## Problem

Monolithic deep Q-learning couples representation and value learning in a single network, creating a destructive feedback loop where representations chase non-stationary value targets while value estimates depend on those shifting representations. Existing remedies like auxiliary losses introduce conflicting gradients that trade representation stability against value accuracy. The paper asks whether a principled, game-theoretic restructuring of the optimization can resolve this trade-off without external learning signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260091/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent consumes raw state observations (low-dimensional vectors for MinAtar and classic control, 84×84 RGB frames for Atari) and outputs a discrete action. For recurrent variants (R2D2 on MinGrid), the perception network additionally maintains a GRU hidden state across timesteps, resetting at episode boundaries. Architecturally, the monolithic baseline network is split at a fixed layer boundary: earlier layers form the perception encoder f_φ producing a latent z, and the final layer(s) form the Q-network Q_θ, keeping total parameter count identical to the baseline.

## Method

SCORER recasts the perception–control interaction as a Stackelberg game: the Q-function (leader) commits to parameters on a slow timescale by minimizing MSBE, while the perception encoder (follower) adapts on a fast timescale by minimizing the sample variance of Bellman errors across a batch, with the leader's parameters held fixed via stop-gradient. The Stackelberg equilibrium is approximated by a two-timescale stochastic approximation algorithm where both learning rates decay linearly but satisfy α_θ,k/α_φ,k → 0, ensuring the follower tracks a near-best response to the slowly evolving leader. Within each update step, the follower updates first (using the leader's current parameters), then the leader updates using the follower's freshly updated representation, with stop-gradients preventing gradient flow between the two players. This division of labor—leader minimizes MSBE, follower minimizes its variance—directly counteracts the bias from high-variance bootstrapped targets that causes divergence in off-policy TD learning, as validated on Baird's counterexample where the monolithic baseline diverges but SCORER converges.

## Results

On MinAtar (Table 1), SCORER delivers dramatic sample-efficiency gains: DQN Breakout improves from 19.16 to 65.69 IQM (3.4×), DDQN Breakout from 36.47 to 64.44, and DuelingDQN Asterix from 39.22 to 52.28, all with no computational overhead (speed ratio ≈ 1.00×). On MinGrid, SCORER R2D2 achieves 25–50% faster time-to-threshold and solves Four Rooms (97% success rate) where baseline R2D2 completely fails (0%). On stability benchmarks, SCORER converges on Baird's counterexample where standard Linear TD diverges, and reaches near-complete solved rates on Stochastic Deep Sea where Bootstrapped DQN plateaus at ~20%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCORER consistently outperforms all monolithic DQN-family baselines (DQN, DDQN, DuelingDQN, DuelingDDQN) on MinAtar, with the largest gains in Breakout (e.g., DQN: 19.16→65.69, a 3.4× improvement) and SpaceInvaders (DQN: 127.78→148.71), while Freeway shows minimal change as it is already near-saturated. Against the state-of-the-art PQN baseline, SCORER PQN achieves modest but statistically significant gains on MinAtar (Breakout 69.97→71.16, SpaceInvaders 137.73→141.85) and outperforms vanilla PQN on Atari-5 in Phoenix and Qbert while matching it in BattleZone, DoubleDunk, and NameThisGame. On MinGrid, SCORER R2D2 dominates baseline R2D2 with 25–50% faster convergence and a 10-point success-rate improvement in Dynamic Obstacles (83% vs. 73%), and completely solves Four Rooms (97%) where R2D2 fails entirely (0%). On stability benchmarks, SCORER converges on Baird's counterexample where the Linear TD baseline diverges, and on Stochastic Deep Sea reaches near-complete solved rates versus the Bootstrapped DQN baseline's ~20% plateau. Ablations confirm that inverting the Stackelberg roles (perception as leader) collapses performance below the monolithic baseline, and that the Bellman Error Variance follower objective substantially outperforms an MSBE-based follower, validating the specific game-theoretic design.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCORER departs from the dominant paradigm of coupling representation and value learning within a single monolithic network (DQN, Rainbow) or via auxiliary self-supervised losses (CURL, IQL), which the authors argue introduces conflicting gradients. Instead, it reframes the perception–control interaction as a Stackelberg game, drawing on bilevel optimization theory (Fiez et al., 2020; Hong et al., 2023) and two-timescale stochastic approximation (Borkar, 1997) to create a principled leader–follower hierarchy. The follower's objective—minimizing the sample variance of Bellman errors rather than the MSBE itself—is a novel loss formulation motivated by Baird's (1995) analysis of divergence in off-policy TD learning. The framework is applied to a broad family of existing algorithms (DQN, DDQN, Dueling DQN, R2D2, PQN) without modifying their architectures, positioning SCORER as an orthogonal training-time coupling mechanism rather than a new algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that a simple learning-rate asymmetry plus a variance-minimization objective can stabilize off-policy Q-learning across multiple algorithm families and environments without added compute, SCORER offers a low-friction, drop-in improvement that practitioners can adopt immediately. The ablation demonstrating that inverting the Stackelberg roles collapses performance below the monolithic baseline provides strong evidence that the specific hierarchical structure (control leads, perception follows) is essential, not incidental. Key limitations include restriction to discrete action spaces and value-based methods, absence of a sample-complexity analysis, and reliance on a single variance objective; extending the framework to actor-critic algorithms for continuous control and deriving information-theoretic follower objectives are natural next steps that could broaden its applicability.
