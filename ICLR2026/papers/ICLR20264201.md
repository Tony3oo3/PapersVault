## Problem

Current second-order optimizers for LLMs (Shampoo, SOAP, Muon) rely on computationally efficient layerwise Hessian approximations, but it remains unclear how much performance is forfeited by these approximations. This paper asks: what are the fundamental performance limits of second-order optimization for LLMs, and which structural properties of the Hessian (full vs. layerwise, second-order vs. higher-order loss terms) are essential for achieving them?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264201/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on LLaMA transformer models (45M and 150M parameters) trained on C4 text sequences, consuming the current model parameters, a batch of training sequences, and a batch size configuration. Rather than materializing the full Gauss-Newton matrix, it computes the quadratic objective and its gradient using Jacobian-vector products (JVPs) via the neural-tangents library, requiring only one additional forward pass per inner optimization step. The output is a parameter update applied via line search, with performance quantified in iteration complexity (steps to reach a target validation loss) and critical batch size scaling behavior across batch sizes from 1.2M to 120M tokens.

## Method

The core method applies full Gauss-Newton preconditioning by minimizing a second-order Taylor approximation of the loss evaluated on a first-order Taylor approximation of the model, which is provably equivalent to the update θ* = θ − G⁻¹g where G is the full Gauss-Newton matrix. To avoid explicit Hessian storage, the quadratic objective and its gradient are computed through JVPs, and an inner optimizer (Muon) solves the resulting least squares subproblem over N steps before a line search determines the final step size. The paper further isolates the role of higher-order loss terms by comparing to GN-prox-linear (which minimizes the full convex loss on the linearized model) and the role of cross-layer curvature by comparing to a layerwise variant that optimizes each layer's second-order Taylor expansion independently. Key findings: full GN achieves a 5.4× reduction in iterations over SOAP, the layerwise variant nearly matches full GN (only 1.4× more steps), and higher-order loss terms beyond second-order contribute negligibly to convergence speed.

## Results

Full Gauss-Newton reaches validation loss 3.25 in 54 training steps at large batch size (240M tokens), a 5.4× reduction over SOAP (292 steps) and a 16× reduction over Muon. The layerwise Gauss-Newton variant, which ignores all cross-layer curvature, requires only 78 steps (1.4× more than full GN) and still delivers a 3.4× gain over SOAP. At batch size 120M tokens, Gauss-Newton achieves loss 3.45 in just 20 steps, while AdamW degrades to loss above 4.4 at the same batch size. The GN-prox-linear variant (which retains higher-order loss terms) performs comparably to Gauss-Newton, indicating that second-order loss curvature is sufficient.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SOAP (the strongest existing second-order baseline), full Gauss-Newton achieves a 5.4× reduction in iteration complexity (54 vs. 292 steps to loss 3.25) and extends the critical batch size well beyond 40M tokens, whereas SOAP plateaus around 12–40M tokens. Against Muon, Gauss-Newton delivers a 16× iteration complexity gain and continues to benefit from increasing batch size through 40M tokens, while Muon shows diminishing returns beyond 12M. Against AdamW, the gap is largest at high batch sizes: at 120M tokens, Gauss-Newton reaches loss 3.45 in 20 steps while AdamW degrades to loss above 4.4, illustrating that first-order methods lose sample efficiency far earlier. The layerwise Gauss-Newton variant (which discards all cross-layer Hessian information) nearly matches full GN (78 vs. 54 steps), suggesting that per-layer curvature captures most of the available second-order benefit. The GN-prox-linear comparison shows that including higher-order loss terms beyond the quadratic approximation yields no meaningful improvement, confirming that the Gauss-Newton (second-order) approximation is the critical component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Hessian-free optimization lineage (Martens, 2010; Martens & Sutskever, 2011b) by leveraging the equivalence between minimizing a second-order Taylor approximation of the loss and applying the full Gauss-Newton preconditioner, but departs from prior second-order LLM optimizers (Shampoo, SOAP, Muon) that rely on computationally efficient layerwise Hessian approximations. Rather than proposing a cheaper approximation, the paper uses Jacobian-vector products to compute the full GN step (with Muon as an inner solver), establishing a practical upper bound on what second-order information can achieve. The layerwise GN variant and GN-prox-linear ablation are novel decompositions that isolate which structural components of the Hessian and loss curvature actually drive the gains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that a layerwise GN preconditioner nearly matches the full GN method, suggesting that future practical optimizers need not model cross-layer curvature to capture most of the available second-order benefit. The finding that higher-order loss terms are negligible (GN ≈ GN-prox-linear) further narrows the design space. However, the study is limited to 45M–150M parameter models and the full GN implementation is ~1.5× slower than standard training, so the 5.4× iteration reduction is an upper bound rather than a deployable result. The significant gap between SOAP and the layerwise oracle points to a concrete target for future work: designing computationally efficient per-layer preconditioners that better approximate the true layerwise GN matrix.
