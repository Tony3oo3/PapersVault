## Problem

KV cache technology reduces inference latency in sequential recommendation but incurs prohibitive per-user storage overhead at scale (hundreds of millions of users with long histories). Existing KV compression methods designed for LLMs compress individual sequences in isolation and fail to exploit the strong collaborative signals that naturally exist across users' K/V representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes user history sequence embeddings (n × d_e) and a target item embedding, producing a compressed KV cache consisting of a low-dimensional user-specific KV (n × d_u, where d_u is typically ~4% of the attention head dimension) and a set of integer global KV indices (n × 1) that point into a shared pool. At decode time, the indices gather high-dimensional collective KV from a GPU-resident pool, which is concatenated with the user-specific KV to reconstruct the full attention keys/values (n × d_a). The final output is a CTR prediction score, with the overall KV cache compressed to as little as 0.8% of its original size.

## Method

CollectiveKV decomposes each item's KV into a low-dimensional user-specific component (linear projection of the sequence embedding) and a high-dimensional collective component retrieved from a learnable global K/V pool (m × d_g) via a router network that maps each item embedding to a pool index. During prefill, only the small user-specific KV and the integer indices are cached externally; during decode, the indices perform a fast GPU-memory gather from the pool, avoiding the expensive CPU-to-GPU transfer of full KV. Training introduces two auxiliary losses: a peak loss that pushes the router's sigmoid-gated outputs toward 1 (ensuring train-inference consistency since the argmax index is non-differentiable), and a load-balance loss (KL divergence to uniform) that prevents the router from collapsing onto a few pool entries. SVD analysis motivates the design by showing that the principal (high-dimensional) subspace of KV exhibits strong cross-user correlation while the residual (low-dimensional) subspace is user-specific, justifying the asymmetric dimension split.

## Results

CollectiveKV compresses the KV cache to as little as 0.8% of its original size (CR = 0.008 for SDIM on Ebnerd) while maintaining or improving recommendation accuracy across all five models and three datasets. On the MicroVideo dataset, SIM+ours achieves GAUC 0.6973 (vs. 0.6954 baseline) and AUC 0.7057 (vs. 0.6933) at a compression rate of 0.016. Inference latency drops dramatically: at batch size 512, CollectiveKV takes 0.695 ms versus 32.991 ms for the standard KV cache on an A100 GPU, a ~47× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CollectiveKV is evaluated as an add-on module on five sequential recommendation baselines: SIM, SDIM, ETA, TWIN (target-attention models), and HSTU (self-attention model). On SIM and SDIM, CollectiveKV consistently improves GAUC, AUC, and Logloss across all three datasets (e.g., SIM on MicroVideo: GAUC 0.6973 vs. 0.6954, AUC 0.7057 vs. 0.6933). On ETA, the method wins on MicroVideo and Ebnerd across all metrics, but on KuaiVideo GAUC drops slightly (0.6677 vs. 0.6699) while AUC and Logloss still improve. On TWIN, CollectiveKV improves GAUC and AUC on all datasets, though Logloss on KuaiVideo is marginally worse (0.4627 vs. 0.4618). On HSTU, gains are smaller but consistent: MicroVideo GAUC improves from 0.6859 to 0.6862 and AUC from 0.7381 to 0.7385, while KuaiVideo GAUC and AUC improve (0.6668/0.7475 vs. 0.6663/0.7454) at a slight Logloss cost. Overall, the method achieves compression rates between 0.8% and 4.3% of the original KV cache size while matching or exceeding baseline accuracy in nearly all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the KV cache paradigm introduced for sequential recommendation by HSTU (Zhai et al., 2024) and MARM (Lv et al., 2024), which precompute and store keys/values to reduce inference latency. It departs from the LLM KV compression literature (Loki, Quest, InfiniGen, MLA) that compresses individual sequences by reducing length or dimension, instead exploiting a property unique to recommendation: cross-user collaborative signals embedded in K/V representations. The SVD-based analysis showing that the principal component subspace of KV is highly correlated across users while the residual is not provides the empirical foundation for the proposed decomposition into a low-dimensional user-specific component and a high-dimensional shared global pool accessed via a learned router.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial for large-scale recommendation systems serving hundreds of millions of users: compressing the per-user KV cache to ~0.8% of its original size while maintaining or slightly improving accuracy directly addresses the storage and GPU-to-CPU transfer bottleneck that makes KV caching impractical at scale. The 47× latency reduction at batch size 512 (Table 2) makes the technique immediately deployable. Open questions include how to automatically determine the optimal user-specific dimension (currently set empirically at ~4% of head dimension), how the global pool size should scale with user population growth, and whether the cross-user sharing paradigm generalizes to other sequential or collaborative prediction tasks beyond CTR.
