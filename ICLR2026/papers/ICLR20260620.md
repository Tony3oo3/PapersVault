## Problem

Existing self-supervised visual representation methods (VAEs, contrastive learning, masked autoencoders) optimize pixel-level objectives that fail to capture spatial correlations between discrete objects or events in images. This limits their ability to distinguish, for example, attractive from repulsive point patterns, and provides no statistical framework for rigorous downstream analysis of spatial organization. The paper asks whether spatial point-process statistics can serve as a self-supervision prior that yields interpretable, mechanistically meaningful representations of spatial localization patterns.

## Input / Output

The model takes 256×256 fluorescence microscopy images (or synthetic point-pattern images rendered with Gaussian point-spread functions and Poisson/Gaussian noise) as input. During training it additionally consumes weakly labeled point locations (from spot detectors like Spotiflow or Otsu thresholding), but at inference only the raw image is required. It produces a 64-dimensional latent code z, a spatially varying Papangelou conditional intensity λ_ξ(X,u|z) over the eroded domain D, and—via MCMC sampling—zero-shot simulated point patterns conditioned on a single query image.

## Method

SI-VAE augments a standard convolutional VAE (3-layer encoder/decoder, 64-dim latent space) with a lightweight prediction head consisting of two two-layer neural networks that model the first-order potential φ_ξ(u,z) and second-order potential ψ_ξ(||u−v||₂,z) of a Gibbs point process. The key self-supervision target is the log-pseudo-likelihood of the Papangelou conditional intensity λ_ξ(X,u|z)=exp{−φ_ξ(u,z)−Σ_v w_uv ψ_ξ(||u−v||₂,z)}, which avoids the intractable partition function of the full Gibbs density. The second-order potential is constrained to be isotropic, symmetric, and weighted by exponential distance-decay kernels w_uv=exp{−||u−v||₂/(2L²)} to ensure identifiability of local interactions from density inhomogeneity. The total loss is the ELBO of the joint model p(X,x,z)=p(X|z)p(x|z)p(z), combining image reconstruction (MSE), point-process pseudo-likelihood, and a β-weighted KL term (β=0.1), trained jointly with Adam at lr=10⁻⁵. This makes the latent space directly interpretable in spatial-statistics terms: the learned potentials encode first-order intensity structure and second-order pairwise interactions (attraction/repulsion), enabling both classification of point-pattern types and zero-shot conditional simulation via birth-death MCMC.

## Results

On synthetic point-pattern classification (6 classes: homogeneous/inhomogeneous × Poisson/Strauss/Thomas), SI-VAE achieves 90% linear-probe accuracy and F1 with ground-truth point locations at SNR 12.8, compared to 48% for a standard VAE with identical architecture. Even with imperfect weak labels from simple Otsu thresholding, SI-VAE retains 80% accuracy, while the VAE baseline remains at 48%. On the OpenCell protein-localization dataset, SI-VAE representations yield a GMM cluster Silhouette score of 0.29 (vesicle vs. nuclear proteins), versus 0.04 for the VAE baseline. Zero-shot conditional simulation from a single query image produces point patterns with relative intensity errors as low as 0.14 (inhomogeneous Strauss) and K-function rejection rates below 0.20 across all process types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against a standard VAE with identical architecture (the only difference being the absence of the point-process supervision target), SI-VAE wins decisively: 90% vs. 48% linear-probe accuracy on the 6-class synthetic benchmark, a gap that persists even at lower SNR (88% vs. 48%). A mask VAE trained with ground-truth binary object-location masks performs better than the image-only VAE (63% vs. 48%) but still falls well short of SI-VAE (90%), confirming that pixel-level spatial information alone is insufficient to capture interaction structure. On the OpenCell dataset, the specialized Cytoself model (Kobayashi et al., 2022) achieves a slightly higher Silhouette score (0.33 vs. 0.29 for SI-VAE) for separating vesicle- from nuclear-localizing proteins, but Cytoself relies on a substantially deeper architecture (two VQ-VAEs plus a classification head) and requires ground-truth protein labels during training, whereas SI-VAE is fully self-supervised and provides interpretable Gibbs-potential outputs. SI-VAE also generalizes to an unseen log-Gaussian Cox process, correctly classifying it as belonging to the Thomas (clustering) family, while the VAE baseline fails to generalize at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

SI-VAE extends the standard VAE framework (Kingma & Welling, 2014) by replacing the pixel-intensity reconstruction target with a spatial point-process likelihood based on the Papangelou conditional intensity and its pseudo-likelihood approximation (Møller & Waagepetersen, 2007). This departs from prior self-supervised approaches to microscopy representation learning—contrastive methods relying on pixel similarity, Cytoself's semi-supervised classification pretext, and masked autoencoders—by grounding the self-supervision in the statistical structure of spatial point patterns rather than image appearance. The formulation also generalizes the Bayesian variational framework of Zhou et al. (2022) from parametric point-process models to non-parametric neural estimation, and connects to hybrid multimodal VAEs (Nalisnick et al., 2019; Wu & Goodman, 2018) through its joint image-and-point-process generative model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SI-VAE provides a statistically interpretable bridge between deep representation learning and spatial point-process statistics, enabling mechanistic explanations of learned spatial patterns (e.g., distinguishing short-range repulsion from long-range attraction in protein localization) and zero-shot conditional simulation of point processes from a single query image—a capability the authors note is the first instance for image-based point-process simulation. Key limitations include the restriction to pairwise, isotropic interactions, the need for a post-hoc saturation parameter for clustering processes, and sensitivity to spot-detection errors in dense regions. Open directions include extending to non-pairwise interactions (score matching, Deep Sets), anisotropic and marked point processes, and more expressive latent priors (GP-VAE, VampPrior).
