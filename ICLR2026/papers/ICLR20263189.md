## Problem

Modern multimodal conversational agents depend on long, reasoning-intensive in-context policies (1K–50K tokens) that impose a 20×–250× fixed token cost over typical user queries and are increasingly difficult for models to follow faithfully. Prior prompt-compression work targets only simple templates and demonstrations, while policy-alignment research is confined to text-only safety specifications; no prior work addresses internalizing complex, multimodal, reasoning-intensive policies into model parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263189/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives only a text query Q and one or more visual inputs I (e.g., CLEVR scene images or real-world photos with bounding boxes) and must produce a policy-compliant response without the policy P in context. The output is either a free-form decision label (ClevrPolicy) or a JSON-formatted tool call specifying tool name, version, and typed arguments such as bounding boxes, text queries, and numerical expressions (GTAPolicy). The policy P itself may contain both textual rules and visual demonstration crops (ClevrPolicy-M), and is available exclusively during training.

## Method

TriMPI is a three-stage training framework: (1) Visually-Masked Continual Pretraining (VM-CPT) performs next-token prediction over the full policy context concatenated with inputs and CoT, masking all visual tokens so that policy knowledge is injected directly into parameters; (2) CoT SFT fine-tunes on chain-of-thought annotations generated by a stronger model; (3) an RL stage using GRPO or DAPO with a novel PolicyRollout (PoRo) extension. PolicyRollout augments each rollout group by additionally sampling responses conditioned on the policy in-context, then computes group-based advantage estimation over the combined set while applying the policy gradient only to the no-policy path, thereby providing policy-grounded exploration without creating a train–inference mismatch. This design yields up to 70.7% and 79.4% absolute accuracy gains over CoT SFT and in-context baselines respectively, with consistent improvements across policy complexities and model sizes.

## Results

On ClevrPolicy-M (N=6), TriMPI w/PoRo-DAPO achieves 85.00% accuracy, representing a 70.7% absolute gain over the CoT SFT baseline (14.30%) and a 79.4% gain over the in-context setting (5.65%). On GTAPolicy, TriMPI w/PoRo-GRPO reaches 81.06% overall score (85.85% tool accuracy, 76.28% argument score), substantially outperforming CoT SFT (54.50%) and in-context (21.51%). The paper also reports up to 93.9% reduction in prompt tokens and 85.7% reduction in prefill inference time after internalization, and TriMPI preserves general reasoning (MMMU-Pro, MMLU-Pro) and safety (WildGuardTest) performance where baselines suffer significant degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TriMPI with PolicyRollout consistently outperforms all baselines across both datasets. On ClevrPolicy-M (N=6), TriMPI w/PoRo-DAPO (85.00%) surpasses CoT SFT+DAPO (74.40%) by 10.6 points and CoT SFT+GRPO (64.50%) by 20.5 points, while on ClevrPolicy-T (N=6) it leads by 10.2 points over CoT SFT+DAPO (67.60%). On GTAPolicy, TriMPI w/PoRo-GRPO achieves 81.06% overall, beating CoT SFT+GRPO (72.22%) by 8.8 points and CoT SFT+DAPO (72.43%) by 8.6 points. The VM-CPT stage contributes additional gains over SFT+RL alone (e.g., 84.70% vs. 64.50% on ClevrPolicy-M with GRPO), and PolicyRollout further improves over standard GRPO/DAPO (e.g., 84.70% vs. 80.80% on ClevrPolicy-M). Notably, DAPO outperforms GRPO on ClevrPolicy (more data) while GRPO excels on GTAPolicy (limited data), suggesting DAPO's bolder updates cause overfitting in low-data regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends deliberative alignment (Guan et al., 2024), which internalized text-only safety specifications into model parameters, to the multimodal domain and to diverse reasoning-intensive agent policies (decision-making, tool-use) beyond safety. The VM-CPT stage adapts continual pretraining techniques from Knowledge-Instruct (Ovadia et al., 2025) and Maini et al. (2024) to handle visual tokens via masking, while PolicyRollout draws on the rollout-augmentation idea from NoisyRollout and R1-ShareVL but substitutes policy context for visual noise as the augmentation signal. The RL backbone builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and DAPO (Yu et al., 2025), with the key modification being the inclusion of policy-conditioned rollouts in the group while computing gradients only on the no-policy path.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in production conversational agents where policies of 1K–50K tokens impose 20–250× the input cost of typical queries, and the three-stage TriMPI recipe (VM-CPT → CoT SFT → PolicyRollout RL) offers a concrete, reproducible path to internalize such policies while preserving general capabilities. The Policy Override and Policy Referral evaluations are useful additions that go beyond end-task accuracy to probe whether the model truly absorbed policy structure. Key limitations include reliance on a synthetic CLEVR-based dataset for the main analysis, only two base model sizes (3B/7B), and the open question of whether the approach scales to policies with very different response formats or to substantially larger models.
