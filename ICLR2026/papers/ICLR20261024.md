## Problem

Prior work on accelerating diffusion model training via representation guidance relies on large external pretrained models (e.g., DINOv2, 9B-parameter VLMs), introducing costly dependencies, extra inference overhead, and domain limitations. The only self-contained alternative, Dispersive Loss, avoids external models but leaves a substantial performance gap. This paper asks whether a diffusion model's own semantically rich deeper layers can serve as intrinsic guidance for its weaker earlier layers, closing that gap without any external supervision.

## Input / Output

For image generation, 256×256 RGB images are encoded to 32×32×4 latents via the Stable Diffusion VAE and conditioned on class labels; for audio, 10-second 44.1 kHz clips are encoded via Stable Audio Open VAE and conditioned on genre/instrument labels; for motion, 22-joint 3D poses (237-dim vectors) are conditioned on text descriptions. LayerSync operates entirely on the patch-wise intermediate feature maps inside the transformer (e.g., 32×32 spatial patches of dimension D), requiring no modification to the input pipeline or output head. The method is applied identically across all modalities with only the layer indices and λ adjusted per architecture size.

## Method

LayerSync introduces a parameter-free regularization term that maximizes cosine similarity between patch-wise features of a designated 'weak' earlier layer k and a 'strong' deeper layer k′ (with stop-gradient on k′), added as λ·L_LayerSync to the standard velocity-matching loss. The key insight is that representation quality is highly heterogeneous across transformer depth—deeper layers (just before the decoder) are semantically richer—so the model's own strongest layers provide a directed, self-contained learning signal for weaker ones. Layer selection follows three heuristics: exclude the final 20% of blocks (decoder-specialized), exclude the very first blocks (local-feature specialists), and enforce a minimum inter-layer distance (e.g., 8 blocks for SiT-XL). The loss scales linearly with batch size (O(BD)) versus the quadratic O(B²D) of Dispersive Loss, and the method is robust to layer-pair choice (std FID ≈ 0.8 across random pairings) and to a wide range of λ values (0.1–0.7).

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, LayerSync achieves FID 1.89 at 800 epochs (with CFG) and FID 6.87 at 800 epochs (without CFG), representing a 23.6% improvement over the SiT-XL/2 baseline (FID 8.99) and an 8.75× training acceleration (reaching FID 8.29 at just 160 epochs vs. 1400 for the baseline). For audio generation on MTG-Jamendo, LayerSync improves FAD-10K by 20.7% (0.199 vs. 0.251 at 650 epochs). For human motion on HumanML3D, FID improves by 7.7% (0.4801 vs. 0.5206), and for video on CLEVRER, FVD improves by 54.7% (120.13 vs. 265.50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LayerSync consistently outperforms the only other self-contained zero-cost method, Dispersive Loss (Wang & He, 2025), across all model sizes: on SiT-XL/2 at 800 epochs, FID is 6.87 vs. 8.08 (23.6% vs. 10.1% improvement over baseline), and at 80 epochs, 11.24 vs. 15.95. Against external-guidance methods, LayerSync (FID 1.89 at 800 epochs, or 1.49 with CFG scheduling) is competitive with REPA (1.80 / 1.42) and REED (1.80 at 200 epochs) while requiring no pretrained external model. Compared to the EMA-based SRA (Jiang et al., 2025), LayerSync achieves a lower FID (1.49 vs. 1.58 with CFG scheduling) while being 40.5% faster per step and using 25.5% fewer FLOPs. LayerSync also surpasses the SiT-XL/2 baseline by 8.75× in training speed (FID 8.29 at 160 epochs vs. 8.3 at 1400 epochs) and outperforms DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27 at 1400 epochs) with fewer training epochs. In non-visual domains, LayerSync improves FAD by 20.7% on audio, FID by 7.7% on human motion, and FVD by 54.7% on video, with no direct baselines reported for these settings beyond vanilla training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on SiT (Ma et al., 2024) as its base architecture and positions itself within the growing line of work on representation regularization for diffusion transformers. It departs from the dominant paradigm of external guidance (REPA with DINOv2, REED with VLMs) and from EMA-based self-alignment (SRA) by proposing a parameter-free, zero-overhead approach: simply maximizing cosine similarity between a model's own shallow and deep intermediate layers. The closest prior work is the Dispersive Loss (Wang & He, 2025), which is also self-contained but undirected; LayerSync introduces a directed learning signal by exploiting the known hierarchy of representation quality across depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LayerSync offers a practically significant efficiency win (8.75× training acceleration on ImageNet 256×256) with no external model dependency, making it immediately adoptable across modalities where pretrained guidance models are unavailable (audio, motion, video). The finding that a trivially simple alignment loss can close most of the gap to methods relying on 9B-parameter VLMs challenges the assumption that strong external supervision is necessary for fast diffusion training. Open questions include whether the alignment loss should be domain-specific (e.g., temporal for video, hierarchical for text), whether long-term training exhibits degradation, and whether the improved inter-layer correlation enables meaningful model compression.
