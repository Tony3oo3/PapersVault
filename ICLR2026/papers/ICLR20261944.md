## Problem

LLM-based formal theorem provers are fragile to even minor transformations of problem statements (e.g., variable renaming, algebraic rewrites), revealing that they fail to learn the underlying symmetry structure of mathematical problems. Existing data synthesis methods (DeepSeek-Prover, Goedel-Prover, STP) expand data volume but do not systematically address this robustness gap from either a symmetry or difficulty perspective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261944/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a Lean 4 formal theorem statement (hypotheses and conclusion in Lean 4 syntax) and directly generates a complete Lean 4 proof without any chain-of-thought intermediate reasoning. Because the training data includes natural language restatements alongside formal statements, the model's output typically begins with a restatement of the problem before the proof, making its average token length (~654) somewhat longer than other non-CoT provers (~116–395). All generated proofs must pass Lean 4 compiler verification to be considered correct.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-stage data augmentation pipeline applied to ~70k seed theorems from DeepSeek-Prover-V1 and STP-Lean. First, EvolDomain uses an LLM to abstract a theorem's logical skeleton and re-instantiate it in 3–5 different mathematical domains (semantic symmetry), while EvolDifficulty applies five expert-designed evolution strategies (adjusting logical structure, mathematical depth, abstraction, constraints, and parameters) in both upward and downward directions to broaden the difficulty spectrum. A two-stage verification (Lean 4 compilation + LLM semantic judgment) filters the evolved statements. Second, EvolAST deterministically parses each verified statement into an Abstract Syntax Tree and applies 7 logical-equivalence rewriting rules (commutativity, associativity, distributivity, De Morgan's laws, etc.) with a probability parameter to generate syntactically diverse but semantically identical variants, requiring no further verification. The pipeline ordering is deliberate: LLM-based stages (modeled as exponential instability amplification) run before the deterministic AST stage (linear amplification) to avoid prematurely magnifying noise. The final ~39.2k verified (statement, proof) pairs are used to fine-tune DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5-Base via SFT followed by PPO-based reinforcement learning with a binary correctness reward, yielding the non-reasoning EvolProver model.

## Results

On FormalMATH-Lite, EvolProver achieves a new SOTA of 53.86% pass@32 among models of comparable size, surpassing the previous best reasoning model DeepSeek-Prover-V2 (CoT) at 51.76%. On MiniF2F-Test, it sets a new SOTA for non-reasoning models at 69.80% pass@32. On Ineq-Comp, EvolProver achieves 52.20% pass@32 on seed problems, 34.02% on transformed variants, and a robustness ratio of 65.17%, all SOTA for non-reasoning models of comparable size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvolProver outperforms all non-reasoning baselines on FormalMATH-Lite, beating the strongest non-reasoning competitor DeepSeek-Prover-V2-7B-non-CoT (50.35%) by 3.51 points and STP (48.59%) by 5.27 points. It also surpasses both reasoning models—DeepSeek-Prover-V2 CoT (51.76%) by 2.10 points and Kimina-Prover-Preview (48.94%) by 4.92 points—while using roughly 7–9× fewer tokens per proof. On Ineq-Comp, EvolProver sets new SOTA for non-reasoning models on all three metrics (seed 52.20%, transformed 34.02%, ratio 65.17%), outperforming DeepSeek-Prover-V2-7B-non-CoT by 6.20 points on seed and 16.27 points on the robustness ratio, though it trails the reasoning model DeepSeek-Prover-V2 CoT on seed (66.23%) and ratio (67.23%). On MiniF2F-Test, EvolProver achieves 69.80% pass@32, establishing a new SOTA for non-reasoning models of comparable size. The ablation baseline EvolProver-Base (trained without augmentation) scores 44.71% on FormalMATH-Lite, confirming a 9.15-point gain from the full data augmentation pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Evol-Instruct lineage (WizardMath, WizardCoder) from informal math problem generation to the formal theorem proving setting, repurposing the 'evolve existing problems' paradigm for Lean 4 statements. It departs from prior formal data synthesis work (DeepSeek-Prover, STP, Goedel-Prover-V2) by evolving already-proven formal statements directly rather than translating from natural language or generating from scratch, and introduces a deterministic AST-based transformation (EvolAST) that guarantees semantic equivalence—addressing the error-proneness of purely LLM-based evolution. The robustness motivation draws on the Ineq-Comp and PutnamGAP benchmarks, which documented fragility of provers under minor perturbations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pipeline offers a practical, extensible recipe for improving formal prover generalizability: EvolAST's rule-based design means the community can incrementally add new equivalence rules without retraining, and the non-reasoning model's competitive performance (53.86% on FormalMATH-Lite) at ~10× lower token cost than reasoning models has direct practical implications for deployment. The 30.74 pp robustness-ratio improvement on Ineq-Comp is the most consequential finding, suggesting that symmetry-aware data augmentation is a viable path to closing the human-vs-prover gap on perturbation robustness. Open questions include whether the pipeline generalizes beyond Lean 4, whether the 30% semantic failure rate of LLM-based evolution can be further reduced, and whether the approach transfers to other proof assistants or to reasoning (CoT) models.
