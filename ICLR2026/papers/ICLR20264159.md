## Problem

Over-squashing in GNNs has been studied predominantly through the lens of low sensitivity (vanishing Jacobian norms), but the original formulation by Alon & Yahav (2021) identified a distinct failure mode: saturating the fixed-size storage capacity of node representations. Existing synthetic benchmarks (Ring-Transfer, Tree-NeighborsMatch) conflate these two issues or fail to test capacity at all, and no prior architecture has attempted to increase storage capacity directly at the model level rather than merely reducing information flow.

## Input / Output

The model takes a graph with per-node feature vectors and an adjacency matrix, and produces node-level or graph-level predictions (classification or regression). Unusually, each node maintains both a standard vector hidden state and a matrix hidden state (the associative memory), and the query that retrieves information from this memory is computed from the concatenation of the node's own previous state with the aggregated states of its neighbors, allowing the retrieval to depend separately on self and neighborhood context. The k-hop aggregation scheme means that at layer l, each node aggregates from all nodes at exact graph distance l rather than only immediate neighbors.

## Method

gLSTM adapts the mLSTM block from xLSTM to the graph message-passing setting by maintaining a per-node matrix hidden state C updated via a fast-weight-programmer outer-product rule, where keys and values are linear projections of the previous vector hidden state. The query vector is computed from the concatenation of the node's own state and its aggregated neighbor states, enabling selective retrieval from the associative memory. Exponential (rather than sigmoid) gating for input and forget gates, inherited from xLSTM, allows the model to revise storage decisions. A k-hop aggregation scheme replaces standard neighborhood aggregation: at layer l, node u collects messages from all nodes at graph distance l, creating a highly connected computational graph that both reduces sensitivity bottlenecks and provides an inductive bias for the recall mechanism by ensuring previously stored information is not re-injected in later rounds. The block structure includes residual connections and input/hidden norms to regulate Jacobian magnitudes, and the architecture is trained end-to-end with standard cross-entropy or MSE loss.

## Results

On the GPP long-range benchmarks, gLSTM achieves state-of-the-art log₁₀(MSE) of −0.715±0.030 on Diameter and −4.036±0.311 on Eccentricity, substantially outperforming the next-best baselines (A-DGN at −0.546 and 0.305 respectively). On SSSP, gLSTM (−2.836±0.178) is second only to A-DGN (−3.402±0.137). On LRGB Peptides-Func, gLSTM achieves the best AP of 0.7250±0.0023, surpassing kGCN-SSM (0.6902) and GCN (0.6860), though GCN retains a slight edge on MAE (0.2460 vs. 0.2527). On the synthetic NAR task, gLSTM maintains perfect recall until the neighbor count equals its memory dimension, whereas the largest GCN tested begins to fail at just N=8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

gLSTM decisively outperforms all standard MPNN baselines (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE, GIN, GCNII, DGC, GRAND) on all three GPP tasks, with the largest margin on Eccentricity where it achieves −4.036 versus the next-best A-DGN at 0.305. Against the strongest dedicated long-range baseline A-DGN, gLSTM wins on Diameter (−0.715 vs. −0.546) and Eccentricity (−4.036 vs. 0.305) but loses on SSSP (−2.836 vs. −3.402). On LRGB Peptides-Func, gLSTM achieves the best AP (0.7250) over kGCN-SSM (0.6902), DRew-GCN (0.6804), and GCN (0.6860), though GCN retains a marginal MAE advantage (0.2460 vs. 0.2527). Against Graph Transformer baselines (GPS, GOAT, Polynormer) on OGBN-arxiv, gLSTM (71.91%) is competitive but trails Polynormer (73.46%) and ChebNet (73.27%). On heterophilic datasets, gLSTM achieves solid MPNN-level performance (e.g., 88.12% on Roman-Empire) but is outperformed by Graph Transformers like Polynormer (92.55%) and CO-GNN (91.57%). The k-hop aggregation ablation is critical: removing it degrades gLSTM from −0.715 to 0.042 on Diameter and from 0.7250 to 0.6030 AP on Peptides-Func, confirming it as a key inductive bias for long-range tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions over-squashing research by splitting it into two distinct failure modes—capacity over-squashing (saturating storage) and sensitivity over-squashing (vanishing Jacobian norms)—reviving the original capacity framing of Alon & Yahav (2021) that was largely subsumed by the sensitivity-based analysis of Topping et al. (2022). Architecturally, it translates the associative memory and exponential gating of xLSTM (Beck et al., 2024) into a message-passing graph setting, using the FWP-style outer-product update rule (Schlag et al., 2021) to maintain a per-node matrix memory, and combines this with the k-hop aggregation scheme from Arroyo et al. (2025) to create a highly connected computational graph. The key departure from prior over-squashing work is targeting capacity at the architecture level rather than mitigating topological or computational bottlenecks through rewiring or adaptive message passing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean separation of capacity from sensitivity over-squashing, validated by the NAR synthetic task and Jacobian/Hessian analyses, gives the field a more precise diagnostic vocabulary and a benchmark that isolates storage limitations from depth-related gradient issues. The gLSTM architecture achieves strong SOTA results on long-range GPP and LRGB benchmarks, demonstrating that associative memory is a practical remedy for capacity over-squashing. However, the paper acknowledges that no theoretical characterization of capacity exists (unlike the Jacobian bound for sensitivity), the architecture lacks the parallelism and efficiency of xLSTM for large-scale training, and k-hop aggregation—while critical for long-range tasks—hurts performance on heterophilic and SSSP benchmarks, suggesting the inductive bias is task-specific. Future work on efficient parallelizable variants and theoretical capacity bounds would strengthen the contribution's practical and foundational impact.
