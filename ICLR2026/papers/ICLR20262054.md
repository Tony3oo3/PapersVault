## Problem

PU learning research is systematically hindered by the absence of a standardized benchmark: prior studies employ inconsistent data generation (different sampling schemes, label ratios, and labeling assumptions), disparate training protocols, and divergent evaluation metrics, making cross-method comparisons unreliable and performance claims difficult to verify. This paper introduces PU-Bench, the first unified open-source benchmark that standardizes the entire experimental pipeline for rigorous and reproducible evaluation of PU learning algorithms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262054/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark ingests standard multi-class or binary classification datasets across five modalities (text at 384-dim embeddings, 28×28 grayscale, 32×32 RGB, 128×128 medical MRI, and 57/126-dim tabular features) and converts them into PU scenarios via a configurable pipeline that specifies the sampling scheme (single-training-set or case-control), label ratio c, and labeling mechanism (SCAR or three SAR variants). Each method receives a labeled-positive set LP and an unlabeled set U (a mixture of hidden positives and true negatives), and produces a binary decision function h_θ(x) estimating the posterior p(Y=1|X=x), evaluated on a held-out ground-truth test set using accuracy, precision, recall, macro-F1, and AUC.

## Method

PU-Bench is built on three interoperable components: (1) a PU Data Generator that binarizes multi-class datasets, applies a chosen sampling scheme (ss or cc), and simulates labeling via four propensity-based strategies (uniform SCAR, posterior-weighted, boundary-weighted, and posterior-sharpening) at configurable label ratios; (2) a Unified Training Pipeline that instantiates 18 state-of-the-art PU methods from external YAML descriptors with shared optimizer, schedule, and backbone specifications (MLP, LeNet, custom CNN, or MRI-specific CNN), ensuring all methods train under identical conditions; (3) a Performance Evaluation Suite that logs five predictive metrics plus wall-clock time and peak GPU memory per epoch. The empirical study executes 2,880 controlled evaluations (18 methods × 8 datasets × 20 configurations covering 14 label ratios under SCAR and 3 SAR mechanisms at 2 label frequencies), with statistical significance assessed via Holm-Bonferroni-corrected paired t-tests against the nnPU baseline.

## Results

Under the conventional setting (case-control sampling, SCAR labeling, c=0.1), no single PU method dominates all 8 datasets. LBE-PU achieves the highest accuracy on MNIST (97.23%) and F-MNIST (98.42%), Dist-PU leads on ADNI (75.02%) and CIFAR-10 (88.09%), PUL-CPBF is best on Connect-4 (88.12%), and P3Mix-E tops IMDb (78.27%). The fully supervised PN oracle ranges from 79.89% (IMDb) to 98.94% (F-MNIST), with the best PU methods closing within 1–3% on most datasets. Computationally, nnPU and PUSB complete epochs in seconds with <1 GB peak memory, while VAE-PU and PUL-CPBF require 7–8 GB and substantially longer training, yet often yield lower or less stable F1 scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark evaluates 18 PU methods across three families. Among Risk-Minimization Estimators, LBE-PU (TPAMI 2021) achieves the highest accuracy on simple images (97.23% MNIST, 98.42% F-MNIST, even surpassing the supervised oracle on MNIST) but degrades on complex medical imaging (ADNI: 65.75%); Dist-PU (CVPR 2022) and PULDA (TPAMI 2023) lead on CIFAR-10 (88.09%, 87.02%) and ADNI (75.02%, 67.91%) respectively. Among Disambiguation-Guided methods, P3Mix-E/C (ICLR 2022) deliver the most stable cross-modality performance, with P3Mix-E topping IMDb (78.27%) and PUL-CPBF (ICML 2024) winning Connect-4 (88.12%); Holistic-PU (NeurIPS 2023) consistently trails, scoring below 65% on most datasets. Generative Distribution Matching methods (VAE-PU, PAN, CGenPU) uniformly underperform, with VAE-PU reaching only 49.24% on CIFAR-10 and PAN collapsing to 38.71% on Spambase. Notably, the simple nnPU baseline (NeurIPS 2017) remains within 1–2% of the best method on most datasets while being the most computationally efficient, and under SAR selection bias at low label ratios (c=0.05), bias-aware methods PUSB and LBE-PU show superior resilience, whereas at high label ratios (c=0.5) robust SCAR learners like VPU can outperform dedicated SAR-aware methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not build on a single prior method but rather carves new ground by providing the first unified, open-source benchmark for the PU learning subfield. Prior PU learning papers (nnPU, PUSB, VPU, LBE-PU, Dist-PU, P3Mix, etc.) each adopted different sampling schemes (case-control vs. single-training-set), label ratios, and labeling assumptions (SCAR vs. SAR), making cross-method comparisons unreliable and results irreproducible. PU-Bench repurposes 18 existing methods and 8 standard datasets but standardizes the entire experimental pipeline—data generation, training protocol, and evaluation metrics—so that all methods are assessed under identical, configurable conditions. Conceptually, it positions itself as an infrastructure contribution analogous to ImageNet or GLUE for their respective fields, rather than proposing a new algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that PU learning performance is highly context-dependent: no single method dominates across all modalities, label frequencies, and selection-bias conditions, and simpler 2017-era baselines (nnPU, VPU) remain competitive against newer, more complex architectures. This challenges the implicit assumption in the literature that algorithmic complexity translates to performance gains and provides practitioners with data-driven guidelines for method selection (e.g., use bias-aware methods like PUSB/LBE-PU under sparse, non-random labeling; use lightweight SCAR methods like VPU/P3Mix when labels are plentiful). A key open question the paper highlights is the gap between current algorithmic capabilities and real-world needs under severe data constraints (extreme label sparsity combined with selection bias), where most methods degrade substantially. The benchmark also exposes that generative distribution matching approaches (VAE-PU, PAN, CGenPU) consistently underperform despite their architectural complexity, suggesting this line of research may need fundamental rethinking.
