## Problem

Existing multimodal medical AI pipelines treat radiology and pathology foundation models as frozen, independent feature extractors whose static latent representations are combined via simple fusion (concatenation, cross-attention), limiting cross-domain synergy and interpretability. Full fine-tuning of large VLMs is computationally prohibitive and often confined to the pretraining domain. The paper asks whether clinically grounded concepts can serve as a dynamic, interpretable semantic bridge that enables true cross-modal co-adaptation before fusion, rather than post-hoc combination of fixed features.

## Input / Output

The method takes a radiology image (CT or MRI, resized to 224×224) and a pathology whole-slide image (20× magnification, tiled into 256×256 patches with contour filtering) as paired inputs per patient. Both are encoded by frozen vision encoders (BiomedCLIP and CONCH respectively) into fixed-dimensional feature vectors. The output is a task-specific prediction (Cox risk score for survival or class logits for grading) plus a 512-dimensional interpretable vector of per-concept cosine alignment scores (256 radiology + 256 pathology concepts) that directly constitutes the fused patient representation fed to the prediction head.

## Method

CTF introduces the Global-Context-Shared Prompt (GCSP) mechanism, a prefix-tuning strategy that prepends a case-specific, cross-domain-conditioned prompt to each concept's text tokens before a frozen text encoder, dynamically reshaping concept semantics per patient. The prompt concatenates three components: a Global Prompt (per-concept, task-adaptive, shared across patients), a Context Prompt (generated via a Mixture-of-Experts gating network conditioned on the complementary modality's vision features, enabling one domain to modulate the other's concept interpretation), and a Shared Prompt (an MLP over concatenated dual-modality features providing holistic patient-level adjustment). Concept selection uses a submodular objective balancing mutual-information-based prognostic relevance and cosine-similarity-based diversity, solved greedily. Only the prompt generators and a small MLP prediction head are trainable (0.5M parameters, 0.15% of the 307M-parameter backbone), while all vision and text encoders remain frozen. The tuned concept embeddings are aligned with vision features via cosine similarity to produce interpretable score vectors that are concatenated and passed through the prediction head, trained end-to-end with Cox partial-likelihood or cross-entropy loss.

## Results

On survival prediction, CTF achieves C-index of 0.713 on TCGA-LGG, 0.579 on TCGA-GBM, and 0.665 on Center1-GC, outperforming the strongest adaptive baseline M4Survive by up to 3.8% (TCGA-LGG) and the strongest latent-fusion baseline PIBD by up to 3.8%. On cancer grading, CTF reaches AUC 0.903 on TCGA-GBMLGG (3-way), 0.854 on Center2-CHS (5-way), and 0.660 on Center1-GC (5-way), surpassing the best fusion baseline by 2.9–3.6% AUC. The method requires only 0.15% additional trainable parameters (0.5M out of 307M) while keeping all foundation model encoders frozen.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CTF outperforms all 11 baselines across both survival and grading tasks on every dataset. Against the strongest adaptive fusion baseline M4Survive, CTF gains +0.004 C-index on TCGA-LGG (0.713 vs 0.709), +0.034 on TCGA-GBM (0.579 vs 0.545), and +0.023 on Center1-GC (0.665 vs 0.642). Against the strongest latent-fusion baselines, CTF beats PIBD by +0.026 C-index on TCGA-LGG and +0.027 on Center1-GC, and beats MOTCAT by +0.016 on TCGA-GBM. For cancer grading, CTF surpasses Cross-Attention (the best latent-fusion method) by +0.035 AUC on TCGA-GBMLGG (0.903 vs 0.868) and M4Survive by +0.024 AUC on Center2-CHS (0.854 vs 0.830). Unimodal pathology baselines (CLAM, TransMIL, ACMIL) consistently trail multimodal methods by 3–10% C-index, confirming the value of radiology-pathology integration, while simple Concat-Fusion underperforms more sophisticated fusion strategies, validating the need for structured cross-domain interaction.

## Relation to Prior Work

CTF repurposes prefix/prompt tuning (Lester et al., Li & Liang) from a unimodal task-adaptation tool into a cross-domain conditioning mechanism, where one modality's visual features dynamically reshape the semantic interpretation of concepts in the other modality before any fusion occurs. It draws on the Mixture-of-Experts gating paradigm (Shazeer et al.) for the Context Prompt component and on Concept Bottleneck Models (Koh et al.) for interpretability, but explicitly departs from the rigid CBM structure by making concepts a tunable, case-specific medium rather than a fixed bottleneck. The paper positions itself against the dominant latent-fusion paradigm (MOTCAT, PIBD, M4Survive), arguing that fusing already-fixed features limits synergy, and instead performs semantic co-adaptation of concept representations prior to fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 0.15% trainable parameter budget while outperforming full-adapter baselines (M4Survive) suggests that cross-domain semantic alignment, rather than architectural adaptation, is the key lever for multimodal medical fusion—a potentially generalizable insight. However, the framework requires fully paired radiology-pathology data, a fixed LLM-generated concept pool, and shows only modest absolute performance on the challenging 5-way gastric cancer grading task (AUC 0.660), limiting near-term clinical deployment. Open questions include graceful handling of missing modalities, dynamic concept pool expansion beyond the initial LLM-generated set, and whether the cross-domain conditioning mechanism generalizes beyond the radiology-pathology pairing to other heterogeneous clinical domains.
