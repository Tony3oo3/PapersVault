## Problem

Forward-Backward (FB) representations, the backbone of Behavioral Foundation Models, cannot generalize across different environment dynamics (e.g., changed obstacles, wind direction, friction) because training on mixed data forces the model to average successor measures over all configurations, causing destructive interference in the latent policy space. This renders BFMs ineffective in real-world settings such as robotics, where transition functions can shift unexpectedly at test time and per-embodiment training is infeasible.

## Input / Output

At test time the method receives a short reward-free trajectory (context length ~100 steps) collected by a random exploratory policy in the unseen environment, plus a task reward function (or a small set of reward-labeled states). The trajectory is fed to a permutation-invariant transformer that outputs a fixed-dimensional dynamics context vector h; this vector is concatenated with the task vector and plugged into the conditioned forward network to extract a greedy zero-shot policy. Notably, the backward network B remains shared across all contexts, and the context trajectory requires no episodic labels or reward annotations.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary extensions to FB. Belief-FB (BFB) pre-trains a permutation-invariant transformer encoder f_dyn in a self-supervised regime (Gaussian prior on the context vector plus a next-state prediction head) to infer a dynamics-encoding vector h from a trajectory; h is then concatenated with the task vector z_FB to condition only the forward network F, while B stays shared. Rotation-FB (RFB) further replaces the uniform hypersphere sampling of z_FB with a von Mises-Fisher distribution centered at h, implemented via Householder reflection, which partitions the policy-encoding space into non-overlapping dynamics-specific cones. Theoretically, this partitioning decouples the worst-case approximation error from the total number of environments k to depend only on k_max (the largest cone size), tightening the regret bound. Empirically, BFB and RFB achieve up to 2× higher zero-shot returns than baselines on both seen and unseen dynamics across discrete (Randomized Four-Rooms, Randomized Pointmass) and continuous (AntWind) environments.

## Results

On unseen (test) dynamics, Rotation-FB (RFB) achieves 0.61 return on Randomized Four-Rooms, 0.55 on Randomized Pointmass, and 640 on AntWind, outperforming all baselines by up to 2×. Belief-FB (BFB) also substantially exceeds baselines (0.40, 0.45, 550 respectively) while maintaining strong in-distribution performance (0.70, 0.76, 680). Notably, Oracle-ID achieves near-perfect in-distribution returns (0.90–0.95) but collapses to near-zero on unseen dynamics (0.0–0.10), whereas RFB generalizes robustly to both seen and unseen configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RFB outperforms all four baselines on every environment for both seen and unseen dynamics. Against Vanilla-FB, RFB achieves roughly 2× higher test returns (e.g., 0.61 vs. 0.15 on Four-Rooms, 640 vs. 250 on AntWind), confirming that the interference identified in Section 3.1 is the primary bottleneck. HILP, the strongest baseline, is surpassed by RFB on test dynamics across all environments (0.61 vs. 0.20 on Four-Rooms, 640 vs. 410 on AntWind), though HILP remains competitive in-distribution. Contextual-FB (Jeen & Cullen, 2024) underperforms RFB on test dynamics (0.18 vs. 0.61 on Four-Rooms, 350 vs. 640 on AntWind), attributed to its reliance on per-context classifiers that do not generalize to unseen configurations. Oracle-ID achieves the highest in-distribution returns but catastrophically fails out-of-distribution (e.g., 780→50 on AntWind, 0.95→0.0 on OGBench), whereas RFB maintains strong generalization (740→640, 0.7→0.55), demonstrating that explicit belief estimation enables true dynamics generalization rather than memorization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Forward-Backward (FB) successor-measure framework (Touati & Ollivier, 2021) and the broader Behavioral Foundation Model paradigm by identifying a fundamental interference limitation when FB is trained across multiple dynamics. It borrows the belief-state estimation technique from Meta-RL (Zintgraf et al., 2020; Dorfman et al., 2021) and repurposes it in a zero-shot, self-supervised setting via a permutation-invariant transformer encoder. The approach departs from Contextual-FB (Jeen & Cullen, 2024), which uses importance-weighting classifiers that must be retrained per novel layout, by instead learning a generalizable dynamics representation that transfers to unseen contexts. The Rotation-FB extension introduces a von Mises-Fisher sampling prior around context directions, a structured inductive bias not present in prior FB or successor-feature work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a critical practical bottleneck for BFMs in robotics and real-world deployment: the inability to handle unexpected dynamics changes (e.g., new obstacles, wind, friction) without retraining. The theoretical result showing that latent-space partitioning tightens the regret bound from depending on total environments k to the largest cluster k_max provides a principled justification for the design. However, limitations remain: evaluations are on relatively simple environments (MiniGrid, Ant, Pointmass), the method inherits FB's restriction to linear reward representations, introduces a sensitive hyperparameter κ, and relies on random exploration at test time which may fail in complex settings. Scaling to large-scale benchmarks like Open X-Embodiment or Kinetix, and combining with smarter test-time exploration, are important open directions.
