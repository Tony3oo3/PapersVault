## Problem

In Bayesian RL, epistemic uncertainty provides a principled basis for exploration, but it remains unclear how to most effectively leverage it to guide learning. Optimism-based methods add uncertainty as a reward bonus, which can be misleading when reward estimates are unreliable, leading to unnecessary exploration and slower convergence. Moreover, no prior online algorithm achieves nearly minimax-optimal sample complexity in infinite-horizon discounted MDPs without assuming a generative model.

## Input / Output

The method takes a prior over MDPs (Dirichlet for transition probabilities, Normal-Gamma for rewards), the current environment state, and a discount factor γ ∈ [0,1). It produces an action via a policy derived from value iteration on the mean MDP, an updated posterior belief (in closed form via conjugacy), and an epistemically guided reward that is a convex combination of the posterior predictive mean reward and the epistemic uncertainty, weighted by a probability of uncertainty P_U(s,a) = E_b(s,a)/E_max. The algorithm generalizes to both infinite-horizon discounted MDPs (policy updated every step, no reset) and finite-horizon episodic MDPs (policy updated and episode reset every H steps).

## Method

EUBRL derives its reward from a probabilistic inference formulation of RL by introducing a binary 'uncertainty' variable U_t and marginalizing over it, yielding the epistemically guided reward r_b^EUBRL(s,a) = (1−P_U(s,a))·r_b(s,a) + P_U(s,a)·E_b(s,a). This mechanism naturally disentangles exploration and exploitation: when the agent is uncertain, it treats epistemic uncertainty as an intrinsic reward encouraging exploration; when confident, it commits to exploiting learned reward estimates. The algorithm alternates between closed-form posterior updates (via conjugate priors) and solving the mean MDP with the epistemically guided reward using value iteration. Theoretically, the paper proves nearly minimax-optimal regret and sample complexity by decomposing per-step regret into quasi-optimism, complexity, and accuracy terms, where an 'epistemic resistance' quantity adaptively reduces per-step regret in proportion to the unfamiliarity of actions chosen by both the current and optimal policies.

## Results

On the Chain environment (5 states, 2 actions, stochastic), EUBRL achieves an average return of 3473 ± 16 over 500 seeds, outperforming all baselines including VBRB (3465 ± 20–50), MBIE-EB (3462), and BEB (3430). On the deterministic Loop environment (2 loops), EUBRL reaches 395 ± 0.04, marginally surpassing RMAX (394 ± 0) and BEB (386 ± 0). On the harder DeepSea and LazyChain benchmarks, EUBRL demonstrates superior sample efficiency and scalability with problem size, and its exploratory variant (EUBRL+) perfectly solves the stochastic DeepSea without failure—a result not observed in prior work.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Chain, EUBRL (3473) narrowly beats the closest Bayesian baseline VBRB (3465) and the frequentist MBIE-EB (3462), while substantially outperforming PSRL (3158), RMAX (3090), and Mean-MDP (3078); BEETLE, which solves the full BAMDP, performs worst (1754) due to its limited exploration. On Loop, EUBRL (395) edges out RMAX (394) and BEB (386), while Mean-MDP (233) is far behind, confirming the necessity of an exploration bonus. On DeepSea, EUBRL is more sample-efficient and scalable than PSRL (which suffers from excessive sampling) and BEB (which does not leverage posterior information in its bonus), and EUBRL+ achieves 100% success on the stochastic variant. On LazyChain, EUBRL consistently outperforms all baselines in both sample efficiency and success rate, even under heavy stochastic noise, with the tied-prior and mutual-information variants further improving convergence speed and success rate respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

EUBRL extends the Bayesian RL lineage (BAMDP, mean-MDP approximation) by replacing the standard additive optimism bonus with an epistemically guided reward derived from probabilistic inference, which disentangles exploration from exploitation rather than conflating them. Theoretically, it builds on quasi-optimism (Lee & Oh, 2025), which achieved minimax-optimality only in finite-horizon episodic MDPs, and extends this framework to the more challenging infinite-horizon discounted setting where trajectory coupling and value-function stationarity complicate the analysis. The probabilistic inference formulation draws on the control-as-inference tradition (Todorov, Toussaint, Levine), but the specific use of a binary uncertainty variable to modulate the reward is novel. Unlike prior optimism-based methods (RMAX, MBIE-EB, BEB, VBRB) that add a hand-crafted bonus to the reward, EUBRL's reward formulation is derived from a principled lower bound on per-step likelihood, making it more resilient to unreliable reward estimates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers the first nearly minimax-optimal sample complexity bound for infinite-horizon discounted MDPs without assuming a generative model, and the first nearly minimax-optimality result in the Bayesian setting—both significant theoretical milestones. The epistemic guidance mechanism offers a clean, general recipe that works with any Bayesian model and any choice of epistemic uncertainty measure (variance, mutual information), potentially unifying disparate exploration strategies. However, the empirical evaluation is confined to small, structured tabular environments (Chain, Loop, DeepSea, LazyChain), and the authors themselves identify open challenges: scalable epistemic uncertainty estimation with function approximation, efficient Bayesian planning at scale, and the sensitivity to prior misspecification (Theorem 5). For practitioners in deep RL, the immediate applicability is limited, but the conceptual framework and theoretical guarantees provide a foundation for future work on principled exploration in complex environments.
