## Problem

Online bidding must simultaneously satisfy a hard budget cap and a long-run ROI (profitability) threshold, yet prior work either handles only budget constraints, requires Slater's condition for ROI feasibility, or measures safety by counting violating rounds rather than the cumulative amount of violation. No unified, modular framework exists that delivers adaptive regret and anytime ROI-violation guarantees under both constraints without Slater's condition, across diverse auction formats and feedback regimes.

## Input / Output

At each round the algorithm receives a context v_t (e.g., private value, pCTR/pCVR features, or a subcampaign vector), constructs an optimistic reward estimator r̂_t and a pessimistic cost estimator ĉ_t from a general online regression oracle, and is given a per-round budget ρ = B/T and ROI threshold γ. It outputs a single bid b_t ∈ ℝ⁺ per round, with a hard-stopping rule that halts bidding once cumulative realized cost reaches B. Constraints are enforced in a mean-field sense: the penalty terms involve E_v[·] over the context marginal rather than per-context realizations, which smooths the safety signal and focuses on systematic risk.

## Method

L2FOB maintains two virtual queues, Q_ρ (budget) and Q_γ (ROI), updated with the clip operator (·)⁺ so that only estimated violations accumulate. The key architectural choice is the look-ahead mechanism: before selecting a bid, the algorithm computes candidate-specific queue values Q_ρ(t,b) and Q_γ(t,b) for every b, then passes them through a convex potential Φ(x)=x² and uses Φ′ as time-varying dual multipliers in a greedy surrogate objective that trades off optimistic reward against predicted one-step constraint violations. This internalizes feasibility at decision time rather than merely tracking past violations, and the rectified (clipped) penalty design means the algorithm focuses purely on reward maximization when constraints appear satisfied. The framework is modular: any online regression oracle satisfying a uniform high-probability error bound can be plugged in, yielding adaptive O(𝓔_r + (ν*/ρ)𝓔_c) regret and O(𝓔_r + 𝓔_c) anytime ROI violation without Slater's condition.

## Results

L2FOB achieves Õ((1+ν*/ρ)√T) regret and Õ(√T) anytime ROI violation in the first-price auction setting, improving over Wang et al. (2023) by a factor of 1/ρ in the budget dependence (their bound is Õ((1+ν*/ρ²)√T)). In the constrained contextual bandit setting on MSLR-WEB30k, L2FOB matches the best-known Õ((1+ν*/ρ)√T) regret while additionally providing an Õ(√T) anytime ROI violation guarantee that prior work lacks. Empirically, L2FOB outperforms both baselines in average reward and maintains stable ROI above the target threshold (γ=1.8 in first-price, γ=1.3 in bandit) throughout the horizon.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Wang et al. (2023) in first-price auctions, L2FOB improves the regret bound from Õ((1+ν*/ρ²)√T) to Õ((1+ν*/ρ)√T), extending the meaningful budget regime from B=Ω(T) down to B=Ω(√T), and adds explicit ROI constraint handling with an anytime violation guarantee that the baseline entirely lacks. Against Castiglioni et al. (2025) in the GP-based uncertainty setting, L2FOB provides the first method to simultaneously achieve optimal-order regret Õ((1+ν*/ρ)√(T·Σγ_j,T)) and a sublinear cumulative ROI violation bound Õ(√(T·Σγ_j,T)); Castiglioni et al. only count the number of violating rounds (which implies Θ(T) violations for sublinear regret) and do not model budget-induced hard-stopping. Against Guo et al. (2025) in constrained contextual bandits, L2FOB matches their Õ((1+ν*/ρ)√T) regret guarantee while additionally delivering an Õ(√T) anytime ROI violation bound and handling multiple simultaneous constraints (budget + ROI), whereas Guo et al. address only a single constraint. Empirically on MSLR-WEB30k, L2FOB consistently outperforms the adapted Guo et al. (2025) method in both average reward and ROI stability, and in the first-price auction experiment it achieves higher reward while maintaining ROI above the γ=1.8 threshold, whereas Wang et al. (2023) fails to reach the target profitability.

## Relation to Prior Work

L2FOB builds on the primal-dual and Lyapunov-optimization lineage for constrained online learning (Yu & Neely 2020; Slivkins et al. 2023; Guo & Liu 2024), but departs in two key ways: (1) it introduces look-ahead virtual queues that predict one-step constraint violations before the bid is chosen, rather than reacting only after cost feedback is observed, and (2) it applies a rectification operator (·)+ so that penalty terms activate only when estimated constraints are actually violated. This design, combined with a convex potential function shaping the dual multipliers, eliminates the need for Slater's condition on ROI constraints—a requirement in Slivkins et al. (2023) and Castiglioni et al. (2022)—and unifies previously separate treatments of first-price auctions, second-price auctions, and contextual bandits under a single modular framework parameterized by general regression oracles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers the first adaptive guarantees that simultaneously bound regret and cumulative ROI violation for online bidding without Slater's condition, and it improves the budget-regime dependence over Wang et al. (2023) from ρ² to ρ, making guarantees informative in the small-budget regime common in practice. The modular oracle-based design means any future improvement in reward/cost estimation (e.g., tighter GP bounds, neural regression) automatically translates into tighter bidding guarantees without redesigning the algorithm. Open questions include whether adaptive learning oracles with error rates matching non-contextual lower bounds can be constructed for contextual settings (Remark 4), and whether the mean-field assumption (requiring knowledge of the context distribution) can be relaxed in theory while preserving the guarantees.
