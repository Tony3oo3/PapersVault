## Problem

Standard decoding strategies that use learned process verifiers (value functions) to guide language model generation suffer from error amplification: even small, seemingly benign errors in the approximate value function compound over long horizons, causing catastrophic degradation in output quality. The paper asks whether more sophisticated decoding strategies can provably mitigate this amplification, and connects the problem to classical approximate sampling theory.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a text prompt x, a base language model π_ref (queried for conditional next-token distributions and densities), and an approximate value function V̂ (an MLP trained via regression on Monte Carlo rollouts) that maps partial sequences to non-negative scores. It produces a full-length response sequence y_{1:H} by running a random walk for T steps on the tree of partial generations, returning either the final node or a uniformly random node from the walk; the output is conditioned on reaching a leaf (full-length) node, which occurs with probability Ω(1/H).

## Method

VGB (Value-Guided Backtracking) casts autoregressive generation as a lazy random walk on the tree of partial sequences, where at each step the walk moves to a child node with probability proportional to π_ref(y_{h+1}|x,y_{1:h})·V̂(x,y_{1:h+1}) or backtracks to the parent with probability proportional to V̂(x,y_{1:h}), and stays put with probability 1/2. This is a generalization of the Sinclair-Jerrum random walk from approximate counting in theoretical computer science, with value functions playing the role of counting oracles. Under a uniform multiplicative error bound on V̂, the walk rapidly mixes (in Õ(H²(1+ε_V)⁴ log(1/δ)) steps) to a stationary distribution proportional to the target tilted distribution π* at the leaves, avoiding the error amplification that plagues action-level rejection sampling. Under a weaker average-case error assumption, the analysis leverages local stationarity of slowly mixing Markov chains to establish coverage guarantees. The value function itself is trained via supervised regression onto Monte Carlo rollouts from the base model.

## Results

On the Dyck grammar task, VGB achieves a robustly non-dominated point on the accuracy–diversity Pareto frontier, outperforming both Block Best-of-N and Block Rejection Sampling across all block sizes (1–16) and candidate counts (2–32) while equalizing the number of base-model queries. On the synthetic ABC task, VGB incurs strictly lower KL-divergence to the target distribution π* than ActionLevelRS, with the gap widening as horizon H increases. On the letter avoidance task, VGB achieves a higher pairwise coherence winrate (judged by GPT-4o-mini) and higher average horizon-normalized log-probabilities (evaluated by Qwen-2.5-1.5B) than ActionLevelRS across a broad range of backtracking weights α. On the parity task, VGB requires less wall-clock time than ActionLevelRS to find a valid reward-1 response, with superior scaling as H grows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VGB is compared against two main families of baselines. Against Block Best-of-N (Mudgal et al., 2024) and Block Rejection Sampling on the Dyck grammar task, VGB achieves a non-dominated Pareto frontier point in accuracy vs. diversity, meaning no baseline simultaneously matches or exceeds VGB on both metrics at equal query budget. Against ActionLevelRS (equivalent to locally constrained decoding / PICARD, Scholak et al., 2021) on the letter avoidance task, VGB wins on both coherence winrate and log-probability across a wide range of α values, while ActionLevelRS is insensitive to α (it reduces to locally constrained decoding regardless). On the synthetic ABC and parity tasks, VGB consistently outperforms ActionLevelRS in KL-divergence to π* and wall-clock efficiency respectively, with the advantage growing with sequence length H. The paper also notes that beam search is provably suboptimal even with a perfect value function (Appendix C), further motivating the sampling-based VGB approach over search-based alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

VGB is positioned as a one-line modification to action-level rejection sampling (Yang & Klein, 2021), but its theoretical backbone is the Sinclair-Jerrum random walk from approximate counting in theoretical computer science, which the paper explicitly generalizes to the language-model setting. Empirically, backtracking heuristics have appeared in recent LLM reasoning work (Botta et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025), but those methods use ad-hoc hyperparameters and lack formal guarantees; VGB provides the first principled, provably-correct backtracking mechanism for value-guided generation. The analysis also draws on modern Markov chain tools—particularly local stationarity (Liu et al., 2024b)—to handle average-case value errors, bridging the gap between statistical learning assumptions and the uniform-error conditions traditionally required in approximate sampling theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper gives the field a formal explanation for the 'curse of horizon' in process-verifier-guided generation: even O(1) multiplicative errors in a learned value function cause exponential degradation under standard decoding, and a simple stochastic backtracking step provably eliminates this amplification. This reframes backtracking from an empirical heuristic to a theoretically grounded component of the decoding toolkit, and opens a concrete pipeline for transferring ideas from approximate counting/sampling (conductance, local mixing) to LLM reasoning. Key limitations include the Õ(H³) runtime for guaranteed leaf output, the need for exact outcome-level rewards in the strongest guarantee, and the fact that empirical validation is on relatively small-scale tasks (Dyck grammar, synthetic parity, short Python test cases). Open questions include whether VGB's guarantees hold under the distribution shifts common in real LLM fine-tuning, whether the H³ cost can be reduced (e.g., via momentum or parallelization), and whether the approach composes with search-based methods like MCTS for reward maximization.
