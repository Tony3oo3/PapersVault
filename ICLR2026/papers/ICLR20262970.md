## Problem

Pre-trained vision-language models (CLIP, SigLIP) deterministically map images and text to a shared embedding space, failing to capture epistemic uncertainties that arise under domain shift. Existing uncertainty quantification approaches for VLMs require retraining, architectural modifications, or substantial inference overhead, making them impractical for safety-critical or active-learning deployments. The paper asks whether reliable, well-calibrated uncertainty estimates can be obtained post-hoc from a frozen VLM with negligible computational cost.

## Input / Output

At setup, the method requires a pre-trained VLM and a small subset of image-text pairs (e.g., 10 mini-batches from LAION-400M or a proxy dataset like CC12M) to estimate Kronecker-factored Hessian factors for the projection layers. At inference, it takes a single image and a set of text prompts, and instead of producing a single cosine similarity score per pair, it outputs a Gaussian distribution (mean and variance) over each cosine similarity, which is then mapped to a class probability vector via a probit approximation. The encoders remain deterministic; only the final linear projection matrices P and Q carry Bayesian posteriors.

## Method

BayesVLM reformulates the InfoNCE contrastive loss into two independent per-modality categorical likelihoods under an i.i.d. assumption, enabling separate Laplace posterior inference for the image projection P and text projection Q without entangling modalities. The posterior covariance is approximated via a Kronecker-factored Generalised Gauss-Newton (KFAC-GGN) Hessian, which decomposes into two smaller matrices (A and B) that are estimated from a data subset with a pseudo-data count τ to compensate for reduced sample size. The key analytical contribution is ProbCosine: a closed-form Gaussian approximation of the cosine similarity distribution derived from the first two moments of the probabilistic image and text embeddings, avoiding costly Monte Carlo sampling. Uncertainty is propagated to class conditionals through a probit link, yielding calibrated predictive distributions. The entire pipeline is post-hoc and training-free—no architectural changes, no additional parameters, and under 5% runtime overhead relative to the deterministic VLM.

## Results

On zero-shot classification with OpenCLIP ViT-B-32, BayesVLM matches CLIP's accuracy (e.g., 93.62% vs 93.61% on CIFAR-10, 68.87% vs 68.99% on FLOWERS-102) while substantially reducing Expected Calibration Error (ECE) across all seven benchmarks — e.g., 4.22 vs 6.59 on FLOWERS-102, 0.72 vs 1.45 on CIFAR-10, and 1.78 vs 5.20 on ImageNet-R. BayesVLM also achieves lower Negative Log Predictive Density (NLPD) than vanilla CLIP on every dataset, indicating less overconfident wrong predictions. In active learning on OfficeHome and ImageNet variants, EPIG and BALD acquisition functions using BayesVLM uncertainties outperform random and entropy-based selection in both weighted accuracy and NLPD across subset sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla CLIP, BayesVLM matches accuracy within ±0.2% on all seven benchmarks while reducing ECE by 35–70% (e.g., 4.22 vs 6.59 on FLOWERS-102, 0.72 vs 1.45 on CIFAR-10) and NLPD by 5–15%, showing it is less overconfident on incorrect predictions. Compared to temperature scaling, BayesVLM achieves similar ACC and NLPD but dominates on ECE across every dataset (e.g., 1.69 vs 4.74 on FOOD-101, 1.78 vs 4.80 on ImageNet-R), indicating that a single scalar temperature cannot capture the input-dependent uncertainty structure that the Laplace posterior provides. TTA (Farina et al., 2024) achieves higher ACC on some benchmarks (81.68% vs 80.43% on FOOD-101, 78.29% vs 74.45% on ImageNet-R) but is substantially worse calibrated (ECE 9.63 vs 4.22 on FLOWERS-102, 11.75 vs 3.57 on UCF101) and incurs an 80× inference cost increase. When applied to the pre-trained probabilistic model PCME++ (Chun, 2024), the ProbCosine approximation improves ECE from 8.81 to 2.79 on FLOWERS-102 and from 26.03 to 19.41 on SUN397 while preserving accuracy, demonstrating that the cosine-similarity distribution derivation is model-agnostic. In active learning, EPIG and BALD with BayesVLM uncertainties consistently outperform both random and entropy-based selection (with and without targeted k-NN) on weighted accuracy and NLPD across OfficeHome and ImageNet-Sketch domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the Laplace approximation (MacKay, 1992) with Kronecker-factored GGN Hessian estimation (Ritter et al., 2018; Schraudolph, 2002) to the projection layers of pre-trained VLMs, a setting not previously explored. It departs from prior VLM uncertainty work—which has focused on calibration (temperature scaling), test-time adaptation, adapter-based fine-tuning, or training probabilistic VLMs from scratch—by requiring no retraining, no architectural changes, and no additional parameters. A key conceptual departure is the i.i.d. assumption that decouples image and text modalities into independent probabilistic models, enabling tractable per-modality posterior inference, and the derivation of an analytical Gaussian approximation (ProbCosine) for the distribution over cosine similarities, avoiding costly Monte Carlo sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BayesVLM provides a practical, near-zero-overhead (<5% runtime) route to well-calibrated epistemic uncertainties for any pre-trained VLM, which is critical for safety-critical deployment and uncertainty-aware decision-making (e.g., active learning, OOD detection). The demonstration that Bayesian acquisition functions (BALD, EPIG) outperform entropy-based selection for VLM fine-tuning bridges a gap between Bayesian active learning and vision-language models. Key limitations include the need for access to (or a proxy for) training data to estimate the Hessian, the restriction to projection layers only, and the Gaussian embedding assumption; open questions include whether the approach extends to deeper layers, fully closed-source settings, and non-Gaussian embedding distributions.
