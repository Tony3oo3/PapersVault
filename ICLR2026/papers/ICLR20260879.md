## Problem

Orthogonal fine-tuning (OFT) preserves the geometric structure of pre-trained weights, but full-dimensional orthogonal matrices are prohibitively expensive in parameters, memory, and computation. Existing sparsity-driven OFT variants (BOFT, qGOFT) restore expressiveness by chaining sparse matrices, yet they suffer from severe OOM failures on large models and run up to 6× slower than LoRA. The paper asks how to design a PEFT method that simultaneously achieves semantic preservation, expressiveness, and multi-dimensional efficiency (parameter counts, memory, and computation).

## Input / Output

PSOFT operates on the pre-trained weight matrices of transformer linear layers (input dimension d, output dimension n) rather than on the model's data inputs directly. At inference, the model still consumes standard text sequences or RGB images and produces class labels or generated text answers. The key structural detail is that PSOFT pre-computes an SVD of each weight matrix once, freezes the top-r principal component (A', B') and the residual (W_res), and during training only updates an r×r orthogonal matrix R (parameterized by a skew-symmetric Q via Cayley transform) plus two r-dimensional scaling vectors α and β, yielding a total of r(r−1)/2 + 2r trainable parameters per layer.

## Method

PSOFT decomposes each pre-trained weight matrix W_pre via SVD into a rank-r principal component W_pri = A'B' and a residual W_res, then confines orthogonal fine-tuning to the r-dimensional principal subspace: the forward pass computes h = (A' diag(α) R diag(β) B' + W_res)ᵀ x. A theoretical condition (RᵀAᵀAR = AᵀA) guarantees that pairwise angles and column norms in the principal subspace are strictly preserved; normalizing A' so that A'ᵀA' = I reduces this to standard orthogonality of R. Orthogonality is enforced efficiently via the Cayley parameterization R = (I−Q)(I+Q)⁻¹ with Q skew-symmetric, approximated by a 5-term Neumann series to avoid explicit matrix inversion. Two tunable vectors α and β (initialized to all-ones) are inserted on either side of R to gradually relax strict orthogonality during training, adding only 2r parameters while enabling task-specific norm and angle adjustments. Because PSOFT trains only the r×r orthogonal matrix rather than two d×r and r×n matrices as in LoRA, it achieves a much higher effective rank (r_PSOFT = √M vs. r_LoRA = M/(d+n)) under the same parameter budget M, and its activation memory scales with r rather than the hidden dimension h.

## Results

On GLUE (DeBERTaV3-base), PSOFT with r=46 achieves 88.04% average accuracy using only 0.08M trainable parameters and 4.1 GB peak memory, outperforming LoRA (87.30%, 1.33M params, 4.5 GB) and DoRA (87.61%, 1.41M params, 5.8 GB) while using ~18× fewer parameters. On LLaMA-3.2-3B (GSM-8K/MATH), PSOFT (r=352, 12.2M params) scores 63.08/15.98, beating LoRA (60.80/15.76) by +2.28% on GSM-8K with comparable memory (36.2 vs 32.2 GB). On LLaMA-3.1-8B commonsense reasoning, PSOFT (r=424) achieves 82.54% average, surpassing DoRA (82.09%) and LoRA (81.11%) while using ~7 GB less memory than DoRA. GOFTv2, qGOFTv2, and BOFT all suffer OOM failures on models ≥3B, whereas PSOFT completes training in all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSOFT achieves the best average accuracy across all four main experimental settings while using the fewest parameters on small models (0.08M vs. 1.29–1.41M for LoRA, DoRA, BOFT, and OFTv2) and the lowest peak memory (4.1 GB on GLUE, 6.2 GB on VTAB-1K). Against LoRA (r=8), PSOFT wins on average accuracy by +0.74% (GLUE), +1.6% (VTAB-1K), +2.28% (GSM-8K), and +1.43% (commonsense avg) while using 94% fewer parameters on small models and comparable memory on large models. Against DoRA, PSOFT matches or exceeds accuracy on all benchmarks while reducing memory by ~1.7 GB (GLUE), ~11.6 GB (ViT), ~7.2 GB (LLaMA-3.2-3B), and ~7.2 GB (LLaMA-3.1-8B). The sparsity-based OFT variants (GOFTv2, qGOFTv2, BOFT) all fail with OOM on LLaMA-3.2-3B and LLaMA-3.1-8B, and GOFTv2/qGOFTv2 also OOM on GLUE and VTAB-1K at moderate sequence lengths, whereas PSOFT completes all experiments. On ViT-B/16 (VTAB-1K), PSOFT (r=46, 0.08M params, 6.2 GB) achieves 73.4% average top-1 accuracy, beating OFTv2 (72.1%), LoRA (71.8%), and DoRA (72.3%) with ~94% fewer parameters and the lowest memory footprint.

## Relation to Prior Work

PSOFT sits at the intersection of two PEFT lineages: low-rank adaptation (LoRA, PiSSA, DoRA, LoRA-XS) and orthogonal fine-tuning (OFT, BOFT, qGOFT). It departs from full-space OFT by restricting orthogonal transformations to the SVD-derived principal subspace of pre-trained weights, and from LoRA by replacing the additive low-rank update with a multiplicative orthogonal transform within that subspace. The Cayley parameterization for the orthogonal matrix and the tunable scaling vectors for gradual orthogonality relaxation are borrowed from prior OFT work (Qiu et al., 2023; Ma et al., 2024), but their application within a low-rank subspace is new. The key conceptual departure is the observation that under a fixed parameter budget M, PSOFT's effective rank scales as √M versus LoRA's M/(d+n), yielding substantially higher expressiveness at equal cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PSOFT offers a practically deployable PEFT method that simultaneously achieves semantic preservation (via guaranteed subspace geometry), low parameter counts, and minimal memory/computation overhead—properties that no single prior method satisfies. The theoretical condition (RᵀGR = G) providing a clean certificate for angle and norm preservation is a useful analytical tool for the broader OFT literature. However, the relaxation via tunable vectors α and β technically breaks the strict orthogonality guarantee, and the empirical evaluation is capped at 8B parameters, leaving open whether the principal-subspace assumption and the √M rank advantage hold at the 70B+ scale where activation memory from the backbone dominates. The method's practical impact will depend on whether the SVD initialization cost and the need for careful rank selection generalize across architectures.
