## Problem

Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) are pretrained on large-scale univariate or homogeneous multivariate series, making them unable to leverage heterogeneous covariates—categorical variables, satellite images, or text—that are prevalent in real-world forecasting. Existing adaptation methods (e.g., ChronosX, TTM-R2, linear-regression adapters) either restrict themselves to point-wise or homogeneous covariates or fail to preserve the generalization gained from pretraining, leaving a gap for a unified, plug-in approach that handles both homogeneous and heterogeneous covariates while keeping the TSFM backbone frozen.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a past target series Y_{1:T}, homogeneous real-valued covariates, heterogeneous covariates (categorical IDs, 64×64×4 satellite images, or free-form text), and optional static covariates, and produces a future forecast Ŷ_{T+1:T+H} as either point predictions or multi-quantile probabilistic forecasts depending on the underlying TSFM. Heterogeneous inputs are first passed through modality-specific encoders (a 4-layer CNN for satellite images, GIST for text) to obtain dense feature sequences H^{(het)}, which are then projected into a latent homogeneous series space of tunable dimension d^{het} (default 4) before entering the fusion pipeline. The TSFM's own tokenizer is reused to embed the homogenized covariates, ensuring dimensional compatibility without architectural redesign.

## Method

UniCA is a plug-in adapter with two core components. First, Covariate Homogenization maps all heterogeneous covariates into dense continuous series representations via a simple linear Covariate Homogenizer (CH), aligning them temporally with observed homogeneous covariates into a unified C_{1:T+H} ∈ R^{(T+H)×M}. Second, a dual attention-based fusion mechanism injects covariate information at two points: a Pre-Fusion module applies Conditional Attention Pooling (CAP, derived from TFT's variable selection) over past covariate tokens conditioned on static features, followed by a Gated Linear Unit to gate the covariate contribution into the tokenized target before the frozen TSFM encoder; a Post-Fusion module applies CAP over future-known covariate tokens and then a self-attention layer to jointly attend over the encoded past and future covariate representations before the predictor. The entire TSFM backbone (tokenizer, encoder, predictor) remains frozen, and only the CH, static embedding layer, CAP/GRN parameters, GLU, and post-fusion self-attention are trained, using the same loss function as the original TSFM pretraining (quantile loss, Huber, or NLL). This design preserves pretrained temporal generalization while enabling flexible integration of arbitrary covariate modalities.

## Results

On 12 unimodal covariate-aware forecasting datasets, Chronos-Bolt (UniCA) achieves the best average MAPE of 0.506 among all adapter-based methods, substantially outperforming specialized covariate-aware models (0.596–0.738 MAPE) and zero-shot TSFMs. On the multimodal MMSP (TS-Image) benchmark, UniCA yields a 6.5% error reduction for TimesFM and 5.9% for Chronos-Bolt over their zero-shot baselines. On Time-MMD (TS-Text), TimesFM (UniCA) ranks among the top performers, outperforming most specialized and pretrained baselines. The Covariate Homogenizer alone, when plugged into TFT and TiDE, achieves 5% and 30.1% MAE reductions on MMSP respectively, confirming its generalizability beyond TSFMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniCA (Chronos-Bolt) achieves 0.506 average MAPE, beating the strongest specialized covariate-aware baseline TimeXer (0.613) by ~17% and the best adapter baseline ChronosX (0.548) by ~8%. Against zero-shot TSFMs, UniCA improves over Chronos-Bolt (ZS) by ~8% and TimesFM (ZS) by ~7% in MAPE, while full fine-tuning (SFT) yields only marginal gains (~1–2%) or even degradation, confirming that UniCA preserves pretrained generalization better than SFT. On the multimodal MMSP benchmark, UniCA-enhanced TimesFM and Chronos-Bolt outperform FusionSF (the dedicated TS-Image method) and their own zero-shot versions by 6.5% and 5.9% respectively. On Time-MMD (TS-Text), TimesFM (UniCA) surpasses Time-LLM, ChatTime, and MM-TSF, while the Covariate Homogenizer plugged into TFT and TiDE reduces MAE by 5%/13% and 30.1%/10.5% on MMSP/Time-MMD respectively, demonstrating that homogenization is a broadly applicable technique beyond TSFM adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniCA builds directly on the Conditional Attention Pooling and Gated Residual Network mechanisms from TFT (Lim et al., 2019), repurposing them as lightweight fusion modules inserted around a frozen TSFM backbone. The plug-in adapter philosophy mirrors parameter-efficient fine-tuning in NLP (adapters, LoRA), but the key departure is the covariate homogenization step: a simple linear projection that converts heterogeneous inputs (categorical IDs, satellite images, text) into dense continuous series before any fusion occurs. This contrasts with prior TSFM adaptation work (ChronosX, TTM-R2, linear-regression adapters) that only handles homogeneous real-valued covariates, and with specialized multimodal forecasters (FusionSF, Time-MMD's MM-TSF) that are trained from scratch and lack pretrained temporal representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world forecasting almost always involves covariates, yet the most powerful pretrained TSFMs cannot natively consume categorical or multimodal inputs. By showing that a single linear layer suffices to 'homogenize' diverse modalities into series space, UniCA offers a low-cost, architecture-agnostic recipe that practitioners can bolt onto any existing TSFM without retraining. Open questions include whether the frozen-backbone assumption degrades when covariates carry strong domain-specific signal, how the approach scales to very large numbers of heterogeneous covariates, and whether the linear homogenizer remains adequate for richer modalities (e.g., video, audio) where temporal structure within the modality itself matters.
