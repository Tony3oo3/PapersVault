## Problem

Existing training-free sparse activation methods for LLM inference (e.g., TEAL, CATS, R-Sparse) select neurons to activate based solely on hidden state magnitudes, ignoring how the weight matrix modulates each activation's influence on downstream layers. This mismatch can discard highly influential activations while retaining low-impact ones, leading to compounding approximation errors and unnecessary performance degradation, especially at high sparsity levels.

## Input / Output

At each layer during inference, WINA consumes the input hidden state vector x ∈ R^n and the pre-computed column-wise ℓ₂-norm vector c of the weight matrix W ∈ R^{m×n} (computed once offline at model loading). It produces a binary activation mask g ∈ {0,1}^n that zeros out n−K input dimensions, yielding a sparsified output y_g = W(g ⊙ x). A prerequisite one-off SVD-based orthogonal tensor transformation (from SliceGPT) is applied to the model before inference to enforce column-orthogonality, taking less than 4 minutes on a single A100 for 7–8B models.

## Method

WINA replaces the standard top-K gating on |x_i| with a joint criterion on |x_i · c_i|, where c_i is the ℓ₂-norm of the i-th column of W, so that the selection directly accounts for how much each activation can influence the next layer's output. Under the column-orthogonality assumption (W^T W is diagonal), the paper proves WINA achieves the optimal approximation error for a single linear layer (Lemma 3.1) and minimizes a provable separable upper bound on the total output deviation for L-layer networks (Theorem 3.2), yielding tighter guarantees than prior magnitude-only methods. The approach is training-free, architecture-agnostic (applicable to attention, MLP, and residual layers), and compatible with INT8/INT4 quantization. A dedicated Triton sparse GEMV kernel is provided, adding only O(BTd) overhead for the element-wise product x ⊙ c relative to O(BTd²) for the matrix multiply, making the gating cost negligible (<0.1%) for typical hidden dimensions.

## Results

On Llama-2-7B commonsense reasoning (8-task average), WINA achieves 69.59% at 25% sparsity and 65.14% at 65% sparsity, outperforming TEAL (61.07%) and R-Sparse (59.37%) at the extreme sparsity level. On Llama-3-8B, WINA reaches 73.09% at 25% sparsity (exceeding the full model's 72.99%) and 65.63% at 65% sparsity. On Phi-4-14B, WINA surpasses TEAL by +4.88% on MMLU, +2.73% on GSM8K, and +9.14% on HumanEval at 65% sparsity, while reducing GFLOPs by up to 63.7% and achieving up to 57% speed-up on RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WINA consistently outperforms all three baselines across every model and sparsity level tested. Against TEAL (Liu et al., 2024a), WINA leads by +0.07% at 25% sparsity on Llama-2-7B, widening to +4.07% at 65% sparsity; on Llama-3-8B the gap grows from +0.27% to +3.61%, and on Mistral-7B from +0.04% to +1.48%. Against R-Sparse (Zhang et al., 2025), WINA's advantage is larger at extreme sparsity: +5.77% on Llama-2-7B, +9.06% on Llama-3-8B, and +9.48% on Mistral-7B at 65% sparsity. Against CATS (Lee et al., 2024), WINA outperforms at both 25% and 40% sparsity (e.g., +1.55% average on Llama-2-7B at 40%), while CATS cannot be evaluated beyond 40% due to its MLP-only applicability. On the additional Phi-4-14B benchmarks, WINA surpasses TEAL by +4.88% on MMLU and +9.14% on HumanEval at 65% sparsity, with the HumanEval score (41.46%) even exceeding the full model baseline (50.61% at 0% sparsity is 50.61, but WINA at 40% reaches 53.00). WINA also maintains its advantage under INT8 and INT4 quantization, outperforming TEAL by +3.86% and +3.68% respectively at 65% sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

WINA extends the training-free sparse activation paradigm established by TEAL, CATS, and R-Sparse, which all rely on top-K selection based solely on hidden-state magnitudes. The key conceptual departure is incorporating the column-wise ℓ₂-norm of the weight matrix into the gating criterion, so that activation selection accounts for how strongly each input dimension influences the next layer's output. The theoretical guarantees (Lemma 3.1, Theorem 3.2) depend on a column-orthogonality assumption enforced via SliceGPT's SVD-based orthogonal tensor transformation, which is applied as a one-off preprocessing step. This positions WINA as a principled refinement of existing magnitude-based gating rather than a fundamentally new sparsification paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WINA offers a simple, plug-and-play mechanism that consistently outperforms prior training-free methods across four LLM families and multiple task categories, with the advantage growing at higher sparsity levels. The provably tighter error bound provides theoretical justification beyond empirical observation, and the demonstrated compatibility with 4-bit/8-bit quantization addresses a practical deployment gap that prior sparse-activation works largely ignore. Open questions include whether joint sparse-activation-quantization can yield further gains, whether dedicated layer-wise sparsity allocation strategies can improve performance, and whether the column-orthogonality assumption (and its associated transformation) is necessary in practice or merely sufficient for the theoretical analysis.
