## Problem

Standard inclusion-based persistent homology in graph learning only traverses increasing subgraph filtrations, so cycles born in the forward pass can never die and the number of permanent features varies across graphs, making bottleneck distances ill-defined. This one-sided view discards topological information about how structures vanish. The paper asks whether a principled contraction-based (time-reversing) complement to forward PH can recover this missing information and improve expressivity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a finite undirected graph with a permutation-equivariant filtration function (e.g., degree-based or learned vertex/edge values) and produces persistence diagrams computed over a sequence that interleaves inclusions of intermediate complexes with contractions of those same complexes. These diagrams are encoded via DeepSets pooling and fed into GIN or GCN backbones to yield a classification label, regression value, or molecular property score. A key structural constraint is that any intermediate complex must be included before it can be contracted, which bounds the total space size and enables a runtime-versus-expressivity tradeoff on large graphs.

## Method

The paper introduces Contraction Homology (CH) as the PH of a sequence of subgraph contractions, then defines Backward PH (contracting intermediate complexes in reverse filtration order) and Forward-Backward (FB) persistence (concatenating the full inclusion phase with the contraction phase). It proves FB-persistence is strictly more expressive than forward and backward PH in isolation, and further introduces Hourglass persistence, which interleaves inclusions and contractions in arbitrary order subject only to the include-before-contract constraint. A general (f,g)-FB persistence framework unifies extended persistence and FB-persistence by pairing two independent filtration functions, with a stability theorem bounding the bottleneck distance in function time. Efficient O(|V|+|E|) algorithms (union-find with a fundamental cycle basis for the forward stage; supernode bookkeeping for the backward stage) make the full pipeline pluggable into end-to-end differentiable GNN training, where both the inclusion filtration and contraction schedule are jointly learned.

## Results

The full forward-backward (FB) method achieves the best or second-best result in 9 of 12 dataset–backbone settings. Headline numbers under the GIN backbone include 73.51% accuracy on PROTEINS (vs. 69.35% for standard PH), 81.27% on NCI1, and 0.40 MAE on ZINC regression. Under GCN, the method reaches 76.37% AUC on OGBG-MOLHIV and 0.44 MAE on ZINC, both best in their rows. In the extended-persistence comparison (Table 2), the (f,g)-FB framework outperforms PersLay by 9–13 accuracy points on NCI109, PROTEINS, and NCI1 under both backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard PH (Horn et al., 2021), the full FB method wins on 10 of 12 settings, with the largest gaps on PROTEINS-GIN (+4.2 pp) and ZINC-GCN (−0.05 MAE); it loses only on MOLHIV-GIN (72.34 vs. 74.34 AUC) and NCI109-GCN (75.87 vs. 76.59). Against RePHINE (Immonen et al., 2023), the FB method wins on 7 of 12 settings (notably PROTEINS-GIN +3.6 pp, NCI1-GIN +2.5 pp, ZINC on both backbones) but trails RePHINE on NCI109-GCN (75.87 vs. 79.50), IMDB-B-GCN (68.00 vs. 70.00), and MOLHIV-GIN (72.34 vs. 72.88), where RePHINE's 0D augmentation provides an edge. Against PersLay (Carrière et al., 2020) in the extended-persistence comparison (Table 2), the (f,g)-FB framework dominates decisively, outperforming PersLay by 9.9–12.7 accuracy points on NCI109, PROTEINS, and NCI1 under both GIN and GCN, and matching it on IMDB-B-GCN (70.00 vs. 70.00). The ablations (Fwd-only, Bwd-only) confirm that neither the inclusion nor contraction component alone matches the jointly learned full model on most datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the persistent homology (PH) framework for graph neural networks by introducing contractions as a first-class topological operation alongside inclusions. Prior PH-based GNN methods (RePHINE, PersLay, standard PH) rely exclusively on inclusion-based filtrations where cycles, once born, can never die; this paper shows that a backward contraction phase captures complementary information and that the two are incomparable (Proposition 1). The authors carefully distinguish their forward-backward (FB) and hourglass persistence from extended persistence (Proposition 5), which uses superlevel-set shrinking rather than intermediate-complex contractions, and from zigzag/bipath filtrations. The framework generalizes the (f,g)-FB construction to arbitrary pairs of filtration functions, subsuming extended persistence as the (f,−f) special case (Proposition 3).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a practical metrizability issue in PH-based GNNs: forward persistence produces infinitely-lived features that make bottleneck distances between different graphs ill-defined, and the contraction phase assigns finite death times to all features. The hourglass persistence offers a principled runtime–expressivity tradeoff for large graphs by interleaving inclusions and contractions, bounding the total space size. Open questions include establishing a functional (bottleneck) stability guarantee for hourglass persistence (only combinatorial stability is proven) and characterizing the optimal interleaving schedule. Empirically, the full model achieves best or second-best results in 9 of 12 settings, but gains over strong baselines like RePHINE are modest, suggesting the practical benefit of contractions may be dataset-dependent.
