## Problem

Existing 3D part segmentation methods either train on small closed-world taxonomies that cannot generalize to unseen categories, or lift 2D SAM masks into 3D, which restricts understanding to visible surfaces, lacks user-controllable interaction, and fails to capture interior structures. No prior model combines native 3D training, promptable interactive segmentation, and scalable feed-forward inference in a single framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264495/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 3D point cloud uniformly sampled to 100,000 points with per-point coordinates, surface normals, and optional RGB (d_in=9), plus a set of positive/negative prompt points. For a single prompt, the decoder produces three candidate binary masks (one per point) with an IoU confidence score for each, from which the best mask is selected; in multi-prompt rounds, previous mask logits are fed back as additional context. In 'Segment Every Part' auto mode, FPS-sampled prompts generate 3N_f candidate masks that are filtered by predicted IoU and NMS to yield a full multi-part decomposition with adjustable granularity via the NMS threshold.

## Method

PartSAM uses a dual-branch triplane encoder where a frozen branch (initialized from PartField) preserves 2D SAM priors learned via contrastive learning, while a learnable branch adapts to native 3D part semantics and accepts normals/RGB through a zero-convolution layer; both branches use PVCNN → triplane projection → transformer to produce a continuous feature field. A SAM-style prompt-guided mask decoder employs a four-layer two-way transformer with three output tokens and one IoU token for parallel decoding, upsampled via distance-based interpolation to per-point mask logits. Training combines focal loss, dice loss, IoU regression, and a triplet contrastive loss over 9 simulated interaction iterations. A model-in-the-loop data pipeline leverages PartField's clustering outputs as pseudo-labels, then uses PartSAM's own interactive segmentation to validate and filter them via IoU thresholds (IoU@1>60 or IoU@10>90), scaling supervision to over 5 million shape-part pairs from Objaverse assets.

## Results

On interactive part segmentation, PartSAM achieves IoU@1 of 56.1 on PartObjaverse-Tiny and 59.5 on PartNet-E, surpassing Point-SAM (29.4 and 35.9) by over 90% relative improvement with a single prompt. On automatic (class-agnostic) part segmentation, PartSAM reaches 69.5 IoU on PartObjaverse-Tiny and 72.4 on PartNet-E, exceeding the second-best method by more than 20 IoU points on both datasets. On the fine-grained 3DCoMPaT++ benchmark, PartSAM also outperforms PartField and its own ablation variant trained without the model-in-the-loop data, confirming the benefit of large-scale native 3D supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PartSAM outperforms all six baselines across both interactive and automatic segmentation tasks. In interactive segmentation (Table 1), PartSAM beats Point-SAM (Zhou et al., 2025) by 26.7 IoU points at IoU@1 on PartObjaverse-Tiny (56.1 vs 29.4) and 23.6 points on PartNet-E (59.5 vs 35.9), with the gap narrowing but remaining significant at higher prompt counts. In automatic segmentation (Table 2), PartSAM leads SAMesh (Tang et al., 2024) by 12.6 IoU on PartObjaverse-Tiny (69.5 vs 56.9) and by a dramatic 45.7 points on PartNet-E (72.4 vs 26.7), where SAMesh's reliance on mesh connectivity via community detection fails. PartField (Liu et al., 2025), the closest competitor, is surpassed by 18.0 IoU on PartObjaverse-Tiny (69.5 vs 51.5) and 13.3 on PartNet-E (72.4 vs 59.1); notably, PartField's performance drops sharply on AI-generated meshes lacking mesh connectivity. SAMPart3D (Yang et al., 2024b) trails by 16.0 and 16.2 IoU, while PartSLIP (Liu et al., 2023) and Find3D (Ma et al., 2024) are substantially behind at 31.5–34.9 and 21.3–21.7 IoU respectively, reflecting the limitations of text-driven and feature-alignment approaches for fine-grained part decomposition.

## Relation to Prior Work

PartSAM directly adapts SAM's promptable encoder-decoder paradigm (two-way transformer decoder, parallel output tokens, IoU prediction head) to the 3D domain, while building its triplane-based dual-branch encoder on PartField's architecture and triplet contrastive loss. It departs from the dominant 2D-lifting paradigm (SAMPart3D, SAMesh, PartField's clustering) by training natively on 3D shape-part pairs rather than distilling SAM's multi-view 2D masks, and from Point-SAM by replacing point-cloud transformers with a continuous triplane feature field that scales to large datasets. The model-in-the-loop annotation pipeline, which uses PartField's clustering outputs as pseudo-labels filtered through PartSAM's interactive segmentation, is a novel data-scaling strategy not seen in prior 3D part segmentation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PartSAM establishes the first foundation-model-style approach to 3D part segmentation that is simultaneously promptable, feed-forward, and trained on native 3D geometry, enabling interior structure segmentation that 2D-lifting methods fundamentally cannot achieve. The model-in-the-loop pipeline (5M+ shape-part pairs) and the demonstrated scaling curve suggest that further data growth will continue to improve performance, opening a path toward truly general 3D part understanding. Key open questions include: whether the approach can produce semantic labels (currently absent, as with 2D SAM), how it handles the long-tail of rare part types in open-world settings, and whether the triplane encoder can be further scaled or replaced by more expressive implicit representations.
