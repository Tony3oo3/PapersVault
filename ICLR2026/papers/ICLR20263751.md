## Problem

Mainstream Class Incremental Learning (CIL) evaluation protocols estimate model performance from only 3–5 randomly sampled class sequences, reporting a mean and standard deviation. The paper demonstrates both theoretically and empirically that this Random Sampling (RS) protocol systematically overestimates the mean, severely underestimates the variance, and fails to capture the true performance bounds, making model comparisons unreliable and potentially misleading for real-world deployment where class arrival order is uncontrollable.

## Input / Output

EDGE consumes only the textual class labels of a dataset (no image data required) and a pre-trained CLIP text encoder to produce d-dimensional semantic embeddings, from which a pairwise cosine similarity matrix is constructed. Given a class-to-task configuration (N classes partitioned into K tasks), the algorithm outputs three constructed sequences—a hard sequence (minimizing inter-task similarity), an easy sequence (maximizing it), and a random medium sequence—on which the target CIL model is trained and evaluated. The final output is a performance distribution estimate (mean, std) plus, when ground truth is available, JSD and Wasserstein distances quantifying how well the three-point estimate approximates the full distribution.

## Method

EDGE's core mechanism is similarity-guided extreme sequence construction. It first encodes each class label via the CLIP text encoder and computes a cosine similarity matrix. It then applies hierarchical clustering at multiple granularities to group semantically similar classes into tasks, followed by a greedy sequential construction: for the hard sequence, it starts with the most isolated task and iteratively appends the least similar remaining task; for the easy sequence, it does the reverse (most similar first). Among all multi-granularity candidates, the sequence with the lowest (or highest) inter-task similarity score S(O) is selected. The theoretical foundation rests on Theorem 3, which proves that lower inter-task similarity yields higher generalization error, and Theorem 2, which shows that incorporating two extreme sequences reduces the required random sample size from O(N ln N / ε²) to a constant proportional to (R^(σ))², making three-point evaluation statistically justified.

## Results

On the fully enumerable 6-class/3-task setting (90 sequences), EDGE consistently reduces distributional distance to the ground truth compared to the standard Random Sampling (RS) protocol. On CIFAR-100, EDGE lowers JSD from 0.44→0.30 (L2P) and 0.38→0.28 (CODA-Prompt), and Wasserstein distance from 2.81→2.00 (L2P) and 3.89→1.42 (Hide-Prompt). The gains are most dramatic on ImageNet-R, where EDGE cuts CODA-Prompt's JSD from 0.65 to 0.20 and Wasserstein distance from 9.85 to 2.37. EDGE also captures true performance bounds far more accurately: for L2P on CIFAR-100, the true lower bound is 71.83%, RS overestimates it at 83.83%, while EDGE estimates 72.83%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between the proposed EDGE protocol and the standard Random Sampling (RS) protocol, which is the de facto evaluation standard in CIL literature. EDGE outperforms RS on JSD in 10 out of 12 method-dataset pairs (tied on EASE and RanPAC for CIFAR-100, where both achieve 0.00 due to near-zero variance). The largest JSD improvement is CODA-Prompt on ImageNet-R (0.65→0.20, a 69% reduction), reflecting RS's severe failure to capture the wide performance spread of unstable methods. On Wasserstein distance, EDGE wins in 10 of 12 pairs, with the most striking gain being CODA-Prompt on ImageNet-R (9.85→2.37). For highly stable methods (EASE, RanPAC), both protocols achieve near-zero distance, confirming that EDGE's advantage is most pronounced precisely where RS is most misleading. The paper also shows that RS can produce incorrect model rankings (e.g., on CIFAR-100, RS suggests DER has a worse lower bound than EWC, while the truth is the opposite), whereas EDGE corrects these inversions, yielding ranking errors of 6 vs. 12 (CIFAR-100) and 2 vs. 10 (ImageNet-R) relative to the true ordering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Lin et al. (ICML 2023), whose generalization error bound for continual learning (cited as Lemma 2) provides the theoretical backbone for Theorem 3, which links inter-task similarity to generalization error. It departs from the mainstream RS evaluation protocol (3 random seeds, as used in L2P, CODA-Prompt, EASE, MOS, RanPAC, and others) by arguing that this protocol is fundamentally inadequate for distributional estimation. The sequence generation mechanism repurposes CLIP text embeddings and hierarchical clustering—tools from vision-language pre-training and unsupervised learning—toward the novel purpose of constructing adversarial class-ordering sequences, a use not previously explored in the CIL evaluation literature (Farquhar & Gal 2018; Hsu et al. 2018; Mundt et al. 2021; Chen et al. 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the standard 3-seed RS protocol systematically overestimates mean accuracy and severely underestimates variance (with a 20% gap between best and worst sequences in some cases) challenges a widely-adopted evaluation practice and has direct implications for model selection and deployment risk assessment in CIL. EDGE's practical advantage is that it requires the same computational budget as RS (3 sequences) while providing substantially tighter distributional estimates, making it a drop-in replacement. Open questions include whether CLIP-based semantic similarity remains a reliable proxy for task difficulty in non-visual or highly abstract domains, whether the near-Gaussian assumption on the performance distribution holds for very long sequences (K > 10), and whether the greedy sequence construction can be replaced by exact optimization as the number of tasks grows. The ground-truth validation is limited to 6-class/3-task enumerable settings; for standard 100-class benchmarks, EDGE's superiority is demonstrated only visually.
