## Problem

Standard MAE pretraining on retinal fundus images exhibits a systematic frequency bias: its uniform-information reconstruction objective over-encodes smooth low-frequency backgrounds while under-encoding the sparse, diagnostically critical high-frequency structures (vessel boundaries, microaneurysms, hemorrhages). CKA analysis reveals that MAE representations align strongly with low-frequency components (CKA=0.990) yet poorly with high-frequency ones (CKA=0.164), while linear probing shows the inverse diagnostic utility—high-frequency tokens outperform low-frequency ones across benchmarks. This mismatch between representational alignment and diagnostic value limits downstream disease detection performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB retinal fundus images as input, pretraining on ~256k unlabeled images from 39 public datasets. For high-frequency extraction, the green channel is specifically used (as it provides the strongest vessel/lesion contrast), processed through a Soft-FOV mask, Gaussian blur, and Butterworth high-pass filter to identify the top 25% of ViT tokens as high-frequency. The output is a frequency-balanced CLS-token embedding from a ViT-B/14 encoder, evaluated via frozen linear probing or full fine-tuning on binary disease classification (DR, AMD, GL) across five benchmarks including an out-of-distribution set (APTOS).

## Method

RetMAE augments standard MAE with a HighFreqMI regularizer that steers the encoder toward compact, high-frequency-sufficient representations without any architectural changes. The mechanism: (1) frequency-selected visible tokens (top 25% by Butterworth-filtered high-frequency score) are fed into an EMA teacher to produce a compact context latent Z_c^HF; (2) mutual information between the trainable latent Z and Z_c^HF is maximized using the MINE estimator, acting as a lower bound on I(Z; Z_c^HF); (3) the total loss is L_rec + 0.1·L_hmi (+ optional 0.01·L_aux for alignment to a pretrained fundus encoder). Theoretically, Theorem 1 establishes that MAE's MSE loss minimizes the conditional MI I(X_V; X_M | Z), while Theorem 2 shows that alignment to an ε-compact context upper-bounds the marginal MI I(X_V; Z), jointly optimizing the information-bottleneck Lagrangian. HighFreqMI is activated only after a 40-epoch warm-up to ensure the EMA teacher has stabilized, and the approach requires no paired text supervision or backbone modifications.

## Results

RetMAE achieves a macro-average AUROC of 0.941 under linear probing with auxiliary losses, surpassing RET-CLIP (0.937) and MAE+aux (0.923) across five retinal benchmarks (IDRiD, RFMiD-DR, RFMiD-AMD, CHAKSU, APTOS). Without auxiliary losses, RetMAE reaches 0.779 AUROC, a +0.094 improvement over standard MAE (0.685). Under full fine-tuning, RetMAE attains 0.928 average AUROC, exceeding RET-CLIP (0.910) and RETFound (0.876). With only ~25.6k unlabeled fundus images (~10% of the full pretraining set), RetMAE already outperforms RETFound trained on 904k images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RetMAE (with auxiliary losses) outperforms RET-CLIP by +0.004 macro-average AUROC (0.941 vs. 0.937) and MAE+aux by +0.018 (0.941 vs. 0.923), with the largest per-dataset gains on RFMiD-AMD (+0.018) and APTOS OOD (+0.012). In the image-only setting (no auxiliary), RetMAE achieves 0.779 AUROC, beating standard MAE (0.685) by +0.094 and RETFound (0.690) by +0.089, but trailing UrFound (0.855) which uses anatomy-guided masking with text supervision. Under full fine-tuning, RetMAE (0.928) surpasses both RET-CLIP (0.910) and RETFound (0.876), confirming gains transfer to the clinical deployment regime. The paper also shows that adding HighFreqMI to signal-level frequency-aware variants (HF masking, HF input concatenation) consistently improves them, indicating latent-level MI regularization captures information that input-level manipulation alone does not recover.

## Relation to Prior Work

RetMAE extends the MAE framework (He et al., 2022) by grounding its reconstruction objective in the mutual information Lagrangian formalized by Huang et al. (2025), then introduces a domain-specific HighFreqMI regularizer estimated via MINE (Belghazi et al., 2018) that aligns the trainable latent to an EMA-teacher context built from high-frequency tokens. Unlike MI-MAE, which enforces mask invariance, RetMAE steers the bottleneck toward clinically salient high-frequency structure, and unlike signal-level frequency-aware MIM variants (e.g., HF masking or HF input concatenation), it operates entirely at the latent level without architectural modification. The paper positions itself against vision-language retinal foundation models (RET-CLIP, FLAIR) by demonstrating that explicit high-frequency MI alignment, rather than language supervision, is the principal driver of diagnostic gains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CKA-based diagnosis that standard MAE strongly encodes low-frequency background (CKA 0.990) while under-representing diagnostically critical high-frequency structure (CKA 0.164)—yet the latter alone yields the best linear-probe AUROC—provides a clear mechanistic explanation for MAE's suboptimal performance on retinal images and a principled correction. The striking data-efficiency result (~2.6k pretraining images surpassing RETFound trained on 904k) suggests that in low-entropy, high-redundancy domains like fundus imaging, targeted frequency-aware objectives can substitute for scale. Key limitations include restriction to color fundus photography, a single ViT backbone, and evaluation on relatively small benchmarks; generalization to other modalities (OCT, wide-field), alternative architectures, and adaptive context targets remain open.
