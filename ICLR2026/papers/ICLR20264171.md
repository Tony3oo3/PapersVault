## Problem

Existing differentiable-rendering-based unsupervised 3D pre-training methods (e.g., UniPAD) are forced to pre-train LiDAR and camera encoders separately because jointly processing full point clouds and images exceeds GPU memory. This separation prevents the encoders from exploiting the complementarity between image semantics and LiDAR geometry during pre-training, leaving a performance gap for fusion 3D perception.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264171/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired LiDAR point clouds (N_p points with xyz coordinates plus d feature channels, e.g., intensity and timestamp) and multi-view RGB images (6 cameras on NuScenes, 5 on Waymo) with known camera-LiDAR extrinsics. It outputs pre-trained weights for three encoders (LiDAR, camera, and fusion) that are transferred to downstream detectors (BEVFusion, CenterPoint) or segmenters (Cylinder3D). A key constraint is that only ~1/100 of raw points and pixels are sampled for the rendering loss to fit within GPU memory, making the sampling strategy critical to performance.

## Method

CLAP enables joint pre-training via two key mechanisms. First, Curvature Sampling estimates geodesic curvature at each LiDAR point by computing the second-order derivative of the predicted SDF (via torch.autograd), then uses these curvature values as multinomial sampling weights to select informative points and their projected pixels for differentiable rendering, reducing the computational burden while preserving scene structure. Second, a set of N_K learnable prototypes represents parts of the 3D scene in a shared feature space; they are optimized via an Expectation-Maximization scheme that minimizes the entropy of soft assignment probabilities between modality-specific embeddings and prototypes. A cross-modal swapping prediction loss (using Sinkhorn-knotted codes) forces each modality to predict the other's prototype assignments, while a Gram Matrix Regularization term minimizes off-diagonal prototype similarities to prevent collapse. The total loss combines the differentiable rendering reconstruction (range + SDF + RGB) with the prototype learning objective, all trained end-to-end without labels.

## Results

On NuScenes (5% fine-tuning), CLAP achieves 51.17% mAP and 57.04% NDS, yielding +2.48% mAP and +1.76% NDS over random initialization — a 100% larger mAP gain than the previous SOTA UniPAD (+1.12% mAP). On Waymo (1% fine-tuning), CLAP achieves 62.87% mAP (Level-1) and 56.88% mAP (Level-2), with an average improvement of +1.28 over random, roughly double the best prior method (OCC-MAE, +0.74). In the extreme few-shot setting (0.5% fine-tuning data), CLAP provides up to +7.22% mAP and +4.71% NDS, demonstrating strong scaling potential.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLAP outperforms all five baselines on both NuScenes and Waymo. Against the closest competitor UniPAD (Yang et al., 2024), CLAP gains +1.36% mAP (51.17 vs 49.81) and +1.75% NDS (57.04 vs 55.29) on NuScenes, and +1.26% average mAP/mAPH on Waymo (+1.28 vs +0.02 improvement over random). PPKT (Liu et al., 2021a) is the second-best on NuScenes mAP (49.58) but trails CLAP by 1.59% mAP and 1.19% NDS. On Waymo, OCC-MAE (Min et al., 2023) is the strongest prior method (+0.74 avg improvement), while CLAP nearly doubles that gain (+1.28). ALSO (Boulch et al., 2023) and SLidR (Sautier et al., 2022) both underperform random initialization on NuScenes mAP, highlighting that not all pre-training strategies transfer well to fusion perception. CLAP also generalizes cross-dataset: on ONCE (40-beam LiDAR), pre-training on NuScenes still improves CenterPoint mAP by +0.81% at convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLAP builds directly on UniPAD (Yang et al., 2024), which introduced differentiable-rendering-based masked reconstruction for 3D pre-training but was forced to pre-train LiDAR and camera encoders separately due to GPU memory constraints. The key departure is enabling joint pre-training of both modalities: Curvature Sampling (using second-order SDF derivatives to select informative points/pixels) reduces the computational burden, while a prototype learning module adapted from SwAV (Caron et al., 2020) creates a shared feature space that bridges LiDAR geometry and image semantics. The neural field rendering decoder follows NeuS (Wang et al., 2021a) with an added surface SDF loss, and the EM training scheme with Gram Matrix Regularization is a novel adaptation of clustering objectives to the cross-modal 3D setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first joint differentiable-rendering pre-training method for fusion 3D perception, addressing a practical bottleneck: separate pre-training prevents the encoders from exploiting the complementarity between image semantics and LiDAR geometry. The scaling property (gains grow from +2.48% mAP at 5% fine-tuning data to +7.22% at 0.5%) suggests that with larger unlabeled pre-training corpora, the advantage could widen substantially, which is relevant as autonomous driving datasets grow. Limitations include noisy curvature estimation in early training (requiring a warm-up phase), prototype assignment noise in unsupervised settings, and the fact that pre-training was not scaled beyond the standard NuScenes/Waymo training splits. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other sensor combinations (e.g., radar) and whether the prototype count and EM scheme can be made more adaptive.
