## Problem

While RL-based post-training (PPO, GRPO) demonstrably improves LLM reasoning beyond SFT alone, the internal mechanistic changes it induces remain unexplored. Existing interpretability work analyzes fixed LLMs without linking observed circuit structures to the specific post-training algorithm that produced them, leaving a gap between external performance gains and internal circuit-level explanations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262162/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired 7B-parameter LLMs (SFT base and RL-fine-tuned counterpart), mathematical problem prompts from GSM8K/MATH/College Math, and each model's own generated token sequences truncated to a fixed fraction α of the mean length. Edge importance is computed via a single forward+backward pass using self-entropy (cross-entropy of the model against its own truncated output) as the loss signal, yielding per-sample edge-weight matrices of shape n_o × n_i. These matrices are aggregated across samples into three scalar metrics—Activation Intensity (mean |W|), Information Complexity (Shannon entropy of |W| histogram), and Distribution Kurtosis (excess kurtosis of W)—enabling direct before/after comparison.

## Method

The paper represents each transformer as a directed acyclic graph over residual pathways, where nodes are attention and FFN sub-modules and edges encode residual information flow. Edge importance is estimated via Edge Attribution Patching (EAP), which approximates the loss change from ablating an edge (O,H) as the inner product −⟨∇_H L, O⟩, computable for all edges simultaneously in one forward+backward pass. The core empirical finding is that online RL (PPO/GRPO) systematically increases Activation Intensity and Information Complexity while decreasing Distribution Kurtosis across four model families, whereas DPO produces weaker or inconsistent changes. The theoretical contribution is a unified gradient-form analysis showing that online RL's on-policy sampling expands the stochastic support of the training distribution, compelling the network to activate dormant circuits, while DPO's offline fixed-support optimization lacks this exploratory pressure—explaining the observed metric divergences.

## Results

Across four ~7B model pairs on MATH, College Math, and GSM8K, online RL fine-tuning (GRPO or PPO) consistently increases Activation Intensity (e.g., DeepSeek-Math: 1.10e-3 → 1.31e-3; Mistral: 6.76e-4 → 7.71e-4 at α=0.1 on MATH) and Information Complexity (e.g., DeepSeek-Math: 0.172 → 0.247; Distilled-Qwen: 0.111 → 0.196), while decreasing Distribution Kurtosis (e.g., DeepSeek-Math: 357 → 223; Mistral: 451 → 307). In contrast, DPO-trained Qwen2.5 shows no clear increase in Activation Intensity (6.95e-4 → 6.90e-4) and even a decrease in Information Complexity on College Math (0.080 → 0.217 at α=0.03 but 0.123 → 0.0995 at α=0.3), confirming that the internal circuitry effects are specific to online RL rather than preference optimization in general.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new method to benchmark against external baselines; instead, it compares the internal circuitry effects of different post-training paradigms (SFT, online RL via GRPO/PPO, and offline preference optimization via DPO) across four model families. Online RL (GRPO on DeepSeek-Math and Distilled-Qwen; PPO on Mistral) consistently produces stronger and more diverse internal activations than the SFT baseline across all three datasets and all four α settings, with Activation Intensity increasing by 15–25% and Distribution Kurtosis decreasing by 20–30%. DPO (Qwen2.5) is the clear outlier: it reduces Distribution Kurtosis (544→483 on MATH) but fails to increase Activation Intensity or Information Complexity, and on College Math its Information Complexity actually drops below the SFT baseline at larger α values. The intervention experiment in Appendix B further confirms causality: raising sampling temperature from 0.6 to 1.0 during GRPO training suppresses the activation-intensity and information-complexity gains and reduces final GSM8K accuracy from 51.2% to 36.5%, demonstrating that effective on-policy exploration is the mechanism driving the observed internal changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Edge Attribution Patching (EAP) framework from Syed et al. (2023) and Nanda (2023) to a new comparative setting: measuring how RL fine-tuning reshapes internal residual pathways. It inherits the graph-theoretic DAG representation of transformer layers from ACDC (Conmy et al., 2023), but departs from prior mechanistic interpretability work by explicitly correlating internal circuit changes with the specific post-training algorithm (PPO, GRPO, DPO) rather than analyzing a single static model. The unified gradient-formulation of post-training objectives from Shao et al. (2024) provides the theoretical lens through which the authors interpret why online RL and DPO produce qualitatively different internal effects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a mechanistic explanation for the empirically observed 'SFT memorizes, RL generalizes' phenomenon (Chu et al., 2025) by showing that online RL systematically engages more internal pathways and diversifies activation patterns, while DPO—despite being mathematically derived from RL—lacks the on-policy exploration dynamics needed to drive these changes. This distinction is practically relevant for practitioners choosing between online RL and preference-based post-training. Key limitations include confinement to mathematical reasoning tasks and 7B-scale models, leaving open whether the observed circuit dynamics generalize to open-ended domains (code, creative writing) and larger architectures. The sampling-temperature intervention in Appendix B provides causal support but was conducted on a single 3B model, so broader validation is needed.
