## Problem

Multi-modal action distributions in robotic manipulation create a fundamental tension: discretizing actions into tokens loses fine-grained continuous variations, while single-stage generative policies (diffusion, flow matching) suffer from mode bouncing—randomly switching between valid action modes across consecutive timesteps, causing trajectory discontinuities and end-effector instability. No existing method simultaneously preserves within-mode continuous detail and enforces stable, consistent coarse-mode selection.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a fixed-size point cloud (from RGB-D via backprojection and FPS), robot proprioception (joint angles, extended with XHand joint angles for dexterous setups), and tactile sensing (5 fingertips × 120 taxels × 3D force vectors). It outputs a continuous action chunk of shape T_p × d_a in joint space, executing only the first T_a ≤ T_p steps before re-planning in a receding-horizon closed-loop scheme. The point cloud is processed through a PointNet-style per-point MLP with max-pooling, while proprioception and tactile channels pass through separate MLPs before concatenation and a final projection into a shared observation embedding.

## Method

PF-DAG is a two-stage framework that explicitly decouples coarse mode selection from fine-grained action generation. Stage 1: a VQ-VAE (compact MLP encoder/decoder) compresses action chunks into K=64 discrete primary modes, and a lightweight MLP classifier (Primary Mode Policy) is trained with cross-entropy to predict the VQ code from the observation embedding, using greedy selection at test time. Stage 2: a mode-conditioned MeanFlow policy with a DiT-style transformer backbone generates a residual Δa via a one-step average velocity field, added to the VQ-decoded reconstruction to produce the final continuous action chunk. The MeanFlow field is trained with squared-error supervision over randomly sampled (τ, r) intervals, and at inference a single function evaluation at (τ,r)=(0,1) yields the action. Theoretically, the paper proves via the law of total variance that this two-stage design achieves a strictly lower MSE bound than single-stage generative policies whenever inter-mode variance is positive, because discretizing into primary modes removes inter-mode variance from the residual error.

## Results

PF-DAG achieves a weighted success rate of 0.72 across 18 core tasks spanning Adroit, DexArt, and MetaWorld, substantially outperforming the strongest baselines DP3 and FlowPolicy (both 0.51). The largest gains appear on MetaWorld, where PF-DAG reaches 0.68 (Medium) and 0.72 (Hard) versus 0.45 and 0.35 for DP3, and on Adroit Pen (0.65 vs. 0.43 for DP3). On real-world tactile dexterous tasks, PF-DAG also leads, e.g., 0.80 on Place Toy Into Bin vs. 0.60 for DP3 and 0.00 for Vanilla BC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PF-DAG outperforms all six baselines on the weighted success metric (0.72 vs. 0.51 for DP3 and FlowPolicy, 0.30 for DP, 0.15 for BC-H, and 0.08 for IBC). The most pronounced advantage is on MetaWorld, where PF-DAG's 0.68/0.72 (Medium/Hard) exceeds DP3's 0.45/0.35 by over 20 percentage points, reflecting the benefit of stable primary-mode selection under short action chunks. On Adroit Pen, PF-DAG (0.65) surpasses DP3 (0.43) and FlowPolicy (0.53) by 12–22 points, while on Adroit Hammer all top methods (PF-DAG, DP3, FlowPolicy) saturate at 1.00. On DexArt, PF-DAG leads on Laptop (0.90 vs. 0.85/0.83) and Faucet (0.72 vs. 0.63/0.42) but ties DP3 on Toilet (0.82) and Bucket (0.47 vs. 0.46). In real-world evaluation, PF-DAG consistently beats DP3 by 5–20 points and Vanilla BC by 20–80 points across four tasks, with the largest gap on tactile dexterous tasks (Wipe Table: 0.70 vs. 0.55 vs. 0.00).

## Relation to Prior Work

PF-DAG sits at the intersection of two established threads: discrete action tokenization (VQ-VAE, FAST, RT-1/RT-2) and generative continuous action policies (Diffusion Policy, Flow Policy, ACT). It departs from prior tokenization work by using the discrete codebook solely for coarse primary-mode selection rather than as the final action representation, and from prior generative policies by conditioning a one-step MeanFlow decoder on the selected mode to eliminate mode bouncing. The theoretical analysis draws on the law of total variance to show the two-stage decomposition strictly reduces the MSE lower bound relative to single-stage latent-variable policies, and the hierarchical structure is distinguished from HDP by learning modes end-to-end from action-chunk clusters rather than relying on task-specific heuristics like contact-point waypoints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PF-DAG offers a principled, theoretically grounded solution to the mode-bouncing problem that plagues single-stage generative policies in closed-loop manipulation, while the one-step MeanFlow decoder enables reactive control at inference speeds comparable to 1-NFE diffusion. The ablation showing that VQ reconstruction alone yields near-zero success (0.01) underscores that the continuous refinement stage is not optional but essential, clarifying the division of labor between the two stages. Open questions include whether the discrete codebook can be shared or meta-learned across tasks to reduce per-task tuning, how the approach scales to very high-dynamics low-latency settings where temporal granularity matters, and whether uncertainty-aware refiners can further improve robustness to out-of-distribution observations.
