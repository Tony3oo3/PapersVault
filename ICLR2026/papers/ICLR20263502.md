## Problem

Existing robust preference alignment methods for LLMs rely on single, narrow heuristics—such as perplexity difference or training loss—to identify noisy preference labels, leaving them blind to the diverse failure modes present in real-world annotation noise. No prior work systematically fuses multiple intrinsic model diagnostics into a holistic reliability assessment, and it remains unclear whether any single signal suffices or whether their combination is essential.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263502/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes noisy preference triples (prompt, chosen, rejected) alongside a small clean meta-dataset of 100–200 pairs and a frozen reference SFT policy. At every training step, the current policy dynamically produces a 3-dimensional diagnostic vector per sample (log-perplexity difference, instance-wise DPO loss, and mean token-level entropy of the chosen response), which is fed to a 2-layer MLP meta-learner that outputs a scalar non-negative weight. The final output is the aligned LLM policy after one epoch of bi-level training, with the meta-learner's learned weighting function available for inference-time sample filtering.

## Method

The core mechanism is a bi-level meta-learning loop that learns a non-linear fusion function V(z; W) mapping the three intrinsic diagnostics—preference consistency (PPLDiff), learning difficulty (DPO loss), and generation confidence (token entropy)—to per-sample reweighting factors. In the inner loop, the weighted DPO loss produces virtual policy parameters; in the outer loop, the meta-learner W is updated by descending the DPO loss of those virtual parameters on a clean meta-batch, so that weights are shaped by downstream alignment quality rather than hand-crafted thresholds. The key insight is that each diagnostic has complementary blind spots (PPLDiff is fooled by fluent misinformation, loss conflates noise with genuine difficulty, entropy conflates ambiguity with knowledge gaps), and the meta-learner discovers non-linear interactions—confirmed via SHAP analysis—that no single heuristic can replicate. A generalization bound shows the meta-learner's clean-data risk is controlled by meta-set size and model complexity, justifying the small meta-dataset requirement.

## Results

On naturally noisy real-world datasets, the proposed diagnostic fusion method achieves 76.9% Reward Model Accuracy on WebGPT Comparisons and 89.6% on Chatbot Arena, outperforming the strongest baseline (PerpCorrect Dynamic) by 3.7 and 1.8 percentage points respectively. On the 10.8M-sample StackExchange dataset, the method reaches 80.6% accuracy versus 78.7% for PerpCorrect (Dynamic) and 74.5% for Vanilla DPO. In GPT-4 pairwise evaluation on Golden HH with 30% noise, the method is preferred over DR-DPO in 62.5% of head-to-head comparisons, confirming that reward-model gains translate to tangible generation quality improvements.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method consistently outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and noise condition tested. Against Vanilla DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), the gap is largest (e.g., +8.6 pp on WebGPT, +6.1 pp on StackExchange-10.8M), reflecting DPO's sensitivity to label corruption. Against rDPO (Chowdhury et al., 2024), which applies a global noise correction, the fusion method gains 2–4 pp, demonstrating the advantage of instance-level adaptive weighting over uniform adjustments. Against DR-DPO (Azar et al., 2024), the most competitive robust baseline, the method still leads by 1.8–2.4 pp on real-world data and by a wider margin under synthetic noise (ε≥0.3). Against PerpCorrect (Kong et al., 2024), the strongest single-heuristic baseline using dynamic PPLDiff, the fusion method wins by 0.8–3.7 pp, with the gap widening under high noise and adversarial attacks (89.4% vs. 78.6% under white-box adaptive attack), confirming that multi-diagnostic fusion overcomes the blind spots of any single perplexity-based signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Meta-WeightNet paradigm (Shu et al., 2019; Ren et al., 2018) from image classification to LLM preference alignment, replacing the single training-loss input with a richer diagnostic vector that fuses perplexity difference, DPO loss, and token-level generation entropy. It departs from prior robust DPO methods (cDPO, rDPO, DR-DPO) that apply global noise corrections, and from PerpCorrect (Kong et al., 2024) that relies on a single PPLDiff heuristic, by arguing that preference reliability is multifaceted and no single internal signal suffices. The conceptual contribution is reframing the alignment model as its own diagnostician, where a meta-learner learns to interpret the model's internal state rather than applying a fixed threshold or rule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic quantitative analysis (via SHAP) of how intrinsic model diagnostics interact in identifying noisy preferences, showing that their fusion is empirically necessary and that their relative importance adapts to noise level. Practical limitations include the requirement for a small clean meta-dataset (100–200 samples) and a 41% training-time overhead from bi-level optimization, which may constrain adoption in resource-sensitive settings. Open questions include whether the diagnostic fusion paradigm transfers to other alignment objectives beyond DPO (e.g., RLHF with PPO), whether the meta-dataset can be eliminated via self-supervised alternatives, and whether the approach generalizes to multi-turn or multi-constraint alignment where preference reliability is even more ambiguous.
