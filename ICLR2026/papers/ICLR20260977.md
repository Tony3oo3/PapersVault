## Problem

Existing reward models for RLHF face a fundamental trade-off: Discriminative RMs (e.g., Bradley-Terry) output uncalibrated scalar scores reflecting only relative preference without probabilistic interpretation, while Generative RMs offer interpretability but require costly point-wise supervision and rigid pairwise input formats. Neither paradigm provides a full probability distribution over response quality that supports uncertainty estimation, absolute quality calibration, and flexible single/multi-response scoring in Best-of-N settings.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single text prompt and one candidate response as input (not a pair), formatted via a structured evaluation prompt template. It produces a probability distribution over 9 ordinal ratings (1–9) by directly extracting and normalizing the LM head's vocabulary probabilities for the numeric tokens '1' through '9' at the final token position—requiring no additional value head or parameters. A continuous reward score is then computed as the weighted average of ratings under this distribution, avoiding the tie-prone argmax decoding.

## Method

OPRM reframes reward modeling as learning a full probability distribution over a discrete ordinal scale {1,…,9}, derived from Random Utility Model theory. The preference probability is computed in closed form as the sum over all score pairs where the chosen rating exceeds the rejected rating (Eq. 6), replacing the intractable continuous integral with a tractable summation. The distribution is obtained parameter-free by repurposing the LM head's token probabilities for digits, leveraging the model's inherent ordinal knowledge of numbers. Region Flooding Tuning (RgFT) further calibrates the distribution to absolute quality by partitioning the scale into three semantic regions (bad {1,2,3}, normal {4,5,6}, good {7,8,9}) and flooding the joint probability region into a lower-triangular shape, which preserves the gradient incentive to push chosen scores upward and rejected scores downward while constraining mass within the correct quality sub-region. This enables semi-supervised training by jointly leveraging quality-labeled and preference-only data.

## Results

OPRM-Qwen2.5-32B achieves an Overall* score of 68.5% (excluding Reward Bench due to contamination), surpassing the previous best public model GPT-4o (66.2%) by 2.3% and Nemotron-4-340B-Reward (63.3%) by 5.2%. With Region Flooding Tuning, OPRM-RgFT-32B further improves to 68.9% Overall* and achieves SOTA 67.3% on PPE-Correctness. The paper reports 2.9%–7.4% improvements over prior reward models across four benchmarks, with the largest gains on RMB (75.6% vs. 73.8% for GPT-4o) and PPE-Correctness (66.1% vs. 63.0% for InternLM2-20B-Reward).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPRM-32B outperforms all 14 baselines on the Overall* metric. Against the strongest public models, it beats GPT-4o (66.2%) by 2.3% and Nemotron-4-340B-Reward (63.3%) by 5.2%, despite being substantially smaller (32B vs. 340B parameters). Among the reproduced DeepSeek baselines, OPRM-32B surpasses DeepSeek-GRM-27B (64.5%) by 4.0% and DeepSeek-BTRM-27B (64.3%) by 4.2%. On the Best-of-N benchmarks that better reflect practical utility, the gains are most pronounced: OPRM-32B leads GPT-4o by 1.8% on RMB (75.6 vs. 73.8) and by 8.5% on PPE-Correctness (66.1 vs. 57.6). RgFT further improves PPE-Correctness to 67.3% (SOTA) but slightly reduces Reward Bench accuracy (88.9 vs. 91.3), suggesting a trade-off between absolute quality calibration and pairwise discrimination on that particular benchmark. The 72B variant underperforms the 32B variant (67.6% vs. 68.5% Overall*), which the authors attribute to Qwen2.5-32B's exceptional zero-shot capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the Bradley-Terry pairwise preference model by reframing reward as a full probability distribution over ordinal quality ratings, grounded in Random Utility Model theory. It draws on the parameter-free technique from UltraFeedback (Cui et al., 2023) of repurposing the LM head's vocabulary probabilities, and borrows structural ideas from deep ordinal regression (SORD, ALDL, POE), but applies them to the reward-modeling setting in a way that unifies discriminative and generative paradigms. The key conceptual departure from prior DRMs is replacing a single uncalibrated scalar with a discrete distribution, and from GRMs is eliminating the need for chain-of-thought critique generation or costly pointwise supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OPRM offers a practical, parameter-free alternative to BT-based reward models that simultaneously improves calibration (80.6% ECE reduction) and enables absolute quality assessment, addressing a long-standing limitation of pairwise reward scores. The Region Flooding Tuning strategy provides a data-efficient path to domain adaptation via semi-supervised learning, which is practically valuable for deploying reward models in new domains. Open questions include whether the ordinal discretization (1–9) is truly necessary versus a continuous formulation, whether annotation noise from coarse AI labeling limits gains on subjective tasks, and whether the approach extends naturally to multi-dimensional or compositional reward signals.
