## Problem

Dataset distillation has been almost exclusively studied for image classification, leaving the more complex object detection setting largely unexplored. The sole prior detection-specific method (DCOD) relies on computationally expensive optimization-based model inversion to generate synthetic images. This paper asks whether high-fidelity condensed detection datasets can be synthesized without any gradient-based optimization, while preserving both spatial and semantic information required for multi-object localization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263536/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a large annotated detection dataset (MS COCO or PASCAL VOC) with per-object bounding boxes and class labels, along with a pre-trained observer detector (e.g., Faster R-CNN-101). It produces a synthesized dataset of IPD images (canvas sizes 484×578 for COCO, 375×500 for VOC) at compression ratios as low as 0.25%, where each image contains copy-pasted object patches with extended bounding boxes and associated channel-wise FPN soft labels (normalized feature maps of shape C×H×W) used for PKD-based supervision during post-evaluation.

## Method

OD³ is a two-stage optimization-free synthesis pipeline. In candidate selection, object patches are extracted from the original dataset and placed onto a blank canvas via random copy-paste with an IoU overlap threshold τ, while a segment-based sampling controller guarantees that no object appears in multiple synthesized images. In candidate screening, a pre-trained observer detector runs inference on the partially assembled canvas and removes any object whose detection confidence falls below threshold η, iteratively refining the composition. A scale-aware dynamic context extension (SA-DCE) adaptively expands bounding boxes inversely proportional to object area, providing richer peripheral context for small objects. For post-evaluation, the paper replaces standard logit-based soft labels with channel-wise FPN feature maps normalized via Pearson correlation (PKD), which the authors show provides substantially more informative supervision for detection than MSE on raw FPN outputs. The framework is supported by a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) showing that the add-then-remove iterative scheme achieves an objective value at least as high as add-only placement.

## Results

On MS COCO at 1.0% compression (IPD), OD³ achieves 22.40 mAP, 39.50 mAP₅₀, and 22.90 mAP₇₅, surpassing the prior best method DCOD by 10.3, 14.8, and 12.5 percentage points respectively. At the extreme 0.25% compression, OD³ still reaches 12.90 mAP and 24.30 mAP₅₀, more than doubling DCOD's 7.20 mAP and 17.20 mAP₅₀. On PASCAL VOC at 2.0% compression, OD³ achieves 58.70 mAP₅₀, exceeding DCOD by 8.0 points. The full-dataset upper bound on COCO is 39.80 mAP / 60.10 mAP₅₀, meaning OD³ at 1.0% recovers roughly 56% of full-data mAP₅₀ performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OD³ dominates all six baselines across every compression ratio on both MS COCO and PASCAL VOC. Against the only prior dataset-distillation method for detection, DCOD (Qi et al., 2024), OD³ gains +5.7 to +10.3 mAP and +7.1 to +14.8 mAP₅₀ on COCO, with the largest gap at 1.0% IPD (39.50 vs. 24.70 mAP₅₀). The coreset-selection baselines—Random (Rebuffi et al., 2017), Uniform (Lee et al., 2024), K-Center (Sener & Savarese, 2017), and Herding (Castro et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2012)—all trail OD³ by 10–27 mAP₅₀ points at 1.0% IPD, confirming that simple image-level selection strategies are far less effective than the proposed instance-level synthesis. On PASCAL VOC, OD³ outperforms DCOD by +0.6, +4.7, and +8.0 mAP₅₀ at 0.5%, 1.0%, and 2.0% IPD respectively, while the coreset methods remain 10–30 points behind. The paper also shows cross-architecture transfer: distilled data trained with a Faster R-CNN observer generalizes to RetinaNet, ViTDet, and Deformable DETR targets with only modest degradation, whereas DETR-based targets suffer the largest drop under extreme compression (Table 11).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends dataset distillation from the well-studied image classification setting to object detection, a task where only one prior optimization-based method (DCOD, Qi et al. 2024) exists. Conceptually, OD³ departs from the pixel-level model-inversion paradigm of DCOD and classification DD by replacing gradient-based synthesis with a copy-paste construction guided by a pre-trained observer detector, leveraging PKD (Cao et al., 2022) and fast knowledge distillation (Shen & Xing, 2022) for soft-label supervision. It also contrasts with coreset selection methods (K-Center, Herding, CSOD) that operate at image-level granularity and typically achieve compression ratios above 20%, whereas OD³ targets extreme compression (0.25%–5%) at the instance level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OD³ demonstrates that optimization-free copy-paste synthesis with observer-based screening can match or exceed optimization-based distillation at extreme compression ratios, offering a dramatically cheaper pipeline (4.7 hours on a single GPU vs. iterative pixel optimization). A key open question highlighted by the paper is the sharp degradation of transformer-based detectors (DETR, ViTDet) under extreme data scarcity, suggesting that distilled datasets may not be equally effective across all architectural paradigms. The method's reliance on ground-truth bounding boxes during synthesis also limits its applicability to label-scarce or fully unsupervised settings, and whether the approach generalizes to other dense prediction tasks (segmentation, keypoint detection) remains unexplored.
