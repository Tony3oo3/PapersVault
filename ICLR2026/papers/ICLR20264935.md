## Problem

Large reasoning models generate uncontrolled-length chain-of-thought outputs, making it difficult to allocate a fixed inference-time budget while maintaining accuracy. Existing length-control methods either degrade performance substantially (S1's budget forcing ignores the solution segment) or demand heavy training resources (L1 requires 700–820 RL steps with 4K response lengths). The paper asks: how can a model be trained to reason effectively under strict, user-defined token budgets without sacrificing solution quality?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264935/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a text prompt (math or code problem) and a total token budget c, which the user splits into a thinking budget t and a solution budget s (c = t + s). The output is a structured sequence: a thinking segment enclosed in  tags followed by a solution segment. If the model has not emitted  by the time t tokens are consumed, the token is forcibly appended and generation continues into the solution phase for up to s tokens. The model trained with a single fixed budget pair (1K + 1K) generalizes to arbitrary unseen (t, s) configurations at test time without further fine-tuning.

## Method

Elastic Reasoning combines two components. First, Separate Budgeting at inference explicitly reserves s tokens for the solution phase, ensuring the final answer is never truncated even when thinking is cut short. Second, Budget-Constrained Rollout is a lightweight GRPO training procedure in which each rollout simulates the inference-time truncation: the policy generates thinking up to t* tokens, and if  has not appeared, it is forcibly inserted before the model generates the solution within s* tokens. The reward is computed on the truncated trajectory, teaching the model to produce correct answers from incomplete reasoning. Training requires only 200 steps (math) or 30 steps (code) with a 2K maximum response length, compared to 700–820 steps for L1 baselines. A key finding is that the model internalizes a flexible reasoning strategy: it prioritizes informative content early in the thinking phase and generates more concise, efficient solutions even in unconstrained settings, reducing average token usage by over 30%.

## Results

On AIME2024, E1-Math-1.5B achieves 35.0% Pass@1 under unconstrained (24K) inference, compared to 27.1% for L1-Max and 24.2% for L1-Exact, while using 32.1% fewer tokens than the original DeepScaleR-1.5B. On MATH500, E1-Math-1.5B reaches 83.6% accuracy with only 1,619 tokens per question. For code, E1-Code-14B attains a Codeforces rating of 1987 (96.0th percentile) and 58.4% on LiveCodeBench, while reducing average token usage by 37.4% (from 17,815 to 11,145 tokens) relative to DeepCoder-14B-Preview2. Training requires only 200 GRPO steps for math and 30 for code, versus 700–820 steps for L1 baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against S1 (Budget Forcing), E1-Math-1.5B consistently outperforms across all token budgets in Figure 4, with the gap widening at tighter constraints (e.g., at 1.5K total tokens, E1 achieves 13.5% vs. S1's substantially lower accuracy). Against L1-Exact, E1-Math-1.5B achieves 35.0% vs. 24.2% on AIME2024 at 24K, and 24.8% vs. 14.7% at the 3K+1K budget. Against L1-Max, E1-Math-1.5B surpasses it at medium and large budgets (24.8% vs. 24.0% at 3K; 27.9% vs. 25.8% at 4K; 35.0% vs. 27.1% at 24K), though L1-Max is slightly stronger at the tightest 1.5K budget (17.5% vs. 13.5%). For code, E1-Code-14B in the unconstrained setting matches or exceeds DeepCoder-14B-Preview2 (58.4% vs. 58.1% LiveCodeBench; 1987 vs. 1945 Codeforces) while using 37.4% fewer tokens, and is comparable to O1-2024-12-17 (Low) at 1987 vs. 1991 Codeforces rating. Notably, E1 requires only 200 training steps versus 700 (L1-Exact) and 820 (L1-Max), and generalizes to unseen budget configurations without additional fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on length-controlled reasoning (S1's budget forcing, L1's RL with prompt-embedded length constraints) by introducing a structural decomposition: explicitly separating the thinking and solution phases with independently allocated token budgets. Rather than penalizing trajectory length in the reward or instructing the model via prompt, Elastic Reasoning trains under forced truncation of the thinking segment via a budget-constrained rollout integrated into GRPO, teaching the model to produce coherent solutions from incomplete reasoning. It also departs from Long2Short methods that compress trajectories post-hoc, instead making the model natively robust to truncation at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: real-world deployment of reasoning LLMs faces hard token/latency/compute budgets, and this paper provides a lightweight (200 RL steps) training recipe that yields models generalizing to arbitrary unseen budgets without further fine-tuning. A notable open question is whether the approach scales to much larger models (70B+) where the 1.5B model shows slight unconstrained degradation; the code model's 30-step training also raises questions about convergence. The finding that budget-constrained training incidentally produces 30–37% shorter trajectories even without constraints suggests a broader principle about RL shaping reasoning efficiency, but whether this holds across architectures and task domains beyond math/code remains to be established.
