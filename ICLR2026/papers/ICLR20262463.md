## Problem

Existing reconstruction-based and prediction-based methods for univariate time series anomaly detection struggle to capture two subtle anomaly types: small point anomalies that appear normal over longer windows, and slowly rising segment anomalies that deviate gradually from periodic patterns. The root cause is that prior methods either focus on frequency components without local dependencies (e.g., FCVAE) or use timing information without frequency details (e.g., KAN-AD), failing to jointly model local trends and evolving periodicity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a univariate time series window of length total_window_size (e.g., 240 points), which is split into non-overlapping seasonal windows (size 48) and overlapping contextual windows (size 4) before FFT conversion. Raw time-domain values are concatenated as covariates with the frequency-domain features before LSTM processing. The model outputs a predicted mean and variance per future time point from each branch; anomaly scores are derived from the discrepancy between predicted and observed values, with the NLL loss masking anomalous regions during training.

## Method

CS-LSTMs employs a dual-branch prediction architecture: the S-LSTM branch segments history into non-overlapping windows, applies FFT to capture evolving periodicity in the frequency domain, and uses a single-layer LSTM to predict the next cycle; the C-LSTM branch uses shorter overlapping windows (also FFT-transformed) to model short-term local fluctuations. A wavelet-based noise decomposition with MAD-estimated VisuShrink soft-thresholding denoises the series before training, separating signal from noise without requiring ground-truth anomaly labels. Training uses a masked Negative Log-Likelihood loss that retains original values at normal points and substitutes denoised estimates at anomalous points, so the model learns the probabilistic distribution of normal behavior without being misled by unreliable anomaly ground truth. The total loss is the sum of both branches' NLL losses, and at inference, anomalies are flagged when observed values deviate from the predicted normal range.

## Results

CS-LSTMs achieves the best Best F1 and Delay F1 across all four primary datasets: Yahoo (0.885 / 0.878), KPI (0.936 / 0.879), WSD (0.910 / 0.857), and NAB (0.996 / 0.918). The headline gains over the strongest baseline (FCVAE) are +3.1% Best F1 on Yahoo, +1.2% on KPI, +10.5% on WSD, and +0.6% on NAB, with corresponding Delay F1 improvements of +3.9%, +2.8%, +16.1%, and +0.7%. The model also uses only 600K parameters (vs. 1.4M for SOTA) and reduces inference time by 40% (4.62 ms vs. 7.73 ms on GPU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CS-LSTMs outperforms all ten baselines on every dataset for both Best F1 and Delay F1. The closest competitor is FCVAE (Wang et al., 2024), which leads on NAB Best F1 (0.972 vs. 0.996) but trails CS-LSTMs by 3.1 points on Yahoo, 1.2 on KPI, and a substantial 10.5 on WSD. KAN-AD (Zhou et al., 2024) is competitive on KPI (0.912 vs. 0.936) and NAB (0.990 vs. 0.996) but falls well behind on Yahoo (0.679 vs. 0.885) and WSD (0.756 vs. 0.910). TFAD (Zhang et al., 2022a) performs well on Yahoo (0.792) but degrades sharply on WSD (0.628) and NAB (0.734), where its frequency-domain decomposition struggles with label noise. Informer (Zhou et al., 2021) and Anomaly-Transformer (Xu et al., 2021) show strong NAB scores but are inconsistent across datasets, with Anomaly-Transformer dropping to 0.274 Best F1 on Yahoo. Reconstruction-based methods (DONUT, VQRAE, AnoTransfer) and the statistical SPOT baseline are consistently the weakest, confirming that prediction-based dual-branch modeling is critical for subtle anomaly detection in UTS.

## Relation to Prior Work

CS-LSTMs sits at the intersection of prediction-based TSAD methods (Informer, Anomaly-Transformer, LSTMAD) and frequency-domain approaches (TFAD, FCVAE), but departs from both by explicitly modeling the *evolution* of periodicity via a sequence of Fourier-transformed windows fed to an LSTM, rather than treating periodicity as a static property of a single window. The noise decomposition strategy repurposes classical wavelet shrinkage (VisuShrink with MAD-based thresholding) from signal processing into the training pipeline of an anomaly detector, addressing the label-scarcity problem that plagues reconstruction-based VAEs like FCVAE and DONUT. The dual-branch design (seasonal + contextual) is conceptually analogous to multi-scale decomposition in TFAD but implemented with recurrent rather than convolutional/transformer backbones, and the masked NLL loss extends standard probabilistic forecasting losses to handle the absence of reliable anomaly ground truth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper targets two practically important but previously under-detected anomaly types (small point spikes and slowly rising segment deviations) that are common in AIOps monitoring, and demonstrates consistent SOTA across four benchmarks with a 40% inference speedup, making it a practical deployment candidate. However, the method remains restricted to univariate series, and the window-size hyperparameters (seasonal and contextual) still require dataset-specific tuning guided by known periodicity, limiting fully automated deployment. Open questions include whether the dual-branch design extends naturally to multivariate settings, whether the wavelet denoising step generalizes to non-Gaussian noise, and whether the masked NLL loss can be replaced by self-supervised alternatives that eliminate the need for any anomaly mask during training.
