## Problem

Generative models of 3D anatomical segmentations face a fundamental trade-off: parametric primitives offer geometric control but lack anatomical realism, while data-driven generative models produce realistic anatomy but cannot independently or compositionally control interpretable geometric attributes (size, shape, position) of specific substructures without retraining. This limits their utility for biophysical simulations where millimeter-scale geometric variations drive physiological outcomes and where clinicians need to constrain multiple substructures simultaneously.

## Input / Output

At inference time, the method takes target geometric moments—mass (volume fraction), centroid (3D position), and scale-normalized covariance (aspect ratio and orientation)—specified per substructure as ellipsoidal primitives, and optionally an existing 3D label map for inpainting. The output is a 128³ voxel 3D multi-class label map (11 channels for cardiac: aorta, pulmonary artery, pulmonary veins, IVC, SVC, LA, RA, LV, RV, myocardium, background) at 2 mm isotropic resolution. Substructures can be individual tissue channels or Boolean unions of multiple channels, and the number of constrained substructures is arbitrary.

## Method

CardioComposer applies inference-time energy-based guidance to a pre-trained unconditional latent diffusion model (3D U-Net denoiser + VAE) without any retraining. At each denoising step, the noised latent is decoded to voxel-space logits, a softmax yields class probabilities, and differentiable geometric moment losses—zeroth-order (mass) for size, first-order (centroid) for position, and scale-normalized second-order (covariance) for shape—are computed per substructure against user-specified targets. The gradient of the weighted aggregate loss with respect to the noisy latent corrects the denoising prediction via a diffusion-posterior-sampling-style update (Eq. 3). Because each substructure's loss is computed independently on its own voxel mask, multiple constraints compose additively, enabling simultaneous control over an arbitrary number of anatomical components including non-convex, branching, and multi-tissue structures.

## Results

In the multi-part compositional generation study (Table 1), geometric guidance achieves FD of 34.6 and MMD of 9.40 (×10³) with zero constraints, compared to 1622 and 55.7 for the implicit conditional baseline, reflecting the baseline's severe degradation when all dropout channels are empty. With one constrained substructure, the proposed method reduces FD from 227 to 38.5 and MMD from 17.1 to 9.39. At three and six constrained substructures, both methods reach comparable quality (FD ≈ 30–36, MMD ≈ 8.1–9.2), with geometric guidance maintaining a slight edge on MMD and COV. Disentangled control experiments confirm that each individual loss (size, position, shape) improves its corresponding conditional-fidelity metric while leaving the others approximately unchanged.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the implicit conditional diffusion model (3D Gaussian heatmap conditioning with per-channel dropout) from Kadry et al. (ECCV 2025), retrained with six substructure channels. At zero and one constrained substructures, geometric guidance dramatically outperforms the baseline: FD drops from 1622→34.6 (0 constraints) and 227→38.5 (1 constraint), and MMD drops from 55.7→9.40 and 17.1→9.39, respectively. This gap arises because the dropout-trained conditional model treats the all-channels-empty configuration as the rarest training case, producing severely degraded anatomy. At three and six constrained substructures, the two methods are broadly comparable (FD within ~3–5 points, MMD within ~0.4–0.6), with geometric guidance achieving slightly better MMD (8.60 vs 9.21 at 3 substructures) and COV (0.58 vs 0.48), while the implicit baseline edges ahead on FD and Precision. The key practical advantage of geometric guidance is that it requires no retraining and handles any number of constrained substructures uniformly, whereas the conditional baseline must be retrained for each specific set of substructures and degrades when fewer than all channels are active.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion posterior sampling framework (Chung et al., 2023) from general inverse-problem guidance to anatomical geometry control, and builds directly on the VAE-LDM architecture and tissue-based inpainting pipeline from Kadry et al. (2024). Prior controllable anatomical generation work (Kadry et al., 2025; de Wilde et al., 2025) relies on conditional training restricted to size-related scalars, while spatial-control methods for images and 3D shapes (Hertz et al., 2022; Epstein et al., 2023) either require retraining for new constraints or offer only coarse localization. CardioComposer departs from both lines by introducing differentiable voxel-wise geometric moment losses (zeroth, first, and scale-normalized second-order) that enable inference-time, compositional, multi-substructure control of an unconditional diffusion model without any architectural modification or retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, retraining-free interface for clinicians and simulation engineers to specify anatomical geometry via interpretable ellipsoidal primitives, directly addressing the controllability-realism trade-off that has limited generative models in biophysical simulation. The demonstrated transferability of loss weightings across cardiac, aortic, spinal, and knee anatomies suggests broad applicability, though the method remains limited to label-class-defined substructures and cannot enforce topological correctness (e.g., connected aortas). Open questions include whether the approach can handle arbitrary substructure definitions (cross-sections, local features), whether topological constraints can be incorporated as additional differentiable losses, and whether the guidance degrades gracefully for rare or pathological anatomies outside the training distribution.
