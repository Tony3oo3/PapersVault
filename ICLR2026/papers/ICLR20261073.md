## Problem

Training LLMs to proactively seek information in multi-turn medical dialogues is difficult because the interaction is inherently uncertain and long-horizon. Existing RL methods fail in this setting: GRPO struggles with credit assignment over many turns, and PPO suffers from unstable value estimation when the environment (user responses) is stochastic. The paper addresses this by formulating multi-turn dialogue as a Hierarchical MDP and designing an uncertainty-aware tree search that adaptively allocates exploration budget where it matters most.

## Input / Output

The assistant agent receives an incomplete clinical case consisting of a brief initial patient context, a multiple-choice medical question, and a set of answer options, then iteratively exchanges turns with a user simulator that is strictly constrained to answer only from a predefined set of atomic facts (refusing out-of-scope queries). The agent must produce a sequence of clarifying questions followed by a single final answer letter, with the dialogue bounded by a maximum of 8 turns. The reward is sparse and based solely on final-answer correctness (+3 correct, 0 incorrect, −1 invalid format).

## Method

ATPO models multi-turn dialogue as a Hierarchical MDP where each macro-action is a full assistant turn, and performs an adaptive tree search over dialogue states. At each frontier node, it samples N candidate turns, computes a composite uncertainty score combining Bellman error (U₁, measuring critic inaccuracy) and action-value variance (U₂, capturing both epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty), and either fully expands all N branches (if U > τ) or prunes to a single randomly selected branch. The resulting tree is decomposed into root-to-leaf trajectories for a PPO-style clipped policy update with turn-level advantages uniformly distributed across tokens, plus a critic trained on backward-pass value targets. Two efficiency mechanisms reduce cost: shared-prefix KV cache reuse across sibling nodes (saving (N−1) prefill computations per expansion) and an asynchronous execution pipeline that overlaps answer generation, user-simulator interaction, and critic value estimation, achieving up to 2,500 tokens/sec/GPU.

## Results

On three multi-turn medical dialogue benchmarks (MedicalExam, MedQA, MedMCQA), ATPO(U₁+U₂) with Qwen3-8B achieves 65.87%, 64.07%, and 53.66% final-answer accuracy, respectively, surpassing GPT-4o (MEDIQ prompting) on MedQA by +0.92% and on MedicalExam by +1.87%. Across all three model sizes (1.7B, 4B, 8B), ATPO consistently outperforms the strongest RL baseline TreePO, with absolute gains on MedQA of +0.82%, +1.73%, and +2.26% for 1.7B, 4B, and 8B respectively. ATPO also achieves its target accuracy using only ~55% of the training turns required by TreePO, demonstrating substantially higher sample efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ATPO(U₁+U₂) with Qwen3-8B surpasses GPT-4o (MEDIQ prompting) on MedQA (64.07% vs. 63.15%, +0.92%) and MedicalExam (65.87% vs. 64.00%, +1.87%), while remaining competitive on MedMCQA (53.66% vs. 53.03%). Against the strongest RL baseline TreePO, ATPO wins on MedQA across all model sizes (+0.82% to +2.26%) and on MedicalExam for 4B and 8B, though TreePO retains a slight edge on MedMCQA for 8B (54.74% vs. 53.66%). GRPO is consistently outperformed by ATPO by 4–6 percentage points on MedQA, confirming that trajectory-level credit assignment is insufficient for multi-turn dialogue. PPO (both MDP and H-MDP variants) lags ATPO by 5–7 points on MedQA, with the hierarchical formulation providing only marginal gains over the flat MDP version. The MEDIQ prompting baseline and DFT/SFT baselines are substantially weaker, with prompting actually degrading performance below the Direct single-turn setting in most configurations. Gemini-2.5-Pro (MEDIQ) remains the strongest overall performer (68.69% on MedQA), indicating that model scale still matters, but ATPO narrows the gap to within ~4.6 points using an 8B model.

## Relation to Prior Work

ATPO sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) tree-based RL for LLMs (TreePO, VinePPO, SPO, TreeRL), which previously operated at the token or segment level for single-turn reasoning, and (2) multi-turn medical dialogue RL (DoctorAgent-RL, ProMed, SCF), which used fixed or unstructured exploration. The key departure is formulating multi-turn dialogue as a Hierarchical MDP (following ArCHer) and performing adaptive tree expansion at the *turn* level, guided by a composite uncertainty metric (Bellman error + Q-value variance) rather than token entropy or a fixed branching factor. This makes the exploration budget allocation principled and state-dependent, in contrast to TreePO's uniform binary tree or GRPO's flat trajectory-level advantage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that uncertainty-aware adaptive tree search can substantially improve sample efficiency (≈55% of the training turns of TreePO for comparable accuracy) while achieving the best accuracy across three medical benchmarks, with Qwen3-8B narrowly surpassing GPT-4o on MedQA. The KV-cache-reuse and asynchronous architecture make tree-based RL practically deployable, addressing a key bottleneck for agentic RL training. Open questions include whether the fixed-threshold expansion rule can be replaced by a learnable policy, whether finer credit assignment within the H-MDP (beyond uniform token-level advantage cloning) yields further gains, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the medical domain to broader multi-turn agentic tasks. A limitation is that the reward is solely final-answer correctness, which may not capture the quality of intermediate questioning.
