## Problem

Existing zero-shot HOI detection methods tightly couple interaction recognition (IR) with a specific object detector and rely on coarse-grained CLIP features, making it impossible to improve IR independently or swap detectors without retraining. This coupling limits generalization to unseen human-object interactions and prevents the framework from benefiting from advances in open-vocabulary detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261105/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image plus detection results (bounding boxes and category labels) from any off-the-shelf detector (e.g., DETR, Grounding-DINO, YOLO-World) without requiring retraining. For each human-object pair, it outputs a multi-label confidence score over a candidate interaction list specific to the detected object category, with the final score modulated by the detector's own confidence and an interactiveness filter. The framework is fully plug-and-play: changing the detector at inference time requires no architectural or training modifications.

## Method

The paper proposes a decoupled framework that formulates IR as a visual question answering task over an MLLM (Qwen 2.5-VL 3B), replacing open-ended text generation with a deterministic generation strategy that computes conditional likelihoods of candidate interactions as confidence scores, enabling training-free multi-label prediction. A spatial-aware pooling (SAP) module replaces naive ROIAlign by merging human and object features through an MLP, aggregating context beyond bounding boxes via cross-attention, and encoding pairwise spatial cues (areas, aspect ratios, IoU, relative direction) into the interaction representation. A one-pass deterministic matching method appends special tokens after each candidate interaction in the prompt and uses cosine similarity between token outputs and the interaction feature to score all candidates in a single LLM forward pass. Training proceeds in two stages: first, SAP is trained with binary focal loss to filter non-interactive pairs; second, the LLM is fine-tuned with LoRA on the matching objective while the visual encoder remains frozen.

## Results

On HICO-DET under zero-shot settings, the proposed method achieves 42.59% average mAP (AvgFull), surpassing the strongest MLLM-based baseline BC-HOI (37.87%) by 4.72% and the best CLIP-based method LAIN (33.76%) by 8.83%. In the cross-dataset setting (trained on HICO-DET, tested on V-COCO), the method reaches 59.91% mAP_role^#2, outperforming the best prior method BCOM (48.87%) by 11.04%. In the training-free setting, the method achieves 31.50% mAP without any fine-tuning, already exceeding the trained ADA-CM (25.19%). Swapping the detector to Grounding-DINO further boosts AvgFull to 44.00% without retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 10 baselines across every zero-shot setting on HICO-DET. Against the strongest MLLM-based competitor BC-HOI (37.87% AvgFull), the gain is +4.72%, with particularly large margins on NF-UC (+3.93%) and UO (+9.42%). Compared to the best CLIP-based methods LAIN (33.76%) and EZ-HOI (36.20%), the improvements are +8.83% and +6.39% respectively. The two-stage methods ADA-CM and BCOM, which do not report UO/UV splits, still trail by over 10% on the settings they do report. In the cross-dataset transfer to V-COCO, the method achieves 59.91% mAP_role^#2, beating BCOM (48.87%) by 11.04% and CMMP (47.65%) by 12.26%. The detector-agnostic property is validated by swapping to Grounding-DINO (+1.41% AvgFull) or Yolo-World (+1.09%) without retraining, whereas ADA-CM and BCOM degrade significantly under the same detector swap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in zero-shot HOI detection, where interaction recognition (IR) is tightly coupled to a specific object detector and relies on CLIP-based vision-language features (ADA-CM, BCOM, CMMP, LAIN). It builds on the pairwise spatial vector formulation from UPT (Zhang et al., 2022a) but repurposes it within a spatial-aware pooling module that feeds an MLLM rather than a task-specific classifier. The key conceptual shift is formulating IR as a deterministic visual question answering task over an MLLM (Qwen 2.5-VL), which prior MLLM-based HOI works (UniHOI, BC-HOI, CMD-SE) had not achieved while still depending on CLIP embeddings and detector-specific features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The detector-agnostic decoupling is practically significant: practitioners can upgrade the object detector (e.g., from DETR to Grounding-DINO) without retraining the IR module, directly benefiting from ongoing progress in open-vocabulary detection. The 12.26% cross-dataset mAP improvement over the prior best (CMMP) and the competitive training-free result (31.50% vs. 25.19% for ADA-CM) suggest MLLMs can substitute for CLIP in relational recognition tasks more broadly. Open questions include whether the approach scales to denser scenes with many human-object pairs, whether larger MLLMs yield further gains, and whether the deterministic matching formulation transfers to other compositional recognition tasks beyond HOI.
