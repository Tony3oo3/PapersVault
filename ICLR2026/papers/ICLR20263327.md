## Problem

VGGT, the state-of-the-art feed-forward 3D reconstruction transformer, suffers from prohibitive computational cost and error accumulation when processing long image sequences, as its Global Attention layer scales quadratically with token count and amplifies minor inaccuracies across frames. The paper identifies a 'token collapse' phenomenon in VGGT's global attention—where many tokens attend to nearly identical regions—revealing substantial redundancy that existing 2D token-merging strategies fail to exploit without severe quality degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263327/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of RGB images (long side resized to 518) and produces camera intrinsics/extrinsics, dense depth maps, 3D point maps, and 2D point tracks in a single forward pass. Each image is tokenized into 1,041 tokens (1 camera token, 4 register tokens, and 1,036 patch tokens from a 28×37 grid). The approach is entirely training-free, modifying only the inference pipeline, and extends VGGT's practical input limit from ~300 frames (where it OOMs) to over 1,000 frames via a VRAM-optimized variant (VGGT*) that discards unused intermediate block outputs.

## Method

FastVGGT applies a tailored, three-part token partitioning strategy to VGGT's Global Attention layers: (1) all tokens from the first frame are designated as non-mergeable destination (dst) tokens to preserve the global coordinate reference; (2) 10% of tokens per frame are retained as salient tokens via fixed-stride sampling, bypassing merging entirely to maintain fine-grained correspondences; (3) remaining tokens are split into src and dst sets using region-based random sampling within each frame's 2D patch grid to ensure spatially balanced coverage. Source tokens are merged into their most cosine-similar dst tokens via averaging before global attention, then unmerged (replicated back) afterward to restore full token resolution for dense decoding. Frame Attention operates on all tokens without merging. The key insight is that VGGT's token collapse creates far more redundancy than 2D ViTs, enabling an aggressive 0.9 merging ratio from block 0 without quality loss—whereas 2D ToMe degrades noticeably beyond 0.3—yielding a 4× speedup on 1,000-image sequences while simultaneously reducing error accumulation.

## Results

On ScanNet-50 with 1000 input images, FastVGGT achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.425 in 180.7 s, compared to VGGT*'s 0.471 in 724.6 s — a 4× speedup while simultaneously improving reconstruction quality. At 500 frames, FastVGGT reaches CD 0.411 in 55.2 s versus VGGT*'s 0.420 in 177.5 s, and at 300 frames it matches VGGT*'s CD (0.416) in just 23.8 s (5.5× faster). For camera pose estimation on 1000 frames, FastVGGT reduces ATE from 0.196 to 0.164 and RPE-rot from 0.997 to 0.667, demonstrating that token merging mitigates error accumulation in long sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VGGT* (the VRAM-optimized baseline), FastVGGT wins on both speed and accuracy for sequences ≥ 300 frames: at 1000 frames it cuts inference time from 724.6 s to 180.7 s (4×) while improving CD from 0.471 to 0.425, and at 500 frames it reduces time from 177.5 s to 55.2 s with CD improving from 0.420 to 0.411. At 100 frames the CD is marginally worse (0.426 vs 0.423) but time drops from 9.1 s to 5.4 s. π³ and StreamVGGT are infeasible at all tested lengths (OOM even at 100 frames), making direct comparison impossible. Fast3R and CUT3R can process long sequences but suffer severe quality degradation: at 1000 frames their CD values (0.684 and 0.786) are 60–85% worse than FastVGGT's 0.425, despite CUT3R being faster (34.8 s). On 7 Scenes and NRGBD, FastVGGT matches or slightly exceeds VGGT* on accuracy/completeness/normal-consistency while being 2.7–2.6× faster (e.g., 28.0 s vs 76.7 s on 7 Scenes Stride-3; 53.1 s vs 136.1 s on NRGBD Stride-3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VGGT, a 1.2B-parameter feed-forward transformer for 3D scene reconstruction, and adapts the training-free token merging paradigm (ToMe, ToMeSD) originally developed for 2D vision tasks to the 3D geometry setting. The key departure is that naive application of existing merging strategies (ToMe, PiToMe) severely degrades VGGT's reconstruction quality, motivating a specialized three-part partitioning strategy that preserves first-frame reference tokens, retains salient tokens, and uses region-based random sampling. The work also connects to the broader DUSt3R lineage of feed-forward 3D reconstruction and to the DINOv3 literature on feature degradation, drawing a conceptual parallel between token collapse in self-supervised 2D models and the redundancy observed in VGGT's global attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FastVGGT provides a practical, training-free path to scaling VGGT from ~300 to 1000+ frames (where the original OOMs), achieving 4× speedup while simultaneously reducing error accumulation on long sequences—a dual benefit that prior acceleration methods (e.g., VGGT-Long) sacrifice for speed. The token collapse diagnosis and the finding that VGGT tolerates a 0.9 merging ratio (vs. 0.3 for 2D ToMeSD) suggest that cross-view geometry transformers have far more exploitable redundancy than 2D models, which could inform efficiency research for other 3D transformer architectures. Open questions include adaptive merging for short sequences, extension to follow-up architectures like π³, long-horizon drift in very long sequences, and handling dynamic scenes.
