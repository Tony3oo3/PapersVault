## Problem

Two-stage LiDAR 3D object detectors suffer from degraded proposal quality due to two root causes: (1) geometric detail loss in proposal features caused by high sparsity and uneven point distribution, where pooling operations filter out high-frequency surface and edge information, and (2) the inability to exploit contextual cues from spatially adjacent and semantically correlated proposals during independent refinement, which is especially problematic under occlusion where an object's points are split across multiple proposals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262881/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw LiDAR point cloud (each point carrying 3D coordinates plus intensity and timestamp features) as input, converts it to a regular voxel grid, and produces 3D oriented bounding boxes (center, dimensions, yaw angle) with per-class confidence scores for vehicles, pedestrians, and cyclists. The pipeline operates on single-frame or multi-frame (up to 4 frames) point clouds, with proposals represented as g×g×g grid tokens internally and final detections emitted via a feed-forward head after collaborative refinement.

## Method

PTN introduces two modules on top of a Voxel R-CNN backbone. The Hierarchical Attentive Feature Alignment (HAFA) module uses a dual-stream design: a coarse-grained stream extracts multi-scale voxel features at grid points via trilinear interpolation and a Transformer encoder, while a fine-grained stream retrieves unsampled foreground points, encodes their geometric relation to the proposal via Euclidean distances to six bounding-box surfaces, and aggregates them with max-pooling; a convolutional alignment layer then fuses both streams. The Collaborative Proposal Refinement Module (CPRM) generates hybrid object queries by combining top-K RPN proposals (with an adaptive K estimated from the classification heatmap) and learnable random queries placed on a uniform BEV grid, then applies a 3D Deformable Box Attention (DBA) decoder that divides each proposal into G² grid points, learns per-grid sampling offsets to pull features from neighboring proposals, and fuses them with learned attention weights—enabling cross-proposal context aggregation without full dense cross-attention.

## Results

On the Waymo Open dataset (single-frame, L2), PTN achieves 73.5/71.2 mAP/mAPH, surpassing the strongest prior method DSVT (73.2/71.0) and the two-stage PV-RCNN++ (71.0/64.9) by a clear margin. On KITTI (test set), PTN reaches 84.11 mAP for Car and 72.11 mAP for Cyclist, outperforming DPFusion (83.53/68.30) and Voxel R-CNN (81.86/64.79). On nuScenes, PTN with adaptive queries (Np=300, Nr=100) attains 47.81 NDS and 38.93 mAP, improving over the baseline (44.55/36.84) by 3.26 NDS and 2.09 mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PTN outperforms all 25 compared baselines on the Waymo L2 mAP/mAPH metric, with the largest gains over two-stage detectors: +2.5/+6.3 over PV-RCNN++ and +3.9/+4.0 over PV-RCNN. Against the strongest single-stage baseline DSVT (73.2/71.0), PTN edges ahead by 0.3/0.2 mAP/mAPH, with particularly large improvements on Pedestrian (+1.6 AP, +1.6 APH) and Cyclist (+1.4 AP, +1.2 APH) where sparse point distributions are most challenging. On KITTI test, PTN leads DPFusion by 0.58 mAP (Car) and 3.81 mAP (Cyclist), and surpasses Voxel R-CNN by 2.25 mAP (Car) and 7.32 mAP (Cyclist). On nuScenes, the adaptive query mechanism (Np=300, Nr=100) yields 47.81 NDS, a 3.26-point improvement over the no-query baseline (44.55), confirming that the hybrid proposal+random query design generalizes across datasets with different object densities.

## Relation to Prior Work

PTN is built directly on the Voxel R-CNN two-stage pipeline (RPN → ROI feature extraction → refinement), inheriting its voxel-based backbone and proposal generation. It departs from standard two-stage detectors by introducing a dual-stream HAFA module that combines coarse multi-scale voxel features with fine-grained raw point features, addressing the geometric detail loss that plagues conventional ROI pooling. The CPRM module adapts the DETR/Deformable-DETR query-and-attention paradigm to the 3D proposal setting, treating RPN proposals (plus learnable random queries) as object queries that interact via 3D parameter-guided deformable attention — a departure from both independent proposal refinement in prior two-stage methods and from the dense feature matching used in existing 3D DETR variants like TransFusion and ConQueR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two concrete failure modes of two-stage LiDAR detectors (sparse-point geometric degradation and lack of cross-proposal context) with well-motivated, modular solutions that are shown to be plug-and-play (validated on PV-RCNN and nuScenes). However, the SOTA margin on Waymo is narrow (73.5 vs. 73.2 mAP over DSVT), and the method still depends on RPN proposal quality for its top-K selection. Open questions include whether the adaptive K mechanism and random queries generalize to datasets with very different object densities, and whether the hierarchical feature alignment can be made more efficient for real-time deployment — the authors themselves note future work on learnable proposal quality with minimal cost.
