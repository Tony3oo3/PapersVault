## Problem

Existing retrieval and re-ranking methods are designed for straightforward semantic matching and struggle to identify documents whose connection to a task is indirect or implicit, requiring fine-grained reasoning to bridge subtle conceptual links. Moreover, current reasoning-enhanced IR approaches lack direct interpretable relevance measurement (providing only relative rankings), offer inflexible test-time scaling (single trajectory), and suffer from limited parallelism due to inherently sequential listwise or setwise processing.

## Input / Output

Given a query and a candidate document, the model is prompted with a task-specific relevance definition (plugged into a Relevance Placeholder) and a fixed 5-level scoring rubric (0–20 Irrelevant through 80–100 Highly Relevant). It produces a structured reasoning trajectory following three steps—query analysis, document analysis, and relevance annotation—concluding with an integer score in <score> tags. For test-time scaling, K independent trajectories are sampled per query-document pair and their scores are combined via weighted averaging (uniform or likelihood-weighted) to yield a single integrated relevance estimate.

## Method

Retro* is a pointwise LLM re-ranker built on two key designs: (1) a rubric-based relevance scoring mechanism that explicitly defines score intervals with interpretable meanings, enabling direct absolute relevance measurement rather than mere relative ordering; and (2) test-time scaling via score integration, where multiple reasoning trajectories are sampled and their scores averaged for more reliable estimates. Training follows a two-stage strategy: a warm-up SFT stage distills concise reasoning trajectories from a powerful teacher model (Qwen3-235B), with data filtered by selecting the trajectory whose score is closest to the integrated mean; followed by an RL stage using GRPO with a composite reward combining an intra-document reward (ternary signal rewarding trajectories whose scores are closest to the group mean, penalizing the furthest) and an inter-document reward (proportion of opposite-polarity trajectories whose scores are correctly dominated), weighted by α=0.75. The pointwise architecture enables massive document-level parallelism, avoiding the sequential bottleneck of listwise and setwise methods.

## Results

On the BRIGHT benchmark (nDCG@10, re-ranking top-100 from BGE-Reasoner-Embed), Retro* (7B) achieves an average of 36.6, outperforming the strongest baseline ReasonRank (7B) at 33.5 by 3.1 points. Scaling to 32B pushes the average to 38.5, surpassing ReasonRank (32B) at 36.6 by 1.9 points. With test-time scaling (mean-score@16), the 7B model reaches 38.7—exceeding its own 32B counterpart—and the 32B model reaches 40.6, establishing a new state-of-the-art. On the traditional BEIR benchmark, Retro* (7B) achieves 55.8 average nDCG@10 (56.8 with test-time scaling), outperforming all reasoning-enhanced baselines including ReasonRank (32B) at 55.4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Retro* (7B) at 36.6 nDCG@10 outperforms all 7B-scale baselines: ReasonRank (7B, listwise) at 33.5, Rank-R1 (7B, setwise) at 25.8, Rank1 (7B, pointwise) at 25.9, RankLLaMA (7B, pointwise) at 19.7, and JudgeRank (7B, pointwise) at 12.7. At the 32B scale, Retro* (32B) at 38.5 surpasses ReasonRank (32B) at 36.6 and Rank1 (32B) at 29.7. Notably, test-time scaling (mean-score@16) lifts the 7B model to 38.7, exceeding even the unscaled 32B model (38.5) and all baselines. On BEIR, Retro* (7B) at 55.8 beats ReasonRank (32B) at 55.4, Rank-R1 (14B) at 54.6, and RankZephyr (7B) at 54.1. On R2MED (out-of-domain biomedical), Retro* (32B) at 44.5 outperforms ReasonRank (32B) at 42.9 and Rank1 (32B) at 39.1, with test-time scaling pushing it to 46.2. Retro* also demonstrates superior parallelism: its pointwise design scales linearly with candidate count, while setwise Rank-R1 and listwise ReasonRank suffer steep latency increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of reasoning-enhanced re-ranking methods (JudgeRank, Rank1, Rank-R1, ReasonRank) by introducing a rubric-based pointwise scoring mechanism that produces interpretable absolute relevance scores rather than relative rankings. It departs from prior listwise and setwise approaches (RankZephyr, Rank-R1, ReasonRank) by adopting a pointwise architecture that enables massive document-level parallelism, and from prior RL-based re-rankers by designing a composite reward combining intra-document scoring stability with inter-document ranking discrimination. The score integration strategy for test-time scaling repurposes the self-consistency idea from reasoning LLMs but adapts it to continuous scores via weighted averaging rather than majority voting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The rubric-based scoring provides a practical capability—interpretable absolute relevance measurement—that existing re-rankers lack, which is important for downstream RAG pipelines that need to threshold or filter documents rather than merely reorder them. The pointwise architecture's parallelism advantage makes reasoning-enhanced re-ranking feasible at scale in production settings where listwise/setwise methods suffer prohibitive latency. Open questions include how the rubric generalizes to truly novel domains beyond the scientific/mathematical/programming focus, whether the synthetic training data (BGE-Reasoner-Data) limits robustness, and how the approach performs when the first-stage retriever is weaker than the SOTA BGE-Reasoner-Embed used in evaluation.
