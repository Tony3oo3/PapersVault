## Problem

Event-based scene understanding has been largely confined to closed-set semantic segmentation, with no existing framework capable of open-vocabulary instance-level segmentation on event streams. Prior works either perform class-agnostic instance segmentation (EventSAM) or open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (OpenESS), but none jointly support free-form language queries at both instance-level and part-level granularity for events. Additionally, no benchmarks exist to evaluate such capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264671/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SEAL takes asynchronous event streams (pixel coordinates, microsecond timestamps, polarity) converted into regular representations, along with user-provided visual prompts (points or boxes) and free-form language queries (noun-level or sentence-level). Events and images are resized to 512×512. The model outputs binary instance/part segmentation masks paired with open-vocabulary class predictions, operating at 22.28 ms inference time with only 99.1M parameters. A prompt-free variant (SEAL++) is also provided in the appendix for generic spatiotemporal OV-EIS without user interaction.

## Method

SEAL builds a lightweight Multimodal Fusion Network on top of a frozen pretrained EventSAM backbone (ViT-B) and SAM mask decoder, which serves as the class-agnostic mask generator. The key supervision signal comes from Multimodal Hierarchical Semantic Guidance (MHSG), which uses SAM to generate masks at three granularity levels (semantic, instance, part) on paired images, pools CLIP features for visual guidance, and leverages an MLLM (OSprey) to generate rich captions for text guidance—enabling annotation-free training with diverse vocabulary. The fusion network comprises three components: a Backbone Feature Enhancer (6 layers of self-attention, cross-attention with text guidance, and FFN) that aligns event features with the CLIP multimodal space; a Spatial Encoding module that injects SAM mask-decoder tokens (encoding shape and position) into pooled mask features to resolve dead masks and semantic conflicts at low resolution; and a Mask Feature Enhancer that applies masked cross-attention to refine mask features. Training uses a two-stage procedure: first training EventSAM on 24K event-image pairs, then training the fusion network with a cosine-similarity distillation loss that aligns event mask features with both image-domain and text-domain MHSG guidance at all three semantic levels.

## Results

On the three closed-set event instance segmentation benchmarks, SEAL achieves the best AP across all settings: 32.3 (Point) / 38.2 (Box) on DDD17-Ins, 22.4 / 28.8 on DSEC11-Ins, and 11.7 / 14.8 on DSEC19-Ins. On the part segmentation benchmark (DSEC-Part), SEAL reaches 13.6 AP (Point) and 18.3 AP (Box), outperforming the best baseline VLPart by 0.7 and 2.2 AP respectively. SEAL also achieves the fastest inference time (22.28 ms) and the smallest parameter count (99.1M) among all compared methods, roughly 3–15× fewer parameters than image-pretrained baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEAL outperforms all eight baselines on every benchmark and prompt type. Against the strongest competitor MaskCLIP++ (Feature Pooling Baseline), SEAL gains +5.4 AP on DDD17-Ins (Box), +3.4 AP on DSEC11-Ins (Box), and +0.7 AP on DSEC19-Ins (Box); the gap narrows on the fine-grained DSEC19-Ins because image-pretrained models benefit from large-scale annotated training data. The AR-CDG baseline OVSAM performs poorly (21.6 AP on DDD17-Ins Box) due to the domain gap between reconstructed event frames and natural images. The AF-DA event-backbone baselines (frame2recon, frame2voxel, frame2spike) remain competitive in efficiency but lag SEAL by 3–5 AP because they lack text-domain guidance and explicit spatial priors. On the part segmentation benchmark (Table 2), SEAL surpasses VLPart by +0.7 AP (Point) and +2.2 AP (Box), confirming that hierarchical semantic guidance benefits fine-grained part-level recognition. In terms of efficiency, SEAL's single-backbone design yields 99.1M parameters and 22.28 ms inference, compared to 228–530M parameters and 88–575 ms for all other baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEAL sits at the intersection of two recent lines of event-vision work: EventSAM (Chen et al., 2024), which adapted SAM to event streams for class-agnostic instance segmentation, and OpenESS (Kong et al., 2024), which achieved open-vocabulary event *semantic* segmentation by distilling CLIP features into an event backbone. Neither prior work combined open-vocabulary recognition with instance-level (or part-level) segmentation for events. SEAL unifies these by building a lightweight multimodal fusion network on top of a frozen EventSAM backbone, guided by a novel hierarchical semantic guidance (MHSG) module that exploits SAM's multi-granularity mask generation, CLIP's vision-language alignment, and an MLLM (OSprey) for rich caption-based text supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By defining OV-EIS and providing four benchmarks spanning label granularity (5→14 classes) and semantic granularity (instance→part), the paper gives the event-vision community a structured evaluation protocol that was previously absent. The parameter-efficient single-backbone design (99M params, 22 ms inference) is practically important for edge deployment of event cameras, which are often power-constrained. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond driving scenes, whether the prompt-free SEAL++ variant (Appendix) can match prompted performance, and whether the narrowing performance gap on fine-grained classes (DSEC19-Ins) signals a fundamental limitation of annotation-free training versus large-scale image pretraining.
