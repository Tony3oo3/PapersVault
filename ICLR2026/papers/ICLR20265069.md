## Problem

Locate-then-edit knowledge editing methods update MLP weights by redirecting a key-to-value mapping, but the key and value vectors are computed without constraints that restrict them to edit-relevant features. While AlphaEdit constrains the update matrix Δ to a null space, the high degrees of freedom in the key and residual vectors themselves still cause significant unintended perturbation to unrelated knowledge. SUIT addresses this gap by identifying and restricting edits to the specific subspaces that encode entity-specific and attribute-critical features.

## Input / Output

The method takes a factual triple (entity, relation, new attribute) and the weights of a pre-trained language model. Before editing, it precomputes an entity-agnostic subspace by extracting key vectors from 10,000 PARAREL entities and performing SVD on the resulting matrix; this subspace is fixed across all edits. At edit time, the key vector is extracted as the average MLP up-projection activation at the entity's last token position across multiple prefix contexts, and the residual vector is optimized via gradient descent on the final edited layer's residual stream. The output is a closed-form weight update Δ added to the MLP down-projection matrix W, producing the edited model.

## Method

SUIT introduces two subspace-aware computations that replace the unconstrained key and residual vectors used by prior methods. First, for the key vector, it decomposes k into an entity-specific component (in subspace K_s, identified as the low-variance directions across entities) and an entity-agnostic component (in K_s^⊥, the top m singular vectors capturing τ_energy fraction of total variance from PARAREL key vectors), then removes the entity-agnostic component to obtain k'. Second, for the residual vector, instead of optimizing a full-dimensional δ, it restricts the update to a 2D subspace spanned by two learned unit directions w1 and w2, where w1 increases the logit of the new attribute and w2 decreases the logit of the old attribute; the update simply swaps the magnitudes of the residual stream along these two directions. The directions are found by maximizing the new-attribute logit with a directional penalty λ(w1ᵀw2)² encouraging orthogonality. Finally, SUIT plugs k' and δ' into AlphaEdit's closed-form formula to compute the weight update Δ, inheriting its null-space preservation constraint while operating on the more precisely specified vectors.

## Results

On COUNTERFACT (1,000 sequential edits, batch size 100), SUIT achieves the highest harmonic mean score S across all three models: 86.8 on LLaMA3, 84.7 on GPT-J, and 86.1 on Qwen2.5, while maintaining near-perfect Efficacy (99.2–99.7%) and strong Generalization (90.3–94.6%). The headline gain is in Specificity: SUIT reaches 74.2% on LLaMA3, 67.7% on GPT-J, and 72.3% on Qwen2.5, compared to 31.0%, 49.0%, and 43.4% for the strongest baseline AlphaEdit. General Capability (GC) is also well preserved, with SUIT scoring 63.0 on LLaMA3 (vs. 63.4 pre-edit) and 30.8 on Qwen2.5 (vs. 28.9 pre-edit). On ZSRE, SUIT similarly leads in Specificity (e.g., 51.9% at ℓ=1 on LLaMA3 vs. 31.2% for AlphaEdit) while matching or exceeding baselines on Efficacy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SUIT outperforms all nine baselines on the harmonic mean score S across every model and batch size. Against the strongest baseline AlphaEdit, SUIT improves Specificity by 43.2 points on LLaMA3 (74.2 vs. 31.0), 18.7 points on GPT-J (67.7 vs. 49.0), and 28.9 points on Qwen2.5 (72.3 vs. 43.4), while maintaining comparable or higher Efficacy and Generalization. MEMIT and PMET, the other locate-then-edit baselines, show substantially lower Specificity (28.2% and 22.6% on LLaMA3) and degrade further at smaller batch sizes, where MEMIT collapses to 0% S at batch size 1 on LLaMA3. Fine-tuning (FT-L) and MEND are far weaker, with FT-L achieving only 1.9% S on LLaMA3 and MEND scoring 0.0% across all settings. Among the additional baselines (ROME, RECT, PRUNE, NSE), RECT is the closest competitor on GPT-J (S=66.8 vs. SUIT's 84.7) but still trails by ~18 points; ROME and PRUNE show poor sequential editing robustness, while NSE preserves Specificity well (99.5% on Qwen2.5) but fails to achieve any editing efficacy (0.0% Eff.).

## Relation to Prior Work

SUIT builds directly on the locate-then-edit paradigm established by ROME and MEMIT, and specifically adopts AlphaEdit's closed-form update formula for the weight perturbation matrix Δ. Its key departure is that prior methods (including AlphaEdit) constrain the update matrix but leave the computation of the key vector k and residual vector δ unconstrained; SUIT instead applies the Linear Representation Hypothesis to decompose these vectors into edit-relevant and edit-irrelevant subspaces, restricting the edit to the former. The SVD-based identification of an entity-agnostic subspace from PARAREL and the two-directional (w1, w2) residual update are novel contributions that repurpose representation-geometry ideas from interpretability research for the editing setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic specificity gains (e.g., 74.2 vs. 31.0 on LLaMA3) over the strongest baseline AlphaEdit, without sacrificing editing efficacy, suggest that subspace-aware vector computation is a critical missing ingredient in locate-then-edit methods. Open questions include whether the entity-specific/agnostic decomposition generalizes beyond the MLP down-projection layer, whether the w1/w2 roles can be fully disentangled (the paper notes cross-effects), and whether the approach extends to larger models or non-Transformer architectures. The preprocessing cost of computing SVD over 10,000 PARAREL entities is a practical consideration for deployment, though it is a one-time cost per model.
