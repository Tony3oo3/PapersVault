## Problem

Despite the importance of semantic associations (e.g., 'bird'→'flew', 'country'→'capital') for language modeling, there is no principled account of how these associations emerge during training of attention-based transformers on natural language data. Prior theoretical analyses relied on unrealistic assumptions—synthetic structured language, architectures lacking positional encodings or residual connections, and non-standard training procedures—creating a significant gap between theory and practical LLMs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language text corpus (e.g., TinyStories, OpenWebText, FineWeb) and an L-layer attention-based transformer with relative positional encodings, causal masking, and residual streams, trained via standard full-batch gradient descent with constant learning rate η. It produces closed-form Frobenius-norm bounds showing each weight matrix (output W_O, value V^(l), query-key W^(l), positional encoding P^(l)) equals a specific composition of three corpus-derived basis functions up to an explicit error term, valid for s ≤ η⁻¹·min(5/(8√T), 1/(12L)) steps. The basis functions are computed directly from corpus statistics: token frequencies, conditional next-token distributions, and prefix co-occurrence probabilities.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a leading-term gradient expansion that yields closed-form weight characterizations at early training stages, when the leading term dominates before higher-order corrections accumulate. The theorem (Theorem 4.1) shows that under sufficiently small Gaussian or zero initialization, all layers share the same weight structure: W_O ≈ sηB̄ (bigram mapping), V^(l) ≈ C(s,2)η²Φ̄ᵀB̄ᵀ (context×bigram composition), W^(l) ≈ (3C(s,4)+2C(s,3))η⁴Q̄ (interchangeability×context composition), and P^(l) ≈ (3C(s,4)+2C(s,3))η⁴Δ (positional analog of Q̄). The three basis functions are: bigram mapping B̄ (frequency-weighted next-token probability minus centering), interchangeability mapping Σ_B̄ = B̄ᵀB̄ (correlation of previous-token distributions capturing functional similarity), and context mapping Φ̄ (average prefix co-occurrence probability). The Q̄ matrix is constructed in four steps: composing Σ_B̄Φ̄, scoring input-output token pairs, applying causal masking and centering, then shifting scores to the preceding token position and averaging across the dataset. Empirical validation on a 3-layer TinyStories model achieves cosine similarity >0.99 with theoretical predictions, and analysis of Pythia-1.4B checkpoints on OpenWebText confirms the features persist in real LLMs with MLP and multi-head attention.

## Results

On a 3-layer attention-based transformer trained on TinyStories (3,000-token vocabulary, T=200), the minimum cosine similarity between theoretically predicted leading-term weights and actually learned weights across all 100 epochs is 0.999496 (attention), 0.999169 (value), and 0.998486 (output), confirming the closed-form characterizations in Theorem 4.1. A causal intervention removing the leading-term projection from each weight matrix shows the output matrix has the largest impact on loss (5.349 → 8.287), followed by value matrices (→ ~6.5), while attention weights have minimal effect (→ ~5.36), consistent with their different gradient orders. On Pythia-1.4B trained on OpenWebText, cosine similarity between layer-wise embedding/attention covariance matrices and the theoretical leading-term features remains high in early training across all layers, gradually drifting as the model learns richer knowledge beyond associations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing methods in the traditional sense; it is a theoretical analysis that validates its own closed-form weight characterizations against empirically learned weights. The key validation is that the gradient leading-term predictions (compositions of bigram, interchangeability, and context mappings) match the actual learned weights with cosine similarity ≥ 0.998 across all 100 epochs for the toy model, and remain informative (cosine > 0.7) even after 100 epochs when loss has dropped from 8.00 to 5.35. The causal intervention (Table 3) confirms the theoretical ordering of gradient magnitudes: removing the output leading term degrades loss most (5.349→8.287), value terms moderately (→~6.5), and attention terms minimally (→~5.36), matching the O(η), O(η²), and O(η⁴) orders predicted by Theorem 4.1. On Pythia-1.4B, the theory generalizes to multi-head attention and MLP architectures, with the MLP ablation suggesting the first-layer MLP functions similarly to the leading-term value mapping at early stages.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on transformer training dynamics (Nichani et al. 2024, Bietti et al. 2023, Tian et al. 2023, Li et al. 2023b) by grounding the analysis in natural language data, a realistic architecture with relative positional encodings and residual streams, and standard full-batch gradient descent. Prior works relied on synthetic structured language, simplified architectures lacking positional encoding or residual connections, or non-standard training procedures (sequential component-wise training, frozen weights), creating a gap between theory and practice. The key technical departure is the use of gradient leading-term expansions to derive closed-form weight expressions as compositions of three interpretable basis functions (bigram, interchangeability, and context mappings), rather than analyzing specific circuit behaviors like induction heads or topic clustering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first explicit closed-form characterization of transformer weights trained on natural language under next-token prediction, providing a mechanistic bridge between corpus statistics and learned representations. The finding that these early-stage features persist well beyond the theoretical regime (cosine similarity > 0.7 after 100 epochs in the toy model, and strong covariance alignment in Pythia-1.4B) suggests they serve as a foundational scaffold for later learning. Open questions include how these features evolve and specialize in deeper layers and with MLP/multi-head attention as training progresses, and whether the three basis functions remain the relevant decomposition at convergence or give way to more complex structures.
