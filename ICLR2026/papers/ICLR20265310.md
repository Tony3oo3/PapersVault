## Problem

Existing diffusion-based style transfer methods operate at the global level, applying style uniformly and overlooking region-wise and pixel-wise semantic correspondence between content and style images. The correspondence cues already encoded in pretrained diffusion U-Nets remain under-exploited, and even when alignment is attempted, methods either apply style globally (causing structural degradation) or build expensive external modules to learn correspondences from scratch, missing the alignment information inherent in the diffusion backbone itself.

## Input / Output

The method takes a content image and a reference style image (both RGB, processed at 512×512 resolution through the Stable Diffusion V1.4 VAE) and produces a single stylized RGB image. No text prompts, additional annotations, or training data are required; the entire pipeline is training-free and runs on a single GPU. Feature injection is applied starting from the 49th DDIM timestep out of 50, and the iterative refinement loop terminates adaptively based on Sobel and Gram loss thresholds rather than a fixed iteration count.

## Method

CoCoDiff introduces two key modules. First, a pixel-wise semantic correspondence module performs a 2D grid search over diffusion timesteps and U-Net layers to identify the feature configuration that best captures semantic structure (validated via PCK on SPair-71k), then constructs a dense alignment map by computing cosine similarity between normalized intermediate features at each spatial location. Second, a fitting cycle and iterative control module uses an indirect correspondence strategy—first transferring the style image toward the content's appearance to obtain an intermediate image I_sty_c, then matching between the original content and I_sty_c to improve alignment accuracy—before injecting attention values at corresponding positions during DDIM inversion. The iterative refinement is guided by a Sobel-based content loss (preserving edges) and a Gram-matrix style loss (preserving texture statistics), with AdaIN applied for tone harmonization, and an adaptive early-stopping criterion prevents over-stylization.

## Results

On the MS-COCO/WikiArt benchmark across 13 artistic styles, CoCoDiff achieves the best score on all four evaluation metrics: FID 18.432, LPIPS 0.549, ArtFID 30.100, and CFSD 0.609. The closest competitor is AdaIN on FID (18.672) and SCSA on CFSD (0.612), while the largest gaps appear against prompt-based methods SMS (FID 31.266, LPIPS 0.821) and InST (FID 21.541, LPIPS 0.785). A user study with 25 participants further confirms CoCoDiff's superiority, with 54.6% preference for style fidelity and 43.8% for content preservation, far exceeding the next-best method (StyleID at 21.2%/13.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoCoDiff outperforms all nine compared baselines on every metric. Among reference-guided methods, the closest competitors are AdaIN (FID 18.672, only 0.24 behind) and SCSA (CFSD 0.612, only 0.003 behind), indicating that simple statistical alignment and sparse-attention approaches capture some style-content balance but lack the fine-grained correspondence that CoCoDiff provides. StyTR² (FID 18.886) and AesPA-Net (LPIPS 0.556) are competitive on individual metrics but trail CoCoDiff on the others, reflecting their inability to simultaneously optimize structure and texture. The prompt-based methods SMS and FreeStyle perform substantially worse (FID 31.266 and 23.654 respectively), confirming that text prompts cannot adequately encode the specific style of a reference image. InST, despite also using diffusion inversion, shows the weakest content preservation (LPIPS 0.785, CFSD 0.881), highlighting the importance of the correspondence-guided injection mechanism. In the user study, CoCoDiff receives 49.2% average preference versus 17.4% for the next-best method (StyleID), a gap of over 2.8×.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoCoDiff sits at the intersection of two diffusion-model threads: (1) attention-based style injection in pretrained U-Nets, as pioneered by StyleID (Chung et al., CVPR 2024), which replaces content keys/values with style keys/values in self-attention blocks; and (2) emergent semantic correspondence from intermediate diffusion features, demonstrated by DIFT (Tang et al., 2023). The paper's key departure is to *combine* these two ideas: it mines DIFT-style features to build a pixel-wise correspondence map, then uses that map to *spatially gate* the StyleID-style attention injection, wrapping the whole process in a cycle-consistency loop with AdaIN tone harmonization. Unlike SMS (Jiang et al., ICCV 2025), which trains an external module to learn correspondences, CoCoDiff is entirely training-free and relies on the diffusion backbone's own representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is that a training-free pipeline can match or beat methods requiring supervised correspondence learning (SMS) or fine-tuning, which lowers the barrier for practitioners who want structure-aware stylization without additional data or compute. However, the approach's dependence on a per-model grid search over (timestep, layer) pairs and hand-tuned hyperparameters (γ, w, τ_c, τ_s) limits its plug-and-play generality; the SDXL results show that fine-grained alignment degrades at scale, suggesting the correspondence signal in larger models may be less reliable or require re-calibration. Open questions include whether the indirect correspondence strategy (first stylizing the style image to the content domain before matching) generalizes to highly dissimilar content–style pairs, and whether the cyclic refinement can be made more efficient than its current iterative loop.
