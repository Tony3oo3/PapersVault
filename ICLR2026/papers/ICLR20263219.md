## Problem

It is commonly believed that increasing RoPE's base frequency θ improves long-context performance, but it remains unclear whether this genuinely adds positional information or merely pushes many dimensions into a NoPE-like state that contributes little. The paper identifies a high-norm 'frequency band' in RoPE dimensions whose location is governed by θ and the training sequence length, and reveals a fundamental interpolation–extrapolation trade-off that contradicts the assumption that θ-scaling is universally beneficial for context extension.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text sequences (WikiText-103, C4) at inference lengths ranging from 512 to 25512 tokens and produces next-token predictions scored by perplexity. The analytical core examines the 2-norm of query and key vectors across RoPE's d/2 frequency-pair dimensions to locate the band, and the FMRoPE intervention modifies the rotation by setting θ equal to the training context length (e.g., θ=512 for L_train=512), optionally adjusting θ at inference to match the target sequence length. Downstream evaluation also includes zero-shot commonsense benchmarks (PIQA, HellaSwag, CommonsenseQA, Social IQa) and Needle-in-a-Haystack retrieval at context lengths up to 8192 tokens.

## Method

The paper first shows that a distinct frequency band—where high 2-norm values concentrate for all tokens—emerges systematically across Gemma, Llama-2/3, Qwen3, and Phi-3, and that replacing RoPE dimensions below this band with NoPE (p-RoPE) causes negligible perplexity change. It then derives a closed-form predictor for the band location by maximizing the coordinate variance of cos(mω) over the uniform training window, yielding an optimal angular frequency x* ≈ 3.657210 and a grid index j* ≈ (d/2)·log_θ(L_train/x*) that matches observed bands with a near-linear scaling (c ≈ 1.0–1.1). The key intervention, FMRoPE, sets θ = L_train during pre-training, shifting the band to the lowest-frequency dimensions and widening the effective frequency range available to the model. Controlled pre-training and YaRN fine-tuning experiments demonstrate a clear trade-off: FMRoPE improves extrapolation beyond the training length but degrades interpolation within it, while larger θ values (10,000–1,000,000) reverse this pattern. The band forms by epoch 6 of pre-training and persists unchanged through position interpolation methods (YaRN, LongRoPE, Llama-3 scaling), indicating it is an early-learned structural property rather than an artifact of context extension.

## Results

On WikiText-103 with L_train=512, FMRoPE (θ=512) dramatically outperforms standard RoPE at extrapolated lengths: at L=1512, FMRoPE (inference θ=1512) achieves PPL 19.09 versus 43.63 for RoPE (θ=10,000), and at L=3512, FMRoPE (inference θ=3512) achieves PPL 23.00 versus >100 for RoPE. With YaRN fine-tuning to L_ft=1512, FMRoPE+YaRN reaches PPL 20.51 at L=15512, compared to 52.59 for RoPE(θ=10,000)+YaRN. However, FMRoPE incurs a small interpolation cost at L=512 (PPL 19.58 vs. 19.35 for RoPE θ=500,000), confirming the interpolation–extrapolation trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard RoPE (θ=10,000/500,000/1,000,000), FMRoPE wins decisively on extrapolation: at L=3512 without fine-tuning, FMRoPE (inference θ=3512) achieves PPL 23.00 while all standard RoPE variants exceed 100; with YaRN fine-tuning, FMRoPE+YaRN reaches 20.51 at L=15512 versus 52.59 for RoPE(θ=10,000)+YaRN. FMRoPE also outperforms NoPE (PPL 21.32 at L=1512) and SPE (77.30 at L=1512) at all extrapolated lengths. However, FMRoPE loses to ALiBi on very long extrapolation (ALiBi maintains PPL 18.39 at L=25512, while FMRoPE degrades to 91.35 at L=25512 without fine-tuning). Transformer-XL and WaveletRPE show competitive short-range perplexity but hit OOM beyond L=3512, preventing direct comparison at longer lengths. The key trade-off is that FMRoPE is slightly worse than standard RoPE at the training length (L=512: 19.58 vs. 19.35), confirming that matching θ to L_train sacrifices interpolation for extrapolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Barbero et al. (2025), who first identified high-norm 'frequency bands' in RoPE dimensions and showed that replacing low-frequency dimensions with NoPE leaves performance unchanged, by explaining *why* and *where* these bands form. It departs from the prevailing θ-scaling paradigm (Xiong et al., 2024; Llama-3) by showing that increasing θ does not add positional information but merely shifts the band toward higher frequencies, pushing more dimensions into a NoPE-like regime. The FMRoPE intervention (setting θ = L_train) connects to Takase & Okazaki's (2019) LRPE and Liu et al. (2024)'s finding that smaller θ aids extrapolation, but provides a mechanistic explanation via a variance-maximization derivation that predicts band location from (θ, L_train, d).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a falsifiable diagnostic (band index) and a simple practical rule—choose θ ≈ L_train when extrapolation is critical, larger θ when interpolation dominates—that could reshape how practitioners configure RoPE for long-context models. However, all controlled experiments use a ~100M-parameter model on WikiText-103, and the FMRoPE perplexity gains, while consistent, have not been validated at the 7B+ scale where production decisions are made. Open questions include whether the interpolation-extrapolation trade-off persists with chain-of-thought reasoning, whether dynamic or adaptive θ scheduling can capture both benefits, and how the band interacts with architectural choices like GQA or sliding-window attention at scale.
