## Problem

The prior MFC-Approx algorithm for learning-augmented metric MST achieved a 2.62-approximation for forest completion and (2γ+1) for MST, yet in practice produced near-optimal trees. This gap raised open questions: are the theoretical bounds tight on pathological instances, can the analysis be tightened, and can better instance-specific guarantees be derived? The paper addresses all three by generalizing the algorithm to use multiple representatives per component and providing a simpler, tighter approximation analysis.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of n points in an arbitrary metric space, an initial forest consisting of a partition into t components each with a spanning tree, and a nonnegative budget b specifying how many extra representatives (beyond one per component) may be selected. It outputs a spanning tree that completes the initial forest using only edges incident to at least one representative, along with the per-component representative sets. The algorithm operates on a coarsened graph of t nodes whose edge weights are defined by the minimum distance from any point in one component to a representative in the other, so the number of distance queries scales with the total number of representatives rather than n².

## Method

MultiRepMFC generalizes MFC-Approx by selecting a nonempty set R_i of representatives per component and computing a weight function ŵ(v_i, v_j) = min{d(P_i, R_j), d(P_j, R_i)} on the coarsened graph, then finding its MST. The key analytical contribution is an instance-specific approximation bound α = 1 + cost(P,R)/w_X(E_t) where cost(P_i, R_i) is the maximum distance from any point in P_i to its nearest representative, yielding a 2-approximation for MFC and 2γ-approximation for MST as a corollary (improving the prior 2.62 and 2γ+1 bounds). Selecting the best representatives (BESTREPS) is shown to be a shared-budget multi-instance generalization of k-center, solved with a 2-approximation that combines Gonzalez's greedy k-center per component with a dynamic programming allocation of the shared budget b across components in O(tb²) time. The worst-case tightness of the 2-approximation is proven by construction, and the instance-specific bound α is shown to be easily computable during the algorithm's execution, serving as a practical proxy for the true approximation ratio.

## Results

MultiRepMFC improves the worst-case approximation for MFC from 2.62 to 2, and for metric MST from (2γ+1) to 2γ, with a tight instance-specific bound α = 1 + cost(P,R)/w_X(E_t). On four real-world datasets (Cooking, FashionMNIST, Names-US, GreenGenes), even a small budget b=t reduces the true approximation gap ε from 0.0022–0.0084 (b=0, MFC-Approx) to 0.0011–0.0034, while the theoretical bound ε_α shrinks from 0.0075–0.0455 to 0.0060–0.0257. DP-MultiRepMFC consistently achieves the best ε_α values and the smallest gap between the bound and true approximation, with runtimes only modestly higher than Greedy or Fixed(ℓ) variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is MFC-Approx (Veldt et al., 2025), which corresponds to MultiRepMFC with b=0 (one representative per component). All MultiRepMFC variants with b>0 strictly improve upon MFC-Approx in both true approximation quality (ε) and theoretical bound (ε_α) across all four datasets; for example, on Names-US, DP-MultiRepMFC at b=2t reduces ε from 0.0061 to 0.0004 and ε_α from 0.0408 to 0.0038. The upper bound is MFC-OPT (the optimal Ω(n²) algorithm), which MultiRepMFC approaches closely at moderate budgets—on FashionMNIST, DP at b=2t achieves ε=0.0009, very near the optimal solution. Among the three BESTREPS strategies, DP-MultiRepMFC produces the best ε_α bounds and the fastest shrinkage of the gap between bound and true approximation, while Fixed(ℓ) often outperforms Greedy in true spanning tree quality (ε), likely because Greedy is too myopic in allocating representatives. The theoretical contribution also improves the worst-case guarantee from 2.62 to 2 for MFC and from (2γ+1) to 2γ for metric MST, with a construction (Theorem 3) proving these bounds are tight.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the metric forest completion (MFC) framework introduced by the same authors in their 2025 ICML paper, which proved an Ω(n²) lower bound for optimal MFC and gave a 2.62-approximation (MFC-Approx) using one representative per component. The key generalization here is allowing multiple representatives per component under a shared budget, interpolating between the prior single-representative algorithm and the exact Ω(n²) algorithm. A secondary contribution is the BESTREPS problem, a new shared-budget multi-instance generalization of k-center, for which a 2-approximation is obtained by combining Gonzalez's greedy k-center with dynamic programming for budget allocation. The approximation analysis is notably simpler than the prior work's β-partitioning approach, yielding tighter bounds (2 vs. 2.62 for MFC; 2γ vs. 2γ+1 for metric MST).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tightened 2-approximation for MFC (proven tight) and the easily computable instance-specific bound α provide practitioners with both stronger worst-case guarantees and a practical proxy for true approximation quality without solving MFC optimally. The empirical finding that even a small representative budget yields near-optimal spanning trees at a fraction of the exact algorithm's runtime makes the framework broadly applicable to large-scale metric MST in arbitrary distance spaces. Open questions include whether subquadratic algorithms can achieve approximation factors below 2 for MFC, whether general lower bounds exist for all subquadratic MFC algorithms, and whether the framework extends to other quality parameters beyond γ-overlap.
