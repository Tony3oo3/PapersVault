## Problem

Standard normalization layers like RMSNorm and LayerNorm discard input norm information during the forward pass and rely on a static, input-independent scaling factor γ, which limits adaptability to distributional shifts and zero-shot generalization. Saturation activation alternatives like DyT preserve norm but suffer from vanishing gradients in extreme regions and cannot dynamically adjust gradient scale based on input magnitude during backpropagation. The paper asks whether a single mechanism can simultaneously provide training stability, efficient gradient-based optimization, and explicit retention of input norm information.

## Input / Output

SeeDNorm takes a D-dimensional activation vector per token (x ∈ R^{N×D}) and outputs a normalized tensor of identical shape, serving as a direct drop-in replacement for RMSNorm or LayerNorm at every normalization site in a transformer (pre-norm, post-norm, QueryNorm, KeyNorm). It introduces only two additional D-dimensional learnable vectors (α and β) per layer, with negligible computational overhead. In the DiT image-generation setting, an AdaSeeDNorm variant incorporates class-conditioned scale and shift terms (γ(c), η(c)) while removing the internal γ, and a multi-head variant splits x and β into n sub-vectors to reduce dot-product variance in vision tasks.

## Method

SeeDNorm extends RMSNorm by replacing the static scaling factor γ with an input-dependent rescaling matrix [σ(x·βᵀ)·α + γ], where σ is tanh (bounding output to [-1,1]) and β is initialized to zero. The dot product x·βᵀ produces a scalar per token that, after tanh activation and element-wise multiplication with α, yields a D-dimensional vector that modulates γ on a per-element, per-token basis, thereby encoding input norm information into the forward pass. During backpropagation, the gradient with respect to x retains the 1/RMS(x) scaling factor from RMSNorm, so gradient magnitude adapts inversely to input norm, while the tanh derivative σ′(x·βᵀ) prevents gradient explosion for large inputs. A multi-head variant partitions x and β into n sub-vectors, computing n smaller dot products to reduce gradient variance (motivated by the theorem that dot-product variance scales linearly with dimension). Weight decay is applied to α and β to control their growth, and the method is validated across OLMoE/OLMo2 LLMs (dense and MoE), ViT/ConvNeXT classification, MAE pre-training, and DiT image generation.

## Results

On OLMoE-1.3B (500B tokens), SeeDNorm reduces validation loss from 2.922 to 2.900 and PPL from 18.63 to 18.12, while improving ARC-Challenge accuracy from 32.3% to 34.5% and ARC-Easy from 62.2% to 65.4%. On the larger OLMoE-7B (1T tokens), gains are more pronounced: ARC-Challenge improves from 40.8% to 44.5% (+3.7 pts) and PIQA from 76.6% to 79.1% (+2.5 pts). In vision, SeeDNorm achieves 82.7% top-1 on ImageNet-1K with ViT-B (vs. 82.3% LayerNorm, 82.5% DyT) and 84.6% with ConvNeXT-L (vs. 84.3% LayerNorm, 84.4% DyT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RMSNorm (the default in all OLMoE/OLMo2 baselines), SeeDNorm wins on every reported metric for both MoE and dense models, with the largest gains in MoE settings (e.g., +3.7 pts on ARC-Challenge for OLMoE-7B, +3.2 pts on ARC-Easy for OLMoE-1.3B). Against DyT (Zhu et al., 2025b), SeeDNorm is a clear winner in language modeling: OLMoE-1.3B-DyT degrades to 2.968 loss and 30.4% ARC-C, while SeeDNorm achieves 2.900 loss and 34.5% ARC-C; in vision, SeeDNorm matches or slightly exceeds DyT (e.g., 82.7% vs. 82.5% on ViT-B, 84.6% vs. 84.4% on ConvNeXT-L). Against LayerNorm (Ba et al., 2016), compared only in ImageNet-1K classification, SeeDNorm achieves equal or better top-1 accuracy across all six model configurations (e.g., 82.7% vs. 82.3% on ViT-B, 85.5% vs. 85.5% on ViT-L MAE fine-tuning). The paper also notes that the advantage of SeeDNorm over RMSNorm widens with more training tokens, suggesting compounding benefits during long pre-training runs.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeeDNorm extends RMSNorm by replacing its static learnable scaling vector γ with an input-conditioned dynamic coefficient σ(x·βᵀ)·α + γ, thereby preserving input norm information in the forward pass that RMSNorm and LayerNorm discard. It also positions itself against DyT (Zhu et al., 2025b), a saturation-activation alternative to normalization, and provides a theoretical result (Proposition B.1) showing that DyT's gradient behavior is approximately equivalent to RMSNorm under constant-norm assumptions, highlighting DyT's inability to adaptively rescale gradients. The multi-head variant draws on the variance-reduction intuition from multi-head attention to stabilize training in high-dimensional settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Normalization layers are a universal component in modern transformers, so a drop-in replacement with minimal parameter overhead and consistent gains across LLMs (dense and MoE) and vision tasks has broad practical relevance. The observation that SeeDNorm's benefits are amplified in MoE architectures—where expert routing causes per-token distributional shifts—suggests a principled reason why input-dependent scaling helps most where data heterogeneity is highest. Open questions include whether layer-specific tuning of initialization and hyperparameters (currently held uniform) could yield further gains, and whether scaling up the parameter capacity of the normalization layer itself is a promising direction for additional performance.
