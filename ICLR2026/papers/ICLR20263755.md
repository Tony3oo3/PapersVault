## Problem

Existing audio and music generation models are confined to a single conditioning modality (text-only or video-only) and a single output domain (sound effects or music), with no unified framework supporting flexible combinations of inputs. This limitation is compounded by a critical data scarcity: available datasets provide supervision for only one conditioning modality, preventing the training of truly generalist, multimodal-conditioned audio generators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263755/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

AudioX accepts any combination of text prompts (encoded via T5-base), video clips (frame features at 5 fps via CLIP-ViT-B/32 plus synchronization features at 25 fps via Synchformer), and audio clips (encoded via a latent audio autoencoder), with missing modalities zero-padded or substituted with natural-language defaults. The model outputs 10-second audio or music in the latent space, decoded by the same autoencoder. For inpainting and music completion tasks, the audio input is a masked or preceding segment of the target, respectively, and the model also handles zero-shot image-to-audio generation without dedicated image training data.

## Method

The architecture is a 24-layer Diffusion Transformer (2.4B total, 1.1B trainable parameters) pretrained from Stable Audio Open, conditioned on a unified multimodal embedding H_c produced by a lightweight Multimodal Adaptive Fusion (MAF) module (60M parameters). MAF operates in three stages: (1) per-modality gates filter and reweight embeddings to suppress noise, (2) modality-specific learnable queries perform cross-attention over concatenated gated embeddings to aggregate cross-modal evidence, and (3) a self-attention layer consolidates the context before residual updates dispatch refined signals back to each modality path. Training follows standard latent diffusion (forward Markov noising, reverse ε-prediction) with classifier-free guidance at inference (scale 7.0, 250 steps). The model is trained on IF-caps, a 7M+ sample dataset built via a two-stage annotation pipeline: Gemini 2.5 Pro generates structured initial annotations (caption, category with counts, SED timestamps, time relations), then Qwen2-Audio performs large-scale caption augmentation from multiple perspectives (rephrasing, category/count, SED, time-relation) to increase linguistic diversity. A key empirical finding is a cross-modal regularization effect: high-quality textual supervision reduces alignment noise and improves generation quality across all conditioning modalities, not just text-to-audio.

## Results

On AudioCaps T2A, AudioX achieves SOTA Inception Score (12.48) and FAD (1.59), outperforming MMAudio (IS 12.03, FAD 4.71) and Tango 2 (IS 10.37, FAD 3.20). On VGGSound T2A, the advantage is more pronounced: AudioX reaches IS 19.58 and FAD 1.33, surpassing the next-best MMAudio (IS 17.83, FAD 2.50). On the instruction-following T2A-bench, AudioX leads all baselines by wide margins—Cat-acc 34.20 vs. 32.40 (Make-An-Audio2), Cnt-acc 12.40 vs. 9.80 (Stable Audio Open), Ord-acc 23.60 vs. 19.80 (Make-An-Audio2), and TS-acc 28.20 vs. 21.80 (Stable Audio Open)—and similarly dominates on AudioTime (Ordering 0.34 vs. 0.76, Timestamp 0.81 vs. 0.61).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AudioX achieves SOTA on T2A across both AudioCaps and VGGSound, beating MMAudio (the strongest unified baseline) by +0.45 IS and −3.12 FAD on AudioCaps, and by +1.75 IS and −1.17 FAD on VGGSound; it also outperforms specialist T2A models like Tango 2 and Stable Audio Open on FAD and FD. On V2A (VGGSound), AudioX trails MMAudio on IS (12.60 vs. 14.95) and alignment (0.26 vs. 0.35) but leads on FAD (1.28 vs. 2.04) and FD (7.84 vs. 6.18 is a loss), indicating competitive but not dominant video-conditioned performance. For music generation, AudioX clearly wins T2M on MusicCaps (IS 3.55 vs. 2.94 for Stable Audio Open; FAD 1.53 vs. 1.88 for TangoMusic) and V2M on V2M-bench (FAD 1.67 vs. 2.46 for VidMuse; FD 24.01 vs. 29.95). The most striking margin is in instruction-following: AudioX surpasses the best baseline (Make-An-Audio2) by +1.8 Cat-acc, +8.4 Cnt-acc, +3.8 Ord-acc, and +9.4 TS-acc on T2A-bench, and by 0.42 in Ordering error and 0.25 in Timestamp on AudioTime—gaps far exceeding those in standard quality metrics. The paper notes that high audio fidelity does not correlate with instruction-following (e.g., Tango 2 has strong synthesis quality but only moderate control metrics), underscoring that AudioX's unified training and IF-caps data specifically unlock fine-grained controllability.

## Relation to Prior Work

AudioX builds directly on Stable Audio Open's DiT backbone and audio autoencoder, extending the latent diffusion paradigm from single-condition tasks (text-to-audio, video-to-audio) to a unified anything-to-audio framework. It departs from prior unified attempts (e.g., Movie Gen, FoleyCrafter) by introducing the Multimodal Adaptive Fusion (MAF) module to handle arbitrary combinations of text, video, and audio conditions, and by addressing the critical data bottleneck through the IF-caps dataset (7M+ samples) constructed via a two-stage Gemini/Qwen2-Audio annotation pipeline. The T2A-bench benchmark further extends the evaluation landscape beyond standard quality metrics to fine-grained instruction-following dimensions (category, count, ordering, timestamp).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework and IF-caps dataset provide a practical foundation for building generalist audio generation systems, with the cross-modal regularization effect (better text supervision improving video-to-audio performance) offering a transferable insight for multimodal model design. However, alignment scores (CLAP, ImageBind) sometimes lag behind specialist baselines like MMAudio on V2A tasks, suggesting the fusion mechanism still struggles with precise cross-modal grounding. Open questions include scalability beyond 10-second clips, whether the cross-modal regularization effect persists at larger scale, and whether the instruction-following advantage generalizes to more complex compositional prompts beyond the four tested dimensions.
