## Problem

Under the linear Q^π realizability assumption, no online RL algorithm is both statistically and computationally efficient. Prior methods either require solving intractable optimization problems, rely on (local) simulator access to resample from visited states, or both. This gap is particularly acute when initial states are stochastic, since the same state may never be encountered twice, making the resampling mechanism used by existing policy-iteration algorithms fundamentally infeasible.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on a finite-horizon MDP (H stages, deterministic transitions, stochastic rewards in [0,1], stochastic initial state) with a given feature map φ(s,a) ∈ R^d of bounded norm. At each episode it receives a trajectory (s₁,a₁,r₁,…,s_H,a_H,r_H) and outputs a policy decision a_h at each step h, a frozen least-squares Q-value estimate per state, and a cumulative regret bound of Õ(√(d²H⁶T)). The key constraint is that data is consumed strictly online—only one trajectory per episode is available, and the algorithm cannot restart from arbitrary previously visited states.

## Method

Frozen Policy Iteration (FPI) maintains per-stage datasets of (s,a,q) tuples and, at each episode, identifies the last step h_t where the state-action pair is not yet covered by the existing dataset (i.e., its elliptical norm exceeds a threshold). Only that single pair and its remaining trajectory reward are appended to the dataset; all earlier (well-explored) pairs are discarded. Crucially, the Q-function for a state s is computed using only the first k_t(s) data points—the prefix up to the moment all actions at s became covered—thereby freezing the policy at s and preventing later policy updates from contaminating the estimate with off-policy data. For regret minimization, FPI layers multiple accuracy levels (2^{-l}) hierarchically: the algorithm starts at the finest level and degrades to coarser levels when exploration is needed, with each level's action set constrained by the previous level's Q-estimates to bound per-step suboptimality. This multi-level freezing design yields the Õ(√(d²H⁶T)) regret bound without any simulator access, and extends to Uniform-PAC guarantees and function classes with bounded eluder dimension.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is a regret bound of Õ(√(d²H⁶T)) for the online regret minimization version (Theorem 2), which is optimal for linear contextual bandits (the H=1 special case). The PAC version (Theorem 1) guarantees at most Õ(d²H⁴/ε²) episodes with suboptimality gap exceeding ε, and the Uniform-PAC extension (Theorem 3) achieves Õ(d²H⁶/ε²). Empirically, a proof-of-concept implementation on CartPole-v1 and InvertedPendulum-v4 (5 seeds, tile coding with 4 tiles/dim, λ=10⁻³, ε=1) shows that the freezing mechanism improves learning performance over a no-freezing variant, though no formal numerical comparison against other RL algorithms is provided.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FPI is the first algorithm to be both statistically and computationally efficient in the standard online RL setting under linear Q^π realizability. Prior methods with polynomial sample complexity (Weisz et al. 2023; Mhammedi 2025) require either computationally intractable optimization or NP-hard oracles, while methods with polynomial running time (Du et al. 2019a; Lattimore et al. 2020; Yin et al. 2022; Weisz et al. 2022) all require (local) access to a simulator for resampling, which is infeasible with stochastic initial states. FPI achieves both PAC and regret guarantees without a simulator, at the cost of requiring deterministic transitions (stochastic transitions marked ✗ in Table 1). The empirical evaluation is limited to a proof-of-concept ablation showing freezing improves performance on CartPole-v1 and InvertedPendulum-v4, rather than a head-to-head comparison against other RL algorithms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the policy-iteration framework (rooted in LSPI) and the eluder dimension machinery to the online RL setting under linear Q^π realizability, a monotone structural assumption that prior computationally efficient methods (Weisz et al. 2023; Mhammedi 2025) could not handle without intractable optimization or oracle access. It departs from simulator-based policy iteration algorithms (Du et al. 2019a; Lattimore et al. 2020; Yin et al. 2022; Weisz et al. 2022) by eliminating the need for state resampling through a 'freezing' mechanism that keeps all dataset entries effectively on-policy. The work also positions itself against the computationally efficient RL literature under linear Bellman completeness (Zanette et al. 2020b; Wu et al. 2024; Golowich & Moitra 2024), offering an alternative under a more practical (monotone) assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first computationally efficient online RL algorithm under linear Q^π realizability with stochastic initial states, closing a notable gap in the computational-statistical landscape for policy-iteration methods. However, the requirement for deterministic transitions significantly narrows applicability, and the H^6 polynomial dependence in the regret bound remains a substantial limitation that the authors flag as an open problem. Extending the freezing mechanism to stochastic dynamics is identified as non-trivial, and the proof-of-concept experiments on CartPole and InvertedPendulum provide only preliminary evidence of practical viability.
