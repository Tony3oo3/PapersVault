## Problem

Existing discrete video tokenizers compress all content at a fixed rate, wasting tokens on simple videos and under-allocating them to complex ones. The paper proves via Shannon's Source Coding Theorem that both fixed-rate and data-agnostic adaptive tokenizers (e.g., ElasticTok's uniform router) are inherently biased, yielding expected token lengths arbitrarily far from the information-theoretic optimum.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261370/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (T×H×W×3) and a target average compression rate (BPP16) as input, and produces a variable-length discrete token sequence whose length N_x is determined per-video by its information complexity, plus a binary mask (~5% overhead) indicating which spatial-temporal positions are retained. The decoder reconstructs the video from these adaptive tokens. In practice, the base tokenizer uses a 4×8×8 temporal-spatial compression (Cosmos), and the adaptive compressor can reduce the token count to as low as 1/16 of the maximum.

## Method

INFOTOK builds on a fixed-length 3D-CNN tokenizer (Cosmos) and adds two components: (1) an ELBO-based router that computes the video's information complexity via the evidence lower bound of the reconstruction likelihood, then deterministically sets the token length as N_x = β·ELBO(x)/E[ELBO(x)], and (2) an 8-layer transformer adaptive compressor that compresses fixed-length embeddings to N_x tokens using likelihood-based token selection—masking out tokens with the highest per-token log-likelihood (lowest information content) rather than using spatial heuristics. The INFOTOK-Flex variant ensembles multiple β values during training so a single model supports a range of compression rates. The paper proves that this ELBO-based router achieves expected token length within β of the Shannon-optimal bound, and empirically demonstrates 2.3× better compression than ElasticTok at equal quality with 11× fewer inference evaluations.

## Results

On TokenBench-256×256 at BPP₁₆=0.81, INFOTOK achieves PSNR 30.08, SSIM 0.881, LPIPS 0.145, and FVD 49, matching the fixed-rate Cosmos-DV (PSNR 30.01, FVD 49) while using 20% fewer tokens (BPP₁₆=0.81 vs 1.00). At the more aggressive BPP₁₆=0.56, INFOTOK still reaches PSNR 29.27 and FVD 70, outperforming ElasticTok at BPP₁₆=0.81 (PSNR 28.26, FVD 141). Across both datasets, INFOTOK reduces FVD by 40–60% and LPIPS by 25–40% relative to ElasticTok at matched compression rates, while requiring only 1 additional decoder pass (2 NFEs total) versus 12 NFEs for ElasticTok's binary-search inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary adaptive baseline ElasticTok, INFOTOK wins decisively at every compression level: at BPP₁₆=0.81 on TokenBench, INFOTOK improves PSNR by +1.82 dB (30.08 vs 28.26), reduces FVD by 65% (49 vs 141), and cuts LPIPS by 41% (0.145 vs 0.244); the gap widens at BPP₁₆=0.56 where INFOTOK gains +1.93 dB PSNR and reduces FVD by 64% (70 vs 194). Remarkably, INFOTOK at BPP₁₆=0.56 (PSNR 29.27) surpasses ElasticTok at BPP₁₆=0.81 (PSNR 28.26), demonstrating a 2.3× compression-rate advantage. Against the fixed-rate Cosmos-DV tokenizer, INFOTOK at BPP₁₆=0.81 matches its PSNR (30.08 vs 30.01) and FVD (49 vs 49) while using 20% fewer tokens, and at BPP₁₆=0.56 retains PSNR 29.27 versus Cosmos's 30.01 at full rate. Against the weaker fixed-rate baselines Open-MAGVIT2 (PSNR 25.77) and OmniTokenizer (PSNR 23.15), INFOTOK leads by 4.3 and 6.9 dB PSNR respectively at BPP₁₆=0.81. Inference efficiency is a clear win: INFOTOK requires only 2 NFEs (one extra decoder pass for ELBO) versus 12 NFEs for ElasticTok's binary search, yielding 11× fewer evaluations and 35× lower wall-clock latency (1.23 s vs 42.75 s per video on an RTX A5000).

## Relation to Prior Work

INFOTOK builds directly on the Cosmos Discrete Video Tokenizer (Agarwal et al., 2025) as its fixed-length backbone, adding an ELBO-based router and a transformer-based adaptive compressor on top. It departs from the prior adaptive tokenization line (ElasticTok, ALIT, One-D-Piece, FlexTok), which relies on data-agnostic uniform sampling of token lengths during training and heuristic search at inference, by grounding token allocation in Shannon's Source Coding Theorem and proving that such uniform routers are provably biased. The use of ELBO as a tractable proxy for per-video information complexity connects the VAE literature to the discrete tokenization setting in a principled way that prior heuristic methods lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical proof that fixed-rate and data-agnostic adaptive tokenizers are inherently suboptimal provides a clear design principle for future video tokenizers: token budgets should scale with information content, not be uniform. Practically, saving 20% tokens without quality loss and reducing inference NFEs by 11× over ElasticTok is meaningful for scaling video generation and understanding to longer sequences. However, the paper evaluates only reconstruction fidelity (PSNR, FVD, LPIPS) and explicitly defers downstream evaluation (video generation, action understanding) due to compute constraints, leaving open whether the adaptive token sequences transfer well to autoregressive or diffusion-based generative models. The single extra decoder pass for ELBO estimation, while far cheaper than ElasticTok's binary search, still adds latency that could be further reduced with lighter-weight complexity estimators.
