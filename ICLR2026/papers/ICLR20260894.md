## Problem

Deep learning precipitation forecasting is split between radar-only nowcasting models that degrade beyond ~2 hours and medium-range NWP-based models that lack the spatial resolution to capture local showers. No existing model efficiently produces high-resolution, probabilistic 8-hour precipitation forecasts across Europe while integrating multi-sensor data, with prior multi-source systems like MetNet-3 requiring 512 TPUs for 7 days of training, being US-only, and using lead-time conditioning that makes inference 48× slower than single-pass prediction.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 1536² km² context patch assembled from five heterogeneous sources at mismatched resolutions and temporal cadences: RainViewer radar composites at 4 km (7 timesteps) and 8 km (1 timestep), EUMETSAT satellite imagery at 8 km (11 channels, 5 timesteps with 1-hour operational delay), NOAA GFS atmospheric variables at 16 km (122 channels, single initialization), GFS precipitation forecasts at 16 km (8 future timesteps), and Copernicus DEM topography at 4 km (3 channels, static). It outputs a 512² km² target region with 48 timesteps (10-min intervals over 8 hours) × 19 intensity-class conditional probabilities, where the final class probability for each pixel and timestep is the cumulative product of all preceding conditional outputs, guaranteeing monotonicity across intensity levels.

## Method

RainPro-8 is a U-Net with 12 MaxViT blocks (36.7 M parameters, <20% of MetNet-3's 227 M) that fuses multi-resolution inputs via Space-to-Depth convolutions in the encoder and reconstructs high-resolution probability maps through transposed-convolution decoding with skip connections. Two key training innovations distinguish it from MetNet-3: (1) an Ordinal Consistent Loss that reformulates outputs as conditional probabilities P(R_t ≥ min(I_c) | R_t ≥ min(I_{c-1})) via Bayes' theorem, so BCE is computed only over pixels where the previous intensity class is active, enforcing ordinality during training rather than only at inference; and (2) single-pass prediction of all 48 timesteps with timestamps encoded into the channel dimension, combined with exponential-decay lead-time weights in the loss to prioritize earlier timesteps without the 48× inference cost of lead-time conditioning. The architecture further reduces parameters through early downsampling before ResNet blocks, halved internal channels (256 throughout), and removal of topographical embeddings.

## Results

On the 8-hour European precipitation forecasting task, RainPro-8 achieves a CSI of 0.279 and FSS of 0.537, outperforming the closest competitor MetNet-3* (CSI 0.270, FSS 0.517) and surpassing operational NWP (GFS: CSI 0.110) and extrapolation-based methods (PySTEPS: CSI 0.149) by 65%+ on CSI. The model uses only 36.7M parameters (vs. 227M for MetNet-3) and achieves a 48× inference speedup (0.03 s/sample vs. 2.12 s/sample) through single-pass prediction of all 48 lead times. On the SEVIR 2-hour radar-only benchmark, the adapted RainPro-2R variant reaches CSI 0.3524 and HSS 0.4501, setting a new state-of-the-art among deterministic nowcasting models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RainPro-8 outperforms MetNet-3* (faithful reimplementation) on all five metrics (CSI 0.279 vs. 0.270, FSS 0.537 vs. 0.517, MAE 0.126 vs. 0.132, MSE 1.503 vs. 1.620, FBI 1.262 vs. 1.318) while being 48× faster at inference due to single-pass prediction. Against operational baselines, RainPro-8 achieves 2.5× higher CSI than GFS (0.279 vs. 0.110) and 1.9× higher CSI than PySTEPS (0.279 vs. 0.149), though PySTEPS remains the least biased (FBI 0.983) and GFS has a marginally lower MSE. Among radar-only deep-learning models, RainPro-8 dominates Earthformer (CSI 0.279 vs. 0.111) and SimVP (CSI 0.279 vs. 0.122) on skill metrics, though both achieve lower MAE/MSE due to their MSE-based training favoring the dominant no-rain class. On the SEVIR benchmark, RainPro-2R surpasses all deterministic baselines (best: SimVP CSI 0.2662) and all generative models (best: DiffCast CSI 0.3077) on pixel-wise CSI and HSS, but DiffCast retains an edge on perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.1812 vs. 0.2353, SSIM 0.6354 vs. 0.5966), reflecting a sharpness-vs-probabilistic-accuracy trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

RainPro-8 is a direct adaptation of MetNet-3's transformer-based probabilistic precipitation forecasting architecture, re-engineered for European data sources (RainViewer radar, EUMETSAT satellite, GFS NWP) at a fraction of the parameter count (36.7M vs. 227M). It departs from MetNet-3 in three key ways: replacing cross-entropy with an ordinal-consistent loss inspired by ordinal regression in semantic segmentation (Fernandes & Cardoso, 2018), substituting lead-time conditioning with single-pass multi-timestep prediction weighted by an exponential decay schedule, and simplifying the U-Net/MaxViT backbone through early downsampling and halved channels. The broader positioning bridges the well-known gap between short-lead-time radar nowcasting (≤3 h) and coarse-resolution medium-range NWP, targeting the 8-hour horizon where neither paradigm performs well.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 48× inference speedup from single-pass prediction (0.03 s vs. 2.12 s per sample) combined with maintained or slightly improved accuracy makes high-resolution probabilistic precipitation forecasting far more deployable in operational settings, and the open-source release lowers the barrier for other regions to adopt similar multi-source pipelines. The ablation showing that lead-time conditioning actually degrades performance (CSI 0.2695 vs. 0.2791) challenges the assumption that per-lead-time specialization is necessary. Open questions include robustness to missing input sources in real operations, automatic threshold learning, and whether the European-specific data fusion strategy generalizes to other continents with different sensor availability.
