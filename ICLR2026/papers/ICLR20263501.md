## Problem

Existing on-policy knowledge distillation methods force the student to match the teacher's output distribution at every token position, which overwhelms smaller models with limited capacity and causes distribution mismatch, particularly in complex reasoning tasks where teacher and student reasoning styles inherently differ. This paper asks whether a more targeted, indirect form of supervision can transfer reasoning ability while letting the student explain its reasoning in its own words.

## Input / Output

The method takes text prompts (from UltraInteract) and the student model generates its own chain-of-thought reasoning responses on-policy. The teacher model does not generate its own outputs; instead it provides supervisory signals (preference rankings over student-proposed candidates and selective distribution matching) on the student's self-generated text. The final output is a full reasoning trace followed by an answer, evaluated for correctness across 10 reasoning benchmarks spanning math, science, code, and instruction following.

## Method

TSD-KD combines three token-selective components. First, indirect distillation: the student proposes its top-k candidate tokens at each position within the 'opener' (the initial segment of the response where cumulative entropy reaches ~10%), and the teacher re-ranks those candidates; a Plackett-Luce preference loss aligns the student's ranking with the teacher's preference without enforcing the teacher's full distribution. Second, direct distillation: a JSD(β) divergence is applied only to tokens where the student is uncertain but the teacher is confident, modulated by a sigmoid gating function on the entropy gap. Third, entropy regularization selectively minimizes the entropy of the top-10% highest-entropy tokens to boost the student's confidence on critical reasoning steps. The key design principle is student-centricity: the student generates its own reasoning, the teacher provides only subtle, targeted feedback on a small subset of important tokens, and the remaining tokens are left for the student to reason freely.

## Results

On the primary Qwen2.5 (14B→1.5B) setup, TSD-KD achieves the best accuracy across all six main benchmarks: GSM8K (60.1), GSM-Plus (40.5), MATH (26.1), MBPP (42.1), IFEval (55.2), and MMLU-STEM (50.0). The largest gain is on MATH, where TSD-KD scores 26.1 versus 18.6 for the best baseline (DistiLLM), a 40.3% relative improvement, and even surpasses the 14B teacher (21.7) by 20.3%. On the four more challenging benchmarks (Table 2), TSD-KD leads on all four, with the student outperforming the teacher on SciQ (93.0 vs. 86.4, +7.6%) and MMLU-Pro-Math (36.9 vs. 77.6 teacher, but +2.5% over best baseline). Overall, TSD-KD improves over the untrained 1.5B student by up to 54.4% in accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSD-KD outperforms all six baselines on the majority of benchmarks. Against the closest on-policy competitor GKD (β=0.9), TSD-KD gains +2.2 on GSM8K, +0.6 on GSM-Plus, +8.0 on MATH, +0.3 on MBPP, +2.9 on IFEval, and +2.3 on MMLU-STEM. The off-policy baselines (Sequence-Level KD and Supervised-KD) are consistently the weakest, confirming the benefit of on-policy training; for example, Supervised-KD scores only 17.9 on MATH versus TSD-KD's 26.1. Among on-policy methods, DistiLLM and MiniLLM are the strongest runners-up on MATH (18.6 and 17.8 respectively), yet both trail TSD-KD by 7.5–8.3 points. Speculative KD is competitive on GSM8K (58.0) but lags TSD-KD by 2.1 points and falls further behind on MATH (18.5 vs. 26.1). Notably, TSD-KD's student model surpasses the 14B teacher on MATH (+20.3%) and SciQ (+7.6%), a result none of the baselines achieve, suggesting that token-selective, student-centric distillation enables reasoning capabilities beyond simple imitation of the teacher.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on on-policy knowledge distillation (GKD, Agarwal et al. 2024), which uses JSD(β) to match the teacher's distribution over the student's self-generated outputs. It departs from the teacher-forcing paradigm by introducing a student-centric, token-selective framework: an indirect distillation component borrows the Plackett-Luce preference model from RLHF (Ouyang et al. 2022; Rafailov et al. 2023) to let the teacher re-rank the student's own candidate tokens rather than imposing its full distribution, while the direct component gates JSD by the entropy gap between teacher and student. The selective entropy regularization draws on recent findings that minimizing entropy on high-uncertainty tokens improves reasoning (Agarwal et al. 2025; Wang et al. 2025). Conceptually, the paper reframes KD from 'mimic the teacher everywhere' to 'intervene minimally at critical branching points, let the student reason in its own words.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, well-ablated recipe for compressing reasoning capability into small models, validated across three model families (Qwen2.5, Gemma2, Qwen3) and ten benchmarks, and it remains effective under LoRA/PEFT constraints. The finding that the student surpasses the teacher in four settings (up to +20.3% on MATH) challenges the implicit assumption that distillation is bounded by teacher quality, suggesting that targeted, student-centric supervision can unlock reasoning paths the teacher itself does not always follow. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to much larger teacher-student capacity gaps, multi-turn or tool-augmented reasoning, and whether the 'opener' heuristic (first 10% cumulative entropy) remains optimal as reasoning traces grow longer with more capable base models.
