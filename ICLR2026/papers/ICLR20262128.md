## Problem

Computer use agents (CUAs) require extensive in-domain knowledge of software interfaces and operational procedures, yet computer-use data—especially fine-grained action trajectories—is scarce, costly to collect, and not naturally archived online. Existing open-source CUAs are constrained by limited data scale and poor cross-platform transferability, while proprietary systems (e.g., Operator, Claude) rely on inaccessible datasets. This paper addresses both bottlenecks by constructing a large-scale, cross-platform training corpus and demonstrating that data-driven scaling yields substantial, consistent gains in GUI understanding, grounding, and end-to-end task completion.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes raw screenshot pixels (no A11y Tree or DOM) as its sole visual observation, paired with a text task instruction and a flat text-description history of prior (action, observation) pairs to keep inference cost low. Outputs are structured in three interchangeable modes: Grounding Mode returns point or bounding-box coordinates for a target UI element; Direct Action Mode emits an executable command enclosed in <operation> and <action> tags; and Reasoned Action Mode prepends a chain-of-thought rationale inside  tags before the same structured action. All actions draw from a unified cross-platform action space (click, write, swipe, long-press, dragTo, hotkey, etc.) that abstracts desktop, mobile, and web interactions into a single vocabulary, with platform-specific arguments (e.g., coordinates, keypresses) specified per action.

## Method

ScaleCUA is built on Qwen2.5-VL at three scales (3B, 7B, 32B) and trained via supervised fine-tuning on a corpus of 471K GUI-understanding examples, 17.1M grounding annotations, and 19K trajectories (averaging 9 steps) spanning Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, iOS, and Web. The data is collected through a dual-loop pipeline: an Agent-Environment Interaction Loop where rule-driven random-walk agents perform depth-first exploration with heuristic pruning to generate weak-semantic navigation trajectories, and an Agent-Human Hybrid Data Acquisition Loop where human experts curate goal-directed demonstrations and review 20% of agent-collected trajectories for quality. A key design choice is the progressive ratio of general multimodal data to GUI-specific data (25% / 50% / 75% for 3B / 7B / 32B), which empirically shows that larger models can absorb more general knowledge without diluting GUI competence. Data augmentation (element cropping, synthetic resolution scaling, reasoning-prompt enrichment) and raw (non-normalized) coordinate supervision further improve grounding accuracy, while the three inference paradigms allow the same model to serve as a modular grounder, a fast direct-action agent, or a slower but more reliable reasoned-action agent within different agentic workflows.

## Results

ScaleCUA-32B sets new state-of-the-art on MMBench-GUI L1-Hard (94.4%), OSWorld-G (60.6%), and WebArena-Lite-v2 (47.4% at 50 steps), outperforming the best native baseline UI-TARS-72B-DPO by +26.0 points on WebArena-Lite-v2. On ScreenSpot-Pro, ScaleCUA-32B achieves 59.2% overall, surpassing GUI-Owl-32B (58.0%) and Qwen2.5-VL-32B (47.6%). In the agentic workflow setting, ScaleCUA-7B as grounder with GPT-4o as planner reaches 28.1% on OSWorld and 36.6% on WindowsAgentArena, exceeding JEDI-7B (25.0% and 32.9% respectively). Scaling from 3B→7B→32B yields monotonic gains across all platforms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On WebArena-Lite-v2 (50 steps), ScaleCUA-32B (47.4%) decisively outperforms all native baselines: UI-TARS-72B-DPO (21.4%), UI-TARS-1.5-7B (26.0%), Qwen2.5-VL-72B (14.4%), and GPT-4o (3.3%), with a +26.0-point margin over the strongest native competitor. On MMBench-GUI L1-Hard, ScaleCUA-32B (94.4%) surpasses GUI-Owl-32B (94.2%), InternVL3-72B (75.7%), and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (64.6%), while even the 3B variant (89.9%) exceeds Qwen2.5-VL-72B by +25.3 points. On ScreenSpot-Pro, ScaleCUA-32B (59.2%) beats GUI-Owl-32B (58.0%) and Qwen2.5-VL-32B (47.6%) but trails UI-TARS-1.5 (61.6%). On OSWorld (50 steps), ScaleCUA-32B (17.7%) still lags behind OpenCUA-32B (34.1%) and UI-TARS-72B-DPO (25.2%), acknowledging a remaining gap in desktop task completion. In the agentic workflow, ScaleCUA-7B as grounder with GPT-4o planner achieves the best OSWorld (28.1%) and WindowsAgentArena (36.6%) scores, beating JEDI-7B (25.0%, 32.9%) and UI-TARS-1.5-7B (19.1%, 26.2%), and reaches 48.3% on AndroidWorld, the highest among all workflow configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the native GUI agent line (UI-TARS, AGUVIS, OS-Atlas) by scaling training data across six platforms (Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, iOS, Web) rather than focusing on a single domain. It builds directly on Qwen2.5-VL as the backbone and adopts the pure-vision observation paradigm established by AGUVIS and UI-TARS, but departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) a dual-loop data pipeline that combines rule-driven random-walk exploration with human expert trajectories, and (2) a unified cross-platform action space that abstracts desktop, mobile, and web operations into a single vocabulary. Unlike proprietary systems (Operator, Claude) or single-platform open efforts (OS-Genesis for mobile/web, JEDI for desktop), ScaleCUA is the first to open-source a dataset spanning all six major platforms with balanced coverage of understanding, grounding, and task completion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary impact is practical: releasing 17.1M grounding annotations, 471K understanding examples, and 19K trajectories across six platforms addresses the data scarcity bottleneck that has constrained open CUA research. The finding that cross-platform training slightly degrades desktop/web grounding while improving mobile generalization reveals a non-trivial tension in multi-domain training that practitioners need to account for. However, the paper acknowledges that planning ability still lags substantially behind GPT-4o in agentic workflows, and the absence of RL, reflection, or hierarchical memory means long-horizon task completion remains a significant open problem. The flat history representation and lack of self-improvement loops are identified as key limitations for future work.
