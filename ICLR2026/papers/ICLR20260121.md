## Problem

Existing layout-to-image generation methods for remote sensing either rely on ambiguous text prompts that cause geometric distortions, or require real-image patches as references during inference, limiting generalization and practical deployment. These approaches struggle particularly with dense small objects and morphologically complex targets (airplanes, ships, vehicles), producing control leakage, structural distortion, and dense-scene collapse that degrade downstream object detection performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260121/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, OF-Diff takes only oriented bounding boxes with category labels as input and produces a 512×512 synthetic RGB remote sensing image, requiring no real-image references. During training, paired real images and their annotations are used; the ESGM converts each bounding box into a shape mask via RemoteCLIP (textual description) and RemoteSAM (segmentation), with random rotation augmentation to build a diverse mask pool. The model is fine-tuned from Stable Diffusion 1.5 in latent space, with only the ControlNet and shape-feature decoder unfrozen.

## Method

OF-Diff introduces three coupled mechanisms: (1) an Enhanced Shape Generation Module (ESGM) that exploits the quasi-invariant geometry of RS objects (rectangular courts, circular tanks, bilaterally symmetric airplanes) to extract precise shape masks as structural priors; (2) an online-distillation dual-decoder architecture where a mix-feature decoder (conditioned on both image and shape features, with stop-gradient on shape) acts as a teacher guiding a shape-feature-only decoder (student) via a consistency loss, so that at inference only the shape-conditioned path is needed; (3) a DDPO reinforcement-learning fine-tuning stage that treats denoising as an MDP and optimizes a composite reward combining KNN distance (diversity in CLIP embedding space) and KL divergence (distributional alignment with real images). The overall training objective is L_s + L_m + λL_c, and the mix-feature is scheduled as c_m = (n/N)·c_i + sg[c_s] to gradually shift reliance from image to shape conditioning.

## Results

On DIOR, OF-Diff achieves the best FID (24.92), KID (0.011), CMMD (0.312), YOLOScore (58.99), and mAP50 (54.44) among all compared methods. On DOTA, it similarly leads with FID 20.84, KID 0.014, CMMD 0.271, CAS 83.79, YOLOScore 55.68, and mAP50 67.89. For downstream object detection, augmenting training data with OF-Diff-generated images yields mAP gains of +2.2% (DIOR) and +1.94% (DOTA) over the unaugmented baseline, with per-class AP50 improvements of up to 8.3% (airplane) and 7.7% (ship) on DIOR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OF-Diff outperforms all four baselines on the majority of metrics across both DIOR and DOTA. Against the closest RS-specific competitor CC-Diff, OF-Diff reduces FID from 49.62 to 24.92 (DIOR) and from 32.40 to 20.84 (DOTA), while improving YOLOScore by 16.8 and 6.1 points respectively, and mAP50 by 0.96 and 1.37 points. Against AeroGen (the other RS-specific method), OF-Diff achieves lower FID (24.92 vs 27.78 on DIOR; 20.84 vs 26.65 on DOTA) and substantially higher YOLOScore (58.99 vs 55.38 on DIOR; 55.68 vs 44.85 on DOTA). The natural-image baselines LayoutDiff and GLIGEN are clearly outperformed on all metrics, with GLIGEN showing particularly poor YOLOScore (6.51 on DIOR, 15.58 on DOTA) and high FID (39.79 on DOTA), confirming that general-purpose L2I models struggle with remote sensing scenes. The only metric where OF-Diff does not lead is CAS on DIOR, where CC-Diff edges ahead by 0.06 points (82.61 vs 82.55), a negligible difference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the layout-to-image (L2I) generation paradigm from natural images (LayoutDiffusion, GLIGEN) to remote sensing, building directly on the ControlNet + Stable Diffusion 1.5 architecture. It departs from CC-Diff's reliance on real-image references at inference by introducing an Enhanced Shape Generation Module (ESGM) that exploits the quasi-invariant geometric shapes characteristic of RS objects (rectangular courts, circular tanks, bilaterally symmetric airplanes), combined with an online-distillation strategy where a mix-feature decoder (conditioned on both image and shape) acts as a stop-gradient teacher for a shape-only decoder. The DDPO fine-tuning step, which uses KNN and KL-divergence rewards in CLIP/ResNet feature space, is borrowed from the RL-for-diffusion literature (Black et al., 2023) but is the first application specifically to RS layout-to-image generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OF-Diff addresses a practical bottleneck in RS data augmentation: the need for real-image references at inference limits scalability and generalization, and the paper demonstrates that shape-prior conditioning plus online distillation can close much of the fidelity gap without such references. The finding that caption-guided generation improves aesthetics but degrades downstream detection performance (higher FID, lower ΔmAP50, more t-SNE outliers) is a useful caution for practitioners who might assume 'prettier' synthetic data is always better. Limitations include moderate computational cost (44 GPU-hours, 27 GB memory), dependence on ESGM mask quality under heavy occlusion, and the fact that DDPO's gains are modest and not uniformly positive across all metrics. Open questions include whether the shape-prior approach transfers to domains with less geometric regularity and whether the online-distillation recipe generalizes beyond RS to other conditional generation settings.
