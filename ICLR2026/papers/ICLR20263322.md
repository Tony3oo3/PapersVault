## Problem

Prior theoretical analyses of Neural Collapse (NC) have largely ignored the role of the optimizer, implicitly suggesting that NC is universal across optimization methods. This paper challenges that assumption by showing that the choice of optimizer—specifically whether weight decay is coupled (as in Adam) or decoupled (as in AdamW)—critically determines whether NC emerges, a distinction that has been overlooked in the literature.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the last-layer weight matrix W ∈ R^{K×P} and last-layer feature vectors h_n ∈ R^P extracted from a trained network, along with the optimizer configuration (type, learning rate, momentum, weight decay). The primary output is the novel NC0 diagnostic metric (the L2 norm of the row sum of W), whose convergence to zero is a necessary condition for NC, alongside the standard NC1–NC4 collapse metrics. Unlike prior NC metrics that plateau at small nonzero values under finite training, NC0 provides a definitive diagnostic: if it diverges, NC cannot occur even when other metrics misleadingly appear to converge.

## Method

The paper introduces NC0 (zero row sum of the last-layer weight) as a mathematically tractable necessary condition for NC, then proves that its training dynamics differ qualitatively across optimizers: under SGD with either coupled or decoupled weight decay, NC0 decays exponentially at a rate proportional to weight decay and momentum; under SignGD (a special case of AdamW) with decoupled weight decay, NC0 converges to a positive constant (K−2)²/λ²; and under SignGD with coupled weight decay (a special case of Adam), NC0 exhibits non-monotonic dynamics that vanish under learning-rate decay. Empirically, the authors conduct over 3,900 training runs across ResNet9, VGG9, and ViT architectures on MNIST, FashionMNIST, CIFAR-10, and ImageNet, varying optimizer, learning rate, momentum, and weight decay, confirming that coupled weight decay is necessary for NC in adaptive optimizers and that momentum accelerates NC beyond merely accelerating loss convergence.

## Results

Across 3,900+ training runs on ResNet9/VGG9 with MNIST, FashionMNIST, and CIFAR-10, the paper demonstrates that coupled weight decay is a necessary condition for Neural Collapse (NC) to emerge. In the main comparison (Table 2, ResNet9 on FashionMNIST), SGD achieves NC0=2.14e-04 and Adam achieves NC0=0.34, both indicating strong NC, while AdamW (NC0=5.33) and SignumW (NC0=3185.69) show NC0 values that are orders of magnitude larger and fail to converge to zero. Theoretical results confirm that SGD with either coupled or decoupled weight decay drives NC0 to zero exponentially, while SignGD with decoupled weight decay (a special case of AdamW) converges NC0 to a positive constant (K-2)²/λ².

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares six optimizers in pairs (coupled vs. decoupled weight decay): SGD/SGDW, Adam/AdamW, and Signum/SignumW. Adam (NC0=0.34, NC3=0.29) clearly outperforms AdamW (NC0=5.33, NC3=0.78) on NC emergence, with AdamW's NC0 actually increasing above its initialization value (≥100% change), indicating a complete failure of collapse. The gap is even more extreme for Signum vs. SignumW: Signum achieves NC0=0.78 while SignumW reaches NC0=3185.69, a four-order-of-magnitude difference. For SGD vs. SGDW, both achieve NC but SGD is substantially better (NC0=2.14e-04 vs. 0.55), consistent with the theoretical result that both converge exponentially but with different rates. Notably, in the best-run comparison (Table 3), AdamW still achieves the lowest NC1 (0.01) and NC2 (0.14) among all optimizers while its NC0 diverges to 8.09, revealing a 'partial NC' phenomenon where some collapse properties hold without full NC. The paper's key finding is that the difference between coupled and decoupled weight decay has a far more pronounced effect in adaptive optimizers (Adam/AdamW) than in SGD, suggesting a deeper interaction with adaptive update dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural collapse (NC) framework established by Papyan et al. (2020) and the theoretical analyses of Zhu et al. (2021) and Mixon et al. (2022), which studied NC under simplified models (unconstrained feature model, gradient flow) that implicitly assumed optimizer universality. It departs from this lineage by introducing NC0, a new necessary-condition metric that is analytically tractable, and by explicitly distinguishing coupled from decoupled weight decay—a distinction prior work treated as equivalent. The theoretical results (Theorems 3.1–3.4) build on the UFM framework but apply it to optimizer-specific update rules (SGD, SignGD) rather than continuous gradient flow, revealing qualitatively different NC0 dynamics depending on how weight decay is integrated into the update.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that AdamW systematically fails to produce NC while Adam does challenges the widely-held assumption that NC is an optimizer-agnostic property of training dynamics, and provides the first theoretical explanation for this optimizer dependence. The NC0 metric offers a practical diagnostic: if NC0 diverges, NC cannot occur, even when other NC metrics misleadingly appear small. Key limitations include the theoretical results being restricted to SignGD on the UFM (not full Adam/AdamW on deep networks), and the open question of whether partial NC (e.g., NC1 and NC2 holding while NC0 and NC3 fail, as observed with AdamW) has independent significance. Future work should extend the analysis to intermediate layers, larger architectures (ViTs, DenseNets), and newer optimizers (Lion, Muon, SOAP).
