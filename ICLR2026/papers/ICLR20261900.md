## Problem

Multi-Resolution Hash Encoding (MHE), the core of Instant-NGP, lacks rigorous spatial characterization from a physical systems perspective, forcing practitioners to rely on heuristics for hyperparameter selection. It is unclear what the effective spatial resolution of MHE actually is, how grid anisotropy manifests in the learned representation, or how hash collisions degrade reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261900/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The characterization framework takes 2D or 3D spatial coordinates and, under a linearized decoder assumption, measures the Point Spread Function (PSF) response to idealized point constraints, yielding a spatial kernel whose FWHM and anisotropy ratio quantify effective resolution. For downstream tasks, the MHE encoding produces feature vectors consumed by an MLP to output radiance/density (NeRF), signed distance values (SDF), or image pixel values (2D regression). The analysis operates in a sparse-supervision regime where the encoding's intrinsic spatial properties are isolated from decoder effects.

## Method

The paper derives a closed-form approximation for the collision-free MHE PSF by averaging the auto-correlation (cubic B-spline) of the multilinear interpolation kernel across L resolution levels, revealing a dominant logarithmic radial decay modulated by a direction-dependent anisotropy factor. A key counterintuitive finding is that the idealized FWHM scales with the average resolution N_avg rather than the finest N_max, and that gradient-based optimization (spectral bias) induces an additional isotropic broadening factor β_opt ≈ 2.54, yielding a total empirical broadening β_emp ≈ 3.0 that governs the practical two-point resolution limit. Hash collisions are modeled as introducing speckle-like side lobes whose variance grows with the load factor, degrading SNR. Leveraging these insights, the authors propose Rotated MHE (R-MHE), which applies a distinct rotation matrix R_l to input coordinates at each resolution level (progressive angles in 2D, polyhedral vertex orientations in 3D), averaging out grid anisotropy across orientations while preserving the exact parameter count and memory footprint of standard MHE.

## Results

On 2D gigapixel image regression, R-MHE (M=8) achieves 24.82±0.01 dB average PSNR, improving over Standard MHE (M=1) at 23.88±0.02 dB by +0.94 dB with zero additional parameters. On the Synthetic NeRF dataset (8 scenes), the best R-MHE variant (Icosahedron) reaches 35.479±0.134 dB versus 35.346±0.105 dB for Standard MHE under the baseline heuristic configuration. The PSF-guided theoretical growth factor (b_theory ≈ 1.38) matches the empirically optimized value (b_opt ≈ 1.3819) across all NeRF scenes, validating the physics-based hyperparameter selection. On 3D SDF benchmarks (Armadillo, Bunny, Spot), all methods saturate at ~0.9986 average IoU, indicating the encoding anisotropy is not the limiting factor at these resolutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Standard MHE (M=1, no rotation), the original Instant-NGP architecture. On 2D image regression, R-MHE consistently wins: M=2 gains +0.74 dB, M=4 gains +0.81 dB, and M=8 gains +0.94 dB over Standard MHE, with the improvement growing as more distinct orientations are sampled. On 3D NeRF, R-MHE variants (Tetra, Cube, Octa, Icosa) all marginally outperform Standard MHE by 0.09–0.13 dB under the baseline heuristic, but these gains fall within one standard deviation of the baseline variance, making them statistically marginal. On 3D SDF, all methods tie at 0.9986 average IoU due to performance saturation at the high resolutions used. The paper also validates that its PSF-derived b_theory (≈1.38) reproduces the empirically tuned b_opt (≈1.3819) for NeRF, confirming the theoretical framework matches the best heuristic without grid search.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Multi-Resolution Hash Encoding (MHE) from Instant-NGP (Müller et al., 2022), adopting its multi-level grid architecture and multilinear interpolation as the object of study. It departs from prior work by importing the Point Spread Function (PSF) framework from physical optics and signal processing, combined with a Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) linearization (Jacot et al., 2018) to isolate the encoding's intrinsic spatial response from the MLP decoder. The analysis draws on classical interpolation kernel theory (Keys, 1981; Thevenaz et al., 2000) to identify the cubic B-spline as the induced kernel, and on spectral bias literature (Rahaman et al., 2018) to explain optimization-induced broadening. Unlike prior work on explicit grids (TensoRF, K-Planes) or dictionary fields that modify the architecture, this paper provides a diagnostic lens applicable to the existing MHE and its variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PSF-based framework offers a principled, a priori method for selecting the growth factor b and other hyperparameters, replacing the ad hoc heuristics currently standard in Instant-NGP pipelines; the paper validates this by showing the theoretical b_theory consistently lands at the empirical optimum across NeRF scenes. The counterintuitive finding that effective resolution is governed by N_avg rather than N_max has practical implications for memory allocation strategies in resource-constrained settings. However, the practical gains from R-MHE are modest: +0.94 dB in 2D image regression but within one standard deviation in 3D NeRF, and zero in SDF where performance saturates. Open questions include whether the PSF methodology transfers cleanly to other grid-based encodings (multi-plane, planar factorizations) and whether R-MHE's benefits become more pronounced under strict memory constraints or sparse viewing geometries where anisotropy artifacts are less masked by ray integration.
