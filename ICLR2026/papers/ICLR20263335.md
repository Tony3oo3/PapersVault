## Problem

Pixel-aligned 3D reconstruction methods (DUSt3R, CUT3R, VGGT) tie geometry to per-ray predictions, which restricts output to visible surfaces and produces duplicated point layers in regions seen by multiple cameras. Existing complete-reconstruction approaches either operate only at the object level in canonical space with mesh supervision, or rely on diffusion-based view synthesis pipelines that do not guarantee physically meaningful point clouds. The paper addresses the gap of feed-forward, pose-free, scene-level 3D reconstruction that recovers both visible and occluded geometry without pixel alignment.

## Input / Output

The model takes K unposed RGB images (no camera intrinsics or extrinsics required) and produces a single complete 3D point cloud P ∈ R^{N×3} expressed in the first input view's coordinate frame. The point cloud is non-pixel-aligned: N points (default 10,000) represent the entire scene within the input frustum rather than K×H×W back-projected rays, and N can be adjusted at inference for resolution-agnostic generation. The model supports an arbitrary number of input views, though training uses 1–2 views due to compute constraints.

## Method

NOVA3R uses a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 trains a 3D latent autoencoder: a transformer encoder (built on TripoSG, 8 self-attention + 1 cross-attention layers) compresses a complete point cloud into M=768 latent scene tokens via farthest-point-sampled queries, and a 3-block flow-matching decoder (joint self-attention + cross-attention) reconstructs the point cloud from noisy queries conditioned on those tokens, trained with a flow-matching loss that handles the unordered nature of point sets. Stage 2 maps unposed images to the same latent space: a VGGT-based image encoder is augmented with M learnable scene tokens that aggregate multi-view information through frame-level and global self-attention, producing scene tokens that condition the frozen Stage-1 flow-matching decoder. The key architectural insight is that learnable scene tokens serve as a view-agnostic, fixed-length global representation decoupled from pixel alignment, enabling complete reconstruction without per-ray supervision and avoiding duplicated geometry in overlapping regions.

## Results

On the SCRREAM scene completion benchmark (K=1), NOVA3R achieves CD=0.048 and FS@0.1=0.882, outperforming the best multi-view baseline VGGT (CD=0.070, FS@0.1=0.810) and the single-view amodal method LaRI (CD=0.059, FS@0.1=0.825). For two-view complete reconstruction (K=2), NOVA3R reaches CD=0.053 and FS@0.1=0.862, again surpassing VGGT (CD=0.065, FS@0.1=0.821). On object completion (GSO, K=1), NOVA3R achieves CD=0.020 and FS@0.1=0.985, beating LaRI (CD=0.025, FS@0.1=0.966) and TRELLIS (CD=0.025, FS@0.1=0.962). NOVA3R also reduces the hole area ratio from ~0.31 (pixel-aligned baselines) to 0.088 and cuts density variance from ~7.1 to 5.1, demonstrating more physically plausible geometry.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NOVA3R outperforms all pixel-aligned multi-view baselines (DUSt3R, CUT3R, VGGT) on complete scene reconstruction: on SCRREAM K=1, it reduces CD from 0.070 (VGGT) to 0.048 and improves FS@0.1 from 0.810 to 0.882; on K=2, CD drops from 0.065 to 0.053. Against the closest amodal competitor LaRI (single-view only), NOVA3R achieves lower CD (0.048 vs. 0.059) and higher FS@0.1 (0.882 vs. 0.825) on scene completion, while also supporting multi-view input. On object completion (GSO), NOVA3R beats both LaRI (CD 0.020 vs. 0.025) and TRELLIS (CD 0.020 vs. 0.025) at K=1, and extends to K=2 where it outperforms TRELLIS (CD 0.023 vs. 0.028). On visible-surface reconstruction (7-Scenes, K=2), NOVA3R matches VGGT on accuracy (0.041 vs. 0.042) while achieving notably better completion (0.033 vs. 0.045) using fewer tokens (768 vs. 2738). Object-level baselines TripoSG and TRELLIS, not trained on scene data, perform substantially worse on scene completion (CD > 0.24), confirming the gap between object and scene reconstruction. NOVA3R also dramatically reduces the hole area ratio (0.088 vs. 0.307 for VGGT) and density variance (5.127 vs. 7.105), quantifying its advantage in producing complete, physically plausible geometry without duplicated points.

## Relation to Prior Work

NOVA3R departs from the dominant pixel-aligned reconstruction paradigm (DUSt3R, CUT3R, VGGT), which ties geometry to per-ray predictions and inherently produces incomplete, duplicated point clouds in overlapping regions. It also diverges from latent 3D generation methods (TRELLIS, TripoSG) that operate in canonical object space and require high-quality mesh supervision, by targeting scene-level reconstruction with point-cloud-only supervision. The architecture bridges these two lines: it reuses VGGT's feed-forward image encoder and TripoSG's point encoder, but introduces learnable scene tokens and a flow-matching decoder to produce a global, view-agnostic, non-pixel-aligned point cloud that captures both visible and occluded geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The non-pixel-aligned formulation addresses a fundamental structural limitation of the current feed-forward 3D reconstruction paradigm: pixel-aligned methods cannot represent occluded regions and produce physically implausible multi-layer geometry in co-visible areas. By decoupling reconstruction from pixel alignment, NOVA3R enables a single unified model for both object and scene completion without mesh supervision. Key open questions include scaling to large multi-view scenes (currently limited to 2 views and small training sets), handling dynamic content, and whether the flow-matching decoder's inference cost (~3s vs 0.5s for Chamfer-based) can be reduced. The approach also raises the broader question of whether pixel alignment is ever necessary for high-fidelity 3D reconstruction.
