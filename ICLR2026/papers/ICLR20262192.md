## Problem

Existing domain generalization methods rely on feature-level invariance and overlook the reasoning processes that underlie generalizable decision-making. While multimodal LLMs offer strong reasoning capabilities, directly distilling class-relevant reasoning chains into MLLMs for classification is challenging: reasoning-chain supervision is harder to optimize than direct label prediction, and reasoning patterns from large commercial models (e.g., GPT-4o) mismatch those of the fine-tuned target model, creating a trade-off between semantic richness and optimization efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image paired with a classification question listing candidate labels (e.g., 'What type of object is in this photo? Choose from the following options: bird, bobcat, cat, …') and produces both a final class label and a structured five-section reasoning chain (<SUMMARY>, <CAPTION>, <REASONING>, <REFLECTION>, <CONCLUSION>). Reasoning chains are generated by GPT-4o without access to ground-truth labels and filtered via rejection sampling to ensure coherence; the model is fine-tuned using LoRA adapters (rank 8) on both the vision encoder and language decoder, keeping the base MLLM (InternVL3-8B or LLaVA-1.5-7B) largely frozen.

## Method

RD-MLDG integrates reasoning into DG through two complementary modules. First, Multi-Task Cross-Training (MTCT) jointly optimizes a direct classification pathway (a 'no-thinking' prompt that predicts the label) and a reasoning-chain pathway (a prompt that generates the full five-section chain), so the simple classification loss acts as a stable anchor that guides the harder-to-fit reasoning supervision. Second, Self-Aligned Reasoning Regularization (SARR) performs iterative self-labeling: the model generates its own reasoning chains, retains only those whose <CONCLUSION> matches the ground-truth label, and fine-tunes on these self-generated chains for N=3 rounds, progressively replacing GPT-4o-style supervision with model-native reasoning that is easier to fit while preserving class-relevant semantic detail. The combined loss in each stage is a sum of the classification cross-entropy and a length-normalized reasoning-chain generation loss, trained with AdamW (lr=5e-4, batch 128, 3 epochs) on 4×A100 GPUs.

## Results

On the standard DomainBed multi-source DG benchmark, RD-MLDG (built on InternVL3-8B) achieves an average accuracy of 86.89% across PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome, and TerraIncognita, surpassing the strongest commercial MLLM GPT-4o (83.46%) by 3.43%p and the best CLIP-based method DGCLDTP (83.19%) by 3.70%p. Per-dataset, RD-MLDG sets new bests on VLCS (87.03%), OfficeHome (91.73%), and TerraIncognita (70.65%), with the largest margin on TerraIncognita (+10.16%p over GPT-4o). In the single-domain generalization setting on OfficeHome, RD-MLDG reaches 89.37% average accuracy, outperforming the best prior method PAPT (62.03%) by a substantial 27.34%p.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RD-MLDG outperforms all 41 compared baselines across all four DomainBed datasets. Against the strongest commercial MLLM GPT-4o (83.46% avg), RD-MLDG gains +3.43%p overall, with the largest per-dataset margin on TerraIncognita (+10.16%p: 70.65% vs. 60.49%) and OfficeHome (+1.61%p: 91.73% vs. 90.12%). Compared to the best CLIP/ViT-based DG method DGCLDTP (83.19%), RD-MLDG leads by +3.70%p, and against the prior best MLLM baseline InternVL3-8B zero-shot (78.47%), the gain is +8.42%p. In the single-domain generalization setting (Table 6), RD-MLDG (89.37% avg on OfficeHome) surpasses the best prior method PAPT (62.03%) by 27.34%p, and in the base-to-new class generalization on FGVC-Aircraft, it improves harmonic-mean accuracy from 19.01% (InternVL3-8B) to 34.63%. On the VOLDOGER VQA/VE benchmark, RD-MLDG with InternVL3-8B reaches 77.28% (VQA) and 72.09% (VE), surpassing GPT-4 (79.56% VQA, 71.85% VE) on VE and remaining competitive on VQA, while LLaVA-1.5-7B+RD-MLDG (71.62% VQA, 70.13% VE) outperforms most open-source MLLMs including BLIP2-FlanT5-XL and PaliGemma.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant DG paradigm of enforcing feature-level invariance (IRM, CORAL, MixStyle, SWAD, CLIP-based prompt methods) by introducing class-relevant reasoning chains as a complementary, process-level signal. It builds on LLaVA-CoT's structured chain-of-thought format (adding a reflection stage) and on the broader MLLM fine-tuning literature (LoRA-based SFT), but is the first to systematically study how reasoning supervision interacts with domain shift. The DomainBed-Reasoning dataset extends the standard DomainBed benchmark, and the two-stage training (MTCT + SARR) draws on multi-task learning and self-labeling ideas, but their specific application to DG is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first quantitative evidence (via MMD) that reasoning-chain embeddings are substantially more domain-invariant than visual embeddings (58.6% reduction in cross-domain divergence), offering a principled justification for reasoning as a DG signal. However, the approach depends on GPT-4o for initial chain generation and on a specific five-section format, raising questions about scalability to open-ended or multi-step reasoning tasks. An open question is whether the gains persist when the reasoning chains are generated by a model of comparable size to the target, or when the task requires compositional rather than attribute-based reasoning (the FGVC-Aircraft base-to-new results, while positive, remain at low absolute accuracy ~35%).
