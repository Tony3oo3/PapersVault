## Problem

Offline change point localization and inference in dynamic multilayer networks had not been previously addressed. While online detection in dynamic multilayer random dot product graphs (D-MRDPGs) and offline detection in single-layer networks existed, no method combined offline localization with statistical inference (limiting distributions and confidence intervals) in the multilayer dynamic setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261076/cropped_page24_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of T binary adjacency tensors of dimension n×n×L (nodes × nodes × layers) generated from a D-MRDPG model with shared latent positions and time-varying layer-specific weight matrices, along with a user-specified threshold τ and a collection of seeded intervals. It produces the estimated number of change points K̃, refined location estimates η̃_k for each change point, and (1−α) confidence intervals constructed by simulating the limiting Brownian-motion-based distribution. The theoretical guarantees require the minimal spacing Δ between change points to scale as Θ(T), and the SNR condition κ√Δ ≥ C·log(T)·√(nL^{1/2} + d²m_max + nd + Lm_max).

## Method

The proposed two-stage algorithm first applies seeded binary segmentation (SBS) with CUSUM statistics on two independent tensor sequences to generate coarse change point candidates, then refines each candidate via tensor heteroskedastic principal component analysis (TH-PCA) applied to the expected CUSUM-transformed adjacency tensor, exploiting its Tucker low-rank structure (rank d in node modes, rank m_t in the layer mode). A final refinement step minimizes a penalized least-squares criterion over a local window to obtain the final estimator η̂_k. The key theoretical contribution is deriving limiting distributions for η̂_k: in the vanishing-jump regime (κ_k→0), κ_k²(η̂_k−η_k) converges to the argmin of a two-sided Brownian motion functional, while in the non-vanishing regime (κ_k→ρ_k>0), η̂_k−η_k converges to the argmin of a two-sided random walk. These limiting distributions enable a fully data-driven confidence interval procedure via Monte Carlo simulation of the Brownian motion with estimated variances.

## Results

Across four simulation scenarios (T=200, L=4 layers, n∈{50,100}), CPDmrdpg achieves near-perfect change point estimation: |K̂−K| ≤ 0.19 and time segment coverage ≥ 95.11% in all scenarios, with 100% coverage in Scenarios 1, 2, and 4 at n=100. The reverse Hausdorff distance d(C|Ĉ) (measuring missed change points) is ≤ 0.42 for CPDmrdpg versus infinite values for gSeg in most scenarios. On the real-world FAO agricultural trade network (T=35, n=75, L=4), the method identifies four change points (1991, 1999, 2005, 2013) with 95% confidence intervals of width ≤ 0.08 time units, aligning with known geopolitical events.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPDmrdpg substantially outperforms gSeg (Chen & Zhang, 2015) across all scenarios: gSeg produces infinite reverse Hausdorff distances (indicating complete failure to detect true change points) and coverage as low as 0% in Scenario 4, while CPDmrdpg maintains coverage ≥ 95.11%. Against kerSeg (Song & Chen, 2024), CPDmrdpg wins on the reverse Hausdorff distance d(C|Ĉ) in every scenario (e.g., 0.02 vs. 2.65 in Scenario 4), indicating far fewer missed change points, though kerSeg(nets.) edges out CPDmrdpg on d(Ĉ|C) in Scenario 3 (0.18 vs. 9.64) where Model 1 assumptions are violated. The online D-MRDPG method of Wang et al. (2025) and the deep-learning approach of Li et al. (2024) are compared in supplementary experiments (Appendix G.1), where CPDmrdpg also demonstrates superior performance. On the real FAO trade data, gSeg fails to detect any change points after 2010, kerSeg produces two temporally implausible detections (1990 and 1992), while CPDmrdpg identifies four well-separated change points matching known policy events. The only scenario where CPDmrdpg does not dominate all metrics is Scenario 3 (layer-specific community size changes violating Model 1), where kerSeg(nets.) achieves slightly better forward Hausdorff distance and coverage, though CPDmrdpg still provides a much tighter reverse distance (0.14 vs. 2.06).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the offline change point detection framework for single-layer dynamic networks (Wang et al., 2021; Padilla et al., 2022) to the multilayer setting, and complements the online D-MRDPG change point detection of Wang et al. (2025) by addressing the offline problem. The two-stage algorithm combines seeded binary segmentation (Kovács et al., 2023) with tensor heteroskedastic PCA (Han et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2022) for low-rank tensor estimation, adapting CUSUM-based scan statistics to the multilayer tensor structure. The key departure from prior work is the use of Tucker low-rank structure in the expected CUSUM-transformed adjacency tensors to enable provably consistent localization and, for the first time in the network literature, derivation of limiting distributions for refined change point estimators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first offline change point localization and inference procedure for dynamic multilayer networks, filling a gap between single-layer offline methods and multilayer online methods. The derivation of limiting distributions (two-sided Brownian motion in the vanishing-jump regime, two-sided random walk in the non-vanishing regime) and the accompanying data-driven confidence interval construction are novel contributions to network change point inference. Key limitations include the assumption Δ = Θ(T) (precluding frequent change points), restriction of inference to vanishing jumps, and the temporal independence assumption; the authors outline extensions to temporal dependence and time-varying latent positions but leave practical procedures for the non-vanishing regime open.
