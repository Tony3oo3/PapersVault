## Problem

Existing machine unlearning methods are predominantly evaluated on balanced forget sets, overlooking the common real-world scenario where data to be forgotten (e.g., a user's activity records) follows a long-tailed distribution. Under such imbalanced forget sets, the paper identifies two critical failure modes: Heterogeneous Unlearning Deviation (models over-forget tail-class samples while under-forgetting head-class samples) and Skewed Unlearning Deviation (the magnitude of deviation is disproportionately severe for tail classes). No prior work has systematically studied or addressed these instance-level deviations in long-tailed unlearning.

## Input / Output

FaLW operates on a pre-trained classifier (ResNet-18 or VGG-16) and requires four data sources: the forget set D_f with a power-law class distribution N_k ∝ k^{-γ}, the retain set D_r, and a held-out validation set D_val used to estimate per-class predictive probability statistics (μ_c, σ_c) at each training step. The method outputs updated model parameters θ_u and, as an intermediate artifact, per-instance dynamic loss weights w_i ∈ [0, 2] that are recomputed every batch. The validation set is consumed only for distribution estimation (not for gradient computation), making it a lightweight auxiliary requirement.

## Method

FaLW is a plug-and-play, instance-wise dynamic loss reweighting module that modulates the forget loss in any gradient-based unlearning objective. For each forget sample (x_i, y_i=c), it computes the model's current predictive probability p_i on the true class and compares it against a Gaussian target distribution N(μ_c, σ_c²) estimated from the model's predictions on unseen validation data of class c, yielding a z-score z_i = (p_i − μ_c)/σ_c. The forgetting-aware weight w_i = 1 + sign(z_i)·(tanh(|z_i|))^{1/η} pushes under-forgotten samples (positive z_i) toward weight 2 (intensifying forgetting) and over-forgotten samples (negative z_i) toward weight 0 (halting forgetting). To counteract the skewed deviation, a class-aware balance factor B_i = (N_f/(C·N_{f,k}))^τ is introduced to modulate the exponent, making the weight response more aggressive for tail-class samples with fewer forget-set members. The final weighted forget loss is combined with the standard retain loss and parameter constraint terms, and the entire scheme can be grafted onto existing baselines (RL, GA, SalUn, SFRon) without modifying their core unlearning gradients.

## Results

On Tiny-ImageNet (VGG-16, γ=1, 10% forget), FaLW achieves an Avg. Gap of 0.35 vs. Retrain, substantially outperforming SalUn (2.45) and SFRon (3.68). On CIFAR-10 (VGG-16, γ=1/2, 40% forget), FaLW achieves an Avg. Gap of 0.40, again far ahead of SalUn (2.14) and SFRon (2.93). Across all 9 baselines on both settings, FaLW consistently attains the lowest Avg. Gap, with Forgetting Accuracy within 0.1–0.3 points of the Retrain gold standard while maintaining Retain Accuracy at 99.98–99.99%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FaLW outperforms all 9 baselines on Avg. Gap in both main settings. Against the closest competitor SalUn, FaLW reduces the gap from 2.45 to 0.35 on Tiny-ImageNet (a 7× improvement) and from 2.14 to 0.40 on CIFAR-10 (a 5× improvement), primarily by correcting SalUn's over-forgetting (FA 22.68 vs. Retrain's 25.63) to near-faithful levels (25.70). SFRon, the second-best baseline, still lags FaLW by 3.33 and 2.53 in Avg. Gap on the two settings respectively, with its Retain Accuracy dropping to 97.10% versus FaLW's 99.98%. Gradient-based baselines (FT, GA, IU) and boundary-based methods (BE, BS) suffer catastrophic over-forgetting, with Avg. Gaps of 26–38 on Tiny-ImageNet, while L1-Sparse and RL show moderate gaps (3.69–38.07) but fail to match FaLW's consistency. The plug-and-play analysis (Table 7) further confirms that integrating FaLW into RL, GA, SFRon, and SalUn each reduces their Avg. Gap, with the largest gain for RL (5.70→1.17) and SalUn (2.44→0.33).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient-based approximate machine unlearning line (SalUn, SFRon, GA, RL) by identifying that all prior methods are evaluated on balanced forget sets, while real-world deletion requests (e.g., a user's activity history) naturally follow long-tailed distributions. Conceptually, it borrows the re-weighting philosophy from long-tailed learning (Cui et al., Cao et al.) but repurposes it for the unlearning objective: instead of up-weighting tail-class samples to improve their accuracy, FaLW dynamically adjusts per-sample forgetting intensity based on how far the model's current confidence deviates from a target distribution estimated on unseen data. The method is explicitly designed as a plug-and-play module that can be grafted onto existing unlearning algorithms rather than replacing them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of Heterogeneous and Skewed Unlearning Deviation under long-tailed forget sets fills a genuine evaluation gap: prior SOTA claims may not transfer to the imbalanced deletion scenarios that actually arise in practice (GDPR-style user data removal). The plug-and-play design lowers adoption friction, and the balance factor provides a principled knob for tail-class sensitivity. However, the method's reliance on a held-out validation set to estimate per-class (μ_c, σ_c) limits applicability in data-constrained settings, and the paper does not explore whether the Gaussian assumption or the z-score formulation is necessary. Open questions include whether the approach extends to generative models, multi-modal unlearning, or settings where no validation data is available.
