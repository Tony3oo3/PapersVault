## Problem

Diffusion models produce blurry, artifact-laden outputs when sampling with fewer neural function evaluations (NFEs) or lower classifier-free guidance (CFG) scales, yet existing samplers still require ~50 steps for acceptable quality and high CFG scales cause oversaturation and reduced diversity. No training-free method existed that could simultaneously improve generation quality under both low-NFE and low-CFG regimes without additional forward passes or retraining.

## Input / Output

HiGS is a plug-and-play modification to the inference loop of any pretrained diffusion model: it consumes the model's own past predictions (CFG-guided or conditional) as a running history buffer and produces a corrected denoiser output at each sampling step, which is then fed to the standard sampler. It operates on latent representations (e.g., 256×256 ImageNet latents, or Stable Diffusion latents at various resolutions) and requires no extra network evaluations, no retraining, and no changes to the model architecture. The method is compatible with arbitrary samplers (Euler, DDIM, DPM++, PLMS, UniPC), guidance variants (CFG, APG, autoguidance), and distilled models (SDXL-Flash, SDXL-Lightning).

## Method

HiGS reinterprets the Euler sampling step as gradient descent on a time-varying energy function and augments it with a momentum-based correction inspired by STORM variance reduction. At each step, it computes the difference between the current CFG prediction and an EMA-weighted average of all past predictions (the history signal), then applies three refinements: (1) optional orthogonal projection to suppress the parallel component that causes oversaturation, (2) a DCT-based sigmoid high-pass filter to remove low-frequency color shifts, and (3) a square-root time schedule that concentrates the correction in early-to-middle sampling steps. Theoretically, this history term reduces the Euler solver's local truncation error from O(h²) to O(h³) and global error from O(h) to O(h²), explaining why fewer steps suffice. The entire procedure adds negligible computation (no extra forward passes) and is fully training-free.

## Results

HiGS achieves a new state-of-the-art FID of 1.61 for unguided ImageNet 256×256 generation using SiT-XL + REPA-E with only 30 sampling steps (vs. 250 for the baseline FID of 1.83). For guided generation, HiGS matches the 250-step CFG baseline (FID 1.26) in just 40 steps (FID 1.32). Across text-to-image models (SDXL, SD3, SD3.5), HiGS consistently improves HPSv2 by 0.01–0.03 and ImageReward by 0.07–0.19 while maintaining identical CLIP scores, with HPSv2 win rates of 79–96% over the CFG baseline. The method adds zero additional forward passes, keeping inference speed at ~6.50 iterations/second.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiGS outperforms the standard CFG baseline across all tested models and samplers. On SiT-XL+REPA (Table 1), HiGS reduces FID from 12.08 to 4.86 and boosts IS from 187.11 to 277.20; on DiT-XL/2, FID drops from 8.73 to 7.15. The headline result is on REPA-E where HiGS achieves FID 1.61 unguided in 30 steps, beating the 250-step unguided baseline (FID 1.83) while using 88% fewer NFEs. For distilled models, HiGS improves SDXL-Flash ImageReward from 0.774 to 0.864 and HPSv2 from 0.273 to 0.298 (97% win rate), and SDXL-Lightning ImageReward from 0.63 to 0.66 (82% win rate). Across all five samplers tested (DDIM, DPM++, DDPM, PLMS, UniPC) on DiT-XL/2, HiGS consistently improves FID by 0.4–7.6 points, confirming complementarity with multistep solvers. The only minor trade-off is a slight Recall decrease in some settings (e.g., REPA-E unguided: 0.66→0.62), while Precision and IS improve.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiGS adapts the momentum-based variance reduction principle from STORM (Cutkosky & Orabona, 2019) to the diffusion sampling ODE, interpreting each Euler step as gradient descent on a time-varying energy function and augmenting it with a history-weighted correction term. It also builds on the CFG formulation (Ho & Salimans, 2022) by reinterpreting the guidance update as gradient ascent and adding a multi-step EMA of past predictions as a momentum signal. Unlike ODE solver improvements (DPM-Solver, EDM) that require specific training objectives, or distillation methods that demand expensive student training, HiGS is entirely training-free and complementary to both. It can be viewed as a training-free analogue of autoguidance (Karras et al., 2024), where the 'worse model' is implicitly defined by the model's own earlier (blurrier) predictions rather than a separately trained network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HiGS offers a genuinely plug-and-play improvement: no retraining, no extra forward passes, and seamless integration with existing samplers, distilled models, and CFG variants (APG, guidance interval). The 8× reduction in sampling steps (250→30) while simultaneously improving FID on ImageNet is a practically significant efficiency gain that could lower inference costs for production text-to-image systems. The method also reduces reliance on high CFG scales, mitigating oversaturation and diversity loss. Open questions include whether the DCT filtering and projection steps generalize to video or 3D generation, whether the hyperparameters (α, t_min, t_max, w_HiGS) can be made fully automatic, and whether the O(h³) truncation error improvement translates to further gains with adaptive step-size solvers.
