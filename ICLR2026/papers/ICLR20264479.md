## Problem

Video diffusion models trained on uncurated web data inevitably learn unsafe concepts (nudity, gore, copyrighted characters), but existing unlearning approaches for video are either inference-time filtering (bypassable with weight access) or costly fine-tuning prone to catastrophic forgetting. No training-free, closed-form weight-update method existed for video diffusion models, leaving open-weight video generators without a practical, permanent sanitization mechanism.

## Input / Output

The method requires only five paired safe/unsafe prompt pairs (e.g., 'a naked woman with blonde hair' vs. 'a woman with blonde hair') and a set of ~1000 neutral prompts generated via GPT-based sampling. It operates on a pre-trained video diffusion model (Open-Sora or ZeroScopeT2V) and produces a modified weight matrix for the cross-attention FFN layers (layers 17–18 in Open-Sora), requiring approximately 0.4 GPU-hours and ~$1 in compute (10 forward passes). The output is a permanently updated model that generates safe videos at inference with zero additional latency, evaluated on 128-frame videos at 8 fps.

## Method

The method computes a refusal vector as the mean difference between unsafe and safe latent activations at a selected layer, then refines it via contrastive PCA (cPCA) that subtracts the covariance of neutral prompts from the covariance of the unsafe-safe differences, yielding a low-rank subspace (rank k=100) that isolates the target concept from unrelated semantics. The correction projects the input into this subspace, subtracts the component aligned with the refusal direction, and projects back. The key technical contribution is showing that this subspace-aware correction can be algebraically absorbed into the weights of the subsequent linear layer as a closed-form update W̃ = W(I − λU_k r̂r̂ᵀ/‖r̂‖² U_kᵀ), making the unlearning permanent at the parameter level with no retraining or inference overhead. Unlike prior image-domain methods (UCE, RECE) that act only on text-conditioned cross-attention, this approach jointly leverages both text and image conditioning inputs to more accurately capture the target concept in the spatio-temporal video setting.

## Results

On T2VSafetyBench with OPEN-SORA, the method reduces average unsafe generation from 48.9% (baseline) to 12.6%, outperforming NullSCE (25.5%) by 12.9 percentage points, while FVD remains stable (160.36 vs. 164.53 baseline) and MM-Notox improves from 20.87 to 20.31. On SafeSora with ZeroScopeT2V, average censorship drops from 68.1% to 9.9%, surpassing SAFREE (43.1%) by 33.2 percentage points, with FVD unchanged (69.02 vs. 69.44) and MM-Notox improving from 22.60 to 22.10. The largest single-category reduction is 69.6% on the Gore category (74.9% → 5.3%). The entire update requires only 5 safe/unsafe prompt pairs and ~0.4 GPU-hours (~$1), compared to ~1k GPU-hours for fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NullSCE (Yi et al., 2025), a fine-tuning-based sequential concept erasure method, the proposed approach achieves substantially lower censorship rates across all five T2VSafetyBench categories (12.6% vs. 25.5% average), with the gap largest on Gore (5.3% vs. not reported) and Copyright & Trademarks (33.0% vs. 48.0%). NullSCE requires per-concept retraining, whereas the proposed method is training-free with a closed-form weight update. Against SAFREE (Yoon et al., 2025), an inference-time token-level filtering guard, the method reduces average censorship from 43.1% to 9.9% on SafeSora (a 33.2 pp improvement), while SAFREE cannot permanently alter model weights and is bypassable. Against UCE (Gandikota et al., 2024) adapted to the T2V setting (Appendix F), the proposed method achieves comparable censorship on the Sexual category (9.1% vs. 8.9%) but with dramatically better video quality: FVD of 63.51 vs. 103.3 (a >60% increase for UCE) and lower MM-Notox (22.62 vs. 23.43), confirming that image-model editing methods degrade temporal coherence when naively transferred to video.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the refusal-vector concept from LLM interpretability (Arditi et al., 2024) to video diffusion models, extending it from a single activation direction to a low-rank subspace refined via contrastive PCA (Abid et al., 2018). It departs from image-domain closed-form editing methods (UCE, RECE) that rely on CLIP-style text encoders and frame-independent architectures, and from video-specific approaches that either filter at inference (SAFREE, VideoEraser) or require fine-tuning (NullSCE). The key conceptual departure is jointly leveraging both text and image conditioning signals to estimate the refusal direction, and absorbing the correction into model weights via a closed-form update rather than applying it at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides a practical, near-zero-cost path to sanitizing open-weight video models before release, which is increasingly important as open-source video generators approach commercial quality. The 5-pair data requirement and ~$1 compute cost make it accessible, but the method's applicability is constrained to architectures where the relevant cross-attention FFN weights can be directly modified, and its adversarial robustness is limited (4% leakage after targeted prompt optimization on previously fully-censored prompts). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger, more complex video models (e.g., Sora-class), whether sequential multi-concept erasure degrades quality at scale, and whether the closed-form update remains stable under subsequent fine-tuning or LoRA adaptation.
