## Problem

Existing optimization-based jailbreak methods rely on either sparse binary ASR signals or fixed, manually crafted scoring templates to guide prompt refinement, both of which provide suboptimal optimization feedback. The paper identifies that the evaluation mechanism itself—the scoring template used by the LLM judge—has been treated as static, yet its quality critically determines how effectively prompts are refined. AMIS addresses this by jointly evolving both jailbreak prompts and scoring templates through meta-optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset of harmful text queries (e.g., 50 from AdvBench or 100 from JBB-Behaviors) along with an initial set of C=10 benign-looking prefix prompts and an initial fine-grained scoring template (1.0–10.0 scale, 0.5 resolution, from AutoDAN-Turbo). It produces two co-evolved outputs: (1) optimized jailbreak prompts per query that elicit harmful responses from the target LLM, and (2) a refined scoring template whose continuous scores are calibrated to align with true binary ASR outcomes. The ASR alignment score used in the outer loop is computed on a [0, 100] scale, measuring how closely each template-assigned score matches the ideal value for the observed attack success/failure label.

## Method

AMIS employs a bilevel meta-optimization structure. In the inner loop (query-level, L=5 iterations), an attacker LLM (Llama-3.1-8B) iteratively generates M=5 new candidate jailbreak prompts conditioned on the top-K=5 previously scored prompts, while a judge LLM (GPT-4o-mini) scores each prompt-response pair using the current fine-grained scoring template on a 1–10 scale; only the top-K prompts are retained at each step. In the outer loop (dataset-level, L'=5 iterations), the scoring template itself is optimized: the ASR alignment score (Eq. 6–7) measures how consistently the template's continuous scores align with ground-truth binary ASR labels across all logged prompt-response-score triplets from the inner loop, and a template-optimizer LLM generates a new template conditioned on the full history of previously used templates and their alignment scores. Between outer iterations, prompt inheritance preserves the top-C/2 prompts from the prior round alongside C/2 fresh prefixes, enabling cumulative quality improvement. The key insight is that calibrating the evaluation signal to true attack outcomes produces denser, more reliable optimization feedback than either binary ASR or a fixed rubric, yielding substantially higher ASR (88% on Claude-3.5-Haiku, 100% on Claude-4-Sonnet on AdvBench).

## Results

On AdvBench, AMIS achieves 100% ASR on Llama-3.1-8B, GPT-4o, and Claude-4-Sonnet, 98% on GPT-4o-mini, and 88% on Claude-3.5-Haiku, with corresponding StrongREJECT (StR) scores of 0.84, 0.87, 0.87, 0.42, and 0.70. On JBB-Behaviors, AMIS reaches 100% ASR on Llama-3.1-8B and GPT-4o-mini, 97% on GPT-4o, 88% on Claude-4-Sonnet, and 78% on Claude-3.5-Haiku. Compared with the second-best baseline, AMIS improves ASR by an average of 26.0% (AdvBench) and 20.2% (JBB-Behaviors), while also achieving substantially higher StR scores (average gains of 0.44 and 0.41, respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMIS outperforms all seven baselines across every target model and both benchmarks. On the most challenging target, Claude-4-Sonnet (AdvBench), AMIS achieves 100% ASR versus 28% (PAIR), 22% (TAP), 6% (PAP), 8% (SeqAR), and 38% (AutoDAN-Turbo) — a gap of over 60 percentage points over the strongest baseline. On Claude-3.5-Haiku, AMIS reaches 88% ASR compared to 46% (PAIR/TAP) and 42% (AutoDAN-Turbo), a 42-point margin. The two additional baselines evaluated in the appendix, Rainbow Teaming (max 38% ASR at 500 iterations) and ActorBreaker (max 48% ASR with GPT-4o-mini attacker), are also substantially outperformed. Notably, AMIS also achieves the highest StR scores in nearly all settings, indicating that its jailbreaks are not only more successful but also produce more specific and convincing harmful responses. The advantage is consistent across both open-weight (Llama-3.1-8B) and closed-source (GPT-4o, Claude) models, confirming generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

AMIS extends the LLM-based iterative jailbreak optimization lineage (PAIR, TAP, AutoDAN-Turbo) by introducing a bilevel meta-optimization structure that co-evolves both jailbreak prompts and the scoring template used to evaluate them. Prior methods treated the evaluation signal as fixed—either a sparse binary ASR label (SeqAR) or a manually crafted fine-grained rubric (AutoDAN-Turbo's 1–10 template, which AMIS adopts as its initialization). The key departure is the outer loop, which uses a proposed ASR alignment score to iteratively refine the scoring template so its continuous ratings better predict true binary attack outcomes, effectively treating the judge as an optimizable component rather than a static oracle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central insight—that the quality of the evaluation signal is a first-order bottleneck in LLM-based jailbreak optimization, not just the prompt search strategy—reframes how the community should think about red-teaming pipelines. Achieving 100% ASR on Claude-4-Sonnet and 88% on Claude-3.5-Haiku with a cost-efficient trajectory underscores both the severity of current alignment gaps and the practical utility of adaptive evaluation. Limitations include reliance on a single LLM-as-judge (GPT-4o-mini) for both scoring and ASR ground truth, non-trivial computational cost from nested loops, and the observation that the outer-loop gain over a fixed template is modest in absolute terms (+2% ASR), suggesting diminishing returns. Open questions include whether the co-optimization paradigm generalizes to other LLM safety tasks (e.g., alignment auditing, robustness evaluation) and whether the scoring template converges to a stable optimum or continues drifting.
