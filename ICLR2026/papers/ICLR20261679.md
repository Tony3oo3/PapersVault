## Problem

Generalizing local navigation policies across diverse robot morphologies is hindered by three intertwined issues: the need for costly embodiment-specific data, the tight coupling of high-level planning with low-level dynamics, and the 'disastrous averaging' problem where deterministic regression fails to capture inherently multi-modal navigation decisions (e.g., choosing left vs. right at a T-junction). Existing hierarchical methods decouple planning from control but assume an ideal velocity tracker, leaving unmodeled dynamic effects and controller tracking errors uncompensated.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a 360-degree 2D LiDAR raycast (144 rays, 4 m range, derived from an onboard depth camera or laser sensor) concatenated with a 7-D body-frame state vector (normalized goal direction, current linear and angular velocities, and Euclidean distance to goal). The LiDAR is processed by a 3-layer CNN and the state by a 2-layer MLP, producing a 256-D embedding that conditions the generative model. The output is a 3-D body velocity command (v_x, v_y, v_yaw), produced as a normalized vector in [0,1] and scaled by embodiment-specific velocity limits, serving as a reactive interface to any low-level locomotion controller.

## Method

CE-Nav is a two-stage IL-then-RL framework that decouples embodiment-agnostic geometric reasoning from embodiment-specific dynamic adaptation. In Stage 1, a conditional normalizing flow (VelFlow, a Real-NVP with 12 coupling layers, hidden dim 512) is trained offline via negative log-likelihood on 10 M state-action pairs generated by a DWA planner in a 2D kinematic simulation, learning the full multi-modal distribution of kinematically-sound velocity commands in a single forward pass. In Stage 2, the frozen VelFlow expert provides a sampled reference velocity v_ref that is concatenated with the state embedding to form a guided state; a lightweight refiner is then trained online with PPO using a hybrid loss that combines the standard PPO objective with an MSE guidance term anchoring the refiner's output to a scaled v_ref. The guidance weight λ is annealed via a curriculum (0.5 → 0.05 over ~5 k steps), initially forcing the refiner to adopt the expert's navigation logic and later granting autonomy to discover dynamically optimal corrections for the target robot's specific tracking errors and controller imperfections.

## Results

On the Unitree Go2 platform, CE-Nav achieves a mean Success Rate (mSR) of 0.8575 and mean SPL of 0.8190 across four obstacle-density test environments, outperforming the strongest RL baseline NavRL (0.6925 mSR, 0.6460 mSPL) by 16.5% and 17.3% respectively, while requiring only 6 hours of adaptation training versus 50 hours for NavRL (8× less). Cross-embodiment evaluation across five platforms (quadrupeds, biped, quadrotor) shows consistent performance with mSR ranging from 0.7450 (Unitree H1) to 0.8600 (MagicDog). In real-world deployment, CE-Nav achieves 0.9167 SR and 0.8913 SPL, substantially exceeding DWA (0.7500/0.6832) and NavRL (0.5083/0.4612).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CE-Nav decisively outperforms all four baselines on both mSR and mSPL. Against the classical DWA planner, CE-Nav gains 21.75 percentage points in mSR (0.8575 vs. 0.6400), as DWA's myopic local planning fails in long-horizon, dense obstacle scenarios. The two imitation-learning baselines—Behavioral Cloning (BC, 0.0275 mSR) and Diffusion Policy (DP, 0.0725 mSR)—suffer severe covariate shift and generalize poorly to novel environments, making them essentially non-functional. The most competitive baseline, NavRL (an end-to-end RL method), achieves 0.6925 mSR but still trails CE-Nav by 16.5 percentage points in mSR and 17.3 in mSPL, while requiring 8× more training time (50 h vs. 6 h). In real-world trials, the gap widens further: CE-Nav reaches 0.9167 SR versus NavRL's 0.5083, confirming that the dynamics-aware refiner's embodiment-specific compensation is critical for physical deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of hierarchical navigation (decoupling planning from control), generative policy modeling (normalizing flows, diffusion policies), and offline-to-online RL (CAL-QL, residual RL). It departs from prior generative-policy work (Diffusion Policy, Flow Matching) by using a conditional normalizing flow (Real-NVP-based VelFlow) as a *frozen* geometric prior rather than an end-to-end trainable policy, and from residual RL by using a conditional refinement architecture instead of additive corrections. The curriculum-based annealing of guidance strength distinguishes it from both static demonstration-guided RL and strict-preservation curriculum methods, positioning the expert as a bootstrapping anchor that the refiner is explicitly permitted to override.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CE-Nav offers a practical recipe for equipping new robot platforms with competent local navigation in ~6 hours of RL training, without any robot-specific data collection, which is a significant reduction in deployment cost for the growing ecosystem of heterogeneous mobile robots. The finding that a deterministic regression-based expert actively degrades RL performance below the no-expert baseline is a useful cautionary result for the broader imitation-to-RL literature. Open questions include whether the 2D raycast abstraction limits performance in 3D cluttered or dynamic environments, how the approach scales to multi-robot or socially-aware settings, and whether the frozen-prior paradigm generalizes beyond local navigation to longer-horizon or manipulation tasks.
