## Problem

VLMs consistently fail on spatial reasoning tasks due to two compounding bottlenecks: inadequate 3D perception inherited from 2D-centric pre-training, and reasoning degradation caused by redundant 3D information that dilutes attention and encourages shortcut heuristics. Prior agentic frameworks address the perception gap but adopt purely accumulative information-gathering strategies, which worsens the redundancy problem. This paper formalizes spatial reasoning as constructing a Minimal Sufficient Set (MSS)—the most compact set of spatial primitives that is simultaneously sufficient to answer a query and free of distracting redundancy—drawing an analogy to the Minimal Sufficient Statistic from statistics.

## Input / Output

The method takes M RGB images from the same scene (no 3D scans or pre-existing point clouds required) and a natural-language spatial query. It produces a final answer, a structured Minimal Sufficient Set maintained as a Python dictionary containing only the spatial primitives needed (3D coordinates, orientation vectors, calibrated cardinal directions, relative positions), and an interpretable step-by-step reasoning trace. The 3D scene representation is reconstructed on-the-fly from the 2D images, and the MSS persists across iterations as shared state between the two agents.

## Method

MSSR is a zero-shot dual-agent framework in which a Perception Agent (PA) and Reasoning Agent (RA) iteratively collaborate to curate the MSS. The PA uses a visual programming paradigm to generate Python scripts invoking a toolbox of expert modules: VGGT for 3D reconstruction, GroundingDINO+SAM2 for object localization, a novel SOG module for language-grounded orientation estimation (reframed as coarse-to-fine multi-choice visual selection over 3D arrows rendered onto both the original and a synthetic canonical view), and numerical computation utilities for coordinate transforms and relative positioning. The RA formulates a high-level reasoning plan, prunes irrelevant items from the PA's output to enforce minimality, and then either issues a targeted natural-language request for missing information or triggers a final chain-of-thought decision over the curated set. The key departure from prior agentic methods is the explicit minimality pursuit: the RA actively subtracts non-contributing information rather than merely accumulating it, and the final answer is derived exclusively from the pruned MSS. The Python execution environment is snapshotted and reloaded across iterations, allowing the PA to build on prior computations without redundant processing.

## Results

On MMSI-Bench, MSSR achieves 49.5% overall accuracy, outperforming the strongest proprietary LLM (o3, 41.0%) by 8.5 points and the GPT-4o backbone (30.3%) by 19.2 points. On ViewSpatial-Bench, MSSR reaches 51.8% overall accuracy (51.0% camera-based, 54.4% person-based), surpassing o3 (51.1%) and all 3D-VLM, specialist, and agentic baselines. The framework also generalizes across backbones: swapping GPT-4o for Qwen3-VL-8B yields 43.1% on MMSI-Bench and 46.5% on ViewSpatial-Bench, still exceeding the corresponding standalone baselines by 11.7 and 9.6 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSSR achieves state-of-the-art results on both benchmarks, outperforming all 15 baselines. On MMSI-Bench, the largest gains are over 3D-VLMs (VLM-3R: 32.0% → +17.5 pts) and the GPT-4o backbone (30.3% → +19.2 pts), while the margin over the strongest proprietary LLM o3 (41.0%) is +8.5 pts. On ViewSpatial-Bench, MSSR (51.8%) edges out o3 (51.1%) by 0.7 pts and surpasses the best specialist LEO (43.7%) by 8.1 pts. Among agentic baselines, MSSR outperforms VADAR (28.9%/33.7%) and ViLaSR (30.2%/38.2%) by 20.6/18.1 and 19.3/13.6 points on MMSI/ViewSpatial respectively. Cross-backbone experiments confirm the framework's gains are backbone-agnostic: even with Qwen2.5-VL-7B, MSSR improves MMSI-Bench accuracy from 25.9% to 39.8% (+13.9 pts), and the Perception Agent is more sensitive to backbone quality than the Reasoning Agent, enabling cost-effective deployment with a strong PA and lighter RA.

## Relation to Prior Work

MSSR builds on the Visual Programming paradigm (Gupta & Kembhavi, CVPR 2023; Suris et al., ICCV 2023) as its execution backbone for the Perception Agent, but departs from prior agentic spatial-reasoning systems (VADAR, ViLaSR, ReAct) in a key way: instead of purely accumulating perceptual information, it explicitly prunes redundancy through a Reasoning Agent that enforces a Minimal Sufficient Set. The SOG module further extends the visual programming toolkit by reframing 3D orientation estimation as a coarse-to-fine multi-choice visual selection task rather than direct regression, addressing a gap in prior tool-augmented VLMs. Unlike monolithic 3D-VLMs that require expensive 3D instruction fine-tuning, MSSR is entirely zero-shot and training-free, preserving the backbone VLM's general capabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical demonstration that reducing information set size (from ~17 to ~6 items) while maintaining sufficiency actually improves reasoning accuracy challenges the default assumption in agentic systems that more gathered information is always better. The framework's interpretable reasoning traces open a practical path for distilling spatial reasoning into smaller models via data annotation, though the preliminary fine-tuning experiment (258 samples, +4.2%) is too small to confirm this at scale. A key limitation is the heavy dependence on perception quality (55.9% of failures are perception errors, dominated by object grounding), and the higher inference latency (~87s vs. ~53s for VADAR) limits real-time deployment. Open questions include whether a dedicated verification agent could close the perception-error gap and whether the minimal-sufficiency principle generalizes beyond indoor scene reasoning.
