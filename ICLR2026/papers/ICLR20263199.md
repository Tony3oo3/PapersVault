## Problem

Muon, Scion, and Gluon are powerful LMO-based optimizers for deep learning, but no principled distributed framework with convergence guarantees exists for them. The few existing distributed variants are heuristic and lack theoretical analysis, while the communication bottleneck inherent to distributed training of these methods remains entirely unaddressed. This paper asks whether Muon can be efficiently distributed without compromising its theoretical and practical benefits.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a product space of per-layer matrix parameter blocks, each equipped with an arbitrary norm (e.g., spectral norm for hidden layers, ℓ∞ norm for embeddings). Workers compute stochastic gradient estimates at their local model copy and transmit only compressed residual messages (never full dense vectors) to the server; the server similarly broadcasts only compressed model-shift updates back to workers. The output is a sequence of LMO-updated parameter matrices plus the compressed communication payloads, with the guarantee that no uncompressed d-dimensional message is ever sent in either direction.

## Method

EF21-Muon combines three ingredients: (1) layer-wise LMO updates over non-Euclidean norm balls (recovering Muon/Scion/Gluon as special cases), (2) bidirectional compression with error feedback—EF21-style gradient compression for the worker-to-server direction and EF21-P-style model-shift compression for the server-to-worker direction—and (3) a key reformulation of the LMO step via sharp operators, which converts the first-power-norm descent structure of LMO methods into a squared-norm recursion compatible with EF21's analysis. This reformulation is the critical technical bridge that enables the first extension of error feedback beyond the Euclidean setting. The full algorithm (Algorithms 2–3) treats each layer independently with its own norm, radius, and smoothness constants, supporting stochastic gradients and per-layer momentum. Convergence is proven under both standard non-Euclidean smoothness and the more general (L⁰,L¹)-smoothness regime, with layer-wise variants capturing anisotropic network structure, achieving O(1/√K) and O(1/K^{1/4}) rates matching state-of-the-art Euclidean EF21 guarantees.

## Results

On a 124M-parameter NanoGPT model trained on FineWeb10B (5B-token budget, batch size 256, sequence length 1024), EF21-Muon with the Rank15%+Natural compressor achieves up to 7× reduction in worker-to-server communication cost relative to the uncompressed Scion/Gluon baseline while reaching the same target test loss of 3.31. The Top15%+Natural compressor yields roughly 4× communication savings at the same accuracy. Table 2 reports per-round communication costs normalized to the identity compressor, ranging from 0.0667 (Rank10%+Natural, Rank5%) to 0.3625 (Top20%), confirming that aggressive compression preserves model quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the uncompressed Scion/Gluon optimizer (identity compressor, relative cost 1.0), which serves as the standard non-distributed LMO-based method. EF21-Muon with bidirectional compression matches this baseline's final test loss (3.31) while reducing per-round w2s communication by up to 7× (Rank15%+Natural, relative cost 0.1010) and ~4× (Top15%+Natural, relative cost 0.1969). The paper does not compare against other distributed Muon variants (e.g., MuLoCo, Dion, or ZeRO-1-based Muon) in experiments, but notes that those methods lack convergence guarantees. Among the tested compressors, combining Rank K or TopK sparsification with the Natural compressor consistently outperforms either alone at the same sparsity level, with Rank15%+Natural achieving the best communication-accuracy trade-off. Compression does slow convergence in terms of training steps (more tokens needed to reach the target loss), but the net communication savings remain substantial because each step transmits far fewer bytes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: the family of non-Euclidean LMO-based optimizers (Muon, Scion, Gluon) that exploit layer-wise geometry for deep learning, and the error-feedback compression framework (EF21, EF21-P) developed for communication-efficient distributed training in the Euclidean setting. The authors explicitly note that prior distributed Muon variants (Liu et al. 2025, Thérien et al. 2025, Ahn et al. 2025) are heuristic with no convergence guarantees, and that no principled distributed framework existed for LMO-based methods. The key technical bridge is reformulating the LMO update via sharp operators, which allows the EF21 descent-lemma machinery (built around squared Euclidean norms) to be adapted to arbitrary norm geometries — a step that was not straightforward, as the authors note their initial LMO-based descent attempt failed to recover known Euclidean rates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first distributed optimizer in the Muon/Scion/Gluon family with rigorous convergence guarantees, filling a gap that limits the practical adoption of these increasingly popular LLM training methods at scale. The extension of error feedback beyond the Euclidean setting is a genuine theoretical contribution that may enable compression for other non-Euclidean algorithms. However, experimental validation is limited to a single 124M-parameter NanoGPT model on FineWeb10B, leaving open whether the 7× communication savings and theoretical rates hold at the multi-billion-parameter scale where these optimizers are most relevant. Open questions include compatibility with 3D parallelism (tensor/pipeline/data), the behavior of non-Euclidean compressors at very high compression ratios, and whether the layer-wise (L0,L1)-smoothness assumptions hold in practice for architectures beyond transformers.
