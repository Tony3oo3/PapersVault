## Problem

Existing automatic radiology report generation models learn statistical correlations between image regions and report sentences, leading to unavoidable hallucinations and unreliable grounding of abnormality findings. While radiologists routinely self-critique their findings before concluding a report, this verification step remains largely unexplored in deep learning models. Prior attempts to introduce reasoning (e.g., chain-of-thought with LLMs) incur high test-time inference costs, making them impractical for low-resource clinical deployment.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single chest X-ray image (resized to 224×224, encoded into a 7×7 patch map of 512-dim features by a Swin Transformer) and produces a free-text radiology report with findings grounded to localized abnormality regions. The report is assembled by retrieving top-ranked sentences from a pre-built repository of 37 abnormality-specific sentence pools and integrating them with a frozen Phi-4B LLM decoder. As a byproduct, the model outputs fine-grained bounding boxes for each detected abnormality via Grad-CAM saliency maps, enabling region-level grounding of the generated findings.

## Method

RadSCR constructs region-based visual proposals as triplets (visual features, Grad-CAM bounding box, learned concept embedding) for each predicted positive abnormality, then applies three self-critiquing mechanisms that modify different components of the proposal: swapping the concept embedding for an alternative abnormality, swapping the visual features for another patient's image, and forming a global complementary proposal from all predicted negatives to catch false negatives. At retrieval time, the discounted similarity score subtracts the alternatives' similarity from the original proposal's similarity, suppressing unreliable matches before selecting top sentences. Sentence representations are further organized by K-means-derived prototypes (K=5 per abnormality) over TF-IDF clusters to enable finer-grained retrieval. Training uses a combined objective of symmetric contrastive loss for visual-language alignment, triplet loss to push alternatives away from the correct sentence, and a contrastive loss for the false-negative complementary proposal, all learned end-to-end with weakly supervised localization.

## Results

On MIMIC CXR, RadSCR achieves CheXbert F1 of 0.610, CE-Abn of 0.572, CE-Organ of 0.744, RadGraph-F1 (Complete) of 0.367, and RadNLI F1 of 0.408, outperforming all 15 baselines by large margins. The largest gains are in clinical efficacy for abnormality detection (CE-Abn: 0.572 vs. 0.399 for the best baseline LLaVA-Rad) and observation-relationship correctness (RadGraph-F1 Complete: 0.367 vs. 0.248 for RGRG). On VinDR-CXR, Swin+RadSCR achieves mAP of 0.308 at IoU=0.1 for abnormality localization, roughly doubling the best prior method (HAM at 0.134). Similar SOTA performance is confirmed on ReXGradient and IU X-ray datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RadSCR outperforms all 15 report-generation baselines on every clinical accuracy metric. Among LMM-based methods, LLaVA-Rad is the closest competitor on CheXbert (Acc 0.487 vs. RadSCR's 0.574) but trails by 17.3 points on CE-Abn (0.399 vs. 0.572) and 14.7 points on RadGraph-F1 Complete (0.220 vs. 0.367). Among VLM-based methods, RGRG leads on RadGraph-F1 (0.321/0.248) yet RadSCR still surpasses it by 10.1/11.9 points respectively. Among retrieval-based baselines, CXR-RePaiR and X-REM are the strongest (CheXbert F1 ≈ 0.40–0.42), but RadSCR exceeds them by ~19 points on CheXbert F1 and ~17 points on CE-Abn. For abnormality localization on VinDR-CXR, Swin+RadSCR (mAP 0.308 at IoU=0.1) more than doubles TDIL (0.125) and HAM (0.134), and substantially outperforms Swin+MLP (0.210). Against larger-decoder models (MAIRA-2 7B, RadFM), RadSCR achieves comparable CheXbert and CE scores despite using a much smaller 4B LLM decoder, confirming the efficiency of the self-critiquing retrieval paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

RadSCR builds on the retrieval-based chest X-ray report generation paradigm (CXR-RePaiR, X-REM) by adding architecture-embedded self-critiquing mechanisms that verify hypothesized abnormality proposals against alternative abnormalities, alternative patient images, and potential false negatives. It deliberately departs from the growing trend of LLM-based test-time reasoning (CoT, MedCoT, MedRAX) for hallucination mitigation, arguing that such approaches incur prohibitive inference cost and are impractical in low-resource clinical settings. The weakly-supervised localization component extends Grad-CAM-based region extraction (as in RGRG, CAMANet) with a proposal-encoding and contrastive alignment framework, while the sentence retrieval with prototype-based similarity draws on hierarchical retrieval ideas from prior medical report generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that multi-faceted self-critiquing learned end-to-end can substantially reduce hallucination and improve clinical accuracy in radiology reporting without requiring expensive test-time LLM reasoning, which is practically important for deployment in resource-constrained clinical environments. A key limitation is that false-negative critique operates only at the whole-chest level rather than localizing specific missed regions, and the optimal strategy for sampling alternative proposals remains an open question. The competitive performance against much larger 7B-parameter models (MAIRA-2, RadFM) suggests that architectural design and grounding mechanisms can partially substitute for raw model scale in this task, though the approach's dependence on a curated report repository and Chest ImaGenome annotations limits immediate generalizability to other imaging modalities or institutions without such infrastructure.
