## Problem

Reinforcement learning for LLM reasoning relies on sparse, outcome-based rewards and undirected entropy regularization, which drives policies toward repetitive, suboptimal reasoning patterns. Existing deep exploration methods (Deep Ensembles, Monte Carlo dropout, density-based pseudo-counts) are either prohibitively expensive or lack theoretical guarantees, leaving no principled, scalable mechanism for directed exploration in LLM reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts (math problems or natural-language-to-SQL questions) and produces full reasoning trajectories ending in a final answer (a boxed numeric value or an executable SQL query). During training, the CFN operates on per-token hidden representations from the LLM backbone, and the resulting intrinsic reward is aggregated at the trajectory level via a sum-then-square-root of local reward variances, then filtered through a three-stage pipeline (percentile, spatial coherence, noise suppression) before being injected into the policy's advantage estimate.

## Method

MERCI exploits the fact that LLM autoregressive generation has known, deterministic transitions (next state = current state + next token), which collapses the Uncertainty Bellman Equation into a simple accumulation of local reward uncertainty along a trajectory, eliminating the need to estimate transition uncertainty. A lightweight Coin Flipping Network (CFN) — an MLP head attached to a frozen LLM backbone — estimates pseudo-counts via Rademacher-distribution regression, converting state novelty into a per-token reward variance proxy. The trajectory-level intrinsic reward is computed as the square root of the summed local variances (avoiding the overestimation that comes from summing per-step standard deviations), then standardized within each GRPO/DAPO group and scaled by a cosine-decaying exploration coefficient γ with a clipping factor α to prevent the bonus from overwhelming the outcome signal. Three-stage bonus filtering (top-percentile retention, contiguous-cluster preservation, and task-irrelevant noise removal) allocates a controlled exploration budget, and the entire CFN is co-trained in parallel with the policy at minimal compute overhead.

## Results

On mathematical reasoning (Qwen2.5-Math-7B), DAPO+MERCI achieves the best average pass@k of 69.0 and mean@k of 44.9 across AIME2024/2025, MATH500, OlympiadBench, College Math, and Minerva, outperforming vanilla DAPO (66.9 / 42.2) and GRPO (65.8 / 40.5). Gains are most pronounced on the hardest benchmark, AIME2024, where DAPO+MERCI reaches 83.3% pass@256 vs. 76.7% for vanilla DAPO. On SQL generation (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct), DAPO+MERCI achieves 64.1% greedy accuracy on Bird (in-domain) and 77.3% on Spider (out-of-domain), surpassing vanilla DAPO (63.2% / 76.8%) and showing the largest out-of-domain improvement among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MERCI consistently outperforms all four baselines across both RL frameworks. Against vanilla GRPO, DAPO+MERCI gains +2.1 points in average pass@k (69.0 vs. 66.9) and +2.7 in mean@k (44.9 vs. 42.2); against GRPO the gap is +3.6 pass@k and +4.2 mean@k. Compared to the entropy-based exploration baseline (Entropy Adv.), MERCI wins by +1.1 pass@k and +1.7 mean@k under DAPO, and by +1.5 / +1.0 under GRPO. Against the RND-based intrinsic reward baseline (iMentor), MERCI leads by +1.7 pass@k and +0.8 mean@k under DAPO, and by +1.0 / +1.3 under GRPO. On SQL generation, MERCI's advantage is most visible out-of-domain: DAPO+MERCI reaches 77.3% Spider greedy accuracy vs. 76.8% for vanilla DAPO and 77.5% for DAPO+Entropy Adv., while on in-domain Bird it achieves 64.1% vs. 63.2% (DAPO) and 62.3% (DAPO+Entropy Adv.). Cross-domain transfer to GPQA and MMLU-Pro further confirms that MERCI-trained policies generalize better, e.g., 73.7% GPQA pass@16 vs. 70.5% for vanilla DAPO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GRPO/DAPO family of value-free RL frameworks for LLM reasoning and the Coin Flipping Network (CFN) pseudo-counting technique from Lobel et al. (2023), but its core conceptual departure is a theoretical one: it shows that the deterministic, known transitions of autoregressive LLM generation collapse the general Uncertainty Bellman Equation (O'Donoghue et al., 2017) into a simple accumulation of local reward uncertainty, making principled deep exploration tractable without ensembles or density models. Unlike prior LLM exploration methods that rely on undirected entropy regularization or heuristic curiosity bonuses (RND, ICM), MERCI derives its intrinsic reward from a formal UBE simplification and corrects a common theoretical error (summing per-step standard deviations vs. summing variances then taking the square root). The method is positioned as the first to bridge model-aware RL exploration theory with the model-free practice of modern LLM RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UBE simplification for known transitions is a clean, reusable theoretical result that could inform exploration design for any autoregressive decision-making setting, and the CFN-based implementation is lightweight enough to co-train with the policy at minimal overhead. However, empirical gains are modest (roughly 1–2 points over strong baselines on 7B/8B models), the method requires a second full LLM instance for the CFN, and the bonus-filtering pipeline introduces several task-specific hyperparameters (top-p%, noise-suppression rules) that may not transfer cleanly. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models or to tasks with genuinely stochastic transitions (tool-use, multi-turn interaction), and whether the CFN's novelty signal remains informative as the policy's distribution shifts substantially during long RL runs.
