## Problem

Existing automated red-teaming methods rely on fixed attack templates or narrow predefined strategy sets, focusing primarily on high-severity outputs while overlooking exploitability. This limits their ability to discover vulnerabilities that are simultaneously easy to trigger and highly harmful, and reduces adaptability to evolving defenses. The paper addresses this gap by formulating jailbreak discovery as an open-ended strategy exploration problem rather than template-based prompt generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262358/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of toxic behavior intents (from HarmBench), a target LLM, and a downgrade model (constructed by progressively weakening the target's safety alignment via toxic fine-tuning or in-context learning) as inputs. It produces high-level textual attack strategies (generalizable across intents) and concrete jailbreak queries generated by a frozen rephrasing model. The framework operates in both white-box settings (direct weight access for downgrade model construction) and black-box settings (ICL-based downgrade models), requiring only the target model's text output for evaluation.

## Method

AUTO-RT decomposes the attack model into a trainable strategy generation model (AM^g) and a frozen rephrasing model (AM^r), formulating jailbreak construction as a constrained MDP solved with PPO. Two key techniques improve exploration: (1) Dynamic Strategy Pruning integrates early termination into the CMDP by inserting diversity and consistency judges as intermediate checkpoints that halt redundant or inconsistent branches and propagate penalties back to AM^g; (2) Progressive Reward Tracking uses a calibrated downgrade model to convert sparse binary safety signals into a graded three-level reward (0/1/2), with the optimal downgrade model selected via a novel First Inverse Rate (FIR) metric that identifies the last model before a sharp increase in safety inconsistency. This reward shaping guides the strategy model toward stronger jailbreaks while the pruning mechanism concentrates exploration on high-value regions of the strategy space.

## Results

Across 16 white-box LLMs, AUTO-RT achieves the highest ASR_tst on 14 of 16 models, with improvements of up to 16.63% over the best baseline. Notable gains include 56.40% on Vicuna 7B (vs. 36.90% for IL) and 48.15% on Gemma 2 2B (vs. 7.49% for IL). In black-box settings (LLaMA3-70B, Qwen2.5-72B), AUTO-RT reaches 14.88% and 14.47% ASR_tst respectively, roughly 2× the best baseline. On the defended model R2D2, the few-shot baseline (27.18%) outperforms AUTO-RT (12.45%), though AUTO-RT still leads all RL-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the RL baseline (Perez et al., 2022), AUTO-RT wins on 14 of 16 models, with the largest margin on Gemma 2 2B (48.15% vs. 6.15%) and Qwen 1.5 4B (51.30% vs. 17.45%). Against Imitation Learning / MART (Ge et al., 2023), AUTO-RT leads on 13 of 16 models, most dramatically on Gemma 2 2B (48.15% vs. 7.49%) and Vicuna 7B (56.40% vs. 36.90%). In the human-template comparison (Table 3), AUTO-RT achieves 38.38% ASR_tst and 38.19% DeD, beating Past-Tense (11.19% / 7.27%) and Human Template (37.35% / 13.15%), but trailing AutoDAN on first-round ASR_tst (38.38% vs. 55.23%) while substantially outperforming it on defense generalization diversity (38.19% vs. 17.88%), indicating AUTO-RT discovers more transferable strategies. The sole loss is on R2D2, where the few-shot baseline (27.18%) surpasses AUTO-RT (12.45%), attributed to R2D2's targeted adversarial training making sampling-based attacks more effective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-based automatic red-teaming line (CRT, Diver-CT, MART) by introducing a hierarchical strategy→query decomposition, where a trainable strategy generation model produces abstract attack plans that a frozen rephrasing model instantiates into concrete jailbreak prompts. It builds directly on the early-terminated MDP formulation of Sun et al. (2021) for its Dynamic Strategy Pruning mechanism, and departs from prior template-based methods (AutoDAN, Rainbow Teaming, PAIR) by replacing fixed strategy sets with open-ended RL exploration over a continuous strategy space. The Progressive Reward Tracking technique, which uses a calibrated downgrade model and the novel First Inverse Rate metric for reward shaping, is a new contribution not present in prior red-teaming or RL reward-shaping literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The strategy-level framing offers a principled way to improve both exploitability and severity coverage simultaneously, and the FIR-based downgrade model selection provides a practical, data-driven criterion for reward shaping that could transfer to other sparse-reward RL settings. However, the approach's effectiveness in black-box settings drops substantially (ASR ~14–15% vs. ~50%+ in white-box), and the reliance on a safety classifier (Llama-Guard) as the reward signal introduces a potential adversarial target. Open questions include whether strategy-level exploration scales to multi-turn or multi-agent attack scenarios, and how the framework interacts with increasingly adaptive defenses that specifically target the strategy space rather than individual prompts.
