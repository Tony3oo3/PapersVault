## Problem

Continuous-time model-based RL requires the agent to decide both what actions to take and when to observe the system, making exploration fundamentally different from the discrete-time case. Prior continuous-time optimistic methods (e.g., OCORL) enforce optimism by jointly optimizing over policies and plausible dynamics, which is computationally intractable in high dimensions and does not support unsupervised learning without an extrinsic reward signal. No existing method provides a scalable, single-scalar mechanism to balance task reward and model uncertainty in continuous time with theoretical guarantees.

## Input / Output

COMBRL consumes noisy state-derivative measurements ẏ(t) = ẋ(t) + ε at times specified by a measurement selection strategy S (which can be equidistant or time-adaptive), along with the corresponding state-action pairs z(t) = (x(t), π(x(t))). Unlike discrete-time RL, the measurement schedule is not fixed: the agent can choose arbitrary observation times, and the number of measurements per episode |S_n| must grow with n to recover the full ODE. The method outputs a learned ODE dynamics model f_n with mean μ_n and epistemic uncertainty σ_n, a control policy π_n, and (in the time-adaptive TaCoS variant) variable-length control intervals Δt that jointly optimize actions and their durations.

## Method

COMBRL learns a probabilistic model of the unknown ODE dynamics f* using GPs, Bayesian neural networks, or probabilistic ensembles, yielding a mean estimate μ_n(z) and epistemic uncertainty σ_n(z) at each state-action point z. In each episode, the policy is selected by solving a continuous-time optimal control problem that maximizes the integral of [r(x,u) + λ_n·‖σ_{n-1}(x,u)‖]/(1+λ_n) subject to the learned dynamics f_n, where the scalar λ_n trades off extrinsic reward against model uncertainty. This replaces OCORL's intractable co-optimization over the confidence set of plausible dynamics with a simple additive intrinsic reward, making the method scalable to high-dimensional settings. Theoretically, COMBRL achieves sublinear regret O(√(I_N³(f*,S)·N)) in the reward-driven case and a sample complexity bound O(√(I_N³/N)) for reducing epistemic uncertainty in the unsupervised (λ_n→∞) setting, where I_N is a model complexity term that depends on the measurement selection strategy.

## Results

COMBRL consistently achieves higher asymptotic returns than both PETS and the mean planner across all evaluated environments, with the largest gains in sparse-reward or underactuated tasks (MountainCar, CartPole) and consistent improvements in higher-dimensional domains (HalfCheetah, Hopper, Humanoid). In the GP experiments, COMBRL matches or exceeds OCORL's performance while requiring roughly 3× less compute time (10.8 min vs. 30.6 min on Pendulum; 1.29 h vs. 4.55 h per episode on MountainCar). In the unsupervised setting (λ→∞), COMBRL's variant achieves the best zero-shot downstream-task performance across all seven evaluated Gym environments. In the time-adaptive TaCoS setting, COMBRL-TaCoS achieves competitive or superior returns while using fewer interactions than its fixed-rate counterpart.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OCORL (Treven et al., 2023), COMBRL matches or exceeds asymptotic return on both Pendulum and MountainCar while reducing training time by approximately 3× (10.8 min vs. 30.6 min on Pendulum; 1.29 h vs. 4.55 h on MountainCar), because OCORL's co-optimization over optimistic dynamics and reparametrization trick are computationally prohibitive. Against PETS (Chua et al., 2018), COMBRL achieves higher asymptotic returns across all environments, with the most pronounced gains in sparse-reward or underactuated settings such as MountainCar and CartPole, confirming that the explicit uncertainty bonus accelerates learning beyond what ensemble disagreement alone provides. Against the mean planner (Yildiz et al., 2021, corresponding to λ=0), COMBRL consistently outperforms, demonstrating that the intrinsic reward term is essential for effective exploration. In the unsupervised downstream-task evaluation, COMBRL's unsupervised variant (λ→∞) outperforms all three baselines across all seven environments, while the balanced COMBRL (static or annealing λ) achieves the best primary-task performance. In the TaCoS time-adaptive setting, COMBRL-TaCoS matches or exceeds OTaCoS (the OCORL variant) while requiring fewer interactions than its own fixed-rate version.

## Relation to Prior Work

COMBRL extends the continuous-time model-based RL line initiated by Yildiz et al. (2021) and Treven et al. (2023, OCORL) by replacing the intractable joint optimization over policies and optimistic dynamics with a simpler reward-plus-uncertainty objective that only requires selecting any model from the confidence set. It is the continuous-time counterpart of SOMBRL (Sukhija et al., 2025b), which studies the same reward-plus-epistemic-uncertainty formulation in discrete time, but the continuous-time setting introduces fundamentally different analysis: uncertainty accumulates along entire trajectories, and the measurement selection strategy (MSS) becomes an explicit design variable affecting regret. The algorithm is model-agnostic (GPs, BNNs, ensembles) and planner-agnostic (iCEM, SAC, TaCoS), decoupling the exploration mechanism from implementation details that prior methods entangled.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first scalable continuous-time optimistic MBRL algorithm with sublinear regret guarantees and the first sample complexity bound for unsupervised continuous-time RL, filling a gap between the theoretically grounded but computationally expensive OCORL and the scalable but purely exploitative Yildiz et al. approach. The single scalar λ_n providing a unified interpolation from greedy exploitation through balanced exploration to pure unsupervised system identification is practically useful and empirically validated. Open questions include whether the theoretical bounds are tight, how the method scales to very high-dimensional or pixel-based control where GP/BNN models struggle, and whether the auto-tuning strategy for λ_n generalizes beyond the tested environments. The time-adaptive extension via TaCoS suggests further gains from adaptive sensing, but the interaction between adaptive MSS and the regret analysis remains underexplored.
