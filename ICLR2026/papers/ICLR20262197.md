## Problem

LLMs exhibit marked performance degradation when instructions contain more than 10 explicit constraints, yet existing datasets cap out at roughly 3–5 constraints per instance and lack scalable training resources for complex instruction following. Prior automated approaches either rely exclusively on code-verifiable constraints (limiting domain coverage) or produce homogenized constraints through LLM-based rewriting, failing to capture the diversity and density of real-world multi-constraint prompts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262197/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each training instance is a text instruction enriched with an average of 13.4 constraints (up to 30+), drawn from 19 constraint types split into rule-based (e.g., paragraph count, keyword inclusion, word limits, JSON format) and model-based (e.g., formality, politeness, persuasiveness) categories. The model must produce a single text response that simultaneously satisfies all embedded constraints; satisfaction is verified per-constraint via deterministic rule-based validators or LLM-based binary (satisfied/violated) judgments, enabling fine-grained partial-credit scoring.

## Method

RECAST is a four-stage data-synthesis pipeline: (1) seed collection from Tülu 3 Persona IF across 27 domains, (2) constraint pool construction using nine specialized rule-based extractors plus LLM-generated model-based constraints with a filtering step to retain only response-satisfied constraints, (3) instruction enhancement where multiple LLMs rewrite the original prompt to naturally integrate selected constraints, with Borda-voting to pick the best version, and (4) multi-model response generation with majority voting for constraint-compliant outputs. On top of SFT on the resulting RECAST-30K dataset, RLVC formulates each constraint as an independent reward channel (average satisfaction rate across all constraints) and optimizes the policy via GRPO, providing fine-grained per-constraint feedback that guides simultaneous multi-objective constraint satisfaction.

## Results

On RECAST-Test (a hierarchical 4-level benchmark with up to 30+ constraints per instruction), RECAST-30K-RLVC achieves 32.33% average Hard Satisfaction Rate (HSR) on Qwen2.5-7B and 31.96% on Llama-3.1-8B, substantially outperforming the corresponding Instruct models (26.46% and 27.25% respectively) and even the much larger Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct (24.75%). On external benchmarks, RECAST-30K-RLVC reaches 77.39% IFEVAL Prompt-Level Loose Accuracy and 61.76% FollowBench HSR on Llama3.1-8B (avg 69.58%), and 74.01% / 63.23% on Qwen2.5-7B (avg 68.62%). General capability is preserved: RECAST-30K-RLVC scores 34.64% avg on GPQA+MuSR (Llama) and 38.39% (Qwen), competitive with or exceeding most baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RECAST-30K-SFT and RLVC outperform all eight fine-tuning baselines on RECAST-Test by wide margins: the closest competitor, Tülu 3 Persona IF (the seed dataset itself), scores 26.96%/26.83% (Llama/Qwen) versus 30.54%/31.25% for RECAST-30K-SFT (+3.6–4.4 pts), while Conifer (21.00%/22.21%), CRaB (13.79%/14.00%), I-SHEEP (17.40%/13.96%), MUFFIN (7.79%/17.13%), ShareGPT (18.83%/19.13%), Evol-Instruct (16.50%/16.46%), and Suri (0.88%/0.67%) all trail by 4–30 points. On IFEVAL and FollowBench, RECAST-30K-RLVC achieves the best scores across both base models (69.58% avg on Llama, 68.62% on Qwen), surpassing Tülu 3 Persona IF (64.69%/65.44%) and all other baselines by 3–58 points. Against the inference-time method DVR (Zhang et al., 2025), RECAST-30K-SFT achieves 12.5%/13.0% average RSR versus DVR's 7.0%/6.5% on Llama/Qwen, a consistent +5.5–6.5 point gap across all four difficulty levels. On general capability (GPQA+MuSR), RECAST-30K-RLVC matches or slightly exceeds most baselines (34.64% Llama, 38.39% Qwen), confirming that the complex-instruction training does not degrade reasoning or knowledge performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of complex instruction-following dataset construction (Conifer, CRaB, MUFFIN, Evol-Instruct, Suri, Tülu 3 Persona IF) by mining constraints directly from real seed responses rather than generating them via LLM rewriting, and by pushing constraint density well beyond the 3–5 constraints typical of prior work to an average of 13.4 (up to 30+). It departs from prior RL-with-verifiable-rewards approaches (which typically use a single reward channel) by formulating per-constraint rewards that combine both rule-based and model-based verifiers within a unified GRPO framework, building on the GRPO algorithm from DeepSeekMath. The key conceptual departure is treating constraint verifiability as a first-class design property of the dataset itself, enabling downstream RL without additional reward-model training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 30K-sample dataset constructed for ~$175 enables an 8B model to outperform a 70B instruct model on complex multi-constraint following, and the verifiable constraint structure opens a path to RL-based optimization without expensive reward models. However, absolute performance remains low (best model ~40% on RECAST-Test), and the RLVC gains over SFT are modest (~1–2 points), leaving open whether the approach scales to even higher constraint densities or whether model-based validator noise becomes a bottleneck at scale. The benchmark's constraint-nesting design (Levels 1–4) provides a useful diagnostic tool, but whether 15+ constraints per instruction reflects genuine real-world demand or is an artificial stress test remains an open question.
