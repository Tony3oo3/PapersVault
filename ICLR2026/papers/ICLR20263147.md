## Problem

Aligning text-to-image diffusion models with multiple black-box reward functions at inference time via weighted-sum guidance requires a number of reward queries that scales with both the number of reward functions M and the number of weight combinations L, making multi-objective Pareto-optimal sampling prohibitively expensive. Existing fine-tuning-based multi-reward approaches (e.g., rewarded soups, DPO) sidestep this query overhead but suffer from reward over-optimization, loss of generation diversity, and poor generalization to unseen prompt distributions.

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt, M black-box reward functions (e.g., LAION aesthetic score, HPSv2, PickScore, compressibility), and L weight vectors on the (M−1)-simplex, the method produces L reward-aligned images approximating the Pareto front. At each of T denoising steps, only M reward functions are queried (one per exemplar) rather than M×L, with N Monte Carlo samples per query; the total query budget is controlled by the hyperparameter K (number of soup steps before switching to true weighted-sum updates). The method is entirely training-free, operating in the latent space of pre-trained models (SD1.5, SDXL, SD3) via DDIM sampling, and requires no gradient access to the reward functions.

## Method

SRSoup computes reward-guided search gradients independently for each of M exemplar denoising distributions (each optimized by a single one-hot reward) using the log-derivative trick, then approximates the gradient for any weighted combination by linearly interpolating these M gradients plus a correction term that accounts for the mean offset between exemplars and the target distribution. The key insight is that during early denoising steps, the isotropic Gaussian distributions centered at different exemplars still overlap significantly (verified empirically via the Bhattacharyya coefficient), so samples drawn from one exemplar distribution serve as statistically valid Monte Carlo estimates for another, as formalized by a total-variation bound via Pinsker's inequality. A hybrid strategy applies SRSoup interpolation for the first K steps (where overlap holds) and switches to true weighted-sum reward queries for the remaining T−K steps, reducing total queries by NKM(L−M) while maintaining comparable Pareto front quality to the full weighted-sum baseline.

## Results

SRSoup achieves comparable multi-reward optimization performance to inference-time weighted-sum guidance while requiring 1.8× fewer reward queries (Aesthetic & Compressibility, Aesthetic & PickScore) and ~2.7× fewer (Aesthetic & HPSv2). Under the standard setting (T=50, N=30, K=20, L=6), SRSoup requires 10,200 queries per prompt versus 15,000 for weighted-sum, reducing inference time from 28.8 min to 21.1 min. On cross-reward evaluation (Table 1), SRSoup maintains CLIP scores of 0.2532–0.2692 across all weight settings for Aesthetic & Compressibility, outperforming AlignProp(soup) (0.1844–0.2464) and TDPO(soup) (0.2361–0.2379), while matching or exceeding DDPO(soup) (0.2552–0.2583).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRSoup consistently outperforms all three model-soup fine-tuning baselines (DDPO-soup, TDPO-soup, AlignProp-soup) on Pareto front quality across both two-objective and three-objective settings, while avoiding the reward over-optimization that causes these methods to produce homogeneous, low-diversity images. Against AlignProp(soup), the gap is most pronounced: AlignProp's CLIP score drops to 0.1844 at w₁=0 (vs. SRSoup's 0.2692), indicating severe prompt-alignment degradation. TDPO(soup) suffers the most severe over-optimization, with CLIP collapsing to 0.1128 at w₁=1 for Aesthetic & PickScore, while SRSoup maintains 0.2238. Against inference-time weighted-sum guidance, SRSoup matches performance with 1.8×–2.7× fewer reward queries, making it the more query-efficient option. SRSoup also outperforms D3PO(soup) and Flow-GRPO(soup) on Pareto front hypervolume, and surpasses the supervised multi-reward method RiC on the CoCo dataset, confirming that training-free gradient interpolation is competitive with or superior to fine-tuning-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the model-soup paradigm of Rewarded Soups (Rame et al., 2023) from the parameter space to the sample space: instead of interpolating fine-tuned model weights, SRSoup interpolates reward-guided search gradients computed at each denoising step. The gradient estimation itself inherits the log-derivative trick from black-box optimization (Wierstra et al., 2014; Nesterov & Spokoiny, 2017), enabling reward-guided steering without differentiable reward access. The key departure from prior inference-time alignment work (Kim et al., 2025; Eyring et al., 2024) is the observation that denoising distributions for different reward weightings overlap in early timesteps, allowing gradient sharing across rewards and reducing the query count from O(NTM·L) to O(NTM·(L−M+1) − NKM(L−M)).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SRSoup provides a practical, training-free recipe for multi-reward T2I alignment that avoids the reward over-optimization plaguing fine-tuning baselines (DDPO, TDPO, AlignProp) while cutting reward queries by up to ~40% compared to weighted-sum guidance. The hybrid strategy (soup early, true weighted-sum late) is grounded in a clean theoretical analysis via Bhattacharyya coefficients, giving practitioners a principled way to choose the switch point K. Open questions include adaptive query allocation across prompts of varying difficulty, scaling to more than three reward functions, and whether the same-noise initialization requirement limits diversity in the generated Pareto set.
