## Problem

Aligning pretrained discrete diffusion models with a reward signal (preference optimization) is fundamentally harder than for autoregressive models because the generative process is a Markov chain over discrete latent variables, making it intractable to backpropagate reward gradients through the full denoising trajectory or compute exact likelihoods over the joint. Existing approaches either resort to expensive online RL with Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization, require restrictive reward forms like Bradley-Terry pairwise preferences, or perform inference-time guidance that inflates sampling cost. This paper asks: can we derive a principled offline objective that aligns the entire diffusion trajectory while operating only on rewards defined over the clean output sequence?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-collected offline dataset of (clean sequence x_0, context c, scalar reward r(x_0, c)) triples, where the context is a cell-type label for DNA design, a 3D backbone structure for protein inverse folding, or a text prompt for language modeling. No on-policy sampling is required during training; intermediate latent variables x_t are reconstructed on-the-fly from x_0 via the forward noising process q(x_t|x_0). The output is a finetuned masked discrete diffusion model whose generated sequences (200-bp DNA, amino-acid sequences, or text responses) are biased toward higher predicted reward while remaining in-distribution, as measured by log-likelihood, k-mer correlation, or scRMSD.

## Method

SDPO decomposes the intractable trajectory alignment objective into T independent subproblems, each aligning the factorized per-step posterior p̂_θ(x_0|x_t, c) toward a reward-tilted target distribution. The key theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) shows that the jointly optimal stepwise posteriors induce a joint p*(x_{0:T}|c) that is also optimal for the full trajectory objective, provided the chain reward admits an additive factorization over per-step rewards r_t. Practically, the per-step alignment is solved via a KL-divergence distribution-matching loss between a Boltzmann policy (reward reweighted by the reference posterior) and a model policy (implicit reward reweighted by the reference posterior), yielding a cross-entropy objective (Eq. 10) over N samples per context. The implicit reward r̃_θ is the log-ratio of model and reference posteriors scaled by β/w(t), where w(t)=1−α_t amortizes the loss across masked tokens. This formulation generalizes DPO (recovered at N=2 with Bradley-Terry rewards) to arbitrary real-valued reward functions and variable sample sizes, and supports an optional iterative-labeling variant that refreshes the dataset with newly generated high-reward samples.

## Results

On DNA sequence design, SDPO achieves a predicted HepG2 activity of 6.30 (median), outperforming the strongest RL baseline DRAKES (5.61) by 12.3%, while maintaining 94.8% ATAC accuracy and 0.936 JASPAR motif correlation. On protein inverse folding, SDPO reaches a predicted ΔΔG of 1.400 with 87.1% positive-stability proportion, surpassing DRAKES (1.095) and diffu-GRPO (1.286). On language modeling with LLaDA-8B-Instruct, SDPO improves GSM8K 5-shot accuracy from 78.6 to 81.2, IFEval from 52.9 to 55.1, and AlpacaEval 2.0 length-controlled winrate from 10.6% to 14.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDPO outperforms all eight baselines on the primary DNA design metric (Pred-Activity), achieving 6.30 vs. DRAKES 5.61 (+12.3%), diffu-GRPO 5.86, and CFG 5.04. Against the offline preference methods D2-DPO and VRPO, SDPO gains 3.33 and 1.70 in Pred-Activity respectively, while D2-DPO retains a marginal edge in 3-mer correlation (0.944 vs. 0.900) but trails on JASPAR correlation (0.883 vs. 0.936). On protein inverse folding, SDPO leads in predicted ΔΔG (1.400) over DRAKES (1.095) and diffu-GRPO (1.286), though DRAKES maintains slightly better structural fidelity (scRMSD 0.918 vs. 0.938) and success rate (78.6% vs. 75.5%); diffu-GRPO suffers severe reward over-optimization with success rate collapsing to 37.2%. In language modeling, SDPO improves GSM8K from 78.6 (base) to 81.2, beating D2-DPO (78.1) and diffu-GRPO (80.5), and achieves the best AlpacaEval 2.0 LC winrate (14.2% vs. 12.6% for diffu-GRPO). Training efficiency also favors SDPO at 0.77 s/step versus 6.02 s for DRAKES and 1.51 s for diffu-GRPO.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDPO extends the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2023) from autoregressive language models to discrete diffusion models, and extends diffusion DPO (Wallace et al., 2024) from Gaussian to the discrete/masked setting. Its key conceptual departure from prior discrete-diffusion alignment work (DRAKES, diffu-GRPO, D2-DPO, VRPO) is the stepwise decomposition: rather than backpropagating reward through the full denoising trajectory or restricting to pairwise Bradley-Terry preferences, SDPO aligns the per-step posterior p̂_θ(x₀|x_t) independently at each diffusion step, with a theoretical equivalence (Theorem 4.1) showing this induces an optimal trajectory solution under additive reward factorization. The method also generalizes the reward model beyond BT to arbitrary scalar rewards, building on the Boltzmann-policy / distribution-matching perspective from f-PO (Han et al., 2024a) and exact preference optimization (Ji et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SDPO provides the first principled offline preference optimization for discrete diffusion models that is both theoretically grounded and computationally efficient (0.77 s/step vs. 6.02 s for DRAKES), which is practically important as discrete diffusion LMs (LLaDA, Dream) gain traction. The equivalence theorem is a clean theoretical contribution, but the induced stepwise reward r_t is complex and not directly computable, limiting interpretability. Open questions include whether the additive-factorization assumption in Theorem 4.1 holds broadly, how the method scales to continuous-time discrete diffusion, and whether the modest gains on language benchmarks (GSM8K +2.6) will grow with larger models or richer reward signals. The iterative-labeling variant suggests a path toward semi-online optimization without full on-policy sampling.
