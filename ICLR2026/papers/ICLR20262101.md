## Problem

Scaling diffusion-based robot planning to long horizons is challenging because existing compositional methods (DiffCollage, GSC) stitch short segments by score-averaging on noisy diffusion states, where the Bethe factorization assumption breaks down. This leads to boundary drift, blurry or unrealistic intermediate frames, and near-zero success on out-of-distribution start-goal combinations. The paper identifies the root cause theoretically (Noisy-Bethe Gap Theorem) and asks whether compositional consistency can be enforced in a domain where factorization actually holds.

## Input / Output

The method takes a start image and a goal image (256×256×3 RGB) as task context, encodes them via the Cosmos tokenizer into a compact latent representation, and performs all planning in this latent space rather than pixel space. At inference, the trajectory is partitioned into n overlapping chunks of three consecutive frames each; the endpoints are anchored to the start and goal boundary variables. The output is a temporally coherent video plan (sequence of latent frames) that is decoded and fed through a separately trained MLP inverse dynamics model to produce executable end-effector actions (3D position, 4D quaternion, gripper state) for a Franka Emika Panda robot.

## Method

The core insight is to enforce boundary agreement on Tweedie (clean-data) estimates rather than on noisy intermediate diffusion states, where the Bethe factorization is provably invalid (Noisy-Bethe Gap Theorem, Eq. 8). The plan is modeled as a chain-structured factor graph of overlapping video chunks, with local priors from a frozen short-horizon DiT diffusion backbone trained once on short clips. At each DDIM step, a joint synchronous-asynchronous message passing loss is computed on the Tweedie estimates: the synchronous term treats the chain as a Gaussian linear system (tridiagonal precision matrix) and drives the global residual to zero in parallel, while the asynchronous term uses one-sided stop-gradient targets with a discount factor to propagate constraints forward and backward in a TD-style manner. The combined loss gradient is then injected via Diffusion Sphere Guidance, which interpolates between the unconditional annealing direction and the normalized descent direction on a hypersphere, preserving sample diversity while steering toward boundary agreement. The entire procedure is training-free and plug-and-play: no fine-tuning, adapters, or task-specific retraining is required at test time.

## Results

On the Compositional Planning Benchmark (100 tasks across 4 robotic manipulation scenes), the proposed method achieves 59±17% overall in-distribution (IND) success rate and 54±14% out-of-distribution (OOD) success rate, compared to 0±1% and 0±0% for DiffCollage/GSC respectively. On OOD tasks, the method substantially outperforms the strongest policy-learning baseline GCDP (54% vs. 15%) and the joint-denoising baseline CompDiffuser (54% vs. 16%). In video quality evaluation (VBench++), the method improves Motion Smoothness from 0.87 to 0.97 and Imaging Quality from 0.55 to 0.69 on OOD tasks over DiffCollage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffCollage/GSC, the primary compositional baseline that stitches short-horizon factors via score averaging on noisy diffusion states, fails catastrophically: it achieves 0–1% success rate across nearly all 100 tasks in both simulation and real-robot settings, with generated plans described as blurry and unrealistic. The proposed method outperforms DiffCollage by 54–59 percentage points on overall success rate, a gap attributable to enforcing boundary agreement on Tweedie estimates rather than noisy intermediate states. Against CompDiffuser (a trained joint-denoising baseline that conditions each chunk on noisy neighbors), the proposed method achieves roughly 3–4× higher OOD success (54% vs. 16%) and 3.5× higher IND success (59% vs. 17%). Compared to the strongest policy-learning baseline GCDP, the proposed method is competitive on IND tasks (59% vs. 56%) but dramatically superior on OOD tasks (54% vs. 15%), demonstrating that the compositional factor-graph formulation generalizes to unseen start-goal combinations far better than end-to-end policy learning. On real hardware (Franka Emika Panda), the method achieves 8–10/10 success on OOD tasks versus 0/10 for DiffCollage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compositional diffusion planning line initiated by DiffCollage (Zhang et al., 2023) and Generative Skill Chaining (Mishra et al., 2023), which stitch short-horizon factors via Bethe-approximation score averaging on noisy diffusion states. The key departure is a theoretical and practical shift: the authors prove (Noisy-Bethe Gap Theorem) that the Bethe factorization breaks down under forward diffusion, and instead enforce boundary agreement on Tweedie (clean-data) estimates using joint synchronous and asynchronous message passing. For the guidance mechanism, they adopt Diffusion Sphere Guidance (Yang et al., 2024) to interpolate between unconditional sampling and loss-driven descent, preserving diversity while enforcing consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of the method means any pretrained short-horizon video diffusion backbone can be scaled to long-horizon planning without retraining, fine-tuning, or task-specific adapters—a practical advantage for robotics where data is expensive. The Noisy-Bethe Gap Theorem provides a clean theoretical explanation for why prior compositional methods (DiffCollage, GSC) fail, which should inform future work on multi-factor diffusion composition beyond robotics. Open questions include: whether the number of composed segments can be inferred automatically from task structure, whether the approach scales to even longer horizons without the computational overhead of test-time backpropagation, and whether the framework transfers to other compositional generation domains (panoramas, long-form video) as the authors note.
