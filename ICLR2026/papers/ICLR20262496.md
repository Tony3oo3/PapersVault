## Problem

Existing diffusion-based OOD detection methods rely on scalar metrics—negative log-likelihood, score norms, or reconstruction error—that collapse denoising uncertainty into a single number and fail to preserve ordering between InD and OOD samples, particularly in near-OOD settings where distributions share low-level statistics. The paper identifies that the eigenvalue spectrum of the posterior covariance provides a more principled and consistent signal of distribution shift, but no prior method exploits this spectral structure for OOD detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262496/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (32×32 in main experiments, 256×256 in high-resolution experiments), adds Gaussian noise at a small set of selected diffusion timesteps (e.g., T=5 steps from {100,150,200,250,300}), and produces a scalar OOD score. The score is computed as the sum of Z-normalized top-K eigenvalue sums across I noise realizations at each timestep, with normalization statistics (μ_t, σ_t) estimated from InD training data only. No OOD labels or OOD data are required at any stage.

## Method

EigenScore exploits the Miyasawa identity, which links the posterior covariance Cov_p[x|x_t] to the Jacobian of the MMSE denoiser (σ_t² ∇D_p(x_t)), and shows via a KL-divergence decomposition that OOD inputs systematically inflate this covariance. Rather than computing the full Jacobian, the method estimates the top-K eigenvalues using a Jacobian-free subspace iteration that approximates Jacobian-vector products via finite differences of the denoiser output (two forward evaluations per direction per iteration). At each selected timestep, the sum of top-K eigenvalues is aggregated over I noise draws, Z-normalized using InD statistics, and summed across timesteps to yield the final OOD score. The theoretical justification for using only top-K eigenvalues (rather than the full trace) comes from Lemma 1, which shows the spectrum flattens to isotropic noise at high timesteps, and Ky Fan's theorem, which guarantees the leading eigenvalues capture maximal discriminative variance.

## Results

EigenScore achieves the best average AUROC of 0.838 across 12 InD–OOD dataset pairs, outperforming the strongest diffusion-based baseline DDPM-OOD (0.817) by 2.1 points and DiffPathV2 (0.810) by 2.8 points. Its advantage is most pronounced in the near-OOD regime (C10 vs. C100), where EigenScore reaches 0.880 AUROC versus 0.618 for DDPM-OOD and 0.554 for DiffPath. On the near-OOD benchmark (Table 2), EigenScore attains 0.849 average AUROC, a 9.5-point margin over DiffPath (0.754) and 27.2 points over DDPM-OOD (0.527). EigenScore also consistently outperforms scalar MSE (0.652 avg) by 18.2 points, confirming that retaining spectral structure rather than collapsing uncertainty into a single scalar is critical.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EigenScore outperforms all likelihood-based baselines (DoS 0.693, TT 0.701, WAIC 0.698, NLL 0.584, IC 0.576) by 14–26 AUROC points on average, confirming that scalar likelihood or typicality scores are unreliable for diffusion-based OOD detection. Against the strongest reconstruction-based diffusion method DDPM-OOD (0.817), EigenScore gains 2.1 points on average and a decisive 26.2-point margin on the near-OOD C10→C100 pair (0.880 vs. 0.618), though DDPM-OOD retains an edge on C10→SVHN (0.944 vs. 0.810) and C100→SVHN (0.870 vs. 0.661). Versus trajectory-based DiffPath (0.784) and DiffPathV2 (0.810), EigenScore leads by 5.4 and 2.8 points respectively, with the gap widening to 33 points on C10→C100 where DiffPath drops to 0.554. LMD (0.771) is outperformed by 6.7 points on average, with EigenScore particularly stronger on C10→C100 (0.880 vs. 0.601) and C100→C10 (0.642 vs. 0.569). On the dedicated near-OOD benchmark (Table 2), EigenScore (0.849 avg) surpasses DiffPath (0.754), LMD (0.580), and DDPM-OOD (0.527) by 9.5, 26.9, and 32.2 points respectively, underscoring its robustness when InD and OOD share low-level statistics.

## Relation to Prior Work

EigenScore builds on the diffusion-based OOD detection line initiated by DDPM-OOD (reconstruction fidelity), DiffPath (score trajectory geometry), and LMD (masked inpainting), but departs from all of them by shifting the signal from scalar reconstruction or score-norm metrics to the eigenvalue spectrum of the posterior covariance. The theoretical backbone recombines the Miyasawa identity (linking posterior covariance to the denoiser Jacobian), Tweedie's formula, and the KL-divergence-to-denoising-error decomposition from Song et al. (2021) and Shoushtari et al. (2025) into a unified argument that spectral inflation is a consistent, order-preserving OOD signal. The practical computation leverages the Jacobian-free subspace iteration of Manor & Michaeli (2024), adapted here to estimate only the top-K eigenvalues rather than the full covariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is robustness in near-OOD settings (e.g., CIFAR-10 vs. CIFAR-100), where likelihood-based and trajectory-based diffusion methods collapse to near-chance AUROC while EigenScore maintains strong separation—addressing a known failure mode of prior diffusion-based OOD detectors. The theoretical framing (posterior covariance inflation as an order-preserving signal, contrasted with the direction-agnostic score norm) gives practitioners a principled reason to prefer spectral over scalar metrics. Open questions include whether eigenvector structure or eigenvalue decay rates carry additional discriminative signal, whether the approach extends to latent-space or score-based SDE formulations, and how to reduce the ~20× NFE overhead relative to DiffPath without sacrificing accuracy.
