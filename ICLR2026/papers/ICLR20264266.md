## Problem

Video diffusion models incur prohibitively high training and inference costs because they must operate at full frame rate across all diffusion timesteps, despite the fact that early (high-entropy) timesteps contain minimal temporal information and consecutive video frames are highly redundant. Existing efficiency approaches—cascaded super-resolution, 3D convolutions, and spatial pyramids—either accumulate errors, fail to scale with attention-based architectures, or are restricted to specific diffusion formulations like flow matching in an autoregressive setting. The feasibility of a temporal pyramid structure for video generation remained unexplored.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt and produces a generated video at resolutions of 640×384 (MiniFlux-vid), 512×512 (AnimateDiff), or 832×480 (Wan), trained on the OpenVID-1M dataset. A distinctive aspect is that during both training and inference, the model internally operates at progressively different temporal resolutions (e.g., 1/4, 1/2, and full frame rate across three stages), so the effective sequence length fed to the transformer varies per stage. The model also supports zero-shot long-video generation beyond its fixed training clip length without architectural modification.

## Method

TPDiff divides the diffusion process into K stages where the frame rate at stage k is reduced to 1/2^(k−1) of the original, so only the final stage operates at full frame rate, cutting average attention cost from T² to ≈0.44T². The key training innovation is stage-wise diffusion: by solving the partitioned probability flow ODE between stage endpoints (derived from DPM-Solver's two-point relationship) and applying data-noise alignment (a Hungarian-assignment that pairs each video with its nearest noise sample), the method obtains a unified, closed-form expression for intermediate latents and training targets that works identically for both DDIM and flow matching. Stage-consistent RoPE positional encoding multiplies frame indices by a stage-dependent power-of-two factor so that the same physical frame receives identical positional encoding across stages, enabling large-amplitude motion. At inference, standard ODE solvers run per stage, with a renoising-and-upsampling step (Eq. 15) bridging adjacent stages to maintain distributional continuity.

## Results

On VBench, MiniFlux-vid-Ours (9B) achieves a total score of 81.95, surpassing its vanilla counterpart (81.54) and outperforming CogVideoX-5B (80.91) and Videocrafter2 (80.44) with fewer parameters. The method achieves 2.16× training speedup and 1.49–1.82× inference speedup across three model architectures (AnimateDiff, MiniFlux-vid, Wan). Temporal metrics improve notably: Dynamic Degree rises from 61.29 to 64.37 (MiniFlux-vid) and from 54.83 to 58.71 (AnimateDiff), while Overall Consistency increases from 27.42 to 27.94 and 27.04 to 27.36 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against same-architecture vanilla baselines, TPDiff consistently improves temporal quality: MiniFlux-vid-Ours gains +0.41 total score and +3.08 Dynamic Degree over MiniFlux-vid-Vanilla, while AnimateDiff-Ours gains +0.49 total and +3.88 DD over AnimateDiff. However, some spatial/aesthetic metrics trade off slightly (e.g., AnimateDiff-Ours drops from 67.16 to 62.19 on Aesthetic Quality; Wan-Ours drops from 97.44 to 92.19 on Motion Smoothness). Among external baselines, MiniFlux-vid-Ours (9B, 81.95) matches RepVideo (4B, 81.94) and exceeds CogVideoX-5B (80.91) and Videocrafter2 (80.44) in total score, though it trails larger models like Step-Video-T2V (30B, 81.83) and Open-Sora 2.0 (11B, 81.71) by narrow margins. The efficiency gains are the primary differentiator: 2.16× training speedup and up to 1.82× inference speedup (Wan) are achieved without degrading overall generation quality, whereas SimDA (1B) achieves lower total score (76.62) with no reported speedup advantage over TPDiff's approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

TPDiff extends the spatial pyramid idea from Pyramid Flow (Yang et al., 2024) to the temporal dimension, progressively increasing frame rate rather than spatial resolution across diffusion stages. It leverages the DPM-Solver relationship between arbitrary points on the ODE path to derive a unified stage-wise training objective applicable to both DDIM and flow matching, and incorporates data-noise alignment (Li et al., 2024) to make the per-stage ODE path approximately deterministic. The temporal pyramid concept itself is borrowed from video understanding (SlowFast, Temporal Pyramid Network), but its application to generative modeling with a single shared model across stages is new. Unlike prior cascaded approaches (Show-1, LaVie) that require separate super-resolution networks, TPDiff uses one model for all frame rates, avoiding error accumulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50% training cost reduction and 1.5× inference speedup are practically significant for a field where video diffusion training costs are already prohibitive, and the framework's generality across DDIM, flow matching, and multiple base architectures (MiniFlux, AnimateDiff, Wan) makes it broadly applicable. The zero-shot long-video generation capability, arising naturally from multi-frame-rate training exposure, is an interesting but under-explored side effect that could open new directions. Open questions include whether the constant-noise-per-stage approximation degrades at larger scales or with more aggressive reduction schedules, and whether the approach transfers to other modalities with similar inter-sample redundancy (e.g., audio, 3D).
