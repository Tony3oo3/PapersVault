## Problem

Low-rank plus sparse composite approximation for LLM compression faces two unresolved challenges: (1) existing methods treat the low-rank and sparse components as largely independent, lacking a principled mechanism for their interaction, and (2) redundancy varies substantially across layers, yet no method provides a globally coordinated way to allocate rank and sparsity budgets. Prior approaches rely on manually set singular-value thresholds and computationally expensive backpropagation, making them brittle and hard to scale.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full weight matrices of a pre-trained LLM (e.g., LLaMA-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B) along with a small calibration set of 128 sequences (context length sampled from C4). It outputs a compressed model where each weight matrix is represented as a factorized low-rank pair (U′, V′) with rank r′ plus a sparse matrix S masked by a binary pattern; the sparse component typically reaches >85% sparsity, enabling efficient SpMM at inference. The user specifies only a total parameter budget K (e.g., 50% of original parameters), and the method automatically determines per-layer rank and sparsity allocations.

## Method

CAP is a two-stage, training-free compression framework. Stage 1 applies Robust Principal Component Analysis (RPCA) via ADMM to decompose each weight matrix W into a low-rank component L (nuclear-norm regularized) and a sparse component S (L1-regularized), reducing the vast weight space into a low-dimensional singular-value subspace and a sparse outlier subspace. Stage 2 introduces Bernoulli random variables for each singular value in L and each non-zero entry in S, with learned retention probabilities optimized via a REINFORCE-style policy gradient on the calibration set, using a moving-average baseline for variance reduction. After a few gradient steps, the learned probabilities serve as global importance scores: the top-K parameters across all layers are retained, producing deterministic binary masks. The final low-rank component is factorized into U′ and V′ (absorbing √σ_i) for storage and inference efficiency, and the compressed matrix is reconstructed as U′(V′)ᵀ + S⊙m_S. This design eliminates manual thresholds, avoids backpropagation through original LLM weights, and automatically adapts rank and sparsity to each layer's redundancy.

## Results

At 50% compression, CAP achieves 69.12% zero-shot accuracy on Phi-3 Mini, 74.05% on Phi-3 Medium, 66.85% on LLaMA-3 8B, and 74.18% on LLaMA-3 70B, outperforming the best uniform-sparsity baseline OATS (68.41/73.39/65.71/73.30%) and Wanda (65.03/70.96/63.27/72.85%) across all four models. On modern instruction-tuned LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct at 50% sparsity, CAP recovers 56.8% GSM8K accuracy versus Wanda's 45.6% (+11.2 points) and 27.2% LongBench-v2 versus 25.1%. For pure low-rank SVD methods on LLaMA-7B, CAP achieves 5.85 WikiText-2 PPL at 20% retention versus 6.08 (Dobi-SVD), 7.12 (SVD-LLM v2), and 7.74 (Basis Sharing), with the gap widening dramatically at 60% (7.06 vs. 15.62 for Dobi-SVD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAP consistently outperforms all uniform-sparsity baselines (SparseGPT, Wanda, DSNoT, OATS) and layerwise-allocation baselines (OWL, AlphaPruning) at every compression ratio tested. At 50% sparsity on LLaMA-3 8B, CAP achieves 66.85% accuracy versus OATS (65.71%), Wanda (63.27%), and SparseGPT (64.66%), a 1.1–3.6 point margin; on Phi-3 Mini the gap widens to 0.7–4.1 points. Against joint compression methods at 50% sparsity, CAP (48.3% on OPT-13B, 59.2% on LLaMA-2-13B) surpasses SLiM-LoRA (48.0/57.9), JSQ (30.7/57.0), and L2QER (OOM/OOM), with the advantage most pronounced on larger models. Compared to pure low-rank SVD methods on LLaMA-7B, CAP's advantage grows with compression: at 60% retention, CAP's 7.06 PPL is 8.56 points better than Dobi-SVD (15.62) and 21.66 points better than Basis Sharing (28.72). Against the structured method LoSparse on DeBERTa-V3-base at 20% retention, training-free CAP (78.2 MNLI / 83.0 QNLI) trails LoSparse with fine-tuning (83.8/88.6) by ~5 points, but CAP with fine-tuning (86.8/91.8) exceeds LoSparse by 3.0/3.2 points, validating RPCA as a superior initialization. On GLUE with BERT-base, CAP (69.1 RTE / 86.2 MRPC / 92.3 SST-2 at 50% params) matches or exceeds distillation-based methods like TinyBERT and MetaDistill while requiring no teacher model or task-specific fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAP builds on two well-established tools—Robust PCA (Candès et al., 2011) for principled low-rank/sparse matrix decomposition and REINFORCE-style policy gradients (Williams, 1992) for discrete optimization—and combines them in a two-stage pipeline for LLM compression. It departs from prior low-rank+sparse methods (LoSparse, LPAF) that rely on manual SVD truncation thresholds and per-layer heuristics by replacing those with a global, budget-constrained allocation learned via Bernoulli masking. Unlike joint compression methods (SLiM, JSQ, L2QER) that use low-rank factors primarily to fit quantization errors, CAP treats the low-rank and sparse components as co-equal structures whose relative allocation across layers is itself the optimization target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, global-allocation framing offers a practical path to aggressive LLM compression without expensive fine-tuning, and the >85% effective sparsity of the sparse component enables meaningful inference speedups via SpMM. However, the policy-gradient stage is explicitly acknowledged as a heuristic with no global-optimality guarantee, and the method's performance is bounded by the quality of the RPCA decomposition in Stage 1. Open questions include whether the approach scales cleanly to 100B+ parameter models, how sensitive it is to the small calibration set (128 sequences), and whether the two-stage design can be unified into a single differentiable objective.
