## Problem

Existing decision-based black-box adversarial attacks assume all queries cost the same, but in practical settings (e.g., NSFW content moderation) flagged queries can be orders of magnitude more expensive than benign ones. Prior stealthy attacks only handled the extreme case where benign queries are free and replaced efficient Monte Carlo gradient estimation with a suboptimal OPT-style procedure. This paper asks how to build a general framework that handles arbitrary cost ratios while retaining the efficient core components of state-of-the-art attacks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a correctly classified source image (500 ImageNet validation samples) and access to a black-box classifier (ResNet-50, ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, or CLIP) that returns only binary decisions (flagged vs. non-flagged), not class scores. It produces a minimal ℓ2-norm adversarial perturbation that flips the classifier's decision, with the optimization objective being total query cost = Q_non-flagged + Q_flagged × c* for an arbitrary cost ratio c* ≥ 1, rather than simply minimizing total query count.

## Method

The framework introduces two plug-and-drop replacements for the core operations of decision-based attacks. Asymmetric Search (AS) replaces binary search by splitting the search interval in a 1:c* ratio at each step, reducing expected cost from Θ(c* log(1/τ)) to O(c* log_{c*+1}(1/τ)); it reduces to standard binary search at c*=1 and to sequential line search at c*=∞. Asymmetric Gradient Estimation (AGREST) shifts the Monte Carlo sampling center from the boundary point x_t toward the adversarial region by an overshooting value ω_t, and reweights high-cost versus low-cost queries via importance sampling with parameter β_t. Under a local linearity assumption, the optimal (ω*, β*, n*) are derived in closed form (Thm. 3), with ω* found by maximizing a one-dimensional objective using Nelder-Mead optimization and QUADPACK for the hyperspherical cap integral. The framework is compatible with HSJA, GeoDA, CGBA, and SurFree, and outperforms stealthy attacks even at c*=∞ while retaining the more efficient Monte Carlo gradient estimation that stealthy attacks discard.

## Results

The primary metric is median ℓ₂ perturbation norm (lower is better) under a fixed total query cost budget. At c*=10² with 150K cost on ResNet-50, AGREST alone reduces the ℓ₂ norm by ~40% across all gradient-based attacks (e.g., HSJA: 4.66→2.49; GeoDA: 4.02→2.19; CGBA: 2.22→1.63). Combining AGREST with Asymmetric Search (AS) yields further gains (e.g., A-HSJA: 2.16; A-GeoDA: 2.11). At the extreme c*=10³ with 250K cost, A-GeoDA achieves 5.78 vs. 10.80 for vanilla GeoDA (a 47% reduction), and A-SurFree achieves 7.59 vs. 17.49 for vanilla SurFree (a 57% reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla SurFree, HSJA, GeoDA, and CGBA, the asymmetric variants (A-SurFree, A-HSJA, A-GeoDA, A-CGBA) achieve substantially smaller ℓ₂ perturbations at high cost ratios: at c*=10³ on ResNet-50, A-GeoDA (5.78) nearly halves the vanilla GeoDA norm (10.80), and A-HSJA (12.28) reduces vanilla HSJA (23.72) by ~48%. At lower asymmetry (c*=2 or 5), improvements are modest (1–10%), consistent with the expectation that binary search and vanilla gradient estimation are already near-optimal when costs are similar. Against Stealthy HSJA (Debenedetti et al., 2024), all four asymmetric attacks outperform it by a wide margin at c*=10⁴, 10⁵, and ∞ on ResNet-50 (Fig. 3), despite retaining the more efficient Monte Carlo gradient estimation that stealthy attacks discard in favor of OPT-style estimation. The one notable exception is CGBA at c*=10³ on ResNet-50, where VA+AGREST (5.73) slightly outperforms the full A-CGBA (6.23), suggesting AS provides marginal benefit for CGBA's curved-path search at this setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decision-based black-box adversarial attack lineage (Boundary Attack → HSJA/GeoDA/CGBA/SurFree) to the asymmetric query-cost setting introduced by Debenedetti et al. (2024). While stealthy attacks addressed the extreme case c*=∞ by replacing binary search with an egg-dropping-inspired line search and discarding the efficient Monte Carlo gradient estimation in favor of OPT-style estimation, this paper handles arbitrary cost ratios c* by introducing Asymmetric Search (a cost-weighted interval split) and AGREST (a shifted, reweighted Monte Carlo estimator that retains the HSJA/GeoDA gradient approximation). The key conceptual departure is that both low-cost and high-cost queries contribute to total cost, and the optimal strategy balances them rather than simply minimizing high-cost queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework has direct practical relevance for content moderation systems (NSFW detection on platforms like X, Facebook, Google) where flagged queries carry real enforcement consequences, and it provides a drop-in replacement for the search and gradient estimation components of any existing decision-based attack. Limitations include a new hyperparameter m (overshooting scheduler rate) that requires per-attack tuning, reliance on a local linearity assumption around the decision boundary, and restriction to the binary source-vs-non-source setting. Open questions include generalizing to multi-class cost asymmetries, applying the framework to vision-language models and LLM jailbreak attacks, and whether the local linearity assumption holds for modern architectures like ViTs and CLIP in practice.
