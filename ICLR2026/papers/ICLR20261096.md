## Problem

Existing generative video frame interpolation methods are locked into a fixed-interpolation paradigm, producing a predetermined number of uniformly spaced intermediate frames. This rigidity prevents users from dynamically adjusting frame counts or frame rates, and prevents the model from reasoning about continuous motion dynamics between the two boundary frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261096/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a start frame x_0, an end frame x_1, and a user-specified timestamp list T = [0, t_1, …, t_n, 1] with arbitrary continuous values in (0,1), and outputs the corresponding intermediate frames. For long-term or streaming interpolation, it additionally consumes a prefix frame (last frame of the prior segment) and up to 8 motion conditioning frames. During tokenization, only spatial compression is applied (no temporal compression) so each latent maps 1-to-1 to a timestamp; training sequences span 3–21 frames with a maximum 2-second temporal gap, and inputs are resized to one of five predefined resolutions (e.g., 480×832, 832×480).

## Method

ArbInterp is built on the Wan2.1-T2V 1.3B DiT video generation model and introduces two core mechanisms. First, Timestamp-aware Rotary Position Embedding (TaRoPE) replaces the integer frame index in temporal RoPE with a continuous normalized timestamp t_k = (k−1)/(N−1) ∈ [0,1], so the model perceives each frame's true relative position rather than a fixed slot, enabling generation at any arbitrary timestamp without extra parameters. Second, for long-sequence interpolation the method decomposes generation into segments and applies an appearance-motion decoupling conditioning strategy: the last frame of the previous segment is prepended as a prefix latent for appearance consistency, while a Motion Semantic Extractor (temporally enhanced CLIP + Q-Former) compresses the last N frames into fixed-length motion tokens injected via cross-attention to preserve motion coherence. Training proceeds in three stages (10k + 5k + 5k steps on 8×96 GB GPUs over 50k OpenVid videos) using flow matching with token-replace conditioning on the boundary frames, and inference supports direct, segment-by-segment, or hierarchical strategies that reduce self-attention cost from O(N²) to O(N²/M).

## Results

On MultiInterpBench, ArbInterp achieves the best performance across all four interpolation ratios (2×, 8×, 16×, 32×) on every metric. At 32× interpolation, ArbInterp reaches FID 26.5, FVD 319.9, LPIPS 0.145, CLIPimg 0.906, and VBench Overall 0.8324, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline DynamiCrafter (FID 46.4, FVD 732.7, LPIPS 0.374, VBench 0.8060). Even at the simplest 2× setting, ArbInterp's FID of 44.9 and LPIPS of 0.076 far exceed DynamiCrafter's 83.6 and 0.249 respectively. The SVD-based variant (ArbInterp-SVD) also consistently outperforms all external baselines, confirming the generality of the TaRoPE design across architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ArbInterp outperforms all four external baselines (LDMVFI, TRF, GI, DynamiCrafter) across every interpolation ratio and every reported metric, with no losses or ties. The closest competitor is DynamiCrafter, which leads among baselines at each ratio but still trails ArbInterp by a wide margin: at 32×, ArbInterp reduces FID by 43% (26.5 vs. 46.4), FVD by 56% (319.9 vs. 732.7), and LPIPS by 61% (0.145 vs. 0.374) relative to DynamiCrafter. TRF and GI, both SVD-based methods, show the weakest performance, with TRF's FID reaching 108.5 at 2× and LPIPS of 0.500 at 32×. LDMVFI, the only latent-diffusion-based baseline, sits in the middle but is still clearly surpassed by ArbInterp on all metrics. Notably, even the SVD-based variant ArbInterp-SVD (which interpolates absolute positional encodings instead of using TaRoPE) beats all external baselines, isolating the benefit of the DiT+TaRoPE architecture as an additional ~15–20% FID improvement over the SVD variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the line of generative VFI methods that adapt pretrained video diffusion models (SVD, Wan) for keyframe interpolation, but departs from their fixed-position paradigm by reinterpreting temporal RoPE as a continuous timestamp signal (TaRoPE), inspired by RIFLEx's demonstration of RoPE's role in temporal sequencing. For long-sequence generation, it extends segment-wise autoregressive approaches (FramePack, StreamingT2V) with a novel appearance-motion decoupling strategy that uses a temporally enhanced CLIP + Q-Former to extract motion semantics, rather than relying on full latent concatenation or simple cross-attention. The framework is instantiated on the Wan 2.1 DiT architecture with flow matching, requiring only spatial (not temporal) compression to simplify the latent-to-timestamp correspondence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ArbInterp's TaRoPE mechanism offers a minimal, parameter-free way to inject continuous temporal control into any DiT-based video model, potentially applicable beyond VFI to tasks like variable-FPS generation or temporal editing. The practical efficiency (20k steps on 8 GPUs, 40% speedup over latent concatenation) makes the approach accessible. However, the paper acknowledges limitations: no text conditioning, a small 1.3B model, and a 50k-video training set constrain the quality ceiling for complex scenes. Open questions include whether TaRoPE generalizes to larger models and whether the appearance-motion decoupling scales to longer horizons without drift.
