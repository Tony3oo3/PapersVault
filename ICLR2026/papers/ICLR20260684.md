## Problem

Existing model merging methods either assume the task identity is known at inference time (making the problem trivially solvable by compression) or require training data and/or parameter training for their routing mechanisms. This paper tackles the more realistic and challenging setting where neither the task nor the correct classification head is known at test time, and no task-specific data is available for router training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260684/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single image (or text sequence for language tasks) as input with no task label provided. Inference is two-pass: the input first passes through a fixed merged model to extract intermediate activations at a chosen layer, then through an adaptively merged model for classification. The output is a single class label determined by taking the maximum logit across all classification heads of the selected tasks, effectively jointly identifying both the correct task and its predicted class.

## Method

MASS operates in two phases. In a one-time fixed merging step, it applies TSV-M (SVD-based low-rank merging with orthogonalization) to all task updates, preceded by a redundancy filter that discards task matrices whose flattened cosine similarity to already-accepted matrices exceeds a threshold ε, preventing similar tasks from dominating the shared subspace. At inference, a projection-based router computes the L2 residual of intermediate activations after orthogonal projection onto each task's right-singular-vector subspace; tasks with the smallest residuals (equivalent to MAP estimation under an isotropic Gaussian prior) are selected via a softmax-thresholded top-k rule. The selected subspaces are then adaptively merged into the pretrained backbone, and all corresponding classification heads are evaluated, with the highest logit across heads determining the final prediction. The entire procedure is training-free and data-free, requiring only a ~2× storage overhead (storing TSVs alongside the merged model) and ~2× forward-pass compute regardless of the number of tasks.

## Results

MASS achieves state-of-the-art MoErging performance across 8 of 9 benchmark configurations, recovering up to 98.6% normalized accuracy (ViT-L-14, 8 tasks) and 96.6% (ViT-L-14, 20 tasks) of individually fine-tuned model accuracy. On the 20-task benchmark, MASS reaches 90.9% normalized accuracy with ViT-B-32, 88.7% with ViT-B-16, and 96.6% with ViT-L-14, outperforming the best MoErging baseline (SMILE-2) by up to ~6 percentage points. Storage overhead is a constant 2× the base model regardless of task count, compared to 2.5×–14× for competing MoErging methods. On the GLUE language benchmark (8 Flan-T5-Base tasks), MASS achieves 98.6% normalized accuracy, the best among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MASS outperforms all seven baselines across nearly all configurations. Against Task Arithmetic and Consensus TA (fixed merging), MASS gains 15–25 percentage points in normalized accuracy, reflecting the advantage of adaptive routing over static weight summation. Compared to TSV-M (the fixed merging method MASS builds upon), routing adds a consistent ~5% normalized accuracy improvement (e.g., 96.5% vs. 91.8% on ViT-B-32/8 tasks). Against the isotropic merging baselines Iso-C and Iso-CTS, MASS leads by 2–6 points, with the gap widening at 20 tasks (90.9% vs. 86.2% for Iso-CTS on ViT-B-32). Among MoErging methods, MASS decisively beats WeMoE, which degrades severely at scale (76.3% at 20 tasks vs. MASS's 90.9% on ViT-B-32), and surpasses SMILE-2 by 2–7 points across all settings. On the shared 8-task/ViT-B-32 setting, MASS achieves 97.6% normalized accuracy versus TwinMerging's reported 95.3%, despite TwinMerging assuming oracle knowledge of the correct classification head while MASS selects both encoder subspaces and the output head at inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

MASS extends the Task Singular Vectors (TSV-M) framework, which performs layer-level SVD-based merging with orthogonalization to reduce inter-task interference, by adding an input-conditioned routing mechanism that selects which task subspaces to activate at inference. It departs from prior MoErging methods (SMILE, WeMoE, TwinMerging) that assume oracle knowledge of the correct classification head at test time, instead jointly routing both the encoder subspaces and the output head via a projection-based, data-free and training-free router. The redundancy-filtering step (discarding task updates whose flattened vectors exceed a cosine-similarity threshold before merging) is a novel preprocessing that prevents similar tasks from dominating the shared subspace.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MASS recovers ~98% of individually fine-tuned accuracy at a constant 2× storage cost regardless of task count, making it a practical drop-in replacement for ensembling in multi-task deployment where the task identity is unknown at inference. The finding that a simple geometric projection onto task singular-vector subspaces matches or exceeds trained MLP routers (which require validation data) validates the low-rank structure of fine-tuning updates as a sufficient routing signal. Open questions include the task-dependent optimal routing layer (variance up to 40% across tasks), whether the non-monotonic accuracy-vs-task-count behavior reflects genuine compositional benefits, and how the method generalizes to truly out-of-distribution tasks not represented in the merged subspaces.
