## Problem

Sparse crime records alone cannot reveal latent high-order crime patterns that depend on heterogeneous contextual factors (POIs, weather, 311 requests) with both spatial and criminal specificity, and prior methods that fuse auxiliary data via homogeneous graphs or simple feature concatenation fail to capture these dual-specific interactions. Additionally, crime data exhibits pronounced non-stationarity and concept drift, rendering conventional offline models ineffective as regional crime distributions shift over days to months.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264865/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 7 days of daily crime counts (N regions × C crime types) alongside multi-source auxiliary tensors (311 service requests, weather, POI density, socio-economic indicators) at the same spatial-temporal resolution, organized as X ∈ R^{N×T×C} and S ∈ R^{N×T×M}. It produces next-day crime quantity forecasts (continuous per-region, per-crime-type values) and binary occurrence predictions. The dataset is split 25:75 into a warm-up phase and an online streaming phase, with the online phase driving iterative fine-tuning and retraining.

## Method

ST-HHOL builds a two-level hierarchical hypergraph: a heterogeneous hypergraph where each crime node and its region-specific contextual nodes form a hyperedge to extract latent crime patterns with spatial/criminal specificity, followed by a homogeneous hypergraph that models high-order co-occurrence among those patterns across regions. A Partially-Frozen GPT-2 (2 layers, 768 hidden) serves as the spatio-temporal dependency learner, freezing FFNs to retain pretrained sequence priors while fine-tuning attention and LayerNorm for crime-specific dynamics. The online learning strategy partitions parameters into spatially-invariant Θ_s (heterogeneous hypergraph, updated via periodic retraining every T months) and temporally-dynamic Θ_d (homogeneous hypergraph + PF-LLM, updated via frequent fine-tuning every τ steps), explicitly disentangling long-term structural drift from short-term fluctuations to handle multi-scale concept drift.

## Results

On crime quantity prediction, ST-HHOL reduces average MAE and MAPE by 5.37%/9.21% (CHI), 3.52%/8.83% (NYC), 2.97%/5.85% (PHI), and 6.45%/11.32% (TOR) compared to the best baseline. On crime occurrence prediction, ST-HHOL achieves the highest Micro-F1 (0.644 on NYC, 0.715 on CHI), Macro-F1 (0.647, 0.717), and True Zero Rate (0.663, 0.736), outperforming the second-best by 0.94%, 1.09%, and 1.69% on NYC, and 0.70%, 0.70%, and 3.08% on CHI. The model also demonstrates superior robustness in low-frequency regions and competitive performance on the NYC Taxi scalability benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ST-HHOL consistently outperforms all 14 baselines across every dataset and crime type. Among re-trained offline models, AGCRN and MoSSL are the closest competitors (e.g., AGCRN achieves 0.99 MAE on CHI Theft vs. ST-HHOL's 0.95; MoSSL achieves 0.98 on NYC Larceny vs. 0.97), but ST-HHOL still leads by a meaningful margin with far lower variance (±0.01 vs. ±0.07–0.39). The dedicated online learning baselines (DLF, FSNet, OneNet) are substantially outperformed—ST-HHOL reduces MAE by 2.3–3.0× on CHI and by 1.5–1.8× on TOR—indicating that generic online update mechanisms are poorly suited to the abrupt, heterogeneous fluctuations in crime data. On occurrence prediction, ST-HHOL achieves the best Micro-F1, Macro-F1, and TZR on both NYC and CHI, with the largest gap (3.08%) on CHI TZR, confirming superior handling of sparse zero-occurrence scenarios. The scalability test on NYC Taxi shows ST-HHOL is competitive with purpose-built traffic models (e.g., 5.38 MAE pick-up vs. GMAN's 5.43), suggesting the framework generalizes beyond crime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends hypergraph-based crime prediction (ST-SHN, ST-HSL, HCL, ST-MoGE) by introducing a two-level hierarchical structure: a heterogeneous hypergraph that fuses multi-source contextual signals (POI, 311 requests, weather) with crime records to extract latent crime patterns, followed by a homogeneous hypergraph modeling co-occurrence among those patterns. For the spatio-temporal dependency learner, it adapts the partially-frozen LLM strategy from ST-LLM+ (Liu et al., 2025a) to crime forecasting, freezing GPT-2's FFNs while fine-tuning attention layers. The online learning component departs from generic drift-adaptation methods (DLF, FSNet, OneNet) by proposing a crime-specific two-phase schedule—biweekly fine-tuning for short-term fluctuations and bi-monthly retraining for long-term shifts—motivated by FFT analysis of crime series periodicity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap: most crime prediction models are offline and degrade under the pronounced non-stationarity of real crime data, while the hierarchical hypergraph design offers interpretable insights into which contextual factors drive which crime types in which regions. Limitations include evaluation on only four cities with four crime types each at daily granularity, and the 47M-parameter PF-LLM backbone (98% from GPT-2) raises questions about deployment cost in resource-constrained municipal settings. Open questions include generalizability to cities with very different urban structures, finer temporal/spatial resolution (hourly, block-level), and whether the partially-frozen LLM strategy transfers to other sparse spatio-temporal domains beyond crime.
