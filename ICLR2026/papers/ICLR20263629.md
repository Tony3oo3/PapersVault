## Problem

In RAG systems, conflicts between a model's parametric knowledge and retrieved context create a reliability dilemma: the model may be outdated while the context is noisy or adversarial, yet existing alignment methods are unidirectional (either factuality-focused or context-faithfulness-focused) and offer no mechanism for bidirectional, fine-grained control over which knowledge source to trust. The paper asks whether it is possible to smoothly and controllably shift an LLM's reliance between its internal parameters and external context at inference time without retraining.

## Input / Output

CK-PLUG operates entirely at inference time during autoregressive decoding, consuming the standard RAG prompt (query + retrieved passages) plus a single scalar tuning parameter α ∈ [0, 1] that governs the parametric-to-contextual balance. In autonomous mode, α is eliminated and computed on-the-fly from the ratio of contextual to parametric entropy. The output is a standard generated text response, but internally the next-token probability distribution is replaced for conflict-flagged tokens with a fused distribution over the top-k union of parameter-aware and context-aware candidates, while non-conflicting tokens pass through unmodified.

## Method

CK-PLUG introduces a Confidence Gain (CG) metric that computes the entropy difference H(p(x|X_q)) − H(p(x|X_r+X_q)) at each decoding step to flag tokens where retrieved context introduces a knowledge conflict (CG < threshold). For flagged tokens, the log-probability is decomposed into a parameter-aware component q_para = log p(x|X_q) and a context-aware component q_cont = log p(x|X_r+X_q) − log p(x|X_q), isolating the context's marginal contribution. A weighted fusion F = α·q_para + (1−α)·q_cont is then applied, constrained to the top-k union of both distributions (following adaptive plausibility constraints), and softmaxed to produce the final next-token distribution. Non-conflicting tokens retain the original RAG distribution unchanged, preventing catastrophic quality degradation from indiscriminate modulation. In adaptive mode, α is set to H_cont/(H_para + H_cont), letting the model's own confidence in each knowledge source automatically determine the balance without manual specification.

## Results

On LLAMA3-8B, CK-PLUG modulates Memory Recall (MR) on NQ from 9.9% (α=0.0, context-reliant) to 71.9% (α=1.0, parameter-reliant), compared to a baseline of 43.5%, demonstrating a wide and smooth control range. Similar broad adjustments are observed on ConFiQA (14.9%–62.5%, baseline 29.2%) and MQuAKE (26.9%–76.5%, baseline 48.9%). In adaptive (α-free) mode, CK-PLUG consistently improves average performance across six KILT RAG tasks, e.g., LLAMA3-8B improves from 42.3% (standard RAG) to 43.5%, and QWEN2.5-7B from 45.4% to 45.9%, while maintaining generation fluency (hit rate within 1–3% of baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external knowledge-conflict resolution methods; instead, it compares CK-PLUG against two internal baselines: standard RAG (w/ RAG) and no-retrieval generation (w/o RAG). In the knowledge-control setting (Table 1), the standard RAG baseline sits at intermediate MR values (e.g., 43.5% on NQ for LLAMA3-8B), while CK-PLUG at α=0.0 pushes MR down to 9.9% (strong context reliance) and α=1.0 pushes it up to 71.9% (strong parameter reliance), with α=0.5 closely matching the baseline (42.1%), confirming smooth, near-linear modulation. In the adaptive-enhancement setting (Table 2), CK-PLUG outperforms both w/o RAG and w/ RAG baselines on all four models across six KILT tasks; for instance, LLAMA3-8B improves from 30.7% (w/o RAG) and 42.3% (w/ RAG) to 43.5% average, and QWEN2.5-7B from 27.2% and 45.4% to 45.9%. The ablation (Table 3) shows that removing the Confidence Gain conflict-detection module causes hit rates to drop sharply (e.g., LLAMA3-8B falls from 83.7% baseline to 53.8% at α=0.0 without ConD), confirming that selective token-level intervention is essential for maintaining generation quality during modulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

CK-PLUG builds on the contrastive decoding framework of Li et al. (2022), which decomposes token predictions into parametric and contextual components, but repurposes this decomposition as a weighted fusion mechanism controlled by a single hyperparameter α rather than as a fixed contrastive objective. It departs from prior knowledge-conflict resolution work (e.g., context-faithful prompting, factuality alignment, parametric pruning) by treating the parametric-vs-contextual balance as a continuously tunable, bidirectional knob at inference time rather than a one-directional alignment target. The entropy-based Confidence Gain metric for conflict detection draws on uncertainty quantification in token logits but applies it specifically as a token-level activation switch for selective modulation, avoiding the quality collapse that indiscriminate probability adjustment would cause.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free nature of CK-PLUG makes it immediately deployable in existing RAG pipelines, and the single-parameter control offers a practical interface for operators to tune knowledge reliance based on retrieval quality or model freshness without retraining. The adaptive (α-free) mode, which automatically balances reliance via entropy-based confidence, addresses the practical difficulty of manual parameter tuning and yields consistent gains across six diverse KILT tasks. Limitations include evaluation restricted to 7–8B open-source models, reliance on a second forward pass (query-only) at every decoding step, and the somewhat artificial nature of counterfactual-context benchmarks; open questions include scalability to larger or multimodal models, interaction with iterative or agentic RAG, and whether the linear α–MR relationship holds under more realistic, noisy retrieval distributions.
