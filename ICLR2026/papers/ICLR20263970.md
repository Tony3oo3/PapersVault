## Problem

Existing incomplete multimodal deep learning methods face a discarding-imputation dilemma: recovery-free approaches lose task-relevant information by ignoring missing modalities, while recovery-based approaches risk injecting noise or semantically misaligned content from unreliable reconstructions. No prior method adaptively decides, per sample, which recovered modalities are genuinely informative versus harmful at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263970/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, DyMo receives an incomplete multimodal sample (any non-empty subset of available modalities such as RGB images, text, or structured tables) along with recovered versions of the missing modalities produced by an external recovery model (e.g., a VAE or image-tabular reconstructor). The network processes arbitrary modality subsets via a transformer with attention masks over dummy tokens for absent modalities, and outputs a single class label. The dynamic selection algorithm iteratively decides which recovered modalities to incorporate before the final prediction, so the effective input composition varies per test sample.

## Method

DyMo combines three components: (1) a multimodal transformer architecture with modality-specific encoders (CNNs for images, MLPs/transformers for text and tables) that handles arbitrary modality subsets; (2) an inference-time dynamic selection algorithm that scores each recovered modality using a Multimodal Task-Relevant Information Reward (MTIR), derived from a theoretical lower bound on mutual information between representations and labels via Hoeffding's inequality, with the empirical CE loss decrease serving as a tractable proxy for information gain; the reward is computed as a Bregman-divergence distance shift toward class prototypes (inspired by prototypical networks) and refined by an asymmetric intra-class similarity calibration term that down-weights unrepresentative samples; (3) a training strategy that randomly samples modality subsets per batch for classification loss plus an auxiliary missing-agnostic contrastive loss to enforce intra-class clustering. The iterative selection loop adds the highest-reward recovered modality at each step and discards all non-positive-reward candidates, ensuring only informative recoveries are fused.

## Results

On PolyMNIST with 80% missing modalities, DyMo_e achieves 96.81% accuracy, outperforming the best recovery-based baseline (OnlineMAE, 90.09%) by 6.72% and the best recovery-free baseline (MTL, 91.14%) by 5.67%. On MST with the most task-relevant modalities {M,T} missing, DyMo_e reaches 86.84% accuracy vs. 85.16% for M3Care† and 84.37% for MTL. On the medical UK Biobank Infarction task with 70% missing tabular features, DyMo_e achieves 76.14% AUC, surpassing M3Care† (75.88%) and MTL (75.73%). DyMo also achieves 13.12% higher accuracy than prior dynamic fusion SOTAs on PolyMNIST at 80% missing rate and 4.11% higher accuracy on DVM with full table missing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyMo outperforms all 13 baselines on the majority of challenging missing scenarios. Against recovery-based methods, DyMo_e surpasses OnlineMAE by 6.72% on PolyMNIST (η=0.8) and by 2.70% on MST ({M,T}), while M3Care† is the closest competitor, matching DyMo on DVM (γ=1: 93.43 vs. 93.36) and CAD (γ=1: 72.48 vs. 72.17) but trailing by 1.68% on CelebA ({T}) and 1.97% on Infarction (γ=0.7). Against recovery-free methods, DyMo leads MTL by 5.67% on PolyMNIST (η=0.8), 2.47% on MST ({M,T}), and 5.82% on CelebA ({T}); MUSE and MAP collapse severely under severe missingness (e.g., MUSE drops to 35.96% on MST {M,T}). Against dynamic fusion SOTAs (QMF, DynMM, PDF, CONCAT), DyMo achieves 13.12% higher accuracy on PolyMNIST at 80% missing and 4.11% on DVM with full table missing, as prior dynamic methods struggle with semantically misaligned VAE reconstructions that DyMo's MTIR reward explicitly filters out. The only near-tie is on CAD (γ=1), where DyMo_e (72.17%) is within 0.31% of M3Care† (72.48%), likely because the recovered table modality is consistently informative across samples, leaving limited room for dynamic selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

DyMo departs from the binary paradigm in incomplete multimodal learning—where methods either impute all missing modalities (MoPoE, M3Care, OnlineMAE) or discard them entirely (ModDrop, MTL, MUSE)—by introducing a third option: selectively fusing only the recovered modalities that contribute task-relevant information. Theoretically, it builds on prototypical networks (Snell et al., 2017) to formulate the MTIR reward in feature space, and on a Hoeffding-based mutual information bound to justify using classification loss decrease as a proxy for information gain. It also extends the dynamic fusion line (QMF, DynMM, PDF), which handles low-fidelity modalities via modality-specific confidence scores, by addressing the harder case of semantically misaligned recoveries through an intra-class similarity calibration term. The multimodal transformer backbone with arbitrary-modality support follows the architecture of MTL and MAP, while the incomplete simulation training strategy generalizes ModDrop's random dropout to sampled modality subsets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discarding-imputation dilemma is a genuine and previously unarticulated gap: recovery-free methods lose information when highly task-relevant modalities are missing, while recovery-based methods inject noise when reconstructions are unreliable. DyMo's modular design (agnostic to the recovery method, no extra architecture overhead at inference) makes it practically deployable in healthcare and marketing settings where data incompleteness is routine. Limitations include: the calibration term can be counterproductive when recovered modalities are consistently informative (CAD/Infarction), the method requires training-set prototypes at inference, and evaluation is restricted to classification. Open questions include extension to dense prediction tasks (segmentation), video modalities, and whether the information-theoretic reward generalizes to settings without a well-defined class structure.
