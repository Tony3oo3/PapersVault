## Problem

Pretrained routers in MoE LLMs assign semantically similar samples to distinct experts with dramatically different routing weights, creating a manifold misalignment between the task embedding space and the routing weight space that causes a 10–20% accuracy gap relative to oracle routing. Existing routing optimization methods (Dense BP, C3PO, prefix/prompt tuning) improve routing quality in isolation but do not explicitly enforce geometric consistency between task understanding and expert assignment across samples, leaving the core generalization bottleneck unaddressed.

## Input / Output

RoMA consumes a set of 49K text samples with ground-truth labels, pre-computed task embeddings from an external embedding model (e.g., NV-Embed-v2, 7.8B), and the per-layer routing weights (softmax outputs) produced by the MoE model's routers. It outputs updated router parameters restricted to the last 5 layers (only 0.0095% of total model parameters, 658K trainable), which produce better-aligned routing weights at inference time with zero additional FLOPs. The routing weights are concatenated across all L MoE layers into a single L×K vector per sample, and the regularization operates on this full-layer representation rather than per-layer weights.

## Method

RoMA introduces a manifold regularization term that penalizes the weighted L2 distance between a sample's concatenated routing weights and those of its k=3 nearest successful neighbors (samples whose MoE predictions are correct) in the task embedding space, with weights given by normalized Gaussian similarity. The training objective combines standard cross-entropy loss on model outputs with this manifold alignment term (scaled by λ), and only router parameters in the last 5 layers are updated via backpropagation while all expert parameters remain frozen. The key mechanism is that by pulling each sample's routing pattern toward those of its semantically similar, correctly-predicted neighbors, RoMA creates coherent task-to-expert bindings that transfer knowledge across related inputs, effectively bridging the gap between pretrained routing and oracle routing. Ablations confirm that regularizing the last token's routing weights in the last 5 layers with k=3 neighbors is optimal, and the manifold regularizer outperforms generic alternatives (L1, L2, entropy) by 5–9% average accuracy.

## Results

RoMA achieves 76.2% average accuracy on OLMoE-7B-A1B (+8.6% over base), 74.7% on DeepSeekMoE-16B-A3B (+8.1%), and 79.5% on Qwen3-30B-A3B (+5.5%), consistently outperforming all baselines across eight benchmarks. The largest single-benchmark gains are on MMLU: +11.2% for OLMoE (57.8→69.0%), +10.6% for DeepSeekMoE (46.2→56.8%), and +4.6% for Qwen3 (74.2→78.8%). Notably, OLMoE-7B-A1B+RoMA with only 1B active parameters surpasses several 7–8B and 13B dense models, while Qwen3-30B-A3B+RoMA (3B active) exceeds 27–34B dense models on MMLU. RoMA achieves these gains by fine-tuning only 0.0095% of model parameters (routers in the last 5 layers) with zero additional inference cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoMA outperforms C3PO (the strongest test-time routing baseline) on all three models: +0.5% avg on OLMoE (76.2 vs 75.7), +0.5% on DeepSeekMoE (74.7 vs 74.2), and +0.9% on Qwen3 (79.5 vs 78.6), while maintaining the same inference cost (C3PO requires 6–7× more FLOPs due to test-time optimization). Against training-based baselines, RoMA leads Oracle Tuning by +1.4% (OLMoE), +2.0% (DeepSeekMoE), and +1.5% (Qwen3) in average accuracy, and Dense BP by +5.0%, +4.9%, and +3.0% respectively. Lightweight adaptation methods (ICL, Prefix Tuning, Prompt Tuning, Router Tuning) all trail RoMA by 4–8% in average accuracy across models. In cross-model comparisons (Table 2), OLMoE-7B-A1B+RoMA (1B active) surpasses Llama3.1-8B (57.7% MMLU) and Qwen1.5-14B (66.7% MMLU), while Qwen3-30B-A3B+RoMA (3B active) exceeds Gemma2-27B (75.2% MMLU) and Yi-34B (73.5% MMLU). RoMA also outperforms PEFT methods (LoRA, DoRA, MoLE) applied to routers by +3.7% to +8.6% average accuracy while introducing zero new parameters at inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoMA repurposes classical manifold regularization (Belkin et al., 2006) for a new target: aligning MoE routing weights with task-embedding geometry, rather than the original setting of preserving neighborhood structure in output representations. It departs from prior routing-optimization work (C3PO, Dense BP, load-balancing losses) by explicitly incorporating the geometric structure of a pretrained embedding space into the fine-tuning objective, rather than optimizing routing purely through task loss or test-time search. The motivation draws on recent findings that MoE routing weights implicitly encode semantic structure (Li & Zhou, 2025) and that routing pathways become more structured during the memorization-to-generalization transition (Li et al., 2025c), but RoMA is the first to enforce this alignment as an explicit training signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RoMA addresses a concrete, quantified bottleneck (10–20% oracle gap) in MoE LLMs with an extremely lightweight intervention (0.0095% of parameters, no inference overhead), making it immediately deployable on existing MoE checkpoints. The finding that small active-parameter MoEs (1–3B) can rival 27–34B dense models after routing alignment has practical implications for cost-sensitive deployment. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to generative/open-ended tasks (current evaluation is multiple-choice), whether the embedding model's quality becomes a hard ceiling at larger scales, and whether the last-5-layers finding generalizes to architectures with different MoE layer distributions.
