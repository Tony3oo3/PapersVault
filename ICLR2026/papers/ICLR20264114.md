## Problem

Verbalized confidence scores from LLMs are empirically overconfident and saturated at high values, making them unreliable for selective prediction and trust calibration. The paper identifies an underexplored root cause: LLMs exhibit heightened 'suggestibility' on topics they know little about, assigning high confidence to multiple mutually exclusive claims simultaneously. This incoherence across related claims is neither detected nor corrected by existing calibration methods.

## Input / Output

Given a short-form question or a long-form generation decomposed into atomic claims, the method takes the LLM's main answer/claim plus 5 self-generated distractor claims (produced via beam search, pseudo-beam search, or direct prompting) and independently elicits a verbalized confidence (P(True) or numerical percentage) for each claim. An off-the-shelf NLI model (DeBERTa-v3, ~184M params) scores entailment and contradiction relationships between claims to compute uniqueness and counterfactuality weights. The output is a single calibrated confidence score in [0,1] per claim, combining the normalized verbalized confidence with a self-consistency estimate.

## Method

DINCO (Distractor-Normalized Coherence) corrects for suggestibility bias by normalizing the main claim's verbalized confidence by the total weighted confidence across self-generated distractors: f^NVC(c₀) = f^VC(c₀) / max(1, f^VC(c₀) + Σ f^VC(c)·w_unique(c)·w_contra(c)). The NLI-based weights w_unique downweight distractors entailed by other distractors (handling redundancy) and w_contra downweights distractors that do not contradict the main claim (ensuring mutual exclusivity). To capture a complementary axis of coherence, DINCO averages this normalized validation confidence with a self-consistency estimate (proportion of sampled generations matching the main answer): f^DINCO(c) = ½ f^SC(c) + ½ f^NVC(c). The entire pipeline is training-free, requires no task-specific tuning, and operates with a fixed inference budget of K=10 (5 distractors + 5 SC samples), working in both gray-box (logit access) and black-box (text-only) settings.

## Results

On TriviaQA, DINCO achieves an average ECE of 0.097 (Qwen3-8B), outperforming the best baseline MSP (0.149) by 0.052, and reaches AUC 0.879 vs. MSP's 0.819. On SimpleQA with GPT-4.1, DINCO reduces ECE to 0.089 versus MSP's 0.263 (a 0.174 improvement), though MSP edges out DINCO on AUC (0.800 vs. 0.786). On long-form FactScore (Qwen3-8B), DINCO achieves ECE 0.076 vs. SC's 0.162, and improves Pearson/Spearman correlation with passage-level FactScore by 0.072/0.074 over the best baseline. Across all three settings, DINCO improves ECE over the best baseline by an average of 0.077, 0.092, and 0.055 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DINCO consistently outperforms all five baselines on ECE and Brier score across TriviaQA, SimpleQA, and FactScore. Against MSP (the strongest overall baseline), DINCO achieves substantially lower ECE (e.g., 0.089 vs. 0.263 on SimpleQA/GPT-4.1; 0.097 vs. 0.149 on TriviaQA/Qwen3-8B), though MSP retains a slight AUC advantage in one setting (0.800 vs. 0.786 on SimpleQA/GPT-4.1). Against self-consistency (SC), DINCO improves ECE by 0.049–0.060 on short-form tasks and by 0.076–0.086 on long-form FactScore, confirming that integrating both generation and validation coherence is more effective than generation coherence alone. The NVC component (distractor normalization without SC) already outperforms SC, but DINCO's full combination is more consistently calibrated, particularly on AUC (e.g., 0.786 vs. 0.729 on SimpleQA/GPT-4.1). Against verbalized confidence (VC/P(True)) and top-K prompting (K-VC), DINCO shows large margins (e.g., ECE 0.097 vs. 0.240 for VC and 0.341 for K-VC on TriviaQA/Qwen3-8B), demonstrating that naive confidence elicitation is severely miscalibrated. On long-form FactScore, DINCO outperforms the long-form SC variant (Zhang et al., 2024a) by 0.086 ECE (Qwen3-8B) and achieves the highest Pearson (0.518) and Spearman (0.538) correlations with passage-level FactScore.

## Relation to Prior Work

DINCO builds on two well-established confidence estimation approaches: P(True) verbalized confidence (Kadavath et al., 2022) and self-consistency (Xiong et al., 2024), which the paper reframes as measuring coherence along the validation and generation axes, respectively. The key departure from prior verbalized-confidence work (e.g., K-VC from Tian et al., 2023) is the use of independent prompting on self-generated distractors rather than joint prompting, which exposes probabilistic incoherence that joint prompting masks because the model can satisfy coherence via simple arithmetic. The NLI-based reweighting of distractors (uniqueness and counterfactuality) is a practical addition that distinguishes DINCO from naive normalization, and the generator-validator disagreement literature (Li et al., 2024; Rodriguez et al., 2025) motivates the final equal-weight combination of the two coherence signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DINCO provides a training-free, zero-resource calibration method that works across short-form QA, long-form biography generation, and both gray-box and black-box settings, which is practically important for off-the-shelf LLM deployment. The finding that DINCO at 10 inference calls outperforms self-consistency at 100 calls (a 10× efficiency gap that scaling cannot close) suggests that the validation-axis signal from distractor normalization captures information fundamentally unavailable from generation-axis sampling alone. Open questions include whether the 32% FLOP overhead is acceptable in latency-sensitive settings, whether the lightweight NLI model (DeBERTa-v3-base) remains sufficient for highly technical domains beyond biomedical, and whether a smaller model can generate adequate distractors to reduce cost below that of self-consistency.
