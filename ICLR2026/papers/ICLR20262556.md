## Problem

Existing text-to-motion generation methods rely on end-to-end supervised mapping that fails to capture the temporal and causal structure of complex natural language instructions, producing oversimplified or incoherent motions. Meanwhile, RL-based approaches like MotionRL and MotionCritic improve quality but are overly complex and overengineered, limiting their scalability and adaptability across diverse motion tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262556/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes free-form natural language descriptions of human motion (e.g., 'a person ties their shoelaces and stands up') and produces a structured output consisting of a decomposed Chain-of-Thought reasoning trace (breaking the description into temporally ordered atomic sub-actions) followed by a sequence of discrete motion tokens. These tokens are decoded by a VQ-VAE motion tokenizer back into continuous 3D joint-position trajectories. The output format is strictly enforced as think-tags containing CoT followed by Motion-tags containing tokens, and generation is conditioned on the reasoning trace to ensure temporal coherence.

## Method

Motion-R1 is a two-stage framework built on a VQ-VAE motion tokenizer and a Qwen-2.5-3B-Instruct LLM backbone. In the first stage, a Decomposed CoT Data Engine uses DeepSeek-R1 to automatically synthesize structured reasoning traces that decompose high-level instructions into atomic sub-actions with explicit temporal ordering; a self-verification pipeline (also using DeepSeek-R1) filters traces for redundancy and hallucination, reducing error rates from roughly 20% to 3%. These (description, CoT, motion) triplets provide cold-start supervised fine-tuning. In the second stage, RL Binding applies GRPO with a composite reward comprising three components: a motion similarity reward (cosine similarity between generated and ground-truth motion embeddings via a pretrained encoder), a semantic similarity reward (cosine similarity between motion and text embeddings in a shared latent space), and a binary format reward enforcing the output structure. This dual-alignment reward design eliminates the need for costly human preference annotations while simultaneously optimizing for motion realism and text fidelity.

## Results

On HumanML3D, Motion-R1 achieves the best R-Precision@2/3 (0.719/0.818), the lowest MM-Dist (2.854), and the highest Diversity (10.026), with a competitive FID of 0.201. On KIT-ML, it sets new records for R-Precision@1/2/3 (0.431/0.638/0.761) and FID (0.287). On BABEL, Motion-R1 leads all four metrics, including R-Precision (0.536), FID (0.53), MM-Dist (6.16), and Diversity (8.90), surpassing the strongest baseline InfiniDreamer by a wide margin on FID (0.53 vs. 1.14).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On HumanML3D, Motion-R1 outperforms all 11 baselines on R-Precision@2/3, MM-Dist, and Diversity, but trails MoMask (FID 0.045), T2M-GPT (0.116), and MotionGPT-2 (0.191) on FID, where its score of 0.201 is competitive but not best. On KIT-ML, Motion-R1 dominates all 8 baselines on R-Precision@1/2/3 and FID (0.287 vs. next-best 0.404 from MLD), though MotionDiffuse achieves a lower MM-Dist (2.958 vs. 3.196). On BABEL, Motion-R1 sweeps all four metrics against TEACH, DoubleTake, DiffCollage, and InfiniDreamer, with FID nearly halved (0.53 vs. 1.14) and R-Precision improved by 1.4 points over InfiniDreamer (0.536 vs. 0.522). The closest overall competitor is MoMask on HumanML3D, which leads on FID and R-Precision@1 but is surpassed by Motion-R1 on every other metric. Inference time is comparable to MotionLLM (2.23 s vs. 2.11 s), confirming that the performance gains do not come at a speed cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

Motion-R1 sits at the intersection of two active threads in text-to-motion generation: VQ-VAE-based motion tokenization (following T2M-GPT and MotionGPT) and LLM-driven generation (MotionLLM, MotionAgent). Its key departure is the introduction of structured Chain-of-Thought reasoning into the motion generation pipeline, inspired by DeepSeek-R1's RL-incentivized reasoning, combined with GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) as the RL backbone. Unlike prior RL-based motion methods (MotionRL, MotionCritic) that rely on human-annotated preference models or critic networks, RL Binding replaces these with lightweight multi-modal cosine-similarity rewards, positioning the work as a simplification and unification of the CoT + RL paradigm for a new modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in removing the human-annotation bottleneck from RL-based motion generation: by using pre-trained motion and text encoders for reward computation and LLM-generated CoT traces for supervision, the entire pipeline is automatable. This makes RL-enhanced motion generation more accessible and scalable to new datasets or action vocabularies. Open questions remain around the quality ceiling of LLM-generated CoT under ambiguous or highly compositional instructions, the sensitivity of reward design to encoder choice, and whether the approach extends to longer-horizon or multi-agent motion sequences. The paper acknowledges these limitations and points toward adaptive reward learning and interactive feedback as future directions.
