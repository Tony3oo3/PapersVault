## Problem

Existing 3D latent representations either model geometry alone or encode appearance as view-independent diffuse color, making them unable to capture realistic view-dependent effects such as specular highlights and Fresnel reflections. This paper asks whether a compact latent code can jointly represent both 3D geometry and the full angular variation of surface radiance, and whether such a representation supports high-fidelity reconstruction and single-image-to-3D generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263411/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tokenizer encoder consumes ~1 million randomly subsampled surface light field points (x, d̂, c) — each a 3D surface location, unit viewing direction, and RGB color — extracted by back-projecting 150 RGBD images (1036×1036, 40° FOV) rendered on a sphere of radius 3.5. The encoder outputs 8192 latent vectors of dimension 32, which are decoded into a flow-matching velocity field for geometry (yielding point clouds or meshes) and into 3D Gaussians with degree-3 spherical harmonics for view-dependent radiance. For generation, a single RGB image (encoded by DINOv2-large) conditions a DiT flow-matching model that produces the same latent code, from which a full 3D object with view-consistent materials is rendered.

## Method

The core contribution is a Perceiver IO encoder that ingests surface light field samples including viewing directions, using a K-nearest-neighbor patchification scheme to approximate 2D patching on scattered 3D surface points and a shifting voxel-grid self-attention for efficiency at ~1M input tokens. Geometry is supervised via a flow-matching velocity decoder (inherited from Shape Tokens) that models surfaces as a 3D probability density, while appearance is supervised by rendering degree-3 spherical-harmonic 3D Gaussians from random viewpoints and matching ground-truth images with L2 + LPIPS loss. A KL regularization term keeps the 8192×32 latent space compact. For generation, a 623M-parameter DiT with DINOv2 image conditioning is trained via flow matching on the learned latent distribution, with the world coordinate rotated per sample so the output aligns with the input view at identity orientation, eliminating the need for post-hoc alignment.

## Results

On appearance reconstruction (Toys4k, TRELLIS lighting), LiTo achieves PSNR 34.16 / SSIM 0.985 / LPIPS 0.023 at simple zoom and PSNR 32.36 / SSIM 0.967 / LPIPS 0.055 at hard zoom, outperforming TRELLIS (PSNR 31.12 / 27.57) by ~3 dB in PSNR across all settings. On geometry (Chamfer distance ×10⁴), LiTo without a mesh decoder scores 94.08 / 88.30 / 98.66 on PBR-Objaverse / Toys4k / GSO, competitive with TRELLIS (95.16 / 92.21 / 105.5) while using a 10× smaller latent space and no coarse geometry oracle. For single-image-to-3D generation, LiTo achieves FID 6.22 and KID 0.009 at the conditioning view versus TRELLIS's FID 12.84 and KID 0.088, while maintaining comparable novel-view quality (FID 6.22 vs. 7.60).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TRELLIS (Xiang et al., 2025), LiTo wins decisively on appearance: PSNR improves by ~3 dB (34.16 vs. 31.12 simple; 32.36 vs. 27.57 hard) and LPIPS drops from 0.034 to 0.023 on Toys4k, attributable to modeling view-dependent radiance via degree-3 spherical harmonics rather than mean-pooled DINOv2 features. On geometry, LiTo (no oracle) is competitive with TRELLIS on PBR-Objaverse (94.08 vs. 95.16) and GSO (98.66 vs. 105.5) but slightly behind on Toys4k (88.30 vs. 92.21), while using a 10× smaller latent (8192×32 vs. ≈20k×11) and no coarse occupancy input. Against geometry-only baselines TripoSG (Li et al., 2025a) and Shape Tokens (Chang et al., 2024), LiTo substantially outperforms on all three datasets (e.g., 88.30 vs. 299.7 and 119.8 on Toys4k), despite also encoding appearance. Against TripoSF (He et al., 2025), LiTo is competitive on PBR-Objaverse (94.08 vs. 83.11) and Toys4k (88.30 vs. 76.99) without requiring a geometry oracle. Against 3DTopia-XL (Chen et al., 2025b), LiTo is far more robust, avoiding the extreme outliers (412.5 on PBR-Objaverse) that plague the PrimX-based approach. For generation, LiTo halves the conditioning-view FID (6.22 vs. 12.84) and KID (0.009 vs. 0.088) over TRELLIS while maintaining similar novel-view FID (6.22 vs. 7.60), confirming that the view-dependent latent enables both higher fidelity to the input and comparable overall quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D latent tokenization line of work (ShapeToken, CLAY, TripoSG, TRELLIS) by introducing surface light field encoding as the input signal, where each sample carries position, viewing direction, and color rather than just position or position+color. It reuses the flow-matching velocity decoder from Chang et al. (2024) for geometry and borrows TRELLIS's pretrained sparse-structure VAE for occupancy prediction, but departs fundamentally in the appearance modeling: where TRELLIS mean-pools DINOv2 features (discarding angular variation) and 3DTopia-XL uses fixed PBR parameters, LiTo encodes full view-dependent radiance via degree-3 spherical harmonics in a 3D Gaussian decoder. The K-nearest-neighbor patchification of 3D surfaces for efficient cross-attention is a novel architectural contribution not present in prior Perceiver-based 3D tokenizers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first 3D latent representation to faithfully reproduce view-dependent effects (specular highlights, Fresnel reflections) from a compact token set, closing a gap that has limited the realism of learned 3D assets. The practical impact is significant: single-image-to-3D generation now produces objects whose materials respond correctly to novel viewpoints, which is essential for applications like AR/VR and game asset creation. Key limitations include the degree-3 spherical harmonic ceiling (constraining transparency and high-frequency specularities) and the requirement for 150 RGBD views during training. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-object scenes, whether higher-order SH or alternative radiance models can break the transparency barrier, and whether one-step flow matching (e.g., MeanFlow) can reduce the current ~9s sampling cost.
