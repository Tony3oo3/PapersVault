## Problem

Existing parameter-modifying knowledge editing methods (ROME, MEMIT, AlphaEdit) fail to preserve fine-grained irrelevant knowledge — facts sharing the same subject as the edited fact but differing in relation and object — because subject representations inherently encode multiple attributes, entangling target and unrelated knowledge in the representation space. This paper identifies this gap, constructs a dedicated benchmark (FINE-KED) to measure it, and proposes a disentanglement-based editing approach that explicitly isolates and protects fine-grained irrelevant knowledge during edits.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a knowledge triple (s, r, o, o*) specifying the edit, along with the LLM's subject representation h_s (last-token hidden state at a designated layer) and relation representation h_r (last-token of the prompt p(s,r) at a potentially different layer). It produces a closed-form rank-one update to the FFN output weight matrix W at the editing layer, computed as W + (W₃ᵀW₃ + E)⁻¹ · Δ_MEMIT, where Δ_MEMIT is the standard MEMIT update and W₃ is a fixed projection from the pre-trained KRD module. The KRD module is trained once offline on ~20k samples and frozen; each individual edit requires only a forward pass through the disentangler and a small optimization of the target-related component δ.

## Method

DiKE comprises two modules. The Knowledge Representation Disentanglement (KRD) module decomposes the subject representation into a target-knowledge-related vector z_e^r and a target-knowledge-unrelated vector z_e^u via two linear projections with GELU activation, trained jointly with three objectives: an InfoNCE contrastive loss that maximizes mutual information between each component and the original representation while treating them as a negative pair, a knowledge constraint loss that forces z_e^r to predict the target object and z_e^u to predict fine-grained irrelevant objects, and an MSE reconstruction loss ensuring the recomposed representation recovers h_s. The Disentanglement-based Knowledge Edit (DKE) module then optimizes a perturbation δ on z_e^r alone to encode the new fact, computes the corresponding FFN value v*, and enforces invariance of the unrelated component by adding ‖W₃(Ŵk* − v₀)‖² and ‖W₃(ŴK₀ − V₀)‖² terms to the standard MEMIT least-squares objective. This yields a closed-form rank-one solution that differs from MEMIT only by a left-multiplication with (W₃ᵀW₃ + E)⁻¹, making the fine-grained preservation constraint computationally negligible while explicitly preventing the edit from altering the disentangled unrelated subspace.

## Results

On FINE-KED (the paper's new benchmark for fine-grained irrelevant knowledge preservation), DiKE achieves the highest Relational Locality across all three models: 70.6% average on LLaMA-3 (8B), 71.3% on GPT-J (6B), and 55.0% on GPT2-XL (1.5B), while maintaining near-perfect Efficacy (99.1%, 99.1%, 97.4% respectively). The largest gains appear on the Hard difficulty level (highly similar relations), where DiKE reaches 72.4% R-Loc on LLaMA-3 versus 69.2% for the best baseline (MEMIT-C). On COUNTERFACT, DiKE achieves a harmonic-mean score of 92.4% on LLaMA-3, slightly outperforming MEMIT (91.1%) and ROME (90.8%), confirming competitive general editing performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiKE outperforms all five baselines on Relational Locality (fine-grained irrelevant knowledge preservation) across all three models and all difficulty levels. On LLaMA-3, DiKE's 70.6% average R-Loc surpasses AlphaEdit (65.6%), MEMIT-C (66.3%), MEND (65.2%), MEMIT (61.5%), and ROME (53.0%) by 5.0, 4.3, 5.4, 9.1, and 17.6 percentage points respectively. The gap is most pronounced on the Hard level (highly similar relations), where DiKE reaches 72.4% versus 69.2% for MEMIT-C and 47.8% for ROME. On COUNTERFACT, DiKE matches or slightly exceeds the strongest baselines (MEMIT 91.1%, ROME 90.8%) with a 92.4% harmonic mean on LLaMA-3, showing that the disentanglement constraint does not sacrifice general editing quality. The constrained variants ROME-C and MEMIT-C, which manually add fine-grained relational constraints per edit, improve over their unconstrained versions but still fall 4–8 points short of DiKE, confirming that ad-hoc constraint sampling is insufficient compared to learned representation disentanglement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the locate-then-edit paradigm established by ROME and MEMIT, which treat FFN layers as key-value associative memory and perform rank-one weight updates. Where ROME/MEMIT directly modify the entangled subject representation and AlphaEdit adds coarse-grained null-space constraints, DiKE introduces a representation-disentanglement step (via InfoNCE contrastive learning and reconstruction) that decomposes the subject representation into target-related and target-unrelated components before editing. The resulting closed-form update is a minimal modification of MEMIT's solution, pre-multiplying the rank-one delta by (W₃ᵀW₃ + I)⁻¹ to enforce invariance of the disentangled unrelated component. The approach also draws on contrastive representation learning (He et al., 2020) and the knowledge-neuron literature (Geva et al., 2023) for its motivation that subject representations encode multiple relational attributes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies a concrete failure mode—degradation of fine-grained irrelevant knowledge (same subject, different relation)—that prior editing methods handle poorly, and provides both a targeted method and a dedicated benchmark (FINE-KED) to measure it. The FINE-KED benchmark with its three difficulty levels (Easy/Middle/Hard based on relational similarity) is a useful addition for the community evaluating preservation guarantees. Limitations include the restriction to structured (s,r,o) triples, the approximation of omitting activation functions in the constraint derivation, and the one-time KRD training that may not generalize to entirely new domains without retraining. Open questions include whether disentanglement-based editing extends to unstructured or semi-structured knowledge, and whether the approach remains effective as model scale increases beyond 8B parameters.
