## Problem

Existing offline RL methods rely on a single shared global Q-function, which fails to capture the compositional structure of tasks where knowledge from one subtask does not transfer to another and geometrically similar states can exhibit distinct long-horizon returns. The paper identifies that in-context learning, particularly via linear attention, has been underexplored as a mechanism for adaptive, local value estimation in offline settings without requiring explicit subtask labels or predefined task decomposition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261314/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query state-action pair (s_query, a_query) and an offline dataset D, from which it retrieves the top-k transitions with the smallest L2 state distance to form a local context set Ω. The feature extractor φ maps each state-action pair to a 64-dimensional vector (tanh-bounded), which is arranged into a prompt matrix Z_0 of shape (2d+1)×(k+1) where the last column holds the query features with masked reward and next-state entries. The output is a context-dependent local Q-value Q̂(s_query, a_query | Ω) produced by the bottom-right element of the linear Transformer output, and a policy extracted via advantage-weighted regression (IQL variant) or behavior-cloning-regularized actor updates (TD3+BC variant); after training, the policy is evaluated independently without further retrieval.

## Method

ICQL formulates Q-learning as a contextual inference problem: for each query state, it retrieves k nearby transitions and feeds them as a prompt into an L-layer linear Transformer whose layers implement SARSA-like weight updates in-context. The prompt matrix Z_0 stacks feature vectors φ(s_i,a_i), discounted next-state features γφ(s_i',a_i'), and rewards r_i for each retrieved transition, with the query column having zeroed reward and next-state entries; the linear attention operation LinAttn(Z;P,G)=PZM(Z^T GZ) with structured P and G matrices (containing trainable C_ℓ ∈ R^{d×d} per layer) iteratively updates a local weight vector w_s across layers, so the final output is w_s^L(Ω)^T φ(s_query, a_query). Training follows the IQL recipe: the critic is optimized via expectile regression on the local Q-estimate, and the policy is extracted via advantage-weighted regression using the context-dependent advantage Q̂(s,a|Ω) − V(s|Ω). Theoretically, under linear approximability of the local Q-function and a set-coverage assumption on the retrieved context, the greedy policy achieves a bounded performance gap of O(ε_approx(1+B_φ) + B_φ√(d+log(1/δ))/(σ|Ω|)).

## Results

On MuJoCo tasks, ICQL achieves an average score of 80.6, outperforming the second-best baseline CQL (74.0) by 8.8%. On Adroit tasks, ICQL scores 35.3 on average, improving over IQL (33.2) by 6.3%. On Kitchen tasks, ICQL reaches 66.8 average, a 16.4% improvement over the best baseline DT (55.8), with the largest single-task gain of 22.0% on Kitchen-Complete (79.3 vs. 52.5). In the extended comparison against 10 baselines (Table 7), ICQL achieves an overall average of 69.7, surpassing IQL (63.2), DMG (62.0), and ReBRAC (60.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICQL outperforms all five primary baselines (BC, DT, TD3+BC, CQL, IQL) on average across all three task families, with the largest margins on Kitchen (+16.4% over DT) and MuJoCo (+8.8% over CQL). On Adroit, ICQL beats IQL by 6.3% on average, with particularly large gains on Door-Human (+73.1%) and Door-Cloned (+53.6%), though it underperforms IQL on Pen-Human (85.6 vs. 89.5) and Hammer-Human (3.7 vs. 7.2), which the authors attribute to smaller dataset size and larger retrieval distances. In the extended comparison (Table 7) against 10 baselines including RA-DT, ReBRAC, DMG, FQL, and QC, ICQL achieves the highest overall average (69.7) and wins on 14 of 18 tasks, losing only on Hopper-Medium (62.6 vs. DMG 92.3), HalfCheetah-Medium-Expert (89.1 vs. QC 94.2), and Kitchen-Mixed (59.5 vs. QC 60.0). The Q-value analysis (Figure 3) shows ICQL's estimates correlate with online SAC at a similarity score of 0.69, compared to 0.29 for IQL, confirming that the performance gains stem from more accurate local value estimation rather than policy optimization differences.

## Relation to Prior Work

ICQL builds directly on IQL's training recipe (expectile regression for the critic, advantage-weighted regression for the actor) and on Wang et al. (2025b), who showed that linear attention layers can implement TD-style updates in-context for Markov Reward Processes. The key departure is extending that mechanism from MRPs to full MDPs with state-action features, and replacing the global Q-function with a family of local Q-functions inferred from retrieved transitions via a linear Transformer. This positions ICQL between classical value-regularization offline RL (CQL, IQL, TD3+BC) and in-context learning literature, but unlike Decision Transformer or Gato, it targets value estimation rather than action generation, and unlike hierarchical RL (MAXQ, Option-Critic), it requires no subtask labels or predefined structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The compositional value-estimation framing addresses a real limitation of global Q-functions in multi-stage tasks, and the 16.4% gain on Kitchen tasks (where subtask structure is explicit) provides concrete evidence that local inference helps. The theoretical guarantee (bounded approximation error under linear approximability and set coverage) gives the method a principled foundation beyond empirical tuning. However, retrieval quality in high-dimensional state spaces remains a practical bottleneck, and the paper acknowledges that more sophisticated retrievers (pre-trained encoders, value-aware learnable retrievers) are needed. The moderate gains on MuJoCo (8.8%) and the negative result on Hammer-Human suggest the approach is most beneficial when local structure is well-defined and data density is sufficient.
