## Problem

Unified multimodal models that jointly perform understanding and generation still lag behind tightly-coupled systems like GPT-4o in instruction following and fine-grained detail preservation for text-to-image generation. Prior work has explored single-step self-CoT reasoning to guide image synthesis, but has not investigated whether multi-turn interleaving reasoning—alternating text-based thinking with image generation and reflection—can further improve visual quality, textures, and fine-grained fidelity beyond what a single generation pass achieves.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263604/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single text prompt as input and produces a two-turn 'text-image-text-image' trajectory: an initial reasoning text, an initial 1024×1024 image, a reflection/improving reasoning text, and a refined 1024×1024 image. Between turns, the initial image is encoded into ViT and VAE features (denoted I_f) and fed back as visual context for the reflection step. A dedicated dual-condition CFG strategy (comparing with/without the initial image features and with/without the reflection text, both at guidance scale 2.0) is applied during the second image generation to maintain fidelity and stability.

## Method

IRG builds on the BAGEL unified integrated transformer (pre-trained on interleaved text-image data) and formulates generation as a two-turn pipeline: T_in → T_out^(1) → I_out^(1) → [encode] → T_out^(2) → I_out^(2). Training follows a two-stage IRGL paradigm with six decomposed learning modes split into two objectives: (1) learning text-based thinking (Initial/Improving Thinking Understanding and Generation Learning) and (2) learning full thinking-image trajectories (Initial/Improving Full Learning). Stage 1 trains on all six modes for 2K steps to build reasoning capability, while Stage 2 fine-tunes on full thinking-image trajectories for 30K steps to optimize the end-to-end IRG pipeline. The IRGL-300K dataset is constructed by using Qwen2.5-VL to generate reasoning texts and GPT-4o to produce high-quality target images, with the base model's own outputs serving as the 'initial image' in paired initial→improved samples. A key design choice is that reflection focuses on refining fine-grained details and visual quality (textures, shadows, finger structures) rather than correcting major semantic errors, distinguishing IRG from prior reflection-based T2I methods.

## Results

IRG achieves state-of-the-art performance among unified models across all five benchmarks: GenEval Overall 0.85 (+0.06 over BAGEL w/ self-CoT), WISE Overall 0.77 (+0.07), TIIF 76.00/73.77 short/long (+8.0/+5.0), GenAI-Bench Overall 0.84 (+0.03), and OneIG-EN Overall 0.415 (+0.091). On GenEval, IRG also surpasses GPT-4o (0.85 vs. 0.84) and FLUX.1-dev (0.85 vs. 0.82), with notable sub-scores of 0.83 on Counting and 0.74 on Position. On WISE, IRG leads all unified models with consistent gains across domains (Biology 0.81, Physics 0.82), though GPT-4o retains a slight edge at 0.80 overall. The ablation confirms that the six decomposed learning modes contribute the largest single improvement (WISE 0.70→0.77, TIIF 68.06→76.00) over the self-CoT baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IRG outperforms its direct base model BAGEL w/ self-CoT on every benchmark: GenEval 0.85 vs. 0.79 (+0.06), WISE 0.77 vs. 0.70 (+0.07), TIIF 76.00 vs. 68.06 (+7.94), GenAI-Bench 0.84 vs. 0.81 (+0.03), and OneIG-EN 0.415 vs. 0.324 (+0.091). Against the strongest generation-only model FLUX.1-dev, IRG wins on GenEval (0.85 vs. 0.82), WISE (0.77 vs. 0.50), and TIIF (76.00 vs. 66.24), demonstrating that interleaving reasoning in a unified model surpasses dedicated diffusion generators. Compared to GPT-4o, IRG achieves a narrow win on GenEval (0.85 vs. 0.84) but trails on WISE (0.77 vs. 0.80), TIIF (76.00 vs. 84.19), and OneIG-EN (0.415 vs. 0.533), indicating that the proprietary model still holds an advantage in instruction following and text rendering. Among open-source unified models, IRG sets new SOTA on GenEval, WISE, TIIF, and GenAI-Bench, surpassing Show-o2 (0.76/0.61/62.80/0.75), Janus-Pro-7B (0.80/0.35/65.38/–), and MetaQuery-XL (0.80/0.55/–/–). The ablation in Table 3 further shows that simply adding high-quality image training to the self-CoT baseline yields only modest gains (WISE 0.70→0.73), while the full IRG pipeline with decomposed learning modes delivers the largest jump (WISE 0.70→0.77, TIIF 68.06→76.00), confirming that the interleaving reasoning structure itself—rather than merely better training data—is the primary driver of improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on BAGEL, a unified multimodal understanding-and-generation model pre-trained on interleaved text-image data, extending its self-CoT capability from a single reasoning pass to a multi-turn 'text-image-text-image' trajectory. It departs from prior reflection-based T2I methods (MM-Interleaved, Lumina-Next, OmniGen2, Thinking with Generated Images) that use reflection to correct major semantic or structural errors, instead targeting fine-grained visual quality and aesthetic refinement in an end-to-end fashion. The six decomposed learning modes and the IRGL-300K dataset represent a new training paradigm that addresses the scarcity of complete multi-turn reasoning-image trajectories by learning text-based thinking as a data-efficient proxy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is positioned as an early exploration of interleaving reasoning in T2I, and the results are encouraging but reveal an important tension: benchmark scores for the initial and refined images are nearly identical (sometimes the initial image scores slightly higher), yet MLLM judges and human annotators consistently prefer the refined output—exposing a persistent gap between what standard T2I benchmarks measure and what humans perceive as quality. Key open questions include whether the approach scales beyond two turns (the paper shows degradation at steps 3–4), how to construct complete IRG training data at scale without relying on GPT-4o distillation, and whether the doubled inference cost (~60s vs ~30s) is acceptable for practical deployment. The decomposed learning strategy and IRGL-300K dataset provide a reusable recipe for future work in reasoning-augmented generation.
