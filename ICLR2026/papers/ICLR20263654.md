## Problem

Personalized image preference assessment—evaluating which of two candidate images a specific user would prefer based on a small set of their reference images—remains largely unexplored. Existing methods target general preferences (text-image alignment, aesthetics) trained on large-scale shared data, but individual user data is scarce, non-scalable, and spans complex multi-dimensional tastes (art style, color, medium, etc.) that simple similarity scores cannot capture or explain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263654/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a small set of user reference images (both preferred and non-preferred, typically 5 pairs) and two candidate images as input. It produces a structured CoT-style response: first a predicted visual preference profile (across 5 elements—art style, color, detail, art medium, saturation—drawn from a 288-term vocabulary) and non-preference profile, then per-dimension interpretable scores for each candidate, and finally a binary preference decision. The output is fully text-based reasoning with explicit alignment annotations (red for preference, blue for non-preference) linking each score back to the predicted profile.

## Method

PreferThinker introduces a preference profile as a bridge across users: although individual tastes are unique, the underlying visual elements are shared, enabling large-scale cross-user training for profile prediction. The model follows a predict-then-assess CoT paradigm—first inferring the user's preference/non-preference profile from reference images, then using it as explicit criteria to score candidates across multiple dimensions before producing a final decision. Training is two-stage: cold-start SFT on the newly constructed PreferImg-CoT dataset (60K samples with Claude 3.7-annotated CoT reasoning) to instill structured reasoning, followed by GRPO-based reinforcement learning to explore diverse assessment paths and improve generalization. A key contribution is a similarity-aware prediction reward that combines SBERT text similarity and DreamSim image similarity (via T2I-generated images from predicted vs. ground-truth profiles) to incentivize accurate profile prediction, which in turn guides more reasonable downstream assessments.

## Results

PreferThinker achieves 88.7% average assessment accuracy across all test splits, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art (Claude 3.7 at 83.6%) by +5.1 points. On the proposed PreferImg benchmark, it reaches 96.6% (Seen-SP), 92.0% (Seen-MP), 96.4% (Unseen-SP), and 92.8% (Unseen-MP), surpassing the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 21–30 points. On the real-user PickaPic dataset, PreferThinker scores 65.7%, ranking second only to PickScore (67.9%), which benefits from PickaPic being its training data. Without explicit CoT, PreferThinker still achieves 93.2% on PreferImg at 0.80s inference speed, the fastest among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PreferThinker (88.7% avg) outperforms all 14 baselines on the PreferImg benchmark, beating the strongest closed-source MLLM Claude 3.7 (83.6%) by +5.1 points and GPT-4o (83.5%) by +5.2 points. The closest personalized method, ViPer (81.2%), degrades sharply on multi-preference users (78.0% Seen-MP vs. PreferThinker's 92.0%) and lacks interpretability. CLIP-based methods (49–55% avg) and general MLLM-based reward models (51–56% avg) perform near chance on personalized assessment, confirming their inability to model individual preferences. On the real-user PickaPic dataset, PreferThinker (65.7%) trails only PickScore (67.9%), which was trained directly on PickaPic data; all other methods score below 66%. The ablation (Table 2) further shows that removing either the SFT cold-start or the GRPO RL stage causes 3–10 point drops, and adding the similarity-aware prediction reward yields an additional +1.7–6.6 points on profile prediction accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MLLM-based image preference assessment (UnifiedReward, LLaVA-Reward) and the single personalized method ViPer by introducing a predict-then-assess CoT paradigm grounded in a learned preference profile. It adopts GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) as the RL post-training algorithm, following the broader trend of applying group-relative policy optimization to MLLMs for reasoning tasks. The key conceptual departure is the preference profile—a fixed set of five visual elements (art style, color, detail, art medium, saturation) that serves as a shared vocabulary bridging individual users, enabling large-scale cross-user training despite per-user data scarcity. Unlike ViPer, which implicitly uses reference images for score regression, PreferThinker explicitly predicts the profile first and then conditions multi-dimensional scoring on it, yielding interpretable reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The preference-profile-as-bridge idea offers a practical recipe for tackling the data-scarcity problem in personalized assessment, and the released PreferImg/PreferImg-CoT datasets (80K users, 1.36M images, 60K CoT samples) provide the first large-scale benchmark for this setting. However, the evaluation is dominated by synthetic users with controlled profiles; on the real-user PickaPic benchmark, PreferThinker's advantage over GPT-4o and Claude 3.7 essentially vanishes (65.7 vs 65.7/64.9), raising open questions about whether the five-element profile captures the subtlety of genuine human taste. The predicted profile's secondary use for personalized image generation is promising but underexplored, and the authors acknowledge that content semantics, cultural context, and user demographics remain unmodeled.
