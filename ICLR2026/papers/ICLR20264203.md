## Problem

High-quality computer use trajectory data is extremely scarce, creating a critical bottleneck for training open-source agents that can match proprietary systems like Claude 3.7 Sonnet. Existing approaches either rely on large-scale human annotation or end-to-end distillation from teacher models, both of which are costly and suffer from error accumulation. This paper asks whether a very small set of human demonstrations can be efficiently augmented to elicit frontier-level computer use capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264203/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the agent receives a single 1280×720 screenshot, a natural-language task description, and a textual log of prior thoughts and actions (no past screenshots are included). It produces a free-form thought process followed by exactly one action drawn from a unified 12-action space (click, right-click, double-click, drag, scroll, key press, hotkey, type text, wait, finish, fail), executed via PyAutoGUI. The ReAct-style scaffold keeps the prompt structure identical between training and inference, with the history field containing only main-trunk human actions to match the decision context available at each step.

## Method

The core contribution is Trajectory Boost: each step of a 312-trajectory human dataset is treated as an environment snapshot (task, screenshot, history), and Claude 3.7 Sonnet is prompted to sample 9 alternative single-step thought-action pairs in parallel, producing a tree where the human action forms the trunk and synthesized actions branch as leaves. A preceding Thought Completion step reconstructs the implicit reasoning behind each human action using an iterative prompt to Claude. This yields 27K training samples from only 312 trajectories, which are used for supervised fine-tuning of Qwen2.5-VL-72B (frozen visual tower, 8192-token context, 2 epochs on 32 GPUs). The key design choice is single-step augmentation rather than end-to-end trajectory distillation, which avoids compounding errors while still injecting the frontier model's diverse planning strategies; the paper shows this outperforms both human-data-only training (17.2 vs. 36.0) and direct distillation (26.2 vs. 36.0) on WindowsAgentArena-V2, and is ~300× faster in data collection time.

## Results

On WindowsAgentArena-V2 (141 tasks across 11 Windows applications), PC Agent-E achieves a 36.0% overall success rate, representing a 141% relative improvement over the base model Qwen2.5-VL-72B (14.9%) and a 10% relative improvement over the teacher model Claude 3.7 Sonnet (32.6%). The model also outperforms Claude 3.7 Sonnet with extended thinking (35.4%) and all open-source baselines including UI-TARS-72B-DPO (26.2%) and UI-TARS-1.5-7B (21.3%). On OSWorld (Linux), PC Agent-E achieves 14.9% total success rate (10.9% on feasible tasks), a 34% relative improvement over the base model's 11.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PC Agent-E (36.0%) decisively outperforms all open-source baselines: UI-TARS-72B-DPO (26.2%), UI-TARS-1.5-7B (21.3%), and the base Qwen2.5-VL-72B (14.9%), with the largest gap being the 141% relative improvement over the base model. Against proprietary models, PC Agent-E surpasses Claude 3.7 Sonnet (32.6%) by 10% in relative terms and narrowly edges out Claude 3.7 Sonnet with extended thinking (35.4%) by 0.6 points, despite the teacher model not having thinking mode enabled during data synthesis. GPT-4o performs poorly at 2.1%, indicating that general-purpose VLMs lack specialized computer-use capability. On the original WindowsAgentArena benchmark, PC Agent-E (27.9%) also surpasses the previous SOTA NAVI framework (19.5%). The paper further shows that Trajectory Boost outperforms direct distillation from Claude 3.7 Sonnet (26.2%) and a combined human+distillation baseline (26.9%), while requiring only 3 hours of synthesis versus ~900 hours for online distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data-synthesis-for-reasoning paradigm (inspired by DeepSeek-R1, S1, and similar work showing that frontier-model-generated data efficiently improves LLM reasoning) to the domain of computer use agents. It departs from prior computer-use data synthesis efforts (Synatra, AgentTrek, OS-Genesis) by grounding augmentation in real human trajectories rather than generating trajectories from scratch, and it differs from standard distillation by performing single-step action sampling on human environment snapshots rather than end-to-end trajectory collection. The native-agent-model framing (single VLM acting step-by-step in ReAct style) follows the dominant paradigm established by Anthropic's computer use and UI-TARS, while the WindowsAgentArena-V2 benchmark is a direct revision of the original WindowsAgentArena to fix evaluation integrity issues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 300× speedup over direct distillation and the ability to train a competitive agent from only 312 human trajectories address the core data-scarcity bottleneck that has held back open-source computer use agents. The discovery of 'infeasible hacking'—where weaker models exploit the evaluation metric for infeasible tasks—exposes a methodological flaw affecting multiple existing benchmarks and motivates more careful evaluation design. Key limitations include the modest absolute performance (36% success rate), the Windows-only training data, the performance degradation beyond 30 steps due to lack of termination awareness, and the modest gains on the 7B model, suggesting the approach primarily benefits models with sufficient base knowledge and grounding. Open questions include whether Trajectory Boost scales to more diverse task distributions, whether the student-surpassing-teacher effect holds at larger data scales, and how to incorporate longer-horizon supervision for robust task termination.
