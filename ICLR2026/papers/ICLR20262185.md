## Problem

Next-token prediction (NTP) suffers from teacher forcing and exposure bias, limiting long-horizon reasoning and planning. Multi-token prediction (MTP) partially alleviates this by predicting several future tokens, but it captures only short-range dependencies and requires one auxiliary head per future token, making it impractical to scale to longer horizons. The paper asks how to provide effective long-range future supervision with a single, scalable auxiliary objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262185/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The forward model receives a text prefix x_{≤t} and produces standard next-token logits as its primary output. A single auxiliary head additionally outputs a summary vector of the future window (x_{t+2}, …, x_{t+τ}): either a multi-hot bag-of-words vector over the full vocabulary (FSP-BoW) or a dense hidden-state embedding from a same-sized reverse language model (FSP-RevLM). At inference time, the auxiliary head is discarded and only the standard NTP head is used, so the method adds no inference-time overhead.

## Method

Future Summary Prediction (FSP) augments the standard NTP loss with a single auxiliary head that predicts a compact summary of the long future rather than individual future tokens. Two variants are explored: (1) FSP-BoW constructs a multi-hot target indicating which tokens appear in the future window and trains the auxiliary head with a tf-idf-reweighted binary cross-entropy loss; (2) FSP-RevLM trains a reverse language model (RevLM) on right-to-left sequences and uses its hidden state as a learned future embedding, which the forward model's auxiliary head matches via an L2 loss. The key insight is that summarizing the entire future trajectory substantially reduces teacher forcing compared to predicting a few immediate tokens, while a single auxiliary head keeps the approach scalable. Synthetic experiments on path-star graphs and sibling discovery show that long-range summaries are necessary for planning and that learned (adaptive) summaries outperform hand-crafted ones when only part of the future is informative. At 8B scale, FSP-RevLM yields up to 5% gains over NTP and MTP on math and coding benchmarks.

## Results

At the 8B-parameter scale (1T tokens), FSP-RevLM achieves the strongest overall performance across six benchmarks, with headline gains of +4.8 points on ARC-Easy (76.6% vs. 71.8% NTP), +4.2 points on MATH (35.1% vs. 30.9% MTP), and +6.3 points on HumanEval+ (54.1% vs. 47.8% NTP). The method also leads on ARC-Challenge (55.9%), GSM8K (70.5%), and MBPP (68.3%). At the 3B scale (250B tokens), FSP-RevLM is competitive with DeepSeek-MTP overall but clearly wins on math reasoning (GSM8K: 43.6% vs. 41.7%; MATH: 21.2% vs. 20.1%), and its relative advantage grows substantially when scaling to 8B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard MTP (Gloeckle et al., 2024), FSP-RevLM wins on 5 of 6 benchmarks at 8B scale, with the largest margin on MATH (+4.2 points, 35.1% vs. 30.9%) and ARC-Easy (+3.0 points), while tying on HumanEval+ (54.1% each) and also leading on GSM8K (+2.7 points) and MBPP (+1.1 points). Against DeepSeek-MTP (Liu et al., 2024), FSP-RevLM achieves a clear sweep at 8B: it leads on ARC-Easy by +14.9 points (76.6% vs. 61.7%), ARC-Challenge by +13.3 points, MATH by +1.6 points, MBPP by +0.5 points, HumanEval+ by +1.5 points, and GSM8K by a marginal +0.1 point. At the 3B scale, DS-MTP is the stronger overall baseline (leading on ARC-Easy, ARC-Challenge, MBPP, and HumanEval+), but FSP-RevLM still wins on both math tasks (GSM8K +1.9, MATH +1.1) and on ARC-Easy, and its advantage over DS-MTP widens substantially at 8B, suggesting FSP-RevLM scales more favorably. The one consistent exception across all comparisons is GSM8K at 8B, where NTP (71.6%) narrowly edges out FSP-RevLM (70.5%), though FSP-RevLM still improves over MTP (67.8%) on that task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-token prediction (MTP) framework of Gloeckle et al. (2024) by replacing the prediction of individual future tokens with prediction of a compact future summary, unifying MTP, random-future-token variants, and the proposed bag-of-words and reverse-LM objectives under a single 'Future Summary Prediction' (FSP) lens. The learned-summary variant (FSP-RevLM) directly extends the reverse-model hidden-state matching mechanism of Twin Networks (Serdyuk et al., 2017) from RNNs to Transformers, while the hand-crafted BoW variant connects to SemFormer (Yin et al., 2024) and set-level prediction objectives (Frydenlund, 2025). The paper departs from DeepSeek-MTP and random-sampling approaches (TRELAWNEY, MuToR, L-MTP) by arguing that heuristically selecting which future tokens to predict is inferior to learning an adaptive summary that focuses on informative content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

In the data-wall regime where compute continues to grow but high-quality training data is finite, FSP offers a principled way to extract more long-horizon signal from fixed corpora, with up to 5% gains on math and coding at 8B scale. The synthetic path-star and sibling-discovery experiments provide a clear mechanistic account of *when* and *why* different future-summary strategies succeed or fail, which is valuable for designing future pretraining objectives. Key open questions include whether the reverse-LM teacher can be replaced by a cheaper mechanism (e.g., self-distillation or learned projections) to avoid the ~2× compute overhead, and whether the gains persist at 70B+ scale or with longer context windows.
