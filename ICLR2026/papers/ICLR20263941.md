## Problem

Long video generation with Diffusion Transformers is bottlenecked by the quadratic scaling of full attention with sequence length, making minute-level generation intractable. Existing sparse attention methods rely on blockwise coarse estimation, whose accuracy-efficiency trade-off is fundamentally constrained by block size: larger blocks reduce computation but degrade selection precision. The paper argues that such coarse-grained estimation is unnecessary and that each token should be precisely allocated to a semantically coherent group.

## Input / Output

The model takes a global text prompt plus per-shot concise descriptions (shot-level textual conditioning) and produces end-to-end multi-shot videos at 480p. The Wan2.1-based variant generates 477 frames at 16 fps (30 s, ~187k tokens), while the MMDiT-based variant extends to 1,441 frames at 24 fps (60 s, ~578k tokens). Each shot is guided by its own description through cross-modal attention, enabling scene transitions and event orchestration at precise time points that a single global prompt cannot control.

## Method

MoGA replaces block-level scoring with a lightweight, learnable token router (a single linear layer followed by softmax gating) that assigns each token to one of M groups, then performs full self-attention independently within each group, reducing complexity from O(N²) to O(N²/M). The router's weights act as implicit cluster centers, enabling direct assignment to learnable anchors without global similarity estimation. To balance long-range coherence with local fidelity, MoGA is coupled with spatiotemporal group attention (STGA), which restricts attention to local windows in latent video space; each token receives outputs from one dynamic MoGA group and two static STGA groups, averaged as the final output. A group balancing loss (adapted from MoE load balancing) prevents router collapse into a few groups. The full system is fine-tuned from pretrained DiT backbones (Wan2.1-1.3B/14B and MMDiT) with a rectified flow objective, and integrates seamlessly with FlashAttention and sequence parallelism since it is kernel-free.

## Results

On 5-second single-shot generation (Wan2.1-14B), MoGA achieves 71.25% sparsity while matching or exceeding full-attention Wan on Subject Consistency (0.9699 vs. 0.9611), Image Quality (0.6994 vs. 0.6680), and Aesthetic Quality (0.5810 vs. 0.5807), while outperforming all sparse baselines (DiTFastAttn, SVG, VMoBA) across every metric. For 30-second multi-shot long video generation, MoGA (14B) substantially outperforms the IC-LoRA+Wan pipeline on Subject Consistency (0.9572 vs. 0.8946) and Background Consistency (0.9475 vs. 0.9169). The MMDiT-based MoGA model (M=20) generates 1,441 frames at 24 fps (60 seconds) at 480p with a context length of ~578K tokens, achieving a 5.33× inference speedup over full attention at 30-second duration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against sparse attention baselines on 5-second generation, MoGA (71.25% sparsity) dominates all competitors: it surpasses DiTFastAttn (50% sparsity) by +0.024 in Subject Consistency and +0.053 in Image Quality, outperforms SVG (50%) by +0.070 in Subject Consistency and +0.064 in Image Quality, and beats VMoBA (31%) by +0.109 in Subject Consistency and +0.088 in Image Quality. Notably, MoGA also matches or exceeds the full-attention Wan baseline on Subject Consistency (+0.009) and Image Quality (+0.032) despite 71.25% sparsity. For 10-second multi-shot generation, MoGA (14B) outperforms EchoShot (full attention) on Cross-Shot DINO (0.6703 vs. 0.5961) and Cross-Shot CLIP (0.8629 vs. 0.8469), and decisively beats IC-LoRA+Wan on Cross-Shot DINO (0.6703 vs. 0.4669). On 30-second long video, MoGA (14B) improves Subject Consistency by +0.063 over IC-LoRA+Wan (0.9572 vs. 0.8946), demonstrating that end-to-end sparse attention avoids the error accumulation inherent in multi-stage keyframe pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoGA repurposes the Mixture-of-Experts routing paradigm (Fedus et al., 2022) from FFN expert selection to attention token grouping, replacing the coarse-to-fine block estimation used by VMoBA, VSA, and DiTFastAttn with a single learnable linear projection whose weights act as implicit cluster centers. It is positioned as a complement to static spatiotemporal window attention (STGA, Gao et al., 2025), which handles local continuity while MoGA handles global, cross-shot coherence. The full system is built on DiT-based video generation models (Wan2.1, MMDiT) and departs from multi-stage keyframe pipelines (IC-LoRA+Wan, Captain Cinema) by performing end-to-end generation without intermediate keyframe synthesis or context compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoGA provides a simple, kernel-free sparse attention that integrates directly with FlashAttention and sequence parallelism, making it a drop-in replacement for full attention in existing DiT video generation stacks. The demonstrated ability to generate minute-level, multi-shot 480p videos at 24 fps with ~580k context length at 71% sparsity while matching or exceeding full-attention quality opens a practical path to longer, more complex video generation without prohibitive compute. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond one minute, how group count should be tuned for different content types, and whether the learned routing generalizes across architectures and resolutions without retraining.
