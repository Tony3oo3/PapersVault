## Problem

Existing automated prompt optimization methods (e.g., EvoPrompt, APE, TextGrad) maximize point-wise accuracy but produce prompts that are brittle to semantically-equivalent paraphrases, causing large performance swings under minor rephrasing. The paper identifies this brittleness as 'textual sharpness' in the discrete semantic space of prompts and asks two questions: how to formally define a sharpness neighborhood over semantically similar prompt variants, and how to design a practical derivative-free algorithm that co-optimizes task accuracy with local flatness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an initial text prompt, a task dataset with training/validation/test splits, and black-box API access to a target LLM plus oracle LLMs (generator, optimizer, evaluator). It produces a single optimized prompt string that maintains high accuracy under paraphrasing. Notably, the semantic neighborhood is defined not by embedding distance but by LLM-based semantic judgment, and the anisotropic variant (ATARE) decomposes the prompt into three sensitivity tiers (Constraint, Method, Style) to shape a non-uniform perturbation region. The entire pipeline is gradient-free and API-only, requiring no access to model parameters.

## Method

TARE adapts Sharpness-Aware Minimization's min-max structure to discrete text via a two-stage loop: an inner adversarial search samples K_t semantically-preserving paraphrases within a neighborhood B(p_t, ρ_t) and identifies the worst-case neighbor p_t,adv*, then an outer robustness update uses the Landscape-Aware Textual Optimizer (LATO) to propose improvements conditioned on both the current prompt and its worst neighbor, selecting the candidate with the best worst-case performance over a fresh neighborhood sample. ATARE extends this with anisotropic neighborhoods: it decomposes the prompt into Constraint/Method/Style tiers, estimates per-component sensitivity from observed loss spikes, and constructs an ellipsoidal neighborhood via a diagonal weight matrix that penalizes large edits to sensitive components while allowing broader exploration of robust ones. Edit locations are sampled with probability inversely proportional to sensitivity (Pr ∝ (1/w_j)^β), ensuring fragile constraints are perturbed cautiously. Updates are accepted only if the new worst-case neighbor improves on the validation set by a fixed margin η, preventing regressions.

## Results

On the GPT-4o backbone, ATARE achieves the highest accuracy across all four reasoning tasks and five LLM backends, reaching 97.3% on Object Counting (Gemini 1.5 Pro), 98.0% on Temporal Sequences (Gemini 1.5 Pro), 99.0% on Tracking Shuffled Objects (Gemini 1.5 Pro), and 95.5% on GSM8K (Gemini 1.5 Pro). Gains are most pronounced on weaker models: on Tracking Shuffled Objects with Llama 3 8B Instruct, ATARE reaches 57.5% versus 50.0% for CoT and 55.5% for Revolve. The relative improvement over TextGrad ranges from 0.0 to 14.5 percentage points depending on task and model. Results under the Claude 3.5 Sonnet backbone show a consistent pattern, with ATARE leading in most settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TARE and ATARE consistently outperform all three baselines—Zero-shot CoT, TextGrad, and Revolve—across nearly every task-model combination. Against CoT, ATARE gains range from 0.0 to 7.5 percentage points, with the largest margins on weaker models (e.g., +7.5 on Tracking Shuffled Objects with Llama 3 8B). Against Revolve, ATARE leads by 1–7 points, with the most notable gap on Tracking Shuffled Objects (e.g., 88.6% vs. 83.0% on Gemini 1.5 Flash 8B, and 57.5% vs. 55.5% on Llama 3 8B). TextGrad, a first-order textual-gradient method, is the weakest baseline; ATARE's relative improvement over it reaches up to 14.5 points (Tracking Shuffled Objects, Llama 3 8B, Claude 3.5 Sonnet backbone). ATARE itself edges out TARE in most settings (e.g., 90.2 vs. 89.8 on Object Counting/GPT-3.5; 87.5 vs. 84.0 on Temporal Sequences/GPT-3.5), confirming the benefit of anisotropic neighborhood shaping. On the Claude 3.5 Sonnet backbone, the same ordering holds, though absolute gaps are slightly smaller.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the min-max structure of Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM, Foret et al. 2021) from continuous weight space into the discrete, semantic space of text prompts, formalizing a 'textual sharpness' metric over paraphrase neighborhoods. It departs from the dominant prompt-optimization lineage (EvoPrompt, APE, TextGrad, Revolve), which the authors argue optimizes point-wise accuracy and thus produces brittle prompts sensitive to semantically equivalent rephrasings. The evolutionary-algorithm framing connects to PromptBreeder and similar LLM-as-optimizer work, but the key distinction is the explicit worst-case neighborhood objective and the anisotropic (ATARE) sensitivity-weighted neighborhood that has no direct analogue in prior prompt search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formalization of textual sharpness gives the field a principled, black-box criterion for prompt robustness that is directly actionable via API-only access, addressing a real deployment failure mode (paraphrase brittleness) that accuracy-only search ignores. The extension to solution optimization (fragile reasoning chains on GPQA/MMLU) suggests the anisotropic framework generalizes beyond prompts, though the gains there are modest. Open questions include whether textual sharpness has a theoretical link to generalization, how the approach scales to multi-turn and tool-augmented settings, and whether the LLM-as-judge semantic distance is stable enough for principled neighborhood control at scale.
