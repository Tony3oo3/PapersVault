## Problem

Estimating mutual information and other information-theoretic quantities for high-dimensional discrete data remains an open problem. Existing approaches either embed discrete data into continuous space (losing native structure and requiring application-specific embedding models) or rely on variational lower bounds that fail in high-MI regimes, requiring sample sizes that grow exponentially with the true MI. No scalable, accurate estimator exists that operates natively on discrete sequences without such workarounds.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of discrete sequences (e.g., a DNA sequence paired with a promoter label, or a source text paired with a summary), where each position in a sequence draws from a finite alphabet of size |χ|. It outputs a scalar estimate of mutual information, KL divergence, or entropy. A notable property is that a single trained model can estimate MI between arbitrary subsets of variables (e.g., a sliding window of a DNA sequence and a label) without retraining, by simply masking positions outside the window. For entropy estimation, a single discrete sequence suffices, with the uniform distribution serving as the reference.

## Method

INFO-SEDD formulates KL divergence estimation via Continuous Time Markov Chains (CTMCs) over discrete state spaces, applying Dynkin's formula to express KL[p‖q] as a time integral of score-function quantities along a forward perturbation process. The key architectural insight is choosing an absorbing-state transition matrix (Q_absorb^tok), which constrains each subcomponent to transition only into a special absorbing state ∅; this allows a single score network trained on the joint distribution to recover marginal scores by evaluating the score at positions where the other variable is fully absorbed (Equation 6). Two variants are provided: INFO-SEDD-J computes MI as KL[joint ‖ product of marginals], while INFO-SEDD-C computes MI as E[KL[conditional ‖ marginal]], the latter being easier to optimize when one variable is low-dimensional. Training uses the DWDSE loss from Lou et al. (2024), and the sparse rate matrix structure (only one subcomponent changes at a time, unit Hamming distance transitions) keeps computational cost linear in sequence length rather than exponential in the full state space.

## Results

On the high-dimensional synthetic benchmark (Table 1), INFO-SEDD achieves near-ground-truth MI estimates across all settings: 9.92±0.12 (MI=10), 20.02±0.21 (MI=20), 29.83±0.54 (MI=30), 39.11±0.65 (MI=40), and 47.77±1.18 (MI=50), with tight standard deviations. In the text summarization model selection task, INFO-SEDD-C achieves the highest Pearson correlation with the consistency human metric (0.740) and fluency (0.679), outperforming all variational baselines. In the genomics consistency test on HUMAN VS. WORM, INFO-SEDD-C closely tracks the classifier-based reference MI across all scrambling probabilities ρ, while competitors like SMILE and MINE produce near-zero or negative estimates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

INFO-SEDD outperforms all seven baselines (GAN-DIME, HD-DIME, KL-DIME, MINDE, MINE, NWJ, SMILE) on the high-dimensional synthetic benchmark, with the gap widening dramatically as MI and dimensionality increase: at MI=50, D=50, INFO-SEDD estimates 47.77 while the best competitor (MINDE) reaches only 32.60 and variational methods (MINE, NWJ) collapse to ~6–7. GAN-DIME and SMILE are the strongest competitors at low MI (MI=10) but degrade rapidly, with GAN-DIME overestimating (12.15 vs. 10) and SMILE also overestimating (12.83 vs. 10), while INFO-SEDD stays within 0.08 of ground truth. In the text summarization consistency test, INFO-SEDD-C and INFO-SEDD-J both show near-linear MI growth with scrambling probability ρ, matching the theoretical expectation, whereas variational estimators (MINE, NWJ, KL-DIME) are capped by their batch-size limitation and produce near-zero or negative values. In the genomics HUMAN VS. WORM consistency test, INFO-SEDD-C tracks the classifier-based reference MI closely, while HD-DIME performs moderately for ρ<1.0 and other methods (SMILE, MINE) fail to maintain increasing MI estimates. For model selection, INFO-SEDD-C achieves a Pearson correlation of 0.740 with the consistency metric, compared to 0.214 for KL-DIME, 0.331 for HD-DIME, and −0.074 for SMILE, confirming that MI is a meaningful signal for summarization quality that only INFO-SEDD can reliably extract from discrete text data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discrete diffusion framework built on Continuous Time Markov Chains (Lou et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2023; Campbell et al., 2022; Austin et al., 2021) from generative modeling to information-theoretic estimation. The key conceptual departure is the use of Dynkin's formula to derive a KL divergence estimator from the score functions of two CTMCs sharing the same generator but differing in initial conditions, combined with an absorbing-state transition matrix that enables a single score model to recover both joint and marginal distributions. This contrasts with prior diffusion-based MI estimators (MINDE, Kong et al.) that operate on continuous data, and with variational estimators (MINE, SMILE, DIME variants) that suffer from exponential sample complexity in high-MI regimes. The paper also departs from the common 'embedding trick' of projecting discrete data into continuous space for estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

INFO-SEDD addresses a genuine gap: scalable, accurate MI estimation for high-dimensional discrete data without requiring application-specific continuous embeddings. The theoretical consistency guarantee (error bounded by score approximation error plus an exponentially decaying truncation bias) provides a principled foundation absent in variational estimators. Practical impact is demonstrated through genomics motif discovery (TATA-BOX localization) and text summarization model selection, where MI serves as a meaningful, annotation-free signal. Open questions include formal sample complexity guarantees for the score network, extension to mixed continuous/discrete data via the Generator Matching framework, and whether the method's advantages persist when pretrained backbones are replaced by smaller or task-specific architectures.
