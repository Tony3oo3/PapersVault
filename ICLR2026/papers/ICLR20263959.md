## Problem

Prior high-probability convergence guarantees for Clipped SGD under heavy-tailed noise (bounded p-th moment, p ∈ (1,2]) achieve rates O(σ_l ln(1/δ) T^{1/p−1}) for convex and O(σ_l² ln²(1/δ) T^{2/p−2}) for strongly convex problems, which were considered optimal only when treating poly(ln(1/δ)) as constant. This paper shows these rates can be strictly improved by a factor of poly(1/d_eff, 1/ln(1/δ)) through a refined analysis that exploits the anisotropy of the noise via a generalized effective dimension, and that the in-expectation lower bounds can actually be broken.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stochastic gradient oracle g(x,ξ) whose noise d(x,ξ) = g(x,ξ) − ∇f(x) satisfies a fine-grained p-th moment condition parameterized by two constants σ_s ≤ σ_l (scalar and vector moments respectively), a G-Lipschitz convex function f, and a μ-strongly convex regularizer r on a closed convex set X. It produces an approximate minimizer (average or weighted-average iterate) with explicit high-probability or in-expectation suboptimality bounds that depend on the generalized effective dimension d_eff = σ_l²/σ_s² ∈ [1, πd/2], the failure probability δ, and the time horizon T. A Stabilized Clipped SGD variant additionally provides anytime guarantees without requiring T in advance.

## Method

The refined analysis rests on two key technical insights. First, a better application of Freedman's inequality: instead of bounding the conditional variance via E[||d_t^u||²] ≤ O(σ_l^p τ^{2−p}), the paper bounds the operator norm ||E[d_t^u (d_t^u)^T]|| ≤ O(σ_s^p τ^{2−p} + σ_l^p G² τ^{−p}), which is strictly tighter when the noise is anisotropic (σ_s < σ_l). Second, finer clipping-error bounds: the biased part satisfies ||d_t^b|| ≤ O(σ_s σ_l^{p−1} τ^{1−p} + σ_l^p G τ^{−p}) rather than the previously used O(σ_l^p τ^{1−p}), and the unbiased second moment bound no longer requires τ ≥ 2G. These tighter bounds, combined with the fine-grained Assumption 4 introducing σ_s alongside σ_l, yield the improved rates O(σ_l d_eff^{−1/(2p)} ln^{1−1/p}(1/δ) T^{1/p−1}) and O(σ_l² d_eff^{−1/p} ln^{2−2/p}(1/δ) T^{2/p−2}) in high probability, and in-expectation rates that break the known lower bounds by a factor of poly(1/d_eff). The paper also establishes matching in-expectation lower bounds via information-theoretic arguments on a fine-grained oracle class parameterized by both σ_s and σ_l.

## Results

For general convex problems under heavy-tailed noise (p∈(1,2]), Clipped SGD achieves a high-probability rate of O(σ_l d_eff^{-1/(2p)} ln^{1-1/p}(1/δ) T^{1/p-1}), improving upon the previous best O(σ_l ln(1/δ) T^{1/p-1}) by a factor of Θ(1/(d_eff^{(2-p)/(2p)} ln(1/δ))). For strongly convex problems, the rate is O(σ_l² d_eff^{-1/p} ln^{2-2/p}(1/δ) T^{2/p-2}). In expectation, the new rates O(σ_l d_eff^{-(2-p)/(2p)} T^{1/p-1}) (convex) and O(σ_l² d_eff^{-(2-p)/p} T^{2/p-2}) (strongly convex) strictly break the known optimal lower bounds Ω(σ_l T^{1/p-1}) and Ω(σ_l² T^{2/p-2}) whenever p<2, and the new in-expectation lower bounds match these upper bounds, confirming optimality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper improves upon three prior theoretical baselines. Against Liu & Zhou (2023), which established O(σ_l ln(1/δ) T^{1/p-1}) for convex and O(σ_l² ln²(1/δ) T^{2/p-2}) for strongly convex problems, the new rates gain a factor of Θ(1/(d_eff^{(2-p)/(2p)} ln(1/δ))) in high probability and Θ(1/d_eff^{(2-p)/(2p)}) in expectation. Against Das et al. (2024), which gave O(σ_l d_eff^{-1/4} √(ln(ln T/δ)/T)) for the special case p=2, the new result works for all p∈(1,2], removes the ln(ln T/δ) dependence (replacing it with ln(1/δ)), eliminates the requirement T≥Ω(ln(ln d)), and does not blow up as σ_l→0. Against the classical in-expectation lower bounds Ω(σ_l T^{1/p-1}) (Nemirovski & Yudin, 1983; Vural et al., 2022) and Ω(σ_l² T^{2/p-2}) (Zhang et al., 2020), the new in-expectation upper bounds strictly surpass them by a poly(1/d_eff) factor when p<2, and the newly established lower bounds for the fine-grained oracle class G_{σ_s,σ_l}^p match the upper bounds exactly, proving optimality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper refines the analysis of Clipped SGD for nonsmooth convex optimization under heavy-tailed noise, building directly on Liu & Zhou (2023) who established the best-known high-probability rates O(σ_l ln(1/δ) T^{1/p-1}) and O(σ_l² ln²(1/δ) T^{2/p-2}) for general p ∈ (1,2]. It extends the effective dimension concept from Das et al. (2024), which was only available for p=2, to a 'generalized effective dimension' d_eff = σ_l²/σ_s² valid for all p ∈ (1,2], by introducing a finer noise assumption that separately bounds the p-th moment of directional and full-norm gradient errors. The two technical departures from prior analysis are: (1) a simpler and tighter application of Freedman's inequality using the operator norm of the conditional covariance matrix rather than its trace, avoiding the complex iterative refinement strategy of Das et al. (2024), and (2) finer clipping error bounds that exploit the σ_s parameter to reduce the bias term from O(σ_l^p τ^{1-p}) to O(σ_s σ_l^{p-1} τ^{1-p} + σ_l^p G τ^{-p}).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The in-expectation upper bounds now match newly established lower bounds, proving optimality of the refined analysis and revealing that the previously known lower bounds Ω(σ_l T^{1/p-1}) were loose under the finer noise model. The generalized effective dimension provides a unified lens for understanding when heavy-tailed noise is 'effectively low-dimensional' and thus less harmful, a concept that could inform practical algorithm design. However, gaps remain in the high-probability bounds (the poly(ln(1/δ)) terms are not yet tight), and it is unclear whether the extra O(φGD/T) term in the convex case is fundamental or an artifact of the analysis. The authors note that both technical insights (better Freedman's inequality usage and finer clipping error bounds) are general and potentially applicable to smooth, nonconvex, and distributed settings under heavy-tailed noise.
