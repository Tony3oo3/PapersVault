## Problem

Existing image forensics models, primarily trained on natural images, underperform on biomedical data where subtle duplications (e.g., copied gel bands, reused microscopy patches) can compromise experimental validity yet remain visually difficult to detect. The BioFors benchmark, while providing real-world forged test images from retracted publications, lacks manipulated training samples, and no prior method simultaneously localizes both source and target duplicated regions across the four heterogeneous biomedical modalities (microscopy, blot/gel, FACS, macroscopy).

## Input / Output

BioTamperNet accepts a pair of RGB biomedical images (resized to 224×224) for external duplication detection, or a single image for internal duplication and cut/sharp transition detection (which is internally split into a pseudo-pair to unify the architecture). It outputs a pair of binary probability masks—one for the source region and one for the target region—upsampled to the original image resolution via bilinear interpolation. Feature hierarchies span from 4096×4096 down to a compact 40×40 token grid (N=1600) where affinity computation is performed.

## Method

BioTamperNet is a Siamese architecture combining a ViT-Base feature extractor (pretrained on ImageNet-1k, fine-tuned on BioFors classes) with affinity-guided self- and cross-attention modules built on Mamba's selective-scan SSM operator. An affinity matrix is constructed via a State Space Similarity mechanism (normalizing SSM hidden states), stabilized with ELU activation, RoPE positional encoding, a spatial suppression kernel to reduce diagonal dominance, and a bidirectional row-column softmax. Three parallel Affinity-Guided SSM (AGSSM) blocks perform self-attention modulated by the flattened affinity map, followed by cross-attention between the two views to align duplicated regions. A lightweight convolutional decoder maps the enriched features to binary masks, and the model is trained end-to-end with weighted BCE losses on synthetic tampering datasets (augmented with GAN-generated patches, geometric transforms, and noise) since BioFors provides no forged training samples. The full model uses 36.7M parameters and 29.6 GFLOPs at 512×512, making it lighter than TruFor and SparseViT while achieving substantially higher MCC across all biomedical categories.

## Results

On the BioFors test set (real-world forgeries from retracted publications), BioTamperNet achieves a combined EDD pixel-level MCC of 0.526 and IDD pixel-level MCC of 0.534, substantially outperforming the strongest baselines (MONet at 0.410 for EDD and DF-PCT at 0.364 for IDD). Per-category, the largest gains appear in Macroscopy EDD (0.577 vs. 0.371 for SparseViT) and Blot/Gel IDD (0.617 vs. 0.466 for DF-PCT). On the CSTD task, BioTamperNet reaches 0.346 pixel MCC versus 0.092 for TruFor, and on the RSIID and Western Blots synthetic benchmarks it attains 0.965 and 0.913 pixel MCC respectively, surpassing SparseViT (0.842, 0.739).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BioTamperNet outperforms all five baseline families across every evaluated category and task. Against traditional keypoint descriptors (BRIEF, SIFT, ORB), the gains are dramatic: combined EDD pixel MCC jumps from 0.202 (BRIEF) to 0.526, a 2.6× improvement, reflecting the inability of hand-crafted features to handle repetitive biomedical textures. Dense feature matching (DF-ZM/DF-PCT/DF-FM) is the strongest conventional baseline, reaching 0.364 pixel MCC on IDD; BioTamperNet improves this to 0.534 (+47% relative), with the largest per-category margin in Macroscopy IDD (0.679 vs. 0.487). Among deep-learning baselines, ManTra-Net (0.231 EDD pixel) and TruFor (0.282 EDD pixel) are designed for splicing artifacts and struggle with duplication-centric forgeries; BioTamperNet exceeds them by 0.295 and 0.244 pixel MCC respectively on EDD. MONet, the only prior method targeting biomedical duplication, achieves 0.410 EDD pixel MCC but does not support IDD or CSTD; BioTamperNet surpasses it by 0.116 on EDD while additionally delivering 0.534 on IDD and 0.346 on CSTD, tasks MONet cannot address.

## Relation to Prior Work

BioTamperNet adapts State Space Models (Mamba, VMamba) from sequence modeling to the biomedical image forensics domain, using selective-scan SSMs as a lightweight replacement for standard multi-head attention in a Siamese duplication-detection pipeline. It builds on the BioFors benchmark (Sabir et al., 2021) and its three tasks (EDD, IDD, CSTD), but departs from prior forensic methods (ManTraNet, TruFor, MONet, SparseViT) that rely on low-level noiseprint, frequency, or boundary artifacts by explicitly modeling source-target duplication structure through affinity-guided self- and cross-attention. The unified Siamese architecture that handles both paired-image (EDD) and single-image (IDD/CSTD) inputs via a pseudo-pair splitting strategy is a novel framing not present in earlier biomedical forensics work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Biomedical image forgery detection is critical for scientific integrity yet has suffered from data scarcity and a lack of methods tailored to duplication-specific manipulations; BioTamperNet's synthetic + GAN-augmented training pipeline and SSM-based architecture address both gaps, and it is the first to establish baselines for IDD and CSTD on BioFors. However, the reliance on synthetically generated training pairs (since BioFors train split contains no forgeries) raises open questions about how well the model generalizes to manipulation types not covered by the augmentation pipeline. The authors identify failure modes with perfectly overlapping source-target regions, highly repetitive textures, and dense-stain blot/gel images, and propose future extensions to video-level forgery detection with temporal attention.
