## Problem

Despite rapid progress in LLMs, their ability to reason over real-world PCB placement and routing tasks—requiring spatial reasoning, domain-specific constraint adherence, and interpretation of professional engineering artifacts—remains unexplored due to the absence of standardized multimodal benchmarks. Existing PCB datasets target vision-only defect detection, and prior LLM-for-EDA benchmarks focus on text-to-HDL or Verilog generation, leaving a clear gap in cross-modal (text ↔ image ↔ design files) evaluation under PCB design rules.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260092/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes three input formats: (1) free-form or multiple-choice text questions on PCB placement/routing concepts, (2) PCB layout images paired with natural-language prompts for multimodal QA, and (3) standalone EDA editor screenshots (silkscreen, copper layers, via/pad views) with no accompanying text. Outputs are scored via top-1 accuracy for choice questions, BERTScore-F1 and SBERT cosine similarity for open-ended answers, and token-level precision/recall/F1 for the screenshot-description task, enabling both discriminative and generative evaluation in a single framework.

## Method

PCB-Bench is constructed through a multi-source knowledge pipeline: domain experts (a senior professor and a PhD researcher) design a structured syllabus spanning 25+ subtopics across macro- and micro-level placement and routing, then author ~1,800 QA pairs with corresponding choice questions, ~500 image-text problems, and curate 174 complete PCB designs from OSHWHub/JLCPCB with schematics, placement files, and editor screenshots. Evaluation is conducted in a unified zero-shot setting across 11+ frontier and open-source (M)LLMs (GPT-4o/5, Claude-Opus-4.1, Gemini-2.5, DeepSeek-V3.1, LLaMA-4, InternVL3, Qwen-VL, etc.), with two domain-specific variants (4-bit QLoRA fine-tuning and FAISS-based RAG over ~4.5M tokens of PCB documents) built on Qwen2.5-7B to test whether lightweight specialization closes the gap. The key design choice is the dual-format (QA + CQ) structure per question, which decouples generative reasoning quality (measured by semantic similarity) from factual accuracy (measured by exact-match), revealing that these two dimensions diverge substantially in fine-grained PCB reasoning.

## Results

On Task 1 (text-based QA/CQ, ~3,700 questions), Claude-Opus-4.1 achieves the highest CQ accuracy (93.30% placement-macro, 94.35% placement-micro, 99.16% routing-macro, 92.32% routing-micro), while DeepSeek-Chat-V3.1-671B leads in QA BERTScore (0.8306–0.8310) and SBERT (0.4912–0.5657). On Task 2 (multimodal image-text, ~500 questions), GPT-5 delivers the most balanced performance (83.26% CBC accuracy, 90.90% BWEI accuracy, 0.8734 BERTScore on placement QA), and Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves perfect BWEI (100%). On Task 3 (PCB design understanding, 174 designs), performance differences are modest: Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-thinking attains the highest BERTScore (0.8293) and InternVL-3-78B the highest F1 (0.8849), with most models clustering between 0.81–0.83 BERTScore and 0.85–0.88 F1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Frontier models (GPT-4o, GPT-5, Claude-Opus-4.1, Gemini-2.5-Pro, DeepSeek-V3.1) form a clear top tier on Task 1, with CQ accuracy above 86% across all subtasks, while open-source models like LLaMA-4-Maverick-400B drop sharply on placement-micro CQ (49.07%) and small models (Ministral-3B at 42.71%, MythoMax-13B at 54.37%) are far behind. On Task 2, GPT-5 leads in overall balance (83.26% CBC, 90.90% BWEI, 0.8734 BERTScore), Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves perfect BWEI (100%) but trails in semantic alignment, and open-source models (InternVL3-78B, Qwen3-VL-8B) show the weakest BWEI (45.45%). Domain-specific variants (QLoRA-fine-tuned and RAG-enhanced Qwen2.5-7B) improve SBERT substantially over the base Qwen (e.g., 0.4687 vs. 0.2236 on placement-macro) and close the CQ gap, yet still fall short of frontier models on BERTScore. A key finding is that CQ accuracy and QA semantic alignment (BERTScore/SBERT) do not always correlate: for instance, LLaMA-4 achieves 92.43% routing-macro CQ but only 0.5149 SBERT, while Rocinante-12B scores 89.92% CQ with 0.4953 SBERT, showing that selection-based and generation-based evaluation capture different competencies. On Task 3, all models cluster tightly (BERTScore 0.8156–0.8293, F1 0.8089–0.8849), indicating that high-level PCB description is comparatively easier, with Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-thinking and InternVL-3-78B marginally ahead in semantic alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first benchmark specifically for evaluating LLMs/MLLMs on PCB placement and routing, a domain previously unaddressed by any LLM evaluation framework. It departs from prior PCB datasets (PCB-DSLR, FICS-PCB, PCB-Vision, etc.), which are vision-only and target defect detection or component recognition rather than design reasoning, and from LLM-for-EDA work (ChipGPT, VerilogEval) that focuses on text-to-HDL code generation rather than spatial layout reasoning. The benchmark repurposes the general LLM evaluation paradigm (QA, multiple-choice, image-text reasoning) but grounds it in PCB-specific constraints, domain conventions, and authentic EDA artifacts (schematics, placement files, editor screenshots), creating a new intersection between foundation model evaluation and electronic design automation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PCB-Bench fills a critical gap by providing a standardized, multimodal evaluation platform for LLMs in a high-stakes engineering domain where prior work was limited to fewer than 20 boards and no open-source benchmarks existed. The findings that CQ accuracy and QA semantic alignment diverge substantially, that even frontier models (GPT-5, Claude-Opus-4.1) struggle with fine-grained micro-level routing, and that domain-specific fine-tuning (QLoRA) can improve SBERT while degrading BERTScore, highlight that general-purpose semantic metrics may be inadequate for PCB-specific correctness assessment. Open questions include whether the benchmark's scale (~3,700 text questions, ~500 multimodal, 174 designs) is sufficient for robust conclusions, whether BERTScore/SBERT truly capture engineering correctness, and whether LLMs can ever achieve the reliability needed to replace human engineers in production PCB design workflows.
