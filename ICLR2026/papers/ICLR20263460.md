## Problem

Aerial vision-language navigation (VLN) for UAVs is severely constrained by small-scale datasets (~10k trajectories), limited rendering environments (primarily AirSim and Unreal Engine), and labor-intensive manual annotation of both flight trajectories and language instructions. These bottlenecks prevent the field from reaching the data scale and environmental diversity needed to train robust navigation models comparable to what embodied manipulation has achieved with million-episode datasets.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of UAV-egocentric RGB images (not a single frame) alongside a natural-language instruction, and outputs one of six discrete flight actions: Forward, Turn Left, Turn Right, Move Up, Move Down, or Stop. The current frame is processed at full resolution (256 visual tokens), while historical keyframes are aggressively compressed to a single token each via visual token merging and grid pooling, with a FIFO memory bank of capacity K=2 retaining the most recent keyframe sets. The landmark grounding module predicts bounding boxes in each frame to determine which historical frames are retained as keyframes.

## Method

OpenFly-Agent builds on the OpenVLA vision-language-action baseline, replacing its single-image input with a keyframe-aware multi-frame pipeline. A keyframe selection mechanism identifies candidate frames at UAV trajectory action transitions (e.g., turns, ascents) and retains those where a landmark grounding module—three cross-attention layers predicting instruction-referenced landmark bounding boxes—reports a bounding box area exceeding a threshold θ. A visual token merging strategy then selects the frame with the largest landmark bounding box as a reference, computes pairwise cosine similarities between its visual tokens and those of other keyframes, and merges high-similarity tokens by averaging while discarding the rest, iteratively across the keyframe set. Within each retained keyframe, grid pooling further compresses tokens, while the current frame's 256 tokens remain uncompressed to preserve the latest observation. The entire model is trained end-to-end with a GIoU loss on the grounding module and standard cross-entropy on the action head, using a batch size of 64 and learning rate of 2e-5.

## Results

On the OpenFly test-seen split, OpenFly-Agent achieves a 34.3% success rate (SR) and 24.9% SPL, outperforming the strongest baseline NaVila (20.3% SR, 17.8% SPL) by 14.0% in SR. On the test-unseen split, OpenFly-Agent reaches 22.6% SR and 19.1% SPL, surpassing NaVila (14.7% SR, 9.6% SPL) by 7.9% in SR. The navigation error (NE) is reduced to 93 m (seen) and 154 m (unseen), compared to 132 m and 202 m for NaVila, respectively. In real-world experiments across 23 outdoor scenes, OpenFly-Agent achieves 26.09% SR and 34.78% OSR, again outperforming Navid and NaVila.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OpenFly-Agent outperforms all five baselines on every metric in both test-seen and test-unseen splits. Against the strongest baseline NaVila (Cheng et al., 2024), it gains +14.0% SR on seen (34.3% vs 20.3%) and +7.9% SR on unseen (22.6% vs 14.7%), while also reducing NE by 39 m (seen) and 48 m (unseen). Navid (Zhang et al., 2024) trails further behind at 13.0% SR (seen) and 10.8% SR (unseen), with SPL roughly half that of OpenFly-Agent. AerialVLN (Liu et al., 2023), the original aerial VLN method, achieves only 7.5% SR (seen) and 7.3% SR (unseen), showing a ~4.6× gap in SR versus OpenFly-Agent on seen. The classical Seq2Seq and CMA baselines (Krantz et al., 2020) remain far below, at 2.9% and 5.4% SR (seen), confirming that VLM-based approaches are substantially more effective for aerial VLN. The zero-shot See-Point-Fly method (Hu et al., 2025), evaluated only on unseen with GPT-4.1, reaches 8.2% SR, still well below OpenFly-Agent's 22.6%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the aerial VLN line initiated by AerialVLN, OpenUAV, and CityNav, which relied on AirSim/UE simulators and manual pilot-driven trajectory collection with human-annotated instructions. OpenFly departs from this lineage in two key ways: (1) it replaces the single-engine, manual pipeline with a multi-engine (UE, GTA V, Google Earth, 3D GS) automated toolchain using A* pathfinding and GPT-4o for instruction generation, and (2) it builds its agent on OpenVLA rather than the encoder-decoder or LLM-prompting architectures used by prior aerial VLN methods, adding keyframe selection and visual token merging to handle multi-frame observation history. The 3D Gaussian Splatting integration for real-to-sim reconstruction is a notable addition not present in any prior aerial VLN platform.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100K-trajectory dataset (10× larger than prior aerial VLN benchmarks) and the fully automated generation pipeline remove the primary bottleneck—manual annotation—that has kept aerial VLN data at ~10K samples, making it feasible to scale the field toward the data volumes that drove progress in embodied manipulation. The real-world experiments on 23 outdoor scenes with a physical Q250 drone validate that the sim-to-real gap is bridgeable, which is critical for practical UAV deployment. However, even the best method achieves only ~34% SR on seen and ~23% on unseen splits, indicating the task remains far from solved and that more powerful models or richer supervision signals are needed. Open questions include whether the discrete 4-DoF action space is sufficient for complex urban environments, and whether the GPT-4o-generated instructions introduce systematic biases that limit generalization.
