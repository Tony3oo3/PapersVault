## Problem

Existing part-based 3D generation methods force all parts to share a single global representation space: implicit vecset approaches lack sufficient geometric detail for fine parts, while explicit voxel methods allocate only a tiny fraction of a shared grid to small parts, severely degrading their quality. No prior work has addressed the fundamental resolution bottleneck that prevents small but complex 3D components from being generated at adequate fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261568/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single-view RGB image or a text prompt as conditioning input and produces a structured 3D object decomposed into K semantically meaningful parts, each rendered as a textured mesh. Internally, the pipeline first outputs a bounding box layout (K cuboid meshes), then generates each part within its own isolated 64³ full-resolution voxel grid, and finally refines these into textured meshes. A maximum of 30 parts per object is supported during training, with sequential sampling extending this at inference.

## Method

FullPart is a three-stage diffusion framework that combines implicit and explicit paradigms: (1) layout generation encodes bounding boxes as cuboid meshes and generates them via vecset diffusion (initialized from TripoSG), (2) coarse structure generation assigns each part its own dedicated 64³ voxel grid (initialized from TRELLIS Stage 1), and (3) refinement decodes occupied voxels into textured meshes (TRELLIS Stage 2). The key technical contribution is a center-corner encoding mechanism: for every voxel token, the model computes the 8 corner coordinates and center in a 2048³ super-high-resolution global coordinate system and injects their positional embeddings, enabling the network to perceive each part's true spatial extent despite per-part normalization. A hybrid DiT architecture alternates intra-part and inter-part attention blocks, with a retained global branch (part ID=0) preserving the pre-trained model's holistic priors. All stages are trained with Conditional Flow Matching, fine-tuned from pre-trained TRELLIS/TripoSG weights, and the accompanying PartVerse-XL dataset (40K objects, 320K human-annotated parts) provides the training signal.

## Results

On the PartVerse-XL test set (100 objects), FullPart achieves an F-Score of 0.81, Chamfer Distance of 0.11, Part-CD of 0.36, and ULIP-Score of 0.24, outperforming all baselines across every metric. On the PartNet/PartNeXt test set, FullPart similarly leads with F-Score 0.77, CD 0.12, Part-CD 0.42, and ULIP-Score 0.25. In a user study with 73 participants, FullPart receives 48.9% preference for global coherence and 60.3% for part structural plausibility, versus the next-best OmniPart at 38.8% and 21.0% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FullPart outperforms all five quantitative baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor OmniPart (shared global voxel grid), FullPart improves F-Score from 0.77 to 0.81, reduces CD from 0.15 to 0.11, lowers Part-CD from 0.42 to 0.36, and raises ULIP-Score from 0.22 to 0.24. The gap is most dramatic versus PartCrafter (implicit vecset tokens), where CD drops from 0.42 to 0.11 and ULIP-Score nearly doubles from 0.13 to 0.24, reflecting PartCrafter's tendency toward fragmented and floating parts. Against TRELLIS (the monolithic foundation model), FullPart gains +0.10 F-Score and −0.05 CD, confirming that per-part full-resolution grids preserve fine details lost in a shared grid. CoPart is compared only in memory/latency benchmarks and the user study, where FullPart dominates (48.9% vs 5.0% global coherence preference). Direct3D-S2 is compared qualitatively (Figure 5) and shows significant detail loss in fine-grained regions due to its monolithic generation without part decomposition.

## Relation to Prior Work

FullPart synthesizes two established 3D generation paradigms—implicit vecset diffusion (from 3DShape2VecSet/TripoSG) for layout and explicit sparse-voxel diffusion (from TRELLIS) for geometry—into a unified three-stage pipeline. It departs from prior part generators (PartCrafter, OmniPart, CoPart, HoloPart) by rejecting the shared global representation space: instead of forcing all parts into one latent set or one N×N×N voxel grid, each part receives its own isolated full-resolution 64³ grid, with a novel center-corner positional encoding to preserve cross-part spatial coherence. The layout stage repurposes a pre-trained vecset diffusion model to generate bounding-box meshes rather than full shapes, converting a regression problem into a diffusion generation task that inherits the foundation model's geometric priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The per-part full-resolution grid directly addresses a long-standing resolution bottleneck in part generation where small components (e.g., chair legs, robot fingers) receive only a handful of voxels in shared grids, and the ablation confirms this is the single largest quality lever. PartVerse-XL (40K objects, 320K human-annotated parts with captions) fills a critical data gap that has constrained the field, and the center-corner encoding offers a reusable technique for any multi-object generation setting with heterogeneous spatial scales. Open questions include the linear compute scaling with part count (370 s for 30 parts), the sensitivity to ambiguous bounding boxes, and whether the approach extends to scene-level or avatar-level decomposition where part counts and inter-part constraints are far larger.
