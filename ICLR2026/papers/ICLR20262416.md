## Problem

Existing Unified Vision-Language Models (UVLMs) unify understanding and generation at the architectural level but treat the two capabilities as parallel, independently callable skills during task solving. This leaves a critical gap: the understanding and generation modules lack close, explicit interaction at inference time, so genuine synergy—where comprehension informs generation and vice versa—remains unrealized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262416/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text query and an optional set of input images, which are encoded by a vision encoder (SigLIP2) and fused with the textual stream. The output is an interleaved thinking trace wrapped in think tags, alternating between textual thoughts and compact latent visual thoughts (at most K=16 tokens each, derived via density-peaks clustering of the generation-side VAE latent grid), followed by a final answer that is either a text response or a generated image. The latent visual thoughts are schematic, sub-image-budget representations rather than full pixel renderings, keeping the reasoning loop lightweight.

## Method

AD-Loop introduces an interleaved analyzing-drafting problem-solving loop in which the model dynamically alternates between textual reasoning (analyzing) and compact latent visual drafting (sketching spatial layouts, cropping regions) before producing the final output. Latent visual thoughts are constructed by encoding an explicit visual thought image through the generation-side encoder and reducing the resulting latent grid to K semantically coherent cluster centers via density-peaks clustering, yielding a deterministic, budget-constrained visual representation. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 performs supervised fine-tuning on ~42K curated interleaved thinking traces (20K understanding, 22K generation) with a combined loss over text thoughts, latent visual thoughts (MSE), and the task output; Stage 2 applies GRPO-based reinforcement learning where the policy samples trajectories both with and without AD-Loop, and a margin-based bonus reward (with threshold δ) is added only when the AD-Loop trajectory meaningfully outperforms the best text-only trajectory, teaching the model to invoke visual drafting adaptively. The framework is architecture-agnostic, demonstrated on both continuous-embedding (BAGEL) and discrete-token (Janus-Pro) UVLM backbones.

## Results

On multimodal understanding benchmarks, AD-Loop (7B) achieves the best scores across all seven evaluated metrics: 90.1 on POPE, 1696.0 on MME-P, 87.6 on MMBench, 74.4 on SEED-Bench, 63.8 on GQA, 57.3 on MMMU, and 69.7 on MM-Vet, yielding an average +2.3% improvement over the strongest unified baseline. On text-to-image generation, AD-Loop attains an overall GenEval score of 0.86 (86%), surpassing all generation-only and unified baselines, with particularly strong gains in positional accuracy (0.80) and attribute binding (0.74). Ablations confirm that adaptive interleaved thinking (T/T+I) outperforms isolated thinking, text-only thinking, and fixed interleaving across both understanding and generation tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AD-Loop outperforms the strongest unified baseline BAGEL (7B) on every comparable understanding metric (e.g., MMB 87.6 vs. 85.0, MMMU 57.3 vs. 55.3, MM-Vet 69.7 vs. 67.2) and on GenEval overall (0.86 vs. 0.82). Against Janus-Pro (7B), which uses discrete VQ tokens, AD-Loop gains 2.7 points on POPE, 128.9 on MME-P, 8.4 on MMB, and 19.3 on MMMU, while also improving GenEval from 0.80 to 0.86. Compared to MindOmni (0.83 GenEval), which employs text-only thinking, AD-Loop's latent visual thoughts yield a 3-point overall advantage, confirming the value of visual reasoning. On understanding-only baselines, AD-Loop surpasses LLaVA-v1.5 by 4.2 points on POPE and 34.3 on MMMU, and outperforms the 13B InstructBLIP by 11.2 on POPE and 14.3 on GQA despite having fewer parameters. The method also beats MMaDA (which uses RL-based thinking) by 4.0 on POPE and 27.1 on MMMU, demonstrating that interleaved visual-textual thinking provides gains beyond text-only RL reasoning. No baseline outperforms AD-Loop on any reported metric in the main comparison tables.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of UVLM research (Emu3, Show-o, Janus-Pro, BAGEL) that achieves unification architecturally—via shared autoregressive or hybrid AR-diffusion backbones—while leaving understanding and generation as parallel, independently callable skills at inference time. It builds on BAGEL as its backbone, adopts the GRPO framework from DeepSeek-R1 for Stage-2 RL, and repurposes density-peaks clustering from Chat-Univi to compress explicit visual drafts into compact latent visual thoughts. The key conceptual shift is from architectural co-location to a cognitive process-level loop: the AD-Loop interleaves textual (analyzing) and visual (drafting) thoughts adaptively during problem-solving, drawing on the broader MLLM reasoning literature (Visual-CoT, CoF, CMoT) and visual-thinking work (Anole, MVoT, Mirage) but making the interaction bidirectional and task-adaptive rather than a fixed alternation schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that generation can genuinely enhance understanding (and vice versa) within a single model, rather than merely coexisting, by showing consistent gains on both task families from a shared thinking loop. The architecture-agnostic design (validated on both discrete-token Janus-Pro and continuous-embedding BAGEL) makes the AD-Loop a potentially drop-in module for future UVLMs. Limitations remain: the compact latent representation under-encodes fine-grained details (scene text, fingers), the model occasionally over-drafts on trivial queries, and strict symbolic reasoning still degrades. Open questions include whether the K=16 token budget for visual thoughts is optimal, how the approach scales to video or 3D generation, and whether the adaptive policy generalizes to tasks not seen during RL training.
