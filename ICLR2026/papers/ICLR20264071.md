## Problem

Existing graph self-supervised learning methods are biased toward either homophilic or heterophilic structural patterns and cannot adaptively handle graphs where both coexist. Contrastive SSL lacks a stable global reference for mixed graphs, while generative SSL produces contradictory reconstruction signals when neighbors have dissimilar attributes. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a unified framework that harmonizes both patterns without requiring prior knowledge of the graph's homophily level.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph with d-dimensional node feature vectors and an edge set, where during training a subset of node features are replaced with learnable parameters (masked nodes). The student network processes this partially masked graph while the teacher network sees the full graph; the prediction loss is computed over all nodes in the latent space, not just the masked subset. The frozen node embeddings produced by the student are then used for downstream linear probing (node classification) or k-means clustering, following the standard SSL evaluation protocol.

## Method

HarmonyGNNs combines two complementary mechanisms. For objective harmonization, it employs a JEPA-inspired teacher-student predictive architecture where the student (seeing a masked graph) predicts the teacher's (EMA-updated, full-graph) embeddings for all nodes, with two node-difficulty-driven dynamic masking strategies (greedy difficulty ranking and Bernoulli sampling with difficulty-informed success rates) that adaptively select which nodes to mask each iteration. For representation harmonization, it constructs a joint node encoding by stacking four feature tokens—linear projection, MLP projection, and two layers of a proposed Weighted GCN (WGCN) with learnable per-edge weights that adaptively balance smoothing and sharpening—then fuses them via a feature-level multi-head self-attention Transformer block and a hierarchical token-merging strategy. The theoretical analysis shows this teacher-student design achieves faster convergence and tighter error bounds than encoder-decoder alternatives due to a smaller contraction factor and absence of encoder-decoder coupling effects.

## Results

On heterophilic graphs, HarmonyGNNs+Prob achieves 85.68% on Cornell, 92.45% on Texas, 93.13% on Wisconsin, and 38.15% on Actor (linear probing accuracy), outperforming the strongest prior SSL baselines by 7.1% on Texas (vs. S3GCL's 86.12%), 9.6% on Roman-Empire (vs. MUSE's 66.26%), and 1.27% on Actor. On homophilic graphs, it matches or slightly exceeds SOTA: 84.82% on Cora, 73.36% on CiteSeer, 83.42% on PubMed, and 71.97% on ArXiv. In k-means clustering, HarmonyGNNs improves over MUSE by 11.26% on Texas and 12.51% on Cornell. Training time is roughly 2× faster than GREET and MUSE on large-scale datasets (e.g., 476s vs. 627s/739s on ArXiv).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HarmonyGNNs outperforms all 29 compared baselines on heterophilic graphs. Against the strongest heterophilic SSL methods, it beats S3GCL by 7.1% on Texas (93.24% vs. 86.12%) and 1.27% on Actor (38.15% vs. 36.88%), and surpasses MUSE by 9.6% on Roman-Empire (75.86% vs. 66.26%) and 3.68% on Squirrel (45.49% vs. 41.57%). On homophilic graphs, HarmonyGNNs matches or slightly exceeds the best baselines: it leads on Cora (84.82% vs. GraphACL/GraphMAE's 84.20%) and ArXiv (71.97% vs. BGRL's 71.24%), while CiteSeer (73.36% vs. GraphACL's 73.63%) and PubMed (83.42% vs. NWR-GAE's 83.44%) are within standard deviation. Traditional SSL methods (DGI, GRACE, BGRL, GraphMAE) are outperformed by 10–30+ points on heterophilic datasets, confirming their homophily bias. Among semi-supervised baselines, HarmonyGNNs surpasses the strongest heterophily-aware models (WRGAT, H2GCN) by 8.4% on Texas and 6.2% on Wisconsin, while matching them on homophilic sets. In k-means clustering, it leads MUSE by 11.26% on Texas and 12.51% on Cornell, and is the only method to exceed 70% on both simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the JEPA teacher-student predictive paradigm from computer vision (Assran et al., 2023) into graph SSL, replacing the contrastive or encoder-decoder objectives that dominate prior graph SSL work (GraphMAE, DGI, GRACE, MUSE, GREET). Architecturally, it extends the standard GCN (Kipf & Welling, 2016a) into a Weighted GCN with learnable edge weights, and combines this with linear/MLP feature projections and a per-node Transformer block to jointly encode node specificity and structural awareness. The key departure from prior heterophily-focused SSL methods (HGRL, DSSL, GREET, MUSE) is the explicit framing of the problem as one of *harmonizing* coexisting homophilic and heterophilic regions within a single graph, rather than treating heterophily as a separate regime requiring dedicated architectures or alternating training strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: no prior graph SSL method simultaneously handles both homophilic and heterophilic patterns without requiring prior knowledge of the graph's structural regime, and the unified framework here achieves SOTA on heterophilic benchmarks while matching SOTA on homophilic ones. The theoretical convergence analysis (showing the teacher-student objective contracts faster than encoder-decoder reconstruction under strong convexity) provides a principled justification for the architectural choice. However, the most dramatic gains (+7–10%) are on very small graphs (Texas ~183 nodes, Wisconsin ~251 nodes), and the improvement on the larger Actor dataset is modest (~1–2%), leaving open whether the advantage persists at scale. The reliance on strong convexity and smoothness assumptions in the theory, and the absence of evaluation on truly large-scale heterophilic graphs beyond Roman-Empire (~2K nodes) and OGBN-Arxiv, are notable limitations.
