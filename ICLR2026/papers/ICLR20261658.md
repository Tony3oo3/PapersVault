## Problem

Supervised fine-tuning of LLMs on even benign datasets can degrade trustworthiness—introducing untruthfulness, stereotypical bias, and ethical violations—yet existing remedies like RLHF or full retraining are computationally expensive and risk further harming downstream performance. This paper asks whether one can identify the specific training samples responsible for trustworthiness failures and repair the model with a targeted, compute-efficient update that preserves perplexity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the post-SFT model parameters, the original SFT training set (prompt–response pairs from the Anthropic HH static subset), and paired trustworthiness evaluation sets where each sample contains a prompt, a proponent (desirable) response, and an opponent (undesirable) response. It produces repaired model parameters obtained via a single gradient-ascent step on a small selected subset, along with the DPP-regularized subset of detrimental training samples. Trustworthiness is scored via log-odds (log P(opponent|prompt) − log P(proponent|prompt)) following a Bradley-Terry formulation, and downstream performance is tracked as perplexity on the held-out SFT split.

## Method

The approach is a two-stage repair pipeline. First, it computes per-sample influence scores γ^j using influence functions with EK-FAC approximations of the inverse Gauss-Newton Hessian, attributing trustworthiness degradation on each metric to specific SFT training examples. Second, it selects a small, diverse subset of the most detrimental samples by maximizing a DPP log-determinant (promoting near-orthogonality in final-layer embedding space) plus a weighted attribution-gain term, solved greedily via submodular maximization. The selected subset is then used in a gradient-ascent update under the Proximal Bregman Response Function (PBRF) framework, which constrains parameter movement in both parameter space (quadratic regularizer) and functional space (Bregman divergence), ensuring downstream perplexity stays within a small tolerance. A key theoretical contribution (Proposition 1) formalizes neighborhood loss transfer: increasing the loss on one sample propagates to embedding-neighbors, which motivates keeping the subset small and diverse to avoid destabilizing the repair. The entire procedure requires only a single gradient-ascent step on the final transformer block, completing in minutes versus hours of retraining.

## Results

Across six LLMs (Pythia 1.4B–6.9B, Qwen2.5 1.5B–7B), the proposed DPP-regularized PBRF repair improves stereotypical bias log-odds by 8.1%–18.2%, truthfulness by up to 9.6% (Qwen2.5-7B), and machine ethics by up to 8.7% (Qwen2.5-7B) relative to the post-SFT model. Perplexity degradation remains below 2.4% for all models, with most under 1%. On reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MMLU), accuracy changes are consistently below 1%, with 99.3% instance-level agreement on MMLU and 97.3% on GSM8K, confirming that trust repairs do not compromise general capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPO (Rafailov et al., NeurIPS 2023), the proposed method achieves lower (better) truthfulness log-odds for all six models (e.g., 0.476 vs. 0.838 for Pythia-1.4B), while DPO wins on stereotypical bias and machine ethics; however, DPO inflates perplexity substantially and requires up to 17 hours of fine-tuning versus minutes for the single-step PBRF repair. Compared to gradient-ascent unlearning baselines (SGA, GA from Yao et al. 2024), PBRF-based repair consistently outperforms on log-odds while SGA and GA frequently diverge and degrade perplexity. KL-regularized gradient ascent (GA+KL, Martens 2020) is stable in perplexity but yields only marginal gains on truthfulness and bias, with results closest to the proposed method on machine ethics. Against BiPO steering vectors (Cao et al., NeurIPS 2024), the proposed method outperforms on average across all three trust metrics—for example, on Pythia-1.4B it achieves +13.4% bias improvement vs. +4.3% for BiPO, and +7.0% truthfulness vs. +0.4%—while also introducing no test-time overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of training data attribution (influence functions, EK-FAC) and model repair via the proximal Bregman response function (PBRF) framework. While prior TDA work (Grosse et al., 2023; Koh & Liang, 2017) has been used for model debugging or unlearning, and PBRF (Bae et al., 2022) was originally formulated for general sample removal, this paper is the first to combine them specifically for trustworthiness repair of post-SFT LLMs, adding a DPP-based diversity regularizer to stabilize subset selection. It positions itself as a compute-efficient alternative to RLHF/DPO pipelines, which require hours of retraining and curated preference data, by reducing the repair to a single gradient-ascent step on ~100 identified samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the dramatic compute reduction: minutes of gradient ascent versus hours of retraining or DPO fine-tuning, making trustworthiness repair feasible under tight deployment budgets. However, the method is currently validated only on models up to 7B parameters and three trust metrics on a single SFT dataset (Anthropic HH), leaving open whether the approach scales to frontier models or generalizes to adversarial/robustness dimensions. The negative correlations between trust metrics (e.g., truthfulness vs. ethics) revealed by the common-subset experiments suggest that joint repair across all dimensions may require more sophisticated multi-objective formulations than the simple aggregation used here.
