## Problem

Existing methods that incorporate demonstration data into RL (e.g., PARROT, imitation learning) implicitly assume demonstrations are optimal and fully aligned with the target task, causing severe performance degradation or outright failure when priors are misaligned or suboptimal. The core gap is the lack of a mechanism to adaptively compose multiple data-driven behavior priors while retaining the ability to bypass them entirely when they are unhelpful, without imposing a performance ceiling from the demonstration data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264252/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes continuous state vectors, one or more unlabeled demonstration datasets (state-action pairs without reward signal or temporal ordering), and the online environment reward. It outputs a continuous MDP action vector at each timestep, produced by whichever lower-level actor the high-level selector chooses. A key structural detail is that the NF priors are pre-trained offline on demonstrations and then frozen during online RL; only the latent policies and the selector's value estimates are updated online, and the selector itself is parameter-free (a softmax over Q-value estimates).

## Method

APC is a hierarchical RL architecture that composes n prior-based actors (each with a state-conditioned Real NVP pre-trained on a distinct demonstration dataset) alongside one prior-free actor that uses an identity flow, all controlled by a learning-free arbitrator selector that softmaxes over each actor's Q-value estimate to pick which actor executes at each step. The critical reward-sharing mechanism exploits NF invertibility: the executed action is mapped through every actor's inverse flow to construct valid latent-space transitions for all actors simultaneously, so every replay buffer is updated at every timestep regardless of which actor was selected. Each latent policy is trained with SAC in its own NF latent space, and the NF parameters remain frozen throughout online learning. The prior-free actor guarantees complete flexibility to diverge from all demonstrations, while the arbitrator (rather than a learned high-level policy) avoids primacy bias by directly comparing value estimates without introducing a second learning loop.

## Results

On the CarRacing environment with suboptimal human demonstrations (~250 mean return), APC reaches the optimal return of ~900 in approximately 30k steps, compared to ~100k for QFilter and ~250k for from-scratch SAC, while PARROT plateaus near the human level (~250). On PointMaze with misaligned demonstrations, APC achieves 100% success in ~0.5M steps, whereas PARROT and IL remain at 0% and 7% respectively after 1.5M steps. Under aligned demonstrations, PARROT converges fastest, but APC still substantially outperforms IL, QFilter, and from-scratch SAC, confirming that its added robustness does not come at a significant sample-efficiency cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APC decisively outperforms PARROT (Singh et al., 2021) under both misaligned and suboptimal demonstration settings: on PointMaze with misaligned priors, PARROT achieves 0% success after 1.5M steps while APC reaches 100% in ~0.5M; on CarRacing with suboptimal human demos, PARROT is capped near the human return (~250) whereas APC reaches the optimal ~900. Against the IL baseline (Lu et al., 2023), APC wins on all three experiments: it converges faster under aligned demos, avoids the 7% ceiling under misalignment, and reaches optimal CarRacing returns in ~30k vs. IL's considerably slower convergence. QFilter (Nair et al., 2018) is the closest competitor, also avoiding complete failure under misalignment, but APC converges substantially faster on PointMaze and reaches optimal CarRacing returns in ~30k vs. QFilter's ~100k steps. Under perfectly aligned demonstrations, PARROT remains the fastest method (its behavior-cloning pre-training nearly solves the task), and APC is slightly slower, but the gap is modest and APC still dominates all other baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PARROT line of work (Singh et al., 2021), which uses pre-trained Normalizing Flows as data-driven behavior priors for RL in a latent action space, by introducing a hierarchical composition of multiple NF priors alongside a prior-free actor. The key conceptual departure is rejecting PARROT's implicit assumption that a single NF prior can be 'undone' by the latent policy under distribution shift; instead, APC explicitly allows the agent to bypass misaligned priors via a learning-free arbitrator selector (Russell & Zimdars, 2003) that compares value estimates across actors. The reward-sharing mechanism, which exploits NF invertibility to propagate transitions to all actors, is a novel technique not present in prior NF-prior or hierarchical RL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

APC addresses a practical gap: real-world demonstrations are often suboptimal, sparse, or task-misaligned, yet most demonstration-guided RL methods assume alignment and optimality. The finding that APC can exploit even misaligned priors for exploration while avoiding their performance ceiling (outperforming from-scratch SAC in some settings) suggests a more robust paradigm for integrating imperfect human data into online RL. Key limitations include linear computational overhead in the number of actors, evaluation restricted to three continuous-control environments, and the open question of whether the arbitrator's Monte Carlo value estimates remain reliable with many actors or in high-dimensional action spaces.
