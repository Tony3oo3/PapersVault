## Problem

Aligning diffusion models with human preferences via KL-regularized RL requires choosing a regularization strength β that balances adaptation against distributional drift and reward hacking. Finding the optimal β conventionally demands expensive alignment sweeps—retraining the model at multiple β values—which is prohibitively costly for large models like SDXL. The paper asks whether one can modulate the effective regularization strength at inference time, after a single alignment, without any additional training.

## Input / Output

At inference, DeRaDiff requires two pre-trained models (a reference and a single aligned model at some anchor β), a text prompt, and a scalar interpolation weight λ ∈ [0,1]. Each denoising step performs two forward passes (one per model) with classifier-free guidance applied independently, then combines the resulting Gaussian posteriors into a single realigned posterior. The output is a standard RGB image decoded from the final latent via the VAE, with no change in resolution or format relative to the base model.

## Method

DeRaDiff extends decoding-time realignment from discrete language models to continuous diffusion trajectories by deriving a closed-form stepwise Gaussian update (Theorem 1). At each reverse-diffusion step, the method computes a geometric mixture of the reference and aligned per-step posteriors—raising each Gaussian density to complementary powers (1−λ) and λ—which yields a new Gaussian with analytically computable mean and variance. This realigned posterior is then sampled from and fed into the next denoising step, iteratively over all N steps. The single tunable parameter λ controls the effective regularization: λ=0 recovers the reference model, λ=1 recovers the aligned model, and 0<λ<1 performs a stable convex interpolation in log-density space. The approach also extends to multi-reward settings where multiple aligned models are combined via a product of powered posteriors, and the paper demonstrates that DeRaDiff can undo reward hacking by using a severely under-regularized model as the anchor with a small λ.

## Results

DeRaDiff achieves near-identical approximation of models aligned from scratch across all three evaluation metrics (PickScore, HPS v2, CLIP) when λ∈[0,1], with mean absolute errors of 0.154%, 0.265%, and 0.430% of the respective means on SDXL (and 0.332%, 0.425%, 0.448% on SD1.5). Roughly 87% of PickScore approximations have errors ≤5×10⁻⁴, and the Bland-Altman mean difference for CLIP is only −0.273% of the original mean, indicating no systematic bias. Using a single anchor model aligned at β=2000, the largest absolute percentage difference across all target β values (500–10000) is 0.14% (PickScore), 0.27% (HPS), and 0.30% (CLIP) for λ≤1. Compute savings are substantial: replacing N alignment sweeps with one alignment plus DeRaDiff inference reduces GPU-hours by a factor of N (e.g., ~90% savings for N=10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against competing inference-time alignment methods; instead, the 'baseline' is the ground-truth model aligned entirely from scratch at each target β. DeRaDiff matches these from-scratch models with extremely small errors: for λ≤1 (convex interpolation regime), the maximum absolute percentage difference is 0.14% (PickScore), 0.27% (HPS), and 0.30% (CLIP) across all target β values from 500 to 10000 using a single β=2000 anchor. Performance is strongest when the target β is higher than the anchor β (i.e., λ<1, interpolating toward the reference), and degrades smoothly when λ>1 (extrapolating beyond the anchor's regularization), where errors grow to ~1–2% for PickScore and ~1–2% for CLIP. The approximation is also robust to using a reward-hacked model as the anchor: DeRaDiff with a small λ can recover the distribution of a healthy β=2000 model from a severely reward-hacked β=250 anchor, reducing FID from 72.98 to 50.71 (30.5% improvement). Across both SDXL and SD1.5, all MAE values remain below 0.5% of the respective metric means, confirming that DeRaDiff is a faithful training-free surrogate for alignment sweeps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends decoding-time realignment from language models (Liu et al., 2024), where a geometric mixture of reference and aligned next-token distributions modulates KL strength at inference, to the continuous iterative denoising setting of diffusion models. The key technical departure is deriving a closed-form Gaussian posterior for the stepwise geometric mixture under the DDPM paradigm (Ho et al., 2020), as opposed to prior diffusion realignment work (Diffusion Blend, Cheng et al., 2025) which operates under the score-based SDE framework. The paper also builds on the standard KL-regularized RL fine-tuning formulation (Jaques et al., 2017; Rafailov et al., 2023) and the latent diffusion architecture (Rombach et al., 2022), positioning its contribution as the first realignment mechanism with a theoretical foundation for the stepwise posterior under DDPM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DeRaDiff eliminates the need for expensive alignment sweeps (reducing N×336 GPU-hours to a single 336 GPU-hour alignment), which is a significant practical saving for teams iterating on diffusion model alignment. The method's simplicity—a single scalar λ modifying the sampling loop—makes it easy to adopt, and the demonstrated ability to undo reward hacking by using a reward-hacked model as anchor with small λ offers a practical remediation tool. Limitations include the stepwise approximation (which may accumulate error over many denoising steps), the restriction to Gaussian per-step posteriors, and the non-convex behavior for λ>1 that can cause ill-conditioned covariances. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-Gaussian or diagonal-variance posteriors, to multi-step SDE samplers, and whether Bayesian optimization over λ (sketched in the appendix) can reliably find the global optimum in practice.
