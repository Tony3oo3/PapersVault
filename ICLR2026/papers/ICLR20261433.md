## Problem

Submodular function minimization (SFM) has been extensively studied under the assumption of access to exact or noisy function-value oracles, but no prior work addresses SFM when only a noisy pairwise comparison (dueling) oracle is available. In many practical settings—recommendation systems, price optimization, LLM alignment—absolute scores are unreliable while relative preferences between two options are natural and robust. This paper is the first to formulate and solve SFM under a dueling oracle, where the algorithm can only query pairs of subsets and receive a probabilistic binary response indicating which has the higher function value.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives the ground set size n, a budget of T oracle calls, and the transfer function ρ (linear with coefficient a ∈ (0,1] or sigmoid with parameter b > 0). At each iteration it selects a pair of subsets (S_t, S_t') and observes a single binary response o_t ∈ {±1} whose probability of being correct is governed by ρ(f(S_t) − f(S_t')). The output is a subset Ŝ_T ⊆ [n] with an additive error E_T = f(Ŝ_T) − min_S f(S). A key structural constraint is that the queried pairs in the proposed algorithms always differ by exactly one element (|S_t △ S_t'| = 1), which is formalized as Restriction 1 and is critical for the matching lower bound.

## Method

The algorithm converts SFM to convex minimization via the Lovász extension f̂ on the hypercube [0,1]^n, then runs projected SGD with subgradient estimators constructed from dueling oracle responses. For the linear transfer function ρ(x) = ax, the estimator ĝ_ti = o_ti / a is unbiased because E[o/a] = f(B_τ(i)) − f(B_τ(i−1)) = g_i, yielding an O(n^{3/2}/(a√T)) error that is optimal up to constants under Restriction 1. For the sigmoid transfer function, unbiased estimators are infeasible, so the paper incorporates Firth's penalized maximum likelihood estimator (which has O(1/k²) bias) to construct low-bias subgradient estimates, achieving O(n^{7/5}/T^{2/5}) error with a novel three-term balance between learning rate η, number of SGD steps T', and per-coordinate query repetitions k. Lower bounds are established via Yao's principle combined with KL-divergence indistinguishability arguments over hard instances, showing Ω(n^{3/2}/√T) for restricted algorithms and Ω(n/√T) in general.

## Results

For the linear transfer function, the proposed algorithm achieves an additive error of O(n^{3/2}/(a√T)), which is optimal up to constants among algorithms satisfying Restriction 1 (bounded symmetric difference between queried sets), as the matching lower bound is Ω(n^{3/2}/(a√T)). Without Restriction 1, the lower bound drops to Ω(n/(a√T)), leaving a gap of O(√n). For the sigmoid transfer function, the algorithm achieves O(C(b)^{1/5}/b^{4/5} · n^{7/5}/T^{2/5}), while the lower bound under Restriction 1 remains Ω(n^{3/2}/√T), indicating a gap in T-dependence. Numerical experiments (Appendix B) confirm that the empirical error scales consistently with the theoretical predictions in both T and n.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is the first paper to address SFM with a dueling oracle, so no direct algorithmic baselines exist for comparison. The primary comparison is between the proposed upper bounds and the established lower bounds: for the linear transfer function, the upper and lower bounds match exactly under Restriction 1, proving optimality; without the restriction, a gap of O(√n) remains. For the sigmoid case, a gap in T-dependence persists (T^{2/5} vs. T^{1/2}), which the authors conjecture can be closed. The numerical experiments in Appendix B validate the theoretical scaling: with n=5 and T ranging from 500 to 2500, the empirical error decreases at the predicted rate, and with T=2000 and n from 4 to 14, the error grows consistently with the n-dependence in the bounds. The paper also notes that prior SFM algorithms (Hazan & Kale 2012; Ito 2019) require access to function values and thus are not directly comparable, as the dueling oracle provides only relative pairwise feedback.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SGD-on-Lovász-extension framework from Hazan & Kale (2012) and Ito (2019), which assumed access to (noisy) function values, to a fundamentally different oracle model where only pairwise comparisons are available. For the sigmoid transfer function, it departs from Saha et al. (2025) on dueling convex optimization by incorporating Firth's bias-reduction method, since the smoothness and strong convexity assumptions required by Saha et al. do not hold for Lovász extensions of general submodular functions. The lower bound proof adapts the KL-divergence indistinguishability technique from Ito (2019) and Auer et al. (2002) to the richer query space of set pairs, requiring new analysis of distributional distances over queried pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first study of SFM under a dueling oracle, a setting motivated by practical scenarios (LLM alignment, recommendation, price optimization) where pairwise preferences are more natural and reliable than absolute scores. For linear transfer functions, the matching upper and lower bounds establish algorithmic optimality, while for sigmoid transfer functions a gap remains in T-dependence (T^{-2/5} vs. T^{-1/2}), which the authors conjecture can be closed. Open questions include tightening the sigmoid bound, understanding n-dependence more finely, handling unknown transfer functions, and exploring whether purely combinatorial (non-continuous-relaxation) approaches could yield more efficient algorithms.
