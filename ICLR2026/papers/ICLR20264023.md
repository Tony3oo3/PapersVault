## Problem

State-of-the-art model merging methods are evaluated on highly idealized benchmarks where all source models share uniform hyperparameters, leaving their robustness in realistic settings largely unexplored. This paper identifies two specific, common failure modes that cause severe performance degradation (up to 24% normalized accuracy loss): disparities in task vector norms (e.g., from differing learning rates) and low prediction confidence of source models (e.g., from label smoothing, Mixup, or focal loss). The paper provides theoretical explanations for both phenomena and proposes a practical remedy.

## Input / Output

DisTaC consumes the task vector τ_t = θ_t − θ_pre from each fine-tuned model, the pre-trained backbone weights, and unlabeled data drawn from each task's input distribution (no labels required). It outputs a conditioned task vector whose norm has been rescaled to a target value and whose source model has been made more confident, ready for insertion into any standard merging formula. The method operates on CLIP ViT-B-32 and ViT-L-14 image encoders across eight RGB classification tasks, with the text encoder frozen throughout.

## Method

DisTaC is a lightweight knowledge distillation procedure that pre-conditions task vectors before merging, addressing both failure modes in a single pass (Algorithm 1). For norm disparity, it rescales the longest task vector to match the mean norm of the others, then distills from the original (pre-scaling) model as teacher into the rescaled student using only soft-target KL divergence (ζ=1) on unlabeled data, with an L2 regularizer anchoring the student near the rescaled initialization to prevent norm drift. For low confidence, it sets the student temperature higher than the teacher temperature (T_stu > T_tcr), forcing the student to learn from a higher-entropy distribution and thereby producing lower-entropy, more confident predictions at inference. The key design choices—shrinking rather than stretching longer vectors, and making sources overconfident before merging (with post-hoc calibration applied afterward)—are supported by both theoretical analysis (Proposition 1 on cosine alignment under norm disparity; Proposition 2 on first-order CE loss degradation from calibration penalties) and empirical scaling experiments.

## Results

On eight vision tasks with CLIP ViT-B-32 and ViT-L-14 backbones, DisTaC pre-conditioning restores post-merge accuracy to near-Original levels under both failure modes. Under Low Confidence, DisTaC yields gains of up to 35.8 percentage points (ViT-B-32) and 63.6 percentage points (ViT-L-14) in absolute accuracy; for example, EMR-Merging jumps from 27.4% to 92.3% (ViT-L-14) and TSVM from 60.7% to 81.5% (ViT-B-32). Under Norm Mismatch, WUDI-Merging recovers from 49.2% to 84.4% (ViT-B-32) and 57.9% to 91.4% (ViT-L-14). The distillation requires only 500 steps (~3.2 s on 2×A100) and uses unlabeled data exclusively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DisTaC is a pre-conditioning step applied before any merging method, so the comparison is each baseline with vs. without DisTaC under the two failure modes. Under Norm Mismatch, all seven baselines degrade substantially (e.g., WUDI-Merging drops from 85.5% to 49.2% on ViT-B-32), but DisTaC restores every method to within 1–2 points of the Original setting. Under Low Confidence, the degradation is even more severe—EMR-Merging collapses to 27.4% (ViT-L-14) and WUDI-Merging to 28.0%—yet DisTaC recovers EMR-Merging to 92.3% and WUDI-Merging to 91.6%, essentially matching the Original benchmark. TSVM benefits the most under Low Confidence on ViT-B-32, gaining 20.8 points (60.7→81.5) and reaching 91.8% normalized accuracy, close to its Original 92.4%. Task arithmetic and TIES, the simplest baselines, also see large recoveries (e.g., TIES: 54.5→68.7 on ViT-B-32 Low Confidence). No baseline is harmed by DisTaC; in every configuration the +DisTaC row meets or exceeds the unconditioned row, confirming that the conditioning is universally beneficial rather than method-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the task arithmetic framework (Ilharco et al., 2023), which defines task vectors as the difference between fine-tuned and pre-trained weights, and on knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) as a mechanism for transferring model behavior. It departs from the broader model-merging literature (TIES, Consensus Merging, TSVM, EMR-Merging, WUDI-Merging, Iso-CTS) by arguing that these methods are evaluated under highly idealized conditions and identifying two previously unexamined failure modes—task vector norm disparity and low source-model confidence—that cause substantial degradation in realistic settings. DisTaC repurposes KD not for compression but as a lightweight preconditioning step that harmonizes task vector norms and sharpens source-model confidence before any merging method is applied.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that well-calibrated (low-confidence) source models are actively harmful to model merging is counterintuitive and challenges the default assumption that calibration is always beneficial; the paper advocates instead for post-hoc calibration of the merged model. Practically, DisTaC offers a simple, low-cost (≈3 seconds on A100s) fix that restores near-ideal merge performance across seven state-of-the-art methods, making model merging viable in heterogeneous real-world deployments where hyperparameters inevitably vary. Limitations include restriction to vision classification tasks, dependence on unlabeled data (which may be unavailable under security constraints), and focus on only two of potentially many interference sources; extension to generation tasks, NLP, and other modalities remains open.
