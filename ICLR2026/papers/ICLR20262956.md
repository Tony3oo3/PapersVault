## Problem

Existing one/few-step generative models trained from scratch (consistency models, MeanFlow) lack explicit distribution-matching guarantees, while methods that do provide such guarantees (e.g., IMM) require multiple particles per training step, limiting scalability. The paper asks whether a single-sample, single-stage training objective can simultaneously yield high-fidelity few-step generation and a provable upper bound on the 2-Wasserstein distance between data and model distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262956/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model operates in the latent space of a Stable Diffusion VAE and takes as input a noise sample from a Gaussian prior, a class label (dropped with 10% probability for unconditional generation), a CFG weight w, and a user-chosen number of sampling steps n. It produces 256×256 or 512×512 RGB images. Unusually, the network is conditioned on two timesteps (t and s) plus the CFG weight, and the same trained model can generate samples in 1, 2, or 4 NFEs without retraining by simply changing the number of Euler steps in the sampling loop.

## Method

TVM parameterizes a two-time-conditioned network F_θ(x_t, t, s) whose output defines both the displacement map f_θ = (s−t)·F_θ and the instantaneous velocity u_θ = F_θ(x_t, t, t). The training loss jointly minimizes a terminal velocity matching term (comparing d/ds f_θ against u_θ evaluated at the model-displaced point x_t + f_θ) and a standard flow-matching term, with the key distinction that the derivative is taken with respect to the terminal time s rather than the initial time t. The paper proves this objective upper-bounds the 2-Wasserstein distance under Lipschitz continuity of u_θ, which motivates minimal architectural changes (RMSNorm-based QK-normalization and normalized AdaLN modulation) to stabilize DiT training. A custom Flash Attention kernel supports JVP forward and backward passes through the attention layers, and a scaled parameterization f_θ = (s−t)·w·F_θ ensures the network output naturally scales with the CFG weight, with a 1/w² loss weighting to prevent gradient explosion.

## Results

On ImageNet-256×256, TVM achieves 3.29 FID with a single function evaluation (1-NFE) and 1.99 FID with 4 NFEs, outperforming all prior from-scratch one/few-step methods and even surpassing the 500-NFE DiT-XL/2 diffusion baseline (2.27 FID). On ImageNet-512×512, TVM achieves 4.32 FID at 1-NFE and 2.94 FID at 4-NFE, again exceeding the 500-NFE DiT baseline (3.04 FID). These results represent state-of-the-art performance for one/few-step generative models trained from scratch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TVM outperforms MeanFlow (the closest competitor) on 1-NFE FID by 0.14 on ImageNet-256 (3.29 vs. 3.43) and by 0.92 on ImageNet-512 (4.32 vs. 5.24), with similar margins at 2-NFE. Against sCT-XL (1.1B params), TVM (678M params) achieves comparable 1-NFE FID on ImageNet-512 (4.32 vs. 4.33) while using ~40% fewer parameters, and clearly wins on ImageNet-256 (3.29 vs. 4.33). With 4 NFEs, TVM surpasses the 500-NFE DiT-XL/2 diffusion baseline on both resolutions (1.99 vs. 2.27 on 256; 2.94 vs. 3.04 on 512), demonstrating that 4 function evaluations suffice to match 500-step ODE integration. TVM also substantially outperforms IMM (8.05 FID at 1×2 NFE), Shortcut (10.60 FID at 1-NFE), and iCT (34.24 FID at 1-NFE) on ImageNet-256. The paper notes an interesting trade-off: higher CFG weights (w=2) improve 1-NFE quality but slightly degrade 2-NFE quality, suggesting a capacity limitation in fitting all step counts simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

TVM generalizes Flow Matching by matching the terminal velocity of a trajectory (w.r.t. the end time s) rather than the initial velocity (w.r.t. the start time t), establishing a clean duality with MeanFlow which differentiates w.r.t. t. It also subsumes Physics-Informed Distillation (PID) as a special case where the start time is fixed at t=1, and provides the 2-Wasserstein distributional guarantee that IMM offers but without requiring multiple particles per training step. Architecturally, it builds on Lipsformer's (Qi et al., 2023) Lipschitz-continuous attention design to address a previously unacknowledged instability in DiT-based training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit 2-Wasserstein upper bound gives TVM a principled distributional guarantee absent from other few-step methods (MeanFlow, sCT), and the Lipschitz-continuity requirement reveals a hidden architectural limitation of standard DiT that may affect other training objectives beyond TVM. The observed trade-off where higher CFG improves 1-NFE but degrades 2-NFE FID suggests a fundamental capacity constraint in fitting all step counts simultaneously, an open question the authors leave for future work. Scaling to video and higher resolutions, and whether the Lipschitz constraint is truly necessary or merely sufficient, remain important open directions.
