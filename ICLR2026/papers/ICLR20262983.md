## Problem

Existing reward models fall into two camps—scalar classifiers that are opaque and generative judges whose reasoning is superficial—neither of which can handle reasoning-intensive preference tasks requiring multi-criteria evaluation, trade-off navigation, and consequence simulation. The paper asks whether reward modeling can be recast as a reasoning task, so that the model produces long, coherent, interpretable reasoning chains before assigning a preference judgment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262983/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a prompt x and two candidate responses y_a, y_b, and produces a structured textual judgment j that includes a task-type tag (<type> Chat or Reasoning), a chain-of-rubrics or self-solved solution, a per-criterion evaluation, and a final <answer> token indicating the preferred response. For chat tasks the rubrics are question-specific (e.g., accuracy for medical questions, safety for harmful content); for reasoning tasks the model first solves the problem independently and then compares both responses against its own solution. The maximum response length is 8,192 tokens, and the model is trained with 7 candidate rollouts per prompt during RL.

## Method

RM-R1 is trained in two stages: (1) reasoning distillation, where ~9K high-quality reasoning traces are synthesized by an oracle model (Claude-3.7-Sonnet, with O3 correcting ~25% of errors) and the base instruct model is fine-tuned via NLL loss to internalize structured judging patterns; (2) RL with verifiable rewards using GRPO, where the reward is a binary correctness signal (±1) on whether the extracted <answer> matches the ground-truth label, with KL regularization against the post-distillation checkpoint. The key rollout mechanism is Chain-of-Rubrics (CoR): the model first categorizes each sample as Chat or Reasoning, then either self-generates evaluation rubrics with justifications (chat) or solves the problem before comparing responses (reasoning), enabling task-adaptive judging strategies. The paper further provides a theoretical analysis (Lemma 1, Proposition 1) showing that high-reward filtering in SFT distillation makes the training disagreement probability strictly smaller than the environment disagreement, giving RL a structurally stronger incentive to abandon trivial shortcuts and adopt robust reasoning.

## Results

RM-R1-DEEPSEEK-DISTILLED-QWEN-32B achieves the best average score of 81.5% across RewardBench (90.9), RM-Bench (83.9), and RMB (69.8), outperforming the strongest ScalarRM (INF-ORM-Llama3.1-70B, 78.8% avg) and GPT-4o (77.7% avg) by up to 4.9%. On RM-Bench, the most reasoning-intensive benchmark, RM-R1-DEEPSEEK-DISTILLED-QWEN-32B sets a new state-of-the-art at 83.9%, exceeding the previous best (Gemini-1.5-pro, 75.2%) by 8.7 percentage points, with 91.8% on math and 74.1% on code. Even the 14B variant (79.6% avg) surpasses all prior leading RMs including the 70B and 340B scalar models while using only 8.7K distillation examples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RM-R1-DEEPSEEK-DISTILLED-QWEN-14B (79.6% avg) outperforms the strongest ScalarRM, INF-ORM-Llama3.1-70B (78.8%), by 0.8 points on average, and Nemotron-4-340B-Reward (77.1%) by 2.5 points, despite being 5x and 24x smaller respectively. Against GenRM baselines, RM-R1-DEEPSEEK-DISTILLED-QWEN-32B (81.5% avg) beats GPT-4o (77.7%) by 3.8 points and Skywork-Critic-Llama-3.1-70B (76.9%) by 4.6 points. On RM-Bench specifically, RM-R1-DEEPSEEK-DISTILLED-QWEN-32B (83.9%) surpasses Gemini-1.5-pro (75.2%) by 8.7 points and GPT-4o (72.5%) by 11.4 points, establishing a clear lead on the most reasoning-intensive benchmark. Among REASRM baselines, RM-R1-DEEPSEEK-DISTILLED-QWEN-14B (79.6% avg) outperforms Self-taught-evaluator-llama3.1-70B (76.2%) by 3.4 points despite being 5x smaller, and exceeds DeepSeek-GRM-27B on RMB (68.5% vs. 69.0% is a near-tie, but RM-R1 leads on RM-Bench at 81.5% vs. unreported). The 32B Instruct variant (RM-R1-QWEN-INSTRUCT-32B) achieves the best RMB score (73.0%) among all open-weight models, surpassing GPT-4o (73.8% is slightly higher) and all scalar baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative reward model (GenRM) paradigm introduced by Zhang et al. (2025, Generative Verifiers) by casting reward modeling explicitly as a reasoning task, and builds on the RLVR training recipe from DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025) and the GRPO optimizer from DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024). It departs from prior REASRMs (DeepSeek-GRM, JudgeLRM, Self-taught Evaluators) that relied on unstructured chain-of-thought or rejection sampling, instead introducing a structured Chain-of-Rubrics (CoR) mechanism with task-type categorization and a two-stage distillation-then-RL training pipeline. The paper is the first to provide a systematic empirical comparison of different reward model training paradigms (cold-start RL, RL+CoR, distillation+RL+CoR) under controlled conditions, isolating which ingredients are actually necessary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a 14B reasoning reward model outperforms 70B/340B scalar RMs and GPT-4o by up to 4.9% on average is practically significant for the RLHF pipeline, suggesting that smaller, interpretable, reasoning-based RMs can replace large opaque scalar models. The finding that RLVR alone is insufficient without a distillation warm-start (cold-start RL becomes unstable and underperforms) provides a concrete training recipe that practitioners can adopt. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multimodal or agentic reward modeling, how to reduce inference cost of long reasoning chains in production RLHF loops, and whether active preference collection guided by rubric insufficiency can further reduce human annotation needs.
