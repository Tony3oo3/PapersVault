## Problem

Real-world temporal data (e.g., heterogeneous blood tests, event logs, social media cascades) consists of multiple signal types recorded at irregular, asynchronous intervals. Existing approaches either align signals to fixed grids via imputation—losing information about the irregularity itself—or are restricted to path-like structures without built-in interpretability. No prior method learns directly from sets of arbitrary-structure graphs representing sparse temporal signals while providing multi-grain, by-design interpretability.

## Input / Output

SUPERMAN takes a set of m graphs (each representing one signal type, e.g., one biomarker trajectory), where nodes carry feature vectors and timestamps and edges encode pairwise time deltas; graphs may be directed path-graphs or arbitrary structures (e.g., news propagation trees). The output is a single binary label produced by additively summing subset-level representations, alongside interpretable importance scores at three resolutions: individual node contributions, whole-graph contributions, and grouped-subset contributions. The additive construction means importance scores are exact contributions to the prediction, not post-hoc approximations.

## Method

SUPERMAN models each signal type as an implicit directed graph and processes the collection via a two-level additive architecture. Within each graph, ExtGNAN (an extension of GNAN) applies multivariate neural networks to groups of features rather than univariate networks per feature, learning a distance function ρ over pairwise time deltas and feature-shape functions ψ_l per feature group; node representations are computed by summing weighted contributions from all other nodes, and graph representations by summing node representations. Across graphs, signals are partitioned into k subsets; single-graph subsets pass through ExtGNAN directly, while multi-graph subsets apply ExtGNAN per graph followed by a DeepSet aggregation. The final prediction is the sum of all subset representations, preserving additive transparency. The paper proves that grouping signals or features strictly increases expressivity (Theorems 3.1–3.2), enabling practitioners to trade fine-grained interpretability for modeling capacity when domain priors suggest natural groupings.

## Results

On the ICU Length of Stay task (PhysioNet2012), SUPERMAN achieves 97.41±0.38 AUPRC, outperforming the best baseline DGM² (97.00±0.40) by 0.41 points. On Crohn's Disease onset prediction (Danish Health Registries), SUPERMAN reaches 83.93±0.27 AUPRC, surpassing the best baseline GRU-D (83.36±0.40) by 0.57 points. On the GossipCop fake news detection task, SUPERMAN attains 97.34±0.2 accuracy, exceeding the best GNN baseline GraphConv (96.77±0.1) by 0.57 points. All improvements are achieved while providing built-in multi-level interpretability (node, graph, and subset importance).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SUPERMAN outperforms all 8 medical baselines on both tasks. On ICU LoS, it leads DGM² (97.00) by 0.41 AUPRC and Trans-mean (96.44) by 0.97; the largest gap is over mTAND (93.02) at 4.39 points. On CD onset, it beats GRU-D (83.36) by 0.57 AUPRC and mTAND (83.17) by 0.76, with a particularly large margin over SeFT (71.22) at 12.71 points. On GossipCop fake news detection, SUPERMAN (97.34 accuracy) surpasses GraphConv (96.77), GCNFN (96.52), GATv2 (96.10), and GraphSAGE (94.45), with the largest gap of 2.89 points over GraphSAGE. Notably, the medical baselines (Transformer, GRU-D, SeFT, mTAND, DGM², MTGNN, RAINDROP) are restricted to path-like graph structures and cannot handle the tree-structured propagation graphs in the GOS task, whereas SUPERMAN operates on arbitrary graph topologies. The paper also reports that SUPERMAN achieves the lowest Expected Calibration Error (0.028) among all baselines on the CD task and shows substantially greater robustness to additive and multiplicative value noise compared to RAINDROP and mTAND.

## Relation to Prior Work

SUPERMAN extends Graph Neural Additive Networks (GNAN), an interpretable GNN inspired by generalized additive models, from single graphs to sets of graphs by introducing ExtGNAN (multivariate feature-group networks) and DeepSets aggregation over graph subsets. It deliberately departs from the dominant imputation-based paradigm for irregular time series (BRTS, SAITS, CSDI, Raindrop) by modeling sparsity directly through implicit path-graph structure with no interpolation or learned imputation. The signal-grouping mechanism, which trades fine-grained interpretability for expressivity, has no direct analogue in prior interpretable GNN work and is motivated by the need to incorporate domain priors in clinical settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The interpretable-by-design architecture with provable expressivity gains from grouping offers a principled way to integrate clinical domain knowledge while retaining auditability—critical for high-stakes medical deployment where post-hoc attribution is insufficient. The O(n²) per-graph complexity (though maskable to linear) and the manual tuning of biomarker groupings as a hyperparameter are practical limitations that may constrain scalability to very large or high-dimensional signal sets. An open question is whether the grouping strategy can be learned end-to-end rather than specified by domain experts, and whether the robustness advantage over imputation-based baselines (particularly under value noise) generalizes beyond the tested medical and social-media domains.
