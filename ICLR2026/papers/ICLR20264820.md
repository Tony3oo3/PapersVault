## Problem

Existing molecular conformer generation methods operate at a single structural scale—denoising atomic coordinates or torsion angles—while neglecting the hierarchical organization of molecules into rigid scaffolds, flexible linkers, and local functional groups. This single-scale focus causes generated conformers to violate global geometric consistency, particularly for ring systems and heavy-atom backbones that anchor the spatial arrangement of the rest of the molecule. The paper demonstrates through a controlled study that explicit geometric guidance from key substructure positions dramatically outperforms subgraph-level or fragment-level guidance in preserving structural validity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D molecular graph (atom types, bond connectivity, and edge features) as input and produces a full 3D conformer as an n×3 coordinate matrix. The generation is decomposed into K nested subgraph stages (e.g., heavy-atom backbone → full molecule, or Murcko scaffold → heavy atoms → full molecule), where each stage outputs a partial coordinate matrix that conditions the next. The framework is backbone-agnostic: it wraps around any equivariant generative model (DDPM, flow matching, score matching) by injecting conditional distance features from the upsampled coarse-stage output into the denoising network.

## Method

MSGEN defines a K-level nested subgraph sequence G¹ ⊂ G² ⊂ … ⊂ G^K = G and trains a separate equivariant denoising model per stage, where stage k conditions on the coordinates R^{k-1} from stage k-1. To bridge the scale mismatch between coarse and fine representations, the paper introduces molecular upsampling: a topological sort over the molecular graph determines an atom ordering, and each fine atom is placed at a random offset (radius τ) from the centroid of its already-positioned neighbors, yielding a chemically informed conditional prior R_cond^k. To prevent distribution shift between training (ground-truth coarse structure) and inference (sampled coarse structure), conditional augmentation perturbs the ground-truth coarse coordinates with a small amount of diffusion noise before upsampling. The training loss decouples across stages via an ELBO derivation, reducing to a standard ε-prediction objective per stage with the upsampled condition as an additional input. The modified equivariant network (e.g., GFN) incorporates inter-atomic distances to the conditional structure alongside the standard inter-atomic distances, preserving SE(3) equivariance throughout the multi-stage pipeline.

## Results

On GEOM-Drugs (δ=1.25Å), GeoDiff+MSGEN achieves 90.41% mean COV-R and 0.8424Å mean MAT-R, outperforming the GeoDiff baseline (87.86% / 0.8686Å) and all other baselines. In the chemical property evaluation on QM9, GeoDiff+MSGEN reduces the mean absolute error in average energy from 0.2597 eV (GeoDiff) to 0.1795 eV, and in HOMO-LUMO gap from 0.3091 eV to 0.2035 eV. For domain generalization (trained on Drugs, tested on QM9 at δ=0.5Å), GeoDiff+MSGEN reaches 83.73% COV-R and 0.3055Å MAT-R, surpassing GeoDiff (74.94% / 0.3492Å) and matching or exceeding models trained natively on QM9.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoDiff+MSGEN outperforms the GeoDiff backbone by +2.55 pp in COV-R (90.41% vs. 87.86%) and reduces MAT-R by 0.026Å, while also improving precision metrics (COV-P: 66.26% vs. 60.17%). Against traditional methods, the improvement is substantial: RDKit achieves only 45.74% COV-R, and VAE/GAN-based methods (CGCF, ConfVAE, GraphDG) score below 56% COV-R. GeoMol (67.16% COV-R) and ConfGF (62.15% COV-R) are the strongest non-diffusion baselines but still trail GeoDiff+MSGEN by over 20 pp in recall. The framework generalizes across backbones: ET-Flow improves from 74.47% to 80.50% COV-R, ConfGF from 62.15% to 65.51%, and EBD shows gains in precision (COV-P: 68.10% vs. 65.97%) despite a negligible COV-R change (91.92% vs. 92.10%). In the domain generalization setting (Drugs→QM9), GeoDiff+MSGEN (83.73% COV-R) surpasses both the GeoDiff baseline (74.94%) and models trained natively on QM9 such as CGCF (78.05%) and ConfVAE (77.84%). The 3-stage variant with MurckoScaffold further improves GeoDiff to 91.05% COV-R and ET-Flow to 81.91%, confirming that additional structural hierarchy yields incremental gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

MSGEN extends the hierarchical diffusion paradigm from vision (cascaded diffusion, image super-resolution) to molecular conformer generation, building directly on GeoDiff's geometric diffusion backbone and the equivariant network architectures (GFN, ET-Flow, ConfGF). It departs from prior structural-guidance methods—SubgDiff's local subgraph denoising, EBD's uniform fragment-level blurring, and Qiang et al.'s single-resolution coarse-to-fine design—by explicitly exploiting the hierarchical chemical organization of molecules (scaffold → functionalized scaffold → full structure) and introducing molecular upsampling to bridge scale mismatches between stages. The conditional augmentation strategy and the ELBO derivation for the multi-stage model are new theoretical contributions not present in prior hierarchical diffusion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular design of MSGEN—plugging into diffusion, flow matching, and score matching backbones without architectural overhaul—makes it a practical drop-in enhancement for existing conformer generation pipelines, which is valuable for the computational chemistry community. The molecular upsampling technique, which respects chemical graph connectivity via topological sorting and anchor-based sampling, offers a reusable primitive for any coarse-to-fine molecular generation task. Open questions include whether the framework scales to macromolecules (proteins, polymers) where the number of hierarchical levels and scale gaps grow substantially, and whether the 2-stage heavy-atom/hydrogen split is optimal or whether data-driven stage decomposition could further improve results.
