## Problem

Existing text-to-motion diffusion models collapse complex multi-action prompts into a single global text embedding, causing omissions, reordering, or unnatural transitions in generated motions. Moreover, standard benchmarks like HumanML3D are dominated by simple single-action descriptions, making it impossible to determine whether improvements on easy motions generalize to temporally ordered, multi-event sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261273/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural-language motion description as input, which is first decomposed into event clauses by Gemini 2.5 Flash and then encoded into per-event tokens by the TMR encoder alongside a single global text token. The output is a 3D human motion sequence in root space (root velocities plus local joint features) at 20 FPS, generated via 10-step UniPC sampling from a 1000-step DDPM schedule. Motions are retargeted to a unified skeleton with the character initially facing the positive Z direction.

## Method

Event-T2M introduces a principled event definition (the smallest semantically self-contained action mappable to a contiguous motion segment) and builds a diffusion denoiser around it. Each event clause is embedded by the TMR encoder into an event token, and these tokens are injected into Conformer blocks via a novel Event-based Cross-Attention (ECA) module where motion tokens serve as queries and event tokens as keys/values, preserving temporal ordering. A complementary Adaptive Textual Information Injector (ATII) provides segment-aware global text conditioning through channel-wise gating, while a Local Information Modeling Module (LIMM) enforces short-horizon kinematic smoothness via depthwise convolutions. Training uses standard DDPM with classifier-free guidance (text dropout at 0.2, guidance scale 4), and the 0.5 residual weighting on feed-forward layers follows Macaron-style architecture for stable optimization under strong event-level supervision.

## Results

On the standard HumanML3D test set, Event-T2M achieves R-Precision Top-1 of 0.562, FID of 0.056, MM-Dist of 2.711, and MModality of 0.949, matching or exceeding all baselines on alignment and diversity metrics. On the event-stratified HumanML3D-E benchmark, Event-T2M's advantage grows with compositional complexity: at ≥4 events it achieves FID 0.265 and R-Precision Top-1 0.466, outperforming the next-best baselines (MoMask FID 0.418, MoGenTS FID 0.423) by 37–38% in FID. On Motion-X, Event-T2M leads all baselines in R-Precision Top-1 (0.519 vs. 0.458 for MoGenTS) and MM-Dist (2.979 vs. 3.498).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On standard HumanML3D, Event-T2M achieves the best R-Precision (Top-1: 0.562 vs. 0.529 for MoGenTS, 0.521 for MoMask) and best MModality (0.949 vs. 1.226 for MMM), though MoGenTS retains a slight FID edge (0.033 vs. 0.056). On KIT-ML, Event-T2M is competitive but trails MoGenTS on FID (0.159 vs. 0.143) and R-Precision Top-1 (0.439 vs. 0.445), while leading on MModality (0.762). On Motion-X, Event-T2M clearly dominates in R-Precision Top-1 (0.519 vs. 0.458 for MoGenTS) and MM-Dist (2.979 vs. 3.498). The most decisive advantage appears on HumanML3D-E: at ≥4 events, Event-T2M's FID of 0.265 beats MoMask (0.418), MoGenTS (0.423), Light-T2M (0.627), GraphMotion (0.857), and AttT2M (1.077) by 37–75%, and its R-Precision Top-1 (0.466) exceeds all baselines by 2.5–10.1 points. Against the retrieval-based ReMoDiffuse, Event-T2M wins on all metrics across all event-count conditions, with the FID gap widening from 0.072 (Condition 2) to 0.318 (Condition 4), confirming that explicit event-level conditioning outperforms retrieval under compositional complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

Event-T2M builds on the diffusion-based text-to-motion paradigm (DDPM, MotionDiffuse, MLD) and the Conformer backbone, but departs from the dominant practice of conditioning on a single CLIP global embedding or per-word token-level cross-attention (AttT2M, MMM). Instead, it introduces an intermediate 'event' unit—decomposed via an LLM and encoded by the motion-specialized TMR encoder—and injects these event tokens through a novel event-based cross-attention (ECA) module that replaces the self-attention sublayer within Conformer blocks. The paper also extends the evaluation landscape by constructing HumanML3D-E, a benchmark stratified by event count, addressing a gap where prior work (GraphMotion, MotionMamba) offered only coarse or proxy definitions of 'complex' motion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a blind spot in the field: state-of-the-art models that achieve near-perfect FID on HumanML3D systematically fail on multi-event prompts, a failure masked by benchmarks dominated by single-action descriptions. The event-level conditioning principle is generalizable and the HumanML3D-E benchmark provides a reproducible protocol for measuring compositional fidelity, which should influence future evaluation practices. Open challenges remain: the model does not enforce long-horizon physical plausibility, does not handle human-object interaction, and the LLM-based decomposition introduces a dependency on prompt engineering quality (93.3% accuracy). Extending event-based conditioning to multimodal (vision, audio) settings and enabling fine-grained event editing are natural next steps.
