## Problem

Neural networks used as amortized solvers for parametric constrained optimization lack inherent mechanisms to guarantee constraint satisfaction at inference time. Existing hard-constrained approaches either require expensive offline preprocessing, rely on loop unrolling for backpropagation (prohibitive in memory and computation), or cannot handle context-dependent feasible sets C(x). This paper addresses the gap of efficiently enforcing convex constraints that vary with the input while maintaining fast training and inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262135/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a context vector x ∈ R^p (e.g., initial/final configurations, obstacle positions, or even 1024×1024 images) and produces a decision variable y ∈ R^d guaranteed to lie in the convex feasible set C(x). The backbone network outputs a raw vector y_raw that is orthogonally projected onto C(x) via a dedicated projection layer. Constraints supported include equalities, box bounds, affine inequalities, second-order cones, ℓ₁-balls, ℓ∞-balls, and simplices, all potentially context-dependent through A(x) and b(x).

## Method

Πnet appends a projection layer to any backbone NN that orthogonally projects the raw output onto C(x) = Π_d(A(x) ∩ K(x)), where A is a hyperplane and K is a Cartesian product of simple convex sets chosen so their projections admit closed-form expressions. The forward pass computes the projection via a finite number of Douglas-Rachford operator splitting iterations (Algorithm 1), exploiting the decomposition to reduce each step to closed-form projections onto a hyperplane and a product set. For backpropagation, rather than unrolling all splitting iterations, the paper applies the implicit function theorem to the fixed-point equation of the splitting map, reducing the VJP to solving a single linear system via bicgstab — each step of which costs the same as one forward splitting iteration. Training uses a self-supervised loss (the optimization objective directly) with Ruiz matrix equilibration to improve conditioning, and the projection layer is active during training (not just inference) to avoid instabilities. The result is a feasible-by-design architecture that surpasses DC3 and JAXopt by orders of magnitude in training time and solution quality while matching or beating their inference speeds.

## Results

On the large non-convex benchmark (d=1000, 500 eq/ineq constraints), Πnet achieves near-zero constraint violation (CV ≈ 0) and relative suboptimality well below the 5% optimality threshold, while DC3 exhibits unacceptably large CV and RS. Training requires only 50 epochs for Πnet versus 1000 for DC3 and ~14 hours for JAXopt. For batch inference (1024 instances), Πnet completes in ~0.28 s compared to ~10,720 s for IPOPT and ~19.4 s for JAXopt on the large non-convex problem, a ~38,000× speedup over the solver.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DC3 (Donti et al., 2021), Πnet decisively wins on solution quality: on the large non-convex problem, DC3 produces unacceptably high CV and RS, while Πnet maintains CV ≈ 0 and RS well below 5%. Training is also dramatically faster (50 vs. 1000 epochs), and inference times are comparable (Πnet ~6 ms vs. DC3 ~2 ms single on large). Against JAXopt (Blondel et al., 2022), Πnet achieves lower RS and CV, trains orders of magnitude faster (minutes vs. ~14 hours on the large dataset), and is ~70× faster on batch inference (0.28 s vs. 19.4 s). Against traditional solvers (IPOPT for non-convex, OSQP for convex), Πnet is ~1,600× faster on single-instance and ~38,000× faster on batch inference for the large non-convex problem. Against cvxpylayers (Agrawal et al., 2019), Πnet matches RS and CV while being ~2× faster on single inference and ~190× faster on batch inference (GPU). For second-order cone programs, Πnet outperforms cvxpylayers by orders of magnitude on large problems (0.91 s vs. 342 s batch) and beats SCS on batch inference, though SCS is faster on single small instances.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of hard-constrained neural networks (HCNNs) and implicit layers. It builds on the Douglas-Rachford operator splitting framework (Bauschke & Combettes, 2017) for computing projections in the forward pass, and on the implicit function theorem (Dontchev & Rockafellar, 2009) for backpropagation, replacing the loop-unrolling strategy used by prior projection-based HCNNs (Chen et al., 2018; Cristian et al., 2023). The key conceptual departure from existing HCNN methods (DC3, RAYEN, LinSATNet) is the decomposition of the feasible set as C = Π_d(A ∩ K) with efficiently projectable components, combined with a specialized splitting that exploits the projection problem structure rather than treating it as a generic composite optimization. Unlike implicit layer approaches (cvxpylayers, JAXopt) that embed full QP/conic solvers, Πnet specializes to the projection subproblem, yielding substantially faster training while maintaining competitive inference speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, GPU-ready JAX implementation that handles context-dependent convex constraints and non-convex objectives, filling a gap left by prior HCNN methods that either require fixed feasible sets or suffer from prohibitive training times on large problems. The orders-of-magnitude training speedup over JAXopt (minutes vs. ~14 hours on large benchmarks) and the robust, low constraint violation across all test instances make this a viable drop-in component for amortized optimization pipelines. The main limitation is the requirement for convex constraint sets; extending to non-convex constraints (e.g., collision avoidance) via sequential convexification or homeomorphisms is identified as key future work. The multi-vehicle motion planning application demonstrates extensibility to fleet-level non-convex objectives, but the decoupled-among-vehicles constraint structure limits immediate applicability to coupled multi-agent scenarios.
