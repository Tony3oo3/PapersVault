## Problem

Prior jailbreak research has concentrated almost exclusively on modern languages, especially English, leaving classical Chinese as an unexplored attack vector. Classical Chinese possesses semantic compression, rich metaphorical devices, and inherent ambiguity that create a 'safety blind spot': models fully comprehend the obscure inputs, yet safety guardrails optimized for modern languages fail to detect harmful intent in this context. The paper asks whether this linguistic gap can be systematically exploited and optimized for automated black-box jailbreak attacks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a harmful instruction (e.g., from AdvBench) and an 8-tuple of discrete strategy parameters spanning role identity, behavioral guidance, mechanism, metaphor mapping, expression style, knowledge relation, contextual setting, and trigger pattern. It produces a classical Chinese adversarial prompt that is submitted to the target LLM in a black-box setting, along with a fitness score in [0, 120] combining a GPT-4o-judged consistency score (0–100) and a binary rejection-keyword score (0 or 20). A two-stage translation module (classical Chinese → modern Chinese → English) normalizes model responses before evaluation to mitigate biases from metaphorical and semantically compressed text.

## Method

CC-BOS formalizes jailbreak prompt generation as search over an eight-dimensional Cartesian product of discrete strategy sets, then explores this space using a Fruit Fly Optimization Algorithm (FOA) with three complementary operators: smell search (local index perturbation with exponentially decaying step size for exploration-to-exploitation transition), vision search (probabilistic copying of the global-best individual per dimension, with attraction strength increasing linearly over iterations), and Cauchy mutation (heavy-tailed index jumps triggered upon K-iteration stagnation to escape local optima). A hash-based deduplication mechanism prevents redundant fitness evaluations, and early stopping halts the search once the best fitness exceeds a threshold τ. The key insight is that classical Chinese induces a 'High Capability-Low Alignment' distributional shift: the model retains sophisticated comprehension of classical syntax and rhetoric while its safety alignment, trained predominantly on modern-language data, lacks corresponding guardrails, making the 8D strategy space particularly effective at evading keyword and template-based defenses.

## Results

CC-BOS achieves a 100% Attack Success Rate (ASR) across all six target LLMs (Gemini-2.5-Flash, Claude-3.7, GPT-4o, DeepSeek-Reasoner, Qwen3, Grok-3) on AdvBench, with average toxicity scores (Avg.Score) ranging from 3.14 to 4.88. On the CLAS 2024 and StrongREJECT benchmarks, CC-BOS maintains 99–100% ASR across all models. In terms of query efficiency, CC-BOS requires only 1.12–2.38 average queries per attack, substantially fewer than the next-best optimization-based baselines (CL-GSO: 1.24–21.42; AutoDAN-Turbo-R: 10.00–16.84).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CC-BOS outperforms all six baselines on every model and every dataset. Against the strongest baseline ICRT, CC-BOS closes the largest gaps on Claude-3.7 (100% vs. 40% ASR on AdvBench; 99% vs. 21% on CLAS) and GPT-4o (100% vs. 74% on AdvBench), while maintaining a consistent 2–12 point lead on models where ICRT already performs well (e.g., Grok-3: 100% vs. 98%). Compared to CL-GSO, CC-BOS improves ASR by 20–56 points (e.g., 100% vs. 44% on Grok-3) while also reducing average query count from 1.24–21.42 to 1.12–2.38. Against AutoDAN-Turbo-R, CC-BOS gains 12–30 ASR points and requires roughly 5–10× fewer queries. The weaker baselines (PAIR, TAP, GPTFUZZER) are largely ineffective on most models, with ASR below 30% in most settings, highlighting that the classical Chinese strategy space combined with bio-inspired optimization is the primary driver of CC-BOS's superior performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the black-box jailbreak attack literature (PAIR, TAP, GPTFUZZER, AutoDAN, CL-GSO, ICRT) by introducing Classical Chinese as the linguistic context for adversarial prompt generation, a setting absent from prior multilingual jailbreak work that focused on low-resource or code-switching scenarios. Methodologically, it departs from CL-GSO's genetic optimization by adopting a Fruit Fly Optimization Algorithm with smell/vision search and Cauchy mutation over a formalized 8-dimensional strategy space (role, behavior, mechanism, metaphor, expression, knowledge, context, trigger). The two-stage translation module (Classical Chinese → Modern Chinese → English) for evaluation is a practical addition not present in prior cross-lingual jailbreak studies, which typically evaluate in the target language directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central empirical claim—that a language with abundant historical corpora (Classical Chinese) is *more* vulnerable than low-resource languages—reframes the multilingual safety problem from a data-coverage issue to a 'High Capability-Low Alignment' distributional shift, with implications for how LLM providers should scope their safety training. However, the 100% ASR is demonstrated on only 50 AdvBench samples across 6 models, and the attack requires a capable LLM (Deepseek-Chat) as the prompt generator, limiting immediate practical deployability. Open questions include whether language-agnostic defenses (e.g., semantic-level intent detection) can close this gap, whether the vulnerability persists as models receive more classical-language safety fine-tuning, and whether the 8-dimensional strategy space generalizes beyond the specific Classical Chinese rhetorical conventions it encodes.
