## Problem

Existing statistical frameworks for GenAI system quality estimation (PPI, PPI++, RePPI) assume i.i.d. source/target sampling and missing-completely-at-random labels, but real evaluations suffer from covariate shift (rater demographics and content distributions differ between lab and deployment) and selection bias (raters non-randomly drop out on sensitive content). These violations cause severe miscoverage in confidence intervals, yet no prior framework simultaneously corrects for both forms of sampling bias while leveraging imperfect LLM persona ratings as auxiliary information.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two data streams: (i) source rating tuples containing rater characteristics X, embedded content V (MiniLM-L6-v2 projected to 15 dims via UMAP), a binary completion indicator C, the observed human rating Y (only when C=1), and a persona rating Ŷ; and (ii) target samples with X, V, and Ŷ but no human ratings. It produces a point estimate of the target mean rating θ_t, an asymptotically valid 1−δ confidence interval, and a plug-in variance estimate. The framework generalizes beyond means to any M-estimation target (variance, quantiles, conditional parameters) by swapping the score function.

## Method

The estimator combines two nuisance components: a persona-augmented regression μ̂(W, Ŷ) trained on source data to predict human ratings (using persona ratings as an auxiliary feature), and a reweighting function α̂(W,C) that corrects for both covariate shift (via density ratio ω₀) and selection bias (via propensity π₀). The doubly-robust property guarantees valid coverage whenever the product of L² estimation errors satisfies ‖α̂−α₀‖·‖μ̂−μ₀‖ = o_P(N_t^{-1/2}), meaning either nuisance can converge at a slower non-parametric rate. To estimate α₀ in high-dimensional text spaces, the authors use a Riesz loss that directly minimizes for the ratio ω₀/π₀ rather than learning each component separately, substantially reducing variance. K-fold cross-fitting ensures nuisance estimates are independent of the fold data, and the framework generalizes Chernozhukov et al. (2023) from covariate-shift-only settings to simultaneous covariate shift plus selection bias with surrogate (persona) ratings.

## Results

Across three datasets (Synthetic, PRISM, DICES), DR (Riesz) achieves the lowest bias and highest coverage among all methods. On PRISM, DR (Riesz) attains 0.93 coverage with 0.46 MAE, compared to 0.66 coverage and 0.63 MAE for RePPI and 0.00 coverage for PPI++. On DICES, DR (Riesz) reaches 0.86 coverage with 0.02 MAE, versus 0.40 coverage and 0.04 MAE for RePPI and 0.01 coverage for PPI++. On the fully synthetic setting, DR (Riesz) achieves perfect 1.00 coverage with 0.03 MAE, while RePPI reaches only 0.56 coverage and PPI++ only 0.03.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DR (Riesz) decisively outperforms both PPI++ and RePPI on all three datasets. Against PPI++ (Angelopoulos et al., 2023b), DR (Riesz) achieves 0.93 vs. 0.00 coverage on PRISM and 0.86 vs. 0.01 on DICES, with substantially lower bias (0.46 vs. 1.03 on PRISM; 0.02 vs. 0.06 on DICES). Against RePPI (Ji et al., 2025), DR (Riesz) improves coverage by 27 percentage points on PRISM (0.93 vs. 0.66) and 46 points on DICES (0.86 vs. 0.40), while also reducing bias (0.46 vs. 0.63 on PRISM; 0.02 vs. 0.04 on DICES). The gap is most pronounced on the semi-synthetic datasets with high-dimensional text embeddings, where PPI++ and RePPI assume MCAR and fail to correct for covariate shift and selection bias simultaneously. DR (Riesz) also outperforms its own classical variant DR (Classical) by 11 points on PRISM coverage (0.93 vs. 0.82) and 54 points on DICES (0.86 vs. 0.32), highlighting the benefit of Riesz loss over separate propensity estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the doubly-robust estimation framework (Bang & Robins, 2005; Chernozhukov et al., 2018) and the Riesz loss machinery (Chernozhukov et al., 2022b, 2023) to the GenAI evaluation setting, where prior prediction-powered inference methods (PPI, PPI++, RePPI) explicitly assume i.i.d. source/target sampling and missing-completely-at-random labels. The key conceptual departure is simultaneously correcting for both covariate shift and selection bias in human ratings while incorporating LLM persona ratings as auxiliary regression features, generalizing Chernozhukov et al. (2023) to M-estimation with surrogate outcomes. The persona-prompting literature (Castricato et al., 2024; Fröhling et al., 2024) provides the practical mechanism for generating surrogate ratings, but this paper is the first to embed those imperfect ratings within a formally valid statistical estimation pipeline under sampling bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first principled statistical framework for obtaining valid confidence intervals on GenAI system quality when human ratings are collected under realistic sampling conditions (demographic mismatch, rater attrition on sensitive content), a gap that existing PPI/RePPI methods silently assume away. The Persona Simulation Framework is a reusable community resource for stress-testing future evaluation methods under controlled bias. Key limitations include the no-concept-drift assumption (P_s(Y|W) = P_t(Y|W)), the overlap/positivity requirement, and the practical complexity of implementing Riesz loss minimization over high-dimensional text embeddings. Open questions include how estimates degrade under concept drift violations, whether the framework extends naturally to multimodal (image, audio) evaluation, and how sensitive coverage is to misspecification of the embedding-based content representation.
