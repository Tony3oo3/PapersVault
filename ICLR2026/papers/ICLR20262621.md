## Problem

Structured images such as charts and geometric diagrams remain difficult for MLLMs because even minor perceptual slips (e.g., misreading a bar height) cascade into erroneous conclusions. Existing visual CoT methods that introduce intermediate visual cues are constrained by low-fidelity image processing (often requiring source code or relying on coarse visual primitives) and by linear, chain-like reasoning that cannot backtrack or explore alternative branches, limiting their effectiveness on complex, realistic benchmarks.

## Input / Output

The system takes a structured image (chart or geometric diagram) and a natural-language query as input. The grounding model localizes referenced elements to absolute pixel coordinates, which drive classical CV operators to produce intermediate processed images (crops, magnified regions, masked series, auxiliary lines, connected points). The final output is a text answer accompanied by a full reasoning trace: the sequence of tool calls, intermediate images stored in a planner-managed image memory, critic feedback, and any self-correction rounds. The image memory is not a linear append-only log; the planner selectively recalls specific historical images by index, keeping the context compact while enabling non-linear exploration.

## Method

PixelCraft is a multi-agent system built on two pillars: (1) high-fidelity tool use, where a Qwen2.5-VL-3B model fine-tuned on 53k programmatically synthesized chart annotations and 2k Inter-GPS geometry samples provides pixel-level grounding coordinates that drive classical CV operators (cropping, magnification, color-based masking, auxiliary-line rendering, point/line construction) inside dedicated tool agents; and (2) a flexible three-stage reasoning workflow — query-aware agent selection by a dispatcher, role-driven discussion coordinated by a planner (which assigns sub-tasks to tool agents and the reasoner while a visual critic validates each processed image in-loop), and post-hoc self-correction by a planning critic that reviews the full trace and triggers re-reasoning. A key architectural distinction is the planner-managed image memory: rather than appending all historical images to an ever-growing context (as in linear visual CoT), the planner stores intermediate visual states and selectively recalls them, enabling explicit branching, backtracking, and trajectory adjustment while reducing long-context overhead.

## Results

PixelCraft achieves the highest accuracy across all three chart-reasoning benchmarks (CharXiv, ChartQAPro, EvoChart) and all three MLLM backbones (GPT-4o, GPT-4.1-mini, Claude-3.7-sonnet). With GPT-4.1-mini, PixelCraft reaches 68.1% on CharXiv, 65.56% on ChartQAPro, and 79.44% on EvoChart, improving over the best baseline by +9.5, +7.71, and +8.16 points respectively. With Claude-3.7-sonnet, it achieves 73.9% (CharXiv), 69.82% (ChartQAPro), and 80.48% (EvoChart). On the Geometry3K auxiliary-line subset, PixelCraft also leads all baselines, e.g., 34.38% with GPT-4.1-mini vs. 32.81% for the next-best method (Reconcile).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PixelCraft outperforms all five baselines across every benchmark and backbone. CoT (Wei et al., 2022) provides a modest 3–6% gain over direct answering but remains the weakest reasoning baseline; PixelCraft exceeds it by 4.3–9.5 points. Multi-agent debate (Du et al., 2023) and Reconcile (Chen et al., 2024) show little to no benefit over CoT and in several cases degrade performance (e.g., Debate drops to 49.38% on ChartQAPro with GPT-4o, below CoT's 56.52%), indicating that generic multi-agent discussion without specialized visual tools is insufficient for structured images. Refocus (Fu et al., 2025), the only prior visual-tool baseline, performs inconsistently and falls below CoT on multiple settings (e.g., 50.88% vs. 68.64% on EvoChart with GPT-4o), suggesting its low-fidelity tools are inadequate for complex charts. Finally, the Self-Refine baseline following Critic-V (Zhang et al., 2025a) actually degrades accuracy under a matched compute budget (62.0% vs. 63.8% CoT on CharXiv with GPT-4.1-mini), confirming that PixelCraft's gains stem from high-fidelity tool use and flexible image-memory reasoning rather than additional reasoning turns.

## Relation to Prior Work

PixelCraft sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) visual chain-of-thought methods that insert intermediate edited images (Refocus, Visual Sketchpad, Chain-of-Images), (2) tool-augmented multimodal reasoning (MM-REACT, ViperGPT, Set-of-Mark), and (3) multi-agent collaboration frameworks (Debate, Reconcile, role-playing agents). It departs from prior visual CoT work by replacing the rigid linear image-append paradigm with a planner-managed image memory that supports selective recall, branching, and backtracking. It also diverges from existing chart-tool approaches (Refocus, Visual Sketchpad) by coupling a fine-tuned pixel-level grounding model with classical CV operators rather than relying on source code or low-fidelity contour/line detection, and from generic multi-agent debate by introducing specialized visual tool agents with in-loop and post-hoc critics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that high-fidelity, pixel-level image editing—achieved by fine-tuning a compact 3B grounding model and driving classical CV operators—combined with a flexible non-linear multi-agent workflow, yields consistent 5–9% gains across three chart benchmarks and a geometry benchmark, and generalizes to real-world infographics. This suggests that the perceptual bottleneck in structured-image reasoning is not primarily a model-capacity issue but a tool-fidelity and reasoning-flexibility issue, a framing that could redirect effort toward better grounding and tool design rather than ever-larger MLLMs. Key open questions remain: whether visual tools can be fully auto-generated and verified without manual curation, whether weaker backbone models can sustain the planning and orchestration load, and whether the image-memory mechanism generalizes beyond structured images to more open-domain visual reasoning.
