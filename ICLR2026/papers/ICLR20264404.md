## Problem

Existing methods for incomplete multi-view multi-label classification handle either missing views or missing labels, but the dual-missing scenario where both occur simultaneously remains largely unexplored. Current approaches rely on loss-based alignment (contrastive learning, information bottleneck) without explicit structural constraints, which leads to under-representation or over-regularization when views are missing and fails to capture stable, discriminative shared semantics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264404/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes six heterogeneous image feature views (DenseSift-1000, DenseHue-100, GIST-512, RGB-4096, LAB-4096, HSV-4096) per sample, with 50% of views randomly dropped per sample and 50% of positive/negative labels randomly removed; missing entries are zero-filled and tracked via binary indicator matrices W (views) and G (labels) that mask all loss computations. The output is a fused multi-label probability vector per sample (sigmoid scores over all c label categories), produced by a weighted combination of per-view classifier outputs where weights are determined by each view's ability to preserve the global label correlation structure.

## Method

SCSD learns multi-view consistent discrete representations by encoding each view through view-specific MLPs into a unified space, then applying grouped vector quantization against a single shared codebook (k=2048, d_c=4) so that all views are mapped into the same limited set of codebook embeddings, naturally aligning cross-view semantics and reducing redundancy. Cross-view reconstruction—where each view's discrete representation is decoded by every view's decoder—further enforces consistency without additional alignment losses. At the decision level, a label-correlation-oriented fusion strategy computes a global label correlation matrix S from ground-truth labels and per-view prediction correlation matrices S^(v), then assigns softmax weights proportional to how well each view's predictions preserve the global correlation structure, requiring no extra networks or learnable parameters. Finally, a fused-teacher self-distillation loss uses the weighted fused prediction as a stop-gradient teacher to guide each view-specific classifier via a multi-label logit distillation (MLD) objective combined with BCE supervision, feeding global cross-view knowledge back into individual branches to improve generalization under missing-label conditions.

## Results

Under the standard dual-missing setting (50% missing views, 50% missing labels, 70% training data), SCSD achieves the best average rank (1.000) on all five datasets. Headline AP values are 0.447 (Corel5k), 0.578 (Pascal07), 0.345 (Espgame), 0.385 (Iaprtc12), and 0.634 (Mirflickr). The most notable gains are 5.83% and 8.15% AP improvements over the second-best method DRLS on Espgame and Iaprtc12 respectively, and average AP improvements of 14.94% over DICNet and 8.65% over SIP across all five datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCSD outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across all five datasets, achieving an average rank of 1.000 in every setting. The closest competitor is DRLS (CVPR 2025), which ranks second on most metrics; SCSD's AP advantage over DRLS ranges from 0.4% (Mirflickr) to 8.15% (Iaprtc12), with the largest gaps on datasets with more complex label spaces. Against DICNet (AAAI 2023), which uses contrastive learning for view consistency, SCSD achieves an average 14.94% AP improvement, and against SIP (ICML 2024), which uses information-bottleneck-based representation learning, the average AP gain is 8.65%. RANK (TPAMI 2025) is competitive on Mirflickr (0.606 vs. 0.634 AP) but trails SCSD more substantially on the other four datasets. The older baselines iMvWL (IJCAI 2018) and NAIM3L (TPAMI 2021) are consistently the weakest, with AP values 15–20% below SCSD on most datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on vector quantization (VQ-VAE, Van Den Oord et al. 2017) and grouped quantization (Baevski et al. 2019) to introduce a shared discrete codebook as a structured mechanism for multi-view consistency, departing from the contrastive-learning (DICNet) and information-bottleneck (SIP) paradigms that dominate prior incomplete multi-view multi-label work. For decision fusion, it replaces learnable-weight or quality-discriminator approaches with a label-correlation-preservation metric, and for training it adapts self-distillation (Zhang et al. 2021) and multi-label logit distillation (Yang et al. 2023) so that the fused multi-view prediction acts as a teacher for each single-view branch. The combination of these three components—discrete shared-codebook representation, correlation-aware weighted fusion, and fused-teacher self-distillation—into a unified framework for the dual-missing setting is the paper's core architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-missing (incomplete views + incomplete labels) scenario is increasingly relevant in real pipelines where sensor failures and annotation budgets co-occur, and this paper provides a clean, well-ablated method that consistently outperforms eight strong baselines across five datasets and six metrics. The shared-codebook mechanism offers a principled alternative to contrastive alignment that avoids the under-representation and over-regularization pitfalls the authors identify in prior loss-based approaches, and the label-correlation fusion criterion is lightweight (no extra network or learnable parameters). Open questions include scalability to very high view-missing rates (where cross-view alignment signal vanishes), extension to more than six views or to genuinely heterogeneous modalities (e.g., text + image + audio), and whether the codebook size and embedding dimension can be adapted per-view rather than shared globally.
