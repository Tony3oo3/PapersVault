## Problem

End-to-end backpropagation requires storing activations across all layers, creating a memory bottleneck that scales linearly with network depth and limits model scalability. Existing block-wise training methods (Forward-Forward, NoProp, greedy layer-wise learning) rely on ad-hoc local objectives without principled coordination between blocks, consistently underperform end-to-end training, and are largely confined to classification tasks on custom architectures. No systematic, theoretically grounded framework existed for converting modern transformer-based networks into genuinely independent trainable blocks that work across diverse tasks including generation and language modeling.

## Input / Output

The framework is applied to transformer-based networks (ViT, DiT, Llama-2-style, recurrent-depth) and takes task-specific inputs: RGB images (32×32 CIFAR, 256×256 ImageNet, 64×64 Tiny-ImageNet) for classification or generation, and text sequences (up to 3072 tokens) for language modeling. During training, Gaussian noise is added to the target variable (class label embeddings, clean images, or token embeddings) at a sampled noise level σ, and each block receives the augmented input (x, z_σ) plus noise-level conditioning via AdaLN. The output is the denoised target: a class prediction, a clean image, or a clean token sequence, with the full network producing the final result by sequentially applying blocks from σ_max to σ_min.

## Method

DiffusionBlocks interprets the residual connections in transformer networks as Euler discretization steps of the probability flow ODE in continuous-time diffusion, enabling a principled decomposition into independently trainable blocks. The conversion follows a three-step recipe: (1) partition L layers into B blocks, (2) assign each block a noise range [σ_b, σ_{b-1}] using equi-probability partitioning of a log-normal noise distribution so each block handles equal cumulative probability mass, and (3) augment each block with noise-level conditioning (AdaLN) and modify its input to include the noisy target z_σ = y + σε. Each block is then trained independently by minimizing a score-matching denoising objective (Eq. 6) over its assigned noise range, requiring gradients for only L/B layers at a time and achieving B× memory reduction. The equi-probability partitioning ensures balanced learning difficulty across blocks, with intermediate noise levels (where denoising is most challenging) receiving narrower intervals. For recurrent-depth models, the framework eliminates K recurrent training iterations entirely by training the network as a single-pass denoiser, while for diffusion models, inference requires only the relevant block per denoising step.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, DiT-L/2 + DiffusionBlocks achieves FID 9.00/10.63 (train/test), matching or slightly beating end-to-end DiT (9.01/12.09) while using only 3× less training memory. On CIFAR-10, DiT-S/2 + DiffusionBlocks improves FID to 30.59/37.20 versus 32.84/39.83 for end-to-end. For text generation, DiffusionBlocks applied to a 12-layer Llama-2-style AR model on LM1B achieves MAUVE 0.71 (vs. 0.50 baseline) and generative PPL of 12.32 (Llama-2-7B) / 30.99 (GPT2-XL), while a recurrent-depth Huginn model improves from MAUVE 0.49 to 0.70 with single-pass training instead of 32 recurrent iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Forward-Forward (Hinton, 2022), DiffusionBlocks achieves 59.30% accuracy on CIFAR-100 versus FF's 7.85%, a massive gap that underscores the failure of ad-hoc contrastive objectives for block-wise training. Against NoProp (Li et al., 2025), DiffusionBlocks scores 46.88% on CIFAR-100, outperforming all three NoProp variants (NoProp-DT 46.06%, NoProp-FM 37.57%, NoProp-CT 21.31%) and being the only method that combines continuous-time formulation with block-wise training. Against the recurrent-depth baseline Huginn (Geiping et al., 2025) on LM1B, DiffusionBlocks improves MAUVE from 0.49 to 0.70 and reduces generative PPL from 17.04 to 16.08 (Llama-2-7B) and 46.73 to 42.43 (GPT2-XL), while eliminating 32 recurrent training iterations in favor of single-pass diffusion training. Notably, on ImageNet and CIFAR-10 image generation, DiffusionBlocks actually surpasses end-to-end backpropagation in FID, suggesting that moderate block partitioning with equi-probability noise allocation can act as a form of implicit curriculum learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the established connection between residual networks and neural ODEs (Haber & Ruthotto, 2017; Chen et al., 2018) and the score-matching framework of continuous-time diffusion models (Song et al., 2021b; Karras et al., 2022), showing that residual connections naturally implement Euler discretization of the probability flow ODE. It departs from prior block-wise training methods (Forward-Forward, greedy layer-wise learning, NoProp) by deriving each block's objective from denoising score matching theory rather than ad-hoc local losses, providing the principled coordination that was missing. Unlike NoProp, which is packaged with a custom CNN architecture, DiffusionBlocks offers a systematic conversion recipe applicable to any residual network, particularly modern transformers, with minimal architectural modifications (noise conditioning via AdaLN and input augmentation).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiffusionBlocks provides a B× reduction in training memory (only one block requires gradients at a time) while maintaining competitive performance across vision, diffusion, autoregressive, recurrent-depth, and masked diffusion architectures — a practical win for scaling large models. The finding that moderate block partitioning (B=2–3) can outperform end-to-end training on ImageNet and CIFAR-10 is counterintuitive and suggests beneficial specialization effects from noise-range assignment, though the paper acknowledges this needs formal theoretical explanation. Key limitations include the requirement for matching input-output dimensions (excluding U-Net architectures), the current restriction to training from scratch rather than converting pretrained models, and the unresolved question of optimal block granularity. The framework's extension to recurrent-depth models, eliminating K training iterations entirely, opens a path toward more efficient test-time compute scaling.
