## Problem

Current all-atom biomolecular interaction predictors built on diffusion models achieve high structural accuracy but routinely produce physically invalid outputs—steric clashes, distorted covalent geometry, and stereochemical errors—because physical validity is not enforced as a hard constraint in the training objective. Existing remedies such as Boltz-1-Steering apply soft physics-informed guidance at inference time, which reduces but cannot eliminate violations. This paper closes the gap by elevating physical validity to a first-class constraint enforced during both training and inference, enabling guaranteed-valid predictions with only 2 denoising steps instead of 200.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard biomolecular interaction inputs—protein sequences, ligand 3D structures, nucleic acid sequences, and MSA features—processed through an atom-attention encoder, MSA module, and PairFormer to produce conditioning features for a diffusion denoiser. The output is a complete all-atom Cartesian coordinate set (N×3) for the predicted complex, with the guarantee that every atom pair satisfies steric, chirality, stereochemistry, planarity, internal-geometry, chain-overlap, and covalent-bond constraints simultaneously. Unlike baselines that require 200 denoising steps, the pipeline completes in 2 steps, and the final coordinates are post-processed by the Gauss-Seidel projection to enforce 100% physical validity before return.

## Method

The core contribution is a differentiable Gauss-Seidel projection module inserted after the diffusion denoiser. It formulates physical validity as a constrained optimization—projecting provisional atom coordinates to the nearest configuration satisfying all m local constraints (scaling to ~13M for a 7,000-atom complex)—solved via a penalty method with a block-diagonal coefficient matrix. Rather than a global linear solve, the module sweeps over constraints sequentially (Gauss-Seidel), updating only the atoms affected by each constraint, which exploits locality and sparsity to achieve quasi-second-order convergence in ~20 sweeps. The module is made differentiable via implicit differentiation: the backward pass solves an adjoint linear system (H + I)ᵀz = ∂L/∂x_proj using conjugate gradients on the GPU, avoiding the memory cost of unrolling iterations. During finetuning, training losses are computed on the projected coordinates with gradients flowing back through the projection, decoupling structural accuracy (handled by the denoiser) from physical validity (handled by the projection). This decoupling is what makes 2-step sampling sufficient: the denoiser no longer needs to spend capacity avoiding physics violations, and the projection guarantees validity at inference regardless of step count.

## Results

Using only 2 denoising steps, the method achieves 100% physical validity across all six benchmarks (CASP15, Test, PoseBusters, AF3-AB, dsDNA, RNA-Protein), while matching or approaching the structural accuracy of 200-step baselines. On the Test set, Complex LDDT is 0.79 (vs. 0.80 for Boltz-1 and 0.83 for Boltz-2); on PoseBusters, it is 0.91 (vs. 0.92 for Boltz-1 and 0.94 for Boltz-2). The method delivers a ~10× wall-clock speedup over 200-step baselines and ~2.3× over Protenix-Mini (5 steps), while guaranteeing physical validity—a property no other few-step method achieves.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Protenix-Mini (5 steps), the method wins decisively on Physical Validity (1.00 vs. 0.27–0.66) while matching or slightly exceeding Complex LDDT on Test (0.79 vs. 0.73) and PoseBusters (0.91 vs. 0.90), and is ~2.3× faster. Against Boltz-1-Steering (200 steps), the method achieves identical 100% validity on CASP15 and Test (both 1.00) and matches PoseBusters accuracy (0.91 vs. 0.92) while being 23–46× faster in wall-clock time. Against Boltz-1 and Protenix (200 steps), the method is within 0.01–0.02 Complex LDDT on all three main benchmarks while achieving 100% validity versus their 0.28–0.65. Against Boltz-2 (200 steps), the method trails on structural accuracy (e.g., 0.79 vs. 0.83 Complex LDDT on Test; 0.91 vs. 0.94 on PoseBusters) and on interface metrics, but matches its 100% Physical Validity on CASP15 and Test and exceeds it on PoseBusters (1.00 vs. 0.88). The authors note the accuracy gap to Boltz-2 stems from the weaker base model (Boltz-1 vs. Boltz-2 architecture) rather than the projection module itself, as applying the module as post-processing to Boltz-2 restores 100% validity while preserving Boltz-2's accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Boltz-1's all-atom diffusion architecture and constraint definitions, and adopts Protenix-Mini's few-step inference schedule, but departs fundamentally from the prevailing paradigm of enforcing physical validity through soft physics-informed guidance (Feynman-Kac steering in Boltz-1-Steering). Instead, it elevates validity to a hard constraint solved by a differentiable Gauss-Seidel projection module drawn from position-based dynamics in computer graphics, integrated end-to-end via implicit differentiation. The key conceptual shift is decoupling structural accuracy (handled by the diffusion network) from physical validity (handled by the projection), so the denoiser no longer needs many steps to 'learn' to avoid clashes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The guaranteed 100% physical validity (versus the soft, probabilistic guarantees of steering methods) combined with a ~10× wall-clock speedup from 200 to 2 denoising steps makes all-atom biomolecular prediction practical for iterative design workflows and downstream molecular dynamics that are destabilized by steric violations. The projection module is model-agnostic (demonstrated as a post-processing step on Boltz-2), suggesting broad adoptability across diffusion-based structure predictors. The main open question is whether single-step inference is achievable by combining the projection with one-step diffusion training, and whether the approach extends to even larger complexes where the constraint count (tens of millions) may stress the Gauss-Seidel convergence.
