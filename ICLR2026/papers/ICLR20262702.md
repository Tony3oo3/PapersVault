## Problem

While SAM is known to suppress noise memorization and improve generalization, its behavior under machine unlearning—where the model must simultaneously retain useful signals and forget designated samples—remains uncharacterized. A key open question is whether SAM's denoising advantage transfers to unlearning or breaks down when the forget set is treated as intentional 'noise' to be fitted, and how the retain-forget weight balance (α) should be set for each optimizer.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained CNN (ResNet-50 or ViT-Small) trained on CIFAR-100 or ImageNet-1K, a forget set F (~5% of training data) stratified by memorization score into high/mid/low difficulty tiers, and the remaining retain set R. The output is an unlearned model evaluated via a Tug-of-War (ToW) metric combining retain accuracy, forget accuracy, and test accuracy, plus membership inference attack correctness and Wasserstein-based feature entanglement scores. For Sharp MinMax, the model is split via gradient-magnitude-based weight masking into a retain sub-model and a forget sub-model before unlearning.

## Method

The paper first proves that under NegGrad (joint gradient descent on R and ascent on F), SAM's noise-suppression mechanism deactivates on the forget set: the perturbation term that normally deactivates noise neurons for retain samples instead reinforces them for forget samples, causing SAM to overfit F nearly as much as SGD while retaining its denoising advantage on R. This yields a signal-surplus result: SAM learns retain signal Θ(||φ||²) faster per epoch than SGD, so it tolerates a smaller retain weight α (gap scales as O(√(d/n)) in the benign-overfitting regime). Motivated by the observation that controlled overfitting to F improves sample-specific forgetting, the authors propose Sharp MinMax, which splits the model into W_R and W_F via gradient-magnitude masking, trains W_R with SAM purely on R to preserve utility, and trains W_F with sharpness maximization (the negation of SAM's objective) on F to deliberately create sharp, overfitting minima that erase the forget samples' influence.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, Sharp MinMax with SAM 0.1 unlearning and ASAM 0.1 pretraining achieves the best ToW score of 92.9%, substantially outperforming the best existing method (SalUn+ASAM 1.0 at 84.6%). On ImageNet-1K, Sharp MinMax with SAM 0.1 achieves 88.2% ToW (ASAM 1.0 pretraining), compared to 84.8% for NegGrad+ASAM 1.0. SAM/ASAM consistently improves ToW across all unlearning methods: e.g., on CIFAR-100, NegGrad+ASAM 1.0 (83.1%) beats NegGrad+SGD (81.8%), and SCRUB+ASAM 1.0 (73.8%) dramatically outperforms SCRUB+SGD (49.1%). For membership inference, SAM-enhanced methods reduce MIA correctness by 5–15 percentage points (e.g., NegGrad+ASAM 1.0 drops from 42.1% to 31.7% average MIA correctness on CIFAR-100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four unlearning baselines: NegGrad (Kurmanji et al., 2023), Random Label/RL (Graves et al., 2021), SalUn (Fan et al., 2023), and L1-Sparse (Jia et al., 2023). SAM/ASAM consistently improves every baseline: on CIFAR-100, NegGrad+ASAM 1.0 gains +1.3 ToW over NegGrad+SGD (83.1 vs 81.8), RL+ASAM 1.0 gains +2.0 (77.0 vs 75.1), and SalUn+ASAM 1.0 gains a dramatic +9.7 (84.6 vs 74.9). The most striking improvement is SCRUB+ASAM 1.0 on CIFAR-100, which jumps from 49.1% to 73.8% ToW. The novel Sharp MinMax algorithm outperforms all baselines by a wide margin: on CIFAR-100 it reaches 91.3–92.9% ToW versus 83.1–84.6% for the best existing methods, with the largest gains on high-memorization forget sets (e.g., 90.6% vs 81.3% for NegGrad+SAM 0.1 on F_high). On ImageNet, Sharp MinMax (88.2%) also surpasses the best baseline NegGrad+ASAM 1.0 (84.8%) by ~3.4 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the signal-noise decomposition framework of Kou et al. (2023) and the SAM denoising analysis of Chen et al. (2023) into the machine unlearning setting, where forget-set signals are treated as noise to be erased. It builds directly on NegGrad/SCRUB (Kurmanji et al., 2023) as the primary unlearning mechanism and on sharpness maximization from Kim et al. (2023) to construct the new Sharp MinMax algorithm. The key conceptual departure from prior SAM theory is showing that SAM's well-known noise-memorization prevention selectively 'shuts off' on the forget set under gradient-ascent unlearning, forcing SAM to overfit forget signals nearly as much as SGD while retaining its denoising advantage on the retain set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous theoretical characterization of how SAM interacts with unlearning objectives, yielding actionable guidance (relaxed α threshold, O(√(d/n)) signal surplus) for practitioners tuning retain-forget trade-offs. The reframing of overfitting as potentially beneficial for sample-specific unlearning—concretized in Sharp MinMax—opens a new design axis for unlearning algorithms, particularly in privacy/compliance settings where exact sample erasure is mandated. Limitations include the theory being restricted to two-layer ReLU CNNs, the simplified signal-noise data model, and the observation that α depends on factors beyond the retain-forget size ratio that remain incompletely characterized. Open questions include behavior under weak retain signals, overlap between retain and forget sets, and whether the 'regularizing' effect of unlearning on loss landscapes generalizes.
