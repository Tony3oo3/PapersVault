## Problem

Standard supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of foundation models performs unconstrained behavior cloning, which causes poor out-of-domain generalization, entropy collapse that limits subsequent RL exploration, and overfitting to the fine-tuning distribution. While RL methods like TRPO and PPO address this via trust-region constraints on policy updates, no equivalent mechanism existed for the supervised offline setting. The paper asks whether the clipped surrogate objective from PPO can be transplanted into SFT to constrain policy drift while preserving target-task learning.

## Input / Output

PSFT consumes offline (prompt, response) token sequences from datasets such as OpenR1-Math-8192 (long chain-of-thought math, up to 10k tokens), UltraFeedback (alignment pairs, 6k tokens), or Geometry-3K (text-image geometry problems). At each training step, the model computes per-token probabilities under both the current policy π_θ and a dynamically updated old policy π_θ_old, forming an importance ratio that is clipped to [1−ε, 1+ε] (ε=0.28 default). The output is a fine-tuned autoregressive model that generates free-form text responses (reasoning chains or aligned answers); for VLM experiments the input additionally includes an image, but only the LLM component is updated while the vision encoder remains frozen.

## Method

PSFT reinterprets SFT as a special case of policy gradient with constant unit advantage (Â_t = 1) and offline data, then replaces the standard cross-entropy loss with PPO's clipped surrogate objective: L^PSFT = E[min(r_t(θ), clip(r_t(θ), 1−ε, 1+ε))], where r_t(θ) = π_θ(a_t|s_t)/π_θ_old(a_t|s_t). The gradient analysis reveals that tokens whose probability ratio exceeds 1+ε receive zero gradient, effectively zeroing out updates for tokens where the offline distribution deviates sharply from the model's current distribution—this is the mechanism that prevents entropy collapse and preserves general capabilities. Crucially, π_θ_old is updated dynamically (every 4–16 steps) rather than fixed to the reference model, allowing gradual knowledge absorption from the dataset while maintaining a moving trust region. An optional warm-up SFT phase aligns the initial old policy with the data distribution before PSFT begins. A convergence proof under L-smoothness and bounded-gradient assumptions establishes an O(1/√T) rate, matching standard non-convex SGD.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, PSFT achieves an in-domain average of 46.98 (PSFT+warm-up: 48.17) versus SFT's 47.99, while substantially outperforming SFT out-of-domain (61.26 vs. 57.90 avg). The most striking gap is on IFEval (instruction-following): PSFT scores 73.03 vs. SFT's 54.42 on Qwen and 69.75 vs. 33.45 on Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, indicating PSFT preserves general capabilities that SFT severely degrades. After subsequent GRPO training, PSFT→GRPO reaches 53.31 in-domain and 64.06 out-of-domain (Qwen), surpassing SFT→GRPO (52.40 / 59.90) on both axes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against iw-SFT (Qin & Springenberg, 2025), PSFT (lr 1e-6, bs 32) wins on all four reported metrics: AIME-24 (19.38 vs. 17.64), AIME-25 (21.98 vs. 20.31), TruthfulQA (67.16 vs. 63.67), and IFEval (73.03 vs. 43.92), with the IFEval gap of ~29 points being the most dramatic. Against DFT (Wu et al., 2025), PSFT also dominates across the board: AIME-24 (19.38 vs. 10.73), AIME-25 (21.98 vs. 10.10), TruthfulQA (67.16 vs. 54.53), and IFEval (73.03 vs. 47.34). The paper notes that DFT exhibits markedly different (unstable) training dynamics and may only benefit from domain-specific signals, while iw-SFT shows training behavior similar to PSFT but ultimately achieves lower performance. In the broader comparison against standard SFT and SFT-KL (the primary baselines throughout the paper), PSFT matches in-domain performance while substantially outperforming on out-of-domain generalization, particularly on instruction-following (IFEval) where SFT degrades by 20+ points but PSFT retains near-base-model scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

PSFT directly repurposes the clipped surrogate objective from PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) and the trust-region philosophy of TRPO (Schulman et al., 2015) into the supervised offline setting, where the advantage is fixed at 1 and the importance ratio constrains token-level probability changes rather than trajectory-level policy shifts. This positions it as a conceptual bridge between SFT-as-behavior-cloning and policy-gradient RL, distinct from prior SFT improvements like iw-SFT (importance re-weighting to tighten the RL lower bound) and DFT (probability-based reweighting), which address distribution mismatch but do not explicitly constrain the magnitude of per-token updates. The paper also differentiates itself from hybrid SFT+RL frameworks (LUFFY, HPT) that mix offline and online losses, since PSFT is purely a supervised objective that happens to inherit RL's stability machinery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Because SFT is the near-universal cold-start step before RL or DPO in modern post-training pipelines, a drop-in replacement that prevents entropy collapse and preserves instruction-following (IFEval) has immediate practical value for teams building reasoning models. The finding that PSFT's initially slower in-domain convergence translates into superior post-RL performance challenges the common heuristic of maximizing SFT accuracy before moving to RL. Key limitations include evaluation primarily on 7B–8B dense models (with one 30B MoE test), limited data diversity (mostly math CoT), and the need to tune the clipping threshold ε and old-policy update frequency; whether the benefits persist at 100B+ scale, with mixed multi-domain data, or on the latest MoE/hybrid architectures remains open.
