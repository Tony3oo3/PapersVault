## Problem

Cost volumes, the standard component in iterative optical flow methods, impose quadratic memory and compute costs with neighborhood radius, forcing most methods to index at 1/8 resolution and limiting accuracy on high-resolution inputs. This paper challenges the assumption that cost volumes are necessary, asking whether feature-space warping alone—combined with a transformer-based update module—can match or exceed cost-volume-based performance while enabling high-resolution processing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265338/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two adjacent RGB frames of arbitrary resolution (evaluated at 540p and 1080p) and produces a dense per-pixel 2D flow field. Internally, features are extracted at 1/2 resolution and flow is refined over 5 iterative steps at that resolution, with the final Mixture-of-Laplace parameters upsampled to full image resolution via convex upsampling. Unlike cost-volume methods constrained to 1/8 indexing, WAFT performs feature look-up at 1/2 resolution, and the 1080p variant trains and infers at full resolution.

## Method

WAFT is a warping-based iterative architecture consisting of a frozen pretrained input encoder (DAv2, Twins-SVT, or DINOv3 backbone with a DPT head and ResNet refinement) and a recurrent update module built from a modified DPT-Small transformer. At each of T=5 iterations, the module concatenates frame-1 features, the frame-2 features warped by the current flow estimate (a single backward-warp lookup rather than a cost volume), and the hidden state, then predicts a residual flow update. The key insight is that the transformer's self-attention implicitly models the long-range dependencies that cost volumes capture explicitly, making the expensive 4D correlation volume unnecessary. This design eliminates both the cost volume and the context encoder found in RAFT-like methods, reducing training memory by 2–5× and enabling high-resolution indexing that yields sharper flow boundaries and lower endpoint error.

## Results

On Sintel (Clean), WAFT-DINOv3-a2 achieves the best EPE of 0.94, outperforming SAMFlow (1.00) and FlowDiffuser (1.02). On KITTI, WAFT-DAv2-a2 achieves the best Fl of 3.31 (all pixels) and 2.03 (non-occluded), beating DPFlow (3.56/2.12) and CCMR+ (3.86/2.07). On Spring, WAFT-DAv2-a2 achieves the best EPE of 0.304 and Fl of 1.197, surpassing DPFlow (0.340/1.311). WAFT is 1.3–4.1× faster than methods with competitive accuracy (e.g., 1.3× faster than Flowformer++, 4.1× faster than CCMR+), with latency of 212–290 ms on an RTX 3090 at 540p.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WAFT outperforms all cost-volume-based iterative methods on Sintel Clean EPE: WAFT-DINOv3-a2 (0.94) beats SAMFlow (1.00), FlowDiffuser (1.02), Flowformer++ (1.07), and CCMR+ (1.07). On KITTI, WAFT-DAv2-a2 achieves the best Fl on both all-pixels (3.31 vs. DPFlow 3.56, CCMR+ 3.86) and non-occluded pixels (2.03 vs. CCMR+ 2.07, DPFlow 2.12). On Spring, WAFT-DAv2-a2 leads on EPE (0.304 vs. DPFlow 0.340) and Fl (1.197 vs. DPFlow 1.311), while WAFT-DINOv3-a2 leads on 1px (3.182 vs. DPFlow 3.442) and WAUC (95.051 vs. DPFlow 94.980). On Sintel Final, Flowformer++ (1.94) and DPFlow (1.97) remain slightly ahead of WAFT-DINOv3-a2 (2.02), though the paper notes this is driven by the outlier sequence 'Ambush 1'; excluding it, WAFT-Twins-a2 (1.639) outperforms Flowformer++ (1.750). In zero-shot cross-dataset evaluation on KITTI(train), WAFT-Twins-a2 reduces Fl-epe from 3.37 (DPFlow) to 2.98 and Fl-all from 11.1 to 9.9, a 11% improvement over the previous best. Compared to older warping-based methods (SpyNet, FlowNet2), WAFT reduces Sintel EPE by at least 64% and Spring EPE by 70%, while also cutting KITTI Fl by 68%.

## Relation to Prior Work

WAFT is positioned as a direct simplification of the RAFT/SEA-RAFT iterative paradigm, retaining the recurrent update structure but replacing the cost volume (introduced by PWC-Net and made standard by RAFT) with a single high-resolution backward warp of the second frame's features. The recurrent update module adopts DPT's vision-transformer architecture, which the authors show is essential for implicitly handling the long-range displacements that cost volumes previously modeled explicitly. By removing both the cost volume and the context encoder, WAFT becomes a minimal 'meta-architecture' that can slot in off-the-shelf pre-trained backbones (Twins, DAv2, DINOv3) without task-specific custom designs, effectively reviving the warping-based approach that fell out of favor after SpyNet and FlowNet2 in 2017.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a six-year-old working assumption in optical flow that cost volumes are necessary for strong accuracy, showing that a transformer-based update module can subsume their role while enabling high-resolution indexing that cost volumes cannot afford. The practical impact is significant: WAFT is 1.3–4.1× faster than competitive methods, uses roughly half the training memory, and its meta-architecture design lowers the barrier to adopting new pre-trained backbones. Open questions include whether the same warping-plus-transformer principle transfers to other dense prediction tasks (stereo, depth), whether further iterations or larger transformers yield diminishing returns, and whether the 'Ambush 1' outlier on Sintel (Final) signals a residual weakness in handling extreme motion.
