## Problem

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) trained with RL produce excessively long chain-of-thought traces with substantial redundancy (e.g., thousands of tokens for trivial problems), a phenomenon called overthinking. Existing RL-based CoT compression methods—whether budget-constrained, adaptive-mode, or full-mode—struggle to simultaneously achieve >50% token reduction on hard problems and consistent accuracy gains, often requiring multi-stage pipelines or manual budget tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261471/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes competition-level math problem text as input and produces a compressed chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a final answer. During RL training, the context window is set significantly larger than the target length (e.g., 16,384 vs. 4,096 tokens) so that responses are rarely hard-truncated; instead, the length reward gently incentivizes brevity. Difficulty is assessed in real-time from the rollout batch (k rollouts per question, split at k/3 and 2k/3 thresholds), adding negligible computational overhead (~3.5%).

## Method

The paper first unifies truncation, group-based, and budget-based length rewards under a single formulation R̂(x,y) = C(y) + λ(y)·S(y), then proposes LASER, which uses a step-function length reward S(y) = α·I(L(y)≤L_T) activated only for correct responses (λ(y)=I(R)), avoiding the over-penalization of long-but-correct explorations inherent in hard truncation. LASER-D extends this with two key mechanisms: (1) a dynamic adapting module that periodically (every N steps) re-estimates target lengths per difficulty bucket using an Expected Correct Responses (ECR) metric on a small monitoring set, selecting the minimal length where at least one correct rollout is expected; and (2) difficulty-aware decoupling, where easy, medium, and hard questions receive distinct target lengths L_A, allowing the model to think briefly on simple queries while preserving longer reasoning for hard ones. The entire pipeline is single-stage GRPO training with no additional SFT, and the automatic adapting mechanism eliminates manual hyperparameter tuning.

## Results

On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, LASER-D (L_T=4096) achieves 60.3% average accuracy across MATH500, AIME2024, AMC2023, and OlympiadBench while using only 3,520 average tokens, compared to 56.9% and 10,177 tokens for the original model. On the hardest benchmark AIME2024, LASER-D reaches 34.2% accuracy (a +5.3 point gain) with 5,750 tokens (a 64% reduction from 15,956). On the 7B model, LASER-D achieves 75.4% average accuracy with 3,315 tokens (vs. 73.3% and 8,213 for the original), and on AIME2024 it reaches 58.3% (+5.2 points) with 5,379 tokens. The method generalizes to out-of-domain benchmarks (GPQA, LSAT, MMLU, LiveCodeBench) with consistent accuracy gains and ~50% token reductions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LASER-D outperforms all six baselines on the primary efficacy-efficiency trade-off. Against truncation (T_8192), LASER-D gains +5.0 points average accuracy (60.3% vs 55.3%) at only a modest token increase (3,520 vs 2,915), and on AIME2024 the gap is +9.4 points (34.2% vs 24.8%). Against group-based Efficient Reasoning (best α=0.1), LASER-D improves average accuracy by +5.2 points (60.3% vs 55.1%) with comparable token usage (3,520 vs 3,402), while avoiding the reward-hacking instability observed in group methods. Against budget-based L1-Max, LASER-D achieves +8.9 points average accuracy (60.3% vs 51.4%) with slightly more tokens (3,520 vs 1,245), but L1-Max's extreme brevity comes at a severe accuracy cost on hard problems. Against adaptive methods Thinkless and AutoThink, LASER-D matches or exceeds their accuracy (60.3% vs 56.1%/57.6%) while using roughly half the tokens (3,520 vs 7,344/5,581), since adaptive methods still consume ~10K tokens on AIME. Against LAPO (the closest full-mode competitor), LASER-D gains +1.2 points average accuracy (60.3% vs 59.1%) with 37% fewer tokens (3,520 vs 5,581), and the gap widens to +4.9 points on AIME2024 (34.2% vs 29.3%). LASER-D also surpasses its own non-dynamic variant LASER by +1.6 points average accuracy (60.3% vs 58.7%) at similar token counts, confirming the benefit of difficulty-aware adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the fragmented landscape of RL-based CoT compression methods (truncation, group-based, and budget-based rewards) under a single reward-shaping formulation (Eq. 1), then proposes LASER—a step-function length reward that grants a bonus only to correct responses under a target length—as a cleaner alternative to continuous penalties or hard truncation. LASER-D further extends this with a difficulty-aware, dynamically adapting target length mechanism that assigns different length budgets to easy/medium/hard queries based on in-batch correctness rates. The work builds directly on the GRPO training paradigm from DeepSeek-R1 and the overthinking problem identified by Chen et al. (2025), while departing from prior methods by rejecting both the reward-hacking-prone group comparisons and the training-instability of budget-based continuous penalties in favor of a simple binary indicator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LASER-D is the first full-mode method to simultaneously improve accuracy (+5.3 on AIME24) and reduce token usage (64%) without multi-stage pipelines, which is practically significant for deployment cost. The unified framework provides a useful taxonomy for the field, clarifying why prior approaches fail (reward hacking in group-based, instability in budget-based, over-penalization in truncation). Key limitations include the focus on mathematical reasoning as the primary testbed, modest gains on the 32B model where accuracy is already saturated, and the open question of whether the difficulty-aware mechanism generalizes to agentic or code-generation tasks where 'difficulty' is harder to estimate from in-batch rollouts.
