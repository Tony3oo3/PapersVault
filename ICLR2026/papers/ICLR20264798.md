## Problem

Multimodal reasoning requires iterative coordination between language and vision, yet it remains unclear what constitutes a meaningful interleaved chain of thought and when interleaving actually helps over unimodal approaches. Existing tool-augmented designs are brittle, and unified models like MVoT treat text and image thoughts as isomorphic representations rather than complementary modalities, limiting generalization beyond training domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264798/cropped_page3_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a multimodal question consisting of a text query and an RGB image, and produces an interleaved reasoning trace that alternates between text thoughts (wrapped in <think> tags) and generated image thoughts (wrapped in <image_start>/<image_end> tags), followed by a final answer in <answer> tags. Image thoughts are concrete visual manipulations—bounding boxes, path overlays, rearranged puzzle pieces, or chart highlights—generated autoregressively via a diffusion-based generation expert within the unified model, rather than retrieved from external tools.

## Method

ThinkMorph fine-tunes the Bagel-7B unified model on ~24K curated interleaved reasoning traces spanning four tasks (Jigsaw Assembly, Spatial Navigation, Visual Search, Chart Refocus) that demand varying levels of visual engagement. The core design principle is complementarity: each text step provides hypotheses or descriptions that the corresponding image step cannot express, and each image step provides spatial or visual evidence that text alone cannot capture. Training optimizes dual objectives—MSE loss for image tokens and negative log-likelihood for text tokens—while special delimiter tokens (<image_start>, <image_end>) enable the model to autonomously switch between modalities mid-reasoning. The data curation pipeline ensures high-quality supervision by using GPT-4.1 to generate step-by-step reasoning conditioned on ground-truth answers, then sanitizing the conversation history so the model learns to produce reasoning that reads as independently derived. This light fine-tuning (8K steps on 16×A100 GPUs) yields a 34.74% average improvement over the base model on vision-centric tasks and reveals three emergent properties: unseen visual manipulations, autonomous mode switching, and superior test-time scaling through diversified multimodal exploration.

## Results

ThinkMorph (7B) achieves an average 20.74% improvement over its base model Bagel-7B across nine benchmarks, with the largest gains on VSP (+75.00%), VisPuzzle (+44.00%), and BLINK (+12.41%). On out-of-domain benchmarks, ThinkMorph reaches 52.67% on SAT, 80.33% on MMVP, and 72.00% on BLINK-J, matching or surpassing models an order of magnitude larger. Averaged across three vision-centric tasks (VSP, VisPuzzle, VStar), interleaved reasoning yields a 34.74% improvement over the base model and outperforms the next-best unimodal mode by 5.33%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against InternVL3.5-38B, ThinkMorph (7B) surpasses it on SAT (52.67% vs. 49.33%) and matches it exactly on MMVP (80.33%), while trailing on VStar (67.02% vs. 76.96%) and BLINK-J (72.00% vs. 80.67%). Against Qwen2.5-VL-72B, ThinkMorph outperforms by 34.00 points on VSP (75.83% vs. 41.83%) and by 10.67 points on BLINK-J (72.00% vs. 61.33%), but falls short on VStar (67.02% vs. 85.86%) and MMVP (80.33% vs. 82.00%). Against Chameleon-7B, ThinkMorph dominates across every benchmark, with margins ranging from 28.8% (VStar: 67.02% vs. 28.27%) to 42.7% (BLINK: 60.07% vs. 16.52%), and Chameleon scores near-zero on VSP and BLINK-J. Overall, ThinkMorph's strongest advantage is on reasoning-intensive, vision-centric tasks (VSP, SAT, VisPuzzle) where its interleaved reasoning provides a decisive edge, while larger VLMs retain an advantage on perception-heavy benchmarks like VStar and CV-Bench.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two prior lines of multimodal interleaved CoT: tool-augmented designs (OpenAI's thinking-with-images, Visual Sketchpad) that rely on external visual modules, and unified-model attempts (MVoT, Zebra-CoT, MetaMorph) that either produce isomorphic text-image pairs or collapse into fixed outputs. The key conceptual departure is the explicit principle that text and image thoughts should be complementary rather than isomorphic, operationalized through a curated ~24K dataset where each modality contributes distinct, non-redundant information. Methodologically, it builds on Bagel as a unified autoregressive base and introduces delimiter tokens for autonomous modality switching, but the core contribution is the data design philosophy and the systematic controlled comparison of text-only, visual-only, and interleaved reasoning modes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic controlled study of when interleaved multimodal reasoning outperforms unimodal alternatives, showing it is essential for spatial/manipulation tasks but redundant for chart reading—guidance that is practically useful for practitioners deciding when to invest in interleaved pipelines. The emergent properties (unseen visual manipulations, autonomous mode switching with efficiency gains, and superior Best-of-N scaling) suggest that light interleaved fine-tuning can unlock capabilities not explicitly supervised, though these are demonstrated only on a single 7B base model across four training tasks. Open questions include whether the complementary-modality principle generalizes to more complex or open-ended visual reasoning, whether emergent behaviors persist at larger scale or with different architectures, and whether the ~3× computational overhead of interleaved inference is justified outside vision-centric domains.
