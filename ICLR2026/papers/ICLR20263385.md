## Problem

Prior symbolic world modeling work has been limited to simple, deterministic environments with abundant interaction data and human-provided guidance such as environment-specific rewards and goals. This paper asks how an agent can reverse-engineer the transition dynamics of a complex, stochastic, and hostile environment from a single unguided exploration episode, with no access to rewards, goals, or domain-specific prior knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263385/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single unguided episode of (state, action, next_state) transitions from Crafter-OO, where each state is a structured, hierarchical object-oriented representation (a Pydantic data model with 100+ key-value pairs covering player inventory, entity positions, terrain tiles, and global properties) and actions are drawn from a discrete set of 19 movement, interaction, placement, and crafting commands. The output is a set of Python law classes (each with a precondition and effect method) plus a learned weight vector, which together define a probability distribution over next-state observables; this distribution supports both discriminative scoring of candidate next states (state ranking) and generative sampling of predicted next states (state fidelity and planning rollouts).

## Method

ONE LIFE represents the transition function as a mixture of modular, programmatic laws, each governing a narrow slice of the state via a precondition-effect structure. An LLM-driven exploration policy (scaffolded on Balrog) collects a single episode of unguided interaction, and a single LLM-based law synthesizer iterates over every transition, identifies changed object attributes, and proposes atomic Python law classes that explain each change. The key architectural insight is a dynamic computation graph: for each transition, only laws whose preconditions are satisfied and whose effects touch the observed variables participate in the loss, so gradients are routed exclusively to relevant laws, enabling precise credit assignment over a large, hierarchical state space. The predictive distribution for each observable is a weighted product-of-experts over active laws, normalized via log-softmax, and the law weights are optimized by L-BFGS maximum-likelihood estimation. This contrasts with PoE-World's static graph where every expert predicts the full next state, causing irrelevant experts to inject noise into the posterior.

## Results

On Crafter-OO, ONE LIFE achieves 18.7% Rank@1 and 0.479 MRR for state ranking, outperforming the PoE-World inference baseline (10.8% Rank@1, 0.351 MRR) by +7.9 percentage points and +0.128 MRR respectively. For state fidelity, ONE LIFE achieves a normalized edit distance of 0.058, comparable to the No-Inference ablation (0.057) and substantially better than PoE-World (0.071) and WorldCoder (0.181). In planning via forward simulation, ONE LIFE correctly identifies the superior plan in all three multi-step scenarios (Zombie Fighter, Stone Miner, Sword Maker), matching the ground-truth environment's plan ranking.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ONE LIFE is compared against two prior symbolic world modeling baselines: PoE-World (Piriyakulkij et al., 2025) and WorldCoder (Tang et al., 2024). Against PoE-World, ONE LIFE wins decisively on state ranking metrics (+7.9% Rank@1, +0.128 MRR) while achieving comparable state fidelity (normalized edit distance 0.058 vs. 0.071). Against WorldCoder, ONE LIFE dominates on all four metrics, with WorldCoder achieving 0.0% Rank@1 and the worst MRR (0.264) of all methods. The paper also reports that ONE LIFE outperforms PoE-World on 16 out of 23 individual evaluation scenarios, indicating broad rather than narrow superiority. In the planning evaluation (Table 2), ONE LIFE's world model correctly ranks plans identically to the ground-truth environment in all three scenarios, whereas no baseline planning results are reported for comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the symbolic world modeling line initiated by WorldCoder (Tang et al., 2024) and PoE-World (Piriyakulkij et al., 2025), which represent transition dynamics as executable code. The key architectural departure from PoE-World is the factorization of the transition function into atomic precondition-effect laws that each predict only a minimal subset of state attributes, enabling a dynamic computation graph where gradients route exclusively to laws relevant to the observed transition, rather than updating a static superposition of full-state experts. The paper also introduces Crafter-OO, a reimplementation of the Crafter environment with an exposed object-oriented state and pure transition function, filling a gap identified in prior work where symbolic world modeling was limited to simple grid-worlds or Atari with externally engineered state access.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that symbolic world models can be learned from a single unguided episode in a complex, stochastic environment, moving the field beyond the deterministic, data-rich, human-guided settings of prior work. The Crafter-OO benchmark and its 30+ scenario evaluation suite with mutator-based distractors provide a reusable testbed for future symbolic world modeling research. However, absolute performance remains modest (18.7% Rank@1), the approach relies heavily on LLM-based law synthesis (costly and potentially brittle), and evaluation is confined to a single environment, leaving open whether the precondition-effect architecture generalizes to continuous state spaces or substantially more complex domains.
