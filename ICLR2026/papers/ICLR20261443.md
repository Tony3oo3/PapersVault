## Problem

Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) are the dominant unsupervised method for extracting interpretable concepts from neural network activations, but they suffer from training instability, can still produce polysemantic features, and rely on sparsity as a proxy for interpretability—a criterion that has been formally criticized as inadequate. This paper asks whether a simpler, more principled alternative exists that directly targets the semantic distinctions between samples rather than reconstructing activation variance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261443/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes D-dimensional activations from a single encoder layer over N samples (e.g., 768-dim vectors from the last transformer block of CLIP, DeBERTa, BART, DinoV2, or AST). It outputs k concept vectors (default k=6144, i.e., 8× the activation dimension) that live directly in the original activation space, making them immediately usable for steering without any encoder/decoder projection. The sole hyperparameter is k, the number of concepts, which is itself interpretable as the desired dictionary size.

## Method

The approach samples N random pairs of data points and computes their pairwise activation differences, then applies a feature-weighted KMeans clustering to these difference vectors to identify recurring directional patterns. The weighting is inversely proportional to the skewness of each difference direction's projection onto the dataset, penalizing highly skewed coordinates that would otherwise dominate Euclidean distance and produce redundant clusters; directions with negative skewness are flipped to treat opposite orientations as equivalent. Formally, under an isotropic covariance assumption, each pairwise difference x_i − x_j is the optimal discriminant direction separating the two samples, grounding the method as an unsupervised analogue of Linear Discriminant Analysis. Because the resulting concept vectors reside in activation space rather than a learned latent code, steering is lossless: adding α·c_i to an activation and then subtracting it exactly recovers the original, with no reconstruction error from projection.

## Results

The Deleuzian method achieves the lowest probe loss on 13 of 20 evaluated tasks across five models and three modalities, with an average rank of 1.65±0.85 among all unsupervised methods (vs. 2.65 for the next-best, TopKSAE). On CLIP WikiArt (Artist), it reaches 0.0119 probe loss, outperforming the best SAE variant (TopKSAE at 0.0125) and approaching supervised LDA (0.0084). On DinoV2 ImageNet, it achieves 0.0055, nearly matching LDA (0.0044) and halving the gap to the best SAE (TopKSAE at 0.0096). On AST AudioSet, it ties the supervised LDA baseline at 0.0164, while on BART CoNLL-2003 NER it reaches 0.2148, substantially below the next-best unsupervised method (TopKSAE at 0.3478).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Deleuzian method outperforms all six SAE variants (Vanilla, Gated, JumpReLU, Matryoshka, TopK, Archetypal) on the majority of tasks, with the closest competitor being TopKSAE, which it beats on 13/20 tasks (e.g., 0.0119 vs. 0.0125 on CLIP WikiArt Artist; 0.0665 vs. 0.0839 on DeBERTa CoNLL NER). ICA is consistently the weakest unsupervised baseline, particularly on text (e.g., 1.4578 on BART CoNLL POS vs. 0.2148 for Deleuzian). The pretrained SAE baselines (ViT-Prisma on DinoV2, EleutherAI on Pythia) are substantially outperformed (e.g., 0.0055 vs. 0.0333 on DinoV2 ImageNet). The supervised LDA upper bound is approached but not matched on most tasks; however, on AST AudioSet the Deleuzian method ties LDA exactly (0.0164), and on BART CoNLL-2003, LDA itself degrades (0.3473) due to violated homoscedasticity assumptions, while Deleuzian achieves 0.2148. In terms of cross-run consistency (MPPC), the Deleuzian method achieves the highest or near-highest values (0.821–1.0) across all models, indicating stable concept extraction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a direct alternative to the sparse autoencoder (SAE) family for unsupervised concept extraction, departing from the reconstruction-and-sparsity paradigm that underpins VanillaSAE, GatedSAE, JumpReLUSAE, MatryoshkaSAE, TopKSAE, and ArchetypalSAE. Methodologically, it builds on Fisher's linear discriminant analysis (1936) to justify pairwise activation differences as optimal separation directions, and on Feature-Weighted KMeans (Huang et al., 2005) for the skewness-weighted clustering step. Philosophically, it reframes concepts following Deleuze's 'Difference and Repetition' as recurring distinctions rather than universal essences, contrasting with the Platonic/Hegelian view implicitly encoded in SAEs' variance-capturing objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in offering a single-hyperparameter, linear-time method that consistently outperforms all tested SAE variants on probe loss across five models and three modalities, while enabling lossless steering (exact reversibility) that SAE-based approaches cannot match due to their projection-based architecture. Key limitations include the dependence on labeled datasets for evaluation (undermining the 'fully unsupervised' claim in practice), the assumption of linear concept representations, and the fixed 6144-dimensional concept space. Open questions include automatic naming/interpretation of extracted concepts, whether the approach extends to non-linear or non-encoder architectures, and whether higher-dimensional concept spaces reveal additional structure.
