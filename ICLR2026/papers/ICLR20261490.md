## Problem

Joint vision-language encoders like BLIP and BLIP-2 achieve strong cross-modal retrieval performance but are impractical for large-scale re-ranking because visual feature extraction via heavy ViT backbones consumes 83–93% of inference time, making per-candidate scoring prohibitively slow. Unlike text retrieval, where lightweight cross-encoder re-rankers are standard in production pipelines, no comparable efficient vision-language re-ranker exists, leaving the discriminative potential of joint encoders largely untapped at scale.

## Input / Output

At query time, EDJE consumes a text query (up to 64 tokens, processed by MiniLM-L12) and a small set of precomputed, compressed vision tokens (e.g., 64 tokens in BF16, ~49 kB per image) that were extracted offline by a frozen ViT encoder and projected through the token-compression adapter. The joint encoder outputs a single scalar re-ranking score per image-text pair, used to reorder a candidate pool (default k=10) retrieved by an upstream embedding model. Vision tokens can be further quantized to FP8 or FP4 on disk with negligible retrieval degradation, reducing per-image storage below 25 kB.

## Method

EDJE shifts the expensive ViT feature extraction entirely offline: images are encoded once, and the resulting token sequence is compressed by a lightweight adapter that uses m learnable query tokens to attend over the n vision tokens via multi-head cross-attention, then projects the m compressed tokens into the language model's embedding space. Online, a compact encoder-only language model (MiniLM-L12, ~33M parameters) processes the compressed vision tokens concatenated with text tokens using standard self-attention, producing a [CLS]-based matching score. Training jointly optimizes three heads—image-text matching with in-batch hard-negative mining, 50%-masked language modeling, and text-embedding recovery via cosine loss—followed by logit-level distillation from the uncompressed local-adapter variant to the token-compressed student. This design decouples vision encoder choice from the joint encoder, enabling any ViT backbone to be paired with any efficient LM through the adapter, and achieves 50k pairs/second throughput with 49 kB per-image storage while matching prior joint encoders on Flickr30k and COCO retrieval.

## Results

With SigLIP2 ViT-L/16@384, EDJE (local variant) achieves 87.8 R@1 text-to-image and 96.5 R@1 image-to-text on Flickr30k zero-shot, and 64.9/81.0 on COCO fine-tuned, using only 33M joint-encoder parameters and 4.14 ms per batch of 64. The token-compressed variant (64 tokens) retains 86.9/96.4 on Flickr30k while reducing per-image storage to 49 kB and inference time to 1.91 ms. EDJE processes 50k image-text pairs per second, compared to 45–101 ms per batch for prior joint encoders (ALBEF, BLIP, BLIP-2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against embedding-based models (CLIP, DFN, MetaCLIP, SigLIP2), EDJE consistently improves zero-shot Flickr30k retrieval: e.g., SigLIP2 ViT-L/16@384 goes from 82.3→87.8 T2I R@1 and 94.8→96.5 I2T R@1, while CLIP ViT-B/16@224 gains up to 15 points in I2T R@1. Against prior joint encoders, EDJE (Local ViT-L/16) slightly trails BLIP-2 on Flickr30k (87.8 vs 88.6 T2I) and COCO (64.9 vs 66.3 T2I), but uses 5× fewer parameters (33M vs 167M), 5.3× less storage (442 kB vs 2,359 kB), and runs 24× faster (4.14 ms vs 98.64 ms). The 64-token compressed variant narrows the accuracy gap to under 1 point while cutting storage to 49 kB (48× less than BLIP-2) and inference to 1.91 ms. On the more challenging full-dataset retrieval protocol (LightningDOT setup), EDJE substantially outperforms LightningDOT: on Flickr Full, T2I R@5 improves from 60.1 to 78.32 and I2T R@5 from 75.1 to 92.4; on COCO Full, T2I R@5 improves from 37.3 to 52.23 and I2T R@5 from 48.0 to 69.86.

## Relation to Prior Work

EDJE repurposes the VLM architecture paradigm (projecting vision tokens into a language embedding space and processing them jointly via self-attention, as in LLaVA) but replaces the large LLM with a compact MiniLM, yielding a lightweight joint encoder suited for discriminative re-ranking. The token-compression adapter draws conceptual inspiration from the Q-Former in BLIP-2 and attention pooling in SigLIP, while the offline precomputation of vision tokens follows the late-interaction storage paradigm of ColBERT/ColPali. The key departure from prior joint encoders (BLIP, BLIP-2, ALBEF) is the explicit separation of the expensive vision backbone into an offline stage, eliminating the 83–93% inference-time bottleneck that made these models impractical for re-ranking at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EDJE closes a notable gap in the retrieval literature: joint-encoder re-ranking, standard in text retrieval, has been absent from vision-language pipelines due to prohibitive vision-encoding costs. By achieving 50k pairs/second throughput at 49 kB per image while matching prior joint encoders on Flickr and COCO, the paper makes large-scale multimodal re-ranking practically deployable. Limitations include training on only 12M pairs, no multilingual or video evaluation, and reliance on a fixed MiniLM backbone; the authors note that scaling to larger datasets, multilingual settings, and video remain open. The interpretability analysis revealing that most native ViT tokens are semantically redundant for retrieval (mapping to unused special tokens) raises broader questions about whether current vision encoders produce more tokens than downstream tasks actually need.
