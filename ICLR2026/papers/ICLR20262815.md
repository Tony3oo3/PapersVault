## Problem

Prior work on Function Vectors (FVs) treated them as compact, format-invariant encodings of ICL task concepts that transfer across contexts. This paper asks whether LLMs actually represent relational concepts abstractly, independent of surface form, and whether the components that encode such abstract structure are the same ones that causally drive ICL performance. The gap is a missing distinction between causal efficacy and representational invariance in the ICL literature.

## Input / Output

The method consumes few-shot ICL prompts (5-shot open-ended, 3-shot multiple-choice) spanning 7 relational concepts × 3 input formats (open-ended English, open-ended French, multiple-choice English), yielding 1050 prompts total, and extracts last-token attention head activations from four autoregressive transformer models (Llama 3.1 8B/70B, Qwen 2.5 7B/72B). Outputs include per-head AIE and Concept-RSA scores, summed Function/Concept Vectors per prompt, and steering effects (ΔP) measured on an AmbiguousICL task that interleaves two competing concepts (e.g., antonym + EN→FR translation) to test whether injected vectors can override the model's default continuation.

## Method

The paper contrasts two complementary attention-head selection procedures. Activation Patching (AP) identifies FV heads by computing the Causal Indirect Effect: head activations from clean runs are transplanted into corrupted prompts (where systematic relations are disrupted), and the resulting change in target-token probability ranks heads by causal contribution. Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA) identifies CV heads by computing a representational similarity matrix of each head's outputs across all prompts, then correlating it (Spearman's ρ) with a binary design matrix that marks prompt pairs sharing the same concept regardless of input format. FVs and CVs are formed by summing the top-K activations selected by each procedure. The key finding is that FV and CV heads are largely disjoint (near-zero top-K overlap for K≤20) despite occupying similar layers, and that FVs are nearly orthogonal across formats while CVs cluster by concept across formats. Steering experiments inject these vectors into the residual stream at chosen layers, revealing that FVs produce larger in-distribution gains but degrade out-of-distribution (carrying format/language artifacts), whereas CVs generalize more consistently across question types and languages at the cost of smaller absolute effects.

## Results

Across four LLMs (Llama 3.1 8B/70B, Qwen 2.5 7B/72B), same-concept FVs extracted from different input formats are nearly orthogonal (within-format FV cluster mean cosine similarity = 0.90), while CVs cluster by concept across formats (mean cosine similarity = 0.55). FV and CV heads are largely disjoint: for K ≤ 20, the top-K head overlap is zero across all four models (Table 1). In steering experiments on AmbiguousICL, FVs extracted in-distribution (OE-ENG) yield the largest ΔP gains, but degrade out-of-distribution (e.g., MC FVs produce format tokens like '(' at +53% instead of concept tokens), whereas CVs maintain concept-aligned outputs (e.g., 'sweet' at +35%) across all formats with lower KL divergence between ID and OOD steering distributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between Concept Vectors (CVs, the new method) and Function Vectors (FVs, from Todd et al. 2024). FVs outperform CVs in-distribution: when both extraction and application use the same format (OE-ENG), FVs produce larger ΔP gains on AmbiguousICL prompts. However, CVs substantially outperform FVs out-of-distribution: FVs extracted from French prompts push the model toward French antonyms (e.g., '_su' +31%, '_dou' +27%) rather than English answers, and FVs from multiple-choice prompts produce format tokens ('(' at +53%) instead of concept tokens, while CVs consistently produce English concept-aligned tokens ('sweet', 'fresh', 'bland') across all three formats. KL divergence analysis confirms CVs yield more consistent ID-vs-OOD steering distributions than FVs, with the gap larger for MC than for OE-FR. Structurally, the two vector types are composed of nearly disjoint attention heads (zero overlap for K ≤ 20 across all models), indicating they are mediated by different circuits operating in similar layers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits Function Vectors (FVs) from Todd et al. (2024) and Hendel et al. (2023), which were widely treated as compact, format-invariant encodings of ICL tasks. The key departure is showing that FVs are actually format-entangled (nearly orthogonal across open-ended vs. multiple-choice prompts for the same concept), and introducing Concept Vectors (CVs) selected via Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA, Kriegeskorte 2008) repurposed from neuroscience to identify attention heads encoding concepts invariantly. The paper also positions itself within the broader literature on linear representations of relational concepts (Hernandez et al., 2024), symbolic-like reasoning in LLMs (Yang et al., 2025), and attention head categorization (Olsson et al., 2022; Yin & Steinhardt, 2025), arguing that CV heads exhibit both invariance and indirection properties characteristic of symbolic processing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dissociation between causal drivers (FVs) and abstract concept representations (CVs) challenges the single-circuit hypothesis for ICL and has direct practical implications: FVs are preferable for in-distribution steering while CVs generalize more robustly across languages and question types. Open questions include whether CVs and FVs interact during inference as detection/execution mechanisms or operate as independent backup circuits, how these head sets emerge during training, and whether the global (all-concepts) RSA selection used here misses concept-specific invariant heads. The finding that FVs carry generic foreign-language or format tokens (e.g., MC brackets) rather than language-specific content suggests a deeper entanglement of task and surface form in causal circuits that may affect how ICL is modeled theoretically.
