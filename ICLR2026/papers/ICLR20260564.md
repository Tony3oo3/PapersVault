## Problem

Existing generative watermarking for tabular diffusion models (e.g., TabWak) relies on DDIM invertibility to recover embedded noise patterns during detection, but tabular pipelines include non-injective steps (quantile normalization) and VAE decoders whose inversion accumulates error, severely degrading both watermark detectability and data fidelity. This makes inversion-based approaches fragile, model-specific, and impractical for the broader class of tabular generative models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained tabular generative model (any architecture supporting i.i.d. repeated sampling), a secret watermark key k, a target table size N, and a target false positive rate α; the provider also shares per-column min/max metadata with the detector. Generation produces a watermarked table of N rows, each selected from m candidates (m calibrated by Equation 6 from N and α). Detection takes a candidate table and the key, computes the mean per-row hash score, and returns a binary watermarked/not-watermarked decision via a threshold derived from the null distribution.

## Method

MUSE embeds watermarks by multi-sample selection: for each row, it draws m i.i.d. candidates from the generative model, scores each with a keyed pseudorandom function (PRF) over selected column values, and keeps the highest-scoring candidate. Two scoring designs navigate the distortion–robustness trade-off: Joint-Vector (JV) hashing concatenates a sparse, quantile-rank-selected subset of columns into one hash (minimizing distortion but fragile to single-cell edits), while Per-Column (PC) hashing independently hashes every column and averages the Bernoulli(0.5) bits (maximizing robustness at the cost of higher collision-induced bias). A theoretical calibration (Theorem 4.1) shows Bernoulli(0.5) scoring is optimal and gives a closed-form m for any target FPR, while a repeated-column-masking mechanism (Theorem 4.3) guarantees multi-sample distribution-preservation. Because the entire pipeline avoids inverting the generative or preprocessing steps, MUSE is model-agnostic and applies identically to diffusion, autoregressive, and masked generative tabular models.

## Results

On four mixed-type tabular datasets (Adult, Default, Shoppers, Beijing), MUSE-JV achieves near-untouched fidelity (e.g., Adult: Marg. 0.979, Corr. 0.963, C2ST 0.883, MLE Gap 0.017) while attaining perfect detection (AUC = 1.000, TPR@0.1%FPR = 1.000) at both 100 and 500 rows. MUSE reduces distortion rates by 84–88% on fidelity metrics (Marg., Corr., C2ST) relative to the best-performing baseline (TabWak*). The PC variant trades ~5–10% fidelity for stronger robustness against post-processing attacks, while still achieving perfect detection scores across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MUSE dominates all five baselines on the joint objective of fidelity + detectability. Against the closest generative competitor TabWak/TabWak*, MUSE-JV improves C2ST by 50–96% (e.g., 0.883 vs. 0.713 on Adult; 0.963 vs. 0.550 on Default) while matching their perfect AUC = 1.000 detection; TabWak* also requires bypassing quantile-normalization inversion with ground-truth data unavailable at deployment. Gaussian Shading (GS) achieves the same perfect detection but at catastrophic fidelity cost (C2ST ≈ 0.06–0.10, MLE Gap up to 0.715 on Beijing), confirming that strong inversion-based signals inherently distort tabular data. TreeRing (TR) is the weakest baseline, with AUC dropping to 0.55–0.86 and TPR@0.1%FPR as low as 0.007, indicating near-random detection. In the edit-based comparison (Table 4), TabularMark and WGTD match MUSE-JV on detection but show significant MLE Gap degradation on Beijing (0.528/0.527 vs. MUSE-JV's 0.209), suggesting post-hoc value edits harm downstream utility. MUSE-PC sacrifices ~5–10% fidelity relative to MUSE-JV but provides the strongest robustness across all six post-processing attacks (row/column/cell deletion, value alteration, shuffling, Gaussian perturbation), giving practitioners a tunable point on the fidelity–robustness spectrum.

## Relation to Prior Work

MUSE departs fundamentally from the inversion-based generative watermarking paradigm (used in image/video diffusion and adapted to tabular data by TabWak), which embeds signals in noise vectors and recovers them by inverting the entire generation pipeline. Instead, it repurposes the multi-sample selection idea from LLM watermarking (Bahri & Wieting, SynthID, WaterMax) to the tabular setting, where computational efficiency makes generating multiple candidates per row practical. The paper also draws on repeated key masking from LLM watermarking to achieve distribution preservation, and positions itself against edit-based tabular watermarking (TabularMark, WGTD) that directly modifies cell values. Conceptually, MUSE carves new ground by showing that tabular data's low generation cost enables a selection-based watermarking paradigm that is entirely model-agnostic, unlike prior approaches tied to specific generative architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As synthetic tabular data proliferates in high-stakes domains (finance, healthcare, fraud detection), reliable provenance and ownership verification become critical, and MUSE provides a practical, generalizable safeguard that avoids the error-accumulation pitfalls of inversion-based methods. The model-agnostic design (validated across diffusion, autoregressive, and masked generative models) means the paradigm can be adopted as new tabular generators emerge without re-derivation. Open questions include the security of the quantile-set selection in JV hashing under key leakage, whether the approach extends to other structured data types (graphs, time series), and how watermark strength should be calibrated in multi-party or federated settings where the detector may not have full metadata access.
