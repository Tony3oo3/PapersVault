## Problem

Hardware-accelerated microscaling FP4 formats (MXFP4 and NVFP4) on NVIDIA Blackwell and AMD GPUs promise to revolutionize LLM inference, yet their practical accuracy and performance remain unproven. The paper identifies two fundamental issues: NVFP4's small group size (16) provably neutralizes traditional outlier-mitigation techniques, and MXFP4's power-of-two scale quantization (E8M0) severely degrades accuracy due to high induced error. No prior work had systematically studied these formats' quantization error characteristics or adapted state-of-the-art PTQ methods to them.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weights in FP16/BF16 precision along with a small set of calibration sequences (1024 from FineWeb for GPTQ/MR-GPTQ; 92,995 from Tulu 3 for QAT experiments). It produces quantized model weights in MXFP4 (4.25 bits/element, group size 32, E8M0 scales) or NVFP4 (4.5 bits/element, group size 16, E4M3 scales) format, with block-wise Hadamard rotations pre-fused into the weight matrices. At inference, activations are rotated online via lightweight fused kernels (QuTLASS) before quantization, enabling the full W4A4 pipeline with negligible overhead on Blackwell GPUs (SM100/SM120).

## Method

MR-GPTQ adapts the GPTQ second-order quantization algorithm to FP4 microscaling formats through three key ingredients: (1) block-wise Hadamard rotations matching the quantization group size (k=16 for NVFP4, k=32 for MXFP4) to normalize weight and activation distributions from Laplace-like to Gaussian, which provably reduces MSE for MXFP4; (2) MSE-optimized scale search via alternating optimization over per-group and per-tensor scales, plus a static activation reordering heuristic applied after scale computation and before GPTQ (then shuffled back) to avoid the 10–20% runtime penalty of dynamic act-order; and (3) format-specific optimizations such as a single static per-tensor scale for MXFP4 with rotations. The QuTLASS kernel suite fuses the weight-side rotation offline and implements a lightweight online activation rotation kernel that achieves near-ideal throughput, yielding up to 3.6× layer-wise and 2.2× end-to-end speedups over FP16 on B200, and 6×/4× on RTX5090.

## Results

On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct W4A4 quantization, MR-GPTQ achieves 75.84% average accuracy (96.08% recovery) for NVFP4 and 73.65% (93.31% recovery) for MXFP4, compared to the FP16 baseline of 78.93%. For large models (Llama-3.3-70B, Qwen3-32B), both NVFP4 and MXFP4 with MR-GPTQ recover 98–99% of FP16 accuracy. QuTLASS kernels deliver up to 3.6× layer-wise speedup on B200 (6× on RTX5090) and 2.2× end-to-end speedup on B200 (4× on RTX5090) over BF16, with the MXFP4 kernel achieving ~15% higher throughput than NVFP4 on B200.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MR-GPTQ outperforms all five baselines on both NVFP4 and MXFP4. On MXFP4, the gains are most dramatic: MR-GPTQ (73.65%) beats GPTQ (70.62%) by +3.03 points, SmoothQuant (70.30%) by +3.35, SpinQuant (69.40%) by +4.25, and QuaRot (62.90%) by +10.75 points. On NVFP4, MR-GPTQ (75.84%) edges out GPTQ (75.72%) by +0.12 and SmoothQuant (75.70%) by +0.14, while beating QuaRot (74.10%) by +1.74 and SpinQuant (73.70%) by +2.14. Notably, QuaRot and SpinQuant—rotation-based methods designed for INT4—actually degrade MXFP4 accuracy relative to plain RTN (62.90% and 69.40% vs. 69.32%), confirming the paper's analysis that standard rotations are poorly suited to FP4 microscaling formats. AWQ is only evaluated in the weight-only (W4A16) setting where it achieves 98.06% recovery on NVFP4, comparable to GPTQ (97.96%) and RTN (98.02%). The key finding is that MR-GPTQ narrows the MXFP4-to-NVFP4 gap from ~6.4 points (RTN) to ~2.2 points, making the lower-bit MXFP4 format practically competitive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GPTQ second-order quantization framework (Frantar et al., 2022) to the newly introduced microscaling FP4 formats (MXFP4, NVFP4), introducing block-wise Hadamard rotations, MSE-optimized scale search, and static activation reordering as format-specific adaptations. It deliberately departs from the rotation-based quantization literature (QuaRot, SpinQuant, QuIP#) by proving analytically and showing empirically that Hadamard transforms, which normalize distributions for INT4, actually degrade NVFP4 accuracy due to its smaller group size and finer E4M3 scale precision. The paper also positions itself against the broader PTQ landscape (SmoothQuant, AWQ, SpQR) by demonstrating that these methods were designed for uniform integer grids and large group sizes, making them poorly suited to the non-uniform FP4 grid and 16/32-element groups.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As NVIDIA Blackwell and AMD CDNA4 GPUs ship with native FP4 tensor cores, this paper provides the first rigorous evidence that FP4 is not an automatic accuracy upgrade over INT4 and that off-the-shelf quantization methods underperform. The practical impact is substantial: MR-GPTQ with QuTLASS kernels achieves 2.2×–4× end-to-end speedups on B200/RTX5090 while recovering 93–99% of FP16 accuracy, giving practitioners a concrete path to leveraging FP4 hardware. Open questions include whether the MXFP4 scale-fitting trick (MXFP4†) can be made hardware-compatible without breaking the power-of-two multiplication assumption, whether findings generalize to MoE or multimodal architectures, and whether the small-batch inference regime (where activation quantization provides no speedup) limits the practical benefit of W4A4 schemes.
