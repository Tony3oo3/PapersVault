## Problem

Previous comparisons between encoder-only and decoder-only language models were confounded by differences in parameter counts, architectures, pre-training data, and training recipes, making it impossible to isolate the effect of the training objective itself. Additionally, it remained unclear whether cross-objective continued pre-training (e.g., adapting a decoder to encoder tasks via MLM/MNTP) could close the performance gap, especially at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264860/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Both model families take raw text sequences (up to 7999 tokens after context extension) as input. Encoders produce bidirectional text representations used for classification, retrieval (nDCG), and embedding tasks, and can generate text by placing three mask tokens at the end of the sequence and iteratively predicting the first mask token. Decoders produce autoregressive text for generative tasks and are also evaluated on encoder-style tasks (MNLI classification, MS MARCO retrieval) to enable direct comparison. All models share a 50,368-token ModernBERT tokenizer.

## Method

The ETTIN suite trains 10 models (5 size pairs from 17M to 1B parameters) where each pair shares identical architecture, data mixture, and hyperparameters, differing only in attention pattern (bidirectional vs. causal) and objective (MLM vs. CLM). Architecture follows a deep-but-thin design inspired by MobileLLM, with RoPE positional embeddings (160k base), GLU MLPs, sliding-window attention (128) with global attention every 3 layers, and pre-norm transformer blocks. Training proceeds in three phases—1.7T tokens of pre-training, 250B of mid-training with context extension to 8000 tokens, and 50B of decay—using a trapezoidal learning rate schedule and a data mixture drawn from DCLM, Dolma, Dolmino, Starcoder, and other curated sources. Cross-objective training continues each final model for 50B tokens on the reverse objective (MNTP for decoders, CLM for encoders) on the highest-quality decay-phase data, enabling direct comparison of whether adaptation can match native-architecture performance.

## Results

ETTIN encoders achieve SOTA open-data performance across all sizes, with Ettin-Enc-150m scoring 88.9 GLUE average (vs. ModernBERT-base at 88.4) and Ettin-Enc-400m scoring 90.8 (vs. ModernBERT-large at 90.4), while also leading on retrieval (48.4 nDCG@10 on MS MARCO for 400M vs. 47.0 for ModernBERT-large). ETTIN decoders similarly set new open-data records: Ettin-Dec-1B achieves 59.0 average across 10 generative benchmarks, outperforming Llama 3.2 1B (56.6) and OLMo-1B (55.1), and Ettin-Dec-150m (46.2) beats SmolLM2-135m (45.2). Critically, cross-objective continued pre-training (50B tokens) fails to close the architectural gap: a 400M encoder scores 91.3 on MNLI versus 89.9 for the 1B decoder, while the 1B decoder scores 59.0 on generative tasks versus 50.7 for the 1B encoder.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against encoder baselines, ETTIN dominates: Ettin-Enc-68m (87.2 GLUE avg) beats DistilRoBERTa (83.8) by 3.4 points, and Ettin-Enc-150m (88.9) edges out ModernBERT-base (88.4) while also leading on retrieval (45.7 vs. 43.9 nDCG@10). At the large scale, Ettin-Enc-400m (90.8) surpasses ModernBERT-large (90.4) and BERT-large (85.2), and Ettin-Enc-1B (91.6) outperforms DeBERTa-v2-XL (90.7) on GLUE while matching it on MTEB (56.0 vs. 56.4). On the decoder side, Ettin-Dec-1B (59.0 avg) beats Llama 3.2 1B (56.6) by 2.4 points and OLMo-1B (55.1) by 3.9 points, with particularly large margins on TriviaQA (29.3 vs. 24.9) and SciQ (93.8 vs. 88.4). At smaller scales, Ettin-Dec-150m (46.2) outperforms SmolLM2-135m (45.2) and Pythia-160m (39.1), while Ettin-Dec-68m (41.8) beats DistilGPT (36.5) by 5.3 points. The key scientific finding is that cross-objective training does not resolve the architectural gap: even after 50B tokens of continued pre-training, the 400M encoder-from-decoder scores only 87.6 on MNLI (vs. 91.3 for the pure 400M encoder), and the 1B decoder-from-encoder scores 52.2 on generative tasks (vs. 59.0 for the pure 1B decoder), confirming that the preferred architecture cannot be substituted by continued training on the reverse objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Pythia suite concept (open-data models at multiple sizes for analysis) to the encoder domain, while using ModernBERT's architecture and training recipe as the shared foundation for both encoder and decoder variants. It departs from prior encoder-vs-decoder comparisons (Charpentier & Samuel 2024, Ethayarajh 2019) that were forced to use incomparable models with different architectures, data, and schedules, by training paired models that differ only in attention pattern and objective. For cross-objective training, it follows LLM2Vec's MNTP formulation but scales it to 50B tokens (5× more than prior work), and for architecture design it follows MobileLLM's deep-but-thin principle for smaller models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 50B tokens of cross-objective continued training still cannot close the gap between purpose-built encoders and decoders (e.g., a 400M encoder beats a 1B decoder on MNLI) has direct practical implications: for classification and retrieval at ≤1B scale, training a dedicated encoder remains superior to adapting a larger decoder. However, the paper acknowledges that at 7B+ scale where large encoder models don't yet exist, decoder-adapted encoders will likely remain the best available option. Open questions include whether the gap persists at larger scales, whether the 1B model's under-training (1/3 of intended tokens due to compute limits) affects conclusions, and what other learning differences (beyond gender bias) the paired suite reveals.
