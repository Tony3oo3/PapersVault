## Problem

Graph anomaly detection under label scarcity is hindered by two intertwined challenges: existing pre-training methods extract only task-agnostic semantics and fail to capture anomaly-relevant cues, while GAD-specific methods apply uniform spectral or edge-reweighting schemes that cannot accommodate the dual-granularity local homophily disparity (high variation across individual nodes and systematically lower homophily for abnormal nodes). This paper asks how to design a GAD-specific pre-training and fine-tuning framework that explicitly addresses both the label-free anomaly signal and node-adaptive structural heterogeneity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an attributed graph (adjacency matrix A and node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d}) plus a small labeled set of exactly 100 nodes (20 anomalies, 80 normal) following the GADBench semi-supervised protocol. Pre-training is entirely label-free, relying on Rayleigh Quotient-guided 2-hop subgraphs extracted per node; fine-tuning uses only the 100 labeled nodes. The output is a binary anomaly score per node via a two-layer MLP on fused representations, where the fusion coefficients C ∈ [0,1]^{n×e} are themselves a structured output encoding node- and dimension-wise reliance on semantic vs. anomaly-sensitive features.

## Method

APF operates in two stages. In pre-training, it builds on DGI but replaces the single low-pass branch with dual learnable K-order Chebyshev polynomial filters (g_L for low-pass semantics, g_H for high-pass anomaly cues) and modifies the contrastive objective so that each node's high-pass representation is matched against a summary computed over its Rayleigh Quotient-maximizing 2-hop subgraph (selected via MRQSampler), injecting label-free anomaly awareness. In fine-tuning, a lightweight Gated Fusion Network generates node- and dimension-wise coefficients C = σ(XW_c + b_c) to adaptively blend the frozen low-pass and high-pass representations, reducing parameter complexity from O(n×e) to O((d+1)×e). An anomaly-aware regularization loss (binary cross-entropy on per-node average fusion weights toward class-specific targets p^a ≤ p^n) enforces that abnormal nodes retain more high-pass anomaly signal. A theoretical result under an Anomalous Stochastic Block Model with degree heterogeneity and class imbalance proves that node-adaptive low/high-pass filtering yields linear separability with probability 1−o_d(1), and the gated fusion is shown to approximate this oracle assignment in a data-driven manner.

## Results

On 10 GADBench datasets under a strict semi-supervised setting (100 labeled nodes: 20 anomalies, 80 normal), APF achieves an average AUPRC of 49.6, outperforming the strongest baseline XGBGraph (42.9) by +6.7 percentage points. APF also gains +3.8% in AUROC and +6.2% in Rec@K on average over all baselines. Even without pre-training, APF (w/o Lpt) reaches 44.7 AUPRC, surpassing all end-to-end baselines including BWGNN (35.4) and SpaceGNN (41.8). Pre-training itself contributes substantial gains: DGI, BWDGI, and APF improve AUPRC by +7.3%, +3.8%, and +4.9% respectively over their end-to-end counterparts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APF outperforms all 23 baselines on average AUPRC (49.6 vs. 42.9 for the best baseline XGBGraph), with particularly large margins over GAD-specific methods like BWGNN (35.4), AMNet (31.3), and SpaceGNN (41.8), and over pre-training methods like DGI (36.6), BGRL (36.6), and GraphMAE (34.2). APF achieves the best per-dataset AUPRC on 8 out of 10 datasets, losing only to XGBGraph on Amazon (83.8 vs. 84.4) and Elliptic (67.7 vs. 77.2), where highly heterogeneous tabular features favor tree-based models. Among pre-training baselines, APF surpasses BWDGI (39.2) by +10.4 AUPRC and PolyGCL (37.1) by +12.5, demonstrating that anomaly-aware pre-training with dual spectral filters substantially outperforms task-agnostic pre-training. Even the ablated variant APF (w/o Lpt) at 44.7 AUPRC exceeds all end-to-end baselines, confirming that the fine-tuning module (gated fusion + anomaly-aware regularization) independently contributes strong gains. On the hardest dataset T-Social (5.7M nodes), APF achieves 77.8 AUPRC versus 59.0 for SpaceGNN and 46.7 for BGRL, highlighting scalability advantages.

## Relation to Prior Work

APF extends the graph pre-training-and-fine-tuning paradigm (DGI, GraphMAE, BGRL) by injecting anomaly awareness into the pre-training objective via Rayleigh Quotient-guided subgraph sampling (borrowed from UniGAD's MRQSampler) and dual learnable Chebyshev spectral filters (adapted from PolyGCL). It departs from GAD-specific spectral methods (BWGNN, AMNet, GHRN) that apply uniform filtering across all nodes, instead introducing a node- and dimension-adaptive Gated Fusion Network that selectively blends low-pass semantic and high-pass anomaly representations per node. The theoretical analysis extends the linear separability results of Baranwal et al. (2021) from a single-pattern CSBM to a mixed homophily/heterophily, degree-corrected, class-imbalanced Anomalous Stochastic Block Model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

APF provides a principled two-stage recipe for GAD under extreme label scarcity (100 labeled nodes), addressing both the label-scarcity and local homophily disparity challenges that limit real-world deployment. The finding that the fine-tuning module (gated fusion + anomaly-aware regularization) alone already surpasses all baselines suggests that adaptive representation fusion may be more critical than the specific pre-training objective, an insight that could guide future GAD design. Limitations include underperformance on tabular-heavy datasets (Amazon, Elliptic) where tree-based models dominate, and the theoretical guarantees rely on idealized filter assignments that the learned soft fusion only approximates. Open questions include whether the framework extends to edge- or graph-level anomaly detection and whether the Rayleigh Quotient subgraph sampling remains effective on very dense or dynamic graphs.
