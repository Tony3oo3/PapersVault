## Problem

Massive Activations (MAs) have been documented in LLMs and ViTs, but their functional role in Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) for visual generation remained largely unexplored. Additionally, standard Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) often produces outputs with insufficient fine-grained local detail synthesis, and no existing guidance mechanism explicitly targets this deficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263560/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pretrained DiTs (SD3, SD3.5, Flux) at 1024×1024 resolution, consuming a text prompt, noised latent z_t, and timestep t as inputs to produce a generated RGB image. DG is entirely training-free: it modifies internal hidden states by zeroing out specific MA dimensions at a chosen block depth m (e.g., m=6 for SD3, m=20 for SD3.5), requiring no additional parameters, unconditional branch, or fine-tuning. The approach adds roughly 1.5× less latency than CFG since only one conditional branch is forwarded before the disruption depth.

## Method

The paper first establishes that MAs in DiTs occur at fixed feature dimensions across all spatial tokens, are governed by the AdaLN residual scaling factor α_k, and are primarily modulated by the input timestep embedding rather than text conditioning. Activation intervention experiments (zeroing MA dimensions at a single block) show that disrupting MAs degrades local detail fidelity while preserving semantic content, identifying MAs as drivers of token-wise local detail synthesis. Building on this, Detail Guidance (DG) constructs a degraded 'detail-deficient' model D_{θ,m} by masking MA dimensions in the hidden state at block m and propagating through remaining blocks, then applies self-guidance: D̂_θ(z_t,t,c) = D_θ(z_t,t,c) + w(D_θ(z_t,t,c) − D_{θ,m}(z_t,t,c)). This formulation is complementary to CFG (which disrupts the conditioning c) and can be stacked additively, yielding joint enhancement of detail fidelity and prompt alignment without any training.

## Results

On the HPSv2.1 benchmark with SD3, DG (training-free) achieves the highest Aesthetic score (6.14) among all guidance methods and competitive HPSv2.1 (30.14 avg), while requiring no unconditional-branch training. When combined with CFG, DG achieves the best HPSv2.1 (30.96) and second-best Aesthetic (6.13). On Pick-a-Pic, DG yields substantial detail-quality gains across all three models, e.g., HPSv2.1 improves from 21.84 to 28.65 and Aesthetic from 5.58 to 6.01 on SD3 (Cond setting). DG is also ~1.5× faster than CFG at inference (10.6 s vs 15.7 s for SD3.5 at 1024×1024).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DG outperforms PAG (the only other training-free baseline) on both HPSv2.1 (30.14 vs 29.20) and Aesthetic (6.14 vs 6.10), confirming that MA-based disruption is a more effective degradation signal than attention-map perturbation. Against the five trained CFG variants (CFG, CFG++, Semantic-CFG, FA-CFG, CFG-Zero), DG achieves the highest Aesthetic score (6.14) without requiring an unconditional branch, while its HPSv2.1 (30.14) is within 0.1–0.4 of the best trained method (CFG-Zero, 30.57). The combined CFG+DG strategy dominates all baselines on HPSv2.1 (30.96) and is competitive on Aesthetic (6.13), demonstrating that DG and CFG target complementary aspects (detail vs. semantics). Notably, DG matches or exceeds all trained methods on the Photo category (28.70 vs. best 29.16 for Semantic-CFG) while leading on Anime (31.14 vs. 31.64 for CFG-Zero), suggesting consistent gains across content types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-guidance framework of Karras et al. (2024), which guides a diffusion model with a deliberately degraded 'bad' version, by identifying a fundamentally different degradation mechanism: disrupting Massive Activation (MA) dimensions rather than reducing model capacity or using under-trained checkpoints. It builds on prior observations of MAs in LLMs (Sun et al., 2024), ViTs (Darcet et al., 2024), and DiTs (Gan et al., 2025; Fang et al., 2025), but is the first to systematically investigate their functional role in the *generation* process rather than in feature extraction or quantization. The paper also positions DG as complementary to CFG and its advanced variants (CFG++, Semantic-CFG, FA-CFG, CFG-Zero), offering a decoupled guidance axis for detail fidelity versus prompt alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean mechanistic finding that MAs drive local detail synthesis while being text-independent and timestep-modulated provides a new interpretive lens for understanding DiT internals, potentially informing future architecture design or training strategies. DG is a practical, training-free technique that integrates with CFG and runs ~1.5× faster than CFG alone, making it immediately deployable. However, the paper acknowledges that DG can compromise semantic alignment for prompts with strong stylistic or identity constraints, and the practical quality gains (≈0.1–0.2 Aesthetic, ≈1–2 HPSv2.1) are modest. An open direction the authors highlight is leveraging MAs during training rather than only at sampling time, which could yield larger improvements.
