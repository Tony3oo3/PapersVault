## Problem

Diffusion-based fMRI-to-image reconstruction methods collapse the inherently hierarchical structure of brain signals into a single static neural embedding that guides the entire generation process. This fixed guidance is redundant during early coarse synthesis stages and insufficient during later fine-grained refinement stages, creating a fundamental mismatch between the multi-level neural representation and the dynamic, stage-dependent demands of image reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262326/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes flattened fMRI voxel signals (filtered via the NSD 'nsdgeneral' mask in 1.8-mm native volume space) as input and produces 512×512 RGB images. The fMRI signal is encoded into M hierarchical embeddings in a single forward pass, which then condition a 10-scale autoregressive generator. Final output uses a Best-of-4 selection strategy based on CLIP similarity (2.64s total), though single-shot generation (N=1) achieves 95.0% CLIP score in 0.92s.

## Method

MindHier introduces three components: (1) a Hierarchical fMRI Encoder of M cascaded transformer blocks that disentangles the fMRI signal into a multi-level feature hierarchy; (2) a Hierarchy-to-Hierarchy Alignment training scheme using cascaded MSE loss to align each encoder block's output with a corresponding CLIP ViT-L/14 layer, plus a SoftCLIP contrastive loss on the terminal feature for semantic anchoring; and (3) a Scale-Aware Coarse-to-Fine Neural Guidance strategy built on VAR/Switti's next-scale prediction paradigm, where the deepest semantic embedding (e_M) seeds the coarsest token map as a Start Token, and progressively shallower embeddings are injected via cross-attention at finer scales using the mapping h_k = M − ⌊M(k−1)/K⌋. A selective attention mask enforces that coarse scales attend only to deep semantic features while fine scales access detail-oriented ones. Training is two-stage: the fMRI encoder is first trained against frozen CLIP features, then the encoder is frozen while the scale-wise autoregressive model (30 transformer layers with SwiGLU, RoPE, RMSNorm, and AdaLN conditioning) is fine-tuned with cross-entropy loss on ground-truth token maps.

## Results

On the NSD test set (1,000 images, 4 subjects), MindHier achieves the best high-level semantic metrics among all compared methods: CLIP score of 96.4%, InceptionV3 of 95.9%, EffNet distance of 0.606, and SwAV distance of 0.329. Inference time is 2.64 seconds per 512×512 image, a 4.67× speedup over MindEye2 (12.14 s). With an auxiliary low-level feature (MindHier†), low-level metrics improve substantially (SSIM 0.461, PixCorr 0.326) while maintaining SOTA high-level scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MindHier outperforms all ten baselines on every high-level semantic metric (CLIP, InceptionV3, EffNet, SwAV) on the NSD test set. The largest margin is over BrainDiffuser (CLIP +5.5%, SwAV −0.092) and Takagi et al. (CLIP +14.3%), while the closest competitor is MindTuner (CLIP 93.8% vs. 96.4%, a 2.6-point gap). Against the strongest diffusion-based baseline MindEye2, MindHier gains +3.4% CLIP, +0.5% Incep, and reduces SwAV from 0.344 to 0.329, while being 4.67× faster (2.64 s vs. 12.14 s). On the THINGS-fMRI dataset, MindHier surpasses BrainFlora by +19.7% in AlexNet(5) accuracy (81.9% vs. 62.2%) and +15.5% in CLIP (73.1% vs. 57.6%), confirming cross-dataset generalizability. In the brain grounding task (Table S4), MindHier achieves the highest overall IoU (18.69) and the best salient-creature localization (33.0% acc@0.5) compared to MindEye and UMBRAE-S1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant diffusion-based paradigm in fMRI-to-image reconstruction (MindEye, MindEye2, BrainDiffuser, etc.), which maps fMRI signals to a single CLIP embedding used as fixed guidance throughout iterative denoising. Instead, it builds on the scale-wise autoregressive modeling paradigm introduced by VAR (Tian et al., 2024) and the Switti architecture (Voronov et al., 2024), repurposing the 'next-scale prediction' framework as a natural scaffold for injecting hierarchical neural features at matching resolutions. The key conceptual departure is replacing a single static guidance signal with a hierarchy-to-hierarchy alignment between multi-level fMRI encoder outputs and intermediate CLIP vision encoder layers, enabling scale-aware coarse-to-fine conditioning that mirrors the 'Forest before Trees' principle from cognitive neuroscience.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MindHier's 4.67× inference speedup (2.64s vs. 12.14s) and deterministic generation (no random noise initialization) are practically significant for moving fMRI-to-image reconstruction toward real-time brain-computer interfaces, a goal that iterative diffusion pipelines make difficult. The paper also identifies a fundamental representational mismatch—collapsing hierarchical brain signals into a single vector—that likely affects all current diffusion-based decoders, suggesting the hierarchical guidance principle could benefit other brain-decoding tasks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to cross-subject settings without extensive finetuning (the 1-hour cross-subject result degrades), whether it can handle temporal dynamics for fMRI-to-video, and how to improve fine-grained texture and facial fidelity where the method still lags.
