## Problem

Existing voice assistant benchmarks (VoiceBench, Spoken-CoQA, Spoken-SQuAD) evaluate models on clean or generic conversational audio, overlooking the unique challenges of wearable devices such as AI glasses: egocentric audio degraded by motion and wind noise, rapid micro-interactions under strict latency constraints, and the need to distinguish device-directed speech from side-talk and background conversations. WearVox fills this gap as the first benchmark purpose-built for realistic wearable voice assistant scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262263/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each task follows a Text-In, Speech-In, Text-Out formulation f(T_I, S_I) → T_O, where T_I is a task-specific text prompt (e.g., search results for grounded QA, tool/function definitions for tool calling) and S_I is multi-channel egocentric audio captured by an AI glasses microphone array. Outputs are task-specific: concise text answers for QA, structured JSON tool calls, a special [Mute] control token to suppress downstream TTS for side-talk, or diarized bilingual translated dialogue. For evaluating existing single-channel SLLMs, the multi-channel recordings are first reduced to a single channel via beamforming.

## Method

The paper introduces WearVox (3,842 multi-channel egocentric recordings across five tasks: Search-Grounded QA, Closed-Book QA, Side-Talk Rejection, Tool Calling, and Bidirectional Speech Translation) and a case study with two new SLLMs built on Llama-4-Scout-17B-16E with a 1B-parameter Conformer speech encoder (pre-trained with BEST-RQ at 12.5 Hz). SC WearLlama processes only the beamformed channel, while MC WearLlama interleaves channel 0 (highest SNR) with the beamformed channel, giving the model access to spatial audio cues. Training uses synthetic speech QA pairs generated by prompting Llama-4-Scout with ASR transcripts, combined with ASR data, while keeping the speech encoder frozen; multi-channel audio is simulated using real-environment RIRs with noise augmentation (SNR −5 to 40 dB) and bystander speech overlap. The key finding is that multi-channel input improves side-talk rejection from 85.4% to 93.9% and overall turn-based accuracy from 61.9% to 66.4%, with the largest gains in noisy outdoor conditions.

## Results

On the WearVox benchmark, most real-time SLLMs achieve turn-based micro-average accuracies ranging from 29.7% (Gemma 3n) to 71.3% (Gemini 2.5 Flash Thinking), with substantial degradation on noisy outdoor audio. The best proprietary model, Gemini 2.5 Flash Thinking, reaches 71.3% turn-based accuracy and 70.1% on speech translation, while GPT-5 w/ Whisper achieves 57.8% turn-based and 92.9% translation. The paper's MC WearLlama (multichannel) attains 66.4% turn-based micro-average, outperforming its single-channel counterpart SC WearLlama (61.9%) by 4.5 points, with the largest gains in side-talk rejection (93.9% vs. 85.4%) and tool calling (63.9% vs. 58.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary SLLMs, Gemini 2.5 Flash Thinking leads on turn-based tasks (71.3% micro-avg) and side-talk rejection (91.4%), while GPT-5 w/ Whisper dominates closed-book QA (70.6%) and speech translation (92.9%). GPT-4o Audio excels at speech translation (76.0%) but collapses on tool calling (8.9%), likely due to its audio-first training not optimizing structured text output. Open-source models (Gemma 3n, Kimi-Audio, Qwen2.5-Omni) trail significantly, with turn-based micro-averages of 29.7–43.6%, attributed to smaller parameter counts (<8B). The paper's MC WearLlama (66.4% turn-based) surpasses GPT-4o Audio (43.1%) and approaches GPT-5 w/ Whisper (57.8%), while its single-channel variant SC WearLlama (61.9%) is competitive with Gemini 2.5 Flash (59.8%). The multichannel advantage is most pronounced in side-talk rejection (+8.5 pp) and tool calling (+5.4 pp), confirming that spatial audio cues improve discrimination of device-directed speech from background interference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the voice assistant benchmarking lineage (Spoken-SQuAD, VoiceBench, CAVA, FDX-Bench) from clean or studio-recorded audio to egocentric, multi-channel recordings captured on consumer AI glasses, introducing side-talk rejection and multi-channel spatial audio as first-class evaluation dimensions. Architecturally, the proposed MC WearLlama follows the Llama 3 Speech / AudioChatLlama recipe (Conformer encoder with BEST-RQ pre-training, synthetic speech-QA alignment on top of a frozen LLM) but replaces the single beamformed channel with an interleaved two-channel (channel-0 + beamformed) input, building on Llama-4-Scout-17B-16E as the language backbone. The paper thus sits at the intersection of the speech-LLM evaluation literature and the array-signal-processing literature, bridging them through a unified benchmark and a minimal multi-channel architecture modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WearVox is the first benchmark to capture the full acoustic complexity of wearable interactions (motion, wind, bystander speech, 13 noise types, 63% outdoor), and its results (29–59% accuracy for leading SLLMs) expose a substantial gap between lab-benchmark performance and real-world wearable readiness. The multi-channel case study provides the first controlled evidence that spatial audio cues yield meaningful gains in side-talk rejection (+8.5 pp) and noise robustness (~5 pp in outdoor noisy settings), pointing to a concrete architectural direction for on-device voice assistants. Open questions include the latency–quality trade-off of reasoning SLLMs on latency-constrained wearables (5.5 s TTFT in thinking mode), cross-device transferability across microphone geometries, and the integration of visual and IMU signals for disambiguation—none of which are addressed here.
