## Problem

Conventional semantic segmentation treats all classes as independent in a flat label space, ignoring the natural hierarchical structure where object-level categories contain part-level subcategories. This leads to semantic inconsistencies and poor disambiguation of visually similar parts, especially in fine-grained or large-category settings. Existing hierarchy-aware methods (e.g., HSSN, LogicSeg) handle hierarchy only at the probability/logit level or as a fixed auxiliary loss term, without structuring the class representation space itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260154/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (512×1024 or 512×512 crops) and produces pixel-wise segmentation masks, identical to the underlying encoder-decoder (e.g., DeepLabV3+ with ResNet-101). It additionally requires a two-level hierarchical label annotation with a fixed mapping from low-level to high-level classes, which is used only during training. At inference, the hierarchical prototype module is entirely removed, so the output pipeline and latency are unchanged from the base model.

## Method

HiPoSeg maintains two prototype memory buffers (high-level and low-level) updated via momentum from batch-wise averages of L2-normalized pixel features projected through a lightweight head. Training employs a hierarchical contrastive objective: feature-prototype and prototype-prototype losses at both levels pull features toward correct prototypes and push distinct prototypes apart, while a margin-based alignment constraint keeps each low-level prototype within distance σ₁ of its parent high-level prototype and enforces inter-high-level separation above σ₂. A phased schedule activates high-level contrast after 7.5% of iterations, low-level contrast after 22.5%, and the alignment loss after 37.5%, implementing a coarse-to-fine curriculum. The entire module is training-only and plug-and-play, introducing no extra parameters or computation at test time and integrating with arbitrary backbones (CNN or Transformer) and decoders.

## Results

HiPoSeg achieves 84.04% mIoU on Cityscapes (ResNet-101), a +10.49%p gain over the DeepLabV3+ baseline and +3.07%p average over competing methods. On ADE20K, Mapillary Vistas 2.0, and PASCAL-Part-108, it reaches 48.99%, 41.42%, and 49.33% mIoU respectively, setting new state-of-the-art on all four benchmarks. Notably, these gains are achieved with ResNet-101, outperforming methods that use stronger backbones (e.g., HRNet-W48), and the method introduces zero additional inference cost since the prototype module is training-only.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiPoSeg outperforms all 23 compared baselines across all four datasets. On Cityscapes, it surpasses the strongest hierarchical-semantics methods HSSN (83.02%) and LogicSeg (83.20%) by +1.02%p and +0.84%p respectively, and beats contrastive methods like Contextrast (82.20%, HRNet-W48) and RegionSeg (81.90%, HRNet-W48) despite using the lighter ResNet-101 backbone. On ADE20K, the gains over HSSN and LogicSeg are +1.30%p and +0.53%p; on Mapillary Vistas 2.0, +1.26%p and +0.70%p; and on PASCAL-Part-108, +1.01%p and +0.87%p. The method also substantially outperforms the direct DeepLabV3+ backbone baseline by +10.49%p (Cityscapes), +4.51%p (ADE20K), +9.77%p (Mapillary), and +2.43%p (PASCAL-Part-108), confirming that the hierarchical prototype contrastive loss is the primary source of improvement rather than backbone choice. No baseline is matched or exceeded by HiPoSeg in any reported setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the prototype memory mechanism from prototypical networks (Snell et al., 2017) and extends it to a two-level hierarchical structure for semantic segmentation. It departs from prior hierarchical segmentation methods (HSSN, LogicSeg) that treat hierarchy as a probability-level or logit-level constraint, instead lifting hierarchy into the representation space itself through class-wise prototype banks at each level. It also extends contrastive learning for segmentation (ContrastSeg, RegionSeg, ProtoSeg) by replacing the flat label assumption with a hierarchical contrastive objective coupled by a margin-based alignment constraint, inspired by the top-down recognition process in human visual cognition (Bar, 2003).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method's training-only, zero-inference-overhead design makes it a practical plug-and-play module that can be attached to any existing segmentation backbone (demonstrated on ResNet-101, HRNet-W48, and SegFormer) without modifying the inference pipeline. The ablation revealing that naively combining high- and low-level contrastive losses without the alignment constraint actually degrades performance (79.16% vs. 80.96%/81.04% individually) highlights a non-trivial gradient-interference problem in multi-level representation learning. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to open-vocabulary or long-tailed segmentation settings, and whether the two-level hierarchy assumption limits applicability to deeper taxonomies.
