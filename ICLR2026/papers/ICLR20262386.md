## Problem

Transformer-based PDE surrogates with fixed patch sizes suffer from two coupled limitations: (1) during autoregressive rollouts, repeatedly applying the same patch grid causes errors to constructively interfere at harmonic frequencies k/p, producing spectral spikes and visible grid artifacts that degrade long-horizon predictions; and (2) the computational cost is locked at training time, preventing users from trading accuracy for speed at inference. The paper identifies temporal coherence in tokenization as an unaddressed source of systematic error and asks whether dynamic patch-size control at inference can simultaneously mitigate artifacts and enable compute-adaptive deployment.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a spatiotemporal tensor of 6 consecutive time frames (H×W×T×C) from a discretized PDE field, padded with learned boundary-condition tokens, and outputs the next-step field prediction. During training the model predicts the delta (change from the previous frame) under a normalized MSE loss, while evaluation metrics are computed on reconstructed fields. The key operational detail is that at inference the effective patch/stride size (and thus token count) can be varied per rollout step from {4, 8, 16}, directly controlling compute: e.g., on a 256² grid, stride 16 yields 256 tokens (~5 GFLOPs) while stride 4 yields 4096 tokens (~170 GFLOPs).

## Method

Overtone introduces two architecture-agnostic tokenization modules—CSM (Convolutional Stride Modulation, which keeps a fixed convolutional kernel and dynamically changes the stride) and CKM (Convolutional Kernel Modulation, which resizes a single base kernel via bicubic interpolation using a pseudoinverse transpose of the interpolation matrix)—applied at both encoder and decoder stages of ViT-based backbones (vanilla, axial, CViT) with hMLP-style two-stage convolutions. During training, the model sees random patch/stride sizes sampled uniformly from {4, 8, 16} at each forward pass, learning to interpret tokens from all grids within a shared embedding space. At inference, a cyclic schedule (4→8→16, repeated) modulates the tokenization scale across autoregressive rollout steps. The core insight is that this cycling disrupts the phase-locking of error injections at patch-lattice harmonics: under a fixed grid, errors at frequencies m/k remain aligned across timesteps and grow quadratically (O(n²)), whereas cycling shifts the patch boundaries so that injections decorrelate, reducing growth to approximately linear (O(n)) and suppressing the characteristic spectral spikes. This yields up to 40% lower VRMSE over 10-step rollouts versus fixed-patch baselines while also enabling a single trained model to serve multiple compute budgets without retraining.

## Results

On 10-step autoregressive rollouts, Overtone's cyclic patch modulation (CSM/CKM) reduces VRMSE by up to 40% compared to fixed-patch-16 baselines (e.g., Active Matter: 0.351 vs 0.370 for Vanilla ViT; Supernova: 1.14 vs 1.75). For next-step prediction, a single flexible CSM/CKM model matches or exceeds separately trained fixed-patch models across all six 2D/3D PDE datasets from The Well at every token count (e.g., Shear Flow at 2048 tokens: CSM 0.00546 vs fixed 0.00677 VRMSE for Vanilla ViT). Against non-patch SOTA surrogates, Overtone (100M) achieves 0.0375 VRMSE on Shear Flow and 0.373 on Turbulent Radiative Layer 2D, substantially outperforming TFNO (0.89/0.81), FFNO (0.105/0.485), SineNet (0.17/0.65), and Transolver (>1/>1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Well benchmark suite (FNO, TFNO, U-Net, CNext-U-Net retrained), Overtone's flexible models outperform across most tasks and compute budgets; for example, on Shear Flow at 2048 tokens, CSM achieves 0.00546 VRMSE versus the Well baseline of 0.1049. In the non-patch comparison (Table 4), Overtone (100M Vanilla ViT + CSM) achieves 0.0375 VRMSE on Shear Flow, beating TFNO (0.89), FFNO (0.105), SineNet (0.17), and Transolver (>1) by wide margins; on Turbulent Radiative Layer 2D, Overtone scores 0.373 versus TFNO (0.81), FFNO (0.485), SineNet (0.65), and Transolver (>1). The most direct comparison is against fixed-patch-16 models trained with the same total compute budget: Overtone wins on all six datasets for both Axial ViT and Vanilla ViT backbones, with the largest gains on Active Matter (+40% Axial) and Rayleigh-Bénard (+38.4% Vanilla). The one case where the advantage is modest is Active Matter with Vanilla ViT (+5.1%), where the fixed-patch model already performs relatively well. Overall, Overtone consistently wins or ties across all evaluated baselines and datasets, with the largest margins against non-patch neural operator architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Vision Transformer lineage for PDE surrogate modeling (vanilla ViT, Axial ViT, hMLP multi-stage encoders) and repurposes kernel interpolation techniques from computer vision (SiCNN, FlexiViT) into architecture-agnostic tokenization modules (CSM, CKM) for autoregressive PDE prediction. The key departure is the discovery that fixed-patch tokenization causes systematic harmonic error accumulation at wavenumbers k/p through temporal coherence during rollouts, and the introduction of cyclic patch modulation at inference as a mitigation strategy. While adaptive patching has been explored in image classification (FlexiViT) and data-driven patchification in PDEs (MATEY), no prior work cyclically modulates patch sizes during autoregressive rollouts or diagnoses the resulting spectral artifact structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Overtone provides a practical, plug-in mechanism for compute-adaptive PDE surrogate deployment—training one model that serves multiple inference budgets without retraining—which is directly relevant as large foundation models for physics (Walrus, MPP, DISCO) are being built. The harmonic artifact diagnosis offers a new lens for understanding long-horizon degradation in patch-based models beyond what training alone can fix. Limitations include the requirement to train with multiple patch sizes (no zero-shot adaptation), single-seed experiments at 50M–100M scale, and restriction to regular grids; open questions include adaptive (non-cyclic) modulation, extension to irregular geometries, and whether the spectral insight generalizes to non-transformer autoregressive models.
