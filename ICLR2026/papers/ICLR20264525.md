## Problem

Existing probabilistic angle-testing techniques for high-dimensional similarity search (CEOs, PEOs) rely on Gaussian-distributed random projection vectors and derive their theoretical guarantees under the asymptotic assumption that the number of projections m tends to infinity, which is impractical since computational cost grows with m. This paper identifies that the Gaussian distribution is suboptimal and that the true determinant of estimation accuracy is the reference angle between a vector and its nearest projection vector, not the distribution itself.

## Input / Output

The method takes high-dimensional query and data vectors (normalized to the unit sphere) and a fixed set of m projection vectors configured via antipodal pairs or cross-polytopes, partitioned into L subspaces in a product-quantization-like fashion (virtually generating m^L projections). For angle comparison (KS1), it outputs a scalar kernel value K_S^1(q,v) = ⟨v, Z_HS(q)⟩ with O(1) per-vector query cost after O(md) projection. For angle thresholding (KS2), it outputs K_S^2(q,v) = ⟨Hq, Z_S(Hv)⟩/A_S(Hv) compared against cos(θ), with O(L) per-vector cost where L ≪ d, enabling a routing decision that skips exact distance computation in graph traversal.

## Method

The core insight is that the reference angle ψ (the angle between a vector and its closest projection vector) fully determines estimation accuracy via a deterministic Beta-distribution relationship (Eq. 4), eliminating the need for asymptotic assumptions on m. Two kernel functions are designed: K_S^1 for pairwise angle comparison and K_S^2 for threshold testing, the latter normalizing by the reference angle A_S(Hv) to achieve angle-sensitivity. To minimize the reference angle, the paper proposes two deterministic projection structures—antipodal projections (Alg. 1) and multiple rotated cross-polytopes (Alg. 2)—that outperform purely random Gaussian projections, with the cross-polytope structure leveraging the known optimal covering property for m=2d. Applied to ANNS, the KS2 routing test is integrated into HNSW graph traversal, skipping exact distance computations for neighbors that fail the probabilistic inequality, yielding 2.5×–3× QPS improvement over vanilla HNSW and 10%–30% over HNSW+PEOs with a 5% smaller index.

## Results

For MIPS (k=10, 2048 projection vectors, top-5 probed), KS1 with the cross-polytope structure S_pol achieves up to 0.81% higher recall than CEOs at Probe@10K (64.355% vs 63.545% on GloVe1M), with consistent but smaller gains on Word (98.404% vs 98.182%) and GloVe2M (55.394% vs 54.916%). For ANNS (k=10), HNSW+KS2 achieves 2.5×–3× higher QPS than HNSW and is 10%–30% faster than HNSW+PEOs across six datasets (SIFT, GloVe1M, Word, GloVe2M, Tiny, GIST), while also reducing index size by approximately 5% compared to HNSW+PEOs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CEOs (Pham, KDD 2021), KS1 with S_pol consistently achieves higher recall across all three MIPS datasets, with the largest gain of 0.81% on GloVe1M at Probe@10K; the improvement is attributed to the smaller reference angle from the structured (non-Gaussian) projection vectors. Against HNSW (Malkov & Yashunin, TPAMI 2020), HNSW+KS2 delivers a 2.5×–3× QPS speedup across all six ANNS datasets, demonstrating the effectiveness of the KS2 routing test in skipping unnecessary exact distance computations. Against HNSW+PEOs (Lu et al., ICML 2024), HNSW+KS2 is 10%–30% faster in QPS and uses ~5% less index space, owing to a simpler testing inequality (Ineq. 9) and fewer stored constants. Against ScaNN (Guo et al., ICML 2020), HNSW+KS2 dominates in the recall region below 85% on most datasets, but ScaNN outperforms it in the high-recall region on Word due to HNSW connectivity issues. The paper also shows that KS2's superiority over PEOs is graph-structure-independent, as NSSG+KS2 outperforms NSSG+PEOs on all datasets except GIST.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on two prior projection-based techniques: CEOs (Pham, 2021) for MIPS via extreme order statistics, and PEOs (Lu et al., 2024) for probabilistic routing in similarity graphs. Both rely on Gaussian-distributed projection vectors and an asymptotic result (Lemma 1.3) requiring the number of projections m → ∞. The key departure is the introduction of a reference-angle-based deterministic relationship (Eq. 4) that eliminates the asymptotic assumption, combined with structured projection configurations (antipodal pairs, cross-polytopes) that provably minimize the reference angle. The authors show that their kernel functions K_S^1 and K_S^2 reduce to the CEOs and PEOs indicators respectively when S is drawn from a Gaussian, positioning their work as a strict generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.5×–3× QPS improvement over HNSW and 10–30% over HNSW+PEOs across six diverse datasets makes this a practically significant advance for production ANNS systems. The conceptual finding that the Gaussian distribution is suboptimal for projection vectors—replacing it with structured configurations on the sphere—challenges a widely adopted default in the random-projection literature. Open questions include whether the optimal projection configuration (related to the best covering problem) can be characterized for m > d+3, and whether the reference-angle framework extends naturally to other distance metrics or non-spherical geometries. The modest 0.8% improvement of KS1 over CEOs suggests the main gains come from the KS2 routing test rather than the projection structure alone.
