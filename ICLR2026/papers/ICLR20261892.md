## Problem

Despite rapid progress in discrete diffusion and flow models, there is no reliable benchmark to evaluate whether Schrödinger bridge (SB) or entropic optimal transport (EOT) solvers actually solve the underlying problem on discrete spaces. Existing evaluations rely on proxy metrics (FID, MSE) that conflate algorithmic quality with implementation details, and practical discrete-space EOT/SB solvers remain largely absent beyond a few initial studies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261892/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a discrete source distribution p0 (a discretized Gaussian on D dimensions with S=50 categories) and a CP-parameterized scalar function v* to construct a target p1 with a known closed-form optimal conditional q*(x1|x0). Solvers receive i.i.d. samples from p0 and p1 plus a factorizable Markov reference process (uniform or Gaussian-like transition matrix with N+1=128 steps) and must produce a generative conditional q_θ(x1|x0) supporting out-of-sample generation. The CP parameterization reduces the normalizing constant computation from O(S^D) to O(KDS), enabling tractable evaluation at D=2, 16, and 64.

## Method

The core construction (Theorem 3.1) shows that any pair (p0, v*) on a discrete space induces a benchmark pair (p0, p1) whose optimal SB solution has the closed form q*(x1|x0) ∝ v*(x1)·q^ref(x1|x0). To make this tractable in high dimensions, v* is parameterized via a rank-K CP decomposition, yielding a mixture of K factorizable distributions whose normalizing constant and sampling reduce to O(KDS) operations (Proposition 3.1). As a byproduct, the paper introduces DLightSB, a static solver that optimizes a reformulated KL objective (Proposition 4.1) over the CP parameters using Monte Carlo estimation, and DLightSB-M, a dynamic variant that projects a reciprocal process directly onto the set of all SBs via a single optimal projection step (Proposition 4.2). The paper also extends CSBM with the online α-IMF update strategy to produce α-CSBM, halving computational cost while maintaining comparable quality.

## Results

On the high-dimensional Gaussian mixture benchmark (D ∈ {2, 16, 64}, S=50 categories), DLightSB achieves the highest Conditional Shape Score across all settings, reaching 0.95 (D=2, Gaussian γ=0.02) and maintaining ≥0.67 even at D=64. DLightSB-M (KL/64) attains comparable performance (e.g., 0.87 Shape / 0.92 Trend at D=16, Gaussian γ=0.02), while CSBM (KL/64) scores 0.87/0.82 and α-CSBM (KL/64) scores 0.89/0.88 on the same setting. KL loss consistently outperforms MSE across all diffusion-based methods, and increasing the number of timesteps N from 16 to 64 generally improves metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against CSBM (Ksenofontov & Korotin, 2025), the existing discrete-space Schrödinger Bridge Matching solver. DLightSB outperforms CSBM (KL/64) on Conditional Shape Score in most settings (e.g., 0.85 vs. 0.87 at D=16 Gaussian γ=0.02, but 0.95 vs. 0.88 at D=2), and on Trend Score it leads more decisively (0.93 vs. 0.82 at D=16). DLightSB-M (KL/64) matches or slightly exceeds CSBM on Trend Score (0.92 vs. 0.82) while being competitive on Shape Score. The new α-CSBM variant achieves quality comparable to full CSBM (e.g., 0.89 vs. 0.87 Shape at D=16) while halving computational cost by training a single model instead of two. All baselines (Independent, Reference, Feature-wise SB) underperform the learned solvers, with Reference degrading most severely (Shape ≤ 0.45) and Feature-wise SB deteriorating as dimensionality increases (0.81 at D=16 dropping to 0.35 at D=64 for uniform γ=0.005).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous-space SB benchmark construction of Gushchin et al. (2023b) to discrete state spaces, using a CP-based parameterization to make the ground-truth conditional distributions tractable. The new solvers DLightSB and DLightSB-M are direct discrete analogues of LightSB (Korotin et al., 2024) and LightSB-M (Gushchin et al., 2024a), while α-CSBM ports the online single-step update strategy of α-IMF (De Bortoli et al., 2024) into the CSBM framework (Ksenofontov & Korotin, 2025). The benchmark construction itself is a byproduct of the CP parameterization needed to make Theorem 3.1 computationally feasible, rather than a standalone methodological contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first standardized benchmark for discrete-space SB/EOT solvers, filling a critical evaluation gap where prior work relied on proxy metrics (FID, MSE) that do not directly measure whether a method solves the underlying transport problem. The benchmark reveals that DLightSB(-M) enjoys an inductive-bias advantage by construction (same CP structure as the ground truth), which the authors acknowledge but argue is useful as an oracle reference. Open questions include whether the CP parameterization is too restrictive for complex real-world discrete data, how the methods scale beyond D=64 given severe memory constraints, and whether the benchmark's Gaussian-mixture structure generalizes to more heterogeneous target distributions.
