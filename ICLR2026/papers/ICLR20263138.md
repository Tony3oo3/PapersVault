## Problem

Diffusion LLMs (dLLMs) support any-order, any-step decoding, which expands the attack surface: harmful content can emerge at arbitrary positions and intermediate decoding steps, bypassing response-level refusals designed for fixed left-to-right autoregressive generation. Per-token KL divergence analysis reveals that existing safety alignment in dLLMs is shallow—safety signals collapse sharply after the first few tokens—and is not robust to decoding order, leaving models vulnerable to template-based attacks like DIJA that inject harmful spans mid-generation.

## Input / Output

The model receives a text prompt and generates a response through iterative masked-diffusion decoding, where at each step a subset of [MASK] tokens is unmasked. When harmful content is detected, the model emits [EOS] tokens at the affected positions, terminating generation. Additionally, the per-step [EOS] probability at the leftmost masked position in the first decoding step serves as a scalar early-rejection signal: if it exceeds a threshold τ, the model halts immediately without producing any output tokens, yielding up to 19.3× faster safe termination.

## Method

A2D modifies the standard masked-diffusion training objective with a single change: for harmful completions, all masked positions are supervised to predict [EOS] rather than their original tokens, while safe completions retain their original reconstruction targets. The corruption rate λ is sampled uniformly from [ε, 1] at each training step, exposing the model to both early and late decoding stages and enforcing consistent alignment across the full generation trajectory. Training uses a dual dataset (Harmful Set for [EOS] supervision, Retain Set for normal reconstruction) and is applied as a post-hoc LoRA fine-tuning on already instruction-tuned dLLMs. The choice of [EOS] as the refusal token leverages its existing role as a padding and end-of-sequence marker, avoiding capability degradation while providing a natural, high-probability safety signal that doubles as a real-time monitoring indicator throughout generation.

## Results

A2D achieves the lowest average attack success rate (ASR) across all three instruction-tuned dLLMs: 6.8% on LLaDA, 9.1% on LLaDA-1.5, and 2.8% on Dream, compared to 21.6–60.2% for all baselines. On the DIJA template-based attack specifically, A2D reduces ASR from over 80% to 1.3% (LLaDA), 3.5% (LLaDA-1.5), and 0.0% (Dream). Capability is largely preserved, with A2D matching or exceeding baselines on General, Math, and Coding benchmarks (e.g., 66.9/44.8/35.6 on LLaDA-1.5 vs. 67.0/46.0/32.1 for VRPO). Early rejection via [EOS] probability thresholding yields up to 19.3× faster safe termination on harmful prompts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

A2D outperforms all three finetuning-based baselines (RT, SFT, VRPO) on average ASR across every model and every individual attack. Against VRPO (the strongest baseline), A2D reduces average ASR from 21.6% to 6.8% on LLaDA, from 48.6% to 9.1% on LLaDA-1.5, and from 22.7% to 2.8% on Dream. The gap is most dramatic on DIJA: VRPO still allows 45.0–72.5% ASR, while A2D achieves 1.3–0.0%. RT and SFT perform poorly on white-box attacks (Prefilling, DIJA), with SFT actually worsening ASR on LLaDA-1.5 (60.1% vs. 58.4% original), confirming that response-level alignment is insufficient for dLLMs. On capability, A2D matches or slightly trails VRPO on Math (e.g., 44.8 vs. 46.0 on LLaDA-1.5) but leads on Coding (35.6 vs. 32.1) and General (66.9 vs. 67.0, within noise), indicating minimal alignment tax.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the shallow-alignment analysis of Qi et al. (2025) from autoregressive LLMs to diffusion LLMs, showing that safety signals decay even more rapidly in dLLMs due to their any-order decoding. It departs from response-level alignment methods (refusal training, SFT) and preference-based approaches (VRPO) by operating at the token level: a single modification to the masked-diffusion training objective that substitutes [EOS] for harmful token targets. The DIJA template attack (Wen et al., 2025) is the key motivating vulnerability, exploiting the mismatch between AR-style response-level refusals and dLLM's non-sequential generation. A2D is positioned as the first alignment method explicitly designed to maintain safety under arbitrary decoding orders and at arbitrary decoding steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As dLLMs gain traction for parallel and interactive generation, the any-order/any-step vulnerability class identified here represents a fundamental safety gap that AR-specific defenses cannot address. The near-complete elimination of DIJA attacks (80%+ → 0–1.3%) with a trivially simple training modification and no inference overhead makes this a practical, low-cost solution for the emerging dLLM ecosystem. Open questions include whether A2D integrates cleanly into initial pretraining alignment pipelines (here it is applied post-hoc), how it behaves on larger or multimodal dLLMs, and whether the [EOS]-based signal remains robust as dLLM architectures evolve. The paper also notes a small alignment tax on math and coding, and the method has only been validated on three open-source dLLMs.
