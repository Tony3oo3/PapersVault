## Problem

Neuroscience suggests that a small subset of biological neurons acts as computational bottlenecks for core cognitive functions, yet no systematic method existed to identify analogous critical neurons in LLMs. Prior vulnerability analyses operated at the parameter or component level, leaving open whether a minimal set of individual neurons governs overall language capability and what happens when they are collectively disrupted.

## Input / Output

The method requires only a single text sequence (minimum 10 tokens) and a pre-trained LLM as input; the text is embedded and perturbed with Gaussian noise (scale α=5) across K=100 Monte Carlo samples. It outputs an ultra-sparse set of critical neurons (as few as 3 out of over 1.1 billion) specified by layer and index, along with a log-scale perplexity degradation score. Notably, the identified critical neuron set is invariant to the choice of input text once the 10-token threshold is met, making the output a model-specific architectural property rather than an input-dependent artifact.

## Method

The proposed Perturbation-based Causal Identification of Critical Neurons method operates in two stages. Stage 1 computes a per-neuron importance score by injecting Gaussian noise into the input embedding K times and averaging the absolute activation differences between clean and noisy forward passes, yielding a sensitivity-ranked list of all neurons. Stage 2 performs a greedy causal verification: it sequentially masks the top-ranked neurons (setting their activations to zero) in steps of Δn, evaluating the resulting perplexity increase until the degradation exceeds a threshold ε=1, at which point the minimal critical set is returned. This reduces the search from O(2^|N|) exhaustive enumeration to O(K·|N|) while preserving the causal guarantee that the identified set is truly necessary for model function. A key finding is that the critical neurons form a tightly coupled computational circuit: masking any proper subset produces only mild degradation, but simultaneously masking the complete set triggers a sharp phase transition with perplexity increases spanning up to 20 orders of magnitude.

## Results

Across 21 LLMs (0.5B–72B parameters), masking as few as 3 critical neurons (out of >1.1 billion in the 72B models) causes catastrophic perplexity increases of up to 20 orders of magnitude (e.g., Gemma-7B: 9.98 → 6.25×10²¹ on WikiText-103). Critical neurons are consistently located in outer-layer MLP down_proj components, and their disruption triggers sharp phase transitions rather than gradual degradation. On seven downstream benchmarks (MMLU-Pro, IFEval, GPQA-Diamond, HumanEval, MATH, MGSM, SimpleQA), all three 70B models drop to 0.0000 accuracy after masking their critical neuron sets, confirming complete loss of language capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its Perturbation-based Causal Identification method against three alternative neuron selection strategies: random selection, activation magnitude (AM) ranking (Sattarifard et al., 2025), and gradient magnitude (GM) ranking (Liu et al., 2025), evaluated on three 70B models (Llama-3.3-70B, Qwen2.5-72B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B). The proposed method decisively outperforms all baselines: while AM and GM methods require masking up to 1,000 neurons and still achieve only modest perplexity increases (plateauing at low values), the proposed method identifies ultra-sparse sets of just 3–7 neurons that trigger catastrophic perplexity increases of 4–20 orders of magnitude. Random masking shows the weakest effect, with gradual perplexity increase even at 1,000 masked neurons. The paper attributes the baselines' failure to their reliance on static importance measures (activation or gradient magnitude) that do not capture dynamic sensitivity to input perturbations, whereas the proposed method's noise-based sensitivity analysis identifies neurons whose collective disruption causes phase-transition collapse. Additionally, control experiments show that masking top-1000 neurons in up_proj or gate_proj layers causes only 0–1 order-of-magnitude perplexity increases, confirming that the catastrophic effect is uniquely localized to MLP down_proj neurons identified by the proposed method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on identifying important model components in LLMs from the parameter/component level (weight outliers, activation outliers, super-weights in MLP down_proj) to the neuron level, and from task-specific interventions (factual knowledge editing, safety alignment) to system-wide functional collapse. It departs most sharply from Yu et al. (2025) on 'super weights,' which found that individual parameters in down_proj layers can reduce accuracy, whereas here the authors show that a handful of entire neurons, when masked collectively, cause complete loss of all language capabilities. The two-stage perturbation-plus-causal-verification framework is novel in its combination of Monte Carlo sensitivity ranking with greedy sequential masking, and the discovery of a sharp phase transition (rather than gradual degradation) has no clear precedent in prior neuron-ablation studies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that as few as three neurons out of over one billion can catastrophically destroy all language capabilities in a 72B model reveals a fundamental architectural vulnerability with direct implications for deployment security, model robustness, and the design of future transformer architectures. The phase-transition behavior—where individual neuron removal is nearly harmless but collective removal triggers sudden collapse—suggests these neurons form a tightly coupled computational circuit, raising open questions about how to distribute critical functions more evenly across the network and whether defensive strategies (redundancy, architectural changes) can eliminate such single points of failure. A key limitation is that the method requires white-box access to model internals, and the findings are specific to current dense transformer architectures; whether the same ultra-sparse bottleneck structure persists in mixture-of-experts or other emerging architectures remains untested.
