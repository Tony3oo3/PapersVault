## Problem

N:M sparsity (e.g., 2:4) offers hardware-friendly structured pruning but enforces a fixed 50% sparsity per group, making it difficult to adapt pruning granularity to the wide variance in weight importance across vectors. Existing hierarchical schemes like Venom (HiNM) add vector-level pruning to exceed 50% overall sparsity, yet the remaining vectors still undergo uniform 2:4 pruning even when all four elements are important, discarding useful information. The paper addresses this by introducing a three-region partitioning with adaptive boundaries and a permutation strategy that aligns salient weights with the resulting sparsity structure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained weight matrix W ∈ R^{m×n} and a target sparsity ts, with the constraint that the input channel dimension must be divisible by 4 to satisfy NVIDIA Sparse Tensor Core hardware requirements. It produces a weight tensor partitioned into three disjoint regions — a dense (4:4) region, a 2:4 sparse region stored in compact format with N:M indices, and a fully pruned (0:4) region — along with per-tile vector indices that a custom dual-stream GPU kernel uses to route dense tiles to standard mma instructions and sparse tiles to mma.sp instructions.

## Method

FlexHiNM-GP partitions each layer's weight matrix into three regions (dense, 2:4 sparse, fully pruned) and jointly optimizes the two boundary parameters (vs, ps) via a concave objective that maximizes total preserved importance score under a fixed target sparsity. Gyro-Permutation, an iterative channel-reordering algorithm, alternates output-channel permutation (before vector pruning) and tile-wise input-channel permutation (before 2:4 pruning), using a coarse-to-fine sampling schedule, balanced K-means clustering, and Hungarian assignment to minimize pruning cost while maintaining cross-layer consistency. During gradual pruning, 2:4 masks are learned end-to-end via the Hard Concrete distribution with a composite loss (task + sparsity + hard-constraint terms) and annealed temperature, ensuring monotonic and irreversible sparsity progression. A custom GPU kernel executes the pruned model using two CUDA streams — one for sparse tiles via mma.sp and one for dense tiles via mma — merging partial outputs with atomicAdd without inter-stream synchronization.

## Results

On DeiT-Base (ImageNet-1K, V=64), FlexHiNM-GP achieves 81.13% top-1 accuracy at 75% sparsity and 79.46% at 80%, the best among all structured methods and within ~0.5% of unstructured pruning. On BERT-Base, FlexHiNM-GP reaches 88.55 F1 on SQuAD and 91.65% on SST-2 at 75% sparsity, closely matching unstructured (89.04 / 91.86%). On LLaMA-2 7B, FlexHiNM-GP improves average accuracy by +1.39% over HiNM-GP at 75% sparsity and maintains consistent gains at 87.5%, while narrowing the gap to unstructured pruning across all six reasoning benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexHiNM-GP consistently outperforms all structured baselines across every model family and sparsity level tested. Against OVW (Tan et al., 2022), which serves as the lower-bound special case (ps=0, pure vector pruning), FlexHiNM-GP gains approximately 2.5–4.5 points on DeiT-Base and 3–5 points on LLaMA-2 7B at 75% sparsity. Against HiNM-V (equivalent to Venom without permutation), the flexible three-region allocation and Gyro-Permutation yield a clear advantage that grows with sparsity: at 95% on DeiT-Base, FlexHiNM-GP reaches 61.43% versus 58.94% for HiNM-GP (the closest structured competitor). Against HiNM-GP (two-level HiNM with permutation but no dense region), FlexHiNM-GP adds +1.39% average on LLaMA-2 7B at 75% and +1.62% on DeiT-Base at 90% sparsity. The gap to unstructured pruning remains small (≤1% on BERT, ≤2% on DeiT at moderate sparsity) while retaining full Sparse Tensor Core hardware compatibility, which unstructured methods lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Venom's two-level hierarchical N:M (HiNM) sparsity by introducing a third dense region, creating a three-region partition (4:4, 2:4, 0:4) with adaptively searched boundaries. It builds on Pool & Yu's channel permutation for N:M sparsity by proposing Gyro-Permutation, which jointly optimizes output and input channel order across tiles in the hierarchical setting, and on the Hard Concrete distribution (Maddison et al.) to enable differentiable 2:4 mask learning during gradual pruning. The vector-level pruning mechanism is inherited from Tan et al.'s outer-vector-wise pruning (OVW), which serves as a special case (ps=0) of the proposed framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlexHiNM-GP offers a practical path to near-unstructured pruning accuracy while retaining full compatibility with NVIDIA Sparse Tensor Cores, which is critical for real-world deployment on Ampere/Ada GPUs. The adaptive boundary search and permutation-aware design address a genuine gap: fixed-ratio N:M pruning cannot accommodate the wide variance in weight importance across layers. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other N:M patterns (e.g., 4:8), how the custom GPU kernel scales to larger batch sizes or multi-GPU settings, and whether the permutation overhead becomes prohibitive for very large models beyond 7B parameters.
