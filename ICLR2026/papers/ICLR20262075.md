## Problem

Existing analyses of RLVR (Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards) identify that induced model changes are sparse but rely on magnitude-based metrics such as entropy and KL divergence, which produce nearly indistinguishable distributions for base and RLVR model outputs. This paper argues that the *direction* of token-level probability shifts—rather than their magnitude—is the critical lens for understanding which sparse updates drive reasoning gains, and that this direction can be captured by the signed log-probability difference Δlog p between the base and RLVR models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262075/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For test-time extrapolation, the method requires both a base LLM and its RLVR-trained counterpart; during autoregressive decoding of a math question, at each position where Δlog p falls below a threshold τ, the base model's sampled token is replaced by one drawn from an extrapolated policy that amplifies the RLVR direction, yielding a single generated reasoning response. For training-time reweighting, the method takes a base LLM and standard RLVR training data (math QA pairs) and produces a retrained model by modifying only the advantage term in DAPO's policy-gradient objective, with all other hyperparameters held fixed.

## Method

The core diagnostic is the signed token-level log-probability difference Δlog p = log π_RL(y_t|x, y_<t) − log π_Base(y_t|x, y_<t), which exhibits a clear bimodal pattern separating RLVR-favored from base-favored tokens and outperforms magnitude-only metrics in a selective token-replacement experiment (reaching RLVR accuracy with ~10% substitutions vs. 20–30% for divergence or entropy). Building on this, the paper proposes two strategies: (1) a test-time selective extrapolation that re-weights the RLVR distribution as π_Extra^γ ∝ π_RL · exp(γ·Δlog p), applied only at positions with large negative Δlog p to amplify reasoning-critical updates without further training; and (2) a training-time advantage reweighting that modifies DAPO's advantage as Ã_{i,t} = [1 + α(1 − π_θ_old(y_{i,t}|x, y_{i,<t}))]·Â_{i,t}, upweighting low-probability tokens that correspond to high-Δlog p positions. A gradient analysis (Lemma 3.1) shows that the ℓ1-norm of RLVR's policy gradient scales with (1 − π_θ), explaining why updates concentrate on rare tokens, and a theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) proves that extrapolating along the learned Δlog p direction strictly improves expected reward under natural policy gradient updates in a tabular softmax bandit setting.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, the proposed probability-aware advantage reweighting achieves 43.75 Avg@32 and 62.33 Pass@16 (averaged across AIME-24, AIME-25, and AMC), improving over DAPO's 42.12 and 57.86 respectively. On Qwen3-8B-Base, the method reaches 46.78 Avg@32 and 72.52 Pass@16 versus DAPO's 44.26 and 69.19. The gains are consistent across all three benchmarks, with the largest improvement on AIME-25 (18.54 vs. 17.6 Avg@32 on Qwen2.5-Math-7B) and AIME-24 (39.06 vs. 35.73). Test-time selective extrapolation along the Δlog p direction further adds 1–3 points over the RLVR model without additional training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DAPO (the primary RLVR baseline), the proposed reweighting method wins on every benchmark and both models: +1.63 Avg@32 and +4.47 Pass@16 on average for Qwen2.5-Math-7B, and +2.52 Avg@32 and +3.33 Pass@16 for Qwen3-8B-Base. Against the PPL reweighting of Deng et al. (2025), the method achieves higher Avg@32 on AIME-24 (39.06 vs. 35.63), AIME-25 (18.54 vs. 16.46), and the overall average (43.75 vs. 41.38), as well as higher Pass@16 on all three benchmarks. Against the Dominate reweighting of Yang et al. (2025b), the method wins on AIME-24 (39.06 vs. 36.35), AIME-25 (18.54 vs. 13.02), and the average (43.75 vs. 43.11) for Avg@32, and on AIME-24, AIME-25, and the average for Pass@16 (62.33 vs. 53.63), but loses on AMC Avg@32 (73.64 vs. 79.97), where Dominate's upweighting of high-probability tokens happens to help. The paper also shows that the method's gains generalize beyond math to the Minerva STEM benchmark (49.72 vs. 46.43 Avg@32 on Qwen2.5-Math-7B).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work analyzing RLVR's token-level effects (Wang et al. 2025b on high-entropy tokens, Huan et al. 2025 on KL divergence, Yang et al. 2025b on gradient norms) by arguing that prior analyses focused on the magnitude of distributional shifts while overlooking their direction. It builds directly on DAPO's policy-gradient recipe as the training baseline and on the token-replacement diagnostic from Meng et al. 2026, introducing the signed log-probability difference Δlog p as a directional metric. The test-time extrapolation method connects to reward-guided decoding (Khanov et al. 2024, Liu et al. 2024), while the training-time reweighting contrasts with PPL-based (Deng et al. 2025) and high-probability-upweighting (Yang et al. 2025b) strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The directional lens (Δlog p) offers a more precise diagnostic for understanding what RLVR actually changes, and the two derived techniques—test-time extrapolation (no additional training) and probability-aware advantage reweighting—provide practical, low-cost ways to squeeze additional reasoning accuracy from existing RLVR pipelines. However, the extrapolation method requires access to both the base and RLVR models, and the evaluation is concentrated on math reasoning benchmarks (AIME, AMC, Minerva), leaving open whether the directional principle generalizes to broader reasoning domains or non-math tasks. The modest 1–3 point gains and the need for careful threshold tuning also suggest the technique is a refinement rather than a paradigm shift.
