## Problem

State-of-the-art video diffusion models produce severe anatomical artifacts and unnatural joint articulations when generating complex human movements, because pixel-level reconstruction losses prioritize static appearance over underlying kinematic dynamics. Existing conditional approaches (2D keypoints, 3D poses projected to 2D) either depend on unavailable control signals or discard crucial 3D geometric information, failing to address the root cause of the problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263473/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (describing subject appearance, background, and action) and optionally SMPL motion parameters (shape β∈R¹⁰, pose θ∈R²⁴ˣ⁶, global orientation γ∈R⁶, root position v∈R³, 3D joint positions η∈R²⁴ˣ³) or a video clip as conditioning input. It produces a human-centric video (480×832×81 frames at 16 FPS for the 1.3B variant, or 708×1280×121 frames at 24 FPS for the 5B variant) and/or a temporally-aligned SMPL motion sequence. Motion tokens are generated at full temporal resolution (51 tokens per frame), unlike video tokens which undergo 4× temporal compression via the VAE, creating an asymmetric sequence length that MVS-RoPE must reconcile.

## Method

EchoMotion extends the DiT framework with a dual-branch architecture that jointly denoises video and SMPL motion tokens within a unified self-attention sequence, modeling the joint distribution p(x,m|y) rather than the video-only p(x|y). A key mechanism is MVS-RoPE, which assigns video tokens standard 3D positions (t,h,w) while motion tokens receive a scaled temporal index t/4 and a spatial diagonal offset (H+i, W+i), simultaneously enforcing the 4:1 temporal correspondence and preventing positional collisions between modalities. The two-stage training strategy first pretrains the motion branch independently on motion-only data, then unfreezes both branches for multi-task training across three paradigms (joint generation, motion-to-video, video-to-motion) with paradigm-specific classifier-free guidance. The backbone is Wan (1.3B or 5B), with the dual-modality blocks replacing either all (1.3B) or half (5B) of the original DiT layers, adding 51% more parameters but only 10.3% more FLOPs due to the small fraction of motion tokens in the sequence.

## Results

On the primary text-to-video benchmark, EchoMotion(Wan-5B) achieves 85.1 Human Anatomy, 99.3 Motion Smoothness, 81.0 Video Quality, and 81.6 Posture Plausibility, substantially outperforming the Wan-5B baseline (83.0, 98.9, 72.8, 68.9) and the video-only fine-tuning ablation (83.1, 98.7, 72.3, 70.2). The most dramatic gain is in Posture Plausibility (+12.7 over Wan-5B) and Video Quality (+8.2), while Aesthetic Quality remains unchanged at 58.3, confirming that kinematic improvements come without sacrificing visual fidelity. On the motion-to-video task, EchoMotion(Wan-5B) achieves 99.1 Motion Smoothness and 82.2 Pose Consistency, competitive with the larger VACE-14B and Wan2.2-Animate-14B models. For video-to-motion recovery on 3DPW, EchoMotion attains 59.8 PA-MPJPE, on par with the generative SOTA ChatHuman (58.7) and well ahead of ChatPose (81.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Wan-5B backbone, EchoMotion(Wan-5B) wins on every kinematic and quality metric: Human Anatomy 85.1 vs 83.0, Motion Smoothness 99.3 vs 98.9, Video Quality 81.0 vs 72.8, Prompt Following 81.5 vs 78.9, and Posture Plausibility 81.6 vs 68.9 (a 12.7-point margin). The video-only fine-tuning ablation (Video Tuning) confirms that simply adding human-centric video data yields only marginal gains (e.g., Posture Plausibility 70.2 vs 68.9), whereas joint motion-video modeling delivers the large jump to 81.6. On the motion-to-video task (Table 4), EchoMotion(Wan-5B) is competitive with the 14B-parameter VACE-14B and Wan2.2-Animate-14B, achieving 99.1 Motion Smoothness (best) and 82.2 Pose Consistency, though it trails slightly on Aesthetic Quality (59.2 vs 61.4) and Overall Quality (65.2 vs 68.9 for Wan2.2-Animate-14B). For video-to-motion recovery on 3DPW, EchoMotion (59.8 PA-MPJPE) is within 1.1 mm of the generative SOTA ChatHuman (58.7) and far ahead of ChatPose (81.9), though it does not match specialized reconstruction models like HMR2.0 (58.4). In motion generation quality (Table 6), EchoMotion(Wan-5B) surpasses MotionGPT on Pose Plausibility (80.8 vs 80.1) and Prompt Following (82.3 vs 72.3) but trails slightly on Motion Smoothness (92.4 vs 93.9). Against top-tier commercial models (Veo 3.1, Kling 2.5 Turbo), EchoMotion remains competitive on Motion Smoothness (99.1 vs 99.3/99.0) but trails on overall Video Quality (80.4 vs 90.2/90.4), an expected gap given the 5B parameter scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

EchoMotion extends the MMDiT (Esser et al., 2024) dual-stream attention paradigm from text-video to video-motion modeling, using Wan (Wan et al., 2025) as its backbone. It departs from prior conditional human video generation methods (Follow-Your-Pose, Animate Anyone, RealisDance) that inject 2D/3D pose signals as external control inputs, instead treating SMPL motion parameters as a co-equal modality denoised jointly with video latents. The MVS-RoPE positional encoding is a novel contribution specifically designed to resolve the 4:1 temporal compression mismatch between video VAE tokens and per-frame motion tokens, a problem absent in prior MMDiT-style architectures that only handle text and video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides strong evidence that the core bottleneck in human video generation is the modeling objective (pixel-only regression) rather than data scale, since joint motion-video training with the same data substantially outperforms video-only fine-tuning. The unified framework's ability to perform bidirectional cross-modal generation (motion-to-video and video-to-motion) with a single model without auxiliary control modules is practically valuable. Key limitations include single-person scope, dependence on a SMPL extraction pipeline for training data, and a visible gap to top commercial models (Veo 3.1, Kling 2.5) in overall video quality. Open questions include whether the joint-distribution approach generalizes to multi-person scenes and whether the SMPL parameterization is sufficient for capturing fine-grained hand and facial articulation.
