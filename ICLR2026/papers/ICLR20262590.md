## Problem

Existing semi-supervised federated learning (SSFL) methods assume that distributed unlabeled data share the same label space as the server's labeled data, but in realistic federated settings, clients inevitably collect samples from unseen classes (outliers) that degrade pseudo-label quality and cause misclassification at inference. This paper formally defines the open-set semi-supervised federated learning (OSSFL) problem and presents the first dedicated framework to address it, bridging the gap between centralized open-set semi-supervised learning (OSSL) and federated learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262590/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 from CIFAR-10/100, SVHN) distributed across M clients as unlabeled data, plus a small labeled set at the server (10–40 samples per seen class). The model produces two outputs simultaneously: a K-way class prediction for inliers (seen classes) and a 2K-dimensional OVA logit vector (K binary inlier/outlier scores) for outlier detection. Pseudo-labels are generated by the global model at the start of each communication round and held fixed during local client training to prevent degradation from noisy supervision.

## Method

FedOpenMatch is a multi-task framework with a shared CNN feature extractor (ResNet-18), a K-way inlier classifier, and a one-vs-all (OVA) outlier detector composed of K binary sub-classifiers. Three key mechanisms differentiate it from prior OSSL methods adapted to FL: (1) gradient stop blocks backpropagation from the OVA branch to the shared backbone, preventing optimization-direction interference between the two classifiers; (2) logit adjustment adds a class-prior correction term (ω·log π) to OVA logits to counteract the inherent inlier-outlier imbalance that causes the OVA classifier to reject most true inliers; (3) logit consistency regularization (LCR) enforces weak-strong augmentation consistency at the raw logit level rather than the softmax probability level, yielding a stronger training signal for both inlier and OVA branches. The server alternates between labeled-data training and client aggregation, while clients exploit unlabeled data via confidence-thresholded pseudo-labels for both the inlier and OVA losses.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, FedOpenMatch achieves the highest open-set accuracy across all eight data settings, with the largest gain of 14.33% over the best baseline (IOMatch) under the CIFAR-100@80@25@Dir(0.1) configuration (47.35% vs. 33.02%). On closed-set accuracy, FedOpenMatch reaches 52.96% under CIFAR-100@80@25@Dir(0.3), outperforming the strongest standard SSFL baseline FedLabel (47.81%) and the best OSSL baseline IOMatch (47.50%). On CIFAR-10 and SVHN, FedOpenMatch similarly leads on both metrics, with open-set accuracy of 62.26% (CIFAR-10@6/4@40@Dir(0.3)) and 71.87% (SVHN@6/4@40@Dir(0.3)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedOpenMatch decisively outperforms all four adapted OSSL baselines on open-set accuracy across every setting. The closest competitor, IOMatch, trails by 9.56–14.33% (e.g., 47.35% vs. 33.02% on CIFAR-100@80@25@Dir(0.1)); BDMatch is highly unstable with standard deviations up to 25.9 points; OpenMatch and SSB collapse to near-random performance (~2–6%), confirming that naively porting centralized OSSL methods to the federated setting fails. On closed-set accuracy (Table 2), FedOpenMatch also beats the strongest standard SSFL baselines: it surpasses FedLabel by 5.15% and FL² by 7.73% on CIFAR-100@80@25@Dir(0.3), and outperforms IOMatch by 2.46% under the same setting. Under the more heterogeneous Dir(0.1) partition, the margins over IOMatch narrow slightly (e.g., 56.54% vs. 50.39% closed-set on CIFAR-100@80@25) but remain consistent. On CIFAR-10 and SVHN, FedOpenMatch leads IOMatch by 0.61–4.33% on open-set accuracy and by 0.52–3.08% on closed-set accuracy, with the advantage widening under Dir(0.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: open-set semi-supervised learning (OSSL), which handles unseen classes in centralized settings via OVA classifiers and consistency regularization (OpenMatch, IOMatch, BDMatch), and semi-supervised federated learning (SSFL), which exploits distributed unlabeled data under label scarcity (SemiFL, FedLabel, FL²). The key departure is formalizing their intersection—OSSFL—as a new problem, where the strict separation of labeled (server) and unlabeled (client) data, combined with cross-client heterogeneity, breaks the assumptions under which centralized OSSL methods operate. The proposed FedOpenMatch adapts the OVA-based detection paradigm from OpenMatch but introduces three federated-specific mechanisms (gradient stop, logit adjustment, logit-level consistency) to address interference, class imbalance, and underutilization that are exacerbated in the distributed setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that naively porting OSSL algorithms to federated settings leads to near-random performance (OpenMatch and SSB score 1–6% open-set accuracy vs. ~17–22% for the labeled-only baseline), a critical negative result that warns against assuming centralized open-set techniques transfer to FL. The practical motivation is strong: real federated deployments (autonomous driving, medical imaging) inevitably encounter unseen classes in client data, and standard SSFL silently mislabels them. Open questions include whether the binary gradient-stop can be made adaptive, whether the approach scales to larger datasets (ImageNet-scale) and more complex modalities, and whether a theoretical analysis can explain why logit-level consistency outperforms probability-level consistency in this setting.
