## Problem

While behavioral diversity is known to improve exploration and robustness in multi-agent systems, there is no principled characterization of *which* reward structures actually make heterogeneous teams outperform the best homogeneous baseline. This paper asks: given a team reward built from nested aggregation operators over agent effort allocations, what curvature properties of those operators guarantee that specialization is rewarded?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262607/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The theoretical analysis takes an N×M effort allocation matrix A (continuous r_ij ∈ [0,1] or discrete r_ij ∈ {0,1}) and a pair of generalized aggregators (inner T_j mapping N agent efforts to a task score, outer U mapping M task scores to a scalar reward), producing the heterogeneity gain ΔR = R_het − R_hom. HetGPS additionally takes environment parameters θ of a differentiable PDec-POMDP and produces optimized θ values that maximize or minimize the empirical gain, alongside trained heterogeneous (independent-parameter) and homogeneous (shared-parameter) MAPPO policies.

## Method

The core theoretical insight is that the team reward R(A) = U(T_1(a_1), …, T_M(a_M)) decomposes into an inner task-level aggregator T and an outer aggregator U, and the sign of ΔR is governed by their Schur-convexity/concavity: strictly Schur-convex T with strictly increasing U yields ΔR > 0 (Thm. 3.1), while Schur-concave T yields ΔR = 0 (Thm. 3.2); for sum-form aggregators this collapses to a simple convexity test on the per-agent function g. Complementing the theory, HetGPS performs bilevel optimization over a differentiable simulator: at each iteration it rolls out both heterogeneous and homogeneous MAPPO teams, computes the empirical gain, and updates environment parameters θ via gradient ascent on the gain (backpropagating through time), while periodically retraining the agent policies via standard MARL. Across Softmax and Power-Sum aggregator families, HetGPS independently rediscovers the theoretically optimal curvature regime (convex inner, concave outer), validating both the theory and the algorithm.

## Results

In the N=M=4 matrix game, the empirical heterogeneity gain ΔR matches theoretical predictions exactly: for continuous allocations, U=min,T=max yields ΔR=0.690±0.026 (theory: 3/4) and U=mean,T=max yields ΔR=0.722±0.002 (theory: 3/4); for discrete allocations, U=min,T=max yields ΔR=1.0 and U=mean,T=max yields ΔR=0.75. In the embodied Multi-goal-capture environment (30M frames), the concave-convex pair U=min,T=max achieves ΔR=0.21±0.03 and U=mean,T=max achieves ΔR=0.12±0.09, while all other pairs yield ΔR≈0 or negative. HetGPS with both Softmax and Power-Sum parametrized aggregators independently converges to the theoretically optimal regime (inner Schur-convex, outer Schur-concave), validating the curvature theory in a learned, bilevel-optimization setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing MARL methods; instead, the central comparison is between neurally heterogeneous teams (independent parameters) and neurally homogeneous teams (shared parameters) under each of the nine aggregator reward structures. The theory predicts—and the experiments confirm—that heterogeneity is strictly advantageous only when the inner aggregator is Schur-convex (T=max) and the outer aggregator is Schur-concave (U=min or U=mean). In the N=M=4 continuous matrix game, the heterogeneous team outperforms the homogeneous team by 0.69–0.72 reward units under these two pairs, while all other seven pairs show ΔR≈0 (within ±0.01). In the embodied Multi-goal-capture and 2v2 Tag environments, the same concave-convex pairs produce positive gains (0.21 and 0.12 for Multi-goal-capture; positive for Tag), while convex-convex pairs (U=max, T=max) actually yield negative empirical ΔR (−0.08±0.06) because heterogeneous agents learn the required uniform coordination more slowly. The HetGPS algorithm, which has no access to the theoretical curvature analysis, independently discovers the same optimal reward regime (maximizing inner temperature τ₁, minimizing outer temperature τ₂), providing a cross-validation between theory and algorithmic search.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of convex analysis and cooperative MARL. While prior work on behavioral heterogeneity (Bettini et al., 2023; 2025) focuses on *how* to induce diversity (distinct policies, role conditioning, intrinsic rewards), this paper addresses the orthogonal question of *when* diversity is actually rewarded, providing the first principled characterization via Schur-convexity/concavity of nested aggregation operators. The HetGPS algorithm extends the environment co-design paradigm of PAIRED (Dennis et al., 2020) by replacing RL-based environment optimization with direct gradient backpropagation through a differentiable simulator, and by operating on multi-agent teams rather than single protagonist-antagonist pairs. The theoretical tools draw on majorization theory (Roberts & Varberg, 1974; Peajcariaac & Tong, 1992) applied to a novel double-aggregator reward structure that unifies settings from Colonel Blotto, level-based foraging, and embodied navigation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The curvature test (convex inner + concave outer aggregators favor heterogeneity) gives MARL practitioners a concrete, computable design principle for reward functions, turning the choice between parameter sharing and distinct policies from an ad-hoc heuristic into a principled decision. HetGPS provides an automated tool for discovering reward structures that favor or suppress heterogeneity, useful for curriculum design and environment co-design. A key open question is how transition dynamics interact with reward curvature in long-horizon embodied settings, where the theory provides necessary but not sufficient conditions. The finding that richer observations eliminate the empirical heterogeneity gain (by enabling behaviorally distinct actions from identical networks) has practical implications for observation design in multi-agent systems.
