## Problem

End-to-end VLA models that jointly learn 2D video forecasting and 3D action prediction suffer from objective misalignment and cannot exploit the vast pool of action-free human/web videos. Prior video-prediction-based approaches (e.g., VPP, Vidar) treat the inverse dynamics component as an afterthought, leaving it as a bottleneck that limits overall policy performance despite a powerful forward model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262530/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a 256×256 RGB observation (single or multi-view: third-person + wrist camera) and a natural-language instruction, and outputs a 7-dimensional robot end-effector action sequence. Internally, the GFDM produces 16-frame future latent predictions via a single denoising step (~86 ms), the GIDM emits discrete latent action tokens from a 128-entry VQ codebook, and a 30M-parameter DiT-B action adapter (10 sampling steps) decodes these into executable commands. Total inference latency is approximately 153 ms on an RTX 4090.

## Method

DeFI decouples robot learning into two independently pretrained modules that are later coupled for end-to-end fine-tuning. The General Forward Dynamics Model (GFDM) is a Stable Video Diffusion backbone with CLIP text conditioning, pretrained on a mixture of human videos (Ego4D, Something-Something-v2) and robot demonstrations (Open X-Embodiment, CALVIN) to predict instruction-conditioned future frames; at inference it is frozen and restricted to a single denoising step to extract motion-relevant latent features cheaply. The General Inverse Dynamics Model (GIDM) is a spatial-temporal Transformer with causal temporal masks and a VQ-VAE codebook, pretrained in a fully self-supervised manner: given DINOv2-encoded frame pairs separated by ~1 s plus T5 instruction embeddings, it infers discrete latent action tokens that are then used by a Transformer decoder to reconstruct the future DINO features, with the VQ bottleneck preventing future-state leakage. During coupled fine-tuning, the frozen GFDM's latent outputs are projected via an MLP into the GIDM's input space, the GIDM is updated to interpret these aligned latents, and a diffusion-based action adapter translates the resulting latent actions into robot commands—allowing the three objectives (future prediction, action inversion, low-level control) to be jointly aligned with only a small amount of robot data.

## Results

On CALVIN ABC-D (multi-view), DeFI achieves an average task length of 4.51, surpassing the previous best VPP (4.33) by 4.2% and Seer (4.28) by 5.4%. On SimplerEnv-Fractal (Google Robot), DeFI reaches 51.2% average success rate under visual matching and 45.4% under variant aggregation, outperforming TraceVLA (42.0%/45.0%) and OpenVLA (27.7%/39.8%). In real-world Franka Panda experiments across 8 tasks, DeFI achieves 81.3% average success rate, substantially exceeding Diffusion Policy (48.2%), OpenVLA (43.8%), and Octo-Base (34.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeFI outperforms all 15 baselines across all three evaluation settings. On CALVIN ABC-D (multi-view), DeFI (4.51) beats the closest competitors VPP (4.33, +0.18) and Seer (4.28, +0.23), both of which integrate video prediction and action reasoning in a single end-to-end VLA framework, demonstrating that decoupled pretraining of forward and inverse dynamics is more effective than joint training. DeFI also surpasses UniVLA (3.80, third-view) which extracts latent actions as pseudo-labels for VLA pretraining, and CLOVER (3.53) and SuSIE (2.69) which use video generation predictions as input to a separate controller. On SimplerEnv-Fractal, DeFI leads TraceVLA by 9.2 points on visual matching (51.2% vs. 42.0%) and matches it on variant aggregation (45.4% vs. 45.0%), while substantially outperforming OpenVLA (27.7%) and Octo-Base (16.8%). In real-world Franka experiments, DeFI (81.3%) exceeds Diffusion Policy (48.2%) by 33.1 points, OpenVLA (43.8%) by 37.5 points, and Octo-Base (34.4%) by 46.9 points, with particularly large gains on complex long-horizon tasks like 'Pour Water' (75% vs. 35–45% for baselines).

## Relation to Prior Work

DeFI builds on two distinct lineages: (1) video diffusion models (Stable Video Diffusion) as forward dynamics predictors, following SuSIE, CLOVER, VPP, and VidMan, and (2) latent action pretraining via VQ-VAE from LAPA (Ye et al., 2024) and UniVLA for inverse dynamics. The key departure is from both the end-to-end coupled VLA paradigm (Seer, UP-VLA, DreamVLA), which the authors argue suffers from 2D/3D objective misalignment, and from video-as-policy approaches (VPP, Vidar) that treat inverse dynamics as a lightweight afterthought. DeFI's conceptual contribution is elevating inverse dynamics pretraining to equal standing with forward dynamics pretraining, each with its own dedicated data source and self-supervised objective, before coupling them only at fine-tuning time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that large-scale action-free human videos (Ego4D, Something-Something-v2) can be productively exploited for robot policy learning when forward and inverse dynamics are pretrained separately, addressing a core bottleneck in VLA scaling. The ablation showing that jointly fine-tuning all modules actually degrades performance (4.40 vs 4.51) is a useful negative result suggesting that frozen world-model representations provide a more stable input distribution for action inference. Limitations include the absence of an LLM for language interaction, domain-shift sensitivity when the frozen GFDM was pretrained on real-world data but evaluated in simulation (SimplerEnv), and the fact that the scaling curve for human video data shows no saturation, leaving open how much further gains are possible. Integrating the decoupled dynamics modules with a language model for embodied reasoning is identified as the natural next step.
