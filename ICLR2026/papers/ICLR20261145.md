## Problem

Generalizable 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) methods that infer 3D representations feed-forward from sparse references have been limited to static, distractor-free scenes. In real-world capture, transient objects (vehicles, pedestrians) disrupt 3D multi-view consistency during training and project as artifacts into reconstructed 3D during inference, yet no prior work addresses this challenge in the generalizable (scene-agnostic, no per-scene optimization) setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261145/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N=4 multi-view reference RGB images (downsampled to 192×256) with known camera extrinsics and intrinsics, plus a scene-images pool of K=8 adjacent views for the two-stage inference. It outputs a 3DGS representation (anisotropic Gaussian primitives with position, opacity, covariance, and SH color), rendered novel-view images, and per-pixel distractor masks. Notably, the distractor masks are predicted feed-forward without any mask supervision, and the inference stage re-selects references from the pool without increasing GPU memory beyond the N-reference budget.

## Method

DGGS's core insight is that re-rendered reference views from the inferred 3DGS remain stable and accurate in non-distractor regions, providing a reliable geometric signal for mask prediction. During training, a Reference-based Mask Prediction module warps non-distractor masks from re-rendered references into the query view (using rendered depth and camera geometry), fuses them via intersection, and filters the initial robust mask M_Rob to remove misclassified static regions; a Mask Refinement module then decouples true distractor from disparity-induced errors, fills distractor regions with pre-trained Entity Segmentation, and adds an auxiliary loss supervising occluded query regions visible only from references. During inference, a two-stage framework first scores all K pool images by their predicted distractor mask size to re-select the N cleanest references, then applies Distractor Pruning to remove Gaussian primitives whose projected positions fall within residual distractor regions, suppressing ghosting artifacts without breaking the pixel-to-Gaussian mapping.

## Results

On the five-scene mean (RobustNeRF + On-the-go), DGGS achieves PSNR 21.74, SSIM 0.758, and LPIPS 0.237, outperforming the best re-trained distractor-free baseline (Mvsplat+SLS at PSNR 19.29) by 2.45 dB and the pre-trained Mvsplat* (PSNR 20.03) by 1.71 dB. The training-only variant DGGS-TR already reaches PSNR 21.02, surpassing all re-trained baselines by at least 1.7 dB. On the more challenging Mountain scene, DGGS-TR achieves PSNR 16.37 versus 15.14 for the best baseline (SLS), confirming gains in high-distractor outdoor settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGGS outperforms all nine baselines across every scene and metric. Against re-trained generalizable 3DGS baselines (Mvsplat, Pixelsplat), DGGS gains 5.5–6.3 dB PSNR, demonstrating that distractor-aware training is essential for stable feed-forward reconstruction. Among scene-specific distractor-free methods adapted to the generalizable setting, SLS (PSNR 19.29) and NeRF-HuGS (PSNR 19.18) are the strongest, but both suffer from over-aggressive distractor prediction that degrades reconstruction fidelity; DGGS-TR surpasses them by 1.7–1.8 dB through its reference-based mask filtering. Compared to pre-trained models (Mvsplat* at 20.03, Pixelsplat* at 20.10), DGGS-TR already leads by ~1 dB, and the full DGGS with its two-stage inference extends this to 1.7 dB, showing that reference scoring and distractor pruning provide meaningful additional gains beyond training alone. On the harder Mountain scene (Table 12), DGGS-TR achieves PSNR 16.37 versus 15.14 for SLS and 13.73 for vanilla Mvsplat, confirming robustness in high-distractor outdoor conditions where baselines degrade most severely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: generalizable (feed-forward) 3D Gaussian Splatting (MVSplat, PixelSplat, MVSgaussian) and scene-specific distractor-free reconstruction (RobustNeRF, NeRF-HuGS, SLS, On-the-go). It builds directly on MVSplat as the baseline generalizable 3DGS framework and on RobustNeRF's heuristic residual-based mask as the starting point for distractor identification. The key conceptual departure is the reference-based mask prediction paradigm: exploiting the observation that non-distractor regions re-render stably from references to filter misclassified query regions, a mechanism absent from both the scene-specific (which relies on iterative optimization or scene-level priors like Colmap) and the generalizable (which assumes clean static scenes) literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formally define and address distractor-free generalizable 3DGS, a practically important gap given that real-world mobile captures inevitably contain transient objects. The reference-based 3D consistency paradigm for mask prediction is a transferable idea that could inform other feed-forward reconstruction settings. Key limitations include performance degradation under extensive common occlusions (no generative completion), efficiency cost from the two-stage inference and segmentation, and reliance on a pre-trained entity segmentation model. Open questions include whether the reference-based paradigm extends to dynamic (non-transient) scenes and whether generative models can fill the occluded-region gap.
