## Problem

Learning-based 3D scene generators overfit to indoor CAD datasets (e.g., 3D-FRONT), failing on out-of-distribution layouts such as outdoor scenes and small-on-large support relations, while LLM-based planners produce semantically diverse but physically implausible placements that violate support, collision, and orientation constraints. No existing training-free method simultaneously preserves open-ended layout diversity, grounds it in visual spatial priors, and enforces mesh-level physical plausibility during generation.

## Input / Output

Given a short or detailed text prompt, Scenethesis retrieves category-matched 3D meshes from a curated Objaverse subset and an HDR environment map, then produces a fully posed interactive scene where each object carries a 5-DoF configuration (uniform scale, azimuthal upright rotation, and 3D translation) organized in a parent-child scene graph rooted at the ground. Wall-mounted and background elements are supplied by the environment map rather than per-object assets, and the pipeline instantiates only LLM-planned categories (not every object hallucinated by the image generator), with a self-check agent triggering re-planning if a planned category is missed by the detector.

## Method

Scenethesis is a training-free, four-stage agentic pipeline: (1) GPT-5 drafts a coarse spatial hierarchy with an anchor object; (2) a vision module generates a guidance image, segments objects via Grounded-SAM, estimates monocular depth with DepthPro to initialize 5-DoF poses, and retrieves 3D assets via CLIP; (3) a physics-aware optimizer iteratively refines each object's pose using RoMa dense semantic correspondences for 2D/3D reprojection alignment, while simultaneously enforcing SDF-based collision constraints (translation push-out and multi-cluster scale shrinkage) and stability constraints (exponential bottom-surface contact loss) computed on 400 uniformly sampled mesh surface points rather than coarse 3D bounding boxes; (4) a GPT-5 judge scores object category accuracy, orientation alignment, and overall spatial coherence, triggering targeted re-planning if any metric falls below threshold. The key distinction from prior work is that physics constraints are enforced in-loop during pose optimization (not as post-hoc correction) and operate at mesh-surface granularity, enabling fine-grained relations like inside/under/on that 3DBB approximations cannot represent.

## Results

On indoor scenes, Scenethesis achieves a scene-level collision rate of 6% and scene-level instability rate of 16.67%, compared to 21%/31.58% for the closest baseline (Holodeck) and 73–75%/78–81% for LLM-only planners (LayoutGPT, SceneTeller). In text-image alignment, Scenethesis attains the highest CLIP (30.71), BLIP (77.17), and VQA (0.8269) scores among all baselines. For interactivity, Scenethesis reaches 0.94 reachability and 0.96 walkability, matching or exceeding all competitors. On outdoor scenes, the method maintains 3.3% scene collision and 6.7% scene instability, confirming robustness beyond indoor distributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Scenethesis outperforms all eight baselines on every reported metric. The closest competitor is Holodeck, which also uses 3D bounding-box collision checks and predefined support locations; Scenethesis reduces scene-level collision from 21% to 6% and scene-level instability from 31.58% to 16.67% while improving reachability from 0.90 to 0.94. LLM-only planners (LayoutGPT, SceneTeller, DiffuScene) suffer the worst physical plausibility (73–75% scene collision, 78–83% scene instability) because they rely on coarse 3DBB proxies and lack mesh-level contact reasoning. Image-conditioned methods (MIDI, Digital Cousins) also fail on physical plausibility (88–91% scene instability) due to hallucinated geometry and absence of explicit physics constraints. LayoutVLM, which uses VLM-guided differentiable optimization with IoU-based collision loss, still exhibits 71.4% scene instability because IoU approximation cannot model small-object stability on large furniture. IDesign achieves moderate collision rates (6.51% object-level) but retains 68.88% scene instability due to its 3DBB-granularity post-hoc cleanup. In text-image alignment (Table 1), Scenethesis leads all baselines by a wide margin on BLIP (77.17 vs. 51.99 for SceneTeller) and VQA (0.8269 vs. 0.8052 for LayoutGPT), confirming that the vision-guided refinement stage improves prompt adherence beyond what LLM-only planning achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

Scenethesis sits at the intersection of LLM-based scene planning (Holodeck, LayoutGPT, SceneTeller), learning-based layout generation (DiffuScene, MIDI), and physics-aware scene synthesis (CAST, Atlas3D). It departs from LLM-only planners by grounding coarse language plans with vision foundation models (Grounded-SAM, DepthPro, RoMa) for metric spatial refinement, and from post-hoc physics correction (CAST) by enforcing SDF-based contact/support constraints in-loop during pose optimization. The training-free, agentic pipeline with a GPT-5 judge-and-repair loop distinguishes it from both trained layout generators (which inherit 3D-FRONT indoor biases) and monolithic NeRF/3DGS approaches that lack per-object editability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: indoor-trained layout generators fail on outdoor scenes and long-tail spatial relations (inside, under, small-on-large), while LLM planners produce physically implausible placements. By combining retrieval-based assets with in-loop SDF physics, Scenethesis achieves near-zero collision rates (0.8% vs. 6–35% for baselines) without any scene-level training, and the generated scenes serve as effective augmentation data for feed-forward generators (improving MIDI-3D's OOD performance on BlendSwap). Key limitations include dependence on the Objaverse retrieval database (no articulated objects), degradation under heavy occlusion, and the inability to handle wall-mounted or background elements beyond environment maps. Open questions include whether generative 3D can eventually replace retrieval for asset diversity, and whether the agentic judge-and-repair loop scales to larger, more complex scenes.
