## Problem

Physics-constrained generative modeling faces an inherent conflict: optimizing for PDE residual accuracy degrades distributional fidelity and vice versa, yet existing methods either enforce constraints at inference time (incurring 10×+ slowdown) or use manually weighted loss terms that fail to resolve gradient conflicts. This paper is the first to explicitly frame the physics-vs-distribution trade-off as a Pareto optimization problem and to show that the two objectives produce systematically opposing gradients, requiring a principled resolution mechanism rather than ad-hoc hyperparameter tuning.

## Input / Output

The model takes Gaussian noise N(0,I) as its initial state and, for conditional benchmarks, a small conditioning vector (Reynolds number for Kolmogorov flow; Mach number, mean angle of attack, pitching amplitude, and reduced frequency for dynamic stall) injected via adaLN-Zero blocks in the DiT backbone. Outputs are full spatio-temporal PDE solution fields at 64×64 (Darcy: pressure and permeability) or 128×128 (Kolmogorov: two velocity components; Dynamic Stall: six fields including pressure, temperature, density, skin friction, and tangential velocity gradients). A stochastic sampler variant optionally resamples Gaussian noise during early integration steps (t < t*), adding distributional diversity without altering the network architecture.

## Method

PBFM builds on a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) flow matching backbone and introduces three key modifications. First, it replaces the standard weighted sum of flow-matching and residual losses with ConFIG conflict-free gradient updates, which project each gradient onto the orthogonal complement of the other and compose unit vectors to guarantee simultaneous descent on both objectives without manual weight tuning. Second, it mitigates Jensen's gap by unrolling: during training, the intermediate sample at time t is integrated forward to t=1 over n ODE steps (with a curriculum increasing n), so the PDE residual is evaluated on a multi-step prediction rather than a single-step approximation, with a t^p scaling factor down-weighting less reliable early-time residuals. Third, at inference it offers a stochastic sampler that, for t < t*, jumps to t=1 in one step and returns to t+dt with fresh Gaussian noise, increasing sample diversity and improving distributional metrics over the purely deterministic ODE integrator. Time is sampled from a logit-normal distribution during training, and σ_min is tuned per problem to balance noise-induced residual floor against generative quality.

## Results

On the Darcy flow benchmark, PBFM achieves a physical residual MSE of 0.838 with a Wasserstein distance of 0.0138 (×10⁻²) and inference time of 0.101 s at 20 NFE, advancing the Pareto front over all baselines. On Kolmogorov flow, PBFM attains the lowest residual (1.362×10⁻¹) and best distributional metrics (WD 1.222×10⁻¹, JS 7.440×10⁻²) among all methods. On the most complex dynamic stall benchmark (6 fields, 2 PDE constraints), PBFM reduces the residual by 32× over FM-OT (0.339×10⁻⁶ vs 11.02×10⁻⁶) while maintaining the best Wasserstein distance (1.814×10⁻⁴) and inference speed (60.47 ms), compared to PCFM's 3906 ms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PBFM dominates CoCoGen and D-Flow on Darcy flow, achieving lower residuals (0.838 vs 1.320 and 2.286) and better Wasserstein distances (0.138 vs 0.249 and 0.147) while being 73× and 31× faster in inference. Against PIDM-ME, PBFM trades a higher residual (0.838 vs 0.022) for a dramatically better distribution (WD 0.138 vs 3.103), confirming the Pareto trade-off the paper targets. On dynamic stall, PBFM outperforms DiffusionPDE and D-Flow on every metric except inference time (which is comparable), reducing the residual by ~33× and the Wasserstein distance by ~30%. The only method that beats PBFM on residual is PCFM (0.143 vs 0.339×10⁻⁶), but PCFM sacrifices distributional fidelity (WD 4.013 vs 1.814×10⁻⁴, SMSE 7.674 vs 0.874×10⁻⁵) and requires ~65× more inference time (3906 ms vs 60.47 ms) due to iterative correction. ECI is limited to boundary-condition enforcement only and cannot handle the nonlinear PDE residual, making it incomparable on the primary physics metric. Overall, PBFM is the only method that simultaneously achieves competitive residuals, best-in-class distributional accuracy, and standard flow-matching inference speed across all three benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

PBFM sits at the intersection of three research threads: flow matching as a generative framework (Lipman et al., 2023), conflict-free multi-task gradient optimization via ConFIG (Liu et al., 2025a), and stochastic noise-resampling samplers inspired by ECI (Cheng et al., 2025). Unlike prior physics-constrained generative methods (PIDM, CoCoGen, DiffusionPDE, D-Flow, PCFM) that either enforce constraints at inference time at significant computational cost or rely on manually tuned loss weights at training time, PBFM integrates physical residuals into training through conflict-free gradient composition and unrolling to mitigate Jensen's gap. The paper's distinctive conceptual contribution is explicitly framing the physics-vs-distribution tension as a Pareto optimization problem, positioning its method as advancing the Pareto front rather than simply minimizing one objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: for the dynamic stall benchmark, PBFM reduces a 76-minute CPU simulation to 0.2 seconds on GPU while maintaining physical consistency, making large-scale uncertainty quantification for aerospace applications (helicopter blades, wind turbines) feasible. The key limitation is increased training cost (up to 3× memory, 2.5× time with 4-step unrolling), though inference speed is unaffected. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 3D PDEs, whether the Pareto-optimal framing generalizes to problems with many more than two physical constraints, and whether the σ_min sensitivity (requiring σ_min ≲ 3×10⁻⁴ for complex flows) limits applicability to problems where near-perfect reconstruction is needed.
