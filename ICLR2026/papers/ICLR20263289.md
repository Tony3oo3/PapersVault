## Problem

In diffusion-based autoregressive video generation, the conditioning signal—internal representations of history frames—remains underexplored. While stronger condition representations consistently improve text- and class-conditioned generation, it is unclear whether better history frame representations can similarly improve VideoAR, and whether such gains are achievable without relying on external vision foundation models (VFMs) that incur training costs and suffer from out-of-distribution issues.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263289/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes clean context frames (5 frames for Kinetics-600 prediction; none for UCF-101 unconditional; class labels for UCF-101 conditional) encoded in a VAE latent space (4×8×8 compression at 128×128 for Kinetics; 32×32 per-frame compression at 256×256 for UCF-101) and autoregressively generates future frames (11 or 16 frames respectively). During training, the same video clip is duplicated as both clean history frames and noised future frames, with history frames additionally subjected to random tube masking for the auxiliary reconstruction objective.

## Method

MiMo (Masked History Modeling) unifies diffusion-based VideoAR with self-supervised history representation learning in a single training process without VFMs. It follows Complete Teacher Forcing, exposing clean history frames alongside independently noised future frames, and applies a random tube mask to history frame tokens. The model is trained with a dual objective: a standard v-prediction (or flow-matching) diffusion loss for denoising future frames, plus a masked reconstruction loss where a lightweight 4-block decoder predicts the masked tokens of the current and next frames from intermediate-layer history features. This dual use of history frames—as denoising conditions and as self-supervised learning input—encourages the model to develop predictive, semantically aligned history representations. At inference, the decoder is discarded and generation proceeds via standard iterative denoising with KV cache, incurring no additional cost.

## Results

On Kinetics-600 video prediction, MiMo-XL achieves an FVD of 8.3, setting a new SOTA among AR methods and outperforming DFoT-XL (11.1) with the same VAE. On UCF-101 unconditional generation, MiMo-XL reaches FVD 240, surpassing the previous AR leader FAR-XL (279) by nearly 40 points using the same DC-AE tokenizer. On UCF-101 class-conditional generation, MiMo-XL achieves FVD 98, beating FAR-XL (108) and ACDiT-XL (111). The VAE reconstruction FVD upper bounds are 3.7 (Kinetics) and 15 (UCF-101), indicating MiMo approaches the tokenizer's representational limit on Kinetics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MiMo-XL achieves the best FVD among all AR methods across all three tasks. On Kinetics-600 prediction, MiMo (8.3) outperforms DFoT-XL (11.1) and MAGI-XL (11.5) despite sharing the same VAE, directly attributing the gain to improved history representations. On UCF-101 unconditional generation, MiMo (240) beats FAR-XL (279) by 39 FVD points with the same DC-AE tokenizer, and outperforms MAGI-XL (298) by 58 points despite both using Complete Teacher Forcing. On UCF-101 class-conditional generation, MiMo (98) surpasses FAR-XL (108) and ACDiT-XL (111), the latter also using CTF, confirming consistent gains from masked history modeling. However, MiMo still trails the best non-AR methods: MAGVITv2 achieves FVD 4.3 on Kinetics prediction and 58 on UCF-101 conditional, indicating that bidirectional non-AR generation retains an advantage over causal AR modeling. The paper also shows MiMo is complementary to VFM-based methods like REPA: combining MiMo with REPA-Both yields FVD 34.133 vs. 36.601 (MiMo alone) and 36.542 (REPA-Both alone) on Kinetics.

## Relation to Prior Work

MiMo builds directly on ACDiT's Complete Teacher Forcing paradigm for diffusion-based VideoAR, which conditions on clean history frames, and integrates the masked autoencoder objective from MAE/VideoMAE as an auxiliary loss applied specifically to those clean history frames. It departs from REPA-style approaches (Yu et al., 2024) that require external vision foundation models for representation alignment, and from masked diffusion methods (Gao et al., 2023; Wei et al., 2023) that apply masking to noisy denoising inputs. The conceptual lineage draws from text-conditioned generation, where stronger condition encoders (T5, LLMs) consistently improve output quality, and extends this principle to the history-frame conditioning signal in autoregressive video modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies history representation quality as a previously underexplored bottleneck in VideoAR and demonstrates that a simple self-supervised masked modeling objective on clean history frames can match or exceed VFM-based representation alignment (REPA) without any external pretrained model. This is practically important because VFMs may not be available or reliable for out-of-distribution video domains, and the approach requires no architectural changes beyond a lightweight decoder. Limitations include evaluation only on small-scale datasets (Kinetics-600 at 128×128, UCF-101 at 256×256) and frame-level rather than patch-level autoregression; open questions include whether the benefit persists at text-to-video scale, whether other pretraining objectives (DINOv2, V-JEPA) yield complementary gains, and whether the approach transfers to longer-horizon generation where history context grows substantially.
