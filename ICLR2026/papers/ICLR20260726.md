## Problem

Existing computational world models for spatial environments rely on long sequential trajectories or fixed hand-designed circuitry to build maps, making them unable to integrate disjoint episodic experiences or rapidly adapt to structural changes. Neuroscience evidence shows that the medial temporal lobe constructs spatial maps by integrating overlapping episodic memories into relational networks, but no prior computational model captures this mechanism. The paper asks whether a neural network can rapidly construct a coherent spatial map from sparse, disjoint one-step transitions without sequential observations.

## Input / Output

ESWM takes an unordered memory bank M of one-step transitions (s_s, a, s_e) that is disjoint, spanning, and minimal, plus a single partially masked query transition q not present in M, and predicts the masked component. In grid environments, states are integer observations (or 6-bit binary codes) and actions are discrete (6-way); in MiniGrid, states are 5×5 egocentric colored-object views with 3 egocentric actions; in ProcThor, states are 224×224 RGB first-person images and actions are continuous 3D vectors [Δx, Δy, Δθ]. The model also outputs an 'I don't know' classification for queries involving unobserved regions, enabling uncertainty-aware exploration and navigation.

## Method

ESWM is a meta-trained function f(M, q) → q* that predicts the masked element of an unseen transition given a set of disjoint episodic memories. Each transition is projected into a shared embedding space (averaging s_s, a, s_e projections) to form a single token; the transformer encoder processes the concatenated memory-bank and query tokens, and three linear heads read out source-state, action, and end-state predictions. Critically, the memory bank is an unordered set rather than a sequence, and the model infers both structural layout and sensory observations jointly from episodic transitions without encoding a fixed structural template in its weights—unlike TEM, which factorizes structure into recurrent weights. Meta-training randomizes state-to-location mappings and environment configurations across trials (over 10³³ possible rooms), preventing memorization and forcing generalizable spatial reasoning. The transformer's self-attention mechanism, analogous to content-addressable memory, is essential: LSTM and Mamba variants overfit and fail to generalize, while the transformer's latent space spontaneously forms a geometric map mirroring the environment's topology, with obstacles creating localized discontinuities.

## Results

In the Random Wall environment, ESWM-T-4L achieves 87.5% source-state, 88.8% action, and 90.3% end-state prediction accuracy on unseen transitions, while correctly classifying 82.7% of unsolvable queries. For navigation, ESWM achieves 96.8% success rate and 99.2% path optimality using only minimal spanning episodic memories (4× fewer than EPN). After introducing new obstacles, ESWM maintains 93% navigation success without retraining, compared to 72% for EPN and 56% for a baseline RL agent. ISOMAP analysis confirms the latent space mirrors the environment's geometry with R²=0.89 correlation between latent and physical path lengths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESWM-T substantially outperforms the sequence-based TEM-T (Whittington et al., 2022) in both Open Arena and Random Wall; TEM-T fails entirely on Random Wall (varying structures) while ESWM-T generalizes, and TEM-T's performance degrades with model size whereas ESWM-T improves. Against EPN (Ritter et al., 2020), ESWM achieves 96.8% navigation success vs. ~78.8% (18% improvement) and 99.2% path optimality vs. ~78.2% (21% improvement), while also exploring 16.8% more unique states and reaching 96.48% of oracle exploration performance. In the adaptability test (new obstacles added post-training), ESWM retains 93% navigation success compared to EPN's 72% and a single-environment RL baseline's 56%. Hand-engineered memory-lookup mechanisms (Coda-Forno et al., 2022a) fail on the transition inference task entirely, confirming the need for learnable attention-based integration. Notably, ESWM requires 4× fewer memories than EPN to achieve its navigation performance, highlighting the efficiency of its episodic memory representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

ESWM departs from the dominant paradigm of sequence-based world models (TEM, GTM-SM, recurrent predictive models) that require continuous trajectories and encode structural knowledge in network weights. While TEM and GTM-SM factorize a fixed structural template (e.g., a 2D grid) from sensory observations, ESWM infers both structure and sensory predictions jointly from an unordered set of disjoint one-step transitions, with no presupposed topology. The transformer architecture is repurposed here as a learnable content-addressable memory over episodic transitions, drawing on the neuroscience of hippocampal relational memory (Eichenbaum, McKenzie) but without the recurrent state update that characterizes prior MTL-inspired models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a coherent spatial map—verified via ISOMAP and geodesic distance correlation—emerges in a transformer's latent space purely from sparse, non-sequential episodic memories, enabling near-optimal zero-shot exploration and navigation without task-specific training. The finding that the minimality constraint during training yields better out-of-distribution generalization than denser memory banks is a practical insight for data-efficient world modeling. Key limitations include reliance on procedurally generated, highly controllable environments (hexagonal grids, MiniGrid, ProcThor) and the absence of real-world robotic validation; extending ESWM to natural, high-dimensional sensory streams with partial observability and dynamic agents remains an open challenge.
