## Problem

Prior end-to-end OMR systems were restricted to piano, monophonic, or single-system scores, and no model had been trained to output ABC notation from images. The field also lacked a large-scale, diverse training dataset and a unified evaluation protocol, making it difficult to assess true generalization across the wide variety of typeset scores encountered in the wild.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264949/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts full-page or multi-page RGB typeset score images of arbitrary height; each image is segmented into overlapping strips (aspect ratio ≤ 1:4), further split into four 448×448 patches per segment, and fed to a frozen vision encoder. The output is an autoregressively generated ABC 2.1 notation string, where all textual elements (titles, lyrics, annotations) are collapsed into a single <|text|> token, and line breaks are explicitly marked every five bars. Inference uses beam search (beam size 10, max length 2048, repetition penalty 1.1).

## Method

Legato follows the multimodal Llama architecture: a frozen 836M-parameter Llama-3.2-11B-Vision encoder produces latent embeddings that serve as cross-attention keys/values for a 101M-parameter transformer decoder (18 layers, cross-attention at only 4 of 18 layers) that autoregressively generates ABC tokens. A key design choice is a data-driven BPE tokenizer (vocabulary 4097) trained on the ABC corpus, which learns composite musical concepts such as chords and short melodic phrases as single tokens. The model is trained on PDMX-Synth, a 238K image-ABC pair dataset constructed by converting PDMX MusicXML files to canonical ABC and rendering them with two pipelines (MuseScore and abcm2ps) plus extensive visual augmentations (random resolution, margins, background color, landscape/portrait, measure numbering) to prevent overfitting to a single renderer. A smaller variant (Legato-small, 8.5M decoder + 2.5M projector) is also provided for controlled comparison with SMT++.

## Results

On the most realistic evaluation set (IMSLP Piano Scores, 32 pages), Legato achieves TEDn of 29.7 and OMR-NED of 44.3, representing a 68.0-point and 47.6-point absolute error reduction over SMT++ (97.7 and 91.9, respectively). On the denser OpenScore String Quartets (252 pages), Legato scores TEDn 60.4 (camera) / 52.1 (rendered) and OMR-NED 58.2 / 32.9, versus SMT++ at 98.6/97.9 and 94.7/94.3. On the in-domain PDMX-Synth test split, Legato achieves ABC character, symbol, and line error rates of 23.3%, 25.8%, and 31.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Legato decisively outperforms SMT++ on every dataset and metric: on IMSLP Piano Scores, Legato reduces TEDn from 97.7 to 29.7 (−68.0) and OMR-NED from 91.9 to 44.3 (−47.6); on the denser OpenScore String Quartets, the TEDn gap is 38.2 points (camera) and 45.8 points (rendered). Even under evaluation settings deliberately favoring SMT++ (e.g., restricting to instances where SMT++ produces valid MusicXML-convertible output, or using Verovio-rendered violin parts with kern error rates), Legato still leads by large margins (e.g., TEDn 7.7 vs. 95.0 on violin parts). Against GPT-5, Legato wins on TEDn across all five datasets (e.g., 29.7 vs. 96.7 on IMSLP) and on OMR-NED as well (44.3 vs. 98.7), though GPT-5 slightly outperforms SMT++ on TEDn, suggesting general-purpose VLMs have some OMR capability but remain far behind a dedicated model. The smaller Legato_small variant (comparable trainable parameters to SMT++) also beats SMT++ on TEDn for all datasets, confirming that the pretrained vision encoder and ABC+BPE tokenization drive the gains rather than raw parameter count.

## Relation to Prior Work

Legato builds on the multimodal Llama architecture (AI@Meta, 2024) by repurposing its pretrained, frozen vision encoder as a feature extractor for music score images, paired with a from-scratch transformer decoder trained on ABC notation. It departs from the prior state-of-the-art SMT++ (Ríos-Vila et al., 2024) in three key ways: replacing the expert-defined **kern vocabulary with a data-driven BPE tokenizer over ABC, substituting a trained-from-scratch vision encoder with a frozen pretrained one, and scaling training data from ~688 pages to over 214K images via the newly constructed PDMX-Synth dataset. The choice of ABC as the output format is itself novel for end-to-end OMR, as prior systems targeted **kern or MusicXML.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Legato is the first end-to-end OMR model to handle multi-page typeset scores and to output ABC notation, and the released PDMX-Synth dataset (238K image-ABC pairs) provides a much larger and more diverse training resource than prior OMR datasets. The ablation finding that SMT++ fails to scale (enlarging model or data yields no improvement) while Legato benefits from the pretrained encoder suggests that general-purpose visual features are critical for OMR generalization, a direction worth pursuing through encoder finetuning. Open questions include extending to handwritten scores, recognizing textual elements (lyrics, titles), and whether finetuning the vision encoder specifically for musical notation would further close the gap on realistic camera images.
