## Problem

Existing preference data selection methods for LLM alignment rely on external signals (GPT-4 scores, reward models) and implicitly assume data quality is an intrinsic property of the data itself, independent of the model being trained. The paper identifies two critical gaps: (1) data quality is actually model-dependent, meaning a pair beneficial for one model can be harmful for another, and (2) the standard influence function over-scores data by overfitting to the validation set, making it unreliable for open-ended preference alignment where no oracle validation set exists.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairwise preference data (prompt, chosen response, rejected response) along with the current model parameters after a short warm-up stage, a validation set used to train a lightweight auxiliary model, and a reference model for computing log-likelihood ratios. It outputs a binary keep/drop indicator per pair, where a pair is retained only if it falls within the medium percentile range of both LossDiff and IRM scores simultaneously. Notably, the entire scoring requires only two forward passes per pair (one on the current model, one on the auxiliary model) with no backpropagation, achieving roughly 100× speedup over exact influence function computation (e.g., ~5 minutes vs. ~10 hours on Llama-3.2-1B).

## Method

The paper first proposes the Truncated Influence Function (TIF), which modifies the standard influence function by discarding both the smallest and largest IF percentiles, retaining only medium-IF pairs. This is grounded in the empirical finding that small-IF pairs are noisy and uninformative while large-IF pairs cause overfitting (enlarging margins on a few pairs while shrinking margins on the majority), making medium-IF data the most reliable training signal for preference alignment. To make TIF practical at scale, the paper introduces two gradient-free scoring functions: LossDiff (the loss difference between the current model and a validation-aligned auxiliary model, formally shown to be proportional to IF via first-order Taylor expansion) and IRM (the implicit reward margin from DPO, which correlates with IF through the model's internal preference signal). The final LossDiff-IRM rule selects pairs in the intersection of medium percentile ranges of both scores, exploiting the fact that their error sources relative to TIF are diverse and partially offset each other, yielding a selection that more closely approximates exact TIF than either score alone.

## Results

LossDiff-IRM achieves an average WinRate improvement of +13.58% over full-data training while using only 50%–64% of the preference data, across Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-8B-Base, and the Pythia series (2.8B/1.4B/410M) under both DPO and SLiC. On Llama-3.1-8B (DPO), LossDiff-IRM reaches 83.97% WinRate on UltraFeedback, 87.08% on AlpacaEval, 86.88% on Vicuna-Bench, and 88.40% on Arena-Hard, outperforming the strongest baseline (MAP) by an average of +6.45% WinRate. On Qwen3-8B-Base (DPO), it achieves 67.32%, 71.52%, 67.19%, and 68.63% WinRate respectively, with the only loss being to RS-DPO on Vicuna-Bench (67.19% vs. 71.90%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CurriDPO (Pattnaik et al., 2024), which uses GPT-4 or reward-model scores for curriculum-based selection, LossDiff-IRM wins on all four benchmarks for both Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B-Base, with the largest gap on Vicuna-Bench (86.88% vs. 74.06% WinRate on Llama-3.1-8B). Against MAP (Huang et al., 2025), a margin-based selection method, LossDiff-IRM achieves an average WinRate improvement of +6.45% on Llama-3.1-8B (e.g., 87.08% vs. 80.88% on AlpacaEval) and +0.51% on Qwen3-8B-Base, where MAP is a closer competitor. Against RS-DPO (Khaki et al., 2024), which generates model-specific preference pairs via rejection sampling, LossDiff-IRM wins on three of four benchmarks for Llama-3.1-8B (e.g., 88.40% vs. 82.05% on Arena-Hard) but loses on Vicuna-Bench for Qwen3-8B-Base (67.19% vs. 71.90% WinRate). Overall, LossDiff-IRM achieves an average WinRate improvement of +2.62% over the best baseline across the two 8B models, confirming that model-dependent selection (via LossDiff-IRM) outperforms both external-signal methods (CurriDPO) and partially model-aware methods (MAP, RS-DPO).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the influence function framework (Koh & Liang, 2017) to the preference alignment setting, proposing the Truncated Influence Function (TIF) as a model-dependent criterion for assessing individual preference pair quality. It departs from the dominant data-centric paradigm in preference data selection (CurriDPO, fDPO, GPT-4 filtering), which treats data quality as an intrinsic property assessed via external signals, by arguing that quality is fundamentally a property of the model being trained. The practical approximation LossDiff-IRM draws on DPO's implicit reward margin (Rafailov et al., 2023) and a validation-aligned auxiliary model, combining two forward-pass-only scoring functions to approximate the gradient-based TIF at a fraction of the computational cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that medium-IF pairs—not high-IF ones—are most valuable for alignment challenges the standard influence function interpretation and has direct implications for the data pipelines behind major LLMs (Llama, Qwen, DeepSeek) that rely on static, model-agnostic filtering. The practical 'less is more' result (50–64% of data yielding +13.58% average WinRate over full-data training) offers a concrete efficiency gain for alignment practitioners. Open questions include whether the validation-set requirement can be eliminated, how the percentile thresholds behave at much larger scale, and whether the model-dependence finding extends beyond DPO/SLiC to other alignment objectives like KTO or SimPO.
