## Problem

While the Linear Representation Hypothesis establishes that binary semantic attributes correspond to directions in LLM embedding space, it offers limited insight into how compositional, set-theoretic semantics—concept inclusion, intersection, and union—are geometrically encoded. Prior extensional views (e.g., Park et al., 2025) model concepts as polytopes defined by their instances but overlook the intensional, attribute-based structure that underlies symbolic abstraction. This paper asks whether the linear geometry of LLM embeddings, when interpreted through the lens of Formal Concept Analysis, naturally induces a concept lattice supporting meet and join operations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes LLM object embeddings (mean of last hidden states over synonym lemmas), attribute directions estimated via regularized Fisher discriminant analysis on positive/negative object clusters, and a binary object-attribute incidence matrix annotated by GPT-4o from WordNet is-a hierarchies. Thresholds for each attribute are set as the midpoint of mean positive and negative projections. Outputs include a soft inclusion score between any two concepts (a scalar in [0,1] computed from weighted sigmoid comparisons of their projection profiles), meet and join projection profiles (element-wise min/max over attribute dimensions), and degrees of equality (harmonic-mean symmetrization of mutual inclusion) used to rank candidate concepts for a given algebraic operation.

## Method

The core contribution is a half-space model that unifies the Linear Representation Hypothesis with Formal Concept Analysis: each binary attribute is represented by a direction vector and a separating threshold, and a concept is the intersection of the half-spaces defined by its attributes (a convex polyhedral cone). A key canonical-form result (Proposition 1) shows that when attribute directions are linearly independent, all thresholds can be absorbed into a single global shift of embeddings, reducing the model to origin-passing half-spaces without loss of the induced lattice. Theorem 1 proves that thresholding a soft sigmoid incidence function at any confidence level δ yields a crisp formal context whose Galois closure is a complete lattice under extent inclusion. For practical computation, concepts are represented by normalized projection profiles over the attribute directions, and a soft inclusion score (weighted sigmoid of the target concept's profile against the source concept's profile) replaces strict set containment. Meet and join are operationalized as element-wise min and max of projection profiles, with degrees of equality defined via harmonic-mean symmetrization of mutual inclusion, enabling ranked retrieval of the correct lattice element from embedding geometry alone.

## Results

On the half-space model evaluation (Table 1), the Linear method achieves F1 scores of 82.5–83.2% on physical domains (WN-Animal, WN-Plant, WN-Food) and 71.4–75.4% on abstract domains (WN-Event, WN-Event) across LLaMA3.1-8B, Gemma-7B, and Mistral-7B. For partial order (subsumption) recovery (Table 2), the Linear method reaches up to 77.1% F1 (LLaMA, WN-Animal) and 75.6% F1 (Gemma, WN-Food), consistently outperforming the Mean baseline (55–67% F1) and Random baseline (33–50% F1). Concept algebra (meet/join) evaluation via MRR shows the proposed operator outperforms both baselines, with the largest gains on physical domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three approaches: a Random baseline (random attribute directions), a Mean baseline (centroid of positive objects as direction), and the proposed Linear method (LDA-estimated discriminative directions). The Linear method dominates on every domain and every model, achieving 15–35 percentage-point F1 gains over Mean and 30–35 points over Random. The gap is largest on physical domains (e.g., Gemma-7B on WN-Animal: 83.2% vs. 50.1% Mean vs. 45.3% Random) and somewhat smaller on abstract domains (WN-Event: 71.4% vs. 52.2% Mean). For partial order recovery (Table 2), the same pattern holds: Linear achieves 77.1% F1 on WN-Animal (LLaMA) versus 66.7% (Mean) and 47.3% (Random). Gemma-7B and LLaMA3.1-8B show the strongest linear separability, while Mistral-7B trails by 1–4 points, suggesting model architecture affects how cleanly attribute directions are encoded.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the Linear Representation Hypothesis (Park et al., ICML 2024) with Formal Concept Analysis (Ganter et al., 2005) by showing that thresholded linear attribute directions induce a concept lattice via half-space intersections. It departs from Park et al. (ICLR 2025), which models concepts purely extensionally as polytopes of token instances, by introducing an intensional (attribute-based) view where concepts are convex regions bounded by their defining attributes. It also extends Xiong & Staab (ICLR 2025), who first connected FCA to masked language models, to autoregressive LLMs with a concrete geometric half-space model and projection-based concept algebra.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, differentiable bridge between continuous embedding geometry and symbolic concept composition (meet/join/subsumption), opening a path toward logic-guided interpretability and controllable multi-attribute steering in LLMs. Key limitations include the assumption of approximately linear attribute separability (which may fail for cyclic or continuously varying concepts), reliance on GPT-4o-generated attribute annotations (introducing potential circularity), and evaluation restricted to WordNet taxonomic domains. Open questions include whether the lattice geometry holds for non-taxonomic relations (part-of, cause, function), domain-specific ontologies (biomedical, legal), and whether the half-space model generalizes to non-linear or high-order attribute interactions.
