## Problem

Existing LLM-based clinical decision support either assumes all patient data is immediately available (ignoring the iterative diagnostic process) or relies on untrained, out-of-the-box LLM behavior without task-specific fine-tuning. No prior work explicitly trains language models to perform the iterative, hypothesis-driven differential diagnosis process that clinicians follow in practice, where information is uncovered step-by-step through sequential test requests.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265295/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an initial patient history (symptoms, comorbidities, family history) and then iteratively receives test results (imaging reports, lab panels, physical exam notes) only when it explicitly requests them, one test at a time from a fixed list of 11 test types. At each step, the hypothesis agent outputs a diagnosis hypothesis with a 0–10 confidence score, and the decision agent outputs a ReAct-formatted action (request a specific test or commit to a final diagnosis from four abdominal conditions). The episode terminates when a diagnosis is given, a token limit is exceeded, or the output format is violated.

## Method

LA-CDM is a two-agent system (hypothesis agent and decision agent) sharing Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct weights fine-tuned via LoRA, trained with three objectives in a cyclic regimen of 100 steps each: (1) supervised fine-tuning for accurate hypothesis generation from partial patient information, (2) GRPO-based reinforcement learning for confidence calibration using a betting-game reward that penalizes overconfident errors, and (3) GRPO-based reinforcement learning for efficient action selection with a composite reward combining a fixed diagnosis-correctness signal and a cost-proportional penalty on test usage. The key insight is that the interplay of these objectives—particularly the hypothesis agent's calibrated uncertainty feeding into the decision agent's action selection—teaches the model to request the most informative tests in a given situation while stopping when confidence is sufficient. The cyclic training (action → hypothesis → calibration, repeated) yields more stable optimization than simultaneous multi-objective training, and the cost reward is normalized so that requesting all tests yields zero net reward, ensuring the model always benefits from additional tests when diagnostically useful.

## Results

On MIMIC-CDM (four abdominal diseases), LA-CDM achieves 81.3% mean class accuracy, 84.1% micro F1, and 81.3% macro F1, while reducing average test cost to $1,295.61 per patient. Training improves the hypothesis agent's accuracy from 75.7% to 81.9% and reduces Expected Calibration Error from 0.069 to 0.037. The model also adapts its testing strategy per patient, e.g., selecting ultrasound in 64.9% of suspected cholecystitis cases and CT in 85.1% of suspected appendicitis cases, consistent with clinical guidelines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LA-CDM substantially outperforms all three baselines. Against OASST (Hager et al., 2024), the best zero-shot LLM in the original MIMIC-CDM evaluation, LA-CDM improves mean accuracy by ~26 percentage points (81.3% vs. 54.9%), with the largest gains on rarer classes (cholecystitis: 83.6% vs. 48.0%; diverticulitis: 75.0% vs. 45.5%). Against SM-DDPO (Yu et al., 2023), a tabular-only RL method, LA-CDM achieves 81.3% vs. 37.0% mean accuracy, demonstrating the critical importance of processing textual modalities like imaging reports. Against zero-shot ReAct (Yao et al., 2022), LA-CDM improves mean accuracy by 6.4 points (81.3% vs. 74.9%) while simultaneously reducing average test cost by ~13% ($1,295.61 vs. $1,480.32). The untrained LA-CDM(ZS) variant (64.5%) further confirms that the gains come from explicit training rather than prompt design alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of RL-based clinical decision-making (SM-DDPO, ED-Copilot) from tabular-only models to full-text LLMs, and departs from zero-shot LLM clinical decision support (Hager et al., Vaid et al., Liu et al.) by introducing explicit task-specific training. Methodologically, it combines GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for both confidence calibration and action selection, adapts the betting-game calibration reward from Stangel et al. (2025), and uses ReAct-style prompting (Yao et al., 2022) for structured test requests. The two-agent hypothesis/decision architecture is novel in this context, drawing on cognitive science of differential diagnosis (Sox et al., 2024) to decompose the problem into hypothesis formation and action selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to explicitly train LLMs for sequential clinical decision-making rather than relying on zero-shot capabilities, with practical implications for reducing diagnostic costs and time in settings like emergency departments. Key limitations include evaluation on only four abdominal diseases in a single dataset, reliance on retrospective data that constrains the space of learnable testing pathways, and the inability to discover genuinely novel diagnostic strategies beyond those demonstrated by clinicians. Open questions include generalization to broader disease spaces, handling of unavailable or novel tests, and whether the hypothesis-driven decomposition scales to more complex multi-system diagnoses.
