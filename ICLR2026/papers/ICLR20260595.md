## Problem

Existing procedural material synthesis methods (MatFormer, VLMaterial) represent node graphs exclusively as text-only programs, ignoring the inherently visual-spatial nature of node-based editing that human artists rely on. Without visual feedback during generation, models must reason about complex spatial relationships and visual outcomes purely from textual tokens, leading to lower quality outputs and higher error rates as material complexity grows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260595/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For conditional (inverse) generation, the model receives a 512×512 rendered image (324 image patches) as the target appearance; for unconditional generation, it starts from a beginning-of-sequence token. During generation, the model also consumes intermediate graph visualizations—either as interleaved 140×140 node previews (mixed conditioning) or as a full graph screenshot using up to 6144 tokens (graph conditioning). The output is a procedural material node graph in COMPACTSBS, a compact YAML-based format that is transpiled back to Adobe Substance Designer's native SBS format and produces five PBR texture maps (base color, normal, roughness, metallic, height).

## Method

MULTIMAT fine-tunes Qwen2.5-VL (7B) to generate node definitions one at a time in topological order, with each new node validated by a transpiler and material engine before the next is produced. Two multimodal conditioning strategies are explored: mixed conditioning interleaves textual node definitions with image patch embeddings of each node's visualized output, while graph conditioning feeds the model a single screenshot of the entire graph with embedded intermediate outputs, more closely mirroring human visual editing. An incremental tree search algorithm backtracks adaptively (discarding 2^(i−1) most recent nodes) when transpilation or execution fails, and an automatic error-repair module fixes common issues like extraneous parameters and type mismatches without additional sampling. The model is trained on 6,878 production-quality materials from Adobe's Substance 3D Assets Repository—the largest and most feature-complete dataset to date—using a bidirectional COMPACTSBS transpiler that reduces program length by over 80% while preserving full Substance Designer functionality including pixel processors and function graphs.

## Results

For unconditional generation, MULTIMAT (Graph) achieves the lowest KID of 2.365 (×100), outperforming MULTIMAT (Mixed) (6.752) and VLMATERIAL (SBS) (14.155) by over 4 and 11 percentage points respectively. For conditional (inverse) generation, MULTIMAT (Graph) attains the highest DREAMSim score of 36.609 and CLIP similarity of 67.907, while VLMATERIAL (SBS) lags at 31.344 and 65.678. With parameter optimization (DiffMat), MULTIMAT+ (Graph) further improves DREAMSim to 40.367 and CLIP to 70.114, whereas VLMATERIAL+ (SBS) gains only ~1% (31.348 DREAMSim). In human evaluation, MULTIMAT+ (Graph) is preferred over MULTIMAT+ (Mixed) 58.3% of the time and over VLMATERIAL+ (SBS) 81.1% of the time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is VLMATERIAL (SBS), a retrained version of VLMaterial (Li et al., ICLR 2025) adapted to the same Adobe Substance Designer dataset and COMPACTSBS format for fair comparison. MULTIMAT (Graph) decisively outperforms VLMATERIAL (SBS) on visual quality: KID drops from 14.155 to 2.365 (unconditional) and from 14.976 to 2.801 (conditional), a gap of over 11 percentage points. On perceptual similarity for inverse rendering, MULTIMAT (Graph) leads VLMATERIAL (SBS) by ~5.3 points in DREAMSim (36.609 vs. 31.344) and ~2.2 points in CLIP (67.907 vs. 65.678). After parameter optimization, the gap widens further: MULTIMAT+ (Graph) reaches 40.367 DREAMSim while VLMATERIAL+ (SBS) barely improves to 31.348, suggesting VLMATERIAL's outputs deviate too far from the target for gradient-based refinement to be effective. On efficiency (NER), MULTIMAT (Mixed) is best (8.923 vs. 14.846 for VLMATERIAL), while MULTIMAT (Graph) is comparable to VLMATERIAL (~15%). In human evaluation, VLMATERIAL+ (SBS) is consistently ranked last, with annotators preferring MULTIMAT+ (Graph) over it 81.1% of the time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the procedural material synthesis line initiated by MATFORMER (Guerrero et al., 2022) and its conditional variant (Hu et al., 2023), as well as the more recent VLMATERIAL approach (Li et al., 2025a) that leveraged LLMs for end-to-end graph generation. The key conceptual departure is treating node graphs as inherently visual-spatial programs rather than text-only sequences: MULTIMAT feeds back rendered intermediate node states to a vision-language model (Qwen2.5-VL) during autoregressive generation, mirroring how human artists interact with node-based editors. It also introduces a bidirectional transpiler (COMPACTSBS) and a substantially larger dataset (6,878 materials with the complete Substance Designer feature set) that prior work could not access due to format and feature limitations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent finding that visual conditioning (especially full graph visualization) substantially outperforms text-only generation validates a core UX principle of node-based tools and suggests that multimodal feedback loops are the right paradigm for synthesizing any visual-spatial program, not just materials. Practical impact is significant for game, film, and VR/AR pipelines where material authoring remains a bottleneck requiring professional training. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even larger or more diverse node graph systems, whether self-generated training data can overcome the severe data scarcity, and whether the paradigm transfers to other visual programming domains (vector graphics, shader node editors). The longer training times relative to text-only baselines and the OCR-like errors in the pure-visual variant are notable practical limitations.
