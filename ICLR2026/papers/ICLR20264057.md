## Problem

Image registration algorithms have not scaled in tandem with ultra-high-resolution imaging: while clinical problems involve ~20M parameters, ex-vivo brain scans at 100μm require solving over 11B parameters, far beyond the ~50M scale at which current methods remain reliable. Existing distributed training techniques (5D parallelism, IO-aware fused kernels) are tailored to GEMM-like transformer operations and do not address the non-GEMM bottlenecks (grid sampling, cross-correlation, mutual information) that dominate registration pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264057/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed 3D medical image volume and a moving 3D medical image volume (potentially multimodal, e.g., 250μm T1 MRI and 100μm FLASH MRI) at native resolution, with volumes up to 1760×1760×1278 voxels. It produces a composite coordinate transform (affine matrix + translation + per-voxel diffeomorphic displacement field) that warps the moving image to the fixed image. The displacement field is a tensor of size dN (d=3 for 3D volumes), and the entire problem is sharded across H GPUs with each host holding N/H voxels of the image, warp, and optimizer state.

## Method

FFDP (Flash Fused Distributed Primitives) combines two complementary contributions. First, IO-aware fused CUDA kernels eliminate intermediate HBM allocations for the three memory-bound workhorse operations: a composite implicit grid sampler computes I(Ax+t+Su(x)) in registers without materializing intermediate grids (O(1) vs O(N) memory), an implicit Parzen windowing kernel accumulates MI histograms in shared memory avoiding B×N tensor materialization, and a fused LNCC kernel reduces 16× HBM overhead to 5× by computing gradients in-place. Second, a distributed framework introduces Grid Parallel (GP), a tensor abstraction that shards images and warp fields across hosts while providing boundary-synchronized halo exchange for correct convolutions, and a Ring Sampler that decomposes bilinear interpolation into partial sums over image shards communicated in a ring topology, enabling the moving image to be sharded without an allgather and scaling problem size linearly with the number of GPUs.

## Results

On the Faux-OASIS dataset at 250 μm (1.32B DOF per pair), FFDP achieves Dice 0.895, InvDice 0.597, and AvgHD90 0.216 mm, improving Dice by 18.1 points, InvDice by 31.6 points, and reducing HD90 by 62.1% over the next-best method (CLAIRE). At 500 μm, FFDP leads with Dice 0.872 and HD90 0.258 mm, while at 1 mm it is competitive (Dice 0.838) with TransMorph (0.851) and VFA (0.851). The framework registers a 100 μm ex-vivo human brain volume (11.8B parameters, 44.8 GB) in ~58 s on 8 A6000 GPUs, and accelerates existing pipelines by up to 7.48× (optimization) and 6.14× (deep learning) while reducing peak memory by up to 59%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FFDP (FireANTs + FFDP) wins decisively at 500 μm and 250 μm across all three metrics, outperforming the next-best method CLAIRE by 6.3 Dice points and 21.9 InvDice points at 250 μm, and by 3.1 Dice points at 500 μm. At 1 mm, TransMorph (Dice 0.851) and VFA (Dice 0.851) slightly edge out FFDP (0.838), but FFDP remains within 1.3 points and is Pareto-efficient in GPU-hours. Patch-based deep methods (TransMorph†, SynthMorph†, UniGradICON†, VFA†) degrade sharply at higher resolutions—UniGradICON drops to Dice 0.359 at 250 μm—while FFDP improves, confirming that patching is insufficient for ultra-high-resolution registration. Among optimization baselines, FireANTs without FFDP (patch-based) achieves Dice 0.777 at 250 μm versus 0.895 with FFDP, a 11.8-point gap attributable to the fused kernels and distributed sharding. In the LNCC backend comparison (Table 1), FFDP's fused kernel runs in 0.50 s with 577.5 MB memory, beating FastLNCC (3.76 s, 1026 MB) by 7.5× and VoxelMorph/TransMorph (57.08 s, 1419 MB) by 114×, while matching FireANTs' Dice score (78.67 vs 78.81). For MI, FFDP reduces memory from 12,206 MB (PyTorch) to 577.5 MB (95.2% reduction) with comparable Dice (75.88 vs 75.88). Overall, FFDP is Pareto-dominant in the accuracy-vs-GPU-hours tradeoff at 250 μm, requiring up to 500× fewer GPU-hours than alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts IO-aware fused kernel techniques (FlashAttention, Dao et al. 2022/2023) and 5D parallelism strategies from large-scale transformer training (Megatron-LM, ZeRO, Ring Attention) to the fundamentally different non-GEMM operations that dominate image registration: grid sampling, normalized cross-correlation, and mutual information. It builds directly on FireANTs (Jena et al., 2024a) as the optimization backbone and extends the distributed registration work of CLAIRE (Mang et al., 2019) by introducing convolution-aware tensor sharding (Grid Parallel) and a ring-based distributed sampler that avoids allgather on the moving image. The key conceptual departure is that prior parallelism techniques were tailored to GEMM-like transformer sub-operations, whereas FFDP identifies and fuses the memory-bound, non-GEMM workhorse kernels unique to registration pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Image acquisition in neuroimaging, connectomics, and computational pathology has outpaced registration algorithms by orders of magnitude; this paper closes that gap by enabling native-resolution registration of 100μm ex-vivo whole-brain volumes (11.8B parameters) in under a minute on 8 GPUs, a task previously infeasible without lossy downsampling. The finding that standard patch-based workarounds degrade in accuracy at higher resolutions (rather than merely being slower) challenges the common practice in the field and motivates the need for truly scalable solvers. Open questions include whether the fused-kernel and sharding strategies generalize to other non-GEMM scientific workloads, how the framework behaves at teravoxel scales (beyond the 11B-parameter demonstration), and whether the current set of supported losses (MSE, LNCC, MI) and transforms (affine + diffeomorphic) suffice for the full range of multimodal registration needs in life sciences.
