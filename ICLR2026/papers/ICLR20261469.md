## Problem

Standard Transformer attention mechanisms treat all time series inputs uniformly in Euclidean space, failing to adapt to the local geometric structure of temporal patterns and making them highly susceptible to real-world noise and anomalies. Additionally, no standardized benchmark exists for evaluating forecasting robustness under realistic, statistically-grounded corruptions (as opposed to synthetic adversarial attacks), leaving a critical gap in principled robustness assessment for time series.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multivariate time series (input length 512 for most datasets, 336 for ETTh1/ETTh2) processed through PatchTST's channel-independent patch embedding, and produces forecasts at horizons of 96, 192, 336, and 720 steps. During evaluation on TSRBench, spikes and level-shift corruptions are injected into the input window while the ground-truth forecasting horizon remains clean, enabling isolated measurement of how input degradation affects prediction quality. The metric prediction network f_θ is pre-trained on both real query vectors sampled during training and synthetic vectors drawn from U(-5,5) to cover the representation space.

## Method

Local Geometry Attention (LGA) replaces the standard dot-product score with a negative squared Mahalanobis distance where the metric tensor G(q) is query-specific and derived from local Gaussian process theory. Concretely, a local kernel-covariance matrix is built from kernel-weighted outer products of (key − query) differences, which is then inverted (with noise regularization) to yield G(q); the negative predictive variance of the resulting local GP serves as a principled density-aware similarity that suppresses off-manifold anomalies. For computational feasibility, a small per-head FFN (two linear layers with GELU and Softplus) is trained to approximate G(q) as a diagonal matrix from the query vector alone, decoupling expensive geometry estimation from inference. The attention score is further optimized by expanding the quadratic form and dropping the query-constant term under softmax, reducing the per-pair cost to two matrix-vector products. This is integrated into PatchTST (as PatchLGA) and also applied to CATS, iTransformer, and Samformer to demonstrate broad architectural applicability.

## Results

On clean data (Severity 0), PatchLGA matches PatchTST across all six datasets (e.g., MSE 0.351 vs 0.352 on ETTm1), confirming no degradation from the geometry-aware attention. Under severe corruption (Severity 5), PatchLGA achieves a 12.3% MSE reduction over PatchTST on ETTm1 (0.734 vs 0.839) and a 21.2% MSE reduction over TimeMixer on Weather (0.454 vs 0.576). The robustness advantage grows monotonically with corruption severity, with the largest gaps appearing at Levels 3–5 where standard attention degrades sharply (e.g., ETTm1 MSE jumps from 0.375 to 0.839 for PatchTST but only to 0.734 for PatchLGA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PatchLGA consistently outperforms PatchTST (SDP attention) across all six datasets, with the gap widening from negligible at Severity 1 to 12.3% MSE reduction on ETTm1 at Severity 5. Against the linear model TimeMixer, PatchLGA wins on all datasets at Severity ≥ 3, most dramatically on Weather where it achieves 0.454 MSE vs TimeMixer's 0.576 at Severity 5 (21.2% lower). Among robust attention variants, LGA substantially outperforms both MoM (Han et al., 2023) and Elliptical attention (Nielsen et al., 2024) at Severity 3–5 on ETTm1 (e.g., 0.734 vs 1.016 for MoM and 0.880 for Elliptical at Severity 5), while the latter two actually underperform standard SDP at lower severities. When integrated into CATS (cross-attention), LGA yields up to 17.1% MSE improvement over SDP, though gains are less consistent due to linear embedding of noisy inputs. In iTransformer (channel-wise attention), LGA provides modest but stable improvements (e.g., 1.266 vs 1.309 MSE at Severity 5 on ETTm1), limited by the architecture's linear embedding that disrupts local temporal geometry. On Samformer, LGA is critical: PatchSamformer with SDP suffers catastrophic degradation (MSE 1.465 at H=96, Severity 5), while PatchSamformer with LGA recovers to 0.715, outperforming even the unpatched Samformer baseline (0.860).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of GP-inspired attention mechanisms (Bui et al., 2024; Chen & Li, 2023) by replacing global kernel assumptions with query-specific local geometry, drawing on local kernel-covariance formulations from manifold learning (Berry & Sauer, 2016) and connecting the resulting Mahalanobis scoring to Riemannian geodesic distance. It departs from prior robust attention work (MoM, Elliptical attention) that was designed for vision/language and shown here to underperform on corrupted time series, instead grounding robustness in the intrinsic local data geometry of temporal patterns. On the evaluation side, TSRBench adapts the ImageNet-C paradigm of multi-severity corruption testing to time series, but replaces adversarial or synthetic perturbations with statistically calibrated spikes and level shifts based on extreme value theory (DSPOT).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TSRBench fills a genuine gap by providing the first standardized, reproducible protocol for evaluating forecasting robustness under realistic (non-adversarial) corruptions with clean ground truth, which is difficult to achieve with real-world anomaly-labeled data. The diagonal approximation of the metric tensor and the restriction to two corruption types (spikes, level shifts) limit generality, and the approach has been validated primarily on PatchTST-family architectures with moderate sequence lengths. Open questions include whether the local geometry prior extends to other corruption families (e.g., missing data, regime changes), whether the diagonal assumption is sufficient for highly correlated multivariate settings, and whether the pre-trained metric network transfers across domains without retraining.
