## Problem

Diffusion models generate diverse samples but often fail to satisfy task-specific objectives (physical validity, human preference, high reward) without additional inference-time compute. Prior inference-scaling methods either perform global search over the base distribution's modes (particle filtering, tree search) or local gradient-based refinement, but scale these two axes in isolation, leaving a gap in jointly optimizing both for challenging decision-making tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261337/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pretrained diffusion model (e.g., SD v1.5, SDXL, FLUX.1, or a Diffuser-based trajectory model) and a verifier function f(x₀) defined on clean samples—ranging from reward functions and Q-functions to VLMs and ImageNet classifiers—without any retraining. It produces samples (images at 256×256 or 512×512, trajectories in PointMaze, or action sequences in D4RL locomotion environments) drawn from the compositional distribution p₀(x₀)·f(x₀)^λ, where λ controls verifier weight. The verifier is queried at intermediate denoising steps via the denoised estimate x₀|t, enabling compute allocation decisions before full generation completes.

## Method

The framework unifies classical search principles for diffusion inference: (1) a BFS-style global search that denoises N particles in parallel, scoring intermediate states via f(x₀|t) and reallocating compute through Srinivasan sampling resampling with an increasing tempering schedule and max-scoring; (2) a DFS-style global search that denoises a single particle and adaptively backtracks (re-injecting noise) when the verifier score drops below a user-set threshold, enabling instance-adaptive compute allocation; and (3) a local search via annealed Langevin MCMC that performs multiple MCMC steps at each noise level using the composed score ∇log q_t + ∇log f̂_t to sample from the compositional target beyond the base model's modes. A key theoretical contribution (Proposition 1) proves that training-free guidance with recurrence is equivalent in the continuous limit to Langevin MCMC on annealed distributions, providing the first rigorous convergence bound (Theorem 1) for recurrent guidance and unifying two previously separate lines of work. Jointly scaling local and global search steps establishes a new Pareto frontier across planning, offline RL, and image generation, outperforming methods that scale either axis alone.

## Results

On D4RL locomotion tasks, the full method (TTS: joint local + global search) achieves an average score of 86.1, matching training-based baselines D-QL (86.3) and QGPO (86.6) while outperforming training-free methods TFG (82.1) and DAS (80.2). For global search on image generation, the improved BFS (Increase, Max, SSP) consistently achieves the highest ImageReward scores across SDv1.5 (0.882), SDv1.8 (1.087), SDXL (1.194/1.291), and FLUX.1 (1.203) at N=4 or N=8 particles. On ImageNet conditional generation, the double-verifier strategy with BFS reduces FID from 133.3 to 118.2 and improves class accuracy from 46.5% to 55.9% at N=8, while also increasing MSP (less OOD) from 0.183 to 0.214.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On D4RL locomotion (Table 3), TTS (joint local+global search) achieves 86.1 average, essentially matching the best training-based methods D-QL (86.3) and QGPO (86.6) without any joint training, while substantially outperforming training-free TFG (82.1) and DAS (80.2); the gap is most pronounced on Medium Hopper where TTS reaches 99.5 vs. DAS's 71.3. For global search on image generation (Table 2), the improved BFS outperforms all prior particle/tree-search methods—FK-steering, DAS (w/o grad), TreeG, SVDD, SoP, and DSearch—across SDv1.5, SDv1.8, SDXL, and FLUX.1, with the largest margins over SVDD (e.g., 0.882 vs. 0.667 on SDv1.5) and SoP (0.882 vs. 0.688). On ImageNet conditional generation (Table 5), the double-verifier BFS achieves FID 118.2 and 55.9% accuracy at N=8, beating single-verifier BFS (FID 133.3, 46.5%) and BoN (FID 155.7, 35.8%) with 2× less compute. For policy distillation (Table 4), TTS-distill outperforms the SOTA online RL method DPPO on all three environments (e.g., 98.8 vs. 92.8 on Hopper). The annealed Langevin MCMC framework from Du et al. (2023) and TFG from Ye et al. (2024) serve as theoretical foundations rather than direct baselines; the paper proves their equivalence (Proposition 1) and shows that scaling local search steps beyond what TFG uses yields further gains in planning and RL tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the growing family of particle-based and tree-search inference-scaling methods for diffusion models (SMC-style approaches like DAS and FK-steering, tree-search methods like SVDD and DSearch) under a single BFS framework, showing they are special cases defined by choices of tempering, scoring, and resampling. It then introduces an adaptive DFS with score-dependent backtracking that departs from the fixed-schedule exploration of prior methods like SoP. For local search, the paper proves a theoretical equivalence (Proposition 1) between training-free guidance with recurrence (TFG) and annealed Langevin MCMC, building on Du et al.'s compositional MCMC framework and Ye et al.'s TFG, and is the first to jointly scale both local and global search dimensions within a classical AI search paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified BFS/DFS framework gives practitioners a clear design space for choosing inference-scaling strategies, and the theoretical unification of TFG recurrence with Langevin MCMC provides the first rigorous convergence guarantees for recurrent guidance, resolving a gap that had limited its adoption. The policy distillation result—where fine-tuning on TTS-generated actions outperforms dedicated online RL methods like DPPO—suggests inference-time scaling could partially substitute for online learning, though this is demonstrated on only three D4RL locomotion tasks. Key open questions include how to automate the remaining hyperparameters (thresholds, guidance strengths, number of local search steps) and whether the joint-scaling advantage persists in higher-dimensional or more complex decision-making settings.
