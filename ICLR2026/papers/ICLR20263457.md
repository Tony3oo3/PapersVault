## Problem

Conventional LoRA optimizers (Adam, AdamW, SGD) fail to achieve efficient feature learning (EFL) because they are transformation-variant: the two factor matrices M and N receive updates of mismatched magnitude under reparameterization. Existing EFL methods either require multiple fine-tuning rounds (LoRA+) or overly complex preconditioner redesigns (LoRA-Rite), and all still require tuning a weight-decay hyperparameter whose role in EFL is poorly understood.

## Input / Output

The method consumes frozen pre-trained weights W ∈ R^{n×m}, a training dataset, and trainable LoRA factor pairs (M_t, N_t) ∈ R^{m×r} × R^{n×r} at each step. At each iteration, the projected gradient is computed on the matrix tangent space T_{MN^T} (dimension mr + nr − r²) via QR decomposition of M and N, rather than on the full lifting space. The output is the updated factor pair (M_{t+1}, N_{t+1}) whose product M_{t+1}N_{t+1}^T is invariant to the choice of reparameterization R ∈ GL(r), guaranteeing that both factors contribute equally to the loss change.

## Method

The paper establishes a general iteration scheme on the quotient manifold R_*^{m×r} × R_*^{n×r} / ~ by applying horizontal lift theory: a descent vector on the matrix tangent space is lifted to the factor space by solving a Sylvester equation M^T Ẋ N = M^T M N^T N K + K M^T M N^T N for the r×r matrix K, which simultaneously removes the r² redundant dimensions and replaces the weight-decay hyperparameter with a data-driven decay term. Preconditioning (Adam moments or RACS row/column scaling) is applied on the matrix tangent space before lifting, which is the key structural choice that preserves transformation invariance and EFL—applying preconditioners on the lifting space, as in ScaledAdam, breaks invariance. Two concrete optimizers are derived: AdamS (lifting Adam with first/second moments and an optional Riemannian inner product) and LRACS (lifting the low-memory RACS optimizer with O(m+n) moment storage). The per-step overhead beyond standard backpropagation is O(r³) for the Sylvester solve plus O(mr² + nr²) for matrix operations, and the weight-decay parameter is entirely eliminated.

## Results

On the Mix-of-Show image generation model, AdamS (the proposed method) achieves a CLIP score of 32.64 and FID of 46.32 at rank 16, representing a 9% improvement over Adam (29.86 CLIP) and a 3% improvement over the state-of-the-art LoRA-Rite (31.90 CLIP). On the GPT-2 medium E2E NLG task at rank 4, LRACS achieves the best BLEU score of 70.4, NIST of 8.85, and CIDEr of 2.54, outperforming all baselines including LoRA-Rite (BLEU 69.3) and AdamW (BLEU 68.6). Both proposed methods are weight-decay-free, eliminating the need to tune the λ hyperparameter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SGD (Amari, 1993), both AdamS and LRACS achieve substantial gains: at r=16 on Mix-of-Show, AdamS improves CLIP from 24.76 to 32.64 and FID from 70.78 to 46.32. Against Adam (Kingma & Ba, 2014), AdamS at r=4 raises CLIP from 25.17 to 32.20 and lowers FID from 72.75 to 54.39; on GPT-2 E2E, AdamS improves BLEU from 68.0 to 69.4. Against Scaled GD (Tong et al., 2021) and Quotient GD (Mishra et al., 2014), both EFL baselines without preconditioning, AdamS at r=16 outperforms Scaled GD by 3.57 CLIP points (32.64 vs. 29.07) and Quotient GD by 4.60 points (32.64 vs. 28.04), while LRACS at r=4 beats Scaled GD by 1.9 BLEU points (70.4 vs. 68.5) and Quotient GD by 3.5 points (70.4 vs. 66.9). Against AdamW (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2017), AdamS at r=16 improves CLIP from 30.78 to 32.64 and FID from 62.57 to 46.32, and on GPT-2 E2E, LRACS beats AdamW by 1.8 BLEU points (70.4 vs. 68.6). Against the state-of-the-art LoRA-Rite (Yen et al., 2025), AdamS at r=16 achieves a 0.74 CLIP improvement (32.64 vs. 31.90) and a 9.36 FID reduction (46.32 vs. 55.68), while LRACS at r=4 on GPT-2 E2E edges out LoRA-Rite by 1.1 BLEU points (70.4 vs. 69.3) and 0.01 CIDEr (2.54 vs. 2.53).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the efficient feature learning (EFL) property from LoRA+ and LoRA-Rite with horizontal lift theory on quotient manifolds, creating a general iteration scheme that can lift any pre-training optimizer (Adam, RACS, Shampoo) into a transformation-invariant LoRA optimizer. It departs from LoRA-Rite by adopting a different Riemannian metric (the Grassmannian quotient metric rather than the one in Yen et al., 2025) and by showing that the Sylvester equation solution K_t naturally subsumes the role of the weight decay matrix, eliminating a hyperparameter. It also corrects a key error in ScaledAdam (Zhang & Pilanci, 2024), which applies preconditioning to the lifting space rather than the matrix tangent space, thereby failing to achieve transformation invariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The general scheme provides a principled, extensible framework for designing EFL-compliant LoRA optimizers by simply choosing a descent vector on the matrix tangent space and applying the Sylvester-based lift, potentially accelerating future optimizer design for PEFT. The removal of the weight decay hyperparameter simplifies the tuning landscape, though the practical time savings depend on how much grid search was previously needed. Limitations include testing only on GPT-2 medium and Mix-of-Show (relatively small models), the O(r³) Sylvester solve overhead per step, and unresolved robustness issues with zero initialization that require a 'robust steps' workaround. Open questions include whether the specific metric choice is optimal, whether the framework scales to 70B+ LLMs, and whether the approach extends to non-transformer architectures or multi-task settings.
