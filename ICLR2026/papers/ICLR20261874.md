## Problem

Adversarial Training (AT) is a well-established technique for robust classifiers, yet it has not been systematically extended to generative models. Standard AT enforces invariance (output unchanged under perturbation), which is fundamentally incompatible with diffusion models' regression-based denoising objective—applying invariance causes the model to learn a wrong distribution. Additionally, existing noise-aware training methods for diffusion models require strong assumptions such as known noise variance or clean/noisy sample labels, limiting practical applicability to real-world corrupted datasets.

## Input / Output

The method trains a U-Net epsilon-predicting diffusion model on RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10, 64×64 CelebA/ImageNet, 256×256 LSUN Bedroom) where up to 90% of training samples may be corrupted with Gaussian noise (σ ∈ {0.1, 0.2}). At inference, the model takes random Gaussian noise x_T ~ N(0, I) and iteratively denoises it over T=1000 steps to produce a generated image. The method does not require knowledge of the corruption variance or clean/noisy labels at training time, and the same trained model can be evaluated on clean data for FID/IS or under adversarial trajectory attacks.

## Method

The core contribution is an equivariance-based adversarial loss for epsilon-predicting diffusion models. Instead of the standard AT invariance term ‖ε_θ(x_t+δ,t) − ε_θ(x_t,t)‖², the paper enforces equivariance: ‖ε_θ(x_t+δ,t) − [ε_θ(x_t,t)+δ]‖², requiring the network's noise prediction to shift by exactly the perturbation amount. The perturbation δ is either random (uniform in a time-varying ℓ_∞ ball of radius r_β(t)) or adversarial (single-step FGSM with random start, projected back to the ball). The radius r_β(t) follows a ray scheduler that shrinks as t→0 to prevent over-smoothing in the content phase, with a randomized bias term γ·β (β~U[0.5,2]) to avoid regularization annealing. The full training loss is the standard DDPM noise-prediction MSE plus a time-weighted equivariance regularization term (λ_t scaled by perturbation norm), and the entire procedure adds one extra backward pass per training step (×2.5 time complexity for adversarial variant).

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 90% of training data corrupted by Gaussian noise (σ=0.2), Robust_adv achieves FID 24.81 vs. DDPM's 102.68 and DDIM's 105.43, a ~4× improvement. On CelebA under the same corruption, Robust_adv reaches FID 16.53 vs. DDPM's 96.03. On ImageNet (64×64, 1.28M images), the method reduces FID from 129.4 to 80.3 at σ=0.2. On clean data (p=0%), Robust_adv shows a modest FID increase (e.g., 28.68 vs. 7.2 on CIFAR-10) due to trajectory smoothing, but generates fewer near-duplicates of training images (memorization) and supports faster sampling with fewer steps (FID 24.34 at 500 steps vs. 28.68 at 1000 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: DDPM (Nichol & Dhariwal, 2021) and DDIM (Song et al., 2021). Under 90% Gaussian noise corruption, Robust_adv decisively outperforms both: on CIFAR-10 at σ=0.2 it achieves FID 24.81 vs. DDPM's 102.68 and DDIM's 105.43; on CelebA at σ=0.2 it reaches 16.53 vs. 96.03 (DDPM) and 89.28 (DDIM). On clean data (p=0%), DDPM wins on FID (7.2 vs. 28.68 on CIFAR-10), but the authors argue the higher FID reflects beneficial smoothing rather than quality loss, and Robust_adv produces fewer memorized near-duplicates. The adversarial perturbation variant (Robust_adv) consistently outperforms the random perturbation variant (Robust_ran) by a large margin (e.g., FID 24.70 vs. 79.21 on CIFAR-10 at σ=0.1), confirming that the equivariance constraint with FGSM-style attacks is critical. Robust_adv also demonstrates superior robustness to iterative adversarial attacks (PGD, EoT-PGD), maintaining FID ~52–61 at 50% attacked timesteps where DDPM degrades to 131–135.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial training (AT) paradigm from classifiers to diffusion models, drawing its regularization structure from TRADES (Zhang et al., 2019) but replacing the invariance objective with an equivariance constraint that respects the score-matching dynamics of denoising. It positions itself against the 'noise-aware training' line (Daras et al., 2024a,c,d), which requires knowledge of noise variance and clean/noisy labels, by requiring neither. The key conceptual departure from prior AT is the demonstration that invariance actively breaks diffusion models (causing trajectory divergence), necessitating the equivariant formulation where the perturbation δ is added to the predicted noise rather than ignored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formal AT framework for diffusion models, filling a clear gap between robust classification and robust generation. The equivariance insight is broadly applicable to any regression-based generative model and provides a principled reason why naive AT transfer fails. Limitations include a 2.5× training cost, FID degradation on clean data (e.g., 28.68 vs 7.2 on CIFAR-10), testing only up to 64×64 on ImageNet, and evaluation restricted to Gaussian corruption. Open questions include whether the approach scales to EDM/latent diffusion at higher resolutions, whether it handles 100% corruption, and whether the equivariant regularization can be distilled into a fine-tuning procedure for pre-trained models.
