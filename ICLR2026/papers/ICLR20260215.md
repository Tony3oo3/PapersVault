## Problem

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards for LLMs suffers from an exploration bottleneck: on difficult problems, all sampled responses in a GRPO group are incorrect, causing group-normalized advantages to collapse to zero and eliminating all gradient signal. Masked diffusion LLMs possess a unique bidirectional inpainting capability that has not been exploited to address this zero-advantage dilemma, leaving a gap between the architectural potential of dLLMs and their RL training efficiency.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt containing a math problem and produces a structured output with a <reasoning> block followed by a <answer> block containing a boxed numerical value. During RL training, generation is constrained to 256 tokens with 128 diffusion denoising steps (block length 32), while evaluation uses 512 tokens. The distinctive input mechanism is that during inpainting, a subset of positions in the initially fully-masked completion sequence are pre-filled with ground-truth reasoning tokens (injected hints) that remain fixed throughout all denoising steps, while the model generates only the remaining masked positions.

## Method

IGPO modifies GRPO's sampling step: when all G responses in a group yield incorrect rewards (zero-advantage), the ground-truth reasoning trace is segmented into variable-length chunks (5–10 tokens each, excluding the final answer), a random fraction η ∈ [0.2, 0.6] of chunks are selected and injected as fixed hints into the masked completion, and the dLLM performs bidirectional inpainting to generate the remaining tokens. Only correctness-verified inpainted completions replace up to λG (λ=0.5) of the original wrong responses, restoring non-zero advantages and meaningful gradients. An entropy-based filter restricts gradient updates to the top 20% highest-entropy hint token positions, preventing destabilizing off-policy updates at positions where the model is already confident. The full recipe is preceded by Length-Aligned SFT on synthetically rewritten concise traces (≤1024 tokens via LLaMA-4-Maverick) that align SFT data length with the 256-token RL budget, avoiding the distribution mismatch caused by verbose 10k+ token traces.

## Results

The full training recipe (Length-Aligned SFT + IGPO) achieves 86.8% on GSM8K, 47.4% on MATH500, 25.9% on AMC (avg@16), and 13.2% on Minerva (pass@1), yielding an average of 43.3%—a +7.3 percentage-point improvement over the LLaDA-8B-Instruct baseline. IGPO alone (without SFT) reaches 83.1/42.8/17.5/12.1 (avg 38.9%), already surpassing the prior best full-attention dLLM (LLaDA-1.5, avg 37.1%). The method reduces all-wrong group occurrences by approximately 60% during RL training, restoring non-zero gradient signals in the zero-advantage regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The full pipeline (SFT + IGPO) outperforms all prior full-attention masked dLLM baselines across every benchmark: it beats LLaDA-1.5 by +3.5 on GSM8K, +4.8 on MATH500, +12.3 on AMC, and +4.4 on Minerva. Even IGPO alone (without SFT) surpasses LLaDA-1.5 on MATH500 (42.8 vs 42.6), AMC (17.5 vs 13.6), and Minerva (12.1 vs 8.8), though it trails slightly on GSM8K (83.1 vs 83.3). Against other RL methods applied to LLaDA-Instruct, IGPO improves over DiffuGRPO by +0.7/+2.6/+2.0/+1.8 and over UniGRPO by +0.9/+3.6/+2.5/+1.1 on the four benchmarks respectively. Among same-size autoregressive LLMs, Qwen2.5-7B still leads on MATH500 (49.8 vs 47.4), but the paper's dLLM matches or exceeds LLaMA3-8B on GSM8K. A compute-matched GRPO+resample ablation confirms that IGPO's gains come from directional inpainting guidance rather than additional sampling volume.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the masked diffusion LLM architecture (LLaDA) and the DiffuGRPO framework for policy optimization in dLLMs, repurposing the bidirectional inpainting capability—an architectural byproduct of stochastic masking training—as a mechanism for guided exploration in RL. Conceptually, it is closest to Prefix-RFT (Huang et al., 2025), which uses demonstration prefixes to steer autoregressive exploration, but extends this to the bidirectional setting where hints can be injected at arbitrary positions rather than only as left-to-right prefixes. The Length-Aligned SFT component addresses a practical gap in dLLM training pipelines where verbose SFT traces (often >10k tokens) mismatch the constrained generation budgets (256 tokens) used in RL and evaluation, a problem less acute for AR LLMs with KV caching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-advantage dilemma is a genuine bottleneck in group-based RL for dLLMs, and IGPO's ~60% reduction in all-wrong groups with a clean theoretical characterization (gradient scales as ρ(1−ρ), KL shift bounded linearly in mixture weight) provides a principled fix. However, the approach is tightly coupled to full-attention masked dLLMs and requires access to ground-truth reasoning traces at training time, limiting its applicability to settings where such supervision is unavailable. Open questions include whether the inpainting-guided exploration generalizes to non-mathematical reasoning tasks, to block-diffusion or other dLLM architectures, and whether the entropy-filtering and partial-injection design choices transfer to other RL objectives beyond GRPO.
