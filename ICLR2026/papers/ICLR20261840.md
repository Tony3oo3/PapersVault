## Problem

Existing PTQ methods for diffusion models treat all calibration samples equally and ignore the frequency evolution property of the denoising process, where low-frequency features are recovered early and high-frequency features late. The paper shows empirically that uniform sample weighting is suboptimal (18 of 50 random weightings beat it on FID) and that no prior quantization work exploits the per-timestep frequency structure to guide quantization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261840/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained full-precision diffusion model (DDPM for pixel-space 32×32 or LDM-4 for latent-space 256×256), a calibration set of generated samples at fixed step intervals across all denoising time steps (following Q-Diffusion), and a validation subset. It outputs a block-wise quantized noise estimation network with learned rounding functions (AdaRound) and EMA-based activation ranges, evaluated under W4A32, W4A8, and W8A8 settings. The quantization is performed layer-by-layer, with the sample and frequency weights re-optimized per block.

## Method

The core contribution is a bi-level meta-learning optimization that jointly learns per-sample weights ω and per-timestep frequency weights λ (a T×4 matrix over Haar DWT subbands a_ll, a_lh, a_hl, a_hh). The inner loop quantizes each block by minimizing a weighted combination of a reconstruction loss L_Q (matching full-precision block outputs) and a frequency loss L_F (weighted L2 differences across the four wavelet subbands), while the outer loop updates ω and λ via alternating gradient steps to minimize a validation loss that includes a regularization term L_Reg. This regularization penalizes increases in the low-to-high frequency weight ratio as timestep decreases, encoding the prior that later denoising steps should prioritize high-frequency fidelity. The learned weights are then fixed and used to perform the final block-wise quantization over the full calibration set.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 32×32 (DDPM), the proposed method achieves FID 4.21 (W4A32) and 4.25 (W4A8), outperforming TFMQ-DM by 0.52 and 0.53 FID respectively. On LSUN-Bedrooms 256×256 (LDM-4), it reaches FID 3.16 (W4A32) and 3.28 (W4A8), improving over TFMQ-DM by 0.44 and 0.40. On FFHQ 256×256, FID drops to 9.20 (W4A32) and 9.34 (W4A8), a 0.69 and 0.63 improvement over TFMQ-DM. On ImageNet 256×256 (class-conditional, 20-step DDIM), the method achieves FID 10.10 (W4A32) and 10.01 (W4A8), with the largest gains in the W4A32 setting (0.4 FID over TFMQ-DM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method is compared against six PTQ baselines: PTQ4DM, Q-Diffusion, PTQD, TFMQ-DM, APQ-DM, and TCAQ-DM. The strongest gains appear in low-bit settings: on CIFAR-10 W4A32, the method achieves FID 4.21 vs. TCAQ-DM's 4.28 and TFMQ-DM's 4.73; on LSUN-Bedrooms W4A8, it reaches FID 3.28 vs. TCAQ-DM's 3.65 and TFMQ-DM's 3.68. On FFHQ W4A32, the improvement over TFMQ-DM is the largest in the paper (9.20 vs. 9.89 FID, a 0.69 gap). However, the method does not uniformly dominate: on ImageNet W4A8, TCAQ-DM achieves a slightly lower FID (9.97 vs. 10.01), and on CIFAR-10 W8A8, TCAQ-DM also edges ahead (4.09 vs. 4.15 FID). Overall, the proposed method wins on the majority of dataset–bit-width combinations, with the most consistent advantage in the W4A32 and W4A8 settings where quantization error is most severe.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PTQ framework for diffusion models established by Q-Diffusion (calibration data construction) and TFMQ-DM (temporal feature maintenance quantization), departing from their assumption that all calibration samples contribute equally to quantization quality. It introduces meta-learning into diffusion model quantization for the first time, drawing on the meta-learning-for-quantization lineage (MetaQuantNet, MEBQAT, MetaMix) that previously targeted standard CNNs. The frequency-weighting component repurposes the known temporal frequency evolution of diffusion models (low-frequency recovery early, high-frequency late) from full-precision model analysis (Yang et al., 2023; Qian et al., 2024) into a quantization-specific loss, a connection no prior PTQ-for-diffusion work had made.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical validation that 18/50 random sample weightings outperform uniform weighting provides concrete evidence that the calibration-sample weighting problem is real and non-trivial, motivating the meta-learning approach. However, the FID improvements over the strongest baseline (TFMQ-DM) are modest (0.4–0.7) and the method adds substantial computational overhead from bi-level optimization with multiple alternating update loops. Open questions include whether the approach scales to large text-to-image models (e.g., Stable Diffusion XL), whether the frequency-weighting benefit persists at higher resolutions or with more complex noise schedules, and whether the added meta-learning complexity is justified given the moderate gains over simpler heuristics.
