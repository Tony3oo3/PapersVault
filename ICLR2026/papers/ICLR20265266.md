## Problem

Existing automated film generation systems produce visually templated, rhythmically flat content because they rely on LLM imagination for camera planning without professional cinematography knowledge, and simply concatenate video clips with limited or desynchronized audio. This results in incoherent or static shots within scenes and a flat, unengaging narrative experience that falls short of professional cinematic standards.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265266/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form text prompt plus optional sets of character and location reference images as input, and produces a fully editable, multi-track OTIO (OpenTimelineIO) timeline containing a video track and five audio tracks (background ambiance, musical scoring, voice-over, foley, and sound effects). Generated video clips are 1920×1080 at 153 frames each, synthesized by Kling Elements 1.6 conditioned on the planned camera language and reference images. The OTIO format ensures seamless integration with professional post-production software rather than delivering a flat, uneditable video file.

## Method

FilMaster operates in two stages. In the Generation Stage, a novel scene-level RAG framework treats an entire scene (multiple shots sharing spatio-temporal context and narrative objective) as a single unified query against a vector database of 440,000 annotated real film clip descriptions; the retrieved cinematic references then guide an LLM to synergistically re-plan coherent camera language (shot types, movements, angles) for all shots in that scene, avoiding the visual incoherence of independent shot-level retrieval. In the Coordination Stage, an Audience-Aware Cinematic Rhythm Control module emulates professional post-production: a Rough Cut is assembled and reviewed by an MLLM simulating a target audience demographic, whose structured feedback drives a Fine Cut (rearrange, trim, accelerate operations) and a multi-scale sound design pipeline that assigns background/music at scene level, voice-over at shot level, and foley/SFX at intra-shot event level, with RAG from a 46,826-asset audio library and LUFS normalization for mixing.

## Results

On the FilmEval user study (5-point scale), FilMaster achieves an average score of 3.79, with Camera Language (CL) at 3.76 and Cinematic Rhythm (CRh) at 3.82, representing a 74.17% improvement in CL and 79.26% in CRh over the best baseline (LTX-Studio). In automatic evaluation (Gemini-1.5-Flash), FilMaster scores 4.50 on CL and 4.32 on CRh, outperforming LTX-Studio (3.74/3.62) by 19.81% on average. On VBench, FilMaster achieves the highest Dynamic Degree score (0.9412 vs. 0.4591 for LTX-Studio), validating the effectiveness of its scene-level camera language design.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FilMaster outperforms all three baselines across every evaluation dimension. Against Anim-Director (Li et al., 2024), which produces static shots with no audio, FilMaster achieves +87.35% average improvement in automatic evaluation and +74.17% in CL from human ratings, primarily due to its scene-level RAG-based camera planning and multi-track sound design. Against MovieAgent (Wu et al., 2025), which uses templated camera language and only basic voice-over, FilMaster gains +105.99% on average in automatic evaluation, with particularly large margins in narrative pacing and video-audio coordination. Against the commercial LTX-Studio, which offers limited A/V sync and a single audio type, FilMaster still leads by +19.81% on average (automatic) and +29.8% in overall user rating (3.79 vs. 2.92), demonstrating that its audience-aware post-production workflow and 5-track soundscape (foley, SFX, VO, background, music) provide a meaningful advantage even over a polished commercial product. When controlling for the video generation model (Kling 1.6, denoted †), FilMaster's advantage over Anim-Director† and MovieAgent† widens to 65.15% and 94.49% respectively, confirming that the gains come from cinematic planning rather than the underlying video model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of video generation, LLM-based planning, and retrieval-augmented generation, but does not directly build on a single prior method. It departs from prior LLM-for-film systems (Anim-Director, MovieAgent, FilmAgent) that rely on LLM imagination for camera planning, instead repurposing scene-level RAG from NLP to retrieve cinematographic references from 440K real film clips. It also moves beyond virtual cinematography work (VC, Drucker et al.) that formulates camera control as constrained optimization in 3D spaces, learning cinematic principles from data rather than encoding them as rules. The multi-agent post-production pipeline draws on recent multi-agent LLM frameworks but applies them to a novel audience-aware editing and sound design workflow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FilMaster is among the first systems to treat professional cinematic principles—expressive camera language and audience-aware rhythmic pacing—as first-class, automated objectives in end-to-end film generation, producing industry-compatible OTIO outputs. The scene-level RAG insight (treating a multi-shot scene as a unified query rather than independent shots) is a conceptually clean solution to the visual incoherence problem, and FilmEval provides a much-needed evaluation framework for high-level cinematic quality that VBench lacks. Key limitations include reliance on proprietary models (Kling, GPT-4o, Gemini), a non-public 440K film dataset, and evaluation that is partly self-referential (MLLMs evaluating MLLM-generated content). Open questions include scalability to feature-length films, generalization to non-narrative content, and whether the approach can be made fully open-source.
