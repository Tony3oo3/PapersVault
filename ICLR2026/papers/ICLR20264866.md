## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on image generation model unlearning (IGMU) focus almost exclusively on perturbing textual prompts, which disrupts semantic alignment, requires external classifiers or auxiliary diffusion models, and fails against robust unlearning defenses like AdvUnlearn and RECE. Critically, these methods overlook the native multi-modal conditioning pathway of diffusion models, leaving a key vulnerability dimension unexplored. This paper asks whether optimizing the image modality alone—while keeping the text prompt unchanged—can systematically break unlearned IGMs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image depicting the erased concept (sourced from the internet, public datasets, or generated by other models), the original sensitive text prompt (left unmodified), and white-box access to the unlearned Stable Diffusion model (V1.4, 2.0, or 2.1). It produces an adversarial image prompt (a 512×512 RGB image decoded from the optimized 4×4×4 latent) that, when fed jointly with the text prompt through the model's multi-modal conditioning pathway, causes the unlearned model to regenerate the previously erased content. The optimization operates entirely within the unlearned model's own VAE encoder/decoder and U-Net, requiring no external components.

## Method

RECALL performs adversarial optimization entirely in the latent space of the unlearned diffusion model across three stages. First, the initial adversarial image is constructed by blending λ=0.25 of the reference image with isotropic Gaussian noise, then both it and the reference are encoded into latent space via the model's VAE encoder. Second, at each of 50 DDIM timesteps, the U-Net predicts noise residuals for both the reference latent and the adversarial latent under the same text-conditioned embedding; the adversarial latent is updated via 20 PGD iterations per step using momentum (β=0.9) and L1-normalized gradients to minimize the MSE between the two noise predictions, with periodic re-injection of the reference latent (γ=0.05 every 5 epochs) to preserve semantic consistency. Third, the optimized latent is decoded to an adversarial image and paired with the original text prompt for final generation. The key insight is that matching noise-prediction trajectories in the U-Net under shared text conditioning forces the unlearned model to follow the reference's denoising path, thereby recovering erased content while maintaining strong text-image semantic alignment—without modifying the prompt or requiring any external classifier or the original (pre-unlearning) model.

## Results

RECALL achieves average Attack Success Rates (ASR) of 80.77% (Nudity-I2P), 88.20% (Nudity-MMA), 65.44% (Nudity-ART), 97.40% (Van Gogh-style), 73.40% (Object-Church), and 97.00% (Object-Parachute) across ten state-of-the-art unlearning methods. It also attains the highest CLIP Score (30.28 average) for semantic alignment, surpassing UnlearnDiffAtk (28.00), P4D (25.00), CCE (20.01), and WACE (19.89). In terms of efficiency, RECALL requires only ~64 seconds per attack versus ~238s for P4D-N, ~232s for UnlearnDiffAtk, and ~243s for WACE-C on the Nudity task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RECALL consistently outperforms all four baselines across all six task settings. Against the strongest text-based baseline UnlearnDiffAtk (Zhang et al., ECCV 2024), RECALL improves average ASR by 16.90% (Nudity-I2P: 80.77 vs. 63.87), 11.68% (Nudity-MMA: 88.20 vs. 76.52), 21.46% (Nudity-ART: 65.44 vs. 43.98), 0.20% (Van Gogh: 97.40 vs. 97.20), 11.00% (Church: 73.40 vs. 62.40), and 37.20% (Parachute: 97.00 vs. 59.80). Against P4D (Chin et al., ICML 2024), RECALL gains 23.16% on Nudity-I2P and 58.80% on Parachute, with the largest margin on adversarially-enhanced unlearning methods like AdvUnlearn (60.56% vs. 2.82% for P4D-N on Nudity-I2P). CCE (Pham et al., ICLR 2024) and WACE (Lu et al., NeurIPS 2025) trail further behind, with CCE averaging 50.42% and WACE-C 57.04% on Nudity-I2P versus RECALL's 80.77%. Notably, RECALL also achieves the highest CLIP Score (30.28 avg.) for semantic alignment, whereas text-perturbing baselines like P4D (25.00) and CCE (20.01) degrade prompt-image coherence by modifying the original text.

## Relation to Prior Work

RECALL extends the line of adversarial attacks on unlearned diffusion models (P4D, CCE, UnlearnDiffAtk, WACE) by shifting the optimization target from the text modality to the image modality of multi-modal conditioning. Prior attacks perturb text prompts or inject learned tokens, but all overlook the native image-conditioning pathway of Stable Diffusion; RECALL repurposes PGD-style latent-space optimization (with momentum) to craft an adversarial image prompt guided by a single reference, while keeping the original text prompt unchanged. Conceptually, it reframes the attack surface: rather than finding a 'jailbreak' text, it finds an image that reactivates the erased concept through the model's own multi-modal fusion mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that adversarially-enhanced unlearning methods (AdvUnlearn, RECE), which were specifically designed to resist text-based adversarial prompts, remain highly vulnerable to image-based attacks (ASR 60–83%), undermining the assumption that adversarial training in the unlearning pipeline provides comprehensive protection. RECALL also serves as a lightweight auditing tool (~64 s per attack) for model owners to verify unlearning robustness before deployment. Open questions include whether the attack transfers to black-box or API-only settings, whether it generalizes to video and large multi-modal models, and whether certifiable unlearning mechanisms can be designed that are provably resistant to multi-modal adversarial inputs.
