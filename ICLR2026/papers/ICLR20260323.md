## Problem

Data-driven models in scientific computing (PDE solvers, weather forecasting, medical imaging) are inherently interpolative and can fail silently on out-of-distribution inputs, yet no practical method exists to certify the reliability of regression predictions. While OOD detection is well-studied for image classification, it remains severely underexplored for regression tasks that dominate scientific machine learning, leaving end-users without a 'certificate of trust' for AI-based scientific predictions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a new input x (a PDE initial condition, satellite humidity snapshot, MRI volume, or image) and the regression model's own prediction y_pred = Ψ_φ(x), concatenating them into a joint variable z = (x, y_pred) that is fed through the trained diffusion denoiser. The output is a scalar joint log-likelihood certificate computed by solving the probability-flow ODE, which is thresholded (median − 1.5σ from training samples) to yield an ID/OOD label; if a small set of ground-truth test samples is available, a scaled-exponential fit between error and log-likelihood additionally provides a quantitative a posteriori error estimate with uncertainty bounds.

## Method

A score-based diffusion model (UViT-type denoiser) is trained independently on joint (x, y) training pairs to approximate the joint distribution p(x, y), with no involvement of the regression model Ψ_φ during diffusion training. For a new input, the certificate is the estimated joint log-likelihood log p(x*, y_pred), computed by numerically integrating the probability-flow ODE using the denoiser's score (via Tweedie's formula) and a Skilling-Hutchinson stochastic divergence estimator. The key insight is that the joint likelihood decomposes as log p(x) + log p(y_pred|x): the input term captures data scarcity while the conditional term encodes task-specific prediction difficulty, and the paper demonstrates empirically and theoretically that p(x) alone is insufficient (e.g., NS-Sines receives high input likelihood yet large errors). A heuristic bound log ℓ ≤ α log ε − log p(x, y_pred) + O(ε^β) motivates the certificate, and the approach is zero-shot, requiring no ground-truth test samples for OOD detection. The method is architecture-agnostic, working with CNO, ViT, UNet, and C-FNO regression backbones, and extends naturally to classification (via stochastic label sampling) and segmentation (via noise-corrupted non-semantic pixels).

## Results

Across five scientific datasets (Wave Equation, NS-PwC, NS-MIX, MERRA-2, Brain Tumor Segmentation), the proposed Joint Likelihood-Based Certificate (JLBC) achieves the best average AUROC (0.945), accuracy (0.899), false positive rate (0.033), and false discovery rate (0.091) among all compared methods. On the most challenging NS-MIX dataset (mixture of multiple PDE distributions), JLBC achieves 0.947 ACC and 0.992 AUROC, substantially outperforming all other diffusion-based baselines (0.786–0.788 ACC, 0.886–0.918 AUROC). The method also demonstrates that input-only likelihood p(x) fails completely as a certificate (ACC drops to 0.404–0.487 on NS-MIX), confirming the necessity of the joint input/output formulation. Certificate computation requires only ~0.02–0.21 s per sample on a single RTX 4090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JLBC outperforms all four diffusion-based baselines (JDPath, JSFNS, JSBDDM, JMSSM) on average across ACC, FPR, FDR, and AUROC, with the largest margin on the most challenging NS-MIX dataset where JLBC achieves 0.947 ACC versus 0.786–0.788 for the others. On individual datasets, JMSSM slightly edges JLBC on Wave Equation AUROC (0.946 vs. 0.936), and JDPath/JMSSM achieve marginally higher ACC on MERRA-2 (0.989/0.987 vs. 0.956) and Brain (0.789 vs. 0.743), but JLBC maintains the lowest FPR and FDR in those settings. The non-diffusion OODC baseline, which requires ground-truth test samples, is substantially worse across all datasets (average ACC 0.617, FDR 0.457). In the Wave Equation experiment, JLBC also clearly surpasses Bayesian-style baselines MC-Dropout (AUROC 0.526–0.642) and Rate-In (AUROC 0.714–0.816), achieving AUROC 0.955 while being roughly 5× faster than Rate-In per sample. A critical ablation shows that replacing the joint likelihood p(x, y_pred) with the input-only likelihood p(x) causes all diffusion-based certificates to fail completely (ACC drops to 0.404–0.487 on NS-MIX), validating the task-aware joint formulation as the key design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the score-based diffusion framework of Song et al. (2020) from generative modeling to OOD detection, specifically by estimating the joint input/output likelihood p(x, y_pred) via the probability-flow ODE. It departs from prior diffusion-based OOD methods (DiffPath, SBDDM, MSSM) that operate on input-only distributions or trajectory features, arguing that task-awareness through the conditional p(y_pred|x) is essential for regression. The paper also extends existing OOD detection baselines to the joint-distribution setting for fair comparison, and positions itself against Bayesian uncertainty approaches (MC-Dropout, Rate-In) that have been applied to scientific ML. Conceptually, it bridges the density-based OOD detection literature (normalizing flows, Gaussian mixtures) with the scientific ML community, where regression dominates but OOD detection has been 'severely underexplored.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a critical gap: scientific ML (PDE solvers, weather forecasting, fluid dynamics) is overwhelmingly regression-based, yet OOD detection has been studied almost exclusively for image classification. The 'certificate of trust' framing is practically important for safety-critical applications where users need to know when a model's prediction is unreliable. Key open questions include: the theoretical bound (Eq. 2) is heuristic and lacks rigorous guarantees; the approach requires training and maintaining a separate diffusion model; calibration of ID/OOD boundaries remains ad-hoc (median ± 1.5σ); and the a posteriori error estimation, while promising, has only been demonstrated on a handful of datasets. Whether the joint-likelihood certificate generalizes to 3D problems, very high-dimensional outputs, or multi-step autoregressive forecasting remains to be seen.
