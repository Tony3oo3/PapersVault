## Problem

Vision-language model evaluation has relied predominantly on English-language benchmarks, and while multilingual benchmarks have expanded, many are translations of English datasets that fail to capture cultural nuances and regional knowledge. Reliable evaluation at the intersection of multilingual and multimodal tasks remains rare, leaving a significant gap in assessing how VLMs perform across diverse languages, scripts, and visual inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262742/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each question is a 4-option multiple-choice item written in its source language, optionally accompanied by one of eight image types (diagrams, graphs, photos, tables, formulas, maps, figures, text-rich images); 55% of the 20,911 questions require image understanding. Closed models receive Chain-of-Thought prompts translated into the question's language with answers wrapped in <ANSWER> tags, while open-weight models receive English instructions and output a JSON-structured choice field. The primary evaluation metric is macro-averaged accuracy across languages, with format-error rates tracked separately.

## Method

KALEIDOSCOPE is constructed through a large-scale open science effort involving contributors from 20 nations across four continents, sourcing real-world exam questions in their original languages rather than translating English benchmarks. The data pipeline involves automated parsing (PDF/web parsers, Mathpix OCR, GPT-4o for non-parsable content), LLM-assisted restructuring (Claude 3.5 Sonnet, GPT-4o) to align questions with images and answer choices, and a three-stage human validation process with two independent annotators at both collection and final review. Each of the 20,911 questions carries 17 metadata fields including source country, license, educational level, subject, and image type, enabling fine-grained diagnostics across modality, language, script, and domain. The evaluation protocol uses zero-shot CoT prompting for closed models and direct JSON answer generation for open-weight models, benchmarking 11 models ranging from 3B to frontier-scale across 18 languages and 14 subjects.

## Results

On KALEIDOSCOPE (20,911 MCQs across 18 languages and 14 subjects), Claude 3.5 Sonnet achieves the highest overall accuracy at 62.91% (55.63% multimodal, 73.54% text-only), followed by Gemini 1.5 Pro (62.10%) and GPT-4o (58.32%). Among open-weight models, Qwen2.5-VL-72B leads at 52.94% overall, while the smallest models (Molmo-7B-D, Pangea-7B) score around 31–33%. A consistent finding is the modality gap: all models perform substantially better on text-only questions, with GPT-4o showing the largest drop (21.6 percentage points) and Molmo-7B-D the smallest (3.69 points).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among closed models, Claude 3.5 Sonnet edges out Gemini 1.5 Pro by 0.8 points overall and 0.6 points on multimodal questions, while GPT-4o trails both by 4–5 points overall—partly due to a 6.52% format error rate (10.5% on multimodal) that drops its raw accuracy; when restricted to valid responses, GPT-4o recovers to 62.10% overall, nearly matching Gemini. Among open-weight models, Qwen2.5-VL-72B (52.94%) clearly outperforms Aya-Vision-32B (39.27%) and Molmo-7B-D (32.87%), and even surpasses GPT-4o's multimodal accuracy (48.40% vs. 49.80%) within 1.4 points. Qwen2.5-VL-7B (39.56%) consistently leads the 7–8B tier over Aya-Vision-8B (35.09%), Molmo-7B-D (32.87%), and Pangea-7B (31.31%). Pangea-7B, despite being trained on 39 languages, shows the worst multimodal performance (27.15%) and the highest format error rate (7.42%), with notable refusals on Telugu (452 questions) and Hindi (130). The largest performance gap between closed and open models is ~10 points overall, narrowing to ~7 points on multimodal tasks, suggesting that visual reasoning is a harder bottleneck for open-weight architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of multilingual and multimodal evaluation, a space the authors identify as largely unoccupied. Prior multilingual benchmarks (Global MMLU, INCLUDE, MEGA) focus on text-only tasks, while multimodal exam benchmarks (M3Exam, EXAMS-V, M5, MaXM) cover limited language sets or rely on translated content. KALEIDOSCOPE departs from the translation-based approach common in the field by sourcing 20,911 questions directly from real-world exams in their original languages, collected through a 20-nation open science collaboration, and combines 18 languages with 55% image-dependent MCQA across 14 subjects—a combination no prior benchmark achieves at this scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes concrete, actionable gaps: a modality gap that paradoxically widens with model scale (GPT-4o: 21.6% vs. Molmo: 3.7%), a persistent Latin-script bias across all evaluated models, and a stark STEM-vs-Humanities reasoning deficit (59.2% vs. 83.7% best-case accuracy). These findings suggest that current VLMs have not yet achieved robust cross-lingual, cross-modal reasoning and that scaling alone may not close the gap. Limitations include imbalanced language coverage (126–2000 questions per language), uncontrolled difficulty across educational systems, and the inherent constraints of MCQA format. Open questions include whether in-language training data can close the low-resource language gap and whether the STEM reasoning deficit reflects a fundamental architectural limitation.
