## Problem

LLMs demonstrate strong logical reasoning but their emotional intelligence lags significantly behind, as they struggle to console distressed users or adapt advice to evolving feelings. While Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has advanced in math, coding, and search, its application to multi-turn empathetic dialogue remains unexplored due to two key obstacles: the lack of a stable, scalable environment for conversational rollouts, and the absence of consistent, verifiable reward signals for emotional intelligence.

## Input / Output

The agent receives an initial user query and iteratively observes the growing dialogue history (up to 8 turns) from a simulated user, generating a response at each turn. In the 'think-then-say' variant, each output is structured as a <think>... block containing strategic reasoning followed by the user-facing reply, with a format reward penalizing violations of this syntax. The training reward is the terminal emotion score e_T ∈ [0,100] from the SAGE simulator, normalized to r_φ = e_T/100, serving as a single scalar signal for the entire multi-turn episode.

## Method

RLVER repurposes the SAGE evaluation framework as a live RL training environment: self-consistent affective user simulators (powered by DeepSeek-V3) are instantiated with diverse personas, backgrounds, goals, and hidden intentions, and after each agent response they perform multi-hop reasoning to update a deterministic emotion score and generate a coherent reply. This deterministic, interpretable emotion score replaces learned reward models, mitigating reward hacking. The agent (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) is optimized via PPO or GRPO using the terminal emotion score as the sole reward, with the 'think-then-say' scaffold enforcing explicit chain-of-thought reasoning before each reply to regularize the policy space. The framework requires no domain-specific supervised fine-tuning—improvements stem entirely from reward-driven optimization against the simulator's emotional dynamics.

## Results

On the Sentient Benchmark, RLVER with PPO and a think-then-say scaffold elevates Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct from a base score of 13.3 to 79.2, a nearly six-fold improvement, while raising the dialogue success rate from 2% to 42% and cutting the failure rate from 76% to 9%. On the out-of-domain Chit-Chat benchmark, the same model improves from 37.8 to 62.1. General capabilities are largely preserved: Math500 drops only from 77.8 to 76.6, LiveCodeBench improves from 26.7 to 28.0, and IFEval remains stable at 68.6 (vs. 70.4 baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the untrained Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model (Sentient score 13.3, 76% failure rate), which RLVER improves by roughly 6× to 79.2 with PPO + thinking. Against the top-5 proprietary models on the Sentient Leaderboard, the 7B RLVER model (79.2) nearly matches Gemini2.5-Pro (82.4) and GPT-4o (79.9), while substantially outperforming GPT-4.1 (68.2), Gemini2.5-Flash-Think (66.1), and OpenAI-o3 (62.7). On Chit-Chat, the RLVER model (62.1) trails all five frontier systems (64.7–83.3), indicating that generalization beyond emotional-support scenarios remains a gap. GRPO provides more stable but lower-ceiling gains (72.0 with thinking) compared to PPO (79.2), and the non-thinking variants underperform their thinking counterparts across both algorithms. General-capability benchmarks (Math500, LiveCodeBench, IFEval) show minimal degradation, confirming that empathetic specialization does not cause catastrophic forgetting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the SAGE evaluation framework (Zhang et al., 2025a)—originally designed as an LLM-as-a-judge system for measuring higher-order social cognition—into a live reinforcement learning training environment that emits deterministic emotion scores as verifiable rewards. Conceptually, it extends the RLVR paradigm (pioneered in math, coding, and search by DeepSeek-R1 and successors) to the domain of multi-turn empathetic dialogue, a setting where prior work has relied almost exclusively on supervised fine-tuning over annotated counseling corpora (ESConv, AUGESC) or rule-based templates. The 'think-then-say' scaffold draws on chain-of-thought prompting but is specifically designed as a policy-space regularizer for empathetic reasoning rather than logical problem-solving.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 7B open-source model can be elevated to near-frontier empathetic performance (13.3 → 79.2 on Sentient Benchmark) purely through RL with simulator-generated rewards, without domain-specific supervised data, while preserving math and coding ability. The finding that more challenging user simulators do not yield better outcomes—moderate, well-calibrated environments are preferable—offers a practical counterintuitive guideline for RL environment design. Key open questions include whether the 'verifiable' reward is truly verifiable (it is deterministic LLM output, not ground-truth), whether the approach generalizes beyond the SAGE simulator, and how to handle multi-party or multimodal affective interactions. The reliance on a single simulator for both environment and reward source introduces a potential circularity that limits the strength of the 'verifiable' claim.
