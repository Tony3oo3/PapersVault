## Problem

Generating explorable 3D scenes from a single image remains highly challenging because existing methods—whether panorama-based, iterative inpainting, or video-diffusion-based—suffer from severe geometric distortions, semantic drift, and cumulative errors when the viewpoint moves far from the original perspective. The core gap is the lack of a mechanism to maintain global 3D geometric consistency and photorealistic quality simultaneously under large, arbitrary camera motions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263248/cropped_page0_idx7.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a fully explorable 3D scene supporting novel view synthesis at arbitrary camera poses. Internally, it generates a 360° equirectangular panorama (via HunyuanPano-DiT), projects it into six 95°-FoV cubemap anchor views with 2.5° overlap, and lifts them into an explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting scaffold reconstructed in 0.5 s on an H20 GPU. The final output is a sequence of photorealistic rendered views guided by the scaffold, with a memory bank mechanism ensuring temporal consistency across long camera trajectories.

## Method

One2Scene decomposes single-image scene generation into three stages: (1) panorama generation to expand visual information, (2) a feed-forward 3D GS scaffold that reformulates monocular panoramic depth estimation as multi-view stereo matching across six sparse cubemap anchor views, leveraging VGGT's geometric priors with a novel bidirectional fusion module (C2E/E2C transformations with residual connections) to enforce cross-view depth consistency, and (3) scaffold-guided novel view synthesis using a Dual-LoRA strategy that processes high-quality anchor views and geometrically-rich (but artifact-containing) scaffold-rendered views through separate LoRA branches before fusing them via 3D attention. The key insight is that projecting a panorama into overlapping perspective views converts an ill-posed monocular depth problem into a tractable multi-view matching task, enabling transfer of robust geometric priors from large-scale multi-view datasets. A memory condition (closest-pose frame from a bank) further stabilizes long-sequence generation.

## Results

On the WorldScore-adapted benchmark (40 scenes across indoor/outdoor, real/stylized categories), One2Scene achieves the best scores across all metrics: NIQE 4.43, Q-Align 4.13, CLIP-I 89.95, TransErr 0.326, RotErr 0.107, and CamMC 0.389. For depth estimation, the zero-shot model surpasses all SOTA panorama depth methods on Stanford2D3D (AbsRel 0.0675, δ₁ 95.20%), and after fine-tuning achieves AbsRel 0.0391 on Matterport3D and 0.0444 on Stanford2D3D. The feed-forward 3DGS scaffold reconstructs a full 360° scene in 0.5 s on an H20 GPU (0.1 s on H100), a 5.6× speedup over AnySplat.

## Baselines & Comparisons

One2Scene outperforms all six scene-generation baselines on every reported metric. Against SEVA (the strongest pose-conditioned baseline), One2Scene improves Q-Align from 3.20 to 4.13, CLIP-I from 87.82 to 89.95, and CamMC from 0.558 to 0.389, indicating substantially better visual quality and geometric stability under large viewpoint shifts. Even when SEVA and VMem are augmented with One2Scene's anchor views (SEVA+Anchor, VMem+Anchor), they still trail One2Scene on all geometric metrics (e.g., CamMC 0.460 and 0.887 vs. 0.389), confirming that the explicit 3D scaffold provides scale and geometric constraints that 2D anchor views alone cannot. DreamScene360 and WonderJourney, which do not support free exploration, show the largest gaps (NIQE 8.40 and 4.97 vs. 4.43; CLIP-I 74.24 and 77.92 vs. 89.95). In the feed-forward 360° reconstruction ablation (Table 2), replacing One2Scene's scaffold with AnySplat degrades Q-Align from 4.13 to 3.61 and CamMC from 0.389 to 0.616, validating the bidirectional fusion module. On depth estimation (Table 3), the zero-shot model already beats Depth Anywhere (AbsRel 0.0675 vs. 0.1180 on Stanford2D3D), and fine-tuning further reduces AbsRel to 0.0391 on Matterport3D, surpassing HRDFuse (0.0967) by over 50%.

## Relation to Prior Work

One2Scene builds directly on VGGT as its geometric backbone, extending it with a bidirectional C2E/E2C fusion module to handle the extremely sparse overlap between cubemap anchor views, and on SEVA's architecture for the final synthesis stage, augmented with a Dual-LoRA strategy to jointly condition on high-quality anchor views and scaffold-rendered views. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant paradigm of treating a generated panorama as a single 2D image for reconstruction (as in DreamScene360, DreamCube) or relying on iterative navigation/inpainting (WonderJourney, Pano2Room) by instead reformulating monocular panoramic depth estimation as a multi-view stereo matching problem across six sparse cubemap views, thereby inheriting robust geometric priors from large-scale multi-view training data. The scaffold-guided synthesis paradigm—using an explicit 3D GS scaffold as a geometric prior for a diffusion-based novel view generator—distinguishes it from video-diffusion approaches (VMem, ReconX) that attempt online reconstruction and suffer from error accumulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The scaffold-guided decomposition offers a practical and principled solution to the long-standing problem of geometric collapse under large viewpoint extrapolation, with the 0.5-second feed-forward scaffold construction making the pipeline viable for near-real-time interactive applications. The explicit 3D scaffold also resolves the scale ambiguity that plagues pose-conditioned synthesis methods like SEVA, producing physically plausible geometry without per-scene optimization. However, the authors acknowledge that subtle inconsistencies remain in generated views and that post-reconstruction processing (à la CAT3D) is still needed for full geometric closure; the approach is also limited to static scenes and relies on the quality of the initial panorama generation. Open questions include whether the scaffold paradigm extends to dynamic or interactive scenes, whether larger-scale training data can further close the gap on depth accuracy, and whether the Dual-LoRA conditioning strategy generalizes to other multi-condition generative settings.
