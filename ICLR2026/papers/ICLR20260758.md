## Problem

Standard sentence-level pooling methods (mean, max, [CLS]) treat LLM token hidden states as an independent set, discarding the relational structure captured by self-attention and making them vulnerable to signal dilution when only a few tokens carry task-relevant information. This paper asks whether reframing pooling as relational learning over a token graph, rather than direct compression, can yield more robust and accurate sentence embeddings from frozen LLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260758/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GLOT consumes the sequence of L token-level hidden states (X ∈ R^{L×d}) produced by a completely frozen LLM backbone (encoder or decoder, ranging from BERT to Mistral-7B) and outputs a single sentence-level embedding vector z ∈ R^D. The LLM is never updated; only the ~8.92M-parameter GLOT head and a minimal task-specific classifier (linear layer or contrastive loss) are trained, requiring as little as 0.42 GB of GPU memory. Sequences are truncated to 128 tokens for GLUE and 512 for IMDB, with padding handled via attention masks.

## Method

GLOT performs three stages: (1) it constructs a sparse token-similarity graph by computing pairwise cosine similarities between token vectors and retaining only edges exceeding a threshold τ (default 0.6), (2) it refines node representations through a 2-layer GAT (TOKEN-GNN) with hidden dimension 128, using Jumping Knowledge in 'cat' mode to fuse multi-layer features, and (3) it aggregates the refined vectors via a learnable attention readout that scores each token with a tanh-MLP, normalizes with softmax, and computes a weighted sum. The key insight is that this graph-based message passing enables multi-hop token interactions before compression, generalizing DeepSets (recovered when K=0) and standard pooling (recovered with fixed weights), while the thresholded graph construction prunes noisy edges to focus message passing on the strongest semantic connections. The approach is trained end-to-end with a task-specific loss (cross-entropy, MSE, or contrastive) while keeping the backbone frozen.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark, GLOT consistently outperforms all baselines across six frozen backbones (BERT through Mistral-7B). For example, on BERT, GLOT achieves 47.49 CoLA MCC (vs. 29.20 for AdaPool) and 90.25% SST-2 accuracy (vs. 87.72%); on Mistral-7B, it reaches 54.30 CoLA MCC and 94.38% SST-2. On the diagnostic stress test with 90% random distractor tokens, GLOT maintains >97% accuracy while AdaPool drops to 78.4% and Mean pooling to 63.8% on Mistral-7B. In terms of efficiency, GLOT uses only 8.92M trainable parameters (vs. 167.8M for LoRA) with 0.42 GB GPU memory and 13.4 ms batch runtime, achieving a 100× speedup over LoRA while still outperforming it on CoLA (53.29 vs. 48.23 MCC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AdaPool (Brothers, 2025), the strongest learnable-pooling baseline, GLOT wins on every GLUE task across all six backbones. The margin is largest on relational tasks: CoLA MCC improves by 18.29 points on BERT (47.49 vs. 29.20) and 11.81 on LLaMA-3B (55.13 vs. 43.32), while QQP F1 gains 22.04 on BERT (62.19 vs. 40.15). On the diagnostic stress test at 90% distractors, GLOT holds 97.2% accuracy on Mistral-7B versus 78.4% for AdaPool, a 18.8-point gap that widens with noise. Against LoRA (Hu, 2022) with r=64 on Mistral-7B, GLOT achieves higher CoLA MCC (53.29 vs. 48.23) and STS-B Spearman (80.51 vs. 54.54) while using 20× fewer trainable parameters (8.92M vs. 167.8M), 80× less GPU memory (0.42 vs. 33.50 GB), and running 108× faster per batch (13.4 ms vs. 1454.6 ms). GLOT also outperforms Full Fine-Tuning on CoLA (53.29 vs. 49.63 MCC) and STS-B (80.51 vs. 55.68 Spearman) with the same Mistral-7B backbone, suggesting that fine-tuning the entire 7B-parameter model actually degrades generalizable representations on small GLUE subsets.

## Relation to Prior Work

GLOT departs from the dominant 'compressive paradigm' of sentence pooling (mean, max, CLS, and learnable variants like AdaPool) by reframing the problem as relational learning before aggregation. It builds on the DeepSets observation that standard pooling treats tokens as an independent set, and on Jumping Knowledge Networks for cross-layer feature fusion, but introduces a novel latent token-similarity graph constructed from frozen LLM hidden states and processed by a lightweight GAT. Unlike prior graph-based NLP work (TextGCN, attention-diffusion GNNs) that relies on corpus-level statistics or fixed syntactic trees, GLOT constructs a dynamic, per-sentence graph purely from the intrinsic geometry of the backbone's output, positioning itself as the first method to perform explicit relational learning within the pooling head itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: GLOT requires only ~9M trainable parameters and 0.42 GB of GPU memory versus 32+ GB for LoRA or full fine-tuning of a 7B model, making high-quality sentence embeddings from frozen LLMs accessible on consumer hardware. The diagnostic stress test (97%+ accuracy at 90% distractor ratio vs. 63–78% for baselines) provides compelling evidence that relational learning before compression is a fundamentally more robust strategy than global summary statistics. Open questions include whether the fixed cosine-similarity threshold can be made learnable, whether the approach extends to fine-tuned backbones, and whether the 'relational learning before compression' paradigm transfers to other modalities such as ViT patch pooling. The method's reliance on a hand-tuned sparsity threshold and only two GNN layers also leaves room for architectural refinement.
