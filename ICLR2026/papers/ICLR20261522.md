## Problem

Visual perception and image generation have been addressed with fundamentally different paradigms—discriminative representation learning versus generative modeling—leading to models that excel at one task but underperform at the other. Discriminative representations generalize poorly to unseen patterns and overlook fine-grained details, while generative models like diffusion models lack high-level semantic understanding. Prior unified approaches either sacrifice generation quality for perception or treat the two tasks as one-directional enhancements, failing to establish a true mutual feedback loop within a single model.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images (encoded into latent space via a VQGAN encoder) and class labels as text prompts. For perception, a single noise-free forward pass through the denoising network with FPN aggregation of four intermediate decoder blocks produces task-specific outputs: class logits, per-pixel segmentation masks, depth maps, or bounding boxes. For generation, the full reverse diffusion process (200–250 DDPM steps) produces synthesized images at 256×256 resolution. During inference for generation, the explicit semantic conditioning is replaced by the current noisy latent, as the network has internalized the semantic cues in its weights.

## Method

GENREP unifies perception and generation in a single diffusion model (LDM-8 or DiT-XL) through three mechanisms. First, generative visual perception learning approximates the conditional distribution p(x|y) via MCMC over intermediate reverse-diffusion states (with burn-in m=50 and thinning k=2), computes the posterior p(y|x) via Bayes' theorem, and distills it into the discriminative head through a KL-divergence regularizer. Second, semantic-driven generation learning modulates the reverse-diffusion noise parameters—augmenting the mean prediction with a learned semantic correction and scaling the variance by an MLP on the perception branch's semantic embedding—to steer synthesis toward desired content. Third, a gradient alignment strategy decomposes the perception and generation gradients into parallel and orthogonal components, then symmetrically damps the parallel (conflicting) components using an adaptive retention factor α=((cos_sim+1)/2)², preserving non-conflicting information while reducing interference. Training proceeds in two phases: first optimizing perception loss alone to establish semantic features, then jointly optimizing both losses with gradient alignment and a momentum-updated semantic reference network.

## Results

GENREP achieves 92.9% top-1 accuracy on CUB-200 and 57.8% on ObjectNet (LAION-5B pretrained), surpassing prior diffusion-based classifiers by 5.3–8.4 points. For dense prediction, it reaches 0.057 AbsRel on NYUv2 depth estimation, 54.6% mIoU on ADE20K closed-set segmentation, and 34.7% mIoU on open-vocabulary segmentation. On open-vocabulary detection, GENREP-DiT attains 56.5 AP on MS COCO and 36.0 AP on LViS v1.0. For image generation, it improves FID to 2.09 on ImageNet-256 (DiT backbone) and 3.84 on CelebA-HQ (LDM backbone), setting new state-of-the-art across all generation benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CUB-200, GENREP (92.9%) outperforms the strongest diffusion-based baselines Clark et al. (91.5%) and Li et al. (91.8%) by 1.4 and 1.1 points respectively, and surpasses the best CNN/Transformer baseline HorNet-B (91.2%) by 1.7 points. On ObjectNet, the OOD gap is even more pronounced: GENREP (57.8%) beats Li et al. (52.5%) by 5.3 points and Clark et al. (49.4%) by 8.4 points, while the best ImageNet-pretrained baseline (ConvNeXt-B, 40.9%) trails by 16.9 points. For depth estimation on NYUv2, GENREP (0.057 AbsRel) edges out ECoDepth (0.059) but is slightly behind DepthAnything (0.056), which benefits from 62M unlabeled depth samples. In closed-set segmentation on ADE20K, GENREP (54.6% mIoU) surpasses VPD (53.7%) and Mask2Former (51.3%) by 0.9 and 3.3 points. For open-vocabulary detection, GENREP-DiT (56.5 AP on COCO, 36.0 AP on LViS) leads SAS-Det (53.0/33.5) by 3.5 and 2.5 AP. On image generation, GENREP improves FID over its base architectures: 2.09 vs. 2.27 (DiT-XL) on ImageNet and 3.84 vs. 5.11 (LDM-4) on CelebA-HQ, confirming that the unified training does not sacrifice—and in fact enhances—generative quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

GENREP builds on the LDM/DiT diffusion backbone and the observation (Zhao et al., 2023; Clark & Jaini, 2023) that diffusion features carry useful semantic information for perception, but departs from prior diffusion-based perception work that sacrifices generation ability. Unlike unified models such as UniGS or Diff-2-in-1 that frame perception as low-level map generation, GENREP explicitly transfers distributional knowledge from the generative process to the discriminative head via MCMC-based posterior estimation, and reciprocally uses perception-derived semantics to modulate the reverse diffusion trajectory. The gradient alignment mechanism for reconciling two competing optimization objectives is a novel contribution not present in prior multi-task or joint discriminative-generative learning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GENREP demonstrates that a single diffusion model can simultaneously achieve SOTA on six perception benchmarks and three generation benchmarks without the typical trade-off, suggesting that the perceived incompleteness of discriminative representations and the low-level bias of generative models can be mutually resolved. The MCMC-based distributional distillation and semantic-driven noise modulation offer reusable components for future unified vision-language models. Key limitations include reduced inference speed (12.6 FPS vs. 19.6 for MaskFormer) and the reliance on a full reverse diffusion pass for the MCMC approximation, leaving open questions about efficiency for real-time deployment and extension to video or multimodal settings.
