## Problem

Full-head 3D-aware GANs conventionally condition on view angle, which introduces a strong directional bias: generation quality and diversity are significantly higher at the conditional view than at all other views, causing global incoherence across head regions. Removing conditioning entirely leads to rapid mode collapse, making training infeasible. The paper identifies the need for a view-invariant conditioning signal that stabilizes training without encoding viewing-direction information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261670/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The generator takes a 512-dimensional CLIP image feature extracted from the subject's frontal view as its semantic condition, along with camera parameters for volume rendering, and produces 512×512 RGB images from arbitrary viewpoints. For single-view inversion (via PTI), a single in-the-wild RGB image is optimized against the generator to recover the latent code and generator parameters, yielding a full 360° 3D head. The model is trained on the 11.2-million-image BalanceHead360 synthetic dataset over 32 million images on 8 NVIDIA H20 GPUs.

## Method

BalanceHead replaces view-angle conditioning with a view-invariant semantic condition: the CLIP feature of the frontal view is shared across all views of the same subject, decoupling generation capability from any specific viewing direction. To supply the required multi-view training data, the authors build BalanceHead360 by using FLUX.1 Kontext to extend ~350k real frontal images into full 360° collections, with a Qwen2.5-VL-based filtering agent removing artifacts and identity drift. The generator builds on HyPlaneHead (StyleGAN2 backbone with a hybrid planar-spherical representation) and incorporates a new ViCiCo loss that jointly enforces consistency between rendered image content and both the camera view and the semantic condition via shuffled negative pairs fed to the discriminator. The 3D-aware adversarial training naturally tolerates the imperfect 3D consistency of FLUX-generated multi-view data, as geometric tri-plane representations make 2D artifacts hard to reproduce, effectively filtering them out during learning.

## Results

On the 360° full-head synthesis task, BalanceHead achieves a FID-random of 3.67 (with ViCiCo loss) and FID-front of 3.51, substantially outperforming the view-conditioned baseline (FID-random 13.82, FID-front 8.42) and the view-semantic-conditioned baseline (FID-random 46.24, FID-front 5.90). In the single-view GAN inversion comparison, BalanceHead attains the best diversity (MKNND 0.456) and lowest reconstruction error (LPIPS 0.076) among all full-head 3D-aware GANs, surpassing HyPlaneHead (MKNND 0.291, LPIPS 0.084), SphereHead (MKNND 0.345, LPIPS 0.083), and PanoHead (MKNND 0.391, LPIPS 0.086).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BalanceHead outperforms all three full-head 3D-aware GAN baselines on both diversity and reconstruction fidelity. Against HyPlaneHead (the strongest prior method), BalanceHead improves MKNND from 0.291 to 0.456 (+57%) and reduces LPIPS from 0.084 to 0.076, indicating both greater identity diversity and sharper reconstructions. SphereHead and PanoHead trail further behind, with PanoHead additionally suffering from severe feature-penetration artifacts and SphereHead producing blurry outputs with limited detail. EG3D and GGHead, while strong on near-frontal views, cannot generate the back-head region at all since they are trained exclusively on near-front-view datasets and lack suitable 3D representations for non-frontal areas. The ablation in Table 1 further confirms that the view-invariant semantic conditioning (FID-random 3.67) is the key driver of improvement over conventional view conditioning (FID-random 13.82) even when both are trained on the same balanced dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the view-conditioning strategy inherited from EG3D through PanoHead, SphereHead, and HyPlaneHead, which the authors identify as the root cause of directional bias and global incoherence in full-head 3D GANs. It builds directly on HyPlaneHead's hybrid planar-spherical representation and extends SphereHead's ViCo loss into the ViCiCo loss by adding semantic-condition consistency. The key conceptual shift is replacing camera-angle conditioning with a view-invariant CLIP semantic feature anchored to the frontal view, paired with a novel 11.2M-image synthetic dataset (BalanceHead360) generated via FLUX.1 Kontext to ensure balanced multi-view coverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 3D-aware GANs are robust to imperfect, 3D-inconsistent multi-view data from 2D generative models challenges the field's assumption that 3D training requires strictly consistent (and expensive) multi-view captures, opening a scalable data-generation paradigm. The 11.2M-image BalanceHead360 dataset and the semantic-conditioning framework are immediately reusable across tri-plane, tri-grid, and spherical representations, as validated in the paper's cross-architecture ablations. Open questions include whether this inconsistency-tolerant training strategy extends to broader 3D generation tasks (full-body, objects) and whether the reliance on FLUX.1 Kontext for data synthesis introduces subtle distributional biases that limit real-world generalization beyond the training domain.
