## Problem

Visual-prompted object detection is expected to outperform text prompts on rare categories due to natural visual-domain compatibility, yet it consistently underperforms text prompts overall. The paper identifies the root cause as a lack of global discriminability in visual prompts: high intra-class variance and heavy inter-class entanglement in the embedding space, stemming from the limited 'current image prompt, current image detect' training paradigm that restricts each iteration to a small N-way classification task.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and K user-specified normalized bounding boxes as visual prompts, from which multi-scale deformable cross-attention extracts prompt embeddings. Two evaluation protocols are supported: Visual-G (generic) uses the mean of N prompts sampled from multiple training images per category, while Visual-I (interactive) uses a single GT box from the test image itself. Output is a set of predicted bounding boxes with per-category confidence scores computed via dot-product similarity between proposal features and prompt embeddings, followed by sigmoid activation.

## Method

DETR-ViP builds on Grounding DINO by inserting a T-Rex-style visual prompt encoder between the Swin Transformer backbone and the deformable encoder, then introduces three key mechanisms. (1) Global Prompt Integration aggregates visual prompts across all samples in a batch, computes per-category mean prototypes, and shares them as a unified classifier, thereby expanding the negative pool and implicitly simulating cross-image prompting. (2) Visual-Textual Prompt Relation Distillation applies a cross-entropy loss between the softmax of the text-text cosine similarity matrix (teacher) and the softmax of the visual-visual cosine similarity matrix (student), transferring semantic relational structure from CLIP text embeddings into the visual prompt space without requiring cross-modal alignment. (3) Selective Fusion introduces a gating vector computed from an auxiliary similarity branch that suppresses prompts for categories absent from the image before attention-based fusion, ensuring stable detection regardless of the number of user-provided prompts. The total loss combines standard DINO detection losses, image-text contrastive alignment, and the relation distillation term.

## Results

Under the Visual-G protocol, DETR-ViP-T achieves 43.2 mAP on COCO and 41.1 mAP on LVIS, while DETR-ViP-L reaches 50.2 mAP on COCO and 49.1 mAP on LVIS. On domain-shifted benchmarks, DETR-ViP-T attains 31.2 mAP on ODinW and 23.6 mAP on Roboflow100, and DETR-ViP-L improves to 34.5 and 24.7 respectively. The gains are most pronounced on rare and common categories (e.g., +9.4 APc and +5.2 APr over T-Rex2-T on LVIS), reflecting the improved discriminability of visual prompts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DETR-ViP-T outperforms T-Rex2-T by +4.4 mAP on COCO and +3.7 mAP on LVIS, with particularly large gains on rare (+5.2 APr) and common (+9.4 APc) categories. Against YOLOE-v8-L (same training data), DETR-ViP-T leads by +6.9 mAP on LVIS overall, +8.7 on APc, and +1.9 on APr. On domain-shifted benchmarks, DETR-ViP-T surpasses the larger T-Rex2-L by +3.4 mAP on ODinW and +5.1 mAP on Roboflow100, indicating stronger generalization. DETR-ViP-L further extends these advantages, beating T-Rex2-L by +3.7 mAP on COCO and +1.5 mAP on LVIS. Notably, DETR-ViP achieves these results using only O365 + GoldG for training, whereas T-Rex2 additionally leverages OpenImages, HierText, CrowdHuman, and SA-1B, making the comparison favorable to the baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

DETR-ViP builds directly on Grounding DINO's encoder-decoder architecture and T-Rex's deformable-attention visual prompt encoder, but departs from T-Rex2's paradigm where visual prompts are extracted as a byproduct of text-prompted training. The paper's core positioning is that visual-prompted detection has been 'largely overlooked' and treated as secondary to text-prompted detection, so it reframes the problem: rather than improving text prompts and hoping visual prompts benefit indirectly, it diagnoses why visual prompts specifically underperform (lack of global discriminability) and applies targeted fixes—global prompt integration, visual-textual relation distillation, and selective fusion. The relation distillation loss draws on the contrastive learning and knowledge distillation literature but applies it to the relational structure of prompt embeddings rather than individual cross-modal alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a well-supported diagnosis (via t-SNE, similarity histograms, and the new IISR metric) that visual prompts suffer from high intra-class variance and inter-class entanglement, and shows that targeted optimization of the prompt embedding space yields substantial gains (+4.4 mAP on COCO, +6.9 on LVIS over YOLOE) with less training data than T-Rex2. The selective fusion finding—that naive early fusion is highly sensitive to prompt count and can cause complete detection failure with a single prompt—addresses a practical robustness gap that prior methods overlooked. A key limitation is that the paper does not address text prompts, which can encode attributes (color, spatial position) that visual prompts inherently cannot; the authors suggest combining discriminative visual prompts with guided language prompts as a promising future direction. Open questions include whether the IISR-guided prompt optimization generalizes to other prompt modalities (keypoints, segments) and whether the global prompt integration strategy scales efficiently to very large category sets.
