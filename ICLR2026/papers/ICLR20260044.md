## Problem

The native 3D Gaussian Splatting parameterization (position, quaternion, scale, SH coefficients, opacity) is fundamentally ill-suited as a learning space for neural networks: it exhibits non-uniqueness (quaternion sign duality and geometric symmetries produce one-to-many mappings) and numerical heterogeneity (components live on disparate manifolds with vastly different scales). These properties cause embedding collisions, training instability, and poor cross-domain generalization in downstream tasks such as compression, generation, and editing, yet prior work has largely assumed Gaussian parameters are naturally compatible with neural learning.

## Input / Output

The method operates on individual 3D Gaussian primitives, converting each into a colored point cloud of P=144 points uniformly sampled from its iso-probability ellipsoid surface (the 2D submanifold where the Gaussian density equals a fixed radius). The encoder produces a 32-D latent vector; the decoder reconstructs the submanifold field via two implicit neural networks queried on a unit sphere grid, then recovers original Gaussian parameters through PCA (for the covariance matrix) and spherical harmonic fitting. Training uses 500K randomly generated Gaussian primitives rather than real scene data, making the model inherently domain-invariant.

## Method

The core contribution is a submanifold field representation that maps each Gaussian primitive to a color field defined on its iso-probability ellipsoid, which the paper proves is a unique (injective) encoding of the Gaussian's geometric and photometric properties, eliminating the ambiguities of the parametric space. The SF-VAE architecture uses a PointNet encoder on the sampled colored point cloud and a decoder composed of two implicit neural networks (a coordinate transform and a color field) that query a canonical unit sphere grid to reconstruct the field. Training employs a novel Manifold Distance loss based on the Wasserstein-2 optimal transport between input and reconstructed colored point clouds, which the paper shows correlates more closely with perceptual quality metrics (PSNR, LPIPS) than parameter-space L1 distances. The approach is trained self-supervised on randomly generated primitives, and the learned 32-D embeddings demonstrate smooth latent interpolation, noise robustness, and improved downstream performance in unsupervised clustering and Gaussian Neural Field regression compared to raw parameter baselines.

## Results

On ShapeSplat, the proposed SF-VAE (submanifold field representation) achieves 63.41 PSNR, 0.990 SSIM, and 0.010 LPIPS, substantially outperforming the parametric MLP/MLP baseline (37.51 PSNR, 0.888 SSIM, 0.152 LPIPS) and the hybrid parametric-encoder/SF-VAE-decoder baseline (44.73 PSNR, 0.896 SSIM, 0.136 LPIPS). On Mip-NeRF 360, SF-VAE reaches 29.83 PSNR and 0.079 LPIPS versus 18.82 PSNR and 0.452 LPIPS for the parametric baseline. The proposed Manifold Distance (M-Dist) metric correlates more closely with perceptual quality (PSNR, LPIPS) than L1 parameter distance, validating its use as a reconstruction objective.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper uses self-implemented, capacity-matched parametric VAE baselines (rather than external 3DGS learning methods) to isolate the effect of representation choice. The full SF-VAE (submanifold field encoder + decoder) dominates both parametric baselines on every perceptual metric across both datasets: on ShapeSplat it gains +25.9 dB PSNR over the MLP/MLP baseline and +18.7 dB over the MLP/SF-VAE hybrid; on Mip-NeRF 360 it gains +11.0 dB and +8.9 dB respectively. LPIPS drops by an order of magnitude (0.152→0.010 on ShapeSplat; 0.452→0.079 on Mip-NeRF 360), indicating far superior perceptual fidelity. Notably, the hybrid baseline (parametric encoder + SF-VAE decoder) already improves substantially over the fully parametric pipeline, suggesting that the decoder's submanifold-field output space is itself beneficial, but the full representation change (encoder operating on the iso-probability surface) is what unlocks the large gains. In cross-domain transfer (Table 2), SF-VAE trained on ShapeSplat generalizes to Mip-NeRF 360 at 19.19 PSNR versus 9.75 for the parametric baseline, and a model trained purely on random synthetic data outperforms both real-data-trained models, confirming domain-agnostic generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) but departs from the growing body of work (PixelSplat, MVSplat, LatentSplat, DiffGS, EAGLES) that treats the native Gaussian parameter tuple θ = {μ, q, s, c, o} as a natural input/output space for neural networks. The key conceptual departure is a formal argument that this parametric space is fundamentally ill-suited for learning due to non-uniqueness (quaternion sign duality, geometric symmetries) and numerical heterogeneity across manifolds. The proposed submanifold field representation — a color field on an iso-probability ellipsoid — draws on differential geometry and optimal transport (Wasserstein-2 distance) to provide a provably unique, geometrically consistent encoding, combined with a PointNet-based VAE for embedding learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a foundational issue that much of the 3DGS learning literature overlooks: the parameterization was designed for gradient-based optimization, not for neural network feature spaces, and this mismatch causes training instability, poor generalization, and discontinuous latent spaces. The finding that a model trained purely on randomly generated Gaussians outperforms domain-specific training on real datasets suggests the representation is genuinely domain-agnostic, which could simplify multi-domain 3DGS pipelines. Limitations include operating at the single-primitive level without modeling inter-splat structure, and validation is primarily on reconstruction rather than full generative or editing pipelines, leaving open whether the representation advantage persists in more complex downstream tasks.
