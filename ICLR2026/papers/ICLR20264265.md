## Problem

Existing RL-based macro placement methods optimize oversimplified proxy objectives (e.g., macro HPWL) and neglect well-established EDA domain expertise such as dataflow guidance, macro grouping, periphery biasing, and I/O keepout, as well as timing feedback from later-stage evaluations. This gap between academic RL solutions and industrial practice produces placements that diverge significantly from expert-designed layouts and fail to meet sign-off PPA requirements in real production flows.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a synthesized netlist (from which a macro-level dataflow graph is extracted via BFS with a 2-hop cap), macro size/footprint specifications, and a fixed-outline canvas discretized into a 2D grid. The state at each step is a multi-channel visual input comprising the current layout canvas, a position feasibility mask, four expert-derived masks (dataflow, grouping, periphery, I/O keepout), a wire mask, and next-step masks. The agent outputs a sequence of grid coordinates (one per macro, following a predefined ordering by connectivity density and footprint), producing a complete macro placement that feeds into downstream global placement, routing, and static timing analysis for PPA evaluation.

## Method

EXPlace integrates EDA expertise into an RL framework (PPO-trained CNN with local and global branches) from two complementary angles. First, Expert Knowledge Injection converts four placement heuristics—dataflow-weighted Manhattan distance, bounding-box grouping cost, periphery distance, and I/O overlap—into incremental step-wise dense rewards and corresponding 2D masks that are concatenated into the state representation; each cost decomposes naturally into pairwise or per-macro terms so that placing one macro yields a dense signal without requiring full-layout evaluation. Second, Expert Workflow Imitation emulates the human iterative refinement loop by applying DPO-based fine-tuning: the policy samples multiple trajectories, partitions them into preferred/rejected sets based on static timing analysis (TNS/WNS) results, and minimizes a DPO loss to shift the trajectory distribution toward better timing without needing a differentiable timing model. A constrained action variant using corner stitching (snapping to macro corners or canvas boundaries) is optionally activated for designs with uniform macro shapes to further promote regularity and periphery bias.

## Results

On the ICCAD 2015 Contest Benchmark (8 cases), EXPlace achieves the best average rank on 5 of 6 metrics, with headline improvements of 32.53% on TNS and 7.74% on WNS over the runner-up method (LaMPlace). On the OpenROAD Benchmark (6 cases), EXPlace attains the best average rank across all six metrics (rWL, WNS, TNS, DRC, Power, Area), reducing TNS by 23.06% versus DREAMPlace and by 5.97% versus the strongest RL baseline MaskRegulate, while achieving zero DRC violations on the bp_fe case. Timing-driven DPO fine-tuning further improves TNS by 12.29% and WNS by 22.68% on average over the pre-trained policy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the analytical placer DREAMPlace 4.1.0, EXPlace dominates on every metric, with TNS improving from an average rank of 4.12 to 1.12 and WNS from 3.75 to 1.75. Versus the RL baseline MaskPlace (which optimizes only HPWL), EXPlace wins on all six metrics, confirming that expert-knowledge-informed rewards substantially outperform purely data-driven wirelength optimization. Compared to MaskRegulate (which adds only a periphery mask to MaskPlace), EXPlace achieves superior overall performance on rWL, NVP, WNS, and TNS, though MaskRegulate edges out EXPlace on rOverflowV due to its extremely tight peripheral packing. Against the BBO method LaMPlace (the overall runner-up on ICCAD 2015), EXPlace still secures a 32.53% average TNS improvement and 7.74% WNS improvement, demonstrating that RL with integrated expert priors can surpass learned-mask-guided black-box search. On OpenROAD, EXPlace outperforms the expertise-driven BBO method Hier-RTLMP by 5.77% on rWL, 4.77% on WNS, 20.54% on TNS, and 84.48% on DRC, establishing that RL with structured expert masks can exceed strong hand-crafted heuristics while also enabling iterative timing refinement that BBO methods cannot support.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mask-based RL framework established by MaskPlace (Lai et al., 2022), which introduced pixel-level visual representations (wire masks) and dense step-wise rewards for sequential macro placement via PPO. The key departure is the systematic integration of four EDA domain expertise types—dataflow, macro grouping, periphery biasing, and I/O keepout—as additional mask channels and dense reward terms, going beyond the single periphery constraint in MaskRegulate (Xue et al., 2024) or the pure HPWL objective in MaskPlace. For the second stage, the paper repurposes Direct Preference Optimization (Rafailov et al., 2023) from NLP to perform timing-driven fine-tuning, a novel application that emulates the iterative refinement workflow of human EDA engineers. Conceptually, the work bridges the gap between academic RL placers that optimize oversimplified surrogates and industrial BBO tools (e.g., Hier-RTLMP) that encode expert heuristics but cannot leverage backend PPA feedback for iterative improvement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that comprehensive domain expertise integration in RL can close a significant portion of the gap between automated and human-designed placements, with 32.53% TNS improvement over the runner-up on ICCAD 2015—substantially easing timing closure in practical design flows. The DPO fine-tuning stage shows that timing-specific optimization (12.29% TNS, 22.68% WNS gains) is achievable without sacrificing wirelength (only 1.77% HPWL change), validating that proxy-objective optimization and sign-off timing can be decoupled. Key limitations include the high cost of timing-driven fine-tuning (15–30 hours per case due to repeated STA evaluations) and case-by-case RL training, though inference is fast (~5–20 seconds). Open questions include developing fast timing predictors to replace expensive STA, improving generalization across heterogeneous designs, and whether the expertise-injection paradigm transfers to other sequential EDA stages (routing, CTS).
