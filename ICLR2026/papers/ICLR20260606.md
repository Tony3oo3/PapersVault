## Problem

Existing hallucination detection methods for LLMs rely on output-level signals (perplexity, semantic entropy) or probe full hidden states without separating the reasoning component from semantic content, making them fragile on complex, reasoning-intensive tasks. The paper identifies that the hidden state space encodes both linguistic expression and internal reasoning processes, but prior work has not provided a principled mechanism to disentangle these two signals for detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260606/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a question and a single generated answer, HARP extracts hidden states at each token position from the final decoder layer and projects them onto the reasoning subspace basis (≈5% of the original hidden dimension, e.g., 256 out of 3584 for Qwen-2.5-7B). The resulting low-dimensional projections feed a 2-layer MLP (hidden dim 1024, ReLU) that outputs a per-token hallucination score in [0,1]; the maximum token score is taken as the QA-pair score, and a binary decision is made by thresholding at α=0.5. During training, beam search generates 10 candidate answers per question to construct supervision labels, but at inference only one sampled answer is required.

## Method

HARP establishes that the LLM hidden state space admits a direct-sum decomposition into a semantic subspace (dominating token prediction) and an orthogonal reasoning subspace (encoding intermediate reasoning). By performing SVD on the unembedding weight matrix W_unemb and applying the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem with k = 95%·d, the top-k right singular vectors span the semantic subspace while the trailing 5% span the reasoning subspace. Hidden states are then projected onto the reasoning subspace basis V_R, yielding compact features that concentrate reasoning information while filtering most noise. A supervised 2-layer MLP trained with binary cross-entropy on these projections produces the hallucination score. The key distinction from prior probing methods is that HARP explicitly discards the semantic component and uses only the ~5%-dimensional reasoning projection, which simultaneously reduces feature dimensionality, suppresses noise, and improves cross-dataset robustness.

## Results

HARP achieves state-of-the-art hallucination detection AUROC across all four QA datasets and both backbone models (Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct and LLaMA-3.1-8B). The headline result is 92.8% AUROC on TriviaQA with Qwen (+7.5% over the previous best) and 92.9% with LLaMA (+16.6%). On the most challenging dataset TyDiQA (long-context reading comprehension), HARP reaches 88.4% (Qwen) and 86.6% (LLaMA), substantially outperforming the best baseline (EigenScore at 74.8% and 82.4% respectively). HARP is a single-pass method, matching the efficiency of Perplexity and HaloScope while surpassing multi-sampling methods like Semantic Entropy and EigenScore.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HARP outperforms all six baselines on every dataset–model combination. Against the closest single-pass competitor HaloScope (Du et al., 2024), HARP gains +7.5 pp on TriviaQA (Qwen: 92.8 vs. 85.3) and +16.6 pp on TriviaQA (LLaMA: 92.9 vs. 76.2), while also leading on NQ Open by +23.3 pp (Qwen) and +26.7 pp (LLaMA). Against the strongest multi-sampling baseline EigenScore (Chen et al., 2024), HARP improves by +13.6 pp on TyDiQA (Qwen: 88.4 vs. 74.8) and +4.2 pp on TyDiQA (LLaMA: 86.6 vs. 82.4), the dataset where EigenScore is most competitive. Perplexity (Ren et al., 2023) and LN-Entropy (Malinin & Gales, 2021) degrade sharply on TyDiQA (30.5% and 47.1% for Qwen), whereas HARP maintains 88.4%. Semantic Entropy (Farquhar et al., 2024) and Lexical Similarity (Lin et al., 2022) trail HARP by 10–20 pp across most settings. Notably, HARP achieves these gains with a single forward pass, avoiding the repeated sampling overhead of the multi-sampling baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

HARP repurposes SVD-based mechanistic interpretability (previously applied to attention heads by Merullo et al. and Cheng et al.) by targeting the Unembedding layer specifically, and reframes hallucination detection as a subspace-projection problem rather than a learned-probe or output-consistency problem. It departs from probing-based detectors (HaloScope, EigenScore) that operate on full hidden states or require multiple samplings, by showing that the orthogonal complement of the Unembedding layer's row space—the reasoning subspace—carries the most discriminative signal. The cognitive 'Reasoning → Expression' analogy provides a novel theoretical motivation absent from prior hallucination detection work, which typically treats hidden states as monolithic feature vectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HARP offers a practical, single-pass hallucination detector that reduces input features to ~5% of the hidden state dimension while outperforming multi-sampling baselines, making it attractive for deployment in latency-sensitive settings. The Reasoning Patch experiment (injecting CoT-derived reasoning-subspace components into a non-CoT run to elicit correct multi-step reasoning) provides compelling causal evidence that the identified subspace genuinely encodes reasoning, not just correlated noise. Open questions include whether the 95% rank threshold and the direct-sum decomposition generalize to non-decoder architectures, non-QA tasks (e.g., summarization, code generation), and whether the hallucination-mitigation demo in Appendix D can be scaled beyond toy interventions.
