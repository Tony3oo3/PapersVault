## Problem

Existing large vision-language models handle visual perception tasks (detection, segmentation, counting) in a task-specific manner, requiring separate modules, training data, and objectives for each task, which limits scalability and cross-task generalization. While recent work has applied reinforcement learning to individual visual perception tasks, no unified framework exists that addresses multiple perception task types within a single shared model using a common training procedure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263188/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image and a free-form text instruction (e.g., a referring expression, a class name, or a reasoning-intensive query) and first generates a structured chain-of-thought reasoning process enclosed in think tags before producing its answer. The answer is a list of bounding boxes (x1, y1, x2, y2) and center points (x, y) in normalized coordinates; depending on the user-specified task type, the system then either returns the bounding boxes directly (detection), feeds them into SAM2 to produce binary segmentation masks (segmentation), or simply reports the count N (counting). This detect-then-segment/count paradigm means the core model always outputs the same intermediate representation regardless of the downstream task.

## Method

VisionReasoner initializes from Qwen2.5-VL (reasoning/localization) and SAM2 (mask generation) and is trained entirely with GRPO reinforcement learning on only ~7k samples drawn from four datasets (LVIS, RefCOCOg, gRefCOCO, LISA++), without any annotated reasoning data. The key design is a unified reward mechanism combining format rewards (thinking-structure compliance, answer-format correctness, non-repetition penalty) with accuracy rewards (optimal one-to-one matched IoU, bbox L1 distance, and point L1 distance), all computed via an efficient batch-computation + Hungarian-algorithm matching pipeline that achieves a 4× speedup over naive matching. The paper's central insight is that detection, segmentation, and counting are all instances of multi-object cognition, so a single reward structure and training loop can optimize all three simultaneously. Ablations confirm that the non-repeat reward prevents degenerate reasoning loops, that reasoning length adapts to query complexity, and that the approach generalizes to VQA tasks without any VQA-specific training data.

## Results

VisionReasoner-7B achieves 37.7 AP on COCO-val (detection), 66.3 gIoU on ReasonSeg-val (segmentation), and 89.2% accuracy on CountBench-test (counting), representing relative improvements of 29.1%, 22.1%, and 13.2% over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline, respectively. On referring expression grounding, it reaches 88.6 AP on RefCOCO-val and 86.1 AP on RefCOCOg-val, outperforming all LVLM baselines. The model is trained on only ~7k samples across four datasets and evaluated zero-shot across ten benchmarks spanning detection, segmentation, and counting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary LVLM baseline Qwen2.5-VL-7B, VisionReasoner achieves a large 8.5-point gain on COCO-val (37.7 vs. 29.2) and consistent improvements on RefCOCO+ val (83.6 vs. 82.3) and RefCOCOg val/test (86.1/87.5 vs. 84.7/85.7), while slightly trailing on RefCOCO testA (90.6 vs. 91.7) and RefCOCO+ testA (87.9 vs. 88.2). Among task-specific detectors, DQ-DETR still leads on COCO (50.2 vs. 37.7) and RefCOCO testA (91.0 vs. 90.6), but VisionReasoner surpasses it on RefCOCO+ val (83.6 vs. 81.7) and RefCOCOg val/test (86.1/87.5 vs. 82.8/83.4). On segmentation, VisionReasoner outperforms Seg-Zero-7B by 3.7 points on ReasonSeg-val (66.3 vs. 62.6) and 6.1 points on ReasonSeg-test (63.6 vs. 57.5), though it trails slightly on RefCOCO testA (78.9 vs. 80.3). On counting, it leads Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 10.4 points on CountBench-test (89.2 vs. 78.8) and 12.0 points on PixMo-test (70.1 vs. 58.1). On VQA benchmarks (OCRBench, RealWorldQA, MMMU-Pro, ChartQA, DocVQA), VisionReasoner matches or slightly exceeds Qwen2.5-VL-7B despite no VQA-specific training, confirming that the RL-based perception training does not degrade general visual-language capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of RL-enhanced LVLMs (VisualRFT, Seg-Zero, EasyR1) by moving from task-specific RL training to a unified reward mechanism that simultaneously optimizes detection, segmentation, and counting within a single model. It builds on Qwen2.5-VL as the backbone and SAM2 as a plug-in segmentation module, while adopting GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) as the training algorithm. The key departure from prior unified perception models like Kosmos is the replacement of cross-entropy supervised fine-tuning with RL-based optimization, which necessitates the Hungarian-algorithm-based multi-object matching pipeline for reward computation. The paper also draws on the chain-of-thought reasoning paradigm from DeepSeek-R1-Zero, applying it to visual perception without requiring annotated reasoning traces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework demonstrates that a single 7B model trained on only ~7k samples can handle ten diverse perception tasks competitively, offering a practical path toward general-purpose visual perception without task-specific architectures. The finding that reasoning traces remain faithful (97% image consistency, 90.5% answer consistency) without any annotated reasoning data suggests RL can induce interpretable intermediate reasoning in perception models. However, the approach still depends on SAM2 as a separate segmentation module, and COCO detection performance (37.7 AP) remains well below dedicated detectors like DQ-DETR (50.2 AP), indicating the unified paradigm has not yet closed the gap with specialized models on standard benchmarks. Open questions include whether the framework scales to video, 3D, or denser multi-object scenes, and whether the Hungarian matching overhead becomes prohibitive at larger object counts.
