## Problem

Input pre-filtering methods for LLM unlearning (e.g., ECO, GUARDRAIL) screen prompts before execution but rely solely on superficial input analysis, making them systematically vulnerable to adversarial attacks. The paper demonstrates that noise prefix attacks can cause a 1,150-fold surge in information leakage and composite question attacks can reverse 42.1% of unlearned knowledge, exposing a critical robustness gap that has been largely unexplored compared to the well-studied vulnerabilities of model-based unlearning.

## Input / Output

The system accepts a raw user query that may contain adversarial perturbations (non-semantic noise tokens, misleading instruction prefixes, or harmful sub-queries embedded among benign ones) and produces a single coherent, safe text response. Unlike pre-filtering methods that make a binary block/pass decision on the input alone, PoRT generates an initial response first, then jointly evaluates the cleaned question–answer pair, and may iteratively regenerate the response through self-correction before returning the final output. The pipeline is designed so that in realistic low-prevalence scenarios (≤0.1% harmful queries), the added latency over a single-pass baseline is under 1%.

## Method

PoRT comprises three modules operating sequentially. First, In-Context Prompt Cleaning (IPC) dynamically constructs a few-shot prompt by retrieving top-k syntactically similar demonstrations from an extensible library and selecting a task-specific instruction, instructing the LLM to strip adversarial noise, disentangle composite queries into numbered sub-questions, and generate both a cleaned question and an initial response. Second, a Post-Judgment module applies a selective classifier trained with CCL-SC (confidence-aware contrastive learning) on an LLM2Vec encoder with an MLP head; unlike pre-filtering, it jointly scores the cleaned (Q, A) pair and outputs one of three labels—compliant, non-compliant, or abstain—using a confidence threshold τ to flag uncertain predictions. Third, Selective Multi-Round Thinking (SMT) is triggered only for non-compliant or abstain outputs, prefixing targeted regeneration instructions that prompt the LLM to rethink and correct its own response; regenerated outputs are re-evaluated by the classifier in an iterative loop until all sub-responses are compliant, at which point they are consolidated into a final coherent answer. The key architectural insight is that evaluating the model's actual response alongside the cleaned input catches indirect leaks (e.g., descriptive queries that avoid forbidden keywords but still elicit forgotten facts) that input-only classifiers fundamentally cannot detect.

## Results

On TOFU (Llama-2-7b-chat-hf, 5% split), PoRT achieves an HFQ of 0.8474 and Model Utility of 0.6721, substantially outperforming the SOTA pre-filtering baseline ECO (HFQ 0.5184, MU 0.6648) and all model-based methods. On WMDP (Zephyr-7b-beta), PoRT reduces hazardous-knowledge accuracy to 25.1%/25.8%/24.8% across Bio/Chem/Cyber (near the 25% random-guess baseline) while perfectly preserving MMLU at 58.9%. Under adversarial attacks, ECO's Forget Probability surges 447-fold (0.0018→0.8055) on TOFU and its WMDP accuracy rebounds to 67.0%, whereas PoRT's HFQ drops by less than 1.51% and WMDP accuracy remains near the 25% baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PoRT outperforms the SOTA pre-filtering method ECO on TOFU by a wide margin in HFQ (0.8474 vs. 0.5184 on the 5% split) while matching its Model Utility (0.6721 vs. 0.6648), indicating more authentic unlearning without utility loss. On WMDP, PoRT matches ECO's unlearning effectiveness (both near the 25% random baseline) while perfectly preserving MMLU at 58.9%, whereas model-based methods like GA (57.3%) and GD (52.9%) degrade general capability. Under adversarial attacks, the gap becomes dramatic: ECO's Forget Probability surges 447-fold on TOFU and its WMDP accuracy rebounds to 67.0%, while PoRT maintains HFQ above 0.8320 and WMDP accuracy near 25%. Model-based baselines (GA, GD, NPO, SimNPO, RMU) all fail on at least one axis—GA causes catastrophic utility collapse (MU→0.0000 on TOFU 10%), while NPO and SimNPO achieve poor HFQ (≤0.57) due to incomplete knowledge removal. GUARDRAIL and the simple Prompt baseline are consistently the weakest, with HFQ below 0.37 and significant utility degradation, confirming that shallow input-level heuristics are insufficient for robust unlearning.

## Relation to Prior Work

PoRT departs from the dominant pre-filtering paradigm (ECO, GUARDRAIL) that judges only the input prompt before generation, instead shifting to a post-judgment architecture that evaluates the joint question–answer pair after inference. Its selective-classification core is built directly on CCL-SC (Wu et al., 2024) and the abstain mechanism from Geifman & El-Yaniv (2017), while the In-Context Prompt Cleaning module repurposes few-shot in-context learning (Brown et al., 2020; Levy et al., 2023) as an adversarial-input sanitizer. The multi-round thinking component draws on iterative self-correction literature (Xi et al., 2025; Tian et al., 2025) but triggers it selectively via the classifier's abstain signal rather than unconditionally. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against both model-based unlearning (GA, NPO, RMU) and the closest agentic approach (ALU, Sanyal & Mandal 2025), arguing that a single LLM's intrinsic reasoning suffices where ALU requires a multi-agent ensemble.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a critical and previously under-explored robustness gap: the SOTA pre-filtering method ECO suffers a 1,150-fold information-leakage surge under simple noise-prefix attacks and a 42.1% reversal of unlearning under composite-question attacks, undermining confidence in deployed unlearning systems. PoRT offers a practical, low-overhead (<7% latency in worst case) alternative that maintains near-random accuracy on WMDP and HFQ > 0.83 on TOFU under attack, making it a viable drop-in for production unlearning pipelines. Open questions include whether the demonstration library can be curated automatically via red-teaming, whether the post-judgment classifier itself is vulnerable to adversarial examples, and whether the fine-grained control problem (forgetting specific attributes while retaining others) can be addressed within this framework.
