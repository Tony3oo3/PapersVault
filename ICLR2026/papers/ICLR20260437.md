## Problem

Standard on-policy RLVR discards all rollout experiences after a single gradient update, wasting computational resources and forfeiting opportunities to learn from prior successful explorations. While experience replay is well-established in classical RL, the role of experience characteristics—what makes a reasoning trajectory genuinely valuable for optimization—remains unexplored for large reasoning models. This paper is the first to systematically identify which properties of past rollouts (correctness level and entropy) predict their utility for RLVR training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text-based reasoning questions (math problems, general reasoning prompts) and produces chain-of-thought trajectories ending in a boxed final answer. During training, each question generates K=8 rollouts from the current policy; successful trajectories are stored in a replay buffer keyed by question, with correctness rates and per-token entropies computed online. The optimized policy is evaluated via Pass@1 or Avg@32 on verifiable benchmarks, with answers extracted by a rule-based verifier.

## Method

ExGRPO operates in two phases. In experience management, successful trajectories are partitioned into difficulty buckets by rollout correctness rate, sampled with a Gaussian-weighted distribution centered at 0.5 to prioritize medium-difficulty questions, and for each sampled question the lowest-entropy trajectory under the current policy is selected as the replay anchor. In experiential policy optimization, a mixed mini-batch combines on-policy GRPO samples with replayed trajectories, where the replayed trajectory is corrected by per-token importance sampling ratios and modulated by a policy-shaping transform f(w)=w/(w+β) that amplifies low-probability signals while damping high-probability ones to preserve exploration. A delayed-start mechanism activates replay only after batch Pass@1 exceeds a threshold, and a retired set removes fully-solved questions to focus optimization on partially mastered problems.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Math 7B, ExGRPO achieves an in-distribution average of 48.3 and out-of-distribution average of 58.3, improving over the on-policy RLVR baseline (45.3 / 56.4) by +3.0 and +1.9 points respectively. Across all five backbone models (1.5B–8B), ExGRPO delivers average gains of +3.5 (ID) and +7.6 (OOD) over on-policy RLVR. In the continual RLVR setting on LUFFY, ExGRPO reaches 51.4 ID / 60.2 OOD, outperforming both the on-policy continuation (48.7 / 60.7) and the original LUFFY checkpoint (50.1 / 57.8) on in-distribution tasks. The extension to continuous-reward RL (Table 2) shows ExGRPO improving over GRPO by +1.7 average points on ArenaHardV2, IFEval, and IFBench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExGRPO outperforms all four zero-RLVR baselines on Qwen2.5-Math 7B: it leads PRIME-Zero by +7.6 ID / +16.9 OOD, Oat-Zero (Dr.GRPO) by +4.6 ID / +13.1 OOD, GPG-Zero by +3.9 ID / +16.7 OOD, and RePO-Zero by +9.1 ID / +11.5 OOD. Against the direct on-policy RLVR baseline, ExGRPO gains +3.0 ID and +1.9 OOD, with the largest per-benchmark improvements on AIME24 (+6.7) and AIME25 (+3.2). In the continual RLVR setting on LUFFY, ExGRPO (51.4 ID / 60.2 OOD) beats the on-policy continuation (48.7 / 60.7) on in-distribution tasks but trails slightly on OOD (−0.5), which the authors attribute to reduced on-policy exposure during joint optimization. Notably, ExGRPO stabilizes training on Llama-3.1-8B Base where on-policy RLVR collapses entirely (3.4 ID → 6.1 ID, 1.2 OOD → 30.8 OOD), and it outperforms RePO under matched training conditions (48.3 vs 39.2 ID, 52.3 vs 46.8 OOD).

## Relation to Prior Work

ExGRPO builds directly on the GRPO/Dr.GRPO RLVR framework and the classical experience replay literature (Lin, 1992; Schaul et al., 2016), adapting them to the setting of large reasoning models. It departs from recent RLVR replay methods (RePO, RLEP, RRL, ARPO) by being the first to systematically analyze which properties of a reasoning experience—rollout correctness and trajectory entropy—determine its learning value, then design a bucketed-sampling and entropy-minimizing selection pipeline around those findings. The policy-shaping mechanism is borrowed from LUFFY (Yan et al., 2025), but here it is applied specifically to off-policy replayed trajectories rather than expert demonstrations, and is shown to be critical for preventing exploration collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and widely felt inefficiency in RLVR training: the discarding of rollout experiences after a single gradient update. The practical takeaway—that medium-difficulty questions paired with low-entropy trajectories form the highest-value replay signal—is actionable for anyone scaling RLVR pipelines. Limitations include the focus on binary verifiable rewards (open-ended or dense-reward tasks remain unaddressed), the neglect of potentially informative 'valuable failures,' and the fact that the continuous-reward extension is only preliminary. Open questions include whether the entropy-based selection generalizes to multi-modal or agentic reasoning, and whether the Gaussian bucketing heuristic is optimal or merely a convenient proxy for a richer difficulty signal.
