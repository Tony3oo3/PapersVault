## Problem

Existing LLM-based TPP models such as Language-TPP are confined to temporal, categorical, and textual modalities, lacking the ability to incorporate visual information or generate rich multimodal content. Moreover, tokenizing images into hundreds of patch tokens per event causes sequence lengths to explode, making the O(N²) self-attention bottleneck prohibitively expensive for the long-range dependencies needed to produce coherent analytical text over extended event histories.

## Input / Output

Each event in the input sequence carries a 32-bit timestamp (encoded as four byte-level special tokens), a categorical type (dedicated special tokens), a free-text description (standard Qwen2.5-VL tokenizer), and a 224×224 visual patch (a single  placeholder aligned with the vision encoder's output rather than hundreds of patch tokens). The model autoregressively generates the next event's time, type, and a natural-language analysis or QA answer; because Qwen2.5-VL does not support image generation, visual output is not produced. Under the 4096-token context window, the adaptive compression mechanism extends the effective history from ~113 to ~292 events on average (up to 2008).

## Method

MM-TPP is built on Qwen2.5-VL-3B with LoRA and follows a sequence-to-sequence paradigm where multimodal events are organized into a structured template with modality-specific prefix tokens. The key architectural contribution is an adaptive inter-event compression mechanism: when the absolute difference between consecutive inter-event intervals |τ_i − τ_{i-1}| falls below a threshold Δ (set to 0.2 s, the median of the empirical distribution), the current event is collapsed into a single <|similar_event|> token instead of its full multimodal template, preserving burst structure while drastically reducing token count. This sequence-level strategy is deliberately distinct from intra-event token pruning or merging used in efficient MLLMs, because TPP components (exact timestamps, type labels, descriptive text) are semantically dense and cannot be safely discarded. Training proceeds in two stages: continued pre-training with next-token prediction over compressed and uncompressed event sequences, followed by supervised fine-tuning on prompt–response pairs for time prediction, type classification, and text generation, with loss applied only to response tokens.

## Results

On DanmakuTPP, MM-TPP achieves the lowest time-prediction RMSE of 5.2987 (vs. 5.3035 for the next-best TPP-LLM) and a type accuracy of 27.62%, though it trails S2P2 (31.48%) and NHP (30.74%) on accuracy. On TAXI-PRO, MM-TPP sets new state-of-the-art on both metrics: RMSE 0.3310 (vs. 0.3336 for TPP-LLM) and accuracy 77.56% (vs. 76.47% for RMTPP). On the DanmakuTPP-QA closed-ended benchmark, MM-TPP outperforms the fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline on 6 of 8 tasks, including a 190.45 RMSE on Task-3 (vs. 220.43) and 44.00% ACC on Task-8 (vs. 43.00%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MM-TPP achieves the best time-prediction RMSE on both datasets, narrowly beating TPP-LLM (5.2987 vs. 5.3035 on DanmakuTPP; 0.3310 vs. 0.3336 on TAXI-PRO) and Language-TPP (5.2987 vs. 5.3845; 0.3310 vs. 0.3376). On type accuracy, MM-TPP leads on TAXI-PRO (77.56% vs. RMTPP's 76.47%) but trails S2P2 (31.48%) and NHP (30.74%) on DanmakuTPP, where it scores 27.62%. Among the LLM-based baselines, MM-TPP consistently outperforms both TPP-LLM and Language-TPP on every metric across both datasets. On the DanmakuTPP-QA closed-ended tasks, MM-TPP surpasses the fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-3B (same base model) on 6 of 8 tasks, with the largest gap on Task-3 (RMSE 190.45 vs. 220.43) and Task-5 (0.10 vs. 0.16), while slightly trailing on Task-2 (1.49 vs. 1.35). Compared to larger MLLMs like Qwen2.5-VL-72B and Gemma3-27B, MM-TPP achieves competitive or superior results on most tasks despite being a 3B model, demonstrating the benefit of task-specific TPP fine-tuning over general-purpose scaling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Language-TPP (Kong et al., 2025), which first integrated textual information into TPP modeling via LLMs, by adding a visual modality and treating text generation as a first-class output alongside time and type prediction. It sits at the intersection of the Transformer-based TPP line (THP, SAHP, AttNHP) and the LLM-based TPP line (TPP-LLM, Language-TPP), but departs from both by building on a multimodal LLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL) and introducing a sequence-level temporal-similarity compression mechanism that is distinct from intra-event token pruning used in efficient MLLMs. The TAXI-PRO dataset construction also builds on the classic NYC Taxi benchmark used in Easy-TPP, augmenting it with map patches and templated descriptions to create a multimodal evaluation scenario.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper advances TPPs from a purely predictive paradigm to a generative one, enabling models to produce coherent analytical text conditioned on multimodal event histories—a capability absent in all prior TPP work. The adaptive compression mechanism is practically important for real-world streams (e.g., DanmakuTPP sequences exceeding 9,600 events) where fixed context windows would otherwise truncate critical history. Key limitations include the absence of image generation (constrained by the Qwen2.5-VL backbone), evaluation on only two datasets, and single-GPU experiments that limit scalability claims. Open questions include whether hybrid compression strategies can handle more diverse temporal patterns, and whether omni-modal backbones (e.g., Chameleon) can close the generation gap for visual outputs.
