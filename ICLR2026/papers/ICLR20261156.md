## Problem

One-step distillation of flow-matching policies reduces inference latency but overlooks two critical factors: the initial noise distribution remains a fixed, uninformed Gaussian rather than being optimized toward high-value actions, and the resulting deterministic point-to-point mapping provides no mechanism for controlling policy stochasticity during online exploration. This paper addresses both gaps by learning a value-guided generative prior and introducing explicit entropy control into the distillation process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261156/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes environment states (low-dimensional state vectors or 64×64 RGB images) and produces a continuous action distribution parameterized as a Gaussian with learned mean and standard deviation. At inference, a lightweight CVAE decoder first generates a state-conditioned advantage prior (a noise vector), which is then fed into the single-step student actor; for online exploration, actions are sampled from the full distribution, while evaluation uses only the deterministic mean. The entire inference pipeline requires just two forward passes (VAE decoder + student), keeping latency at ~0.51 ms per step.

## Method

GSFlow operates in two phases. First, a Q-Guided Prior Learning phase uses Advantage Noise Selection: for each state, N candidate actions are generated by the flow-matching teacher with different random noises, and the noise producing the highest-Q action is designated the 'advantage noise'; a conditional VAE is then trained to model the state-conditioned distribution of these advantage noises, replacing the standard Gaussian prior with an informed, potentially multi-modal distribution. Second, an Entropy-Regularized Distillation phase trains a dual-headed student policy (outputting both mean and std) with a composite loss combining L2 distillation to the teacher (computed on the deterministic mean for low variance), negative Q-value maximization (using sampled actions), and an entropy bonus with a learned temperature that automatically balances exploration and exploitation. The theoretical analysis frames the prior as amortized best-of-N optimization, bounding the performance gap by the CVAE reconstruction error scaled by the Lipschitz constant of the value landscape.

## Results

On the OGBench offline benchmark, GS-flow achieves an average score of 47.1, outperforming the strongest baseline FQL (38.5) by 8.6 points and the next-best flow method IFQL (21.8) by 25.3 points. On D4RL AntMaze, GS-flow reaches 86.1 average, surpassing FQL (83.5) and all diffusion-based methods. In the offline-to-online setting, GS-flow achieves 88.6 average on OGBench (vs. FQL's 67.6) and 96.8 on D4RL AntMaze, while on the challenging Puzzle-4x4 task it improves from 17% to 100% success rate, matching RLPD and far exceeding FQL's 38%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GS-flow outperforms all 10 baselines across every benchmark. Against the closest competitor FQL (Park et al., ICML 2025), GS-flow gains +8.6 on OGBench (47.1 vs 38.5), +2.6 on D4RL AntMaze (86.1 vs 83.5), and +20.6 in offline-to-online OGBench (88.6 vs 67.6); the gap is largest on multi-modal tasks like Cube Double Play (51.3 vs 36) and Puzzle-4x4 (17→100% vs 8→38% online). Among diffusion-based baselines, IDQL (79.2) and SRPO (73.8) are the strongest on D4RL but are far behind GS-flow (86.1) and also trail on OGBench (17.2, 9.1). Gaussian-policy baselines IQL (19.1/57.2) and ReBRAC (29.7/78.7) are consistently outperformed. In the offline-to-online setting, GS-flow (88.6) surpasses RLPD (41.6) and Cal-QL (10.0) on OGBench, while on D4RL AntMaze it achieves 96.8 vs. RLPD's 95.7 and Cal-QL's 97.3 (a near-tie with Cal-QL). On Adroit tasks, GS-flow (111.5) edges out FQL (110.0) and substantially outperforms RLPD (88.0) and IFQL (55.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-step distillation framework of FQL (Park et al., 2025) for flow-matching policies by addressing two overlooked design choices: the initial noise distribution and the deterministic nature of the distilled actor. The Q-guided prior draws on the 'golden noise' idea from image generation (Zhou et al., 2025) and the learned-prior approach of DSRL (Wagenmaker et al., 2025), but departs by using a flexible CVAE rather than a Gaussian and integrating the prior directly into the distillation pipeline. The entropy-regularized distillation adapts the maximum-entropy principle from SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018) into the student policy's output head, transforming the conventional point-to-point distillation into a point-to-distribution paradigm. The theoretical analysis frames the prior learning as amortized optimization, providing a Lipschitz-based performance bound that connects the CVAE reconstruction error to the achievable return gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that two seemingly minor design choices in flow-matching policy distillation—the starting noise distribution and the output parameterization—can be leveraged to substantially improve both exploitation quality and online exploration, with particularly large gains on multi-modal tasks (e.g., Puzzle-4x4: 17%→100% vs. FQL's 8%→38%). This bridges the gap between expressive generative policies and the practical exploration mechanisms of actor-critic methods, offering a template for integrating value-guided generation with stochastic policy control. Open questions include whether the CVAE-based prior scales to high-dimensional VLA models, whether the approach extends to discrete action spaces, and whether the training overhead of the Advantage Noise Selection module remains acceptable at larger scales. The method is currently validated only on continuous control benchmarks, leaving its applicability to language-conditioned or hierarchical settings unexplored.
