## Problem

Existing self-supervised learning methods for fMRI discard fine-grained temporal dynamics by discretizing signals into fixed-size patches or compressing them into static connectivity graphs, and they cannot naturally handle the heterogeneous repetition times (TRs) across multi-site datasets. This paper addresses the need for a unified representation-learning framework that models brain activity as continuous-time dynamics, is agnostic to temporal irregularity, and produces transferable features without costly numerical SDE solvers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263468/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw BOLD time-series from 450 ROIs (Schaefer-400 cortical parcellation plus Tian-Scale III subcortical regions) with their native, irregular time-stamps—no resampling or fixed-length padding is required, so scans of 76 to 240+ time points from different TRs are handled natively. The output is a universal feature A, obtained by aggregating the optimal control signals α* across time points, which serves as a compact, task-agnostic representation for downstream regression (age, Flanker) and classification (gender, autism, ADHD, schizophrenia) via linear probing or fine-tuning.

## Method

BDO models continuous latent brain dynamics as an Itô diffusion SDE in a low-dimensional space and reframes representation learning as a Stochastic Optimal Control problem, where a transformer-parameterized control policy α^θ acts as the latent encoder that steers the SDE to align with observed fMRI data. The training objective unifies masked autoencoding (reconstructing masked observations from context) and JEPA-style latent prediction (aligning with an EMA target encoder) into a single SOC cost, derived as an ELBO via Girsanov's theorem. For computational efficiency, the drift is approximated as piecewise locally linear, yielding closed-form Gaussian moments for the latent states that are computed in O(log k) time via a parallel scan algorithm—eliminating the need for numerical SDE solvers during both training and inference.

## Results

BDO (86M parameters) achieves state-of-the-art performance across all evaluated tasks. On HCP-A (full fine-tuning), it reaches age prediction ρ=0.851 (MSE 0.273) and gender ACC 79.40%, surpassing the best task-specific model BrainNetTF (ρ=0.780, ACC 75.00%) and the best fMRI SSL model BrainLM (ρ=0.818, ACC 72.78%). On clinical diagnosis (FT), BDO achieves 69.32% ACC on ABIDE, 64.16% on ADHD200, and 82.86% on HCP-EP, outperforming all baselines by 2–11 percentage points. Even under linear probing, BDO matches or exceeds task-specific models, demonstrating strong representation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BDO outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and metric in both linear probing and full fine-tuning settings. Against the strongest task-specific model BrainNetTF, BDO gains +2.96% ACC on ABIDE, +9.87% on ADHD200, and +11.43% on HCP-EP (FT). Among SSL baselines, BrainMass is the closest competitor, but BDO still leads by +2.05% on ABIDE, +0.25% on ADHD200, and +6.67% on HCP-EP. The general-purpose SSL methods MoCo and BYOL trail BDO by 4–12% in diagnosis accuracy, confirming that domain-agnostic contrastive/alignment objectives are insufficient for fMRI. On HCP-A trait prediction (FT), BDO improves age ρ from 0.818 (BrainLM) to 0.851 and gender ACC from 72.78% to 79.40%, while also achieving the best Flanker task ρ of 0.464. Notably, BDO's 86M parameter count is comparable to or smaller than all baselines (85–90M), and even its 5M variant approaches the performance of full-size SSL models, highlighting superior parameter efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

BDO departs from the dominant fMRI SSL paradigm (BrainLM, Brain-JEPA, BrainMass) that discretizes BOLD signals into fixed-size spatiotemporal patches or static connectivity graphs, instead modeling brain activity as a continuous-time Itô diffusion in a low-dimensional latent space. The core conceptual move is reframing the stochastic optimal control (SOC) policy as the latent encoder itself, which naturally unifies masked autoencoding and JEPA-style latent prediction into a single control-cost objective — a formulation inherited from the SOC-ELBO equivalence of Li et al. (2020) and the amortized inference framework of Park et al. (2024). For computational tractability, the paper adopts the locally linear SDE approximation from continuous-discrete state-space models (Schirmer et al., 2022; Ansari et al., 2023), yielding closed-form Gaussian marginals computable in O(log k) via parallel scan, thereby avoiding the numerical solvers that have limited prior continuous-time approaches to small-scale applications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework directly addresses a practical bottleneck in multi-site fMRI research: heterogeneous repetition times (0.735 s in UK Biobank vs. 1.5–3.0 s in ABIDE) that break discrete patch-based or graph-based SSL methods. The simulation-free inference and the fact that even a 5 M-parameter BDO matches or exceeds 85–90 M-parameter SSL baselines suggest the approach could become a practical default for large-scale fMRI representation learning. However, the locally linear SDE approximation introduces a variational gap and potential error accumulation over long recordings, the framework's complexity (SDE + transformer encoder + EMA target + parallel scan) raises adoption barriers, and the neurobiological interpretability of the learned continuous dynamics remains largely unexplored beyond the aggregated universal feature.
