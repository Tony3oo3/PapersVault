## Problem

LoRA and similar low-rank adaptation methods consistently underperform full fine-tuning in accuracy and converge more slowly, and prior work has attributed this gap primarily to inaccurate gradient approximation within the low-rank subspace. This paper identifies a second, overlooked source of the gap: misalignment of the optimizer's internal state (AdamW's first and second moments) with the evolving low-rank constraint, compounded by the need to manually tune the LoRA scaling factor α.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a frozen pre-trained backbone (LLaMA-7B/2-7B/3-8B/3.1-70B for text, ViT-B/16 for 224×224 RGB images) and injects trainable low-rank factor pairs (U, V) into selected linear layers (Q, K, V, Up, Down for transformers; Q, K, V, Dense for ViT). At inference time the low-rank update UVᵀ is merged into the frozen weights, so there is zero additional latency. The method is evaluated on commonsense reasoning (8 QA benchmarks), image classification (4 datasets including medical imaging), language modeling (WikiText-2), and math reasoning (Orca-Math).

## Method

LoFT introduces six building blocks that collectively make low-rank updates replicate full AdamW fine-tuning dynamics: (1) alternating updates of U and V to eliminate the quadratic cross-term that appears when both are updated simultaneously; (2) gradient scaling by (VᵀV)⁻¹ (or (UᵀU)⁻¹) to remove the scale ambiguity of the UVᵀ factorization; (3) first-moment calibration via a matrix C_k = (V_{k-1}ᵀV_k)(V_kᵀV_k)⁻¹ that re-projects the momentum into the current subspace; (4) second-moment calibration using Khatri-Rao cross-term accumulators to align the variance estimate across changing subspaces; (5) reconstruction of the full-model AdamW update followed by projection onto the low-rank subspace; and (6) gradient clipping computed on the projected full gradient. The key insight is that optimizer moments, not just gradients, must be projected into the low-rank space; this eliminates the α hyperparameter entirely and makes LoFT provably reduce to standard AdamW in the full-rank limit. The main memory cost is O((m+n)r²) for the second-moment cross-terms, though a simplified variant omitting this step incurs <6% overhead over LoRA with only ~0.1% accuracy loss.

## Results

On commonsense reasoning (8 benchmarks, LLaMA-7B/2-7B/3-8B), LoFT at r=16 achieves average accuracies of 76.08%, 80.46%, and 85.63% respectively, outperforming LoRA (73.57%, 68.69%, 84.97%) and DoRA (71.11%, 79.71%, 84.96%). On image classification (ViT-Base, 4 datasets), LoFT r=16 reaches 76.12% average accuracy, surpassing full fine-tuning (75.86%), LoRA (75.46%), and DoRA (74.74%). On WikiText-2 language modeling, LoFT r=4 achieves 31.75 PPL on GPT-2 (vs. LoRA 34.80, Full FT 29.51) and 19.26 PPL on GPT-2 Large (vs. LoRA 19.78, Full FT 19.42), slightly beating full fine-tuning at the larger scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoFT consistently outperforms LoRA across all model scales and tasks: by +2.51% average on LLaMA-7B, +11.77% on LLaMA2-7B, and +0.66% on LLaMA3-8B for commonsense reasoning, and by +0.66% on ViT-Base image classification. Against DoRA, LoFT leads by +4.97% (LLaMA-7B), +0.75% (LLaMA2-7B), +0.67% (LLaMA3-8B), and +1.38% (image classification). On WikiText-2 (GPT-2, r=4), LoFT achieves 31.75 PPL, beating LoRA (34.80), rsLoRA (32.96), LoRA-Pro (32.79), LoRA-GA (37.34), LoRA+ (36.15), and AdaLoRA (55.67), while remaining within 2.2 PPL of full fine-tuning (29.51). On GPT-2 Large, LoFT (19.26 PPL) slightly surpasses full fine-tuning (19.42), suggesting implicit regularization from the low-rank structure. The gap over LoRA narrows at LLaMA3-8B (already strong at 84.97%) but LoFT still wins on 7 of 8 individual tasks. At very low ranks (r=1,2,4), LoFT's advantage over both LoRA and DoRA becomes dramatically larger, with DoRA showing near-zero scores on Winogrande at r=4 while LoFT retains 76.40%.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoFT builds directly on LoRA's low-rank reparameterization but departs from the dominant line of prior work (DoRA, LoRA-Pro, LoRA-GA, AltLoRA) that focuses on improving gradient approximation within the low-rank subspace. The key conceptual departure is identifying that optimizer state misalignment—specifically in AdamW's first and second moments—constitutes a separate and critical source of the performance gap, not merely a gradient-direction issue. While concurrent work AltLoRA also adopts alternating updates, it only recovers SGD with momentum; LoFT extends alignment to the full AdamW dynamics (momentum, variance, clipping, weight decay) and is the first method to provably reduce to full fine-tuning in the full-rank limit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for the LoRA scaling hyperparameter α and enabling robust performance at extremely low ranks (r=1), LoFT offers a practical path toward parameter-efficient fine-tuning in resource-constrained and multi-user settings where tuning budgets are limited. The main limitations are increased memory (up to ~26% over LoRA) and training latency (up to ~3.2×), though the LoFT(simple) variant mitigates both at negligible accuracy cost. Open questions include whether the optimizer-state alignment principle transfers cleanly to other optimizers (a Muon variant is sketched but not fully validated at scale), how it interacts with quantization and differential privacy, and whether the alternating-update requirement can be relaxed without losing the provable full-rank recovery guarantee.
