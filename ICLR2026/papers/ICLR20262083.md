## Problem

Generating videos from complex multi-event prompts (e.g., sequential actions in one sentence) remains unsolved due to an inherent trade-off: decomposing prompts into multiple short clips improves action fidelity but causes temporal inconsistency, while feeding a single complex prompt preserves coherence but leads to event omission and temporal hallucination. The paper identifies the root cause as temporal misalignment between video tokens and event conditions, plus conflicting attention coupling between motion-related regions and multiple event text tokens in the cross-attention layer.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single complex text prompt containing 2–4 sequential events (optionally an initial image for I2V) and produces a 480×832, 81-frame video. Temporal segmentation of events into video-token intervals is handled externally via GPT-4o-mini (~2.65 s), user-specified ratios, or uniform splitting, with minimal performance difference across strategies. For I2V, the first frame is synthesized by Qwen-Image from a GPT-4o-reparsed initial-state description, and TS-Attn is applied over the first 40% of denoising steps (vs. 20% for T2V).

## Method

TS-Attn is a training-free plug-and-play module that replaces cross-attention in the early denoising stages of DiT-based video models. It first performs Motion Region Extraction (MRE): the cross-attention map for the subject token is thresholded at its mean and eroded (kernel 3) to produce a binary mask identifying motion-related video tokens. Then Event-aware Attention Modulation (EAM) operates on those masked tokens in two steps: (1) Attention Rearrangement applies a positive bias (max−mean of QK^T) to the temporally aligned event tokens and a negative bias (min−mean) to all other events for each temporal segment of video queries, and (2) Attention Reinforcement adaptively scales these biases by a factor in [1, 1.5] inversely proportional to the sharpness of the current attention distribution, so flatter (more ambiguous) distributions receive stronger correction. The final modulated attention is A = softmax((QK^T + M_s ⊙ R(Q,K) ⊙ B(Q,K))/√d), and for multiple subjects the per-subject bias terms are summed. The entire mechanism adds only ~2% inference overhead over the base model.

## Results

On StoryEval-Bench (GPT-4o verifier), TS-Attn achieves 56.2% average on Wan2.2-A14B (+7.9 pp over baseline, 16.4% relative gain) and 50.2% on Wan2.1-14B (+12.6 pp, 33.5% relative gain), with a 57.3% relative improvement on CogVideoX-5B (16.4%→25.8%). The method adds only 2% inference latency (863 s vs. 846 s on A100) compared to the base Wan2.2-A14B model. On the I2V task, Wan2.2-I2V-A14B + TS-Attn reaches 54.4% average, surpassing SkyReels-V2-I2V-14B (36.9%) and Framepack-13B (33.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TS-Attn on Wan2.2-A14B (56.2%) substantially outperforms the multi-prompt baselines DiTCtrl+Wan2.2-A14B (46.5%) and MEVG+Wan2.2-A14B (43.1%), while also surpassing the strongest single-prompt open-source models SkyReels-V2 (40.6%) and MAGI-1-24B (35.8%), as well as closed-source Kling-1.5 (40.1%) and Hailuo (35.1%). On the I2V task, Wan2.2-I2V-A14B + TS-Attn (54.4%) leads all baselines including SkyReels-V2-I2V-14B (36.9%), Framepack-13B (33.5%), and MAGI-1-I2V-24B (34.2%). Inference efficiency is a key differentiator: TS-Attn adds only 17 s (2%) over Wan2.2-A14B (846 s→863 s), whereas DiTCtrl requires 2749 s and MEVG 2453 s due to multi-stage generation. Against multi-prompt compositional methods, TS-Attn (56.2%) also beats VideoTetris (49.7%) and TALC (47.1%) on the same Wan2.2-A14B backbone. The consistent gains across architectures (Cross-DiT, MM-DiT) and scales (1.3B to 24B) confirm the method's plug-and-play generality.

## Relation to Prior Work

TS-Attn positions itself against two dominant paradigms for multi-event video generation: sequential multi-prompt methods (MEVG, DiTCtrl) that sacrifice temporal consistency for action fidelity, and single-pass methods using hard-masked or local-global cross-attention (VideoTetris, TALC) that struggle with fine-grained temporal transitions. It also departs from training-based approaches (MinT, ShotAdapter) that require timestamp-labeled data for post-training. Conceptually, the method draws on the adaptive thresholding idea from ConceptAttention (Helbling et al., 2025) for motion region extraction, but its core contribution is a training-free, single-pass attention bias mechanism that dynamically redistributes cross-attention along the temporal dimension—something no prior multi-event method achieves without either repeated inference or architectural retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TS-Attn offers a practical, plug-and-play solution to a recognized limitation in current video generation models: the inability to faithfully render multi-event prompts in a single pass. The 33.5% StoryEval-Bench improvement with only 2% inference overhead, validated across four architectures and two verifier models, makes it immediately deployable on existing production systems. However, absolute performance on the 'Hard' category remains modest (44.3%), and the method's reliance on accurate temporal segmentation (even if coarse) and a single motion subject per frame limits its applicability to highly complex, multi-subject, or very long-form scenarios. Open questions include whether the attention-rearrangement principle generalizes to other attention-based generation tasks (e.g., multi-concept image generation) and how it interacts with emerging autoregressive video architectures.
