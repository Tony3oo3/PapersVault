## Problem

In discounted online convex optimization, the discount factor λ governs how rapidly past losses are forgotten, but in real-world applications (e.g., economic modeling, control systems) λ is an intrinsic parameter rather than a tunable hyperparameter. While OGD with a known λ achieves O(1/√(1−λ)) discounted regret, no prior algorithm adapts to an unknown λ. The fundamental obstacle is that the standard meta-expert framework (Hedge, Fixed-Share) requires all experts to share a unified performance measure, which is impossible when experts are configured for different discount factors.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the algorithm receives a convex loss function f_t over a convex domain W ⊆ R^d with bounded gradients (‖∇f_t‖ ≤ G) and bounded diameter (D), and outputs a decision point w_t ∈ W. The key guarantee is a uniform O(√(log T/(1−λ))) discounted regret bound that holds simultaneously for all λ in the continuous interval [1−1/τ, 1−1/T], where τ is a minimal window length, without requiring knowledge of λ. The continuous interval is discretized via a geometric series {1−2^i/T : i = 0,…,N} with N = ⌈log₂(T/τ)⌉, and the final bound is extended back to the continuous range using a convex-combination lemma.

## Method

The algorithm (SOGD) constructs N+1 OGD experts, each with a step size η_i = (D/G)√(2^i/T) tuned to a discretized discount factor λ_i = 1−2^{i−1}/T, and sequentially aggregates them using instances of Discounted-Normal-Predictor with conservative updating (DNP-cu). The critical technical contribution is a novel analysis (Theorem 2) showing that DNP-cu with internal discount factor ρ provides a discounted payoff guarantee for any external factor η ≥ ρ, enabling the Combiner (Algorithm 3) to merge two experts operating under different discount factors. Specifically, when combining B_{k−1} and A_k via DNP-cu with ρ = λ_k, the cross-factor regret term is bounded using the η ≥ ρ guarantee (equation 13) for the larger factor λ_i, while the same-factor term uses the ρ-matched guarantee (equation 14). This sequential aggregation, ordered in descending λ, yields the uniform bound with only an O(√log T) adaptivity overhead over the known-λ case.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is Theorem 3: SOGD achieves a uniform O(√(log T/(1−λ))) λ-discounted regret bound holding simultaneously for all λ ∈ [1−1/τ, 1−1/T], without requiring knowledge of λ. Empirically, on the ijcnn1 dataset (T=10,000 rounds, d=22, absolute loss), SOGD matches the discounted cumulative loss of OGD with a specifically tuned step size across discount factors λ ∈ {0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8, 0.85, 0.9, 0.996, 0.998, 0.999}. For λ=0.999 and 0.998 the two algorithms show nearly identical loss curves, while for λ=0.996 OGD has a slightly lower initial loss but SOGD converges to a comparable steady-state value. The method is robust to the hyperparameter Z, with all tested values (Z=1/T, 1/(2T), 1/(4T), 1/(8T)) yielding similar performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SOGD against four baselines on ijcnn1: (1) OGD with a tuned step size η = D√(2(1−λ))/G from Theorem 1, (2) SAOL (Daniely et al., 2015), (3) SCB (Jun et al., 2017), and (4) OGD with an untuned step size η₁ = D/(2G). SOGD matches OGD-with-tuned-η in discounted cumulative loss across all tested discount factors (0.5–0.999), with the key advantage that it does not require prior knowledge of λ. Against SAOL and SCB, SOGD clearly outperforms both baselines for every discount factor tested, as shown in Figures 3 and 6. The untuned OGD baseline performs worst overall, confirming that step-size selection is critical. The practical takeaway is that SOGD achieves near-optimal discounted regret (matching the oracle OGD that knows λ) while remaining fully adaptive to the unknown discount factor, at the cost of an additional √(log T) factor in the theoretical bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SOGD framework of Zhang et al. (2022a), which used DNP-cu to sequentially aggregate OGD experts with different step sizes for adaptive regret, to the discounted OCO setting. The key departure from prior work is that the standard meta-expert framework (Hedge, Fixed-Share) fundamentally fails here because experts configured for different discount factors operate under different performance measures, making unified regret decomposition impossible. The paper's central technical contribution is a novel discounted payoff analysis of DNP-cu (Theorem 2) showing it can aggregate two experts even when they use different discount factors, a property not available to any previously studied meta-algorithm. The result also directly answers an open question left by Zhang et al. (2024), who established O(1/√(1−λ²)) bounds for known λ but could not handle the unknown-λ case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm to achieve uniform discounted regret O(√(log T/(1−λ))) simultaneously for all λ in a continuous interval without knowing λ in advance, which is critical in applications like economic modeling and control systems where the discount factor reflects intrinsic system dynamics rather than a tunable hyperparameter. The √(log T) overhead is shown to be a necessary cost of adaptivity, mirroring the well-known log T penalty in strongly adaptive regret. Open questions include extending the framework to time-varying discount factors and exploiting strong convexity or exp-concavity for tighter bounds, both of which remain unresolved.
