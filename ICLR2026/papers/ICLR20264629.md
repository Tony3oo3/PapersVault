## Problem

Binary verifiable rewards in RLVR are brittle: they produce all-0 or all-1 rollout groups that yield zero relative advantage under GRPO, and they under-credit format-sensitive or partially correct answers (false negatives). Reward models offer dense, continuous feedback but are noisy and prone to reward hacking on hard-to-verify tasks. The open question is how to structurally combine these two signals so that verifier reliability is preserved while dense RM nuance is exploited for learning.

## Input / Output

For each math problem prompt, the method generates N rollouts (8 for Qwen3-4B, 16 for OctoThinker-8B) and scores each with both a rule-based verifier (math_verify, producing 0/1) and a reward model (AceMath-RM-7B, producing a continuous scalar). The shaped hybrid reward per rollout is a bounded continuous value: incorrect-group scores are rescaled to [-α, α] and correct-group scores to [(1−β), (1+β)], then multiplied by a per-prompt difficulty weight in [w_min, w_max]. This reward feeds into standard GRPO advantage computation; the final output is a trained reasoning policy evaluated via pass@1 (verifiable tasks) or GPT-4o-as-judge (hard-to-verify tasks).

## Method

HERO introduces two mechanisms on top of GRPO. First, stratified normalization partitions a rollout group by the rule-based verifier's binary output, then applies min-max normalization to RM scores within each partition, bounding them to narrow ranges (e.g., ±0.05 for correct/incorrect groups on easy-to-verify data, ±0.1 for mixed data). This ensures dense RM signals refine quality distinctions only within verifier-defined correctness tiers, preventing RM drift from overriding correctness semantics. Second, variance-aware advantage reweighting computes the standard deviation of RM scores across rollouts per prompt and maps it through a bounded logistic function (w_min=0.4, w_max=3.0, k=6) to up-weight high-variance (challenging) prompts and down-weight trivial ones. The final per-rollout reward is the product of the stratified-normalized score and the difficulty weight, which is then used in GRPO's group-relative advantage. Ablations confirm that dense negative-range rewards matter more than positive-range ones, and that naive linear blending of rule and RM signals (without stratification) destabilizes training.

## Results

On Qwen3-4B-Base trained with easy-to-verify samples, HERO achieves 62.0 average on easy-to-verify tasks (MATH500, AMC, Minerva, Olympiad) and 66.3 on hard-to-verify tasks (HardVerify-Math, TextBookReasoning), outperforming the reward-model-only baseline (56.4 / 54.6) and the rule-based verifier baseline (58.3 / 57.1). The largest gain appears on hard-to-verify evaluations, where HERO surpasses RM-only by +11.7 points and verifier-only by +9.2 points. On the weaker OctoThinker-8B-Hybrid-Base backbone, HERO similarly leads across all three training regimes, reaching 41.0 / 34.6 (easy/hard) when trained on hard-to-verify samples versus 35.4 / 30.9 (RM-only) and 34.6 / 27.8 (verifier-only).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HERO consistently outperforms all four baselines across every training regime and evaluation setting. Against the AceMath-7B-RM reward-model-only baseline, HERO gains +5.6 to +11.7 points depending on the regime, with the largest margin on hard-to-verify tasks (e.g., 66.3 vs. 54.6 with easy-to-verify training). Against the math_verify rule-based verifier baseline, HERO improves by +3.7 to +9.4 points, with the most dramatic gain when training on hard-to-verify samples where the verifier is brittle (56.8 vs. 47.4 on easy-to-verify tasks). Against the General Reasoner model-based verifier (1.5B), HERO leads by +3.4 to +5.5 points across regimes. Against Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct used as an LLM-as-verifier, HERO still wins on all settings (e.g., 62.0 vs. 58.1 on easy-to-verify tasks with easy-to-verify training; 66.3 vs. 62.5 on hard-to-verify tasks), confirming that structured hybrid reward integration outperforms simply scaling up the verifier model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the RLVR/GRPO paradigm (Shao et al., 2024; DeepSeek-R1) that trains reasoning policies using binary verifier rewards, and on reward-model-based RL (AceMath-RM, Liu et al., 2024) that uses continuous preference scores. It departs from prior work by identifying that neither signal alone is sufficient—binary verifiers are brittle on hard-to-verify samples (low recall, zero gradient when all rollouts share the same label) while RMs are noisy and prone to reward hacking—and by showing that naive linear blending of the two destabilizes training. The key methodological departure is stratified normalization, which rescales RM scores within verifier-defined correctness groups rather than mixing them globally, combined with variance-aware prompt reweighting to focus capacity on informative samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in RLVR: as reasoning tasks grow harder and more open-ended, binary verifiers increasingly fail to provide useful signal, stalling policy improvement on exactly the prompts that matter most. HERO's stratified normalization offers a clean, interpretable mechanism for combining sparse and dense supervision that practitioners can adopt with minimal architectural changes to existing GRPO pipelines. Limitations include dependence on reasonably accurate rule-based verifiers (currently well-supported only in math), sensitivity to range hyperparameters (α, β), and evaluation on hard-to-verify tasks that still relies on LLM-as-judge protocols. Open questions include extending the framework to domains lacking high-precision verifiers, incorporating process-level (step-by-step) supervision, and developing adaptive online calibration of the reward ranges.
