## Problem

Standard decoding strategies (top-k, nucleus sampling) offer no direct control over semantic properties like instruction adherence or factuality, while existing interventionist methods (activation steering, contrastive decoding) that do provide such control frequently degrade general text quality by pushing the model into out-of-distribution states. The paper asks whether generation can be steered toward a desired behavior without directly manipulating the model's internal representations or output logits.

## Input / Output

At each decoding step, AGD consumes the model's pre-softmax probability distribution over the vocabulary, a user-defined Region of Interest (ROI) specified as a subset of input token embeddings or a set of attention heads, and a candidate set formed by top-k tokens above a minimum probability threshold (k=5, π_min=0.05 in experiments). It produces the next generated token selected by maximum attribution to the ROI, along with per-token relevance scores that serve as an interpretability trace explaining why that token was chosen over alternatives. The method is entirely inference-time and fine-tuning-free, requiring no model retraining or architecture modification.

## Method

AGD reframes decoding as a search for the token most justified by a given rationale: at each step, it restricts candidates to the top-k high-probability tokens, computes a Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (AttnLRP) score from each candidate's logit back to the ROI, and selects the token with the highest aggregated attribution. The ROI is flexibly defined per task — instruction embeddings for constraint following, pre-identified parametric knowledge heads for closed-book factuality, or in-context retrieval heads/context embeddings for open-book grounding. Unlike interventionist baselines that modify logits or residual streams, AGD is selectionist: it leaves the forward pass untouched and only changes which candidate is emitted. An entropy-gated variant (AGD_LRPe) applies guidance only when the output distribution's Shannon entropy exceeds a threshold (80th percentile, τ=1.734), defaulting to greedy decoding otherwise, which reduces backward passes from ~1.06 to ~0.58 per token and preserves output quality while retaining most adherence gains.

## Results

On instruction following, AGD with LRP attribution (AGD_LRP) boosts Prompt-Level Accuracy on IHEval from 66.0% (greedy) to 79.1% for Llama 3.1 (8B), a 13.1-point gain, while the entropy-gated variant (AGD_LRPe) preserves Quality Score (76.4 vs. 81.3 greedy) and improves Session Stability Rate on SysBench by 7.9 points. On closed-book factuality, AGD targeting parametric knowledge heads improves recall on HotPotQA from 34.6% (greedy) to 39.6% for Llama 3.1. In the open-book setting, AGD grounded in context embeddings achieves 91.4% recall on TriviaQA and 87.9% on HotPotQA (with 80% distractor noise), outperforming all baselines by 4–6.6 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGD consistently outperforms nucleus sampling across all models and both instruction-following benchmarks, with the largest gap on IHEval PLA (e.g., 79.1% vs. 63.6% for Llama 3.1). Against Context-aware Decoding (CAD), AGD_LRP achieves higher PLA on IHEval (79.1% vs. 73.9% for Llama 3.1) and higher CSR on SysBench (74.3% vs. 72.2%), while CAD occasionally matches AGD on ISR. In the closed-book factuality setting, AGD targeting parametric heads surpasses DoLA on HotPotQA recall (39.6% vs. 34.3% for Llama 3.1) and TriviaQA (82.4% vs. 81.2%), though gains on Natural Questions are marginal (63.0% vs. 63.8%). In the open-book setting, AGD grounded in context embeddings outperforms CAD by 3.5–4.2 points on TriviaQA and 4.2–6.6 points on HotPotQA (with 80% distractor noise), demonstrating that the attribution mechanism provides value beyond contrastive logit modification, particularly under noisy retrieval conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

AGD departs from the dominant 'interventionist' paradigm for controlled LLM generation—activation steering (Rimsky et al., 2024; Stolfo et al., 2025) and contrastive logit modification (CAD, DoLA)—by introducing a 'selectionist' alternative that never alters the model's forward pass. It repurposes Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (Bach et al., 2015; Achtibat et al., 2024), previously used only for post-hoc explanation, as an active token-selection criterion within the decoding loop. The identification of parametric knowledge heads and in-context retrieval heads follows the methodology of Kahardipraja et al. (2025), extending their interpretability analysis into a generative control signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AGD offers a fine-tuning-free, interpretable mechanism for improving instruction adherence and factual grounding that avoids the quality degradation typical of activation-based steering, with the entropy-gated variant further reducing computational overhead to ~1.3–1.6× greedy decoding. Its primary limitation is inherent to selection: it cannot produce a token the model never proposes, and it requires task-specific ROI definitions. Open questions include whether more efficient attribution proxies can reduce the backward-pass cost, and whether the ROI concept can be extended to monosemantic circuits for finer-grained control.
