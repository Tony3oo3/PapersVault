## Problem

Despite the growing use of MLLMs for emotion understanding, no dedicated benchmark exists to evaluate emotion-specific hallucinations, leaving a critical gap between general-purpose hallucination evaluation and the unique challenges of affective reasoning. MLLMs lack the embodied and experiential grounding humans use to interpret emotions, relying solely on data-driven learning from external behavioral cues, which makes them particularly susceptible to generating incorrect or ungrounded emotional interpretations.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance is an adversarial pair consisting of a basic question (testing core perception/reasoning) and a hallucinated question (containing intentionally fabricated emotional content), both requiring a single-word YES/NO response. A response is scored correct only if the model answers both questions in the pair accurately, making the evaluation stricter than single-question accuracy. Inputs span four modalities with specific constraints: text (emotion psychology statements averaging 19.9 words, SOUL reviews averaging 108.7 words), images (579.5×466.5 px average from Twitter15/17), audio clips (4.2 s average from RAVDESS), and videos split into short (4.3 s, 870×477 px from MER 2023) and long (60 s, 638×360 px from Social-IQ 2.0). Beyond accuracy, the benchmark reports Yes/No bias via Pct. Diff (target ~0) and False Positive Ratio (target ~0.5) to detect overconfident affirmation tendencies.

## Method

EmotionHallucer organizes 2,742 questions into two dimensions—emotion psychology knowledge (theory, definition, finding subcategories sourced from Shiota & Kalat's textbook) and real-world multimodal perception (category, intensity, reasoning-result, reasoning-cue subcategories from six emotion datasets)—each instantiated as adversarial basic/hallucinated QA pairs. The evaluation framework is training-free and prompt-based, following the POPE/VideoHallucer paradigm of binary VQA to avoid caption-length sensitivity and LLM self-evaluation bias. As a mitigation strategy, the paper proposes PEP-MEK (Predict-Explain-Predict with Modality and Emotion Knowledge), a plug-and-play framework that first prompts the model to extract structured modality-specific and emotion-specific knowledge (scene mood, facial expressions, vocal prosody, social interactions, emotion definitions), then makes an initial binary prediction, generates a self-verification explanation referencing the extracted knowledge, and finally refines the prediction. This three-stage reasoning loop requires no fine-tuning and yields an average 9.90% accuracy improvement across Qwen2.5-Omni, Emotion-LLaMA, and Gemini-2.5-Flash, with the largest gains (16.38%) on the emotion-specific Emotion-LLaMA model.

## Results

On EmotionHallucer-NoAudio (the main 11-model comparison), Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves the best overall accuracy of 51.58%, followed by Gemini-2.5-Flash (50.56%) and GPT-5 (49.35%). Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL (72B) leads at 43.02% overall, while all other open-source models fall at or below the 25% random-guessing baseline. GPT-5 achieves the highest hallucinated-question accuracy (78.17%), indicating superior hallucination detection, while Emotion-LLaMA (18.86%) and LLaVA (10.27%) perform worst, with Emotion-LLaMA showing a strong Yes-bias (FP Ratio 0.63).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Closed-source models consistently outperform open-source counterparts: Gemini-2.5-Pro (51.58%) beats the best open-source Qwen2.5-VL (43.02%) by 8.56 points overall. GPT-5 (49.35%) and QvQ-Max (47.98%) also surpass all open-source models, with GPT-5 showing the strongest hallucinated-question detection (78.17% vs. 62.15% for Qwen2.5-VL). Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL (72B) is the only one to clearly exceed the 25% random-guessing threshold; Llama3.2-vision (29.91%) and Qwen2.5-Omni (25.44%) barely surpass it, while Emotion-LLaMA (18.86%), Video-ChatGPT (18.44%), and LLaVA (10.27%) all fall below chance. Notably, the emotion-specific Emotion-LLaMA underperforms general-purpose models, exhibiting a strong Yes-bias (FP Ratio 0.63, Pct. Diff 0.12), suggesting its fine-tuning on emotional content introduces confirmation bias. GPT-4o (40.98%) trails GPT-5 by 8.37 points, with GPT-5's reasoning capability providing a clear advantage in hallucination detection. The PEP-MEK framework, when applied to Qwen2.5-Omni, Emotion-LLaMA, and Gemini-2.5-Flash, yields an average 9.90% overall accuracy improvement, with Emotion-LLaMA benefiting most (+16.38 points).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the adversarial binary QA hallucination detection framework established by POPE (Li et al., 2023b) and VideoHallucer (Wang et al., 2024) to the previously unexplored domain of emotion understanding. While prior hallucination benchmarks target general-purpose visual tasks (objects, attributes, counting, events), EmotionHallucer introduces a fundamentally different challenge: hallucinations here involve both factual knowledge of emotion psychology (factuality) and perceptual-reasoning over ambiguous multimodal cues (faithfulness), a duality absent from object-level hallucination. The paper also positions itself against the growing body of emotion MLLM work (AffectGPT, EMER, Emotion-LLaMA, Omni-Emotion), identifying that none of these efforts have addressed hallucination as a distinct failure mode.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EmotionHallucer fills a clear gap by providing the first systematic evaluation of emotion hallucination across 41 models and four modalities, revealing that even top closed-source models achieve only ~50% overall accuracy and that all open-source models fail to exceed random guessing on the full multimodal benchmark. The finding that emotion-specific models (Emotion-LLaMA) underperform general-purpose models on hallucination detection is a cautionary signal for the emotion-AI community. Open questions include whether the binary QA format adequately captures the nuance of emotional misperception, how findings generalize to open-vocabulary or continuous-valence emotion representations, and whether the PEP-MEK framework's gains transfer to downstream emotion-aware applications beyond detection.
