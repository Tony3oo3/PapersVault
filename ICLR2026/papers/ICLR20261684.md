## Problem

Active learning for regression lacks a principled mechanism to target informative interior regions of the input space. Distance-based sampling, the dominant strategy, overemphasizes peripheral points while neglecting dense interior regions where multiple labeled samples' influences compete, and existing density-aware corrections offer limited control over interior exploration.

## Input / Output

At each acquisition step, TESSAR consumes a pool of unlabeled tabular samples and the current labeled set (serving as Voronoi sites), along with feature representations extracted from a 2-layer MLP (512 hidden units, ReLU, neural tangent parameterization). It outputs a batch of q samples to query, each scored by the product of a VLDM-derived weight, a minimum feature-space distance to labeled sites, and a Voronoi-cell-density representativity score. The method operates entirely in learned feature space rather than raw input space, and the Voronoi structure is updated dynamically within each batch as new sites are added.

## Method

The core mechanism is the Voronoi-based Least Disagree Metric (VLDM), which estimates a sample's proximity to Voronoi faces without explicitly constructing the diagram: it perturbs each labeled site with Gaussian noise at multiple variance levels and measures how often the sample's nearest-site assignment flips, taking the infimum disagreement probability over all perturbed configurations that do flip the assignment. A theoretical result under L-Lipschitz assumptions shows that points near Voronoi faces exhibit high prediction variance, justifying their informativeness. VLDM is combined multiplicatively with a distance score (DIST, minimum feature distance to any site, promoting peripheral exploration) and a cell-density score (BIN, sum of squared intra-cell distances, promoting representativity). A dynamic update subroutine (UPDATEVLDM) incrementally revises distances and VLDM values after each within-batch selection, reducing computation by a factor of q compared to full recomputation.

## Results

TESSAR is evaluated on 14 tabular regression datasets using RMSE difference relative to Random (more negative = better). In the performance profile, TESSAR achieves R_TESSAR(0) = 41%, meaning it is the best-performing algorithm in 41% of all dataset-step-repetition cases, significantly exceeding the next-best baseline LCMD (29%). TESSAR attains the best or second-best RMSE on 12 out of 14 datasets, with the largest margins on KEGG undir (−0.1615), MLR kNN (−0.1201), and Online video (−0.1183). In the penalty matrix, TESSAR outperforms every other algorithm in its row, while no other algorithm exceeds TESSAR by more than a 0.3 penalty in its column.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TESSAR is compared against eight baselines: Coreset, ProbCov, BALD, BatchBALD, BADGE, BAIT, ACS-FW, and LCMD. TESSAR achieves the best RMSE on 11 of 14 datasets, tying with LCMD on CT slices (both −0.0679) and trailing LCMD slightly on Query (−0.0047 vs −0.0054) and BADGE on Road (0.0001 vs −0.0004). The closest competitor overall is LCMD, which is second-best on 9 datasets but consistently trails TESSAR by a small margin (e.g., −0.1598 vs −0.1615 on KEGG undir, −0.1182 vs −0.1201 on MLR kNN). Bayesian methods (BALD, BatchBALD) and gradient-based methods (BADGE, BAIT) are substantially outperformed on most datasets, with BALD in particular showing positive RMSE differences (worse than Random) on 8 of 14 datasets. ProbCov and ACS-FW are the weakest baselines, frequently performing at or below Random level. The performance profile confirms TESSAR's dominance: it leads at every δ threshold, with a 12-percentage-point gap over LCMD at δ=0 (41% vs 29%).

## Relation to Prior Work

TESSAR adapts the Least Disagree Metric (LDM) from Cho et al. (2024), originally designed for deep active classification, to the regression setting by reinterpreting Voronoi cell boundaries as the regression analog of decision boundaries. It departs from the dominant distance-based sampling paradigm (Wu et al., 2019; Ash et al., 2020) that overemphasizes peripheral regions, and from clustering-based methods like LCMD (Holzmüller et al., 2023) that incorporate density corrections but lack explicit interior-region targeting. The key conceptual move is treating Voronoi face proximity as a geometric uncertainty signal—samples whose cell assignment is unstable under site perturbations—thereby importing disagreement-based intuition from classification into a label-free, purely geometric framework for regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete gap in active regression: distance-based methods systematically undersample dense interior regions where multiple labeled points' influences compete, and the Voronoi-face mechanism provides a principled, theoretically grounded remedy (via Lipschitz + Popoviciu's inequality linking face proximity to prediction variance). However, the computational cost scales with the perturbation budget N and pool size, limiting scalability to very large datasets, and all experiments are restricted to tabular data with a 2-layer MLP under homoskedastic noise. Open questions include whether the Voronoi-face principle extends to heteroskedastic settings, image or other high-dimensional modalities, and whether the dynamic update strategy can be made sublinear in N.
