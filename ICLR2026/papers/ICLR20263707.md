## Problem

Multimodal models lack reliable visual-outcome verification: they cannot accurately judge whether a generated image matches its prompt, obeys physical laws, or follows correct reasoning steps. This gap prevents self-critique and iterative refinement during interleaved reasoning and generation, which the authors argue is essential for next-generation multimodal systems. The paper systematically quantifies this deficiency and proposes a trainable generative verifier to close it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263707/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The verifier accepts a generated image (or a sequence of 8 images for dynamic physics) alongside a text prompt, code snippet, bounding-box/point annotation, or game-state screenshot, and returns a structured JSON containing a binary true/false judgment and a free-text explanation pinpointing the specific misalignment. In the OmniVerifier-TTS pipeline, when the judgment is false the verifier additionally emits an edit prompt—a rephrased, instruction-style description of what to fix—which is fed back to the unified multimodal model for targeted regional editing rather than full regeneration.

## Method

The paper introduces two automated data-construction pipelines (Image-Fixed/Prompt-Modified and Prompt-Fixed/Image-Inpainting) that leverage GPT-5 for prompt generation and modification, SAM 2.1 for object segmentation, and FLUX.1-dev for inpainting to create paired true/false verification examples at scale. OmniVerifier-7B is trained from Qwen2.5-VL-7B via DAPO reinforcement learning with a 9:1 rule-based accuracy reward and format reward, requiring no cold-start SFT. Ablation training on four task families reveals three atomic capabilities—Explicit Alignment, Relational Verification, and Integrative Reasoning—where the first two generalize strongly across tasks under RL while the third requires domain-specific data. For generation, OmniVerifier-TTS implements a sequential test-time scaling loop: the UMM generates an image, the verifier judges alignment and produces an edit prompt, and the UMM performs fine-grained editing, iterating up to 10 rounds until the verifier returns true, achieving higher quality than parallel Best-of-N with roughly 47% fewer generation steps.

## Results

On ViVerBench, OmniVerifier-7B achieves 0.653 overall accuracy, an 8.3-point improvement over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline (0.570) and surpassing GPT-4o (0.645), approaching the 72B-parameter Qwen2.5-VL-72B (0.661). For test-time scaling, OmniVerifier-TTS boosts Qwen-Image by +3.7 on T2I-ReasonBench (55.5→59.2) and +4.3 on GenEval++ (0.675→0.718), while improving GPT-Image-1 by +2.5 and +3.2 respectively. Sequential TTS consistently outperforms parallel Best-of-N (e.g., 59.2 vs. 58.1 on T2I-ReasonBench with Qwen-Image) while requiring only ~47% of the inference time. On VL-RewardBench, OmniVerifier-7B achieves 62.80 overall accuracy versus 46.72 for the untrained Qwen2.5-VL-7B (+16.08).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniVerifier-7B (0.653) surpasses GPT-4o (0.645) on ViVerBench overall and approaches the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B (0.661) using only 7B parameters, representing an 8.3-point gain over its own base model. On T2I-ReasonBench, OmniVerifier-TTS with Qwen-Image reaches 59.2, outperforming the unrefined Qwen-Image (55.5), the parallel Best-of-N variant (58.1), and the QwenVL-TTS baseline (57.4); with GPT-Image-1 it reaches 79.3 vs. 76.8 baseline and 78.1 parallel. On GenEval++, OmniVerifier-TTS (Qwen-Image) achieves 0.718, beating the baseline (0.675), parallel TTS (0.693), and QwenVL-TTS (0.682); with GPT-Image-1 it reaches 0.721 vs. 0.689 baseline. On VL-RewardBench, OmniVerifier-7B achieves 62.80 overall accuracy, a +16.08 improvement over Qwen2.5-VL-7B (46.72), with the largest gain in hallucination detection (+24.3). The paper also shows that using Gemini 2.5 Pro as the verifier further raises the ceiling (e.g., 81.6 on T2I-ReasonBench with GPT-Image-1), confirming that verifier quality directly scales generation performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of self-critique in LLMs (Self-Refine, Kumar et al.), unified multimodal generation (Bagel, Janus-Pro, Qwen-Image), and the emerging 'thinking with images' paradigm (OpenAI o3, DeepEyes, MINT-CoT), but departs from all of them by introducing visual-outcome verification as a first-class capability rather than treating vision as static context or focusing solely on text-outcome scoring. The RL training recipe draws on DAPO and rule-based reward frameworks from the LLM reasoning literature, while the data construction pipelines repurpose SAM 2.1 segmentation and FLUX inpainting in a novel verification-data context. The sequential test-time scaling paradigm is positioned as a direct alternative to parallel Best-of-N selection, repurposing the verifier's generative explanations as edit prompts rather than mere scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even the strongest SOTA VLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro at 0.745) trails human performance (0.932) by a wide margin on visual verification underscores that reliable self-critique of generated images remains an open bottleneck for next-generation multimodal systems. The identification of three atomic capabilities with distinct generalization patterns (strong cross-task transfer for alignment/relational tasks vs. minimal transfer for integrative reasoning) provides a practical, minimalist training recipe that could guide future verifier development. Key limitations include error accumulation in sequential TTS due to backbone editing constraints, the still-weak integrative reasoning capability, and the dependence on a single 7B backbone; scaling the verifier and addressing distribution drift during multi-round editing are critical open questions.
