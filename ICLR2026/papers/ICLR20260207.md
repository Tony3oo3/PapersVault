## Problem

Traditional Message Passing Neural Networks scale linearly with the number of edges, making them infeasible for large directed graphs with 10^8–10^9 nodes. The prior ICG method addressed this for undirected graphs but its approximation quality degraded for sparse graphs (requiring communities to scale with N/√E) and it could not handle edge directionality. This paper closes both gaps by enabling efficient, direction-aware approximation of arbitrary sparse or dense directed graphs with a community count independent of graph size.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a directed adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{N×N} and a normalized node feature matrix X ∈ [-1,1]^{N×D}, and produces a low-rank IBG parameterized by target/source community affiliation matrices U,V ∈ [0,1]^{N×K}, a magnitude vector r ∈ R^K, and community feature matrices F,B ∈ R^{K×D}. The weight matrix Q_A is adaptively constructed from A's sparsity (via parameter Γ) to balance edge and non-edge contributions. Downstream, the IBG-NN outputs node-level predictions (classification labels, temporal signal forecasts, or knowledge graph completions) in O(N) time and space rather than O(E).

## Method

The Intersecting Block Graph (IBG) approximates a directed graph as a sum of K overlapping bipartite components, each defined by a source community U_i and target community V_i with a scalar weight r_i, yielding C = U diag(r) V^⊤. The key theoretical contribution is the Densifying Weak Regularity Lemma, which proves that minimizing a weighted Frobenius norm (with weights that down-weight non-edges relative to edges) guarantees a small densifying cut similarity error of O(√(1/K)) using only O(1/ε²) communities—entirely independent of N or sparsity. In practice, the IBG is fitted via gradient descent on a restructured loss (Proposition 4.2) with O(K²N + KE) time and O(KN + E) space, exploiting A's sparsity and the IBG's low-rank structure separately. The IBG-NN architecture then processes signals through target/source synthesis and analysis operations (U F, V B, U†X, V†X) plus community-level MLPs, achieving O(NK) per-layer cost and O(N) overall complexity for downstream tasks.

## Results

On directed node classification, IBG-NN achieves 77.63% on Squirrel, 80.15% on Chameleon, and 83.76% ROC AUC on Tolokers, setting state-of-the-art among all compared methods. On large-scale benchmarks, IBG-NN with subgraph SGD reaches 92.3% on Reddit (0.1% condensation), 50.5% on Flickr (0.5%), 64.4% on Arxiv (0.05%), and 61.9% on Products (0.02%), surpassing all coarsening and condensation baselines. Runtime experiments show IBG-NN is 5.26×–5.68× faster than DirGNN on sparse graphs and exhibits a square-root scaling advantage on dense graphs, consistent with its O(N) vs. O(E) complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IBG-NN outperforms all undirected GNN baselines (GCN, GAT, H2GCN, GPR-GNN, FSGNN, GloGNN) by large margins on Squirrel (77.63% vs. best 74.80% for GPR-GNN) and Chameleon (80.15% vs. 78.30% for GPR-GNN). Against directed-graph-specific methods, IBG-NN beats DirGNN by 2.5% on Squirrel and 0.4% on Chameleon, and edges out FaberNet by 0.9% on Squirrel while trailing by 0.18% on Chameleon (80.15 vs. 80.33). The ablation isolating densification shows that IBG-NN without directionality already surpasses its predecessor ICG-NN by 6% on Squirrel and 11.2% on Chameleon, confirming that the densifying cut similarity is the primary driver of improvement. On large-scale benchmarks (Table 2), IBG-NN with subgraph SGD beats all coarsening methods (Random, Herding, K-Center) and condensation methods (GCOND, SFGC, GC-SNTK, SimGC) at every condensation ratio tested, e.g., 92.3% vs. 91.1% (SimGC) on Reddit at 0.1% and 50.5% vs. 47.1% (GCOND) on Flickr at 0.5%. On Tolokers, where the graph is already dense, IBG-NN matches ICG-NN and GCN/GAT within noise (83.76 vs. 83.73/83.70), suggesting densification benefits diminish when sparsity is low.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Intersecting Community Graph (ICG) framework of Finkelshtein et al. (2024a) in three key ways: (1) generalizing from undirected to directed graphs via separate source/target community affiliations (IBG), (2) introducing a densifying cut similarity that reweights non-edges relative to edges, and (3) proving a semi-constructive weak regularity lemma where the required number of communities is O(1/ε²) independent of graph size and sparsity, whereas the ICG bound was O(N/(√E·ε²)) and degraded for sparse graphs. The theoretical foundation draws on the classical Weak Regularity Lemma (Frieze & Kannan, 1999) and its graphon extension (Lovász, 2007), but the constructive, gradient-descent-optimizable formulation with a deterministic certificate for the high-probability event is new. The densification mechanism is not a mere renormalization but is argued to actively strengthen connectivity patterns, improving downstream accuracy alongside efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sparsity-independent approximation guarantee is a genuine theoretical advance that removes the primary limitation of prior low-rank graph approximation methods, making them applicable to the sparse, directed graphs that dominate real-world applications (social networks, knowledge graphs, traffic). The O(N) inference complexity (vs. O(E) for MPNNs) becomes increasingly valuable as graph sizes grow, and the one-time O(E) IBG fitting cost is amortized across hyperparameter searches and time steps. Open questions include: how many communities K are needed in practice for very large sparse graphs (the theory guarantees O(1) but constants matter), whether the non-convex optimization reliably finds good IBG approximations at scale, and whether the densification benefit persists on extremely sparse graphs (E ≪ N) where the signal-to-noise ratio in the weighted loss may be low.
