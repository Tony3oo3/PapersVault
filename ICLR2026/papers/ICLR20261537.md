## Problem

Existing underwater 3D reconstruction methods either assume rigid static scenes or rely on binary motion masks, failing to jointly model complex non-rigid dynamics (object motion, surface deformation, time-varying medium properties) under wavelength-dependent attenuation and scattering. Additionally, real-world underwater observations exhibit input-dependent noise that varies across viewpoints and frames, yet most methods apply uniform deterministic losses, producing ghosting artifacts and temporal inconsistency in ambiguous regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261537/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes monocular RGB video sequences with calibrated camera poses and normalized timestamps, initialized via COLMAP SfM to obtain sparse 3D point clouds for Gaussian placement. It produces a time-parameterized 3D Gaussian scene with a neural medium field, enabling novel view synthesis at 23.81 FPS, depth map extraction via alpha-blending, medium-free renderings by disabling the neural medium module, and per-pixel uncertainty maps highlighting surface-view ambiguity and motion-induced inconsistency. Datasets span in-the-wild internet videos (NUSR, DRUVA at 1920×1080) and controlled captures (SeaThru at 900×1400).

## Method

UDF initializes a canonical representation of 3D Gaussian ellipsoids embedded in a spectral neural medium field (conditioned on ray direction via spherical harmonics), then encodes both into a shared 4D neural voxel space using K-planes planar factorization across six 2D planes. A deformation network predicts per-Gaussian temporal offsets in translation, scaling, and rotation, while a medium offset network updates attenuation and backscatter coefficients conditioned on 2D scene flow vectors derived from projected Gaussian center displacements. For uncertainty, the method computes per-pixel variance from two physically grounded cues: surface-view radiance ambiguity (squared dot product of ray direction with a pseudo-normal from depth gradient) and inter-frame flow inconsistency (Horn-Schunck-style residual between aggregated scene flow and image gradients). This total variance modulates a heteroscedastic negative log-likelihood rendering loss, adaptively suppressing supervision in low-confidence regions during training while being disabled at inference.

## Results

On NUSR, UDF achieves 33.73 PSNR / 0.965 SSIM / 0.051 LPIPS on the Turtle scene, improving over the original NUSR method by +5.63 dB PSNR and reducing LPIPS by 76%. On DRUVA, UDF reaches 34.03 PSNR / 0.964 SSIM on A11, outperforming WaterSplatting by +1.70 dB PSNR. On SeaThru, UDF attains 32.95 PSNR / 0.930 SSIM / 0.065 LPIPS on Panama, surpassing 4DGS by +7.05 dB PSNR. Across all three datasets, UDF consistently achieves the best or near-best PSNR and LPIPS, with only marginal SSIM losses to WaterSplatting on a few static SeaThru sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UDF outperforms all 12 baselines on the majority of scenes and metrics. Against the closest competitor WaterSplatting [11], UDF gains +1.25 dB PSNR on NUSR Sardine, +8.53 dB on Turtle, and +1.70 dB on DRUVA A11, while WaterSplatting retains a marginal SSIM edge on a few static SeaThru sequences (e.g., Curaçao: 0.948 vs. 0.940). Compared to the original NUSR method [12], UDF improves PSNR by 2.83–5.63 dB across all four NUSR scenes and reduces LPIPS by up to 76% (Turtle: 0.216→0.051). Against 4DGS [46], which lacks explicit medium modeling, UDF achieves substantially higher PSNR (e.g., +7.05 dB on SeaThru Panama, +12.83 dB on IUI3-RedSea), confirming the importance of the neural medium module. SeaThru-NeRF [10] and DynamicNeRF [13] are consistently outperformed by 2–10 dB PSNR, reflecting their inability to handle dynamic geometry and medium jointly. On DRUVA, UDF surpasses TiNeuVox [101] and 4DGaussian [52] by 6–11 dB PSNR, and in the appendix comparison on SeaThru, UDF leads UW-GS [14], SeaSplat [76], and Gaussian Splashing [77] by 1.5–2.1 dB PSNR on Curaçao.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting + underwater medium line (SeaThru-NeRF, WaterSplatting) into the dynamic regime by embedding both Gaussian geometry and a spectral neural medium field into a shared 4D K-Planes space, then predicting temporal deformations of Gaussians and motion-conditioned medium offsets via separate networks. It departs from prior dynamic underwater methods (NUSR, UW-GS) that handle motion via binary masks or ignore the participating medium during rendering, and from generic 4DGS that assume a clear-air medium. The key conceptual departure is a heteroscedastic rendering loss whose per-pixel variance is derived from two physically grounded cues—surface-view radiance ambiguity (ray–pseudo-normal alignment) and Horn-Schunck-style inter-frame flow inconsistency—rather than from learned or Bayesian uncertainty estimates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UDF addresses a practically important gap: in-the-wild underwater video reconstruction where visibility degrades non-uniformly across frames and viewpoints, and where both geometry and the scattering medium evolve over time. The motion-aware medium offset (conditioning medium updates on projected Gaussian scene flow) is a clean mechanism that prior underwater methods lack, and the uncertainty formulation offers a template for down-weighting unreliable observations in other scattering-media settings. Open questions remain around active/non-uniform lighting, epistemic uncertainty, fine-scale fast-object motion, and whether the photometric-only supervision can fully disentangle medium from object albedo in spectrally biased (deep-water) conditions.
