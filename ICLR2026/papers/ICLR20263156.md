## Problem

It remains unclear whether reinforcement learning on LLMs can genuinely acquire new reasoning procedures absent from the base model, or merely sharpen existing latent skills. Prior benchmarks mix topics and difficulty, making it impossible to isolate learnability (can RL solve problems where pass@K=0?) from generalization (do learned skills transfer to OOD variants?). Additionally, standard GRPO training collapses on such hard tasks because the absence of any successful rollout eliminates the reward-difference gradient signal entirely.

## Input / Output

For Manufactoria, the input is a text prompt specifying a custom DSL (puller/painter nodes) and an acceptance criterion (e.g., 'accept if tape contains substring BRRR'); the output is a factory program in that DSL, scored by whether it routes all test tapes correctly. For BouncingSim, the input is a fully specified 2D physics scene (polygon containers, ball positions/velocities, rotation rates, gravity) and the output is a Python function predict_position(t) returning exact [x,y] coordinates rounded to 2 decimals, verified against Box2D oracle trajectories. For competition coding, the input is a natural-language algorithmic problem and the output is a complete program passing all hidden test cases within time limits.

## Method

The paper introduces DELTA, a controlled benchmark of synthetic programming families (Manufactoria as a fully OOD custom-syntax puzzle, BouncingSim for physics simulation, and competition coding), paired with a staged RL training recipe that overcomes the pass@K=0 barrier. The key mechanism is a two-phase reward schedule: a warm-up phase optimizes a dense per-test pass-rate reward to push the model out of the all-zero region, followed by a switch to binary full-pass reward that sharpens partial solutions into verified completions. This produces a distinctive grokking phase transition — a long exploration plateau (hundreds of steps at <1% full-pass) followed by an abrupt jump to near-perfect accuracy. Additional acceleration strategies include experience replay (reinserting successful traces into future rollouts) and feedback-in-the-loop (injecting verifier failure messages mid-generation). Generalization is then probed along three axes — exploratory (harder same-family variants), compositional (unseen skill combinations), and transformative (qualitatively new dynamics) — revealing strong transfer for the first two but persistent failure on transformative cases.

## Results

On the Manufactoria-HAS family (where Qwen3-4B-Instruct achieves 0% full pass rate at pass@128), staged RL training with dense-to-binary rewards produces a grokking phase transition, reaching 100% full pass rate after ~450 steps of exploration. On BouncingSim generalization, RL-trained Qwen3-4B-Instruct achieves 70–85% full-pass on in-distribution Basic, 50–75% on Easy, 15–50% on Medium, and single digits on Hard (exploratory axis); 60–70% on unseen compositional combinations (e.g., ROT_BOX+MOV_BOX); and near-zero on transformative (periodic trajectory) cases. GPT-5 leads overall on BouncingSim, but even it drops from ~80% to ~10% on MULTI_OBJ from Basic to Medium difficulty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing RL methods in a head-to-head fashion; instead, it compares training strategies and model configurations. Naive GRPO with binary full-pass reward completely fails on pass@K=0 tasks (stagnates at 0%), while per-test pass-rate reward alone saturates quickly (<0.01% full pass after ~100 steps). The proposed staged warm-up (per-test → full-pass) is the only strategy that achieves the grokking transition to 100%. Experience replay and feedback-in-the-loop accelerate grokking but introduce off-policy instability. On BouncingSim, GPT-5 substantially outperforms Qwen3-4B-Instruct and other open LLMs (which trail at ≤30–40% on easy-to-medium tiers and near-zero on Hard/Extreme), but even GPT-5 struggles with compositional and multi-object settings. Compared to OMEGA's math generalization results, DELTA's coding tasks show stronger compositional transfer (60–70% vs. weak transfer in OMEGA), attributed to structural modularity of simulation code, while transformative generalization remains near-zero in both settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends OMEGA's controlled generalization framework (exploratory, compositional, transformative axes) from synthetic math to programming, where templated generators yield automatically verifiable tasks with dense per-test-case rewards. It builds directly on GRPO (DeepSeek-R1) as the RL backbone but identifies and addresses a fundamental limitation: when no rollout succeeds (pass@K=0), GRPO has no gradient signal. The paper positions itself against the skeptical camp (Yue et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2025) arguing RL cannot exceed base-model representational limits, and alongside the optimistic camp (Liu et al., 2025b) showing RL can expand reasoning boundaries, by providing a controlled testbed that isolates the mechanism. It also connects to the grokking literature (Power et al., 2022) but is, to the authors' knowledge, the first to demonstrate grokking-like phase transitions during RL fine-tuning of LLMs on reasoning tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DELTA benchmark and the staged warm-up recipe offer a concrete, reproducible path for enabling RL to solve previously unsolvable problem families, directly challenging the prevailing assumption that RL merely sharpens latent skills. The grokking phase transition—hundreds of steps of near-zero reward followed by abrupt convergence to near-perfect accuracy—suggests that RL training dynamics on hard tasks are qualitatively different from what aggregate metrics on mixed pools reveal, motivating explicit tracking of the 'hard frontier' subset. Key limitations remain: transformative generalization (qualitatively novel solution schemas) still fails, the warm-up recipe does not universally unlock all families (e.g., Manufactoria-PREPEND), and results are primarily demonstrated on 4B-scale models. Open questions include whether the dense-reward-to-binary-reward principle transfers to math and formal verification, and how to handle the transformative axis where models must invent entirely new invariants.
