## Problem

Group DRO, which up-weights high-loss groups to reduce worst-group performance gaps, fails in multilingual ASR because the CTC loss scales with input and transcript length and varies with linguistic/acoustic properties, making group losses incomparable. This causes the DRO weight update to overemphasize groups with inherently high losses (e.g., due to long utterances) rather than groups that are genuinely underperforming, ultimately worsening worst-language accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw speech audio of variable duration (batches are constructed to target ~50 seconds of total audio per batch) and produces a language identification token followed by a character-level transcription via CTC decoding. Two pre-trained self-supervised encoders (XLS-R with 128 languages, MMS with 1406 languages) are fine-tuned with two added Transformer layers and a softmax head; all parameters are updated jointly for the combined LID+ASR task.

## Method

CTC-DRO introduces two modifications to group DRO: (1) a length-matched batch sampler that selects all samples from a single group per batch while targeting a fixed total audio duration, so that summed per-batch CTC losses are comparable across groups; and (2) a smoothed weight update rule q_g ← q_g·exp(η_q·L_g/(q_g+α)) / Σ, where the smoothing parameter α prevents any single group's weight from dominating. The smoothed update is derived as the Hedge algorithm solution to a generalized maximization objective max_q Σ log(q_g+α)·L_g, and a Lagrangian analysis shows the optimal weights still increase monotonically with group loss but are more evenly distributed. The model parameters are updated via gradient descent on the weighted sum of per-utterance CTC losses scaled by |G|, keeping computational overhead minimal (one scalar weight per group).

## Results

On the ML-SUPERB 2.0 benchmark across five language sets (6 languages each) with both MMS and XLS-R backbones, CTC-DRO reduces the worst-language CER by up to 47.1% relative to the baseline (e.g., 68.8% → 45.0% for XLS-R on set 2) and the average CER by up to 32.9%. CTC-DRO achieves the best worst-language CER in all 14 model-set configurations and the best average CER in 13 of 14. In contrast, standard group DRO worsens the worst-language CER in 7 of 14 settings (up to +57.5% relative) and increases the average CER in all 14 settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against standard group DRO (Sagawa et al., 2020), which is the natural baseline for worst-group optimization. CTC-DRO decisively outperforms group DRO on the worst-language CER in all 14 settings (7 language sets × 2 backbones), with group DRO actually *worsening* the worst-language CER in 7 of those 14 settings (e.g., 60.8% → 86.6% for MMS set 1; 90.0% → 96.3% for MMS set 1 with extra data). Group DRO also increases the average CER in every single setting, whereas CTC-DRO improves it in 13 of 14. The sole exception where CTC-DRO does not beat the plain baseline on average CER is XLS-R on set 3 (17.7% vs. 17.0%), though it still wins on worst-language CER there. The paper attributes group DRO's failure to the Hedge-style weight update concentrating nearly all weight on a single high-loss group (often due to longer audio durations inflating CTC loss), while CTC-DRO's smoothed update keeps weights more evenly distributed throughout training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends group DRO (Sagawa et al., 2020) to the specific challenge of CTC-based multilingual ASR, where the standard group DRO assumption of comparable group losses is violated by CTC's length-dependent scaling and irreducible linguistic/acoustic variance. The smoothed weight update (Equation 10) is derived as a generalization of the Hedge algorithm (Slivkins, 2019) that underlies group DRO's online optimization, while the length-matched batching addresses a practical issue not addressed in prior DRO work. The paper positions itself as the first robust optimization method to successfully reduce cross-lingual ASR disparities, departing from prior DRO-for-speech work (Gao et al., 2022) that reported negative results, and from architectural approaches (Chen et al., 2023; Lu et al., 2024) that condition on language identity rather than directly optimizing worst-group performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CTC-DRO offers a low-overhead training modification (tracking one scalar per group) that substantially reduces worst-language error in multilingual ASR, making it practically adoptable for existing CTC pipelines built on XLS-R or MMS. The finding that standard group DRO actively degrades performance in this setting (worsening worst-language CER in 7/14 configurations) is an important caution for practitioners applying DRO to domains with incomparable group losses. The smoothed maximization objective is generalizable beyond ASR to any setting with variable-length inputs and irreducible group-level loss variance (e.g., medical imaging, video transcription). Open questions include whether the approach extends to pre-training, whether groups can be learned automatically rather than pre-defined, and whether disparities can be further reduced beyond the current improvements.
