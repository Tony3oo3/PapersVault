## Problem

Existing spatio-temporal graph neural networks rely on static topologies and offline training, making them unsuitable for real-world streaming scenarios where graphs expand and distributions shift. While continual spatio-temporal forecasting (CSTF) methods have been proposed, they typically employ simple backbones (stacks of graph/temporal convolutions) that struggle with dynamic correlations and long-term drift, and their adaptation strategies (direct parameter expansion, prompt concatenation) are weakly coupled with the backbone, failing to balance stability, adaptability, and interpretability.

## Input / Output

At each incremental period τ, the model receives a streaming spatio-temporal tensor X_τ ∈ R^{N_τ × T_h} (12-step lookback) over an evolving graph G_τ whose node count grows without any predefined adjacency matrix, and produces a 12-step-ahead forecast Y_τ ∈ R^{N_τ × T_f}. The graph evolves as G_τ = G_{τ-1} + ΔG_τ, with node counts ranging from 655→871 (PEMS-Stream), 480→1698 (CA-Stream), to 1087→1202 (AIR-Stream), covering gradual, explosive, and stable expansion regimes.

## Method

STBP couples a general spatio-temporal backbone with a scalable contextual pattern bank. The backbone comprises a Frequency-Domain Network (FreNet) that applies FFT with a learnable complex embedding to extract stable low-frequency components (periodicity, trends) resilient to distribution drift, followed by a Dual-Stream Linear Graph Attention (DLGA) module that uses random feature mapping to reduce attention from O(N²) to O(N) while incorporating the pattern bank as a second key stream to assess input-to-stored-knowledge relevance. The contextual pattern bank P_τ ∈ R^{N_τ × d} consists of three trainable components (P^(0), P^(1), P^(2)) that interact with the backbone via a gating function and attention mechanism to model node-level heterogeneity and relevance. During initial training (τ=1), both components are jointly optimized; in subsequent periods the backbone is frozen while the pattern bank is expanded by concatenating new parameters for added nodes and fine-tuned, acting as prompts that guide the frozen backbone to adapt without revisiting historical data.

## Results

STBP achieves the best average MAE across all three datasets: 12.31 on PEMS-Stream, 15.65 on CA-Stream, and 15.77 on AIR-Stream (averaged over 3/6/12-step horizons). Compared to the strongest baseline (EAC), STBP reduces average MAE by 21.44% on PEMS-Stream, 21.93% on CA-Stream, and 2.35% on AIR-Stream. On AIR-Stream, STBP also achieves the best RMSE (25.70) and MAPE (14.94%), outperforming the next-best EAC (31.18 RMSE, 20.17% MAPE). In the few-shot setting (10% training data for later periods), STBP further extends its lead, e.g., 13.58 MAE vs. 16.13 for EAC on PEMS-Stream.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STBP outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset and metric. Among conventional STGNNs, GWNet (MAE 19.87 on PEMS-Stream) and STID (26.07) perform worst because they are retrained from scratch each period and cannot exploit historical knowledge; iTransformer (21.60) improves via online fine-tuning but still suffers catastrophic forgetting. Among CSTF methods, the prompt-based approaches (EAC, STRAP) outperform full-parameter fine-tuning methods (TrafficStream, STKEC, PECPM), confirming the benefit of freezing the backbone and tuning only lightweight parameters. EAC is the closest competitor (15.67 MAE on PEMS-Stream vs. STBP's 12.31), but STBP's dual-stream linear graph attention and frequency-domain backbone provide a clear 21% MAE advantage. STRAP degrades notably on CA-Stream (22.17 MAE vs. EAC's 20.42), indicating that retrieval-based pattern matching struggles under rapid, large-scale topology expansion (+254% node growth). On AIR-Stream, the gap narrows (STBP 15.77 vs. EAC 20.20 MAE), likely because the modest +10% node expansion is less challenging, yet STBP still leads by 22%.

## Relation to Prior Work

STBP sits at the intersection of spatio-temporal graph neural networks and continual learning, building directly on the CSTF paradigm established by TrafficStream, STKEC, PECPM, STRAP, and EAC. It departs from prior CSTF methods in two key ways: (1) replacing the simple CNN/GCN backbones used by most baselines with a frequency-domain network (FreNet) and a dual-stream linear graph attention (DLGA) module that models dynamic spatial correlations at O(N) cost, and (2) introducing a structured three-component contextual pattern bank (P⁰, P¹, P²) that interacts with the frozen backbone via gating and attention rather than the simpler parameter expansion or prompt concatenation used by EAC and STRAP. The linear attention mechanism draws on Katharopoulos et al. (2020), while the frequency-domain temporal processing follows recent work on stable component extraction for distributional robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STBP offers a practical deployment paradigm for urban sensing: freeze a general backbone once, then adapt to new nodes and distributions by expanding a small set of pattern parameters—avoiding costly full retraining while preserving historical knowledge. The ~21% MAE reduction on PEMS-Stream and CA-Stream over the best CSTF baseline (EAC) is substantial, though the gain on AIR-Stream is more modest (~2.4%), suggesting the approach benefits most from rapid topological expansion. A key open question is cross-domain generalization: the paper acknowledges that STBP is validated only in single-task settings and that significant structural divergence between source and target domains remains untested. The authors also point toward spatio-temporal foundation models and LLM integration as natural next steps.
