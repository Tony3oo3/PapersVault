## Problem

Standard value-based RL trains critics to estimate absolute state values V(s), yet only value differences are behaviorally meaningful for control — the absolute scale is a gauge degree of freedom that invites drift and is ill-posed in preference-based or human-in-the-loop settings. No prior method provides a Bellman-consistent critic that directly learns antisymmetric value differences as its primary objective.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 84×84 grayscale game frames (4-frame stacking, scaled to [0,1]) as state observations. The critic receives pairs of states from the training batch — with 33% probability the second state is drawn from the same episode as the first to encourage temporally coherent comparisons — and outputs a single scalar value difference Δ(s_i, s_j). The policy outputs action probabilities via PPO, while R-GAE advantages computed from the pairwise differences drive the policy gradient update.

## Method

RV replaces the absolute value critic with a siamese antisymmetric difference head: a shared CNN encoder f_enc(s) is applied to both states in a pair, and the value difference is computed as Δ_θ(s_i, s_j) = wᵀ(f_enc(s_i) − f_enc(s_j)) with a single bias-free linear projection, enforcing antisymmetry and zero self-difference by construction. The paper proves a pairwise Bellman operator T_π is a γ-contraction on bounded antisymmetric functions with a unique fixed point equal to true value differences, and derives well-posed 1-step, n-step, and λ-return targets that handle terminal states via case distinctions over successor flags. Generalized advantage estimation is reconstructed from pairwise differences (R-GAE) by anchoring relative values to zero per trajectory, and the paper proves this yields an unbiased policy-gradient estimator identical to standard GAE up to a trajectory-constant baseline. A trajectory-ranking initialization estimates per-episode offsets from the learned differences to reduce the variance inflation caused by the zero-anchor, and the full system is trained as a drop-in replacement for the PPO critic on 49 Atari games.

## Results

On the 49-game Atari benchmark (40M frames, 10 seeds), PPO+RV achieves competitive performance, exceeding standard PPO on 30/49 games (61%) and Direct Advantage Estimation (DAE) on 37/49 games (75%). Notable wins include BattleZone (21,780 vs. 17,367 for PPO), Qbert (15,262 vs. 14,293), TimePilot (10,213 vs. 4,342), and RoadRunner (43,346 vs. 25,076). The method also reports aggregate metrics (median, IQM, mean, optimality gap) with 95% stratified bootstrap confidence intervals, confirming competitive overall performance. Each 40M-frame run takes ~65 minutes on a single A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPO+RV is compared against two baselines: standard PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) and Direct Advantage Estimation (DAE, Pan et al., 2022). Against PPO, PPO+RV wins on 30/49 games (61%), with particularly large margins on TimePilot (10,213 vs. 4,342, +135%), RoadRunner (43,346 vs. 25,076, +73%), and BattleZone (21,780 vs. 17,367, +25%), but suffers notable losses on Centipede (1,226 vs. 4,386, −72%), Zaxxon (846 vs. 5,009, −83%), and StarGunner (24,831 vs. 32,689, −24%). Against DAE, PPO+RV is more consistently superior, winning on 37/49 games (75%), with DAE struggling on many games (e.g., Enduro at 0.0, MontezumaRevenge at 0.1, DemonAttack at 2,477). The aggregate metrics (median, IQM, mean, optimality gap) reported in Figure 3 confirm that PPO+RV is broadly competitive with PPO overall, while clearly outperforming DAE. The paper notes that the relative critic uses the same CNN encoder and network architecture as PPO, making the comparison a direct drop-in replacement of the absolute value head with the antisymmetric pairwise difference head.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PPO and GAE, replacing the absolute value critic with a pairwise antisymmetric function Δ(s_i, s_j) that learns value differences as the primary objective. Conceptually, it connects to Bertsekas's differential training of rollout policies (1997) and relative value iteration in average-reward MDPs, but departs from those by operating in discounted MDPs with a full Bellman-consistent pairwise operator. It also relates to Direct Advantage Estimation (DAE) and dueling networks, which restructure what the critic learns, but RV is distinct in that it never predicts an absolute value for any state and enforces antisymmetry by architecture. The paper explicitly positions itself as filling a gap: pairwise objectives existed in preference-based RL and inverse RL, but not as a Bellman-consistent value critic with proven contraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gauge-invariance argument—that only value differences are behaviorally meaningful—provides a principled justification for removing the arbitrary offset degree of freedom from value learning, which is particularly relevant for preference-based and human-in-the-loop settings where absolute scale is ambiguous. The theoretical contributions (pairwise Bellman contraction, R-GAE unbiasedness, variance analysis) are clean and complete. However, practical limitations temper the impact: pairwise training is O(B²), the antisymmetric head restricts critic expressivity, and experiments are confined to discrete-action Atari with PPO. Open questions include whether RV generalizes to continuous control, off-policy methods, and whether the trajectory-ranking initialization scales to very long horizons where B_t variance inflation becomes severe.
