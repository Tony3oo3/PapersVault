## Problem

Multi-modal datasets frequently suffer from missing modalities, and while pre-trained generative models offer a natural completion mechanism, they perform poorly on domain-specific data and are impractical to fine-tune due to limited complete samples, restricted API access, and high computational cost. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has shown strong promise in NLP but remains largely unexplored for data-level missing modality completion, where the goal is to reconstruct the actual missing modality (not latent features) so the completed dataset can be directly reused for downstream training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an incomplete sample (either an image lacking its caption or a text lacking its image) together with a pre-constructed dual knowledge base of paired image-text entries drawn from both the target dataset's complete samples and a public external corpus (e.g., CC3M or NWPU-Caption). It outputs the raw missing modality—a natural-language caption generated by BLIP2/LLaVA or an image generated by Stable Diffusion XL/Juggernaut-XL—rather than a latent embedding, yielding a fully paired multi-modal dataset ready for downstream CLIP retrieval or LLaVA captioning training. The generation is conditioned on a retrieved exemplar caption or image injected into the prompt, and multiple candidates are produced before a scoring-based selection picks the final output.

## Method

RAG4DMC builds a unified dual knowledge base by (i) training a lightweight bidirectional MLP to map image embeddings to text embeddings and vice versa, (ii) pruning irrelevant external samples via K-means clustering with centroid-level and instance-level cosine-similarity thresholds, and (iii) aligning the remaining external embeddings into the internal embedding space through iterative orthogonal Procrustes rotation (SVD-based, refined with CSLS mutual-nearest-neighbor pairs). At inference, a two-stage multi-modal fusion retrieval first performs intra-modal top-k cosine search, then re-ranks candidates using a weighted fusion score that combines the intra-modal similarity with a cross-modal similarity computed between the query's pseudo-embedding (from the learned MLP) and the candidate's opposite-modality embedding. The top-ranked exemplar is injected as a prompt into a pre-trained generator (BLIP2 for I2T, SDXL for T2I), which produces n candidate completions; a final selection step scores each candidate by a weighted combination of semantic alignment (cosine to the pseudo-embedding) and a quality metric (BLEU for captions, NIQE for images) to pick the most coherent output.

## Results

On MSCOCO (70% total missing rate), RAG4DMC achieves 46.6/47.5 I2T/T2I R@1 and 117.2 CIDEr, approaching the oracle Complete upper bound of 48.9/49.6 R@1 and 127.9 CIDEr. On Flickr30K (40% missing), it reaches 52.9/53.8 R@1 and 70.4 CIDEr, nearly matching the Complete baseline (53.2/52.6, 71.2). On the domain-specific RSICD dataset, RAG4DMC recovers to 8.7/9.7 R@1 and 33.9 CIDEr, almost closing the gap to Complete (10.3/10.3, 34.0), while all other RAG variants collapse to ~5 R@1 due to domain shift in the CLIP encoder.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAG4DMC consistently outperforms both external baselines across all three datasets and all metrics. Against GTI-MM (Feng et al., 2024), RAG4DMC gains +5.6 I2T R@1 and +5.8 T2I R@1 on MSCOCO (46.6 vs 41.0, 47.5 vs 41.7) and +6.2 CIDEr (117.2 vs 111.0); on RSICD the gap widens to +0.4 R@1 and +1.8 CIDEr. Against Knowledge Bridger (Ke et al., 2025), RAG4DMC improves by +4.1 I2T R@1 and +3.0 T2I R@1 on MSCOCO (46.6 vs 42.5, 47.5 vs 44.5) and +5.0 CIDEr (117.2 vs 112.2). The internal RAG ablations further decompose the gains: adding external data without filtering (Combined-RAG) provides no benefit over internal-only Vanilla-RAG, filtering and alignment (KFA-RAG) add +1.0 R@1, and the multi-modal fusion retrieval in RAG4DMC contributes a further +0.7 R@1 and +1.4 CIDEr over KFA-RAG. On the domain-specific RSICD dataset, the advantage is most dramatic: RAG4DMC recovers to near-oracle performance (8.7 R@1) while all RAG variants without fusion retrieval collapse to ~5 R@1, confirming that the cross-modal fusion mechanism is critical under domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the retrieval-augmented generation paradigm from NLP (Lewis et al., 2020) to the multi-modal missing modality completion (MMC) setting, positioning itself as the first RAG-based approach operating at the raw-data level rather than the latent-feature level. For cross-domain alignment of internal and external embeddings, it borrows the orthogonal Procrustes strategy from MUSE (Lample et al., 2018). The method departs from prior data-level completion work (GTI-MM, Knowledge Bridger, DiCMoR) by grounding generation in retrieved exemplars from a dual knowledge base, and from the closest prior RAG-for-MMC work (MissRAG) by explicitly reconstructing missing modalities in data space rather than approximating missing inputs in feature space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck—missing modalities in real multi-modal datasets—without requiring fine-tuning of large generative models, making it accessible even when only API access to generators is available. A notable finding is that naively merging external data (Combined-RAG) can actively harm performance in domain-specific settings (RSICD), underscoring that filtering and alignment are not optional but critical. Open questions include whether the approach extends beyond image-text pairs to richer multi-modal configurations (audio, video, 3D), whether the CLIP-based embedding space is a bottleneck for fine-grained domains, and how the method scales when the internal complete-sample set is extremely small.
