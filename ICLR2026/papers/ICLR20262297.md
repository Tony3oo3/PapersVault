## Problem

Existing visual token compression methods in MLLMs (e.g., Chat-UniVi, PACT) merge similar tokens to reduce sequence length but discard or inadequately preserve the original spatial and temporal positions, causing severe degradation on layout-sensitive tasks such as OCR, counting, and temporal grounding. No prior method enables a single compressed token to retain multiple distinct positional cues, leaving a gap between aggressive compression and structural fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262297/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images (512×28×28 to 1280×28×28 pixels) or video sequences (up to 64 frames, 128×28×28 to 768×28×28 pixels) paired with a text prompt, and produces a text answer or caption. Visually, the input tokens are clustered and compressed by 55% (spatial-only) or 94% (spatiotemporal) before entering the LLM, with PPE operating purely on the RoPE dimension indices rather than modifying token embeddings. The 3D M-RoPE sections are configured as [16, 24, 24] for video and [32, 32] for images, with K=8 and K=32 preserved position IDs respectively.

## Method

PPE exploits the per-dimension independence of RoPE rotations to encode multiple original position IDs within a single compressed token: the embedding dimension D is split into K contiguous chunks, and each chunk is assigned a different constituent token's position ID (selected by top-K proximity to the cluster center). This is formalized for both 1D RoPE and 3D M-RoPE, where each spatial/temporal axis section is independently subdivided into K groups. PPE is parameter-free and plug-and-play, requiring no architectural changes to the underlying MLLM or clustering algorithm. It further supports cascade compression by re-applying the same top-K ID selection at multiple transformer layers (e.g., layers 11, 23, 35), progressively reducing token count while retaining positional anchors at each stage. The key insight is that similar tokens can share feature embeddings during merging, and their distinct positions can coexist across different RoPE frequency bands without interference.

## Results

On image benchmarks with 55% token reduction, PPE achieves 79.32% average (vs. 67.61% for Chat-UniVi and 82.96% for the Dense baseline), with particularly large gains on layout-sensitive tasks: TextVQA 77.14% (+19.48 over Chat-UniVi), DocVQA 76.79% (+24.31), and ChartQA 74.52% (+24.92). On video benchmarks, PPE at 55% reduction reaches 58.69% average, surpassing both Chat-UniVi (56.74%) and the Dense baseline (58.52%); under aggressive 94% spatiotemporal compression, PPE still achieves 57.62% vs. 55.70% for Chat-UniVi. PPE introduces zero additional parameters and negligible inference overhead, with peak memory usage identical to the baseline compression method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Chat-UniVi (the primary compression baseline), PPE delivers consistent and large gains: +11.71 on Image Average, +19.48 on TextVQA, +24.31 on DocVQA, and +1.95 on Video Average at 55% reduction; at 94% spatiotemporal reduction, PPE still leads by +1.92 on Video Average. Against PACT (7B, 67% reduction, training-free), PPE-3B (55% reduction) outperforms on VideoMME (58.70 vs. 57.60) and MMBench-EN (84.78 vs. 80.30) while using fewer parameters. Against SparseVLM (7B, 66% reduction), PPE-3B substantially outperforms on MMBench-EN (84.78 vs. 64.10) and TextVQA (77.08 vs. 57.80). Against ToMe (57% reduction, training-free), PPE improves MMBench-EN by +0.26 and TextVQA by +1.22. Against VisionZip (55% reduction), PPE yields smaller but consistent gains in the SFT setting (e.g., +2.04 on TextVQA, +5.68 on DocVQA) and mixed results in the training-free setting. Compared to dense MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-3B, InternVL2.5-4B, LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B), PPE at 55% reduction matches or exceeds the 3B dense baseline on VideoMME and MVBench, and at 90% cascade compression retains comparable performance, demonstrating that positional preservation enables aggressive compression without significant quality loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

PPE extends the RoPE/M-RoPE positional encoding framework (Su et al., 2024; Bai et al., 2025) by exploiting the per-dimension independence of rotary rotations to pack multiple position IDs into a single compressed token's embedding. It departs from prior token compression methods (Chat-UniVi, PACT, ToMe, VisionZip) that assign a single representative position to each merged cluster, instead splitting the embedding dimension into K sub-groups so each compressed token retains K distinct spatiotemporal anchors. The method is built directly on Chat-UniVi's DPC-KNN clustering as its merging backbone and is designed as a parameter-free, plug-and-play operator orthogonal to the compression algorithm itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, parameter-free nature of PPE makes it immediately adoptable across existing MLLM compression pipelines without retraining, and the demonstrated 90%+ token reduction with minimal performance loss has direct practical value for deployment of vision-language models on resource-constrained hardware. However, the overall gains remain modest (2–5% on average), and under aggressive compression (90%) fine-grained text recognition still degrades, suggesting a fundamental trade-off between compression ratio and spatial precision. Open questions include whether the GCD-based choice of K generalizes to non-standard RoPE configurations, whether cascade compression can be pushed beyond three stages, and whether the approach transfers to generative (image/video synthesis) settings where positional fidelity may be even more critical.
