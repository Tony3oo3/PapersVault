## Problem

Existing dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting methods rely on per-frame optimization, which memorizes instantaneous scene states rather than learning underlying motion dynamics, causing temporal inconsistency and blur under fast or complex motion. Additionally, sparse control-node approaches that propagate motion via spatial k-NN suffer from neighborhood drift when large displacements separate initially nearby points, leading to erroneous motion transfer and visual artifacts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263895/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view or monocular video sequences (e.g., 1–2 cameras on HyperNeRF, ~20 synchronized views on Neural 3D Video, each 300 frames except one 1200-frame scene) and produces a compact dynamic 3D Gaussian representation consisting of a canonical Gaussian cloud, 2048 sparse control nodes, and a temporal attention network. At inference, it renders novel views in real time at 149.5 FPS with 60 MB storage, operating on a sliding window of T=6 frames per forward pass.

## Method

Mango-GS introduces a decoupled control node representation where each node comprises a canonical 3D position and a learnable latent feature code; a lightweight MLP computes learned affinity scores between Gaussian canonical parameters and node codes, replacing brittle spatial k-NN with semantics-aware neighborhoods that remain valid under large displacement. A multi-frame temporal attention network (MLP backbone interleaved with temporal self-attention blocks along the time axis, fused via a lightweight gating module) predicts per-node translation, rotation, and scale for all T frames simultaneously, capturing long-range motion dependencies. Node deformations are propagated to the dense Gaussian cloud via the pre-computed learned k-NN weights. Training is end-to-end with a temporal input masking strategy, a top-k hard-frame photometric loss that reweights gradients toward the most challenging frames each iteration, and a three-term motion-aware loss (temporal difference, amplitude, and direction) that explicitly supervises frame-to-frame coherence.

## Results

On the HyperNeRF-vrig dataset, Mango-GS achieves the best PSNR (26.2 dB), MS-SSIM (0.78), and lowest temporal LPIPS (0.0196) among all compared methods, while rendering at 149.5 FPS with 60 MB storage. On the Neural 3D Video dataset, it attains the highest PSNR (31.89 dB) and competitive SSIM (0.942) and LPIPS (0.049). The method is over 3× faster than the next-best methods (MotionGS at 39.9 FPS, TimeFormer at 40.9 FPS) while simultaneously delivering superior temporal coherence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mango-GS outperforms all seven baselines on the HyperNeRF-vrig PSNR (26.2 vs. 25.4 for E-D3DGS, the runner-up) and MS-SSIM (0.78 vs. 0.71 for MotionGS), with a margin of 0.8 dB and 0.07 respectively. On temporal coherence (tLPIPS), Mango-GS achieves 0.0196, beating the previous best MotionGS (0.0229) by 14% and TimeFormer (0.0265) by 26%. In rendering speed, Mango-GS at 149.5 FPS is roughly 3.3× faster than E-D3DGS (45.2 FPS) and 3.7× faster than TimeFormer (40.9 FPS), while D-3DGS and GaGS are an order of magnitude slower (14.2 and 12.0 FPS). On Neural 3D Video, Mango-GS leads in PSNR (31.89 vs. 31.84 for TimeFormer) and matches 4DGS on SSIM (0.942), though E-D3DGS edges it slightly on LPIPS (0.048 vs. 0.049). Storage is competitive at 60 MB, lower than D-3DGS (172 MB) and SC-GS (85 MB) but slightly above TimeFormer (46 MB) and GaGS (48 MB). Overall, Mango-GS achieves the best quality–speed trade-off, dominating on both spatial fidelity and temporal consistency while being the fastest method by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

Mango-GS sits at the intersection of two recent threads in dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting: sparse node-guided deformation (SC-GS, which propagates motion from a small set of control points via spatial k-NN) and temporal Transformer modeling of Gaussian dynamics (TimeFormer, which applies cross-frame attention directly to all Gaussians). The paper departs from SC-GS by decoupling each control node into a canonical position and a learned latent code, replacing brittle Euclidean k-NN with a semantics-aware affinity, and departs from TimeFormer by confining temporal attention to the sparse node space rather than the full Gaussian cloud, thereby avoiding prohibitive compute. The multi-frame windowed formulation, temporal input masking, and composite top-k/motion-aware loss are new training strategies layered on top of this architectural combination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: Mango-GS achieves the best reconstruction quality on both HyperNeRF and Neural 3D Video while rendering at 149.5 FPS—over 3× faster than the closest temporal-attention competitors—making high-fidelity dynamic scene reconstruction genuinely real-time. The decoupled node representation addresses a concrete failure mode (neighborhood drift under large non-rigid motion) that limits prior sparse-control methods, and the ablation showing that naive spatial k-NN actually degrades performance below a single-frame baseline underscores the importance of learned affinity. Open questions include whether the fixed T=6 window can be extended to longer sequences without quality loss, how the method scales to scenes with topological changes or extreme deformations, and whether the approach generalizes to monocular video without multi-view supervision.
