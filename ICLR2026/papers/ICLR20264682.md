## Problem

While diffusion sampling has become a central generative paradigm, it was unclear whether it could fundamentally fail for distributions where direct sampling is trivial. This paper formalizes and proves that when the associated denoising problem exhibits an information-computation gap, near-optimal polynomial-time score-matching drifts can still produce samples arbitrarily far from the target distribution, establishing a rigorous computational bottleneck for diffusion-based sampling.

## Input / Output

The method operates on n×n sparse rank-one matrices x = uuᵀ where u is a k-sparse unit vector, observed through the Gaussian channel y_t = tx + √t·g. The drift function m̂(y,t) must be polynomial-time computable and is trained via score-matching on finite samples. The output is a generated sample x̂_T = m̂(ŷ_T, T) whose quality is measured in Wasserstein-1 distance from the target μ; the paper shows this distance can be as large as 1 − o(1) (or 1/2 − o(1) for the centered mixture variant), meaning the generated samples are essentially uncorrelated with the true distribution.

## Method

The core construction modifies a base polytime drift m̂₀ by gating it with a hypothesis test φ: below the algorithmic threshold t_alg the drift is truncated to small norm (enforced by the information-computation gap), and above t_alg it is multiplied by φ(y,t) which detects whether the input contains true signal versus pure noise. This yields a drift that is superpolynomially close to optimal in score-matching error yet causes the discretized SDE to never accumulate enough signal to produce a correct sample. A second theorem extends the failure to all C/t-Lipschitz polytime near-optimal drifts, ruling out the possibility that smoothness alone rescues sampling. The paper also proves a reduction showing that accurate polytime diffusion sampling would imply near-Bayes-optimal denoising, so the contrapositive confirms the impossibility. Empirically, a graph neural network denoiser trained on the sparse rank-one model achieves near-optimal MSE but generates samples concentrated near zero, confirming the theoretical prediction.

## Results

The paper's main contribution is theoretical: it proves that near-optimal polynomial-time score-matching drifts (Theorem 1) and all Lipschitz near-optimal drifts (Theorem 3) produce diffusion samples with Wasserstein-1 distance at least α − o(1) from the target, where α = liminf ∫‖x‖μ(dx) > 0. In the numerical experiment (n=350, k=20), the GNN denoiser achieves the lowest MSE among three tested methods but still cannot overcome the algorithmic threshold at t_alg = n/2. The generated diffusion samples have Frobenius norms concentrated near 0, yielding a measured W1 distance of at least 0.48 from the target distribution (theoretical prediction: 0.50), confirming complete sampling failure despite near-optimal denoising performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three polynomial-time denoisers on the sparse rank-one matrix problem (n=350, k=20): (a) a spectral-plus-projection estimator, (b) a power-method approximation (25 iterations), and (c) a learned graph neural network (GNN). The GNN denoiser outperforms both the spectral algorithm and its power-iteration approximation in MSE across the full range of t/n, confirming that learned methods can approach the conjectured optimal polytime denoiser. However, all three methods fail identically at the algorithmic threshold t_alg = n/2: below this threshold, none can achieve MSE significantly below 1 (the null estimator's error), and above it, all achieve near-zero MSE. Critically, despite the GNN's superior denoising performance, the diffusion samples it generates have Frobenius norms concentrated near 0, producing a W1 distance of ≥0.48 from the target — demonstrating that even the best available denoiser leads to completely incorrect sampling. The paper does not compare against alternative sampling methods (e.g., MCMC), but notes that exploiting the latent structure (sampling u then computing x = uu^T) would succeed, highlighting that the failure is specific to the direct diffusion approach on x.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the diffusion sampling framework of Song & Ermon (2019) and Song et al. (2021), connecting it to the information-computation gap literature in high-dimensional statistics (Brennan et al. 2018; Bandeira et al. 2022; Schramm & Wein 2022). Koehler & Vuong (2024) informally suggested that denoising problems with information-computation gaps could cause DS failure, but the authors note this remark is actually false in a strict sense (one can construct a polytime drift that ignores the denoising problem entirely and still samples correctly, at the cost of terrible score-matching). Earlier physics and Bayesian statistics papers (Montanari et al. 2007; Ghio et al. 2024; Huang et al. 2024) observed apparent DS failures for structured distributions but provided no formal claims. This paper is the first to rigorously prove that near-optimal score-matching drifts (among polytime algorithms) can produce samples arbitrarily far from the target, and that even Lipschitz near-optimal drifts fail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a fundamental limitation of the diffusion sampling paradigm: when the associated denoising problem has an information-computation gap, no polytime drift that is a near-optimal score matcher can produce correct samples, even though sampling from the target distribution may be trivial. This has direct practical implications—practitioners should exercise caution when adopting diffusion sampling for structured distributions where other sampling methods (e.g., exploiting latent structure) are available. Key limitations include the conditional nature of the results (relying on widely accepted conjectures analogous to P≠NP), the restriction to distributions supported on bounded sets, and the fact that non-Lipschitz polytime drifts that are near-optimal score matchers are not fully ruled out. Open questions include whether the assumptions can be derived directly from information-computation gaps, and whether the failure extends to other time-change parameterizations and practical generative tasks like image synthesis.
