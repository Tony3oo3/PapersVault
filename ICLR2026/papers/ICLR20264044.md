## Problem

Solving high-dimensional mean-field games (MFGs) remains a major computational challenge: the fixed-point structure of MFGs makes naive iteration diverge, and existing PDE-based or mesh-based solvers suffer from the curse of dimensionality. While fictitious play provides a convergent fixed-point scheme in theory, computing exact best responses at each step is intractable in high dimensions, and prior neural approaches either require a variational (potential) formulation or rely on expensive backpropagation through ODE/PDE solvers.

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial particle distribution μ (sampled from a dataset or analytic density), interaction and terminal cost functionals F and G (which may depend on the population distribution ρ), and for image tasks, source-domain images encoded in a VAE latent space. It outputs a neural velocity field v_θ parameterizing the equilibrium flow, transport trajectories in Lagrangian coordinates, and for image translation, target-domain images decoded from the latent space. A key structural detail is that the algorithm operates entirely in Lagrangian (particle) coordinates with time-discretized trajectories, and the flow matching step converts these into a valid Eulerian velocity field without requiring a spatial mesh.

## Method

The paper proposes a proximal fixed-point scheme that, at each outer iteration, (1) samples particle trajectories by integrating the current velocity field v_θ, (2) updates the particles via several steps of gradient descent on the MFG cost functional (using finite-difference approximations of ∂_tt X and ∂_t X plus (sub)gradients of F and G), and (3) trains v_θ via a flow-matching regression loss that matches the network output to the empirical particle velocities. The key insight is that flow matching serves as a 'trajectory disentangling' operator: it maps arbitrary (possibly intersecting) particle trajectories to a unique velocity field whose induced density satisfies the continuity equation, while provably preserving or reducing the dynamic cost and leaving interaction/terminal costs unchanged. Theoretically, the paper proves sublinear convergence for the optimal control setting and linear (exponential) convergence under strong convexity of F and G, and establishes equivalence between the Lagrangian and Eulerian formulations under regularity conditions on the velocity field.

## Results

On the image-to-image translation tasks (formulated as MFGs with KL-divergence terminal cost in VAE latent space), the proposed particle-based flow-matching method achieves FID of 12.44 on Handbag→Shoes and 9.68 on CelebA Male→Female. These results are essentially on par with Q-Flow (12.34 and 9.66) and outperform all other baselines including flow-matching methods (OT-CFM, SI, Rectified Flow, SB-CFM), GANs (Disco GAN, Cycle GAN), and Neural Optimal Transport (NOT). On the non-potential MFG in R², the algorithm converges to a stationary point with a residual of 10⁻¹ after 100 outer loops.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves FID scores nearly identical to Q-Flow (12.44 vs. 12.34 on Handbag→Shoes; 9.68 vs. 9.66 on Male→Female), the strongest baseline, while outperforming all other methods. Among flow-matching baselines, SB-CFM is the closest competitor (12.70 / 11.55), followed by OT-CFM (13.01 / 12.88) and Rectified Flow (13.91 / 14.01); Stochastic Interpolants (SI) performs worst among flow methods (15.87 / 16.39). GAN-based methods (Disco GAN, Cycle GAN) and Neural Optimal Transport (NOT) are substantially outperformed, with Disco GAN showing the largest gap (22.42 / 35.64). Notably, the method's advantage over flow-matching baselines is most pronounced on the CelebA task, where it improves FID by 1.87 over SB-CFM and 3.2 over OT-CFM, suggesting the MFG-based proximal fixed-point scheme provides a meaningful inductive bias beyond standard conditional flow matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of mean-field game theory and modern flow-matching generative models. It extends the fictitious play framework for MFGs (Cardaliaguet & Hadikhanloo, 2017; Yu et al., 2024) by replacing the exact best-response computation with a proximal particle update, and introduces flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) as a simulation-free mechanism to learn the velocity field from particle trajectories. Unlike prior neural MFG solvers that backpropagate through PDE/ODE solvers (Ruthotto et al., 2020; Huang et al., 2023) or rely on mesh-based discretizations, this work operates entirely in Lagrangian coordinates with a decoupled FM training step, enabling scalability to high-dimensional image data. The key conceptual departure is the 'trajectory disentangling' property of FM, which provides a rigorous bridge between the particle-based (Lagrangian) and density-based (Eulerian) formulations of MFGs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first particle-based, simulation-free method for solving high-dimensional MFGs with theoretical convergence guarantees (sublinear in general, linear under convexity), addressing a fundamental scalability bottleneck that has limited MFG computation to low-dimensional PDE solvers. The equivalence results between Lagrangian and Eulerian formulations via FM are a clean theoretical contribution that could inform future work on particle methods in optimal transport and control. However, convergence is only proven for the optimal control setting (F, G independent of ρ), leaving the general non-potential MFG case theoretically open; practical sources of error (sampling, finite differences, FM approximation) are not yet accounted for in the analysis. The empirical results on image translation are competitive with Q-Flow but do not clearly surpass it, suggesting the method's primary value is in its generality and theoretical grounding rather than raw performance.
