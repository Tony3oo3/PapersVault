## Problem

Standard representational similarity methods align each layer of one network to its single best-matching layer in another independently, producing asymmetric mappings, no unified global alignment score, and poor performance when networks differ in depth. These limitations stem from enforcing rigid one-to-one layer correspondences and ignoring the global activation structure across all layers simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262806/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes layer-wise activation matrices X_ℓ ∈ R^{T×n_ℓ} and Y_m ∈ R^{T×n_m} from two networks of potentially different depths (L vs. M layers), or equivalently fMRI voxel response matrices from two subjects treated as 'layers' with voxels as 'neurons.' It produces a soft layer-to-layer transport plan P ∈ R^{L×M} with uniform marginals (each source layer distributes exactly 1/L mass, each target receives 1/M), per-pair neuron couplings Q_{ℓm}, a single scalar network-level alignment score, and reconstructed activations evaluated by held-out correlation. The mass-conservation constraints ensure no layer is arbitrarily overweighted or ignored, and depth mismatches are resolved by distributing a source layer's mass across multiple target layers.

## Method

MOT operates at two nested levels: an inner soft-matching OT aligns neurons within each candidate layer pair (ℓ,m) using correlation distance on tuning functions, yielding a scalar cost C_{ℓm} and a neuron coupling Q_{ℓm}; an outer OT over the resulting L×M cost matrix finds a globally consistent layer coupling P with uniform row and column marginals. When L=M, the Birkhoff-von Neumann theorem guarantees P is a scaled permutation (one-to-one matching); when L≠M, the soft coupling naturally distributes mass across multiple target layers to handle depth mismatches. A rotation-invariant extension (MOT+R) alternates between OT updates and orthogonal Procrustes rotations to capture geometric equivalences in rotation-invariant spaces like Vision Transformers. A three-level extension adds a checkpoint-level OT over training trajectories, enabling alignment of two models across their entire training histories.

## Results

On LLM alignment (reconstruction correlation on held-out data), MOT consistently outperforms all baselines, with the largest gap for cross-family comparisons (e.g., LLaMA-3.2 1B vs Qwen-2.5 3B: MOT 0.432 vs Pairwise Best OT 0.380). On vision model alignment, the rotation-augmented variant MOT+R achieves substantially higher reconstruction scores than all baselines, reaching 0.850 for ViT-MAE Base vs ViT-MAE Large (vs. 0.596 for Pairwise Best+R) and 0.790 for DINOv2 Small vs DINOv2 Giant (vs. 0.418 for Pairwise Best+R). On cross-subject fMRI alignment, MOT matches pairwise OT in reconstruction correlation (e.g., 0.244 vs 0.245 for Subject A vs B) while uniquely recovering structured region-to-region correspondences. Across all domains, MOT transport plans exhibit high hierarchy scores (0.83–1.00) compared to pairwise methods (0.50–0.98), confirming emergent diagonal layer correspondences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MOT against four baselines: Random Layer Assignment (Perm-P), Single-Best OT (top-1 layer from MOT's soft plan), Independent Pairwise OT (greedy per-layer matching), and rotation-aware variants. On LLMs, MOT wins across all six model pairs, with the most substantial gain for cross-family comparisons (LLaMA-3.2 1B vs Qwen-2.5 3B: 0.432 vs 0.380 for Pairwise Best OT, a 14% relative improvement). On visual cortex fMRI data, MOT ties or marginally underperforms Pairwise Best OT in raw reconstruction correlation (e.g., 0.244 vs 0.245) but uniquely produces structured, biologically meaningful region-to-region transport plans that pairwise methods fail to recover. On vision models, vanilla MOT is mixed against Pairwise Best OT (sometimes winning, sometimes losing), but MOT+R decisively outperforms all baselines by large margins—e.g., 0.850 vs 0.596 (ViT-MAE Base vs Large) and 0.778 vs 0.394 (DINOv2 Small vs Large)—demonstrating that rotation invariance is critical for capturing geometric equivalences in transformer representations. The hierarchy score analysis (Appendix G) confirms that MOT and MOT+R plans are consistently more diagonal (H=0.83–1.00) than pairwise plans (H=0.50–0.98), while pairwise linear predictivity and RSA baselines also fail to reveal hierarchical structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Soft-Matching optimal transport metric (Khosla and Williams, 2024) from pairwise layer comparisons to a globally consistent multi-level framework. Standard representational similarity methods (RSA, CKA, Procrustes, linear predictivity) and even Soft-Matching itself enforce rigid one-to-one layer correspondences optimized independently per layer; MOT departs from this by jointly inferring soft layer-to-layer couplings and neuron-to-neuron transport plans under mass-conservation constraints, drawing on the Birkhoff-von Neumann theorem to guarantee permutation solutions when depths match. The rotation-invariant extension (MOT+R) further incorporates orthogonal Procrustes transformations, bridging the gap between rotation-sensitive OT methods and rotation-invariant metrics like CKA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MOT provides a principled, single-score framework for comparing networks of arbitrary depth, a setting where all prior pairwise methods break down. The emergent hierarchical structure in transport plans—revealing that deeper networks spread computations across multiple layers that shallower networks compress—offers a concrete, interpretable lens on how architectural choices shape representational organization. Key limitations include the O(L²n³ log n) computational cost, which restricts scaling to very wide or deep models, and the evaluation scope (limited model families and four fMRI subjects). Open questions include whether the observed convergence patterns reflect the contravariance principle or the Platonic representation hypothesis, and whether the framework can be extended with structural priors (e.g., smoothness) for more interpretable alignments.
