## Problem

RLVR methods like GRPO suffer from entropy collapse driven by a self-reinforcing loop: the policy repeatedly samples dominant reasoning modes, receives positive feedback, and further amplifies them while suppressing alternatives. Existing exploration-enhancing techniques (higher temperature, entropy regularization, DAPO clip-higher) flatten the output distribution uniformly but fail to shift probability mass away from dominant modes, leaving the self-reinforcing loop intact and limiting generalization to out-of-distribution benchmarks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a mathematical question and produces a group of G reasoning traces (each ending in a final answer), scored by a binary rule-based reward on answer correctness. The key structural detail is that the G rollouts are generated in two stages of G/2 each, separated by a temporary unlearning step on the rollout model; the rollout model is re-synchronized from the policy model at the start of every iteration, so the unlearning effect is confined to a single rollout and never accumulates into the policy parameters.

## Method

EEPO inserts a sample-then-forget mechanism into the GRPO rollout: after sampling the first G/2 trajectories, it performs a single gradient-ascent step on the rollout model using a complementary loss L_comp = −log(1 − p_clip) that penalizes high-probability (dominant) tokens more heavily than low-probability ones, effectively suppressing the just-sampled responses. An entropy-conditioned gate (activating only when a short-horizon moving average of token-level entropy falls below threshold α) ensures unlearning triggers at the onset of mode collapse rather than disrupting healthy exploration. Because the update is a single step without momentum and the rollout model is reset from the policy each iteration, the intervention is lightweight, temporary, and fully decoupled from the GRPO policy update. All G trajectories from both stages are then used together in the standard GRPO objective, with the importance-sampling denominator computed from the (potentially modified) rollout model that generated each trajectory.

## Results

EEPO achieves average relative improvements over GRPO of 24.3% on Qwen2.5-3B (21.0% → 26.1%), 33.0% on Llama3.2-3B-Instruct (17.6% → 23.4%), and 10.4% on Qwen3-8B-Base (34.7% → 38.3%) across five mathematical reasoning benchmarks. On the hardest benchmark (AIME 2024), EEPO reaches 6.7% on Qwen2.5-3B versus 3.3% for GRPO, and 20.0% on Llama3.2-3B-Instruct versus 13.3%. On AMC 2023, EEPO achieves 45.0% (Qwen2.5-3B) and 62.5% (Qwen3-8B-Base) compared to 30.3% and 50.0% for GRPO respectively. EEPO also scales to Qwen3-14B-Base, improving average accuracy from 40.6% to 44.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EEPO consistently outperforms GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) across all three model families and all five benchmarks, with the largest relative gains on smaller models (24.3% on Qwen2.5-3B, 33.0% on Llama3.2-3B-Instruct) and a more modest but still significant 10.4% on Qwen3-8B-Base. Against DAPO's clip-higher variant (Yu et al., 2025), EEPO achieves substantially higher average accuracy: 26.1% vs. 22.9% on Qwen2.5-3B, 23.4% vs. 19.5% on Llama3.2-3B-Instruct, and 38.3% vs. 32.7% on Qwen3-8B-Base. The gap is most pronounced on competition-level benchmarks (AMC 2023, AIME 2024/2025), where DAPO clip-higher plateaus or even degrades (e.g., 10.0% on AIME25 for Qwen3-8B-Base vs. 23.3% for EEPO), while EEPO maintains gains. EEPO also surpasses other exploration strategies (higher temperature, increased entropy regularization, more rollouts) by 2.2–5.1 absolute points on average, and these gains are additive: combining EEPO with increased entropy or clip-higher yields further improvements (27.9% and 26.6% respectively vs. 26.1% for EEPO alone on Qwen2.5-3B).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), the dominant RLVR algorithm for LLM reasoning, by inserting a temporary unlearning step between two rollout stages. It departs from prior exploration-enhancement strategies—DAPO's clip-higher (Yu et al., 2025), entropy regularization, and temperature scaling—that modify the objective or sampling distribution uniformly, by instead operating directly on the rollout model's weights to suppress recently sampled trajectories. The mechanism repurposes machine unlearning (Liu et al., 2024), previously studied for privacy and knowledge erasure, as a lightweight, single-step intervention confined to the rollout phase, decoupling exploration from policy optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EEPO offers a practical, low-overhead remedy for entropy collapse in RLVR that scales from 3B to 14B parameters and transfers across base and instruction-tuned models without altering the GRPO objective. The finding that a single gradient step of complementary-loss unlearning suffices—without accumulation or additional hyperparameter tuning—makes the technique easy to integrate into existing RLVR pipelines. Open questions include whether the sample-then-forget mechanism generalizes beyond verifiable-reward domains (e.g., open-ended generation, code), whether more sophisticated unlearning schedules (multi-step, curriculum-based) could yield further gains, and how the method interacts with longer-horizon or multi-turn reasoning settings.
