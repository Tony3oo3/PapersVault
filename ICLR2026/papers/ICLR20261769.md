## Problem

Evidential Deep Learning (EDL) has been analyzed almost exclusively from a probabilistic perspective, leaving the role of optimization dynamics during training underexplored. A critical gap is that commonly used uncertainty measures (e.g., Vacuity of Evidence) may contradict the training objective, misrepresenting predictive reliability. The paper asks: which uncertainty measures are actually consistent with the optimization process of EDL trained with the UCE loss?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261769/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100) as input, processed by a VGG16 backbone with a final linear classifier layer. It outputs a class label (argmax of Dirichlet mean) and a scalar uncertainty score computed as MPU = (K−1)·α_ŷ − Σ_{j≠ŷ} α_j, where α are the Dirichlet parameters derived from exponential-activated logits. Unlike VoE which only reflects total evidence magnitude, MPU captures the margin between the predicted class and all other classes, making it sensitive to both insufficient evidence (OOD) and conflicting class evidence (misclassification).

## Method

The paper establishes that minimizing the UCE loss implicitly drives the classifier toward a Crammer-Singer multi-class SVM solution by proving a margin-aware lower bound on the loss (Theorem 1) and showing the gradient dynamics mirror the SVM dual solution (Proposition 1). Building on this, it introduces the optimization-consistency principle: an uncertainty measure is valid only if its value monotonically decreases as a sample approaches the global optimum of the training objective. Guided by this principle, the authors propose Margin-aware Predictive Uncertainty (MPU), defined as the difference between the predicted class's Dirichlet parameter and the sum of all others, which is provably aligned with the UCE loss. The model is trained with VGG16, UCE loss, and exponential activation only—no KL regularization, entropy regularization, or other post-hoc constraints—demonstrating that the UCE loss alone suffices when paired with an optimization-consistent uncertainty measure.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the proposed UCE+MPU method achieves 93.35% classification accuracy (vs. 90.13% for Re-EDL and 90.09% for R-EDL) and 99.41% AUPR on misclassification detection, the best among all methods. For OOD detection, UCE+MPU achieves AUPR of 87.36 (SVHN), 88.92 (CIFAR-100), 88.71 (GTSRB), 72.82 (Places365), and 79.79 (Food101), outperforming all EDL variants on 4 of 5 OOD datasets. Replacing VoE with MPU as the uncertainty measure under the same UCE loss yields dramatic AUPR gains of +19.06 to +38.40 across OOD benchmarks, confirming the optimization-consistency principle. On CIFAR-100, UCE+MPU achieves the best AUPR on GTSRB (76.06), Places365 (49.62), CIFAR-10 (76.05), and misclassification detection (94.34).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed UCE+MPU method achieves the highest classification accuracy (93.35%) and misclassification detection AUPR (99.41%) on CIFAR-10, outperforming Re-EDL (90.13% acc, 98.81 AUPR) and R-EDL (90.09% acc, 98.98 AUPR) by 3.2% and 0.6% respectively. On OOD detection, UCE+MPU wins on CIFAR-100 (88.92 vs. 88.31 for Re-EDL), Places365 (72.82 vs. 73.42 for Re-EDL — actually Re-EDL edges ahead here), and Food101 (79.79 vs. 80.83 for Re-EDL), while Re-EDL leads on SVHN (89.94 vs. 87.36) and GTSRB (90.53 vs. 88.71). The most striking comparison is within the same UCE-trained model: switching from VoE to MPU as the uncertainty measure boosts AUPR by +38.40 (SVHN), +22.47 (CIFAR-100), +19.06 (GTSRB), +29.69 (Places365), and +34.14 (Food101), demonstrating that the choice of uncertainty measure is critical and must be optimization-consistent. Against non-EDL baselines (MC Dropout, DUQ), UCE+MPU consistently outperforms by 5–10 AUPR points on OOD detection. On CIFAR-100 (Table 3), UCE+MPU achieves the best AUPR on GTSRB (76.06), Places365 (49.62), CIFAR-10 (76.05), and misclassification detection (94.34), with the advantage over baselines growing with the number of classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Evidential Deep Learning (EDL) by Sensoy et al. (2018), which models predictive uncertainty via Dirichlet distributions trained with losses like UCE, Brier Score, or T2ML. Prior EDL work has focused almost exclusively on probabilistic refinements—designing priors, imposing Fisher information or entropy constraints—while treating the loss function as a black box. This paper departs by introducing an optimization-theoretic lens, establishing that UCE loss implicitly performs Crammer-Singer multi-class SVM margin maximization, and proposing the optimization-consistency principle as a new criterion for selecting uncertainty measures. It also connects to the broader literature on margin-based classification losses and the known overconfidence issues of UCE reported in subsequent EDL studies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The optimization-consistency principle offers a principled, loss-specific criterion for choosing uncertainty measures, replacing the current practice of defaulting to VoE without justification. The finding that VoE—the standard measure in the original EDL paper—violates this principle while MPU satisfies it explains the known overconfidence pathology of UCE+VoE and provides a concrete fix. However, the approach is tightly coupled to UCE loss; the authors acknowledge that on CIFAR-10, UCE+MPU underperforms Re-EDL with Brier Score, leaving open whether analogous margin-aware measures can be derived for other EDL losses. Broader questions remain about whether optimization-consistency generalizes beyond Dirichlet-based EDL to other evidential or Bayesian frameworks.
