## Problem

Temporal-difference methods suffer from bootstrapping bias where value estimation errors compound across steps, making long-horizon tasks particularly difficult. While chunked critics (Q-chunking) alleviate this by performing multi-step value backup, extracting policies from them is challenging: the policy must output the entire action chunk open-loop, which sacrifices reactivity and becomes intractable as chunk length grows. The paper also notes that no formal theoretical analysis of Q-learning with action chunking critics existed prior to this work.

## Input / Output

The method takes the current environment state (concatenated with a goal specification in goal-conditioned settings) as input and produces a partial action chunk of size h_a (e.g., 1 or 5 actions) as output, which is executed closed-loop before re-observing the state. The full chunked critic internally operates on action chunks of size h (e.g., 25), but the policy never needs to predict the full chunk. Policy extraction is performed at test-time via best-of-N sampling (N=32) from a behavior-cloning flow policy, selecting the partial chunk that maximizes the distilled critic value.

## Method

DQC decouples the critic chunk size h from the policy chunk size h_a (h_a << h) by training a distilled partial critic Q_ψ^P(s_t, a_{t:t+h_a}) that approximates the maximum value achievable when a partial action chunk is optimally extended to a full chunk. This distilled critic is trained via expectile regression against the full chunked critic Q_φ(s_t, a_{t:t+h}), which itself is trained with h-step TD backup using an implicit value target V_ξ(s_t) learned via quantile regression. At test-time, the policy is extracted by best-of-N sampling from a frozen behavior-cloning flow policy, selecting the partial action chunk that maximizes Q_ψ^P. Theoretically, the paper introduces the open-loop consistency condition and proves that under strong open-loop consistency, action chunking Q-learning converges to a near-optimal policy with sub-optimality gap bounded by O(ε_h · H · H̄), and that closed-loop execution of the chunking policy further reduces the gap under bounded optimality variability.

## Results

On OGBench, DQC achieves success rates of 98%, 92%, 34%, 92%, 96%, and 83% on cube-triple, cube-quadruple, cube-octuple, humanoidmaze-giant, puzzle-4x5, and puzzle-4x6 respectively. DQC outperforms the previous state-of-the-art SHARSA on 5 out of 6 environments (e.g., 98% vs 83% on cube-triple, 96% vs 1% on puzzle-4x5) and substantially outperforms Q-chunking (QC) across all environments (e.g., 92% vs 35% on cube-quadruple, 96% vs 20% on puzzle-4x5). DQC also beats n-step return backup (NS) on 4 of 6 environments, with NS only winning on humanoidmaze-giant (95% vs 92%) and puzzle-4x6 (91% vs 83%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DQC outperforms SHARSA (Park et al., 2025b), the previous state-of-the-art on OGBench, on 5 of 6 environments with large margins (e.g., +15 pts on cube-triple, +28 pts on cube-quadruple, +73 pts on puzzle-4x5), tying on cube-octuple (34% each). Against Q-chunking (QC, Li et al., 2025b), DQC wins decisively on all six environments, demonstrating that decoupling the policy chunk size from the critic chunk size is critical—QC struggles when forced to predict full action chunks open-loop (e.g., 20% vs 98% on cube-triple). DQC also beats n-step return backup (NS) on 4 of 6 environments, confirming that the chunked critic provides value-learning benefits beyond what a single-step critic with multi-step returns can achieve; NS only edges out DQC on humanoidmaze-giant (95% vs 92%) and puzzle-4x6 (91% vs 83%). The naive decoupling baseline (DQC-naive, which predicts full chunks but only executes a partial one) performs far worse than DQC on most tasks, validating the need for the distilled partial critic. IQL and HIQL, which do not use multi-step or chunked backups, are consistently outperformed, with IQL achieving at most 66% (cube-triple) and HIQL at most 24% (humanoidmaze).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the action chunking Q-learning line (Li et al., 2025b) by identifying a critical gap: prior chunked critics require the policy to predict the full action chunk open-loop, which is both hard to learn and suboptimal for reactive environments. The key departure is decoupling the critic chunk size (h) from the policy chunk size (h_a), using a distilled partial critic trained via implicit maximization (building on IQL's expectile/quantile regression) to bridge the two. The algorithmic framework draws on IDQL's best-of-N sampling with a flow-matching behavior policy, while the theoretical contribution—formalizing the open-loop consistency condition and characterizing value bias under it—is the first rigorous analysis of Q-learning with chunked critics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical foundation for action chunking Q-learning, identifying the open-loop consistency condition as the key requirement for near-optimality and showing that weak consistency alone is insufficient (arbitrarily bad policies can result). Practically, DQC achieves large gains over prior SOTA (SHARSA) and especially over Q-chunking on hard long-horizon tasks, suggesting the policy learning bottleneck for full action chunks was a major underappreciated limitation. Open questions include state-dependent chunk sizes, extending guarantees to arbitrary off-policy data beyond the strong OLC assumption, and whether the decoupling principle generalizes to other temporally-extended action formulations (options, skills).
