## Problem

Existing structural causal bandit (SCB) algorithms restrict the agent's action space to observational (L1) and interventional (L2) quantities, excluding counterfactual (L3) actions despite their potential to yield superior rewards. This paper asks whether realizable counterfactual regimes can be systematically incorporated into the bandit action space and, if so, how to prune the resulting exponentially larger space without sacrificing optimality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a semi-Markovian causal diagram G (with directed and bidirected edges) and a designated reward variable Y; the agent knows G and Y but not the SCM's functional mechanisms F or exogenous distribution P(U). Actions are counterfactual variable sets X_* = {X_{i[w_i]}} rather than simple do(X=x) interventions, and the agent must physically execute counterfactual randomization to sample rewards Y_{X_*}. The output is a refined action space of representative CTF-POMISs (each covering dozens of equivalent CTF-(PO)MISs), which is then fed to standard solvers (Thompson Sampling or KL-UCB) to minimize cumulative regret over T rounds.

## Method

The paper introduces three layered contributions. First, it defines Counterfactual Minimal Intervention Sets (CTF-MIS) with a complete graphical characterization (Thm. 1): a counterfactual set is minimal iff every variable has a directed path to Y in the intervened subgraph AND each variable's subscript intersects its ancestors in the relevant subgraph, eliminating redundant actions via CTF-calculus rules. Second, it introduces Counterfactual Possibly-Optimal MIS (CTF-POMIS) characterized by a novel counterfactual regime graph H_{X_*} that preserves causal relations among counterfactual variables; a CTF-MIS is CTF-POMIS iff the interventional border IB(H_{X_*}, Y) is empty (Thm. 2). Third, Algorithm 1 enumerates all representative CTF-POMISs via an edge-selection approach running in O(n² · 2^|E|), exploiting the key insight that each representative counterfactual (of the form {X_{i[Pa(X_i)]}}) covers an entire equivalence class of CTF-(PO)MISs sharing the same effect propagation pattern, thereby avoiding the super-exponential enumeration of all realizable counterfactuals.

## Results

Across three synthetic tasks, CTF-POMIS-based agents achieve substantially lower cumulative regret (CR) than CTF-MIS-based agents: in Task 1, CTF-POMIS + TS reaches CR = 66.69 (≈39% of CTF-MIS CR), and CTF-POMIS + KL-UCB reaches CR = 148.19 (≈39% of CTF-MIS CR). In Task 2, CTF-POMIS + TS achieves CR = 387.01 (≈57% of CTF-MIS CR) and CTF-POMIS + KL-UCB achieves CR = 831.24 (≈70% of CTF-MIS CR). In Task 3, when the optimal action lies outside the POMIS set, POMIS-based agents suffer linear regret, while CTF-POMIS agents maintain sublinear regret; when the optimal action is in L≤2, POMIS converges faster due to its smaller action space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three arm-selection strategies—CTF-POMIS, CTF-MIS, and POMIS (from Lee & Bareinboim, NeurIPS 2018)—each paired with Thompson Sampling (TS) and KL-UCB. CTF-POMIS consistently dominates CTF-MIS in cumulative regret, reducing it to roughly 39–57% (TS) and 39–70% (KL-UCB) across Tasks 1 and 2, confirming that pruning to possibly-optimal counterfactual actions yields substantial regret savings. Against the original POMIS baseline, CTF-POMIS ultimately prevails in Tasks 1 and 2: although POMIS may temporarily show lower CR due to its smaller action space (fewer arms to explore), CTF-POMIS achieves strictly lower asymptotic regret because it includes counterfactual arms that can be optimal. In Task 3 (left), where the true optimal action is a counterfactual not contained in any POMIS, the POMIS baseline suffers linear regret (a strictly positive gap Δ_{L≤2} > 0), while CTF-POMIS maintains sublinear regret. In Task 3 (right), where the optimal action happens to lie in L≤2, POMIS converges faster simply because it has fewer arms, but this is a special case that does not undermine the theoretical guarantees since the agent cannot know a priori whether the optimum is in L≤2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Structural Causal Bandit (SCB) framework of Lee and Bareinboim (2018) from L2-level interventions to L3-level counterfactual actions, introducing the CTF-SCB problem. It repurposes the MUCT/IB graphical machinery from SCB by applying it to a novel 'counterfactual regime graph' that preserves causal relations among counterfactual variables, and leverages the CTF-calculus (Correa and Bareinboim, 2025) and the Counterfactual Unnesting Theorem (Correa et al., 2021) to derive equivalence relations among counterfactual arms. The realizability criterion from Raghavan and Bareinboim (2025) determines which counterfactual events constitute valid actions, bridging the gap between the theoretical L3 layer and physically executable bandit arms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens a new research direction at the intersection of causal inference, decision theory, and online learning by showing that counterfactual actions can strictly dominate all L2 interventions, challenging the long-held assumption that L3 quantities are irrelevant to sequential decision-making. Key limitations include the requirement of a known causal diagram, the assumption that the agent can physically execute counterfactual actions (requiring suitable counterfactual mediators), and the restriction to static SCMs. Open questions include extending the framework to unknown or partially known graphs, dynamic environments, and practical protocols for implementing counterfactual randomization in real-world settings.
