## Problem

Continuous (soft) tokens for Chain-of-Thought reasoning offer theoretical expressivity advantages—encoding superpositions of reasoning paths—but practical training has been severely limited: prior methods either apply continuous tokens only at inference on discrete-trained models, or require distilling from ground-truth discrete CoTs with backpropagation through time that caps the CoT at ~6 tokens. No scalable method existed to learn continuous CoTs via reinforcement learning without reference discrete CoT annotations.

## Input / Output

The model receives a text prompt containing a math problem and produces a two-phase output: a chain-of-thought phase of up to 128–512 continuous (noisy embedding) tokens, followed by a final answer of up to 32 discrete tokens in \boxed{} format. During the CoT phase, instead of sampling a discrete token, the model feeds the full softmax probability distribution (at temperature τ=0.5 for soft or τ=0.0001 for fuzzy) as a weighted mixture of token embeddings plus Gaussian noise into the next transformer step. The final answer is always decoded as standard discrete tokens, and a greedy shadow sequence (argmax at each step) is monitored for the stopping phrase 'The final answer is:'.

## Method

The core mechanism is injecting Gaussian noise (σ·N(0,Id), with σ = 0.33× the RMS norm of token embeddings) into the probability-weighted embedding mixture h_t^0 = p_{t-1}E at each CoT step, converting the otherwise deterministic soft-token process into a stochastic policy amenable to RL. This makes the per-step log-probability tractable: log π(h̃_t^0 | h̃_{<t}^0) = −(1/2σ²)‖h̃_t^0 − h_t^0‖² + const, enabling standard Reinforce-family algorithms (they use RLOO with G=32 samples per prompt and B=2 prompts per update). The computational overhead is minimal—only storing the V-dimensional probability vector and adding noise at the input embedding layer—allowing CoTs of hundreds of tokens versus the 6-token limit of prior distillation-based approaches. They distinguish 'soft' tokens (τ=0.5, genuinely continuous mixtures) from 'fuzzy' tokens (τ=0.0001, near-discrete embeddings plus noise), and find that training with either then deploying with standard hard-token greedy inference yields the best pass@1 while preserving pass@32 diversity and out-of-domain robustness.

## Results

On mathematical reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH-500, OlympiadBench) with Llama-3.2-3B, Llama-3.1-8B, and Qwen-2.5-3B Instruct models, continuous CoT training (soft/fuzzy tokens) matches discrete hard-token CoT training on greedy pass@1 (e.g., 77.2% vs 75.9% on GSM8K for Llama-3B) while consistently surpassing it on pass@32 (e.g., 97.9% vs 94.1% on GSM8K for Llama-3B), indicating greater reasoning diversity. A striking exception is Llama-8B-Instruct trained on GSM8K and evaluated on MATH-500, where hard training collapses to 20.2% greedy pass@1 while fuzzy/soft training recovers to 44.6–44.7%. On out-of-domain benchmarks (HellaSwag, ARC, MMLU), soft/fuzzy training preserves the base model's negative log-likelihood of correct answers, whereas hard training degrades it (e.g., ARC NLL: 2.88–2.92 for fuzzy/soft vs 3.16–3.19 for hard on Llama-3B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three training paradigms—hard (standard discrete CoT), soft (probability-mixture embeddings with Gaussian noise, τ=0.5), and fuzzy (near-hard embeddings with noise, τ=0.0001)—all trained via RLOO reinforcement learning, against the un-fine-tuned base model. On pass@1, soft and fuzzy training are broadly competitive with hard training across most model-dataset combinations, with a notable exception where Llama-3B on MATH shows hard outperforming soft by ~5%. On pass@32, soft/fuzzy training consistently outperforms hard training (e.g., 97.9% vs 94.1% on GSM8K for Llama-3B; 98.3% vs 95.1% for Llama-8B), demonstrating preserved reasoning diversity. The most dramatic comparison is Llama-8B-Instruct trained on GSM8K and tested on MATH-500: hard training collapses to 20.2% greedy pass@1 (vs 44.4% base), while fuzzy/soft training recovers to 44.6–44.7%, matching the base model. On out-of-domain robustness (HellaSwag, ARC, MMLU), soft/fuzzy training preserves the base model's NLL of correct answers (e.g., ARC NLL 2.88–2.92) while hard training degrades it (3.16–3.19), indicating a 'softer touch' on general capabilities. For inference, hard (discrete) decoding consistently outperforms soft/fuzzy inference across all training methods, meaning the best deployment strategy is soft/fuzzy training followed by standard hard inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous-token reasoning line initiated by Coconut (Hao et al., 2024) and Soft Thinking (Zhang et al., 2025), both of which replace discrete CoT tokens with probability-weighted mixtures of token embeddings. The key departure is making these continuous tokens amenable to RL training: prior work either applied continuous tokens only at inference time on discrete-trained models, or required distillation from ground-truth discrete CoTs with BPTT through the full CoT (limiting Coconut to 6 tokens). By injecting Gaussian noise into the soft token embeddings, the authors obtain the stochasticity needed for RLOO-style policy optimization, eliminating both the distillation requirement and the BPTT computational bottleneck.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that continuous CoT RL training can be deployed with standard discrete-token inference, removing the need for non-standard decoding at serving time. The finding that soft/fuzzy training better preserves base-model entropy profiles and out-of-domain NLL suggests a path to RL fine-tuning that avoids the overconfidence and capability degradation commonly observed with hard-token RL. Open questions include whether the benefits persist at larger model scales (beyond 8B), whether the noise mechanism generalizes to non-mathematical reasoning tasks, and whether the pass@32 diversity gains translate to downstream applications like self-consistency or best-of-n selection at scale.
