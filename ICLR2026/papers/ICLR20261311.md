## Problem

Standard attribution evaluation metrics (Insertion/Deletion, Infidelity, Sensitivity-N) construct perturbed inputs by zeroing pixels or adding noise, which pushes samples far outside the data distribution and introduces spurious semantic cues (e.g., black regions that reinforce certain classes). This OOD distortion makes it impossible to disentangle genuine attribution failure from model instability on unrealistic inputs, undermining the reliability of faithfulness rankings across attribution methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261311/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

FUD takes a 256×256 RGB image, a pixel-level attribution map from any explainer, and a binary deletion mask specifying the fraction of low-attribution pixels to remove (swept from 10% to 90% in 10% steps). It outputs a sequence of in-distribution evaluation images generated by a pretrained unconditional diffusion model (256-channel U-Net with classifier guidance at scale 4.0), from which a single FUD faithfulness score and a deletion confidence curve are derived. The classifier under evaluation (ResNet-50 or ViT-B/16, both ImageNet-pretrained and frozen) is queried on each reconstructed sample to track confidence decay.

## Method

FUD formulates attribution evaluation as a conditional sampling problem: given a hard mask M that preserves high-attribution pixels, it samples from P(x^t | z, x̃, M) ∝ P(x^t) · P(z|x^t) · P(x̃|x^t, M), where the three factors correspond to (i) a learned score-based image prior s_θ(x^t) that pulls samples back onto the data manifold, (ii) a classifier-gradient term −∇_{x^t} log P(y|x^t) that suppresses hallucinated class-supporting evidence in the inpainted region, and (iii) a Dirac-delta likelihood that fixes unmasked pixels to their original values. The classifier gradient is deliberately delayed until the last 5% of DDIM steps (t < 50 out of 1000) because gradients on off-manifold samples are unreliable; before that threshold, only the score prior and mask constraint drive the trajectory. In practice, the Dirac constraint is implemented as a hard overwrite of masked-out pixels after each diffusion step rather than an infinite gradient, and a hard (binary) mask is preferred over a soft Gaussian constraint, which degrades PSNR from 34.0 to 27.6 dB. The entire pipeline requires no retraining of the target classifier—only a pretrained unconditional diffusion model and the frozen classifier's input gradients.

## Results

FUD produces intermediate evaluation samples that are significantly harder to detect as out-of-distribution: on ResNet-50, the Energy-based OOD detector achieves AUROC of only 0.6863 on FUD samples versus 0.8974 for INS/DEL and 0.8773 for Sen-N (closer to 0.5 = random guessing = more in-distribution). On image quality, FUD achieves PSNR of 25.20 dB and SSIM of 0.75, substantially outperforming INS/DEL (10.49 dB / 0.27), Sen-N (13.63 / 0.13), and INFID (16.64 / 0.22). For evaluation smoothness (Kendall's τ), FUD yields τ > 0.8 for most of the 11 attribution methods on ResNet-50, whereas INS/DEL often drops below 0.6 (as low as 0.21 for gradient-based methods like IG and GIG).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FUD outperforms all seven compared evaluation protocols on both OOD-detection and image-quality metrics. Against the three classical baselines (INS/DEL, Sen-N, INFID), FUD reduces OOD-detector AUROC by 0.19–0.21 on ResNet-50 and 0.23–0.29 on ViT-B/16, while boosting PSNR by +8.6 dB over the next-best (INFID) and +14.7 dB over INS/DEL. Against the four more recent OOD-aware methods (IDSDS, ROAD, Distill, Gevaert et al.), FUD still achieves the lowest AUROC (0.6863 vs. 0.7123–0.8226 on ResNet-50; 0.6450 vs. 0.6675–0.8329 on ViT-B/16) and the highest PSNR (25.20 vs. 10.59–23.83). The closest competitor is Gevaert et al. (2024), which reaches PSNR 23.83 and AUROC 0.6675 on ViT-B/16, but FUD still leads on every metric. On smoothness (Kendall's τ), FUD consistently produces more monotonic confidence-decay curves than INS/DEL across all 11 attribution methods, with the largest gains for gradient-based explainers (e.g., IG improves from τ=0.22 to 0.69 on ResNet-50).

## Relation to Prior Work

FUD repurposes score-based generative modeling (Song et al., 2020; Ho et al., 2020) as an evaluation mechanism rather than a generative one: the learned score function ∇ₓ log P(x) is used to pull zero-masked or noise-masked samples back onto the data manifold, while a classifier-gradient term (−∇ₓ log P(y|x)) enforces a 'no new class evidence' constraint. This builds on earlier generative in-filling approaches (Chang et al., 2019; Agarwal & Nguyen, 2020) that also used learned models to replace removed pixels, but departs from them by formally deriving a joint posterior that combines the image prior, the classifier correction, and a hard Dirac masking constraint into a single Langevin update rule. The paper positions itself against the dominant perturbation-based AEMs (Insertion/Deletion, Infidelity, Sensitivity-N) by arguing their core operation—setting pixels to zero—systematically produces OOD inputs, a claim validated with Energy-based OOD detection (Liu et al., 2020) and seven perceptual quality metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FUD challenges a foundational assumption in attribution evaluation—that zeroing or blurring pixels is a neutral way to 'remove' a feature—by showing quantitatively that such perturbations push samples far off the data manifold (AUROC ≈ 0.90 for INS/DEL vs. ≈ 0.69 for FUD) and produce non-monotonic, unreliable confidence curves. This reorders the ranking of attribution methods (e.g., IG and GIG improve from τ ≈ 0.22 to ≈ 0.69 under FUD), suggesting that some methods previously deemed unfaithful may have been penalized by evaluation artefacts rather than genuine explanation quality. Key limitations include the computational overhead of diffusion sampling (~4 s/image at 100 DDIM steps), the need for resolution alignment with the pretrained score model, and reliance on an unconditional ImageNet diffusion prior that may not transfer cleanly to other domains. Open questions include whether task-specific score functions or faster samplers (DPM-Solver, DDIM variants) can close the efficiency gap, and whether the model-centric OOD definition (in-distribution relative to the classifier's learned manifold, not human perception) is the right criterion for all evaluation settings.
