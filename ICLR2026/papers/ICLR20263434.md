## Problem

Vanilla gradient ascent (GA) for backdoor unlearning does not eliminate the trigger's influence but shifts it to other classes—a phenomenon the authors call trigger shifting. Standard evaluation metrics (clean accuracy, label flipping ratio) fail to capture this failure mode, masking the fact that the 'unlearned' model develops a new backdoor on previously unaffected classes. The paper provides a theoretical explanation for why this occurs and proposes a fix.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pre-trained text classifier (BERT, DistilBERT, Llama2-7B, or Qwen3-8B) that has been fine-tuned on a poisoned dataset. It consumes three inputs: the poisoned model parameters, a set of detected poisoned samples (identified via CUBE clustering on the poisoned model's embeddings), and the clean training subset. The output is a cleaned model whose parameters are updated over 30 unlearning epochs (α=2, β=0.05), evaluated on both clean and trigger-injected test sets to verify that no new trigger-induced misclassification has emerged.

## Method

RGA replaces the fixed-strength GA objective with a dynamically weighted one: the GA coefficient λ = exp(−α · KL(f_θc*(yp|xp) ∥ f_θp(yp|xp))) decays exponentially as the current model's prediction distribution on poisoned samples diverges from the original poisoned model's distribution, effectively switching off gradient ascent once the backdoor association is neutralized. This is combined with a standard clean-data retention loss and an L2 regularization term anchoring the updated parameters to the base pretrained model. The theoretical contribution (Propositions 1–2) shows that in binary classification, maximizing the cross-entropy loss on the target class is algebraically equivalent to minimizing it on the opposite class (up to a bounded residual), which is the root cause of trigger shifting; the multiclass extension shows the same redistribution effect across K−1 remaining classes. The exponential-KL schedule is chosen over linear decay because it responds directly to the model's actual deviation from the poisoned state rather than a fixed step count, and it requires only forward-pass probabilities, keeping computational cost comparable to vanilla GA.

## Results

On BERT-base across SST-2, HSOL, and AG-News, RGA achieves ΔPACC (gap to ReTrain's PACC) of 1.04–4.55, compared to GA's 15.03–41.12 and NPO's 12.63–38.97, while maintaining CACC within ~1–2% of ReTrain. On Llama2-7B SST-2, RGA reduces ΔPACC to 2.71 (AddSent) and 5.20 (BadNets) versus GA's 43.86 and 26.10, though HiddenKiller remains challenging (ΔPACC=20.39). RGA's unlearning time per epoch (~38–52 s) is comparable to GA (~32–44 s) and NPO (~41–63 s), and roughly 1/10 of full retraining (~335–477 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RGA decisively outperforms vanilla GA on the key trigger-shifting metric ΔPACC across nearly all settings: on BERT SST-2, RGA's ΔPACC ranges from 1.04 to 4.55 versus GA's 15.03 to 41.12, meaning RGA's PACC is within ~2 points of the ReTrain gold standard while GA's PACC collapses to ~50% (chance level). Against NPO, RGA wins on most configurations (e.g., BERT AddSent: ΔPACC 4.55 vs. 38.97; Llama2 AddSent: 2.71 vs. 35.96), but NPO edges out RGA on Llama2 SST-2 BadNets (ΔPACC 1.10 vs. 5.20) and HiddenKiller (12.10 vs. 20.39), suggesting that syntactic triggers on large models remain a hard case for both methods. In terms of clean utility (CACC), RGA stays within 1–3% of ReTrain in most rows, while GA often degrades CACC by 1–2 points and NPO occasionally overfits (e.g., NPO CACC=96.66 vs. ReTrain 96.14 on Llama2 HiddenKiller). Overall, RGA is the only method that consistently keeps ΔPACC below 5 across BERT and DistilBERT for all three attacks, confirming that the dynamic KL-based penalty effectively prevents trigger shifting without sacrificing model utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient-ascent (GA) unlearning paradigm for backdoor removal (Li et al., 2021; Shen et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2022) by identifying a fundamental flaw—trigger shifting—that prior work overlooked. It also positions itself against NPO (Zhang et al., 2024b), which addresses catastrophic collapse from GA but still permits unbounded loss growth. The key conceptual departure is the theoretical proof (Propositions 1–2) showing that GA on the poisoned class is mathematically equivalent to gradient descent on the complementary class, thereby re-associating the trigger with a new label rather than neutralizing it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that GA-based unlearning relocates rather than eliminates backdoors undermines a widely adopted defense strategy and reveals that standard evaluation metrics (CACC, LFR) systematically underreport the risk. The proposed PACC and ΔPACC metrics, combined with the KL-divergence-based dynamic penalty in RGA, offer a practical and low-overhead fix that preserves model utility while preventing trigger shifting across architectures and scales. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to generative or non-classification tasks, how imperfect poisoned-sample detection (e.g., CUBE's lower recall on HSOL with Llama2) interacts with the unlearning dynamics, and whether the exponential-KL schedule is optimal compared to other adaptive decay strategies.
