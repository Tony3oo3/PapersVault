## Problem

CLIP suffers significant performance degradation in multi-label classification because its contrastive pretraining focuses on the most prominent object and its discriminative prompts at inference introduce no co-occurrence information, causing it to miss co-occurring labels. The paper asks whether and how label co-occurrence can be explicitly injected into CLIP's inference to improve multi-label recognition without any training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB test image (224×224 for ResNet-101/ViT-B/16, or original resolution when combined with TagCLIP) and outputs per-label probabilities for all q classes, from which multi-label predictions are derived. No model parameters are updated; the only optional external input is a tiny subset of training data (1–2%) used solely to estimate label co-occurrence probabilities, or alternatively co-occurring labels generated by ChatGPT-4o. The method is fully training-free and backbone-agnostic.

## Method

DualPrompt rewrites the standard CLIP prompt into two parallel branches: a Discriminative Prompt (DiP, 'A photo of a {label}') that activates discriminative features of the target object, and a Correlative Prompt (CoP, 'A photo of a {label} often includes a {label_1}, …, {label_m}') that activates correlative features encoding co-occurrence patterns. A causal-inference analysis shows that CoP alone causes object hallucination because the path from co-occurring labels to the prediction remains active even when the target object is absent. The paper derives that the Total Direct Effect (TDE) calibration—subtracting the indirect co-occurrence effect—is algebraically equivalent to simply summing the softmax probabilities from both prompts, yielding the final prediction p(y_k=1|x, P_k^c) + p(y_k=1|x, P_k^d). This keeps the positive effect of co-occurrence (recognizing inconspicuous objects via correlative patterns) while removing the negative effect (hallucinating the target from its co-occurring neighbors alone).

## Results

On MS-COCO (80 classes), DualPrompt with ViT-B/16 and no training data achieves 67.7 mAP / 63.6 F1, and with 1% data for co-occurrence estimation reaches 69.4 mAP / 65.0 F1. Combining with TagCLIP yields the best result of 70.0 mAP / 66.1 F1. On the more challenging VG-256 (256 classes), DualPrompt+TagCLIP with 2% co-occurrence estimation achieves 40.7 mAP / 42.7 F1, and on Objects365 (365 classes) it reaches 37.9 mAP / 31.9 F1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualPrompt consistently outperforms all baselines across both backbones and all three datasets. Against vanilla CLIP (ViT-B/16, no training), DualPrompt gains +2.8 mAP (67.7 vs 64.9) with zero training data and +4.5 mAP (69.4 vs 64.9) with 1% co-occurrence estimation. Against the training-based prompt-tuning methods DualCoOp and TaICLIP (both using 1% training data), DualPrompt (ViT-B/16, no training) achieves 67.7 mAP, surpassing DualCoOp by +12.6 mAP and TaICLIP by +4.1 mAP, demonstrating that the training-free approach is substantially more data-efficient. Against the strongest training-free baseline TagCLIP (68.7 mAP), DualPrompt alone (67.7 mAP) is slightly behind, but DualPrompt+TagCLIP closes the gap to 69.2 mAP (+0.5) and reaches 70.0 mAP (+1.3) with 1% co-occurrence estimation, establishing the new state of the art on MS-COCO. The same pattern holds on VG-256 and Objects365, where DualPrompt+TagCLIP achieves the best mAP in every setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CLIP's zero-shot inference pipeline, departing from prior multi-label approaches that modify the image encoder (TagCLIP's ViT patch features) or require prompt fine-tuning (DualCoOp, TaICLIP). Its key conceptual departure is shifting the intervention to the text prompt side: rewriting the discriminative prompt into a correlative prompt that embeds co-occurring labels, then combining both via a total-direct-effect (TDE) calibration borrowed from causal inference (Pearl, 2001; Xie et al., 2024). The method is explicitly complementary to TagCLIP rather than a replacement, and it positions co-occurrence as a first-class signal that CLIP's contrastive pretraining and single-label prompts systematically ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, backbone-agnostic nature of DualPrompt makes it a practical drop-in improvement for CLIP-based multi-label pipelines, and the causal-inference framing gives a clean theoretical account of why naive co-occurrence injection causes object hallucination. A notable limitation is the dependence on co-occurrence labels (from ChatGPT or a tiny annotated subset), which introduces a dataset-specific prior that may not transfer well to open-vocabulary or highly novel domains. Open questions include whether the dual-prompt idea extends to other VLMs beyond CLIP, how it behaves at much larger label spaces (thousands of classes), and whether the TDE calibration can be made fully parameter-free without the ad-hoc λ=1 choice.
