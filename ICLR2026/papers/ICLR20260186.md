## Problem

Reliable computer-use agents require accurate grounding of natural language instructions to specific on-screen elements, but high-quality desktop grounding datasets are scarce. Existing desktop resources rely on automated accessibility-tree traversal or synthetic interface generation, producing incomplete, imprecise, or low-density annotations that fail to capture the fine-grained, high-resolution, icon-heavy UIs of real desktop software. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a large-scale, expert-human-annotated desktop dataset and demonstrating that data quality can substitute for raw scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260186/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a high-resolution desktop screenshot (0.39–7.0 MP, mean 2.03 MP) paired with a natural language instruction of one of three types: direct (referencing element attributes or text), functional (describing the intended action, e.g., 'Open a new tab'), or spatial (specifying relative position to a named anchor element). It outputs a single 2D point (û, v̂) in image pixel coordinates; the prediction is correct if the point falls inside the ground-truth axis-aligned bounding box. Bounding boxes are extremely small on average (0.13% of image area), and screenshots contain up to 542 annotated elements, making precise localization the central challenge.

## Method

GROUNDCUA is built from over 10,000 task demonstrations by trained annotators across 87 open-source desktop applications, with every visible UI element in each keyframe densely annotated (bounding box, label, category, OCR text), yielding 3.56M+ annotations across 56K screenshots. From these annotations, a multimodal LLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) generates diverse instruction–screenshot pairs in three types (Direct, Functional, Spatial), producing a 700K-sample SFT set and a 10K-sample RL set. GROUNDNEXT models (Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct at 3B and 7B) are trained in two stages: supervised fine-tuning on the 700K instructions (full model, 2 epochs, 8×H100), followed by RL post-training using RLOO with a custom 6-level discrete reward based on normalized signed distance to the target bounding box, which explicitly penalizes near-misses more mildly than far misses and encourages interior predictions toward the box center. The key empirical finding is that 700K high-quality, densely grounded instructions outperform 9M-sample corpora (e.g., JEDI) on five benchmarks, and the RL stage yields modest but consistent gains because SFT with GROUNDCUA already captures most of the achievable performance.

## Results

GROUNDNEXT-3B (RL) achieves a 68.4% average across five grounding benchmarks (ScreenSpot-Pro, OSWorld-G, MMBench-GUI, ScreenSpot-v2, UI-Vision), outperforming the next-best 3B model (InfiGUI-G1-3B at 56.3%) by 12.1 points. GROUNDNEXT-7B (RL) reaches 70.5% average, surpassing the strongest 7B competitor (GTA1-7B at 63.1%) by 7.4 points. In the agentic OSWorld-Verified setting with o3 as planner, GROUNDNEXT-3B attains 50.6% overall task success—comparable to the much larger JEDI-7B (51.0%) and exceeding OpenCUA-72B (46.1%) and proprietary APIs like Qwen3-VL-Flash (41.6%) and Claude-4-Sonnet (41.4%). All results are achieved with only 700K SFT samples and 10K RL samples, far less than the 9M datapoints used by JEDI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GROUNDNEXT dominates at both 3B and 7B scales. At 3B, GROUNDNEXT-3B (RL) leads all RL-tuned baselines by a wide margin: +12.1 avg over InfiGUI-G1-3B (56.3), +14.9 over GUI-G1-3B (53.5), and +20.4 over UI-R1-E-3B (48.0). At 7B, GROUNDNEXT-7B (RL) outperforms GTA1-7B (63.1) by 7.4 points, InfiGUI-G1-7B (62.4) by 8.1 points, and UI-TARS-1.5-7B (57.8) by 12.7 points. Notably, even the SFT-only GROUNDNEXT-3B (66.4 avg) surpasses all RL-tuned 3B baselines, underscoring the data-quality advantage. On the agentic OSWorld-Verified benchmark, GROUNDNEXT-3B (50.6) nearly matches JEDI-7B (51.0) despite being less than half the parameter count, and exceeds OpenCUA-72B (46.1) and all proprietary APIs except Claude-4.5-Sonnet (62.9). The largest single-benchmark gap is on UI-Vision (in-domain), where GROUNDNEXT-3B scores 62.1 vs. 22.0 for InfiGUI-G1-3B, reflecting the benefit of training on expert-annotated desktop data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the dominant scaling paradigm in GUI grounding, where prior work (JEDI with 9M samples, OS-Atlas with 14.5M, UGround with 9M) relies on large-scale automated or synthetic data collection. It departs from this by arguing that dense, human-verified annotations from expert demonstrations (700K SFT + 10K RL) can outperform orders-of-magnitude larger corpora. Methodologically, it builds on Qwen2.5-VL as the base VLM and adopts the RLOO policy optimization from Ahmadian et al. (2024) for RL post-training, but pairs it with a simple discrete distance-based reward rather than the more complex Gaussian or reward-model-based schemes used by concurrent RL-tuned GUI agents (GUI-G2, InfiGUI-G1). The instruction generation pipeline is also distinct: rather than relying on pretrained captioning models, it prompts a multimodal LLM with ground-truth bounding boxes, labels, and OCR to produce tightly grounded instructions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core finding—that 700K expert-annotated samples outperform 9M+ automated/synthetic samples by 12+ points on average—challenges the field's assumption that grounding performance scales primarily with data volume, and provides a concrete recipe (dense human annotation + diverse instruction types + simple RL) for data-efficient desktop grounding. The 3B model matching JEDI-7B and surpassing OpenCUA-72B in agentic OSWorld-Verified evaluation suggests practical viability for resource-constrained deployments. Open questions remain around cross-domain generalization (web/mobile performance lags desktop), whether more sophisticated reward signals could yield larger RL gains, and how the dataset's open-source application bias affects transfer to proprietary software. The released dataset and models are likely to become a standard resource for desktop grounding research.
