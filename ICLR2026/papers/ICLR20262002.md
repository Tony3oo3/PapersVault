## Problem

Diffusion models inevitably exhibit a modeling gap between their learned distribution and the true data distribution due to architectural and dataset limitations. Existing inference-time enhancement methods (e.g., Z-Sampling, Auto-Guidance) bridge this gap from a single fixed perspective with limited flexibility, and prior extrapolation-based approaches often introduce artifacts. The paper asks whether a unified, flexible framework can leverage diverse weak-to-strong model pairings to systematically close the strong-to-ideal gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262002/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

W2SD takes a text prompt, initial Gaussian noise, a designated strong model M^s, and a weak model M^w, and produces a generated image or video. The strong/weak pair is not restricted to different architectures: it can be defined by weight differences (fine-tuned vs. base, LoRA-scaled, MoE expert selection), condition differences (high vs. low CFG, refined vs. raw prompts), or pipeline differences (ControlNet/IP-Adapter vs. standard DDIM). The reflection operation is applied only during the last λ of T total denoising steps, so the method adds 2λ score evaluations on top of the standard T-step pipeline.

## Method

W2SD introduces a reflective operator M_inv^w(M^s(·)) that, at each reflection step, first denoises the latent x_t with the strong model and then inverts the result back to noise level t using the weak model, yielding a refined latent x̃_t. Theorem 1 shows this operation shifts x_t along the weak-to-strong score gap Δ1 = ∇log p^s − ∇log p^w, which under a relative bias consistency condition (bias ratio ≈ 2, formalized in Theorem 2) approximates the unattainable strong-to-ideal gap Δ2 = ∇log p^gt − ∇log p^s. Theorem 3 further proves that the Fisher Divergence between the ground-truth and W2SD-refined distributions is reduced by a factor of ε² relative to the strong model alone. The framework is entirely training-free, requires no additional parameters, and the improvements from different gap types (weight, condition, pipeline) are shown to be cumulative, with Juggernaut-XL + W2SD achieving up to 90% HPSv2 winning rate over the original model.

## Results

On Pick-a-Pic, W2SD with Juggernaut-XL (strong) and SDXL (weak) improves HPS v2 from 31.6412 to 32.0992, and combining weight gap with condition gap pushes HPS v2 to 32.9623 with a 90% winning rate over the original model. On ImageNet 50K with DiT-MoE-S, W2SD reduces FID from 15.1032 to 9.1001 and increases Inception Score from 45.44 to 55.53. On VBench for video generation, W2SD improves Subject Consistency from 96.08% to 97.14% and Aesthetic Quality from 57.71% to 59.37% over AnimateDiff with Juggernaut-XL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SD1.5 (Rombach et al., 2022), W2SD with DreamShaper as the strong model lifts HPS v2 from 24.96 to 30.49 and MPS from 46.87 to 53.13 on Pick-a-Pic, a substantial gain in human preference. Against SDXL (Podell et al., 2024), W2SD with Juggernaut-XL improves HPS v2 from 29.87 to 32.10 and MPS from 45.74 to 54.26; using only the guidance-scale condition gap (same SDXL weights), HPS v2 still rises from 29.87 to 31.20. Against DiT-MoE-S (Fei et al., 2024) on ImageNet 50K, W2SD reduces FID by nearly 40% (15.10 → 9.10) and boosts IS from 45.44 to 55.53, demonstrating that even a 71M-parameter model benefits significantly. Against AnimateDiff (Guo et al., 2024) on VBench, W2SD improves Subject Consistency (96.08% → 97.14%) and Aesthetic Quality (57.71% → 59.37%) but slightly degrades Motion Smoothness (96.88% → 96.87%), reflecting Juggernaut-XL's inherent trade-off versus SDXL in temporal coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

W2SD unifies and generalizes the inference-time reflection paradigm introduced by Z-Sampling (Bai et al., 2024) and the degraded-model guidance of Auto-Guidance (Karras et al., 2024), reframing both as specific instances of a broader weak-to-strong gap-bridging mechanism. Where prior methods are confined to a single perspective (guidance-scale gap or a from-scratch degraded model), W2SD decouples the 'strong' and 'weak' roles from any particular component, allowing weight differences, condition differences, and pipeline differences to all serve as the reflective signal. The theoretical contribution (Theorems 1–3) formalizes when the computable weak-to-strong gap approximates the unattainable strong-to-ideal gap via a polynomial bias-ratio condition, going beyond the heuristic justifications in earlier reflection-based samplers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers practitioners a plug-and-play, training-free recipe for improving diffusion sampling by pairing any two models with a directional capability gap, and the demonstrated cumulative stacking of weight, condition, and pipeline gaps (reaching 90% HPSv2 winning rate) suggests a composable path toward near-ideal generation without retraining. Key limitations include the need for two forward passes per reflection step (mitigated but not eliminated by the time-efficiency analysis), the requirement that the bias-ratio condition hold (with identified failure modes of model conflict and over-similarity), and the fact that the 'ideal model' remains undefined in practice—users must heuristically choose what 'strong' means. Open questions include whether the framework extends naturally to flow-matching or autoregressive generative models, and whether the optimal weak-to-strong pairing can be learned or discovered automatically rather than specified manually.
