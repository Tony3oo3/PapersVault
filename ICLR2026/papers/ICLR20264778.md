## Problem

Quantization and sparsification are individually approaching their compression limits for LLMs, and naively combining them fails because they impose conflicting requirements on weight distributions: quantization favors compact numerical ranges (motivating Hadamard rotation to suppress outliers), while pruning benefits from high variance to reveal sparse patterns. No existing training-free method can achieve aggressive joint compression such as W4A4KV4 with 50% sparsity without severe performance degradation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a Hadamard-rotated LLM weight matrix per linear layer, a Hessian approximation H = 2XX^T estimated from 128 calibration samples (WikiText2, sequence length 2048), a binary pruning mask (e.g., from WANDA), and a partitioning ratio α (default 50%). It produces sparse (50% unstructured or 2:4 semi-structured) INT4 quantized weights by adding a computed compensation ΔW^OBR to the unpruned elements before final quantization. The entire pipeline is training-free and operates layer-by-layer, requiring only a single calibration pass.

## Method

OBR formulates a second-order Hessian objective that minimizes downstream task loss perturbation from weight changes, then makes it tractable via row-wise decoupling (approximating the output-side curvature matrix G as Identity in the Kronecker-product Hessian, yielding C_out independent sub-problems). The key mechanism is group error compensation: weights are partitioned into a retain set R (robust to compression) and an eviction set E (suffering compression error), and a closed-form solution Δw_R* = −H_RR^{-1} H_RE e_E transfers the error from E into R by solving a linear system. Two sequential rounds are applied: first compensating pruning error (E₁ = pruned slots, R₁ = unpruned slots), then compensating quantization error (E₂ = first α fraction of R₁, R₂ = remainder). The Hessian thus acts as a structured 'bridge' that redistributes compression-induced distortions rather than treating them as noise, yielding an explainable closed-form solution without any gradient-based optimization.

## Results

On Llama2-7B with W4A4KV4 + 50% sparsity, OBR_GPTQ achieves 8.40 WikiText2 perplexity (vs. 5.47 FP16) and 53.45% average zero-shot accuracy, while OBR_RTN reaches 9.23 perplexity and 56.49% accuracy. On Llama2-70B, OBR_GPTQ attains 4.69 perplexity, narrowing the gap to the FP16 baseline (3.32) to just 1.37. The method delivers up to 4.72× speedup and 6.4× memory reduction over FP16-dense inference using INT4 2:4 sparse GEMM kernels on A100 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OBR decisively outperforms the naive combination of QuaRot + WANDA, which collapses to 5868.24 perplexity on Llama2-7B (vs. OBR_GPTQ's 8.40), confirming that quantization and pruning are fundamentally incompatible without error compensation. Against the strongest joint baseline SparseGPT+GPTQ, OBR_GPTQ wins on perplexity across all model sizes: 8.40 vs. 12.94 on Llama2-7B (3.54 lower), 7.06 vs. 7.89 on Llama2-13B, and 4.69 vs. 4.78 on Llama2-70B; OBR_RTN also beats SparseGPT+GPTQ on Llama2-7B (9.23 vs. 12.94). On zero-shot accuracy, OBR_RTN consistently edges out SparseGPT+GPTQ (e.g., 56.49% vs. 51.57% on Llama2-7B, 62.37% vs. 60.74% on Llama2-13B). Under SpinQuant rotation, OBR_RTN improves average accuracy by 7.69% over SparseGPT+GPTQ on Llama2-7B (53.11% vs. 45.42%). Under FlatQuant rotation, OBR narrows the perplexity gap to FP16 to just 1.4 on Llama2-7B (6.87 vs. 5.47), compared to a 2.93 gap with QuaRot. Against BitNet-2B-4T (a 1.58-bit model trained from scratch), OBR-compressed Qwen2.5-3B (W4A4KV4+50%) achieves comparable zero-shot accuracy (62.77% vs. 70.27%) with better WikiText2 perplexity (11.79 vs. 13.67), while being a post-training method applicable to any existing model.

## Relation to Prior Work

OBR builds on the Hessian-based post-training compression lineage (OBD → OBQ → SparseGPT/GPTQ) and the Hadamard-rotation quantization line (QuaRot, SpinQuant, FlatQuant), but departs from prior joint compression work (DJPQ, OBQ, JSQ) in two key ways. First, it identifies and explicitly addresses the distributional conflict between quantization (favoring compact weight ranges) and pruning (favoring high variance), which prior joint methods either ignored or handled via iterative optimization. Second, it replaces gradient-based or annealing-based joint optimization with a closed-form group error compensation derived from a row-wise decoupled Hessian, making the solution both tractable and interpretable. The pruning-then-quantization ordering follows the theoretical analysis of Harma et al. (2024), and the activation-aware pruning mask from WANDA is treated as a plug-in component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OBR is the first training-free method to achieve W4A4KV4 with 50% sparsity on LLMs, a compression level that was previously infeasible without catastrophic degradation, and it delivers 4.72× speedup and 6.4× memory reduction on hardware that already supports INT4 sparse GEMM (Ampere/Hopper). The group error compensation mechanism is general and applies to pruning-only and quantization-only settings as well, suggesting it could serve as a post-processing enhancement for existing compression pipelines. Key limitations include the row-wise decoupling approximation (which increases calibration time to ~36h for 70B models vs. ~2h for SparseGPT+GPTQ), the treatment of pruning masks and rotation matrices as fixed inputs, and a narrowing advantage at higher bit-widths where standalone methods have not yet hit their limits. Open questions include whether learnable rotations and masks co-optimized with OBR could further close the gap to FP16, and whether faster Hessian solvers can reduce the one-time compression cost.
