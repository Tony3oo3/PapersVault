## Problem

Effective exploration in RL requires identifying states where an agent has something to learn, but prior intrinsic-motivation methods either rely on explicit quasimetric distance learning with episodic memory (e.g., ETD) or on full state reconstruction via world models. The paper asks which representations should drive exploration and argues that temporal contrastive representations—sufficient statistics for any Q-function—can guide exploration more simply and effectively by rewarding states with unpredictable future outcomes, without reconstruction or episodic memory.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state vectors (e.g., 29-dim for Ant, 268-dim for Humanoid, 18-dim for Arm, 1345-dim one-hot for Craftax) and actions, along with a trajectory buffer of past rollouts. Future states are sampled from the buffer using geometric offsets Δ ~ GEOM(1−γ) rather than from a learned dynamics model, and the future state can optionally be restricted to specific state components (e.g., torso position) to incorporate prior knowledge. The output is a scalar intrinsic reward (the negative L1 or L2 distance between the state-action encoder output and the future-state encoder output) that is fed into PPO or SAC to update a stochastic policy.

## Method

C-TeC trains two encoders, φ_θ(s_t, a_t) and ψ_θ(s_f), via the InfoNCE loss where positive pairs are (s_t, a_t, s_f) sampled from the discounted future-state occupancy over the trajectory buffer and negatives are drawn from the product of marginals. The intrinsic reward is simply the negative contrastive similarity r_intr = ‖φ_θ(s_t,a_t) − ψ_θ(s_f)‖, which the policy maximizes via PPO or SAC. The key theoretical insight is that in the limit of perfect representations this reward equals the negative reverse KL divergence D_KL[p_T(s_f|s,a) ‖ p_T(s_f)], a mode-seeking objective that rewards state-actions whose conditional future distribution is concentrated on states the marginal has not yet covered—distinct from forward-KL mean-seeking or pure surprise maximization. Unlike ETD, C-TeC requires no quasimetric parametrization (MRN), no episodic memory, and no explicit temporal distance, making it compatible with both on-policy and off-policy algorithms while achieving comparable or superior state coverage across locomotion, manipulation, and open-world environments.

## Results

C-TeC achieves state-of-the-art state coverage across locomotion, manipulation, and open-world environments using only intrinsic rewards. After 500M environment steps, C-TeC covers 2500±300 unique states in Ant-Hardest-Maze, 230±40 in Humanoid-U-Maze, and 135,000±10,000 unique cube positions in Arm-Binpick-Hard. In Craftax-Classic, C-TeC unlocks more achievements (sum of achievement success rates) than RND, ICM, and E3B. The method also demonstrates sample efficiency, retaining strong coverage at 50M steps (1916±430 in Ant-Hardest-Maze) and 30M steps (1119±304).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RND, ICM, and APT (Figure 5), C-TeC consistently outperforms all three baselines in unique state coverage across Ant-Large-Maze, Humanoid-U-Maze, and Arm-Binpick-Hard, with the gap widening over training as baseline rewards saturate toward zero while C-TeC maintains non-zero reward. Against ETD (Jiang et al., 2025) (Figure 3), C-TeC is competitive on Ant-Hardest-Maze, outperforms ETD on Humanoid-U-Maze (with higher variance), loses to ETD on Arm-Binpick-Hard, and significantly outperforms ETD on Crafter, which the authors attribute to C-TeC's forward-looking exploration signal versus ETD's backward-looking episodic novelty. In Craftax-Classic (Figure 7), C-TeC outperforms RND, ICM, and E3B in total achievements unlocked, with PPO-RNN yielding the best performance. In the Noisy TV setting (Xland-Minigrid), C-TeC achieves full grid coverage unaffected by noise, whereas surprise-based methods typically get trapped. The key advantage over ETD is algorithmic simplicity: C-TeC requires no episodic memory, no quasimetric/MRN architecture, and works in both on-policy and off-policy settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

C-TeC builds directly on the contrastive reinforcement learning framework of Eysenbach et al. (2021, 2022), which established that InfoNCE-trained critics over discounted state occupancy measures yield successor-representation-like features. The key departure is from Jiang et al. (2025, ETD), which also uses temporal contrastive learning for exploration but requires a quasimetric (metric residual network) architecture and an episodic memory to compute backward-looking temporal distances. C-TeC instead uses the negated contrastive similarity score as a forward-looking intrinsic reward, eliminating both the quasimetric constraint and episodic memory, making it compatible with off-policy algorithms like SAC. This positions C-TeC as a simpler, more flexible instantiation of the same underlying idea: that temporal contrastive representations encode sufficient structure to guide exploration without explicit world models or reconstruction objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single scalar (the negated contrastive similarity between current state-action and a sampled future state) is sufficient to drive complex exploratory behavior across locomotion, manipulation, and open-world survival, without task rewards, world models, or episodic memory. The information-theoretic analysis (reverse KL / mode-seeking interpretation) provides a clean theoretical account of why the reward avoids degenerate solutions like the Noisy TV problem, and the ablation showing that a monolithic critic fails while the separable representation parameterization succeeds underscores that the learned temporal structure is what matters. Limitations include evaluation only on state-vector observations (no pixel-based or partially observed settings), reliance on a trajectory buffer, and the need to tune the critic norm (L1 vs. L2) per environment. Open questions include whether the method transfers to high-dimensional visual inputs, how it interacts with extrinsic task rewards, and whether the forward-looking signal advantage over backward-looking methods (ETD) generalizes beyond the tested environments.
