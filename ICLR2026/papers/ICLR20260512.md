## Problem

Existing hallucination-mitigation methods for LVLMs rely on forward-pass attention maps, which cannot reliably distinguish hallucinated from correct tokens because they ignore gradient-based signals that reveal how token influence propagates through the model. The paper identifies a decisive mechanistic pattern: hallucinations occur when prior output tokens exhibit low saliency to the next token prediction, indicating a failure of contextual memory that attention-only methods fail to capture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260512/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard LVLM inputs (an RGB image plus a text prompt) during autoregressive decoding. At each decoding step, it computes token-level saliency scores via a backward pass (gradient of cross-entropy loss with respect to attention matrices), producing a per-token grounding score that quantifies how strongly each previously generated output token influences the next prediction. SGRS consumes these scores to filter candidate tokens before commitment, while LocoRE modifies attention weights in-place in the forward pass without any gradient computation, keeping it deployable on large models where SGRS's backward pass is infeasible.

## Method

The core diagnostic is LVLMs-Saliency, defined as the element-wise product of attention weights and their gradients (|A ⊙ ∇A|) with causal triangular masking and layer-wise ℓ₂-normalization, which quantifies token-level contextual grounding. Building on the finding that hallucinated tokens exhibit significantly lower saliency from prior outputs, the paper proposes two complementary inference-time interventions: Saliency-Guided Rejection Sampling (SGRS) samples K candidates per step, computes each candidate's saliency over target layers and output-token positions, and rejects those below an adaptive threshold τ = α · (mean saliency of the last W accepted tokens), forcing resampling until a contextually grounded token is selected; and Local Coherence Reinforcement (LocoRE) multiplies attention weights from the current query to the most recent w_s output tokens by a distance-aware gain γ_j = 1 + β·𝕀((P−j) ≤ w_s), actively counteracting the saliency decay that triggers hallucination. The two mechanisms form a closed loop: SGRS acts as a gatekeeper blocking low-saliency tokens at entry, while LocoRE acts as a stabilizer reinforcing contextual dependencies after commitment, with LocoRE alone serving as a lightweight (<2% latency) plug-and-play alternative when gradient computation is impractical.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B, SGRS+LocoRE achieves the best CHAIR_S (35.6) and CHAIR_I (8.2) among all compared methods, outperforming EAH (36.4/9.9) and Reverse-VLM (35.3/9.3), while also attaining the highest MME total score (668.33 vs. CausalILLM's 656.00). POPE F1 reaches 87.0 and Accuracy 87.5, competitive with MemVR (87.1 F1) and above all other baselines. LocoRE alone (without SGRS) already reduces CHAIR_S from 51.0 to 38.4 and CHAIR_I from 15.2 to 11.2, demonstrating that the attention-reinforcement component carries most of the hallucination-reduction benefit. Across six LVLMs (LLaVA-1.5-7B/13B, Qwen2-VL-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B/32B, InternVL-7B/13B), SGRS+LocoRE consistently improves CHAIR_S by 3.4–12.4 points and POPE F1 by 0.4–2.3 points over each model's baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGRS+LocoRE achieves the best CHAIR_S (35.6) and CHAIR_I (8.2) among all 18 compared methods, surpassing the closest competitors EAH (36.4/9.9) and Reverse-VLM (35.3/9.3) on both descriptive hallucination metrics. On MME, the combined method scores 668.33 total, exceeding CausalILLM (656.00) and MemVR (648.30), with particularly strong gains on Existence (195.00) and Color (175.00) subtasks. On POPE, SGRS+LocoRE reaches 87.0 F1 / 87.5 Acc, marginally below MemVR (87.1/87.4) but above all other baselines including Vissink (86.0/86.5) and CCA-LLaVA (86.4/86.5). The paper explicitly contrasts with EAH, noting that LocoRE preserves model output diversity (higher recall) because it modifies attention weights in-place rather than replacing attention heads. Compared to TAME and Vissink, which redistribute attention over system or visual tokens, LocoRE's focus on output-token-to-output-token coherence yields substantially better CHAIR scores (35.6 vs. 41.3 for TAME and 52.4 for Vissink), confirming that contextual text dependencies—not just visual grounding—are critical for long-form generation fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant attention-sink paradigm (OPERA, DOPRA, EAH, TAME, Farsight) that diagnoses hallucination through forward-pass attention patterns, arguing that attention maps alone cannot distinguish correct from hallucinated tokens. It draws on the information-flow perspective of 'Label Words' (Wang et al., 2023) to introduce a gradient-aware saliency metric (attention × gradient) that captures how token influence propagates through the network, and shifts focus from cross-modal (image-to-text) attention to output-to-output token dynamics during autoregressive generation. Unlike prior methods that rebalance visual attention or penalize anchor tokens in logits, the proposed SGRS and LocoRE operate exclusively within the output token stream, treating hallucination as a failure of contextual memory rather than a failure of visual grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first interpretable, token-level diagnostic (LVLMs-Saliency) that links a measurable quantity to hallucination onset, supported by a causal intervention experiment showing that artificially reducing saliency increases hallucination rates. LocoRE's simplicity (a single multiplicative gain on recent output attention) and <2% latency overhead make it a practical drop-in for any LVLM, while SGRS's backward-pass requirement limits its deployment to models ≤13B, leaving open whether the saliency-hallucination relationship holds at 70B+ scale. The acknowledged failure cases (high-saliency hallucinations driven by model overconfidence) suggest that saliency is a necessary but not sufficient signal, pointing toward future work combining it with confidence calibration or external verification.
