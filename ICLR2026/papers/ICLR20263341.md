## Problem

Existing diffusion-based image generators are confined to fixed shapes and resolutions, with no explicit control over viewpoint, field-of-view, or output resolution. Perspective methods implicitly learn spatial correlations from training data without controllable spatial attributes, while panoramic methods produce fixed-resolution outputs from a single central viewpoint, requiring lossy projection for novel views. No unified framework exists that jointly controls viewpoint, FOV, and resolution while generating high-quality images across diverse shapes (perspective, panoramic, fisheye).

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (generated via BLIP-2) together with explicit spatial conditions—viewpoint (θ, φ), field-of-view, and target resolution—and produces a complete spherical scene representation as an intermediate latent, from which arbitrary-shaped images are rendered. The output supports any projection type (perspective, equirectangular, fisheye) at any resolution, with aspect ratios emerging naturally from the joint specification of FOV and resolution. The spherical latent is structured as 10 diamond-shaped patches on a subdivided icosahedron, each mapped to a rectangular feature grid whose spatial dimensions scale with the subdivision level (e.g., 64×64 at L=5, 128×128 at L=6).

## Method

ASIG combines two components: (1) a mesh-based spherical latent diffusion that represents the scene on an L-subdivided icosahedron, where a UNet (initialized from SDXL) denoises Gaussian noise into a complete spherical latent, and Seam-Aware Padding (SAP) copies boundary features from mesh-adjacent neighbor patches at every convolutional layer to enforce semantic and spatial continuity across the sphere; and (2) a spherical neural field that extracts multi-scale VAE decoder features, refines them with spherical residual blocks that remap receptive fields to the icosahedron topology, and then uses a coordinate-conditioned convolutional latent sampler to render arbitrary regions at specified viewpoints, FOVs, and resolutions without geometric distortion. Training proceeds in two stages: first, the spherical neural field is trained end-to-end (with frozen VAE encoder) using L1, LPIPS, and GAN losses against ground-truth RGB samples at arbitrary resolutions; second, the UNet is finetuned for the reverse diffusion process with seam-enforced denoising, where both the noisy latent and target noise are augmented by SAP to align training with inference and prevent boundary artifacts.

## Results

On the Matterport3D test set, ASIG achieves the best scores across all metrics and all three image shapes. For perspective images, ASIG reaches FID 14.68, KID* 0.59, and CLIP-FID 3.58, substantially outperforming the next-best PanFusion (FID 21.33, KID* 0.99, CLIP-FID 5.44). For panoramas, ASIG attains FID 25.49, FAED 1.47, KID* 0.97, and CLIP-FID 2.94, beating SMGD (FID 28.92, KID* 1.02) and PanFusion (FID 37.09, KID* 1.59). For fisheye images, ASIG achieves FID 10.29, KID* 0.51, and CLIP-FID 1.99, roughly halving the FID of the best prior method (PanFusion, FID 17.16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASIG outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric across all three image shapes. On perspective generation, ASIG reduces FID from 21.33 (PanFusion, the best prior method) to 14.68 (−31%) and KID* from 0.99 to 0.59 (−40%), while also achieving the best no-reference quality scores (NIQE 4.18 vs. 5.57 for SDXL Pano., MUSIQ 67.03 vs. 53.16). On panoramas, ASIG achieves FID 25.49 vs. 28.92 for SMGD and 37.09 for PanFusion, with the lowest KID* (0.97 vs. 1.02 for SMGD) and CLIP-FID (2.94 vs. 3.10). On fisheye images, ASIG nearly halves the FID of the best competitor (10.29 vs. 17.16 for PanFusion) and achieves the lowest KID* (0.51 vs. 0.87). The arbitrary-resolution methods (INFD, Kim et al.) and LoRA-finetuned SD/SDXL panoramas are consistently the weakest baselines, confirming that simply upscaling or finetuning fixed-grid models cannot match a unified spherical representation. Notably, ASIG also generalizes to out-of-domain scenes, maintaining CLIP-Scores close to vanilla SDXL (31.81 vs. 32.27) while surpassing all panoramic baselines (PanFusion 31.14, SMGD 25.12).

## Relation to Prior Work

ASIG builds directly on the SDXL latent diffusion backbone and the INFD paradigm of coupling neural fields with diffusion for arbitrary-resolution synthesis, but departs fundamentally from both by replacing the rectangular latent grid with a mesh-based spherical representation on a subdivided icosahedron. Where prior perspective methods (SD, SDXL, DiT) implicitly encode spatial attributes from training data, and panoramic methods (MVDiffusion, PanFusion, SMGD) are locked to fixed equirectangular grids at a single viewpoint, ASIG introduces explicit, decoupled control over viewpoint, FOV, and resolution through a spherical neural field that samples from a unified scene representation. The Seam-Aware Padding mechanism addresses a practical gap in applying standard 2D convolutions to non-rectangular spherical patches, a problem not encountered in prior rectangular-grid diffusion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying perspective, panoramic, and fisheye generation in a single model with explicit spatial control, ASIG eliminates the need for separate specialized pipelines and the distortion/quality loss inherent in projecting between representations. The icosahedron mesh avoids the polar distortion of ERP and the face-boundary discontinuities of cubemaps, offering a cleaner geometric foundation for spherical generative modeling that could transfer to related tasks (3D scene generation, novel view synthesis, video). Open questions include scalability to higher resolutions and longer sequences, whether the two-stage training (neural field then diffusion) can be unified, and how the approach extends beyond the indoor Matterport3D training domain to more diverse scene categories.
