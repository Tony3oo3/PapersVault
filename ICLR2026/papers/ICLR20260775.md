## Problem

Post-training methods (SFT, RL) applied to general-purpose foundation models consistently underperform on agentic tasks because these models lack agentic inductive biases, forcing post-training to simultaneously learn diverse agentic behaviors and align them to expert demonstrations—a fundamental optimization tension. No prior work has explored inserting an agentic-specific continual pre-training stage between standard pre-training and post-training to resolve this conflict.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260775/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a text research question and autonomously orchestrates a multi-step ReAct loop over five tools (Google Search, page Visit, Python Interpreter, Google Scholar, File Parser), with a 128K-token context window and up to 128 tool calls per task. Outputs are structured as interleaved reasoning chains, tool invocations with responses, and a final cited answer or report. Evaluation spans 10 benchmarks covering general web search (BrowseComp, GAIA, xbench-DeepSearch, WebWalkerQA) and scenario-targeted tasks (HLE, DeepResearch Bench, Frames, SEAL-0, Academic Browse).

## Method

AgentFounder introduces Agentic Continual Pre-Training (Agentic CPT) as a new intermediate stage between pre-training and post-training, using ~300B tokens in two stages: Stage 1 (~200B tokens, 32K context) establishes core planning and reasoning, while Stage 2 (~100B tokens, 128K context) develops long-horizon planning. Training data is synthesized via two complementary methods: First-order Action Synthesis (FAS) generates planning and reasoning data without any API calls by transforming static knowledge into entity-anchored questions and using LLMs to produce diverse first-step analyses with reject sampling; Higher-order Action Synthesis (HAS) reuses post-training trajectories by expanding each step into N+1 alternative reasoning-action candidates and converting the trajectory into a contrastive multi-option decision process with explicit correct/incorrect judgments. This shifts the learning objective from trajectory imitation to step-wise decision-making, enabling the model to explore the action space at each step rather than memorize a single path.

## Results

AgentFounder-30B achieves state-of-the-art results across 10 agentic benchmarks, scoring 39.9% on BrowseComp-en, 43.3% on BrowseComp-zh, 72.8% on GAIA, 73.0% on Xbench-DeepSearch, and 71.9% on WebWalkerQA. On scenario-targeted benchmarks, it reaches 31.5% Pass@1 on HLE (first open-source model above 30%), 89.6% on Frames, 43.9% on SEAL-0, 75.3% on Academic Browse, and 47.9% on DeepResearch Bench RACE Overall. General capability is largely preserved, with only ~1.3-point drops on MMLU, SuperGPQA, and GPQA relative to the Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base initialization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AgentFounder-30B outperforms all open-source deep research agents on BrowseComp-en by a wide margin (39.9 vs. 30.0 for DeepSeek-V3.1, the strongest open-source baseline), closing the gap to OpenAI-o3 (49.7) and Deep Research (51.5) to within ~10 points. On GAIA, it achieves 72.8%, surpassing even OpenAI-o3 (70.5) and Claude-4-Sonnet (68.3), while on Xbench-DeepSearch it leads all models at 73.0% (vs. 71.0 for DeepSeek-V3.1 and 70.0 for GLM-4.5). On BrowseComp-zh, AgentFounder-30B (43.3) trails DeepSeek-V3.1 (49.2) and OpenAI-o3 (58.1), which the authors attribute to limited Chinese training data. On scenario-targeted benchmarks, AgentFounder-30B sets new records: 31.5% on HLE (beating DeepSeek-V3.1's 29.8% and all closed-source agents), 89.6% on Frames (vs. 84.0 for OpenAI-o3), 43.9% on SEAL-0 (vs. 42.6 for DeepSeek-V3.1), and 75.3% on Academic Browse (vs. 65.0 for DeepSeek-V3.1). On DeepResearch Bench RACE Overall, it scores 47.9%, exceeding OpenAI Deep Research (46.5) and Gemini Deep Research (49.7 is actually higher, so it trails Gemini but beats OpenAI DR). General tool-use on ACEBench also improves from 67.2 (Qwen3-30B-A3B) to 70.0, confirming that Agentic CPT does not sacrifice broader tool-use ability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continual pre-training (CPT) literature—which has primarily targeted domain-specific knowledge adaptation (Gupta et al., 2023; Ke et al., 2023; Yıldız et al., 2025)—to the novel objective of instilling agentic inductive biases (tool invocation, multi-step planning, long-horizon reasoning) at the foundation-model level. It departs from the dominant deep-research-agent training paradigm (WebSailor, WebThinker, ASearcher, GLM-4.5, DeepSeek-V3.1), which applies SFT and RL directly on general-purpose LLMs, by arguing that this creates an irreducible optimization tension between learning agentic capabilities and aligning to expert demonstrations. The data synthesis pipeline (FAS and HAS) is positioned against prior trajectory-based data generation (WebSailor's knowledge-graph synthesis, WebShaper's information-seeking formalization) by eliminating the need for expensive API calls and by reframing trajectories as step-level decision problems rather than sequences to imitate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a 30B model trained with Agentic CPT can outperform substantially larger open-source agents (DeepSeek-V3.1, GLM-4.5, Kimi-K2) across 10 benchmarks, the paper provides strong evidence that the bottleneck for open-source deep research agents lies in the foundation model rather than in post-training recipes. The logarithmic scaling law for agentic CPT data (54.2% → 62.2% over 15B–315B tokens) and the two-stage long-context curriculum offer a concrete, reproducible recipe for the community. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond web-research agents to broader tool-use settings (the ACEBench result is promising but preliminary), whether the minor general-capability regression (≈1.3 points) can be eliminated, and whether the MoE-specific expert-activation benefits observed here transfer to dense architectures.
