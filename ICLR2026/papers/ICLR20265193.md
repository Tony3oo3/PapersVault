## Problem

Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs) replace fixed nonlinearities with trainable spline-based activation functions, yet their initialization strategies remain largely unexplored. The standard KAN paper uses a simple baseline (unit scaling weights, Glorot-distributed residual weights, small-Gaussian basis weights), but existing MLP initialization theory cannot be directly transferred because KAN layers contain three distinct trainable parameter types and grid-dependent basis functions. This paper addresses the gap by proposing and systematically evaluating new initialization schemes tailored to the spline-based KAN architecture.

## Input / Output

The initialization schemes are applied to the three trainable parameter types in each KAN layer—residual weights r_ji, scaling weights c_ji, and spline basis weights b_jim—before training begins. Evaluation spans function fitting on 2D/3D coordinate inputs (domain [-1,1]^d, 4000 training samples, 200×200 or 30×30×30 evaluation grids) and forward PDE solving (Allen-Cahn, Burgers', Helmholtz) using 4096 collocation points with Residual-Based Attention weighting. Architectures vary from 1–4 hidden layers with widths 2^1 to 2^6 and spline grid sizes G ∈ {5, 10, 20, 40}, with outputs measured as final training loss and relative L² error against reference solutions.

## Method

The paper derives three initialization families by adapting variance-preservation principles to the KAN layer structure, where each output is a sum of (G+k+1) terms (one residual + G+k spline basis). LeCun-inspired schemes enforce forward-pass variance matching, yielding σ_r and σ_b proportional to 1/√(n_in(G+k+1)) scaled by activation/basis second moments; two variants differ in how the spline-basis moment μ_B^(0) is handled (numerical sampling vs. normalized basis functions). Glorot-inspired schemes additionally balance backward-pass gradient variance via harmonic averaging of forward and backward constraints, incorporating derivative moments μ_R^(1) and μ_B^(1). The most effective approach is an empirical power-law family σ_r = (1/(n_in(G+k+1)))^α, σ_b = (1/(n_in(G+k+1)))^β, where grid search over (α,β) identifies favorable regions (α ∈ {0.25, 0.5}, β ≥ 1.0 for function fitting; β ∈ [0.75, 1.25] for PDEs). NTK spectral analysis confirms that Glorot and power-law initializations produce well-conditioned, stable eigenvalue spectra, while baseline and LeCun schemes yield collapsing, poorly conditioned kernels that explain their underperformance.

## Results

On function fitting benchmarks (5 target functions, 2,400 architecture configurations), the power-law initialization (α=0.25, β=1.0) outperforms the baseline in 94.79–100% of runs on both loss and L² error, while Glorot-inspired initialization achieves 60–78% improvement rates. On forward PDE benchmarks (Allen-Cahn, Burgers, Helmholtz), power-law maintains 73.61–100% improvement rates, and Glorot reaches 50–76%. On the Feynman dataset (20 dimensionless equations), power-law (α=0.25, β=1.75) achieves the lowest final training loss and L² error on the majority of equations in both small and large architectures, with losses often several orders of magnitude below baseline (e.g., 2.16×10⁻⁸ vs. 1.64×10⁻⁴ on I.12.11 for the large architecture).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the standard KAN initialization from Liu et al. (2025), which sets c_ji=1, r_ji via Glorot, and b_jim from N(0, 0.1²). The power-law initialization (α=0.25, β=1.0) decisively outperforms this baseline across all benchmarks: it achieves simultaneous improvement in both loss and L² error in 94.79–100% of function fitting runs and 73.61–94.44% of PDE runs. The Glorot-inspired initialization is the second-best scheme, outperforming baseline in 60–78% of function fitting runs and 50–76% of PDE runs, with its advantage concentrated in parameter-rich architectures (larger depth, width, and grid size). The LeCun-inspired variants (both numerical and normalized) consistently underperform the baseline, with success rates below 30% on function fitting and near-zero for the normalized variant on PDEs. On the Feynman dataset, the baseline initialization exhibits a notable pathology where the larger architecture (G=20, 3 layers × 32 neurons) often performs worse than the smaller one (G=5, 2 layers × 8 neurons), whereas both Glorot and power-law enable the larger architecture to reduce loss by several orders of magnitude relative to the smaller one.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical MLP initialization literature (LeCun 1998, Glorot 2010) to the spline-based Kolmogorov-Arnold Network architecture, where the three distinct weight types (residual, scaling, and spline-basis) and the grid-dependent basis functions make direct transfer of variance-preserving schemes non-trivial. It builds on prior KAN initialization work for Chebyshev-based variants (Rigas et al. 2026) and the general KAN formalism (Liu et al. 2025), but is the first systematic study targeting the standard spline-based formulation. The NTK-based training dynamics analysis draws on the PIML NTK framework (Wang et al. 2022) extended here to incorporate Residual-Based Attention weights (Anagnostopoulos et al. 2024), and the empirical power-law family is motivated by the observation that theory-driven schemes do not always align with empirical performance (Mishkin & Matas 2016).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that initialization is a first-order factor in KAN training quality, with the empirical power-law scheme (α≈0.25, β≈1.0–1.75) consistently outperforming both the standard baseline and theory-driven Glorot/LeCun variants across function fitting, PDE solving, and the Feynman dataset. A key open question is the absence of a theoretical justification for why specific power-law exponent ranges work well, leaving the scheme as a practical heuristic rather than a principled method. The complete failure of the LeCun-normalized variant on PDEs (0% success rate) highlights that normalization strategies designed for forward-pass variance preservation can be actively harmful when derivatives enter the loss. Future work should address transferability across KAN variants (Chebyshev, residual-free), other domains (RL, generative modeling), and the interaction between initialization and adaptive optimization.
