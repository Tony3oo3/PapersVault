## Problem

Selecting the best pre-trained time series model from a growing model hub without expensive fine-tuning of all candidates remains unsolved. Existing model selection methods are designed for standardized vision feature extractors, ignore temporal dependencies and model heterogeneity in time series, and lack mechanisms to generalize across unseen datasets and varying forecasting horizons.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263471/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series dataset (segmented into patches of size S), a model hub of K pre-trained models (each characterized offline by meta-information, a DAG-derived topological embedding via graph2vec, and a functional embedding from Gaussian noise probing), and a target forecasting horizon H. It outputs a vector of K predicted ranking scores without any fine-tuning, from which the top-ranked model is selected; the scores are trained to correlate with ground-truth fine-tuning MSE via a joint ranking + prediction loss.

## Method

SwiftTS employs a lightweight dual-encoder: a temporal-aware data encoder that patches the input series, applies self-attention over patch embeddings, and aggregates multiple random subsets via mean pooling; and a knowledge-infused model encoder that concatenates meta-information (architecture type, parameter count, GMACs, hidden dimension, pre-training domain), a graph2vec topological embedding of the model's computational DAG, and a functional embedding obtained by feeding fixed Gaussian noise through each model. Patchwise cross-attention between model and data embeddings yields fine-grained compatibility scores, which are mapped to ranking predictions through a horizon-adaptive mixture-of-experts module (a router assigns softmax weights to G=4 MLP experts based on the target horizon). Training uses MAML-style meta-learning with two task-sampling strategies—cross-dataset and cross-horizon—where support sets drive inner-loop adaptation and query sets drive outer-loop meta-gradient updates, improving out-of-distribution robustness across both domains and forecasting ranges.

## Results

SwiftTS achieves the best average weighted Kendall's τ_ω across all four forecasting horizons on 14 datasets: 0.470 (H=96), 0.453 (H=192), 0.411 (H=336), and 0.432 (H=720), with an overall average of 0.442. It also attains the highest top-1 selection probability (Pr(top-1) = 0.339) and top-3 probability (Pr(top-3) = 0.607), and correctly identifies the best model in 28 out of 56 dataset-horizon combinations (Num.Top-1 = 28), far exceeding the next-best baseline (Model Spider, 6/56). The method maintains predominantly positive τ_ω across nearly all dataset-horizon pairs, whereas several baselines exhibit negative correlations on multiple settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SwiftTS outperforms all six baselines on the primary metric (average τ_ω) and top-k selection probability. The closest competitor is Model Spider (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2023), which achieves τ_ω = 0.296 and Pr(top-1) = 0.304; SwiftTS improves τ_ω by 0.146 (to 0.442) and Pr(top-1) by 0.035 (to 0.339). Etran (Gholami et al., ICCV 2023) is the strongest feature-analytic method with τ_ω = 0.150, but SwiftTS more than triples its ranking correlation. DISCO (Zhang et al., AAAI 2025) achieves only τ_ω = 0.033, and LogME (You et al., ICML 2021) and RankME (Garrido et al., ICML 2023) yield negative average τ_ω (−0.041 and −0.119 respectively), indicating their rankings are worse than random on average. AutoForecast (Abdallah et al., CIKM 2022) performs near zero (τ_ω ≈ 0.069) and never selects the top-1 model. Notably, SwiftTS is the only method that maintains positive τ_ω across all four horizons simultaneously, while Etran degrades to negative values at H=336 and several baselines (RankME, LogME, Regression) become strongly negative at longer horizons.

## Relation to Prior Work

SwiftTS extends the pre-trained model selection paradigm from vision (Model Spider, RankME, LogME) to time series forecasting, where the added dimension of forecasting horizons and the heterogeneity of TSFM architectures (encoder-only, decoder-only, encoder-decoder) make prior feature-analytic methods inapplicable. It repurposes MAML's inner/outer-loop meta-learning for cross-dataset and cross-horizon generalization, and borrows graph2vec to encode model architectures as DAGs, but the core novelty lies in the dual-encoder design that pairs a patch-based temporal data encoder with a knowledge-infused model encoder (meta-information + topology + functional embeddings) connected via patchwise cross-attention. The horizon-adaptive expert composition module introduces a lightweight MoE-style router that was absent from prior model selection work, which typically required separate models or retraining per horizon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the time series foundation model ecosystem rapidly expands (MOIRAI, TimesFM, Chronos, TTM, ROSE, etc.), the practical cost of brute-force fine-tuning all candidates becomes prohibitive, making efficient selection a critical deployment bottleneck. SwiftTS addresses this with a framework whose runtime is largely independent of dataset size, a property that scales favorably as both model hubs and downstream datasets grow. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to truly novel architectures unseen during meta-training, how it performs when the model hub grows beyond ~14 models with highly diverse training objectives, and whether the meta-dataset construction (requiring fine-tuning results across dataset-model-horizon triples) remains tractable as the combinatorial space expands. The paper's reliance on a fixed set of 8 models and 14 datasets, while standard for the field, limits confidence in extreme OOD scenarios.
