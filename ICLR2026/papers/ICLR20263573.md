## Problem

4D hand motion modeling is typically split into estimation (reconstructing poses from visual observations) and generation (synthesizing poses from structured inputs), a separation that prevents flexible integration of heterogeneous conditions and blocks knowledge transfer between the two tasks. In practice, visual inputs suffer from occlusions or absence while structured signals exhibit temporal discontinuities, demanding a framework that can handle arbitrary combinations of incomplete, multi-modal conditions within a single model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263573/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts any subset of video frames (full-size, not hand-cropped), 2D skeleton keypoints (21×2, normalized to [0,1]), 3D skeleton keypoints (21×3), and MANO parameters, each paired with a per-frame binary availability mask to handle temporal incompleteness. It outputs a 4D hand motion sequence of length N (48 during training) parameterized by MANO (pose, shape, global orientation, root translation) in a canonical coordinate space defined by the first frame's camera, which decouples hand motion from dynamic camera trajectories without requiring explicit extrinsics.

## Method

UniHand unifies estimation and generation as conditional motion synthesis via a two-stage pipeline. A Joint VAE with a motion encoder and separate condition encoders (for 2D/3D keypoints) aligns all structured signals into a shared 512-dim latent space, decoded autoregressively in segments of 8 with a KL-regularized global token. For visual inputs, a frozen DINOv2 backbone produces dense tokens from full frames, and a hand perceptron module uses trainable hand tokens plus an initialization pose token as queries to attend over these tokens with 3D RoPE, yielding one hand token per frame without detection or cropping. A 16-layer transformer latent diffusion denoiser then predicts the clean latent z₀, fusing structured conditions at the latent level and integrating vision hand tokens via cross-attention at every denoising step, with classifier-free guidance using independent learnable unconditional tokens per modality to support flexible condition combinations.

## Results

On the DexYCB benchmark (camera coordinate space), UniHand achieves a PA-MPJPE of 4.08 mm and AUC of 0.918, outperforming all image-based and video-based baselines. Under the most severe occlusion level (75%–100%), UniHand maintains a PA-MPJPE of 4.26 mm and AUC of 0.912, demonstrating robustness where other methods degrade significantly. On the HOT3D egocentric benchmark (world coordinate space), UniHand achieves the best PA-MPJPE of 4.76 mm and the lowest acceleration error (4.93 mm), though its global alignment scores (G-MPJPE 63.97, GA-MPJPE 25.24) remain higher than optimization-based methods like HaWoR that explicitly use camera extrinsics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DexYCB, UniHand (PA-MPJPE 4.08) outperforms the strongest video-based baseline HaWoR (4.76) by 0.68 mm and the best image-based method WiLoR (5.01) by 0.93 mm; the gap widens under severe occlusion where UniHand (4.26) leads HaWoR (5.07) by 0.81 mm and Spurr et al. (10.65) by over 6 mm. On HO3D, UniHand (6.7 mm) beats WiLoR (7.5), HaMeR (7.7), and Deformer (9.4), confirming generalization to out-of-distribution object interactions. On HOT3D (world space), UniHand achieves the best PA-MPJPE (4.76 vs. HaWoR's 5.47) and lowest acceleration error (4.93 vs. Dyn-HaMR's 5.16), but trails HaWoR (47.35) and Dyn-HaMR (59.04) on G-MPJPE and GA-MPJPE, reflecting its reliance on canonical-space modeling without explicit camera extrinsics. Overall, UniHand wins on pose accuracy and temporal smoothness across all three benchmarks, while losing on global trajectory alignment in dynamic-camera settings to methods that incorporate SLAM-based camera estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the latent diffusion paradigm for motion generation (Ho et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2023) and the VAE-based latent alignment strategy from earlier hand pose work (Wan et al., 2017; Yang et al., 2019), extending both into a single unified framework. It departs from the prevailing separation between estimation pipelines (HaMeR, WiLoR, Deformer) and generation pipelines (GraspDiff, MGD) by recasting estimation as a special case of conditional motion synthesis, and from multi-stage detection-based approaches by introducing a hand perceptron that operates on full-frame DINOv2 features without cropping. The canonical coordinate space formulation also distinguishes it from world-space methods (HaWoR, Dyn-HaMR) that rely on explicit SLAM-based camera trajectory estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying estimation and generation under one diffusion model, UniHand enables flexible conditioning on heterogeneous and potentially incomplete signals (vision, 2D/3D skeletons, MANO parameters) within a single checkpoint, which is practically valuable for VR, digital avatars, and robotics where input modalities vary. The demonstrated robustness under severe occlusion (75–100%) and temporally missing frames addresses a persistent failure mode of prior estimation methods. A key open limitation is that under large dynamic camera motion, the canonical-space formulation still lags behind optimization-based methods that explicitly leverage camera extrinsics for global trajectory consistency, pointing toward future integration of camera estimation within the generative loop.
