## Problem

SIGN-SGD is a popular memory-efficient optimizer and gradient compression scheme, but its effective stepsize depends on unknown problem-specific quantities (the initial suboptimality Δ* and the L∞-smoothness constant), forcing costly grid-search tuning. This paper eliminates that dependency by introducing a parameter-free variant that adapts the stepsize automatically at every iteration, while also extending the analysis to stochastic and distributed settings where prior parameter-free methods lacked guarantees.

## Input / Output

At each iteration the method consumes the current (exact or stochastic) gradient vector, the current parameter vector, and—critically—the sign of the previous gradient (or, in the memory-efficient variant, only two scalars: the coordinate-wise max and min of the previous gradient). It outputs an updated parameter vector via a sign-based step whose magnitude is computed on the fly from accumulated local smoothness estimates and a running upper bound on the initial suboptimality. No external learning-rate schedule, grid search, or restart procedure is required; the stepsize emerges entirely from the trajectory of past gradients.

## Method

The core algorithm, ALIAS (Automatic Local per-Iteration Approximation of the Stepsize), sets the per-iteration stepsize as γ^t = λ^t · √(d^t), where λ^t = 1/√(η^t) accumulates local L∞-smoothness ratios ‖∇f(x^{i+1})−∇f(x^i)‖₁/‖x^{i+1}−x^i‖∞ in an Adagrad-Norm fashion, and d^t is a non-decreasing upper bound on Δ* = f(x⁰)−f(x*) updated via max with a cumulative inner-product estimator. A memory-efficient variant swaps the l₁/l∞ norms in the smoothness ratio and approximates the l∞-norm of the gradient difference using only the stored sign plus two scalars (max and min of the previous gradient), reducing per-step memory to match vanilla SIGN-SGD. An Adam-style momentum extension (Algorithm 3) layers exponential moving averages of first and second moments on top of the sign update, yielding coordinate-wise normalization that empirically outperforms tuned AdamW and Prodigy. Convergence is established under convexity and L∞-smoothness for exact, stochastic (with coordinate-wise bounded variance), and multi-node distributed oracles, with the stochastic rate matching the original SIGN-SGD bound up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

On 130M-parameter LLaMA pre-training (C4, 100k steps), ALIAS Adam version with weight decay, momentum, and cosine schedule achieves a validation loss of 2.918 and perplexity of 18.504, outperforming tuned ADAMW (2.929 / 18.698) and PRODIGY with cosine schedule (2.930 / 18.727). On the 350M LLaMA model, ALIAS Adam version reaches 2.707 loss / 14.984 perplexity, again the best among all methods tested. On Tiny ImageNet fine-tuning with a 28M Swin Transformer, ALIAS Adam version with cosine schedule attains 79.16% final accuracy, surpassing SIGN-SGD (78.89%), ADAMW (77.61%), and PRODIGY (77.94%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALIAS Adam version with cosine schedule outperforms all six baselines on the primary 130M LLaMA benchmark: it beats tuned SIGN-SGD with cosine schedule (2.918 vs. 2.923 loss), ADAMW (2.918 vs. 2.929), and PRODIGY with cosine schedule (2.918 vs. 2.930). Against the other parameter-free optimizers (Table 7), ALIAS Adam version (2.918 loss) surpasses DoG (2.939), D-ADAPTATION with Adam (2.927), MOMO with Adam (2.925), and PRODIGY (2.930). The advantage is consistent at scale: on the 350M LLaMA model, ALIAS Adam version (2.707) edges out SIGN-SGD (2.717), ADAMW (2.719), and PRODIGY (2.715). On Tiny ImageNet Swin fine-tuning, ALIAS Adam version with cosine schedule (79.16%) leads SIGN-SGD (78.89%), ADAMW (77.61%), and PRODIGY (77.94%). Notably, the basic ALIAS without momentum (Table 1) is slightly below tuned SIGN-SGD with cosine schedule (3.006 vs. 2.980 loss), but the Adam-style momentum variant closes and reverses this gap, and the entire pipeline requires no learning-rate grid search, yielding ~1.5× end-to-end speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SIGN-SGD (Bernstein et al., 2018) by replacing its manually-tuned stepsize with a per-iteration adaptive scheme, drawing on the AdaGrad-Norm accumulation strategy (Duchi et al., 2011) for the smoothness estimate and the Adam-style momentum framework (Kingma, 2014; Mishchenko & Defazio, 2023) for the practical variant. It departs from the broader parameter-free optimization literature (DoG, D-Adaptation, Coin Betting) by working in the near-stationary-point paradigm under L-infinity smoothness rather than the regret-minimization paradigm under L2 smoothness, and by preserving the sign-based memory efficiency that distinguishes SIGN-SGD from full-gradient methods. The paper also contrasts its single-pass ALIAS algorithm with its own SOS Sign-SGD variant, which achieves near-optimal rates but requires multiple restarts and is impractical for large-scale training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Eliminating learning-rate grid search for SIGN-SGD removes a significant practical bottleneck in large-scale training, yielding a reported ~1.5× end-to-end speedup, and the memory-efficient variant retains the original SIGN-SGD's O(1) per-parameter state while maintaining adaptivity. The Adam-style momentum variant outperforming both tuned AdamW and Prodigy across LLM pre-training and vision fine-tuning suggests that sign-based parameter-free optimization is a viable alternative to the dominant AdamW paradigm. Limitations include suboptimal theoretical rates (L∞³ vs. L∞), convexity assumptions in the analysis, and a slight performance gap for the memory-efficient variant; open questions include whether the approach scales to billion-parameter models and whether the L1-smoothness requirement for the memory-efficient variant holds broadly in practice.
