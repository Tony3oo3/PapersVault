## Problem

Most theoretical work on Transformer in-context learning (ICL) assumes fully supervised contexts with explicitly labeled pairs, yet in practice Transformers perform well even when labels are sparse or absent. This paper introduces in-context semi-supervised learning (IC-SSL), asking how a Transformer can leverage the geometric structure of many unlabeled contextual points—alongside a few labeled ones—to learn a robust, context-dependent representation that enables accurate predictions in low-label regimes.

## Input / Output

The model receives a context of n total points: m labeled pairs (x^(i), y^(i)) with binary labels y^(i) ∈ {0,1} and n−m unlabeled covariates x^(i) ∈ R^d, all fed as raw coordinate vectors into a single end-to-end Transformer. It outputs per-point prediction probabilities for the unlabeled labels and, implicitly, a context-dependent feature vector φ(x^(i)) for every point that depends on the full set of covariates (not just the individual point). In the ImageNet100 experiments, inputs are 4096-dim VGG-29 features; in image-manifold experiments, inputs are 1024-dim flattened 32×32 grayscale images scaled to [0, 0.001] to match synthetic-manifold distance scales.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage Transformer trained end-to-end with a cross-entropy loss on unlabeled samples. The first stage (TF_rep) performs in-context representation learning: a sub-module using RBF attention computes a discrete graph Laplacian from pairwise Euclidean distances (the RBF kernel naturally yields exp(−‖x^(i)−x^(j)‖²/2h) weights), and a second sub-module using linear attention implements block power iteration to extract the bottom-k Laplacian eigenvectors as context-dependent features φ. The second stage (TF_sup) performs categorical ICL by implementing functional gradient descent in its forward pass: self-attention layers execute the kernelized GD update f^(i)_{ℓ+1} = f^(i)_ℓ + Σ_j α[w_{y_j} − E(w|f^(j)_ℓ)]κ(φ^(i),φ^(j)), while MLP layers approximate the softmax expectation E(w|f). The two stages are instantiated within a single Transformer and jointly optimized, so the modular construction acts as a strong mechanistic inductive bias rather than a hard constraint, allowing the network to deviate from the strict two-stage algorithm when beneficial.

## Results

On ImageNet100 (3% labels, 5000 episodes), the proposed ORIG+E2E-ICL model achieves higher ICL accuracy and separation than a scale-matched 2-layer Transformer baseline, with the largest gaps at small training-set sizes; both outperform EIG+ICL. On the cylinder manifold (in-distribution), ORIG+E2E-ICL surpasses the strongest baseline (EIG+ICL) by 5–7 percentage points, reaching ~90% accuracy at ≥25% labels. On a 5-factor product manifold in R¹⁵, the end-to-end model retains an 8–10% margin over EIG+ICL across the full label spectrum. On Stable-Diffusion image manifolds (zero-shot OOD), the model achieves ~77% accuracy with 15% labels and >62% at just 3% labels, exceeding baselines by 10–20 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is a scale-matched 2-layer Transformer (~1.5M parameters) trained end-to-end following the MetaICL formulation (Min et al., 2022), where unlabeled samples receive a special unknown token. The proposed ORIG+E2E-ICL model consistently outperforms this baseline on ImageNet100, with the accuracy gap largest at small training-set sizes (<1000 episodes); the baseline undergoes a sharp phase transition around ~1200 episodes that coincides with an abrupt increase in its separation score. Against the spectral reference EIG+ICL (ground-truth Laplacian eigenvectors + ICL head), the end-to-end model wins by 5–7 points on the cylinder, 8–10 points on the product manifold, and 10–20 points on image manifolds. The logistic-regression baselines (EIG+LR, ORIG+RBF-LR) perform markedly worse, confirming that manifold-aware in-context representation learning is essential. Notably, three labels suffice for the proposed model to reach a correctness level that baselines cannot match even after seeing 10× more labels, highlighting a substantial data-efficiency advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three threads of Transformer ICL research: the RKHS-based functional gradient descent construction for categorical ICL (Wang et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2024), the in-context computation of graph Laplacian eigenvectors via linear attention (Cheng et al., 2025), and classical semi-supervised manifold learning via Laplacian Eigenmaps. The key departure is showing that a single end-to-end Transformer can perform both spectral representation learning (via RBF attention computing the discrete Laplacian, then linear attention implementing block power iteration) and in-context gradient descent in its forward pass, rather than treating eigenvector computation as an offline preprocessing step. This bridges the gap between graph-Transformer positional encodings and ICL, providing an explicit forward-pass mechanism for why depth matters in semi-supervised ICL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic reference model for understanding how standard Transformers exploit unlabeled context, with the phase-transition observation in baseline Transformers (sharp accuracy jump at ~1200 episodes correlating with separation score) offering a concrete diagnostic for when representation learning 'clicks.' The zero-shot transfer from synthetic 3D manifolds to Stable Diffusion image manifolds (77% accuracy at 15% labels, 62% at 3% labels) suggests the learned geometry-aware computation is modality-agnostic. However, the scope remains limited to small episode-style datasets (n=100) and the ImageNet100 experiment uses pre-extracted VGG features rather than raw pixels, leaving open whether the two-stage construction's inductive bias remains advantageous at scale or whether a sufficiently large end-to-end Transformer would subsume it without the explicit spectral scaffolding.
