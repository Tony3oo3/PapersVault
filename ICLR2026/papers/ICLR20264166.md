## Problem

Training computer-use agents requires massive amounts of precisely labeled GUI interaction trajectories (screenshots paired with exact click coordinates and typed strings), but manual annotation at the necessary scale is prohibitively expensive. Meanwhile, millions of publicly available screen-recorded tutorial videos implicitly contain this supervision—showing where users click, what they type, and how interfaces respond—but lack the structured action labels needed for training. The paper addresses how to automatically convert these passive, unlabeled video recordings into high-quality, structured training data without any human annotation.

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests raw screen-recorded videos (predominantly 720p+, ~10,000 hours from 50+ YouTube channels) and produces 1.52 million ReAct-style interaction steps formatted as (screenshot, inner monologue, action type, parameters) tuples spanning ~26 billion tokens. For the downstream agent, input is a natural-language instruction plus up to five recent screenshots and action/rationale history; output is the next GUI action as a structured tool call (e.g., left_click with (x,y) coordinates, or type with a literal string). Video preprocessing uses a YOLOv8x-based cursor detector at 1–2 fps to retain only segments where ≥80% of frames show a cursor for 6+ seconds, while event detection operates at 4 fps on 10-second clips and action parameterization uses a dynamic frame-rate policy (4–30 fps) to preserve fast interactions.

## Method

The core contribution is VIDEO2ACTION, an inverse dynamics module with two sequentially applied VLM stages (both Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, full-parameter SFT): (1) a dense action-event detector that performs prompt-free temporal grounding over raw video, emitting typed GUI interaction spans with tight start/end timestamps; and (2) an action-parameterization recognizer that takes each localized segment and predicts both the action type and its structured parameters (pointer coordinates, typed text, scroll direction). A GPT-5-based inner-monologue generator then produces step-level rationales conditioned on pre/post keyframes, action details, and surrounding ASR transcripts. The mined trajectories are used in a two-stage training recipe: Stage 1 is continued pretraining on 26B tokens of interleaved video-text sequences (loss masked to text only), followed by Stage 2 supervised fine-tuning on 8B tokens of clean human-annotated trajectories in a chat format. This decoupling—broad but noisy video supervision first, then clean SFT—stabilizes grounding before consolidating policy, and the long video trajectories (avg. 39 steps, 42% exceeding 20 steps) specifically enable test-time scaling that shorter SFT-only data cannot provide.

## Results

On OSWorld-Verified, the full VideoAgentTrek pipeline (Stage-1 video pretraining + Stage-2 SFT) achieves 15.78% task success at 50 steps, a 70% relative improvement over the SFT-only baseline (9.3%) and more than 3× the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (4.5%). On AgentNetBench, step success rate rises from 38.5% (base) to 69.3% (full method), a 5.2-point gain over SFT-only (64.1%). The method also enables test-time scaling: task success increases from 14.13% to 15.78% when the step budget expands from 20 to 50, whereas the SFT-only model remains flat at ~9.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external agent methods; instead it ablates its own two-stage training recipe against three internal baselines: the unmodified Qwen2.5-VL-7B base, SFT-only (Stage-2 on curated human data), and CPT-only (Stage-1 on video data without SFT). The full Stage-1+Stage-2 pipeline wins on every end-to-end benchmark: OSWorld-Verified (15.78% vs. 9.30% SFT-only, +70% relative), AgentNetBench (69.3% vs. 64.1%, +5.2 pp), and Windows Agent Arena (17.39% vs. 15.38%). The sole exception is OSWorld-G grounding accuracy, where SFT-only (31.56) slightly edges out the full method (30.50), suggesting that Stage-1 pseudo-label noise marginally degrades low-level pointer localization even as it substantially improves task-level planning. CPT-only (Stage-1 without SFT) drops grounding to 26.24, confirming that the video pretraining must be followed by clean SFT to consolidate grounding while retaining the long-horizon planning gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the inverse-dynamics-from-video paradigm established by VPT (Baker et al., 2022) in robotics to the GUI/computer-use agent domain, where prior work (AgentTrek, OpenCUA, AGUVIS) relied on human-annotated trajectories or programmatic synthesis. The two-stage Video2Action module—temporal event detection followed by action parameterization—repurposes VLM-based video grounding (Qwen2.5-VL) for a task previously addressed only by instrumented logging or manual annotation. The inner-monologue generation via GPT-5 extends ReAct-style trajectory construction (Yao et al., 2023) to the unlabeled-video setting, and the two-stage training recipe (continued pretraining on noisy video-derived data, then SFT on clean human data) follows the decoupling principle from AGUVIS and UI-TARS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pipeline demonstrates that 39K passive YouTube tutorials can yield 1.52M structured interaction steps at near-zero annotation cost, offering a scalable alternative to the prohibitively expensive manual trajectory collection that currently bottlenecks GUI agent research. The test-time scaling differential—where the video-pretrained model improves with additional step budget while the SFT-only model remains flat—suggests that long-horizon video exposure teaches planning and error-recovery capabilities that short-horizon SFT cannot instill, an insight with implications for how agent training data should be structured. Key limitations remain: the pipeline is restricted to English desktop UIs, relies on the VLM backbone for OCR quality, and the absolute success rates (15.8% on OSWorld-Verified) indicate substantial headroom; extending to mobile, multilingual, and OCR-enhanced settings is an open direction.
