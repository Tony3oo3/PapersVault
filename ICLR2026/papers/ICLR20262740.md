## Problem

Whether language models can effectively self-refine their own responses remains unresolved: prior studies tested refinement only on verifiable tasks (competition math, code) with simplified scaffolds, while real users pose open-ended queries and provide varying degrees of feedback. The emergence of reasoning models that exhibit self-reflection in their chains-of-thought further motivates a systematic evaluation, but no existing benchmark supports both self-refinement and guided refinement with fine-grained, controllable feedback across diverse domains.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance consists of a problem query (averaging 347 tokens of context passage where applicable) and the model's previous-turn response; in guided refinement, the failed checklist items are heuristically rephrased from interrogative form ("Does the response accurately…?") into imperative feedback ("The response should accurately…") and appended to the next user turn. The evaluator LM (GPT-4.1, temperature 0.0) returns a binary Yes/No per checklist item (average 9.9 items, max 23), from which two scores are derived: Acc_t (fraction of items satisfied) and Pass_t (1 only if all items are satisfied, 0 otherwise). Evaluation runs for up to 5 turns, and in the self-refinement setting the model must also decide whether to terminate or continue.

## Method

REFINEBENCH introduces a unified checklist-based evaluation framework that supports three refinement settings—self-refinement (no feedback), fully guided refinement (all failed items provided), and partially guided refinement (a configurable ratio of failed items provided, with the model expected to infer the rest). Checklists are generated by prompting multiple LMs (GPT-4o, GPT-4.1, Claude-Sonnet-3.7) with the problem and reference answer, then validated via backtranslation (filtering items the evaluator LM rejects on the reference answer; only 1.1% removed) and human expert review (96.1% appropriateness across 854 items). The benchmark comprises 1,000 problems across 11 domains (Math 32%, Humanities/Social Science 19%, Law 14%, etc.) and 239 subjects, covering both verifiable (exact-match) and non-verifiable (free-form) tasks, with non-textual elements (plots, tables) converted to textual descriptions. By varying the feedback ratio and domain, the framework isolates whether models fail at identifying what to fix versus applying the correction once identified—a distinction prior benchmarks could not capture.

## Results

In the self-refinement setting, even the strongest frontier LMs achieve modest Pass_5 scores: Gemini-2.5-Pro reaches 31.3% (+1.8% over 5 turns) and GPT-5 reaches 29.1% (+1.7%), while most models show near-zero or negative improvement (e.g., DeepSeek-R1 declines by −0.1%, LLaMA-4-Maverick by −1.8%). In the guided refinement setting, large open-weight LMs (>70B) and proprietary LMs achieve near-perfect scores by turn 5: Claude-Opus-4.1 reaches 98.4% (+79.7%), o3-mini reaches 98.2% (+78.7%), and GPT-4.1 reaches 95.5% (+72.2%), while smaller models (<8B) show far less improvement (e.g., LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct only +28.7%). The gap between self- and guided-refinement is stark: providing explicit checklist feedback transforms a 31% ceiling into a 95%+ ceiling for capable models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Across 34 models, the paper reveals a clear hierarchy. In self-refinement, proprietary reasoning models (Gemini-2.5-Pro at 31.3%, GPT-5 at 29.1%, o4-mini at 22.5%) outperform open-source reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1 at 7.9%, Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking at 17.4%) and instruction-tuned models (LLaMA-3.1-405B at 5.8%, GPT-4o at 6.9%), but all remain below 32%. Notably, DeepSeek-R1 and its distilled variants show declining self-refinement trends (−0.1% to −2.5%), attributed to reduced reasoning token counts and diminished self-correction behavior in later turns. In guided refinement, the ranking shifts: Claude-Opus-4.1 (98.4%) and o3-mini (98.2%) lead, followed by Claude-Sonnet-4 (96.6%), o4-mini (96.4%), and GPT-4.1 (95.5%), while GPT-5 (79.0%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (94.7%) trail despite their strong self-refinement baselines. Smaller open-weight models (<8B) struggle in both settings, with LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct reaching only 30.1% under guided refinement versus 98.4% for Claude-Opus-4.1, highlighting a significant capability gap that scales with model size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the refinement evaluation line from Self-Correct (Welleck et al., 2023) and Self-Refine (Madaan et al., 2023) through the critical analysis of Huang et al. (2024), which showed LMs cannot self-correct reasoning without external feedback. Unlike prior refinement benchmarks (CriticBench, CriticEval, RealCritic) that treat refinement as a proxy for critique quality and focus on verifiable tasks, REFINEBENCH introduces a unified checklist-based multi-turn framework covering both self-refinement and guided refinement across 11 domains including free-form generation. The evaluation pipeline builds on the LLM-as-an-Interviewer codebase (Kim et al., 2024a), and the checklist creation draws on the CheckEval framework (Lee et al., 2025f) and checklist-based alignment work (Viswanathan et al., 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The stark contrast between self-refinement (best: 31.3% for Gemini 2.5 Pro) and guided refinement (best: 98.4% for Claude-Opus-4.1) reveals that the core bottleneck is not the ability to apply corrections but the ability to identify what needs fixing—a diagnostic that prior benchmarks could not isolate. The finding that DeepSeek-R1, despite its claimed self-verification training, shows decreasing performance across turns challenges the assumption that RL-trained reasoning models inherently self-correct. Open questions include whether the Law-domain self-refinement success generalizes, whether longer thinking or new scaffolding can close the self-refinement gap, and how to design training objectives that teach models to identify their own errors without explicit feedback. As a 1,000-problem, 11-domain benchmark with fine-grained feedback control, REFINEBENCH provides a practical testbed for tracking progress on this capability.
