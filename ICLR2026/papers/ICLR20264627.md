## Problem

Standard open-domain QA benchmarks assume a single gold answer per question, but a substantial fraction of questions—especially multi-hop ones—admit multiple equally valid answers (27.6% in MuSiQue). Current RL training pipelines reward only the reference answer and implicitly penalize alternatives, delivering misleading reward signals that systematically understate model capability. Existing ambiguity-handling efforts rely on costly manual annotation that does not scale to multi-hop datasets.

## Input / Output

The model receives a single open-domain QA question and iteratively queries a Wikipedia search index (2018 dump, 100-word chunks, E5 embeddings, FAISS top-5 retrieval) through a tool-call interface. Unlike single-answer baselines, A² SEARCH produces a reasoning trajectory that may terminate with multiple answers within a single rollout, with the number of answers adapting to the question's ambiguity level (averaging 1.51 for 7B and 1.23 for 3B). Tool-response tokens are masked from the policy loss during training since they originate from the external retriever.

## Method

The framework has two components. First, an annotation-free data pipeline automatically mines alternative answers: five search models (ReSearch-7B/32B, Search-R1-7B/14B/32B) generate 16 trajectories per question; trajectories matching the reference or from models that fail entirely are filtered out; four proprietary LLM verifiers (Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet, OpenAI o3/o4-mini) apply majority voting (η=3) to confirm evidence support; and an LLM clustering step groups semantically equivalent answers. This yields alternative answers for ~19% of 49,938 training questions. Second, the model is trained end-to-end with GRPO using an AnsF1 outcome reward R = 1 − α(1 − AnsF1) (α=0.4) that balances precision and recall across multiple reference answers, encouraging the model to produce all valid answers when evidence warrants it while penalizing over-generation.

## Results

On four multi-hop QA benchmarks (HotpotQA, 2Wiki, MuSiQue, Bamboogle), A²SEARCH-7B achieves a macro-average AnsF1@1 of 48.4% and Recall@1 of 51.2% with a single greedy rollout, outperforming the substantially larger ReSearch-32B (46.2% AnsF1@1) and all 7B baselines. On four general QA benchmarks (NQ, TriviaQA, PopQA, AmbigQA), A²SEARCH-7B reaches 55.0% AnsF1@1 and 55.8% Recall@1. Even the smaller A²SEARCH-3B model achieves 43.1% AnsF1@1 on multi-hop and 49.9% on general QA, surpassing all 3B baselines. The model generates an average of 1.51 answers per question (7B) and 1.23 (3B), adapting its output to the ambiguity level of each dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

A²SEARCH-7B (48.4% AnsF1@1) outperforms ReSearch-32B (46.2%) on multi-hop benchmarks despite being 4.5× smaller, and also beats it on general QA (55.0% vs 54.1%). Against same-size 7B RL baselines, A²SEARCH-7B leads by a wide margin: +9.4 points over AFM-MHQ-7B (39.0%), +9.1 over ReSearch-7B (39.3%), and +12.0 over Search-R1-7B (36.4%) on multi-hop AnsF1@1. The prompt-based baselines (IRCoT-7B at 20.9%, Iter-RetGen-7B at 23.2%) trail by 25+ points, confirming the advantage of RL-trained agentic search. Notably, A²SEARCH-7B's single-rollout Recall@1 (51.2%) approaches ReSearch-32B's three-rollout Recall@3 (54.9%), indicating that ambiguity-aware training recovers most of the diversity that multi-sampling provides. The SinSearch-7B ablation (44.4%), trained identically but without alternative answers, confirms that the ambiguity-aware data pipeline contributes +4.0 points over single-answer training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the agentic RL search paradigm established by Search-R1 and ReSearch, which train LLMs to interleave reasoning with Wikipedia retrieval via GRPO. The key departure is addressing a blind spot in all prior RL-based QA work: the assumption of a single gold answer. While AmbigQA and ASQA previously handled ambiguity through manual annotation limited to single-hop questions, A²SEARCH introduces an automated four-step pipeline (sampling, filtering, verification, grouping) that scales ambiguity detection to multi-hop datasets and feeds alternative answers directly into the RL reward via an AnsF1 metric. The training framework itself follows the GRPO formulation from DeepSeekMath and the entropy-control techniques from Skywork-OR1, but the multi-answer reward design and the evidence-verified data construction are novel contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 27.6% of MuSiQue training examples admit multiple valid answers suggests that standard single-answer RL training has been systematically providing misleading reward signals, understating true model capability. The annotation-free pipeline and AnsF1 reward offer a practical, scalable path to correcting this without the cost of manual re-annotation, and the open-sourcing of code, data, and weights makes the approach immediately adoptable. Open questions include whether the ambiguity taxonomy generalizes beyond Wikipedia-based QA, whether the reliance on strong proprietary verifiers (even with open alternatives shown) limits reproducibility at scale, and whether evaluation protocols in the broader QA community will shift to multi-answer metrics. The paper also leaves open whether the gains are purely from better training signals or partly from the increased effective training data (more answers per question).
