## Problem

Spine disorders require level-aware, multimodal reasoning across X-ray, CT, and MRI to localize pathology at specific vertebral levels, yet AI progress is constrained by the absence of traceable, clinically-grounded instruction data and standardized spine-specific benchmarks. Existing spine datasets target low-level perception tasks (segmentation, binary classification) and fail to capture the full clinical decision-making workflow from diagnosis through treatment planning and prognosis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260511/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multimodal inputs including X-ray, CT, and MRI images (standardized to 25 slices per case for CT/MRI) alongside text queries and de-identified clinical case records. Outputs span four task types: multiple-choice answers, open-ended clinical reasoning, multi-turn doctor-patient consultation dialogues, and structured diagnostic reports organized into six sections (imaging findings, AI-assisted diagnosis, bifurcated patient/physician treatment recommendations, risk-prognosis assessment, postoperative management, and diagnostic rationale with disclaimer). Report outputs are scored on a 10-dimension rubric (each 1–5 points) covering imaging accuracy, diagnostic correctness, guideline consistency, technical feasibility, and reasoning coherence.

## Method

SpineMed-450k is built through a clinician-in-the-loop pipeline: PaddleOCR extracts text and layout from PDFs, a novel Picture Context Matching algorithm binds figures to surrounding paragraphs via caption-pattern regex, and a two-stage LLM process (draft generation followed by revision with explicit prompts) produces 450k+ instruction instances from textbooks, guidelines, open datasets (Spark, VerSe), Europe PMC case reports, and ~1,000 de-identified hospital cases. SpineBench (487 MCQs + 87 report prompts) is validated by 17 board-certified orthopedic surgeons split into three independent review groups. SpineGPT fine-tunes Qwen2.5-VL-7B via a three-stage curriculum: (1) general medical + non-spine orthopedic data, (2) spine-specific QA with long reasoning chains, and (3) multi-turn dialogue and report generation with extended 49k-token context, using DeepSpeed Zero2→Zero3 and the ms-swift framework on 8×A100 GPUs.

## Results

On SpineBench, SpineGPT (7B) achieves an overall average of 87.44%, with 89.46% on text-only multiple-choice QA, 84.46% on multimodal image QA, and 87.24/100 on medical report generation. This surpasses all open-source LVLMs by at least 4.18 points and exceeds GPT-4o (81.60%) by 5.84 points, while trailing only Gemini-2.5-Pro (89.23%) by 1.79 points despite using less than 7% of its parameters. Notably, SpineGPT's text-only QA score (89.46%) exceeds every evaluated model, including GPT-5 (87.41%) and GPT-5-mini (89.12%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpineGPT (7B) outperforms all open-source LVLMs on SpineBench, beating the strongest open-source model GLM-4.5V (21B) by 4.18 points (87.44% vs. 83.26%) and the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B by 7.56 points (87.44% vs. 79.88%). Among proprietary models, SpineGPT surpasses GPT-4o (81.60%) by 5.84 points and Claude-4 (80.28%) by 7.16 points, and narrowly trails GPT-5-mini (87.01%) by 0.43 points while exceeding GPT-5 (85.54%) by 1.90 points. The only model that outperforms SpineGPT overall is Gemini-2.5-Pro (89.23%), a gap of just 1.79 points despite Gemini using over 100B parameters versus SpineGPT's 7B. On text-only QA specifically, SpineGPT (89.46%) leads all models including GPT-5-mini (89.12%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (88.44%). However, on medical report generation, SpineGPT (87.24/100) lags behind top proprietary models like GPT-5-mini (93.56) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (93.32), indicating that while its diagnostic reasoning is strong, its structured report writing still has room for improvement relative to the largest proprietary systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of medical instruction tuning and spine-specific clinical reasoning. It departs from prior spine datasets (VerSe, RSNA LumbarDISC, BUU-LSPINE, Spark) that target single-modality perception tasks—segmentation, detection, or binary classification—by introducing the first large-scale, multi-modal instruction corpus (450k instances) designed for full-workflow clinical reasoning spanning diagnosis, treatment planning, and prognosis. Methodologically, it extends the generalist-to-specialist LVLM trend (LLaVA-Med, PMC-LLaMA, MedGemma) with a clinician-in-the-loop curation pipeline and a three-stage curriculum learning strategy on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, but does not build on a specific prior spine-reasoning framework, as none existed at the required scale and cognitive depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Spine disorders affect 619 million people globally, and the paper addresses a concrete gap: the absence of level-aware, multi-modal instruction data and a standardized benchmark for evaluating AI in spine clinical workflows. The practical finding that a 7B model fine-tuned on SpineMed-450k achieves ~98% of Gemini-2.5-Pro's performance is significant for hospital deployment under data-privacy constraints (local inference within firewalls). Key limitations include the model's 7B scale, the absence of reinforcement learning, and evaluation primarily on the authors' own benchmark; open questions include whether these gains transfer to other orthopedic subspecialties, whether RL or larger backbones close the remaining gap to proprietary models, and whether the clinician-in-the-loop pipeline generalizes beyond spine.
