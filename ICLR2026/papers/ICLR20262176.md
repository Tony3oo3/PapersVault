## Problem

Aesthetic poster generation demands simultaneous precision in text rendering, abstract artistic content, striking layouts, and holistic stylistic harmony—requirements that existing modular pipelines (VLM layout planner + separate background generation) break by decoupling these elements, and that simpler end-to-end design generators (greeting cards, product ads) do not address. The field also lacks large-scale, purpose-built datasets for aesthetic posters, further constraining fully generative solutions.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single free-form text prompt describing the desired poster content, style, and textual elements. At inference, the prompt is first enriched by a Qwen3-based MLLM (Magic Prompter) that appends detailed aesthetic cues to guide layout and content, then fed into the fine-tuned Flux-dev backbone to produce a complete poster image in one pass. An optional VLM-driven critique loop can iteratively refine the output by injecting structured textual reflections and VAE-encoded image feedback into the conditioning branch.

## Method

PosterCraft applies a four-stage cascaded training pipeline to a standard Flux-dev diffusion backbone without any architectural modifications. Stage 1 fine-tunes on 2M synthetic text-rendering samples (Text-Render-2M) with flow-matching loss to establish stroke-level text fidelity. Stage 2 performs region-aware supervised fine-tuning on 100K curated posters (HQ-Poster-100K) using a per-pixel weight map that assigns 0.6 to large text, 0.2 to small text, and 1.0 to non-text regions, balancing text accuracy against overall aesthetic style. Stage 3 applies Direct Preference Optimization with best-of-n selection over 6K preference pairs (Poster-Preference-100K) scored by HPSv2 plus Gemini-verified text accuracy, injecting a unified aesthetic-text reward signal via LoRA (rank 64). Stage 4 fine-tunes with joint vision-language conditioning (LoRA rank 128) where T5-encoded VLM reflections and VAE-encoded image feedback are concatenated with the prompt embedding, enabling iterative multimodal refinement. Each stage is supported by a fully automated dataset-construction pipeline, and the entire workflow is demonstrated to be architecture-agnostic via transfer to SD3.5.

## Results

On the primary text-quality benchmark (100 aesthetic poster prompts, 300 images), PosterCraft achieves Text Recall 0.787, Text F-score 0.774, and Text Accuracy 0.735, surpassing all open-source baselines and the commercial Ideogram-v2 (0.711/0.685/0.680) while remaining within ~1% of the top closed-source model Gemini2.0-Flash-Gen (0.798/0.786/0.746). On HPSv3 aesthetic scoring, PosterCraft attains 10.7402, the highest among all compared models including Gemini2.0-Flash-Gen (10.5251) and Flux1.dev (10.4066). On the general text-rendering benchmark CVTG-2K, PosterCraft reaches Word Accuracy 0.5814 and NED 0.7581, outperforming FLUX.1-dev, AnyText, TextDiffuser-2, RAG-Diffusion, and 3DIS, though trailing the specialized TextCrafter (0.7370/0.8679). On LongText-Bench (English), PosterCraft scores 0.631, second only to X-Omni (0.900) and ahead of FLUX.1-dev (0.607), HiDream-I1-Full (0.543), and OmniGen2 (0.561).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PosterCraft outperforms all open-source baselines on the primary text-quality metrics: it beats Flux1.dev (its own backbone) by +0.064 recall and +0.068 accuracy, surpasses BizGen (0.689/0.661/0.641) and SD3.5 (0.565/0.542/0.497) by wide margins, and dramatically exceeds poster-specific generators PosterMaker (0.522/0.488/0.467) and Playground-v2.5 (0.157/0.146/0.132). Against the commercial Ideogram-v2, PosterCraft wins on recall (+0.076) and F-score (+0.089) but trails slightly on accuracy (0.735 vs. 0.680 — actually PosterCraft wins here too by +0.055). The only model that edges out PosterCraft on text metrics is Gemini2.0-Flash-Gen, which leads by a narrow 0.011 recall and 0.011 F-score gap. On HPSv3 aesthetic scoring, PosterCraft (10.7402) actually surpasses Gemini2.0-Flash-Gen (10.5251) and all other models, indicating superior holistic visual appeal. On the general CVTG-2K benchmark, PosterCraft (0.5814 Word Acc.) trails the specialized TextCrafter (0.7370) but beats all other open-source text-rendering methods; combining PosterCraft with TextCrafter's training-free optimizer further improves to 0.7544. On LongText-Bench, PosterCraft (0.631) is second only to the much larger X-Omni (0.900) and outperforms FLUX.1-dev, HiDream-I1-Full, OmniGen2, and all poster-specific baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

PosterCraft departs from the dominant modular paradigm in aesthetic poster generation (e.g., PosterLlama, PosterLLaVA, POSTA), where a VLM plans layout and a separate diffusion model renders the background. Instead, it argues that a single diffusion backbone (Flux-dev) can be upgraded through a four-stage cascaded training workflow—text-rendering SFT, region-aware poster fine-tuning, DPO-based aesthetic-text preference optimization, and VLM-driven reflection—without any architectural modification. The DPO objective is adapted from Rafailov et al. (2023) to the diffusion setting via ELBO over the denoising chain, and the image-level feedback injection draws on OmniControl (Tan et al., 2024). The paper also extends beyond product-centric or greeting-card design generation (PosterMaker, BizGen, DesignDiffusion) to full aesthetic posters with abstract artistic content and holistic stylistic coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a carefully staged, architecture-agnostic training recipe can close much of the gap between open-source diffusion models and leading commercial systems (Gemini2.0-Flash-Gen) for aesthetic poster generation, validated on both Flux-dev and SD3.5. The four constructed datasets (Text-Render-2M, HQ-Poster-100K, Poster-Preference-100K, Poster-Reflect-120K) provide reusable resources for the community. Open questions include whether the workflow transfers to multilingual typography, product-poster scenarios with explicit layout hints, and stronger future backbones; the authors also note that the base model's pretraining coverage sets an upper bound that the pipeline cannot fully overcome.
