## Problem

Existing video understanding benchmarks include videos from multiple domains but with inconsistent semantic spaces, making it impossible to isolate and fairly measure a model's generalization to visual distribution shifts. There is no dedicated dataset that preserves cross-domain semantic consistency while introducing realistic domain shifts (visual style, viewpoint, environmental condition), leaving the domain generalization capability of video understanding models—especially fine-tuned LVLMs—largely unevaluated.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262212/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample pairs a video clip (≤10 min for training, longer for testing) with a text question from one of four categories (Action Recognition, Attribute Identification, Object Identification, Temporal Understanding). For multiple-choice evaluation, the model receives the video plus the question and six candidate options, outputting a single letter; for open-ended evaluation, it generates a free-form answer scored on a 10-point scale (5 for accuracy/sequence correctness + 5 for relevance) by an automated LLM judge. The 11 domains span cartoon, game, movie/TV, virtual, egocentric, surveillance, shaky, foggy, night, rainy, and snowy, all constrained to 37 predefined daily human activities to ensure semantic consistency.

## Method

VUDG is constructed via a progressive multi-expert annotation framework that cascades distinct large models to mitigate self-reinforcement bias: Qwen2.5-VL-7B selects domain-appropriate videos matching a fixed activity list, Gemini-2.5-Flash generates initial open-ended QA pairs, DeepSeek-V3 converts them into six-option multiple-choice questions, and Gemini-2.5-Pro performs automated quality screening before mandatory human expert review. The dataset supports three DG protocols—multi-source (Leave-One-Domain-Out), single-source (Leave-But-One-Domain-Out), and zero-shot—with training using LoRA (rank 128, alpha 256) for LVLMs and full fine-tuning for non-LLM VideoQA baselines. A key design choice is collecting training and testing data from strictly disjoint sources (open-source dataset training splits vs. test splits and online platforms) to prevent data leakage in LVLM evaluation.

## Results

In zero-shot evaluation on VUDG's 11 domains, Qwen2.5VL-7B achieves the highest domain-average accuracy of 72.1% on multiple-choice QA, followed by VideoLLaMA3-7B (65.1%), GPT-4o (64.6%), and Tarsier2-7B (62.8%). Under multi-source domain generalization (trained on 10 domains, tested on the held-out 11th), VideoLLaMA2-7B leads with 66.9% D-Avg, while non-LLM baselines HBI and EMCL4QA collapse to near-random levels (17.3% and 18.1% respectively). Full fine-tuning across all 11 domains yields an upper bound of ~68.8–68.9% D-Avg, revealing that even the best fine-tuned models lose 15–20 percentage points when evaluated under domain shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the 11 zero-shot LVLMs evaluated, Qwen2.5VL-7B (72.1% D-Avg) outperforms the next-best open model VideoLLaMA3-7B (65.1%) by 7.0 pp and the closed-source GPT-4o (64.6%) by 7.5 pp, indicating that massive pre-training alone does not fully mitigate domain-shift challenges. Tarsier2-7B (62.8%) trails Qwen2.5VL-7B by 9.3 pp, with its weakest performance on Environmental Condition domains (60.3% avg). A clear generational gap separates earlier models: VideoLLaMA2-7B (33.4%), Video-LLaVA-7B (23.5%), and VideoChat2-7B (14.0%) are all far below the 50% threshold, while Video-ChatGPT-7B (13.6%) and MiniGPT4-Video (13.8%) perform at or near the 6-option random baseline (16.7%). In the multi-source DG setting, non-LLM VideoQA methods HBI (17.3%) and EMCL4QA (18.1%) are effectively at chance level, whereas LLM-based VideoLLaMA2-7B (66.9%) and Qwen2.5VL-3B (61.4%) retain meaningful generalization—yet both still fall 2–7 pp short of their full-fine-tuning upper bounds, confirming that domain shift remains an unsolved challenge even for state-of-the-art LVLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by identifying a gap between two existing research threads: domain generalization in video tasks (VideoDG, Ani-GIFs, ARGO1M, MDVAD) which focuses on classification or anomaly detection, and multi-domain video understanding benchmarks (VideoVista, Video-MME, MVBench) which include diverse domains but with inconsistent semantic spaces that confound domain-shift evaluation with semantic differences. VUDG departs from both by maintaining a fixed semantic space (37 predefined daily activities) across 11 domains that vary only in visual style, viewpoint, and environmental conditions, thereby isolating the domain-shift variable. The progressive multi-expert annotation framework, which cascades distinct models (Gemini-2.5-Flash → DeepSeek-V3 → Gemini-2.5-Pro) to avoid self-reinforcement bias, is a methodological departure from prior single-model or purely manual annotation pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VUDG provides the first dedicated benchmark for evaluating domain generalization in video understanding, a setting that aligns with the rapid deployment of LVLMs in real-world applications where distribution shifts are inevitable. The finding that even the strongest zero-shot model (Qwen2.5VL-7B at 72.1%) shows inconsistent cross-domain performance, and that fine-tuned models drop 15+ percentage points under single-domain DG, quantifies a robustness gap that motivates future work on more generalizable training strategies. Key limitations include the focus on visual shifts only (no audio or textual domain shifts in the main evaluation), a moderate dataset size (~7.9K videos), and the reliance on LLM-based annotation that, despite multi-model cascading and human review, may still carry residual biases. Open questions include whether the observed degradation patterns hold for larger or differently-architected models, and how to extend the benchmark to multimodal (audio-visual) domain generalization.
