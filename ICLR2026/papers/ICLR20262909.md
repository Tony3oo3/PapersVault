## Problem

Current sub-quadratic sequence models (Mamba-2, Gated DeltaNet) face three interrelated limitations: they sacrifice model quality for algorithmic efficiency, they lack state-tracking capabilities (failing even on parity), and their theoretically linear decoding is hardware-inefficient due to low arithmetic intensity, leaving GPU compute idle during the memory-bound state update. Mamba-3 addresses all three gaps from an inference-first, SSM-centric design perspective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262909/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a token sequence (text, tokenized with Llama-3.1) and produces next-token predictions via a fixed-size hidden state of dimension N (typically 64 or 128) that is updated at each timestep. In the MIMO variant, the state update uses a matrix-matrix product (rank-R) rather than an outer product, increasing per-step FLOPs by a factor of R without growing the state size. Training uses 100B FineWeb-Edu tokens at 2K context length; evaluation spans perplexity, downstream accuracy, retrieval (NIAH, SQuAD, TQA), and synthetic state-tracking tasks.

## Method

Mamba-3 introduces three SSM-rooted modifications to Mamba-2's recurrence. First, an exponential-trapezoidal discretization replaces the first-order Euler rule with a second-order convex combination of interval endpoints, which is equivalent to a data-dependent width-2 convolution on the state-input and, combined with learnable B/C biases, obviates the short causal convolution used in prior models. Second, the SSM is formulated as complex-valued, which under discretization yields a real SSM whose transition matrix is a block-diagonal of 2×2 data-dependent rotation matrices—equivalent to applying data-dependent RoPE to the B and C projections—recovering state-tracking ability (parity, modular arithmetic) that real-valued SSMs lack. Third, a MIMO formulation replaces the outer-product state update with a matrix-matrix product, increasing decoding arithmetic intensity by up to 4× at fixed state size, thereby overlaying compute with memory I/O and improving hardware utilization without increasing decode latency.

## Results

At the 1.5B scale, Mamba-3 (MIMO) achieves 57.6% average downstream accuracy and 10.24 FineWeb-Edu perplexity, improving by +2.2 points over Transformer, +1.9 over Mamba-2, and +1.8 over GDN. Mamba-3 (SISO) achieves 56.4% average accuracy, outperforming the next-best model (GDN at 55.8%) by +0.6 points. Across state-size experiments, Mamba-3 (MIMO) with state size 64 matches the perplexity of Mamba-2 with state size 128, achieving equivalent language modeling quality at half the decode latency. On formal language tasks, Mamba-3 solves Parity (100%) and Modular Arithmetic without brackets (98.5%), while Mamba-2 and Mamba-3 without RoPE perform at or near random guessing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mamba-3 (SISO) consistently outperforms Mamba-2 across all four model scales on average downstream accuracy (e.g., 56.4% vs. 55.7% at 1.5B) and FineWeb-Edu perplexity (10.35 vs. 10.47), while also beating GDN by +0.6 points at 1.5B. Mamba-3 (MIMO) extends these gains substantially, reaching 57.6% average accuracy at 1.5B—surpassing GDN by +1.8 points, Mamba-2 by +1.9, and Transformer by +2.2. On retrieval tasks (Table 2), Mamba-3 (SISO) matches or exceeds Mamba-2 on synthetic NIAH at 2K context (88.2% vs. 62.0% for NIAH-Single-1 at 4K) but both lag behind the Transformer baseline on real-world information extraction (SWDE, FDA). In hybrid configurations, Mamba-3 (SISO) with pre-gate grouped RMSNorm achieves near-perfect NIAH scores (100% at 1K and 2K, 96% at 4K for NIAH-Single-2), outperforming Mamba-2 hybrid (which drops to 0% at 4K). On state-tracking tasks, Mamba-3 matches GDN[-1,1] on Parity (100%) and approaches it on Modular Arithmetic with brackets (87.75% vs. 93.50%), while Mamba-2 fails entirely (0.90% on Parity). Mamba-3 (SISO) kernels are faster than both Mamba-2 and GDN at the standard bf16, d_state=128 setting (0.156 ms vs. 0.203 ms and 0.257 ms respectively), and Mamba-3 (MIMO) incurs only marginal additional decode latency (0.179 ms) while increasing FLOPs by up to 4×.

## Relation to Prior Work

Mamba-3 extends the Mamba-2 (Dao & Gu, 2024) line of work by making three SSM-principled modifications: formalizing Mamba-1/2's heuristic discretization as 'exponential-Euler' and generalizing it to a more expressive 'exponential-trapezoidal' rule; reintroducing complex-valued state dynamics (dropped from Mamba-1/2 for efficiency) via a data-dependent RoPE trick that recovers state-tracking capabilities; and adopting a MIMO formulation motivated by inference-time arithmetic intensity rather than the training-speed motivation that drove S5's earlier MIMO design. The paper explicitly positions these changes as arising naturally from the SSM viewpoint but not from the linear-attention or test-time-regression perspectives that dominate recent work, and it benchmarks against Gated DeltaNet (Yang et al., 2025) and Transformer baselines as the current Pareto frontier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: Mamba-3 advances the performance-efficiency frontier for sub-quadratic sequence models, with the MIMO variant matching Mamba-2's perplexity at half the state size and the complex-valued update recovering state-tracking (parity, modular arithmetic) that Mamba-2 fundamentally cannot express. The finding that BC bias plus exponential-trapezoidal discretization obviates the short causal convolution long considered essential in recurrent architectures challenges a widely-held design assumption. Open questions include whether MIMO's R-fold training overhead is acceptable at frontier scale, the optimal norm configuration for hybrid Mamba-3/attention architectures (which the authors predict will dominate deployment), and whether the retrieval weaknesses of fixed-state models can be further mitigated without reverting to quadratic attention.
