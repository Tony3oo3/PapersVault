## Problem

Existing high-resolution metric depth estimation frameworks like PatchRefiner V1 rely on heavyweight pretrained depth models (e.g., ZoeDepth, DepthAnything) in both the coarse and refiner branches, requiring at least 16 forward passes per image and making end-to-end training infeasible due to GPU memory constraints. This results in inference times exceeding one second and forces suboptimal stage-wise training. The paper addresses how to achieve fast, lightweight high-resolution depth estimation while maintaining or improving accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264000/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single high-resolution RGB image (4K at 2160×3840 for synthetic evaluation, 1024×2048 for real-domain CityScapes) and produces a full-resolution metric depth map. The input is split into overlapping patches (540×960 or 256×512 depending on dataset); a coarse branch processes a downsampled global view while the refiner branch processes individual patches. The final depth is computed as the sum of the coarse depth (cropped to the patch region) and a residual depth map predicted by the refiner, yielding a consistent high-resolution output.

## Method

PRV2 replaces the heavy pretrained depth model in the refiner branch with lightweight encoders (MobileNetV4-Small, EfficientNet-B5, or ConvNeXt-Large), reducing parameters by up to 9.2× and enabling end-to-end training. To compensate for the loss of depth-aligned features, a Coarse-to-Fine (C2F) module with Guided Denoising Units (GDUs) processes refiner features bottom-to-top, using coarse-branch features as guidance to produce a sigmoid weight map that element-wise filters noisy shortcut features. A Noisy Pretraining strategy pretrains the entire refiner branch (encoder + C2F + F2C) by replacing coarse features with random N(0,1) noise, forcing the network to learn depth-relevant representations without guidance. For synthetic-to-real transfer, a local Scale-and-Shift Invariant Gradient Matching (local SSIGM) loss computes gradient residuals within randomly sampled local windows after per-window scale-and-shift alignment, improving boundary delineation by 25.1% over PRV1 while preserving scale accuracy.

## Results

On UnrealStereo4K (4K synthetic), PRV2_C (ConvNext-Large refiner) achieves a new SoTA RMSE of 0.884 and δ1 of 98.863%, surpassing PRV1 (RMSE 0.892) while using 1.5× fewer parameters (245.8M vs. 369.0M) and running 2.3× faster (0.62s vs. 1.45s). The lightweight PRV2_M (MobileNetV4-Small) improves RMSE by 22.2% over the ZoeDepth coarse baseline (1.003 vs. 1.289) while being 9.2× smaller and 10.7× faster than PatchFusion. On CityScapes (real domain), the local SSIGM loss yields a +25.1% boundary F1 gain over PRV1 (36.54 vs. ~29.2) while maintaining comparable scale RMSE (8.527 vs. 8.533).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the prior SoTA PatchRefiner (PRV1), PRV2_C wins on RMSE (0.884 vs. 0.892), δ1 (98.863% vs. 98.821%), and all other metrics while being 1.5× smaller (245.8M vs. 369.0M) and 2.3× faster (0.62s vs. 1.45s). Against PatchFusion (PF), even the smallest PRV2_M variant achieves a lower RMSE (1.003 vs. 1.088) with 9.2× fewer parameters and 10.7× faster inference. On CityScapes, the local SSIGM loss improves boundary F1 by 25.1% over PRV1's ranking+SSI losses (36.54 vs. ~29.2) at essentially identical scale RMSE. When stacked on top of DepthPro (ICLR 2025), PRV2_M reduces UnrealStereo4K RMSE from 1.285 to 0.824 and CityScapes boundary F1 from 29.46 to 37.51, confirming complementarity with strong coarse models. Against the weaker DenseDepth baseline, PRV2_M cuts RMSE from 2.552 to 1.898 (−25.6%). Finally, compared to a Metric3D-pretrained ConvNext encoder, the Noisy Pretraining strategy achieves 5.1% lower RMSE (0.883 vs. 0.931), validating that joint pretraining of C2F/F2C modules matters beyond encoder initialization alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct successor to PatchRefiner (PRV1, ECCV 2024) and PatchFusion (CVPR 2024), inheriting the tile-based dual-branch architecture and the Fine-to-Coarse (F2C) fusion module. The key conceptual departure is replacing the heavy pretrained depth backbone (ZoeDepth, DepthAnything) in the refiner branch with lightweight encoders (MobileNetV4, EfficientNet-B5, ConvNeXt-Large), which necessitates the novel Coarse-to-Fine (C2F) module with Guided Denoising Units and a Noisy Pretraining strategy to compensate for the lost depth-aligned feature representation. The local SSIGM loss extends the gradient-matching formulation from Li & Snavely (2018) and Ranftl et al. (2022) by computing it within randomly sampled local windows against pseudo-labels, rather than globally, to better transfer high-frequency structure from synthetic to real domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: PRV2 achieves comparable or superior accuracy to prior SOTA while using up to 9.2× fewer parameters and running 10.7× faster, and it enables end-to-end training that was infeasible with the heavier PRV1 framework. The finding that a 47M-parameter lightweight encoder with proper denoising and pretraining can match a 369M-parameter pretrained depth model in the refiner role challenges the implicit assumption that high-capacity depth-pretrained backbones are necessary for fine-grained refinement. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly zero-shot settings, whether joint multi-domain training can further improve generalization, and whether the coarse branch remains the quality ceiling for the overall system.
