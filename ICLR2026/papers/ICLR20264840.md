## Problem

Existing Table-as-Multimodality approaches statically process both text and image views for every table-query pair, introducing redundancy and conflicting signals that degrade performance, while also requiring costly fine-tuning of large MLLM backbones. No prior method dynamically selects which modality (or combination) to use per instance, nor achieves multimodal table understanding without expensive MLLM adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264840/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes each table in two parallel forms—a text serialization fed through TableGPT2-7B's input embedding layer (3,584-dim) and a screenshot processed by Ovis2-8B's vision transformer (6,144-dim)—alongside a natural language query embedded via all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (384-dim). These are concatenated into a 10,112-dim vector for routing. Outputs are benchmark-specific: concise answers for WTQ/TAT-QA/HiTab, unitless numerics for TABMWP, full sentences for FeTaQA, and JSON labels (True/False or Entail/Contradict/Neutral) for TabFact/InfoTabs.

## Method

TableDART introduces a 2.59M-parameter two-layer MLP gating network that maps the concatenated multimodal representation to logits over three inference paths (Text-only, Image-only, Fusion), trained with a combined objective of KL divergence against empirically correct paths plus a resource-aware regularizer that penalizes expensive routes. All large constituent models (TableGPT2-7B, Ovis2-8B) remain frozen; only the gate is trained on a 10,000-sample mixture, making it ~10× more parameter-efficient than MLLM fine-tuning baselines like HIPPO. For the Fusion path, a training-free LLM agent (Gemini 2.0 Flash) receives both single-modality outputs and the original table, operating as either an Arbitrator (resolving conflicts by selecting the more reliable answer) or a Rescuer (synthesizing a novel correct answer from partially correct fragments). The key insight is that instance-level dynamic routing—allocating expensive multimodal fusion only where single modalities fail—outperforms static one-size-fits-all fusion while reducing average inference latency by 24.5%.

## Results

TableDART (TableGPT2-7B + Ovis2-8B) achieves a 74.86% average accuracy across six accuracy-based benchmarks and 36.11 BLEU on FeTaQA, surpassing the strongest MLLM-based baseline HIPPO-8B (70.84% avg.) by +4.02% in average accuracy and +2.93 BLEU points. The framework also outperforms its own constituent single-modality models (TableGPT2-7B: 69.14%, Ovis2-8B: 69.49%) and the Gemini 2.0 Flash MLLM (56.92% avg.). In zero-shot generalization on unseen datasets, TableDART maintains 74.37% accuracy versus HIPPO-8B's 63.00%, a +18.05% gap. Dynamic routing also reduces average inference latency by 24.5% (2.20s vs. 2.92s) compared to a non-adaptive fusion baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TableDART (TableGPT2-7B + Ovis2-8B) outperforms the strongest MLLM-based baseline HIPPO-8B on five of seven benchmarks, achieving 74.86% vs. 70.84% average accuracy (+4.02%) and 36.11 vs. 33.18 BLEU on FeTaQA. It also surpasses the Gemini 2.0 Flash MLLM (56.92% avg.) by a wide margin, confirming that gains come from the routing mechanism rather than the Fusion agent's backbone capacity. Among single-modality baselines, TableDART beats the best Table-as-Text model TableLlama-7B (37.77%) and the best Table-as-Image model SynTab-LLaVA-7B (59.28%) by large margins, and exceeds its own constituent models TableGPT2-7B (69.14%) and Ovis2-8B (69.49%) by 5.72% and 5.37% respectively. The generalization variant using Qwen2.5-VL-7B instead of Ovis2-8B still achieves 70.25% average, a +7.14% gain over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B standalone baseline (63.11%), demonstrating model-agnostic effectiveness. In zero-shot evaluation on unseen datasets (HiTab, FeTaQA), TableDART maintains 74.37% accuracy while HIPPO-8B drops sharply to 63.00%, an 18.05% gap that highlights the routing policy's generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

TableDART sits at the intersection of three established paradigms for table understanding—Table-as-Text (TableGPT2, TableLlama), Table-as-Image (Ovis2, Qwen2.5-VL, SynTab-LLaVA), and Table-as-Multimodality (HIPPO)—but departs from the dominant MLLM-fine-tuning approach by introducing a dynamic routing mechanism inspired by mixture-of-experts literature (GShard, Uni-Perceiver). Rather than jointly fine-tuning a large multimodal backbone to process both modalities for every input, it reuses frozen single-modality experts and trains only a 2.59M-parameter MLP gate to select among Text-only, Image-only, or Fusion paths per instance. The Fusion path itself is a training-free LLM agent (Gemini 2.0 Flash) that acts as either an arbitrator or a rescuer, drawing on multi-source consensus and reasoning literature rather than learned multimodal representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a plug-and-play, parameter-efficient framework that outperforms a fully fine-tuned 8B MLLM (HIPPO) while training ~10× fewer parameters, making multimodal table understanding accessible under tight training budgets. The zero-shot generalization result—TableDART holding at 74.37% on unseen datasets while HIPPO drops to 63.00%—suggests that learned routing policies are more transferable than fine-tuned multimodal representations, an insight with implications beyond tables. Open questions include whether the framework's reliance on an external API for the Fusion agent limits real-world deployment, whether the routing policy generalizes to other structured data (forms, documents, code), and whether the 10K-sample training mixture is sufficient for the gate to capture the full diversity of real-world table-query interactions.
