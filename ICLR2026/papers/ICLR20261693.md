## Problem

Multivariate time-series imputation faces three coupled challenges: irregular sampling and blockwise gaps break short-range continuity, the coexistence of slow trends and sharp transients stresses a model's spectral bias, and practical deployments demand reproducible inference at moderate cost. Existing generative approaches (e.g., conditional diffusion) either leak information from unobserved timestamps, require many stochastic reverse steps with sampling variance, or lack an explicit mechanism to reconcile global structure with local detail along the generative trajectory.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a partially observed multivariate series x ∈ R^{T×D} and a binary mask M ∈ {0,1}^{T×D}, from which it constructs a structured endpoint concatenating observed values, a per-time visibility flag, and left/right moving-average context channels (window w=10). At inference it produces a single deterministic imputation of all missing entries; observed coordinates are clamped exactly to the linear bridge at every ODE step by a data-consistency projection, so the output is measurement-preserving by construction. Metrics (MSE/MAE) are computed exclusively on missing indices after inverse per-channel standardization.

## Method

TG-MSFM learns a data-conditioned velocity field via deterministic flow matching on a linear bridge from Gaussian noise to the structured endpoint, with supervision restricted to missing coordinates (gap-only loss). A time-aware Transformer backbone uses visibility-masked self-attention so queries attend only to observed timestamps, preventing leakage. The velocity is decomposed into scale-specific heads on a fixed 1D pyramid (strides {1,2,4}) and blended by a time-dependent softmax gate that schedules coarse-to-fine spectral emphasis along the trajectory, with a light anti-aliasing filter stabilizing the finest branch. At inference, a second-order Heun integrator (two velocity evaluations per step) is coupled with a per-step data-consistency projection that overwrites all observed and conditioning coordinates with their exact linear-bridge values, yielding a deterministic, measurement-preserving trajectory with N ∈ [200,400] steps for a favorable speed-quality trade-off.

## Results

TG-MSFM achieves the best MSE and MAE on all ten benchmarks (averaged over missing ratios {0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7} and 5 seeds). Headline MSE values include 0.120 on ETTh1, 0.044 on ETTh2, 0.020 on ETTm2, 0.101 on Electricity, 0.102 on Weather, 0.029 on Exchange, and 0.047 on PEMS03. The largest relative improvements over the best baseline are on Exchange (57% MSE reduction vs. iTransformer's 0.068), ETTh2 (53% vs. Mtsci's 0.093), and PEMS03 (28% vs. PatchTST's 0.065). On the speed-quality trade-off (Fig. 2), TG-MSFM dominates the Pareto frontier on ETTh1, with diminishing returns around N≈250 ODE steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TG-MSFM outperforms all 12 baselines on every dataset for both MSE and MAE. Against the strongest non-generative baselines, it beats DLinear by 17% MSE on ETTh1 (0.120 vs. 0.144) and iTransformer by 57% on Exchange (0.029 vs. 0.068). Among diffusion-based imputers, it surpasses CSDI by 21% on ETTh1 (0.120 vs. 0.151) and by a large margin on Electricity (0.101 vs. 0.533), where CSDI's stochastic sampling introduces substantial variance. PriSTI and Mtsci are the closest diffusion competitors on ETTh2 (0.110 and 0.093 respectively), yet TG-MSFM still leads by 60% and 53% in MSE. On PEMS03, PatchTST is the best baseline (0.065 MSE), and TG-MSFM improves by 28% (0.047). The speed-quality analysis (Fig. 2) further shows that at matched NFE budgets, TG-MSFM achieves lower MSE than CSDI, with steeper accuracy gains from additional steps due to its coarse-to-fine gate rather than merely damping sampling noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts deterministic flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023b) from generative modeling to multivariate time-series imputation, departing from the dominant diffusion-based imputation paradigm (CSDI, PriSTI, SSSD) by replacing stochastic reverse sampling with a single deterministic ODE trajectory. It builds on the Transformer backbone (Vaswani et al., 2017) but introduces two task-specific modifications: visibility-masked self-attention that restricts aggregation to observed timestamps, and a time-gated multi-scale velocity decomposition that schedules coarse-to-fine spectral emphasis along the flow. The per-step data-consistency projection coupled with Heun integration is a novel inference-time mechanism not present in prior flow-matching or diffusion imputers, and the gap-only training objective aligns supervision exclusively with missing coordinates rather than the full sequence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TG-MSFM offers a lightweight, reproducible alternative to diffusion-based imputers for settings where deterministic point estimates and measurement preservation are prioritized (clinical records, industrial monitoring, auditing). The consistent gains across ten diverse benchmarks with fixed hyperparameters suggest the design is robust, but the method remains limited by its linear bridge, quadratic attention cost, and lack of calibrated uncertainty. Open questions include whether data-adaptive bridges and time warps can reduce the 200–400 ODE steps, whether the approach extends to streaming or very long sequences, and how it combines with posteriorization (ensembles, conformal bands) when uncertainty estimates are needed.
