## Problem

In distributed LLM serving, cache-affinity scheduling (co-locating requests with shared prompt prefixes to maximize KV cache reuse) fundamentally conflicts with load-balancing scheduling (evenly distributing requests across instances). Existing schedulers such as Mooncake, Preble, and Dynamo operate within a single mapping space, applying prompt-aware routing to a subset of requests and load-aware routing to the rest, and thus cannot simultaneously achieve both objectives. This paper identifies that the root cause is the constraint of a single mapping function and asks whether a dual-mapping design can break this trade-off.

## Input / Output

The scheduler consumes each incoming request's prompt prefix (hashed at an adaptive length determined by a prefix hotness tree) along per-instance metadata: pending prefill token counts and KV cache block occupancy. It produces a binary routing decision selecting one of two hash-derived candidate instances, and—when an instance is detected as overloaded—a batch migration decision identifying which queued requests to relocate to their backup candidate. The routing decision is gated by a TTFT SLO threshold (pending prefill tokens that fit within the SLO window), and migration decisions are scored by a per-request TTFT benefit computed from both source and target instance states.

## Method

DualMap maps each request to exactly two candidate instances via two independent hash functions over the request's prompt prefix, drawing on the power-of-two-choices principle to guarantee near-optimal load concentration while preserving prefix locality. An adaptive hash prefix length mechanism uses a sliding-window hotness tree to extend the hash key for skewed prefixes (preventing hotspot concentration) and shorten it for cold prefixes (improving cache reuse). SLO-aware routing prioritizes the cache-hitting candidate until its pending prefill tokens exceed a TTFT-SLO-derived threshold, at which point it switches to the less-loaded candidate—avoiding the oscillation problem of per-request Min-TTFT estimation. Hotspot-aware rebalancing, inspired by Cuckoo hashing, performs non-recursive single-round batch migration of queued requests within their fixed candidate pair when both candidates are overloaded, scoring each request by the TTFT difference between source and target. A dual-hash-ring structure (two consistent-hashing rings, one per hash function) ensures that instance additions or removals remap only a localized arc of prefixes, enabling elastic scaling without global cache invalidation.

## Results

DualMap improves effective request capacity by up to 2.25× under a 5s TTFT SLO compared to the best baseline. On the Tool&Agent workload, effective request capacity increases by up to 125% and goodput improves by 16.7%–48%; on the Conversation workload, effective request capacity increases by 40.6%–80% and goodput improves by 14.3%–40%. DualMap reduces P50 TTFT by 55.4%–97.4% and P90 TTFT by 82.3%–97% relative to the best baseline, while achieving cache hit rates of 62.5% (Conversation) and 96.4% (Tool&Agent) of the theoretical upper bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualMap is compared against four baselines: Cache Affinity, Least Loaded, Min TTFT (Mooncake), and Preble. On the Tool&Agent workload, Cache Affinity performs worst due to severe load imbalance from skewed prefix popularity, while Preble and Min TTFT achieve intermediate results by trading cache hit rate for load balance. DualMap consistently outperforms all four baselines on both effective request capacity and goodput across all QPS levels and model sizes (7B and 14B). On the Conversation workload (less skewed), DualMap still leads by 40.6%–80% in effective request capacity over the best baseline, confirming gains are not solely from handling skew. In the ablation, DualMap's SLO-aware routing alone reduces P50/P90 TTFT by 23.5%/18.5% over Min TTFT, and adding hotspot-aware rebalancing further reduces P90 TTFT by 11.3%, showing both techniques contribute meaningfully.

## Relation to Prior Work

DualMap departs from prior LLM serving schedulers (Mooncake/Min TTFT, Preble, Dynamo) that operate within a single mapping space and must trade off cache affinity against load balancing per request. It repurposes the Power of Two Choices principle from randomized load balancing (Mitzenmacher, 2002) and Cuckoo hashing (Pagh & Rodler, 2001) into a dual-mapping framework where each prefix is bound to exactly two candidate instances, breaking the single-space constraint. The scaling mechanism further borrows from consistent hashing (Karger et al., 1997), adapted into a dual-hash-ring to preserve locality during cluster membership changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.25× effective request capacity gain under TTFT SLO constraints is practically significant for production LLM serving, where SLO compliance directly determines cost and user experience. The dual-mapping framing offers a clean conceptual resolution to a tension that prior systems handled with ad-hoc per-request heuristics, and the implementation on vLLM with Ascend NPUs demonstrates deployability. Open questions include generalization beyond Qwen2.5 and the two evaluated workloads, behavior at much larger cluster scales (beyond 32 instances), interaction with model-parallel or expert-parallel deployments, and whether the SLO-aware switching threshold requires per-model calibration in heterogeneous clusters.
