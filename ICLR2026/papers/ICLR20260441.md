## Problem

Existing parameter shift rules (PSRs) for VQE gradient estimation require observations at fixed, equidistant locations and provide no uncertainty information, preventing reuse of previous measurements and adaptive control of quantum computation cost. This paper asks whether a Bayesian formulation of PSRs can simultaneously enable flexible observation placement, uncertainty-aware gradient estimation, and cost-adaptive optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260441/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes D angular circuit parameters in [0,2π)^D, a target Hamiltonian (e.g., Heisenberg or Ising on Q qubits), and noisy energy observations whose variance scales as 1/N_shots. It produces optimized circuit parameters, a full posterior distribution over the gradient (mean and variance per direction), and an adaptive per-observation shot count determined by solving a constrained minimization so that the current point lies within the GradCoRe. Unlike standard PSRs that require exactly 2V_d observations at prescribed offsets per direction, Bayesian PSR accepts observations at any set of locations, including those accumulated from prior SGD iterations.

## Method

The core mechanism is a derivative Gaussian Process with the physics-informed VQE kernel (a product of Dirichlet-kernel-like terms reflecting the exact Fourier structure of VQE objectives), which yields an analytic closed-form gradient estimator with posterior uncertainty. Theorem 3.1 proves that for equidistant noiseless observations, Bayesian PSR reduces exactly to the generalized PSR of Wierichs et al., while Theorem 3.2 shows that for V_d=1 the shift α=π/2 minimizes posterior gradient variance regardless of noise level. The GradCoRe strategy defines a region where per-direction gradient variance falls below a threshold κ(t) proportional to the current gradient norm, and solves a grid-search problem to find the minimum total shot count satisfying this constraint at each SGD step. This adaptive allocation, combined with reuse of up to R·2V_d·D previous observations, accelerates convergence compared to fixed-shot SGD and outperforms SMO-based baselines (NFT, EMICoRe, SubsCoRe) and BO-based baselines (SGLBO) on energy and fidelity metrics.

## Results

On the Ising Hamiltonian with a 5-qubit, 3-layer Efficient SU(2) circuit, GradCoRe (the proposed SGD with Bayesian PSR and adaptive shot allocation) achieves faster convergence to lower energy and higher fidelity than all baselines as a function of cumulative measurement shots. GradCoRe outperforms fixed-shot SGD (N_shots = 128–1024) and Bayes-SGD across the entire optimization trajectory, while also surpassing SGLBO, Bayes-NFT, EMICoRe, and SubsCoRe in both ΔEnergy and ΔFidelity. The paper reports statistical significance test results in Appendix F.1 (Table 3) and additional experiments with varying qubit counts, circuit depths, and the Heisenberg Hamiltonian.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GradCoRe outperforms all five compared baselines on both energy and fidelity metrics. Against SGLBO (Tamiya & Yamasaki, 2022), which uses Bayesian optimization to select step sizes, GradCoRe converges faster by leveraging the VQE kernel's physics-informed structure for gradient estimation. Against Bayes-NFT and EMICoRe (Nicoli et al., 2023a), which operate in the SMO framework, GradCoRe achieves lower final energy with fewer total shots by reusing observations across SGD iterations and adaptively allocating shots via the GradCoRe constraint. Against SubsCoRe (Anders et al., 2024), which also minimizes observation costs but within the SMO paradigm, GradCoRe demonstrates superior convergence speed. The original NFT (Nakanishi et al., 2020) is excluded from the main comparison because it is outperformed by its Bayesian variant (Bayes-NFT), and GIBO (Müller et al., 2021) is excluded as an even weaker baseline than NFT. Notably, Bayes-SGD alone (without GradCoRe's adaptive shot strategy) performs on par with standard SGD with PSR, indicating that the key advantage comes from the uncertainty-driven adaptive observation cost control rather than the Bayesian gradient estimator in isolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work applying Gaussian processes to VQE optimization, which previously focused on SMO-based methods (NFT, Bayesian NFT, EMICoRe, SubsCoRe) or BO-based step-size selection (SGLBO). The key departure is using derivative GPs with the physics-informed VQE kernel directly as a gradient estimator within SGD, yielding a 'Bayesian PSR' that generalizes classical parameter shift rules to arbitrary observation locations and provides analytic uncertainty. The GradCoRe concept adapts the confident-region idea from function prediction (Nicoli et al., 2023a) to gradient prediction, enabling adaptive shot allocation in SGD rather than SMO. Theoretically, the paper formalizes the relationship between Bayesian PSR and existing PSRs, showing the latter emerge as noiseless, equidistant special cases with Bayesian regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The adaptive shot allocation via GradCoRe provides a principled way to reduce quantum computation cost while maintaining gradient accuracy, which is practically important given that shot count dominates VQE runtime. The theoretical result that α=π/2 is provably optimal for first-order PSRs (Theorem 3.2) gives a formal justification for a heuristic widely used in practice. Limitations include consideration of only shot noise (no hardware noise), testing on small systems (5 qubits, 3 layers), and reliance on the VQE kernel's structural assumptions about the circuit. Open questions include whether the approach extends to hardware-noise regimes, larger problem sizes, and whether optimal combinations of SGD-GradCoRe with SMO-based methods can further improve performance.
