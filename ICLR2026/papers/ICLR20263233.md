## Problem

Existing neural rendering methods for autonomous driving simulation either support only pinhole cameras (rasterization-based 3DGS), render too slowly for practical use (NeRF-based), or force a quality tradeoff between camera and LiDAR fidelity when jointly modeling both sensors. No prior method can render arbitrary camera models (fisheye, rolling shutter) and spinning LiDAR sensors in real-time while maintaining high fidelity for both modalities simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263233/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-camera images (including high-distortion fisheye lenses), spinning LiDAR scans with irregular azimuth/elevation sampling patterns, and per-frame SE(3) sensor poses. It produces rendered camera images at arbitrary novel viewpoints (supporting any projection model and time-dependent effects like rolling shutter) and rendered LiDAR scans with per-beam intensity and ray-drop probability. The scene is decomposed into a static background and dynamic objects, each parameterized with learnable pose offsets for controllable re-simulation at novel timestamps.

## Method

SimULi extends 3DGUT with LiDAR support via a factorized representation: camera and LiDAR attributes are encoded into separate sets of 3D Gaussians (G_c and G_l) coupled by a nearest-neighbor anchoring loss that pulls camera Gaussian means toward LiDAR-supervised surfaces without forcing a single representation to satisfy both sensors. For LiDAR rendering, the paper derives an automated tiling strategy that computes elevation tile boundaries from the normalized CDF of beam angles (equalizing beam count per tile) and a ray-based culling mechanism using a summed-area table over a dense culling grid to skip Gaussians that intersect no LiDAR rays. Camera Gaussians carry 3rd-order spherical harmonics for view-dependent color and are rendered via volumetric integration with a bilateral grid for appearance normalization; LiDAR Gaussians use SH for view-dependent intensity and a softmax-decoded ray-drop probability. The Unscented Transform projects 7 sigma points per Gaussian to handle non-linear camera models and rolling shutter natively, and an entropy regularization encourages binary opacity and sparsity in LiDAR Gaussians to match the surface-intersection nature of LiDAR returns.

## Results

On the Waymo Interp. static benchmark, SimULi achieves 30.15 dB PSNR and 0.881 SSIM, outperforming the strongest baseline SplatAD (27.82 dB) by over 2 dB and all other methods by a wide margin. It also delivers the best LiDAR metrics: 0.003 m median L2 depth error, 0.136 m chamfer distance, and 0.944 ray-drop accuracy. In terms of speed, SimULi renders camera views at 156.90 MP/s and LiDAR at 11.33 MR/s, making it 3.1× faster than SplatAD on cameras and 4.7× faster on LiDAR, and over 10× faster than ray-tracing methods like LiDAR-RT. On the dynamic Waymo Dynamic benchmark, SimULi extends its lead to 32.35 dB PSNR (vs. 30.60 for SplatAD) while maintaining the fastest rendering speed at 179.45 MP/s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SplatAD (the closest prior method, also 3DGS-based with LiDAR support), SimULi wins on PSNR by +2.33 dB (static) and +1.75 dB (dynamic), improves chamfer distance from 0.175 m to 0.136 m, and renders cameras 3.1× faster (156.90 vs. 49.98 MP/s) and LiDAR 4.7× faster (11.33 vs. 2.40 MR/s); SplatAD edges out SimULi only on LiDAR intensity RMSE (0.061 vs. 0.064). Against NeuRAD (NeRF-based joint simulation), SimULi gains +2.66 dB PSNR, reduces chamfer distance by 17%, and is 11.9× faster on camera rendering. Against LiDAR-RT (ray-tracing, LiDAR-only), SimULi achieves better depth (0.003 m vs. 0.005 m MedL2) and chamfer distance (0.136 vs. 0.169 m) while rendering >10× faster, though LiDAR-RT retains a slight edge on ray-drop accuracy (0.962 vs. 0.944) due to its U-Net refinement. Against camera-only 3DGS methods (OmniRe, StreetGS, DeformableGS), SimULi improves PSNR by 2.2–3.6 dB and renders 3–30× faster, while also providing LiDAR outputs those methods cannot produce. Against UniSim and AlignMiF (NeRF-based joint methods), SimULi dominates on every metric, with PSNR gains of 7+ dB and rendering speeds 19–785× higher.

## Relation to Prior Work

SimULi extends 3DGUT (Wu et al., 2025b), which already supports non-linear camera models and rolling shutter via the Unscented Transform, by adding LiDAR rendering through an automated tiling strategy and ray-based culling. It departs from the closest prior work, SplatAD (Hess et al., 2025), in three key ways: (1) supporting arbitrary camera models rather than pinhole-only, (2) replacing manual tiling heuristics with an automated histogram-equalization-based scheme, and (3) factorizing camera and LiDAR information into separate Gaussian sets coupled by a nearest-neighbor anchoring loss, rather than encoding all sensors into a unified particle set as in SplatAD, UniSim, and NeuRAD. The dynamic scene decomposition follows the Neural Scene Graphs formulation (Ost et al., 2021), and the particle representation is grounded in 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to render arbitrary (including fisheye) camera models and spinning LiDAR sensors simultaneously in real-time, closing a significant practical gap for AV simulation where raw sensor data with distortion and time-dependent effects is the standard input for downstream perception models. The factorized representation with anchoring loss resolves the long-standing cross-sensor inconsistency tradeoff that forced prior joint methods to sacrifice one modality's quality for the other. Open questions include whether the factorized approach generalizes to additional sensor types (radar, ultrasonic), whether the per-scene training requirement can be relaxed toward feed-forward or few-shot settings, and whether the anchoring strategy scales to very large multi-vehicle scenes with many dynamic actors.
