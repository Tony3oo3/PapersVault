## Problem

No benchmark exists for model merging research that clearly separates MLLM training tasks from evaluation, and prior merging work has focused almost exclusively on visual classification or single-modality LLMs. The paper also identifies that existing MLLM merging methods (e.g., AdaMMS, UQ-Merge) either require test-set access, can only merge two models at a time, or lack fine-grained capability categorization. Additionally, combining different modalities (vision, audio, video) into an omni-language model typically requires expensive re-training on new modality-text data, which model merging could potentially bypass.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multiple fine-tuned MLLM checkpoints (all sharing a common base model) and computes task vectors as the parameter difference between each fine-tuned model and the base. For capability merging, five expert models are merged (VQA, Geometry, Chart, OCR, Grounding); for modality merging, three single-modality models (vision-language with CLIP encoder, audio-language with BEATs encoder, video-language with LanguageBind encoder) are merged while preserving each modality's unique encoder and connector. The output is a single merged model of the same size as the base, which accepts image+text, audio+text, or video+text queries and produces text answers or bounding box coordinates, with no additional inference-time overhead.

## Method

OptMerge builds on WUDI Merging's data-free loss that minimizes layer-wise interference between the merged vector and each task vector, but addresses two failure modes: (1) task vectors contain redundant noise from shared foundational skills, and (2) the optimization can take shortcuts by inflating the merged vector's norm to achieve orthogonality. For full fine-tuning models, OptMerge centers task vectors by subtracting their mean, performs SVD to isolate core task-specific knowledge, and uses the top-k singular components (U₁:ₖΣ₁:ₖV₁:ₖᵀ) as the input subspace in the loss, effectively denoising while preserving principal features. For LoRA fine-tuned models, it replaces Adam with SGD (exploiting implicit regularization to navigate null-space flat regions), applies direct truncated SVD without centering to reduce Frobenius norm, and initializes the merged vector with the mean of task vectors to prevent norm explosion. The rank k is set to rank(τᵢ)/n_tasks, and optimization runs for 300 iterations per linear layer, requiring only ~2.6–22 GB GPU memory versus ~240–256 GB for mixture training.

## Results

On Qwen2-VL-7B-Base (LoRA fine-tuning), OptMerge achieves 63.30 average across 10 benchmarks (VQA, Geometry, Chart, OCR, Grounding), outperforming the strongest baseline TIES w/ DARE (61.88) by 1.42 points and surpassing the full Qwen2-VL-Instruct model (62.23) by 1.07 points. On InternVL2.5-1B (full fine-tuning), OptMerge reaches 57.44 average, nearly matching Mixture Training (57.66) while requiring only 0.22h and 2.62GB GPU memory versus 25.38h and 240GB for data-based training. On real Hugging Face checkpoints, OptMerge achieves 66.70 average, exceeding Qwen2-VL-Instruct (62.23) by 4.47 points. The ablation study confirms an average 2.48% gain from OptMerge's components (SGD + initialization + low-rank) over WUDI Merging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OptMerge outperforms all 8 compared merging baselines on the Qwen2-VL LoRA benchmark (Table 3), with the largest margin over WUDI Merging (63.30 vs. 58.65, +4.65) and Iso-C (63.30 vs. 26.69, which catastrophically fails because LoRA task vectors are already low-rank and further singular-value averaging destroys the LLM). The strongest baseline, TIES w/ DARE (61.88), is beaten by 1.42 points, and OptMerge also surpasses the full Qwen2-VL-Instruct model (62.23) by 1.07 points without any training data. On InternVL2.5 full fine-tuning (Table 2), OptMerge (57.44) leads all merging methods but trails Mixture Training (57.66) by 0.22 points, while still requiring ~115x less GPU memory. On real Hugging Face checkpoints (Table 6), OptMerge (66.70) beats TIES w/ DARE (66.58) by 0.12 and Qwen2-VL-Instruct (62.23) by 4.47 points. For modality merging (Table 5), TSV Merging (67.34 avg) slightly edges out OptMerge (66.88) on the two audio-visual QA benchmarks, though both outperform online composition methods (NaiveMC 66.79, DAMC 66.79).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the task-vector-based model merging framework (Task Arithmetic, Ilharco et al. 2023) and the optimization-based WUDI Merging (Cheng et al., ICML 2025) to the multimodal LLM setting, where prior merging work focused on visual classification or single-modality LLMs (code/math). It departs from existing MLLM merging efforts (AdaMMS, UQ-Merge) by providing a data-free, multi-model benchmark with fine-grained capability categorization (VQA, Geometry, Chart, OCR, Grounding) rather than pairwise merging or test-set-dependent hyperparameter search. OptMerge itself modifies WUDI's loss by inserting SVD-based low-rank denoising of task vectors and practical optimizer choices (SGD, mean initialization) to stabilize the merged-vector optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark fills a clear gap: no prior resource systematically evaluated model merging across distinct MLLM capabilities with controlled fine-tuning, and the theoretical bound (Theorem 3.1) provides the first explanation of why over-trained expert models merge poorly. The finding that data-free merging can match or exceed mixture training (and that modality merging yields complementary gains over single-modality models) suggests a scalable, low-cost path toward omni-modal LLMs without retraining on new modality-text data. Limitations include evaluation restricted to ≤7B models, single-epoch fine-tuning, and English-centric benchmarks; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to reasoning-focused MLLMs, multilingual settings, and larger-scale (32B+) models where the paper shows only preliminary results.
