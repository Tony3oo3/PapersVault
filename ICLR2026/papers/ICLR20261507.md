## Problem

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) trains LLMs on reasoning tasks through trial-and-error, where the model is blind to the optimization objective during generation and must discover reward patterns from sparse, fragmented reward signals. This makes RLVR inefficient, especially when the reward space is sparse and the expected behavior is hard to reach, as the model cannot learn what it does not know exists. The paper asks whether explicitly providing the model with a natural-language description of the reward function (the 'rules of the game') during training can leverage LLMs' in-context learning ability to make reinforcement finetuning more efficient.

## Input / Output

During training, the model receives a reasoning problem (K&K logic puzzle, math problem, or CountDown number game) as a text prompt, augmented with an in-context motivation in the system prompt that spells out the exact scoring rules (e.g., correctness score of +2 for correct, -1.5 for wrong-but-parseable, -2 for unparseable; format score of +1 for correct tags, -1 otherwise). The model produces a structured reasoning response with a final answer enclosed in task-specific tags (<think>...<answer>...</answer> for logic puzzles, \boxed{} for math, or an equation in <answer> tags for CountDown). Critically, the motivation is present only during training; validation and inference are conducted without it, and the model generalizes across this gap.

## Method

MeRF (Motivation-enhanced Reinforcement Finetuning) is a minimal modification to the standard RLVR pipeline: the natural-language description of the verifiable reward function is injected directly into the training prompt as an in-context motivation, making the model aware of the optimization objective at generation time. The RL algorithm remains GRPO (group size 8, clip 0.2, KL penalty 0.001), so the external reward signal is unchanged; the only difference is that the model now sees the scoring rules while generating. The key mechanism is that this in-context motivation provides a 'forward' information channel about the reward space, complementing the 'backward' gradient signal from sparse reward samples, which encourages more structured exploration (higher entropy, better pass@k) and prevents the model from collapsing to suboptimal solutions like format-only reward hacking. Ablations show performance scales with consistency between the motivation and the actual reward function, and the model can even adapt to deliberately adverse (opposite-score) motivations through the RL training dynamics, eventually discounting the misleading signal while retaining the structural information.

## Results

On K&K Logic Puzzles, MeRF achieves a 0.63 average accuracy (in-domain, 3–7 people) with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, outperforming the RLVR baseline (0.51) by 12 percentage points, and reaches 0.83 with Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct vs. 0.72 for RLVR. On math benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, AMC23, MATH500), MeRF with Qwen2.5-7B-Base yields average pass@1 of 36.78% vs. 33.38% for RLVR (+3.40%) and pass@8 of 54.95% vs. 50.45% (+4.50%). MeRF also demonstrates superior training efficiency, achieving better pass@4 and pass@8 at step 140 than the final RLVR model at step 280.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeRF consistently outperforms the RLVR baseline across all tested models and difficulty levels. With Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, MeRF achieves 0.65 in-domain average vs. 0.51 for RLVR (+14 pp), and with Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, 0.83 vs. 0.75 (+8 pp). The 14B MeRF model (0.83 overall) surpasses DeepSeek-R1 (0.81 overall) and significantly outperforms GPT-4o (0.37) and o1-2024-12-17 (0.42) on this benchmark. On math benchmarks, MeRF improves over RLVR by +3.40% pass@1 and +4.50% pass@8 on average, with the largest gain on AMC23 (55.0 vs. 47.5 pass@1). The DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B + MeRF model (0.86 overall) approaches o3-mini-high (0.94) while starting from a much weaker base (0.15), demonstrating that in-context motivation enables smaller models to close the gap with frontier systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLVR paradigm (Lambert et al., 2024) by injecting a natural-language description of the reward function directly into the training prompt as an 'in-context motivation,' bridging the typically inference-time mechanism of in-context learning (Brown et al., 2020; Wei et al., 2022) with the parameter-update-based RL training loop. It departs from the standard RLVR setup where the model is 'blind' to the optimization objective during generation and must discover reward patterns purely through sparse, fragmented reward signals. The method is algorithm-agnostic (demonstrated with GRPO) and builds on the observation that verifiable rewards, by definition, admit natural-language descriptions that LLMs can process in-context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MeRF offers a drop-in, zero-cost modification to any RLVR pipeline that consistently improves training efficiency and final performance across model families and task domains (logic, math, number games), making it practically attractive for practitioners already running RLVR. The mechanistic analysis—showing that in-context motivation prevents entropy collapse and sustains exploration (pass@k) where vanilla RLVR stagnates—provides a useful diagnostic lens for understanding RLVR training dynamics. Open questions remain: the motivation is static throughout training, and the paper acknowledges that models with weak generalization may struggle with the train/validation prompt gap, leaving the design of dynamic or adaptive motivations as a natural next step.
