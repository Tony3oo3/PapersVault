## Problem

Optimal transport flow matching (OT-FM) promises straighter ODE paths by pairing noise and data via OT, but its batch-based Sinkhorn solver costs O(n²/ε²) per batch, and results only improve meaningfully at very large n (≈10⁶), making it impractical. The default independent coupling (I-FM) avoids this cost but produces high-curvature flows that demand many NFEs at inference. The paper asks whether one can retain the OT-guided pairing benefits without the quadratic batch-size bottleneck.

## Input / Output

The method consumes d-dimensional Gaussian noise vectors (d=3072 for 32×32, d=12288 for 64×64 pixel space, or latent dimensions for 256×256) and produces generated images via ODE integration. For class-conditional generation, a one-hot class vector (1000 or 13 classes) is appended to the noise; for super-resolution, a downsampled-and-noised image (16×16 or 8×8) serves as a continuous condition. The precompute phase requires only the full dataset (N up to 2.56M) and the noise distribution, producing a single potential vector g ∈ ℝ^N that is then reused throughout training.

## Method

SD-FM replaces batch-OT with a semidiscrete OT formulation that exploits the finite dataset size N. In a one-off precompute phase, a dual potential vector g ∈ ℝ^N is fitted via SGD on the concave semidual objective, using a novel unbiased χ² convergence criterion and a convergence analysis valid for both ε=0 and ε>0. During FM training, each freshly sampled noise x₀ is assigned to a data point by a maximum inner product search (MIPS): k* = argmax_k (g_k + x₀ᵀx₁⁽ᵏ⁾) for ε=0 (a Laguerre-cell assignment), or categorical sampling over a softmax for ε>0. This O(Nd) lookup per pair is negligible relative to the FM gradient cost Θ, eliminating the O(n²/ε²) Sinkhorn bottleneck entirely. The paper further derives a generalized Tweedie formula for SD-OT couplings with a correction δ_ε that vanishes as ε→0, enabling score-based guidance (e.g., corrected classifier-free guidance) directly from the velocity model.

## Results

On ImageNet-64 unconditional generation, SD-FM (ε=0, PCA-500) achieves FID 45.62 with Euler-4 NFE, nearly halving I-FM's 79.95, and reaches FID 8.42 with Dopri5 vs. I-FM's 9.10. On PetFace-64, the improvement is even more dramatic: SD-FM (full d=12288) drops FID from 56.53 to 20.54 at Euler-4 and from 13.38 to 7.50 at Euler-16. For class-conditional ImageNet-64, SD-FM achieves FID 26.04 (Euler-4) and 3.63 (Dopri5) versus I-FM's 34.51 and 3.91. On CelebA 8× super-resolution (σ=0.1), SD-FM improves PSNR from 17.52 (I-FM) to 17.94 (ε=0.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against I-FM (independent coupling, Lipman et al. 2023), SD-FM wins on every metric across all datasets and NFE budgets, with the largest gains at low inference budgets: FID drops from 79.95 to 45.62 (ImageNet-64, Euler-4) and from 56.53 to 20.54 (PetFace, Euler-4), representing roughly 2× improvements. Against OT-FM with large Sinkhorn batches (Zhang et al. 2025, n up to 2^19, ε∈{0.01, 0.1}), SD-FM achieves comparable or better FID at a fraction of the pairing cost—OT-FM's O(n²/ε²) precompute becomes prohibitive at 64×64 resolution (over 10 days for large n), while SD-FM's MIPS lookup costs only O(Nd) per pair, negligible relative to the FM gradient step Θ. For MeanFlow one-step generation on ImageNet-256 latent space (Geng et al. 2025 setup), SD-OT coupling outperforms the independent coupling baseline across all NFE values in Figure 6, confirming that the semidiscrete pairing benefit extends beyond standard FM to consistency-model training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OT-FM paradigm (Pooladian et al., 2023; Tong et al., 2024) by replacing batch optimal transport—matching n noise to n data points via Sinkhorn—with a semidiscrete OT formulation that treats the full dataset of N points as the discrete target. The key departure from Zhang et al. (2025), who showed OT-FM requires very large n (≈10⁶) to overcome statistical bias, is that SD-FM eliminates the batch-size bottleneck entirely: a one-time SGD precompute fits a potential vector of size N, and each noise is then paired via a maximum inner product search in O(N). The method also builds on the stochastic SD-OT optimization of Genevay et al. (2016) and the Laguerre-cell geometry of Mérigot (2011), extending the convergence analysis to the ε=0 (unregularized) case that prior work had not addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SD-FM offers a practically compelling alternative to OT-FM: it removes the O(n²/ε²) Sinkhorn cost while delivering substantially better FID than independent-coupling FM, especially at low inference budgets (e.g., 4-step Euler), for a negligible per-pair overhead. The generalized Tweedie formula (Proposition 3) and the resulting guidance correction (Proposition 4) open a path to principled classifier-free guidance for SD-coupled flows, which was previously unavailable. Open questions include scaling to datasets with billions of points (where the O(N) MIPS per sample and the SGD precompute become non-trivial), whether approximate MIPS can further reduce the per-pair cost, and how SD couplings interact with other flow-straightening techniques like Reflow.
