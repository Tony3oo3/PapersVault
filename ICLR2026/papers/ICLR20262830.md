## Problem

Existing LLM debiasing methods either require computationally expensive fine-tuning or modify user prompts, which degrades experience in multi-turn interactions. The prior neuron-level approach IG² was designed for masked language models, cannot capture cross-pair bias relationships, and lacks a systematic way to identify bias-triggering input words. This paper asks whether bias in modern autoregressive LLMs can be localized to specific neurons and neutralized without any training or prompt modification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262830/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained LLM (Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.2-3B, or Mistral-7B) and a set of demographic attributes (gender, nationality, profession, religion) as input, with no training data or prompt rewriting required. It produces a modified model in which a subset of projection-layer neurons have their activations clamped to a constant, yielding debiased text generation at inference time. Stereotype cues (adjectives and nouns) are automatically selected per model and per attribute, so the pipeline is model-specific and attribute-specific rather than relying on a fixed vocabulary.

## Method

The framework operates in three stages. First, stereotype cue selection identifies bias-inducing adjectives and nouns by computing Shannon entropy over the model's predicted demographic-group distributions across sentence templates; cues with the lowest entropy (most concentrated predictions) are selected. Second, two complementary Integrated Gradients strategies attribute bias to individual neurons at the projection layer: Forward-IG quantifies each neuron's contribution to skewed demographic predictions by integrating the gradient of inverse entropy along an interpolation path, while Backward-IG identifies neurons driving group-dependent output disparities by integrating the gradient of Jensen-Shannon Divergence across demographic groups. Third, the top-N neurons by average attribution score have their activations fixed to a constant C, directly disrupting their contribution to biased generation. A theoretical result (Theorem 1) bounds the bias change by the product of the local bias-gradient norm and the output-shift magnitude, formally linking attribution-guided neuron modification to measurable bias reduction.

## Results

On StereoSet (Llama-3.1), FBA reduces the stereotype score (SS, ideal = 50%) from 77.34% to 68.75% on Gender while maintaining LMS at 100%, and BBA achieves a near-ideal SS of 49.31% on Religion with ICAT of 91.14% (vs. 78.48% base). On BBQ (Llama-3.1), BBA attains the best average accuracy of 81.70% (vs. 72.84% base) by improving ambiguous-context accuracy from 54.50% to 74.46% with minimal loss in disambiguated accuracy. On WinoBias (Llama-3.1), BBA reduces the stereotype–anti-stereotype Gap from 25.26% to 1.02% while keeping P_other at 0%, indicating no degradation in language modeling capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FBA and BBA consistently outperform all five baselines on the combined ICAT metric across both Llama-3.1 and Llama-3.2 on StereoSet, with the largest gains in the Religion domain (ICAT 91.14–93.67% vs. 78.48–81.01% base). Auto-Debias is largely ineffective and sometimes worsens bias (e.g., SS increases from 77.34% to 79.69% on Llama-3.1 Gender). Prompt-based methods (Prefix Prompting, Self-Debiasing, DDP) show inconsistent results: Prefix Prompting occasionally improves SS but degrades LMS, while Self-Debiasing can dramatically worsen bias (WinoBias Gap jumps from 25.26% to 71.22% on Llama-3.1). IG², the closest prior neuron-level method, is effective only on Llama-3.2 and causes severe language-modeling degradation (LMS drops to 66.41% on Gender; P_other surges to 13.39% on WinoBias), making it impractical. On BBQ, BBA achieves the highest average accuracy (81.70% on Llama-3.1) by balancing ambiguous and disambiguated accuracy, whereas prompt methods boost ambiguous accuracy at the cost of a sharp drop in disambiguated accuracy (e.g., Self-Debiasing drops Acc_dis from 91.18% to 73.02%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Integrated Gradients (IG) attribution framework from Sundararajan et al. (2017) and the bias-specific variant IG² from Liu et al. (2024, ICLR) to modern autoregressive LLMs. The key departure is twofold: (i) it identifies and addresses specific failures of IG² on LLMs—namely that lower-layer neuron contributions are negligible (gradients decay by 2–7 orders of magnitude) and that IG² only captures pairwise demographic bias while ignoring cross-pair relationships; and (ii) it introduces a model-specific, entropy-based stereotype cue selection step that replaces the predefined trigger words used in prior work. The intervention is also relocated to the projection layer (the final linear map before token logits), where neuron activations have direct, unmediated influence on output probabilities, rather than to arbitrary intermediate layers as in IG².

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, training-free and prompt-free debiasing pipeline that preserves language modeling quality (LMS) while reducing stereotypical bias, which is appealing for deployment settings where fine-tuning is infeasible and prompt modification degrades multi-turn UX. Theorem 1 provides a clean upper bound linking the magnitude of output-space perturbation to bias reduction, giving practitioners a principled way to calibrate the intervention strength (β, C). Open questions include whether the constant-activation intervention can be replaced by learned or input-dependent corrections, whether the approach scales to models with 70B+ parameters where projection-layer dimensionality makes per-neuron intervention costly, and whether the identified bias neurons are stable across fine-tuning or continued pre-training.
