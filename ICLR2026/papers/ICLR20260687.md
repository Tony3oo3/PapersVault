## Problem

MLLMs struggle with long video understanding because their limited context windows force reliance on uniform frame sampling or static pre-selection, both of which assume all moments are equally important and cannot correct initial selection errors during reasoning. This rigidity prevents the model from iteratively gathering fine-grained temporal evidence precisely when and where it is needed, fundamentally capping performance on tasks requiring multi-step visual investigation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260687/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a text question and a uniformly sampled low-frame-rate video overview (64 frames at a default low fps) as its initial context. During reasoning, it can issue up to 4 <video_zoom> tool calls, each specifying a JSON-formatted time segment [start_sec, end_sec] and target fps, constrained so that (end_sec − start_sec) × fps ≤ 16 frames per call, yielding a total budget of at most 128 frames. The output is a structured multi-turn trajectory: thinking in <think> tags followed by either a tool call or a final answer in <answer> tags, with the environment returning high-fps clips or error messages between turns.

## Method

VideoZoomer reframes long video understanding as a sequential tool-interaction task: a 7B MLLM (initialized from Qwen-2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) acts as an active agent that first 'glances' at a coarse low-fps overview, then iteratively 'zooms' into specific temporal segments at higher fps via a <video_zoom> tool to gather fine-grained evidence. Training uses a two-stage strategy: a cold-start SFT phase on ~11K curated trajectories distilled from GPT-4o/Gemini-2.5-Pro, augmented with reflection data where the expert model corrects the student's failed rollouts to teach error recovery and diverse reasoning patterns; followed by GRPO-based reinforcement learning extended to multi-turn tool-calling with a token-level loss mask. The RL reward combines accuracy, format compliance, and a conditional tool-use bonus (awarded only when the final answer is correct) to encourage exploration without redundant calls. This design lets the model learn an adaptive policy for when, where, and at what temporal resolution to request additional visual information, rather than committing to a fixed frame selection upfront.

## Results

VideoZoomer (7B) achieves 68.8/55.8 on MLVU (dev/test), 57.7 on LongVideoBench, 65.2 on VideoMME (overall), 41.5 on LVBench, 67.9 on VideoMMLU, 52.2 on VideoMMMU, and 80.3 on LongVideoReason-eval, yielding gains of +10.5 to +9.5 over its Qwen2.5-VL-7B base model across all nine benchmark splits. On the reasoning-heavy LongVideoReason-eval, it surpasses GPT-4o (60.7) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (67.3) by 19.6 and 13.0 points respectively, while using a maximum of 128 frames. The model also demonstrates superior frame efficiency, matching or exceeding the base model's 128-frame accuracy with only ~48 frames on MLVU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest open-source 7B baselines, VideoZoomer wins on every reported benchmark: it outperforms Video-R1 by +3.8 on MLVU-dev, +5.7 on LongVideoBench, +4.1 on VideoMME, +2.8 on LVBench, +6.6 on VideoMMLU, +2.4 on VideoMMMU, and +7.5 on LongVideoReason-eval. Compared to its own base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B, the agentic zoom strategy adds +10.5 on MLVU-dev and +9.5 on LongVideoReason-eval, with the largest relative gains on fine-grained perception tasks (e.g., Action Count on MLVU jumps from 13.6 to 50.5). Against proprietary models, VideoZoomer surpasses GPT-4o on VideoMMLU (67.9 vs 44.9) and LongVideoReason (80.3 vs 60.7), and beats Gemini-1.5-Pro on LVBench (41.5 vs 33.1), VideoMMLU (67.9 vs 53.9), and LongVideoReason (80.3 vs 67.3), though it trails both on VideoMME overall (65.2 vs 71.9/75.0) and LongVideoBench (57.7 vs 66.7/64.0). On the reasoning-specific LongVideoReason-eval, VideoZoomer also outperforms LongVILA-R1 (80.3 vs 67.9) despite using a smaller effective frame budget, highlighting the efficiency advantage of its dynamic zoom policy over static context extension.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-for-multimodal-reasoning line (GRPO from DeepSeekMath, DAPO) to a multi-turn, tool-integrated setting, introducing a token-level loss mask over tool-call trajectories. It departs from prior long-video approaches in two key ways: (1) replacing static frame selection (uniform sampling or one-shot salient-frame selectors) with an iterative, agent-driven 'glance-then-zoom' policy, and (2) training a small 7B open-source model end-to-end rather than relying on prompting proprietary LLMs as in VideoDeepResearch or Deep Video Discovery. The cold-start SFT phase with distilled exemplar and reflection trajectories is a novel data-construction recipe that bridges imitation learning and RL exploration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 7B model with dynamic temporal focusing can match or exceed proprietary systems (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-Pro) on long-video reasoning while consuming substantially fewer frames (e.g., ~48 frames vs. 128 for comparable or better accuracy), offering a practical and efficient paradigm for video understanding. Open questions include whether the agentic policy generalizes to even longer videos (hours-scale), whether the reliance on proprietary models (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Pro) for cold-start data distillation limits reproducibility, and whether the two-stage SFT+RL recipe transfers to other agentic multimodal tasks beyond video. The finding that doubling SFT data degrades performance underscores that reasoning-pattern diversity, not volume, drives capability.
