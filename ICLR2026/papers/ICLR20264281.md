## Problem

Fine-tuning T2V-based controllable generation pipelines (e.g., DiT + ControlNet) for video restoration suffers from distribution drift caused by imperfect text-video alignment in training data, leading to unrealistic textures and degraded temporal coherence relative to the native T2V model. Existing approaches either rely on post-hoc temporal enhancements or accept this quality gap, and simply using a more accurate VLM captioner does not fully resolve the modality gap in the T2V model's latent space.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality input video (resized to 1024×1024, 17–37 frames) and a text description automatically generated by CogVLM2-Video (to match the T2V model's training configuration). The LQ video is encoded by a 3D VAE, passed through a lightweight control feature projector to strip degradation artifacts, then patchified and noised as visual tokens. The output is a high-quality restored video at 1024×1024; for higher-resolution inputs, aggregation sampling with direct block concatenation (rather than Gaussian-weighted averaging) is used to avoid overlapping-region artifacts.

## Method

Vivid-VR builds on CogVideoX1.5-5B with a redesigned ControlNet for input-video conditioning, training only the control feature projector, ControlNet blocks (N/7 of DiT's N blocks), and dual-branch connectors while freezing the DiT backbone and VAE. The core training innovation is a concept distillation strategy: the T2V model itself is used to synthesize 100K training pairs by adding noise (T/2 steps) to source videos and denoising under text conditioning, producing samples with inherent text-visual alignment in the T2V latent space; these are mixed with 500K real pairs to mitigate distribution drift. Architecturally, a control feature projector (three cascaded spatiotemporal residual blocks) filters degradation artifacts from VAE-encoded LQ latents, and each of the N ControlNet connectors fuses control features into DiT via a dual-branch design combining an MLP for static feature mapping with a cross-attention module for dynamic control feature retrieval, enabling both content preservation and adaptive signal modulation.

## Results

Vivid-VR achieves the best no-reference quality scores across all six benchmarks. On the UGC50 real-world testset, it attains NIQE 4.361, MUSIQ 67.61, CLIP-IQA 0.450, DOVER 14.46, and MD-VQA 88.89, outperforming the strongest competitor (SeedVR2-7B) by 1.87 on NIQE, 21.49 on MUSIQ, and 6.25 on DOVER. On the AIGC50 testset, Vivid-VR scores DOVER 14.51 and MD-VQA 89.69, again leading all baselines. On full-reference metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) for synthetic benchmarks, Vivid-VR trails SeedVR2-7B and DOVE, which the authors attribute to the known misalignment between pixel-level metrics and human perceptual preference in generative restoration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vivid-VR outperforms all eight baselines on every no-reference metric across all six testsets. Against the strongest T2V-based competitor SeedVR2-7B, Vivid-VR gains +1.87 NIQE (lower is better), +21.49 MUSIQ, +0.180 CLIP-IQA, and +6.25 DOVER on UGC50, and +1.79 NIQE, +17.83 MUSIQ, and +3.17 DOVER on AIGC50. The one-step diffusion method DOVE is the closest competitor on YouHQ40 (DOVER 14.00 vs. 14.61) and AIGC50 (DOVER 14.49 vs. 14.51), but Vivid-VR still leads on all five no-reference metrics. Reconstruction-based Real-ESRGAN and image-based SUPIR trail significantly, with SUPIR showing the largest gap on MUSIQ (e.g., 58.25 vs. 67.61 on UGC50) due to its lack of temporal modeling. On full-reference metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) for synthetic benchmarks, SeedVR2-7B and DOVE achieve higher scores (e.g., SeedVR2-7B reaches PSNR 26.07 on SPMCS vs. Vivid-VR's 21.73), which the authors attribute to the known limitation of pixel-level metrics in generative restoration where multiple plausible HQ outputs exist.

## Relation to Prior Work

Vivid-VR sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the progression from reconstruction-based video restoration (EDVR, BasicVSR++) through GAN-based methods to diffusion-based generative restoration; (2) the recent shift from T2I diffusion models (SUPIR, UAV) to T2V DiT-based models (SeedVR, STAR, DOVE) for temporal coherence; and (3) the ControlNet paradigm for conditioning generation on input content. Its key departure from prior T2V-based restoration work (SeedVR, STAR) is identifying and addressing 'distribution drift' — the degradation in texture realism and temporal consistency that arises when fine-tuning a pretrained T2V model on imperfectly aligned text-video pairs. The concept distillation strategy (using the T2V model itself to synthesize aligned training samples) and the redesigned dual-branch ControlNet connector (MLP + cross-attention) are the paper's architectural and training contributions, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously underappreciated failure mode — distribution drift from imperfect multimodal alignment during fine-tuning of controllable T2V pipelines — and provides a practical remedy that is broadly applicable to any scenario where a pretrained generative model is adapted to a new conditioning task. The finding that even a stronger VLM captioner (Qwen2.5-VL) cannot eliminate the latent-space misalignment is a useful negative result that redirects attention toward data-level solutions rather than captioning improvements. Key limitations include inheriting CogVideoX1.5-5B's 50-step inference cost (~6K GPU hours for training) and the open question of whether one-step or few-step distillation can preserve the quality gains. Extending to larger T2V models (14B+) and reducing inference latency remain important directions for practical deployment.
