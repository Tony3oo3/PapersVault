## Problem

Discrete Flow Models (DFMs) suffer from slow iterative sampling, and existing acceleration methods (DCD, ReDi) rely on distillation-based finetuning that incurs 10–20% additional training cost on top of the base model. No prior work has addressed whether comparable few-step speedups can be achieved with only a lightweight preprocessing step requiring orders of magnitude less compute, without a pretrained teacher.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263655/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The preprocessing phase takes a dataset of M discrete sequences (e.g., SMILES strings of length 32–72 over vocabularies of size 40–74, or 28×28 binary / 32×32×3 8-bit pixel images) and produces M paired source-target samples by tracing each data point backward along a closed-form velocity field for T steps. At inference time, the trained model takes a single uniform prior sample over the discrete state space and produces a generated sequence in as few as 1–64 steps (molecules, MNIST-Binary) or 8–1024 steps (CIFAR-10), evaluated via validity/uniqueness/novelty for molecules and FID/IS for images.

## Method

PAIRFLOW derives, for the first time, closed-form forward and backward velocity fields for Discrete Flow Matching under a uniform prior, where the velocity at each token is a weighted mixture over dataset points with weights determined by the Hamming distance between the current sequence and each data point. The key algorithmic step is to use the closed-form backward velocity (Eqn. 12) to trace each data point back toward the source distribution for T iterations, yielding a source-target pair that is guaranteed to cover all training data by construction. This avoids the impractical coverage problem of forward sampling (only ~63% of the dataset is recovered even with |X₁| source samples) and eliminates the need for a pretrained teacher. The resulting pairs produce straighter probability paths (lower conditional Total Correlation), enabling the model to recover data with fewer token transitions. The entire pairing procedure is fully parallelizable and costs at most 1.7% of full model training compute, yet yields few-step performance matching or exceeding distillation-based methods that require up to 143× more compute.

## Results

On QM9, PAIRFLOW achieves 223.4 valid molecules at 1 step (12.8× over UDLM's 17.5) and 416.0 at 2 steps (231% improvement over UDLM's 125.5), while requiring only 0.2% of base training compute for preprocessing. On MNIST-Binary, PAIRFLOW reduces 1-step FID from 130.57 (UDLM) to 40.58 (68.9% improvement), outperforming UDLM+DCD (53.84) at 1 step. On ZINC-250k, PAIRFLOW achieves 146.3 valid molecules at 2 steps versus 75.9 for UDLM+ReDi. The preprocessing cost is at most 1.7% of full training (1.4 min on MNIST-Binary vs. 80 min base training), compared to 50–61% for DCD and ReDi finetuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAIRFLOW consistently outperforms UDLM+ReDi in all few-step settings: on QM9, 223.4 vs. 59.7 (1-step) and 416.0 vs. 232.4 (2-step) validity; on ZINC-250k, 146.3 vs. 75.9 (2-step). Against UDLM+DCD, PAIRFLOW is competitive but slightly behind at 1–2 steps on QM9 (223.4 vs. 323.0 at 1 step; 416.0 vs. 530.8 at 2 steps), yet requires only 0.69% of DCD's total compute. On MNIST-Binary, PAIRFLOW beats UDLM+DCD at 1 step (FID 40.58 vs. 53.84) and is comparable at 2 steps (15.61 vs. 16.09), while being 28.6× faster in preprocessing. Compared to the base UDLM, PAIRFLOW delivers dramatic few-step gains (12.8× validity improvement at 1 step on QM9, 68.9% FID reduction at 1 step on MNIST-Binary) with negligible overhead. When combined with subsequent distillation, PAIRFLOW+DCD and PAIRFLOW+ReDi push beyond all prior distillation results, e.g., 453.8 vs. 323.0 (1-step QM9 validity) and 221.5 vs. 75.9 (2-step ZINC-250k validity).

## Relation to Prior Work

PAIRFLOW extends the ReFlow paradigm (Liu & Gong, 2023) of straightening probability paths via source-target coupling from continuous to discrete flow models, but departs critically from ReDi (Yoo et al., 2025) by eliminating the need for a pretrained teacher. Where ReDi iteratively generates pairs by sampling from a trained model and then finetunes, PAIRFLOW derives closed-form forward and backward velocity fields for discrete flow matching under a uniform prior, enabling direct data-to-source inversion without any model in the loop. The closed-form velocity construction draws on analogous results for continuous flow matching (Karras et al., 2022; Bertrand et al., 2025) but is, to the authors' knowledge, the first derivation in the discrete setting, with the Hamming distance playing the role that Euclidean distance plays in the continuous case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a fully parallelizable preprocessing step costing at most 1.7% of base-model training matches or exceeds distillation-based acceleration (DCD, ReDi) that requires 10–20% additional compute, and the resulting models also serve as stronger teachers for subsequent distillation. However, the method is tied to the uniform-prior / uniform-state model family (UDLM), and the continuous-domain extension (Appendix E) shows diminishing returns at higher dimensions, suggesting the closed-form pairing advantage may not transfer cleanly to high-dimensional continuous data. Open questions include whether analogous closed-form constructions exist for non-uniform priors or structured (e.g., masked) corruption schedules, and whether the Hamming-distance-based weighting can be generalized to richer token similarity metrics.
