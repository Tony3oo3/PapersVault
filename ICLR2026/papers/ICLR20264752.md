## Problem

Energy-based voting (E-voting) is an effective test-time scaling strategy for recurrent reasoning models, but it requires an explicitly defined energy function that most promising recurrent architectures (HRM, recurrent transformers) do not possess. The paper asks whether a model-agnostic voting criterion exists that works without energy functions, and whether a simple, lightweight recurrent architecture can match or surpass state-of-the-art performance when paired with such a strategy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264752/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Inputs are 9×9 Sudoku boards with 17–42 pre-filled digits (depending on difficulty tier: standard, hard, extreme) or 30×30 binary maze grids specifying passable/impassable cells plus a start and goal position. Outputs are complete filled boards or full optimal paths, evaluated by board accuracy (all cells correct simultaneously). At test time, K independent trajectories are generated from K randomly sampled initial latent states, and a single trajectory is selected via the voting rule before producing the final output.

## Method

C-voting selects among K candidate trajectories by computing, for each, the average top-1 softmax probability across all prediction positions (e.g., blank Sudoku cells) and choosing the candidate with the highest average confidence. This requires only a readout head producing class probabilities—no energy function—and is justified by the observation that, under calibration, maximizing average top-1 probability approximates maximizing expected accuracy across positions. The companion architecture ItrSA++ is a ~3M-parameter recurrent block that mixes a randomly initialized latent state with the embedded input via cross-attention, applies S self-attention layers, and a SwiGLU MLP, all with RMSNorm, repeated T times. This design avoids the two-fold hierarchical structure of HRM and the Kuramoto oscillator dynamics of AKOrN, relying instead on plain attention with randomized initialization to enable diverse candidate trajectories that C-voting can exploit.

## Results

Using board accuracy (proportion of fully correct boards) as the metric, ItrSA++ with C-voting achieves 95.2% on Sudoku-extreme, 94.4% on Sudoku-hard, and 78.6% on Maze-hard with ~3M parameters. C-voting applied to the pre-trained AKOrN model yields 94.4% on Sudoku-hard, a 4.9% absolute improvement over the original E-voting (89.5%). The model requires roughly one-ninth the parameters of HRM (~3M vs. ~27M) while matching or exceeding its performance across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HRM (~27M params), ItrSA++ with C-voting achieves a dramatic 40.2-point gain on Sudoku-extreme (95.2% vs. 55.0%) and a 4.1-point gain on Maze-hard (78.6% vs. 74.5%), while using only ~3M parameters. Against AKOrN with its native E-voting, C-voting applied to the same pre-trained AKOrN model improves Sudoku-hard board accuracy from 89.5% to 94.4% (+4.9 points), demonstrating that confidence-based selection is a superior trajectory criterion even when an explicit energy function is available. ItrSA++ with C-voting also outperforms AKOrN on Sudoku-hard (94.4% vs. 89.5%) and on Sudoku-extreme, where AKOrN's performance is not explicitly reported but ItrSA++ reaches 95.2%. On Maze-hard, AKOrN fails entirely (0% board accuracy), making ItrSA++ the only method that solves the task. C-voting applied to HRM yields only limited gains because HRM was originally designed with a fixed initial state, so introducing per-sample randomness destabilizes its dynamics; nonetheless, non-trivial improvements are still observed with more samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions C-voting as a model-agnostic alternative to E-voting (Miyato et al., 2025), which is restricted to recurrent models with explicit energy functions such as AKOrN. The voting paradigm itself draws on self-consistency in LLMs (Wang et al., 2023) and the broader test-time scaling literature for recurrent models (Anil et al., 2022; Geiping et al., 2025), but the specific criterion—average top-1 probability as a confidence proxy—is novel in this context. The companion architecture ItrSA++ repurposes cross-attention from Perceiver (Jaegle et al., 2021) and SwiGLU (Shazeer, 2020) within a recurrent attention loop, deliberately stripping away the hierarchical structure of HRM and the Kuramoto oscillator dynamics of AKOrN in favor of a simpler design optimized for randomized initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

C-voting's key practical value is its universality: any recurrent model with randomized initialization can adopt it without architectural modification, unlike E-voting which requires a closed-form energy. The 9× parameter reduction (3M vs. 27M) while outperforming HRM on Sudoku-extreme is a notable efficiency result, though the comparison is confounded by HRM's original fixed-initialization design. A significant open question highlighted by the Maze-hard results is that C-voting's effectiveness is bounded by the model's calibration—when the model is overconfident on wrong predictions, voting cannot rescue performance. The authors suggest extensions to masked language models, text infilling, and image inpainting, but these remain untested.
