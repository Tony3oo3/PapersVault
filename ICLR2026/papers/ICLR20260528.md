## Problem

Existing training-free guidance methods for diffusion models, particularly DPS-based approaches, rely on a single-point approximation of the conditional expectation E[x₀|xₜ] to compute guidance gradients, introducing systematic bias that causes cross-condition interference—guiding toward one condition inadvertently degrades other conditions that should remain decoupled. The paper also argues that prior evaluations report only alignment metrics, masking the trade-off between condition compliance and overall sample quality (e.g., FID, molecular stability).

## Input / Output

At each reverse diffusion step, the method consumes the current noisy state xₜ, a user-defined differentiable loss on clean data (e.g., pixel-wise degradation loss for inpainting/SR/deblurring, a classifier cross-entropy for handwriting, a quantum-property predictor for molecules, or a CLIP Gram-matrix style loss), and a pretrained denoising network. It outputs a corrected intermediate state x_{t-1}^{new} that incorporates the averaged Monte-Carlo guidance direction. The approach is fully plug-and-play: no retraining of the diffusion model or any auxiliary network is required, and it operates at standard resolutions (256×256 for images, 512×512 for SD3.5, 3D coordinates for molecules, 2D stroke trajectories for handwriting).

## Method

ABMS replaces DPS's single-point gradient estimate with a Monte-Carlo average over M samples drawn from the one-step reverse transition kernel p(x_{t-1}|xₜ): for each sample x_{t-1}^{(m)}, the pretrained denoiser produces a clean estimate x̂₀, the conditional loss is evaluated, and the results are averaged to form the guidance direction. The paper proves (Proposition 1) that under K-Lipschitz continuity of the loss and monotonic improvement of denoiser accuracy along the reverse chain, ABMS attains a strictly lower expected estimation error than plain DPS, with the gap governed by the Jensen term and the law of total covariance. The resulting guidance vector is rescaled to lie on a hypersphere of radius √n·σₜ (following DSG's spherical Gaussian constraint) with a cosine-scheduled guidance rate ωₜ to prevent manifold drift. The strategy is compatible with higher-order and stochastic samplers (DDIM, flow matching) and requires only M additional forward passes through the denoiser per step, which can be parallelized; empirically M=3 already captures most of the benefit.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 image inverse problems, ABMS achieves the best FID across all three tasks: 19.25 (inpainting), 33.06 (super-resolution), and 41.65 (Gaussian deblurring), outperforming DSG (23.63, 34.28, 45.27) and DPS (30.57, 41.36, 52.13). In the dual-conditional handwriting task, ABMS at guidance scale 0.1 preserves style score at 0.878 while boosting content to 0.999, whereas DSG at the same scale collapses style to 0.534. For molecular inverse design on QM9, ABMS (M=3) achieves the lowest MAE across all six quantum properties (e.g., 0.4182 for Δε vs. DSG's 0.4558) while maintaining molecular stability within 2% of EEGSDE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPS (Chung et al., 2023), ABMS improves FID by 11.32 (inpainting), 8.30 (SR), and 10.48 (deblurring) on ImageNet, with consistent gains in PSNR and SSIM as well. Compared to LGD (Song et al., 2023), ABMS achieves substantially lower FID (19.25 vs. 28.65 on inpainting) while also improving reconstruction fidelity. The closest competitor is DSG (Yang et al., 2024), which also constrains guidance to the data manifold; ABMS outperforms DSG on FID across all three image tasks (e.g., 19.25 vs. 23.63 on inpainting) and on PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS, indicating that more accurate gradient estimation complements manifold constraints. In molecular design (Table 3), ABMS (M=3) beats both CEDM (Hoogeboom et al., 2022) and EEGSDE (Bao et al., 2022) on MAE for all six quantum properties while keeping molecular stability (MS) within 2% of EEGSDE, e.g., 0.4182 vs. 0.4854 MAE for Δε. The most striking comparison is in the handwriting task (Table 1): DSG at scale 0.1 drops style score from 0.899 to 0.534 (a 40% relative loss), whereas ABMS at the same scale retains 0.878, directly demonstrating the cross-condition interference that ABMS mitigates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS, Chung et al., 2023) as its foundational guidance mechanism and adopts the spherical Gaussian constraint from DSG (Yang et al., 2024) for guidance magnitude control. It departs from the line of prior improvements (LGD-MC, MPGD, DSG) that focused on preventing intermediate states from deviating from the data manifold, by instead targeting the root cause of imprecise guidance: the single-point Tweedie estimate's bias in approximating the conditional expectation. The proposed ABMS strategy inserts one additional backward denoising step with Monte-Carlo averaging before evaluating the guidance loss, a conceptually simple modification that the authors argue prior manifold-preserving methods overlooked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-focus evaluation framework (condition compliance vs. global sample quality) exposes a previously under-characterized cross-condition interference problem: even manifold-preserving methods like DSG significantly degrade orthogonal attributes (e.g., style score drops from 0.899 to 0.166) as guidance scale increases. The plug-and-play nature of ABMS and its validation across heterogeneous tasks (handwriting trajectories, image inverse problems, 3D molecular design, flow-matching text-to-image) make it a practical drop-in improvement for the broader DPS-based guidance community. Open questions remain about scaling to very few-step samplers, the optimal number of Monte-Carlo samples under compute constraints, and whether the monotonic-denoiser-accuracy assumption (A2) holds universally across architectures.
