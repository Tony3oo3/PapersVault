## Problem

Training LLM agents in partially observable, long-horizon environments is bottlenecked by the scarcity of high-quality supervision: SFT depends on costly expert demonstrations with limited diversity, while RL struggles to discover high-return trajectories under sparse rewards. Existing post-training methods overlook a readily available source of supervision—unintended yet successful goals that agents accomplish within their own rollouts—leaving most agent-generated experience unused.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265029/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes agent-generated trajectories (sequences of observations and actions collected during rollouts in POMDP environments), natural language instructions, and a small set of ground-truth demonstrations. An auxiliary LLM (Llama-3.3-70B) processes each full trajectory in a zero-shot manner to produce multiple relabeled goal-instruction pairs and per-action relevance labels, which are stored in a dynamically updated buffer of fixed size. The output is a fine-tuned Llama-3.2-1B agent policy trained via a combined SFT objective over both ground-truth and relabeled demonstrations, with the relabeled buffer refreshed on-policy at every training step.

## Method

Hindsight Supervised Learning (HSL) adds a parallel training branch with three stages: (1) the agent rolls out on sampled instructions to collect trajectories; (2) an auxiliary LLM performs zero-shot goal identification (extracting all K≥0 goals actually achieved along the trajectory, not just the final goal on failures) and action relevance labeling (marking each action as relevant or irrelevant to each relabeled goal); (3) the agent is fine-tuned on the relabeled demonstrations using a masked SFT loss that zeroes out gradients for irrelevant actions, combined with a reweighting scheme that up-samples trajectories solving harder tasks more optimally. A theoretical analysis derives an upper bound on the expert-agent policy discrepancy showing that SFT on relabeled demonstrations strictly tightens the bound, with the gap governed by occupancy coverage and hindsight-expert optimality. The framework is modular and composable with existing post-training pipelines (SFT, DPO/ETO), and the on-policy buffer refresh keeps the hindsight distribution aligned with the agent's evolving behavior.

## Results

On ALFWorld, SFT+HSL achieves 93.57% (seen) and 97.76% (unseen) success rate, improving over SFT (82.14%/78.36%) by 11.4 and 19.4 points respectively. DPO+HSL reaches 92.86%/94.78%, surpassing DPO (85.71%/82.84%) by 7.2 and 11.9 points. On PlanCraft, SFT+HSL and DPO+HSL both reach ~75% success rate (vs. 70.00% and 71.25% for SFT and DPO alone), while on WebShop the gains are smaller (66.97% and 70.52% vs. 63.81% and 69.54%). Notably, HSL with fewer than one quarter of ground-truth demonstrations outperforms SFT-only or DPO-only models trained on the full dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HSL consistently outperforms all five baselines across all three benchmarks. Against REACT (Yao et al., 2023), which uses Llama-3.3-70B directly for reasoning and action selection, SFT+HSL achieves 93.57% vs. 33.57% on ALFWorld seen, demonstrating that relabeling trajectories is far easier than predicting actions. BEHAVIORCLONE (Zeng et al., 2024) and BAGEL (Murty et al., 2024) both leverage Llama-3.3-70B for data synthesis but use a fixed dataset; HSL's on-policy, continuously updated relabeling buffer yields 9.3–10.5 point gains over BAGEL on ALFWorld unseen. SELFIMIT (Shi et al., 2023), which fine-tunes only on the agent's own successful trajectories, underperforms even plain SFT on ALFWorld unseen (76.87% vs. 78.36%), confirming that mining all achieved goals (including uninstructed ones) is critical. Finally, HSL provides additive gains on top of DPO/ETO (Song et al., 2024), improving ALFWorld unseen from 82.84% to 94.78% and WebShop from 69.54% to 70.52%, with the largest relative gains on ALFWorld's diverse, long-horizon goal space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) from goal-conditioned RL (Andrychowicz et al., 2017) to the LLM agent setting, replacing the offline RL objective with SFT/DPO and leveraging an auxiliary LLM to perform the relabeling via zero-shot reasoning over partial observations. It departs from prior language-conditioned HER variants (Cideron et al., 2020; Ghosh et al., 2019) by relabeling all goals achieved along a trajectory rather than only the final goal of failed episodes, and by maintaining an on-policy, continuously refreshed relabeled buffer rather than a fixed synthetic dataset. Two additional techniques—irrelevant-action masking and demonstration reweighting—address the suboptimality of trajectories collected under different instructions, and a theoretical upper bound (Theorem 1) connects the relabeled-data SFT loss to the expert-agent discrepancy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HSL offers a practical, plug-in mechanism for improving LLM agent training that is sample-efficient (outperforming full-data baselines with only a quarter of ground-truth demonstrations) and compatible with existing SFT and DPO pipelines, reducing the costly reliance on expert demonstrations. Key limitations include the dependence on a powerful external relabeling LLM, the binary relevance labeling assumption, the requirement for a predefined goal space, and the continued need for some ground-truth data to stabilize training. Open questions include extending the framework to open-world environments without predefined goals, jointly learning the relabeler and agent, and handling unintended goals that may be low-value or unsafe in real deployments.
