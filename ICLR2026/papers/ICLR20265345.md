## Problem

Existing Test-Time Training (TTT) methods update fast weights with extremely small mini-batches (16–64 tokens), resulting in GPU FLOPs utilization below 5% and limiting nonlinear state sizes to 0.1–5% of total model parameters. This makes them computationally inefficient on modern hardware, difficult to implement without error-prone custom kernels, and unsuitable for N-dimensional data like images or videos where fine-grained per-token causality is not natural.

## Input / Output

For novel view synthesis, the model consumes up to 128 posed RGB images at 960×536 (≈1M tokens) plus Plücker ray embeddings for camera poses, and outputs rendered novel-view images at the same resolution. For language modeling, it processes 1D text sequences up to 32,768 tokens and predicts next-token distributions. For autoregressive video diffusion, it takes a text prompt plus previously denoised latent frame chunks and generates subsequent video frames, handling sequences up to 56,160 visual tokens (≈107K tokens under teacher-forcing with interleaved noisy/clean chunks). In all cases, the chunk boundary aligns with the data's internal structure: the full image set, three consecutive video frames, or a fixed 2K–4K token window for text.

## Method

LaCT replaces per-token or small-batch fast-weight updates with extremely large chunk updates (2,048 to 1M tokens), treating tokens within each chunk as an unordered set. The fast-weight network is a nonlinear SwiGLU-MLP whose three weight matrices are updated via a summed gradient over the entire chunk, followed by either L2 weight normalization or a Muon (Newton-Schulz orthogonalization) update rule. A window attention layer (causal or bidirectional) is integrated in parallel with the TTT layer, sharing Q/K/V projections, to capture local dependencies that the set-based TTT update ignores. Different orderings of the update and apply operations produce equivalent attention masks (full, block-wise causal, shifted, strided), allowing the same architecture to handle bidirectional image sets, causal text, and interleaved noisy/clean video chunks. This design achieves up to 70% GPU utilization in pure PyTorch, scales the fast-weight state to 40% of total model parameters, and supports context parallelism by sharding tokens within a chunk across devices.

## Results

On novel view synthesis (GSO, 512×512, 48 views), LaCT achieves 39.1 PSNR with O(n) prefill compute (1.4s) versus 39.3 PSNR for full attention (16.1s prefill) and 33.7 PSNR for Perceiver attention (16.8s prefill). On the DL3DV scene dataset at 960×536, LaCT reaches 28.9 PSNR with 128 input views (~1M tokens), surpassing LongLRM (limited to 32 views) and 3D Gaussian Splatting. For language modeling at 32K context, LaCT (760M and 3B) achieves lower per-position validation loss at larger token indices and higher S-NIAH retrieval accuracy than both GLA and DeltaNet. On autoregressive video diffusion (14B params, 56K tokens), LaCT matches full-attention denoising loss while outperforming Mamba2+SWA and SWA-only baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaCT clearly outperforms Perceiver-style register attention on NVS quality (37.6 vs 33.7 PSNR at 48 views, 256×256; 39.1 vs 33.7 at 512×512) while also being ~12× faster in prefill (1.4s vs 16.8s) and slightly faster in rendering (38.7 vs 34.4 FPS). On scene-level NVS (DL3DV), LaCT surpasses LongLRM, which is limited to 32 input views, and outperforms optimization-based 3D Gaussian Splatting with sparse views, remaining competitive up to 128 input views (1M tokens). For language modeling, LaCT consistently outperforms both GLA and DeltaNet (augmented with the same sliding window attention) in per-position validation loss at longer token indices and in S-NIAH retrieval accuracy at both 760M and 3B scales, with the Muon-optimized variant further improving over the momentum variant. On autoregressive video diffusion, LaCT achieves comparable denoising loss to the full block-wise causal attention baseline while clearly outperforming both Mamba2+SWA and SWA-only baselines across all evaluated timesteps and video lengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Test-Time Training (TTT) framework of Sun et al. (2024), which introduced online-adapted fast weights as a sub-quadratic alternative to softmax attention, by inverting the conventional design choice of small mini-batch sizes (16–64 tokens) in favor of extremely large chunks (2K–1M tokens). It draws on GAU's in-layer hybrid of local attention and recurrence for the window-attention/TTT combination, adapts Muon (Jordan et al., 2024) as a test-time optimizer for fast-weight updates, and builds on Wan 2.1 as a video diffusion backbone. Conceptually, LaCT is closest to InfiniAttention's chunk-level recurrence but replaces the limited delta-rule update with a more expressive nonlinear SwiGLU fast-weight network, and departs from Block-Recurrent Transformers by using learned neural memories rather than register tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical contribution is removing the dependency on error-prone custom GPU kernels: LaCT achieves up to 70% A100 utilization with dozens of lines of pure PyTorch, versus <5% for prior TTT methods even with hand-written kernels. This efficiency unlock enables scaling nonlinear state size to 40% of total model parameters (an order of magnitude beyond the 0.1–5% typical of prior work), which the ablations show is critical for long-context performance. Open questions include whether the large-chunk paradigm degrades on tasks with strong per-token causal structure beyond what sliding window attention can compensate, whether the approach extends to even longer contexts (beyond 1M tokens) without further architectural changes, and whether the Muon test-time optimizer's benefits generalize to other fast-weight architectures. The paper's validation across three modalities (image sets, text, video) strengthens confidence but leaves open whether the optimal chunk size is truly task-agnostic or requires careful tuning per data structure.
