## Problem

The O2NC framework achieves optimal O(δ⁻¹ε⁻³) oracle complexity for finding Goldstein stationary points of non-smooth non-convex functions, but its correspondence to practical SGDM relies on auxiliary randomization (uniform interpolation or exponential scaling) that is absent in real implementations. While a general derandomization is provably impossible (Jordan et al., 2023), this paper asks whether the gap can be closed for the important subclass of weakly convex functions, and shows that it can.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes stochastic subgradients evaluated directly at the current iterate (no random intermediate points) along with an initial iterate w₀ and user-specified hyperparameters (γ, η, D, K, T). The output is a uniformly random block average w̄_T drawn from K blocks of T iterations each, which is guaranteed to be a Goldstein (δ,ε)-stationary point or a (μ,ε)-regularized stationary point. A key structural constraint is that the weak convexity parameter ρ must satisfy ρ ≤ O(δ⁻¹ε⁻¹) (or ρ ≤ O(μ^{1/2}ε^{-3/2}) for the restarted variant) to preserve the optimal rate.

## Method

The core insight is that for a ρ-weakly convex risk R, the function value change R(w_n) − R(w_{n−1}) is upper-bounded by ⟨ĝ_n, Δ_n⟩ + (γ/2)‖Δ_n‖² for any γ ≥ ρ, which motivates framing increment updates as online learning over quadratic losses without any random interpolation. Two OGD instantiations are provided: (i) clipped OGD over a D-ball, yielding a deterministic clipped-SGDM variant with complexity O(δ⁻¹ε⁻³ + ρ³δ² + δ⁻¹), and (ii) periodically restarted unconstrained OGD that resets the momentum to zero every T steps, yielding a momentum-restarted SGDM variant with complexity O(μ^{1/2}ε^{-7/2} + ρ^{7/3}μ^{-2/3} + μ^{1/2}). In both cases the dominant term matches the optimal dimension-free rate, and the weak convexity parameter ρ appears only in subdominant terms, meaning stronger stationarity targets tolerate higher non-convexity. The restarted variant is empirically validated on ResNet-101 and ViT trained on CIFAR-10, where it outperforms standard SGDM by up to ~2 percentage points in test accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-101, D-O2NC (T=50) achieves 94.81% test accuracy, outperforming SGDM (93.91%) by 0.9 percentage points, while also reducing training loss from 2.55×10⁻³ to 1.38×10⁻³. On CIFAR-10 with ViT, D-O2NC (T=50) reaches 86.22% test accuracy versus SGDM's 84.26%, a 1.96-point improvement, with training loss dropping from 2.78×10⁻¹ to 2.44×10⁻¹. Theoretically, D-O2NC achieves the optimal dimension-free rate O(δ⁻¹ε⁻³) for Goldstein (δ,ε)-stationarity while remaining fully deterministic (up to stochastic gradient estimation), with the weak convexity parameter ρ admissible up to O(δ⁻¹ε⁻¹) without dominating the optimal rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary experimental baseline is standard SGDM (Mai & Johansson, 2020), which D-O2NC consistently outperforms across all metrics: on CIFAR-10, D-O2NC (T=50) beats SGDM by 0.9 pp (ResNet-101) and 1.96 pp (ViT) in test accuracy, while also converging faster in training loss. The paper also provides a theoretical comparison with SGD (Davis & Drusvyatskiy, 2019) and SGDM (Mai & Johansson, 2020), which achieve O(ρ/ε⁴) and O(ρ²/ε⁴) rates for Moreau-envelope stationarity respectively; D-O2NC's O(δ⁻¹ε⁻³) Goldstein-stationarity rate is not directly comparable but implies the same O(δ⁻¹ε⁻³) complexity under the restrictive choice δ=ε/(2ρ), while additionally tolerating larger ρ. Against INGD (Davis et al., 2022), D-O2NC is strictly superior in being both deterministic and applicable to stochastic oracles, whereas INGD is randomized by design, dimension-dependent (O(d·log(ρ)/(δε))), and does not extend to the stochastic setting. The momentum-restart mechanism in D-O2NC is the key differentiator from vanilla SGDM, and the paper confirms that the advantage persists under varied momentum (0.9, 0.95, 0.99) and learning rate (0.01, 0.05, 0.1) configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the O2NC framework of Cutkosky et al. (2023), which achieves optimal O(δ⁻¹ε⁻³) oracle complexity for non-smooth non-convex optimization but relies on random interpolation or scaling to recover SGDM. The key departure is showing that for ρ-weakly convex objectives, the randomization can be eliminated entirely by reformulating the online learning module around quadratic losses ⟨ĝₙ,·⟩ + (γ/2)‖·‖², yielding a fully deterministic algorithm. The (μ,ε)-regularized stationarity criterion is inspired by Zhang & Cutkosky (2024) but uses a supremum norm penalty to achieve exact equivalence with Goldstein stationarity, and the periodically restarted OGD variant draws on the unconstrained OMD structure of E-O2NC while removing exponential weighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result draws a clean theoretical boundary: Jordan et al. (2023) proved no dimension-free deterministic rate exists for general non-smooth non-convex functions, yet weak convexity is precisely the structural property that unlocks derandomization at optimal rates. Practically, the momentum-restarted SGDM variant is a one-line modification (reset momentum every T steps) that consistently improves test accuracy by 1–2 percentage points over standard SGDM on ResNet and ViT, suggesting the restart mechanism may be broadly useful. Open questions include extending the periodically restarted technique to Adam and schedule-free SGD, and closing the gap between the O(ε⁻⁵) rate obtained via Goldstein-to-Moreau-envelope translation and the optimal O(ε⁻⁴) rate for Moreau envelope stationarity.
