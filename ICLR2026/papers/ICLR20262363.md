## Problem

Bidirectional video diffusion models require all frames to be denoised jointly, making real-time motion control impossible since control inputs must be fully specified before generation begins. Existing autoregressive video diffusion models are limited to text-to-video or simple control signals (pose, camera motion), and suffer from severe quality degradation and motion artifacts in few-step generation due to train-test mismatch and error accumulation. No prior AR video diffusion model achieves real-time, fine-grained motion-controllable image-to-video generation with visual quality competitive with bidirectional baselines.

## Input / Output

The model takes a reference image (first frame), per-frame motion trajectories (keypoint coordinate heatmaps where each point is a bright spot with intensity 0–1 mimicking touch force), and positive/negative text prompts as input, and produces video frames sequentially one latent at a time (frame-wise, not chunk-wise). Videos are resized to predefined bucket resolutions (e.g., 480×368, 640×368, 368×640) to handle varying aspect ratios. The KV cache holds 7 frames of causal context, and the first-frame latency is 0.44 s on a single NVIDIA H20 GPU, enabling frame-by-frame interactive control where unsatisfactory frames can be regenerated on the fly.

## Method

AR-Drag uses a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 fine-tunes Wan2.1-1.3B-I2V with trajectory, text, and reference-image conditioning, then distills it into a 3-step causal AR student via DMD and adversarial losses; critically, the Self-Rollout mechanism replaces teacher forcing by denoising each frame step-by-step from pure noise during training and updating the KV cache only with the fully denoised self-generated frame, preserving the Markov property and eliminating the train-test gap that Self-Forcing's single-step collapse leaves. Stage 2 formulates AR video generation as an MDP and applies GRPO with a composite reward (LAION aesthetic score for visual quality + Co-Tracker-based trajectory alignment for motion control). To make the extremely long AR decision chain tractable for RL, selective stochasticity is introduced: a single randomly chosen denoising step per frame uses an SDE update while all remaining steps follow the deterministic ODE solver, reducing the effective horizon by 5–20× while retaining sufficient exploration for stable policy optimization.

## Results

AR-Drag achieves the best performance across all six metrics in the main comparison: FID 28.98, FVD 187.49, Aesthetic Quality 4.07, Motion Smoothness 0.9948, and Motion Consistency 4.37, while also delivering the lowest first-frame latency of 0.44 s on a single NVIDIA H20 GPU. This latency is less than 1% of bidirectional methods (e.g., Tora at 176.51 s, MagicMotion at 1426.37 s) and roughly half that of the closest AR baseline Self-Forcing (0.95 s). The model uses only 1.3B parameters and a 3-step denoising schedule, yet surpasses the 5B MagicMotion on every quality and controllability metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AR-Drag outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the bidirectional motion-controllable methods, the latency advantage is dramatic: AR-Drag's 0.44 s is ~200× faster than Tora (176.51 s), ~150× faster than DragNUWA (94.26 s), and ~3,200× faster than MagicMotion (1426.37 s), while simultaneously achieving lower FID (28.98 vs. 32.84 for Tora, 30.04 for MagicMotion) and higher Motion Consistency (4.37 vs. 3.97 for Tora, 3.95 for MagicMotion). Against the closest AR baseline, Self-Forcing (fine-tuned for I2V motion control), AR-Drag reduces latency by more than half (0.44 s vs. 0.95 s) and improves FID by 5.49 points (28.98 vs. 34.47), FVD by 128.38 points (187.49 vs. 315.87), and Motion Consistency by 0.31 (4.37 vs. 4.06). Notably, AR-Drag also surpasses the larger 5B MagicMotion model on all quality and controllability metrics despite using only 1.3B parameters, which the authors attribute to the RL post-training enabling finer-grained motion alignment that MagicMotion (without RL) cannot achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three active research threads: (1) bidirectional motion-controllable video diffusion (DragNUWA, DragAnything, Tora, MagicMotion), which achieves strong control but requires joint denoising of all frames and thus cannot support real-time interaction; (2) few-step autoregressive video diffusion (Self-Forcing, CausVid), which enables sequential generation but previously lacked motion control and suffered from train-test mismatch; and (3) RL post-training for diffusion models (GRPO, FlowGRPO, DanceGRPO), which has been applied to text-to-image but not to autoregressive video. The key conceptual departure is the Self-Rollout mechanism, which corrects Self-Forcing's single-step KV-cache collapse into a full step-by-step rollout, restoring the Markov property needed for valid GRPO training, combined with selective stochasticity (one SDE step per frame) to keep the extremely long AR decision horizon tractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving 0.44 s first-frame latency (vs. 176–1426 s for bidirectional baselines) while matching or exceeding their visual quality at only 1.3 B parameters, this work makes interactive, user-adjustable video generation practically feasible on a single GPU. The Self-Rollout + selective-stochasticity framework is a general recipe for applying policy-gradient RL to any long-horizon autoregressive generative model, not just video. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models and longer videos without further variance control, how to handle physically implausible or cartoon-style motion (the reward model is grounded in physical plausibility), and whether parallel sampling can reduce the remaining per-frame latency.
