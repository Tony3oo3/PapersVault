## Problem

Bidirectional language models (BERT, RoBERTa, DeBERTa) consistently outperform unidirectional models (GPT, MobileLLM) on natural language understanding benchmarks, yet the theoretical reason for this advantage remains unclear. Existing information bottleneck analyses of LMs are largely descriptive or limited to single-pair MI estimation, lacking a joint, layer-wise framework that can compare how much information each layer carries about both the input and the target simultaneously.

## Input / Output

FlowNIB is a post-hoc framework: the LM is frozen after fine-tuning, and a single forward pass caches triplets (X, Y, Z_ℓ) for every layer. The method then trains two MINE-style critics on this fixed cache, producing per-epoch information-plane coordinates (I(X;Z_ℓ), I(Z_ℓ;Y)) and a final Optimal Information Coordinate (OIC) per layer. Because the two MI estimates come from a shared critic under a common optimization schedule, the reported values are relative scores for cross-layer and cross-model comparison rather than absolute calibrated MI, and are optionally normalized by per-layer effective dimensionality to control for scale differences.

## Method

FlowNIB extends MINE by jointly approximating I(X;Z_ℓ) and I(Z_ℓ;Y) within a single optimization process governed by a monotonically decaying schedule α(t) that shifts emphasis from input-preservation to target-prediction over T training steps. This shared-critic design places both MI quantities on one continuous geometric trajectory, resolving the incomparability problem of independently trained MINE critics. From the trajectory, the Optimal Information Coordinate (OIC) is selected as the point that jointly maximizes a weighted sum of the two MI values, with a scale-balanced weight derived from the range of each coordinate. The theoretical backbone is Theorem 2.1, which proves via monotonicity of conditional entropy that bidirectional representations (conditioning on both past and future tokens) retain at least as much mutual information about the input and target as unidirectional ones, with strict inequality when future context reduces input uncertainty or contributes predictive signal. A complementary generalized effective dimensionality measure (ℓ₂ participation ratio of the layer covariance spectrum) characterizes the spectral richness of each representation, and Lemma 2.3 shows bidirectional layers exhibit higher effective dimensionality throughout depth.

## Results

Across 15 NLU datasets (GLUE, commonsense reasoning, and regression), bidirectional models with single-token masking consistently outperform unidirectional models with next-token generation, even at substantially smaller parameter counts. DeBERTa-v3-Large (435M) achieves 84.73% average classification accuracy and 0.184/0.282 MAE/MSE on regression, surpassing the much larger MobileLLM-600M (600M) at 76.55% and 0.193/0.302. Even the smallest bidirectional model, RoBERTa-Base (125M), reaches 79.95% accuracy—exceeding MobileLLM-600M by over 3 points. FlowNIB mutual information estimates confirm that bidirectional layers carry systematically higher I(X;Z) and I(Z;Y) than unidirectional counterparts (e.g., DeBERTa-v3-Large I(Z;Y)=1.472 vs. MobileLLM-600M I(Z;Y)=0.737 on SST-2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is architectural (bidirectional vs. unidirectional) rather than methodological, with FlowNIB serving as the analytical lens. Among bidirectional models, DeBERTa-v3-Large (435M) is the strongest, reaching 84.73% accuracy and 0.184 MAE, outperforming RoBERTa-Large (355M, 83.95%) and ModernBERT-Large (395M, 83.84%) by roughly 0.8–0.9 points. Among unidirectional models, MobileLLM-600M (600M) is the best at 76.55% accuracy, yet it is still surpassed by every bidirectional model tested, including the 125M RoBERTa-Base (79.95%) and 149M ModernBERT-Base (79.73%). GPT-2 Large (762M) is the weakest unidirectional model at 72.07%, trailing even the 135M SmolLM2 by 0.75 points. In the MI analysis (Tables 3–4), bidirectional models dominate: DeBERTa-v3-Large achieves I(X;Z)=4.372 and I(Z;Y)=1.472 on SST-2, versus MobileLLM-600M's 3.937 and 0.737, confirming that the accuracy gap tracks the information gap. Linear probing further validates this: bidirectional models show higher probe accuracy at nearly every layer depth, independent of the FlowNIB estimator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Information Bottleneck (IB) principle (Tishby et al., 2000) and MINE-based mutual information estimation (Belghazi et al., 2018) to a joint, layer-wise analysis of bidirectional versus unidirectional language models. Recent IB applications to LMs (Wang et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025) are largely descriptive—explaining model behavior, attribution, or pruning—whereas this work introduces FlowNIB, a post-hoc framework that simultaneously estimates I(X;Z_ℓ) and I(Z_ℓ;Y) under a shared optimization schedule, making the two quantities directly comparable across layers and architectures. The empirical protocol builds on the authors' own PredGen (Kowsher et al., 2025b) and RoCoFT (Kowsher et al., 2025a) frameworks for single-token prediction and parameter-efficient fine-tuning, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean information-theoretic justification for the long-observed NLU advantage of bidirectional encoders, and FlowNIB offers a reusable diagnostic tool for quantifying layer-wise information capacity in any transformer. However, the core theoretical result (more context → lower conditional entropy → higher MI) is close to tautological, and the MI estimates are neural lower bounds used only for relative comparison, limiting their interpretability as absolute quantities. Open questions include whether the MI advantage persists for generation tasks, whether FlowNIB can be used prospectively to guide architecture or pruning decisions, and whether the findings generalize to the increasingly dominant decoder-only paradigm at scale.
