## Problem

Policy-based GFlowNet training operates under an actor-critic framework where a critic (evaluation function V) must reliably estimate the KL divergence between forward and backward subtrajectory distributions at each state. The previously used λ-TD objective for learning V only considers edge-wise mismatches from events starting at a given step, requires the backward policy π_B to remain fixed, and does not support off-policy data collection. This paper identifies that the flow balance conditions underlying value-based training also yield a principled policy evaluator, but no balance-based objective had been derived for learning V.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a graded DAG with states partitioned into H+2 layers, a non-negative score function R(x) over terminating states, and trajectories sampled either on-policy (from π_F) or off-policy (from a data-collection policy π_D). The Sub-EB objective evaluates all subtrajectories τ_{i:j} of varying lengths within each sampled trajectory, with a weight w_{i,j} = λ^{j-i}/Σλ^{j-i} controlling the bias-variance trade-off across subtrajectory lengths. The output is a trained forward policy π_F that generates samples x ∝ R(x), along with a learned evaluation function V and (optionally) a parameterized backward policy π_B.

## Method

The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) shows that for any fixed forward policy π_F, the evaluation function V(s) = log F*(s) − D_KL(P_F(τ_{s:}‖P_B(τ_{s:})) satisfies a Subtrajectory Evaluation Balance (Sub-EB) condition: the expected log-ratio of forward subtrajectory flow times exp(V) equals that of the backward counterpart, for all subtrajectory pairs (i,j). This yields the Sub-EB objective L_V(φ) = E_{P_F(τ)}[Σ_{τ_{i:j}} w_{i,j} δ_V(τ_{i:j};φ)²], which learns V using subtrajectory-wise mismatches from both before and after each state, in contrast to λ-TD's edge-wise, forward-only formulation. The training follows an actor-critic loop: sample trajectories, update V via the Sub-EB gradient (with θ frozen), then update π_F via a policy-gradient step using advantage estimates A^γ derived from V. Two key flexibility gains follow: (1) π_B can be parameterized and jointly updated with V under the same objective, eliminating the need for a separate backward phase; (2) an offline variant (Algorithm 2) uses a backward evaluation function W learned via a symmetric backward Sub-EB objective, enabling π_D ≠ π_F and integration of exploration techniques like local search.

## Results

On the 256×256 hypergrid, Sub-EB significantly improves training stability and convergence rate over the λ-TD policy-gradient method (RL), with both outperforming Sub-TB and CV in final D_TV. On the 128×128 and E4×E4×E4 grids, Sub-EB and RL achieve similar final performance but both converge faster than Sub-TB and CV. On 10-node Bayesian network structure learning, Sub-EB achieves the highest average reward among RL, Sub-TB, and Q-Much, with the best Flow Consistency in Sub-graphs (FCS) metric, while all four methods attain comparable diversity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Sub-EB against four baselines: CV (empperical control-variate estimator), RL (λ-TD policy-gradient), Sub-TB (value-based subtrajectory balance), and Q-Much (RL-inspired value-based). Sub-EB outperforms RL primarily in training stability on the 256×256 hypergrid, where RL exhibits larger variance across runs, while both converge to similar final D_TV. Sub-EB and RL both outperform Sub-TB and CV in convergence speed on all three hypergrid sizes, with Sub-TB showing slower convergence due to its off-policy data-collection design. On BN structure learning, Sub-EB achieves the highest average reward and best ECS (distribution-modeling quality) compared to RL, Sub-TB, and Q-Much, though all four methods reach similar diversity. The local-search variant Sub-EB-B achieves the highest average reward among all six methods tested, at the expectedpected cost of reduced diversity, confirming the optimality-diversity trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate training paradigms for GFlowNets: value-based methods (which optimize flow balance conditions like Sub-TB) and policy-based methods (which use an actor-critic framework with a learned evaluation function V). The key theoretical insight is that, for a fixed forward policy, the state flow function F(s) satisfying the Sub-TB balance condition coincides with the true KL-divergence-based evaluation function V(s), thereby providing a principled balance-based objective (Sub-EB) for learning the critic. This directly extends Niu et al. (2024), which introduced policy-gradient GFlowNet training but left the reliable learning of V as an open problem solved only by the λ-TD objective, and mirrors the subtrajectory-wise structure of Madan et al. (2023)'s Sub-TB objective in the policy-based setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Sub-EB objective addresses a practical bottleneck in policy-based GFlowNet training: the λ-TD critic can be unstable, particularly on larger or higher-dimensional tasks, and it cannot accommodate parameterized backward policies or offline data collection without additional algorithmic machinery. By enabling these flexibilities within a single objective, the paper broadens the applicability of policy-based methods to settings (e.g., offline RL-style training, adaptive backward policies) that were previously restricted to value-based approaches. Open questions include whether the λ-decay weighting is optimal, how Sub-EB integrates with more advanced policy-based methods like TRPO, and whether the theoretical connection between F and V extends to non-Markovian or continuous-state settings.
