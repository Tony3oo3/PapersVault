## Problem

Vision-language representation models (VLRMs) like CLIP degrade significantly when query styles diverge from pretraining distributions (e.g., sketches, artworks, low-resolution previews), and existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods (LoRA, VPT) are static—applying a single shared parameter set to all inputs—so they cannot adapt to diverse or unseen query styles at inference. The paper asks how to achieve per-query, style-conditioned weight adaptation that is both flexible enough for heterogeneous inputs and stable enough for reliable optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a query image in any style (sketch, art, low-resolution) or a text query, along with a gallery image database, and produces ranked retrieval results (Top-1/Top-5) with style-adaptive feature embeddings. The query is first passed through a frozen DINOv2 encoder to extract a style latent vector z from the [CLS] token, which conditions the hypernetwork; the adapted VLRM backbone then encodes the query for similarity matching. During training, anchors are randomly sampled from a single style with their ground-truth positives, exposing the model to cross-style variation without explicit style labels.

## Method

Hystar performs a dynamic-static hybrid PEFT in the spectral domain: the pretrained weight matrix W₀ is SVD-decomposed as UΣVᵀ, and only diagonal singular-value increments ΔΣ are learned (W = U(Σ+ΔΣ)Vᵀ), bounding the spectral norm of updates for training stability. The increment Δs is split into a dynamic component generated by a two-layer hypernetwork conditioned on the DINOv2 style embedding z (applied to attention layers 4, 7, 10, 13) and a static component (learnable offsets on MLP layers) that provides globally shared cross-style calibration. For training, the paper introduces StyleNCE, an optimal-transport-weighted contrastive loss that uses Sinkhorn iterations to compute a transport plan T over the batch, setting negative weights ωᵢⱼ = Tᵢⱼ to amplify hard cross-style negatives while maintaining balanced contributions. This design—dynamic attention for style-specific flexibility, static MLPs for semantic stability, and OT-weighted hard-negative mining—achieves SOTA multi-style retrieval with only ~3.5% additional parameters over the base CLIP model.

## Results

On the DSR fine-grained multi-style retrieval benchmark, Hystar-BLIP achieves Top-1 accuracies of 75.6% (Art), 91.0% (Sketch), 98.8% (Low-Res), and 82.0% (Text), outperforming the strongest baseline FreestyleRet-BLIP by up to 10.5 points on Sketch and 8.3 points on Low-Res. On the DomainNet zero-shot retrieval benchmark, Hystar leads all baselines across five unseen styles, reaching 75.7% Top-1 on Clipart and 19.0% on Quickdraw (vs. 14.5% for VPT). In zero-shot classification on DomainNet, Hystar attains the highest accuracy in all six styles, with particularly large gains on Sketch (71.2% vs. 67.3% for FreestyleRet) and Quickdraw (22.9% vs. 15.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hystar consistently outperforms all 16 baselines across the DSR, DomainNet retrieval, and DomainNet classification benchmarks. Against vanilla CLIP/BLIP, Hystar-BLIP gains 23.5 points on Sketch (91.0% vs. 67.5% for CLIP) and 53.8 points on Low-Res (98.8% vs. 45.0%), demonstrating that static VLRM representations fail severely under style shift. Among PEFT baselines (LoRA, VPT, (IA)³, AdaptFormer, SSF), Hystar-BLIP leads by 17–19 points on Sketch and 17–19 points on Low-Res, confirming that static parameter sets cannot adapt to per-query style. The closest competitor, FreestyleRet-BLIP, is surpassed by Hystar-BLIP by 9.8 points on Sketch (91.0% vs. 81.2%) and 8.3 points on Low-Res (98.8% vs. 90.5%), while on Text queries Hystar-BLIP edges ahead by 0.4 points (82.0% vs. 81.6%). On DomainNet zero-shot retrieval, Hystar beats the best baseline (VPT) by 4.0 points on Clipart and 4.5 points on Quickdraw, and on DomainNet classification it leads FreestyleRet by 3.9 points on Sketch and 7.0 points on Quickdraw. On the 16-shot ImageNet/SUN397 classification protocol, Hystar achieves the best New (74.70) and H (77.03) averages, narrowly surpassing MaPLe (+0.08 on New) and TCP (+0.15 on H), while accepting a 0.44-point drop on Base compared to TCP.

## Relation to Prior Work

Hystar sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) hypernetworks (Ha et al., 2016) for instance-conditioned parameter generation, (2) SVD-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning (SVFT, Lingam et al., 2024) that constrains updates to the pretrained spectral subspace, and (3) optimal-transport-weighted contrastive objectives (Cuturi, 2013; Peyré et al., 2019) for hard-negative mining. The most direct prior work is FreestyleRet (Li et al., 2024a), which uses style-cluster priors from training data to guide prompt learning; Hystar departs from this by replacing discrete style-cluster conditioning with a continuous, per-input hypernetwork that can adapt to unseen styles without requiring a pre-annotated style library. The dynamic-static hybrid design (dynamic attention, static MLP) is a novel architectural choice not present in prior PEFT or hypernetwork literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and increasingly important gap: VLRM-based retrieval degrades sharply under style shift (sketch, art, low-res), and existing PEFT methods are static while style-cluster methods cannot generalize to unseen styles. Hystar's 3.5% parameter overhead with consistent 10–25% Top-1 gains over strong baselines makes it a deployable option for multi-style search engines. However, the 58.8% inference latency increase and the still-poor performance on extremely abstract styles (25.3% on Surrealist Abstract Art) highlight that the approach has not fully solved the out-of-distribution problem. Open questions include whether the hypernetwork-SVD paradigm transfers to other modalities (video, audio) or to generative retrieval, and whether the OT-weighted loss can be made more scalable for very large batches.
