## Problem

Multimodal learning methods routinely assume that modalities are fully observed in both training and deployment, yet real-world missingness patterns (sensor failures, cost-driven collection, diagnostic workflows) differ between source and target environments. This missingness-induced distribution shift biases estimates of a modality's predictive and information-theoretic value, potentially leading to flawed decisions about which modalities to collect at deployment. The paper formalizes this gap and shows that naively evaluating on complete samples conflates missingness with signal.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two modalities X1, X2, a target label Y, binary missingness indicators, and covariates C that explain the missingness mechanism (MAR). It produces two types of output: (i) IPW-corrected discriminative performance (AUROC) for unimodal and multimodal models, and (ii) a four-way PID decomposition (shared, unique-1, unique-2, complementary) estimated under the full distribution Ω rather than the observed subset Ω_obs. A key structural requirement is that all modalities must be paired per instance (e.g., a patient's ECG and CXR), and the method assumes a notion of instance where all modalities are jointly observable.

## Method

ICYM²I applies a double inverse probability weighting (IPW) correction under the MAR and positivity assumptions: both the training loss and the evaluation metric are reweighted by 1/(1−p(m|C)) to shift from the observed distribution Ω_obs to the underlying distribution Ω. For predictive performance (ICYM²I-learn), this means training with a weighted loss and evaluating with IPW-corrected metrics (e.g., corrected AUC, Brier score). For information decomposition (ICYM²I-PID), the method corrects the three-way mutual information estimator and modifies the Bertschinger projection set Δ_Ω so that the Sinkhorn-Knopp marginal-matching step enforces IPW-corrected unimodal distributions rather than raw observed marginals. The PID optimization then minimizes the corrected three-way mutual information over q parameterized as a product of two unimodal networks, with the unrolled Sinkhorn-Knopp ensuring gradient propagation. The critical insight is that correcting only training or only evaluation is insufficient; both must be adjusted to recover unbiased estimates of modality informativeness.

## Results

On the bitwise logic operators (AND, OR, XOR) with 50% MAR missingness in X₂, the naive 'Observed' approach severely distorts informativeness estimates: for XOR it inflates Unique 1 from 0.00 (Oracle) to 0.34, while ICYM²I recovers 0.00; for AND it deflates X₁'s AUROC from 0.83 (Oracle) to 0.66, while ICYM²I recovers 0.83. On the real-world ECG/CXR structural heart disease cohort (n=98,397), naive decomposition attributes ~5% unique information to CXR, whereas ICYM²I reduces this to 1.8% and increases shared information from 0.10 to 0.48, fundamentally altering the conclusion about CXR's independent diagnostic value. On semi-synthetic UR-FUNNY and Hateful Memes with 70% enforced missingness, ICYM²I consistently brings AUROC and PID estimates closer to Oracle than the uncorrected Observed baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against competing multimodal methods; instead, it benchmarks three estimation strategies: Oracle (training and evaluating on the full distribution Ω), Observed (the current practice of using only complete samples from Ω_obs), and ICYM²I (the proposed IPW-corrected training and evaluation). ICYM²I consistently recovers Oracle-level estimates across all settings. In the bitwise logic example, Observed produces dramatic biases (e.g., XOR Unique 1: 0.34 vs. Oracle 0.00; AND X₁ AUROC: 0.66 vs. Oracle 0.83), while ICYM²I matches Oracle within noise (XOR Unique 1: 0.00; AND X₁ AUROC: 0.83). On the real-world ECG/CXR cohort, Observed and ICYM²I yield similar AUROC values (ECG: 0.83 vs. 0.82; CXR: 0.72 vs. 0.73), but the PID decomposition diverges sharply: Observed attributes 0.11 unique information to ECG and 0.10 shared, while ICYM²I attributes 0.07 unique to ECG and 0.48 shared, suggesting CXR adds little independent signal. Under MCAR (Table 7), Observed and ICYM²I both closely match Oracle, confirming no correction is needed when missingness is truly random. Under MNAR (Table 8), both Observed and ICYM²I remain biased relative to Oracle, as expected since no correction can recover the true distribution without observing the unobserved missingness covariates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: Partial Information Decomposition (PID) for quantifying modality informativeness (Williams & Beer, 2010; Bertschinger et al., 2014; Liang et al., 2024a) and inverse probability weighting (IPW) for correcting selection bias (Robins et al., 1994). Prior multimodal learning methods handle missingness through imputation, architecture design, or ensembling, but implicitly assume a stable missingness process between source and target environments. The paper's key departure is formalizing missingness as a distribution shift problem and applying a double IPW correction (to both training and evaluation) to recover unbiased estimates of predictive performance and PID components, rather than attempting to improve robustness to missing modalities at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the standard practice of discarding incomplete samples or assuming stable missingness systematically biases estimates of modality informativeness, with practical consequences for data collection decisions. The healthcare case study is particularly compelling: naive analysis overestimates CXR's unique contribution to structural heart disease detection (~5% vs. 1.8% after correction), potentially justifying costly and unnecessary imaging. Key limitations include the MAR assumption (no guarantees under MNAR), restriction to two modalities, and the requirement for paired data. Open questions include extending PID to more than two modalities, handling unpaired or unaligned modalities, and validating the approach in longitudinal or streaming settings where missingness patterns evolve over time.
