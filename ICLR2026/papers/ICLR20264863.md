## Problem

Although SVD-based low-rank decomposition has been widely proposed to compress VLMs, the authors show through system-level profiling that conventional full-matrix SVD on QKV projections does not reduce decoding latency and can even increase it, because reconstructing K and V from a shared low-rank latent requires repeated memory accesses to the entire latent matrix. Additionally, standard SVD truncation treats all weight elements equally, ignoring the well-documented fact that certain 'superweights' are far more sensitive to perturbation than others.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard VLMs (LLaVA, SmolVLM, Qwen-VL, Molmo) that take an RGB image and a text prompt and produce a generated text response. During inference, the KV cache stores per-head low-rank latents C_Kh ∈ R^{L×r} and C_Vh ∈ R^{L×r} (where r ≪ H, the head dimension) instead of full K and V vectors, and a fused Triton kernel reconstructs K_h and V_h on-chip at tile granularity without ever materializing them in VRAM. The compressed model supports W8A8 or W8A4 weight-activation quantization with per-token symmetric activation quantization and per-channel symmetric weight quantization with learnable clipping.

## Method

WSVD applies SVD at per-head granularity: each head's projection submatrix W_Kh ∈ R^{E×H} is decomposed into A_Kh B_Kh with rank r obtained by truncating the H singular values, yielding a much smaller rank than full-matrix SVD (r/R ratio reduction in both memory and compute). To counteract the amplified approximation error from per-head decomposition, the authors compute element-wise Fisher information scores over a 256-sample calibration set and perform local fine-tuning of A_h and B_h under a Fisher-weighted Frobenius loss, preserving the most accuracy-critical weights. For low-precision inference, a fixed Hadamard matrix S1 and a learnable orthogonal matrix S2 are applied to rotate weights and activations before quantization, and S2, A_h, B_h are jointly fine-tuned under a Fisher-weighted quantization-aware training objective. A custom fused Triton kernel integrates the low-rank up-projection directly into the flash decoding pipeline at tile granularity, streaming C_Kh tiles from VRAM, forming K_h tiles in on-chip registers, and immediately contracting with the query—eliminating redundant kernel launches and VRAM round-trips that plague naive implementations.

## Results

Under W8A8 quantization with ρ₁=50%, WSVD achieves 67.10% average accuracy across four LLaVA models on ScienceQA-IMG and SEED-Bench, only 1.1% below the FP16 baseline (68.23%), while reducing cache size to 25% of FP16. In system-level evaluation on RTX 4090, WSVD-noQ achieves 1.8× decoding speedup over Flash Decoding at 8K sequence length (1.66 ms vs. 2.92 ms per layer), scaling to 2.3× on RTX 5090 and 2.6× on RTX 3060 at 16K tokens. Under FP16 (WSVD-noQ), the method matches or exceeds FP16 accuracy at ρ₁=70% on LLaVA-Next 13B (73.57% vs. 73.23% on ScienceQA-IMG).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WSVD outperforms all SVD-based baselines (ASVD, SVD-LLM, QSVD-noQ, DobiSVD) in FP16 accuracy at every parameter ratio, with the gap widening as compression increases (e.g., on SmolVLM at ρ₁=70%, WSVD-noQ reaches 60.93% on ScienceQA while ASVD drops to 0.20%). In the low-precision setting (Table 2), WSVD beats QSVD by 1.03% average, DuQuant by 3.79%, QVLM by 8.01%, and QASVD by 12.32%, while staying within 1.13% of FP16. For system-level latency, WSVD-noQ achieves 1.8× speedup over Flash Decoding on RTX 4090 and up to 2.3× on RTX 5090 at 8K context, and surpasses Palu (group-head SVD) by combining finer per-head granularity with a fully fused Triton kernel. The calibration overhead is also lower: WSVD requires 38 minutes vs. 96 minutes for QSVD on LLaVA-1.5 13B (~2.5× less tuning time).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SVD-based compression line (ASVD, SVD-LLM, QSVD, DobiSVD) by shifting from full-matrix QKV decomposition to per-head submatrix decomposition, a finer granularity that the authors show is critical for actual latency reduction. It incorporates Fisher-information-weighted local fine-tuning (building on FWSVD and WoodFisher) to preserve accuracy under aggressive rank truncation, and integrates QuaRot-style Hadamard rotation with local quantization-aware training for low-precision inference. On the systems side, WSVD departs from prior SVD implementations by fusing low-rank reconstruction directly into the Flash Decoding kernel via Triton, eliminating the materialization of full K/V tensors that prior approaches (including Palu) still require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is demonstrating that conventional full-matrix SVD can actually *increase* decoding latency due to shared-latent reconstruction overhead, and that per-head decomposition with a fused kernel resolves this, yielding 1.8–2.3× speedups across GPUs and sequence lengths while maintaining accuracy within ~1% of FP16. This makes low-rank VLM compression practically deployable rather than merely theoretically attractive. Open questions include whether the per-head granularity advantage persists for architectures with cross-attention or multi-modal fusion beyond simple concatenation, and whether the occasional accuracy *gains* over FP16 (observed at ρ₁=70% on LLaVA-Next 13B) reflect a robust regularization effect or dataset-specific noise.
