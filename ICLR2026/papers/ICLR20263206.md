## Problem

Existing IB-based multi-modal clustering methods suffer from a vicious cycle where noisy pseudo-labels degrade feature learning, which in turn produces worse pseudo-labels, leading to progressively overconfident and incorrect clustering. Additionally, these methods over-rely on accurate mutual information estimation, which is inherently biased for high-dimensional data, yet no prior work addresses how to calibrate IB against such estimation bias while simultaneously producing trustworthy (well-calibrated) clustering outputs.

## Input / Output

The method takes N samples each composed of M (2 or 3) modalities—typically handcrafted image descriptors (wavelet moments, CENTRIST, LBP) and/or textual descriptions—and a predefined number of clusters C. It outputs a cluster assignment from the cluster head operating on fused features, along with per-modality calibrated probability distributions from the calibration heads. The pseudo-label screening dynamically adjusts how many samples contribute to training: early in training only the most confident samples are selected, and this set grows as the model becomes more reliable.

## Method

CLIB employs a parallel multi-head architecture: one cluster head processes an adaptively weighted-average fused representation, while M modality-specific calibration heads each process a single modality's IB-compressed feature. Feature extraction uses three IB objectives—compression (min I(X;H) via variational KL), inter-modal alignment (max I(H^i,H^j) via NT-Xent), and fusion preservation (max I(H^m;H^f) via MINE)—trained in a two-stage protocol (100-epoch IB warmup, then 100-epoch calibration). A key mechanism is an entropy-based pseudo-label screening score S(P)=1−H(P)/(2·H_max) that dynamically selects high-quality pseudo-labels from calibration heads to supervise the cluster head; gradient backpropagation is stopped from calibration heads to the bottleneck to prevent conflicting objectives, while the cluster head's gradients flow back to refine IB features. Theoretically (Theorem 1), modalities with larger MI estimation bias produce flatter calibration distributions, receive lower quality scores, contribute fewer selected samples, and thus exert smaller gradient influence—allowing the model to autonomously down-weight unreliable modalities without explicit bias correction.

## Results

CLIB achieves state-of-the-art performance on all three metrics (ACC, NMI, ECE) across all five benchmark datasets. Headline results include 77.8% ACC and 69.3% NMI on Caltech-3V (beating the runner-up DIVIDE by 6.2% ACC), 56.3% ACC on ESP-Game, and 51.6% ACC on IAPR. On the trustworthiness metric ECE, CLIB shows particularly large margins: 7.8% on IAPR (vs. 22.4% for MVCAN, a ~3× reduction), 12.1% on ESP-Game (vs. 21.7% for MFLVC), and 10.5% on MIRFlickr (vs. 19.5% for MFLVC). The method also demonstrates robust parameter sensitivity, with ACC variance within 20% across the full (α, β) grid search.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLIB outperforms all 10 deep multi-modal clustering baselines on every metric across all five datasets. On clustering accuracy, the largest margins are on Caltech-3V where CLIB (77.8% ACC) beats DIVIDE (71.6%) by 6.2% and MVCAN (70.1%) by 7.7%, and on ESP-Game where it exceeds MFLVC (52.1%) by 4.2%. On NMI, CLIB leads by 6.7% over MVCAN on Caltech-3V (69.3 vs. 62.6) and by 7.1% over MFLVC on ESP-Game (39.9 vs. 32.8). The most striking advantage is on ECE: CLIB reduces calibration error by roughly 45–65% relative to the best prior method on each dataset (e.g., 7.8% vs. 22.4% on IAPR, 12.1% vs. 21.7% on ESP-Game, 10.5% vs. 19.5% on MIRFlickr). Among the compared methods, DIVIDE and MVCAN are the strongest ACC/NMI competitors but do not report ECE (DIVIDE) or show substantially higher ECE (MVCAN), while COPER (ICLR'25) and ROLL (CVPR'25) trail CLIB by 5–15% in ACC and 10–25% in ECE across datasets. Classical methods (KM, Ncuts) and older deep methods (EAMC, SiMVC, CoMVC) are consistently outperformed by 15–30% in ACC.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLIB extends the Information Bottleneck framework (Tishby et al., 2000; Alemi et al., 2017) to multi-modal clustering by combining variational inference for compression, NT-Xent (van den Oord et al., 2018) for inter-modal alignment, and MINE (Belghazi et al., 2018) for fusion preservation. It departs from prior IB-based multi-modal clustering methods (MSDIB, SWIB, PTIB, SDCIB) by explicitly acknowledging that MI estimators are biased in high-dimensional settings and introducing a parallel multi-head calibration architecture to correct for this bias, rather than assuming estimator accuracy. The pseudo-label screening mechanism based on information redundancy (dividing entropy by 2×H_max) also distinguishes it from max-probability-based selection used in FixMatch, TCL, and Jia et al. (2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to explicitly incorporate Expected Calibration Error into the optimization objective of an unsupervised clustering method, addressing the overconfidence problem that is particularly dangerous in safety-critical applications. The theoretical proof (Theorem 1) connecting MI estimation bias to reduced sample selection provides a principled explanation for why cross-modal calibration can correct for estimator errors, offering a new design principle for IB-based methods. However, the method is limited to complete single-label multi-modal data with a predefined number of clusters, and all experiments use small benchmark datasets (≤12K samples) with MLP backbones, leaving open whether the calibration mechanism scales to larger, more complex multi-modal settings or handles missing modalities.
