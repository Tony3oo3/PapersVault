## Problem

Existing neural MD models enforce strict equivariance at every layer (limiting expressivity and increasing overhead), rely on sequential autoregressive rollouts (preventing parallel inference and accumulating error over long horizons), and are trained single-task on individual molecules at fixed timesteps (preventing zero-shot transfer to unseen compounds or timeframes). No prior method simultaneously addresses all three limitations while achieving state-of-the-art accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262914/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ATOM consumes a molecule as an unstructured point cloud of 3D atomic positions and velocities (no predefined bond graph or edge labels), augmented with per-atom norms and atomic numbers, plus a scalar time lag Δt. It outputs future atomic positions at P discrete timesteps in parallel (not sequentially), and because T-RoPE encodes time as a continuous rotary phase, the same pretrained model can be queried at arbitrary ΔT values at inference without retraining. During multitask pretraining, Δt is sampled from a log-uniform distribution over [8 fs, 24000 fs] to cover multiple time horizons simultaneously.

## Method

ATOM is a transformer neural operator with a quasi-equivariant design: an E(3)-equivariant lifting layer maps raw positions, velocities, and phase features into a symmetry-aware embedding, after which standard (non-equivariant) transformer blocks operate freely, trading strict symmetry for expressivity. The core attention mechanism is heterogeneous: the phase-space embedding Z serves as the query while all three feature streams (X, V, Z) form key-value pairs, with learnable weights modulating their relative contributions. Temporal Rotary Position Embedding (T-RoPE) adapts RoPE to irregular time lags by scaling rotation angles by cumulative timestamps, making attention translation-invariant in time and enabling stable interpolation/extrapolation across arbitrary ΔT. The model requires no molecular graph, instead using fully connected point-cloud attention that naturally captures long-range non-bonded interactions. For multitask pretraining on TG80 (80 compounds, >2.5 M fs of DFT trajectories), random-walk positional encoding on a radius graph provides molecule-identifying features, and label-noise regularization (Gaussian perturbations on positions and velocities) improves robustness to DFT numerical noise.

## Results

On MD17, ATOM achieves average S2S MSE reductions of 14.96% and S2T MSE reductions of 8.3% over the best SOTA baselines, with particularly large gains on Toluene (4.66 vs 11.00 S2S vs EGNO) and Aspirin (6.82 vs 9.64). On the larger MD22 molecules, ATOM dramatically outperforms EGNO by 94.06% on DHA (S2S MSE 10.60 vs 178.39) and 97.30% on Ac-Ala3-NHME (9.65 vs 357.89). In the multitask TG80 setting, ATOM achieves a 39.74% average S2T MSE improvement over EGNO on out-of-domain molecules, with OOD ATOM beating ID EGNO in 4 of 5 cluster folds, demonstrating zero-shot cross-molecule generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EGNO (Xu et al., 2024), the closest neural-operator baseline, ATOM wins on 3 of 7 MD17 molecules for S2S (Aspirin, Salicylic, Toluene) but loses on Malonaldehyde (14.72 vs 12.92), Naphthalene (0.50 vs 0.39), and Uracil (0.63 vs 0.58); the average S2S improvement is 14.96%. On MD22, ATOM's fully connected point-cloud attention is decisive: it outperforms EGNO by 94–97% on DHA and Ac-Ala3-NHME, where EGNO's fixed bond graph fails to capture long-range interactions. Against MACE (Batatia et al., 2023), ATOM wins on Aspirin, Toluene, and Naphthalene but loses on Ethanol (3.52 vs 2.06 S2S) and Malonaldehyde (14.72 vs 17.99 — actually ATOM wins here; MACE is worse). In the multitask TG80 OOD setting, ATOM beats EGNO on all five UMAP cluster folds (21.93%–58.83% S2T MSE reduction), and also outperforms EGNN-R and EGNN-S, which show extreme variance (e.g., EGNN-S reaches 738 S2T MSE on Cluster 2). The key differentiator is that ATOM's zero-shot OOD performance (e.g., 60.95 S2T on Cluster 3) surpasses EGNO's in-distribution performance (141.16), confirming transferable dynamics learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

ATOM builds directly on EGNO (Xu et al., 2024), which first cast molecular dynamics prediction as neural operator learning, and adapts Rotary Position Embedding (Su et al., 2023) to irregular time lags via T-RoPE. It departs from the dominant equivariant GNN paradigm (EGNN, MACE, SE(3)-Transformers) in three key ways: enforcing equivariance only in a lifting layer rather than end-to-end (quasi-equivariance), operating on fully connected point clouds rather than predefined molecular graphs, and decoding multiple future timesteps in parallel rather than autoregressively. The multitask pretraining protocol with cluster-based cross-validation extends the single-task, fixed-horizon evaluation convention that has characterized MD benchmarking since MD17.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ATOM is the first method to demonstrate zero-shot generalization to unseen molecules in molecular dynamics, with OOD performance matching or exceeding in-distribution baselines trained specifically on those molecules. The TG80 dataset (80 compounds, 2.5M+ femtoseconds, numerically stable via D4 dispersion corrections) provides a much-needed large-scale multitask benchmark that addresses the poor benchmarking practices the authors identify in the field. Key limitations include the absence of molecules with more than 15 heavy atoms in TG80 and the lack of an energy-based inductive bias, which may permit long-horizon drift; the authors propose a framewise energy head with force supervision as a natural next step. The quasi-equivariance finding—that relaxing strict symmetry in deeper layers improves accuracy—has implications beyond MD for any equivariant architecture design.
