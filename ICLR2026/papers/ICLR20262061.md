## Problem

Scaling multimodal LLMs to full 3D medical volumes is hindered by prohibitive memory and compute costs, while existing workarounds—2D slice sampling, fixed-length token compression, and uniform-ratio pruning—disrupt volumetric continuity, discard clinically subtle findings, and ignore instruction-specific information needs. No prior method simultaneously achieves instruction-aware adaptive token reduction, differentiable training under discrete token dropping, and FlashAttention-compatible inference acceleration in a 3D-native medical setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262061/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a 3D CT/MRI volume of size D×H×W, partitioned into non-overlapping 14×14×14 patches and spatially merged only along the in-plane (H,W) axes with stride S while preserving full depth resolution, yielding N_v = D·(H'/S)·(W'/S) visual tokens; this is concatenated with the instruction token sequence. The output is a free-text or multiple-choice clinical answer, and the internal visual token sequence has a sample-specific variable length (typically ~0.39K tokens vs. 7.0K for the unpruned baseline), with pruned tokens fully removed from KV cache, attention masks, and positional encodings rather than merely zeroed.

## Method

Photon integrates two core modules at a selected decoder layer ℓ = ℓ_{n/4}. Instruction-conditioned Token Scheduling (ITS) first computes instruction self-affinity centrality scores from intra-instruction attention, then uses these as weights in instruction-to-visual cross-attention to produce per-token saliency scores ρ_j; a lightweight Instance-aware Threshold Predictor (ITP) maps distributional statistics (shape, scale, compressed-tail descriptors) of the raw saliency logits to a per-sample threshold θ, and tokens with ρ_j < θ are hard-dropped in the forward pass. Surrogate Gradient Propagation (SGP) makes this discrete selection differentiable: a straight-through mask routes upstream gradients back to retained tokens, while a custom surrogate gradient on the retention probabilities q_j combines a directional term (derived from first-order Taylor importance scores η_j = ⟨activation, gradient⟩) and a magnitude factor (row-normalized |activation·gradient| sums), scaled by max{q(1−q), ε_sat} to prevent vanishing updates. Three regularization objectives—soft retention band, perturbation-based robustness loss, and mask-flip penalty—stabilize training and suppress language-only hallucination bias. Training proceeds in two stages: Phase 1 aligns only the 3D patch embedding layer on volume-report pairs, and Phase 2 unfreezes all modules to finetune with the full objective at lr 1e-5.

## Results

On the 3D-RAD benchmark (finetuned), Photon 3B achieves 83.07% on Existence Detection, 52.86% on Static Temporal Diagnosis, 77.01% on Longitudinal Temporal Diagnosis, and 42.33 BLEU on Anomaly Detection, outperforming the strongest 34B baseline (M3D-P) by up to 9.05 BLEU points on Anomaly Detection and 11.93 BLEU on Image Observation. On DeepTumorVQA, Photon 3B reaches a total average of 0.686 (multi-choice) and 0.619 (free-text), surpassing the best baseline (RadFM 13B at 0.662/0.555) by 2.4% and 6.4% respectively. Photon also achieves 4.12 tokens/s inference speed with only ~0.39K retained tokens per sample, compared to 2.30 tokens/s and 7.0K tokens for the full Qwen2.5-VL backbone, while reducing peak inference memory from 26.0 GiB to 9.2 GiB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Photon 3B outperforms all 3D medical MLLM baselines on every finetuned 3D-RAD task. Against the largest competitors M3D-L 27B and M3D-P 34B, Photon gains 9.05 BLEU on Anomaly Detection (42.33 vs 33.28), 11.93 BLEU on Image Observation (51.59 vs 39.66), and 4.22 BLEU on Medical Measurement (37.74 vs 33.52), while using roughly 10× fewer parameters. On DeepTumorVQA, Photon 3B beats RadFM 13B (the strongest 3D baseline) by 2.4% in multi-choice and 6.4% in free-text overall accuracy, with the largest gap in measurement subtypes (+35.3% MRA on lesion volume). Compared to general-purpose MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL 3B, Lingshu 7B, OmniV 1.5B), Photon's finetuned results exceed their zero-shot scores by 23–77 points on classification tasks. Against token-pruning methods VisionZip and HiPrune, Photon achieves higher accuracy on all three 3D-RAD classification tasks while running 1.8× faster (4.12 vs 2.30 tok/s) and using dynamic token counts (~0.39K) rather than fixed ratios. In the training-free zero-shot setting on MedFrameQA, Photon's plug-in ITS module reduces tokens from 7.0K to 3.28K with negligible accuracy loss (0.460→0.457), confirming the pruning strategy generalizes without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

Photon extends the 3D medical MLLM line (RadFM, M3D, OmniV-Med) by replacing their fixed-length visual token compression with instruction-conditioned variable-length scheduling, and adapts the straight-through estimator from filter/neuron pruning (Molchanov et al., 2016) to the token-level setting via a custom surrogate gradient that routes loss-driven importance back to retention probabilities. It departs from general-domain token pruning methods (VisionZip, LLaVA-PruMerge, ATP-LLaVA) by making the pruning threshold instance-specific and instruction-aware rather than a global ratio, and by performing discrete token removal during training (not just inference) so that the model learns to prune. The backbone is Qwen2.5-VL, with only the 3D patch embedding layer re-aligned in Phase 1, preserving the pretrained ViT and LLM priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: reducing visual tokens from ~7K to ~0.4K (≈94% reduction) while simultaneously improving accuracy over both 2D-slice and fixed-length 3D baselines suggests that redundant tokens were actively harming attention in medical VQA, not merely wasting compute. The SGP mechanism is a reusable recipe for making any discrete token-selection operation differentiable during training, which could transfer to other efficient-MLLM settings beyond medical imaging. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very high-resolution or long-sequence volumes (e.g., whole-body CT), whether the instruction-conditioned threshold predictor generalizes to open-ended report generation rather than templated VQA, and whether the gains persist when competing against future 3D-native MLLMs that natively support variable-length inputs.
