## Problem

Existing vision-language navigation agents predict actions regardless of perceptual confidence, ignoring uncertainty that arises from visually similar structures, occlusions, and ambiguous spatial cues. This leads to unreliable grounding (e.g., confusing similar doors) and unsafe path choices when traversability is unclear. The paper addresses this gap by explicitly modeling three forms of perceptual uncertainty—geometric, semantic, and appearance—and integrating them into the agent's observation space for informed decision-making.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264300/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each navigation step, the agent receives K multi-view RGB-D panoramic observations (resized to 224×224) along with camera intrinsics and poses, plus a natural language instruction. It back-projects pixels into a sparse pseudo-lidar point cloud, segments views with SAM2, and extracts CLIP embeddings to enrich each Gaussian primitive with semantic attributes. The output is a 20-dimensional per-Gaussian 3D Value Map (position, scale, rotation, opacity, color, semantics, and three uncertainty scalars) that is projected to 768-d features and fused with the instruction embedding to produce a probability distribution over candidate waypoints, from which the next action is selected via nearest-neighbor mapping.

## Method

The agent constructs a Semantic Gaussian Map (SGM) by initializing differentiable 3D Gaussian primitives from RGB-D observations and jointly optimizing them via differentiable α-blending rendering for color, depth, and semantic consistency, then pruning low-contribution primitives. On top of SGM, geometric uncertainty is estimated through variational inference over position and scale perturbations (minimizing KL divergence with Gaussian and uniform priors), semantic uncertainty via variational inference over semantic attribute perturbations, and appearance uncertainty via a block-diagonal Fisher Information approximation of the reconstruction-loss Hessian per Gaussian. These three uncertainty scalars are attached to each Gaussian to form a unified 3D Value Map, whose features are aggregated and concatenated with the instruction embedding, then processed by a multi-layer transformer (inherited from DUET) to predict waypoint probabilities. Training follows a two-stage protocol: pretraining with masked language modeling, single-step action prediction, and object grounding, followed by DAgger fine-tuning with pseudo-expert guidance over a dynamic topological memory.

## Results

On R2R val unseen, the method achieves 78% SR and 66% SPL, surpassing the previous best (VER at 76% SR, 65% SPL) by 2% and 1% respectively. On REVERIE val unseen, it reaches 37.65% RGS and 27.01% RGSPL, improving over BEVBert (34.71%/24.44%) by 2.94% and 2.57%. On RxR val unseen, it attains 65.2% SR and 65.6% nDTW, outperforming BEVBert (64.1%/63.9%) with comparable SDTW (53.5% vs 52.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On R2R val unseen, the method outperforms all 10 compared baselines in SR, achieving 78% versus VER (76%), 3DGS-VLN (77%), and BEVBert/GridMM (75%), with the largest gap over DUET (72%) at +6%. On REVERIE val unseen, it leads all 14 baselines in RGS (37.65% vs. 3DGS-VLN's 36.73% and BEVBert's 34.71%) and RGSPL (27.01% vs. 3DGS-VLN's 26.74%), with the most substantial margin over DUET (+5.5% RGS). On RxR val unseen, it surpasses BEVBert by 1.1% SR and 1.7% nDTW while maintaining comparable SDTW (53.5% vs. 52.6%). On REVERIE test unseen, the method achieves the best RGS (35.68%) but trails 3DGS-VLN slightly on RGSPL (25.50% vs. 25.76%), indicating a marginal trade-off in path-length-weighted grounding on the held-out test set. The method also achieves the lowest Navigation Error on R2R val unseen (2.12m vs. 2.43m for 3DGS-VLN), suggesting more precise trajectory planning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DUET dual-scale graph transformer baseline by introducing a Semantic Gaussian Map (SGM) built on 3D Gaussian Splatting as an explicit 3D scene representation, and then layers three forms of perceptual uncertainty (geometric via variational inference, semantic via attribute perturbation, and appearance via Fisher Information) on top to form a unified 3D Value Map. The variational inference framework for geometric uncertainty follows Li & Cheung (NeurIPS 2024), while the Fisher Information approximation for appearance uncertainty draws on Goli et al. (CVPR 2024) and Hanson et al. (CVPR 2025). The key conceptual departure from prior 3DGS uncertainty work, which targets novel view synthesis and reconstruction quality, is repurposing per-primitive uncertainty as navigational affordances and constraints within an embodied agent's decision loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By grounding uncertainty directly in physically meaningful 3D primitives rather than entangled latent features, the 3D Value Map offers an interpretable and modular way to make VLN agents aware of when their perception is unreliable, which is a prerequisite for safe real-world deployment. The transferability experiment (Table 13) showing that 2D-rendered uncertainty maps improve three unrelated agents suggests the uncertainty signal itself is broadly useful beyond the specific 3DGS pipeline. However, the framework remains confined to static indoor Matterport3D scenes, does not perform active perception to reduce uncertainty, and the performance gains over strong baselines (2% SR on R2R, ~3% RGS on REVERIE) are modest, leaving open whether the added complexity is justified at scale or in dynamic environments.
