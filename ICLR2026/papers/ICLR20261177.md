## Problem

Diffusion-based neural samplers trained via stochastic optimal control suffer from mode collapse when the target Boltzmann distribution is multimodal with large energy barriers: early in training, only a few high-weight trajectories carry signal, causing the global loss to concentrate probability mass on a single mode and ignore the rest. Existing methods solve the SOC problem in one shot without any mechanism to preserve mode coverage during the gradual approach to the target.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unnormalized energy function E(x) and inverse temperature β (so the normalizing constant Z is never computed), along with a memoryless reference path measure P^ref defined by a base drift/noise schedule (continuous SDE) or a masked CTMC generator (discrete). It outputs samples from π(x) ∝ exp(−βE(x)) in either continuous R^d or discrete {1,…,N}^d state spaces. For the discrete case, the state space is augmented with a mask symbol M, and the reference starts from the fully-masked configuration; for continuous cases, the reference terminal law ν is a tractable Gaussian, making the reward r(x) = −βE(x) − log ν(x) available in closed form.

## Method

PDNS applies the proximal point method on the space of path measures: instead of solving the global SOC objective, it iteratively solves a KL-penalized subproblem that keeps the new path measure close to the previous iterate, with the penalty strength controlled by a step size η_k. Proposition 3.1 shows the optimal solution at step k is a geometric interpolation P^k ∝ (P^ref)^{λ_k}(P^*)^{1−λ_k} with λ_k = ∏(1/(η_i+1)) → 0, so the terminal marginal is π^{1−λ_k}ν^{λ_k} — a progressive annealing from the reference to the target. Practically, each proximal step is instantiated as a proximal WDCE loss: for continuous diffusion, a weighted denoising score-matching objective where the Radon-Nikodym weights are tempered by the factor (η_k/(η_k+1)); for discrete masked diffusion, a weighted denoising cross-entropy with analogous tempered CTMC Girsanov weights. The step size η_k can follow a predefined schedule or be chosen adaptively via a one-dimensional KL trust-region constraint, balancing convergence speed against mode coverage.

## Results

On continuous synthetic benchmarks (Table 2), PDNS achieves the best Sinkhorn distance on 3 of 4 tasks: MW-54 (0.08±0.01), Funnel (129.54±4.15), GMM40 (327.83±54.68), and MoS (353.05±68.63), with MMD of 0.059±0.017 on GMM40 (best). On particle systems (Table 3), PDNS attains the best W2 on LJ-13 (1.57±0.01) and LJ-55 (3.95±0.01) and the best energy-weighted W2 on both (1.01±0.18 and 21.97±3.14). On discrete Ising/Potts models (Table 5), PDNS reduces magnetization error by 5–40× over LEAPS and outperforms MH on Potts by >10×. On maximum cut (Table 6), PDNS achieves 0.882–0.973 max/avg cut ratio, substantially exceeding random baselines (0.675–0.865).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDNS outperforms the strongest recent baselines NAAS and ASBS on most continuous benchmarks: on GMM40 (50D), PDNS achieves Sinkhorn 327.83 vs. NAAS 496.48 and ASBS not reported; on MoS (50D), PDNS reaches 353.05 vs. NAAS 394.55 and SCLD 656.10. On particle systems, PDNS beats ASBS on LJ-13 energy-weighted W2 (1.01 vs. 1.28) and LJ-55 (21.97 vs. 27.69), and matches ASBS on DW-4 W2 (0.51 vs. 0.38, where ASBS is slightly better). Against the original WDCE/PDDS baseline, PDNS shows the proximal step is critical: the paper demonstrates that removing the proximal term causes mode collapse on the Ising model (Fig. 1), while PDNS maintains full mode coverage. On discrete tasks, PDNS reduces magnetization error by 5× (Ising β_low: 9.0e-3 vs. LEAPS 3.0e-2) and 40× (Potts β_low: 8.4e-4 vs. LEAPS 3.6e-1) over LEAPS, and outperforms MH on Potts by >10× in both magnetization and correlation error. On maximum cut, PDNS achieves 0.882–0.973 max cut ratio across graph sizes, far exceeding the random baseline (0.675–0.865), demonstrating practical utility for combinatorial optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the stochastic optimal control (SOC) formulations of continuous diffusion samplers (PIS, DDS, AS) and discrete masked-diffusion samplers (MDNS) under a single path-measure framework, then applies the proximal point method in that space to decompose the global SOC problem into a sequence of local subproblems. The concrete training objective extends the weighted denoising cross-entropy (WDCE) loss of Phillips et al. (2024) and Zhu et al. (2025) with a KL-proximal penalty against the previous iterate, yielding a 'proximal WDCE' that tempers importance weights and constrains each update to a local move in path space. The theoretical analysis draws on Girsanov's theorem for Radon-Nikodym derivatives and the reciprocal property of Schrödinger bridges, while the discrete case leverages the CTMC Girsanov theorem from Ren et al. (2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Mode collapse in multimodal Boltzmann sampling is a persistent practical bottleneck that limits the reliability of diffusion-based samplers in molecular dynamics, statistical physics, and combinatorial optimization; PDNS provides a principled, theoretically grounded remedy with convergence guarantees (geometric interpolation toward the target) and an adaptive scheduler that balances exploration speed against stability. The unified continuous/discrete treatment is a useful contribution, but the authors acknowledge two limitations: only a single proximal WDCE instantiation is explored, and experiments are confined to controlled benchmarks rather than large-scale real-world applications. Open directions include other proximal objectives, non-memoryless reference dynamics, and extension to more general state spaces.
