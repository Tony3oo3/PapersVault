## Problem

Standard VAE tokenizers for latent diffusion models produce latent spaces dominated by low-level details with poor diffusability, while semantic regularization approaches (e.g., VA-VAE) still require the encoder to learn semantic structure from scratch via a competing regularization loss. The paper asks whether directly aligning a pretrained visual foundation encoder—rather than learning semantics from scratch—yields a more diffusion-friendly latent space with competitive reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264160/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tokenizer takes RGB images (resized to 224×224 for the DINOv2-L/14 encoder with patch size 14, producing 16×16 spatial feature maps) and outputs a continuous latent code of 32 or 64 channels at 1/16 downsampling. For generation, the diffusion model consumes this latent along with a class label (ImageNet) or text prompt (LAION T2I) and produces images at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution with variable aspect ratios. The tokenizer itself is trained on 256-resolution images but generalizes to 512-resolution generation without retraining.

## Method

AlignTok implements a three-stage progressive alignment of a frozen DINOv2-L/14 encoder into a diffusion-friendly tokenizer. Stage 1 (Latent Alignment) freezes the encoder and trains a 2-layer MLP adapter (projecting 1024→32 dims) plus a CNN decoder with reconstruction loss only, establishing a semantically grounded latent space without KL regularization. Stage 2 (Perceptual Alignment) jointly optimizes encoder, adapter, and decoder with reconstruction loss plus an L2 semantic preservation loss that constrains current latents to remain close to the frozen Stage-1 latents, preventing catastrophic semantic collapse while allowing the encoder to capture fine-grained perceptual details. Stage 3 (Decoder Refinement) fine-tunes only the decoder to improve reconstruction fidelity while keeping the latent space fixed. The key insight is that aligning an encoder that already encodes rich semantics is fundamentally easier than learning semantics from scratch, making the resulting latent space more structured and diffusion-friendly.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, AlignTok (f16d32) achieves a gFID of 2.17 with CFG at 80K steps (64 epochs), substantially outperforming VA-VAE (3.13) and Vanilla VAE (3.31), while also achieving the highest linear probing accuracy (35.09% vs. 22.96% and 6.04%). At 800 epochs with QKNorm, AlignTok reaches gFID 1.37, surpassing VA-VAE's 1.52. On LAION text-to-image generation at 100K steps, AlignTok achieves gFID 30.27 and KID 0.015, outperforming FLUX VAE (gFID 35.78, KID 0.018) across all human-preference metrics (PickScore 0.603 vs. 0.397, ImageReward 0.564 vs. 0.162), though reconstruction FID is higher (0.443 vs. 0.102).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlignTok consistently outperforms both Vanilla VAE and VA-VAE across all generation metrics on ImageNet: at f16d32, it achieves gFID 2.17 (w/ CFG) vs. 3.31 (Vanilla VAE) and 3.13 (VA-VAE CNN), with a 1.5–1.9 point gap. Against the architecture-matched VA-VAE† (ViT encoder trained from scratch), AlignTok still leads by ~1.0 gFID point (2.17 vs. 3.16) and 1.5 points in linear probing accuracy (35.09% vs. 33.57%), indicating the benefit comes from pretrained semantic initialization rather than architecture alone. In unconditional generation, the gap widens dramatically: AlignTok achieves gFID 11.80 vs. 29.12 (Vanilla VAE) and 19.12 (VA-VAE). On LAION text-to-image, AlignTok beats FLUX VAE on gFID (30.27 vs. 35.78), KID (0.015 vs. 0.018), and all preference metrics (PickScore, ImageReward, CLIP, VQA), but trails on reconstruction FID (0.443 vs. 0.102). Convergence is approximately 5× faster than VA-VAE (~60K vs. 300K steps for comparable quality), and 50-step AlignTok generations surpass VA-VAE's 250-step outputs. AlignTok also achieves strong generation at lower CFG scales, reducing dependence on aggressive guidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of training VAEs from scratch (or adding semantic regularization à la VA-VAE) by instead starting from a pretrained visual foundation encoder (DINOv2) and progressively teaching it reconstruction through a three-stage alignment procedure. Conceptually, it inverts the difficulty hierarchy: rather than forcing a reconstruction-trained encoder to also learn semantics via a regularization loss, it preserves existing semantics and adds reconstruction ability on top. The approach is distinct from RAE (which freezes the encoder entirely, yielding high-dimensional latents) and from discrete-tokenizer work that repurposes pretrained encoders for autoregressive models; AlignTok targets continuous tokenizers for diffusion and introduces a lightweight adapter plus a semantic preservation L2 loss as the key architectural and training innovations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a ~5× convergence speedup for diffusion model training and consistent generation-quality gains over both vanilla VAE and VA-VAE across class-conditional, unconditional, and text-to-image settings make this a straightforward drop-in improvement for practitioners. The simplicity of the method (one adapter, one L2 loss, three training stages) lowers the barrier to adoption. Open questions include closing the reconstruction gap with FLUX VAE, extending to higher resolutions and video, and whether the alignment strategy transfers to discrete tokenizers or autoregressive generation. The paper also notes that combining RAE's high-channel semantic latents with AlignTok's fine-tuning could yield the best of both worlds, suggesting a productive future direction.
