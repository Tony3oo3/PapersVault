## Problem

Existing automated data augmentation (AutoDA) frameworks are designed for image data and fail to capture time series-specific properties such as autocorrelation, distribution shape, and high-order dynamics. Representation learning alternatives follow a two-stage pipeline that decouples augmentation from downstream objectives, limiting adaptability. No prior framework jointly optimizes augmentation selection probability and intensity in an end-to-end manner conditioned on time series features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263399/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts univariate or multivariate time series of arbitrary length and extracts 24 Catch22 descriptive statistics (autocorrelation, distribution-shape, and differential-based features) as a static conditioning vector shared across all layers. Each of K=3 stacked augmentation layers outputs a probability vector over n transformations and a corresponding intensity vector; the final output is a sequentially transformed time series fed to a downstream model. A raw-transform bias term retains the unmodified input with probability p_rb to regularize against over-augmentation.

## Method

AutoDA-Timeseries stacks K augmentation layers, each containing two MLPs that predict a transformation-selection probability vector and an intensity vector, conditioned on the global Catch22 feature vector and the previous layer's probability vector. Transformation selection is made differentiable via Gumbel-Softmax with a learnable per-layer temperature that balances exploration (high temperature, uniform selection) and exploitation (low temperature, deterministic selection). Training uses a composite loss with learnable weights combining the task-specific loss, an intra-layer Shannon-entropy diversity term, and an inter-layer KL-divergence term, all backpropagated jointly with the downstream model in a single end-to-end stage. This design allows the framework to adapt both which transformations to apply and how strongly, per sample and per layer, without any proxy model or manual policy search.

## Results

AutoDA-Timeseries consistently achieves the best performance across all five evaluated tasks. On long-term forecasting with RNN, it attains MSE 0.3968 and MAE 0.3930, outperforming the best baseline (UniformAugment at MSE 0.4416) by ~10%. On classification with TCN, it reaches 73.0% accuracy (+6.7% over NoAug), and on anomaly detection with UNet it achieves F1 0.7478 vs. 0.7171 for the best baseline (UniformAugment). The method also leads on short-term forecasting (SMAPE 11.068 on RNN) and regression (MSE 1.0350 on MLP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoDA-Timeseries outperforms all seven baselines across every task and downstream model. The three representation-learning baselines (TS2Vec, InfoTS, AutoTCL) generally degrade performance substantially—e.g., on long-term forecasting with RNN, TS2Vec inflates MSE from 0.5408 (NoAug) to 1.3851, and InfoTS to 1.5163—confirming the paper's argument that two-stage contrastive pipelines are poorly aligned with downstream architectures like RNNs. Among the four image-origin AutoDA baselines, UniformAugment is the strongest competitor (e.g., MSE 0.4416 on RNN forecasting, F1 0.7171 on UNet anomaly detection), but AutoDA-Timeseries still leads by 10%+ in MSE and 4% in F1. RandAugment, TrivialAugment, and A2Aug yield only marginal or negative gains over NoAug on most tasks, underscoring that directly transferring image-based augmentation policies to time series is ineffective. The consistent wins across TCN/ROCKET (classification), RNN/Autoformer (forecasting), CNN/MLP (regression), and UNet/VAE (anomaly detection) demonstrate that the feature-conditioned, end-to-end optimization strategy generalizes across diverse model families.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by transplanting the automated data augmentation (AutoDA) paradigm—previously confined to image classification (RandAugment, TrivialAugment, UniformAugment, A2Aug)—into the time series domain, where no general-purpose AutoDA framework existed. It departs from the two-stage representation learning pipeline (TS2Vec, InfoTS, AutoTCL) by adopting a single-stage, end-to-end formulation where augmentation policies are jointly optimized with the downstream model. The technical core repurposes Gumbel-Softmax categorical reparameterization and MLP-based policy generators, conditioning them on Catch22 time series features to make augmentation selection modality-aware rather than modality-agnostic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing the first task-agnostic AutoDA framework for time series, this work gives practitioners a drop-in augmentation module that avoids the representation-mismatch problem of contrastive pretraining and the modality-blindness of image-based AutoDA. The consistent gains across five heterogeneous tasks (classification, long/short-term forecasting, regression, anomaly detection) and multiple architectures (TCN, ROCKET, RNN, Autoformer, CNN, MLP, UNet, VAE) suggest broad practical utility. Open questions include scalability to very long or highly non-stationary sequences, whether the 24-feature Catch22 conditioning remains sufficient for domains with complex multivariate dynamics, and how the framework behaves under extreme data scarcity or distribution shift in real-world deployment.
