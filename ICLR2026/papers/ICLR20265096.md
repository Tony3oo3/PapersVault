## Problem

State-of-the-art self-gated activation functions (GELU, SiLU, Meta-ACON, etc.) improve CNN layers but fail to enhance Transformer layers, where non-local context is already modeled by attention. The authors identify this as the 'non-local tension' problem, rooted in a 'convergence limitation': saturating gating functions (sigmoid, ERF) assign nearly identical weights to pre-activations that differ substantially in importance, neutralizing the additional signal that non-local token mixers inject into features.

## Input / Output

FleS is a drop-in activation replacement that consumes the same inputs as the host backbone (RGB images at standard resolutions for vision tasks, token sequences for NLP) and produces the same outputs (class labels, bounding boxes, GLUE task scores). The distinguishing input to the activation module is a channel-wise 'effective mean response' vector — the mean of positive pre-activations per channel computed over a reference region (full mini-batch for ImageNet, a 9×15 patch for COCO detection, or a token-level statistic for NLP) — which is fed through two small MLPs (channel reduction ratio 32) to yield the scaling coefficients.

## Method

FleS modifies the standard self-gated form φ(x̃)=ρ(x̃)·x̃ into φ(x̃)=κ_ve·ρ(κ_ho·x̃)·x̃, where κ_ve (vertical scaling) rectifies the activation bound and κ_ho (horizontal scaling) modulates the gating steepness. Both coefficients are derived from the normalized channel effective mean response vector {x̄_c⁺} (mean of non-negative pre-activations per channel, divided by the cross-channel average), processed by lightweight MLPs with Softplus output to ensure positivity. The positive-only statistic is motivated by a formal result (Proposition 4.1) showing that under a flat response distribution, negative features contribute negligibly compared to positive ones, so excluding them prevents cancellation effects. This adaptive dual scaling keeps the gating function operating in its informative (non-saturated) region for high-importance features, preserving discriminative weight differences that a fixed ρ would collapse, thereby resolving the convergence limitation and mitigating non-local tension in Transformer and other non-local architectures.

## Results

On ImageNet with Swin-Min (120 epochs), FleS achieves 71.4% Top-1 accuracy, outperforming the GELU baseline (68.7%) by 2.7 points and the best competing method AdaShift (69.7%) by 1.7 points. On PoolFormer-S12 (300 epochs), FleS reaches 79.4% Top-1, surpassing IIEU (78.6%) and Meta-ACON (78.0%). On ResNet-50, FleS attains 80.1% Top-1 versus AdaShift's 79.9% and IIEU's 79.7%, while adding only ~2.5M parameters and negligible FLOPs overhead. On GLUE (BERT-Tiny), FleS-SeqGate improves CoLA MCC from 12.13 (GELU) to 23.48 and MNLI-matched accuracy from 65.58 to 71.77.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FleS consistently outperforms all 13 compared activation methods across every backbone and dataset tested. Against static self-gated baselines (GELU, SiLU, Mish), FleS gains 1.7–2.7 points on Swin-Min and 1.2–2.8 points on PoolFormer-S12. Against dynamic self-gated methods that also exploit non-local cues (Meta-ACON, SMU, Pserf), FleS achieves substantially larger margins: +3.1 points over Meta-ACON on Swin-Min and +1.4 points over SMU on PoolFormer-S12, whereas those methods barely beat or even underperform the GELU baseline on Transformer layers. Against the closest decision-making-based competitors IIEU and AdaShift (same author's prior work), FleS still leads by 1.9 and 1.7 points on Swin-Min, and by 0.8 and 1.2 points on PoolFormer-S12. On ResNet-50, FleS (80.1%) edges out AdaShift (79.9%) and IIEU (79.7%) while matching the parameter count of SE-Net-augmented ReLU. On GLUE, FleS-SeqGate improves CoLA MCC by +11.35 over GELU on BERT-Tiny and SST-2 accuracy by +2.22, confirming generalizability to NLP. Notably, Pserf fails to train (NaN) on PoolFormer-S12, and StarReLU underperforms GELU on that backbone, highlighting FleS's robustness advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decision-making analytical framework introduced in Cai (2023, IIEU) to identify a newly formalized problem—non-local tension—wherein saturating gating functions in self-gated activations (e.g., Sigmoid in SiLU, ERF in GELU) lose discriminative power for high-importance features, thereby neutralizing the benefits of non-local context already modeled by Transformer attention. It departs from prior dynamic activation methods (Meta-ACON, SMU, Pserf) that inject non-local cues into the gating mechanism but fail to improve over static GELU in Transformer layers, arguing that the issue is not the absence of non-local information but the convergence limitation of the scaling function itself. The proposed FleS mechanism builds directly on the SiLU self-gated form φ(x̃)=ρ(x̃)·x̃, adding two adaptive scaling coefficients (vertical and horizontal) derived from channel-wise effective mean responses of positive pre-activations, processed by lightweight MLPs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of non-local tension provides a principled explanation for why prior context-aware activation methods underperform in Transformers, a gap that has limited progress in activation design for non-local architectures. The practical FleS model achieves substantial gains (2.7–6.5% Top-1) across Swin-Transformer, PoolFormer, ResNet, and BERT backbones with minimal parameter overhead, suggesting that activation function design remains an underexploited degree of freedom in modern architectures. Open questions include whether the channel effective mean response indicator generalizes to architectures without clear channel semantics (e.g., state-space models), whether the positive-only indicator assumption holds in domains where negative responses carry meaningful signal, and whether the convergence limitation can be addressed through architectural changes rather than post-hoc scaling.
