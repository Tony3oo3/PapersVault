## Problem

Denoising Diffusion Variational Inference (DDVI) for Deep Gaussian Processes initializes the reverse diffusion SDE from a fixed, unconditional Gaussian prior that is typically far from the true posterior over inducing variables. This large posterior gap forces the reverse-time SDE to traverse long, complex trajectories, resulting in inefficient inference, high variance, and slow convergence. The paper addresses this by replacing the unconditional start with a learnable, data-dependent initial distribution that progressively adapts toward the posterior during training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes input features x (tabular data or ResNet-20 convolutional features for CIFAR-10) and, crucially, uses the inducing inputs Z^(l) ∈ R^{M_l × d_in} at each layer as structured, low-dimensional proxies for the amortized network rather than raw data. This design avoids the dimensional mismatch between mini-batch inputs [B, d_in] and inducing variables [M_l, d_out], enabling batch-wise amortized inference. The output is a full posterior distribution over all inducing variables U = {u^(l)}_{l=1}^L, from which predictive means and variances are obtained via standard sparse GP forward propagation through the DGP layers.

## Method

DBVI replaces DDVI's unconditional Gaussian start with an amortized initial distribution p_0^θ(U_0|x) = N(μ_θ(x), σ²I), where a layer-wise neural network μ_θ^(l) maps each inducing input z^(l) to a d_out-dimensional mean, keeping variance fixed. The theoretical core reinterprets the prior via Doob's h-transform, yielding a forward bridge SDE with an additional drift term g(t)²∇_{U_t} log p(U_0|U_t) and a reverse bridge SDE driven by a conditional score s_cond = s_φ + h. Proposition 2 derives closed-form Gaussian marginals for the Doob-augmented bridge with coupled ODEs for mean m_t and variance κ_t, and Proposition 3 combines these into a tractable per-mini-batch ELBO with a score-matching quadratic term ‖(U_t − m_t)/κ_t + s_cond‖². The amortizer is conditioned on inducing inputs Z^(l) rather than raw data, preserving global dataset structure while matching the dimensionality of inducing variables. Training jointly optimizes the drift network s_φ, amortizer μ_θ, and DGP hyperparameters γ by maximizing the ELBO, with the reverse bridge SDE simulated via Euler-Maruyama discretization for reparameterized gradients.

## Results

On image classification, DBVI achieves 99.10% accuracy on MNIST (4-layer), 91.07% on Fashion-MNIST, and 95.68% on CIFAR-10, consistently outperforming DDVI (99.01%, 90.85%, 95.56%) and all other baselines. On large-scale physics datasets, DBVI attains the best AUC of 0.889 on SUSY and 0.859 on HIGGS (5-layer DGP), surpassing DDVI (0.886, 0.857) and SGHMC (0.884, 0.853). On the Frey Faces reconstruction task, DBVI achieves the lowest RMSE of 7.52 and best NLL of 1.12, compared to DDVI's 7.64 and 1.17. Across 10 UCI regression benchmarks, DBVI consistently yields the lowest test NLL and MSE, with particularly large gains on YearMSD and Airline where DDVI suffers from slow convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DBVI outperforms all four baselines across every evaluated task. Against DDVI (the closest competitor and direct predecessor), DBVI gains 0.09–0.22% accuracy on image classification (e.g., 95.68% vs. 95.56% on CIFAR-10 4-layer) and 0.002–0.003 AUC on large-scale SUSY/HIGGS, with the gap widening on regression benchmarks where DDVI's unconditional initialization causes slow convergence. Against IPVI, DBVI leads by 1.2–2.2% accuracy on classification and 0.006–0.007 AUC on large-scale tasks, while also being more stable (IPVI's adversarial training is noted as prone to instability). Against SGHMC, DBVI achieves comparable or higher accuracy/AUC while being 5–15× faster per iteration (e.g., 0.55s vs. 1.22s on MNIST, 0.74s vs. 8.61s on CIFAR-10). Against DSVI, DBVI leads by 1.7–3.7% accuracy on classification and 0.011–0.013 AUC on large-scale data, reflecting the expressiveness advantage of diffusion-based posteriors over mean-field Gaussians. The per-iteration overhead of DBVI over DDVI is minimal (~0.05s), attributable to the amortized network forward pass.

## Relation to Prior Work

DBVI is a direct extension of DDVI (Xu et al., 2024), which itself adapts the reverse-time SDE and Girsanov-based ELBO machinery from score-based generative modeling (Song et al., 2020) to posterior inference in deep Gaussian processes (Damianou & Lawrence, 2013). The key departure is replacing DDVI's fixed unconditional Gaussian start with a learnable, amortized initial distribution parameterized by a neural network conditioned on inducing inputs, formalized through Doob's h-transform to yield a diffusion bridge process. This bridges two previously separate threads—diffusion bridge models (Zhou et al., 2023; Li et al., 2023a) and amortized variational inference (Kim et al., 2018; Margossian & Blei, 2023)—within the DGP inference setting, while preserving the exact theoretical structure (reverse-time SDEs, Girsanov ELBO) of DDVI as a special case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic path-length reduction (e.g., 0.368 vs. 12.167 on Concrete) provides concrete evidence that the amortized bridge initialization fundamentally shortens the inference trajectory, translating into faster convergence and slightly better predictive accuracy across regression, classification, and reconstruction tasks. The structured amortization via inducing inputs is a practical design choice that avoids the dimensional mismatch and overfitting pitfalls of conditioning on raw data, making the approach scalable to millions of samples (SUSY, HIGGS). Open questions include whether the bridge formulation extends to non-Gaussian or non-linear forward SDEs, whether tighter theoretical guarantees on posterior quality can be established, and whether the approach generalizes beyond DGPs to other hierarchical Bayesian models with complex posteriors.
