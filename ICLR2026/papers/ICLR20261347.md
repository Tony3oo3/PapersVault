## Problem

Post-training VLMs for image quality assessment (IQA) typically depends on costly human-annotated labels via supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, yet self-supervised self-evolving techniques that have proven effective for reasoning tasks remain largely unexplored for perceptual domains. The core challenge is that IQA is inherently continuous and subjective—there is no absolute correct score—making the design of effective self-supervision signals non-trivial compared to discrete reasoning tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261347/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images in two modes: during the offline stage, pairs of images are fed to the VLM alongside a comparative prompt asking which has better perceptual quality; during the online stage, individual images are paired with a scoring prompt requesting a float rating between 1 and 5 (rounded to two decimal places). The VLM output is structured with an internal reasoning monologue enclosed in think tags followed by the final answer in a boxed{} format, and positional bias is mitigated by randomly permuting image order in pairwise comparisons.

## Method

EvoQuality is a fully self-supervised, iterative two-stage framework that eliminates all ground-truth labels. In the offline stage, the VLM queries itself K=32 times per image pair using a comparative prompt and applies majority voting to produce high-confidence pseudo-ranking labels (1, 0, or 0.5 for tie). In the online stage, these pseudo-labels are converted into a fidelity reward (adapted from the Thurstone model and FRank) that measures agreement between the model's predicted comparative probability and the consensus preference, then guides policy updates via GRPO with KL regularization against a fixed reference policy. The key insight is adapting self-consistency to IQA's inherently relative nature: rather than regressing to absolute scores, the model learns from pairwise majority-voted rankings, which provides a more robust and generalizable learning signal. The loop can be iterated (Round 1 on authentic images, Round 2 adding synthetic distortions), progressively refining the VLM's perceptual judgment without any human annotation.

## Results

EvoQuality@Round2 achieves a weighted-average PLCC of 0.762 and SRCC of 0.719 across seven out-of-distribution IQA benchmarks, representing a 31.8% and 33.7% improvement over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B zero-shot baseline (0.615 PLCC / 0.570 SRCC). On the challenging TID2013 dataset, EvoQuality reaches 0.674 PLCC, exceeding the next-best method (NIQE at 0.548) by 0.126. The method surpasses the strongest supervised VLM baseline, Q-Insight (0.704 PLCC / 0.667 SRCC WAVG), by more than 0.05 on both metrics, despite using no ground-truth labels during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvoQuality@Round2 outperforms all handcrafted (NIQE, BRISQUE) and discriminative deep-learning baselines (NIMA, MUSIQ, CLIP-IQA+, MANIQA, etc.) by a wide margin on the weighted average, with the largest gap on TID2013 (0.674 vs. 0.548 for NIQE, the best non-VLM method). Among VLM-based supervised models, EvoQuality beats Q-Insight (0.704) and VisualQuality-R1 (0.667) on WAVG PLCC, and surpasses Q-Align (0.663) and DeQA-Score (0.652) as well. However, EvoQuality trails supervised models on the in-domain KONIQ dataset (0.835 vs. 0.953 for DeQA-Score) and on LIVEW (0.847 vs. 0.891 for DeQA-Score), where ground-truth labels provide a direct advantage. The most dramatic wins occur on TID2013 (0.674 vs. 0.483 for Q-Insight, +0.191) and PIPAL (0.649 vs. 0.608 for Q-Insight), confirming superior generalization to synthetic and perceptual-restoration distortions. When stacked on top of VisualQuality-R1 (Table 4), EvoQuality further lifts out-of-distribution PLCC from 0.722 to 0.768, demonstrating additive value beyond standalone use.

## Relation to Prior Work

EvoQuality sits at the intersection of three research threads: self-consistency-based self-evolving methods (Wang et al., 2022; TTRL; MM-UPT) that were previously applied only to discrete reasoning tasks like math and geometry; ranking-based IQA models (VisualQuality-R1, Compare2Score, dipIQ) that reframe quality assessment as pairwise comparison; and GRPO-based RL post-training (Shao et al., 2024). The key conceptual departure is adapting the self-consistency voting paradigm—where a model's own diverse outputs are aggregated via majority vote to produce pseudo-labels—to the continuous, subjective domain of perceptual quality, where no absolute correct answer exists. Unlike prior self-evolving work that relies on verifiable correctness (e.g., a math answer is right or wrong), EvoQuality must define a meaningful reward signal from relative preferences, which it achieves by combining Thurstone-model comparative probabilities with a fidelity reward (Tsai et al., 2007) computed against self-generated pseudo-rankings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: it provides a scalable path to improve VLM-based IQA without the prohibitively expensive human subjective studies that have bottlenecked the field, and the framework can be stacked atop existing supervised models to boost out-of-distribution generalization. However, the method still requires an in-domain pool of unlabeled images (here, KONIQ), and the iterative voting-plus-GRPO loop carries substantial computational cost (~12 hours per epoch on 8 A100s). Open questions include whether the approach transfers to other subjective perceptual tasks (aesthetics, video quality, generative realism), whether more efficient active-sampling strategies can reduce the voting budget, and whether the in-domain performance gap versus supervised methods can be closed with more evolution rounds or larger unlabeled corpora.
