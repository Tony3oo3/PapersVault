## Problem

Existing LLM agent RL methods sample independent chain-based trajectories, which wastes rollout budget through redundant prefix generation and provides only trajectory-level outcome rewards. In long-horizon multi-turn agent tasks, this sparse supervision makes it difficult to attribute credit to specific steps, leading to unstable training and even collapse. The paper asks whether more fine-grained process supervision can be constructed under a limited rollout budget while relying solely on outcome rewards.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262951/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text question and a search environment (local Wikipedia retrieval or real web search API) as input, and produces a multi-turn ReAct trajectory of Thought-Action-Observation tuples ending in a text answer. Each tree node in the rollout corresponds to one complete agent step (a thought, a parsable search action, and the returned observation), not a token or sentence. The rollout budget is explicitly constrained in both total tokens and number of tool-calls, with the tree structure sharing common prefixes to reduce redundant generation.

## Method

Tree-GRPO replaces independent chain-based rollouts with a tree-search sampling strategy where each node is a complete agent step. The rollout proceeds in an initialize-then-expand fashion: M independent chains are generated in parallel, then N non-leaf nodes are randomly sampled per tree for L expansion iterations, yielding M×(L×N+1) rollouts under a budget of M·B + L·N·B/2 (where B is the expected cost of one full trajectory). Group relative advantages are estimated at two levels: intra-tree (within each tree's shared-prefix group, providing step-level preference signals whose granularity is set by subtree depth) and inter-tree (across all trees, providing a stable global baseline). The final advantage is the sum of both. A key theoretical result shows that the intra-tree GRPO gradient is structurally equivalent to step-level DPO under a binary preference assumption, differing only in the weight term, thereby embedding an implicit step-level preference learning objective into online RL without any additional supervision.

## Results

On multi-hop QA (HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, Musique, Bamboogle), Tree-GRPO achieves average EM scores of 19.1 (Qwen2.5-1.5b), 36.8 (Qwen2.5-3b), 36.8 (Qwen2.5-7b), and 45.3 (Qwen2.5-14b), yielding relative improvements of 69%, 16%, 38%, and 8.4% over chain-based GRPO respectively. On single-hop QA (NQ, TriviaQA, PopQA), Tree-GRPO achieves 47.5, 50.0, 50.0, and 55.7 average EM across the same model sizes, with gains of 9.5%–4.0% over GRPO. On web-agent QA (GAIA), Tree-GRPO achieves a 28% average F1 improvement over GRPO (e.g., 24.3 vs 15.0 on GAIA Lv.2 for Qwen2.5-14b). Under a quarter of the rollout budget (≈2 vs ≈8 trajectories per prompt), Tree-GRPO still outperforms chain-based GRPO with a 112% relative improvement on multi-hop QA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tree-GRPO consistently outperforms chain-based GRPO across all model scales (1.5b–14b) and all three task categories, with the largest gains on multi-hop QA for small models: 69% relative improvement on Qwen2.5-1.5b (19.1 vs 11.3 avg EM) and 38% on Llama3.2-3b (36.8 vs 26.7). Against GSPO, Tree-GRPO wins on multi-hop QA for every model size (e.g., 36.8 vs 33.5 on Qwen2.5-3b; 45.3 vs 44.2 on Qwen2.5-14b), while GSPO occasionally matches or slightly edges Tree-GRPO on single-hop QA for larger models. Compared to the non-RL baselines (ReAct and Search-o1), Tree-GRPO yields dramatic improvements—e.g., on Qwen2.5-3b multi-hop QA, Tree-GRPO reaches 36.8 EM versus 15.8 for ReAct and 17.9 for Search-o1. On web-agent QA (Table 2), Tree-GRPO beats GRPO on all four benchmarks, most notably on GAIA where it achieves 24.3 F1 on Lv.2 versus 15.0 for GRPO (Qwen2.5-14b), a 28% average improvement. Notably, under a quarter of the rollout budget (≈2 trajectories/prompt vs ≈8), Tree-GRPO still surpasses chain-based GRPO by 112% on multi-hop QA, highlighting its budget efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

Tree-GRPO extends the GRPO family of group-based RL (DeepSeek-R1) to multi-turn agent settings by replacing independent chain-based rollouts with tree-search sampling anchored at the agent step level (Thought-Action-Observation tuples). It is conceptually adjacent to prior tree-based RL methods such as VinePPO, SPO, and TreeRL, but departs from them by operating at the semantically meaningful agent-step granularity rather than token/sentence level, and by deriving step-level process supervision signals purely from outcome rewards without requiring a separate process reward model. The theoretical contribution bridges intra-tree group-relative advantage estimation to step-level DPO (Bradley-Terry preference learning), connecting two previously separate lines of work (online RL and offline preference optimization) under a shared gradient structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in reducing rollout cost by roughly 4× while simultaneously improving performance, which directly addresses the dominant bottleneck in multi-turn agent RL training where token and tool-call budgets are expensive. The implicit step-level preference learning property offers a scalable, plug-and-play alternative to process reward models that require costly step-level annotations. However, the binary preference assumption underlying the DPO equivalence is restrictive, web-agent QA results are limited by training data scarcity, and the method's gains diminish for larger models (14B) where chain-based RL already performs well. Open questions include whether the tree-structure hyperparameters (M, N, L) can be automatically adapted to task complexity, and whether the approach extends to non-binary or continuous reward settings.
