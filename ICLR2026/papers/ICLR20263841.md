## Problem

Existing long story visualization methods rely on either auto-regressive generation (suffering error accumulation) or fixed reference images (lacking global coherence and propagating reference flaws), both of which degrade in quality as story length increases. Neither paradigm can simultaneously maintain semantic consistency across all frames and generate fine-grained character interactions that go beyond the base Stable Diffusion model's capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263841/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes per-frame text prompts for a story of length B (up to 100 frames) and random noise as initial input. During initialization, only text prompts guide generation; in subsequent external iterations, the full-length B images from the previous iteration serve as reference images. The output is a sequence of B RGB images at 1024×1024 resolution, refined over L=10 external iterations, with each iteration producing a complete updated story sequence that feeds into the next.

## Method

Story-Iter introduces an external iterative paradigm that goes beyond the internal denoising steps of diffusion models: each iteration regenerates all B story frames using the full-length previous iteration as reference, progressively converging the global embedding distribution for semantic consistency. The key architectural component is Global Reference Cross-Attention (GRCA), a plug-and-play module that encodes all reference frames via CLIP global embeddings, projects them into a small number of tokens (n=4), and uses them as keys and values in cross-attention with the intermediate denoising features as queries. GRCA directly reuses IP-Adapter's pretrained cross-attention weights without any training, and its adaptive token-similarity mechanism selectively aggregates features from reference frames most relevant to the current frame. A linear weighting strategy λ_i = λ_1 + q×(i−1) (with λ_1=0.3, λ_L=0.5 over 10 iterations) balances text controllability against visual consistency, increasing the reference influence as iterations progress. The entire pipeline is training-free, built on pretrained Stable Diffusion (or SDXL) with DDIM sampling.

## Results

On the long story benchmark (up to 100 frames), Story-IterXL achieves the best aCCS of 0.802 and aFID of 94.30, outperforming StoryDiffusion (aCCS 0.768, aFID 102.44) by +3.4% in aCCS and −8.14 in aFID. On the regular-length StorySalon benchmark, Story-IterXL reaches aCCS 0.818 and aFID 14.63, surpassing StoryDiffusion (aCCS 0.765, aFID 14.84) by +5.3% in aCCS. Story-IterXL also leads in CLIP-T (text-image alignment) among story-specific methods on both benchmarks (0.318 long, 0.310 regular), though FLUX.1.Kontext achieves a lower aFID (78.03) on long stories at the cost of significantly worse CLIP-T (0.290) and much higher compute (371 PFLOPs, 68 GB VRAM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Story-IterXL wins on aCCS (semantic consistency) against all story-visualization baselines: it beats StoryDiffusion by +3.4% (0.802 vs 0.768), 1prompt1story by +1.2% (0.802 vs 0.790), and ConsiStory by +4.1% (0.802 vs 0.761) on the long story benchmark. On aFID, Story-IterXL (94.30) outperforms StoryDiffusion (102.44), ConsiStory (108.83), and Nano Banana (106.14), but trails FLUX.1.Kontext (78.03) and IP-AdapterXL (88.69); however, FLUX.1.Kontext requires 371 PFLOPs and 68 GB VRAM versus Story-IterXL's 43 PFLOPs and 19 GB, and FLUX.1.Kontext scores much lower on CLIP-T (0.290 vs 0.318), indicating weaker text alignment. On the regular-length StorySalon benchmark, Story-IterXL achieves the best aCCS (0.818) and aFID (14.63), surpassing StoryDiffusion (0.765 / 14.84) and IP-AdapterXL (0.758 / 14.70). The AR-paradigm baselines (AR-LDM, StoryGen) degrade substantially on long stories (aFID > 126), confirming the error-accumulation limitation the iterative paradigm addresses. Story-IterXL also leads in human preference scores (4.65/5) for long story visualization, outscoring StoryDiffusion (4.35/5) and IP-AdapterXL (4.11/5) on alignment, interaction, consistency, and overall preference.

## Relation to Prior Work

Story-Iter departs from both the Auto-Regressive paradigm (StoryGen, AR-LDM) and the Reference-Image paradigm (StoryDiffusion, ConsiStory) by introducing an external iterative refinement loop that uses all B frames from the previous iteration as references. Mechanically, it repurposes IP-Adapter's decoupled cross-attention weights in a training-free manner, but replaces the single-image reference with a Global Reference Cross-Attention (GRCA) module that encodes all reference frames via compact CLIP global embeddings (4 tokens each), sidestepping the memory bottleneck of StoryDiffusion's Consistent Self-Attention on intermediate denoising features. The paper also introduces a new long-story benchmark (up to 100 frames) generated with GPT-4o, extending evaluation beyond the StorySalon regular-length setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free iterative paradigm offers a practical path to long-story consistency without fine-tuning, and the GRCA's use of global embeddings makes it scalable to 200+ frames with modest VRAM (19 GB vs. 40 GB for StoryDiffusion). However, the 250-minute inference time for 100 frames at 1024×1024 remains a significant practical barrier, and the paper acknowledges a fundamental trade-off: more iterations improve consistency (aCCS) but degrade text-image alignment (CLIP-T). Open questions include whether content-aware scheduling (e.g., LLM-guided early stopping) can resolve this trade-off, and whether the approach generalizes to video or 3D narrative generation.
