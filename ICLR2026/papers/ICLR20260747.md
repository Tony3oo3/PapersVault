## Problem

Existing MLLM benchmarks for action understanding rely on randomly sampled or human-curated distractors that fail to probe fine-grained action discrimination. When distractors are instead generated by SOTA specialist action recognition models (hard example mining), even GPT-4o drops from 87.6% to 52.2% accuracy on EPIC-KITCHENS-100, revealing that current MLLMs cannot reliably contrast visually or semantically similar actions. The paper asks how to both expose this weakness and train MLLMs to overcome it.

## Input / Output

The model consumes video clips (8–64 frames depending on the benchmark), a text prompt or question, and optionally narrations of the prior two actions for temporal-order tasks. Outputs include free-text captions, open-ended or multiple-choice QA answers, action start/end timestamps (rounded to 2 decimal places within a 3-second padded window), and structured action labels. For structured prediction, a two-stage pipeline first queries an external action recognition model (e.g., TIM) for its top-K confident classes, then the MLLM selects among those K candidates plus the ground-truth narration, enabling fair comparison with specialist models on datasets with ~4K action types.

## Method

LLaVAction combines three key design choices on top of LLaVA-Video/OV backbones (SigLIP-384 visual encoder + Qwen2 LLM). First, a learnable action token is inserted between visual and text tokens in the sequence; three classification heads (noun, verb, action) are attached to its final-layer hidden state and trained with cross-entropy loss, providing intermediate supervision that forces the token to aggregate action-relevant visual information (analogous to a CLS token). Second, the training data is reformulated across five complementary perspectives: adversarial MQA with AVION-generated distractors (OOD relative to the TIM-based test benchmark), temporal boundary detection with random 3-second padding, prior-action-conditioned sequential prediction, direct action description, and GPT-4o-distilled general video QA. Third, a two-stage inference pipeline uses an external model's top-K predictions to narrow the candidate set before the MLLM makes its final selection, achieving 190× speedup over multi-round appending while enabling structured output. The full model is fine-tuned for 2 epochs on 530K EPIC-KITCHENS-100 video-language pairs mixed with LLaVA-Video-178K replay data, raising LLaVA-Video-7B from 34.8% to 74.1% on the OOD EPIC-KITCHENS-100-MQA benchmark.

## Results

On the newly introduced EPIC-KITCHENS-100-MQA benchmark (hard distractors from TIM), LLaVAction-7B achieves 74.1% top-1 accuracy in the OOD setting (trained with AVION distractors), a 21-point improvement over GPT-4o (52.2%) and a 39-point improvement over the zero-shot LLaVA-Video-7B baseline (34.8%). On the standard EPIC-KITCHENS-100 action recognition benchmark, LLaVAction-7B achieves 58.3% (action label) and 63.2% (action narration) top-1 accuracy, surpassing the previous SOTA TIM (56.4%) and AVION (54.4%). The model also achieves SOTA on EPFL-Smart-Kitchen-30 (46.6% vs. 40.0%), MECCANO (51.7% vs. SlowFast's 42.8%), and Animal Kingdom (61.0% Jaccard vs. VideoMAE's 53.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLaVAction-7B decisively outperforms GPT-4o (52.2%) by 21 points on EPIC-KITCHENS-100-MQA, and surpasses all zero-shot open-source MLLMs (LLaVA-Video-7B at 34.8%, LLaVA-OV-7B at 30.5%) by over 35 points. On the established EPIC-KITCHENS-100 action recognition benchmark, LLaVAction-7B (58.3% action label / 63.2% action narration) beats the previous SOTA specialized models TIM (56.4%) and AVION (54.4%), as well as LVMAE (52.1%), M&M (53.6%), and TAdaFormer (51.8%). On EPFL-Smart-Kitchen-30, LLaVAction-7B achieves 46.6% trained accuracy vs. Multi-modal VideoMAE (40.0%) and VideoMAE (37.5%), and even in zero-shot (36.2%) it surpasses AVION (19.3%) and LLaVA-Video-7B (22.5%). On MECCANO, LLaVAction-7B (51.7%) beats SlowFast (42.8%) by nearly 9 points after just one epoch of training. On Animal Kingdom, LLaVAction-7B (61.0% Jaccard) outperforms VideoMAE (53.1%) and InternVL3-8B (58.7%), while on the broader MLLM benchmarks (Table 7), LLaVAction-7B improves over LLaVA-Video-7B on 10 of 13 benchmarks (e.g., +4.3 on CVRR, +2.5 on MVBench, +2.3 on PerceptionTest) but shows minor regressions on VideoDC (−0.1) and NextQA (−0.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LLaVA-Video and LLaVA-OV MLLM architectures, extending them with an action token (analogous to the CLS token in ViT) and a two-stage structured prediction pipeline for fair comparison with specialist action recognition models. Conceptually, it departs from prior MLLM benchmarking work (which uses random, human-annotated, or closed-source LLM-generated distractors) by leveraging SOTA specialist action recognition models (TIM, AVION) for hard example mining, drawing from the adversarial training literature. The data reformulation paradigm also extends previous MLLM training approaches that relied solely on captioning or simple QA by incorporating adversarial distractors, temporal detection, and prior-action conditioning as distinct training objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EPIC-KITCHENS-100-MQA benchmark reveals a substantial and previously underappreciated gap in MLLM fine-grained action discrimination (GPT-4o drops 35 points from random to hard distractors), providing the field with a more rigorous evaluation tool. The hard example mining paradigm is generalizable to any action dataset and offers an efficient alternative to human or LLM-based distractor generation. Open questions include whether the OOD training strategy (AVION distractors for training, TIM for testing) is the right trade-off, whether the action token design transfers to non-egocentric or non-kitchen domains without retraining, and whether the two-stage pipeline's upper-bound limitation (GT may not be in top-K) becomes problematic as action spaces grow beyond 4K classes.
