## Problem

Existing video editing methods either require DDIM inversion as an extra inference stage to preserve reference video structure, or rely on task-specific adapter modules (e.g., separate ControlNets or cross-attention encoders) for each conditioning signal. Both approaches limit the ability to unify diverse editing tasks—ranging from local object edits to global style transfer and camera re-rendering—within a single model without architectural modifications per task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260934/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a reference video (encoded via 3D VAE), task-specific multi-modal conditions (object images, style images, camera pose sequences, text prompts, or an edited first frame), and a noisy latent, all of which are tokenized and concatenated along the frame dimension into a single input sequence. The output is a generated edited video at arbitrary resolution and length (e.g., 672×384×77 in experiments), with no fixed-length constraint. Unlike DDIM-inversion methods, there is no separate noise-initialization stage; the noisy latent is standard Gaussian noise processed jointly with all conditioning tokens in one forward pass.

## Method

UNIC formulates all video editing inputs as three token types—noisy video latent, reference video VAE tokens, and multi-modal condition tokens—and concatenates them into a single sequence processed by the native full 3D self-attention of a DiT, eliminating adapter modules and DDIM inversion entirely. Two key mechanisms enable effective task unification: (1) Task-aware RoPE assigns positional indices differently depending on whether a condition has frame-to-frame correspondence with the video (reusing noisy-token indices) or not (using a base offset plus a fixed per-task offset and slot count), preventing index collisions across variable-length inputs; (2) Condition Bias adds a learnable, zero-initialized task-specific embedding to each condition token, allowing the model to disambiguate when the same modality (e.g., an image) serves different semantic roles across tasks. Training follows a hard-to-easy sequential strategy (re-camera control first, then ID tasks, then style/propagation) to prevent easier tasks from dominating optimization, and the entire framework adds only thousands of trainable parameters on top of a frozen flow-matching DiT base model.

## Results

On the six-task UNIC benchmark, the unified model achieves leading or competitive results across all tasks. For ID Swap, UNIC attains CLIP-I 0.725 and DINO-I 0.429, outperforming VACE (0.712/0.423) and Pika (0.700/0.393). For Re-Camera Control, UNIC reduces RotErr to 1.275 and TransErr to 5.667, beating the task-specific ReCamMaster (1.454/5.695). For Propagation, UNIC achieves RefVideo-CLIP 0.840, surpassing AnyV2V (0.812) and VACE-I2V (0.574). On Stylization, UNIC reaches CSD-Score 0.259 and ArtFID 37.619, approaching the specialized StyleMaster (0.306/38.213) while matching or exceeding it in video quality metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unified VACE baseline, UNIC wins on ID Insert (CLIP-I 0.598 vs 0.522; DINO-I 0.245 vs 0.110), ID Swap (CLIP-I 0.725 vs 0.712; DINO-I 0.429 vs 0.423), ID Delete (CLIP-Score 0.217 vs 0.211; Aesthetic 5.493 vs 5.332), and Propagation (RefVideo-CLIP 0.840 vs 0.574). Against the commercial Pika system, UNIC loses on ID Insert identity metrics (CLIP-I 0.598 vs 0.689; DINO-I 0.245 vs 0.387) but surpasses it on ID Swap across all identity and quality metrics (CLIP-I 0.725 vs 0.700; Aesthetic 6.056 vs 5.210). Against the task-specific ReCamMaster, UNIC achieves lower camera errors (RotErr 1.275 vs 1.454; TransErr 5.667 vs 5.695) and better video quality (Smoothness 0.933 vs 0.917; Aesthetic 4.826 vs 4.738). Against the specialized VideoPainter on ID Delete, UNIC trails in reconstruction fidelity (PSNR 19.171 vs 22.987) but leads in prompt alignment (CLIP-Score 0.217 vs 0.212) and aesthetic quality (5.493 vs 5.403). Against StyleMaster on Stylization, UNIC achieves lower CSD-Score (0.259 vs 0.306) but better content preservation (ArtFID 37.619 vs 38.213) and higher aesthetic score (5.276 vs 5.121). Overall, the unified model matches or exceeds task specialists on most metrics while offering the unique advantage of flexible task composition within a single architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the in-context conditioning paradigm established in image editing (OmniGen, OminiControl, ACE) to the video domain, where prior work has relied on either DDIM inversion (Video-P2P, AnyV2V, FLATTEN) or task-specific adapter/ControlNet modules (VideoAnyDoor, StyleMaster, VACE). Building on the Wan video diffusion transformer base model, UNIC replaces all auxiliary control modules with a single concatenated token sequence processed by native full 3D attention, introducing Task-aware RoPE and Condition Bias to resolve positional conflicts and task ambiguity that arise from heterogeneous condition modalities. The approach is conceptually closest to FullDiT's full-attention multi-control video generation but pushes further into diverse editing tasks with varying condition lengths and frame correspondences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UNIC demonstrates that a single model can handle six structurally different video editing tasks (local ID edits, global stylization/propagation, re-rendering) without architectural modifications, and exhibits emergent task composition — a capability that adapter-based or DDIM-inversion methods cannot easily support. The hard-to-easy sequential training strategy and the finding that multi-task training improves human representation in camera control over single-task training offer practical guidance for multi-task video model development. Key open questions include the upper limit on unifiable tasks, the computational cost of full attention over long concatenated sequences (up to ~70M tokens for re-camera control), and whether the framework extends to audio-driven, multi-shot, or visual-effects editing without further architectural changes.
