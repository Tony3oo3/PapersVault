## Problem

RLVR training for LLM reasoning requires massive data and extensive rollout computations, leading to high costs and low data efficiency. Existing data selection methods address either offline curation (relying on single metrics like reward trends or gradient alignment that require prior training) or online rollout filtering (e.g., GRESO's zero-variance filter that treats all non-zero-variance samples equally), but no prior work integrates optimized strategies for both stages simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B) and an RLVR training dataset (e.g., DAPO-Math, ~17k samples) as input. It first extracts last-token embeddings from the final layer to construct a sample similarity graph, then produces a curated 20% training subset via PageRank-weighted DPP pruning followed by difficulty-aware normal-distribution sampling (requiring G=8 offline rollouts per sample to compute accuracy scores). During online training, it outputs a pruned rollout batch consisting of the top α_e% samples ranked by explorability plus the bottom ρ=5% by historical exploration frequency, ultimately yielding a trained LLM with improved reasoning at ~57% of full training time and 40% of rollout budget.

## Method

DEPO integrates two-stage data selection for RLVR. In the offline phase, it constructs a sample graph from last-token embeddings, applies a PageRank-weighted Determinantal Point Process (maximizing det(diag(w^{1/2})·S·diag(w^{1/2})) via greedy Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization) to retain diverse and influential samples, then performs offline rollouts on the pruned subset and samples according to a normal distribution N(μ=0.5, σ=0.2) over accuracy scores to match difficulty to model capability. In the online phase, it defines a sample-level explorability metric E = |Â_i| · e(o_i^t) · I(q,a,o_i^t) that weights rollout entropy by absolute advantage and filters out pathological high-entropy negative rollouts via threshold λ=1.5, aggregated over a sliding window of s=5 epochs. Each training batch is composed of the top α_e% samples by explorability (with α_e decaying linearly from 1.0 at rate d=0.05) plus the bottom ρ=5% by historical selection frequency (dynamic replay), ensuring under-explored samples receive sufficient training while computational resources concentrate on high-potential samples.

## Results

Using only 20% of the DAPO-Math training data, DEPO achieves an average accuracy of 61.4% (offline-only) and 61.1% (offline+online) on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, nearly matching full-dataset GRPO (61.7%) while reducing training time to 57% and rollout count to 40%. On AIME24, DEPO reaches 62.8% vs. 63.4% for full GRPO, and on AIME25 it reaches 50.9% vs. 52.7%, yielding a 1.85× speed-up on AIME24 and 1.66× on AIME25. The same pattern holds across DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B (57.3% vs. 58.2% full) and Qwen2.5-Math-7B (36.6% vs. 37.3% full), with DEPO consistently outperforming all offline and online baselines by 2–4 points in average accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEPO outperforms all five baselines across every benchmark and model. Among offline methods, LearnAlign (gradient alignment, 58.7% avg) is the strongest baseline, yet DEPO-Offline surpasses it by 2.7 points (61.4%) using only 20% of the data without requiring prior training epochs. SFT-based methods PPL-Top (57.3%) and PPL-Middle (57.5%) underperform DEPO by roughly 4 points, attributed to the mismatch between perplexity-based difficulty and RL reward optimization. LIMR (reward trends, 58.2%) also trails DEPO by 3.2 points. For online selection, GRESO (historical zero-variance filtering, 58.1% avg) improves over random online filtering (56.7%) but still lags DEPO's explorability-guided pruning (61.1%) by 3.0 points, while DEPO uses the same 40% rollout budget. Notably, DEPO with 20% data and 40% rollouts (57% total time) achieves 61.1% average, within 0.6 points of full GRPO (61.7%), demonstrating that the combined offline+online strategy recovers nearly all performance lost from data reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEPO builds on the RLVR/GRPO framework introduced by DeepSeek-R1 and extends the data-efficiency line of work that has so far addressed either offline selection (LIMR's reward trends, LearnAlign's gradient alignment, PPL-based perplexity pruning) or online selection (GRESO's zero-variance filtering) in isolation. The offline phase repurposes Determinantal Point Processes and PageRank—tools well-established in submodular optimization and web ranking—into a unified kernel for joint diversity-influence pruning, then layers a difficulty-aware normal-distribution sampler on top. The online phase introduces an explorability metric that fuses token-level entropy, normalized absolute advantage, and a pathological-trajectory filter, going beyond GRESO's binary zero/non-zero variance distinction. The key departure from prior work is the explicit integration of both stages into a single pipeline with a linearly decaying rollout budget, rather than treating offline curation and online pruning as independent problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for teams running RLVR at scale: matching full-dataset GRPO performance with 20% of the data and ~40% of rollouts translates directly into reduced GPU-hours on expensive H200 clusters. However, the offline difficulty-sampling step still requires 44+ hours of rollouts on the pruned subset, which limits the method's applicability to very large or rapidly changing datasets. Open questions include whether the explorability metric generalizes to non-binary or dense-reward settings, whether the DPP+PageRank combination is truly necessary versus simpler diversity heuristics, and how the approach behaves when the base model's capability shifts substantially mid-training (the paper's dynamic-curation experiments suggest limited gains from periodic resampling). The finding that SFT-based perplexity selectors underperform RLVR-native selectors also highlights a broader gap in transferring data-selection intuitions across training paradigms.
