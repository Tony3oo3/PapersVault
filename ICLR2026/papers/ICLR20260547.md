## Problem

Feedforward novel view synthesis methods excel at interpolative reconstruction but cannot extrapolate to occluded or unseen regions, while generative diffusion-based NVS methods require known camera poses and are limited to in-domain viewpoints. This paper addresses the gap by enabling pose-free, extrapolative novel view synthesis that simultaneously produces geometrically aligned 3D structure, without requiring dense posed inputs or per-scene optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260547/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N unposed, sparse reference RGB images (resized to 512×512) and an arbitrary target camera pose, producing a novel view image and a corresponding pointmap at that viewpoint. The target pose can lie outside the convex hull of reference poses (extrapolative setting), and the output pointmap is generated as a geometric continuation of the projected reference geometry, ensuring scale-and-shift alignment without post-hoc fitting. The jointly generated image and pointmap form an aligned colored point cloud suitable for 3D scene completion.

## Method

The framework extends warping-and-inpainting to multi-view settings using two parallel diffusion U-Nets: an image branch (initialized from Stable Diffusion 2.1) and a geometry branch (initialized from Marigold), both employing aggregated spatial cross-attention across all reference views. The key contribution is Cross-Modal Attention Instillation (MoAI), where the spatial attention maps (query and key features) from the image denoising U-Net are injected into the geometry denoising U-Net during both training and inference, creating a synergistic multi-task loop: geometry completion's deterministic structural constraints regularize image inpainting, while image semantics sharpen geometry attention. A proximity-based mesh conditioning module converts the sparse predicted point cloud into a dense mesh via ball-pivoting, augmenting the correspondence condition with depth and normal maps and applying normal-direction masking to filter erroneous projections. All pointmap coordinates are normalized into the target camera's local frame to stabilize training across diverse poses.

## Results

On the zero-shot DTU benchmark (2-view, extrapolative), MoAI achieves 15.58 PSNR / 0.615 SSIM / 0.184 LPIPS, outperforming the best feedforward baseline PixelSplat (14.66 PSNR) by +0.92 dB and the best warping-inpainting baseline LucidDreamer (11.14 PSNR, 1-view) by +4.44 dB. On in-domain RealEstate10K (2-view, extrapolative), the method reaches 17.41 PSNR / 0.614 SSIM / 0.229 LPIPS, surpassing NoPoSplat (14.36 PSNR) by +3.05 dB. In interpolative settings the method remains competitive (16.58 PSNR on DTU, 24.23 PSNR on RealEstate10K), with a slight PSNR gap to NoPoSplat on RealEstate10K (25.03) but a large LPIPS advantage (0.088 vs 0.160).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoAI outperforms all feedforward baselines (PixelSplat, MVSplat, NoPoSplat) in extrapolative settings by 1–3 dB PSNR on both DTU and RealEstate10K, while remaining competitive in interpolative settings (slightly below NoPoSplat in PSNR on RealEstate10K at 24.23 vs 25.03, but substantially better in LPIPS at 0.088 vs 0.160). Against warping-and-inpainting methods, MoAI achieves a decisive +4.4 dB PSNR over LucidDreamer and +5.7 dB over GenWarp in the 1-view DTU extrapolative setting, demonstrating that proximity-based mesh conditioning and cross-modal attention instillation effectively filter erroneous projections. Compared to large-model baselines, MoAI surpasses ViewCrafter by +1.52 dB PSNR (15.56 vs 14.04) in the 1-view DTU setting and LVSM by +0.32 dB (15.58 vs 15.23) in the 2-view setting, while running in ~9.7 s versus ViewCrafter's 209 s for 25 frames. ZeroNVS is compared qualitatively only; the paper notes it requires manual FOV/elevation specification and ~2+ hours of NeRF distillation per scene, whereas MoAI produces aligned geometry directly in a single forward pass.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the warping-and-inpainting paradigm (LucidDreamer, GenWarp) from single-image to multi-view settings and generalizes it from the image domain to geometry, formulating novel view synthesis as a joint inpainting task for both RGB and pointmaps. It adopts the aggregated multi-view attention mechanism from GenWarp and the reference-network architecture from Animate Anyone, while leveraging off-the-shelf geometry predictors (DUSt3R, MASt3R, VGGT) for pose-free correspondence. The key architectural departure is cross-modal attention instillation (MoAI), which substitutes the geometry U-Net's spatial attention maps with those from the image U-Net, creating a synergistic multi-task learning loop absent in prior single-modality or independently-trained approaches. Proximity-based mesh conditioning via ball-pivoting surface reconstruction further distinguishes the method by converting noisy sparse point clouds into dense, filtered geometric cues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint image-and-geometry synthesis with guaranteed alignment eliminates the need for separate NeRF/3DGS optimization or scale-and-shift fitting, offering a practical pipeline for 3D scene completion from unposed images in under 10 seconds. The robustness to heavily perturbed geometry (15% Gaussian noise, 80% sparsity) suggests the diffusion prior effectively absorbs errors from external predictors, making the pose-free methodology more reliable in practice. Open questions include whether the cross-modal attention instillation generalizes beyond the image-geometry pair to other modality combinations, whether the reliance on a specific external geometry predictor (VGGT) limits scalability, and how the method performs on very large scenes or with more than 4 reference views where attention cost grows quadratically.
