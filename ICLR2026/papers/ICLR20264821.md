## Problem

In federated learning with domain shift, existing prototype-based methods rely on static clustering or simple averaging to align cross-domain features, which cannot adapt to the evolving semantic separability and variance structure that emerge as training progresses. The number and arrangement of prototype clusters remain fixed, preventing the model from capturing in-class substructures or eliminating redundant clusters as the feature distribution refines over communication rounds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264821/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Clients hold RGB images from distinct domains (e.g., MNIST, SVHN, USPS for digit recognition; Amazon, Caltech, DSLR, Webcam for office objects; or medical imaging patches) and upload local prototype cluster centers computed via FINCH clustering on extracted features. The server produces an evolved global prototype topology—a variable set of cluster centers per class whose count changes across rounds—which is distributed back to clients. For segmentation tasks (Fed-Polyp, Fed-Prostate), the output is a pixel-level mask; for classification, a class label. The prototype topology itself is a structured output that changes in cardinality and geometry over training.

## Method

FedPTE treats the global prototype set as a dynamically evolvable topological structure maintained by the server. For each prototype cluster, the server formulates a Bayesian hypothesis test under a Normal-Inverse-Wishart conjugate prior: a split is triggered when the marginal likelihood ratio of a two-component model over a one-component model exceeds a threshold, and a merge is triggered when the ratio of a one-component model over a two-component model exceeds its threshold. To prevent training instability from abrupt structural changes, imbalance penalties (based on sub-cluster size ratio for splits) and KL-divergence-based semantic penalties (for merges) are subtracted from the log-evidence before the decision is made. On the client side, a prototype topology-aware contrastive loss (InfoNCE-style with cosine similarity and temperature) pulls each sample's feature toward all positive-class prototype centers while pushing it away from negative-class centers, jointly optimized with cross-entropy. The convergence analysis shows O(1/√T) rate to a stationary point, with the penalty mechanism bounding the per-round topology change δ.

## Results

On the Digit benchmark (5 clients, ResNet-10), FedPTE achieves an average accuracy of 93.98%, outperforming the strongest baseline FedPLVM (91.55%) by 2.43 points and FPL (90.10%) by 3.88 points. On the Office benchmark (4 clients, ResNet-10), FedPTE reaches 73.01% average accuracy versus FedPLVM's 67.86%. With CLIP-ViT-B-32 on Office, FedPTE attains 98.59% average accuracy (including 100% on DSLR and Webcam), surpassing MPFT (94.08%) by 4.51 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedPTE outperforms all nine baselines on both Digit and Office under the ResNet-10 setting. The closest competitor is FedPLVM (91.55% avg on Digit), which FedPTE surpasses by 2.43 points, with the largest per-domain gap on SVHN (84.93 vs 81.15) and MNIST-M (92.65 vs 90.17). FPL (90.10%) and FedDyn (87.76%) trail by 3.88 and 6.22 points respectively, while FedProto (87.52%) and FedPall (87.48%) are roughly 6.5 points behind. FedHEAL (84.63%) and Moon (83.08%) show particularly weak performance on SVHN (59.80% and 62.10% vs FedPTE's 84.93%), indicating difficulty handling the most distributionally shifted domain. FedOPT (59.45%) collapses under domain shift, scoring only 26.00% on SVHN. With CLIP on Office, FedPTE (98.59%) beats MPFT (94.08%) by 4.51 points, and on Digit with CLIP, FedPTE (77.62%) still exceeds MPFT (72.27%) by 5.35 points, confirming robustness across backbone architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedPTE extends the prototype-based federated domain adaptation line (FPL, FedProto, FedPLVM, FedHEAL, MPFT) by replacing static or heuristically-updated prototype clusters with a dynamically evolvable topology governed by Bayesian hypothesis testing. The split/merge decision mechanism draws directly on the Normal-Inverse-Wishart conjugate prior framework from Bayesian GMM literature (Diebolt & Robert, 1994; Zhao et al., 2023), repurposing marginal likelihood ratios as evidence for structural changes rather than for component count estimation in a single model. The client-side contrastive loss adapts the InfoNCE-style formulation (van den Oord et al., 2018) to align local features with the server's evolved prototype centers, adding a topology-aware positive/negative structure that prior prototype methods (which typically use a single per-class center) do not exploit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete limitation of existing prototype-based FL methods—static clustering cannot track the evolving semantic separability and variance structure that emerge as training progresses—by providing a principled, evidence-driven mechanism with a convergence guarantee of O(1/√T). The consistent 2–5% improvements over strong baselines across digit, office, and medical imaging benchmarks (including segmentation) suggest the approach generalizes beyond toy settings. However, the server-side BGMM inference adds per-round computational overhead that may become prohibitive at very large client or class counts, and the penalty hyperparameters (β_split, β_merge) still require manual tuning despite the adaptive-weight variant showing only mixed gains. Open questions include whether the Bayesian framework scales to thousands of classes, how it interacts with communication-efficient FL (e.g., quantization, sparsification), and whether the topology evolution can be made fully asynchronous.
