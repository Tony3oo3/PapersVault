## Problem

While reinforcement learning has driven major advances in LLM reasoning, the parameter dynamics during RL training remain largely unexplored. Existing interpretability work focuses on post-hoc explanations of trained models, leaving open whether RL-guided parameter updates follow consistent, predictable principles that could be exploited for efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261570/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

AlphaRL consumes a short early training window (as little as 10% of total RL steps) from which it extracts the Rank-1 singular vectors u₁ and their scaled magnitudes (α·σ₁) per module, along with the corresponding checkpoint accuracies on MATH-500. It produces a single predicted Rank-1 update matrix per module that, when added to the base model, yields accelerated weights matching near-full-training performance. The target is set to relative accuracy y* = 1, and the prediction is applied uniformly across all evaluation benchmarks without per-dataset tuning.

## Method

The paper discovers two properties via SVD of the parameter update ΔW = θ_RL − θ_base: (1) Rank-1 Dominance, where retaining only the top singular component (rescaled to match the full update's L2 norm) recovers >99% of reasoning gains, and (2) Rank-1 Linear Dynamics, where the Rank-1 subspace direction evolves with R² > 0.91 linearity across training steps. AlphaRL exploits both by fitting a single-component PLS regression between the scaled Rank-1 trajectory vectors and checkpoint accuracies over the early window, then inverting this linear map at the target accuracy to obtain the predicted final update vector per module. This vector is combined with the right singular vector v₁ to reconstruct the full Rank-1 update, which is injected into the base model—bypassing the remaining RL steps entirely. The approach requires no extra modules, hyperparameter tuning, or architectural modifications, and is orthogonal to existing acceleration techniques.

## Results

AlphaRL achieves up to 2.5× training speedup while retaining >96% of final reasoning performance. On Qwen3-8B-Base with DAPO, Training 40%+AlphaRL reaches an average of 53.31 across six benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, MATH-500, Minerva, GPQA, GSM8K), matching the fully trained model's 53.38. The Rank-1 Subspace alone recovers an average of 99.17% of RL-induced reasoning gains across 13 LLMs and 10 training algorithms, and its evolution exhibits an average R² of 0.914 linearity, enabling accurate prediction from early checkpoints with <5% error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between RL training (DAPO, GRPO, RLOO) and non-RL paradigms (SFT, DIST, DPO, On-Policy Distillation) on the Rank-1 dominance property. RL methods achieve 100% relative accuracy with only the Rank-1 Subspace on MATH-500, while SFT recovers only 2.50% and offline DIST only 1.50% (Table 8), demonstrating that Rank-1 dominance is unique to RL. In terms of update magnitude, RL (DAPO) has an average L2 norm of 0.01 versus 10.75 for SFT and 21.24 for offline DIST (Table 7), yet RL's Rank-1 fraction of the update norm is 19.3% compared to 7.7% (SFT) and 12.6% (DIST), confirming far greater concentration. DPO and On-Policy Distillation show intermediate low-rank structure (Rank-1 fractions of 11.42% and 10.45% respectively) but still fall well short of RL's 100% Rank-1 recovery. For AlphaRL acceleration specifically, the method outperforms the non-accelerated 40%-trained model by 7.01 points (DAPO: 53.31 vs 46.30) and 4.12 points (GRPO: 49.42 vs 45.30) in average accuracy, while the fully trained model gains only 0.07 and 2.10 points over the 40%+AlphaRL variants respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant post-hoc interpretability paradigm for RL-trained LLMs (neuron attribution, circuit analysis, sparse autoencoders) by instead analyzing the parameter update matrix ΔW through SVD across the full training trajectory. It repurposes classical linear algebra tools (SVD, PLS regression) in a novel context: characterizing the low-rank structure of RL-induced updates and exploiting its linear temporal dynamics for prediction. The AlphaRL acceleration method is positioned as orthogonal to and multiplicatively compatible with existing RL algorithms (GRPO, DAPO, RLOO) and acceleration paradigms, requiring no architectural modifications. Control experiments against SFT and distillation establish that the discovered properties are specific to the RL training process rather than a generic artifact of fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single Rank-1 direction captures ~99% of RL reasoning gains and evolves linearly (R² > 0.96) provides a surprisingly simple mechanistic account of how complex multi-step RL optimization produces reasoning capabilities, potentially bridging the gap between empirical scaling observations and theoretical understanding. Practically, AlphaRL's 2.5× speedup with >96% performance retention is directly applicable to large-scale RL training where compute is the bottleneck. Key limitations include the absence of rigorous theoretical grounding (findings are empirical), dependence on the stability and design of the underlying RL algorithm, and untested applicability to multimodal or very large-scale agent settings. Open questions include whether nonlinear forecasting could further improve prediction accuracy, and whether the Rank-1 structure can be exploited for training monitoring or reward signal design.
