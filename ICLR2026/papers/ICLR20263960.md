## Problem

DPO's performance is highly sensitive to reference-policy misalignment: when the SFT reference model conflicts with human preferences on ambiguous pairs, those pairs dominate the gradient signal and degrade generalization. Existing remedies either discard the reference entirely (risking catastrophic forgetting) or apply static data filtering that does not account for the evolving model state. The paper asks whether a lightweight, sample-aware modification to DPO can selectively suppress unreliable preference signals while retaining the reference's useful guidance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard DPO preference triplets (prompt x, preferred response y_w, dispreferred response y_l) together with a frozen reference policy π_ref (the SFT model). At each mini-batch step it computes two per-sample quantities — the reference alignment ratio π_ref(y_w|x)/π_ref(y_l|x) and the instantaneous DPO loss — and uses them to dynamically exclude a fixed number q of the most ambiguous unaligned samples from the gradient update. The output is the updated policy π_θ, which generates aligned text responses; no new data format or architectural change is required, and the modification integrates into any existing DPO-type trainer with a few lines of code.

## Method

RAPPO (Reliable Alignment for Preference Policy Optimization) is an order-aware variant of DPO inspired by the selective-update principle of Ordered-SGD. At each step it (1) partitions the mini-batch into Aligned and Unaligned subsets using a threshold τ on the reference alignment ratio, (2) sorts the Unaligned subset by per-sample DPO loss in ascending order, and (3) discards the top-q highest-loss unaligned samples before computing the gradient, so the update is taken over the remaining s−q samples. The key insight is that high-loss unaligned samples correspond to cases where the reference policy most strongly conflicts with the human label, and removing them reduces gradient variance and tightens the stability-based generalization bound. The paper proves that among all rules keeping K_t untrusted items, selecting those with the largest DPO margin (smallest loss) maximizes the expected first-order risk decrease, minimizes conditional variance of the signed decrease, and yields the tightest uniform-stability generalization gap. Empirically, RAPPO improves reward scores by up to 7.7% on IMDb, reduces toxicity to 2.28% (vs. 6.30% for the best baseline), and achieves 65% GPT-4 win rate on PKU-SafeRLHF, with only ~25% wall-clock overhead over standard DPO.

## Results

On the IMDb sentiment control task, RAPPO-4 achieves a reward score of 1.6811, surpassing the strongest baseline SIMPO (1.5537) by 7.7% and DPO (1.5513) by 8.4%. On the Real-Toxicity-Prompts toxicity control task, RAPPO-4 reduces toxicity to 2.28%, compared to 6.30% for DPO and 7.49% for SIMPO. On the PKU-SafeRLHF benchmark, RAPPO attains the best safety rate (0.573), helpfulness (0.693, +34.8% over DPO), and harmlessness (0.357, −21.0% vs. DPO), with a 65% GPT-4 win rate over all baselines. On summarization, RAPPO achieves GPT-4 win rates of 58.8% (GPT-J-6B) and 74.5% (Llama-3.1-8B) over SIMPO and DPO respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAPPO consistently outperforms all six baselines across every evaluated task. On IMDb, RAPPO-4 (1.6811) beats SIMPO (1.5537, the strongest baseline) by 7.7%, DPO (1.5513) by 8.4%, DPO-Offset (1.5526) by 8.3%, and IPO (1.5446) by 8.8% in reward score. On toxicity control, RAPPO-4 (2.28%) reduces toxicity by 64% relative to DPO (6.30%) and by 70% relative to SIMPO (7.49%), while DPO-Offset actually worsens toxicity to 8.11%. On the PKU-SafeRLHF benchmark, RAPPO surpasses DPO, CPO, KTO, and SIMPO on all four metrics (safety rate, helpfulness, harmlessness, and GPT-4 win rate), with the largest gap being +34.8% helpfulness over DPO. On summarization, RAPPO wins 58.8% of GPT-4 pairwise comparisons against SIMPO on GPT-J-6B and 74.5% against DPO on Llama-3.1-8B; a multi-judge evaluation (GPT-4, GPT-4o, DeepSeek-V3, Claude Opus 4.1) confirms RAPPO's advantage with an average 64.25% win rate over SIMPO and inter-judge Kendall's τ of 0.65.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAPPO sits at the intersection of two research threads: DPO-style preference optimization (Rafailov et al., 2023) and the selective-update principle from Ordered-SGD (Kawaguchi & Lu, 2020), which orders samples by loss to amplify informative signal. Unlike prior data-selection methods (RSO, Selective DPO) that filter the dataset before training, or reference-free approaches (SIMPO, ORPO) that discard the reference policy entirely, RAPPO retains the reference and uses it dynamically as a per-batch gate: samples where the reference is misaligned with human preference are identified, and only the most ambiguous (highest-loss) among them are temporarily excluded from each update. This makes RAPPO a lightweight, model-adaptive curriculum that is orthogonal to and composable with existing DPO variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and well-motivated failure mode of DPO—sensitivity to ambiguous samples under a weak or misaligned reference—without requiring architectural changes or additional training stages. The theoretical guarantee (tighter stability-based generalization bound) and the empirical consistency across four tasks, multiple model scales (GPT-2 to Llama-3.1-8B), and four independent LLM judges lend credibility to the approach. Key limitations include evaluation only on models ≤8B parameters and a fixed hyperparameter q rather than an adaptive selection rule, both flagged as future work. The simplicity of the modification (a few lines of code) makes it immediately adoptable, but whether the gains persist at frontier-model scale or with more complex multi-turn preference data remains open.
