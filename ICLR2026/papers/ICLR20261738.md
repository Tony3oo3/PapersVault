## Problem

Existing preference optimization methods (DPO, SimPO, ORPO, KTO) rely heavily on large volumes of paired human preference labels, which are prohibitively expensive to acquire at $10–30 per data point. While vast pools of unpaired SFT data (e.g., UltraChat) contain implicit preference signals, they are typically discarded because they lack explicit winning/losing comparisons. This paper addresses how to jointly learn from a small number of paired labels and a large pool of unpaired responses in a principled, theoretically grounded manner.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two data sources: a small set of paired preference triplets (prompt, winning response, losing response) as small as 1% of UltraFeedback (~611 pairs), and a large pool of unpaired single prompt-response pairs (e.g., 10% of UltraChat-200k, ~20,786 samples) that require no comparison structure. The output is a LoRA-fine-tuned policy model (Phi-2, Mistral-7B, or Llama3-8B) that is evaluated on AlpacaEval2.0 (length-controlled and raw win rates) and MT-Bench. Notably, the unpaired data consists of individual responses rather than pairs, so the method must assign a binary pseudo-label to each single response relative to a learned threshold rather than comparing two responses directly.

## Method

SSPO reformulates preference optimization as a Bayes-optimal binary classification problem over pairwise comparisons, using SimPO's reference-free reward r_θ(x,y) = (β/|y|) log π_θ(y|x). The key theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) proves that under sub-Gaussian reward distributions, an optimal threshold δ* exists that separates winning and losing reward values with probability ≥ 1−α. In practice, the threshold is estimated via kernel density estimation on the paired data's reward distributions, minimizing the empirical Bayes risk, and stabilized with exponential moving averages during training. Unpaired responses are pseudo-labeled as winning or losing based on whether their normalized reward exceeds this threshold. The training objective jointly optimizes the paired preference loss and the pseudo-labeled unpaired classification loss, with an adaptive exponential-decay scheduler (curriculum learning) that starts focused on the reliable paired data and gradually shifts weight toward the pseudo-labeled unpaired data, all in a single training stage without iterative generation loops.

## Results

On UltraFeedback with Mistral-7B, SSPO trained on only 1% of paired data achieves a length-controlled win rate (LC) of 26.7% and raw win rate (WR) of 18.1% on AlpacaEval2.0, surpassing the strongest baseline SPA (18.2% LC) trained on the same 1% and even all baselines trained on 10% (best: SPA at 19.1% LC). At 10% paired data, SSPO reaches 30.0% LC and 20.7% WR, extending the gap to +10.9pp over SPA. On domain-specific datasets (UltraMedical-Preference, DSP Business), SSPO with Llama3-8B similarly outperforms all baselines regardless of data size, e.g., 17.7% LC vs. 15.7% (SPA) on UltraMedical at 10%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSPO outperforms all six baselines (DPO, ORPO, SimPO, SSRM, KTO, SPA) across every backbone, data size, and domain. The gap is most dramatic in the data-scarce 1% setting: with Mistral-7B on UltraFeedback, SSPO achieves 26.7% LC versus SPA's 18.2% (+8.5pp) and KTO's 16.4% (+10.3pp), and even exceeds SPA trained on 10% (19.1% LC) by 7.6pp. Among baselines, SPA is consistently the strongest, followed by KTO and DPO; ORPO and SimPO tend to underperform, particularly at 1% where SimPO drops to 13.2% LC on Mistral. On the medical domain (UltraMedical-Preference), SSPO with Llama3 reaches 17.7% LC at 10% vs. SPA's 15.7% and SSRM's 12.9%. On DSP Business, SSPO with Mistral achieves 17.9% LC at 10% vs. KTO's 16.7% and SPA's 16.0%. MT-Bench scores remain relatively flat (7.5–8.0) across all methods, indicating that SSPO's gains are concentrated in single-turn preference quality rather than multi-turn coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

SSPO builds directly on SimPO's reference-free reward function and DPO's Bradley-Terry classification framing, reformulating preference optimization as a Bayes-optimal binary classification problem. It departs from iterative semi-supervised methods like SSRM and SPA, which require multiple rounds of inference, generation, and re-training, by instead using a single-stage training loop with a theoretically grounded reward threshold (via KDE) to pseudo-label unpaired SFT data. The key conceptual departure is treating unpaired single responses as pseudo-labeled classification targets rather than positive demonstrations (as in SFT-augmented baselines) or requiring pre-existing response pairs (as in SSRM).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× data efficiency result (1% labeled pairs outperforming 10% baselines) is practically significant for domains where expert annotation is prohibitively expensive, and the method's simplicity (KDE threshold + adaptive scheduler) makes it easy to integrate into existing PO pipelines. However, the sub-Gaussian assumption underlying Theorem 1 may not hold for all reward distributions in practice, and the flat MT-Bench scores across methods suggest gains are concentrated in win-rate metrics rather than multi-turn reasoning quality. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger unpaired corpora, how sensitive it is to distributional mismatch between paired and unpaired data, and whether the threshold-based pseudo-labeling degrades when the reward model is poorly calibrated.
