## Problem

Bridging the formal precision of Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) specifications with the contextual richness of natural language remains a major bottleneck for reliable engineering and AI safety. Existing datasets linking LTL and NL are small-scale (~2–5k examples), confined to single niche domains (robotics, navigation), or translate logic into overly technical, template-like forms rather than context-grounded natural language, leaving no large-scale resource for training generalizable logic-to-language models.

## Input / Output

Each VERIFY record is a triplet of (LTL formula in Spot-canonical form, canonical ITL string, contextual NL description) paired with a domain label (one of 13 areas) and an activity field that defines the propositional variables (p–w) in domain-specific terms. The benchmark tasks cover all six translation directions among the three representations (LTL↔ITL, LTL→NL, ITL→NL, NL→LTL, NL→ITL), with the NL→LTL and NL→ITL tasks additionally consuming the domain and activity context as input to ground the logical recovery.

## Method

The dataset is constructed through a four-stage pipeline: (1) a recursive LTL enumerator generates formulas up to depth 25, which are canonicalized to NNF and verified for satisfiability and non-triviality via the Spot model checker; (2) a deterministic AST-visitor maps each verified LTL formula to a canonical ITL string using a provably complete formal grammar (proven by structural induction in Appendix C), where each LTL operator is replaced by a controlled English keyword template; (3) DeepSeek-R1 generates domain-specific NL descriptions conditioned on the LTL/ITL pair, the target domain, and variable semantics; (4) quality is assured through manual expert review of 10,000 samples and Llama 3.3 70B LLM-as-judge evaluation on 18% of the data, confirming >97% semantic correctness. For benchmarking, T5, BART, Llama 3, Mistral, CodeLlama, and DeepSeek Coder are fine-tuned on 80/10/10 stratified splits, evaluated with BERTScore, ROUGE-L, Spot-based semantic equivalence, exact match, and tree edit distance.

## Results

On the most challenging NL→LTL task, the best model (DeepSeek-Coder-6.7B fine-tuned) achieves only 31.5% Semantic Equivalence, 5.4% Exact Match, and 74.2% Syntactic Correctness, confirming that precise logical form recovery from natural language remains far from solved. For the generation direction (LTL→NL), fine-tuned LLMs substantially outperform encoder-decoder models: Llama-3-8B-Instruct reaches BERTScore F1 of 0.91 and ROUGE-L F1 of 0.67, versus 0.67/0.41 for T5-large. The ITL intermediate representation consistently improves NL generation quality (e.g., T5-large BERTScore jumps from 0.67 to 0.89 when translating from ITL instead of LTL). Error analysis of 100 incorrect NL→LTL predictions reveals that 41% of failures stem from incorrect operator scope and 28% from temporal operator confusion (e.g., U vs. W).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All six baselines are evaluated on the same VERIFY test split, so comparisons are purely architectural. DeepSeek-Coder (code-focused LLM) leads on NL→LTL Semantic Equivalence at 31.5%, outperforming Llama-3-8B (28.2%), Mistral-7B (25.6%), CodeLlama-7B (25.4%), and T5-large (22.3%) by 3.3–9.2 percentage points. On NL→ITL Exact Match, Llama-3-8B edges out DeepSeek-Coder (4.3% vs. 4.1%), while T5-large trails all LLMs at 2.2%. For the generation direction (Table 2), Llama-3-8B achieves the highest BERTScore F1 on both LTL→NL (0.91) and ITL→NL (0.94), surpassing T5-large (0.67/0.89) by 0.24 and 0.05 respectively, and BART-large (0.63/0.78) by 0.28 and 0.16. Code-focused models (CodeLlama, DeepSeek-Coder) show a consistent but modest advantage in Syntactic Correctness for logic-generation tasks (74–79% vs. 66–74% for general LLMs), suggesting code pre-training helps with formal grammar adherence. No model exceeds 32% Semantic Equivalence on NL→LTL, indicating a substantial gap that the VERIFY dataset is designed to help close.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LTL-to-NL translation line of work (Cherukuri et al. 2022, Pan et al. 2023, NL2TL/Chen et al. 2023, Lang2LTL/Liu et al. 2023) by addressing three simultaneous gaps: scale (prior datasets are 2k–28k examples), domain diversity (prior work is confined to robotics, navigation, or hardware verification), and contextual richness (prior NL is often imperative commands or lifted templates rather than domain-grounded descriptions). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of ITL, a provably complete intermediate representation with a deterministic AST-driven mapping from LTL, which no prior dataset provides. The construction pipeline also departs from purely template-based or purely LLM-based generation by combining Spot model checking, formal grammar-based ITL generation, and LLM-driven contextual NL synthesis with multi-stage validation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a concrete gap for researchers needing a large, multi-domain, formally verified resource for logic-to-language translation, and the ITL layer offers a general strategy for bridging formal and informal representations beyond LTL. However, the NL→LTL direction remains severely underperforming (best model: 31.5% semantic equivalence), with error analysis pointing to operator-scope and temporal-operator confusion that current autoregressive LLMs struggle with—suggesting structured decoding or neurosymbolic architectures are needed. Open questions include whether LLM-generated NL adequately captures the ambiguity and variation of real human-written requirements, whether the ITL benefit generalizes to other logics (STL, CTL), and whether the 13-domain coverage is sufficient for true cross-domain generalization. The reliance on LLMs for both generation and validation introduces a correlated-error risk the authors acknowledge but cannot fully eliminate.
