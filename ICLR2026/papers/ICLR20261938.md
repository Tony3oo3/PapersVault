## Problem

Open-source Diffusion LLMs (e.g., LLaDA, Dream) suffer from two critical inference bottlenecks: they lack Key-Value (KV) Cache support due to their bidirectional attention, and parallel decoding of multiple tokens simultaneously degrades generation quality by violating token dependencies under the conditional independence assumption. As a result, their practical throughput often falls below that of autoregressive models, undermining the core promise of diffusion-based parallel generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261938/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (or an image plus text prompt for the multimodal LLaDA-V variant) and produces a fully unmasked text sequence of configurable length (up to 1024 tokens). Generation is structured in fixed-size blocks (default 32 tokens), where within each block the model iteratively unmask tokens over T diffusion steps. For the multimodal setting, the input image is a standard RGB photograph and the output is a free-form text description; the block size must be kept large (96) for LLaDA-V to avoid accuracy loss.

## Method

Fast-dLLM introduces two complementary, training-free acceleration mechanisms. First, a block-wise approximate KV Cache exploits the observation that KV activations are highly similar across adjacent diffusion steps within a block: before generating a block, KV entries for all other blocks are computed and cached, then reused across the block's internal steps and refreshed only after the block completes; a DualCache variant additionally caches suffix tokens for further reuse. Second, a confidence-aware parallel decoding strategy selects which masked tokens to unmask at each step by thresholding per-token confidence (max softmax probability) rather than using a fixed top-K, with a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) showing that when each token's confidence exceeds 1−ε and (n+1)ε ≤ 1, greedy parallel decoding via the product of marginals is exactly equivalent to greedy sequential decoding under the true joint distribution. A factor-based variant further adapts the number of parallel tokens per step by solving (n+1)(1−c⁽ⁿ⁾) < f, dynamically balancing speed and quality.

## Results

Fast-dLLM achieves up to 27.6× end-to-end throughput improvement over vanilla LLaDA (8-shot, generation length 1024, DualCache) with only ~1.3% accuracy drop on GSM8K (76.0% vs. 77.3%). On the standard 5-shot/512-token setting, the combined KV Cache + Confidence-Aware Parallel Decoding yields 8.1× speedup on GSM8K (54.4 tok/s vs. 6.7 tok/s) at 77.2% accuracy (vs. 77.5% baseline), and 7.5× on MBPP (44.8 tok/s) at 28.2% (vs. 29.4%). On Dream-Base, maximum gains reach 7.8× (MBPP, 512 tokens) and 6.5× (MATH, 512 tokens) with ≤1.6% accuracy loss. On multimodal LLaDA-V, Fast-dLLM achieves 9.9× speedup on MathVista (28.2 tok/s, 56.6% acc) and 8.5× on MathVerse (23.3 tok/s, 28.6% acc).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against dLLM-Cache (Liu et al., 2025), Fast-dLLM achieves 54.4 tok/s vs. 18.0 tok/s on GSM8K at 256 tokens (3× higher throughput) while maintaining comparable accuracy (78.5% vs. 79.2%). Against DPad (Chen et al., 2025), Fast-dLLM reaches 54.4 tok/s vs. 7.3 tok/s at 256 tokens (~7.5× higher throughput) with slightly lower accuracy (78.5% vs. 79.1%); the paper notes these methods are orthogonal and can be combined for multiplicative gains (DPad+Parallel+PrefixCache reaches 54.0 tok/s). Against dParallel (Chen et al., 2025), which uses learned distillation to reduce decoding steps, Fast-dLLM's KV cache integration on top of dParallel further boosts throughput from 49.9 to 92.0 tok/s (1.84× additional gain) with a slight accuracy improvement (76.5% → 77.1%). Against LLaMA 3 (Grattafiori et al., 2024), an autoregressive model, PrefixCache-equipped LLaDA reaches or exceeds LLaMA throughput at small batch sizes (batch ≤ 8) but falls behind at larger batches due to diffusion models' inherent compute-bound nature from full attention during decoding.

## Relation to Prior Work

Fast-dLLM builds directly on the LLaDA and Dream masked diffusion LLM architectures and adopts the block-wise generation paradigm from Block Diffusion (Arriola et al., 2025) to make KV cache reuse feasible under bidirectional attention. It departs from prior diffusion-LLM caching methods (dLLM-Cache, DPad, Sparse-dLLM) by exploiting temporal similarity of KV activations across adjacent diffusion steps rather than content-based or sparsity-based eviction, and introduces a DualCache variant that also caches suffix tokens. The confidence-aware parallel decoding extends LLaDA's top-K token selection with a threshold/factor strategy grounded in a formal theorem characterizing when product-of-marginals decoding is equivalent to true joint decoding, a theoretical treatment absent from prior parallel-decoding work for diffusion LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving up to 27.6× end-to-end speedup with ≤2-point accuracy loss across math, code, and instruction-following benchmarks, Fast-dLLM makes open-source diffusion LLMs practically competitive with autoregressive models for the first time, removing a key barrier to their deployment. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) provides a principled, worst-case condition for safe parallel decoding that could guide future decoding strategies beyond the specific threshold/factor heuristics used here. Limitations include sensitivity to block size in multimodal settings (LLaDA-V loses >8% accuracy at small blocks), the fact that diffusion models remain compute-bound and fall behind AR models at large batch sizes, and the approximation error that grows with step distance—motivating layer-aware or adaptive caching as future work.
