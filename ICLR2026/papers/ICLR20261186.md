## Problem

Crowdsourced Arena platforms provide reliable human-preference evaluations for visual generative models but are costly, slow, and inherently limited in scalability. While VLMs offer a promising automated substitute, their inherent hallucinations and biases undermine alignment with true human preferences, and existing VLM-based evaluation methods rely on static full-dataset traversal, making them inefficient.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261186/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a new text-to-image or text-to-video model and a curated dataset of 500 image / 300 video instances, each containing K=4 model outputs (512×512 images or 512×512, 8 FPS, 5 s videos) with human-voted rankings. The VLM (GPT-4o for images, Qwen-VL-Max for videos) receives K+1=5 shuffled outputs per instance to eliminate positional bias. The output is a conservative score S = μ − 3σ, a leaderboard rank, and the posterior mean/variance of model capability, with evaluation stopping once σ drops below 0.75—typically in fewer than 90 model runs.

## Method

K-Sort Eval integrates two key mechanisms on top of K-Sort Arena's Bayesian K-wise comparison framework. First, a posterior correction method models VLM misalignment as observation noise: the noise-aware likelihood P̃(D|θ) = λP(D|θ) + (1−λ)P_n(D) yields a noisy posterior that is a weighted mixture of the noise-free posterior and the prior (Lemma 1), where the weight λ′ = sigmoid(κρ′) is computed from the Spearman rank correlation between the VLM's prediction and the human-voted supervision in the dataset. Second, a dynamic matching strategy selects the next dataset instance by jointly maximizing an uncertainty criterion (promoting matchups between comparable-strength models to maximize information gain) and a diversity criterion (minimizing overlap of Gaussian capability distributions among the K opponents), avoiding redundant comparisons. The dataset is curated by filtering K-Sort Arena votes via Spearman correlation with the global leaderboard (τ=0.75) and Llama Guard for harmful content, and the VLM is guided by swapping (random shuffling) and rule augmentation prompts aligned with human voting criteria.

## Results

K-Sort Eval achieves near-perfect alignment with K-Sort Arena (human-voted): all evaluated models receive identical rankings, with score deviations of only 0.03–0.15 (e.g., FLUX.1-dev: 28.86 vs. 28.83; CogVideoX-5b: 33.63 vs. 33.60). In terms of efficiency, 91% of image model evaluations and 93% of video model evaluations complete in fewer than 90 model runs (VLM calls), compared to ~50,000 runs for FID or 1,600 runs for GenAI-Bench. In the preference-scoring comparison (Figure 4), GPT-4o with posterior correction achieves the highest Spearman correlation with actual human preferences, outperforming all CLIP-based scorers (ImageReward, PickScore, HPS, VQAScore).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against K-Sort Arena (the human-voted ground truth), K-Sort Eval matches all rankings exactly and reproduces scores within 0.03–0.15, confirming that the VLM-based pipeline faithfully approximates human preferences. In the preference-scoring comparison (Figure 4), GPT-4o with posterior correction achieves the highest correlation with human votes, consistently outperforming all four CLIP-based scorers (ImageReward, PickScore, HPS, VQAScore), which show substantially lower correlation. The correction step (λ′ weighting) further improves GPT-4o's correlation by reducing the influence of noisy VLM observations. On efficiency, K-Sort Eval requires fewer than 90 runs in the vast majority of cases, compared to 50,000 runs for FID and 1,600 runs for GenAI-Bench, representing a 17×–556× reduction in model invocations. The ablation confirms that removing posterior correction degrades accuracy (e.g., FLUX.1-dev rank shifts from 5 to 3, score deviates by 0.49), while removing dynamic matching increases runs from 81 to 500 (full dataset traversal).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on K-Sort Arena (Li et al., 2025b), which introduced K-wise free-for-all human preference comparisons with Bayesian probabilistic modeling for visual generative models, and extends it by replacing human votes with VLM judgements. It draws on the VLM-as-a-Judge paradigm (Chen et al., 2024; Liu & Zhang, 2025) and adapts LLM-as-a-Judge mitigation strategies (swapping from Zheng et al., 2023; rule augmentation from Bai et al., 2022), but departs from prior VLM evaluation work (VIEScore, T2I-CompBench, VideoScore) by introducing a principled Bayesian posterior correction that treats VLM-human misalignment as structured observation noise rather than attempting to eliminate it through prompting alone. The dynamic matching strategy also distinguishes it from static evaluation methods that require full dataset traversal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

K-Sort Eval addresses a critical scalability bottleneck: Arena-based evaluation is the gold standard for human-aligned assessment but is prohibitively expensive and slow for rapid model iteration. The <90-run evaluation cost (vs. 50,000 for FID or 1,600 for GenAI-Bench) makes preference-based evaluation feasible for frequent model updates, and the public dataset and code lower the barrier for the community. However, the method is tightly coupled to the K-Sort Arena ecosystem and its curated dataset (800 instances), raising open questions about generalization to new domains, evolving model landscapes, and whether the Bayesian correction framework transfers to other VLM judges or evaluation criteria. The reliance on proprietary VLMs (GPT-4o, Qwen-VL-Max) as judges also introduces cost and availability constraints that partially offset the efficiency gains.
