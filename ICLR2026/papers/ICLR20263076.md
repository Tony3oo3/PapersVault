## Problem

Directly applying reinforcement learning to Image Aesthetic Assessment (IAA) fails to activate aesthetic reasoning patterns in multimodal LLMs without prior aesthetic knowledge, and existing reward designs are incomplete: rank-based rewards calibrate ordering but not absolute scores, while scalar regression rewards calibrate scores but ignore pairwise preference. Additionally, high-quality aesthetic reasoning data is scarce because artist-level rationales require expensive expert annotation, and extensive SFT causes entropy collapse that hinders subsequent RL optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263076/cropped_page0_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image paired with a fixed aesthetic scoring prompt and produces a structured two-part output: a chain-of-thought critique covering five aesthetic dimensions (light and shadow, mood and narrative, composition, color, exposure) enclosed in  tags, followed by a single float score in [0, 1] (rounded to two decimals) inside <answer> tags. Training uses a combined 15K-sample set drawn from AVA, TAD66K, and FLICKR-AES in a 2:2:1 ratio, with MOS values normalized to [0, 1].

## Method

Aes-R1 is a two-stage framework. First, AesCoT is an automatic data pipeline that uses GPT-4.1 to generate multi-dimensional aesthetic critiques from image-score pairs (with the score hidden from the model), then filters outputs for score leakage, reasoning-score inconsistency, and factual hallucinations via automated checks and human audits, yielding AesCoT-3K/10K. Second, RAPO (Relative-Absolute Policy Optimization) is a novel RL algorithm built on GRPO-style group sampling (K=4 outputs per prompt) that jointly optimizes two reward components: a relative rank reward based on FRank that computes pairwise comparison probabilities under a Gaussian score-difference model, and an absolute error reward using a Gaussian kernel on the predicted-vs-ground-truth score gap. The model is cold-started with 1 epoch of SFT on AesCoT to induce aesthetic reasoning patterns, then fine-tuned with RAPO using DAPO-style clipping and reduced KL penalty. The key insight is that human aesthetic judgment is inherently dual-axis—comparative (which image is better) and absolute (how good is this image)—so the reward must enforce both ordinal consistency and score calibration simultaneously.

## Results

Aes-R1 achieves an average PLCC of 0.6337 and SRCC of 0.6186 across five IAA benchmarks (TAD66K, AVA, FLICKR-AES, PARA, AADB), representing a 47.9% and 34.8% improvement over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone (0.4285/0.4589). On the in-domain AVA test set, Aes-R1 reaches PLCC 0.6702 and SRCC 0.6619, outperforming the strongest MLLM baseline Q-Insight (0.5964/0.5898) by 7.4/7.2 points. The method also demonstrates strong OOD generalization, achieving the highest average SRCC (0.6079) among AVA-only-trained models, matching ArtiMuse which was pretrained on a large-scale human-annotated corpus.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Aes-R1 outperforms all 15 baselines on average PLCC and SRCC. Against the strongest MLLM-based competitor Q-Insight (0.5954/0.5813), Aes-R1 gains +3.8/+3.7 points, with the largest margin on TAD66K (+5.3/+3.6 PLCC/SRCC). VisualQuality-R1, which uses only rank reward, achieves competitive SRCC (0.5930) but suffers severely on PLCC (0.4429), confirming that rank-only optimization fails to calibrate absolute scores; Aes-R1's combined relative-absolute reward fixes this gap. Among vanilla MLLMs, GPT4.1 is the strongest (0.5171/0.5491), yet Aes-R1 still leads by +11.7/+7.0 points, demonstrating that task-specific RL training with aesthetic reasoning data substantially exceeds general-purpose model capabilities. On OOD datasets (PARA, AADB), Aes-R1 maintains its lead over all baselines, and in the AVA-only training setting (Table 5), it achieves the highest average SRCC (0.6079), matching ArtiMuse (0.6073) which was pretrained on a large-scale human-annotated corpus, while outperforming Q-Align (0.6035) and VILA (0.5748). Hand-crafted methods (NIQE, BRISQUE) and the deep-learning NIMA baseline are far behind, with average PLCC below 0.50, underscoring the advantage of MLLM-based approaches for aesthetic assessment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-for-image-assessment line initiated by VisualQuality-R1 and Q-Insight from image quality to aesthetic assessment, but departs from their single-objective reward designs (rank-only or scalar-error-only) by introducing a joint relative-absolute reward (RAPO). The RL framework is built on GRPO's group-based advantage estimation and DAPO's relaxed clipping, while the rank reward component directly adapts FRank's fidelity-loss formulation to pairwise aesthetic comparisons. The two-stage SFT-then-RL pipeline follows the DeepSeek-R1 cold-start paradigm, and the AesCoT data pipeline repurposes GPT-4.1 as an expert annotator to generate chain-of-thought aesthetic critiques, addressing the scarcity of reasoning-annotated aesthetic data that has limited prior MLLM-based IAA to score-only supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that RL with a carefully designed dual reward can achieve competitive aesthetic assessment from only 15K training samples, offering a data-efficient alternative to the large-scale SFT pipelines (e.g., ArtiMuse) that dominate the field. The entropy analysis revealing that excessive SFT degrades downstream RL performance provides a practical guideline for the SFT/RL allocation problem that is broadly relevant to post-training MLLMs. Open questions include whether the relative-absolute reward decomposition generalizes to other subjective evaluation tasks (e.g., video aesthetics, creative design scoring), whether the AesCoT pipeline scales to new aesthetic domains without re-annotation, and whether the 1-epoch cold-start finding holds for larger backbones or more diverse training distributions.
