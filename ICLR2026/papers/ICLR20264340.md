## Problem

The EDLAE recommender model (Steck, 2020) provides closed-form solutions only for the special case b=0 in its emphasis weighting scheme, leaving the broader hyperparameter range b>0 theoretically unexplored. It is unclear whether better-performing models exist in this unexplored region, and even if they do, direct computation of the b>0 solutions incurs O(n⁴) complexity that is impractical for large-scale recommendation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264340/cropped_page14_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a binary user-item interaction matrix R ∈ {0,1}^{m×n} along with hyperparameters (dropout rate p, emphasis weights a and b, and optionally an L2 coefficient λ) and produces a full n×n item-to-item similarity matrix W computed entirely via closed-form solution. Because the objective decouples column-wise, each column W_{*i} is solved independently, and the solution is deterministic for fixed hyperparameters—no iterative optimization or early stopping is involved. Predictions for missing entries are obtained as RW, where larger values indicate higher interaction likelihood.

## Method

The paper reformulates the EDLAE training objective as a Decoupled Expected Quadratic Loss (DEQL), which expresses the loss as a sum of per-column expected quadratic terms E[‖Y^{(i)} − X^{(i)}W_{*i}‖²_F], enabling independent column-wise optimization. This generalization yields closed-form minimizers for all b≥0: for b>0 the solution W_{*i} = H^{(i)−1}v^{(i)} is unique and always exists (including the previously unexplored regime b>a), while for b=0 the solution is non-unique with arbitrary diagonal entries (Steck's zero-diagonal solution is one instance). To make the b>0 computation tractable, the authors decompose H^{(i)} = H₀ + E₁^{(i)} + E₂^{(i)} where H₀ is i-independent and E₁, E₂ are rank-1, then apply Miller's matrix inverse theorem to compute all n column solutions in O(n³) total time rather than O(n⁴). Empirically, DEQL(L2) with b>0 consistently outperforms the b=0 EDLAE baseline, and on sparse datasets with high item-user ratios (Amazon-Books, Yelp2018), the optimal b/a ratio exceeds 1, contradicting the original a≥b assumption.

## Results

Under the weak generalization setting, DEQL(L2) achieves the best performance on Amazon-Books with R@20=0.0751 and N@20=0.0613, outperforming the strongest deep learning baseline SSM(GNN) (0.0590/0.0459) by 27% and 34% respectively. On Yelp2018, DEQL(L2) reaches R@20=0.0685 and N@20=0.0576, surpassing all LAE and most deep learning baselines. Under the strong generalization setting, DEQL(L2) consistently outperforms the EDLAE (b=0) baseline across all six datasets, with the largest gain on Games (R@20: 0.2998 vs. 0.2851, +5.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEQL(L2) outperforms all LAE-based baselines (EASE, EDLAE, DLAE, ELSA) on Amazon-Books and Yelp2018, and matches or slightly exceeds them on Gowalla. Against deep learning baselines, DEQL(L2) achieves the largest margin on Amazon-Books, beating SSM(GNN) by 27% in R@20 and 34% in N@20, and outperforming SimpleX, LightGCN, DGCF, SGL-ED, and PinSage by wide margins. On Yelp2018, DEQL(L2) surpasses all LAE baselines but is slightly behind SSM(GNN) in N@20 (0.0576 vs. 0.0609) while leading in R@20 (0.0685 vs. 0.0737 for SSM(GNN) — actually SSM(GNN) leads here at 0.0737). On Gowalla, DEQL(L2) is competitive with the top deep learning models (SimpleX 0.1872, SSM(GNN) 0.1869) but trails them slightly in R@20. The key finding is that the b>0 solutions from DEQL consistently beat the b=0 EDLAE baseline, confirming that the original EDLAE's restricted hyperparameter range is suboptimal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends EDLAE (Steck, 2020), which introduced dropout and emphasis weighting into linear autoencoder recommenders but only provided a closed-form solution for the special case b=0. By reformulating the EDLAE objective as a Decoupled Expected Quadratic Loss (DEQL), the authors derive a unified closed-form solution covering the full hyperparameter range b≥0, including the previously unexplored region b>a. The efficient computation algorithm repurposes Miller's classical matrix inverse theorem to reduce complexity from O(n⁴) to O(n³), matching the cost of EASE and EDLAE. The work sits within the broader LAE lineage (SLIM → EASE → EDLAE → ELSA → RLAE/RDLAE) and positions DEQL as a general loss framework that subsumes EDLAE as a special case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that b>a can outperform the standard a≥b regime on sparse, high-item-user-ratio datasets challenges a core design assumption in EDLAE and suggests that emphasizing dropped entries is not universally beneficial. However, the paper acknowledges a key limitation: DEQL provides no theoretical guidance on which hyperparameter choices (a, b, p, λ) yield the best test performance, leaving selection to empirical grid search. The DEQL framework is general enough to inspire other specialized objectives for LAE models, and the low-rank extension (Appendix D) opens a path toward scalability for very large item catalogs. Open questions include whether the b>a phenomenon has a deeper statistical explanation and whether the framework can be extended to handle the i-dependent Σ_xx case under low-rank constraints.
