## Problem

Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) is the standard mechanism for improving conditional fidelity in diffusion models, yet its effect on sampling dynamics remains poorly understood, especially under multimodal conditional distributions. Prior theoretical analyses either assume unimodal (single-Gaussian) conditionals, yielding clean but incomplete results, or impose only weak distributional assumptions, producing qualitative insights without sharp predictions. The most prominent unexplained phenomenon is why increasing the guidance weight consistently improves semantic alignment while simultaneously collapsing sample diversity.

## Input / Output

The theoretical analysis takes a conditional distribution modeled as a K-component Gaussian mixture (with shared covariance σ²I and weights π_k) and a guidance weight ω > 1, and produces a stage-wise characterization of the probability flow ODE trajectories. Empirically, the method is applied to Stable Diffusion v3.5 for text-to-image generation at 1024×1024 resolution on the COCO 2017 validation set (5,000 captioned images), where the time-varying guidance schedule replaces the constant ω in the standard CFG formula during the reverse ODE integration.

## Method

The paper models the conditional distribution as a Gaussian mixture and analyzes the CFG probability flow ODE under a linear-mean-decay noise schedule, revealing that guided sampling decomposes into three successive stages. In the early Direction Shift stage, CFG amplifies the global score, accelerating trajectories toward the scaled class-weighted mean ωμ̄ and inducing initialization bias plus norm inflation (Theorem 3.2). In the intermediate Mode Separation stage, CFG is locally neutral—the geometry of attraction basins is ω-independent and weaker modes retain genuine basins (Theorem 3.3)—but the inherited early-stage bias ensures most trajectories never enter weaker basins (Proposition 3.4). In the late Concentration stage, CFG scales the local restoring force within each mode, causing pairwise trajectory distances to shrink faster than under standard conditional sampling (Theorem 3.5). As a practical byproduct, the theory motivates a linear time-varying schedule (TV-CFG) that weakens guidance in the early and late stages while peaking in the middle, which empirically improves both ImageReward and diversity metrics over constant-weight CFG and prior interval/β schedules.

## Results

On COCO 2017 with Stable Diffusion v3.5 (NFE=10, ω=9), TV-CFG achieves IR=0.932, Diversity=1.223, FID=30.26, and Saturation=0.298, substantially outperforming vanilla CFG (IR=0.223, Diversity=1.081, FID=38.99, Saturation=0.574). At lower guidance (ω=3), TV-CFG matches vanilla CFG on CLIP (0.319 vs 0.320) while already improving IR (0.859 vs 0.894) and Diversity (1.092 vs 1.066). The advantage grows with guidance scale: at ω=9, TV-CFG's IR is 4.2× that of vanilla CFG, and its FID is 8.7 points lower. Across NFE budgets (Table 2), TV-CFG maintains its lead at low NFEs where vanilla CFG nearly collapses (e.g., NFE=5, ω=9: TV-CFG IR=0.145 vs vanilla IR=−1.137).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla CFG, TV-CFG delivers decisive gains at high guidance: at ω=9, IR improves from 0.223 to 0.932 (4.2×), FID drops from 38.99 to 30.26, Saturation halves from 0.574 to 0.298, and Diversity rises from 1.081 to 1.223, while CLIP remains comparable (0.319 vs 0.313). Against interval-CFG, TV-CFG wins on IR (0.932 vs 0.723 at ω=9) and Diversity (1.223 vs 1.160) but concedes on FID (30.26 vs 28.41) and Saturation (0.298 vs 0.193), indicating interval-CFG is slightly more conservative in over-saturation. Against β-CFG, TV-CFG achieves higher IR (0.932 vs 0.790) and lower FID (30.26 vs 32.18) at ω=9, while β-CFG edges ahead on Diversity (1.242 vs 1.223) and Saturation (0.305 vs 0.298, nearly tied). The same pattern holds under APG: TV-APG reaches IR=0.941 at ω=9 versus vanilla APG's 0.434, confirming the scheduling principle transfers across guidance formulations. Overall, TV-CFG is the strongest method on the quality metric (IR) across all guidance scales, while β-CFG and interval-CFG trade off some quality for marginally better diversity or lower saturation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of theoretical analyses of Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) that began with Ho & Salimans (2021) and was formalized under unimodal (single-Gaussian) conditional assumptions by Chidambaram et al. (2024), Wu et al. (2024), Bradley & Nakkiran (2024), and others. The key departure is modeling conditionals as Gaussian mixtures, which prior work could not handle, thereby revealing a three-stage sampling structure (Direction Shift → Mode Separation → Concentration) that unifies previously disconnected observations about norm inflation, mode suppression, and within-mode contraction. The proposed time-varying guidance schedule is empirically related to Interval-CFG (Kynkäänniemi et al., 2024) and β-CFG (Malarz et al., 2025), but here it is derived from the stage-wise theory rather than heuristically motivated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three-stage decomposition provides the first principled explanation for why high guidance weights collapse diversity: weaker modes are not destroyed by the guidance itself in the separation stage, but are starved of trajectories by the initialization bias accumulated in the high-noise stage. This reframing has direct practical implications for designing guidance schedules that preserve diversity without sacrificing fidelity. Limitations include the restriction to ODE sampling (not SDE), a specific linear noise schedule, and Gaussian mixture conditionals; extending the framework to stochastic dynamics, arbitrary noise schedules, and non-Gaussian mixtures remains open. The empirical gains from the proposed TV-CFG schedule, while consistent, are moderate rather than transformative, suggesting the theory's main value is explanatory rather than as a drop-in replacement for existing heuristics.
