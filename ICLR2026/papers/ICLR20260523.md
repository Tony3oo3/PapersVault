## Problem

Existing interpretability methods for localizing factual knowledge in transformers (activation patching, ablations) overwrite upstream residual stream information, making it impossible to distinguish whether a model component is actively extracting finetuned knowledge or merely passing along information computed at earlier positions. This prevents faithful identification of which mechanisms are necessary and sufficient for retrieving newly learned relation information during generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260523/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained model, a supervised-finetuned model (both decoder-only transformers), a token sequence prompt, and a binary grafting mask that specifies which weight matrices to swap at which token positions. It produces next-token logits scored by top-k relation completion accuracy, with the key output being a localization map identifying which specific components (attention, FFN, output projection) at which positions (first entity, last token) are necessary and sufficient for finetuned knowledge retrieval. The grafting is applied dynamically during a single forward pass using a KV-cache, so different token positions can use different model configurations simultaneously.

## Method

Dynamic weight grafting intervenes on model parameters rather than activations: at each token position during a forward pass, specific weight matrices (e.g., W^Q, W^K, W^V, O, FFN up/down projections) are swapped between the pretrained and finetuned models according to a position- and component-specific mask, while the residual stream flows uninterrupted. This preserves upstream computations that activation patching would destroy, enabling causal mediation analysis at the level of individual mechanisms. The paper identifies two retrieval pathways—'enrichment' at the first entity token position and 'recall' at the final token position—and further localizes the recall pathway to the output projection matrix and feedforward networks in the final layers, plus task-specific attention at the first entity, using a reversal-curse-based experimental design that separates task-specific from relation-specific model components.

## Results

Using dynamic weight grafting on four decoder-only LLMs (Gemma-1.1-2B, Llama-3.2-1B, GPT-2 XL, Pythia-2.8B), the paper shows that grafting only the first entity (FE) and last token (LT) positions nearly recovers full SFT performance (100% top-5 accuracy), while grafting everything except FE and LT ((FE+LT)^C) drops to near-zero accuracy comparable to the pretrained baseline. Individual pathways are partially sufficient: the 'recall' pathway (LT alone) achieves 53% top-5 in Gemma and the 'enrichment' pathway (FE alone) reaches 28% in GPT-2 XL. At the component level, the output projection (O) matrix plus feedforward networks in the final quarter of layers recover most of the recall pathway's performance, and a hybrid model combining task-specific attention at the first entity with relation-specific O+FFN at the last token recovers 63% top-5 in Gemma and 34% in Llama-3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an interpretability paper rather than a method-comparison paper, so it does not benchmark against competing localization methods on a shared metric. Instead, it compares grafting configurations against two natural baselines: the pretrained model (lower bound, ~0% top-5) and the fully finetuned model (upper bound, 100% top-5). The key finding is that FE+LT grafting nearly matches the SFT upper bound while (FE+LT)^C matches the pretrained lower bound, establishing necessity and sufficiency. The paper also contrasts its findings with Geva et al. (2023), who showed via ablation that knocking out attention is more harmful than knocking out FFN for relation completion; dynamic weight grafting reveals that when task-specific attention is already present, the O matrix and FFN at the final token are nearly sufficient, a nuance that destructive ablation methods cannot capture. GPT-2 XL shows an unusually strong LT^C result (grafting everything except the last token still retains some performance), which the authors attribute to its 48-layer architecture providing an alternative enrichment pathway not present in the other models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends static weight grafting (Panigrahi et al., 2023; Ilharco et al., 2022) into a dynamic, token-wise and component-wise intervention method, combining the non-destructive properties of parameter-space grafting with the causal mediation logic of activation patching (Meng et al., 2022a; Geva et al., 2023). The key conceptual departure from prior localization work is that by swapping weights rather than activations, upstream computations remain intact, allowing the authors to distinguish whether a component is actively extracting new information or merely passing along previously computed enrichment. The paper also leverages the reversal curse (Berglund et al., 2023; Allen-Zhu and Li, 2023) as a practical tool to disentangle task-specific from relation-specific mechanisms during component-level grafting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Dynamic weight grafting offers a less destructive alternative to activation patching for localizing finetuned knowledge, and the clean identification of two distinct retrieval pathways (entity enrichment and final-token recall) with component-level attribution (O matrix + FFN in final layers, task-specific attention) provides a concrete mechanistic account that prior ablation-based methods could not isolate. However, the findings are limited to small models (1.2B–2.8B) on synthetic single-hop relation data, and the authors acknowledge that the combinatorial space of grafting configurations is only partially explored, that multi-hop settings remain untested, and that the identified components have not yet been interpreted. Whether these mechanisms persist or reorganize in larger, more capable models is an open question with direct implications for knowledge editing and model safety.
