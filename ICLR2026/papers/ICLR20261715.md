## Problem

Monolithic tool-integrated reasoning models train a single policy to interleave thoughts and tool calls under full context, which becomes unstable as horizons lengthen and tool diversity grows, and generalizes poorly to unseen tasks. Agentic systems decompose work across specialized modules but are typically training-free or rely on offline training decoupled from live multi-turn dynamics, preventing them from adapting to evolving tool outputs or recovering from early mistakes. The central gap is how to train an agentic system's planner on-policy inside the live multi-turn loop under sparse, trajectory-level rewards.

## Input / Output

The system takes a text query and a fixed toolset of five tools (Base Generator, Python Coder, Google Search, Wikipedia Search, Web Search) with associated metadata. At each turn, the planner emits a structured action containing a justification, context, sub-goal, and tool name; the executor converts this into a Python command, and the verifier returns a binary STOP/CONTINUE signal. The final output is a solution generated by the Solution Generator conditioned on the accumulated evolving memory, which is an explicit, deterministic, structured record of all actions, execution results, and verification signals across turns (not latent chain-of-thought tokens).

## Method

AGENTFLOW coordinates four specialized modules—Action Planner (the only trainable component), Tool Executor, Execution Verifier, and Solution Generator—through a shared evolving memory and toolset, operating as a multi-turn MDP where each turn cycles through planning, execution, and verification until the verifier signals termination. To train the planner on-policy within this loop, the paper introduces Flow-GRPO, which samples groups of G on-policy trajectories, assigns a single verifiable final-outcome reward (via LLM-as-judge) to the entire trajectory, and broadcasts it uniformly to every turn, thereby decomposing the multi-turn credit-assignment problem into a sequence of independent single-turn PPO-style policy updates with group-normalized advantages for variance reduction. A formal equivalence proof shows that maximizing the global multi-turn objective is mathematically equivalent to maximizing the expected token-level local objective at each state under the on-policy induced state distribution, and a convergence analysis establishes monotonic policy improvement. Only the planner is fine-tuned (learning rate 1e-6, KL coefficient 0.001, max 3 turns during training, 10 turns at inference) while the executor, verifier, and generator remain frozen Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct instances.

## Results

AGENTFLOW with Flow-GRPO (7B Qwen2.5 backbone) achieves 57.3% average accuracy on search-intensive tasks (Bamboogle, 2Wiki, HotpotQA, Musique), 33.1% on GAIA (agentic), 51.5% on mathematical reasoning (AIME24, AMC23, GameOf24), and 63.5% on scientific reasoning (GPQA, MedQA). These results surpass the ~200B-parameter GPT-4o by 8.2% (search), 15.8% (agentic), 16.4% (math), and 18.0% (scientific). The system also outperforms the strongest 7B tool-integrated baselines by 14.9% on search (vs. AutoGen), 14.0% on agentic (vs. Search-R1), 14.5% on math (vs. ToRL), and 4.1% on scientific (vs. TIR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGENTFLOW (w/ Flow-GRPO) wins across all four task categories against every baseline tested. Against the strongest proprietary model GPT-4o (~200B), it gains +8.2% on search (57.3 vs. 49.1), +15.8% on agentic (33.1 vs. 17.3), +16.4% on math (51.5 vs. 35.1), and +18.0% on scientific (63.5 vs. 45.5). Among 7B tool-integrated reasoning models, it surpasses Search-R1 by +24.0% on search and +14.0% on GAIA, VerlTool by +18.3% on search, ToRL by +14.5% on math, and TIR by +4.1% on scientific reasoning. Against the training-free agentic system AutoGen (same 7B backbone), AGENTFLOW improves by +14.9% on search, +26.8% on GAIA, +19.9% on math, and +6.5% on scientific tasks. The ablation in Table 3 further shows that offline SFT of the planner causes a catastrophic 19.0% average drop, while in-the-flow Flow-GRPO training yields a +17.2% gain over the frozen baseline, confirming that on-policy optimization inside the multi-turn loop is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two established but disconnected lines of work: monolithic tool-integrated reasoning models (Search-R1, ToRL, ReSearch) that train a single policy to interleave thoughts and tool calls, and training-free agentic systems (AutoGen, MetaGPT, OctoTools) that decompose tasks across specialized modules but rely on handcrafted orchestration. AGENTFLOW departs from both by introducing a trainable four-module architecture (planner, executor, verifier, generator) with an evolving memory, and by directly optimizing the planner on-policy inside the multi-turn loop. The training algorithm, Flow-GRPO, extends GRPO's group-normalized advantage and PPO-style clipped objective to the multi-turn setting by broadcasting a single verifiable trajectory-level reward to every turn, thereby reducing multi-turn credit assignment to a sequence of single-turn policy updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a 7B-backbone agentic system with in-the-flow RL outperforms the ~200B GPT-4o across all four task domains suggests that structured decomposition and adaptive planning can substitute for raw model scale in tool-augmented reasoning, which has significant practical implications for cost-efficient deployment. The finding that offline SFT on GPT-4o trajectories causes a 19% performance collapse while in-the-flow Flow-GRPO yields a 17.2% gain is an important negative result for practitioners considering distillation-based approaches to agentic planning. Open questions include whether the four-module decomposition and five-tool set generalize to open-ended environments with dozens of tools, whether the approach scales beyond 7B backbones without architectural changes, and whether the LLM-as-judge reward signal introduces systematic biases that limit real-world applicability.
