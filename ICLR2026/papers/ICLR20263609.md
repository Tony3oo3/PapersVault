## Problem

Radiance field representations face a fundamental trade-off: neural formulations like NeRF offer high expressivity but require costly ray marching for rendering, while primitive-based methods like 3D Gaussian Splatting achieve real-time efficiency through splatting but sacrifice representational power due to their simple analytic density forms. No prior representation reconciles both properties—neural expressivity and splatting-based rendering efficiency—within a single unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263609/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics and produces a compact 3D scene representation of ellipsoid-bounded neural primitives (99 parameters each, requiring roughly 10× fewer primitives and 6× fewer total parameters than 3DGS for equivalent quality). Rendering from this representation yields novel-view images at over 100 FPS on an RTX 4090; notably, the density field is never directly evaluated during training or inference—only its closed-form antiderivative is queried at ray entry and exit points.

## Method

Each primitive's volumetric density is parameterized by a shallow one-hidden-layer neural network with periodic (SIREN) activation (8 hidden neurons, frequency multiplier ω₀=30), spatially bounded by an ellipsoid defined by center, scale vector, and rotation quaternion. The critical insight is that this specific network architecture admits an exact closed-form antiderivative for line integrals along arbitrary view rays, enabling computation of a perspectively accurate 2D splatting kernel via two evaluations of the antiderivative at the ray–ellipsoid intersection points—eliminating ray marching entirely. View-dependent color is encoded with 4-band spherical harmonics, and final pixel colors are obtained through standard front-to-back alpha blending of depth-sorted primitives. A novel densification strategy based on gradient magnitude of network weights (rather than 3DGS's screen-space position gradients) controls the primitive population, and a geometric regularization term penalizing extreme anisotropy in primitive scale vectors stabilizes the otherwise under-constrained optimization of millions of small networks.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF360, the method achieves 27.21 PSNR / 0.791 SSIM / 0.216 LPIPS at 115 FPS using only 93 MB of memory, matching 3DGS quality (27.21 PSNR) while using ~8× less memory (93 MB vs. 734 MB). On Tanks & Temples, it reaches 23.59 PSNR / 0.846 SSIM / 0.162 LPIPS at 158 FPS with 80 MB, slightly outperforming 3DGS (23.14 PSNR, 411 MB). On Deep Blending, it achieves 29.20 PSNR / 0.892 SSIM / 0.264 LPIPS at 178 FPS with 82 MB, comparable to 3DGS (29.41 PSNR, 676 MB). The headline claim is matching 3DGS quality and speed while using 10× fewer primitives and 6× fewer parameters per scene.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS (the primary baseline), the method matches PSNR on Mip-NeRF360 (27.21 vs. 27.21) and slightly outperforms on Tanks & Temples (23.59 vs. 23.14 PSNR), while using 8× less memory (93 vs. 734 MB) and 10× fewer primitives; it is slightly slower (115 vs. 152 FPS on Mip-NeRF360). Against BetaGS, the method loses on quality (27.21 vs. 28.75 PSNR on Mip-NeRF360; 23.59 vs. 24.85 on T&T) but uses substantially less memory (93 vs. 356 MB; 80 vs. 200 MB). Against T-3DGS, the method is slightly lower in PSNR on Mip-NeRF360 (27.21 vs. 27.31) and T&T (23.59 vs. 23.95) but uses comparable memory (93 vs. 152 MB) and is much slower (115 vs. 265 FPS). Against monolithic neural methods (Mip-NeRF360, INGP, Plenoxels), the method is 100×+ faster (115 FPS vs. <1–9 FPS) with comparable or better quality. Against other splatting-based analytic primitives (GES, ConvSplat, Vol3DGS), the method achieves comparable PSNR with 4–8× less memory. The AutoInt ablation (Fig. 7) shows that the proposed closed-form integration avoids the view-dependent density artifacts that plague derivative-graph-based neural integration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges the long-standing dichotomy between monolithic neural radiance fields (NeRF and successors) that require costly ray marching, and primitive-based splatting methods (3DGS and variants) that sacrifice expressivity for rendering speed. The key technical enabler is the observation that single-hidden-layer networks with periodic (SIREN-style) activations admit closed-form line integrals, a result from Lloyd et al. (2020) and Subr (2021) previously applied to numerical integration but not to volumetric rendering. By combining this analytical integrability with ellipsoid-bounded spatial containment (from EVER, Mai et al. 2025) and the splatting/alpha-blending pipeline of 3DGS, the authors make neural density fields directly splattable without ray marching. The densification strategy is adapted from 3DGS but reparameterized to use network-weight gradients rather than screen-space position gradients, since the latter is incompatible with neural primitives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a neural density field can be integrated in closed form along arbitrary view rays challenges the field's working assumption that neural representations and splatting-based rendering are fundamentally incompatible. Practically, achieving 3DGS-quality novel-view synthesis with 10× fewer primitives and 6× fewer parameters is a meaningful efficiency gain, and the advantage arises from the representation itself rather than from auxiliary control frameworks. Key limitations include a 2.5× longer training time due to the complex optimization landscape of millions of small networks, and the fact that the per-primitive expressivity gain (1.6× more parameters per primitive) is more than offset by the large reduction in primitive count. Open questions include whether better optimization strategies (e.g., stochastic preconditioning) can close the training-time gap, and whether the approach extends naturally to textured primitives, adaptive densification, and 4D dynamic scenes.
