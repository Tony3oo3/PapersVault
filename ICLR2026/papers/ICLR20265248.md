## Problem

Imitation learning policies for robot manipulation achieve high precision and generalization but are fundamentally speed-limited by the pace of teleoperated demonstrations and inference latency. Naively increasing action execution frequency causes accumulated state errors from altered transition dynamics and violates physical torque/velocity limits, while asynchronous inference introduces misalignment between planned and actual robot states. The paper asks: how can any action-chunk imitation policy be accelerated beyond demonstration speed without degrading task success?

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pre-trained action-chunk policy (diffusion policy or VLA such as π0.5) that consumes RGB images, proprioceptive joint state, and a language instruction to produce a chunk of T_a waypoints. RACE reinterprets these waypoints as desired joint states (not motor commands), interpolates them into a continuous path via cubic spline with current velocity as boundary condition, and applies TOPP-RA using the robot's torque and velocity constraint polytopes. The output is a time-optimal speed profile s(t) over the path plus a Best-of-N–selected chunk that maximizes a smoothness objective, yielding a dynamically feasible, adaptively timed execution plan rather than a fixed-rate action sequence.

## Method

RACE is a three-part, policy-agnostic wrapper around any action-chunk imitation policy. (1) The policy is retrained or fine-tuned to predict reached states from demonstrations instead of action commands, making the imitation target invariant to execution timing; a higher-gain low-level controller then tracks these states during rollout. (2) Each predicted state chunk is interpolated into a geometric path and reparameterized by TOPP-RA, which projects generalized second-order kinodynamic constraints into the path phase-space (squared velocity, pseudo-acceleration) and solves a backward reachability pass followed by a forward greedy pass to find the minimum-time speed profile respecting torque and velocity limits. (3) Under asynchronous inference, RACE samples N candidate chunks from the generative policy and selects the one maximizing J(q) = s_end / ∫|q''|² ds, a curvature-based smoothness score that provably enlarges the initial controllable set K₀, ensuring the TOPP solver remains solvable even when the robot has drifted from the policy's expected start state.

## Results

RACE achieves Pareto-optimal speed-accuracy trade-offs across simulation and real-world tasks. In simulation (RoboMimic), RACE reaches up to 2× speedup over demonstration while maintaining or improving success rate (e.g., Tool Hang: SR 0.715 at 2.05× SOD vs. SAIL's 0.610 at 0.94×). On the real-world Door Insertion task, RACE achieves 4× the original policy speed without degrading success rate. On throughput-intensive VLA tasks (Fruit Packaging, Trash Cleaning), RACE doubles cumulative successes compared to baselines, and on a dynamic conveyor-belt pick task at 2.5× unseen speed, RACE maintains 53% success while both baselines drop to 0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary named baseline is SAIL (Arachchige et al., 2025), which also accelerates imitation policies by using states as actions and task-specific acceleration rules. RACE outperforms SAIL in success rate on all four RoboMimic tasks (Lift: 0.995 vs. 0.930, Can: 0.965 vs. 0.890, Square: 0.805 vs. 0.750, Tool Hang: 0.715 vs. 0.610), achieving maximal speedup without sacrificing accuracy. On the two most precision-demanding tasks, RACE also achieves substantially higher speedup (Square: 1.82× vs. 1.62×; Tool Hang: 2.05× vs. 0.94×), demonstrating the benefit of task-agnostic TOPP-based retiming over SAIL's per-task tuned acceleration. SAIL edges out RACE slightly on speedup for the simpler Lift (2.52× vs. 2.07×) and Can (1.97× vs. 1.81×) tasks, but at the cost of 5–10 percentage points lower success rate. Against the simpler Action Fast-forward and State Fast-forward baselines (shown in Figures 4–6), RACE consistently dominates the speed-success Pareto frontier, with naive 4×/8× acceleration causing severe success-rate drops (especially on Square and Tool Hang) while RACE maintains near-original success rates at comparable or higher speeds.

## Relation to Prior Work

RACE sits at the intersection of imitation learning and optimal control, departing from the standard IL paradigm where policies are inherently speed-limited by their demonstrations. It repurposes TOPP-RA (Pham & Pham, 2018) — originally a classical robotics planning tool — as an inference-time retimer for generative action chunks, and adapts Best-of-N sampling (Touvron et al., 2023) with a novel physical smoothness objective to resolve asynchronous misalignment. The closest prior work, SAIL (Arachchige et al., 2025), also uses states-as-actions but relies on task-specific acceleration rules and conditional generation rather than task-agnostic time-optimal planning and test-time search. RACE is complementary to inference-acceleration methods (diffusion distillation, parallel decoding) that reduce latency but do not address the physical execution challenges of moving faster than demonstrations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RACE addresses a practical bottleneck in robot deployment: strong IL policies are speed-capped by teleoperation demonstrations, limiting industrial throughput. The 4× speedup on high-precision real-world tasks without success-rate degradation suggests that the speed-precision trade-off in manipulation is less fundamental than previously assumed, at least for tasks where the policy has learned the correct geometry. Open questions include whether the approach extends to highly dynamic or contact-rich tasks where kinodynamic constraints are more complex, how it interacts with RL fine-tuning for further speed gains, and whether the theoretical controllability guarantees (Proposition 1) hold under the stochasticity of real-world VLA models at scale.
