## Problem

Existing diffusion-based OOD detection methods such as DiffPath require propagating samples through entire denoising trajectories, incurring 10–1000 sequential forward passes per query and making real-time deployment impractical. Meanwhile, likelihood-based and reconstruction-based detectors suffer from well-documented pathologies (e.g., higher likelihood on OOD data) or brittleness to architectural choices. The paper asks whether a single, cheaply computable statistic derived from the score function's local geometry can match the accuracy of these expensive trajectory-based approaches.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single pre-trained unconditional diffusion model (a CelebA-trained DDPM for 32×32/64×64 vision tasks, or an EDM denoiser for proprioceptive RL transitions) and a query sample, which is corrupted to one or two preselected noise levels. At each noise level, one forward pass and one Jacobian-vector product yield the score-curvature ratio T(x); a KDE fitted offline on in-distribution T(x) values converts this into a calibrated anomaly score (negative log-likelihood), and the maximum across timesteps is reported as the final OOD score. No OOD data is needed at any stage—noise-level selection and KDE calibration use only in-distribution samples.

## Method

SCOPED exploits the Fisher information identity E_p[Tr(∇s)] = E_p[||s||²] to define the typicality ratio T(x) = ||s_θ(x_t,t)||² / Tr(∇_x s_θ(x_t,t)), which concentrates near 1 on the typical set of the data distribution. The Jacobian trace is estimated via Hutchinson's stochastic trace estimator, reducing curvature computation to a single JVP with cost comparable to one extra forward pass. Rather than thresholding T(x) on a fixed value, the method fits a kernel density estimate to T(x) on in-distribution data and uses −log h(T(x)) as the anomaly score, providing a flexible, parametric-free calibration. For vision, SCOPED evaluates at two timesteps (t=1 for fine detail, t=300 for coarse structure, chosen from the SNR curve using only ID data) and takes the maximum NLL; for RL, a single mid-schedule timestep suffices. A global sign factor derived from the score vector corrects the sign ambiguity between the squared-norm numerator and the signed Jacobian-trace denominator. Because each probe is independent of all other timesteps and samples, evaluations fully parallelize, yielding an order-of-magnitude reduction in effective function evaluations compared to serial trajectory-based methods.

## Results

On four vision benchmarks (CIFAR-10, SVHN, CelebA, CIFAR-100), SCOPED (two-step, 2F+2J) achieves an average AUROC of 0.892, while SCOPED (Single, 1F+1J) reaches 0.884. The Oracle variant (best single timestep with hindsight) attains 0.944 average AUROC. In RL, SCOPED achieves perfect AUROC (1.000) on all DMC environment shifts (reacher, finger-turn, humanoid) and near-perfect AUROC on D4RL hopper-v2 and walker2d-v2, with ant-v2 being the hardest case. The key efficiency gain is that SCOPED requires only 2 forward passes + 2 JVPs (≈4 effective evaluations) versus 10 for DiffPath/MSMA, 350 for DDPM-OOD, and 10,000 for LMD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCOPED (avg 0.892) trails the strongest baselines DoS (0.928) and MSMA (0.928) by ~3.6 AUROC points on average, and DiffPath (0.918) by ~2.6 points, but requires only 2F+2J versus 10F for DiffPath/MSMA and an unknown (likely high) cost for DoS. Against diffusion-specific baselines, SCOPED substantially outperforms LMD (0.868, 10⁴F), DDPM-OOD (0.742, 350F), and NLL (0.652, 1000F) while using 1–4 orders of magnitude fewer evaluations. SCOPED beats DiffPath on three of nine dataset pairs (C10→CelebA: 0.940 vs 0.897; SVHN→C10: 0.971 vs 0.939; SVHN→C100: 0.959 vs 0.953) but loses on the CelebA-as-ID pairs where DiffPath reaches near-perfect scores (0.998–1.000). The single-step variant (1F+1J, avg 0.884) remains competitive with the two-step version, confirming that one forward pass plus one JVP is often sufficient. The Oracle variant (0.944) shows that the gap to the best baselines is largely attributable to timestep selection rather than the statistic itself, and all noise levels are chosen using only in-distribution data, avoiding the OOD-benchmark tuning used by prior diffusion-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCOPED builds on the score-based diffusion framework (DDPM, EDM, SDE formulations) by repurposing the score function's local geometry—specifically the ratio of score norm to Jacobian trace—as an OOD test statistic grounded in Fisher information and the information-theoretic typical set. It departs sharply from prior diffusion-based OOD methods (DiffPath, MSMA, DDPM-OOD, LMD) that require serial trajectory integration or many forward passes, instead probing the model at one or two independently chosen noise levels with a single JVP via Hutchinson's estimator. The calibration step (KDE on in-distribution scores) is borrowed from anomaly detection practice, and the noise-level selection strategy is motivated by the monotone SNR decay of the forward diffusion process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The order-of-magnitude reduction in function evaluations (2F+2J vs. 10F–10⁴F for prior diffusion methods) combined with full parallelizability makes diffusion-based OOD detection viable in compute-constrained and real-time settings, which was a key barrier for prior trajectory-based approaches. The extension to proprioceptive RL (D4RL, DMC) is the first diffusion-based OOD validation in that domain, though the authors frame these as diagnostic case studies rather than competitive benchmarks. Open questions include whether the score-curvature statistic generalizes to autoregressive or multimodal models, whether more principled noise-step selection can close the gap to the oracle variant, and why the C10-vs-C100 pair remains weak (AUROC ≈ 0.48), suggesting the statistic may struggle with fine-grained semantic shifts within a shared visual domain.
