## Problem

AlphaFold3's iterative Pairformer recycling and multi-step diffusion process make inference prohibitively slow (minutes per long sequence), blocking high-throughput applications like virtual screening and differentiable protein design. While consistency models have accelerated image diffusion to single-step generation, directly applying them to AlphaFold3 fails due to variable-length protein sequences and the additional Pairformer recycling bottleneck that standard consistency methods cannot address.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263636/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DCFold consumes the same inputs as AlphaFold3—protein sequences, multiple sequence alignments, and optionally ligand or nucleic acid sequences—and produces a 3D all-atom structure plus confidence scores (pLDDT, pDE, pAE) in a single forward pass (1 diffusion step, 1 Pairformer recycle) rather than the original multi-step iterative pipeline. In the binder design setting, it additionally outputs candidate binder amino acid sequences (55–65 residues) that are differentiable with respect to the folding model, enabling gradient-based sequence optimization.

## Method

DCFold applies a Dual Consistency training framework that jointly distills both bottlenecks of AlphaFold3: (1) Diffusion Consistency distills the multi-step EDM sampler into a single-step generator by minimizing MSE between outputs at paired timesteps, and (2) Pairformer Consistency enforces cycle-to-cycle representation invariance (MSE between adjacent pair and single representations) so that one 16-block Pairformer pass suffices. The key technical innovation is Temporal Geodesic Matching (TGM), a timestep-pairing schedule grounded in the temporal Fisher information metric: the paper proves that the geodesic distance on the temporal information manifold is locally equivalent to the square root of KL divergence (Proposition 1), then derives a closed-form Fisher information expression for the EDM noise schedule (Proposition 2) that explicitly incorporates data dimensionality D. This ensures that on long sequences, where KL divergence accumulates linearly with dimension, the effective training gap between paired timesteps remains constant, preventing the distribution-shift instability that causes standard consistency schedules to collapse on variable-length proteins.

## Results

On Posebusters V2, DCFold achieves 94.29% best-case and 90.48% worst-case RMSD < 5 Å, matching or exceeding AlphaFold3 (93.81%/87.62%) while using only 1 diffusion step and 1 Pairformer recycle. On the Recent PDB benchmark, DCFold reaches 0.824/0.850/0.800 TM-score (PL-complex/Monomer/PP-complex) with 94.9%/95.7%/92.2% success rate, surpassing both AlphaFold3 and AF3 ODE. Inference time drops from ~93–212 s (AlphaFold3) to ~4–27 s (DCFold) across token bins, yielding up to 24× speedup on short sequences and >7.7× on longer ones. In binder hallucination, DCFold achieves an average in silico success rate of 0.29/0.78 (physics/model constraints) versus BindCraft's 0.26/0.69.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AlphaFold3 (full multi-step), DCFold matches or slightly exceeds worst-case RMSD (90.48% vs 87.62% at <5 Å) and TM-score/SR on Recent PDB (e.g., 0.800/92.2% vs 0.788/91.1% on PP-complex), while best-case RMSD is marginally lower (58.10% vs 67.14% at <1 Å), indicating a tighter output distribution. Against AF3 ODE (single-step without retraining), DCFold consistently wins across all RMSD thresholds and all Recent PDB categories, with the largest gains in success rate (+2.6 to +5.2 pp). In binder design, DCFold outperforms BindCraft on 4 of 6 targets (notably H3: 0.23 vs 0.16, VirB8: 0.21 vs 0.15, LTK: 0.47 vs 0.43 under physics constraints) and achieves a higher average (0.29 vs 0.26). Among consistency-model baselines, naive CD collapses (25.6% SR), sCM is infeasible, and TGM outperforms ECM (77.5% vs 75.7% SR) with identical per-step cost (11.6 s), confirming that the geodesic scheduling is critical for variable-length protein sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

DCFold applies the Consistency Model (CM) framework from image generation (Song et al., 2023; Lu & Song, 2024; Geng et al., 2024) to the AlphaFold3 architecture, which had not previously been amenable to such distillation due to its variable-length outputs and iterative Pairformer recycling. The key departure from prior CM work is the Temporal Geodesic Matching scheduler, which replaces fixed Euclidean timestep pairing with a Fisher-information-based geodesic distance to handle the non-uniform information accumulation across protein sequence lengths. Additionally, the Dual Consistency framework extends the consistency principle beyond the diffusion module to the Pairformer's iterative cycles, treating each cycle as a temporal step — a novel application not present in prior consistency literature. The method is initialized from Protenix, an open-source AlphaFold3 reimplementation, and inherits the EDM noise schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 15× inference speedup makes AlphaFold3-level all-atom structure prediction feasible for high-throughput downstream tasks (virtual screening, large-scale binder design) that were previously impractical, and the single-step, fully differentiable pipeline unlocks gradient-based protein design with AF3-level models — something previously only possible with the less capable AlphaFold2. The TGM scheduler provides a principled, theoretically grounded solution to a general problem (consistency training on variable-dimensional data) that could transfer to other structured-output generative tasks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to even larger biomolecular complexes, whether the modest accuracy gap on best-case RMSD (58.1% vs 67.1% at <1 Å on Posebusters V2) matters in practice, and whether the 16-block Pairformer reduction in Stage 2 limits ceiling performance on particularly difficult targets.
