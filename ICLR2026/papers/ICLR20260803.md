## Problem

Modeling wind-driven object dynamics from video is challenging because wind is invisible, spatially nonuniform, and temporally varying, while object deformations depend on unknown material properties. Existing dynamic scene reconstruction methods capture only visible motion without recovering the underlying wind field, and differentiable physics simulators are restricted to simple predefined forces (e.g., gravity, constant drag) that cannot represent complex fluid-object interactions. No prior method jointly recovers both the invisible spatio-temporal wind force field and the visible object dynamics from sparse-view videos while enforcing physical consistency.

## Input / Output

The method takes sparse-view RGB videos (4 training + 4 test viewpoints for synthetic; 360° surround GoPro footage for real-world scenes) of wind-object interaction as input. It outputs a spatio-temporal wind force field on a 128³ Eulerian grid, the dynamic 3D object state as deformed 3D Gaussians (positions, velocities, deformation gradients, and covariance matrices), and supports downstream tasks including novel view synthesis, forward simulation under user-specified wind conditions, and wind retargeting to previously unseen objects. Physical properties (Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, density) are inferred by an MLLM (GPT-5) rather than optimized, and wind source direction is estimated from RGB-depth pairs via MLLM reasoning.

## Method

DiffWind couples an Eulerian grid-based wind field (simulated via HOME-LBM, a high-order moment-encoded Lattice Boltzmann Method) with a Lagrangian particle-based object representation (3D Gaussians densified with invisible internal points via octree voxel filling), linked through the Material Point Method (MLS-MPM) where wind forces are applied to the MPM background grid to drive particle deformation. Reconstruction proceeds by sequentially optimizing the wind force field at each timestep to minimize photometric loss (L1 + D-SSIM) between differentiable 3DGS renderings and observed frames, with the object state from the previous step serving as the initial condition to avoid multi-step gradient instability. A physics-informed loss constrains the reconstructed wind force direction to align with the LBM-simulated velocity field (computed with object particles as solid boundary conditions), enforcing Navier-Stokes compliance without requiring ground-truth wind data. The 3DGS representation is regularized with anisotropy and opacity losses to prevent needle artifacts and floaters under large deformations, and 3D region segmentation via contrastive learning (OmniSeg3D-style) assigns region-wise physical properties inferred by GPT-5.

## Results

On the synthetic WD-Objects dataset, DiffWind achieves an average PSNR of 47.15, SSIM of 0.9983, and LPIPS of 0.0008 for novel view synthesis, substantially outperforming the best baseline GaussianPrediction (35.79 / 0.9811 / 0.0229) by over 11 dB in PSNR. On the real-world dataset, DiffWind leads on all four scenes (e.g., 26.18 PSNR on POTHOS vs. 24.95 for Deformable-GS). In the forward simulation user study, DiffWind scores 4.34/5 in visual quality, nearly 1.5 points above the best video-diffusion baseline DynamiCrafter (2.84/5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffWind outperforms all four dynamic-scene-reconstruction baselines (Deformable-GS, Efficient-GS, 4D-GS, GaussianPrediction) by a wide margin on both synthetic and real-world novel view synthesis, with the largest gap on the synthetic set (≈11 dB PSNR over GaussianPrediction, ≈20 dB over Efficient-GS). Among the reconstruction baselines, GaussianPrediction and Deformable-GS are the closest competitors, but both still trail DiffWind by 10+ dB on average, indicating that purely appearance-driven deformation fields cannot capture the physics of wind-driven motion. For forward simulation, DiffWind (4.34/5) decisively beats all four video-diffusion baselines (SVD 2.49, CogVideoX 2.34, VideoCrafter 2.33, DynamiCrafter 2.84) in a 32-participant user study, with the gap most pronounced on the Cloth and Sock scenes where physical plausibility of fabric deformation is critical. The paper also notes that physics-based baselines PhysDreamer and PhysFlow perform even worse than the dynamic-scene methods (≈18 PSNR on Ficus) because they only optimize initial velocity and Young's modulus for simple motion patterns, making them unsuitable for complex wind-induced dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffWind sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) dynamic 3D scene reconstruction via 3D Gaussian Splatting (Deformable-GS, 4D-GS, GaussianPrediction), which captures visible motion but ignores physical causes; (2) differentiable physics-based system identification (PhysGaussian, PAC-NeRF, PhysDreamer), which recovers material parameters but is restricted to simple predefined motions like gravity or constant force; and (3) fluid simulation via the Lattice Boltzmann Method (HOME-LBM), which models wind dynamics but has not been coupled with deformable solid simulation in a differentiable reconstruction pipeline. The key departure is the joint particle-grid formulation: objects are Lagrangian MPM particles while wind is an Eulerian LBM grid, with the MPM P2G/G2P loop serving as the differentiable interface between the two domains. This coupling, combined with LBM as a physics-informed directional constraint on the reconstructed force field, is the paper's central architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework opens a new application space—wind retargeting, where a recovered wind field can be transferred to entirely different objects—that is infeasible for purely data-driven dynamic scene methods. The introduction of WD-Objects (synthetic + real-world) provides the first dedicated benchmark for wind-driven dynamics, addressing a gap the authors explicitly note. Key limitations remain: the method handles only single objects (no inter-object collisions), MPM is restricted to continuum materials, and reconstruction takes ~2 hours per scene on a single GPU. Open questions include extending to multi-object interactions, non-continuum objects (e.g., rigid bodies, springs), and whether the LBM constraint generalizes to highly turbulent regimes beyond its stability range.
