## Problem

Existing generative-model-based Bayesian optimization methods require a two-stage pipeline: first fit a surrogate (regression or classification) model to the objective, then train a generative model conditioned on that surrogate's predictions. This compounds approximation errors from both stages and adds significant computational overhead. The paper asks whether a generative model can be trained directly from noisy observation values to produce a proposal whose density is proportional to the acquisition function, eliminating the intermediate surrogate entirely.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of noisy scalar evaluations (x_i, y_i) collected over T rounds, a search space X that may be discrete, combinatorial, or mixed (e.g., protein sequences over a 20-letter alphabet), and a user-specified utility function (probability of improvement, expected improvement, soft-EI, or mean) with an annealing threshold τ_t. It produces a learned generative proposal distribution q_t whose density tracks p_0(x)·a_t(x), from which a batch of B candidate points is sampled for parallel evaluation. Unlike latent-space BO, all inference occurs in the original observation space, and the generative backbone can be any architecture (mean-field categorical, causal transformer, diffusion, or flow matching) as long as it supports density evaluation or sampling.

## Method

GenBO reframes the acquisition function a_t(x) = E[u(y)|x, D_t] as a target distribution p_u*(x) ∝ p_0(x)·a_t(x) (or p_0(x)·exp(a_t(x)) for signed utilities) and trains a generative model q to match it directly from noisy utility values u(y_i). Two loss families are provided: (1) a DPO-style pairwise preference loss (Eq. 15) that uses only the sign of utility differences between point pairs, with a robust variant (Eq. 18) correcting for preference-flip noise, and (2) a forward KL or balanced forward KL loss (Eqs. 20–21) that uses importance-weighted sample-level utilities to match the target density. The algorithm iterates: minimize the loss to fit q_{t-1}, sample a batch B_t from it, evaluate the black-box objective, append observations to D_t, and repeat. Theoretically, under RKHS regularity assumptions, the learned log-density converges to the target at a rate governed by the inverse Hessian operator H_n^{-1}, and the resulting proposals concentrate at the global optima of f via reward-weighted regression dynamics.

## Results

On the ALOHA text optimization task (|X| > 11.8M, B=8, T=10), GenBO with the robust preference loss (rPL) and EI utilities shows the fastest early improvements, while PI-based GenBO reaches the exact optimum by the final round; GenBO runs ~3× faster than VSD by eliminating the intermediate surrogate. On the Ehrlich protein design tasks (M=15, 32, 64; B=128, T=32), KL-based GenBO variants outperform or match all baselines, with the balanced forward KL loss showing the clearest advantage at M=64. On the FoldX SASA task (M=228, B=64, T=20), most GenBO variants far outperform LaMBO-2, VSD, CbAS, and random mutation, converging to higher cumulative maximum values much more rapidly. On FoldX Stability, GenBO variants struggle relative to VSD and CbAS, which exploit low-diversity exploitation around the initial dataset more effectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against **CbAS** (Brookes et al., 2019), GenBO consistently outperforms across all four tasks; CbAS under-performs on ALOHA due to using only the last batch for retraining, and is outpaced on Ehrlich and SASA. Against **VSD** (Steinberg et al., 2025), GenBO matches or exceeds performance on ALOHA (reaching the exact optimum) and Ehrlich functions, and is ~3× faster in wall-clock time by avoiding the CNN classifier surrogate; however, VSD outperforms GenBO on the challenging FoldX Stability task where low-diversity exploitation is key. Against **LaMBO-2** (Gruver et al., 2023), GenBO variants substantially outperform on the SASA task and match or exceed on Ehrlich functions, while LaMBO-2 also struggles on Stability. Against **random mutation / GA** (POLI, González-Duque et al., 2024), GenBO achieves significantly lower simple regret and higher cumulative maximum across all tasks, with the gap most pronounced on SASA where GenBO's uninformative-prior variants exploit extrapolation far more effectively than random search.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenBO draws a direct conceptual bridge between DPO's reward-model-free training (Rafailov et al., 2023) and likelihood-free Bayesian optimization (Song et al., 2022), showing that the DPO reparameterization trick can eliminate the intermediate surrogate model that prior generative BO methods (VSD, CbAS, LaMBO-2) require. Where VSD and CbAS train a classifier for the improvement event and then fit a generative model on top, GenBO trains the generative model directly from noisy utility values using either a robust Bradley-Terry preference loss or a balanced forward KL objective. The theoretical analysis borrows from the kernelized bandits literature (Abbasi-Yadkori, 2012) and reward-weighted regression (Strupl et al., 2022) to establish convergence of the learned proposal to the acquisition-function-weighted target distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By collapsing the two-stage surrogate-then-generative pipeline into a single model, GenBO reduces both approximation error and wall-clock cost (3× faster than VSD in experiments), making large-batch, high-dimensional, and combinatorial BO more tractable. Open questions include extending the framework to acquisition functions not expressible as expected utilities (e.g., Thompson sampling, UCB), understanding sensitivity to the fixed prior and temperature parameters, and establishing sublinear cumulative regret bounds. The finding that exploration (uninformative prior) helps on SASA but hurts on stability suggests the method's performance is task-dependent in ways not yet fully characterized.
