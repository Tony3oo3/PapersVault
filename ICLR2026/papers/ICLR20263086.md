## Problem

Molecule-protein interaction (MPI) prediction is severely limited by the scarcity of experimentally validated labels and by the narrow reliance on only molecular and protein features, ignoring broader biological context such as genes, metabolic pathways, and functional annotations. Existing knowledge-graph-based approaches either treat the KG as a static auxiliary feature source or apply pseudo-labeling heuristics that lack global distributional consistency, leaving a gap in how to systematically leverage heterogeneous biological data to generate reliable supervision for unlabeled pairs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D molecular and protein conformations (encoded by Uni-Mol with 512-dim embeddings) and a unified multimodal biological KG containing 6.48M directed relations across 8 entity types (UniProt proteins, CHEBI molecules, KEGG pathways, GO terms, Pfam families, EC numbers, KEGG compounds/modules). It produces a dense M×N interaction score matrix (10k molecules × 5k proteins in experiments), an entropically regularized OT transport plan whose entries serve as soft pseudo-labels (thresholded at δ=0.5 for extraction), and ranked KG link-prediction scores for held-out molecule-protein edges.

## Method

KGOT closes a loop between score learning, pseudo-label generation, and KG training in three stages. First, a scoring function S(x,y) is trained on labeled pairs using an inverse optimal transport (IOT) loss—KL divergence between the predicted and ground-truth Sinkhorn transport plans—which generalizes InfoNCE and enforces global batch-level matching structure. Second, the trained scorer produces a full M×N score matrix; an entropically regularized OT problem (Sinkhorn-Knopp, ε=0.01) is solved with an additional molecular-similarity constraint (λ=0.1) that penalizes divergence between the pseudo-label inner-product structure and the true molecular similarity matrix, yielding a transport plan whose high-mass entries become pseudo-positive interactions. Third, these pseudo-labels are written back into the KG as a new 'pseudo_interaction' relation, and standard KG embedding models (TorusE, RotatE, MuRE, etc.) are trained jointly on real and pseudo edges with a multi-objective loss combining standard KG triple loss and a pseudo-label alignment term (α=0.1), making the framework model-agnostic and enabling label-efficient MPI retrieval with multimodal biological context.

## Results

On the DUD-E virtual screening benchmark (zero-shot), KGOT achieves 83.45% AUROC, 51.20% BEDROC, and 39.10% EF@0.5%, outperforming the previous best method DrugCLIP (80.93% AUROC, 50.52% BEDROC, 38.07% EF@0.5%) by approximately 2.5 percentage points in AUROC. On the more challenging LIT-PCBA benchmark, KGOT reaches 62.45% AUROC and 9.12% EF@0.5%, surpassing the best baseline Gnina (60.93% AUROC) and DrugCLIP (57.17% AUROC). For KG link prediction, the OT pseudo-label augmentation boosts Hits@5 from 70.0% to 74.9% (TorusE) and Hits@1 from 30.8% to 43.6% (ComplEx-FF).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KGOT outperforms all 12 baselines across both virtual screening benchmarks. Against the closest competitor DrugCLIP (Science 2026), KGOT gains +2.5% AUROC on DUD-E (83.45 vs. 80.93) and +5.3% AUROC on LIT-PCBA (62.45 vs. 57.17), with consistent improvements in early-recognition metrics (BEDROC, EF@0.5%). Against traditional docking methods, the advantage is substantial: KGOT exceeds Glide-SP by 6.8% AUROC on DUD-E and 9.3% on LIT-PCBA, and outperforms AutoDock Vina by 11.9% and 1.5% AUROC respectively. Deep learning baselines (Pafnucy, OnionNet, Planet, DeepDTA, BigBind) are all surpassed by wide margins, with Planet (71.60% AUROC on DUD-E) being the strongest non-contrastive baseline. For KG link prediction, pseudo-label augmentation yields consistent gains across all five embedding architectures (PairRE, RotatE, MuRE, TorusE, ComplEx-FF), with the largest relative improvement seen in ComplEx-FF (Hits@1: 30.8% → 43.6%, a 42% relative increase), while MuRE shows a slight Hits@1 drop (15.9% → 13.7%) but gains in Hits@3 and Hits@5.

## Relation to Prior Work

KGOT builds directly on the inverse optimal transport (IOT) formulation of Shi et al. (2023), which showed that contrastive learning (InfoNCE) is a special case of an OT-based scoring objective; here the IOT loss is repurposed not just for supervised training but as the engine for global pseudo-label generation over the full molecule-protein score matrix. On the knowledge-graph side, the paper departs from prior KG-enhanced MPI methods (KG-MTL, BioKDN) that treat the KG as a static auxiliary feature source or apply local denoising: KGOT instead writes OT-derived pseudo-interactions back into the KG as a new relation type and trains KGE models jointly on the augmented graph, closing a loop between score learning, pseudo-labeling, and KG completion. The molecular/protein backbone (Uni-Mol) and the virtual-screening evaluation protocol follow DrugCLIP (Jia et al., 2026), positioning KGOT as a direct successor in the cross-modal retrieval line.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, model-agnostic recipe for converting sparse MPI labels into dense pseudo-supervision while preserving global matching consistency (marginal constraints) that heuristic top-k or random pseudo-labeling cannot enforce; the consistent gains across five KGE architectures and two virtual-screening benchmarks suggest the approach is robust rather than architecture-specific. Open questions include whether uniform OT marginals are the right biological prior (the paper acknowledges this in Appendix E), how the method scales beyond the 10k×5k score matrix used in experiments, and whether the pseudo-label write-back strategy transfers to other interaction types (protein-protein, drug-drug). The MuRE degradation (Hits@1 dropping from 15.9% to 13.7%) hints that the pseudo-label signal is not universally beneficial, warranting further investigation into when and why the augmentation helps or hurts.
