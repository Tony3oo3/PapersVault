## Problem

Infrequent-communication distributed training strategies (e.g., Local SGD) reduce bandwidth overhead but suffer a persistent performance gap relative to fully synchronous DDP when applied to adaptive optimizers. The paper identifies the root cause as a timescale mismatch: the optimizer's fast-moving momentum (β₁≈0.9) decays too rapidly between infrequent synchronizations, leaving workers reliant on noisy local gradients and destroying the shared optimization trajectory. The central question is whether a distributed adaptive optimizer with momentum timescales suited to infrequent communication can close the DDP gap while providing convergence guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard model parameters, clipped stochastic gradients, per-momentum decay rates (β_{1,j}), convex combination weights (ω_j), and independent communication periods (K_x for parameters, K_j for each momentum, K_v for second momentum) alongside a learning-rate schedule. It produces trained model parameters with a provable O(1/√T) convergence rate for smooth non-convex objectives. Notably, the recommended N=1 quasi-hyperbolic variant adds zero memory overhead and only one additional hyperparameter (ω₁) over the base optimizer, while the general N-momentum form requires N additional first-momentum buffers and N additional hyperparameters.

## Method

MT-DAO composes the update direction as a convex combination of N slow-moving first momenta (each with its own decay rate β_{1,j} and independent synchronization period K_j) and the current gradient (receiving weight 1−Σω_j), implementing a quasi-hyperbolic structure that preserves long-term trajectory information while remaining responsive to local landscape changes. Each momentum state is synchronized with the global average at its own frequency, decoupled from parameter synchronization, so that slow momenta (β≈0.999) retain mutual information across communication rounds while fast components track recent gradients. The paper provides the first convergence analysis for multi-momentum distributed optimizers, deriving an O(1/√T) rate where the step-size constraint reveals a key trade-off: larger β_j values tighten the step-size bound via β_ω but reduce the communication penalty ψ, making slow momenta inherently more robust to infrequent synchronization. Empirically, the slow momentum acts as a regularizer that increases cosine similarity between worker pseudo-gradients, reduces inter-worker momentum variance, and lowers the rate of parameter change between syncs, collectively aligning worker trajectories and enabling MT-DAO to match or exceed DDP perplexity while communicating 10× less.

## Results

On 720M-parameter language models trained on SmolLM2, MT-DAO (β₁=0.999, ω₁=0.98) reaches a target validation perplexity in 24% fewer training steps and 35% less wall-clock time than the single-momentum ADOPT-DDP baseline, while communicating 10× less. Across model scales from 16M to 720M, MT-DAO reduces iso-token wall-clock time by 6–27% relative to DDP on 50–100 Gbit/s Ethernet interconnects. At the 1.3B scale with Adam, MT-DAO achieves higher aggregate zero-shot/few-shot accuracy than AdamW-DDP, excelling on reasoning-heavy benchmarks (ARC-Challenge, HellaSwag), and progressively closes the gap to QHAdamW-DDP as training continues.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MT-DAO consistently outperforms Local ADOPT (Cheng & Glasgow, 2025) in validation perplexity across all model scales (16M, 125M, 720M), closing the performance gap that prior infrequent-communication methods exhibit relative to DDP. Against the Nesterov outer optimizer baseline (Charles et al., 2025), MT-DAO achieves lower perplexity at all scales; Nesterov improves over Local ADOPT but remains below both MT-DAO and DDP, consistent with the finding that an outer momentum coalesces per-round gradients less finely than MT-DAO's inner multi-timescale modification. Relative to the DDP analogue QHADOPT-DDP (Ma & Yarats, 2019), MT-DAO trails at 16M, matches at 125M, and at 720M reaches the same target perplexity approximately 8% faster in wall-clock time and ~5% fewer tokens, with the time advantage amplified by 10× less communication. At the 1.3B scale, MT-DAO with Adam surpasses AdamW-DDP (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019) on aggregate downstream accuracy and on the most challenging reasoning tasks (ARC-Challenge, HellaSwag), while remaining competitive with QHAdamW-DDP early in training and closing the gap over the course of training. MT-DAO also outperforms ADOPT-DDP (Taniguchi et al., 2024) in both perplexity and wall-clock time at every scale tested, with the largest gains (35% time reduction) observed at 720M.

## Relation to Prior Work

MT-DAO sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: multi-momentum optimizers (AggMo, QHM, AdEMAMix) that were studied exclusively in centralized settings, and distributed infrequent-communication adaptive optimizers (Local SGD, Local Adam, DiLoCo, Des-LoC) that relied on single-timescale momenta. The paper's key conceptual contribution is diagnosing the persistent performance gap of infrequent-communication adaptive optimizers as a timescale mismatch between momentum half-life and communication period, then resolving it by transplanting the multi-momentum structure into the distributed regime with decoupled synchronization frequencies. This yields the first convergence guarantees for distributed multi-momentum optimizers and subsumes prior methods (Local Adam, DiLoCo with Nesterov outer optimizer, Des-LoC) as special cases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MT-DAO provides a drop-in modification (one extra hyperparameter, no additional memory in the N=1 quasi-hyperbolic variant) that closes the perplexity gap with DDP while communicating 10× less, translating to 6–27% wall-clock savings on Ethernet interconnects and enabling practical cross-datacenter and wide-area training. The convergence analysis for MT-DAO-SGDM is the first for any distributed multi-momentum optimizer, and the empirical validation spans six inner optimizers and scales from 16M to 1.3B. Open questions include extending guarantees to adaptive variants and streaming settings, validating on non-IID data and other modalities, and whether higher momentum counts (N>1) become necessary for very long communication intervals (K>512).
