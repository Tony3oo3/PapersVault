## Problem

In massively multimodal settings with dozens to hundreds of heterogeneous input streams, existing Mixture-of-Experts architectures route tokens based solely on static token-expert similarity, overlooking the rich temporal dependencies and delayed cross-modal effects (e.g., a drug's physiological impact manifesting hours later in a different sensor). This prevents experts from specializing on meaningful interaction types and limits the model's ability to capture time-evolving multimodal dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes heterogeneous multimodal time series where each sensor stream (IMU, ECG, SpO2, clinical notes, chest X-rays, audio, video, text) is treated as a distinct modality with its own sampling rate and noise profile. For sequence-level tasks (MIMIC-IV, MOSI), the target is a single static label per sequence while RUS is computed by sweeping lags backward from the sequence end; for per-timestep tasks (PAMAP2, WESAD, Opportunity), labels are dense. The RUS estimation is decoupled from model training and computed once per dataset, then cached and reused across downstream tasks.

## Method

MERGE has two core components: (1) a multi-scale BATCH estimator that computes temporal Redundancy-Uniqueness-Synergy (RUS) via directed information decomposition across multiple time lags, using lag-conditioned discriminators and Sinkhorn-Knopp alignment to find the optimal marginal-matching coupling Q*; and (2) an interaction-aware router that feeds temporal RUS sequences into the MoE gating—attention over pairwise R/S values, a GRU over uniqueness sequences, concatenated with token features through an MLP to produce routing logits. Three auxiliary losses (JSD-based for redundancy and uniqueness, probability-based for synergy) enforce the routing principle that redundant modalities share experts, unique modalities diversify, and synergistic pairs route to dedicated cross-attention synergy experts, making expert specialization principled and interpretable rather than emergent.

## Results

MERGE achieves the best or near-best results across 6 multimodal benchmarks spanning healthcare, activity recognition, and affective computing. Headline numbers include 91.37% accuracy on PAMAP2 (vs. 87.74% for the strongest baseline FuseMoE), 85.40% AUROC on MIMIC-IV in-hospital mortality (vs. 83.28% for I2MoE), 81.88% AUROC on MIMIC-IV length-of-stay, 55.74% accuracy on WESAD affect recognition, and 84.32% accuracy on Opportunity activity recognition. The only notable exception is MOSI sentiment classification, where FuseMoE (75.65% accuracy, 78.43% AUROC) outperforms MERGE (72.04%, 77.25%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MERGE is compared against six baselines: standard Transformer, mTAND (multi-time attention), MulT (cross-modal attention fusion), MISTS (hierarchical time-aware MoE), FuseMoE (Laplace-gated MoE for irregular modalities), and I2MoE (interaction-type-specific MoE). MERGE outperforms all baselines on PAMAP2 by a large margin (91.37% vs. 87.74% for FuseMoE, +3.63 points) and on MIMIC-IV IHM (85.40% AUROC vs. 83.28% for I2MoE, +2.12 points). On WESAD and Opportunity, MERGE leads FuseMoE by 1.82 and 1.17 accuracy points respectively. The one clear loss is on MOSI, where FuseMoE achieves 75.65% accuracy and 78.43% AUROC versus MERGE's 72.04% and 77.25%, a gap of roughly 3.6 points in accuracy. On MIMIC-IV LOS, MERGE edges out FuseMoE on AUROC (81.88 vs. 81.74) but trails slightly on F1 (74.43 vs. 75.18), making it a near-tie on that dataset. Overall, MERGE wins or ties on 5 of 6 datasets, with the MOSI gap suggesting that the temporal RUS routing strategy is less advantageous when the modality set is small (3 modalities) and the task is a single static label.

## Relation to Prior Work

MERGE sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) Partial Information Decomposition (PID), which quantifies multimodal interactions as redundancy, uniqueness, and synergy (Williams & Beer, 2010; Bertschinger et al., 2014), and (2) Mixture-of-Experts architectures for multimodal fusion (LIMoE, FuseMoE, I2MoE, Flex-MoE). The key departure is that prior MoE routers dispatch tokens based on static token-expert similarity, while MERGE extends PID to the temporal domain via directed information (Weissman et al., 2012) and uses the resulting multi-lag RUS sequences to explicitly guide which modality pairs should or should not share an expert. The multi-scale BATCH estimator (Liang et al., 2023) is repurposed to compute these temporal RUS values efficiently across many lags simultaneously, and the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm enforces the marginal-matching constraints that define the optimal coupling distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap: as real-world systems (ICUs, wearables, smart environments) accumulate dozens to hundreds of heterogeneous sensor streams, static similarity-based routing becomes insufficient for capturing delayed cross-modal effects. The decoupling of RUS estimation from MoE training is a practical design choice—RUS values are computed once per dataset and cached—making the approach scalable to many downstream tasks. Open questions include whether the framework extends to end-to-end joint learning of RUS and routing, whether it scales to LLM-MoE or VLM settings where modality counts and sequence lengths are far larger, and how sensitive performance is to RUS estimation errors in low-data or highly imbalanced regimes (the paper shows sparse RUS degrades performance substantially). The interpretability of routing patterns aligned with domain knowledge (e.g., insulin-furosemide synergy, chest-hand redundancy during locomotion) is a notable qualitative contribution for high-stakes applications like healthcare.
