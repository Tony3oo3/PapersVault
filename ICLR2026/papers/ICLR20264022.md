## Problem

Prior formal verification benchmarks such as DAFNYBENCH evaluate LLMs only on single-function annotation completion, leaving untested the compositional reasoning required when correctness must be established across multiple interacting functions with data dependencies. This paper asks whether frontier LLMs can generate specifications that propagate contracts across function boundaries in multi-function programs, and finds a dramatic collapse: models achieve >99% syntactic well-formedness yet near-zero end-to-end verification (best 2% at Pass@8) on compositional tasks.

## Input / Output

The input is a Dafny program of 2–5 functions arranged in an acyclic call graph (chain or DAG topology) in which all contract clauses (requires, ensures, reads, modifies, decreases) preceding each method's opening brace have been stripped, while the implementation body and any in-loop invariants remain intact. The model must regenerate the missing inter-function contracts so that the entire program passes Dafny 4.10.0's verifier end-to-end; success is binary (verified or not) and is computed only over syntactically parseable outputs. The benchmark comprises 400 mechanically verified programs: 300 chain-structured instances and 100 non-chain DAG instances drawn from 10 topology templates.

## Method

DAFNYCOMP is built through a two-stage synthesis pipeline. Stage 1 (program assembly) selects functions from LeetCodeDataset with cyclomatic complexity >5 and ≥10 lines, composes them into 2–5-function chains or DAGs with explicit data-flow dependencies, then validates each composition via static type-constraint propagation, deterministic formatting, and execution against intersected unit tests. Stage 2 (formal translation) incrementally converts the validated Python programs into Dafny using an AST-guided fragment-by-fragment approach with Claude-4-Sonnet, applying up to 10 verifier-feedback refinement iterations per program; 564 of 1,200 attempts yield verified Dafny code, from which 300 chain and 100 DAG programs are retained. Evaluation employs two test-time scaling protocols: independent Pass@k sampling (k ∈ {1,2,4,8}) and a verifier-in-the-loop multi-turn self-refinement loop (up to 3 turns) where the model receives its prior output plus the Dafny error message and must revise while preserving method signatures. A manual analysis of 900 failure cases identifies three dominant breakdown modes—specification fragility (39.2%), implementation-proof misalignment (21.7%), and reasoning instability (14.1%)—that collectively explain why local syntactic competence does not compose into global verification success.

## Results

On the DAFNYCOMP chain split (300 multi-function Dafny programs), all 11 evaluated frontier LLMs exhibit a dramatic Syntax-Verified gap: at Pass@8, the average Syntax Correct Rate is 94.36% (SD=4.86%) while the average Verified Rate is only 0.55% (SD=0.58%), yielding a 93.82% gap. The best-performing model, GEMINI-2.5-PRO, reaches just 2.00% end-to-end verification at Pass@8, with most models below 1%. This contrasts sharply with prior single-function Dafny benchmarks where the same models achieve >99% syntax correctness and >58% verified success. Under verifier-in-the-loop multi-turn refinement (up to 3 turns), the best models improve to ~9.67% verified rate on the chain split, but success remains far from saturation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All 11 evaluated models—spanning OpenAI (GPT-4O, GPT-4.1, O4-MINI), Google (GEMINI-2.5-PRO/FLASH), DeepSeek (R1, V3, V3.1), and Alibaba (QWEN3-CODER-480B, QWEN2.5-CODER-32B, QwQ-32B)—fail to achieve meaningful compositional verification, with verified rates clustered in the 0–2% range at Pass@8. GEMINI-2.5-PRO is the sole model to reach 2% verified success at Pass@8, marginally ahead of QWEN3-CODER-480B (1.0%) and O4-MINI/DEEPSEEK-V3.1 (0.67% each); GPT-4O and DEEPSEEK-R1 trail at 0.33%. Reasoning-specialized models (QwQ-32B, DEEPSEEK-R1) show no clear advantage over general-purpose counterparts, with QwQ-32B achieving 0% verification at all k values. The universal collapse contrasts with prior single-function Dafny benchmarks (e.g., DAFNYBENCH) where these same models achieve >58% verified success, confirming that the bottleneck is compositional contract propagation rather than surface-level syntax. Under multi-turn verifier-in-the-loop refinement, DEEPSEEK-R1 and O4-MINI reach 9.67% verified rate at turn 3 on the chain split, but the DAG extension yields lower rates (1–7%), indicating that broader topologies further compound the difficulty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-function Dafny annotation benchmark (DAFNYBENCH, Loughridge et al. 2024) to multi-function compositional programs, adopting the chain-based assembly strategy from Hu et al. (2025) and the incremental AST-guided translation pipeline from Wen et al. (2024). The key conceptual departure is shifting the evaluation target from local annotation completion within isolated methods to cross-function contract propagation, where a caller's postcondition must imply a callee's precondition. It also connects to the broader compositional-reasoning literature (Dziri et al. 2023; Li et al. 2024) by instantiating that challenge in the stricter setting of formal verification, where specifications must be semantically precise across all execution paths rather than merely plausible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a stark and well-evidenced gap: frontier LLMs that achieve >58% verified success on single-function Dafny tasks collapse to ≤2% on compositional programs, identifying specification fragility, implementation-proof misalignment, and reasoning instability as the three dominant failure modes. This reframes the 'specification bottleneck' as fundamentally a compositional-reasoning problem rather than a local annotation problem, with implications for any system requiring modular verification (distributed protocols, smart contracts, safety-critical software). Open questions include whether cyclic call graphs, mutual recursion, and liveness/resource-bounded properties further widen the gap, and whether the current scarcity of verified multi-function training data is a primary bottleneck that synthetic data generation could address.
