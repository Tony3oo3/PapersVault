## Problem

Existing end-to-end autonomous driving systems follow a sequential perception→prediction→planning paradigm with dense BEV features, causing information loss, cumulative errors, and restricted task-relation modeling. The quadratic-complexity attention in Transformer decoders further limits scalability for long-range spatiotemporal inputs, making it difficult to scale up both model size and input resolution without prohibitive memory costs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265179/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes 6-camera surround-view images (resolutions from 256×704 to 512×1408 depending on variant), canbus ego-vehicle status encoded via MLP, and a FIFO memory queue of Top-K=256 history task queries spanning 4 frames (2 seconds). Outputs include 3D bounding boxes with classes, vectorized map instances (20 points each with class labels), multi-modal agent trajectories with probabilities, a 6-waypoint ego trajectory over 3 seconds at 2 Hz, and point-level depth maps used for 3D token positioning. Notably, all representations are sparse token-level rather than dense BEV grids, and the ego query is initialized from canbus signals rather than learned from scratch.

## Method

DriveMamba replaces the sequential Transformer decoder with a unified Mamba decoder containing three types of bidirectional SSM (B-Mamba) layers operating in parallel: View Correspondence Learning (task queries extract semantics from raw sensor tokens via 3D-position-sorted linear attention), Task Relation Modeling (dynamic inter-task dependencies without manual ordering), and Long-term Temporal Fusion (FIFO memory queue with motion-aware layer normalization). A hybrid spatiotemporal scan strategy assigns different bidirectional serialization orders to each layer type: Horizontal-Vertical alternation for view correspondence, a trajectory-guided Local-to-Global spiral for task relation modeling (where token importance is weighted by proximity to predicted future waypoints), and Spatial-First for temporal fusion. Sensor tokens are positioned in 3D space via a dedicated depth prediction branch rather than uniform ray-level embeddings, enabling reliable spatial sorting. The entire system is trained single-stage end-to-end with dense supervision at every decoder layer, and scales from 40M to 607M parameters through simple stacking of B-Mamba layers, achieving 3.2× speedup and 68.8% less GPU memory compared to Transformer-based counterparts at equivalent resolution.

## Results

On nuScenes open-loop planning (ST-P3 metric), DriveMamba-Tiny achieves 0.44 m average L2 error and 0.15% average collision rate at 17.9 FPS, reducing L2 by 42.1% over UniAD (0.76 m) and 38.9% over VAD (0.72 m). With ego-status input, DriveMamba-Large reaches 0.33 m L2 and 0.06% collision, matching DriveTransformer-Large (0.33 m) while using 6.1× fewer parameters. On Bench2Drive closed-loop evaluation, DriveMamba-Base achieves 65.50 Driving Score and 36.82% Success Rate, surpassing DriveTransformer-Large (63.46 DS, 35.01% SR) with 3.7× lower latency (164.3 ms vs. 599.1 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DriveMamba-Tiny outperforms UniAD by 42.1% in average L2 (0.44 vs. 0.76 m) and VAD by 38.9% (0.44 vs. 0.72 m) without ego-status, while also achieving 10× and 4× higher FPS respectively. Against the strongest sparse-query baseline DriveTransformer-Large (†), DriveMamba-Large (†) matches its 0.33 m L2 and slightly improves collision rate (0.06% vs. 0.07%) at 3.7× lower latency. DriveMamba-Tiny (†) beats ParaDrive (†) by 20.8% in L2 (0.38 vs. 0.48 m) and 48% in collision (0.13% vs. 0.25%), but trails SparseDrive-B (†) on collision rate (0.13% vs. 0.06%) despite a 34.5% L2 advantage (0.38 vs. 0.58 m). In closed-loop Bench2Drive, DriveMamba-Base surpasses DriveTransformer-Large in Driving Score (65.50 vs. 63.46) and Efficiency (158.33 vs. 100.64) with 3.7× less latency, while DriveMamba-Tiny achieves 53.54 DS at only 55.8 ms—faster than all Transformer-based baselines. The paper also reports that DriveMamba outperforms all baselines on middle-task perception (detection mAP 50.8, mapping mAP 64.8, motion minADE 0.55 m) in Table 14.

## Relation to Prior Work

DriveMamba extends the Mamba/Vision Mamba line of work (selective state space models with bidirectional scanning) from 2D image understanding to end-to-end autonomous driving, replacing the quadratic-complexity Transformer decoders used in UniAD, VAD, and DriveTransformer with a pure linear-complexity SSM decoder. It departs from the dominant sequential perception→prediction→planning paradigm and dense BEV feature construction by adopting a fully sparse, task-centric token formulation with a FIFO memory queue inherited from StreamPETR for temporal fusion. The trajectory-guided 'Local-to-Global' scan is a novel geometric prior not present in prior Transformer-based planners, which rely on global uniform self-attention without ego-centric interaction ordering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.2× speedup and 68.8% GPU memory reduction over Transformer-based E2E planners make long-horizon, high-resolution driving sequences tractable, and the 17.9 FPS of DriveMamba-Tiny is practically deployable on automotive hardware. The finding that a pure SSM decoder (no attention at all) matches or exceeds Transformer-based planners on both open-loop and closed-loop benchmarks suggests SSMs are a viable alternative backbone for scalable driving models. However, the authors acknowledge that the excessive entanglement of the unified framework constrains debugging and flexible problem localization, and the performance gap over DriveTransformer-Large in closed-loop driving score (66.82 vs. 63.46) is modest, leaving open whether further scaling of the Mamba decoder will widen this advantage.
