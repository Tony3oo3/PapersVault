## Problem

VLA models built on 2D-pretrained VLMs lack the spatial awareness needed for precise 3D robotic manipulation. Existing remedies either inject explicit 3D sensor data (depth maps, point clouds) that suffer from noise, hardware heterogeneity, and incomplete dataset coverage, or rely on depth estimators whose accuracy caps the VLA's performance. The paper asks how to implicitly endow VLAs with 3D comprehension without any explicit 3D input or depth estimator at inference time.

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes multi-view RGB images (primary and wrist cameras) and a text instruction, and additionally feeds the same images through a frozen VGGT to produce pixel-level spatial supervision signals. At inference time, the model takes only the standard multi-view RGB images and text instruction and outputs a continuous robot action sequence via the base VLA's action expert (e.g., flow-matching head or MLP), introducing zero additional structures or computational overhead. The alignment operates at the per-patch (per-pixel) level, matching each VLA visual token to the VGGT spatial representation at the corresponding spatial location, with extra positional embeddings appended to preserve token order in the autoregressive sequence.

## Method

Spatial Forcing (SF) introduces a cosine-similarity alignment loss between the VLA's intermediate visual embeddings (extracted at the 24th of 32 transformer layers) and the latent spatial representations produced by a frozen VGGT 3D foundation model processing the same multi-view images. The VLA tokens are projected through batch normalization and a two-layer MLP for dimensional compatibility before alignment, and the total training objective is L_action + α·L_align (α=0.5). The key design choices are: (1) supervising relatively deep but not the deepest layers, since the last layers lose vision-specific features as modalities converge into a modality-agnostic space; (2) adding positional embeddings to the VGGT target to preserve spatial ordering in the autoregressive context; and (3) using VGGT's global alternating-attention backbone to yield scene-consistent 3D representations rather than per-frame estimates. A depth-probing experiment (freezing the VLA and training only a DPT head) confirms that unaligned visual embeddings lack meaningful spatial structure, while SF-aligned embeddings recover rich 3D geometry, and t-SNE visualizations show the aligned features adopt the target's relational manifold without collapsing into it.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, Spatial Forcing (SF) achieves a 98.5% average success rate across four task suites (Spatial 99.4%, Object 99.6%, Goal 98.8%, Long 96.0%), setting a new state-of-the-art without requiring any explicit 3D sensor inputs. On the RoboTwin 2.0 bimanual benchmark, SF achieves the highest average success rate, yielding substantial improvements over the base model π₀ across all tasks, with particularly large gains on hard (domain-randomized) tasks. SF also accelerates training by 3.8× and reaches 75.8% success rate using only 5% of the training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SF outperforms the strongest 2D VLA baseline, OpenVLA-OFT (97.1% avg), by 1.4 points on LIBERO, and surpasses the best explicit-3D VLA, 3D-CAVLA (98.1%), by 0.4 points—all without any additional depth or point-cloud sensor input. Against GeoVLA (97.7%), which fuses point-cloud features via a dedicated Point Embedding Network, SF gains 0.8 points while using only 2D RGB images. The gap over the π₀ base model (94.2%) is 4.3 points, and over SpatialVLA (78.1%), which relies on estimated depth, the margin is 20.4 points. On RoboTwin, SF consistently outperforms π₀ across all easy and hard tasks, with the largest gains on domain-randomized (hard) settings, indicating that SF captures genuine spatial relationships rather than shortcut correlations. In real-world experiments, SF improves success rates by up to 47.5% over the base model on the stack-glass-cups task and achieves 32.5% vs. 27.5% (3D-CAVLA) and 12.5% (OpenVLA-OFT) on the complex Transfer-Block-from-Cardboard task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the representation alignment paradigm (REPA, Geometry Forcing, 3DRS) from image/video generation and VQA to the VLA domain, using VGGT's latent spatial representations as the alignment target. It departs from the dominant 3D-VLA paradigm (GeoVLA, 3D-CAVLA, SpatialVLA) that injects explicit 3D sensor data or depth-estimator outputs into the input pipeline, instead supervising intermediate visual embeddings via cosine similarity. The base models (OpenVLA-OFT, π₀) and the VGGT 3D foundation model are used as-is; the contribution is the alignment loss and the empirical finding that mid-depth layers (24th of 32) are optimal for supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for depth cameras, point clouds, or depth estimators, SF removes a major practical bottleneck for deploying VLAs across heterogeneous robot hardware and existing datasets lacking 3D annotations. The 3.8× training speedup and strong data efficiency (75.8% SR with 5% data) are practically significant given the scarcity of real-world robot demonstrations. Open questions include whether implicit alignment suffices for tasks requiring precise metric depth (e.g., insertion, force-controlled manipulation), how the method scales to multi-embodiment or long-horizon settings beyond current benchmarks, and whether the VGGT dependency can be replaced by lighter-weight 3D priors.
