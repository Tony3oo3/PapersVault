## Problem

Existing interpretability methods for detecting anomalous behaviors in LLMs (e.g., SAEs, PCA, supervised probes) operate on activations computed from a fixed dataset and therefore require distributionally similar data to the anomaly of interest. This is a fundamental limitation when the fine-tuning data is proprietary or the threat (e.g., a backdoor) is by definition out-of-distribution. The paper asks whether open-weight models can be monitored and controlled without access to their training distribution.

## Input / Output

The method requires the weights of both a base model and its fine-tuned counterpart to compute the weight difference ΔW, plus a calibration corpus of realistic user traffic (e.g., WildChat, UltraChat) to establish per-direction normal cosine-similarity ranges. At inference, each incoming text prompt is processed token-by-token; the system outputs a binary anomaly flag per input and, in steering mode, produces modified (orthogonalized) activations that suppress the flagged behavioral direction. The behavioral vectors themselves—top-k left singular vectors of ΔW for each layer's attention output projection and MLP down-projection—are the reusable interpretive artifact, with k=20 yielding 40 directions per layer.

## Method

WEIGHTWATCH computes the weight difference ΔW = W_post − W_base for each linear layer that directly updates the residual stream (attention O_proj and MLP W_down), then performs SVD on each ΔW and extracts the top-k left singular vectors as 'behavioral vectors' that encode the principal directions of fine-tuning-induced change. During inference, the method monitors the cosine similarity between each token's activation and every behavioral vector, maintaining a streaming min/max range calibrated on benign traffic; an input is flagged anomalous if any token's similarity falls outside the current range. For steering, flagged activations are orthogonalized against the offending direction (a ← a − (a·u)u) before being passed to subsequent layers, effectively suppressing the fine-tuned behavior. A theoretical bound shows the false positive rate decreases as 2t/n + O(1/n²) with calibration sample count n and number of monitored directions t, and the entire pipeline is data-free with respect to the fine-tuning distribution.

## Results

WEIGHTWATCH flags 93%–100% of backdoor-triggered completions on first sight across LoRA, full-parameter fine-tuning, and poisoned PPO models, while maintaining a false positive rate below 1% on both calibration data and MMLU. For unlearning verification, detection rates range from 36.21% (Zephyr-RMU) to 95.42% (Circuit Breaker) with FPR ≤ 1.79%. In steering experiments, the method recovers 76.92% of WMDP-Bio and 38.61% of WMDP-Cyber performance on Zephyr-RMU, and when combined with a prompt prefix, raises the Circuit Breaker model's HarmBench attack success rate from 9.6% to 82.08%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is BEAT (Yi et al., 2025), a state-of-the-art black-box backdoor detection method. At a 2% FPR operating point, WEIGHTWATCH achieves 98.72%–100% TPR across all five PPO trojan models, while BEAT achieves only 0.32%–31.95% TPR — a gap of 67–99 percentage points. Even when BEAT's FPR budget is relaxed to 10%, it still trails WEIGHTWATCH on trojan2 (1.92% vs. 100%) and trojan4 (26.84% vs. 100%), and only approaches parity on trojan3 (97.76% vs. 100%). The activation-difference-norm baseline is more competitive on trojan1 (97.44%) and trojan2 (96.49%) but degrades sharply on trojan3 (11.50%) and trojan5 (19.81%), showing inconsistent performance. The paper also notes that activation-based methods like PCA and SAEs are fundamentally unsuitable for this setting because they require labeled backdoor examples during training, which are unavailable by definition for unknown backdoors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the weight-difference (task arithmetic) framework of Ilharco et al. (2023) and the mechanistic observation of Jain et al. (2024) that safety fine-tuning is concentrated in top singular vectors of ΔW, extending both from model editing and safety-specific analysis to a general, data-free monitoring and control procedure. It departs from the dominant activation-based interpretability paradigm (SAEs, PCA, probing) by operating entirely on model parameters, thereby eliminating the need for distributionally similar calibration data that those methods require. The steering component connects to representation engineering (Zou et al., 2023) but discovers steering directions unsupervised from weights rather than from labeled activation contrasts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With thousands of open-weight fine-tunes available but training data typically proprietary, WEIGHTWATCH offers a practical, lightweight auditing tool that requires only base and fine-tuned weights plus a small calibration set of generic traffic. Key limitations include the assumption of base-model access, vulnerability to dimension-shuffling evasion, and the linear FPR bound that necessitates sufficient calibration samples. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to models without a known base, its adversarial robustness, and whether the weight-difference signal degrades for very large or multi-stage fine-tuning pipelines. The dual-use nature—enabling both defense and jailbreaking (82% ASR on circuit breaker)—raises important deployment and governance questions.
