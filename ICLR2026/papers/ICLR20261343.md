## Problem

Existing hallucination mitigation methods for LVLMs are either retraining-based (requiring substantial data and compute) or contrastive-decoding-based (incurring dual inference overhead and uniformly adjusting all tokens regardless of hallucination risk). No prior approach can selectively edit hallucination-prone intermediate representations in a single forward pass while also offering controllable hallucination levels for diverse use cases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261343/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on an RGB image and text prompt fed into a frozen LVLM (LLaVA-1.5 or InstructBLIP). During inference, it intercepts attention-layer hidden states at every transformer layer, applies a per-token binary edit decision from the Router (based on first-layer representations), and produces hallucination-reduced text captions. A scalar hyperparameter α ∈ [-1, 1] controls both the direction (suppress vs. amplify) and intensity of the edit, enabling flexible hallucination control without retraining.

## Method

HIRE consists of two lightweight modules (~0.05B parameters) that operate on frozen LVLM intermediate representations without modifying model weights. The Editor is a dual-encoder autoencoder with a semantic encoder and a hallucinatory encoder, trained via InfoNCE contrastive learning to disentangle semantic content from hallucination signals; it computes a layer-level hallucination-reduction direction δ_l from the divergence between authentic and visually-degraded representations, then produces token-specific editing vectors Δ_tl through attention-based fusion and decoding. The Router is a single MLP that takes first-layer transformer representations and outputs a binary edit/no-edit decision per token, trained via reference-free DPO on preference pairs ranked by CHAIR score. At inference, only tokens flagged by the Router are edited across all subsequent layers, reducing computational overhead by ~30% compared to uniform editing while maintaining hallucination suppression performance.

## Results

On the CHAIR benchmark (max new tokens = 512), HIRE reduces sentence-level hallucination (CHAIR_S) from 51.3 to 30.2 and instance-level hallucination (CHAIR_I) from 16.8 to 9.7 on LLaVA-1.5, representing ~40% and ~42% reductions respectively, while adding only 1.58 TFLOPs of overhead (11.81 vs. 10.23 baseline). On InstructBLIP, it achieves CHAIR_S=39.0 and CHAIR_I=11.5. On the POPE benchmark, HIRE reaches 87.27% accuracy and 87.23 F1 (ALL setting) on LLaVA-1.5, and on AMBER it scores 89.12, both surpassing the prior best method Octopus (85.79/83.44 and 88.95 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HIRE outperforms all ten baselines on both LLaVA-1.5 and InstructBLIP across CHAIR, POPE, and AMBER. Against the strongest CD-based method Octopus, HIRE reduces CHAIR_S by 9.0 points (30.2 vs. 39.2) and CHAIR_I by 1.4 points (9.7 vs. 11.1) on LLaVA-1.5, while using roughly half the inference cost (11.81 vs. 21.39 TFLOPs). Compared to VCD, M3ID, and AVISC (all requiring dual forward passes at ~20 TFLOPs), HIRE achieves 16–18 point lower CHAIR_S at less than half the computational cost. Against the steering-based methods Projectaway and VTI, HIRE improves CHAIR_S by 11.8 and 5.6 points respectively on LLaVA-1.5. On POPE (ALL), HIRE gains +1.48 accuracy and +3.79 F1 over Octopus on LLaVA-1.5. In the short-description setting (max tokens = 64), HIRE still beats Nullu (CHAIR_S 15.2 vs. 17.0) and M3ID+DPO (15.2 vs. 13.5 on CHAIR_I), confirming consistent gains across generation lengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

HIRE adapts the dual-encoder autoencoder architecture from TruthX (Zhang et al., 2024), which edits LLM representations in a 'truthful space,' to the multimodal LVLM setting, and pairs it with a DPO-trained Router for selective token editing. It departs from the two dominant hallucination-mitigation paradigms—retraining (HA-DPO, HDPO) and contrastive decoding (VCD, SID, ICD, M3ID)—by operating entirely in the intermediate representation space during a single forward pass, avoiding both the training cost of retraining and the dual-inference overhead of CD. The approach is further motivated by prior findings (Azaria & Mitchell, 2023; Li et al., 2024) that hallucinated and truthful representations are linearly separable in latent space, which HIRE exploits to construct an explicit editing direction rather than relying on probability-level contrast.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HIRE offers a practical, single-pass hallucination mitigation method with a lightweight 0.05B-parameter module that can be attached to any LVLM without weight modification, and its α hyperparameter enables bidirectional control (suppress or amplify hallucination), which is valuable for applications like creative writing where some hallucination is desirable. The paper demonstrates strong generalization across model families (LLaVA, InstructBLIP, Qwen-2.5-VL, TinyLLaVA) and shows composability with existing steering methods (Projectaway, Nullu). A key open question is whether selective training on the most critical hidden states (rather than all tokens and layers) can further improve data efficiency and reduce noise sensitivity, a direction the authors explicitly flag for future work.
