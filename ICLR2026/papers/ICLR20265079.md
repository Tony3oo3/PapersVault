## Problem

Compound AI systems—DAGs of heterogeneous components like LLMs, retrievers, and model selectors—are increasingly deployed but remain difficult to optimize end-to-end due to non-differentiable structures, mixed configuration types (prompts, weights, hyperparameters), and the high cost of full-system runs for global reward estimation. Prior methods optimize individual configuration types in isolation, failing to capture cross-component interactions and often degrading performance on some tasks while improving others.

## Input / Output

The method takes a compound AI system specified as a directed acyclic graph with heterogeneous components, a training dataset of input instances, a user-defined global reward function, and initial configuration policies for each component. It iteratively produces updated configuration policies (optimized prompts, fine-tuned model weights, tuned hyperparameters, and model selections) along with adapted local reward functions. The LRFs are implemented as a shared Llama 3 8B backbone with LoRA (rank 32) and separate linear projection heads per component, trained with a pairwise log-sigmoid ranking loss on preference pairs collected via Monte Carlo downstream execution.

## Method

OPTIMAS learns one Local Reward Function (LRF) per component, each trained to satisfy a local-global alignment property: the LRF's ranking of candidate outputs must correlate with the expected global reward conditioned on that output. A lightweight online adaptation mechanism updates LRFs with small mini-batches of preference data whenever any component's configuration changes, avoiding expensive full retraining. Each component is then optimized locally using a method matched to its configuration type—PPO for trainable model parameters, OPRO-style prompt search for textual prompts, and probabilistic sampling for discrete model selection or low-dimensional hyperparameters. The paper proves that under mild regularity conditions, the minimizer of the ranking loss satisfies the alignment property and that the iterative algorithm converges to a component-wise maximum, effectively performing coordinate maximization on the global objective.

## Results

OPTIMAS achieves an average relative improvement of 11.92% over the best baseline across five compound AI systems. The largest gains are on STARK-PRIME (50.54 MRR vs. 41.40 for DSPy, +22.1%) and AMAZON (24.24% accuracy vs. 21.89% for REINFORCE, +14.3%). On HOTPOTQA, OPTIMAS reaches 50.48 F1 (vs. 44.90 for DSPy, +12.4%), and on BigCodeBench it achieves 38.92% pass rate (vs. 36.67% unoptimized, +9.0%). OPTIMAS is the only method that improves performance across all five tasks simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPTIMAS outperforms all five baselines on every task where they overlap. Against DSPy (Khattab et al., 2023), which is the strongest general-purpose baseline, OPTIMAS gains +22.1% MRR on STARK-PRIME (50.54 vs. 41.40), +12.4% F1 on HotpotQA (50.48 vs. 44.90), and +6.06% accuracy on AMAZON (24.24 vs. 18.18); notably, DSPy actually degrades performance on AMAZON below the unoptimized baseline. Against TextGrad (Yuksekgonul et al., 2025), OPTIMAS leads by 3.36 points on AMAZON, 12.17 on STARK-PRIME, 25.62 on HotpotQA, and 3.21 on BigCodeBench. REINFORCE (Williams, 1992) is only applicable to AMAZON (where trainable local models exist) and trails OPTIMAS by 2.35 points (21.89 vs. 24.24) while requiring 3× more data. LLMSelector (Chen et al., 2025b) is limited to PUBMEDQA and is outperformed by 1.2 points (67.93 vs. 69.13). HBC (Le et al., 2018) is consistently the weakest baseline, underperforming even the unoptimized system on STARK-PRIME and BigCodeBench.

## Relation to Prior Work

OPTIMAS unifies and extends several partially overlapping lines of work: prompt optimization (OPRO, DSPy, TextGrad), model selection (LLMSelector), and process reward modeling for multi-step tasks. Prior methods each handle a single configuration type in isolation, while OPTIMAS introduces a shared LRF architecture with per-component projection heads that simultaneously optimizes heterogeneous configurations (textual prompts, continuous model parameters via PPO, discrete model selection, and scalar hyperparameters) within one framework. The convergence guarantee is grounded in Tseng's (2001) block coordinate descent theory, reframing the local optimization loop as coordinate maximization over a heterogeneous objective. The preference-based LRF training (pairwise log-sigmoid ranking loss) draws on the Bradley-Terry / DPO lineage but applies it to dynamic, configuration-dependent alignment rather than static reward modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As compound AI systems (LLM + tools + retrievers + ML models) become the dominant deployment pattern, a general, data-efficient optimization framework with convergence guarantees addresses a critical practical gap. The finding that baselines like DSPy can *degrade* performance on some tasks while OPTIMAS consistently improves across all five benchmarks highlights the risk of optimizing components in isolation. Open questions include scalability to much larger systems with dynamic topologies, whether the non-convex block-coordinate limitation becomes a practical bottleneck, and how the framework handles adversarial or safety-sensitive global objectives. The paper's acknowledgment that global optimality requires additional structural assumptions (PL/KL conditions) leaves room for future theoretical work.
