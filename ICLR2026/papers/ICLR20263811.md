## Problem

Video Class-Incremental Learning (VCIL) requires models to continuously acquire new action categories while preserving previously learned knowledge, but existing approaches either store exemplars (raising memory and privacy concerns) or naively transplant image-based CIL techniques that flatten or underutilize temporal structure. The core gap is the lack of a unified, exemplar-free framework that explicitly disentangles frame-shared spatial semantics from temporal dynamics to simultaneously mitigate catastrophic forgetting and capture motion patterns.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 8 uniformly sampled frames per video (via TSN strategy) processed through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone with lightweight adapters, alongside CLIP text embeddings for all known class labels. At inference, no task ID or stored exemplar is required; the final video representation is a residual sum of the spatial frame-mean feature and expert-weighted spatiotemporal features, which is matched against text embeddings via cosine similarity to yield an action class label. The spatiotemporal encoder uses a 3-layer, 2-head multi-head self-attention module operating on the [CLS] token concatenated with frame features.

## Method

StPR builds on a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 with per-task adapter modules for spatial features and task-specific spatiotemporal encoders (experts) for temporal modeling. The first component, Frame-Shared Semantics Distillation (FSSD), computes a per-channel importance score I_{c,j} = T_{c,j}·μ_{c,j}/σ²_{c,j} by combining Fisher Information (semantic sensitivity, inversely proportional to cross-frame variance) with cosine-aligned classification contribution against text features, then applies this as a weighted L2 distillation loss between the frozen previous-task model and the current model to selectively preserve critical spatial channels. The second component, Temporal Decomposition-based Mixture-of-Experts (TD-MoE), extracts a decoupled temporal representation V^{tem} = V^{st} − V̄^s (the deviation of attention-weighted spatiotemporal features from the uniform spatial mean), and routes inputs to experts via cosine similarity between V^{tem} and precomputed class-level temporal anchors, enabling task-ID-free dynamic expert weighting. Training optimizes a symmetric video-text contrastive loss on both spatial and spatiotemporal features plus the FSSD regularization term, with only adapters and spatiotemporal encoders updated (≈10.6M trainable parameters out of 86M backbone).

## Results

On the TCD benchmark, StPR achieves 94.67% average accuracy on UCF101 (10×5s) and 68.12% on HMDB51 (5×5s), surpassing the best exemplar-free baseline ST-Prompt† (84.75% and 60.14%) by 9.9 and 8.0 points respectively. On SSv2, StPR reaches 40.79% (10×9s) and 37.30% (5×18s), competitive with the exemplar-based CoSTEO (41.44% and 36.60%). On Kinetics400 (vCLIMB), StPR attains the highest final accuracy of 57.83% (10-task) and 53.95% (20-task), outperforming CSTA (56.09% and 52.20%), though with higher backward forgetting (14.01% vs. 4.97%) due to the exemplar-free constraint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StPR dominates all exemplar-free baselines on UCF101 and HMDB51: it outperforms ST-Prompt† (the strongest exemplar-free method) by 9.9 points on UCF101 (10×5s) and 8.0 points on HMDB51 (5×5s), and exceeds the exemplar-based CoSTEO by 8.6 and 6.4 points respectively. On SSv2, StPR (40.79%) narrowly trails CoSTEO (41.44%) in the 10×9s setting but surpasses it in the 5×18s setting (37.30% vs. 36.60%), while clearly beating ST-Prompt† (39.98% and 35.44%). On Kinetics400, StPR achieves the highest final accuracy (57.83% at 10 tasks, 53.95% at 20 tasks) over CSTA (56.09%, 52.20%) and all exemplar-based methods, though its backward forgetting (14.01%, 15.09%) is higher than exemplar-based counterparts (e.g., SMILE+BiC at 6.25%, CSTA at 4.97%), reflecting the inherent trade-off of operating without stored samples. Against gradient-based regularization (EWC), FSSD consistently yields 1.2–2.5 points higher accuracy across UCF101 and HMDB51 splits, confirming the advantage of semantic-importance-aware distillation over parameter-level Fisher approximation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CLIP frozen-encoder + adapter paradigm (AdaptFormer) for parameter-efficient video understanding, but departs from prior VCIL work by explicitly decomposing the spatiotemporal representation into two functionally distinct channels: frame-shared spatial semantics (preserved via a Fisher-information-weighted distillation loss, FSSD) and temporal dynamics (exploited for task-ID-free expert routing, TD-MoE). Unlike exemplar-based methods (TCD, FrameMaker, HCE) that replay stored clips, or image-CIL methods (L2P, S-iPrompts, ST-Prompt) that flatten temporal structure, StPR treats spatial stability and temporal plasticity as separable concerns, each addressed by a dedicated mechanism. The FSSD loss also contrasts with parameter-level regularization (EWC) by computing importance analytically from feature statistics aligned with text embeddings, avoiding backpropagation-based Fisher estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Exemplar-free VCIL is practically important for privacy-sensitive and memory-constrained deployments (surveillance, edge robotics), and this paper shows that explicit spatiotemporal disentanglement can close much of the gap to exemplar-based methods on UCF101 and HMDB51. However, on Kinetics400 the backward forgetting (14–15%) remains substantially higher than exemplar-based baselines (5–7%), and SSv2 gains over the strongest exemplar-free baseline (ST-Prompt†) are modest, suggesting the decomposition is most effective when temporal cues are discriminative. Open questions include whether the per-task expert pool scales gracefully to very long task sequences, whether temporal anchors can be compressed or learned jointly, and whether the framework extends to open-world or streaming settings where class boundaries are unknown.
