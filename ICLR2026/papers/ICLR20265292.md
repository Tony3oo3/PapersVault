## Problem

GPT-style next-token prediction has revolutionized NLP, but language sequences cannot capture the rich spatial-temporal details of the visual world. Existing video diffusion models excel at short-term generation but struggle with long-term prediction, while autoregressive video models handle long contexts but underperform diffusion in generation quality. No prior work unifies the self-supervised sequential modeling of GPT with the sharp synthesis quality of diffusion at the video clip level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265292/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pretraining, a raw video is uniformly sampled into N frames, randomly partitioned into K clips, and encoded via SDXL VAE into latent patch tokens. Each clip is formatted with special tokens: clean clips use <img> and </img> boundary tokens around the latent, while noisy clips prepend a <diff> hint token and a scalar noise level α_k. The model receives an interleaved sequence [NS(1), CL(1), NS(2), CL(2), …, NS(K)] and outputs denoised latent features for each noisy clip, trained with L2 loss against the original clean latents. For downstream tasks, the same architecture accepts text prompts (via SDv2 text encoder), class labels, or a single conditioning frame as extra tokens with causal attention to clip tokens.

## Method

Video-GPT treats each video clip as a 'word' in a visual language and introduces next clip diffusion: the model autoregressively denoises the next noisy clip conditioned on all previous *clean* clips, rather than noisy ones as in prior diffusion-forcing approaches. The architecture is a vanilla transformer (Phi-3-mini backbone) with a three-level hierarchical attention mask — clip-level (noisy clip k attends to clean clips 1…k−1), frame-level (noisy frames attend bidirectionally within their clip, clean frames attend causally), and patch-level (full spatial attention within each frame's patch tokens). Diffusion uses flow matching with a single shared noise level α_k per clip, and the model predicts the clean latent directly rather than noise or velocity. Training is fully self-supervised on 70M unlabeled videos (Panda-70M) with a progressive strategy that increases frame count from 16 to 80 and clip length from 1 to 48 over four stages. At inference, the model iteratively denoises each new clip using all previously generated clips as context with history-conditioned classifier-free guidance (scale c=3), enabling autoregressive video prediction up to one minute long.

## Results

On the Physics-IQ Benchmark, Video-GPT achieves a Phys. IQ Score of 34.97, surpassing the second-best method (VideoPoet at 29.50) by more than 5 points and the strongest closed-source models (Kling 1.6 at 23.64, Gen3 at 22.80, Sora at 10.00) by a wide margin. On Kinetics-600, Video-GPT attains the best FVD(500) of 315.40 and FVD(5000) of 89.44 among all vanilla-transformer-architecture models. After fine-tuning, Video-GPT also achieves state-of-the-art FVD of 53 on UCF-101 class-to-video generation and 58.9% top-1 accuracy on UCF-101 video classification, outperforming VideoMAEv2 (56.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Video-GPT decisively outperforms all 15 compared baselines on the Physics-IQ Benchmark, scoring 34.97 versus VideoPoet's 29.50 (2nd) and Seine's 29.13 (3rd), a gap of over 5 points. Against top closed-source models, Video-GPT exceeds Kling 1.6 (23.64) by 11.3 points, Gen3 (22.80) by 12.2 points, and Sora (10.00) by 25 points. On Kinetics-600, Video-GPT achieves the lowest FVD(500) of 315.40, beating Seine (332.80), LVM (356.48), and Open-Sora-Plan (384.27). On UCF-101 class-to-video, the fine-tuned Video-GPT reaches FVD 53, edging out LARP and FAR (both 57) and MAGVITv2 (58), while the from-scratch variant (FVD 489) confirms the critical role of self-supervised pretraining. For video classification, Video-GPT's linear probe (58.9% top-1) surpasses VideoMAEv2 (56.4%) and MemDPC (54.1%), and on MSR-VTT retrieval it outperforms FiT (R@1 18.8), SupportSet (12.7), and VideoCLIP (10.4) in a zero-shot setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

Video-GPT draws a direct analogy to GPT's next-token prediction, repurposing the autoregressive sequential modeling paradigm for video by treating each clip as a 'visual word.' Within each clip, it employs flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022) for parallel denoising, while between clips it maintains causal autoregressive ordering — a hybrid that departs from both pure diffusion video models (Sora, Kling, Wan) and pure autoregressive approaches (LVM, VideoPoet). The transformer backbone inherits from Phi-3-mini, and the hierarchical attention mask (clip-level, frame-level, patch-level) is a novel design that distinguishes this work from prior hybrid AR+diffusion efforts like Self-forcing (frame-level) or Transfusion/ACDiT (image-domain). Crucially, the pretraining is fully self-supervised on unlabeled video, analogous to how GPT is pretrained on raw text without task-specific annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-supervised nature of next clip diffusion means Video-GPT can scale to virtually all internet video without expensive annotation pipelines, mirroring GPT's data advantage in NLP — a practical pathway toward video world models. The 5+ point gap over the second-best model on Physics-IQ Benchmark and the 13-point ablation gap between next-clip-diffusion and next-token-prediction suggest the clip-level hybrid is not merely incremental but captures a qualitatively different inductive bias for temporal reasoning. Open questions include whether the paradigm scales beyond 3.8B parameters, whether a 3D VAE would further improve spatial fidelity, and how multi-modal (text+video) pretraining would interact with the clip-level autoregressive structure. The authors also note future directions in reinforcement-driven world interaction, hinting at applications beyond passive prediction.
