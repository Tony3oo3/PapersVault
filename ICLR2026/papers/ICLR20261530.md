## Problem

Existing cybersecurity LLMs either depend on proprietary APIs that risk exposing sensitive data or use open models with insufficient domain adaptation—most perform only one training stage (e.g., limited pretraining or SFT without the other) and lack comprehensive benchmarks covering tool proficiency and qualitative answer quality. No prior effort combines large-scale domain-specific continual pretraining, agentic data augmentation for SFT, and full openness of data, model, and code.

## Input / Output

RedSage accepts standard text prompts and user queries (evaluated in both 0-shot and 5-shot settings) and produces free-form text responses, multi-turn dialogues, CLI/Kali tool commands, and vulnerability analyses. The RedSage-Bench evaluation uses 30,240 four-option MCQs scored by normalized log-likelihood accuracy and 240 open-ended Q&A items scored by an LLM-as-judge rubric assessing factual correctness (True/False) and answer quality (0–10) across helpfulness, relevance, depth, and detail. The 8B model is designed for local deployment on consumer-grade GPUs, enabling privacy-preserving on-premise inference without cloud API calls.

## Method

RedSage is built on Qwen3-8B-Base through a three-stage pipeline: (1) continual pretraining on CyberFineWeb (~11.7B tokens extracted from FineWeb via a fine-tuned ModernBERT-base classifier, mixed 30% with FineWeb-Edu to prevent catastrophic forgetting, deduplicated with MinHash-LSH) followed by RedSage-Seed (28.6K curated documents from MITRE, OWASP, Kali, tldr-pages, etc.); (2) supervised fine-tuning on 266K multi-turn conversations generated by an agentic augmentation pipeline where a Planner Agent (Llama-3.3-70B) derives skill sets and augmentation strategies per seed chunk, and an Augmenter Agent instantiates them as grounded role-based dialogues, supplemented with general instruction data from SmolLM3; (3) Direct Preference Optimization using the Tulu 3 8B Preference Mixture. Training uses the Axolotl framework on 32×A100-64GB GPUs with DeepSpeed ZeRO Stage 3, AdamW optimizer, and a fixed LR of 2.5×10⁻⁶ for CPT. The key differentiator is the agentic augmentation step, which expands 28.6K seed documents into 266K diverse conversations (9.2× sample growth) while preserving technical depth, combined with the only fully open pipeline (data, model, code) in the cybersecurity LLM space.

## Results

On the proposed RedSage-Bench (30K MCQs), RedSage-8B-Ins achieves 85.73% macro accuracy, the best 8B instruction-tuned result, surpassing Qwen3-8B (81.85%) by +3.88 points. Across six established cybersecurity benchmarks (CTI-Bench, CyberMetric, MMLU-CSec, SecBench, SecEval, SECURE), RedSage-8B-Ins attains a mean accuracy of 81.30%, outperforming the strongest 8B baseline Qwen3-8B (75.71%) by +5.59 points. On the Open LLM Leaderboard general benchmarks, RedSage-8B-DPO reaches a 74.33% mean, exceeding Qwen3-8B (65.92%) by +8.41 points and even surpassing the larger Qwen3-32B (73.17%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RedSage-8B-Ins decisively outperforms all 8B-scale cybersecurity-tuned baselines: it leads Qwen3-8B by +5.59 mean points, Foundation-Sec-8B-Instruct by +5.86, DeepHat-V1-7B by +5.86, Llama-Primus-Merged by +10.07, and Lily-Cybersecurity-7B by +25.56 across the six cybersecurity benchmarks. The gap is most pronounced on CTI-Bench RCM (76.70 vs. 69.40 for Foundation-Sec) and SecEval MCQ (72.48 vs. 65.46 for Qwen3-8B), while the margin narrows on CyberMetric (89.80 vs. 88.60 for Qwen3-8B). Among base models, RedSage-8B-Base (84.56 mean) surpasses Qwen3-8B-Base (80.81) by +3.75 points, with the largest gains on CTI-RCM (+24.9) and MMLU-CSec (+5.0). On general LLM benchmarks, RedSage-8B-DPO (74.33 mean) beats Qwen3-8B (65.92) by +8.41 and even exceeds the 4×-larger Qwen3-32B (73.17) by +1.16, though it trails GPT-5 (91.07) by a wide margin. The DPO variant slightly reduces cybersecurity MCQ accuracy (84.83 vs. 85.73 for Ins) but improves general instruction-following (IFEval 83.44 vs. 79.97), illustrating a trade-off between domain specialization and general capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the domain-adaptive LLM training pipeline for cybersecurity by combining three previously separated stages—large-scale continual pretraining (building on FineWeb filtering, analogous to Foundation-Sec-8B and PRIMUS), curated seed data (extending the MITRE/OWASP/Kali resource collection approach), and agentic multi-turn augmentation for SFT (inspired by AgentInstruct but applied to security workflows). It departs from prior work by integrating all stages into a single open pipeline with DPO alignment, whereas prior efforts (Foundation-Sec, DeepHat, Lily-Cybersecurity) typically addressed only one or two stages and kept data or models closed. The benchmark construction extends existing cybersecurity evaluations (SecEval, CyberMetric, CTI-Bench, SECURE) by jointly covering knowledge, offensive skills, and tool proficiency with LLM-as-judge quality scoring, a dimension absent from all prior benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling privacy-preserving, on-premise cybersecurity assistance at 8B scale on consumer GPUs, addressing the data-privacy constraints that make proprietary APIs (GPT-5, SecGemini) unsuitable for sensitive security operations. The agentic augmentation pipeline (Planner + Augmenter agents) offers a reusable recipe for converting static domain documents into diverse multi-turn training data, potentially transferable to other specialized domains. Open questions include whether the LLM-generated augmentation faithfully preserves technical accuracy at scale, how the model handles rapidly evolving threat landscapes (static training data vs. live threats), and whether the offensive-security knowledge it encodes can be adequately safeguarded against misuse despite the paper's responsible-use framing.
