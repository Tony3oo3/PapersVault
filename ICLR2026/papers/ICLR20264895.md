## Problem

Map generation requires producing vectorized geometric primitives (polylines, polygons) rather than pixel grids, making standard rasterized generative models structurally mismatched. Existing multi-stage pipelines (segmentation followed by heuristic post-processing) are class-specific, accumulate errors across stages, and cannot serve as a unified foundation model for diverse map elements like roads and buildings simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264895/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

MARS takes 512×512 aerial image tiles (0.6 m GSD from NAIP) as input and produces a sequential token stream encoding all vectorized map primitives in the tile. Each object is emitted as [class_token, x₁, y₁, x₂, y₂, …, x₁, y₁] where coordinates are discretized into 224 levels shared across x and y dimensions; road networks are decomposed into individual single-polyline strokes, and buildings are closed-loop polygons. In interactive mode, user click coordinates can be injected at any point in the sequence to redirect or extend generation.

## Method

MARS converts vectorized maps into a sequential language via a map-to-sequence algorithm: a stroke-based decomposition splits complex road graphs at intersection nodes (edge degree > 3) and merges consecutive segments within a 30° angle tolerance into single polylines, while buildings are represented as closed-loop vertex sequences. The architecture pairs a Swin-L vision encoder (with a convolutional multi-level feature aggregator and adaptive upsampling) to a vanilla autoregressive transformer decoder that cross-attends to visual features and generates class and coordinate tokens under causal attention. A shared vocabulary unifies semantic class tokens (R, B, P, W, etc.) with discretized spatial tokens and special delimiters, trained end-to-end with cross-entropy loss under teacher forcing; objects are ordered by distance-to-centroid then clockwise angle for reproducible decoding. Pretraining on the 3M-image MAP-3M dataset (buildings from Overture Maps, roads from OpenStreetMap) followed by fine-tuning yields state-of-the-art or competitive results on Cityscale, SpaceNet, and AICrowd without any post-processing, and the autoregressive structure enables emergent prompt-following for human-in-the-loop map correction.

## Results

On Cityscale, MARS achieves a TOPO F1 of 82.88% (P=84.28, R=81.53), outperforming the previous best RNGDet++ (78.44%) by 4.44 points. On SpaceNet, MARS reaches 82.05% F1 (P=79.68, R=84.56), trailing RNGDet++ (82.51%) by only 0.46 points while achieving the highest recall of all methods. For building extraction on AICrowd-V1, MARS attains 97.32% IoU and 97.94% AR, closely matching the specialized GeoFormer (98.1% IoU) despite using no task-specific post-processing or tuned hyperparameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MARS decisively outperforms all segmentation-based baselines (Seg-UNet, Seg-DRM, Seg-Improved, Seg-DLA) and the earlier graph-based methods (RoadTracer, Sat2Graph, TD-Road) on both Cityscale and SpaceNet F1, with margins of 4–16 points. Against the strongest prior road-specialized model RNGDet++ (Xu et al., 2023), MARS wins on Cityscale F1 by 4.44 points (82.88 vs. 78.44) driven by substantially higher recall (81.53 vs. 72.58), but narrowly trails on SpaceNet F1 by 0.46 points (82.05 vs. 82.51) due to lower precision (79.68 vs. 91.34). SamRoad (Hetang et al., 2024), which leverages SAM with heuristic post-processing, is outperformed by MARS on both datasets in F1 (82.88 vs. 77.23 on Cityscale; 82.05 vs. 80.52 on SpaceNet). For building extraction on AICrowd-V1, MARS (IoU 97.32, AR 97.94) closely matches the specialized GeoFormer (IoU 98.1, AR 97.8) and surpasses Pix2Poly (IoU 95.03) by over 2 points, all without any vertex-confidence thresholds or NMS hyperparameters. The consistent pattern is that MARS's autoregressive design yields the highest recall across all road benchmarks, while segmentation-based methods trade recall for precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

MARS departs from the dominant multistage pipeline paradigm (rasterized segmentation followed by class-specific vectorized post-processing) used by SAM-based methods (SamRoad, SAMPoly-Build) and specialized road-graph extractors (RNGDet, Sat2Graph, TD-Road). Instead, it repurposes the auto-regressive sequence-to-sequence framework from language modeling, treating vectorized map primitives as a formal language via a map-to-sequence conversion that leverages a stroke-based decomposition algorithm (Yan et al., 2024) for road networks. The architecture itself is deliberately minimal—Swin Transformer encoder plus a vanilla causal decoder with cross-attention—positioning the contribution as a paradigm shift (language-modeling perspective) rather than an architectural innovation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MARS is the first end-to-end model to unify road and building generation without any class-specific post-processing, and MAP-3M (3M images, 10× larger than prior benchmarks) provides the data scale needed for foundational map pre-training. The emergent 'Chat with MARS' interactive correction capability opens a new human-in-the-loop paradigm for map maintenance. Key open questions include whether autoregressive decoding can be made competitive with one-shot segmentation in inference speed, how the approach scales beyond two classes to finer-grained ontologies (e.g., highway vs. pedestrian path), and whether the modest performance margins over specialized models will widen with further scale.
