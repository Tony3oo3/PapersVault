## Problem

LLMs typically produce a single uniform answer, overlooking the diversity of human judgments that shapes real-world responses to digital content. While the Wisdom of Crowds principle shows that aggregated independent opinions outperform individual experts, operationalizing this with human participants is costly and difficult to scale. The paper asks whether LLMs can be steered to embody distinct human-like personas whose aggregated responses improve predictive and reasoning performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260555/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a stimulus (ad image/video, webpage screenshot, or tweet text) plus embedding-retrieved few-shot exemplars, each persona agent produces a quantitative score (e.g., CTR percentile 0–100, likability 0–10) preceded by a qualitative rationale grounded in its demographic-psychographic profile. The framework aggregates individual scores via mean (continuous tasks) or majority vote (categorical tasks) into a single collective prediction, and a neutral expert-mode LLM synthesizes all rationales into one collective explanation. A strict 300-token generation budget is enforced uniformly across all agents and baselines to isolate the effect of persona diversity from output length.

## Method

Social Agents is a multi-agent framework that operationalizes the Wisdom of Crowds by instantiating N diverse LLM-based persona agents, each conditioned on a unique demographic (age, gender) and psychographic (values, interests, lifestyle) profile drawn from a Persona Agent Factory. A moderator selects a diverse panel, and each agent is prompted independently (no inter-agent communication) to first generate a persona-grounded rationale and then a quantitative prediction, enforcing a rationale-then-score chain-of-thought that improves reproducibility and interpretability. Predictions are aggregated via mean (continuous) or majority vote (categorical), and the framework is model-agnostic, evaluated across nine LLMs/VLMs of varying scales with 5-shot in-context learning and no task-specific fine-tuning. The key insight is that persona conditioning induces structured between-group variation—each agent draws from a distinct yet related distribution—rather than the within-group noise of repeated identical calls, which is what gives crowd wisdom its predictive power over brute-force repetition.

## Results

Across eleven behavioral prediction tasks and nine model backbones, Social Agents achieves an average 21.5% improvement over the No-Persona baseline, with gains of 30.5% on low-level judgment tasks and 9.9% on high-level reasoning tasks. The largest single-model gain is 164.2% on webpage likability (GPT-4o Pearson r rises from 0.28 to 0.74), while Ad CTR prediction sees a 34.7% MAPE reduction (72.45→47.60) and ROAS prediction a 39.8% MAPE reduction in the Real Estate domain. On long-term video memorability, GPT-4o under Social Agents reaches Spearman ρ = 0.41 versus 0.33 for No-Persona, though it remains below the task-specific Henry model (0.55).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Social Agents consistently outperforms the No-Persona (single-LLM expert) baseline across all eleven tasks and all nine model backbones, with average gains of 21.75% on tweet engagement, 28.2% on Ad CTR MAPE, 27.9% on ROAS MAPE, and 97.95% on webpage likability Pearson r. Against task-specific trained experts, Social Agents (GPT-4o) beats fine-tuned LCBM by 34.36% in MAPE on Ad CTR and exceeds XGBoost by 10.45% on webpage likability Pearson correlation (0.74 vs. 0.67) and by 126.9% on ROAS PE@30 in the Creative industry. On behavioral attribute classification, Social Agents surpasses Behavior-LLaVA (zero-shot) by up to 55.3% on persuasion, 37.6% on action, and 34.3% on emotion (all labels), but underperforms it by ~22.7% on the coarser clubbed-emotion classification—the only systematic regression observed. The one clear loss is on long-term video memorability, where Henry (trained on dedicated memorability corpora) achieves Spearman ρ = 0.55 versus Social Agents' best of 0.41. Compared to the Wisdom of the Silicon Crowd (WoSC) multi-model ensemble, Social Agents with persona diversity achieves substantially lower MAPE on CTR (~29% vs. ~50%) and higher Pearson r on webpage likability (0.74 vs. 0.35–0.55), demonstrating that structured persona diversity outperforms brute-force model ensembling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the classical Wisdom of Crowds principle (Surowiecki, Galton) through LLM persona prompting, building on prior demonstrations that LLMs can adopt demographic personas (Santurkar et al., 2023; Tess et al., 2025). It departs from the 'Wisdom of the Silicon Crowd' line (Schoenegger et al., 2024), which aggregates outputs from multiple distinct LLMs, and from self-consistency (Wang et al., 2023), which repeats the same prompt, by instead conditioning a single backbone on diverse demographic-psychographic profiles to induce structured inter-persona variation. The task suite is organized via Construal Level Theory (Trope & Liberman, 2010), and baselines include task-specific expert models (LCBM, Henry, Behavior-LLaVA) trained on large behavioral corpora, positioning Social Agents as a training-free, in-context alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Social Agents offers a scalable, interpretable, and model-agnostic substitute for costly human crowd studies in behavioral and marketing prediction, with gains averaging 21.5% across nine models and eleven tasks. A key open limitation is the age gradient in persona alignment (r drops from ~0.71 for 18-24 to ~0.22 for 55+), reflecting underrepresentation of older demographics in LLM training corpora; as models better capture this variation, collective predictions should improve further. The framework's reliance on a fixed 10-persona panel and mean aggregation, while effective, leaves open questions about optimal persona composition for novel domains and whether the approach extends to multi-turn or interactive behavioral settings.
