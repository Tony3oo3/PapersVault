## Problem

While in-context learning (ICL) in transformers has been theoretically analyzed for linear models and single-task nonlinear settings, its behavior on structured geometric data—specifically, regression of Hölder functions on low-dimensional manifolds—remains unexplored. This paper asks how the attention mechanism relates to classical kernel methods and whether transformers can achieve minimax-optimal regression rates when data lie on a manifold of intrinsic dimension d ≪ D.

## Input / Output

The method takes a prompt of n input-output pairs {(x_i, y_i)} where x_i are i.i.d. samples from a uniform distribution on a compact d-dimensional Riemannian manifold M ⊂ [-b,b]^D, along with a new query point x_{n+1} on M. The prompt is embedded into a (D+5) × (2n+1) matrix with sinusoidal positional encodings and a constant row, and the transformer outputs a scalar prediction f(x_{n+1}). During training, Γ independent tasks (each with its own α-Hölder function and prompt) are observed to minimize empirical squared loss.

## Method

The central contribution is a 5-layer transformer construction that exactly implements the Nadaraya-Watson kernel estimator with a Gaussian kernel of bandwidth h, using the attention mechanism to compute pairwise interactions (differences x_{n+1} − x_i, squared norms, and softmax-weighted averages) via interaction, gating, and decrementing lemmas. The final layer applies softmax attention over the computed kernel scores to produce the weighted average of y_i values, yielding zero approximation error. Building on this exact implementation, the paper derives a squared generalization error bound with two terms: a statistical term scaling as nD³Γ^{-1/2}√log(nDΓ) (governing how well the transformer learns the kernel algorithm from Γ tasks) and an approximation term scaling as n^{-2α/(2α+d)}[log n]^{1+3d/2} (matching the minimax rate for Hölder regression on d-dimensional manifolds up to log factors). Crucially, the exponent in the prompt-length dependence is the intrinsic dimension d rather than the ambient dimension D, and numerical experiments on S² confirm that learned attention scores are highly correlated with the Gaussian kernel.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1) establishes that the squared generalization error of transformer-based ICL is bounded by C₁(nD³Γ^{-1/2}√(log(nDΓ))) + C₂(n^{-2α/(2α+d)}[log n]^{1+3d/2}), where the second term matches the minimax regression rate for Hölder functions on d-dimensional manifolds up to a logarithmic factor. Empirically, the learned attention scores in the last transformer layer show strong Pearson correlation with the Gaussian kernel: 0.86±0.21 (n=4), 0.75±0.22 (n=8), 0.69±0.22 (n=16), and 0.67±0.19 (n=32), with p-values decreasing from 0.14 to 0.03. The generalization error bound is validated numerically: in log-log scale, the MSE vs. Γ initially follows the theoretical slope of −0.5, and the MSE vs. n decays faster for larger Γ, consistent with the two-term error decomposition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper that establishes new results rather than benchmarking against competing methods, so no direct empirical baselines are compared. The closest prior work is Kim et al. (2024), which derived approximation and generalization error bounds for a transformer with one linear attention layer and a deep feedforward network for in-context regression of Besov functions in ℝ^D. The key distinction is that this paper's error bound scales exponentially with the prompt length n with an exponent depending on the intrinsic manifold dimension d, whereas Kim et al.'s bound depends on the ambient dimension D. Additionally, this paper uses a 5-layer multi-head attention architecture with nD attention heads (resembling LLM architectures) and constant-depth feedforward layers, in contrast to Kim et al.'s single linear attention layer plus deep FFN. The paper also positions itself against Li et al. (2023) on transformers as in-context algorithm learners, noting that its novel contribution is the explicit construction bridging attention to kernel methods and the covering number calculation for the manifold setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ICL theory for transformers from linear models (Bai et al. 2023; Von Oswald et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2024) and single-task nonlinear approximation (Yun et al. 2019; Takakura & Suzuki 2023) to multi-task in-context regression on Riemannian manifolds. Its core technical contribution builds on the Nadaraya-Watson kernel estimator (Nadaraya, 1964; Watson, 1964), constructing a 5-layer transformer that exactly implements kernel regression with zero approximation error, and reuses interaction/gating lemmas from Havrilla & Liao (2024) for the transformer construction. Unlike Kim et al. (2024), which studied ICL of Besov functions in R^D with a single linear attention layer plus deep FFN, this paper fully leverages multi-head attention (nD heads) and incorporates manifold geometry so that error bounds depend on intrinsic dimension d rather than ambient dimension D.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical framework connecting the attention mechanism to classical kernel regression in the ICL setting, providing a principled tool for analyzing nonlinear transformer ICL beyond the linear-model regime. The minimax-optimal rate (up to log factors) depending on intrinsic dimension d offers a theoretical justification for empirical observations that transformer scaling laws depend on data manifold dimension. Key limitations include the clean-data assumption, fixed prompt length, and the idealized exact-kernel construction; extending to noisy observations, variable-length prompts, and broader function classes (e.g., Besov on manifolds) remains open. The empirical validation on spherical harmonics and GPT-2 attention scores is suggestive but limited in scale and diversity.
