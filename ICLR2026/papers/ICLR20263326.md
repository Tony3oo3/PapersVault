## Problem

Vision-Language Models suffer from limited fine-grained visual perception, which is a critical bottleneck for real-world applications. Existing remedies are inadequate: supervised fine-tuning on distilled data compromises general capabilities, while reinforcement fine-tuning in 'thinking-with-image' paradigms prioritizes textual tool manipulation over genuine perceptual improvement. The scarcity of high-quality perceptual training data further constrains progress, as large-scale distillation is computationally expensive and yields diminishing returns.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on two task-specific input formats. Stage 1 (Caption Self-Refining) takes an original image, a diffusion-reconstructed image generated from the model's own caption, and the original caption text, producing a structured JSON list of refinement actions that identify factual errors and omissions. Stage 2 (Visual-Operation Predicting) takes a pair of highly similar images (original and edited) and outputs the precise visual operation instructions that account for their differences. The self-synthesized Viper10K dataset comprises 7K Stage-1 and 3K Stage-2 samples, all generated by the model itself using Qwen-Image and OmniGen2 diffusion models for reconstruction and editing respectively.

## Method

ViPER establishes a closed-loop self-evolutionary paradigm built on a coarse-to-fine two-stage task. The data synthesis module creates a bidirectional vision-language mapping: a VLM generates captions, a diffusion model reconstructs images from those captions, and the visual discrepancies between original and reconstructed images serve as grounded feedback for the VLM to critique and refine its own descriptions (Stage 1) or to infer fine-grained editing operations from image pairs (Stage 2). Training uses a modified GRPO algorithm (following DAPO's decoupled clipping ranges and removed KL penalty) with a reward computed via BGE-M3 semantic similarity between split output sentences and ground-truth sequences, weighted 95% correctness and 5% format. Crucially, the VLM within the data synthesis pipeline is dynamically updated from training checkpoints, enabling co-evolution of model and data without any external cold-start. Ablations confirm that the sequential two-stage RL outperforms mixed training, and the model spontaneously develops 'thinking-with-image' behaviors (e.g., 'scan', 'zoom in', 'focus on') and shifts attention toward critical visual details.

## Results

On seven multimodal benchmarks, Qwen-Viper-3B achieves an overall score of 60.4 (vs. 58.7 for Qwen2.5-VL-3B, +1.7) and Qwen-Viper-7B reaches 66.0 (vs. 64.4 for Qwen2.5-VL-7B, +1.6). The most pronounced gains appear in fine-grained perception, where the 3B and 7B models improve by 4.4% and 6.0% respectively. Qwen-Viper-7B also outperforms the larger InternVL3-8B (64.7) and InternVL2.5-8B (63.3) on the overall metric while using fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen-Viper-7B (66.0 overall) outperforms InternVL3-8B (64.7) and InternVL2.5-8B (63.3) despite using fewer parameters, and also surpasses MiniCPM-V2.6 on MMStar (66.2 vs. 57.5), MME-RW (59.0 vs. –), and HallusionBench (54.4 vs. 48.1). Against GPT-4o-0513, Qwen-Viper-7B wins on MMStar (66.2 vs. 64.7), MME-RW (59.0 vs. 45.2), and CRPE (77.6 vs. 76.6), but trails on RealWorldQA (71.4 vs. 75.4) and BLINK (57.6 vs. 68.0). Compared to the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B, ViPER yields consistent gains across all seven benchmarks, with the largest improvement on RealWorldQA (+2.9) and MMStar (+2.3). In the ablation against SFT-only training on Viper10K, RL training provides an additional +1.4 overall (66.0 vs. 64.6), confirming that the RL stage is essential beyond mere data exposure. Finally, in Appendix E, Qwen-Viper-7B (66.0) substantially outperforms Qwen2.5-VL-7B fine-tuned on the larger ViRL39K dataset (64.3), demonstrating that self-synthesized perception-focused data is more effective than 4× larger math/chart-oriented RL data for visual perception tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViPER builds on the GRPO/DAPO reinforcement learning lineage (from DeepSeek-R1) for policy optimization, but departs fundamentally from the dominant 'thinking-with-image' paradigm that relies on iterative tool use and multi-step interaction. Rather than distilling from stronger models or augmenting with external visual tools, ViPER introduces a self-bootstrapping closed loop where diffusion models (Qwen-Image, OmniGen2) serve as visual critics that externalize the VLM's own textual reasoning into observable image differences, creating training data without external supervision. The two-stage coarse-to-fine task structure (caption self-refining → visual-operation predicting) is a deliberate departure from both SFT-based perception training and mixed-task RL, positioning the work at the intersection of generative modeling and reinforcement fine-tuning for VLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-evolutionary paradigm addresses a practical bottleneck: the scarcity of high-quality fine-grained perception data and the computational cost of distillation. The finding that cold-start data is unnecessary (and potentially constraining) challenges a common assumption in RL-based VLM training, while the spontaneous emergence of visual-operation verbs in chain-of-thought tokens provides mechanistic evidence that generation and understanding are reciprocally coupled. Limitations include evaluation only on the Qwen2.5-VL family (3B/7B), dependence on diffusion model fidelity for data quality, and a relatively small 10K dataset; open questions include whether the self-evolution loop can iterate multiple times with compounding gains and whether the approach transfers to video or higher-resolution perception tasks.
