## Problem

Global visual geolocation remains challenging because existing paradigms—large-scale image retrieval, grid-based classification, and generative diffusion—each fail to exploit the inherently hierarchical structure of geography (continent → country → region → subregion → city) while remaining scalable and interpretable. Retrieval methods require indexing millions of image embeddings with limited interpretability, classifiers collapse multi-scale geographic structure into flat outputs, and generative models underperform at fine spatial scales. No prior work has embedded a planet-scale geographic hierarchy in a geometry that naturally accommodates exponential branching, nor has it formulated geolocation as alignment to a compact set of geographic entities rather than to individual images.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, encoded by a frozen vision backbone (DINOv3, ViT-L/14, or StreetCLIP) into a fixed-dimensional feature vector. It produces a hierarchical prediction path—country, region, subregion, and city—resolved via beam search (width 10) over 240k entity embeddings in 128-dimensional hyperbolic space, with the final geographic coordinate estimate taken from the predicted city entity's stored lat/lon. Unlike retrieval methods that return nearest-neighbor images, the output is an interpretable chain of named geographic entities, and the search space is sub-linear in the number of training images (~240k entities vs. ~9.6M images).

## Method

HierLoc represents each geographic entity (country, region, subregion, city) as a point on the Lorentz hyperboloid H_K^128, initialized from a fixed anchor and updated by a learnable tangent-space vector derived from concatenated multimodal features (averaged image embedding, CLIP text embedding, and SphereM+ coordinate encoding) passed through an MLP. Images are projected into the same hyperbolic space and refined via a four-level cross-modal attention module (8 heads per level) where entity embeddings serve as keys/values and the image as query; only the image stream is updated, keeping entity prototypes stable. Training minimizes a Geo-Weighted Hyperbolic InfoNCE (GWH-InfoNCE) loss that reweights each negative entity by a Laplace-decayed haversine distance between the image's true location and the negative entity's coordinates, emphasizing geographically proximal negatives to sharpen fine-scale discrimination. Inference performs hierarchical beam search using FAISS FlatIP with a time-coordinate sign flip to convert Lorentz inner-product maximization into standard Euclidean MIPS, yielding a 6.56× speedup over the generative RFM S2 baseline while reducing mean geodesic error by 19.5% on OSV5M.

## Results

On the OSV5M benchmark (4.8M train / 210k test images), HierLoc with DINOv3 backbone achieves a GeoScore of 3963 and reduces mean geodesic error to 861 km, setting new state-of-the-art across all hierarchical levels: 82.9% country, 55.0% region, 40.7% subregion, and 23.3% city accuracy. This represents a 19.5% reduction in mean geodesic error and a 43.2% improvement in subregion accuracy over the strongest prior baselines. On the MediaEval'16-trained model evaluated on IM2GPS, the median error drops to 21.4 km (vs. 70.5 km for PIGEON), and on YFCC4K the median error is 341.9 km while matching the mean geodesic error (2058 km) of the much larger RFM_10M S2 generative model trained on 10× more images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HierLoc (DINOv3) outperforms all 13 baselines on every reported OSV5M metric, with the largest margins over retrieval-based methods: SC Retrieval (Haas et al., 2023) trails by 366 GeoScore points and 525 km in mean geodesic error, while the generative RFM S₂ (Dufour et al., 2025) is surpassed by 196 GeoScore points and 208 km. Against the strongest classification baselines (LocDiff, PIGEON, GeoDecoder), HierLoc gains +8.8% country, +20.1% region, and +43.2% subregion accuracy. On IM2GPS, HierLoc achieves the best median error (21.4 km vs. 70.5 km for PIGEON) and best 25 km recall (51.9%), though it trails GeoDecoder (69.0%) and GeoReasoner (86.0%) at the 200 km and 750 km thresholds respectively. On IM2GPS3K, HierLoc nearly halves PIGEON's median error (73.4 vs. 147.3 km) and leads at 25 km (43.8% vs. 36.7%), but loses to GeoReasoner at 1 km (11.3% vs. 10.0%) and 750 km (74.1% vs. 83.0%). On YFCC4K, HierLoc beats PIGEON on median (341.9 vs. 383.0 km), 25 km (30.2% vs. 23.7%), and 200 km (43.3% vs. 40.6%), but falls slightly behind at 1 km (8.4% vs. 10.4%) and 2500 km (75.8% vs. 77.7%). Against the generative RFM_10M S₂ on YFCC4K, HierLoc matches the mean geodesic error (2058 km) while being trained on 10× fewer images, though it trails by 21 GeoScore points and 3.3 points at 25 km recall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hyperbolic representation learning line (Poincaré embeddings for hierarchical structures, hyperbolic neural networks in the Lorentz model) to the domain of global visual geolocation, where no prior work had embedded geographic entity hierarchies in hyperbolic space. It departs from the three dominant geolocation paradigms—image-to-image retrieval (SC Retrieval, PIGEON), grid-based classification (PlaNet, CPlaNet), and generative diffusion (RFM S2, LocDiff)—by reformulating the task as image-to-entity alignment over a compact 240k-entity hierarchy, coupled with a novel Geo-Weighted Hyperbolic InfoNCE loss that incorporates haversine distance into the contrastive objective. The cross-modal attention module and beam-search inference over the entity tree are new architectural components, while the multimodal entity construction (averaged image embeddings, CLIP text, Sphere2Vec coordinates) draws on existing location-encoder and vision-backbone literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HierLoc demonstrates that a compact, interpretable entity hierarchy in hyperbolic space can outperform both retrieval and generative approaches on OSV5M while reducing inference cost by over 10× and storage by 20×, making billion-scale deployment feasible. The 43% subregion accuracy gain and 19.5% reduction in mean geodesic error are substantial, and the thorough ablations (backbone, manifold, loss components, beam width) confirm the gains are architectural rather than backbone-dependent. Key limitations remain: sub-kilometer accuracy is bounded by the 240k-entity discretization, and performance degrades in underrepresented regions (inland Asia, central Africa) due to training data imbalance. The broader implication—that hyperbolic entity embeddings with geo-aware contrastive learning generalize to any hierarchically structured multimodal task (biodiversity taxonomies, linguistic families, knowledge graphs)—opens a promising research direction beyond geolocation.
