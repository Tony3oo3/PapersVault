## Problem

Multimodal LLMs need high-resolution visual input for fine-grained perception, but processing full high-resolution images is computationally prohibitive. Existing Region-of-Interest (RoI) methods face a difficult trade-off: training-based approaches require large-scale annotated datasets, while training-free methods that exploit the model's internal cross-attention are computationally inefficient, demanding multi-pass prefill stages or relying on the slow auto-regressive decoding process. The paper addresses the gap of an efficient, annotation-free RoI identification method that avoids both costly supervision and high-latency decoding.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution image (e.g., 336×336 for LLaVA-1.5) and a text query as input. The RPN, operating on features from the MLLM's middle layers, produces a dense RoI map in a single partial forward pass (only up to layer B+R of the LLM, not the full model). This map is post-processed into a binary foreground mask, which drives either Box Upscaling (independent crops per connected component) or Masked Upscaling (one unified crop with masking) to generate high-resolution visual embeddings. These are appended to the original visual tokens, and the full MLLM then auto-regressively decodes the final textual answer on this augmented context.

## Method

SD-RPN is a self-distillation framework that trains a lightweight Region Proposal Network (R transformer blocks) on top of B frozen MLLM layers to predict a dense RoI map. The key insight is that the MLLM's own response-to-image attention from middle layers contains a strong but noisy RoI signal; a pseudo-label pipeline first removes sink tokens (identified by high L2-norm of visual features) and then applies a selective binary classification that labels only high-confidence foreground tokens (attention ≥ τ_fg·a_max) and clearly-background tokens (outside the minimal bounding box of foreground, attention ≤ τ_bg·a_max), while ignoring ambiguous middle-range tokens. The RPN is initialized from the MLLM's own layers B to B+R and trained with masked binary cross-entropy on these pseudo-labels, using the model's self-generated responses (not external teacher outputs) for representational consistency. At inference, the RPN predicts the RoI in one forward pass through partial LLM layers, completely decoupling localization from auto-regressive generation, enabling a two-stage process: predict RoI → crop and upscale → generate answer.

## Results

On Document & OCR benchmarks (Table 1), SD-RPN achieves an average accuracy of 34.6% on LLaVA-1.5-7B (+7.1 over baseline, +3.9 over S2, +2.8 over ViCrop), with the largest gains on DocVQA (+12.7) and TextVQA (+12.7). On Vision-Centric benchmarks (Table 2), SD-RPN boosts V-Star Overall by +20.4 on LLaVA-1.5-7B (70.7 vs. 50.3 baseline) and +11.5 on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (89.5 vs. 78.0 baseline), while also improving HR-Bench 8K Overall by +7.8 and +9.9 respectively. Throughput is maintained at 0.51–0.62× of baseline, compared to 0.39–0.42× for ViCrop.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SD-RPN consistently outperforms both S² (full-tuning) and ViCrop (training-free) across all model families on the Document & OCR benchmarks. On LLaVA-1.5-7B, SD-RPN achieves 34.6% average vs. 30.7% (S²) and 31.8% (ViCrop), with the largest margin on DocVQA (34.2 vs. 27.1/27.0) and TextVQA (58.8 vs. 52.6/57.2). SD-RPN also maintains higher throughput (0.62×) than ViCrop (0.42×) while delivering superior accuracy. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, SD-RPN (89.5 V* Overall) slightly trails DeepEyes (90.1) but surpasses Thyme (82.2), and achieves a higher HR-Bench 8K Overall (73.5) than both DeepEyes (72.6) and Thyme (72.0). The gains are most pronounced on weaker baselines (LLaVA-1.5, DeepSeek-VL) where fine-grained perception is the primary bottleneck, while on the already-strong Qwen2.5-VL the improvements are more modest (+2.7–3.0% average) but still consistent across all benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the RoI-centric paradigm for efficient high-resolution perception in MLLMs (VILA-HD, ViCrop, S²), (2) the self-distillation framework where a model teaches a lighter student from its own internal signals (Zhang et al., 2019), and (3) the TwigVLM strategy of initializing a small trainable module from a pretrained MLLM's middle layers (Shao et al., 2025). It departs from prior RoI methods by rejecting both the annotation-heavy training route and the computationally expensive training-free attention extraction, instead proposing a denoising pipeline (sink-token removal + selective binary classification) that converts the MLLM's noisy cross-attention into sparse pseudo-labels for training a lightweight RPN. The theoretical analysis in Appendix F further distinguishes this work by formally showing why regressing on denoised attention outperforms thresholding raw attention maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SD-RPN offers a practical, drop-in module that improves fine-grained perception across multiple MLLM families (LLaVA-1.5, DeepSeek-VL, Qwen2.5-VL) with only ~152K pseudo-labeled samples and no external annotations, making it attractive for practitioners who cannot afford large-scale supervised fine-tuning. The counter-intuitive finding that self-generated responses yield better distillation targets than ground-truth SFT responses challenges the standard assumption that a stronger teacher always helps, and points toward representational consistency as a key principle in self-distillation. Open questions remain around the identified failure modes (incomplete activation, localization errors, spatial relationship errors), scalability to video and document understanding, and whether the approach extends to adaptive token allocation beyond fixed RoI cropping.
