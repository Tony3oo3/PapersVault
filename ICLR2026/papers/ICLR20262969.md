## Problem

Prior energy-latency attacks on MLLMs (e.g., Verbose Images) uniformly suppress the EOS token across all positions, ignoring that different Part-of-Speech tags exhibit markedly different propensities to trigger sequence termination, and overlooking the role of sentence-level structural patterns in inflating output length. This uniform strategy applies suppressive pressure inefficiently to positions unlikely to terminate, limiting the achievable verbosity and leaving a significant gap between current attack efficacy and the model's true generation ceiling.

## Input / Output

The attack takes a clean RGB image (resized to 224×224 for InstructBLIP/Qwen2.5-VL or 448×448 for InternVL3-8B) and a fixed text prompt, and produces an ℓ∞-bounded adversarial perturbation (ε=8, step size η=1) that is imperceptible to human observers. When the perturbed image is fed to the target MLLM, the model generates a verbose, repetitive text sequence that drives output toward the maximum token limit (1024 in main experiments, up to 32K in transfer tests), with the perturbation crafted under a white-box assumption via 300 PGD iterations.

## Method

LingoLoop combines two synergistic loss mechanisms optimized jointly via PGD. First, the POS-Aware Delay Mechanism constructs a Statistical Weight Pool by empirically measuring EOS prediction probabilities conditioned on the preceding token's POS tag across 10,000 image captions; the Linguistic Prior Suppression loss (L_LPS) then applies POS-weighted suppression on EOS probabilities, concentrating gradient pressure on linguistically termination-prone contexts (e.g., after punctuation) rather than uniformly. Second, the Generative Path Pruning Mechanism introduces a Repetition Promotion Loss (L_Rep) that penalizes the mean L2 norm of hidden states across all transformer layers for each generated token, compressing the model's latent trajectory into a low-variance subspace that forces repetitive looping. The two losses are balanced by a dynamic weight λ(t) that normalizes their magnitudes from the previous iteration and applies a logarithmic temporal decay, so optimization initially prioritizes loop induction before shifting to fine-grained EOS suppression. The combined objective is minimized over 300 PGD steps with momentum, projecting the perturbation back onto the ℓ∞ ball at each step.

## Results

On MS-COCO, LingoLoop drives Qwen2.5-VL-3B to generate 1020.38 tokens (15.3× clean, 2.6× Verbose Images) with 7090.58 J energy and 32.94 s latency, consistently hitting the 1024-token generation ceiling. Across all four evaluated MLLMs (InstructBLIP, Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B, InternVL3-8B), LingoLoop achieves 2.4–3.0× more tokens than Verbose Images and 7.9–15.3× more than clean inputs. When the external generation cap is relaxed to 32K tokens, the attack induces up to 23,844 tokens on MS-COCO (351.9× clean) and 23,054 tokens on ImageNet (367.6× clean), demonstrating persistent looping behavior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole SOTA baseline is Verbose Images (Gao et al., 2024, ICLR), which induces verbosity by uniformly suppressing EOS probabilities via imperceptible image perturbations. LingoLoop outperforms Verbose Images on every model and dataset: on MS-COCO it achieves 2.6–3.0× more tokens (e.g., 1020.38 vs. 394.74 on Qwen2.5-VL-3B; 1002.08 vs. 332.29 on InstructBLIP) and 2.4–2.6× higher energy consumption. The gap is most pronounced on Qwen2.5-VL-3B, where LingoLoop hits the 1024-token ceiling while Verbose Images plateaus at ~395 tokens. Random noise and clean inputs produce negligible verbosity (within 2–3% of clean), confirming that the gains are attributable to the structured attack rather than perturbation magnitude. In transfer settings (Appendix D.3–D.4), LingoLoop also surpasses Verbose Images on cross-model transfer to Qwen2.5-VL-32B (357.4 vs. 309.8 tokens intra-family) and on closed-source GPT-4o/Gemini 2.5 Pro (22.7%/30.6% more induced words).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Verbose Images attack (Gao et al., ICLR 2024), which first demonstrated that imperceptible image perturbations can induce lengthy outputs from MLLMs by uniformly suppressing EOS token probabilities. LingoLoop departs from this uniform strategy in two ways: it introduces a POS-Aware Delay Mechanism that selectively suppresses EOS based on part-of-speech statistics of preceding tokens, and a Generative Path Pruning Mechanism that constrains hidden-state L2 norms to trap the model in repetitive loops. The work also situates itself within the broader sponge-attack / energy-latency attack literature (Shumailov et al., 2021; NICGSlowDown; NMTSloth), which has previously targeted CNNs, image captioners, and NMT systems but had not yet exploited linguistic structural cues in MLLM autoregressive decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated 367× token amplification under relaxed generation limits and the consistent saturation of max-token ceilings across four diverse MLLMs highlight a concrete, exploitable vulnerability in cloud-hosted MLLM services, with direct implications for operational cost and denial-of-service risk. The finding that standard decoding-time defenses (repetition penalty, no_repeat_ngram_size) are ineffective against the attack underscores a gap in current MLLM deployment safeguards. Open questions include whether the white-box crafting requirement (even with demonstrated cross-model and cross-lingual transferability) limits the practical threat model, and whether more sophisticated guardrail models or internal-state monitors can reliably detect the induced looping without degrading legitimate long-form generation.
