## Problem

Variational Preference Learning (VPL) aims to personalize RLHF by encoding user-specific latent variables from preference data, but the paper identifies and demonstrates that VPL suffers from posterior collapse: under sparse preference data and expressive decoders, the latent variable becomes uninformative and is effectively ignored, reducing the model to an implicit single-reward model that overlooks minority preferences. This failure mode, well-known in VAEs, had not previously been identified or addressed in preference learning frameworks.

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes a set of n preference pairs (prompt x, chosen response y_w, rejected response y_l) per user, tokenized via a supervised fine-tuned Llama-3 backbone (3B or 8B), and additionally processes a fictitious 'swap' version where chosen and rejected responses are inverted. The output is a 1024-dimensional user-specific latent z_K (produced by a 2-step P-IAF flow) that conditions the reward decoder, which scores any (x, y) pair to yield a personalized reward r_φ(x, y, z_K). The latent is inferred purely from preference data without access to explicit user-type labels.

## Method

SPL introduces three mechanisms to prevent posterior collapse. First, Swap-guided Base Regularization constructs a fictitious swap annotator by inverting all preference pairs and enforces a mirroring property on the Gaussian base posterior: the posterior means must exhibit sign-reversal (μ ≈ −μ_swap) while log-variances remain invariant (ℓ ≈ ℓ_swap), enforced via a cosine-similarity guidance loss. Second, Preferential Inverse Autoregressive Flow (P-IAF) decomposes the encoder's context vector into swap-reversal (c_d) and swap-invariant (c_s) components, feeding c_d exclusively to the shift functions and c_s exclusively to the scale functions of a 2-step IAF, which provably tightens the swap-probability error bound compared to standard IAF by eliminating cross-context leak terms. Third, Adaptive Latent Conditioning applies FiLM-style scale-and-shift modulation in the decoder, dynamically amplifying or attenuating the latent's contribution to reward prediction based on signal confidence. The overall objective minimizes negative ELBO plus the guidance loss, with theoretical analysis (Appendix B) showing P-IAF achieves a tighter swap-probability error bound than vanilla IAF under shared Lipschitz assumptions.

## Results

On the challenging UF-P-4 dataset (4 preference types, diverse prompts), SPL achieves 61.56% preference-prediction accuracy with Llama-3.2-3B and 62.21% with Llama-3.1-8B, outperforming all baselines (BTL, DPL, VPL) which cluster around 57%. On UF-P-2, SPL reaches 63.28% (3B) and 63.71% (8B) versus ~62.3–62.7% for baselines. SPL also achieves 100% accuracy on the simpler Pets dataset and maintains 70–97% Active Units across all KL weights β, whereas VPL collapses to 0% AU in most settings, confirming that SPL prevents posterior collapse while improving prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPL consistently outperforms all three baselines across every dataset and model size. Against BTL (standard RLHF), SPL gains the largest margin on UF-P-4: +4.49 pp (3B) and +4.81 pp (8B), indicating that a single universal reward fundamentally cannot capture multi-type preferences. Against DPL, which models reward as a distribution but ignores individual users, SPL improves by +4.52 pp (3B) and +4.55 pp (8B) on UF-P-4, showing that user-specific latents add value beyond distributional modeling. Against VPL, the closest competitor, SPL gains +4.53 pp (3B) and +5.07 pp (8B) on UF-P-4; on UF-P-2 the gap is smaller (+0.91 pp and +1.05 pp) because VPL partially avoids collapse at β=3×10⁻⁶. On the simple Pets dataset, SPL matches VPL's near-perfect accuracy (100% vs. 99.67% for 3B) but dramatically outperforms VPL with the 8B model (100% vs. 75.33%), where VPL's posterior collapse is most severe. SPL achieves these gains with only ~2% additional GPU time and ~0.4 GB peak memory over VPL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Variational Preference Learning (VPL, Poddar et al. 2024), which introduced user-specific latent variables into RLHF reward modeling, by identifying and fixing a critical failure mode: posterior collapse. The solution draws on Inverse Autoregressive Flow (Kingma et al. 2016) for richer posteriors and FiLM-style conditioning (Perez et al. 2018) for adaptive decoding, but the key departure is the swap-guided regularization that exploits the inherent symmetry of preference pairs (swapping chosen/rejected responses) to enforce a mirroring structure in the encoder. The P-IAF component further modifies standard IAF by decomposing the context into swap-reversal and swap-invariant parts fed separately to shift and scale functions, a design not present in prior flow-based variational inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to identify posterior collapse in preference learning frameworks, a failure mode that silently reduces personalized models to single-reward models and undermines the fairness goals of pluralistic alignment. The swap-guided approach is computationally lightweight (minimal overhead over VPL) and robust across KL weights, making it practical for real-world deployment. However, the empirical accuracy gains over VPL are modest (~1–5% on UF-P), and the framework is limited to single-turn comparison data; extending to multi-turn dialogue and genuinely noisy real-world annotation remains open. The theoretical analysis (tighter swap-probability-error bounds for P-IAF vs. IAF) provides a principled justification but relies on Lipschitz and boundedness assumptions that are hard to verify in practice.
