## Problem

Recurrent 3D reconstruction models like CUT3R compress scene history into a fixed-length state, but their softmax-based state update effectively applies a constant learning rate of 1.0, forcing the model to fully overwrite historical information with each new observation. This causes catastrophic forgetting and severe performance degradation when sequences exceed the ~64-frame training context, while full-attention alternatives (VGGT, StreamVGGT) suffer quadratic memory growth and out-of-memory failures beyond a few hundred frames.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous online stream of RGB images (arbitrary resolution, patchified into tokens via a DINO/CroCo-style image tokenizer) and, for each frame, produces a camera pose (3×4), camera intrinsic (3×3), and a pixel-aligned canonical pointmap (W×H×3) in real time. The internal state is a fixed-size matrix of n tokens with channel dimension c that never grows with sequence length, enabling constant 6 GB GPU memory and 20 FPS inference over thousands of frames. Camera pose and intrinsic can be extracted either by regressing from image tokens or by solving PnP/Weiszfeld from the predicted pointmap.

## Method

TTT3R reformulates CUT3R's recurrent state update as a Test-Time Training (TTT) gradient-descent step, where the state acts as a fast weight and the cross-attention between state queries and observation keys provides the gradient direction. The key contribution is a training-free, closed-form per-token learning rate β_t = σ(mean_m(Q_S K_X^T)) derived from the alignment confidence between memory and the incoming observation, which gates the update intensity per state token. High-confidence alignments (strong geometric match) receive larger update steps, while low-confidence regions (e.g., textureless surfaces) are suppressed, preventing the uniform overwriting that causes forgetting in vanilla CUT3R. The resulting update rule S_t = S_{t-1} − β_t · softmax(Q_S K_X^T) V_X is a drop-in replacement requiring no additional parameters, no fine-tuning, and no extra FLOPs, yet it yields a 2× improvement in global pose estimation over CUT3R on 1000-frame sequences while preserving identical speed and memory footprint.

## Results

On long-sequence camera pose estimation (1000 frames, TUM-dynamic), TTT3R achieves an ATE of 0.106 m, a 2× improvement over CUT3R (0.173 m), while maintaining identical inference speed (20 FPS) and GPU memory (6 GB). On video depth estimation (500 frames, KITTI), TTT3R reduces Abs Rel from 0.152 to 0.131 and improves δ<1.25 from 80.2% to 86.9% over CUT3R. On 3D reconstruction (7-scene), TTT3R maintains consistent Chamfer Distance over hundreds of views where CUT3R degrades severely, approaching the offline VGGT upper bound. All gains are achieved training-free with zero additional parameters or FLOPs over the CUT3R baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TTT3R decisively outperforms CUT3R on long sequences, halving ATE (0.106 vs 0.173) and improving δ<1.25 by 6.7 points on depth, while preserving CUT3R's constant 6 GB memory and 20 FPS throughput. Against Point3R, TTT3R wins on sequences beyond 700 frames where Point3R exhausts GPU memory, and also achieves lower Chamfer Distance on 7-scene reconstruction. StreamVGGT and VGGT (full-attention methods) serve as offline upper bounds but OOM beyond ~150 frames, making them infeasible for long-sequence streaming. Among non-TTT heuristics (periodic Reset, EMA shrinkage, BurnIn keyframes), TTT3R outperforms all on both pose and depth, with the Reset mechanism providing the closest competitor (ATE 0.126) but still trailing TTT3R by 16%. Against learnable gating mechanisms (ScalarLR, ConditionLR, TokenLR) that require fine-tuning on 4–64 frame sequences, TTT3R's training-free confidence-guided learning rate achieves substantially better long-sequence generalization (e.g., ATE 0.106 vs 0.154 for the best gating variant, TokenLR). On short sequences (≤90 frames), TTT3R remains competitive with CUT3R and achieves the best overall performance among online methods in Table 6, though a gap to offline methods like VGGT (ATE 0.012 on TUM) persists.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the state update in CUT3R (a recurrent 3D reconstruction model) through the lens of Test-Time Training (TTT), a framework recently developed in the RNN literature (DeltaNet, Mamba, Gated Linear Attention) where the hidden state is interpreted as 'fast weights' updated via gradient descent. The key departure from prior TTT work is that the learning rate is not a learned parameter but is derived in closed form from the cross-attention alignment confidence between the state and incoming observations, yielding a training-free, per-token gating mechanism. Conceptually, the paper bridges the 3D reconstruction community (CUT3R, Point3R, StreamVGGT) with the modern RNN/TTT community, showing that CUT3R's softmax-based update implicitly corresponds to a fixed learning rate of 1.0, which is the root cause of its catastrophic forgetting on long sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a zero-cost, plug-and-play modification to CUT3R that doubles pose estimation accuracy on long sequences while preserving real-time speed (20 FPS) and constant 6 GB memory, making long-sequence online 3D reconstruction substantially more viable. However, the paper acknowledges that TTT3R mitigates but does not eliminate forgetting (failure beyond ~1000 frames without state reset), and it has not yet matched offline full-attention methods like VGGT in reconstruction accuracy. The TTT perspective opens a design space for future work: more sophisticated retention/adaptation schedules, parallelizable recurrent architectures, and potentially extending the framework beyond CUT3R to other stateful 3D models.
