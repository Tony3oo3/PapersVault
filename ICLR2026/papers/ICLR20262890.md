## Problem

Diffusion planners achieve strong performance in offline RL but require many iterative denoising steps for inference, making them impractical for real-time decision-making. Existing acceleration approaches either rely on behavior cloning (which degrades on suboptimal, multi-modal datasets) or integrate consistency models into actor-critic frameworks (requiring complex concurrent multi-network training and noise-aware reward models). The paper asks whether consistency trajectory distillation can be augmented with a direct reward objective to produce a single-step sampler that selects high-reward modes from suboptimal offline data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262890/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The planner takes a fixed-length sequence of past states (h=1 for MuJoCo, or start and goal positions for Maze2d) and outputs a fixed-length sequence of future actions (c=16 for MuJoCo, or full state sequences of horizon 128–384 for Maze2d, from which actions are recovered via a reverse dynamics model). The student model performs a single denoising step from pure Gaussian noise to a clean action trajectory, conditioned on the state sequence, requiring only 1 NFE at inference versus 20–256 for the multi-step teacher or Diffuser.

## Method

RACTD trains a student consistency model via three jointly optimized losses: a CTM loss that enforces anytime-to-anytime consistency along the PFODE trajectory (using a frozen EDM teacher as the solver), a DSM loss anchoring predictions to the training data distribution, and a reward loss that maximizes the output of a pre-trained, noise-free return-to-go network evaluated on the generated action. The reward model (4 ConvBlocks + Linear) is trained independently on the offline dataset and operates entirely in the clean state-action space, eliminating the need for noise-aware reward estimation required by classifier-guided multi-step diffusion. The key insight is that the reward objective acts as a mode-selection mechanism: the unconditioned teacher faithfully captures the full multi-modal behavior distribution from mixed-quality data, while the reward loss during student distillation steers the single-step sampler toward the high-reward modes. All three components (teacher, reward model, student) are trained in a fully decoupled pipeline, avoiding the concurrent optimization instability of actor-critic methods.

## Results

On D4RL Gym-MuJoCo (online model selection), RACTD achieves an average score of 101.5 across 9 tasks, a 9.7% improvement over the best prior method (Diffusion QL at 89.3), while requiring only 1 NFE versus 5 for Diffusion QL, 2 for Consistency AC, and 20 for Diffuser. On the long-horizon Maze2d benchmark, RACTD reaches 133.4 average score with a single denoising step, surpassing Diffuser (119.5) which requires up to 256 NFEs, yielding up to 114× wall-clock speedup. On FrankaKitchen, RACTD achieves 62.5 (online) with 1 NFE, outperforming Diffusion QL (65.15 with 5 NFEs) and Consistency AC (43.3 with 2 NFEs) on the suboptimal partial/mixed datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RACTD outperforms all 13 baselines on the Gym-MuJoCo benchmark, achieving best or second-best scores on 8 of 9 tasks (the sole exception being HalfCheetah Medium-Expert where reward guidance is less beneficial). The closest competitor, Diffusion QL (89.3 avg, 5 NFE), is surpassed by 12.2 points on average, with the largest gaps on Hopper-Medium (115.5 vs 96.6) and Walker2d-Medium (118.8 vs 87.3). Consistency AC (89.8 avg, 2 NFE) and Consistency BC (75.9 avg, 2 NFE) are both clearly outperformed, confirming that the reward-aware distillation objective is more effective than concurrent actor-critic training. Diffuser (77.5 avg, 20 NFE) is beaten by 24 points on average while requiring 20× fewer function evaluations. On Maze2d, RACTD (133.4 avg, 1 NFE) surpasses Diffuser (119.5, up to 256 NFE) by 13.9 points and CTD (123.4, 1 NFE) by 10 points, with the largest gain on Large Maze (143.8 vs 123.0). On FrankaKitchen, RACTD (62.5 online) matches or exceeds Diffusion QL (65.15) on partial data while using 5× fewer NFEs, and decisively outperforms Consistency AC (43.3) and Flow Q-learning (53.4). Traditional model-free baselines (CQL, IQL, DT, TT, MOPO, MOReL, MBOP) are all substantially outperformed, with RACTD exceeding the best of these (TT at 78.9) by 22.6 points on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Consistency Trajectory Models (Kim et al., 2023) from image generation to offline RL by adding a reward-aware loss term to the standard CTM+DSM distillation objective. It departs from prior consistency-model applications to decision-making, which either perform behavior cloning (struggling with suboptimal data) or embed consistency models in actor-critic frameworks requiring concurrent multi-network training. The reward model architecture and guided-sampling paradigm are adapted from Diffuser (Janner et al., 2022), but the key conceptual shift is moving reward optimization from the multi-step sampling loop into a single-step distillation loss, enabling a fully decoupled training pipeline where the teacher, student, and reward model are trained independently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 142× inference speedup (1 NFE vs. 80 for the EDM teacher) makes diffusion-based planners viable for time-sensitive applications like real-time robotics, where multi-step denoising is prohibitive. The decoupled training framework removes the need for noise-aware reward models and concurrent actor-critic optimization, significantly simplifying the training pipeline. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-differentiable reward models, how training stability scales with more complex environments, and whether the reward-as-mode-selection trade-off (reduced sample diversity) becomes problematic in multi-task or distribution-shift settings. The paper acknowledges that CTD is prone to loss fluctuations and that incorporating the reward term may amplify this instability.
