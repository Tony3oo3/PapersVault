## Problem

Existing audio editing systems are limited in two ways: they require users to specify low-level, template-like operations (e.g., 'add birds', 'remove rain') rather than accepting high-level declarative instructions (e.g., 'make this sound like a sunny forest'), and they operate only on monaural audio, discarding spatial cues essential for immersive experiences. No prior framework can jointly reason over language and audio to decompose abstract user intent into a sequence of concrete editing operations while preserving spatial information.

## Input / Output

The system takes a 10-second stereo (binaural) audio clip at 24 kHz and a free-form declarative editing instruction as input. The ALM planner outputs a sequence of 2–5 atomic editing steps in natural language (e.g., 'Remove the sound of bell ring', 'Add the sound of seagulls calling at front by 3dB'), which the LDM editor then executes sequentially to produce the final edited stereo audio. The intermediate atomic steps are inspectable and editable by users, enabling human-in-the-loop control before the diffusion-based editing is applied.

## Method

SmartDJ decouples planning from execution into two independently trained modules. The ALM planner, initialized from Audio Flamingo 2 (3B parameters with a CLAP audio encoder and Qwen2.5-3B LLM), is fine-tuned with LoRA on cross-attention layers to autoregressively generate atomic editing steps conditioned on the original audio embedding and the declarative instruction. The LDM editor is a 597M-parameter Diffusion Transformer operating on a stereo audio VAE latent space (128 channels, 7.5× compression via 1D-CNN with snake activations); at each step it concatenates the previous audio latent with a noised target latent and denoises under cross-attention conditioning from FLAN-T5 text embeddings of the atomic step, using DDIM sampling with classifier-free guidance. A scalable data synthesis pipeline uses GPT-4o as a 'sound designer' to generate paired declarative instructions and atomic step sequences, while a rule-based signal-processing composer renders the corresponding before/after stereo audio by manipulating independently parameterized sound events (direction, volume, reverberation, timbre), producing 240K declarative pairs and 1M single-step pairs for supervised training.

## Results

On the declarative instruction audio editing task (Table 1), SmartDJ achieves the best FD (10.60), FAD (1.52), LSD (1.40), and CLAP (0.21) among all methods, with KL (2.84) comparable to the best baseline Audit (2.80). Inference time is 13.1s total (2.40s per edit step), significantly faster than training-free baselines (301–406s) while only slightly slower than Audit with ALM (11.6s). On single-step editing operations (Table 2), SmartDJ consistently outperforms all baselines across Add, Remove, Extract, Volume, Time, Reverb, Timbre, and Change Direction tasks, with particularly large margins on spatial metrics (e.g., GCC of 39.05 vs. 66–74 for baselines on Add). Human evaluation confirms SmartDJ is preferred with ≥80% win rate on quality and ≥87% on alignment for declarative editing (p < 0.001).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SmartDJ outperforms all six baselines on the primary declarative editing task. The end-to-end Audit (w/o ALM) performs worst overall (FD 28.56, FAD 10.00), confirming that directly mapping declarative instructions to final audio without intermediate reasoning is ineffective. Among training-free baselines guided by ALM-generated atomic steps, SDEdit (FD 19.66), ZETA (FD 20.74), and AudioEditor (FD 19.91) all trail SmartDJ (FD 10.60) by roughly 9–10 points, while DDIM Inversion is notably worse (FD 24.70, FAD 9.43). The trainable Audit baseline with ALM (FD 21.50, FAD 5.67) is the closest competitor but still lags SmartDJ by ~11 FD and ~4.15 FAD, with the gap attributed to Audit's mel-spectrogram VAE discarding phase information critical for spatial cues. On single-step operations, SmartDJ's advantage widens dramatically on spatial metrics: for Change Direction, GCC drops from 40–82 (baselines) to 26.02, and CRW from 167–218 to 48.69. In the ablation comparing against AudioSep (a dedicated sound separation model) on the Extract task, SmartDJ achieves comparable or slightly better FAD (2.55 vs. 2.86) and KL (0.78 vs. 0.88), showing competitive performance even on specialized separation tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

SmartDJ extends the audio editing lineage from Audit (Wang et al., 2023) and WavCraft (Liang et al., 2024), which rely on fixed procedural templates like 'add the sound of birds,' to a declarative paradigm where users specify high-level scene goals. It builds directly on Audio Flamingo 2 as the ALM backbone (fine-tuned with LoRA) and adapts diffusion-based editing techniques originally developed for images (SDEdit, P2P, IP2P) to the audio domain. The key architectural departure is the planner-editor separation: an ALM decomposes declarative instructions into atomic steps, which a stereo-aware DiT-based LDM then executes sequentially, rather than a single monolithic diffusion model handling the entire transformation. The stereo VAE (inspired by DAC and Stable-Audio-Open) replaces the mel-spectrogram VAE in Audit, preserving phase information critical for spatial cues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to combine declarative instruction understanding with stereo audio editing, opening a path toward natural-language-driven sound design for VR/AR, gaming, and cinematic post-production. The scalable GPT-4o-powered data synthesis pipeline addresses a fundamental bottleneck—no public dataset pairs declarative instructions with multi-step editing trajectories—and could be adapted to other sequential editing domains. Key limitations include the need for retraining to support new atomic operations, the ALM's difficulty with contradictory or purely emotional instructions (e.g., 'eerie yet cheerful'), and the 13-second inference time versus 2 seconds for end-to-end baselines. The authors identify end-to-end joint training of the ALM and generative backbone as the natural next step toward tighter semantic-signal coupling.
