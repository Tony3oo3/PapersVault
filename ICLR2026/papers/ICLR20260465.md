## Problem

Conventional 2D local attention patterns (window, sliding, neighborhood) in vision transformers use row-major token ordering, which causes spatially adjacent tokens to be non-contiguous in the 1D sequence. This misalignment produces many partial blocks and limits the empty block ratio when executed under block-sparse kernels like FlexAttention, preventing the substantial speedups that high sparsity would otherwise enable. The paper asks whether a different token ordering can make local attention patterns more block-sparse without changing the attention semantics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D image feature maps (e.g., 56×56 to 256×256 spatial resolutions) produced by patchifying RGB images, and the feature map dimensions determine a unique Hilbert-curve path that is computed once and cached. After Hilbert reordering, windows or neighborhoods are formed as contiguous segments of the 1D sequence, and the attention output retains the same shape as the input feature map before being fed to a classification head. The approach is resolution-agnostic: any (H, W) feature map size maps to its own Hilbert path, and the same path is reused across layers and batches.

## Method

The core mechanism reorders image tokens along a Hilbert curve before constructing local attention windows or neighborhoods, exploiting the curve's locality-preserving property so that 2D-adjacent tokens become contiguous in the 1D sequence. This transforms what were previously scattered partial blocks into compact full blocks surrounded by empty blocks, raising the empty block ratio from, e.g., 87.5% to 96.88% (window attention) or 80.17% to 87.84% (neighborhood attention) under FlexAttention's block-sparse execution. Three attention variants are defined—Hilbert Window Attention (HWA), Hilbert Slide Attention (HSA), and Hilbert Neighborhood Attention (HNA)—and two full models are instantiated: Hilbert Window Transformer (HWT, analogous to Swin with 1D-shifted windows and global RPB) and Hilbert Neighborhood Transformer (HNT, analogous to NAT with 1D neighborhood attention). The entire pipeline is implemented via FlexAttention's programmable mask_mod and score_mod interface, requiring no hand-written kernels, and the Hilbert path is cached at initialization to avoid per-forward reordering cost.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (224×224), HWT-T achieves 81.0% top-1 accuracy (vs. Swin-T's 81.2%) and HNT-mini achieves 81.6% (vs. NAT-mini's 81.8%), with at most a 0.2% accuracy drop. In terms of efficiency, HWA(Flex) delivers up to 4.0× forward speedup over dense WSA (0.68 ms vs. 2.74 ms at 128×128 input, 16×16 window), while HSA/HNA achieve up to ~18× speedup over unoptimized SA. On CIFAR-10/100, HWT and HNT match Swin and NAT within 0.1–0.2% accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Swin Transformer (WSA), HWT matches accuracy within 0.2% on ImageNet-1K while HWA(Flex) runs 4.0× faster in forward pass and 1.8× faster in backward pass at 128×128 resolution, with sparsity rising from 87.5% to 98.44%. Against NAT (NA2D), HNT similarly matches accuracy (81.6% vs. 81.8%) while HNA(NAT) is 1.8× faster than NA2D(NAT) at 56×56 and ~2× faster at 96×96. Against xFormers, HWA(xFormers) is dramatically faster than WSA(xFormers) (e.g., 0.79 ms vs. 24.46 ms forward at 128×128) because HWA's contiguous 1D windows allow block-diagonal fused dense kernels, whereas WSA's scattered windows force an element-wise sparse path. Against FlexAttention baselines (WSA(Flex), SA(Flex), NA2D(Flex)), the Hilbert variants consistently show higher empty-block sparsity (e.g., 98.44% vs. 87.50% for window attention; 95.87% vs. 87.89% for slide attention) and correspondingly lower runtimes. Against NATTEN, HNA(NAT) outperforms NA2D(NAT) at higher resolutions (e.g., 0.51 ms vs. 1.07 ms forward at 96×96, K=11×11), confirming that Hilbert reordering benefits even already-optimized 2D neighborhood kernels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: local attention architectures for vision (Swin Transformer's window attention, NAT's neighborhood attention) and block-sparse kernel optimization (FlexAttention, FlashAttention, NATTEN, xFormers). It identifies a specific mismatch between how 2D local attention patterns are laid out in row-major 1D sequences and how block-sparse kernels exploit sparsity, then resolves it by reordering tokens along a Hilbert curve so that spatially local windows become contiguous 1D blocks. Unlike prior work that designs new attention patterns or hand-optimizes kernels for fixed patterns, this paper treats the token ordering itself as the optimization variable, making it a drop-in reordering that composes with existing block-sparse backends without kernel modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for anyone deploying vision transformers at high resolution: up to 4× speedup for window attention and 18× for slide attention with ≤0.2% accuracy loss on ImageNet, and the approach is backend-agnostic (works with FlexAttention, FlashAttention-2, xFormers, and NATTEN). A useful negative result is that WSA under FlexAttention is actually slower than dense WSA due to partial-block overhead, highlighting that naively applying block-sparse kernels to row-major local attention can be counterproductive. Open questions include whether the Hilbert reordering generalizes to video (3D), whether it composes well with other sparsity patterns (e.g., cross-attention in diffusion models), and whether the modest accuracy gap on CIFAR-100 (0.1–0.2%) persists at larger model scales or with longer training.
