## Problem

Existing large-scale robot manipulation datasets are costly, tightly coupled to specific embodiments, and critically lack the fine-grained intermediate representations (subtasks, traces, bounding boxes, affordance boxes, grasp poses) that plan-then-execute VLA paradigms require. Prior annotation efforts are either fully automatic with limited reliability, small-scale, or misaligned with step-wise actions, leaving a gap in both scale and annotation quality for training and benchmarking embodied reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263847/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes two-view RGB observations (one third-person, one wrist camera), a language instruction, and optionally a Flexible Chain-of-Thought (F-CoT) sequence containing heterogeneous intermediate representations serialized as text tokens or drawn as visual prompts on additional input images. The Executor produces multi-step continuous action chunks via a diffusion head, while the Planner generates intermediate representations (subtasks, primitive skills, object/gripper bounding boxes, affordance boxes, 2D traces, grasp poses, contact points, placement proposals) and embodied VQA answers. The underlying dataset spans 230k episodes across 571 scenes and 6 robot arms, with ~61M-frame object grounding, ~70M-frame gripper traces, and ~760k language clip annotations, all temporally synchronized with executed actions.

## Method

RoboInter-VLA follows a plan-then-execute architecture with a VLM-based Planner (Qwen2.5-VL or LLaVA-One-Vision, trained via cross-entropy on curated VQA) and a Qwen2.5-VL + Diffusion Transformer Executor that uses an information aggregator to compress hidden states of all tokens and intermediate representations into conditioning features. Three variants are supported: IC-E2E (Planner VLM injected as a stronger feature extractor into the Executor), EC-E2E (joint optimization of intermediate-representation reasoning and action generation), and Modular (independent Planner and Executor bridged by F-CoT, with asynchronous inference). F-CoT is a flexible chain-of-thought that composes subsets of intermediate representations in textual or visual form, enabling task-specific configuration. The dataset is annotated via a lightweight GUI (RoboInter-Tool) combining SAM2-based segmentation, gripper detection, and human-in-the-loop verification, producing dense per-frame labels across 10+ representation categories that substantially exceed prior work in both scale and annotation diversity.

## Results

On third-party embodied benchmarks, RoboInter-Qwen-7B achieves 65.8% on Where2Place, 85.6% on RoboRefIt, and 74.4 on RoboVQA, substantially outperforming its base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B (18.9%, 75.8%, 38.4) and the embodied VLM RoboBrain-2.0-7B (63.6%, 8.8%, 31.6). On the RoboInter-VQA benchmark, RoboInter-LlavaOV-7B leads spatial generation (82.9% Object Grounding, 46.3% Grasp Affordance) and temporal understanding (81.9% Visual Trace, 83.9% Task Planning T/F), while RoboInter-Qwen-7B achieves the best temporal generation (323 DTW for Trace, 63.4 BLEU for Task Planning). For the VLA Executor, RoboInter-Te-Modular achieves the best learned open-loop mOLS of 0.3543 (vs. 0.3086 Vanilla), and in real-world closed-loop evaluation, EC-E2E attains 60.0% OOD success with only an 8.3% ID→OOD drop, outperforming IC-E2E (58.3% OOD, 19.0% drop) and Vanilla (38.3% OOD, 26.7% drop).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against general VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-OV, InternVL3), RoboInter models achieve 3–7× improvements on embodied benchmarks (e.g., RoboInter-Qwen-7B: 85.6% vs. 75.8% RoboRefIt; 74.4 vs. 38.4 RoboVQA) while maintaining competitive grounding scores (88.4% vs. 87.2% Refcoco-g) and only marginal drops on general benchmarks (MME 2281 vs. 2306). Against the closest embodied VLM, RoboBrain-2.0, RoboInter-Qwen-7B gains 76.8% relative on RoboRefIt (85.6% vs. 8.8%) and 42.8% on RoboVQA (74.4 vs. 31.6), though RoboBrain-2.0-7B slightly leads on Where2Place (63.6% vs. 65.8% is actually RoboInter winning). On the RoboInter-VQA benchmark, closed-source models (GPT-4o-mini, Gemini-2.5-Flash) and general VLMs score near-random on spatial generation (<40%), while RoboInter models reach 75–83%, confirming the necessity of embodied intermediate representation training. For the VLA Executor, RoboInter-Te-Modular (mOLS 0.3543) outperforms VLA-OS (0.3035) and Vanilla (0.3086) in open-loop, and in real-world closed-loop, both IC-E2E (77.3% ID) and EC-E2E (60.0% OOD) surpass OpenVLA (53.3% ID / 20.0% OOD) and π₀ (73.3% ID / 46.7% OOD), with EC-E2E showing the smallest ID→OOD degradation (8.3% vs. 18.3% for π₀). On SimplerEnv cross-embodiment evaluation, RoboInter-IC-E2E (65.7 avg) surpasses CogACT (63.8) and π₀ (43.0), validating transferability of the pretrained Planner to unseen robot platforms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the plan-then-execute VLA paradigm (ECoT, VLA-OS, π0.5) by addressing its critical bottleneck: the absence of large-scale, high-quality intermediate representation annotations. Architecturally, it builds on InternVLA-M1 and CogACT for the Executor design, and on Qwen2.5-VL / LLaVA-One-Vision for the VLM Planner backbone. Unlike prior annotation efforts (LLARVA's auto-generated traces, ECoT's Gemini-based pseudo-CoT, ShareRobot's small-scale human+auto labels), RoboInter uniquely combines human-in-the-loop verification with 10+ simultaneous intermediate representation types (subtasks, skills, segmentation, bounding boxes, affordance, grasp poses, traces, contact points) at per-frame density across 230k episodes, and packages this into a unified data-benchmark-model suite rather than a single contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RoboInter-Data and RoboInter-VQA are likely to become standard resources for the embodied AI community, filling a gap that has constrained plan-then-execute research: no prior dataset offered dense, multi-type, temporally-synchronized intermediate annotations at this scale. The F-CoT framework provides a practical, modular interface for researchers to select and combine representations without re-architecting their VLA. Key open questions include the inference-latency cost of explicit reasoning (2.2s vs 0.13s for EC-E2E vs IC-E2E), whether the ID/OOD trade-off between implicit and explicit conditioning generalizes beyond tabletop tasks, and whether the annotation pipeline can extend to deformable-object or bimanual manipulation at comparable quality.
