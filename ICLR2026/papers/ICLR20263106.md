## Problem

Model quantization and attention sparsification are two orthogonal compression techniques for video diffusion transformers, but naively combining them causes severe performance degradation due to an amplified attention shift: sparsity discards low-magnitude attention weights while quantization introduces systematic perturbations to the remaining products, and these effects compound rather than simply add. No prior work addresses this interaction, leaving a gap between the theoretical Pareto frontier of combined compression and what is actually achievable in practice.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language text prompt and produces a high-resolution video clip (e.g., 720×1280p at 60–80 frames) from a pre-trained video diffusion transformer (Wan2.1-1.3B/14B or HunyuanVideo-13B) running at W4A8 or W6A8 precision with as low as 15% attention density. During calibration, a small set of 20 random samples is used per transformer block (15 epochs each); during inference, a fixed cache-refreshing interval of 5 denoising steps governs when full-attention residuals are recomputed versus reused.

## Method

QuantSparse is a unified post-training compression framework with two complementary components. During calibration, Multi-Scale Salient Attention Distillation (MSAD) aligns quantized attention with its FP counterpart using two memory-efficient supervision signals: global guidance distills attention on downsampled token sequences (average pooling with stride s, reducing O(L²) to O(L̃²)) to capture coarse structural topology, while local guidance focuses high-resolution supervision on a small set of top-k salient tokens that dominate the heavy-tailed attention distribution. During inference, Second-Order Sparse Attention Reparameterization (SSAR) recovers information lost under sparsity by caching and reusing the second-order residual (the temporal difference of first-order residuals), which exhibits significantly higher temporal stability than the first-order residual under quantization noise; an SVD projection onto the top-r dominant principal components further suppresses residual temporal variance at negligible computational cost. The framework achieves 3.68× storage reduction and 1.88× inference acceleration on HunyuanVideo-13B at W4A8 with 15% attention density while maintaining near-lossless quality (20.88 PSNR vs. 16.85 for the SOTA quantization-only baseline Q-VDiT).

## Results

On HunyuanVideo-13B under W4A8 quantization with 15% attention density, QuantSparse achieves 20.88 PSNR, 0.678 SSIM, and 0.273 LPIPS, substantially outperforming the SOTA quantization baseline Q-VDiT (16.85 PSNR at 100% density) while delivering 3.68× storage reduction and 1.88× end-to-end inference acceleration. On Wan2.1-14B under the same W4A8/15% setting, QuantSparse reaches 18.22 PSNR and 1.74× speedup with 3.80× storage compression. Notably, QuantSparse at 15% density even surpasses the full-precision model on VQA (81.19 vs. 81.23) and CLIPSIM (0.184 vs. 0.184) on HunyuanVideo, demonstrating near-lossless compression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QuantSparse decisively outperforms all nine baselines across both models. On HunyuanVideo-13B (W4A8, 15% density), QuantSparse achieves 20.88 PSNR, exceeding Q-VDiT+SVG (16.66) by 4.22 dB and QuaRot+SVG (15.34) by 5.54 dB, while also surpassing the quantization-only Q-VDiT at 100% density (16.85) by 4.03 dB. On Wan2.1-14B (W4A8, 15%), QuantSparse reaches 18.22 PSNR versus Q-VDiT+SVG's 15.18 and QuaRot+SVG's 13.40, a gap of 3.04 and 4.82 dB respectively. The naive combination of existing quantization (Q-VDiT, QuaRot) with sparse attention (SVG, DFT, Jenga) consistently degrades performance relative to quantization alone, confirming the paper's 'amplified attention shift' hypothesis. QuantSparse also matches or slightly exceeds the full-precision model on perceptual metrics (VQA, CLIPSIM) while providing 1.74–1.88× speedup and 3.68–3.80× storage reduction, a trade-off no baseline achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established compression lines for diffusion transformers: post-training quantization (Q-VDiT, ViDiT-Q, QuaRot) and sparse attention (DiTFastAttn, SparseVideoGen, Jenga). It builds most directly on DiTFastAttn's first-order temporal residual caching, extending it to a second-order residual with SVD-based projection to handle the temporal instability introduced by quantization noise. The key conceptual departure is the formal demonstration (Proposition 3.1) that quantization and sparsification are not independently composable—their interaction produces a super-additive 'amplified attention shift'—which motivates the unified MSAD calibration and SSAR inference pipeline rather than treating the two techniques as orthogonal plug-ins.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: 3.68× storage reduction and 1.88× end-to-end speedup on 13B–14B video diffusion models while maintaining near-lossless quality (20.88 PSNR vs. 16.85 for the SOTA quantization-only baseline Q-VDiT). The finding that naive stacking of quantization and sparsification is substantially worse than either alone (0.685 vs. 0.350 attention shift) is a useful warning for the broader compression community and suggests that future multi-technique pipelines require co-design. Open questions include whether the SVD-rank and cache-interval hyperparameters transfer across architectures without re-tuning, whether the calibration overhead (15 epochs per block) remains acceptable as models grow beyond 14B, and whether the approach extends to autoregressive or hybrid video generation paradigms.
