## Problem

Existing few-shot fine-tuning methods for vision-language models rely on either parameter-efficient tuning (which limits model flexibility) or point-wise consistency constraints (which restrict individual sample representations). Both paradigms neglect the geometric structure of the data distribution in feature space, causing distortion of the pretrained semantic manifold and degrading generalization performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263701/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (processed by CLIP ViT-B/16) and frozen text class prompts (e.g., 'a photo of a {class}') to produce class labels via softmax over cosine similarities. During training, K-shot subsets (K=1,2,4,8,16) are used with random cropping and horizontal flipping augmentation, and the final prediction logits are a weighted sum of fine-tuned and zero-shot outputs (α=0.3). The model operates on 11 benchmark datasets spanning general, fine-grained, scene, texture, and satellite imagery classification.

## Method

MPS-Tuning introduces two complementary mechanisms: (1) Manifold Alignment Regularization (MAR), which constrains the L1-norm difference between Gram matrices of the original and fine-tuned models at both batch level (global topology) and token level (local geometry), theoretically proven to upper-bound the p-th order Gromov-Wasserstein distance under a fixed natural coupling; and (2) Hierarchical Manifold Sculpting (HMS), a multimodal query-support contrastive matching task that pulls same-category image-text pairs closer and pushes different-category pairs apart, extended to intermediate transformer layers via a pseudo-forward projection that bypasses attention modules. The training employs a hierarchical partial fine-tuning strategy (first layer group frozen, second group with zero-initialized parallel linear layers, third group fully trainable) and optimizes the combined loss L_CE + λ₁·L_MAR + λ₂·L_HMS with SGD and cosine decay over 50 epochs.

## Results

On the 11-dataset CLIP benchmark (ViT-B/16), MPS-Tuning achieves the highest average accuracy across all few-shot settings: 73.55% (1-shot), 76.66% (4-shot), and 86.85% (16-shot). The advantage over the strongest baseline widens with more shots, reaching +5.37% over GalLop (81.48%) at 16-shot. On domain generalization (trained on ImageNet, tested on ImageNet-Sketch and ImageNet-V2), MPS-Tuning achieves 64.41% average, outperforming the next best method AMU-Tuning (63.57%) by 0.84%. Training efficiency is comparable to existing methods at 95.65 FPS on SUN397.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPS-Tuning outperforms all 16 baselines on average across the 11-dataset benchmark in every shot setting. At 1-shot, it leads TAC (73.18%) by 0.37% and MMRL (72.67%) by 0.88%; at 4-shot, it edges out TAC (76.49%) by 0.17% and TextRefiner (75.90%) by 0.76%; at 16-shot, the gap widens dramatically to +5.37% over GalLop (81.48%) and +6.16% over TAC (80.69%). The method is particularly strong on cross-domain datasets: on FGVCAircraft (16-shot) it reaches 69.03% vs. 48.48% for GalLop and 43.27% for LDC, and on UCF101 it achieves 89.87% vs. 84.14% for GalLop. On domain generalization (ImageNet→Sketch/V2), MPS-Tuning (64.41% avg) surpasses AMU-Tuning (63.57%), LDC (62.94%), and TAC (62.94%). The paper notes that MPS-Tuning does not achieve SOTA on a few specific 1-shot and 2-shot datasets, attributing this to the Gram matrix alignment struggling under extreme data scarcity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms for adapting CLIP in few-shot settings—parameter-efficient fine-tuning (CoOp, CoCoOp, CLIP-Adapter) and point-wise consistency regularization (PromptSRC)—by reframing the problem in terms of the geometric structure of the feature manifold. Its core theoretical contribution derives a tractable upper bound on the Gromov-Wasserstein distance (Memoli, 2011) via Gram matrix alignment, which to the authors' knowledge is the first application of GW distance theory to VLM fine-tuning. The Hierarchical Manifold Sculpting component extends contrastive query-support matching to intermediate transformer layers via a pseudo-forward projection, a design that builds on the semantic hierarchy observations in CLIP (Gandelsman et al., 2024) but applies it to a new optimization objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GW-distance framing provides a principled, theoretically grounded alternative to ad-hoc consistency losses, and the observed adaptive behavior of MAR—automatically relaxing constraints on cross-domain datasets while preserving structure on in-domain ones—suggests a more nuanced mechanism than rigid point-wise constraints. However, the method still underperforms SOTA in extreme 1-shot and 2-shot regimes, and the Gram matrix alignment may struggle when the manifold structure is too sparsely sampled to be meaningful. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to larger VLMs (e.g., SigLIP-2, EVA-CLIP), whether the GW connection can be exploited for more direct optimization, and whether the partial fine-tuning strategy generalizes beyond ViT-B/16.
