## Problem

Existing crowd simulation methods either model individuals independently (causing error accumulation over long horizons) or rely on simplified microscopic physics (e.g., Social Force Model) that fail in dense, interactive scenarios. Deep learning approaches lack macroscopic physical constraints and diffusion-based variants suffer from high inference latency. The paper addresses the absence of a global, mass-conserving physical law that can regularize microscopic trajectory prediction while remaining computationally efficient.

## Input / Output

The model consumes per-pedestrian positions, velocities, accelerations, destination coordinates, static obstacle positions, and a window of h historical frames, all sampled at a unified 0.08 s timestep after cubic interpolation. It outputs the next-step acceleration (from which position and velocity are updated via standard integration) and a continuous density field over a discrete spatial grid. During training the Neural ODE integrates density over τ future steps, but at inference only the single-step trajectory prediction network f_θ is invoked autoregressively, making the ODE component a training-time regularizer rather than a runtime dependency.

## Method

STDDN embeds the fluid-dynamics continuity equation ∂ρ/∂t + ∇·(ρv) = 0 as a differentiable physical constraint by formulating density evolution as a Neural ODE whose right-hand side is a Density-Velocity Coupled Graph (DVCG) module. The DVCG constructs a dynamic graph where current-step velocities define incoming edges and predicted next-step velocities define outgoing edges, so the flux at each grid node is jointly determined by pedestrian motion and local density. A Differentiable Density Mapping module uses a temperature-controlled softmax over squared distances to grid centers to produce smooth, gradient-friendly density values, while a Continuous Cross-Grid Detection module computes Jensen-Shannon divergence between consecutive position distributions to generate differentiable cross-grid masks that gate flux terms. Lightweight node embeddings (outer products of per-node vectors) reduce the weight matrix storage from O(N²) to O(N·d). Training jointly minimizes a weighted sum of velocity prediction error and ODE-integrated density error, and the Euler solver is chosen over higher-order methods to align with the discrete autoregressive inference schedule.

## Results

STDDN achieves the best MAE and OT across all four primary datasets: 0.8875/1.3582 on GC, 1.7747/3.6503 on UCY, 0.5185/0.6918 on ETH, and 0.2952/0.1445 on HOTEL. Compared to the second-best method SPDiff, STDDN reduces inference latency by 50–90% (e.g., 86.85 ms vs. 206.99 ms on GC; 44.66 ms vs. 471.05 ms on UCY) while simultaneously improving MAE by 2.6–12.7% and OT by 2.5–19.8%. The model also uses fewer parameters than most baselines (0.05–0.20M), making it both more accurate and more efficient.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STDDN outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across all four primary datasets. Against the closest competitor SPDiff (physics-guided diffusion), STDDN improves MAE by 2.6% (GC), 5.4% (UCY), 6.0% (ETH), and 12.7% (HOTEL), while cutting inference latency by 50–90%—the largest gap being 471 ms → 44.7 ms on UCY. Pure physics-based methods (SFM, CA) are competitive on ETH/HOTEL (lower density) but degrade sharply on dense, long-duration scenes like GC and UCY, where STDDN's continuity-equation constraint provides the most benefit. Data-driven methods (STGCNN, PECNet, MID) suffer from large error accumulation in long-horizon simulation; MID in particular shows the worst OT scores (35.2 on GC, 47.9 on UCY) due to its multi-step diffusion inference. Among physics-guided baselines, PCS and NSP are competitive on ETH/HOTEL but trail STDDN by 6–13% in MAE on dense datasets, confirming that macroscopic density constraints (beyond individual-level SFM) are critical for long-term stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

STDDN builds on two key lineages: the Neural ODE framework (Chen et al., 2018) for continuous-time dynamics, and the SPDiff architecture (Chen et al., 2024) for the microscopic trajectory prediction backbone. However, it departs fundamentally from prior physics-guided crowd simulation methods (PCS, NSP, SPDiff) that embed only microscopic Social Force Model interactions, by introducing the macroscopic continuity equation from fluid dynamics as a differentiable structural constraint. This reframes trajectory prediction as a density-transport problem on a dynamic graph, where individual velocities define edge fluxes and a Neural ODE integrates the resulting density evolution — a formulation not previously applied to crowd simulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent practical bottleneck: diffusion-based physics-guided methods (SPDiff, MID) require multiple forward passes per frame, making large-scale real-time simulation infeasible, while purely data-driven methods accumulate errors over long horizons. STDDN's single-pass inference with 50–90% latency reduction alongside accuracy gains makes physics-consistent crowd simulation more deployable in safety-critical applications. Open questions remain around boundary handling in open systems (the continuity equation's mass conservation is violated by pedestrian entry/exit), the trade-off between hard and soft physical constraints, and whether the grid-based formulation can be replaced by implicit spatial representations for more complex geometries.
