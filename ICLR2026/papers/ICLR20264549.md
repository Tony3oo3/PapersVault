## Problem

Online reinforcement learning for diffusion models is hindered by intractable likelihoods, forcing existing GRPO-style methods to discretize the reverse sampling process. This introduces three fundamental drawbacks: forward-reverse inconsistency (risking degeneration into cascaded Gaussians), solver restriction to first-order SDE samplers, and a complicated two-model optimization scheme for classifier-free guidance. The paper asks whether diffusion reinforcement can instead be performed directly on the forward (noising) process, decoupling policy optimization from the sampling procedure.

## Input / Output

The method takes text prompts and clean generated images (not full denoising trajectories) along with scalar reward scores as training data, and produces a single CFG-free velocity-predictor network for text-to-image generation at 512×512 resolution. Data collection can use any black-box ODE or SDE solver, and only the final clean images with their associated rewards are stored for the gradient step. The trained model is initialized from the conditional branch of SD3.5-Medium (2.5B parameters) without the unconditional branch, and is evaluated with a 40-step first-order ODE sampler.

## Method

DiffusionNFT replaces policy gradient with a contrastive supervised learning objective on the forward diffusion process. It splits collected samples into positive (high-reward) and negative (low-reward) subsets, then defines a 'reinforcement guidance' direction Δ as the proportional difference between the implicit positive and negative velocity fields (Theorem 3.1). The training loss (Eq. 5) jointly minimizes flow-matching errors on both branches using an implicit parameterization: v_θ⁺ = (1−β)v_old + βv_θ and v_θ⁻ = (1+β)v_old − βv_θ, so a single network v_θ is optimized toward v_old + (2/β)Δ (Theorem 3.2). This formulation is likelihood-free, preserves forward-process consistency, allows arbitrary solvers for data collection, and natively integrates guidance into the learned policy without a separate CFG model. Practical components include a soft EMA update of the sampling policy (η_i schedule), adaptive time-dependent loss weighting via self-normalized x_0 regression, and group-normalized reward-to-optimality-probability mapping.

## Results

On the GenEval task, DiffusionNFT improves the CFG-free SD3.5-Medium score from 0.24 to 0.98 within ~1k iterations, while FlowGRPO reaches only 0.95 after >5k steps with additional CFG. On OCR, DiffusionNFT achieves 0.97 in just 150 steps versus FlowGRPO's 0.96 at 1k steps. In multi-reward joint training, the final CFG-free 2.5B model surpasses CFG-based larger models (SD3.5-L at 8B and FLUX.1-Dev at 12B) across all in-domain and out-of-domain metrics, including PickScore 24.03, HPSv2.1 0.315, and Aesthetic 6.17.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FlowGRPO, DiffusionNFT achieves 3×–25× wall-clock efficiency while matching or exceeding final scores on every single-reward task: GenEval 0.98 vs 0.97 (1k vs 4k steps), OCR 0.97 vs 0.96 (150 vs 1k steps), and PickScore 24.03 vs 23.62 (2k vs 4k steps). In the multi-reward setting, the CFG-free 2.5B DiffusionNFT model outperforms the CFG-based SD3.5-L (8B) on GenEval (0.98 vs 0.71), OCR (0.97 vs 0.68), PickScore (24.03 vs 22.91), and HPSv2.1 (0.315 vs 0.288), and surpasses FLUX.1-Dev (12B) on GenEval (0.98 vs 0.66), OCR (0.97 vs 0.59), and PickScore (24.03 vs 22.84). Notably, DiffusionNFT operates entirely without CFG, whereas FlowGRPO and the larger baselines all rely on CFG at inference, making the comparison particularly favorable to the proposed method. The only metric where FlowGRPO retains an edge is Aesthetic (6.42 vs 6.17 in the PickScore single-reward setting), though this is a narrow margin on an out-of-domain metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Negative-aware FineTuning (NFT) paradigm from LLMs (Chen et al., 2025c) into the diffusion domain, but makes a fundamental conceptual departure from the dominant GRPO-style approach (FlowGRPO, DanceGRPO) by performing policy optimization on the forward noising process rather than the reverse denoising process. Where prior diffusion RL methods discretize the reverse SDE to obtain tractable Gaussian transitions for policy gradients, DiffusionNFT instead defines a contrastive flow-matching loss between implicit positive and negative policies, eliminating the need for likelihood estimation and SDE-based samplers. The implicit parameterization technique for integrating guidance into a single model draws on recent guidance-free training work (Chen et al., 2025a), and the overall framework is positioned as a supervised-learning-based alternative to policy gradient methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that diffusion RL can operate entirely on the forward process, this work removes several structural bottlenecks (trajectory storage, SDE solver coupling, forward-reverse inconsistency) that have constrained the field, and the 3×–25× wall-clock efficiency gains make online diffusion RL substantially more practical. The finding that a CFG-free model can rapidly surpass CFG-based baselines through RL post-training challenges the assumption that guidance is an irreplaceable inference-time crutch. Open questions include whether the forward-process formulation generalizes to video generation, whether the theoretical guarantees (requiring unlimited data and capacity) hold in practice at scale, and whether the negative-aware component remains critical when reward signals are sparse or noisy.
