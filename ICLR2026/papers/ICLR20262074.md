## Problem

Vision Transformers produce high-norm artifact tokens that, during knowledge distillation, dominate the MSE objective and bias student gradients toward mimicking a few outlier patches rather than consolidating the majority inlier structure. Prior mitigation (ViTKD's random masking) indiscriminately removes informative signals alongside artifacts, creating an inherent trade-off between artifact suppression and information preservation that has no principled resolution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262074/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes standard 224×224 RGB images and operates on patch-level feature maps (n×d matrices) extracted at selected intermediate ViT layers (e.g., layers 17 and 23 for ViT-L). The LoRA-based adapter modifies teacher features in-place before they serve as distillation targets, while the student produces task-specific outputs—class labels, segmentation masks, or depth maps—via a linear probe on the final hidden layer. No task-specific losses are used during distillation; only feature-level alignment is optimized.

## Method

SiNGER refines teacher features by adding a low-rank perturbation (ΔF = F·φ_down·φ_up) via a frozen-teacher LoRA adapter, where the perturbation is constrained to the left-nullspace of the next transformer block so that the block's output remains unchanged (information preservation by construction). The adapter is initialized with the r smallest left singular vectors of the linearized FFN of the next block (obtained by removing GELU and biases, justified empirically as the dominant source of norm inflation). Training jointly optimizes three losses: standard feature-matching KD to the refined teacher, an outlier suppression loss penalizing patches whose norms exceed a percentile threshold, and a Gram-matrix information preservation loss aligning pairwise feature relations at the next layer. This nullspace-guided design ensures that outlier energy is redirected into directions invisible to the subsequent block, suppressing artifacts without eroding the informative signal that makes distillation from larger teachers beneficial.

## Results

SiNGER achieves consistent multi-task gains over FitNet across all four teacher–student pairs (ViT-L→S, ViT-L→T, DeiT-III-L→B, DeiT-III-S→T). Headline numbers include 79.13% top-1 on ImageNet-1K (ViT-L→S, +6.63 over FitNet), 30.06 mIoU on ADE-20K (+4.91), and 0.9026 RMSE on NYUd-v2 (+0.0877). The largest relative improvements appear on domain-shift (DS) and fine-grained (FG) benchmarks, where SiNGER gains up to +13.37 and +8.70 points respectively. The only task where SiNGER does not improve over the non-distilled student is iNaturalist-2019, attributed to the teacher's intrinsic long-tail uncertainty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SiNGER outperforms FitNet on every task across all four teacher–student configurations, with the most dramatic gains on domain-shift robustness (up to +13.37 points on DeiT-III-L→B) and fine-grained classification (up to +8.70 on ViT-L→S). Against ViTKD, SiNGER's advantage is overwhelming: on ViT-L→ViT-T, ViTKD collapses to 5.07% ImageNet top-1 and 2.08% DS accuracy, while SiNGER reaches 70.59% and 38.87% respectively. On DeiT-III-L→B, ViTKD actually beats FitNet on ImageNet (66.54 vs 60.00) but still trails SiNGER by 12.83 points, and ViTKD's random masking degrades ADE-20K mIoU to 19.58 versus SiNGER's 29.47. The sole exception is iNaturalist-2019, where SiNGER slightly underperforms the non-distilled student (41.11 vs 43.95 for ViT-T), which the authors attribute to the teacher's high entropy on long-tail classes rather than to artifact-related degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the discovery of high-norm artifacts in ViT representations (Darcet et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025), (2) FitNet-style intermediate feature distillation (Romero et al., 2015) as the standard KD framework for ViTs, and (3) LoRA's low-rank adapter structure (Hu et al., 2022) repurposed here not for task adaptation but for principled teacher-feature refinement. The key departure from prior artifact-mitigation work (register tokens, ViTKD's random masking) is the nullspace-guided perturbation: by restricting the adapter's update to the left-nullspace of the next block, SiNGER suppresses outlier norms while provably leaving the next-block output unchanged, resolving the suppression-vs-preservation trade-off that plagued earlier approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously underappreciated failure mode in ViT distillation—outlier-driven gradient bias that causes students to overfit to a few high-norm tokens—and provides a mathematically grounded, low-overhead fix (1.2% extra parameters, ~10% training time). The consistent multi-task gains (6–13 points over FitNet across classification, segmentation, and depth) suggest that artifact-aware distillation is a necessary ingredient for transferring knowledge from large ViTs, not an optional refinement. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-modal or non-ViT backbones, whether suppressing artifacts is sufficient or whether the root power-iteration-like accumulation mechanism should be addressed at the pre-training level, and the mild finding that nullspace initialization does not measurably outperform random initialization (Table 4) leaves the theoretical motivation partially unvalidated in practice.
