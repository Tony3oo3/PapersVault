## Problem

Few-shot industrial anomaly detection is challenging because limited normal samples make it difficult to learn comprehensive representations that distinguish subtle anomalies, especially under category-agnostic multi-class conditions. Prior approaches either rely on textual prompts from vision-language models, require class-specific memory banks, or demand large training sets. This paper reveals that foundation visual encoders trained purely on natural images already encode anomaly-sensitive structure in their embeddings, and that this property can be exploited with a lightweight projector to achieve strong few-shot detection without any textual assistance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image resized to 512×512×3 and produces both an image-level anomaly score (average of Top-K highest patch scores, with K=10 for MVTec-AD/BTAD/DTD and K=6 for VisA) and a pixel-level anomaly heatmap obtained by upsampling patch-level scores to original resolution. The frozen DINOv3 ViT-B backbone extracts 32×32 patch tokens, and the 6-layer ViT projector (11.8M trainable parameters) operates on these tokens in latent space. The full pipeline runs at ~128.7 ms per image on a single RTX 3090 with 1,386 MiB peak memory.

## Method

FOUNDAD exploits the observation that the L2 distance between a foundation encoder's embedding of an anomalous image and its normal counterpart scales with the pixel area of the anomaly. The architecture uses two identical frozen encoders (Anomaly-Aware and Reference) sharing parameters θ, plus a trainable nonlinear Manifold Projector implemented as a 6-layer self-attention ViT with residual connections. During training, CutPaste-based structural anomalies are synthesized on normal images (gated by a Bernoulli threshold σ=0.5), and the projector is trained via L2 loss to map the anomalous embedding back onto the normal feature manifold. At inference, the projector maps test image features to their estimated manifold position, and the per-patch L2 distance between original and projected features serves as the anomaly score. The entire approach requires no text prompts, no class-specific parameters, and only 97.8M total parameters, yet outperforms multi-class and one-class few-shot baselines across MVTec-AD, VisA, BTAD, and DTD.

## Results

In the multi-class-one-model few-shot setting, FOUNDAD (DINOv3 backbone) achieves 96.1% I-AUROC, 97.9% AUPR, 96.8% P-AUROC, and 92.8% PRO on MVTec-AD in the 1-shot regime, and 92.6% I-AUROC, 92.0% AUPR, 99.7% P-AUROC, and 98.0% PRO on VisA. Performance improves with more shots, reaching 97.1% I-AUROC and 93.5% PRO on MVTec-AD at 4-shot. Notably, FOUNDAD achieves near-saturated pixel-level AUROC (99.7%) on VisA across all shot settings without any textual prompts, using only 97.8M total parameters (11.8M trainable in the projector).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOUNDAD outperforms all 11 baselines across every metric and shot setting on both MVTec-AD and VisA. Against the closest multi-class competitor IIPAD (Lv et al., ICLR 2025), FOUNDAD leads by 1.9% I-AUROC and 3.0% PRO on MVTec-AD (1-shot), and by 2.8% P-AUROC and 10.7% PRO on VisA (1-shot). Against the strongest one-class baseline LogSAD (Zhang et al., CVPR 2025), FOUNDAD surpasses it by 2.2% P-AUROC and 9.8% PRO on VisA (1-shot) while operating in the harder multi-class setting without text prompts. Classical methods (SPADE, PatchCore, FastRecon) degrade severely in the multi-class regime, with I-AUROC dropping below 77% on MVTec-AD even at 4-shot. Prompt-based methods (WinCLIP, PromptAD, AnomalyCLIP, AdaptCLIP, InCTRL) all rely on textual assistance and trail FOUNDAD by 2–12% in PRO on VisA. AnomalySD (Yan et al., 2024), which uses a diffusion model, is outperformed by FOUNDAD by 2.5% I-AUROC and 3.6% PRO on MVTec-AD (1-shot) despite FOUNDAD's simpler architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

FOUNDAD sits at the intersection of foundation visual encoders (DINOv3, DINOv2, SigLIP) and few-shot anomaly detection, drawing conceptual inspiration from I-JEPA's latent-space prediction and SimSiam's contrastive-free training, but departs by keeping the encoder entirely frozen and training only a lightweight nonlinear projector. It builds on CutPaste for structural anomaly synthesis and positions itself against the dominant trend of text-prompt-based few-shot detectors (WinCLIP, PromptAD, LogSAD, IIPAD) by demonstrating that pure visual embeddings suffice. The multi-class-one-model formulation extends the more common one-class-one-model paradigm, and the manifold-projection framing offers a new geometric interpretation of how foundation encoders implicitly encode normality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for industrial inspection: a 97.8M-parameter model with 128.7ms inference and no text prompts achieves state-of-the-art multi-class few-shot detection, making deployment substantially simpler and cheaper than multi-model pipelines like LogSAD (DINO+CLIP+SAM). The finding that DINOv3 (pure visual, no text alignment) outperforms CLIP and SigLIP for fine-grained anomaly localization challenges the assumption that vision-language grounding is necessary for this task. Open questions include how different anomaly synthesis strategies (beyond CutPaste) affect the learned manifold projection, and the method's vulnerability to orientation changes and severe appearance variations noted in the failure cases.
