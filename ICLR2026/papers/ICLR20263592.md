## Problem

Existing visual table understanding (VTU) datasets predominantly rely on synthetic renderings of serialized tables, creating a train-test mismatch where models fail to generalize to real-world table visualizations featuring merged cells, background colors, font variations, and embedded images. Additionally, these datasets provide fixed examples with single visualizations and predefined instructions, offering no access to underlying serialized data for reformulation or extension.

## Input / Output

Each example pairs a table image (original Wikipedia screenshot, PubTabNet document crop, or synthetic HTML render) with a natural-language instruction, and the model produces task-specific outputs ranging from free-form text answers and generated biographies to markdown table structures, binary verification labels, and numerical computations. The dataset also provides HTML serializations alongside images, enabling models to learn from both naturally occurring and synthetic visualizations, and supporting cell-highlighting operations that preserve original visual features rather than relying on lossy re-rendering.

## Method

TABLET's construction pipeline traces each table from 14 seed datasets back to its original Wikipedia visualization using the Wikipedia archiving API (retrieving articles at their historical revision), matching tables via Levenshtein edit distance on markdown representations with a 0.7 similarity threshold, and rendering synthetic HTML images only when originals are unavailable (11.5% of tables). The 4M examples across 21 tasks are unified into an instruction-tuning format with source identifiers, HTML serializations, and metadata for full traceability. For evaluation, they fine-tune Qwen2.5-VL-7B (full SFT) and Gemma 3-4B (LoRA) on TABLET variants of different sizes and compare against MMTab-trained baselines across 15 held-in and held-out benchmarks. They also introduce VisualTableQA, a 306-example benchmark where questions require joint reasoning over visual cues (e.g., colors, embedded images) and tabular structure, filtered to exclude questions solvable from lossy synthetic representations alone.

## Results

Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-7B on TABLET-L (4M examples) achieves state-of-the-art results on 11 out of 15 table understanding benchmarks. On held-in tasks, TABLET-L substantially outperforms the 0-shot baseline (e.g., HiTab F1: 67.5 vs. 31.2; FeTaQA BLEU: 31.5 vs. 7.0; TAT-QA accuracy: 32.5 vs. 6.9) and the MMTab baseline (e.g., ToTTo BLEU: 30.4 vs. 12.6; HiTab F1: 67.5 vs. 41.5). On held-out tasks, TABLET-L also leads on AIT-QA (70.8 vs. 51.7 0-shot), PubHealthTab (70.2 vs. 64.7), and the new VisualTableQA benchmark (45.6 vs. 42.4 0-shot, 41.1 MMTab). The mixed original+synthetic training variant (TABLET-B_mix) achieves the lowest degradation score (7.87) when evaluated on original vs. synthetic table images, compared to 28.90 for 0-shot and 22.35 for synthetic-only training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is MMTab (Zheng et al., 2024), the prior state-of-the-art VTU dataset using synthetic table renderings. TABLET-L fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B outperforms MMTab on 11 of 14 evaluated tasks, with the largest margins on HiTab (67.5 vs. 41.5 F1, +26.0), FeTaQA (31.5 vs. 1.7 BLEU, +29.8), TAT-QA (32.5 vs. 9.4 accuracy, +23.1), and ToTTo (30.4 vs. 12.6 BLEU, +17.8). MMTab retains an edge on WikiBio (6.4 vs. 3.8 BLEU), TabMCQ (89.1 vs. 87.9 accuracy), and HybridQA (27.1 vs. 25.1 accuracy), though the HybridQA gap is attributed to its training set being excluded from TABLET fine-tuning for held-out evaluation. On the new VisualTableQA benchmark requiring joint visual and tabular reasoning, TABLET-L achieves 45.6% exact match versus 41.1% for MMTab and 42.4% for 0-shot, a 16% relative improvement over MMTab with no task-specific training examples. The paper also shows that TABLET-M (1M examples, balanced) performs competitively with TABLET-L on most tasks, offering a practical trade-off for resource-constrained settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

TABLET builds directly on MMTab (Zheng et al., 2024), the prior state-of-the-art VTU dataset, reusing its instruction templates for structure-understanding tasks while replacing synthetic renders with original Wikipedia visualizations. It also incorporates structure-aware parsing from DocStruct4M (Hu et al., 2024) and aggregates examples from 14 seed datasets spanning table QA, fact verification, numerical reasoning, and generation. The key conceptual departure from prior VTU resources is the preservation of lossless original table images (88% of tables) rather than re-rendering serialized tables, addressing a train-test mismatch that the authors argue causes models to fail on real-world visual cues like merged cells, background colors, and embedded images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TABLET is the largest VTU training resource to date (4M examples, 21 tasks) and the first to systematically preserve original visualizations at scale, making it a likely default training set for future VLM-based table agents. The VisualTableQA benchmark fills a gap by requiring joint visual-perceptual and tabular reasoning, a capability no prior benchmark explicitly tests. Limitations include Wikipedia-centric sourcing (raising contamination concerns), a single primary backbone (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), and no ablation isolating individual task contributions. Open questions include whether benefits transfer to reasoning or self-reflection architectures, and whether the visual complexity metric generalizes beyond Wikipedia-style tables.
