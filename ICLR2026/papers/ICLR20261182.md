## Problem

ExG foundation models are hindered by two limitations: (i) most recordings come from controlled labs using bulky, expensive devices, leaving free-living data largely untapped, and (ii) existing models are task-specific, relying on tailored frequency filters and architectures that prevent transfer across tasks (e.g., a gaze-tracking model cannot be applied to emotion recognition). This paper addresses both by collecting unobtrusive earphone-based free-living ExG data and proposing a task-agnostic tokenization scheme that eliminates the need for task-specific preprocessing.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-channel ExG signals (EEG, EMG, EOG, ECG) captured by around-the-ear electrodes at 200 Hz, segmented into non-overlapping 4-second windows. Each window is decomposed into 12 physiology-informed frequency bands (e.g., EEG delta 0.5–4 Hz, EMG mid-frequency 45–95 Hz, EOG overall 0.1–20 Hz, QRS complex 8–50 Hz), and each band-channel-time patch is projected into a shared embedding space. The model outputs either a discrete classification label (video/audio interest, taste, touch, smell) or a continuous gaze position estimate reported as angular error in degrees, with the same encoder handling all tasks without task-specific filtering.

## Method

The core contribution is Physiology-informed Multi-band Tokenization (PiMT), which replaces task-specific narrow-band filtering with a fixed bank of 12 canonical sub-band filters grounded in established physiological knowledge, giving the model fine-grained access to all relevant spectral features simultaneously. Each band-specific signal is segmented into non-overlapping patches (0.5 s) and projected via a shared linear tokenizer into a 3D structured sequence indexed by frequency, channel, and time. A Bidirectional-Mamba encoder (8 layers, hidden dim 16) processes the flattened sequence with linear-time complexity, chosen over Transformers to handle the increased sequence length from the frequency dimension. Pre-training on 50 hours of unlabeled free-living data uses six joint reconstruction objectives—autoencoding, masked reconstruction, frequency-domain amplitude and phase reconstruction, and their masked variants—each with a dedicated lightweight MLP decoder, trained with weighted MAE losses. Fine-tuning freezes or updates the encoder and attaches task-specific decoders (mean-pooled classification head or patch-level linear regression head) trained on labeled data.

## Results

On the DailySense dataset, PiMT with free-living pre-training achieves an average F1-score of 87.6% across five classification tasks (video, audio, taste, touch, smell) and a gaze tracking angular error of 6.00°, outperforming the strongest baseline (PatchTST with pre-training) by 14.4 percentage points in F1 and 0.42° in gaze error. Without pre-training, PiMT still leads all baselines at 81.9% average F1 and 6.11° gaze error. On four public benchmarks (DREAMER, SEED, Sleep-EDF, BCI Competition IV 2b), PiMT achieves F1-scores of 0.910, 0.820, 0.822, and 0.693 respectively, consistently surpassing both PatchTST and Bidirectional-Mamba across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PiMT outperforms all six baselines on every DailySense task. Without pre-training, PiMT (81.9% avg F1) beats the strongest baseline Bidirectional-Mamba (77.9%) by 4.0 points and PatchTST (72.4%) by 9.5 points; the gap widens dramatically with pre-training, where PiMT reaches 87.6% versus PatchTST's 73.2% (a 14.4-point margin). On gaze tracking, PiMT achieves 6.00° error with pre-training, improving over PatchTST (6.42°) and Bidirectional-Mamba (6.53° without pre-training). On the four public benchmarks, PiMT wins on all datasets: DREAMER (0.910 vs. 0.889 PatchTST), SEED (0.820 vs. 0.756), Sleep-EDF (0.822 vs. 0.810), and BCI Competition IV 2b (0.693 vs. 0.657), with the largest gains on SEED (+6.4 points) and BCI IV 2b (+3.6 points). The paper also shows that pre-training on the 50-hour free-living DailySense data outperforms pre-training on larger controlled corpora (TUAR 98.6 hrs, TUAR+TUSZ 498.6 hrs), achieving 87.6% vs. 85.3% and 85.8% average F1 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) ExG-specific architectures (EEGNet, DeepConvNet, EEGConformer) that rely on task-tailored frequency filters, (2) general-purpose time-series backbones (PatchTST, Bidirectional-Mamba) that treat signals as generic sequences, and (3) self-supervised ExG pre-training (BrainBERT, BIOT, BrainWave) that learns representations from lab-controlled corpora. The key departure is the Physiology-informed Multi-band Tokenization (PiMT) scheme, which replaces task-specific narrow-band filtering with a fixed 12-band filter bank grounded in canonical EEG/EMG/EOG/ECG physiology, combined with a Bidirectional-Mamba encoder and six reconstruction-based pre-training objectives. By pairing this with free-living earphone data (NeuroBuds/DailySense), the paper moves the ExG foundation-model agenda from controlled-lab, task-specific pipelines toward scalable, task-agnostic representation learning in the wild.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 50 hours of free-living earphone data outperforms 498.6 hours of controlled EEG (TUAR+TUSZ) for pre-training challenges the prevailing assumption that scale alone drives representation quality, suggesting that ecological diversity of recordings may be more transferable than volume of lab data. The DailySense dataset is the first to unify ExG-based evaluation across all five human senses, providing a practical benchmark for wearable physiological sensing. However, the participant pool (22 subjects, limited demographic range) and the substantial cross-subject performance drop (58.6% F1) highlight that full population-level generalization remains an open problem. Future work will need to scale data collection across diverse populations and address inter-subject variability to realize the paper's vision of a true ExG foundation model.
