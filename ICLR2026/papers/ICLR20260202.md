## Problem

Transformer-based 3D reconstruction models like VGGT rely on dense global self-attention whose memory and compute scale quadratically with total token length, making them infeasible beyond a few hundred frames. Even streaming variants such as StreamVGGT, which introduce causal attention and KV caching, still accumulate an ever-growing cache that exhausts 80 GB GPUs around 1k frames. No existing method can process arbitrarily long video sequences (e.g., 10k frames) under standard hardware constraints while maintaining reconstruction accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB video frames at 518 px height (original aspect ratio preserved), processed in sliding windows of 10 consecutive frames. Before tokenization, each window is registered via ORB/SIFT feature matching and homography estimation, then composed into a compact composite view whose token count scales with the merged support area rather than the raw frame count. Outputs are depth maps, camera poses, point maps, and feature tracks predicted jointly by dedicated heads, with each prediction aligned to the first frame of the window. The KV cache is bounded to top-k=5 historically relevant frames plus the most recent one, and multi-view selection retains the top-20 camera tokens.

## Method

IncVGGT is a training-free, two-pronged redundancy-elimination framework applied on top of the VGGT/StreamVGGT backbone. On the input side, a registration-and-composition module aligns K frames to a common reference via cumulative homographies, then uses a normalized span gate (horizontal coverage of warped supports ≤ λ) to decide whether to merge all frames into one composite view or recursively bisect the window, with band-limited warping, mask-aware feathering, and lightweight void inpainting to produce a stable composite. On the history side, a global-local KV cache pruning rule computes per-key relevance scores from the current attention matrix and retains only the top-k highest-scoring slots plus the most recent one, converting the growing cache into a constant-size set of S=k+1 entries. Together, these two mechanisms reduce per-step attention cost from O(L²) to O(T̃·S) and bound stored KV memory to O(S·d_h) per head, both independent of sequence length, enabling smooth operation on 10k-frame inputs under an 80 GB GPU.

## Results

On 500-frame sequences, IncVGGT achieves 58.5× fewer attention operators, 9× lower peak memory, 25.7× less energy, and 4.9× faster inference compared to StreamVGGT, while maintaining comparable depth accuracy (e.g., Sintel Abs Rel 0.341 vs. 0.328; KITTI δ<1.25 71.9% vs. 72.2%). Critically, IncVGGT sustains 10k-frame sequences in 797 s on an 80 GB A100, whereas VGGT OOMs beyond 300 frames and StreamVGGT beyond 1k frames. Per-frame latency remains nearly constant (~85 ms) from 100 to 300 frames, in contrast to StreamVGGT's sharp rise from 106 ms to 455 ms over the same range.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against StreamVGGT (the primary streaming baseline), IncVGGT matches accuracy within 1–3% on all depth metrics (e.g., Sintel Abs Rel 0.341 vs. 0.328; KITTI δ<1.25 71.9% vs. 72.2%) and on multi-view reconstruction (7Scenes Acc 0.0266 vs. 0.0241; NRGBD Acc 0.0516 vs. 0.0520), while delivering 4.9× faster inference at 500 frames, 9× lower memory, and the ability to process 10k frames where StreamVGGT OOMs at 1k. Against VGGT (the dense-view oracle), IncVGGT is slightly worse on short-sequence depth (Sintel Abs Rel 0.341 vs. 0.298) but is the only method that scales beyond 300 frames, completing 10k frames in 797 s where VGGT cannot allocate memory. Against CUT3R and Point3R, IncVGGT clearly wins on depth accuracy across all three datasets (e.g., Sintel Abs Rel 0.341 vs. 0.421/0.452; KITTI δ<1.25 71.9% vs. 88.1%/84.2%), confirming that the registration-and-pruning design preserves geometric quality better than prior streaming approaches with persistent state.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VGGT's feed-forward transformer architecture for joint depth/pose/point-map prediction and on StreamVGGT's causal-attention KV-caching paradigm for online video processing. It departs from both by introducing two orthogonal redundancy-removal mechanisms: (1) classical image registration (ORB/SIFT + homography) to compose overlapping frames into composite views before tokenization, and (2) a top-k-plus-recency KV-cache pruning rule borrowed conceptually from LLM inference efficiency work (attention sinks, key-token selection). Unlike VGGT-Long, which handles long sequences via offline chunking and post-hoc alignment, IncVGGT operates fully online and training-free, requiring no architectural modification to the pretrained backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By bounding both input token count and cache size to constants independent of sequence length, IncVGGT makes transformer-based 3D reconstruction viable on 80 GB GPUs for 10k-frame inputs where VGGT OOMs at ~300 frames and StreamVGGT at ~1k frames, with 58.5× fewer operators and 9× less memory at 500 frames while staying within 1–3% accuracy of StreamVGGT. The training-free, architecture-agnostic design means it can be dropped onto any VGGT/StreamVGGT checkpoint without retraining, which is practically important for edge deployment in VR/AR, robotics, and autonomous driving. A key limitation is that the registration module relies on planar homography, which degrades under large viewpoint changes or texture-poor scenes; whether finetuning or learned registration could close the small accuracy gap remains open.
