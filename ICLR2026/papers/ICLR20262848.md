## Problem

Existing Outlier Exposure (OE)-based OOD detection methods enforce output-level uniformity on OOD samples but provide no explicit control over representation geometry, leading to unstable ID-OOD feature separation and insufficient capacity to capture the rich, evolving diversity of real-world OOD patterns. The paper asks whether a pre-specified geometric structure can structurally decouple ID and OOD representations while preserving ID classification performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262848/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-1k) through a backbone (WRN-40-2, ResNet-18/50, DenseNet-121, or ViT-B-16) followed by a projection layer that maps features into the OEFS dimension (1024 for CIFAR, 2048 for ImageNet). At inference, the normalized feature is projected onto K fixed EBVs and V fixed VEBVs; the VPC Score is the weighted L2 activation difference between the two subspaces (default α=−1, β=100), yielding a continuous OOD likelihood that is thresholded into a binary ID/OOD decision. No learnable classifier head is used at test time.

## Method

VPC constructs an Orthogonal Equiangular Feature Space (OEFS) on a unit hypersphere from K+V prototype vectors arranged as a simplex Equiangular Tight Frame, partitioned into K Equiangular Basic Vectors (EBVs) for the ID subspace and V Vast EBVs (VEBVs) for the OOD subspace. Training jointly optimizes three losses on ID and auxiliary OE data: (1) an Evidential Neural Collapse (ENC) loss that fuses geometric cosine evidence with a uniform Dirichlet prior to stabilize ID-feature alignment to EBVs, (2) a VEBV subspace attraction loss that maximizes the L2 projection of OOD features onto the VEBV span, and (3) an orthogonality constraint loss that forces OOD features into the null space of ID prototypes via a uniformity objective over EBV logits. The key insight is that the simplex ETF zero-sum property makes the uniformity loss mathematically equivalent to a hard orthogonality constraint, and the large VEBV bank provides scalable directional anchors for diverse OOD modes without sacrificing ID compactness.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (WideResNet-40-2, auxiliary OOD data), VPC achieves an average FPR95 of 2.27% and AUROC of 99.18%, outperforming the strongest baseline PFS (2.68% / 98.66%) by 0.41% FPR95. On CIFAR-100, VPC reaches 32.04% FPR95 and 93.65% AUROC, beating DAL (32.89% / 93.21%) and PFS (34.35% / 93.33%). On the large-scale ImageNet-1k benchmark, VPC with ResNet-50 attains 56.50% average FPR95 and 84.70% AUROC (vs. PFS at 57.85% / 84.13%), and with ViT-B-16 achieves 56.20% FPR95 and 85.20% AUROC while maintaining 80.32% ID accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the four directly comparable auxiliary-OOD baselines, VPC consistently wins on both FPR95 and AUROC across all benchmarks: on CIFAR-10 it beats PFS by 0.41% FPR95 (2.27 vs 2.68) and DAL by 0.90% (2.27 vs 3.17); on CIFAR-100 it beats DAL by 0.85% FPR95 (32.04 vs 32.89) and PFS by 2.31% (32.04 vs 34.35); on ImageNet-1k (ResNet-50) it beats PFS by 1.35% FPR95 (56.50 vs 57.85). The original OE and Energy-OE baselines are substantially outperformed (e.g., 3.44% vs 2.27% FPR95 on CIFAR-10), confirming that geometric subspace separation surpasses output-level uniformization. Against post-hoc scorers (MSP, Maha, KNN) and contrastive methods (CSI, CIDER) evaluated under different training paradigms, VPC's auxiliary-OOD results remain superior (e.g., 2.27% vs 12.97% FPR95 for KNN+ on CIFAR-10). As a scoring function on the same trained model, VPC Score outperforms MSP, EDL Prob, and Uncertainty on CIFAR-100 across all three backbones (e.g., 32.04% vs 33.95% FPR95 for MSP on WRN-40-2), with the advantage most pronounced on the harder CIFAR-100 setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

VPC sits at the intersection of three established lines: Outlier Exposure training (Hendrycks et al., 2018), Neural Collapse geometry (Papyan et al., 2020), and Evidential Deep Learning (Sensoy et al., 2018). It departs from prior NC-based OOD methods like PFS (Wu et al., 2024), which constrain only the ID classifier weights, by expanding the simplex ETF into a dual-subspace Orthogonal Equiangular Feature Space (OEFS) where a bank of Vast EBVs (VEBVs) provides a dedicated, high-capacity OOD subspace. The evidential prior is repurposed from output-layer calibration to a geometric rate-limiter on feature-prototype alignment, and the VEBV attraction loss replaces the standard OE uniformization objective with a subspace-directed attraction that preserves OOD representational diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OEFS framework offers a clean, interpretable geometric solution to the long-standing ID/OOD interference problem in joint training: by structurally partitioning the feature space into orthogonal ID and OOD subspaces, it protects Neural Collapse convergence on the ID side while giving OOD features a rich, expandable anchor space. The finding that L2 activation in either subspace alone is nearly sufficient for detection (Table 10) suggests the geometric separation is robust, but the method still requires auxiliary OE data and a tuned VEBV count (V=1000 optimal for CIFAR-100, with degradation at V=2000), leaving open how well it scales to very large semantic spaces or operates without external OOD data. The modest parameter overhead (~4% for ResNet-18) makes it practical, but the reliance on a projection layer to match OEFS dimensionality adds architectural coupling that may not transfer trivially to all backbones.
