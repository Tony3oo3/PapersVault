## Problem

Fine-tuning safety-aligned LLMs on user-provided data can override safety alignment, yet existing defenses (Vaccine, BackdoorAlign, Lisa, Antidote, DirectionAlign, ConstrainedSFT) all require large curated safety datasets, calibration sets, or introduce utility degradation. The paper asks whether safety alignment can be fully restored with a single safety example at negligible computational cost, without sacrificing downstream task performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261252/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a corrupted (fine-tuned) LLM and a single safety instance consisting of one harmful prompt paired with a safe refusal response (e.g., 'How can I bypass system security checks?' → 'Sorry, I can't help you. Please follow security guidelines.'). This instance is selected from a candidate pool of 256 harmful instructions via a bilevel optimization procedure. The output is a safety-aligned LLM fine-tuned for 10 epochs at learning rate 2×10⁻⁵ on that single example, restoring ASR to pre-fine-tuning levels while preserving utility on downstream tasks (SQL, GSM8K, SAMSum) and general benchmarks (MMLU, MT-bench).

## Method

The paper formulates safety recovery as a first-order, penalty-based bilevel optimization problem: the upper level selects a binary data-selection vector w (with ℓ₂ regularization for sparsity) to identify the single most critical safety instance, while the lower level performs standard SFT on the selected example starting from the corrupted model. The key empirical insight is that the safety gradient, when decomposed via SVD, occupies a low-rank intrinsic subspace (effective rank < 20) whose dominant directions are nearly opposite to harmful fine-tuning gradients, explaining why one update suffices. Subspace similarity analysis (Frobenius overlap of left singular vectors) confirms that a single general refusal example aligns >0.8 with the full batch safety gradient across all tested models. Theoretically, under a local r-effective-rank assumption on the Hessian and a PL condition, the paper proves a dimension-free convergence rate of O(r·(ℓ/μ)·log((L(θ₀)−L*)/ε)), independent of model parameter count d, which explains why convergence is fast regardless of model size or harmful fine-tuning scale.

## Results

On Llama-2-7B-Chat fine-tuned with SQL Create, One-shot FT achieves ASR=0.0 and HS=1.00 (matching the original aligned model exactly) while preserving utility at SQL=99.4, MMLU=45.76, and MT-bench=7.16, requiring only 0.02 h additional GPU time. On GPT-4.1, the same single-instance patch recovers ASR from 12.4 to 0.0 with HS=1.00 and negligible utility loss (SQL=99.8, MMLU=86.38). Across all tested models (Llama-2/3.1, Mistral-7B, Qwen-2.5-7B) and attack types (harmful injection, identity shifting, backdoor poisoning, patch poisoning, paraphrase attacks), one-shot recovery consistently restores ASR to near-initial levels without degrading downstream task performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

One-shot FT ties STAR-DSS on safety (both achieve ASR=0.0, HS=1.00) but is 122× faster (0.02 h vs. 2.45 h additional GPU time), making it the most efficient full-recovery method. It decisively outperforms the next-best safety methods—DirectionAlign (ASR=2.1) and ConstrainedSFT (ASR=3.3)—while also matching or exceeding them on utility (SQL 99.4 vs. 96.8/98.5; MMLU 45.76 vs. 44.94/45.26). Against the remaining baselines (Vaccine ASR=14.6, Lisa ASR=12.0, Antidote ASR=10.8, BackdoorAlign ASR=6.9), One-shot FT reduces ASR by 6.9–14.6 points and avoids the utility degradation they incur (e.g., BackdoorAlign drops SQL to 91.5 and MMLU to 43.19). On GPT-4.1, One-shot FT is the only method tested that fully restores safety (ASR 12.4→0.0) without any utility loss, whereas BackdoorAlign leaves ASR at 3.9 and sacrifices 2.1 MMLU points. In summary, One-shot FT is the only method that simultaneously achieves perfect safety recovery, zero utility degradation, and sub-minute computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the bilevel optimization framework for LLM data selection (ScaleBiO, SEAL) to formulate safety-driven data selection, and on the intrinsic dimensionality analysis of Aghajanyan et al. (2020) to justify SVD-based parameter projection. It departs sharply from the dominant line of safety-recovery defenses (Vaccine, BackdoorAlign, Lisa, Antidote, DirectionAlign, ConstrainedSFT, STAR-DSS), which all require large curated safety datasets, calibration sets, or complex correction mechanisms. The key conceptual departure is the discovery that safety gradients occupy a low-rank subspace nearly opposite to harmful gradients, making a single well-chosen refusal instance sufficient to neutralize arbitrary-scale harmful fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For LMaaS providers, the practical implication is dramatic: safety can be restored in 1–2 minutes of GPU time with a single example, versus hours and large datasets for prior methods, while preserving downstream utility. The low-rank gradient structure and dimension-free convergence proof offer a mechanistic explanation that could generalize to other alignment properties beyond safety. Open questions include whether the approach degrades under adaptive multi-turn attacks, whether the residual ASR gap on Mistral/Qwen (~10–20%) reflects a fundamental model-specific limitation, and whether the single-instance guarantee holds for more subtle alignment dimensions (e.g., helpfulness calibration, multi-lingual safety).
