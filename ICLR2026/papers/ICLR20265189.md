## Problem

Existing DRL schedulers for dynamic cloud workflow scheduling (CADWS) rely on monolithic, single-pathway policy networks that apply a fixed set of decision rules uniformly across all scheduling scenarios. This rigidity prevents them from adapting to the wide spectrum of SLA deadline tightness (from extremely tight to very relaxed) that dynamically arriving workflows exhibit, leading to suboptimal cost–deadline trade-offs. No prior work has introduced a Mixture-of-Experts architecture for dynamic workflow scheduling, and existing MoE gating mechanisms (simple linear layers) cannot exploit the rich DAG structure and dynamic system context needed for effective expert selection.

## Input / Output

At each decision step, DEFT consumes a global state comprising: (1) all active workflow DAGs with per-task computational demands and completion status, (2) the current VM pool with processing speeds, hourly rental costs, queue lengths, and existing lease times, (3) the feature vector of the current ready task, and (4) a normalized deadline tightness score derived from γ (where d_i = a_i + γ·minMakespan(W_i)). The output is a priority score over a dynamically sized action set that includes both reusing already-leased VMs and provisioning new VMs of any available type; the highest-priority action is executed. The action space changes every step as VMs are rented or released, making the output dimension variable.

## Method

DEFT replaces the fixed Priority Mapping Module (PMM) of GATES with a Mixture-of-Experts layer containing four MLP experts, each pre-trained via OpenAI-ES on workflows with a single deadline tightness (γ ∈ {1.25, 1.75, 2.25, 5.0}) to specialize in distinct scheduling behaviors ranging from aggressive SLA-saving to cost-efficient VM-saving. A novel graph-adaptive gating network then routes each decision to the most appropriate expert: it first encodes the workflow DAG with a two-layer GAT to produce a structural embedding, then forms a cross-attention query by concatenating this DAG embedding, the ready-task features, VM state embeddings, and the deadline tightness score, while learnable expert tokens serve as both keys and values. The resulting attention weights are sparsified via Top-1 selection, activating exactly one expert per step. Training follows a two-phase pipeline: Phase 1 independently pre-trains each expert on fixed-γ instances; Phase 2 jointly fine-tunes the SEM, gating network, and all experts on mixed-deadline instances (γ sampled from {1.0, 1.25, 1.5, 1.75, 2.0, 2.25, 3.0}) using OpenAI-ES with population size 40 over 3000 generations. This design enables the policy to fluidly switch between specialized scheduling strategies as deadline pressure and resource conditions evolve across the scheduling horizon.

## Results

On the primary dynamic-deadline benchmark (Table 1), DEFT achieves the lowest total scheduling cost across all workflow scales: 52.46 (S), 86.60 (M), and 137.69 (L). The advantage over the strongest DRL baseline GATES grows with scale, from 0.9% on small workflows to 11.4% on medium and 29.6% on large. Heuristic schedulers (ProLis, GRP-HEFT) incur total costs 3–10× higher than any DRL method, confirming their inability to handle dynamic deadlines. DEFT also maintains a favorable VM/SLA balance, e.g., on L-scale it cuts VM fees to 70.88 (vs. GATES' 145.20) while keeping SLA penalties at 66.81.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEFT outperforms all five baselines on total cost at every scale. Against the strongest DRL baseline GATES (IJCAI 2025), DEFT improves total cost by 0.9% (S), 11.4% (M), and 29.6% (L), with the gap widening as workflow size and deadline variability increase. SPN-CWS (ICSO 2024) is the second-best DRL method; DEFT beats it by 4.4% on S, 1.3% on M, and 7.8% on L. ES-RL (ICSO 2022) degrades sharply with scale (65.39→225.46), while DEFT's cost grows more gradually (52.46→137.69). The two heuristic baselines—ProLis (IEEE TPDS 2017) and GRP-HEFT (IEEE TPDS 2019)—are far behind: ProLis incurs 3.5× the cost of DEFT on S and 5.5× on L, while GRP-HEFT avoids all SLA penalties but at 5.7× (S) to 7.7× (L) the total cost of DEFT, reflecting its over-provisioning of expensive VMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEFT builds directly on GATES (Shen et al., IJCAI 2025), inheriting its GNN-based State Embedding Module and replacing only the monolithic Priority Mapping Module with a Mixture-of-Experts architecture. The MoE design draws conceptual inspiration from sparsely-gated MoE layers in large language models (Shazeer et al., 2017; Fedus et al., 2022), but departs from prior MoE-in-DRL work for combinatorial optimization (MVMoE, SHIELD) by targeting dynamic, deadline-varying settings and introducing a graph-adaptive cross-attention gating mechanism that encodes DAG topology, task states, and deadline tightness—rather than relying on simple linear or MLP gating. The two-phase training strategy (expert pre-training at fixed γ, then joint fine-tuning with the gating network) is a novel adaptation of MoE training to the RL scheduling context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DEFT demonstrates that deadline-aware expert specialization combined with graph-structured routing can substantially improve cost efficiency in dynamic cloud scheduling, with gains scaling from ~1% at small scale to ~30% at large scale over the strongest DRL baseline. The minimal inference overhead (~0.22s/step vs. 0.22s for GATES) makes the approach practically deployable. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-tenant environments with competing SLAs, whether expert routing decisions can be made interpretable for cloud operators, and whether the 4-expert Top-1 configuration generalizes to domains where deadline tightness is not the primary scheduling driver (e.g., energy-aware or multi-objective settings).
