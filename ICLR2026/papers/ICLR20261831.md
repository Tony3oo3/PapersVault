## Problem

Prior concept erasure methods for text-to-image diffusion models either fine-tune the U-Net denoiser (computationally expensive and degrading non-target generation) or edit early text-encoder layers where visual attributes are causally localized. However, directly modifying these early layers causes collateral damage to unrelated concepts because they encode foundational, broadly shared features rather than concept-specific semantics, and this limitation is especially severe for high-level abstract concepts like nudity that emerge only after multi-layer integration.

## Input / Output

During training, HiRM consumes only text prompts (single-word for styles/objects, or multi-term strings for nudity) with no image data required. The output is a modified first transformer block of the CLIP text encoder (self-attention + MLP weights), which serves as a drop-in replacement for the original encoder in any downstream model (SD1.5, Flux1.dev, LoRA-augmented U-Nets) without retraining the denoiser. The steering coefficient c (500 for HiRM-R, 1 for HiRM-S) controls misdirection strength, and the method requires only ~1.2 s training time and 1.6 GB memory per concept.

## Method

HiRM decouples the location of parameter updates from the target of semantic supervision: it fine-tunes only the first transformer block of the CLIP text encoder (identified as the causal region for visual attributes via prior causal tracing) while applying the erasure loss to the final block's high-level token representations. Two variants are proposed: HiRM-R steers each token's final-layer representation toward an independently sampled random unit vector, and HiRM-S aligns them with semantically defined directions (e.g., a supercategory like 'Painting' for 'Van Gogh', or a safety vector constructed by subtracting an empirical nudity vector from a nudity-related prompt embedding). The loss is a token-averaged L2 distance between the final-block output h^(L) and the scaled target vector, with all other encoder blocks and the U-Net kept frozen. This design avoids 'representation shattering' that occurs when perturbing early-layer features directly, because the supervision signal targets the integrated, concept-specific semantics rather than the foundational building blocks shared across all concepts.

## Results

On the UnlearnCanvas benchmark, HiRM-S achieves the best overall Average Accuracy (AA) for object erasure (96.94%) and near-best for style erasure (95.54%), while HiRM-R reaches 94.65% and 94.24% respectively. Both variants complete training in 1.20 s with only 1.60 GB memory, compared to 7,372 s / 16.30 GB for ESD and 2,870 s / 28.92 GB for Ediff. On the I2P nudity benchmark, HiRM-S attains 0.66% attack success rate with a COCO-10k FID of 6.75 and CLIP score of 0.306, balancing erasure strength with minimal utility degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiRM-S outperforms Diff-Q (the closest text-encoder-only baseline) on object erasure AA (96.94% vs. 96.19%) and is within 0.3% on style (95.54% vs. 95.81%), while training in 1.20 s versus Diff-Q's closed-form edit. Against training-based U-Net methods, HiRM-S dominates on efficiency: it is ~6,000× faster than ESD (1.20 s vs. 7,372 s) and uses 10× less memory than Ediff (1.60 GB vs. 28.92 GB), while matching or exceeding their AA scores (e.g., 96.94% object AA vs. ESD's 64.05% and Ediff's 76.39%). On the I2P nudity benchmark, HiRM-S achieves 0.66% ASR, competitive with SALUN (0.57%) and RECE (0.57%), but with substantially better utility preservation (COCO FID 6.75 vs. SALUN's 11.33 and RECE's 29.65). HiRM-S also achieves 0.00% on Ring-77 and 3.30% on MMA-Diffusion, outperforming Diff-Q (5.26% and 21.20%) and ESD (74.74% and 57.40%) on adversarial robustness. The main trade-off is that SALUN and RECE achieve slightly lower ASR on white-box attacks (Unlearn-DiffAtk: 9.80% and 21.83% vs. HiRM-S's 19.01%) and MMA (0.90% and 0.40% vs. 3.30%), but at the cost of significantly degraded generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Diff-QuickFix (Basu et al., 2023), which identified the first CLIP text-encoder block as the causal locus for visual attributes and proposed closed-form editing there, by decoupling the update location (first block) from the supervision target (final block's high-level representations). It also adapts Ring-A-Bell's (Tsai et al., 2023) empirical nudity-vector construction into a safety misdirection target for abstract concepts. Conceptually, HiRM draws on the LLM unlearning idea of Representation Misdirection Unlearning (Li et al., 2024) and on Toker et al.'s (2024) finding that coherent semantics emerge only in late encoder layers, combining these into a single training objective that avoids the 'representation shattering' observed when supervising at early layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HiRM offers a practically attractive safety patch: it trains in ~1.2 s with 1.6 GB memory (vs. thousands of seconds for U-Net fine-tuning), transfers zero-shot to Flux and LoRA-customized models, and composes synergistically with denoiser-based erasers (e.g., cutting ESD's Ring-A-Bell ASR from 41% to 13%). Key limitations include uniform token-level guidance that ignores per-token importance, and the multi-concept extension via naive weight averaging still shows a notable in-domain retention drop (65.6%). Open questions include whether the decoupling strategy generalizes to non-CLIP encoders (e.g., T5-only pipelines), compositional prompts, and whether more sophisticated module-fusion can close the multi-concept retention gap.
