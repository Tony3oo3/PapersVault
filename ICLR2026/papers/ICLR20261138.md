## Problem

Standard LM pretraining via next-token prediction models only intra-document token correlations, ignoring rich inter-document correlations (e.g., a paper and its code implementation, a novel and its screenplay) that arise from shared latent concepts. As high-quality web data depletes, this missed signal represents an underexplored avenue for improving performance without acquiring more unique data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261138/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed pretraining document collection (582M documents, 482B tokens from de-duplicated DCLM) and produces both a pretrained transformer LM (3B or 6B parameters, Llama 3 architecture with QK-norm) and a synthetic document corpus of 75B–250B tokens. Documents are embedded as 1,024-dimensional vectors via Qwen3-0.6B-Embedding for pairing; the synthesizer operates within an 8,192-token context window (4,096 per document), and synthetic documents are generated at temperature 1.0 with top-p 0.9, then filtered for 13-token shingle repetitions before joint training.

## Method

SBP operates in three steps: (1) nearest-neighbor pairing via ScaNN with 8-bit quantization to find document pairs with inner-product similarity > 0.75, followed by shingle-based deduplication; (2) synthesizer-tuning, where a transformer initialized from a pretrained checkpoint is fine-tuned on the conditional objective log p(d2|d1) over curated pairs using a small constant learning rate (5e-6), so each seed d1 maps to multiple possible d2 outputs, encouraging diverse high-entropy synthesis; (3) synthesis at scale, where seed documents are sampled uniformly and the conditional model generates new documents, which are then mixed with real data for joint pretraining without repeating any synthetic document. The key insight is a Bayesian hierarchical interpretation: the synthesizer implicitly learns a posterior over latent concepts shared between related documents (P(c|d1)), a signal absent from the standard marginal-likelihood pretraining objective, effectively performing self-distillation where the conditional model acts as a teacher that has internalized inter-document structure.

## Results

SBP consistently improves upon the repetition baseline across all three compute scales (200B, 1T-3B, 1T-6B) on both perplexity and QA accuracy. On average, SBP captures 42% of the oracle's QA accuracy improvement at 200B-scale, 48% at 1T-scale (3B), and 58% at 1T-scale (6B), where the oracle has access to 20× more unique data. The largest individual gains include +3.74% on WebQS (1-shot) and +2.65% on ARC-Easy (0-shot) at 200B-scale, while at 1T-scale (6B) SBP delivers +3.79% on WebQS and +1.90% on SciQ. Perplexity improvements are more modest at 1T-scale (e.g., −0.06 on OpenWebText2 for 6B), consistent with known plateauing of perplexity at scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SBP against two references: a repetition baseline (repeating D_pretrain 20×, following the data-constrained scaling protocol of Muennighoff et al. [39]) and an oracle upper bound with access to 20× more unique tokens. SBP consistently outperforms the repetition baseline on every benchmark at all three scales, with average QA accuracy gains of +2.32% (200B), +0.84% (1T-3B), and +1.32% (1T-6B). Relative to the oracle, SBP recovers 42–58% of the achievable improvement, with the ratio increasing with model size (42% → 48% → 58%), suggesting SBP's benefit scales favorably. On some individual benchmarks at 1T-scale (6B), SBP even exceeds the oracle (e.g., SciQ: +1.90 vs. +1.80; WebQS: +3.79 vs. +5.22 is close), indicating that inter-document correlations can sometimes provide signal that additional unique data does not. The repetition baseline plateaus in training dynamics while SBP continues to improve, confirming that synthetic data captures signal absent from repeated real data.

## Relation to Prior Work

SBP departs from standard LM pretraining, which treats documents as i.i.d. samples and only models within-document token correlations, by explicitly capturing inter-document correlations through a conditional synthesizer trained on the pretraining corpus itself. It distinguishes itself from teacher-distillation-based synthetic data approaches by requiring no external teacher model—the synthesizer is bootstrapped from the same data the student will train on. The closest prior work is Guu et al. [23], which modeled conditional distributions between neighboring sentence pairs but never performed pretraining experiments, and the data-constrained LM literature [39], which established repetition as the standard baseline that SBP improves upon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SBP offers a self-contained path to improving LM pretraining as high-quality internet text depletes, without relying on external teacher models or human annotation. The Bayesian hierarchical interpretation (the synthesizer implicitly learns a posterior over latent concepts) provides a principled explanation for why the method works, and the qualitative finding that synthesized documents abstract core concepts before generating new narratives suggests a mechanism beyond simple paraphrasing. Open questions include whether SBP's scaling law differs from standard pretraining, whether the embedding step can be replaced by the model's own activations, and whether the modest absolute gains at 1T scale (0.84–1.32% QA accuracy) will grow proportionally at frontier scales. A key limitation is the substantial additional compute required for synthesizer-tuning and large-scale synthesis (2.5K–8.4K B200 hours).
