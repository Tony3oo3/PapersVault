## Problem

Visual autoregressive (AR) models consistently underperform diffusion models on image generation, yet prior analyses have focused on individual components like tokenizer design or rasterization order rather than the full system. This paper identifies the core bottleneck as generator-tokenizer inconsistency: the AR model produces token sequences that the tokenizer decoder cannot faithfully reconstruct, due to (1) exposure bias causing the model to generate unseen token sequences at inference, and (2) embedding unawareness, where the model optimizes only discrete token indices without considering how those tokens are embedded in the tokenizer's continuous space.

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label from ImageNet-1K as conditioning input and generates a 256×256 RGB image. During training, the AR transformer predicts a sequence of 256 discrete VQGAN tokens (codebook size 1024) in raster-scan order; at inference, the sampled token sequence is decoded by the frozen tokenizer decoder into the final image. The method is strictly plug-and-play: it requires no modifications to the tokenizer, the generation order, the inference pipeline, or any external model, and the only added parameters are a small 2-layer MLP (2.1–4.2M) for the embedding regularization head.

## Method

reAR introduces a token-wise consistency regularization with two complementary components. First, Noisy Context Regularization replaces each input token with a random codebook entry with probability ε sampled from U(0, f(t)) under a truncated linear annealing schedule, forcing the causal transformer to predict the correct next token from a corrupted context and thereby reducing sensitivity to exposure bias. Second, Codebook Embedding Regularization adds a cosine-distance loss that aligns the generator's hidden state at a shallow layer (layer 0) with the codebook embedding of the current token, and at a deep layer (~3/4 depth) with the codebook embedding of the next token, via a trainable 2-layer MLP projection. The combined objective L_reAR = L'_AR + λ·L_re is trained jointly with the standard next-token prediction loss, requiring no architectural changes beyond the lightweight MLP head. This design ensures the generator learns to produce token sequences whose embedding trajectories remain within the tokenizer decoder's training distribution, even when individual token predictions are imperfect.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K 256×256 class-conditional generation, reAR-L (461M params, 400 epochs) achieves FID 1.86 and IS 316.9 with a standard rasterization-order VQGAN tokenizer, improving over the vanilla AR-L baseline (FID 3.02, IS 256.2) by a large margin. With the AliTok tokenizer, reAR-B-AliTok (177M params) reaches FID 1.42, matching the diffusion model REPA (675M params, FID 1.42) while using only 26% of the parameters. reAR-S (201M params) outperforms LlamaGen-XXL (1.4B params) with FID 2.00 vs. 2.34, demonstrating that the regularization yields substantial gains even at small model scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

reAR substantially outperforms the vanilla raster AR baseline (AR-L†) at the same 461M scale, reducing FID from 3.02 to 1.86 and improving IS from 256.2 to 316.9. Against larger raster AR models, reAR-S (201M) beats LlamaGen-XL (775M, FID 2.62) and LlamaGen-XXL (1.4B, FID 2.34) by 0.62 and 0.34 FID respectively. reAR-L (461M) surpasses MAR-L (479M, FID 1.98) and VAR-d30 (2.0B, FID 1.92) with fewer parameters, and outperforms RandAR-XXL (1.4B, FID 2.15). However, reAR-L (FID 1.86) does not match RAR-XL (955M, FID 1.50) or Maskbit (305M, FID 1.52) on the standard VQGAN tokenizer. With the AliTok tokenizer, reAR-B-AliTok (177M) achieves FID 1.42, tying REPA (675M, FID 1.42) and beating Maskbit (305M, FID 1.52) and AR-AliTok-B (177M, FID 1.50) at the same parameter count.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the standard visual autoregressive pipeline (VQGAN-style patch tokenizers + decoder-only transformer in raster order, following RAR and DiT architectures) but reframes the performance gap with diffusion models as a generator-tokenizer inconsistency problem rather than a tokenizer or ordering problem. Prior work has attacked this gap by redesigning tokenizers (TiTok, AliTok, FlexTok, GigaTok), randomizing token order (RAR, RandAR), or switching paradigms (MAR, VAR), while REPA aligns diffusion hidden states with external DINO-v2 features. reAR departs from all of these by introducing a lightweight, tokenizer-agnostic regularization (noisy context + codebook embedding alignment) that requires no architectural changes, no external models, and no modification to the inference pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of reAR makes it immediately applicable to any existing visual AR setup, and the parameter efficiency (177M matching REPA's 675M) is notable for practitioners seeking to unify vision and language in a single AR framework. However, evaluation is limited to ImageNet-1K class-conditional generation at 256×256; performance on text-to-image benchmarks, larger resolutions, or video remains untested. Open questions include whether the empirically chosen regularization layers generalize to much deeper models, whether a theoretical account of generator-tokenizer alignment exists, and whether the approach transfers to continuous-token AR models like MAR.
