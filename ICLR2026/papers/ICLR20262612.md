## Problem

Existing EEG foundation models (BIOT, LaBraM) compress spatiotemporal token features into a single CLS token or apply global average pooling before classification, discarding second-order statistics and the intrinsic spatiotemporal structure that are critical for EEG decoding. Standard Global Covariance Pooling (GCP) partially addresses this by computing a covariance descriptor, but it flattens all tokens into one matrix, overlooking the temporal and channel hierarchy. The paper asks whether a pooling head can be both statistics-aware and geometry-aware while preserving the underlying spatiotemporal structure.

## Input / Output

RHOP is a plug-and-play head that consumes the spatiotemporal token tensor X ∈ R^{D×T×N} (D channels, T temporal segments, N tokens) and a CLS token y₀ produced by a pretrained or from-scratch EEG backbone (BIOT or LaBraM). For each token, it computes temporal first- and second-order statistics across channels (mean vector in R^T and covariance in R^{T×T}), then produces a compact sparse precision vector g that is concatenated with y₀ and passed through a linear layer with softmax to yield class probabilities. The module adds only 0.4K–4.6K parameters depending on dataset and backbone, with matrix sizes at most 15×15.

## Method

RHOP operates in three stages on the SPD manifold under the affine-invariant metric. First, a Quotient Gaussian Embedding normalizes each token's covariance to correlation form (removing per-time scale) and jointly encodes the mean and normalized covariance into an SPD matrix Y_n ∈ S_{T+k}^{+,1} via a block construction, yielding scale-invariant descriptors. Second, a Riemannian Gaussian Embedding aggregates the token set {Y_n} by computing the Fréchet mean (one Karcher-flow iteration) and the tangent-space covariance, then embeds the (mean, covariance) pair into a single SPD descriptor G via a Lie-group-isomorphic block matrix. Third, a sparse inverse covariance estimation (iSICE) layer is applied to G to emphasize partial correlations, and the resulting upper-triangular vector is fused with the CLS token for classification. Unlike standard GCP, which collapses all tokens into one covariance matrix, RHOP preserves per-token temporal structure and captures high-order interactions through the Riemannian Gaussian aggregation.

## Results

On TUEV (6-class epileptic event classification), LaBraM-Base+RHOP achieves 67.85% Cohen's Kappa and 84.20% Weighted F1, outperforming the LaBraM-Base baseline (66.37% Kappa, 83.12% F1) and all GCP variants. On TUAB (binary abnormality detection), LaBraM-Base+RHOP reaches 82.44% Balanced Accuracy and 91.05% AUROC, surpassing the baseline (81.40%, 90.22%). On BCIC2B (motor imagery) and PhysioP300 (ERP), RHOP improves Balanced Accuracy to 69.01% and 65.17% respectively, with AUROC gains of +1.15 and +1.51 points over the LaBraM-Base baseline. RHOP adds only 0.4–4.6K parameters and trains in 0.05–21.5 min/epoch depending on dataset and backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RHOP consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across all four datasets. Against non-foundation baselines (SPaRCNet, ContraWR, CNN-Transformer, FFCL, ST-Transformer), RHOP with LaBraM-Base achieves 63.80% Balanced Accuracy on TUEV versus 39.79–43.84% for these methods, a gap of 20+ points. Against the foundation-model baselines (BIOT, LaBraM-Base with default heads), RHOP improves Cohen's Kappa by +1.48 points on TUEV (67.85% vs. 66.37%) and AUROC by +0.83 points on TUAB (91.05% vs. 90.22%). Among GCP variants, iSICE is the strongest competitor, but RHOP still wins: on TUEV with LaBraM-Base, RHOP achieves 67.85% Kappa vs. 61.34% for iSICE (+6.51 points), and on TUAB, 91.05% AUROC vs. 90.59% for iSICE. Notably, iSQRT-COV and SVD-Padé actually degrade performance below the plain backbone on TUEV (e.g., SVD-Padé drops Kappa to 57.98%), while RHOP maintains or improves upon the baseline. On the CBraMod backbone (Appendix E.1), RHOP also outperforms the original multi-layer classifier (81.31% vs. 79.73% Balanced Accuracy on TUAB), confirming backbone-agnostic gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

RHOP sits at the intersection of three established lines: Riemannian geometry on SPD manifolds for EEG (Congedo et al., Pan et al., Kobler et al.), global covariance pooling heads from vision (iSQRT-COV, iSICE, SVD-Padé), and the recent wave of EEG foundation models (BIOT, LaBraM, CBraMod) that typically terminate with a CLS token or global average pooling. The method repurposes the quotient Gaussian embedding from Lovrić et al. (2000) and the Lie-group Riemannian Gaussian construction from Nguyen (2021) into a unified pooling head, but departs from prior GCP by preserving per-token spatiotemporal structure rather than flattening all tokens into a single covariance matrix. To the authors' knowledge, this is the first geometry-aware pooling head explicitly designed for EEG foundation backbones, bridging the gap between Riemannian statistics and transformer-based pretraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RHOP offers a minimal-overhead (~1–5K parameters), backbone-agnostic drop-in that consistently improves accuracy and reduces training time relative to heavier GCP alternatives, making it practically attractive for EEG practitioners who already use foundation backbones. The Gaussianity analysis (Table 8) provides a clear statistical rationale: RHOP operates on near-Gaussian temporal-segment features, whereas standard GCP produces strongly non-Gaussian, heavy-tailed descriptors after flattening, explaining the performance gap. Open questions include whether the quotient (scale-invariant) formulation is always preferable to raw covariance for other modalities (ECG, MEG, fMRI), and whether the approach extends to larger foundation models or longer sequences where the SPD matrix dimension grows.
