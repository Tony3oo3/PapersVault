## Problem

LLMs increasingly generate bibliographies from parametric knowledge, and prior work shows these lists match many coarse bibliometric and graph-structural regularities of human citation networks. However, it remained unresolved whether LLM-generated reference lists can be reliably distinguished from human ones using the induced citation-graph topology and textual content signals, and which type of signal actually carries the discriminative information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262126/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on paired undirected citation graphs built from 10,000 focal papers (≈275k references) from SciSciNet, where each graph contains the focal paper as a central node connected to its references, with edges representing citations retrieved from SciSciNet. Node features come in two forms: a 5-dimensional structural vector (degree, closeness, eigenvector centrality, clustering coefficient, and graph edge count) or a 3072-dimensional title/abstract embedding (OpenAI text-embedding-3-large; SPECTER2 at 768-d used as a robustness check). The output is a binary class label per graph (LLM-generated vs. ground truth), with a third field-matched random baseline class used to validate that any detected signal is not trivially separable from noise.

## Method

The paper employs a progressive three-stage classification pipeline. First, node-level structural metrics are aggregated into graph-level descriptors (mean, median, IQR, max-to-mean ratios) and fed to a Random Forest, which cleanly rejects the random baseline (~0.90 accuracy) but barely separates GPT-4o from ground truth (~0.61). Second, title/abstract embeddings are summed to graph level and classified with the same RF, raising GPT-vs-ground-truth accuracy to ~0.83. Third, four GNN architectures (GCN, GAT, GIN, GraphSAGE) are trained with node-level features under a consistent 70/15/15 stratified split and 500-run hyperparameter sweep; with embedding node features, all architectures reach ~93% test accuracy on GPT vs. ground truth, while structural node features remain near chance. The random baseline is constructed by field-level without-replacement permutation of ground-truth references, preserving out-degree and field distributions while destroying latent citation structure. Robustness is confirmed by replicating the full pipeline with Claude Sonnet 4.5 as generator and with SPECTER2 embeddings, and by cross-model transfer experiments where classifiers trained on GPT-4o graphs generalize above chance to Claude graphs.

## Results

On the primary task of distinguishing GPT-4o-generated from ground-truth citation graphs (9,218 paired graphs from SciSciNet), structure-only features (degree/closeness/eigenvector centrality, clustering, edge count) yield near-chance RF accuracy of 60.8% and GNN test accuracy of 51–58%. In contrast, 3072-d OpenAI title embeddings raise RF accuracy to 83.5% and GNN test accuracy to 93.1–93.8% across GCN, GraphSAGE, GAT, and GIN. Both feature types cleanly reject the field-matched random baseline (RF: 89.6–95.3%; GNNs: 88.4–97.4%), confirming that LLM bibliographies are structurally realistic but semantically distinguishable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between structure-only graph descriptors and semantic title/abstract embeddings as node features, evaluated with both RF and four GNN architectures (GCN, GAT, GIN, GraphSAGE). For the critical GT-vs-GPT task, embeddings dominate structure by a wide margin: RF jumps from 60.8% to 83.5%, and GNNs jump from ~51–58% to ~93–94%. Against the field-matched random baseline, both feature types perform well (RF: 89.6–95.3%; GNNs: 88.4–97.4%), confirming that the random baseline is trivially separable while the GT-vs-LLM distinction is the hard case. Robustness checks with Claude Sonnet 4.5 (RF: 76.9% GT-vs-Claude with OpenAI embeddings; GNNs: ~94%) and SPECTER2 embeddings (RF: 75.0% GT-vs-GPT; 68.8% GT-vs-Claude) confirm the semantic fingerprint is not tied to a single generator or encoder. Cross-model transfer (train on GPT-4o, test on Claude) retains 68–80% GNN accuracy and 72% RF accuracy, indicating a shared semantic bias across LLM families. A random-vector control (i.i.d. 3072-d features) collapses all classifiers to chance (~50%), ruling out dimensionality as the source of the gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own prior line of work (Algaba et al., 2024; Mobini et al., 2025; Algaba et al., 2025) that documented LLM bibliographies matching human citation networks in coarse bibliometric and graph-structural regularities while exhibiting systematic semantic biases. It departs from single-citation hallucination detectors like LLM-Check by formulating the problem as graph-level classification of entire induced citation graphs, and from prior GNN graph-classification benchmarks (which focused on traditional citation networks) by introducing a paired, field-matched random baseline that isolates the LLM-specific signal from generic structural realism. The methodological toolkit—RF on aggregated descriptors, then GNNs (GCN, GAT, GIN, GraphSAGE) with structural vs. embedding node features—follows standard graph-learning practice but is applied here to a novel paired-graph detection setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean dissociation between structural indistinguishability (~0.60 accuracy) and semantic detectability (~0.83–0.93 accuracy) provides a concrete operating principle for auditing LLM-generated bibliographies: detection pipelines should target embedding-level content signals (topical drift, recency tilt, prestige bias) rather than coarse graph topology, which LLMs already reproduce faithfully. Open questions include which specific semantic dimensions drive separability, whether the fingerprint persists when models have access to external retrieval databases, and whether the signal generalizes beyond title/abstract to full-text. The protocol (paired graphs, domain-matched randomization, stepwise feature-to-GNN escalation) also offers a reusable recipe for stress-testing bibliographic authenticity at scale without manual curation.
