## Problem

Aligning LLMs with human preferences requires costly human annotation, motivating active data selection to minimize the labeling budget. Existing active selection methods either lack theoretical guarantees (heuristic approaches like APLP) or depend on restrictive assumptions such as linear latent reward functions (APO), and none directly leverage the LLM being aligned to guide data selection, ignoring that different models require different data for effective alignment.

## Input / Output

At each iteration, the method consumes a pool of prompt-response triplets (x, y₁, y₂) regenerated by the current LLM (3 responses per prompt across 1000 prompts, yielding 3000 candidate triplets) along with the LLM's LoRA parameters. It outputs a batch of 50 triplets selected for human preference annotation, which are then used to update the LLM via DPO training. The selection criterion operates on LoRA gradients randomly projected to 8192 dimensions, keeping computation tractable for 2B–7B parameter models while preserving inner-product structure via the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma.

## Method

ACTIVEDPO derives a theoretically grounded selection criterion from a bound on the reward estimation error (Proposition 1), showing that the error in estimating the reward difference between two responses is controlled by the norm of the gradient difference of the DPO implicit reward function, weighted by V_{t-1}^{-1} (a matrix accumulating outer products of previously selected gradient features that acts as a diversity regularizer). The selection criterion maximizes this gradient-based uncertainty, explicitly using the LLM's own parameters to identify data most informative for that specific model. To make the criterion tractable, the method computes gradients only on LoRA parameters (reducing dimension to ~1–2% of full model size), applies random projection to 8192 dimensions, performs batch selection with within-batch V_{t-1} updates to encourage diversity across selections in the same batch, and normalizes all gradients to unit L2 norm to prevent the criterion from biasing toward shorter responses.

## Results

ACTIVEDPO consistently achieves the highest average reward across all six model–dataset combinations (TLDR and WebGPT evaluated with Llama-2-7B, Gemma-2B, and Qwen3-4B), outperforming Random, APO, and APLP baselines under a fixed labeling budget of 50 triplets per iteration. The advantage is most pronounced in later iterations, where ACTIVEDPO's gradient-based uncertainty criterion continues to select informative data while APLP's reward-difference criterion degrades. Win-rate results (Fig. 4) confirm the same ordering, with ACTIVEDPO maintaining a lead over all baselines in the final iterations. The method also demonstrates model-dependence: different LLMs (e.g., two Gemma variants trained on disjoint SFT subsets) benefit from different preference data subsets, validating the need for LLM-aware selection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACTIVEDPO outperforms all three baselines (Random, APO, APLP) in average reward across every model–dataset combination tested. Against APLP (Muldrew et al., 2024), ACTIVEDPO shows a clear and consistent advantage; APLP performs competitively on Gemma-2B but actually falls below Random on Llama-2-7B, likely because its heuristic reward-difference criterion selects confidently-wrong triplets early and then high-margin (already-correct) triplets later, providing diminishing returns. Against APO (Das et al., 2024), ACTIVEDPO also wins consistently; APO's performance is erratic across settings because its theoretical guarantees rely on a linear latent reward function assumption that does not hold for DPO's non-linear implicit reward. Random selection serves as the lower bound and is outperformed by ACTIVEDPO in all six settings, confirming that the gradient-based, model-aware selection criterion adds meaningful value over uniform sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

ACTIVEDPO sits at the intersection of two research threads: DPO-based LLM alignment (Rafailov et al., 2023) and principled active preference learning from neural dueling bandits (Verma et al., 2025a). It departs from prior active selection methods in two key ways: (1) unlike APO (Das et al., 2024) and Mehta et al. (2023), it handles non-linear reward functions via NTK-based uncertainty rather than assuming linearity; and (2) unlike APLP (Muldrew et al., 2024) and all other baselines, it uses the LLM being aligned itself to parameterize the reward model that drives selection, making the criterion model-specific rather than model-agnostic. The practical acceleration techniques (LoRA gradients + Johnson-Lindenstrauss random projection) follow the approach of Xia et al. (2024) for influence-based data selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a real bottleneck in LLM alignment: the cost of human preference annotation. By providing a theoretically grounded, model-specific selection criterion, ACTIVEDPO offers a principled alternative to both heuristic methods (APLP) and methods with unrealistic assumptions (APO). However, the theoretical guarantee relies on NTK theory developed for fully connected networks, and the extension to transformers is argued but not formally proven — a significant gap the authors acknowledge. Open questions include whether the gradient-based criterion's advantage persists at larger model scales, whether the NTK assumptions hold in practice for modern LLMs, and how to further reduce the computational cost of per-data-point gradient computation that remains the dominant overhead.
