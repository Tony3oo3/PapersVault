## Problem

Federated LoRA fine-tuning faces two compounding challenges: separately aggregating the two low-rank matrices B and A introduces a systematic bilinear aggregation error that accumulates over rounds, while aggregating the product BA and then decomposing it back into low-rank factors introduces decomposition drift due to the non-uniqueness of matrix factorization (especially under rank deficiency or eigenvalue multiplicities). The paper asks whether a federated fine-tuning scheme can simultaneously eliminate the aggregation error and minimize the decomposition drift.

## Input / Output

Each client receives a frozen pretrained weight matrix W^0 ∈ R^{d_out × d_in}, shared semi-orthogonal basis matrices L ∈ R^{d_out × k} and R ∈ R^{k × d_in} (where k = min{d_in, d_out}), and a local dataset D_n. The client updates a single low-rank matrix A^t ∈ R^{r × k} (r ≪ k) and transmits it to the server, which aggregates the k × k Gram matrix Q = A^T A. The server outputs the next-round matrix A^{t+1} after eigendecomposition and Procrustes alignment, yielding the full model W = W^0 + L A^T A R. Transmitting one r × k matrix instead of two reduces per-round uplink communication by more than half compared to standard federated LoRA.

## Method

FLoRG reparameterizes the LoRA module from two matrices (B, A) to a single matrix A^t with the update ΔW = L A^T A R, where L and R are fixed semi-orthogonal bases shared across all clients, making the framework compatible with arbitrary parameter matrix dimensions. The server aggregates the Gram matrix Q^{t+1} = (1/N) Σ (A_n^{t+1/2})^T A_n^{t+1/2}, which is a linear operation that preserves positive semi-definiteness and eliminates the bilinear aggregation error inherent in separately averaging B and A. After eigendecomposition of Q^{t+1}, the server applies a Procrustes alignment: it solves min_S ||S Ã^{t+1} − A^t||_F^2 subject to S^T S = I, whose closed-form solution S* = UV^T (from the SVD of A^t(Ã^{t+1})^T) projects the decomposed matrix onto the target r-rank subspace while selecting the decomposition nearest to the previous round, thereby minimizing drift. A convergence analysis under nonconvex losses shows that the Procrustes alignment term vanishes when the optimal alignment is used, yielding a tighter bound than without alignment.

## Results

FLoRG achieves the highest testing accuracy across GLUE datasets (MNLI, QNLI, WNLI, RTE) on three base models (OPT-125M, RoBERTa-large, Llama-3.2-3B), outperforming all five baselines in most settings. On OPT-125M, FLoRG improves over the strongest baseline by up to 1.52 points on MNLI (87.35 vs. 85.83) and 1.13 on WNLI (65.28 vs. 64.15); on RoBERTa-large, margins reach 0.45 on QNLI (92.58 vs. 92.13) and 0.37 on WNLI (66.48 vs. 66.11); on Llama-3.2-3B, gains are up to 0.84 on WNLI (68.73 vs. 67.89). FLoRG also reduces the total communication overhead by up to 2041× compared to baselines to reach the same target accuracy (e.g., 8.2×10⁶ vs. 1.25×10¹⁰ transmitted parameters for OPT-125M at 80% target accuracy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLoRG consistently outperforms FedIT (separate aggregation of B and A) by the largest margins, e.g., +7.93 on MNLI/OPT-125M and +6.07 on WNLI/Llama-3.2-3B, confirming that the bilinear aggregation error in FedIT is the dominant performance bottleneck. Against FeDeRA (product aggregation + SVD decomposition), FLoRG wins on all datasets except QNLI/OPT-125M where the gap is negligible (89.52 vs. 89.88), demonstrating that Procrustes alignment effectively mitigates the decomposition drift that FeDeRA suffers from. FFA-LoRA (freezing matrix A) is the weakest baseline overall, trailing FLoRG by 2–6 points, indicating that restricting updates to a single factor limits adaptation capacity. FedSA-LoRA (sharing only A for aggregation) is the closest competitor, with FLoRG winning by 0.3–1.1 points in most settings; however, FedSA-LoRA narrowly edges FLoRG on QNLI/Llama-3.2-3B (93.27 vs. 93.12). FedEx-LoRA (residual correction for aggregation error) is competitive on QNLI but still trails FLoRG on MNLI, WNLI, and RTE across all three model scales, and incurs the highest communication cost (up to 1.25×10¹⁰ parameters vs. 8.2×10⁶ for FLoRG).

## Relation to Prior Work

FLoRG extends the federated LoRA line of work (FedIT, FeDeRA, FFA-LoRA, FedSA-LoRA, FedEx-LoRA) by replacing the standard two-matrix LoRA module (B·A) with a single low-rank matrix whose Gram matrix (AᵀA) is aggregated at the server. This design is conceptually related to SingLoRA (Bensaid et al., 2025), which reformulated LoRA with a single matrix in a centralized setting, but FLoRG is the first to exploit the Gram-matrix structure specifically to make federated aggregation linear rather than bilinear. The Procrustes alignment step, which projects the eigendecomposed matrix onto the previous round's subspace, is a novel stabilization mechanism not present in any of the five baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2041× communication reduction (relative to FedEx-LoRA) and the elimination of systematic aggregation error make FLoRG a practically attractive option for bandwidth-constrained federated fine-tuning of LLMs. The ablation revealing that without Procrustes alignment, FLoRG degrades to FeDeRA-level performance underscores that decomposition drift is a first-order concern, not a minor artifact—this insight may inform future federated matrix-factorization methods beyond LoRA. Open questions include whether the fixed semi-orthogonal basis L and R remain effective when clients have heterogeneous model architectures or when the rank r must adapt per layer, and whether the approach extends to non-PSD aggregation scenarios (e.g., signed updates).
