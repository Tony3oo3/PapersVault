## Problem

The online-to-nonconvex (O2NC) conversion framework of Cutkosky et al. (2023) unifies nonconvex optimization as an online learning problem but suffers from three key limitations: the deterministic method requires a complex fixed-point iteration subroutine that introduces an extra logarithmic factor, the stochastic method assumes a bounded second-order moment of the stochastic gradient (stronger than the standard variance bound), and different online learners are needed for the two settings. No single algorithm simultaneously achieves the best-known deterministic rate O(ε^{-1.75}) and the optimal stochastic rate O(σ²ε^{-3.5}).

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a smooth nonconvex function F with Lipschitz gradient (constant L₁) and Lipschitz Hessian (constant L₂), a stochastic gradient oracle with bounded variance σ², and an initial point x₀. Each iteration requires exactly two gradient queries: one at the midpoint wₙ = x_{n-1} + ½Δₙ and one at the extrapolated point zₙ = xₙ + ½Δₙ. The output is the average iterate over K episodes of length T, with the guarantee that the expected gradient norm at the returned point is O(ε) after O(ε^{-1.75} + σ²ε^{-3.5}) total gradient calls, smoothly interpolating between the deterministic and stochastic regimes.

## Method

The core contribution is a doubly optimistic hint function for the online optimistic gradient method. The first layer of optimism (standard) assumes the gradient-hint discrepancy gₙ − hₙ evolves slowly, enabling the update Δ_{n+1} = Π(Δₙ − ηh_{n+1} − η(gₙ − hₙ)) with a single projection. The second, novel layer of optimism assumes consecutive update directions change slowly due to smoothness, which is exploited by setting the hint hₙ = ∇F(z_{n-1}) where z_{n-1} = x_{n-1} + ½Δ_{n-1} is an extrapolated point. This single-gradient-call construction replaces the multi-step fixed-point iteration of Cutkosky et al. and, crucially, makes ‖gₙ − hₙ‖ proportional to ‖Δₙ − Δ_{n-1}‖, enabling a telescoping argument with the negative term −(1/4η)‖Δₙ − Δ_{n-1}‖² in the regret bound that was previously neglected. The stochastic variant simply replaces deterministic gradients with their stochastic counterparts, requiring only the standard bounded-variance assumption. An adaptive step size ηₙ = γD/√(α + Σ‖gᵢ − hᵢ‖²) is also provided, replacing the global Lipschitz constant with a local one while maintaining the same complexity.

## Results

The proposed Online Doubly Optimistic Gradient algorithm achieves a unified gradient complexity of O(ε^{-1.75} + σ²ε^{-3.5}) for smooth nonconvex optimization, smoothly interpolating between the best-known deterministic rate and the worst-case optimal stochastic rate. In the deterministic setting (σ=0), this recovers O(ε^{-1.75}), eliminating the extra logarithmic factor present in prior O2NC-based methods. In the stochastic setting, the σ²ε^{-3.5} term matches the known lower bound of Arjevani et al. (2023), and the algorithm requires only the standard bounded-variance assumption (σ²) rather than the stronger bounded-second-moment condition (G²) used by Cutkosky et al. (2023). The adaptive step size variant (Theorem 4.2) maintains the same complexity while replacing the global Lipschitz constant L₁ with a local constant L̂₁*.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is against the O2NC framework of Cutkosky et al. (2023), which is the direct predecessor. In the deterministic setting, the proposed method removes the logarithmic factor, improving from O(ε^{-1.75} log(1/ε)) to O(ε^{-1.75}), matching the best-known rate of Li & Lin (2022, 2023) but with a much simpler single-gradient-call hint instead of a fixed-point iteration. In the stochastic setting, the method achieves O(σ²ε^{-3.5}) under the standard bounded-variance assumption, whereas Cutkosky et al. (2023) requires the stronger bounded-second-moment assumption (G²) and achieves O(G²ε^{-3.5}). Unlike Cutkosky & Mehta (2020), which attains the optimal stochastic rate O(σ²ε^{-3.5}) but only O(ε^{-2}) in the deterministic case, the proposed algorithm simultaneously achieves both best-known rates with a single unified algorithm. The method also outperforms the naive optimistic hint (h_n = g_{n-1}) which yields only O(ε^{-11/6}), by exploiting the negative telescoping term in the regret bound through the extrapolated-point hint construction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the online-to-nonconvex (O2NC) conversion framework of Cutkosky et al. (2023), which recasts nonconvex stationary-point finding as an online learning problem over update directions. The key departure is replacing the original framework's complex fixed-point iteration subroutine (which introduced a logarithmic factor in the deterministic case) with a simple single-gradient-call hint at an extrapolated point, motivated by a novel 'doubly optimistic' assumption: not only does the gradient-hint discrepancy evolve slowly (standard optimism), but the update direction itself changes slowly due to smoothness. This second layer of optimism allows exploiting a negative term in the regret bound that was previously neglected, enabling a telescoping argument that tightens the convergence rate. The optimistic online gradient descent template is inherited from Rakhlin & Sridharan (2013) and Joulani et al. (2020), while the adaptive step size scheme draws on the thresholding strategies of Kavis et al. (2019) and Antonakopoulos et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm to simultaneously achieve the best-known deterministic rate O(ε^{-1.75}) and the worst-case optimal stochastic rate O(σ²ε^{-3.5}) within a single unified framework, resolving a key limitation of the original O2NC conversion that required different online learners for each setting. The elimination of the fixed-point iteration and the logarithmic factor simplifies both the algorithm and its analysis, making the O2NC framework more modular and easier to extend. The adaptive step size variant represents a first step toward fully parameter-free algorithms, though the choices of D and T still require problem-specific knowledge. Open questions include whether the extrapolated-point hint construction extends to quasi-Newton or second-order settings, and whether the gap between the best upper bound O(ε^{-1.75}) and the known lower bound Ω(ε^{-12/7}) can be closed.
