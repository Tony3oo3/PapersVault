## Problem

Data-centric CoT distillation methods—augmentation, selection, and mixing—have gained popularity for transferring reasoning from large teacher LLMs to smaller students, yet no systematic benchmark exists to compare their effectiveness across methods, teacher-student pairs, and data settings. This gap leaves practitioners without principled guidance on which data manipulations to apply, which teacher-student combinations work best, and how distillation performance generalizes out-of-distribution.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes teacher-generated CoT traces (question, rationale, answer tuples) from black-box APIs (GPT-4, Gemini-1.5-Pro, Claude-3.5, o4-mini) and applies a parameterized transformation M(D_source, Θ) to produce a target training set for LoRA fine-tuning of open-source student models (Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B, Gemma-7B, Qwen-2.5-7B, and smaller 0.5B–3B variants). No teacher logits or weights are required—only text-level CoT rationales—making the approach fully black-box. Evaluation spans 11+ reasoning datasets across textual (SQA, CSQA, ARC, GSM8K, MATH, ANLI, Date), visual (Visual-CoT, OK-VQA, CLEVR), and agentic (WebArena) modalities, reporting accuracy under both in-distribution and out-of-distribution conditions.

## Method

DC-CoT decomposes data-centric CoT distillation into three orthogonal operations evaluated under a unified protocol. For augmentation, it implements four strategies: Question Rephrasing (paraphrase + regenerate CoT), Question Augmentation (generate novel parallel problems), Answer Augmentation (multiple diverse rationales for the same answer), and Reverse Thinking (generate forward reasoning, synthesize a backward question, then retain only forward-backward consistent quadruplets). For selection, it compares Teacher Correctness filtering, Student Error prioritization, and LLM-as-a-Judge scoring (validated at κ=0.84 against human raters). For mixing, it blends CoTs by reasoning length or teacher origin using a ratio α. The key empirical finding is that Reverse Thinking augmentation yields the largest average gain (+24.64% over Vanilla CoT on Llama-3.1-8B), substantially outperforming both selection and mixing, and that the optimal strategy is task-dependent: Reverse excels on structured logic (MATH, GSM8K, Date), Answer Augmentation on commonsense (SQA, CSQA), and LLM-as-a-Judge filtering is essential for agentic and visual tasks where simple correctness checks fail to verify grounding.

## Results

On Llama-3.1-8B, Reverse Thinking augmentation achieves the highest textual average accuracy of 66.45%, a +24.64% absolute gain over the Vanilla CoT baseline (41.81%). Answer Augmentation follows at 57.58%, while data selection (Teacher Correctness filtering: 44.73%) and mixing (Teacher Mixing: 41.97%) yield only marginal improvements over no manipulation. In a direct comparison against white-box logit-based KD on ARC-Challenge (teacher: Llama-3.1-70B), DC-CoT with Reverse Thinking reaches 69.2% accuracy versus 64.8% for standard KL-divergence KD, demonstrating that explicit reasoning-step transfer via data augmentation outperforms output-distribution matching.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Reverse Thinking (Chen et al., 2024) is the dominant augmentation strategy, achieving 66.45% textual average and substantially outperforming Zero Shot CoT (Kojima et al., 2022; 45.90%), Question Rephrasing from MetaMath (Yu et al., 2023; 49.56%), and Question Augmentation (Li et al., 2024a; 51.27%) by 15–21 points. Among selection baselines, Teacher Correctness filtering (Ho et al., 2022) provides a modest +1.93% over Vanilla CoT, while LLM-as-a-Judge filtering (inspired by I-SHEEP, Liang et al., 2024) actually underperforms the no-selection baseline (41.04% vs. 42.80%), suggesting that nuanced quality scoring can be counterproductive when it over-prunes diverse rationales. Both mixing strategies from Li et al. (2025b) (Length Mixing: 41.43%, Teacher Mixing: 41.97%) slightly underperform the no-mixing baseline, indicating that for 7–8B students, reshuffling existing CoTs is less impactful than generating new diverse ones. In the white-box comparison on ARC-Challenge, DC-CoT (Reverse) at 69.2% beats standard KL-divergence KD (Hinton et al., 2015) at 64.8% by 4.4 points, confirming that explicit reasoning-step transfer via data augmentation is more effective than output-distribution matching for reasoning tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CoT distillation lineage (Orca, Distilling Step-by-Step, LLaMA-3.1-R1) by shifting focus from *which* teacher or *what* loss to use toward *how to manipulate the training data itself*. It operationalizes three previously studied but never jointly benchmarked data-centric operations—augmentation (Reverse Thinking from Chen et al. 2024, Question/Answer Augmentation from MetaMath), selection (teacher-correctness filtering from Ho et al. 2022, LLM-as-a-Judge from I-SHEEP), and mixing (length/teacher blending from Li et al. 2025b)—into a unified evaluation framework. Unlike prior distillation papers that test one technique in isolation on a single task, DC-CoT systematically crosses these operations across multiple teacher-student pairs, modalities (text, visual, agentic), and distributional settings (IID, OOD, cross-domain).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides practitioners with the first actionable decision framework for data-centric CoT distillation: which augmentation to pair with which filtering for which task type, and when a smaller teacher may outperform a larger one. The 'learnability gap' finding—that a 3B student benefits more from GPT-4-mini than GPT-4 on visual reasoning—challenges the default assumption that the strongest available teacher is always optimal, though the evidence is limited to one student-task combination. Key open questions include whether these findings generalize beyond 7-8B students, whether the non-monotonic data-scaling behavior of Vanilla CoT persists at larger scales, and how the framework extends to non-Transformer architectures. Budget constraints (no GPT-o4, single agentic benchmark) limit the breadth of conclusions.
