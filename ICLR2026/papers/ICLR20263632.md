## Problem

LLMs exhibit 'reasoning miscalibration' at inference time: they overthink on simple queries (wasting tokens on tangential reasoning) and underthink on hard queries (terminating prematurely under fixed budgets). Existing mitigations impose a single global token limit, which cannot simultaneously address both failure modes. The paper asks how to characterize and correct this miscalibration by adaptively allocating computation across reasoning steps based on their uncertainty structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language query and a total token budget B_i (e.g., 8192 hard cutoff). A lightweight planner LLM (LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct) decomposes the query into m sub-questions with estimated complexity scores, adding ~14.73 s of overhead. The main reasoning LLM then answers each sub-question under its locally allocated token budget, and a synthesis step aggregates the sub-answers into the final response. For open-source models, tokens before the  tag and final output are counted; for o4-mini, reasoning and output tokens are summed per OpenAI's API.

## Method

PLAN-AND-BUDGET is a two-stage, model-agnostic test-time framework grounded in the Budget Allocation Model (BAM), which formalizes reasoning as a sequence of sub-questions whose epistemic uncertainty follows an inverse power law U_epistemic(s_ij | b_ij) = c_ij / b_ij^β_ij. Solving the Lagrangian under a fixed budget yields a closed-form allocation (Eq. 6) with a unimodal relationship between allocated tokens and sub-question complexity. Because c_ij and β_ij are not observable in black-box LLMs, the practical method replaces the exact optimum with decay-based scheduling (polynomial, cosine, exponential, etc.) that front-loads budget toward early, high-uncertainty sub-questions. The Plan step decomposes the query and estimates per-sub-question complexity weights w_ij; the Budget step computes b_ij = ⌊(w_ij · ρ_ij) / Σ_k(w_ik · ρ_ik) · B_i⌋, combining content-driven weights with a positional decay prior ρ_ij. No retraining or fine-tuning is required—only prompting and a single lightweight planner call.

## Results

On TravelPlanner (the most challenging agentic planning benchmark), PLAN-AND-BUDGET achieves up to 193.8% E³ improvement (DS-Qwen-32B: 0.16 → 0.47) and up to 70% accuracy gain (14.33% → 23.11% pass rate) while reducing token usage by ~20%. On MATH-500, E³ improves by 20–29% across all four models (e.g., DS-LLaMA-70B: 4.55 → 5.89; o4-mini: 13.25 → 15.95) with comparable or higher accuracy. On NaturalInstructions, E³ gains range from 14% to 36% (e.g., QwQ-32B: 1.47 → 2.00). Notably, DS-Qwen-32B with PLAN-AND-BUDGET (E³=0.47) matches the efficiency of the larger DS-LLaMA-70B without planning (E³=0.50) on TravelPlanner.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLAN-AND-BUDGET consistently outperforms both the Vanilla (no planning, no budget) and Global Budget (fixed token limit) baselines across all four models and three datasets, with E³ gains of 20–194% depending on task complexity. Against the early-termination baseline Certaindex (Fu et al., 2024) on MATH-500, PLAN-AND-BUDGET achieves higher accuracy (e.g., 90.04% vs. 81.44% on DS-Qwen-32B) and lower token usage (1336 vs. 1986), yielding a substantially better E³ (6.07 vs. 3.34). The Global Budget baseline reduces tokens but often degrades accuracy (e.g., o4-mini on TravelPlanner drops from 11.58% to 8.33% pass rate), whereas PLAN-AND-BUDGET's adaptive local budgeting preserves or improves accuracy while still cutting tokens. Planning alone (Planned + Global Budget) already yields 2–13% E³ gains, confirming that structured decomposition is a key component, but adding local decay-based budgeting provides additional 14–33% E³ improvements. The most striking comparison is on TravelPlanner, where DS-Qwen-32B with PLAN-AND-BUDGET (E³=0.47) nearly matches the larger DS-LLaMA-70B without any planning (E³=0.50), demonstrating that uncertainty-aware budgeting can close the small-to-large model efficiency gap without retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the epistemic/aleatoric uncertainty decomposition (Hüllermeier & Waegeman, 2021) and the Bayesian interpretation of in-context learning (Falck et al., 2024) to formalize LLM reasoning as a budget allocation problem under uncertainty. It departs from prior test-time scaling work (Snell et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2025) that focuses on simply increasing compute, and from fixed-budget or early-termination approaches (Certaindex, global token limits) that treat all reasoning steps uniformly. The key conceptual departure is reframing reasoning miscalibration as a resource-allocation problem solvable via Lagrangian optimization, then approximating the optimal solution with lightweight decay-based scheduling rather than expensive per-step uncertainty estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The E3 metric (A²/T) offers a more principled way to evaluate efficiency-accuracy tradeoffs than accuracy-per-token, and the finding that structured planning plus adaptive budgeting can close the efficiency gap between a 32B and 70B model without retraining is practically significant for resource-constrained deployments. However, the method requires an additional LLM call for planning, adding latency overhead (though small at 3-7%), and the large percentage gains on TravelPlanner (193.8%) are from a very low baseline (0.16→0.47), so absolute performance remains modest. A key open question is whether the planner can be internalized into a single model via fine-tuning, eliminating the extra inference call and making the approach truly end-to-end.
