## Problem

Instruction-based image editing (IIE) models apply a uniform multi-step denoising process across the entire image, even though instructions typically modify only local regions while the rest remains essentially unchanged. This creates significant spatial redundancy (wasted computation on unedited areas) and temporal redundancy (highly similar velocity outputs at adjacent timesteps), yet the denoising-only IIE paradigm has not been systematically analyzed for such redundancies or accelerated accordingly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262329/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

RegionE is a training-free wrapper applied to existing IIE models (Step1X-Edit, FLUX.1 Kontext, Qwen-Image-Edit), which natively operate at 1k resolution. The Instruction-DiT receives three token streams — text/prompt tokens, noise tokens (evolving per timestep), and instruction tokens (static reference image) — and outputs only the noise-component velocity. RegionE partitions the noise tokens into edited and unedited subsets mid-denoising, processes them separately, and reassembles them spatially to produce the final edited image at the same resolution as the reference.

## Method

RegionE operates in three stages: a Stabilization Stage (6 steps of unmodified denoising to let DiT outputs stabilize and cache KV pairs), a Region-Aware Generation Stage, and a Smooth Stage (2 full denoising steps to eliminate boundary artifacts). The core innovation is the Adaptive Region Partition, which performs a one-step Euler prediction of the final image and thresholds cosine similarity against the instruction image to separate edited from unedited regions — exploiting the observation that unedited regions follow near-linear trajectories (reliable one-step prediction) while edited regions follow curved trajectories. Unedited regions are then resolved in a single step, while edited regions undergo local iterative denoising with two acceleration mechanisms: the Region-Instruction KV Cache (RIKVCache), which replaces discarded unedited and instruction tokens in attention with cached KV pairs to preserve global context, and the Adaptive Velocity Decay Cache (AVDCache), which skips DiT calls at adjacent timesteps by scaling a cached velocity with a timestep-dependent decay factor fitted from data. Periodic forced full-image steps refresh the KV cache to counteract similarity decay over timesteps.

## Results

RegionE achieves end-to-end speedups of 2.572×, 2.409×, and 2.059× on Step1X-Edit, FLUX.1 Kontext, and Qwen-Image-Edit respectively, while maintaining the highest reconstruction fidelity among all acceleration methods: PSNR of 30.520, 32.133, and 31.115; SSIM of 0.939, 0.917, and 0.937; and LPIPS of 0.054, 0.057, and 0.046. GPT-4o editing quality scores (G-O) of 6.948, 6.538, and 7.731 match or exceed the vanilla models, confirming negligible perceptual degradation. Latency drops from 27.945s→10.865s (Step1X-Edit), 14.682s→6.096s (FLUX.1 Kontext), and 32.125s→15.604s (Qwen-Image-Edit).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RegionE (T&S) dominates all six baselines on every reconstruction metric across all three models. Against the strongest temporal baseline TeaCache, RegionE improves PSNR by 2.3–3.8 dB (e.g., 30.520 vs. 28.262 on Step1X-Edit), reduces LPIPS by ~25–35%, and achieves a higher speedup (2.572× vs. 2.493×). Against the strongest spatial baseline RAS, RegionE gains 3.7–5.9 dB in PSNR and 1.2–1.7× additional speedup, while RAS suffers larger perceptual drops (G-O: 6.615 vs. 6.948 on Step1X-Edit). FORA and Δ-DiT show the largest quality degradation (PSNR 20–25 dB, G-O drops of 0.5–1.0), indicating that naive temporal caching without region awareness is particularly harmful for IIE. ToCa, a token-level spatial method, is the weakest baseline overall (lowest speedup 1.26–1.30×) and fails entirely (OOM) on Qwen-Image-Edit. RegionE is the only method that simultaneously achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, GPT-4o scores, and competitive speedup, validating the benefit of jointly addressing spatial and temporal redundancy in a region-aware manner.

## Relation to Prior Work

RegionE builds on the residual/velocity cache lineage established by Δ-DiT and TeaCache, which exploit temporal redundancy by reusing intermediate features across timesteps in diffusion transformers. However, it departs from these methods in two key ways: (1) it introduces an adaptive region partition that exploits the trajectory difference between edited and unedited regions—a property unique to the IIE task and not present in standard image generation—and (2) it combines this spatial decomposition with a timestep-aware velocity decay model (AVDCache) that subsumes the residual cache as a special case. The Region-Instruction KV Cache (RIKVCache) further distinguishes the work by reinjecting global context into local generation, addressing a bias that purely local attention would introduce. Unlike prior spatial-redundancy methods (RAS, ToCa) that operate on token-level sensitivity, RegionE partitions at the semantic region level based on the editing instruction's effect.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RegionE provides a practical, training-free path to near-real-time instruction-based image editing (2.5× speedup with PSNR > 30), which is directly relevant to interactive creative tools and production pipelines where latency is a bottleneck. The method's model-agnosticism—validated across three architecturally distinct IIE models—suggests the trajectory-based region partitioning principle may generalize to future editing models. Limitations include the 6–10% memory overhead from KV caching, the need for per-model threshold tuning (η, δ), and the observation that global editing tasks (where spatial redundancy is minimal) see reduced speedups. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video editing, higher-resolution settings (only preliminarily tested), and whether the forced full-computation steps can be made adaptive rather than fixed.
