## Problem

Existing meta-network approaches for neural network interpretability analyze single static checkpoints and rely on Euclidean embeddings, leaving the temporal traces of optimization underused and unable to capture the hierarchical, heavy-tailed geometry of parameter graphs. No prior work has embedded neural parameter graphs in hyperbolic space while simultaneously preserving permutation symmetry and modeling the evolution of structure across training epochs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262820/cropped_page9_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of T parameter graph snapshots (typically 80–100 checkpoints) extracted from an MLP's training trace, where nodes are neurons and biases and edges carry signed weight labels; the meta-network never sees the underlying data (images, functions). It produces hyperbolic node embeddings in the Poincaré ball, Fermi-Dirac link probabilities, signed weight predictions decomposed into a power-law magnitude tied to geodesic distance and a tangent-space polarity, and a fixed-dimensional temporal representation vector obtained by pooling edge embeddings per snapshot and then across time for downstream classification or regression.

## Method

GTH-GMN constructs permutation-equivariant parameter graphs at each checkpoint, embeds them in the Poincaré ball, and processes them with a hyperbolic graph attention layer that scores edges by negative geodesic distance and aggregates via Einstein gyromidpoints computed in the Klein model. Temporal smoothness is enforced through an EvolveGCN-style GRU that recurrently updates the Q/K/V attention kernel parameters from pooled tangent-space summaries, avoiding storage of past embeddings. A signed weight regressor decouples magnitude (a power-law function of hyperbolic distance with node-dependent scale and decay) from polarity (a conformally normalized tangent-space inner product), importing scale-free inductive biases from network science. Optimization proceeds in two phases: a standard Euclidean backpropagation pass for kernels and regressors, followed by a Riemannian pass using RAMSGrad-style adaptive updates with conformal rescaling and exponential-map projection to refine node positions on the manifold. The architecture is equivariant to within-snapshot neuron permutations and invariant to permutations of past snapshots, ensuring functionally equivalent networks share the same representation.

## Results

On INR classification, the temporal hyperbolic meta-network (GTH-GMN) achieves 95.6±0.18% test accuracy on MNIST and 80.72±0.29% on Fashion-MNIST, outperforming the strongest baselines (NFNNP: 92.9% and 75.6% respectively). On CIFAR-10 generalization prediction, GTH-GMN attains a Kendall's τ of 0.846±0.004, trailing the neural functional baselines (NFNHNP: 0.934, NFNNP: 0.922). On the sinusoid regression benchmark, GTH-GMN reaches 1.06±0.24 test MSE, marginally beating GMN (1.13±0.08) and DWSNets (1.39±0.06).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DWSNets (Navon et al., 2024), GTH-GMN wins decisively on all three tasks: +9.9 pp on MNIST (95.6 vs 85.71), +13.7 pp on Fashion-MNIST (80.72 vs 67.06), and −0.33 MSE on sinusoid (1.06 vs 1.39). Against the neural functional baselines NFNHNP and NFNNP (Zhou et al., 2023a,b), GTH-GMN wins on both INR classification benchmarks (+3.1 pp and +2.7 pp on MNIST; +8.0 pp and +5.1 pp on Fashion-MNIST) but loses on CIFAR-10 generalization prediction (0.846 vs 0.934 and 0.922 Kendall's τ), which the authors attribute to the trade-off between geometric interpretability and fine-grained tensor detail retention. Against StatNN (Unterthiner et al., 2021), GTH-GMN also trails on CIFAR-10 (0.846 vs 0.915). Against GMN (Lim et al., 2023), GTH-GMN achieves a slight edge on sinusoid regression (1.06 vs 1.13 MSE) though with higher variance (±0.24 vs ±0.08), which the authors link to curvature-induced sensitivity in the Poincaré ball.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) neural meta-networks that treat trained weights as data objects for classification or generalization prediction (Unterthiner et al., 2021; Navon et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2023a,b), (2) graph-based parameter representations that model neurons and weights as nodes and edges with permutation-equivariant GNNs (Lim et al., 2023; Kofinas et al., 2024), and (3) hyperbolic geometric embeddings that capture hierarchical and heavy-tailed structure more compactly than Euclidean spaces (Nickel & Kiela, 2017; Ganea et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2019). The key departure is that prior meta-networks operate on single checkpoints in Euclidean space, while prior hyperbolic GNNs handle static or dynamic graphs but not neural parameter graphs with permutation symmetry. GTH-GMN is the first to combine temporal parameter-graph sequences, Poincaré-ball embeddings, EvolveGCN-style kernel recurrence (Pareja et al., 2019), and within-snapshot permutation equivariance into a single architecture, importing power-law distance-magnitude priors from network science (Allard et al., 2017; Krioukov et al., 2010).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a concrete geometric lens on how neural networks self-organize during training: radial drift in the Poincaré ball tracks influence strength, angular separation tracks functional specialization, and temporal smoothness of trajectories reflects optimization dynamics. This could enable intrinsic explainability that is grounded in the model's own weight-space geometry rather than post-hoc probes. However, the interpretability claims are supported primarily by qualitative PCA visualizations (Figure 2) rather than quantitative validation against known functional modules, and the CIFAR-10 generalization-prediction result (Kendall's τ = 0.846) trails strong neural-functional baselines (0.934), suggesting a real trade-off between geometric interpretability and raw predictive fidelity. Open questions include whether the hyperbolic structure captures genuinely meaningful self-organization or merely provides a convenient low-distortion embedding, how the approach scales to deeper/wider networks or non-MLP architectures, and whether the temporal signal adds information beyond what a well-tuned single-checkpoint hyperbolic encoder could extract.
