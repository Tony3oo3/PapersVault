## Problem

Skill-based meta-RL methods (e.g., SiMPL) learn reusable low-level skills from offline demonstrations, but real-world datasets are often corrupted by noise from hardware aging, sensor drift, or environmental perturbations. When offline data is suboptimal, the skill library becomes corrupted and this degradation propagates to the high-level policy, causing sharp performance declines in long-horizon tasks. Existing approaches treat all offline trajectories equally, allowing low-quality samples to dominate skill learning without any mechanism to identify or suppress them.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state observations as continuous vectors (60-dim for Kitchen, 76-dim for Office, 2-dim for Maze2D, 10-dim for AntMaze) along with a reward-free offline trajectory dataset collected from perturbed expert policies. During meta-test, only a small number of interaction trajectories are used to fine-tune the high-level policy while the low-level skill policy remains frozen. The output is a continuous action at each timestep (low-level) and a skill latent z selected every H_s steps (high-level), where z parameterizes the low-level policy for a fixed-duration skill execution.

## Method

SISL decouples training into a high-level policy π_h that exploits the current skill library and a skill-improvement policy π_imp that deliberately perturbs trajectories near the offline data manifold using RND-based intrinsic motivation to discover higher-quality behaviors. A prioritized online buffer B_on selectively retains only the highest-return trajectories, forming a self-improving curriculum that guides π_imp toward empirically successful regions. For skill refinement, a learned reward model assigns hypothetical maximum returns to offline trajectories, and a softmax prioritization scheme reweights offline samples by task relevance. A dynamic mixing coefficient β, computed from the average returns of offline and online buffers, adaptively balances their contributions to the SPiRL skill loss. Every K_iter iterations, the skill encoder, prior, and low-level policy are re-trained on both prioritized buffers and the high-level policy is reinitialized, progressively denoising the skill library while preserving generalization.

## Results

SISL achieves the highest final test average return across all four long-horizon environments (Kitchen, Office, Maze2D, AntMaze) at every noise level. Under high noise (Kitchen σ=0.3, Office σ=0.3), SISL maintains returns of 3.48 and 1.68 respectively, while the closest skill-based baseline SiMPL collapses to 0.81 and 0.11. Even with expert (noise-free) data, SISL leads: 3.97 on Kitchen, 2.86 on Office, 0.87 on Maze2D, and 0.81 on AntMaze. In Maze2D at σ=1.5, SISL reaches near-perfect task completion (0.99) while SiMPL drops to 0.68.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SISL outperforms all four baselines (SAC, PEARL, SPiRL, SiMPL) across every environment and noise level. The non-meta baselines SAC and PEARL, which lack skill decomposition and offline data, are essentially non-competitive on long-horizon tasks (e.g., 0.01 on Kitchen, 0.00 on Office/AntMaze). SPiRL, which uses skills but trains per-task without meta-learning, degrades sharply under noise (Kitchen drops from 3.11 to 0.83; Office from 0.65 to 0.42). SiMPL, the closest skill-based meta-RL competitor, shows the most dramatic failure under noise: Kitchen collapses from 3.40 (expert) to 0.81 (σ=0.3), and Office from 2.50 to 0.11, while SISL retains 3.48 and 1.68 respectively. In Maze2D, higher noise actually benefits SISL (0.99 at σ=1.5 vs. 0.87 at expert) as diverse trajectories improve skill coverage, whereas SiMPL degrades from 0.80 to 0.68. The paper attributes SiMPL's instability to its fixed skill library and unstable task encoder under noisy data, which SISL addresses through iterative skill refinement and maximum return relabeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the skill-based meta-RL lineage from SPiRL (offline skill learning) and SiMPL (skill-based meta-RL with a context encoder built on PEARL) by introducing a decoupled skill-improvement policy that actively perturbs trajectories near the offline data manifold to discover higher-quality rollouts. The key departure is the self-improving refinement loop: rather than treating the offline skill library as fixed (as in SiMPL), SISL iteratively denoises it through a prioritized online buffer and maximum return relabeling, which assigns task-relevant hypothetical returns to offline trajectories via a learned reward model. The authors position this as the first framework to explicitly handle suboptimal or noisy offline demonstrations in the skill-based meta-RL setting, a gap they identify as critical given real-world data corruption from sensor drift, hardware aging, and environmental perturbations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: real-world offline datasets are rarely clean, and the paper demonstrates that existing skill-based meta-RL methods (SiMPL, SPiRL) degrade sharply under even moderate noise, while SISL maintains near-expert performance. The counterintuitive finding that higher noise in Maze2D (σ=1.5) actually *improves* SISL over the expert-data baseline suggests the improvement policy can exploit diversity in noisy trajectories to discover skills the original data missed. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to complex, non-sparse reward structures (the authors note per-task reward standardization would be needed), whether zero-shot skill adaptation at test time is feasible without the current fine-tuning overhead, and whether the 16% per-iteration computational cost remains acceptable at larger scales.
