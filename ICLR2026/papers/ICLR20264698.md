## Problem

Existing synthetic graph benchmarks like GraphWorld generate isolated, independent graphs, confining evaluation to transductive settings where models train and test on the same structure. This single-graph paradigm makes it impossible to systematically study inductive generalization to unseen graphs or to evaluate robustness under controlled distribution shifts—two capabilities identified as critical for building graph foundation models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264698/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The generator takes a two-tier parameter specification: universe-level parameters (a K×K edge propensity matrix with controlled heterogeneity, per-community degree propensity vectors in [-1,1], and Gaussian feature centroids) and family-level parameters (ranges for homophily, average degree, node count, participating community count, degree separation, and power-law exponent). Each generated graph instance includes an adjacency matrix, node feature vectors drawn from community-specific Gaussians, and ground-truth community labels, with the key constraint that community semantic identities (structural patterns, degree rankings, feature centroids) persist across all graphs in a family.

## Method

GraphUniverse extends Degree-Corrected Stochastic Block Models (DC-SBMs) from single-graph to multi-graph generation via a three-level hierarchical architecture: a Universe level defines K persistent communities with fixed structural, degree, and feature properties; a Family level specifies allowed ranges for graph-level parameters; and a Graph level samples individual instances by drawing parameters from family ranges and inheriting community identities from the universe. Edge generation uses a Bernoulli formulation (following Rohe et al., 2018) where each pair connects with probability min(1, θ_i θ_j P_scaled[c(i),c(j)]), with degree factors coupled to communities via rank-based assignment from power-law distributions. A two-stage rescaling of the community probability matrix enforces target homophily (via separate diagonal/off-diagonal scaling) and target edge density (via global multiplier), preserving the fine-grained heterogeneity of the universe-level propensity matrix. This design enables the first systematic evaluation of inductive generalization, distribution-shift robustness, and size generalization across diverse graph architectures.

## Results

The benchmark reveals that strong transductive performance is a poor predictor of inductive generalization: GIN dominates transductively but fails inductively, while Neural Sheaf Diffusion excels inductively but falters transductively. GPS consistently achieves top inductive performance across homophily and degree settings, and is one of only three architectures (with NSD and GIN) to effectively solve triangle counting, though GIN fails to generalize to larger graphs. Under controlled distribution shifts (±0.1 homophily, ±4 average degree, ±200 nodes), robustness is highly context-dependent—identical shifts can produce opposite effects depending on the training regime. For real-world alignment, GraphUniverse-generated datasets show positive Spearman rank correlations with real datasets across all five benchmarks, while GraphWorld shows negative correlations for half the datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among 13 benchmarked architectures, GPS (graph transformer) is the strongest inductive performer across all property settings, while DeepSet and GraphMLP (non-message-passing baselines) consistently rank last. GIN achieves the best transductive accuracy but collapses inductively, suggesting its success stems from memorizing a single graph's structure rather than learning generalizable patterns. Neural Sheaf Diffusion (NSD) shows the opposite pattern—strong inductive generalization aided by topological biases but weaker transductive performance. For size generalization (RQ3), GPS and NSD maintain triangle-counting accuracy when scaling from 200 to 700 nodes, while GIN and standard MPNNs (GCN, GraphSAGE, GATv2) fail to generalize. Heterophily-specialized models (FAGCN, H2GCN, ChebNet) outperform baselines in transductive low-homophily regimes (0.05–0.25) but lose their advantage inductively, where standard architectures like GATv2 and GraphSAGE match or exceed them. GraphSAGE exhibits a counterintuitive vulnerability: increasing homophily from a low baseline (0.0–0.1) actually degrades its performance, while GIN is highly sensitive to degree shifts in low-degree graphs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Degree-Corrected Stochastic Block Model (Karrer & Newman, 2011) and its Bernoulli reformulation (Rohe et al., 2018) from generating individual graphs to generating entire families of graphs with persistent semantic communities, enabling inductive evaluation. The key departure is from GraphWorld (Palowitch et al., 2022) and related single-graph synthetic generators, which are fundamentally confined to transductive settings where models train and test on the same structure. By introducing a three-level hierarchical architecture (Universe → Family → Graph), the paper decouples global community identities from local structural properties, a design that has no direct precedent in prior synthetic graph generation tools.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework enabling systematic evaluation of inductive generalization in graph learning at scale, addressing a gap explicitly identified in recent position papers (Bechler-Speicher et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025) as critical for graph foundation model development. The finding that transductive performance is a poor predictor of inductive generalization, and that robustness to distribution shift is context-dependent on both architecture and initial graph regime, challenges common evaluation practices in the field. Limitations include the DC-SBM foundation's inability to capture higher-order motifs, geometric constraints, or overlapping communities, and the real-world validation is limited to five datasets. Open questions include whether the framework can be extended to more sophisticated generative models and whether it can serve as a data augmentation tool for graph foundation model pre-training.
