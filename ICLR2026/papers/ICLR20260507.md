## Problem

Gradient-based data selection methods (TracIn, Influence Functions) are computationally infeasible for multi-billion-parameter LLMs due to repeated backpropagation or inverse-Hessian computations. Off-the-shelf smaller proxies offer a workaround but are suboptimal: their learning dynamics are opaque, their sizes are fixed to a handful of options, and there is no principled way to align them with the target model for influence estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260507/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a warmed-up target LLM and a small probe set (1% of the training data, split 10% for second-moment estimation and 90% for alignment). It produces a low-rank compressed proxy whose size is flexibly controlled by a sparsity ratio ρ (e.g., 0.3–0.7), which is then used to compute per-sample influence scores over the full candidate training set, yielding a top-k selected subset for fine-tuning. The embedding layer and LM head are kept uncompressed, and the proxy's low-rank factorization (A_ℓ, B_ℓ per layer) is what makes downstream influence computation cheap.

## Method

IPROX is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (IPSVD) replaces standard SVD with an influence-preserving variant: each weight matrix W_ℓ is reweighted by the square roots of second-moment matrices C_{δ,ℓ}^{1/2} and C_{h,ℓ}^{1/2} (derived from a small probe set via skinny SVDs), so the truncated decomposition retains components most relevant to gradient-based influence rather than merely minimizing Frobenius reconstruction error. A theoretical bound (Proposition 4.1) shows that minimizing the expected squared directional effect E_z[e_ℓ(z)²] controls the distortion in pairwise influence scores. Stage 2 (alignment) fine-tunes the proxy by (i) projecting the target model's full gradients into the low-rank proxy space via the chain rule and minimizing a distance between projected and native proxy gradients, and (ii) anchoring the proxy's output logits to the target's via a temperature-scaled KL divergence loss. This keeps the proxy efficient for downstream influence computation (no high-dimensional reconstruction needed) while stabilizing training and preventing collapse.

## Results

IPROX consistently outperforms off-the-shelf proxies across all four model families (Llama3.2-3B, Gemma3-4B, Qwen3-4B, Qwen2-7B) on BBH and TyDiQA, while remaining competitive on MMLU. On Qwen3-4B, a 1.5B IPROX proxy (ρ=0.7) achieves 64.18 average, surpassing the larger 1.7B off-the-shelf proxy (63.81). On Llama3.2-3B, IPROX at ρ=0.3 (2.5B) reaches 48.97 average versus 47.35 for the 1B off-the-shelf proxy, and reduces influence calculation time from ~90 minutes to ~38–44 minutes. Against the two algorithmic baselines (Layer Extraction and Influence Scorer), IPROX achieves average gains of +2.81 on Llama3.2-3B and +1.99 on Gemma3-4B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against off-the-shelf proxies (the primary comparison), IPROX wins on BBH and TyDiQA at every sparsity level across all four model families, while MMLU differences are marginal (±0.3). The most striking result is on Qwen3-4B, where the 1.5B IPROX proxy (64.18 avg) beats the larger 1.7B Qwen3-1.7B off-the-shelf proxy (63.81 avg). Against the two algorithmic baselines, IPROX (ρ=0.3) achieves 48.97 avg on Llama3.2-3B versus 46.16 for Layer Extraction and 45.70 for Influence Scorer (+2.81 over the stronger baseline), and 47.06 on Gemma3-4B versus 45.00 and 44.76 (+1.99). The gains are largest on TyDiQA (+5.80 and +5.28 respectively), while both baselines are slightly competitive or marginally ahead on MMLU. The authors note that both baselines are computationally cheaper but do not preserve gradient information, limiting their ability to identify task-relevant training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of gradient-based data selection (TracIn, Influence Functions) and LLM compression via low-rank factorization. It departs from prior proxy-based selection work (LESS, S2L) that relies on off-the-shelf smaller models with fixed sizes and no mechanism to align their gradient dynamics with the target. The key conceptual departure is deriving the proxy directly from the target model through an influence-preserving SVD (IPSVD) that reweights singular values by second-moment statistics, followed by a gradient-alignment and KL-anchoring stage borrowed from knowledge distillation. The theoretical grounding via Proposition 4.1 connects the compression objective to influence preservation, which prior SVD-based compression work (SVD-LLM, CALDERA, MoDeGPT) does not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IPROX makes gradient-based data selection practical for multi-billion-parameter LLMs by reducing influence computation cost by over 50% while maintaining or improving selection quality, which is a meaningful efficiency win for practitioners who cannot afford full-model TracIn or K-FAC runs. However, the method has inherent limits: embedding and LM head layers cannot be compressed, rank below ~10% of original size degrades quality sharply, and layer-wise approximation errors compound in deep networks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, how it interacts with parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA), and whether the alignment stage can be made more sample-efficient. The modest but consistent gains (1–3 points over off-the-shelf proxies) suggest the framework is sound but the headroom for further improvement in proxy quality remains.
