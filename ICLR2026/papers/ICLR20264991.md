## Problem

Fine-tuning diffusion models with RL-based methods requires KL regularization for stability, but marginal likelihoods are intractable for diffusion models, forcing practitioners to either ignore KL (causing training instability) or use trajectory KL (yielding subpar results and initial value function bias). The paper asks whether marginal KL regularization can be achieved without intractable likelihoods, and whether shaping intermediate denoising distributions—rather than only the final output—can improve fine-tuning effectiveness.

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the method takes a text prompt and Gaussian noise as input and produces a 256×256 image, fine-tuning Stable Diffusion v2 via LoRA adapters. P-GRAFT operates on a 'stitched' model: the fine-tuned model handles early denoising steps (from pure noise down to an intermediate timestep t), after which the reference model takes over for the remaining steps. For Inverse Noise Correction on flow models, a small 16M-parameter noise corrector first transforms standard Gaussian noise into a learned inverse noise distribution, which is then fed into the 65M-parameter pre-trained flow model to produce 256×256 images on CelebA-HQ and LSUN-Church.

## Method

The paper introduces GRAFT, a unified framework showing that rejection-sampling-based fine-tuning (RAFT, RSO, Best-of-N) implicitly solves a KL-regularized reward maximization problem with reshaped rewards, thereby providing marginal KL regularization without intractable likelihoods. Building on this, P-GRAFT extends the framework to intermediate timesteps: rewards computed on fully denoised samples are assigned to partially denoised states, and the model is fine-tuned only on the early denoising steps before switching to the reference model. A bias-variance tradeoff justifies this choice—later timesteps have exponentially simpler score functions (reducing bias) but noisier conditional rewards (increasing variance), with the optimal intermediate timestep empirically around 0.25N. For flow models, Inverse Noise Correction exploits ODE reversibility via backward Euler iteration to compute the 'inverse noise' distribution that, when fed to the pre-trained model, recovers the true data distribution; a small auxiliary flow model is then trained to generate this inverse noise from standard Gaussian, improving FID at reduced FLOPs without requiring any explicit reward signal.

## Results

On text-to-image generation with Stable Diffusion v2, P-GRAFT(0.25N) achieves the best VQAScore of 71.94 on GenAI-Bench, 76.12 on T2ICompBench++-Val, and 80.96 on GenEval — an 8.81% relative improvement over the base SDv2 (66.87 on GenAI-Bench). For unconditional image generation via Inverse Noise Correction, FID drops from 11.93 (1000-step base model) to 8.02 (200-step Noise Corrector + 200-step pre-trained model) on CelebA-HQ, while reducing FLOPs/image from 6.869×10¹² to 1.806×10¹². On molecule generation (QM9), P-GRAFT(0.25N) improves stability from 90.50% to 92.61% using only 1× the sampling rounds of GRAFT (which requires 9×).

## Baselines & Comparisons

P-GRAFT(0.25N) outperforms the base SDv2 model (Rombach et al., 2022) by 5.07 points on GenAI-Bench VQAScore (71.94 vs. 66.87), 6.92 points on T2ICompBench++-Val (76.12 vs. 69.20), and 7.47 points on GenEval (80.96 vs. 73.49). Against DDPO (Black et al., 2023), P-GRAFT(0.25N) wins decisively on all three benchmarks — notably, DDPO actually underperforms the unmodified SDv2 baseline (65.70 vs. 66.87 on GenAI-Bench), while P-GRAFT achieves a 6.24-point margin over DDPO. The paper also reports that DDPO failed to improve over SDv2 even with additional samples, FLOPs, KL regularization, and a per-prompt advantage estimator. GRAFT (the full-distribution variant) also beats both baselines but is consistently outperformed by P-GRAFT(0.25N), confirming the bias-variance tradeoff: shaping the distribution at an intermediate noise level yields better fine-tuning than shaping the final data distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies rejection sampling fine-tuning methods (RAFT, RSO, Reinforce-Rej) under a Generalized Rejection Sampling (GRAFT) framework and proves they implicitly solve KL-regularized reward maximization — a connection previously established only for autoregressive models. It then leverages the temporal structure of diffusion/flow models to extend this to intermediate timesteps (P-GRAFT), justified via a bias-variance tradeoff grounded in Bakry-Emery theory and Tweedie's formula. For flow models, it repurposes the ODE reversibility already used in image inversion (Renoise) and 2-rectification to propose Inverse Noise Correction, a reward-free adapter that corrects the initial noise distribution rather than the velocity field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GRAFT/P-GRAFT framework offers a principled, implementation-simple alternative to PPO/DDPO for diffusion fine-tuning that sidesteps the intractable marginal KL problem, and the bias-variance analysis gives practitioners a concrete knob (intermediate timestep) to tune. However, the main T2I experiments use SDv2 (not SDXL or newer models) with LoRA, and the inverse noise correction is validated only on 65M-parameter pixel-space flow models, leaving open whether these gains transfer to large-scale latent diffusion or video generation. The finding that DDPO underperforms the base model even with more compute is notable but may reflect implementation sensitivity rather than a fundamental limitation of policy gradients.
