## Problem

Existing LLM-based drug discovery methods are data-intensive, operate as black boxes without transparent reasoning, and optimize narrowly for binding affinity rather than comprehensive druggability. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing structured, biochemistry-informed reasoning trajectories and a multi-objective preference optimization that balances affinity with drug-likeness indicators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264810/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the primary de novo design task, the method takes only a 1D protein pocket amino acid sequence (no 3D coordinates) and produces a candidate molecule in canonical SMILES format preceded by a structured reasoning trajectory organized into five tagged dimensions (Characterization, Stability, Guidance, Conservation, Optimization). For molecule editing, it accepts a SMILES string plus a natural-language task description; for protein optimization, it accepts a protein sequence and a target property to improve. The reasoning output uses special XML-like tokens to delineate each analytical dimension, making the intermediate logic explicitly inspectable by domain experts.

## Method

DRUGTRAIL combines two modules: (1) Clinical Chemistry-Informed Reasoning (CCIR), which constructs a 2,000-sample Pocket-Reasoning-Ligand dataset via LLM-generated trajectories filtered through a three-stage quality pipeline (conflict resolution, thinking-budget length constraints, and adversarial domain-consistency checking against expert-authored 'golden' examples), followed by supervised fine-tuning to instill the structured reasoning format; and (2) Druggability-Tailored Preference Optimization (DTPO), which applies GRPO reinforcement learning with a composite reward that jointly scores ligand-based similarity (Tanimoto coefficients against PubChem-retrieved, Vina-screened bioactive references with exponential rank attenuation), rule-based drug-likeness (soft Lipinski RO5 via sigmoid decay on MW, LogP, HBD, HBA), and reasoning completeness (token-pair matching). The key design choice is replacing expensive per-step affinity scoring with an online-computable hybrid reward that couples structural similarity to known actives with intrinsic physicochemical constraints, enabling efficient RL without sacrificing the connection to practical pharmacological criteria.

## Results

On CrossDocked2020 (100 test complexes), DRUGTRAIL (Qwen3-8B + CCIR + DTPO) achieves a Vina Dock energy of −6.82 kcal/mol and 41.0% IMP (fraction of generated molecules outperforming references), far surpassing all LLM baselines (best: DeepSeek-R1 at −0.36, 0.2% IMP). On drug-likeness metrics, DRUGTRAIL reaches QED 0.57, SA 0.72, and LPSK 4.81, outperforming all 3D generative baselines (e.g., TargetDiff: QED 0.49, SA 0.60; Pocket2Mol: QED 0.39, SA 0.65). In zero-shot molecule editing (ZINC, 200 molecules), DRUGTRAIL improves hit ratios by 30–40 percentage points over base models (e.g., water solubility: 76.5% vs. 38.3%). For GFP protein optimization, fitness jumps from ~0.08–0.10 (base) to 0.57–0.62 (DRUGTRAIL) across medium and hard difficulty levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLM baselines (DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3-235B-A22B, QwQ-32B, DeepSeek-V3, Llama-3.1-8B), DRUGTRAIL achieves a decisive win on binding affinity: Vina Dock E of −6.82 vs. −0.36 (DeepSeek-R1) to +21.44 (Llama-3.1-8B), and IMP of 41% vs. 0–0.82% for all baselines. Against 3D structure-based drug design methods, DRUGTRAIL trades a small amount of binding affinity (Vina Score −6.82 vs. TargetDiff −7.41, Pocket2Mol −7.05) for substantially better drug-likeness: QED 0.57 vs. 0.39–0.49, SA 0.72 vs. 0.34–0.66, and LPSK 4.81 vs. 4.57–4.89, indicating more synthetically feasible and pharmacologically viable candidates. Against its own base models (Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B without CCIR or DTPO), the full pipeline improves QED from 0.12–0.17 to 0.55–0.57 and SA from 0.23–0.28 to 0.69–0.72, confirming that both the structured reasoning (CCIR) and druggability-tailored optimization (DTPO) contribute meaningfully. In zero-shot molecule editing and GFP protein optimization, DRUGTRAIL consistently outperforms base models by 30+ percentage points (editing) and 5× in fitness (protein), demonstrating transferability without task-specific retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRUGTRAIL builds directly on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeek-R1, repurposing it for biomolecular generation with a domain-specific multi-objective reward. It departs from the dominant LLM-for-drug-discovery paradigm (domain-specific pre-training à la NatureLM, BioGPT, or fine-tuning on millions of molecular samples) by showing that a small SFT corpus of 2,000 structured reasoning trajectories plus lightweight RL on a general-purpose LLM (Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B) suffices to activate latent pharmacological knowledge. Conceptually, it also moves beyond the single-metric binding-affinity optimization that characterizes most structure-based drug design methods (Pocket2Mol, TargetDiff, DiffSBDD), introducing a druggability-tailored preference signal that couples ligand similarity with rule-based drug-likeness indicators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that interpretable, reasoning-grounded molecule generation can match or exceed specialized 3D generative models on drug-likeness metrics (QED 0.57 vs. 0.39–0.49; SA 0.72 vs. 0.60–0.66) while providing human-readable justification for each design decision—a property critical for expert trust in high-stakes pharmaceutical workflows. The zero-shot transfer to molecule editing and protein optimization suggests the structured reasoning prior is task-agnostic, though these results are exploratory and not yet validated at scale. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes to targets with complex allosteric sites or multi-target scenarios, whether the 2,000-sample SFT corpus is sufficient for broader chemical space coverage, and whether the absence of explicit 3D geometric input fundamentally limits binding accuracy for sterically demanding pockets.
