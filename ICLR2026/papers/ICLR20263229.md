## Problem

Quantization and feature caching are individually effective acceleration techniques for diffusion models, but their joint deployment has been largely unexplored. The paper identifies that naively combining them causes severe quality degradation through two mechanisms: cache-induced redundancy in PTQ calibration datasets and amplified exposure bias from variance drift in the sampling distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263229/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is a training-free, plug-and-post-processing pipeline applied on top of any pre-trained diffusion transformer (DiT, LDM, FLUX.1, PixArt-Σ, Stable Diffusion, Open-Sora). It consumes a small calibration set of 800 intermediate feature samples (generated by the full-precision model across diffusion timesteps) plus a reference batch for variance estimation, and produces corrected quantization parameters and per-timestep variance scaling factors. It supports W8A8, W6A8, and W4A8 quantization with various cache intervals, evaluated at 256×256 and 512×512 image resolutions and on video/text-conditional tasks.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary, training-free techniques. First, Temporal-Aware Parallel Clustering (TAP) constructs a weighted similarity matrix combining spatial cosine similarity and temporal exponential-decay similarity, then applies parallel spectral clustering with random subsampling to reduce complexity from O(n³) to O(rn), selecting diverse and informative calibration samples that restore PTQ efficacy degraded by cache-induced redundancy. Second, Variance Compensation (VC) derives a closed-form channel-wise scaling factor K_t by analytically minimizing a combined rQNSR + MSE objective between the quantized-cached intermediate features and calibration references, correcting the per-timestep variance drift that drives exposure bias accumulation. Together, TAP and VC reduce FID by 8.24 and sFID by 6.34 over the naive quantization+cache baseline while achieving up to 12.7× end-to-end speedup.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with W8A8 quantization and 50 diffusion steps, Q&C† (PTQ4DiT + Learn-to-Cache + TAP + VC) achieves FID 5.43, sFID 19.52, IS 250.68, and Precision 0.7895 at 12.7× speedup, nearly matching the full-precision DDPM baseline (FID 5.22 at 5×). At 250 steps, Q&C† reaches FID 4.68 at 3.12× speedup, closely tracking the original DDPM (FID 4.53 at 1×). The ablation confirms that naively stacking PTQ4DiT with Learn-to-Cache degrades FID to 13.67, while adding TAP+VC recovers it to 5.43—a reduction of 8.24 FID points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At 50 steps on ImageNet 256×256, Q&C† (FID 5.43, 12.7×) substantially outperforms all quantization-only baselines: PTQ4DM (FID 17.52), Q-Diffusion (FID 14.61), PTQD (FID 15.21), and RepQ (FID 7.17), all at 10× speedup. Compared to the cache-only baseline Learn-to-Cache (FID 5.21, 6.3×), Q&C† trades a marginal 0.22 FID increase for a 2× speedup gain (12.7× vs 6.3×). Against the naive combination of PTQ4DiT + Learn-to-Cache (FID 13.67, 11.5×), TAP+VC reduces FID by 8.24 points while adding 1.2× speedup. At 250 steps, Q&C† (FID 4.68, 3.12×) slightly outperforms PTQ4DiT alone (FID 4.63, 2×) in speed while maintaining comparable quality, and beats FORA (FID 5.06, 2.06×) on both FID and speed. On video generation (Open-Sora, VBench), Q&C achieves the highest Motion score (98.62) and Aesthetic score (59.15) among all compared methods including QuantCache and Q-DiT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two independently developed acceleration lines for diffusion models: post-training quantization (PTQ4DiT, Q-Diffusion, PTQD, RepQ) and feature caching (DeepCache, Learn-to-Cache, FORA). While each mechanism has been extensively studied in isolation, the paper is the first to systematically analyze their joint deployment, identifying that the combination introduces two failure modes—calibration dataset redundancy and variance-driven exposure bias—that neither mechanism exhibits alone. The TAP component repurposes spectral clustering (Yan et al., 2009) with a temporal-aware parallel subsampling scheme to reduce complexity from O(n³) to O(rn), while the VC component adapts the rQNSR objective (Finkelstein et al., 2019) into a closed-form variance correction factor, distinguishing it from prior error-correction approaches that require additional neural networks (Wimbauer et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a 12.7× end-to-end speedup on DiT-XL/2 while recovering near-full-precision FID (5.43 vs. 5.22) makes real-time or near-real-time diffusion generation feasible on a single GPU. The finding that QAT does not resolve the quantization-cache inconsistency (QAT+DeepCache still yields FID 8.95) is important for practitioners who might assume higher-quality quantization eliminates the interaction problem. Open questions include whether the variance-compensation formulation generalizes to non-Gaussian feature distributions or to architectures beyond DiT (e.g., U-Net-based LDMs at scale), and whether the TAP clustering strategy remains effective when the cache interval N is very large, further concentrating the calibration distribution.
