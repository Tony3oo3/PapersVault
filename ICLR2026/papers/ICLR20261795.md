## Problem

Existing uncertainty estimation methods for LLMs either quantify query-level uncertainty (requiring intractable marginalization over the entire output space) or rely on response-level heuristics like log-probabilities that lack theoretical grounding. This gap is especially acute for long-form reasoning tasks, where models generate multi-step solutions without indicating when their reasoning paths are unreliable, hindering deployment in high-stakes applications that demand self-assessment.

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt and a specific generated response, TokUR applies low-rank Gaussian perturbations (rank r'=8, strength σ_q=0.1) to the query and key weight matrices of all attention layers, sampling M=2 perturbed weight variants per token. It outputs per-token uncertainty scores decomposed into Total, Aleatoric, and Epistemic components, which are then summed across all T tokens to yield a single response-level uncertainty scalar. Notably, the actual decoding uses the unperturbed mean weights W_0; perturbations are used exclusively for uncertainty quantification, keeping the generated text identical to standard inference.

## Method

TokUR performs a compact SVD on each attention layer's weight matrix and injects low-rank Gaussian noise (rank r' ≪ r) along the top r' left singular vectors, constructing a variational low-rank isotropic Gaussian posterior over the weights without any retraining. For each decoding step, the predictive distribution of the next token is estimated via Bayesian Model Averaging over M perturbed weight samples, and the total uncertainty is decomposed into aleatoric uncertainty (expected entropy over weights) and epistemic uncertainty (mutual information between the token and the weights, computed as TU minus AU). Token-level uncertainties are aggregated by summation into a response-level score, which the paper proves is an unbiased estimator of the ideal query-level uncertainty. The approach is entirely training-free and integrates seamlessly with vLLM, requiring only the SVD of attention weights at initialization and lightweight perturbation sampling at each decoding step.

## Results

On MATH500 (the most challenging math benchmark), TokUR (TU) achieves 80.64% AUROC and 56.79% AUPRC with Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, while TokUR (EU) reaches 82.86% AUROC and 81.35% AUPRC with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, establishing new state-of-the-art for incorrect reasoning path detection. In test-time scaling (Table 4), TokUR (EU) with Maj@N at N=16 improves accuracy from 26.42% (LL baseline) to 28.28% on MATH500 and from 47.10% to 50.38% on GSM8K with Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, yielding 3–4 point gains in the low-sample regime. Across all three math benchmarks and five non-math tasks (logical reasoning, code generation, truthfulness), TokUR consistently ranks as the best or second-best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TokUR outperforms all ten baselines across AUROC, AUPRC, and ACC* on MATH500 for both Llama models. Against the strongest internal-signal baselines, TokUR (TU) beats Self-Certainty (Kang et al., 2025) by ~35 AUROC points (80.64 vs. 45.09) and DeepConf (Fu et al., 2025) by ~36 points on Llama-3.2-1B MATH500; on the 8B model, TokUR (EU) surpasses Self-Certainty by ~12 AUROC points (82.86 vs. 71.21). Classic log-probability baselines—Log-Likelihood (Murray & Chiang, 2018) and Predictive Entropy (Malinin & Gales, 2021)—are substantially weaker, with LL achieving only 44.43% AUROC versus TokUR's 80.64% on the 1B model. External-signal methods (Semantic Entropy from Kuhn et al., 2023; SAR from Duan et al., 2024) require an auxiliary NLI/embedding model and, while competitive on the 8B model (SE reaches 89.24% AUROC), are not directly comparable to TokUR's training-free, internal-signal-only approach. In test-time scaling (Table 4), TokUR (EU) with Maj@N at N=16 improves MATH500 accuracy from 26.42% (LL) to 28.28% and GSM8K from 47.10% to 50.38%, outperforming both Self-Certainty and DeepConf by 3–8 points in the low-sample regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

TokUR extends the Bayesian LoRA line of work (BLoB, TFB) from short-form classification/QA to long-form reasoning by introducing a token-level uncertainty decomposition (Total = Aleatoric + Epistemic) that is compatible with autoregressive decoding via stepwise posterior sampling. It departs from prior query-level methods (which require intractable marginalization over the full output space) and from response-level heuristics like log-likelihood or P(True) (which lack theoretical grounding) by proving that the cumulative token-level uncertainty is an unbiased estimator of the ideal query-level uncertainty. The low-rank SVD-based perturbation mechanism is directly inherited from TFB's training-free Bayesianization, but TokUR is the first to apply it in a token-by-token fashion across an entire reasoning trace and to connect the resulting per-token signals to downstream reasoning-quality metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TokUR provides a training-free, theoretically grounded uncertainty signal that can be plugged into existing LLM inference pipelines (vLLM-compatible) for hallucination detection, best-of-N selection, and test-time scaling without external reward models or NLI verifiers. The finding that epistemic uncertainty (model parameter disagreement) is the strongest predictor of reasoning errors—rather than aleatoric uncertainty or raw log-probability—offers a principled lens for understanding where LLMs fail. Key open questions include the efficiency cost of repeated perturbation sampling at scale, whether token-level aggregation can capture multi-step logical inconsistencies, and the unresolved high-variance estimation problem that limits reliability in production settings.
