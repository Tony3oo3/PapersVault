## Problem

Video diffusion models trained solely on raw pixel data fail to internalize meaningful 3D geometric structure in their intermediate representations, leading to poor geometric coherence and long-term consistency in generated videos. The paper demonstrates via linear probing that features from a pretrained video diffusion model cannot reconstruct meaningful depth or 3D geometry, revealing a fundamental gap between 2D pixel-space learning and the underlying 3D nature of the physical world.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264045/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For camera-conditioned generation, the model takes a single first frame (256×256) plus per-frame camera poses as input and autoregressively generates subsequent video frames; for action-conditioned generation, it takes context frames plus discrete actions (384×224, 32-frame sequences). During training, 16 frames are fed into VGGT at 512×512 resolution to extract geometric alignment targets. A notable output is that the aligned intermediate features can be passed through VGGT's depth head to reconstruct explicit 3D geometry (depth maps, point maps) at inference time, enabling unified video-and-4D generation without additional architectural changes.

## Method

Geometry Forcing aligns intermediate features of an autoregressive video diffusion model (U-ViT backbone with Flow Matching) with geometric representations from the VGGT 3D foundation model via two complementary objectives: Angular Alignment maximizes cosine similarity between a Conv3D-projected diffusion latent and VGGT features, enforcing directional consistency; Scale Alignment predicts the full VGGT feature from the L2-normalized projected latent via a separate lightweight head, preserving magnitude information while avoiding the instability of direct MSE. The total loss is L_FM + 0.5·L_Angular + 0.05·L_Scale, applied at the mid-level (layer 3 of 7) transformer block. The key design choice is internal representation alignment rather than explicit geometry conditioning (e.g., ControlNet with rendered depth), which the paper shows yields stronger geometric supervision. The method introduces zero inference overhead since alignment is purely a training-time regularizer, and it is compatible with any feed-forward 3D foundation model supporting multi-image input.

## Results

On RealEstate10K 256-frame camera-conditioned generation, Geometry Forcing (GF) reduces FVD from 364 (DFoT baseline) to 243, with GF+REPA further reaching 237. GF also achieves the best LPIPS (0.51), SSIM (0.38), PSNR (11.87), RPE (0.3337), and RVE (272) among all baselines. On 16-frame generation, GF attains FVD 193 and LPIPS 0.32, outperforming all competitors. On Minecraft action-conditioned generation, GF reduces FVD from 216 (NFD) to 205, and on text-to-video (Wan2.1), GF improves aesthetic quality from 0.58 to 0.59 and imaging quality from 0.56 to 0.59.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GF outperforms all six baselines across every reported metric. Against DFoT (Song et al., 2025), the primary autoregressive video diffusion baseline, GF reduces 256-frame FVD by 33% (364→243) and improves RPE from 0.3575 to 0.3337. Against REPA (Yu et al., 2024a), which aligns with semantic DINOv2 features, GF achieves lower FVD (243 vs. 297) and lower RVE (272 vs. 315), confirming geometric alignment is more effective than semantic alignment for 3D consistency. Against VideoREPA (Zhang et al., 2025c), GF substantially wins on FVD (243 vs. 455) and RPE (0.3337 vs. 0.3823), though VideoREPA achieves a lower RVE (190 vs. 272), suggesting it handles revisit consistency differently. Against Cosmos (Agarwal et al., 2025), a large-scale world foundation model, GF achieves dramatically lower FVD (243 vs. 934) and better SSIM (0.38 vs. 0.20) on 256-frame generation. On Minecraft, GF improves NFD (Cheng et al., 2025) FVD from 216 to 205, demonstrating cross-domain generalization. On text-to-video, GF applied to Wan2.1 (Wan et al., 2025) yields consistent gains in aesthetic quality (0.58→0.59), imaging quality (0.56→0.59), and motion smoothness (0.98→0.99).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the REPA (Representation Alignment) paradigm from image diffusion to video diffusion, but repurposes the alignment target from semantic features (DINOv2) to geometric features from a 3D foundation model (VGGT). The base autoregressive video diffusion framework is Diffusion Forcing Transformer (Song et al., 2025), and the geometric teacher is VGGT (Wang et al., 2025b). The key methodological departure from REPA is the decoupled Angular + Scale alignment formulation, which replaces a single cosine-similarity loss with two complementary objectives to handle the scale mismatch between diffusion latents and geometric features. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as orthogonal to prior 3D-consistency approaches (e.g., joint RGB+point-map modeling, ControlNet-based explicit geometry conditioning) by internalizing geometry into the representation rather than injecting it as an external signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The linear-probing observation that video diffusion models trained on raw video do not implicitly encode meaningful 3D geometry is a useful diagnostic for the field, and the proposed fix (a lightweight alignment loss with no inference overhead) is a practical, drop-in training trick likely to be adopted by video generation pipelines. However, the evaluation is conducted at a small scale (10K clips, 256×256, 2,500 steps), and VGGT itself is trained primarily on static scenes, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to large-scale dynamic video or high-resolution generation. The authors identify scaling to larger models/datasets and using reconstructed 3D geometry as structured memory for long-horizon world modeling as the most promising next steps.
