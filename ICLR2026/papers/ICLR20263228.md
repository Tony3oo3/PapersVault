## Problem

Despite strong performance on general-domain vision-language tasks, medical MLLMs consistently underperform in zero-shot medical image interpretation, yet the underlying cause remains unclear. No prior work has systematically examined whether failures stem from inadequate semantic grounding (lacking medical knowledge) or inadequate visual grounding (failing to localize clinically relevant image regions). This paper is the first to rigorously validate that visual grounding deficiency is a pervasive and fundamental failure mode specific to medical image analysis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263228/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on medical images across 8 imaging modalities (CT, MRI, X-ray, OCT, dermoscopy, microscopy, fundus, ultrasound) paired with text questions in a zero-shot VQA setting. Internally, it extracts cross-attention weights from the last input text token to N² image patch tokens across all LLM layers and heads, reshaping them into spatial attention maps of size N×N. The VGMED evaluation dataset provides image-bbox-question triplets (~28K samples) where each question is explicitly grounded in an annotated bounding box, enabling fine-grained alignment measurement between attention distributions and ground-truth regions.

## Method

VGRefine is a two-step, training-free inference-time intervention. Step I (Attention Triage) identifies the top K attention heads with the lowest KL divergence to ground-truth regions on natural COCO images (avoiding medical data leakage), aggregates their attention maps, suppresses low-activation regions via a percentile threshold p, and converts the result into a binary mask M ∈ {0,1}^{N²}. Step II (Attention Knockout) applies this mask via element-wise multiplication to the cross-attention weights α_q^{ℓ,h} between question tokens and visual tokens at the layer most relevant to visual grounding (ℓ=16 for the 7B model), explicitly preventing question tokens from receiving information from clinically irrelevant visual regions. The approach requires no retraining, no external expert models, and no new medical knowledge, yet achieves SOTA performance across 6 Med-VQA benchmarks (110K+ samples) by simply redirecting the model's existing attention toward relevant regions.

## Results

VGRefine applied to HuatuoGPT-V-7B achieves 68.4% average closed-ended accuracy across four medical VQA benchmarks (VQA-RAD, SLAKE, PathVQA, PMC-VQA), improving over the base model's 65.3% by +3.1 points, with the largest gains on PathVQA (+11.3%) and VQA-RAD (+5.6%). On the MMMU Health & Medicine benchmark, VGRefine reaches 47.2% average accuracy (vs. 45.8% baseline), and on OmniMedVQA it achieves 74.4% average across 8 imaging modalities (vs. 71.3%), with notable improvements on CT (+7.5%), X-Ray (+8.1%), and MRI (+6.4%). All evaluations are conducted in a zero-shot setting without additional training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VGRefine outperforms all 12 compared baselines on the primary medical VQA benchmarks. Against the strongest medical MLLM baseline LLaVA-Tri (59.8/43.4/59.0 on VQA-RAD/SLAKE/PathVQA), VGRefine leads by +11.4, +33.5, and +8.6 points respectively. Among general-domain MLLMs, Qwen-VL-Chat (48.9% avg) and LLaVA-v1.6-7B (48.5% avg) trail VGRefine by roughly 20 points, confirming that domain-specific grounding matters. The base model HuatuoGPT-V-7B (65.3% avg) is improved by +3.1 points, with the largest single-benchmark gain on PathVQA (+6.9 points). In the attention-method comparison (Table F.4), VGRefine (68.4% avg) beats AdaptVis (66.7%), ViCrop (65.5%), and PAI (33.3%), demonstrating that its two-step attention triage-and-knockout strategy is more effective than prior training-free attention manipulation approaches. On OmniMedVQA (Table 3), VGRefine (74.4% avg) surpasses MedPLIB (60.6%) and VILA-M3-8B (53.0%) by 13.8 and 21.4 points respectively, and on MMMU Health & Medicine (Table 2) it leads all baselines including HuatuoGPT-V-7B (45.8%) by 1.4 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual grounding analysis framework established by Zhang et al. (2025a) for general-domain MLLMs to the medical imaging domain, where no prior systematic study existed. While the medical MLLM literature (LLaVA-Med, HuatuoGPT-V, VILA-M3, LLaVA-Tri) has focused on training data scale and domain-specific fine-tuning to improve VQA accuracy, none have diagnosed *why* these models underperform in zero-shot settings. The paper also positions VGRefine within the broader line of inference-time attention manipulation methods (PAI, AdaptVis, ViCrop) but departs from them by using a clinically-guided head-selection procedure and a binary knockout mask rather than continuous attention reweighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By isolating visual grounding as a distinct and pervasive failure mode in medical MLLMs—separate from semantic grounding—the paper provides a diagnostic tool (VGMED + AR/KL/JS metrics) that the field can use to triage model weaknesses before investing in more medical knowledge. The finding that a training-free, two-step attention refinement yields consistent gains across 8 imaging modalities and 6 benchmarks suggests that much of the current performance gap may be addressable without additional data or retraining. Open questions include whether the grounding deficit persists in 3D volumetric imaging, whether it interacts with semantic grounding failures in complex multi-step clinical reasoning, and whether the attention-knockout approach generalizes to generative or segmentation tasks beyond VQA.
