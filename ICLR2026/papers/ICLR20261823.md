## Problem

Existing multimodal VAEs that separate shared and private (modality-specific) latent variables rely on capacity-based or shortcut-preventing heuristics, which fail to achieve clean disentanglement when the likelihood model is insufficient (e.g., when shared information occupies a small fraction of the input). Without a clean separation, shared information leaks into private encodings and vice versa, causing weak cross-modal coherence, wasted model capacity, and poor generation quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes M aligned modalities as input (e.g., 5 background-styled 64×64 digit images for PolyMNIST-Quadrant, a 256×256 RGB bird image plus 10 text captions for CUB-HQ, or 5 omics views of 100 dimensions for TCGA). For CUB-HQ, images are preprocessed through a pretrained VAE encoder into 4×32×32 tensors before entering the model. The output is a factorized latent space per modality consisting of a shared code z and a private code w_m, from which the model can independently vary either variable to produce controllable cross-modal generations (e.g., text-to-image, image-to-image) at the original resolution via a pretrained decoder or a DiT denoiser.

## Method

IDMVAE builds on the MMVAE+ ELBO and adds two mutual-information-based regularizations plus diffusion priors. First, a cross-view contrastive MI loss (L_CrossMI) maximizes I(z_m; z_n) across modalities to explicitly extract shared factors that pure likelihood modeling may miss. Second, a generative augmentation loss (L_GenAug) implements cycle-consistency by mixing z from one sample with w_m from another to synthesize a new sample, then enforcing that re-encoding this sample recovers the correct z and w_m posteriors—removing redundancy without requiring domain-specific augmentations. Third, the latent prior p(z) is parameterized as a DDPM diffusion process (decomposed via the KL split in Eq. 3) and jointly trained end-to-end with the encoders and decoders, providing a flexible, structured prior that captures clustering in the latent space. The three components are complementary: CrossMI ensures z captures shared information, GenAug strips private information from z and shared information from w_m, and the diffusion prior improves unconditional generation by matching the structured posterior geometry.

## Results

On PolyMNIST-Quadrant, IDMVAE achieves 98.3% digit accuracy from the shared latent z (vs. 38.2% for MMVAE+) while keeping quadrant leakage in z at 27.1% (near the 25% random baseline), and 99.9% quadrant accuracy from the private latent w_m. On CUB-HQ, z→Category classification reaches 76.4% (vs. 63.3% for MMVAE+ and 67.2% for DisentangledSSL). On TCGA, combining z and w yields 71.8% prediction accuracy (vs. 69.0% for MMVAE+), and adding a diffusion prior further improves unconditional generative coherence from 7.0% to 66.4% on PolyMNIST-Quadrant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDMVAE substantially outperforms all baselines on the primary disentanglement metric (z→Digit) across all three datasets: 98.3% vs. 49.2% (MMVAE), 53.6% (MoPoE-VAE), 15.7% (DMVAE), 38.2% (MMVAE+), and 26.3% (SBM-VAE) on PolyMNIST-Quadrant. On CUB-HQ, IDMVAE achieves 76.4% z→Category, beating DisentangledSSL (67.2%), MMVAE+ (63.3%), and MMVAE (60.2%), while also reducing direction leakage in z to 59.2% (vs. 78.7% for MMVAE+). On TCGA, IDMVAE's combined z+w prediction (71.8%) exceeds MMVAE+ (69.0%) and DisentangledSSL (69.0%), with both individual latents being predictive (70.7% and 70.8%). In generative coherence (Table 2), IDMVAE achieves 89.8% self-generation digit accuracy and 88.1% cross-generation accuracy, compared to 12.0% and 11.9% for MMVAE+, and the diffusion prior variant reaches 66.4% unconditional coherence versus 0.0% for all baselines. The only baseline that partially matches IDMVAE on a single metric is MMVAE+ on w→Quad (both 99.9%), but MMVAE+ fails to extract shared information into z, confirming that its shortcut-preventing mechanism alone is insufficient without explicit MI-based regularization.

## Relation to Prior Work

IDMVAE builds directly on MMVAE+ (Palumbo et al., 2023), which introduced the shared/private latent factorization with an auxiliary-prior trick to prevent shortcut learning, but departs from it by replacing capacity-based and heuristic disentanglement with explicit mutual-information regularizations. The cross-view MI maximization draws on the multi-view information bottleneck framework of Federici et al. (2020) and contrastive MI estimation (Oord et al., 2018), while the generative augmentation loss is inspired by cycle-consistency (Zhu et al., 2017) and the augmentation-based disentanglement of Bai et al. (2021), but eliminates the need for domain-specific augmentation rules by using the model's own decoder to synthesize augmented views. The diffusion-based latent prior is jointly trained with the VAE objective, contrasting with two-step approaches (Palumbo et al., 2024; Wesego & Rooshenas, 2024) that first learn VAE representations and then apply diffusion in a separate stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that pure likelihood modeling in multimodal VAEs is fundamentally insufficient for extracting shared variables when the shared signal occupies a small fraction of the data (e.g., digits in PolyMNIST-Quadrant), and that MI-based regularization plus generative augmentation closes this gap without requiring domain-specific augmentations. The joint training of diffusion priors with the VAE objective (rather than post-hoc) is a practical recipe that substantially improves unconditional generation coherence, which prior multimodal VAEs largely failed at. Open questions include extending the framework to missing modalities, to settings where information is shared only locally among views rather than globally, and to higher-dimensional modalities where the current ResNet-based decoders produce blurry outputs requiring a separate DiT denoiser.
