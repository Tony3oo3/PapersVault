## Problem

Riemannian metrics on SPD manifolds are essential for building SPD neural networks, but existing options face a trade-off: eigendecomposition-based metrics (AIM, LEM, BWM) are computationally expensive, while the Cholesky-based Log-Cholesky Metric (LCM), though efficient, relies on diagonal logarithm and exponentiation that cause numerical instability for small eigenvalues. The underlying product structure of the Cholesky manifold's diagonal log metric had not been recognized, leaving the design space for new Cholesky-based metrics unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes SPD covariance matrices of varying sizes (20×20 for radar, 93×93 for HDM05 skeleton sequences, 63×63 for FPHA hand gestures) and produces class labels through Riemannian MLR classifiers or residual blocks. All Riemannian operators—geodesics, log/exp maps, parallel transport, weighted Fréchet mean, and gyro operations—reduce to closed-form expressions involving only Cholesky decomposition and element-wise diagonal power operations, avoiding matrix logarithms, exponentials, or eigendecompositions entirely. A tunable deformation parameter θ continuously interpolates between LCM (θ→0) and the proposed metrics (θ=1), allowing adaptation to datasets with different Cholesky diagonal statistics.

## Method

The key insight is that the diagonal log metric on the Cholesky manifold decomposes as a product: a standard Euclidean metric on the strictly lower triangular part crossed with n copies of a 1D Riemannian metric on R_{++} for the diagonal entries. By substituting the 1D power metric (θ-EM) or Bures-Wasserstein metric (GBWM) for the diagonal component, the authors derive two new Cholesky metrics—θ-DPM and M-DBWM—which pull back via the Cholesky diffeomorphism to two SPD metrics: Power-Cholesky Metric (θ-PCM) and Bures-Wasserstein-Cholesky Metric ((θ,M)-BWCM). All Riemannian and gyro operators under these metrics admit closed-form expressions built from diagonal powers rather than diagonal log/exp, yielding superior numerical stability (stable down to ε=1e⁻³⁰ vs. LCM's failure at ε=1e⁻³ for 3×3 matrices). A diagonal power deformation parameter θ provides a continuous family connecting LCM to the new metrics, and the full gyrovector space structure is established, enabling direct construction of SPD MLR classifiers and Riemannian residual blocks with per-sample complexity O((1+C)/3 · n³) flops.

## Results

On the GyroSPD backbone, θ-PCM achieves 97.04% accuracy on Radar and 71.93% on HDM05, while θ-BWCM reaches 72.74% on HDM05 and 91.00% on FPHA, consistently outperforming LCM (96.29%, 68.37%, 89.83%) and AIM (96.80%, 66.05%, 85.77%). On the SPDNet backbone with 3-block HDM05, θ-BWCM achieves 67.40% accuracy versus 62.33% for LCM and 61.14% for AIM. Both proposed metrics match or exceed LCM's training speed (e.g., 1.51s vs. 1.66s per epoch on HDM05) while being substantially faster than AIM (21.65s) and LEM (2.02s). In numerical stability tests, the diagonal log metric (LCM) fails at 51% for ε=1e-3 on 3×3 matrices, whereas θ-DPM and θ-DBWM remain stable down to ε=1e-30.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AIM (Pennec et al., 2006), both θ-PCM and θ-BWCM achieve higher accuracy on all three datasets (e.g., 72.74% vs. 66.05% on HDM05) while being 10–14× faster in training time (1.58s vs. 21.65s per epoch on HDM05). Against LEM (Arsigny et al., 2005), the proposed metrics win on accuracy across all datasets and are comparable or faster in runtime. Against LCM (Lin, 2019), the closest competitor sharing the Cholesky product structure, θ-PCM and θ-BWCM consistently outperform by 2–5% on HDM05 (71.93%/72.74% vs. 68.37%) and by ~1.3% on FPHA (91.17%/91.00% vs. 89.83%), while matching or slightly exceeding LCM's speed. Against BWM (Bhatia et al., 2019) and PEM (Dryden et al., 2010), the comparison is primarily in tensor interpolation: θ-PCM mitigates the swelling effect that plagues both BWM and PEM, producing interpolation patterns visually similar to LCM without the numerical instability. The paper also shows that in high-dimensional scalability tests (n=512), PCM and BWCM are the fastest metrics, while AIM and BWM become prohibitively slow due to their reliance on eigendecomposition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Lin (2019), who introduced the Log-Cholesky Metric (LCM) by pulling back a Riemannian metric from the Cholesky manifold to the SPD manifold. The key conceptual advance is making explicit the product structure of the diagonal log metric on the Cholesky manifold — a Euclidean metric on the strictly lower triangular part combined with n copies of a 1D Riemannian metric on R_{++} for the diagonal — which was implicit in LCM but never formally recognized as a design principle. By treating this product structure as a template, the authors substitute the diagonal log with diagonal power (yielding PCM) and diagonal Bures-Wasserstein (yielding BWCM) metrics, then pull back via Cholesky decomposition. The application layer (Riemannian MLR classifiers, residual blocks) follows the frameworks of Chen et al. (2024c) and Katsman et al. (2024), with the diagonal power deformation echoing the deformed-metric framework of Thanwerdas and Pennec (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The product-structure insight opens a principled design space: any Riemannian metric on R_{++} can be lifted to a Cholesky metric and then to an SPD metric, potentially yielding many more fast, stable metrics beyond the two proposed here. The practical impact is most visible in numerical stability — PCM and BWCM remain stable down to ε=1e-30 where LCM fails at ε=1e-3 — which matters for real-world SPD data with near-singular covariance matrices. However, accuracy gains over LCM are modest (1–3% on HDM05, negligible on Radar/FPHA), and the optimal deformation parameter θ is dataset-dependent, limiting plug-and-play utility. Open questions include whether the product-structure framework can be extended to non-diagonal Cholesky components, whether the stability advantage persists under adversarial or out-of-distribution inputs, and whether the gyrovector-space structure enables new architectures beyond the MLR and residual blocks demonstrated here.
