## Problem

Existing graph-LLM bridges (e.g., Q-Former-style connectors) compress variable-sized molecular graphs into a fixed number of query tokens, collapsing stereochemistry and substructural context—especially for larger molecules—and typically require costly joint fine-tuning of the LLM backbone, limiting efficiency and generalization. The paper asks whether a connector-only, frozen-backbone alignment can preserve substructure fidelity while matching or exceeding the accuracy of full fine-tuning approaches.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 2D molecular graph (encoded by a frozen graph encoder into node embeddings), 3D conformer geometry, a SMILES atom sequence (used by a lightweight Next-Atom Predictor to compute per-position surprisal for segmentation), and a text instruction prompt. The number of dynamic query tokens is not fixed but adapts to molecular complexity: entropy peaks along the SMILES determine how many substructure segments (and thus tokens) are produced. The frozen LLM then generates outputs including binary/continuous property predictions, free-text molecular descriptions, retrosynthesis and forward reaction SMILES, reagent predictions, and multiple-choice answers on MoleculeQA.

## Method

EDT-Former introduces two coupled components. First, Entropy-Guided Patching uses a 0.54M-parameter Next-Atom Predictor (NAP) to compute surprisal e_t = -log p(a_{t+1}|a_{1:t}) along the SMILES sequence, detects local maxima refined by non-maximum suppression and a prominence threshold, and segments the molecule at those peaks; each segment's graph nodes are average-pooled from frozen encoder embeddings to yield a variable number of dynamic substructure tokens. Second, the Dynamic Query Transformer concatenates these dynamic tokens with 16 learnable modality anchors into a query bank, refines them through L layers of self-attention (mixing global anchor context with local dynamic content) and cross-attention (retrieving node-level evidence from the frozen graph encoder), then projects the enriched queries into the LLM embedding space via a linear layer. Training is two-stage: the bridge is pretrained with cross-modal contrastive, modality-matching, and masked substructure reconstruction losses, then alignment-tuned against instruction prompts while both the graph encoders and the LLM backbone remain frozen—only the bridge parameters (~84M) are updated. Theoretical analysis (Appendix A) proves that surprisal peaks concentrate at true change points under piecewise-stationarity and calibration assumptions, and that peak-based cutting minimizes an entropy upper bound on within-segment pooling loss under a token budget.

## Results

On 10 zero-shot molecular property prediction tasks (TDC/MoleculeNet), EDT-Former (8.3B) achieves the best average accuracy, with standout results of 82.34% on PAMPA, 72.48% on BBBP, and 82.15% on HIA, exceeding the strongest baselines by >20% relative gain on several tasks (e.g., CLINTOX 56.55% vs. 27.96% for Mol-LLaMA-2). On MoleculeQA (SFT), EDT-Former reaches 68.34% total accuracy, outperforming Mol-Llama3.1 (66.21%) and GPT-5 10-shot (56.12%). On Mol-Instructions, EDT-Former achieves the best property prediction MAE (0.0062) and competitive description generation (BLEU-2 0.424, ROUGE-L 0.717), while leading on retrosynthesis (Exact 0.387, BLEU 0.930) and forward reaction prediction (BLEU 0.964, RDK FTS 0.776).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDT-Former outperforms all molecular LLM baselines (Mol-LLaMA-2/3.1, Mol-Ins.-Llama2/3.1, 3D-MoLM, LLaMo, MolReasoner, HIGHT) across property prediction, MoleculeQA, and Mol-Instructions tasks, with the largest margins on CLINTOX (56.55% vs. 27.96% for Mol-LLaMA-2, a ~102% relative gain) and HIV (46.56% vs. 26.16% for 3D-MoLM). Against general LLMs, EDT-Former SFT (68.34% total on MoleculeQA) surpasses GPT-5 10-shot (56.12%), GPT-4 10-shot (53.47%), and GPT-3.5 10-shot (29.29%), while its 10-shot variant (58.78%) also exceeds GPT-5. On Mol-Instructions retrosynthesis, EDT-Former leads HIGHT (Exact 0.387 vs. 0.008; BLEU 0.930 vs. 0.863) and Mol-LLaMA-3.1 (Exact 0.387 vs. 0.340); on forward reaction prediction it achieves the best BLEU (0.964) and RDK FTS (0.776) over all baselines. The only notable loss is on DILI (50.27% vs. GPT-4o's 53.47%), where the zero-shot general LLM retains a small edge. EDT-Former also demonstrates backbone-agnostic gains: attaching the connector to Mistral-8B, Qwen3-8B, Llama2-7B, and Llama3.1-8B yields +38% to +102% average accuracy improvements over text-only baselines, confirming the connector's transferability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the BLIP-2/Q-Former paradigm—where a lightweight adapter with fixed-length learnable queries bridges frozen encoders to a frozen LLM—from vision to molecular graphs. Prior molecular systems (Mol-LLM, Mol-LLaMA, 3D-MolT5, UniMolT) all inherit the fixed-query bottleneck, compressing variable-sized molecules into a constant number of tokens and losing stereochemistry and substructure. EDT-Former departs by replacing fixed queries with entropy-guided dynamic tokens whose count and placement adapt to molecular complexity, drawing conceptual inspiration from BLT's entropy-driven byte patching but targeting graph substructures rather than text. The frozen-backbone training protocol is inherited directly from BLIP-2's efficiency rationale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated failure mode (structural information loss from fixed-length compression) with a clean, interpretable mechanism (entropy peaks as change-point detectors), and demonstrates that the resulting connector-only training is ~3.5× faster and uses half the GPU memory of LoRA fine-tuning—making multimodal molecular LLMs substantially more accessible. Open questions include whether SMILES-order entropy peaks reliably capture chemically meaningful boundaries for very large or highly branched molecules, whether the approach generalizes to non-molecular graph domains, and whether the small NAP model (0.54M params) is sufficient for all structural regimes. The 10-shot result beating GPT-5 on MoleculeQA is striking but confounded by modality differences (graph+3D vs. text-only), so the true generalization gap to frontier LLMs remains unclear.
