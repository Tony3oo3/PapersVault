## Problem

Diffusion large language models (dLLMs) based on masked diffusion require up to 256 denoising steps to match GPT-2 quality, creating a severe inference bottleneck. Existing distillation methods for dLLMs (SDTT, DUO, DiMO) rely on heuristic trajectory matching or proxy gradients, lack rigorous theoretical grounding, and still fail to match GPT-2 within 32 NFEs. This paper addresses the gap by providing a principled, theoretically-derived distillation framework for masked diffusion language models that achieves state-of-the-art few-step generation.

## Input / Output

The student model takes a fully masked token sequence (all positions set to [MASK]) as input and produces a clean text sequence of length 1024 through iterative unmasking in as few as 8 NFEs (vs. 1024 for the teacher). At inference, the Reward-Guided Ancestral Sampler (RGAS) modulates its strategy across denoising steps: early steps use gradient tilting (h>0, M=1) to steer global structure, while late steps switch to multi-candidate re-ranking (h=0, M>1) for local refinement. For protein generation, the same framework takes fully masked amino acid sequences and outputs variable-length protein sequences evaluated by pLDDT structural confidence scores.

## Method

DiDi-Instruct reformulates the Integral KL (IKL) divergence minimization between student and teacher forward marginals as a policy gradient problem via a Score-Function Identity (Theorem 3.1), yielding a tractable update rule that differentiates only through the student's initial generation log-probability rather than through non-differentiable discrete sampling paths. The intractable log-density ratio reward is estimated by an auxiliary discriminator trained with binary cross-entropy to distinguish corrupted student samples from corrupted teacher samples, whose optimal output directly encodes the density ratio. Training stability is ensured through grouped reward normalization (GRPO-style standardization within mini-batches) and a two-step score decomposition that exposes the student to intermediate corruption levels, preventing entropy collapse in multi-step regimes. At inference, the Reward-Guided Ancestral Sampler (RGAS) augments the student's unmasking logits with the discriminator's reward gradient in early steps and performs softmax re-ranking over M candidates in late steps, reducing perplexity by 30% over unguided ancestral sampling.

## Results

On OpenWebText, DiDi-Instruct achieves generative PPL of 62.2 at 8 NFEs, 38.2 at 16 NFEs, 25.0 at 32 NFEs, 21.9 at 64 NFEs, and 18.4 at 128 NFEs, consistently outperforming all baselines across the full NFE range. At 16 NFEs the student already surpasses the 1024-step teacher (PPL 38.53), and at 1024 NFEs DiDi-Instruct reaches PPL 15.62, a >24% reduction over the strongest baseline. Generative entropy remains within ~1% of the teacher (5.15–5.21 vs. 5.22), confirming negligible diversity loss. Inference throughput reaches 2366 tokens/sec on a single H100, a 13.2× speedup over a same-size AR model at matched perplexity, while distillation training completes in ~1 H100 GPU-hour versus 20+ hours for multi-round baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiDi-Instruct outperforms SDTT (Deschenaux & Gulcehre, 2025) across all NFE budgets on OpenWebText PPL; the paper notes that even the most recent SDTT cannot match the GPT-2 baseline within 32 NFEs, whereas DiDi-Instruct already beats GPT-2 at 16 NFEs. Against DUO (Sahoo et al., 2025), DiDi-Instruct achieves lower PPL and superior distribution matching (higher MAUVE) with reduced mode collapse (lower Self-BLEU), and consistently improves upon DUO in zero-shot likelihood on seven out-of-domain corpora and in downstream fine-tuning (MMLU, PubMed, GLUE MRPC). Compared to the GPT-2 autoregressive baseline, DiDi-Instruct achieves comparable or better PPL at a fraction of the compute (16–128 NFEs vs. 1024 sequential token predictions), yielding a 13.2× throughput speedup at matched quality. For protein sequence generation, the DiDi-Instruct student distilled from DPLM (Wang et al., 2024) consistently achieves higher pLDDT scores than the DPLM teacher across 8–512 NFEs, surpassing the pLDDT > 70 confidence threshold with as few as 8–32 NFEs while maintaining competitive sequence diversity (MMseqs2 cluster entropy).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Integral KL (IKL) divergence distillation framework from Diff-Instruct (Luo et al., 2023b), originally developed for continuous-space diffusion models, to the discrete masked diffusion language model (MDM) setting. It departs from prior dLLM distillation methods—SDTT (trajectory matching), DUO (consistency distillation via USDM duality), and DiMO (proxy-gradient token-level matching)—by reformulating the IKL objective as a policy gradient problem with an adversarial discriminator providing the log-density-ratio reward, thereby avoiding non-differentiable discrete sampling paths. The score-function identity (Theorem 3.1) and the GAN-inspired density-ratio estimator (Lemma 3.2) are the key conceptual bridges between the continuous Diff-Instruct lineage and the discrete MDM domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiDi-Instruct establishes the first principled distribution-matching distillation recipe for masked diffusion language models, achieving 64× inference acceleration with negligible entropy loss and 20× faster distillation training compared to competing multi-round methods. The extension to protein sequence generation (DPLM) suggests the framework generalizes beyond natural language to other discrete sequence domains. Key open questions include scaling to billion-parameter models (memory cost of jointly maintaining teacher, student, and discriminator), whether the adversarial reward signal remains stable at larger scales, and whether the framework can be adapted to conditional or instruction-following generation settings.
