## Problem

Existing PBR texture generation methods face two fundamental gaps: (1) two-stage approaches rely on imperfect shaded intermediates that accumulate errors, while joint multi-channel generation ignores the domain gap between albedo (intrinsic color) and metallic-roughness (illumination-dependent properties), and (2) UV-space inpainting suffers from topological ambiguities that produce seams and discontinuities. Additionally, open-source 3D datasets are severely imbalanced, with high-quality metallic and roughness maps far scarcer than shaded or albedo images.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D mesh (UV-parameterized via Xatlas) and a single reference image (real-captured or AI-generated) as input, and produces a complete UV texture set at 2048×2048 resolution in GLB format containing albedo, metallic, and roughness maps. Per-view generation operates at 512×512 or 768×768 resolution, with 6 views for PBR generation and up to 18 additional views synthesized for inpainting. The final material maps are projected from multi-view space to UV space using angle-weighted averaging across overlapping texels to ensure view consistency.

## Method

LumiTex is an end-to-end multi-branch diffusion framework initialized from FLUX.1-dev with flow matching loss. A multi-modal DiT (19 double-stream blocks) fuses per-view geometry tokens (VAE-encoded normals and canonical coordinates), reference image tokens (VAE + DINOv2 features), and learnable material embeddings, followed by a multi-view DiT (38 single-stream blocks) with 3D RoPE to enforce cross-view consistency. The key architectural insight is a multi-view illumination context branch that reconstructs shaded images and produces view-aware shaded keys/values via cross-view attention; these are then consumed by separate albedo and MR branches through a lighting-aware material attention mechanism (cross-attention from material queries to shaded keys/values), enabling disentangled material reasoning conditioned on shared illumination priors. For texture completion, a geometry-guided LVSM (24-layer decoder-only transformer) synthesizes dense novel views from sparse inputs using Plücker ray maps and geometry conditions, avoiding UV-space discontinuities. Training proceeds in stages: the illumination context branch is trained for 20K steps, frozen, then material branches are trained for 20K steps, followed by 10K steps at higher resolution.

## Results

On 133 held-out objects from Objaverse/Objaverse-XL, LumiTex achieves the best scores across all texture and relighting metrics: FID 160.8 (vs. 196.6 for the strongest PBR baseline Hunyuan3D-2.1), CLIP-FID 14.89, LPIPS 0.1903, and CLIP-I 0.9417 for texture fidelity; and FID 99.6, CLIP-FID 10.63, LPIPS 0.0831 for relighting. In a user study with 23 3D modelers, LumiTex scores 4.48/5 on rendering quality and 4.61/5 on texture completeness, surpassing the next-best method (Hunyuan3D-2.1) by 0.79 and 0.63 points respectively. The method also outperforms all texture-only baselines (SyncMVD-IPA, MV-Adapter, Step1X-3D, UniTEX) by a wide margin on every metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LumiTex outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest PBR competitor, Hunyuan3D-2.1 (trained on private data), LumiTex reduces texture FID by 18% (160.8 vs. 196.6), CLIP-FID by 21% (14.89 vs. 18.84), and LPIPS by 21% (0.1903 vs. 0.2413), while also achieving higher CLIP-I (0.9417 vs. 0.9268) and lower relighting FID (99.6 vs. 103.7). Against texture-only methods, the gap is even larger: SyncMVD-IPA, the best texture baseline, scores FID 222.1 and LPIPS 0.2504, roughly 38% and 32% worse than LumiTex respectively. The PBR-specific baselines Paint-it and DreamMat are substantially weaker, with Paint-it achieving the worst FID (293.3) and LPIPS (0.3769) among all methods. In the user study, LumiTex leads by 0.79 points on rendering quality and 0.80 points on metallic accuracy over Hunyuan3D-2.1, confirming that the quantitative gains translate to perceptual improvements valued by professional 3D modelers.

## Relation to Prior Work

LumiTex builds on the multi-view diffusion paradigm established by MV-Adapter, Hunyuan3D-Paint, and SyncMVD for consistent texture generation, and on the PBR-specific line of work from DreamMat, Material Anything, and CLAY. It departs from two-stage approaches (which generate shaded images then decompose materials) by integrating illumination context learning and material generation into a single end-to-end DiT pipeline initialized from FLUX.1-dev. The key architectural departure is the multi-branch design with a lighting-aware material attention mechanism that conditions separate albedo and MR branches on shared shaded embeddings, addressing the domain gap that multi-channel joint-prediction methods (MaterialMVP, MCMat) overlook. For texture completion, it replaces UV-space inpainting (Paint3D, TexGen) with a geometry-guided LVSM operating in view space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PBR texture generation is a bottleneck for production pipelines in games, film, and AR/VR, and this paper offers a practical end-to-end solution (1.5 min inference, 28 GB GPU) that outperforms both open-source and commercial systems. The illumination-context-as-shared-prior design is a clean architectural answer to the data imbalance problem (scarce MR maps vs. abundant shaded images), and the view-space inpainting avoids UV topological ambiguities. Open limitations include the 768×768 per-view resolution cap (insufficient for 4K film/AAA assets) and the absence of transparency/transmission modeling, both of which constrain near-term deployment in high-fidelity production.
