## Problem

Complex scientific problems are domain-specific, open-ended, and unbounded, requiring LLMs to explore vast solution spaces without guaranteed global optima. Existing approaches—whether post-training methods (SFT, RLVR) that suffer from entropy collapse and rarely exceed base-model capabilities, or workflow-driven evolutionary pipelines that are brittle and rely on static pretrained knowledge—fail to simultaneously learn from experience, maintain population diversity, and iteratively build upon previously discovered high-quality solutions.

## Input / Output

At each iteration, the LLM receives a prompt constructed from the problem description, the current candidate solution (code in Python, YAML, or a domain-specific DSL), its scalar reward, auxiliary textual/structured feedback from an evaluator, and a lineage of ancestor solutions with their respective rewards and feedback. The model outputs a SEARCH/REPLACE diff (for code tasks) or a complete YAML geometry specification (for physics simulation), which is executed against a task-specific evaluator (e.g., COMSOL FEA, sklearn pipelines, or constraint solvers) to produce a new reward signal. Solutions are normalized into canonical code format before embedding for diversity computation.

## Method

HELIX integrates three complementary mechanisms into a single iterative loop. First, GRPO-based reinforcement learning updates the LLM policy parameters using verifiable rewards, with group-normalized advantages computed over G rollouts per candidate solution. Second, an NSGA-II multi-objective selection operates over the cumulative solution pool, using reward and a diversity score (1 − mean cosine similarity to k nearest neighbors in a pretrained embedding space) to maintain a Pareto-front population that balances exploitation and exploration. Third, in-context learning prompts are constructed from the lineage tree of each selected candidate, embedding ancestor solutions and their feedback so the model can reason over its own discovery history. This unification means the model simultaneously improves its intrinsic policy through parameter updates and leverages discovered solutions as in-context exemplars, addressing entropy collapse and the inability of memory-less RL to build on prior explorations.

## Results

Across 20 tasks spanning five categories (machine learning, physics simulation, circle packing, function minimization, and symbolic regression), HELIX achieves the best result on 17 tasks using only a 14B model (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B). Headline numbers include a new world-record sum of radii of 2.63598308 for circle packing in a unit square (vs. 2.519 for SLSQP and 2.611 for GPT-4o), an RMSE of 1.747 on Boston Housing (vs. 3.031 for GPT-4o), and an F1 of 82.07 on Adult Income (vs. 80.36 for LightGBM and 76.91 for GPT-4o). On the Biology symbolic regression task, HELIX reaches an NMSE of 2.98e-8, roughly two orders of magnitude better than the LLM-SR baseline (3.06e-6). The paper reports an average F1 improvement of 5.95 points over GPT-4o on the Adult and Bank Marketing datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HELIX outperforms GPT-4o (both direct prompting and OpenEvolve) on 18 of 20 tasks, with the largest margins in physics simulation (e.g., Inductor: 9.609 vs. 1.652 for GPT-4o+OpenEvolve; Beam Bending: 17.298 vs. 6.352) and Boston Housing (RMSE 1.747 vs. 2.937). Against OpenEvolve (the open-source AlphaEvolve implementation), HELIX wins on 19 of 20 tasks, losing only on the Demultiplexer task (14.260 vs. 12.341 for Qwen+OpenEvolve, though GPT-4o+OpenEvolve scores 8.645). Versus task-specific baselines, HELIX surpasses LightGBM on all four ML tasks (e.g., Adult Income F1 82.07 vs. 80.36; Bank Marketing F1 80.65 vs. 75.28), SLSQP on both circle packing tasks (2.636 vs. 2.519; 4.664 vs. 4.522), and SLSQP/Trust-constr on all five function minimization tasks (reaching R=1.000 on four of five). For symbolic regression, HELIX beats LLM-SR on Biology (2.98e-8 vs. 3.06e-6) and Physics (2.76e-5 vs. 7.62e-5) but trails LLM-SR on Chemistry (7.32e-6 vs. 4.12e-6) and Material Science (4.46e-6 vs. 3.21e-9). Against COMSOL's built-in Parameter Scan and Topology Optimization, HELIX dominates on Inductor (9.609 vs. 6.111/6.248), Beam Bending (17.298 vs. 4.771), and Magnetic Torque (11.045 vs. 10.273), though Topology Optimization retains an edge on Demultiplexer (23.555 vs. 14.260).

## Relation to Prior Work

HELIX sits at the intersection of two active research threads: RL-based LLM training (GRPO, RLVR) and LLM-driven evolutionary search (AlphaEvolve, LLaMEA). Pure RL approaches suffer from entropy collapse and cannot leverage previously discovered solutions, while workflow-driven evolutionary methods keep the model static and are highly sensitive to hand-crafted pipeline design. HELIX departs from both by coupling GRPO policy updates with NSGA-II-based population selection and lineage-tree in-context prompting, so the model simultaneously learns from reward feedback and builds on a curated, diverse set of past solutions. The diversity metric via embedding-space KNN is a practical addition not present in prior LLM-evolution hybrids.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a 14B open-weights model, augmented with RL and evolutionary search, can match or exceed GPT-4o across 18 of 20 heterogeneous scientific tasks, including a new circle-packing record—suggesting that iterative, diversity-aware exploration is a viable path for cost-efficient scientific discovery. Key limitations include the requirement for a verifiable scalar reward (ruling out many truly open-ended discovery settings), reliance on COMSOL for physics evaluation, and evaluation on a single model family up to 32B. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to problems with noisy, delayed, or partially observable rewards, and whether the NSGA-II selection strategy remains effective as solution spaces grow combinatorially.
