## Problem

Current RLVR methods for LLM reasoning (PPO, GRPO) follow generalized policy iteration with iterative evaluation-improvement cycles, requiring complex heuristics like clipping, KL regularization, and data selection to combat training instability and entropy collapse. The paper identifies that LLM math reasoning induces a structurally simpler MDP—deterministic, tree-structured, with binary terminal rewards—than the general control settings these algorithms were designed for, raising the question of whether a minimalist approach exploiting this structure can match or exceed their performance while preserving diversity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (e.g., a competition math problem) and autoregressively generates a full chain-of-thought reasoning response ending with a boxed answer. During training, n responses are sampled per prompt to compute mean-centered binary rewards (verified by math-verify), and the centered reward is broadcast to every token in the response for credit assignment. At inference, actions are sampled via softmax over the learned Q-values with a temperature parameter ρ, aligning with standard LLM decoding practices.

## Method

ROVER proves that in deterministic tree-structured MDPs with binary terminal rewards, the optimal action is recovered by acting greedily over the Q-function of a fixed uniformly random policy (Theorem 1), bypassing the generalized policy iteration loop entirely. The Q-function is parameterized intrinsically through the LLM's own parameters as Q(s,a) = ρ(log π_θ(a|s) − log π_θ_old(a|s)), eliminating a separate value network while using the previous-epoch policy as a stable anchor. To balance quality and diversity, actions are sampled via softmax over Q-values with temperature ρ rather than pure greedy selection, with a theoretical performance guarantee that tightens as ρ→0 (Theorem 2). Training uses group reward centering (subtracting the empirical mean of n sampled rewards) and broadcasts the centered reward to all tokens, with the loss being a simple squared error between the current Q-value and a stop-gradient Bellman target.

## Results

On Qwen3-8B-Base, ROVER achieves an average pass@1 of 48.4 across seven math and O.O.D. benchmarks, outperforming the strongest baseline (DAPO at 41.1) by +7.3 points. On the competition-level subset (AIME24, AIME25, HMMT25), ROVER gains +8.2 pass@1 and +16.8 pass@256 over the best baseline. ROVER also achieves the highest diversity, with +20.5% more distinct reasoning strategies than the average of all baselines, and the best O.O.D. generalization on GPQA-diamond (50.2 vs. 46.6 for DAPO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROVER consistently outperforms all three primary RL baselines (GRPO, DAPO, REINFORCE++) on every benchmark for both Qwen3-4B-Base and Qwen3-8B-Base, with the largest gaps on the hardest tasks: +47.1% relative improvement over DAPO on AIME24 and nearly 2× REINFORCE++ on HMMT25 (14.6 vs. 7.1). On pass@k (k up to 256), ROVER sustains gains while all baselines saturate or even degrade at large k (e.g., DAPO drops below the base model on AIME25 after k>4), yielding +16.8 pass@256 over the best baseline. On the tool-use task (Search-R1 setup), ROVER beats the GRPO-based Search-R1 baseline by 1.8% average across six QA benchmarks and shows 55% higher response diversity (cosine distance 0.065 vs. 0.042). On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, ROVER surpasses DeepScaleR-1.5B (trained on the same 40k dataset) on both pass@1 and pass@64, and exceeds ProRLv2-1.5B on pass@64 despite using 3× less training data and ~1/16th the GPU hours. The only comparison where ROVER does not lead is pass@1 vs. ProRLv2 on the 1.5B model (42.2 vs. 52.6 on AIME24), though the authors note this is an unfair comparison due to ProRLv2's 3.4× larger training set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the Generalized Policy Iteration (GPI) paradigm that underpins PPO, GRPO, and their variants, which iteratively alternate between policy evaluation and policy improvement. Instead, it exploits the specific MDP structure of LLM math reasoning (deterministic, tree-structured transitions with binary terminal rewards) to prove that a single evaluation of a fixed uniformly random policy suffices to recover optimal actions, bypassing the entire iterative loop. The closest prior work is He et al. (ICML 2025), which empirically observed that uniform-policy values uncover optimal policies in generative flow networks, and Laidlaw et al. (2023), which noted similar phenomena in discrete games; this paper provides the first theoretical analysis for the LLM reasoning setting and translates it into a practical algorithm (ROVER) that parameterizes Q-values through the LLM's own parameters rather than a separate value network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If the core theoretical result generalizes beyond the tested scope, ROVER offers a radically simpler alternative to PPO/GRPO for RLVR—eliminating KL regularization, clipping heuristics, and the non-stationary evaluation target that cause training instability and entropy collapse. The substantial pass@k and diversity gains (+16.8 pass@256, +20.5% distinct strategies) suggest that preserving exploration is not merely a nice-to-have but a first-order driver of reasoning quality. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to models beyond 8B parameters, whether the practical approximations (relative Q-parameterization, group reward centering, broadcast rewards) preserve the theoretical guarantees at scale, and whether the tree-structured MDP assumption holds for more complex reasoning tasks (e.g., multi-step tool use with stochastic transitions).
