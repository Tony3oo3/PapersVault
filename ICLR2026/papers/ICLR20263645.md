## Problem

Existing Lorentz-based hyperbolic neural networks (e.g., HCNN) remain only partially intrinsic: their fully connected layers apply standard Euclidean matrix-vector products in the ambient Minkowski space before projecting back to the hyperboloid, and their normalization layers either ignore gyro-variance or rely on computationally expensive iterative Fréchet mean solvers. This mixing of Euclidean and hyperbolic operations compromises geometric consistency and limits representational fidelity. The paper asks whether a fully intrinsic architecture—where every parameter, computation, and update is defined natively on the Lorentz model—can outperform these partially intrinsic designs.

## Input / Output

The network consumes 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10/100), genomic sequences of 70–1000 base pairs (TEB, GUE), and graph adjacency/feature data (AIRPORT, CORA, PUBMED), all of which are lifted to points on the Lorentz hyperboloid with fixed curvature K = −1. Outputs are class labels (image/genomics) or node labels (graphs), produced by a final PLFC head whose spatial coordinates encode signed hyperbolic distances to learned Lorentz hyperplanes, with the time coordinate recovered from the hyperboloid constraint. All intermediate feature maps are channel-last tensors with an extra time channel appended, maintaining the Lorentz constraint at every spatial position.

## Method

The core contribution is the Point-to-Hyperplane Lorentz Fully Connected (PLFC) layer, which replaces the Euclidean affine map y = Wx + b with closed-form signed Lorentzian distances from the input point to m learned Lorentz hyperplanes (parameterized by orientation z_k and offset a_k), then reconstructs the output as the unique hyperboloid point whose signed distances to coordinate hyperplanes equal those logits. This is complemented by GyroLBN, a batch normalization that couples gyro-centering via the closed-form Lorentzian centroid with gyro-scaling via Fréchet variance, avoiding the iterative Fréchet mean solvers used by GyroBN while retaining gyrogroup-consistent statistics. Additional intrinsic modules include a log-radius-preserving concatenation (scaling each block by a digamma-based factor to keep expected log-radius invariant to channel count), a mask-and-project Lorentz dropout, and Lorentz activations that act on spatial coordinates and recompute the time coordinate. A theoretical result (Theorem 2) shows PLFC exactly preserves pre-logit margins while the prior LFC head strictly contracts them, providing a geometric explanation for the empirical gains.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, ILNN (ResNet-18 backbone) achieves 95.36% ± 0.13 top-1 accuracy, surpassing the Euclidean ResNet-18 baseline (95.14%) by +0.22 pp and the strongest prior hyperbolic model HCNN-Lorentz (95.14%) by +0.22 pp. On CIFAR-100, ILNN reaches 78.41% ± 0.23, exceeding the Euclidean baseline (77.72%) by +0.69 pp and HCNN-Lorentz (78.07%) by +0.34 pp. On the genomic GUE benchmark, ILNN achieves MCC scores of 83.90 (TATA core-promoter), 92.48 (non-TATA promoter), and 64.76 (Covid variant), outperforming both Euclidean CNN and HCNN variants by 2–13 pp. On graph node classification, replacing Hypformer's linear head with PLFC yields 96.03% (AIRPORT), 85.68% (CORA), and 82.52% (PUBMED), improving over Hypformer by +1.03, +0.68, and +1.22 pp respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ILNN outperforms all 23 baselines across every evaluated dataset. On CIFAR-10/100, the closest competitors are Euclidean-Poincaré-B (Nguyen et al., 2025) at 95.23% and HCNN-Lorentz (Bdeir et al., 2024) at 95.14%; ILNN exceeds both by +0.13 and +0.22 pp respectively, while the Euclidean ResNet-18 (He et al., 2015) and Euclidean-Poincaré-G (Ganea et al., 2018) baselines trail by +0.22 pp. The Euclidean-Poincaré-H variant (Fan et al., 2023) collapses to 81.72% on CIFAR-10, far below all other methods. On the genomic GUE benchmark, HCNN-S and HCNN-M (Khan et al., 2025) underperform the Euclidean CNN on several tasks (e.g., Covid variant: 36.71/14.81 vs. 63.62), whereas ILNN matches or exceeds the Euclidean score (64.76 vs. 63.62) and leads all hyperbolic baselines by 2–13 pp. On graph classification, Hypformer+PLFC (Yang et al., 2024 backbone) surpasses Hypformer by +1.03 pp on AIRPORT, +0.68 pp on CORA, and +1.22 pp on PUBMED, and also outperforms the next-best methods FPS-T (Cho et al., 2023) and SGFormer (Wu et al., 2023) on AIRPORT and PUBMED.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the HCNN line of work (Bdeir et al., 2024), which first brought convolution, normalization, and classification fully into the Lorentz model but still relied on ambient Minkowski matrix-vector products in its fully connected layer (LFC) and used a Lorentzian centroid without gyro-structure in its normalization (LBN). It also builds on the gyrogroup batch normalization framework of Chen et al. (2025a), which introduced gyro-centering and gyro-scaling but depended on computationally expensive Fréchet mean iterations. The key departure is a strict 'fully intrinsic' design principle: every operation—from the point-to-hyperplane FC layer's closed-form Lorentzian distances to GyroLBN's closed-form centroid and variance—is expressed solely in terms of Lorentz geometry primitives, eliminating all ambient Euclidean linear maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean architectural template for building hyperbolic networks without any Euclidean 'leakage,' and the margin-preservation theorem (Theorem 2) gives a concrete theoretical reason why the PLFC head should outperform the LFC head: it preserves pre-logit margins exactly while LFC contracts them. The genomics results are the most practically compelling, showing that HCNN's partially intrinsic design can cause severe under-performance (MCC collapse to 14–37 on Covid variant classification) that a fully intrinsic design recovers. Open questions include whether the intrinsic principle yields larger gains on higher-dimensional or larger-scale benchmarks (e.g., ImageNet, large-scale genomics), and whether the added algebraic complexity of gyro-operations is justified when the CIFAR gains are under 1 percentage point.
