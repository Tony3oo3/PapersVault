## Problem

Existing federated multi-task learning (FMTL) algorithms assume model congruity—users must employ fully or partially homogeneous architectures—limiting their applicability when users have heterogeneous models and tasks. Moreover, prior multi-model contrastive methods rely on pairwise alignment (e.g., InfoNCE applied to each model pair), which cannot capture the joint dependencies among representations from more than two models. This paper addresses both gaps by proposing a contrastive objective that simultaneously aligns N heterogeneous models' representation spaces without requiring shared parameters.

## Input / Output

Each user n holds a task-specific local labeled dataset D^n (ranging from 50 to 1000 training samples depending on task complexity) and a heterogeneous pre-trained foundation model (ViT-Base/Small/Large/Tiny, SegFormer-B0/B1, or BERT/DistilBERT variants) fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 16). Users also access a shared unlabeled public dataset of 5000 samples (e.g., Pascal VOC, COCO, or Flickr30K). Rather than transmitting model parameters, each user sends a representation matrix Z^n ∈ R^{B×d} (B=32, d=256) computed from a public-data batch to the server, which returns an aggregated matrix S^n and weighting vector α^n of size B^M (M=3 randomly selected peers). The output is a customized local model per user with an aligned representation space across all participating models.

## Method

The core contribution is the Muscle loss, a contrastive objective that treats the N-tuple of representation vectors (one from each model for the same data sample) as a joint positive, with any combination where at least one vector comes from a different sample as a negative. The loss incorporates theoretically grounded weighting coefficients α_j = exp(−½ Σ γ_{m,m'} z_{j_m}^m · z_{j_m'}^{m'}) that upweight negatives whose constituent vectors are more mutually dissimilar, a mechanism absent in pairwise InfoNCE. The paper proves that minimizing Muscle loss is equivalent to maximizing a lower bound on the mutual information I(z_i^n; {z_i^m}_{m≠n}), which includes conditional MI terms that pairwise alignment cannot capture. In the FedMuscle algorithm, users perform E local training epochs on their private data, then T contrastive-learning epochs where they exchange representation matrices with the server; the server computes S^n and α^n using only M randomly selected peers' matrices to control downlink communication cost from B^{N−1} to B^M. This design naturally handles both model and task heterogeneity since no parameter sharing is required—only low-dimensional representation vectors are communicated.

## Results

In the uni-modal Setup1 (6 users, heterogeneous ViT models, tasks: MLC/IC100/IC10), FedMuscle achieves an average per-user performance improvement (Δ) of +26.70% (Pascal VOC), +28.65% (COCO), and +16.88% (CIFAR-100) over local training, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline CoFED (+5.83%, +9.85%, +5.99% respectively). In the multi-modal Setup2 (10 users spanning CV and NLP tasks with ViT, SegFormer, and BERT models), FedMuscle achieves Δ = +14.39%. When integrated into CreamFL for image-text retrieval, replacing the heuristic contrastive regularization with Muscle loss improves the global model's Δ from +0.93% to +1.17% (1K test images) and from +0.88% to +1.94% (5K test images).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedMuscle consistently outperforms all baselines on the overall Δ metric across all three public datasets in Setup1. The strongest baseline, CoFED (Cao et al., 2023), achieves Δ of +5.83% to +9.85%, while FedMuscle reaches +16.88% to +28.65%—a gap of roughly 11–19 percentage points. SAGE (Liu et al., 2025) and FedHeNN (Makhija et al., 2022) perform near or below local training on several tasks (e.g., FedHeNN Δ = −0.41% on Pascal VOC), while FedMuscle delivers large gains on every individual user. FedRCL (Seo et al., 2024) and SimCLR (Chen et al., 2020) achieve modest improvements (+2–5%) but are far below FedMuscle; notably, SimCLR actually degrades performance (Δ = −4.94%) when CIFAR-100 is the public dataset. Pseudo-labeling (Cascante-Bonilla et al., 2021) is competitive on MLC but weak on IC100. In the multi-modal Setup2, FedMuscle improves 9 out of 10 users over local training (Δ = +14.39%), with the only exception being DistilBERT on text classification. When integrated into CreamFL (Yu et al., 2023), Muscle loss improves the global model's retrieval performance over CreamFL's original contrastive regularization (e.g., i2t_R@1: 49.32 vs. 49.20 on 1K test images). Compared to the Gramian-based contrastive loss (Cicchetti et al., 2025), Muscle loss improves Δ by 11.1–28.4% depending on the public dataset, while also reducing per-user computational cost by avoiding Gramian determinant computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the InfoNCE contrastive loss (Oord et al., 2019) from its standard pairwise (two-model) formulation to a simultaneous N-model alignment objective, introducing theoretically grounded weighting coefficients that emphasize more dissimilar negatives. It departs from the dominant FMTL paradigm of sharing model parameters (fully or partially homogeneous architectures) by instead learning a shared representation space, and it provides the mutual information lower-bound justification that the recent Gramian-based multi-model contrastive loss (Cicchetti et al., 2025) lacked. The FedMuscle algorithm also builds on knowledge-distillation-based FL for model heterogeneity (FedDF, FedHeNN) and multi-modal FL (CreamFL), but replaces heuristic distillation or CKA-based similarity with the principled Muscle loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: most FMTL algorithms assume model congruity, yet real-world FL deployments involve users selecting different foundation models based on resource constraints, making parameter sharing infeasible. The MI lower-bound guarantee (Theorem 1) gives the N-model contrastive objective a clean information-theoretic interpretation that pairwise alignment cannot provide, and the communication-efficient design (sharing only low-dimensional representations rather than model parameters) adds a privacy layer relevant to fine-tuned FMs. Open questions include scalability when N is large (the B^M downlink cost grows combinatorially), the dependence of the MI bound on batch size, and whether the approach can operate without a shared public dataset.
