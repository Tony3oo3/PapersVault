## Problem

Existing composed video retrieval (CoVR) benchmarks treat video as a purely visual-textual medium, overlooking audio signals—speech, music, environmental sounds—that are often decisive for comprehensive video understanding. No prior framework systematically evaluates retrieval tasks requiring simultaneous modifications across both visual and auditory modalities, leaving a critical gap in assessing multimodal compositional reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263816/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a source video (5–15 s, average 11.8 s) with its audio track and a natural-language modification instruction (average 52.6 words) as input, and produces a ranked list of target videos from a candidate pool. The audio track is not consumed as raw waveforms by the retrieval model; instead, it is first transcribed into fine-grained textual descriptions by Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct, which are then concatenated with the modification query before entering the LLM backbone. Evaluation uses Recall@K (K ∈ {1, 3, 5, 10}) with candidate pools shuffled five times to mitigate composition variance.

## Method

AudioVLM2Vec extends VLM2Vec by explicitly injecting audio semantics into the embedding pipeline. Given a video, visual frames are encoded via a pretrained image encoder and a lightweight projection layer, while in parallel the audio track is processed by Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct to generate a dense natural-language description covering lexical content, para-linguistic features, musical genre, and environmental sound hierarchy. This audio-derived text is concatenated with the user's modification instruction and fed into the Qwen2-VL LLM backbone, where multi-head self-attention jointly aligns visual tokens and audio-derived text tokens in a shared semantic space; the final embedding is extracted from the LLM and optimized via contrastive learning. A controlled ablation on the OmniEmbed backbone confirms that replacing native latent audio tokens with explicit audio-as-text captions yields a +19.1 R@1 gain, demonstrating that textual audio representations are substantially more effective than latent audio embeddings for compositional retrieval.

## Results

On the OmniCVR benchmark (5,000 gold-standard test triplets), AudioVLM2Vec achieves 66.98 R@1, 77.84 R@3, 80.86 R@5, and 84.40 R@10 overall, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline VLM2Vec (38.44 R@1) by +28.5 points. On audio-centric queries specifically, AudioVLM2Vec reaches 77.2 R@1 and 94.2 R@10, while VLM2Vec drops to 12.4 R@1 and OmniEmbed to 13.6 R@1, exposing a critical failure mode in existing systems. In a zero-shot transfer to MSR-VTT text-to-video retrieval, AudioVLM2Vec also edges out VLM2Vec by +1.8 R@1 (37.90 vs. 36.10), confirming that audio-to-text enrichment generalizes beyond the training domain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AudioVLM2Vec wins decisively against all six baselines on every metric. Against the strongest large-model baseline VLM2Vec (Qwen2-VL), it gains +28.5 R@1 overall and a staggering +64.8 R@1 on audio-centric queries (77.2 vs. 12.4), demonstrating that explicit audio-to-text injection is the decisive factor. OmniEmbed-v0.1-multivent (Qwen2.5-Omni), despite natively accepting audio waveforms, trails AudioVLM2Vec by +35.1 R@1 overall and +63.6 R@1 audio-centric, revealing that latent audio tokens are far less effective than transcribed semantic captions for compositional retrieval. Among lightweight models, CLIP (27.54 R@1) and ImageBind (17.28 R@1) are the most competitive but remain 39–50 points below AudioVLM2Vec; task-specific CoVR (11.46 R@1) and BLIP (6.30 R@1) perform poorly, confirming that vision-only or generic cross-modal alignment is insufficient for omni-composed retrieval. On the out-of-domain MSR-VTT zero-shot evaluation, AudioVLM2Vec still outperforms VLM2Vec by +1.8 R@1, indicating that the audio-to-text mechanism provides transferable visual context even when captions rarely mention sound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the composed video retrieval (CoVR) line of work (WebVid-CoVR, EgoCVR, Dense-WebVid-CoVR), which has so far addressed only visual modifications, by introducing audio as a first-class modality in the retrieval task. Architecturally, AudioVLM2Vec builds directly on VLM2Vec (Jiang et al., 2025), injecting audio semantics via Qwen2-Audio-generated captions rather than native audio tokens. The data pipeline repurposes existing large-scale video corpora (HowTo100M, MSR-VTT, VATEX, YouTube8M, YouCook2, VALOR) and leverages Qwen2.5-Omni for omni-modal annotation, departing from prior CoVR benchmarks that relied on purely synthetic or vision-only annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OmniCVR is the first large-scale benchmark to systematically evaluate composed retrieval across vision, audio, and text, filling a clear gap where prior CoVR benchmarks ignored the audio stream entirely. The finding that current 'full-modality' embedding models (OmniEmbed, ImageBind) collapse to near-chance performance on audio-centric queries—while a simple audio-to-text transcription step recovers most of the gap—raises important architectural questions about whether latent audio tokenization is sufficient for fine-grained compositional reasoning. Key limitations include the inference latency of the intermediate transcription step and the fact that the benchmark's integrated queries (57%) may over-represent the difficulty of real-world audio-visual co-modification. Open questions include whether end-to-end audio-aware embeddings can match the audio-to-text approach without the latency penalty, and how these findings generalize to longer-form video and more diverse acoustic environments.
