## Problem

Existing continuous space-time video super-resolution (C-STVSR) methods decouple spatial and temporal modeling into separate 2D implicit representations and explicit optical-flow warping, making them vulnerable to flow-estimation errors at motion boundaries, limited to pairwise temporal context, and lacking a principled anti-aliasing mechanism for arbitrary-scale sampling. No prior method provides a unified spatio-temporal representation that simultaneously avoids warping, supports multi-frame motion reasoning, and guarantees alias-free reconstruction across scales.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB video (T×H×W×3) degraded by bicubic spatial downsampling at a random factor in [1.2, 4] and temporal subsampling (e.g., ×8 from 240 fps to 30 fps), and produces a continuous spatio-temporal signal V̂(x,y,t) that can be sampled at any desired spatial and temporal grid spacing to yield a high-resolution, high-frame-rate video. Unlike fixed-scale VSR methods, the same trained model supports arbitrary integer or non-integer upsampling factors in both space and time without retraining, and the sampling step can incorporate an analytical Gaussian PSF whose variance is set per-dimension to control anti-aliasing.

## Method

V³ represents video as a Video Fourier Field (VFF): a finite sum of N=512 sinusoidal basis functions B_i(x,y,t) = a_i·sin(ω_i·(x,y,t)+φ_i) over the joint (x,y,t) domain, where the frequency vectors ω_i are learned globally once during training and shared across all spatial-temporal voxels, while amplitudes a_i and phases φ_i are predicted locally by a small convolutional head on top of an RVRT video encoder with large spatio-temporal receptive fields. This design lets the model reason over multiple frames jointly, encode translational motion as simple phase shifts, and avoid explicit warping entirely. Anti-aliasing at arbitrary output scales is achieved in closed form by multiplying each basis function by a frequency-dependent Gaussian attenuation factor ξ(ω_i,σ)=exp(−|ω_i|²/8π²σ²), eliminating the need to learn a scale-appropriate PSF from data. The full pipeline (encoder → VFF coefficient prediction → PSF-aware sampling) is differentiable and trained end-to-end with L1 loss on 80×80×14 patches from Adobe240, using AdamW with cosine annealing over 2.5M iterations.

## Results

On the primary C-STVSR benchmark (spatial ×4, temporal ×8), V³ achieves 32.29 dB PSNR / 0.917 SSIM on Adobe240 (average over all frames) and 32.26 dB / 0.919 on GoPro, outperforming the strongest baseline BF-STVSR by ~2.2 dB PSNR. On Vid4 (spatial ×4, temporal ×2), V³ reaches 26.76 dB / 0.818 SSIM, a ~0.9 dB gain over BF-STVSR. In the AVSR setting on REDS, V³ surpasses per-frame arbitrary-scale image SR methods (e.g., HIIF) by up to ~2 dB at ×2 scaling, and in pure frame interpolation (S×1, T×8) it achieves 33.43 dB PSNR versus 29.37 dB for BF-STVSR. V³ is also the fastest method (1.27 s inference) and uses less VRAM (6.1 GiB) than MoTIF (8.4 GiB) and BF-STVSR (10.4 GiB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

V³ outperforms all 15 compared baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the closest C-STVSR competitor BF-STVSR (Kim et al., CVPR 2025), V³ gains ~0.9 dB PSNR on Vid4 and ~2.1–2.2 dB on GoPro/Adobe240, with a similar margin over MoTIF (Chen et al., ICCV 2023) and VideoINR (Chen et al., CVPR 2022). The two-stage DAIN→EDVR pipeline is the weakest baseline, trailing V³ by ~3.3 dB on Vid4. In the AVSR setting on REDS (Table 2), V³ surpasses per-frame arbitrary-scale image SR methods (RDN-LTE, RDN-CLIT, RDN-HIIF) by 1.8–2.0 dB at ×2 scaling, and also beats dedicated AVSR methods SAVSR (Li et al., AAAI 2024) and ST-AVSR (Shang et al., ECCV 2024) by ~1–1.7 dB. For fixed-scale VSR (Table 7), the V².⁵ variant matches or exceeds BasicVSR (Chan et al., CVPR 2021), TTVSR (Liu et al., CVPR 2022), and FTVSR (Qiu et al., ECCV 2022) in PSNR while being the only method supporting arbitrary-scale upsampling. In temporal consistency (tOF, Table 4), V³ achieves 0.254 versus 0.323 for BF-STVSR, confirming superior motion coherence. V³ is also the fastest method in inference (1.27 s vs. 1.88–3.03 s for competitors) with lower VRAM usage than MoTIF and BF-STVSR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant C-STVSR paradigm (VideoINR, MoTIF, BF-STVSR) that factors video into a spatial INR plus a temporal motion model (optical flow or trajectories) connected by explicit warping. Instead, it repurposes classical Fourier analysis by representing the entire video as a finite sum of 512 learned 3D sinusoids over (x,y,t), with a neural video encoder (RVRT) predicting only local amplitudes and phases. The anti-aliasing mechanism draws on the signal-theoretic Gaussian PSF in frequency domain, extending the idea from Becker et al. (2025, THERA) for images to the video setting, and the backbone borrows from the video restoration transformer literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~2 dB PSNR gain over prior C-STVSR methods, achieved with a simpler and faster architecture, suggests that the warping-based decoupling of spatial and temporal modeling was a significant bottleneck rather than a necessary design choice. The analytically correct, parameter-free anti-aliasing (just rescaling Fourier coefficients) is a clean, reusable contribution that avoids the need to learn scale-appropriate filtering from data. Open questions include whether the finite 512-term basis becomes a bottleneck at very high scaling factors or with complex high-frequency content, and how the method extends to non-downsampling degradations (motion blur, sensor noise, compression) beyond the current bicubic + subsampling setup.
