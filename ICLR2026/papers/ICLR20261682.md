## Problem

Prior cross-modal learning work (MultiMAE, 4M) relies on large-scale internet-based datasets to pre-train generalist vision models, but many real-world deployments (household robots, AR/VR devices) operate in a single, bounded physical space where generalization to the wider world is unnecessary. This paper asks whether restricting pre-training to multimodal data collected exclusively within a specific test space—using cross-modal prediction as the self-supervision signal—can yield representations that match or exceed those of internet-scale generalists for downstream tasks in that same space.

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the model consumes paired multimodal samples (RGB, depth, surface normals, Canny edges, and pseudolabel feature maps from CLIP, ImageBind, SAM, ViTDet, and Mask2Former) collected from a single test space. During transfer and evaluation, only RGB images are fed to the ViT-S or ViT-B encoder, which produces task-agnostic representations; a task-specific head is then fine-tuned on a small external transfer set (annotated in a different space) to output segmentation masks, detection boxes, or captions. Critically, no task labels from the test space itself are used at any stage, keeping pre-training and evaluation in the same space valid.

## Method

Test-Space Training (TST) collects unsupervised multimodal data from a bounded test space and pre-trains an encoder-decoder ViT using multimodal masked modeling (MultiMAE-style), where the objective is to predict masked tokens of one modality from the remaining tokens of all other modalities. The modality dictionary is scaled in two stages: first with hardware-acquirable sensory modalities (RGB, depth, surface normals, Canny edges), then with pseudolabel modalities generated by running off-the-shelf networks (CLIP, ImageBind, SAM, ViTDet, Mask2Former) on the test-space RGB frames, effectively distilling their learned feature spaces onto deployment-space data without accessing the original external training sets. The paper shows that scaling the number of modalities within the test space is more effective than scaling unimodal RGB data from external spaces, and that no single modality is irreplaceable—performance is stable across modality subsets. TST-MM with the full modality set outperforms internet-based generalists (DINOv2, 4M-21, CLIP) and task specialists on segmentation, detection, and captioning in the test space, and can also adapt a pre-trained generalist (4M-21) to the test space via continued multimodal masked modeling.

## Results

TST-MM (trained from scratch on test-space multimodal data) achieves 60.85 mIoU on ProcTHOR semantic segmentation, outperforming DINOv2 (54.50) and 4M-21 (53.24), and 49.38 mAP on ProcTHOR object detection, surpassing 4M-21 (41.43) and DINOv2 (40.28). The adapted variant TST-MM (adapted), which fine-tunes 4M-21 on test-space data, reaches 36.44 mIoU on ScanNet++ segmentation and 51.25 mAP on ProcTHOR detection, setting new state-of-the-art across all evaluated tasks. On image captioning, TST-MM from scratch (34.3 CIDEr) matches 4M-21 (36.2 CIDEr) despite seeing no text during pre-training, while TST-MM (adapted) reaches 39.9 CIDEr, approaching LLaVA-1.5 (40.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TST-MM (from scratch) decisively outperforms all internet-based generalist baselines: on ProcTHOR segmentation it beats DINOv2 by 6.35 mIoU (60.85 vs. 54.50) and 4M-21 by 7.61 mIoU (60.85 vs. 53.24); on ProcTHOR detection it surpasses 4M-21 by 7.95 mAP (49.38 vs. 41.43) and DINOv2 by 9.10 mAP (49.38 vs. 40.28). Against task specialists (Mask2Former, ViTDet, SAM, LLaVA-1.5), TST-MM matches or exceeds them on segmentation (60.85 vs. 56.72 mIoU on ProcTHOR) and detection (49.38 vs. 44.10 mAP on ProcTHOR), while on captioning it trails LLaVA-1.5 by 6.3 CIDEr (34.3 vs. 40.6) but matches 4M-21's 36.2 CIDEr despite no text pre-training. The adapted variant TST-MM (adapted) further extends these margins, reaching 36.44 mIoU on ScanNet++ (vs. 30.60 for DINOv2, +5.84) and 51.25 mAP on ProcTHOR detection (vs. 44.10 for task specialists, +7.15). CLIP and MAE baselines are consistently the weakest generalists, with CLIP scoring only 23.19 mIoU on ScanNet++ segmentation and MAE at 13.74, confirming that unimodal or weakly multimodal internet pre-training is substantially outperformed by test-space multimodal training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the multimodal masked modeling lineage (MultiMAE, 4M, 4M-21) by adopting their cross-modal pre-training objective, but reframes the problem from generalist pre-training on internet-scale data to specialist pre-training on a single deployment space. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) restricting both pre-training and evaluation to the same physical space (a 'specialization' setup inspired by developmental psychology), and (2) using off-the-shelf pseudolabel networks (CLIP, ImageBind, SAM, Mask2Former, ViTDet) as additional modalities to distill their feature spaces onto test-space data only. The paper positions itself against test-time training (which adapts to individual instances) and domain adaptation (which adapts task-specific networks), instead learning task-agnostic representations specialized to a space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that multimodal test-space data can substitute for internet-scale pre-training in deployment scenarios, with practical implications for privacy (no external data harvesting) and efficiency (smaller specialist models can replace larger generalists at inference). The specialization-generalization tradeoff and the finding that scaling modalities outperforms scaling unimodal external data are actionable insights for practitioners. However, the strongest results (Table 1) rely on pseudolabel networks trained on external data, weakening the 'no external access' claim; the bare-sensors setup still trails DINOv2. Open questions include closing the gap to the fully-supervised upper bound, exploring more hardware modalities (IMU, radar, haptics), and whether the approach scales to more complex or dynamic environments.
