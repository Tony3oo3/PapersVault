## Problem

Large vision-language models frequently produce visual hallucinations—describing objects, attributes, or spatial relationships not present in the input image—yet the representational mechanism behind these failures has remained unclear. Prior mitigation strategies (extra visual supervision, RL-based alignment, post-hoc attention reshaping) have shown partial benefits but lack a shared explanation of *why* hallucinations emerge at the level of internal representations. This paper identifies that hallucinations correspond to isotropic scattering of transformer key vectors across adjacent visual patches, which blurs object boundaries and weakens cross-modal attention, and proposes a targeted fix at the KV-cache level.

## Input / Output

DSCR takes a standard RGB image and a free-form text query as input, and produces a natural-language text response as output. At inference time, an off-the-shelf monocular depth estimator (e.g., Depth-Anything-v2) generates a depth map that is min-max normalized to [0,1] and resized to the vision encoder's patch grid (e.g., 24×24 for LLaVA-1.5). A key practical property is query-agnosticism: once the KV cache is refined for a given image, the same refined cache can be reused for multiple different questions without recomputation, and the method requires no weight updates or fine-tuning of the VLM.

## Method

DSCR is a training-free, model-agnostic refinement of the Transformer's visual KV cache that injects geometric and spatial priors into key and value vectors before text decoding. For every pair of visual tokens, it computes a combined proximity score from a Gaussian kernel over depth difference (σ_d=0.6) and a Gaussian kernel over 2D Euclidean distance (σ_s=0.6), raised to exponents α=0.6 and β=0.8 respectively; these scores are row-normalized into importance weights. Each key (and value) vector is then replaced by a weighted sum of all other vectors using these proximity weights, effectively clustering tokens on the same depth surface and separating those across depth discontinuities—acting as a graph-Laplacian smoothing on the key-vector graph. The refinement is applied to layers 10–39 across all attention heads via a single inter-tensor multiplication, adding roughly 1–3 seconds per image. The underlying insight, validated by PCA visualizations and a novel Boundary Contrast metric, is that coherent alignment of adjacent key vectors is necessary for reliable cross-modal attention, and DSCR restores this coherence using depth and spatial cues without any parameter updates.

## Results

On the MME hallucination benchmark, DSCR achieves total scores of 925.96 (LLaVA-1.5), 901.56 (LLaVA-1.6), and 872.61 (Qwen-VL), improving over baselines by +33.28, +12.05, and +47.60 points respectively. On the newly introduced Depth Hallucination Mini-Benchmark, DSCR reaches an average score of 78.5 versus 55.5 for the baseline, a 41.4% relative gain. On CHAIR (LLaVA-1.5), DSCR attains the lowest instance-level hallucination rate (CHAIR_I = 11.2) while matching the best sentence-level score (CHAIR_S = 39.2). On POPE (GQA), DSCR consistently lifts F1 by +0.03 across Random, Popular, and Adversarial settings for LLaVA-1.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSCR outperforms all five compared methods on the MME hallucination total for LLaVA-1.5: it beats OPERA (914.69) by +11.27, AGLA (890.17) by +35.79, HALC (899.43) by +26.53, VCD (892.68) by +33.28, and DAMO (872.20) by +53.76. On the Depth Hallucination Mini-Benchmark, DSCR (78.5) surpasses OPERA (75.5) by 3.0 points and VCD (70.5) by 8.0 points. On CHAIR with LLaVA-1.5, DSCR ties OPERA on CHAIR_S (39.2) but achieves a lower CHAIR_I (11.2 vs. 11.6), while VCD actually worsens hallucination (CHAIR_S 54.4). The one notable exception is Qwen2.5-VL, where DSCR (1039.97) falls slightly below the baseline (1042.91) and AGLA (1040.31), suggesting diminishing returns on already-strong models. Importantly, DSCR is composable: stacking it on top of OPERA or VCD yields further absolute gains (e.g., AGLA+DSCR reaches 931.37 on LLaVA-1.5, the highest single score in the table).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two previously separate literatures: KV cache manipulation (which has focused on compression and inference efficiency) and hallucination mitigation in VLMs (which has relied on contrastive decoding, attention reshaping, or RL-based alignment). DSCR repurposes the KV cache as a representational intervention point by injecting depth and 2D spatial proximity cues via a graph-Laplacian-style weighted smoothing, drawing on classical image priors (local smoothness, depth discontinuities as edges) and FC-CRF-style Gaussian proximity kernels. The PCA-based mechanistic analysis of key-vector coherence during hallucination is novel in the VLM hallucination literature, where prior work has largely treated hallucination as a black-box decoding failure rather than a representational geometry problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DSCR offers a practical, training-free, and model-agnostic plug-in that can be stacked on top of existing hallucination mitigations (VCD, OPERA, etc.) with minimal overhead (~1.7 s extra per image), making it immediately deployable in production VLM pipelines. The mechanistic finding that isotropic scattering of key vectors underlies hallucination provides a testable hypothesis for future work on representational diagnostics. Key limitations include applicability primarily to LLaVA-style architectures (not Q-former-based models like BLIP-2), reliance on an external depth estimator (though even a 0.03B model suffices), and the depth hallucination mini-benchmark being small (50 images), which limits confidence in the headline 41.6% figure. Open questions include whether query-aware refinement or joint training with the depth module could further close the gap, and whether the KV-coherence diagnosis generalizes to video or 3D VLMs.
