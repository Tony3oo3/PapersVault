## Problem

Existing equivariant robot learning methods require structured geometric inputs such as 3D point clouds or top-down RGB-D images with known alignment, preventing their use in low-cost or dynamic setups where only raw RGB images from arbitrarily placed cameras are available. RAVEN addresses this by being the first end-to-end SE(3)-equivariant policy learning framework that operates directly on RGB image observations without requiring depth, segmentation, or fixed camera placement.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (84×84 in simulation, 224×224 in real-world) along with their camera intrinsics/extrinsics and end-effector pose and gripper state, using a 2-step history window for temporal context. Images are downsampled by a pretrained ResNet-18 into 5×5 (simulation) or 7×7 (real-world) feature maps, where each grid cell becomes a geometric token. The output is a 16-step trajectory of SE(3) gripper poses and states (first 8 steps executed at evaluation), supporting both relative (end-effector frame) and absolute (world frame) action representations.

## Method

The core insight is to represent image patches as collections of 3D rays originating from the camera aperture, which naturally transform under SE(3) roto-translations unlike 2D pixels. A pretrained ResNet-18 encodes each image into a feature map, and each grid cell is assigned an SE(3) pose via camera projection, producing 'geometric tokens' (feature vectors canonicalized in local frames plus SE(3) poses). These tokens are processed by an SE(3) Geometric Transformer using Geometric Transform Attention (GTA), where attention scores depend only on relative transformations between tokens, and feedforward layers operate in canonical local frames to preserve equivariance. The action decoder is a lightweight GTA-based transformer trained with flow matching to output a velocity field, with action tokens posed at the current gripper pose to enable relative action prediction. This canonicalization-based approach avoids the computational overhead of equivariant convolutions, yielding training speed ~1.6× faster than prior equivariant diffusion methods while achieving 12–35% higher success rates across simulation and real-world benchmarks.

## Results

On the 12-task MimicGen benchmark (100 demos), RAVEN achieves 66% average success rate, outperforming the strongest baseline (EquiDiffPo at 54%) by 12%. On the 6-task DexMimicGen bimanual benchmark (50 demos), RAVEN reaches 82% average success rate, a 17% improvement over the best baseline. In real-world experiments on 4 manipulation tasks, RAVEN achieves 81% average progress and 63% overall completion versus 46% and 24% for pretrained Diffusion Policy. RAVEN also trains 1.6× faster than EquiDiffPo (2.8h vs 7.3h on the Threading D2 task).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAVEN outperforms all three baselines across every benchmark. Against EquiDiffPo (Wang et al., 2024), the strongest equivariant baseline, RAVEN gains 12% on MimicGen (66% vs 54%), 17% on DexMimicGen (82% vs 62%), and 23% on viewpoint generalization (71% vs 48%). Against Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023), both the from-scratch (42%) and pretrained (52%) variants, RAVEN leads by 14–24% on MimicGen; in the real-world setting the gap widens to 35% in average progress (81% vs 46%). Against ACT (Zhao et al., 2023), the transformer-based behavior cloning baseline, RAVEN achieves roughly 3× the success rate on MimicGen (66% vs 21%). Notably, RAVEN trained with only 50 demos on DexMimicGen matches or exceeds baselines trained with 100 demos, and it trains 1.6× faster than EquiDiffPo while delivering superior performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAVEN extends the equivariant robot manipulation line (EquiDiffPo, EquiBot) by removing the requirement for structured geometric inputs like point clouds or aligned top-down views, replacing them with a ray-based encoding of raw RGB images. The core attention mechanism builds directly on Geometric Transform Attention (GTA, Miyato et al., 2023), which the authors adapt from 2D pixel-coordinate contexts to full SE(3) ray-patch poses. The policy head uses flow matching (Chisari et al., 2024) rather than diffusion, and the overall architecture leverages a pretrained ResNet-18 as a lightweight front-end to reduce the 3D token count, a practical departure from prior equivariant methods that process full-resolution spatial data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making SE(3) equivariance accessible with only calibrated RGB cameras, RAVEN removes a significant practical barrier for deploying equivariant policies on low-cost or dynamic robot setups where depth sensors or fixed camera rigs are unavailable. The 1.6× training speedup over EquiDiffPo while achieving higher accuracy addresses a key adoption obstacle for equivariant methods. Open questions include whether the approach extends to uncalibrated or moving cameras, whether integrating depth or point cloud modalities into the geometric token framework yields further gains, and whether the robot-object-sensor equivariance (as opposed to the stronger robot-object equivariance achievable with point clouds) is sufficient for open-world generalization.
