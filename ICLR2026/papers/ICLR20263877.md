## Problem

Existing weakly-supervised counting methods are restricted to a single object category (typically person), while fully-supervised class-agnostic methods require expensive point-level annotations. Although MLLMs offer a natural text-promptable interface for class-agnostic counting, they severely underestimate object counts in dense scenes due to sparse-object bias in their pretraining corpora. This paper asks how to bootstrap MLLMs for accurate class-agnostic counting using only image-level count labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263877/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single RGB image and a text prompt naming the target category (e.g., 'How many cars are there in the image?') and auto-regressively generates a scalar count as text. During training, the MLLM additionally receives sets of four images (for the ranking objective) and multi-round dialogue instructions that progressively narrow a count range. The text prompt is the sole category specification, making the system fully class-agnostic and promptable at test time without retraining.

## Method

WS-COC fine-tunes LLaVA-OneVision-7B via LoRA (rank 128) using only image-level counts, combining three strategies. First, divide-and-discern dialogue tuning (D³T) reformulates counting as iterative binary range judgments: starting from [1, 2000], the model answers 'Are there more than τ objects?' and the range halves each round until U−L < 0.2c, at which point the model predicts the exact count. Second, compare-and-rank count optimization (CRCO) samples four images from count-stratified groups of the same category and trains the MLLM to output their ascending count ranking via language modeling loss, sidestepping the vision-to-scalar modality gap. Third, global-and-local counting enhancement (GLCE) activates at inference: if the global prediction exceeds 100, the image is split into a 2×2 grid, each sub-image is queried independently, and the final count is the average of the global prediction and the sum of local predictions, exploiting their complementary under/over-estimation biases in dense scenes.

## Results

On FSC-147 (test set), WS-COC achieves MAE 13.91 and RMSE 97.28 using only image-level count supervision, surpassing fully-supervised methods CountDiff (MAE 14.83), VLPG (MAE 17.60), and VLCounter (MAE 17.05), while remaining competitive with T2ICount (MAE 11.76) and CountGD (MAE 14.76). In dense scenes (>100 objects), WS-COC reduces MAE from 149.69 (MLLM-Zero) and 82.44 (WS-COC-Base) to 54.37. Cross-dataset evaluation shows WS-COC achieving the best MAE on PUCPR+ (42.30) and ShanghaiTech SHA (128.9), with competitive results on CARPK (10.39) and SHB (34.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WS-COC (MAE 13.91 on FSC-147 test) outperforms all other weakly-supervised baselines: GCNet (17.83), CLIP-Count* (30.01), and T2ICount* (28.55), and also beats the zero-shot MLLM-Zero (38.19) and its own baseline WS-COC-Base (21.08). Among fully-supervised methods, WS-COC surpasses CountDiff (14.83), CountGD (14.76), VLPG (17.60), VLCounter (17.05), CLIP-Count (17.78), and ZSOC (22.09), but is outperformed by T2ICount (11.76) and GroundingREC (10.12). On cross-dataset benchmarks, WS-COC achieves the best MAE on PUCPR+ (42.30 vs. VLCounter's 48.94) and ShanghaiTech SHA (128.9 vs. GCNet's 148.9), while on CARPK it trails VLCounter (6.46) and T2ICount (8.61) but is competitive with CountDiff (10.32) and VLPG (10.14). Notably, WS-COC's advantage is most pronounced in dense scenes, where it reduces MAE by ~63% over MLLM-Zero and ~34% over WS-COC-Base, highlighting the effectiveness of the GLCE inference strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: weakly-supervised counting (which uses only image-level counts but is restricted to a single category like persons, e.g., GCNet, Xiong et al.) and class-agnostic counting (which handles arbitrary text-prompted categories but requires costly point-level annotations, e.g., CLIP-Count, T2ICount, CountGD). By repurposing an MLLM (LLaVA-OneVision) as the counting engine and introducing three training/inference strategies (divide-and-discern dialogue tuning, compare-and-rank count optimization, global-and-local counting enhancement), the authors bridge this gap without any point-level supervision. The compare-and-rank strategy is conceptually related to CrowdCLIP's ranking approach but differs in sampling different images rather than crops of one image, and in using language-modeling loss rather than contrastive optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: replacing point-level annotations with image-level counts dramatically reduces labeling cost while achieving MAE competitive with or better than several fully-supervised SOTA methods on FSC-147, CARPK, PUCPR+, and ShanghaiTech. However, a clear limitation remains for extremely dense scenes (>300 objects), where even fully-supervised methods struggle and WS-COC's MAE stays above 180, suggesting that pixel-level spatial understanding is still a fundamental bottleneck. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video counting, whether the MLLM backbone's pre-training distribution (sparse objects) can be further corrected, and whether the three strategies generalize to other MLLM-driven perception tasks beyond counting.
