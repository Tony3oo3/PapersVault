## Problem

Vision-language models exhibit high prompt sensitivity: small rewordings of natural language prompts cause large performance fluctuations, undermining reliable real-world deployment. Existing prompt learning methods (CoOp, MaPLe, MMRL, etc.) optimize for a fixed set of prompt templates and remain brittle to prompt phrasing variation within the same task. This paper targets the underexplored axis of intra-task prompt robustness—stability across semantically equivalent prompts—rather than the more commonly studied cross-task or cross-domain generalization.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text prompt (a class name embedded in a natural-language template) as input, processed by a frozen CLIP-ViT-B/16 backbone with multi-modal prompt tokens (depth 9, length 2) injected into both vision and text encoders. During training, a pool of 60 hand-crafted templates is randomly split into 30 disjoint sets for inner- and outer-loop adaptation; at inference, the model accepts any prompt formulation without architectural changes. Output is a class label with per-class confidence scores, and the key evaluation metric is the standard deviation of accuracy across diverse prompt phrasings in addition to base/novel/harmonic-mean accuracy.

## Method

Promise is a dual-loop meta-finetuning framework (MAML-inspired, using Reptile first-order approximation) that treats different prompt templates as 'tasks' within a fixed label space. The inner loop adapts learnable token embeddings on a subset of prompts P_in, while the outer loop optimizes shared meta-parameters on disjoint prompts P_out to encourage prompt-invariant representations. Two key modules enhance robustness: (1) an adaptive prompt weighting mechanism that assigns learnable weights on the probability simplex (via entropic mirror descent with temperature) to dynamically prioritize templates with higher generalization utility, and (2) a token-specific learning rate module—a 3-layer MLP hypernetwork that generates per-token learning rates conditioned on encoded prompt features, enabling context-aware fine-grained adaptation. The paper further proves that the weighted, preconditioned inner step yields a guaranteed one-step decrease in outer empirical risk, contracts across-prompt sensitivity, and tightens a data-dependent generalization bound at the post-inner initialization.

## Results

On the base-to-new generalization benchmark (averaged over 11 datasets), Promise achieves a harmonic mean of 82.33, surpassing the strongest baseline MMRL (81.20) by 1.13 points, with base accuracy of 86.14 and novel-class accuracy of 78.84. In cross-dataset generalization (ImageNet→11 targets), Promise reaches an average of 68.68, outperforming HPT++ (68.02) and HiCroPL (67.68). On domain generalization (ImageNet→V2/S/A/R), Promise achieves 62.23 average accuracy, beating FedMVP (60.91) and HPT++ (60.82) by 1.32 points. The method also reduces prompt sensitivity, with the fraction of risk-decreasing inner steps rising from ~61% (unweighted) to ~78% (full Promise).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Promise outperforms all 15 compared baselines on the harmonic mean H across the 11-dataset base-to-new benchmark, with the largest margins over CoCoOp (+6.50 H) and CLIP (+10.63 H). The closest competitor is MMRL (81.20 H), which Promise surpasses by 1.13 points on H and 1.68 points on novel-class accuracy; HPT++ (80.95) and ProMetaR (80.49) trail by 1.38 and 1.84 points respectively. On novel-class accuracy specifically, Promise (78.84) leads CoPrompt (77.23) by 1.61 points and MMRL (77.16) by 1.68 points, confirming that the meta-finetuning framework primarily benefits generalization to unseen categories. In cross-dataset transfer, Promise (68.68 avg) beats HPT++ (68.02) and HiCroPL (67.68), though it slightly underperforms HPT++ on FGVCAircraft (28.15 vs 28.60) and DTD (47.92 vs 53.19). On domain generalization, Promise (62.23 avg) leads FedMVP (60.91) and HPT++ (60.82) by 1.32 points, with the largest single-domain gain on ImageNet-V2 (+1.61 over HPT++). Ablations confirm that both adaptive prompt weighting (+1.05 H over no-weighting) and token-specific learning rates (+1.04 H over no token-LR) contribute additively to the overall gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

Promise sits at the intersection of meta-learning (MAML/Reptile) and prompt tuning for VLMs, but departs from both lines in a specific way: prior meta-learning-based prompt methods (GRAM, MetaPrompt, ProMetaR) treat different datasets or domains as meta-tasks, whereas Promise treats different phrasings of the *same* task as the meta-tasks, targeting intra-task prompt robustness rather than cross-domain transfer. The token-specific learning rate module extends MetaSGD from per-task scalars to per-token, data-conditioned rates via a small hypernetwork, and the adaptive prompt weighting introduces a simplex-constrained, temperature-controlled mechanism absent from prior prompt learners. The dual-loop structure is MAML-inspired but instantiated with Reptile-style first-order approximation and applied specifically to prompt-template variation within a fixed label space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Prompt sensitivity is a practical deployment bottleneck for VLMs, and Promise offers a plug-and-play meta-finetuning wrapper that can be layered on top of existing prompt learners (MaPLe, MMRL, IVLP) without architectural changes, with no inference-time overhead. The theoretical guarantees (one-step outer-risk decrease, sensitivity contraction, tightened generalization bound) provide a principled justification for the weighted-preconditioned inner step, though the authors acknowledge the smoothness and alignment assumptions are only approximate at CLIP scale. Open questions include whether the modest 1–2 point harmonic-mean gains scale to larger backbones or longer prompts, whether the 60-template pool is sufficient for truly open-ended user phrasing, and whether the approach extends to generative or multi-turn VLM settings where prompt variation is even more unbounded.
