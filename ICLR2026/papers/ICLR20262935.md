## Problem

Existing KV cache eviction methods rely on raw attention weights (query-key similarity) to rank KV pair importance, ignoring the attention redistribution that occurs when a token is actually removed from the cache. This oversight leads to suboptimal retention decisions under tight memory budgets, where the removal of one pair shifts the attention landscape and invalidates the original importance ranking. Additionally, prior methods fail to account for the spatial-temporal dynamics in KV importance, where importance patterns shift across positions and fluctuate over decoding steps.

## Input / Output

During the prefill phase, ReST-KV consumes a small query window of size S_w=32 (following SnapKV's convention) along with the full KV cache accumulated so far, and a per-layer cache budget B. It outputs a compressed KV cache containing at most B pairs per layer per head, selected via top-k on the final smoothed importance indicator. Eviction is applied once at prefill; during subsequent decoding steps, new KV pairs are simply appended without further compression. The method is head-wise and layer-wise independent, so different heads in the same layer can retain different KV pairs.

## Method

ReST-KV reformulates KV eviction as a layer-wise output reconstruction problem: for each candidate KV pair n, it computes the L2 distance between the full MHA output and the MHA output with pair n removed, yielding the closed-form indicator I_t[n] = A_t[n]/(1−A_t[n]) · ‖MHA(x_t, ⟨K_T, V_T⟩) − v_n W_O‖₂. This indicator decomposes into a nonlinear attention reweighting term (amplifying high-attention pairs beyond linear scaling) and a redistribution sensitivity term (measuring how well remaining pairs compensate for the removed value). To stabilize selection, two smoothing mechanisms are applied: (1) an exponential moving average (α=0.3) over the recent query window to dampen temporal fluctuations in importance, and (2) an adaptive window-based spatial smoother that computes the shift in average top-B indices between the front and rear halves of the observation window, then averages the temporally-smoothed indicator over a correspondingly shifted and resized spatial window (controlled by β=2000). The final top-B KV pairs per head per layer are selected by argmax on this doubly-smoothed indicator.

## Results

On the RULER benchmark (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, B_total=1024L), ReST-KV achieves an average accuracy of 82.27% across 4k–128k context lengths, outperforming SnapKV (67.11%) and PyramidKV (66.97%) by 15.2% on average, while retaining 68.28% accuracy even at 128k tokens where less than 1% of the original cache is kept. On LongBench (16 datasets), ReST-KV achieves up to 2.58% improvement over the best baseline (SnapKV) under tight budgets (e.g., Mistral-7B at B_total=64L: 40.05 vs. 37.53). At 128k context length, ReST-KV achieves a 10.61× decoding latency reduction over full cache with FlashAttention-2, while incurring only ~2% additional prefill overhead over SnapKV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReST-KV consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every benchmark. Against SnapKV (the strongest competitor), ReST-KV gains 15.2% average accuracy on RULER (82.27 vs. 67.11) and up to 2.58% on LongBench under tight budgets (Mistral-7B, B_total=64L: 40.05 vs. 37.53). Against PyramidKV, the gap is even larger on RULER (82.27 vs. 66.97), with ReST-KV maintaining 68.28% at 128k context versus PyramidKV's 48.93%. StreamingLLM and H2O are substantially outperformed on all tasks (e.g., on LongBench Llama2-7B at 64L: ReST-KV 25.54 vs. StreamingLLM 11.83, H2O 12.42). TOVA is competitive on some LongBench subtasks but still trails ReST-KV by 1–3 points on average. LaCache, the most recent baseline, is outperformed by a wide margin on Llama2-7B at the tightest budget (25.54 vs. 13.51). ReST-KV is also shown to be compatible with AdaKV's head-wise budget allocation, yielding an additional 0.72% accuracy gain when combined (31.20 vs. 30.48 on Llama2-7B LongBench average).

## Relation to Prior Work

ReST-KV extends the KV cache eviction line of work initiated by StreamingLLM (fixed patterns), H2O (cumulative attention weights), and SnapKV (windowed attention pooling), all of which use query-key similarity as the sole eviction signal. The key departure is reformulating eviction as a layer-wise output reconstruction problem, which analytically decomposes importance into a nonlinear attention reweighting term and a redistribution-sensitivity term that prior methods ignore. The spatial-temporal smoothing (EMA + adaptive windowing) draws on observations from MInference's vertical-slash attention patterns and Keyformer's normalization of post-eviction attention shifts, but applies them to the eviction indicator itself rather than to attention computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10.61× decoding latency reduction at 128k context and 36% peak memory savings make long-context inference practically viable on commodity GPUs, and the method's compatibility with existing budget-allocation strategies (PyramidKV, AdaKV) and quantization techniques broadens its deployability. A key open question is whether the prefill-only eviction strategy (no re-evaluation during decoding) remains optimal for very long generations, and whether the reconstruction-based indicator generalizes to multi-modal or structured-memory settings. The paper also leaves open tighter integration with adaptive per-layer/per-head allocation, which currently yields only modest gains (~0.5–0.7%).
