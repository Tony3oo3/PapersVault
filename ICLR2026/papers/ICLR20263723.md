## Problem

LLMs lack the ability to introspectively predict their own success probability and the computation required to achieve it, forcing test-time scaling methods like Best-of-N to use fixed sample budgets regardless of marginal benefit. Existing solutions—learned verifiers, reward models, or scalar confidence scores—either add substantial inference overhead (extra models or forward passes) or cannot capture the joint reward-cost trade-off needed for principled adaptive compute allocation.

## Input / Output

At each decoding step, the method consumes the standard forward-pass logits over a small set of reserved vocabulary tokens (masked out from the actual next-token distribution) and produces a joint categorical distribution over discretized final-reward bins and remaining-length bins. This joint distribution is then used to compute a sampling utility—a linear combination of expected maximum reward, total compute, and latency—which drives meta-actions (continue, branch, or prune specific prefixes) and ultimately selects the final answer. No extra model, architectural change, or forward pass is required beyond the standard generation step.

## Method

ZIP-RC repurposes a contiguous block of reserved tokens in the LLM's output vocabulary: at every decoding step, the softmax over their logits parameterizes a joint distribution p(reward_bin, length_bin | prefix), trained with cross-entropy against targets derived from a learned verifier's score and actual remaining generation length, regularized by a KL term to preserve the original policy. The full joint (rather than separate marginals) is essential because it enables closed-form computation of order statistics—expected maximum reward across a sample pool and expected maximum latency—under planned pruning horizons, which is intractable from marginals alone. At inference, a meta-MDP over the prefix tree selects meta-actions (multisets of prefixes to expand) by maximizing a sampling utility that linearly combines expected max reward, total compute, and latency with tunable coefficients α and β, yielding adaptive behaviors such as spawning more samples on high-variance (hard) problems and aggressively pruning low-value or overlong trajectories on easy ones.

## Results

At matched generation cost (α=0.1, latency-bound setting), ZIP-RC sampling achieves the highest accuracy across all three models and all five benchmarks. On the hardest benchmark (AIME 2024), ZIP-RC improves accuracy by up to 12.7% over Majority Voting (65.8% vs. 53.1% for Qwen3-1.7B) while using equal or lower normalized generation cost. On the Mixed-Difficulty benchmark, ZIP-RC reaches 92.2% (Qwen3-1.7B), 86.0% (LFM2-1.2B), and 74.1% (LFM2-350M), outperforming all baselines. The method also traces smooth Pareto frontiers: adjusting β trades quality for cost (analogous to increasing N in BoN), while α shifts emphasis between compute and latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZIP-RC sampling outperforms all three baselines on every benchmark and model. Against Majority Voting (Wang et al., 2023), the largest gains appear on AIME 2024: +12.7 pp for Qwen3-1.7B (65.8 vs. 53.1), +11.3 pp for LFM2-1.2B (60.9 vs. 49.6), and +11.9 pp for LFM2-350M (38.8 vs. 26.9), while using equal or lower generation cost. Against Weighted BoN with an external reward model (Li et al., 2023), ZIP-RC leads by 11.1 pp on AIME for Qwen3 (65.8 vs. 54.7) and by 6.3 pp on the Mixed benchmark for LFM2-350M (74.1 vs. 67.8), again at matched or lower cost. Against Weighted BoN with self-evaluation (Manvi et al., 2024), ZIP-RC still wins on all benchmarks, with the clearest margin on AIME 2024 (+6.4 pp for Qwen3, +5.8 pp for LFM2-1.2B, +7.4 pp for LFM2-350M) and on the Mixed benchmark (+0.6 to +3.0 pp). The adaptive allocation is the key differentiator: ZIP-RC directs more samples to harder problems and weaker models, whereas fixed-budget baselines spend equally regardless of difficulty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the introspective-inference direction initiated by Ren et al. (2023), who used a model's own logits as a scalar reward proxy, by repurposing a small set of reserved vocabulary logits to predict a full joint distribution over future reward and remaining generation length within the same forward pass. It builds on the MDP formulation of text generation from Ramamurthy et al. (2022) to introduce a meta-MDP that treats test-time search as a dynamic resource-allocation problem, and departs from prior early-stopping/pruning methods (Manvi et al., 2024; Fu et al., 2025) that rely on scalar confidence signals by leveraging order-statistic computations over the joint distribution to optimize an explicit sampling utility balancing reward, compute, and latency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ZIP-RC offers a practically attractive path to adaptive test-time compute: no extra models, no architectural changes, and no additional forward passes, making it easy to retrofit onto existing LLMs. The meta-MDP formalization and sampling utility provide a principled, tunable framework (via α and β) that replaces ad-hoc heuristics with explicit reward-cost trade-offs, and the smooth Pareto frontiers demonstrated across three model scales suggest the approach generalizes. Key limitations include reliance on sufficient sample diversity during inference (acknowledged by the authors), evaluation restricted to small models (≤1.7B) and mathematical benchmarks, and the need for a learned verifier to define training targets. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to open-ended generation, multi-turn dialogue, or non-mathematical reasoning, and how to improve inference-time diversity to unlock further gains.
