## Problem

Transfer learning suffers from negative transfer, where leveraging pretrained source representations degrades target-task performance, yet there has been no theoretical guarantee that any strategy can reliably prevent this failure. Existing empirical remedies (domain similarity metrics, adversarial gating, ensembling) lack rigorous worst-case protection and often require access to source data or specialized architectures. This paper asks whether a simple structural modification can provably eliminate negative transfer.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two inputs simultaneously: (1) the raw target-domain sample x (an image, text sequence, tabular row, or single-cell transcriptome with spatial coordinates) and (2) a frozen penultimate-layer feature vector f_rep(x) extracted from a pretrained source model. The trainable residual encoder h(x) operates on the raw input, while f_rep(x) is held fixed; their concatenation (f_rep(x), h(x)) is fed to a shallow linear adapter that outputs a class label or regression score. No source-domain data or model weights beyond the frozen feature extractor are required at adaptation time.

## Method

REFINE augments any frozen pretrained representation f_rep(x) with a trainable ReLU residual encoder h(x) learned on target data, then fits a linear adapter w on the concatenated feature (f_rep(x), h(x)). The key theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) shows that, under Hölder-smoothness assumptions, the excess risk of the REFINE predictor is bounded by a rate that seamlessly interpolates between the standard nonparametric minimax rate n^{-2β/(2β+d)} (when f_rep is uninformative, so h recovers the full target function) and a near-parametric p·log(n)/n rate (when f_rep is informative, so h only needs to fit the small residual). Corollary 4.4 establishes a no-negative-transfer guarantee: for any target function in the informative class F^β(f_rep, γ), REFINE's worst-case excess risk is no worse than the minimum of training from scratch or linear probing on f_rep, up to logarithmic factors. The approach is architecture-agnostic, requires no source data access, and uniquely supports adapt-time multimodality extension (e.g., injecting spatial coordinates into a single-cell model pretrained without them).

## Results

Across seven single-source transfer tasks spanning image and text domains (CIFAR100→10, CIFAR10→100, CIFAR10→STL, Clipart→Sketch, USPS→MNIST, Books→Kitchen, DVD→Electronics), REFINE consistently achieves the highest accuracy among all adaptive methods, with gains of 5–15% over the strongest baseline. On the most challenging label-space shifts (CIFAR100→10 and CIFAR10→100), REFINE reaches 54.40% and 18.59% accuracy respectively, compared to 43.22% and 5.20% for DANN-Gate. On USPS→MNIST, REFINE attains 70.05% accuracy and 0.9582 AUC, outperforming the best baseline (LinearProbe at 67.00%) by over 3%. On cross-topic text transfer, REFINE delivers 72.72% (Books→Kitchen) and 70.34% (DVD→Electronics) accuracy, exceeding all alternatives by 2–4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REFINE outperforms all five adaptive baselines (LinearProbe, Adapter, LoRA, DANN-Gate, and Distillation) on every transfer task evaluated. Against LinearProbe [26], REFINE gains 10–13% accuracy on the hardest label-space shifts (CIFAR100→10: 54.40% vs 38.93%; CIFAR10→100: 18.59% vs 7.01%) and 3% on USPS→MNIST (70.05% vs 67.00%). Against Adapter [18], the margin is similar: 16% on CIFAR100→10 and 12% on CIFAR10→100, with consistent 2–4% gains on text transfer. Against LoRA [19], REFINE leads by 11% on CIFAR100→10 (54.40% vs 43.14%) and 5% on USPS→MNIST (70.05% vs 64.82%). Against DANN-Gate [45], the advantage is largest under severe mismatch: 11% on CIFAR100→10 and 13% on CIFAR10→100, while on CIFAR10→STL REFINE still leads by 6% (53.42% vs 47.71%). Distillation [16] is excluded from Table 1 due to class-space mismatch but appears in the stress-test setting (Table 2), where REFINE again outperforms it by 3–9% accuracy under 80% label noise (56.58% vs 53.51%) and semantic confusion (58.65% vs 57.80%). Notably, REFINE also matches or exceeds the no-transfer baseline (NoTrans) in most settings, confirming its no-negative-transfer guarantee empirically.

## Relation to Prior Work

REFINE repurposes the residual connection — a structural component originally designed to ease optimization in deep networks (ResNet, gradient boosting) — as a mechanism for provably preventing negative transfer in transfer learning, a role entirely unexplored in prior work. It departs from the dominant empirical remedies (domain similarity metrics, adversarial gating à la DANN-Gate, ensemble methods like SAFEW) by requiring no access to source data and no specialized loss functions, and from parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, Adapter) by operating on frozen features rather than modifying pretrained weights. The paper also carves out an under-explored sub-problem, adapt-time multimodality extension, where a modality absent during pretraining becomes available only at adaptation time, which existing multimodal fusion and PEFT frameworks do not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a theoretical guarantee that a transfer learning method is never worse than training from scratch, closing a long-standing gap between empirical negative-transfer mitigations and formal safety. The convergence rate's seamless transition from nonparametric to near-parametric when source representations are informative gives practitioners a principled understanding of when and why transfer helps. Open questions include extending the theory beyond squared-loss regression to classification and multi-task settings, understanding how the tuning parameter ρ interacts with modern large-scale foundation models, and whether the adapt-time multimodality extension framework generalizes beyond spatial omics to other sequential-modality scenarios.
