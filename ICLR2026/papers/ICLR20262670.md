## Problem

Existing causal inference methods require retraining a model for every new dataset, limiting practical deployment. While prior-data fitted networks (PFNs) enable test-time Bayesian inference via in-context learning, the two concurrent PFN-based causal inference approaches are either restricted to back-door adjustment or mix multiple causal graphs without identifiability guarantees, leading to asymptotically non-informative estimators. The paper asks how to train PFNs that provide valid Bayesian causal estimators across multiple identifiable settings (back-door, front-door, instrumental variables) without retraining.

## Input / Output

At inference time, CausalFM consumes an entire observational dataset D_n = {(x_i, a_i, y_i)} as in-context support (no retraining) along with a query covariate vector x, and outputs a full posterior predictive distribution over the CATE parameterized as a K-component Gaussian mixture, yielding both a point estimate τ̂(x) = Σ π_k(x)μ_k(x) and calibrated uncertainty. The model is trained entirely on synthetic data sampled from SCM-based priors, with the support set drawn from the observational distribution and the target drawn from the interventional distribution, so the network learns to map observational evidence to counterfactual outcomes in a single forward pass.

## Method

CausalFM constructs C-SCM-Priors: probability measures over structural causal models that are compatible with a chosen causal inference setting, organized via Cluster-DAGs that compress the causal structure into clusters of observed and latent variables. Each cluster is parameterized by a Bayesian neural network (inspired by TabPFN) whose weights are drawn from a prior, with constraints ensuring identifiability (e.g., positivity, instrument independence). A key theoretical result (Theorem 4.3) proves that any well-specified prior must assign zero mass to identifiability-violating SCMs, otherwise the posterior-predictive interventional distribution is misspecified and the estimator is asymptotically inconsistent. The PFN (a permutation-equivariant transformer with a GMM head) is trained with a modified negative-log-likelihood loss where the context dataset is sampled from the observational distribution P_obs^S while the target (x, y) is sampled from the interventional distribution P_int^S after applying do(A=a), forcing the network to learn the observational-to-interventional mapping purely through in-context learning.

## Results

On the standard CATE (back-door) setting, CausalFM achieves the best PEHE of 0.515±0.20 across 10 synthetic datasets and 0.478±0.18 on the semi-synthetic Jobs dataset, outperforming all 8 baselines including CausalPFN (0.557/0.528) and DoPFN (0.586/0.482). In the instrumental variable setting, CausalFM achieves 0.422±0.16 PEHE for binary IVs (beating DoPFN's 0.523 and matching DeepIV's 0.427) and 0.579±0.21 for continuous IVs. For front-door adjustment, CausalFM achieves 0.847±0.34 PEHE, substantially outperforming DoPFN (1.274) and the best plug-in front-door learner (0.889).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the standard CATE (back-door) setting, CausalFM (0.515 synthetic / 0.478 Jobs) outperforms both PFN-based competitors—CausalPFN (0.557/0.528) and DoPFN (0.586/0.482)—as well as all six classical CATE estimators, with the closest standard baseline being X-learner (0.563 synthetic). In the IV setting, CausalFM (0.422 binary / 0.579 continuous) beats DoPFN by a wide margin (0.523/0.675) and is competitive with the best dedicated IV estimator DeepIV (0.427/0.516), though it trails DeepIV on continuous IVs. For front-door adjustment, CausalFM (0.847) substantially outperforms DoPFN (1.274) and the best plug-in front-door learner with a neural network (0.889). A key structural advantage is that CausalFM is the only method evaluated that handles all three identifiability settings (back-door, front-door, IV) within a single framework, whereas CausalPFN is restricted to back-door only and DoPFN mixes settings without identifiability guarantees, which the paper's Theorem 4.3 shows leads to asymptotically biased estimates.

## Relation to Prior Work

CausalFM extends the Prior-Data Fitted Network (PFN) paradigm from Müller et al. (2022) and the TabPFN architecture (Hollmann et al., 2023) to causal inference, where the key challenge is the gap between observational and interventional distributions. It departs from two concurrent PFN-based causal works: CausalPFN (Balazadeh et al., 2025), which is restricted to back-door adjustment, and Do-PFN (Robertson et al., 2025), which mixes multiple causal settings without enforcing identifiability. The paper's central conceptual departure is constructing SCM-based priors that explicitly encode identifiability assumptions (via C-DAGs and constraint sets), rather than placing priors on observational distributions alone, and proving (Theorem 4.3) that omitting identifiability from the prior renders the posterior asymptotically inconsistent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CausalFM provides the first unified PFN framework that handles back-door, front-door, and instrumental-variable settings with identifiability guarantees, offering a practical recipe for training causal foundation models without per-dataset retraining. The theoretical result that identifiability must be baked into the prior (not learned) is a clean negative result for the Do-PFN approach and clarifies a design principle for future causal foundation models. However, evaluation is limited to synthetic and semi-synthetic data due to the fundamental problem of missing potential outcomes, and the empirical gains over specialized estimators are modest (competitive rather than dominant). Open questions include validation on real-world A/B experiments, incorporation of interpretability/fairness constraints, and scaling to more complex causal structures.
