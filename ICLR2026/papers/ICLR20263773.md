## Problem

Automated graphic design generation requires simultaneously adhering to multiple heterogeneous user-provided conditions—primary visual elements (images), secondary visual elements, and textual elements (both specified by semantic descriptions and bounding boxes). Existing single-condition models (image-driven or layout-driven) fail to generalize across condition types, while multi-condition approaches lack fine-grained spatial control over each sub-condition and suffer from semantic leakage that compromises compositional harmony.

## Input / Output

The model takes three inputs: a global text prompt describing the overall design, a multi-subject image (primary visual elements) rendered at half the target resolution, and a semantic layout of up to 10 elements, each consisting of a semantic description (capped at 30 tokens) paired with a bounding box. The output is a single RGB graphic design image that integrates all conditions. The paper also contributes a 400K-sample synthetic training dataset with multi-condition annotations and a 1,000-sample benchmark for evaluation.

## Method

CreatiDesign builds on FLUX.1-dev's MM-DiT architecture with LoRA (rank 256, adding only 4.1% parameters). The multi-subject image is encoded via the native VAE into subject tokens, while each layout element's semantic description is encoded by T5 and its bounding box by Fourier positional encoding, then fused through an MLP into layout tokens. All token types (prompt, noise image, subject, layout) are concatenated and processed through stacked MM-DiT blocks with multimodal attention, using positional encoding shifts to avoid spatial conflicts. The key architectural innovation is a pair of attention masks—a layout mask restricting each layout token to attend only within its bounding box region, and a subject mask restricting each subject token to its corresponding image region—thereby preventing semantic leakage between conditions while preserving fine-grained spatial controllability. Training uses 400K synthetic samples generated by an automated pipeline combining LLM-based theme generation, hierarchical layout rendering, LayerDiffuse-style foreground/background synthesis, and GroundingSAM2-based entity annotation.

## Results

On the CreatiDesign Benchmark (1,000 samples), CreatiDesign achieves an average score of 69.28, outperforming the strongest single-condition baseline FLUX.1-Fill (52.24) and the strongest multi-condition baseline OmniGen (38.63) by large margins. Key headline metrics include M-DINO 65.75 for multi-subject preservation, spatial alignment of 78.94 for secondary visual elements, and NED of 94.68 for text rendering. Compared to the base FLUX.1-dev model, CreatiDesign improves the average score by +21.78 points while introducing only 4.1% extra parameters via LoRA fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the image-driven baseline FLUX.1-Fill, CreatiDesign trades a small margin on subject preservation (M-DINO 65.75 vs. 69.05) for dramatically better layout control (spatial 78.94 vs. 67.55, text Sen. Acc 78.30 vs. 12.07, NED 94.68 vs. 56.69), yielding a 17-point average advantage (69.28 vs. 52.24). Against the layout-driven baseline BizGen, CreatiDesign substantially outperforms on subject preservation (M-DINO 65.75 vs. 22.93) while matching or exceeding BizGen on text rendering (NED 94.68 vs. 94.61, Sen. Acc 78.30 vs. 75.89). Against multi-condition baselines OmniGen and PosterMaker, CreatiDesign leads on every metric, with average scores of 69.28 vs. 38.63 and 45.42 respectively. Against Gemini 2.0, CreatiDesign wins on subject preservation (M-DINO 65.75 vs. 29.68) and secondary visual element alignment (spatial 78.94 vs. 59.41), though Gemini 2.0 is competitive on text NED (88.71 vs. 94.68). Compared to the unmodified FLUX.1-dev base model, CreatiDesign improves the average by +21.78 points, with the largest gains in subject preservation (+47.99 on M-DINO) and text spatial alignment (+43.65), confirming that the multi-condition architecture and attention masks are the primary drivers of improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

CreatiDesign builds directly on the FLUX.1-dev MM-DiT architecture, adding multi-condition control through minimal modifications (LoRA + AdaLN on subject/layout tokens, Fourier-encoded layout tokens, and a multimodal attention mask). It departs from prior single-condition specialists (image-driven models like FLUX.1-Fill, layout-driven models like BizGen and HiCo) by unifying heterogeneous conditions in one forward pass, and from prior multi-condition frameworks (OmniGen, PosterMaker, Gemini 2.0) by introducing explicit attention masks that prevent semantic leakage between sub-conditions. The automated dataset pipeline—combining LLM-generated design themes, HLG-based layout rendering, LayerDiffuse-inspired foreground generation, and GroundingSAM2 annotation—is a novel contribution that addresses the data scarcity problem no prior graphic design paper had solved at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 400K-sample dataset and 1K-sample benchmark fill a critical gap in multi-condition graphic design evaluation, giving the community a standardized way to measure subject preservation and layout alignment simultaneously. The multimodal attention mask mechanism is a clean, generalizable technique that could transfer to other multi-condition generation tasks beyond graphic design. The 'free lunch' editing capability—where sequential edits preserve non-edited regions without retraining—suggests the architecture has broader utility for iterative design workflows. Open questions remain around facial detail preservation and dense text rendering, which the authors acknowledge as limitations of the current dataset and model.
