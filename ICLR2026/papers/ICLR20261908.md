## Problem

Prior omni-modal LLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-Omni, Phi-4-MM) achieve strong joint vision-audio understanding but rely on relatively simple architectural choices and lack systematic ablation of critical design decisions such as cross-modal alignment and temporal encoding. Training such systems is also prohibitively expensive—Qwen2.5-Omni requires 1.2T tokens—leaving open whether careful architecture and data design can match or exceed frontier performance at a fraction of the cost.

## Input / Output

The model accepts flexible modality combinations: video is decomposed into a sequence of temporally correlated frames plus a continuous audio track (handled by a unified encoder for both speech and natural sound), alongside standalone images and text prompts. In RL training it processes up to 64 video frames with 1024-token prompts and 2048-token responses; for sports broadcasting it ingests FHD 1920×1080 clips at 128 frames per point. Output is text-based (answers, captions, navigation actions, translations) and can be paired with an off-the-shelf TTS module for spoken responses.

## Method

Three architectural innovations form the core: (i) OmniAlignNet projects vision and audio embeddings into a shared latent space via learnable query vectors and three self-attention layers, then aligns them with a symmetric CLIP-style contrastive loss; (ii) Temporal Embedding Grouping (TEG) partitions the time axis into chunks of duration T_G and interleaves vision and audio embeddings by timestamp to encode relative temporal order; (iii) Constrained Rotary Time Embedding (CRTE) injects absolute timestamps via RoPE-style rotations with a bounded maximum horizon T_max, balancing fine-grained and coarse temporal sensitivity. Training follows a two-stage recipe—modality-specific pretraining followed by omni-modal joint training on 24M curated conversations that combine implicit learning (existing video-QA data with audio tracks) and explicit learning (synthesized omni-modal captions from a data engine that corrects modality-specific hallucination). A final GRPO reinforcement-learning stage further improves omni-modal reasoning, with audio input shown to accelerate convergence over video-only training.

## Results

OmniVinci achieves a state-of-the-art average score of 53.73 on omni-modal benchmarks, surpassing Qwen2.5-Omni (49.66) by +4.07, with particularly large gains of +19.05 on DailyOmni (66.50) and +2.83 on WorldSense (48.23). On audio understanding, OmniVinci scores 58.40 on MMAR (+1.7 over Qwen2.5-Omni) and achieves competitive speech recognition WERs of 1.7 (LibriSpeech-clean) and 3.7 (LibriSpeech-other). On video understanding, it reaches 68.2 on Video-MME (+3.9 over Qwen2.5-Omni) and 61.3 on LongVideoBench (+3.6 over NVILA), all while using only 0.2T training tokens—a 6× reduction compared to Qwen2.5-Omni's 1.2T.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen2.5-Omni (the primary baseline), OmniVinci wins decisively on DailyOmni (+19.05), WorldSense (+2.83), MMAR (+1.7), and Video-MME (+3.9), but trails on Omnibench (46.47 vs. 56.13), suggesting Qwen2.5-Omni retains an edge on image-audio tasks. Compared to GPT-4o, OmniVinci surpasses it on WorldSense (48.23 vs. 42.60) and is competitive on speech recognition (WER 1.7 vs. 2.5 on LibriSpeech-clean). Against NVILA, OmniVinci leads on all video benchmarks (Video-MME 68.2 vs. 64.2, LongVideoBench 61.3 vs. 57.7, MVBench 70.6 vs. 68.1) while maintaining comparable image understanding scores. On speech recognition, OmniVinci achieves an average WER of 6.3, slightly behind Phi-4-MM (5.2) but ahead of Whisper-large-v3 (7.1) and Qwen2.5-Omni (6.8). In downstream tasks, OmniVinci matches NVILA on R2R-CE navigation (NE 5.67 vs. 5.43) and substantially outperforms Qwen2.5-Omni on tennis broadcasting (85.7% vs. 48.6% on point-ending classification) and medical CT interpretation (+2.0 pp average accuracy). On SPORTU sports understanding, OmniVinci (67.30) approaches GPT-4o (68.79) while far exceeding Qwen2.5-Omni (60.49).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the omni-modal LLM line initiated by Qwen2.5-Omni, Phi-4-MM, and Gemini by providing a systematic architectural study rather than primarily a data-scaling effort. It builds on RoPE/RoFormer (Su et al., 2024) and the RoTE timestamp-rotation idea from OmCat (Goel et al., 2024) to propose Constrained Rotary Time Embedding, and adapts ImageBind-style contrastive alignment (Girdhar et al., 2023) into the OmniAlignNet module for vision-audio embedding harmonization. For post-training, it extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) to accommodate audio tokens alongside visual ones in the RL objective. The key departure from prior omni-modal models is the rigorous ablation of each architectural choice (TEG, CRTE, OmniAlignNet) before scaling, which prior technical reports largely omitted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6× training-token reduction (0.2T vs. 1.2T for Qwen2.5-Omni) while achieving larger benchmark gains makes omni-modal model development substantially more accessible, and the open-source release lowers the barrier for the community. The finding that modalities reinforce each other not only in perception but also in RL-based reasoning (Key Insight 3) suggests a principled argument for why omni-modal training is superior to sequential modality training. Open questions include whether the data synthesis pipeline (omni-modal data engine) generalizes to new domains without manual curation, whether the architectural gains persist at much larger model scales, and whether the omni-modal advantage extends to generation tasks (e.g., joint video-audio synthesis) rather than understanding alone.
