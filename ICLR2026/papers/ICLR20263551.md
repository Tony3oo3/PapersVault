## Problem

Non-prehensile manipulation with dexterous multi-fingered hands remains largely unexplored because two critical resources are missing: large-scale, contact-aware trajectory datasets that cover diverse object geometries and force conditions, and a whole-hand motion planner that coordinates coupled multi-finger contact forces. Prior work has focused on single-contact pushing with grippers or rods, leaving the stability and efficiency advantages of distributed multi-finger contact unrealized.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a fused 3D point cloud of the target object (from three calibrated Realsense D435i depth cameras), a 3-DoF target pose (x/y translation + yaw), and a rolling window of 5 past frames containing hand joint positions/angles, wrist pose, object pose, per-finger contact positions, and pressing forces. Tactile input is a per-finger vector field of 33 markers × 4 channels (normal force from indentation depth, tangential shear from 2-D marker displacement) captured at 120 fps by monochrome vision-based R-Tac sensors. The policy outputs a 5-frame rollout of wrist rotation/translation and finger joint angles executed at 30 Hz, plus predicted per-finger tactile vector fields for the next 5 frames that serve as force commands for the trajectory policy.

## Method

DexMove combines a hybrid data pipeline with a three-component flow-matching policy. The data pipeline synthesizes 2M force-augmented wrist-finger trajectories in MuJoCo via rejection sampling and rigid-body no-slip kinematics, then augments them with ~300k frames of real fingertip force distributions captured by a wearable exoskeleton fitted with isomorphic vision-based tactile sensors, bridging the sim-to-real tactile gap. The policy stack consists of: (i) a flow-matching network (5-layer MLP with PointNet++ conditioning via FiLM) that generates an initial contact hand pose from the object point cloud and target pose; (ii) DexMove-Policy, a transformer-based flow-matching model that fuses 5 past state tokens, a global target token, and a time token via cross-attention, then predicts a 5-frame hand trajectory conditioned on a desired force schedule; and (iii) TaFo-Net, a transformer decoder that predicts future tactile vector fields using per-finger spatial encoding, cross-finger multi-head self-attention (to capture inter-finger force coupling), and finger-wise causal temporal attention (to enforce temporal consistency and goal-conditioned reasoning). The key architectural insight is that historical tactile fields implicitly encode environmental properties such as surface friction, allowing the force planner to adapt without explicit friction estimation, while cross-finger attention models the coupled dynamics that single-contact methods cannot represent.

## Results

DexMove achieves a 77.8% average success rate across six objects and three yaw-angle ranges, substantially outperforming the best gripper-based baseline (CORN at ~42.2%). Execution efficiency is dramatically improved: DexMove completes repositioning in 8.3–12.4 s versus 36.1–62.1 s for DyWA and CORN, a roughly 4× speedup. The method also shows strong robustness to unseen surface friction (Fric. B), with only a marginal performance drop (e.g., 86.7% → 86.7% for small yaw errors), whereas DyWA and CORN degrade by 13–17 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DexMove is compared against two gripper-based non-prehensile methods (DyWA and CORN) and an open-loop replay baseline. On success rate, DexMove (77.8% average) outperforms CORN (~42.2%) by ~35.6 percentage points and DyWA (~41.1%) by ~36.7 percentage points, with the gap widening for larger yaw targets (60–90°: DexMove 65% avg vs. CORN 43.3% and DyWA 41.7%). On execution time, DexMove (8.3–12.4 s) is approximately 4–5× faster than both DyWA (36.1–60.6 s) and CORN (41.4–62.1 s), attributed to multi-finger contact reducing the number of action primitives needed. Both gripper baselines predominantly fail on cylindrical objects due to reliance on a single contact point, while DexMove's distributed multi-contact strategy enables stable rotation. Under unseen friction (Fric. B), DexMove maintains near-identical performance, whereas DyWA drops by 13.3 pp and CORN by 6.7 pp on average, highlighting DexMove's superior robustness to unmodeled surface variability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends non-prehensile manipulation from the dominant single-contact gripper/rod paradigm (CORN, DyWA, planar pushing) to multi-finger dexterous hands with closed-loop tactile feedback. Architecturally, it adopts flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) for both contact-pose generation and trajectory prediction, with rectified-flow fine-tuning (Liu et al., 2022) for efficient inference, and builds its tactile sensor on the R-Tac design (Lin et al., 2025) augmented with marker-based shear estimation. The key departure is the hybrid data pipeline: large-scale MuJoCo trajectory synthesis (2M sequences) is bridged to real-world force fidelity via a wearable exoskeleton that captures human fingertip contact data, addressing the sim-to-real tactile gap that purely simulation-based approaches cannot close.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DexMove is the first framework to demonstrate tactile-guided, multi-contact non-prehensile manipulation with a dexterous hand, achieving a 77.8% success rate and ~300% efficiency gain over single-contact gripper baselines. The hybrid simulation-plus-human-demonstration data paradigm offers a reusable recipe for other contact-rich dexterous tasks where sim-to-real tactile gaps are prohibitive. Open questions remain: the system struggles with articulated objects, spherical objects, and certain hand poses (e.g., pushing a tall can by its lid), and the integration of prehensile and non-prehensile strategies in a unified policy is left for future work. The reliance on ArUco markers for pose tracking (though a markerless variant is shown) and the 30 Hz control rate also limit immediate deployment in faster or more cluttered settings.
