## Problem

Existing sparse autoencoders (ReLU, JumpReLU, TopK) enforce non-negativity on their sparse codes, which fragments inherently bidirectional semantic concepts (e.g., male vs. female) into separate unidirectional features and discards half the representation space. Additionally, no principled framework existed to derive SAE variants from the original dictionary learning formulation, leaving design choices ad hoc.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261708/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes LLM residual stream activations x ∈ R^d at a chosen layer ℓ (one token at a time) and produces a signed sparse latent code z ∈ R^P with exactly k non-zero entries that may be positive or negative, plus a reconstructed hidden state x̂ = Dz + b. Unlike conventional SAEs where all activations are clamped to be non-negative, AbsTopK preserves the sign of each retained activation, so a single feature dimension can encode both poles of a semantic axis. The sparsity level k is set to roughly one-tenth of the hidden dimension per model.

## Method

The paper first establishes a unified framework by unrolling one step of the proximal gradient method for sparse coding, showing that ReLU, JumpReLU, and TopK SAEs are precisely the proximal operators of ℓ₁, ℓ₀, and constrained-ℓ₀ regularizers respectively—all with an implicit non-negativity constraint. AbsTopK is then derived by removing this constraint from the ℓ₀ regularizer, yielding a hard-thresholding operator that retains the k largest-magnitude activations regardless of sign. The resulting architecture uses encoder z = AbsTopK(W^T x + b_e) and decoder x̂ = Dz + b, trained end-to-end by minimizing reconstruction MSE via Adam (30k steps, batch 4096, lr 3e-4) on the Pile dataset. This single modification—allowing negative activations—enables a single dictionary atom to represent a bipolar concept via the sign of its coefficient, reducing feature fragmentation and improving reconstruction fidelity, interpretability, and steering performance across four LLMs and seven downstream tasks.

## Results

On the safety-utility trade-off (Table 1), AbsTopK SAE achieves the smallest MMLU degradation across all four models (e.g., −1.4 on Qwen3-4B L18 vs. −2.1 for TopK and −2.3 for JumpReLU) while attaining the highest or near-highest HarmBench refusal scores (e.g., 81.3 on Qwen3-4B L18, 91.3 on Llama3.1-8B L24). On the seven SAEBench probing/steering tasks (Figure 4, Table 3), AbsTopK outperforms TopK and JumpReLU on the majority of metrics, with the largest gains on Spurious Correlation Removal (SCR: 0.35 vs. 0.26/0.28 on Qwen3-4B L18) and Targeted Probe Perturbation (TPP: 0.36 vs. 0.31/0.30). In unsupervised reconstruction, AbsTopK consistently achieves lower normalized MSE and higher Loss Recovered scores across sparsity levels. On the bidirectional feature analysis (Table 2), 20.2–21.5% of AbsTopK features encode opposite-meaning semantics, compared to only 1.8–2.6% for TopK.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TopK SAE (Gao et al., 2025), AbsTopK consistently wins on MMLU preservation (e.g., −1.4 vs. −2.1 on Qwen3-4B L18; −0.9 vs. −1.7 on Llama3.1-8B) and matches or exceeds it on HarmBench (81.3 vs. 78.2 on Qwen3-4B L18; 91.3 vs. 89.2 on Llama3.1-8B). Against JumpReLU SAE (Rajamanoharan et al., 2025), AbsTopK shows a similar pattern of superior MMLU retention and competitive-to-better HarmBench scores across all models. Against ReLU SAE (Cunningham et al., 2023), AbsTopK dominates on both metrics by a wide margin (e.g., 75.9 vs. 74.4 MMLU and 81.3 vs. 78.5 HarmBench on Qwen3-4B L18). The most notable comparison is against DiM (Arditi et al., 2024), a supervised method requiring labeled data per concept: AbsTopK matches or slightly trails DiM on HarmBench (e.g., 91.3 vs. 92.4 on Llama3.1-8B; 89.2 vs. 90.0 on Gemma3-12B L40) but consistently preserves more general capability on MMLU (−0.9 vs. −1.3 on Llama3.1-8B; −1.8 vs. −2.0 on Gemma3-12B L40), and actually surpasses DiM on HarmBench for Qwen3-4B L18 (81.3 vs. 80.6) and Gemma3-12B L6 (65.6 vs. 65.4). On the seven SAEBench probing/steering tasks, AbsTopK outperforms both TopK and JumpReLU on the majority of metrics, with the largest relative gains on SCR (0.35 vs. 0.26/0.28) and TPP (0.36 vs. 0.31/0.30) on Qwen3-4B L18.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the design of sparse autoencoders (ReLU, JumpReLU, TopK) through a single proximal-gradient unrolling of the dictionary learning objective, showing that each SAE nonlinearity is the proximal operator of a specific sparsity regularizer with an implicit non-negativity constraint. The key departure from prior SAE work is identifying this non-negativity as a structural limitation that fragments bidirectional semantic axes (e.g., male/female) into redundant unidirectional features, and proposing AbsTopK—a hard-thresholding operator derived from the ℓ₀ norm without the non-negativity constraint—as the natural remedy. The framework also situates SAEs within the broader unrolled dictionary learning literature (Gregor & LeCun, Tolooshams & Ba) while connecting to the linear representation hypothesis and Difference-in-Mean steering methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that removing a single architectural constraint (non-negativity) allows an unsupervised SAE to match or exceed the supervised Difference-in-Mean method—previously shown to outperform SAEs on steering benchmarks—challenges the assumption that SAEs are inherently inferior for model control. This opens the door to richer, more compact interpretable dictionaries where a single feature encodes a full semantic axis rather than requiring paired features. Open questions include whether multi-step proximal unrolling or differentiable approximations of the ℓ₀ operator can further close the gap, whether the bidirectional structure generalizes to non-residual-stream activations at scale, and whether the fragmentation diagnosis extends to other dictionary learning settings beyond LLMs.
