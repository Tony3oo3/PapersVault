## Problem

Existing reward models suffer from two fundamental limitations: modality imbalance, where most RMs are restricted to text and image with poor support for video, audio, and 3D, and preference rigidity, where training on fixed binary preference pairs embeds a single implicit notion of preference that cannot adapt to diverse, user-specific evaluation criteria. No prior benchmark or dataset systematically evaluates reward models across all omni-modal tasks under free-form, natural-language preference specifications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263682/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance is a tuple (x, y₁, y₂, c) where x is a task prompt (text, image+text, video+text, audio+text, or 3D+text), y₁ and y₂ are two candidate model responses in the target modality, and c is a free-form natural-language criterion specifying the evaluation dimension (e.g., 'the response should prioritize factual accuracy over brevity'). The RM must predict which response is preferred under criterion c, with a three-way output space {y₁, y₂, tie} in the harder w/ Ties setting. The discriminative model outputs a scalar reward score per response, while the generative model first produces a chain-of-thought critique of both responses before emitting the final preference label.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary reward models trained on Omni-RewardData (248K general preference pairs + 69K instruction-tuning pairs with GPT-4o-generated free-form criteria). Omni-RewardModel-BT is a discriminative RM built on MiniCPM-o-2.6 with frozen vision/audio encoders, trained via the Bradley-Terry pairwise loss on the full dataset, where user-specific criteria are injected as system messages to condition the value head. Omni-RewardModel-R1 is a generative RM built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, trained from scratch using GRPO-based reinforcement learning on only 10K samples (3% of the data), where the model generates an explicit CoT explanation followed by a preference prediction, and the RL reward signal is computed by matching the predicted preference against ground truth. The instruction-tuning data is critical: ablations show that removing it causes a clear performance drop, confirming that free-form criteria conditioning is what enables the model to dynamically adjust reward scores rather than relying on a fixed implicit preference.

## Results

On Omni-RewardBench (w/ Ties setting), Omni-RewardModel-BT achieves 65.36% overall accuracy, surpassing the best proprietary model Claude 3.5 Sonnet (66.54%) by a narrow margin and the best open-source model Gemma-3 27B (65.12%). Under the w/o Ties setting, Omni-RewardModel-BT reaches 73.68% accuracy. On VL-RewardBench, Omni-RewardModel-BT attains 76.3% overall accuracy, setting a new SOTA that exceeds the previous best (Skywork-VL-Reward at 73.1%) and GPT-4o (65.8%). The generative variant Omni-RewardModel-R1 achieves 60.18% on Omni-RewardBench while providing explicit chain-of-thought reasoning, outperforming all specialized RMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Omni-RewardModel-BT outperforms all specialized reward models (UnifiedReward1.5 at 59.69%, IXC-2.5-Reward at 48.20%, HPSv2 at 54.02%, PickScore at 53.04%) by a substantial margin of 5.7–17.2 points on Omni-RewardBench overall accuracy. Among proprietary MLLMs used as generative RMs, Claude 3.5 Sonnet (66.54%) and GPT-4o (64.62%) are the strongest, with Omni-RewardModel-BT (65.36%) falling between them but uniquely covering all nine tasks including audio and 3D. On VL-RewardBench, Omni-RewardModel-BT (76.3%) surpasses GPT-4o (65.8%), Gemini-1.5-Pro (67.2%), and the previous SOTA Skywork-VL-Reward (73.1%), while also exceeding UnifiedReward (66.1%) and IXC-2.5-Reward (65.8%). The generative Omni-RewardModel-R1 (60.18%) trails the discriminative variant by ~5 points but provides interpretable CoT reasoning and still beats all specialized RMs. Notably, Omni-RewardModel-BT achieves SOTA on the underrepresented TA2T (70.59%) and T2A (63.99%) tasks, where even GPT-4o scores only 62.75% and 44.53% respectively, directly addressing the modality imbalance problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bradley-Terry preference learning framework and GRPO-based reinforcement learning (from DeepSeek-R1) to the omni-modal reward modeling setting, where the RM must handle text, image, video, audio, and 3D inputs simultaneously. It departs from prior multimodal RMs (PickScore, HPSv2, IXC-2.5-Reward, UnifiedReward) that are typically trained for specific modalities or task pairs and rely on fixed binary preference labels, by introducing free-form natural-language criteria that condition the reward signal. The work also builds on the heterogeneous preference alignment line (PAL, SyncPL) but extends it from text-only to truly omni-modal scenarios, and on prior RM benchmarks (RewardBench, VL-RewardBench, Multimodal RewardBench) by adding the missing modalities and the free-form preference dimension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Omni-RewardBench is the first benchmark to systematically evaluate RMs across five modalities with free-form criteria, and it reveals that even the strongest proprietary model (Claude 3.5 Sonnet) achieves only 66.54% accuracy, while specialized RMs lag further behind—indicating a substantial gap in current omni-modal alignment tooling. The finding that instruction-tuning data is critical for handling free-form preferences (removing it causes a clear performance drop) provides a practical recipe for building more flexible RMs. Key open questions include whether the RL-based generative RM (trained on only 3% of data) can scale, whether multi-turn conversational preferences can be captured, and whether the observed cross-task correlations (0.8–0.9 for understanding tasks) will hold as models improve on individual modalities.
