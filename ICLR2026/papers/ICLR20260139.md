## Problem

Existing XAI methods for time series attribute predictions at isolated time steps, overlooking temporal dependencies and the dynamics of how predictions evolve over time. In online monitoring (healthcare, finance), practitioners need to understand why predictions change between adjacent steps—e.g., a sepsis risk rise from 10%→50% signals deterioration while 90%→50% signals recovery—but no method directly explains such prediction changes, and appropriate evaluation metrics for this setting remain largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

Given a trained online time series classifier f with fixed lookback window W and two overlapping input windows X_{T1-W+1:T1} and X_{T2-W+1:T2} (constrained to T2−T1 < W so windows share temporal context), the method produces attribution scores φ(f, X_{t,d} | T1→T2) for every feature d at every time step t in the combined range [T1−W+1, T2], quantifying each feature's contribution to the prediction change Δ. The prediction-difference wrapper g reformulates the problem as a single-prediction explanation over the concatenated (T2−T1+W)×D input, enabling seamless adaptation of existing single-time XAI methods without architectural modification to the underlying model.

## Method

SWING extends Integrated Gradients by replacing the zero-baseline straight-line path with three mechanisms: (i) Retrospective Baseline Selection (RBS) uses the immediately preceding window X_{T_i−W:T_i−1} as baseline to stay near the data manifold and mitigate OOD effects; (ii) Dual-Path Integration (DPI) averages attributions over four baseline-input path pairs (γ_{1,1}, γ_{1,2}, γ_{2,1}, γ_{2,2}) to guarantee online completeness; and (iii) Piecewise-Linear Historical Integration (PHI) slides the window through M intermediate historical time points rather than interpolating directly, keeping the integration trajectory on the temporal data manifold. The broader Delta-XAI framework introduces a prediction-difference wrapper g that adapts 14 existing XAI methods to the prediction-change setting, alongside a comprehensive evaluation suite (CPD, AUPD, MPD, AUMPD, CPP, AUPP, MPP, AUMPP, Corr.) that uses forward-fill substitution to avoid OOD artifacts in faithfulness and sufficiency scoring. SWING provably satisfies online completeness, implementation invariance, and skew-symmetry, and requires only a single hyperparameter (n_samples=50) for path discretization.

## Results

On the MIMIC-III decompensation benchmark (LSTM backbone, K=50 removed points, forward-fill substitution), SWING achieves the best score on all nine evaluation metrics: CPD 23.87, AUPD 16.23, MPD 22.27, AUMPD 15.52 (faithfulness, higher is better), CPP 17.76, AUPP 5.85, MPP 18.20, AUMPP 6.06 (sufficiency, lower is better), and Corr. 0.40 (magnitude alignment). The closest baseline on faithfulness is WinIT (CPD 19.64, AUPD 12.25), while on sufficiency the best baseline is TIMING (CPP 31.22, AUPP 13.36). SWING's advantage is most pronounced on preservation metrics, where it reduces CPP by ~44% relative to the next-best method (TIMING, 31.22 vs. 17.76).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SWING outperforms all 14 baselines on every metric in the primary MIMIC-III table, with the largest margins on sufficiency (CPP, AUPP) and correlation. Among gradient-based methods, IG (CPD 13.42, CPP 33.55) and DeepLIFT (CPD 13.58, CPP 35.96) are the strongest single-step baselines, but SWING nearly doubles their CPD (23.87) and cuts CPP by ~47% (17.76 vs. 33.55), confirming that temporal path integration is critical for prediction-change attribution. The most recent time-series-specific methods—TIMING (CPD 14.99, CPP 31.22), WinIT (CPD 19.64, CPP 29.22), and TimeX++ (CPD 11.12, CPP 34.21)—all trail SWING on both faithfulness and sufficiency, with WinIT being the closest on CPD but still 17% lower. Masking-based methods (Dynamask, Extrmask, ContraLSP, TimeX) show the weakest sufficiency (CPP 29–53) and near-zero correlation (0.03–0.08), indicating their attributions poorly align with actual prediction-change magnitudes. Perturbation-based methods (LIME, FO, AFO) are competitive on some sufficiency sub-metrics (e.g., FO's AUMPP 6.24 is close to SWING's 6.06) but lag substantially on faithfulness (CPD 2–13 vs. 23.87) and correlation. The paper also demonstrates that naive subtraction of Dynamask attributions across two time steps yields near-zero CPD (0.14) and negative correlation (−0.19), validating the need for the wrapper-based adaptation used throughout Delta-XAI.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Integrated Gradients (Sundararajan et al., 2017) lineage by redefining the integration path from a zero-baseline straight line to piecewise-linear trajectories over shifted historical windows, directly addressing the out-of-distribution problem that plagues standard IG in temporal settings. It also builds on TIMING (Jang et al., 2025) by adapting its sequential-removal evaluation metrics (CPD, CPP) to a prediction-difference wrapper, and unifies 14 prior time series XAI methods (FIT, WinIT, Dynamask, TimeX, TimeX++, ContraLSP, etc.) under a single framework. The key conceptual departure is shifting the explanation target from a single-time-step prediction to the *difference* between two adjacent predictions, which the authors argue is the practically relevant quantity in online monitoring but has not been formally addressed by prior time series XAI work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes time series explainability around prediction changes rather than static predictions, which is arguably the more clinically and operationally meaningful question in monitoring settings (e.g., why did sepsis risk drop from 90% to 70%?). The unified Delta-XAI framework and its evaluation suite (faithfulness, sufficiency, completeness, coherence, efficiency) provide a missing standard for comparing methods in the online setting. Limitations include the restriction to T2−T1 < W (overlapping windows), additional computational overhead over vanilla IG, and the fact that some baselines required non-trivial adaptation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-step prediction changes, very long sequences where the piecewise path becomes expensive, and whether the theoretical guarantees (completeness, skew-symmetry) hold under model drift or concept shift in truly online deployment.
