## Problem

Existing flow-based any-to-any generation methods (e.g., CoDi, OmniFlow) suffer from three coupled limitations: they require restrictive data pairing (text-anchored or fully-paired triplets), incur high computational cost from modeling joint multi-modal distributions, and depend on brittle multi-stage training pipelines that separately optimize alignment and generation components. This makes them impractical for scaling to diverse modality sets with limited paired data.

## Input / Output

FlowBind accepts any non-empty subset of modalities (text, image, audio, point cloud) as input and generates any other subset as output, supporting one-to-one, one-to-many, and many-to-one translation. All modalities are first projected into a shared 768-dimensional semantic latent space via frozen per-modality encoders (EmbeddingGemma for text, CLIP for images, CLAP for audio), and outputs are decoded back to pixel/waveform/token space by frozen per-modality decoders. At inference, the auxiliary encoder is discarded entirely; translation is performed purely by integrating learned per-modality ODEs backward (source→latent) and forward (latent→target), with multi-source conditioning aggregated by simple averaging in the shared latent.

## Method

FlowBind replaces the standard Gaussian prior with a learnable shared latent z* that captures cross-modal commonality, and connects each modality to this latent via its own invertible per-modality flow (drift network v_θ^i). All components—an auxiliary encoder H_φ producing z* and N drift networks—are trained jointly under a single flow-matching loss on partially paired data, with a critical stabilization trick: gradients through the encoder are stopped for t∈(0,1] but allowed at t=0, where the loss provably decomposes into unexplained conditional variance plus drift approximation error (Proposition 1), driving the encoder to minimize information loss across all modalities. At inference, any-to-any translation is achieved by solving the source modality's ODE backward to recover the shared latent, then solving the target modality's ODE forward, requiring no auxiliary encoder. The architecture uses lightweight MLPs with AdaLN-zero time modulation operating in compact 768-dim semantic representations, yielding a 568M-parameter model trainable in ~48 GPU-hours on H100s.

## Results

FlowBind achieves the best generation quality among all generalist models across all six one-to-one tasks: T→I FID of 17.39 (vs. OmniFlow 22.97, CoDi 24.80), I→T CIDEr of 46.26, T→A FAD of 4.19, A→T CIDEr of 55.11, I→A FAD of 2.50, and A→I FID of 26.60. Notably, FlowBind outperforms dedicated specialist models on image-to-audio (FAD 2.50 vs. Seeing&Hearing 5.31) and audio-to-image (FID 26.60 vs. Sound2Vision 42.55) without any modality-specific adjustments. The model achieves these results with only 568M trainable parameters (6× fewer than OmniFlow's 3.2B), 48 GPU-hours of training (10× less than OmniFlow's 480 GPU-hours), and 0.15% of CoDi's training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two primary flow-based generalist baselines, FlowBind wins on generation quality (FID/FAD/CIDEr) in all six one-to-one tasks. Versus OmniFlow (3.2B params, 480 GPU-hr), FlowBind (568M params, 48 GPU-hr) improves T→I FID from 22.97 to 17.39, I→A FAD from 5.67 to 2.50, and A→I FID from 106.03 to 26.60, while using 10× less compute and 1.79% of the training data. Versus CoDi (4.3B params), FlowBind improves I→T CIDEr from 16.40 to 46.26, A→T CIDEr from 6.62 to 55.11, and I→A FAD from 14.58 to 2.50, using only 0.15% of CoDi's data. On alignment metrics (Table 3), FlowBind leads in 4 of 6 tasks (I→T CLIP 29.74, T→A CLAP 29.08, I→A AIS 82.89, A→I AIS 78.17) but trails OmniFlow on T→I CLIP (28.35 vs. 31.52) and A→T CLAP (36.70 vs. 45.08), which the authors attribute to OmniFlow's initialization from strong text-anchored specialist models. Against specialist baselines, FlowBind surpasses Seeing&Hearing on I→A (FAD 2.50 vs. 5.31; AIS 82.89 vs. 75.11) and Sound2Vision on A→I (FID 26.60 vs. 42.55; AIS 78.17 vs. 62.39), while remaining competitive with FLUX.1 on T→I FID (17.39 vs. 22.06) despite being a generalist model. In many-to-one and one-to-many settings (Tables 4–5), FlowBind consistently outperforms both CoDi and OmniFlow on alignment scores, with particularly large gains on (T+I)→A CLAP (28.13 vs. 4.85/7.68) and I→(T+A) AIS (74.34 vs. 58.65/63.99).

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowBind extends the conditional flow matching framework (Lipman et al., 2023) and the direct data-to-data invertible flow paradigm (Liu et al., 2025; He et al., 2025) from two-modality settings to multi-modality any-to-any generation. It departs from CoDi (Tang et al., 2023), which anchors all modalities to a fixed text embedding and requires multi-stage training, and from OmniFlow (Li et al., 2025b), which models a joint velocity field across all modalities and depends on fully-paired data. The key conceptual departure is replacing the fixed Gaussian prior with a learnable shared latent that factorizes the multi-modal flow into independent per-modality drifts, trained jointly under a single flow-matching objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlowBind offers a practically significant efficiency gain—6× fewer parameters and 10× less compute than OmniFlow while achieving superior quality—by operating in compact semantic spaces and decomposing cross-modal alignment from intra-modal generation. The theoretical result (Proposition 1) showing that the flow-matching loss at t=0 reduces to minimizing conditional variance provides a clean, regularization-free mechanism for learning the shared latent, avoiding the contrastive losses used in prior direct-flow work. Limitations include reliance on strong frozen per-modality encoders/decoders (CLIP, CLAP, EmbeddingGemma), which constrains scalability to modalities lacking such infrastructure, and the shared latent is learned jointly rather than being a reusable fixed representation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video or higher-fidelity generation, and whether the shared latent can be pre-trained independently for plug-and-play modality addition.
