## Problem

Predicting multi-task learning (MTL) gains for arbitrary task groups is essential for efficient task grouping, but existing approaches face a cost-accuracy tradeoff: data-driven methods (e.g., MTGNet, HOA) require training many MTL models to obtain ground-truth gains, while white-box methods (e.g., TAG) are limited to pairwise estimates and incur high computational overhead from auxiliary forward/backward passes. No prior method combines the theoretical grounding of gradient-based affinity with the generalization power of data-driven models at low computational cost.

## Input / Output

ETAP consumes two inputs: (1) a single MTL training run over all n tasks, from which per-step gradients of shared parameters are extracted (already computed during standard backpropagation, so no extra forward passes are needed), and (2) a small set of 5–10 training task groups with measured ground-truth MTL gains. The output is a per-task predicted MTL gain for any candidate group, which is then fed into a branch-and-bound search to select up to B task groups maximizing total predicted gain. The affinity scores are computed as normalized dot products between task-specific gradient vectors, making the white-box stage essentially free beyond one standard MTL training run.

## Method

ETAP is a two-stage ensemble predictor. In the white-box stage, pairwise task-affinity scores are computed during a single MTL training run by measuring the alignment between task t_j's gradient on shared parameters and task t_i's parameter update (including momentum), normalized by t_j's loss; these are time-averaged over K steps and aggregated to group-level scores by averaging over all task pairs in the group. In the data-driven stage, a B-spline basis expansion maps group-level affinity scores to a higher-dimensional feature space, where a regularized linear regression (fitted via cross-validation on the small training set) produces initial MTL gain predictions; a second ridge-regression model, trained on multi-hot encoded task-group membership vectors, predicts and corrects residual errors between these initial predictions and ground-truth gains. The final prediction is the sum of the non-linear mapped affinity prediction and the residual correction, enabling accurate per-task gain estimation with far fewer training groups than purely data-driven baselines.

## Results

ETAP achieves the highest correlation with ground-truth MTL gains across all four datasets: 0.45 (CelebA), 0.84 (ETTm1), 0.50 (Chemical), and 0.74 (Ridership) with |G_train|=10, substantially outperforming TAG (0.10–0.47) and MTGNet (0.22–0.61). For end-to-end group selection, ETAP with 4 groups achieves total MTL loss of 49.41 (CelebA), 3.89 (ETTm1), 4.83 (Chemical), and 17.59 (Ridership), approaching the optimal values (48.38, 3.82, 4.67, 16.79) while requiring only 10 training groups. ETAP also reduces pairwise affinity computation runtime by 46–71% compared to TAG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ETAP outperforms TAG (Fifty et al., 2021) on all four datasets for both pairwise affinity correlation (e.g., 0.32 vs 0.16 on CelebA, 0.47 vs 0.43 on ETTm1) and group-level MTL gain prediction correlation (e.g., 0.45 vs 0.10 on CelebA, 0.84 vs 0.47 on ETTm1), while reducing runtime by 46–71%. Against MTGNet (Song et al., 2022), ETAP achieves higher and more stable prediction correlations with only 5–10 training groups (e.g., 0.41 vs 0.10 on CelebA, 0.77 vs 0.43 on ETTm1 at |G_train|=5), whereas MTGNet exhibits large variance and negative R² values at small training sizes. ETAP also surpasses GRAD-TAE (Li et al., 2024) on pairwise affinity correlation (0.32 vs 0.09 on CelebA; 0.47 vs −0.27 on ETTm1) and achieves lower group-selection loss on all datasets. Compared to the Linear Surrogate (Li et al., 2023), ETAP improves both correlation (0.57 vs 0.49 on CelebA) and F1 for positive/negative transfer classification (0.70 vs 0.61 on CelebA, 0.31 vs 0.18 on ETTm1). Finally, ETAP's group-based strategy outperforms PCGrad (Yu et al., 2020) in final MTL loss, reducing error by up to 7.4% on ETTm1 (3.89 vs 4.19) and 6.6% on Ridership (17.59 vs 18.84) with 4 groups.

## Relation to Prior Work

ETAP builds directly on TAG's (Fifty et al., 2021) gradient-based inter-task affinity measurement, but eliminates TAG's need for n additional forward/backward passes per step by reusing gradients already computed during standard MTL training. It then departs from purely white-box methods by layering a two-stage data-driven ensemble (B-spline non-linear mapping followed by ridge-regression residual correction) on top of the affinity scores, bridging the gap between TAG's linear pairwise estimates and the non-linear, higher-order interactions that data-driven methods like MTGNet and HOA capture at much higher computational cost. The framework also positions itself against GRAD-TAE (Li et al., 2024) and Linear Surrogate (Li et al., 2023), which rely on extensive MTL training for ground-truth gains, and against PCGrad (Yu et al., 2020), which resolves gradient conflicts during training rather than pre-selecting groups.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: ETAP achieves stable, competitive MTL gain predictions with as few as 5 training groups, where data-driven baselines like MTGNet are unstable or require substantially more supervision. This lowers the barrier to effective task grouping in real-world MTL deployments where training many MTL models for ground-truth gains is prohibitive. However, the evaluation is limited to small task sets (7–10 tasks), the residual model's multi-hot encoding captures only first-order task membership effects, and the shared-encoder MTL architecture assumption may not generalize to more complex topologies. Open questions include scalability to hundreds of tasks, integration with online or streaming task addition, and whether the branch-and-bound search remains the optimal integration point as group budgets grow.
