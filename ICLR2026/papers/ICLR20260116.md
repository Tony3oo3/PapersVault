## Problem

Existing multi-agent simulation (MAS) methods for generating multi-turn agentic dialogue data are computationally expensive due to sequential turn-by-turn autoregressive generation, offer limited control over task complexity and dialogue length, and critically lack global context awareness for the assistant role—making it difficult to ensure tool-use consistency, factual accuracy, and long-term planning coherence across the full trajectory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260116/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sampled candidate tool list from a predefined pool and task specifications (a set of subtasks with associated required tools and step counts per subtask) as input, and produces full multi-turn conversational trajectories C = (o⁰, a¹, o¹, …, aⁿ) where actions alternate between function calls and natural language responses, and observations alternate between tool outputs and user replies. A key structural constraint is that tool calls and their outputs are generated in parallel within each sub-trajectory to ensure consistency, and user queries are required to contain all necessary parameter values upfront. The generated trajectories (8,000 instances in experiments) are then used as supervised fine-tuning data via LoRA for downstream tool-calling LLMs.

## Method

ToolACE-MT extends the non-autoregressive generation paradigm (from NAT and masked diffusion) to the dialogue turn level through a three-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (Coarse-Grained Initialization) generates a structurally complete but semantically coarse skeleton by composing subtask trajectories sequentially, with tool calls and outputs produced in parallel to guarantee consistency. Stage 2 (Iterative Refinement) applies two alternating mask-and-fill operations: Complexity Injection replaces masked turns with realistic complications (clarification questions, tool-awareness failures, error simulations, chitchat) via a mask-and-extend operation, while Reasonability Refinement randomly masks non-adjacent turns and regenerates them with an LLM, using a separate LLM judger to accept or reject each revision. Stage 3 (Offline Verification) applies a hybrid of rule-based checks (format compliance, executability, repetition, ID hallucination) and model-based checks (decomposed sub-questions handled by independent LLM checking experts) to filter invalid samples. The key architectural insight is that by generating the full trajectory non-autoregressively and refining it iteratively, the assistant role retains access to global task structure and inter-step dependencies—something impossible in autoregressive MAS where each turn is produced without knowledge of the overall plan.

## Results

On BFCL-v3, ToolACE-MT achieves 40.25% multi-turn function-calling accuracy, a 31% absolute improvement over the base Llama3.1-8B-Inst (9.25%) and surpassing larger models including Llama3.1-70B (12.50%) and DeepSeek-V3 (29.87%). On ACEBench, ToolACE-MT reaches 51.0% multi-turn accuracy and 34.0% Agent Process Accuracy, compared to 48.0% and 15.0% for the MAS baseline. On τ-Bench, ToolACE-MT achieves 20.6% average Pass@1 (Retail 25.2%, Airline 16.0%), outperforming MAS (15.9% avg). The method also reduces data generation cost by ~20% (188k vs. 275k API calls) while yielding a higher verification pass rate (72.3% vs. 61.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base Llama3.1-8B-Inst, ToolACE-MT achieves a 31% absolute gain in multi-turn accuracy (40.25% vs. 9.25%), demonstrating that the generated data substantially enhances agentic capabilities. Compared to the Multi-Agent Simulation (MAS) baseline from ToolFlow (Wang et al., 2025), ToolACE-MT wins on every multi-turn subcategory of BFCL-v3 (e.g., 57.50% vs. 46.50% on Base, 31.50% vs. 19.00% on Miss Func) and on ACEBench Agent Process Accuracy (34.0% vs. 15.0%), while also requiring ~32% fewer API calls for data generation. Against ToolACE-10k (Liu et al., 2025, primarily single-turn data), ToolACE-MT achieves 40.3% vs. 8.6% on BFCL multi-turn and 34.0% vs. 14.0% on ACEBench Agent-PA, confirming the advantage of multi-turn-specific training data. ToolACE-MT also outperforms the much larger Llama3.1-70B-Inst (40.25% vs. 12.50% multi-turn) and DeepSeek-V3 (40.25% vs. 29.87%), though it remains below GPT-4o (50.00%) and slightly above Gemini-2.5-Pro (34.62%). On τ-Bench, ToolACE-MT leads MAS by 4.7 points in average Pass@1 (20.6% vs. 15.9%), with the gap driven primarily by the Retail domain (25.2% vs. 21.7%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the non-autoregressive generation paradigm—originally developed for token-level machine translation (Gu et al., ICLR 2018) and iterative mask-and-fill decoding (Ghazvininejad et al., EMNLP 2019)—to the turn level of multi-turn agentic dialogue construction. It departs from the dominant multi-agent simulation (MAS) approach (ToolFlow, APIGen-MT) where LLMs sequentially role-play user, assistant, and tool in autoregressive back-and-forth, replacing it with a parallel coarse-to-fine pipeline: structurally complete skeleton generation followed by iterative mask-and-extend refinement. The closest prior work, APIGen-MT (Prabhakar et al., 2025), also uses a two-stage process but still relies on autoregressive MAS in its second stage, whereas ToolACE-MT eliminates sequential agent interaction entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that non-autoregressive, globally-aware generation can outperform autoregressive MAS on multi-turn function-calling benchmarks (40.25% vs. 31.38% on BFCL-v3) while reducing API costs by ~30% and improving verification pass rates (72.3% vs. 61.1%), offering a practical efficiency win for teams scaling agentic data pipelines. Open questions remain: whether the approach generalizes to even longer or more open-ended dialogues, whether the quality ceiling imposed by the generator LLM (GPT-4o vs. GPT-4o-mini gap) can be closed, and whether the non-autoregressive paradigm extends beyond function-calling to broader agentic settings like embodied or web navigation. The persistently low Agent End-to-End Accuracy for 8B models (8.4%) also signals that data quality alone may not suffice for the hardest agentic tasks.
