## Problem

Existing approaches to instilling cognitive reasoning skills (verification, retrying) into LLMs require distillation from stronger teacher models, extensive pre-training, or assume the base model already exhibits these behaviors. This paper asks how to prime a model to acquire such skills during RL without access to a superior model, by leveraging the model's own outputs restructured into skill-demonstrating traces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262844/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a base model's own sampled solutions (64 per question across 4 diverse CoT prompts) and self-generated reflections, then assembles them into structured SFT traces wrapped in <sample>, <reflect>, <answer>, and <verdict> tags with glue phrases between attempts. The trained model produces longer reasoning traces with explicit verification and retry patterns, always terminating in a final answer within <answer> tags; at inference time, budget forcing can append a <sample> trigger to extend reasoning up to 8,192 tokens.

## Method

SkillFactory is a three-stage pipeline: (1) data curation samples diverse solutions from the base model, generates reflections that correctly judge each solution's validity, then shuffles correct and incorrect solution-reflection pairs into traces that always end on a correct answer (Algorithm 1); (2) SFT on these 'silver' traces for two epochs at low learning rate, explicitly not expected to improve task accuracy but to internalize the structural pattern of sample-reflect-retry; (3) RL via GRPO with binary correctness rewards on held-out questions, which refines when and how the primed skills are deployed. The key insight is that the *structure* of skill usage (explicit tags, ordered attempts, verification steps) matters more than the quality of individual reasoning steps, and that a model primed with this structure generalizes to harder task variants and out-of-domain tasks post-RL even when its pre-RL accuracy is lower than distillation baselines.

## Results

On harder Countdown variants (4–6 arg) after training only on Countdown-3arg, SkillFactory→GRPO achieves 25.1% accuracy, the highest among all methods, outperforming R1 Distill→GRPO (21.2%) by +3.9 points and RL-Only (15.8%) by +9.3 points. On out-of-domain tasks (Acronym, Letter CD, Multiplication, CSQA, GSM8K), SkillFactory→GRPO reaches 35.7% overall, nearly matching R1 Distill→GRPO (35.9%). In the OpenThoughts setting, SkillFactory with just 1k SFT rows achieves 42.1% overall on GPQA/AIME25/AMC/Math500, closely approaching QwQ distillation with 10k rows (42.5%), and surpassing it on AMC (37.5% vs 36.5%) and Math500 (64.6% vs 58.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SkillFactory→GRPO clearly outperforms RL-Only on harder Countdown variants (25.1% vs 15.8%) and overall OOD (35.7% vs 31.9%), and similarly dominates BOLT→GRPO (25.1% vs 13.7% on Countdown; 35.7% vs 33.0% overall) and STaR→GRPO (25.1% vs 9.7% on Countdown; 35.7% vs 30.2% overall). Against the strongest baseline, R1 Distill→GRPO, SkillFactory wins on Countdown (25.1% vs 21.2%) and Acronym (12.1% vs 6.0%) but trails slightly on Multiplication (35.0% vs 37.1%), CSQA (60.8% vs 63.8%), and GSM8K (68.2% vs 72.9%), resulting in a near-tie on overall OOD (35.7% vs 35.9%). In the OpenThoughts setting, SkillFactory with only 1k SFT rows (42.1% overall) nearly matches QwQ distillation with 10k rows (42.5%), and surpasses it on AMC (37.5% vs 36.5%) and Math500 (64.6% vs 58.6%), though it lags on AIME25 (9.7% vs 15.3%) and GPQA (56.7% vs 59.5%). Notably, SkillFactory's SFT-stage accuracy on Countdown is much lower than R1 Distill's (2.8% vs 11.7%), yet this relationship reverses after RL, suggesting that structural priming matters more than raw task performance pre-RL.

## Relation to Prior Work

SkillFactory sits at the intersection of self-distillation (STaR, BOLT) and RL-based reasoning training (GRPO, DeepSeek-R1), but departs from both in a key way: rather than filtering to correct outputs (STaR) or requiring a stronger teacher model (R1 distillation, QwQ), it restructures the model's own diverse samples—both correct and incorrect—into traces that explicitly demonstrate cognitive skills like verification and retry. This builds on Gandhi et al.'s observation that RL amplifies pre-existing cognitive behaviors and Li et al.'s finding that trace structure matters more than content, but the novelty is in showing that the skill structure can be synthesized from the model's own imperfect outputs without any external teacher. The method is conceptually closest to BOLT's bootstrap philosophy but differs in using structured tags and reflection steps rather than in-context learning from a strong model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that teams without access to frontier reasoning models can still prime smaller models for effective RL by restructuring their own outputs, lowering the barrier to skill acquisition. The finding that lower SFT accuracy can yield higher post-RL performance challenges the common assumption that SFT should maximize task accuracy before RL. However, the gains over strong baselines (R1 Distill→GRPO) are modest (2–4 points on Countdown, roughly tied on OOD), the OpenThoughts setting shows SkillFactory does not clearly beat QwQ distillation at 10k scale, and only two cognitive skills are demonstrated. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models, whether it generalizes to more complex or compositional skills, and whether the 'priming' effect persists as base models already exhibit stronger latent reasoning.
