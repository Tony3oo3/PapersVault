## Problem

Existing unbiased (distortion-free) watermarking schemes for LLMs preserve the original output distribution but suffer from inherently weak statistical signals, requiring long token sequences for reliable detection and remaining vulnerable to paraphrasing, smoothing, and truncation attacks. The paper asks whether the detection signal can be amplified without sacrificing the unbiasedness guarantee that keeps generation quality intact.

## Input / Output

At generation time, the method takes a pretrained LM distribution P_M, a text prompt, n independent secret keys (sk_1,…,sk_n), a logits reweight strategy F, and a hash function h applied to prefix n-grams to produce per-step watermark keys; it outputs a watermarked token sequence sampled from the n-fold composed distribution. At detection time, given the generated sequence and all n keys, the method computes n per-key detector scores (e.g., DiPmark green-token fractions) and aggregates them into a single ensemble score, from which a p-value and binary watermark/no-watermark decision are derived under a chosen false-positive-rate threshold.

## Method

ENS recursively composes n independent applications of a base unbiased logits reweight function F, each governed by a distinct secret key, so that the final sampling distribution is F(…F(F(P_M, k_1), k_2)…k_n). The paper proves by induction that this sequential composition remains unbiased in expectation over the key space, since F is unbiased for *any* input distribution and the keys are i.i.d. At detection, the base detector is run n times with different keys and the per-key scores are summed, yielding an approximate √n signal-to-noise-ratio gain under conditional independence. A key theoretical trade-off is identified: the promoted token set shrinks as γ^n|V| under strict intersection, so the per-key effect size decays as (εγ)^n, making the optimal ensemble size n* ≈ 1/(2 log(1/εγ)) rather than unbounded. Empirically, ENS-MCMark (n=3) achieves the highest TPR and lowest p-values across Llama-3.2-3B, Mistral-7B, and Phi-3.5-mini on C4, MMW, Dolly CW, and WaterBench, while remaining robust to GPT-4o-mini paraphrasing, DIPPER paraphrasing, back-translation, and random token replacement attacks.

## Results

On the 500-token C4 text generation task, ENS-MCMark(l=20, n=3) achieves the highest detection performance across all baselines: 99.89% TPR at 0.1% FPR, 99.57% TPR at 0.01% FPR, and 98.59% TPR at 0.001% FPR, with a median p-value of 2.58×10⁻³¹. At the shorter 250-token setting, ENS-MCMark reaches 95.70% TPR@0.1% FPR (p-value 1.43×10⁻¹⁴), surpassing MCMark alone (94.36%, 4.18×10⁻¹³) and SynthID(m=30) (93.83%, 1.91×10⁻¹²). For the weaker base watermarks, ensembling yields dramatic gains: ENS-DiPmark(α=0.3, n=5) nearly doubles TPR@0.1% from 41.48% to 75.05% at 250 tokens, and ENS-γ-reweight(n=5) lifts TPR from 52.00% to 73.98%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiPmark, ensembling with n=5 nearly doubles TPR@0.1% FPR at 250 tokens (75.05% vs. 41.48% for α=0.3; 74.74% vs. 49.90% for α=0.4) and pushes 500-token TPR above 94%, while reducing median p-values by 3–4 orders of magnitude. Against γ-reweight, ENS-γ-reweight(n=5) improves 250-token TPR@0.1% from 52.00% to 73.98% and 500-token TPR from 81.26% to 94.12%. Against the strongest sampling-based baseline SynthID(m=30), ENS-MCMark(l=20, n=3) achieves higher TPR at every FPR threshold (95.70% vs. 93.83% at 250 tokens; 99.89% vs. 99.24% at 500 tokens) and a substantially lower median p-value (1.43×10⁻¹⁴ vs. 1.91×10⁻¹²). Against MCMark alone, the ensemble variant (n=3) yields a modest but consistent gain: 95.70% vs. 94.36% TPR@0.1% at 250 tokens and 99.89% vs. 99.34% at 500 tokens, with the p-value improving from 4.18×10⁻¹³ to 1.43×10⁻¹⁴. Under robustness attacks (GPT-4o-mini paraphrase, DIPPER, back-translation, 10% token replacement), ENS-MCMark consistently achieves the highest TPR and lowest p-values among all methods tested, e.g., 84.17% TPR@0.1% under back-translation versus 75.69% for SynthID and 38.74% for ENS-DiPmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the logits-based unbiased watermarking line initiated by γ-reweight (Hu et al., 2023), refined by DiPmark (Wu et al., 2023), and further improved by MCmark (Chen et al., 2025). Rather than designing a new base reweighting rule, ENS introduces a general composition operator that stacks n independent instances of any existing unbiased reweighting function F, proving that the sequential composition preserves the marginal unbiasedness guarantee. The framework is orthogonal to the base watermark choice and also applies to sampling-based methods in principle, though the paper's analysis and experiments focus on the logits-based setting. Conceptually, it reframes the weak-signal limitation of unbiased watermarks as an aggregation problem solvable by key diversity rather than by altering the per-token perturbation magnitude.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Unbiased watermarking is the most deployment-ready class of LM watermarks because it provably preserves output quality, yet its inherently weak statistical signal has been a persistent practical bottleneck. ENS offers a plug-and-play, theoretically grounded remedy that practitioners can layer on top of any existing logits-based unbiased watermark without retraining or re-architecting the generator. However, robustness under strong paraphrasing (GPT-4o-mini, DIPPER) remains limited—TPR drops to single or low-double digits for most variants—and the non-monotonic detectability curve with ensemble size (driven by promotion-set sparsity) means the optimal n is method- and parameter-dependent, requiring careful tuning. Open questions include whether adaptive or non-strict-intersection ensemble designs can push robustness further, and how the framework extends to multi-modal or streaming watermarking settings.
