## Problem

The prevailing approach to distilling reasoning from LLMs—behavioral cloning from Chain-of-Thought rationales—teaches small language models to mimic surface-level textual patterns rather than the underlying algorithmic structure of thought, producing brittle models that generate logically inconsistent arguments on novel problems. This paper argues that distillation should transfer the structural blueprint of reasoning directly, not the text itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262717/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text question as input and produces a step-by-step reasoning trace in which each step is generated conditioned on a predicted semantic signal (one of 7 categories such as Contrast, Addition, or Conclusion), followed by a final answer. Training data consists of long-thinking traces from teacher LLMs (DeepSeek-R1, GPT-oss-120B) segmented into individual steps and labeled with semantic signals via a hybrid keyword-matching and GPT-4.1 validation pipeline. At inference, an adaptive confidence threshold τ determines whether to continue reasoning or switch to a 'Response Generation' signal to terminate the trace early.

## Method

The framework has three components: (1) Logic Representation Distillation, which segments teacher reasoning traces into steps and assigns each a semantic signal from a 7-category taxonomy derived from discourse markers (e.g., 'but' → Contrast, 'in addition' → Addition); (2) a dual-branch training architecture where Branch 1 inserts a Signal Embedding Layer before the LM head to condition next-token generation on the current semantic signal, while Branch 2 uses a dedicated head to predict the next signal, with the combined objective L = (1−β)L_token + βL_signal serving as a regularizer for logical coherence; (3) adaptive signal-guided inference that checks the signal predictor's confidence against threshold τ and triggers early termination via the 'Response Generation' signal when confidence is low. The student is trained with LoRA (r=16) on Qwen2.5 base models, and the core insight is that forcing the model to anticipate the logical function of the next step before generating its content compels internalization of reasoning structure rather than mere text imitation.

## Results

On the 14B Qwen2.5 model, Reasoning Scaffolding (Teacher=DeepSeek-R1) achieves Pass@1 scores of 0.858 (StrategyQA), 0.887 (CommonsenseQA), 0.917 (TruthfulQA), 0.942 (GSM8K), and 0.928 (MATH-500), representing an average ~14% improvement over the original base model and ~8% over CoT/Long-Thinking SFT baselines. The method is effective across all model scales: the 0.5B model jumps from 0.268 to 0.861 on TruthfulQA, and the 7B model reaches 0.922 on MATH-500. Signal prediction accuracy exceeds 85% with the adaptive strategy on the 14B model, and the method outperforms the official DeepSeek-distilled model on all QA benchmarks while matching or exceeding it on math.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard CoT distillation baseline (Shridhar et al., 2023, implemented as CoT SFT), Reasoning Scaffolding wins on all five benchmarks for both 7B and 14B models — e.g., on the 14B model it improves TruthfulQA from 0.831 to 0.917 (+8.6 pts) and MATH-500 from 0.882 to 0.928 (+4.6 pts). Against the official DeepSeek-distilled model (Long Thinking Distill), the gap is most dramatic on QA tasks: the distilled model scores only 0.544 on TruthfulQA and 0.670 on CommonsenseQA (7B), while Reasoning Scaffolding reaches 0.879 and 0.866 respectively, suggesting that standard distillation optimized for math/code transfers poorly to commonsense reasoning. Against Long Thinking SFT (the strongest SFT baseline), Reasoning Scaffolding still leads on StrategyQA (0.858 vs. 0.768), TruthfulQA (0.917 vs. 0.812), and MATH-500 (0.928 vs. 0.901) at the 14B scale. The method also outperforms the Llama3.1-8B baseline on all benchmarks, confirming cross-architecture generalizability. Notably, the 0.5B student with Reasoning Scaffolding (0.861 on TruthfulQA) surpasses the 7B CoT SFT model (0.752), demonstrating that structural distillation can compensate for a 14× parameter gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reasoning distillation literature (Shridhar et al., 2023; Ho et al., 2022) by departing from the standard behavioral-cloning paradigm that fine-tunes SLMs on teacher-generated CoT rationales. It builds explicitly on Concept Bottleneck LLMs (Sun et al., 2025), which introduce a concept bottleneck for token-level decoding, by lifting the bottleneck to the level of discrete semantic discourse signals (e.g., Contrast, Conclusion) that govern step-to-step reasoning flow. The multi-task training objective—jointly predicting the next semantic signal and generating the corresponding reasoning step—draws on the structural-coherence findings of Li et al. (2025a) but operationalizes them as an explicit, interpretable scaffold rather than an implicit property of the training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a widely acknowledged weakness of CoT distillation—student models that are fluent but logically brittle—by showing that a lightweight structural regularizer (7 discourse-level signals) can substantially improve both accuracy and logical consistency across diverse benchmarks and model scales. However, the signals remain at the discourse-marker level rather than encoding formal logical or algorithmic operations, and the annotation pipeline still depends on an LLM oracle (GPT-4.1) for validation, limiting full self-supervision. Open questions include whether finer-grained logical signals (e.g., modus tollens, quantifier scope) could further close the gap to formal reasoning, and whether the framework generalizes to non-textual or multi-modal reasoning tasks.
