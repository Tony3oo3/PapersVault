## Problem

Existing anchor-based multi-view clustering methods lack probabilistic interpretability: they do not assign meaningful probability associations to the anchor graph or the soft labels of data points, nor do they analyze the relationship between input data and final clustering results in a unified manner. This omission complicates the acquisition of k distinct connected components in the bipartite graph and limits the end-to-end explainability of the clustering pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method takes V multi-view feature matrices X_v ∈ R^{d_v × n} (where n is the number of data points and d_v is the view-specific dimension) along with the number of clusters k. It outputs a probability transition matrix G ∈ R^{c × n} from data points to categories, where each column sums to one and the argmax of each column yields the hard cluster label. Internally, the anchor graph S ∈ R^{m × n} is constrained to be a valid probability transition matrix (non-negative with column sums equal to 1), and the anchor-to-category matrix H ∈ R^{c × m} carries the same constraint, giving every intermediate matrix a direct probabilistic interpretation.

## Method

UEMCP learns a shared consensus anchor matrix A ∈ R^{d × m} and view-specific orthogonal projection matrices P_v that jointly reconstruct each view as X_v ≈ P_v A S, where S is the anchor graph interpreted as a data-to-anchor transition probability matrix. The key insight is that the data-to-category transition probability G is factorized as G = HS (anchor-to-category times data-to-anchor), and the Frobenius-norm loss ‖HS − G‖_F² enforces this probabilistic chain in a single end-to-end optimization. View weights α_v are adaptively learned via a Cauchy-Schwarz closed form based on per-view reconstruction error, so views contributing more to the consensus anchor receive higher weight. The non-convex problem is solved by alternating minimization: S is updated column-wise via a quadratic program with simplex constraints, A and P_v are updated via SVD-based orthogonal Procrustes solutions, G is updated by SVD of HS, H is updated by nearest-anchor assignment, and α_v by the closed-form ratio. The overall time complexity is O(n) in the number of data points, making it suitable for large-scale multi-view datasets.

## Results

UEMCP achieves the best ACC across all seven datasets, reaching 68.00% on Caltech101-20, 50.27% on Scene15, 31.50% on SUNRGBD, 29.60% on Reuters, 23.56% on NUSWIDEOBJ, 10.62% on AWA, and 10.28% on NoisyMNIST. On NMI, UEMCP leads on six of seven datasets (e.g., 42.70% on Scene15, 29.95% on Reuters) but trails FPMVS on Caltech101-20 (53.60% vs. 63.28%). The method also attains the highest Purity and F1-score on all datasets, with F1-score improvements of up to 3.94% over the next-best method (EDMC) on Caltech101-20 (62.40% vs. 58.46%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UEMCP outperforms all eight baselines on ACC across every dataset. Against the closest competitor EDMC (Qin et al., 2024), UEMCP gains 2.27–4.53% ACC (e.g., 50.27% vs. 48.00% on Scene15, 31.50% vs. 27.00% on SUNRGBD, 10.28% vs. 9.00% on NoisyMNIST). SFMC (Li et al., 2022) and BMVC (Zhang et al., 2019) are substantially weaker, with SFMC reaching at most 59.50% ACC on Caltech101-20 and BMVC at most 29.80% on Scene15. LMVSC (Kang et al., 2020) also trails UEMCP by 12–19% ACC on most datasets. Notably, UEMCP is the only method that avoids out-of-memory failures on all seven datasets, whereas ETLMSC and SFMC fail on NUSWIDEOBJ, AWA, and NoisyMNIST. The sole exception is NMI on Caltech101-20, where FPMVS (63.28%) surpasses UEMCP (53.60%), suggesting the probabilistic transition formulation may underperform on smaller, well-separated datasets for that specific metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

UEMCP extends the anchor graph-based multi-view clustering lineage that originated with Li et al. (2015) and was refined by subsequent works (Li et al. 2022, Xia et al. 2023, You et al. 2023) to address the O(n²)–O(n³) bottleneck of full graph methods. Its key departure is assigning explicit probabilistic meaning to both the anchor graph (as a data-to-anchor transition matrix) and the soft labels (as a data-to-category transition matrix), and learning a shared category transition matrix across views in a single end-to-end optimization rather than through separate anchor selection and graph construction stages. This contrasts with prior anchor methods that treat the bipartite graph as a purely structural object and rely on post-hoc spectral clustering or Laplacian rank constraints to extract connected components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The probabilistic reinterpretation offers a more principled and interpretable framework for anchor-based multi-view clustering, potentially guiding future work on uncertainty-aware or Bayesian extensions. However, the empirical gains over the strongest baseline (EDMC) are modest (1–5 percentage points across metrics), and the method still assumes complete, view-aligned data—extending it to incomplete or partially aligned multi-view settings remains open. Whether the probabilistic framing yields tangible benefits beyond interpretability (e.g., better calibration of cluster confidence, principled model selection for the number of clusters) is not explored and represents a natural next step.
