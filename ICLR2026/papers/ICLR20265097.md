## Problem

Robust state estimation in RL from noisy multimodal observations typically relies on explicit probabilistic noise models (Bayesian filters, VAEs) that require known noise distributions and restrict the state estimate to a parametric form, limiting generalization to unseen corruptions. This paper asks whether uncertainty can instead be captured geometrically—via a learned metric space where distances encode action-based temporal proximity—enabling noise-agnostic sensor fusion without any prior knowledge of the corruption family.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265097/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N sensor modalities (RGB and depth images at 84×84 for MuJoCo or 128×128 for Fetch, plus variable-size 6-D point clouds for Fetch), each stacked over 3 consecutive frames to ensure Markovianity, along with the previous action for the transition model. It produces a 64-dimensional latent state estimate z in a shared metric space, which is then fed to a SAC policy to output the next action. Notably, the fusion step is a closed-form inverse-distance-weighted average of per-modality encodings relative to the transition prediction, requiring no learned fusion network.

## Method

METRICMM learns a shared latent metric space Z where Euclidean distances correlate with the minimum number of actions needed to transition between environment states, using per-modality encoders (3-layer CNNs for images, a compact SetTransformer for point clouds) and a latent transition model φ_T. Sensor fusion is performed via inverse distance weighting: each modality's encoding is weighted by the inverse of its distance to the transition prediction, so corrupted observations (far from the prediction) are automatically down-weighted without explicit noise modeling. Training uses four losses combined linearly: a contrastive temporal distance loss (pulling adjacent states to unit distance and pushing random pairs apart), a latent transition consistency loss, and a multimodal invariance loss aligning all encoders to the same metric space. The key insight is that transition-model uncertainty is local in the action-induced metric space, so geometric distance to the prediction serves as a sufficient uncertainty signal, eliminating the need for probabilistic observation models or noise augmentation during training.

## Results

On Fetch-Slide with Failure corruption applied simultaneously to two of three modalities, METRICMM maintains a return of 9.26 at 10% corruption probability and remains positive (1.33) even at 90% corruption, while all baselines drop below zero by 50–75% corruption. On Fetch-PickAndPlace under Patch corruption to two modalities, METRICMM achieves 1.91 at 10% and 0.92 at 75% corruption, whereas the best baseline (AMDF) falls to −1.75 at 75%. Across seven corruption families on six MuJoCo locomotion tasks, METRICMM exhibits the flattest performance degradation curve, preserving a large fraction of clean returns up to high perturbation probabilities. Under single-modality failure at probability 1.0 on Fetch-Slide, METRICMM retains returns of 7.29–8.90 regardless of which modality is dropped, while baselines show a systematic overreliance on RGB/depth (returns near 7–9 when only point clouds are corrupted, but dropping to −1 to −4 when image or depth is lost).

## Baselines & Comparisons

METRICMM outperforms all four baselines across every corruption family and severity level tested. CURL (contrastive learning on augmented views) and GMC (cross-modal contrastive alignment) are competitive at low corruption probabilities (e.g., CURL scores 7.43 vs. METRICMM's 9.26 at 10% Failure on Fetch-Slide) but collapse sharply once the corrupted modality dominates the fused signal, dropping below zero by 50–75% corruption. AMDF (attention-weighted differentiable Bayesian filter) starts from the weakest clean performance (3.05 at 10%) and degrades fastest among the probabilistic baselines, falling to −2.41 at 99% corruption. CORAL (reconstruction + temporal contrastive alignment) shows moderate degradation but still loses 4–6 points of return relative to METRICMM at mid-to-high severities. A key qualitative finding is that baselines exhibit a systematic overreliance on RGB/depth: when only point clouds are corrupted at probability 1.0, most baselines retain returns of 7–9 (indicating they barely use 3D structure), whereas METRICMM shows a more uniform sensitivity profile (7.29–8.90 across all single-modality drops), confirming that its invariance loss distributes credit meaningfully across all sensor modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

METRICMM extends the line of metric-learning-for-RL work (Steccanella & Jonsson 2022; Park et al. 2023, 2024; Eysenbach et al. 2022; Wang et al. 2023) that constructs latent spaces where distances encode temporal/action-based proximity, but repurposes this geometry for a different goal: uncertainty-aware multimodal state estimation rather than policy learning or planning. It departs from Bayesian filtering and probabilistic state-space approaches (Deep Kalman Filters, RSSM, α-MDF) by eliminating explicit noise distributions and generative models entirely, replacing them with a deterministic inverse-distance-weighting fusion in a learned metric space. The multimodal alignment loss draws on cross-modal contrastive methods (GMC, CORAL), while the encoder architectures (CNN + Set Transformer) follow standard practice in the field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a purely geometric, noise-agnostic formulation can match or exceed explicit probabilistic filters across seven unseen corruption families and multi-modality failure settings, without any noise-aware training—a practical simplification for robotics deployment where noise characteristics are unknown or non-stationary. Key limitations include the symmetric-metric assumption (preventing exact quasimetric capture of irreversible dynamics) and the deterministic-dynamics requirement, both of which constrain applicability to stochastic or highly asymmetric environments. Open questions include extending the framework to stochastic transitions (possibly via distributional divergences instead of norms), non-stationary environments, adversarial robustness, and validation on real-world robotic hardware.
