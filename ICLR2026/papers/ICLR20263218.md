## Problem

Existing LLM training libraries optimize for large parameter counts (via ZeRO-3/FSDP, Tensor/Pipeline parallelism) but provide no easy-to-use abstractions for long-context training. Sequence parallelism (SP) is the key enabler for scaling input lengths, yet integrating it requires invasive manual code refactoring—inserting communication collectives, managing activation layouts, and ensuring forward/backward correctness—making it impractical for diverse architectures and hardware. The paper asks whether SP can be lifted into a deep-learning compiler stack to enable fully automated, PyTorch-native sequence parallelism.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard PyTorch transformer model (Llama-2/3.1/3.2 variants with GQA or full attention), preprocessed token sequence batches of shape [batch, seq_length], and a user-specified SP group size. The user interface requires only a few lines: registering the compiler passes, initializing distributed training, and calling model.compile(). The output is a compiled distributed SP training pipeline that automatically manages activation layouts, inserts all-to-all collectives, and ensures forward/backward correctness, extending the maximum trainable sequence length by up to 2.7× (NVIDIA) and 2.5× (AMD) over hand-written baselines at negligible throughput cost.

## Method

AutoSP introduces two compiler passes within PyTorch 2.0's Dynamo stack. The first is an automated SP pass operating on Torch-IR (chosen over Aten-IR because it preserves layer-level operators like linear and attention, making buffer-resizing and collective-insertion tractable). It performs program analysis to extract batch, sequence, and model dimensions from input nodes and attention outputs, then transforms the graph by: (a) inserting all-to-all collectives before and after attention layers to toggle between sequence-sharded and head-sharded layouts, (b) resizing intermediate buffers to partial sequence length for MLP/linear layers while keeping full sequence for attention, and (c) recomputing manually indexed tensors such as causal masks. The second pass is a sequence-aware activation checkpointing (SAC) strategy that modifies PyTorch 2.0's min-cut-based AC by removing the conservative infinite-capacity edges that ban rematerialization of compute-heavy operators (mat-muls, linear projections) outside attention layers. This is justified by the observation that at long contexts (s ≫ d, h, d_ffn), the FLOP fraction of linear/MLP operations is O(1/s), making their rematerialization nearly free in runtime while yielding large memory savings.

## Results

AutoSP extends the maximum trainable sequence length by up to 2.7× on NVIDIA (GH200) and 2.5× on AMD (MI250) hardware compared to hand-written DeepSpeed-Ulysses, at 97–98% of the baseline's per-iteration speed. On 8× A100-80GB GPUs, AutoSP enables up to 5.6× longer sequences than ZeRO-3 (FSDP) and up to 3× longer than both DS-Ulysses and RingAttention (on the 8B model), while reducing per-iteration time by 5× versus ZeRO-3. The SP-aware AC pass alone contributes an additional 1.66× trainability gain over the SP pass with only a 7% runtime cost, and reduces attention/MLP activation memory by 13.03× and 2.22× respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ZeRO-3 (FSDP) compiled with torch.compile(), AutoSP wins decisively on both axes: it trains 5–5.6× longer sequences (3B/8B models) while running 5× faster per iteration, because ZeRO-3's parameter-sharding communication overhead is far heavier than SP's all-to-all at attention boundaries. Against hand-written DeepSpeed-Ulysses, AutoSP achieves up to 3× longer trainable sequences (8B model) at 97–98% of the baseline's per-iteration speed on NVIDIA and 87–97% on AMD, with the gap attributable to the SP-aware AC pass rematerializing compute-heavy mat-muls that the hand-written baseline stores. Against RingAttention, AutoSP similarly extends trainability by up to 3× (8B) with negligible runtime cost; RingAttention itself is slower than DS-Ulysses due to its p-step ring communication pattern exchanging key/value blocks sequentially across SP groups, whereas Ulysses uses a single all-to-all. The trainability advantage over both SP baselines is most pronounced for the 8B model, where large hidden-dimension activations from linear projections and MLPs dominate memory, and least pronounced for the 13B model where optimizer states already consume ~50% of available memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work lifting distributed training strategies into PyTorch 2.0's compiler stack—exemplified by SimpleFSDP and DeepCompile, which automate ZeRO-3/FSDP—as a series of compiler passes. However, those prior efforts target parameter-count scaling, not long-context training. AutoSP departs by lifting DeepSpeed-Ulysses sequence parallelism (a fundamentally different sharding axis: sequence vs. parameters) into the same compiler ecosystem, operating at Torch-IR rather than Aten-IR to preserve layer-level semantics. It also modifies PyTorch 2.0's existing min-cut-based activation checkpointing pass (Chillee) by relaxing its conservative no-rematerialization constraint on compute-heavy operators, justified by an analytical FLOP-ratio argument specific to long sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AutoSP reduces the engineering barrier to long-context training from invasive manual code refactoring (inserting all-to-alls, managing activation layouts, handling backward-pass correctness) to a few lines of compiler registration, which is practically significant for research groups without systems expertise. The key open limitation is that the operator sets (RESIZE_BUFS, INDEX_OPS, ATTN_OPS) are manually curated from hand-written transformer graphs, raising questions about generalization to novel architectures (e.g., MoE, sliding-window attention, or non-standard attention variants). The evaluation is also confined to models up to 13B parameters and sequence lengths up to 128K, leaving open whether the O(1/s) FLOP-ratio argument and the 97%-of-baseline performance claim hold at the 70B+ scale where optimizer states and communication overhead dominate. Whether the approach extends to other SP strategies (RingAttention, context parallelism) or composes with expert parallelism remains unexplored.
