## Problem

Standard contrastive augmentations (crops, flips, rotations) are isometric transformations with |det A| = 1, meaning they produce zero incremental information entropy at the instance level (ΔH = 0). Moreover, deep encoders act as information bottlenecks governed by the Data-Processing Inequality, so even if input-level entropy were increased, it could be nullified during encoding. The paper identifies the missing principled mechanism for jointly generating entropy at the input and preserving it through the encoder.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 96×96 for STL-10, 224×224 for ImageNet) that are first patchified into ViT-style patches where the mini-batch dimension occupies the channel axis. SAIB is applied exclusively to the query branch, producing a transformed view whose 256-dimensional feature representation (via global average pooling) is contrasted against the anchor. Downstream, a frozen linear probe on top of the pretrained encoder yields class labels, and the same features transfer to segmentation (mIoU) and detection (mAP) on PASCAL VOC 2012.

## Method

IE-CL introduces a learnable Sample Augmentation Incremental Block (SAIB) that performs a nonlinear residual transformation (1×1-Conv → 3×3-Conv → 1×1-Conv with 2× channel expansion, wrapped in skip connections) on patchified query images, guaranteeing a local Jacobian determinant |det J| > 1 and thus strictly positive incremental entropy ΔH > 0. To prevent the encoder from erasing this entropy gain, every convolutional layer is spectrally normalized, bounding the encoder's Lipschitz constant and keeping E[log|det J_f|] from becoming excessively negative. A KL-divergence term between the SAIB-transformed query distribution and the anchor distribution constrains semantic drift, while a negative entropy term −λH(Z′⁻) directly optimizes for representation diversity. The full objective L = L_InfoNCE + β·D_KL − λ·H(Z′⁻) + η·L_reg_encoder + γ·R(g_φ) is trained end-to-end on a MoCo-style momentum encoder at batch size 256, and SAIB can be plugged into other SSL frameworks (BYOL, SimSiam, SimCLR) as a drop-in module.

## Results

On ImageNet with ResNet-18 (batch 256), IE-CL achieves 59.4% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline Light-MoCo (57.9%) by 1.5 points and MoCo-v3 (56.1%) by 3.3 points. On smaller datasets, IE-CL reaches 92.1% on CIFAR-10, 69.5% on CIFAR-100, and 91.9% on STL-10, consistently leading all baselines. With ResNet-50 on ImageNet at 800 epochs, IE-CL attains 73.2% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the next-best method Matrix-SSL (71.9%) by 1.3 points. On PASCAL VOC 2012 transfer tasks, IE-CL achieves 78.12 mIoU (segmentation) and 74.41 mAP (detection), exceeding the best baseline MoCo-v2 by 0.8 and 0.5 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IE-CL outperforms all 15 baselines across every dataset and metric reported. On ImageNet (ResNet-18, batch 256), the largest margin is against SimSiam (59.4% vs. 33.2%, +26.2 points) and MoCo-v2 (59.4% vs. 52.5%, +6.9 points), while the closest competitor Light-MoCo trails by 1.5 points (59.4% vs. 57.9%). On CIFAR-100, IE-CL leads BYOL (69.5% vs. 69.2%) and MoCo-v3 (69.5% vs. 68.8%) by narrow but consistent margins. With the stronger ResNet-50 backbone on ImageNet (Table 1), IE-CL at 800 epochs reaches 73.2%, beating Matrix-SSL (71.9%) and SimSiam (71.7%) by 1.3 and 1.5 points respectively, and maintaining a 256 batch size versus 4096 for SimCLR/SwAV. On PASCAL VOC 2012 transfer, IE-CL surpasses MoCo-v2 (the best baseline) by +0.8 mIoU and +0.5 mAP, and exceeds the supervised upper bound on mIoU (78.12 vs. 76.91). The plug-and-play SAIB module also improves BYOL (+0.81), SimCLR (+0.68), SimSiam (+0.72), and MoCo-v2 (+1.25) on ImageNet-100, confirming broad applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MoCo momentum-contrast framework and the InfoNCE loss formulation from SimCLR, but departs from the standard reliance on static, human-engineered augmentations (crops, flips, color jitter) by introducing a learnable non-isometric transformation (SAIB) that provably expands local representation volume via a Jacobian determinant > 1. Conceptually, it reframes the contrastive learning objective through the Data-Processing Inequality, identifying the deep encoder as an information bottleneck that can nullify input-level entropy gains, and proposes a joint optimization of entropy generation (SAIB) and entropy preservation (Spectral Normalization). This positions the work at the intersection of information-theoretic analysis (Donsker-Varadhan, mutual information lower bounds) and practical augmentation design, extending the alignment-uniformity decomposition of Wang & Isola with an explicit entropy-dynamics perspective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play SAIB module offers a practical, low-overhead (~9% extra compute) way to improve contrastive and non-contrastive SSL methods under small-batch settings, which is relevant given hardware constraints in many labs. The theoretical framing—formalizing why isometric augmentations yield zero incremental entropy and why the encoder bottleneck necessitates explicit regularization—provides a cleaner design principle for future augmentation strategies. However, SAIB's reliance on convolutional patch-level operations limits its direct applicability to ViT or emerging backbone architectures (e.g., Vision KANs), and the paper does not explore whether the entropy-preservation argument generalizes to non-convolutional encoders. An open question is whether the Jacobian-determinant condition can be enforced more globally (across the full encoder) rather than locally at the input, and whether the framework extends to video or multi-modal contrastive learning.
