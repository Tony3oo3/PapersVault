## Problem

High-performance visual trackers are too slow for deployment on resource-constrained edge devices, while fast trackers sacrifice accuracy. Existing remedies—cross-layer knowledge distillation and runtime token sparsification—suffer from suboptimal manual teacher-student layer assignments that disrupt feature hierarchy, and from extra computational overhead that ignores temporally-global optimization across frame sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260128/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 5 template images and 1 search image per frame, each resized to 112×224 and split into ViT patches. Templates are updated with a linear strategy that always retains the first frame and the immediately preceding frame to guard against occlusion. The output is four discrete coordinate tokens (from a shared vocabulary) that, when dequantized, yield the target's bounding box, predicted autoregressively frame by frame.

## Method

FARTrack builds on the ARTrack autoregressive Transformer encoder (ViT-Tiny backbone) and introduces two compression mechanisms. First, Task-Specific Self-Distillation performs layer-by-layer distillation on trajectory-sequence tokens rather than visual features: each layer acts as student for the next, minimizing KL divergence on the teacher's trajectory predictions, which preserves temporal information and eliminates the need for hand-crafted cross-layer pairings. Second, Inter-frame Autoregressive Sparsification computes a retention mask from the sum of template-to-search and template-to-command attention weights, then propagates the sparsification result autoregressively to subsequent frames, achieving a temporally-global optimal token reduction without any extra runtime computation. The training pipeline proceeds in three stages—frame-level pretraining on COCO2017, self-distillation to reduce depth from 15 to 10 or 6 layers, and sparsification training on continuous video sequences—using a combined loss of cross-entropy, SIoU, and KL divergence.

## Results

FARTrack_tiny achieves 70.6% AO on GOT-10k at 135 GPU FPS and 53 CPU FPS, outperforming AsymTrack-B (67.7% AO) by 2.9% at the same GPU speed. The fastest variant, FARTrack_pico, reaches 343 GPU FPS and 121 CPU FPS while still scoring 62.8% AO on GOT-10k. On TrackingNet, FARTrack_nano achieves 79.1% AUC (close to AsymTrack-B's 80.0%) at nearly 2× the GPU speed (210 vs. 135 FPS). On LaSOT, FARTrack_tiny attains 63.2% AUC, surpassing MixFormerV2-S (60.6%) by 2.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FARTrack_tiny outperforms AsymTrack-B by 2.9% AO on GOT-10k (70.6% vs. 67.7%) while matching its GPU speed (135 FPS) and nearly doubling its CPU speed (53 vs. 32 FPS). On LaSOText, FARTrack_tiny edges out AsymTrack-B by 0.4% AUC (45.0% vs. 44.6%), and on NFS it achieves the highest AUC (66.9%) among all compared trackers. FARTrack_pico surpasses MixFormerV2-S by 0.9% AO on GOT-10k while delivering roughly 3× the GPU speed (343 vs. 133 FPS) and 4× the CPU speed (121 vs. 31 FPS). On TrackingNet, FARTrack_nano (79.1% AUC) is within 0.9% of AsymTrack-B (80.0%) but runs at 210 vs. 135 GPU FPS. On UAV123, FARTrack_tiny beats AsymTrack-T by 1.2% AUC (65.8% vs. 64.6%) and AsymTrack-S by 0.2%. On VastTrack, FARTrack_tiny matches MixFormerV2-B exactly at 35.2% AUC, confirming robustness across 2,115 object categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

FARTrack extends the autoregressive visual tracking lineage established by ARTrack (Wei et al., CVPR 2023) and ARTrackV2 (Bai et al., CVPR 2024), which represent object trajectories as discrete token sequences predicted by a Transformer encoder. It departs from two established efficiency strategies: cross-layer knowledge distillation (as in MixFormerV2, AVTrack) that relies on hand-crafted teacher-student layer pairings, and runtime token sparsification (as in DynamicViT, OSTrack) that adds per-frame computational overhead. Instead, FARTrack introduces layer-by-layer self-distillation on trajectory-specific tokens and a sequence-level autoregressive sparsification that propagates template masks across frames, positioning itself as a bandwidth-friendly compression of the ARTrack family rather than a new tracking paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers a practically important speed-performance tradeoff for edge deployment: FARTrack_pico achieves 343 FPS on GPU and 121 FPS on CPU while retaining 62.8% AO on GOT-10k, and FARTrack_tiny matches or exceeds AsymTrack-B in accuracy at comparable GPU speed. The finding that reducing template token retention from 100% to 75% actually *improves* tracking accuracy (70.0% → 70.6% AO) suggests background tokens are not merely redundant but actively detrimental, a useful insight for template-based trackers broadly. Open questions include whether the autoregressive sparsification strategy transfers to other autoregressive vision tasks (e.g., video generation, action recognition) and whether the self-distillation approach generalizes beyond ViT-Tiny to larger backbones where the representational gap between adjacent layers may be less critical.
