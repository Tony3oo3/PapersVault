## Problem

Existing visual foundation models (VGGT, DUSt3R, MonST3R) can reconstruct 3D geometry from monocular video but treat each observation in isolation, lacking any mechanism for persistent scene memory, efficient relocalization upon revisiting familiar environments, or geometrically consistent pose refinement that accounts for dynamic objects. No prior system unifies dynamic-static separation, long-term spatial memory with recall and update, and stable camera trajectory estimation into a single framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262199/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a monocular RGB video stream and processes it frame-by-frame through VGGT for initial depth/pose, RAFT optical flow at 840×480, and SAM2 for mask refinement. Outputs include per-frame camera poses in SE(3), aligned 3D point clouds, depth maps, binary dynamic-object masks, and a persistent CPU-resident memory bank of downsampled static landmarks with DINOv2 (1024-d) and PointNet++ (512-d) feature descriptors indexed by a hash table. The memory backend is invoked every 20 frames rather than per-frame, and GPU memory remains near VGGT's baseline (~11.9 GB vs 11.7 GB) regardless of accumulated scene count.

## Method

CogniMap3D integrates three components built on top of VGGT's feed-forward depth and pose predictions. First, a multi-stage motion cue pipeline progressively isolates dynamic regions: GMM clustering of RAFT optical flow yields a coarse flow mask, a geometry residual (observed flow minus camera-induced flow computed via pointmap reprojection) is thresholded by Otsu's method, and 3D keypoint displacement outliers refine the final mask, which is then tracked across frames with SAM2. Second, a cognitive mapping system stores each scene as a dual representation—2D DINOv2 global embeddings from static keyframes and 3D PointNet++ features on octree-structured, voxel-downsampled static point clouds—retrieved via hash-based approximate nearest neighbor search and verified by ICP alignment of static point clouds before fusing new observations into the map. Third, a factor graph jointly optimizes camera poses and 3D landmarks using projection factors, a SE(3) prior on the initial pose, and inter-frame motion smoothness constraints, solved with Levenberg-Marquardt under Huber loss; landmarks recalled from memory receive downweighted projection covariance to enforce stronger geometric constraints during revisits.

## Results

On camera pose estimation, CogniMap3D achieves ATE of 0.176 on Sintel, 0.012 on TUM-dynamic, and 0.019 on ScanNet, outperforming VGGT (0.189/0.028/0.023) and CUT3R (0.213/0.046/0.099) across all three datasets. For video depth estimation, it reports Abs Rel of 0.295 on Sintel (vs. VGGT 0.299) and 0.069 on KITTI (vs. VGGT 0.072), while matching VGGT on Bonn (0.058 vs. 0.054). On 7-Scenes 3D reconstruction, it achieves mean accuracy of 0.086 and completion of 0.089, slightly beating VGGT (0.088/0.092) on accuracy but trailing on normal consistency (0.751 vs. 0.784).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among feed-forward VFM methods (Category III), CogniMap3D achieves the best ATE on all three datasets: 0.176 on Sintel (vs. VGGT 0.189, CUT3R 0.213, DUSt3R 0.290), 0.012 on TUM-dynamic (vs. VGGT 0.028, CUT3R 0.046), and 0.019 on ScanNet (vs. VGGT 0.023, CUT3R 0.099). It also leads in RPE trans on TUM (0.010 vs. VGGT 0.020) and ScanNet (0.011 vs. VGGT 0.015), though VGGT retains a slight edge in RPE rot on Sintel (0.529 vs. 0.600) and ScanNet (0.326 vs. 0.331). Against optimization-based VFM methods (Category II), CogniMap3D is competitive on Sintel ATE (0.176 vs. MonST3R-GA 0.111, MASt3R-GA 0.185) but significantly outperforms them on TUM-dynamic (0.012 vs. MASt3R-GA 0.038) and ScanNet (0.019 vs. MonST3R-GA 0.077). For depth estimation (Table 1), CogniMap3D slightly beats VGGT on Sintel (Abs Rel 0.295 vs. 0.299; δ<1.25 68.6 vs. 62.4) and KITTI (0.069 vs. 0.072) but trails marginally on Bonn (0.058 vs. 0.054). On 7-Scenes reconstruction (Table 2), it matches or slightly beats VGGT on accuracy (0.086 vs. 0.088) and completion (0.089 vs. 0.092) but lags on normal consistency (0.751 vs. 0.784), while substantially outperforming CUT3R (0.126/0.154/0.727) and MonST3R-GA (0.248/0.266/0.672) on all three metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

CogniMap3D extends the visual foundation model (VFM) lineage (DUSt3R → MonST3R → CUT3R → VGGT) by adding a persistent cognitive memory system that none of these prior methods possess. It builds directly on VGGT for initial depth and pose regression, and adapts MonST3R's geometry-residual principle for dynamic-static separation, but replaces MonST3R's threshold-based detection with a multi-stage progressive refinement (GMM flow clustering → geometry residual → 3D keypoint outlier analysis). The factor graph optimization for pose refinement is positioned as a VFM-compatible alternative to classical SLAM (DROID-SLAM, DPVO, LEAP-VO), avoiding the need for camera intrinsics while maintaining geometric consistency through static-region constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The persistent memory bank introduces a genuinely new capability to the VFM landscape: the ability to recognize previously visited scenes, recall stored 3D geometry, and incrementally refine maps across multiple visits without reprocessing the full sequence. This is particularly relevant for applications like autonomous navigation and AR where revisiting environments is common. However, the paper acknowledges that mismatching incidents can introduce noise, registration accuracy is sensitive to lighting/occlusion/viewpoint changes, and the memory system's scalability to truly diverse, large-scale environments remains untested. Open questions include whether the hash-based ANN + ICP validation pipeline generalizes beyond the tested scene counts and whether the cognitive mapping loop can handle significant structural changes (e.g., construction, seasonal variation).
