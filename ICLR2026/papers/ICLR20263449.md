## Problem

Existing LLM unlearning methods based on gradient ascent and its variants (e.g., NPO, WGA) suppress the exact target response but inadvertently redistribute probability mass into high-likelihood regions corresponding to semantically related rephrasings — a phenomenon the authors term the squeezing effect. This produces spurious unlearning that is invisible to standard metrics like ROUGE and truth ratio, yet still leaks the targeted knowledge through paraphrased outputs. No prior work explicitly identifies or counteracts this normalization-driven probability shift into the model's own belief neighborhoods.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (Llama 2/3, Zephyr-7B), an unlearning dataset of prompt-response pairs to forget, and optionally a retention dataset for regularization. A key operational detail is that BS-T requires computing the model's top-k token predictions at each position during training (with stop-gradient), while BS-S requires sampling N high-confidence generations per forget prompt via temperature-controlled decoding before or during fine-tuning. The output is the updated LLM parameters that assign low likelihood to both the original targets and the model's own high-confidence continuations, evaluated on TOFU, MUSE, and WMDP benchmarks.

## Method

The paper proposes a bootstrapping framework that incorporates the model's own high-confidence predictions (its 'beliefs') into the unlearning objective to directly counteract the squeezing effect. BS-T constructs a soft target at each token position by interpolating between the one-hot target label and the stop-gradient top-k model distribution, then minimizes a cross-entropy loss against this soft target, spreading the forgetting signal across the target and its belief neighborhood. BS-S extends this to the sequence level by sampling N high-confidence generations from the model for each forget prompt and augmenting the unlearning set, so that entire harmful continuations are suppressed rather than isolated tokens. Theoretically, using the AKG learning-dynamics decomposition, the paper proves that BS-T reshapes the residual gradient term so that repulsion is distributed across the top-k belief neighborhood rather than concentrated on the single target token, and that off-policy BS-S corresponds to a kernel-weighted aggregation of BS-T residuals over multiple belief-aligned sequences.

## Results

On TOFU (10% forget, Llama 3.2 1B), BS-S achieves the best aggregate score of 0.61 (Mem. 0.59, Util. 0.63), outperforming NPO (Agg. 0.58) and WGA (Agg. 0.53). On WMDP (Zephyr-7B-β), BS-S reaches near-random forgetting accuracy (Bio 0.26, Cyber 0.27) while retaining the highest MMLU utility (0.54) among all baselines except RMU (0.55). Across all three model scales (1B/3B/8B) and forget ratios (1%/5%/10%), BS-S consistently delivers the top aggregate and memorization scores, with BS-T as a strong runner-up offering higher computational efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BS-S outperforms all five baselines on aggregate and memorization scores across every model scale and forget ratio on TOFU. Against NPO (the strongest baseline), BS-S gains +0.03 Agg. on 1B (0.61 vs. 0.58) and +0.01 on 3B/8B, while also improving utility (0.63 vs. 0.58 on 1B). On WMDP, BS-S achieves the lowest (best) Cyber score (0.27) and ties for the lowest Bio score (0.26), while retaining MMLU at 0.54—surpassing NPO (0.44), GradDiff (0.43), and WGA (0.48) by 6–11 points. RMU is the only baseline that occasionally matches or exceeds BS-S on utility (e.g., 0.74 vs. 0.70 on 3B-10% TOFU), but it lags substantially on memorization (0.44 vs. 0.58). SimNPO is consistently the weakest baseline, with Agg. scores 0.10–0.35 below BS-S, indicating that its simplified NPO weighting fails to achieve reliable forgetting. BS-T trades a small amount of memorization for higher efficiency, making it a practical alternative when sequence-level sampling is too costly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient-ascent lineage of LLM unlearning (Eldan & Russinovich, 2023; NPO, WGA, GradDiff) by identifying a systematic failure mode—the squeezing effect—where softmax normalization redistributes suppressed probability mass into semantically related high-likelihood regions, producing spurious unlearning that standard metrics fail to detect. The proposed bootstrapping framework (BS-T, BS-S) repurposes the model's own high-confidence predictions as auxiliary forgetting targets, a conceptual inversion of self-distillation. The theoretical analysis is grounded in the AKG learning-dynamics decomposition of Ren & Sutherland (2025), showing how BS-T reshapes the residual term to spread forgetting pressure across the belief neighborhood rather than concentrating it on the target token alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of spurious unlearning as a systematic (not corner-case) phenomenon challenges the reliability of widely adopted evaluation metrics (ROUGE, Truth Ratio, Probability) and suggests that many reported unlearning gains may be overstated. The bootstrapping remedy is practical, compatible with existing objectives (NPO, WGA, GradDiff), and validated across three benchmarks and multiple model families, making it a drop-in improvement for the OpenUnlearning ecosystem. Open questions include whether the squeezing effect persists at larger scales (70B+), how on-policy BS-S behaves under the evolving-belief regime that falls outside the AKG framework, and whether the approach generalizes to multi-turn or agentic settings where harmful knowledge is distributed across longer contexts.
