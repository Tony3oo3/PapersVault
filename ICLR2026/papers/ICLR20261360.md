## Problem

Data-specific neural representations (INRs, neural compression) typically treat visual data as coordinate-to-RGB mappings or apply bit-level quantization, ignoring the fact that pixels are discrete samples of an underlying continuous signal. State-space models (SSMs) naturally encode inputs as projections onto learned basis functions, making them well-suited for compact signal representation, but they have not been explored for this purpose. Two practical barriers prevent direct SSM use: they operate only on 1D sequences and inherently preserve input length, making them unsuitable for multi-dimensional visual data and for compression.

## Input / Output

The method overfits a small network to a single visual datum: individual images (Kodak at 512×768, CLIC2020 up to 2048px), video sequences (Bunny 132 frames, UVG 300/600 frames at 1080p, DAVIS short clips), or 3D objects (Objaverse furniture voxelized to 32³ binary grids). The output is the trained network's weights themselves, which serve as the compact representation; reconstruction is performed by a lightweight deconvolutional decoder (for images/3D) or by existing NeRV decoders (for video), so the inference cost at decode time is unchanged from the baseline methods.

## Method

The core contribution is the Structured State-Space Kernel (S3K), which converts SSM dynamics into a convolutional kernel by observing that the final hidden state h_{L-1} = [Ā^{L-1}B̄, Ā^{L-2}B̄, …, ĀB̄, B̄]·[φ₀, φ₁, …, φ_{L-1}]ᵀ is a convolution with a structured kernel. This kernel naturally compresses L input positions into N output channels (the state size), solving the length-preservation problem, and extends to n-dimensions via outer products of independent 1D kernels, solving the 1D-only limitation. The full LPNet-S3K architecture applies S3K convolutions at each level of a Laplacian pyramid decomposition for multi-scale encoding, augmented with input-adaptive B, real-valued A/B parameters, and subsequent 1×1 convolutions for expressivity. Two theorems provide theoretical grounding: Theorem 4.1 shows SSMs decompose inputs into complex exponentials (linking them to classical signal compression), and Theorem 5.1 proves the S3K output retains sufficient information to reconstruct the original sequence via the learned state-space parameters.

## Results

On Kodak, LPNet-S3K achieves 28.09 PSNR / 0.9331 MS-SSIM, outperforming ConvNeXt (25.99/0.8830) by over 2 dB and LPNet-Mamba (27.51/0.9227) by 0.58 dB. On CLIC2020, it reaches 26.33/0.8692, and on Objaverse (3D voxels) it achieves 18.34/0.8492, the best across all three modalities. On the NeRV video benchmarks, Ours-S (3.0M params) attains 39.99 PSNR on Bunny and 33.86 avg PSNR on DAVIS, ranking first at every model size tested, while Ours-P (3.3M) reaches 34.36 avg PSNR on UVG, surpassing larger-parameter baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SSM baselines (S4, S4D, S4ND, S5, Mamba) in the baseline architecture (Table 1), all SSM variants consistently outperform the Transformer baseline, and the Laplacian pyramid variant (c) yields the best results for every SSM, with S4ND reaching 26.61 PSNR vs. Transformer's 24.67. LPNet-S3K outperforms LPNet-Mamba by 0.58 dB on Kodak and 0.17 dB on CLIC2020, confirming that the structured kernel formulation is more effective than a raw Mamba block plus standard convolution for compression. Against ConvNeXt (the standard CNN encoder), LPNet-S3K gains 2.10 dB on Kodak and 1.94 dB on CLIC2020, demonstrating the benefit of both the Laplacian pyramid architecture and the S3K kernel. On NeRV video benchmarks, Ours-S beats SNeRV by 0.35 dB on Bunny (39.99 vs. 39.64 at 3.0M) and by 2.30 dB on DAVIS (33.86 vs. 31.56 avg), while Ours-P surpasses PNeRV by 1.14 dB on UVG (34.36 vs. 33.22) at comparable parameter counts. The only baseline that outperforms LPNet-S3K is the MLP-based FINER method (28.68 vs. 28.09 PSNR on Kodak), but FINER requires 8× more training time per epoch (0.4s vs. 0.05s) and has slower decoding, making LPNet-S3K the preferred choice for real-time applications.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes state-space models (S4, S4D, S5, Mamba) from their original role in sequence modeling (classification, translation) into compression primitives for data-specific neural representations, drawing on HiPPO's interpretation of SSM hidden states as projections onto orthogonal polynomial bases. The S3K kernel distills SSM dynamics into structured convolutional weights, bridging the gap between SSM expressiveness and the multi-dimensional, downsampling-friendly operations that visual data require. Architecturally, it builds on Laplacian pyramid decomposition from traditional compression and integrates with existing NeRV decoder frameworks (HNeRV, SNeRV, PNeRV), positioning itself as an encoder drop-in rather than a full pipeline redesign.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that SSMs are theoretically and empirically well-suited for compact data-specific representation because they encode the input signal itself via learned basis projections, rather than capturing semantic patterns as Transformers do. However, the explicit kernel construction incurs ~20× memory overhead relative to plain convolution, limiting scalability, and the method has only been validated in the overfitting-to-single-datum paradigm. Open questions include whether a dedicated SSM-aware decoder could close the remaining gap to MLP-based INRs, whether S3K's compressive properties transfer to autoencoder-based generative models, and whether more efficient kernel formulations (avoiding explicit materialization) can match the reconstruction quality at lower cost.
