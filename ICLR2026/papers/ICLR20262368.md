## Problem

Existing black-box LLM fingerprinting methods rely on memorizing fixed query-key pairs with atypical responses, making them both easily detectable by malicious deployers (who can filter atypical queries/outputs) and fragile under common deployment modifications such as finetuning, quantization, and pruning. No prior fingerprint is simultaneously stealthy and robust across realistic deployment scenarios, leaving model owners without a reliable mechanism to verify ownership of open-weight models deployed behind APIs.

## Input / Output

The embedding procedure takes an instruction-tuned LLM's weights, a semantic domain dataset (e.g., French text from the Lucie corpus), a disjoint regularization dataset (50/50 Alpaca + OpenWebText), and Red-Green watermark hyperparameters (private key ξ, context size k=1, strength δ=4, green ratio γ=0.25). The output is a fingerprinted LLM whose responses to any query within the semantic domain carry a statistical watermark signal, while responses outside the domain remain statistically indistinguishable from the base model. Detection consumes 1000 domain-specific queries (capped at 200 tokens each) sent to a suspicious API and produces a binary ownership signal via a one-sided Z-test on the concatenated responses, with a configurable false-positive rate (α=1e-3).

## Method

The core idea replaces brittle query-key memorization with a semantically conditioned statistical watermark: the model is trained to embed a Red-Green watermark signal only in responses to prompts from a chosen semantic domain (e.g., French), while preserving its distribution on all other domains. Training uses knowledge distillation against a frozen copy of the original model: on the semantic domain, the model minimizes KL divergence between its logits and the watermarked logits (computed by applying the Red-Green boost to the teacher's distribution); on a regularization dataset, it minimizes a positive-deviation total variation distance to prevent the watermark from amplifying low-probability tokens outside the domain. Detection concatenates responses from multiple domain queries and applies a Z-test on the green-token ratio, exploiting the fact that detectability increases monotonically with total token count—so even if individual responses carry a weakened signal (due to paraphrasing, finetuning on the domain, or pre-filling), accumulating enough queries recovers a statistically significant fingerprint. This design makes the fingerprint inherently stealthy (natural-language queries and statistically watermarked but fluent responses are indistinguishable from normal traffic) and robust (no single point of failure that an adversary can target or a deployment step can erase).

## Results

The proposed semantically conditioned watermark fingerprint achieves a Fingerprint Success Rate (FSR) of 1.0 (100%) across all 25 prominent deployment scenarios (sampling, quantization, pruning, finetuning, active adversaries) and 5 targeted adversarial scenarios, while base (non-fingerprinted) models yield FSR = 0.0 (no false positives). Model utility is preserved: average benchmark accuracy across 8 benchmarks drops by at most 0.01 (e.g., LLAMA3.1-8B: 0.63 → 0.62; QWEN2.5-3B: 0.58 → 0.58). Detection requires 1000 queries from the semantic domain (French), costing approximately $0.20 at GPT-5 pricing, with a target false positive rate of 1e-03.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two black-box fingerprinting baselines: Instructional Fingerprinting (IF; Xu et al., NAACL 2024), which memorizes 8 query-key pairs via SFT, and Scalable Fingerprinting (SF; Nasery et al., 2025), which memorizes 1024 query-key pairs via SFT with Perinucleus Sampling. The proposed method achieves FSR = 1.0 in every tested scenario, while both baselines fail systematically: IF drops to 0.0 under 50% pruning, all finetuning configurations (Alpaca, Dolly, OMI with LoRA or full), and several system prompts; SF drops to 0.0 under Int4 quantization (QWEN2.5-3B, LLAMA3.1-8B), 50% pruning, all finetuning, and most active adversarial attacks. Even SF's larger query set (1024 pairs vs. IF's 8) does not compensate for the brittleness of exact-match detection. Under targeted adversaries (finetuning on the semantic domain, anti-watermark paraphrasing, English pre-filling), the proposed method still maintains FSR = 1.0, whereas both baselines drop to 0.0–0.4. The key advantage is the statistical nature of the watermark signal, which accumulates over 1000 queries and tolerates per-token perturbations that would break exact string matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads—black-box model fingerprinting (Xu et al., 2024; Nasery et al., 2025) and LLM watermarking (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023; Gu et al., 2024)—into a single paradigm. Prior fingerprinting methods rely on memorized query-key pairs, which are brittle to deployment modifications and detectable by adversaries; prior watermarking work targets all model outputs or requires generation-time access. The key departure is the first successful distillation of a Red-Green watermark into a single semantic domain (via KL-divergence minimization with a domain-specific regularization loss), repurposing a content-traceability tool as an ownership-verification mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first black-box fingerprint that is simultaneously stealthy and robust across all 25 common deployment scenarios and 5 targeted adversarial attacks tested, addressing a critical gap for open-weight model licensing enforcement. The counterintuitive finding that semantically conditioned watermarks are more robust to finetuning than full watermarks (Fig. 4) suggests domain restriction is a design principle, not just a stealth mechanism. Open questions include whether the semantic-domain constraint is acceptable for end users in that domain, whether stealth holds against adversaries who know the domain, and whether the approach scales to much larger models where distillation cost becomes prohibitive.
