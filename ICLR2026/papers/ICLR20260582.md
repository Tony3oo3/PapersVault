## Problem

Existing RLVR methods for mathematical reasoning suffer from a systematic neglect of harder questions on two fronts: algorithmically, GRPO's group relative advantage estimation implicitly suppresses update magnitudes for both easier and harder questions (peaking at moderate difficulty), and data-wise, augmentation strategies rephrase questions for diversity without systematically increasing intrinsic difficulty. This paper addresses both gaps to focus training on the model's solvable weaknesses.

## Input / Output

The method takes mathematical questions as input (text-only from the MATH dataset or multimodal geometry problems from GEOQA-8k) and produces step-by-step reasoning chains ending with a final answer in \boxed{} format. Training uses binary accuracy rewards (1/0) with a group size of 8 responses per question. The MQR component reformulates each original question into three variants (background, term, sub-problem) that must preserve the exact gold answer, expanding the 7.5k MATH dataset to 30k without requiring solution regeneration.

## Method

MathForge combines two complementary components. Algorithmically, DGPO first replaces GRPO's standard-deviation normalization with mean absolute deviation (MAD) in the advantage estimator, provably making the total per-question update magnitude a constant G regardless of accuracy rate (Theorem 2 vs. Theorem 1's 2G√(p(1−p))). It then applies a softmax-based difficulty-aware question-level weighting (DQW) that upweights questions with lower mean accuracy, using a temperature T=2.0 to balance focus on hard questions against batch-wide learning. Data-wise, MQR uses a large reasoning model (OpenAI o3) to generate three types of harder reformulations per question—adding irrelevant story background, inventing abstract mathematical terminology, and nesting an independent sub-problem—each constrained to preserve the original gold answer. The two components form a synergistic loop: MQR expands the data frontier with more challenging questions, and DGPO ensures the policy update budget is allocated toward mastering those challenges.

## Results

On the MATH dataset with Qwen2.5-Math-7B, MathForge (DGPO + MQR) achieves an average score of 42.17% across six benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, AMC23, MATH500, Minerva, Olympiad), a +4.56% improvement over the GRPO baseline (37.61%). DGPO alone yields 39.79% (+2.18% over GRPO), while MQR-augmented data with GRPO reaches 41.04% (+3.43%). The framework generalizes across model sizes (1.5B to 7B) and extends to the multimodal domain, where DGPO achieves 59.95% on GeoQA versus 57.43% for GRPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MathForge outperforms all six policy-optimization baselines by a substantial margin: +4.56% over GRPO, +4.77% over Dr.GRPO, +4.24% over GPG, +4.23% over DAPO, +4.46% over GSPO, and +3.91% over GRPO-AD (the difficulty-aware variant of Zhang & Zuo). DGPO alone (39.79%) already surpasses every baseline, with the largest gap over GRPO-AD (+1.53%), indicating that the difficulty-balanced advantage estimation and question-level weighting address a more fundamental issue than the advantage reweighting in GRPO-AD. MQR with GRPO (41.04%) also beats all algorithmic baselines, confirming that data difficulty augmentation is complementary to algorithmic improvements. The gains are consistent across model sizes: MathForge improves over GRPO by +4.45% on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, +3.54% on Qwen2.5-3B, and +2.86% on DeepSeek-Math-7B. In the multimodal setting, DGPO achieves 59.95% on GeoQA, outperforming GRPO (57.43%), GPG (59.02%), and DAPO (59.02%) by +2.52%, +0.93%, and +0.93% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) / PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) lineage for RLVR-based mathematical reasoning by identifying a specific mathematical property of GRPO's group relative advantage estimation: the total update magnitude follows 2G√(p(1−p)), implicitly suppressing learning on both very easy and very hard questions. While concurrent work (Zhang & Zuo, 2025) also recognizes difficulty-awareness in GRPO, this paper departs by first providing a clean theoretical rectification (replacing standard deviation with mean absolute deviation to normalize update magnitude to a constant) and then layering an explicit softmax-based question-level reweighting on top. On the data side, MQR extends the answer-preserving rephrasing paradigm of MetaMath (Yu et al., 2024) and Liang et al. (2025) by systematically targeting multiple cognitive dimensions (noise filtering, abstraction, multi-step composition) rather than generic paraphrasing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'balance-then-reweight' decomposition (DGAE + DQW) offers a principled, interpretable, and drop-in improvement to GRPO that is compatible with other policy optimization variants (GPG, DAPO, GSPO), making it a practical upgrade for the RLVR community. The MQR strategy's constraint of preserving the gold answer eliminates the need for solution regeneration, keeping augmentation costs low (~$184 for 22.5k questions) while yielding a 3.43% gain over GRPO alone. Open questions include whether the difficulty metric (negative mean accuracy) remains well-calibrated as the policy improves during training, whether the approach transfers to non-binary reward settings or non-mathematical reasoning domains, and whether the MQR strategies generalize to other problem types beyond competition mathematics.
