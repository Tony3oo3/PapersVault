## Problem

Standard RL fine-tuning of diffusion models locks in a fixed reward function and KL regularization weight, making it impossible to adjust the trade-off between multiple conflicting objectives (e.g., aesthetics vs. text-image alignment) or the degree of deviation from the base model at inference time. The paper asks whether a one-time fine-tuning procedure can enable generation aligned with any user-specified linear combination of basis rewards and any regularization strength, without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261735/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference time, the user provides a text prompt, a preference weight vector w over m basis reward functions (e.g., ImageReward, VILA, PickScore), and optionally a regularization modification factor lambda that rescales the KL weight to alpha/lambda. The method produces a single 512x512 (SDv1.5) or 1024x1024 (SDXL) RGB image whose expected reward profile matches the user's specified combination. The preference weights are consumed as coefficients in a drift-function blend at every denoising step, so changing w or lambda requires no retraining, only a different mixing schedule during the backward SDE rollout.

## Method

The key theoretical result (Proposition 1) shows that the backward SDE drift of a reward-aligned diffusion model equals the pre-trained drift minus a control term u^(r,alpha) that depends on the reward. By applying a Jensen-gap approximation (swapping E and exp inside u), the control term becomes linear in the reward, yielding the decomposition f^(r(w),alpha) approx sum_i w_i f^(r_i,alpha) (Lemma 2). DB-MPA exploits this linearity by evaluating m independently RL-fine-tuned models (one per basis reward) at each denoising step and taking the weighted average of their drifts. DB-KLA similarly blends the fine-tuned drift with the pre-trained drift using coefficient lambda to control KL strength. To eliminate the m-fold inference overhead, DB-MPA-LS replaces the deterministic blend with a Bernoulli (or categorical) random draw of a single fine-tuned LoRA adapter per step; Proposition 2 proves this stochastic selection yields identical marginal distributions to the deterministic blend, reducing cost to that of a single model forward pass.

## Results

On the Short-DrawBench 1k test set with two rewards (ImageReward and VILA), DB-MPA at w=0.5 achieves a weighted reward improvement of 0.42 over Stable Diffusion, closely matching DB-MPA-LS (0.39) and approaching the MORL oracle upper bound. DB-MPA outperforms Rewarded Soup, CoDe, and RGG by 3.92×, 1.95×, and 1.33× respectively on this metric. Inference time is 11.11 s/image for DB-MPA (2× SD) and 5.64 s/image for DB-MPA-LS (matching SD's 5.46 s), versus 185.26 s for CoDe and 121.58 s for RGG. On GenEval (700 held-out prompts), DB-MPA and DB-MPA-LS consistently dominate all baselines across every preference weight.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DB-MPA consistently outperforms all practical baselines across every preference weight. Against Rewarded Soup (Rame et al., 2023), which linearly interpolates model parameters, DB-MPA achieves 3.92× higher weighted reward at w=0.5, and the gap widens to 3.3×–8.2× as the number of rewards grows from 2 to 4. Against CoDe (Singh et al., 2025), a gradient-free particle-search method, DB-MPA yields 1.95× higher weighted reward while running 16.7× faster (11 s vs. 185 s per image). Against RGG (Kim et al., 2025), a reward-gradient guidance method, DB-MPA achieves 1.33× higher weighted reward at 11× lower inference cost (11 s vs. 122 s). DB-MPA-LS matches DB-MPA's Pareto performance at SD-level speed (5.64 s vs. 5.46 s), making it the most efficient option. The only method that exceeds DB-MPA is the MORL oracle (Wu et al., 2023), which requires a separately fine-tuned model for each (w, λ) pair and is infeasible at deployment; DB-MPA's Pareto front closely tracks this upper bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RL fine-tuning framework for diffusion models (DPOK, Fan et al. 2023) and adapts the Jensen gap approximation from diffusion posterior sampling (Chung et al., 2023) to derive a linear decomposition of the reward-dependent drift term. Conceptually, it extends the decoding-time realignment idea from LLMs (DeRa, Liu et al. 2024) to the continuous SDE setting of diffusion models, but departs from the closest prior work, Rewarded Soup (Rame et al. 2023), by blending backward diffusion drift functions rather than interpolating model parameters. The DB-MPA-LS variant introduces a novel theoretical result (Proposition 2) showing that Bernoulli sampling of individual drifts yields the same marginal distribution as their weighted average, enabling single-model-speed inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, theoretically grounded path to user-controllable multi-reward alignment without retraining, which is critical for deployment settings where preferences vary across users and contexts. The DB-MPA-LS variant is particularly impactful as it eliminates the linear m× inference overhead, making multi-preference alignment as cheap as standard Stable Diffusion. Limitations include the Jensen gap approximation degrading when the KL weight α becomes very small (Remark 2), and the method is restricted to linear reward combinations. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-linear or adversarial reward compositions, how approximation error scales with model capacity, and whether the drift-blending principle generalizes to other generative architectures beyond diffusion.
