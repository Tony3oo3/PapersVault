## Problem

Autoregressive video generation models distilled from short-horizon bidirectional teachers (typically 5 seconds) suffer severe quality degradation—motion collapse, over-exposure, and error accumulation—when generating beyond the teacher's training window. The root cause is a dual train-inference mismatch: the student is trained only on short clips with dense teacher supervision, so it never encounters the compounding errors that arise in long rollouts, and the fixed-cache training versus rolling-cache inference further compounds temporal inconsistencies.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single text prompt and autoregressively generates a video up to 4 minutes 15 seconds (255 seconds) long, using a 4-step denoising schedule (timesteps 1000, 750, 500, 250) with a rolling KV cache of 21 latent frames. Inference runs at 17 FPS on a single H100 GPU (~22 GB memory for a 50-second video), and the base architecture is the Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B diffusion transformer converted to a causal autoregressive generator. No real video data is needed at training time—only the pretrained teacher model and text prompts from VidProM.

## Method

Self-Forcing++ extends the student's rollout to N frames far beyond the teacher's M-frame horizon (e.g., 100 s vs. 5 s), then re-injects diffusion noise into the degraded rollout (backward noise initialization) to create a temporally consistent starting state for distillation. A contiguous window of length K (matching the teacher's native horizon) is uniformly sampled from the N-frame rollout, and an extended Distribution Matching Distillation loss is computed within that window, teaching the student to correct its own accumulated errors using the teacher's implicit knowledge of the data distribution. A rolling KV cache is used identically during both training and inference, eliminating the train-inference mismatch present in Self-Forcing. Optionally, GRPO with an optical-flow-magnitude reward is applied to suppress abrupt scene transitions and improve long-range temporal smoothness. Scaling training iterations (up to 25× the baseline budget) progressively extends the model's reliable generation horizon from ~5 s to over 4 minutes without requiring any long-video training data.

## Results

On 5-second short-horizon generation, Self-Forcing++ achieves a VBench total score of 83.11 and semantic score of 80.37, matching or slightly exceeding Self-Forcing (83.00 / 80.14). On 50-second long-horizon generation, the method achieves a Visual Stability score of 90.94 and Dynamic Degree of 55.36, surpassing the next-best baseline (SkyReels-V2) by 30.5 and 16.2 points respectively. At 100 seconds, Self-Forcing++ maintains a Visual Stability of 84.22 and Dynamic Degree of 54.12, while all baselines drop below 57 and 39 on those metrics. With scaled training (25× budget), the model generates a 255-second video with negligible quality loss, representing a 50× extension over the 5-second teacher horizon.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Self-Forcing++ is most directly comparable to Self-Forcing (same 1.3B Wan2.1 backbone, same 17 FPS throughput). On 5-second clips the two are nearly tied (83.11 vs 83.00 total VBench score), but on 50-second generation Self-Forcing++ achieves a Visual Stability of 90.94 versus 40.12 for Self-Forcing—a more than 2× gap—while also improving Dynamic Degree from 34.35 to 55.36 (+61%). Against CausVid, the advantage is similar: Visual Stability 90.94 vs 40.47 and Dynamic Degree 55.36 vs 37.35. At 100 seconds the gap widens further, with Self-Forcing++ scoring 84.22 Visual Stability and 54.12 Dynamic Degree while Self-Forcing drops to 32.03 and 26.41, and CausVid to 39.21 and 34.60. Among diffusion-forcing baselines, SkyReels-V2 is the closest competitor (60.41 Visual Stability at 50s) but still trails by over 30 points. The paper notes that baselines' high Temporal Quality scores (e.g., 88–89) are misleading, as they reflect motion stagnation rather than genuine temporal coherence, a failure mode captured by the proposed Visual Stability metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Self-Forcing (Huang et al., 2025) and CausVid (Yin et al., 2025) lineage of autoregressive video generation by addressing their core limitation: quality collapse beyond the 5-second teacher horizon. The key methodological departure is backward noise initialization combined with extended Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) over self-generated long rollouts, allowing a short-horizon teacher to supervise the student at arbitrary temporal offsets. It further incorporates GRPO with an optical-flow reward (building on DanceGRPO and Flow-GRPO) to suppress abrupt scene transitions, and introduces a Gemini-2.5-Pro-based Visual Stability metric to correct known biases in VBench for long-video evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that minute-scale video generation becomes achievable without long-video training data or long-video teachers, relying solely on a 5-second bidirectional model and scaled compute. The observed scaling property (25× training budget → 255-second coherent video) suggests a path to arbitrarily long generation, though it remains untested beyond the base model's positional embedding limit. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger architectures or different base models, how to incorporate long-term memory for occluded objects, and whether the training cost (48 H100 GPU-days) is prohibitive for broader adoption. The identification of VBench's bias toward over-exposed frames is a useful correction for the field's evaluation practices.
