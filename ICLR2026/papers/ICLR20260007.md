## Problem

Existing differentiable reward fine-tuning methods for diffusion-based motion generation (e.g., DRaFT, AlignProp, DRTune) backpropagate through the entire multi-step denoising trajectory, causing sparse and coarse-grained optimization with vanishing gradients at early steps, and excessive memory consumption from storing the full computation graph. Additionally, the scarcity of human-annotated motion preference pairs makes it difficult to train a motion-specific reward model, forcing reliance on general-purpose retrieval models that inadequately capture motion preferences.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and operates on intermediate noisy motion states at randomly sampled denoising steps (t ~ U(0,T)) during the reverse diffusion process, rather than only the final clean motion. The reward model (a fine-tuned ReAlign retrieval encoder) evaluates these noisy states directly, with a noise-aware formulation that for ODE-based samplers uses a one-step clean prediction x̂₀ = π'θ(x_t, t, c) and for SDE-based samplers scores the noisy state x_t itself. The output is a 3D human motion sequence (joint positions over time) aligned to the text description, evaluated on HumanML3D and KIT-ML benchmarks.

## Method

EasyTune's core insight is that the recursive gradient dependence in the denoising trajectory (formalized in Corollary 1) is the root cause of both memory overhead and vanishing gradients; by applying a stop-gradient operation to the input of each denoising step (Eq. 7), the gradient at step t depends only on the current step's parameters, decoupling it from the full T-step chain. This yields O(1) memory per step instead of O(T), enables dense step-wise optimization, and eliminates the vanishing product of Jacobians that suppresses early-step updates. For the reward model, the paper introduces Self-refinement Preference Learning (SPL), which dynamically mines preference pairs by treating ground-truth motions as preferred and top-incorrect retrieval results as non-preferred, then fine-tunes a pre-trained retrieval model via KL-divergence alignment (following Pick-a-Pic) without any human-annotated preference data. The framework is validated across six pre-trained diffusion backbones (MLD, MLD++, MotionLCM, MDM, MoDiffuse) and achieves 70.7% FID improvement over the MLD base with only 31.16% of DRaFT-50's additional memory and a 7.3× training speedup.

## Results

On HumanML3D, EasyTune (step optimization) achieves R-Precision@1 of 0.581 (+15.3% over MLD base), FID of 0.132 (70.7% improvement), MM Dist of 2.637 (+13.6%), and Diversity of 9.465, while using only 22.10 GB peak memory. Compared to DRaFT-50, EasyTune improves MM Dist by 7.7% (2.637 vs. 2.872) while requiring only 31.16% of its additional memory overhead (6.89 GB vs. 22.11 GB) and achieving a 7.3× training speedup. On KIT-ML, MDM+EasyTune improves R-P@1 from 0.403 to 0.442 (+9.7%) and FID from 0.497 to 0.284 (42.9% reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EasyTune outperforms all differentiable-reward fine-tuning baselines (DRaFT-10/50, ReFL-10/20, AlignProp, DRTune) on every metric in Table 1: it achieves the best R-P@1 (0.581 vs. 0.565 for DRaFT-10), best FID (0.132 vs. 0.195 for DRaFT-10), and best MM Dist (2.637 vs. 2.703 for DRaFT-10), while also using the least memory (22.10 GB vs. 37.32 GB for DRaFT-50). Against the broader text-to-motion generation baselines in Table 2, EasyTune-enhanced MLD surpasses ParCo (R-P@1: 0.581 vs. 0.515), ReMoDiffuse (0.581 vs. 0.510), and SoPo (0.581 vs. 0.528), and EasyTune-enhanced MLD++ reaches R-P@1 of 0.591 with FID 0.069. Generalization is confirmed across six pre-trained backbones (MLD, MLD++, MotionLCM-1S/4S, MDM-50S, MoDiffuse), with consistent R-P@1 gains of 3.7%–13.7% and FID reductions of 11.7%–59.7%. The SPL reward model also outperforms ReAlign, TMR, LaMP, and TEMOS on text-motion retrieval (R@1: 69.31 vs. 67.59 for ReAlign), validating the self-refinement preference learning component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the differentiable-reward fine-tuning paradigm for diffusion models established by DRaFT (Clark et al., 2024) and partially addressed by DRTune (Wu et al., 2025a), which introduced stop-gradient operations but still retained recursive gradient dependencies across the denoising trajectory. EasyTune's key departure is a theoretically grounded (Corollary 1–2) step-by-step optimization that fully decouples each denoising step via stop-gradient, reducing memory from O(T) to O(1) and eliminating vanishing-gradient effects in early steps. For the reward model, it adapts the Pick-a-pic (Kirstain et al., 2023) KL-divergence preference objective but replaces human-annotated pairs with self-mined retrieval-failure pairs (SPL), a mechanism not previously applied to motion generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7.3× training speedup and ~69% reduction in additional memory overhead make differentiable-reward alignment of motion diffusion models practically accessible on a single GPU, removing a major barrier to adoption. The SPL mechanism eliminates the need for costly human preference annotation, which is a persistent bottleneck in the motion domain. However, the paper acknowledges that the reward model captures only semantic alignment and provides limited physical grounding; building a unified reward that jointly encodes physical plausibility and semantic coherence remains an open direction. The reliance on retrieval-based pair mining also introduces a risk of noisy or ambiguous preference signals that could limit reward model quality at scale.
