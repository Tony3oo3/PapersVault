## Problem

Deploying LLMs on edge devices is constrained by dynamically managed memory that varies with system workload, making fixed-size compressed models unreliable. Re-compressing a model to fit available memory is prohibitively expensive (hours on cloud GPUs), and existing elastic-training approaches require GPU resources and curated datasets tailored to a single model type. No prior post-training framework systematically combines quantization and structured pruning in a one-shot pass that supports multiple architectures (Transformers, SSMs, hybrids) and enables on-device configurable pruning without re-computation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weights (7B–9B parameter models across Transformers, SSMs, and Mamba-Transformer hybrids), a small calibration set (128 Alpaca samples at 2048 tokens for weight-sorting, 51,800 Alpaca samples at 256 tokens for fine-tuning, and 256 WikiText-2 samples for PTQ), and a target global pruning rate. It produces a single 4-bit quantized model with pre-sorted weight channels that can be adaptively pruned on-device at any rate from 0% to 35% simply by dropping the least-ranked columns, yielding 4×–6.1× model size reduction. The entire compression pipeline executes once on a single 48 GB A6000 GPU, and the on-device pruning requires no gradient computation or re-quantization.

## Method

UniQL performs a one-shot cloud compression pipeline with three stages: (1) structured weight-sorting that groups weights per block (MLP, MHSA, Mamba) and reorders channels by importance scores—ridge leverage scores for MLP, activation-norm products for attention Q/K, and state-aware correlations from SSM hidden states for Mamba—while avoiding the O(n³) pseudo-inverse via a 20× faster alternative; (2) masked LoRA fine-tuning on the un-pruned sorted model where a random global pruning rate is sampled each step to mask the least-ranked channels, making the model robust to any future pruning level; and (3) 4-bit group-wise quantization (GPTQ-adapted) with a quantization-aware SVD decomposition for W_v/W_o that fuses eigenvalues Σ into U so each column's eigenvalue acts as a per-group scaling factor, reducing quantization error. A fused RoPE kernel with symmetric index sorting handles the positional embeddings for pruned head dimensions, and the final model supports on-device adaptive pruning by simply truncating sorted channels at the desired rate.

## Results

UniQL achieves 4×–6.1× model size reduction with 4-bit quantization and up to 35% structured pruning in a single cloud pass, while maintaining accuracy within ~5% of FP16 baselines at 15% pruning (e.g., 71.4% vs. 74.0% on Llama-3.1-8B, 70.5% vs. 76.0% on Nemotron-H-8B). On edge hardware (Orin Nano 8G), UniQL delivers 2.7×–3.4× token throughput improvement over FP16 and 1.7×–2.1× speedup over TAO-HQQ (W4A16) in time-per-output-token. The compression pipeline completes in 7h43m on a single A6000 GPU for Llama-3.1-8B (including fine-tuning and PTQ), versus 16h46m for SVD-LLM and 7h03m for MoDeGPT (without PTQ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MoDeGPT (ICLR 2025), UniQL wins decisively at both 15% and 25% pruning: at 15%, UniQL achieves 71.4% vs. 64.2% on Llama-3.1-8B and 68.1% vs. 52.2% on Qwen-2.5-7B; at 25%, the gap widens to 67.7% vs. 59.3% and 64.0% vs. 48.4%, respectively. MoDeGPT's reliance on pseudo-inverse causes numerical instability when D_int >> D_h (e.g., Qwen-2.5-7B with D_int=18944), leading to catastrophic accuracy drops. Against SVD-LLM (ICLR 2025), UniQL consistently outperforms: at 15% pruning, 71.4% vs. 60.6% on Llama-3.1-8B; at 25%, 67.7% vs. 54.2%. UniQL also supports SSMs and hybrid models (Bamba-v2, Nemotron-H, Mamba2) where neither baseline has results. Against PTQ baselines (TRT-AWQ, TAO-HQQ, GPTQ), UniQL matches or slightly exceeds accuracy at 0% pruning (e.g., 72.9% vs. 72.4% for TAO-HQQ on Llama-3.1-8B) while uniquely adding on-device adaptive pruning. In compression time, UniQL's matrix decomposition is 22× faster than MoDeGPT (19 min vs. 7h03m) and 1.8× faster than SVD-LLM (19 min vs. 35 min), and the full pipeline with FT+PTQ (7h43m) is 2.2× faster than SVD-LLM (16h46m).

## Relation to Prior Work

UniQL builds on the structured weight-decomposition lineage of MoDeGPT (Lin et al., 2025) and SVD-LLM (Wang et al., 2025b), but departs from both by replacing the computationally expensive pseudo-inverse with ridge leverage scores and by introducing a quantization-aware SVD that fuses eigenvalues into the left singular vectors to reduce INT4 quantization error. It also extends the compression scope beyond Transformers to SSMs (Mamba) and hybrid architectures, for which no unified post-training compression framework previously existed. The masked LoRA fine-tuning on the un-pruned sorted model (rather than the pruned model, as in SVD-LLM) and the on-device configurable pruning rates distinguish UniQL from elastic-training approaches like Flextron and LLaMaFlex, which require curated training data and are tied to a single model family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation—dynamic resource availability on edge devices making fixed-size compressed models unreliable—is well-articulated and addresses a real deployment gap that prior one-shot compression methods do not fill. The one-pass O(1) compression supporting multiple pruning rates (vs. O(n) re-computation) is a clean systems contribution, and the extension to SSMs and hybrids is timely as these architectures gain traction. Limitations include the continued need for a server-class GPU for the initial compression pass, reliance on the relatively small Alpaca dataset for fine-tuning, and the fact that adaptive pruning is restricted to structured channel removal (no unstructured or head-level granularity at deployment). Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to larger models (70B+) and whether the masked fine-tuning can be replaced by lighter recovery strategies.
