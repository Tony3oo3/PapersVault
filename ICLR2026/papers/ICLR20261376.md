## Problem

Universality results for equivariant neural networks are rare and typically restricted to impractical settings: they either require hidden spaces whose dimension scales with group size (regular or higher-order tensor representations) or are confined to specialized architectures in the invariant regime. A key open question is whether standard separation notions adequately characterize the approximable function class for equivariant networks, and what role depth and readout layers play in achieving universality under separation constraints.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on vectors in permutation representations R^X, where a finite group G acts on a finite set X, and produces either G-equivariant maps R^X → R^X or G-invariant maps R^X → R. The universality guarantee is not over all continuous equivariant/invariant functions but precisely over those respecting the network's separation relation: for invariant networks this is a single equivalence relation ρ, while for equivariant networks it is a tuple of per-coordinate relations (ρ_x)_{x∈X} that jointly constrain which input pairs each output coordinate may identify. The approximation is in the topology of uniform convergence on compact sets, with network widths (multiplicities of layer spaces) allowed to grow arbitrarily.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified framework of universality classes parameterized by layer spaces (convolutional, invariant, PointNet, linear) and proves three main theorems. For invariant networks, Theorem 1 shows that appending a fully connected readout layer L after invariant layers I guarantees approximation of every continuous function respecting the separation relation ρ, resolving known pathologies where separation power and approximation power diverge. For equivariant networks, the authors demonstrate via a CNN example that standard separation is too coarse (it identifies the trivial relation while the network is strictly non-universal), and introduce entry-wise separability: the collection of separation relations obtained by projecting each network output onto individual coordinates. Theorem 2 then proves that once entry-wise separation stabilizes with depth (which occurs at finite depth by prior results), the network achieves entry-wise separation-constrained universality. Theorem 3 provides an alternative route: a final convolutional layer of width 1 acts as an equivariant surrogate for the fully connected readout, yielding universality without requiring depth stabilization, and recovers prior results such as the universality of 3-layer PointNets.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or quantitative benchmarks. The main contributions are three universality theorems: (1) Theorem 1 establishes that invariant networks with a fully connected readout layer achieve separation-constrained universality, i.e., they approximate exactly the class of continuous functions respecting their separation relation; (2) Theorem 2 shows that equivariant networks achieve entry-wise separation-constrained universality once the entry-wise separation relation stabilizes with depth (guaranteed at finite depth D by Corollary 1); (3) Theorem 3 proves that equivariant networks with a width-1 convolutional output layer achieve entry-wise separation-constrained universality without any depth requirement. Additionally, Example 3 demonstrates that standard (global) separation is strictly coarser than entry-wise separation for equivariant networks, making the latter necessary to characterize their universality class.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not perform empirical comparisons but instead subsumes and extends several prior theoretical results. Theorem 3 directly recovers the universality result of Segol & Lipman (2020) for 3-layer PointNets (U_σ(C,P,C) = C_{S_n}(R^n, R^n)) as a corollary. The framework generalizes the WL-separation-constrained universality of Chen et al. (2019) and the orbit-separation universality of Joshi et al. (2023) from the invariant setting to a unified equivariant framework. Crucially, the paper resolves a gap identified by Pacini et al. (2025b), who showed that invariant networks with identical separation power could have strictly different approximation power (U_σ(C,I) ⊊ U_σ(P,I) ⊊ C_{S_n}(R^n,R)); the new theorems show that adding a fully connected readout (Theorem 1) or a convolutional output layer (Theorem 3) eliminates this mismatch. The introduction of entry-wise separability (Definition 6) is shown to be strictly stronger than standard separation in the equivariant case (Example 3), where standard separation would predict full universality but the actual universality class is a proper subset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the separation-constrained universality framework of Pacini et al. (2025b) from shallow to deep equivariant networks, and unifies previously architecture-specific results (Chen et al. 2019 for WL-separation in GNNs, Joshi et al. 2023 for orbit separation in geometric GNNs, Segol & Lipman 2020 for PointNets) under a single general theorem. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of entry-wise separability, motivated by the observation (Example 3) that standard separability is too coarse for equivariant settings: convolutional networks can have trivial global separation yet fail to be universal. The invariant-network result (Theorem 1) generalizes the readout-layer universality of Zaheer et al. (2017) and Qi et al. (2017) to arbitrary layer spaces, while the equivariant theorems (2, 3) subsume the Segol & Lipman result as a special case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first general, architecture-agnostic characterization of when deep equivariant networks are universal, identifying depth stabilization and readout layers as the decisive mechanisms rather than representation dimensionality. The entry-wise separability concept is a clean and necessary refinement that resolves a gap between separation power and approximation power in the equivariant setting. Limitations are significant: results are restricted to permutation representations and point-wise activations, provide no quantitative approximation rates, and say nothing about optimization or trainability. Open questions include extending the framework to non-permutation representations, higher-order tensor layers, and establishing whether the depth thresholds are tight in practice.
