## Problem

Track-and-Stop (TAS) and Sticky Track-and-Stop (S-TAS) are pioneering algorithms for pure exploration problems that enjoy asymptotic optimality as the confidence parameter δ→0, yet their non-asymptotic (finite-confidence) guarantees remained completely unknown. This gap is significant because in practice δ is moderate, and the sampling rules of both algorithms are known to be unstable when data are scarce, making it unclear whether their empirical competitiveness translates into theoretical finite-confidence bounds.

## Input / Output

The algorithms receive K arms from a σ²-sub-Gaussian canonical exponential family with means constrained to a structure set M ⊆ [μ_min, μ_max]^K (encoding e.g. Lipschitz or unimodal constraints), a confidence level δ ∈ (0,1), and a set-valued answer function i*(μ) mapping each mean vector to its correct answers. They output a recommended answer from a finite answer space I and a stopping time τ_δ. A key structural requirement is that for every μ ∈ M, at least one correct answer i ∈ i*(μ) satisfies μ ∉ cl(¬i), ensuring the problem is well-posed. For S-TAS, an additional problem-dependent constant ε_μ (guaranteed by upper hemicontinuity of i_F) controls how quickly the candidate answer set collapses.

## Method

The analysis proceeds under a sequence of good events E_t where the cumulative KL divergence between empirical and true means is bounded by 8K log(s) for all s ≥ √t, with total failure probability ≤ 2eK. The core technical step is lower-bounding the stopping rule's max-min optimization (over answers and confounding instances) by the quantity the algorithm actually uses in its sampling rule—specifically, the sum over past rounds of inf_{λ∈¬i_s} Σ_k ω_k(s) d(μ̃_k(s), λ_k)—up to sub-linear error terms. For TAS, the single-valuedness of i*(μ) allows a direct switch from ¬i*(μ) to ¬i_s; for S-TAS, an additional time T_μ is needed where upper hemicontinuity of i_F(μ) ensures the candidate set I_s collapses to i_F(μ) ∪ (I \ i*(μ)), after which the same argument applies. The resulting bounds take the form E_μ[τ_δ] ≤ 2eK + 10K⁴ + T_0(δ), where T_0(δ) is implicitly defined by β_{t,δ} ≤ tT*(μ)⁻¹ − g(t) with g(t) growing as Õ(t^{3/4}), recovering asymptotic optimality as δ→0.

## Results

The paper establishes the first non-asymptotic (finite-confidence) upper bounds for both Track-and-Stop (TAS) and Sticky Track-and-Stop (S-TAS). For TAS (Theorem 1), the expected stopping time satisfies E_μ[τ_δ] ≤ 2eK + 10K⁴ + T₀(δ), where T₀(δ) is the smallest t such that β_{t,δ} ≤ tT*(μ)⁻¹ − g(t), with g(t) = 64σDLK²log(K)√(t log²(t)) + 16σD√(Kt^{3/2} log(t)). For S-TAS (Theorem 2), the bound is analogous but includes an additional problem-dependent constant T_μ that captures the time needed to stabilize the candidate answer set. Both bounds recover the known asymptotic optimality (T*(μ)·log(1/δ) scaling) as δ→0, with sub-linear correction terms of order Õ(t^{3/4}).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental comparisons or benchmark tables. The key positioning is relative to prior finite-confidence analyses that required algorithmic modifications: Degenne et al. (2019) proposed an optimistic TAS variant with confidence intervals (solving a harder max-max-min problem), and Barrier et al. (2022) stabilized TAS by skewing toward uniform exploration when data are scarce. In contrast, this paper provides finite-confidence guarantees for the original, unmodified TAS algorithm of Garivier & Kaufmann (2016), without requiring any such modifications. For S-TAS, the paper provides the first finite-confidence guarantees for arbitrary multiple-answer pure exploration problems, a setting where no prior algorithm had such guarantees. The authors note that their proof technique (approximating the stopping rule with the sampling rule under good events) differs fundamentally from the asymptotic analysis of Degenne & Koolen (2019), which relies on convergence of N(t)/t to the convex hull of oracle weights.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first non-asymptotic (finite-confidence) guarantees for the original Track-and-Stop (TAS, Garivier & Kaufmann, 2016) and Sticky Track-and-Stop (S-TAS, Degenne & Koolen, 2019) algorithms, which previously only enjoyed asymptotic optimality as δ→0. Prior attempts at finite-confidence analysis required substantial modifications to TAS—Degenne et al. (2019) introduced an optimistic variant with confidence intervals in the sampling rule, and Barrier et al. (2022) skewed the sampling toward uniform exploration—both at the cost of algorithmic complexity or empirical performance. The present work shows that the unmodified original algorithms can be analyzed directly using a good-event framework inspired by finite-confidence techniques in Degenne et al. (2019) and Jourdan & Degenne (2023), with the key technical step being to approximate the stopping rule's max-min optimization with the sampling rule's per-round oracle-weight computation up to sublinear terms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

These results close a long-standing gap by providing theoretical justification for the empirically observed strong performance of TAS and S-TAS at moderate confidence levels, and constitute the first finite-confidence analysis for the general multiple-answer pure exploration setting. The bounds, while recovering asymptotic optimality, carry additional problem-dependent constants (e.g., T_μ for S-TAS) and polylogarithmic factors, leaving a gap between lower and upper bounds in the finite-confidence regime. Open questions include whether T_μ can be eliminated by a more strategic answer-selection rule, whether the analysis extends to infinite answer spaces (as in Poiani et al., 2025), and whether tighter finite-confidence lower bounds can be established for general pure exploration problems.
