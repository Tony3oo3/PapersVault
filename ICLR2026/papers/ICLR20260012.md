## Problem

Test-time alignment of LLMs faces a fundamental trade-off: token-level guided decoding suffers from the curse of horizon (unreliable credit assignment over long trajectories), while response-level iterative refinement suffers from the curse of dimensionality (intractable search over the full sequence space). No existing framework resolves this tension, leaving a gap between the precision of local guidance and the stability of global refinement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260012/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TMPC takes a text prompt, a frozen LLM (e.g., LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct), and a reward model (or a direct evaluation signal such as unit-test pass rates for code) as inputs, and produces a single aligned output — a translation, long-form response, or program — selected as the highest-scoring candidate across all planning iterations. The method requires no parameter updates; the frozen LLM serves simultaneously as the dynamics model (deterministic text transitions) and the proposal distribution for generating candidate rollouts. Subgoals are task-specific: sentence spans in translation, coherent content chunks in long-form generation, and partially-correct code snippets (passing at least one unit test) in program synthesis.

## Method

TMPC adapts Model Predictive Control (specifically the MPPI aggregation scheme) to discrete text generation via a receding-horizon planning loop. Two principles drive the framework: (1) Hindsight Subgoal Identification, where after each batch of K rollouts is scored, high-reward segments exceeding a threshold α are retrospectively extracted and stored in a bounded buffer B, with lower-quality entries evicted as better ones arrive; (2) Subgoal-Conditioned Re-Generation, where subsequent iterations generate new full-length rollouts by conditioning the LLM on sampled subgoals from B, so that each planning step builds upon previously validated successes rather than exploring from scratch. This subgoal-level operation shortens the effective credit horizon (mitigating the curse of horizon) while constraining the search to high-reward regions (mitigating the curse of dimensionality). The aggregation function G selects non-contiguous high-reward actions from rollouts, and the final output is the best-scoring full response across all iterations, requiring only 3 iterations × 3 rollouts (10 total LLM calls) for long-form tasks.

## Results

On WMT'24 paragraph-level MT (zh→en), TMPC achieves 94.62 SEGALE_comet with 0.00 NA Ratio, surpassing GPT-4o (94.58) and the Best-of-60 baseline (90.97) with a fraction of the compute. On long-form HH-RLHF responses, TMPC outperforms both the strongest training-time baseline (DPO) and the strongest test-time baseline (Best-of-20) in GPT-4 win rate using only 10 total generations (3 iterations × 3 rollouts + 1 initial). On MBPP program synthesis, TMPC reaches a 61% pass rate, substantially exceeding TPO (48% at 4 iterations) and Best-of-35 sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On zh→en translation, TMPC (94.62) exceeds GPT-4o (94.58) and all test-time baselines, with the largest margins over token-level methods ARGS (63.99) and GenARM (61.18), which suffer severe NA Ratio degradation (31–40%). On zh→ru, TPO (92.63) edges out TMPC (91.53), while on zh→de, training-time DPO (93.48) and SimPO (93.40) remain ahead of TMPC (91.73), suggesting TMPC's advantage is most pronounced where the base model has stronger linguistic familiarity. On long-form HH-RLHF responses, TMPC outperforms DPO and Best-of-20 in GPT-4 win rate with only 10 generations, and matches or beats TPO at equal compute (10 LLM calls each) while TPO needs ~2× the budget to close the gap. On MBPP, TMPC (61% pass rate) substantially outperforms TPO (48% at 4 iterations) and Best-of-35, demonstrating that systematic subgoal-based exploration of partially-correct solutions is more effective than unstructured sampling or iterative textual critique. RE-Control (86.39 on zh→en) is the strongest token-level method but still trails TMPC by 8.2 points, confirming that response-level planning with hindsight subgoals avoids the compounding errors of token-level guidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

TMPC adapts the receding-horizon control structure of Model Predictive Control (MPC) and the soft aggregation of MPPI from continuous control to discrete text generation, but departs from standard MPC by replacing its assumption of predefined segment boundaries with hindsight subgoal identification discovered from rollouts. It positions itself against two families of test-time alignment: token-level guided decoding (ARGS, GenARM, RE-Control), which suffers from the curse of horizon, and response-level iterative refinement (TPO), which suffers from the curse of dimensionality. The subgoal-buffer mechanism draws on hierarchical RL and hindsight goal generation, but uniquely identifies subgoals purely in hindsight from a frozen LLM's rollouts without requiring an explicit environment, learned policy, or task-specific segmentation heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TMPC offers a unified, task-agnostic test-time alignment framework that works across structurally different tasks (sentence-bounded translation, boundary-free long-form generation, and fragmented code synthesis) without any parameter updates, which is practically valuable for deploying frozen LLMs in settings where fine-tuning is infeasible. However, the method is fundamentally constrained by the base model's expressiveness—it can only steer generation toward outputs already within the model's distribution—and its reliance on a trained reward model limits applicability to domains where reliable preference signals exist. Open questions include whether iterative subgoal accumulation can shift the output distribution beyond the base model's support, and how TMPC integrates with lightweight training-time adaptation under distribution shift.
