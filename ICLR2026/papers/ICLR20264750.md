## Problem

Implicit Neural Representations (INRs) with Random Fourier Coding (RFC) for sparse-view CT reconstruction cannot leverage the rectangular shape priors prevalent in industrial objects, and their harmonic representation is fundamentally limited by the Gibbs phenomenon at discontinuities. Existing INR variants fail to explicitly encode the axis-aligned or rotated rectangular structures that dominate man-made industrial CT scans, leading to wave-like artifacts and blurred edges.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264750/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse sinogram (parallel-beam projections at 12, 14, or 16 angles for industrial data; 48 angles for medical data) as the sole training signal in a self-supervised setting—no paired dense-view data is required. It outputs a 2D CT attenuation coefficient map by evaluating a coordinate-to-attenuation function over a spatial grid. The encoding produces a fixed-length feature vector (456 dimensions for industrial, 3000 for medical) that is fed to a small MLP, keeping total trainable parameters around 2.5×10⁵.

## Method

NAB replaces random Fourier features with a set of M differentiable rectangular bins, each constructed as the Hadamard product of two 1D square waves (differences of shifted hyperbolic tangent functions) along orthogonal axes. A rotation matrix is applied in the input coordinate space before computing the tanh differences, allowing each bin to adapt its orientation, position, size, and steepness end-to-end via gradient flow from the projection-domain L2 loss. A multi-scale steepness mechanism (sampling k_i from a set of values) generalizes the sharp rectangular bins to smoother, bulge-like shapes for curved geometries. The binning vector is mapped to the attenuation coefficient by a 4-layer ReLU FCN, and all bin parameters (u_i, v_i, h_i, w_i, θ_i, k_i, λ_i) are jointly optimized with the network weights. The authors prove that as steepness → ∞, the NAB encoding converges to binary indicator vectors, establishing a theoretical link to random hard bins in kernel methods.

## Results

On the CaCO₃ industrial dataset (16 views), NAB achieves 43.61 dB PSNR and 0.996 SSIM, outperforming the strongest INR baseline (INR_f) by 14.60 dB and Instant-NGP by 12.80 dB, while using only 2.52×10⁵ trainable parameters (vs. 2.96×10⁶ for Instant-NGP). On the Workpieces dataset with curved geometries (16 views), NAB reaches 36.26 dB PSNR / 0.938 SSIM, still leading INR_f by 2.92 dB. On the Medical-Axial dataset (48 views, no rectangular structures), NAB improves over INR_RFC by 0.56 dB PSNR (33.28 vs. 32.72), demonstrating robustness beyond its rectangular-prior design.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NAB decisively outperforms all classical iterative methods (FBP, SIRT, NAG_LS) by 10–19 dB PSNR, confirming that self-supervised neural approaches dominate on sparse-view CT. Against deep-learning baselines, NAB leads Instant-NGP by 12.80 dB (16-view CaCO₃) and 5.09 dB (16-view Workpieces) in PSNR while using roughly 12× fewer parameters. Compared to the closest INR baseline (INR_f with random Fourier features and identical FCN architecture), NAB gains 9.64–14.60 dB on CaCO₃ and 1.20–5.39 dB on Workpieces, with the smaller gap on Workpieces attributed to curved geometries that partially violate the rectangular prior. Even against the much larger INR_l₂ (4× more parameters, 7-layer FCN), NAB still wins by 4.36–4.72 dB on CaCO₃. On the Medical-Axial dataset (no rectangular structures), NAB achieves a modest 0.56 dB PSNR gain over INR_RFC, confirming that the multi-scale steepness mechanism generalizes beyond rectangular shapes, though the advantage narrows considerably when the shape prior is absent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard Implicit Neural Representation (INR) paradigm for CT reconstruction, which encodes spatial coordinates via Random Fourier Features (Tancik et al., 2020) or hash grids (Müller et al., 2022), by replacing the encoding with a structured, differentiable binning mechanism built from shifted hyperbolic tangent functions. It explicitly targets the limitation that random encodings cannot exploit the rectangular shape priors prevalent in industrial CT, drawing a theoretical bridge to random hard bins in kernel methods (Rahimi & Recht, 2007) while making the bins learnable end-to-end. The multi-scale steepness extension generalizes the rectangular bins toward smoother bump-like functions, positioning the method between rigid geometric priors and fully flexible INR representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work offers a principled, interpretable alternative to black-box encodings for self-supervised CT reconstruction, with the added benefit of end-to-end learnable bin geometry (position, size, rotation, steepness). However, the gains are most pronounced when the rectangular prior matches the data (industrial objects); on medical lung CT the improvement over a matched RFC baseline is only ~0.56 dB, suggesting the prior's value is domain-specific. Open questions include whether combining NAB with Fourier features yields complementary benefits, whether the approach extends naturally to 3D volumes, and what the NTK convergence behavior of the tanh-based encoding looks like during training.
