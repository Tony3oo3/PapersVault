## Problem

Classical Random Utility Models used in RLHF rely on the Independence of Irrelevant Alternatives (IIA) assumption, which collapses diverse human preferences into a single universal utility function. When utilities are genuinely correlated across items, the logit model cannot make correct counterfactual predictions, yet no prior work established whether pairwise preference data (the standard collection paradigm) is even sufficient to recover correlation structure, or what data and sample complexity are needed for the correlated probit model.

## Input / Output

The method consumes best-of-three preference observations—triplets (i,j,k) with the full ranking of the three alternatives—rather than the conventional pairwise comparisons. It outputs the mean vector μ and covariance matrix Σ of the underlying multivariate normal utility distribution, subject to the normalization constraints ⟨μ,1⟩=0, Σ1=0, and Tr(Σ)=n. The estimator requires N ≥ Õ(n²ε⁻²γ⁻²⁴) samples where γ is a lower bound on the probability of any particular three-way ranking, and runs in polynomial time.

## Method

The paper first proves (Theorem 3.2) that pairwise comparisons are fundamentally insufficient to identify correlations in the probit model, since for μ=0 all pairwise probabilities are 1/2 regardless of Σ. It then shows best-of-three observations are both necessary and sufficient: for each triplet, the 3D probit is projected onto a 2D subspace orthogonal to 1, and an isotropic whitening transform converts the ranking probabilities into recoverable angles between transformed direction vectors. For the general n-alternative case, the estimator aggregates O(n²) (rather than O(n³)) triplet-level estimates by solving a consistent scaling system across overlapping triplets, yielding a polynomial-time algorithm with near-optimal finite-sample guarantees matching a matching lower bound.

## Results

On synthetic data (Table 1), the probit model trained on best-of-three observations matches the ground-truth 'direct' method across all covariance structures (e.g., 0.79 median accuracy on the binary-correlated case vs. 0.50 for logit and 0.45–0.54 for pairwise probit). On real-world datasets (Table 2), best-of-three probit achieves 0.65–0.68 median accuracy on Sushi, 0.59–0.62 on MovieLens, and 0.59–0.62 on Netflix, consistently outperforming pairwise probit by 1–3 percentage points. The theoretical sample complexity is Õ(n²ε⁻²γ⁻²⁴ log(n/δ) log⁶(n/(γε))), matching a matching lower bound up to logarithmic factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four methods: logit (McFadden, 1980), matrix completion (Koren et al., 2009), probit trained on pairwise comparisons, and the proposed probit trained on best-of-three comparisons. The headline result is that best-of-three probit consistently outperforms pairwise probit by 1–3 percentage points in median accuracy across all real datasets (e.g., 0.68 vs. 0.65 on Sushi-A, 0.61 vs. 0.59 on Netflix-100k), confirming the theoretical finding that pairwise data is fundamentally insufficient to recover correlations. Against the logit baseline, best-of-three probit matches or slightly exceeds it (e.g., 0.61 vs. 0.61 on Jokes, 0.61 vs. 0.61 on Netflix-100k), with the gap being modest because mean effects dominate on these datasets. Against matrix completion, best-of-three probit is competitive or slightly better on most configurations (e.g., 0.61 vs. 0.60 on MovieLens-10k onehot, 0.61 vs. 0.61 on Netflix-100k), while matrix completion requires many more observations per user to learn the choice distribution. On synthetic data with strong correlations (μ=0, Σ=bin), best-of-three probit achieves 0.79 accuracy matching the direct ground-truth method, while pairwise probit drops to 0.45–0.54 and logit remains at 0.50, starkly illustrating the IIA failure mode.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the classical RUM literature (Luce, McFadden, Train) where the logit model and its IIA assumption dominate, and from the RLHF pipeline (Christiano et al.) that inherits this pairwise-comparison paradigm. While prior probit estimation work (Bunch 1991, Natarajan et al. 2000, Ding et al. 2024) addressed computational challenges of fitting multinomial probit models, none established what preference data is fundamentally required for identifiability. The paper carves new ground by proving that pairwise comparisons are information-theoretically insufficient for recovering correlations in the probit model, and that best-of-three observations are both necessary and sufficient — a result with no prior analogue in the choice-modeling or RLHF literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The impossibility result for pairwise data challenges a foundational assumption in how RLHF preference data is collected, suggesting that current pipelines may be structurally blind to correlated user preferences. However, the empirical improvements on real datasets (Netflix, MovieLens, Sushi) are modest, with the paper itself noting that mean effects dominate and the gain over logit is small, leaving open whether the theoretical gap translates to meaningful personalization gains at scale. Open questions include whether the sample complexity bounds (polynomial in n with high-degree log factors) are tight in practice, whether higher-order data (best-of-4+) yields further gains, and how these results extend to user-specific (personalized) correlated models rather than population-level ones.
