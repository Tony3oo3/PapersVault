## Problem

Existing low-rank compression methods for large foundation models rely on linear SVD truncation, which causes severe information loss at high compression ratios. While nonlinear kernels could recover more information by operating in higher-dimensional spaces, they require reconstructing full weight matrices during forward propagation (prohibitive memory cost) and are incompatible with adaptive per-matrix rank allocation. The paper addresses both limitations simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261577/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on individual pre-trained weight matrices (m×n) within attention (Q, K, V, Out) and MLP (FC1, FC2) layers of transformer models, replacing each with two low-rank factors A∈R^{m×h×r}, B∈R^{n×h×r} and a shared coefficient vector μ∈R^h. The compressed model then processes standard inputs (text sequences for LLMs, RGB images for vision transformers) and produces next-token predictions or class labels. A key design constraint is that the forward pass never materializes the full m×n weight matrix, keeping memory usage linear in the compressed parameter count rather than the original.

## Method

NLA approximates each weight matrix W via a piecewise-linear kernel k_pw(A,B) = Σ_l μ_l‖A_{i,l} − B_{j,l}‖², which implicitly maps low-rank factors into a higher-dimensional space to capture nonlinear interactions lost by linear truncation. To avoid reconstructing the full weight matrix during inference, a dedicated forward algorithm decomposes the kernel into input self-terms, output self-terms, and an interaction term, each computed directly from the low-rank factors and shared coefficients. For adaptive compression, each matrix is partitioned into h segment-level triplets (A_l, μ_l, B_l), and an importance score per triplet is computed as a smoothed product of parameter sensitivity |w·∇_w L| and its temporal uncertainty. A cubic sparsity schedule gradually reduces the global budget from b_0 to b_f during retraining, and at each step the least-important triplets are masked (μ_l set to zero), allocating more pieces to sensitive matrices and fewer to less critical ones.

## Results

On LLaMA-7B at 50% compression, NLA achieves a WikiText-2 perplexity of 15.21, substantially lower than SVD-LLM (23.97) and all other SVD-based baselines; after fine-tuning, NLA further reduces perplexity to 9.68 vs. 13.26 for SVD-LLM. On common-sense reasoning (7 benchmarks), NLA achieves 0.37 average accuracy at 50% compression, outperforming the best baseline SVD-LLM (0.33) by ~12% relatively. On Swin-Base (ImageNet-1K), NLA reaches 82.7% top-1 accuracy at 54.2% compression, surpassing PELA (82.4% at only 29.2% compression) while reducing parameters by 35.3% more.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NLA consistently outperforms all linear low-rank baselines (SVD, FWSVD, ASVD, SVD-LLM) on LLaMA-7B across language modeling and common-sense reasoning tasks. At 50% compression, NLA's WikiText-2 perplexity (15.21) is 37% lower than SVD-LLM (23.97), and at 70% compression NLA (39.91) even beats SVD-LLM at 60% (42.30), demonstrating superior robustness under aggressive compression. On common-sense reasoning, NLA achieves 0.37 average accuracy vs. 0.33 for SVD-LLM at 50%, with the gap widening at 70% (0.33 vs. 0.31). For vision models, NLA on Swin-Base achieves 82.7% top-1 accuracy at 54.2% compression, outperforming PELA (82.4% at 29.2%), TinyBERT (78.8% at 33.3%), and MaskAlign (79.1% at 33.3%) while compressing the model more than 2× more aggressively. In the cross-scale comparison (Table 12), NLA also beats MoDeGPT on both OPT-125M (31.34 vs. 38.37 perplexity) and OPT-1.3B (19.77 vs. 21.92) with fewer parameters. NLA is also compatible with quantization: combined with 4-bit GPTQ, NLA achieves 12.18 perplexity at 1.9 GB, outperforming SVD-LLM+4-bit (13.29 at 2.1 GB) and standalone 3-bit GPTQ (16.28 at 2.8 GB).

## Relation to Prior Work

NLA repurposes the piecewise-linear kernel from Dynamics-inspired Neuromorphic Learning (Pei & Wang, 2023) as a nonlinear low-rank approximator for pretrained weight matrices, a use case that prior work had not explored for model compression. It departs from the entire line of SVD-based linear compression methods (FWSVD, ASVD, SVD-LLM) by replacing the linear inner product with a kernel that operates in a higher-dimensional feature space, thereby reducing information loss from rank truncation. The adaptive budget allocation mechanism borrows the cubic sparsity scheduling from Movement Pruning (Sanh et al., 2020) and applies it at the granularity of kernel segments (triplets), enabling per-matrix compression ratios during retraining. A key engineering departure is the forward algorithm (Algorithm 1) that decomposes the kernel into self-terms and interaction terms, avoiding the explicit reconstruction of full weight matrices that plagues other kernel-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NLA addresses a fundamental limitation of linear low-rank compression—irreducible information loss from singular value truncation—by showing that a lightweight nonlinear kernel can recover substantially more of the original weight structure at the same or higher compression ratios. The practical significance is amplified by the memory-efficient forward algorithm, which reduces training memory to ~12% of the naive kernel reconstruction for OPT-1.3B, making nonlinear compression viable on standard hardware. Limitations include a modest increase in inference latency (~45% slower than linear SVD on LLaMA-7B) and the requirement for a retraining phase, which limits applicability to truly post-training-only settings. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-modal models, whether the piecewise-linear kernel is optimal or whether other lightweight nonlinearities could further close the gap, and how the method interacts with emerging quantization-aware training pipelines at scale.
