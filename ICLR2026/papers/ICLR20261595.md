## Problem

Outcome-based RL for LLMs assigns reward only at the end of a reasoning chain, treating all intermediate steps as equally important and encouraging redundant overthinking. Existing credit-assignment methods (VinePPO, SPO, GTPO) decompose the final reward into step-level signals but lack theoretical guarantees of fairness, particularly for partially correct chains where some steps are valid despite an incorrect final answer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261595/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a math problem prompt and a set of LLM-generated reasoning steps (extracted and compressed from a vanilla rollout) as input. It produces step-level Sequential Shapley Value (SSV) credit scores and a fine-tuned LLM policy that generates shorter reasoning chains. Unusually, the reasoning steps are reordered into alternative chains via an Insertion MDP purely for credit computation—not for generation—and the reward signal is binary (correct/incorrect final answer) evaluated on each reordered chain.

## Method

SSVPO models the reasoning process as an Insertion MDP where a state is a partial reasoning chain and an action inserts a candidate step at an arbitrary position. The Sequential Marginal Contribution (SMC) measures the reward change from inserting a step, and the Sequential Shapley Value (SSV) averages SMC across all permutations and insertion positions, extending classical Shapley values from the spatial (multi-agent) domain to the temporal (sequential reasoning) domain. SSV provably satisfies four fairness axioms (efficiency, symmetry, additivity, null step) and serves as a variance-minimal, action-independent value baseline in a PPO-style objective, yielding the advantage A_t^SSV = SMC − SSV. This enables fair credit to partially correct steps, identifies zero-credit redundant steps for chain shortening, and reduces PPO clipping bias for more stable policy optimization.

## Results

On Qwen3-4B, SSVPO achieves 86.7% overall accuracy across 7 math benchmarks, outperforming the vanilla model (82.1%) by 4.6% and the best outcome-based baseline RLOO (84.9%) by 1.8%, while reducing average token usage from 6,327 (vanilla) to 5,178 (−18.1%). On the lighter DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B backbone, SSVPO reaches 68.7% overall accuracy versus 66.3% for SPO and 63.7% for VinePPO, an 11.6% gain over the vanilla model. SSVPO achieves the best accuracy on 6 of 7 benchmarks (second only to RLOO on SVAMP by 0.2%) and the best token efficiency on 5 of 7, with the largest token reduction on GSM8K (1,320 vs. 2,215 vanilla).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against outcome-based RL baselines on Qwen3-4B, SSVPO outperforms RLOO on 6 of 7 accuracy benchmarks (narrowly losing SVAMP by 0.2%) and on 5 of 7 token-efficiency benchmarks, with the largest accuracy margin on AIME'25 (+5.6 pp) and the largest token saving on GSM8K (−345 tokens). SSVPO dominates GRPO on all 7 accuracy benchmarks (up to +5.0 pp on MATH-500) and on 5 of 7 token counts. SSVPO also beats DAPO on all 7 accuracy benchmarks and all 7 token counts, with DAPO actually using more tokens than the vanilla model overall (+7.9%). Against credit-assignment baselines on the DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B backbone, SSVPO achieves the highest accuracy on all 7 benchmarks (68.7% overall vs. 66.3% SPO and 63.7% VinePPO), though SPO and VinePPO use fewer tokens on some harder benchmarks (e.g., SPO uses 7,137 vs. SSVPO's 8,059 on AMC'23), suggesting SSVPO trades a small token increase for substantially higher accuracy on complex problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

SSVPO extends the Shapley value framework from cooperative game theory and multi-agent RL (where it measures each agent's marginal contribution across coalitions) to the temporal domain of LLM reasoning chains, where steps are sequentially dependent and order-sensitive. The key conceptual departure is the Insertion MDP, which models reasoning as a process of inserting steps at arbitrary positions rather than appending them, enabling the computation of position-aware marginal contributions. This builds directly on PPO's clipped surrogate objective and the Shapley-Q credit assignment lineage from MARL, while addressing a gap identified in recent credit-assignment RL methods (VinePPO, SPO, GTPO) that lack theoretical fairness guarantees and can misattribute credit in partially correct chains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first step-level credit assignment method for LLM RL training with provable fairness (four Shapley axioms adapted to sequential reasoning) and variance-minimality guarantees, addressing a fundamental limitation of outcome-based RL where all steps receive uniform credit regardless of actual contribution. The 18.1% token reduction alongside accuracy gains is practically significant for deployment cost, and the identification of zero-credit steps offers a principled mechanism for chain shortening. Open questions include the computational cost of evaluating all permutations (which scales factorially with chain length, mitigated here by the reorder_num hyperparameter), whether the Insertion MDP's assumption that reordered chains remain evaluable holds for very long or highly structured reasoning, and whether the approach generalizes beyond mathematical reasoning to other domains where intermediate steps lack verifiable correctness signals.
