## Problem

Existing audio-language models produce only short, surface-level music descriptions (broad genre, tempo, instrumentation) and answer only shallow questions, because training data is dominated by short Western instrumental clips with superficial annotations that omit harmonic structure, vocal/lyric grounding, and cultural context. Music's inherently layered nature—spanning surface acoustic attributes, mid-level harmonic and formal structure, and higher-level lyrical and cultural meaning—demands deliberate multi-step reasoning that no prior model has been trained to perform.

## Input / Output

The model consumes full-length songs (up to 20 minutes, extending AF3's ~10-minute limit) alongside a text prompt requesting a caption, QA answer, or reasoning trace, with context extended to ~24k tokens to accommodate long-form outputs. Outputs are structured: captions average ~452 words covering harmony, structure, timbre, lyrics, and cultural context; QA and captioning tasks produce a chain-of-thought trace enclosed in <think> tags followed by a final answer in <answer> tags. Rotary Time Embeddings (RoTE) replace standard RoPE in the audio encoder, using absolute timestamps (40 ms stride) rather than token indices to preserve fine-grained temporal information like chord progressions and tempo changes.

## Method

Music Flamingo is built in three stages. First, an enhanced AF3 backbone is continue-pretrained on multilingual ASR (Emilia, CoVoST, MUST, Amazon-SIFT), multi-talker ASR (CHiME, Switchboard, ALI Meeting), and speech-centric skills to strengthen vocal and spoken-language understanding. Second, the backbone is fine-tuned on MF-Skills (~5.2M samples: ~3.4M layered captions and ~1.8M skill-targeted QAs), a dataset produced by a four-stage pipeline: frontier-model caption synthesis on 30 s segments, MIR-tool metadata extraction (madmom, essentia, Chordino, Parakeet), LLM-based generation of multi-aspect captions and QAs grounded in that metadata, and MLLM quality filtering. Third, a post-training stage introduces MF-Think (~176K chain-of-thought examples grounded in music theory) for cold-start SFT, followed by GRPO reinforcement learning with three custom rewards: a binary format reward for tag structure, an accuracy reward for QA, and a structured-thinking reward that scores captions against ground-truth metadata fields (genre, BPM, key, structure, instruments, theory, etc.) via normalized string matching.

## Results

Music Flamingo achieves state-of-the-art results across 12 music understanding and reasoning benchmarks. Headline numbers include 76.83% accuracy on MMAU-Music (vs. 73.95% for AF3), 74.58% on MuChoMusic (vs. 52.10% for Qwen3-Omni), 90.86% on Medley-Solos-DB instrument classification, and a dramatic 12.9 WER on Opencpop Chinese lyrics transcription (vs. 53.7 for GPT-4o). On the proposed SongCaps captioning benchmark, human experts rate Music Flamingo 8.3/10 versus 6.5/10 for AF3, with LLM-as-a-judge scores of 8.8 (coverage) and 8.0 (correctness).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Music Flamingo outperforms its direct predecessor Audio Flamingo 3 on nearly all shared benchmarks: MMAU-Music (76.83 vs. 73.95), NSynth Source (75.89 vs. 65.5), Music Instruct (97.1 vs. 92.7), and SongCaps human rating (8.3 vs. 6.5), though it trails AF3 on Music AVQA (73.6 vs. 76.7). Against Qwen3-Omni, the gap is most dramatic on MuChoMusic (74.58 vs. 52.10, a 22.5-point margin) and MusicCaps captioning (8.8 vs. 7.2 GPT-5 score). On lyrics transcription, Music Flamingo dramatically surpasses both GPT-4o and Qwen2.5-Omni: 12.9 WER on Opencpop Chinese (vs. 53.7 and 55.7) and 19.6 WER on MUSDB18 English (vs. 32.7 and 68.7). It edges out Gemini-2.5 Flash on MMAU-Pro (65.60 vs. 64.90) and beats Pengi on GTZAN genre classification (84.45 vs. 80.00). The only clear loss is on Music AVQA, where AF3's audio-visual grounding gives it a 3.1-point advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

Music Flamingo extends the Audio Flamingo 3 backbone by first strengthening it with multilingual ASR and multi-talker data, then specializing it for music through a large-scale curated dataset (MF-Skills) and a post-training recipe combining chain-of-thought cold-start (MF-Think) with GRPO reinforcement learning. It departs from prior music LALMs (Mu-LLaMA, MusiLingo, LLARK) that relied on short instrumental clips and surface-level captions, instead scaling to full-length multicultural songs with layered, theory-aware annotations spanning harmony, structure, timbre, lyrics, and cultural context. The GRPO formulation with custom music-specific rewards (format, accuracy, structured thinking) adapts the DeepSeek-R1 paradigm to an open-ended audio reasoning setting, while Rotary Time Embeddings address the temporal grounding gap in Whisper-based encoders for fine-grained musical perception.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses the fundamental data bottleneck in music understanding by releasing MF-Skills (5.2M examples) and MF-Think (176K CoT examples), providing the community with the first large-scale, multi-aspect music reasoning corpus that goes beyond short Western instrumental snippets. The layered captioning formulation—connecting surface attributes to mid-level harmonic structure to higher-level lyrical and cultural meaning—offers a new template for how audio-language models should reason about complex, multi-modal signals. Key limitations remain: underrepresented cultural traditions, gaps in instrument-specific fine-grained skills (e.g., piano technique), and reliance on a single Whisper-based encoder that the authors' own linear probing shows is weak on low-level music properties like key detection. Open questions include whether the data curation pipeline generalizes to other audio domains and whether the RL post-training recipe transfers to non-music reasoning tasks.
