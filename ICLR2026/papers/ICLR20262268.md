## Problem

Existing LLM-based traffic signal control agents operate independently at single intersections, ignoring inter-intersection cooperation that is essential for network-wide optimization. This local-only design causes upstream spillback and suboptimal flow, while the multi-step reasoning needed for effective cooperation conflicts with the strict real-time constraint (decisions within a few seconds) and the varying complexity of traffic scenarios that demands adaptive reasoning depth.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262268/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent receives lane-level numerical features (queued/moving vehicle counts, average waiting time, occupancy) for its own and neighboring intersections, a directed subgraph encoding spatial relations, and a window of Δt=5 historical observation-action pairs, all serialized into structured text prompts. The output is a selected signal phase (one of four: ETWT, ELWL, NTST, NLSL) accompanied by a binary utility flag indicating whether the cached cooperative reasoning influenced the decision, plus a cached textual reasoning trace that serves as contextual guidance for subsequent timesteps.

## Method

CoLLMLight decouples cooperative reasoning from real-time decision-making via an asynchronous architecture: a spatiotemporal-aware cooperative reasoning (SR) module performs multi-step analysis (critical lane identification, spatial interaction analysis, temporal pattern analysis, reflection) over historical and spatial context and caches its output, while a real-time decision (RD) module rapidly selects a signal using only the latest observation and the cached SR guidance. To balance cooperation quality with reasoning efficiency, a two-stage cost-aware optimization is applied: first, adaptive reasoning chain optimization fine-tunes the LLM (LoRA on Llama 3.1-8B) on GPT-4o-generated traces, retaining only the shortest SR trace that supports the best long-term RD outcome; second, PPO-based reinforcement learning refines both modules with a composite reward that multiplies the RD correctness signal by a term penalizing reasoning length and rewarding the agent's own assessment of SR utility. This design enables the model to produce shorter reasoning chains under light traffic and longer ones under congestion, while keeping RD latency below the 3–5 s yellow-light window.

## Results

CoLLMLight-8B achieves the best zero-shot performance across all four real-world datasets on all three metrics. On the most challenging New York 1 network (196 intersections), it attains ATT of 1000.4s, AWT of 90.5s, and AQL of 1816.8, outperforming the best RL baseline (Advanced-CoLight: ATT 1037.9) and the closest LLM baseline (LLMLight-8B: ATT 1187.4). On New York 2, CoLLMLight-8B reduces AWT to 167.6s versus 359.8s for Advanced-CoLight and 388.7s for LLMLight-8B. On the smaller Jinan and Hangzhou networks, it achieves ATT of 267.9s and 308.5s respectively, maintaining consistent advantages over all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoLLMLight-8B outperforms all 12 baselines on every metric across all four real-world datasets. Against the closest LLM baseline LLMLight-8B (independent, non-cooperative agent), CoLLMLight-8B reduces ATT by 16.2% on New York 1 (1000.4 vs. 1187.4) and by 15.9% on New York 2 (1345.1 vs. 1599.4), with the gap widening on AWT (90.5 vs. 143.1 on NY1; 167.6 vs. 388.7 on NY2), confirming that inter-intersection cooperation is critical on dense networks. Among RL baselines, Advanced-CoLight is the strongest on New York (ATT 1037.9 on NY1) while AttendLight leads on Jinan/Hangzhou, but CoLLMLight-8B surpasses both consistently, e.g., ATT 1000.4 vs. 1037.9 (NY1) and 267.9 vs. 310.2 (Jinan). Transportation baselines (FixedTime, MaxPressure) are substantially outperformed, with MaxPressure being the best conventional method (ATT 1223.3 on NY1) yet still 18% worse than CoLLMLight-8B. Notably, CoLLMLight-8B also outperforms larger unoptimized LLMs within the same framework (Llama3.1-70B: ATT 1082.6; Qwen3-32B: 1040.8; R1-32B: 1117.2 on NY1), demonstrating that the cost-aware optimization strategy is more effective than simply scaling model size.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoLLMLight extends LLMLight (Lai et al., 2025), which introduced LLM-based agents for single-intersection traffic signal control, by adding explicit inter-intersection cooperative reasoning over spatiotemporal context. It also departs from the RL-based cooperative TSC lineage (CoLight, Advanced-CoLight, CosLight), which encodes cooperation implicitly through graph attention or collaborator matrices, by instead using language-driven semantic reasoning to make cooperation interpretable and generalizable. The asynchronous decision architecture and cost-aware optimization (adaptive reasoning chain fine-tuning + PPO with length-aware rewards) are novel combinations not present in either the LLM-TSC or MARL-TSC literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to bring explicit, interpretable cooperation to LLM-based TSC, addressing a fundamental gap where prior LLM agents operated in isolation and could cause upstream spillback. The zero-shot transfer from a synthetic 4×4 grid to real-world networks of up to 196 intersections, with an 8B model outperforming 70B baselines, suggests that task-specific optimization can substitute for raw model scale in structured decision-making. Open questions include scalability beyond ~200 intersections in real deployment, the reliance on oracle optimal signals (a*) for RL reward computation, and whether the cooperative reasoning generalizes to fundamentally different traffic regimes (e.g., incident-driven disruptions) not captured in the CityFlow simulation.
