## Problem

VLMs frequently misidentify fundamental geometric primitives (points, lines, circles) and their spatial relations in diagrams, yet existing end-to-end benchmarks conflate perceptual errors with reasoning failures, obscuring the true source of model limitations. No prior benchmark provides a canonical, unambiguous formal target that enables exact program-level evaluation of perception independently from reasoning, and no scalable training method exists to improve perception without forcing models off their natural-language pretraining distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263739/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The VLM receives a rendered pixel-level geometric diagram (3×H×W RGB image) and produces a free-form natural language description of the visible primitives and their relations. This NL output is then passed through a fine-tuned NL2DSL translator that converts it into a GEODSL program, which is scored against the ground-truth program via a weighted F1 metric (equal 0.25 weights over points, lines, circles, and constraints) computed through Hungarian-algorithm optimal matching. The VLM is never required to emit DSL directly; it remains in the NL regime throughout inference and training.

## Method

GEODPO combines two components: (1) GEOPERCEIVE, an automated data pipeline where a generation engine samples diverse GEODSL programs via iterative construction (triangles, quadrilaterals, circles, then progressive additions of points/lines/constraints) and a PyTorch-based solving engine renders each program to a pixel diagram by minimizing a composite loss of geometric constraint terms and visual plausibility penalties; and (2) a translator-guided DPO training loop in which an NL2DSL translator (LoRA-fine-tuned Qwen2.5-7B) converts sampled NL descriptions into GEODSL programs, whose scores against ground truth serve as reward signals. Preference pairs are constructed by sampling N=10 NL descriptions per diagram, scoring them via the translator, and selecting winner-loser pairs with score difference exceeding δ_min=0.3. The DPO loss then aligns the VLM to prefer geometrically accurate NL descriptions while regularizing against the reference policy. This design avoids the order-permutation explosion that plagues sequence-level SFT on DSL, mitigates distribution shift from NL pretraining, and yields perception-focused supervision at scale without human annotation.

## Results

On the GEOPERCEIVE in-domain benchmark, GEODPO improves overall perception scores by +14.2% (Qwen2.5-VL: 57.96→66.19), +15.35% (InternVL3: 58.44→67.41), and +26.46% (LLaVA-Next: 41.01→51.86) relative to raw models. On the out-of-domain GEOPERCEIVE-OOD set, GEODPO yields +1.83% to +8.0% gains, while SFT provides only marginal or negative improvements. On the MathVista geometry subset (downstream reasoning), GEODPO achieves a +39.0% relative gain (InternVL3: 29.06→40.39), substantially outperforming SFT's +3.4% improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GEODPO against two baselines per model family: the raw (unfine-tuned) VLM and a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) variant trained on the same GEOPERCEIVE data. GEODPO consistently outperforms both baselines across all three model families (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3, LLaVA-Next) on in-domain perception, with overall score gains of +14.2% to +26.46% over raw models. Against SFT, GEODPO achieves a consistent but smaller margin (+2.17% to +1.76% overall), while critically avoiding SFT's degradation on the Constraints category (e.g., InternVL3 SFT drops constraints F1 from 52.40 to 49.09, whereas GEODPO raises it to 62.50). On out-of-domain evaluation, the advantage widens: GEODPO gains +1.83% to +8.0% over raw models, while SFT is nearly neutral or negative (e.g., InternVL3 SFT: −0.29% overall). On the MathVista geometry reasoning subset, GEODPO delivers a dramatic +39.0% relative gain over raw InternVL3 (29.06→40.39), compared to only +3.4% from SFT (29.06→30.05), confirming that preference-based RL generalizes far better than token-level SFT under distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024) as the preference-optimization backbone but departs from prior geometry DSLs (AlphaGeometry, GMBL, Inter-GPS) by proposing GEODSL, which enforces a one-to-one correspondence between diagrams and programs to eliminate the semantic ambiguity that plagues existing formal languages. Conceptually, it also departs from the dominant SFT paradigm for VLM fine-tuning: rather than training the VLM to emit DSL directly (which forces it off its NL pretraining manifold), GEODPO keeps the model in the NL regime and uses a separately trained NL-to-DSL translator as a reward model. The benchmark itself is positioned against prior geometry datasets (GeoQA, UniGeo, MathVista) that rely on binary answer-matching and conflate perception errors with reasoning failures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first scalable, automatically generated benchmark that isolates geometric perception from reasoning, enabling practitioners to diagnose whether VLM failures stem from visual misreading or logical error. The finding that perception-only DPO training yields a +39% gain on downstream MathVista reasoning suggests that geometric perception is a major, previously under-addressed bottleneck for VLM reasoning. Limitations include the small OOD perception set (100 hand-curated diagrams), restriction to 2D Euclidean geometry, and the critical dependence on translator quality (ablation shows significant degradation with fewer training samples). Open questions include whether the translator-guided RL paradigm generalizes to other formal domains (e.g., circuit diagrams, chemical structures) and whether the approach scales to 3D or non-Euclidean settings.
