## Problem

In RLVR training for multi-modal LLMs, combining expert supervision with reinforcement learning leads to entropy collapse: SFT-then-RL anchors the policy to a static expert distribution, while hybrid-policy methods introduce a distributional mismatch between the current policy and expert trajectories, both suppressing exploration and accelerating convergence to suboptimal behaviors. No existing framework provides a mechanism for stable, controllable exploration that preserves policy entropy while still leveraging expert guidance directionally.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image paired with a text question (e.g., geometry or math problems) and generates a chain-of-thought reasoning trace enclosed in <think> tags followed by a final answer in <answer> tags. Training uses ~9.7K question-response pairs from ViRL39K with GPT-4o-generated CoT responses validated for correctness, format, and logical coherence; the expert response serves only as a log-probability baseline and does not participate in gradient computation or reward scoring.

## Method

CalibRL reinterprets expert supervision as a distributional calibration baseline rather than an imitation target, computing a log-probability gap Δℓ_i between the model's on-policy response and the expert response. Two complementary mechanisms drive controllable exploration: (1) an advantage weighting |Â_i| that scales updates by group-wise rareness, amplifying reinforcement for rare correct responses and suppression for rare incorrect ones; and (2) a LeakyReLU-based asymmetric activation LeakyReLU(−s_i·Δℓ_i, α) that moderates overconfident updates while preserving their corrective direction, with α controlling the degree of further adjustment once the response probability crosses the expert baseline. The exploration loss |Â_i|·LeakyReLU(−s_i·Δℓ_i, α) is added to the standard GRPO objective with a small balance weight λ (default 0.1), requiring only one additional forward pass per prompt (for the expert log-probability) with no extra backward passes or reward queries.

## Results

On in-domain geometry reasoning (GeoEval, Geo3K, GeoQA), CalibRL achieves an average accuracy of 44.93%, improving over the GRPO baseline (39.48%) by 5.45 percentage points, with the largest gain on GeoEval (33.44% vs. 26.15%). On out-of-domain benchmarks (Science, Spatial Reasoning, MathVerse, MathVision, MathVista, MMMU, ScienceQA), CalibRL reaches 59.36% average, a 2.61-point improvement over GRPO (57.24%). Notably, on the challenging GeoEval set (comprising samples where even GPT-4o failed CoT validation), CalibRL achieves 33.44% while SFT+GRPO collapses to 6.00% and RL-PLUS drops to 20.69%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CalibRL consistently outperforms all four compared baselines across both in-domain and out-of-domain settings. Against GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), CalibRL gains +5.45 points in-domain (44.93 vs. 39.48) and +2.61 points out-of-domain (59.36 vs. 57.24). Against LUFFY (Yan et al., 2025), CalibRL wins by a wide margin: LUFFY actually underperforms GRPO in-domain (38.64 vs. 39.48, a −0.84 drop) and trails CalibRL by 6.29 points. Against RL-PLUS (Dong et al., 2025), the gap is even larger in-domain (44.93 vs. 34.68, a −4.8 drop for RL-PLUS relative to GRPO), with CalibRL also leading out-of-domain (59.36 vs. 56.51). Against the entropy-regularization variants from Cui et al. (2025), CalibRL (44.93) surpasses both KL-Cov (40.55) and Clip-Cov (38.98) on in-domain accuracy, confirming that guided exploration via expert calibration is more effective than unguided entropy regularization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) by adding a controllable exploration term that reinterprets expert supervision as a distributional calibration baseline rather than an imitation target. It departs from existing hybrid-policy frameworks (LUFFY, RL-PLUS, ReLIFT, CHORD) that inject SFT supervision directly into the RL objective via log-likelihood maximization, which the authors argue causes unidirectional probability mass shifting and accelerates entropy collapse. The specific novelty is a two-part loss: group-rarity advantage weighting (|Â_i|) to emphasize rare informative responses, and a LeakyReLU-based asymmetric activation that uses the expert log-probability gap as a calibrated threshold for modulating update strength and direction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that existing hybrid-policy methods, which showed gains in text-only LLM settings, actually fail to improve over plain GRPO in multi-modal reasoning—a gap the authors attribute to the vastly larger state space of MLLMs making uncontrolled exploration particularly harmful. The calibration perspective (expert as reference, not target) offers a principled alternative that preserves entropy while providing directional guidance, and the approach generalizes across model scales (3B–32B) and architectures (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3). Open questions include whether the method extends to non-verifiable reward settings, whether the specific LeakyReLU form is optimal versus other smooth asymmetric functions, and whether the gains persist for longer, more open-ended reasoning chains beyond geometry and math.
