## Problem

KL regularization is ubiquitous in policy gradient algorithms for LLM reasoning, yet the design space—KL direction (forward vs. reverse), normalization (normalized vs. unnormalized), and estimator choice (k1/k2/k3)—is scattered across the literature and often conflated with off-policy estimation. The paper asks a focused question: under off-policy sampling, what exact importance weighting is required for each KL variant so that the optimized surrogate yields the true gradient of the intended KL-regularized objective? It also identifies a concrete bug: GRPO's k3-based KL penalty omits an essential importance weight, causing its gradient to diverge from the intended objective.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt (math problem), a batch of responses sampled from a reference policy π_old, and per-response reward signals (e.g., correctness of the final answer). It produces updated policy parameters θ for the current policy π_θ, which in turn generate reasoning responses. A key implementation detail is that log-probabilities from π_old are pre-computed and stored, so only one model (π_θ) needs to reside in GPU memory during the training step, unlike GRPO which requires simultaneous access to both the current and reference models. The framework operates iteratively: the updated policy periodically becomes the new reference (π_old ← π_θ every K steps or when token-level KL exceeds a threshold κ).

## Method

The RPG framework derives exact off-policy policy gradients for all four KL-regularized objectives (forward/reverse × normalized/unnormalized) by applying the Generalized Policy Gradient Theorem with proper importance weights w(x) = π_θ(x)/π_old(x), showing that the widely-used k3 estimator is exactly the unnormalized KL divergence. For each objective, it provides both a fully differentiable surrogate loss and a REINFORCE-style loss using the stop-gradient operator, proving their gradient-equivalence to the target objective (Proposition 4.1). The key practical innovation is RPG-Style Clip, a dual-clip truncation of importance ratios into [1−ε₁, 1+ε₂] with an additional lower bound c for negative advantages, which stabilizes off-policy REINFORCE-style updates while preserving the correct gradient direction. The framework also corrects GRPO's KL term by inserting the missing importance weight w_{i,t} into the k3 estimator, and employs an iterative reference-update scheme to maintain a practical KL trust region without over-regularizing toward the initial checkpoint.

## Results

With 8K context length on Qwen3-4B, RPG-REINFORCE-URKL achieves 52.1% best accuracy on AIME25, surpassing the official Qwen3-4B-Instruct baseline (47%) and DAPO (47.4%). RPG-UFKL reaches 61.8% best on AIME24, the highest among all methods tested. On AMC23, RPG-URKL attains 95.4% best accuracy. Across all three benchmarks, RPG variants consistently outperform both GRPO and DAPO, with the largest gains on the harder AIME problems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO, all RPG variants achieve substantial gains: RPG-UFKL improves AIME24 best accuracy from 44.0% to 61.8% (+17.8 pts) and AIME25 from 40.6% to 48.7% (+8.1 pts), while RPG-REINFORCE-URKL pushes AIME25 to 52.1% (+11.5 pts over GRPO). Against DAPO, the improvements are more moderate but consistent: RPG-UFKL leads on AIME24 (61.8% vs. 57.4%, +4.4 pts) and RPG-REINFORCE-URKL leads on AIME25 (52.1% vs. 47.4%, +4.7 pts). On AMC23, gains over DAPO are smaller (RPG-URKL 95.4% vs. DAPO 94.3%, +1.1 pts), reflecting the near-saturation of that benchmark. The paper also reports that RPG-REINFORCE with RPG-Style Clip improves accuracy by up to +6 absolute points over DAPO (likely at shorter context lengths in the appendix). Notably, RPG-REINFORCE-URKL's 52.1% AIME25 score exceeds the official Qwen3-4B-Instruct model (47%) without any additional model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the scattered design space of KL-regularized policy gradient methods (forward/reverse KL, normalized/unnormalized distributions, k1/k2/k3 estimators) that appear across PPO, GRPO, and DAPO into a single 'RPG' framework with explicit off-policy importance weighting. It departs from GRPO by identifying that the widely-used k3 KL penalty omits a critical importance weight under off-policy sampling, and from classical REINFORCE by making it viable off-policy through a dual-clip mechanism (RPG-Style Clip) that preserves the correct gradient direction. The connection to Natural Policy Gradient (showing NPG as a local quadratic approximation of RPG) and the equivalence between the k3 estimator and unnormalized KL (Appendix D) provide theoretical grounding that prior work left implicit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified RPG framework gives practitioners a principled way to choose KL direction, normalization, and estimator without ad-hoc tuning, and the identification of GRPO's missing importance weight corrects a subtle but real bias in a widely-adopted algorithm. Practically, RPG-REINFORCE with RPG-Style Clip requires only one model in GPU memory (vs. two for GRPO), enabling more efficient training at scale, and achieves competitive or superior accuracy on AIME benchmarks. Open questions include developing principled schedules for the clipping hyperparameters (ε1, ε2) that currently introduce a controllable bias-variance tradeoff, and whether the iterative reference-update scheme generalizes beyond mathematical reasoning to other sequential decision-making domains.
