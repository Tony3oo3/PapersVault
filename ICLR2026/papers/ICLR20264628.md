## Problem

Existing operators for composing pretrained diffusion models—Product of Experts (PoE) for conjunction and Mixture of Experts (MoE) for disjunction—violate fundamental algebraic laws such as idempotency, distributivity, and De Morgan duality, exhibit unstable tail behavior under negation, and introduce location-dependent temperature biases that distort sampling. No principled calculus existed that could support arbitrary nested logical compositions (e.g., XOR, majority, one-hot) over score models while guaranteeing stable, unbiased generation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of k pretrained score models (each providing a score function s_t^i and an Itô-estimated log-likelihood during the reverse SDE), a logical composition formula F over those models (e.g., (p1 ∨ p2) ∧ ¬p3), and a continuously differentiable reference function c(x) that anchors negation. At each SDE step it outputs a composite score field, a composite log-likelihood, and a Feynman-Kac weight field g_t(x); these are fed into a standard diffusion sampler (optionally with SMC resampling) to produce samples from the composed distribution. The entire pipeline is training-free and operates purely online during inference.

## Method

The paper lifts the Dombi t-norm from fuzzy logic to probability densities via a referenced mapping φ_c(p) = p/(p+c), yielding a one-parameter family (λ > 0) of De Morgan dual operators: conjunction p1 ∧_λ p2 = (p1^{-λ} + p2^{-λ})^{-1/λ}, disjunction p1 ∨_λ p2 = (p1^{λ} + p2^{λ})^{1/λ}, and referenced negation ¬_c p = c²/p. In score space, each operator reduces to a softmax-weighted convex combination of component scores with dynamic responsibility weights α^i = softmax(λ log p^i), guaranteeing ‖s1 ∧_λ s2‖ ≤ max(‖s1‖, ‖s2‖) and eliminating the alignment-dependent temperature bias of PoE. The parameter λ interpolates between the harmonic mean (λ=1), the geometric mean (λ→0), and the exact min/max lattice (λ→∞), with explicit bounds quantifying the idempotency and distributivity bias at finite λ. To correct for the non-commutativity of nonlinear compositions with the noising operator, the authors derive closed-form Feynman-Kac corrector terms for both negation and power-norm compositions, enabling unbiased sampling via weighted SDEs and SMC resampling.

## Results

On the SAT-like MNIST composition experiment, Dombi composition maintains Sat > 0.9 even for XOR₁₀ (a formula with over 1000 score terms), while PoE/MoE composition completely collapses (Sat = 0.00) for all non-trivial formulas. On Stable Diffusion conjunctive prompts, Dombi (λ=10) achieves a mean CLIP score of 25.50±2.54 and ImageReward of −1.18±0.85, outperforming SuperDiff (24.87, −1.33) and scaled PoE (24.41, −1.55). For multi-target molecular generation, Dombi with FKC correction (γ=2) achieves a joint docking product of 81.63±25.91, substantially exceeding TargetDiff (62.19±27.08) and improving max-docking from −7.24 to −8.25.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PoE/MoE (Hinton 1999; Jordan & Jacobs 1994), Dombi composition is dramatically superior: on the SAT experiment, PoE/MoE scores 0.00 on all non-trivial formulas (XOR, OneHot, Maj₁₀) while Dombi maintains Sat ≥ 0.89; on Stable Diffusion, Dombi (λ=10) achieves CLIP 25.50 vs. 24.41 for scaled PoE (Liu et al. 2022). Against SuperDiff (Skreta et al. 2025b), Dombi wins on both CLIP (25.50 vs. 24.87) and ImageReward (−1.18 vs. −1.33) for conjunctive prompts, and shows substantially more stable mixture coefficients over the generation trajectory. For contrastive prompting, Dombi (λ=10) edges out ICN (Ho & Salimans 2021) on ImageReward (1.21 vs. 1.14) while ICN retains a small CLIP advantage (7.29 vs. 7.02); the 'and ¬' baseline (SuperDiff + negation) is clearly worse on both metrics (5.44, 0.51). In molecular generation, Dombi with FKC (γ=2) outperforms TargetDiff (Guan et al. 2023) on joint docking product (81.63 vs. 62.19) and max-docking (−8.25 vs. −7.24), while maintaining comparable validity and drug-likeness scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and generalizes several existing score-composition operators—Product of Experts (Hinton, 1999), Mixture of Experts (Jordan & Jacobs, 1994), harmonic mean (Garipov et al., 2023), and geometric mean—under a single Dombi t-norm framework from fuzzy logic (Dombi, 1982). It departs from the standard probabilistic interpretation of composition (products for conjunction, mixtures for disjunction) by showing these operators violate fundamental algebraic properties (idempotency, distributivity, De Morgan duality), and instead grounds composition in a fuzzy-logic calculus that approximately satisfies lattice axioms. The paper extends the Feynman-Kac Corrector framework of Skreta et al. (2025a) to Dombi operators, enabling exact sampling from composed distributions, and leverages the Itô density estimator (Karczewski et al., 2025a) for efficient inference-time density estimation to compute dynamic responsibility weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first principled algebraic foundation for composing pretrained diffusion models, enabling complex logical operations (XOR, majority, one-hot) that were previously intractable with PoE/MoE, which completely fail (0% satisfaction) on such compositions. The Dombi parameter λ offers a continuous tradeoff between compositional precision and mixture stability, with explicit bounds quantifying both, and the FKC extension ensures unbiased sampling. Open questions include dynamic λ scheduling during inference, scaling to very large compositions beyond the 10-model toy experiments, and whether the modest real-world improvements on Stable Diffusion and molecular docking will hold at larger scales. The framework also opens avenues for neurosymbolic integration where modular diffusion models solve combinatorial tasks.
