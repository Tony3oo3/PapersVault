## Problem

While iterative DPO with on-policy sampling has been shown to outperform vanilla DPO, the practical recipe for when to use on-policy versus off-policy preference data lacks clear guidelines, with prior works arbitrarily choosing one or the other. This paper reveals a systematic effectiveness discrepancy: on-policy data can yield 3× the effectiveness of off-policy data for Llama-3 but only 0.4× for Zephyr, indicating that the optimal data source depends on the model's current alignment state rather than being universally fixed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two sets of preference candidates (on-policy samples drawn from the current policy and off-policy candidates from a static dataset like UltraFeedback), a preference model (PairRM, a 0.4B DeBERTa-V3-based pairwise ranker) for preference annotation, and a reference model (the initial SFT checkpoint). It produces an aligned LM policy and a boundary score V_off/(V_off+V_on) computed over ~2,000 prompts, where a score below 0.5 indicates the preference injection stage (use diverse off-policy data) and above 0.5 indicates the preference fine-tuning stage (use high-quality on-policy data). The boundary measurement requires only ~3.2% of a single DPO training epoch's compute, making it a lightweight diagnostic.

## Method

The core contribution is the alignment stage assumption, which posits that DPO-based alignment transitions from a preference injection stage (where high intra-diversity off-policy data is more effective at building the reward landscape) to a preference fine-tuning stage (where high-quality on-policy data is more effective at refining within the high-reward region). The boundary measurement algorithm (Algorithm 1) operationalizes this by computing a preference consistency score: for each prompt, it compares how the ground-truth preference model P* ranks on-policy versus off-policy candidate pairs, using a simplified XNOR indicator over m=n=2 samples per distribution. Theoretically, this is grounded in the bijection between reward functions and policies (Theorem 5.1), the intractability of the optimal solution with finite data (Theorem 5.2), and the sufficiency of preference consistency for text-distribution identity (Theorem 5.4, Definition 5.5), which together show that selecting preference candidates is equivalent to estimating the general text distribution π_G. The approach is validated across five models (Llama-3, Zephyr, Phi-2, Qwen2.5, Pythia) and two alignment methods (DPO, SLiC-HF), demonstrating that the two-stage structure and boundary measurement generalize beyond DPO.

## Results

On AlpacaEval 2.0 (LC Win Rate), the paper reveals a systematic effectiveness discrepancy between on-policy (PC_on) and off-policy (PC_off) preference candidates that varies by model and alignment stage. For Llama-3-8B-Instruct, PC_on consistently outperforms PC_off (e.g., 34.04 vs. 27.73 in iteration 1; 44.52 vs. 27.83 in iteration 2), indicating it is already in the preference fine-tuning stage. For Zephyr-7B, PC_off wins in the first iteration (20.77 vs. 13.70) but PC_on wins in the second iteration after PC_off training (33.28 vs. 23.77), showing a stage transition. For Phi-2-2.7B, PC_off consistently outperforms PC_on (5.97 vs. 4.21 in iteration 1), indicating it remains in the preference injection stage. The boundary measurement algorithm correctly identifies these stages with scores >0.5 (fine-tuning) or <0.5 (injection), achieving ~3.2% computational overhead relative to one training epoch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external alignment methods but instead compares data strategies (PC_on vs. PC_off) across five models (Llama-3, Zephyr, Phi-2, Qwen2.5, Pythia) and two alignment losses (DPO, SLiC-HF). The central finding is that neither on-policy nor off-policy data is universally superior: Llama-3 benefits from PC_on in all settings (Δ<1), Phi-2 benefits from PC_off in all settings (Δ>1), and Zephyr transitions from favoring PC_off (first iteration, +12.65 vs. +5.58) to favoring PC_on (second iteration, +12.51 vs. +3.00). The PC_llama dataset (off-policy for Zephyr but high-quality, low-diversity) isolates the diversity vs. quality effect: in the injection stage, high-diversity PC_off (+12.65) beats high-quality PC_llama (+5.41); in the fine-tuning stage, high-quality PC_llama (+8.55) and PC_on (+12.51) both beat low-quality PC_off (+3.00). The boundary measurement algorithm (Algorithm 1) correctly predicts the optimal data choice in most cases, with boundary scores of 0.62–0.69 for Llama-3 (fine-tuning), 0.23–0.25 for Phi-2 (injection), and 0.40→0.66 for Zephyr (transitioning from injection to fine-tuning). Generalizability is confirmed on Qwen2.5-1.5B and Pythia-6.9B (both in injection stage, PC_off wins) and with SLiC-HF loss (similar patterns, though Llama-3 shows model collapse in one PC_off→PC_on setting).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) and the growing line of iterative DPO work that incorporates on-policy sampling (Yuan et al., 2024; Tajwar et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2024; Rosset et al., 2024). It departs from this literature by challenging the prevailing assumption that on-policy data is uniformly superior, instead proposing a two-stage framework (preference injection vs. preference fine-tuning) that explains when each data type is more effective. The theoretical grounding via the Bradley-Terry model and preference consistency measurement is novel, and the boundary measurement algorithm provides a practical diagnostic that prior iterative DPO work lacked—those works chose on-policy or off-policy data empirically or as a fixed cold-start heuristic without a principled criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a concrete, computationally cheap (≈3.2% of one training epoch) decision rule for selecting preference data during DPO-based alignment, moving the choice from an empirical art to a principled, stage-aware procedure. The two-stage framing also connects naturally to the exploration-exploitation trade-off in RL, suggesting broader applicability to RLVR and offline RL settings. Key limitations include the binary stage switch (rather than a smooth transition), dependence on a specific preference model for the boundary decision, and a model collapse phenomenon observed with SLiC-HF that the framework does not fully explain. Open questions include adaptive data blending strategies and whether the framework extends to RL-based alignment methods like PPO or GRPO.
