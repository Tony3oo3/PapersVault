## Problem

Existing text-to-ECG diffusion models learn waveform morphology purely from data, ignoring two critical sources of clinical knowledge: (i) decades of physiological ODE-based cardiac simulators that encode mechanistic P-QRS-T dynamics, and (ii) broader experience-based regularities distributed across large EHR corpora that go beyond narrow patient metadata. This leaves a disconnect between statistical generation and mechanistic plausibility, and under-leverages retrieval-augmented clinical context for conditioning medical time-series generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a conditioning tuple c=(t, m, r) where t is the original clinical diagnosis text, m encodes basic metadata (age, sex, optionally heart rate), and r is a concise physiologically grounded report generated by GPT-4.1-mini from the top-3 most similar EHR admissions retrieved via tri-view Jaccard similarity over diagnoses, medications, and procedures. The output is a 10-second, 12-lead ECG waveform sampled at 500 Hz, produced by running the reverse diffusion in a 4×128 VAE latent space and decoding with the full VAE. The Beat Decoder additionally predicts a single QRS-aligned cycle (0.2 s pre-R to 0.4 s post-R, 300 samples) used exclusively for simulator-informed regularizers during training.

## Method

SE-Diff couples a VAE latent space with a lightweight Beat Decoder and two simulator-informed regularizers derived from the McSharry ODE-based ECG simulator: a per-lead Euler loss that penalizes deviations of the predicted beat's discrete derivative from the ODE right-hand side f_z, and an inter-lead constraint that enforces Einthoven's triangle and Goldberger's central terminal identities by matching the child lead's derivative to the linear combination of parent leads' simulator derivatives. In parallel, an LLM-powered retrieval pipeline identifies clinically similar patients from MIMIC-IV-CLINICAL, retrieves their ECG diagnoses and measurements, and distills them into a concise report that augments the text conditioning via cross-attention in a 1D U-Net denoiser with FiLM-style modulation. The VAE (encoder, decoder, and Beat Decoder) is trained first with reconstruction, KL, beat, and spectral losses, then frozen; the diffusion model is trained with the standard DDPM noise-prediction objective plus the two simulator regularizers (λ=3×10⁻³ for Euler, γ=5×10⁻² for inter-lead), all applied only during training without modifying the reverse sampling process. At inference, standard DDPM sampling with classifier-free guidance produces the latent, which is decoded to the signal domain.

## Results

On MIMIC-IV-ECG (internal), SE-Diff achieves the best performance across all five metrics: MAE 0.0923, NRMSE 0.0714, MAE_HR 8.43 bpm, rCLIP 0.9470, and rFID 0.9509. On the external PTB-XL dataset, SE-Diff similarly leads with MAE 0.1076, NRMSE 0.0630, MAE_HR 8.24 bpm, rCLIP 0.8901, and rFID 0.8583. The closest competitor, DiffuSETS, trails SE-Diff by 15% in MAE_HR (13.29 vs. 8.43 bpm) and 1.7% in rCLIP on MIMIC-IV-ECG, and by 54% in MAE_HR (17.88 vs. 8.24 bpm) on PTB-XL. SE-Diff also achieves the lowest beat-level interval errors (e.g., PR 7.30 ms, QT 4.50 ms on MIMIC-IV-ECG) and the largest downstream classification gains under class imbalance (F1 58% vs. 42% unbalanced for gender; F1 72% vs. 56% for SVT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SE-Diff outperforms all four baselines on every metric across both MIMIC-IV-ECG and PTB-XL. The strongest competitor is DiffuSETS (Lai et al., 2025), the only prior method generating 12-lead, 10 s ECGs from clinical text; SE-Diff reduces heart-rate error by 36% on MIMIC (8.43 vs. 13.29 bpm) and 54% on PTB-XL (8.24 vs. 17.88 bpm), while also improving rCLIP by 1.7 and 2.1 percentage points respectively. SSSD (López Alcaraz & Strodthoff, 2023) and WGAN (Adib et al., 2022) are substantially weaker, with SSSD showing MAE_HR of 27.37 bpm and WGAN showing rCLIP of only 0.5688 on MIMIC. BeatDiff (Bedin et al., 2024), designed for beat-level reconstruction from indirect signals, performs worst on signal fidelity (MAE 0.7464 on MIMIC, 0.9888 on PTB-XL) and semantic alignment (rCLIP 0.5167), confirming that its single-beat focus does not transfer well to full 10-second, 12-lead generation. The consistent advantage of SE-Diff on the external PTB-XL cohort (where it was not trained) further indicates that the simulator-informed and experience-augmented components generalize beyond the training distribution rather than overfitting to MIMIC-specific patterns.

## Relation to Prior Work

SE-Diff extends the latent diffusion framework for ECG generation (DiffuSETS, SSSD) by injecting two forms of external knowledge that prior methods lack: (1) a lightweight McSharry ODE-based ECG simulator, coupled through a Beat Decoder and Euler/inter-lead regularizers, which had previously been used only in GAN-based ECG synthesis (SimGANs, ECG ODE-GAN) but never in diffusion training; and (2) an LLM-powered retrieval-augmented conditioning pipeline that pulls clinically similar EHR records to produce a physiologically grounded text report, adapting RAG from NLP to medical time-series generation. The VAE + U-Net denoiser backbone follows the standard LDM recipe, and the inter-lead constraint formalizes Einthoven's triangle identities as a differentiable loss, a formulation not seen in prior ECG generators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two concrete gaps in text-to-ECG generation—absence of mechanistic cardiac priors and under-leveraging of population-level clinical experience—yielding consistent gains in signal fidelity, heart-rate accuracy, and text-ECG alignment on both MIMIC-IV-ECG and external PTB-XL. The downstream classification results (e.g., F1 jumping from 42% to 58% under 10% minority sampling) suggest practical utility for data augmentation in imbalanced clinical settings. Open questions include whether the class-specific simulator parameters (fitted on top-20 ECG categories) generalize to rare or complex arrhythmias, whether the frozen Beat Decoder limits expressivity for atypical morphologies, and whether the EHR retrieval pipeline transfers across institutions with different coding practices.
