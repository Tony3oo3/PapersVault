## Problem

EEG spatial super-resolution from low-density to high-density recordings is hindered by a consistency-fidelity tradeoff: static conditioning strategies (feature concatenation, cross-attention) either overfit to the sparse input causing distribution shift, or generate HD-like content that deviates from the observed LD signal. Existing diffusion-based approaches inject the LD condition once as a fixed prior, leaving the per-step denoising trajectory unguided and unable to adapt its correction strength across timesteps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260273/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-density EEG recording (e.g., 8 or 16 channels over a fixed temporal window) and produces a high-density reconstruction (62 or 256 channels) at configurable upsampling scales of 2×, 4×, 8×, or 16×. The LD signal is encoded by the same frozen VAE encoder used for the HD latent, yielding multi-scale condition features that are re-queried at every reverse diffusion step rather than injected once. The output is decoded from the latent space back to the time-domain HD signal, evaluated at signal, feature, and downstream-task levels.

## Method

SRGDiff reframes EEG super-resolution as dynamic conditional generation in a frozen VAE latent space, where a U-Net denoiser is augmented with two lightweight modules. The Residual Direction Module (RDM) predicts a per-step path residual from the LD features and timestep embedding, which is additively fused with the current latent to supply step-wise temporal and spatial corrections. The Step-Aware Modulation Module (SMM) then applies a step-dependent affine transformation (channel-wise scale γ_t and bias β_t) to calibrate how strongly the residual influences each denoising step, with a linearly decaying weight balancing LD features against the sinusoidal timestep embedding. Training is two-stage: the VAE is first pre-trained on HD EEG with reconstruction, spectral (STFT), and KL losses, then the diffusion model is trained with a combined objective of noise prediction, residual supervision (matching the forward-noising difference z₀−z_t), and SMM regularization to prevent excessive modulation. This design couples the LD forward-noising trajectory with the HD reverse-denoising trajectory, re-estimating the corrective direction at every step rather than relying on a static global prior.

## Results

On Localize-MI at the most challenging 8× scale, SRGDiff achieves NMSE 0.2957, PCC 0.8323, and SNR 5.22 dB, outperforming the strongest baseline STAD (NMSE 0.3649, PCC 0.7216, SNR 3.87 dB) by ~19% in NMSE and ~35% in SNR. On SEED at 2×, SRGDiff reduces NMSE from ESTformer's 0.3288 to 0.1632 (>50% reduction) and raises PCC from 0.8368 to 0.9102. At 8× on SEED-IV, SRGDiff attains SNR 6.03 dB versus ESTformer's 2.98 dB (~102% relative gain). In downstream emotion recognition, SRGDiff at 2× on SEED reaches 70.19% accuracy, approaching the 71.52% ground-truth ceiling, while all baselines drop to 47–69%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRGDiff outperforms all six baselines across every dataset, scale factor, and metric. Against the strongest EEG-specific baselines, ESTformer (KBS 2025) and STAD (IEEE TCE 2025), SRGDiff achieves the largest margins on SEED/SEED-IV: at 8×, SRGDiff's SNR of 4.59 dB on SEED exceeds ESTformer's 3.89 dB by ~18%, while on SEED-IV the gap widens to 6.03 vs. 2.98 dB (~102%). On Localize-MI at 16×, SRGDiff (SNR 4.02 dB) surpasses STAD (3.26 dB) by ~23% and ESTformer (2.57 dB) by ~56%. The time-series imputation baselines (SaSDim, SADI, RDPI) and the earlier diffusion method DDPMEEG are consistently weaker, with NMSE values 1.5–2.5× higher than SRGDiff at 8×, indicating that generic imputation strategies struggle with the spatial-topographic structure of EEG. In downstream emotion recognition, SRGDiff at 2× on SEED (70.19%) nearly matches ground-truth accuracy (71.52%), whereas ESTformer reaches 68.87% and STAD only 54.37%, confirming that the reconstruction quality translates into practical utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

SRGDiff builds on the latent diffusion framework of Rombach et al. (2022) and the EEG-specific VAE autoencoder of Aristimunha et al. (2023), but departs sharply from prior EEG super-resolution methods (ESTformer, STAD, DDPM-EEG) that condition the denoiser via static feature concatenation or cross-attention. The key conceptual departure is borrowing the residual-diffusion idea from medical imaging and video (Ou et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2024) and making it dynamic: the residual direction is re-estimated at every reverse step from the low-density observation and timestep, rather than being injected once as a fixed offset. This couples the LD forward-noising trajectory with the HD reverse-denoising trajectory, reframing SR from a static projection to an iterative, step-aware conditional generation process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistency-fidelity tradeoff in EEG SR—where interpolation-based methods overfit to sparse inputs while generation-based methods drift from them—is a practical bottleneck for deploying lightweight BCI systems, and SRGDiff's step-aware residual guidance offers a principled mechanism to balance both. The three-level evaluation protocol (signal, feature, downstream) is a useful template for the field, and the substantial EEG-FID and downstream-accuracy gains suggest the reconstructed signals are neurophysiologically meaningful, not just numerically close. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other neuroimaging modalities (MEG, fMRI), whether the per-step residual prediction can be made more computationally efficient for real-time BCI, and how performance holds under cross-subject or cross-session transfer where the LD-to-HD mapping is less stable.
