## Problem

Existing EEG-based visual decoding methods rely on multi-stage unCLIP pipelines with large diffusion models (often >3B parameters), which compound errors across stages, require training EEG encoders from scratch on limited paired data, and are computationally impractical for real-time brain-computer interface deployment. No lightweight framework exists that directly and coherently maps noisy EEG signals to reconstructed images without sequential error accumulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263428/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single EEG epoch (63 channels × 1000 ms at 200 Hz, baseline-corrected and normalized to approximately [-1, 1]) and produces a full-resolution RGB image via a multi-scale VQ-VAE decoder. During generation, the EEG embedding acts as the coarsest-scale conditioning token, and the model autoregressively predicts 10 progressively finer residual maps (spatial sizes 1×1 through 16×16) using classifier-free guidance (ratio 4.0) and top-k sampling (k=900). For retrieval, the same EEG encoder produces a 200-d embedding compared via cosine similarity against CLIP image embeddings of 200 test concepts.

## Method

AVDE is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 fine-tunes LaBraM (pre-trained on 2000+ hours of diverse EEG) with a combined contrastive loss (CLIP-style InfoNCE with learned temperature) and MSE regression against frozen CLIP image embeddings, aligning EEG and image representation spaces. Stage 2 trains a 16-layer decoder-only transformer using VAR's next-scale prediction objective: a pre-trained multi-scale VQ-VAE tokenizes images into K=10 residual maps of increasing resolution, and the transformer predicts each finer scale R_k conditioned on all previous scales and the projected EEG embedding [s], with a block-wise causal attention mask ensuring proper autoregressive context. The EEG embedding directly serves as the coarsest representation, eliminating the need for separate conditioning mechanisms and maintaining a direct signal-to-image connection. This design reduces parameters to 425M (vs. 3.8B for diffusion baselines), cuts inference time to 91 ms, and produces intermediate outputs that mirror the hierarchical V1→V2/V4→IT progression of biological visual processing.

## Results

On THINGS-EEG, AVDE achieves 30.0% Top-1 and 58.2% Top-5 accuracy in 200-way within-subject zero-shot retrieval, and 14.3% Top-1 / 32.9% Top-5 in the cross-subject setting, outperforming the previous best (ATM) by 3.1 and 3.4 points respectively. For reconstruction on Subject-08, AVDE attains the highest scores across all metrics: PixCorr 0.188, SSIM 0.396, CLIP 0.795, and SwAV 0.557 (lower is better). In terms of efficiency, AVDE uses only 425.3M parameters (vs. 3818.1M for Li et al.), requires 1350.5G FLOPs (vs. 8738.6G), and completes inference in 91.2 ms with 1809.6 MB peak memory (vs. 310.4 ms and 4826.7 MB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AVDE outperforms all five baselines on both retrieval and reconstruction. Against EEGNetV4, EEGConformer, and NICE, the gains are substantial: AVDE improves within-subject Top-1 by 11.4, 17.8, and 5.8 points respectively, and cross-subject Top-1 by 5.4, 7.2, and 3.0 points. The closest competitor is ATM (Li et al., 2024), which uses a similar contrastive-alignment + diffusion pipeline; AVDE still leads by 3.1 points on within-subject Top-1 and 2.8 points on cross-subject Top-1, while also surpassing it on every reconstruction metric (e.g., PixCorr 0.188 vs. 0.160, CLIP 0.795 vs. 0.786 on Subject-08). CognitionCapturer (Zhang et al., 2025) is weaker still, with notably lower AlexNet(5) (0.610 vs. 0.889) and CLIP (0.744 vs. 0.795) scores, suggesting its multimodal approach does not recover fine-grained visual structure as effectively. Notably, AVDE achieves these gains while using roughly 10% of the parameters and 3.4× less inference time than the diffusion-based baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

AVDE sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: large-scale pre-trained EEG encoders (LaBraM, trained on 2000+ hours of diverse recordings) and hierarchical autoregressive image generation (VAR's next-scale prediction). It departs from the dominant unCLIP+diffusion pipeline used by prior EEG-to-image methods (Li et al. 2024, Zhang et al. 2025, Xiao et al. 2025) by replacing the multi-stage diffusion decoder with a single autoregressive transformer and by initializing the EEG encoder from a pre-trained model rather than training from scratch on limited EEG-image pairs. The contrastive alignment stage follows the CLIP-based representation matching established by BrainCLIP and Mind's Eye, but the generative backbone is the key architectural departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 90% parameter reduction (425M vs. 3.8B) and 3.4× speedup make EEG-based visual decoding substantially more viable for real-time BCI deployment, where latency and hardware constraints are critical. The observation that intermediate generation scales correlate with distinct brain regions (occipital → early, frontal → late) offers a testable computational model of hierarchical visual processing, though the causal interpretation remains speculative. Open questions include whether the autoregressive approach scales to higher-resolution or more semantically complex scenes, whether the cross-subject gains from LaBraM pre-training transfer to other neural modalities beyond EEG/MEG, and whether the hierarchical generation structure is genuinely necessary or merely a convenient inductive bias that could be matched by a sufficiently well-tuned diffusion model.
