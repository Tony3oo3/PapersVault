## Problem

Memory-constrained fine-tuning of pre-trained networks on edge devices is hindered by the high memory cost of backpropagation, yet existing sparse-update methods either fix a static subnetwork for all tasks (ignoring task-dependent channel importance) or require computing full gradients to select channels (contradicting the very memory constraints they aim to satisfy). The paper asks whether one can identify which layers to update a priori from architecture alone, and how to dynamically select channels within those layers without violating the memory budget.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260443/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained backbone (MobileNetV2, MCUNet, ProxylessNAS, SwinT, BERT, or RoBERTa), a downstream training set, and a scalar memory budget B_mem that jointly caps both weight-storage and activation-storage during the update phase. It outputs the fine-tuned backbone weights after a fixed number of epochs. Unusually, the selection granularity is the input channel of each convolution (or attention head), and the same set of channels is re-sampled from scratch at every epoch boundary, so no per-channel importance scores are ever stored across epochs.

## Method

TraDy rests on two empirical propositions validated across architectures and seeds: (i) the relative ranking of layers by a Reweighted Gradient Norm (RGN = gradient norm / channel memory cost) is architecture-determined and stable across downstream tasks, enabling a one-time offline selection of the top-K layers; (ii) channel-level RGN distributions are task-dependent and cannot be fixed in advance. Within the pre-selected layers, TraDy uniformly and randomly samples input channels each epoch until the combined weight-plus-activation memory reaches B_mem, then updates only those channels via standard SGD. Freezing along the input-channel dimension is shown analytically to be the only axis that simultaneously yields structured weight sparsity and activation sparsity, achieving up to 99% activation sparsity, 95% weight-derivative sparsity, and 97% FLOPs reduction in weight-gradient computation while matching or exceeding the accuracy of static sparse-update baselines.

## Results

Across three CNN architectures (MobileNetV2-w0.35, MCUNet-in1, ProxylessNAS-w0.3) and seven downstream image classification datasets, TraDy achieves the best average top-1 accuracy among all memory-constrained strategies in 7 out of 9 model-budget combinations, with average gains of 0.2–0.6% over the SU baseline. Under the most restrictive memory budgets, TraDy reaches 74.91% (MobileNetV2), 77.37% (MCUNet), and 76.12% (ProxylessNAS) average accuracy, compared to full fine-tuning baselines of 77.62%, 80.04%, and 79.31% respectively. The method simultaneously achieves up to 99% activation sparsity, 95% weight derivative sparsity, and 97% reduction in FLOPs for weight derivative computation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TraDy consistently outperforms the SU baseline (Lin et al., NeurIPS 2022) across all 9 model-budget combinations, with average accuracy gains of 0.2–0.6% (e.g., 74.91% vs 74.22% on MobileNetV2 at the tightest budget; 79.06% vs 78.53% on MCUNet at the loosest). Against the Velocity method (Quélennec et al., WACV 2024), TraDy wins in 7 of 9 settings but is narrowly outperformed on MCUNet at B_mem=112640 (79.06% vs 79.23%) and ProxylessNAS at B_mem=101376 (77.46% vs 77.61%). Critically, while Velocity achieves comparable accuracy, it operates at only 20–40% activation sparsity versus TraDy's 97–99%, and yields only ~88% FLOPs savings versus TraDy's 97%, because neuron-level selection requires storing full activation maps. TraDy also surpasses the oracle D-Det RGN upper bound in most settings, which the authors attribute to the stochastic resampling avoiding local minima that deterministic maximal-gradient selection can fall into.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the on-device sparse update line initiated by Lin et al. (2022) and refined by Kwon et al. (2024) and Quélennec et al. (2024), all of which select a fixed subset of parameters to update under a memory budget. TraDy departs from these static or neuron-level approaches by (i) grounding the selection in heavy-tailed SGD theory to justify structured input-channel freezing, (ii) introducing a memory-aware Reweighted Gradient Norm metric that reorders channel importance relative to raw gradient norms, and (iii) replacing fixed channel assignments with per-epoch stochastic resampling within architecturally pre-selected layers. The key conceptual departure is the decomposition of the selection problem into an architecture-determined layer filter (justified by Proposition 3.1) and a task-dependent channel sampler (justified by Proposition 3.2), which prior static methods conflate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TraDy provides a theoretically motivated, implementation-light recipe for fine-tuning under sub-100 KB memory budgets that simultaneously achieves high weight and activation sparsity (95–99%) and ~97% FLOPs reduction in weight-derivative computation—dimensions that prior methods optimize separately. The finding that layer importance is architecture-invariant while channel importance is task-dependent gives practitioners a clear offline/online split: rank layers once on any proxy task, then sample channels dynamically at runtime. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to non-convolutional architectures without re-deriving the channel-freezing geometry, whether adaptive K selection can close the remaining gap to full fine-tuning, and how to exploit activation-gradient sparsity to reduce the backpropagation latency that the current method does not address. The absence of on-hardware validation and the modest (0.5–1.5 pp) accuracy gains over SU limit immediate deployment impact.
