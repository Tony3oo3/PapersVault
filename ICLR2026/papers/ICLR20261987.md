## Problem

Predicting binding affinity across diverse biomolecular interactions (protein-ligand, protein-protein, antibody-antigen) remains challenging due to scarce high-quality experimental labels and the fact that existing methods are specialized to individual interaction types, limiting generalizability. While AlphaFold 3 enables accurate structure prediction, directly repurposing its generative architecture for representation learning is non-trivial, as generative objectives optimize for structure reconstruction rather than capturing features informative for functional properties like affinity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261987/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ADiT accepts amino acid sequences and all-atom 3D coordinates for both proteins and small molecules, tokenizing each protein residue as one token and each heavy atom in small molecules as one token. Evolutionary information is injected via pre-trained ESM-2-650M embeddings (zero for small-molecule tokens), and chemical features come from RDKit; no MSA or structural template inputs are required. The model outputs atom-level, token-level, and complex-level representations that are aggregated (Atom2Token average pooling, Token2Complex sum pooling) and passed through a linear or MLP head to produce a scalar binding affinity score.

## Method

ADiT (Atom-level Diffusion Transformer) re-engineers AlphaFold 3's diffusion transformer for representation learning by removing the heavy trunk conditioning module and MSA/template dependencies, replacing them with ESM-2 sequence features and explicit RDKit molecular features. The architecture follows a hierarchical atom→token→atom flow: atom representations are updated by DiT blocks with sparse local attention (each group of 32 atoms attends to 128 sequence-neighboring atoms), pooled to token level, refined by global token-level DiT blocks, then broadcast back and refined again by atom-level DiT blocks. Notably, the design is non-equivariant—no SE(3) invariance or geometric biases are imposed—relying instead on centroid-centering and random rotation augmentation during pre-training, which the authors argue provides greater flexibility to capture non-geometric features governing binding thermodynamics. Pre-training uses a denoising objective on 150K PDB clusters with fixed Gaussian noise (σ=0.5 Å), and fine-tuning sets the diffusion timestep to zero (clean-sample regime) with a reduced learning rate. Three variants (ADiT-S 12M, ADiT-M 35M, ADiT-L 253M) demonstrate consistent scaling gains across all four benchmark tasks.

## Results

On protein-ligand binding affinity (LBA-30), ADiT-L achieves the best RMSE of 1.308, Pearson 0.645, and Spearman 0.647, outperforming the previous best GET-PS (RMSE 1.309, Pearson 0.633, Spearman 0.642). On LBA-60, ADiT-L achieves RMSE 1.246, Pearson 0.797, and Spearman 0.795, surpassing ProNet (RMSE 1.343) and ProFSA (RMSE 1.377). On the Davis drug-target benchmark, ADiT-L attains MSE 0.198 and r²m 0.751, and on SKEMPIv2 protein-protein affinity, it reaches Pearson 0.691 and Spearman 0.560. On the HER2 antibody-antigen test set, ADiT-L achieves Pearson 0.567 and Spearman 0.576, outperforming GearBind+P (0.515/0.517) and FoldX (0.246/0.329).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADiT-L achieves state-of-the-art results on both LBA-30 and LBA-60 protein-ligand splits, beating GET-PS by 0.001 RMSE and 0.012 Pearson on LBA-30, and ProNet by 0.097 RMSE and 0.032 Pearson on LBA-60. On the Davis drug-target benchmark, ADiT-L (MSE 0.198, r²m 0.751) narrowly edges out NHGNN-DTA (MSE 0.196, r²m 0.744) on r²m while being marginally worse on MSE. On SKEMPIv2 protein-protein affinity, ADiT-L achieves the best Pearson (0.691) over Prompt-DDG (0.677) but trails it on Spearman (0.560 vs. 0.591), indicating strong but not dominant performance on this ranking task. On the HER2 antibody-antigen set, ADiT-L clearly outperforms GearBind+P (Pearson 0.567 vs. 0.515) and FoldX (0.567 vs. 0.246), demonstrating robust generalization to high-edit-distance antibody variants. Notably, even the smallest ADiT-S (12M params) outperforms most baselines on LBA-30, and the paper shows that simply fine-tuning the generative Protenix model (an AlphaFold 3 reproduction) yields substantially worse results than ADiT's purpose-built representation learning approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the AlphaFold 3 diffusion transformer architecture from a generative structure-prediction model into a representation learner for binding affinity prediction, guided by the insight that the heavy trunk conditioning module (MSAs, templates) becomes unnecessary when the structure is already provided as input. It draws on the DiT block design (Peebles & Xie, 2023) for hierarchical atom-token representation learning and adopts the denoising pre-training objective from Zaidi et al. (2023), while replacing AlphaFold 3's MSA and template dependencies with ESM-2 sequence features and RDKit molecular descriptors. Conceptually, it departs from the prevailing trend of equivariant geometric deep learning by deliberately using a non-equivariant transformer with only centroid-centering and random rotation augmentation, arguing that strong geometric inductive biases may constrain the model's ability to capture non-geometric features (electrostatics, chemical semantics) that govern binding thermodynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ADiT is the first unified all-atom foundation model that achieves competitive or state-of-the-art performance across four distinct biomolecular interaction types (protein-ligand, drug-target, protein-protein, antibody-antigen) with a single architecture, addressing the fragmentation of prior task-specific methods. The demonstrated scaling behavior and wet-lab-validated antibody affinity maturation results suggest practical utility in drug discovery pipelines. Key limitations include the requirement for fine-tuning (no zero-shot capability), restriction to protein-protein and protein-ligand interactions (excluding nucleic acids), and the modest parameter scale (253M) constrained by compute, leaving open whether substantially larger models or alternative pre-training objectives (e.g., varying noise schedules) could yield further gains.
