## Problem

The PATE framework for privacy-preserving knowledge transfer suffers a fundamental diversity-privacy trade-off in generative settings: as output diversity increases, independently sampled teacher votes split across many tokens, causing no token to exceed privacy thresholds and collapsing utility. Prior PATE adaptations for text generation either ignored diversity preservation or artificially constrained it (e.g., top-k truncation), distorting the output distribution. The paper asks whether this trade-off is inherent and designs an ensemble sampler that provably transfers diversity without additional privacy cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sensitive dataset D partitioned into n disjoint subsets, each instantiated as a teacher via in-context learning (a prompt of ~10 examples fed to a shared LLM such as Llama-3.1-8B) or fine-tuning. At each autoregressive step, each teacher produces a full next-token probability distribution over the vocabulary V (128k tokens for Llama-3-8B), and the ensemble sampler returns a single token or a <fail> signal. The final output is a complete response string R generated token-by-token, with the constraint that only tokens supported by at least τ teachers (per the diversity-preservation guarantee) can be returned under a given privacy budget.

## Method

The core mechanism is ensemble coordination: instead of each teacher independently sampling a token (Cold PATE), all teachers draw shared exponential randomness ρ = (u_j)_{j∈V} and each selects its token via the Gumbel-Max transform y_i = argmax_j p_j^{(i)}/u_j. This makes votes positively correlated (identical distributions yield identical tokens; agreement probability is the weighted Jaccard similarity) while preserving the critical low-sensitivity property that changing one teacher's data affects at most one histogram entry. The resulting coordinated histograms are peaky with large margins between top counts, so tokens supported by many teachers—even with small per-teacher probability q—surface as high-count peaks with probability Ω(q), enabling diversity transfer under threshold T = O(τ) or (ε,δ)-DP with τ = O(ε^{-1}log(1/δ)). The aggregation step applies standard DP mechanisms (NoisyArgMax for homogeneous ensembles, weighted sampling for heterogeneous ensembles) unchanged to these histograms, inheriting their privacy guarantees. The paper formalizes diversity preservation via two properties—transfer (probability q across ≥τ teachers is preserved in the aggregate) and relevance (no token is amplified beyond its average teacher probability)—and proves these guarantees are asymptotically tight, with empirical results showing 4–8× reductions in required privacy budget over Cold PATE.

## Results

On the curated Planet Z task (n=10⁴ teachers), Hot PATE (coordinated ensembles) achieves 40% coverage at T=4000 for k=20 and 20% coverage at T=2000 for k=100, whereas Cold PATE (independent ensembles) requires T≤1000 and T≤250 respectively — a 4× and 8× reduction in privacy budget. Coordinated ensembles produce maximum histogram counts above 0.25n (far exceeding the maximum token average probability of 0.14n for k=20 and 0.03n for k=100), while independent ensembles are limited to counts near their expectations. On the natural Dolly 15K instruction-generation task (n=512), coordinated ensembles maintain high coverage and support at T=0.5n, while independent ensembles transfer only a single token or fail entirely at T>0.17n. Coordinated ensembles also exhibit margins consistently above 0.4n between the top two counts, enabling data-dependent privacy analysis that yields exponentially more queries per privacy budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole experimental baseline is Cold PATE, which uses independent ensembles (each teacher samples independently from its distribution) with the same NoisyArgMax or threshold aggregation. Hot PATE (coordinated ensembles) dominates Cold PATE on every reported metric — coverage, support-size, average yield, maximum count, and margin — with the advantage growing as output diversity increases. On the curated task, the privacy-budget improvement is 4× for k=20 and 8× for k=100, meaning Hot PATE achieves the same utility at a fraction of the privacy cost. On the natural Dolly 15K task, independent ensembles fail to produce any yield (return a relevant token) for diverse prefixes at T>0.17n, while coordinated ensembles maintain high coverage even at T=0.5n. The margin between the top two histogram counts is consistently above 0.4n for coordinated ensembles versus near-zero for independent ensembles, which is critical for data-dependent privacy analysis that can process exponentially more queries per budget. No comparison is made against other PATE variants (e.g., SeqPATE, Flocks of Stochastic Parrots) in the experiments, though the paper discusses them qualitatively in the related work section.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PATE framework (Papernot et al., 2017; 2018) from its standard 'Cold PATE' form—where teachers sample independently—to a 'Hot PATE' variant using coordinated sampling via the Gumbel-Max trick with shared randomness. Prior PATE adaptations for diverse text generation (Tian et al., 2022; Duan et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2023) either inherited Cold PATE's unfavorable diversity-privacy trade-off or explicitly suppressed diversity to mitigate it; Hot PATE shows this trade-off is an artifact of independent sampling rather than inherent. The DP aggregation components draw on Cohen et al. (2021) for weighted sampling and Cohen and Lyu (2023) for data-dependent privacy accounting via the target-charging technique.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing diversity preservation as a first-class utility criterion and showing it can be achieved without additional privacy cost, the paper reframes how private generative systems should be evaluated and designed. The orders-of-magnitude improvement in privacy budget (4×–8×) for diverse tasks makes private synthetic data generation and summarization substantially more practical, and the drop-in compatibility with existing Cold PATE pipelines lowers adoption barriers. Open questions include scaling to very large vocabularies, handling heterogeneous ensembles where teachers represent individual users, and whether the coordinated-sampling advantage persists under full DP composition over long generation sequences. A practical limitation is the need for access to teacher distributions (or seed-controlled sampling APIs), which may not be available for all proprietary models.
