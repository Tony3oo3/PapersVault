## Problem

Existing learning-based trajectory planners rely heavily on expert demonstrations that rarely cover safety-critical events and often contain undesirable behaviors such as speeding, leaving models without explicit safety awareness. Additionally, directly applying Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to multi-objective planning fails because per-group reward normalization erases cross-group scale differences, causing rare high-variance safety-violation groups to receive similar advantages as abundant low-variance safe groups, thereby suppressing optimization for safety-critical objectives.

## Input / Output

The model consumes ego vehicle state history, surrounding agent states, and vector map (drivable area, road elements) as input, and autoregressively generates a 15-second trajectory (10 Hz) as a sequence of discrete motion tokens from a 1024-token vocabulary per agent category, where each token encodes a 0.5-second displacement and heading change via K-disk clustering. Surrounding agent motions are jointly predicted alongside the ego trajectory, with the ego planner additionally conditioned on explicit planning principles (safety, comfort, rule compliance) that are absent from the agent predictor.

## Method

Plan-R1 is a two-stage dual-model framework: first, a 6-layer transformer decoder with factorized attention (temporal self-attention, agent-map cross-attention, agent-agent cross-attention) is pre-trained via next-motion-token prediction on 1M expert scenes to capture diverse human-like multi-agent behaviors; second, the ego policy is fine-tuned with rule-based RL using a dual-model rollout where a trainable ego planner interacts with a frozen copy of the pre-trained model serving as a reactive world model for surrounding agents. The key algorithmic contribution is Variance-Decoupled GRPO (VD-GRPO), which replaces GRPO's per-group normalization (dividing by group standard deviation) with centering and a fixed global scaling constant, preserving absolute reward magnitudes so that rare safety-violation groups naturally produce larger gradients and remain dominant throughout training. The reward function is a product of binary safety indicators (collision, drivable area) and a weighted sum of soft cost terms (comfort, TTC, speed compliance, progress), ensuring safety violations nullify all reward.

## Results

On the nuPlan benchmark, Plan-R1 (learning-based, no post-processing) achieves state-of-the-art reactive closed-loop scores of 87.69 (Val14), 77.20 (Test14-hard), and 90.04 (Test14-random), surpassing the strongest prior learning-based method, Diffusion Planner, by +4.89, +7.98, and +7.11 points respectively. In the non-reactive setting, Plan-R1 matches Diffusion Planner on Val14 (88.98 vs. 89.87) and leads on Test14-hard (77.45 vs. 75.99) and Test14-random (91.23 vs. 89.19). With rule-based post-processing, Plan-R1* reaches 94.72 NR-CLS and 93.54 R-CLS on Val14, the highest among all methods including rule-based and hybrid planners. On the interPlan robustness benchmark, Plan-R1* achieves 72.33, outperforming PDM-Closed* (69.64) and PLUTO* (63.88).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among learning-based planners, Plan-R1 decisively outperforms Diffusion Planner in the reactive setting (the more realistic and challenging evaluation), winning by +4.89 on Val14, +7.98 on Test14-hard, and +7.11 on Test14-random, while slightly trailing it in non-reactive Val14 (88.98 vs. 89.87). Plan-R1 also surpasses PLUTO by +9.58 reactive points on Val14 and +17.46 on Test14-hard, and beats PlanTF by +10.74 reactive points on Val14. Against rule-based and hybrid methods (with post-processing), Plan-R1* achieves the highest NR-CLS (94.72) and R-CLS (93.54) on Val14, edging out Diffusion Planner* (94.26/92.90) and PDM-Closed* (92.84/92.12). On the interPlan robustness benchmark, Plan-R1* (72.33) outperforms PDM-Closed* (69.64) and PLUTO* (63.88), though it trails PLUTO without post-processing (57.74 vs. 56.64), which the authors attribute to PLUTO's contrastive training pipeline aligning with interPlan's perturbation strategy. Overall, Plan-R1's advantage is most pronounced in reactive and long-tail settings, where interaction-aware simulation and explicit safety alignment provide the largest gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

Plan-R1 adapts the LLM two-stage paradigm (pre-training as a general predictor, then RL fine-tuning for alignment) to trajectory planning, building on the autoregressive motion-token formulation from MotionLM and the factorized-attention multi-agent architecture from SMART. It departs from prior IL+RL hybrids (Gen-Drive, TrajHF, BC-SAC) by replacing preference data or imitation regularizers with interpretable rule-based rewards, and from standard GRPO by identifying that per-group variance normalization systematically down-weights rare safety-violation groups in multi-objective planning. The dual-model design (trainable ego planner + frozen reactive world model) extends the closed-loop RL setup beyond simple GT replay, drawing on the reactive evaluation protocol of nuPlan but using a learned predictor rather than IDM for surrounding agents during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and underappreciated failure mode: planners trained on expert data silently inherit undesirable behaviors (speeding, low TTC) because demonstrations are not safety-optimal, and the rule-based RL fine-tuning provides a clean, scalable fix without requiring expensive preference annotations. The VD-GRPO analysis—showing that GRPO's variance normalization actively suppresses safety-critical gradients in multi-objective settings—is a useful diagnostic for anyone applying group-based RL to tasks with hard priority hierarchies. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond nuPlan's simplified reactive (IDM) setting to truly interactive human agents, whether the 5M-parameter model scales to more complex scenes, and whether the fixed reward weights transfer across driving cultures and regulations.
