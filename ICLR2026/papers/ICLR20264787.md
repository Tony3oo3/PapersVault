## Problem

LLMs trained with next-token prediction (NTP) exhibit an inherent anticipatory capacity—pre-capturing future tokens in their softmax distributions—but the rigid one-hot fitting objective suppresses this capacity, limiting long-range planning and multi-step reasoning. No prior work has systematically profiled this anticipatory signal or provided a lightweight training strategy to activate it without architectural modifications or additional prediction heads.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a token sequence and a sliding window of k future tokens (k=10 by default) to construct a soft target distribution; at inference, it generates one token at a time using the standard single-head autoregressive decoder, requiring no architectural changes. The token embeddings from the initial embedding layer are used to compute pairwise semantic similarity matrices for the random-walk weighting, and the model's own frozen softmax probabilities at each step provide the anticipatory-preference weights. Maximum sequence lengths are 2048 for math and 512 for code/commonsense tasks.

## Method

Next-ToBE replaces the one-hot target in NTP with a soft target distribution over a bag of k future tokens, decomposing the loss into a dominant standard NTP term for the immediate next token and a weighted, normalized cross-entropy term over k−1 look-ahead tokens. The look-ahead weights w_{x_{t+j}} = α_{x_{t+j}} · β_{x_{t+j}} combine two signals: α (frozen, self-distillation) captures the model's intrinsic anticipatory preference by thresholding and power-scaling its current-step softmax probabilities, while β is computed via a random-walk-with-restart on a k×k graph whose transition matrix fuses temporal proximity (Gaussian decay) and semantic similarity (dot-product of token embeddings) via element-wise multiplication. The normalization of look-ahead token probabilities prevents small-probability tokens from dominating the loss, and the overall coefficient λ (≈0.05) keeps the immediate-next-token term dominant. The method introduces no additional parameters or heads, requiring only a modification to the target distribution within the standard NTP training loop.

## Results

On mathematical reasoning (Table 1), Next-ToBE achieves the highest average accuracy across all three model sizes: 38.3% on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B (+4.4% over NTP, +3.7% over MuToR), 42.7% on Qwen2.5-Math-7B (+3.3% over NTP, +1.6% over MuToR), and 26.7% on Llama3.1-8B-instruct (+2.8% over NTP, +2.2% over MuToR). On code generation and commonsense reasoning (Table 2), Next-ToBE similarly leads: e.g., 75.2% avg code accuracy and 89.4% avg commonsense accuracy on Qwen2.5-7B. In terms of efficiency, Next-ToBE incurs only ~20% additional training time and ~32% peak memory over standard NTP, while reducing peak memory by up to 68% compared to MTP and MuToR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Next-ToBE is compared against three baselines: standard NTP (one-hot next-token prediction), MTP (Medusa-style multi-token prediction with auxiliary heads), and MuToR (register-token-based future prediction within NTP). Across 36 comparisons spanning 3 model sizes and 12 benchmark categories, Next-ToBE attains the highest accuracy in 35 out of 36 cases. Against NTP, gains range from +2.2% to +4.4% average on math and +2.2% to +3.6% on code; against MTP, gains are +3.7% to +4.4% on math and +2.2% to +3.9% on code; against MuToR, gains are +1.6% to +2.2% on math and +1.9% to +2.7% on code. Notably, Next-ToBE achieves these gains with a single prediction head (no auxiliary heads or register tokens), using only ~12.6 GB peak memory versus 16.6 GB for MTP and 39.5 GB for MuToR, and ~2h30min training time versus 2h56min (MTP) and 2h39min (MuToR). The one exception where Next-ToBE does not lead is Qwen2.5-Math-7B on AIME24, where MuToR and Next-ToBE tie at 20.0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the NTP framework, departing from the MTP paradigm (Medusa, Gloeckle et al.) that adds auxiliary prediction heads, and from MuToR which introduces register tokens. Conceptually, Next-ToBE is framed as a structured form of label smoothing where the one-hot target is replaced by a soft distribution over a future token window, with weights determined by the model's own anticipatory signal (self-distillation) and a random-walk-with-restart ranking over temporal-semantic relations borrowed from ASAP-attention (Chen et al., 2025a). The lineage runs from the observation that LLMs implicitly anticipate future tokens (Pal et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2024; Dong et al., 2025) to the hypothesis that the one-hot NTP objective suppresses this capacity, and finally to a minimal loss-level intervention that activates it without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Next-ToBE offers a parameter-free, single-head modification to the standard NTP loss that consistently outperforms MTP baselines across three model families (Qwen, Llama) and three task domains (math, code, commonsense) at substantially lower memory cost (~32% vs ~75% overhead). The demonstration that anticipatory capacity can be cultivated from scratch during pretraining (GPT-2-124M) suggests the principle generalizes beyond fine-tuning, though the pretraining evidence is limited to a small-scale experiment. Open questions include whether the confidence-reasoning trade-off (optimal next-token probability around 0.75–0.79) can be exploited for inference-time control, whether the approach extends to non-text sequential data (time series, multimodal), and whether the modest perplexity increase during pretraining is acceptable at scale.
