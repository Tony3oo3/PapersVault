## Problem

Existing CTR prediction methods model long-term and short-term user interests separately but fuse them with linear operators (concatenation, attention, gating) that assume aligned feature spaces and cannot disentangle stable preferences from transient noise in short-term behaviors. This paper identifies three specific failures of such linear fusion: misaligned heterogeneous feature spaces from divergent feature selection, suboptimal inductive biases from decoupled late-fusion pipelines, and unchecked noise propagation that corrupts long-term representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a long-term behavior sequence (e.g., 90–256 historical clicks) encoded into a single dense vector h^L, and K short-term sessions (e.g., 10–16 recent interactions segmented by temporal rules) each encoded into session-level embeddings h_i^S. Unusually, h^L serves as the initial state x_0 of the diffusion forward process (progressively noised to x_T), while the short-term session embeddings act as conditional guidance during the reverse denoising process. The output is a generated fused interest representation x_0 that feeds a standard CTR head to produce pCTR, alongside user static features, ad features, and candidate ad information.

## Method

iFusion reformulates interest fusion as a conditional generation process: the long-term representation is noised forward, then progressively denoised under short-term guidance to produce the fused representation. Two core components drive this: (1) DCFG decomposes the short-term guidance into a core-preference signal (extracted via average pooling, acting as a low-pass filter) and a transient-fluctuation signal (extracted via attention, acting as a high-pass filter), each applied with independent guidance strengths γ_cp and γ_tf in a generalized classifier-free guidance formula grounded in an energy-based score decomposition; (2) MARN processes the K sessions autoregressively rather than in parallel, so each session's denoised output conditions the next session's generation, capturing sequential interest evolution. A consistency loss enforces noise-invariant representations to enable single-step inference, and training jointly optimizes CTR cross-entropy, an interest-evolution loss, a guidance-disentanglement loss, and the consistency term.

## Results

iFusion achieves the best AUC across all four datasets: 0.8512 on Amazon Book, 0.9347 on Taobao, 0.6652 on Ali Ads, and 0.7685 on the Industrial dataset, outperforming the strongest traditional baseline (MTGR) by 0.0072, 0.0051, 0.0037, and 0.0037 AUC respectively. In online A/B tests over 7 days with hundreds of millions of users, iFusion delivers a statistically significant +2.44% CTR gain and +2.61% eCPM gain (p < 0.001). The method also incurs only a 0.302% TP99 latency increase in production, confirming practical deployability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

iFusion outperforms all 11 baselines across all four datasets. Among traditional CTR methods, MTGR (the strongest baseline) is the closest competitor, with iFusion gaining +0.0072 AUC on Amazon, +0.0051 on Taobao, +0.0037 on Ali Ads, and +0.0037 on the Industrial dataset. Among diffusion-based methods, DiffuMIN is the nearest competitor, and iFusion surpasses it by +0.0085 AUC on Amazon, +0.0059 on Taobao, +0.0057 on Ali Ads, and +0.0062 on Industrial. The gap over diffusion baselines is notably larger than over traditional baselines, suggesting that the DCFG disentanglement mechanism and MARN autoregressive denoising specifically address limitations in existing diffusion-based guidance. The paper notes that even a 0.001 AUC gain is considered practically significant in CTR prediction, making all reported improvements meaningful. Online A/B tests confirm the offline gains translate to production: +2.44% CTR and +2.61% eCPM with p < 0.001.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022), repurposing both for a task they were not originally designed for: fusing long-term and short-term user interest representations in CTR prediction. It departs from the dominant discriminative fusion paradigm (concatenation, attention, gating in DIN/DIEN/SIM/TWIN/MTGR) by reformulating interest fusion as a conditional denoising process, and extends prior diffusion-based recommendation work (DiffuRec, DreamRec, DiffuMIN) by introducing multi-session autoregressive guidance (MARN) and a disentangled dual-channel guidance mechanism (DCFG) rather than single-vector conditioning. The energy-based and Langevin dynamics framing provides a theoretical bridge between the diffusion reverse process and the multi-scale nature of user interest signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a generative formulation of interest fusion can outperform all discriminative baselines while incurring negligible latency overhead (0.3%), making diffusion models practically viable for real-time CTR serving—a concern that has limited prior adoption. The zero-data robustness guarantees (Theorems 3–4) and the finding that single-step inference with consistency distillation is optimal (performance degrades with more steps) offer practical guidance for deployment. Open questions include whether the DCFG disentanglement generalizes to more than two interest scales, whether the autoregressive MARN advantage persists at very large session counts under strict latency budgets, and whether the energy-based framing transfers to other fusion tasks (CVR, GMV) as the authors suggest.
