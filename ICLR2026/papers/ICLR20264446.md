## Problem

Existing conformal prediction methods for multivariate outputs rely on residual-based conformity scores that impose fixed geometric constraints (e.g., ℓ₂ balls or rectangles) and over-concentrate around a single mode, producing overly conservative prediction areas that fail to capture the true shape of multimodal predictive distributions. The paper asks whether density-based conformity scores from normalising flows can yield compact, geometry-free, potentially disjoint prediction regions while preserving finite-sample coverage guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes a covariate vector x (or a temporal context window encoded by an RNN/Transformer for time series) and a held-out calibration set, and produces a prediction region Γ_ε(x) ⊆ ℝ^{D_y} defined as the superlevel set of the estimated conditional density above a conformal threshold τ_ε. The region can be non-convex and disconnected, and its area/volume is computed efficiently via importance sampling in the flow's latent space rather than direct Monte Carlo in data space. For time series, the entire future trajectory x_{T+1:T+H} is treated as a single multivariate output, with exchangeability assumed across independent trajectories.

## Method

JAPAN trains a conditional normalising flow to estimate p(y|x), then uses the log-density log p̂(y|x) as the conformity score, thresholding at the (1−ε)-quantile of calibration log-densities to form the prediction region {y : log p̂(y|x) ≥ τ_ε}. The key efficiency gain comes from Proposition 3: because the flow is bijective, area estimation is shifted to the latent (Gaussian) space via an importance-sampling estimator that only requires N samples from the base distribution, reducing sample complexity by orders of magnitude compared to naive Monte Carlo in data space. Theoretically, Proposition 1 shows that if the estimated density preserves the ranking of the true density (up to a monotone transformation), the resulting region is identical to the optimal density-threshold region; Proposition 2 bounds the area difference under approximate rank preservation. The framework is further extended to unconditional, conditional-on-prediction, posterior, latent-only (recovering CONTRA as a special case), and τ_ε(x)-adaptive thresholding variants, each decoupling or recombining the predictive and density-estimation components.

## Results

On four multivariate regression benchmarks (Energy, RF2D, RF4D, SCM20D), JAPAN achieves the smallest prediction areas while maintaining ~0.90 marginal coverage across all datasets. The advantage is most dramatic in the high-dimensional RF4D setting, where JAPAN's area is 24.11±2.33 versus 59.27 (CONTRA), 111.63 (PCP), and 1180.65 (CQR) for the nearest baselines. On four time series datasets (COVID-19, Particle-1, Drone, Pedestrian), JAPAN again produces the most compact regions, yielding up to an order-of-magnitude smaller volumes (e.g., 400.94 vs. 1048.69 for CQR on COVID-19; 0.89 vs. 3.33 for CQR on Particle-1). All methods achieve comparable marginal coverage near the nominal 0.90 level, so the primary differentiator is area/volume efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JAPAN outperforms all 11 baselines on prediction area across every dataset while maintaining comparable marginal coverage (~0.90). The gap is most pronounced in the high-dimensional RF4D setting: JAPAN's area of 24.11 is 2.5× smaller than CONTRA (59.27), 4.6× smaller than PCP (111.63), and ~49× smaller than CQR (1180.65). Among the closest competitors, CONTRA (latent-space conformal balls) and PCP (local L2-ball unions) are the only methods that partially avoid hard geometric constraints, yet both still include low-density regions that JAPAN's density-thresholding excludes. Residual-based and quantile-based methods (CQR, MCQR, Dist-Split, RCP) suffer the largest area inflation in RF4D, with areas exceeding 1000 units versus JAPAN's 24.11. Copula-based methods (CopulaCPTS, VanillaCopula) and CFRNN show high variance and occasional numerical instability (e.g., MCQR reporting volumes on the order of 10¹⁷ in the conditional-modelling ablation). On time series, JAPAN similarly dominates: on COVID-19 its area (400.94) is 2.6× smaller than CQR (1048.69), and on Particle-1 it is 3.7× smaller than CQR (3.33 vs. 0.89). The only near-tie is on SCM20D, where JAPAN (61.28) is marginally smaller than CONTRA (61.93) but both are competitive.

## Relation to Prior Work

JAPAN extends the authors' own prior work CCN-JPR (English & Lippert, 2024) by providing a comprehensive treatment across multivariate regression and time series, new theoretical guarantees linking density estimation quality to area efficiency, multiple density-based conformity score variants, and an efficient latent-space area computation method. It departs from the dominant residual-based conformity score paradigm (CQR, MCQR, RCP, NLE) and from latent-space ball constructions (CONTRA, PCP) by thresholding directly on the full log-density—including the Jacobian area-change term—yielding truly geometry-free regions in data space. The TARFLOW architecture (Zhai et al., 2024) is adapted for time series, and the framework subsumes CONTRA as a special case (latent-density-only scoring), unifying two previously separate approaches under one umbrella.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, flexible framework for constructing compact, potentially disjoint prediction areas with finite-sample validity, addressing a key practical limitation of residual-based conformal methods that over-concentrate around the mean and fail on multimodal distributions. The theoretical results (rank-preservation optimality, bounded area degradation under approximate rank preservation) give practitioners confidence that better density estimation directly translates to tighter regions. Key open questions include: (i) JAPAN only guarantees marginal, not conditional, coverage—though the tau-adaptive extension hints at a path forward; (ii) the framework's behavior under significant distribution shift or underfitted flows in very high dimensions remains less characterized; (iii) whether the posterior-modelling variant can offer theoretical advantages in discrete-output settings is left for future work.
