## Problem

Existing spatial-temporal benchmarks for VLMs either evaluate egocentric reasoning from single or few camera views, or assess static geometric reasoning on maps, but none test the ability to jointly integrate graphical map context with multi-view video observations for reasoning about moving targets across large-scale camera networks. This gap leaves unassessed the geographic spatial-temporal intelligence required for real-world applications such as traffic management and emergency response, where models must perform perspective switching between maps and videos, reason across non-overlapping fields of view, and infer over unobserved spatial-temporal regions.

## Input / Output

Each question provides a rendered map image (annotated with camera positions, orientations, FoV polygons, north arrow, and scale bar) alongside 1–3 video clips from non-overlapping cameras, uniformly sampled so the total frame count stays within 20. For basic tasks (Geo-Location, Arrival Time-Interval, Motion-State, Causal Reordering), the model outputs a multiple-choice selection; for combinatorial forecasting tasks (Next Spot, Trajectory, Multi-Target Trajectory Forecasting), it must generate both a camera ID and a corresponding time interval (or a sequence thereof). The novel ST-IoU metric multiplies an exact-match indicator on the predicted camera ID by the temporal IoU between predicted and ground-truth intervals, so a spatially wrong prediction scores zero regardless of temporal accuracy.

## Method

GTR-Bench is constructed via an automated pipeline that segments target trajectories from CityFlow (outdoor vehicles, 31 cameras, ~985 m average inter-camera distance) and MTMMC (indoor pedestrians, 16 cameras, ~31 m average distance), calibrates each camera with a manually annotated 3×3 homography matrix to project 2D bounding boxes onto a real-world map, and algorithmically derives velocity, direction, and distance per timestamp. Seven tasks are organized into two tiers: three basic tasks (Geo-Location, Arrival Time-Interval, Motion-State) that isolate individual reasoning dimensions, and four combinatorial tasks (Causal Reordering, Next Spot Forecasting, Trajectory Forecasting, Multi-Target Trajectory Forecasting) that require integrating all three. Distractor generation is scenario-specific—synthetic cameras placed along road networks outdoors and cameras from different floors or functionally distinct areas indoors—combined with camera-ID randomization to prevent superficial heuristics. The benchmark comprises 420 questions (60 per task) from 364 videos, balanced across outdoor/indoor and three complexity tiers, and underwent two-stage manual quality verification. Evaluation covers 13 VLMs (5 proprietary APIs, 8 open-source models deployed on 8×V100 GPUs with temperature 0.1 and max_new_token 16384) plus a traditional ReID baseline, revealing that even the best model (Gemini-2.5-Pro, 34.9%) trails human performance (78.61%) by over 40 points.

## Results

On GTR-Bench (420 questions across 7 geo-temporal reasoning tasks in outdoor and indoor camera networks), the best proprietary model Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves only 34.93% average accuracy, far below human performance of 78.61%. The best open-source model, InternVL3-38B, reaches 30.76%, while the open-source average is 23.82% versus 33.15% for proprietary models. A traditional ReID-based baseline (45.72% on its applicable subset) actually outperforms all VLMs, highlighting the difficulty of the benchmark. Performance drops sharply from basic tasks (e.g., Geo-Location ~60%) to combinatorial forecasting tasks (e.g., Trajectory Forecasting ~13% ST-IoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All 13 VLMs fall substantially short of human performance (78.61%), with the best model (Gemini-2.5-Pro, 34.93%) achieving less than half the human score. Proprietary models (avg 33.15%) consistently outperform open-source models (avg 23.82%), with the top three proprietary models (Gemini-2.5-Pro, GPT-5, Claude-4-Sonnet) clustered within 1 point of each other (34.93–34.03). Among open-source models, InternVL3-38B (30.76%) leads, followed closely by Qwen2.5-VL-32B (30.45%) and GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking (28.51%), while smaller models (2B–8B) score between 17.65% and 23.05%. Notably, a traditional ReID-based CV baseline (45.72% on its applicable task subset) outperforms every VLM, underscoring that current VLMs have not yet surpassed specialized visual matching for geo-temporal tasks. Performance degrades sharply from basic tasks (Geo-Location ~60%, Arrival Time-Interval ~50–77%) to combinatorial forecasting tasks (Trajectory Forecasting ST-IoU ~0–13%), and most models perform better outdoors than indoors, with the exception of Gemini-2.5-Pro which shows the reverse pattern.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two existing benchmark families: geographic reasoning benchmarks (ReasonMap, SpatialLLM) that evaluate static geometric tasks on maps, and spatial-temporal reasoning benchmarks (ST-VLM, STI-Bench, ViewSpatial-Bench, MultiSPA) that evaluate egocentric/allocentric motion reasoning from single or few cameras. GTR-Bench deliberately bridges this gap by requiring joint reasoning over both graphical map context and multi-view video with non-overlapping fields of view, a combination absent from prior work. It also repurposes established multi-camera tracking datasets (CityFlow, MTMMC) and ReID infrastructure not for traditional CV tracking but as the substrate for VLM evaluation, introducing a novel ST-IoU metric that composes spatial correctness with temporal interval overlap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a stark capability gap (best model 34.9% vs. human 78.61%) and, more importantly, diagnoses three specific failure modes—imbalanced context utilization, weak temporal forecasting, and poor map-video alignment—that point toward concrete research directions for improving VLM spatial-temporal intelligence in real-world applications like traffic management and emergency response. The consistent large gap between MCQ accuracy and ST-IoU scores suggests VLMs can identify locations but fundamentally lack temporal prediction mechanisms, an open problem that current architectures do not address. Limitations include the relatively small scale (420 questions, 364 videos) and the fact that the benchmark's difficulty may partly reflect the narrowness of the camera network topology rather than generalizable geo-temporal reasoning.
