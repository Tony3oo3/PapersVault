## Problem

CLIP vision encoders are vulnerable to typographic attacks where text overlaid on images causes targeted misclassification, malicious generation, and VLM jailbreaks. Existing defenses (fine-tuning, learnable prefixes, sparse autoencoders) all require gradient-based optimization, are computationally expensive, and offer no mechanistic insight into which model components drive the vulnerability. No gradient-free, interpretable defense that directly targets the causal circuit responsible for typographic sensitivity had been identified.

## Input / Output

The dyslexic CLIP models accept standard RGB images at the same resolution as the base model and produce class labels via zero-shot classification, serving as direct drop-in replacements. The circuit construction phase additionally requires a typographic dataset (images with overlaid adversarial text) and a non-typographic benchmark to guide head selection, but the resulting model incurs zero inference-time overhead beyond the standard forward pass. The ablation modifies only the residual stream contribution of selected attention heads to the cls token, leaving all spatial token computations and the input image untouched.

## Method

Dyslexify identifies a sparse set of attention heads (the 'typographic circuit') in the latter half of the vision encoder that causally transmit typographic information to the cls token, using a Typographic Attention Score T_{i,ℓ} that quantifies each head's spatial attention to text regions. The circuit is constructed iteratively: heads are ranked by T_{i,ℓ} and added one at a time, accepting a head only if it improves typographic-attack robustness (ΔAcc_typo > 0) without degrading non-typographic accuracy beyond a tolerance ε. Defense is applied by zeroing the cls-token contribution of each circuit head (H_{i,ℓ,cls} → 0) while preserving all spatial contributions, requiring no gradient steps or weight updates. Causal validation is provided by manipulating the cls self-attention weight α in circuit heads: increasing α (strengthening the attention sink) suppresses typographic vulnerability, while decreasing α amplifies it, confirming the heads' causal role. The approach scales to billion-parameter models on consumer hardware and reduces typographic attack success by up to 22% on ImageNet-100-typo while preserving standard accuracy within 1%.

## Results

Dyslexify improves zero-shot accuracy on typographic attack benchmarks by up to +22.06% (ViT-G on ImageNet-100-typo) and up to +31.11% (ViT-B on Disentangling), while degrading standard ImageNet-100 accuracy by less than 1% across all model sizes (ViT-B through ViT-BigG). The constructed typographic circuits are sparse, ablating at most 10.1% of all attention heads (29 out of 288 for ViT-L). In the medical domain (WhyLesionCLIP), typographic attacks reduce melanoma detection accuracy by up to 22.1%, and Dyslexify recovers up to 19.3% accuracy under attack while also improving base-model accuracy on three of four non-attacked datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole direct baseline is Defense-Prefix (DP; Azuma & Matsui, ICCV 2023), a gradient-based method that learns a prefix token for CLIP's language transformer. On ViT-L, Dyslexify outperforms DP on two of three real-world typographic benchmarks: RTA-100 (71.00% vs. 62.20%, a 8.8-point gap) and PAINT (76.36% vs. 71.82%, a 4.5-point gap). However, DP substantially outperforms Dyslexify on the Disentangling benchmark (82.78% vs. 60.56%), where the attack format (Post-it notes) may interact differently with the ablated circuit. On non-typographic benchmarks, DP retains a slight edge on Food-101 (89.83% vs. 89.31%) and ImageNet-100 (81.70% vs. 79.52%), though the latter is attributed to DP being trained on ImageNet-100-typo and inadvertently capturing useful classification features. Dyslexify wins on Aircraft (34.62% vs. 32.94%) and, critically, requires no gradient-based optimization, runs 3.8× faster on ViT-B, and fits within 24 GB VRAM for billion-parameter models where DP fails with out-of-memory errors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of typographic attack defenses in CLIP (fine-tuning, Defense-Prefix, Sparse Autoencoders) by introducing a gradient-free, mechanistic-interpretability-based approach. It builds conceptually on Hung et al. (2024) Attention Tracker for the Typographic Attention Score, and on the attention-sink literature (Xiao et al., 2023) to explain how specialized heads gate typographic information into the cls token. The key positioning is that prior defenses all require gradient-based optimization, whereas Dyslexify identifies and ablates a small causal circuit of attention heads at inference time, making it the first causal-intervention defense for typographic attacks in CLIP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical release of drop-in dyslexic CLIP models (ViT-B through ViT-BigG) that are substantially more robust to typographic attacks while preserving <1% standard accuracy loss makes this immediately deployable in safety-critical settings, as demonstrated in melanoma detection. However, the defense only protects the cls token, leaving spatial-token pathways (used by VLMs like LLaVA and IP-Adapters) unaddressed, and the significant OCR degradation (8–30%) limits applicability where text recognition is needed. An open question is whether the identified circuits generalize to adaptive or more sophisticated typographic attacks, which the authors note cannot be evaluated due to the non-differentiable nature of the threat.
