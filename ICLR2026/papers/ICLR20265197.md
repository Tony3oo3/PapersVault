## Problem

Existing video anomaly detection methods assume a fixed definition of what constitutes an anomaly, making them unable to handle concept drift in open-world scenarios where the same behavior may be normal or abnormal depending on context (e.g., a pedestrian on a road is normal in crime surveillance but abnormal in freeway monitoring). The paper formalizes this as a divergence between training and testing conditional distributions P(Y|V) and proposes a language-guided paradigm that conditions predictions on both the video and a user-provided textual anomaly definition, provably eliminating concept drift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265197/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video clip (sampled at 8-frame intervals, 10 fps for DoTA) and a natural-language anomaly definition (either category names or free-form descriptions) as input, producing a frame-level binary anomaly score vector of length L and a multi-class anomaly probability vector of length L×C. During training, the definition is randomly sampled as either class names or anomaly descriptions from the PreVAD dataset; at inference, users supply arbitrary text prompts. The model operates under weak supervision with only video-level labels, and the detection head fuses language-guided and language-agnostic scores via a learnable parameter.

## Method

LaGoVAD implements the joint mapping Φ:(V,Z)→Y using a CLIP ViT-B/16 image encoder plus a 2-layer rotary-position-encoding Transformer as the temporal encoder for video, a CLIP text encoder for the anomaly definition, and a 2-layer co-attention Transformer for cross-modal fusion, followed by separate binary detection and multi-class classification heads. Two regularization strategies address overfitting in the multimodal joint space: (1) Dynamic Video Synthesis concatenates semantically similar segments retrieved via pre-computed CLIP k-nearest neighbors to create longer videos with varied anomaly ratios, generating frame-level pseudo-labels on the fly for supervised loss; (2) Contrastive learning with hard negative mining aggregates frame features weighted by sigmoid anomaly scores into foreground and background video-level representations, then applies a symmetric InfoNCE-style loss between these and text features, where the background of an abnormal video serves as the hard negative. Training optimizes four losses jointly (MIL, MIL-align, DVS, contrastive) under weak supervision, and the paper introduces PreVAD (35,279 videos, 35 subcategories, multi-level taxonomy with anomaly descriptions) as the largest video anomaly dataset to support this paradigm.

## Results

Under Protocol 1 (zero-shot cross-domain detection), LaGoVAD trained on PreVAD achieves the best performance across all seven test datasets: UCF-Crime AUC 81.12, XD-Violence AP 74.25, MSAD AUC 90.41, UB-Normal AUC 58.07, DoTA AUC 62.60, TAD AUC 89.56, and LAD AUC 78.91. Under Protocol 2 (concept drift evaluation), LaGoVAD attains XD-drift@5 AUC 85.7 / AP 37.1 and MSAD-drift@5 AUC 85.6 / AP 40.1, outperforming all LLM-based and multi-modal baselines. For multi-class classification, LaGoVAD reaches 78.13% accuracy and 63.80 F1 on XD-Violence, surpassing the next-best method (VadCLIP on PreVAD) by 6.75% accuracy and 5.81 F1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaGoVAD (PreVAD) outperforms VadCLIP (PreVAD) on all seven datasets, with the largest gains on DoTA (+12.01 AUC) and XD-Violence (+6.82 AP), confirming that language-guided detection and the two regularization strategies provide consistent improvements over the prior SOTA VAD architecture. Against open-vocabulary OVVAD, LaGoVAD achieves comparable UCF AUC (81.12 vs. 82.42 with AIGC+XD training) but substantially higher XD AP (74.25 vs. 63.74), and uniquely covers all seven test sets. On the concept drift protocol (Protocol 2), LaGoVAD surpasses VadCLIP by +4.4 AUC and +1.3 AP on XD-drift@5, and by +0.4 AUC and +21.3 AP on MSAD-drift@5, while LLM-based methods (Qwen2-VL, Qwen2.5-VL) lag far behind at 60–63 AUC. For multi-class classification, LaGoVAD leads all baselines on XD-Violence (78.13% Acc, 63.80 F1) and UCF-Crime accuracy (51.72%), though VadCLIP (PreVAD) edges it slightly on UCF F1 (17.81 vs. 16.64). Scene-dependent CMRL and multi-domain MultiDomain are significantly weaker, with CMRL reaching only 46.74 AP on XD and MultiDomain limited to three of seven test sets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the VadCLIP and Open-Vocabulary VAD (OVVAD) architectures, adopting their CLIP-based vision encoder, Transformer temporal encoder, and MIL loss formulation. However, it departs fundamentally from the fixed-definition assumption shared by all prior VAD paradigms (closed-set, open-set, domain generalization, and scene-dependent methods) by explicitly modeling the anomaly definition as a stochastic input variable Z and learning a joint mapping Φ:(V,Z)→Y. The two proposed regularizers—dynamic video synthesis and contrastive learning with in-sample hard negative mining—are novel contributions that address the overfitting inherent in the expanded multimodal joint space, drawing inspiration from data augmentation and CLIP-style contrastive objectives but applied in a VAD-specific manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: real-world anomaly definitions shift with policy, environment, and user intent (e.g., pedestrian-on-road is normal in urban surveillance but abnormal on a freeway), a problem prior methods structurally cannot handle. The PreVAD dataset (35,279 videos, 35 categories, text descriptions) is the largest VAD resource to date and provides a scalable curation pipeline that could become a standard pretraining benchmark. Open questions remain: the architecture is acknowledged as simplified (2-layer Transformer, basic co-attention), contradictory or ambiguous user definitions produce unreliable outputs, and it is unclear whether the paradigm's benefits persist when integrated into more sophisticated temporal architectures or scaled to longer videos.
