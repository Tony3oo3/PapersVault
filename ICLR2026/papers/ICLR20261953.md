## Problem

Existing token-caching acceleration methods for diffusion transformers (e.g., ToCa, DuCa, TaylorSeer) rely on manually designed per-layer sparsity schedules and retain full forward computations in several predefined denoising steps, which limits their acceleration potential—especially in few-step models like FLUX. The paper asks whether layer-wise token sparsity can be learned end-to-end under a global compression budget, eliminating both manual heuristics and the need for rigid full-step designs.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained diffusion transformers (PixArt-α, DiT-XL/2, FLUX.1-schnell, Wan2.1-1.3B) and takes only text prompts or class indices during training—no image data is used. At inference, it consumes the same text prompts or class labels as the original model and produces generated images at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution (or 2-second, 8-fps videos at 256×256 for Wan2.1), but with a learned per-layer, per-timestep token sparsity mask that skips recomputation for a fraction of tokens. The only trained component is a compact cost predictor of size (T×L)×|S|, which is resolution-independent and transfers from 256×256 training to 512×512 evaluation without retraining.

## Method

DiffSparse formulates token sparsity allocation as a differentiable optimization problem solved by a three-component pipeline: (1) a learnable sparsity cost predictor with (T×L)×|S| parameters outputs a normalized cost matrix quantifying the quality cost of each candidate sparsity rate for every layer-timestep pair; (2) a dynamic programming solver (O((L·T)²·|S|)) selects the optimal per-layer sparsity configuration that minimizes total cost while satisfying a global sparsity ratio R; (3) a token selector ranks tokens by a composite importance score (self-attention influence, cross-attention entropy, cache-reuse frequency, spatial bonus) and reuses cached features for the lowest-scoring tokens. Training uses a two-stage strategy—Stage 1 independently optimizes full-step positions and layer sparsity costs, then Stage 2 merges them via a warm-start constant δ and fine-tunes the unified cost matrix—guided by an LPIPS perceptual distillation loss with Straight-Through Estimation to backpropagate through the discrete binary masks. This eliminates the need for predefined full-computation steps and replaces manual sparsity tuning with a compact, differentiable, end-to-end optimization.

## Results

On PixArt-α with 20 DPM++ steps evaluated on MS-COCO (30k samples, 256×256), DiffSparse at R=43% achieves a 1.74× speedup with FID-30k of 26.91, surpassing the unpruned baseline (FID 28.20) while all competing methods degrade. At a more aggressive R=54% (1.91× speedup), DiffSparse still attains FID 27.79, better than the original model. On DiT-XL/2 (ImageNet, 50 DDIM steps), DiffSparse reaches FID 2.81 at 2.07× speedup versus 3.05 for ToCa. On Wan2.1-1.3B (VBench, 20 steps), DiffSparse achieves a VBench score of 43.83 at 2.05× speedup, exceeding both the unpruned model (43.82) and DuCa (43.56 at 1.69×).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffSparse outperforms all six compared baselines on FID at comparable or higher speedups. At ~1.74× speedup on PixArt-α, DiffSparse (FID 26.91) beats ToCa (28.35) by 1.44 FID points, DuCa (27.98) by 1.07, DiCache (28.19) by 1.28, TaylorSeer (29.08) by 2.17, FORA (29.67) by 2.76, and DeepCache (29.61) by 2.70. On DiT-XL/2 (ImageNet), DiffSparse improves FID from 3.05 (ToCa) and 3.04 (DuCa) to 2.81 at a similar ~2.07× speedup, an 8% relative gain over ToCa. On Wan2.1-1.3B (VBench), DiffSparse achieves a higher VBench score (43.83 vs. 43.56) than DuCa while delivering a substantially larger speedup (2.05× vs. 1.69×) at the same MACs budget. Notably, at R=54% DiffSparse's FID (27.79) surpasses the unpruned PixArt-α (28.20), a result none of the baselines achieve at any sparsity level tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffSparse extends the token-level feature caching line (ToCa, DuCa) by replacing their manually designed per-layer, per-timestep sparsity schedules with an end-to-end learnable sparsity cost predictor solved via dynamic programming. It also departs from the full-step design inherited from FORA and DeepCache, where several denoising steps must be computed in full to correct accumulated caching errors; DiffSparse's two-stage training strategy eliminates this requirement by gradually redistributing computation. The token selector itself is borrowed from ToCa/DuCa, and the paper explicitly positions its contribution as orthogonal to the ranking heuristic, focusing instead on *where* and *how much* sparsity to apply.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 54% compute reduction on PixArt-α that *improves* FID over the unpruned model, and consistent gains across DiT-XL/2, FLUX, and Wan2.1 without architecture-specific tuning. The cost predictor's small parameter count ((T×L)×|S|) and demonstrated transfer from 256×256 to 512×512 without retraining make it attractive for deployment. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to substantially different architectures (e.g., MMDiT-style dual-stream transformers), whether the quality-improvement-over-original effect holds beyond PixArt-α, and how the method interacts with other acceleration strategies (distillation, step reduction) in production pipelines.
