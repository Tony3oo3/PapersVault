## Problem

Existing Unsupervised Environment Design (UED) methods approximate task difficulty (regret) using only value-function loss (e.g., PVL), ignoring the contribution of transition-model mismatch to the true regret. Additionally, all prior UED methods sample tasks based solely on individual difficulty, overlooking how training on one task accelerates or hinders progress on others. TRACED addresses both gaps by augmenting regret with a transition-prediction error term and introducing a lightweight Co-Learnability metric that captures cross-task transfer.

## Input / Output

The method consumes environment observations (5×5 pixel grids with a direction scalar for MiniGrid; 24-dim state vectors for BipedalWalker), task parameter vectors θ, and agent trajectories collected during PPO rollouts. The transition-prediction model (a 1-layer LSTM with hidden size 128) is trained online to reconstruct the next observation or state, and its per-step reconstruction error (L1 for images, L2 for continuous states) is averaged over an episode to produce the ATPL scalar. The output is a rank-transformed Task Priority score per task in the buffer, which governs both new-task sampling (inversely proportional to rank) and prioritized replay, ultimately yielding a trained generalist PPO policy evaluated zero-shot on held-out levels.

## Method

TRACED decomposes one-step regret into three terms—value estimation error, reward gap, and future value gap—and shows that PVL captures only the first term while the future-value gap is influenced by mismatch between the learned transition model P̂ and true dynamics P. To correct this, the method trains a recurrent transition-prediction model f_φ (LSTM) to minimize one-step reconstruction loss, and defines ATPL as the episode-average of this loss; the combined regret estimate is PVL + α·ATPL, with a theoretical bound (Lemma 1) showing the dynamics-induced component of the future-value gap is upper-bounded by L_V·C_ℓ·ATPL. A second novelty is Co-Learnability, which measures the average reduction in difficulty of other replayed tasks after training on task i, approximating its marginal transfer contribution without Shapley-value computation. These two signals are fused into Task Priority = Rank(TaskDifficulty + β·CoLearnability), and the entire scoring mechanism is dropped into ACCEL's evolutionary loop (sample → replay → mutate lowest-priority levels), with the rank transform preventing outlier tasks from collapsing the curriculum. Ablations confirm ATPL drives the primary complexity ramp-up while Co-Learnability adds further gains when paired with the refined regret.

## Results

On MiniGrid, TRACED at 10k PPO updates achieves a mean solved rate of 0.70±0.04 across 12 held-out mazes, surpassing ACCEL at 20k updates (0.54±0.09) and all other baselines. On PerfectMazeLarge (51×51), TRACED reaches 27%±23% solved rate at 10k, exceeding ACCEL's best 20k rate of 20%±25%. On BipedalWalker, TRACED at 10k outperforms all baselines at 20k across all four aggregate metrics (median, IQM, mean, optimality gap), with a mean return of 89.95±12.95 versus ACCEL's 49.67±11.24 at 10k. TRACED achieves these gains with only ~6% wall-clock overhead over ACCEL at the same update budget, halving total training time relative to ACCEL's 20k runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRACED outperforms all five main baselines (DR, PLR⊥, ADD, ACCEL, CENIE) on both MiniGrid and BipedalWalker. Against the primary baseline ACCEL, TRACED at 10k updates matches or exceeds ACCEL at 20k on MiniGrid (mean 0.72 vs. 0.54) and BipedalWalker (mean return 89.95 vs. 49.67 at 10k), while halving wall-clock training time. On the extreme PerfectMazeLarge benchmark, TRACED (27%±23% at 10k) beats ACCEL's best 20k rate (20%±25%), though on the even harder PerfectMazeXL, ACCEL narrowly leads (12%±28% at 20k vs. TRACED's 10%±14% at 10k). DR and PLR⊥ are consistently the weakest baselines, with PLR⊥ showing particularly poor performance on complex mazes (e.g., 0.0 on Labyrinth, Maze, Maze2 in ablation settings). CENIE (BipedalWalker only) is outperformed by TRACED across all aggregate metrics at 10k updates. SFL is compared only in the multi-agent JaxNav environment (Appendix R) following its original protocol, with TRACED reported to outperform it there as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRACED builds directly on ACCEL's evolutionary regret-based curriculum loop, replacing its sole reliance on Positive Value Loss (PVL) for regret approximation with a two-component score: PVL plus a transition-prediction error (ATPL) derived from a learned recurrent dynamics model. The regret decomposition (Eq. 2) provides the theoretical motivation, showing that PVL captures only the value-estimation component while the future-value gap also depends on dynamics mismatch. The second departure is the introduction of Co-Learnability, a lightweight surrogate for Shapley-value-based cross-task transfer that the authors note has no prior counterpart in the UED literature. CENIE's novelty-based scoring is positioned as complementary rather than competing, and the method remains agnostic to the level generator or editor, preserving ACCEL's evolutionary mutation mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a 6% computational overhead (one extra LSTM forward pass per step) yields a 22% relative gain in zero-shot solved rate and halves the training budget needed to match prior SOTA, which is meaningful for practitioners running UED pipelines. The theoretical bound (Theorem 1) gives a principled justification for the ATPL term, though the optimal α = L_V·C_ℓ is not directly estimable in practice, leaving heuristic tuning of α and β as an acknowledged limitation. Open questions include whether the Co-Learnability surrogate can be replaced by stronger causal estimators, whether ATPL remains informative under highly stochastic transitions, and whether the approach transfers to off-policy or model-based student algorithms (SAC, TD3, DreamerV3) beyond PPO.
