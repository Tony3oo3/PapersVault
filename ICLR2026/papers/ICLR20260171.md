## Problem

Existing asynchronous SGD methods for distributed learning with heterogeneous data distributions either rely on unrealistic similarity assumptions across workers or fail to achieve the theoretical lower bound on time complexity. The only method proven optimal under arbitrary data heterogeneity (Malenia SGD) is synchronous, requiring all workers to wait and discarding in-flight computations. This paper closes the gap by asking whether an asynchronous method can match the optimal time complexity without any data-similarity assumptions.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes n workers each holding a local data distribution D_i (with heterogeneous computation times τ_i ordered so that τ_1 ≤ … ≤ τ_n), a stepsize γ, and an initial point x^0 ∈ R^d. Each worker continuously samples ξ_i ~ D_i and computes stochastic gradients ∇f_i(x; ξ_i), which are sent to a central server. The output is an ε-stationary point x satisfying E[‖∇f(x)‖²] ≤ ε, where f is the average of all local objectives. Notably, the algorithm is parameter-free in its design: it does not require prior knowledge of the gradient variance σ² or the target accuracy ε, unlike the optimal synchronous baseline Malenia SGD.

## Method

Ringleader ASGD operates in rounds of exactly n model updates using a two-phase structure. In Phase 1 (gradient collection), the server buffers stochastic gradients into a per-worker table {(G_i, b_i)} until at least one gradient from every worker has arrived, performing no updates. In Phase 2 (sequential updates), the server performs exactly one update per worker in sequence, each using the averaged gradient from the full table (1/n)Σ G_i/b_i, and broadcasts the new model to the worker whose turn it is. Gradients arriving from workers not yet updated in Phase 2 are stored in a temporary buffer and carried into the next round rather than discarded, ensuring no computation is wasted. This buffering mechanism, combined with the gradient table (inspired by SAG/IAG), bounds all delays at 2n−2 and eliminates the need for data-similarity assumptions, since every update incorporates information from all n workers. The result is the first asynchronous SGD to achieve the optimal time complexity O((LΔ/ε)(τ_n + τ_avg·σ²/(nε))) under arbitrary data heterogeneity and heterogeneous computation times.

## Results

Ringleader ASGD achieves the optimal time complexity O(LΔ/ε · (τ_n + τ_avg · σ²/(nε))) for smooth nonconvex distributed optimization under heterogeneous data, matching the theoretical lower bound of Tyurin & Richtárik (2024). The key improvement over Naive Minibatch SGD is replacing τ_n with τ_avg in the variance-dependent term, which yields significant speedups in high-noise regimes. Empirically, on MNIST and Fashion-MNIST with 100 heterogeneous clients (non-IID Dirichlet partition, α=0.1), Ringleader ASGD converges faster in wall-clock time than both Malenia SGD and IA²SGD, despite sharing the same theoretical time complexity as Malenia SGD, thanks to its n sequential updates per round versus one synchronous update.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IA²SGD (Wang et al., 2025), Ringleader ASGD strictly improves the time complexity: it uses the tighter smoothness constant L (≤ L_max) and, crucially, replaces τ_n with τ_avg in the variance term, reducing the complexity from O(L_max Δ/ε · (τ_n + τ_n σ²/(nε))) to O(L Δ/ε · (τ_n + τ_avg σ²/(nε))). Against Malenia SGD (Tyurin & Richtárik, 2024), Ringleader ASGD matches the optimal time complexity (up to the constant factor L vs. L_f) while adding three practical advantages: it is fully asynchronous (no synchronization barrier), parameter-free (no need to know σ or ε in advance), and discards no ongoing computations. In the empirical evaluation on MNIST and Fashion-MNIST with 100 heterogeneous clients, Ringleader ASGD achieves faster wall-clock convergence than both Malenia SGD and IA²SGD, with the gap over Malenia SGD attributed to its n sequential updates per round enabling more optimization steps within the same time budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

Ringleader ASGD synthesizes three distinct prior contributions into a single algorithm: the gradient-table architecture of IA²SGD (Wang et al., 2025) that eliminates the need for data-similarity assumptions, the delay-control philosophy of Ringmaster ASGD (Maranjyan et al., 2025d) that made asynchrony optimal in the homogeneous-data setting, and the two-phase round structure of Malenia SGD (Tyurin & Richtárik, 2024) that achieves the known lower bound for heterogeneous data. The key departure from all three is the buffering mechanism: rather than discarding stale gradients (Ringmaster) or synchronizing and discarding in-flight work (Malenia), Ringleader ASGD stores fast workers' extra gradients in a temporary table and applies them in the next round, preserving all computation while bounding delays at 2n−2. This makes it the first asynchronous method to match the Tyurin–Richtárik lower bound under arbitrary data heterogeneity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result closes a gap that Tyurin & Richtárik (2024) left open: their optimal heterogeneous-data method (Malenia SGD) was synchronous, and it was previously unknown whether any asynchronous variant could match the lower bound without similarity assumptions. The parameter-free design (no need to know σ or ε in the algorithm) and the extension to arbitrarily time-varying computation speeds (Appendix D) strengthen practical relevance. Limitations include the assumption of instantaneous communication, the requirement that the smoothness-type constant L be within a constant factor of L_f for the optimality claim, and empirical validation restricted to toy MLP experiments on MNIST/Fashion-MNIST. Open questions include how the method interacts with non-negligible communication costs, whether L ≈ L_f holds for realistic deep-network losses, and whether the buffering overhead becomes significant at very large n.
