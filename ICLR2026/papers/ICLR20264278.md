## Problem

When training attention-based regression models with a joint MSE + PCC loss, the Pearson correlation coefficient (PCC) frequently plateaus early in training while MSE continues to decrease — an empirically observed but theoretically unexplained phenomenon. This paper provides the first rigorous analysis of this "PCC plateau," identifying two fundamental limitations: a gradient conflict where MSE optimization (magnitude matching) paradoxically suppresses the PCC gradient (shape matching), and a capacity ceiling imposed by the convex hull constraint of softmax attention aggregation, both of which are exacerbated by high within-sample data homogeneity.

## Input / Output

The method operates on sets of element embeddings (e.g., pathology image patches, consecutive video frames, or tabular feature tokens) within each sample, which are aggregated via attention to produce a single scalar regression prediction per sample. A defining characteristic of the target applications is high within-sample homogeneity — elements within a sample are more similar to each other than to elements in other samples (e.g., nearby tissue regions share cell types, consecutive frames share visual content) — and this is precisely the regime where the PCC plateau is most severe and where ECA provides the largest gains.

## Method

The paper proposes Extrapolative Correlation Attention (ECA), a drop-in replacement for standard softmax attention with three theoretically-motivated components. Scaled Residual Aggregation (SRA) extrapolates beyond the convex hull by scaling the attention-weighted residual with a learnable per-sample factor γ_s ≥ 1, directly breaking the PCC capacity bound of convex aggregation. Dispersion-Aware Temperature Softmax (DATS) adapts the attention temperature inversely to in-sample dispersion, sharpening attention weights when embeddings are homogeneous to prevent near-uniform collapse that would nullify SRA's benefit. Dispersion-Normalized PCC Loss (DNPL) rescales the PCC term by the current prediction standard deviation (with stop-gradient) to counteract the O(1/σ_ŷ) gradient attenuation. The theoretical foundation includes a gradient decomposition showing the PCC/MSE gradient ratio decays at O(1/σ_ŷ^{3/2}) during training, and a PCC gain upper bound for any convex aggregator that depends on the ratio of convex hull radius to baseline prediction variance.

## Results

On the MOSI multimodal sentiment analysis benchmark, ALMT+ECA achieves PCC 0.806, F1 0.859, and MAE 0.695, outperforming the strongest baseline ALMT (PCC 0.783, F1 0.851, MAE 0.721) by +2.3% PCC, +0.8% F1, and −3.6% MAE. On the 10xProteomic spatial transcriptomics dataset, EGN+ECA improves PCC@F by +14.64% (0.602→0.690), PCC@M by +13.81% (0.629→0.716), and reduces MSE by 9.83% (0.056→0.051) over the EGN baseline. On three UCI tabular regression tasks, FT-Transformer+ECA consistently improves PCC (by 0.012–0.042) while reducing MSE across Appliance, Online News, and Superconductivity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MOSI, ALMT+ECA outperforms all 10 baselines on every metric simultaneously: it beats the closest competitor ALMT (PCC 0.783, F1 0.851, MAE 0.721) by +2.3% PCC, +0.8% F1, and −3.6% MAE, and surpasses DLF (PCC 0.781) and MISA (PCC 0.778) by a wider margin. Notably, simply adding a PCC loss to ALMT (ALMT+L_PCC) yields only a marginal PCC gain (0.783→0.791) while degrading F1 (0.851→0.834) and MAE (0.721→0.731), confirming the MSE–PCC optimization conflict identified in the theory. On 10xProteomic, EGN+ECA dominates EGN across all three PCC variants (PCC@F +14.64%, PCC@S +11.89%, PCC@M +13.81%) and also reduces MSE by 9.83%, with the PCC plateau clearly visible in EGN's training curves but absent in EGN+ECA. On the three UCI tabular tasks, FT-Transformer+ECA improves PCC by 0.042 (Appliance), 0.012 (Online News), and 0.010 (Superconductivity) while reducing MSE in all cases, with ablations confirming each ECA component (SRA, DATS, DNPL) contributes to the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new theoretical ground at the intersection of attention-based regression and correlation optimization. While prior work has studied softmax attention expressivity limits in language modeling (Yang et al., 2017; Kanai et al., 2018) and multi-head attention rank bottlenecks (Bhojanapalli et al., 2020), no prior analysis connects the convexity of softmax aggregation to a fundamental PCC improvement ceiling, nor does any prior work derive the gradient conflict between MSE and PCC under joint training. The ECA module repurposes the standard attention readout by adding a learnable extrapolation factor (SRA), a dispersion-adaptive temperature (DATS), and a gradient-rescaled PCC loss (DNPL), positioning itself as a drop-in replacement for the convex pooling step in existing architectures like FT-Transformer, ALMT, and EGN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PCC plateau is a recurring practical failure mode in spatial transcriptomics, multimodal sentiment analysis, and time-series regression where shape matching matters as much as magnitude; this paper provides the first diagnostic framework (gradient ratio decay, convex hull radius) to identify when and why it occurs. The convex hull capacity bound (Theorem 2.2) is a clean, architecture-agnostic result that suggests any model using softmax pooling for regression faces an inherent correlation ceiling under homogeneous inputs, motivating broader reconsideration of readout design. Open questions include whether the analysis extends to non-linear readout heads or multi-task settings, whether SRA extrapolation can hurt generalization on out-of-distribution data, and whether the three-component ECA can be simplified without losing the plateau-breaking effect.
