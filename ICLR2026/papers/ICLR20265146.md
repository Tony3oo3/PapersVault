## Problem

Global textual backpropagation methods like TextGrad suffer two depth-scaling failure modes in deep compound AI systems: exploding textual gradients (feedback messages grow exponentially with depth, exceeding LLM context windows) and vanishing textual gradients (compression of lengthy feedback strips actionable specificity, leaving upstream nodes with generic, unusable guidance). These pathologies make global textual optimization impractical for long-horizon agentic workflows with more than ~10–20 nodes, despite working reasonably well in shallow pipelines.

## Input / Output

TEP consumes a predefined stochastic computation graph (SCG) whose nodes are LLM agents with configurable prompts, along with task inputs sampled from a distribution and task-level objectives available only at the final output (no intermediate ground-truth labels). It produces updated node prompts and system parameters (including per-node critic rubrics and temperature settings) through iterative local optimization. A key constraint is that the critic model defaults to the same LLM as the node model, and all feedback signals are kept within bounded token length and specificity thresholds to prevent the explosion/vanishing pathologies.

## Method

TEP adapts equilibrium propagation from energy-based models to textual SCGs via a two-phase, node-local procedure that eliminates multi-hop backward feedback chains entirely. In the free phase, each node independently runs an LLM critic with a structured two-component rubric (task-independent quality metrics plus task-dependent performance criteria) and iteratively refines its prompt until critic scores stabilize, with temperature sampled uniformly from U(0.3, 0.9) to balance exploration and exploitation. In the nudged phase, a minimal proximal prompt edit with bounded modification intensity is applied at each node, guided by task-level objectives propagated via forward signaling rather than backward gradient chains; the system then re-equilibrates under the nudged configuration. The final update at each node is computed by an LLM-defined operator that jointly combines free-phase and nudged-phase feedback, with validation-based selection retaining only edits that do not reduce performance. This design yields O(1) per-node context complexity independent of graph depth, enabling parallel execution across all nodes and graceful degradation as workflow depth increases.

## Results

TEP achieves the best performance on all four compound AI benchmarks: 62.02% accuracy on PubMedQA, 42.72 MRR on STARK-PRIME, 48.72 F1 on HotpotQA, and 38.97% pass@1 on BigCodeBench. The largest gains appear on complex reasoning tasks, with an 8.1% relative improvement over the next-best method (DSPy) on HotpotQA and a 3.4% absolute gain over TextGrad on BigCodeBench. In depth-scaling experiments, TEP maintains near-constant token complexity and only modest performance degradation (37%→33% update success rate) as workflow depth increases from ×1 to ×5, whereas TextGrad's feedback messages grow exponentially (2K→32K tokens) and its effective update rate collapses from 36% to 5% under summarization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEP outperforms all five baselines across every benchmark. Against CoT (Wei et al., 2022), TEP gains 4.7–26.4 points depending on the task, with the largest margin on HotpotQA (48.72 vs. 33.92 F1). Against HBC (Le et al., 2018), TEP leads by 3.2–27.6 points, with HBC performing particularly poorly on multi-hop reasoning (21.16 F1 on HotpotQA). Against DSPy (Khattab et al., 2024), the strongest non-TEP baseline, TEP improves by 1.32 MRR on STARK-PRIME, 1.76 Acc. on PubMedQA, 3.82 F1 on HotpotQA, and 5.16 pass@1 on BigCodeBench. Against TextGrad (Yuksekgonul et al., 2025), TEP achieves consistent gains of 1.4–24.0 points, with the most dramatic improvement on HotpotQA (48.72 vs. 24.86 F1), where global backpropagation's vanishing gradient severely degrades upstream nodes. Against TextGrad with summarization (Chen et al., 2025), TEP leads by 1.0–24.6 points, confirming that compression-based fixes cannot fully address the depth-scaling failure modes that TEP's local equilibrium approach avoids.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TextGrad (Yuksekgonul et al., 2025), which pioneered automatic differentiation via text for compound AI systems by back-propagating LLM-generated feedback through stochastic computation graphs. It departs from TextGrad's global propagation paradigm by identifying two depth-scaling failure modes (exploding and vanishing textual gradients) and replacing long-range feedback chains with a local, two-phase optimization inspired by Equilibrium Propagation in energy-based models (Scellier & Bengio, 2017). The work also positions itself against summarization-based compression (FedTextGrad, Chen et al., 2025) and locally-trained reward approaches (OPTIMAS, Wu et al., 2025), arguing that neither addresses the fundamental signal degradation at depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As compound AI systems grow deeper (multi-hop QA, multi-agent tool use, long-horizon planning), the inability of global textual backpropagation to scale becomes a practical bottleneck; TEP's local equilibrium approach offers a path to optimizing such systems while preserving black-box LLM components without fine-tuning. The formal depth-scaling analysis (exponential token growth, geometric specificity decay) provides a useful diagnostic framework for practitioners building deep agentic pipelines. Open questions include extending TEP to dynamic SCGs where the graph structure changes at runtime, scaling to very large multi-agent systems, and whether the local critic quality assumption holds when the critic model is weaker than the actor.
