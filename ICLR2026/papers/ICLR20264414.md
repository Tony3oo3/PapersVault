## Problem

Standard flow matching on convex domains via log-barrier mirror maps suffers from two failures: the induced dual distributions are heavy-tailed (violating moment conditions for well-posed ODEs), and Gaussian priors mismatch these heavy-tailed targets, causing velocity fields to blow up. No prior framework provided Wasserstein convergence guarantees for constrained flow matching without assuming bounded support.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264414/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a Student-t prior sample in the dual Euclidean space and a set of linear inequality parameters (aᵢ, bᵢ) defining the convex polytope constraint. It produces samples guaranteed to lie strictly inside the constraint set by construction (via the inverse mirror map), and in the watermarked image task, 64×64 AFHQv2 images that satisfy user-specific linear feasibility checks. Training data is pre-watermarked by projecting clean images (with added noise) onto the polytope before flow-matching training.

## Method

The approach combines two co-designed components: a regularized mirror map Ψ(x) = −(1/(1−κ))Σ(−φᵢ(x))^(1−κ) + ½‖x‖² that is strongly convex (so primal Wasserstein distance is bounded by dual Wasserstein distance) and controls dual tail decay to guarantee finite p-th moments for all p ≥ 1 when κ < β/p; and a Student-t prior whose polynomial tails suppress the spurious mode near x/t in the conditional distribution, keeping the velocity field bounded. Flow matching is trained in the dual Euclidean space with straight-line interpolation (equivalent to geodesic interpolation under the squared Hessian metric in primal space), and the paper establishes the first spatial Lipschitzness and temporal regularity bounds for the velocity field under only polynomial tail assumptions on the target, yielding explicit Wasserstein error rates that decompose into discretization, approximation, and early-stopping terms.

## Results

On the 10-dimensional polytope task, Mirror t-Flow achieves MMD 0.995×10⁻² and KL divergence 1.424, outperforming all baselines while maintaining 100% feasibility. On the 6-dimensional L₂ ball task, Mirror t-Flow achieves MMD 5.329×10⁻² and KL 0.162, again the best among all methods. On the AFHQv2 watermarked image generation task (64×64), Mirror Flow with κ=0.05 achieves FID 4.27 and CMMD 0.023 in 3 hours, substantially outperforming MDM (FID 7.29, CMMD 0.170, 13 hours).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mirror t-Flow outperforms all three baseline families on both synthetic tasks. Against Gauge Flow Matching (Li et al., 2025), the proposed method reduces KL divergence from ~5.0 to 1.42 on the polytope and from ~0.29 to 0.16 on the L₂ ball, while also achieving 100% feasibility (Gauge Vanilla only reaches 95.26%). Against Reflected Flow Matching (Xie et al., 2024), Mirror t-Flow improves MMD from 1.217 to 0.995 and KL from 2.034 to 1.424 on the polytope, and from 5.935 to 5.329 and 0.285 to 0.162 on the L₂ ball. Against Mirror Diffusion Models (Liu et al., 2023a), the advantage is most dramatic on the L₂ ball where MDM's MMD degrades to 36.16 (likely due to heavy-tailed dual distributions from the log-barrier), while Mirror t-Flow maintains 5.33. On the real-data AFHQv2 watermarking task, Mirror Flow achieves FID 4.27 vs. MDM's 7.29 and CMMD 0.023 vs. 0.170, while training 4× faster (3 hours vs. 13 hours).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the flow matching framework (Lipman et al., 2023) to constrained convex domains by combining it with the mirror-map transformation introduced for diffusion models by Liu et al. (2023a), while drawing on the regularized barrier construction from Vural et al. (2022). It departs from prior mirror-based methods (MDM, Gauge Flow Matching, Reflected Flow Matching) in two critical ways: replacing the standard log-barrier with a regularized mirror map that guarantees finite moments in the dual space, and substituting Gaussian priors with Student-t priors to match the heavy-tailed dual distributions. Theoretically, it extends the error-bound literature for flow matching (Bansal et al., 2024; Zhou & Liu, 2025) by establishing spatial Lipschitzness and temporal regularity under polynomial tail assumptions rather than the bounded-support conditions required by prior analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first Wasserstein convergence guarantees for flow matching under polynomial tail assumptions, removing a restrictive bounded-support requirement that limited prior theoretical analyses. The co-design principle—jointly selecting the mirror map and prior to control dual-space tail behavior—offers a practical recipe for constrained generative modeling that prior methods lacked. Open questions include whether the exponential dependence on the Lipschitz constant can be improved, whether the framework extends to non-convex constraint sets, and whether adaptive Student-t degrees of freedom can further improve sample quality. The practical impact is demonstrated on watermarked image generation, where the method achieves competitive FID/CMMD scores with substantially less training time than the MDM baseline.
