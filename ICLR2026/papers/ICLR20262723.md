## Problem

Existing vision-language reasoning systems either perform single-turn grounding without reference propagation across dialogue turns (VisionReasoner) or conduct multi-round interaction without explicit, verifiable reasoning traces (SegLLM). This leaves two critical gaps: (i) reasoning steps do not cite the bounding boxes they rely on, making coordinate hallucinations undetectable and credit assignment ambiguous as context accumulates, and (ii) no learning signal enforces semantic coherence between global scene descriptions and local region evidence, causing drift at deeper turns.

## Input / Output

At each turn the model receives an RGB image, a natural-language query, an optional set of reference bounding boxes serialized as [x1,y1,x2,y2] tokens, and a sliding dialogue memory of prior structured outputs. The output is a four-block structured trajectory — <scene> (global caption), <focus> (caption restricted to the referenced region), <think> (free-form reasoning that must verbatim cite the reference coordinates), and <answer> (JSON containing 2D bounding boxes for detection or sparse 2D points for segmentation) — with constrained decoding enforcing tag schema and JSON validity so that rewards are well-defined on both intermediate reasoning and final predictions.

## Method

RegionReasoner extends VisionReasoner's unified perception-reasoning backbone (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) to a multi-round setting and is optimized end-to-end with GRPO over multi-turn rollouts. Two new reward signals are introduced on top of inherited base rewards: a reference-citation reward that gives +1 when required boxes are correctly cited in <think>, applies a 0.5× penalty for hallucinated coordinates, and a global-local consistency reward computed as a weighted sum of asymmetric keyword overlaps between <scene>/<focus> and <think> plus a lightweight spatial-relation lexicon prior. The policy is trained with clipped GRPO using GAE advantages from a learned value head, a sliding memory window for context budget, and a turn-depth curriculum; constrained decoding guarantees parseable trajectories so that both the reasoning trace and the final geometric prediction receive structured, verifiable training signal.

## Results

On RegionDial-Bench (7-round multi-turn dialogues), RegionReasoner-7B achieves a mean AP50 of 80.7 on RefCOCO+ Multi-turn and 78.2 on RefCOCOg Multi-turn for detection, and a mean gIoU of 69.6 and 66.5 respectively for segmentation. Gains are most pronounced at later turns: on RefCOCO+ detection, the margin over VisionReasoner-7B grows from +1.0 at R1 to +17.7 at R7, indicating substantially slower error accumulation. On the external V* benchmark, RegionReasoner-7B reaches 72.77 overall (68.42 spatial), outperforming Qwen2.5-VL-7B (67.53) and VisionReasoner-7B (69.63) without a dedicated visual-search module.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RegionReasoner-7B outperforms all seven baselines on both detection and segmentation. Against the closest competitor VisionReasoner-7B (its direct architectural predecessor), it gains +5.9 AP on RefCOCO+ detection and +4.6 on RefCOCOg, with the gap widening to +17.7 AP at R7 on RefCOCO+. Over Seg-Zero-7B (segmentation-centric RL), gains are +7.6 AP (RefCOCO+) and +7.1 (RefCOCOg) for detection, and +5.6 gIoU / +6.0 gIoU for segmentation. Against SegLLM-7B (multi-round segmentation without explicit reasoning or RL), RegionReasoner leads by +8.9 gIoU on RefCOCO+ and +9.8 on RefCOCOg. The general-purpose VLMs Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen2-VL-7B trail by 30+ points, confirming that task-specific RL with structured rewards is essential. On the external V* benchmark, RegionReasoner beats GPT-4V (72.77 vs. 54.97 overall) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (72.77 vs. 67.53), but falls slightly short of SEAL (72.77 vs. 75.39), which uses a dedicated visual-search mechanism tightly coupled to LLaVA.

## Relation to Prior Work

RegionReasoner extends VisionReasoner's structured perception-reasoning framework (explicit output tags, format/geometry rewards) from single-turn to multi-round dialogue, while incorporating the multi-round interaction protocol pioneered by SegLLM for referring segmentation. The key departure is the introduction of reference-grounded thinking—requiring the reasoning trace to explicitly cite previously localized bounding boxes—and a global-local consistency reward that aligns scene-level and region-level captions with the reasoning trace, neither of which appears in either prior work. The model is initialized from Qwen2.5-VL-7B and optimized with GRPO, placing it in the broader RL-for-VLMs lineage (Vision-R1, VLM-R1, VL-Rethinker) but distinguishing itself by targeting multi-turn spatial grounding rather than single-pass reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and underexplored failure mode: as multi-turn visual dialogues deepen, localization errors compound and semantic drift between global descriptions and local evidence degrades performance. The reference-citation mechanism makes reasoning verifiable and specifically targets error accumulation (gains grow from +5.6 at R5 to +17.7 at R7 over VisionReasoner), while the consistency reward stabilizes semantics in cluttered scenes. Limitations include the 7B scale, maximum 7-round depth, reliance on RefCOCO-family datasets, and use of sparse points rather than full masks for segmentation. Open directions noted by the authors include richer relation graphs, mask-level grounding, longer dialogues, video, and learnable entailment-based consistency checks.
