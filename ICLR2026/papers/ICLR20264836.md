## Problem

RL post-training of generative music models with coherence-based rewards induces reward hacking: the policy collapses to repetitive, low-diversity chord progressions that score well harmonically but break creative flow in live jamming. Standard KL regularization is insufficient to prevent this diversity collapse in the online accompaniment setting, where the model must adapt in real time without access to future input.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264836/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a live melody stream at sixteenth-note resolution (ON-p / HOLD-p tokens) and produces a chord accompaniment sequence (ON-c / HOLD-c tokens) under a strict online constraint: at each frame t, the chord y_t is generated conditioned only on x_{<t} and y_{<t}, never on x_t or future tokens. Sequences are capped at 256 frames, and for deployment the system uses a fixed 4-beat lookahead with a 4-beat commit horizon to handle network latency at 80 BPM.

## Method

GAPT augments PPO-based RL post-training with a co-evolving Transformer discriminator D_ψ(y) that scores how data-like a policy-generated chord trajectory is, providing an adversarial reward R_adv = −log(1−D_ψ(y)) alongside the coherence and rule-based rewards. A two-phase update schedule stabilizes the nonstationary adversarial signal: Phase 1 warms up the discriminator with fixed-interval updates (one discriminator step per five PPO updates for 200 steps), then Phase 2 switches to confidence-gated updates where the discriminator is frozen unless the moving average of the adversarial reward over the last three PPO steps exceeds a threshold (τ=1.0). The discriminator is trained on chord-only inputs (input-agnostic) with label smoothing (α=0.1) to capture a general prior that transfers to unseen melodies. The policy objective combines the ensemble coherence reward, rule-based penalties, the adversarial realism reward, a KL penalty against an offline full-context anchor, and an entropy bonus, all optimized with PPO.

## Results

On fixed-melody simulation, GAPT achieves the best harmony (0.497 note-in-chord ratio) and diversity (26.645 Vendi Score) on the test set, and strong performance on the out-of-distribution Wikifonia dataset (0.470 harmony, 11.295 diversity). The adversarial reward specifically recovers diversity lost under coherence-only RL: GAPT w/o Adv. Training drops to 20.814 diversity on the test set and 8.034 on Wikifonia, matching ReaLchords' collapse. In the user study with 12 expert musicians, GAPT significantly outperforms ReaLchords on adaptation speed and perceived control/agency (p < 0.05) while achieving the highest mean rating on all three Likert-scale questions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is ReaLchords (Wu et al., 2024), which uses coherence-only RL post-training. GAPT outperforms ReaLchords on diversity by a large margin (26.645 vs. 20.968 Vendi Score on the test set; 11.295 vs. 8.417 on Wikifonia) while matching or slightly exceeding its harmony (0.497 vs. 0.484 on test; 0.470 vs. 0.475 on held-out). The ablation GAPT w/o Adv. Training confirms the adversarial reward is responsible for the diversity gain, as removing it collapses diversity to levels comparable to ReaLchords. Online MLE, the supervised-only baseline, achieves the highest diversity (29.491) but the lowest harmony (0.368), showing that RL is necessary for coherence but insufficient without adversarial regularization. In the user study, GAPT significantly beats ReaLchords on adaptation speed and perceived control/agency (p < 0.05), with qualitative feedback noting ReaLchords 'just keeps giving me the same two chords' while GAPT 'catches my key and chord changes faster.'

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ReaLchords (Wu et al., 2024), which introduced RL post-training with self-supervised coherence rewards for melody-to-chord accompaniment, by diagnosing the resulting diversity collapse as reward hacking and proposing an adversarial mitigation. The adversarial reward formulation is borrowed directly from GAIL (Ho & Ermon, 2016), where a discriminator's realism estimate serves as a policy reward, but here it is repurposed as a regularizer within PPO/GRPO post-training rather than as the sole training signal. The two-phase adaptive discriminator update schedule draws on GAN training stability literature (Arjovsky et al., 2017) to address the nonstationarity problem that arises when the discriminator co-evolves with the policy. The paper positions GAPT as a complement to (and in their setting, a necessary replacement for) the KL-regularization commonly used in RLHF pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reward hacking is a pervasive failure mode across RL post-training of generative models (LLMs, dialogue, music), and this paper demonstrates that a lightweight GAIL-style discriminator is a practical, generalizable mitigation that preserves both task coherence and output diversity. The real-time user study with expert musicians provides rare empirical validation that algorithmic improvements in diversity translate to perceived agency and adaptation speed in live human-AI interaction. Limitations include evaluation on a single narrow task (monophonic melody-to-chord), small model scale (8-layer, 512-dim transformers), and symbolic music only; open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-agent co-adaptive training, polyphonic or audio-level generation, and whether the two-phase schedule generalizes to larger-scale RLHF settings.
