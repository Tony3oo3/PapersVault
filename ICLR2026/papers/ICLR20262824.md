## Problem

Text-to-image models systematically misrepresent regions, reinforcing socio-economic stereotypes and producing visually homogeneous outputs across countries. Existing diversity metrics either depend on narrowly curated reference datasets or capture only low-level visual dissimilarity, making them unable to reveal fine-grained, country-specific biases along interpretable dimensions like affluence, maintenance, or entity appearance. GeoDiv addresses this gap by providing a reference-free, multidimensional framework that quantifies geographical diversity in an interpretable manner.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a set of 250 synthetic images generated per entity-country pair (e.g., 'a photo of a house in Nigeria') at 768×768 (SD2.1) or 1024×1024 (SD3m, SD3.5, FLUX.1), along with the entity and country labels. It outputs four normalized diversity scores (Affluence, Maintenance, Entity-Appearance, Background-Appearance) computed via the Normalized Hill Number, plus mean 1–5 ratings for Affluence and Maintenance per country, and full per-question attribute-value distributions that enable practitioners to identify and explicitly prompt underrepresented attributes.

## Method

GeoDiv decomposes geo-diversity into two complementary axes. The Socio-Economic Visual Index (SEVI) prompts a VLM (Gemini-2.5-flash) to rate each image on 1–5 scales for Affluence (impoverished→luxury) and Maintenance (severely damaged→excellent), then computes diversity over the resulting score distributions. The Visual Diversity Index (VDI) first uses an ensemble of five LLMs (consolidated by an aggregator LLM) to generate entity-specific attribute questions and a fixed set of background questions (split into indoor/outdoor categories); a VQA model then answers these for each image, with a visibility pre-check filtering undetectable attributes and a NOTA option reducing hallucination. Diversity for each question is quantified by the Normalized Hill Number—exp(Shannon entropy) of the answer distribution, scaled to [0,1] by the answer-set size—and Entity/Background scores are obtained by averaging across all related questions. The entire pipeline is validated against human annotations from Prolific crowdworkers, with Gemini-2.5-flash achieving 86% VQA accuracy and Spearman ρ=0.76 on Affluence, while open-source Qwen2.5-VL serves as a practical alternative (ρ=0.83 average correlation with Gemini across all four axes).

## Results

Across four T2I models (SD2.1, SD3m, SD3.5, FLUX.1) evaluated on 160,000 images spanning 10 entities and 16 countries, overall GeoDiv scores decrease in newer models: SD2.1 (0.4251) > SD3m (0.3655) > SD3.5 (0.3455) > FLUX.1 (0.3153). FLUX.1 achieves the highest mean Affluence (3.82/5) and Maintenance (4.73/5) ratings but the lowest Maintenance Diversity-Score (0.15), revealing a polish-over-diversity trade-off. Country-level biases are stark: India, Nigeria, and Colombia receive the lowest mean Affluence (2.31) and Maintenance (3.34), while Japan, UAE, and UK score highest (Affluence 3.53, Maintenance 4.30). Background Diversity-Scores are uniformly low (0.31 average), with 77% of Nigerian car images showing dirt/gravel roads versus 85% paved roads for the US.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoDiv is compared against Vendi-Score (Friedman & Dieng, 2023) and DIMCIM (Teotia et al., 2025) as existing diversity baselines. Against Vendi-Score, GeoDiv demonstrates that only Entity-Appearance Diversity correlates meaningfully (Pearson's ρ=0.56), while Maintenance diversity shows near-zero correlation (ρ=0.06), confirming that Vendi-Score's embedding-based approach cannot capture socio-economic or background dimensions of geo-diversity. Against the real-world GeoDE dataset, synthetic images score substantially lower on Entity-Appearance Diversity (0.44 vs. 0.60) and Maintenance Diversity (synthetic ~0.38 vs. 0.61 for GeoDE), though Background-Appearance gaps are smaller (0.31 vs. 0.42). DIMCIM is discussed in Appendix K as another diversity measurement baseline, but the paper's primary quantitative comparison is with Vendi-Score, where GeoDiv's multidimensional, interpretable scores clearly outperform a single uninterpretable scalar. The framework also validates its VQA pipeline against human annotations, with Gemini-2.5-flash achieving 86% overall accuracy on VDI questions and Spearman ρ=0.76 on Affluence ratings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-VLM evaluation paradigm (TIFA, DSG, GRADE, OASIS) from prompt-consistency and stereotype detection to a multi-axis geo-diversity measurement problem. It departs from prior geo-diversity work (Hall et al., 2023/2024; Askari Hemmat et al., 2024) that relied on curated reference datasets like GeoDE or uninterpretable pairwise-distance metrics (Vendi-Score, FID), by introducing a reference-free, interpretable framework that decomposes diversity into socio-economic (SEVI) and visual (VDI) axes. The diversity computation itself draws on Hill Numbers (Leinster, 2021), repurposing a biodiversity metric for attribute-distribution uniformity in generated images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GeoDiv provides the first systematic, interpretable diagnostic for auditing geographical representation in T2I models, revealing that no current model spans diverse socio-economic strata and that newer models (FLUX.1) trade visual diversity for polish. The framework is modular and extensible, but its reliance on a closed-source VLM (Gemini-2.5-flash) and limited coverage (16 countries, 10 entities) constrain scalability; open questions include whether the observed GDP-diversity negative correlation generalizes, how cultural representation (partially explored) can be rigorously quantified, and whether prompt-based mitigation strategies transfer across entities and regions.
