## Problem

Web agents operate in long-horizon, sequential decision-making settings where outcome-based supervision is sparse and delayed, making it difficult to guide step-level decisions or support inference-time scaling. Existing Process Reward Models for web navigation are limited: scalar WebPRMs collapse progress into coarse, weakly grounded scores, while checklist-based WebPRMs rely on brittle template matching that fails under layout or semantic changes and often mislabels superficially correct actions as successful. The paper addresses the need for a reasoning-first WebPRM that produces interpretable, robust, and progress-aware step-level judgments.

## Input / Output

The model receives a task instruction, the current page observation encoded as an accessibility tree (text-only encoding of interactive elements and attributes), the full action-and-reasoning history of prior steps, and two candidate actions each paired with the agent's reasoning trace for that action. It autoregressively generates a structured justification in a fixed XML-like format (State → Criteria → Analysis → Answer) that first summarizes the current state, induces task-specific evaluation principles with weights, compares the two candidates against those principles, and concludes with a binary preference verdict selecting the action most conducive to task completion. The design is instantiated in pairwise preference but extends naturally to multi-candidate ranking via a Knockout Tournament at inference time.

## Method

WebArbiter is a Transformer-decoder (Qwen2.5/3 backbone, LoRA-fine-tuned) that formulates process reward modeling as text generation rather than scalar scoring or checklist matching. Its key mechanism is principle induction: at each step the model dynamically derives task-specific evaluation criteria (e.g., correctness, progress, safety) from the instruction and current state, then grounds them in the page to assess whether each candidate action advances task completion. Training follows a two-stage pipeline: Stage 1 (reasoning distillation) uses a stronger teacher LLM to synthesize structured, principle-guided justifications, which are then learned via standard negative log-likelihood on 10K samples; Stage 2 (RL with GRPO) optimizes the model against binary verifiable rewards (R ∈ {−1, +1}) based on verdict correctness, with KL regularization against the distilled reference policy to stabilize reasoning style. Ablations show that cold-start RL alone is unstable across environments, narrative reasoning without explicit principles is insufficient for cross-domain generalization, and the two stages are complementary—distillation provides stable reasoning grounding while RL acts as an amplifier that widens the margin between truly progress-making and spurious trajectories.

## Results

On WEBPRMBENCH, WebArbiter-7B achieves 74.60% Avg. BoN Accuracy and 89.19% Avg. Pairwise Accuracy, outperforming the strongest proprietary LLM baseline (GPT-5) by 9.1 points on BoN Acc and the previous SOTA WebPRM (WebShepherd-8B) by 31.3 points. In reward-guided trajectory search on WebArena-Lite, WebArbiter-7B delivers up to +6.4 points in success rate over WebShepherd-8B (47.01% vs. 40.65% with GPT-4o policy; 40.52% vs. 34.34% with GPT-4o-mini policy). The 3B variant also substantially outperforms WebShepherd-3B (59.06% vs. 43.60% Avg. BoN Acc), confirming the pipeline's effectiveness at smaller scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WebArbiter-7B decisively outperforms all proprietary LLM-as-judge baselines: it beats GPT-5 (the strongest) by 9.1 points on Avg. BoN Acc (74.60 vs. 65.50) and by 7.1 points on Avg. Pairwise Acc (89.19 vs. 82.13), with similar margins over GPT-4o, Claude-3.7-Sonnet, Gemini-2.5-Flash, and DeepSeek-R1. Against the previous SOTA WebPRM, WebShepherd-8B, WebArbiter-7B achieves a 31.3-point gain on Avg. BoN Acc (74.60 vs. 43.28) and a 24.9-point gain on Avg. Pairwise Acc (89.19 vs. 64.34), with the largest per-environment gaps on AssistantBench (+40.0 BoN) and WorkArena (+44.7 BoN). Open-source LLM baselines (Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3-70B) trail WebArbiter-7B by 20–30+ points on BoN Acc, confirming that generic instruction-tuned models lack the step-level discrimination needed for web reward modeling. In the downstream trajectory search on WebArena-Lite, WebArbiter-7B surpasses WebShepherd-8B by +6.2 points (GPT-4o-mini policy) and +6.4 points (GPT-4o policy) in average success rate, with the largest gains in Shopping (+11.7 / +14.0) and Reddit (+13.0 / +5.0), while the gap narrows in the more template-driven CMS domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

WebArbiter extends the reasoning reward model paradigm (Guo et al., 2025b), which casts judging as explicit chain-of-thought generation followed by a verdict, to the web-agent setting. It departs from the two existing WebPRM families—scalar scoring (Miao et al., 2025) and checklist-driven generative feedback (Chae et al., 2025)—by replacing fixed templates with dynamically induced, task-specific principles grounded in the current state. The training pipeline combines knowledge distillation of principle-guided justifications from a teacher LLM with GRPO-based RL (Shao et al., 2024), building on the WEBPRM Collection dataset from WebShepherd (Chae et al., 2025) for both training data and the Best-of-N Knockout Tournament inference protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WEBPRMBENCH is the first multi-environment benchmark dedicated to WebPRM evaluation, filling a gap where prior work (WebRewardBench) covered only Mind2Web and limited WebArena data; it will likely become the standard reference for comparing step-level reward models. The ablation finding that RL alone is unstable across environments while explicit principle induction is the critical component for cross-domain generalization (with RL acting as an amplifier) offers a useful training recipe for practitioners. A key open limitation is that text-only accessibility-tree observations prevent the model from verifying element-level grounding or interaction effects, as evidenced by the safe-action bias and element-reference hallucination failure modes; integrating visual observations or environment feedback remains an important direction.
