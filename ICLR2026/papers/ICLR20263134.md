## Problem

There is no automated framework to select the best pretrained vision encoder and LLM combination for a given downstream task in vision-language models. Existing model selection methods (EMMS, LogME, LEEP, NLEEP) rely on zero-shot transferability metrics that fail for VLMs because vision-text alignment is required before the LLM can interpret image embeddings, and exhaustive grid search is infeasible given that training a single 7B VLM candidate exceeds 100 GPU hours and over 150,000 LLMs are available on HuggingFace.

## Input / Output

Mordal consumes a user-specified zoo of pretrained vision encoders and LLMs (e.g., 7×7 = 49 candidates), an alignment dataset (LLaVA-1.5-Instruction by default), and a target task dataset used both for CKA-based clustering and for evaluating alignment performance. The target task data is fed through each vision encoder to compute representation activations for clustering, while the alignment data is partially trained on during evaluation. The output is the single best-performing VLM candidate (a specific vision encoder + LLM pair) along with a full ranking of all candidates by predicted alignment performance, measured via weighted Kendall's τ against ground-truth grid search.

## Method

Mordal reduces search cost along two axes: shrinking the candidate set and shortening per-candidate evaluation. For candidate reduction, it performs two-step CKA-based clustering: first clustering vision encoders by representation similarity on target task data, then clustering LLMs using fixed image embeddings from each vision encoder cluster's medoid, with the Cartesian product yielding candidate clusters. For evaluation speedup, it uses a two-stage protocol: inter-cluster evaluation applies the Successive Halving Algorithm (SHA) to early-stop poorly performing cluster representatives, and intra-cluster evaluation applies an observational scaling law that fits a log-linear regression between log(data sample ratio) and log(task error) from a few partial training runs, then extrapolates to predict full-data performance without completing training. Intermediate checkpoints from larger sample ratios are reused at smaller ratios to avoid redundant computation, and LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning keeps per-candidate training tractable.

## Results

Mordal identifies the best VLM candidate (vision encoder + LLM pair) for a given downstream task using 8.9×–11.6× fewer GPU hours than exhaustive grid search (5439 GPU hours for 49 candidates), while successfully selecting the top-1 model in 6 out of 7 evaluation tasks. In terms of ranking quality, Mordal achieves a mean weighted Kendall's τ of 0.870 across seven datasets, compared to 0.515 for the strongest baseline (EMMS), representing approximately 69% higher ranking accuracy. The largest individual gains appear on ScienceQA (τ = 0.960 vs. 0.770 for EMMS) and DocVQA (τ = 0.897 vs. 0.172 for EMMS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mordal outperforms all four transferability-based baselines (EMMS, LogME, LEEP, NLEEP) on every one of the seven evaluation datasets in weighted Kendall's τ. EMMS is the strongest baseline, reaching τ = 0.770 on ScienceQA and 0.682 on GQA, but Mordal still leads by 0.19 and 0.13 respectively; on Doc QA tasks the gap widens dramatically (e.g., 0.897 vs. 0.172 on DocVQA). LogME and LEEP perform poorly, with LogME producing negative τ on GQA (−0.162) and ChartQA (−0.144), indicating rankings worse than random. NLEEP is the second-strongest baseline (mean τ ≈ 0.414), competitive on AI2D (0.614) but still trailing Mordal by 0.28. The paper attributes Mordal's advantage to its alignment-aware evaluation: baselines rely on zero-shot transferability metrics that are unreliable for VLMs requiring vision-text alignment, whereas Mordal actually trains the feature projector and uses CKA-based clustering plus scaling prediction to estimate full-data performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

Mordal extends the pretrained model selection literature (EMMS, LogME, LEEP, NLEEP) from classification/regression/language-only settings to the VLM context, where vision-text alignment via a feature projector makes zero-shot transferability metrics unreliable. It borrows CKA-based representation similarity (Kornblith et al.) for candidate clustering, the Successive Halving Algorithm for early stopping, and observational scaling laws (Lin et al., Ruan et al.) for performance prediction, but recombines them into a two-stage inter-/intra-cluster search pipeline specifically designed for alignment-required VLMs. The work also builds on the LLaVA-style VLM architecture (frozen vision encoder + trainable projector + LoRA-tuned LLM) as the target of selection, and is implemented on the Cornstarch distributed training framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With over 150,000 LLMs and dozens of vision encoders available on HuggingFace, the inability to systematically select the best pretrained combination for a given downstream task is a practical bottleneck for VLM deployment in healthcare, robotics, and accessibility. Mordal's 8.9×–11.6× speedup over grid search makes automated selection feasible within realistic compute budgets, and the finding that no single VLM dominates across tasks (no silver bullet) underscores the need for task-aware selection. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger models (>7B), alternative projector architectures beyond MLP, end-to-end trained VLMs without pretrained components, and automated hyperparameter tuning to reduce sensitivity to clustering thresholds and exploration parameters.
