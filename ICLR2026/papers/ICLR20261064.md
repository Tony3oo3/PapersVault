## Problem

While reinforcement learning has been widely adopted for MLLM post-training, its application to multimodal reward modeling remains largely unexplored. Directly applying standard RL algorithms (PPO, Reinforce++) to reward modeling causes training instability or collapse due to numerical overflow in the policy ratio, extreme advantage values from binary reward labels, and a fundamental inconsistency between the model's reasoning process and its final judgment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261064/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a question paired with an image and two candidate responses, and must produce a structured output consisting of a chain-of-thought comparative analysis enclosed in think tags followed by a single binary choice (1 or 2) in answer tags. Ties are explicitly disallowed, forcing a definitive preference. The strict output format is enforced through a dedicated formatting reward component, and the reasoning must be logically consistent with the final choice, verified by an external MLLM judge during training.

## Method

StableReinforce refines the PPO/Reinforce++ objective in three ways: (1) Pre-Clip clamps the log-probability ratio to [log(10⁻³), log(10³)] before exponentiation, preventing numerical overflow when the current and reference policies diverge; (2) an Advantage Filter applies a 3-sigma rule to zero out standardized advantages exceeding ±3, eliminating extreme values caused by the near-binary reward distribution; (3) a Consistency Reward, gated by correctness, uses Qwen2.5-VL-7B as a referee to verify that the reasoning conclusion matches the final answer, with the final reward computed as Result × (1 + 0.5 × Consistency) + 0.5 × Formatting. Training follows a two-phase progressive-difficulty strategy: a GPT-4o-generated chain-of-thought SFT cold-start on 200K preference pairs (R1-Reward-200K), followed by RL on the harder subset where GPT-4o required ≥2 sampling attempts or failed entirely, using Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct as the base model.

## Results

On the VL Reward Bench, R1-Reward (7B) achieves 71.92% Overall Accuracy and 71.44% Macro Accuracy, surpassing the previous SOTA reward model IXC-2.5-Reward (65.80%) by 6.1 points and all proprietary models (best: Gemini-1.5-Pro at 67.20%). On the Multimodal Reward Bench, R1-Reward reaches 82.2% Overall, a 14.3% improvement over the prior SOTA (MM-RLHF-Reward at 67.1%). With majority voting over 15 samples (Voting@15), performance further improves to 76.46% on VL Reward Bench and 83.3% on Multimodal Reward Bench, demonstrating strong test-time scaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R1-Reward outperforms the previous SOTA reward model IXC-2.5-Reward by 6.1 points on VL Reward Bench Overall Acc (71.92% vs. 65.80%) and by 15.6 points on Multimodal Reward Bench Overall (82.2% vs. 66.6%), while using only 200K training samples versus IXC-2.5-Reward's 1M+. Against MM-RLHF-Reward, R1-Reward gains 21.8 points on VL Reward Bench (71.92% vs. 50.15%) and 15.1 points on Multimodal Reward Bench (82.2% vs. 67.1%). R1-Reward also surpasses all proprietary models on VL Reward Bench, exceeding Gemini-1.5-Pro (67.20%), GPT-4o (65.80%), and Claude-3.7-Sonnet (66.31%) in Overall Accuracy. On the MM-RLHF Reward Bench, R1-Reward achieves 80.59% Acc (vs. MM-RLHF-Reward's 82.00%) and 54.35% Acc+ (vs. 63.00%), showing a modest gap on this in-distribution benchmark, but Voting@15 closes the gap to 86.47% Acc and 67.39% Acc+. Downstream validation confirms R1-Reward's utility: training Qwen-2.5-VL-3B with R1-Reward scoring improves Hallucination Bench aAcc from 63.19 to 65.29 and WildVision from 48.4 to 52.2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the rule-based RL paradigm from DeepSeek-R1 (which incentivizes reasoning in LLMs) into the multimodal reward modeling setting, where the model must compare two responses and output a preference judgment. It builds directly on Reinforce++/PPO as the training algorithm but identifies and fixes two specific instabilities—log-probability ratio overflow under negative advantages and advantage normalization blowup under the binary (1/0) reward distribution inherent to preference tasks—that cause training collapse in this domain. The consistency reward, which uses a separate MLLM as a referee to verify reasoning-answer alignment, departs from the standard format+result reward structure of DeepSeek-R1 by adding a third, multiplicative reward component gated on correctness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to demonstrate that RL with chain-of-thought reasoning can substantially improve multimodal reward models, achieving SOTA on three benchmarks with only 200K training samples (vs. 1M+ for prior SOTA) and showing strong test-time scaling via majority voting. The identified stability failures of PPO/Reinforce++ under binary-reward settings are generalizable to other rule-based RL tasks with sparse labels, making the pre-clip and 3-sigma advantage filter broadly useful. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models or more complex reward formulations, whether more sophisticated test-time aggregation (beyond majority voting) can close the remaining gap on MM-RLHF Bench, and whether the consistency reward generalizes to multi-class or continuous reward settings.
