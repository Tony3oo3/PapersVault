## Problem

Existing sampling strategies for diffusion-based LLMs (dLLMs), such as confidence-based selection (Fast-dLLM) and semi-autoregressive block decoding, operate with a static, constant sampling speed throughout generation, causing suboptimal efficiency and significant accuracy drops under high-parallel decoding. No prior method dynamically adapts both the number of tokens sampled per step and their positions in the sequence based on the evolving state of the diffusion process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260156/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the iterative denoising loop of dLLMs (LLaDA 8B, Dream 7B), consuming a text prompt and a fully masked token sequence of length L (typically 256 tokens) as input, and producing a fully unmasked generated text sequence after N diffusion steps. At each step the bidirectional transformer predicts all masked tokens simultaneously, and the sampling strategy decides which predictions to accept (unmask) versus which to retain as [MASK] for the next step, making the per-step acceptance pattern the critical control variable.

## Method

SlowFast Sampling is a two-stage dynamic strategy governed by three golden principles: Certainty (high-confidence tokens are more likely correct), Convergence (token confidence stabilizes over iterations), and Positional (high-confidence tokens cluster in contiguous regions). In the Exploratory Stage (Slow), the model cautiously unmasks only top-k_slow high-confidence tokens per step while tracking a convergence endpoint — the furthest position exceeding a minimum confidence threshold τ_min_conf — and monitors the variance of these endpoints over a sliding window of W_hist predictions. Once the variance drops below a stability threshold σ_stable², the Accelerated Decoding Stage (Fast) begins, parallelly unmasking all tokens within the identified stable span [s_cycle, e_cycle] that exceed a high confidence threshold τ_high_conf, while caching predictions for out-of-span positions to avoid redundant computation. This cyclical slow-fast process advances s_cycle to e_cycle each cycle until the full sequence is generated, and integrates with dLLM-Cache for additional speedup, achieving up to 15.63× and 34.22× acceleration on GPQA respectively.

## Results

On the GPQA benchmark (8-shot, 1024 tokens), SlowFast Sampling achieves a 15.63× speedup on LLaDA 8B (1.60 → 25.00 TPS) with essentially zero accuracy drop (31.47 → 31.47), and up to 34.22× (54.75 TPS) when combined with dLLM-Cache. Across 8 benchmarks under standard settings (256 tokens), SlowFast Sampling yields speedups ranging from 2.19× (MATH) to 5.24× (BBH) over vanilla diffusion decoding, while maintaining or slightly improving task scores. With dLLM-Cache integrated, throughput reaches up to 10.13× on BBH and 9.13× on GPQA. Notably, LLaDA + SlowFast + Cache (54.75 TPS) surpasses the autoregressive LLaMA3 8B (33.79 TPS) in inference speed on GPQA while retaining comparable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Fast-dLLM (Wu et al., 2025), which performs parallel decoding based solely on token confidence thresholds, SlowFast Sampling consistently achieves higher speedups across all 8 benchmarks — e.g., 4.94× vs. 3.54× on GPQA, 5.24× vs. 2.66× on BBH, and 3.20× vs. 1.64× on GSM8K — while maintaining comparable or slightly better accuracy. The advantage is most pronounced on reasoning-heavy tasks (BBH, GPQA) where the dynamic two-stage exploration/acceleration strategy identifies stable regions more effectively than static confidence-based selection. Against the autoregressive LLaMA3 8B (Dubey et al., 2024), LLaDA equipped with SlowFast Sampling + dLLM-Cache (Kp=500, Kr=30) achieves 54.75 TPS versus 33.79 TPS for LLaMA3 8B on GPQA (8-shot, 1024 tokens), a 62% throughput advantage, though at a modest accuracy cost (28.79 vs. 31.92). With the more conservative cache setting (Kp=100, Kr=5), the accuracy gap narrows to 30.13 vs. 31.92 while still delivering 48.80 TPS. Overall, SlowFast Sampling wins on speed across all comparisons and matches or slightly exceeds baseline accuracy on most benchmarks, with the largest accuracy gains observed on MMLU (+4.45) and MMLU-pro (+0.55).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on accelerating diffusion-based LLMs (dLLMs) by moving beyond static sampling strategies—confidence-based selection (Fast-dLLM) and semi-autoregressive block decoding—toward a dynamic, two-stage approach. It builds directly on dLLM-Cache for feature reuse and is motivated by empirical observations (the Three Golden Principles) about how token confidence converges and clusters spatially during iterative denoising. The key conceptual departure is treating the sampling schedule as an adaptive, state-dependent process rather than a fixed per-step rule, which prior methods like low-confidence remasking and random remasking do not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for dLLM deployment: achieving 15–34× speedups with minimal accuracy loss on GPQA, and surpassing autoregressive LLaMA3 8B in raw throughput (54.75 vs. 33.79 TPS), suggests that dLLMs can become practically competitive if sampling is well-designed. The Three Golden Principles offer a reusable conceptual framework for understanding and optimizing dLLM decoding beyond this specific method. Open questions include whether the speedups generalize to much longer sequences (beyond 1024 tokens), to other dLLM architectures beyond LLaDA/Dream, and whether the accuracy trade-off at higher cache aggressiveness (e.g., Kp=500, Kr=30 yielding −3.13 accuracy) is acceptable for downstream applications.
