## Problem

While gradient descent is widely used for rank-one matrix denoising in high dimensions, the analytical learning dynamics—specifically the exact trajectories of the inner products between learned and ground-truth singular vectors—have been largely uncharacterized for the rectangular (Wishart) model. Prior work by Bodin & Macris (2021) obtained closed-form dynamics only for the symmetric Wigner case, leaving the more practically relevant rectangular setting with general structured signal and noise unresolved.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy observation matrix X = Z + λu*ᵀv* ∈ ℝ^{p×n} (with p/n → c > 0), initial unit vectors (u₀, v₀), and SNR parameter λ, and produces deterministic closed-form approximations for the time-dependent inner products q_u(t) = ⟨u*, u_t⟩ and q_v(t) = ⟨v*, v_t⟩, as well as the loss gap trajectory. These approximations are expressed as integrals against the Marcenko-Pastur measure and its co-measure, combined with convolutions of hyperbolic basis functions (cosh, sinh), and can be evaluated numerically in O(N_t² N_x) time via grid-based quadrature over the bounded MP support.

## Method

The core technique constructs a coupled system of differential equations for Stieltjes-transform (resolvent) characteristic functions associated with the Riemannian gradient flow, then proves existence and uniqueness of solutions within a family defined by good transition kernels via Picard iteration and Grönwall's lemma. In the high-dimensional limit, large random matrix theory (resolvent convergence to the MP Stieltjes transform) replaces all random quantities with deterministic limits, yielding a closed-form system solvable by Laplace transforms and contour integration. The resulting deterministic approximations for q_u(t) and q_v(t) are shown to converge almost surely to the true random dynamics uniformly on compact time intervals. In the large-time limit, the asymptotic values exhibit a signed BBP phase transition at the critical SNR λ = c^{1/4}, with the sign determined by the initial alignment α_v/√(1+λ²) + α_u/√(λ²+c), revealing that estimation becomes infeasible when this quantity vanishes.

## Results

The paper derives closed-form deterministic approximations for the gradient flow learning dynamics (inner products q_u(t), q_v(t)) of rank-one matrix denoising, proving almost sure convergence as p,n→∞. The key quantitative finding is a phase transition at the critical SNR threshold λ = c^(1/4): below this threshold, the inner products converge to 0 (estimation fails), while above it, they converge to non-zero values matching the BBP phase transition (e.g., for c=0.5, the threshold is λ≈0.841). Validation experiments with p=900, n=1200 confirm that the deterministic approximations closely track empirical GD trajectories (learning rate η=0.005), with accuracy improving as SNR increases. The loss function gap also admits a closed-form approximation, and the paper shows that with random initialization, GD reaches the global optimum (loss→0) when λ < c^(1/4), avoiding saddle points on the spherical manifold.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without direct method-to-method benchmarking; the 'baselines' are the empirical GD trajectories and the classical BBP phase transition. The deterministic approximations from Theorem 1 are validated against actual GD runs (Figures 1a–1f), showing tight agreement between theory and practice for both sub-critical (λ=0.3) and super-critical (λ=1.5) SNR regimes. The asymptotic limits in Theorem 2 are compared with the BBP phase transition (Baik et al., 2005): the squared inner products |⟨û₁,u*⟩|² and |⟨v̂₁,v*⟩|² from BBP match the squared limits of q_u^∞ and q_v^∞, but the paper's signed result additionally reveals that the sign depends on the initial conditions through Sgn{α_v/√(1+λ²) + α_u/√(λ²+c)}, a nuance absent from the unsigned BBP prediction. Methodologically, the paper extends the framework of Bodin & Macris (2021), who obtained closed-form dynamics only for the symmetric Wigner model, to the more challenging rectangular Wishart model with general structured noise, requiring higher-order poles in the Laplace domain and new almost-sure convergence results for bilinear resolvent forms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the analytical framework of Bodin & Macris (2021), who derived closed-form learning dynamics for rank-one matrix denoising under the symmetric deformed Wigner model, to the more general and practically relevant rectangular deformed Wishart model with asymmetric noise. The key technical departure is the use of large random matrix theory tools (Marcenko-Pastur measures, resolvent convergence, Stieltjes transforms) to handle the structural asymmetry and higher-order differential equations inherent in the rectangular setting. The paper also establishes existence and uniqueness of solutions to the governing system via a transition-kernel integral representation, a step absent from the prior Wigner-model analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-form deterministic approximations enable practical applications such as data-driven early stopping and SNR estimation without requiring full SVD, which is computationally prohibitive in high dimensions. The identification of 'bad initial points' where the gradient flow converges slowly or fails to learn—despite high SNR—provides actionable guidance for initialization strategies. Open questions include the central limit theorem for fluctuations around the deterministic limit, extension to multi-rank and nonlinear models, and generalization to stochastic gradient descent, all of which would deepen understanding of learning dynamics in high-dimensional inference.
