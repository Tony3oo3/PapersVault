## Problem

Training LLM agents with reinforcement learning in interactive environments is hampered by sparse, delayed, and sometimes unverifiable rewards, making credit assignment to intermediate actions extremely difficult. Existing process-supervision approaches either require costly and biased handcrafted step labels, produce overly fine-grained token-level rewards that destabilize multi-turn training, or rely on exact state overlaps that are rare in open-ended language environments. The paper asks how to design a credit-assignment strategy that is label-efficient, stable across long horizons, and generalizable to both verifiable and unverifiable reward settings.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a task prompt and sequential environment observations (text for WebShop and SOTOPIA, RGB grid images for VisualSokoban) and produces a chain-of-thought followed by a textual action at each step. The implicit PRM, initialized from the same base model as the policy, outputs a scalar implicit step reward per action (not per token), computed as the log-ratio of the PRM's action probability to the previous policy snapshot's. The final trained artifact is the policy model, which is compatible with standard RL algorithms (GRPO, RLOO, REINFORCE++, DAPO) and requires no additional rollouts or annotated step labels beyond trajectory-level outcome rewards.

## Method

iStar alternately optimizes an implicit process reward model (PRM) and the policy model in a self-reinforcing loop. The implicit step reward for action a_t is defined as β·log(π_φ(a_t|o_{1:t},x) / π_θ_old(a_t|o_{1:t},x)), measuring how much the freshly updated PRM prefers the action over the previous policy snapshot. The PRM is trained online via a multi-turn DPO objective on positive-negative trajectory pairs (ranked by an outcome reward verifier), which the paper proves is equivalent to a Bradley-Terry model with a step-wise reward function. During policy updates, episode-level advantages from outcome rewards are combined with step-level advantages from the implicit step rewards (A = A^E + α·A^S), providing dense per-action credit while gating it by final task success. This step-level granularity—rather than token-level—keeps variance manageable in long multi-turn rollouts, and the absence of a KL penalty allows the policy to explore action regions critical for task completion.

## Results

On WebShop, iStar (RLOO w/ iStar) achieves 86.5% success rate and 93.6% score, surpassing the strongest agentic RL baseline GiGPO (84.1% / 91.2%) and the single-turn RL method PRIME (81.5% / 91.3%). On VisualSokoban, iStar reaches 91.7% success, a 5.8-point gain over GiGPO (85.9%) and 6.1 points over vanilla GRPO (85.6%). In SOTOPIA (open-ended social interaction with unverifiable rewards), iStar improves goal completion on hard scenarios by 14% in self-chat (7.92→8.06) and by 48% when interacting with GPT-4o (6.68→7.16) over vanilla GRPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

iStar consistently outperforms all vanilla RL baselines (PPO, GRPO, RLOO, REINFORCE++) by 4–9 points in success rate on WebShop and 5–10 points on VisualSokoban, confirming that implicit step rewards provide meaningful credit-assignment gains beyond outcome-only training. Against the closest agentic RL competitor GiGPO (Feng et al., 2025), which computes step-level advantages via same-state grouping, iStar wins by 2.4 points on WebShop success and 5.8 points on VisualSokoban success, attributed to finer-grained per-action credit rather than relying on rare state overlaps. The single-turn RL method PRIME (Cui et al., 2025), which uses token-level implicit process rewards, trails iStar by 5.0 points on WebShop success and is inapplicable to VisualSokoban (non-binary rewards) and SOTOPIA (open-ended states). On SOTOPIA, where GiGPO and PRIME are entirely inapplicable, iStar (GRPO w/ iStar) still surpasses all vanilla RL baselines and frontier LLMs (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro, DeepSeek-R1, Claude-Sonnet-4-Thinking) by 0.8–1.5 points on hard-scenario goal completion, demonstrating robustness to unverifiable rewards. The method also shows ~2× sample efficiency over vanilla RLOO on WebShop, reaching the baseline's final score in roughly half the training steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

iStar extends the implicit process reward model (PRM) paradigm from single-turn, token-level settings (Yuan et al., 2025; Cui et al., 2025) to multi-turn, step-level agentic RL, replacing the frozen reference model in DPO with a moving policy snapshot and deriving the objective from a multi-step MDP rather than a one-step bandit. It departs from prior agentic RL credit-assignment approaches—handcrafted step labels, generative reward models, and same-state grouping (GiGPO)—by requiring only trajectory-level outcome preferences, making it applicable to open-ended environments with unverifiable rewards. The theoretical grounding draws on the DPO-as-Q-function result (Rafailov et al., 2024a) to show the multi-turn DPO objective yields a step-wise reward function under a Bradley-Terry model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Credit assignment remains a central bottleneck for training LLM agents in long-horizon interactive environments, and iStar offers a plug-and-play strategy compatible with GRPO, RLOO, REINFORCE++, and DAPO that requires no additional rollouts or step annotations. The demonstrated generalizability to unverifiable rewards (SOTOPIA) where prior methods like GiGPO and PRIME are inapplicable broadens the scope of RL-based agent training to open-ended social and dialogic tasks. Key limitations include the separate PRM and policy models (doubled memory/compute), the single-objective PRM in SOTOPIA, and the reliance on a moving reference that may drift; future work on unified models, multi-objective PRMs, and application to math/code reasoning could further strengthen the approach.
