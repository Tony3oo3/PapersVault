## Problem

Existing fMRI-to-video decoding methods are subject-specific, requiring over 12 hours of per-subject fMRI data and extensive retraining before they can be applied to a new patient, making them impractical for clinical settings such as large-scale screening or rehabilitation. This paper is the first to formulate fMRI-to-video reconstruction in a truly subject-agnostic setting, where the model must generalize to previously unseen subjects without any additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263220/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes preprocessed fMRI voxel sequences (256×256 single-channel images in 32k fs LR surface space, restricted to 8,405 HCP-MMP atlas vertices across early visual, ventral, and dorsal ROIs, with a 6-second hemodynamic shift applied) and produces a reconstructed video via an AnimateDiff T2V diffusion model conditioned on a control image, a blurry video, and a text caption. Inference on a new subject requires no retraining and completes in under 10 seconds per video on a single RTX 4090 GPU.

## Method

VCFLOW comprises three modules: (1) a Hierarchical Cognitive Alignment Module (HCAM) that partitions fMRI signals into early-visual, ventral-stream, and dorsal-stream ROIs, extracts features via a ViT backbone and linear projections, and aligns each stream to a different level of the CLIP hierarchy (early ViT layers for low-level features, final vision embeddings for semantics, video embeddings for motion) using a BiMixCo contrastive loss; (2) a Subject-Agnostic Redistribution Adapter (SARA) that expands the token sequence and uses a redistribution layer to disentangle subject-invariant semantic tokens from subject-specific tokens, trained jointly with BiMixCo alignment, inter-subject bidirectional InfoNCE contrastive loss, and a subject-identity classifier; and (3) a Hierarchical Explicit Decoder (HED) that attaches cognitively grounded auxiliary tasks to each stream—caption generation and object classification for ventral, key-object segmentation for early visual, and blurry-video VAE reconstruction for dorsal—before feeding the fused representations into a diffusion prior (DALL-E 2-style) and a T2V diffusion model for final video synthesis.

## Results

On the cc2017 fMRI-to-video benchmark (subject-agnostic setting, trained on subjects 2&3, tested on subject 1), VCFLOW achieves 14.0% 50-way frame semantic accuracy, 0.396 SSIM, 10.478 PSNR, 18.2% 50-way video semantic accuracy, and 0.940 CLIP-pcc (spatiotemporal coherence) on average across three subjects. Compared to the subject-specific NEURONS baseline (21.0% 50-way frame, 26.1% 50-way video), VCFLOW incurs only a ~7% average drop while requiring zero per-subject retraining and completing inference in under 10 seconds per video on a single RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VCFLOW outperforms both subject-agnostic baselines across nearly all metrics. Against NEURONS* (subject-agnostic adaptation of the subject-specific model), VCFLOW gains +38.6% on 50-way frame semantic accuracy (14.0% vs. 10.1%), +13.0% on 50-way video semantic accuracy (18.2% vs. 16.1%), and +9.0% on PSNR (10.478 vs. 9.612). Against GLFA* (which pretrains on all subjects except the test subject), VCFLOW achieves +45.8% on 50-way frame accuracy and +189.1% on SSIM (0.396 vs. 0.137), indicating substantially better pixel-level reconstruction. Even against GLFA with full subject-adaptive pretraining (all subjects included), VCFLOW still leads on 50-way frame accuracy (+20.7%) and SSIM (+128.9%). The only setting where VCFLOW trails is direct comparison with the fully subject-specific NEURONS (21.0% vs. 14.0% on 50-way frame), a ~7% average gap the authors attribute to the inherent cost of forgoing per-subject training, while VCFLOW simultaneously surpasses NEURONS on SSIM (+8.5%), PSNR (+10.0%), and CLIP-pcc (+0.6%).

## Relation to Prior Work

VCFLOW extends the fMRI-to-video reconstruction line (MinD-Video, NeuroClips, NEURONS) by reformulating it in a subject-agnostic setting, drawing on the ventral-dorsal dual-stream hypothesis to hierarchically decompose brain features and align them with multi-level CLIP embeddings. It builds directly on NEURONS' explicit auxiliary task framework and inference pipeline, while departing from GLFA's data-level functional alignment (which requires pretraining on all subjects) in favor of a feature-level contrastive disentanglement strategy inspired by ViT register tokens. The contrastive objectives (BiMixCo, InfoNCE) are repurposed from representation learning literature to enforce cross-subject semantic invariance at the feature level rather than the data level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first subject-agnostic fMRI-to-video framework, offering a clinically scalable path (10-second inference, no per-patient retraining) that could enable large-scale screening and rehabilitation applications currently blocked by 12+ hour subject-specific training. However, evaluation is limited to three subjects on a single dataset (cc2017), and failure cases with rare or semantically complex stimuli reveal that the approach still struggles with long-tail content. Open questions include whether the dual-stream decomposition generalizes beyond the visual cortex, whether performance scales with more training subjects, and whether the 7% gap to subject-specific methods can be closed without sacrificing the zero-retraining guarantee.
