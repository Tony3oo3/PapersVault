## Problem

Equivariant neural networks lose expressivity on symmetric inputs because the output becomes invariant to transformations beyond the input's own symmetries (symmetry increase). Prior work documented this phenomenon in isolated cases—collapse-to-zero for full degeneration, empirical observations for specific feature spaces—but lacked a unified mathematical characterization of all degeneration types and a principled, computable framework for predicting and preventing harmful symmetry increases within the equivariant framework.

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework takes a feature space (a G-representation, e.g., a direct sum of spherical harmonic irreps V_{l=l0}^{⊕r}) and an input symmetry group H (e.g., the dihedral group D_{kh} of a k-fold structure) and produces the symmetry infimum I_G(V, H), the unique minimal orbit type the feature space can support, along with a degeneration classification (full, axial, half, or none). In the applied setting, centered point clouds or molecular geometric graphs are encoded by TFN or HEGNN architectures, and the resulting equivariant embeddings are used for graph discrimination or molecular isotropic polarizability prediction on QM9.

## Method

The paper proves that for any compact Lie group representation and closed subgroup H, a unique minimal orbit type exists in the fixed-point subspace V^H (Thm 3.1), defining the symmetry infimum. It then shows that Michel's Criterion (a dimension-decrease condition on fixed-point subspaces of adjacent supergroups) is both necessary and sufficient for orbit type identification in high-multiplicity representations (Prop 4.2), yielding a two-step algorithm: an orbit-type test (Algo 1) checking adjacent supergroups, and a symmetry-infimum search (Algo 2) finding the minimal orbit type among all supergroups. Under a manifold hypothesis for data and C^∞-density of TFN parameterizations (Thm 5.1), the paper proves that almost isovariant maps are generic and full relative isovariance is achievable by increasing representation multiplicity (Thm 5.2). Practical guidelines follow: for orientation-dependent tasks, select feature components whose orbit types contain p_Y(H); for general tasks, avoid components where the infimum collapses to the full group, which annihilates discriminative power.

## Results

The paper's central quantitative result is Table 1, which predicts the symmetry infimum I_{O(3)}(V_{l=l_0}^{⊕r}, D_{kh}) for all combinations of k-fold symmetry and feature degree l_0, identifying three degeneration types: full (to O(3)), axial (to D_{∞h}), and half (to D_{2kh}). In the graph discrimination experiment (Fig. 5), embedding difference norms exhibit a sharp binary pattern: values are either >10^{-3} (structures distinguishable) or <10^{-6} (indistinguishable), a gap of over 10^3×, confirming the theoretical predictions across all 12 TFN/HEGNN configurations. On QM9 isotropic polarizability prediction, feature components where molecular symmetry increases to O(3) yield substantially higher MAE loss, and adding 1-degree features that cause full degeneration provides no significant improvement, validating the design guidelines in §4.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not benchmark against competing methods in the standard sense; instead, it validates its predictions against prior empirical and theoretical frameworks. The paper's framework subsumes and extends the collapse-to-zero theory of Cen et al. (2024), which only explains full degeneration, by providing a complete characterization of all three degeneration types (full, axial, half). It also goes beyond the orbit-type descriptions of Kaba & Ravanbakhsh (2023) by offering a computable algorithm (Algo. 1–2) and practical feature-design guidelines within the equivariant framework, rather than relaxing equivariance. The empirical validation uses TFN and HEGNN as test architectures (not as competing baselines), confirming that the predicted symmetry infima match observed embedding behavior across all tested configurations (varying channels 1/4/16 and layers 1–4). The QM9 experiments further show that the theoretical guidelines for avoiding harmful symmetry increase (e.g., excluding components that cause full degeneration) lead to measurably better prediction performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends several partial analyses of expressivity degradation in equivariant neural networks. Joshi et al. (2023) observed the phenomenon empirically, Cen et al. (2024) provided a collapse-to-zero theory covering only the most extreme case (full degeneration), and Kaba & Ravanbakhsh (2023) connected it to orbit types and Curie's principle without a rigorous reduction strategy. The present work builds on Michel's Criterion (1980) and the Ihrig-Golubitsky framework (1984) to prove a complete characterization: a unique symmetry infimum exists for any feature space and input symmetry, is computable via a finite algorithm, and is generically achieved by expressive equivariant maps (extending Dym & Maron's C⁰ approximation to C^∞ density).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework gives ENN practitioners a principled, computable tool for predicting which feature-space choices will cause orientational information loss on symmetric inputs, moving the design process from trial-and-error to informed selection. The three-way taxonomy (full, axial, half degeneration) explains previously puzzling empirical observations that could not be accounted for by the collapse-to-zero model alone. Limitations include the focus on SO(3)/O(3) and the high-multiplicity assumption (r > dim G) needed for the Michel criterion's sufficiency, though the authors note results often hold for r = 1. Open questions include extending the framework to non-compact groups, continuous symmetries beyond 3D rotations, and whether the manifold hypothesis can be relaxed further for noisy real-world data.
