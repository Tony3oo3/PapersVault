## Problem

Improving antibody binding affinity from a single lead sequence without any antigen-specific context—no complex structure, no antigen-specific training data, no epitope/paratope labels—remains an open challenge in therapeutic protein design. Prior property-enhancement methods like PropEn required hundreds of related sequences already present in training data and operated on sequence alone, making them inapplicable to true one-shot scenarios where the lead is far from the training distribution. This paper asks whether a model can learn generalizable, causal directions of affinity improvement across diverse binding environments and transfer them to entirely unseen antibody seeds.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single lead antibody sequence (heavy + light chain variable domain) as input, with its structure predicted by ESMFold; no antigen, complex structure, or fine-tuning is provided at test time. It outputs a set of antibody sequence variants (typically 5,000 sampled designs) with controllable edit distances (tuned via sampling temperature and iteration count, yielding ~5–12 edits from the seed). The lead is consumed through frozen GearNet sequence-structure embeddings (3072-dim per residue, padded to 151/150 residues for heavy/light chains), and outputs are decoded by a lightweight 2-layer MLP trained on pOAS, producing per-residue amino-acid distributions.

## Method

AFFINITYENHANCER learns a residual embedding-space operator that maps low-affinity antibody embeddings toward high-affinity ones, trained on matched pairs from SKEMPI 2.0 where each pair consists of two sequences targeting the same antigen, within a capped sequence distance, and with strictly higher measured affinity for the target. The architecture comprises three modules: a frozen GearNet embedder producing sequence-structure representations, a 4-block Graph Transformer (4.2M parameters) that learns the residual transformation using residue-residue adjacency from predicted structure, and a frozen pOAS-trained MLP decoder that maps embeddings back to sequences. The key theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) shows that the matching rule—same environment, close in sequence space, with a minimum affinity improvement—guarantees a lower bound on causal latent movement and an upper bound on spurious drift, so averaging across many environments cancels spurious directions while aligning causal ones. At inference, the lead is embedded, the residual Graph Transformer is applied, and the result is decoded to produce affinity-enhanced variants, all without any antigen-specific information or fine-tuning on the test seed.

## Results

Across four held-out seeds (three internal antibodies + Trastuzumab), AFFINITYENHANCER achieves a mean binder rate of 50.1% and improved-binder rate of 8.46% over 4,555 designs in the edit-distance window [5,12], improving all 4/4 seeds. For Trastuzumab specifically, the model reaches 79.4% binder rate and 31.5% improved rate with a mean edit distance of 7.9±1.8. The sequence-only PropEn baseline fails entirely (0% binders, 0% improved, mean ED 55.8), while the structure-conditioned AntiFold and inpainting IgCraft baselines produce fewer improved binders and smaller affinity gains in predicted pKD. Ablations confirm that data matching, GearNet embeddings, the Graph Transformer architecture, and adjacency information are each necessary for strong performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PropEn (sequence-only, Tagasovska et al. 2024), AFFINITYENHANCER wins decisively: PropEn generates sequences 25–68 edits from the seed (mean ED 55.8), producing zero predicted binders and zero improved binders across all four seeds, whereas AE stays within ED 7.08 and achieves 50.1% binder / 8.46% improved rates. Against AntiFold (structure-conditioned inverse folding, Høie et al. 2024), AE shifts the predicted pKD distribution upward for nearly every seed, while AntiFold—constrained to conform to the seed's structure—mostly proposes variants that retain binding at similar or lower affinity. Against IgCraft (antibody-specific generative inpainting, Greenig et al. 2025), AE outperforms on both binder and improved-binder counts; IgCraft fails to propose CDR sequences (all-CDR or heavy-only) that retain or improve binding given the framework context. In Appendix F, AE also outperforms MLDE (Tran & Hy 2024, latent directed evolution) in all tested settings: MLDE could not generate a sufficient number of designs with reasonable edit distances (default settings yielded ED >30), and where comparable EDs were forced, AE produced higher predicted affinities and concentrated edits in CDRs rather than spreading them across frameworks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends PropEn (Tagasovska et al., 2024), which used matched-pair supervision to implicitly learn property-improving directions in sequence space, by adding structure-aware embeddings (GearNet), an adjacency-informed Graph Transformer, and an explicit same-environment (same-antigen) constraint in the matching rule. The key conceptual departure is moving from PropEn's in-distribution regime—where the lead sequence must already appear in training data—to a true one-shot setting where the lead is substantially out-of-distribution and no antigen context is available. The authors also draw a deliberate distinction from preference learning (DPO, ReFT), arguing that their local-improvement pairs (nearby sequences with small measured affinity differences) encode stepwise causal movements rather than global winner/loser preferences, and they provide a Lipschitz-based theoretical analysis formalizing why matched pairs isolate causal over spurious variation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

One-shot antibody affinity maturation without antigen structures or target-specific data is a practical bottleneck in drug discovery where complex structures are unavailable and labeled affinity data is scarce; AFFINITYENHANCER offers a drop-in framework that leverages pretrained biomolecular representations to bridge this gap. However, all validation is in silico via the Cortex oracle, only four held-out seeds are tested (with very low improved-binder rates for Seeds 1 and 3: 0.04% and 0.06%), and no wet-lab confirmation is provided, leaving the practical utility of the predicted gains unverified. Open questions include whether incorporating epitope or antigen context would disambiguate competing improvement routes, whether the causal-signal distillation framework transfers to other protein properties beyond affinity, and whether the approach scales to the full diversity of binding modes encountered in therapeutic development.
