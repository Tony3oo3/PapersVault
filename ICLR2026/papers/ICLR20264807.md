## Problem

LLM reasoning in anesthesiology remains underexplored: existing medical benchmarks predominantly assess factual recall (System 1) and overlook the complex, multi-system clinical decision-making that anesthesiology demands (System 2). No comprehensive dataset suite exists to support the full training pipeline—continued pre-training, supervised fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards—for anesthesiology-specific reasoning in LLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264807/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark (AnesBench) takes bilingual (English/Chinese) multiple-choice questions organized by three cognitive demand levels (System 1, System 1.x, System 2) and evaluates models via zero-shot CoT prompting at temperature 0 with a 2048-token output cap, reporting accuracy per cognitive level. The training datasets feed different stages: AnesCorpus (2.4M documents) for CPT, AnesQA (20.7K QA pairs with five question-type labels) for SFT, and AnesR1 (10.3K MCQs with full CoT reasoning trajectories) for both SFT and RLVR. The Morpheus models output structured chain-of-thought reasoning culminating in a final MCQ answer extracted via regex patterns like 'Answer is' or 'Answer:'.

## Method

AnesSuite introduces a four-component dataset suite: AnesBench (7,972 bilingual MCQs annotated for cognitive demand level via DeepSeek-R1 with 60% manual verification), AnesCorpus (2.4M documents filtered by dual-keyword frequency from FineWeb with two-stage n-gram/LCS decontamination against AnesBench), AnesQA (20.7K QA pairs generated by a dual-model pipeline using LLaMA3.3-70B-Instruct for question generation and Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct for filtering/answering), and AnesR1 (10.3K MCQs with CoT trajectories generated by DeepSeek-R1 and filtered via rejection sampling requiring correct final answers). The Morpheus baseline models (7B/14B/32B, initialized from Qwen2.5) are trained in two stages: a 100-step SFT cold-start on AnesR1, followed by GRPO (group size 5, binary outcome reward, max 4 epochs) using the Verl framework. A key finding is that this limited anesthesiology-specific RL training not only rivals larger models on domain benchmarks but also transfers to general medical and broad-domain reasoning tasks, suggesting reasoning skills acquired in sparsely exposed domains can generalize.

## Results

On AnesBench, the best proprietary model (Gemini-2.5-Pro) achieves 0.85 average accuracy, while the best open-source model (DeepSeek-R1) reaches 0.82. System 2 (complex reasoning) questions remain the hardest, with most open-source models scoring below 0.50 and even GPT-4o only reaching 0.59. The Morpheus baseline models, trained with SFT + GRPO on AnesR1, achieve 0.63 (7B), 0.69 (14B), and 0.72 (32B) average accuracy, rivaling or exceeding models one size class larger (e.g., Morpheus-32B at 0.72 surpasses Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct at 0.70).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Morpheus-7B (0.63 avg) outperforms its base Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (0.59) by +4 points and nearly matches Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct (0.64); Morpheus-14B (0.69) surpasses Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct (0.68) by +1 point; Morpheus-32B (0.72) exceeds Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct (0.70) by +2 points, demonstrating that SFT+GRPO on AnesR1 enables smaller models to rival or beat models one size class larger. Among proprietary models, Gemini-2.5-Pro leads with 0.85 average accuracy, followed by DeepSeek-R1 (0.82) and Gemini-2.5-Flash (0.81), while GPT-4o (0.76) and Claude-3.7-Sonnet (0.77) trail by 4–9 points. Medical-specific models (HuatuoGPT-o1 72B at 0.71, BioMistral 7B at 0.31) do not outperform general-purpose reasoning models, with DeepSeek-R1 exceeding the best medical model by 11 points. A consistent gap exists across all models between System 1 (factual recall, 0.70–0.89) and System 2 (complex reasoning, 0.26–0.77), indicating that anesthesiology-specific reasoning remains the primary bottleneck rather than factual knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the anesthesiology benchmarking line initiated by CAB (Zhou et al., 2025), which focused primarily on Chinese-language factual retrieval, by introducing a bilingual, three-level cognitive demand framework (System 1, System 1.x, System 2) that explicitly targets complex clinical reasoning. It also builds on the reasoning-LLM paradigm established by DeepSeek-R1 and GRPO, applying reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards to a specialized medical subdomain where prior work (Hypnos, OARA) used anesthesiology corpora only for SFT without enhancing reasoning. The complete training pipeline (CPT → SFT → RLVR) departs from prior medical LLM efforts that typically addressed only one or two of these stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The suite provides the first infrastructure for systematically studying anesthesiology reasoning, revealing that even top proprietary models (GPT-4o, Claude-3.7-Sonnet) struggle with System 2 tasks (~0.5–0.6 accuracy), suggesting the core bottleneck is reasoning rather than knowledge. The finding that CPT with domain-specific corpus improves English but degrades Chinese performance raises important questions about multilingual knowledge transfer in continued pre-training. Limitations include System 2 questions being constructed from abstract scenarios rather than real clinical cases, and the absence of multimodal inputs, both of which constrain ecological validity for real-world anesthetic decision-making.
