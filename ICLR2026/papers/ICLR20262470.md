## Problem

Existing virtual cell models for perturbation-response prediction are data-hungry black boxes: they require large-scale single-cell datasets, generalize poorly to unseen perturbations, and produce predictions without mechanistic explanations grounded in biological principles. This undermines their credibility for scientific discovery and makes them difficult for biologists to trust when designing downstream experiments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262470/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a triplet (cell line, drug perturbation, target gene) and, at inference time, retrieves a support set of analogous and contrastive training cases from a knowledge-graph-augmented corpus. It outputs a binary label for either differential expression (DE) or directional change (DIR), accompanied by a stepwise Chain-of-Thought explanation that traces the causal reasoning from drug mechanism through signaling pathways to the predicted gene response. The evaluation is strictly zero-shot with respect to test drugs: the retrieval corpus contains only training-set perturbations, so the model must extrapolate from functionally analogous drugs.

## Method

VCWorld replaces the conventional regression formulation with a three-stage knowledge-based inference pipeline powered by an LLM (Gemini-2.5-Flash). First, it constructs an open-world biological knowledge graph integrating PubChem, DrugBank, UniProt, Gene Ontology, Reactome, STRING, and CORUM, then uses the LLM to generate rich symbolic text descriptions for each node from its k-hop neighborhood. Second, it retrieves a balanced evidence set (analogue cases with positive labels and contrast cases with negative labels) using a hybrid similarity score that combines LLM-generated semantic similarity with graph-structural path similarity, weighted by a tunable α. Third, a structured Chain-of-Thought prompt instructs the LLM to reason step-by-step over the query's biological context and the retrieved evidence, producing both the binary prediction and a verifiable mechanistic hypothesis. The entire system operates in a few-shot regime (30% of perturbations for retrieval), requiring no gradient-based training of the LLM itself.

## Results

On the GeneTAK benchmark (5 cell lines, 348 drug perturbations, 2,000 highly variable genes), VCWorld with Gemini-2.5-Flash achieves the highest overall accuracy across all cell lines: 0.61–0.71 on the Differential Expression (DE) task and 0.66–0.72 on the Directional Change (DIR) task. In terms of F1 score, VCWorld reaches 0.63 on both DE and DIR (average across cell lines), with balanced precision (0.59) and recall (0.68). On the threshold-free AUPRC metric, VCWorld achieves an average above 0.80 (e.g., 0.85 on C32), substantially outperforming all baselines which remain in the 0.36–0.65 range.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VCWorld (Gemini-2.5-Flash) outperforms all three deep-learning baselines on every cell line for both DE and DIR tasks. Against scVI (the strongest baseline), VCWorld improves DE accuracy by 0.03–0.23 (e.g., 0.70 vs. 0.66 on C32, 0.68 vs. 0.48 on HepG2C3A) and DIR accuracy by 0.19–0.31 (e.g., 0.72 vs. 0.47 on C32, 0.69 vs. 0.38 on PANC-1); the F1 gap is even larger (0.63 vs. 0.21 on DE). Against STATE, VCWorld's advantage is overwhelming: DE accuracy of 0.61–0.71 versus STATE's 0.09–0.47, and F1 of 0.63 versus 0.17, indicating STATE fails to capture basic perturbation patterns in this few-shot setting. Against CPA, VCWorld similarly dominates (DE accuracy 0.61–0.71 vs. 0.17–0.30; F1 0.63 vs. 0.03), with CPA performing below the random baseline on most cell lines. The graph-based GAT baseline is the closest competitor on DE accuracy (0.54–0.64) but still trails VCWorld by 0.04–0.14 per cell line and is far behind on F1 (0.49 vs. 0.63) and AUPRC (0.38–0.47 vs. 0.72–0.84), confirming that VCWorld's knowledge-grounded reasoning provides a decisive edge over purely structural or statistical approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

VCWorld extends the retrieval-augmented generation paradigm (Lewis et al., 2020) by replacing flat semantic retrieval with a graph-guided causal evidence framework that scores similarity using both LLM-generated textual descriptions and knowledge-graph topology. It also builds on chain-of-thought prompting (Wei et al., 2022) as the final reasoning stage, but departs from prior LLM-based biological reasoning work (CoTox, DrugReasoner, SUMMER) by grounding the CoT in a multi-source open-world biological knowledge graph (PubChem, DrugBank, UniProt, GO, Reactome, STRING, CORUM) rather than relying on free-text retrieval alone. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against the dominant black-box virtual cell models (scVI, CPA, STATE) that learn end-to-end mappings from perturbation to expression, arguing that structured biological priors plus iterative LLM reasoning can match or exceed their accuracy while providing interpretable, stepwise mechanistic explanations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine credibility gap in virtual cell modeling: black-box predictors that cannot explain *why* a gene is up- or down-regulated are difficult for biologists to trust or use for hypothesis generation. The GeneTAK benchmark, derived from Tahoe-100M with a 3:7 train/test split to simulate few-shot conditions, provides a useful and reproducible evaluation setting aligned with prior work (STATE). However, several open questions remain: the evaluation covers only five cell lines and drug perturbations (no genetic or combinatorial perturbations), the AUROC values for the best VCWorld configuration are at or below 0.5 despite high AUPRC (raising calibration and metric-computation concerns for a binary-output model), and the strong dependence on a single proprietary LLM (Gemini-2.5-Flash) for peak performance limits reproducibility. The authors themselves flag future directions including multi-agent reasoning, broader perturbation types, and more systematic cross-model benchmarks.
