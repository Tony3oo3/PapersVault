## Problem

Existing feed-forward novel view synthesis methods (NeRF, Gaussian Splatting) assume clean input images, and degradation-specific approaches (e.g., DeblurNeRF, LLNeRF) are tailored to narrow corruption types without generalizability. No prior framework simultaneously performs universal image restoration and 3D-consistent NVS in a single feed-forward pass under arbitrary, unknown degradations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261926/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N (typically 3) degraded multi-view RGB images with known camera poses as input, with no prior knowledge of the degradation type. It produces restored clean images, a rendered novel-view image, and an explicit 3D Gaussian point cloud. Inference uses DDIM sampling with 3 fixed steps and completes within one second for three input views.

## Method

ReSplat jointly trains a feed-forward GS backbone (MVSGaussian) and a residual denoising diffusion restoration model (DiffUIR) in a coupled loop: at each diffusion step, the pseudo-clean images are fed to the GS model to extract 3D Gaussian centroids, which are then used as self-guidance for the next diffusion step. Two key modules enable this coupling: (1) a GS-guided multi-view alignment module that projects multi-view features onto Gaussian centers and performs self-attention across views, ensuring 3D-consistent restoration; and (2) a pre-filtering module that predicts per-view reliability maps conditioned on the original corrupted inputs, modulating the GS aggregation weights to suppress residual artifacts before feature fusion. Training alternates between a UIR residual prediction loss and a novel-view rendering loss, with the GS model pre-trained on clean data before joint fine-tuning.

## Results

On the LLFF degradation dataset with three multi-view inputs, ReSplat achieves the best NVS PSNR across all five corruption types: 23.15 (motion blur), 24.46 (snow), 21.99 (haze), 19.76 (low-light), and 24.11 (rain), outperforming the strongest baseline DiffUIR by 0.4–0.9 dB. For multi-view image restoration, ReSplat similarly leads with PSNR of 27.14 (motion blur), 32.07 (snow), 26.45 (haze), 22.82 (low-light), and 31.28 (rain). On real-world datasets (DeblurNeRF, REVIDE, LLNeRF, NTURain, RSVD), ReSplat maintains its lead, e.g., 22.91 PSNR on real motion blur and 22.92 on real low-light, while completing three-view inference in under one second with three DDIM steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReSplat outperforms all four baselines on every corruption type and every metric in the main comparison. Against the strongest baseline DiffUIR (a diffusion-based universal restoration model used in an IR→NV pipeline), ReSplat gains 0.4–0.9 dB PSNR on NVS and 0.5–1.6 dB on image restoration, with the largest NVS margin on low-light (+0.89 dB) and the largest IR margin on snow (+0.87 dB). GAURA, which performs NVS directly without a separate restoration stage, trails ReSplat by 1.9–4.5 dB PSNR on NVS and provides no image restoration output. PromptIR and AiRnet, both non-diffusion universal restoration models, are substantially weaker: they lag ReSplat by 2.4–13.4 dB PSNR on NVS (e.g., PromptIR scores only 6.37 PSNR on low-light NVS vs. ReSplat's 19.76) and by 5–26 dB on image restoration, indicating that diffusion-based residual learning is critical for handling severe degradations like haze and low-light.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReSplat sits at the intersection of two research threads: universal image restoration via residual denoising diffusion (DiffUIR, Zheng et al. 2024) and feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting (MVSGaussian, Liu et al. 2025). It departs from GAURA (Gupta et al. 2024), the closest prior degradation-agnostic NVS method, by explicitly incorporating a pretrained 2D restoration prior rather than relying solely on the feed-forward model's implicit capacity to handle corruption. The key conceptual departure is the self-guided mechanism: Gaussian centroids extracted at intermediate diffusion steps are fed back as 3D cross-attention queries, creating a mutual information loop between the restoration and geometry-estimation components that neither prior line of work achieves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first feed-forward GS framework that jointly performs degradation-agnostic restoration and NVS without per-scene optimization, making it practical for sparse-view, real-world settings where degradation type is unknown. The self-guided residual diffusion idea—using intermediate 3D geometry to condition subsequent denoising steps—offers a reusable pattern for coupling 2D generative priors with explicit 3D representations. Limitations include the computational overhead of multi-step diffusion at inference (mitigated to ~1s with 3 DDIM steps), inherited 3DGS weaknesses on specular and transparent surfaces, and evaluation restricted to relatively simple scene geometries (LLFF-scale). Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-resolution or unbounded scenes, and whether the 3D-guidance benefit persists under more severe or temporally varying degradations.
