## Problem

RL fine-tuning for LLM reasoning has focused heavily on algorithmic improvements while overlooking the training environment itself. Existing environments fail to simultaneously satisfy three critical properties: scalability (math/code tasks require expensive expert annotation), generalizable reasoning (game-based skills are too specialized), and verifiability (clear objective correctness signals). This paper asks whether structured data (e.g., knowledge graphs) can be automatically transformed into reasoning environments that meet all three criteria.

## Input / Output

The method takes a KGQA question and a set of knowledge graph triples (extracted from Freebase) as structured in-context, which is embedded directly in the LLM prompt as a soft constraint rather than queried via an external tool. The triples are organized into supporting subgraphs (valid reasoning paths) and distractor subgraphs (semantically relevant but incorrect paths), with a retention ratio controlling how much supporting information is present (SIE-100% through SIE-0%). The model outputs a step-by-step reasoning chain within <think> tags followed by a final answer in <answer> tags, verified by exact string match against the ground-truth answer entity.

## Method

The SIE framework automatically constructs reasoning environments from knowledge graphs via a four-step pipeline: (1) bidirectional multi-hop retrieval from both question and answer entities to obtain a seed subgraph, (2) Dijkstra shortest-path extraction to isolate the supporting subgraph containing valid reasoning paths, (3) two-stage semantic filtering (relation-level then triple-level reranking with a cross-encoder) to select challenging distractor triples within context-length limits, and (4) merging and shuffling supporting and distractor triples into a single in-context environment, with partial variants created by randomly dropping a fraction of supporting triples while adjusting distractor count to keep total length constant. The LLM is then fine-tuned with GRPO (group relative policy optimization), which eliminates the need for a separate critic model by using group-normalized advantages. Rewards are rule-based and verifiable: a binary answer reward for exact match plus a format reward for correct tag structure, preventing reward hacking. The key design insight is that the environment dynamics are encoded entirely as structured text in the prompt (a 'soft constraint'), making the setup trivially scalable and compatible with any RL algorithm, while the explicit schemas in structured data provide the verifiability that math/code environments lack at scale.

## Results

On structured reasoning (KGQA), RL w/ SIE achieves 93.4% on WebQSP, 87.7% on CWQ, and 85.8% on GrailQA with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, surpassing the no-context RL baseline by 33.7, 51.0, and 65.0 points respectively. On out-of-domain generalization, the same model reaches 87.4% on GSM8K, 61.6% on MATH500, 49.5% on KK-easy, and 29.0% on KK-hard, improving over CoT by 58.2, 18.6, 7.5, and 9.5 points. Even under the most information-scarce setting (SIE-0%, no supporting facts), RL fine-tuning still yields 72.8% on WebQSP and 87.1% on GSM8K, demonstrating robustness to incomplete environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four baselines: (1) CoT prompting (training-free), (2) SFT w/ SRD (supervised fine-tuning on distilled reasoning chains), (3) RL w/o Context (RL without structured environment), and (4) ToG (Think-on-Graph tool-using agent). RL w/ SIE decisively outperforms SFT w/ SRD by over 40 points on all three structured reasoning tasks (e.g., 93.4% vs. 40.5% on WebQSP for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), demonstrating that in-context RL exploration is far more efficient than imitation learning on distilled data. Compared to RL w/o Context, adding the SIE yields gains of 30–65 points, confirming the structured environment is critical. On out-of-domain tasks, RL w/ SIE improves GSM8K by 58.2 points and MATH500 by 18.6 points over CoT for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. Against the ToG agent, the RL-trained 7B model achieves 93.4% on WebQSP versus ToG's 32.1%, and even under SIE-0% (no supporting facts) still reaches 72.8%, matching or exceeding GPT-3.5+ToG (76.2%). REINFORCE++ matches GRPO in performance, while PPO lags by 5–10 points on structured tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant trend in LLM reasoning research that focuses on RL algorithm optimization (GRPO, REINFORCE++, PPO) by instead centering on the *environment* as the key lever for eliciting reasoning. It repurposes structured data (Freebase KG triples) as in-context soft-constraint environments for RL fine-tuning, a framing that bridges the KGQA literature (ToG, Plan-on-Graph) and the RL post-training literature (DeepSeek-R1, GRPO). Unlike prior structured-reasoning work that relies on prompt engineering or SFT on distilled reasoning chains, SIE treats the structured context as an explorable environment with rule-based verifiable rewards, and unlike game-based RL environments, it achieves scalability through automated subgraph extraction from existing KGs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, reproducible recipe for constructing scalable RL environments from structured data, addressing a gap the authors identify as underappreciated in the RL-for-reasoning literature. The finding that RL in SIE outperforms SFT on the same distilled data by 40+ points, and that even SIE-0% (no supporting facts) still yields strong gains through parametric knowledge activation and negative constraints from distractors, offers practical guidance for practitioners designing post-training pipelines. Open questions include whether the framework extends to other structured data types (databases, tables) beyond KGs, whether it scales to 70B+ models, and whether the modest absolute math gains (MATH500: ~61%) can be closed with longer training or richer environments.
