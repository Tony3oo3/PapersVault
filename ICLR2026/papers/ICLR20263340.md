## Problem

Current search-augmented LLM agents treat the entire multi-hop reasoning trajectory as a monolithic MDP optimized only via sparse terminal rewards, making them catastrophically brittle: a single decomposition, retrieval, or reasoning error at any step contaminates all subsequent states and derails the final answer. Unlike humans, who pause, correct a flawed step, and continue from the corrected point, these agents lack any mechanism for targeted self-correction, causing performance to degrade precipitously as reasoning chains extend beyond ten steps.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural-language multi-hop question and iteratively produces structured rounds, each containing an observation over retrieved evidence, a concise sub-answer, and a search query, all formatted with explicit XML-style tags (<search>, <observation>, <sub_answer>, <answer>). Retrieved evidence is injected between <information> tags by an external search tool (E5 retriever over Wiki-18 for offline evaluation, Google Search API for online). The process terminates when the model emits a final answer inside <answer> tags, which is scored against a gold reference using word-level EM and F1.

## Method

ERL introduces three erasure operators that surgically remove faulty trajectory segments and regenerate in place, triggered by threshold-based checks on dense stepwise rewards: (1) sub-answer erasure discards the observation and sub-answer when the F1-based sub-answer reward falls below α, resetting to the prior state; (2) search erasure removes only the query when the TF-IDF coverage-gain reward (minus a redundancy penalty) falls below α in later rounds, preserving the correct observation; (3) plan erasure resets the entire trajectory when the first-round search reward falls below β. These erasure mechanisms are combined with a round-based reasoning structure (observation → sub-answer → search per round) and a PPO training objective with GAE advantages, where token-level reward attribution aligns search, sub-answer, and final-answer rewards to their respective closing tags. The key insight is that fine-grained, in-place correction—rather than monolithic trajectory optimization—transforms brittle reasoning into a resilient process that recovers gracefully from individual mistakes.

## Results

ESearch (trained with ERL) achieves new SOTA on all four multi-hop QA benchmarks. With Qwen2.5-3B, it gains +6.06% EM and +9.94% F1 on average over the best baseline (StepSearch) under offline retrieval, and +10.90% EM and +13.18% F1 under online retrieval. With Qwen2.5-7B, the gains are +4.78% EM and +6.56% F1 (offline) and +5.98% EM and +7.88% F1 (online). On Bamboogle (7B, offline), ESearch-base reaches 0.534 EM / 0.656 F1, surpassing the previous best (StepSearch-base at 0.467 EM / 0.573 F1) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESearch consistently outperforms all seven baselines across all four datasets and both model scales. The closest competitor is StepSearch, which uses stepwise PPO rewards; ESearch-base (3B) exceeds it by +8.6% EM on HotpotQA (0.415 vs. 0.329) and +8.6% EM on Bamboogle (0.414 vs. 0.328). Search-R1 and ZeroSearch, which rely on sparse terminal rewards or retrieval simulation, trail ESearch by 10–25% EM on most datasets. R-Search (multi-reward RL) and SSRL (self-search RL) show moderate performance but remain 10–15% below ESearch on F1. ReSearch (7B) achieves 0.362 EM on HotpotQA, well below ESearch-base's 0.434. In the direct RL-algorithm comparison (Table 2), ERL outperforms both PPO and GRPO by 6–15% EM on the 3B model, with GRPO showing faster initial convergence but eventual instability, while ERL maintains stable training throughout.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the search-augmented RL line initiated by Search-R1 and StepSearch, which train LLMs to interleave retrieval and reasoning via PPO/GRPO with stepwise rewards. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of erasure operators that treat the reasoning trajectory not as an irreversibly committed sequence but as an editable manuscript: faulty sub-answers, search queries, or initial plans can be selectively deleted and regenerated in place, rather than propagating errors forward. This contrasts with prior work (Search-R1, R-Search, StepSearch, SSRL) that optimizes the full trajectory monolithically and can only improve via reward shaping, never via structural correction of already-committed steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The erasure mechanism addresses a genuine structural weakness of current search-augmented agents—catastrophic fragility where one misstep derails an entire multi-hop chain—and the ablations confirm that the three erasure types are complementary, with sub-answer correction being the most impactful. However, the framework's reliance on gold evidence and gold sub-answers for reward computation limits its applicability to open-ended or unannotated settings, and the sequential erasure-retry design introduces non-trivial computational overhead that may not scale to very long reasoning chains. A key open question is whether local, step-by-step erasure can be replaced by a global trajectory-level error understanding that resolves multiple concurrent failures in a single corrective pass.
