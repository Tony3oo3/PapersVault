## Problem

Instance-level image retrieval models are typically trained and evaluated within a single domain (e.g., landmarks), leaving it unclear how well they generalize to unseen domains in real-world retrieval. Most re-ranking methods rely on heavy transformer architectures that overfit to the training domain, and no prior work has conducted a systematic single-source domain generalization evaluation across diverse retrieval benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262123/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ELViS takes two sets of M=600 local descriptors (each projected to D=128 dimensions) extracted from a query image and a candidate image using a frozen foundation model (DINOv2, DINOv3, or SigLIP2) with a domain-specific local feature detector. It outputs a single scalar similarity score that re-ranks a shortlist of top-400 images initially retrieved by global descriptors. The model contains only 96K learnable parameters and processes each image pair in ~101 μs, making it roughly 7–10× faster than competing transformer-based re-rankers.

## Method

ELViS operates in the space of local descriptor similarities rather than raw descriptor vectors, computing the M×M similarity matrix S = QᵀX and refining it via entropy-regularized optimal transport (Sinkhorn-Knopp, 10 iterations) with descriptor-dependent dustbin gains predicted by a 2-layer MLP, which suppresses uninformative descriptors (e.g., background patches). The refined matrix S′ is then reduced to per-descriptor votes by taking row- and column-wise maxima, each vote is transformed by a learnable scalar function f (2-layer MLP with sigmoid output), and all votes are summed into the final similarity score. Training uses a modified BCE loss where a learnable function g reshapes the penalty curve to differentiate errors in overlapping positive/negative regions; g is discarded at inference since it acts as a monotonic reparameterization that does not alter ranking. This design injects strong inductive biases—operating on correspondence patterns, explicit dustbin-based filtering, and interpretable vote counting—yielding substantially better out-of-domain generalization than descriptor-based transformer re-rankers while being an order of magnitude faster.

## Results

Trained on landmarks (GLDv2) with DINOv2 descriptors, ELViS achieves 53.9 mAP average across 8 datasets, outperforming the best descriptor-based competitor AMES (50.4 mAP) by +3.5 mAP. The advantage is most pronounced out-of-domain (OOD), where ELViS reaches 55.0 mAP versus 49.7 for AMES (+5.3 mAP), while being slightly weaker in-domain (50.5 vs. 52.4, −1.9 mAP). ELViS contains only 96K parameters and runs at 101 μs per image pair, roughly 9× faster than AMES (952 μs) and 6.5× faster than RRT (656 μs). When trained on household items (SOP), ELViS again leads in average (51.5 vs. 43.8 for R²Former) and OOD (51.0 vs. 42.8) performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three main learned re-ranking baselines, ELViS consistently wins on average and OOD mAP while trailing slightly on ID: versus AMES (50.4 avg, 49.7 OOD), ELViS gains +3.5 avg and +5.3 OOD but loses −1.9 ID; versus RRT (49.2 avg, 48.6 OOD), ELViS gains +4.7 avg and +6.4 OOD; versus R²Former (49.1 avg, 48.6 OOD), ELViS gains +4.8 avg and +6.4 OOD. The parameter-free Chamfer+OT baseline (48.2 avg, 50.2 OOD) is the strongest OOD competitor among non-learned methods, but ELViS still surpasses it by +5.7 avg and +4.8 OOD. In the image-matching comparison (Table H), ELViS (53.9 avg) outperforms SuperGlue (48.5 avg, 49.7 OOD) by +5.4 avg and +5.3 OOD, and decisively beats OmniGlue (43.7 avg) and spatial verification (43.6 avg). On the extreme-domain-shift APL benchmark (Table G), ELViS (e.g., 55.1 R@1 on Alps) substantially outperforms AMES (44.0 R@1) and the general-purpose AnyLoc (40.7 R@1), though the specialist AstroLoc (98.1 R@1) remains far ahead due to task-specific training. ELViS is also ~9× faster than AMES and ~6.5× faster than RRT, enabling re-ranking of significantly more candidates within a fixed time budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

ELViS extends the optimal transport framework from SuperGlue (Sarlin et al., 2020) by replacing fixed dustbin gains with descriptor-dependent gains predicted by a small MLP, and repurposes OT not for hard correspondence matching but for soft similarity matrix refinement. The voting-and-counting aggregation step draws directly from classical retrieval work (Tolias & Jégou, 2014; Jegou et al., 2008), but replaces hand-crafted kernels with a learnable scalar function. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant descriptor-based re-ranking paradigm (RRT, R2Former, AMES) by operating entirely in similarity space, arguing that correspondence patterns are more transferable than raw descriptor geometry. The single-source domain generalization evaluation protocol unifying eight datasets is, to the authors' knowledge, the first such benchmark for instance-level retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 96K-parameter model with strong inductive biases (OT refinement + learnable voting) outperforms 2000K+ parameter transformer re-rankers by 3–6 mAP on out-of-domain data while being ~10× faster, challenging the assumption that architectural complexity is necessary for competitive retrieval. The finding that even the untrained Chamfer+OT baseline is a strong OOD performer suggests that the field has been over-parameterizing the re-ranking step at the expense of generalization. Open questions include whether the ~1–2 mAP in-domain gap versus AMES can be closed without sacrificing OOD performance (the hybrid ELViS+AMES model partially addresses this), and whether the similarity-space formulation extends to other retrieval settings such as video or 3D. The 8-dataset domain generalization benchmark itself provides a much-needed evaluation standard beyond single-domain testing.
