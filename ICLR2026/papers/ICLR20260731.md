## Problem

Short, underspecified user queries (typically one or two words) in text-to-image retrieval produce large, ambiguous search subspaces with semantic collisions and no mechanism to control the quality of retrieved images. Existing VLM-based retrieval systems focus exclusively on semantic alignment, offering no way to steer results toward user-preferred dimensions such as aesthetics or relevance. This paper introduces quality-controllable retrieval (QCR), a new task where retrieval is explicitly conditioned on user-specified quality requirements.

## Input / Output

The method takes a short text query (e.g., "a dog") and a pair of discretized quality conditions (relevance level and aesthetic level, each from {Low, Medium, High} or a five-level variant) as input. It produces a human-readable enriched text query generated by a fine-tuned LLM, which is then encoded by a frozen VLM text encoder and matched against precomputed image embeddings via FAISS to return the top-η retrieved images. The VLM (OpenCLIP ViT-H-14), caption model, and aesthetic scorer are all frozen; only the small LLM (GPT2-1.5B or Qwen2.5-0.5B) is trained, making the system compatible with any pretrained VLM without modification.

## Method

The core mechanism is Quality-Conditioned Query Completion (QCQC): a generative LLM is fine-tuned via autoregressive next-token prediction to expand short queries into descriptive sentences conditioned on discretized quality levels. For each gallery image, the pipeline generates a caption (CoCa or BLIP-2), an aesthetic score (pretrained predictor), and a relevance score (cosine similarity of image-caption features from OpenCLIP); continuous scores are partitioned into categorical levels via percentile thresholds (33rd/66th for three levels). Training instructions take the form "Relevance: [level], Aesthetic: [level], Query: " followed by the caption, so the LLM learns to append quality-aware descriptive phrases (pose, scene, aesthetics) that steer retrieval toward the desired embedding-space region. At inference, the LLM generates a completed query for the given quality condition, which is encoded by the frozen VLM and searched against the gallery. The paper also provides a theoretical rank-perturbation analysis showing that query completion, modeled as a structured perturbation to the query embedding matrix, can increase the rank of the similarity matrix under mild conditions, enabling finer-grained image distinctions.

## Results

On Flickr2.4M (2.4M images), QCQC with Qwen2.5-0.5B and CoCa captions achieves the highest average aesthetic score of 5.270 and average relevance of 0.390 under the High-High quality condition, compared to 4.735/0.350 for the prefix-only baseline. On MS-COCO (118K images), the method reaches 5.024 aesthetic and 0.387 relevance under High-High. Critically, QCQC is the only method that exhibits a consistent monotonic increase in both metrics as quality conditions shift from Low to High, demonstrating true quality controllability (Ctrl = ✓) rather than incidental score variation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares QCQC against five baselines: prefix-only retrieval, LLaMA3-8B-Instruct, GPT-4o, a pretrained (PT) LLM without fine-tuning, and a finetuned (FT) LLM conditioned on randomly assigned quality scores. Under the High-High condition on Flickr2.4M, QCQC achieves 5.270 average aesthetic and 0.390 average relevance, outperforming GPT-4o (5.056 / 0.361) by +0.214 Aes and +0.029 Rel, and LLaMA3 (4.842 / 0.352) by +0.428 Aes and +0.038 Rel. The decisive advantage is controllability: QCQC is the only method whose scores increase monotonically from Low to High conditions in both metrics, while LLaMA3 shows near-zero variation (Aes range 4.72–4.84) and GPT-4o shows an aesthetic trend but no consistent relevance trend. The FT baseline (finetuned on random scores) improves over prefix-only but fails to produce any quality-dependent ordering, confirming that the quality conditioning signal—not just fine-tuning on captions—is what enables control. On MS-COCO, QCQC similarly leads under High-High (5.024 Aes / 0.387 Rel) versus GPT-4o (5.048 / 0.351) and LLaMA3 (4.911 / 0.344), with the same controllability gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of text-to-image retrieval (T2IR) with VLMs (CLIP, OpenCLIP) and the query auto-completion literature (Bar-Yossef & Kraus 2011; Cai & De Rijke 2016; Wang et al. 2023b; Lei et al. 2024). It departs from prior query-completion work, which targets semantic disambiguation for document retrieval, by conditioning completion on discretized quality dimensions (relevance and aesthetics) specific to a given image gallery. The theoretical analysis draws on matrix perturbation theory to show that appending descriptive suffixes to short queries increases the rank of the similarity matrix, providing a formal justification for why query enrichment improves discriminability. Unlike post-retrieval filtering or re-ranking approaches, QCQC performs quality control at the query-generation stage, keeping the VLM frozen and requiring no architectural modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: short user queries (1–2 words) are underspecified for modern VLMs, and existing retrieval systems offer no mechanism to steer results toward user-preferred quality levels. The framework's transparency (human-readable enriched queries) and compatibility with any frozen VLM make it a lightweight, deployable augmentation rather than a replacement. Key limitations include dependence on the reliability of aesthetic/relevance scoring models, dataset-specificity (the completion model must be retrained per gallery), and the narrow quality spectrum available in real datasets, which compresses score differences. Open questions include extending to additional quality dimensions (interestingness, diversity, personalization), handling multi-turn interactive quality refinement, and scaling the approach to truly massive galleries where per-gallery fine-tuning becomes impractical.
