## Problem

Existing UV unwrapping methods—whether optimization-based, bottom-up, or learning-based—fail to simultaneously satisfy the higher-level quality standards that professional artists expect: clean boundaries, efficient space utilization, and semantic coherence. Learning-based approaches in particular produce fragmented, overlapping, or topologically incoherent UV maps that are impractical for rendering pipelines, while traditional methods require hours of manual seam placement and island adjustment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262838/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a 3D mesh (vertices, faces, normals, curvature) and an initial UV map generated by Blender's Ministretch algorithm as joint input to the parameterization module, which predicts a per-vertex 2D offset added to the initial UV to produce the final artist-style map in [0,1] space. The seam prediction stage (SeamGPT) operates on the raw mesh alone and outputs connected cut-vertex sequences that partition the surface into semantically meaningful charts. Inference runs on consumer-grade GPUs with under 10 GB memory for meshes with fewer than 1000 faces.

## Method

ArtUV decomposes UV unwrapping into two stages: (1) SeamGPT autoregressively predicts semantically meaningful cutting seams via an hourglass Transformer decoder over quantized coordinate tokens, and (2) a supervised auto-encoder refines the initial optimization-based UV into an artist-style map by learning the residual offset rather than the full 3D-to-2D mapping. The parameterization architecture chains a residual MLP with importance-based dimension scaling (UV > vertices > normals = curvature = degree), five SAGE-Conv layers for local graph feature propagation, and a pyramid attention encoder–coarse-to-fine decoder for global interaction and multi-scale offset prediction. Training uses a four-term loss combining L1 reconstruction (after Horn's-method rotation alignment), differentiable-rendering silhouette L2, Jacobian-SVD distortion, and a normal-flip overlap penalty, optimized on the newly curated ArtUV-200K dataset of 200K artist-validated UV islands.

## Results

On the FAM benchmark, ArtUV achieves a distortion of 8.91 with a runtime of 36 seconds and 14 UV fragments, outperforming XAtlas (distortion 9.44, 80.4 s, 1292 fragments), Nuvo (distortion 32.24, 2925.8 s, 1 fragment), and FAM (distortion 76.28, 5656.3 s, 1 fragment). On the ArtUV-200K benchmark (with artist-marked seams), ArtUV attains the lowest distortion (9.52), highest UV utilization (72.57%), and highest Artist-Level score (4.22/5) among all methods including Blender, Maya, 3DsMax, and manual artist unwrapping (10.90, 70.08%, 4.12).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Nuvo (Srinivasan et al., ECCV 2024), ArtUV reduces distortion from 32.24 to 8.91 (a 3.6× improvement) and cuts runtime from 2925.8 s to 36 s (~81× faster), while producing semantically organized UV islands rather than a single disordered fragment. Against FAM (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2024), the gap is even larger: distortion drops from 76.28 to 8.91 and runtime from 5656.3 s to 36 s (~157× faster); FAM's point-cloud-based approach yields irreparable overlapping artifacts that are impractical for rendering pipelines. Against XAtlas, ArtUV achieves slightly lower distortion (8.91 vs. 9.44) while reducing fragment count from 1292 to 14 and halving runtime (36 s vs. 80.4 s), indicating that SeamGPT's semantic seam prediction produces far more coherent charts than XAtlas's geometric-only segmentation. On the ArtUV-200K benchmark with fixed artist seams, ArtUV also surpasses professional software (Blender, Maya, 3DsMax) and even manual artist unwrapping in both distortion and UV utilization, with a user-study Artist-Level score of 4.22/5 versus 4.12 for manual work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of optimization-based UV unwrapping (LSCM, SLIM, SCAF) and learning-based approaches (Nuvo, FAM), but departs from both by reframing the parameterization problem as learning a residual offset from an optimization-based initial UV map to an artist-style target, rather than learning the full 3D-to-2D mapping from scratch. For surface cutting, it directly adopts SeamGPT's autoregressive seam prediction, and for initialization it relies on Blender's ministretch (SLIM-family) algorithm, making the neural component a refinement layer on top of classical methods. The key conceptual departure from prior learning-based work (Nuvo, FAM) is the use of mesh topology (faces, adjacency) and an optimization-based prior as structured input, avoiding the point-cloud discretization that destroys topological relationships in those methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ArtUV-200K dataset (200K curated artist-style UV islands) fills a notable gap in publicly available high-quality UV training data, and the 36-second end-to-end runtime (vs. thousands of seconds for per-model-trained baselines) makes automated artist-quality UV unwrapping practically viable in production pipelines. However, the method's sensitivity to seam quality and its inability to handle UV island reuse limit its applicability to complex or highly symmetric models. Open questions include whether the offset-learning formulation generalizes to higher-resolution meshes beyond the 500-vertex island cap, and whether the semantic coherence of SeamGPT's cuts can be further improved to reduce the distortion artifacts that arise from poor seam placement.
