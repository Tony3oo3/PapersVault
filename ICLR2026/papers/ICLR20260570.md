## Problem

Offline RL policies suffer from out-of-distribution (OOD) actions when they leave the training distribution, and prior methods address this by regularizing statistical distances (KL divergence, Wasserstein distance) between the learned and behavior policies. However, KL divergence is overly restrictive and impedes policy improvement, while Wasserstein distance does not provably prevent OOD actions, and both require a hand-tuned regularization weight that is sensitive per task and dataset. Additionally, the optimal policy can be multimodal, which unimodal policy classes cannot represent.

## Input / Output

The method takes a state observation (continuous vector or RGB image) and produces a continuous action in a compact action space (typically [-1,1]^d). The key structural detail is that the policy is a composition of two flows: a BC flow policy mapping from a uniform distribution over a hypersphere B_l^d (where l is chosen so the action space is contained in B_l^d) to the action space, and a reflected flow noise generator that maps from the same hypersphere to a manipulated noise distribution within that hypersphere. The source distribution's bounded support is what enables the support constraint to be enforced by construction rather than by regularization.

## Method

ReFORM is a two-stage flow policy that enforces the support constraint by construction. First, it learns a BC flow policy via flow matching with a bounded uniform source distribution U(B_l^d) (a hypersphere) to approximate the behavior policy's action distribution. Second, it learns a reflected flow noise generator that transforms the same bounded source into a manipulated noise distribution, optimized to maximize the Q-value of the composed policy while provably staying within the source support (Theorem 1). The reflected flow uses a projected Euler step that compensates outbound velocity at the hypersphere boundary, ensuring all generated noise remains in B_l^d. A one-step distillation of the BC flow avoids expensive backpropagation through the ODE during actor updates. This eliminates the need for any statistical distance regularization or its associated hyperparameter, while retaining the multimodal expressiveness of flow-based policies.

## Results

On 40 tasks from the OGBench benchmark (4 environments × 5 tasks × 2 dataset qualities), ReFORM achieves the best overall performance on the performance profile curves for both CLEAN and NOISY datasets, using a single constant set of hyperparameters across all tasks. On the CLEAN dataset, ReFORM outperforms DSRL (second) and FQL(M) (third), both of which use hand-tuned per-environment hyperparameters. On the NOISY dataset, ReFORM's advantage widens: DSRL and FQL(M) drop to near-zero on antmaze tasks, while FQL(S) becomes second-best. On D4RL antmaze and adroit environments, ReFORM also consistently achieves the best or comparable normalized returns (e.g., 97±0 on antmaze-umaze-v2 vs. 99±1 for FQL(M); 71±4 on antmaze-large-play-v2 vs. 61±21 for FQL(M)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReFORM is compared against three flow-policy baselines: FQL (Park et al., 2025b) in three regularization-strength variants, IFQL (Hansen-Estruch et al., 2023b), and DSRL (Wagenmaker et al., 2025). On the CLEAN dataset, ReFORM wins the overall performance profile, with DSRL (hand-tuned noise bounds) and FQL(M) (hand-tuned α per environment) ranking second and third; ReFORM's key advantage is that it uses a single constant hyperparameter set while the baselines require per-environment tuning. On the NOISY dataset, the gap widens dramatically: DSRL and FQL(M) collapse to 0±0 on all five antmaze-large-explore tasks, while ReFORM scores 87–91; FQL(S) becomes the runner-up on NOISY. IFQL consistently underperforms all other methods, often scoring near zero on antmaze tasks and remaining close to the BC policy level on manipulation tasks, because its importance-sampling-based approach is less efficient at finding high-Q actions. On D4RL, ReFORM matches or exceeds FQL(M) on most antmaze tasks (e.g., 97 vs. 99 on umaze, 85 vs. 92 on medium-play) and clearly outperforms it on harder tasks (71 vs. 61 on large-play, 69 vs. 85 on large-diverse), while on adroit pen tasks ReFORM is competitive (64–70 vs. 59–66 for FQL(M)). The paper also notes that ReFORM's training time is roughly 2× FQL due to the two-stage flow, but this is offset by eliminating the need for hyperparameter search.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReFORM sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) flow-matching and diffusion-based policies for offline RL (FQL, IFQL, DSRL), which address multimodal action distributions but rely on statistical-distance regularization (Wasserstein, KL) to prevent OOD actions; (2) support-constrained policy optimization, which is theoretically less restrictive than density-based regularization but has been difficult to enforce in practice; and (3) noise steering/manipulation from generative-model fine-tuning (image diffusion, latent-space RL), where prior work uses unimodal or tanh-squashed noise. The key departure is combining a bounded-support source distribution (uniform on a hypersphere) with a reflected flow noise generator, so that the support constraint is satisfied by construction rather than by a regularizer, eliminating the sensitive α hyperparameter that plagues FQL, IFQL, and DSRL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single set of hyperparameters across 40 tasks outperforms baselines with per-environment hand-tuned weights, removing a major source of fragility and tuning cost in offline RL. The theoretical results (KL is sufficient but not necessary for support; Wasserstein does not enforce support at all) cleanly justify why the support-constraint formulation is the right one. Open questions include whether the BC model's own OOD errors can be diagnosed or mitigated, whether adaptive or learned reflection terms could further improve the noise generator, and whether the approach extends to diffusion policies or other generative backbones. The method also inherits the BPTT cost of the noise generator and trains roughly 2× slower than FQL, which may matter at scale.
