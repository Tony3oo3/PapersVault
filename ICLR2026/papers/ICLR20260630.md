## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of continual learning either focus on converged models (missing intermediate training dynamics) or rely on restrictive assumptions like Gaussian data distributions and linear models. Moreover, bounding forgetting requires two-sided bounds on population risk for each task, which standard generalization theory does not provide. This paper addresses both gaps by deriving training-time bounds on forgetting and generalization for vanilla CL under the NTK regime.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of T classification tasks (15 Permuted/Rotated MNIST tasks at 28×28 grayscale, or 20 Split CIFAR-100 tasks at 32×32 RGB), where each task provides a batch of labeled images. Beyond standard class predictions, the theoretical framework outputs explicit upper bounds on forgetting F_{t_T} and generalization error G_{t_T} that are functions of the training iteration t_T, the loss Lipschitz constant ρ, and cross-task kernel norms. The algorithm additionally maintains a memory buffer of at most m=100 sampled examples per task and a set S of stored orthogonalized gradient vectors that are released after each task transition.

## Method

The paper derives closed-form kernel gradient flow solutions for vanilla CL under the NTK regime, then uses Rademacher complexity to obtain two-sided population risk bounds, yielding the first intermediate-stage upper bounds on both forgetting and generalization. Two key lemmas show that (i) reducing the loss Lipschitz constant ρ monotonically tightens both bounds, and (ii) zeroing the cross-task kernel K_k(X_τ, X_k) eliminates the dominant interference terms. Building on these insights, OGD+ redefines the projection subspace to span gradients from the last task evaluated on all prior samples (not just that task's own data), guaranteeing zero cross-task kernel between any task pair. OPGD further augments OGD+ with Gradient-norm Aware Minimization (GAM), which penalizes the maximal gradient norm within a local ball to reduce the Lipschitz constant, jointly tightening both forgetting and generalization bounds while preserving plasticity.

## Results

On the three standard CL benchmarks, OPGD achieves the highest average accuracy (ACC) across all datasets: 86.27% on Permuted MNIST (15 tasks), 89.15% on Rotated MNIST (15 tasks), and 68.17% on Split CIFAR-100 (20 tasks). For backward transfer (BWT, where higher/less-negative is better), OPGD records −7.73 on Permuted MNIST and −3.69 on Rotated MNIST, outperforming all baselines, while on Split CIFAR-100 it achieves −12.58. The paper reports average relative gains of +4.59% in ACC and +36.73% in BWT over the strongest prior method (OGD) across the three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OGD (Farajtabar et al., 2020), OPGD improves ACC by +4.10, +11.63, and +4.26 points on Permuted MNIST, Rotated MNIST, and Split CIFAR-100 respectively, and improves BWT by +4.65 and +14.74 on the two MNIST variants; however, OGD+ (the intermediate step) actually underperforms OGD on Split CIFAR-100 (61.84 vs. 63.91 ACC), which the authors attribute to excessive orthogonality reducing plasticity. Against GAM (Zhang et al., 2023), OPGD dominates on every metric across all three datasets, with the largest gap on Split CIFAR-100 (+6.47 ACC, +10.05 BWT). Against EWC (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017), OPGD wins on ACC for all datasets and on BWT for Permuted MNIST and Split CIFAR-100, though EWC achieves a slightly better BWT on Rotated MNIST (−2.33 vs. −3.69). Against MAS (Aljundi et al., 2018), OPGD wins on ACC across all benchmarks but MAS achieves substantially better BWT on Rotated MNIST (+1.60 vs. −3.69) and Split CIFAR-100 (−3.54 vs. −12.58). Against SI (Zenke et al., 2017), OPGD wins on ACC everywhere, while SI edges out OPGD on Permuted MNIST BWT (−3.36 vs. −7.73). Against ER (Chaudhry et al., 2019b) and A-GEM (Chaudhry et al., 2019a), OPGD achieves the best ACC on all three datasets and the best BWT on all three datasets, confirming that the orthogonal-plus-penalized gradient strategy outperforms both replay-based and constraint-based baselines in the main evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NTK-based theoretical analysis of continual learning initiated by Bennani et al. (2020), Doan et al. (2021), and Karakida & Akaho (2022), which were confined to converged models and often assumed ridge regularization. It departs from this line by deriving bounds at intermediate training stages for vanilla CL (no regularization) and by using Rademacher complexity to obtain two-sided population risk bounds, a technical requirement absent in prior work. On the algorithmic side, it refines OGD (Farajtabar et al., 2020) into OGD+ by projecting onto the orthogonal complement of all prior tasks' gradients (not just the immediately preceding task), and integrates Gradient-norm Aware Minimization (Zhang et al., 2023) to control the Lipschitz constant, yielding the OPGD algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled theoretical explanation for why gradient orthogonality and flatness minimization each independently reduce forgetting and improve generalization in CL, and shows that their combination (OPGD) yields substantial empirical gains (e.g., +36.7% BWT on Rotated MNIST). A key open question is whether the NTK regime's predictions transfer to finite-width, non-kernel networks, since the entire analysis relies on the infinite-width limit. The observed trade-off—longer training improves generalization but worsens forgetting—suggests that adaptive stopping or task-aware scheduling could be a productive direction. Extending the framework beyond classification benchmarks and to more complex architectures remains an important limitation.
