## Problem

Symbolic regression algorithms treat syntactically distinct but mathematically equivalent expressions (e.g., log(x₁²x₂³) vs. 2log(x₁)+3log(x₂)) as separate outputs, causing redundant exploration of the search space and slow convergence. No unified, scalable framework existed to embed symbolic equivalence into modern SR algorithms such as MCTS, DRL, and LLMs, since explicitly enumerating all equivalent variants grows exponentially with expression length.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset of (x, y) pairs, a context-free grammar (defining non-terminals, terminals, and production rules for constructing expressions), and a set of rewrite rules encoding known mathematical identities (e.g., log(ab)→log(a)+log(b), sin²(a)+cos²(a)→1). It outputs a closed-form symbolic expression with numerically fitted coefficients (via BFGS). The e-graph representation is key: rather than storing all equivalent variants explicitly, it shares common sub-expressions across e-classes, enabling compact encoding of up to 2^(n-1) equivalent forms with sub-exponential memory.

## Method

EGG-SR introduces an EGG module—an equality graph extended to grammar-based symbolic expressions—that compactly encodes the set of symbolically equivalent variants of any predicted expression via e-classes and e-nodes, built through equality saturation (iterative pattern matching and merging of rewrite rules). The module is integrated into three SR frameworks: (1) EGG-MCTS uses the e-graph as a transposition table to identify equivalent search-tree paths and jointly backpropagate rewards and visit counts, reducing the effective branching factor; (2) EGG-DRL revises the policy gradient estimator to aggregate log-probabilities over K equivalent sequences sharing the same reward, yielding a Rao-Blackwellized estimator with strictly lower variance; (3) EGG-LLM enriches the feedback prompt with multiple equivalent expressions extracted from the e-graph to guide subsequent LLM generations. Theoretically, EGG-MCTS achieves a tighter regret bound (κ_∞ ≤ κ) and EGG-DRL provides an unbiased, lower-variance gradient estimator compared to their standard counterparts.

## Results

On trigonometric datasets (Table 1), EGG-MCTS achieves median NMSE of <1E-6 on the two simplest noiseless settings (2,1,1) and (3,2,2), compared to 0.006 and 0.033 for standard MCTS, and reduces NMSE from 0.144 to 0.006 on the harder (4,4,6) noiseless setting. EGG-DRL similarly improves over DRL, e.g., reducing NMSE from 0.277 to 0.161 on (3,2,2) noiseless and from 14.44 to 5.67 on (5,5,5) noisy. On LLM benchmarks (Table 2), EGG-LLM(GPT-3.5) achieves NMSE of 0.0121 vs 0.0214 on Bacterial growth (IID) and 0.0419 vs 0.0516 on Stress-Strain (OOD), while EGG-LLM(Mistral) reduces OOD NMSE on Oscillation II from 0.0291 to 0.0114.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EGG-MCTS outperforms standard MCTS (Sun et al., ICLR 2023) on 7 of 8 trigonometric settings, with the largest gains on noiseless (4,4,6) where NMSE drops from 0.144 to 0.006 (24× improvement); the sole exception is noisy (3,2,2) where MCTS achieves 0.007 vs EGG-MCTS's 0.012. EGG-DRL beats standard DRL (Petersen et al., ICLR 2021) on 7 of 8 settings, with the most dramatic gain on noisy (5,5,5) (NMSE 5.67 vs 14.44), but loses on noisy (4,4,6) where DRL achieves 2.46 vs EGG-DRL's 5.09. EGG-LLM (GPT-3.5) consistently improves over LLM-SR (Shojaee et al., ICLR 2025) across all four scientific benchmarks, e.g., Bacterial growth IID NMSE 0.0121 vs 0.0214. With Mistral, EGG-LLM wins on Oscillation II and Stress-Strain but underperforms LLM-SR on Bacterial growth (IID 0.0101 vs 0.0026; OOD 0.0107 vs 0.0037), suggesting the benefit of equivalence-aware feedback is model- and task-dependent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: (1) e-graphs for compactly encoding symbolically equivalent expressions (Willsey et al., 2021; de França & Kronberger, 2023, 2025), (2) transposition tables in MCTS that share statistics across identical states (Childs et al., 2008; Leurent & Maillard, 2020), and (3) Rao-Blackwellization for variance reduction in policy gradients (Casella & Robert, 1996). Prior work applied e-graphs to genetic programming-based SR for deduplication and simplification, but no prior study integrated them simultaneously into MCTS, DRL, and LLM-based SR with matching theoretical guarantees. The key conceptual departure is treating the e-graph as a symbolic-equivalence-aware transposition table for MCTS and as a Rao-Blackwellizing sigma-field for DRL, rather than merely a simplification tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified EGG-SR framework gives SR practitioners a single, theoretically grounded mechanism to exploit mathematical identities across the three dominant modern SR paradigms, with provable regret and variance improvements. However, several open questions remain: domain constraints for rewrite rules (e.g., log validity) are not systematically enforced, the LLM component lacks theoretical analysis, and the approach has not yet been extended to SymNet-based or GP-based SR where most current scientific publications operate. The practical impact is further limited by the fact that the rewrite-rule set must be hand-curated per operator family, and the e-graph construction cost grows with the number of applicable identities.
