## Problem

Existing layer pruning methods for LLMs assign static importance scores based on heuristics such as weight magnitudes, activation norms, or sensitivity analysis, implicitly assuming each layer's contribution is independent of the others. The paper demonstrates that layer importance is context-dependent: pruning one layer shifts the relative importance of remaining layers, and evaluating layers in isolation leads to suboptimal pruning decisions, particularly under multi-layer removal. This work addresses the gap by explicitly modeling inter-layer dependencies through a cooperative game-theoretic formulation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM (Transformer or non-Transformer, 32–64 layers) and a small calibration set (10 BookCorpus prompts of up to 128 tokens) as input, and produces a pruned model with specific layers removed along with approximate Shapley value scores for each layer. Internally, the method operates on binary masks of length L (one bit per layer indicating retention), and the surrogate network maps these masks to a scalar performance score defined as the ratio of original to pruned perplexity. The Hamming weight (number of retained layers) is stratified across predefined levels matched to the target pruning ratio.

## Method

The paper formulates layer pruning as a cooperative game where each Transformer layer is a player and model perplexity on calibration data defines the utility, with Shapley values quantifying each layer's marginal contribution to the coalition. Since exact Shapley computation requires evaluating 2^L subsets (infeasible for L ≥ 32), a two-stage approximation is employed: Stage 1 generates ~8,000 diverse pruning masks via stratified Monte Carlo sampling with controlled Hamming weights and evaluates each masked model on calibration data to obtain PPL-based scores; Stage 2 trains a lightweight two-layer feed-forward surrogate (CELU hidden layer at 2× input width, sigmoid output) on these mask–score pairs to predict performance for unseen masks. The trained surrogate then enables efficient Monte Carlo estimation of Shapley values over 80,000 candidate masks without repeated full-model forward passes, reducing computation from ~320 hours to ~30 minutes on a single V100 GPU. Layers are ranked by their estimated Shapley values and the lowest-contributing layers are removed sequentially to reach the target compression ratio, with the Hamming weight stratification ensuring balanced coverage across pruning ratios and preventing bias toward extreme sparsity.

## Results

On LLaMA-2-7B-hf, the proposed method achieves PPL of 38.12/105.24/28.77 (WikiText2/PTB/C4) when removing 12 layers, substantially outperforming SLEB (58.12/135.13/43.87) and ShortGPT (157.99/295.15/98.86). On Meta-LLaMA-3-8B at 9 layers removed, the method yields PPL 45.26/70.11/33.12 while Shortened-LLaMA catastrophically degrades to 5928/9774/2049. On zero-shot benchmarks at 5.5B parameters, the method achieves 0.5227 average accuracy across 8 tasks, surpassing SLEB (0.5138), ShortGPT (0.5050), and SliceGPT (0.3865).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method consistently outperforms all four depth-wise baselines (SliceGPT, SLEB, ShortGPT, Shortened-LLaMA) across all three evaluated Transformer models and all pruning ratios. Against SLEB, the closest competitor, the advantage grows with pruning depth: at 12 layers removed on LLaMA-2-7B, Ours achieves PPL 38.12 vs. SLEB's 58.12 on WikiText2 (a 34% reduction). Against ShortGPT and Shortened-LLaMA, the gap is dramatic under aggressive pruning—on Meta-LLaMA-3-8B at 9 layers removed, Shortened-LLaMA's PPL explodes to 5928 while Ours maintains 45.26. SliceGPT is uniformly the weakest baseline, with PPL values 3–15× higher than Ours at every pruning level. On zero-shot tasks, Ours leads SLEB by 0.9% average accuracy at 5.5B and by 1.4% at 4.3B, while SliceGPT lags by 13–14%. Against width-wise baselines (Wanda-sp, FLAP, LLM-Pruner), Ours achieves lower PPL at matched parameter counts while also delivering superior throughput and latency, since depth pruning removes entire layers rather than reducing hidden dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in LLM depth pruning (ShortGPT, SLEB, Shortened-LLaMA, SliceGPT), which assigns static, layer-independent importance scores via heuristics like block influence or PPL sensitivity. It draws on cooperative game theory—specifically Shapley values—as a principled alternative for quantifying inter-layer dependencies, building on the GTAP framework (Diaz-Ortiz Jr et al., 2023) that applied power indices to neuron-level pruning, and on Zhang et al. (2024) and Sun et al. (2025) who identified Shapley-based pruning as theoretically ideal but computationally infeasible for LLMs. The key methodological contribution is a two-stage approximation (stratified Monte Carlo mask sampling + a lightweight surrogate network) that makes Shapley estimation tractable at LLM scale, a gap none of the prior game-theoretic or heuristic pruning works addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly modeling inter-layer dependencies rather than assuming fixed importance rankings, this framework offers a more principled path to depth pruning that consistently outperforms heuristic baselines across Transformer and non-Transformer architectures (RWKV, Mamba) and integrates cleanly with post-training quantization. The practical overhead (~30 minutes on a single V100) makes it feasible for real deployment pipelines. Open questions include whether the surrogate generalizes reliably to pruning ratios far from the training distribution, how the method scales to 100B+ parameter models with hundreds of layers, and whether the calibration-data sensitivity observed in ablations (particularly under aggressive pruning) limits robustness in low-data regimes.
