## Problem

Diffusion models suffer from severe overfitting when trained on limited data, exhibiting U-shaped FID curves where performance degrades after an initial improvement. Existing remedies—fine-tuning pretrained backbones or static data augmentation—either depend on source-target distributional similarity or risk pushing the training distribution away from the true data distribution (augmentation leakage). The paper asks whether the adaptive support-expansion principle of Wasserstein Distributionally Robust Optimization (WDRO) can be transplanted into diffusion training to mitigate overfitting without external pretraining.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes random Gaussian noise (and optionally a class label for conditional generation) and produces synthetic RGB images at resolutions ranging from 32×32 (CIFAR-10) to 256×256 (LSUN-Church, few-shot datasets). During training, the method consumes a small set of real images (as few as 100) and iteratively generates adversarial perturbations of those images to form an augmented training mixture, so the effective input to the score network at each step is a blend of original and worst-case samples drawn from a Wasserstein ball around the empirical distribution.

## Method

WILD-Diffusion reformulates the diffusion training objective as a min-max problem over a Wasserstein uncertainty set centered at the limited data distribution, then applies the strong-duality result of Gao & Kleywegt (2023) to convert the intractable inner maximization into a tractable surrogate loss φ_γ(θ; x, t) = sup_{x'} {ℓ(θ; x', t) − γ·c(x', x)}. A 'Bi-level Interval Update' strategy alternates between (i) standard SGD parameter updates on a mixture of original and adversarial samples at every step, and (ii) periodic (every m epochs) regeneration of worst-case samples via K steps of gradient ascent on the surrogate loss, which acts as a soft, distribution-level constraint governed by the penalty γ. A warmup phase (20% of total epochs) trains on the limited data alone before adversarial samples are introduced. The paper further establishes a convergence guarantee (Theorem 3.6) by proving an upper bound on the worst-case objective (Lemma 3.5) and bounding the total-variation distance between the generated and target distributions, extending the standard diffusion convergence analysis of Lee et al. (2022) to the distributionally robust setting.

## Results

On low-resolution benchmarks (CIFAR-10, FFHQ, CelebA-HQ), WILD-Diffusion consistently reduces FID by 10–16% over the corresponding EDM baseline when only 20% of training data is used (e.g., 7.89 vs. 9.38 FID on FFHQ with EDM-NCSN++). Gains diminish as data increases, dropping to ~1–4% at 100% data. In the few-shot (100-image) setting, WILD-Diffusion achieves the best FID across all four datasets in both pretrained (12.54 on Obama) and non-pretrained (34.52 on Obama) settings, outperforming the strongest prior method LD-Diffusion by at least 0.46 FID.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WILD-Diffusion is a plug-and-play wrapper that consistently improves every diffusion baseline it is applied to: EDM-DDPM++ (12.14 vs. 13.91 FID on CIFAR-10 20%), EDM-NCSN++ (7.89 vs. 9.38 on FFHQ 20%), Patch Diffusion (11.78 vs. 12.53 on CIFAR-10 20%), and DeepCache (13.96 vs. 15.33 on CIFAR-10 20%). Against GAN-based methods, WILD-Diffusion substantially outperforms BigGAN (12.14 vs. 21.58), StyleGAN-v2 (12.14 vs. 23.08), CR-BigGAN (12.14 vs. 20.62), and DiffAugment (12.14 vs. 14.04) on CIFAR-10 at 20% data. In the few-shot (100-image) regime with pretraining, WILD-Diffusion edges out the strongest diffusion baseline LD-Diffusion (12.54 vs. 13.00 FID on Obama; 12.21 vs. 12.48 on Dog) and clearly surpasses LPDM-8 (12.54 vs. 14.27 on Obama). Without pretraining, WILD-Diffusion beats Patch Diffusion (34.52 vs. 41.47 on Obama) and all GAN-based few-shot methods (MineGAN 50.63, TransferGAN 48.73, FreezeD 41.87, DiffAugment 46.87). The method also outperforms the DRO-for-diffusion work of Wang et al. (2025a) on CIFAR-10 (12.14 vs. 13.91 at 20% data), though that work targets a different problem (training/sampling distribution mismatch rather than limited-data overfitting).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the Wasserstein Distributionally Robust Optimization (WDRO) framework—originally developed for robust classification and decision-making (Gao & Kleywegt, 2023; Sinha et al., 2018)—into the diffusion training loop, using the surrogate loss reformulation (Blanchet & Murthy, 2019) to make the inner worst-case maximization tractable. It departs from prior limited-data diffusion work (DreamBooth, LPDM-8, LD-Diffusion), which relies on fine-tuning pretrained backbones and thus inherits source-target distributional assumptions, by instead expanding the support of the training distribution from scratch via adversarial sample generation. The convergence analysis directly extends Lee et al. (2022) by proving an upper bound on the worst-case objective (Lemma 3.5) that accounts for the additional WDRO layer, yielding a total-variation guarantee that recovers the standard diffusion result as ρ→0.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WILD-Diffusion offers a principled, plug-and-play alternative to ad-hoc augmentation for limited-data diffusion training, with the practical advantage of being orthogonal to existing efficiency techniques (Patch Diffusion, DeepCache) and applicable across architectures (DDPM++, NCSN++, ADM). The convergence guarantee, while relying on standard log-Sobolev and Lipschitz assumptions, is the first to cover the WDRO-augmented diffusion objective, giving theoretical grounding that purely empirical augmentation methods lack. Open questions include how the fixed interval-update schedule (every m epochs) interacts with very large-scale training, whether the penalty parameter γ can be adapted dynamically, and whether the approach extends naturally to conditional or multi-modal generation settings where the Wasserstein ball geometry becomes more complex.
