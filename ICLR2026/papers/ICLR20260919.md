## Problem

Standard autoregressive language models generate token-by-token from a fixed vocabulary, inducing a tree-structured state space that restricts each sentence to a single compositional path. Dynamic-vocabulary methods (e.g., CoG, GDV) introduce variable-length spans but fail to explicitly model the DAG structure that arises when the same sentence can be composed via different span sequences, leaving exploration biased toward a single path. Prior GFlowNets-based language models operate at the token level and remain confined to tree-structured spaces, never leveraging GFlowNets' core advantage in DAG exploration.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prefix (up to ~32 tokens) and a retrieval corpus, from which it retrieves top-k documents (k=1024 at inference, k=128 during training) via DPR+FAISS and extracts all substrings of 2–8 tokens as candidate spans, forming a per-prefix dynamic vocabulary alongside the base word-level vocabulary and a terminal symbol. For text generation, the output is a continuation string assembled by sequentially appending sampled spans; for knowledge-intensive QA, the model computes the likelihood of each candidate answer option by summing over all generation paths via dynamic programming and selects the highest-probability option.

## Method

FoSS constructs a DAG-structured state space by applying a DAG-Inducing Probabilistic Span Segmentation algorithm that modifies forward maximum matching with a stochastic early-stopping mechanism, producing multiple overlapping segmentation trajectories per training document so that different span paths share common sub-sequences. The forward policy is a span language model with a causal-attention prefix encoder and a bidirectional-transformer span encoder (for multi-word phrases) plus standard token embeddings (for single words and the terminal action), computing action probabilities via inner-product scoring over the dynamic vocabulary. Training uses the subtrajectory balance objective with a hybrid online-offline strategy: trajectories are drawn from the policy, a reward-prioritized replay buffer, and the segmented training set, with a Bernoulli mixing probability controlling the online/offline ratio. The reward function combines a language-model likelihood (fluency) and a Bradley-Terry preference model score (human-likeness) with a tunable trade-off parameter α, and the backward policy is a uniform distribution over all valid suffixes in the dynamic vocabulary.

## Results

On WikiText-103 (in-domain), FoSS achieves a MAUVE score of 31.65 (nucleus) and 30.78 (greedy), improving by 5.51 over CoG and 8.22 over the fine-tuned Transformer under nucleus sampling. On the out-of-domain Law-MT benchmark, FoSS reaches 32.17 MAUVE (nucleus), outperforming the Law-MT fine-tuned Transformer by 6.96. On knowledge-intensive tasks, FoSS improves accuracy over the fine-tuned Transformer by up to 3.5% (e.g., 26.20% vs. 22.71% on OpenBookQA). GPT-4 pairwise evaluation confirms FoSS is preferred over all baselines, with 74–75% win rates against CoG and kNN-LM in the out-of-domain setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FoSS consistently outperforms all six baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the strongest span-based baseline CoG, FoSS gains 5.51 MAUVE (nucleus, in-domain) and 4.03 (nucleus, out-of-domain), with the gap widening to 6.82 under the En-Wiki scaling setting. Compared to GFlowNets-FT (the closest GFlowNets-based baseline operating at the token level), FoSS improves by 2.04 MAUVE in-domain and 3.68 out-of-domain, demonstrating the benefit of the DAG-structured span state space over the tree-structured token space. FoSS also surpasses the fully fine-tuned Transformer by 8.22 MAUVE in-domain and 6.96 out-of-domain, and beats kNN-LM and RETRO by margins of 9+ MAUVE points in most settings. On knowledge-intensive tasks (Table 3), FoSS achieves the best or near-best accuracy across all five benchmarks, with the largest gain of 3.49 points over the fine-tuned Transformer on OpenBookQA (26.20% vs. 22.71%). GPT-4 pairwise evaluation (Table 2) confirms these quantitative gains align with human preferences, with FoSS preferred in 53–75% of comparisons depending on the baseline and setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: dynamic-vocabulary span-based generation (CoG, GDV, kNN-LM) and Generative Flow Networks (GFlowNets). Prior GFlowNets-based language models (Hu et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2025) operate at the token level, yielding tree-structured state spaces that reduce GFlowNets to a degraded form of discrete soft Q-learning. Conversely, span-based models like CoG implicitly induce a DAG (multiple segmentation paths to the same state) but never explicitly model or exploit this structure. FoSS is the first to combine these threads, using a probabilistic span segmentation algorithm to construct an explicit DAG and training with subtrajectory balance (Madan et al., 2023) to navigate it, while adapting CoG's prefix-encoder + phrase-encoder architecture as the forward policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that explicitly modeling the DAG structure of span composition yields consistent gains over both tree-structured GFlowNets and standard span-based models, with the ablation showing a ~10% relative MAUVE drop when the DAG is removed. The out-of-domain result—FoSS outperforming a fully fine-tuned Transformer on Law-MT without any domain-specific training—suggests the GFlowNets framework provides genuine generalization benefits beyond memorization. However, the evaluation is confined to GPT-2-scale models and relatively small corpora (WikiText-103, Law-MT), and the single-retrieval-per-prefix design trades off diversity against kNN-LM-style per-step retrieval. Open questions include whether the DAG advantage persists at LLM scale, whether richer retrieval strategies can close the diversity gap, and whether the framework extends to tasks beyond open-ended continuation.
