## Problem

Existing gradient-based solver distillation methods (LD3, S4S) for accelerating diffusion ODE sampling suffer from unstable loss scales, limited parameter spaces, and complex training schemes that fail to preserve fine-grained image details. These approaches also lack a principled way to incorporate theoretical solver knowledge into the trainable parameterization, leading to slow convergence and suboptimal quality in low-NFE regimes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261358/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes Gaussian noise (plus optional text prompts for Stable Diffusion or class labels for ImageNet) as input and produces generated images at resolutions ranging from 32×32 (CIFAR-10) to 512×512 (MS-COCO via Stable Diffusion). It operates as a drop-in solver replacement: the frozen pre-trained diffusion model is queried at learned timesteps, and the student solver uses 4–10 NFE versus 10–20 NFE for the teacher, with no architectural changes to the underlying model. In latent-space settings, the discriminator operates on 64×64 latent representations rather than pixel space.

## Method

The Generalized Solver (GS) parameterizes each ODE step as a weighted sum over all previously computed points and velocity directions (not just the most recent K), with coefficients decomposed into fixed theoretical values from DPM-Solver++(3M) plus trainable additive corrections initialized to zero. The timestep schedule is learned via a stick-breaking (cumprod) transformation, and decoupled evaluation timesteps allow the model to be queried at slightly shifted times. The Generalized Adversarial Solver (GAS) augments the standard distillation loss (LPIPS in pixel space, L1 in latent space) with an R3GAN relativistic adversarial loss, where the discriminator compares student outputs against teacher-generated images from independent noise samples. This adversarial component specifically targets artifact removal and detail fidelity in low-NFE settings where pure regression distillation produces visible degradation despite low LPIPS/L1 values.

## Results

GAS achieves the best FID across all six evaluated datasets and NFE settings (4–10). On FFHQ (64×64), GAS reaches FID 7.86 at NFE=4 and 3.79 at NFE=6, compared to 10.63 and 4.62 for the strongest prior method S4S Alt. On latent-space ImageNet-256, GAS achieves FID 5.38 at NFE=4 (vs. 5.13 for S4S† which uses different teacher hyperparameters), and on MS-COCO (512×512, Stable Diffusion) it reaches FID 14.71 at NFE=4. The teacher models achieve FID 2.03 (CIFAR10), 2.60 (FFHQ), 4.10 (ImageNet), and 14.10 (MS-COCO), showing GAS closes most of the quality gap with only 4–6 function evaluations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAS outperforms all seven baseline categories across every dataset and NFE setting. Against standard solvers (DPM++(3M), UniPC(3M), iPNDM(3M)), GAS achieves 5–10× lower FID at NFE=4 (e.g., 7.86 vs. 46.14 on FFHQ). Against timestep-optimization methods (GITS, DMN), GAS is consistently superior, with the gap most pronounced at low NFE (e.g., 7.86 vs. 21.38 for UniPC[GITS] on FFHQ at NFE=4). Against the most direct competitors in solver/timestep training—LD3 and S4S—GAS wins on all datasets: on FFHQ at NFE=4, GAS achieves 7.86 vs. 17.96 (LD3) and 10.63 (S4S Alt); on ImageNet-256 at NFE=4, GAS reaches 5.38 vs. 9.19 (LD3). The only case where a baseline is competitive is S4S† on ImageNet (5.13 vs. 5.38), but this uses significantly different teacher hyperparameters and is flagged as not fairly comparable. The ablation in Table 3 further shows that the GS parameterization alone (without adversarial loss) already outperforms S4S by a large margin (e.g., FID 4.39 vs. 31.44 on CIFAR-10 at NFE=4), confirming that the theoretical-guidance parameterization is the primary driver of improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits squarely in the solver-distillation lineage initiated by LD3 (Tong et al., 2024) and S4S (Frankel et al., 2025), which frame ODE solver acceleration as gradient-based optimization of a lightweight student sampler against a high-NFE teacher. The key conceptual departure is the additive parameterization: rather than learning solver coefficients from scratch or scaling them multiplicatively (as in S4S), GAS uses a pre-defined theoretical solver (DPM-Solver++) as a fixed backbone and trains only small additive corrections, which dramatically stabilizes training. The second departure is the incorporation of an R3GAN relativistic adversarial loss on top of the standard distillation objective, borrowing from the GAN literature to address fine-grained detail preservation that regression losses alone cannot capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, low-parameter recipe for reducing diffusion model inference from ~20 NFE to 4–6 NFE with FID scores close to the teacher, requiring only hours of training on a single GPU and a small dataset (as few as 1,400–5,000 teacher-generated samples). A key open question is scalability: the method requires backpropagation through the entire solver trajectory, which may become prohibitive for video generation or very large models. Additionally, the paper acknowledges that separate training may be needed for each target NFE, and performance degrades at NFE=1–2 where the parameter space is too constrained. The finding that adversarial loss can improve FID while worsening the regression loss (LPIPS/L1) opens an interesting question about the relationship between pixel-level fidelity and distributional quality in few-step generation.
