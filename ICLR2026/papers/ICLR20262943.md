## Problem

Synthesizing photorealistic, physically plausible fire in in-the-wild 3D scenes remains unsolved: CFD and VFX pipelines require handcrafted geometry and expert tuning, producing a pronounced sim-to-real gap; large video models generate visually appealing flames but lack physical consistency and precise controllability; and commercial software merely overlays pre-stored effects. No existing framework automatically aligns combustion simulation with real scene geometry and materials while supporting fine-grained user control over ignition, intensity, and airflow.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes multi-view RGB images (captured with an iPhone or standard cameras) and user-specified ignition location plus optional fire parameters (buoyancy coefficient α, reaction rate k, wind vector, thermal diffusivity β, charring rate ε_c). It produces a temporally coherent fire video at 256³ simulation resolution, rendered at 2.37 s/frame on an RTX 4090D, combining Planck-law fire emission, material-dependent smoke color (e.g., white for wood, black for plastic), progressive surface charring, and Phong illumination on the 3DGS background. An optional Wan2.1 diffusion refinement pass can be applied to enhance high-frequency lighting details, though it may introduce minor temporal inconsistencies.

## Method

FieryGS tightly couples three modules within a 3DGS pipeline. First, PGSR reconstructs the scene, then 3DGS segmentation (SAM-guided contrastive learning + HDBSCAN clustering) partitions Gaussians into material regions, and GPT-4o performs zero-shot combustion property reasoning (material type, burnability, thermal diffusivity, smoke color) from the best-visibility viewpoint, costing ~$0.55 per scene. Second, a volumetric combustion simulation on a 256³ grid solves incompressible Navier-Stokes with buoyancy and vorticity confinement forces, a reaction coordinate Y for flame propagation, and a simplified heat-transfer equation for surface charring, all implemented in Taichi with semi-Lagrangian advection and Gauss-Seidel pressure projection. Third, a unified volumetric renderer accumulates Planck blackbody emission for fire, material-specific smoke scattering, 3DGS radiance with progressive charring dimming, and spectral Phong illumination from hot voxels, followed by ACES tone mapping—producing a single composited image per frame without separate compositing passes.

## Results

On 6 real-world scenes (4 custom-captured, MipNeRF360 Garden, Tanks and Temples Playground), FieryGS achieves the highest Aesthetic Quality (0.624) and Imaging Quality (0.702) scores from VBench, while attaining the lowest DINO Structure Score (0.38) among all methods, indicating superior visual fidelity with the best scene-structure preservation. In user studies (86 and 88 participants), FieryGS is preferred over all baselines in 63–89% of pairwise comparisons for both perceptual realism and physical plausibility, across both image and video modalities. The average simulation-and-rendering runtime is 2.37 s/frame on an RTX 4090D with peak GPU memory below 10 GB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FieryGS outperforms all three baselines on every quantitative metric. Against AutoVFX (Blender-physics-driven VFX), FieryGS leads by a wide margin on Aesthetic Quality (0.624 vs. 0.488), Imaging Quality (0.702 vs. 0.603), and DINO Structure (0.38 vs. 1.04), with user preference at 77.8–88.9%. Against Runway-V2V (commercial video-to-video diffusion), FieryGS edges out on Aesthetic Quality (0.624 vs. 0.605) and substantially on DINO Structure (0.38 vs. 0.68), while Imaging Quality is essentially tied (0.702 vs. 0.701); user preference is 66.5–85.3%, with the video gap being the smallest (66.5% perceptual realism), reflecting Runway's visually appealing but structurally altered outputs. Against Instruct-GS2GS (text-driven static 3DGS editing), FieryGS dominates across the board (Aesthetic 0.624 vs. 0.451, Imaging 0.702 vs. 0.394, DINO 0.38 vs. 0.66), with user preference at 63.0–85.5%, as Instruct-GS2GS produces only static, low-fidelity edits without temporal dynamics. Qualitatively, Runway-V2V significantly alters scene geometry (e.g., transforming a plate into a groove), AutoVFX yields unconvincing flames in complex indoor settings, and Instruct-GS2GS cannot localize or animate fire, whereas FieryGS preserves scene structure while capturing ignition, flame spread, carbonization, and illumination.

## Relation to Prior Work

FieryGS sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) 3DGS-based scene reconstruction and segmentation (PGSR, SAGA, Gaussian Grouping), (2) MLLM-driven physical property inference (GaussianProperty, PUGS, NeRF2Physics), and (3) physics-based combustion simulation and volumetric fire rendering (Nguyen et al. 2002, Nielsen et al. 2022, Liu et al. 2024a). The closest prior work, AutoVFX, uses Blender's physics engine with LLM-generated scripts for flame effects in 3DGS, but lacks spatiotemporal physical coupling and fine-grained parameter control. FieryGS departs from all prior approaches by tightly integrating zero-shot MLLM material reasoning, a custom Taichi-based volumetric combustion solver, and a unified fire+smoke+3DGS renderer into a single automated pipeline, eliminating the manual geometry modeling and expert tuning that characterizes CFD/VFX workflows.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to produce physically grounded, user-controllable fire synthesis directly from multi-view images of real scenes without handcrafted geometry or expert parameter tuning, lowering the barrier for applications in AR/VR, virtual fire drills, and film production. Key limitations include the absence of mass loss and structural degradation modeling, restriction to object-level (not large-scale) scenes, and sensitivity to non-uniform 3DGS point distribution and material misclassification under occlusion. Open questions include whether the MLLM reasoning step can be replaced by learned per-scene material fields, how to extend the simulation to building-scale conflagrations, and whether the optional generative refinement (Wan2.1) can be made temporally and 3D-consistent without altering background content.
