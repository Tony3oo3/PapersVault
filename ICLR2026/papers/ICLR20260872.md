## Problem

Existing transformer attribution methods predominantly target MLP neurons and study relatively simple concepts such as factual associations or syntax, while largely overlooking the attention mechanism's role in concept encoding. No unified, concept-agnostic pipeline exists that can map arbitrary, complex concepts to specific model components in a scalable, automated manner. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a method that attributes concepts to attention heads and pairs it with a minimal intervention strategy for controlling concept strength.

## Input / Output

The method takes a concept vector (constructed via SAE decoder features, difference-in-means over positive/negative prompt sets, or unembedding matrix rows for vision labels) and a positive reference dataset of roughly 100–200 examples, and produces a sparse module of 3–10 attention heads selected from the full H×L grid of the model. For intervention, a single scalar s is applied to scale the residual-stream contributions of the identified heads during the forward pass, modifying model outputs without altering any stored weights. The only modality-specific difference is the token position used: the last token for LLMs versus the [CLS] token for ViTs.

## Method

SAMD scores each attention head (l, h) by computing the average cosine similarity between its residual-stream contribution a_{l,h}(p) and the concept vector v_c across the positive dataset, then selects the TopK highest-scoring heads as the concept-associated module; this requires only a single forward pass per input and is agnostic to how the concept vector was constructed. SAMI intervenes by multiplying the magnitude of the module heads' contributions by a scalar s in the residual stream update (equivalent to scaling specific rows of the multi-head attention output projection matrices), with s > 1 amplifying and s < 1 diminishing the concept. The key insight is that cosine similarity between a head's linear contribution and a concept vector provides a reliable, computationally cheap indicator of that head's functional relevance, and that only a remarkably sparse set of 3–10 heads out of hundreds is sufficient to capture a concept. The discovered modules are stable before and after LLM post-training, generalize across languages for multilingual concepts, and transfer from LLMs to ViTs without architectural changes.

## Results

On HarmBench, the Safety Module negative intervention achieves attack success rates of 71.1% (Llama-2-7B), 78.0% (Qwen-7B), and 84.3% (Gemma-7B), substantially outperforming both GCG and ORTHO baselines on two of three models. On GSM8K, amplifying the reasoning module improves Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct from 84.61% to 85.44% and Gemma-7B-Base from 54.36% to 56.71%, with gains generalizing to the MATH benchmark (+0.80/+0.58). On FQuAD, SAMI reduces the French response ratio from 85.35% to 1.66%, beating the best SAE vector steering result of 3.98%. On ImageNet-1K, suppressing the recognition module in ViT-B/32 drives target-label accuracy to 0% while preserving generalization on other labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GCG (Zou et al., 2023), the Safety Module intervention achieves a 36.6-point higher ASR on Llama-2-7B (71.1% vs. 34.5%) and a 30.8-point gain on Gemma-7B (84.3% vs. 53.5%), but trails GCG by 1.5 points on Qwen-7B (78.0% vs. 79.5%). Against ORTHO (Arditi et al., 2024), SAMI wins decisively on Llama-2-7B (71.1% vs. 22.6%) and Gemma-7B (84.3% vs. 73.0%), while again slightly underperforming on Qwen-7B (78.0% vs. 79.2%). Against SAE vector steering (Lieberum et al., 2024) on the FQuAD multilingual task, SAMI with a single fixed scalar s=−1 reduces the French response ratio to 1.66%, outperforming the best SAE coefficient (3.98%) that required an extensive grid search. Notably, SAMI is prompt-agnostic and compute-efficient, requiring no per-prompt optimization as GCG does, and only modifies ~0.1% of model weights.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the residual stream decomposition framework (Elhage et al., 2021) by explicitly separating individual attention head contributions and comparing them via cosine similarity to concept vectors, adapting the logit-lens intuition (nostalgebraist, 2020) from token-level to concept-level attribution. It departs from the dominant neuron/MLP attribution paradigm (Meng et al., Geva et al.) by targeting attention heads instead, and from prior attention-head studies (induction heads, successor prediction) by handling arbitrary, complex concepts rather than narrowly defined behaviors. The intervention strategy (SAMI) contrasts with vector-steering and MLP-editing approaches by requiring only a single scalar multiplier on the identified heads' outputs, avoiding pre-computed vectors or weight modifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unified, concept-agnostic pipeline for attention head attribution that scales across modalities (LLMs and ViTs) and concept types (SAE features, reasoning, safety, image labels), demonstrating that knowledge is sparsely encoded in just 3–10 heads per concept. The practical implications are notable: a single scalar on ~0.1% of weights enables jailbreaking (+72.7% ASR), modest reasoning gains (+1.6% GSM8K), and targeted image-classification suppression. Key limitations include the absence of causal validation (correlation-based attribution may be over- or under-complete), no theoretical sample-complexity or stability guarantees, and the observation that negative interventions can produce untruthful outputs, suggesting the discovered modules may be polysemantic. Open questions include whether finer-grained causal analysis would refine the modules and whether the approach extends to deeper circuit-level understanding.
