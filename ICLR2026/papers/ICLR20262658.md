## Problem

Existing tools for monitoring and steering LLM activations rely on probes that output a single token or scalar, severely limiting the range of model behaviors they can detect or control. No prior method can answer open-ended, natural-language questions about what a model's internal representations encode, nor can it steer those representations using a natural-language specification of desired behavior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262658/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For reading, the system takes activations from a specific layer (k=15) of the target LLM (typically ~50 tokens × 4096 dimensions) along with a free-form natural-language question, and produces a natural-language answer by greedy decoding from the decoder. For control, it additionally takes a QA pair expressing the desired behavior (e.g., 'Q: How will the model speak? A: Like a pirate') and produces a gradient with respect to the activations, which is backpropagated through the target LLM's LoRA parameters to modify its weights rather than its activations at inference time.

## Method

Latent Interpretation Tuning (LIT) fine-tunes a decoder LLM—initialized as an exact copy of the target LLM—on a curated LATENTQA dataset of (activations, question, answer) triples. The dataset is generated by prepending diverse control prompts (goals, personas, extractive QA) to stimulus prompts, capturing target-LLM activations from the stimulus, and using a capable LLM (o1-preview) to write descriptive and reasoning-based QA pairs about the resulting completions. Three design decisions ensure generalization: masking control-token activations to prevent shortcut learning, training on three data types (control, stimulus, stimulus+completion) for coverage, and using a stronger LLM to generate faithful completions. During training, activations are patched into layer 0 of the decoder while the decoder is trained with LoRA (rank 32) to maximize the log-probability of the answer given the patched activations plus the question. For control, the STEER operation computes the gradient of the decoder's cross-entropy loss on the QA pair with respect to the activations, then backpropagates through the target LLM's LoRA adapters at layers 0 through k, enabling natural-language-specified steering without task-specific data.

## Results

On the feature extraction task (Table 1), LIT achieves an average accuracy of 70.4% across 6 relational tasks on Llama-3-8B-Instruct, outperforming linear probes (43.2%) by 32.2% absolute and Patchscope (32.2%) by 38.2% absolute. On the hidden system prompt task, LIT outperforms GPT-4 prompting by 18.7% absolute on the hard setting (scientists) and 2.7% on the easy setting, and outperforms SelfIE by 77.3% and 76% respectively. On the CrowS Pairs debiasing task, LIT is the only method that statistically significantly reduces bias, lowering the mean log-likelihood difference from 4.05 to 3.70 and percent stereotype from 64.3% to 60.9%, while all other baselines either fail to improve or worsen the metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIT decisively outperforms Patchscope (Ghandeharioun et al., 2024) on feature extraction by 38.2% absolute accuracy across all 6 relational tasks, demonstrating that training a decoder on LATENTQA data is far more effective than the untrained activation-patching approach. Against SelfIE (Chen et al., 2024), LIT achieves a 77.3% absolute improvement on the hard hidden-system-prompt task (scientists) and 76% on the easy task, confirming that the distribution shift between embeddings and latents makes untrained patching brittle. On the CrowS Pairs debiasing task, LIT is the only method that statistically significantly reduces bias (3.70 vs. 4.05 baseline log-likelihood difference), while RepE (Zou et al., 2023) actually increases the difference to 4.38, and DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) achieves only a marginal, non-significant reduction to 3.82. In the sentiment generation experiment, LIT achieves the highest diversity (Dist-3 of 0.73 positive, 0.68 negative) and competitive sentiment scores, outperforming DExperts (Liu et al., 2021) which suffers from severe diversity collapse (Dist-3 of 0.20 and 0.17). Overall, LIT wins on every primary comparison, with the largest margins against the untrained LATENTQA baselines (Patchscope, SelfIE) and the only statistically significant improvement over all baselines on the debiasing task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on decoding LLM activations into natural language, building directly on SelfIE (Chen et al., 2024a) and Patchscopes (Ghandeharioun et al., 2024a), which patch activations into a copy of the LLM without any training. The key departure is introducing Latent Interpretation Tuning (LIT), which trains a decoder LLM on a curated dataset of activation-QA pairs, addressing the distribution shift that makes untrained patching brittle. The data curation pipeline draws inspiration from Visual Instruction Tuning (Liu et al., 2023), adapting the image-to-instruction conversion paradigm to the activation-to-QA setting. For control, the paper positions itself against RepE (Zou et al., 2023) and token-level methods (SFT, DPO), arguing that natural-language-specified activation gradients can handle nonlinear concepts that linear steering vectors cannot.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LATENTQA provides a unified, expressive interface for both reading and writing LLM internals in natural language, replacing the limited scalar/single-token probes that dominate current interpretability tooling. The scaling results (30%→100% accuracy from 1B to 8B; consistent loss reduction with more data) suggest a clear path to more capable interpretability systems, and the ability to elicit behaviors unseen in training (Golden Gate Claude, harmful knowledge) indicates the decoder captures genuine latent structure rather than memorizing patterns. Key limitations include the small training set (16,732 points across only 3 control types), the absence of ground truth for latents (risking hallucination), and the fact that control experiments use small evaluation sets (30 prompts for harmful elicitation). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more diverse control types, whether the 16K-point dataset is sufficient for production-grade interpretability, and whether the activation-level control that bypasses safety training raises new alignment concerns.
