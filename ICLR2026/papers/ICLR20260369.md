## Problem

Learned optimizers like VeLO require exorbitant meta-training compute (4000 TPU months, ~10× GPT-3) yet still fail to generalize beyond ~600M parameter tasks, making them impractical for billion-scale pretraining. The paper asks whether a performant, general-purpose learned update rule can be meta-trained on a tiny fraction of that compute while scaling stably to billion-parameter models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260369/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

At each step, Celo2 consumes the current parameter tensor, its gradient, a user-specified learning rate, and rolling optimizer state (three momentum accumulators with β∈{0.9, 0.99, 0.999}, one RMS accumulator with β=0.95, and Adafactor-style row/column accumulators). It produces an updated parameter tensor and updated state; the per-parameter update is a scalar MLP output that is RMS-normalized (and optionally Newton-Schulz orthogonalized for 2D+ tensors) before being scaled by the learning rate and applied. Step size is fully decoupled from the learned rule, leaving it as a single tunable hyperparameter.

## Method

Celo2 meta-trains a 2-layer MLP (8 hidden units, ~200 parameters) as a per-parameter update rule on four 8×8 image classification tasks (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, SVHN) using Persistent Evolution Strategies with truncated unrolls (100–2000 steps) for only 4.5 GPU hours. The key architectural insight is fully-normalized learned updates: both MLP inputs (gradient, parameter, momentum, RMS, Adafactor features) and the MLP output are RMS-normalized per tensor, which forces the network to learn a task-invariant direction rather than raw magnitudes. For 2D+ parameters, the MLP output is additionally orthogonalized via 5-step Newton-Schulz iteration (borrowed from Muon), while 1D parameters (embeddings, biases) use standard AdamW. Task augmentation (random global rescaling of optimizee parameters during meta-training) and an RMS decay of 0.95 are critical for generalization. This recipe yields stable, competitive performance on GPT-3 1.3B (1,000,000× larger than meta-training tasks), ImageNet ViT (25× longer unrolls), and Atari PPO, outperforming both AdamW and VeLO under matched tuning budgets.

## Results

Celo2, meta-trained on only 4.5 GPU hours of 8×8 image classification tasks, generalizes to GPT-3 1.3B (1,000,000× larger than its meta-training distribution) and outperforms both well-tuned AdamW and VeLO on FineWeb pretraining. On GPT-2 124M FineWeb, Celo2 with RMS×0.2 achieves a final validation loss of 3.35588, marginally beating Muon (3.35636) and default Celo2 (3.36785). On ImageNet ViT (50K steps, 25× longer than meta-training unrolls), Celo2 reaches VeLO's target final loss approximately 2× faster, with all optimizers plateauing at ~66% test accuracy. On Atari RL (PPO), Celo2 matches well-tuned AdamW while VeLO stagnates at substantially lower returns.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AdamW, Celo2 outperforms on GPT-3 1.3B and ImageNet ViT pretraining (lower validation loss at matched step budgets) and matches on Atari RL tasks, where AdamW is near-optimally tuned (ε=1e-5). Against VeLO (meta-trained with 4000 TPU-months), Celo2 wins decisively on every evaluated task: it generalizes stably to GPT-3 1.3B where VeLO fails, reaches VeLO's ImageNet target loss ~2× faster, and outperforms VeLO by a significant margin on Atari RL where VeLO's reward stagnates. Against Muon (a hand-crafted orthogonalization-based optimizer), Celo2 RMS×0.2 achieves a slightly lower final validation loss on GPT-2 124M FineWeb (3.35588 vs. 3.35636), with the two methods differing only in whether orthogonalization is applied to a learned MLP update (Celo2) or to momentum (Muon). Notably, Celo2 achieves all these results with ~10 orders of magnitude less meta-training compute than VeLO.

## Relation to Prior Work

Celo2 is a direct successor to Celo (Moudgil et al., 2025), which reduced meta-training compute from VeLO's 4000 TPU months to 24 GPU hours but suffered performance degradation. It retains the Adafactor MLP architecture from Metz et al. (2022a) and the PES meta-training framework, but departs in three key ways: (1) normalizing the MLP *output* via per-step RMS (rather than only normalizing inputs), (2) decoupling step-size scheduling from update-rule learning, and (3) integrating Newton-Schulz orthogonalization (borrowed from Muon, Jordan et al., 2024) into the learned update. This positions Celo2 as a bridge between hand-designed optimizer techniques (orthogonalization, decoupled weight decay, distinct 1D/2D update rules) and learned optimization, rather than a purely learned alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a drop-in Optax optimizer meta-trained in 4.5 GPU hours that stably trains a 1.3B-parameter GPT-3 model, removing the perceived compute barrier that has kept learned optimizers out of production pretraining pipelines. Open questions include whether the ~5× memory overhead (vs Adam's 3×) is acceptable at scale, whether mixed-precision (bfloat16) training introduces instabilities specific to the learned rule, and whether a general learned scheduler can replace the manual step-size knob. The paper explicitly frames stability for foundation-model training as its north star, suggesting future work will target longer training horizons and larger model families.
