## Problem

Directly applying RL (à la DeepSeek-R1-Zero) to MLLMs fails to elicit complex, human-like reasoning (questioning, reflection, self-correction) because of the absence of large-scale, high-quality multimodal CoT data. Even when cold-start SFT is used to bootstrap reasoning, the resulting model suffers from an 'overthinking optimization problem' where correct reasoning concentrates in short chains while the model generates long, incorrect ones, making subsequent RL optimization difficult.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262165/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image paired with a text question and produces a structured response containing a chain-of-thought reasoning process (enclosed in <think> tags) followed by a final answer (in <answer> tags). The reasoning process is expected to exhibit natural cognitive behaviors—questioning, self-reflection, and inspection—rather than the rigid step-by-step 'Pseudo-CoT' typical of prior multimodal CoT datasets. During RL training, the maximum generation length is progressively constrained (4K → 8K tokens), and the model is evaluated on multimodal math benchmarks (MathVista, MathVerse, MM-Math) and general vision benchmarks.

## Method

Vision-R1 combines cold-start initialization with RL training in two phases. First, a 200K multimodal CoT dataset (Vision-R1-cold) is constructed via 'Modality Bridging': an MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) generates a Pseudo-CoT and detailed image description from image-question pairs, and those textual descriptions are fed to the text-only reasoning LLM DeepSeek-R1 to extract high-quality CoT with natural cognitive processes; the base MLLM is then SFT'd on this dataset. Second, RL training uses GRPO with a binary hard-formatting-result reward (1 only if both format and answer are correct), augmented by Progressive Thinking Suppression Training (PTST): Stage 1 restricts reasoning to 4K tokens with 16 samples per query to suppress overthinking and lock in correct reasoning patterns, then Stage 2 relaxes to 8K tokens with 8 samples to allow progressively longer, more complex reasoning. This two-stage schedule avoids the optimization failure observed when long (16K) reasoning is allowed from the start.

## Results

Vision-R1-7B achieves 73.5% on MathVista (ALL), only 0.4% below OpenAI O1 (73.9%), while using only 10K multimodal math data during RL training. Scaling to 32B and 72B parameters, Vision-R1-32B and Vision-R1-72B reach 76.4% and 78.2% on MathVista, respectively, with average improvements of ~11% and ~9% over their Qwen2.5-VL base models across MathVista, MathVerse, MM-Math, and DynaMath. On the complex MathVista sub-tasks (GEO, ALG, GPS), Vision-R1-7B scores 80.3%, 79.0%, and 83.2%, exceeding the 7B base model by over 10% on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vision-R1-7B (73.5% MathVista) nearly matches OpenAI O1 (73.9%) and surpasses all open-source models including Qwen2.5-VL-72B (73.5%, tied) and InternVL2.5-78B (71.9%), despite having 10× fewer parameters than the latter. Against its direct base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B (68.1%), Vision-R1-7B gains +5.4% on MathVista and +5.7% average across all four benchmarks. The 32B and 72B variants show even larger gains over their respective Qwen2.5-VL bases: +11.0% and +8.9% average, with the 32B model jumping +20.4% on MM-Math (34.9→55.3). Among prior reasoning MLLMs, Vision-R1-7B outperforms Mulberry-7B (63.1%) by +10.4% on MathVista and LLaVA-CoT-11B (54.8%) by +18.7%. On MathVerse, Vision-R1-7B (52.4%) ranks Top-1 among open-source models, surpassing URSA-8B (45.7%) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (51.3%), while on MM-Math it is second only to Qwen2.5-VL-72B (45.6% vs. 40.2%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DeepSeek-R1 paradigm (RL-induced reasoning emergence in LLMs) to the multimodal setting, building directly on GRPO as the optimization algorithm. It departs from the R1-Zero 'pure RL' recipe by showing that direct RL fails for MLLMs due to the absence of large-scale multimodal reasoning data, and instead introduces a two-stage pipeline: a cold-start SFT on a 200K dataset generated via a novel 'Modality Bridging' technique (converting visual information to text through an MLLM before feeding it to DeepSeek-R1 for CoT extraction), followed by RL with a Progressive Thinking Suppression Training (PTST) schedule. Prior multimodal CoT works (LLaVA-O1, Mulberry) rely on manually designed step-level reasoning that the authors term 'Pseudo-CoT,' lacking natural cognitive processes like questioning and reflection; this paper's data generation explicitly targets those human-like reasoning patterns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical blueprint for applying R1-style RL to MLLMs, identifying the 'overthinking optimization problem' (correct reasoning concentrates in short CoT sequences after cold-start, making naive RL optimization difficult) and offering PTST as a clean, well-ablated solution. The finding that a 7B model can approach 70B+ performance on math reasoning (73.5% vs. 73.9% for O1 on MathVista) suggests that reasoning quality, not parameter count, is the primary bottleneck for multimodal math. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond math to broader multimodal reasoning, whether the dependence on a strong text-only reasoning LLM (DeepSeek-R1) for data generation limits scalability, and whether the cold-start data's synthetic nature introduces distributional biases that RL cannot fully correct.
