## Problem

Existing pre-trained material property prediction models face two fundamental challenges: diversity (they are trained on a narrow set of PES-derived properties for crystals and struggle to generalize across the full spectrum of material systems and properties) and disparity (material tasks are governed by distinct physical laws, so jointly training them in one model causes knowledge conflicts that degrade downstream performance). MoMa addresses both by replacing the monolithic pre-train-then-fine-tune paradigm with a modular framework that trains task-specialized modules independently and adaptively composes them per downstream task.

## Input / Output

The method accepts crystal or molecular structures (encoded as graphs) and outputs a scalar material property value (e.g., band gap in eV, formation energy in eV/atom, dielectric constant). The pre-trained GNN backbone (JMP/GemNet-OC or Orb/GNS) produces a fixed-dimensional embedding per material, which is then used both for kNN-based module performance estimation in representation space and as the feature input to a task-specific regression head. The AMC composition step operates purely on these embeddings and ground-truth labels from the downstream training set, requiring no gradient-based updates or additional trainable parameters.

## Method

MoMa operates in two stages. First, it trains 18 task-specific modules (either full fine-tuned backbones or parameter-efficient adapters) on high-resource material datasets and centralizes them in MoMa Hub. Second, the Adaptive Module Composition (AMC) algorithm composes a tailored module for each downstream task in three training-free steps: (1) it estimates per-module prediction quality via leave-one-out kNN label propagation in each module's representation space, (2) it solves a convex quadratic program to find optimal composition weights that minimize the weighted ensemble proxy error subject to simplex constraints, and (3) it merges modules in weight space (a weighted average of parameters, justified by linear mode connectivity) before fine-tuning on the target task. A key theoretical result (Appendix B) shows that the kNN proxy risk upper-bounds the post-fine-tuning risk under mild assumptions on representation closeness and fine-tuning stability, providing formal justification for the proxy-driven composition. This design avoids the noisy optimization landscape of search-based methods and the data-scarcity limitations of router-based approaches, while remaining computationally efficient (under 30 seconds for the full AMC pipeline).

## Results

Across 17 material property prediction tasks, MoMa (Full) achieves the best average rank of 1.35 (±0.86) and the lowest MAE in 14/17 tasks, while MoMa (Adapter) follows with an average rank of 2.59 (±1.12). Together the two variants hold 16/17 best results. MoMa (Full) delivers a 14.0% average MAE improvement over the strongest non-modular baseline (JMP-FT) and a 24.8% average improvement over the multi-task baseline (JMP-MT). In few-shot settings (10-shot), MoMa's normalized test MAE advantage over JMP-FT widens from 0.03 (full data) to 0.15.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoMa (Full) outperforms the strongest baseline JMP-FT (direct fine-tuning of the JMP pre-trained model) in 14/17 tasks with a 14.0% average MAE reduction, with particularly large gains on 2D Formation Energy (0.0495 vs. 0.125, ~60% reduction) and 2D Band Gap (0.375 vs. 0.582, ~36% reduction). Against the multi-task baseline JMP-MT, MoMa (Full) wins 16/17 tasks with a 24.8% average margin, confirming that modular composition avoids the knowledge conflicts inherent in joint multi-task training. MoMa also surpasses the mixture-of-experts baseline MoE-(18) in 15/17 tasks and the universal atomic model UMA in 14/17 tasks. The only clear losses for MoMa (Full) are on Exfoliation Energy (36.3 vs. 35.4 for JMP-FT) and Elastic Anisotropy (2.86 vs. 2.42 for JMP-FT), while Piezoelectric Modulus is essentially tied (0.174 vs. 0.174). The adapter variant MoMa (Adapter) is competitive with JMP-FT on most tasks (average rank 2.59 vs. 3.12) at substantially lower GPU memory cost, making it suitable for compute-constrained settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoMa sits at the intersection of pre-trained material property prediction models (JMP, UMA, Orb) and the modular deep learning paradigm (adapters, LoRA, model soup, mixture-of-experts). It departs from the dominant pre-train-then-fine-tune paradigm by arguing that the diversity and disparity of material tasks make single-model or multi-task approaches fundamentally limited, and instead trains task-specific modules and composes them adaptively. The key methodological departure is the Adaptive Module Composition (AMC) algorithm, which replaces the search-based (LoRAHub) and router-based (MoE-(18)) composition strategies with a training-free, representation-driven approach using kNN proxy error and convex optimization—motivated by the observation that heterogeneous material representations make downstream prediction error an unreliable composition signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoMa offers a practical, open-source platform for modularizing and distributing materials knowledge, with the privacy-preserving property that proprietary data need not be shared to contribute modules. The finding that modular composition outperforms multi-task learning by 24.8% on average—and that the gap widens in few-shot settings—challenges the common assumption that joint training on more tasks is always beneficial for disparate material properties. Open questions include whether the framework scales to hundreds or thousands of modules, whether it extends beyond crystalline and organic materials to polymers or amorphous systems, and whether the AMC weights can serve as a reliable interpretability tool for discovering non-obvious property relationships.
