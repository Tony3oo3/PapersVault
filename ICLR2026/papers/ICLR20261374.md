## Problem

Diffusion Large Language Models (DLLMs) suffer from a severe quality-speed trade-off: their standard decoding process is irreversible, so once a token is unmasked it cannot be revised even as richer context becomes available. This irreversibility causes early errors in parallel decoding to become permanently embedded and propagate throughout the output, forcing a choice between slow single-token decoding (high quality) and fast multi-token decoding (degraded quality).

## Input / Output

WINO operates as a drop-in decoding replacement for existing DLLMs (LLaDA-8B-Instruct for text, MMaDA-8B-MixCoT for vision-language). The input is a text prompt or an image-plus-text prompt; the response sequence is initialized as all [MASK] tokens with a fixed generation length (256 or 512) and decoded in blocks of length 128 using semi-autoregressive left-to-right ordering. The output is the fully unmasked text sequence (reasoning chain plus final answer, or an image caption). Internally, WINO appends an auxiliary shadow block of the same length as the current block to the sequence for verification, but this is invisible to the user-facing I/O.

## Method

WINO introduces a training-free, parallel draft-and-verify decoding loop that makes DLLM generation revokable. In the Draft phase (Wide-In), all [MASK] positions in the current block are simultaneously unmasked if their argmax confidence exceeds a low threshold τ₁, enabling aggressive multi-token generation per step. In the Verify phase (Narrow-Out), an auxiliary shadow block of all-[MASK] tokens—assigned the same position IDs as the current block but blocked from attending to its own corresponding position via a custom attention mask—re-evaluates every previously unmasked token; any token whose shadow-block confidence falls below a stricter threshold τ₂ is re-masked for correction in a future step. Both phases complete in a single forward pass over the extended sequence [Y_left, Y_cur, Y_right, Y_shad], and the loop iterates until no [MASK] tokens remain in the block. The design ensures that appending the shadow block does not alter the model's output for the current block (preserving draft quality) while forcing verification to rely on global context rather than trivial identity matching, thereby enabling iterative error correction that standard irreversible decoding cannot provide.

## Results

On language benchmarks (LLaDA-8B), WINO improves accuracy across all 8 tasks while reducing decoding steps by 1.94×–6.37×: GSM8K accuracy rises from 73.24% to 75.82% (+2.58%) with a 6.10× step reduction and 5.66× TPS speedup; ARC-E jumps from 59.13% to 81.19% (+22.06%) with 6.37× step reduction. On multimodal benchmarks (MMaDA-8B), WINO achieves up to 10.05× step reduction on Flickr30k (CIDEr 53.67→53.83) and 9.10× on ScienceQA (accuracy 30.89%→42.24%, +11.35%), while also boosting MMMU-val from 18.56% to 24.00% (+5.44%) with 6.65× step reduction. GPU memory overhead is minimal: ~2.4% in the standard semi-autoregressive setting (L_b=128, L=256) and <8% in the full-diffusion extreme (L_b=L=512).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaDA standard decoding (1 token/step), WINO wins on 7 of 8 language benchmarks in both accuracy and speed, tying only on MBPP (36.40% for both) while still delivering a 2.65× step reduction. Against MMaDA standard decoding, WINO improves accuracy on all 6 multimodal benchmarks (e.g., +11.35% on ScienceQA, +5.44% on MMMU-val) with 5.01×–10.05× step reductions. Against Fast-dLLM-parallel (Wu et al., 2025), WINO achieves higher accuracy on GSM8K (75.82% vs. 72.33%) and MMMU-val (24.00% vs. 19.89%) while also matching or exceeding its speedup (6.10× vs. 3.15× on GSM8K; 6.65× vs. 7.18× on MMMU-val). Against the Entropy-Bounded sampler (Ben-Hamu et al., 2025), WINO dominates on both metrics: GSM8K accuracy 75.82% vs. 70.37% with 6.10× vs. 2.76× step reduction, and MMMU-val 24.00% vs. 17.87% with 6.65× vs. 4.20× step reduction. The key differentiator is the revocation mechanism: without verification ("Only Draft" ablation), aggressive drafting at τ₁=0.6 actually degrades GSM8K accuracy to 70.28%, confirming that the verify-and-re-mask step is essential for breaking the quality-speed trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the semi-autoregressive decoding paradigm of LLaDA and MMaDA, positioning its contribution as a decoding-level intervention rather than an architectural change. It departs from existing DLLM acceleration work in two directions: KV-cache methods (Block Diffusion, Fast-dLLM-cache) target computational redundancy, while sampling-compression methods (Fast-dLLM-parallel, EB Sampler) adjust how many tokens to unmask per step. WINO's key conceptual departure is identifying the *irreversibility* of standard diffusion decoding as the root cause of the quality-speed trade-off, and introducing a shadow-block verification mechanism that makes token commitments revokable—effectively transplanting the draft-and-verify philosophy of speculative decoding into the bidirectional diffusion setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With proprietary systems like Mercury and Gemini Diffusion already demonstrating >1000 tok/s, the practical viability of open-source DLLMs hinges on resolving their quality-speed trade-off; WINO provides a training-free, plug-and-play solution that simultaneously accelerates and improves accuracy, making it immediately deployable on existing checkpoints. The speedup is inherently coupled to base-model confidence (easier tasks yield larger gains), and the shadow block, while small in memory overhead, does scale linearly with block length in full-diffusion settings. A natural open question is whether integrating revokable sampling into the training objective—so the model learns to produce more robust initial drafts and self-correct more efficiently—could decouple speedup from task difficulty and unlock further gains.
