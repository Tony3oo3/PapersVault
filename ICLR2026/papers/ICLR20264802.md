## Problem

Post-training quantization of object detection models is degraded by task-irrelevant activations (background clutter, sensor noise) that broaden activation ranges and skew distributions toward non-informative responses, especially under low-bit (4-bit) settings where coarse quantization levels must simultaneously cover anomalies and informative inliers. Existing PTQ methods lack a principled criterion to identify and exclude these anomalies, leading to suboptimal preservation of task-relevant features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264802/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires only a pretrained full-precision detector and 64 calibration samples (no labels), operating as a drop-in layer-wise procedure. It accepts RGB images for 2D detection (COCO) or LiDAR point clouds for 3D detection (nuScenes), and outputs per-layer quantization parameters (scale and zero-point) that convert the model to INT4/INT8 inference. The detection head is retained in FP16 to isolate quantization effects, and the first layer of camera-based models is kept at INT8 while CenterPoint's first layer uses FP16.

## Method

InlierQ computes a gradient-aware volume saliency score (L1 norm of the loss gradient) from object heatmap top-K activations, where the loss is a negative log-likelihood restricted to the K largest heatmap entries per channel to emphasize task-relevant regions. A two-component Gaussian mixture model is then fitted to these saliency scores via the EM algorithm, yielding a per-volume posterior probability of belonging to the inlier versus anomaly distribution. Volumes with posterior probability above a threshold τ form the inlier set, and quantization parameters are optimized to minimize the Hessian-weighted (Fisher Information Matrix) reconstruction error exclusively over this inlier set. This inlier-centric objective, combined with the heatmap-top-K loss design, suppresses anomaly-induced range inflation and distributional skew while preserving object-relevant activation structure, achieving up to +3.2% mAP on 3D detection under W4A4.

## Results

Under W4A4 (INT4) post-training quantization, InlierQ achieves 34.7% mAP on both RetinaNet and Faster R-CNN for 2D COCO detection, surpassing BRECQ (34.0%, 32.7%) and LiDAR-PTQ (34.4%, 34.3%). For 3D nuScenes detection, the gains are more pronounced: CenterPoint (LiDAR) reaches 46.6% mAP / 58.1 NDS vs. BRECQ (43.4% / 56.3) and LiDAR-PTQ (39.5% / 54.0), a +3.2% mAP improvement, while DETR3D (camera) achieves 26.4% mAP / 35.2 NDS vs. BRECQ (24.8% / 33.8) and LiDAR-PTQ (25.2% / 34.0). Under W8A8, all methods are within 0.1–0.3% of full precision, confirming that the inlier-centric design is most beneficial at aggressive 4-bit activation precision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InlierQ is compared against BRECQ (Hessian-informed block reconstruction) and LiDAR-PTQ (task-loss-aligned PTQ for LiDAR detection) across four detectors and three bit-width settings. Under W8A8, all three methods are within 0.1–0.3% mAP of full precision, indicating that 8-bit levels suffice to cover the full activation range including anomalies. The differentiation emerges at W4A4: on 2D COCO, InlierQ gains +0.7% mAP over BRECQ and +0.3% over LiDAR-PTQ on RetinaNet, and +2.0% over BRECQ and +0.4% over LiDAR-PTQ on Faster R-CNN. The largest advantage appears on 3D LiDAR detection (CenterPoint, W4A4), where InlierQ achieves 46.6% mAP / 58.1 NDS, outperforming BRECQ by +3.2% mAP and LiDAR-PTQ by +7.1% mAP, reflecting the severe range inflation caused by sparse LiDAR anomalies under 4-bit activation quantization. For camera-based 3D (DETR3D, W4A4), InlierQ leads BRECQ by +1.6% mAP and LiDAR-PTQ by +1.2% mAP. Overall, InlierQ wins or ties on every W4A4 configuration and matches baselines at W8A8, with the margin widening as activation bit-width decreases.

## Relation to Prior Work

InlierQ builds directly on the optimization-based PTQ framework established by Nagel et al. (2020b) and Li et al. (2021), which approximate the quantization objective via a Taylor expansion and the Fisher Information Matrix. It departs from this lineage by rejecting the implicit assumption that all activations should be weighted equally: rather than minimizing quantization error over the full activation distribution, InlierQ introduces a principled inlier-anomaly decomposition using gradient-aware heatmap saliency scores and an EM-fitted two-component Gaussian mixture. This distinguishes it from outlier-suppression methods (SmoothQuant, SVDQuant, DMQ), which target extreme magnitudes, and from prior detection-specific PTQ (AQD, HQOD, LiDAR-PTQ), which mitigate anomalies heuristically without a formal separation criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The inlier-centric framing offers a reusable conceptual tool for any task where a small subset of spatial locations carries most of the signal (detection heatmaps, segmentation maps, keypoint fields), and the EM-based decomposition is label-free and requires only 64 calibration samples, making it practical for deployment. The +3.2% mAP gain on LiDAR 3D detection under W4A4 is practically significant for on-device autonomous driving, where 4-bit inference is the target. Open questions include whether the heatmap-saliency criterion generalizes to detection architectures without explicit heatmaps (e.g., query-based DETR variants without top-K selection), and whether the inlier-anomaly split transfers to other modalities such as video or multi-sensor fusion without re-deriving the saliency signal.
