## Problem

In decision-based black-box adversarial attacks, only the top-1 predicted label is observable and query budgets are strictly limited, making it extremely difficult to identify which image regions are most sensitive to perturbation. Existing region-aware methods either require surrogate models or white-box access, or (as in PAR) use a coarse binary keep/remove decision that cannot capture continuous, per-pixel sensitivity. This paper addresses the gap by introducing the first decision-based method that assigns a continuous sensitivity score to every pixel without gradients, confidence scores, or surrogate models.

## Input / Output

SeRI takes as input a source RGB image (C×W×H), an initial adversarial example produced by a base attacker (e.g., HSJA or CGBA), and a hard-label oracle that returns only the top-1 class per query. The total query budget Q is split with 80% allocated to the base attacker and 20% (P=20%) to SeRI's refinement loop. The method outputs an optimized adversarial example with reduced ℓ₂-norm perturbation and a continuous sensitivity heatmap S ∈ ℝ^{C×W×H}, computed as the element-wise ratio of the final perturbation to the initial one, where higher values indicate regions where perturbation was amplified and lower values indicate regions where it was suppressed.

## Method

SeRI defines pixel sensitivity in terms of the perturbation decision boundary g(d) — the minimum radius at which the model's prediction flips — rather than confidence drops or binary patch removal. At each iteration, it selects the sub-region with the highest local ℓ₂-norm, constructs two candidate perturbations by scaling the perturbation within that region up (k̂=1.8) or down (ǩ=0.2) while preserving the global ℓ₂-norm, and compares their decision boundaries using the Approximation Decision Boundary Approach (ADBA) to keep the variant with the smallest boundary distance. The selected region is then recursively split into four equal sub-regions (quad-tree), enabling progressively finer-grained sensitivity estimation down to individual pixels. Theoretical analysis proves monotonic decision-boundary descent, convergence to a stationary perturbation, and O(2^{-kα}) consistency of the region-wise sensitivity estimate under piecewise Hölder continuity. SeRI is designed as a plug-in post-processor that can be appended to any base decision-based attacker with minimal additional query overhead (~4 queries per iteration).

## Results

On ImageNet with VGG19, CGBA+SeRI achieves the lowest average ℓ₂-norm of 0.96 (median 0.54) for non-targeted attacks at 10,000 queries, while HSJA+SeRI achieves the best targeted attack performance at 18.5 (median 12.3) ℓ₂-norm under the same budget. On the adversarially trained WideResNet (CIFAR-100), CGBA+SeRI reaches 1.02 (0.62) ℓ₂-norm at 10,000 queries, reducing perturbation strength by approximately 30% compared to CGBA+PAR. Across all three model/dataset combinations, the 'Attacker + SeRI' variants consistently achieve the lowest ℓ₂-norms in both targeted and non-targeted settings at 5,000 and 10,000 queries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeRI is evaluated as a plug-in refinement module on top of four base attackers (HSJA, CGBA, RayS, ADBA) and compared against the PAR (patch-wise adversarial removal) baseline. In every configuration, 'Attacker + SeRI' outperforms both the base attacker alone and 'Attacker + PAR': for example, CGBA+SeRI reduces the ℓ₂-norm from 1.19 (CGBA) and 1.03 (CGBA+PAR) to 0.96 at 10,000 queries on ImageNet-VGG non-targeted attacks. For targeted attacks, HSJA+SeRI achieves 18.5 ℓ₂-norm at 10,000 queries versus 33.7 (HSJA) and 20.4 (HSJA+PAR), a 45% reduction over the base attacker. On the adversarially trained WideResNet (CIFAR-100), the gap widens: CGBA+SeRI reaches 1.02 versus 1.19 (CGBA) and 1.07 (CGBA+PAR) at 10,000 queries, indicating SeRI's continuous sensitivity estimation is particularly beneficial against stronger defenses. ADBA+SeRI achieves the best non-targeted ℓ₂-norm at 2,000 queries (2.78) on ImageNet-VGG, slightly edging out CGBA+SeRI (2.92) in that low-budget regime. Overall, SeRI consistently wins or ties across all 12 attack configurations tested, with the largest margins observed on adversarially trained models and in targeted attack settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeRI builds directly on the decision-based black-box attack lineage (HSJA, CGBA, RayS, ADBA) and the region-sensitivity idea introduced by PAR (Shi et al., 2022), which uses a binary patch-wise keep/remove strategy. The key conceptual departure is redefining sensitivity in terms of its impact on the perturbation decision boundary g(d) rather than confidence drop or gradient magnitude, enabling a continuous per-pixel sensitivity score under hard-label queries. SeRI also inherits the Approximation Decision Boundary Approach (ADBA) for query-efficient boundary comparison, and its quad-tree recursive partitioning strategy is positioned as a fine-grained alternative to PAR's one-shot binary decisions. The method is designed as a plug-in optimizer that can be attached to any existing base attacker without modifying its core logic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SeRI provides the first continuous, gradient-free sensitivity estimation for decision-based attacks, filling a gap between coarse binary region selection (PAR) and inapplicable white-box XAI methods. Its plug-in architecture (Attacker + SeRI) makes it broadly applicable and the ~30% perturbation reduction on adversarially trained WRN models suggests particular value against stronger defenses. Limitations include inapplicability to randomized defenses, a minimum query budget requirement for meaningful refinement, and current restriction to image classification. Open questions include extension to multi-output tasks (detection, segmentation), behavior under stronger task-specific defenses, and whether the continuous sensitivity formulation can be combined with other perturbation optimization strategies beyond the current greedy descent.
