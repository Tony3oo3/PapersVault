## Problem

Prior scaling-law studies attributed post-training quantization (PTQ) degradation primarily to training data scale, predicting that larger datasets would make quantization progressively worse. This paper identifies a critical confound: the observed degradation is actually driven by learning rate decay during training, not by token count, and had been overlooked because open-source model releases typically provided only a single final checkpoint with no intermediate training trajectories.

## Input / Output

The analysis consumes hundreds of intermediate training checkpoints from six open-source LLM families (OLMo, OLMo2, SmolLM3, Apertus, OpenSci, Amber; up to 32B parameters and 15T tokens) plus self-trained Pythia-160M models on FineWebEdu up to 100B tokens. Quantization is applied via GPTQ (primary), AWQ, and LLM.int8() at 3-bit and 4-bit using C4 as calibration data with group size 128. Outputs include relative cross-entropy quantization error, 5-shot accuracy degradation across 12 downstream benchmarks, 2D loss landscape slices, and Hessian trace/sharpness estimates tracked throughout training rather than only at the final checkpoint.

## Method

The paper combines large-scale trajectory analysis with controlled pretraining experiments to isolate the effect of training dynamics on PTQ robustness. In controlled runs (Pythia-160M, WSD schedule, FineWebEdu), it shows that quantization error spikes sharply when the learning rate decays regardless of total token budget, and that higher peak learning rates consistently yield lower quantization error at comparable validation loss. It then demonstrates that LAtest Weight Averaging (LAWA) over stable-phase checkpoints can match or surpass learning-rate cooldowns in 3-bit PTQ performance, and that model souping across data configurations reduces degradation below any individual run. The geometric mechanism is established via 2D loss landscape slices spanned by {Θ_K, Θ_{K-1}, Θ̂_K} and Hessian statistics (trace via Hutchinson estimator, sharpness via power iterations using PyHessian), revealing that larger learning rates and weight averaging both produce flatter minima, making the model less sensitive to the weight perturbations introduced by quantization.

## Results

Across six open-source LLM families (up to 32B parameters, 15T tokens), quantization error (measured as relative cross-entropy loss increase under GPTQ 3/4-bit) remains relatively stable during the constant-LR phase but spikes sharply once the learning rate decays, regardless of total token budget. In controlled 160M-parameter experiments (100B tokens, FineWebEdu), raising the peak learning rate from 1e-3 to 3e-3 consistently reduces 4-bit and 3-bit quantization error at comparable full-precision validation loss. LAtest Weight Averaging (LAWA) over a rolling window of 5 checkpoints matches or surpasses all intermediate LR cooldowns on 3-bit PTQ validation loss, and model souping across OLMo2 data mixtures achieves lower PTQ error than any individual ingredient. Hessian trace and sharpness (max eigenvalue) surge during LR decay, mirroring the quantization error spike, while larger LRs produce flatter minima with lower sharpness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four quantization backends (GPTQ, AWQ, LLM.int8()) and finds all exhibit the same qualitative trend: quantization error is stable under constant LR and spikes during decay (Figure 10), confirming the phenomenon is not GPTQ-specific. Against learning-rate cooldowns, LAWA (Kaddour, 2022) underperforms in full-precision validation loss but matches or beats all cooldown variants on 3-bit PTQ validation loss (Figure 7), making it a strictly better choice when quantization is the objective. AdamC (Defazio, 2025), which suppresses the end-of-training gradient norm spike, produces nearly identical quantization degradation to AdamW despite different gradient and weight norms (Figure 20), ruling out gradient magnitude as the sole driver. Comparing cosine decay to WSD schedules at matched token budgets (Figure 5, 21), WSD yields slower or no growth in δ_PTQ with increasing tokens, whereas cosine decay reproduces the previously reported monotonic increase, showing that the 'more data → worse quantization' scaling trend in Kumar et al. (2024) is confounded by the LR schedule shape rather than data volume per se.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the post-training quantization literature (GPTQ, AWQ, LLM.int8()) and the loss landscape visualization framework of Goodfellow et al. (2015) and Li et al. (2018), but departs sharply from the scaling-law perspective of Kumar et al. (2024) and Ouyang et al. (2024), who attributed quantization degradation primarily to training data volume. The authors reframe the problem as one of training dynamics—specifically learning rate schedule and weight averaging—rather than dataset scale, and connect quantization robustness to the geometric flatness of the loss landscape via Hessian trace and sharpness analysis. Weight averaging (LAWA, model souping) is repurposed here not as a generalization technique but as a quantization-robustness intervention, extending its role beyond the optimization-equivalence framing of Sandler et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that quantization error spikes are driven by learning rate decay rather than token count directly challenges a published assumption in the field and provides practitioners with concrete, low-cost interventions (higher peak LR, weight averaging along trajectories) to improve PTQ quality without retraining. This positions quantization robustness as a first-class criterion during hyperparameter tuning, which the authors argue should be standard practice before large-scale runs. Open questions remain around optimizer choice, schedule-free methods, and whether the geometric mechanism (flatter minima) generalizes to sparse or sub-quadratic architectures, as the controlled experiments are limited to 160M-parameter dense transformers.
