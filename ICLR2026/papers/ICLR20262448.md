## Problem

Existing federated learning methods address either inter-client distribution shift or intra-client distribution drift, but not both simultaneously, and typically require prior knowledge such as the number of client clusters or the specific type of non-IID heterogeneity. No prior framework handles the joint presence of training-time and test-time drift while remaining agnostic to client identity and distribution type, leaving a gap for robust FL under fully dynamic, real-world heterogeneity.

## Input / Output

During training, each client provides its local dataset (features and labels) from which a compact d-dimensional distribution profile is extracted via a pretrained model's latent space; the profile dimension satisfies d/|θ| ≤ 3.5×10⁻³, making communication overhead negligible relative to model updates. At test time, only unlabeled features are required: the client extracts a label-free sub-vector of the profile (marginal moments only) and receives a single pre-trained model via nearest-neighbor matching in profile space, with no gradient steps or retraining. The output is an assigned model parameter vector per client, from which standard class predictions are produced.

## Method

FEROMA decouples model identity from client or cluster identity by associating each model with a data distribution characterized by a differentially private distribution profile. The Distribution Profile Extractor (DPE) operates in four steps: global min/max alignment of latent vectors, shared PCA on synthetic reference points (ensuring a common geometric basis across all clients), Monte-Carlo subsampled moment computation (mean and covariance of marginal and class-conditional latents), and coordinate-wise Laplace noise for (ε,0)-DP. The Distribution Profile Mapper then computes softmax-weighted association weights between current-round and previous-round profiles, with an optional threshold τ that discards weak associations; inspecting the support of these weights automatically selects the aggregation strategy per client per round—clustered (multiple weights survive), personalized (exactly one weight), or global fallback (no similar profile). At test time, a label-free profile is matched to the nearest final-round profile to assign the best pre-trained model in one shot, requiring no further optimization.

## Results

FEROMA achieves an average accuracy of 90.7% on MNIST, 79.9% on Fashion-MNIST, 44.2% on CIFAR-10, 39.9% on CIFAR-100, 72.4% on CheXpert, and 42.4% on Office-Home, computed as unweighted averages over all drift frequencies, non-IID types, and severity levels. These results represent gains of up to 14.1 pp over the best baseline (CFL) on MNIST, 14.3 pp on Fashion-MNIST, and 10.3 pp on CIFAR-10, while maintaining standard deviations as low as 0.6–2.9 pp. FEROMA also scales to 100 clients while retaining >85% accuracy on MNIST, with training time per 20 rounds comparable to FedAvg.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FEROMA outperforms all 10 baselines on every dataset. The closest competitor is CFL (clustered FL), which FEROMA surpasses by 14.1 pp on MNIST (90.7 vs 76.6), 14.3 pp on Fashion-MNIST (79.9 vs 65.6), and 10.3 pp on CIFAR-10 (44.2 vs 33.9). On CIFAR-100, FEROMA leads FedEM (the best baseline there) by 8.2 pp (39.9 vs 31.7). On the real-world CheXpert dataset, FEROMA edges out FedDrift by a narrow 0.1 pp (72.4 vs 72.3) while achieving lower variance (0.6 vs 0.8); on Office-Home it leads FedDrift by 0.3 pp (42.4 vs 42.1). The soft-clustering baselines FedRC and FedEM perform poorly under drift (30.7–30.9% on MNIST), and the personalized methods pFedMe and APFL trail FEROMA by 37–21 pp on MNIST, confirming that distribution-profile-based adaptive aggregation substantially outperforms fixed-strategy approaches under combined shift and drift.

## Relation to Prior Work

FEROMA builds directly on the descriptor-based perspective from FLUX (Fenoglio et al., 2025), extending it from static clustered FL to drift-aware temporal matching across rounds. Conceptually, it departs from the three dominant paradigms for handling FL heterogeneity—clustered FL (which requires known cluster counts and hard/soft assignments), personalized FL (which optimizes per-client models), and test-time adaptive FL (which requires online retraining)—by reframing the problem as distribution-profile matching that dynamically recovers any of these strategies per round. The paper positions itself as the first framework to jointly address both inter-client shift and intra-client drift during training and test time without prior knowledge of distribution modes, cluster counts, or heterogeneity types.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FEROMA offers a practical, deployable path for real-world FL where data distributions are both heterogeneous across clients and non-stationary over time—conditions under which most existing methods degrade or require unrealistic assumptions. The key open questions include: (1) dependence on a pretrained model for latent-space embedding, which limits performance when the task is difficult or data is scarce; (2) inability to handle entirely unseen distributions at test time or rare distributions absent from the final training round; and (3) the acknowledged gap in capturing P(Y|X) concept shift with label-free profiles alone. Future work on periodic checkpointing in sparse descriptor-space regions and integration with unsupervised test-time adaptation could address these gaps.
