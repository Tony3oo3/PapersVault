## Problem

Existing deepfake detection benchmarks feature homogeneous training sources and low-quality test images, failing to reflect industrial scenarios where abundant training data coexists with unseen forgery types and novel data domains. Additionally, while MLLMs show promise for explainable detection, they lack the structured reasoning depth needed for robust out-of-distribution generalization, and prior MLLM-based detectors still rely on small vision models for the final classification decision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262259/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB facial image (resolutions ranging from 256×256 to 1536×1536 across evaluation splits) along with a user query as input. It produces a binary real/fake verdict preceded by a structured reasoning trace organized into five tagged thinking patterns: fast judgement, reasoning, planning, self-reflection, and conclusion. The reasoning trace is the primary output artifact, providing a transparent forensic-style explanation of which artifacts were detected and how the final decision was reached.

## Method

VERITAS builds on InternVL3-8B and is trained via a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (Pattern-Guided Cold-Start) first performs SFT on 36K samples generated by a multi-step annotation pipeline (ensemble-voted artifact detection → visual detail extraction → pattern-tagged reasoning chain rewriting), then applies MiPO, a preference optimization variant that mixes two types of non-preference data (correct-but-imprecise traces and incorrect traces) with human-annotated preferred traces to steer toward faithful, fine-grained reasoning. Stage 2 (Pattern-Aware GRPO) performs online RL with a pattern-aware reward that assigns higher reward (2.0) when planning or self-reflection patterns lead to correct answers, penalizes overthinking (−0.5 to −1.0) when these patterns produce errors, and adds a reflection-quality reward scored by an external model plus a format reward. The key insight is that incentivizing specific reasoning patterns (rather than raw length or accuracy alone) drives adaptive planning and self-reflection, yielding significant OOD gains on unseen forgeries and domains.

## Results

On the HydraFake benchmark, VERITAS achieves 90.7% average accuracy across four hierarchical evaluation splits (In-Domain, Cross-Model, Cross-Forgery, Cross-Domain), surpassing the previous best small-vision detector Co-SPY (84.7%) by 6.0% and the strongest generic MLLM Gemini-2.5-Pro (78.9%) by 11.8%. The gains are most pronounced on Cross-Forgery (90.3% avg vs. 85.9% for Co-SPY) and Cross-Domain (82.2% avg vs. 74.7% for Co-SPY), where existing detectors degrade most severely. Even the restricted VERITAS-MINI variant (trained on only FF++, StyleGAN, SDXL, FFHQ) reaches 85.8% average, outperforming all MLLM-based forgery detectors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VERITAS (90.7% avg) outperforms the strongest small-vision detector Co-SPY (84.7%) by 6.0% and D³ (81.1%) by 9.6%, with the largest margins on Cross-Forgery (+4.4% over Co-SPY) and Cross-Domain (+7.5% over Co-SPY), where traditional detectors degrade most. Against generic MLLMs, VERITAS surpasses Gemini-2.5-Pro (78.9%) by 11.8% and GPT-4o (60.8%) by 29.9%, while the base model InternVL3-8B without training scores only 58.3%, confirming a 32.4% gain from the proposed pipeline. Among MLLM-based forgery detectors, VERITAS beats FakeVLM (77.3%) by 13.4% and SIDA-13B (76.3%) by 14.4%; even the data-restricted VERITAS-MINI (85.8%) exceeds all of them. The cold-start model (87.3%) already surpasses Co-SPY, and P-GRPO adds a further 3.4% on average, with the largest improvement on Cross-Forgery (+2.9%) and Cross-Domain (+2.1%), validating the pattern-aware reward's role in incentivizing planning and self-reflection for unseen artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of MLLM-based deepfake detection (M2F2-Det, FFAA, SIDA), where the MLLM serves as a post-hoc explainer on top of a small vision model's binary decision. Instead, VERITAS internalizes detection into the MLLM's own reasoning process, drawing on the pattern-aware reasoning literature from LLMs (s1, GThinker) that shows structured thinking patterns (planning, self-reflection) improve OOD generalization. The two-stage training pipeline (SFT cold-start + P-GRPO) follows the DeepSeek-R1 RLVR paradigm but introduces a novel pattern-aware reward that incentivizes adaptive use of planning and self-reflection specifically for forensic reasoning, rather than rewarding reasoning length or vanilla accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HydraFake dataset with its four-level hierarchical OOD protocol (in-domain, cross-model, cross-forgery, cross-domain) addresses a critical gap: existing benchmarks use homogeneous training sources and low-quality test images that don't reflect industrial deployment. The finding that SOTA detectors achieve >90% on cross-model but drop to 50–80% on cross-forgery and cross-domain scenarios quantifies a real generalization bottleneck. Open questions include whether the pattern-aware reasoning framework transfers to video deepfake detection, whether the approach scales to the rapidly evolving landscape of commercial generators (GPT-4o, Dreamina), and whether the cold-start model can serve as a general foundation for other forensic tasks beyond facial forgery.
