## Problem

CLIP is a discriminative model that aligns images and text in a shared embedding space, but generating images from text has always required a separate decoder (diffusion, GAN, or flow). Prior attempts to invert CLIP directly either optimize raw pixels and produce visible artifacts, or require fine-tuning CLIP itself. This paper asks whether a frozen CLIP encoder, combined with a suitable implicit representation, can serve as a decoder on its own—without any pretrained generative network or CLIP modification.

## Input / Output

For text-to-image synthesis, the method takes a natural language prompt and produces a 224×224 RGB image by optimizing a per-sample FINER INR (a 5-layer MLP mapping 2D coordinates to RGB) over 400 gradient steps through the frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 image encoder. For downstream tasks, it additionally accepts an input image (for reconstruction or controlled editing) or a content–style image pair (for neural style transfer), with the INR initialized from the input rather than from a retrieved anchor. The output is always a rendered image from the optimized INR weights, not a latent code or a fixed decoder pass.

## Method

The core mechanism inverts CLIP's image encoder by optimizing a frequency-aware implicit neural representation (FINER, with variable-periodic activations that stratify frequencies across layers) so that the CLIP embedding of the rendered image matches a projected text embedding. Three key components stabilize this inversion: (1) Adversarial Weight Perturbation (AWP) trains the initial INR on a Gaussian-blurred anchor image under weight-level perturbations, yielding a robust low-frequency starting point; (2) an orthogonal Procrustes rotation, computed from the k nearest caption–image pairs, bridges CLIP's modality gap by mapping the text embedding onto the image sub-manifold before optimization; and (3) a Gaussian learning-rate schedule shifts the optimization focus from low-frequency to high-frequency INR layers in a coarse-to-fine manner, while a blending loss anchors outputs to natural-image statistics retrieved from a LAION-Aesthetics index. The entire pipeline uses a frozen CLIP backbone, zero trainable parameters beyond the per-sample INR, and no pretrained decoder.

## Results

On 10,000 MS-COCO captions, CLIP⁻¹ achieves a FID of 72.5, CLIPSIM of 38.6, and Inception Score of 9.5, using a frozen ViT-B/32 CLIP with zero trainable parameters. Among tuning-free, decoder-free inversion methods, this represents a 2.2× FID improvement over DAS-ViT (161.8) and a 1.7× IS improvement (9.5 vs. 5.7). The method also outperforms DAS Ensemble (FID 121.6, IS 8.36) and CLIP-Inv (FID 140.1, IS 4.8) on all three metrics, while requiring no CLIP fine-tuning or pretrained generative decoder.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest concurrent tuning-free inversion method DAS (Fort & Whitaker, 2025), CLIP⁻¹ nearly halves the FID (72.5 vs. 161.8 for DAS-ViT; 72.5 vs. 121.6 for DAS Ensemble) and almost doubles the Inception Score (9.5 vs. 5.7 / 8.36), while also improving CLIPSIM (38.6 vs. 22.7 / 36.9). Compared to CLIP-Inv (Kazemi et al., 2024), CLIP⁻¹ achieves a substantially lower FID (72.5 vs. 140.1) and higher IS (9.5 vs. 4.8), though CLIP-Inv reports a higher CLIPSIM (61.4 vs. 38.6) which the authors attribute to overfitting to the target embedding at the expense of visual quality. Against fine-tuned inversion methods CLIPAG (FID 42.3, IS 18.7) and EB-CLIP XXL (FID 23.4), CLIP⁻¹ trails on FID and IS but matches or exceeds them on CLIPSIM (38.6 vs. 34.7 and 33.5), and does so without any CLIP fine-tuning or additional training parameters. Against large generative models (DALL-E 3, GLIDE, LDM-KL-8), CLIP⁻¹ is clearly inferior on FID and IS, but the paper explicitly frames its contribution as demonstrating that a frozen discriminative encoder alone can produce coherent, semantically aligned images without any external decoder, rather than competing on absolute fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model-inversion line of work (Kazemi et al., 2024; Fort & Whitaker, 2025) by replacing direct pixel-space optimization with a frequency-aware Implicit Neural Representation (FINER, Liu et al., 2024), enabling coarse-to-fine generation without structural artifacts. It departs from CLIPAG and EB-CLIP, which fine-tune CLIP itself, by keeping the encoder entirely frozen and instead introducing three lightweight stabilization mechanisms: adversarial weight perturbation (AWP, Wu et al., 2020) for robust INR initialization, orthogonal Procrustes projection (Wang & Mahadevan, 2008) to bridge CLIP's text-image modality gap, and a natural-image blending loss. The augmentation strategy follows CLIPDraw (Frans et al., 2022), and the overall framing positions CLIP not as a text encoder for a separate decoder but as a self-contained generative system whose latent space already encodes sufficient visual structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a frozen discriminative model can produce coherent, semantically aligned images with zero trainable parameters, offering a practical interpretability tool for probing CLIP's behavior on out-of-distribution or adversarial prompts before errors propagate into larger pipelines. Its practical efficiency (7.8 s, 3.2 GB VRAM on an RTX 4060) makes it accessible on consumer hardware, unlike diffusion baselines that go out of memory on the same GPU. However, the FID of 72.5 remains an order of magnitude above diffusion models (12–27), and fine-grained spatial details remain under-constrained since all regularization comes from CLIP losses and the INR prior. Open questions include whether the INR-based approach can scale to higher resolutions or more complex compositional prompts, and whether a learned natural-image prior could close the remaining quality gap.
