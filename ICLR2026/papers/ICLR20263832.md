## Problem

Prior contextual online bilateral trade work (Gaucher et al., 2025) restricted valuations to linear functions of the context, leaving the general nonparametric case open. This paper asks whether sublinear regret is achievable when buyer and seller valuations are arbitrary L-Lipschitz functions of a d-dimensional context, under the most information-constrained feedback model (one-bit: only whether a trade occurred) and strong budget balance (same price posted to both parties). The core difficulty is that the gain-from-trade reward is discontinuous in both the price and the valuations, and the valuations themselves are never observed.

## Input / Output

At each round the learner observes a d-dimensional context vector x_t ∈ [0,1]^d and must post a single price p_t ∈ [0,1] to both the seller and the buyer simultaneously (strong budget balance). The only feedback received is the binary indicator I(s_t ≤ p_t ≤ b_t); the valuations s_t, b_t and the gain from trade are never revealed. The algorithm takes as input the horizon T, context dimension d, and optionally the Lipschitz constant L (a multi-scale variant removes this requirement), and outputs a sequence of prices with a worst-case regret guarantee of Õ(LT^{(d-1)/d}).

## Method

The algorithm maintains a hierarchical tree with branching factor 2^d over the context hypercube, where each node at level ℓ corresponds to a 2^{-ℓ}-sized region. When the traversal reaches an active (unmarked) node, two routines execute sequentially: REDUCE probes two prices p_L and p_U (centered on the parent's marking price at distance L·2^{-(ℓ-1)}) and waits for both to be rejected; by Lipschitzness, this geometrically forces all future valuations in the node's region to lie within O(L·2^{-ℓ}) of the diagonal, capping the achievable GFT. GUESS then samples uniformly at random from an ε-grid over the candidate price interval until a trade is accepted, marking the node. The key insight is that REDUCE's 'hide-and-seek' step converts the discontinuity problem into a bounded-GFT problem, reducing the per-node guessing regret from O(2^{-ℓ}/ε) to O(2^{-2ℓ}/ε) and yielding the optimal T^{(d-1)/d} rate. For unknown L, a multi-scale variant tests geometrically increasing Lipschitz scales in both REDUCE and GUESS, incurring only an extra O(log²(T)·L) factor.

## Results

The paper establishes a regret upper bound of Õ(L·T^((d-1)/d)) for the nonparametric contextual bilateral trade problem under one-bit feedback and strong budget balance (Theorem 1), with a matching lower bound of Ω(L·T^((d-1)/d)) even under full feedback (Theorem 3), proving tightness up to logarithmic factors. When the Lipschitz constant L is unknown, the multi-scale algorithm achieves O(log²(T)·L²·T^((d-1)/d)) regret (Theorem 2). Empirically (Figure 3), the observed regret slope for d ∈ {2,3,4} matches the theoretical T^((d-1)/d) rate, with d=2 appearing to grow at an even better rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include a direct algorithmic comparison table, as it is primarily a theoretical contribution. However, it positions its results against two key baselines: (1) the linear contextual model of Gaucher et al. (2025), which achieves Õ(T^(2/3)) regret but only under two-bit feedback and a restrictive linear valuation assumption; the present work handles the strictly harder one-bit feedback and arbitrary Lipschitz valuations at the cost of a dimension-dependent polynomial rate. (2) The generic Lipschitz bandit lower bound of Ω(T^((d+2)/(d+3))) from Lu et al. (2009) and Slivkins (2014), which would apply if the GFT reward were an arbitrary Lipschitz function; the paper's key technical contribution is exploiting the specific discontinuous structure of the GFT function to break this bound and achieve the tighter T^((d-1)/d) rate. The matching lower bound (Theorem 3) confirms that no algorithm can improve upon this rate in the nonparametric Lipschitz setting, even with full feedback.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contextual online bilateral trade problem from the linear valuation model of Gaucher et al. (2025) to arbitrary L-Lipschitz functions of the context, while preserving the two most stringent constraints: one-bit feedback (only observing whether a trade occurred) and strong budget balance (posting the same price to both parties). The algorithmic framework draws on the adaptive zooming / hierarchical tree construction of Slivkins (2014) and Cesa-Bianchi et al. (2017) for nonparametric contextual bandits, but the key technical departure is a novel REDUCE subroutine that deliberately seeks price rejections to geometrically constrain the adversary's valuation placement, exploiting the specific discontinuous structure of the gain-from-trade reward in a way that generic Lipschitz bandit techniques cannot.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first tight characterization (matching upper and lower bounds up to logarithmic factors) of nonparametric contextual bilateral trade under the most information-constrained feedback model, showing that the rate T^((d-1)/d) is achievable even when the learner only sees a single bit per round. This is notable because prior contextual no-regret algorithms required relaxing budget balance to handle one-bit feedback. Open questions include whether the tight rate survives under noisy valuations, how the results extend to profit maximization (as opposed to gain-from-trade maximization) with general Lipschitz contexts, and whether other budget balance notions (weak, global) yield better rates in the nonparametric setting.
