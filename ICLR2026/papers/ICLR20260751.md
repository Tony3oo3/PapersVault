## Problem

Current safety CoT tuning methods train Large Reasoning Models to imitate idealized, error-free refusal scripts, but this instills a critical vulnerability the authors term the 'Snowball Effect': a minor reasoning deviation in an early step progressively amplifies through the chain, causing either a gradual descent into harmful compliance or an escalating spiral of unnecessary over-refusal. The core gap is that these imitation-based approaches provide no training signal for how to recognize and recover from one's own flawed intermediate reasoning steps, leaving models without dynamic self-correction capability.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a single-turn user prompt (harmful, jailbreak, or benign) and generates a multi-step Chain-of-Thought reasoning chain followed by a final summary response. During training, the input is a prompt paired with an adversarially constructed CoT trajectory that contains an intentionally injected flawed step (a temptation or hesitation) immediately followed by a corrective step, and the model is trained to reproduce the entire self-correcting sequence. The dataset is small (1,000 samples: 800 temptation-correction, 200 hesitation-correction), matching the data volume of baselines like STAR-1.

## Method

AdvChain constructs an adversarial safety reasoning dataset by programmatically rewriting existing safe or helpful CoTs using a teacher LLM. For Temptation-Correction samples, a harmful rationalization step is injected at a coherent insertion point within a base safe refusal chain, followed by a strong correction step that identifies the danger and steers reasoning back to refusal; for Hesitation-Correction samples, an overcautious refusal step is injected into a base helpful chain, followed by a correction that re-evaluates the prompt and resumes the helpful path. The base LRM is then fine-tuned via standard autoregressive supervised learning on these self-correcting trajectories, maximizing the log-likelihood of generating the full adversarial CoT and final summary. The key insight is that training on trajectories containing intentional errors and their corrections teaches the model to recognize and halt reasoning drift, breaking the cognitive inertia that allows the snowball effect to compound, rather than merely memorizing the form of a correct refusal.

## Results

On HarmBench, AdvChain (1k samples) achieves an Attack Success Rate of 4.50% on DeepSeek-R1-7B and 4.00% on Qwen3-4B, approaching RealSafe-R1 (15k samples) at 2.00% and 2.50% respectively, while substantially outperforming same-data baselines STAR-1 (8.00%/2.50%), SafeChain (38.00%/33.00%), and UnsafeChain (26.00%/17.00%). Against the CoT-Hijacking attack, AdvChain reduces ASR to 9.33% (R1-7B) and 8.67% (Qwen3-4B), compared to 54.67% for STAR-1 and 44.00% for SafeChain. On over-refusal (XSTest), AdvChain achieves 18.00% ORR versus 42.00% for STAR-1 and 66.40% for RealSafe-R1, while preserving reasoning accuracy (e.g., Math-500: 93.40% vs. 92.80% base).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against STAR-1 (same 1k data), AdvChain achieves lower ASR on most benchmarks (e.g., 4.50% vs. 8.00% on HarmBench for R1-7B) and dramatically outperforms on CoT-Hijacking (9.33% vs. 54.67% ASR), while also reducing over-refusal from 42.00% to 18.00% on XSTest. Against SafeChain and UnsafeChain (also 1k), AdvChain shows consistent ASR improvements (e.g., 4.50% vs. 38.00% and 26.00% on HarmBench for R1-7B) and lower over-refusal rates. Against RealSafe-R1 (15k data, 15× more), AdvChain is slightly worse on raw ASR (4.50% vs. 2.00% on HarmBench) but substantially better on over-refusal (18.00% vs. 66.40% on XSTest), demonstrating a superior safety-utility trade-off at a fraction of the data cost. On adversarial robustness (WJ-AdvHarm, SafeUnlearning), AdvChain matches or exceeds RealSafe-R1 for Qwen3-4B (10.68% vs. 13.33% on WJ-AdvHarm; 17.44% vs. 37.21% on SafeUnlearning). Reasoning capabilities are preserved, with Math-500 scores within 1–2 points of the base models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant Safety CoT Tuning paradigm (STAR-1, SafeChain, UnsafeChain, RealSafe-R1), which trains LRMs to imitate idealized, error-free refusal chains. The key conceptual shift is reframing alignment from preventing flawed thoughts to actively correcting them: AdvChain constructs adversarial training data where reasoning chains contain intentional errors (temptation or hesitation) followed by explicit self-correction steps. This draws on the broader adversarial-training intuition but applies it to the model's own internal reasoning trajectory rather than external input perturbations, and it simultaneously addresses both harmful compliance and over-refusal through two complementary sample types.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Snowball Effect provides a concrete, empirically validated diagnostic for why current CoT safety alignment is brittle: models learn the form of correct reasoning but lack error-recovery signals, so minor deviations compound unchecked. The practical implication is significant—matching 15k-data baselines with only 1k adversarial samples suggests a more data-efficient path to robust alignment. However, limitations remain: the method depends on teacher-model quality for adversarial example generation, addresses only single-turn corrections, and is validated exclusively on small open-source models (≤7B), leaving open whether the approach transfers to frontier-scale LRMs or multi-turn adversarial settings.
