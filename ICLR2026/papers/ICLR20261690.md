## Problem

Existing multimodal hallucination benchmarks have largely saturated with SOTA MLLMs due to oversimplified binary (true/false) evaluation protocols and limited task diversity, while prior AI-generated counter-commonsense (CCS) benchmarks (HallusionBench, PhD, WHOOPS) still suffer from insufficient sample diversity, suboptimal image realism, and narrow question formats. FREAK addresses this gap by introducing a fine-grained, photorealistic CCS benchmark with diverse question types that can meaningfully discriminate hallucination severity across current state-of-the-art multimodal models.

## Input / Output

Each FREAK item pairs a photorealistic CCS image with either a multiple-choice question (four options: the correct CCS-aligned answer, a commonsense distractor, an AI-generated distractor, and a fixed 'not listed' option) or a free-form question. For MCQs, cyclic permutation is applied across all six option orderings to eliminate position bias, and correctness is determined by the selected option; for free-form questions, GPT-5-mini acts as a judge categorizing responses into Correct, Commonsense Error, or Other Error. The primary metrics are overall accuracy and Hallucination Rate (the proportion of responses that default to the commonsense answer rather than the visually correct CCS detail).

## Method

FREAK's core contribution is a three-step automated 'generate-then-edit' pipeline that produces photorealistic images with localized counter-commonsense violations. First, LLMs generate diverse CCS descriptions as tuples (O, A, W) — target object, correct attribute, and counter-commonsense attribute — guided by five modification types (quantity, color/shape, deletion/addition, replacement, logical/physical violation). Second, Seedream 3.0 synthesizes a normal photorealistic image from (O, A), and SeedEdit 3.0 then applies the localized CCS modification W to produce the final image, ensuring overall realism while embedding a single anomalous detail. Third, LLMs generate questions and distractors conditioned on the full tuple, followed by manual human verification of all 1,786 images and 1,799 questions across six subtasks (Detection, Counting, Attribute, Analysis, Position, OCR). This approach overcomes the key limitation of direct prompt-to-image generation, which fails to produce realistic CCS images because generation models lack CCS training data and default to commonsense-compliant outputs.

## Results

On FREAK, SOTA MLLMs achieve only 42–45% overall accuracy, far below the human baseline of 86.71%, revealing a ~40-point gap. The best model, Gemini-2.5-Pro, reaches 45.49% accuracy with a 40.26% hallucination rate, while mainstream models cluster in the 30–43% range. Counting is the weakest subtask (best: 24.91% by MiniCPM-4V), whereas Attribute and OCR are relatively stronger. Enabling CoT reasoning degrades accuracy for most models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B drops from 39.39% to 33.39%) and increases hallucination rates, indicating that extended reasoning amplifies reliance on parametric knowledge over visual evidence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FREAK is positioned against five prior AI-generated counter-commonsense (CCS) benchmarks. Compared to VLind-Bench (il Lee et al., 2025), where GPT-4o achieves 89.4% on binary Y/N questions, FREAK drops GPT-4.1 to 42.01% on free-form and multiple-choice questions, demonstrating substantially higher discriminative power. PhD-ccs (Liu et al., 2025) reports ~79% for GPT-series models on Y/N tasks, while HallusionBench (Guan et al., 2024) yields 62.28% for GPT-4V—both are far easier than FREAK's 42.01%. VLMBias (Vo et al., 2025) focuses solely on counting and achieves 20.25% with o4-mini, a narrow scope compared to FREAK's six subtasks. WHOOPS (Bitton-Guetta et al., 2023) provides no GPT-series evaluation and uses only 500 images. FREAK's key advantage is its combination of photorealistic images, fine-grained localized CCS edits, diverse question formats (free-form + MCQ with cyclic permutation), and six cognitive subtasks, making it the most challenging and comprehensive CCS hallucination benchmark to date.

## Relation to Prior Work

FREAK extends the line of AI-generated counter-commonsense (CCS) hallucination benchmarks (HallusionBench, PhD, WHOOPS, VLind-Bench) by replacing their manual modification or direct prompt-to-image pipelines with a 'generate-then-edit' paradigm (Seedream 3.0 + SeedEdit 3.0) that produces photorealistic images with localized fine-grained anomalies. It also departs from the binary true/false evaluation protocol dominant in prior work (POPE, AMBER, HallusionBench) by introducing free-form and four-option multiple-choice questions with a structured distractor design that explicitly isolates commonsense bias. The benchmark's six subtask taxonomy (Detection, Counting, Attribute, Analysis, OCR, Position) broadens the evaluation scope beyond the object-presence focus of earlier benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FREAK exposes a ~40-point accuracy gap between SOTA MLLMs (~45%) and untrained humans (86.71%) on tasks that are straightforward for humans, providing a non-saturated evaluation tool as prior benchmarks have reached metric saturation. The mechanistic finding that CoT reasoning consistently degrades fine-grained visual performance—models initially identify correct answers but generate hallucinated content that reverses their judgment—challenges the assumption that extended reasoning universally helps multimodal tasks and points toward the need for better visual-linguistic alignment rather than more textual deliberation. Key limitations include the relatively small scale (1,786 images) constrained by manual verification, dependence on specific generation/editing models, and the open question of whether scaling the dataset or improving visual encoders would close the observed gap.
