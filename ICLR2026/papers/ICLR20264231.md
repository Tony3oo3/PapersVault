## Problem

Prior LLM memorization research is split between controlled studies on small models with synthetic data (precise but unrepresentative) and observational studies on large pretrained models (representative but lacking causal control). This gap makes it difficult to measure causal quantities like the number of duplicates needed to memorize a test set example, or to disentangle whether memorization stems from data simplicity or repetition. HUBBLE bridges this gap by providing fully open-source pretrained models with controlled, randomized perturbations at a scale accessible to academic researchers.

## Input / Output

The training input is the DCLM-baseline-1.0 corpus (100B or 500B tokens) with perturbation texts spliced into training sequences between documents, each surrounded by EOS tags and duplicated at controlled rates of {0×, 1×, 4×, 16×, 64×, 256×}. Perturbations total only 79.9M tokens (0.08% of the 100B corpus), and at most one perturbation example is inserted per sequence. The output is a suite of 1B and 8B parameter LLMs (standard and perturbed variants) accompanied by memorization evaluations via three elicitation methods—loss, loss-based choice, and generative continuation—plus membership inference ROC AUC scores and unlearning effectiveness metrics across copyright, privacy, and test set contamination domains.

## Method

HUBBLE trains Llama 3-architecture models (with OLMo's 50K tokenizer and untied embeddings for interpretability) on the DCLM corpus after decontaminating it of any documents matching perturbation texts. Perturbations are designed across three risk domains: copyright (Gutenberg passages stratified by popularity, Wikipedia articles, MRPC/PAWS paraphrases), privacy (templated YAGO biographies with 9 PII attributes, ECtHR court cases, PersonaChat dialogues), and test set contamination (PopQA, Winogrande in infill and MCQ forms, MMLU, HellaSwag, PIQA, plus new ELLie and MUNCH sets). The core experiment uses a 2×2×2 factorial design (model size × standard/perturbed × 100B/500B tokens) to establish dilution effects, while timing runs insert perturbations only during specific training quarters to study ordering effects. The key design principle is randomization: which specific texts are inserted and at what duplication rate are randomized, enabling causal measurement of memorization strength as a function of frequency relative to corpus size.

## Results

The core dilution experiment shows that for the same duplication level, the 8B model trained on 500B tokens exhibits weaker memorization than the 100B-token model across nearly all tasks and domains. On the HUBBLE MIA benchmark (Table 1), membership inference ROC AUC ranges from near-random (0.53–0.56) at Dup=1 to near-perfect (0.996–1.0) at Dup=256 for the 8B/500B model on Gutenberg Unpopular, with MinK%++ as the strongest attack. For privacy, PII reconstruction on the 8B (100B tokens) perturbed model reaches near-100% accuracy with just 16 duplications. On the unlearning benchmark, no method (RMU, RR, SatImp) achieves the desired target of matching the standard model on the forget set while retaining performance elsewhere; SatImp performs best but still degrades the keep set.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Loss | 0.629 | 0.539 | 0.556 | 0.732 | 0.996 | 1.0 |
| MinK% | 0.629 | 0.539 | 0.556 | 0.732 | 0.996 | 1.0 |
| MinK%++ | 0.666 | 0.545 | 0.620 | 0.813 | 0.987 | 0.949 |

## Baselines & Comparisons

HUBBLE is a model suite and benchmark rather than a single method, so comparisons are structured as internal ablations and method evaluations. On general capabilities, HUBBLE models perform on par with other open-source models (Pythia, OLMo) at similar parameter and data scales, confirming no degradation from perturbations. Among MIA methods, MinK%++ achieves the highest AUC at low-to-mid duplication (e.g., 0.813 at Dup=16 vs. 0.732 for Loss/MinK%), but surprisingly does not reach 100% AUC at Dup=256 (0.949) unlike the simpler Loss and MinK% baselines. Among unlearning methods, SatImp produces checkpoints closest to the desired target, while RMU and RR perform worse and degrade both the keep set and general test performance. The standard (unperturbed) models serve as the gold-standard reference, showing that all unlearning methods shift the model toward this baseline but overshoot, erasing distribution-level knowledge beyond the targeted forget set.

## Relation to Prior Work

HUBBLE bridges a gap between two existing lines of memorization research: controlled studies on small synthetic models (Zhang et al., 2023; Allen-Zhu & Li, 2024) that allow precise causal measurement but are far from commercial scale, and observational studies of large pretrained models (Prashanth et al., 2025) that lack the randomization needed for causal inference. By inserting randomized perturbations (with controlled duplication levels) into a real 500B-token DCLM corpus and training Llama-3-architecture models at 1B and 8B parameters, the paper enables causal memorization measurements at a scale previously inaccessible to academic researchers. The design follows the open-source scientific release paradigm of Pythia and OLMo, but is specifically structured around memorization risk domains (copyright, privacy, test set contamination) rather than general capability analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HUBBLE is likely to become a standard reference suite for memorization research, analogous to how Pythia became the go-to for LLM scaling analysis, because it is the first fully open-source model suite with known, randomized perturbations across multiple risk domains at practical model sizes. The two practical best practices it establishes—diluting sensitive data via larger corpora and ordering sensitive data early in training to exploit natural forgetting—give model developers concrete, empirically validated mitigation strategies. Open questions remain about how these findings scale to the 10T+ token regime of commercial models, whether the forgetting mechanism generalizes to non-English or multimodal settings, and how to operationalize the MIA and unlearning benchmarks the paper introduces into production auditing pipelines.
