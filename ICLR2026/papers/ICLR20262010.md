## Problem

Pre-trained equivariant materials foundation models require domain-specific fine-tuning to handle physicochemical diversity and level-of-theory mismatches, but full fine-tuning overfits on small domain datasets while existing parameter-efficient methods (ELoRA, adapters) constrain how parameters are updated without controlling which parameters change. No sparsity-promoting adaptation strategy exists for equivariant MLIPs that selectively modifies only the most physically relevant interaction paths while strictly preserving E(3) symmetry.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained equivariant MLIP (MACE-MP-0b3, MACE-OFF23, or NequIP-OAM-L) and a small domain-specific dataset of atomic structures (positions + species), producing a fine-tuned model that predicts per-atom energy (meV/atom), forces (meV/Å), and optionally per-atom magnetic moments (μB). The adaptation operates exclusively on the path-weight tensors within equivariant layers, adding sparse delta weights (ΔW) to frozen pre-trained weights; at inference, ΔW is merged into W so execution speed and memory are identical to the original model. The method also reports total sparsity (fraction of all parameters unchanged) and layer-wise sparsity (fraction of ΔW entries exactly zero) as interpretability metrics.

## Method

The method decomposes each path-weight tensor as W' = W + ΔW, where W is the frozen pre-trained weight and ΔW is a sparse adaptation tensor. Sparsity in ΔW is enforced via Soft Threshold Reparameterization (STR) with a learnable per-layer threshold τ, but the key innovation is decoupling the gradient updates of τ and ΔW (separate learning rates and weight decays), which resolves training instability observed with naive STR in equivariant architectures. Because ΔW modulates only the learnable scalar weights on fixed Clebsch-Gordan coefficients, E(3) equivariance is preserved by construction regardless of which entries are pruned. The method supports module-specific tuning (linear layers, FCTP layers, or both) and two sparsity regimes: a low-sparsity setting (λ_τ=0.01) achieving best accuracy with ~3% parameter updates, and a high-sparsity setting (λ_τ=0.3) achieving comparable accuracy with ~0.5% updates while revealing physically interpretable patterns such as enhanced d-orbital contributions in transition metals.

## Results

On the rMD17 molecular benchmark, the proposed method (Ours L, λ_τ=0.01) surpasses both full fine-tuning and ELoRA on 8 of 10 molecules for energy MAE, while the high-sparsity variant (Ours H, λ_τ=0.3) outperforms ELoRA across all 10 molecules while updating only ~3% of parameters. On the LAM inorganic crystal benchmark, Ours L achieves dramatically lower energy MAE than full fine-tuning on systems with large distribution shift (e.g., Sn: 2.18 vs. 32.74 meV/atom; H2O-PD: 3.67 vs. 11.67 meV/atom), and Ours H matches full fine-tuning accuracy while updating merely 0.5–0.7% of parameters. On the TM-O-Spin magnetic benchmark, Ours L achieves 9.50±0.11 meV/atom energy MAE, outperforming full fine-tuning (11.58±2.87) and ELoRA (12.44±1.88) with 90.21% total sparsity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: full-parameter fine-tuning and ELoRA (low-rank adaptation for equivariant GNNs). Against ELoRA, the proposed method consistently wins: on rMD17, Ours(L) beats ELoRA on 8/10 molecules for energy, and Ours(H) outperforms ELoRA across all 10 molecules; on LAM, Ours(L) achieves substantially lower energy MAE on Sn (2.18 vs. 9.33), H2O-PD (3.67 vs. 4.98), and AgUAu-PBE (1.26 vs. 1.89). Against full fine-tuning, the proposed method matches or exceeds accuracy on most datasets while updating only ~3% (L) or ~0.5% (H) of parameters; the most dramatic gains appear on distribution-shifted systems like Sn (2.18 vs. 32.74 meV/atom energy) and H2O-PD (3.67 vs. 11.67 meV/atom), where full fine-tuning overfits to the small domain-specific dataset. On the TM-O-Spin magnetic benchmark, Ours(L) achieves the best energy (9.50 vs. 11.58 Full, 12.44 ELoRA) and force (70.46 vs. 96.89 Full, 116.31 ELoRA) MAE. On the larger MP-mag dataset, full fine-tuning edges out the proposed method on force prediction (37.56 vs. 39.59–42.11 meV/Å), suggesting dense updates become more competitive with sufficiently large in-domain data, while the proposed method retains the best magnetic moment accuracy (0.011–0.014 μB vs. 0.013–0.015 μB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Soft Threshold Reparameterization (STR) from the computer vision sparsity literature and adapts it to the fine-tuning of equivariant MLIPs, with a key modification: decoupling the threshold parameter τ from the weight update ΔW to prevent training instability in equivariant architectures. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm (LoRA, adapters, ELoRA) that constrains *how* parameters are updated (e.g., via low-rank factorization), instead controlling *which* parameters are updated by promoting sparsity in the path-weight tensors. The method is applied on top of MACE and NequIP foundation models, positioning itself as complementary to rather than competitive with existing equivariant adaptation methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that 0.5–3% parameter updates suffice for domain adaptation of MLIPs, combined with physically interpretable sparsity patterns (e.g., enhanced d-orbital channels for transition metals), offers a practical and transparent alternative to dense fine-tuning for the materials science community. The extension to magnetic moment prediction and magnetism-aware energy modeling broadens the scope of foundation MLIPs beyond conventional force/energy tasks. Open questions include whether structured sparse pre-training (rather than post-hoc fine-tuning) can deliver genuine inference speedups, and whether the observed sparsity patterns generalize across architectures and chemical domains beyond the tested benchmarks.
