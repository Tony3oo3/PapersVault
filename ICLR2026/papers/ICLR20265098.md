## Problem

Existing text-to-3D scene generation methods treat layout composition as a purely geometric problem, omitting physical reasoning and producing scenes with floating objects, unstable stacking, and interpenetrations that collapse under simulation. No prior approach integrates differentiable rigid-body contact simulation into the generation pipeline to guarantee physically plausible, intersection-free, and simulation-ready multi-object scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265098/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single natural-language text prompt as input and internally generates a reference image to guide object extraction and spatial relation inference. The output is a set of individually segmented, textured 3D meshes arranged in a physically stable, intersection-free configuration, organized as a hierarchical scene tree encoding parent-child support relations. The resulting scene is directly importable into a physics simulator for downstream tasks such as scene editing and robotic policy evaluation.

## Method

PAT3D decomposes generation into three stages: (1) a VLM extracts object labels and pairwise physical dependencies (on, contain, support) from a generated reference image, organizing them into a hierarchical scene tree rooted at the ground; (2) a layout initialization module back-projects monocular depth to obtain a preliminary arrangement, then refines it via breadth-first traversal of the scene tree—enforcing horizontal containment for parent-child pairs and non-overlap for siblings, while lifting children above parents along gravity to guarantee intersection-free initial conditions; (3) a simulation-in-the-loop optimization refines the initial layout q₀ by running a differentiable affine-body dynamics simulator (GPU-optimized, IPC-style contact with Coulomb friction) forward to static equilibrium, then backpropagating a semantic consistency loss (bounding-box containment of children within parents) through the time-stepped solver via implicit differentiation, updating q₀ with Adam over up to 50 iterations. The key insight is that artificial time-stepping converts the intractable static-equilibrium constraint into a sequence of differentiable steps, enabling gradient-based layout correction while preserving physical validity.

## Results

PAT3D achieves the highest CLIP Score (31.79) and Physical Plausibility Score (88.5) among all methods, while being the only approach that simultaneously achieves zero simulated displacement and zero penetration ratio. The ablation rows confirm that layout initialization eliminates all interpenetration (Pen.Ratio drops from 14.11 to 0) and simulation-in-the-loop optimization then restores physical stability (Displacement drops from 2.91 to 0) while boosting the Physical Plausibility Score from 34.2 to 88.5. Evaluation is conducted on 18 text prompts describing contact-rich scenes, with metrics rendered from 18 viewpoints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAT3D outperforms all three baselines on every metric. Against GraphDreamer (Gao et al., CVPR 2024), PAT3D improves the Physical Plausibility Score from 40.0 to 88.5 and eliminates all interpenetration (Pen. Ratio 61.72 → 0); GraphDreamer also struggles with spatial reasoning, often ignoring stated constraints and producing chaotic arrangements. Against Blender-MCP (Ahuja, 2025), PAT3D reduces displacement from 1.03 to 0, indicating Blender-MCP's scenes are unstable under simulation (floating objects, suspended plates), while PAT3D's scenes remain in static equilibrium. Against MIDI (Huang et al., 2024), PAT3D achieves a substantially lower Pen. Ratio (0 vs. 110.80) and higher Physical Plausibility Score (88.5 vs. 62.7); MIDI avoids interpenetration only by producing cluttered, tightly packed configurations that compromise individual object quality. On VQA Score, PAT3D (0.68) is slightly below MIDI (0.63) and Scene Init. (0.70), but the gap is small and PAT3D leads on the more discriminative CLIP Score (31.79 vs. 29.68).

## Relation to Prior Work

PAT3D builds on the text-to-3D scene generation line (GraphDreamer, MIDI, Blender-MCP) by replacing their purely geometric or heuristic layout composition with a physics-grounded pipeline. The simulation backbone draws directly from the incremental potential contact (IPC) and affine body dynamics literature, with the differentiable time-stepping formulation from Fang et al. (2021) enabling gradient-based layout optimization. The key departure from prior work is the integration of a VLM-extracted hierarchical scene tree as an intermediate representation that encodes physical dependencies (support, containment) and drives both intersection-free initialization and the simulation-in-the-loop loss, a combination absent from all prior scene generation methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PAT3D is the first framework to guarantee that text-generated 3D scenes are simultaneously intersection-free, physically stable, and simulation-ready, opening a direct path to downstream applications like robotic manipulation policy evaluation and interactive scene editing that were previously infeasible with geometric-only pipelines. However, the evaluation is limited to 18 prompts on a single GPU, the optimizer is local (no global convergence guarantee), and the spatial relation vocabulary (on, contain, support) is narrow—relations like 'hanging' or 'attached to' remain unhandled. Scaling to larger scenes, broader relation types, and global optimization strategies are the most pressing open questions.
