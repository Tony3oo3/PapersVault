## Problem

Standard flow-based generative models pair noise and data independently during training, which inherently induces curved ODE trajectories and high inference costs (NFEs > 100). Existing OT-based coupling methods that address this issue rely on mini-batch discrete OT, which suffers from the curse of dimensionality, produces biased transport plans, and lacks convergence guarantees for the learned coupling.

## Input / Output

The method takes i.i.d. Gaussian noise samples (optionally conditioned on a class label) and produces generated RGB images. It operates in pixel space for CIFAR-10 (32×32×3) or in a pretrained VAE latent space of size 28×28×4 for ImageNet256. A key operational detail is that all noise samples for the entire training run are generated upfront and stored as (seed, index) tuples, with the index determined by the precomputed SDOT map, rather than being sampled fresh each iteration.

## Method

AlignFlow introduces a two-stage training procedure: in Stage 1, it computes a Semi-Discrete Optimal Transport (SDOT) map between the continuous noise distribution p₀ and the fully discrete empirical data distribution p₁ by optimizing dual weights via gradient ascent on the Laguerre-cell objective, partitioning the noise space into Laguerre cells each deterministically mapped to one data point. In Stage 2, a standard FGM (Flow Matching, Shortcut Model, MeanFlow, etc.) is trained using this precomputed map to deterministically pair each noise sample with its assigned data point, replacing the random coupling in Algorithm 1. Because the data distribution is fully specified by the finite dataset, SDOT avoids the curse of dimensionality that plagues discrete OT between two continuous distributions, and the convexity of the SDOT objective provides guaranteed convergence. The approach is batch-invariant (each noise sample always maps to the same data point regardless of batch size), adds less than 1% computational overhead, and integrates as a plug-and-play component into any existing FGM framework.

## Results

On ImageNet256 with DiT-B/2, AlignFlow consistently reduces FID-50k across all tested FGM algorithms: Flow Matching improves from 125.62 to 93.16 (NFE=4) and from 305.04 to 276.18 (NFE=1); Live Reflow improves from 94.75 to 60.23 (NFE=4); and Shortcut Models improve from 33.11 to 30.31 (NFE=4). On ImageNet256 with MeanFlow (NFE=1), AlignFlow reduces FID from 3.43 to 3.23 for SiT-XL/2 (676M params) and from 15.53 to 13.75 for SiT-B/4. On CIFAR-10 with U-Net, AlignFlow achieves FID-50k of 3.71 (DOPRI5) versus 3.82 for Minibatch OT, with improvements across all ODE integrators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlignFlow is compared against four categories of baselines. Against Minibatch OT (Tong et al., 2023) on CIFAR-10 U-Net, AlignFlow achieves lower FID-50k across all three ODE integrators (e.g., 3.71 vs. 3.82 with DOPRI5), demonstrating both faster convergence and better final quality. Against vanilla Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2022) on ImageNet256 DiT-B/2, AlignFlow reduces FID-50k by 32.46 points at NFE=4 (93.16 vs. 125.62) and by 28.86 points at NFE=1. Against the Shortcut Model family (Frans et al., 2025), which includes Consistency Training, Live Reflow, and Shortcut Models, AlignFlow provides consistent improvements of 2.7–34.5 FID points depending on the specific algorithm, with the largest gains for Live Reflow (↓34.52 at NFE=4). Against MeanFlow (Geng et al., 2025) on ImageNet256, AlignFlow improves FID at NFE=1 across all four SiT backbone sizes, from ↓1.78 (SiT-B/4) to ↓0.20 (SiT-XL/2), confirming scalability to 676M-parameter models. In all comparisons, AlignFlow wins without any architectural or hyperparameter modifications, validating its plug-and-play design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Flow Matching framework (Lipman et al., 2022) and the SDOT solver with MRE-based convergence criteria (Liu et al., 2021), but departs fundamentally from prior OT-based coupling methods for FGMs. Where Tong et al. (2023) and Pooladian et al. (2023) estimate discrete OT plans between minibatches of noise and data samples, and Kornilov et al. (2024) learn a continuous OT map via ICNN, AlignFlow observes that the training data is inherently finite (discrete) while the noise prior is continuous, making the natural formulation semi-discrete OT rather than fully discrete or fully continuous. This reframing eliminates the curse of dimensionality that limits discrete OT and removes the unguaranteed convergence of learned continuous maps, yielding a deterministic, batch-invariant noise-to-data alignment (NDA) that can be precomputed with negligible overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AlignFlow provides a clean, plug-and-play coupling mechanism that consistently improves FID across a wide range of FGM architectures (U-Net, DiT, SiT) and training objectives (Flow Matching, Shortcut, MeanFlow, Live Reflow) with less than 1% additional training cost, making it immediately adoptable by practitioners. The deterministic, batch-invariant nature of the SDOT map addresses a practical bottleneck for large-scale training where memory constraints force small batches, and the bypass of the curse of dimensionality provides a principled reason for the gains. Open questions include extension to text-to-image generation (sketched in Appendix A via text clustering), whether the approach transfers to non-image modalities, and how it interacts with progressive or multi-resolution training schedules.
