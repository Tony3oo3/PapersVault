## Problem

RL-based reasoning training is notoriously resource-intensive, typically requiring full-parameter updates on large models and substantial compute. This paper probes the fundamental efficiency limits of RL reasoning by asking whether a tiny 1.5B base model with only low-rank parameter updates (LoRA) can achieve reasoning performance competitive with or superior to SOTA full-parameter RL models, and at what minimal cost.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (a mathematical or scientific problem) and generates a structured reasoning trace followed by a final answer, with a maximum completion length of 3,000 tokens during training. Training rewards are verifiable signals composed of accuracy, format compliance, and length penalties, applied to outputs sampled in groups of 4 from the policy. Evaluation uses zero-shot Pass@1 Mean@1 across six benchmarks (AIME24/25, AMC23, MATH500, GPQA Diamond, Minerva), with a robust variant using Mean@10 over 10 seeds.

## Method

Tina post-trains the DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B base model using LoRA (rank 32, alpha 128) during GRPO-based reinforcement learning, updating only a small fraction of parameters while keeping the base weights frozen. Two key training tricks accelerate adaptation: the LoRA alpha is set to 4× the rank (rather than the conventional 2×) to more strongly incorporate learned reasoning structures, and the cosine learning rate schedule is stretched over 2× the training horizon to maintain a higher effective learning rate throughout. The paper identifies a 'phase transition' in training dynamics where format-related metrics (format reward, completion length) sharply destabilize while accuracy reward evolves gradually, with peak reasoning performance consistently occurring just before this transition. The authors hypothesize that LoRA's efficiency stems from rapidly adapting the structural format of reasoning rewarded by RL while largely preserving the base model's pre-trained knowledge, making it a more compute-efficient process than full-parameter relearning.

## Results

The best Tina model (Tina-Open-RS2) achieves a 50.60% average zero-shot Pass@1 score across six reasoning benchmarks (AIME24/25, AMC23, MATH500, GPQA Diamond, Minerva), with 43.33% on AIME24, at an estimated $9 USD training cost. All five Tina models achieve average scores between 48.16% and 50.60%, with training completed in only 19%–57% of a single epoch. The paper reports a >20% reasoning performance increase over the base model (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B at 41.18% avg) and a 260× reduction in computational cost compared to full-parameter SOTA RL reasoning models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tina models are compared against their corresponding full-parameter RL-trained counterparts on the same 1.5B base model. Tina-Open-RS2 achieves the largest margin, beating the Open-RS2 baseline by 9.0 percentage points (50.60% vs. 41.60% avg). Tina-Open-RS1 and Tina-Open-RS3 outperform their baselines by 4.09 and 3.39 points respectively, and Tina-STILL-3 beats STILL-3-1.5B-preview by 3.3 points (48.16% vs. 44.86%). The one exception is Tina-DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview, which narrowly trails the DeepScaleR baseline by 0.36 points (48.38% vs. 48.74%), though it reaches this level at only 19% of a training epoch versus the full run. Against other SOTA 1.5B reasoning models (L1-Exact at 47.93%, FastCurl at 46.45%), the best Tina model (50.60%) leads by 2.7 and 4.2 points respectively, all while using LoRA-based parameter-efficient training at a fraction of the compute cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-established threads: parameter-efficient fine-tuning via LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) and RL-based reasoning with verifiable rewards using GRPO (Shao et al., 2024; DeepSeek-AI, 2025). It departs from the dominant paradigm in open-source reasoning models (STILL, DeepScaleR, Open-RS, SimpleRL, L1), which all employ full-parameter RL training, by substituting LoRA adapters for full weight updates during the RL loop. The conceptual framing draws on the knowledge-capacity vs. reasoning-capacity distinction (Allen-Zhu and Li, 2024) to hypothesize that LoRA's low-rank updates are sufficient because RL reasoning primarily requires format adaptation rather than knowledge integration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving competitive reasoning performance at ~$9 (vs. hundreds of dollars for full-parameter baselines) dramatically lowers the barrier to RL-based reasoning research and could enable broader experimentation with training recipes and reward designs. The 'phase transition' observation and the format-adaptation hypothesis, if validated at scale, could reshape how the community thinks about what RL actually learns in reasoning models. Key open questions include whether the findings generalize beyond 1.5B models and math/science benchmarks, whether the format-vs-knowledge separation holds for more complex reasoning tasks, and whether the observed 'less compute, more performance' inversion for LoRA persists with longer training or larger datasets.
