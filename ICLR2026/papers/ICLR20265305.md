## Problem

Standard representation-similarity metrics (CKA, SVCCA, PWCCA, RSA) compare activation sets pointwise at fixed inputs, remaining blind to how features rotate and evolve as inputs move along natural directions. Two models can score near-identical under these metrics yet behave very differently under adversarial or corruption stress because their intermediate features undergo different path-dependent transports. The paper asks whether a gauge-invariant, path-sensitive statistic can capture this missing geometric structure and relate it to robustness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained network's layer activations on a pool of images (N_pool = 2048 for gauge fixing), constructs a small closed loop in input space as a 12-point circle on a 2D PCA plane fitted to 512 nearest training neighbors in pixel space, and evaluates the layer at each loop vertex. Features are globally z-scored (or ZCA-corr whitened) and, if the post-readout dimension exceeds 1024, projected by a fixed Johnson-Lindenstrauss map. The output is a scalar h_norm ∈ [0,1] (Frobenius gap of the composed holonomy matrix from identity, normalized by 2√p), the full SO(p) holonomy matrix H, and its eigen-angle multiset {θ_j}, all reported per layer, per radius, and per training regime.

## Method

The estimator treats a layer's representation as a field over input space and defines a discrete connection: for each edge of a small loop, it finds a shared k-NN neighborhood at the edge midpoint in whitened feature space, softly centers both point clouds, learns a shared q-dimensional right-singular subspace from the stacked clouds, and solves a rotation-only (SO(q)) Procrustes alignment within that subspace. The local rotation is embedded back to SO(p) via B R^(q) B^T + (I − BB^T), and composing all edge rotations around the loop yields the holonomy matrix H(γ). Global whitening fixes the gauge, shared-midpoint neighbors control index mismatch, and the SO (not O) constraint prevents reflection flips; together these guardrails ensure h_norm vanishes at small radii and is invariant to orthogonal (and, post-whitening, affine) reparameterizations. The paper proves these invariances, establishes a linear null for affine layers, derives an O(r) small-radius limit, and provides a finite-sample error decomposition separating subspace-truncation, index-mismatch, and curvature contributions via Davis-Kahan/Wedin and Procrustes perturbation bounds.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18, layer 2), holonomy at r=0.10 ranges from 3.04×10⁻⁷ (label smoothing) to 4.74×10⁻⁷ (adversarial PGD training), with strong regime-level correlations to robustness: r≈0.94 with FGSM accuracy and r≈−0.96 with corruption accuracy. On MNIST, deeper layers show larger holonomy (Hidden 2 mean 2.86×10⁻⁶ vs. Hidden 1 6.42×10⁻⁷ at r=0.10) with fitted radius slopes of 6.10×10⁻⁶ and 1.54×10⁻⁶ respectively. A key control shows that despite near-maximal aligned CKA (0.987), composed holonomy remains nonzero, confirming that holonomy captures pathwise geometry invisible to pointwise similarity. Small-radius behavior is consistent with the predicted O(r) scaling, with a fitted slope of 1.44×10⁻⁷ (95% CI [−1.07×10⁻⁷, 4.22×10⁻⁷]) on CIFAR-10 layer 2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compete with a single baseline method but positions holonomy as complementary to existing pointwise similarity metrics (CKA, SVCCA, PWCCA). The critical comparison is against CKA: on MNIST Hidden 1, two representations achieve aligned CKA of 0.987 (near-identical pointwise overlap), yet their composed holonomy remains measurably nonzero, demonstrating that CKA is blind to pathwise geometry. Across the four CIFAR-10 training regimes, holonomy separates adversarially trained models (4.74×10⁻⁷) from ERM (3.46×10⁻⁷) and label smoothing (3.04×10⁻⁷) at r=0.10, tracking the robustness–accuracy tradeoff that CKA does not capture. Per-seed partial correlations conditioning on clean accuracy are modest (r≈0.22–0.28 for FGSM/PGD-10, near zero for corruption), indicating that holonomy's strongest signal is at the regime level rather than as a per-run predictor. Ablations confirm that the estimator is stable: varying (k,q) over a 9-point grid changes h_norm by only 7.20×10⁻⁷, and the linear null (replacing nonlinearities with identity) drives holonomy to the numerical floor (~10⁻⁸).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the classical differential-geometric notion of holonomy (Ambrose–Singer, 1953) as a diagnostic for learned neural representations, combining it with orthogonal Procrustes alignment (Schönemann, 1966; Kabsch, 1976) and ZCA whitening (Kessy et al., 2018) to define a discrete, gauge-invariant connection on layer activations. It positions itself as complementary to pointwise similarity metrics (CKA, SVCCA, PWCCA) that compare unordered activation sets, and to infinitesimal equivariance tests (Lenc & Vedaldi, 2015; Gruver et al., 2022) that measure local Lie-derivative error without composing transports around closed loops. Unlike gauge-equivariant architectures (Cohen et al., 2019; Bronstein et al., 2021) that build a connection into the model by design, this work measures the emergent transport of standard CNNs/MLPs, making it a post-hoc probe rather than an architectural constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Representation holonomy offers a practical, layer-wise diagnostic that captures path-dependent geometry invisible to CKA/SVCCA, and its correlation with adversarial and corruption robustness (r ≈ 0.94–0.96 at the regime level) suggests it could serve as a cheap, label-free early-training signal for model selection. However, the per-seed partial correlations after controlling for clean accuracy are modest (0.22–0.28), and the experiments are limited to MNIST and CIFAR-10/100 with small loop radii, leaving open whether the signal persists at ImageNet scale or in deeper architectures. Key limitations include dependence on loop construction (PCA planes may miss semantic directions), the rotation-only Procrustes discarding scaling/shear, and the assumption of a single global feature metric via whitening. Promising extensions include constraining loops to augmentation orbits, applying the probe to diffusion/score networks where the learned field should theoretically be curl-free, and developing per-class or token-wise gauges for transformers.
