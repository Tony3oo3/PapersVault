## Problem

Prior work (Fotakis et al., 2021) showed that Gaussian mean estimation from coarse samples is sample-efficient under convex partitions but left two fundamental questions open: which convex partitions are identifiable (i.e., allow unique mean recovery), and whether a computationally efficient algorithm exists for all identifiable convex partitions. The prior algorithm required an exponential-size grid search, and no geometric characterization of identifiability was known.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives i.i.d. coarse samples, each being a set P from an unknown convex partition of R^d (encoded as linear inequalities or via a separation oracle with inner radius), along with accuracy ε, confidence δ, and a norm bound D on the true mean. It outputs an estimate μ̂ satisfying ||μ̂ − μ*||₂ ≤ ε with probability 1−δ, using Õ(dD²/α⁴ + d/(α⁴ε²)) samples where α quantifies information preservation. For the regression-with-friction application, inputs are (x, z=c(y)) pairs and the output is a weight vector ŵ with squared error Õ(d/(α⁴n)).

## Method

The paper provides two main results. First, a geometric characterization: a convex partition is non-identifiable if and only if almost every set is a slab (translation-invariant) in some direction, proven by combining variance reduction inequalities for truncated Gaussians with the equality case of the Prékopa-Leindler inequality to show that unit-variance projections force constant cross-sections. Second, a polynomial-time algorithm running projected SGD on the coarse negative log-likelihood, which is convex under convex partitions. Two key technical challenges are addressed: (i) the log-likelihood is not globally strongly convex, so local strong convexity around μ* is established via α-information preservation and Pinsker's inequality; (ii) the second moment of stochastic gradients can be unbounded for unbounded partition sets, resolved by reducing the general partition to an R-local partition (all sets bounded) using high-probability concentration of Gaussian samples, then bounding gradient moments on the local partition.

## Results

The paper provides two main theoretical results. First, Theorem 3.1 gives a geometric characterization: a convex partition is not identifiable if and only if almost every set in the partition is a slab (translation-invariant) in some fixed direction. Second, Theorem 3.2 establishes the first polynomial-time algorithm for coarse Gaussian mean estimation, using m = Õ(dD²log(1/δ)/α⁴ + d·log(1/δ)/(α⁴ε²)) coarse samples and running in time polynomial in m and the bit complexity of the observations. In the standard regime where α = Ω(1) and D = O(1), this reduces to Õ(d/ε²) samples and Õ(d/ε²) time, matching the optimal sample complexity. As a concrete application, Theorem 3.3 yields an efficient algorithm for linear regression with market friction achieving ‖ŵ − w*‖²₂ ≤ Õ(d/(α⁴n)) in poly(n,d) time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with Fotakis et al. (2021), the key prior work on coarse Gaussian mean estimation. Fotakis et al. established that sample-efficient estimation (Õ(d/ε²) samples) is possible for identifiable convex partitions, but their algorithm relied on brute-force search over an exp(d/ε)-sized ε-net, making it computationally intractable in high dimensions. This paper matches their sample complexity exactly while providing the first polynomial-time algorithm, thereby closing the statistical-to-computational gap. Additionally, this paper resolves two open questions left by Fotakis et al.: (1) a complete geometric characterization of which convex partitions are identifiable (Theorem 3.1), and (2) the existence of a computationally efficient algorithm for all identifiable convex partitions (Theorem 3.2). The paper also extends beyond Fotakis et al. by handling the mixture-of-partitions model and applying the techniques to the regression-with-friction problem of Rosett (1959), for which no prior efficient algorithm existed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Fotakis et al. (2021), who established sample-efficient (but computationally intractable) Gaussian mean estimation from coarse samples under convex partitions and proved NP-hardness without convexity. The current work resolves the two open questions left by that paper: a geometric characterization of identifiability for convex partitions (via a novel combination of variance reduction inequalities and the Prékopa-Leindler inequality) and the first polynomial-time algorithm matching the optimal Õ(d/ε²) sample complexity. The algorithmic approach builds on the convexity of the coarse log-likelihood (itself due to Fotakis et al.) but introduces local strong convexity arguments and a reduction from general partitions to 'local' partitions to control gradient second moments. The paper also generalizes Sheppard's classical 1D corrections for binned data to high-dimensional non-uniform convex partitions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean geometric characterization (non-identifiability iff the partition is a.e. parallel slabs) provides a simple, checkable criterion that practitioners can use to determine whether their coarsening scheme preserves enough information for mean recovery. The application to linear regression with market friction (Rosett, 1959) demonstrates a concrete econometric use case where exact prices are unobserved and only ranges are available. Key open questions remain: extending to unknown covariance (where the log-likelihood becomes non-convex) and to distribution families beyond Gaussians. The authors note the variance-reduction + Prékopa-Leindler combination may have broader applications to other coarse estimation problems.
