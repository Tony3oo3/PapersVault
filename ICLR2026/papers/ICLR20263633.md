## Problem

Catastrophic forgetting in continual learning for diffusion models is typically addressed with replay (prone to distributional drift) or EWC with a diagonal Fisher approximation (which neglects cross-parameter correlations and fails to capture the dominant curvature direction). The paper identifies that the diagonal Fisher is particularly inadequate for diffusion models because most of the Fisher's curvature is concentrated in off-diagonal terms, yet no prior work has exploited the specific gradient geometry that diffusion models exhibit at low signal-to-noise ratios.

## Input / Output

The model is a label-conditioned UNet diffusion model that takes a class label as conditioning input and generates 32×32 RGB images (MNIST and FashionMNIST are padded from 28×28 grayscale to 32×32; ImageNet-1k is downsampled to 3×32×32). Evaluation is performed via Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) between generated samples and each task's held-out test set, reporting average FID across all tasks seen so far and a forgetting metric measuring FID degradation after subsequent task training.

## Method

The key theoretical insight is that in variance-preserving diffusion models at low SNR (later timesteps), the score function approximates a scaled identity map, causing per-sample gradients to become collinear with their population mean and the empirical Fisher to reduce to an effective rank-1 matrix aligned with the mean gradient direction. This yields a practical EWC penalty that uses only the mean gradient vector (computed via Monte-Carlo sampling over timesteps) as the dominant curvature direction, making it as computationally cheap as the diagonal approximation while capturing the off-diagonal structure the diagonal misses. The rank-1 EWC penalty is paired with a generative distillation term that matches the current model's noise predictions to a frozen teacher from the previous task on replayed samples, encouraging cross-task parameter sharing so that EWC's quadratic constraint operates within a shared optimum. The full training objective combines the current-task denoising loss, the rank-1 EWC penalty over all prior tasks, and the generative distillation loss, trained with a standard DDPM loss and DDIM sampling.

## Results

On class-incremental image generation, the proposed Rank-1 EWC with generative distillation achieves the lowest average FID and forgetting across all four datasets. On MNIST and FashionMNIST, forgetting is nearly eliminated (F = 0.6±0.1 and 0.9±0.3 respectively), and on the longest-horizon ImageNet-1k (20 tasks), forgetting is more than halved relative to generative distillation alone (15.2±4.8 vs. 46.2±12.9). Average FID at the final task improves from 69.0 (GD-only) to 48.5 on ImageNet-1k and from 61.2 to 50.5 on CIFAR-10, narrowing the gap to the non-continual upper bound (11.7 and 23.3 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: diagonal Fisher EWC (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) and generative distillation (Masip et al., 2025), both augmented with the same replay setup. Against diagonal Fisher EWC, the rank-1 variant wins decisively on every dataset: on ImageNet-1k it reduces average FID from 73.8 to 48.5 and forgetting from 25.8 to 15.2; on CIFAR-10 it cuts FID from 72.6 to 50.5 and forgetting from 17.9 to 7.4. Against generative distillation alone, rank-1 EWC provides complementary gains: on ImageNet-1k it more than halves forgetting (15.2 vs. 46.2) and lowers FID from 69.0 to 48.5, while on MNIST and FashionMNIST it reduces forgetting to near-zero (0.6 and 0.9 vs. 2.3 and 3.9). Notably, diagonal EWC combined with distillation often matches or even degrades relative to distillation alone (e.g., CIFAR-10 FID 72.6 vs. 61.2), confirming that the diagonal approximation captures almost no useful curvature in diffusion models, whereas the rank-1 structure does.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of elastic weight consolidation (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017), diffusion model training (Ho et al., 2020), and generative distillation for continual learning (Masip et al., 2025). It departs from the standard EWC formulation by showing that the diagonal Fisher approximation—used universally in practice—is fundamentally inadequate for diffusion models, whose low-SNR gradients are nearly collinear and thus induce an effective rank-1 Fisher. Rather than replacing replay or distillation, the paper positions its rank-1 EWC as a complementary constraint that operates within the shared parameter region that generative distillation establishes, addressing the known failure mode of EWC in overparameterized models with disjoint task optima.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The rank-1 Fisher insight provides a principled, low-cost correction to a widely-used approximation that the paper shows is essentially useless (Frobenius error ≈ 1.0) for diffusion models, and the resulting method nearly eliminates forgetting on MNIST/FMNIST and halves it on ImageNet-1k. However, the key Assumption 1 (score function approximates a linear operator) is motivated by autoencoder theory but not rigorously validated for large nonlinear architectures, and all experiments use 32×32 images with a single UNet family. Open questions include whether the rank-1 structure persists for non-MSE objectives (the VAE ablation suggests it weakens), for attention-based architectures, and at full ImageNet resolution, which would determine whether this is a broadly applicable property of diffusion training or an artifact of the specific DDPM parameterization studied here.
