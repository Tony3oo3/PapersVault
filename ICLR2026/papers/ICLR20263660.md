## Problem

Amortized diffusion-based simulation-based inference models are locked to the prior distribution used during training, so incorporating updated domain knowledge or conducting prior sensitivity analysis requires costly retraining on new simulated parameter-data pairs. Existing amortized prior-adaptation methods (e.g., ACE, Distribution Transformer) circumvent this by pre-training over a meta-prior, but this restricts the set of usable priors to those seen during training and cannot handle arbitrary, correlated, or non-factorized priors specified post-hoc.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen, pre-trained diffusion-based SBI model (e.g., Simformer), the observed data x, the original training prior p_train(θ), and a user-specified target prior q(θ) provided as an analytical density at test time. The prior ratio r(θ) = q(θ)/p_train(θ) is fit as a generalized Gaussian mixture (weights need not be positive or sum to one) via gradient-based optimization. The output is a set of posterior samples θ or posterior-predictive trajectory samples x*, with the number of function evaluations (N × (N_L + 1)) serving as a tunable accuracy-cost knob.

## Method

PriorGuide exploits the identity q(θ|x) ∝ r(θ)·p(θ|x) (Proposition 1) to express the target posterior score as the trained score plus a guidance term ∇_{θ_t} log E_{p(θ_0|θ_t,x)}[r(θ_0)]. To make this tractable, the reverse transition kernel p(θ_0|θ_t,x) is approximated as a Gaussian whose mean comes from Tweedie's formula and whose covariance is σ(t)²/(1+σ(t)²)·I, and the prior ratio r(θ) is approximated as a K-component generalized GMM. The resulting integral has a closed-form solution, yielding a weighted sum of correction vectors (μ_i − μ_{0|t})ᵀ Σ̃_i⁻¹ ∇_{θ_t} μ_{0|t} that is added to the original score at every diffusion step. To recover asymptotic correctness despite the Gaussian kernel approximation, interleaved Langevin dynamics steps (N_L per diffusion step) refine the samples at low noise levels where the approximation is provably exact (Proposition 2). The same guidance mechanism extends directly to posterior-predictive sampling by applying the prior-ratio correction to the joint (x*, θ) score.

## Results

On posterior inference across six SBI problems, PriorGuide consistently outperforms the unadapted Simformer baseline, with the largest gains under strong and mixture priors (e.g., on Turin with strong priors, MMTV drops from 0.56 to 0.08). Against ACE, PriorGuide achieves lower MMTV in most settings (e.g., 0.06 vs 0.12 on OUP strong priors; 0.08 vs 0.47 on Turin strong priors), though ACE occasionally matches or beats PriorGuide on RMSE for high-dimensional Gaussian Linear models (0.10 vs 0.23 on 10D strong priors). On posterior predictive inference (OUP and Turin time-series forecasting), PriorGuide achieves RMSE of 0.21 and 0.13 respectively under strong priors, matching or exceeding both baselines on MMD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PriorGuide is compared against two baselines: Simformer (Gloeckler et al., 2024), the unadapted diffusion-based SBI model with no prior modification, and ACE (Chang et al., 2025), which amortizes prior adaptation by pre-training over a meta-prior of factorized histogram priors. Against Simformer, PriorGuide wins decisively on all six models and all three prior families, with MMTV improvements of 3–10× under strong priors (e.g., Turin: 0.56→0.08; OUP: 0.37→0.06). Against ACE, PriorGuide achieves lower MMTV in 5 of 6 models under strong priors and in all 6 under mixture priors, indicating better distributional fidelity; however, ACE achieves lower RMSE on the two high-dimensional Gaussian Linear models (0.10 vs 0.23–0.28), suggesting ACE's pre-trained factorized prior encoding is more effective for point-estimate accuracy in those specific settings. On C2ST (where 0.5 is ideal), PriorGuide is consistently closer to 0.5 than both baselines, confirming better two-sample discrimination. The key practical advantage is that PriorGuide handles arbitrary (including correlated, non-factorized) target priors at test time without any retraining, whereas ACE is restricted to factorized priors from its pre-defined meta-prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

PriorGuide builds directly on Simformer (Gloeckler et al., 2024), a transformer-based diffusion model for amortized SBI, and repurposes the score-guidance mechanism from diffusion posterior sampling (Chung et al., 2023; Song et al., 2023a) to steer the reverse diffusion process toward a new prior. The key departure from prior amortized prior-adaptation methods (ACE, Distribution Transformer, Sensitivity-aware SBI) is that those approaches pre-train over a meta-prior or discrete set of prior specifications, whereas PriorGuide performs adaptation purely at test time by fitting a GMM to the prior ratio q(θ)/p_train(θ) and injecting it as an analytical guidance term, requiring no retraining or meta-prior coverage. The theoretical foundation draws on Tweedie's formula for the reverse transition kernel and Langevin dynamics for corrective MCMC, positioning the method within the broader test-time compute paradigm (Snell et al., 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PriorGuide decouples expensive simulator-based training from the specification of prior beliefs, enabling post-hoc prior sensitivity analysis and rapid incorporation of domain expert knowledge without retraining—practically important for expensive simulators in engineering, neuroscience, and climate science. Key limitations include the requirement that the target prior overlap substantially with the training prior (OOD priors destabilize guidance), the GMM approximation of the prior ratio which may struggle with complex multimodal ratios, and scalability challenges for dim(θ) ≫ 20 due to per-component matrix operations. Open questions include whether advanced covariance estimation (e.g., Rissanen et al., 2025) can reduce the NFE budget, whether the method extends to flow-matching or consistency-model-based SBI, and how to handle priors with genuinely disjoint support from the training distribution.
