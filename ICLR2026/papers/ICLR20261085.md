## Problem

Existing character image animation methods are largely confined to single-human settings and rely on 2D control signals (skeleton maps, SMPL renderings) that entangle motion with body morphology, making them unable to generalize to multi-humanoid scenarios involving diverse character forms, dense interactions, and frequent occlusions. The absence of explicit 4D spatiotemporal modeling—particularly depth—prevents these methods from resolving occlusion relationships or distinguishing genuine size differences from camera distance, while 2D pixel-wise training losses inadvertently couple motion with human-specific appearance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image containing multiple humanoid characters and a driving video (49 consecutive frames at 480P) as input, producing an animated video of the reference characters performing the poses from the driving video. SMPL parameters are extracted per-character using CoMotion, and the reference image is segmented into per-character masks to isolate identity features. Notably, the model is trained exclusively on human video data yet generalizes at inference to diverse humanoid forms (robots, anthropomorphic animals, anime characters, pixelated stick figures) without retraining.

## Method

MotionWeaver builds on Wan2.1-I2V-14B-480P and introduces three components. The Unified-Choreography Core (UCC) extracts identity-agnostic motion tokens by mapping SMPL joint coordinates onto a standardized skeleton with fixed inter-joint distances, then binds each character's motion tokens to its appearance tokens (extracted via VAE + patchifier) through a group attention module where motion serves as query and identity as key/value. The Hyper-Scene Integrator (HSI) fuses these unified motion representations with video latents in a shared 4D space using two complementary mechanisms: a depth-aware attention that encodes the z-axis via sinusoidal depth tokens supervised by an occlusion loss, and a Dynamic C-RoPE that applies position-dependent rotary matrices to model the (t, x, y) axes, with camera-space key positions projected onto the image plane for alignment. The Hierarchical-4D Supervision (H4S) switches the training objective based on noise level: at high-noise timesteps it applies MSE plus occlusion loss to establish correct depth ordering early, while at low-noise timesteps it replaces the occlusion loss with a motion-level loss that decodes predicted latents through a 4D pose detector stem, injecting strong 4D motion priors and reducing overfitting to human appearance.

## Results

On the DualDynamics benchmark (300 two-character interaction videos), MotionWeaver achieves FVD 145.7, FID 19.41, LPIPS 0.3213, and CLIP 0.9041, outperforming all seven baselines across every metric. The closest competitor, RealisDance-DiT, records FVD 164.6 and FID 23.26, yielding roughly 11.5% and 15% relative improvements respectively. MotionWeaver also leads on perceptual and structural metrics (LPIPS 0.3213 vs. 0.3271; SSIM 0.5428 vs. 0.5399) and on identity preservation (CLIP 0.9041 vs. 0.8813), confirming its advantage in multi-humanoid settings with dense interactions and frequent occlusions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionWeaver wins on all eight metrics against every baseline, with the largest margins over MimicMotion (FVD 312.4 vs. 145.7, a 53% reduction) and MusePose (FVD 298.3 vs. 145.7, a 51% reduction). Against the two strongest single-character methods adapted to multi-human settings—RealisDance-DiT and UniAnimate-DiT—MotionWeaver still leads by 11.5% on FVD and 15% on FID, while also improving CLIP score by 2.3 and 4.4 points respectively, indicating better identity preservation. The gap widens further on LPIPS (0.3213 vs. 0.3271 for RealisDance-DiT and 0.3482 for UniAnimate-DiT), reflecting superior perceptual quality under occlusion. Qualitatively, the paper highlights that Animate-X is fundamentally limited to single-character scenarios, while MimicMotion, MusePose, StableAnimator, and HumanVid fail to generalize across diverse humanoid forms (robots, anthropomorphic animals, game avatars). RealisDance-DiT and UniAnimate-DiT, though the closest competitors, still exhibit identity confusion when characters swap positions and struggle with dense inter-character occlusions—failures that MotionWeaver's depth-aware attention and dynamic C-RoPE explicitly address.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the character image animation line from GAN-based to diffusion-transformer methods (Animate-X, HumanVid, StableAnimator, UniAnimate-DiT, RealisDance-DiT), all of which operate in single-human settings with 2D control signals. It builds most directly on MTVCrafter's 3D motion tokenization and on DanceTogether/Structural Video Diffusion's multi-person attempts, but departs from both by replacing mask-based identity binding with identity-agnostic motion tokens explicitly bound via group attention, and by constructing a shared 4D space (depth-aware attention + Dynamic C-RoPE) rather than relying on naive cross-attention. The base model is Wan2.1-I2V-14B-480P, and the hierarchical supervision design is motivated by the known coarse-to-fine denoising behavior of rectified-flow diffusion transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a genuine gap: no prior benchmark or method was designed for multi-humanoid animation with diverse character forms (robots, anthropomorphic animals, game avatars) and dense occlusions. The DualDynamics benchmark and MultiHuman46 dataset provide the field with the first dedicated evaluation and training resources for this setting. Key limitations include blurry hands in real-world human scenes (a deliberate trade-off to avoid human-centric bias) and high inference cost that precludes real-time use. Open questions include whether the 4D-anchored paradigm generalizes to more than two characters at training time (currently only at inference), whether hand-level control can be reintroduced without sacrificing humanoid-form generalization, and whether the approach extends to longer sequences beyond 49 frames.
