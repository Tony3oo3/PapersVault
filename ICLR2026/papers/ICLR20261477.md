## Problem

Proteome-scale PPI screening requires scoring ~200M protein pairs, but accurate residue-level models (e.g., PLM-Interact) are non-factorizable and need explicit per-pair inference, taking ~4.9 GPU-months for the human proteome. The gap is a method that retains residue-level interaction fidelity while producing per-protein embeddings amenable to approximate nearest-neighbor search, reducing screening from months to minutes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261477/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each protein sequence is encoded once by a frozen ESM2-8M backbone into per-residue d-dimensional embeddings, which are then mapped through a fixed SORF transform (2048 frequencies) and softmax-weighted attention pooling to yield a single 4096-dimensional vector per protein. The PPI score for any pair is simply the dot product of their two vectors, so the output is both a scalar interaction logit and an indexable embedding for HNSW-based retrieval of top-K candidate interactors across the entire proteome.

## Method

RaftPPI factorizes the standard Pred&Agg pipeline (residue-residue contact scoring + attention aggregation) into a dot product between per-protein embeddings. Residue-residue interactions are modeled with a Gaussian kernel approximated via Structured Orthogonal Random Features (SORF), giving O(L log d + Ld') per-protein cost instead of O(Ldd') for dense RFF. Aggregation uses rank-1 separable attention: a lightweight per-residue scorer produces softmax weights, and the protein embedding is the weighted sum of RFF-mapped residue vectors, so ℓ(A,B) = ⟨h_A, h_B⟩. Training uses an adaptive negative weighting loss (self-adversarial, temperature τ=4) that up-weights harder constructed negatives based on model confidence, mitigating biases from easy negative sampling. At inference, all N protein embeddings are cached and indexed with HNSW, reducing proteome-wide top-20% retrieval to ~6 minutes on a single A100 GPU or ~3.3 minutes on a single Intel Xeon 6 CPU.

## Results

RaftPPI achieves the best average test AUROC of 75.29% across seven sequence- and degree-controlled PPI benchmarks, edging out PLM-Interact (75.14%) and ESM2-MLP (74.25%). On the human proteome retrieval task (~200M candidate pairs), RaftPPI retrieves the top-20% pairs in 5.7 minutes on a single A100 GPU (or 3.3 minutes on an Intel Xeon 6 CPU), covering 75.1% of true interacting pairs, compared to 148.47 A100 GPU-days (~4.9 months) for PLM-Interact which covers only 61.2%. In retrieval Recall@20%, RaftPPI reaches 48.33% (estimated) / 47.91% (full proteome), substantially outperforming PLM-Interact (30.81%), TUnA (30.44%), and ESM2-MLP (27.77%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RaftPPI achieves the best average AUROC (75.29%) among all 11 methods, narrowly surpassing PLM-Interact (75.14%) and ESM2-MLP (74.25%). RaftPPI wins on 4 of 7 datasets (Guo +5.33, Gold +3.69, Pan +1.18, Huang +2.51 over PLM-Interact) but loses on Richoux (69.88 vs 78.66) and D-SCRIPT (82.06 vs 84.77), with Du essentially tied (75.06 vs 75.20). All non-PLM baselines (D-SCRIPT, DeepFE, Richoux-FC/LSTM, SPRINT, Topsy-Turvy) fall well below the unsupervised ESM2-NoFT baseline (62.07%), confirming that pretrained PLM embeddings are essential under controlled splits. In proteome-scale retrieval, RaftPPI's factorized design yields a 10^4-fold speedup: 5.7 min vs 148.47 GPU-days for PLM-Interact, while also achieving higher Recall@20% (48.33% vs 30.81%). The protein-level variant RaftPPI-P (dot-product of [CLS] embeddings) scores 71.90% AUROC and 45.73% Recall@20%, confirming that the residue-level Gaussian-kernel design contributes ~3.4 AUROC points and ~2.6 Recall points over a simple embedding dot product.

## Relation to Prior Work

RaftPPI factorizes the standard Pred&Agg pipeline (residue-level contact scoring followed by attention pooling) established by D-SCRIPT and extended by Topsy-Turvy and TT3D into a form where each protein is encoded once into a fixed-length embedding. The key technical move is approximating the Gaussian residue-residue kernel via Structured Orthogonal Random Features and replacing dense pairwise attention with rank-1 separable attention, so that the full interaction score reduces to a dot product between per-protein embeddings. For the training objective, the paper adapts the self-adversarial negative weighting from RotatE (Sun et al., 2019) to PPI, where constructed negatives vary widely in informativeness. This positions RaftPPI between the accurate-but-slow per-pair PLM classifiers (PLM-Interact, TUnA, ESM2-MLP) and the fast-but-coarse sequence-similarity methods (SPRINT), offering residue-level fidelity at retrieval speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10^4-fold speedup (GPU months to minutes on a single A100 or Xeon CPU) makes proteome-wide PPI screening practically accessible for the first time with residue-aware models, enabling iterative hypothesis generation and integration into multi-stage pipelines (e.g., pre-screening before AlphaFold-Multimer rescoring). A notable finding is that non-factorizable PLM classifiers, despite superior binary classification AUROC, underperform the simple ESM2-NoFT baseline in retrieval under sequence-controlled splits, suggesting that joint pair encoding does not transfer to the sparse, imbalanced retrieval setting. Open questions include whether the factorized kernel-attention formulation can incorporate structural or evolutionary information beyond ESM2 sequence embeddings, whether it generalizes to other biomolecular interaction types (protein-ligand, protein-DNA), and whether the approximation error from rank-1 attention and finite RFF dimension becomes limiting at larger proteome scales or for weaker interactions.
