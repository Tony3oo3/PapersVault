## Problem

Current LLM safety mechanisms rely on reactive pattern-matching to block known harmful content, creating an inherent asymmetry where novel adversarial prompts (jailbreaks, encoding obfuscation, multi-turn exploits) bypass defenses faster than they can be catalogued. This paper reframes LLM safety as an out-of-distribution detection problem, arguing that robust protection comes from characterizing the distribution of safe usage rather than enumerating all possible harms, thereby providing a principled defense against unknown-unknown attacks.

## Input / Output

The method takes raw text prompts or LLM-generated tokens as input, embedding them via three sentence transformers (Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B, BGE-M3, E5-Large-v2) into normalized vectors on unit hyperspheres of varying dimensions. It produces a single anomaly score in [0,1] (sigmoid-normalized negative log-likelihood) that can be thresholded for safe/harmful classification. In streaming mode integrated with vLLM, the system evaluates every 20 tokens during generation, enabling immediate termination of harmful outputs with sub-6% latency overhead on 5,000-prompt workloads.

## Method

T3 extends the Forte framework from vision to text by computing four per-point PRDC metrics (Precision, Recall, Density, Coverage) that quantify how each test embedding relates to k-nearest-neighbor balls in the reference safe set, across three independent encoders. These 4K-dimensional PRDC feature vectors are then modeled using either a GMM (components selected via BIC) or One-Class SVM (RBF kernel) trained exclusively on safe data, with the anomaly score derived from negative log-likelihood. The key theoretical contribution proves that under the null hypothesis (safe = safe), the metrics have closed-form expectations (e.g., E[Recall] = k/n, E[Density] = 1/m), and establishes consistency—showing the metrics provably separate distributions under partial support mismatch, density shift, and local perturbation regimes. This multi-view, nearest-neighbor-based approach exploits the isotropic geometry of LLM embeddings (concentration of measure) where safe text forms a tight annulus around the mean, making geometric deviations a reliable signal of adversarial content without any training on harmful examples.

## Results

On six toxicity and hate speech benchmarks, T3-OCSVM achieves AUROC ≥ 0.96 on 5 of 6 datasets and dramatically reduces false positive rates: FPR@95 of 2.0% on OffensEval (vs. 75.2% for the best baseline DuoGuard) and 3.5% on Davidson (vs. 61.7%), representing a 10–40× reduction. On domain-specific PolyGuard benchmarks, T3 achieves near-perfect AUROC (99.6% on Code, 99.8% on HR) with FPR@95 below 1%, while all baselines exceed 65% FPR@95. T3 also maintains <0.6% AUROC variance across 14+ languages and achieves sub-6% latency overhead when integrated into vLLM for continuous token-level safety monitoring.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T3 decisively outperforms all 14 baselines on every toxicity and hate speech benchmark. Traditional OOD methods (CIDER, GMM, RMD, VIM, FDBD, NNGuide, OpenMax, ReAct, AdaScale) fail catastrophically, with FPR@95 typically exceeding 90%, rendering them unusable for safety applications. Among specialized safety models, LlamaGuard (3-1B and 4-12B) and WildGuard achieve FPR@95 = 1.0 (flagging all safe prompts) on most toxicity benchmarks; MD-Judge improves to ~0.82–0.92 FPR@95; DuoGuard reaches 0.62–0.91; and PolyGuard is the strongest baseline at 0.22–0.54 FPR@95. T3-OCSVM reduces FPR@95 by 10–40× over the best specialized model (e.g., 2.0% vs. 75.2% on OffensEval, 3.5% vs. 61.7% on Davidson) while simultaneously achieving higher AUROC. On adversarial/jailbreak benchmarks (AdvBench, HarmBench, JailbreakBench, MaliciousInstruct), T3 again dominates: FPR@95 of 15.8% on AdvBench vs. 66.5% for PolyGuard, and 45.4% on JailbreakBench vs. 72.0% for PolyGuard. The only notable exception is OR-Bench (over-refusal), where LlamaGuard 3-1B achieves a lower FPR@95 (14.8%) than T3-GMM (22.2%), though T3-OCSVM still leads in AUROC (0.934 vs. 0.891).

## Relation to Prior Work

T3 extends the Forte framework (Ganguly et al., 2025c) from vision to text, providing the first rigorous theoretical analysis of per-point PRDC metrics in the textual domain and establishing expected values under both null and alternative hypotheses. It departs from the dominant reactive safety paradigm (LlamaGuard, RLHF, Constitutional AI) by reframing LLM guardrailing as an out-of-distribution detection problem grounded in the concentration-of-measure geometry of LLM embeddings (Liu et al., 2023a), rather than enumerating known harmful patterns. The multi-encoder design (Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B, BGE-M3, E5-Large-v2) with GMM/OCSVM density estimation on aggregated PRDC features is a novel architectural choice that avoids the fine-tuning paradox identified by Uppaal et al. (2023), where task-specific fine-tuning degrades the geometric structure needed for OOD detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10–40× reduction in false positive rates (e.g., 2.0% vs. 75.2% FPR@95 on OffensEval) directly addresses the over-refusal problem that plagues production LLM deployments, and the zero-shot transfer across 14+ languages and specialized domains (code, HR, cybersecurity) eliminates the need for per-domain or per-language retraining. The vLLM integration with sub-6% overhead for continuous 20-token-interval monitoring makes real-time guardrailing practical for the first time. A key limitation is that T3 fails when the in-distribution training set itself contains harmful patterns (demonstrated on Anthropic hh-rlhf), revealing that curation quality is a shared vulnerability across all safety methods; the authors suggest hybrid architectures combining T3's efficient typicality screening with reasoning-based methods for near-boundary cases as a path forward.
