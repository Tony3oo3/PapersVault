## Problem

Existing protein structure autoencoders face multiple limitations: they operate on the complex SE(3) manifold requiring equivariance constraints, discretize continuous coordinates into tokens (losing reconstruction fidelity), combine multiple auxiliary training objectives with hand-tuned weights, and are restricted to fixed sequence lengths. The paper asks whether a simpler, more accurate protein autoencoder can be built in a continuous, compact latent space that avoids all these complications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262375/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PROTEINAE takes protein backbone atom coordinates (Cα, N, C, O) as a tensor x ∈ R^{N×4×3} (N = 32–256 residues in training) and produces a continuous latent z of shape (N, 8) after a dimension bottleneck (no length downsampling by default). The flow decoder then reconstructs full backbone coordinates from z plus Gaussian noise via ODE integration. For generation, PLDM samples z from a learned latent distribution using an SDE schedule with a tunable temperature γ, which is decoded into novel backbone structures.

## Method

PROTEINAE uses a non-equivariant Diffusion Transformer (DiT) with pair-biased attention and RoPE positional encodings, operating directly on E(3) coordinates without SE(3) equivariance. The encoder processes atom-level features through an All-Atom Attention module (sequence-local atom interactions), a 5-layer DiT stack, and a two-stage bottleneck (1D convolution for length compression + linear projection to 8 dimensions), followed by LayerNorm in place of KL regularization. The decoder mirrors the encoder with a DiT stack and All-Atom Attention Decoder that predicts the velocity field v_t^θ for flow-matching-based reconstruction. Training uses a single flow-matching loss with no auxiliary terms, and the downstream PLDM is a 200M-parameter DiT trained with flow matching on the latent space, sampled via an SDE schedule that trades designability for diversity through a noise scale parameter γ.

## Results

PROTEINAE achieves state-of-the-art Cα RMSD of 0.23±0.15 on CASP14 Targets and 0.28±0.20 on CASP15 TS-domains, outperforming the next-best baselines (ProToken at 0.99±0.69 and ESM3 VQ-VAE at 1.02±1.82) by roughly 4×. For unconditional backbone generation, PROTEINAE-PLDM (γ=0.35) reaches 93% designability and 204 diversity, rivaling RFdiffusion (96%, 247) while being ~10× faster in sampling time (~1.6 s vs ~15 s). On physicochemical prediction (ATLAS), PROTEINAE attains 45.36% Spearman's ρ on FlexRMSF (Fold split) and 30.87% on FlexBFactor (Fold split), surpassing ESM3 by over 10% on FlexRMSF SupFam (44.71 vs 39.68).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PROTEINAE decisively outperforms all reconstruction baselines: it reduces Cα RMSD by ~4× over ProToken (0.23 vs 0.99 on CASP14 T) and ESM3 VQ-VAE (0.23 vs 1.02), and by ~7× over DPLM-2 (0.23 vs 1.99), with the gap widening dramatically on oligomeric targets (0.31 vs 3.08 for ESM3 VQ-VAE). CHEAP, which relies on ESM2 features and ESMFold prediction, is the weakest baseline (11.15 RMSD), confirming its fundamental limitation. For generation, PROTEINAE-PLDM (93% Des, 204 Div) substantially outperforms all latent-based methods—LatentDiff (17% Des, 34 Div), LSD (69%, 203), and MLLMs ESM3/DPLM-2 (61–63%, 127–130)—and is competitive with top SDMs like RFdiffusion (96%, 247) and FrameFlow PDB (91%, 278), while being ~10× faster. On physicochemical prediction, PROTEINAE beats ESM3 by 5.03 points on FlexRMSF SupFam (44.71 vs 39.68) and 7.27 points on FlexBFactor Fold (30.87 vs 23.60), and surpasses AminoAseed and Vanilla VQ across all splits. The only metric where PROTEINAE-PLDM trails a baseline is diversity (204 vs 247 for RFdiffusion, 278 for FrameFlow PDB), reflecting a slight trade-off between designability and structural variety.

## Relation to Prior Work

PROTEINAE adapts the diffusion autoencoder paradigm from DiTo (Chen et al., 2025) — originally developed for image tokenization — to protein structure encoding, replacing the VQ-VAE + discrete tokenization pipeline used by ESM3, DPLM-2, and ProToken with a continuous latent space trained via a single flow-matching loss. Architecturally, it draws on the non-equivariant DiT stacks of AlphaFold3 and Proteina, operating directly on E(3) coordinates rather than the SE(3) manifold, and adopts Proteina's SDE-based sampling strategy for the downstream latent diffusion model. The key conceptual departure is eliminating equivariance constraints, multiple auxiliary losses (FAPE, distance, violation, KL), and discrete codebooks in favor of a streamlined non-equivariant architecture with LayerNorm-based latent normalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that high-fidelity protein structure encoding does not require SE(3) equivariance or discrete tokenization, challenging a prevailing assumption in the field and offering a substantially simpler training recipe (single flow-matching loss, no weight tuning). The 3–10× reconstruction improvement over the next-best autoencoder and the 10×/16× speed/memory gains for latent-space generation make this a practically useful foundation for scalable protein design pipelines. However, PROTEINAE is limited to monomers (no ligands, DNA, RNA), the PLDM generation quality remains slightly below top structure-based models (93% vs 96% designability), and sequence-length extrapolation beyond the training range causes structural collapse — all open problems the authors flag for future work.
