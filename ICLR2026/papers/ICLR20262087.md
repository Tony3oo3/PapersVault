## Problem

Monolithic autoregressive MLLMs that unify understanding and generation suffer from a fundamental semantic-structural conflict: the MSE regression loss needed for visual token generation pulls shared representations toward low-level reconstructability, causing catastrophic forgetting of the high-level semantic understanding learned via cross-entropy. Prior monolithic models (Emu, Chameleon, Show-o2) have not resolved this tension, yielding generation quality far below diffusion-based systems while simultaneously degrading comprehension.

## Input / Output

The model consumes arbitrarily interleaved sequences of text tokens and continuous visual tokens (81 tokens per image, encoded by a ViT), with understanding inputs supporting native multi-resolution images from 56 to 1024 pixels on the long side. For generation, the autoregressive backbone predicts 81 visual token embeddings at 252×252 resolution, which are then decoded into pixel-level RGB images by a separate pre-trained diffusion decoder (from Nexus-Gen). The same architecture also accepts a source image plus an editing instruction to produce a locally or globally edited image.

## Method

ORION resolves the semantic-structural conflict through two mechanisms: (1) a single-hidden-layer MLP vision head (8× expansion, GELU) that acts as a non-linear key-value memory, decoupling the MSE gradient pressure from the shared LLM hidden states so the backbone need only emit semantically sufficient information rather than linearly decodable visual coordinates; and (2) a Representation Consistency Loss that, at every visual-token position, minimizes KL divergence between the student's text-classification logits and those of a frozen teacher (the original Qwen2.5-VL), anchoring semantics during structural learning. Training follows a three-stage progressive recipe—vision-head warm-up (MSE only, 20M images), full-parameter pretraining (understanding-heavy mixture), and multi-task SFT (understanding + generation + editing)—on a 7B Qwen2.5-VL backbone with the Nexus-Gen diffusion decoder, using continuous (not VQ-VAE discrete) visual tokens to preserve semantic richness.

## Results

On the GenEval text-to-image benchmark, ORION achieves an overall score of 0.82, matching the generation-only FLUX.1-dev (0.82) and surpassing all prior monolithic unified models (Janus-Pro 0.80, Show-o2 0.76, Emu3 0.66, Chameleon 0.39). On multimodal understanding, ORION scores 83.7 on MMBench, 64.2 on MMStar, 64.5 on MMVet, 78.1 on SEED-Bench, and 67.4 on RealWorldQA, largely preserving (and in some cases exceeding) the Qwen2.5-VL base model's understanding while adding generative capability. The model also demonstrates zero-shot emergent capabilities including interleaved generation-understanding dialogue and cross-lingual generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GenEval, ORION (0.82) ties the generation-only FLUX.1-dev and decisively outperforms all prior monolithic models: Janus-Pro (0.80, +0.02), Show-o2 (0.76, +0.06), Emu3 (0.66, +0.16), Chameleon (0.39, +0.43), and Orthus (0.58, +0.24). However, ORION trails the strongest parallel-architecture models Mogao (0.89) and BAGEL (0.88) by 0.07 and 0.06 respectively, as well as cascaded models OmniGen2 (0.86), BLIP3-o (0.84), and UniWorld-V1 (0.84). On understanding benchmarks, ORION improves over its Qwen2.5-VL base on MMBench (83.7 vs. 79.1) and MMStar (64.2 vs. 63.9), while slightly regressing on MMVet (64.5 vs. 67.1), SEED-Bench (78.1 vs. 79.5), and RealWorldQA (67.4 vs. 68.5). Among unified models, ORION's MMBench score of 83.7 surpasses all cascaded baselines (79.1) and monolithic competitors (Janus-Pro 79.2, Show-o2 79.3, Emu3 58.5, Chameleon 35.7), but falls short of BAGEL's 85.0. The key finding is that ORION achieves the best monolithic-architecture results on both generation and understanding simultaneously, validating the decoupling-and-alignment framework as a path to competitive unified performance without task-specific parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operates within the monolithic autoregressive paradigm for unified multimodal models, building directly on the Qwen2.5-VL backbone and Nexus-Gen's diffusion decoder. It departs from prior monolithic works (Emu, Chameleon, Janus-Pro, Show-o2) by formally identifying the 'semantic-structural representation conflict' and addressing it through two targeted interventions: a non-linear MLP vision head (replacing the linear heads in Emu/Nexus-Gen) and a KL-divergence-based representation consistency loss that distills from a frozen teacher. The progressive three-stage training recipe and curated data pipeline are positioned as the missing engineering layer that prior monolithic attempts lacked, rather than as a fundamentally new architectural idea.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the strongest evidence to date that a purely monolithic autoregressive model—without separate parameters or task-specific modules—can match more complex cascaded and parallel unified architectures on both understanding and generation benchmarks, validating architectural simplicity as a viable path. The zero-shot emergent capabilities (interleaved generation-then-understanding, cross-lingual generation) suggest the unified representation space has compositional properties not yet fully exploited. Key open questions remain: the deterministic nature of regression-based token prediction limits generative diversity, image editing fidelity is constrained by the semantic encoder's lack of low-level detail, and the model has not been trained on explicit interleaved dialogue data, leaving its conversational generation potential largely untested.
