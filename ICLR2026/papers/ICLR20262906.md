## Problem

State-of-the-art PU learning methods substantially underperform their supervised counterparts on complex datasets (e.g., a 14.26% gap on CIFAR-100), primarily because unreliable pseudo-labels introduce noisy pairs that dominate the representation learning optimization, preventing the acquisition of discriminative intra-class representations. Existing approaches either require auxiliary negative samples or pre-estimated class priors to mitigate this issue, limiting their practical applicability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262906/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 96×96 for STL-10, 64×64 for xBD) or multi-channel remote sensing imagery (6×128×128 for ABCD, combining pre- and post-disaster views) as input, with only a small set of positive samples labeled and a large pool of unlabeled data containing an unknown mixture of positives and negatives. It outputs a binary classification (positive vs. negative) via a ResNet-18 backbone with a two-class softmax head. Notably, NcPU requires no auxiliary negative samples and no pre-estimated class prior π_p, making it directly applicable in settings where only positive annotations are available.

## Method

NcPU combines two mutually reinforcing modules under an EM-inspired framework. The NoiSNCL module replaces the standard BYOL non-contrastive loss 2(1−⟨q̃,k̃⟩) with a square-root variant 2√(1−⟨q̃,k̃⟩), which inverts the gradient magnitude ordering so that clean same-class pairs exert stronger pull than noisy pairs, ensuring representation alignment is driven by reliable supervision. The PLD module maintains class-conditional prototypes via momentum-updated normalized embeddings and employs a PhantomGate mechanism with self-adaptive thresholding (SAT) to assign conservative negative pseudo-targets while allowing regret-based correction of previously mislabeled samples. The two modules iterate: PLD refines pseudo-labels (E-step) based on the current discriminative representations, and NoiSNCL tightens intra-class clusters (M-step) under the refined supervision, jointly converging toward a local optimum without any auxiliary information.

## Results

NcPU achieves the best OA across all five datasets without requiring auxiliary negatives or pre-estimated parameters: 97.36% on CIFAR-10, 88.28% on CIFAR-100, 91.40% on STL-10, 91.10% on ABCD, and 87.60% on xBD. On CIFAR-10, NcPU (97.36% OA) surpasses the supervised baseline (96.96% OA) and the strongest PU method LaGAM (95.78% OA, which requires auxiliary negatives). On CIFAR-100, NcPU achieves 88.28% OA, closing the gap to supervised (89.65%) to within 1.37% while outperforming the next-best no-auxiliary method WSC (75.39%) by 12.89%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NcPU outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset. Against the strongest no-auxiliary baselines, NcPU gains 6.81% OA over WSC on CIFAR-10 (97.36 vs 90.55), 12.89% over WSC on CIFAR-100 (88.28 vs 75.39), and 5.78% over DistPU on STL-10 (91.40 vs 85.62). On the remote sensing datasets, NcPU leads ABCD by 2.20% over TEDn (91.10 vs 88.90) and xBD by 0.78% over uPU (87.60 vs 86.82). NcPU also surpasses LaGAM (95.78% on CIFAR-10, 84.82% on CIFAR-100), which is the only baseline requiring auxiliary negative samples, while NcPU needs none. Remarkably, NcPU matches or exceeds the fully supervised baseline on CIFAR-10 (97.36 vs 96.96 OA) and ABCD (91.10 vs 92.00 OA, within 0.9%), demonstrating that noisy-pair robust representation alignment can close the PU-to-supervised gap without any extra supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the BYOL non-contrastive representation learning framework (Grill et al., 2020) and the self-adaptive thresholding mechanism from FreeMatch (Wang et al., 2023b), repurposing both for the positive-unlabeled learning setting. It departs from recent contrastive PU methods (LaGAM, WSC, PiCO) that incorporate supervised contrastive losses with pseudo labels, arguing instead that the non-contrastive formulation—pulling only same-class representations—naturally mitigates the harmful effect of noisy pairs. The theoretical grounding via the EM framework (with NoiSNCL as the M-step and PLD as the E-step) distinguishes it from prior PU analyses that relied on contrastive learning objectives with uniformity terms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying representation quality (rather than supervision quality alone) as the primary bottleneck in PU learning, the paper reframes how the community should approach the gap between PU and supervised performance. The NoiSNCL loss—a simple square-root modification—provides a broadly applicable tool for noisy-pair robust representation alignment that could transfer to other weakly supervised settings beyond PU learning. Open questions include whether the framework extends to multi-class or partial-label scenarios, whether incorporating masked image modeling backbones further closes the remaining gap to supervised performance, and whether the EM convergence guarantees hold under more realistic distributional assumptions.
