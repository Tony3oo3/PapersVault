## Problem

Existing sports benchmarks for MLLMs either cover a single sport, rely on multiple-choice formats that cannot train generative reasoning, or lack the fine-grained Chain-of-Thought annotations and precise visual grounding needed to evaluate whether a model truly connects visual evidence to abstract rule knowledge. SportR addresses this by providing the first multi-sport benchmark with fully human-authored CoT rationales and an explicit bounding-box grounding task across five ball and racket sports.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260780/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 4,789 static sports images and 2,052 normal-speed video clips (basketball, soccer, table tennis, badminton, American football) paired with a progressive 13-question hierarchy (Q1–Q13) that escalates from infraction detection to penalty prediction, free-form explanation, tactic identification, and—uniquely—precise bounding-box coordinate output for the infraction location. Outputs are structured as text answers enclosed in <answer> tags, free-form explanations in <Explanation> tags, or coordinate tuples like [x1, y1, x2, y2] for the grounding task, with the most complex tasks additionally requiring a <think> reasoning block before the final answer.

## Method

SportR's core design is a three-level reasoning pyramid (perception → fundamental rule reasoning → elite scenarios) instantiated as a progressive QA suite where each image or video is annotated with a single human-authored CoT rationale that serves simultaneously as ground truth for penalty prediction, as the target for free-form explanation, and as a training signal for step-by-step reasoning. To validate the benchmark as a training resource, the authors fine-tune Qwen2.5-VL-7B in two stages: a cold-start SFT phase (10% of SportsImage, 6 epochs) that teaches answer format and CoT structure, followed by GRPO-based reinforcement learning where the reward combines an LLM-as-Judge binary correctness signal (weighted 1.0) with a GIoU-derived grounding reward (weighted 0.5) and a format incentive for proper <think>/<answer> tagging. Evaluation uses an ensemble of three proprietary LLM judges (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Claude 4 Sonnet) averaged to mitigate self-preference bias, validated against 660 human-annotated samples with Pearson r > 0.65, alongside standard IoU for the grounding task.

## Results

On SportsImage, GPT-5 achieves the best zero-shot performance with 69.19% on infraction identification (Q1) and 44.21% on foul classification (Q2), but all models score below 7% IoU on the explicit visual grounding task (Q5). After SFT+RL training on SportsImage, Qwen-VL-7B improves dramatically: Q1 rises from 48.29% to 84.19%, Q3 (penalty prediction) from 21.69% to 52.34%, and Q7 (defensive tactic) from 21.81% to 87.07%, though grounding IoU only reaches 9.94%. On SportsVideo, GPT-5 leads with 34.39% on foul classification and 41.83% on penalty prediction, while the image-only-trained Qwen SFT+RL model surprisingly surpasses GPT-5 on video infraction identification (59.52% vs. 59.17%), demonstrating cross-modal generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's trained model (Qwen-VL-7B SFT+RL) outperforms GPT-5 on three of seven SportsImage tasks: infraction identification (84.19% vs. 69.19%), penalty prediction (52.34% vs. 44.49%), and defensive tactic identification (87.07% vs. 58.82%), but trails GPT-5 on foul classification (51.54% vs. 44.21%—actually a win), free-form explanation (27.44% vs. 41.34%), and visual grounding (9.94% vs. 5.70%—a win). Among zero-shot baselines, GPT-5 dominates on most reasoning tasks, while Claude 4 Sonnet is the strongest on defensive tactics (73.30%) and Gemini 2.5 Pro leads on free-form explanation (35.16%). LLaVA-Next-8B and DeepSeek-VL2 score 0% and 0.91% IoU respectively on grounding, indicating a near-complete failure at spatial localization. On SportsVideo, GPT-5 leads on foul classification (34.39%), penalty prediction (41.83%), and offensive tactics (60.82%), but the image-only-trained Qwen SFT+RL model surpasses it on video infraction identification (59.52% vs. 59.17%), confirming cross-modal transfer. Overall, no model exceeds 52% on any core reasoning task, and grounding IoU remains below 10% across all models, underscoring the benchmark's difficulty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of multimodal sports QA benchmarks (SPORTU, SoccerNet-XFoul, FSBench, FineBadminton) by unifying five sports under a single progressive reasoning hierarchy and, critically, by providing 6,841 fully human-authored Chain-of-Thought annotations that enable supervised and RL-based training rather than evaluation-only use. It departs from prior multi-sport work (notably SPORTU) by replacing multiple-choice and slow-motion formats with open-ended generation on normal-speed video, and by introducing an explicit bounding-box grounding task for rule infractions that no prior sports benchmark has attempted. The training methodology (SFT followed by GRPO) follows the recent DeepSeek-R1/DeepSeekMath paradigm of cold-start fine-tuning plus group-relative policy optimization, adapted here with a GIoU-based reward for the grounding task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SportR fills a concrete gap: prior sports benchmarks either covered a single sport or lacked the fine-grained CoT traces and spatial grounding annotations needed to train and verify rule-based reasoning. The finding that explicit visual grounding remains near-failure even after SFT+RL (IoU 4.61% → 9.94%) highlights a fundamental disconnect between abstract rule knowledge and localized visual evidence that the field has not yet addressed. The cross-modal generalization result (image-trained model improving video infraction detection from 25.5% to 59.5%) suggests that static-image reasoning may serve as a transferable foundation for temporal sports understanding. Open questions include extending grounding to video, scaling to additional sports, and whether the current CoT annotation granularity is sufficient to close the domain-knowledge gap revealed by the error analysis.
