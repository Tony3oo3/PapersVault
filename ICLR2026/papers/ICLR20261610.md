## Problem

Standard supervised fine-tuning with cross-entropy loss drives the token-level probability distribution toward a one-hot target, collapsing output diversity and erasing plausible alternatives preserved in the pre-trained model. Existing remedies either modify only decoding (nucleus sampling, best-of-N) or alter the training objective (e.g., GEM's reverse-KL with entropy regularization) but fail to simultaneously preserve a compact set of plausible candidates while driving irrelevant tail tokens to near-zero. The paper formalizes this requirement as Tail-Suppressed Plausible Diversity (TSPD) and asks how to achieve it within a principled, drop-in SFT framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard text prompts and instructions (capped at 2,048 tokens) and produces generated text responses or code completions, identical in format to any SFT-trained LLM. The unusual aspect is the dual probability mapping: during training, logits are projected through sparsemax+ to compute the loss, while at inference the same logits are mapped through standard softmax for calibrated decoding. This decoupling means the model's internal logit geometry is shaped by sparsemax's margin-induced sparsity, but the user-facing probability distribution remains smooth and fully supported.

## Method

TS² decouples the logit-to-probability mapping between training and inference. During training, it minimizes a Sparsemax+ loss: the standard sparsemax Fenchel-Young loss (which provides gradient masking outside the active support set, acting as an implicit early-stopping mechanism) augmented with a tail-suppression penalty α·(−log(1−Σ_{i∉S} p_i^sf)) that explicitly drives out-of-support softmax probabilities toward zero. During inference, the same logits are decoded with softmax, restoring smooth, calibrated probabilities where plausible near-ties survive for sampling. Theoretically, sparsemax expands pairwise probability gaps linearly (derivative = 1) within its support while softmax strictly contracts them, so training with sparsemax sharpens discrimination faster; Corollary 5 then guarantees that the resulting softmax distribution satisfies TSPD when the sparsemax margin is sufficiently large. The tail-suppression term closes the remaining gap: without it, the admissible softmax tail mass grows with vocabulary size and can approach 1, leaving non-negligible probability on clearly implausible tokens.

## Results

On AlpacaEval (BoN@32), TS² achieves a 33.12% win rate on Llama-3.1-8B and 37.48% on Qwen-2-7B, outperforming GEM (31.53% / 33.89%) and CE (29.77% / 31.41%). On HumanEval, TS² reaches pass@100 of 87.00% (Llama) and 91.50% (Qwen), a 4.3% relative gain over GEM. On the six OpenLLM Leaderboard tasks, TS²'s average BoN@32 accuracy reaches 88.88% on Llama-3.1-8B, a 13.2-point absolute improvement over GEM (75.69%). TS² also leads all three diversity metrics (N-gram, 100-Self-BLEU, Sent-BERT) across both architectures, improving N-gram diversity by 17.0% over GEM on Llama.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TS² consistently outperforms all four baselines across every reported metric and both model architectures. Against GEM (the strongest prior method for diversity-preserving SFT), TS² gains +1.59 pp win rate on Llama and +3.59 pp on Qwen, while also improving N-gram diversity by 17.0% (Llama) and 23.8% (Qwen). Against NEFTune, the gap is larger: TS² achieves +3.35 pp win rate on Llama and +7.12 pp on Qwen, with substantially higher diversity scores. CE+WD and CE+label smoothing (0.2) both trail TS² by 3+ pp in win rate; label smoothing in particular drops to 28.25% on Llama, confirming that indiscriminate probability spreading hurts quality. On code generation (HumanEval pass@100), TS² leads GEM by 3.6 pp (87.00% vs 83.40%) on Llama and 1.3 pp (91.50% vs 90.20%) on Qwen, and on OpenLLM Leaderboard BoN@32 it surpasses GEM by 13.2 pp on Llama, indicating that the diversity preserved by TS² translates into substantially better sample efficiency for knowledge-intensive tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Fenchel-Young loss framework (Blondel et al., 2019) and the sparsemax projection (Martins & Astudillo, 2016), repurposing them for LLM supervised fine-tuning in a way neither was originally designed for. It departs from the dominant CE-based SFT paradigm and from GEM (Li et al., 2025), which uses reverse-KL with entropy regularization, by introducing a decoupled training/inference mapping: a Sparsemax+ loss (sparsemax Fenchel-Young loss plus an explicit tail-suppression penalty) for training, and standard softmax for decoding. Unlike decoding-time diversity methods (nucleus, top-k) or embedding-noise regularizers (NEFTune), TS² reshapes the logit geometry during training itself, making it complementary to rather than competitive with those approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled train-with-sparsemax+/test-with-softmax paradigm offers a drop-in replacement for CE in existing SFT pipelines that simultaneously improves accuracy and diversity, directly addressing the well-documented 'alignment tax.' The ablation revealing that neither decoupling alone nor suppression alone suffices—only their combination works—highlights a non-trivial interaction between gradient masking and tail control that is not obvious from either component in isolation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, whether it composes with RLHF/DPO stages, and whether the BoN evaluation protocol (which favors diverse models) overstates gains in single-sample deployment settings. The theoretical tail-mass bound growing with vocabulary size (Corollary 5) also suggests the suppression term may need re-tuning for very large vocabularies.
