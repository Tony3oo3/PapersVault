## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of in-context learning (ICL) generalization for Transformers rely on restrictive assumptions such as pairwise orthogonal token patterns, independent token sampling, or i.i.d. data, limiting their applicability to realistic non-i.i.d. scenarios with distribution shift. This paper asks: under what conditions do nonlinear multi-layer, multi-head Transformers generalize effectively in ICL when training and target distributions differ, and how do optimization dynamics (mini-batch GD) interact with loss landscape geometry to control this generalization?

## Input / Output

The model consumes a length-N_p ICL prompt constructed by concatenating N_e demonstration (input, output-sequence) pairs with a query input into a single flattened vector in R^{N_p × D}. Training uses N weighted prompt-response pairs optimized via mini-batch gradient descent with learning rate decay η_k = 1/k^α. The theoretical output is a high-probability generalization error bound decomposed into empirical risk, a distributional discrepancy term, an algorithmic stability term, and a vanishing statistical term; the practical output is autoregressive next-token prediction where each intermediate token feeds back into the prompt for subsequent predictions.

## Method

The paper establishes a generalization framework built on two pillars: (1) a uniform algorithmic stability bound for Transformers under mini-batch GD (Theorem 1), which reveals that the Lipschitz smoothness constant γ of the loss landscape determines whether iteration count Q can scale polynomially (smooth case) or must be restricted to O(log N) (non-smooth case), with an optimal step-size choice yielding O(N^{-1}) stability convergence; and (2) a hypothesis-space-independent discrepancy measure (Theorems 2–3) that quantifies distribution shift, giving an asymptotically vanishing bound in the i.i.d. case and a sequential Rademacher complexity-based bound in the non-i.i.d. case. Combining these yields the main generalization bound (Theorem 4) achieving O(N^{-1/2}) convergence under i.i.d. data, and a weighted-sample optimization problem (Remark 5) for cross-domain settings. A separate error accumulation analysis (Theorem 5) shows that autoregressive prediction errors compound multiplicatively across tokens, requiring the inference length N_c to grow at most logarithmically with N to prevent generalization collapse.

## Results

The paper establishes that under i.i.d. data, the ICL generalization error achieves the optimal O(N^{-1/2}) convergence rate regardless of loss landscape smoothness, provided appropriate choices of batch size and iteration count. For non-i.i.d. settings, the bound degrades to O(N^{max{ζ3, ζ1+ζ2γ/(1+γ)−1}}) (smooth) or O(N^{max{2Lζ2+ζ3, ζ4, ζ1−1}}) (non-smooth), where ζ3 captures the distributional discrepancy. A key finding is that token-wise prediction errors accumulate polynomially, requiring the inference length N_c to grow at most as O((ln N)^{ζ2}) to prevent generalization collapse. Algorithmic stability under mini-batch GD achieves O(N^{-1}) when the step size is appropriately chosen, with the iteration count Q scaling polynomially in N for smooth landscapes but only logarithmically for non-smooth ones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so comparisons are made at the level of assumptions and coverage rather than empirical benchmarks. Table 1 positions the paper against six prior theoretical works (Li et al. 2024b, Feng et al. 2023, Chen et al. 2024, Bai et al. 2024, Yang et al. 2024b, Li et al. 2024a). The key distinction is that prior works each require at least one restrictive assumption: orthogonal token patterns (Li et al. 2024b, Feng et al. 2023, Li et al. 2024a), i.i.d. sampling (Chen et al. 2024), or special input structures (Bai et al. 2024, Yang et al. 2024b). This paper is the only one that simultaneously handles multi-layer multi-head architectures, optimization-dependent analysis, distribution shift, no special input structure, and orthogonality-free data. The empirical validation uses GPT-2 (12 layers, 8 heads) on a 10-dimensional linear regression task, confirming the O(N^{-1/2}) convergence and the logarithmic threshold for error accumulation predicted by the theory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discrepancy-based generalization framework of Kuznetsov & Mohri (2015, 2020) from non-stationary time series forecasting to the ICL setting, making the discrepancy measure hypothesis-space independent. It adapts the algorithmic stability analysis for SGD under bilevel optimization (Bao et al., 2021) to multi-head, multi-layer Transformers trained with mini-batch gradient descent. The key departure from prior ICL generalization work (Li et al. 2023, Huang et al. 2024, Chen et al. 2024, Wu et al. 2024) is the simultaneous removal of restrictive assumptions: no orthogonal token patterns, no i.i.d. sampling, no single-head restriction, and no special prompt structure, while explicitly handling distribution shift between training and target domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first ICL generalization framework that jointly accounts for optimization dynamics (mini-batch GD), loss landscape smoothness, multi-layer multi-head architecture, and non-i.i.d. distribution shift without idealized input structures, providing practitioners with concrete guidance on batch size, iteration count, and prediction length. The error accumulation result (Theorem 5) giving a theoretical justification for constraining autoregressive generation length to O(log N) is practically relevant for understanding degradation in long-form generation. Limitations include bounds that grow exponentially in depth L, empirical validation restricted to a simple linear regression task with GPT-2, and the non-i.i.d. analysis requiring the existence of a 'related' subset of training data. Open questions include tightening bounds via gradient stability techniques and extending empirical validation to more complex language tasks.
