## Problem

Matching-centric stereo disparity estimation is inherently vulnerable to visual ambiguities (occlusion, non-Lambertian surfaces, repeated patterns) that produce erroneous correspondences local refinement cannot recover. While Vision Transformers have shown strong capacity in geometry regression tasks, their use in stereo has been largely confined to view feature extractors within conventional matching pipelines, leaving direct disparity regression from binocular input largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263009/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a rectified stereo image pair (left and right 3-channel RGB views) and produces a disparity map for the left view. Disparity is not regressed as a scalar but parameterized as a probability distribution over 128 uniformly spaced bins covering the range [0, 381], with the final estimate computed as the expectation within a local window around the peak. Training uses 392×768 image crops, and the shift-embedding tokenizer applies K=8 horizontal shifts of 24 pixels each to the right view before tokenization.

## Method

DispViT replaces explicit cost-volume matching with a single-stream ViT (initialized from DepthAnythingV2) that directly regresses disparity from a unified token sequence of both views. The stereo tokenizer blends left-view tokens (pretrained PatchEmbed) with right-view tokens encoded via shift-embedding—each of K shifted variants is tokenized into a separate channel group, and all groups are summed with the left tokens so each spatial position encodes a spectrum of alignment hypotheses. A Disparity-Aware RoPE (DA-RoPE) extension rotates value vectors by relative position to embed translational equivariance into the value pathway, while asymmetric (half-zero) initialization of the right-view convolution prevents early training degeneracy. A DPT head fuses multi-scale ViT features to output the probability distribution, supervised by cross-entropy plus L1 loss. A lightweight NMRF-based refinement module, trained separately with the ViT frozen, then sharpens boundaries and thin structures via single-pass geometry warping guided by the initial disparity.

## Results

On Scene Flow (the primary benchmark), DispViT+ achieves EPE 0.34 and BP-1 3.50, outperforming the best matching-centric method Selective-IGEV (EPE 0.44, BP-1 4.98) and the best hybrid method BridgeDepth (EPE 0.37, BP-1 3.67), while running in 0.118 s. The standalone DispViT regressor (without refinement) reaches EPE 0.53 in just 0.092 s, already competitive with RAFT-Stereo (EPE 0.56, 0.40 s). In zero-shot evaluation, DispViT+ achieves D1 3.2 on KITTI-12, D1 3.5 on KITTI-15, BP-2 2.4 on Middlebury, and BP-1 2.3 on ETH3D, matching or exceeding BridgeDepth and Monster across all four datasets. On the ETH3D leaderboard, DispViT+ reports AvgErr 0.12 px and RMSE 0.25 px, on par with DEFOM-Stereo and BridgeDepth (0.11/0.26).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DispViT+ decisively outperforms all four matching-centric baselines on Scene Flow: it beats RAFT-Stereo by 0.22 EPE, DLNR by 0.14, Selective-IGEV by 0.10, and NMRF by 0.11, while also being faster than RAFT-Stereo (0.118 s vs 0.40 s). Against hybrid methods, DispViT+ edges out BridgeDepth (EPE 0.34 vs 0.37) and DEFOM-Stereo (0.34 vs 0.42) on Scene Flow, and in zero-shot it surpasses BridgeDepth on Middlebury (BP-2 2.4 vs 4.3) and Monster† (2.4 vs 2.9). On KITTI 2012, DispViT+ achieves the best Out-2 (1.26/1.59) and Out-3 (0.82/1.02) among all listed methods, beating BridgeDepth (1.32/1.65) and DEFOM-Stereo (1.43/1.79). However, on KITTI 2015 foreground D1, DispViT+ (3.10/3.26) trails DEFOM-Stereo (2.23/2.24) and BridgeDepth (2.73/2.62), likely due to the frozen regressor's limited adaptation to sparse, noisy LiDAR annotations. On ETH3D, DispViT+ (AvgErr 0.12, RMSE 0.25) is within 0.01 px of the best methods (DEFOM-Stereo, BridgeDepth at 0.11/0.26), confirming competitive accuracy with improved inference speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

DispViT departs from the dominant matching-centric paradigm (cost volumes + iterative refinement, as in RAFT-Stereo, IGEV, Selective-IGEV) and from hybrid methods that bootstrap stereo with monocular depth (DEFOM-Stereo, Monster, BridgeDepth). Conceptually, it revives the direct-regression idea of DispNetS (Mayer et al., 2016) but makes it viable for the first time by leveraging a single-stream ViT's global attention to overcome the locality bottleneck that limited convolutional regressors. The architecture builds on DINOv2/DepthAnythingV2 pretrained weights, a DPT prediction head, and NMRF's lightweight refinement module, while introducing shift-embedding tokenization, DA-RoPE, asymmetric initialization, and probability-based disparity parameterization as the key enablers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single-stream ViT can directly regress disparity competitively without explicit matching, the paper establishes regression-centric stereo as a credible alternative paradigm with inherent robustness to occlusion, transparency, and repeated-pattern ambiguities that plague matching pipelines. The ~4× speedup over RAFT-Stereo and the finding that a better regression prior (not a better refiner) drives the 24% gain over NMRF suggest that future stereo progress may come from improving holistic geometric reasoning rather than refining local correspondences. Open questions include whether scaling stereo pretraining data can close the remaining gap on real-world benchmarks (ETH3D, KITTI FG), how to handle mirror-induced visual illusions that remain a failure mode, and whether monocular depth priors can be distilled into the stereo regressor to further improve distribution-shift robustness.
