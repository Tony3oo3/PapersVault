## Problem

Existing LLM unlearning methods either leak the very data they aim to erase, sacrifice fluency and adversarial robustness, or depend on costly external reward models. The paper addresses the need for a unified, self-contained unlearning approach that prevents residual leakage, preserves general model utility, and avoids reliance on external reward models by reframing forgetting as a verifiable reinforcement learning task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262369/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a base LLM (e.g., Phi-3-Mini-4K-Instruct, 3.8B parameters), a set of target entities to forget (e.g., a person's name), and a probe query set reused from the RWKU rejection-tuning dataset. It bootstraps a synthetic forget corpus by running the base model on the probes and using a GPT-4-conditioned NER prompt to extract the top-50 descriptive entities per target, which define the forbidden token set. The output is a fine-tuned policy that suppresses all completions containing any of those entities while maintaining downstream task performance, evaluated via ROUGE-L recall on forget/neighbor sets, membership-inference scores, and standard utility benchmarks.

## Method

PURGE adapts Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to unlearning by defining a binary verifiable reward φ(x)=1[x∩D'_F=∅] that assigns 1 when no forbidden entity tokens appear in a completion and 0 otherwise. For each probe query, the method samples W completions from the current policy, computes group-relative advantages by normalizing rewards within the group (mean/std), and updates the policy via a clipped PPO objective with an explicit KL penalty against a reference policy. This eliminates the need for an external reward model entirely, since the reward is computed by simple token-set intersection. The paper provides two theoretical guarantees: Theorem 1 proves geometric decay of forbidden-token leakage probability under sampling mixing, and Theorem 2 bounds the utility drop on retained tasks by the square root of the KL divergence between the unlearned and original policies. Empirically, PURGE achieves up to ×46 fewer tokens per forget target than SoTA methods while improving fluency by +5.48% and adversarial robustness by +12.02% over the base model.

## Results

On the RWKU Famous People benchmark with Phi-3-Mini-4K-Instruct, PURGE reduces forget-set recall by 5.53% (FB), 10.07% (QA), and 12.02% (Adversarial Attack) relative to the base model, while achieving the highest fluency score (FLU = 140.90) among all methods, a +7.32 improvement over base. PURGE uses up to ×46 fewer tokens per forget target than DPO (6,412 vs. 615,585 tokens) and ×22 fewer than GA/NPO, while preserving 98% of original utility. Across 12 evaluation aspects, PURGE outperforms the intact base model on 8, ranks second-best on 5, and first on 2, and is robust against 8 of 9 adversarial attack types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PURGE outperforms Gradient Ascent (GA), DPO, Rejection Tuning (RT), and In-Context Unlearning (ICU) on all three forget-set splits (FB, QA, AA), with the largest margin over DPO (e.g., AA: 0.481 vs. 0.600) and ICU (AA: 0.481 vs. 0.484). Against NPO, PURGE trades off forget-set strength (NPO achieves substantially lower recall, e.g., FB 0.296 vs. 0.428) for superior neighbor-set retention (PURGE FB 0.513 vs. NPO 0.431) and the highest fluency score (140.90 vs. 133.15), while using ×22 fewer tokens per target. PURGE also uses ×46 fewer tokens than DPO (6,412 vs. 615,585) and ×22 fewer than GA/NPO (298,067 each), making it the most token-efficient method tested. On the MIA set, PURGE ties for best RM score (38.64, tied with RT) and achieves a competitive FM score (40.26), indicating effective resistance to membership inference attacks. Overall, PURGE is the only method that simultaneously improves fluency over the base model while reducing forget-set recall, whereas RT and ICU degrade fluency substantially (121.40 and 127.36 respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework from DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024), originally designed for verifiable mathematical reasoning, and applies it to LLM unlearning by treating forbidden-token suppression as a verifiable reward signal. It departs from the three dominant unlearning paradigms—gradient-ascent fine-tuning, preference optimization (DPO/NPO), and rejection tuning—by eliminating the need for external reward models or preference pairs, instead using a simple binary indicator over extracted entities. The conceptual bridge to Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR, Lambert et al., 2024) positions unlearning alongside math and code generation as a task amenable to objective, measurable optimization rather than subjective preference learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ×46 token efficiency gain and the theoretical guarantees (geometric decay of forbidden-token probability, KL-bounded utility retention) offer a practical and principled path toward regulatory compliance under GDPR and the EU AI Act without full retraining. However, the method operates at the surface-token level, leaving open whether internal representations are truly modified or merely rendered harder to elicit; the binary reward also cannot capture semantic paraphrases beyond extracted entities. Key open questions include whether PURGE generalizes to batch unloading, to reasoning models with chain-of-thought, and whether the NER-based forget corpus construction can be made fully automated without manual validation.
