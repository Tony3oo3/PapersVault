## Problem

The existing CQC estimation procedure requires first estimating the CCDF contrasting function h(y₁,y₀,x) and then inverting it to recover the CQC, which obscures the estimate, prevents explicit parameterisation of the CQC, and ties estimation error to the complexity of the intermediate function rather than the CQC itself. This limits interpretability, constrains the use of informative model structures, and makes evaluation computationally expensive.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular observational data (outcome Y ∈ ℝ, covariates X ∈ ℝᵈ, binary treatment A ∈ {0,1}) split into two halves: one for nuisance estimation (propensity score via logistic regression, conditional CCDFs via kernel estimation) and one for CQC fitting. It produces a parameterised CQC function g_θ(y₀|x) that maps an untreated response value to the corresponding treated response value, with θ̂ recovered via gradient descent. The CQC model can be a correctly specified linear scale-and-shift form or a fully connected ReLU neural network, and the output is directly interpretable as a transport map in response space.

## Method

The core contribution is reframing CQC estimation as an M-estimation problem via a novel loss ℓ̄(y₁,y₀,x) = ∫_{g*(y₀|x)}^{y₁} [F₁(t|x) − F₀(y₀|x)] dt, whose minimiser over y₁ is exactly the CQC g*(y₀|x). A doubly robust gradient estimator ζ_dr is then derived by combining inverse-probability-weighted indicator terms with the CCDF difference, yielding an unbiased gradient of the population loss that retains double robustness: the estimation error depends on the product of propensity and CCDF estimation errors rather than either alone. Training proceeds via projected gradient descent (or Adam) on the parameterised CQC model g_θ(y₀|x) = θᵀf(y₀,x) using sample-split data, with theoretical guarantees of O(1/√n) loss convergence under convexity and O(log n/n) under strong convexity (bounded-below treated density). The explicit parameterisation allows the CQC to be directly constrained, regularised, and inspected, and the estimation error scales with the complexity of the CQC itself rather than the upstream CCDF contrast.

## Results

The primary metric is mean absolute error (MAE) of the CQC estimate, reported with 95% confidence intervals over 100 runs. Across all three experimental axes (varying CQC slope γ, varying nuisance parameter error, and varying sample size n), the direct doubly-robust estimator (both linear 'DR-Lin' and neural network 'DR-NN' parameterisations) achieves the lowest MAE, with the linear model performing marginally better due to correct specification. The direct approach's advantage grows substantially as the CQC slope increases, while the inverting baseline's error degrades. On the real-world Work First employment dataset, the linear CQC model reveals that the range of potential earnings improvement narrows as participant age increases, with younger participants showing multiplicative wage scaling and older participants showing a more uniform shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the inverting doubly-robust CQC estimator (Givens et al., 2024, 'Inv. DR'), the proposed direct estimator wins on MAE across all tested settings: varying CQC steepness, varying nuisance parameter error, and varying sample size. The gap is most pronounced at higher CQC slopes (larger γ), where the inverting method's error grows while the direct linear model's error remains relatively flat. However, the inverting approach is somewhat less sensitive to nuisance parameter estimation error, a limitation the authors acknowledge. Against the CQTE estimator of Kallus and Oprescu (2023), the direct CQC estimator performs significantly better in almost all settings, with the CQTE approach performing comparably to the inverting CQC method. An IPW variant of the proposed method (without double robustness) performs substantially worse, confirming the value of the doubly-robust construction. The S-Learner baseline performs comparably to the direct DR approach in most settings except when the CQC slope is zero.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Givens et al. (2024), which introduced the conditional quantile comparator (CQC) estimand and an inversion-based estimation procedure that first estimates the CCDF contrasting function h(y₁,y₀,x) and then inverts it to recover the CQC. The key departure is replacing this two-step procedure with a direct M-estimation framework that explicitly parameterizes the CQC, drawing on the doubly robust gradient estimation paradigm already established for CATE (Kennedy et al., 2023; Nie and Wager, 2020) and CQTE (Kallus and Oprescu, 2023). The loss function is constructed so that its gradient recovers the CCDF contrast, but the optimization operates on the CQC parameters directly, making estimation error depend on CQC complexity rather than the intermediate function's complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CQC is a promising but young estimand that bridges CATE and CQTE by working in response space rather than quantile space, making it more interpretable for practical treatment-effect communication. Having a direct, explicitly parameterizable estimator enables model constraints (smoothness, linearity, neural architectures) and faster evaluation, which the inversion approach cannot support. However, the paper acknowledges that the direct estimator is empirically somewhat more sensitive to nuisance parameter error than the inversion method, and the convergence guarantees require linearity in parameters (limiting applicability to neural networks). Open questions include whether a doubly robust estimator of the conditional-expectation form (common for CATE/CQTE) can be constructed for the CQC, and whether convergence results extend to non-linear parameterizations.
