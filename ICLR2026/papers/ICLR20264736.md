## Problem

Most offline RL methods rely on conservative action-level regularizations to prevent OOD exploitation, which severely limits sample efficiency and OOD generalization—critical shortcomings in real-world settings where data is scarce (e.g., industrial control, robotics). Existing state-stitching approaches that bypass action constraints still require sufficient state-action coverage for valid transitions, and prior T-symmetry-based methods (TSRL) remain tied to action-level backbones like TD3+BC, inheriting the same over-conservatism.

## Input / Output

During training, TELS consumes an offline dataset of (s, a, s') transitions (state vectors with 105 features in the DC cooling testbed; standard MuJoCo/Antmaze state vectors in benchmarks), typically at 0.5%–10% of original D4RL sizes (5k–100k samples). At inference, given only the current state s from the environment, it outputs a single optimized action a* via the learned TS-IDM, with no additional training or environment interaction required. The entire TS-IDM training completes in ~20 minutes (PyTorch) or ~5 minutes (JAX) on a single GPU.

## Method

TELS introduces a T-symmetry enforced inverse dynamics model (TS-IDM) that jointly learns a state encoder/decoder, a latent inverse dynamics module h_inv, and a pair of latent ODE forward/reverse dynamics (h_fwd, h_rvs) constrained by h_fwd = −h_rvs, with ODE property enforcement applied to both encoder and decoder (unlike prior TSRL which only enforced it on the encoder). This produces a compact, well-structured latent state space where state-stitching is meaningful even for OOD samples. Policy optimization then proceeds entirely in this latent space: a state-value function is learned via IQL-style expectile regression, and a guide-policy (deterministic or stochastic) is trained to output the reward-maximizing next latent state, regularized by a T-symmetry consistency term ℓ_T-sym that penalizes state-action pairs violating the learned dynamics. The final action is extracted by feeding the guide-policy's output back through h_inv and the action decoder, completely bypassing action-level behavioral constraints.

## Results

On reduced-size D4RL benchmarks (5k–100k samples, 0.5%–10% of original), TELS achieves state-of-the-art normalized scores across all 20 tasks, with particularly dramatic gains on the hardest settings: 100.9±6.8 on Hopper-me (0.5% data) vs. 50.9±8.6 for TSRL, and 47.2–62.9 on Antmaze-medium/large (10% data) where all other baselines score 0.0. On the real-world DC cooling control testbed, TELS achieves the best energy efficiency (20.17% ACLF) with zero thermal safety violations, compared to CQL's 10.3% ACLF but 40.99% violations. Training is highly efficient: the JAX implementation completes in just 20 minutes on a single RTX 4090, versus 450–1200 minutes for most baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TELS outperforms all nine baselines on every D4RL task in the small-sample setting. The closest competitor is TSRL (same T-symmetry idea but with TD3+BC action-level constraints), which TELS beats by 15–50 points on locomotion tasks (e.g., 100.9 vs. 50.9 on Hopper-me) and by 47–63 points on Antmaze-medium/large where TSRL scores 0.0. POR shares TELS's state-stitching policy optimization but operates in the original state space without T-symmetry regularization; TELS surpasses it by 30–67 points (e.g., 87.4 vs. 20.1 on Walker2d-me). Conservative methods (TD3+BC, CQL) and model-based methods (MOPO, COMBO) fail catastrophically on small datasets, often scoring below 20. IQL and DOGE perform moderately on locomotion but collapse to near-zero on Antmaze-medium/large. The diffusion-based IDQL is competitive on some locomotion tasks (e.g., 38.8 on Halfcheetah-me) but still trails TELS and scores near-zero on Antmaze. On the real-world DC cooling testbed, TELS achieves the best ACLF (20.17%) with zero thermal violations, while CQL achieves lower ACLF (10.3%) but with 40.99% safety violations, and IQL/TSRL achieve 40.89%/27.16% ACLF respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

TELS synthesizes two previously separate lines of offline RL research: the T-symmetry enforced representation learning of TSRL (Cheng et al., 2023) and the state-stitching policy decomposition of POR (Xu et al., 2022a). The key architectural departure from TSRL is replacing its state-action encoder with a state-only encoder and adding an inverse dynamics module inside the T-symmetry enforced latent space, thereby decoupling representation learning from behavioral policy biases and enabling the model to double as an execute-policy. A further technical improvement over TSRL's TDM is enforcing ODE consistency on the state decoder (not just the encoder), which the authors show is critical for stable training. The value function learning follows IQL's expectile regression, and the guide-policy optimization borrows the AWR formulation from POR but adds T-symmetry consistency as an additional regularizer in latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in offline RL: most methods require large, well-covered datasets, while real-world deployments (industrial control, robotics, healthcare) often have only a few thousand samples. By grounding the latent space in a fundamental physical symmetry rather than data-distribution statistics, TELS achieves OOD generalization that is not merely an interpolation over the dataset, which is validated by the extreme deletion experiment and real-world DC cooling deployment. The main open limitation is that the framework is designed for deterministic dynamics; adapting T-symmetry enforcement to stochastic environments remains unresolved. Additionally, the strong ODE and T-symmetry regularizations can constrain model expressivity, suggesting a trade-off between fundamental pattern extraction and flexibility that future work should address.
