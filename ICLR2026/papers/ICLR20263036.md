## Problem

Standard vision-language models autoregressively generate text tokens, forcing them to model both task-relevant semantics and surface-level linguistic variability (word choice, paraphrasing), which wastes training compute and makes the target distribution unnecessarily complex. Additionally, token-by-token decoding introduces latency that prevents real-time streaming applications from performing selective or sparse decoding. The paper asks whether predicting continuous semantic embeddings in a latent space, rather than discrete tokens, can yield a more efficient and equally capable vision-language model.

## Input / Output

The model consumes video inputs uniformly sampled at 256² resolution (8–32 frames; single images are duplicated to match the video shape) along with a textual query of up to 512 tokens. The primary output is a 1536-dimensional continuous target embedding produced in a single forward pass; a lightweight y-decoder translates this embedding into human-readable text only when invoked. For discriminative tasks (classification, retrieval, VQA), candidate labels or answers are encoded by the same y-encoder and the nearest match to the predicted embedding is selected, eliminating any generation step entirely.

## Method

VL-JEPA replaces autoregressive token prediction with embedding-space prediction: a frozen V-JEPA 2 ViT-L x-encoder (304M) maps visual input to visual tokens, a predictor built from the last 8 layers of Llama-3.2-1B (490M trainable params, with causal attention disabled for bi-directional vision-query interaction) maps ⟨S_V, X_Q⟩ to a predicted target embedding Ŝ_Y, and an EmbeddingGemma-300M y-encoder provides the target S_Y. Training uses bi-directional InfoNCE loss in the shared 1536-d embedding space, which simultaneously aligns predictions with targets and regularizes against collapse, avoiding the need for a heavy language decoder during training. The key insight is that multiple valid answers for the same input (e.g., 'the lamp is turned off' vs. 'room will go dark') are orthogonal in one-hot token space but map to nearby points in embedding space, collapsing a multimodal target into a single coherent mode and simplifying learning. A two-stage training procedure first establishes vision-language alignment via query-free pretraining on 3.3B caption pairs (Datacomp, YFCC-100M, ACTION100M), then adds VQA capability through supervised finetuning. At inference, the non-autoregressive design natively supports selective decoding: the continuous embedding stream is monitored and decoded only when local variance exceeds a threshold, reducing decoding operations by ~2.85× while preserving output quality.

## Results

On 8 video classification datasets, VL-JEPA_BASE (1.6B params, 3.3B samples) achieves 52.5% average top-1 accuracy, surpassing the best baseline PE-Core ViT-G (2.3B params, 86B samples) at 44.7% by 7.8 points. On 8 text-to-video retrieval datasets, VL-JEPA_BASE achieves 63.7% average Recall@1 vs. PE-Core-G's 58.1%. After supervised finetuning, VL-JEPA_SFT reaches 75.4% average classification accuracy and 63.8% retrieval Recall@1, approaching specialist-model performance. In the strictly controlled comparison (§4.4), VL-JEPA achieves 14.8 CIDEr and 41.0% top-5 accuracy at 15M samples seen, versus 7.1 CIDEr and 27.2% for the token-predictive VLM baseline with the same encoder, data, and compute budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VL-JEPA_BASE outperforms all zero-shot generalist baselines (CLIP, SigLIP2, PE-Core) on both classification and retrieval averages, despite seeing 4–26× fewer training samples (3.3B vs. 12.8B–86B). The gap is largest on motion-centric benchmarks (SSV2: 19.3 vs. 9.0 for PE-Core-G; EK-100: 33.2 vs. 13.6), while VL-JEPA is relatively weaker on appearance-centric tasks (Kinetics-400: 64.8 vs. 76.4). On VQA, VL-JEPA_SFT (1.6B) achieves 61.5 on GQA, 69.9 on TallyQA, 85.7 on POPE, and 86.3 on POPEv2, matching or exceeding InstructBLIP (Vicuna-13B: 49.5/68.0/79.0) and approaching Qwen-VL-7B (59.3) and LLaVA-1.5 (Vicuna-7B: 62.0) with far fewer parameters. On WORLDPREDICTION-WM, VL-JEPA_SFT sets a new SoTA at 65.7%, surpassing GPT-4o (53.6%), Claude-3.5-Sonnet (49.1%), and Gemini-2.0 (48.5%). In the controlled token-vs-embedding comparison, VL-JEPA with a 0.5B predictor outperforms a 1B LLM-based VLM by 7.7 CIDEr points (14.8 vs. 7.1) and 13.8 top-5 accuracy points (41.0% vs. 27.2%) at 15M samples, using 50% fewer trainable parameters. Selective decoding reduces inference cost by ~2.85× (decoding at 0.35 Hz matches uniform 1 Hz performance) without quality loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

VL-JEPA extends the Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) paradigm—previously applied to unimodal image (I-JEPA) and video (V-JEPA) representation learning—into the vision-language domain, where it replaces autoregressive token prediction with continuous embedding prediction. Conceptually, it occupies a middle ground between CLIP-style contrastive models (which align but do not predict) and generative VLMs (which predict in token space), inheriting the retrieval/classification strengths of the former and the conditional generation capability of the latter while training in a simplified latent target space. The architecture composes existing components (V-JEPA 2 vision encoder, Llama-3.2-1B predictor layers, EmbeddingGemma-300M text encoder) but the key departure is the training objective: predicting a target embedding via InfoNCE rather than next-token cross-entropy, which the authors argue reduces the effective target distribution from multiple disjoint token sequences to a single coherent mode in continuous space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that embedding-space supervision can match or exceed token-space VLMs with roughly half the trainable parameters, and that the non-autoregressive design natively enables selective decoding for streaming video—reducing decoding operations by ~2.85× while preserving quality. This is practically significant for real-time applications (robotics, AR/VR, live action tracking) where latency and compute budget are binding constraints. However, the authors explicitly scope the contribution: VL-JEPA is not positioned as a universal VLM replacement, and evaluation on reasoning, tool use, and agentic tasks—where token-generative VLMs currently excel—is absent. Open questions include whether the embedding-space advantage persists at much larger scale, whether more advanced anti-collapse regularizers (VICReg, SIGReg) further improve the gap, and whether latent-space reasoning (analogous to chain-of-thought) can be developed within this framework.
