## Problem

Generalist robot manipulation policies lack long-term visual memory, making them unable to complete tasks that require recalling past observations such as object locations, completed action counts, or previously visited states. Naively extending the observation context is computationally prohibitive and brittle under covariate shift, while indiscriminate subsampling introduces irrelevant or redundant information. The paper addresses how to efficiently select and retain only task-relevant frames from long observation histories so that existing VLA backbones can reason over minutes of memory at real-time inference speeds.

## Input / Output

The high-level policy consumes the last 8 frames per camera (320×180, subsampled to 2 Hz from 15 Hz), the text task instruction, and up to 8 previously selected keyframes, and outputs a language subtask plus indices of candidate keyframes from the recent window. The low-level policy takes the current frame, proprioceptive joint states, and the predicted subtask, producing a chunk of 15 joint-velocity actions executed open-loop for 8 steps before replanning. The two policies run asynchronously on separate GPUs at ~1 Hz and ~2 Hz respectively, with image observations queued between high-level predictions.

## Method

MemER is a hierarchical framework pairing a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct high-level policy with a fine-tuned π₀.5 low-level policy. The key mechanism is that the high-level VLM, leveraging its video-understanding pretraining prior, simultaneously predicts the next language subtask and nominates candidate keyframes from the recent 8-frame context; a lightweight 1D single-linkage clustering step (merge distance d=5, median-index selection per cluster) then consolidates all nominations since the start of the trajectory into a compact set of at most 8 selected keyframes that are fed back into the high-level policy at the next timestep. Training requires only ~50 teleoperated demonstrations per task with semi-automatic keyframe labels derived from subtask boundary frames, and the high-level policy is further stabilized by linearly interpolating its fine-tuned weights with the base model (α=0.8) to preserve robustness against low-level retries and freezes.

## Results

On three real-world long-horizon manipulation tasks requiring minutes of visual memory, MemER achieves 59/60 object retrievals and 57/60 optimal-path searches on Object Search, only 1 wrong scoop on Counting (vs. 61 for the no-history baseline), and near-perfect scores on Dust & Replace (20/20 bottom dust, 19/20 top dust, 18/20 bottom replace, 20/20 top replace). These results are on par with a human high-level policy (58/60, 58/60, 0, 19/20, 19/20, 18/20, 17/20) and outperform the best naive baseline (Long History, 32 frames) by approximately 34% on average across all tasks. Inference latency stays below 0.8 s per high-level prediction on a single RTX 4090, well within the 1 Hz requirement for closed-loop deployment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MemER substantially outperforms all memory-limited baselines: the No History policy (current-frame only) fails catastrophically on Counting (61 wrong scoops vs. 1) and scores below 50% on every Dust & Replace sub-metric; Short History (8 frames) and Long History (32 frames) improve but remain 34% worse than MemER on average, confirming that naively extending context is insufficient. MemER matches or exceeds the Human HL oracle on most sub-metrics (e.g., 59 vs. 58 object retrievals, 20 vs. 19 top-shelf dusting), with the small gap attributable to low-level policy execution failures. In the offline comparison against proprietary VLMs (Table 2), MemER's fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieves 0.80 trajectory accuracy on Object Search versus 0.15 for GPT-5 and 0.21 for Gemini Robotics-ER 1.5; on Dust & Replace the gap is 0.87 vs. 0.67 (GPT-5) and 0.19 (Gemini), with both API models failing primarily by nominating too many non-informative keyframes. The authors note that API-based VLMs were unusable in closed-loop deployment due to 10–15 s latency, further motivating the fine-tuned open-weight approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

MemER extends the hierarchical VLA paradigm of Shi et al. (2025) 'Hi Robot' by adding a persistent visual memory mechanism: the high-level VLM nominates candidate keyframes from its recent context, which are consolidated via 1D single-linkage clustering into a compact episodic memory fed back into the policy. This departs from prior long-context robot policies (Torne et al. 2025, Fang et al. 2025) that extend context to at most ~2 dozen frames, and from video keyframe selection methods in CV (Hu et al. 2025a) that rely on separate scoring models, by having the policy itself nominate keyframes without additional models. The approach also contrasts with navigation memory work that uses explicit geometric/semantic maps, which is inapplicable to manipulation where the robot modifies the environment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MemER demonstrates that robot policies can handle tasks requiring minutes of visual memory (hundreds of frames) while maintaining sub-second inference on a single GPU, a practical milestone for real-world deployment. The finding that visual keyframe memory outperforms both text-only and text+visual memory representations challenges the assumption that condensed textual summaries are sufficient for robot task state tracking. Key open questions include: how to add a forgetting mechanism for tasks spanning hours, whether the approach extends to multi-embodiment or mobile manipulation settings, and whether the keyframe-nomination capability can be integrated into unified VLA pretraining rather than requiring a separate high-level model.
