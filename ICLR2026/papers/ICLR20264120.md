## Problem

Existing joint speech-text models like Kimi-Audio operate at 12.5Hz audio frame rates, creating a significant frequency mismatch with text tokens (~3Hz) that dilutes semantic information and hinders the LLM's core capabilities, while also incurring high computational costs. This paper addresses both the resolution mismatch and the efficiency bottleneck by reducing the LLM's input/output frame rate to 5Hz without sacrificing speech generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264120/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

User speech is processed through a Whisper-Large-v3 encoder with an adapter (for continuous representations) and an S3Tokenizer producing 25Hz discrete semantic tokens; text queries are tokenized at ~3Hz. The LLM backbone operates at a unified 5Hz frame rate (grouping factor k=5), while the Speech Refined Head outputs 25Hz individual speech tokens that are converted to waveforms via a Flow Matching model and HiFi-GAN vocoder. System prompts control whether the model produces text-only, parallel speech-text, or Chain-of-Modality-enhanced outputs.

## Method

DRVOICE introduces Dual-Resolution Speech Representations (DRSR): a grouping mechanism (Eq. 3) compresses 25Hz speech tokens into 5Hz grouped representations for LLM input, while a dedicated Speech Refined Head (SRH) projects the LLM's final hidden state into group-sized embeddings, splits them into k sub-embeddings, and autoregressively generates individual 25Hz speech tokens conditioned on preceding speech tokens. The parallel architecture adds speech and text token embeddings at each timestep (Eq. 1), with a Text Head and SRH operating simultaneously on shared LLM hidden states. Training uses a CoM-Mixing strategy across seven interaction patterns (S2M, S2T, T2M, T2T, STC, SAC, SUC) and a Core-Cocktail two-stage procedure that merges the aggressively fine-tuned model with the base Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (α=0) before a low-learning-rate refinement stage, preserving the LLM's text capabilities while adapting to speech generation.

## Results

DRVOICE-7B achieves new state-of-the-art on all four major speech benchmarks: OpenAudioBench (72.04 overall, S2T), VoiceBench (80.17 overall, S2T), UltraEval-Audio (56.66 overall, S2S), and Big Bench Audio (74.0 overall, S2T&S2S). Notably, DRVOICE operates at a 5 Hz frame rate for both input and output (vs. 12.5–25 Hz for all baselines), reducing training GPU hours by nearly 50% while maintaining competitive speech quality (UTMOS 4.29, ASR-WER 8.36). The largest margin over the nearest competitor (Kimi-Audio) is on Big Bench Audio, where DRVOICE scores 74.0 vs. 55.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRVOICE-7B outperforms all six baselines on every benchmark. Against the strongest competitor Kimi-Audio (7B, parallel speech-text), DRVOICE leads by +2.96 on OpenAudioBench (72.04 vs. 69.08), +3.24 on VoiceBench (80.17 vs. 76.93), +13.87 on UltraEval-Audio (56.66 vs. 42.79), and +18.8 on Big Bench Audio (74.0 vs. 55.2). Against Qwen2.5-Omni (7B, text-driven), the margins are +5.70, +7.34, +6.20, and +20.1 respectively. The interleaved models (GLM-4-Voice, Baichuan-Omni-1.5, Step-Audio2-Mini) trail DRVOICE by 9–28 points across benchmarks, with the largest gaps on Big Bench Audio (e.g., 74.0 vs. 43.8 for GLM-4-Voice). DRVOICE achieves these gains while operating at a 5 Hz frame rate—roughly 2.5× lower than the 12.5 Hz used by Kimi-Audio and GLM-4-Voice—demonstrating that the dual-resolution grouping mechanism preserves performance at substantially reduced computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRVOICE extends the parallel speech-text modeling paradigm introduced by Moshi (Defossez et al., 2024), where speech and text embeddings are summed as a joint autoregressive input, but departs from it in two key ways: (1) a Dual-Resolution Speech Representation mechanism that compresses 25 Hz input tokens to 5 Hz via linear grouping while using a dedicated Speech Refined Head (SRH) to autoregressively regenerate individual 25 Hz output tokens, and (2) a Core-Cocktail two-stage training strategy that borrows the model-merging idea from LM-Cocktail (Xiao et al., 2024) to counteract catastrophic forgetting during aggressive multimodal fine-tuning. The Chain-of-Modality (CoM) training strategy is adapted from SpeechGPT (Zhang et al., 2023a) and extended into a multi-pattern data-mixing curriculum covering seven interaction modes. Compared to the closest parallel-model competitor Kimi-Audio (12.5 Hz), DRVOICE's core architectural departure is the asymmetric input/output resolution, which the authors argue better matches the ~3 Hz natural rate of text tokens and reduces LLM sequence length by 2.5×.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5 Hz operating point achieving SOTA on four major benchmarks (OpenAudioBench, VoiceBench, UltraEval-Audio, Big Bench Audio) while cutting training GPU-hours by ~50% is a practically significant efficiency gain for the speech-LLM community, suggesting that the field's default 12.5 Hz frame rate may be unnecessarily high. The Core-Cocktail training recipe (high-LR stage → merge with base LLM at α=0 → low-LR stage) offers a reusable solution to the well-known learning-rate dilemma in multimodal fine-tuning. Open questions remain: the model still lags Qwen2.5-Omni on speech-text alignment (ASR-WER 8.36 vs. 3.48), lacks full-duplex interaction, and is limited to speech (no music, environmental audio, or visual modality), all of which the authors flag as future work. Whether the dual-resolution insight generalizes to other tokenizers or higher-frequency regimes beyond 25 Hz is not yet explored.
