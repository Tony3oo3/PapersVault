## Problem

ML models deployed in real-world settings suffer from performance degradation as data distributions shift over time, yet prior work on temporal generalization either relied on future data during adaptation, used non-transparent hyperparameter selection, or was confined to small-scale models. This paper asks whether parameter interpolation or extrapolation from historical checkpoints alone—without any access to future data or validated assumptions about the data-generating process—can reliably produce models that generalize to future time points.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264395/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of model checkpoints {θ₁, …, θ_t} produced by sequential fine-tuning on temporally ordered data segments, and outputs estimated future parameters θ̃_{t+δ} for a specified future horizon δ. A critical constraint is that all hyperparameters (e.g., the downscaling factor α or extrapolation step size) are tuned using only the current validation set, simulating a realistic deployment where future data is unavailable. Evaluation spans tasks from 70M-parameter T5 (language modeling, news summarization) to 770M-parameter T5-large, as well as DistilBERT and DenseNet-121 on WILDS-Time benchmarks, with forward transfer measured over 12-month or multi-year horizons.

## Method

The paper benchmarks two families of approaches under a strict no-future-access protocol. For interpolation, it evaluates model merging (convex combinations of past checkpoints) and parameter downscaling (scaling the most recent checkpoint toward the origin by a scalar α ∈ [0,1], motivated by the observation that parameter norms grow during continual learning and correlate with overconfidence). For extrapolation, it applies a first-order Taylor approximation to the parameter trajectory, estimating the instantaneous rate of change from the most recent two checkpoints and projecting forward by a tunable step size α. A key design choice is sequential fine-tuning (continual learning), where each time step's model is initialized from the previous one, ensuring consecutive parameters remain in the same loss basin and making the parameter trajectory interpretable. The central empirical finding is that none of these methods consistently outperforms the simple baseline of deploying the most recent checkpoint; downscaling is the only approach that avoids systematic performance degradation across datasets.

## Results

The paper's central finding is that no parameter interpolation or extrapolation method consistently outperforms the simple baseline of deploying the most recent model checkpoint across all six temporal datasets. For language modeling with T5-small under continual learning, downscaling achieves the lowest average perplexity (32.5) compared to model averaging (34.0) and Taylor extrapolation (35.7). On yearly news summarization, Taylor expansion yields an average perplexity of 9.274±0.060, substantially worse than the recent model (7.582±0.019), while downscaling matches or slightly beats the recent model (7.562±0.006). The optimal extrapolation factor α for Taylor expansion is frequently less than 1 or even negative, indicating that forward extrapolation is often counterproductive.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks its interpolation and extrapolation methods against five baselines on the WILDS-Time datasets (Figure 3): two domain generalization methods (GroupDRO, IRM, DeepCoral) and two continual learning methods (EWC, A-GEM), alongside ERM. No method—including the paper's own downscaling and Taylor extrapolation—consistently outperforms the others across the four WILDS-Time datasets (Yearbook, FMoW, HuffPost, arXiv). The domain generalization baselines (GroupDRO, IRM, DeepCoral) and continual learning baselines (EWC, A-GEM) show mixed results, with different methods winning on different datasets and future horizons. The paper's key conclusion is that the simple 'recent model' baseline (equivalent to ERM on the latest data) is surprisingly competitive, and none of the more sophisticated DG or CL baselines provide a reliable advantage for forward temporal generalization. This reinforces the paper's broader argument that without strong assumptions about the data-generating process, no algorithmic approach can guarantee superior future performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two threads of prior work—parameter interpolation (model merging à la Ilharco et al. 2022, Yadav et al. 2024) and parameter extrapolation (Taylor-series approaches à la Nasery et al. 2021, Bai et al. 2023)—under a single temporal generalization framework, while imposing a stricter constraint than most prior evaluations: no access to future data for hyperparameter tuning or validation. It departs from Nylund et al. (2024), whose Time Vectors method requires future unlabeled data and a large fixed pre-trained model, and from Dziadzio et al. (2024), whose model-averaging evaluation used curated datasets without strong temporal shifts. The paper also positions itself against the broader domain generalization literature (GroupDRO, IRM, DeepCoral) and continual learning (EWC, A-GEM), using them as baselines rather than as the primary focus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that no interpolation or extrapolation method consistently beats the trivial 'use the latest checkpoint' baseline across six diverse temporal tasks—serves as an important reality check for a field that has made strong generalization claims. The practical takeaway that a single scalar downscaling of recent parameters is the only method that avoids performance degradation is both simple and actionable. The paper identifies the core open question as making explicit, validated assumptions about how the data-generating process evolves over time, rather than designing new parameter-space algorithms. Limitations include the reliance on sequential fine-tuning as a prerequisite (which may not hold in all deployment settings) and the fact that the no-free-lunch argument, while correct, leaves practitioners without a constructive path forward.
