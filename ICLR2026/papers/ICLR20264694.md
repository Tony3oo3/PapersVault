## Problem

Frequency-based neural operators (FNO, WNO) lose orthogonality and eigenfunction properties on irregular geometries, degrading accuracy. The recent Latent Mamba Operator (LaMO) handles irregular domains but lacks a frequency-domain implementation, causing spectral mixing and poor recovery of high-frequency and singular features. This paper addresses both gaps by integrating reproducing kernels (Takenaka-Malmquist systems) directly into state-space model transfer functions, yielding a neural operator whose output exactly matches Adaptive Fourier Decomposition (AFD) with provable convergence.

## Input / Output

The method takes a PDE parameter function a (encoding coefficients, initial and boundary conditions) defined on a spatial domain Ω ⊂ R^d, together with spatial mesh coordinates, and produces the corresponding PDE solution field u(x,t). A cross-attention lifting operator first compresses N_s physical tokens into M ≪ N_s latent tokens, so the heavy spectral and SSM computations operate on the small latent set rather than the full mesh. The architecture is mesh-agnostic, accepting point clouds, structured meshes, regular grids, and irregular domains interchangeably, and the final output is projected back to the physical space via a local decoder Q.

## Method

AMO's core mechanism replaces LaMO's kernel-integral SSM with a frequency-domain transfer function parameterized by a Takenaka-Malmquist (TM) system in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space. Each processing block contains (i) a TM layer where a small MLP predicts data-dependent poles {a_k} in the unit disk, from which orthonormal TM bases B_i are constructed via the Blaschke-product recursion, and (ii) a bidirectional SSM whose transfer function is set to the conjugate of B_i, so the SSM computes the AFD coefficient ⟨z_i, B_i⟩ as a zero-lag correlation in the frequency domain. Aggregation layers combine skip connections with Hadamard products of the extracted coefficients and TM bases, and the final output is a sum of AFD partial sums projected back to physical space. Because the output exactly equals the AFD expansion, AMO inherits AFD's convergence guarantees and error bounds, and the adaptive poles allow the operator to allocate spectral resolution where the solution varies most rapidly (e.g., near boundaries for Darcy flow, in the interior for reaction-diffusion problems). The overall complexity is O(N_s·D + N·M·log M), linear in the number of mesh points.

## Results

AMO achieves the lowest relative L² error across all six benchmark PDE datasets, with an average improvement of 28.42% over the second-best model (LaMO). The largest gains are on Airfoil (0.0020 vs. 0.0041, a 51.2% reduction), Darcy flow (0.0021 vs. 0.0039, 46.2%), and Navier-Stokes (0.0278 vs. 0.0460, 33.3%). On the European option pricing task, AMO reaches 0.0006 relative L² error with only 1.21M parameters and 20.8 s/epoch training time, outperforming LaMO (0.0008, 3.52M params) by 25% in error while using 66% fewer parameters. On the real-world latex glove DIC dataset, AMO's best result (2.32E-02 at 12 hidden layers) surpasses IFNO's best reported result (3.30E-02 at 24 layers).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMO outperforms all 13 baselines on every benchmark dataset. The closest competitor is LaMO (Tiwari et al., 2025), the prior SOTA Mamba-based operator, which AMO beats by 14–51% in relative L² error; the gap is largest on Airfoil (0.0020 vs. 0.0041) and Darcy (0.0021 vs. 0.0039), where LaMO's non-orthogonal kernels and low-pass filtering bias degrade high-frequency and singularity recovery. Frequency-based operators (FNO, U-FNO, F-FNO, WMT) suffer the most on irregular geometries and Navier-Stokes, with errors 2–10× higher than AMO, confirming that fixed Fourier/wavelet bases lose orthogonality on non-rectangular domains. Transformer-based solvers (Transolver, Transolver++, OFormer, LSM) are competitive on regular grids but still trail AMO by 10–30% on irregular geometries and by >3× on Navier-Stokes. ONO (Xiao et al., 2023b), which uses orthogonal attention, is outperformed by AMO on all six benchmarks while AMO also trains ~2.7× faster and uses ~3× less GPU memory by avoiding explicit orthogonalization. On the European option pricing task, AMO achieves 0.0006 relative L² with 1.21M parameters, beating LaMO (0.0008, 3.52M) and FNO (0.0016, 3.78M) in both accuracy and efficiency. On the real-world latex glove DIC dataset, AMO (2.32E-02 at 12 layers) surpasses IFNO (3.32E-02 at 12 layers; best reported 3.30E-02 at 24 layers) and FNO (4.66E-02 at 12 layers) across all tested depths.

## Relation to Prior Work

AMO directly extends LaMO (Tiwari et al., 2025) by replacing its kernel integral formulation with reproducing kernels in a Hardy-space RKHS, specifically constructing Takenaka-Malmquist orthonormal bases from data-dependent poles. The SSM blocks are parameterized via the state-free transfer function approach of Parnichkun et al. (2024), but with the transfer function set to the conjugate of the TM basis, which makes each block compute an AFD coefficient. This design is fully guided by the Adaptive Fourier Decomposition theory of Qian (2010; Qian et al., 2012), giving AMO a rigorous convergence guarantee that neither LaMO nor standard frequency-based operators (FNO, WNO) possess on irregular geometries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AMO provides the first neural operator with a provable AFD convergence guarantee, bridging the gap between the mathematical theory of adaptive signal decomposition and practical PDE solving on arbitrary meshes. The adaptive pole mechanism offers a degree of interpretability (poles cluster near boundaries for elliptic problems, in the interior for reaction-diffusion) that is absent in black-box SSM or attention-based operators. Open questions include whether the TM/Hardy-space framework extends naturally to higher-dimensional or non-simply-connected domains, whether the cross-attention lifting bottleneck limits scalability to very large N_s, and whether the convergence rates in Theorem D.6 are tight in practice for the weak-ℓ^p decay assumptions.
