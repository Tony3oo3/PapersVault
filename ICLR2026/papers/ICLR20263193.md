## Problem

Train-based speculative decoding methods rely on vanilla knowledge distillation over the full training dataset, treating all tokens as equally important. However, the acceptance rate is governed by the L1-norm between draft and target distributions, and not all tokens contribute equally to reducing this gap. The paper identifies that this heterogeneity is overlooked, leading to avoidable training overhead on low-value, already-saturated tokens.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen target model (LLaMA3-8B-Instruct) and a raw training dataset (ShareGPT) as input. A single offline forward pass of the target model computes per-token flatness scores (cosine similarity to the uniform distribution over the vocabulary), which are averaged to a sample-level score. The output is a distilled training subset (top-k% by flatness) and a trained single-layer LLaMA-style draft model, with the entire selection overhead being a one-off 2,242-second pass (~3.85% of full training time).

## Method

The core insight comes from a budget-limited KD analysis: modeling one training step as minimizing KL(p‖r) subject to KL(r‖q) ≤ θ, solved analytically for Gaussian distributions, reveals that the per-step L1-norm reduction ΔL1 increases monotonically with the target distribution's variance. This motivates 'flatness'—cosine similarity between a token's target distribution and the uniform distribution—as a practical, permutation-invariant proxy for token importance that depends only on the target model and is computable offline without a warmed-up draft. The SFDD pipeline aggregates token-level flatness to the sample level by averaging, sets a quantile-derived threshold, and retains only high-flatness samples for training. Plugged into EAGLE-2, SFDD achieves over 2× training speedup at 50% retention while keeping inference speedup within 4% of the full-dataset baseline, outperforming entropy, top-1 probability, margin, energy score, and perplexity as selection metrics.

## Results

At a 50% data retain ratio, SFDD achieves an average inference speedup of 2.41× and average acceptance length of 2.56 across five downstream tasks (GSM8K, Alpaca, MT-Bench, CNN/DM, NQ), staying within 4% of the full-dataset baseline (2.49× speedup). This represents a 2.02× training speedup (58,227s → 28,787s) while preserving nearly all inference performance. SFDD consistently outperforms all six alternative data-selection metrics, with the largest gap over Margin (2.41× vs. 2.15×) and the smallest gap over Top-1 Probability (2.41× vs. 2.23×).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFDD outperforms all six competing data-selection metrics on both average speedup and acceptance length across every downstream task. The closest competitor is Top-1 Probability (2.23× avg speedup), which still trails SFDD by 0.18×; Entropy, Energy Score, and PPL cluster tightly around 2.20×, while Margin lags furthest at 2.15×. Random filtering (2.20×) performs comparably to the heuristic metrics, confirming that informed selection is necessary. Notably, SFDD at 50% retention (2.41×) is only 0.08× below the unfiltered full-dataset baseline (2.49×), whereas the next-best metric (Top-1 Probability) is 0.26× below it. On individual tasks, SFDD's advantage is most pronounced on GSM8K (2.69× vs. 2.49× for Top-1) and Alpaca (2.66× vs. 2.44×), while the gap narrows on NQ (2.14× vs. 1.98×). The paper also shows that at 70% retention, SFDD nearly matches or even exceeds the full-dataset speedup on Alpaca (2.77× vs. 2.71×), suggesting filtering can remove noisy or redundant samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the EAGLE-2 framework for training draft models in speculative decoding and the theoretical link between acceptance rate and L1-norm distance established by Leviathan et al. (2023). It departs from prior work that focused on loss function design (KL-divergence vs. L1-norm objectives, as in DistillSpec) by shifting the optimization lever to the data side: rather than changing how the draft model is trained, it changes what it is trained on. The flatness metric draws on data importance literature (entropy, top-1 probability, margin, energy score, PPL) but is derived specifically from the acceptance-rate objective, making it the first data-selection criterion purpose-built for SD draft model training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2× training speedup at 50% data retention with less than 4% inference speedup loss is a practically significant efficiency gain for a task (draft model training) that is becoming increasingly compute-intensive as SD methods scale to multi-epoch SFT and RL-style training. The approach is orthogonal to loss-function improvements and can be stacked with them. Key limitations include evaluation on a single target model (LLaMA3-8B-Instruct), a single training dataset (ShareGPT), and a single SD framework (EAGLE-2), leaving open whether the flatness principle generalizes across architectures, model scales, and data distributions. The paper also notes that token-level filtering (the more natural granularity) is currently infeasible due to forward-pass integrity constraints, making sample-level aggregation a necessary but potentially lossy approximation.
