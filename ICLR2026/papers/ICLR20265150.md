## Problem

Conventional frame-wise quantization in neural audio codecs imposes a strong locality bias that limits the capture of high-level semantic and structural cues essential for stable autoregressive modeling in speech language models. Additionally, the non-uniform information density of speech signals is ignored by constant-bitrate codecs, causing redundant coding in low-complexity regions (e.g., silence) and reducing generation efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265150/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For zero-shot TTS, GogoSpeech takes input text and a short speech prompt (encoded into group-wise tokens by Gogo) and produces a target speech waveform at 24 kHz. The codec operates at a fixed token rate of 47 Hz (10 tokens per group of 20 frames at ~94 Hz frame rate), with the speech backbone comprising the first 3 tokens per group at ~14 Hz. The GRPO-trained token allocator adaptively reduces the average fine-token rate to ~36 Hz by assigning fewer tokens to acoustically simple groups, with skipped positions filled by masking tokens before flow-matching reconstruction.

## Method

Gogo partitions mel-spectrograms into non-overlapping groups of 20 frames, appends 10 learnable queries per group, and quantizes the query positions via FSQ (levels [8,8,8,5,5], codebook size 12,800) after a 12-layer Transformer encoder with asymmetric masking. Coarse-to-fine token ordering is enforced through nested dropout (randomly retaining the first n_k tokens) combined with a loss balancer that shifts weight from ASR loss to CFM loss as more tokens are retained, and a gradient reweighting scheme compensating for fewer updates on fine tokens. Reconstruction uses a 12-layer DiT flow-matching model conditioned on the quantized features, followed by a pretrained Vocos vocoder. GogoSpeech is a two-stage LLaMA-based SLM: Stage I autoregressively generates the 3-token backbone per group at ~14 Hz, and Stage II conditionally generates the remaining 7 fine tokens per group. A lightweight 2-layer Transformer token allocator, trained via a modified GRPO (exhaustive enumeration over all budget choices, no KL penalty, joint reward on token count and mel-spectrogram distance), predicts the fine-token budget for each group from its backbone, reducing average token rate from 47 Hz to 36 Hz with minimal quality loss.

## Results

On the LibriTTS test-clean set, Gogo achieves UT-MOS 4.19, DNS-MOS 3.99, STOI 0.92, PESQ-WB 2.59, and WER 6.35 at a token rate of 47 Hz, surpassing most 50 Hz codecs (e.g., MagiCodec, X-codec2, TAAE, DualCodec) on DNS-MOS and PESQ metrics. On the Seed-TTS test-en set for zero-shot TTS, GogoSpeech attains the best speaker similarity (SIM 0.667), best long-form stability (SIM† 0.725, WER† 1.788), best subjective similarity (SMOS 4.381), and best relative naturalness (CMOS +1.832). With the GRPO-trained token allocator, the average token rate drops from 47 Hz to 36 Hz with only marginal quality loss (SIM 0.662, WER 2.469) and a reduced RTF of 0.455.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For codec reconstruction (Table 1), Gogo at 47 Hz outperforms all 50 Hz baselines (MagiCodec, X-codec2, TAAE, DualCodec, Mimi) on DNS-MOS (3.99 vs. 3.96/3.90/3.89/3.80/3.62) and PESQ-WB (2.59 vs. 2.55/2.45/2.14/2.02/1.64), while matching or exceeding them on UT-MOS (4.19 vs. 4.21/4.17/4.27/4.05/3.16). Against high-rate codecs (DAC/EnCodec at 600 TPS), Gogo achieves comparable UT-MOS (4.19 vs. 3.78/3.13) at roughly 1/13 the token rate. For zero-shot TTS (Table 3), GogoSpeech wins on SIM (0.667 vs. 0.660 for FireRedTTS-1S, 0.654 for CosyVoice 2), long-form SIM† (0.725 vs. 0.716 for F5-TTS), long-form WER† (1.788 vs. 1.812 for F5-TTS), SMOS (4.381 vs. 4.331 for CosyVoice 2), and CMOS (+1.832 vs. +1.730 for F5-TTS). F5-TTS retains the edge on short-form WER (1.830 vs. 2.394) and RTF (0.184 vs. 0.535) due to its fully non-autoregressive design, but GogoSpeech with the token allocator narrows the RTF gap to 0.455 while maintaining superior quality. Among autoregressive systems, GogoSpeech with the allocator is second only to XTTS-v2 in RTF (0.455 vs. 0.208) yet substantially outperforms it on every quality metric (SIM 0.662 vs. 0.463, SMOS 4.253 vs. 2.426, CMOS +1.587 vs. −0.961).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant frame-wise quantization paradigm in neural audio codecs (EnCodec, DAC, SoundStream) by introducing group-wise quantization with learnable queries and FSQ, drawing on the query-based tokenization ideas from ALMTokenizer and TiTok. It extends the hierarchical semantic-then-acoustic modeling paradigm of AudioLM and Moshi by operating the 'semantic' stage at a dramatically lower token rate (~14 Hz backbone vs. 25–50 Hz in prior work), and by using conditional flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) as the generative decoder rather than a standard vocoder. The adaptive token allocator is a novel application of GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) to codec-level budget allocation, and the coarse-to-fine token ordering leverages nested dropout (Rippel et al., 2014) in a speech-specific context with a loss balancer that dynamically shifts emphasis between ASR and reconstruction objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The group-wise granularity-ordered design directly addresses a core tension in speech language models: producing tokens that are simultaneously autoregressive-friendly (low perplexity for high-level cues) and information-rich enough for perceptual quality. The practical impact is significant—the token allocator reduces average inference cost by ~23% (47→36 Hz) with negligible quality loss, and the two-stage generation improves long-form stability. However, the system is built on a 1B-parameter LLM, and scalability to larger models remains untested; the 47 Hz token rate is still higher than the most aggressive low-bitrate codecs (25 Hz), and placeholder tokens in the flow-matching decoder can introduce artifacts. Whether the group-wise quantization principle generalizes beyond speech (e.g., music, multi-channel audio) is an open question.
