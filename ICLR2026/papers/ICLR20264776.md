## Problem

Existing LLM watermarking methods face a fundamental conflict when applied to reasoning models: token-based approaches (e.g., KGW) apply pseudo-random logit biases that can corrupt the chain-of-thought reasoning flow, while semantic-aware methods improve quality but introduce significant latency or require auxiliary models. No prior framework specifically decouples the model's internal reasoning from its final answer to protect logical integrity while still embedding a robust, detectable watermark.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the LLM's internal thinking-phase token sequence along with the per-step probability distributions over the vocabulary, plus the standard generation context (prompt, previously generated tokens). It produces watermarked text only during the answering phase, leaving the thinking phase completely unmodified. Detection is stateless and requires no access to the PSV or original prompt—it uses the standard KGW z-test on green-list token frequency, making it drop-in compatible with existing detection pipelines.

## Method

ReasonMark decouples generation into an undisturbed Thinking Phase and a watermarked Answering Phase. During thinking, a Criticality Score (CS = GCC · log(1+CPS)) identifies top-K Critical Tokens by jointly measuring global causal influence (via JS-divergence-weighted probability propagation) and competitive persistence (via logit-margin-weighted top-k recurrence). These tokens' embeddings are subjected to PCA, and the first principal component becomes the Principal Semantic Vector (PSV), a continuous semantic compass for the answer phase. At each answering step, the standard green/red list partition is retained, but the logit bonus for each green-list token is modulated by its cosine similarity to the current PSV (δ_{i,w} = δ_0 + δ_λ · s_{w,i-1}), so semantically coherent tokens receive stronger watermarking. The PSV itself evolves via an exponential moving average whose rate is itself scaled by the generated token's semantic alignment, allowing smooth topical tracking while remaining anchored to the original reasoning trajectory.

## Results

On C4, ReasonMark achieves the lowest perplexity among all watermarking methods (10.31 for Qwen3-32B, 10.54 for DeepSeek-R1-32B), nearly matching the no-watermark baseline (10.55/10.82), while also attaining the highest detection AUC (99.31/99.52). On WMT16-DE-EN, it obtains the best BLEU scores (9.916/9.653), exceeding the no-watermark baseline (7.851/7.622). On mathematical reasoning, ReasonMark achieves the highest mACC on both AIME (69.86/71.34) and GSM8K (93.96/95.14), closely matching or slightly exceeding the no-watermark baselines, with detection AUC above 99.9% on AIME.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReasonMark outperforms all 11 baselines on text quality (PPL on C4) and task accuracy (mACC on AIME/GSM8K), with its PPL of 10.31 (Qwen3) being lower than even the no-watermark baseline of 10.55, while KGW degrades PPL to 12.15 and SWEET to 12.46. On detection AUC, ReasonMark leads on C4 (99.31 vs. EWD's 99.22), AIME (99.95 vs. EWD's 99.91), and GSM8K (95.94 vs. EWD's 95.82), though the margins over EWD are narrow. On WMT BLEU, ReasonMark (9.916) substantially exceeds the next-best MorphMark (9.752) and Unigram (8.441), and even surpasses the no-watermark baseline (7.851), whereas most token-based methods (KGW 7.351, SWEET 7.209) degrade translation quality. The main exception is WMT AUC on DeepSeek, where EWD (86.45) edges out ReasonMark (85.10), though ReasonMark still leads on Qwen3 (87.25 vs. EWD's 86.80). Semantic-based baselines (SemStamp, k-SemStamp, SimMark) cluster in the middle on PPL (11.18–11.42) and AUC (97.65–98.10 on C4), consistently trailing ReasonMark on both quality and detectability.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReasonMark builds directly on the KGW green-red list partitioning and z-test detection framework, but departs from it by replacing the fixed logit bonus with a semantically-adaptive, token-specific strength modulated by a Principal Semantic Vector (PSV). Conceptually, it bridges the gap between token-based watermarks (which disrupt reasoning coherence) and semantic-aware methods like SemStamp/SimMark (which require auxiliary models or incur high latency) by distilling the model's own chain-of-thought into a lightweight PCA-based vector that guides watermarking without additional inference passes. The two-phase decoupling—leaving the thinking phase entirely untouched and watermarking only the answer phase—is the paper's key architectural departure from prior work, which applies watermarking uniformly across all generated tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: reasoning LLMs (DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3, etc.) are increasingly used in high-stakes domains where provenance tracking is essential, yet standard watermarks corrupt the chain-of-thought and degrade answer accuracy. The PSV-guided approach is attractive because it adds only ~8% latency and requires no auxiliary model, making it deployable in production. Key limitations include dependence on the model exposing a distinct thinking phase (limiting applicability to black-box or non-reasoning models), sensitivity to very short or hallucinated reasoning traces, and the fact that the PCA-based PSV is a linear summary that may miss higher-order semantic structure. Open questions include whether learned (nonlinear) semantic vectors would further improve quality-detection trade-offs, and how the method behaves under multi-turn dialogue where the thinking phase is interleaved with user input.
