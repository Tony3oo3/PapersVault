## Problem

While LLMs achieve high accuracy in general machine translation, they default to literal translation in subtitle scenarios that demand liberal, expressive rendering of emotions and tone. Existing preference optimization methods (DPO, PPO) optimize the full response as a unit, which is inadequate for subtitle translation where each short line requires fine-grained, context-dependent alignment and outcome-level supervision dilutes the gradient signal across segments.

## Input / Output

The model receives a prompt containing a system instruction plus n source subtitle lines (n=35 in experiments) and produces a corresponding sequence of n target-language translations. Lines are generated sequentially, with each translation conditioned on the previously generated translations in the same prompt, so the model must maintain discourse coherence. The MuSC dataset (from Youku) provides ~1–2M aligned line pairs per direction across six language pairs (en→de, en→fr, en→zh, ko→zh, zh→en, zh→th), with average source lines of ~6–10 tokens, reflecting the short, conversational nature of subtitles.

## Method

ALPO is a two-phase training procedure: first SFT on 80% of the corpus, then segment-level preference optimization on the remaining 20%. In the sampling phase, for each line s_i the SFT model generates k=15 candidate translations conditioned on the prefix of previously chosen translations; a Qwen3-14B evaluator scores all candidates for vividness, and a top-3 candidate is selected as the next prefix. The alignment loss applies a DPO-style Bradley-Terry objective per segment, weighted by an adaptive factor w(s_i) = 1(s_i)·δ(s_i) that gates out low-diversity lines and up-weights lines with richer candidate sets. A dynamic β_i normalizes the reward gap per segment, and a scheduled prefix-mixing strategy (probability λ of using the chosen vs. a random candidate as context) mitigates exposure bias. This process-supervised, segment-granular design contrasts with outcome-supervised DPO/PPO, which the paper shows theoretically and empirically to suffer from gradient dilution on multi-segment responses.

## Results

ALPO (Qwen2.5-14B) achieves the highest vividness scores across all six translation directions, reaching 76.6 (en→zh), 74.8 (en→de), 78.8 (en→fr), 70.5 (ko→zh), 81.7 (zh→en), and 74.2 (zh→th). On en→zh, ALPO scores 90.6 accuracy and 84.3 naturalness, surpassing GPT-5 (92.4/87.0) on vividness by 5.5 points while remaining competitive on the other two dimensions. The 14B ALPO model outperforms all SOTA chat and reasoning LLMs (GPT-4o, Qwen-Max, DeepSeek-V3.1, DeepSeek-R1, GPT-5) on vividness in every direction, and also exceeds human translation in accuracy and naturalness. Ablation confirms the gating function contributes the largest gain (+6.4 vividness on en→zh), followed by the importance score (+4.2) and prefix mixing (+3.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALPO decisively outperforms all traditional MT systems (VideoDubber, NLLB-3.3B, MADLAD-10B, Google Translate) by 20–30+ points on vividness across all directions. Against SOTA chat LLMs (GPT-4o, Qwen-Max, DeepSeek-V3.1), ALPO wins on vividness by 13–17 points (e.g., 76.6 vs 59.8 for GPT-4o on en→zh) while matching or exceeding them on accuracy and naturalness. Versus reasoning LLMs (DeepSeek-R1, GPT-5), ALPO achieves the highest vividness in every direction (76.6 vs 70.8/71.1 on en→zh; 81.7 vs 73.5/75.2 on zh→en), though GPT-5 retains a small edge on accuracy (92.4 vs 90.6) and naturalness (87.0 vs 84.3) for en→zh. ALPO surpasses human gold references on all three dimensions for en→zh (90.6/84.3/76.6 vs 83.6/82.6/71.5), suggesting that preference-optimized LLMs can match or exceed human expressiveness when video context is unavailable. The gains over the SFT baseline are substantial: +4.2 accuracy, +2.3 naturalness, and +17.5 vividness on en→zh, confirming that ALPO's segment-level preference alignment is the primary driver of improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO/preference-optimization lineage (Rafailov et al., 2024; SimPO, KTO, IPO) from outcome-supervised, full-response alignment to a process-supervised, segment-level paradigm (ALPO) motivated by the structure of subtitle translation where each line's quality depends on local context. It builds on the LLM-as-a-Judge framework (Zheng et al., 2023a) by empirically validating a 14B model as a reliable translation evaluator, and departs from the closest prior subtitle-translation work (VideoDubber, Wu et al., 2023) by reframing the task as a preference-alignment problem targeting expressiveness rather than length-controlled accuracy. The empirical investigation into literal vs. liberal translation across domains and the observation that chat LLMs systematically favor literal translation while reasoning LLMs favor liberal translation are novel positioning contributions that inform the method design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: LLMs produce accurate but flat subtitle translations that fail to convey emotional tone, a critical requirement for entertainment localization. The released MuSC dataset (6 directions, ~100+ programs each) and the ALPO code provide a foundation for the community. A key open question is that LLM-based subtitle translation cannot access video/audio context that human translators use, so the paper itself identifies multimodal translation as the necessary next step. Additionally, the human evaluation (Table 4) shows ALPO still trails gold references in most pairwise comparisons, suggesting the current text-only paradigm has a ceiling that preference optimization alone cannot break.
