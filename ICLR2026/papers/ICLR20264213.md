## Problem

Existing diffusion-based protein design methods cannot guarantee strict adherence to hard structural constraints such as specific bond lengths, angles, chirality, or spatial inclusion/exclusion zones. Soft guidance approaches only bias samples probabilistically, while per-step projection of noisy intermediate states onto nonconvex constraint sets introduces statistical bias and disrupts the diffusion trajectory. This paper addresses the gap by proposing an inference-time constrained diffusion framework that achieves perfect constraint satisfaction without degrading structural diversity.

## Input / Output

The method takes noisy 3D backbone coordinates (in SE(3) space) from RFDiffusion's x₀-prediction parameterization, along with task-specific constraint specifications: either non-covalent bond targets (e.g., 2.9±0.2 Å bond lengths, 155±10° C=O···N angles, 120±10° Cα-N···O angles for PDZ-peptide contacts) or spatial region definitions (e.g., a 20Å×40Å×40Å box with a conical exclusion zone). It outputs a clean protein backbone that exactly satisfies all specified constraints while preserving local stereochemistry and secondary structure. The method is inference-time only, reusing pretrained RFDiffusion/RoseTTAFold weights without retraining, and operates on the x₀-prediction parameterization to leverage existing folding model architectures.

## Method

The core contribution is a stochastic proximal sampling scheme that replaces per-step projection of noisy states with a predict-prox-renoise cycle: (1) the denoiser predicts a clean structure x̂₀ᵗ from the noisy state xₜ, (2) a proximal operator corrects this prediction toward the feasible set using a Moreau envelope of the constraint indicator (g(x) = λₜ/2 · dist_C(x)²), and (3) the corrected clean state is renoised via the forward kernel to produce the next noisy sample. The proximal subproblem is decomposed via consensus ADMM into a local block F (stereochemical bond lengths/angles plus the data anchor term) and a global block G (long-range non-covalent bonds or spatial constraints), with a dual variable tracking the mismatch between the two copies. Theoretical analysis proves that the proximal step contracts constraint violation by (2λₜηₜ)^(-1/2) per step (Theorem 6.1) and that scheduling λₜ = cₜ/ηₜ with non-decreasing cₜ guarantees vanishing terminal violation (Theorem 6.2). In practice, a single ADMM sweep per diffusion step suffices, with warm-starting across steps, and the method achieves 100% constraint satisfaction on both PDZ motif scaffolding and vacancy-constrained pocket design tasks.

## Results

On the novel PDZ domain benchmark (31,000 samples), the proposed method achieves 100% constraint satisfaction and 21.0% usable percentage, while all three baselines (Standard RFDiffusion, Recentering, and CGD) produce zero samples satisfying the non-covalent bond constraints. On the molecule encapsulation task (4,000 samples), the method achieves 100% constraint satisfaction and 97.8% usable percentage, compared to 27.4% constraint satisfaction and 24.2% usable for the best baseline (Recenter+CGD). The method also maintains the lowest radius of gyration (12.4 Å on PDZ, 14.8 Å on encapsulation), indicating compact, realistic folds without the extended conformations seen in CGD outputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four baselines: Standard RFDiffusion, Recentering of Mass Guidance (Braun et al., 2024), Constraint-Guided Diffusion via Sequential Monte Carlo (Lee et al., 2025), and Projected Diffusion Models (Christopher et al., 2024). On the PDZ domain benchmark, all three RFDiffusion-based baselines achieve 0% constraint satisfaction across 31,000 samples each, producing zero usable structures, while the proposed method achieves 100% constraint satisfaction and 21.0% usable samples (up to 83% for well-posed ligands). On molecule encapsulation, CGD achieves 21.6% constraint satisfaction and Recenter+CGD reaches 27.4%, but both suffer from inflated radii of gyration (23.9–26.6 Å vs. 14.8 Å for the proposed method), indicating unfolded or distorted conformations. The Projected Diffusion Model (Christopher et al., 2024) fails to converge when enforcing both local and global constraints simultaneously, and even when only global constraints are enforced, it produces 0% usable structures with atoms concentrated on boundary edges. The proposed method dominates on every metric across both tasks, with the most dramatic gap being the transition from 0% to 100% constraint satisfaction on the PDZ benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on RFDiffusion as the underlying backbone generator and departs from the prior projected-diffusion framework (Christopher et al., 2024) by replacing per-step projections onto noisy intermediate states with a predict-prox-renoise cycle that applies feasibility corrections only to the predicted clean posterior. The ADMM decomposition separating local stereochemistry from global topology is a novel architectural choice not present in prior constrained-diffusion or guidance-based protein design methods (Ho & Salimans, 2022; Gruver et al., 2023; Lee et al., 2025). Theoretically, the work extends proximal optimization analysis to the stochastic diffusion setting, providing convergence-to-feasibility guarantees that prior projected-diffusion results (valid under convexity) do not cover for the nonconvex constraint sets typical in protein design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic 0%-to-100% constraint satisfaction gap over all baselines on the PDZ benchmark, combined with the demonstration that the natural per-step projection alternative actively fails (non-convergence or zero structure realism), provides a clear practical recipe for hard-constraint protein design that is complementary to existing guidance methods. The novel PDZ domain benchmark establishes the first systematic standard for evaluating constrained diffusion in modular domain engineering. Key limitations include the prox-regularity assumption (which may not hold for highly nonconvex protein constraints), evaluation on only two tasks with a single backbone model, and the computational overhead of ADMM iterations, leaving open whether the approach scales to more complex multi-objective design problems or generalizes beyond RFDiffusion.
