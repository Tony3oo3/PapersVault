## Problem

Batch Normalization is essential for stabilizing SNN training due to discrete spikes and surrogate gradients, but its fixed-momentum moving average cannot track the rapidly shifting activation distributions inherent to online RL. This imprecision causes suboptimal exploration, poor trajectory quality, and degraded policy updates. Unlike ANNs that can often omit BN entirely, SNNs critically depend on it, making accurate moving-statistics estimation a prerequisite for deploying energy-efficient SNN agents in RL.

## Input / Output

For continuous control, the SNN actor receives a state vector normalized to (−1, 1) via tanh and outputs a continuous action by linearly scaling the decoder membrane potential from (−1, 1) to the environment's action range. For discrete control, a 128-dimensional RAM observation is fed directly into the spiking Q-network, which outputs Q-values read from non-firing output neurons. In both settings, the SNN executes 5 simulation time steps per environment step before resetting all neuron states, and CaRe-BN layers are inserted between every pair of adjacent layers.

## Method

CaRe-BN introduces two complementary mechanisms for precise BN statistics under nonstationary RL dynamics. First, the confidence-adaptive update (Ca-BN) replaces the fixed-momentum EMA with a Kalman-inspired optimal linear combination: the weight on the new mini-batch statistic is inversely proportional to its variance (σ²/N for mean, 2σ⁴/(N−1) for variance), while the weight on the previous estimate is inversely proportional to a tracked squared-deviation confidence (D_i^μ, D_i^σ). This automatically accelerates adaptation during distributional shifts and suppresses small-batch noise during stable phases. Second, the re-calibration mechanism (Re-BN) periodically (every T_cal = 5000 steps) draws M = 100 batches from the replay buffer and recomputes BN statistics via the aggregated-batch formula, correcting accumulated drift at negligible overhead (M/T_cal ≈ 2% of training cost). At inference, CaRe-BN is identical to standard BN and fuses into synaptic weights with zero additional cost.

## Results

On five MuJoCo continuous-control benchmarks (IDP, Ant, HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d) using CLIF neurons and TD3, CaRe-BN achieves a 5.90% average performance gain (APG) over the ANN baseline, the highest among all tested SNN-RL and SNN-BN methods. The largest per-environment improvement is on Ant-v4 (5373 vs. 4770 for ANN, +12.6%). Across all spiking neuron models and RL algorithms, CaRe-BN improves SNN performance by up to 22.6% over vanilla SNNs (DDPG + CLIF). CaRe-BN also reduces final-policy variance to 17.71% (DDPG) and 21.24% (TD3), lower than ANN baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CaRe-BN outperforms all eight baselines on the APG metric. Against SNN-RL architecture baselines, it beats ILC-SAN (4.64% APG) by 1.26 points, MDC-SAN (0.37%) by 5.53 points, pop-SAN (−6.66%) by 12.56 points, and ANN-SNN conversion (−21.11%) by 27.01 points. Against SNN-specific BN variants, CaRe-BN surpasses TABN (3.25%) by 2.65 points, TEBN (0.69%) by 5.21 points, BNTT (−1.62%) by 7.52 points, and tdBN (−2.28%) by 8.18 points. The most notable per-environment gain is on Ant-v4, where CaRe-BN reaches 5373±159 versus 4770±1014 for ANN (+12.6%) and 4590±1006 for pop-SAN (+17.1%). CaRe-BN also achieves the lowest standard deviations across most environments (e.g., 159 on Ant vs. 1014 for ANN), indicating substantially more stable training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends standard Batch Normalization (Ioffe & Szegedy, 2015) into the SNN-RL setting, departing from prior SNN-specific BN variants (tdBN, BNTT, TEBN, TABN) that were designed under the static-distribution assumption of supervised learning. It also diverges from the ANN-RL convention of omitting BN entirely, arguing that SNNs' reliance on surrogate gradients makes normalization indispensable. The confidence-adaptive update mechanism is inspired by the Kalman filter's optimal linear estimation, repurposing it to dynamically weight mini-batch versus moving-average statistics under nonstationary RL dynamics, while the re-calibration step borrows the replay-buffer resampling idea from off-policy RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CaRe-BN provides a lightweight, drop-in normalization module that removes a key practical barrier to deploying SNN-based RL agents on neuromorphic hardware, where the 100× energy savings per inference (16.48 nJ vs. 1775.36 nJ) are the primary motivation. The finding that proper normalization—not architectural complexity—is the bottleneck for SNN-RL performance reframes where the field should invest effort. Open questions include whether the Gaussianity assumption (Assumption 1) holds for deeper or more complex SNNs, whether the approach extends to model-based or offline RL, and whether the 5.9% SNN-over-ANN advantage persists on harder, higher-dimensional control tasks beyond MuJoCo.
