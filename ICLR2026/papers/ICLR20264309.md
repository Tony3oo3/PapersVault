## Problem

Safe exploration in RL requires maintaining safety constraints at every timestep during learning, yet methods with provable safety guarantees (e.g., safe Bayesian optimization, MPC-based approaches) do not scale to high-dimensional continuous control, while scalable deep RL methods typically lack safety guarantees throughout training. Among the few scalable methods that do provide guarantees (e.g., SAILR), the theoretical analysis only bounds simple regret—allowing arbitrarily poor performance during exploration—as long as the final policy is near-optimal. SOOPER addresses both gaps by providing a scalable algorithm that guarantees constraint satisfaction at every episode and establishes a novel sublinear cumulative regret bound.

## Input / Output

The method consumes continuous state observations (or 64×64 grayscale image embeddings from a pretrained DrQ encoder for vision tasks), a pessimistic prior policy trained under worst-case dynamics in a confidence set F₀ (derived from offline data via MOPO or from a simulator with bounded sim-to-real gap), a probabilistic dynamics model (neural ensemble providing nominal predictions μₙ and epistemic uncertainty σₙ), and a scalar safety budget d. It outputs a stationary stochastic policy that satisfies the cost constraint J_c(π,f) ≤ d with probability 1−δ at every episode, along with a cumulative regret bound of O(Γ_{N log N}^{7/2} √N) that depends on the maximal information gain of the dynamics kernel.

## Method

SOOPER operates in two coupled modes: (i) safe online data collection via online cost tracking, where the agent executes its learned policy πₙ until the sum of realized accumulated cost and a pessimistic cost-value estimate Q_{c,n}^{π̂} (augmented with an uncertainty penalty λ_pessimism‖σₙ‖) would exceed the safety budget d, at which point it switches to the conservative prior π̂; and (ii) optimistic planning in a modified 'planning MDP' where trajectories terminate upon the prior being invoked, with the terminal reward set to the pessimistic value V_r^{π̂} of the prior policy. This termination design incentivizes the agent to discover policies that safely outperform the prior, while the pessimistic cost tracking (Theorem 1) guarantees constraint satisfaction throughout all episodes. Exploration and expansion of the implicit safe policy set are driven by a single unified intrinsic-reward objective (Eq. 9) that adds γ^t λ_explore‖σₙ‖ + √(γ^t) λ_expand‖σₙ‖ bonuses to the reward, enabling the agent to simultaneously explore unknown dynamics and expand the set of reachable safe policies without a separate reward-free phase. The practical implementation follows an MBPO-style model-based actor-critic architecture with a neural ensemble for dynamics, reward, and cost estimation, making it directly extensible to standard deep RL tooling.

## Results

Across five RWRL/SafetyGym/RaceCar tasks with dynamics mismatch, SOOPER is the only method that satisfies the safety constraint throughout learning in all tasks, while consistently matching or outperforming SAILR, CRPO, and Primal-Dual on cumulative reward among constraint-satisfying methods. On the offline-to-online PointGoal1 task, SOOPER outperforms all baselines while maintaining safety. On real hardware (RaceCar at 60 Hz), SOOPER achieves roughly 2× the reward of the simulator-trained prior policy while satisfying the constraint throughout training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOOPER is compared against three baselines: SAILR (advantage-based intervention for safe RL), CRPO (CMDP solver without safety guarantees during learning), and Primal-Dual (constrained optimization). On the dynamics-mismatch tasks (Figure 3), SOOPER is the only method that never violates the safety constraint across all episodes; SAILR, CRPO, and Primal-Dual all exhibit constraint violations during learning. Among methods that do satisfy constraints, SOOPER achieves the highest or tied-highest cumulative reward in every task. In the offline-to-online setting (Figure 5a), SOOPER outperforms all three baselines on both reward and constraint satisfaction. On real hardware (Figure 6), SOOPER improves reward by approximately 2× over the prior policy while remaining safe, whereas baselines either violate constraints or show smaller improvements. The key differentiator is that SOOPER's pessimistic prior invocation (Algorithm 1) provides a hard safety guarantee at every timestep, whereas SAILR's advantage-based intervention and the CMDP solvers lack such per-step guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOOPER extends the termination-based safety mechanism of SAILR (Wagener et al., 2021), which also switches to a backup policy when a safety criterion is violated, but departs from it in two key ways: it provides a cumulative regret bound (rather than simple regret) and relaxes the informal assumption that reset probability vanishes over time. The optimistic exploration component draws on UCRL-style planning (Auer & Ortner, 2006) and the intrinsic-reward framework of Sukhija et al. (2025), while the practical architecture follows MBPO (Janner et al., 2019). The central conceptual departure from prior safe-exploration work is the unification of exploration, exploitation, and safe-set expansion into a single tractable objective (Eq. 9), replacing the common 'expand-then-explore-exploit' two-phase strategy that restricts analysis to simple regret.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SOOPER addresses a critical gap in deployable RL: maintaining safety throughout online learning while still converging to near-optimal performance, with a cumulative regret guarantee that bounds performance at every episode rather than only at the end. The validation on real hardware (a 60 Hz race car) and vision-based control demonstrates practical relevance beyond simulation. Open questions highlighted by the authors include extending to high-probability constraints over specific states, the non-episodic (single-trajectory) setting, and scaling to more complex tasks with more powerful world models. The reliance on a well-calibrated probabilistic dynamics model and a valid pessimistic prior policy remains a practical bottleneck that limits immediate adoption in highly non-stationary or adversarial environments.
