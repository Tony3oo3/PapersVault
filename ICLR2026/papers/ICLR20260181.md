## Problem

Online RL for continuous control has been dominated by single-task or strictly offline regimes, reinforcing the view that RL from online interaction does not scale to hundreds of tasks simultaneously. No prior benchmark or method has demonstrated that a single agent can be trained via online interaction across hundreds of diverse tasks spanning multiple domains and embodiments, leaving open whether the foundation-model recipe (large-scale pretraining followed by light RL) can be applied to continuous control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260181/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent consumes a 128-dim low-dimensional state vector, a natural-language instruction (encoded to a 512-dim vector via frozen CLIP-ViT/B), and optionally a 224×224 RGB image (encoded to 768-dim via frozen DINOv2/B). Outputs are 16-dim continuous action vectors selected by trajectory optimization (planning) in the latent space of the world model, warm-started by a learned policy prior. All tasks share unified state and action dimensions via masking, and language instructions follow a fixed two-part format describing the embodiment and the task objective.

## Method

Newt extends TD-MPC2's self-predictive (decoder-free) world model to the massively multitask online setting by adding language and optional image conditioning, and by leveraging demonstrations in four complementary ways: (1) model-based pretraining of all components on demonstrations before any online interaction, (2) constrained planning that biases the planner toward the pretrained policy and anneals to zero, (3) 50/50 oversampling of demonstrations alongside online data in the replay buffer, and (4) a model-based behavior-cloning loss added to the maximum-entropy policy objective to regularize Q-value estimation. The world model is trained with joint-embedding prediction, cross-entropy reward regression (instead of MSE, to handle heterogeneous reward scales across tasks), and TD-learning with per-task discount factors. Actions are produced by planning in the latent space over a 3-step horizon, with the policy prior serving as both a warm-start and a distillation target for planned actions.

## Results

On MMBench (200 tasks, 10 domains, 100M total environment steps), Newt achieves a normalized score of 0.438 with state observations and language conditioning, outperforming PPO, FastTD3, and vanilla TD-MPC2. Language conditioning alone improves the score from 0.371 to 0.438. For few-shot transfer to 20 unseen tasks, the pretrained Newt agent reaches 0.868 at 100k steps versus 0.480 for a from-scratch baseline (zero-shot: 0.192 vs. 0.013). Adding 224×224 RGB observations yields a marginal overall gain (+0.004) but significant improvements in manipulation domains (RoboDesk +0.125, Meta-World +0.069) at the cost of a slight decrease in DMControl (−0.029).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Newt outperforms all four main RL baselines (PPO, FastTD3, TD-MPC2 without language/pretraining/BC, and single-task TD-MPC2) on the overall 200-task average, with the largest gaps in DMControl, DMControl Extended, ManiSkill3, and MiniArcade. Against the language-conditioned multitask BC baseline, Newt achieves higher asymptotic performance in most domains, though in MuJoCo, Box2D, and Atari the gap narrows substantially and Newt sometimes matches BC. The vanilla TD-MPC2 baseline (same parameter count, no language, no pretraining, no BC loss) is consistently below Newt, confirming that the algorithmic additions (language conditioning, model-based pretraining, demonstration oversampling, and BC loss) collectively contribute to the performance gain. The 200 single-task TD-MPC2 agents (5M steps each, 1B total) serve as the demonstration source and represent an upper bound on per-task performance that the single multitask agent approaches but does not fully match in all domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends TD-MPC2, a self-predictive (decoder-free) world model for model-based RL, from its prior scope of single-task online or limited multi-task offline settings to a massively multitask online regime spanning 200 tasks across 10 heterogeneous domains. Conceptually, it adapts the 'foundation model recipe'—large-scale pretraining followed by light RL—that has proven successful in game-playing and reasoning, to continuous control, where the community has largely relied on supervised learning over massive demonstration datasets (RT-2, Octo, Open X-Embodiment) or narrow single-task RL. The key algorithmic departures from TD-MPC2 include language conditioning via CLIP, optional visual inputs via DINOv2, model-based pretraining on demonstrations, a behavior-cloning loss in the policy objective, and cross-entropy value regression to handle heterogeneous reward scales across tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the prevailing assumption that online RL does not scale in continuous control by demonstrating that a single 20M-parameter agent can be trained simultaneously on 200 diverse tasks, outperforming PPO, FastTD3, and vanilla TD-MPC2 baselines. MMBench, with its 200 tasks, 10 domains, language instructions, demonstrations, and open-source infrastructure (200+ checkpoints, 4000+ demos, docker images), provides the first common testbed for massively multitask RL and is likely to become a standard benchmark. Key open questions remain: visual RL at this scale is computationally prohibitive, language understanding is a bottleneck for zero-shot generalization (unseen instructions can halve success rates), convergence rates vary dramatically across tasks, and the approach has not yet been validated on real-world robotics.
