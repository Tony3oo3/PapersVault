## Problem

Existing surgical triplet datasets like CholecT50 provide only frame-level class labels without bounding boxes, have ambiguous temporal boundaries, and originate from a single institution, restricting the task to weakly supervised classification. This prevents precise spatial localization of instrument-action-target interactions and limits generalizability across surgical settings. The paper addresses this by introducing ProstaTD, the first fully-supervised surgical triplet detection dataset with dense bounding boxes, clinically standardized temporal boundaries, and multi-institutional robot-assisted prostatectomy videos.

## Input / Output

The method takes individual surgical video frames at 640×640 resolution as input and outputs bounding boxes (center, width, height) for each visible instrument instance along with a structured triplet label from 89 categories (7 instruments × 10 actions × 10 targets). Annotations are released in both COCO and YOLO formats, with bounding boxes focused on instrument head and joint regions rather than full instrument extent. Each frame may contain up to 6 concurrent instrument instances, reflecting the higher instrument concurrency of prostatectomy compared to cholecystectomy.

## Method

TDnet employs a teacher-student architecture built on a shared YOLOv12 backbone with a main triplet detection head and three auxiliary classification heads for instrument, action, and target. A key design choice is a shared foreground set: positive bounding boxes selected by the triplet head via a score-IoU alignment criterion are reused to supervise all auxiliary heads, avoiding inconsistent sample assignment across branches. In the self-distillation stage, the frozen teacher produces softened logits for all four classification heads, and the student (with frozen backbone and detection head) optimizes only its classification heads using a fused BCE objective that blends teacher probabilities (weight 0.8) with ground-truth labels (weight 0.2). This instance-level distillation is the first to tie soft supervision to individual positive detections rather than global image labels, providing localized gradients that specifically mitigate the severe long-tail class imbalance in triplet annotations.

## Results

On ProstaTD (71,775 frames, 21 videos, 5-fold CV), the proposed TDnet achieves the best full-triplet detection performance with mAP_IVT of 36.1% @IoU 0.5 and 33.1% @IoU 0.5:0.95, surpassing the strongest baseline YOLOv12 (34.3% / 31.5%) by 1.8 and 1.6 percentage points respectively. TDnet also leads on the verb component (mAP_V = 61.7% @0.5) while maintaining competitive instrument detection (mAP_I = 89.9% @0.5) at 126.6 FPS. Weakly-supervised challenge methods (Tripnet-Det, RDV-Det) collapse to near-zero mAP_IVT (~0.1%), confirming that bounding box supervision is essential for reliable triplet localization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TDnet outperforms all 12 baselines on the primary mAP_IVT metric, beating the closest competitors YOLOv12 and YOLOv10 (both 34.3% @0.5) by 1.8 points and RT-DETR (33.0%) by 3.1 points. RT-DETR achieves the highest instrument-only score (mAP_I = 91.6% @0.5) thanks to its transformer-based design, but this advantage does not transfer to full triplet detection, where TDnet's multi-task auxiliary heads and instance-level self-distillation yield a 3.1-point gain. The two weakly-supervised CholecTriplet2022 challenge methods (Tripnet-Det, RDV-Det) produce near-zero mAP_IVT (~0.1%), demonstrating that class-label-only supervision is fundamentally insufficient for spatially grounded triplet detection. Surgical-specific models TAPIR (28.4%) and MCIT-IG (29.6%) underperform general-purpose detectors, likely because their architectures are optimized for sparse or semi-supervised settings rather than dense fully-supervised training. Classical two-stage and single-shot detectors (Faster R-CNN 25.9%, SSD 27.1%, Cascade R-CNN 21.6%) trail the YOLO family by 7–13 points on mAP_IVT, confirming that modern anchor-free real-time detectors are better suited to the crowded multi-instrument scenes in prostatectomy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the surgical triplet recognition line initiated by CholecT40/45/50 (Nwoye et al.) from frame-level classification to fully supervised detection with bounding boxes, and from single-institution cholecystectomy to multi-institutional robot-assisted prostatectomy. The CholecTriplet2022 challenge attempted triplet detection but relied on weak supervision (class labels only), yielding near-zero mAP; ProstaTD is the first dataset to provide dense, per-frame bounding box annotations with clinically defined temporal boundaries. Methodologically, the proposed TDnet repurposes the YOLOv12 backbone with multi-task auxiliary heads and instance-level self-distillation, while the evaluation protocol builds on the prior 'ivtmetrics' toolkit with COCO-style global ranking and 101-point interpolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProstaTD establishes the first fair benchmark for fully supervised surgical triplet detection, moving the field beyond the ambiguous, weakly-supervised settings of CholecT50 and enabling spatially grounded instrument-tissue interaction modeling essential for real-time surgical guidance. The abysmally low absolute performance (best mAP_IVT ≈ 36%) and the finding that task-specific surgical methods (TAPIR, MCIT-IG) underperform generic detectors like YOLOv12/RT-DETR suggest the detection formulation is substantially harder than prior classification work implied, leaving considerable room for future progress. Open questions include temporal modeling beyond frame-level detection, handling the pronounced long-tail distribution of rare triplets, and cross-domain generalization to other surgical procedures.
