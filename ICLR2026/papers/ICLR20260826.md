## Problem

Photorealistic view synthesis from a single photograph has been limited to either slow diffusion-based pipelines (minutes per view) that produce less sharp nearby views, or feedforward regression methods that sacrifice fidelity. No prior approach simultaneously delivers state-of-the-art perceptual quality, metric-scale 3D representations, and sub-second synthesis time on a single GPU for interactive AR/VR use cases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260826/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB image (resized to 1536×1536) and produces approximately 1.2 million 3D Gaussians arranged in a 2-layer 768×768 grid, each with 14 attributes (position, scale, orientation, color, opacity). The output is a persistent metric 3D representation—not a single rendered image—that can be re-rendered in real time (>100 FPS) from arbitrary nearby viewpoints, with absolute scale suitable for coupling to physical headsets. Spherical harmonics are deliberately omitted to keep the output size tractable.

## Method

SHARP uses a Depth Pro ViT backbone (two 326M-parameter ViTs) feeding into a DPT-based depth decoder that outputs a two-layer depth map (visible surfaces plus occluded/view-dependent regions), followed by a small U-Net depth adjustment module inspired by C-VAE information bottlenecks that resolves monocular depth ambiguity during training (replaced by identity at inference). A Gaussian initializer unprojects the adjusted depth into base Gaussians in normalized space, and a DPT-based Gaussian decoder predicts per-attribute deltas composed via attribute-specific activation functions (e.g., softplus for depth, sigmoid for color/opacity). Training uses a two-stage curriculum—100K steps on 700K synthetic scenes with perfect ground truth, then 60K steps of self-supervised fine-tuning on 2.65M real images where the model's own novel-view renderings serve as pseudo-ground-truth inputs—combined with a carefully tuned loss suite including perceptual loss with Gram-matrix terms, floater-suppression regularizers, and a computation-graph surgery mechanism to fit full-resolution perceptual losses within A100 memory.

## Results

SHARP achieves the best DISTS and LPIPS scores across all six evaluation datasets in the zero-shot regime. On ScanNet++, it reaches DISTS 0.071 and LPIPS 0.154, and on Middlebury DISTS 0.097 and LPIPS 0.358. Compared to the strongest prior method (Gen3C), SHARP reduces LPIPS by 25–34% and DISTS by 21–43% while synthesizing the 3D representation in under 1 second (vs. ~830 s for Gen3C), a three-order-of-magnitude speedup. The resulting Gaussian representation renders at >100 FPS in real time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHARP outperforms all six baselines on both DISTS and LPIPS across every dataset. Against the strongest prior method Gen3C (diffusion-based, ~830 s inference), SHARP reduces LPIPS by 25–34% and DISTS by 21–43% (e.g., ScanNet++: 0.154 vs 0.227 LPIPS; ETH3D: 0.554 vs 0.734 LPIPS) while being ~900× faster. Among feedforward baselines, TMPI is the closest competitor on DISTS for Middlebury (0.158 vs 0.097) and Booster (0.232 vs 0.119), but SHARP still leads by 38–49% on DISTS. LVSM is competitive on WildRGBD DISTS (0.095 vs 0.069) but trails on LPIPS (0.257 vs 0.190). The diffusion-based SVC and ViewCrafter are consistently the weakest on both metrics, with ViewCrafter showing the highest LPIPS on Middlebury (0.751) and Tanks and Temples (0.759). Flash3D, the only other 3D-Gaussian-based feedforward method, is outperformed by a wide margin on all datasets (e.g., ScanNet++ DISTS 0.374 vs 0.071).

## Relation to Prior Work

SHARP sits at the intersection of feedforward 3D Gaussian reconstruction (Flash3D, Splatter Image) and the Depth Pro metric depth backbone, repurposing both for single-image view synthesis. It departs from diffusion-based scene-level methods (Gen3C, ViewCrafter, SVC) by targeting a narrower but practically critical regime—nearby views at interactive rates—rather than attempting far-view synthesis. The depth adjustment module is a direct adaptation of the C-VAE information-bottleneck idea (Sohn et al., 2015) to resolve monocular depth ambiguity during training, a component absent from prior feedforward Gaussian splatting methods. The two-layer depth output and attribute-specific Gaussian composition scheme are novel architectural choices not present in Flash3D or TMPI.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three-orders-of-magnitude speedup over diffusion-based SOTA while simultaneously improving perceptual fidelity (25–34% LPIPS, 21–43% DISTS) makes real-time photo-to-3D conversion viable for AR/VR headsets and interactive photo browsing—a use case previously blocked by synthesis latency. The explicit limitation to nearby views (~0.5 m baseline) is a deliberate design trade-off; the authors identify combining diffusion priors for far views with feedforward Gaussian rendering for near views as the natural next step. The lightweight depth adjustment module (2M-parameter U-Net) offers a general recipe for handling depth ambiguity in any regression-based view synthesis pipeline, and the computation-graph surgery trick for perceptual losses is broadly applicable to memory-constrained training.
