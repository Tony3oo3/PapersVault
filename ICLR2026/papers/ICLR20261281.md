## Problem

While Mixture-of-Experts routing has been studied at the expert level (domain specialization, co-activation, weight correlations), the cross-layer mechanistic question remains open: how do components in different layers—token embeddings, attention outputs, and prior MoE outputs—collectively shape the routing decisions of downstream MoE layers? No prior work has decomposed router inputs into their constituent cross-layer contributions to quantify which components promote or inhibit specific experts at which depths.

## Input / Output

The method takes 32-token sequences from C4 (or OpenR1-Math-220k for generalizability checks) and the full weights of four MoE-based LLMs (OLMoE 7B, DeepSeek-V2-Lite 16B, Qwen3-30B-A3B, Mixtral-8x7B), using their routing weight matrices, attention head projections, and expert FFN weights to compute decomposed scores. Outputs are structured as cross-layer matrices: score variance maps (sending layer × receiving layer), APS/ANS matrices capturing promotion vs. inhibition direction, and per-expert variance trajectories across all subsequent layers. A causal metric (AARV) additionally quantifies how much zeroing a single component's scores reshuffles the top-K expert selection in target layers.

## Method

The core contribution is a recursive score decomposition: since the MoE layer input is a sum of the token embedding plus all prior attention and MoE outputs, and the assignment score is a dot product with the routing weight vector, the total score decomposes linearly into sub-scores assigned by each component. An approximation to RMS layer normalization (distributing the shared normalization factor uniformly across components) makes this decomposition tractable while preserving relative magnitudes. The decomposition is applied recursively—layer outputs split into attention heads, heads split into (head, query, key) tuples, and MoE outputs split into individual expert contributions—enabling analysis at multiple granularities. Influence is quantified via score variance (Proposition 1: higher variance means greater routing impact), direction via APS/ANS (Proposition 2: positive scores promote, negative inhibit), and causal effect via AARV (average absolute rank variation of top-K experts when a component's scores are zeroed). The framework is validated across four production MoE models and an IOI task, revealing cross-layer entanglement stripes, persistent expert influence, and the differential long-range vs. short-range effects of MoE versus attention components.

## Results

Across four MoE LLMs (OLMoE, DeepSeek-V2-Lite, Qwen3-30B-A3B, Mixtral-8x7B), the paper finds that MoE layer outputs consistently exhibit higher scoring variance than attention layer outputs when contributing to downstream routing, except in the bottom 3–4 receiving layers. A causal ablation on OLMoE's M1E9 expert yields Spearman rank correlations of 0.595 (M1→M5), 0.708 (M1→M10), and 0.580 (M1→M15) between an expert's scoring variance and the AARV (average absolute ranking variation of top-K experts) in the receiving layer, confirming that high-variance components materially shift top-K selections. Token-embedding scoring variance drops sharply after the first two MoE layers, while specific experts (e.g., M1E9 and M4E14 in OLMoE, M2E92 in Qwen) maintain persistent cross-layer influence up to the final layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a mechanistic-interpretability analysis paper rather than a method paper, so it does not benchmark against competing routing or interpretability baselines. Instead, it compares routing-contribution patterns across four production MoE architectures of varying scale (7B–47B total params, 16–48 layers, 8–128 routed experts). The key cross-model comparison reveals that the dominance of MoE outputs over attention outputs in shaping downstream routing is consistent across all four models, while the specific 'entanglement stripes' (e.g., M1 and M4 in OLMoE) vary in prominence: Qwen shows a multi-stripe pattern, DeepSeek and Mixtral show weaker or fewer stripes. The paper also contrasts its variance-based influence metric with the 'Super Expert' identification from Su et al. (2025), finding that not all previously identified Super Experts exhibit high scoring variance under the proposed decomposition (e.g., M2E54 in DeepSeek ranks #55 out of 64), suggesting that extreme output magnitude alone does not guarantee strong routing influence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transformer decomposition framework (Elhage et al., 2021; Geva et al., 2021) from analyzing attention/FFN outputs to recursively decomposing MoE router assignment scores into contributions from tokens, attention heads, and upstream MoE layer outputs. Prior MoE interpretability work (Muennighoff et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2024; Lo et al., 2025) focused on expert-level phenomena—domain specialization, co-activation patterns, and weight/gate-score correlations—while largely overlooking how components in other layers shape routing decisions. The IOI circuit methodology from Wang et al. (2023) is repurposed here to identify function heads and study their routing influence, bridging mechanistic circuit analysis with MoE-specific interpretability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic cross-layer decomposition of MoE routing, revealing that routing is not purely local but shaped by long-range entanglement (e.g., M1 and M4 in OLMoE disproportionately influencing layers 5–14). The findings have practical implications for expert prefetching, load-balancing heuristics, and post-NAS compression strategies. Limitations include the layer-normalization approximation (distributing a single RMS factor across components), analysis restricted to 32-token C4 sequences, and causal validation limited to a single model (OLMoE). Open questions include whether the entanglement patterns persist at longer context lengths, in multilingual settings, or in models with different MoE architectures (e.g., shared experts in DeepSeek), and whether the identified high-influence experts can be leveraged for targeted fine-tuning or pruning.
