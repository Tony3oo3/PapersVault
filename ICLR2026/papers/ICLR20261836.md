## Problem

Generative planners based on flow matching produce high-quality paths in a single or few ODE steps but offer no formal safety guarantees and can yield incomplete paths near constraints. Existing certification-based methods (e.g., SafeDiffuser) enforce CBF safety on intermediate latent states during generation, causing distributional drift and local traps where waypoints stall near barrier boundaries. The paper addresses this semantic misalignment between what needs certifying (the executed path) and where interventions are applied (intermediate latents that are never executed).

## Input / Output

The method takes task-specific conditioning (start/goal observations for maze navigation, current state plus task score for locomotion, initial joint configuration plus block states for manipulation) along with CBF parameters (ε, ρ, δ) and a scaling constant α, and produces a safe waypoint sequence τ₁ᶜ ∈ ℝ^(d×(H+1)) where H is the planning horizon. Safety is enforced waypoint-wise: each waypoint must satisfy b(τ) ≥ δ (a robust margin beyond the nominal safe set). The two-phase structure means the prediction phase initializes from pure Gaussian noise while the correction phase starts from the predicted path, so the total sampling horizon T = Tᵖ + Tᶜ can be split to trade off prediction quality against the number of expensive CBF-QP evaluations.

## Method

SafeFlowMatcher introduces a two-phase prediction-correction (PC) integrator that decouples path generation from safety certification. In the prediction phase, the learned FM vector field is integrated once (or a few steps) from pure noise to obtain a candidate path without any safety intervention. In the correction phase, the path is refined using vanishing time-scaled flow dynamics (VTFD) with factor α(1−t) that contracts the prediction error toward the target, combined with a per-waypoint CBF-based quadratic program that minimally perturbs the vector field to enforce finite-time convergence to a robust safe set C_δ. A monotonically decreasing relaxation weight w_t^k (vanishing after time t_w) prevents QP infeasibility in early correction steps. The paper proves a barrier certificate establishing forward invariance of C_δ and a finite convergence time bound T ≤ t_w + (δ − b(τ_{t_w}))^(1−ρ)/(ε(1−ρ)), and shows empirically that enforcing safety only on the executed path avoids the distributional drift and local traps that plague methods intervening on intermediate latents.

## Results

On Maze2D (maze-large-v1), SafeFlowMatcher achieves the highest score of 1.632±0.003 while maintaining certified safety (BS1=BS2=0.010≥0) and a 0% local trap rate, outperforming all safety-aware baselines that suffer 12–100% trap rates. With the closed-form CBF solver and T^c=4, SafeFlowMatcher generates a complete safe path in 0.023 s—50× faster than RES-SafeDiffuser (1.208 s)—while the QP-based version completes in 0.157 s versus 9.998 s for SafeDiffuser. On high-dimensional tasks, SafeFlowMatcher attains the best score on Walker2D (0.331 vs. 0.283 SafeDiffuser, 0.264 SafeFM) and Block Stacking (0.76 vs. 0.72/0.73), while maintaining BS≥0 across all locomotion and manipulation environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SafeFlowMatcher dominates all safety-aware CBF-based baselines (SafeDiffuser, SafeDDIM, SafeFM variants) on Maze2D: it achieves a score of 1.632 with 0% trap rate, whereas the best CBF baseline (TVS-SafeDDIM η=1.0) reaches only 1.549 with 65% trap rate, and ROS-SafeDiffuser/ROS-SafeDDIM/ROS-SafeFM all hit 100% trap rate. Against guidance-based safety methods (Truncation, CG, CG-e), SafeFlowMatcher is dramatically superior—CG scores 0.505 with 100% traps versus SafeFlowMatcher's 1.632 with 0% traps. Among safety-disabled baselines, SafeFlowMatcher matches the score of FlowMatcher (1.632) while adding certified safety, and outperforms Diffuser (1.572) and DDIM (1.565) on score while also being safer. On high-dimensional tasks, SafeFlowMatcher wins on Walker2D (0.331 vs. 0.283 SafeDiffuser, 0.264 SafeFM) and Block Stacking (0.76 vs. 0.72/0.73), though on Hopper SafeFM (0.675) edges out SafeDiffuser (0.435) and the paper reports SafeFlowMatcher as best overall. In terms of efficiency, SafeFlowMatcher with T^c=4 and closed-form CBF is 50× faster than SafeDiffuser (0.023 s vs. 1.208 s), and even the QP-based version (0.157 s) is ~64× faster than SafeDiffuser's QP solver (9.998 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) as the generative backbone and finite-time convergence CBFs (Ames et al., 2019) as the safety certification mechanism, combining them in a way that departs fundamentally from SafeDiffuser (Xiao et al., 2025). Where SafeDiffuser enforces CBF constraints at every intermediate sampling step—causing distributional drift and local traps—SafeFlowMatcher introduces a prediction-correction integrator that decouples generation from certification, applying safety only to the final executed path. The vanishing time-scaled flow dynamics (VTFD) in the correction phase is a novel mechanism with formal error-convergence guarantees (Lemmas 2–3), and the barrier certificate (Theorem 1) provides a finite-time flow invariance proof specific to the flow-matching setting, going beyond the standard CBF-QP formulation used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SafeFlowMatcher addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying generative planners: existing certification-based methods (SafeDiffuser, SafeFM) suffer from severe local traps (65–100% trap rates) and high computational cost, making them unsuitable for real-time robotic control. The 50× speedup over SafeDiffuser (0.023s vs. 1.208s) combined with 0% trap rate and a formal finite-time convergence guarantee makes certified generative planning viable for time-critical applications. Open questions include whether the symmetric zero-mean prediction error assumption holds in higher-dimensional tasks, whether the framework extends to learned/perception-based CBFs (the authors note compatibility but do not test it), and how the relaxation schedule generalizes to highly non-convex safe sets beyond the maze environment.
