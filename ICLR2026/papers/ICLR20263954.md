## Problem

RL-based video understanding models using the Reason-Then-Respond paradigm have been developed for either QA or captioning in isolation, but naively combining their reward signals during training causes mutual performance degradation. The authors attribute this to a fundamental paradigm conflict: QA is a convergent, low-entropy task requiring a unique answer, while captioning is a divergent, high-entropy task demanding comprehensive descriptions, and these opposing objectives interfere during RL optimization.

## Input / Output

The model consumes video clips (16 frames at 196×28×28 resolution during training; up to 128 frames at 1 fps for QA inference) paired with a text prompt, and produces a structured output consisting of a reasoning chain enclosed in think tags followed by a final answer in answer tags. For QA tasks the answer is a short text response (e.g., a multiple-choice letter); for captioning it is a multi-sentence descriptive paragraph. The two proxy tasks modify the input: DarkEventInfer replaces event segments with black screens, and MixVidQA interleaves frames from two distinct clips at 1.5–2 s intervals.

## Method

VidBridge-R1 introduces two intermediate proxy tasks to bridge the convergent/divergent gap: DarkEventInfer (inspired by fill-in-the-middle) masks one event segment per video with a black screen and requires the model to infer the obscured content from surrounding context, while MixVidQA (inspired by style mixing) interleaves two Kinetics clips and poses questions targeting only one, forcing selective attention. Training applies GRPO directly to Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct without an SFT cold-start stage, using task-specific reward functions (LLM-judged three-tier scoring for DarkEventInfer, binary correctness for MixVidQA/VideoQA, AutoDQ recall+precision plus a keyword reward for captioning) gated by an implicit format reward that zeros all task rewards if the output structure is invalid. KL divergence regularization is omitted (λ=0) to allow faster reasoning emergence, and a pre-filtering step discards samples where all sampled responses receive identical rewards to maintain non-zero advantage signals.

## Results

VidBridge-R1 achieves 64.3 overall on Video-MME, 59.3 on LongVideoBench, and 61.9 on MVBench for general video understanding, outperforming the strongest baseline (VideoRFT) by 2.1, 1.9, and 1.6 points respectively. On video captioning, it reaches 35.2 F1 on DREAM-1K and 12.5 accuracy on VidCapBench, surpassing all baselines. On reasoning tasks, VidBridge-R1 scores 81.6 on NExT-QA, 97.1 on IntentQA, 70.7 on Causal-VidQA, and 40.0 on Video-Holmes, exceeding the best baseline by an average of 1.9% across five conventional QA benchmarks, and achieves 117.0 and 49.0 on the held-out DarkEventInfer-Test and MixVidQA-Test sets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VidBridge-R1 outperforms all four baselines across every evaluated benchmark. Against the strongest competitor VideoRFT, it gains +2.1 on Video-MME (64.3 vs. 62.2), +1.9 on LongVideoBench (59.3 vs. 57.4), +1.6 on MVBench (61.9 vs. 60.3), +3.7 F1 on DREAM-1K (35.2 vs. 31.5), and +0.4 accuracy on VidCapBench (12.5 vs. 12.1). On reasoning tasks, VidBridge-R1 leads VideoRFT by +2.3 on MMVU (54.7 vs. 52.4), +1.1 on NExT-QA (81.6 vs. 80.5), +2.2 on IntentQA (97.1 vs. 94.9), +1.6 on Causal-VidQA (70.7 vs. 69.1), and +2.0 on Video-Holmes (40.0 vs. 38.0). The gains are most dramatic on the proxy-task test sets: DarkEventInfer-Test (117.0 vs. 77.0 for VideoRFT) and MixVidQA-Test (49.0 vs. 23.0), confirming that the intermediate proxy tasks transfer to unseen data. Notably, the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B without reasoning actually scores higher on Video-MME (59.4) than with forced reasoning (53.4), highlighting that naive reasoning elicitation degrades performance on simpler tasks, whereas VidBridge-R1's RL training with proxy tasks resolves this trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Reason-Then-Respond paradigm (from OpenAI o1/o3 and DeepSeek-R1) to video understanding via GRPO-based RL, building directly on the GRPO formulation from DeepSeekMath. It departs from contemporaneous video RL models (Video-R1, VideoChat-R1, VideoRFT, VideoCap-R1) that each specialize in either QA or captioning, by identifying a fundamental paradigm conflict between convergent (QA) and divergent (captioning) optimization objectives under RL. The proposed intermediate proxy tasks—DarkEventInfer (inspired by fill-in-the-middle in code generation) and MixVidQA (inspired by style mixing in image generation)—are a novel training-strategy contribution rather than an architectural one, applied on top of the standard Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first video reasoning model to simultaneously handle QA and captioning under the Reason-Then-Respond paradigm, addressing a practical limitation where prior RL-based video models sacrifice versatility for task-specific gains. The entropy analysis providing a mechanistic explanation for the QA-captioning conflict, and the finding that SFT cold-start actively impairs subsequent RL performance (contradicting common practice), offer useful guidance for the broader multimodal RL community. Open questions include whether the proxy-task bridging strategy generalizes to other conflicting task pairs (e.g., detection + captioning), whether it scales to larger models, and how to address the hallucination issues the authors acknowledge in their future work.
