## Problem

Prior Referring Video Object Segmentation methods rely on a cascaded 'locate-then-segment' pipeline that collapses rich linguistic semantics into coarse geometric prompts (points, boxes), creating an information bottleneck that limits holistic scene understanding and temporal consistency. While pretrained text-to-video (T2V) models possess the fine-grained pixel control and spatio-temporal reasoning needed to overcome this, existing attempts either use them as frozen feature extractors for a separate decoder or ignore text conditioning entirely, failing to exploit the full generative dynamics for a language-guided, video-to-mask transformation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262230/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video clip (encoded to a latent via a frozen VAE encoder) and a natural language query (encoded by a frozen text encoder) to produce a per-frame binary segmentation mask sequence. Unconventionally, the video latent itself serves as the ODE starting point (z₀) rather than Gaussian noise, and the text query acts as a disambiguating conditional force throughout the trajectory. The VAE decoder is separately fine-tuned on MeViS to specialize in reconstructing binary masks from the latent space, while the encoder remains frozen.

## Method

FlowRVS reformulates RVOS as a text-conditioned continuous flow: learning a velocity field v(z_t, c, t) via Flow Matching that deterministically deforms the video latent z₀ into the target mask latent z₁ along an ODE trajectory, fine-tuning the 1.3B-parameter Wan 2.1 DiT block. Three synergistic adaptations address the asymmetric, convergent nature of video-to-mask flow: (1) Boundary-Biased Sampling oversamples t=0 with probability p=0.5 to concentrate gradient updates on the critical initial text-guided velocity; (2) Start-Point Augmentation samples z₀ from the VAE posterior N(μ_V, σ_V²) rather than using the deterministic mean, regularizing the velocity field near the data manifold; (3) Direct Video Injection concatenates the original video latent z₀ with the current state z_t at every ODE step, providing a persistent source anchor with negligible overhead. The VAE decoder is additionally fine-tuned with a combined Focal + Dice loss to bridge the domain gap between continuous video latents and binary masks.

## Results

FlowRVS achieves state-of-the-art J&F of 51.1 on MeViS (+1.6 over the previous best SAMWISE at 49.5), 69.6 on Ref-YouTube-VOS (+0.3 over ReferDINO at 69.3), and 73.3 on Ref-DAVIS17 in a zero-shot setting (+2.7 over SAMWISE at 70.6). On MeViS, the J and F scores are 47.6 and 54.6 respectively; on Ref-DAVIS17, they reach 68.4 and 78.2. The zero-shot Ref-DAVIS17 result is particularly notable as the model was trained only on Ref-YouTube-VOS without any DAVIS fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowRVS outperforms all compared baselines across all three benchmarks. On MeViS, it surpasses the strongest 'locate-then-segment' method SAMWISE (49.5) by 1.6 J&F and ReferDINO (49.3) by 1.8 J&F, with the largest gap over VISA (43.5) at 7.6 points. On Ref-YouTube-VOS, FlowRVS edges out ReferDINO (69.3) by 0.3 and SAMWISE (69.2) by 0.4 J&F, while maintaining a clear lead over earlier methods like DsHmp (67.1) and SOC (66.0). On Ref-DAVIS17, the zero-shot result of 73.3 J&F exceeds SAMWISE (70.6) by 2.7 and VISA (69.4) by 3.9, despite no DAVIS-specific training. The advantage is most pronounced on MeViS, where the motion-centric queries expose the temporal-consistency limitations of cascaded pipelines, and on Ref-DAVIS17, where the flow-based paradigm's generalization to unseen datasets is demonstrated.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowRVS departs from the dominant 'locate-then-segment' paradigm (ReferFormer, LISA, VISA, ReferDINO, SAMWISE) that collapses rich semantics into intermediate geometric or feature prompts, and from prior T2V-based approaches (VD-IT, HCD) that use video diffusion models merely as frozen feature extractors feeding a separate decoder. Conceptually, it extends the flow-matching framework (Lipman et al., 2022) and the visual-to-visual generative repurposing idea from DepthFM (Gui et al., 2025) by introducing a text-conditioned, convergent video-to-mask ODE, inverting the standard divergent noise-to-video direction. The backbone is the Wan 2.1 1.3B DiT, fine-tuned end-to-end rather than used as a static encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that modeling video understanding as a conditional continuous deformation can outperform cascaded pipelines, and the start-point-focused adaptations (especially Boundary-Biased Sampling, which alone yields +10 J&F) provide a practical blueprint for adapting generative foundation models to discriminative tasks. The strong zero-shot transfer to DAVIS17 (73.3 J&F) suggests the learned flow captures more generalizable spatio-temporal correspondences than task-specific decoders. Limitations remain for complex relational phrases and counting/ordering logic, and the paradigm's applicability to other video understanding tasks (beyond segmentation) is left as future work.
