## Problem

Existing TKG representation learning approaches model entity- or relation-level correlations, but TKG datasets are constructed from events that inherently contain heterogeneous causalities—static, dynamic, non-causality, and spurious causality—making entity/relation-level modeling inadequate for event prediction. No prior work provides a theoretical framework or practical mechanism to disentangle these four causalities at the event level, and existing TKGs lack explicit supervision signals to distinguish them.

## Input / Output

HEDRA consumes timestamped TKG quadruples (s, r, o, t) as a historical event sequence, updating entity and relation representations at each timestamp via a relation-aware GCN before constructing event representations by jointly encoding subject, relation, and object through an MLP. The output is a relation probability distribution over all candidate relations for a given (s, o) pair, decoded via ConvTransE, alongside intermediate event representations decomposed into orthogonal static and dynamic components that are used for multi-view message passing across timestamps.

## Method

HEDRA is grounded in a TKG structural causal model (SCM) that formally defines four event-level causalities and identifies backdoor paths, with do-calculus justifying the progressive disentanglement strategy. A Counterfactual Detector Module disentangles non-causality by fusing attention-based event importance with KL-divergence-based distributional discrepancy over a k-NN candidate graph, trained with a contrastive loss that pulls low-non-causality event pairs closer and pushes high-non-causality pairs apart. An Instrumental Variable-guided module then separates spurious from genuine causality by scoring edges with an IV encoder, partitioning them via a quantile threshold into genuine and spurious subgraphs, and enforcing robustness through multi-view (genuine/spurious/all) heterogeneous convolution with an alignment-separation loss. Finally, an Evolutionary Orthogonal Module projects each event representation into static and raw dynamic components, orthogonalizes the dynamic component against the static one, and trains with an evolutionary loss that enforces temporal dependence for dynamic components (via GRU) and temporal independence for static components (via group mean), before fusing static-view and dynamic-view message passing to update entity and relation representations for prediction.

## Results

HEDRA achieves state-of-the-art performance across all five TKG datasets, with average improvements of 5.70%, 7.51%, 7.21%, and 2.30% over the runner-up (DECRL) in MRR, Hits@1, Hits@3, and Hits@10 respectively. On ICEWS14, HEDRA reaches 47.86 MRR and 35.28 Hits@1, surpassing the previous best (DECRL at 42.90 MRR) by 11.56% in MRR. On the large-scale GDELT dataset (1.7M training events), HEDRA achieves 24.64 MRR, outperforming DECRL (22.74) by 8.36%, while several baselines (RE-NET, DACHA, DHyper) fail to complete training due to timeout or out-of-memory errors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HEDRA outperforms all 11 baselines on MRR, Hits@1, and Hits@3 across all five datasets. The closest competitor is DECRL (NeurIPS 2024), which HEDRA surpasses by 4.96 MRR points on ICEWS14 (47.86 vs. 42.90) and 3.41 points on ICEWS18 (46.77 vs. 43.36). On GDELT, HEDRA leads DECRL by 1.90 MRR points (24.64 vs. 22.74), while RE-NET and DACHA time out and DHyper runs out of memory. On the smaller WIKI and YAGO datasets, gains are more modest (0.14% and 0.60% MRR improvement over TiRGN and DECRL respectively), reflecting near-saturation performance. The one exception is ICEWS18 Hits@10, where RE-NET (75.66) marginally edges out HEDRA (75.64), attributed to RE-NET's global graph mechanism retaining more candidates in the top-10 range, while HEDRA's causality-focused design prioritizes top-rank precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

HEDRA sits at the intersection of TKG representation learning and graph causal learning. On the TKG side, it departs from the dominant paradigm of modeling entity- or relation-level correlations (RE-GCN, TiRGN, DHyper, DECRL) by shifting the unit of analysis to the event (quadruple) and arguing that heterogeneous causalities among events are the relevant signal for prediction. On the causal-learning side, it adapts the Instrumental Variable framework from Gao et al. (AAAI 2023) and the dynamic-graph causality ideas of Zhao & Zhang (ICLR 2024) to a setting where no explicit causality labels exist, wrapping them in a novel event-level structural causal model with backdoor adjustment. The counterfactual detector and evolutionary orthogonal module are new architectural components not present in prior TKG or graph-causal work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal SCM for event-level causality in TKGs, giving the community a principled vocabulary (non-causality, spurious, static, dynamic) and a progressive disentanglement recipe that could guide future TKG reasoning beyond correlation-based methods. However, practical adoption is tempered by roughly 2× the training cost of the prior SOTA (DECRL), fixed quantile and loss-weight choices that limit adaptability, and modest gains on entity-centric datasets (WIKI, YAGO) where the event-level framing offers less advantage. Open questions include whether the IV-guided module can be replaced by a learned calibration, how the approach scales to GDELT-scale data, and whether LLM-derived semantic priors can complement the explicit causal disentanglement for long-tail relations.
