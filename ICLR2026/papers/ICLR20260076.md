## Problem

Pan-cancer screening in large CT volumes is hindered by extreme foreground-background imbalance (lesions occupy only 0.085% of volume area), making it difficult for models to localize diverse tiny lesions across organs. Existing sliding-window methods process all sub-volumes indiscriminately, wasting computation on healthy regions and inflating false positives, which impedes real-world clinical deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260076/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a full 3D CT volume (chest or abdomen) as input, which is pre-processed with HU clipping (chest: [-900, 650], abdomen: [-175, 250]) and normalized to [0,1], then divided into overlapping sub-volumes of size 96×96×64 via sliding window. The Glance model outputs a binary selection (with/without lesions) per sub-volume, and the Focus model produces pixel-level segmentation masks only on selected sub-volumes. The final output is a lesion segmentation mask and a per-scan detection result, with 83.3% of sub-volumes discarded at inference, reducing computation by 5.7×.

## Method

GF-Screen couples a lightweight 3D ResNet-18 Glance model (coarse sub-volume classification) with a SwinUNETR Focus model (pixel-level segmentation), trained jointly. The key innovation is training the Glance model via reinforcement learning where the Focus model's segmentation results serve as rewards: a binary detection reward (1 if predicted segmentation overlaps ground-truth mask, 0 otherwise) is assigned per sub-volume. Because the selection action is non-differentiable, the authors introduce Group Relative Learning (GRL), which normalizes rewards within each group of N=16 sub-volumes to compute relative advantages, then optimizes the Glance policy using a PPO-style clipped objective with KL regularization against a frozen reference model and a small auxiliary classification loss. This group-relative mechanism prioritizes high-advantage sub-volumes (optimal diagnostic views with complete lesion information) and discards low-advantage ones, simultaneously improving segmentation accuracy, reducing false positives by 23.1%, and cutting inference cost by 5.7×.

## Results

On 16 internal datasets across 9 lesion types, GF-Screen achieves 60.8% average DSC for segmentation and 95.9% average F1-Score for detection, surpassing the second-best method VoCo by 4.7% DSC and 3.7% F1. On the FLARE25 public validation leaderboard, GF-Screen scores 58.6% DSC and 52.2% NSD, exceeding the FLARE24 champion (nnUNet-based) by +25.6% DSC and +28.2% NSD. GF-Screen also reduces inference time to 28 seconds per scan (5.7× faster than the next-best method) and cuts false positive rates to 15.6% average versus 38.7% for the next-best method (SuPreM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GF-Screen outperforms all 9 automatic pan-cancer segmentation baselines (nnUNet, SwinUNETR, 3D UX-Net, CLIP-driven, TransUNet, UniMiSS+, VoCo, SuPreM, PASTA) on both segmentation DSC and detection F1-Score across all 9 lesion types. The closest competitor, VoCo (56.1% DSC), is surpassed by 4.7% on average, with the largest gaps on pancreas tumor (+11.9%) and pleural effusion (+3.4%). On false positive rates, GF-Screen (15.6% avg.) dramatically outperforms all baselines, with SuPreM (38.7%) and PASTA (42.6%) being the next best. In inference speed, GF-Screen (28 s/scan) is 5.7× faster than the next-fastest method (3D UX-Net, 102 s) and 7× faster than PASTA (197 s). On the FLARE25 leaderboard, GF-Screen (58.6% DSC, 52.2% NSD) exceeds the FLARE24 champion nnUNet-based solution (33.0% DSC, 24.0% NSD) by a large margin. Interactive segmentation methods (SegVol, SAT, ULS, LesionLocator) achieve higher DSC on some lesion types but require manual prompts and are not applicable to automatic screening; GF-Screen remains competitive on lung nodules, COVID-19, pancreas, kidney, and adrenal lesions without any user input.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) from the LLM reasoning literature (Shao et al., 2024) to a pure vision task, leveraging the natural grouping of cropped sub-volumes as candidate actions without requiring an LLM to generate them. It departs from prior RL-in-vision work (Wang et al., 2020; Huang et al., 2022) that relied on PPO with a separate value model, replacing it with group-relative advantage estimation that eliminates the critic. Conceptually, it extends the radiologist-inspired 'glance and focus' paradigm from image classification to dense 3D medical segmentation, where prior pan-cancer screening methods (nnUNet, SwinUNETR, VoCo, SuPreM, PASTA) uniformly process all sliding-window crops without discarding healthy regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5.7× inference speedup achieved simultaneously with higher segmentation accuracy addresses a critical deployment bottleneck for large-scale CT screening, and the substantial false-positive reduction (15.6% vs. 38–62% for competitors) is clinically meaningful for reducing unnecessary follow-ups. However, the paper acknowledges that real-world clinical validation (reader studies, clinician-in-the-loop evaluation) remains necessary, and the framework is currently limited to 9 lesion types in chest/abdomen CT, with head-neck and lymph node detection left as future work. An open question is whether the group-relative learning paradigm generalizes beyond the specific sub-volume grouping structure that makes it applicable here.
