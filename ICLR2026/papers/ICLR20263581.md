## Problem

Existing KV cache compression methods for LLMs rely on token eviction or merging, which permanently loses information and cannot extrapolate beyond the pretrained context window, causing sharp performance degradation on long-context benchmarks. The paper identifies that KV states exhibit strong energy concentration in low-frequency DCT components, but this frequency-domain structure has not been exploited for decoder-only LLMs due to their autoregressive nature.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263581/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the KV cache tensors (key and value states) accumulated during inference of a decoder-only LLM. It applies DCT along the sequence dimension, retains a fraction γ of low-frequency components, and produces a compressed KV cache of size L = γ·N in the time domain. A fixed set of S sink tokens (default 4) is always kept uncompressed, and key states are compressed before RoPE is applied, with position indices reassigned within the cache rather than the original sequence.

## Method

FreqKV transforms the KV cache into the frequency domain via DCT along the sequence dimension, discards high-frequency components (retaining ratio γ, default 0.5), and reconstructs a shorter time-domain cache via IDCT with a rescaling factor √(L/N) to restore amplitude. Compression is applied iteratively: once the cache reaches its maximum size N, the non-sink tokens are compressed, and the process repeats as new tokens arrive, so earlier tokens undergo progressively more compression while recent tokens remain nearly intact. Key states are compressed before RoPE and then encoded with cache-relative position indices, enabling context extension without position extrapolation. The method is parameter-free and architecture-agnostic, requiring only minimal LoRA fine-tuning (rank 8, 1000 steps at 8K length) to adapt the model to the compressed attention pattern.

## Results

On LongBench (LLaMA-2-chat-7B, 50% retaining ratio), FreqKV achieves an average score of 37.85, surpassing the best baseline ThinK (37.07) and SnapKV (36.81). On RULER at 16K evaluation length (exceeding LLaMA-3-instruct's native 8K window), all eviction/merging baselines collapse to near-zero (≤0.10), while FreqKV maintains 47.30 average. On LongGenBench, FreqKV achieves 54.2% on GSM8K and 31.2% on MMLU, outperforming the best baseline (PyramidKV/SnapKV at 49.6%/26.2%). With only 8K training length, FreqKV extrapolates LLaMA-2-7B to 256K context with stable PPL of 7.73, and compression overhead remains below 0.3% of total decoding time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreqKV consistently outperforms all 11 baselines on LongBench average: it beats ThinK (37.07) and SnapKV (36.81) by ~0.8 points, and GemFilter (31.64) by over 6 points. The most dramatic separation appears on RULER at 16K: every eviction-based method (GemFilter, FastKV, SnapKV, PyramidKV) and merging method (D2O, ThinK) collapses to 0.00–0.10 because they compress after the first decode step and use out-of-bound position embeddings, whereas FreqKV retains 47.30 by compressing before RoPE and using in-window position indices. On LongGenBench, FreqKV leads by 4.6 points on GSM8K (54.2 vs. 49.6 for SnapKV/PyramidKV) and 5.0 points on MMLU (31.2 vs. 26.2). On PG-19 perplexity, FreqKV trained at 8K matches or beats LongLoRA trained at 32K (7.02 vs. 7.22 at 32K eval) and substantially outperforms LoCoCo (7.02 vs. 7.27 at 8K eval). Notably, even at an extreme 1% retaining ratio where most baselines degrade sharply (e.g., GemFilter drops to 11.50 avg), FreqKV remains stable at 35.54, demonstrating robustness under aggressive compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreqKV departs from the dominant KV cache compression paradigm of token eviction (SnapKV, PyramidKV, FastKV) and token merging (CaM, KVMerger, D2O), which permanently lose or approximate information, by instead transforming KV states into the frequency domain via DCT and retaining only low-frequency components. It also differs from context-extension methods that introduce additional parameters or modules (LoCoCo's convolutions, Activation Beacon's special tokens) by being entirely parameter-free and architecture-agnostic. The frequency-domain perspective draws on the Frequency Principle and prior encoder-based work (FNet, Fourier Transformer), but FreqKV is the first to apply DCT-based compression to KV states in decoder-only LLMs, leveraging the autoregressive structure through iterative compression where earlier tokens are progressively compressed more than recent ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FreqKV offers a practical, drop-in solution for context window extension that requires no architectural changes, no additional parameters, and only minimal LoRA fine-tuning at 8K to extrapolate to 256K tokens with stable perplexity. Its robustness under aggressive compression (1% retaining ratio) where eviction-based methods collapse entirely suggests a fundamentally different information-preservation mechanism. Open questions include whether the low-frequency energy concentration holds universally across architectures and training regimes, whether performance on formula-heavy or highly local tasks (as seen on Proof-pile) can be improved, and whether the iterative compression accumulates distortion over extremely long contexts beyond 256K.
