## Problem

Learned Sparse Retrieval (LSR) has achieved strong English results but extensions beyond English remain fragmented: BGE-M3's sparse head underperforms and lacks cross-lingual support, while SPLADE-X and BLADE require separate models per language pair. A straightforward fix—attaching a multilingual MLM head to a multilingual encoder—causes severe semantic collapse, where representations lose interpretable term semantics. No single model existed that unified multilingual and cross-lingual sparse retrieval with transparent, grounded lexical representations.

## Input / Output

MILCO accepts text queries and documents in any of 39+ languages (tokenized by the multilingual encoder) and produces a dual-view sparse lexical representation: an English pivot view (max-pooled logits over a ~280k English vocabulary) and a source view (original input tokens weighted by a learned [ECHO] score). The combined bag-of-weighted-tokens is directly indexable in an inverted index, and post-hoc mass-based pruning at inference time allows variable sparsity per document (e.g., ~30 active dimensions on average) without retraining, unlike fixed-dimension dense or Matryoshka methods.

## Method

MILCO stacks three components: a multilingual encoder (bge-m3-unsupervised), a lightweight MLP connector that projects multilingual hidden states into the English embedding space, and a LexEcho head that decodes both an English MLM view (LogSat + max-pooling, as in SPLADE) and a source view via a dedicated [ECHO] token that selectively preserves entities lost in cross-lingual projection. Training is a two-stage recipe: first, Sparse Alignment Pretraining (SAP) minimizes a sparse-aware MSE loss between the model's English view and SPLADE-v3 teacher representations on 594M bitext pairs, grounding multilingual inputs in the English lexicon; second, Sparse Contrastive Training (SCT) refines retrieval effectiveness via KL distillation from a cross-encoder plus L1 sparsity regularization. SAP is a prerequisite—without it, contrastive training causes semantic collapse—and the LexEcho head adds +4.2% nDCG@10 on MIRACL by recovering tail entities (e.g., non-Latin-script names) that the English view cannot represent.

## Results

On MIRACL (18 languages, nDCG@10), MILCO (SAP, SCT_KD, LexEcho) achieves 72.3 average, outperforming the strongest dense/multi-vector baseline BGE-M3-All (71.5) and the largest sparse baseline T-Splade (54.5) by +0.8 and +17.8 points respectively. On MKQA cross-lingual retrieval (R@100, 25 languages), MILCO reaches 76.6, surpassing BGE-M3 Dense+Sparse (75.3) and Qwen3-Embed-8B (67.9). On MLDR long-document retrieval (nDCG@10, 13 languages), MILCO scores 74.4, exceeding M3-All (65.0) by +9.4 points despite using only 512-token passages. With mass-based pruning to ~30 active dimensions, MILCO still outperforms Qwen3-Embed-0.6B (1024 dims) on nDCG@10 while achieving 3× lower latency and 10× smaller index size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MILCO (72.3 nDCG@10) outperforms BGE-M3's full hybrid ensemble (Dense+Sparse+Multi-vec, 71.5) by +0.8 on MIRACL, and by a wider margin on MKQA (76.6 vs. 75.3 R@100) and MLDR (74.4 vs. 65.0 nDCG@10). Against Qwen3-Embed, MILCO wins on all three primary benchmarks: +11.8 over Qwen3-0.6B and +2.5 over Qwen3-8B on MIRACL, +22.2 and +8.7 on MKQA, and +24.3 and +15.3 on MLDR. Among sparse baselines, MILCO surpasses T-Splade (54.5) by +17.8, M3-Sparse (53.9) by +18.4, and BM25 (31.9) by +40.4 on MIRACL. On MKQA, MILCO's zero-shot cross-lingual R@100 of 76.6 exceeds BM25 (39.9) by +91.9% and BGE-M3-Sparse (45.3) by +69.1%, confirming that the shared English lexical space eliminates the vocabulary mismatch that cripples source-view-only sparse models. On the LIMIT benchmark, MILCO achieves R@100 of 73.5 while all dense baselines (E5-Mistral, GritLM, Qwen3-Embed, Gemini-Embed) collapse to near-zero, highlighting the representational capacity advantage of sparse lexical embeddings. The only setting where MILCO trails is BEIR English (54.4 vs. Qwen3-0.6B's 56.2), attributed to Qwen3's instruction tuning; however, on large-collection BEIR subsets (≥1M docs), MILCO reverses the gap to +2.8.

## Relation to Prior Work

MILCO extends the SPLADE family of learned sparse retrieval models (Formal et al., 2021; Lassance et al., 2024) from English-only to 39 languages by inserting a multilingual connector between a multilingual encoder and an English MLM head, collapsing all languages into a shared English lexical space. This departs from prior multilingual sparse approaches (SPLADE-X, BLADE) that required separate models per language pair, and from BGE-M3 whose sparse component underperforms and lacks cross-lingual support. The training recipe introduces Sparse Alignment Pretraining (SAP), a novel bitext-based alignment stage that grounds multilingual inputs in English sparse space before contrastive distillation, addressing the semantic collapse that occurs when contrastive training is applied directly to multilingual sparse models without such grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MILCO demonstrates that a single 560M sparse model can match or exceed 7B+ dense models on multilingual retrieval while offering transparent, inspectable representations and dynamic efficiency via post-hoc pruning (30 active dimensions beating Qwen3-0.6B's 1024 dimensions with 10× smaller index). The finding that contrastive training without alignment pretraining causes semantic collapse in multilingual sparse models is an important practical warning for the community. Limitations include the fixed English pivot (potentially suboptimal for non-English-dominant queries), the 512-token context window requiring passage splitting for long documents, and underperformance on some benchmarks (NeuCLIR, BEIR English) relative to larger dense models. Open questions include whether adaptive or multi-pivot strategies could further improve tail-language performance and whether the SAP stage generalizes to low-resource language pairs with limited bitext.
