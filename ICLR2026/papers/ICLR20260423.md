## Problem

Existing continuous-time multi-agent reinforcement learning (CT-MARL) methods rely on Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations, but safety constraints such as collision penalties introduce value discontinuities that make HJB-based PINN training intractable. No prior work has explicitly incorporated state constraints into the CT-MARL formulation, leaving a gap between safe MARL (which is discrete-time) and CT-MARL (which ignores safety).

## Input / Output

Each agent's policy network takes its local observation o_i augmented with a scalar time-interval feature Δt, producing a continuous-time action; the critic networks take the global state x and output two z-independent scalars (return value and constraint value). The optimal auxiliary state z* is computed per-episode via a one-dimensional scalar search over a task-dependent range [z_min, z_max], rather than being propagated through auxiliary dynamics or solved via root-finding at execution. The dynamics and cost networks take concatenated state-action pairs (x, u) and predict the next state and instantaneous stage cost respectively, enabling model-free actor updates.

## Method

The core contribution is an epigraph reformulation that augments the system with an auxiliary state z following ż = -l(x,u) - ln(γ)·z, converting the discontinuous constrained value into a continuous auxiliary value V(x,z) = min_u max{sup_τ c(x(τ)), ∫γ^{τ-t}l dτ - z}. This yields a well-posed HJB PDE whose unique viscosity solution is proven via a contradiction argument. The learning framework uses a z-independent actor-critic architecture: the outer optimization computes z* by solving a scalar feasibility condition against the learned critics, while the inner optimization trains two PINN-based critic networks (return and constraint) with three complementary losses — an HJB residual loss, a rollout-based target loss (critical for infinite-horizon settings lacking boundary conditions), and a value-gradient-iteration loss that enforces gradient consistency. The actor is updated by minimizing an epigraph-based advantage function where true dynamics and cost are replaced by jointly trained neural networks, eliminating the need for root-finding at deployment and removing the nonstationary noise of random z sampling used in prior epigraph methods.

## Results

The paper evaluates EPI on six continuous-time safe MPE scenarios (Corridor, Formation, Line, Target, Simple Spread, Cooperative Predator-Prey) and multi-agent MuJoCo tasks (Half Cheetah, Ant variants), reporting cumulative reward (task cost + safety penalty) and constraint violation rate over 30,000 episodes. EPI consistently achieves the lowest cost and constraint violation across all scenarios, occupying the lower-left (desirable) region of the cost-vs-violation scatter plots in Figs. 3 and 6. The didactic coupled-oscillator experiment further validates that EPI's learned value and action closely track the analytical LQR ground truth, while the ablation without epigraph reformulation produces severely mis-scaled values (requiring a ×1/20 rescale to share the y-axis) and unstable actions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EPI outperforms all four baseline families across both MPE and MuJoCo benchmarks. Against EPPO (Zhang et al., 2025b), the closest epigraph-based competitor, EPI achieves lower cost and fewer violations because EPPO randomly samples the auxiliary state z during training, introducing nonstationary noise that prevents stable convergence; EPI instead computes z* via a scalar search and trains z-independent critics. Against MACPO (Gu et al., 2021), EPI achieves comparable or lower constraint violations but substantially lower task cost, since MACPO's hard trust-region update is overly conservative and causes agents to retreat rather than pursue targets. Against MAPPO-Lag and SAC-Lag (Gu et al., 2021; Haarnoja et al., 2018), EPI shows more stable training and better final performance, as Lagrangian relaxation is known to oscillate under tight safety requirements. Against the CBF baseline (Zhang et al., 2025a), EPI achieves lower cost while maintaining similar or better violation rates, since CBF's reliance on a learned barrier function gradient introduces approximation errors that distort the effective safe set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two relatively recent research threads: continuous-time MARL via PINN-based HJB solving (Wang et al., 2025; Mukherjee & Liu) and epigraph-based safe optimal control (So & Fan, 2023; Zhang et al., 2025b). The key departure is that prior CT-MARL methods cannot handle state constraints because they introduce value discontinuities that break PDE-based learning, while prior epigraph methods operate in discrete time. The authors bridge this gap by formulating a CT-CMDP and applying an epigraph reformulation that converts discontinuous constrained values into a continuous auxiliary value function amenable to HJB-PDE training. A second methodological departure from the discrete-time epigraph literature (Zhang et al., 2025b) is the revised outer optimization: instead of randomly sampling z during training and performing root-finding at execution, the authors make all networks z-independent and compute z* via a scalar search during training, yielding a z-independent deployable policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formulation of safe CT-MARL with explicit state constraints, filling a gap between the well-developed discrete-time safe MARL literature (MACPO, MAPPO-Lag) and the emerging continuous-time MARL literature. The theoretical contribution—proving existence and uniqueness of viscosity solutions for the epigraph-based HJB PDE—provides a rigorous foundation that prior PINN-based CT-RL work lacked. However, the method's reliance on learned dynamics and cost networks (rather than truly model-free operation) and its demonstrated sensitivity to stochastic disturbances (performance degrades significantly under mid/high noise) limit its applicability to real-world systems with substantial uncertainty. Open questions include scalability to larger agent populations, extension to stochastic HJB formulations, and whether the z-independent design generalizes to more complex constraint structures (e.g., coupled or time-varying constraints).
