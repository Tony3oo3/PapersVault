## Problem

Temporal Change Description (TCD) and Future Satellite Image Forecasting (FSIF) in Satellite Image Time Series have historically been treated as disjointed tasks, each limited by an inability to model long-range temporal dynamics spanning years. Existing description methods lack the semantic depth of modern MLLMs, while generative forecasting approaches rely on coarse text or metadata conditioning that cannot ensure patch-level temporal consistency. The paper addresses the absence of a unified framework where deep temporal understanding directly drives consistent future generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260912/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of temporally ordered RGB satellite images (with inter-frame gaps spanning 5–10 years) along with explicit time-interval metadata, and produces two coupled outputs: a concise natural-language change description (targeting ~30 words) and a single future satellite image for the next time point. Unlike video models that assume fixed short intervals, the temporal tokens are dynamically conditioned on arbitrary time gaps via an MLP, and the MLLM's internal semantic feature vector M_t—not just the generated text—serves as the primary conditioning signal for the diffusion generator.

## Method

TAMMs couples a frozen DeepSeek-VL2 MLLM with a frozen Stable Diffusion U-Net through two lightweight, trainable modules. Temporal Adaptation Modules (TAM) reprogram the MLLM via a Physical Time Encoder that injects learnable [TIME_DIFF] tokens conditioned on actual inter-frame intervals, combined with Contextual Temporal Prompting that focuses the model on articulating change narratives, yielding both a text description and a rich semantic vector M_t. The Semantic-Fused Control Injection (SFCI) mechanism then translates M_t into multi-scale, spatially-aware control signals through an Enhanced Control Module that runs in parallel with the U-Net: a Structural Path (frozen 3D Control Block) captures low-level spatio-temporal dynamics, a Semantic Path projects M_t via level-specific processors, and adaptive gated fusion units dynamically interpolate between the two streams before Temporal Transformers refine long-range dependencies. Training follows a staged protocol—first pre-training the structural path on visual data alone, then training semantic components with the structural path frozen—to prevent catastrophic forgetting and ensure stable integration of high-level reasoning into the generative process.

## Results

On the curated 150-sequence Google Earth test set, TAMMs achieves the best BLEU-4 (0.2669), METEOR (0.3312), and CIDEr-D (0.9030) for Temporal Change Description, and the highest PSNR (12.07), best LPIPS (0.4931), and a dominant TCS of 0.9690 for Future Satellite Image Forecasting. The TCS advantage is particularly striking: TAMMs scores 0.9690 versus 0.7624 for DiffusionSat and 0.2170 for GeoSynth-Canny, indicating substantially more temporally coherent predictions. TAMMs slightly trails MCVD on SSIM (0.1831 vs. 0.2098) and TEOChat on ROUGE-L (0.4690 vs. 0.4735), but leads on all other reported metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For Temporal Change Description, TAMMs outperforms all five baselines on BLEU-4, METEOR, and CIDEr-D, with the largest margins over the specialized RSICC-Former (0.2669 vs. 0.1285 BLEU-4) and the general MLLM DeepSeek-VL2 (0.9030 vs. 0.7362 CIDEr-D). The closest competitor is TEOChat, which edges out TAMms on ROUGE-L (0.4735 vs. 0.4690) but trails on all other text metrics. For Future Satellite Image Forecasting, TAMMs achieves the best PSNR (12.07 vs. 11.89 for DiffusionSat) and best LPIPS (0.4931 vs. 0.4970 for MCVD), while the headline TCS metric shows a decisive win: 0.9690 versus 0.7624 (DiffusionSat), 0.2170 (GeoSynth-Canny), and 0.1930 (MCVD). TAMMs is the only model to simultaneously lead on both tasks, whereas each baseline is restricted to one task and lags substantially on the other's metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously disjointed satellite image time series tasks—temporal change description and future image forecasting—into a single MLLM-diffusion architecture. It builds on DeepSeek-VL2 as a frozen MLLM backbone, DiffusionSat's U-Net and 3D Control Block for the generative component, and the ControlNet paradigm for conditional diffusion, but departs from all three by injecting the MLLM's high-level semantic feature vector (not just text) directly as a multi-scale spatial control signal via the SFCI mechanism. The Temporal Adaptation Modules (PTE + CTP) represent a parameter-efficient reprogramming of a general-purpose MLLM for the sparse, long-interval temporal structure of satellite imagery, which video-centric MLLMs are not optimized for.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core finding that direct semantic feature injection into the diffusion control path (TCS 0.969) substantially outperforms text-only guidance (TCS 0.941) or structural-only control (TCS 0.762) validates the hypothesis that high-level temporal reasoning must be translated into fine-grained spatial signals for consistent forecasting—a principle that could generalize beyond satellite imagery. The proposed TCS metric addresses a real evaluation gap, though its simplicity (centroid distance + area ratio) limits its discriminative power for complex multi-region changes. Key limitations include the small 150-sequence test set, reliance on a single training dataset (fMoW), and the frozen MLLM constraint that caps the depth of temporal reasoning. Open questions include scalability to very long horizons, rare abrupt events, and whether the semantic-to-control translation generalizes to other time-series forecasting domains.
