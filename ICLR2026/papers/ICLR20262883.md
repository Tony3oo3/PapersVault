## Problem

Existing multimodal emotion benchmarks evaluate models with fragmented task designs that lack a unified cognitive framework, making it impossible to pinpoint exactly where a model's reasoning breaks down as task complexity increases. Meanwhile, even state-of-the-art MLLMs produce reasoning chains that appear coherent but are unfaithful—substituting template matching for genuine mental-state simulation and failing to track beliefs, intentions, and causal attributions consistently. The paper addresses both gaps by introducing a Theory-of-Mind-organized hierarchical benchmark and a ToM-guided reinforcement learning method that trains models to reason about mental states explicitly.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multimodal inputs (video sampled to 16 frames at up to 256×28×28 pixels per frame, audio, and text) paired with a task-specific ToM prompt that prescribes a structured reasoning template. The output is a single structured string: a multi-step reasoning chain enclosed in <think> tags, followed by the final task-specific answer (class label, generated explanation, or extracted tuple) in <answer> tags. The reasoning chain must follow task-specific step headers derived from the ToM prompt, and the model operates within an 8,192-token context window with a 4,096-token maximum generation length.

## Method

TMPO is a two-stage training framework built on Qwen2.5-Omni-7B. Stage 1 performs supervised fine-tuning on curated ToM-aligned reasoning chains produced by a four-step LLM pipeline (generation, filtering, enhancement, correction), teaching the model to emit structured <think>…<answer>…</answer> outputs. Stage 2 applies Group-wise Reward Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a four-component reward: structure (proportion of required XML delimiters and step headers present in correct order), content (task-specific metric on the final answer), process (normalized count of ToM-domain keywords in the reasoning chain), and consistency (an LLM judge penalizing internal or external logical contradictions). The key insight is using intermediate mental states extracted from the reasoning chain as process-level supervision signals in the RL objective, shifting emotional reasoning from a general emergent capability to a domain-acquired skill. Reward weights are set to μ_structure=0.4, μ_content=1.0, μ_process=0.1, μ_consistency=1.0, prioritizing correctness and logical grounding over stylistic compliance.

## Results

On the HitEmotion benchmark (24 tasks across 3 cognitive levels), TMPO (SFT + GRPO) achieves top performance on 16 of 24 tasks, surpassing all proprietary and open-source baselines. At the most cognitively demanding Level 3 (Emotion Cognition and Reasoning), TMPO scores 73.13 on EER, 72.27 on EI, and 72.45 on LR, outperforming Gemini-2.5-Pro (66.13, 65.13, 59.23) by 7–13 points. At Level 1 (perception), TMPO reaches 77.12 on FESD and 79.12 on MSA, slightly trailing Gemini-2.5-Pro (78.39, 74.20) on some perception-heavy tasks. ToM-style prompting alone yields consistent gains for closed-source models (e.g., +16.52 on SFA for GPT-4o, +17.20 for Gemini-2.5-Flash), confirming the value of structured cognitive scaffolding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TMPO (SFT + GRPO) decisively outperforms all 13 open-source baselines (ranging from 0.5B to 38B parameters) across all three cognitive levels, with the largest margins at Level 3 (e.g., +22.82 on EER over Qwen2.5-VL-32B, +29.32 on SFA over Qwen2.5-Omni-7B). Against the strongest proprietary model Gemini-2.5-Pro, TMPO wins on 10 of 24 tasks, most dramatically at Level 3 (EER: 73.13 vs. 66.13; EI: 72.27 vs. 65.13; LR: 72.45 vs. 59.23; SFA: 54.16 vs. 41.22), but trails on perception-heavy Level 1 tasks like FESD (77.12 vs. 78.39) and MER (75.41 vs. 72.11, where it actually wins). Compared to GPT-4.1 + ToM prompt, TMPO leads on EER (73.13 vs. 65.86), EI (72.27 vs. 69.00), and SFA (54.16 vs. 47.75), while GPT-4.1 + ToM retains an edge on MECPE (28.11 vs. 39.34 — actually TMPO wins here too). The paper notes that on direct perception tasks (e.g., facial expression recognition), TMPO's 7B backbone limits raw sensory quality, so it cannot fully match the perception of larger proprietary models, but its reasoning-focused optimization compensates strongly on all tasks requiring causal, contextual, or second-order mental-state inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multimodal affective computing literature by reframing emotion understanding through a Theory-of-Mind lens, departing from prior benchmarks (EmoBench, EmotionHallucer, MME-Emotion) that evaluate emotion tasks in a flat, undifferentiated manner. Methodologically, it builds directly on GRPO (DeepSeek-R1) for the preference optimization stage, but introduces a novel multi-dimensional reward function that uses intermediate mental states as process-level supervision—a departure from outcome-only rewards in prior RL-for-reasoning work. The ToM-style prompting mechanism extends generic Chain-of-Thought by structuring reasoning along first-order, second-order, and causal attribution levels drawn from developmental psychology, rather than relying on undifferentiated 'think step-by-step' instructions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HitEmotion provides the first hierarchical diagnostic that pinpoints exactly where MLLMs' emotional reasoning breaks down, revealing that even Gemini-2.5-Pro and GPT-4.1 fail below 60% on all Level-3 (cognition/reasoning) tasks—a concrete, actionable gap for the field. TMPO demonstrates that a 7B open-source model can surpass proprietary systems on 16 of 24 tasks through reasoning-focused RL, offering a practical pathway for resource-constrained deployment. Key limitations include the 7B base model's bounded perception (Level-1 tasks remain weak), the ToM framework's applicability to non-emotion social reasoning domains, and the reliance on LLM-judged consistency rewards that may introduce their own biases. Open questions include whether scaling TMPO to larger omni-modal backbones closes the remaining perception gap and whether the cognitive hierarchy generalizes beyond the specific 24 tasks tested.
