## Problem

Progressive vision token pruning in MLLMs is constrained by two core misunderstandings: shallow layers are misinterpreted as critical for multimodal integration when they are actually passive propagators, and rigid uniform pruning schedules fail to exploit the highly non-uniform redundancy profile across layers. This leads to suboptimal efficiency-accuracy trade-offs, with state-of-the-art methods like PDrop achieving only ~47% token reduction while HiDrop demonstrates that ~90% compression is possible with negligible performance loss.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (partitioned into 576 patches by a vision encoder and projected into the LLM's hidden space) and a text instruction, producing a standard text response. Unusually, vision tokens are not present in the first 8 transformer layers (late injection at layer 9), are progressively pruned through middle layers via differentiable top-k selection, and are fully discarded before layer 25 (early exit), so the model's forward pass operates on a dynamically shrinking set of visual tokens with persistent RoPE position identifiers that never change across layers.

## Method

HiDrop partitions the LLM into three functional stages and applies a tailored strategy to each: (1) Late Injection bypasses shallow layers entirely, injecting the full 576 vision tokens only at layer 9 where cross-modal fusion actually begins; (2) Concave Pyramid Pruning in middle layers aggressively reduces tokens early in the fusion window and more gradually later, with pruning locations identified by the Inter-Layer Visual Attention Similarity (ILVAS) metric and token selection performed via a Differentiable Top-K operator that provides continuous relaxation for end-to-end training; (3) Early Exit discards all remaining vision tokens at layer 25 once the model transitions to language-dominant reasoning. Implementation-level optimizations include persistent positional encoding to avoid RoPE misalignment under dynamic token sets, a lightweight auxiliary single-query attention pass for token scoring that preserves FlashAttention compatibility, and parallel decoupling of vision KV projection from the text prefill critical path.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B, HiDrop retains 98.3% of baseline average performance while pruning 88.9% of vision tokens (576→64), and still achieves 96.5% at 91.7% pruning (576→48). Training time drops from 159.3 to 94.4 GPU hours (1.72× speedup), inference FLOPs fall from 3.82T to 0.42T (88.9% reduction), and prefill latency decreases from 63.6 ms to 32.6 ms. Across three LLM backbones (2.7B, 7B, 13B), HiDrop consistently delivers 94.8–98.7% of vanilla performance under 64–206 average visual tokens, with only 1.5–1.6% average degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiDrop outperforms all four baselines at every pruning ratio tested. At 88.9% pruning (64 tokens), HiDrop achieves 98.3% average vs. PDrop (94.2%), TwigVLM (95.3%), VoCo-LLaMA (90.4%), and FastV (82.8%)—a 4.1-point gap over the closest competitor PDrop. At the most aggressive 91.7% pruning (48 tokens), HiDrop retains 96.5% while PDrop cannot even be evaluated under this protocol, TwigVLM drops to 87.3%, and FastV collapses to 70.2%. The advantage is most pronounced on vision-heavy benchmarks: at 88.9% pruning, HiDrop leads PDrop by 3.9 points on GQA (60.5 vs. 56.6) and by 3.8 points on POPE (86.4 vs. 82.6). Training-free baselines (FastV, PDrop†) suffer the largest degradation, confirming that end-to-end training with differentiable token selection is critical for aggressive compression. HiDrop also achieves a 4.8× higher pruning ratio than PDrop (89.0% vs. 46.9%) while incurring only 1.6% average performance loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the progressive vision token pruning line (FastV, PDrop, TwigVLM, VoCo-LLaMA) but departs from it on two conceptual grounds: it argues that shallow LLM layers are passive propagators rather than active fusion sites, motivating a Late Injection strategy that bypasses them entirely, and it replaces the rigid uniform-ratio schedules of prior work with a Concave Pyramid Pruning schedule guided by an inter-layer attention similarity metric (ILVAS) and a differentiable top-k operator for end-to-end token selection. The differentiable top-k component builds on Liu et al. (2024b) but applies it specifically to vision token selection within a hierarchical pruning framework, whereas prior in-LLM pruning methods (PDrop, TwigVLM) rely on non-differentiable hard top-k or heuristic thresholds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 90% visual token compression with only ~1.6% average performance drop and 1.72× training speedup makes MLLM deployment substantially more practical, and the hierarchical layer-dynamics analysis (shallow=propagator, middle=sparse fusion hub, deep=language-dominant) provides a reusable diagnostic framework for future architectures. Limitations include evaluation confined to the LLaVA-1.5 connector-based family; whether the specific injection/exit/filtering layer indices transfer to video MLLMs, next-generation architectures with different depth, or non-connector designs (e.g., Qwen2-VL) remains open. The paper also does not explore interaction with other efficiency techniques (quantization, speculative decoding) that practitioners would likely combine.
