## Problem

Object detection in low-light conditions suffers from degraded feature representations due to low contrast and heavy noise, yet existing remedies—preprocessing enhancement, modified detector architectures, or domain adaptation—either add inference-time computational overhead or fail when the source-to-target domain gap is large (e.g., daytime-to-nighttime driving). The paper addresses the gap of improving low-light detection without any additional inference cost or architectural modification to the baseline detector.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260864/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method consumes a raw low-light RGB image directly by the unmodified baseline detector (DSFD, YOLOv3, or YOLOv8) and outputs standard bounding boxes with class labels, incurring zero extra parameters, FLOPs, or latency relative to the baseline. During training, the same low-light image is additionally routed through a detachable auxiliary pipeline that produces a dense pixel-wise supervisory target image, which is discarded entirely at test time.

## Method

The framework attaches a detachable U-Net decoder to the detector's backbone via skip connections, used exclusively during training to predict a fused target image generated from the input itself. This target is constructed by first applying a self-supervised enhancement module (SCI) to brighten the image, then a self-supervised denoising module (SDAP) to suppress the amplified noise, and finally performing Fourier-domain fusion that combines the phase of the enhanced image (preserving structural edges) with the amplitude of the denoised image (providing a clean, noise-suppressed spectral envelope). The decoder's reconstruction of this fused target via MSE loss provides dense pixel-wise supervision that complements sparse bounding-box labels, encouraging the backbone to retain structural and semantic cues lost in low-light conditions. Because the entire auxiliary pipeline is removed at inference, the deployed detector is architecturally identical to the baseline with no additional computational cost.

## Results

On DARK FACE, the proposed SGLDet framework achieves 76.6 mAP with the DSFD detector, a +16.9 mAP improvement over the 59.7 mAP baseline, while incurring zero additional parameters, FLOPs, or inference time. On ExDark, it reaches 78.6 mAP with YOLOv3-608 (+2.2 mAP over the 76.4 baseline). On nuImages nighttime driving scenes, it achieves 55.3 mAP trained from scratch (+2.9 mAP over YOLOv8-m) and 58.0 mAP with daytime pretraining (+1.6 mAP), maintaining 170.1 FPS real-time inference in both settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGLDet outperforms all 12 baselines across all three datasets. On DARK FACE, it surpasses the strongest competitor DAI-Net (68.5 mAP) by 8.1 mAP and supervised LLIE Retinexformer (68.1 mAP) by 8.5 mAP, while adding zero inference overhead—whereas Retinexformer adds 43.73 ms and GLARE adds 746.72 ms. On ExDark, it beats DAI-Net (78.3 mAP) by 0.3 mAP and MAET (77.7 mAP) by 0.9 mAP, and notably outperforms all self-supervised LLIE methods (best: SCI at 77.9 mAP) by 0.7 mAP. On nuImages, the advantage is most pronounced in the domain adaptation setting: SGLDet (58.0 mAP) exceeds the pretrained baseline (56.4 mAP) by 1.6 mAP, while MAET (53.8 mAP) and DAI-Net (54.4 mAP) actually degrade below the baseline by 2.6 and 2.0 mAP respectively, confirming that domain adaptation fails under large day-to-night domain gaps. Enhancement-based methods like T2 (51.1 mAP) and GLARE (51.7 mAP) also underperform the baseline when trained from scratch on nuImages, and GLARE drops to 0.2 FPS, making it impractical for real-time driving.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing existing self-supervised modules (SCI for enhancement, SDAP for denoising) into a detachable, training-only auxiliary pipeline that generates a Fourier-fused supervisory target for the detector backbone, rather than deploying them as inference-time preprocessing. It departs from the three dominant paradigms in low-light detection—LLIE-as-preprocessing, modified detector architectures, and domain adaptation—by eliminating inference-time overhead entirely while still benefiting from enhancement and denoising signals. The Fourier-domain fusion strategy (denoised amplitude + enhanced phase) is a specific technical contribution that addresses the well-known trade-off between noise amplification from enhancement and detail loss from denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-inference-overhead design makes this framework directly deployable in real-time autonomous driving systems where adding even a few milliseconds is prohibitive, and the detector-agnostic formulation (validated on DSFD, YOLOv3, YOLOv8) broadens its practical applicability. A practically important finding is that domain adaptation methods (MAET, DAI-Net) actively degrade performance on nuImages when the day-to-night domain gap is large, suggesting these approaches have a fundamental limitation in real driving scenarios. Open questions include whether the Fourier fusion strategy generalizes to other adverse conditions (rain, fog, motion blur) and whether the training-time auxiliary pipeline can be further simplified or made more efficient for very large-scale training.
