## Problem

Autoregressive image generation with diffusion loss (e.g., MAR) avoids VQ-VAE quantization errors, but the theoretical relationship between conditional diffusion and autoregressive diffusion with diffusion loss remains underexplored. Specifically, two issues are unaddressed: (1) how condition errors propagate and whether autoregressive patch denoising can correct them, and (2) 'condition inconsistency,' where extraneous information accumulates in sequentially generated conditions, degrading patch generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261947/cropped_page8_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single ImageNet class label as input and produces a 256×256 (or 512×512) RGB image. The autoregressive backbone (GPT-XL) sequentially generates per-patch conditions, which are refined by an OT module before guiding a MAR-based denoising MLP to produce latent patches; a KL-16 LDM VAE decoder then maps the assembled latent to pixel space. Training uses 1000-step cosine noise schedules with batch size 2048 over 400 epochs.

## Method

The paper first proves theoretically that iterative patch denoising in autoregressive models stabilizes the condition distribution and causes the conditional probability gradient norm to decay exponentially (Theorem 2), showing that AR condition generation inherently refines conditions. To address residual 'condition inconsistency' (extraneous information accumulation quantified in Lemma 6), the authors introduce an Optimal Transport-based condition refinement module formulated as a Wasserstein Gradient Flow (Proposition 2), whose JKO discretization guarantees monotonic convergence to the ideal condition distribution (Theorem 3). Practically, the algorithm (ACO) alternates between AR condition prediction, DDIM denoising, Sinkhorn-regularized OT transport between the current condition distribution and an EMA-maintained target, and gradient-based condition updates with adaptive entropy regularization. The full system combines a GPT-XL autoregressive backbone, a MAR-based denoising module, and the OT refinement step, achieving FID 1.31 on ImageNet 256×256.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, the proposed method (Ours(MAR)) achieves the best FID of 1.31 and highest IS of 324.2, outperforming all compared diffusion and autoregressive baselines. The AR-only variant (Ours(AR)) reaches FID 1.52 and IS 317.6, still surpassing MAR (FID 1.55, IS 303.7) and MDTv2-XL/2 (FID 1.58, IS 314.7). Scalability experiments show consistent gains across model sizes (208M–943M) and at 512×512 resolution (FID 1.58 vs. MAR's 1.73).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed Ours(MAR) achieves the best FID (1.31) and IS (324.2) among all methods, beating the closest AR-based competitors De-MAR (FID 1.47) and RAR (FID 1.50) by 0.16 and 0.19 FID respectively, and surpassing MAR (FID 1.55) by 0.24. Against diffusion-based methods, Ours(MAR) outperforms MDTv2-XL/2 (FID 1.58), DiffiT (FID 1.73), DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27), and LDM-4 (FID 3.60) by substantial margins. The AR-only variant (Ours(AR), FID 1.52) slightly trails De-MAR (1.47) and RAR (1.50) in FID but leads them in IS (317.6 vs. 305.8 and 306.9). In the scalability study, the performance gap over MAR widens with model size: from 0.35 FID at 208M to 0.24 at 943M, and at 512×512 resolution the method achieves FID 1.58 vs. MAR's 1.73. Precision and Recall remain competitive (0.81/0.63 for Ours(MAR)), though MDTv2-XL/2 achieves slightly higher Recall (0.65) and De-MAR higher Precision (0.83).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoregressive-with-diffusion-loss paradigm introduced by MAR (Li et al., 2024a), which eliminated VQ-VAE quantization by replacing discrete token prediction with continuous denoising. It departs from prior work by providing the first systematic theoretical comparison between conditional diffusion and autoregressive diffusion with diffusion loss, formalizing how patch denoising iteratively refines conditions and causes condition-error influence to decay exponentially. The Optimal Transport (Wasserstein Gradient Flow) formulation for condition refinement is a novel addition not present in prior AR or diffusion image generation literature, drawing on JKO scheme theory from mathematical physics rather than from the generative modeling community.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical framework clarifies why autoregressive condition generation is inherently beneficial for diffusion-based denoising (exponential error decay) and identifies a concrete failure mode—condition inconsistency from extraneous information accumulation—that prior AR+diffusion methods did not formally address. However, the practical gains are modest (FID 1.55→1.31 over MAR at 943M), experiments are limited to ImageNet class-conditional generation at 256×256 and 512×512, and the authors explicitly acknowledge the absence of validation on very large models. Open questions include whether the OT refinement generalizes to text-to-image or video generation, whether the Sinkhorn-based implementation scales to high-resolution multi-patch settings, and whether the theoretical convergence guarantees hold under the non-convex losses of real neural networks.
