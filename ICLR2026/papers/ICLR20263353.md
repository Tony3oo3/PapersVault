## Problem

Existing multi-object tracking systems rely on detection-centric training objectives (IoU, cross-entropy) that fail to jointly optimize temporal stability, spatial awareness, and identity preservation, leading to three persistent error types: post-occlusion ID switches, temporal inconsistency during posture changes, and cross-subject ID switches in crowded scenes. No prior method provides a unified, differentiable, tracking-specific loss that can be integrated into arbitrary MOT architectures without modifying their forward pass or inference logic.

## Input / Output

UniTrack consumes the detection features and bounding box predictions already produced by a base MOT model (TrackFormer, MOTR, FairMOT, ByteTrack, GTR, or MOTE) over a sliding window of W=5 consecutive frames, and outputs the same trajectory and identity assignments as the base model but with improved ID consistency. The graph is constructed per training step from node embeddings derived from detection features, with edge weights encoding pairwise association confidence; no additional input modalities or architectural changes are required. All computational overhead (O(n²t) for n objects over t frames, ~5% extra memory) is incurred exclusively during training, with zero inference cost.

## Method

UniTrack formulates tracking as a differentiable flow-conservation problem over a temporal sequence of weighted directed graphs, where nodes are detected objects and edges encode spatial-temporal associations. The unified loss combines three components: a flow loss that encourages confident object-to-object associations scaled by an exponential detection-quality term (addressing post-occlusion ID switches), a spatial coherence loss that penalizes geometrically distant associations weighted by learned attention (addressing cross-subject ID switches), and a temporal coherence loss that penalizes abrupt velocity changes weighted by outgoing flow confidence (addressing temporal inconsistency). Adaptive weighting of the spatial and temporal terms is computed at each training step from the algebraic connectivity (second-smallest eigenvalue) of separate spatial and temporal graph Laplacians, so fragmented spatial relationships automatically increase spatial emphasis while disrupted temporal flows increase temporal emphasis. The entire loss is differentiable with respect to flow variables, positions, and velocities, enabling standard backpropagation through the base model with no architectural modifications.

## Results

On MOT17, UniTrack integrated with GTR (UT-GTR) achieves 79.1% MOTA (+3.8%), 74.8% IDF1 (+3.3%), and 67.9% HOTA (+8.8%), reducing identity switches by 34.2% (1445→951). On SportsMOT, UT-GTR delivers the largest gains: 84.5% MOTA (+9.7%), 73.6% IDF1 (+12.3%), and 66.1% HOTA (+11.7%), with a 53.8% reduction in ID switches (2364→1092). Across all six tested architectures on MOT17, UniTrack consistently improves MOTA by 1.8–3.8% and IDF1 by 2.5–8.8%, with the strongest identity-preservation gains observed on GTR and TrackFormer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniTrack is added as a plug-and-play loss to six diverse MOT architectures, and in every case it improves MOTA, IDF1, and HOTA over the unmodified baseline. The largest absolute gains appear with GTR: on MOT17, UT-GTR improves MOTA by +3.8% and HOTA by +8.8% while cutting ID switches by 34.2%; on SportsMOT the gains reach +9.7% MOTA and +12.3% IDF1 with a 53.8% ID-switch reduction. TrackFormer benefits most in identity preservation (IDF1 +8.8% on MOT17, +7.9% on MOT20), though its ID-switch count slightly increases on MOT17 (643→705) while dropping sharply on MOT20 (643→314). ByteTrack, already a strong tracker, sees more modest but consistent gains (+1.8% MOTA, +2.5% IDF1 on MOT17) with a 15% reduction in ID switches. MOTE, the strongest baseline, still improves by +2.5% MOTA and +3.2% IDF1 on MOT17, reaching 84.5% MOTA. FairMOT and MOTR show mid-range improvements (+2.7–2.8% MOTA) but with slight ID-switch increases on MOT17, suggesting the adaptive weighting trades a small number of switches for substantially better detection and association quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniTrack carves a distinct niche by repurposing graph-theoretic concepts (flow conservation, Laplacian spectral analysis) as a training-time loss rather than an architectural redesign, explicitly contrasting itself with prior graph-based MOT methods (Neural MOT solver, SUSHI, GTR, DiffMOT) that modify the tracking pipeline itself. It departs from classical MOT training objectives (IoU + cross-entropy) and from joint detection-tracking losses (MOTR, TrackFormer) that still treat spatial and temporal consistency as separate terms, instead unifying them into a single differentiable graph optimization. The adaptive weighting via algebraic connectivity of spatial and temporal Laplacians is a novel application of spectral graph theory to loss balancing, though the individual components (flow conservation, spatial distance penalties, velocity smoothness) are standard tools from network flow and physics-informed learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design—adding a loss term with zero architectural or inference modifications—makes UniTrack immediately applicable to any existing MOT system, which is practically valuable for practitioners who cannot retrain from scratch or modify deployed architectures. The ablation revealing that removing the temporal component *increases* MOTA by 2.1% while inflating ID switches by 32% (masked by MOTA's linear FP/FN summation) is a useful diagnostic insight about metric limitations. However, the dramatic SportsMOT gains (9.7% MOTA for GTR) contrast sharply with near-zero MOT20 improvements for the same model (63.6→63.8 MOTA), raising questions about where the loss truly helps versus where baseline architectures already saturate. Open questions include whether the O(n²t) training cost and 5% memory overhead remain acceptable at extreme densities (beyond MOT20's ~170 objects/frame), and whether the single-camera formulation can be extended to multi-camera settings without combinatorial graph growth.
