## Problem

Existing graph anomaly detection (GAD) methods operate under a closed-set assumption, presuming training anomalies fully represent all possible anomaly types, which fails in real-world scenarios where novel anomaly patterns emerge at inference. This open-set GAD problem is further compounded by extreme label scarcity (only a handful of labeled anomalies from a single class) and severe class imbalance, causing models to overfit the dominant normal class and produce biased decision boundaries that cannot generalize to unseen anomalies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an attributed graph G=(V,E,X) with a small labeled training set consisting of 50 anomalous nodes drawn from a single 'seen' anomaly class and 5% of normal nodes, while the test set contains both seen and unseen anomaly classes. The model (a 2-layer GraphSAGE with 64 hidden units) outputs a per-node anomaly score in [0,1], where high scores indicate anomalous behavior. The open-set protocol alternates which anomaly class is designated as 'seen' during training, evaluating generalization to all remaining anomaly classes at test time.

## Method

DEMO combines three synergistic components within a dynamic training loop. First, a multi-sample mixup strategy adaptively fuses all seen anomaly embeddings using softmax-weighted similarity scores (with learnable projection matrices) to synthesize diverse anomaly representations that expand the decision boundary, regularized by a diversity term preventing degenerate self-mixing and a consistency loss between original and synthesized views. Second, an energy gradient-driven influence mechanism computes per-sample weights via the Hessian-inverse of energy gradients projected onto validation loss gradients, dynamically upweighting uncertain boundary samples that are most informative for generalization while suppressing harmful ones. Third, a class-aware pseudo-labeling module maintains a memory bank of historical selection counts per class to adaptively adjust asymmetric confidence thresholds (anomaly threshold increases, normal threshold decreases over training), enabling reliable pseudo-label assignment for unlabeled nodes under severe imbalance. The total loss integrates the energy-weighted classification loss, the mixup consistency loss, and the pseudo-labeled unlabeled loss with dataset-specific balancing coefficients.

## Results

On three small-scale datasets (Photo, Computers, CS), DEMO achieves AUC-ROC of 0.9023, 0.8439, and 0.9448, and AUC-PR of 0.6330, 0.6458, and 0.8857, respectively, outperforming all 15 baselines on every metric. On large-scale datasets, DEMO reaches AUC-ROC of 0.7097 (Yelp), 0.6364 (ogbn-arxiv), and 0.4967 (ogbn-mag), with several baselines (TAM, OCGNN, ANO-S, DOM-S) running out of memory on the two largest graphs. DEMO's advantage is most pronounced on AUC-PR, where it improves over the second-best method by up to 17.60% on small-scale datasets, reflecting its strength in the severely imbalanced anomaly-detection setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEMO outperforms all 13 baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the strongest open-set baseline NSReg, DEMO gains +6.63% AUC-ROC on Photo (0.9023 vs. 0.8360), +10.36% on Computers (0.8439 vs. 0.7403), and +4.16% on CS (0.9448 vs. 0.9032), with AUC-PR improvements of +15.53%, +10.21%, and +7.42% respectively. Among semi-supervised GAD methods, DEMO surpasses GGAD (the best on CS AUC-ROC at 0.9081) by 3.67% on CS and by 10.03% on Photo AUC-ROC; it also beats SpaceGNN by 9.93% AUC-ROC on Photo and 1.43% on Computers. Unsupervised methods (ANOMALOUS, DOMINANT, AnomalyDAE, GAAN, CoLA, CONAD) are substantially weaker, with the best unsupervised method CoLA achieving only 0.5618 AUC-ROC on Photo versus DEMO's 0.9023. On large-scale datasets, DEMO edges out NSReg by 1.16% AUC-ROC on Yelp and 2.33% on ogbn-arxiv, while several baselines (TAM, OCGNN, ANO-S, DOM-S) fail entirely with out-of-memory errors on ogbn-arxiv and ogbn-mag. The GNN+OpenMax baseline, which adapts classic open-set classification to GNNs, is consistently outperformed, confirming that the multi-sample mixup and energy-gradient mechanisms provide meaningful gains over simple open-set scoring.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEMO builds on the multi-sample mixing strategy from M-mix (Zhang et al., KDD 2022), repurposing it to synthesize diverse anomaly representations rather than hard negatives for contrastive learning, and on energy-based influence functions (Chen et al., ICLR 2025; Han et al., 2022) to derive a gradient-driven sample reweighting mechanism. It departs from the closest prior open-set GAD method, NSReg (Wang et al., ICLR 2023), which enforces compact normal-node representations via structural regularization, by instead emphasizing the role of anomaly samples in shaping decision boundaries and explicitly addressing the compounding challenges of label scarcity and severe class imbalance. The paper also draws on semi-supervised pseudo-labeling with adaptive thresholds (FlexMatch, FreeMatch) but introduces a class-aware, memory-bank-guided variant tailored to the extreme imbalance in binary GAD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Open-set GAD is a practically critical problem because real-world anomaly types evolve and are never fully captured in training data, yet no dedicated benchmark exists for this setting—the paper simulates it by partitioning class distributions, which limits external reproducibility. The consistent superiority across six datasets and multiple supervision levels is encouraging, but absolute performance on large-scale graphs (e.g., AUC-ROC ≈ 0.50 on ogbn-mag) remains far from deployable, and the energy-gradient mechanism's computational cost (requiring Hessian approximations) may limit scalability. Open questions include whether the framework extends to edge-level or subgraph-level anomalies, whether the mixup strategy generalizes beyond node-level embeddings, and whether a true open-set GAD benchmark with naturally occurring unseen anomaly types would yield different conclusions.
