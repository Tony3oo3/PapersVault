## Problem

Autoregressive long video generation suffers from progressive error accumulation (drifting) because training assumes clean inputs while inference conditions on the model's own error-prone outputs. Existing mitigations—noise rescheduling, frame anchoring, and anti-drifting sampling—only alleviate rather than correct these errors, capping videos at roughly 10 seconds to 1 minute with homogeneous, repetitive scenes. The paper identifies the root cause as a training-test hypothesis gap and asks whether a model can be taught to actively detect and repair its own accumulated errors.

## Input / Output

SVI takes a reference image (or five motion frames for the SVI-Film variant) plus a text prompt or a sequential prompt stream (one prompt per 5-second clip) as primary input, with optional audio embeddings (cross-attention) for talking or skeleton tokens (element-wise addition) for dancing. Generation is autoregressive: each new clip conditions on the last frames of the previous clip, and the prompt stream enables scene-to-scene transitions. The base architecture is Wan2.1-I2V-14B at 480P, and outputs are non-looping videos scalable from 50 seconds to 15+ minutes with no additional inference cost beyond standard per-clip sampling.

## Method

The core contribution is Error-Recycling Fine-Tuning (ERFT), a closed-loop training procedure that repurposes the DiT's own self-generated errors as supervisory signals. Concretely, (i) historical errors are probabilistically injected into clean video latents, noise, and reference images to simulate the degradation seen at inference; (ii) the model's predicted velocity is integrated one step forward and backward to approximate the error-embedded output, from which residuals yield three error terms (E_vid, E_noi, E_img); (iii) these errors are stored in a replay memory banked by discretized timestep (Z=500 per slot) and selectively resampled for new training inputs, with E_img drawn across all timesteps to mimic cross-clip accumulation. The training objective (Eq. 6) supervises the model to predict an 'error-recycled velocity' that always points toward the clean latent regardless of the corrupted current state, effectively teaching the DiT to correct its own mistakes. Only LoRA adapters (rank 128) are trained on 300–6K short videos, making the approach data-light and drop-in compatible with existing video DiTs.

## Results

On the 50-second Consistent Video Generation benchmark, SVI-Shot achieves 98.13% subject consistency and 98.19% background consistency, outperforming the next-best FramePack (93.08%/94.72%) by over 5 points. When extended to 250-second ultra-long generation, SVI-Shot maintains 97.50% subject consistency (only a 0.63% drop), whereas FramePack degrades by 13.71% and Wan 2.1 by 7.03%. In the Creative Video Generation setting with prompt-stream-driven scene transitions, SVI-Shot reaches 93.52% subject consistency and 62.81% imaging quality, while all prior long-video methods fail to produce coherent scene transitions. For conditional generation, SVI-Talk achieves FVD 390 (vs. MultiTalk's 520) and SVI-Dance achieves PSNR 20.01 / FVD 299 (vs. UniAnimate-DiT's 18.97 / 337).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SVI-Shot decisively outperforms all four long-video baselines (Wan 2.1, StreamingT2V, HistoryGuidance, FramePack) on subject and background consistency across all four settings, with the largest margins in ultra-long generation where FramePack drops 13.71% in subject consistency while SVI-Shot drops only 0.63%. FramePack is the closest competitor on consistency metrics but suffers from near-zero dynamic degree (0.00%) in ultra-long settings, indicating frozen/repetitive motion, whereas SVI-Shot maintains 21.43% dynamic degree. HistoryGuidance and StreamingT2V show the most severe degradation at 250 seconds (subject consistency falling below 70%), confirming their inability to handle extended autoregressive generation. In the creative (prompt-stream) setting, all prior methods fail to produce coherent scene transitions, while SVI-Shot achieves 93.52% subject consistency with 55.63% dynamic degree. For conditional generation, SVI-Talk surpasses MultiTalk on FVD (390 vs. 520) and Sync-C (6.12 vs. 1.26), and SVI-Dance outperforms UniAnimate-DiT on PSNR (20.01 vs. 18.97), SSIM (0.71 vs. 0.69), and FVD (299 vs. 337), demonstrating that the error-recycling approach transfers effectively to domain-specific controls.

## Relation to Prior Work

SVI builds on the Wan 2.1 video Diffusion Transformer as its base architecture but departs fundamentally from the three dominant trends in long-video generation—noise rescheduling (FreeNoise, Rolling Diffusion), frame anchoring (StreamingT2V, MovieDreamer), and error-robust sampling (FramePack, Self-Forcing)—by reframing the problem as a training-test hypothesis gap rather than a pure error-accumulation issue. Instead of handcrafted anti-drifting mechanisms that merely alleviate degradation, SVI introduces Error-Recycling Fine-Tuning, which closes the loop by injecting the DiT's own self-generated errors back into training as supervisory signals, effectively repurposing the model's restoration capacity for generation. The bidirectional one-step integration for error curation and the timestep-discretized replay memory are novel engineering contributions that make this closed-loop training efficient and practical.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SVI's error-recycling principle is potentially generalizable beyond video to any autoregressive generative setting (LLMs, image generation) where the train-test distribution mismatch causes compounding errors, and the paper explicitly draws this connection to hallucination in long-context LLMs. Practical limitations remain: the method is validated only on a single 14B base model with small-scale training data (6K clips), style shifts can be misinterpreted as errors to 'correct,' and real-time streaming generation is not yet supported. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger model families, whether the replay memory design generalizes to non-flow-matching schedulers, and how to handle identity persistence across scene transitions in creative mode without explicit long-term memory.
