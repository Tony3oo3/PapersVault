## Problem

Existing LLM agent benchmarks are static or synchronous: environments only change when the agent acts, and evaluation typically ignores intermediate steps. This leaves critical real-world challenges—handling asynchronous events, operating under temporal constraints, adapting to noise, and collaborating with other agents—largely untested. Gaia2 addresses this gap by introducing asynchronous, event-driven environments where the world evolves independently of agent actions, paired with action-level verification that makes the benchmark directly usable for reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR).

## Input / Output

Each scenario provides a user task instruction within a Mobile universe (a smartphone-like environment with 12 apps, 101 tools, and 400K–800K tokens of structured/unstructured content), and the agent receives environment notifications at configurable verbosity levels (low/medium/high) that gate which events are observable. The agent produces a trajectory of tool calls (read and write), and the ARE Verifier outputs a pass/fail signal by matching every state-changing write action against an oracle DAG, checking four dimensions: consistency (exact argument matching for rigid fields, LLM rubric for flexible ones), causality (dependency ordering), timing (tolerance windows), and completeness (all oracle actions matched). Read actions are unconstrained, so the verifier is goal-oriented rather than path-optimal, and the benchmark comprises 1,120 scenarios across 7 capability splits (Execution, Search, Ambiguity, Adaptability, Time, Noise, Agent2Agent).

## Method

The core technical contribution is the ARE (Agents Research Environments) platform, an event-driven simulation framework where time advances continuously and independently of the agent, enabling evaluation of temporal awareness and responsiveness. ARE introduces five abstractions—apps (stateful APIs with typed read/write tools), environments (collections of apps plus a time manager and rules), events (timestamped, DAG-organized actions and state changes), notifications (a configurable observability layer), and scenarios (initial state plus a DAG of scheduled events with verification logic)—that together support asynchronous, multi-turn, multi-agent evaluation. The ARE Verifier is a standalone, reusable component that performs fine-grained credit assignment by matching agent write actions to oracle actions via hard checks (exact IDs), soft checks (LLM rubric for text content), causal ordering, and timing windows, achieving 0.98 agreement and 0.99 precision on 450 hand-labeled trajectories. Agents are evaluated with a model-agnostic ReAct scaffold augmented with pre-step hooks (injecting queued notifications) and post-step hooks (checking termination), and the benchmark's Noise and Agent2Agent splits apply environment-level modifiers (random tool failures, app replacement by sub-agents) to base scenarios without requiring new annotations.

## Results

On Gaia2 (1,120 scenarios across 7 capability splits), GPT-5 (high) achieves the strongest overall pass@1 of 42.1%, followed by Claude-4-Sonnet Thinking (37.8%) and Claude-4-Sonnet (34.8%). Among open-source models, Kimi-K2 leads with 20.1% pass@1, while Llama 4 Maverick and GPT-4o trail at 7.4%. Notably, no model dominates across all capabilities: GPT-5 (high) scores 0% on the Time split due to inference latency, while Gemini-2.5-Pro (7.3%) and Claude-4-Sonnet (8.1%) achieve the best Time scores, and Execution/Search are the easiest splits (GPT-5 high reaches 69.2% and 79.6% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-5 (high) leads overall at 42.1% pass@1 with an 8-point margin over Claude-4-Sonnet (34.8%), but it scores 0% on the Time split due to long inference latency, whereas Gemini-2.5-Pro (7.3%) and Claude-4-Sonnet (8.1%) achieve the best Time scores. Claude-4-Sonnet Thinking (37.8%) outperforms GPT-5 (low) (34.6%) on Execution (62.1% vs. 52.7%) and Adaptability (42.1% vs. 30.2%), while GPT-5 (low) leads on Search (64.2% vs. 60.6%) and Ambiguity (39.6% vs. 27.3%). Among open-source models, Kimi-K2 (20.1%) substantially outperforms Qwen3-235B Thinking (15.7%) and GPT-OSS-120B (13.7%), particularly on Adaptability (24.0% vs. 16.2% and 10.6%). Gemini-2.5-Pro (25.8%) is the strongest model that balances Time performance with overall accuracy, while Grok-4 (15.7%) is an outlier with very high Search (57.5%) but near-zero Time (0.0%) and low Adaptability (4.4%). The paper also shows that in Agent2Agent settings, heterogeneous teams (Claude main + Llama app-agents) achieve 18.3% pass@1 versus 8.5% for all-Llama, indicating that stronger executors at the app-agent level provide independent gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

Gaia2 extends the GAIA benchmark's 'simple for humans, hard for AI' task philosophy from web-only search to a full consumer mobile environment with 12 apps and 101 tools, while departing from the synchronous paradigm of prior agent benchmarks (AppWorld, ToolSandbox, τ-bench, VendingBench) by introducing asynchronous, event-driven environments where the world evolves independently of agent actions. The ARE framework subsumes and generalizes existing benchmarks (GAIA, τ-bench, BFCL-v3, VendingBench) under a unified event-DAG abstraction, and the write-action verifier extends ToolSandbox's milestone-based intermediate checks to full action-level verification with causal and temporal constraints. The Agent2Agent split operationalizes Google's recently proposed A2A protocol for interoperable multi-agent systems, and the ReAct scaffold follows Yao et al. (2023b) with minimal extensions for asynchronous notification injection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Gaia2 is the first benchmark to simultaneously stress-test temporal awareness, noise robustness, ambiguity resolution, and multi-agent collaboration under a single verifiable evaluation framework directly applicable to RLVR, filling a critical gap between static benchmarks and real deployment conditions. The inverse scaling finding on the Time split—where reasoning-heavy models like GPT-5 (high) score 0% in default mode but 34.4% in instant mode—challenges the assumption that more test-time compute universally improves agent performance and points toward adaptive compute strategies as a necessary next step. The ARE framework's generality (validated by reimplementing five existing benchmarks) and the verifier's 0.99 precision position it as potential infrastructure for the next generation of agent training pipelines, though the sim2real gap, single-threaded orchestration limitations, and the observed verifier hacking (agents embedding code to fool LLM judges) remain open challenges.
