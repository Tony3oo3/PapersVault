## Problem

Existing 3D LMMs embed 3D positions as fixed spatial prompts within visual features, which works for static backgrounds but fails to capture temporally varying dynamic objects such as position shifts and deformations. These methods apply a unified spatial representation to the entire scene, neglecting the temporal dimension where objects and backgrounds exhibit fundamentally different motion patterns. The paper asks how to extend LMMs to understand both static and dynamic elements in 4D (spatiotemporal) physical scenes.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view video sequences (typically 6–12 seconds) along with pre-computed camera poses (SfM), depth maps (MVS), and intrinsic parameters to construct 4D coordinate tensors [x,y,z,t] via geometric projection from 2D pixels to world coordinates. Text instructions containing explicit 3D position and timestamp values are tokenized and spatiotemporally encoded. Outputs are natural-language responses that include 4D coordinate annotations (e.g., 'At 1.5s, it is at [11.15, 2.08, -7.32, 12.45, 0.30, -7.15]'), dense captions, QA answers, and spatiotemporal grounding results with separate spatial (SAcc) and temporal (TAcc) accuracy metrics.

## Method

LLaVA-4D introduces a three-stage architecture built on LLaVA-1.5-7B with a CLIP-ViT-L-336px vision encoder. First, a dynamic-aware 4D coordinate encoding applies learnable Fourier features to 3D position and 1D time, where the temporal encoding is modulated by optical flow (via softmax) to capture motion magnitude rather than fixed frame-rate steps. Second, a spatiotemporal-disentangled vision embedding decomposes multi-view visual features into spatial components (cross-view correlation at the same timestamp) and temporal components (same-view correlation across adjacent frames), then fuses them with the 4D coordinate embeddings through a cross-attention mechanism with a learned gating coefficient. Third, coordinate-aligned language embedding projects the fused visual features into language space and applies the same PE/TE encodings to textual position and time tokens, enabling the LLM to reason over aligned spatiotemporal representations. Training proceeds in three stages: content alignment on 2D/3D data, spatiotemporal coordinate alignment on visual grounding data, and 4D instruction fine-tuning on the newly constructed Chat4D dataset (224.6K samples with spatiotemporal coordinate annotations).

## Results

On the 3D benchmarks, LLaVA-4D achieves 85.3 C@0.5 on Scan2Cap, 97.8 C on ScanQA, 54.3 F1@0.5 on Multi3DRefer, and 53.2 SAcc@0.5 on ScanRef, outperforming the strongest 3D baseline (Video-3D LLM) across all four. On the 4D Chat4D benchmark, LLaVA-4D reaches 93.5 C, 17.2 B-4, 58.9 SAcc@0.5, and 54.6 TAcc, substantially exceeding the best 3D method (Video-3D LLM: 89.4 C, 52.8 SAcc, no TAcc reported). On VSI-Bench for spatial reasoning, LLaVA-4D attains a 48.6 average score, marginally surpassing Spatial-MLLM (48.4). On temporal understanding, LLaVA-4D achieves 58.9 SAcc and 54.6 TAcc, dramatically outperforming LLaVA-ST (15.2/7.3) and Grounded-VideoLLM (9.4/5.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLaVA-4D outperforms all 13 compared baselines on every metric where both methods report results. On 3D benchmarks, it surpasses the strongest 3D baseline Video-3D LLM by 1.5 points on Scan2Cap C@0.5 (85.3 vs. 83.8), 1.6 on Multi3DRefer F1 (54.3 vs. 52.7), and 1.5 on ScanRef SAcc (53.2 vs. 51.7). On the 4D Chat4D benchmark, the gap widens considerably: LLaVA-4D leads Video-3D LLM by 4.1 C (93.5 vs. 89.4) and 6.1 SAcc (58.9 vs. 52.8), while also reporting TAcc (54.6) which no 3D baseline can produce. On VSI-Bench, LLaVA-4D (48.6 avg) narrowly beats Spatial-MLLM (48.4 avg), with advantages in Obj.Count (68.2 vs. 65.3), Abs.Dist. (35.3 vs. 34.8), and Room Size (49.6 vs. 45.1). On temporal understanding (Table 3), LLaVA-4D achieves 58.9 SAcc and 54.6 TAcc, exceeding LLaVA-ST by 43.7 and 47.3 points respectively, and Grounded-VideoLLM by 49.5 and 49.5 points, while maintaining comparable tIoU (61.5 vs. 58.7 and 47.0). GPT-4o with Coarse Correspondences reports only ScanQA (87.0 C) and no 4D metrics, making direct comparison limited.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D LMM paradigm (3D-LLM, LLaVA-3D, Video-3D LLM) that embeds 3D positions as fixed spatial prompts into visual features, by adding a temporal dimension to create a dynamic-aware 4D coordinate encoding. It builds on LLaVA-1.5-7B as the base architecture and adopts learnable Fourier features for coordinate encoding, but departs from prior 3D methods in two key ways: (1) incorporating optical flow into temporal encoding to capture motion patterns, and (2) disentangling visual features into spatial and temporal components before fusing with 4D coordinates via cross-attention. Unlike task-specific 4D vision-language models (4D LangSplat, VG4D), this work frames 4D scene understanding as a general LMM capability across captioning, QA, and grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of Chat4D (224.6K samples with spatiotemporal coordinate annotations) fills a notable gap in 4D vision-language benchmarks, and the spatiotemporal disentanglement insight—that spatial and temporal components of visual features are more discriminative for separating objects from background than raw visual features—offers a reusable design principle for dynamic scene representation. A key limitation is the reliance on external SfM/MVS pipelines for 4D coordinate construction, which reduces end-to-end efficiency; the authors note that end-to-end geometric models (e.g., MonST3R) could address this. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences, more complex multi-object dynamics, and whether the cross-attention fusion generalizes beyond the tested scene types.
