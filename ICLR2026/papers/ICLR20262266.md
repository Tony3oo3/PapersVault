## Problem

While diffusion models progressively denoise random noise into images, the temporal dynamics of when semantic concepts (e.g., age, style, weather) become irreversibly locked into the generation trajectory remain poorly understood. Existing interpretability approaches—cross-attention attribution maps, concept bottleneck models, and sparse autoencoders—localize concepts spatially or map latents to interpretable directions but largely overlook the temporal dimension, often require additional training, and are not model-agnostic. This paper asks: at which timestep does a concept become committed to the denoising trajectory such that later interventions can no longer alter it?

## Input / Output

The framework takes a neutral base prompt (e.g., 'a realistic photo of a person'), a concept-augmented prompt (e.g., 'a realistic photo of an old person'), and an intervention timestep t_s as input, and produces a CIS curve—a probability function over the normalized timestep axis [0,1] indicating the likelihood that the target concept persists in the final image. Concept presence is determined by a Large Vision-Language Model (Qwen-VL-3B) in a VQA setup returning binary yes/no labels, which are averaged across 100 random seeds per concept-prompt pair to suppress seed-level noise. For the editing application, the CIS-informed timestep selects when to switch prompts, yielding an edited image that balances content preservation with semantic alignment, evaluated via CLIP_img, CLIP_txt, and CLIP_dir metrics.

## Method

PCI is a training-free, model-agnostic probing mechanism: generation begins under a base prompt P_b, and at a chosen timestep t_s the text conditioning is switched to a concept prompt P_c, after which denoising continues to completion. The resulting image is evaluated by an LVLM via VQA to determine whether the target concept is present, and repeating this across all timesteps and multiple seeds yields the Concept Insertion Success (CIS) curve. Quantitative analysis uses band-limited crossing times τ_q (the earliest normalized timestep where CIS reaches probability q) and bandwidth W_{70→50} to characterize insertion timing and transition sharpness across models and concept categories. Applied to five state-of-the-art models (SD 2.1, SDXL, SD 3.5, PixArt-alpha, FLUX.1-dev) over approximately 800 concept-context pairs, PCI reveals that global factors (style, weather, color) lock in early, human attributes mid-trajectory, and out-of-distribution concepts unusually early, with rectified-flow models inserting concepts earlier than diffusion-based models. For text-driven editing, CIS-guided prompt switching within the [0.5, 0.7] probability window outperforms NTI+P2P and Stable Flow on all three CLIP-based metrics, achieving a superior preservation-semantic tradeoff without requiring attention maps or segmentation modules.

## Results

On the text-driven image editing benchmark (88 concept pairs, 1760 images), PCI-guided editing at τ₅₀ achieves CLIP_img = 0.8885, CLIP_txt = 0.2236, and CLIP_dir = 0.1387, outperforming both NTI+P2P and Stable Flow across all three metrics. The CIS-guided window [0.5, 0.7] consistently yields the best preservation–semantic-strength trade-off: τ₇₀ pushes CLIP_txt to 0.2341 and CLIP_dir to 0.1678 but drops CLIP_img to 0.8353, while τ₃₀ preserves content (CLIP_img = 0.9343) but fails to insert the concept (CLIP_txt = 0.2125). Across the full CIS sweep, CLIP_img decreases monotonically with earlier intervention while CLIP_txt and CLIP_dir increase, confirming the temporal insertion dynamics revealed by the CIS curves.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PCI-guided editing against two strong training-free baselines: NTI+P2P (Mokady et al., CVPR 2023) and Stable Flow (Avrahami et al., CVPR 2025). At the recommended τ₅₀ intervention point, PCI outperforms NTI+P2P on all three metrics (CLIP_img 0.8885 vs. 0.8666, CLIP_txt 0.2236 vs. 0.2215, CLIP_dir 0.1387 vs. 0.0979) and Stable Flow on all three (CLIP_img 0.8885 vs. 0.8324, CLIP_txt 0.2236 vs. 0.2152, CLIP_dir 0.1387 vs. 0.0631). The advantage over Stable Flow is particularly pronounced on CLIP_dir (0.1387 vs. 0.0631, more than 2×), indicating that PCI edits produce semantic shifts far more aligned with the intended prompt change. NTI+P2P is the closer competitor on CLIP_txt (0.2215 vs. 0.2236), but PCI still leads on both content preservation and directional consistency. The paper notes that PCI-τ₇₀ further extends the CLIP_txt and CLIP_dir lead (0.2341 and 0.1678) at the cost of reduced preservation, confirming that the [0.5, 0.7] CIS window is the optimal operating range for balanced editing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant static-interpretability paradigm for diffusion models (attribution maps, concept bottleneck layers, sparse autoencoders) that answers *where* concepts appear in an image, by introducing a temporal behavioral probe: Prompt-Conditioned Intervention (PCI), which simply switches the text prompt at a chosen denoising timestep and measures whether the inserted concept survives to the final image. It is conceptually adjacent to prior dynamic-interpretability work (Wu et al. 2022 on content/style disentanglement over time; Tinaz et al. 2025 and Huang et al. 2025 on timestep-resolved autoencoders) but is training-free, annotation-free, and model-agnostic, requiring no access to internal representations. For the editing application, it positions itself against inversion-based methods (NTI+P2P) and layer-selection methods (Stable Flow), replacing their heuristics with a CIS-informed timestep selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PCI/CIS provides a lightweight, universally applicable diagnostic that turns diffusion time into an interpretable axis, revealing that concept-locking timing is not a fixed property of the concept but is jointly determined by model architecture (rectified-flow models lock earlier than U-Net/DiT diffusion models), context alignment (OOD pairings like 'horse in a living room' lock earlier than in-distribution ones), and concept category. This has direct practical implications: practitioners editing with SD 3.5 or FLUX must intervene earlier than with SD 2.1/SDXL, and the optimal editing window [0.5, 0.7] CIS is a simple, actionable guideline. Open questions include whether the framework extends to video diffusion, whether CIS can be computed more cheaply (the paper notes runtime scales with number of intervention points), and whether the context-dependence findings can be exploited for more targeted, context-aware editing strategies.
