## Problem

Existing neural combinatorial optimization methods are anchored in construction or improvement paradigms that treat solutions as monolithic objects, leaving the rich localized decision patterns within high-quality solutions largely unexplored and resulting in inefficient data utilization. The paper asks whether combinatorial optimization can adopt a self-supervised pretraining paradigm—akin to masked auto-encoding in NLP and vision—to enable scalable, fine-grained representation learning from reference solutions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264537/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a graph instance (node features and a 0-1 adjacency matrix) plus, during the correction phase, a partial solution indicating which variables are already committed. It outputs a heatmap of selection probabilities over all candidate variables (edges for TSP/CVRP, nodes for MIS), which a greedy selection function converts into a feasible binary solution vector. For variable-cardinality problems like MIS, the model additionally estimates solution size by summing predicted probabilities, and a completion step enforces feasibility if the final iterate is infeasible.

## Method

MaskCO reframes learning to optimize as solution-level self-supervised learning: during training, a random fraction of an optimal solution is masked and the model is trained via binary cross-entropy to predict the full solution, turning one (instance, solution) pair into exponentially many (instance, partial-solution) examples that force the model to internalize fine-grained local decision patterns. The architecture is an encoder-decoder Transformer where the adjacency matrix is added directly to attention logits (QK^T + A) without scaling, with SwiGLU activations and pre-norm. At inference, a multi-step decoding schedule (K steps with a linear growth function γ) progressively commits the highest-confidence variables, followed by a mask-and-reconstruct correction loop that iteratively masks a fraction p of the current solution and regenerates the masked region, enabling local-search-like refinement over T total forward passes. The masking rate p controls the exploration-exploitation trade-off, and the framework is modular—selection functions, schedules, and correction strategies can be swapped without retraining.

## Results

On TSP, MaskCO (T=320) achieves near-zero optimality gaps of 0.0000%, 0.0020%, and 0.0071% on TSP-100/500/1000 in just 8s/6s/18s, reducing the gap by >99% and achieving a 10× speedup over the previous SOTA neural solver (Fast T2T: 0.42% gap, 18.3 min on TSP-1000). On CVRP-1000, MaskCO (T=10240) reaches a 0.438% gap in 7m33s, a 34.9× speedup over NeuOpt (0.103% gap, 4h49m). On MIS, MaskCO (T=1k) achieves 0.49% gap on RB graphs and 3.73% on ER graphs, a 72% average improvement over prior methods with a 3× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaskCO decisively outperforms all neural baselines on TSP: against the previous SOTA Fast T2T, it reduces the TSP-1000 gap from 0.42% to 0.0071% (98.3% reduction) while being 60× faster (18s vs 18.3 min); against DIFUSCO the gap drops from 1.31% to 0.0071% (99.5% reduction). On CVRP, MaskCO (T=10240) achieves 0.086% gap on CVRP-100 in 8m17s, beating NeuOpt's 0.103% gap (4h49m) by 34.9× in speed, and outperforms LEHD (1.859% gap, 1.83h) on CVRP-1000 by 76% in gap. On MIS, MaskCO (T=1k) achieves 0.49% gap on RB graphs vs Fast T2T's 2.89% (83% improvement) and 3.73% on ER vs Fast T2T's 9.34% (60% improvement). On TSPLIB generalization, MaskCO reduces the gap from 0.28% (Fast T2T) to 0.033% on TSPLIB50-200 and from 0.93% to 0.115% on TSPLIB101-1000. MaskCO matches the exact optimality of classical solvers (Concorde, LKH-3) on TSP-100/500 while being orders of magnitude faster, and its learned representations transfer to alternative decoding (AR, bidirectional AR, consistency fine-tuning) without retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaskCO transplants the masked auto-encoding paradigm from NLP (BERT) and vision (MAE, MaskGIT) into Neural Combinatorial Optimization, reframing solution generation as a self-supervised reconstruction task on reference solutions. It departs from the dominant construction-based (autoregressive, non-autoregressive) and improvement-based (RL local search) paradigms in NCO, as well as from diffusion-based solvers (DIFUSCO, T2T, Fast T2T) that model solution generation as a denoising process. Rather than building on a specific prior CO architecture, the paper repurposes the encoder-decoder Transformer with a binary cross-entropy objective over masked positions, arguing that the training paradigm—learning fine-grained local decision patterns from partial solutions—is the key differentiator rather than the generative model family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The >99% optimality gap reduction over prior SOTA neural solvers on TSP, combined with 10× speedups and consistent gains on CVRP and MIS, suggests that self-supervised masked generation may become a foundational training paradigm for NCO, analogous to how BERT reshaped NLP. The demonstrated transferability of learned representations to alternative decoding strategies (autoregressive, bidirectional, consistency) and the optimal-solution-free self-training paradigm (where the student outperforms its 2-opt teacher) open a path toward scalable, label-efficient CO solvers. Open questions include whether the paradigm extends to problems with highly structured or hierarchical constraints beyond those tested, how the correction mechanism scales to very large instances, and whether the current reliance on high-quality reference solutions (or expensive pseudo-labeling) limits practical adoption.
