## Problem

Vision-Language Models face severe computational bottlenecks from long visual token sequences, and existing token pruning methods rely on attention scores that the paper shows to be unreliable: [CLS] attention in vision encoders suffers from attention sink (over-focusing on background), while LLM attention exhibits position bias that corrupts importance estimates. The paper identifies these failures through systematic analysis and asks whether more effective, learnable pruning criteria can achieve aggressive token reduction without significant accuracy loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262257/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (processed into 576–2880 visual tokens by a frozen vision encoder) paired with a text query, and produces a text answer after reducing the visual token budget to as few as 32 tokens (94.4% reduction). At inference, the token budget is split 3:1 between the two pruning stages (e.g., 129 tokens after the encoder, 43 after the LLM's 12th layer), with 10% of the first-stage budget reserved for diversity tokens. The framework is plug-and-play across LLaVA-v1.5, LLaVA-NeXT, Video-LLaVA, and Qwen2.5-VL, requiring only 0.53M additional parameters.

## Method

LearnPruner is a two-stage pruning framework. In Stage 1 (post-encoder), a lightweight MLP-based Learnable Pruning Module (LPM) predicts per-token importance scores via binary classification, trained end-to-end with a Straight-Through Estimator and a pruning loss that encourages minimal token retention; a greedy max-min cosine-similarity procedure then adds diversity tokens to preserve background context. In Stage 2 (LLM layer 12), text-to-vision attention—shown to resist the position bias that corrupts vision-to-vision attention—is averaged over query tokens to select the top-k most query-relevant tokens. The key insight is that [CLS] attention is an unreliable proxy for foreground salience, while middle-layer text attention provides robust query-aware guidance, and that a learnable criterion outperforms any fixed attention heuristic under tight token budgets.

## Results

On LLaVA-v1.5-7B, LearnPruner (0.53M learnable params) preserves 94.8% relative accuracy with only 32 vision tokens retained (94.4% reduction), 96.9% at 64 tokens, and 98.5% at 128 tokens. On LLaVA-NeXT-7B (2,880 tokens), it retains 97.5% accuracy at 320 tokens and 94.0% at 160 tokens. Efficiency gains include 3.2× total inference speedup and 8.9× KV cache reduction on LLaVA-NeXT-7B, and 2.3× total speedup with 6.8× KV cache reduction on LLaVA-v1.5-7B at 32 tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LearnPruner (0.53M params) decisively outperforms all training-free baselines at aggressive pruning budgets: at 32 tokens it achieves 94.8% relative accuracy vs. FastV (58.6%), SparseVLM (77.5%), VisionZip (87.8%), DART (88.0%), VisPruner (89.7%), and DivPrune (90.5%). Against the strongest training-based competitor TwigVLM (610M params), LearnPruner trails by only 0.5% at 128 tokens (98.5% vs. 99.0%) but leads by 0.9% at 64 tokens (96.9% vs. 96.0%), while using 1,150× fewer learnable parameters. Compared to VisionZip‡ (20.9M params), LearnPruner wins at all three budgets (98.5% vs. 97.3%, 96.9% vs. 95.1%, 94.8% vs. not reported) with 40× fewer parameters. On LLaVA-NeXT-7B, LearnPruner leads all baselines at every budget, reaching 99.3% at 640 tokens and 94.0% at 160 tokens, outperforming TwigVLM (99.1% and not reported) and VisionZip‡ (97.5% and 93.3%). On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, LearnPruner maintains 94.1% relative accuracy at 88.9% token reduction while FastV drops to 71.4%, confirming cross-architecture generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

LearnPruner builds on the learnable token sparsification mechanism from DynamicViT (Rao et al., 2021), repurposing its STE-based binary classification for VLM visual token pruning. It departs from the dominant attention-score-based pruning paradigm (FastV, SparseVLM, VisPruner, VisionZip) by demonstrating that [CLS] attention in vision encoders is unreliable due to attention sink, and that shallow-layer LLM attention is biased by positional encoding. The two-stage design—learnable importance prediction after the vision encoder followed by text-attention-guided selection in the LLM's middle layer—synthesizes ideas from both encoder-side and decoder-side pruning literature into a unified framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear empirical diagnosis of why existing attention-based pruning criteria fail (attention sink in ViT, attention shift in LLMs), which is valuable context for the broader VLM efficiency community. The practical result—95% accuracy retention at 5.5% token budget with only 0.53M trainable parameters—makes it a strong candidate for deployment in resource-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the learnable module generalizes across architectures without retraining, how the method behaves at even more aggressive pruning ratios (below 32 tokens), and whether the heuristic diversity-token selection can be made fully learned. The paper also acknowledges failure cases on complex scenes where the constrained token budget causes irreversible information loss.
