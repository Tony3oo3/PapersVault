## Problem

Existing sequential disentanglement methods rely on VAEs or GANs with multiple auxiliary loss terms (up to five hyperparameters for balancing), struggle to produce high-quality samples on real-world data, and lack a probabilistic formalization within diffusion-based modeling. Additionally, no established evaluation protocol exists for assessing sequential disentanglement on high-resolution, real-world visual data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262976/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts arbitrary-length sequences of continuous-valued elements: video frames (64×64 or 256×256 RGB, compressed via a pre-trained VQ-VAE into 4×32×32 latents), audio mel-spectrograms (80-dim, 68-frame segments), or time series (10–672 timesteps, 6–10 features). It outputs a single static latent vector shared across all frames plus a per-frame dynamic latent vector (typically 2–64 dim), from which the full sequence can be reconstructed or recombined with factors from another sequence for swap tasks. For zero-shot evaluation, the model trained on one dataset (e.g., VoxCeleb) is applied directly to an unseen dataset (e.g., MUG or CelebV-HQ) without fine-tuning.

## Method

DiffSDA formulates sequential disentanglement as a joint diffusion process with two components: a dependent prior over the static factor s₀ and dynamic factors d₀^{1:V} (modeled by a latent DDIM), and a conditional denoising process p(x₀^τ | x_T^τ, s₀, d₀^τ) that reconstructs each frame given both factors. A sequential semantic encoder (U-Net or MLP backbone + LSTM) extracts s₀ and d₀^{1:V} from the input, while an EDM-style stochastic decoder with AdaGN conditioning on z₀^τ = (s₀, d₀^τ) performs denoising in only 63 NFEs. The entire model is trained with a single score-matching loss (no KL, contrastive, or mutual-information terms), where disentanglement emerges structurally: the shared static factor cannot encode per-frame dynamics, and the low-dimensional dynamic factors cannot encode identity. The dependent (rather than independent) prior over s₀ and d₀^{1:V} yields a ~13% FVD improvement over independent modeling, and a VQ-VAE compression stage enables handling of 256×256 video frames.

## Results

On the primary conditional swap task across four video datasets, DiffSDA achieves the best AED (static preservation) on 3 of 4 datasets (e.g., 0.540 on CelebV-HQ vs. 0.631 for SPYL and 0.751 for DBSE) and the best AKD (dynamic preservation) on all four (e.g., 6.932 on CelebV-HQ vs. 39.16 for SPYL). Reconstruction quality is orders of magnitude better, with MSE of 3e-7 on MUG versus 0.001 for both SPYL and DBSE. On audio (TIMIT), DiffSDA achieves a disentanglement gap of 42.29% (Static EER 4.43%, Dynamic EER 46.72%), surpassing DBSE's 31.11%. On time series, DiffSDA attains AUPRC 0.50 and AUROC 0.87 on PhysioNet mortality prediction, outperforming DBSE (0.47/0.86) and the supervised baseline (0.44/0.80).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffSDA outperforms the two primary sequential disentanglement baselines—SPYL (Naiman et al., 2023) and DBSE (Berman et al., 2024)—across nearly all video, audio, and time-series benchmarks. On video swap, DiffSDA reduces AKD by 5–8× versus SPYL (e.g., 6.932 vs. 39.16 on CelebV-HQ) and by 3–5× versus DBSE, while achieving the best AED on 3 of 4 datasets (narrowly losing to DBSE on TaiChi-HD by 0.001). On audio, DiffSDA's disentanglement gap of 42.29% on TIMIT exceeds DBSE (31.11%) and SPYL (29.81%) by 11–12 percentage points. On time series, DiffSDA surpasses DBSE, SPYL, and GLR (Tonekaboni et al., 2022) on all five prediction/classification metrics, and even exceeds the supervised baseline on AUPRC and AUROC. On the MUG judge benchmark (Table 9), DiffSDA is competitive but trails DBSE (86.90%) and SPYL (85.71%) on accuracy (81.15%), though it achieves reconstruction MSE of 2.67e-7 versus ~1.3e-3 for both—orders of magnitude better. Older VAE/GAN baselines (DSVAE, C-DSVAE, MoCoGAN, R-WAE, S3VAE, SKD) are consistently outperformed on all reported metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sequential disentanglement line of work (DSVAE, MoCoGAN, SPYL, DBSE) by replacing the VAE/GAN backbone with a diffusion-based probabilistic framework, providing the first formal probabilistic modeling for diffusion-based sequential disentanglement. It departs from prior methods in two key ways: (1) static and dynamic factors are modeled as interdependent rather than independent, and (2) the entire model is optimized with a single score-matching loss rather than the 2–5 carefully balanced loss terms typical of VAE/GAN-based approaches. The architecture draws on EDM for efficient sampling, LDM/VQ-VAE for handling high-dimensional inputs, and DDIM for latent prior modeling, while the modal-agnostic design (U-Net for video, MLP for audio/time series) contrasts with modal-dependent animation methods like FOM and Animate Anyone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiffSDA establishes that diffusion models can serve as a unified, single-loss framework for sequential disentanglement across modalities, reducing the hyperparameter burden that has plagued VAE/GAN-based methods. The introduction of a new unsupervised evaluation protocol (AED/AKD) and the first zero-shot disentanglement benchmark address longstanding gaps in how the field assesses real-world performance. Open questions remain: the frame-by-frame generation limits spatio-temporal coherence, computational cost is high (3× RTX 4090 for 256×256 video), and the PCA-based multifactor exploration is preliminary rather than a principled disentanglement mechanism. Extending to latent video diffusion models and sensor data are natural next steps.
