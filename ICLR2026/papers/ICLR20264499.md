## Problem

Learning-based TSP solvers suffer from poor generalization and limited scalability when instances deviate from their fixed training distributions, while classical OR heuristics like LKH-3 require domain-specific parameter calibration that is costly and non-trivial. Existing LLM/VLM approaches for TSP either attempt unreliable end-to-end solution construction or rely solely on textual coordinate lists, failing to produce competitive results on large-scale practical instances. The paper asks how pre-trained generative models can be repositioned as practical complements to established OR solvers rather than standalone solvers, enabling scalable optimization across diverse TSP distributions and scales without any task-specific training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a TSP instance defined by 2D node coordinates, renders the current tour as a 20×20-inch gridded image (with adaptive grid spacing based on instance extent), and feeds it alongside structured textual prompts containing meta-instructions, prior selection trajectories (subproblem coordinates, node counts, objective gains, solver runtimes), and the pending subproblem queue to a VLM. The VLM outputs one or more axis-aligned box coordinate quadruples (x_min, x_max, y_min, y_max) as brief text (≤100 tokens). The final output is a refined cyclic node sequence (the optimized tour) and its total Euclidean tour length, with the VLM never directly constructing the tour itself.

## Method

ViTSP is a zero-shot, vision-guided decomposition framework with three modules: (1) LKH-3 warm-start with default parameters, (2) VLM-based visual selection where pre-trained models (GPT-4.1 and o4-mini) identify promising box regions on the rendered tour image, and (3) Concorde exact solving of the extracted subproblems. The key reformulation converts each selected box into a partially asymmetric TSP (free nodes inside the box plus fixed segments outside), then applies the Jonker–Volgenant dummy-node transformation to produce a standard STSP compatible with Concorde. A zoom-in reselection mechanism handles very dense instances by recursively subdividing oversized boxes. The VLM (I/O-intensive) and solver (CPU-intensive) modules run asynchronously on separate cores, coordinated through a shared global solution, trajectory pool, and subproblem queue, with P=8 slave solvers screening candidates and a single master solver updating the global tour. This architecture eliminates all training and data curation while leveraging the VLM's generic spatial reasoning to provide adaptive, instance-specific decomposition heuristics that escape local optima.

## Results

On 33 unseen TSPLIB instances (1k–88k nodes), ViTSP achieves an average optimality gap of 0.24%, outperforming LKH-3 (more RUNS) at 0.31% and Concorde at 0.34%. ViTSP attains the best gap on 20 of 33 instances and reduces LKH-3's gaps by 3.57%–100%, with the advantage growing sharply beyond 4,000 nodes. On the synthetic TSP-10K benchmark, ViTSP reaches a 0.70% gap versus 1.06% for LKH-3 (more RUNs) and 1.81% for SIT, which was specifically trained on TSP-10K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest heuristic baseline LKH-3 (more RUNS, matched runtime), ViTSP achieves lower gaps on 9 of 11 very-large instances, with the largest relative reductions on pla7397 (0.28% vs 0.29%, marginal) and d15112 (0.22% vs 0.84%, a 74% gap reduction). Concorde finds near-optimal solutions on instances up to ~18k nodes but fails entirely on pla33810 and pla85900, where ViTSP still delivers 0.52% and 0.83% gaps. All end-to-end learning-based methods (AM, DIFUSCO, DeepACO) catastrophically fail on OOD TSPLIB instances, with gaps exceeding 50–175%, far worse than the simple FI heuristic (~12–22%). The local-improvement methods SIT and INViT are the closest learning-based competitors (1.42–6.60% and 6.34–11.94% gaps respectively) but still trail ViTSP by 2–10× on most very-large instances. UDC suffers OOM beyond 5,000 nodes, and the LLM-based EoH exhibits extreme variance (3.10% to 961%), confirming that text-only heuristic generation is unreliable at scale. ViTSP's consistent 0.16–1.13% gaps across all 11 very-large instances, without any task-specific training, demonstrate clear superiority over every baseline tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViTSP departs from the dominant paradigm of using LLMs/VLMs as end-to-end TSP solvers (Yang et al. 2023, Elhenawy et al. 2024) or as text-only heuristic designers (Liu et al. 2024), instead repurposing pre-trained VLMs as visual subproblem selectors that guide an off-the-shelf exact solver (Concorde). It also builds on the local-improvement decomposition literature (Li et al. 2021, Cheng et al. 2023, Zheng et al. 2024) but replaces learned selection policies with zero-shot VLM reasoning over rendered tour images. The ATSP-to-STSP dummy-node transformation from Jonker & Volgenant (1983) and Cirasella et al. (2001) is reused to make VLM-selected subproblems compatible with symmetric TSP solvers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ViTSP demonstrates that a general-purpose VLM, with no task-specific training or fine-tuning, can provide decomposition heuristics that consistently outperform the SOTA heuristic solver (LKH-3) on very-large-scale TSPs (up to 88k nodes), reducing optimality gaps by up to 100%. This opens a practical pathway for integrating generative AI into operations research pipelines where abundant training data is unavailable and retraining is infeasible. Key open questions include: how to interpret and control the VLM's decomposition decisions, whether complementary operations (e.g., sequence selection) could further improve results, and how to reduce API costs for the largest instances ($39.40 for pla85900). The framework's reliance on online VLM APIs also raises reproducibility and latency concerns for edge deployment.
