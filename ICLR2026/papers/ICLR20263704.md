## Problem

Existing streaming algorithms for low-rank approximation (e.g., Frequent Directions, online ridge-leverage score sampling) maintain near-optimal rank-k subspaces but can change the output subspace drastically at every step, incurring high recourse. This paper formalizes and studies consistent low-rank approximation: maintaining a (1+ε)-approximation to the optimal rank-k cost at all times while minimizing the total squared subspace distance (recourse) between consecutive outputs, a gap that is critical in practice because subspace changes trigger costly retraining of downstream models.

## Input / Output

The input is a matrix A ∈ R^{n×d} whose rows arrive one-by-one in a stream, with a rank parameter k and accuracy parameter ε; entries may be assumed integer-bounded by M for the main results. At each time t, the algorithm outputs a k×d orthonormal factor matrix V^(t) defining the rank-k subspace, and the total recourse is the sum of ‖P_{V^(t)} − P_{V^(t−1)}‖_F² over all consecutive pairs. The recourse metric is basis-invariant (it measures the squared mass of the projection difference), so it is insensitive to arbitrary rotations within the same subspace.

## Method

For additive ε·‖A^(t)‖_F² error, the algorithm simply recomputes the top-k right singular vectors whenever the squared Frobenius norm grows by a (1+ε) factor, yielding O(k/ε · log(ndM)) recourse. For the harder relative (1+ε)-multiplicative error, the paper first applies online ridge-leverage score sampling (Braverman et al., 2020) to reduce the effective stream length to O(k/ε² · polylog(ndM)) rows, then runs a casework algorithm on the reduced stream: if the bottom √k singular values of the current top-k subspace contribute less than (ε/3)·OPT mass (HEAVY=FALSE), it replaces one bottom singular vector per incoming row, incurring only √k recourse over √k steps before a full recompute; if they contribute significant mass (HEAVY=TRUE), it retains the optimal subspace since Cauchy interlacing guarantees substantial overlap. This yields k^{3/2}/ε² · polylog(ndM) total recourse, and a matching lower bound of Ω(k/ε · log(n/k)) is shown to hold even with full input known in advance.

## Results

On the Landmark dataset (71,952 rows, d=2704) with k=25, the proposed additive-error algorithm (Algorithm 4) achieves a median cost ratio of 1.000 (i.e., matching the optimal low-rank cost) for accuracy parameters (1+ε) ∈ {1.1, 2, 5}, with the median rising to 1.1907 at (1+ε)=100. In terms of recourse, the proposed algorithms achieve total recourse of 100–300 across the 5000-row stream, compared to 121,904 for Frequent Directions — a factor of over 400× improvement. The approximation quality degrades gracefully with larger ε, with mean cost ratios of 1.0016 at (1+ε)=5 and 1.598 at (1+ε)=10, though variance increases substantially at (1+ε)=100 (std dev 51.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its consistent low-rank approximation algorithms against Frequent Directions (FD) by Ghashami et al. (2016), the standard deterministic algorithm for online low-rank approximation. On the Landmark dataset with k=25 and n=5000, FD incurs a total recourse of 121,904, while the proposed algorithms achieve recourse in the range of 100–300 — a factor of over 400× lower. This large gap motivates the paper's central claim that existing online LRA algorithms are not designed for stability and can fail catastrophically on recourse, even for simple inputs where the optimal subspace barely changes. The proposed algorithms also match or nearly match the optimal low-rank approximation cost in practice (median ratio 1.000 for (1+ε) ≤ 5), despite only guaranteeing additive error rather than multiplicative error. The paper notes that the large variance at (1+ε)=100 is attributed to the optimal low-rank cost being very small relative to the additive Frobenius error bound, rather than a fundamental limitation of the algorithm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces the problem of consistent low-rank approximation, combining the streaming low-rank approximation literature (Frequent Directions, online ridge leverage score sampling) with the consistency/recourse framework developed for k-clustering and facility location (Lattanzi & Vassilvitskii, 2017; Fichtenberger et al., 2021). It departs from prior streaming LRA work by showing that standard algorithms like Frequent Directions and ridge leverage sampling fail catastrophically on recourse, and from the consistent clustering literature by addressing a fundamentally different geometric structure (subspace distance rather than center movement). The algorithmic core builds on the Eckart-Young theorem and projection-cost preserving sketches, with a novel casework on singular value contributions to achieve sub-quadratic recourse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formal study of consistent low-rank approximation, establishing both upper bounds (k^{3/2}/ε² · polylog for relative error) and a matching-order lower bound (Ω(k/ε · log(n/k))), which clarifies the fundamental difficulty of the problem. The practical motivation—reducing costly retraining of downstream ML models when feature subspaces shift—is compelling for dynamic pipelines in recommendation, biometrics, and data compression. Open questions include extending the results to sliding-window and insertion-deletion streams (handling implicit deletions), closing the gap between the k^{3/2} upper bound and k lower bound, and understanding whether the recourse cost function (squared subspace distance) is the most natural choice for all downstream applications.
