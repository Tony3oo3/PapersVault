## Problem

Deep multivariate time-series anomaly detectors achieve strong accuracy but remain black boxes, outputting a single score with no insight into the anomaly's cause. Because real-world anomalies are heterogeneous—spanning point spikes, contextual drifts, and violations of inter-variable dependencies—methods tuned to one type systematically miss others. PGRF-Net addresses this by reframing detection as a diagnostic process that simultaneously flags anomalies and attributes them to specific underlying mechanisms.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sliding window of w=60 timesteps across N variables (25–55 channels depending on the dataset) as a single multivariate matrix. Beyond the scalar final anomaly score, it produces a structured five-panel diagnostic report: four decomposed evidence scores (predictive, structural, contextual, spike), instance-dependent fusion weights indicating which evidence channel dominates, and a relational mask diff (M_t vs. M_base) that pinpoints which specific variable dependencies have shifted. All outputs are generated in a single forward pass with 0.059 ms per-step inference latency.

## Method

PGRF-Net decomposes each input window via FFT into low- and high-frequency components, encodes them with dedicated Conformer encoders, and fuses the representations into a global context vector. Three learnable prototype banks (structural dependency masks, context vectors, spike archetypes) are specialized end-to-end during Stage 1, where a Gumbel-Softmax selector produces a dynamic relational graph used to compute four evidence scores: predictive MSE under the learned dependency structure, Frobenius-norm deviation of the dynamic mask from a fixed baseline, cosine distance to a weighted context prototype, and minimum distance to spike prototypes. Stage 1 is trained unsupervised on normal data with a focal-weighted prediction loss plus structural regularization (L1 sparsity, DAG acyclicity, temporal stability) and prototype clustering. In Stage 2, the Conformer encoders are frozen while a lightweight gating MLP learns instance-dependent softmax weights over the three explanatory channels, trained with a pseudo-normal suppression loss (penalizing gate activation on low-error windows) and an entropy-sharpening term that forces selective attribution to the most relevant evidence channel.

## Results

PGRF-Net achieves the highest average point-adjusted F1-score of 97.41% across five MTSAD benchmarks (MSL, SMAP, PSM, SMD, SWaT), ranking first on MSL (97.53%), SMD (97.75%), and SWaT (97.37%), and second on SMAP (95.55%) and PSM (98.85%). On threshold-free metrics, it attains the best average AUC-PR (32.49%) and AUC-ROC (71.89%). At the event level, PGRF-Net achieves the best Range-F1 on MSL (96.38%), SMD (97.16%), and SWaT (95.68%), with an overall average Range-F1 of 96.08%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGRF-Net outperforms all 11 baselines on average F1-score. The closest competitor is H-PAD (Shen et al., ICLR 2025) at 96.86% avg F1, which PGRF-Net surpasses by 0.55 points; H-PAD leads on SMAP F1 (97.21 vs. 95.55) and PSM F1 (99.12 vs. 98.85), while PGRF-Net wins decisively on MSL (97.53 vs. 95.45) and SMD (97.75 vs. 95.45). GSC-MAD (Zhang et al., 2024) trails at 95.10% avg F1, with PGRF-Net gaining 2.31 points, primarily on MSL and SMD. MEMTO (Song et al., 2023) achieves 94.99% avg F1 but is outperformed by PGRF-Net on every individual dataset. Among older baselines, DCdetector (Yang et al., 2023) reaches 94.37% but suffers a high-recall/low-precision tradeoff (e.g., 85.60% recall on SMD vs. PGRF-Net's 99.80%), while D3R (Wang et al., 2023) at 91.73% and DMamba (Chen et al., 2024) at 78.51% show substantially larger gaps. On threshold-free AUC-PR, PGRF-Net leads all methods (32.49% avg vs. H-PAD's 30.05%), confirming superior ranking quality in imbalanced settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

PGRF-Net synthesizes three previously separate research threads—prototype-based normality modeling (MNAD, MEMTO, H-PAD), dependency-aware anomaly detection (Anomaly Transformer, GSC-MAD, Causalformer), and prediction-based detection (LSTM/Transformer forecasters)—into a unified diagnostic framework. Rather than treating any single signal (reconstruction error, association discrepancy, or forecast deviation) as the primary anomaly indicator, it decomposes anomalies into four complementary evidence channels and learns to fuse them via a gated mechanism. The Conformer encoder is borrowed from speech recognition, the DAG constraint from continuous structure learning (DAGs with No Tears), and the Gumbel-Softmax selector from discrete variational inference, but their combination into a multi-evidence diagnostic pipeline is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: operators in industrial IoT and healthcare need not just an anomaly flag but an explanation of *why* the system is anomalous (spike vs. drift vs. broken dependency). The four-way decomposition with adaptive gating offers a concrete step toward evidence-based monitoring without sacrificing detection accuracy. Key open questions remain: the DAG constraint cannot model feedback loops common in cyber-physical systems, the baseline structural mask assumes stationary normality, and the method lacks human-centered evaluation of whether the diagnostic output is actually actionable. Contamination-robust prototype learning and multi-regime baselines are identified as important future directions.
