## Problem

Single-neuron convex relaxations are known to be incomplete for general ReLU networks (the single-neuron convex barrier), and multi-neuron relaxations have been proposed heuristically to improve precision. However, it remained entirely open whether multi-neuron relaxations can provably overcome this barrier given sufficient resources, or whether they offer any fundamental theoretical advantage over single-neuron methods. This paper provides the first rigorous expressiveness analysis of multi-neuron convex relaxations.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a ReLU network (or a network with non-polynomial activations such as tanh/sigmoid) and a compact input convex polytope X ⊆ ℝ^d as input, and characterizes the bounding error of the relaxation on the network output. The key outputs are: (i) a proof that for any fixed relaxation with finite resources, there exist networks where the bounding error exceeds any given threshold T > 0, and (ii) a partition complexity bound showing multi-neuron relaxations require at most A(f,X) subproblems (the number of distinct activation patterns) versus an unbounded number for single-neuron relaxations.

## Method

The paper establishes a universal convex barrier by first proving that the most precise layerwise relaxation P_1 is incomplete (Theorem 3.3): if a subnetwork maps the input to a non-convex set whose convex hull contains points where the subsequent subnetwork attains its extremum, no layerwise relaxation can recover exact bounds, and the error is unbounded. This is extended to cross-layer relaxations via a pumping lemma (Lemma 4.1) that inserts dummy identity layers between subnetworks to block inter-layer information flow, showing P_{max(1,⌊αL⌋)} is incomplete for every α < 1 (Theorem 4.2). On the positive side, completeness is recovered by either (i) an equivalence-preserving width expansion that copies input variables into every hidden layer so P_1 can compute the exact convex hull of the output, or (ii) partitioning the input into convex sub-polytopes that remain convex through all layers. The paper further shows that multi-neuron relaxations preserve the full expressivity of ReLU networks (every continuous piecewise linear function is exactly bounded by some transformed network) and achieve strictly lower partition complexity than single-neuron relaxations in branch-and-bound.

## Results

The paper establishes a universal convex barrier: for any cross-layer relaxation P_{max(1,⌊αL⌋)} with α ∈ (0,1), there exist ReLU networks where the bounding error is arbitrarily large (Theorem 4.2), extending the single-neuron barrier to all multi-neuron relaxations. On the positive side, Theorem 5.1 shows that P_1 becomes complete under equivalence-preserving network transformations (adding O(d) copy neurons per layer), and Corollary 5.2 implies every continuous piecewise linear function can be exactly bounded by some layerwise multi-neuron relaxation. Proposition 5.6 establishes a strict partition complexity separation: multi-neuron BaB requires at most A(f,X) subproblems while single-neuron BaB requires at least A(f,X), with an exponential gap demonstrated for the max function in [0,1]^d (2^{d-1} vs. 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or benchmark comparisons. The central theoretical comparison is between single-neuron relaxations (Triangle, IBP, DeepPoly) and multi-neuron relaxations (P_1, M_k, P_r). The paper shows that multi-neuron relaxations do NOT overcome the convex barrier — they are inherently incomplete just like single-neuron ones (Theorems 3.3, 4.2). However, multi-neuron relaxations strictly dominate single-neuron ones in two respects: (1) under equivalence-preserving transformations, multi-neuron P_1 preserves the full expressivity of ReLU networks (every continuous piecewise linear function is exactly bounded), while single-neuron relaxations are limited to 1-D functions (Baader et al., 2024); and (2) under convex polytope partitioning, multi-neuron BaB has partition complexity at most A(f,X) while single-neuron BaB requires at least A(f,X), yielding an exponential gap (e.g., 1 vs. 2^{d-1} for the max function in [0,1]^d). The case study in §5.1 further shows that even the weak M_1 relaxation exactly bounds the max function in [0,1]^d, while k-ReLU (equivalent to Triangle in that setting) returns an inexact upper bound of 1.5 instead of 1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-neuron convex barrier (Salman et al., 2019; Baader et al., 2024) to a 'universal convex barrier' covering all multi-neuron convex relaxations, including the most precise cross-layer relaxation P_r for any r < L. It builds directly on the k-ReLU multi-neuron framework of Singh et al. (2019a) and the expressivity impossibility results of Baader et al. (2024), formalizing a general P_r / M_k relaxation hierarchy and proving that no finite-resource convex relaxation can be complete. On the positive side, it departs from prior work by showing that multi-neuron relaxations preserve the full expressivity of ReLU networks under equivalence-preserving transformations and achieve strictly lower partition complexity in branch-and-bound, capabilities unavailable to any single-neuron method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The universal convex barrier result fundamentally reframes the certification landscape: convex relaxations, regardless of how many neurons or layers they jointly consider, can never be complete verifiers on their own and must serve as subroutines within complete methods like branch-and-bound. The positive separations (expressivity preservation, lower partition complexity) provide concrete theoretical justification for investing in multi-neuron relaxations for both verification (as BaB bounding subroutines) and certified training, directions the paper identifies but leaves unexplored. Key open questions include designing practical multi-neuron relaxations that exploit the theoretical advantages, developing certified training objectives tailored to multi-neuron relaxations, and understanding whether the partition complexity gap translates to meaningful runtime improvements on real networks.
