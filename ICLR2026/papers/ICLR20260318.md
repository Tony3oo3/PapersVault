## Problem

Existing fidelity and coverage metrics for generative models are biased by out-of-distribution samples and lack interpretable absolute scores: a score of x should indicate performance equivalent to x proportion of good samples, but no current metric achieves this linear degradation. A 2025 position paper argued all existing metrics are flawed, highlighting the urgent need for robust, calibrated evaluation tools for high-stakes applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260318/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N real and M synthetic samples as DINOv2-ViT-L/14 embeddings (1024-dim for images) and produces two scalar scores in [0,1]: Clipped Density (fidelity) and Clipped Coverage (coverage). Both scores are directly interpretable as the equivalent proportion of good samples in a hypothetical mixture, enabling absolute quality judgments without requiring a reference model for comparison. The k-NN parameter (default k=5) controls the ball radius for the nearest-neighbor approximation.

## Method

Clipped Density modifies the k-NN ball-based Density metric in two ways: it caps each synthetic sample's fidelity contribution at 1 (preventing over-occurring samples from masking bad ones) and clips each real ball's radius to the median NND_k (preventing outlier real samples from creating disproportionately large balls that inflate the score). The result is normalized by the real data's own leave-one-out score and clipped to 1. Clipped Coverage counts the number of synthetic samples within each real ball (capped at 1 per real sample) rather than using a binary in/out test, then applies a theoretically derived calibration function g—computed from the Beta-Binomial expected value of the uncalibrated metric—to enforce exact linear score degradation as the proportion of bad samples increases, ensuring both interpretability and normalization independent of dataset size or k.

## Results

Clipped Density and Clipped Coverage are the only metrics that pass all four fidelity sanity checks (linearity, stability under mode dropping, stability under matched outliers, and symmetry with sensitivity) and all three coverage checks. On real datasets, absolute scores reveal that even the best diffusion models achieve only ~0.4 Clipped Density and ~0.4 Clipped Coverage on CIFAR-10 and FFHQ (equivalent to 40% good samples), ~0.7 on LSUN Bedroom, and ~1.0 on ImageNet. The RESFLOW CIFAR-10 example demonstrates the practical impact: the original Density metric reports an inflated score of 2.47 driven by 4 real outlier images, while Clipped Density correctly assigns 0.00. Across all four datasets, diffusion models consistently outperform GANs on both metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Clipped Density outperforms all seven fidelity baselines: Precision and Density fail the symmetry/sensitivity test (4d) due to outlier-induced large balls; symPrecision and Precision Cover inappropriately capture coverage information (failing test 4b); α-Precision and P-precision lack robustness to matched outliers (failing 4c); and TopP deviates from linear decay (failing 4a). For coverage, Clipped Coverage is the only metric achieving linear score degradation with increasing bad-sample proportion (Figure 1), while Recall, symRecall, β-Recall, TopR, and P-recall all fail at least one robustness or symmetry test. On real datasets (Figure 6), Clipped Density remains bounded and stable where Density exceeds 1 (e.g., RESFLOW at 2.47, WGAN-GP at 1.74), and Clipped Coverage provides interpretable absolute values that correlate with human error rates (Pearson r > 0.8 on CIFAR-10, ImageNet, and LSUN Bedroom). The paper also confirms that Clipped Density and Clipped Coverage capture the fidelity-coverage trade-off under GAN truncation, a property not reliably exhibited by the baseline metric pairs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Density and Coverage metrics of Naeem et al. (2020), which themselves build on the k-NN ball support approximation framework of Kynkäänniemi et al. (2019). It departs from the entire lineage of prior improvements (symPrecision/symRecall, P-precision/P-recall, Precision/Recall Cover, α-Precision/β-Recall, TopP&R) by introducing a dual clipping mechanism—capping individual sample contributions at 1 and, for fidelity, clipping ball radii to the median NND_k—that no prior metric combines. The work is explicitly motivated by the position paper of Raisa et al. (2025), which demonstrated that all existing fidelity and coverage metrics fail basic sanity checks and none supports absolute interpretation; this paper addresses both gaps simultaneously through empirical calibration (Clipped Density) and a theoretical Beta-Binomial derivation (Clipped Coverage).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The absolute interpretability of the proposed metrics reveals that even top diffusion models on CIFAR-10 and FFHQ achieve only ~40% equivalent quality, a stark contrast to the near-saturated FID scores that suggest near-perfect generation. This reframes the practical question from 'which model is better?' to 'is any model good enough for deployment?', which is critical in high-stakes domains like medical imaging (demonstrated on Chest X-rays). Limitations include the absence of a theoretical analysis in the infinite-sample limit, reliance on a specific embedding model (DINOv2) whose choice remains an open question, and the fact that the benchmark of sanity checks, while comprehensive, is not exhaustive. The paper also does not address memorization or other quality dimensions beyond fidelity and coverage.
