## Problem

Efficiently selecting informative gene perturbations from the ~20,000 protein-coding human genes is critical for drug discovery, but exhaustive CRISPR-Cas9 screening is infeasible. Existing Bayesian optimization approaches for perturbation design rely on generic uni-modal gene representations and ignore rich domain-specific biological knowledge such as pathway enrichment, limiting both sample efficiency and interpretability of the selected experiments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265332/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

BioBO consumes three L2-normalized gene embedding modalities (Achilles dependency scores, Gene2Vec co-expression vectors, and GenePT literature-derived vectors) concatenated or fused in latent space, along with iteratively accumulated phenotype labels and pathway databases (Gene Ontology or Hallmark). At each of 20 acquisition cycles it outputs a batch of 20 gene perturbations to label, the BNN posterior mean and variance over all unlabeled genes, and pathway-level enrichment statistics (adjusted p-values, odds ratios, combined scores) explaining why the selected genes were prioritized. The total budget of 400 perturbations corresponds to less than 5% of the ~10,556-gene pool with complete embeddings.

## Method

BioBO makes two methodological contributions within a Bayesian optimization loop using a Monte Carlo dropout BNN surrogate. First, it fuses multimodal gene embeddings (Achilles, Gene2Vec, GenePT) via concatenation or cross-attention in latent space, which the paper shows improves surrogate predictive quality specifically near the optimum rather than globally—precisely the regime that drives acquisition function performance. Second, it augments the acquisition function with a biologically informed prior constructed from enrichment analysis: at each iteration, the top-10% labeled genes are scored against pathway databases using a combined p-value and odds-ratio metric, and unlabeled genes belonging to significantly enriched pathways receive elevated selection probability via a softmax over pathway scores with a temperature parameter. This prior is integrated through the π-BO framework, where its influence decays as β/L_n with the number of labeled points, yielding a no-harm regret guarantee that BioBO's regret is asymptotically bounded by vanilla BO's regret, ensuring robustness to noisy or mismatched pathway information.

## Results

On the GeneDisco benchmark, BioBO (BioUCB-HM with multimodal Fusion) achieves a cumulative top-k recall of 0.109 on IFN-γ and 0.178 on IL-2, outperforming the best standard BO baseline (UCB with Fusion: 0.100 and 0.174 respectively) and the random baseline (~0.050). Across all 24 dataset–modality–acquisition settings, BioBO achieves the best performance in 23/24 cases. The paper reports a 25–40% improvement in labeling efficiency (i.e., fewer experiments needed to reach a target recall) over conventional BO, with multimodal fusion alone contributing a 4–40% gain over single-modality surrogates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares BioBO against two primary baselines: (1) standard BO with UCB/EI/TS acquisition functions using single-modality or fused gene embeddings (the GeneDisco protocol from Mehrjou et al., 2021 / Lyle et al., 2023), and (2) DiscoBAX (Lyle et al., 2023), a dedicated acquisition function for genomic perturbation design. BioBO (BioUCB-HM + Fusion) outperforms standard UCB + Fusion by 9% on IFN-γ (0.109 vs. 0.100) and by 2% on IL-2 (0.178 vs. 0.174) in cumulative top-k recall; the gap is larger with single-modality embeddings (e.g., 14% on IL-2 with Achilles: 0.163 vs. 0.143). DiscoBAX is found to underperform all standard acquisition functions (UCB, EI, TS) and is dropped from subsequent experiments. The enrichment-analysis prior improves the base acquisition in 23 out of 24 settings, with the Hallmark database consistently outperforming Gene Ontology. The multimodal Fusion surrogate also beats every single modality across all acquisition functions, confirming that the gains come from both richer representations and biologically informed acquisition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the BO-based gene perturbation design framework established by GeneDisco (Mehrjou et al., 2021) and DiscoBAX (Lyle et al., 2023) along two axes: (i) replacing single-modal gene embeddings with multimodal fusion (Achilles + Gene2Vec + GenePT) in the BNN surrogate, and (ii) injecting enrichment-analysis-derived biological priors into the acquisition function via the π-BO framework (Hvarfner et al., 2022). The enrichment prior construction directly adopts the combined p-value/odds-ratio scoring from Enrichr (Chen et al., 2013), while the theoretical no-harm regret bound is inherited from π-BO's Theorem 1. The key conceptual departure from prior BO-for-perturbation work is the principled integration of a domain-specific, pathway-level prior that decays with accumulated data, rather than treating biological knowledge as a hard constraint or a one-shot candidate filter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 25–40% labeling-efficiency gain is practically meaningful for expensive CRISPR screens where each perturbation batch costs days of wet-lab time, and the no-harm guarantee makes the method safe to deploy even when pathway databases are noisy or mismatched. A notable analytical insight is that multimodal fusion improves BO not by sharpening the surrogate globally but by improving local predictive quality near the optimum, which reframes how surrogate quality should be evaluated in BO settings. Limitations include retrospective evaluation only (no prospective wet-lab validation), reliance on pre-computed embeddings that may not transfer to novel cell types, and the enrichment prior's dependence on curated pathway databases that may lag behind emerging biology. Open questions include whether the framework extends to combinatorial perturbations, single-cell resolution, or settings where the phenotype is multi-objective.
