## Problem

Existing stepsized Newton methods achieve at best O(k^{-2}) global convergence under Hölder-continuous Hessian assumptions, falling short of the optimal Ω(k^{-7/2}) rate. The paper asks whether simpler stepsize schedules—avoiding the complexity of accelerated tensor methods—can close this gap, and how to handle the practical difficulty that smoothness parameters are typically unknown.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261648/cropped_page9_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a convex function with Hölder-continuous Hessian (p=2) or third derivative (p=3), an initial point x^0, and either known smoothness parameters (q, M_q) for the Root Newton (RN) schedule or backtracking hyperparameters (σ_0, ρ, β) for the Universal Newton (UN) procedure. All norms are measured in the local Hessian metric ‖·‖_{x^k} = ‖·‖_{∇²f(x^k)}, making the method affine-invariant. The output is a sequence of damped Newton iterates x^{k+1} = x^k − α_k[∇²f(x^k)]^{-1}∇f(x^k) with a per-iteration stepsize α_k ∈ (0,1] computed as the unique positive root of a polynomial, yielding a guaranteed suboptimality bound f(x^k) − f_* = O(k^{-(q-1)}) where q = p+ν ∈ [2,4].

## Method

The core insight is that regularizing the second-order Taylor model with a (2+β)-th order term in local Hessian norms, min_y {Φ_x(y) + (σ/(2+β))‖y−x‖_x^{2+β}}, is exactly equivalent to a damped Newton step whose stepsize α is the unique positive root of P[α] = 1 − α − α^{1+β}σ‖∇f(x)‖_x^{*β}. By reparametrizing with θ = α^β σ‖∇f(x)‖_x^{*β}, the polynomial simplifies to P_θ[α] = 1 − α − αθ, giving the closed-form stepsize α_k = 1/(1+θ_k) with θ_k = (9M_q)^{1/(q−1)}‖∇f(x^k)‖_{x^k}^{*(q−2)/(q−1)}. Analyzing under Hölder continuity of the third derivative (p=3, q up to 4) rather than just the Hessian yields the improved O(k^{-3}) global rate. For unknown smoothness parameters, the paper introduces a Gradient-Regulated Line Search (GRLS) that directly maximizes the one-step decrease bound, and a practical backtracking procedure (UN) that starts with a small regularization estimate and increases it until the theoretically predicted decrease is achieved, both converging as if the optimal (q, M_q) were known in advance.

## Results

The paper establishes that the proposed Root Newton (RN) stepsize schedule achieves a global convergence rate of O(k^{-(p+ν-1)}) under L_{p,ν}-Hölder continuity, reaching O(k^{-3}) when the third derivative is Hölder continuous (q=4). This improves upon the O(k^{-2}) rate of AICN (Hanzely et al., 2022) and the O(k^{-1/2}) rate of Damped Newton (Nesterov & Nemirovski, 1994). The backtracking procedure UN and linesearch GRLS achieve the same min_{q∈[2,4]} O(k^{-(q-1)}) rate without requiring prior knowledge of the smoothness parameters. Empirically, RN matches AICN in convergence speed, UN outperforms Super-universal Newton, and GRLS outperforms all other linesearch procedures on the challenging nonconvex Rosenbrock function.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AICN (Hanzely et al., 2022), RN achieves a strictly faster global rate (O(k^{-3}) vs. O(k^{-2}) under third-derivative Hölder continuity) while matching AICN's O(k^{-2}) rate under L_{2,1} assumptions; empirically, RN and AICN show nearly identical convergence curves on logistic regression and polytope feasibility. Against Damped Newton (Nesterov & Nemirovski, 1994), RN improves the global rate from O(k^{-1/2}) to O(k^{-3}) and preserves much larger stepsizes when far from the solution. Against Super-universal Newton (Doikov et al., 2024), the proposed UN backtracking procedure demonstrates faster convergence in Figures 2b and 3b, while also being invariant to linear transformations (unlike GRN which uses l_2 norms). Against Greedy Newton (Shea & Schmidt, 2025), GRLS uses nearly indistinguishable stepsizes on convex problems (logistic regression, polytope feasibility) but clearly outperforms GN on the nonconvex Rosenbrock function (Figure 1). Against Armijo linesearch, both GRLS and GN converge faster on all tested convex problems. The first-order Gradient Method baseline confirms the expected trade-off: faster per-iteration cost but slower per-iteration convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the regularization-stepsize duality framework of Hanzely et al. (2022) from cubic (3rd-order) regularization to general (2+β)-order regularization in local Hessian norms, and builds on the s-relative size analysis of Doikov et al. (2024) for global linear convergence. The key conceptual departure is analyzing the classical second-order Newton method under Hölder continuity of third derivatives, connecting it to third-order tensor methods — a perspective the authors note is novel and somewhat unexpected. A technical reparametrization via an implicit parameter θ (encapsulating both the regularization strength σ and exponent β) is the main analytical tool that makes the higher-order polynomial stepsize tractable, avoiding direct manipulation of α_k^{1+β} terms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers the first Newton method stepsize schedules achieving O(k^{-3}) global convergence (improving on the O(k^{-2}) of AICN), and provides the first convergence guarantees for the widely-used Greedy Newton exact linesearch. The practical backtracking algorithm UN converges as if optimal smoothness parameters were known, removing a key hyperparameter-tuning burden. However, the gap to the information-theoretic lower bound Ω(k^{-7/2}) for Hölder-continuous Hessians remains wide, and the assumptions (bounded level sets, γ-bounded Hessian change) may not hold for all practical problems. The connection showing GRLS and Greedy Newton select nearly identical stepsizes in practice offers a theoretical explanation for why the simpler GN works well, which could guide future linesearch design.
