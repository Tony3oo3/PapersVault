## Problem

LayerNorm is a standard component of virtually all transformer LLMs, but its role at inference time is poorly understood, and its non-linearity complicates mechanistic interpretability by making component attribution and interaction analysis intractable. No prior work had demonstrated that LN could be removed from all GPT-2 model sizes (up to 1.5B parameters) with minimal performance degradation, nor had the interpretability consequences of LN-free models been systematically characterized.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained GPT-2 checkpoint (Small 124M through XL 1.5B) and fine-tunes it on OpenWebText, replacing each LN layer with a purely linear 'FakeLN' transformation that uses a fixed scalar σ_avg in place of the per-token standard deviation. The output is a suite of LN-free GPT-2 models that produce standard next-token logits but contain no non-linear normalization anywhere in the residual stream, making all residual-to-logit mappings linear. The fine-tuning data requirement scales sublinearly with model size (157M tokens for Small vs. 413M for XL).

## Method

The core technique is sequential LN removal: each LN block is replaced with a FakeLN (a linear transformation initialized close to the original LN) and the model is fine-tuned for a fixed number of steps before the next block is removed. The removal order is MLP blocks layer-by-layer, then QK blocks, then V blocks, then the final LN, with the gaps between removals tuned per model size. An auxiliary loss penalizes variance in per-token residual stream standard deviations to prevent gradient explosions during the transition, and an exponential moving average tracks σ_avg for larger models where batch-level estimates are noisy. This sequential 'weaning' approach is necessary because removing all LNs simultaneously irreparably breaks performance. The resulting LN-free models achieve cross-entropy losses within +0.03 to +0.1 of their originals, and enable exact direct logit attribution (0% NMAE vs. ~49% with LN), elimination of first-token norm inflation, and deactivation of confidence neurons.

## Results

LN-free GPT-2 models achieve cross-entropy losses within +0.03 to +0.1 of the best-performing model (original or vanilla fine-tuned) on The Pile-filtered across all four model sizes (Small through XL). On OpenWebText validation, the gap is smaller, ranging from +0.014 (Large) to +0.067 (Small). The fine-tuning data required scales sublinearly: GPT-2 Small (124M params) needs 157M tokens while GPT-2 XL (1.5B params) needs only 413M tokens (a 2.6× increase for a 12× parameter increase). On standard benchmarks (HellaSwag, PIQA, BoolQ, WinoGrande), LN-free models maintain accuracy within 1–2 percentage points of their original counterparts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against two baselines: (1) the original GPT-2 models (no fine-tuning) and (2) vanilla fine-tuned models (same number of steps, same LR schedule, but LN retained). On The Pile-filtered, LN-free models trail the best baseline by +0.025 to +0.086 CE, with the gap generally smaller than the gap between vanilla fine-tuned and original models, indicating that the performance cost is attributable to LN removal rather than fine-tuning itself. On OpenWebText, LN-free models sometimes outperform the original (e.g., GPT-2 Small: 3.0797 vs 3.1006) but trail the vanilla fine-tuned control. The one notable exception is GPT-2 XL on The Pile, where three outlier samples with tokens absent from OpenWebText produce absurdly high CE losses (up to 5 million), though the 99.9th percentile matches the original. For generalization beyond GPT-2, the LN-free Pythia 70M achieves 0.2636 HellaSwag accuracy versus 0.2679 for the original and 0.2667 for vanilla fine-tuned, a 0.034 gap consistent with the GPT-2 Small results. On interpretability metrics, LN-free models achieve 0.00% NMAE for Direct Logit Attribution (vs 49.07% for the original), but attribution patching accuracy does not significantly improve (mean per-layer improvement of −0.026, σ=0.082), suggesting its limitations stem from other nonlinearities (SoftMax, MLP activations) rather than LN.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Heimersheim's (2024) NeurIPS workshop demonstration that LayerNorm can be removed from GPT-2 Small via fine-tuning into a full protocol that scales to GPT-2 XL, adding sequential per-block removal, an auxiliary norm-consistency loss, and EMA-based sigma estimation for stability. It positions itself within the mechanistic interpretability literature where LN's nonlinearity is a well-known obstacle (Bricken et al., 2023; Nanda, 2023a; Elhage et al., 2021), and differentiates from concurrent work by Zhu et al. (2025) who replace LN with a still-nonlinear Dynamic Tanh, and from Nabeshima (2024) who trains small models from scratch without normalization. The interpretability analyses build directly on DLA (nostalgebraist, 2020), attribution patching (Nanda, 2023a), and the confidence-neuron framework (Stolfo et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LN-free GPT-2 model suite provides a practical resource for interpretability research where LN's nonlinearity confounds component attribution and interaction analysis, and the finding that DLA becomes exact (0% NMAE vs. ~50%) validates the approach. The surprising negative result—that attribution patching accuracy does not improve without LN—redirects attention to other nonlinearities (softmax, MLP activations) as the true bottleneck for first-order causal tracing methods. Open questions include whether the protocol generalizes to RMSNorm-based architectures (the authors argue it should, given the near-equivalence), whether parameter-efficient fine-tuning suffices, and whether the persistent small performance gap and overconfidence in LN-free models reveal a genuine functional role for LN's nonlinearity in calibration.
