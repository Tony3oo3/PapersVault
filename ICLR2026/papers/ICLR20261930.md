## Problem

Existing KG-enhanced LLM reasoning methods—whether rule-based (constraining generation with pre-defined rules) or imitation-based (mimicking paths from fine-tuning data)—are confined to the distribution of their training data or rule sets, failing to generalize to novel, out-of-distribution reasoning patterns on knowledge graphs. This paper asks how to incentivize LLMs to autonomously explore beyond their prior experience to discover new reasoning paths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261930/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language question and a relevant subgraph of a knowledge graph (represented as a set of relational triples) as input. It produces a structured long chain-of-thought exploration process (reasoning paths traversing the KG, enclosed in <think> tags) followed by final answer entities (in <answer> tags). The exploration paths are sequences of connected triples, and the model is expected to discover paths that may deviate from the most common or training-distribution reasoning patterns.

## Method

EoG is a two-stage training framework. First, a Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) stage uses long CoT reasoning data distilled from Gemini 2.5 Flash to establish foundational structured graph-exploration capability. Second, a two-phase Reinforcement Learning stage using GRPO: Phase 1 optimizes the exploration policy with an outcome reward (entity-level F1 score of final answers), while Phase 2 introduces a path-refined reward that measures the proportion of ground-truth reasoning triplets (subject, relation, object all present as substrings) articulated in the generated reasoning text, combined as R_joint = R_outcome + α·R_path. The path reward steers exploration toward semantically meaningful and efficient paths, reducing futile exploration while the outcome reward ensures answer correctness. Ground-truth paths for datasets lacking explicit annotations are constructed via a BFS-based search followed by LLM semantic verification.

## Results

EoG (Llama-3.1-8B) achieves state-of-the-art performance across all five KGQA benchmarks, reaching 92.8 Hit@1 / 81.3 F1 on WebQSP, 86.6 / 77.9 on CWQ, 92.1 / 90.6 on GrailQA, 70.6 / 61.9 on QALD10-en, and 85.3 / 84.3 on 2WikiMultihop. These results surpass the strongest open-source baselines (GCR, DoG) by 1–12 points on F1 and exceed closed-source deep-thinking models (Gemini-2.5-Pro, GPT-5) on four of five datasets. The SFT-only variant (EoG_SFT) underperforms the full EoG by 4–14 F1 points, confirming that the RL stage with path-refined reward is the primary driver of the gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EoG (Llama-3.1-8B) outperforms the strongest open-source baseline GCR (Llama-3.1-8B) on both reported datasets: WebQSP (92.8 vs 92.2 Hit@1, 81.3 vs 79.1 F1) and CWQ (86.6 vs 75.8 Hit@1, 77.9 vs 61.7 F1), with the CWQ F1 gap of 16.2 points being the largest margin in the table. Against DoG (Llama-3.1-8B), EoG leads on WebQSP (92.8 vs 91.4 Hit@1), CWQ (86.6 vs 76.2 Hit@1), and 2WikiMultihop (85.3 vs 84.1 Hit@1). Against closed-source models, EoG surpasses Gemini-2.5-Pro on CWQ (86.6 vs 71.9 Hit@1, 77.9 vs 65.3 F1), QALD10-en (70.6 vs 58.6 Hit@1, 61.9 vs 48.3 F1), and 2WikiMultihop (85.3 vs 85.1 Hit@1, 84.3 vs 82.6 F1), and beats GPT-5 on CWQ (86.6 vs 74.1 Hit@1, 77.9 vs 67.6 F1) and QALD10-en (70.6 vs 59.2 Hit@1, 61.9 vs 50.4 F1). On WebQSP and GrailQA, EoG also edges out both closed-source models (92.8 vs 92.1/86.1 Hit@1; 92.1 vs 91.6/90.5 Hit@1). The Qwen2.5-7B variant of EoG similarly outperforms all open-source baselines and remains competitive with closed-source models, confirming the method's architecture-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

EoG departs from the two dominant paradigms in KG-enhanced LLM reasoning—rule-based constraint (ToG, DoG, GCR) and imitation-based fine-tuning (RoG, KG-Agent, CoT-KR)—by framing graph reasoning as an exploration problem solved via reinforcement learning. It directly adopts GRPO from DeepSeekMath as the optimization backbone and uses long-chain-of-thought distillation from Gemini 2.5 Flash for cold-start SFT, but its core novelty is the two-stage reward design: an outcome F1 reward followed by a path-refined reward that scores how faithfully the model's reasoning trace covers ground-truth relational triples. This positions the work at the intersection of RL-for-reasoning (DeepSeek-R1 lineage) and structured knowledge-graph navigation, rather than extending either line alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 7–8B open-source model, trained with only ~36–120 RL steps on task-specific data, can match or exceed GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro on five KGQA benchmarks, suggesting that targeted RL exploration is a compute-efficient alternative to scaling model size for structured reasoning. The OOD experiments (training on one dataset, evaluating on another) show that the RL stage, not the SFT distillation, is what confers cross-domain robustness—a useful diagnostic for practitioners deciding where to invest training effort. Open questions include whether the substring-matching path reward generalizes to open-domain or multi-hop settings without explicit ground-truth paths, whether the approach scales to graphs with millions of entities, and whether the BFS+LLM-verification pipeline for constructing r_g introduces systematic biases that limit the reward signal.
