## Problem

Prior LLM-based steganographic methods either modified a pre-existing cover text or failed to achieve full capacity, meaning the hidden message and the carrier text could not be of the same length. This paper asks whether a meaningful text can be perfectly concealed inside a different, equally coherent text of identical token length, and what the implications are for AI safety (e.g., smuggling unfiltered LLM outputs through aligned ones) and for our understanding of LLM knowledge and hallucination.

## Input / Output

The protocol takes three inputs: a secret text e to hide, a secret prompt key k that steers the topic, tone, and style of the carrier text, and a discrete autoregressive LLM with access to all output logits. It produces a stegotext s of exactly the same token length as e, generated from scratch (no pre-existing cover is modified), which is coherent and topically aligned with k. Decoding requires the same LLM run under identical numerical conditions (same quantization, same hardware) plus the key k, and recovers e exactly. A secondary prompt k' can optionally precede e to lower token ranks and improve stegotext quality, at the cost of a larger secret key.

## Method

Calgacus is a training-free, rank-based encoding protocol. To hide e in s: (1) tokenize e and, for each token, record its rank in the LLM's next-token probability distribution conditioned on the preceding tokens of e; (2) prompt the LLM with the secret key k and generate s autoregressively, but at every step select the token at the recorded rank position rather than sampling. To decode, the receiver recomputes the ranks of s's tokens after k, then regenerates e by selecting the token at each rank position from the LLM's distribution without k. The key insight is that when e is well-modeled by the LLM, its token ranks are low, so the stegotext s is forced to pick high-probability tokens after k, yielding a coherent, topically steered text. This achieves full capacity (stegotext and secret are the same length), a property absent in prior LLM steganography, and works with modest 8B open-source models (e.g., Llama 3 8B) on consumer hardware in seconds.

## Results

On 1000 Reddit posts (truncated to 85 tokens), stegotexts generated by Calgacus with Llama 3 8B fall entirely within the cumulative log-probability distribution of real texts, confirming plausibility. However, stegotexts are on average less probable than their corresponding originals, making them distinguishable by an LLM (verified with both Llama 3 8B and Phi-3 3.8B). In real text, only ~40% of tokens receive rank 1 from the LLM, yet the stegotext must follow prescribed ranks from the hidden message, causing a systematic probability drop concentrated in rank-1 tokens. Encoding and decoding an 85-token message takes ~2.3 seconds on a laptop with an RTX 4070 using quantized Llama 3 8B, and the protocol works with 8B-parameter open-source models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not perform head-to-head quantitative comparisons with prior LLM steganography methods (Meteor, Wu et al. 2024, Zamir 2024); instead, it validates the protocol by showing stegotexts remain within the plausibility envelope of 1000 real Reddit texts. The key empirical finding is that while stegotexts are indistinguishable from real texts to humans, an LLM can on average identify the original as more probable than its stegotext — a gap that narrows but persists even when a different LLM (Phi-3 3.8B) scores the texts. Among the six LLMs tested for stegotext quality (GPT-2 163M, Phi-3 3.8B, Llama 3 8B, Qwen3 8B, Phi-4 14B, Gemma 3 27B), Llama 3 8B offers the best quality-to-speed trade-off; larger models (Gemma 3 27B, Phi-4 14B) yield no significant improvement, while GPT-2 163M produces poorly steered output. The paper's unique contribution over prior work is full-capacity encoding (stegotext and secret message have identical token length), which none of the cited baselines achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of LLM-based text steganography initiated by Ziegler et al. (2019) and advanced by Meteor (Kaptchuk et al., 2021), Wu et al. (2024), and Zamir (2024), all of which encode a secret message into a longer or differently-distributed cover text. Calgacus's distinguishing contribution is full capacity: the stegotext and the hidden message have identical token length, achieved by a minimal modification to autoregressive generation—replacing stochastic sampling with deterministic rank-based token selection. The paper also draws on deniable encryption (Canetti et al., 1997) to formalize a security property and on Goldwasser et al. (2023) for a plausibility metric, while positioning the broader implications within the Oulipo tradition of constraint-based writing and Dennett's intentional stance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The protocol's simplicity (a few lines of code on an 8B open-source model) makes it immediately deployable, raising urgent AI safety concerns: a company could covertly serve unfiltered LLM outputs inside the compliant responses of an aligned model, with the user's local LLM performing the decoding. The paper reframes hallucination not as factual error but as the absence of attributable authorial intent, challenging how we reason about LLM 'knowledge' and trust in generated text. Open questions include whether stegotexts can be reliably detected by a third-party LLM (the paper shows a probability gap but not a robust detector), whether the deniability property holds under more sophisticated attacks that exploit the stegotext's structure, and how the method extends to non-text modalities (images, audio, whale vocalizations) where the authors note the framework generalizes naturally.
