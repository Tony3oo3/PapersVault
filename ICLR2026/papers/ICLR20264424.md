## Problem

The learning-to-cut literature has established sample complexity upper bounds for specific concept classes (constant CG weights, neural-network-generated CG weights) but provides no lower bounds, leaving open whether those bounds are tight and how the choice of score function affects learnability. Moreover, two scores—branch-and-cut tree size and gap closed—are used to evaluate cut quality, yet no principled analysis has compared them simultaneously in terms of theoretical difficulty and practical utility as a proxy for the more expensive tree-size objective.

## Input / Output

Each ILP instance (A, b, c) is encoded as a bipartite graph with n variable vertices and m constraint vertices; variable vertices carry a 3D feature vector (objective coefficient, cut coefficient, shared RHS) while constraint vertices carry (1,1,1), and edges between variable i and constraint j are weighted by a_ij. The GNN additionally receives the m candidate CG cuts from the Simplex optimal tableau as auxiliary input vectors of size m+1 (left-hand side and right-hand side per cut), and outputs a scalar score per candidate; the selected cut is the argmax over candidates with ties broken uniformly at random.

## Method

The theoretical contribution establishes the first sample complexity lower bounds for learning-to-cut by constructing ILP instances whose redundant constraints are shattered by the concept class, then proving the resulting CG cuts produce scores (for both gap closed and tree size) separated by Ω(γ), yielding m_L(ε,δ) = Ω(VCdim(F[n])/ε). For ReLU networks with W weights and L layers this gives Ω(W̄L log(W̄/L)/ε), nearly matching known upper bounds and showing the two scores are of comparable learning difficulty. The bounds hold for both the full CG-cut pool and the restricted tableau-cut pool. Empirically, a GNN is trained with cross-entropy loss to predict per-cut scores from the tableau, and the key finding is that training on gap closed generalizes well to minimizing tree size across Set Cover, Facility Location, Knapsack, and Vertex Cover benchmarks, confirming gap closed as a practical and computationally tractable proxy.

## Results

On 250 test instances, the GNN trained on B&C tree size achieves an average tree size of 8.27 on Set Cover and 128.85 on Facility Location, while the GNN trained on gap closed achieves 8.65 and 134.61 respectively. Both GNN variants outperform the Parallelism heuristic (8.29 and 144.09) and all other handcrafted baselines (Efficacy, Mix, Random). The gap-closed-trained GNN remains within ~0.4 (Set Cover) and ~5.8 (Facility Location) of the tree-size-trained GNN, confirming gap closed as a practical proxy, though both remain far from the optimal predictor (4.95 and 86.31).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the GNN cut selector against four handcrafted heuristics (Parallelism from Lodi 2009, Efficacy, Mix, Random) and an oracle (Optimal). On Set Cover, the GNN trained on B&C tree size (8.27) marginally beats Parallelism (8.29) and clearly outperforms Efficacy (9.90), Mix (9.30), and Random (9.71); the gap-closed-trained GNN (8.65) also beats all four heuristics. On Facility Location, the advantage is more pronounced: the tree-size GNN (128.85) improves over Parallelism by ~15 nodes (144.09) and over Efficacy by ~5 nodes (123.63 is actually better than the GNN here, so the GNN loses to Efficacy on this problem). The gap-closed proxy (134.61) remains competitive with Mix (133.72) and well below Random (152.46). Overall, the GNN matches or beats the state-of-the-art Parallelism heuristic on both problems, validating the learning-to-cut approach, while the small gap between tree-size and gap-closed training confirms the theoretical finding that the two scores are of comparable difficulty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sample complexity analysis of learning-to-cut initiated by Balcan et al. (2021) and Cheng et al. (2024), which provided only upper bounds for specific concept classes (fixed CG weights and neural-network-generated CG weights, respectively). The key departure is providing the first quantitative lower bounds, valid for a broad family of concept classes including ReLU and graph neural networks, by leveraging fat-shattering dimension arguments from Anthony & Bartlett (2009) and VC-dimension lower bounds for piecewise-linear networks from Bartlett et al. (2019). The paper also uniquely analyzes both the gap-closed and B&C tree-size scores simultaneously—both theoretically and empirically—whereas prior work treated them in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that gap closed and tree size are theoretically equivalent in sample complexity (with lower bounds nearly matching known upper bounds) gives practitioners confidence that the cheaper gap-closed proxy does not incur a fundamental learning-theoretic penalty. This resolves a practical concern: one can train on gap closed without fearing an intrinsic information-theoretic gap relative to the true objective. Open questions include whether the upper bound for gap closed can be improved by exploiting the fact that only cuts cutting off the fractional LP solution are relevant, and whether the 1/ε gap between upper and lower bounds can be closed under distributional assumptions.
