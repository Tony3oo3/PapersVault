## Problem

Existing LLM-based dual-arm robot planning methods optimize task success rate and completion time but largely neglect parallelism between the two arms, resulting in sequential single-arm execution that underutilizes dual-arm collaboration. This paper formalizes the Dual-Arm Cooperative Scheduling Problem, which requires jointly optimizing arm assignment and temporal scheduling to minimize makespan while respecting structural, temporal, and physical constraints across multiple concurrent task packages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264889/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes natural language instructions describing multiple task packages (e.g., 'make carrot slices, apple salad, and cream bread'), environment state descriptions, and task knowledge retrieved via RAG (m3e-base retriever over a hybrid short-term/long-term memory). The output is a complete time-synchronized dual-arm execution schedule: per-arm tables listing each meta-operation with its start/end time interval, plus the total makespan. An intermediate validated DAG with typed nodes (pick, use, place, container-switch, task_completion) and directed dependency edges serves as the structural bridge between the LLM's reasoning and the scheduling algorithm.

## Method

RoboPARA employs a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (Dependency Graph-based Planning) uses RAG-augmented prompts to have an LLM (GPT-4o or DeepSeek V3) generate a DAG encoding step dependencies, then applies a rule-based structural validator (Alg. 2) that detects three categories of invalid edges (cross-object place dependencies, skipped tool-use links, and unrelated use dependencies) and iteratively re-prompts the LLM for correction (up to 2 retries). Stage 2 (Graph Re-Traversal-based Parallel Planning) runs a temporal scheduler (Alg. 4) over the validated DAG using a min-heap event queue, an arm selector (Alg. 3) that enforces object-lock consistency (the same arm must execute the entire pick-use-place chain for one object), and a deadlock detection-and-rollback mechanism that identifies when both arms are locked to different objects while a dual-arm task awaits both, then rolls back the later pick chain to free an arm. The key architectural insight is that explicit DAG modeling with iterative correction separates the LLM's role (generating structurally valid dependency graphs) from the scheduling algorithm's role (optimizing parallel traversal), enabling systematic exploitation of cross-package step merging and intra-package parallelism that prompt-only baselines cannot achieve.

## Results

On the X-DAPT dataset with GPT-4o, RoboPARA achieves a Task Efficiency Index (TEI) of 1.956 on easy packages, 1.347 on medium, and 0.869 on hard, consistently outperforming all 7 baselines by at least 1.3×–1.5× in successful steps per unit time across kitchen, office, greenhouse, and factory scenes. RoboPARA reduces execution time by 30%–50% and achieves a 34% higher success rate than the average of other methods on hard packages, where baseline failure rates exceed 2.7× those of RoboPARA. In terms of parallelism, RoboPARA produces over 2.8× more parallel steps than all baselines on easy packages and over 10× on medium/hard packages, with an average of 4.5× more parallel and collaborative steps overall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoboPARA dominates all 7 baselines across every metric and difficulty level. VOYAGER fails completely (100% failure rate, zero TEI) as it cannot generate concrete per-arm execution steps. RoCo, which maps two LLM agents to two arms, is nearly unable to solve any task after 50+ prompting iterations and shows the lowest TEI among functional baselines (0.454 on hard). Among the strongest baselines, FLTRNN and LLM-Planner achieve the highest TEI on hard packages (0.481 and 0.404 respectively) but still fall far short of RoboPARA's 0.869, and both exhibit zero parallelism (PPR=0.000) while RoboPARA achieves PPR=0.121. On easy packages, LLM-Planner leads among baselines with TEI=1.494, yet RoboPARA still exceeds it by 31% (1.956) while simultaneously achieving 2.9× higher parallelism (APR 0.342 vs. 0.119). The gap widens dramatically on hard packages, where RoboPARA's DAG correction and deadlock-aware scheduling yield a 34% higher success rate than the baseline average and over 10× more parallel steps, whereas baselines like LLM³ and ChatGPT-Prompts see their failure rates spike above 45%.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoboPARA departs from the dominant LLM-based dual-arm planning line (RoCo, FLTRNN, LLM-Planner, Embodied TaPA) by reframing the objective from task success rate and completion time to explicit makespan minimization with arm-assignment constraints. While prior methods produce linear or hierarchical action sequences that implicitly serialize the two arms, RoboPARA introduces a two-stage pipeline—RAG-augmented LLM DAG generation with iterative structural correction, followed by a graph re-traversal scheduler with deadlock-aware rollback—that treats parallelism as a first-class optimization target. The X-DAPT dataset is positioned as the first benchmark purpose-built for evaluating this parallelism dimension, filling a gap the authors identify in existing evaluation protocols.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30–50% execution-time reduction and 34% success-rate gain on hard packages demonstrate that parallelism-aware scheduling is not a minor refinement but a substantial efficiency lever for dual-arm systems, with direct implications for humanoid and industrial deployment. Key limitations temper the impact: the framework consumes ~1.3× more tokens than baselines due to iterative DAG correction, the skill library is tightly coupled to predefined scenario templates limiting generalization, and the main experiments cover only 4 of the 10 dataset scenes. Open questions include whether the DAG-correction loop can be replaced by more efficient verification, whether the scheduling algorithm generalizes beyond the pick-use-place abstraction, and how the approach scales to more than two arms or to truly open-ended environments.
