## Problem

Conventional HDR video reconstruction methods are fundamentally constrained to 2D pixel-level alignment within narrow temporal windows (3–7 frames), causing ghosting, color drift, and geometric flickering in dynamic scenes. No prior work jointly reconstructs HDR radiance, 3D scene flow, geometry, and tone-mapping in a unified 4D spatio-temporal framework, leaving a gap between 2D HDR merging and 4D dynamic scene reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263882/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-camera alternating-exposure monocular video (frames cycling through low, mid, and high exposure levels) along with pre-computed COLMAP camera poses as input. It produces a continuous 4D HDR radiance field that can render novel views at arbitrary target exposures (both HDR and LDR), synthesize novel time steps via explicit 3D scene flow, and output corresponding depth maps. The evaluation protocol uses 9 synchronized GoPro cameras at 3 exposure levels, training on one camera's alternating frames and evaluating against the remaining 8 viewpoints at matched exposures.

## Method

HDR-NSFF extends Neural Scene Flow Fields (NSFF) with three key components: (1) a learnable tone-mapping module using a piecewise camera response function with per-channel white balance and leaky-thresholding to map rendered HDR radiance to LDR observations in an end-to-end differentiable manner; (2) exposure-invariant semantic optical flow via DINO-Tracker (DINOv2 features + SAM2 motion masks) that replaces color-dependent RAFT, providing stable motion cues under extreme exposure variation; and (3) a generative prior (Difix3D+) applied as a scheduled regularizer that synthesizes pseudo multi-view supervision to recover saturated or unobserved regions. The framework is representation-agnostic, demonstrated on both NeRF-based NSFF and 4D Gaussian Splatting (MoSca) backbones, and is optimized end-to-end with photometric, flow, depth, CRF smoothness, and generative losses.

## Results

On the proposed HDR-GoPro dataset for novel view synthesis, HDR-NSFF achieves 32.63 PSNR, 0.9444 SSIM, and 0.0554 LPIPS (full scene), substantially outperforming all baselines. For the more challenging novel view and time synthesis task (temporal interpolation from even-indexed frames), the method reaches 32.75 PSNR and 0.9448 SSIM, again leading all competitors. On synthetic data with extreme exposure variation (−2EV to +5EV), HDR-NSFF attains 35.07 PSNR and 0.9465 SSIM, surpassing the next-best method (NeRF-WT) by over 4 dB in PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HDR-NSFF decisively outperforms all five baselines across every metric on both real and synthetic data. The closest competitor is NeRF-WT (29.70 PSNR), which uses per-image appearance embeddings but still trails HDR-NSFF by ~3 dB in PSNR and 0.011 in SSIM on the full-scene metric. HDR-HexPlane (20.70 PSNR), the most directly comparable dynamic HDR method, is surpassed by over 12 dB in PSNR, largely because it lacks explicit 3D scene flow modeling. Methods without any HDR-aware appearance modeling—NSFF (18.02), 4DGS (20.94), and MotionGS (14.61)—fail catastrophically under alternating exposures, with MotionGS producing near-random results (0.3976 SSIM). The ablation rows confirm that DINO-Tracker contributes the largest single gain (+1.3 dB over the no-DT variant), while the generative prior provides a smaller but consistent improvement in LPIPS (0.0621→0.0554) by recovering saturated regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NSFF (Neural Scene Flow Fields) framework for dynamic scene reconstruction by introducing HDR-specific components: a learnable piecewise tone-mapping module, exposure-robust semantic flow via DINO-Tracker, and a generative prior (Difix3D+) as a regularizer. It departs fundamentally from prior HDR video reconstruction methods (LAN-HDR, HDRFlow, NECHDR) that operate in 2D pixel space, and from prior HDR novel view synthesis work (HDR-NeRF, HDR-Plenoxel, HDR-HexPlane) that either assumes static scenes or lacks explicit 3D motion modeling. The key conceptual shift is from 2D pixel-level exposure merging to a unified 4D spatio-temporal radiance field that jointly optimizes HDR radiance, scene flow, geometry, and tone-mapping end-to-end.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HDR-GoPro dataset (9 synchronized cameras, 3 exposure levels, 12 scenes) is the first real-world benchmark for dynamic HDR space-time view synthesis, filling a clear gap in the literature. The framework's representation-agnostic design (validated on both NeRF and 4DGS pipelines) suggests broad applicability to future 4D reconstruction methods. However, the method's reliance on COLMAP for camera poses limits its robustness in extreme exposure-varying scenes, and it does not handle motion blur from long exposures—both important practical constraints. An open question is whether the generative prior's marginal quantitative benefit (slightly negative PSNR, small LPIPS gain) justifies its computational cost, or whether better-conditioned multi-view inputs would make it unnecessary.
