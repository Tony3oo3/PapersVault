## Problem

Referring Image Segmentation training suffers from misleading gradients injected by poorly aligned, instance-specific visual signals, since each sample provides only one annotated object while the model must reason over the full image. Existing methods compensate by adding increasingly complex cross-modal fusion modules, implicitly assuming all image regions are equally informative, yet misaligned gradients from irrelevant regions can dominate optimization and degrade generalization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 448×448 RGB image and a natural language referring expression (e.g., 'the giraffe closest to people') as input, and outputs a binary pixel-wise segmentation mask indicating the referred object. During training, the input image is modified by zeroing out poorly aligned blocks before the segmentation forward pass, while at inference the original unmasked image is used directly, incurring no additional overhead. The visual backbone is Swin-B producing multi-scale features and the text backbone is BERT producing token embeddings.

## Method

AML is a two-stage training strategy that requires no architectural changes. In Stage 1 (forward-only), PatchMax Matching Evaluation (PMME) projects visual patches and language tokens into a common embedding space via Johnson-Lindenstrauss random Gaussian matrices (dimension D_a=2048), computes a row-wise softmax similarity matrix, and takes the max over tokens per patch to yield a fine-grained alignment heatmap. In Stage 2, Alignment-aware Filtering Masking (AFM) upsamples this heatmap to pixel resolution, identifies pixels below a threshold τ=0.4, applies a dropout ratio ρ=0.25 to retain some weak pixels for generalization, aggregates into 32×32 block masks using a conservative any-triggers-all policy, and zeros out the corresponding image regions before the standard segmentation loss is computed. The key insight is that suppressing gradients from misaligned regions early in training acts as a curriculum, concentrating optimization on reliable visual-textual correspondences and improving both accuracy and robustness without any inference-time cost.

## Results

On the RefCOCO family benchmarks (standard training), AMLRIS (CARIS+AML) achieves 77.89/79.53/74.99 mIoU on RefCOCO val/testA/testB, 71.33/75.61/64.61 on RefCOCO+ val/testA/testB, and 69.24/69.73 on RefCOCOg val/test, yielding an average of 72.9 mIoU across all 8 splits — a new SOTA. In oIoU, the method reaches 75.45/78.33/72.12 (RefCOCO), 67.37/73.19/59.51 (RefCOCO+), and 65.67/66.78 (RefCOCOg), averaging 69.8. Under combined training, AMLRIS further improves to 71.9 average oIoU, surpassing the previous best (MagNet, 70.9) by +1.0. Cross-dataset robustness tests (trained on RefCOCO+, tested on RefCOCO/RefCOCOg under 7 visual perturbations) show average mIoU gains of +3.50 and +2.34 respectively over the CARIS baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMLRIS (CARIS+AML) achieves state-of-the-art mIoU across all 8 RefCOCO-family splits, outperforming the previous best MagNet by +0.46/+0.10/+0.88 on RefCOCO val/testA/testB, +1.23/+1.11/+1.02 on RefCOCO+, and +0.71/+0.58 on RefCOCOg. Against the direct baseline CARIS, AML yields consistent gains of +1.12% (RefCOCO val), +2.00% (RefCOCO+ val), and +1.22% (RefCOCOg test) in mIoU, and +0.80%/+1.83%/+1.78% in oIoU respectively. The method also surpasses DETRIS by +2.25% mIoU on RefCOCO val and +2.86% on RefCOCO+ val, and CGFormer by +0.96% and +2.77% on the same splits. In the combined-training setting, AMLRIS leads all methods including LLM-based approaches (PerceptionGPT-7B, GSVA-7B) with 71.9 average oIoU vs. 71.4 and 70.1. On the multi-object GRES benchmark, AML+ReLA improves cIoU from 28.49 to 30.86 (+2.37) and gIoU from 17.33 to 21.08 (+3.75) at 5k iterations, confirming early-stage optimization benefits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant RIS paradigm of architectural innovation (cross-attention in CARIS, pixel-word attention in LAVT, language adapters in DETRIS) by proposing a training-strategy-level intervention: alignment-aware pixel masking during optimization. It builds directly on CARIS as its base model but is conceptually closer to the masking-based augmentation line (MaskRIS, NeMo, Mask Grounding), except that the masking is driven by a computed patch-token similarity map rather than random or distortion-aware heuristics. The use of Johnson-Lindenstrauss random projection to bridge the dimensional gap between unpaired vision and language backbones is a theoretical contribution that connects RIS to the dimensionality-reduction literature, with a formal guarantee (Theorem 1) that cross-modal inner products are preserved with high probability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of AML (no architectural changes, zero inference overhead, ~17% training overhead) makes it immediately applicable to any existing RIS framework, which is practically valuable for the community. The consistent SOTA across all 8 RefCOCO-family splits and the cross-dataset robustness gains under 7 visual perturbations suggest the alignment-filtering principle generalizes beyond a single architecture. Open questions include whether the fixed hyperparameters (τ=0.4, ρ=0.25) transfer to other domains, whether the approach extends to video or foundation-model settings as the authors note, and whether the modest multi-object gains on GRES (only +0.19% at 150k iterations) indicate a fundamental limitation of hard masking for compositional expressions.
