## Problem

Temporal-wise distillation (TWD) for SNNs applies a single constant teacher output to supervise all timesteps, ignoring the inherently evolving temporal dynamics of spiking networks. Additionally, truncated inference—required under strict latency and energy constraints—suffers from progressive information loss because early timesteps carry incomplete accumulated membrane potentials, and TWD does not ensure that early predictions converge toward the full-length prediction.

## Input / Output

RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) are converted to time series via direct encoding, where pixel values are repeatedly fed into the input layer at each of T discrete timesteps. The SNN (ResNet-18/19/34 or Spiking-Transformer with LIF neurons) processes these spikes over T steps and outputs a class label. A key design property is that the model supports reliable inference at any truncated timestep T'=1,…,T, not only at the full training length, enabling deployment under variable latency budgets.

## Method

MEOM integrates two complementary distillation components. Temporal Multi-Perspective Distillation (TMPD) applies time-indexed random masks to the teacher's intermediate features, producing per-timestep perturbed teacher logits that provide diverse, semantically consistent supervision ('many eyes'), replacing TWD's identical target across all steps. Temporal Progressive Distillation (TPD) computes cumulative average logits at each timestep and enforces cross-entropy alignment between consecutive cumulative predictions, progressively driving early truncated outputs toward the full-length prediction ('one mind'). The overall loss combines temporal-wise cross-entropy, TMPD KL divergence, and TPD alignment with weights α=1, β=0.5, γ=0.3. Theoretical analysis proves TMPD induces implicit regularization yielding a strictly lower error floor than TWD, and TPD guarantees a strictly smaller truncation accuracy drop than both neighbor-step consistency and no-consistency baselines.

## Results

On CIFAR-10/100 with ResNet-19, MEOM achieves 97.13%/82.85% top-1 accuracy at T=4, surpassing the strongest KD baseline TWSNN (96.97%/82.47%) and approaching the ANN teacher (97.20%/82.57%). On ImageNet at T=4, MEOM reaches 71.64% with ResNet-34 (vs. TWSNN's 71.04%) and 76.77% with the Spiking Transformer S-8-384 (vs. BKDSNN's 75.48%), outperforming all SNN baselines. Under truncated inference (T=1–5), MEOM consistently maintains the highest accuracy across all truncation points, with CIFAR-100 ResNet-19 at T=3 even surpassing the ANN teacher.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEOM outperforms all 15 baselines on CIFAR-10/100 across every reported timestep. Against the strongest KD baseline TWSNN (Yu et al., 2025), MEOM gains +0.16%/+0.38% on CIFAR-10/100 at T=4 with ResNet-19, and +0.08%/+0.66% at T=6. On ImageNet, MEOM beats TWSNN by +0.60% on ResNet-34 (71.64 vs. 71.04) and BKDSNN by +1.29% on S-8-384 (76.77 vs. 75.48). Among directly-trained SNNs, MEOM surpasses RateBP (the strongest non-KD method) by +0.87%/+2.14% on CIFAR-10/100 at T=4 with ResNet-19. Notably, on CIFAR-100 with ResNet-19 at T=4, MEOM (82.85%) slightly exceeds the ANN teacher (82.57%), and under truncated inference at T=3 it again surpasses the teacher. The only setting where MEOM does not lead is CIFAR-10 ResNet-19 at T=2, where it ties TWSNN at 96.65% but gains +0.35% on CIFAR-100 (81.82 vs. 81.47%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Temporal-Wise Distillation (TWD/TWSNN, Yu et al. 2025), which supervises each SNN timestep with a single ANN teacher output, by identifying two limitations: the constant teacher signal ignores the SNN's evolving temporal dynamics, and per-timestep supervision does not guarantee that truncated predictions converge to the full-length prediction. MEOM addresses these with TMPD (mask-weighted teacher features inspired by multi-teacher ensemble distillation in ANNs) and TPD (progressive cumulative-average alignment), positioning itself as a unified framework that subsumes TAD and TWD as special cases. It also contrasts with time-flexibility methods (SEENN, HSD, MTT, SSNN) that use adaptive truncation or pairwise consistency, arguing that progressive alignment to a single full-length target is more principled for deployment under strict latency constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is clear for neuromorphic deployment where inference must be truncated to meet energy/latency budgets; TPD's guarantee that early predictions approximate the full-length output addresses a real deployment gap that pairwise consistency methods leave open. The theoretical analysis (gradient variance reduction from TMPD, geometric truncation-error decay from TPD) provides a clean formal justification, though it relies on standard PL-condition and SGD bounds. An interesting open question is why random masks outperform learnable ones—the paper attributes this to learnable masks adapting to unreliable intermediate predictions, but this suggests the perturbation is closer to data augmentation than to capturing genuine temporal structure, leaving room for temporally-aware mask strategies. The approach is also limited to LIF neurons with surrogate gradients and has not been tested on more complex spiking dynamics or non-visual modalities.
