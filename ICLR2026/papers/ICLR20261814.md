## Problem

Existing multi-view clustering methods fail to simultaneously balance effectiveness, efficiency, completeness, and consistency for nonlinearly separable data. While kernel-based methods capture nonlinear structure, they incur high computational costs from pairwise similarity computations, and linear methods like K-means cannot handle arbitrary cluster shapes. The paper asks whether multi-view clustering can be decomposed into a three-level optimization that addresses all four properties at once.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261814/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-view dataset X = {X^1, ..., X^V} (each view n×m), the number of true clusters k, and the number of linear clusters p (required to exceed k). Before entering the EKNN framework, each view is preprocessed via self-representation to obtain an n×n coefficient matrix Θ^v, which serves as the actual input to the linear clustering layer. The primary output is a shared binary partition matrix H (n×k); intermediate outputs include per-view linear partitions W^v (n×p), nonlinear partitions U^v (p×k), and in the extension, a shared subspace representation Θ_C used with spectral clustering for the final assignment.

## Method

EKNN decomposes multi-view clustering into three jointly optimized sub-problems: (1) K-means-based linear clustering on self-represented data points, (2) kernel K-means-based nonlinear clustering on the resulting linear cluster centers, and (3) K-means-based multi-view integration that reconstructs a shared partition H from per-view partition products W^v Ũ^v. The key insight is that globally nonlinear clusters are locally linear, so over-partitioning into p > k linear clusters followed by kernel-based regrouping into k nonlinear clusters captures arbitrary shapes at K-means-level cost. All three objectives are unified into a single loss (Eq. 6) with balance weights α, β, γ, solved by alternating minimization where each sub-problem reduces to a closed-form K-means or spectral clustering update. The extension introduces a shared subspace representation Θ_C learned via self-expressiveness on H, regularized by nuclear norm, which feeds into spectral clustering for the final result.

## Results

On the seven multi-view benchmark datasets, EKNN's extension (multi-view subspace learning variant) achieves the best or near-best ACC on 5 out of 7 datasets, reaching 85.22% on COIL20, 91.73% on ORL, 60.55% on Reuters, 73.41% on ANIMAL, and 82.50% on NoisyMNIST. In terms of NMI, the extension leads on 5/7 datasets (e.g., 95.10% on ORL vs. 94.96% for RMSL, 86.20% on NoisyMNIST vs. 85.91% for SSSL-M). In terms of efficiency, EKNN with p=160 runs in 414.9–2319.5 s across datasets, roughly 7–14× faster than RMSL and SSSL-M (which require 1640–32407 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EKNN's extension outperforms the strongest baselines (SSSL-M and RMSL) on ACC for COIL20 (+0.86 over SSSL-M), ORL (+0.17 over SSSL-M), Reuters (+1.09 over SSSL-M), ANIMAL (+1.57 over SSSL-M), and NoisyMNIST (+1.03 over SSSL-M), but trails MVEC on BBCSport (94.21 vs. 96.88) and RMSL on Football (91.20 vs. 91.57). On NMI, the extension edges out SSSL-M on ORL (95.10 vs. 94.96), BBCSport (94.70 vs. 94.51), Reuters (42.90 vs. 41.55), ANIMAL (79.00 vs. 77.49), and NoisyMNIST (86.20 vs. 85.91), yet falls short of RMSL on Football (90.25 vs. 92.29). On F-score, the extension wins over SSSL-M on Reuters (+1.37), ANIMAL (+1.51), and NoisyMNIST (+1.14), but is consistently behind RMSL on COIL20 (77.10 vs. 81.20), BBCSport (92.15 vs. 95.35), and Football (79.47 vs. 83.87). The base EKNN (without the subspace-learning extension) underperforms all strong baselines on most metrics, suggesting the shared subspace representation Θ_C is critical for competitive performance. Overall, the extension achieves the best average ACC across the seven datasets (74.79%) compared to SSSL-M (73.75%) and RMSL (76.28%), while being 7–14× faster in runtime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the classical K-means and kernel K-means algorithms, reframing them as 'layers' in a neural-network-like architecture (EKNN) for multi-view clustering. It departs from prior multi-view clustering methods (DiMSC, DMF, MVEC, RMSL, SSSL-M) that typically integrate views at the feature or affinity-matrix level, by instead decomposing the problem into a three-level hierarchy: linear K-means on raw data, kernel K-means on the resulting linear clusters, and a final K-means-based multi-view integration via partition matrices. The self-representation subspace clustering framework (Elhamifar & Vidal) provides the input representation, and the Augmented Lagrange Multiplier handles the nuclear-norm regularization, following standard low-rank representation techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three-level decomposition offers a principled way to balance efficiency (linear clustering reduces data size before expensive nonlinear steps) with effectiveness and multi-view consistency, and the ~5–10× speedup over RMSL and SSSL-M is practically meaningful for large-scale multi-view data. However, the base EKNN underperforms SSSL-M and RMSL on most accuracy metrics, and the 'explainability' claim is relatively thin since each component is a well-understood classical algorithm with an obvious interpretation. Open questions include whether the three-level structure generalizes to incomplete or partially aligned multi-view settings, and whether the K-means-as-layers analogy can be extended to deeper or more flexible architectures beyond the fixed three-layer design.
