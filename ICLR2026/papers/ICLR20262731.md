## Problem

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) primarily reinforces reasoning paths a model already knows, failing to teach genuinely new capabilities beyond the base model's current scope. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) can inject new knowledge but requires large pre-collected demonstration sets and generalizes poorly out-of-distribution. The paper asks how to combine RL and SFT so that the model acquires new reasoning patterns on its hardest questions while preserving RL's generalization strengths, without relying on expensive pre-collected data.

## Input / Output

The model receives a math problem as a plain-text prompt and generates a chain-of-thought reasoning response ending with a bracketed final answer (e.g., [016]). During training, each question is sampled N=8 times per rollout; questions where all 8 rollouts are incorrect (acc=0) are flagged as 'hardest' and routed to an external solver (DeepSeek-R1 or human annotator) for a high-quality CoT solution, which is then verified by extracting the final answer and checking it against ground truth before entering the SFT buffer.

## Method

ReLIFT runs GRPO-based RL as the primary training loop while adaptively interleaving single-step SFT on the hardest questions. During each RL rollout, questions with zero correct answers out of 8 are identified, their high-quality CoT solutions are collected and correctness-filtered, and stored in a fine-tuning buffer; once the buffer reaches a threshold M (default 64), one SFT step is performed on those examples using a cross-entropy loss augmented with an entropy regularization term (α=1e-4) to prevent the fine-tuning update from collapsing the model's exploratory behavior. The scheduling is adaptive: SFT is applied more frequently early in training when the model's accuracy is low, then RL dominates as the model improves. Ablations show that both the adaptive scheduling (vs. uniform or per-step interleaving) and the selection of only the hardest questions (vs. random or all questions) are critical, as uniform or random variants degrade accuracy and inflate response length.

## Results

ReLIFT achieves a state-of-the-art overall accuracy of 52.6% on Qwen2.5-Math-7B across five math reasoning benchmarks (AIME-24, AIME-25, AMC, MATH-500, OlympiadBench) and one OOD benchmark (MMLU-Pro), outperforming all prior RLVR methods by an average of +6.7 points. Notably, ReLIFT uses only 8K demonstration samples and 52×8 GPU hours, compared to 46K demonstrations and 73×8 GPU hours for the closest hybrid baseline (LUFFY). ReLIFT also produces significantly more concise responses (3,502 tokens average) than SFT-based methods (5,533 tokens) while maintaining the highest accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReLIFT outperforms all five compared baselines on overall accuracy. Against the strongest prior RLVR method, Oat-Zero (45.9%), ReLIFT gains +6.7 points; against OpenReasoner-Zero (45.3%) and SimpleRL-Zero (39.0%), the margins are +7.3 and +13.6 points respectively. Among hybrid SFT+RL methods, ReLIFT surpasses LUFFY (50.9%) by 1.7 points, SFT then RL v2 (50.8%) by 1.8 points, and SFT then RL v1 (50.7%) by 1.9 points, while also using 5.75× fewer demonstrations (8K vs. 46K) and fewer GPU hours (52×8 vs. 73×8 for LUFFY). ReLIFT achieves the best or second-best score on every individual benchmark, with its largest single-benchmark gains on OlympiadBench (+3.5 over LUFFY) and MMLU-Pro (+1.3 over LUFFY), confirming both improved reasoning and stronger OOD generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GRPO policy optimization framework (Shao et al., 2024) as its RL backbone, positioning itself within the broader RLVR training paradigm that has driven recent reasoning breakthroughs (DeepSeek-R1, Kimi k-series, OpenAI o-series). It departs from prior hybrid SFT+RL approaches—such as LUFFY's off-policy augmentation, sequential SFT-then-RL pipelines, and loss-combination methods—by introducing an adaptive, difficulty-triggered interleaving strategy where SFT steps are inserted only when the model encounters questions it cannot solve (acc=0 during rollout). The conceptual lineage draws on the observation that RLVR reinforces existing behaviors rather than creating new capabilities (Yue et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2025), but the paper's specific contribution is the systematic difficulty-stratified analysis that motivates the scheduling mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReLIFT addresses a fundamental limitation of RLVR—its inability to teach the model reasoning patterns it has never seen—while avoiding the data hunger and OOD fragility of pure SFT. The practical impact is significant: achieving SOTA (52.6% overall) with only 8K demonstrations and 52×8 GPU hours versus 46K/113.5×8 for competing hybrid methods, making high-quality reasoning training more accessible. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger models (the paper only tests up to 8B), whether true online data collection (as opposed to the offline proxy used here) maintains the same benefits, and whether the adaptive scheduling generalizes to non-mathematical reasoning domains. The finding that naive interleaving (ReLIFT-all) causes training collapse underscores that the scheduling strategy is as important as the data selection.
