## Problem

Robust fine-tuning (RFT) from non-robust pretrained backbones — the dominant scenario in practice since nearly all public pretrained models lack adversarial robustness — often suffers from 'suboptimal transfer,' where clean accuracy drops dramatically below standard fine-tuning even at small perturbation strengths (e.g., 1/255). Prior RFT methods (TWINS, AutoLoRA, RoLi) all assume access to robust pretrained features, leaving the non-robust backbone case unaddressed. The paper identifies that RFT delays task adaptation relative to standard fine-tuning, and this delay strongly correlates with the severity of suboptimal transfer.

## Input / Output

The method operates on 224×224 RGB images resized and normalized to [0,1], producing class labels for fine-grained image classification (200–257 classes across CUB-200-2011, Stanford Dogs, Caltech-256, Stanford Cars, and FGVC-Aircraft). During training, adversarial perturbations are generated via APGD (7 steps) under an ℓ∞-norm constraint with a time-varying budget ε(t) = α(t)·ε_g. Evaluation reports clean accuracy, adversarial accuracy at ε_g (via APGD with 10 steps or AutoAttack), and the proposed expected robustness metric computed as the trapezoidal AUC of the accuracy curve over [0, ε_g] in steps of 1/255.

## Method

Epsilon-Scheduling is a two-hinge linear schedule over the perturbation strength during robust fine-tuning: for the first T₁ epochs the model trains with zero perturbation (standard fine-tuning), then the perturbation linearly ramps from 0 to the target ε_g over T₂−T₁ epochs, and finally remains constant at ε_g. The default hyperparameters (T₁=12, T₂=37 out of 50 epochs) are derived from the observed task-adaptation delay in the most severe suboptimal-transfer case (SWIN on Aircraft). The key insight is that by deferring the robust objective, the model first adapts to the downstream task (reaching high clean accuracy) before gradually incorporating adversarial robustness, effectively acting as a curriculum that avoids the feature distortion caused by early strong perturbations. The paper also introduces 'expected robustness' — the uniform expectation of accuracy over [0, ε_g] — as a complementary evaluation metric that captures the full accuracy-robustness trade-off and enables more informative model selection than clean or worst-case accuracy alone. Experiments across six non-robust backbones (ViT, SWIN, ConvNext, ResNet-50, CLIP-ViT, CLIP-ConvNext) and five datasets at ε_g ∈ {4/255, 8/255} show consistent improvements in expected robustness, with an automated variant that detects adaptation convergence from validation accuracy also provided.

## Results

At the moderate perturbation regime (ε_g = 4/255), Epsilon-Scheduling (RFT-scheduler) dramatically improves clean accuracy over RFT-fix across all six backbones and five datasets; for example, SWIN on Aircraft jumps from 7.70% to 73.80% clean accuracy, and ClipCNX on Cars from 2.80% to 90.90%. At the high perturbation regime (ε_g = 8/255), RFT-fix almost always collapses to near-random performance (e.g., 1.30–8.64% clean accuracy), while RFT-scheduler consistently preserves strong clean accuracy (42.80–90.00%) and achieves higher expected robustness in all 30 model–dataset configurations. Paired t-tests confirm statistically significant gains for clean accuracy (p < 10⁻⁸), adversarial accuracy (p < 10⁻⁴), and expected robustness (p < 10⁻⁶) at both perturbation levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is RFT-fix, the standard practice of fine-tuning with a fixed perturbation strength ε_g throughout all training epochs (as in Hua et al., 2024). Epsilon-Scheduling (RFT-scheduler) wins on clean accuracy in virtually every configuration: at ε_g = 4/255 it improves average clean accuracy by 15–61 percentage points depending on the backbone (e.g., +61.04 for ClipViT, +23.38 for SWIN), and at ε_g = 8/255 the gap is even larger since RFT-fix collapses to near-random levels. On expected robustness (the paper's proposed metric), RFT-scheduler outperforms RFT-fix in all 30 model–dataset configurations at both perturbation levels. RFT-fix retains a slight edge on worst-case adversarial accuracy in only 9 of 30 cases at ε_g = 4/255 and 2 of 30 at ε_g = 8/255, but these gains come at the cost of catastrophic clean-accuracy loss (suboptimal transfer). The improvements are most pronounced on difficult fine-grained tasks (Aircraft, Cars) and with weaker or multi-modal backbones (ViT, ClipViT, ClipCNX), where RFT-fix clean accuracy drops below 10% while the scheduler maintains 55–90%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical adversarial training framework of Madry et al. (2018) to the transfer learning setting, building on prior work on linear warmup schedules in adversarial training from scratch (Pang et al., 2021; Debenedetti et al., 2023). The key departure is that all prior robust fine-tuning methods (TWINS, AutoLoRA, RoLi) assume access to robust pretrained backbones, whereas this paper is the first to propose an RFT strategy specifically targeting non-robust backbones, which constitute the vast majority of publicly available pretrained models. Epsilon-Scheduling generalizes simple linear warmup by introducing a two-hinge structure (initial zero-perturbation phase followed by a linear ramp), and the paper additionally introduces 'expected robustness' as a new evaluation metric that interpolates between clean and worst-case accuracy across the full perturbation range.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a critical practical gap: since nearly all widely used pretrained models are non-robust, the ability to robustly fine-tune from them is essential for real-world deployment, yet prior research had largely dismissed this scenario as infeasible. The finding that robust fine-tuning delays task adaptation—and that this delay is the primary mechanism behind suboptimal transfer—opens new avenues for understanding the optimization dynamics of adversarial objectives in transfer learning. Open questions include whether a theoretical account of the delay exists, whether similar dynamics occur in NLP or other modalities, and whether more sophisticated (learned or theoretically motivated) schedules can further close the remaining robustness gap. The technique is simple enough to be immediately adoptable, but robustness at high perturbation levels remains limited even with the scheduler.
