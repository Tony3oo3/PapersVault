## Problem

Visual prompt tuning adapts frozen vision Transformers to downstream tasks with minimal parameters, but the learned prompt embeddings remain abstract black-box vectors with no spatial grounding, no human-understandable semantic meaning, and no cross-layer structure. Existing interpretable methods (part-prototype networks, attribution maps) either operate only at the final layer, use class-specific prototypes that prevent cross-category concept sharing, or fail to connect discovered concepts back to the prompt embeddings themselves. This paper is the first to make visual prompts directly interpretable by linking them to category-agnostic concept prototypes grounded in specific image regions across multiple network depths.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images resized to 518×518 and processes them through a frozen ViT backbone (DeiT-S/B or DinoV2-S/B/L with register tokens). Beyond the final class label, IVPT produces per-concept region maps (h×w spatial probability maps assigning each patch to one of m concepts), interpretable prompt embeddings at each of the last four layers (with m = 17, 14, 11, 8 prototypes respectively, fused into n = 4 final prompts), and per-concept importance scores reflecting each concept's contribution to the classification decision. The entire interpretable machinery adds only ~107K tunable parameters (~0.12% of ViT-B's 86M parameters).

## Method

IVPT replaces unconstrained prompt vectors with prompts derived from category-agnostic concept prototypes via two modules: Concept Region Discovery (CRD) computes attention maps using negative squared Euclidean distance between prototypes and patch embeddings (with learnable spatial bias and softmax across concepts), then hard-assigns each patch to its highest-scoring concept to form non-overlapping region maps; Intra-region Feature Aggregation (IFA) computes a probability-weighted average of patch features within each region to produce the corresponding prompt embedding. A cross-layer prompt fusion mechanism uses a Gumbel-Softmax grouping layer to partition fine-grained shallow-layer prompts into n coarse-grained groups, which are averaged, layer-normalized, and passed through an MLP to yield the final n prompts injected into the network. Training optimizes three losses jointly: cross-entropy on averaged concept-conditioned category scores, a part-shaping loss (orthogonality, equivariance, foreground/background presence, entropy, total variation) that enforces spatially coherent and non-overlapping regions, and a concept region consistency loss (KL divergence) that aligns grouped fine-grained region maps with coarse-grained regions from the deepest layer, enforcing a fine-to-coarse semantic hierarchy.

## Results

On CUB-200-2011, IVPT achieves the best consistency, stability, and accuracy across all five backbones. With DinoV2-B, IVPT reaches 75.3% consistency, 75.9% stability, and 90.8% accuracy, outperforming the strongest part-prototype baseline (Huang et al., 68.6/71.4/89.9) and the best VPT variant (Gated Prompt Tuning w/ Proto., 70.3/71.8/90.4). On DinoV2-L, IVPT attains 72.6% consistency, 77.4% stability, and 91.1% accuracy. On PartImageNet and PASCAL-Part, IVPT similarly leads with 63.2%/71.5%/74.2% and 72.6%/77.4%/86.4% (Con./Sta./Acc.), respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IVPT outperforms all conventional part-prototype baselines (ProtoPNet, ProtoPool, TesNet) by large margins on both interpretability and accuracy; for example, on DinoV2-B it gains +47.7% consistency over ProtoPNet (75.3 vs. 27.6) and +24.1% over TesNet (75.3 vs. 51.2). Against the strongest part-prototype method (Huang et al., ICCV 2023), IVPT leads on DinoV2-B by +6.7% consistency and +4.5% stability, and on DinoV2-L by +5.2% consistency and +3.1% stability, though it trails by 2.2% consistency on the smaller DinoV2-S backbone. Among vanilla VPT methods (VPT, E2VPT, Gated Prompt Tuning), IVPT achieves dramatically higher interpretability scores (e.g., 75.3 vs. 14.6 consistency on DinoV2-B for VPT-Deep) while also matching or exceeding their accuracy. When prototypes are naively appended to VPT methods (w/ Proto. variants), IVPT still wins on all three metrics across every backbone, with the largest gap on DinoV2-B consistency (+5.0% over Gated Prompt Tuning w/ Proto.). On PartImageNet and PASCAL-Part, IVPT extends these gains, surpassing Huang et al. by +4.6% consistency and +9.2% stability on PASCAL-Part while also achieving the highest accuracy (86.4% vs. 78.9%).

## Relation to Prior Work

IVPT sits at the intersection of two research threads: visual prompt tuning (VPT, Jia et al. 2022), which injects learnable tokens into frozen ViTs for parameter-efficient adaptation, and part-prototype interpretable networks (ProtoPNet, ProtoPool, TesNet, Huang et al.), which ground predictions in human-understandable image regions. The key departure is that prior prototype methods were designed for conventional CNN architectures and operate only at the final layer with class-specific prototypes, while VPT methods treat prompts as opaque black-box vectors. IVPT bridges this gap by redefining prompts as region-grounded, category-agnostic concept embeddings organized hierarchically across layers, borrowing part-shaping losses from PDISCOFormer (Aniraj et al. 2024) to enforce spatial coherence of discovered regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to make the prompts themselves interpretable rather than only the final features or logits, which is critical for trust in safety-critical domains like medical imaging and autonomous driving. The cross-layer, fine-to-coarse concept hierarchy mirrors human visual reasoning and enables multi-granularity explanations that single-layer methods cannot provide. However, the approach relies on in-domain concept prototypes and is evaluated primarily on fine-grained classification, leaving open whether the interpretability gains transfer to open-world or generative settings. The paper also acknowledges that in complex scenes with occlusions, prototypes can misalign, suggesting robustness in cluttered environments remains an open challenge.
