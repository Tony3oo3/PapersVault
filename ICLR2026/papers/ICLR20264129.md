## Problem

Scattering transform (ST) distance provides informative, perception-aligned gradients for audio quality assessment, but its computational cost scales linearly with the number of paths P, making it impractical as a differentiable loss for stochastic gradient descent in neural network training. This limits the use of ST-based perceptual losses in differentiable digital signal processing (DDSP) tasks such as unsupervised sound matching, where the state-of-the-art multiscale spectral loss (MSS) is uninformative for misaligned or spectrotemporally modulated signals.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference audio signal (32768 samples at 8192 Hz for granular/chirplet synths; 44.1 kHz for TR-808) and a differentiable synthesizer decoder as input. A lightweight CNN encoder (604K parameters) operates on a constant-Q transform (5 octaves, 12 bins/octave) of the reference to predict synthesizer parameters θ_synth (2 parameters for granular/chirplet, 14 for TR-808). The decoder is nondeterministic, introducing random time shifts, and the training objective is to minimize the ST distance between reference and reconstruction without any parameter supervision.

## Method

SCRAPL exploits the tree-like decomposability of the ST loss into a sum over P paths, sampling a single path per iteration to obtain an unbiased gradient estimator (Proposition 3.1). To control the resulting variance, it introduces P-Adam, which maintains per-path first- and second-order moment estimates with decay rates adapted to the recency of each path's last visit, and P-SAGA, which stores the last P-Adam update for every path and combines it with a running average to form a lower-variance gradient. A θ-importance sampling heuristic further biases path selection toward those with high gradient curvature in the synthesizer parameter space, computed once before training via stochastic power iteration on Hessian-vector products. The full pipeline is applied to the joint time-frequency scattering transform (JTFS, 315 paths), achieving accuracy within a factor of two of full-tree JTFS at roughly a factor of two the runtime of MSS.

## Results

On the unsupervised granular synth sound matching task, SCRAPL with θ-importance sampling achieves 65.7% ± 4.2 θ_synth L1 error, compared to 42.4% for full-tree JTFS and 20.5% for the supervised P-loss upper bound. SCRAPL is approximately 19× faster than full JTFS (89.8 ms vs. 1730 ms per optimization step) while remaining within a factor of two in accuracy. All MSS-based losses (195–370% θ_synth L1) and pretrained embedding losses (159–166%) are dramatically outperformed by SCRAPL, which is the first method to effectively optimize a nondeterministic granular synthesizer in an unsupervised setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against full-tree JTFS (Muradeli et al., DAFx 2022), SCRAPL trades ~23 percentage points of θ_synth L1 accuracy (65.7% vs. 42.4%) for a ~19× reduction in per-step runtime (89.8 ms vs. 1730 ms) and a ~5× reduction in peak memory (2503 MB vs. 12967 MB). Against all four MSS variants from DDSP (Engel et al., ICLR 2020) and its revisited version (Schwär & Müller, 2023), SCRAPL is a clear winner: 65.7% vs. 195–370% θ_synth L1, with MSS methods failing to optimize the slope parameter at all (all stuck near 241–246%). Against the pretrained embedding losses MS-CLAP (Elizalde et al., ICASSP 2023) and PANNs Wavegram-Logmel (Kong et al., 2020), SCRAPL also dominates (65.7% vs. 159–166%), and uniquely optimizes both density and slope parameters rather than only density. On the Roland TR-808 task (Table 4), SCRAPL with θ-IS achieves 1050/1110 JTFS audio distance (micro/meso), outperforming all MSS variants in the unaligned (meso) setting and preserving the drum transient, though full JTFS remains superior (490/523). The supervised P-loss upper bound (20.5%) remains substantially better than any unsupervised method, as expected.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCRAPL extends the scattering transform literature (Mallat 2012; Andén & Mallat 2014) by making multivariable STs tractable as differentiable loss functions through stochastic path sampling, building directly on the tree-like decomposability of ST paths. It departs from the closest prior work, pruned graph scattering (pGST, Ioannidis et al. 2020), which fixes a ~10% path subset, by sampling a single path per step and stabilizing the resulting noisy gradient via path-wise adaptations of Adam (P-Adam) and SAGA (P-SAGA). The θ-importance sampling heuristic draws on non-uniform mini-batch sampling ideas from Schmidt et al. (2017) but applies them to the path dimension of a scattering transform rather than the data dimension, and is informed by the DDSP framework (Engel et al. 2020) where the decoder's parameter space provides auxiliary structure for biasing the sampling distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCRAPL makes joint time-frequency scattering (JTFS) — a transform with strong neurophysiological grounding and perceptual validity — practical as a training-time loss for neural audio models, opening a new class of DDSP problems involving nondeterministic synthesizers (granular, stochastic textures) where spectral losses like MSS fundamentally fail. The paper's ablation and 20-seed experiments provide solid evidence for the Pareto positioning, but SCRAPL still trails full JTFS by a factor of ~1.5 in accuracy and fails to recover low-energy signal components (e.g., drum decay), suggesting the single-path approximation has a hard floor. Open questions include convergence guarantees for the biased θ-IS variant, generalization to other ST architectures (spiral, roto-translation), and whether path importance can be meta-learned during training rather than computed once at initialization.
