## Problem

State-of-the-art music practice error detection (Polytune) suffers from two architectural limitations: its late-fusion design restricts inter-stream alignment to a single joint encoder layer, limiting cross-modality comparison, and its reliance on audio-only score representation introduces frequency ambiguity that degrades detection of concurrent notes. The paper asks whether alignment can be decoupled from feature extraction and whether combining audio with symbolic score representations can resolve this ambiguity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261609/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes three inputs: a score audio spectrogram and a practice audio spectrogram (each segmented into 2.145-second windows, 2048-point FFT, 128-sample hop, 512 mel-bins, split into 16×16 ViT patches yielding 512 tokens per segment), and a symbolic score prompt consisting of tokenized MIDI events (Time, On/Off, Label, Note) that shares the same vocabulary as the output. The T5 decoder autoregressively emits a stream of MIDI-like tokens where each note event carries an explicit error label (Correct, Missed, or Extra), with wrong notes represented as a paired missed-plus-extra event.

## Method

LadderSym introduces a two-stream interleaved encoder (Ladder) where a cross-attention alignment module precedes each ViT block, alternating direction per layer (score→practice, then practice→score) and additively fusing the cross-attention output into the target stream before it passes through its own ViT block. This design decouples temporal alignment from feature extraction, allowing each stream to specialize asymmetrically (practice stream retains local detail, score stream encodes global and cross-stream features) while still performing fine-grained alignment at every depth. The second innovation prepends a symbolic score prompt (tokenized MIDI with all notes labeled Correct) before the start-of-sequence token in the T5 decoder, giving the decoder an unambiguous reference that bypasses overlapping-frequency issues inherent in audio-only scores. The full model (12 encoder layers, 8 decoder layers, 172M parameters) is trained end-to-end with weighted cross-entropy loss (10× error weight), token-shuffling augmentation, and cosine-annealed AdamW optimization, achieving more than 2× F1 improvement on missed notes over Polytune on MAESTRO-E.

## Results

On MAESTRO-E, LadderSym achieves 54.7% F1 for missed notes (vs. 26.8% for Polytune) and 86.4% F1 for extra notes (vs. 72.0%), while maintaining 94.4% F1 for correct notes. On CocoChorales-E, it reaches 61.7% missed-note F1 and 61.4% extra-note F1, outperforming Polytune (51.3% and 46.8% respectively). On a newly curated real-world beginner piano dataset, LadderSym improves missed-note F1 from 63.9% to 78.5% over Polytune without any fine-tuning. The model is also more efficient, with 172M parameters (vs. 192M for Polytune) and ~97ms encoder latency (vs. 129ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LadderSym outperforms the prior state-of-the-art Polytune (Chou et al., 2025) on every metric across both datasets. The largest gain is on MAESTRO-E missed notes, where LadderSym more than doubles Polytune's F1 (54.7% vs. 26.8%), and on CocoChorales-E extra notes (61.4% vs. 46.8%, a 14.6-point improvement). Against the explicit-alignment baseline (Benetos et al., 2012; Wang et al., 2017, updated with MT3 and DTW), LadderSym achieves roughly 8–10× higher missed-note F1 (54.7% vs. 6.6% on MAESTRO-E; 61.7% vs. 7.7% on CocoChorales-E) and 2× higher extra-note F1, confirming that latent alignment with cross-stream interaction is far more robust than DTW-based sequence matching. On the real-world beginner dataset, LadderSym also leads Polytune on missed notes (78.5% vs. 63.9% F1) and extra notes (81.6% vs. 80.6%), demonstrating that the architectural gains generalize beyond synthetic data. Statistical tests (paired t-tests and Wilcoxon signed-rank, Bonferroni-corrected α=0.017) confirm all improvements are significant with p-values below 10⁻⁶⁷.

## Relation to Prior Work

LadderSym builds directly on Polytune (Chou et al., 2025), which established the latent-alignment paradigm for music error detection by replacing explicit DTW-based alignment with a transformer encoder-decoder that compares practice and score audio in latent space. The paper departs from Polytune in two specific ways: it replaces the single late-fusion joint encoder layer with interleaved cross-attention alignment modules at every layer (the 'Ladder' encoder), and it augments the audio-only score input with a symbolic MIDI prompt fed to the T5 decoder. The encoder design draws on hybrid multimodal fusion literature (Flamingo, BLIP) but diverges by performing iterative layer-wise cross-attention on full token sequences rather than conditioning on a single compressed vector, and by preserving asymmetric feature extraction across streams.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'chicken-and-egg' data problem in music error detection—no large-scale real-world annotated datasets exist—remains the field's central bottleneck, and LadderSym's potential as a human-in-the-loop annotation tool (cutting labeling time by ~3×) offers a concrete path toward addressing it. The architectural finding that frequent inter-stream alignment with asymmetric feature extraction outperforms both early and late fusion is a generalizable design principle for sequence-comparison tasks beyond music, potentially informing reward modeling in RL or skill assessment in other domains. Key limitations persist: missed-note detection in dense concurrent passages remains the hardest category (56.3% F1), segment-boundary errors from sustained notes crossing context windows are unaddressed, and the model cannot handle dramatic tempo deviations. The real-world validation set, while only 20 tracks, is the first of genuine beginner errors and confirms generalization beyond synthetic data.
