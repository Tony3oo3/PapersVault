## Problem

Existing DNAm prediction methods (CpGPT, MethylGPT, DeepCpG) operate under an imputation paradigm that requires partially measured methylation values as input, making them inapplicable to samples with no experimental DNAm data. Since array-based platforms capture only 1–3% of the ~28 million CpGs in the human genome, the vast majority of sites remain unmeasured in typical datasets, and no prior method can reconstruct a complete methylome for an entirely unmeasured sample. This paper asks whether whole-genome DNAm can be inferred from gene expression and DNA sequence alone, without any measured methylation input.

## Input / Output

For each sample–CpG pair, the model receives a ~25K-gene expression vector (log2-transformed TPM with pseudocount, quantized to [0,1] for batch-effect mitigation), a 1 kb DNA sequence window centered on the target CpG, and discrete genomic annotations (CpG island index, region type, chromosome). The output is a single β value in [0,1] per CpG; aggregating predictions across all ~27 million CpGs yields a complete whole-genome methylation profile for the sample. Critically, no DNAm measurement is required at inference time—the gene expression profile is shared across all CpGs for a given sample, while the DNA sequence and annotations are CpG-specific, so each site is predicted independently.

## Method

MethylProphet combines three encoding modules fed into a 12-layer Transformer encoder (768 hidden dim, 12 heads, ~110M params). A 6-layer Bottleneck MLP (hidden size 1024, bottleneck factor 4, ~70M params) compresses the ~25K-gene expression vector into a single compact embedding, avoiding the quadratic cost of attending over all gene tokens. A DNABERT-2-inspired BPE DNA tokenizer converts the 1 kb sequence into ~200 subword tokens (≈5× compression), each mapped to a learnable embedding, so that CpGs sharing sequence motifs receive similar representations without fixed per-CpG IDs. These embeddings, plus a learnable global token, a CpG island composite embedding (island-specific + region-type), and a chromosome embedding, are concatenated into a single sequence and processed by the Transformer; the final state of the global token passes through a linear layer and sigmoid to produce the predicted β value. Training is fully supervised with MSE loss over 1.6 billion CpG-sample pairs (ENCODE WGBS, 322B tokens) or ~450 million pairs (TCGA chromosome 1, 91B tokens), using AdamW with cosine LR decay on 32–64 NVIDIA L40s GPUs for one epoch.

## Results

On ENCODE WGBS data (Val CpG – Train Sample split), MethylProphet achieves a median across-sample PCC (MAS-PCC) of 0.72 and median across-CpG PCC (MAC-PCC) of 0.93, with MSE of 0.011 and MAE of 0.068. On TCGA chromosome 1 (Train CpG – Val Sample split), it reaches MAS-PCC of 0.55 and MAC-PCC of 0.93. In the most challenging setting (Val CpG – Val Sample, i.e., both unseen CpGs and unseen samples), MethylProphet still attains MAS-PCC of 0.34 and MAC-PCC of 0.93 on ENCODE, and MAS-PCC of 0.39 and MAC-PCC of 0.91 on TCGA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MethylProphet outperforms all four baselines on MAS-PCC across both datasets. Against CpGPT (the strongest imputation-based competitor), MethylProphet gains +0.05 MAS-PCC on ENCODE (0.369 vs 0.319) and +0.07 on TCGA chr1 (0.545 vs 0.479), while matching MAC-PCC (0.940 vs 0.940 on ENCODE; 0.925 vs 0.925 on TCGA). Against MethylGPT, the advantage is larger: +0.07 MAS-PCC on ENCODE and +0.11 on TCGA, with a notable MAC-PCC gap on TCGA (0.925 vs 0.836). DeepCpG performs poorly, with near-zero or negative MAS-PCC, indicating it fails to capture cross-sample variability. Against the CNN+attention baseline of Levy-Jurgenson et al., MethylProphet improves MAS-PCC by +0.17 on ENCODE (0.72 vs 0.55) and +0.20 on TCGA (0.42 vs 0.22) in the Val CpG – Train Sample setting, while also reducing MSE by roughly 3×. Critically, MethylProphet is the only method that can predict DNAm for entirely unseen samples without any measured methylation input, a capability the other baselines lack by design.

## Relation to Prior Work

MethylProphet departs from the imputation paradigm of recent Transformer-based methylation models (CpGPT, MethylGPT), which require partially observed DNAm values at inference and operate on only ~10⁴ CpGs via masked pre-training. It also extends earlier sequence/expression-based predictors (DeepCpG, Levy-Jurgenson et al.) that were limited to small CpG subsets or specific cancer cohorts. Architecturally, the model repurposes a bottleneck MLP for gene compression (inspired by Bachmann et al.) and a BPE-based DNA tokenizer (inspired by DNABERT-2) within a standard Transformer encoder, but the key conceptual contribution is the paradigm shift: predicting whole-genome DNAm from gene expression and local sequence alone, with zero measured methylation input. The paper positions itself within the broader genomics foundation-model trend (Geneformer, scGPT, scFoundation) but applies it to a cross-modality prediction task at unprecedented scale (322B tokens).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: MethylProphet can reconstruct full methylomes for the hundreds of thousands of RNA-seq-only samples in public repositories (e.g., 241K in GEO) that lack any methylation assay, enabling genome-wide epigenetic analysis at 100× the sample scale of prior array-limited studies. This opens applications in epigenetic aging clocks, cancer subtype stratification, and non-invasive biomarker discovery at full-genome resolution. Key limitations include the proof-of-concept framing (no architectural innovation), TCGA evaluation restricted to chromosomes 1–3, and the small ENCODE sample count (95) that constrains sample-level generalization. Open questions include whether performance scales further with more chromosomes/tissues, whether the model captures causal regulatory relationships or merely statistical correlations, and how predictions perform in clinically actionable settings.
