## Problem

Existing front-door (FD) adjustment methods estimate only population-average causal effects, while practical decision-making requires personalized (conditional) effects τ(C). The few conditional FD estimators that exist (e.g., LobsterNet) lack debiasedness, meaning their convergence is bottlenecked by nuisance estimation accuracy. This paper closes both gaps by introducing debiased learners for heterogeneous FD effects that achieve quasi-oracle rates even when nuisance functions converge as slowly as n^{-1/4}.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264376/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. tabular samples V = (C, X, Z, Y) where C is a vector of observed covariates, X ∈ {0,1} is a binary treatment, Z ∈ {0,1} is a binary mediator, and Y is a continuous outcome, under the FD structural assumptions (unmeasured confounding between X and Y, but Z unconfounded given C and (X,C) blocks spurious Z–Y paths). It outputs the conditional front-door effect τ(C) = E[Y|do(X=1),C] − E[Y|do(X=0),C] as a function of C, estimated via cross-fitted regressions on pseudo-outcomes. Nuisance functions (propensities e, mediator probabilities q, outcome regressions m) are learned with XGBoost, and the final τ(C) regression uses ridge OLS on the constructed pseudo-outcomes.

## Method

The paper proposes two debiased learners built on orthogonal statistical learning. FD-DR-Learner constructs a front-door pseudo-outcome (FDPO) φ_{x̄}(V;η) combining inverse-probability-weighted residuals, density-ratio corrections, and a g-formula term, whose conditional mean equals τ_{x̄}(C); regressing φ₁ − φ₀ on C yields a doubly robust estimator whose error decomposes into second-order products of nuisance errors. FD-R-Learner instead exploits a Robinson partial-linear decomposition of the FD data-generating process: it estimates b(C) (the X→Z pathway effect) and g(XC) (the Z→Y pathway effect) each via a standard back-door R-Learner sub-procedure, then composes them as τ(C) = b(C)·γ_g(C) where γ_g is learned by regressing a pseudo-g function (which corrects for propensity error) on C. Both learners use cross-fitting (two-way for FD-DR, three-way for FD-R) and attain quasi-oracle rates whenever all nuisances converge at n^{-1/4}, with FD-DR offering double robustness and FD-R offering lower variance under weak overlap by avoiding density-ratio estimation.

## Results

In synthetic experiments (d=10, R=100 replications, n ranging from 1,000 to 50,000), both FD-DR and FD-R learners achieve substantially lower RMSE than the plug-in baseline (FD-PI) across all settings. When nuisance functions are restricted to converge at the n^{-1/4} rate, FD-DR and FD-R maintain reliable convergence while FD-PI converges much more slowly. Under weak-overlap stress (steepening treatment logit with κ_e up to 10), FD-R remains stable and consistently outperforms both FD-PI and FD-DR, which suffers variance inflation from inverse weights. In the FARS seat-belt case study, over 95% of state-year units show decreased fatality under primary-law regimes, with age, time of day, and driver status identified as dominant heterogeneity drivers via SHAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the plug-in front-door estimator (FD-PI), which directly substitutes estimated nuisances into the identification formula (Eq. 49). FD-DR and FD-R consistently dominate FD-PI in RMSE across all synthetic regimes: varying sample size, n^{-1/4}-rate nuisance convergence, injected nuisance noise (ρ from 0 to 1.0), and weak-overlap severity. Under increasing nuisance noise (ρ ≥ 0.4), FD-PI's RMSE deteriorates rapidly while both proposed learners remain stable, with FD-R achieving the lowest RMSE at higher noise levels. Under weak overlap (κ_e = 8–10), FD-DR suffers noticeable variance inflation due to its use of inverse propensity weights and density ratios, whereas FD-R avoids these ratios entirely and outperforms both FD-PI and FD-DR. The paper also positions its methods against prior FD estimators (Fulcher et al. 2019, Guo et al. 2023, Jung et al. 2024) and the conditional FD estimator LobsterNet (Chen et al. 2025), noting that all prior methods either target only average effects or lack the debiasedness/quasi-oracle rate guarantees provided here, though no direct numerical comparison with those methods is conducted.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the DR-Learner (Kennedy, 2023) and R-Learner (Nie & Wager, 2021) — both developed under the back-door/ignorability criterion — to the front-door (FD) identification framework of Pearl (1995), using the orthogonal statistical learning template of Foster & Syrgkanis (2023) for the error analysis. Prior FD estimation work (Fulcher et al., 2019; Guo et al., 2023; Jung et al., 2024) targeted only the average treatment effect, and the one existing conditional-FD estimator (Chen et al., 2025, LobsterNet) lacks debiasedness. The key conceptual move is showing that the FD heterogeneous effect decomposes into two back-door R-Learner subproblems (X→Z and Z→Y), enabling a three-stage procedure that inherits quasi-oracle guarantees without requiring density-ratio estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a concrete gap: practitioners needing personalized causal effects under unmeasured confounding with a valid mediator now have two debiased options with explicit rate guarantees, rather than relying on plug-in estimators that degrade under slow nuisance convergence. The FARS seat-belt case study demonstrates practical utility in public-policy settings. Key limitations include the restriction to binary mediators (many real-world settings have continuous or multi-dimensional mediators) and the positivity assumption, which the authors acknowledge inflates variance for FD-DR under weak overlap. Extending both learners to continuous mediators and developing adaptive routing between FD-DR and FD-R based on overlap diagnostics are natural next steps.
