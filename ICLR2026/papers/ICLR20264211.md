## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary object detection methods suffer from two intertwined limitations: they depend on heavy text encoders or LLM prompting at inference time, incurring substantial computational overhead, and their tightly coupled multimodal training objectives create an optimization conflict that forces a trade-off between closed-set detection accuracy and open-world generalization. No prior framework simultaneously eliminates inference-time text encoding and resolves the gradient interference between localization and semantic alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264211/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image as input and produces a set of bounding boxes each paired with an open-vocabulary category label drawn from both base (seen) and novel (unseen) classes. At inference, no text encoder or LLM is invoked; all semantic knowledge is pre-computed into a fixed prototype pool (6,003 prototypes) that is queried via cosine similarity, enabling 135 ms per-image latency with a ResNet-50 backbone. The DETR decoder uses 2,000 object queries by default, and the semantic predictor maps each query to a probability distribution over the full base∪novel category space.

## Method

DeCo-DETR introduces a three-component decoupled cognition framework built on a standard DETR backbone. First, the Dynamic Hierarchical Concept Pool (DHCP) offline constructs a two-level prototype matrix by generating region-level descriptions with LLaVA, filtering them via CLIP cosine-similarity thresholding, then clustering with K-Means (coarse) and DBSCAN (fine), and refines prototypes online via momentum-based soft-assignment updates. Second, Hierarchical Knowledge Distillation (Hi-Know DPA) projects decoder queries into the shared CLIP embedding space through a learnable MLP, computes softmax-weighted aggregation over all prototypes to form semantic-enhanced queries, and distills a frozen CLIP teacher's distribution via KL divergence. Third, Parametric Decoupling Training (PD-DuGi) splits optimization into a localization stream (standard Hungarian-matching detection loss on the backbone/decoder) and a semantic alignment stream (cross-entropy against CLIP teacher targets on a separate predictor), with explicit gradient stopping between streams and a cosine-annealed weighting schedule that prioritizes detection early and shifts toward alignment later.

## Results

On OV-COCO (V-OVD setting), DeCo-DETR achieves 41.3% AP₅₀ⁿᵒᵛᵉˡ, surpassing the strongest baseline LBP (37.8%) by +3.5 points, with 56.7% AP₅₀ᵇᵃˢᵉ and 53.1% overall AP₅₀. On the long-tailed OV-LVIS benchmark, DeCo-DETR sets a new record with 35.2% overall AP (29.4% AP_r, 33.1% AP_c, 38.9% AP_f), edging out Mamba (35.0%). Inference latency is 135 ms/image with a ResNet-50 backbone (44M params, 235 GFLOPs), adding only ~15 ms over Deformable DETR while eliminating the need for a text encoder at test time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeCo-DETR achieves the best novel-class AP₅₀ in three of four OV-COCO settings: +3.5 over LBP on V-OVD (41.3 vs 37.8), +5.8 over RALF on G-OVD (47.1 vs 41.3), and +3.7 over CAKE on WS-OVD (45.5 vs 41.8). On C-OVD, however, BIRDet retains a lead (46.2 vs 44.9 novel AP₅₀; 58.6 vs 56.3 overall), making it the one setting where DeCo-DETR trails the strongest baseline. On OV-LVIS, DeCo-DETR edges out Mamba by 0.2 AP (35.2 vs 35.0) and leads on rare-class AP_r (29.4 vs 29.3) and frequent-class AP_f (38.9 vs 36.8), while Mamba is slightly ahead on common-class AP_c (34.2 vs 33.1). Compared to distillation-based methods (ViLD, DK-DETR, OADP, CAKE), DeCo-DETR consistently improves novel AP by 3–12 points across settings while maintaining 135 ms inference latency—roughly 2× faster than fusion-based approaches like Grounding DINO (~280 ms) that require a text encoder at test time.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeCo-DETR extends the DETR-based OVOD lineage initiated by ViLD and refined by DK-DETR and DetCLIP, which distill CLIP/VLM knowledge into detectors but retain text-encoder dependency at inference. It departs from this line by replacing online text encoding with a pre-computed hierarchical prototype space (DHCP) built offline from LLaVA region descriptions filtered through CLIP, and by introducing gradient-isolated dual-stream training (PD-DuGi) to resolve the known optimization conflict between localization and semantic alignment. Conceptually, it positions itself against prompt-based methods (DetPro, PromptDet) and recent text-light approaches (CAKE) that still struggle with fine-grained detection, framing the core contribution as a 'decoupled cognition' paradigm rather than a single architectural change.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical bottlenecks in OVOD deployment—text-encoder latency at inference and the closed-set/open-world accuracy trade-off—offering a 2× speedup over fusion-based methods like Grounding DINO while maintaining competitive AP. However, the approach still requires a large LVLM (≥13B) for offline prototype construction, and the fixed prototype space may limit true open-world generalization to categories well-represented in the training distribution. Open questions include how the hierarchical prototype space adapts to genuinely novel semantic domains absent from the initial LLaVA descriptions, and whether the decoupling strategy generalizes beyond DETR-style set-prediction to other detection paradigms.
