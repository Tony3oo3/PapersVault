## Problem

Feed-forward novel view synthesis methods diverge into bias-driven approaches (relying on explicit 3D representations like NeRF/3DGS and SfM-estimated camera poses) and data-centric approaches (learning 3D implicitly from images). This paper asks which paradigm scales better as training data grows, and demonstrates that methods with less 3D knowledge dependence accelerate more with data volume, eventually outperforming their 3D-knowledge-driven counterparts—a principle the authors term 'the less you depend, the more you learn.'
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261422/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes exactly 2 sparse, unposed RGB images at 224×224 resolution (constrained by DINOv2's patch size of 14) with no camera pose information for either input or target views. It produces a single synthesized novel view image at the same resolution. Unusually, the target viewpoint is specified implicitly through a learned 7D latent pose (translation + quaternion) rather than explicit SE(3) coordinates; at inference, explicit camera control is recovered by fine-tuning a lightweight linear mapper from SE(3) to the latent space using only 1.8% of the training data.

## Method

UP-LVSM is a pure Transformer encoder-decoder that encodes input images via a pretrained DINOv2 backbone into patch tokens, then reconstructs a scene representation as implicit latent tokens (no explicit 3D geometry). The core novelty is the Latent Plücker Learner: an autoencoder that distills the target image into a compact 7D latent pose token (translation + quaternion), creating a bottleneck that prevents the latent from encoding image content, then analytically upsamples this token into pixel-level Plücker ray embeddings (o×d, d) to provide fine-grained per-ray conditioning for the decoder. This design eliminates both explicit scene structure (replacing NeRF/3DGS with learned latents) and pose annotations (replacing SfM poses with self-supervised latent pose learning), and the paper's scalability analysis across four datasets shows this dual removal yields the steepest performance gains as training data increases from 1K to 66K scenes.

## Results

On RealEstate10K, UP-LVSM achieves 28.82 PSNR / 0.891 SSIM / 0.104 LPIPS on the full evaluation set, outperforming all pose-dependent and pose-free baselines. In the most challenging small-overlap setting, UP-LVSM reaches 24.54 PSNR, surpassing the next-best method LVSM (22.71 PSNR) by 1.83 dB. Across four datasets (RealEstate10K, DL3DV, ACID, Objaverse), UP-LVSM consistently exhibits the highest scalability, with an average PSNR gain of 2.63 per 4× data increase on RealEstate10K, compared to 0.64 for LVSM and 0.39 for MVSplat.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UP-LVSM outperforms all seven compared baselines on RealEstate10K full evaluation. Against the strongest posed data-centric baseline LVSM (27.60 PSNR), UP-LVSM gains +1.22 PSNR despite requiring no pose annotations at all. Against bias-driven posed methods MVSplat (26.45) and PixelSplat (25.51), the margins are +2.37 and +3.31 PSNR respectively. In the posed-target setting, UP-LVSM beats NoPoSplat (25.46) by +3.36 PSNR and its own PT-LVSM variant (26.00) by +2.82 PSNR. Among concurrent unposed methods, UP-LVSM surpasses the bias-driven SPFSplat (25.84) by +2.98 PSNR and the data-centric Rayzer (27.48, trained with N≥5 views vs. UP-LVSM's N=2) by +1.34 PSNR. The advantage is most pronounced in the small-overlap regime, where UP-LVSM leads LVSM by 1.83 PSNR, confirming that removing 3D knowledge dependencies yields the largest gains under the most challenging conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

UP-LVSM builds directly on the data-centric Transformer paradigm established by LVSM (Jin et al., 2025), which replaced explicit 3D representations (NeRF, 3DGS) with latent scene tokens, and on RUST (Sajjadi et al., 2023), which first demonstrated NVS without any pose annotations. The key departure is the Latent Plücker Learner, an autoencoder-style module that distills a target view into a compact 7D pose token and analytically expands it into pixel-level Plücker ray conditions, thereby eliminating both explicit scene structure and all pose dependencies simultaneously. The paper also positions itself against concurrent unposed methods (Rayzer, SPFSplat) and provides a systematic empirical analysis across bias-driven (MVSplat, NoPoSplat) and data-centric (LVSM, PT-LVSM) methods that prior work had not conducted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that reducing dependence on explicit 3D knowledge (both scene structure and SfM-derived poses) unlocks superior data scalability—provides a concrete design principle for the field, suggesting that the era of abundant 2D data favors implicit over explicit 3D modeling. The noise-injection and Objaverse control experiments convincingly attribute the scalability bottleneck to SfM pose noise rather than to the information content of poses themselves, which reframes how practitioners should view pose annotations. Open questions remain: extrapolation performance degrades sharply for all methods, the DINOv2 patch size of 14 limits fine-grained rendering, and it is unclear whether the scalability trend persists beyond the 66K-scene scale tested here, particularly as video-based training data becomes available.
