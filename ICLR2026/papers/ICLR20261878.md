## Problem

Existing vision-language medical segmentation models frequently produce masks that contradict the accompanying text on key attributes such as lesion count, laterality, and coarse spatial position. The paper traces this failure to two root causes: (i) highly templated clinical language (e.g., ~7,000 cases sharing only ~300 text templates) causes standard one-to-one InfoNCE contrastive learning to generate massive false negatives, degrading cross-modal alignment; and (ii) predominantly vision-driven, unidirectional cross-attention lacks an explicit language-dominant, spatially aware pathway, preventing effective injection of textual semantics into the pixel grid.

## Input / Output

The method takes a medical image (chest X-ray, chest CT slice, or colonoscopy frame) together with a short clinical-style text description specifying attributes like laterality, anatomical location, and lesion quantity, and produces a pixel-level binary segmentation mask. The CLIP text encoder is kept frozen throughout training to preserve stable semantic anchors, while the visual encoder (a hybrid CNN+KAN U-Net) is fine-tuned at a small learning rate. Text descriptions are deliberately short and location/quantity-oriented rather than long free-form reports, which is what makes the templated-reuse problem acute.

## Method

C2Seg is a two-stage framework. In Stage I (pretraining), Cluster-aware Contrastive Learning (CaCL) computes text-text cosine similarities in the frozen CLIP space, applies row-mean debiasing and temperature scaling to produce soft label distributions, and then matches the predicted image-text similarity distributions to these soft targets via a bidirectional cross-entropy objective, replacing hard one-to-one InfoNCE and suppressing false-negative repulsive gradients. In Stage II (fusion), the Bidirectional Complementary Attention Module (BCAM) runs two parallel paths: a vision-dominant path that softmaxes attention over the language axis and aggregates language values to yield a sentence-injected visual feature map at full spatial resolution, and a language-dominant path that normalizes the transposed attention over the spatial axis, applies each token's spatial weights to visual values, and aggregates across tokens to produce a spatially coherent language-guided feature map. A KAN-based Attention Gating (K-Gate) module then applies two independent KAN gating heads (tanh-bounded) to each modality stream for nonlinear, fine-grained feature selection before a 1×1 convolution fuses the gated streams. KAN layers also replace linear projections in the visual encoder (hybrid CNN+KAN) and in BCAM's key/value computations, providing nonlinear modeling capacity with limited parameter overhead.

## Results

C2Seg achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four medical segmentation benchmarks. On QaTa-COV19, it reaches 85.25% Dice and 76.97% mIoU with HD95 of 12.71 and ASSD of 3.38; on MosMedData+, it attains 77.81% Dice and 65.17% mIoU with HD95 of 15.02 and ASSD of 3.76. On the colonoscopy datasets, C2Seg scores 91.82% Dice / 86.81% mIoU (CVC-ClinicDB) and 91.92% Dice / 85.27% mIoU (Kvasir), all with the lowest HD95 and ASSD among compared methods. Notably, C2Seg uses only 18.92M parameters, substantially fewer than most multimodal competitors (e.g., LAVT 118.6M, SLViT 131.5M, RefSegformer 195M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

C2Seg outperforms all 21 baselines on Dice and mIoU across all four datasets, and achieves the best HD95 on every dataset. On QaTa-COV19, it surpasses the strongest multimodal competitor MedLangViT by +0.98% Dice, +1.04% mIoU, −1.80 HD95, and −0.59 ASSD; on MosMedData+, the margins over MedLangViT are +1.86% Dice, +2.00% mIoU, −3.27 HD95, and −0.36 ASSD. On CVC-ClinicDB, C2Seg leads TGANet (the prior best) by +1.89% Dice and +2.25% mIoU, though TGANet retains a marginal ASSD advantage (2.11 vs. 2.23). On Kvasir, C2Seg beats LAVT by +1.09% Dice, +0.37% mIoU, −2.28 HD95, and −0.17 ASSD. Against unimodal baselines, C2Seg gains roughly 4–5% Dice over nnUNet on QaTa-COV19 and ~5% on MosMedData+, confirming the substantial benefit of incorporating textual guidance. With only 18.92M parameters, C2Seg is 6–10× smaller than the largest competitors (LAVT, SLViT, RefSegformer) while delivering superior accuracy on all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

C2Seg builds directly on the UKAN hybrid CNN+KAN encoder (Li et al., 2025a) and the frozen CLIP text encoder (Radford et al., 2021), while adopting KAN layers (Liu et al., 2025) as nonlinear components in attention and gating. Its core architectural departure targets M3Att/DualA-style bidirectional fusion (Liu et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2025), which the authors argue remains vision-centric because the 'language-dominant' branch only updates text tokens without producing a spatially-aligned feature map. On the pretraining side, CaCL extends standard InfoNCE contrastive learning by replacing hard one-to-one labels with batch-wise soft distributions derived from text-text similarity, a natural response to the templated-report problem that prior medical contrastive methods (ConVIRT, MedCLIP) do not explicitly address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles a concrete and underappreciated failure mode in medical VLMs—templated clinical language creating false negatives in contrastive pretraining—and offers a principled soft-label fix that is easy to adopt in existing pipelines. However, the authors acknowledge the absence of a dedicated quantitative text-mask consistency metric (relying on qualitative visualization) and do not test robustness to noisy or incomplete text, leaving open whether the gains generalize beyond clean, structured descriptions. The KAN integration, while well-motivated, shows only marginal gains over linear/MLP alternatives in ablation, suggesting the architectural novelty is secondary to the contrastive-learning and bidirectional-attention contributions.
