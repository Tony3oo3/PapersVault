## Problem

Existing time-series forecasting benchmarks evaluate models only at the holistic, end-to-end level, leaving it unclear which internal design components (e.g., instance normalization, series decomposition, patch vs. token embedding, MLP vs. Transformer) are effective under specific data conditions. Ongoing debates in the literature produce conflicting recommendations without systematic, module-level evidence to resolve them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265115/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a lookback window of L past time steps across d_X variables and produces H-step future predictions, evaluated under both univariate (d_X=1) and multivariate (d_X>1) settings with short-term (L>H) and long-term (L≤H) horizons. The model selection toolkit additionally takes computed dataset characteristics (trend, seasonality, stationarity, shifting, transition, correlation, n-feature, HL-ratio) and outputs a recommended module configuration (pre-processing, embedding, fusion type, feed-forward architecture) without any training.

## Method

TIMERECIPE defines a canonical architecture with five components—pre-processing, embedding, feed-forward modeling, projection, and post-processing—and systematically benchmarks combinations of 2 pre-processing options (instance normalization, series decomposition), 5 embedding strategies (token, patch, invert, frequency, none), 3 feed-forward architectures (MLP, Transformer, RNN), and 2 fusion types (temporal, feature), yielding over 100 distinct model configurations across 10,000+ experiments on LTSF, PEMS, and M4 datasets. The framework automatically adjusts inter-module connections, hidden dimensions, and tensor operations based on the selected configuration, enabling fair comparison of individual module contributions. A correlation analysis using t-tests links statistically significant module effectiveness to data properties (e.g., instance normalization helps when shifting is high; patch embedding helps when trend is high). A training-free LightGBM regression model is then trained on the benchmark rank scores to predict the optimal module configuration for new datasets from their computed characteristics, selecting architectures that approach the globally optimal configuration without exhaustive search.

## Results

Across 102 evaluated forecasting scenarios (spanning LTSF, PEMS, and M4 datasets with varying horizons and univariate/multivariate settings), TIMERECIPE identifies module-level configurations that outperform the best existing SOTA model in 92 cases (>90%), achieving an average forecasting error reduction of 5.4% (std=2.88%, t-test p=0.0069) and an average ranking improvement of 13.66 positions. On PEMS03 (horizon 12, multivariate), the top TIMERECIPE configuration achieves MSE 0.714 versus iTransformer's 0.739 (rank 7). The training-free LightGBM-based model selection toolkit selects top-3 architectures that consistently outperform the existing best model on out-of-distribution datasets (e.g., Social_12_S: MSE 0.0897 vs. PatchTST's 0.0994, a 5.0% improvement).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIMERECIPE outperforms all three compared SOTA baselines across the majority of scenarios. Against **PatchTST** (Nie et al., ICLR 2023), TIMERECIPE's exhaustive search finds configurations with up to 16.4% lower MSE (Social_12_S: 0.0854 vs. 0.0994) and 0.9% lower MSE on ETTh1_24_M (0.2963 vs. 0.2988); the LightGBM toolkit's top-3 selections also beat PatchTST on both datasets. Against **DLinear** (Zeng et al., AAAI 2023), TIMERECIPE achieves 1.3% lower MSE on Environment_48_S (0.2912 vs. 0.2950) and 1.4% lower MSE on Security_12_S (74.22 vs. 75.29), with toolkit selections matching or exceeding DLinear in 2 of 3 test cases. Against **iTransformer** (Liu et al., ICLR 2024), TIMERECIPE's best configuration on PEMS03 (horizon 12) achieves MSE 0.714 versus iTransformer's 0.739, with iTransformer ranking only 7th among all evaluated module combinations. Overall, TIMERECIPE wins in 92/102 scenarios, with the gap widening on datasets with strong trend or shifting characteristics where decomposition and normalization modules prove most impactful.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIMERECIPE departs from existing time-series forecasting benchmarks (TFB, GIFT-Eval, BasicTS, ProbTS) that evaluate entire end-to-end models, instead decomposing the canonical architecture (pre-processing, embedding, feed-forward modeling, projection, post-processing) into independently swappable modules and benchmarking their individual and joint effectiveness. It unifies the design space of widely adopted models—Informer, Autoformer, PatchTST, iTransformer, DLinear, FITS, TimeMixer—under a single parameterized framework, making them special cases of module combinations. The paper also positions itself against the ongoing architectural debate (Transformer vs. MLP vs. RNN) by treating architecture as one module choice among several, rather than the defining characteristic of a model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TIMERECIPE provides the first systematic, module-level recipe for supervised time-series forecasting, offering practitioners data-property-conditioned guidance (e.g., use invert embedding when correlation is high, use patch embedding when trend is strong) rather than a single 'best' model. The training-free LightGBM-based selection toolkit that consistently picks architectures beating SOTA without any model training is a practical contribution for real-world deployment. Key limitations include the purely empirical nature of the findings (no theoretical explanation for why certain modules interact with data properties in specific ways), the exclusion of foundation/prompt-based models and highly specialized designs like Crossformer, and the reliance on a fixed set of six data-property descriptors that may not capture all relevant characteristics. Open questions include whether these module-level insights transfer to foundation models and whether the correlations generalize to unseen domains beyond the benchmarked datasets.
