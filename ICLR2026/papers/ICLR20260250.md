## Problem

Prior work on LLM social bias has focused narrowly on conformity in isolated, single-round settings, leaving unexplored how models build rapport from interaction history, selectively integrate high-quality peer information, and resist misleading inputs under complex multi-agent dynamics. No existing benchmark provides fine-grained, simultaneous control over peer reliability, current peer behavior, and the model's own self-confidence to systematically dissect these social reasoning abilities.

## Input / Output

The benchmark is dynamically instantiated per model: peer responses in both the interaction history and current round are constructed from the model's own self-consistency belief distribution (T stochastic samples per question), so each model encounters a tailored stress test rather than a fixed benchmark. The model receives a multiple-choice question, a simulated history of prior Q&A rounds with peer answers at a controlled rapport level (0–100%), and current-round peer responses in one of three behavioral modes (support, oppose-hard, oppose-easy). Output is a single MCQ answer evaluated on four metrics: accuracy, utility (fraction of originally-wrong answers corrected by peer input), resistance (fraction of originally-correct answers preserved), and robustness (O-K Δ, the relative accuracy change from isolated to social setting).

## Method

KAIROS constructs a two-step dynamic evaluation: (1) extract each model's original answer and confidence via self-consistency sampling (T stochastic generations, entropy-based high/low confidence split), then (2) simulate a quiz-style multi-agent contest where peer rapport is controlled by varying the fraction of history rounds in which peers align with the model's prior answers, and current-round peers are assigned support/oppose-hard/oppose-easy roles derived from the model's belief distribution. For mitigation, the paper compares prompting (empowered, reflective), SFT on templated gold responses, and GRPO reinforcement learning with four systematically varied factors: MAS vs. non-MAS training context, normal vs. debating system prompt, outcome-based vs. debating reward, and low-confidence vs. low-correctness data filtering. The key finding is that GRPO trained with full MAS context and a simple outcome-based reward (NS-OR) achieves the best accuracy-robustness trade-off, while debate-style reasoning and data filtering improve surface accuracy but degrade robustness under social perturbation.

## Results

Across 11 models, GPT-5 achieves the highest Original accuracy (90.17%) with minimal robustness loss (O-K Δ = −1.4%), while Gemini-2.5-Pro suffers the largest social degradation (−10.5% O-K Δ under Base). Smaller models (≤32B) show an average O-K Δ of −5.65% under Base prompting, which worsens to −11.25% under Empowered prompting; larger models (>32B) reverse this trend, achieving +0.12% O-K Δ under Empowered. Among training-based mitigations, GRPO-MAS-NS-OR (Normal system prompt + Outcome reward + MAS context) yields the best accuracy–robustness trade-off, averaging 65.6% Original and 60.7% KAIROS accuracy across small models, with GRPO improving Original accuracy by +12.3% and KAIROS accuracy by +16.4% over SFT on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper adopts the Empowered and Reflected prompting strategies from Weng et al. (2025) as its primary prompting baselines, alongside a Base (no special prompting) condition. Empowered prompting helps larger models (>32B) close the robustness gap (O-K Δ improves from −3.64% to +0.12%) but paradoxically worsens it for smaller models (−5.65% → −11.25%), because the Original accuracy gain outpaces the KAIROS gain. Reflected prompting is consistently detrimental, degrading KAIROS accuracy by 2.83% on average for small models and producing the worst O-K Δ (−11.30%). For training-based mitigations, SFT uniformly degrades robustness (e.g., Qwen2.5-14B O-K Δ drops from −8.7% to −25.3%), while GRPO-MAS-NS-OR outperforms both SFT and all prompting baselines on both accuracy and robustness, achieving 76.4% Original and 71.5% KAIROS accuracy on Qwen2.5-14B versus 65.3%/48.8% for SFT. GRPO with MAS context and outcome rewards is the only configuration that simultaneously improves task performance and preserves (or slightly improves) robustness relative to Base, particularly for models ≥7B.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conformity-bias evaluation line initiated by Weng et al. (2025) and Zhu et al. (2024), which measured LLM alignment with peer answers in single-round, isolated settings. KAIROS generalizes that task by introducing multi-round interaction history (rapport), model-tailored peer behaviors (support, oppose-hard, oppose-easy), and self-confidence conditioning, creating a richer social simulation. On the mitigation side, it moves beyond the prompting-based debiasing explored in prior conformity work to systematically compare SFT and GRPO-based reinforcement learning under varied reward and context configurations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that model scale is the dominant moderator of social susceptibility in MAS, and that the simplest GRPO configuration (normal system prompt + outcome reward + MAS context) outperforms elaborate debate-style RL—a practically useful negative result for practitioners designing post-training pipelines. Key open questions remain: how to make sub-32B models robust without sacrificing accuracy, whether the MCQ-based evaluation underestimates real-world vulnerability (the open-ended results suggest it does), and how these susceptibilities interact with adversarial manipulation in deployed multi-agent systems. The KAIROS benchmark itself is a useful tool for tracking progress on social robustness as MAS deployments grow.
