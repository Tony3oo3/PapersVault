## Problem

Existing self-refinement methods for LLMs are fundamentally reactive: they apply a fixed number of refinement iterations after generation is complete, without the ability to autonomously determine whether, when, or how to refine based on the evolving generation context. This post-hoc paradigm leads to error propagation, unnecessary token overhead, and reliance on external feedback signals that are costly or unavailable in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265124/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a text query alongside a system prompt that enforces a three-tag output structure: <think> for the reasoning trajectory, <refine> (nested within <think>) for in-process correction segments, and <answer> for the final response. Unlike post-hoc methods that regenerate entire responses, PASR produces a single generation trace where refinement segments are interleaved with forward reasoning, and the model may invoke <refine> multiple times at arbitrary points during generation. The final answer is extracted from the complete trace, with refinements having already influenced subsequent reasoning steps.

## Method

PASR formulates proactive self-refinement as a Markov Decision Process and trains the model using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), an on-policy RL algorithm. At each generation timestep, the model chooses between content generation (extending reasoning forward) and trace refinement (revising previously generated content), with the policy shaped by a hybrid reward combining format compliance, LLM-judged accuracy, and a comparison-based refinement signal. The refinement reward is the key design choice: it samples multiple standard (non-refined) responses to estimate a baseline accuracy, then assigns positive reward only when the refined output exceeds that baseline by a tolerance margin, negative reward when it falls below, and a small penalty when it is comparable—thereby encouraging timely refinements while discouraging both harmful and unnecessary modifications. This comparison-based signal avoids the reward hacking that coarse refinement-triggered rewards produce, and the on-policy exploration lets the model discover adaptive refinement strategies that prompting or supervised fine-tuning cannot elicit.

## Results

On Qwen3-8B, PASR achieves an average score of 69.1 across 10 benchmarks, improving over the vanilla model (60.9) by 8.2 points while reducing average token consumption by 41.6%. On Qwen2.5-7B, PASR reaches 61.7 average (vs. 55.9 vanilla, +4.8) with only an 8.4% increase in token usage. Notable individual gains include +14.1 on DROP and +5.2 on MATH (Qwen3-8B), and +26.0 on MMLU (Qwen2.5-7B). PASR is the strongest method that operates without oracle or external feedback signals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PASR outperforms all non-oracle baselines on both backbones. On Qwen3-8B, PASR (69.1 avg) beats Self-Refine (65.0, +4.1), SCoRe (64.0, +5.1), RISE (59.2, +9.9), PTR (54.5, +14.6), STaR (38.7, +30.4), and ISC (38.6, +30.5). On Qwen2.5-7B, PASR (61.7) edges out PTR (61.6, +0.1) and Self-Refine (57.5, +4.2), and substantially surpasses SCoRe (48.2, +13.5), STaR (49.7, +12.0), and ISC (43.7, +18.0). The only method that exceeds PASR is Self-Refine+ with oracle ground-truth feedback (72.8 on Qwen3-8B, 62.3 on Qwen2.5-7B), which requires access to reference answers at inference time — a signal unavailable in practical deployment. Among oracle-free methods, PASR is the clear leader, with the largest margins over SFT-based baselines (STaR, ISC) that suffer from poor generalization to unseen tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant post-hoc self-refinement paradigm (Self-Refine, Reflexion, PTR, SCoRe, STaR, RISE), where refinement is applied after a full response is generated, by formulating refinement as an in-process, sequential decision problem during generation. It repurposes GRPO (originally from DeepSeekMath) as the training mechanism, replacing the SFT-on-synthetic-data or prompt-engineering approaches used by prior self-refinement methods. The key conceptual shift is from a fixed-iteration, externally-triggered correction loop to an autonomous, context-conditioned policy that decides whether, when, and how to refine, with a comparison-based reward that explicitly penalizes unnecessary or harmful refinements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that PASR reduces token consumption by 41.6% while improving accuracy by 8.2% on Qwen3-8B is significant for deployment cost, and the demonstration that neither prompting nor SFT can elicit proactive self-refinement (both actually degrade performance) clarifies a gap in the field's understanding of when RL is genuinely necessary. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger or reasoning-specialized models (gains were smaller on Qwen2.5-14B than Qwen3-8B), how the method handles tasks requiring genuinely new knowledge beyond the model's parameters, and whether the LLM-as-a-judge reward signal introduces systematic biases that limit generalization to out-of-distribution tasks.
