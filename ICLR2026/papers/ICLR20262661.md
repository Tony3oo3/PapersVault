## Problem

When LoRA-MoE adapters are fine-tuned with GRPO, the scalar task reward is blind to the router's internal decisions, causing expert collapse and severe underutilization of the modular parameter budget. Existing load-balancing strategies rely on auxiliary differentiable losses designed for supervised training, which are not naturally compatible with the critic-free, reward-driven GRPO objective. The paper asks whether routing supervision can be injected directly into the RL reward signal without auxiliary losses or architectural modifications.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts (arithmetic word problems for unimodal tasks) or image-plus-text prompts (geometry diagrams with algebraic angle labels for multimodal tasks) and produces a single text response containing a step-by-step reasoning chain enclosed in <think> tags followed by a final answer in <answer> tags. During generation, every LoRA-MoE module (inserted into gate, up, and down FFN projections of each transformer block) records its per-token routing probability vector; these statistics are aggregated post-hoc into two scalar metrics—mean Shannon entropy and load-balancing MSE—that feed the routing reward. No additional input channels or output modalities are introduced beyond the standard text generation pipeline.

## Method

RO-GRPO augments the standard GRPO task reward with a mechanism-aware routing reward R_route computed from internal routing statistics collected during rollout. Two complementary metrics are extracted: (1) mean per-token routing entropy (lower = more confident expert selection) and (2) mean squared error between each module's average expert utilization and a uniform distribution (lower = better load balance). These are normalized and combined via two strategies: a Smooth curriculum that sigmoidally transitions from penalizing high entropy to penalizing high MSE over training progress, and a Relative gating that grants a sparse positive reward only when both metrics simultaneously improve over their exponential moving averages. The resulting R_total = R_task + R_route is fed into GRPO's group-relative advantage computation, so the policy gradient implicitly shapes routing behavior without any differentiable auxiliary loss or backward-pass gradient through the router. The approach requires no architectural changes, adds only O(TE) post-hoc computation per layer, and is validated across E ∈ {2,4,8} experts on both unimodal (GSM8K, MATH, SVAMP, MGSM) and multimodal (Geometry3k, MathVista, MathVerse, WeMath) benchmarks.

## Results

On unimodal mathematical reasoning (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), RO-GRPO (Smooth, E=2) achieves the best GSM8K score of 91.51% and MGSM of 62.18%, while RO-GRPO (Relative, E=4) reaches 93.33% on SVAMP and 54.58% on MGSM. On multimodal reasoning (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct), RO-GRPO (Relative, E=2) attains the top Geometry3k score of 41.93%, and RO-GRPO (Relative, E=4) achieves 66.26% on WeMath and 60.20% on MathVista. Across all expert counts (E∈{2,4,8}), RO-GRPO consistently reduces load-balancing MSE (e.g., 0.020→0.016 at E=2 unimodal) while maintaining comparable routing entropy, indicating improved expert utilization without sacrificing decision confidence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO (LoRA), the standard single-adapter baseline, RO-GRPO (Smooth, E=2) wins on GSM8K by +1.06 pp (91.51% vs. 90.45%) and on MGSM by +8.51 pp (62.18% vs. 53.67%), while RO-GRPO (Relative, E=4) edges out on SVAMP by +0.33 pp (93.33% vs. 93.00%). Against GRPO (LoRA-MoE), the vanilla multi-expert baseline without routing supervision, RO-GRPO (Smooth, E=2) improves GSM8K by +2.12 pp (91.51% vs. 89.39%) and MGSM by +0.43 pp, while RO-GRPO (Relative, E=4) gains +3.94 pp on SVAMP (93.33% vs. 91.30%) and a striking +8.43 pp on MGSM (54.58% vs. 46.15%). In the multimodal setting, RO-GRPO (Relative, E=2) outperforms GRPO (LoRA) on Geometry3k by +3.49 pp (41.93% vs. 38.44%) and GRPO (LoRA-MoE) by +3.66 pp, and RO-GRPO (Relative, E=4) leads on WeMath by +2.29 pp over GRPO (LoRA) (66.26% vs. 63.97%). The Aux-Loss baseline, which adds a differentiable load-balancing penalty to the GRPO objective, consistently underperforms RO-GRPO on both task accuracy and load-balancing MSE (e.g., 0.036 vs. 0.016 at E=2 on GSM8K), confirming that reward-based routing supervision is more effective than auxiliary losses in the RL setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) by augmenting its scalar task reward with routing statistics from LoRA-MoE adapters, drawing on the MoE load-balancing literature (Fedus et al., 2022; Shazeer et al., 2017; Zoph et al., 2022) that has so far been confined to supervised training via auxiliary losses. The key conceptual departure is reframing routing supervision as a reward signal within the policy-gradient loop rather than as a differentiable auxiliary objective, which the authors argue is more natural for RL where the reward is already the sole optimization signal. It also builds on the LoRA-MoE architecture (Dou et al., 2024) and the broader PEFT-MoE line (MixLoRA, MoCLE) while being the first to systematically study these architectures under GRPO-based reinforcement fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first empirical evidence that a scalar RL reward can align a model's internal routing mechanism, not just its external behavior, opening a path toward 'mechanism alignment' for complex modular architectures. However, the absolute gains are modest (typically 1–3 pp over vanilla LoRA-MoE), the evaluation is limited to mathematical reasoning on 7B/32B Qwen models, and the method is specific to LoRA-MoE with soft routing. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to full MoE backbones, top-k sparse routing (where results are less clear), non-mathematical tasks, and whether the curriculum scheduling is necessary or a simpler fixed-weight formulation would suffice at scale.
