## Problem

Autoregressive language models generate one token per forward pass, creating a fundamental sequential bottleneck that limits inference speed. Existing parallelization approaches—speculative decoding, multi-token prediction, discrete diffusion—either retain irreducible sequential components or assume conditional independence between tokens, capping both achievable speedup and representational power. The paper asks whether arbitrary-length token sequences with full inter-token dependencies can be generated in a single model call without independence assumptions.

## Input / Output

The model receives a context prefix of tokens plus a set of continuous auxiliary variables u_i ∈ [0,1] (one per future token position), embedded via their 32-bit binary float representation mapped through a learned linear layer into the transformer's embedding space. For O-PTP, the output is a one-hot prediction per future token extracted via argmax; for C-PTP, the output is the full conditional distribution P(t_k | t_<i, u_i,…,u_{k−1}) per position, from which tokens are sampled using the same inverse-CDF Pick function as standard autoregressive decoding. The key structural constraint is that C-PTP withholds u_k when predicting t_k, preserving the original autoregressive conditional rather than collapsing it to a point mass.

## Method

The core insight is relocating the source of randomness from post-hoc sampling to model inputs: by feeding the auxiliary variables u_i that determine token selection into the transformer, future tokens become deterministic functions of (context, auxiliaries), enabling joint prediction in one forward pass. Two variants are proposed—One-Hot PTP (O-PTP) predicts a single token per position via argmax, and Categorical PTP (C-PTP) recovers the full conditional distribution by conditioning on all past auxiliaries except the current one (Theorem 2). Training proceeds either by distilling a teacher (reverse-engineering u_k from the teacher's cumulative distribution for each training token) or via inverse autoregressive training where auxiliaries are iteratively solved under the student's own distribution. For deployment, Partial Quadratic Decoding allocates compute across parallel verification branches based on O-PTP confidence scores, enabling parallel drafting and verification in a single model call via gated LoRA adapters.

## Results

On the SpecBench benchmark (diverse tasks: multi-turn conversation, translation, summarization, QA, math, RAG), O-PTP distilled from Vicuna-7B achieves a 2.40× wall-clock speedup and 4.21 accepted tokens per speculative decoding step, outperforming all 9 competing parallelization baselines on the task-average metric. On CodeContests, O-PTP accepts 7.0±0.1 tokens per step versus 6.2±0.1 for independent multi-token prediction, demonstrating the benefit of modeling token dependencies via auxiliary variables. C-PTP trained from scratch on NYC taxi pickup sequences matches an autoregressive baseline (perplexity 19.88 vs. 19.81), confirming the framework works without a teacher.

## Baselines & Comparisons

O-PTP achieves the highest task-average speedup (2.40×) and most accepted tokens (4.21) across all 9 SpecBench baselines, with the closest competitor being SAMD (2.34×, 3.95 accepted) and Eagle-2 (2.29×, 3.93 accepted). O-PTP wins on 5 of 6 individual tasks; SAMD edges it slightly on MTC (2.79 vs. 2.77) and QA (2.05 vs. 1.91), while Eagle-2 leads on QA (2.08 vs. 1.91). The gap widens substantially against older or independence-based methods: Medusa (2.08×, 2.38 accepted) and Lookahead (1.46×, 1.57 accepted) trail by over 1× in speedup. On CodeContests, O-PTP (7.0 accepted) outperforms independent multi-token prediction (6.2 accepted), confirming that auxiliary-variable-based coordination captures token dependencies that independent heads miss. The paper also shows PTP draft models outperform autoregressive draft models of the same size in vanilla speculative decoding, since PTP generates multiple tokens per student call.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transfers two ideas from the normalizing flow literature—Inverse Autoregressive Flows (Kingma et al., 2016), which iteratively identify latent variables to enable parallel generation, and Freeform Flows (Draxler et al., 2024), which distill a single-step generator—to the discrete token prediction setting. The key architectural departure from prior multi-token prediction methods (Medusa, Hydra, MTP) and discrete diffusion models is that PTP feeds the sampling random variables (auxiliary variables u) as model inputs rather than sampling post-hoc, making future tokens deterministic functions of those inputs and thus jointly predictable in one forward pass. For error correction, the paper builds on Quadratic Decoding (Samragh et al., 2025) but proposes Partial Quadratic Decoding, which uses confidence estimates to allocate compute across verification branches, reducing the quadratic cost that limits full quadratic decoding at longer proposal lengths.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical results (Theorems 1 and 2) establish that PTP can model arbitrary dependencies between tokens in a single call without fundamental length restrictions, which is a clean guarantee that independent-prediction and diffusion-based methods lack. Practically, the 2.4× speedup on SpecBench and 4.2 accepted tokens per step demonstrate that the framework scales to 7B-parameter models and diverse tasks. However, the empirical gains over the strongest baselines (SAMD at 2.34×, Eagle-2 at 2.29×) are modest, and the experiments focus on distillation rather than training from scratch at scale, leaving open whether PTP models trained natively would match or exceed autoregressive quality at large scale. The speedup also requires heavily parallelized hardware to materialize, and the self-verification variant (Appendix H) trades distributional fidelity for higher acceptance rates—an important trade-off for deployment.
