## Problem

In RL training for reasoning LLMs, the autoregressive rollout phase dominates training time (~70%), making it the primary efficiency bottleneck. While quantizing the rollout actor could accelerate inference, naively doing so introduces two critical failures: importance sampling instability that causes training collapse, and a fundamental scale mismatch where RL weight updates (order 10⁻⁷) are far smaller than quantization errors (order 10⁻³), effectively freezing the quantized model.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts and a full-precision actor model, which is one-shot quantized (INT8 or FP8) to a lower-bit representation for rollout generation. The quantized actor produces token sequences that are then used to compute policy gradients in the full-precision parameter space. The output is the updated full-precision actor model; the quantized model is re-quantized from the updated weights at each RL step. Weight quantization uses channel-wise scaling while activation quantization uses token-wise scaling, leveraging vLLM's optimized INT8/FP8 GEMM kernels.

## Method

QuRL quantizes the old actor for rollout while maintaining full-precision parameters for gradient updates, transforming on-policy RL into an off-policy setting. It adopts a decoupled PPO objective where the behavior policy is the quantized actor and the proximal policy is the full-precision actor, then introduces Adaptive Clipping Range (ACR) that dynamically widens the upper clipping bound by 1/r_{i,t} (where r is the proximal-to-behavior ratio) to prevent the biased gradient estimation that causes training collapse beyond ~1000 steps. To address the weight-update/quantization-error scale mismatch, Update-Aware Quantization (UAQ) applies invariant scaling with s>1 to linear layers before RL begins, simultaneously reducing quantization error by factor s and amplifying weight updates by factor s, yielding an s² improvement in the update-to-noise ratio. Empirically, s=1.5 provides the best trade-off between capturing training dynamics and maintaining numerical stability.

## Results

On the DeepScaleR benchmark (GRPO, 1.5B model), QuRL with UAQ achieves 55.48% average accuracy across five math reasoning tasks (AIME 2024, AMC, MATH, Minerva, Olympiad), closing the gap to the BF16 RL baseline (56.40%) to just 0.92%. On GSM8K (PPO, 0.5B model), QuRL INT8 reaches 53.55% accuracy versus 55.35% for BF16 RL. On AIME 2024 (DAPO, 7B model), QuRL with UAQ under FP8 achieves 33.27% Avg@32, exceeding the BF16 baseline of 31.67%. Throughput experiments show 20–30% speedup for 7B models and up to 90% speedup for 32B models with INT8 quantization on H100 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is FlashRL (Liu et al., 2025), which uses Truncated Importance Sampling (TIS) with decoupled PPO to handle quantized rollouts. QuRL with UAQ consistently outperforms FlashRL across all three experimental settings: on DeepScaleR (GRPO), QuRL w/ UAQ achieves 55.48% average vs. FlashRL's 53.80% (+1.68%), with the largest gain on AIME 2024 (40.52 vs. 36.77, +3.75%). On GSM8K (PPO), QuRL INT8 reaches 53.55% vs. FlashRL's 51.40% (+2.15%). On AIME 2024 (DAPO, 7B), QuRL w/ UAQ under FP8 achieves 33.27% Avg@32 vs. FlashRL's 32.60% (+0.67%), and even exceeds the BF16 baseline (31.67%). Vanilla INT8/FP8 RL without any correction collapses to near-zero accuracy on AIME 2024, highlighting that both ACR and UAQ are necessary to maintain training stability over extended horizons (>1000 steps).

## Relation to Prior Work

QuRL sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: off-policy RL with decoupled behavior/proximal policies (Hilton et al., 2022; Fu et al., 2025) and post-training quantization via invariant scaling (SmoothQuant, Xiao et al., 2023). The closest concurrent work, FlashRL (Liu et al., 2025), also quantizes rollout but relies on Truncated Importance Sampling to handle the behavior-proximal gap; QuRL departs by showing TIS is insufficient beyond ~500 steps and proposing Adaptive Clipping Range to dynamically adjust trust-region bounds. The Update-Aware Quantization component repurposes SmoothQuant's invariant scaling in a non-standard way—choosing s>1 to amplify weight updates rather than minimize quantization error—occupying a deliberate middle ground between PTQ and QAT that neither paradigm alone can fill.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since rollout consumes ~70% of RL training time for reasoning LLMs, the 20–80% throughput gains from INT8/FP8 quantized decoding are practically significant for anyone scaling RLVR pipelines. The identification of the weight-update-quantization mismatch (updates of order 10⁻⁷ vs. quantization noise of order 10⁻³) is a useful diagnostic that explains why naive quantization silently freezes the rollout model. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 4-bit or mixed-precision settings, whether ACR generalizes to non-PPO objectives (e.g., REINFORCE-style methods), and whether the modest 1–3% accuracy gap over FlashRL narrows or widens at larger model scales or longer training horizons.
