## Problem

Existing privacy extraction attacks on RAG systems can trigger data leakage but fail to identify which specific sentences in a mixed response originate from the private knowledge base versus the LLM's pre-trained content, and they degrade sharply in multi-domain settings where the attacker lacks prior knowledge of the corpus. This paper addresses both gaps by exploiting the inherent knowledge asymmetry between RAG systems (which retrieve from an external database) and standard LLMs (which rely solely on pre-trained parameters) to enable fine-grained, sentence-level privacy localization across heterogeneous domains.

## Input / Output

The method operates in a fully black-box setting, requiring only API access to a RAG system and a standard LLM. Adversarial queries are decomposed into two concatenated parts (q1 for domain-triggering keywords, q2 for retrieval-contextual prompting), and both systems receive the same query to produce text responses. Responses are segmented into sentences, each receiving a continuous similarity feature score (combining cosine similarity and NLI-adjusted corrections) and a final binary label indicating whether the sentence is derived from the knowledge base. The classifier is trained on manually annotated sentence-level labels from 30 adversarial queries per dataset, split 7:3 into training and test sets.

## Method

The framework has three phases. First, adversarial query generation uses an open-ended template (q1) incorporating domain keywords plus a retrieval-activation prompt (q2: 'provide contextual information based on the retrieved content') to maximize response divergence between RAG and standard LLM outputs; in multi-domain settings, an iterative refinement loop feeds extracted privacy fragments back into q1 to progressively target unknown domains without prior corpus knowledge. Second, similarity feature scores are computed by embedding each RAG sentence with all-MiniLM-L6-v2, taking the maximum cosine similarity against all standard LLM response sentences, then adjusting via a Deberta-v3-large-mnli NLI model that subtracts contradiction logits, leaves neutral scores unchanged, and adds entailment logits to correct for surface-level lexical overlap that masks semantic opposition. Third, a small DNN with ReLU activation is trained in a supervised fashion on the annotated (score, label) pairs to classify each sentence as private (y=1) or non-private (y=0), achieving over 90% ESR in single-domain and ~80% in multi-domain settings.

## Results

On single-domain datasets, the method achieves ESR above 90% (93.55% on HealthCareMagic, 95.65% on Enron Email with LLaMA3.1-8B) and F1-scores above 92%. On the multi-domain NQ-train_pairs dataset, ESR drops to 80.00% (LLaMA3.1-8B) but remains above 76% across all tested LLMs. AUC values exceed 84% in all settings, confirming consistent discriminative ability. The method outperforms all baselines by over 30% in F1-score across every dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior RAG privacy extraction attacks: RAG-Privacy (Zeng et al., 2024) and RAG-Thief (Jiang et al., 2024), extended to sentence-level evaluation via regex-based extraction. On HealthCareMagic, the proposed method achieves 93.55% ESR versus 57.58% (RAG-Privacy) and 61.37% (RAG-Thief), a gap of 32–36 percentage points. On Enron Email, the advantage widens to 34–43 points (95.65% vs. 61.25% and 52.75%). The gap is most dramatic on the multi-domain NQ dataset, where RAG-Privacy drops to 37.25% ESR and RAG-Thief to 41.73%, while the proposed method maintains 80.00% thanks to its iterative query refinement strategy. In F1-score, the method exceeds both baselines by 29–60 percentage points across all three datasets, indicating substantially better precision in distinguishing private from non-private sentences. The two additional GPT-4o-based baselines (content-based and LLM-based) also fall well short, with the LLM-based method being the strongest competitor at 65.22% ESR on HCM but still trailing by 28 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior RAG privacy extraction attacks (RAG-Privacy, RAG-Thief, Qi et al.) in two key dimensions: it shifts from coarse-grained leakage detection to fine-grained sentence-level localization, and extends from single-domain to multi-domain settings. Conceptually, it repurposes the implicit observation that RAG and standard LLM responses differ when external knowledge is involved, formalizing this as a quantitative divergence signal (δ_Q) and building a three-phase classification pipeline (adversarial query generation → NLI-refined similarity scoring → DNN classification) around it. Unlike membership inference attacks that require exact document copies, or poisoning attacks that require knowledge base access, the framework operates in a fully black-box setting using only response comparison, while the iterative query refinement mechanism for multi-domain scenarios has no direct analogue in prior RAG security literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete, previously unaddressed gap in RAG security: even when privacy leakage occurs, defenders cannot pinpoint which specific sentences originate from the knowledge base, making targeted mitigation impossible. The finding that the attack remains effective against existing defenses (intention detection, re-ranking, summarization, distance thresholds) and even benefits from differential privacy noise in multi-domain settings underscores that current RAG deployments lack adequate sentence-level privacy controls. Key open questions include the scalability of the manual annotation requirement for the classifier, the interaction with stronger DP guarantees (ε→0), and whether the approach generalizes when knowledge base content heavily overlaps with LLM pre-training data. The reliance on relatively weak baselines (regex, GPT-4o prompt-based) limits the strength of the performance claims.
