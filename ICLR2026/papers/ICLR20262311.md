## Problem

Multi-modal LLMs underperform contrastive CLIP models on fine-grained visual recognition (FGVR), and adapting them typically requires large annotated datasets while overfitting to seen categories and generalizing poorly to unseen ones. Existing policy optimization methods for MLLMs do not address the core FGVR challenge of high intra-class variance combined with low inter-class variance, leaving a substantial gap in both data efficiency and base-to-new category generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262311/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a text query (e.g., 'What is the name of the bird in the photo?') and produces a structured chain-of-thought trace followed by a fine-grained sub-category label. In closed-world evaluation, the query embeds a predefined candidate class list as a multiple-choice prompt; in open-world evaluation, the model predicts freely over its full vocabulary. The CoT output is segmented into four tagged stages—<analysis>, <options>, <comparison>, <prediction>—and the final answer is wrapped in <answer> tags, with success measured by text inclusion or relative semantic similarity against the ground-truth sub-category.

## Method

Fine-R1 uses a two-stage training framework on Qwen2.5-VL base models. Stage 1 (CoT SFT) fine-tunes the model on a curated 404-sample dataset of structured reasoning chains generated by Qwen2.5-VL-32B, where each chain follows a 'visual analysis → candidate sub-categories → comparison → prediction' template that forces the model to propose confusable candidates and then resolve them via textual knowledge. Stage 2 (TAPO) builds on DAPO and introduces triplet augmentation: for each anchor image, a positive image from the same sub-category and a negative image from the most visually similar different sub-category are sampled. Intra-class augmentation pools rollouts from both anchor and positive images into a single reward-computed set while updating the policy only on the anchor, increasing intra-class diversity. Inter-class augmentation adds a KL-divergence loss term that maximizes the distributional gap between the policy conditioned on the anchor/positive image and that conditioned on the negative image, with double entropy regularization to keep both distributions stable. The full objective combines the standard clipped policy gradient with the KL-divergence term and entropy penalties, trained with only 4-shot data per seen category.

## Results

On closed-world FGVR (4-shot per category, 6 datasets), Fine-R1-7B achieves 91.71% accuracy on seen categories and 85.70% on unseen categories (88.71% average), surpassing the best CLIP model SigLIP-L (84.44% avg) and the best general MLLM Qwen2.5-VL-7B (80.20% avg). On open-world FGVR, Fine-R1-7B reaches 74.80% relative semantic similarity on average, a +23.75% improvement over Qwen2.5-VL-7B (51.05%) and +30.98% over the reasoning MLLM DeepPerception-7B (43.82%). The 3B variant also outperforms SigLIP-L in closed-world (85.19% vs. 84.44%) while using fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fine-R1-7B outperforms all 12 baselines on closed-world FGVR. Against CLIP models, it surpasses the strongest (SigLIP-L, 84.44% avg) by +4.27% overall and +5.16% on unseen categories, while also beating CLIP-L (+16.17%) and EVA-G (+15.22%). Among general MLLMs, it exceeds Qwen2.5-VL-7B (80.20%) by +8.51% and Qwen2-VL-7B (77.88%) by +10.83%, with the largest gap on Bird (+20.0% over Qwen2.5-VL-7B). Against the reasoning MLLM DeepPerception-7B (83.12%), Fine-R1-7B gains +5.59% overall and +6.46% on unseen categories. In open-world evaluation, Fine-R1-7B (74.80% relative semantic similarity) leads Qwen2.5-VL-7B by +23.75% and DeepPerception-7B by +30.98%, confirming that the CoT+TAPO framework provides substantially stronger generalization to unseen sub-categories than both general and reasoning MLLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the R1-style two-stage training paradigm (CoT SFT followed by policy-optimization RL) from LLMs to multimodal fine-grained visual recognition, building directly on DAPO as the RL backbone and adapting PAPO's KL-divergence and double-entropy regularization terms. Its key departure from prior FGVR work is the introduction of Triplet Augmented Policy Optimization (TAPO), which injects intra-class and inter-class contrastive signals into the policy gradient objective—addressing the high-intra-class/low-inter-class variance problem that generic RL-for-MLLM methods do not target. The structured CoT format (visual analysis → candidate subcategories → comparison → prediction) also departs from prior CoT-SFT approaches that use generic 'think step by step' rationales, instead eliciting explicit candidate generation and pairwise comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that MLLMs can be adapted to FGVR with only 4-shot data per category while matching or exceeding contrastive CLIP models, which is practically significant for knowledge-intensive domains (ornithology, automotive, botany) where exhaustive annotation is infeasible. The H3 analysis—that visual features and stored subcategory knowledge remain largely unchanged while the model's ability to *deploy* that knowledge improves—reframes the FGVR bottleneck as a reasoning-pathway problem rather than a perception or knowledge-acquisition problem, suggesting future work should focus on reasoning architecture rather than feature engineering. Open questions include whether the approach scales to thousands of subcategories, whether it transfers to non-visual fine-grained tasks, and whether the RL stage's computational cost (multiple rollouts per prompt with triplet sampling) is prohibitive at larger scale.
