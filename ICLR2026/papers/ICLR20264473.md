## Problem

Learning high-fidelity generative models from corrupted observations (noisy, blurred, masked, or undersampled) without access to clean data remains a long-standing challenge in scientific and medical imaging. Existing corruption-aware diffusion methods inherit multi-step sampling costs and, more fundamentally, their mode-covering ELBO objective forces the learned distribution to spread probability mass across all regions suggested by noisy data, yielding overly diffuse samples. This paper reveals that score distillation—traditionally viewed only as an acceleration tool—can actually improve generation quality beyond the teacher when applied in corrupted-data regimes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset of N corrupted measurements y = A(x) + σε, where A is a known non-invertible operator (identity, Gaussian blur, random binary mask, 2× downsampling, or Fourier-domain undersampling) and σ is a known noise level; no clean images are ever accessed. The corruption operator may vary per sample (e.g., different random inpainting masks). The output is a one-step generator G_θ: R^d → R^d that maps a latent noise vector z ~ N(0, I_d) directly to a clean sample approximating the unknown clean distribution p_X, evaluated on RGB images at 32×32 or 64×64 resolution and complex-valued multi-coil MRI signals.

## Method

RSD is a two-phase framework. Phase I pretrains a corruption-aware diffusion teacher f_φ on the corrupted data using an objective matched to the forward operator (standard diffusion for noiseless corruption, a Tweedie-style loss for additive noise, a masked inpainting loss, or a masked Fourier-space loss). Phase II distills the teacher into a one-step generator G_θ via the SiD (Score Identity Distillation) Fisher-divergence loss, with a critical corruption-respecting modification: generated samples are passed through the same corruption pipeline A(·)+σε before training the auxiliary fake diffusion model f_ψ, ensuring the distillation process mirrors the observation model. This mode-seeking distillation objective concentrates the generator's probability mass on high-density regions supported by the teacher's score field while discarding the diffuse, low-density regions that the mode-covering teacher includes. The framework is modular—any corruption-aware pretraining objective can be plugged into Phase I, and the same objective must be used consistently for both teacher and fake diffusion during Phase II. In a linear Gaussian setting, the paper proves the distilled generator achieves a strictly smaller Wasserstein-2 distance to the clean distribution than the noisy teacher, with the gap quantified as (d−r)σ².

## Results

On CIFAR-10 denoising (σ=0.2), RSD (one-step, zero-shot) achieves FID 4.77, surpassing the best zero-shot teacher (Teacher-Truncated, FID 12.21) by 2.6× and the best few-shot baseline SFBD (FID 13.53, using 50 clean images) by 2.8×, while approaching the clean-data DDPM upper bound (FID 4.04). On CelebA-HQ (σ=0.2), RSD reaches FID 6.48 vs. Teacher-Truncated's 13.90 and SFBD's 6.49. Across FFHQ and AFHQ-v2 (Table 3), RSD consistently outperforms teachers (e.g., FFHQ: 6.29 vs. 14.67; AFHQ-v2: 5.42 vs. 9.82). On multi-coil MRI (Table 6), RSD achieves FID 10.71 at R=4 vs. Teacher Diffusion's 32.31 and L1-EDM's 27.64, with up to 30× inference speedup from one-step generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RSD (one-step, zero-shot) decisively outperforms all zero-shot corruption-aware diffusion teachers: on CIFAR-10 (σ=0.2) it achieves FID 4.77 vs. Teacher-Truncated's 12.21 (2.6× improvement) and Teacher-Full's 60.73 (12.7× improvement); on CelebA-HQ it reaches 6.48 vs. 13.90 and 61.14 respectively. Against few-shot baselines that require 50 clean images for initialization, RSD (using zero clean images) beats SFBD (13.53 on CIFAR-10, 6.49 on CelebA-HQ) and EM-Diffusion (86.47 on CIFAR-10), and substantially outperforms Ambient Diffusion (114.13) and SURE-Score (132.61). RSD approaches but does not match clean-data upper bounds: on CIFAR-10 it is within 0.73 FID of DDPM (4.04) and 2.81 of EDM (1.97); on CelebA-HQ it is within 3.22 of DDPM (3.26). On general corruptions (Table 4), RSD beats both Teacher Diffusion and EM-Diffusion across Gaussian deblurring, random inpainting, and super-resolution in both noiseless and noisy regimes. On multi-coil MRI (Table 6), RSD outperforms both the Fourier-space Teacher Diffusion and the L1-EDM baseline at all acceleration factors R∈{2,4,6,8}, with the largest gap at R=4 (FID 10.71 vs. 32.31 and 27.64). On conditional inverse problems (Table 9), RSD's learned prior achieves PSNR 24.11 and LPIPS 0.025, surpassing EM-Diffusion (23.16, 0.022) and all other zero-shot/few-shot baselines while approaching full-shot DPS (25.91, 0.010).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: corruption-aware diffusion models (Ambient Diffusion, Tweedie Diffusion, Fourier-space variants) that learn from degraded observations, and score distillation (SiD, DMD, SDS) that compresses multi-step diffusion into one-step generators. The key conceptual departure is reframing distillation from a pure acceleration tool into a quality-improvement mechanism: by corrupting generator outputs with the same operator before training the fake score model, the distillation phase induces mode-seeking behavior that sharpens the learned distribution beyond what the teacher achieves. The two-phase RSD pipeline is modular—Phase I can use any corruption-aware objective from Table 1, and Phase II applies SiD with a corruption-respecting procedure—making it a drop-in enhancement over existing corruption-aware diffusion methods rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial for scientific and medical imaging (MRI, astronomy, seismology) where clean ground truth is unavailable or prohibitively expensive: RSD delivers 30× inference speedup while simultaneously improving FID over the teacher, all without any clean images. The conceptual insight—that forward-KL (mode-covering) diffusion training under noise forces diffuse probability mass, while reverse distillation (mode-seeking) corrects this—opens a path to understanding when and why distillation helps beyond acceleration. Open questions include extending the theoretical analysis (currently limited to linear Gaussian settings) to general nonlinear corruptions, handling truly unknown corruption operators, and exploring whether the quality gain persists at scale (e.g., ImageNet-resolution or video). The Proximal FID metric for model selection under corruption is a useful but underexplored tool that could benefit the broader corrupted-data generation community.
