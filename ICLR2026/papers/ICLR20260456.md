## Problem

Existing regret bounds for stochastic linear bandits with heteroscedastic noise scale with √Λ where Λ = Σσ_t² is the cumulative variance, but this dependence is suboptimal: even if the noise is near zero for a large fraction of rounds, Λ remains unchanged. This paper asks whether the √Λ barrier can be broken when the action set is fixed, and shows that the correct statistical complexity is governed by the harmonic mean of the variances rather than their sum.

## Input / Output

The algorithms receive a fixed action set A ⊂ R^d (finite or infinite, with ‖a‖₂ ≤ 1), observe the noise variance σ_t at each round t (bounded by known constants σ_min and σ_max), and output a recommended action â_T after T rounds. The resulting simple regret bound has the form Õ(d[Σ(1/σ_t²) − Σ_{i=1}^{Õ(d)} 1/[σ^(i)]²]^{−1/2}), where σ^(i) denotes the i-th smallest variance; the subtraction of the Õ(d) smallest variances reflects the irreducible cost of estimating a d-dimensional parameter vector. For finite action sets, the dimension dependence improves from d to √(d log|A|).

## Method

The paper proposes two variance-adaptive algorithms. VAEE (for large/infinite action sets) maintains a candidate set of non-eliminated arms and at each round pulls the arm maximizing the Mahalanobis exploration bonus ‖a‖_{V_{t−1}^{−1}}, using variance-weighted covariance updates V_t = V_{t−1} + σ_t^{−2}a_t a_t^⊤ and eliminating arms whose worst-case reward falls below the current best. VAGD (for finite action sets) first computes a nearly G-optimal design π with core set size at most 4d log log d + 16, then adaptively allocates pulls to the arm minimizing Σ_{τ∈T(a)} 1/(σ_τ²·π(a)), ensuring balanced information collection weighted by observed precision. The key theoretical insight is that the elliptical potential lemma applied to the variance-weighted updates yields a bound on the exploration bonus that depends on the harmonic mean of the variances, and a matching lower bound via Le Cam's two-point method and Yao's minimax principle confirms this rate is information-theoretically tight up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

The paper establishes a simple regret bound of $\tilde{O}\left(d\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{1}{\sigma_t^2}-\sum_{i=1}^{\tilde{O}(d)}\frac{1}{[\sigma^{(i)}]^2}\right]^{-1/2}\right)$ for VAEE (infinite action set) and $\tilde{O}\left(\sqrt{d\log|\mathcal{A}|}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{1}{\sigma_t^2}-\sum_{i=1}^{\tilde{O}(d)}\frac{1}{[\sigma^{(i)}]^2}\right]^{-1/2}\right)$ for VAGD (finite action set), both depending on the harmonic mean of variances rather than the cumulative variance $\Lambda$. A nearly matching lower bound of $\Omega\left(d\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{1}{\sigma_t^2}\right]^{-1/2}\right)$ confirms optimality. Under concrete variance profiles (Table 2), VAEE improves over Weighted OFUL by factors ranging from $T^{1/2}$ (fast-decaying noise: $\tilde{O}(d/T^{3/2})$ vs $\tilde{O}(d/T)$) to $T^{1/4}$ (front-loaded super-precision: $\tilde{O}(d/T^{6/5})$ vs $\tilde{O}(d/\sqrt{T})$).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All prior methods (Weighted OFUL, Weighted OFUL+, VOFUL, VOFUL2, SAVE, LinNATS, VarCB) achieve simple regret bounds that scale with $\sqrt{\Lambda}$, where $\Lambda=\sum_t\sigma_t^2$ is the cumulative variance. By the HM-AM inequality, the harmonic-mean rate $\left(\sum_t 1/\sigma_t^2\right)^{-1/2}$ is always at most $\sqrt{\Lambda}/T$, so VAEE's bound is strictly sharper than every $\sqrt{\Lambda}$-type bound for any variance sequence. Concretely (Table 2), under fast-decaying noise ($\sigma_t^2=1/t^2$), VAEE achieves $\tilde{O}(d/T^{3/2})$ versus Weighted OFUL's $\tilde{O}(d/T)$, a $T^{1/2}$ improvement; under front-loaded super-precision noise, the gap widens to $T^{1/4}$. VOFUL and VOFUL2 additionally suffer worse dimension dependence ($d^{9/2}$ and $d^{3/2}$ respectively) compared to VAEE's $d$. For finite action sets, VAGD further improves the dimension factor from $d$ to $\sqrt{d\log|\mathcal{A}|}$, outperforming VarCB's $\sqrt{|\mathcal{A}|\Lambda d}$ dependence. The established lower bound $\Omega(d(\sum_t 1/\sigma_t^2)^{-1/2})$ matches VAEE's upper bound up to logarithmic factors, confirming that the harmonic-mean rate is information-theoretically unavoidable in the fixed-action-set setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the variance-aware linear bandit line (Zhou et al. 2021, Zhou & Gu 2022, Zhao et al. 2023a, Jia et al. 2024), which all characterize complexity via cumulative variance Λ = Σσ_t², by showing that for fixed action sets the correct measure is instead the harmonic mean of variances. The exploration mechanism builds on OFUL (Abbasi-Yadkori et al. 2011) with variance-weighted updates, while the finite-action variant (VAGD) repurposes G-optimal design theory (Lattimore & Szepesvári 2020; Lattimore et al. 2020) with an adaptive allocation rule. The lower bound uses Le Cam's two-point method and Yao's minimax principle, and the heavy-tailed extension adapts the pseudo-Huber loss from Li & Sun (2024). The key conceptual departure from prior work is that He & Gu (2025) proved √Λ is unavoidable for time-varying action sets, but this paper shows that restriction to a fixed action set fundamentally changes the information-theoretic landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result to break the √Λ barrier for linear heteroscedastic bandits, providing a clean and tight (matching lower bound) characterization of statistical complexity in terms of the harmonic mean of variances. The structural case study showing Weighted OFUL fails by a T^Θ(√T) factor in specific variance profiles (not merely due to loose analysis) clarifies why prior algorithms are suboptimal. Open questions include extending to unknown variances, non-fixed contexts sampled from an unknown distribution, and MDP settings where variance is governed by transition dynamics. The result is primarily theoretical with no empirical validation, limiting immediate practical impact but providing a sharp benchmark for future algorithm design.
