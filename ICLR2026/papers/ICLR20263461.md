## Problem

High-quality training data for information-seeking (IS) agents is scarce and difficult to construct. Existing synthesis methods follow an information-driven paradigm—first retrieving web content, then prompting LLMs to generate questions—which produces inconsistencies between information structure and reasoning structure, as well as between questions and their answers. No established formalization exists for IS tasks, making it impossible to systematically control reasoning complexity or verify structural correctness during synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263461/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The synthesis pipeline takes seed questions grounded in Wikipedia articles (18k after filtering) and produces expanded formal questions represented as lists of KP triplets [variable, relation, constant/variable], along with 5,000 ReAct-format trajectories (Thought-Action-Observation triples with Search, Visit, and Answer actions) for downstream training. The trained agent receives a natural language IS question and outputs an answer entity set, with trajectories structured using explicit XML markers (think, action, observation, answer) to make agent behavior machine-parsable for supervised learning.

## Method

WebShaper formalizes IS tasks using set-theoretic Knowledge Projections (KP), where R(V) = {u | ∃v∈V, (u,v)∈R or (v,u)∈R}, composed via R-Union and Intersection operations into recursive target sets. A KP Representation encodes each task as a list of triplets [X, r, S] with constants and variables, flattening recursion into intersection chains. An agentic Expander (ReAct-based, equipped with Search, Summarize, and Validate tools) performs Layer-wise Expansion: it traverses the KP graph layer by layer, replacing leaf constants with newly retrieved and validated sub-questions, thereby avoiding both redundancy (constants linked to constants) and reasoning shortcuts (constants directly connected to the target). The resulting dataset is used to train IS agents via SFT (with observation masking) followed by GRPO reinforcement learning, achieving state-of-the-art open-source performance on GAIA (60.1 avg with Qwen-2.5-72B) and WebWalkerQA (52.2 avg).

## Results

WebShaper achieves state-of-the-art performance among open-sourced information-seeking agents on both GAIA and WebWalkerQA benchmarks. On GAIA, WebShaper with Qwen-2.5-72B backbone reaches 60.1 average (69.2/63.4/16.6 across Levels 1/2/3), outperforming the next best open-source method WebSailor (55.4) by 4.7 points. On WebWalkerQA, WebShaper (Qwen-2.5-72B) achieves 52.2 average, the highest among all open-source methods. The gap to the closed-source OpenAI Deep Research system (67.4 on GAIA) narrows to 7.3 points, making WebShaper the only open-source method exceeding 60 on GAIA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WebShaper consistently outperforms all open-source agentic baselines across both benchmarks and all backbone sizes. On GAIA, WebShaper (Qwen-2.5-72B) achieves 60.1 average, beating WebSailor (55.4) by 4.7 points, WebDancer on QwQ-32B (51.5) by 8.6 points, and WebThinker-RL (48.5) by 11.6 points. On WebWalkerQA, WebShaper (Qwen-2.5-72B) reaches 52.2, surpassing WebDancer (47.9), WebThinker-RL (46.5), and Search-o1 (34.1). The only method that outperforms WebShaper is the closed-source OpenAI Deep Research system (67.4 on GAIA), which WebShaper narrows to within 7.3 points. In the SFT data comparison (Table 2), WebShaper training data also outperforms WebWalkerQA, E2HQA, and MHQA datasets by 3.8–7.7 points on GAIA average across all three backbones, confirming the quality advantage of the formalization-driven synthesis over information-driven alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant information-driven paradigm for IS data synthesis (WebDancer, TaskCraft, WebWalkerQA, WebSailor), which first retrieves and organizes web content and then prompts an LLM to generate questions from that content. Instead, WebShaper inverts this pipeline: it first formalizes IS tasks using set-theoretic Knowledge Projections (KPs) with R-Union and Intersection operations, then uses an agentic Expander to collect information guided by the formal specification. The training pipeline (SFT + GRPO-based RL) and the ReAct agent architecture are inherited from WebDancer and Yao et al. (2023), while the formalization itself draws conceptual inspiration from Lean 4 in theorem proving and propositional logic in KBQA, but applies set-theoretic constructs to a domain where no prior formalization existed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formalization-driven paradigm addresses a genuine bottleneck in IS agent development: the scarcity of high-quality, structurally consistent training data that is difficult to obtain via crowdsourcing. The Knowledge Projection framework offers principled controllability over reasoning depth and complexity, which could generalize to other agent training domains beyond web-based IS. However, the synthesis cost (~20 LLM completions, 6 search + 6 visit calls, ~7 min per example) is substantially higher than information-driven methods, and the empirical gains over strong baselines like WebDancer and WebThinker are modest (a few points on GAIA). Open questions include whether the set-theoretic formalization extends to multi-modal or real-time IS tasks, whether the layer-wise expansion strategy is optimal versus other graph traversal orders, and whether the approach remains cost-effective at the scale needed for frontier agent training.
