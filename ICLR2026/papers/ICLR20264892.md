## Problem

REPA, the dominant technique for improving diffusion transformer training, relies on external pre-trained visual encoders (e.g., DINOv2, CLIP) to provide alignment targets for intermediate features. This introduces two critical issues: distribution mismatch when the encoder's pre-training data differs from the generation target, and substantial additional computational cost for pre-training or fine-tuning the encoder. The paper asks whether the semantic guidance that REPA provides to early transformer layers can be obtained entirely from the model's own internal representations, without any external supervision.

## Input / Output

The model is conditioned on a class label and generates 256×256 RGB images. During training, input images are encoded by the Stable Diffusion VAE into 32×32×4 latents, and each image is independently noised K=2 times with distinct Gaussian noise samples and timestamps, producing two noisy latents per image per training step. The dual-path structure means a single forward pass through the decoupled transformer processes both noisy views, yielding two condition feature maps that are then aligned.

## Method

DUPA builds on the Decoupled Diffusion Transformer (DDT) architecture, which separates a condition encoder (extracting low-frequency semantic features z_t from noisy latents) from a velocity decoder (predicting the denoising velocity). The key mechanism is dual-path condition alignment: for each image, K=2 independent noise samples (different ε and t) create two noisy views, each processed through DDT to produce a condition feature. A cosine-similarity alignment loss (analogous to REPA's loss but between self-generated conditions rather than external encoder features) pulls these two conditions toward a shared cluster center, providing unsupervised semantic guidance to the early encoder layers. The total training objective combines the standard velocity-prediction loss averaged over both paths with the alignment loss weighted by λ=0.5. This eliminates the need for any external visual encoder while achieving FID=1.46 on ImageNet 256×256 in 400 epochs, with 5× faster training and 10× faster inference compared to the DDT baseline.

## Results

DUPA-XL/2 achieves FID=1.46, sFID=4.45, IS=296.2, Precision=0.84, and Recall=0.62 on ImageNet 256×256 after only 400 training epochs (2M iterations) with CFG scale w=1.60. This outperforms all evaluated methods that do not rely on external visual encoder supervision, and narrows the gap to REPA (FID=1.42, trained for 800 epochs) to within 3%. At the 400K-iteration scale, DUPA-XL/2 reaches FID=8.71 versus 12.8 for DDT-XL/2 and 17.2 for SiT-XL/2, while also delivering 5× faster training and 10× faster inference compared to DDT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DUPA consistently outperforms all baselines across every model size (B/2, L/2, XL/2) on FID, sFID, IS, and Precision, with the largest gains at smaller scales (e.g., FID 25.2 vs. 33.0 for SiT-B/2, a 24% relative improvement). Against DDT (the architectural base), DUPA improves FID by 3.7 points at XL/2 (8.71 vs. 12.8) and by 3.8 points at L/2 (11.1 vs. 14.9) at 400K iterations. At the full 2M-iteration scale, DUPA-XL/2 (FID=1.46) surpasses all unsupervised and contrastive-learning baselines (MaskDiT, ΔFM, Disp-Loss, MDTv2, etc.) and all masked-image-modeling/autoregressive methods (MaskGIT, LlamaGen, VAR, MAR, MagViT-v2), while remaining within 0.04 FID of REPA (1.42), which requires 800 epochs and an external DINOv2/CLIP encoder. The paper also shows that simply doubling DDT's batch size or training steps cannot match DUPA's performance, confirming that the dual-path alignment mechanism—not just more compute—is the source of the gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

DUPA builds directly on the Decoupled Diffusion Transformer (DDT) architecture, adopting its condition encoder–velocity decoder split, and is conceptually derived from REPA's representation alignment loss, which aligns intermediate DiT features with those of a frozen external visual encoder (e.g., DINOv2, CLIP). The key departure is replacing the supervised alignment target with an unsupervised one: instead of matching noisy-image features to a pre-trained encoder's output, DUPA independently noises the same image multiple times and aligns the condition features extracted from each noisy view, treating them as positive pairs in a self-supervised clustering objective. This positions DUPA between REPA-style supervised alignment and contrastive-learning approaches (ΔFM, Disp-Loss) that separate negative pairs, as it explicitly pulls together representations of the same underlying image across different noise realizations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DUPA demonstrates that the primary benefit of REPA—providing stable low-frequency semantic guidance to early transformer layers—can be achieved without any external encoder, eliminating both the distribution-mismatch risk and the substantial pre-training cost of large visual models. The practical impact is significant: 5× faster training and 10× faster inference over the DDT baseline, with FID within 3% of REPA at half the training epochs. Open questions include whether the approach extends to text-to-image generation (acknowledged as future work), whether it generalizes to domains where the 'same image, different noise' assumption is less clean, and whether the DDT encoder-decoder architecture is truly required or whether the alignment loss transfers to vanilla DiT/SiT without architectural changes.
