## Problem

Dense 4D reconstruction—jointly recovering 3D geometry, 3D motion, and camera pose from casually captured images—remains ill-posed and is typically addressed by slow per-scene optimization or fragmented feedforward models that handle individual tasks in isolation. No unified feedforward architecture exists that holistically estimates all 2D and 3D perception signals from a single representation, and the scarcity of dense, large-scale 4D ground-truth annotations further limits data-driven approaches.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of unposed RGB images (any aspect ratio, trained at 512px width) and their camera intrinsics as input, and in a single forward pass outputs a set of per-pixel dynamic 3D Gaussians (center, velocity, quaternion rotation, scale, spherical-harmonic color, opacity) defined in the first camera's canonical coordinate frame, plus a relative camera pose (translation and quaternion) estimated directly by a feedforward MLP head rather than a PnP solver. From this explicit 4D representation, depth maps, 3D scene flow, 2D optical flow, and novel-view/time-interpolated renderings are all obtained by differentiable rasterization without additional network heads.

## Method

UFO-4D uses a weight-sharing ViT encoder and a cross-attention decoder (inspired by DUSt3R and NoPoSplat) to process both images jointly, with DPT-based heads predicting per-pixel Gaussian attributes and a 3-layer MLP pose head predicting relative camera pose. The key technical contribution is a unified differentiable 4D rasterizer that extends standard 3DGS alpha-blending to render not only color images but also dense point maps and scene flow maps at any interpolated time t' ∈ [t, t+1] by translating each Gaussian along its velocity vector. This shared-primitive design means that supervising rendered images (MSE + LPIPS photometric loss) backpropagates gradients into the same Gaussian centers and velocities that produce geometry and motion, creating a tight regularizing coupling between all tasks. Training is semi-supervised: sparse ground-truth losses on motion, points, and pose are combined with self-supervised photometric and edge-aware smoothness losses on rasterized outputs, which compensates for noisy or missing annotations in real-world datasets like Stereo4D.

## Results

On geometry estimation, UFO-4D achieves the best pointmap EPE on Stereo4D (0.659), KITTI (2.568), and Bonn (0.162), while remaining competitive on Sintel (3.866 vs. MonST3R's 3.607). On scene flow, UFO-4D achieves more than 3× lower EPE than the best competitor on both Stereo4D (0.049 vs. 0.175 for DynaDUSt3R) and KITTI (0.137 vs. 0.463). For pose estimation, UFO-4D's fully feedforward pose head achieves ATE of 0.0101 on Stereo4D, roughly 4.5× better than MonST3R (0.0458) and 6× better than St4RTrack (0.0602), without requiring PnP+RANSAC post-processing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UFO-4D outperforms all five baselines on the primary Stereo4D benchmark across geometry (EPE 0.659 vs. 0.811 for DynaDUSt3R, 1.504 for MonST3R) and motion (EPE3D 0.049 vs. 0.175 for DynaDUSt3R, a 3.6× improvement). On KITTI, UFO-4D achieves the best geometry EPE (2.568) and dramatically leads on scene flow (0.137 vs. 0.463 for DynaDUSt3R), with the gain attributed to both more accurate feedforward pose estimation and dense photometric supervision. On Bonn, UFO-4D achieves the best EPE (0.162) but the authors note this is partly due to textureless regions where large overlapping Gaussians introduce blending errors, making per-pixel methods like MonST3R (0.188) and St4RTrack (0.181) competitive. On Sintel, UFO-4D is slightly behind MonST3R (3.866 vs. 3.607 EPE) and ZeroMSF (3.637), which the authors attribute to those methods' training on the Spring dataset reducing the domain gap. For pose estimation, UFO-4D's direct feedforward pose head beats MonST3R and St4RTrack (which rely on PnP+RANSAC) by 4–6× on ATE across all three pose benchmarks, and even when PnP+RANSAC is applied to UFO-4D's own geometry, the feedforward head still yields ~16.6% higher accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

UFO-4D extends the DUSt3R/NoPoSplat encoder-decoder architecture (ViT encoder with cross-attention decoder and DPT heads) by replacing per-pixel pointmap outputs with a unified Dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting representation, building directly on the Gaussian primitive formulation from Luiten et al. (2024). The key conceptual departure from prior feedforward 4D methods (DynaDUSt3R, MonST3R, ZeroMSF) is that geometry and motion are not estimated as independent per-pixel regressions but emerge from the same set of 3D Gaussian primitives, enabling differentiable rasterization of image, point, and flow maps simultaneously. This tight coupling means that photometric supervision on rendered images regularizes geometry and motion estimates, and vice versa—a training synergy absent in fragmented per-pixel approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3× reduction in scene flow EPE and the unified feedforward formulation (no test-time optimization, no PnP post-processing) make dense 4D reconstruction from casual image pairs practically viable for robotics and autonomous driving. The self-supervised photometric loss addresses the critical bottleneck of sparse, noisy 4D annotations in real-world datasets like Stereo4D. However, the linear-motion and constant-brightness assumptions limit applicability to short time intervals, and the per-pixel Gaussian count makes long-sequence extension memory-prohibitive without a compact scene representation. Open questions include whether non-linear motion models and time-varying Gaussian attributes can close the gap to more complex dynamics, and whether the Bonn weakness (textureless regions with large overlapping Gaussians) can be resolved without sacrificing the spatial continuity that enables the photometric loss.
