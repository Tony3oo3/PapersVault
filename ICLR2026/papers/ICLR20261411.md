## Problem

Existing Continual Category Discovery (CCD) methods assume that data at each streaming stage is drawn from a single, stationary domain distribution. In realistic open-world deployments, unlabeled streams arrive from diverse sources with concurrent domain shifts (varying cameras, lighting, styles) and emerging novel categories, a setting the authors formalize as Open-World CCD (OW-CCD) and for which no prior method is designed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled base dataset of known categories followed by T sequential unlabeled RGB image streams, where each stream mixes known and novel categories from a domain that may differ from the base. It produces pseudo-labels for known-class samples (via sparse optimal transport), novel cluster assignments (via Affinity Propagation), and an incrementally updated ViT-B/16 model whose classifier head expands to accommodate newly discovered categories. Evaluation uses continual clustering accuracy (cACC) reported separately for All, Old, and New categories on both the known and unknown domains.

## Method

PRISM is a divide-and-conquer framework built on a DINO-pretrained ViT-B/16 backbone (only the final transformer block is fine-tuned). First, a High-Frequency-Driven Category Separation (HCS) module applies DFT to decompose each image into low- and high-frequency components; because high-frequency bands encode domain-invariant structural semantics, a density score (max cosine similarity of high-frequency features to known prototypes) is fit with a two-component GMM to split the stream into known-like and unknown-like subsets. Second, a Sparse Assignment Matching (SAM) module assigns pseudo-labels to known samples by solving a proximal optimal transport problem whose L2-norm proximal term yields sparser, more reliable transport plans than standard Sinkhorn, solved efficiently via a Fenchel-Legendre dual with block coordinate descent. Third, an Invariant Knowledge Transfer (IKT) module generates style-perturbed views of unknown samples by resampling low-frequency statistics from Gaussians estimated on the previous stage, then enforces view-invariant ranking consistency by minimizing KL divergence between Plackett-Luce likelihoods computed under the original and perturbed views. The overall loss combines cross-entropy on pseudo-labeled known and Affinity-Propagation-clustered novel samples with the IKT rank loss, updated online at each stage.

## Results

On the SSB-C benchmark (CUB-C, Original domain), PRISM achieves 49.3% All / 64.9% Old / 44.2% New cACC, surpassing the strongest CCD baseline VB-CGCD (34.2% / 51.8% / 26.3%) by 15.1 points on All accuracy. Under the Corrupted domain setting, PRISM maintains 44.0% All accuracy versus VB-CGCD's 31.7%, a 12.3-point margin. On DomainNet (Real → Painting), PRISM reaches 60.9% All on the source domain and 39.2% on the target domain, outperforming VB-CGCD by 3.6 and 6.8 points respectively. Across all five DomainNet domain pairs and three SSB-C fine-grained datasets, PRISM consistently achieves the highest cACC in both known and unknown domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRISM outperforms all ten baselines across every dataset and domain setting. The closest competitor is VB-CGCD (Dai & Chauhan, 2025), which PRISM surpasses by 15.1 points on CUB-C Original All-accuracy (49.3 vs. 34.2) and by 6.8 points on DomainNet Real→Painting target-domain All-accuracy (39.2 vs. 32.4). Among CCD-specific methods, DEAN (Park et al., 2024) and PromptCCD (Cendra et al., 2024) trail PRISM by roughly 13–20 points on SSB-C, while G&M (Zhang et al., 2022) and Happy (Ma et al., 2024) perform worst, dropping below 20% All-accuracy on CUB-C. GCD-style baselines (GCD, SimGCD, SPTNet, RLCD) and the proxy-anchor method PA-CGCD (Kim et al., 2023) all fall in the 22–30% range on CUB-C Original, confirming that methods not designed for streaming or domain shift struggle severely in the OW-CCD setting. The gap is particularly pronounced on novel-category discovery (New accuracy), where PRISM leads VB-CGCD by 17.9 points on CUB-C Original (44.2 vs. 26.3), indicating that the IKT module's domain-invariant ranking constraint is critical for robust new-class identification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Continual Category Discovery (CCD) — which itself builds on Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) and Novel Class Discovery (NCD) — by introducing the Open-World CCD (OW-CCD) setting where data streams exhibit domain shifts across sessions. Methodologically, it repurposes three ideas from distinct subfields: frequency-domain decomposition (borrowed from domain generalization and style transfer), proximal optimal transport (from domain adaptation and OT-based matching), and Plackett-Luce ranking (from learning-to-rank) to form a unified divide-and-conquer pipeline. It departs from prior CCD methods (DEAN, PromptCCD, VB-CGCD) that assume single-domain streams, and from prior domain-adaptation-for-discovery work (HiLo, CDAD-Net) that requires full dataset access rather than session-based streaming.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OW-CCD is a more faithful model of real-world deployment where new categories and domain shifts arrive simultaneously in unlabeled streams, and the paper provides the first systematic treatment of this combined challenge. The theoretical bound showing high-frequency components are domain-invariant (under smoothness assumptions) gives a principled justification for the separation strategy, though the GMM-based thresholding remains a heuristic that may not generalize to highly overlapping known/unknown distributions. Open questions include scalability beyond three stages, handling multiple concurrent domain shifts, and whether the frequency decomposition can be replaced by learned domain-invariant representations. The exclusion of the most directly relevant baselines (HiLo, CDAD-Net) due to their full-dataset requirement limits the strength of the empirical comparison.
