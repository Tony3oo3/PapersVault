## Problem

Transformers for time series foundation models (TSFMs) typically inherit architectural hyperparameters (width, depth, number of heads) from text-based models, but it is unclear whether these choices are well-calibrated for the time-series modality. The paper addresses this gap by asking: what structural properties of time-series data make their Transformer representations fundamentally different from text or vision, and can these properties be exploited for principled compression and design?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261446/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The TSFMs under analysis consume univariate time series of length T, partitioned into L = T/k patches of size k (with k ≪ d, e.g., k=1 for Chronos, k=16 for Chronos-Bolt), and embed them into R^{d×L} via either quantization-based or continuous (MLP) patch embeddings. The model outputs probabilistic time series forecasts evaluated by WQL and MASE losses. The paper's analytical focus is on internal structure: singular value spectra of the embedding matrix, numerical ε-ranks of Q/K/V attention projections per layer, and how these ranks evolve with depth.

## Method

The paper develops a rank-structure framework with three theoretical pillars. First, it proves that smooth continuous embeddings of low-dimensional patches yield sharply decaying singular values (Theorem 1: polynomial O(j^{-ν}) or exponential O(ρ^{-j}) decay; Theorem 2: numerical rank of an MLP embedding bounded by (1+ε^{-2})k, linear in patch size rather than ambient dimension d). Second, it shows that low-rank inputs make attention matrices compressible: Q/K/V projections can be replaced by rank-d̃ versions with Frobenius error O(√d · σ_{d̃+1}(Ξ)), and this bound is tight (Theorem 3). Third, it introduces 'flow-of-ranks,' proving that a single attention layer can inflate the k-th singular value of the output to the order of the ⌊k/h⌋-th singular value of the input (Theorem 4), explaining why early layers are most compressible and ranks grow with depth. Practically, they apply truncated SVD to a pretrained Chronos model (achieving 65.4% inference-time and 81.4% memory reduction at ~24% parameter ratio with no accuracy loss) and pretrain compressed models using a layer-dependent rank schedule d̃_i = ⌈d̃_0(1+i)^α⌉ that allocates more rank to deeper layers, expanding the time-accuracy Pareto frontier.

## Results

Compressing the pretrained Chronos model via truncated SVD to ~23.7% of its original attention matrix size preserves forecasting accuracy (WQL and MASE scores within 1.03–1.05 of the original), while compressing a same-size T5 LLM to the same ratio causes severe degradation (LPPL 1.652, overlap 0.345). Pretraining a compressed Chronos with a layer-dependent rank schedule (d̃₀=3, α=0.27, size ratio 0.075) achieves 65.4% inference time reduction and 81.4% memory reduction with WQL/MASE scores of 1.034/0.988 (in-domain) and 0.966/0.982 (zero-shot) relative to the original model. The flow-of-ranks design (increasing rank per layer) outperforms a fixed-rank schedule at every compression level.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two compression strategies against the original uncompressed Chronos baseline: (1) post-hoc truncated SVD on a pretrained model, and (2) pretraining a model with low-rank attention parameterized by a flow-of-ranks schedule. At the 23.7% compression ratio, post-hoc SVD on Chronos yields WQL/MASE of 1.053/1.005 (in-domain) and 1.030/1.011 (zero-shot), essentially matching the original, while the same ratio on T5 produces LPPL 1.652 and overlap 0.345, indicating severe quality loss. This demonstrates that TSFMs are far more compressible than LLMs of identical architecture. Pretraining a compressed model extends the viable compression range: at 7.5% size ratio (d̃₀=3, α=0.27), the model achieves 0.346× inference time and 0.186× memory with WQL/MASE of 1.034/0.988 (in-domain) and 0.966/0.982 (zero-shot), outperforming the post-hoc SVD approach which degrades sharply below ~20%. The flow-of-ranks layer-dependent schedule consistently outperforms a fixed-rank schedule at every compression level, confirming that rank must grow with depth to capture the increasing input complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior low-rank attention work (Linformer, Performer, Nyströmformer, Scatterbrain), which treats low rank as an architectural assumption or approximation technique, by showing that for time series the low-rank structure is *inherited from the data modality itself*: small patch sizes and smooth continuous mappings concentrate inputs into low-rank subspaces, which then provably makes attention matrices compressible. It also explicitly distinguishes itself from LoRA-style parameter-efficient fine-tuning, which learns low-rank *updates* on top of full-rank weights, whereas here the attention weights themselves are factorized, yielding inference-time savings. The theoretical tools draw on classical polynomial approximation (Chebyshev, Bernstein ellipses) and singular value inequalities, applied in a new context to connect embedding geometry to attention compressibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 65% inference-time and 81% memory reduction with no accuracy loss is a practically significant win for deploying TSFMs in resource-constrained settings, and the flow-of-ranks framework provides a principled, layer-dependent schedule for allocating rank across depth that generalizes beyond Chronos (validated on Moirai, WaveToken, VisionTS). Open questions include whether the univariate theoretical analysis extends cleanly to multivariate settings, whether the compression benefits persist at much larger model scales, and whether the flow-of-ranks phenomenon interacts with decoder-only or mixture-of-experts architectures in ways not captured by the current theorems. The paper also leaves the training dynamics of quantization-based embeddings (e.g., Chronos) unexplained, noting this is beyond scope.
