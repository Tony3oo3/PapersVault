## Problem

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) produce reasoning hallucinations—logically coherent but factually incorrect reasoning traces that are more deceptive than traditional hallucinations because errors are embedded within structured multi-step reasoning. Prior work identified errors in generated traces but did not uncover the underlying mechanistic causes, and no principled metric existed to distinguish genuine deep reasoning from shallow pattern-matching at the step level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260041/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The detection method (RHD) takes a question and its LRM-generated reasoning trace, requiring access to layer-wise hidden states projected into vocabulary space via LogitLens, plus attention matrices across selected layers. It outputs a scalar hallucination score per trace (a weighted combination of four indicators) and supports both binary detection (AUC/PCC) and multi-trace ranking (MC1/MC2/MC3). The mitigation method (GRPO-R) takes a standard RL training setup with outcome rewards and produces a fine-tuned reasoning model, with the reasoning score computed on-the-fly from intermediate hidden states during rollout.

## Method

The paper introduces the Reasoning Score, which computes the mean Jensen-Shannon divergence between vocabulary distributions induced by projecting selected late-layer hidden states (via LogitLens) and the final-layer anchor distribution, quantifying per-step reasoning depth. Building on this, the RHD detection framework combines four signals: overall reasoning depth, early-step CV (fluctuation), an Attention Score measuring how much later steps attend to earlier shallow or overthinking steps, and the Pearson correlation between reasoning scores and step-level perplexity (spurious verification). For mitigation, GRPO-R integrates a clipped reasoning score as a potential-based reward shaping term into GRPO, redistributing credit across intermediate steps while preserving the optimal policy; a theoretical analysis via Rademacher complexity shows this shaping tightens the generalization gap by reducing the effective policy class complexity.

## Results

On ReTruthQA (R1-7B), RHD achieves the best AUC across all three domains: 0.798 (Math), 0.719 (Science), and 0.736 (MultiHopQA), outperforming the strongest baselines (SelfCheckGPT at 0.773/0.682/0.689 and GPT-4o at 0.751/0.705/0.712). In multi-trace ranking, RHD reaches MC1 of 0.659/0.621/0.766, substantially exceeding the next-best methods. For mitigation, GRPO-R improves over standard GRPO on DeepSeek-R1-1.5B, gaining +1.8 pts on MATH500 (0.788 vs 0.770), +3.4 pts on AIME 2024 (0.367 vs 0.333), and +5.5 pts on GPQA-diamond (0.414 vs 0.359).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RHD outperforms all 14 baselines on both AUC and MC1 across all three ReTruthQA domains for both R1-7B and R1-14B. The closest competitors are SelfCheckGPT (ensemble) and GPT-4o (LLM-as-Critic), which lead in binary detection AUC among baselines but trail RHD by 2.5–5.3 AUC points; in multi-trace ranking, RHD's advantage is even larger (e.g., +25.5 MC1 over GPT-4o on MultiHopQA). Uncertainty-based methods (P(True), PPL, LN-Entropy) and PRMs (Qwen2.5-PRM) consistently underperform, with PRMs showing the weakest generalization across domains. Length-Score is competitive in multi-trace ranking (reflecting that longer traces are more error-prone) but poor in binary detection. For mitigation, GRPO-R beats standard GRPO on 4 of 5 benchmarks for DeepSeek-R1-1.5B and 3 of 5 for Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, with the largest gain on AIME 2024 (+3.4 and +10.0 pts respectively), confirming that step-level reasoning-score shaping improves both in-domain and out-of-distribution reasoning quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: mechanistic interpretability of transformers (specifically the LogitLens technique and the finding that later layers perform reasoning while earlier layers transmit information) and hallucination detection in LLMs. It departs from prior hallucination detection work—which relies on surface-level text signals, uncertainty estimation, or external critic models—by probing internal layer representations to identify reasoning-specific error patterns. On the mitigation side, it extends GRPO (from DeepSeekMath/DeepSeek-R1) with potential-based reward shaping (Ng et al., 1999) to inject step-level reasoning depth rewards, addressing a gap where outcome-based RL ignores intermediate reasoning quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first mechanistic account of *why* reasoning hallucinations occur in LRMs (shallow pattern-matching, incorrect backtracking, spurious verification), which is more actionable than prior error-identification approaches. The ReTruthQA dataset fills a notable gap as the first benchmark specifically targeting reasoning hallucination in multi-step LRMs. However, practical adoption is constrained: the Reasoning Score requires full access to intermediate layer hidden states (ruling out API-only models), and the GRPO-R mitigation experiments are limited to 1.5B models trained on only 2,000 examples, leaving open whether the approach scales to frontier-scale reasoning models or latent CoT architectures.
