## Problem

Classical model-agnostic feature importance methods (LOCO, CPI, SHAP) produce unreliable attributions under feature correlation because they cannot isolate a feature's unique predictive contribution from that of its statistical dependents. The prior Disentangled Feature Importance (DFI) framework addresses this but is restricted to the ℓ₂ loss and relies on computationally intensive Gaussian optimal transport, limiting its applicability to classification and complex high-dimensional distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261950/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a black-box predictor f, a differentiable loss ℓ, labeled data {(X_i, Y_i)}, and an independent set of unlabeled auxiliary covariates {X̃_i} (which can be substantially larger than the labeled set) used solely to train the flow. It outputs two levels of importance: latent scores φ_{Z_j} in the disentangled space and original-feature scores φ_{X_l} obtained by weighting latent scores with the squared Jacobian of T⁻¹, each accompanied by p-values and confidence intervals derived from the efficient influence function.

## Method

FDFI learns a flexible disentanglement map T: X → Z via flow matching (a regression-based velocity field along a linear interpolation path), replacing the restrictive Gaussian OT map so that arbitrary feature distributions can be mapped to independent latent coordinates. Latent importance for Z_j is defined as the expected increase in loss when Z_j is resampled from its marginal while conditioning on Z_{-j}, generalizing the conditional-variance definition of DFI to any differentiable loss. Importance is attributed back to original features by summing over latent directions weighted by (∂X_l/∂Z_j)², the squared Jacobian of the inverse flow. A key theoretical result is that the estimand is Neyman-orthogonal to the transport map T, so the cross-fit estimator achieves √n-consistency and asymptotic normality even when T is estimated nonparametrically at slower rates, yielding valid Wald confidence intervals and hypothesis tests with controlled Type I error.

## Results

On synthetic benchmarks (d=50, 5 correlated blocks), all methods control Type-I error at the nominal 5% level on independent null features, but FDFI and DFI achieve substantially higher AUC and statistical power than LOCO and CPI, with FDFI remaining robust as correlation ρ increases from 0 to 0.9 while LOCO/CPI degrade sharply. On the CTG dataset (n=2126, d=21, binary cross-entropy loss), FDFI demonstrates substantially higher statistical power than LOCO and CPI in identifying essential fetal heart rate features. On two RNA-seq datasets (TCGA-PANCAN-HiSeq: n=801, d=20531; glioblastoma scRNA-seq: n=632, d=23257), FDFI-selected gene sets achieve higher average prediction accuracy than both DFI and the ad-hoc CPI-after-clustering approach across two-fold splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FDFI consistently outperforms LOCO and CPI on AUC and statistical power across all correlation strengths and sample sizes in the synthetic study; the gap widens markedly as ρ increases, where LOCO/CPI importance scores are diluted by multicollinearity while FDFI's flow-based disentanglement preserves signal. Against DFI (Gaussian OT + ℓ₂ loss only), FDFI achieves comparable or better synthetic performance and clearly superior real-data results: on both RNA-seq datasets, FDFI-selected features yield higher prediction accuracy than DFI, and on CTG, FDFI consistently outperforms DFI, underscoring the benefit of flexible flow matching over the restrictive Gaussian transport assumption. FDFI also beats the ad-hoc approach (CPI applied to cluster representatives from Spearman hierarchical clustering) on CTG prediction accuracy (Figure 3c), showing that principled disentanglement outperforms heuristic feature grouping. The FDFI (CPI) variant is selected as the representative method after showing superior finite-sample power over FDFI (SCPI) in low-sample/low-correlation regimes, despite their theoretical equivalence under ℓ₂ loss (Lemma 2.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Disentangled Feature Importance (DFI, Du et al. 2025), which maps correlated features into an independent latent space via Gaussian optimal transport to compute importance scores, but is restricted to the ℓ₂ loss and a parametric OT map. The key methodological departure is replacing the Gaussian OT with a flexible flow-matching transport (Lipman et al. 2022), enabling arbitrary feature distributions, and generalizing the attribution formula to any differentiable loss function. On the inference side, the paper borrows the Neyman-orthogonality / efficient-influence-function machinery from the double/debiased machine learning literature (Chernozhukov et al. 2018; Williamson & Feng 2020) to establish semiparametric efficiency for the new estimators, a step that DFI did not provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FDFI addresses a long-standing practical failure mode: standard attribution methods (LOCO, CPI, SHAP) systematically understate the importance of correlated features, which is pervasive in genomics, clinical data, and any domain with redundant measurements. By providing valid confidence intervals and hypothesis tests alongside the point estimates, the paper moves feature importance from a descriptive diagnostic toward a tool for formal statistical decision-making (feature pruning, experimental design, model debugging). The main open limitation is that FDFI is a local first-order sensitivity measure in the learned latent space, so it may fail for highly non-smooth or combinatorial predictors (e.g., XOR-type interactions), a regime the authors explicitly flag as future work.
