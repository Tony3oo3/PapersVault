## Problem

Existing Mixture of LoRA Experts (MoLE) frameworks rely on non-differentiable TopK routing that assigns a fixed number of experts to every token, preventing adaptive allocation based on token complexity and hindering end-to-end optimization. ReLU-based dynamic routing alternatives (e.g., ReMoE) introduce instability by potentially routing tokens to zero experts. The paper asks whether a routing mechanism can be simultaneously differentiable, token- and layer-adaptive, and guaranteed to activate at least one expert.

## Input / Output

The method consumes token embeddings from a frozen pretrained LLM (Llama-3.2-3B/8B or Qwen3-1.7B) at each target module (Q, K, V, O, Up, Gate, Down projections). A lightweight shared MLP (one per unique input dimension, e.g., 2048 and 6144 for Qwen3-1.7B) predicts a scalar sparsity factor λ per token, which together with gate logits produces a sparse probability vector over 8 LoRA experts (rank 8, scaling 16). The final output is the base model output plus the weighted sum of LoRA expert outputs, yielding next-token logits for instruction-tuning or a classification score for GLUE tasks.

## Method

LD-MoLE replaces the discrete TopK selection with Sparsegen, a closed-form projection onto the probability simplex parameterized by a sparsity scalar λ, ensuring differentiability and guaranteeing at least one activated expert per token (Lemma 1). A shared MLP predicts λ per token, enabling the model to adaptively determine how many experts to activate at each layer without manual hyperparameter tuning. The paper derives an analytical sparsity loss (Proposition 2) that maps a target expert count k to a feasible interval for λ, allowing direct regularization of expert sparsity via a ReLU penalty on the lower bound. Training combines standard cross-entropy, a conventional load-balancing loss, and the sparsity loss with coefficients α and β. The shared MLP design decouples parameter cost from layer count, requiring only one MLP per unique input dimension rather than per layer.

## Results

LD-MoLE achieves the highest average accuracy across all three base models: 85.18% on Llama-3.1-8B, 82.05% on Llama-3.2-3B, and 81.63% on Qwen3-1.7B, evaluated on 9 benchmarks (MMLU-Pro, ARC-C, ARC-E, OpenBookQA, CommonsenseQA, SWAG, HellaSWAG, CoLA, RTE). The method uses 8 LoRA experts (rank 8, scaling 16) with a shared MLP predicting a token-specific sparsity factor λ, and the main comparison is conducted with α=1.0 and β=0 (sparsity loss disabled). Trainable parameters range from 1.26% (MoLA-2468 on Llama-3.1-8B) to 4.34% (LD-MoLE on Qwen3-1.7B), with LD-MoLE adding only ~0.1–0.2% over the fixed-routing baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MoLA (TopK routing, Gao et al. 2024), LD-MoLE wins on average across all three models: +2.70% over MoLA(8888) and +2.33% over MoLA(2468) on Llama-3.1-8B, +3.87% and +3.33% on Llama-3.2-3B, and +2.39% and +2.35% on Qwen3-1.7B. The gains are most pronounced on reasoning-heavy benchmarks (ARC-E, SWAG, HellaSWAG) and MMLU-Pro, while MoLA retains a slight edge on RTE for Llama-3.2-3B (90.61 vs. 88.38). Against ReMoLE (ReLU-based dynamic routing, Wang et al. 2025), LD-MoLE achieves +0.71% on Llama-3.1-8B, +0.63% on Llama-3.2-3B, and +0.61% on Qwen3-1.7B in average accuracy. ReMoLE exhibits notable instability: it drops to 83.95 on CoLA (Llama-3.2-3B) and 83.74 on RTE (Qwen3-1.7B), whereas LD-MoLE maintains 86.02 and 87.72 respectively, consistent with the zero-activation issue in ReLU routing that LD-MoLE's Sparsegen formulation avoids by guaranteeing at least one active expert per token.

## Relation to Prior Work

LD-MoLE extends the Mixture of LoRA Experts (MoLE) framework (Wu et al., 2024) by replacing the non-differentiable TopK routing used in MoLA (Gao et al., 2024) with a closed-form Sparsegen projection (Laha et al., 2018) onto the probability simplex, controlled by a token-dependent sparsity parameter λ predicted by a lightweight shared MLP. This departs from both fixed-k TopK routing (which requires manual hyperparameter tuning and is non-differentiable) and ReLU-based dynamic routing (ReMoE, Wang et al., 2025), which can suffer from zero-activation instability. The paper further introduces an analytical sparsity loss derived directly from the Sparsegen closed-form solution, providing a principled mechanism to regularize the number of activated experts without heuristic thresholds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LD-MoLE offers a clean, differentiable, and analytically grounded alternative to both fixed and ReLU-based routing in the MoLE setting, with the guarantee that every token activates at least one expert. However, the practical gains are modest (+0.5–0.7% over the strongest dynamic baseline ReMoLE), and the sparsity loss reveals a clear performance trade-off where disabling it yields the best scores, limiting its immediate utility. The evaluation is confined to small models (1.7B–8B) and a limited set of benchmarks, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger architectures or pretraining regimes. Future work on integrating the routing into pretraining and extending to multi-modal settings could broaden its impact.
