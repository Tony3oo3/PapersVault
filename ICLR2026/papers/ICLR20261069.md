## Problem

Stop-gradient (SG) and exponential moving average (EMA) procedures are standard components of non-contrastive self-supervised learning (e.g., SimSiam, BYOL, V-JEPA) and empirically prevent representation collapse, yet there is no established link between these iterative procedures and the optimization of any well-defined objective function. The paper asks three questions: (a) do SG/EMA minimize any objective, (b) do they converge and avoid collapse, and (c) are their stationary points stable as dynamical systems?

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of video views (different crops/transforms of the same clip) from Kinetics710 and SSv2 training sets, processed by a ViT-Base encoder (student) and ViT-T predictor, producing n-dimensional visual representation embeddings. For downstream evaluation, an attentive pooling classifier maps these embeddings to action class labels, with top-1 accuracy reported on K400 and SSv2 validation splits. In the theoretical linear analysis, the input reduces to scalar or low-dimensional vectors with covariance matrices [xx^T] and [yx^T] fully characterizing the data distribution.

## Method

The paper provides a dual theoretical analysis. From an optimization perspective, it proves (Proposition 3.2) via Schwarz's integrability theorem that the SG and EMA update vector fields are not gradients of any smooth scalar function, confirming a conjecture from BYOL, while showing that convergence (when it occurs) avoids the degenerate zero minimum. From a dynamical systems perspective in the linear case (f_θ(x)=Ax, g_ψ(z)=Bz), it characterizes all equilibria as finite sets of algebraic varieties parameterized by positive-definite solutions S=A^TA to a quadratic matrix equation (Proposition 4.5), proves their existence via Brouwer's fixed point theorem (Proposition 4.7), and establishes asymptotic stability of nontrivial equilibria via Lyapunov/Arnol'd linearization (Proposition 4.10). It further shows that minimizing the original objective without SG/EMA always drives A→0 (collapse), confirming that the asymmetry is essential. Empirical validation uses V-JEPA code on video classification and synthetic scalar-input simulations.

## Results

The paper is primarily theoretical, with empirical validation on video action classification (Kinetics710 + SSv2 training, K400 and SSv2 evaluation) using a simplified V-JEPA setup (ViT-Base encoder, ViT-T predictor, ℓ₁ loss, 300K iterations). The key empirical finding is that neither SG nor EMA minimizes the original objective E(θ,ψ) in practice, and neither converges (parameter increment norms do not tend to zero). Classification accuracy increases initially for both procedures before decreasing (SG) or plateauing (EMA), with EMA consistently outperforming SG. Performance is lower than the full V-JEPA ViT-L model (72.9% K400, 67.4% SSv2) due to the simplified setup. In synthetic linear experiments (m=1, n=2), 92.8% of EMA trials and 82.0% of SG trials converge to the nontrivial outer-circle equilibrium S₂, with the remainder converging to the trivial origin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the SG (SimSiam, Chen & He 2021) and EMA (BYOL, Grill et al. 2020) training procedures on the same video classification task. EMA achieves better downstream classification accuracy than SG on both K400 and SSv2, consistent with the original BYOL and SimSiam findings. However, the paper's central contribution is not a performance comparison but a theoretical one: it proves that neither procedure minimizes the original objective E(θ,ψ) nor any other well-defined smooth function (Propositions 3.1–3.2), confirming the BYOL authors' conjecture. Both procedures avoid representation collapse when they converge, and in the linear case their nontrivial equilibria are asymptotically stable (Proposition 4.10). The paper also rederives key dynamical system results from Tian et al. (2021) without the restrictive assumptions (e.g., isotropic data, bounded eigenvalues) required in the original proofs, making the theory more broadly applicable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dynamical-systems analysis of non-contrastive SSL initiated by Tian et al. (2021), Wang et al. (2021), and Littwin et al. (2024), but deliberately removes the isotropic-data and eigenvalue-boundedness assumptions those works required, rederiving their key lemmas under strictly weaker conditions. It also departs from the purely dynamical-systems framing by adding an optimization-theoretic perspective, proving (via Schwarz's integrability theorem) the conjecture stated by the BYOL authors that neither SG nor EMA corresponds to gradient descent on any well-defined scalar function. The empirical validation uses the V-JEPA codebase (Bardes et al., 2024) on Kinetics710/SSv2, positioning the analysis within the current JEPA family of models rather than the earlier BYOL/SimSiam setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing ambiguity: SG and EMA are not optimization procedures in any standard sense, yet they empirically outperform true gradient descent on the original objective (which provably collapses in the linear case). The characterization of equilibria as finite algebraic varieties with asymptotic stability provides a rigorous guarantee that, in the linear regime, once training reaches a nontrivial fixed point it will not drift to collapse under further training. The major open question the paper explicitly flags is what these procedures actually learn in the general nonlinear case, where the dynamical-systems analysis does not extend; the empirical observation that parameter increments never vanish (i.e., the algorithms do not converge) in realistic ViT training further underscores this gap.
