## Problem

Current molecular graph representation learning encodes geometric parameters (bond angles, torsion angles) only implicitly through combinations of atom and bond tokens, causing prediction errors to accumulate and failing to capture the interdependent relationships among higher-order geometric structures. This indirect representation also creates ambiguity in local chirality, since molecules with different handedness can produce nearly identical distance matrices. The paper addresses these gaps by asking whether geometric parameters can be modeled as first-class structural tokens with explicit, physically constrained interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260720/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The conformation predictor takes a 2D molecular graph (atom features, edge features, and optionally RDKit-derived initial 3D coordinates) and outputs binned pairwise interatomic distances, bond angles, and torsion angles over the full (0, 2π) range. The task predictor consumes these predicted geometric quantities (or ground-truth 3D conformations when available) along with the molecular graph to produce scalar property predictions (e.g., HOMO-LUMO gap in meV, binding affinity in kcal/mol, or ROC-AUC scores for virtual screening). During pre-training, the task predictor additionally receives noisy ground-truth conformations and must denoise them, making it robust to the residual errors of the conformation predictor.

## Method

TetraGT introduces bond angles and torsion angles as explicit structured tokens alongside atom and edge tokens, and designs a spatial tetrahedral attention mechanism that selectively communicates between neighboring face angles sharing a vertex and dihedral angles sharing a common face, guided by tetrahedral angle inequality constraints (Lemma 1). A Directed Cycle Angle Loss (DCA loss) predicts angles over the full (0, 2π) range with a counterclockwise reference direction, enabling explicit local chirality discrimination that distance-only methods cannot achieve. A hierarchical virtual node architecture assigns a dedicated virtual node to each structural order (atoms, edges, bond angles, torsion angles) plus a molecule-level node, alleviating information bottlenecks from cross-order compression. A local sampling strategy restricting attention to w nearest neighbors reduces the interaction complexity from O(N³) to O(wN²). Training proceeds in three stages—conformation prediction, multi-task pre-training with noisy-conformation denoising, and downstream fine-tuning—using a 4:1 distance-to-angle loss ratio for optimal performance.

## Results

On PCQM4Mv2, TetraGT-24 achieves 65.9 meV MAE (with RDKit conformers) and 67.1 meV (2D-only), matching or surpassing the previous SOTA TGT (67.1 meV with RDKit) while using fewer parameters (215M vs. 203M). On OC20 IS2RE, TetraGT attains 397.7 meV average energy MAE and 9.14% EwT, outperforming TGT (403.0 meV, 8.82%) and UniMol+ (408.8 meV, 8.61%). On QM9, TetraGT achieves state-of-the-art MAE on 5 of 12 properties (ε_H: 8.5, ε_L: 8.7, Δε: 15.6, C_v: 0.019, R²: 35), and on PDBBind it reaches R=0.852 / MAE=0.909, surpassing Transformer-M (R=0.830 / MAE=0.940).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TetraGT outperforms TGT (Hussain et al., 2024) on all primary benchmarks: 1.2 meV lower MAE on PCQM4Mv2 with RDKit (65.9 vs. 67.1), 5.3 meV lower energy MAE on OC20 IS2RE (397.7 vs. 403.0), and lower MAE on all 12 QM9 properties. Against UniMol+ (Lu et al., 2023), TetraGT achieves comparable PCQM4Mv2 performance (67.1 vs. 69.3 meV) with 3× less pre-training time (33 vs. 112 A100 GPU days) and superior OC20 results (397.7 vs. 408.8 meV). On PDBBind, TetraGT (R=0.852, MAE=0.909) surpasses Transformer-M (R=0.830, MAE=0.940) and SIGN (R=0.797, MAE=1.027). On Peptides-func, TetraGT achieves 72.86% AP, beating DRew (71.50%) and GRED (71.33%); on Peptides-struct it reaches 0.2421 MAE, below Graph ViT (0.2449) and MGT (0.2453). On LIT-PCBA, TetraGT (82.4% avg ROC-AUC) edges out GEM-2 and TGT (both 81.5%). The only area where TetraGT does not dominate is QM9 properties like dipole moment (μ: 0.017 vs. SaVeNet's 0.0085) and polarizability (α: 0.032 vs. SaVeNet's 0.035), where specialized equivariant energy models retain an advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

TetraGT extends the Graph Transformer lineage (EGT, Graphormer, TGT, UniMol+) by promoting bond angles and torsion angles from implicit positional features to first-class structured tokens, building directly on TGT's triangle-inequality-constrained distance prediction and EGT's edge-token global attention paradigm. The key conceptual departure is the tetrahedral attention mechanism, which generalizes the triangle inequality principle from pairwise edges to higher-order face-angle and dihedral-angle constraints derived from Lemma 1, enabling direct communication between geometric-parameter tokens rather than routing all information through atom and bond embeddings. The Directed Cycle Angle Loss and hierarchical virtual node architecture are novel components with no direct predecessor in the molecular GT literature, though the virtual node idea draws on recent graph transformer work (Li et al., 2024b; Xing et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By treating geometric parameters as explicit tokens, TetraGT addresses a fundamental representational gap: distance-only models (TGT, UniMol+) cannot distinguish enantiomers, and the 136% improvement in chirality prediction accuracy (76.7% vs. 32.5%) demonstrates that this gap has tangible consequences for drug discovery tasks where stereochemistry is critical. The method achieves SOTA on PCQM4Mv2 and OC20 IS2RE with competitive training costs (33 vs. 112 A100 GPU days vs. UniMol+), suggesting the explicit angle-token approach is not merely a theoretical curiosity but a practical architectural choice. Open questions include whether the O(wN²) complexity with local sampling suffices for very large biomolecular systems (beyond the ~64-atom limit tested on OC20), whether the tetrahedral constraints generalize to non-tetrahedral local geometries (e.g., planar sp² centers), and whether the three-stage training pipeline (conformer prediction → pre-training → fine-tuning) can be simplified without sacrificing the denoising robustness it provides.
