## Problem

Class-incremental learning assumes all tasks share a fixed domain distribution, making models brittle when data arrive from evolving environments. Existing domain-shift CIL variants either require shared label spaces across domains, access to target-domain data, or multi-domain supervision per class—none handle the case where each class is observed in exactly one domain and the model must generalize bidirectionally to unseen class-domain combinations at test time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260642/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (processed as 16×16 patches by a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone) and produces a class label via cosine similarity against evolving domain-aware prototypes. At inference, a non-parametric domain prototype matching step routes each input to the appropriate domain-specialized prompt before classification. Evaluation uses both standard continual-learning accuracy (Final, Avg) and the novel BiDoT Score, which measures accuracy specifically on class-domain pairs that were never jointly supervised during training.

## Method

XEED combines three components: (1) domain-specialized prompts (length 5) inserted after the class token of a frozen ViT, trained with an auxiliary linear classifier so they encode domain characteristics while remaining disentangled from class semantics; (2) synthetic cross-domain image generation using IP-Adapter on SDXL, where a residual vector (image embedding minus CLIP text embedding) strips class-specific content and injects only domain features into a target class's text prompt via modified cross-attention in layout/style transformer blocks; (3) evolving prototype embeddings per class-domain pair, incrementally updated with generated samples and used for cosine-similarity classification. The key insight is that generative replay of domain-transferred synthetic images, combined with prototype-based semantic expansion, enables bidirectional domain knowledge transfer without storing real data or retraining the backbone.

## Results

XEED achieves the highest BiDoT scores across all three datasets, with a 31.41-point F-BiDoT improvement over the second-best baseline (CPrompt, 33.78) on PACS (65.19) and a 10.69-point gain on Office-31 (78.08 vs. 67.39 for CODA-P). In terms of standard accuracy, XEED attains 61.86% Final and 78.51% Avg on PACS, 80.72% Final and 83.19% Avg on Office-31, and 35.30% Final and 48.40% Avg on DomainNet, yielding a 7.1% higher average standard accuracy over the best baseline. Notably, even offline joint fine-tuning (Joint-FT) shows large accuracy drops under XIL (e.g., 49.74% Final on PACS vs. 61.86% for XEED), confirming that bidirectional domain transferability is not an emergent property of current architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

XEED dominates all six baselines on the BiDoT metric (the paper's primary evaluation of cross-domain transferability) across every dataset, with the largest margin being +31.41 F-BiDoT over CPrompt on PACS (65.19 vs. 33.78). On standard Final accuracy, XEED outperforms CODA-P (80.72 vs. 75.84) and CPrompt (80.72 vs. 73.90) on Office-31, and beats Joint-FT by 12.12 points on PACS (61.86 vs. 49.74). The only losses are on DomainNet Final and Avg accuracy, where CPrompt edges ahead (37.26 vs. 35.30 Final; 50.60 vs. 48.40 Avg) and CODA-P is slightly higher on Final (36.08 vs. 35.30), though XEED still leads both on F-BiDoT and A-BiDoT there. Traditional regularization methods (LwF, EWC) and the simpler prompt method (S-Prompts) are consistently the weakest, with F-BiDoT scores below 30 on PACS and below 13 on DomainNet, confirming that parameter-level or single-prompt strategies cannot support bidirectional domain transfer. Prototype-based SimpleCIL performs competitively on Office-31 (69.06 F-BiDoT) but collapses on PACS and DomainNet, highlighting that prototype classification alone is insufficient without the generative cross-domain expansion that XEED provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends class-incremental learning (CIL) by introducing a new problem setting (XIL) where each class is observed in exactly one domain during training but must be evaluated across all domains at test time, requiring bidirectional domain knowledge transfer. It departs from prior domain-incremental learning work (Shi & Wang 2023, Kundu et al. 2020, Simon et al. 2022) that either fixes the label space, provides multi-domain supervision per task, or requires access to target-domain data. Methodologically, XEED combines prompt tuning (Jia et al. 2022, CODA-Prompt, CPrompt) with diffusion-based synthetic exemplar generation (IP-Adapter/SDXL, building on generative replay ideas from Meng et al. 2024) and prototype-based classification (SimpleCIL), creating a pipeline that disentangles class and domain semantics via residual-guided cross-attention modulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The XIL setting captures a realistic deployment scenario (e.g., industrial robots encountering new parts in new visual styles) that standard CIL benchmarks ignore, and the BiDoT Score provides a concrete metric for a previously unmeasured dimension of generalization. The finding that even offline Joint-FT fails to develop natural bidirectional domain transferability suggests that standard training objectives do not encourage the class-domain disentanglement needed for cross-domain class generalization, pointing to a genuine gap in current representation learning. Limitations include reliance on expensive diffusion-based generation, evaluation on only three datasets, and the somewhat restrictive assumption that each class appears in exactly one source domain; open questions include whether the approach scales to dozens of domains and whether lighter-weight alternatives to diffusion-based synthesis could achieve comparable transfer.
