## Problem

The dominant paradigm for adapting LLM workflows—optimizing discrete prompts within a pipeline—is brittle and struggles to enforce the formal type/structure compliance required for structured tasks, reducing workflow adaptation to an expensive discrete search with no principled gradient-based training signal. This is especially problematic when intermediate reasoning steps are latent variables not present in annotated data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263844/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes typed inputs (a natural-language question, or a financial table paired with a question) and produces typed outputs (a numeric answer, a SymPy arithmetic expression, or a classification label) that must pass validation via parse/canon functions. The workflow is a directed acyclic hypergraph where intermediate nodes (rationales, sub-expressions) are latent variables unobserved in training data, and type constraints act as hard support restrictions on each adaptor's output distribution.

## Method

TACs recast an entire LLM workflow as a typed, unnormalized probabilistic program: each LM step is a rank-1 LoRA adaptor whose output is masked to valid type representations, deterministic steps are delta distributions, and the full workflow defines a joint unnormalized distribution over all node assignments. The TACSTaR training algorithm uses MC-EM—the E-step samples complete execution traces (falling back to a rationalization TAC conditioned on the correct answer when forward execution fails), and the M-step maximizes the unnormalized log-likelihood of collected traces. A key theoretical result proves that the optimization bias from dropping the intractable partition-function gradient is bounded by a constant times (1 − Z_θ), which vanishes as the model learns type compliance during training. An amortized variant replaces the fixed rationalization heuristic with a learned inference-network TAC trained via self-normalized importance sampling to better approximate the posterior over latent variables.

## Results

TACs consistently outperform DSPy prompt-optimization baselines across all four evaluated tasks and three model families. Headline gains include FinQA improving from 12.0% to 24.7% (Qwen3-8B) and 12.7% to 34.0% (Gemma 2 27B), MGSM-SymPy from 57.1% to 75.9% (Gemma 2 27B), MGSM from 1.6% to 27.3% (Gemma 7B), and MuSR from 36.5% to 62.6% (Gemma 7B). On the larger Gemma 2 27B model, TACs achieve 82.2% on MGSM (vs. 81.9% DSPy) and 65.0% on MuSR (vs. 51.5% DSPy). Type compliance (estimated partition function Z_θ) reaches ~99.5% by epoch 9, confirming the theoretical justification for the unnormalized training objective.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DSPy (the primary prompt-optimization baseline using MIPROv2 and BootstrapFewShotWithRandomSearch), TACs win on every task-model combination tested. The margin is largest on smaller models and structured tasks: FinQA with Gemma 7B improves by 9.0 points (0.7→9.7), MGSM with Gemma 7B by 25.7 points (1.6→27.3), and MGSM-SymPy with Gemma 2 27B by 18.8 points (57.1→75.9). On the strongest model (Gemma 2 27B), TACs still lead on MuSR (65.0 vs. 51.5) and MGSM-SymPy (75.9 vs. 57.1), while MGSM is nearly tied (82.2 vs. 81.9). Against the untyped STaR baseline on MGSM, TACs achieve 82.2% vs. 76.9% (Gemma 2 27B) and 27.3% vs. 10.5% (Gemma 7B), demonstrating that typed, structured workflow adaptation substantially outperforms unstructured self-taught reasoning, particularly for smaller models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes STaR (Zelikman et al., 2022) from a single-step, untyped rationalization loop to a typed, multi-step hypergraph framework where each transformation is a PEFT-adapted LM or deterministic algorithm. It departs fundamentally from prompt-optimization frameworks like DSPy and LangChain, which treat the workflow as a fixed-parameter system and search over discrete prompts, by instead casting the entire workflow as an unnormalized probabilistic program with continuous, gradient-trainable parameters. The unnormalized distribution arising from type constraints connects the work to classical structured prediction (CRFs, SPENs), while the MC-EM training loop and amortized inference network draw on variational inference and importance sampling literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled alternative to brittle discrete prompt search for structured LLM workflows, with a clean theoretical guarantee that the tractable unnormalized objective converges to true MLE as type compliance is learned. However, evaluation is limited to five tasks and three model families (all ≤27B parameters), and the engineering overhead of defining type systems and validation logic via LangFun may be non-trivial for practitioners. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models and more complex multi-agent workflows, how sensitive performance is to the choice of type definitions, and whether the MC-EM training loop remains stable with deeper or more branching hypergraphs.
