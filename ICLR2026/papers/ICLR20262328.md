## Problem

Existing AI-generated image detectors either act as black-box classifiers offering no explanation of why an image is synthetic, or rely on MLLMs that hallucinate rationales without spatial grounding. The fundamental gap is the absence of a high-quality dataset with human-annotated, region-level bounding boxes and captions that can supervise MLLMs to produce grounded, human-aligned explanations rather than unverified textual claims.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262328/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (512×512) as input and produces a structured multi-part output: a binary verdict (real vs. AI-generated) wrapped in <verdict> markers, one or more bounding boxes with descriptive captions for each fake region enclosed in <think> markers, and image-level quality tags (e.g., perspective errors, texture errors, artistic style) in <tag> markers. On average, each AI-generated image contains 5.42 region-caption pairs, and the full inference pipeline takes ~7.8 seconds on 2× A100 GPUs.

## Method

FakeXplainer fine-tunes MLLMs (base: Qwen2.5-VL-32B) via a two-stage pipeline on the newly introduced FakeXplained dataset (8,772 images from 28 generators, annotated by 23 trained annotators with bounding boxes, captions, and image-level tags). Stage 1 is LoRA-based SFT that teaches the model a structured Chain-of-Thought output format using <think>, <tag>, and <verdict> markers. Stage 2 applies progressive GRPO (pGRPO) with three reward functions—classification accuracy, relaxed IoU for region grounding (scaled by η=1.1), and format validity—where the IoU weight increases linearly from 0.5 to 1.0 over training to prevent early over-optimization of fragmented bounding boxes. This progressive reward shaping enables a natural curriculum: the model first stabilizes on formatting and classification, then refines localization on top of that foundation, achieving 98.2% accuracy and 36.0% IoU while remaining model-agnostic across architectures with and without native grounding capabilities.

## Results

FakeXplainer (Qwen-2.5-VL-32B) achieves 98.2% classification accuracy and 36.0% IoU for artifact localization on the FakeXplained test set, outperforming all traditional and MLLM-based baselines. On out-of-distribution datasets (FakeClue, Chameleon, GPT-Image-1, FaceForensics++, MMFR-Dataset), it maintains 80.1–87.4% accuracy, consistently exceeding the next-best method by 5–40 points. The model also achieves 0.267 BLEU-2 and 0.251 ROUGE-L for explanation quality, far surpassing FakeShield (0.004/0.003) and LEGION (0.072/0.055).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FakeXplainer outperforms every baseline on classification accuracy. Among segmentation-based methods, it beats ObjectFormer (98.2% vs. 95.4% Acc; 36.0% vs. 29.9% IoU) and SegFormer (98.2% vs. 94.5% Acc; 36.0% vs. 28.9% IoU), showing that MLLM-based grounding localizes artifacts more faithfully than pixel-level segmentation. Against classification-only detectors, the margin is 5.4–10.0 points over NPR (91.4%), DMD (92.8%), DIRE (91.1%), AfPr (88.7%), ComFor (88.2%), and AEROB (87.3%). Among MLLM-based competitors, FakeXplainer dominates FakeShield (98.2% vs. 80.1% Acc; 36.0% vs. 2.8% IoU) and LEGION (98.2% vs. 58.3% Acc; 36.0% vs. 9.8% IoU), with explanation quality (BLEU-2) exceeding FakeShield by over 60× and LEGION by ~3.7×. On all five OoD benchmarks, FakeXplainer leads the next-best method by at least 5 points (e.g., 87.4% vs. 78.6% on MMFR-Dataset; 86.4% vs. 86.1% on FaceForensics++), confirming that grounded reasoning generalizes better than black-box classification under distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MLLM-based AIGI detection (FakeShield, LEGION, FakeVLM) by introducing spatially grounded, human-annotated supervision (bounding boxes + captions) rather than relying on model-generated or GPT-4V-produced rationales. The training pipeline adapts the SFT-then-GRPO recipe from DeepSeek-Math to the vision-language setting, adding a progressive IoU-weighted reward that addresses the fragmentation problem observed when localization rewards are applied uniformly from the start. It departs from segmentation-based detectors (SegFormer, ObjectFormer) by leveraging the MLLM's intrinsic grounding capability without external modules like SAM, and from binary classifiers (NPR, DMD, ComFor) by producing structured, interpretable outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FakeXplained dataset (8,772 images with region-level human annotations) fills a concrete gap that prior MLLM-based detectors could not address, and the finding that grounded reasoning yields substantially better OOD generalization than black-box classification (e.g., 80.1% vs. 51.3% on GPT-Image-1) suggests a broader principle: explicit spatial grounding acts as a regularizer against distribution shift. However, the approach is fundamentally limited to images with human-perceptible artifacts; fully photorealistic synthetic images without semantically describable flaws remain undetectable by this paradigm. Open questions include whether the progressive GRPO reward design transfers to other grounding tasks, and whether the 36% IoU ceiling reflects an inherent limitation of text-based bounding box output versus pixel-level segmentation.
