## Problem

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) generate redundant Chain-of-Thought reasoning—exhibiting both overthinking (re-verifying already-correct steps) and underthinking (oscillating without convergence on hard problems)—which inflates decoding length and inference cost without accuracy gains. Existing remedies either require expensive fine-tuning that risks degrading general capabilities or impose invasive inference-time modifications. The paper asks whether a training-free, non-invasive mechanism can dynamically detect and truncate redundant thinking online while preserving LRM accuracy.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a problem/question and the LRM's streaming token output, which is asynchronously segmented into sub-thoughts at predefined reflection tokens (e.g., '\n\nWait', '\n\nBut'). The verifier only inspects the last N sentences of each sub-thought (200–400 tokens) rather than the full 8K–128K reasoning sequence, performing two binary classification checks (answer existence and answer equivalence) via prefill-only inference. The output is the LRM's response with redundant intermediate thinking truncated and a final answer emitted, guided by gentle or forceful stop prompts injected at the point of detected redundancy.

## Method

TrimR deploys a lightweight, pretrained 7B instruction-tuned verifier (no fine-tuning of either the LRM or verifier) that performs two simplified binary checks: (1) whether a sub-thought contains a definitive answer, and (2) whether two consecutive answer-bearing sub-thoughts are semantically equivalent. Overthinking is triggered when M consecutive equivalent answers are detected, and underthinking when no convergence occurs within a token-budget fraction R_thres and N_thres reasoning rounds; in both cases, a guidance prompt is injected to halt or redirect the LRM's generation. A rolling-hash-based repetition detector catches token loops in real time. The entire pipeline runs asynchronously via the T4S system integrated with vLLM on Ascend NPUs, where the verifier is invoked infrequently (reflection tokens account for ~0.5% of output) and can serve multiple LRM instances concurrently at an 8:1 ratio, keeping verification overhead negligible relative to the 16–70% runtime reductions achieved.

## Results

TrimR achieves up to 70% runtime reduction on GPQA Diamond for DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B (11,366s → 3,411s), with 53–70% runtime savings across all four benchmarks for that model. For QwQ-32B and Pangu-R-38B, runtime reductions range from 16–39% and 19–39% respectively, while token usage drops by 8–46% overall. Accuracy is largely preserved (drops <1.7%) and in several cases actually improves (e.g., R1Q-32B gains +2.0% on MATH500 and +13.2% on GPQA Diamond; QwQ-32B gains +1.2% on MATH500). TPR reductions reach 74.7% for R1Q-32B on GPQA Diamond, indicating substantial per-request latency improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TrimR is compared against three concurrent baselines: CoThink (instruction-model-guided reasoning), SpeedAdapt (hidden-state modulation for reasoning speed), and Certaindex (semantic-entropy-based early stopping). On R1Q-32B, TrimR dominates all baselines: it achieves 40.1% token reduction on MATH500 (vs. CoThink's 36.6% and SpeedAdapt's 7.3%) while improving accuracy by +2.4%, whereas CoThink drops accuracy by −2.0% and Certaindex by −4.0%. On AIME24, TrimR's 35.6% token reduction far exceeds CoThink's 12.5% (which suffers a severe −13.3% accuracy drop) and SpeedAdapt's 12.7%. On QwQ-32B, TrimR achieves 23.3% token reduction on AIME24 with zero accuracy loss, outperforming SpeedAdapt (9.3% reduction) and CoThink (16.2% reduction). Overall, TrimR consistently delivers the best accuracy–efficiency trade-off, with the largest margins on the weaker R1Q-32B model where redundant reasoning is most prevalent.

## Relation to Prior Work

TrimR carves out a distinct niche among efficient-reasoning methods by being simultaneously training-free, non-invasive, and verifier-based. It departs from training-based approaches (RL with length rewards, SFT compression) that risk degrading general capabilities, and from self-evaluation methods (SpeedAdapt, SelfThink, AlphaOne) that add inference latency through extra model calls. Conceptually, it repurposes the early-stopping criterion from numerical optimization and the overthinking/underthinking taxonomy from cognitive science, but the key methodological departure is simplifying the detection problem from full-sequence PRM/ORM scoring to two binary classification tasks (answer existence and equivalence) solvable by a 7B instruction-tuned model with no fine-tuning. The asynchronous T4S system design builds on vLLM's paged-attention infrastructure but introduces a parallel verifier pipeline with prefix-caching and rolling-hash repetition detection that prior serving systems (Certaindex, CoThink) do not provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For production deployments of LRMs, TrimR offers a drop-in efficiency layer that reduces runtime by up to 70% without retraining or modifying the base model, which is critical as reasoning models become standard in enterprise AI stacks. The finding that a small 7B verifier with simple binary prompts can replace complex reward models for redundancy detection lowers the barrier to adoption significantly. Open questions include: (1) generalizability to LRMs that do not use structured reflection tokens as thought delimiters, (2) whether the under-2% accuracy regressions compound under adversarial or out-of-distribution inputs, and (3) how the method interacts with emerging test-time scaling strategies like MCTS or iterative self-refinement that may benefit from the 'redundant' exploration TrimR prunes. The threshold calibration (R_thres, N_thres, M) remains heuristic and model-specific, suggesting room for adaptive or learned stopping criteria.
