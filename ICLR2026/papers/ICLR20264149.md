## Problem

Existing off-policy evaluation estimators for ranking (ranking-wise IPS, independent IPS, RIPS) all require the logging policy to be sufficiently stochastic to satisfy common support conditions, but large-scale industrial systems typically deploy fully deterministic policies. Under deterministic logging, these estimators incur severe downward bias because they assign zero weight to all rankings not selected by the logging policy, making accurate OPE effectively infeasible in the most common practical setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a logged dataset of (context, ranking, click vector, observable reward) tuples collected under a logging policy, along with a new ranking policy to evaluate and estimated click probabilities (learned via a 3-layer neural network from the logged data). It outputs a scalar policy value estimate V̂(π; D) representing the expected sum of post-click rewards under the new policy, with theoretically characterized bias and variance. Unlike standard IPS-based methods that require the logging policy's action probabilities, CIPS only requires click probability estimates, which remain well-defined even when the logging policy is deterministic.

## Method

CIPS replaces the standard policy-probability importance weight π(A|x)/π₀(A|x) with a click-based importance weight p_c(x,a,π)/p_c(x,a,π₀), where p_c(x,a,π) is the marginalized probability that a user with context x clicks action a under policy π. This exploits the intrinsic stochasticity of user click behavior: even under a fully deterministic logging policy, users still click each presented action with non-zero probability, providing a source of exploration that policy-level weights lack. Unbiasedness is guaranteed under two conditions—click-wise common support (far weaker than ranking-wise or position-wise support) and independence of potential rewards—both of which are more realistic than the stochasticity requirements of prior methods. The CDR extension adds a doubly robust correction using a regression model q̂_r(x,a) for expected potential rewards, which reduces variance (via the Δ_r term in the variance decomposition) without introducing additional bias relative to CIPS.

## Results

Under fully deterministic logging (α=∞), CIPS achieves substantially lower MSE than all baselines (IPS, IIPS, RIPS) across synthetic and real-world (KuaiRec) experiments, with the advantage driven almost entirely by squared bias reduction rather than variance. CIPS with estimated click probabilities (via a 3-layer neural network) performs close to the oracle CIPS (true CTR) upper bound, confirming practical applicability. On KuaiRec with both deterministic logging and new policies (ε=0.0, α=∞), CIPS still achieves lower MSE than baselines despite some residual bias from partial click-wise common support violations. CDR further reduces variance relative to CIPS without introducing additional bias, as confirmed by ablation studies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIPS outperforms all three baselines—ranking-wise IPS (Kiyohara et al., 2023), position-wise IIPS (Li et al., 2018), and cascade RIPS (McInerney et al., 2020)—on MSE and squared bias under deterministic logging, where all baselines suffer severe downward bias from common support violations. IPS is the worst baseline, with its ranking-wise support condition violated in 5 of 6 possible rankings in the toy example, leading to the largest underestimation. IIPS is slightly better than IPS (support violated in 6 of 9 position-action pairs vs. 5 of 6 rankings) but still incurs substantial bias. RIPS falls between the two, with its prefix-based support violated in 3 of 15 cases. CIPS's advantage is most pronounced at high determinism (large α) and grows with ranking length K; when the logging policy is sufficiently stochastic (small α), IIPS performs on par with CIPS, confirming that CIPS's primary contribution is addressing the deterministic-logging regime rather than the standard stochastic setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ranking OPE literature (Li et al. 2018, Kiyohara et al. 2022/2023, McInerney et al. 2020), which has primarily addressed high variance from large action spaces under stochastic logging, by targeting the orthogonal problem of severe bias under deterministic logging. It departs from the standard IPS/DR framework (Dudík et al. 2014) by replacing logging-policy importance weights with click-probability-based importance weights, drawing a conceptual parallel to the deficient-support literature (Sachdeva et al. 2020, Felicioni et al. 2022) but addressing the strict special case where all stochasticity in the logging policy is eliminated. The CDR extension follows the standard doubly-robust correction pattern, and the Plackett-Luce-based policy parameterization is borrowed from prior synthetic OPE experiments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work challenges the long-held assumption that reliable OPE is infeasible under deterministic logging, which is the dominant deployment regime in large-scale industrial ranking systems. The practical impact is significant: it enables policy evaluation in settings where all prior estimators produce substantial downward bias. Key limitations include the need for accurate click probability estimation (bias grows with estimation error per Theorem 3.2) and residual bias when both logging and new policies are fully deterministic (violating even click-wise common support). Open questions include how to further mitigate bias in the doubly-deterministic case (the authors suggest action embeddings à la Saito & Joachims 2022) and whether the click-probability weighting idea generalizes beyond ranking to other combinatorial action spaces.
