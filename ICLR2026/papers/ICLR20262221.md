## Problem

Distributed self-supervised learning (D-SSL) must handle highly heterogeneous (non-IID) data across clients, yet there is no rigorous theoretical understanding of how different D-SSL paradigms—contrastive learning vs. masked image modeling, federated vs. decentralized—differ in their robustness to this heterogeneity. Prior work proposed algorithmic fixes but left open fundamental questions such as whether MIM-based D-SSL is inherently more robust than CL-based D-SSL, and how network connectivity affects robustness in decentralized settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images partitioned across N clients with non-IID label distributions (simulated via Dirichlet sampling with parameter α ranging from 1e5 for IID down to 0.001 for extreme heterogeneity). The theoretical analysis operates on a simplified generative model with 2N classes in d-dimensional space, where each client holds two dominant classes plus a rare class with sublinear sample count. The output is a pre-trained encoder (ResNet-18 or ViT) whose feature representations are evaluated by fine-tuning on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet; the MAR variant additionally requires each client to transmit compact masked embeddings (z_i) to neighbors or the server for the A-MMD alignment term.

## Method

The paper constructs a formal non-IID data model and derives stationary conditions for both SimSiam (CL) and MIM objectives under linear embeddings, then defines a Representability Vector (RV) to quantify how uniformly the learned feature space covers the data-generating basis directions. By bounding the RV for local, decentralized-global, and federated-global representations, they prove (Theorem 4.4) that MIM-based D-SSL has strictly smaller sensitivity to heterogeneity than CL-based D-SSL as dimension grows, and (Theorem 4.6) that federated learning is no less robust than decentralized learning, with robustness in DecL increasing monotonically with average network connectivity. As a practical instantiation, they introduce MAR loss, which augments the MIM reconstruction objective with an adaptive MMD (A-MMD) alignment regularizer between local and global masked representations, weighted by a cosine-decay schedule that applies stronger alignment early in training when client divergence is highest. The full algorithms (FedMAR and DecMAR) are validated across ResNet-18 and Tiny-ViT backbones under both FL and DecL settings, outperforming six SOTA federated SSL baselines on CIFAR-10/100 while matching or exceeding them on ImageNet.

## Results

On the non-IID federated SSL benchmark (100 clients, Dirichlet α=0.1, Mini-ImageNet pre-training), FedMAR with ResNet-18 achieves 92.70% on CIFAR-10, 70.82% on CIFAR-100, and 65.36% on ImageNet fine-tuning accuracy. With a lightweight Tiny-ViT backbone (11.60M params, 0.88 GFLOPs), FedMAR reaches 90.03%, 71.28%, and 75.99% respectively, surpassing all CNN-based baselines on every benchmark. The theoretical robustness claim is further validated in Table 1, where distributed MIM (MAE) shows a much smaller IID-to-non-IID accuracy drop (e.g., ↓0.01% on ImageNet for MAE+ViT) compared to distributed CL (SimSiam) (e.g., ↓2.84% on ImageNet for SimSiam+ViT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedMAR (ResNet-18) achieves the best CIFAR-10 accuracy (92.70%), outperforming the strongest baseline LDAWA (89.95%) by 2.75 points and Orchestra (88.87%) by 3.83 points. On CIFAR-100, FedMAR leads with 70.82%, edging out Orchestra (70.11%) by 0.71 points and FeatARC (64.11%) by 6.71 points. On ImageNet, FedMAR (ResNet-18) at 65.36% is competitive with FedU (65.10%) and FedEMA (65.24%) but trails FeatARC (68.17%) by 2.81 points; however, the Tiny-ViT variant of FedMAR reaches 75.99%, surpassing all baselines by a wide margin (≥7.8 points). Against the weakest baselines, FedMAR gains are substantial: +20.68% over FedU and +10.31% over FedU² on CIFAR-10, and +13.93% over LDAWA on ImageNet. Overall, FedMAR wins or ties on 5 of 6 benchmark-architecture combinations, with the sole loss being ImageNet under the ResNet-18 backbone where FeatARC's clustering-based aggregation provides a slight edge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical analysis of Wang et al. (2022), who showed SSL outperforms supervised learning in distributed settings but only for the specific CL+FL combination, to a full comparison across all D-SSL paradigms (MIM vs. CL, FL vs. DecL). It builds on the MIM reconstruction objective from MAE (He et al., 2022) and the MMD distributional discrepancy framework (Gretton et al., 2012) to propose MAR loss, a practical refinement guided by the theoretical insights. The paper departs from prior D-SSL algorithmic work (FedU, Orchestra, LDAWA) by providing the first rigorous representability-based comparison of how different pre-training objectives and network topologies interact with data heterogeneity, rather than proposing yet another aggregation strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that MIM-based D-SSL is inherently more robust than CL-based D-SSL provides a principled reason to shift away from the CL-dominated design space in federated self-supervised learning, and the connectivity-robustness relationship offers concrete guidance for network topology design in decentralized deployments. Limitations include the simplified non-IID model (two dominant classes per client, linear embeddings in theory) and the modest practical gains from MAR loss (1-2% over vanilla MIM), leaving open whether the robustness ordering holds under more complex heterogeneity patterns or non-linear architectures. The work opens the question of whether the representability framework can be extended to analyze other SSL paradigms (e.g., generative, token-based) in distributed settings.
