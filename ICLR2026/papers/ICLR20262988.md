## Problem

Vision-language-action (VLA) models compress visual observations into low-bandwidth semantic embeddings that fail to explicitly encode future dynamics, limiting fine-grained motor control. Video-centric world models offer a more suitable foundation, but existing approaches rely on single-view generation misaligned with multi-view robot perception, and serial video-to-action pipelines require aggressive latent compression that destroys the spatial and contact cues needed for precise manipulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262988/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes synchronized multi-view observations (one head-mounted and two wrist-mounted cameras), a natural-language instruction, and sparsely sampled keyframe memory from prior history, optionally augmented with robot joint state. It produces two coupled outputs: autoregressively generated future multi-view video chunks (N frames per step) and a 54-step high-frequency (30 Hz) torque action trajectory decoded from the same latent features. The autoregressive chunk-wise formulation with sparse memory enables long-horizon coherence beyond a single generation window.

## Method

GE-Base is a multi-view video DiT (LTX-Video 2B backbone) that autoregressively predicts future head- and wrist-view observations, using cross-view attention in selected transformer blocks to enforce spatial consistency across perspectives and sparse memory frames for extended temporal context. GE-Act is a lightweight 160M-parameter action decoder block-wise aligned with GE-Base: rather than compressing final-layer video latents, it cross-attends to multi-scale latent features at every matching DiT depth, preserving fine-grained geometry and contact cues. Both branches are trained with a flow-matching (velocity-matching) diffusion objective. At inference, a slow-fast asynchronous schedule runs the video branch with a single denoising step at 5 Hz while the action branch performs multi-step denoising at 30 Hz, yielding 54-step action predictions within 200 ms on a single RTX 4090.

## Results

On EWMBench, GE-Base achieves a total score of 4.7010 (Scene: 0.9427, Motion: 1.6676, Semantics: 2.0907), outperforming the next-best model Kling (3.8698) by 0.83 points, with the largest margin on the Motion dimension (1.6676 vs. 0.9440). On the AgiBot G1 manipulation benchmark, GE-Act consistently surpasses π₀, UniVLA, and GR00T N1 on both Step-wise Success Rate (SR) and End-to-End Success Rate (E2E) across five diverse dual-arm tasks. In the pretraining ablation, in-domain embodied pretraining yields 64% SR / 81% E2E, which improves to 76% SR / 89% E2E when combined with general video pretraining, versus near-zero success from scratch or general-video-only initialization. GE-Act generates 54-step torque trajectories within 200 ms on a single RTX 4090 using asynchronous slow-fast inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On EWMBench, GE-Base outperforms all five video-generation baselines (Kling, Hailuo, COSMOS, OpenSora, LTX) on every sub-metric, with the most dramatic advantage on Motion (1.6676 vs. 0.9440 for Kling, 0.3442 for OpenSora), reflecting its robotic-domain pretraining. On the AgiBot G1 manipulation benchmark, GE-Act beats π₀, UniVLA, and GR00T N1 on both SR and E2E across all five tasks; the asynchronous inference mode further improves latency-sensitive tasks like dynamic tracking. On Dual Franka, GE-Act surpasses π₀ and GR00T N1 despite those models being trained on large-scale Franka data, using only 250 teleoperated episodes (≈1 hour). On Agilex Cobot Magic, UniVLA and GR00T N1 achieve 0% success on complex deformable-object tasks (cloth/box folding), while GE-Act maintains high success rates. On RoboTwin, GE-Act uses an all-in-one joint fine-tuning strategy (200 demos across 4 tasks) and outperforms task-specific π₀ and GO-1 on three of four tasks, trailing only slightly on 'lift pot' due to task interference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant VLA paradigm (RT-2, π₀, GR00T N1, UniVLA) that compresses visual observations into language-centric semantic embeddings, and from serial video-to-action pipelines (GR-2, Video Prediction Policy, VidMan) that decode actions from compressed video latents. Its key architectural departure is the block-wise parallel alignment between the video diffusion backbone (GE-Base, built on LTX-Video DiT) and the action decoder (GE-Act), where action blocks cross-attend to multi-scale visual features at matching transformer depths rather than consuming only final-layer latents. This positions GE between the 'video-as-policy-encoder' family and the 'unified video-action generator' family, avoiding both the information loss of heavy latent compression and the pixel-level generation cost of joint video-action diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GE demonstrates that a single video-generative framework can simultaneously serve as a world simulator (multi-view, instruction-conditioned, autoregressive) and a real-time manipulation policy (54-step trajectories in 200 ms), with cross-embodiment transfer requiring only ~1 hour of teleoperation. This is practically significant for reducing the data burden of deploying manipulation policies on new robot platforms. However, pretraining relies on a single dual-arm dataset (AgiBot-World-Beta), evaluations are limited to upper-body tabletop tasks with parallel-jaw grippers, and the compute cost of pretraining remains high. Open questions include whether the parallel block-aligned design generalizes to dexterous hands, mobile manipulation, and highly dynamic scenes that demand faster-than-5 Hz video updates.
