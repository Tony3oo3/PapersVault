## Problem

Text-to-two-person-interaction generation is hindered by two compounding limitations: (1) two-person motion corpora are markedly smaller than single-person ones (InterHuman: <8k vs. HUMANML3D: >14k sequences), limiting coverage of coordination patterns and out-of-distribution generalization; and (2) prior methods compress structurally rich interaction prompts (median 21 tokens) into a single sentence embedding, discarding token-level cues about initiation, response, and contact ordering that are essential for faithful spatiotemporal alignment.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language prompt describing a two-person interaction (tokenized to at most 75 word-level CLIP tokens) and produces a two-person 3D motion sequence X = [x₁, x₂] ∈ R^{2×T×N×3}, where each agent's per-frame state comprises global joint positions and velocities (3N each), 6D local rotations (6N), and binary foot-ground contact (4). Generation uses 1000 diffusion training steps with a cosine schedule and 50-step DDIM sampling at inference with classifier-free guidance weight 3.5.

## Method

Text2Interact combines a data synthesizer (InterCompose) with a generation model (InterActor). InterCompose uses an LLM to sample diverse two-person interaction descriptions from a theme-tag space, decomposes each into role-specific single-person prompts, generates one agent's motion via the pretrained MoMask generator, and trains a conditional DDPM reaction model to synthesize the second agent given the first agent's motion and the shared text; a two-stage filter (cosine-similarity threshold δ=0.58 for text-motion alignment, plus a k-NN annulus in the evaluator embedding space for distributional novelty) retains only high-quality, diverse samples. InterActor is a 12-block diffusion Transformer that replaces sentence-level AdaLN conditioning with word-level cross-attention, where each motion token attends to all CLIP word tokens to preserve fine-grained temporal and spatial cues, and alternates with a motion-motion interaction module (self-attention + inter-agent cross-attention) to model coordination. An adaptive interaction loss L_AdaInteract supervises inter-person joint-pair distances with inverse-distance weighting (1/(d_ij + ε)), emphasizing spatially proximate joints (e.g., hands in handshakes) over distant ones, in contrast to uniform-weight baselines.

## Results

On the InterHuman test set, InterActor achieves R-Precision Top-1 of 0.483, Top-2 of 0.638, and Top-3 of 0.717, setting new state-of-the-art on all three text-motion alignment metrics. It also attains the best MM Distance (3.778) and MModality (1.051), with an FID of 5.191 that is statistically indistinguishable from the previous best (InterMask, 5.154) within 95% confidence intervals. In a user study on out-of-distribution prompts, 81.1% of evaluators preferred InterActor's text-motion consistency and 80% preferred its motion quality over InterMask.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InterActor outperforms the previous state-of-the-art, InterMask, on all three R-Precision metrics (Top-1: 0.483 vs. 0.449, a 3.4-point gain), MM Distance (3.778 vs. 3.790), and MModality (1.051 vs. 1.737), while its FID (5.191) is marginally higher than InterMask's (5.154) but within the 95% confidence interval of the latter. Against in2IN, InterActor leads on R-Precision Top-1 (0.483 vs. 0.425) and FID (5.191 vs. 5.535) but trails on MModality (1.051 vs. 1.215). Compared to MoMat-MoGen, InterActor wins on R-Precision (0.483 vs. 0.449) and FID (5.191 vs. 5.674) but has slightly lower Diversity (7.900 vs. 8.021). Against the earlier diffusion-based baselines InterGen, RIG, ComMDM, MDM, and T2M, InterActor achieves substantial improvements across all metrics, with the largest gaps in R-Precision (e.g., 0.483 vs. 0.371 for InterGen) and FID (5.191 vs. 5.918 for InterGen). The user study on out-of-distribution LLM-generated prompts further confirms InterActor's advantage over InterMask, with 81.1% and 80% user preference for text-motion consistency and motion quality respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-person interaction generation line (InterGen, InterMask, RIG, in2IN) by addressing two specific weaknesses: limited training data and coarse sentence-level text conditioning. It builds directly on MoMask as a single-person motion prior and on InterGen's motion representation and loss suite, but departs from the field's standard practice of compressing interaction prompts into a single CLIP sentence embedding by introducing word-level cross-attention that preserves token-level temporal and spatial cues. The InterCompose data synthesizer is a novel compositional strategy that repurposes single-person diffusion priors and LLM-generated role-specific descriptions to expand the two-person distribution without additional motion capture, a direction not explored by prior interaction generators that train end-to-end on the small InterHuman corpus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The data-synthesis-by-composition pipeline offers a practical, scalable remedy to the chronic scarcity of two-person motion data, and the word-level conditioning plus adaptive interaction loss provide a template for fine-grained text-to-motion alignment in any multi-agent setting. However, the method still lacks physics-based constraints (floating, ground penetration are acknowledged), is limited to exactly two agents, and the FID gains from synthetic-data fine-tuning come at roughly flat R-precision, suggesting the pipeline improves distributional coverage more than semantic alignment. Open questions include whether the composition strategy generalizes to three or more agents, whether video-based motion learning can replace the MoCap-free single-person prior, and whether the adaptive proximity weighting transfers to interactions with objects or environments.
