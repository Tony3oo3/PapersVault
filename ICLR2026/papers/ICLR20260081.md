## Problem

Audio LLMs exhibit a phenomenon the authors term test-time inverse scaling: enabling chain-of-thought reasoning during inference actively degrades performance, with longer reasoning chains yielding progressively worse results than direct answering. The authors diagnose this not as a fundamental limitation of reasoning but as a training failure—existing SFT and outcome-only RLVR methods never teach models to reason coherently, so unguided thinking accumulates hallucinations and logical inconsistencies. The gap is a principled way to reward the reasoning process itself rather than only the final answer.

## Input / Output

The model (Qwen2.5-Omni-7B) receives an audio clip, a text question, and a set of four multiple-choice options. It produces a structured two-part output: a chain-of-thought reasoning trace enclosed in <think>... tags, followed by the selected answer in <answer>...</answer> tags. At inference, the maximum reasoning length (max_think_len) is a tunable parameter that the authors sweep to identify a model-specific 'reasoning sweet spot' where accuracy peaks, typically around 35–40 tokens for the full CESAR model.

## Method

CESAR trains via online GRPO (K=8 sampled responses per input) with a decomposed reward that pairs verifiable signals (binary accuracy weighted α₁=5.0 and XML format compliance) with three reasoning-process rewards (each α=1.0): (1) a consistency reward measuring concept-overlap semantic similarity between the reasoning trace and both the final answer and the full question context, penalizing hallucinated or off-topic reasoning; (2) a keywords reward that sums three sub-scores—structured analytical patterns (sequential organization, comparative analysis, systematic evaluation), logical-rigor markers (premise-deduction, evidential support, hypothetical reasoning), and domain-specific audio terminology (acoustic properties, musical concepts, speech analysis); and (3) a linear overthinking penalty that decreases with reasoning length to discourage verbose, error-accumulating traces. The authors additionally apply answer-invariant question-rephrasing data augmentation to prevent superficial textual shortcuts. This process-oriented reward design, applied on top of the same GRPO pipeline and base model as the Ke-Omni-R baseline, resolves test-time inverse scaling and reveals a controllable reasoning sweet spot at inference.

## Results

On MMAU Test-mini, CESAR achieves 77.10% total accuracy with reasoning, surpassing Gemini 2.5 Pro (71.60%), GPT-4o Audio (62.50%), and the RL baseline Ke-Omni-R (74.60%). On MMSU, CESAR reaches 81.07% average on reasoning tasks (approaching human 86.77%) while also improving perception to 48.45% over the base model's 42.50%. On the more challenging MMAU-Pro benchmark, CESAR scores 56.4% average, outperforming Ke-Omni-R (54.5%) and GPT-4o Audio (52.5%), trailing only Gemini 2.5 Flash (59.2%) among AI models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CESAR decisively outperforms the primary RL baseline Ke-Omni-R (77.10% vs. 74.60% on MMAU Test-mini), with the gap widening on MMAU-Pro (56.4% vs. 54.5%) and MMSU reasoning tasks (81.07% vs. 78.06%), confirming that process-oriented rewards yield gains beyond outcome-only RLVR. Against proprietary models, CESAR surpasses Gemini 2.5 Pro by 5.5 points (77.10% vs. 71.60%) and GPT-4o Audio by 14.6 points (77.10% vs. 62.50%) on MMAU Test-mini. On MMAU-Pro, CESAR (56.4%) beats GPT-4o Audio (52.5%), Audio Flamingo 3 (51.7%), and R1-AQA (34.1%), though it trails Gemini 2.5 Flash (59.2%)—the only model that outperforms it. Compared to the base Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, CESAR improves reasoning-mode accuracy by 11.9 points (77.10% vs. 65.20%) and even non-reasoning accuracy by 5.1 points (73.70% vs. 68.60%), demonstrating that cultivating reasoning creates synergistic gains across both modes. On MMSU, CESAR achieves near-human reasoning (81.07% vs. human 86.77%) while simultaneously outperforming all other models on perception tasks (48.45% avg vs. 47.09% for Ke-Omni-R and 39.67% for GPT-4o Audio).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLVR (Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards) paradigm for audio reasoning, building directly on GRPO-based methods like Ke-Omni-R and R1-AQA that optimize solely for final-answer correctness and format compliance. The key conceptual departure is shifting from outcome verification to rewarding the reasoning process itself, introducing a multi-faceted reward suite (semantic consistency, structured pattern keywords, domain terminology, and a linear overthinking penalty) layered on top of standard accuracy and format signals. It also positions against SFT-on-CoT approaches (Audio-CoT, AudioReasoner) that the authors argue teach template mimicry rather than genuine analytical skill, and against the broader Audio LLM training literature (Qwen2.5-Omni, Audio Flamingo, SALMONN) that has not addressed reasoning quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification and diagnosis of 'test-time inverse scaling' in Audio LLMs—where longer reasoning chains actively degrade performance—is a useful reframing that redirects the field from questioning whether reasoning can work in audio to how to train it properly. The practical 'reasoning sweet spot' concept gives practitioners a training-free inference-time lever. However, the perceptual bottleneck (48.45% vs. 91.24% human on MMSU perception) remains the dominant constraint, and the keyword-matching approach to incentivizing 'structured reasoning' is a surface-level heuristic that may not generalize beyond the specific taxonomies used. Open questions include whether process-oriented rewards transfer to other modalities (vision, robotics) and whether more principled process supervision (e.g., LLM-judged reasoning quality) can replace the brittle keyword scaffolding.
