## Problem

Existing molecular representation learning methods predominantly target central chirality via handcrafted stereochemical tags or limited 3D encodings, and fail to generalize to more complex stereogenic forms such as axial chirality. E(3)-invariant architectures are fundamentally unable to distinguish enantiomers, while SE(3)-invariant models treat molecular graphs as homogeneous structures, diluting stereochemical signals during global aggregation and lacking explicit, localized descriptors for chiral centers or stereogenic axes.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D atomic coordinates, 52-dimensional atom-type features (atom type, degree, formal charge, hydrogen count, hybridization), and explicit annotations partitioning atoms into three disjoint sets: chiral atoms, chiral-related atoms (substituents around chiral centers or nearest atoms along a stereogenic axis), and non-chiral atoms. For ECD prediction, the output is a multi-task, multi-label structure jointly predicting peak count (0–6), discretized peak positions (20 classes), and peak-height sign (binary), while OR prediction maps to a binary rotation label at 589.3 nm. Chiral-related atoms are ordered by CIP priority to construct the chirality matrix, a non-trivial preprocessing step that differs between central and axial chirality.

## Method

ChiDeK introduces a chiral determinant kernel that generalizes the classical 3×3 chirality-matrix determinant into a k-dimensional learnable embedding: the chirality matrix is projected through learnable weights, layer-normalized, QR-decomposed, and the determinant of the R factor is extracted, yielding a reflection-sensitive, SE(3)-invariant chiral descriptor per chiral atom. A cross-attention mechanism then uses chiral atoms as queries and chiral-related plus non-chiral atoms as keys/values, with distance-aware Gaussian Kernel with Pair Type (GKPT) biases encoding geometric interactions; the pairwise bias is updated layer-by-layer through query-key interactions. Three separate multi-layer projectors initialize chiral, chiral-related, and non-chiral atoms independently, preserving their distinct stereochemical roles. Theoretical analysis proves the generalized chirality product equals a positive scaling factor times the original chirality product, preserving the R/S sign convention, and two strategies (weight regularization and auxiliary QR on weights) guard against rank deficiency that would collapse the determinant to zero.

## Results

On central chirality tasks, ChiDeK achieves 99.8% R/S classification accuracy, 72.8% enantiomer ranking accuracy, and 53.3% ECD peak-height symbol accuracy. On the newly introduced axial chirality benchmark (ACMP), ChiDeK achieves 69.2% optical rotation accuracy and 71.2% ECD peak-height symbol accuracy, surpassing the best baseline (SPMS) by 4.2% and 10.8% respectively. Across all axial chirality subtasks, ChiDeK yields over 7% higher accuracy on average compared to state-of-the-art baselines, while all other SE(3)-invariant methods (SphereNet, ChIRo, ChiGNN, ECDFormer) remain near random chance (~50–53%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChiDeK decisively outperforms all seven baselines on axial chirality tasks. The E(3)-invariant DimeNet++ and the central-chirality-specific methods (Tetra-DMPNN, ChIRo, ChiGNN) all collapse to near-random performance (~50%) on axial OR and ECD symbol prediction, confirming their inability to capture stereogenic axes. SphereNet and ECDFormer, which incorporate torsional information, marginally exceed chance (52.5% and 53.5% OR accuracy) but still fail to meaningfully encode axial chirality. SPMS, the only baseline that partially encodes axial stereochemistry via spherical projections, is the strongest competitor at 65.0% OR and 60.4% ECD symbol accuracy, yet ChiDeK surpasses it by 4.2% and 10.8% respectively. On central chirality, ChiDeK matches or slightly exceeds the best baselines (99.8% R/S vs. 99.7% for Tetra-DMPNN; 72.8% ranking vs. 72.0% for ChIRo), confirming no regression on the well-studied central case.

## Relation to Prior Work

ChiDeK builds directly on the chirality matrix formulation from ChiralFinder (Shi et al., 2026), generalizing the 3×3 determinant into a high-dimensional kernel via QR decomposition, and adopts the GKPT distance-encoding module (Shuaibi et al., 2021) for geometric bias in attention. It departs from prior SE(3)-invariant architectures (SphereNet, ChIRo, ChiGNN) that treat molecular graphs as homogeneous and dilute stereochemical signals during global aggregation, by explicitly partitioning atoms into chiral, chiral-related, and non-chiral subsets and routing them through a dedicated chiral encoder and cross-attention pathway. The framework also extends beyond the central-chirality focus of nearly all prior learned representations (Tetra-DMPNN, ChiGNN, ChIRo) to handle axial stereogenic axes, a gap the authors identify as largely unaddressed in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of the ACMP benchmark (1,192 axially chiral enantiomers with TD-DFT-computed ECD and OR labels) fills a notable gap in stereochemistry-aware ML evaluation, and the >7% average accuracy gain on axial tasks over all baselines demonstrates that explicit stereogenic encoding is not merely a theoretical nicety but a practical necessity for complex chirality. However, the axial dataset remains small, ECD sign accuracy for central chirality plateaus near 53% across all models (attributed to conformer-label mismatches in CMCDS), and the framework has not yet been extended to planar or helical chirality. Open questions include whether the chiral determinant kernel generalizes to multi-center or distributed stereogenic systems, and whether the cross-attention design scales to larger molecules with many chiral centers.
