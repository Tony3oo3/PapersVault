## Problem

Combining multiple spatial conditions (e.g., pose + segmentation, depth + edges) for text-to-image diffusion models typically requires expensive retraining or manual weight tuning, and existing training-free fusion strategies like MaxFusion are easily disrupted by early-stage denoising noise and fail to reconcile conflicting control signals. The paper tackles how to fuse multiple pre-trained ControlNet branches in a fully training-free manner that remains robust to noise and handles both conflicting and complementary conditions without degrading text-image alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264416/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (captioned via BLIP-2 for evaluation) together with two or more spatial condition maps—such as OpenPose keypoints, semantic segmentation masks, depth maps, Canny edges, or HED soft edges—each processed by its own pre-trained ControlNet branch. It produces a single generated image at 512×512 (Stable Diffusion v1.5, U-Net backbone) or higher resolution (FLUX.1-dev, DiT backbone) using 50 UniPC sampling steps with classifier-free guidance. No additional training or weight tuning is required; the only user-facing hyperparameter is a unified threshold δ (default 0.7) governing the fusion strategy.

## Method

Cross-ControlNet fuses intermediate features from multiple ControlNet branches through three tightly coupled, training-free modules. PixFusion performs pixel-wise feature selection guided by Gaussian-smoothed spatial standard-deviation maps, switching to mean fusion when a correlation threshold indicates overlapping conditions, thereby suppressing early-stage denoising noise. ChannelFusion operates along the channel dimension using a standard-deviation consistency ratio R_c to adaptively apply either hard selection (keep the stronger modality) or precision-weighted soft fusion (derived via variational inference / ELBO maximization), circumventing the threshold-degradation problem where fixed cosine-similarity thresholds lose discriminative power in high dimensions. KV-Injection transfers foreground- and background-specific key-value pairs across ControlNet self-attention layers under text-derived cross-attention masks, explicitly decoupling competing spatial cues so each condition is enforced in its designated region. The design is grounded in two empirical observations: cross-branch features are naturally spatially aligned, and condition strength is quantifiable via spatial and channel-wise variance.

## Results

Under conflicting conditions (Pose+Seg), Cross-ControlNet achieves mIoU-P of 0.3719 and MSE-S of 0.1983, improving mIoU by 5.4% and reducing MSE over the strongest training-free baseline MaxFusion (mIoU 0.3528, MSE 0.2016) while maintaining competitive CLIP alignment (0.2978). Under complementary conditions (HED+Seg), it attains the best SSIM (0.2404) and lowest MSE-S (0.1008) among all compared methods. On the DiT-based FLUX.1-dev model, Cross-ControlNet reduces inference time by 24.8% versus EasyControl (19.6 s vs. 26.0 s) while achieving higher CLIP text-alignment scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cross-ControlNet outperforms all six U-Net-based baselines on condition-fidelity metrics (MSE and mIoU) under both conflicting condition pairs. Against the strongest training-free baseline MaxFusion, it improves mIoU by 5.4% (0.3719 vs. 0.3528) and reduces MSE-S by 1.6% (0.1983 vs. 0.2016) on Pose+Seg, with similar gains on Pose+Depth. Multi-ControlNet achieves slightly higher CLIP scores (0.2999 vs. 0.2978 on Pose+Seg), indicating marginally better text alignment, but at the cost of substantially worse condition fidelity (mIoU 0.3145 vs. 0.3719). Training-based methods AnyControl and Uni-ControlNet are clearly outperformed on all metrics, with mIoU gaps exceeding 15 percentage points. On FLUX.1-dev, Cross-ControlNet trades a small amount of conditional fidelity (higher MSE) for better text alignment (CLIP 0.3075 vs. 0.2983) and 24.8% faster inference compared to EasyControl, reflecting its design emphasis on conflicting rather than complementary conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free multi-condition fusion line initiated by MaxFusion (Nair et al., 2024), which simply averages ControlNet branch outputs, by introducing three noise-aware fusion modules grounded in two empirical observations about spatial alignment and variance-based condition strength. The KV-Injection module repurposes key-value transfer ideas from the training-free image-editing literature (Cao et al., 2023; Tewel et al., 2024; Alaluf et al., 2024) and adapts them to disentangle foreground/background in multi-condition generation—a setting where prior editing methods do not apply. The ChannelFusion soft-fusion rule is derived from a variational inference / ELBO framework, providing a principled Bayesian justification for precision-weighted feature averaging that goes beyond the ad-hoc weighting in MaxFusion or Cocktail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free design means practitioners can combine any pair of pre-trained ControlNets without retraining, which is practically valuable as the ControlNet ecosystem grows with new modalities. The threshold-degradation analysis (cosine similarity concentrating near zero in high dimensions) offers a clean explanation for why naive pixel-level fusion degrades at deeper layers, and the ablation showing ChannelFusion should be restricted to the final layer is a useful practical guideline. Limitations include dependence on pre-trained single-condition ControlNets, increased VRAM (~12 GiB vs. ~10.6 GiB for MaxFusion), and residual artifacts under extreme conflicts; the authors note future work on video and 3D extension.
