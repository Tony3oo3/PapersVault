## Problem

Existing post-training quantization (PTQ) methods for diffusion models assign uniform weights to calibration samples across all timesteps, ignoring that activation distributions and gradient directions vary significantly across the denoising trajectory. This uniform treatment induces gradient conflicts during quantization, where optimizing the quantized model for one timestep degrades performance at others, leading to uneven quality across the diffusion process. The paper identifies this gradient conflict as a previously overlooked issue specific to quantized diffusion models and addresses it through timestep-aware sample weighting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained full-precision diffusion model (DDPM or LDM-4) and a set of calibration samples generated by running the full-precision model at fixed intervals across denoising timesteps, and produces a quantized model with reduced bit-widths (e.g., W4A32, W4A8) that generates synthetic RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10) or 256×256 (LSUN-Bedrooms, ImageNet) resolutions. For class-conditional generation on ImageNet, the input additionally includes a class label alongside Gaussian noise. The quantized model shares the same architecture and inference procedure as the full-precision model, so deployment-time hardware efficiency is unchanged.

## Method

The paper proposes a meta-learning-based calibration framework that learns per-sample importance weights to align quantization gradients across timesteps. The validation loss combines standard MSE reconstruction with a gradient matching term that penalizes misalignment between gradients computed on different timestep groups, encouraging coherent optimization directions. Direct optimization of the bi-level objective is intractable due to third-order terms, so the authors derive a simplified one-step-ahead algorithm (Algorithm 2) and prove via two lemmas that it implicitly minimizes the original objective. In practice, timesteps are partitioned into 5 groups, and sample weights are updated every T iterations using a pseudo-gradient step that evaluates how well the one-step quantized model's gradients align across groups. The overall pipeline applies AdaRound with block-wise reconstruction for weight quantization and EMA-based activation quantization, with the learned sample weights modulating the calibration loss at each block transition.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (DDPM, W4A32), the proposed method achieves FID 4.28 and sFID 4.56, outperforming the strongest baseline TFMQ-DM (FID 4.73). On LSUN-Bedrooms (LDM-4, W4A32), it reaches FID 3.14 and sFID 7.22 versus TFMQ-DM's 3.60/7.61. On ImageNet (LDM-4, W4A32), it achieves FID 10.17 and sFID 7.40, compared to TFMQ-DM's 10.50/7.98. Gains are consistent across both W4A32 and W4A8 settings, with FID improvements of 0.33–0.46 over the prior state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all four baselines on FID across every dataset and bit-width setting. Against the strongest competitor TFMQ-DM (CVPR 2024), it achieves FID improvements of 0.45 (CIFAR-10 W4A32), 0.46 (LSUN-Bedrooms W4A32), and 0.33 (ImageNet W4A32), while also improving sFID by up to 0.58 on ImageNet. Compared to Q-Diffusion (ICCV 2023), the gap is larger: 0.80 FID on CIFAR-10 W4A32 and 1.71 on LSUN-Bedrooms W4A32. PTQD (NeurIPS 2023) and PTQ4DM (CVPR 2023) trail further behind, with PTQ4DM showing particularly severe degradation under W4A8 (FID 20.72 on LSUN-Bedrooms). The only metric where the proposed method does not lead is sFID on ImageNet W4A8, where TFMQ-DM edges ahead (7.35 vs. 7.55), though the FID advantage (9.96 vs. 10.29) still holds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the PTQ pipeline established by Q-Diffusion (calibration data generation via fixed-interval sampling) and TFMQ-DM (EMA-based activation quantization and temporal feature preservation), while departing from their core assumption that all calibration samples contribute equally. It reframes the calibration problem through the lens of gradient conflict—previously identified in diffusion model *training* (Hang et al., 2023; Go et al., 2023) but not in the quantization context—and introduces a meta-learning bi-level optimization to learn sample-wise weights that align gradient directions across timesteps. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 4.1) connects the practical Algorithm 2 to the original bi-level objective via a surrogate gradient-matching loss, bridging meta-learning theory and quantization practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of gradient conflict as a distinct failure mode in PTQ of diffusion models provides a principled explanation for why uniform calibration underperforms, and the gradient-alignment weighting offers a drop-in improvement (≈1 extra GPU hour) that practitioners can adopt without changing the underlying quantization scheme. However, the evaluation is limited to DDPM and LDM-4 on three datasets, and the method inherits the block-wise AdaRound+BRECQ pipeline, so its interaction with more aggressive or mixed-precision quantization strategies remains unexplored. Open questions include whether the meta-learning overhead scales to very large timestep counts (e.g., 1000+ steps in production models) and whether the gradient-alignment objective generalizes to other generative architectures such as flow matching or autoregressive diffusion.
