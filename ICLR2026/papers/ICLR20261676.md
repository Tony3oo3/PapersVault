## Problem

Model fusion for decision-making remains underexplored despite its potential to combine pre-trained experts into stronger agents without retraining. Two natural subproblems—direct policy fusion and dynamics model fusion—appear structurally different, and the latter suffers from exponential computational blow-up when fusing ensembles of dynamics models (the standard approach for uncertainty estimation). The paper asks whether a single principled framework can unify both subproblems and make dynamics fusion tractable at single-model cost.

## Input / Output

For policy fusion, the method consumes per-expert logits over a shared finite vocabulary (enforced by a common tokenizer) and emits a fused next-token distribution at each decoding step, from which a token is sampled or argmax'd; EMSelect additionally outputs a selected expert index per step. For dynamics fusion, the method takes a current state and action (plus up to k recent state-action pairs for the ADETM history window) and produces a fused next-state prediction via Langevin sampling or MAP on the combined energy, along with a temporal-consistency uncertainty score computed as the variance across stacked-history predictions. All fusion is training-free at inference time: no gradient updates to the constituent experts are required.

## Method

The core mechanism is energy additivity: both softmax policy outputs (where log-probabilities are negative Boltzmann energies) and energy-based transition model likelihoods are written as p(y|x) ∝ exp(−E(x,y)), so fusing n experts reduces to E_fuse = Σ λᵢ Eᵢ, yielding a logarithmic opinion pool (geometric mixture) that is the unique minimizer of the weighted reverse-KL projection. For dynamics, the paper introduces ADETM, which wraps an ETM energy network with an MLP state encoder and a multi-head-attention action-sequence encoder over a fixed history window; uncertainty is estimated by stacking k valid history slices from a FIFO queue and measuring prediction dispersion, replacing N-model ensembles with a single model per dataset and reducing fusion cost from O(M×N) to O(M). EMSelect complements EMFuse by running a sequential tournament where, at each decoding step, the incumbent expert is compared against the next via pairwise KL to the fused reference, committing to the expert whose policy is locally closest to the consensus. The entire pipeline—energy computation, fusion, uncertainty scoring, and expert selection—operates in log-space or energy-space without any retraining of the constituent models.

## Results

On OpenCompass (Family Q), EMFuse achieves 63.49% average accuracy on the subject-mix (finance/mathematics/medication) and 89.21% on the finance-suite, outperforming all training-free baselines. EMSelect further adds +1.31 and +1.18 points respectively over EMFuse. On D4RL MuJoCo medium (offline RL with ADETM), EMFuse achieves an average IQM normalized return of 50.1, exceeding the Mixed-data oracle (47.8) and both parameter-space baselines (Soup 42.7, RegMean 43.8) by 2.3–7.4 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMFuse is compared against three training-free baselines: Model Soup (parameter-space averaging), RegMean (regularized weight merging), and PackLLM (logit-space perplexity-based fusion). On the OpenCompass subject-mix, EMFuse (63.49%) beats Soup (60.88%) by +2.61 points and RegMean (60.31%) by +3.18 points, while narrowly exceeding PackLLM (63.15%) by +0.34 points. On the finance-suite, the margins widen substantially: EMFuse (89.21%) surpasses PackLLM (88.27%) by +0.94, Soup (83.51%) by +5.70, and RegMean (82.58%) by +6.63. In the D4RL offline-RL setting, EMFuse with ADETM (50.1 avg IQM) outperforms Soup (42.7) by +7.4 and RegMean (43.8) by +6.3, and even edges the Mixed-data oracle (47.8) by +2.3, demonstrating that energy-additive dynamics fusion with single-model uncertainty estimation is competitive with or superior to data-matched baselines. EMSelect, the KL-guided per-step selection built on top of EMFuse, adds a further +1.18–1.31 points on OpenCompass, with gains concentrated in finance (FPB +2.07, LendingClub +1.42) and medication (MedQAM +2.68) but small regressions on some math sets (MGSMZ −2.40).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate strands of model fusion—direct policy fusion (logit-space methods like PackLLM) and dynamics model fusion (ensemble-based world models in offline RL)—under a single energy-additive framework rooted in the classical Product-of-Experts / Logarithmic Opinion Pool literature (Hinton 2002; Heskes 1998). For dynamics, it builds directly on Energy-based Transition Models (Chen et al., ICML 2024) and the any-step context mechanism from ADMPO (Lin et al., ICLR 2025), wrapping them in an ADETM architecture that replaces per-environment ensembles with a single model plus stacked-history dispersion. The conceptual departure from prior work is treating energy as a 'common currency' so that the same fusion rule E_fuse = Σ λᵢ Eᵢ applies identically whether the experts are LLM softmax policies or transition-energy networks, rather than designing separate fusion procedures for each setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, training-free recipe for combining domain-specialized decision-making models without retraining, and ADETM concretely solves the exponential parameter/FLOP blow-up that makes cross-ensemble dynamics fusion intractable (5× fewer parameters, ~3.6× fewer FLOPs vs. N=5 ensembles). The consensus-vs-commitment trade-off between EMFuse (LogOP) and EMSelect (KL-guided tournament) is a useful design axis, though EMSelect's gains are uneven (notably negative on some math tasks). Key open questions include extending beyond shared-vocabulary settings (heterogeneous tokenizers), whether the energy framework generalizes to non-Boltzmann output heads, and whether the modest empirical margins (0.3–6.6% on discrete, 2.3–7.4 points on continuous) persist at larger model scales or with more than three experts.
