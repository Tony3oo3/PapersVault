## Problem

Organismal biology datasets contain millions of images annotated only with species names, lacking the instance-level descriptive captions that enable rich multimodal alignment in other scientific domains like radiology. The core challenge is generating faithful, image-specific captions at scale without hallucination, since biological species differentiation depends on subtle morphological details that MLLMs tend to misdescribe when conditioned solely on noisy images.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 224×224 RGB biological images and text inputs (either taxonomic species names or descriptive captions) through a shared ViT-B/16 visual encoder and text encoder. A key architectural detail is the dual-projector design: the visual encoder output is routed through two separate projectors, one matched to taxonomy labels and one to captions, so each text view aligns with its own visual projection. For all downstream evaluation (classification, retrieval), embeddings are extracted from the taxonomy projector, while the caption projector provides additional training-time supervision.

## Method

B10CAP extends B10CLIP by adding synthetic descriptive captions as a second text supervision signal alongside species names, trained with InfoNCE contrastive loss. The synthetic captions are generated by InternVL3 38B (accelerated via vLLM on 12 H100 GPUs) conditioned on two domain-specific contexts: (1) Wikipedia-derived visual information extracted via a two-stage Qwen3 pipeline (8B verification + 32B extraction) yielding 132K trait-focused descriptions covering 29.5% of taxa, and (2) taxon-tailored format examples curated via Gemini Deep Research for 347 taxonomic classes (896 examples total) that guide the MLLM to emphasize species-relevant morphological traits. The dual-projector architecture decouples the two heterogeneous supervision signals, preventing caption noise from corrupting the taxonomy-aligned embedding space. The theoretical framing treats images and captions as two noisy projections of a shared latent trait vector in morphospace, so contrastive alignment with faithful captions encourages the model to recover diagnostic characters while suppressing spurious environmental factors like pose and lighting.

## Results

On zero-shot species classification across 10 benchmarks, BIOCAP achieves a mean top-1 accuracy of 46.4%, outperforming B10CLIP (37.6%) by 8.8% and the original CLIP (19.4%) by 27.0%. The largest gains appear on Fungi (+23.5% over B10CLIP) and Rare Species (+7.1%). On natural-language retrieval tasks (INQUIRE-Rerank AP@50, Cornell Bird and PlantID Recall@10), BIOCAP achieves a mean of 53.5%, surpassing B10CLIP (31.6%) by 21.9% and SigLIP (41.1%) by 12.4%, with particularly strong results on PlantID (82.6% I2T, 83.3% T2I vs. 48.4%/45.5% for B10CLIP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BIOCAP is compared against five baselines: CLIP (ViT-B/16), SigLIP, FG-CLIP, BioTrove-CLIP, and B10CLIP. On classification, BIOCAP wins on all 10 benchmarks, with the most dramatic margin over B10CLIP on Fungi (64.4% vs. 40.9%, +23.5%) and Medicinal Leaf (51.4% vs. 38.5%, +12.9%). Against the general-purpose CLIP, BIOCAP leads by 27.0% on average, while SigLIP (32.3%) and FG-CLIP (20.7%) remain far behind on biological tasks. On retrieval, BIOCAP dominates: it achieves 53.5% mean vs. B10CLIP's 31.6% (+21.9%), SigLIP's 41.1% (+12.4%), and FG-CLIP's 36.2% (+17.3%). The only exceptions are the Behavior and Context sub-tasks of INQUIRE-Rerank, where SigLIP edges out BIOCAP (37.2/41.4 vs. 33.6/37.0), suggesting captions primarily strengthen appearance- and species-level semantics rather than behavioral context. Overall, the consistent wins across both classification and retrieval confirm that trait-grounded synthetic captions provide complementary supervision beyond taxonomic labels alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends B10CLIP (Gu et al., NeurIPS 2025), a biological foundation model trained solely on species labels, by introducing synthetic descriptive captions as a complementary contrastive supervision signal. It draws on the broader synthetic-caption-for-CLIP literature (BLIP, VeCLIP, FG-CLIP, ALIP, LaCLIP) but departs from those general-domain efforts by incorporating two domain-specific context sources—Wikipedia-derived visual information and taxon-tailored format examples—to mitigate hallucination in fine-grained biological imagery. The morphospace/latent-trait-vector framing provides a causal-generation justification for why aligning images with faithful captions should recover diagnostic characters, a theoretical angle absent from prior synthetic-caption work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete gap in organismal biology, where instance-level textual descriptions are largely absent unlike in biomedical imaging, and demonstrates that domain-grounded synthetic captions yield substantial gains (8.8% on classification, 21.3% on retrieval over B10CLIP). The released TreeOfLife-10M-Captions dataset and the scalable generation pipeline (10M images in ~30 hours on 12 H100s) make the approach immediately reusable. Open questions include whether the Wikipedia-coverage limitation (29.5% of taxa) can be closed with alternative knowledge sources, whether the dual-projector design is necessary or an artifact of the specific supervision heterogeneity, and whether the same recipe transfers to other label-only scientific domains (astronomy, geology, materials) that the authors explicitly name as motivated but do not evaluate.
