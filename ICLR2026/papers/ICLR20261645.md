## Problem

INR-based surrogates for ensemble simulations face a fundamental fidelity-speed dilemma: deep MLP-based models like FA-INR achieve high reconstruction quality but incur prohibitive inference latency (2569 TFLOPs/10⁹ pts), while fast embedding-based models like Explorable-INR and K-Planes lack the expressiveness to capture complex non-linear interactions in high-dimensional ensemble data. No existing architecture simultaneously delivers state-of-the-art fidelity and embedding-level inference speed for multi-parameter scientific simulation surrogates.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3D spatial coordinate x (on Cartesian grids at 128³–256³ resolution or unstructured Voronoi meshes with ~11.8M vertices) and a d_c-dimensional condition parameter vector c (3–6 parameters depending on the simulation) as input, and outputs a scalar field value v. The learned function f_θ(x, c) is continuous over both domains, enabling zero-shot queries at arbitrary coordinates and unseen parameter combinations. For the spatio-conditional generalization task, models are trained on ×2 downsampled fields and must reconstruct full-resolution outputs, probing the continuity of the learned representation.

## Method

DRR-Net implements the Decoupled Representation Refinement paradigm by decoupling expensive deep-network computation from the inference path. During training, multi-resolution feature grids are first enriched via non-parametric transformations (structural super-resolution to a common resolution, followed by positional-encoding feature upsampling), then passed through a deep GLU-based refiner network that produces a residual offset added back to the preprocessed embeddings. The resulting refined grid is cached, so inference reduces to a single trilinear interpolation plus a lightweight 3-layer decoder MLP, matching the speed of pure embedding-based models. The condition encoder uses a hierarchical 'local-then-global' unification: per-parameter multi-resolution 1D lines are first unified locally, then concatenated and refined globally to capture cross-parameter interactions, before being split back into per-parameter lines for interpolation. A complementary data augmentation strategy, Variational Pairs (VP), generates physically-plausible training samples by perturbing coordinates and/or conditions with truncated Gaussian noise and approximating the new value via spatial (and optionally conditional) interpolation of neighboring ground-truth fields, replacing the piecewise-constant assumption of prior Variational Coordinate methods.

## Results

On the conditional generalization task, DRR-Net achieves state-of-the-art fidelity on Nyx (PSNR 44.69, Rel L2 3.18e-02, SSIM 0.986) and Cloverleaf3D (PSNR 48.69, Rel L2 9.81e-02, SSIM 0.994) while being up to 27× faster at inference than the high-fidelity FA-INR baseline (10.7s vs 287.2s on Nyx). On MPAS-Ocean, FA-INR retains a 2.9 dB PSNR advantage (52.13 vs 49.26) due to the unstructured mesh geometry, but DRR-Net still outperforms the comparable grid-based Explorable-INR by 3.2 dB while matching its inference speed (4.8s vs 5.3s). DRR-Net is also the most parameter-efficient model, using only 0.9M parameters on Cloverleaf3D versus 4.5M for Explorable-INR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the high-fidelity MLP-based FA-INR, DRR-Net wins on Nyx (PSNR 44.69 vs 42.79) and Cloverleaf3D (PSNR 48.69 vs 47.60) while being 27× and 10× faster at inference, respectively; it loses on MPAS-Ocean (PSNR 49.26 vs 52.13) due to the unstructured spherical mesh geometry that disadvantages grid-based encoders, yet remains 19× faster. Against the fast embedding-based Explorable-INR, DRR-Net consistently wins on fidelity across all three datasets (by 3.3 dB on Nyx, 3.2 dB on MPAS-Ocean, and 3.5 dB on Cloverleaf3D) while maintaining comparable inference latency (within ~1 second). Against K-Planes, DRR-Net dominates on every metric across all datasets, with PSNR gains of 15.8 dB (Nyx), 33.8 dB (MPAS-Ocean), and 18.7 dB (Cloverleaf3D). Overall, DRR-Net uniquely occupies the top-left corner of the fidelity–speed Pareto frontier on two of three datasets and remains competitive on the third.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two competing INR design philosophies—fast embedding-based structures (Plenoxels, Instant NGP, K-Planes, Explorable-INR) and expressive deep MLPs (FA-INR, NeRF variants)—into a new architectural paradigm called Decoupled Representation Refinement (DRR). Rather than building on a single prior method, DRR repurposes the idea of offline knowledge baking (analogous in spirit to distillation) to let a deep refiner network enrich a compact embedding grid in a one-time precomputation step, so that inference reduces to cheap interpolation plus a lightweight decoder. The data augmentation contribution, Variational Pairs (VP), extends and corrects the Variational Coordinates (VC) strategy of Zhang et al. (2024) by replacing its implicit piecewise-constant value assumption with a physically plausible interpolation-based value estimate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DRR paradigm offers a general, drop-in strategy for resolving the fidelity-speed dilemma in INR-based surrogates, with the 27× inference speedup over FA-INR at SOTA quality making interactive scientific data exploration (volume rendering, parameter sweeps) practically feasible for the first time. The paradigm is structure-agnostic in principle, opening avenues to apply it to hash grids, adaptive grids, or 3D Gaussian Splatting, and to explore richer refiner architectures (CNNs, Transformers) that could unlock further gains. Key open questions remain: current methods are limited to Cartesian grids and interpolation within the training parameter hull, and the tight coupling between base embeddings and the refiner (disabling the refiner yields unintelligible output) raises questions about robustness and interpretability of the learned representation.
