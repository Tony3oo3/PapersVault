## Problem

Existing flow-matching-based solvers for linear inverse problems either approximate posterior scores via gradient corrections or alternate generative updates with data-consistency steps, but lack a unified theoretical justification connecting these approaches to Bayesian posterior sampling. Flower addresses this gap by providing an ancestral-sampling interpretation that unifies plug-and-play (PnP) methods and generative inverse solvers under a single Bayesian framework, while achieving state-of-the-art reconstruction quality with nearly identical hyperparameters across diverse inverse problems.

## Input / Output

Given degraded measurements y = Hx + n (where H is a known linear forward operator and n is Gaussian noise with known variance σ_n²), a pretrained U-Net velocity network trained via flow matching, and a number of iterations N, Flower produces a reconstructed image x₁. The method operates on 128×128 (CelebA) and 256×256 (AFHQ-Cat) RGB images normalized to [-1,1]. Beyond N and σ_n, the only tunable hyperparameter is γ ∈ {0,1}, which controls whether uncertainty in the refinement step is sampled; in practice γ=0 (deterministic refinement) yields better PSNR. The proximal step is solved via conjugate gradients (max 50 iterations, tolerance 10⁻⁵) rather than explicit matrix inversion, keeping memory usage comparable to PnP-Flow (~0.22 GB).

## Method

Flower iterates N times over three steps: (1) a flow-consistent destination estimation x̂₁(x_t) = x_t + (1−t)v_t^θ(x_t) that uses the pretrained velocity network to predict a denoised target, interpretable as E[X₁|X_t=x_t]; (2) a measurement-aware refinement that computes the proximal operator of the data-fidelity term F_y(x) = ‖Hx−y‖²/(2σ_n²) at x̂₁, yielding a refined destination μ_t, with optional Gaussian uncertainty sampling κ_t ~ N(0, Σ_t) controlled by γ; and (3) a time-progression step that re-projects the refined destination along the flow trajectory by interpolating with fresh source noise: x_{t+Δt} = (1−t−Δt)ε + (t+Δt)x̃₁(x_t,y). The key theoretical contribution is showing that these three steps collectively draw from the transition distribution p_{X_{t+Δt}|X_t=x_t,Y=y}, so by induction the final output is an approximate posterior sample from p_{X₁|Y=y}. This Bayesian construction reveals that the proximal refinement in Step 2 is equivalent to sampling from a Gaussian approximation of the conditional posterior, and it distinguishes Flower from PnP-Flow by replacing a gradient update with a proximal operation, which empirically improves reconstruction quality.

## Results

On CelebA (100 test images), Flower1-OT achieves the best PSNR in deblurring (34.98 dB), super-resolution (32.36 dB), and box inpainting (31.19 dB), and near-best in denoising (32.28 dB) and random inpainting (33.08 dB). With five-evaluation averaging (Flower5-OT), it leads in deblurring (35.67 dB), super-resolution (33.09 dB), and box inpainting (31.87 dB). On AFHQ-Cat, Flower1-OT wins in deblurring (28.64 dB) and box inpainting (26.19 dB), while PnP-GS remains strongest in denoising (32.58 dB vs. 31.69 dB). A key practical advantage is that Flower uses nearly identical hyperparameters (only N and γ) across all five inverse problems, unlike competing methods that require per-task tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest flow-based competitor PnP-Flow (Martin et al., 2025), Flower1-OT wins in deblurring (+0.50 dB), super-resolution (+1.27 dB), and box inpainting (+0.72 dB) on CelebA, while PnP-Flow1 edges ahead in denoising (31.80 vs. 32.28 is actually Flower winning; PnP-GS at 32.64 is the denoising leader). Flower decisively outperforms OT-ODE (Pokle et al., 2024) across all five tasks, with the largest gap in box inpainting (+1.79 dB) and denoising (+1.74 dB). D-Flow (Ben-Hamu et al., 2024) and Flow-Priors (Zhang et al., 2024) are substantially weaker in deblurring and super-resolution (gaps of 2–4 dB) but D-Flow surprisingly leads in random inpainting on CelebA (33.67 vs. 33.08 dB). PnP-GS (Hurault et al., 2022) remains the strongest denoising baseline (32.64 dB) but does not support inpainting tasks. DiffPIR (Zhu et al., 2023) is consistently outperformed by Flower in all overlapping tasks, with the largest gap in deblurring (34.98 vs. 32.77 dB). Overall, Flower achieves state-of-the-art or near-best results across all five linear inverse problems with a single set of hyperparameters, while also matching the runtime of PnP-Flow (~5.6 s vs. 3.0 s per image) and using minimal memory (0.217 GB).

## Relation to Prior Work

Flower sits at the intersection of flow-matching generative models and plug-and-play (PnP) inverse solvers. It adopts the same ΠGDM Gaussian approximation for p_{X_1|X_t} used by OT-ODE and ΠGDM, but departs from their score/gradient-correction strategy by instead performing ancestral sampling with a proximal data-consistency step, closely mirroring the structure of PnP-Flow (Martin et al., 2025) while replacing its gradient update with a proximal operator. The key conceptual departure is the Bayesian justification: Flower is the first flow-based inverse solver to frame its iterative procedure as approximate posterior sampling via ancestral sampling, thereby formally linking PnP-style denoise-then-project iterations to the generation of samples from p_{X_1|Y=y}.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Flower provides a principled Bayesian interpretation for PnP-style flow solvers, which previously lacked such a justification, and achieves state-of-the-art results among flow-based methods with nearly identical hyperparameters across diverse linear inverse problems—a practical advantage over methods requiring per-task tuning. The finding that γ=0 (ignoring posterior uncertainty in the refinement step) outperforms the theoretically required γ=1 is a useful practical insight, though it means the method does not produce exact posterior samples. Open questions include whether the Bayesian framework extends cleanly to nonlinear forward operators (discussed but not empirically validated), whether the γ=0 preference persists at scale, and whether the ancestral-sampling interpretation can be tightened to reduce the gap between theory and practice.
