## Problem

Existing multimodal DPO methods construct only a single negative image per comparison via basic perturbations or similarity retrieval, reducing supervision to a one-dimensional deviation from the positive and failing to capture the multi-faceted nature of visual errors (object identity, color, spatial layout, contextual mismatch). This narrow optimization axis encourages spurious shortcuts and persistent hallucinations, while even recent listwise extensions suffer from homologous negatives with substantial semantic overlap.

## Input / Output

The method operates on vision-language models (LLaVA-1.5-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B/3B) that take an RGB image paired with a text instruction as input and produce a grounded text response. During training, positive image-prompt pairs come from the RLHF-V dataset (~5K samples with segment-level human feedback), while three negative images per instance are selected from the COCO training set via the SAE-guided importance sampling procedure. The model is fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 64, α=128) on 2 A100 GPUs with an effective batch size of 32.

## Method

MISP-DPO introduces two core components. First, a sparse autoencoder is trained on CLIP-space difference vectors between positive and candidate negative images to decompose semantic deviations into disentangled latent factors; negatives are then scored by reconstruction error (informativeness), semantic deviation from the positive, and mutual diversity, with a greedy diversity-promoting selection algorithm picking the top-K candidates. Second, the standard pairwise DPO loss is replaced with a Plackett-Luce multi-negative ranking objective that forces the model to assign the positive image higher preference than all selected negatives simultaneously. An importance sampling scheme using SAE-derived scores provides an unbiased gradient estimator with O(1/K) mean-square error, enabling efficient training with only three negatives per instance. A complementary text-based DPO loss (image-grounded negative responses) is combined with the image-based loss via a balance parameter λ.

## Results

MISP-DPO achieves a 30.09% average improvement over the LLaVA-1.5-7B base model, reducing MMHalBench hallucination rate to 32.29% (vs. 51.04% base) and raising HallusionBench all-answer accuracy to 57.52%. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, it attains the best HallusionBench aA of 71.24, POPE accuracy of 88.66, and MMVP accuracy of 79.00. On Qwen2.5-VL-3B, MISP-DPO yields a 19.89% average improvement, with WildVision reward of +8.6 and win rate of 52.4, the highest among all methods tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MISP-DPO consistently outperforms all three baselines across every backbone and benchmark. Against standard DPO (text-only preference), MISP-DPO achieves a 30.09% vs. 21.13% average improvement on LLaVA-1.5-7B and 19.89% vs. 13.12% on Qwen2.5-VL-3B, with the gap driven primarily by hallucination reduction (HalRate 32.29% vs. 37.50% on LLaVA; 13.54% vs. 18.75% on Qwen-3B). Against mDPO (single crop-generated negative), MISP-DPO is substantially stronger: mDPO yields only +0.22% average improvement on LLaVA and even degrades Qwen2.5-VL-7B (−1.16%), while MISP-DPO improves all three backbones. Against CHiP (hierarchical textual supervision + visual contrastive loss), MISP-DPO wins on every metric for LLaVA (e.g., HallusionBench aA 57.52 vs. 51.95) and Qwen-3B (MMVP 74.25 vs. 70.33), though CHiP edges out MISP-DPO on POPE for Qwen2.5-VL-7B (88.18 vs. 88.66 is actually MISP-DPO winning; CHiP's 88.18 is lower). A random multi-negative ablation confirms that the gains come from SAE-guided semantic selection rather than merely increasing the number of negatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) multimodal DPO methods (mDPO, CHiP, S-VCO, Re-Align) that align VLMs via image-text preference pairs but rely on a single negative per comparison, and (2) multi-negative preference optimization in text (Softmax-DPO, DMPO) that uses Plackett-Luce ranking over multiple negatives. The key departure is bringing the multi-negative Plackett-Luce formulation into the multimodal setting, where negatives must capture cross-modal semantic shifts rather than textual paraphrases. The SAE-guided importance sampling for negative selection is novel in this context, repurposing sparse autoencoder interpretability tools (previously used in representation learning and attribute recognition) to structure the visual negative space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored limitation—single-negative supervision collapses visual error space to one axis—by providing a principled, interpretable mechanism for selecting diverse negatives. The theoretical guarantees (unbiasedness, O(1/K) MSE bound) for the importance-sampling gradient estimator add rigor that prior heuristic negative-construction methods lack. Limitations include reliance on GPT-based evaluation (acknowledged by authors), a fixed COCO negative pool that may not generalize to open-domain or video settings, and the finding that only 3 negatives are beneficial, suggesting the approach may not scale to larger candidate sets without further refinement. Open questions include whether SAE decomposition in CLIP space captures all relevant failure modes and whether the framework extends to video or multi-image inputs.
