## Problem

Semivalue-based data valuation assigns each data point a score reflecting its contribution to a downstream task, but these scores depend on the practitioner's choice of utility. In practice, the utility is either a tunable trade-off between competing criteria (e.g., helpfulness vs. harmlessness in LLM fine-tuning) or must be selected among several equally valid metrics (e.g., accuracy vs. F1 for a classifier), and no principled way existed to quantify how much the resulting data-point rankings would shift under such utility changes. This paper addresses that gap by asking: how robust are semivalue-based data valuation results to the utility choice?

## Input / Output

The method takes a training dataset D of n points, a semivalue weight vector ω (Shapley, (α,β)-Beta Shapley, or Banzhaf), and a two-dimensional family of utilities u_α = α₁u₁ + α₂u₂ (either a convex combination of two fixed criteria or the linear-fractional span of two test-set statistics λ and γ). It produces per-point data values φ(z_i; ω, u_α), a 2D spatial signature S_{ω,D} ⊂ R² (one embedding per data point), and a scalar robustness metric R_p ∈ [0,1] for any chosen number of pairwise swaps p. Once semivalue scores are computed via Monte Carlo approximation, R_p is obtained in O(n² log n) time with negligible additional cost relative to the model-training overhead.

## Method

The core contribution is a unified geometric representation: given a semivalue ω, each data point z_i is embedded into R² as ψ_{ω,D}(z_i) = (φ(z_i; ω, u₁), φ(z_i; ω, u₂)), so that any utility in the family becomes a linear functional and ranking data points reduces to sorting projections onto the utility direction α̅ ∈ S¹. Building on this, the paper defines a robustness metric R_p as the normalized average minimal geodesic distance on the unit circle one must rotate from a utility direction before p pairwise swaps occur in the induced ranking, with a closed-form O(n² log n) computation. A key theoretical result (Proposition 3.3) shows that R_p increases with the collinearity of the spatial signature, which in turn depends on how the semivalue weights ω_j concentrate on coalition sizes j where the cross-utility alignment factor r_j is large. This explains the empirical finding that Banzhaf—whose weights peak at intermediate coalition sizes—consistently yields near-collinear embeddings and the highest R_p across all datasets and scenarios tested.

## Results

The proposed robustness metric R_p is evaluated across 8 binary classification datasets and 3 semivalues (Shapley, (4,1)-Beta Shapley, Banzhaf) for p ∈ {500, 1000, 1500}. Banzhaf consistently achieves the highest R_p in the majority of settings: on TITANIC, Banzhaf R_500 = 0.44 versus 0.058 for both Shapley and (4,1)-Beta Shapley; on CREDIT, Banzhaf R_500 = 0.82 versus 0.084 for Shapley. On BREAST, where all three semivalues yield similar collinearity, the gap narrows (R_500: 0.22, 0.23, 0.34). The R_p values closely track the Kendall rank correlations from Table 1: low-correlation contexts (TITANIC, CREDIT under Shapley) show low R_p, while high-correlation contexts (WIND, BREAST) show high R_p.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three semivalue methods—Data Shapley (Ghorbani & Zou, 2019), (4,1)-Beta Shapley (Kwon & Zou, 2022), and Data Banzhaf (Wang & Jia, 2023)—using the proposed R_p robustness metric as the evaluation criterion. Banzhaf wins on 7 out of 8 datasets at p=500 (all except BREAST where the gap is small), with the most dramatic margins on TITANIC (0.44 vs. 0.058) and CREDIT (0.82 vs. 0.084). Shapley and (4,1)-Beta Shapley are nearly indistinguishable in R_p across all datasets, differing by at most 0.01–0.02. The geometric explanation is that Banzhaf's weight profile (concentrated on intermediate coalition sizes) produces near-collinear spatial signatures in R², which maximizes the average angular distance to ranking swaps. The R_p metric also agrees with Kendall and Spearman rank correlations: contexts where Shapley shows negative correlation between accuracy and F1 rankings (TITANIC: −0.19, CREDIT: −0.47) correspond to the lowest R_p values, confirming that the metric captures genuine ranking instability rather than an artifact.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the semivalue-based data valuation framework (Shapley, Beta Shapley, Banzhaf) and the cooperative-game-theoretic marginal contribution formalism, but departs from the dominant focus on efficient computation by asking a different question: how stable are the induced rankings when the utility changes? It unifies two previously separate scenarios (utility trade-off and multiple-valid-utility) under a single geometric representation—the spatial signature—where each data point is embedded in R² and any utility becomes a linear functional. This geometric lens connects to prior robustness analyses by Wang & Jia (2023) on semivalue fluctuation under algorithmic randomness and by Diehl & Wilson (2025) on the gameability of underspecified utilities, but provides a general, closed-form robustness metric (R_p) rather than case-specific observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper gives practitioners a concrete, computable diagnostic (R_p) to determine whether their data valuation results are trustworthy before committing to costly re-training pipelines or top-k selections, directly addressing a practical gap where arbitrary utility choices can produce contradictory rankings. The consistent finding that Banzhaf achieves the highest robustness—explained by near-collinearity of its spatial signature—offers actionable guidance for method selection. Limitations include the restriction to linear-fractional binary classification metrics and small-scale experiments (100 training points, simple models), leaving open whether the geometric insights and Banzhaf's advantage persist at scale with deep models and non-linear-fractional utilities. The paper also raises the broader question of whether semivalue-based data valuation is a reliable heuristic at all in contexts where robustness is low.
