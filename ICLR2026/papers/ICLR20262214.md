## Problem

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) for LLM agents depends heavily on large collections of hand-crafted task queries paired with ground-truth answers, creating a severe data bottleneck that limits training scale. Existing query-synthesis pipelines are offline and cannot dynamically adjust task difficulty to provide effective RL advantages, while self-play training—proven effective for reasoning and alignment—has not been explored for agentic scenarios where the agent interacts with external tools.

## Input / Output

The method takes a ground-truth answer (not a question) sampled from a pre-defined set of 50,000 answers drawn from public training data (NQ, HotpotQA, ARPO), and the LLM simultaneously generates a multi-hop search question and solves it. The proposer and solver each interact with a local E5 retriever over the Wiki-2018 corpus (top-3 documents per query, max 10 search rounds per trajectory). The final output is an updated LLM policy that serves as a stronger deep search agent, evaluated via LLM-as-a-judge pass@1 accuracy on seven QA benchmarks.

## Method

Search Self-Play (SSP) frames agent training as a zero-sum adversarial game where a single LLM policy plays two roles: a proposer that generates increasingly difficult search questions from a given ground-truth answer via multi-turn search tool calls, and a solver that answers the generated question through ordinary deep search. A critical anti-hacking mechanism is RAG verification: all search results collected during the proposer's trajectory are fed to the solver as context (with 4 randomly injected noisy documents from other trajectories), and the question is only accepted if the solver can correctly answer without further searching—ensuring the question is both valid and solvable. The solver is optimized with GRPO (n=5 rollouts per question) to maximize answer accuracy, while the proposer is updated with REINFORCE (n=1) using a reward of 1 minus the solver's average success rate, incentivizing harder questions. The cooperative RAG-verification constraint is enforced via rejection sampling rather than joint optimization, filtering out invalid questions before the adversarial update step. A replay buffer with periodic reset (cleared every 10 steps) balances data reuse against overfitting, and the entire pipeline requires no human-annotated trajectories or task queries.

## Results

SSP delivers substantial gains across all training paradigms. From scratch, Qwen2.5-7B-Base improves from 22.3 to 48.7 average pass@1 accuracy (+26.4), with a remarkable +40.4 on TriviaQA. On continual training of search-specialized agents, SSP pushes Search-R1-7B from 53.9 to 55.7, ZeroSearch-7B from 43.6 to 45.9, and R-Search-7B from 52.8 to 54.6 average. At scale, Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct+SSP reaches 58.5 average, achieving state-of-the-art on 5 of 7 benchmarks. All results are reported as pass@1 accuracy using LLM-as-a-judge (Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SSP against three search-specialized agent baselines under continual training: Search-R1-7B (53.9 avg), R-Search-7B (52.8 avg), and ZeroSearch-7B (43.6 avg). SSP consistently outperforms all three, with the strongest baseline Search-R1-7B improving from 53.9 to 55.7 (+1.8), R-Search-7B from 52.8 to 54.6 (+1.8), and ZeroSearch-7B from 43.6 to 45.9 (+2.3). The gains are smaller for already-trained agents (1.8–2.3 points) compared to from-scratch training (8.0–26.4 points), reflecting diminishing returns as the base model already possesses strong search capabilities. Notably, SSP also generalizes across model families, improving LLaMA-3.1-8B by +9.6 and Qwen3-8B by +3.8, confirming architecture-agnostic effectiveness. At the 32B scale, Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct+SSP (58.5 avg) surpasses all 7B baselines, achieving SOTA on 5 of 7 benchmarks including NQ (62.6), TriviaQA (82.8), HotpotQA (62.8), MuSiQue (32.0), and Bamboogle (69.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) agentic RLVR methods (Search-R1, ZeroSearch, R-Search) that train search agents with verifiable answer rewards but require large curated question sets, and (2) self-play training for LLMs (Cheng et al. 2024a, Chen et al. 2024, Absolute Zero) that eliminates human supervision but has been confined to internal-knowledge tasks like math and code. The key departure is equipping the proposer with external search tools and a RAG-based verification pipeline, which breaks the internal-knowledge bottleneck of prior self-play and makes the approach applicable to open-domain agentic scenarios where answers must be grounded in retrieved evidence rather than parametric memory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SSP offers a scalable pathway to self-supervised agentic training that removes the need for human-annotated question-answer pairs, directly addressing the data-scarcity bottleneck that limits RLVR in agentic settings. The co-evolutionary dynamic creates an adaptive curriculum where task difficulty automatically tracks the solver's capability, a property that offline synthesis pipelines lack. However, the evaluation is confined to a local E5 retriever over Wiki-2018 with a 10-step search cap, and the answer set D is still drawn from public training data, so the method has not yet been validated against real open-web search or truly zero-data conditions. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to other tool sets (GUI, code) and whether the proposer-solver co-evolution can sustain improvements over very long training horizons without the answer set becoming a limiting factor.
