## Problem

Compressor-predictor agentic LM systems (e.g., Deep Research, Claude Code) are widely deployed but designed largely through trial-and-error, with no task-agnostic method to evaluate compressor quality independently of downstream performance. This attribution gap makes it impossible to determine whether gains come from better compression or better prediction without costly, task-specific pairwise sweeps across the full compound system.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261728/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a raw context document X and a query Q as input; the compressor LM produces a query-conditioned compressed summary Z (capped at 4096 tokens), and the predictor LM generates the final answer Y. Beyond the standard compression-prediction pipeline, the paper also outputs a mutual information estimate I(X;Z|Q) computed via a Monte Carlo estimator over N sampled contexts and M compressions per context, and constructs rate-distortion curves where rate is MI per output token and distortion is 1 minus accuracy. The MI estimator requires only log probabilities from inference engines (e.g., SGLang), not full vocabulary distributions, and uses a cross-family proxy model at 7-8B scale to evaluate log probabilities for smaller 1-3B compressors to mitigate miscalibration.

## Method

The paper frames the compressor LM as a noisy channel between raw context and predictor, enabling information-theoretic analysis of the communication between the two models. The key technical contribution is a practical, unbiased Monte Carlo estimator of mutual information I(X;Z|Q) that decomposes into log-likelihood ratios computable directly via modern inference servers, upper-bounded by log(N) where N is the number of sampled contexts. Building on this, the paper defines information rate (MI per token) and distortion (1 − accuracy) to perform rate-distortion analysis, fitting exponential-decay curves analogous to the Gaussian rate-distortion function. Across five datasets and three model families (Llama-3, Qwen-2.5, Gemma-3), the analysis reveals that information rate correlates strongly with downstream accuracy and perplexity (r = −0.84, R² = 0.71), that scaling compressors from 1.5B to 7B yields 60% accuracy gains versus only 12% from scaling predictors from 70B to 405B, and that larger compressors are simultaneously more accurate and up to 4.6× more token-efficient, producing sublinear FLOPs-per-generation scaling.

## Results

On LongHealth, scaling a QWEN-2.5 compressor from 1.5B to 7B improves accuracy by 60% (3.1× over 1–1.5B models), while scaling the predictor from 70B to 405B yields only a 12% gain. The 7B QWEN-2.5 compressor is 4.6× more token-efficient and conveys 5.4× more bits of mutual information per token than its 1.5B sibling, with only a 1.3% increase in FLOPs-per-generation. Mutual information rate (bits/token) correlates strongly with downstream accuracy (r = −0.84, R² = 0.71) and perplexity, providing a task-agnostic proxy for system performance. In a Deep Research pipeline, a QWEN-2.5-14B compressor paired with GPT-4o achieves 2.3% higher RACE scores at 28.1% of the uncompressed API cost, and local 3B compressors recover 99% of frontier-LM accuracy at 26% of API costs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external summarization or compression methods; instead, it compares compressor-predictor configurations against each other and against an uncompressed GPT-4o-only baseline. The central finding is that compressor scaling dominates predictor scaling: on LongHealth, going from a 1.5B to a 7B QWEN-2.5 compressor yields a 60% accuracy gain, whereas upgrading the predictor from 70B to 405B Llama adds only 12% (1% on FinanceBench). Against the uncompressed GPT-4o baseline, 7–8B compressors surpass it by 4 pp on LongHealth and recover 97% on FinanceBench. In the Deep Research setting, a QWEN-2.5-14B compressor + GPT-4o predictor beats the uncompressed GPT-4o baseline by 2.3% RACE at 28.1% of the cost, and even a 3B local compressor recovers 99% of frontier accuracy at 26% cost. Across model families, QWEN-2.5 compressors consistently outperform Llama-3 and Gemma-3 in both accuracy and token efficiency, establishing the hierarchy: compressor family > compressor size > predictor size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two largely separate literatures: information-theoretic analysis of neural representations (information bottleneck, MI estimation, rate-distortion theory) and the rapidly growing but design-agnostic field of multi-agent LM systems. While prior work has applied information theory to understand internal representations within a single model (Tishby et al., 2000; Shwartz-Ziv & Tishby, 2017) or to evaluate summarization quality (Darrin et al., 2024), this paper is the first to treat the inter-model communication channel in a compressor-predictor pipeline as a noisy channel amenable to MI estimation. The rate-distortion framing repurposes classical source-coding theory (Cover & Thomas, 2005) to characterize the trade-off between compression fidelity and downstream prediction error, providing a task-agnostic quality signal analogous to how perplexity predicts LM performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled, task-agnostic metric (mutual information rate) for evaluating compressor quality in agentic systems, eliminating the need for costly task-specific pairwise sweeps when swapping model components. The practical implication is significant: local compressors as small as 3B can recover 99% of frontier-LM accuracy at 26% of API costs, enabling a shift from cloud-heavy to on-device architectures. Key limitations include reliance on proxy models for MI estimation at the 1-3B scale, restriction to single-round non-reasoning communication, and the open question of whether these scaling principles extend to iterative multi-agent workflows, reasoning-augmented models, and MoE architectures where compute cost depends on activated experts rather than total parameters.
