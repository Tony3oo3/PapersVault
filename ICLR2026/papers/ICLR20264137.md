## Problem

Existing LLM pruning methods face a fundamental trade-off: one-shot heuristics (SparseGPT, Wanda) are fast but cannot natively enforce hardware-friendly n:m semi-structured sparsity and degrade at high sparsity levels, while optimization-based approaches (MaskLLM, MaskPro) enforce strict n:m patterns but demand thousands of GPU hours and hundreds of GB of memory for billion-parameter models. No prior method directly and efficiently optimizes n:m structured masks for LLMs within a single-GPU budget.

## Input / Output

The method consumes frozen pretrained LLM weights (LLaMA-2 7B/13B/70B) and 128 calibration sequences of 2048 tokens from C4. Rather than operating on full weight matrices, the hypernetwork processes individual groups of m=4 consecutive weights, outputting logits over the 6 valid 2:4 mask patterns per group. The output is a complete set of n:m structured sparse masks applied to all linear layers (Q, K, V, O, Up, Down, Gating projections), yielding a pruned model with 50% sparsity that is directly deployable on hardware with native 2:4 sparsity support (e.g., A100, H100).

## Method

HyperPrune uses a lightweight shared 4-layer feedforward hypernetwork (256/512 hidden dims, a few thousand parameters) that maps each group of m consecutive weights to logits over the |S^{n:m}| valid mask patterns, with Gumbel-Softmax relaxation enabling differentiable mask sampling. Context-awareness is achieved through learnable layer embeddings (one per layer) and seven component embeddings (Q, K, V, O, Up, Down, Gating) that condition the shared hypernetwork, balancing cross-layer generalization with component-specific specialization. Training proceeds layer-by-layer: for each layer, the hypernetwork is optimized for 200–2000 steps to minimize reconstruction loss minus a feature-outlier regularizer (preserving weights aligned with high-magnitude activations) plus a continual-pruning regularizer (penalizing hypernetwork output drift on previously pruned layers to prevent catastrophic forgetting). Theorem 1 provides an information-theoretic justification, showing that minimizing the expected reconstruction error under the n:m constraint is approximately equivalent to maximizing mutual information between dense and pruned layer outputs. A sparse prior from SparseGPT is injected via a cosine-similarity logit bias with exponentially annealed strength, and the entire pipeline runs on a single A100 (80 GB) by loading only the current layer's weights into GPU memory.

## Results

Under 2:4 semi-structured sparsity, HyperPrune achieves the best mean zero-shot accuracy across all three LLaMA-2 scales: 53.76% (7B), 59.25% (13B), and 68.57% (70B), while maintaining competitive Wikitext-2 perplexity (10.11, 7.60, and 5.13 respectively). On LLaMA-2-7B, it outperforms the strongest optimization-based baseline MaskPro (52.81% mean) and the best heuristic Pruner-Zero (52.02%). On LLaMA-2-70B, HyperPrune surpasses Pruner-Zero (67.69%) and SparseGPT (64.61%) by a notable margin, and is the only method evaluated at this scale besides heuristics, as MaskPro is infeasible due to memory constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperPrune consistently outperforms all five baselines on mean zero-shot accuracy across every model scale. Against the heuristic methods, the margins are substantial: on LLaMA-2-7B, HyperPrune (53.76%) beats SparseGPT (51.00%) by 2.76 points, Wanda (48.78%) by 4.98 points, and Magnitude (45.78%) by 7.98 points. Against the optimization-based MaskPro, HyperPrune leads by 0.95 points on 7B (53.76 vs 52.81) and 0.28 points on 13B (59.25 vs 58.97), while MaskPro is entirely infeasible at 70B scale due to memory constraints. Against Pruner-Zero, the closest heuristic competitor, HyperPrune wins by 1.74 points on 7B, 0.87 on 13B, and 0.88 on 70B. In terms of perplexity, HyperPrune achieves the best or near-best PPL at each scale (10.11 on 7B, 7.60 on 13B, 5.13 on 70B), confirming that the accuracy gains are not at the expense of language modeling quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

HyperPrune sits at the intersection of hypernetwork-based parameter generation (Ha et al., 2017) and the n:m semi-structured sparsity formulation introduced by MaskLLM (Fang et al., 2024) and refined by MaskPro (Sun et al., 2025). The key departure is architectural: rather than learning per-layer or per-group mask parameters whose memory scales with the full model, HyperPrune uses a single shared hypernetwork conditioned on lightweight layer and component embeddings, reducing memory to O(single layer size) and enabling 70B-scale pruning on one A100. The feature-outlier regularization explicitly subsumes Wanda's (Sun et al., 2024) weight-activation importance score as a special case, while the continual-pruning term borrows from continual-learning literature to prevent catastrophic forgetting during sequential layer-wise optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: HyperPrune reduces mask-training cost from ~1200–2300 GPU hours (MaskLLM) to 7–15 GPU hours while matching or exceeding accuracy, making n:m structured pruning feasible on a single 80 GB GPU for models up to 70B. The information-theoretic theorem (mutual information maximization ≈ MSE minimization under n:m constraints) provides the first principled justification for differentiable structured mask learning, though it relies on small-perturbation and Gaussian-input assumptions that may not hold uniformly across all layers. Open questions include robustness under domain shift (the method depends on calibration data), whether the shared hypernetwork's capacity-generalization trade-off limits performance at extreme sparsity levels, and how well the approach composes with quantization or low-rank adaptation for further compression.
