## Problem

Current language models perform all internal computation within a single forward pass per token, constrained to discrete vocabulary spaces optimized for communication rather than reasoning. Existing test-time scaling methods (CoT, RL-based reasoning) require curated human-annotated data or complex reinforcement learning, and their performance is bounded by the base pretrained model. This paper asks whether a model can learn to iteratively refine its predictions within a single token step through a continuous-space pondering mechanism, using only standard self-supervised pretraining.

## Input / Output

The model takes a standard text sequence mapped to token embeddings as input and produces a next-token probability distribution as output, identical in interface to any autoregressive LM. The distinguishing detail is that between the input and the final output, the model performs s additional forward passes (configurable at inference time), each consuming a 'pondering embedding'—a continuous vector computed as the probability-weighted sum of all vocabulary embeddings—added residually to the original input. This makes the effective computation per token scale linearly with s while the parameter count remains fixed.

## Method

PonderLM inserts an iterative refinement loop within each token generation step: after a standard LM forward pass yields a probability distribution P over the vocabulary, the model computes a pondering embedding T = P·V (a matrix product yielding a continuous vector in embedding space), adds it residually to the original input embeddings, and feeds the result back into the same LM for another forward pass. This process repeats for s steps, with the final distribution P^s used for cross-entropy loss. The entire mechanism is learned end-to-end via standard self-supervised pretraining on general corpora—no human annotations, RL, or architectural modifications beyond the residual feedback loop are required. Because the pondering embedding is a continuous weighted sum rather than a discrete token, the process is fully differentiable and overcomes the expressiveness limitations of fixed vocabularies. The method is architecture-agnostic (validated on GPT-2, Pythia, and LLaMA) and orthogonal to parameter scaling and CoT-based test-time scaling, introducing a third scaling axis.

## Results

On 9 downstream NLP benchmarks (5-shot), PonderPythia-2.8B achieves 61.5% average accuracy, surpassing Pythia-6.9B (60.1%) and nearly matching Pythia-12B (61.7%), while using 63% fewer parameters. PonderPythia-1B reaches 56.2% average accuracy, slightly exceeding TinyLlama-1.1B (55.9%) which was trained on 10× more tokens (3T vs. 300B). In 0-shot settings, PonderPythia-2.8B scores 60.4% vs. Pythia-6.9B's 59.1% and Pythia-12B's 60.5%. Scaling curves show PonderPythia matches the official Pythia's final loss with 59% fewer training tokens and achieves lower loss at every FLOP budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against same-size official Pythia models, PonderPythia wins on every benchmark in both 5-shot and 0-shot settings, with average gains of +3.8 to +5.8 points (5-shot) and +2.4 to +4.4 points (0-shot). PonderPythia-2.8B (61.5% avg 5-shot) surpasses Pythia-6.9B (60.1%) by 1.4 points and nearly matches Pythia-12B (61.7%) despite having less than a quarter of the parameters. PonderPythia-1B (56.2%) slightly edges out TinyLlama-1.1B (55.9%) on 5-shot accuracy while using only 300B tokens versus TinyLlama's 3T tokens (1/10 the data). Against comparable OPT and Bloom models, PonderPythia consistently leads by 2–5 points in average accuracy at each size tier. In the ablation comparison (Table 2), PonderPythia with just 3 pondering steps (Pile PPL 14.16, 5-shot avg 20.64) outperforms Looped Transformer (6 loops: PPL 15.18, 5-shot 17.44), Pause Tokens (6 pauses: PPL 16.55, 5-shot 15.88), Last Hidden State (6 steps: PPL 15.30, 5-shot 18.18), and Linear Projector (6 steps: PPL 15.29, 5-shot 17.61), confirming gains come from the specific pondering mechanism rather than merely added computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the test-time compute scaling landscape by introducing a distinct computational space: a weighted sum of token embeddings derived from the model's own predictive distribution, rather than operating in hidden-state space (Coconut, looped transformers) or discrete token space (CoT, pause tokens). It departs from the dominant CoT paradigm by requiring no human-annotated reasoning data, no reinforcement learning, and no special tokens—learning the pondering mechanism purely through standard self-supervised pretraining. The method is positioned as orthogonal to both parameter scaling and inference-time CoT scaling, proposing a potential 'third scaling axis' for language models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a simple matrix multiplication (P·V) plus residual addition yields a 2.8B model that surpasses a 6.9B baseline and rivals a 12B model, while a 1B model matches one trained on 10× more data. Key limitations include the modest scale tested (≤2.8B params, 300B tokens) and linear inference overhead per pondering step. Open questions include whether the gains persist at frontier scale, whether token-adaptive pondering can reduce compute, and how the pondering process relates to interpretability—questions the authors explicitly flag as future work.
