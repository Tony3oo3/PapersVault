## Problem

Adapting reinforcement learning fine-tuning to diffusion language models (DLMs) is hindered by intractable sequence-level likelihoods. The leading approach, diffu-GRPO, approximates token-level likelihoods via a single-step unmasking that is computationally cheap but severely biased because it discards token correlations. More principled ELBO-based sequence-level estimators suffer from a variance-cost dilemma: accurate estimates require many network evaluations (up to 128 in prior work), making them impractical for RL training loops.

## Input / Output

The method operates on masked diffusion language models (specifically LLaDA-8B-Instruct, an 8B-parameter DLM) that generate text in parallel via iterative denoising rather than left-to-right autoregression. Given a text prompt (e.g., a math word problem, a Sudoku grid, or a coding task), the model produces a structured completion containing a <reasoning> block followed by an <answer> block. During RL training, G completions are sampled per prompt, scored by task-specific reward functions (correctness, format compliance, unit-test pass rate), and used to compute group-relative advantages at the sequence level.

## Method

GDPO introduces a Semi-deterministic Monte Carlo (SDMC) estimator for the sequence-level ELBO that replaces the outer random-time integral with a fixed Gaussian quadrature (N=2 or 3 points) while retaining a single Monte Carlo sample for the inner random-masking integral, reducing total network evaluations per likelihood to just N. The key insight from variance decomposition is that random time selection dominates ELBO variance, so fixing time points via quadrature eliminates the dominant variance source while the inner masking variance is small and stable across timesteps. The GDPO loss then uses sequence-level importance ratios r_g = exp(ELBO_new)/exp(ELBO_old) and unnormalized group-relative advantages, replacing the token-level ratios of diffu-GRPO. Theoretical analysis proves the SDMC estimator achieves O(1/NK) variance and O(1/N⁴) or O(1/N⁸) squared bias under quadrature, strictly dominating the O(1/NK) MSE of a generic double Monte Carlo estimator.

## Results

Using LLaDA-8B-Instruct as the base model with N=3 Gaussian quadrature points, GDPO (with SFT) achieves 82.79% on GSM8K, 39.6% on MATH500, 74.22% on Countdown, and 26.42% on Sudoku at 256 generations. On coding, GDPO reaches 39.6% on HumanEval and 50.6% on MBPP (256 generations), a 10-point absolute gain over the pretrained checkpoint on MBPP without any SFT. The most dramatic improvement is on Countdown, where GDPO jumps from 19.5% (base) to 74.22%, more than doubling the diffu-GRPO baseline of 31.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GDPO (with SFT) outperforms diffu-GRPO on all four planning/math tasks at 256 generations: GSM8K 82.79 vs. 79.8, MATH500 39.6 vs. 37.2, Countdown 74.22 vs. 31.3, and Sudoku 26.42 vs. 12.9. Against wD1, GDPO wins decisively on Countdown (74.22 vs. 51.2) and Sudoku (26.42 vs. 25.2), while being slightly ahead on GSM8K (82.79 vs. 80.8) but trailing on MATH500 (39.6 vs. 34.4 — actually GDPO leads here too). On coding, GDPO surpasses diffu-GRPO on MBPP (50.6 vs. 45.5 at 256) and HumanEval (39.6 vs. 39.0), though it slightly underperforms on HumanEval at 128 generations (26.2 vs. 29.3). In the direct comparison with coupled-GRPO (DiffuCoder) in Table 7, GDPO leads on Countdown (67.19 vs. 43.75 at 256) and Sudoku (24.17 vs. 21.0 at 256), confirming that sequence-level ELBO-based optimization provides a stronger training signal than token-level coupled likelihoods.

## Relation to Prior Work

GDPO extends the GRPO lineage (Shao et al., 2024) to diffusion language models by replacing the token-level importance ratios that Diffu-GRPO (Zhao et al., 2025) approximated via one-step unmasking with sequence-level ELBO-based likelihoods. The key methodological departure is the Semi-deterministic Monte Carlo (SDMC) scheme, which treats the ELBO's time integral as a deterministic quadrature problem (Gaussian quadrature with 2–3 points) while retaining a single Monte Carlo sample for the masking integral, thereby eliminating the dominant variance source identified through a law-of-total-variance decomposition. This positions GDPO between the crude but cheap token-level heuristics of Diffu-GRPO and coupled-GRPO (Gong et al., 2025b) on one hand, and the expensive double-Monte-Carlo ELBO estimators of prior DLM work (Nie et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2025) on the other.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GDPO provides the first principled, low-variance, and computationally cheap RL algorithm for DLMs, making post-training of diffusion language models accessible on modest hardware (2 H100 GPUs) and opening the door to scaling RL-based reasoning to the DLM paradigm. The theoretical contribution—showing the ELBO integrand is C^∞ and provably convex/monotone under Increasing Conditional Divergence conditions—gives a clean justification for quadrature-based estimation, though verifying these conditions in practice remains non-trivial. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger DLMs (only 8B is tested), whether data-driven quadrature weights and locations could further reduce variance, and whether the unnormalized advantage formulation generalizes beyond the tested benchmarks.
