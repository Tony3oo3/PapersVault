## Problem

Continual learning for pre-trained vision-language models must simultaneously preserve general pre-trained knowledge (forward stability), retain previously learned task knowledge (backward stability), and acquire new task-specific knowledge (plasticity). Existing LoRA-based continual learning methods such as O-LoRA, InfLoRA, and SD-LoRA focus primarily on backward stability through inter-task orthogonality constraints but do not explicitly protect the principal subspace of pre-trained weights that encodes general transferable knowledge, and they initialize updates in suboptimal directions that limit plasticity.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained vision-language models (CLIP ViT-B/16 for image classification and LLaVA-1.5-7B for multimodal instruction tuning), taking RGB images paired with text prompts or class names as input and producing class labels or free-form text responses as output. A key practical property is that the LoRA adapters are merged into the base model weights after each task's training, so inference incurs zero additional overhead compared to the original pre-trained model. The method is evaluated under the multi-domain task incremental learning (MTIL) setting where each task introduces a new dataset with its own class vocabulary, and under multimodal continual instruction tuning with diverse VQA formats.

## Method

KeepLoRA's core insight is that general pre-trained knowledge resides in the principal subspace (top singular components) of weight matrices, while domain-specific adaptations are encoded in the residual subspace. For each new task, the method computes the first-step gradient G_t, projects it onto the subspace orthogonal to both the pre-trained principal subspace W_p and the accumulated dominant feature directions M_{t-1} of all previous tasks, and performs SVD on this projected gradient to initialize the LoRA matrices: A is set to the top-r left singular vectors (frozen throughout training) and B to the corresponding scaled right singular vectors. Only B is optimized during training, which Proposition 3.1 shows is equivalent to gradient descent constrained to span(A_t), and Proposition 3.2 proves this initialization is the optimal solution to maximizing the projected gradient norm subject to orthogonality constraints. After training, the LoRA is merged into the base weights, and the dominant feature directions of the current task are extracted and appended to M_t for use in subsequent tasks.

## Results

On the MTIL benchmark (CLIP ViT-B/16, 11 classification tasks), KeepLoRA achieves 69.0% Transfer, 77.5% Average, and 86.1% Last accuracy, outperforming the strongest LoRA-based baselines (InfLoRA: 67.4/76.3/85.2) and reference-data methods (ZSCL: 68.1/75.4/83.6). On the MLLM-DCL benchmark (LLaVA-1.5-7B, 5 VQA domains), KeepLoRA reaches 33.71% Transfer, 54.19% Average, and 64.41% Last, surpassing SEFE (33.49/51.86/59.91). On the UCIT benchmark (6 VQA datasets), KeepLoRA attains 28.40% Transfer, 55.37% Average, and 67.84% Last, leading all compared methods. The architecture-extension variant KeepLoRA+ further improves to 69.9% Transfer and 79.0% Average on MTIL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KeepLoRA outperforms all three LoRA-based continual learning baselines across every metric: vs. InfLoRA (closest competitor) it gains +1.6 Transfer, +1.2 Average, and +0.9 Last on MTIL; vs. O-LoRA the margins are larger (+2.5 Transfer, +4.2 Average, +7.8 Last); vs. SD-LoRA it leads by +1.9 Transfer, +5.0 Average, and +6.9 Last. Against reference-data regularization methods, KeepLoRA beats ZSCL by +0.9 Transfer, +2.1 Average, and +2.5 Last while requiring no external reference data. Among architecture-extension methods, KeepLoRA+ (69.9 Transfer, 79.0 Average) surpasses IAP (69.2/76.8) and MoE-Adapters (68.9/76.7) without adding inference-time modules. On the LLaVA-based benchmarks (MLLM-DCL and UCIT), KeepLoRA consistently leads SEFE, O-LoRA, CL-MoE, and vanilla LoRA-FT on all three metrics, with the largest gap on the Last metric (e.g., 64.41 vs. 59.91 for SEFE on MLLM-DCL). The one notable exception is IAP, which edges out KeepLoRA by 0.2 points on Transfer (69.2 vs. 69.0) on MTIL, though KeepLoRA+ recovers the lead at 69.9.

## Relation to Prior Work

KeepLoRA extends the gradient-projection line of work for LoRA-based continual learning (O-LoRA, InfLoRA, SD-LoRA) by identifying a critical gap: prior methods constrain updates to be orthogonal to previous task subspaces but do not explicitly protect the principal subspace of pre-trained weights that encodes general transferable knowledge. The key conceptual departure is the SVD-based analysis showing that general knowledge resides in the principal subspace while domain-specific adaptations live in the residual subspace, motivating a dual-orthogonality constraint (to both W_p and M_{t-1}) that no prior LoRA-CL method imposed. The gradient-informed initialization of the frozen down-projection matrix A, derived from the first-step gradient projected into the residual subspace, further distinguishes KeepLoRA from methods that use random or SVD-of-A initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KeepLoRA provides a principled, inference-overhead-free solution to the three-way tradeoff (plasticity, backward stability, forward stability) in VLM continual learning, with the LoRA adapter mergeable into base weights for deployment. The SVD-based subspace decomposition offers a reusable analytical lens for understanding where different types of knowledge are encoded in pre-trained models. Limitations include reliance on two energy-ratio hyperparameters (ε_w, ε_f) that require per-architecture tuning, evaluation limited to CLIP ViT-B/16 and LLaVA-1.5-7B, and the need to compute full SVDs of weight matrices and task features at each task boundary, which may be costly for very large models. Open questions include whether the principal/residual subspace separation holds for non-attention layers, transformer decoders, or much larger foundation models, and how the method behaves under non-sequential or interleaved task orderings.
