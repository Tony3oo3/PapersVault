## Problem

Masked diffusion models (MDMs) generate text by iteratively unmasking [MASK] tokens, but the choice of which position to unmask next is typically governed by ad hoc heuristics like max-confidence or max-margin. These rule-based schedules, while effective, are suboptimal: the paper demonstrates that higher-yield unmasking trajectories exist beyond the deterministic max-confidence order, yet no principled method has been proposed to discover them. The gap is a learned, theoretically grounded unmasking policy that provably outperforms heuristic references.

## Input / Output

At each denoising step, the policy model receives the frozen MDM's transformer features (a d-dimensional vector per position) concatenated with the Top-K token probabilities at each masked position, and outputs a softmax distribution over the currently masked indices to select the next unmasking position. The base MDM (e.g., LLaDA-8B) remains frozen throughout; only the lightweight 134M-parameter policy model (one Transformer layer + 3-layer MLP) is trained. The final output is a fully unmasked sequence of length L (e.g., 128 tokens for math reasoning, 81 cells for SUDOKU), with rewards computed by an external verifier on the terminal output.

## Method

The paper casts MDM denoising as a KL-regularized MDP where the agent selects which masked position to unmask and the environment dynamics are the frozen MDM's per-position token distributions. Training uses a GRPO-style clipped objective with a strong heuristic reference policy (max-confidence, softmax-temperature, or Top-K) as a trust region, yielding two theoretical guarantees: convergence to a fixed point with strictly higher expected reward than the reference, and a KL-tightening result showing the learned policy's terminal-output distribution is closer to the true data distribution. Since the output-level objective is intractable (requiring marginalization over all trajectories), the authors derive a tractable token-wise surrogate loss whose gradient matches the output-level gradient at the first optimization step (Proposition 1). The practical training algorithm is memory-efficient: it samples trajectories under the old policy, caches all MDM features and token distributions, then frees the 8B MDM from GPU memory before performing mini-batch gradient updates on only the 134M policy model.

## Results

Using LLaDA-8B-INSTRUCT as the frozen base MDM, the learned unmasking policy achieves 81.7% accuracy on SUDOKU, 36.2% on ZEBRA, 70.3% on GSM8K, and 28.4% on MATH500. The largest gain is on SUDOKU, where the policy improves over random unmasking by 20.1 percentage points and over max-confidence by 11.2 percentage points. On GSM8K, the policy exceeds max-confidence by 1.9 points (70.3% vs. 68.4%), and on MATH500 it ties the best heuristic (Margin, 28.4%) while surpassing max-confidence by 1.2 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The learned unmasking policy outperforms all four heuristic baselines on every benchmark. Against max-confidence (Chang et al., 2022), the strongest single-path heuristic, the policy gains +11.2 points on SUDOKU (81.7% vs. 70.5%), +2.5 points on ZEBRA (36.2% vs. 33.7%), +1.9 points on GSM8K (70.3% vs. 68.4%), and +1.2 points on MATH500 (28.4% vs. 27.2%). Against max-margin (Kim et al., 2025), the policy wins on SUDOKU (+10.4 pts), ZEBRA (+1.6 pts), and GSM8K (+3.2 pts), and ties on MATH500 (both 28.4%). Against entropy-based sampling (Ye et al., 2025), the policy leads on all four benchmarks, with the largest margin on SUDOKU (+14.6 pts). Against random unmasking (Zheng et al., 2025), the policy dominates across the board, with gains ranging from +2.5 pts (ZEBRA) to +20.1 pts (SUDOKU). The paper also shows compatibility with diffu-GRPO post-training: when applied on top of a diffu-GRPO-tuned LLaDA, the policy adds +1.3 points over max-confidence on GSM8K (76.4% vs. 75.1%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GRPO policy optimization framework (Shao et al., 2024) and the convergence theory for verifiable-reward RL (Mroueh, 2025), applying them to a new domain: learning the unmasking position-selection policy in masked diffusion models. It departs from prior RL-on-MDM work (Zekri & Boullé, 2025; Zhao et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2025) that post-trains the MDM itself, and from DCOLT (Huang et al., 2025) that jointly optimizes both the policy and the MDM, by freezing the MDM and training only a lightweight unmasking policy under a KL-regularized MDP formulation with an explicit reference policy. The theoretical contribution—proving that the optimized policy provably yields a terminal-output distribution closer to p_data than the reference—extends Mroueh's convergence result to the discrete denoising setting with a support argument specific to MDMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled, theoretically grounded alternative to ad-hoc unmasking heuristics (max-confidence, max-margin) in MDMs, with provable convergence and KL-tightening guarantees that prior rule-based schedules lack. The consistent gains across four benchmarks (up to 11.2% over max-confidence on SUDOKU) demonstrate that the choice of unmasking order is a meaningful bottleneck, and that a small learned policy (134M params on top of an 8B MDM) can capture structure that simple heuristics miss. Open questions include whether the learned policy generalizes to larger, more diverse language datasets beyond the four benchmarks tested, and how to stabilize policy optimization in the much larger search spaces of open-ended text generation where Top-K restriction becomes necessary. The paper also acknowledges that gains on mathematical reasoning (GSM8K, MATH500) are more modest than on structured puzzles, suggesting the method's effectiveness depends on the regularity of the underlying ordering structure.
