## Problem

Existing prompt optimization methods for text-to-image generation either rely on general-purpose LLMs that lack alignment with T2I-specific text distributions, depend on user-collected prompts of inconsistent quality, or employ reinforcement learning that is computationally expensive and tied to a single T2I model. The paper identifies that high-quality prompts should align with the large-scale text distributions (including aesthetic fine-tuning data) used during T2I model training, and asks how to efficiently sample from this distribution to expand simple user inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264478/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

TIPO accepts a user's coarse prompt in either tag-based form (comma-separated terms, e.g., 'scenery, water, wind') or natural language form (1–2 short sentences, or a truncated long prompt under 40 words), optionally accompanied by metadata tags (style, aspect ratio, quality, year). It produces a structured, enriched prompt containing an expanded tag sequence (18–72 tags depending on length category) and a refined multi-sentence natural language description (2–18 sentences), formatted with metadata headers. The output is a text prompt intended for downstream T2I models (SDXL, SD-3.5, FLUX, etc.), not an image itself; the 200M-parameter model performs text-to-text transformation in roughly 1 second per prompt.

## Method

TIPO trains a lightweight 200M-parameter LLaMA model on a curated 30M-pair, 40B-token caption corpus (Danbooru2023, GBC10M, CoyoHD11M) using a multi-task pre-sampling objective. The key training mechanism is prefix-based dropout: for tag prompts, tags are shuffled and a random prefix is used as input with the full set as target; for NL prompts, the first sentence is preserved while subsequent sentences are randomly dropped, ensuring the simple prompt is always a prefix of the complete prompt. At inference, TIPO decomposes optimization into composable subtasks (short_to_tag, tag_to_long, short_to_long, and their chained composites) executed sequentially, progressively building a context-rich prompt that preserves the user's original semantics while appending distribution-consistent detail. This 'text sampling before image sampling' strategy avoids both the distribution mismatch of LLM rewriting and the per-model cost of RL, achieving up to 29.4% lower latency than competing optimizers while maintaining strong alignment with T2I training text distributions as verified by Fréchet Distance and MMD in embedding space.

## Results

TIPO achieves the best overall average rank (2.07) across all evaluation settings, outperforming GPT-4o-mini (3.00), MagicPrompt (3.00), and Promptist (3.87). On in-domain tag-based prompts, TIPO attains the lowest FDD (0.2282) and AI Corrupt Score (0.0805), with a competitive Aesthetic Score of 6.2571. In human preference evaluation over 1,400+ pairwise comparisons from 221 volunteers, TIPO achieves a 51.3% overall win rate (52.5% out-of-domain), and prompt generation latency is reduced by up to 29.4% compared to Promptist.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4o-mini, TIPO substantially outperforms on FDD (0.2282 vs. 0.5414) and AI Corrupt Score (0.0805 vs. 0.2510) for tag-based prompts, but GPT retains an edge on Aesthetic (6.3676 vs. 6.2571) and Vendi diversity (16.814 vs. 13.307); in the out-of-domain setting, GPT leads on Aesthetic (6.7125 vs. 6.0536) while TIPO dominates Vendi (21.571 vs. 8.9718). Against MagicPrompt, TIPO wins on FDD (0.2282 vs. 0.3247), Aesthetic (6.2571 vs. 6.1609), and Corrupt (0.0805 vs. 0.4976) for tag-based prompts, and in human preference the win ratio is 48:11 in TIPO's favor. Against Promptist, TIPO achieves better FDD (0.2282 vs. 0.2350), Aesthetic (6.2571 vs. 5.9468), and Corrupt (0.0805 vs. 0.4331) on tag-based prompts, with a 101:30 human preference win ratio. Against Gemini-2.0-Flash-Image (which performs its own self-refinement), TIPO still yields lower AI Corrupt (0.0700 vs. 0.0928) and FDD (0.7964 vs. 0.8084) with nearly identical Aesthetic scores (5.2649 vs. 5.2584), demonstrating that even a strong closed-source integrated optimizer can be marginally improved by TIPO's lightweight pre-sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIPO departs from three established lines of prompt optimization for T2I: (1) zero-shot LLM rewriting (e.g., GPT-4o-mini), which the authors argue misaligns because LLMs are trained on general natural language rather than T2I-specific prompt distributions; (2) community-prompt fine-tuning (MagicPrompt, prompt-extend), which inherits inconsistent user expertise; and (3) RL-based optimization (Promptist, PAE), which is model-specific and computationally expensive. Rather than building on any single prior method, TIPO reframes prompt optimization as a multi-task pre-training problem: a lightweight LLaMA-based model is trained on a 40B-token corpus of T2I training captions to learn distribution-aligned text pre-sampling, effectively repurposing the pre-training paradigm for prompt engineering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: a 200M-parameter model running in ~1 second per prompt can match or beat frontier LLMs on T2I prompt quality, making automated prompt refinement accessible on consumer hardware. The distribution-alignment hypothesis is cleanly validated through embedding-space distance metrics (FD, MMD) across multiple encoders, providing a principled explanation for why the method works. However, out-of-domain performance is mixed (GPT-4o-mini still wins on Aesthetic for SD-3.5-Large), and the paper acknowledges open questions around personalization, image-feedback-aware refinement, and generalization to truly novel user inputs. The gap between the abstract's 62.8% win rate claim and the body's 51.3% overall figure also warrants scrutiny.
