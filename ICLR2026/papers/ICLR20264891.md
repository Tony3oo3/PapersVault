## Problem

Existing physics-constrained generative models either require joint parameter-solution training data (often unavailable in observational settings), handle only simple global constraints, or enforce constraints via inference-time projections that introduce discontinuities. This paper addresses the gap of fine-tuning flow-matching models to enforce parameter-dependent PDE constraints using only observational data, while simultaneously recovering the hidden physical parameters that govern those constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264891/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained flow-matching model on noisy or low-fidelity observational data (64×64 pixel-space PDE fields or latent-space images) and the specification of the governing PDE operator with unknown parameters. It jointly outputs a physically consistent field solution and a latent parameter field (e.g., permeability, Young's modulus, sound speed, viscosity) at the same spatial resolution. For the image application, a text prompt and class label condition generation, and the parameter α becomes a polynomial color transform applied outside the latent space. Fine-tuning is extremely lightweight—20 gradient steps (~15 min on one GPU) for Darcy—after which sampling proceeds at base-model cost with no inference-time adjustments.

## Method

The core contribution is a post-training fine-tuning procedure that recasts reward-based distribution tilting as a stochastic optimal control problem via Adjoint Matching, where the reward is defined by weak-form PDE residuals computed with randomly sampled Wendland-wavelet test functions (compactly supported local polynomial kernels that avoid high-order derivatives). A key architectural novelty is the joint evolution of the state x and latent parameter α along learned vector fields: the parameter flow is initialized from a surrogate base flow constructed by an inverse predictor φ (pre-trained to map solutions to parameters by minimizing PDE residuals), and a residual U-Net head learns the fine-tuned parameter dynamics. The authors introduce a scaled memoryless noise schedule σ²(t)=(1−κ)2η_t, proving it retains the theoretical memoryless property while providing a numerical stabilization knob, and add a running-state regularization cost that anchors fine-tuned parameter estimates to base-model counterparts, enabling a tunable trade-off between residual reduction and distributional fidelity. The U-FNO backbone (for PDEs) or DiT-based LFM (for images) is augmented with ~6M parameters of zero-initialized correction heads that condition the state flow on α and predict the parameter flow.

## Results

On the linear elasticity benchmark under boundary-condition misspecification, the proposed joint Adjoint Matching method achieves a BC error of 1.71×10⁻⁶ (vs. 6.98×10⁻⁵ for base FM and 2.32×10⁻⁵ for PBFM), relative weak/strong residuals of 6.15/3.79, and the lowest distributional distances (MMD_x = 0.15, MMD_α = 0.12). On the Helmholtz wave-propagation problem with damped-to-lossless model mismatch, the joint model attains the best weak residual (4.3×10⁰) and the lowest MMD_x (0.06) among all methods. Fine-tuning is computationally lightweight: the noisy Darcy experiment requires only 20 gradient steps (~15 min on a single NVIDIA L40S), after which sampling proceeds at base-model cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PBFM (Baldan et al., 2025), the proposed method trades a marginal residual difference (6.15 vs. 6.32 relative weak residual on elasticity; 4.3 vs. 8.33 on Helmholtz, where Ours wins) for a dramatic improvement in distributional fidelity: MMD_x drops from 0.92 to 0.15 and MMD_α from 0.54 to 0.12 on elasticity, and MMD_x improves from 0.09 to 0.06 on Helmholtz. Against FM+ECI (Cheng et al., 2024), the proposed method is decisively superior on PDE residuals (6.15 vs. 1010 relative weak residual) and distributional metrics (MMD_x 0.15 vs. 1.16), though FM+ECI achieves exactly zero BC error by hard projection at the cost of severe distributional distortion. On the Stokes lid-driven cavity, the joint model uniquely enters the low-MMD_α regime (≈0.07–0.13) while Base AM ablations remain at 0.22–0.28, highlighting the advantage of jointly evolving the parameter field. Overall, the method consistently achieves the best or near-best residuals while maintaining the smallest distributional shift across all four PDE benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Adjoint Matching framework (Domingo-Enrich et al., 2025) for reward-based fine-tuning of flow-matching models by introducing a joint state–parameter evolution that handles parameter-dependent PDE constraints without requiring paired solution–coefficient training data. It departs from pre-training physics-loss approaches (Bastek et al., PBFM) by operating as a lightweight post-training step, and from projection-based inference methods (ECI, Utkarsh et al.) by reshaping the entire output distribution rather than correcting individual trajectories. The inverse predictor φ and the scaled memoryless noise schedule (κ) are novel architectural and theoretical additions that enable the joint flow to regularize toward base-model parameter estimates while still reducing PDE residuals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap in physics-informed generative modeling: enforcing parameter-dependent PDE constraints when coefficient fields are unobserved, a common situation in observational science. The computational efficiency (20 gradient steps, <15 min on one GPU for Darcy) makes it attractive for rapid adaptation of pre-trained simulators. Open questions include extension to coupled multi-physics systems, stochastic or chaotic dynamics, and whether the joint-evolution approach scales to high-dimensional parameter spaces; the current experiments are limited to 2D linear or mildly nonlinear PDEs on 64×64 grids.
