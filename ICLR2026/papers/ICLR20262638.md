## Problem

Current video MLLMs treat video as sparse frames and rely on brute-force context expansion, lacking the ability to construct, update, and predict with an implicit 3D world model from continual sensory experience. Existing benchmarks overwhelmingly probe linguistic understanding and semantic perception rather than the deeper spatial cognition and predictive world modeling required for true 'spatial supersensing.' The paper asks whether scaling data and context alone can close this gap, and if not, what architectural principles might.

## Input / Output

The model consumes arbitrarily long room-tour videos (10–240 minutes) sampled at 1 FPS for the Recall task and 24 FPS for the Count task, paired with a text question. VSR outputs a multiple-choice answer identifying the correct temporal order of a novel object's appearances across four distinct spatial locations; VSC outputs a cumulative object count that evolves dynamically over time and can be queried at multiple streaming timestamps. A key design constraint is that video length is unbounded by construction, deliberately exceeding any fixed context window to force selective, structured perception rather than indiscriminate token accumulation.

## Method

Cambrian-S is a family of spatially-grounded video MLLMs (0.5B–7B) built on an upgraded Cambrian-1 base (SigLIP2-SO400m encoder, Qwen2.5 LM, 2-layer MLP connector) and trained in four stages: vision-language alignment, image instruction tuning, general video instruction tuning on 3M samples, and spatial video instruction tuning on VSI-590K (590K QA pairs from 10 sources spanning annotated real videos, simulated scenes, and pseudo-annotated web videos) mixed with general video data to prevent capability regression. The key architectural innovation is a Latent Frame Prediction (LFP) head—a two-layer MLP trained jointly with the language head via MSE and cosine-distance losses on 290K 1-FPS video clips—that predicts the next frame's latent representation. At inference, the cosine distance between predicted and actual frame features yields a self-supervised 'surprise' score that drives two mechanisms: (1) surprise-driven memory management, where low-surprise frames are 2× compressed before entering a fixed-size long-term memory pool and high-surprise frames are retained, enabling retrieval-augmented answering on VSR; and (2) surprise-driven event segmentation, where high-surprise frames trigger segment boundaries, buffered features are summarized into per-segment counts, and segment answers are aggregated for VSC. This predictive-sensing approach substantially outperforms Gemini-2.5-Flash and other long-context baselines on both VSI-SUPER tasks while maintaining stable GPU memory across video lengths.

## Results

Cambrian-S-7B achieves 67.5% on VSI-Bench, surpassing the best proprietary model Gemini-2.5-Pro (51.5%) by over 16 absolute points and the best open-source model InterVL3.5-8B (56.3%) by 11.2 points. On the new VSI-SUPER benchmark, however, even Cambrian-S-7B struggles: VSR accuracy drops from 38.3% (10 min) to 0% (>60 min) under 1 FPS sampling, and VSC essentially fails across all durations. With the proposed predictive sensing (surprise-driven memory and event segmentation), Cambrian-S substantially outperforms Gemini-2.5-Flash on both VSI-SUPER tasks, reaching 38% MRA on 10-minute VSC streams versus under 15% for Gemini-Live and GPT-Realtime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On VSI-Bench, Cambrian-S-7B (67.5%) decisively outperforms all proprietary baselines—Gemini-2.5-Pro (51.5%), Gemini-1.5-Pro (45.4%), and GPT-4o (34.0%)—as well as all open-source models, with the closest competitor InterVL3.5-8B trailing by 11.2 points. On general video benchmarks, Cambrian-S-7B remains competitive: it leads on EgoSchema (76.8 vs. 73.5 for Qwen2.5-3B) and Perception Test (69.9), though it trails Gemini-2.5-Pro on VideoMMMU (38.6 vs. 83.6) and VideoMME (63.4 vs. 75.0 for Gemini-1.5-Pro), reflecting a spatial-specialization trade-off. On the new VSI-SUPER benchmark, Gemini-2.5-Flash achieves 41.5% VSR and 10.9% VSC at 60 minutes, while vanilla Cambrian-S-7B drops to 6.0% VSR and 0.0% VSC at the same duration—demonstrating that data scaling alone does not solve continual spatial sensing. With the surprise-driven memory system, Cambrian-S outperforms both Gemini-1.5-Flash and Gemini-2.5-Flash on VSR across all video lengths while maintaining stable GPU memory. For VSC, surprise-driven event segmentation yields consistently higher and more stable counts than Gemini-2.5-Flash (whose predictions saturate at small constants), and in streaming evaluation Cambrian-S reaches 38% MRA at 10 minutes versus under 15% for Gemini-Live and GPT-Realtime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Cambrian-1 (Tong et al., 2024) as its architectural base, upgrading the visual encoder to SigLIP2 and the language backbone to Qwen2.5, then extends the VSI-Bench spatial reasoning formulation (Yang et al., 2024d) from short, single-scene clips to arbitrarily long streaming video via the VSI-SUPER benchmark. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant 'scale the context window' paradigm for long video understanding (LongVILA, Flash-VStream, MovieChat) by proposing predictive sensing—leveraging self-supervised next-latent-frame prediction error as a control signal for memory and segmentation—drawing on predictive coding theory (Rao & Ballard, 1999; Friston, 2010) and world-model literature (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018). The VSI-590K dataset scales up VSI-Bench's spatial question taxonomy to 590K QA pairs across 10 sources, filling a data gap the authors identify as the primary bottleneck for spatial MLLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that a model achieving SOTA spatial reasoning (67.5% on VSI-Bench, +30% absolute) drops to 0% on VSI-SUPER beyond 60 minutes—challenges the assumption that spatial competence transfers to longer contexts and that scaling data or context length is sufficient. The proof-of-concept showing that a simple prediction-error (surprise) signal outperforms Gemini-2.5-Flash on both VSR and VSC suggests a viable path toward unbounded video understanding, though the authors explicitly frame this as an early prototype with limited scale and generalizability. Open questions include whether the LFP-based surprise signal generalizes beyond indoor walkthroughs, whether the synthetic VSI-SUPER construction (Gemini-edited objects) captures the full difficulty of real-world spatial memory, and whether true predictive world modeling requires richer objectives than next-frame latent prediction. The VSI-SUPER benchmark and VSI-590K dataset are likely to become standard references for evaluating and training spatial video MLLMs.
