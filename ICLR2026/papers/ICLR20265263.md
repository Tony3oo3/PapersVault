## Problem

Existing deep learning approaches for protein inverse folding achieve strong sequence recovery but lack explicit mechanisms to reuse fine-grained structure-sequence patterns (recurring motifs) that are evolutionarily conserved across natural proteins. This limits their ability to ground predictions in the rich diversity of known local folding rules, particularly for short or novel backbones where global context is sparse.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265263/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PRISM takes a 3D protein backbone (atomic coordinates) and an initial sequence estimate produced by an off-the-shelf base estimator (e.g., AIDO.Protein-IF or ProteinMPNN-CMLM), which together form a crude query embedding via a joint structure-sequence encoder. The method retrieves K=35 similar per-residue potential-motif embeddings from a fixed vector database of ~3.9M entries (built from CATH-4.2 training split) and outputs per-residue logits over the 20-amino-acid vocabulary, decoded via argmax to yield the final designed sequence. The entire retrieval step runs on GPU in ~1.2 ms per protein, adding only ~14% overhead over the base estimator.

## Method

PRISM formulates inverse folding as a latent-variable probabilistic model that factorizes into representation, retrieval, attribution, and emission stages. A joint encoder (AIDO.Protein, a 16B-parameter MoE protein language model) produces per-residue embeddings that capture local 3D-sequential context (potential motifs); a deterministic Top-K cosine-similarity kernel retrieves the K nearest potential motifs from a precomputed vector database; and a hybrid multihead self-cross attention (MHSCA) decoder aggregates retrieved fragments (as self-attention Q/K/V) with a separate structural encoding (as cross-attention query) to emit refined per-residue logits. Under the deterministic reduction, the training objective collapses to standard per-residue cross-entropy over the 20-amino-acid vocabulary, with gradients flowing through the MHSCA decoder and structure encoder while retrieval is treated as fixed. The framework is base-estimator-agnostic and optionally supports finetuning the joint encoder via a trainable Plackett-Luce retrieval kernel with KL regularization.

## Results

On the CATH-4.2 test set (All), PRISM achieves a perplexity of 2.71 and amino acid recovery (AAR) of 60.43%, outperforming the strongest baseline AIDO.Protein-IF (PPL 2.94, AAR 58.60%) and all other methods by wide margins. On TS50 and TS500, PRISM sets new state-of-the-art with PPL 2.43/AAR 67.92% and PPL 2.27/AAR 70.53% respectively. Foldability metrics (via AlphaFold2) also improve: RMSD drops to 1.56 Å, sc-TM rises to 0.913, and pLDDT reaches 0.835 on CATH-4.2, confirming that higher sequence recovery translates to better structural fidelity. Runtime overhead is negligible at ~14.3% over the base estimator (1.05s vs 0.92s per protein).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRISM outperforms all ten baselines across every benchmark and metric. Against the strongest baseline AIDO.Protein-IF (16B-parameter MoE), PRISM reduces perplexity from 2.94 to 2.71 and boosts AAR from 58.60% to 60.43% on CATH-4.2, while also improving foldability (RMSD 1.56 vs 1.58, sc-TM 0.913 vs 0.912, pLDDT 0.835 vs 0.831). Against mid-tier methods, the gap is substantial: PiFold (PPL 4.55, AAR 51.66%), ProteinMPNN (PPL 4.61, AAR 45.96%), and LM-Design (PPL 4.41, AAR 54.41%) all trail PRISM by 1.7–2.0 PPL and 6–15 AAR points. On out-of-distribution benchmarks, PRISM extends its lead: on CAMEO 2022 it achieves AAR 64.63% vs 63.52% (AIDO), 46.24% (ESM3), and 33.58% (MultiFlow); on the PDB date split it reaches AAR 67.47% vs 66.27% (AIDO) and 57.91% (DPLM-2). Even the structure-only variant PRISM(str. enc.) without any protein language model already surpasses ProteinMPNN-CMLM (PPL 3.39 vs 5.03, AAR 49.17% vs 48.62%), isolating retrieval as an independent performance driver.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRISM builds directly on AIDO.Protein (Sun et al., 2024) as its joint encoder and base estimator, but departs from the monolithic encoder paradigm that characterizes all prior inverse folding methods (ProteinMPNN, PiFold, LM-Design, DPLM, AIDO.Protein-IF) by introducing an explicit retrieval mechanism over a vector database of fine-grained structure-sequence embeddings. The latent-variable formulation (representation → retrieval → attribution → emission) provides a theoretical grounding absent from prior work, and the hybrid MHSCA decoder is a new architectural component that jointly leverages self-attention over retrieved fragments and cross-attention with structural encodings. Conceptually, the paper repurposes retrieval-augmented generation—a technique well-established in NLP—into the protein design domain at residue-level granularity, treating local structure-sequence neighborhoods as reusable 'potential motifs.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRISM establishes that fine-grained retrieval of conserved local patterns provides a principled, scalable inductive bias for inverse folding that is complementary to large pretrained encoders, with the unimodal ablation confirming retrieval as an independent performance driver even without sequence-level priors. The negligible runtime overhead (~14.3%) and base-estimator-agnostic design make it a practical drop-in enhancement for existing pipelines. Open questions include jointly learning amortized posteriors for the embedding and attribution latents via KL terms, systematic study of iterative refinement paradigms, and pairing PRISM with surface-based or biochemistry-aware generators to extend beyond backbone-conditioned design. The coverage saturation result (fixed CATH-4.2 database suffices) simplifies deployment but also suggests the current retrieval granularity may not capture all relevant motif diversity for truly novel folds.
