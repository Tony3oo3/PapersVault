## Problem

Existing Mixture-of-Experts adaptations for CLIP suffer from two critical limitations: sequential expert training is computationally expensive and accumulates clustering errors, and fine-tuning on new data causes catastrophic forgetting of zero-shot classification capabilities. No prior method simultaneously enforces meaningful expert specialization through parallel training while preserving the pretrained knowledge that underpins CLIP's strong generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263089/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images and text prompts with context lengths of 77 tokens (standard) or 248 tokens (long-context, via linearly interpolated positional embeddings). Long captions exceeding the context limit are split into multiple semantically coherent sub-captions (typically 4–5 per image), each weighted in a multi-positive InfoNCE objective. Outputs are image-text similarity scores used for zero-shot classification (top-1 accuracy) and bidirectional retrieval (R@1, R@5), with the text encoder also serving as a conditioning component for Stable Diffusion XL image generation.

## Method

CLIP-FMoE introduces a two-stage training pipeline. In Stage 1 (Isolated Constrained Contrastive Learning), two-level K-means clustering on base-model image embeddings partitions data into N clusters × M sub-clusters; N MLP series are then trained in parallel (not sequentially) on their respective clusters, with a Constrained Sampler ensuring all samples in each batch originate from the same sub-cluster to sharpen in-batch negatives. A Fusion Gate—a sigmoid-gated linear projection—blends the frozen base MLP with the newly trained expert MLP at each layer, explicitly preserving pretrained representations. In Stage 2, the trained MLP series become frozen MoE experts, and only the router and a second Fusion Gate (which blends the base MLP with the MoE mixture output) are trained on the full dataset using multi-positive InfoNCE plus load-balancing regularization. This design ensures expert specialization through isolated domain-specific training while the dual Fusion Gate mechanism prevents catastrophic forgetting of zero-shot capabilities.

## Results

On zero-shot classification across 11 benchmarks (ViT-L/14, CC3M/LLaVA-ReCap, 77-token context), CLIP-FMoE achieves 68.65% average top-1 accuracy, reducing the degradation from the original CLIP (69.62%) to under 1%, compared to ~3–4% drops for vanilla fine-tuning (66.08%), Up-cycling (66.69%), and CLIP-MoE (66.47%). On standard image-text retrieval (MS COCO, Flickr 8K, Flickr 30K), CLIP-FMoE attains 82.68% average R@1, outperforming all baselines. In the long-context setting (248 tokens, ShareGPT4V), CLIP-FMoE reaches 77.2% average retrieval R@1, surpassing TULIP (75.9%) and LongCLIP (73.9%) by 1.3% and 3.3% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base OpenAI CLIP, CLIP-FMoE preserves zero-shot classification within 1% (68.65 vs. 69.62 avg.) while substantially improving retrieval (82.68 vs. 73.96 avg. R@1), demonstrating that the Fusion Gate effectively prevents catastrophic forgetting. Compared to Sparse Up-cycling (Komatsuzaki et al., 2023), CLIP-FMoE gains +1.96% on classification and +1.07% on retrieval, attributed to the ICCL clustering-based initialization that avoids the homogeneity problem of replicated MLPs. Versus CLIP-MoE (Zhang et al., 2024c), CLIP-FMoE improves classification by +2.18% and retrieval by +0.39%, while reducing total training time by 72.5% (53 min vs. 194 min) through parallel expert training instead of sequential stages. In the long-context setting, CLIP-FMoE outperforms LongCLIP (Zhang et al., 2024a) by +3.3% average retrieval R@1 (77.2 vs. 73.9) and TULIP (Najdenkoska et al., 2025) by +1.3% (77.2 vs. 75.9), while also achieving higher zero-shot classification (69.51 vs. 68.91 and 61.87 respectively on 17 benchmarks), indicating that the Fusion Gate + ICCL pipeline is more effective than distillation-based or coarse-grained alignment strategies for preserving generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MoE-for-CLIP line initiated by CLIP-MoE (Zhang et al., 2024c) and Sparse Upcycling (Komatsuzaki et al., 2023), departing from their sequential expert-training or homogeneous-replication strategies in favor of a parallel, clustering-driven initialization (ICCL) that guarantees expert diversification. The Fusion Gate mechanism is a novel architectural addition not present in prior MoE-CLIP work, drawing conceptually from model-merging literature (task arithmetic, Model Soups) but applied dynamically per-token rather than as a static weight combination. The long-context evaluation follows LongCLIP's positional-embedding interpolation protocol, positioning CLIP-FMoE as a drop-in replacement for the text encoder in downstream generative pipelines like Stable Diffusion XL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck for practitioners who want to scale CLIP without the compute cost of uniform parameter growth: the 72.5% training-time reduction over CLIP-MoE with only a 14% inference FLOPs increase makes MoE-CLIP feasible on modest GPU clusters. The Fusion Gate's ablation reveals a non-trivial tradeoff—applying it only in Stage 2 preserves classification but degrades retrieval—suggesting that knowledge preservation must be active during expert specialization, not just at integration time. Open questions include how the two-level K-means clustering quality (and its sensitivity to the base model's embedding space) scales to larger datasets or more experts, and whether the approach transfers to video-text or multilingual settings where the clustering structure may differ substantially.
