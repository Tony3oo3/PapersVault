## Problem

Sparse recovery theory has been developed almost exclusively under the assumption of homogeneous (i.i.d.) noise across all measurements. When observations come from mixed-quality sources—e.g., a small set of expert-labeled data with low noise and a larger set of weakly-labeled data with high noise—no prior work established sample-size conditions for information-theoretic or algorithmic support recovery, nor quantified how many low-quality samples are needed to replace one high-quality sample.

## Input / Output

The decoder receives n₁ high-quality noisy linear measurements (noise variance σ₁²) and n₂ > n₁ low-quality measurements (noise variance σ₂² > σ₁²) of an s-sparse signal β* ∈ R^p, with i.i.d. Gaussian design rows. In the agnostic setting the decoder treats all rows identically; in the informed setting it knows each row's noise variance. The output is the support S* = {i : β*_i ≠ 0} (information-theoretic results assume β* ∈ {0,1}^p so support recovery equals signal recovery), or the signed support (algorithmic results require min_{i∈S*}|β*_i| ≥ ρ > 0).

## Method

For information-theoretic recovery, the paper applies Chernoff bounds to the (rescaled) MLE and takes a union bound over all s-sparse supports, yielding a sufficient condition of the form α₁n₁ + α₂n₂ > n* where n* = 2s log(p/s) (sublinear regime) or 2h(α)p (linear regime). The ratio γ = α₁/α₂ is defined as the Price of Quality: in the agnostic setting γ is uniformly bounded by 2, while in the informed setting γ can diverge (e.g., γ = Θ(log SNR₁ / SNR₂) in the low-SNR₂/high-SNR₁ regime). For algorithmic recovery, the paper extends Wainwright's LASSO phase-transition proof to the heterogeneous agnostic setting by applying a QR decomposition to X_S and controlling the resulting orthogonal matrix via Haar-measure properties, showing the LASSO threshold n_ALG = 2s log(p−s)+s+1 is unchanged and the regularization condition depends only on the average noise level σ_avg² = (n₁σ₁² + n₂σ₂²)/n, making high- and low-quality samples contribute equally to the algorithmic threshold.

## Results

In the agnostic setting, the Price of Quality γ (number of low-quality samples to replace one high-quality sample) is uniformly bounded: γ ≈ 1 in the high-SNR₂ regime and γ ≈ 2 − σ₁²/σ₂² < 2 in the low-SNR₂ regime (Theorem 1). In the informed setting, γ can grow arbitrarily large: γ ≈ σ₂²/σ₁² in the low-SNR regime, γ = Θ(log SNR₁ / SNR₂) → ∞ in the low-SNR₂ & high-SNR₁ regime, and γ ≈ log SNR₁ / log SNR₂ in the high-SNR regime (Theorem 2). For algorithmic recovery, the LASSO threshold n_ALG = 2s log(p−s) + s + 1 is identical to the homogeneous-noise case and depends on the noise only through σ_avg² = (n₁σ₁² + n₂σ₂²)/n, meaning high- and low-quality samples contribute equally to the sample-size requirement (Theorem 3).

**Summary of the Price of Quality γ across SNR regimes (agnostic vs. informed setting).**

| SNR Regime | Agnostic γ (Thm 1) | Informed γ (Thm 2) |
|---|---|---|
| High SNR (σ₂² = o(s)) | γ ≈ 1 | γ ≈ log(s/σ₁²) / log(s/σ₂²) |
| Low SNR₂, High SNR₁ (σ₂² = ω(s), σ₁² = o(s)) | γ ≈ 2 − σ₁²/σ₂² < 2 | γ = Θ(log SNR₁ / SNR₂) → ∞ |
| Low SNR (σ₁² = ω(s)) | γ ≈ 2 − σ₁²/σ₂² < 2 | γ ≈ σ₂²/σ₁² (arbitrarily large) |

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; the key comparisons are between the two decoder settings and between information-theoretic and algorithmic thresholds. In the agnostic setting, the Price of Quality is bounded by 2, meaning one high-quality sample is never worth more than two low-quality samples under the sufficient condition. In the informed setting, where the decoder knows per-sample variances and uses the rescaled MLE, the Price of Quality is unbounded and can diverge to infinity in the low-SNR₂ & high-SNR₁ regime, revealing that knowledge of noise structure dramatically increases the value of high-quality data. On the algorithmic side, the LASSO in the agnostic setting exhibits the same phase transition threshold as the homogeneous-noise case (Wainwright, 2009), with the regularization condition depending only on σ_avg² — a striking robustness result showing that computational recovery is insensitive to data heterogeneity. This creates a fundamental gap: the information-theoretic threshold is sensitive to the noise distribution (via γ), while the algorithmic threshold is not, highlighting that the LASSO treats all samples equally regardless of quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical sparse recovery framework of Wainwright (2009) and Gamarnik & Zadik (2022) from homogeneous to heterogeneous (mixed-quality) noise. While prior work assumed a single noise variance (or could rescale to one), here the decoder faces two distinct noise levels and the authors introduce the 'Price of Quality' to quantify the trade-off between high- and low-quality samples. The information-theoretic analysis follows the Chernoff-bound/union-bound methodology of Gamarnik & Zadik (2022), while the LASSO threshold proof adapts Wainwright's (2009) first-order optimality argument using QR decomposition and Haar-measure properties to handle the non-scalar noise covariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals a fundamental asymmetry: the information-theoretic threshold is sensitive to data heterogeneity (price of quality can be unbounded in the informed setting), while the LASSO threshold is completely insensitive, depending only on the average noise level. This has practical implications for combining data from sources of differing quality (e.g., LLM-annotated vs. human-annotated labels). Open questions include whether the agnostic information-theoretic condition is tight, whether LASSO recovery in the informed setting exhibits a different price of quality, and whether these results extend to correlated designs or non-Gaussian noise.
