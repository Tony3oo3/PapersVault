## Problem

Existing negative preference optimization methods for diffusion models (e.g., Diff-NPO, CHATS) require training two separate networks—one on standard preferences and one on inverted preferences—doubling computational cost and introducing a distribution mismatch when weights are merged at inference, which dilutes the learned negative alignment signal. The paper asks whether explicit negative preference modeling can be achieved within a single unified diffusion network without these architectural and optimization compromises.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261624/cropped_page5_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method fine-tunes pretrained latent diffusion models (SD1.5 at 512×512 and SDXL at 1024×1024) using paired preference data (winner/loser image pairs with shared text prompts) from Pick-a-Pic v2. At inference, the single fine-tuned model takes a text prompt and produces a generated image via standard DDIM sampling with classifier-free guidance (scale 7.5, 50 steps), requiring no separate negative model or weight-merging step.

## Method

Diff-SNPO exploits the inherent dual-branch structure of CFG-enabled diffusion models by training the conditional branch on positive preferences and the unconditional (null-conditioned) branch on inverted (negative) preferences within a single shared network, eliminating the need for a second model and post-hoc weight merging. A key finding is that naively applying this opposing-preference scheme with standard Diff-DPO causes progressive blurring, because DPO's tendency to decrease both winner and loser likelihoods, combined with conflicting gradients across shared parameters, drives the model toward low-contrast outputs. To fix this, the paper adapts Bounded DPO (BDPO) to the diffusion setting, replacing the losing-sample log-likelihood with a mixture that retains a non-vanishing reference-policy contribution, thereby bounding the loser term and preserving winner likelihood throughout training. The final objective (Eq. 22) is a tractable upper bound derived via Hölder's inequality over the diffusion trajectory, applying the BDPO-corrected margin with branch-specific conditioning and preference flipping, yielding stable single-model negative preference optimization with 2× training speedup over dual-model baselines.

## Results

On SD1.5 (Pick-a-Pic v2), Diff-SNPO achieves HPSv2 27.23, Pick Score 22.24, Aesthetic Score 5.63, and ImageReward 0.69, outperforming all baselines on three of four metrics. On SDXL, Diff-SNPO reaches HPSv2 28.33 and Pick Score 22.69, matching or exceeding Diff-NPO while using only half the training and inference compute. The method also achieves the highest negative implicit accuracy (57.45%) among all methods, confirming stronger negative preference alignment without the weight-merging degradation seen in Diff-NPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Diff-DPO (Wallace et al., 2024), Diff-SNPO wins decisively on all four metrics for SD1.5 (e.g., ImageReward 0.69 vs 0.30, a 2.3× improvement) and on HPSv2 and Pick Score for SDXL, confirming that negative preference modeling adds substantial value beyond standard positive-only DPO. Against Diff-NPO (Wang et al., 2025), Diff-SNPO outperforms on every metric for SD1.5 (HPSv2 27.23 vs 26.92, ImageReward 0.69 vs 0.38) while requiring only half the training and inference compute, since it uses a single network instead of two. On SDXL the gap narrows: Diff-SNPO edges out Diff-NPO on HPSv2 (28.33 vs 28.30) and Pick Score (22.69 vs 22.67) but trails on Aesthetic Score (5.81 vs 5.94), which the authors attribute to the dataset's 'win' samples being less aesthetically pleasing than SDXL's already-strong base outputs. Against CHATS (Fu et al., 2025), Diff-SNPO wins on HPSv2, Pick Score, and ImageReward for SD1.5 but loses on Aesthetic Score (5.63 vs 5.68); on SDXL, CHATS retains a small ImageReward advantage (1.05 vs 1.01) while Diff-SNPO leads on HPSv2 and Pick Score. Overall, Diff-SNPO achieves the best or near-best alignment scores across both backbones while being 2× faster in training and inference than the dual-model baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads: negative preference optimization for diffusion models (Diff-NPO, CHATS) and the Bounded DPO stabilization technique (Cho et al., 2025). Rather than training two independent networks and merging their weights at inference as Diff-NPO does, Diff-SNPO repurposes the inherent conditional/unconditional dual-branch structure of CFG-enabled diffusion models, assigning positive preferences to the conditional branch and inverted (negative) preferences to the unconditional branch within a single network. The key methodological departure is the adaptation of BDPO's mixture-based loser term to the diffusion setting, which the authors show is necessary to prevent a blurring artifact that arises from conflicting gradients between the two branches when using standard DPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: Diff-SNPO halves both training memory and per-step compute relative to dual-model baselines while also doubling inference throughput, making negative preference optimization substantially more accessible for large-scale deployment. The diagnosis of the blurring artifact in naive single-model NPO (gradient interference between opposing branch updates resolved by parameter averaging) is a useful cautionary finding for anyone attempting similar unifications. A noted limitation is that on the stronger SDXL backbone, the aesthetic score lags slightly behind Diff-DPO, which the authors attribute to dataset bias rather than methodological weakness; whether this persists with better-curated preference data remains open. Generalization beyond text-to-image (e.g., video diffusion, where CFG is equally central) is an obvious next step not explored here.
