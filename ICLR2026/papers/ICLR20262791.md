## Problem

Existing disease progression models capture only population-level trends and rely on diffusion-based denoising that disrupts the inherently continuous, monotonic nature of disease dynamics. Moreover, standard autoencoder latent spaces do not guarantee cross-patient alignment or correlation with clinical severity, making trajectories uninterpretable. The paper addresses how to learn patient-specific, semantically structured latent trajectories that faithfully model individualized disease progression.

## Input / Output

The method takes a preprocessed baseline brain MRI (N4 bias-corrected, skull-stripped, resampled to 1.5 mm³, registered to a standard template), patient attributes (sex, age, clinical status), and a target future time gap T (in years). It outputs a predicted future brain MRI at that arbitrary time point and an interpretable latent trajectory where vector direction encodes patient identity and magnitude monotonically encodes disease severity. Unlike standard flow matching that normalizes time to [0,1], the temporal variable here directly represents the physical time gap, enabling prediction at any future horizon without retraining.

## Method

Δ-LFM operates in two stages. First, a 3D U-Net autoencoder is trained with a novel ArcRank loss that decomposes each patient's latent representations via SVD and enforces two constraints: angular consistency (all timepoints for the same patient align along a shared direction) and monotonic magnitude ordering (later scans have larger norms), with a pull term preventing excessive separation between adjacent timepoints. Second, a flow-matching velocity field (also a 3D U-Net) is trained to regress the empirical latent velocity (z_j − z_i)/(t_j − t_i) over the temporal interval [0, T], where T is the actual time gap rather than a normalized [0,1] range. At inference, the learned velocity is integrated via fixed-step Euler updates (dt = 0.01) to transport the baseline latent state forward to the target time, then decoded to produce the predicted MRI. Patient attributes and the target time are injected through AdaLN conditioning at each U-Net decoder layer, enabling the model to capture heterogeneous progression speeds across individuals.

## Results

On three Alzheimer's disease longitudinal MRI benchmarks (ADNI, AIBL, OASIS-3), Δ-LFM achieves the best image quality and structural fidelity across all metrics. On ADNI, it reaches 30.59 dB PSNR and 94.62 SSIM, exceeding the strongest baseline (MambaControl) by +0.87 dB and +1.0 SSIM points. More critically, it attains the lowest Δ-RMAE (0.436 on ADNI, 0.417 on AIBL, 0.473 on OASIS), representing ~21% relative error reduction over MambaControl on ADNI and AIBL, and ~16% on OASIS, confirming superior alignment with actual disease progression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Δ-LFM outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across all three datasets. The closest competitor is MambaControl (a ControlNet-based diffusion model), which Δ-LFM surpasses by 0.87 dB PSNR and 1.0 SSIM on ADNI, and by ~21% relative Δ-RMAE reduction on both ADNI (0.436 vs. 0.554) and AIBL (0.417 vs. 0.525). DiffuseMorph, the strongest deformation-based method, is notably weaker on Δ-RMAE (0.516 on ADNI vs. 0.436), indicating it captures ventricular changes but misses cortical progression. Among diffusion-based baselines, TADM (residual denoising) and BrLP (volumetric-ratio conditioning) both underperform Δ-LFM by a wide margin on Δ-RMAE (0.697 and 0.630 on ADNI, respectively), confirming that random denoising disrupts temporal continuity. ImageFlowNet, the only other flow-based method, trails Δ-LFM by 0.153 Δ-RMAE on ADNI, suggesting that the ArcRank latent alignment and [0,T] temporal sampling provide meaningful gains over generic flow matching. On AIBL, Δ-LFM ties DiffuseMorph on Region MAE (0.226) but still leads decisively on Δ-RMAE (0.417 vs. 0.482), highlighting that structural similarity alone does not capture progression fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reformulates conventional flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023; Dao et al., 2023) by replacing the normalized [0,1] interpolation interval with a clinically meaningful temporal range [0,T], and pairs it with a novel ArcRank loss that enforces patient-specific latent alignment via SVD-based angular and magnitude constraints. It departs from the dominant diffusion-based progression models (SADM, TADM, BrLP) by using a deterministic velocity-field formulation that preserves temporal continuity, and from prior personalization efforts (BrLP's volumetric-ratio conditioning) by learning an explicit patient trajectory axis in latent space. The time-encoding strategy follows TADM (Litrico et al., 2024), while the conditioning mechanism (AdaLN) draws from BrLP (Puglisi et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled way to generate interpretable, patient-specific disease trajectories at arbitrary future timepoints, and the emergent organization of the latent space by diagnostic status (CN/MCI/AD) without explicit supervision suggests the learned geometry captures clinically meaningful structure. The proposed Δ-RMAE metric addresses a real gap in longitudinal evaluation where standard PSNR/SSIM are inflated by shared anatomy. Key limitations include the assumption of linear latent trajectories and constant-velocity progression, restriction to Alzheimer's disease, and sensitivity to the ArcRank loss weight (λ_arc > 0.01 causes collapse). Open questions include generalization to rapidly progressing or treatment-altered diseases (e.g., brain tumors), modeling nonlinear progression dynamics, and multi-center harmonization.
