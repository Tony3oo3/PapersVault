## Problem

Long-horizon LLM inference is bottlenecked by the ever-growing KV cache, and existing memory-bounded strategies either incur high orchestration overhead (offloading, retrieval) or rely on attention-guided eviction heuristics that assume recent attention is a reliable proxy for future importance. This assumption breaks for long reasoning and generation tasks where a token may be critical much later despite low recent attention, and attention bias can cause premature eviction of needed tokens. The paper asks whether token importance can be learned intrinsically at creation time, independent of transient query states, to drive principled eviction under a fixed memory budget.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM with all backbone weights frozen and a text token sequence as input, augmenting each self-attention layer and KV head with a lightweight retention gate (a single-hidden-layer MLP of width 512 mapping d→512→h). At inference, each token receives a scalar retention score β∈[0,1] computed once at creation; the effective score at step t is β_i^(t−i), and when the cache exceeds budget M, the token with the lowest current score is evicted. The output is generated text under a strict, fixed KV memory budget, with the retention scores also serving as a diagnostic for layer- and head-specific functional roles.

## Method

TRIM-KV introduces retention-gated attention, which replaces the non-differentiable binary eviction indicator α with a smooth exponential decay β_i^(t−i), where β_i is predicted by a per-layer, per-head retention gate g(x_i) applied to the token's contextual embedding. This makes the attention computation differentiable and allows end-to-end training of all gates jointly. Training uses a two-part objective: a quality loss combining forward KL distillation from the frozen baseline LLM with standard next-token prediction, and a capacity loss that applies a hinge penalty on the sum of retention scores exceeding a target budget M at each step. Only the gate parameters are updated (parameter-efficient fine-tuning), while all other model weights remain frozen. At inference, the gates produce scores on the fly and eviction reduces to a simple argmin over current retention scores, adding negligible overhead and achieving ~2× higher decoding throughput than full-cache decoding at 32K context.

## Results

On math reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, GSM8K, MATH-500), TRIM-KV achieves a 198.4% relative improvement over attention-guided eviction baselines (R-KV, SnapKV) at the same KV budget, and a 58.9% pass@1 gain over the SOTA learnable retrieval baseline SeerAttn-R. On LongProc (Qwen3-4B, 2048 budget), TRIM-KV reaches 96.5 on Countdown-0.5K and 93.5 on Countdown-2K, matching or exceeding the full-cache model (96.0 and 90.0 respectively). On LongMemEval-S (32K budget), TRIM-KV scores 44.8 vs. 27.6–27.8 for StreamingLLM/SnapKV and 49.4 for full KV. On LongBench-V2 (Phi-3-mini-128K, 4096 budget), TRIM-KV achieves 34.09 average accuracy, surpassing full KV (28.79) by +18.41%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRIM-KV consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every benchmark. Against heuristic eviction methods (StreamingLLM, H2O, SnapKV, R-KV), the advantage is most dramatic in low-budget regimes: at 2048 KV budget on LongProc, TRIM-KV scores 96.5 on Countdown-0.5K versus 57.0 (SnapKV) and 88.5 (R-KV), and it surpasses these methods even when they are granted 4× more KV budget on math reasoning. Against the learnable retrieval baseline SeerAttn-R (Gao et al., 2025), TRIM-KV yields a 58.9% pass@1 gain at the same budget while operating in a stricter pure-eviction regime (no CPU-GPU offloading). Against LocRet (Huang et al., 2024) on LongBench-V2, TRIM-KV achieves +18.41% relative improvement over full KV while LocRet drops by −2.64%. Against the query-agnostic KeyDiff baseline (Park et al., 2025), TRIM-KV substantially outperforms, confirming that intrinsic token importance at creation time is a stronger eviction signal than key diversity alone. Notably, in several settings (e.g., Qwen3-4B on AIME24, Countdown-0.5K/2K), TRIM-KV even surpasses the full-cache model, suggesting selective retention acts as a regularizer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant attention-guided eviction paradigm (H2O, SnapKV, StreamingLLM, R-KV), which uses recent attention scores as a proxy for token importance, by instead learning each token's intrinsic importance at creation time via a lightweight retention gate. It also contrasts with prior learnable eviction methods (LocRet, NAACL) that target only the prefill stage, and with forgetting-transformer approaches (Forgetting Transformer, Titans) that require training from scratch. The exponential decay formulation draws on the Ebbinghaus forgetting curve from cognitive science, while the training recipe (distillation from a frozen LLM plus a capacity hinge loss) repurposes standard knowledge-distillation machinery for a plug-in, parameter-efficient fine-tuning setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TRIM-KV offers a practical, drop-in solution for memory-bounded LLM inference: it is FlashAttention-compatible, adds negligible per-token overhead, and achieves ~2× decoding throughput over full-cache while matching or exceeding full-cache accuracy in several settings. The observation that selective retention can act as a regularizer—suppressing noise from uninformative tokens and occasionally surpassing the full cache—is a useful conceptual insight for the field. Open questions include whether jointly training retention gates with attention layers (rather than on a frozen backbone) yields further gains, how to enable per-head variable-length caches under current FlashAttention assumptions, and whether the approach extends to multimodal or tool-calling settings.
