## Problem

Existing RL-based alpha mining frameworks suffer from three interconnected limitations: reward sparsity (feedback only upon completing a full formula), sequential token representations that ignore the hierarchical structure of mathematical expressions, and mode collapse toward a single optimal formula rather than the diverse, non-correlated portfolio that production trading requires. AlphaSAGE addresses all three by replacing the RL objective with a generative flow network trained under a dense, multi-faceted reward and a structure-aware graph encoder.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260110/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes cross-sectional panels of N assets × M raw features (OHLCV, VWAP, volume) over a historical training window, along with next-period returns as supervision. Each candidate alpha is parsed into an Abstract Syntax Tree (AST) with typed relational edges (unary, commutative binary, non-commutative binary, rolling-operator-to-feature, rolling-operator-to-window) that the RGCN encoder processes. The output is a diverse pool of formulaic alpha expressions (capped at MaxLen=20 tokens), which are then dynamically re-selected and linearly combined each period into a single tradable 'Mega-Alpha' portfolio signal.

## Method

AlphaSAGE replaces the standard RL sequential-decision framing with a Generative Flow Network that constructs alphas token-by-token over a DAG of partial ASTs, learning a forward policy whose sampling distribution is proportional to a composite reward rather than collapsing to a single mode. A Relational Graph Convolutional Network (RGCN) with six relation types encodes each partial AST into a structure-aware embedding that conditions the GFlowNet's next-token distribution, replacing the sequential LSTM/RPN representations used by prior work. Training uses the Trajectory Balance loss augmented with an entropy regularizer, and the reward combines three time-annealed components: terminal IC (predictive power), a Structure-Aware reward that penalizes mismatches between embedding proximity and behavioral distance among K-nearest alphas, and a Novelty reward that penalizes correlation with a dynamically updated library of previously discovered alphas. An early-stopping mechanism (probability proportional to current length over MaxLen) prevents unbounded expression growth while preserving syntactic validity.

## Results

On CSI300, AlphaSAGE achieves IC=0.079, ICIR=0.496, RIC=0.094, RICIR=0.583, AR=7.62%, MDD=−17.3%, and SR=1.71, ranking first on all seven metrics. On S&P500, it reaches IC=0.052, ICIR=0.493, AR=19.47%, MDD=−4.2%, and SR=6.32, again leading all baselines. Across CSI500 and CSI1000, AlphaSAGE attains the best or near-best values on most correlation and portfolio metrics, with the largest margins over RL-based baselines (AlphaGen, AlphaQCM) on ICIR and RICIR. Training time is also the lowest among learned methods: 0.49 h on CSI300 versus 1.34–1.72 h for AlphaGen/AlphaQCM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlphaSAGE outperforms all eight baselines on the majority of metrics across all four datasets. Against the strongest RL-based competitor AlphaGen (KDD 2023), AlphaSAGE improves IC by 36% on CSI300 (0.079 vs 0.058) and SR by 125% (1.71 vs 0.76), while also reducing MDD from −22.6% to −17.3%. The GAN-based AlphaForge (AAAI 2025) is the closest competitor on S&P500 (AR 17.24% vs 19.47%; SR 6.30 vs 6.32), but AlphaSAGE leads on every correlation metric there. On CSI500, the LLM-based AlphaAgent (KDD 2025) edges out AlphaSAGE on ICIR (0.396 vs 0.379), yet AlphaSAGE still wins on IC, RIC, RICIR, AR, MDD, and SR. On CSI1000, AlphaQCM (ICML 2025) achieves a higher RIC (0.107 vs 0.096), but AlphaSAGE dominates on IC, ICIR, RICIR, AR, MDD, and SR. Traditional ML baselines (MLP, LightGBM, XGBoost) and the GP method are consistently outperformed by 2–5× on IC and by large margins on portfolio metrics, confirming that learned generative approaches substantially outperform static or evolutionary search in this setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant RL-based paradigm for formulaic alpha mining (AlphaGen, AlphaQCM) by replacing sequential decision-making with Generative Flow Networks, which natively sample diverse candidates proportional to reward rather than collapsing to a single optimal mode. It further replaces the standard sequential (LSTM/Transformer) encoding of expressions with an RGCN operating on Abstract Syntax Trees, explicitly modeling relational structure between operators and features. The combination stage follows AlphaForge's dynamic linear re-weighting, and the training objective builds on the Trajectory Balance loss from Malkin et al. (2022), augmented with a multi-faceted reward (IC + structure-behavior alignment + novelty) and entropy regularization to address the reward-sparsity and mode-collapse problems that plague prior RL approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled solution to the diversity problem in alpha portfolios: by learning a global generative distribution over high-quality alphas rather than greedily constructing one portfolio, it produces structurally distinct, low-correlation factors that stabilize downstream OLS combination (formally supported by the condition-number analysis in Appendix F). A key open question is whether the IC-dominated reward generalizes across all market regimes—the authors themselves note (Appendix E.8) that short, turbulent subperiods can decouple cross-sectional predictability from realized P&L, suggesting that path-dependent or regime-aware reward components may be needed. Scalability to very large universes (beyond CSI1000/S&P500) and integration with real-time trading pipelines remain practical challenges, though the reported runtime (~0.5 h on CSI300) is competitive with existing systems.
