## Problem

Inference-time activation steering of LLMs requires identifying which internal modules (attention heads or layers) most strongly govern a target behavior, yet existing selection methods rely on linear probes, ad-hoc heuristics, or expensive cross-validation. Because behavior-relevant features in transformer activations are highly entangled and often not linearly separable, these approaches frequently select suboptimal or even harmful modules, leading to unstable generations, hallucinations, or unintended degradations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264406/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes last-token activations from each attention head (or layer) along with a behavior-contrastive dataset of query–answer pairs labeled as behavior-aligned or behavior-violating. It outputs a per-module AUC-ROC relevance score that ranks all heads/layers, and then modifies the standard additive steering equation by scaling each selected head's steering vector by its normalized relevance score (s/s_max), yielding importance-weighted interventions rather than uniform-strength ones.

## Method

For each module, REAL trains a vector-quantized autoencoder whose encoder projects the head activation into a lower-dimensional embedding partitioned into U semantic units, each quantized by nearest-neighbor search in a shared learnable codebook of size K. Training combines the standard VQ reconstruction, codebook, and commitment losses with a supervised contrastive loss on the quantized embeddings that pulls same-behavior samples together and pushes different-behavior samples apart, yielding a disentangled latent space where behavior-relevant and behavior-irrelevant factors occupy distinct codebook entries. After training, two lightweight class-conditional autoregressive GRU priors are fit over the discrete code sequences (one on positive, one on negative examples), and the log-likelihood ratio between them serves as a calibrated per-example behavior score. The AUC-ROC of these scores over a validation set gives the final module relevance ranking, and the top-S modules are selected for intervention with strength proportional to their normalized scores. This design captures sequential compositional dependencies among codes via the autoregressive prior, avoiding the linearity assumption that limits conventional probing.

## Results

On TRUTHFULQA (MC1/MC2), REAL_ITI achieves an average relative improvement of 20% over ITI across eight LLMs, with the largest gain of +81.5% on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (44.43 vs. 24.48 MC1) and +25.0% on Llama-7B (34.27 vs. 27.42 MC1). On knowledge-conflict QA (NQSWAP/MACNOISE, Llama3-8B), REAL outperforms the strongest baseline SPARE on all four metrics, reaching 80.17% contextual EM on NQSWAP and 92.84% on MACNOISE. In zero-shot cross-domain transfer (CLUTRR, MQuAKE), heads selected on TRUTHFULQA improve exact-match accuracy by 4.33 and 5.95 points over ITI respectively, without any additional tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REAL_ITI consistently outperforms ITI (Li et al., 2023a) across all seven models where both are reported, with gains ranging from +3.4% (Llama3.1-8B-Instruct MC2) to +81.5% (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct MC1); ITI notably degrades below the no-steering baseline on Qwen and Llama3.1, while REAL always improves. Against LoFiT (Yin et al., 2024), REAL_LoFiT yields modest but consistent gains of +2.0–4.1% on MC1/MC2 for Llama2-7B-Chat and Llama-7B, confirming that better head selection complements preference-tuned steering vectors. On knowledge-conflict QA, REAL surpasses SPARE (Zhao et al., 2025) on all four NQSWAP/MACNOISE metrics (e.g., +3.83% parametric, +3.19% contextual on NQSWAP) and also beats contrastive decoding methods (DoLa, CAD) and SAE-based methods (SEA). In zero-shot cross-domain transfer to CLUTRR and MQuAKE, REAL_ITI (51.33/36.40 EM) outperforms ITI (47.00/30.40) and REAL_LoFiT (63.00/42.20) outperforms LoFiT (59.78/40.52), demonstrating that the selected heads generalize beyond the training domain. On the four general alignment datasets (Corrigibility, Hallucination, Myopic Reward, Survival Instinct), steering the top-5 REAL-ranked layers with a +1 multiplier consistently increases target-behavior token probability (e.g., 0.504→0.546 for Hallucination), whereas the last-5 layers show weaker and less consistent effects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the vector-quantized autoencoder (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) — originally designed for discrete representation learning in vision and multimodal models — as a module-selection mechanism for LLM activation steering. It departs from the dominant linear-probe paradigm (ITI, LoFiT) and SAE-based approaches (SPARE) by learning a disentangled, quantized latent space per attention head via semantic-unit partitioning and a supervised contrastive loss, then scoring heads with autoregressive GRU priors over the resulting discrete codes. The adaptive, importance-weighted steering strength (scaling by normalized AUC) extends the standard fixed-strength intervention used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Module selection is a critical but underexplored bottleneck in activation steering, and REAL provides a principled, data-efficient alternative to linear probing that generalizes zero-shot across domains (TruthfulQA → MQuAKE/CLUTRR) and architectures (MHA and GQA). The finding that ITI-selected heads and REAL-selected heads have minimal overlap suggests linear probes are systematically missing behavior-relevant heads, which has implications for the broader interpretability literature. Open questions include scalability to 70B+ models, whether the VQ-AE disentangling insight transfers to non-attention modules (FFN layers), and whether the method can be made fully unsupervised to remove the need for behavior-contrastive training data.
