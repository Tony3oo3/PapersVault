## Problem

Existing physics benchmarks for LLMs (SciQ, ScienceQA, PHYBench, UGPhysics) cap at high-school or early-undergraduate difficulty and rely on binary scoring, leaving a gap in evaluating whether models can perform graduate-level, open-ended calculation in frontier condensed matter subfields such as superconductivity, strongly correlated systems, and topological states. Additionally, binary accuracy metrics cannot distinguish near-miss solutions (e.g., a missing symmetry factor) from completely wrong answers, obscuring the specific failure modes that matter for scientific reasoning.

## Input / Output

Each problem is an open-ended calculation question in condensed matter physics, presented as a natural-language statement with given symbols and a hint; the model must produce a complete step-by-step derivation and a final answer enclosed in a LaTeX \boxed{} environment, restricted to the symbols provided in the problem. The benchmark spans five answer types (tuple, equation, numeric, expression, interval) across six CMP domains, and the evaluation pipeline outputs a continuous SEED score (0–100), a binary expert-labeled accuracy flag, and a multi-label error-type classification (eight categories from Concept/Model Misuse to Redundant Content).

## Method

CMPhysBench comprises 520 questions manually authored and verified by PhD students and postdoctoral researchers from 17 standard graduate textbooks, with difficulty ranging from introductory undergraduate to advanced graduate level. The key evaluation contribution is the Scalable Expression Edit Distance (SEED) metric, which extends the original Expression Edit Distance (EED) from PHYBench in three directions: (1) answer-type unification via abstract syntax trees (ASTs), where equations are canonicalized by moving all terms to one side, tuples are scored component-wise by positional matching, intervals encode boundary openness symbolically, and numeric answers incorporate unit conversion, scientific-notation parsing, and relative-tolerance rounding; (2) expanded symbolic coverage including matrices, vectors, and inequalities canonicalized as f(·) # 0; and (3) robust LaTeX preprocessing that strips formatting wrappers, normalizes implicit multiplication, unifies Unicode symbols, and auto-balances parentheses to build reliable ASTs from noisy LLM outputs. The final score is computed as a piecewise function of the relative tree-edit distance r: 100 for exact match, 60 − 100r for 0 < r < 0.6 (linear partial credit), and 0 for r ≥ 0.6, yielding non-binary, interpretable partial credit. Error analysis is performed by prompting GPT-4o to classify incorrect responses into eight error categories, validated at 98% agreement against 300 manually annotated expert labels.

## Results

On CMPhysBench (520 graduate-level condensed matter physics problems), the best-performing model Grok-4 achieves a SEED score of 36.04 and 28.9% expert-labeled accuracy, followed by o3 (34.31 SEED) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (30.79 SEED). Most models cluster in the 20–28 SEED range (14–20% accuracy), while distilled/smaller variants (QwQ-32B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B) score only 15–17 SEED. For the evaluation metric comparison, SEED achieves a Spearman correlation of ρ=0.90 with human expert ratings, substantially outperforming EED (ρ=0.56), GPT-4o judgment (ρ=0.56), xVerify-9B-C (ρ=0.51), and OlympiadBench-rule (ρ=0.41).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between the proposed SEED metric and four alternative evaluation methods, measured by Spearman correlation with human expert binary labels. SEED (ρ=0.90) decisively outperforms EED from PHYBench (ρ=0.56), which struggles with equations, tuples, and intervals beyond simple expressions. GPT-4o-based LLM-as-a-judge also achieves only ρ=0.56, and xVerify-9B-C scores ρ=0.51, both failing to handle equivalent symbolic transformations common in CMP. OlympiadBench's rule-based metric performs worst at ρ=0.41, as its simplistic matching cannot account for structural or mathematical equivalence. SEED's advantage stems from its type-agnostic AST design, support for partial credit via tree-edit distance, and physics-aware normalization (unit conversion, scientific notation, rounding tolerance), making it the only metric that reliably distinguishes near-miss solutions from completely incorrect ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of physics benchmarks for LLMs (SciQ, ScienceQA, PHYBench, UGPhysics, GPQA) by narrowing to a single subfield—condensed matter physics—at graduate level, and by replacing binary or expression-only scoring with a structure-aware metric. The SEED metric is a direct extension of PHYBench's Expression Edit Distance (EED), adding support for equations, tuples, intervals, unit conversion, and scientific notation via SymPy-based AST tree-edit distance. The answer-type taxonomy follows OlympiadBench, and the automated error categorization is validated against xVerify-style expert annotation, positioning the work at the intersection of domain-specific benchmarking and fine-grained symbolic evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark quantifies a substantial capability gap: even the strongest model (Grok-4) reaches only 36/100 SEED and ~29% accuracy, and the dominant error mode is conceptual/model misuse (40–50%) rather than algebraic mistakes, suggesting that domain knowledge—not raw mathematical reasoning—is the bottleneck. The observation that long-chain-of-thought reasoning models do not consistently outperform general models on these problems challenges the assumption that extended deliberation always helps in knowledge-intensive domains. Open questions include whether targeted fine-tuning on CMP corpora can close the conceptual gap, whether SEED generalizes to other STEM subfields, and whether the mathematical-vs-physical reasoning gap identified here persists as models scale.
