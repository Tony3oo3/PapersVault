## Problem

Existing domain generalization methods predominantly rely on local flatness (e.g., sharpness-aware minimization) to improve out-of-domain performance, but recent theoretical and empirical work shows flatness does not universally guarantee better generalization. This paper identifies mode connectivity—a global geometric property where distinct local minima with vastly different out-of-domain accuracies are linked by continuous low-loss pathways—as an underexploited structure for DG, and addresses the curse of dimensionality that prevents conventional optimizers from effectively traversing these pathways in high-dimensional parameter spaces.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained CLIP ViT backbones (ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14) or ResNet50, consuming RGB images from unseen target domains and producing class labels. BOA is applied as a post-training refinement step after an ERM or SAM warm-up, using only visual prompt tuning (a small set of learnable prompt tokens) rather than full encoder fine-tuning. Evaluation follows the DomainBed leave-one-domain-out protocol across five benchmarks (PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome, TerraIncognita, DomainNet), with model selection performed on a test-domain validation set rather than a standard source-domain split.

## Method

BOA navigates mode connectivity pathways by iteratively performing two physics-inspired operations within a bounded 'billiard table' (a sub-level set of training loss): (1) line search to locate the loss contour boundary along a current direction, and (2) specular reflection using the negative normalized gradient as the surface normal to redirect the trajectory while preserving momentum magnitude. To overcome the curse of dimensionality, BOA constrains all operations to a low-dimensional Krylov subspace K_K = span{g, Hg, ..., H^{K-1}g} (K=5–20) constructed from training gradients and Hessian-vector products (approximated via finite differences). A key empirical finding is that oracle test gradients exhibit strong cosine alignment with this training-derived Krylov subspace, enabling the initial incident direction p_0 = -Σ H^k g to approximate the negative test gradient without access to test data. The reflection operator is further projected into the Krylov subspace to keep the trajectory within this reduced space, and the best model along the resulting trajectory is selected via a validation set.

## Results

On the DomainBed benchmark with a ViT-B/16 backbone and visual prompt tuning, BOA achieves an average out-of-domain accuracy of 78.1% across five datasets (VLCS, PACS, OfficeHome, TerraIncognita, DomainNet), surpassing the previous best method DISAM (76.5%) by 1.6%. The largest single-dataset gain is on VLCS, where BOA reaches 86.5% versus SAM's 82.9% (+3.6%). The advantage is consistent across backbone scales: BOA improves over SAM by 2.4% (ViT-B/32), 1.9% (ViT-B/16), and 1.4% (ViT-L/14) in average accuracy, and also outperforms SAM by 1.6% on a ResNet50 CNN backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BOA outperforms all 15 baselines on every individual dataset and in average accuracy. Against the strongest sharpness-aware baselines, BOA beats DISAM (76.5% avg) by 1.6%, SAGM (76.4%) by 1.7%, and SAM (76.2%) by 1.9%; the gap widens to 3.6% on VLCS specifically (86.5% vs. SAM's 82.9%). Against classical DG methods, BOA leads IRM (75.6%) by 2.5%, DANN (75.1%) by 3.0%, CORAL (76.0%) by 2.1%, and IIB (76.0%) by 2.1%. The largest absolute margin is over the plain ERM baseline (73.4%), where BOA gains 4.7% in average accuracy. Notably, BOA achieves these results using only visual prompt tuning (not full fine-tuning), while several baselines marked with a star in the original table involve full encoder fine-tuning, making the efficiency advantage particularly striking. The gains are most pronounced on the hardest domain (TerraIncognita: 60.3% vs. 56.2% for SAM, +4.1%) and least on the easiest (PACS: 97.4% vs. 97.1% for DISAM, +0.3%), suggesting BOA is most beneficial under severe domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant flatness-seeking paradigm in domain generalization (SAM, SWAD, GSAM, DISAM) by shifting focus to a global geometric property—mode connectivity—where distinct local minima are linked by continuous low-loss pathways. It extends SAM's implicit two-dimensional Krylov subspace (K₂ = {g, Hg}) to a K-dimensional Krylov subspace (K_K = {g, Hg, …, H^{K-1}g}) and introduces a physics-inspired traversal algorithm (BOA) that navigates these pathways via line search and reflection. The key conceptual departure is the empirical discovery that test gradients strongly align with training-derived Krylov subspaces, providing a 'free lunch' for approximating oracle directions without test data access. Prior mode connectivity work (Garipov et al., Draxler et al.) established the phenomenon but did not exploit it for out-of-distribution generalization; this paper is the first to do so.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Krylov alignment finding offers a practical geometric bridge between training and test distributions that could inform other OOD generalization settings beyond DG, and the BOA framework provides an architecture-agnostic post-training optimization step compatible with parameter-efficient tuning. However, the method requires a test-domain validation set for model selection (acknowledged as a practical compromise), and the oracle test gradient experiments reveal a performance ceiling that the Krylov approximation only partially reaches. Open questions include whether the alignment holds under more extreme distribution shifts, in non-visual modalities, or with full fine-tuning rather than VPT, and whether the billiard dynamics can be adapted to dynamic or streaming domain shift scenarios.
