## Problem

Prioritized Experience Replay (PER) samples transitions in proportion to their absolute temporal difference error (TDE), assuming larger TDEs indicate higher learning potential. However, the TDE is a biased proxy because the bootstrapped target Q-value itself is an approximation; when future state estimates are inaccurate, high-TDE transitions may carry misleading targets that degrade rather than improve value estimates. No existing prioritized sampling scheme accounts for the reliability of the target, leaving a gap between TDE magnitude and actual learning utility.

## Input / Output

The method consumes transitions (state, action, reward, next state, done flag) stored in a replay buffer of size N, along with their current absolute TDEs and an episode positional encoding vector φ that maps each transition to its parent trajectory. This positional encoding is essential because the reliability score requires the sum of TDEs across the full episode, which is only defined for terminated episodes; for ongoing episodes, a conservative estimate using the maximum episodic TDE sum in the buffer is substituted. The output is a mini-batch of k transitions sampled with probability proportional to the reliability-adjusted criterion Ψ_t, each accompanied by an importance-sampling weight w_t that corrects the non-i.i.d. bias introduced by non-uniform sampling.

## Method

ReaPER introduces a reliability score R_t = 1 − (Σ_{i=t+1}^n δ_i^+ / Σ_{i=1}^n δ_i^+) that quantifies the fraction of an episode's total TDE resolved before transition t, capturing the intuition that targets for later transitions (closer to terminal states) are more trustworthy. The sampling criterion Ψ_t = R_t^ω · δ_t^{+α} multiplies the TDE magnitude by a reliability weight, so transitions with large TDEs but unreliable downstream targets are down-weighted. Theoretically, under the assumption that target bias is bounded by the sum of downstream absolute TDEs, the paper proves a convergence hierarchy (Uniform ≥ PER ≥ ReaPER in expected squared Q-value error) and shows that ReaPER approximates the optimal inverse-variance-weighted sampling distribution. The full algorithm integrates priority updates (recomputing TDEs and reliabilities whenever a transition or its episode-mates are trained on), regularization exponents α and ω to dampen extreme values, and weighted importance sampling with exponent β to maintain unbiased gradient estimates, all within a standard DDQN training loop.

## Results

On continuous control environments, ReaPER reaches predefined score thresholds 16.6%–32.6% faster than PER and 25.0%–41.4% faster than uniform replay (CARTPOLE: 32.6%/41.4%, ACROBOT: 16.6%/25.0%, LUNARLANDER: 21.1%/37.1%). On the ATARI-10 benchmark, ReaPER achieves a 22.97% higher median peak score than PER and a 229.78% higher median peak score than uniform replay, winning 8 of 10 games and tying 2. Under partial observability (single-frame observations), the median improvement over PER widens to 34.98%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReaPER is compared against PER (Schaul et al., 2015) and uniform experience replay across all environments. Against PER, ReaPER consistently wins: it reaches score thresholds faster in all three continuous control environments (16.6%–32.6% fewer steps) and achieves a 22.97% higher median peak score on ATARI-10, winning 8 of 10 individual games and tying 2. Against uniform replay, the margins are substantially larger (25.0%–41.4% in continuous control; 229.78% on ATARI-10), confirming that both prioritized strategies outperform random sampling. Under partial observability, ReaPER's advantage over PER grows to 34.98%, suggesting the reliability adjustment is particularly beneficial when the agent's value estimates are less stable. The paper notes that ReaPER achieved these gains with minimal hyperparameter tuning (α=0.4, ω=0.2), and that further tuning of α, ω, β, and learning rate may yield additional improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Prioritized Experience Replay (PER, Schaul et al. 2015) by identifying a fundamental limitation: absolute TDE is a biased proxy for learning potential when bootstrapped target Q-values are themselves inaccurate. The key conceptual departure is introducing a reliability score derived from downstream TDEs within the same episode, which down-weights transitions whose targets are likely misleading. The theoretical analysis builds on standard Q-learning convergence decompositions but replaces global exploration assumptions with a local bias bound (Assumption 3.4) linking target bias to unresolved downstream TDEs, making the analysis more applicable to practical deep RL. The paper positions itself against several alternative prioritization strategies (entropy-based, reward-based, sequence-based, learning-based) but notes none have displaced PER in state-of-the-art algorithms, making PER the natural baseline to improve upon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since PER is embedded in virtually all state-of-the-art off-policy RL algorithms (Rainbow, Ape-X, MuZero, Agent57, EfficientZero), a principled improvement to its sampling criterion has broad practical relevance. The convergence hierarchy (Uniform ≥ PER ≥ ReaPER) and variance-reduction result provide formal justification beyond empirical gains. Key limitations include reliance on terminal states for meaningful reliability computation, O(N) overhead for episodic TDE-sum updates, and evaluation restricted to DDQN with finite-horizon episodes. Open questions include extension to infinite-horizon settings, actor-critic methods, and whether adaptive or learned reliability estimates could further reduce the computational cost.
