## Problem

Existing novel view synthesis methods either rely solely on RGB frames, which limits robustness under rapid camera motion due to sparse and under-constrained training views, or exploit event data through per-scene optimization (NeRF/3DGS), undermining scalability to unseen environments. No prior work achieves generalizable, optimization-free novel view synthesis that jointly leverages the complementary strengths of asynchronous event streams and sparse RGB imagery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263142/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 2, 4, or 6 sparse RGB frames, a continuous event stream captured between them, and an arbitrary novel view camera trajectory as input. RGB frames are first processed by an off-the-shelf MVS model (VGGT) to obtain camera parameters and per-pixel depths, then projected into each target frustum; the event stream is converted into adaptively sliced voxel grids (short slices of m events + long slices of 2m events, with m sampled from [1×10⁵, 3×10⁵]). The output is a 49-frame photorealistic video at 384×672 resolution along the specified trajectory, generated in a single forward pass without per-scene optimization.

## Method

EA3D comprises two components: an Event-Augmented Feature Renderer (EA-Renderer) and a 3D-aware video diffusion model. The EA-Renderer operates in three stages: (1) appearance features are extracted by projecting RGB frames into target frustums and encoding with CogVideoX's 3D VAE encoder; (2) event features are extracted via adaptive temporal slicing of the event stream into overlapping short/long voxel grids, encoded by a 4-layer 3D convolutional network; (3) a Perceiver cross-attention module fuses the unposed event features (as keys/values) with the posed appearance features (as queries) to produce view-conditioned 3D features. The diffusion model is an adapted CogVideoX DiT architecture where the original single-image condition is replaced by the rendered 3D feature volume, which is patch-embedded and concatenated with Gaussian noise for iterative denoising. Training is end-to-end on the newly introduced Event-DL3DV dataset (10K scenes with simulated events at randomized contrast thresholds and resolutions) using a combined diffusion loss and a reconstruction loss that aligns EA-Renderer outputs with ground-truth VAE features, enabling the model to generalize to real event streams without per-scene optimization.

## Results

On the most challenging 2-view setting, EA3D achieves 22.82 PSNR / 0.732 SSIM / 0.251 LPIPS on DL3DV and 23.50 PSNR / 0.756 SSIM / 0.218 LPIPS on Tanks-and-Temples, outperforming all baselines by 3.7–6.0 dB in PSNR. On real event data (DSEC, 2-view), EA3D reaches 24.89 PSNR / 0.792 SSIM / 0.211 LPIPS, a 6.2 dB PSNR margin over the next-best method (ViewCrafter at 18.71). Under fast-motion conditions (M3ED), EA3D achieves 22.05 PSNR versus 18.36 for the event-based frame interpolation baseline VDM-EVFI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EA3D consistently outperforms all five baselines across every metric and view setting. Against the optimization-based event methods, EA3D gains +4.81 dB PSNR over E-NeRF and +5.98 dB over Event3DGS on DL3DV (2-view), while also being optimization-free (0.03 h inference vs. 3.62 h for E-NeRF). Against the RGB-only generalizable baselines, EA3D leads ViewCrafter by +3.72 dB PSNR and NVS-Solver by +5.07 dB on DL3DV (2-view), with the gap widening to +6.18 dB and +6.21 dB respectively on real DSEC event data. On the M3ED fast-motion benchmark, EA3D surpasses the event-based frame interpolation method VDM-EVFI by +3.69 dB PSNR (22.05 vs. 18.36), confirming that event-augmented 3D diffusion generalizes beyond the event camera trajectory. The advantage is most pronounced in the 2-view setting where appearance overlap is minimal, and it persists (though narrows slightly) at 4 and 6 views.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: event-augmented novel view synthesis (E-NeRF, Event3DGS, EF-3DGS), which achieves high fidelity under fast motion but requires per-scene optimization, and generalizable diffusion-based NVS (ViewCrafter, NVS-Solver), which avoids optimization but relies solely on RGB and degrades under large baselines. EA3D departs from both by adapting CogVideoX's DiT-based video diffusion backbone to condition on 3D features rendered by a novel EA-Renderer that fuses posed RGB appearance features with unposed event geometry features via Perceiver cross-attention. The adaptive event slicing strategy and the use of a 3D VAE encoder as the appearance encoder are practical design choices that bridge the domain gap between the event encoder and the pretrained diffusion weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being the first to make event-augmented NVS generalizable (no per-scene optimization), EA3D opens the door to deploying event cameras in fast-motion 3D reconstruction pipelines (autonomous driving, aerial robotics) without the per-scene fitting cost that has limited prior event-based methods. The Event-DL3DV dataset is a practical resource that the community can reuse for training and benchmarking event-augmented 3D models. Key limitations remain: inference is still diffusion-speed (49 frames in one forward pass but non-trivial compute), the pipeline depends on an upstream MVS model (VGGT) for camera poses and depths, and the method has only been validated on static scenes—dynamic or non-rigid scenes remain an open question. Whether the trajectory-agnostic robustness demonstrated here extends to truly unconstrained, multi-object dynamic environments is untested.
