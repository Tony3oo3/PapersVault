## Problem

Vision-language models generate hundreds to thousands of visual tokens per image, most of which are redundant, yet existing compression methods face a fundamental trade-off: attention-guided selection preserves salient regions but retains redundant duplicates and discards background context, while duplication-aware pruning improves diversity but may eliminate semantically critical high-attention tokens. No prior training-free method simultaneously optimizes both importance preservation and information diversity at high compression ratios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261763/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the visual token embedding matrix X ∈ R^{T×D} produced by a pre-trained vision encoder (e.g., 576 tokens from CLIP-ViT for LLaVA-1.5 at 336×336, or up to 2880 tokens for LLaVA-NeXT with multi-part encoding), and outputs a compressed token sequence X̃ ∈ R^{N×D} with N ≪ T (e.g., 64, 128, or 192 tokens). Compression is applied at the vision encoder stage before the LLM, making it a plug-and-play early-compression module. An optional dynamic mechanism adjusts N per image based on a global redundancy score, so complex scenes retain more tokens while uniform backgrounds are compressed more aggressively, keeping the average token budget fixed.

## Method

PRUNESID is a training-free, two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (PSCA) redefines PCA to decompose along the token dimension rather than the feature dimension: after sigmoid rescaling and mean-centering, a low-rank SVD of the transposed token matrix yields K principal semantic directions, and each token is assigned to the direction with its largest absolute projection, forming K semantically coherent groups. Stage 2 (intra-group NMS) ranks tokens within each group by their projection score and greedily suppresses any token whose cosine similarity to an already-selected token exceeds an adaptive threshold τ = λ·ρ, where ρ is the image-level mean pairwise similarity and λ = N/32. A theoretical analysis via the Inclusion-Exclusion Principle shows PSCA maximizes the total semantic information term while NMS bounds the pairwise redundancy term, jointly approximating a constrained maximization of effective information. An information-aware dynamic compression ratio (φ = 1 − ρ) further allocates the token budget proportionally to each image's semantic diversity, improving average performance across heterogeneous datasets.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5 with only 64 tokens retained (11.1% of the original 576), PRUNESID-Dyn achieves 96.3% average accuracy across 11 benchmarks, surpassing VisionZip (94.0%) and HiRED (88.4%). At extreme compression on LLaVA-NeXT (160 tokens, 5.6% retention), PRUNESID maintains 92.8% average accuracy, a 2.5-point improvement over VisionZip (90.3%). On Video-LLaVA with 6.6% token retention, PRUNESID reaches 95.5% average accuracy versus VisionZip's 93.2%. The method also achieves 7.8× faster prefilling (27.8 ms vs. 218 ms) on LLaVA-NeXT 7B while improving POPE F1 from 86.6% to 89.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRUNESID outperforms all eight baselines across every tested architecture and compression level. Against the strongest early-compression baseline VisionZip, PRUNESID gains +1.9% average accuracy at 64 tokens on LLaVA-1.5 (95.9% vs. 94.0%), +2.5% at 160 tokens on LLaVA-NeXT (92.8% vs. 90.3%), and +2.3% on Video-LLaVA (95.5% vs. 93.2%). Attention-guided methods (HiRED, SparseVLM, FastV) suffer the largest drops under aggressive compression—HiRED falls to 88.4% and FastV to 76.3% at 64 tokens—because they discard background context. Duplication-aware methods (DART, DivPrune) perform better than attention-guided baselines but still trail PRUNESID by 1.3–3.3% at 64 tokens, confirming that similarity-only pruning loses semantically critical high-attention tokens. On Qwen2-VL 7B, PRUNESID beats PACT by +0.8% to +4.6% across retention ratios, and on Qwen2.5-VL it surpasses VisionZip by +1.6% to +2.2%, including on high-resolution benchmarks (HRBench-8k, XLRS) where it preserves 86.5–98.3% of baseline performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two established paradigms for training-free visual token compression in VLMs: attention-guided selection (SparseVLM, FastV, HiRED, VisionZip) and similarity-based redundancy pruning (DART, DivPrune). It departs from both by introducing a PCA-driven semantic grouping stage (PSCA) that operates on the token dimension rather than the feature dimension, followed by intra-group NMS inspired by object detection, thereby explicitly decoupling importance scoring from redundancy elimination. The dynamic compression ratio mechanism further extends the static-budget assumption shared by all prior methods, allocating tokens per-image based on a global redundancy score.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7.8× prefilling speedup at 5.6% token retention with 92.8% accuracy on LLaVA-NeXT makes aggressive compression practically viable for deployment-constrained settings, and the training-free, architecture-agnostic design (validated on LLaVA-1.5/NeXT, Mini-Gemini, Qwen2-VL, Video-LLaVA) lowers adoption barriers. A key open question is the acknowledged limitation on fine-grained or instruction-specific queries, where redundancy pruning within a salient region can discard locally critical details; the authors suggest task-adaptive or instruction-aware filtering as a natural next step. The dynamic compression ratio's benefit (0.4–1.0%) is modest and concentrated on high-variance benchmarks, leaving open whether more sophisticated per-region or per-query budget allocation could yield larger gains.
