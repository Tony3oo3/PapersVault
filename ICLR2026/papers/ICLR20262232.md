## Problem

Existing evaluations of object hallucination in MLLMs rely on static benchmarks and curated image sets, which constrain analysis to fixed visual scenarios and fail to expose model-specific or unanticipated vulnerabilities. There is no systematic, fully automatic method that actively generates images designed to induce hallucination while guaranteeing the target object remains absent, making it difficult to determine whether hallucination errors are isolated or indicative of deeper structural failure modes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262232/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GHOST takes an RGB input image (336×336 baseline) that does not contain a specified target object, along with a binary query prompt (e.g., 'Do you see a knife in the image?'). It produces a visually similar image that causes the MLLM to answer 'Yes' to the query, verified by an open-vocabulary object detector (OWLv2) to confirm the target object is genuinely absent. The optimization operates entirely in the CLIP embedding space, and the final image is decoded via Stable Diffusion unCLIP starting from a partially noised latent of the original image, preserving high-level structure while introducing subtle misleading cues.

## Method

GHOST introduces a lightweight MLP mapper that projects CLIP image embeddings into the MLLM's vision-token space, decoupling the optimization loop from the diffusion generator and enabling efficient gradient-based search in embedding space. The optimization minimizes a joint objective combining three terms: an adversarial loss that maximizes the MLLM's probability of outputting 'Yes' (via the mapper), a CLIP cosine-similarity penalty against a compositional text embedding of the target object (preventing direct object encoding), and an L2 regularization toward the original image's CLIP embedding (preserving semantics). Once the optimized embedding exceeds a confidence threshold, Stable Diffusion unCLIP generates the final image by denoising a partially noised latent of the original image conditioned on the optimized embedding, with up to 4 generation attempts per sample. This decoupled design is ~5× faster than prior methods like DASH, achieving 28–32% hallucination success rates on Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-v1.6, and GLM-4.1V-Thinking, and revealing transferable vulnerabilities across models (e.g., 66.5% success on GPT-4o from Qwen-optimized images).

## Results

GHOST achieves hallucination success rates of 29.9% on Qwen2.5-VL, 28.1% on LLaVA-v1.6, and 32.4% on GLM-4.1V-Thinking on COCO, compared to 0.1% for DASH on the same dataset. Transferability is strong: images optimized for Qwen2.5-VL induce hallucinations in GPT-4o at 66.5% and in GLM-4.1V at 72.0%. On image quality, GHOST achieves the best semantic fidelity FID (25.00 for Qwen) while remaining competitive in distributional realism (47.03 vs. 46.19 for SD v2.1). Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL on GHOST images improves POPE accuracy from 88.7 to 93.2 and reduces CHAIRs from 3.8 to 2.9, with minimal impact on VQAv2 (89.5→89.4) and captioning (72.8→71.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DASH (Augustin et al., 2025), the closest prior method that also incorporates MLLM feedback for image generation, GHOST achieves a roughly 300× higher success rate on COCO (29.9% vs. 0.1% for Qwen2.5-VL), despite operating on a much smaller input pool (9,423 vs. 118,000 images). DASH requires the MLLM, diffusion model, generated image, and object detector all in the optimization loop, making it ~5× slower per sample; GHOST decouples optimization from generation via a CLIP-embedding-space mapper, achieving ~10 s/sample on a single A100. Against the diffusion baselines (Stable Diffusion v2.1 and SD unCLIP) in Table 2, GHOST matches or slightly trails on distributional realism FID (47.03 vs. 46.19/46.51 for Qwen) but substantially outperforms on semantic fidelity FID (25.00 vs. 41.71/31.67), confirming that GHOST preserves the original image's identity while introducing only subtle misleading cues. Human evaluation further validates that 89% of annotators confirm the target object is absent in GHOST images, comparable to DASH's reported 87%.

## Relation to Prior Work

GHOST builds on Stable Diffusion unCLIP for embedding-conditioned image generation and departs from DASH (Augustin et al., 2025), the closest prior work, by decoupling optimization from the diffusion pipeline: rather than backpropagating through the MLLM, diffusion model, and object detector simultaneously, GHOST optimizes a single CLIP embedding and uses a lightweight MLP mapper to bridge it to the MLLM's vision encoder space. This contrasts with pixel-space adversarial attacks (AnyAttack, AttackVLM) that preserve image semantics, and with prompt-based synthesis methods (AutoHallusion, ImageNet-D) that lack model-specific feedback. The design is also informed by DEEM's observation that diffusion models learn more robust, distribution-aligned representations than discriminative encoders, motivating the use of diffusion as the image generator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 300× improvement in hallucination discovery rate over DASH (28–32% vs. 0.1%) makes systematic stress-testing of MLLMs practically feasible, and the cross-model transferability (66.5% on GPT-4o from Qwen-optimized images) suggests these are shared architectural vulnerabilities rather than idiosyncratic bugs. The fine-tuning mitigation demonstrates GHOST's dual role as both diagnostic and corrective tool. Key limitations include the dependency on unCLIP-style embedding conditioning (excluding many popular diffusion architectures) and the focus on object-centric hallucinations; extending to broader hallucination modalities and more diverse generative backends remains open.
