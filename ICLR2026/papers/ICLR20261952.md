## Problem

Existing video-queried sound separation (VQSS) methods rely on semantic cues and time-frequency masking, which fail under homogeneous interference (e.g., multiple same-category sources) and overlapping soundtracks, producing spectral holes and temporal misalignment. No prior work has explored generative VQSS, and the unique multi-conditioned nature of the task (jointly conditioned on noisy audio and video) introduces challenges absent from standard flow-matching setups.

## Input / Output

The model takes an 8-second mixed audio clip (16 kHz, 80-bin mel-spectrogram) and a 4 FPS video sequence as input. Audio is compressed via a 1D VAE into a 20-dimensional latent (shape 20×256), while video is encoded by CAVP into 512-dimensional temporal features that are expanded to match the audio time axis before concatenation. Output is a separated audio waveform reconstructed through the VAE decoder and a BigVGAN vocoder, with 25 ODE sampling steps as the recommended operating point (~2.17 FPS throughput).

## Method

AlignSep is the first generative VQSS framework built on conditional flow matching, training a 4-layer feedforward Transformer (576 hidden dim, 158.94M params) to estimate the transport vector field from mixed-audio latents to clean-audio latents, conditioned on temporally-aligned video features. The key architectural choice is a concatenation-based temporal fusion: video features are expanded to the audio time dimension and concatenated with audio latents (plus timestep encoding), rather than using cross-attention, which ablations show is critical for capturing fine-grained temporal correspondence under homogeneous interference. The video encoder (CAVP) incorporates temporal synchronization supervision to capture dynamic cross-modal correlations beyond static semantics. The paper provides an in-depth analysis showing that VQSS's multi-conditioned structure (jointly conditioned on noisy audio and video) makes the posterior highly multi-modal and piecewise non-smooth, rendering rectified-flow acceleration suboptimal; classifier-free guidance (scale 4.5) is used to balance quality and diversity during sampling.

## Results

On VGGSound-Clean, AlignSep achieves 73.38 S_A-A, 27.89 S_A-V, and 96.88 T_A-V, substantially outperforming the best baseline (†Davis-flow) on temporal alignment (96.88 vs. 82.32) and semantic consistency. On the more challenging VGGSound-Hard benchmark (homogeneous interference), AlignSep reaches 95.76 T_A-V, a 10.17-point margin over the strongest baseline (CLIPSep at 85.59). On Music-Clean, AlignSep leads in S_A-A (72.28) and S_A-V (28.92) but trails OmniSep slightly on T_A-V (66.67 vs. 68.89). In MOS evaluation, AlignSep attains the highest Overall Score on VGGSound-Clean (4.31) and VGGSound-Hard (4.43), and the best AVC on all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlignSep outperforms all five baselines on VGGSound-Clean across all three metrics, with the largest gap in T_A-V (96.88 vs. 82.32 for †Davis-flow, a 14.56-point improvement). On VGGSound-Hard, where homogeneous interference demands temporal reasoning, AlignSep's 95.76 T_A-V exceeds the best baseline (CLIPSep, 85.59) by over 10 points, while OmniSep and †Davis-flow drop to 76.27, revealing their inability to model fine-grained temporal correspondence. On Music-Clean, AlignSep leads in semantic scores (S_A-A 72.28, S_A-V 28.92) but is marginally behind OmniSep on T_A-V (66.67 vs. 68.89), suggesting that for semantically diverse music sources, cross-attention-based semantic alignment remains competitive. In MOS evaluation, AlignSep achieves the highest Overall Score on VGGSound-Clean (4.31) and VGGSound-Hard (4.43), while OmniSep edges it out on Noise Residuals for Music-Clean (4.09 vs. 3.82). The retrained †Davis and †Davis-flow baselines consistently underperform the original VQSS methods, indicating that dataset-specific training does not fully compensate for architectural limitations in temporal modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video-queried sound separation (VQSS) line of work (CLIPSep, i-Query, OmniSep) by replacing the dominant mask-based discriminative paradigm with a conditional flow-matching generative framework, building directly on the flow matching formulation of Lipman et al. (2022). It repurposes the CAVP temporal visual encoder from Diff-Foley (Luo et al., 2023) and a 1D VAE latent space from AudioLDM (Liu et al., 2023a), but departs from prior VQSS methods by treating audio-visual temporal alignment as a first-class modeling objective rather than an implicit byproduct of semantic matching. The introduction of VGGSound-Hard also extends the evaluation landscape beyond the existing VGGSound-Clean and MUSIC-Clean benchmarks, which only test cross-category separation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The generative formulation addresses a concrete failure mode of mask-based VQSS (spectral holes under overlapping same-category sources) and the VGGSound-Hard benchmark provides the field with a much-needed stress test for temporal alignment under homogeneous interference. The finding that rectified flow acceleration degrades performance substantially in this multi-conditioned setting (S_A-V: 57.36 vs. 73.64) challenges the assumption that trajectory straightening is universally beneficial and suggests that iterative correction loops remain important for tasks with highly multi-modal posteriors. Open questions include scalability beyond 8-second clips and 4 FPS, whether the concat-over-cross-attention finding generalizes to other multi-conditioned generative tasks, and whether the 25-step sweet spot holds at higher resolutions or longer sequences.
