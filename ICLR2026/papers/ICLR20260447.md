## Problem

VLM-based HOI detectors face a persistent trade-off: architectures optimized for fully supervised benchmarks generalize poorly to zero-shot and open-vocabulary settings, while zero-shot-oriented methods underperform under full supervision. A second challenge is supervision sparsity—ground-truth annotations cover only a small fraction of the densely connected human-object pairs in a scene, leaving valid unlabeled pairs and informative negatives unexploited during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260447/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image and a set of detected bounding boxes (humans and objects) from any off-the-shelf detector; it does not require detector-specific intermediate features, only box coordinates and VLM feature maps (ROI-aligned at 7×7 resolution). Output is a set of HOI triplets ⟨human box, verb logits, object box, object class⟩, where interaction scores are computed per human-object pair and combined with detection confidence via a log-odds fusion. The plug-and-play design means swapping the upstream detector (DETR, YOLO-World, Grounding-DINO, Qwen3-VL) requires no retraining of the HOI module.

## Method

LINK introduces a two-component architecture: a Human-Object Geometrical Encoder that encodes pairwise spatial relations via sinusoidal positional encodings of normalized box centers/sizes and a Multi-Modal Fusion module, and a VLM Linking Decoder that performs dual-branch cross-attention (a spatial latent-bottleneck branch with box-guided attention and a semantic branch in the VLM's native high-dimensional space) before a CLIP-initialized classification head. On top of this, a Progressive Learning Strategy uses a teacher-student paradigm: the teacher is first trained with standard sparse GT supervision, then the student is jointly supervised by GT labels on matched queries plus dense KL-divergence distillation at three levels (feature maps, per-layer encoder/decoder queries, and final logits) applied to *all* candidate human-object pairs, including negatives. This dense, instance-level supervision contrasts subtle spatial and semantic differences between positive and negative instances, yielding more discriminative HOI representations without sacrificing zero-shot or open-vocabulary generalization.

## Results

On HICO-DET with a Swin-L backbone and CLIP-ViT-L, LINK achieves 49.06/53.63 mAP (Full/Rare), surpassing the previous best (HORP, 47.53/46.81) by +1.53/+6.82 mAP. At the smaller R50/CLIP-L scale, LINK reaches 42.92/45.03 mAP, outperforming the prior SOTA by 3.87/6.37 mAP (relative gains of 9.9%/16.5%). In zero-shot RF-UC evaluation, LINK† (ViT-L) attains 39.40 HM, exceeding the previous best (HOLa, 32.69) by +6.71. On the open-vocabulary SWiG-HOI benchmark, LINK sets a new SOTA of 17.97 mAP (Full) and 12.15 mAP (Novel), outperforming SGC-Net by +0.77/+0.69 mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LINK outperforms all prior methods at the Swin-L/CLIP-L scale on HICO-DET, achieving 49.06/53.63 mAP (Full/Rare) versus HORP's 47.53/46.81 and PViC's 44.32/44.61, with the largest margin (+6.82 mAP) on the Rare subset. At the R50/CLIP-L scale, LINK (42.92/45.03) edges out DebiaHOI (42.93/42.41) on Full and surpasses it by +2.62 on Rare, while slightly trailing InterProDa (45.21) on Rare by 0.18 mAP. Against the BLIP-2-based BC-HOI, LINK with the same VLM backbone improves Full mAP from 43.01 to 43.72 and Rare from 45.76 to 45.82. In zero-shot RF-UC, LINK† (39.40 HM) dominates all zero-shot-oriented methods (HOLa 32.69, CLIP4HOI 31.59, CMMP 31.07) and fully-supervised methods (PViC 27.85, HOICLIP 29.40). On SWiG-HOI, LINK (17.97 Full, 12.15 Novel) surpasses SGC-Net (17.20/12.46) on Full but trails slightly on Novel, while maintaining the best Rare score (17.88 vs. 16.55). The plug-and-play evaluation (Table 8/12) shows LINK generalizes to open-vocabulary detectors (Grounding-DINO Swin-Base: 39.69 Full) without any retraining, whereas PViC degrades to 15.83 when the detector is swapped.

## Relation to Prior Work

LINK builds directly on the two-stage VLM-based HOI detection paradigm, extending the PViC codebase and adopting its attention-guidance mechanism, while borrowing pairwise spatial encoding from UPT. It departs from prior zero-shot-oriented methods (HOICLIP, CLIP4HOI, CMMP, HOLa) that apply lightweight modifications to CLIP, and from fully-supervised query-based detectors (PViC, UPT) that are tightly coupled to specific object detectors. The key architectural departure is decoupling HOI reasoning from detector-specific features by using ROI-aligned VLM features plus explicit geometric encodings, and the key training departure is a progressive teacher-student strategy that provides dense multi-level distillation over all human-object pairs rather than only matched ground-truth queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play detector compatibility (maintaining performance across DETR, Deformable-DETR, YOLO, Grounding-DINO, and Qwen3-VL without retraining) addresses a practical deployment bottleneck that prior query-based methods cannot resolve. The consistent gains across six diverse foundation models (CLIP, BLIP, DINOv2, DINO@448, SigLIP2, Florence2) suggest the progressive learning strategy is a general-purpose adapter rather than a CLIP-specific trick. Open questions include whether the teacher-student overhead can be eliminated via self-distillation or online adaptation, whether the approach extends to video HOI or 3D interaction detection, and whether the dense supervision signal degrades when the teacher and student use fundamentally different VLMs. The comparison showing a 0.52B task-specific model outperforming GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-pro on HOI triplets also raises broader questions about the role of MLLMs in structured perception tasks.
