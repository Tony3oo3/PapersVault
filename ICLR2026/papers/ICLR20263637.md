## Problem

Existing temporal graph networks for EEG modeling discretize time into fixed recurrent steps, which conflicts with the inherently continuous and stochastic nature of brain dynamics, leading to compounded cumulative errors and an inability to capture instantaneous nonlinear transitions such as seizure onset. This paper is the first to explicitly formulate EEG brain networks as a continuous-time dynamical system governed by a Neural ODE, addressing two non-trivial challenges: obtaining a robust initialization against EEG transients and defining a vector field objective that faithfully captures underlying neural dynamics rather than surface-level predictions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 19-channel EEG recordings (10-20 montage) segmented into 12-second epochs with 1-second steps, which are processed via STFT into spectral graph snapshots with top-τ=3 sparse adjacency matrices. Unusually, the input is consumed through a dual pathway: deterministic spectral graph features and stochastic raw EEG representations are encoded separately before being concatenated into the ODE initial state. The primary output is a sequence of K predicted future brain network graphs, but the model also yields the continuous latent vector field f_θ as an interpretable artifact, and after unsupervised pre-training with graph forecasting loss, the pooled ODE trajectory is fine-tuned for seizure detection classification.

## Method

ODEBRAIN integrates three components: (1) a dual-encoder initialization where a GRU-GCN extracts deterministic spectral graph features z^g and a CNN extracts stochastic temporal features z^s from raw EEG, concatenated as z_0 = [z^s, z^g]; (2) a temporal-spatial ODE solver with a gated vector field f_θ = (g(z_0)+1)⊙h(z_0) − λ(z^s)z_0, where the sigmoid gate provides state-adaptive modulation and the Softplus decay term conditioned on z^s regularizes trajectories under noisy inputs, solved via RK4; (3) a graph embedding forecasting decoder Ω that projects latent ODE solutions back to graph node attributes with a multi-step structural forecasting loss (L2 on predicted vs. ground-truth graph structures) rather than simple signal reconstruction. The key differentiator is that the continuous-time formulation provides arbitrary temporal resolution, the stochastic regularization mitigates ODE error accumulation, and the learned vector field f_θ itself serves as a clinically interpretable metric whose gradient flow patterns distinguish seizure from normal brain states.

## Results

On TUSZ (12s seizure detection), ODEBRAIN (single-step) achieves the best AUROC of 0.881±0.006, F1 of 0.496±0.017, and ACC of 0.877±0.004, outperforming all discrete and continuous baselines. On TUAB, ODEBRAIN reaches AUROC 0.857±0.005, F1 0.774±0.005, and ACC 0.778±0.003. The multi-step variant (ODEBRAIN†) also leads on TUSZ with AUROC 0.875 and F1 0.488, while maintaining competitive performance on TUAB (AUROC 0.849, F1 0.770). The paper reports 6.0% and 8.1% relative improvements in F1 and ACC over the best baseline on TUSZ, and 1.2% and 2.4% gains in F1 and AUROC on TUAB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ODEBRAIN outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across both TUSZ and TUAB. Among discrete methods, DCRNN is the strongest baseline (TUSZ AUROC 0.825, F1 0.416), but ODEBRAIN surpasses it by 5.6 AUROC points and 8.0 F1 points. Among continuous methods, latent-ODE (AUROC 0.849, F1 0.470) and Neural SDE (AUROC 0.851, F1 0.467) are the closest competitors on TUSZ; ODEBRAIN leads them by 3.2 and 3.0 AUROC points respectively. On TUAB, DCRNN achieves the highest baseline AUROC (0.848), yet ODEBRAIN still edges ahead by 0.9 points (0.857) and by 0.5 F1 points (0.774 vs. 0.769). The gap is most pronounced on TUSZ F1, where ODEBRAIN (0.496) exceeds the next-best GDEs (0.475) by 2.1 points and CNN-LSTM (0.347) by 14.9 points, reflecting the benefit of continuous trajectory modeling for capturing transient seizure dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

ODEBRAIN sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) temporal graph networks for EEG (DCRNN, EvolveGCN, AMAG, EvoBrain) that model brain connectivity as discrete-time sequences, and (2) Neural ODEs applied to brain signals (BrainODE for fMRI, graph ODEs for emotion EEG, fractional-dynamics models for BCI). The paper departs from both by being the first to cast EEG brain-network forecasting as a continuous-time dynamical system over time-varying spectral graphs, replacing the recurrent backbone with a NODE whose vector field is gated and stochastically regularized. The dual-encoder initialization (spectral GRU-GCN + raw-EEG CNN) and the adaptive-decay vector field are novel architectural choices not present in prior NODE-for-brain work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The continuous-time formulation removes the rigid windowing assumption of recurrent models, enabling arbitrary temporal resolution and more faithful capture of abrupt transitions such as seizure onset. The gradient-field visualization offers a clinically interpretable lens on brain-state dynamics that discrete methods cannot provide. However, the framework still operates on fixed 12-second epochs, limiting true long-horizon continuous modeling, and evaluation is confined to seizure detection on two datasets (TUSZ, TUAB); generalization to other neurological disorders, cognitive tasks, or longer recordings remains open. The modest 6–8% metric gains over strong baselines suggest the continuous-time framing is principled but not yet transformative for practical clinical deployment.
