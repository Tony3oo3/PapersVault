## Problem

Compressing large audio-visual models for edge deployment is hindered by two issues: (1) latent-space distillation requires matching feature dimensions between teacher and student, while output-space distillation is less performant, and (2) uniform distillation across all modalities injects noise when a modality is uninformative for a given sample (e.g., a visually occluded object whose sound is still audible). The paper addresses both by proposing an architecture-agnostic distillation objective that operates on pairwise token relationships and an adaptive mechanism that suppresses uninformative modalities.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired RGB images (3×H×W, e.g., 224×224) and audio mel-spectrograms (1×B×F, e.g., 128×1024) that are tokenized by separate ViT/PVTv2 encoders. The teacher's last-layer token embeddings (z_m ∈ R^{N_m×C} for m ∈ {audio, vision, fused}) are kernelized into instance-wise Gram matrices of shape N×N, which the student must match. Downstream outputs are class probabilities over C categories (VGGSound, 310 classes) or pixel-wise binary masks for sound-source localization (AVS-Bench S4/MS3). The student can use a different input resolution than the teacher (e.g., 112×112 vs. 224×224), in which case the teacher's Gram matrix is 2×2 average-pooled to match the student's token count.

## Method

Kernelized Token Distillation (KTD) replaces direct latent-embedding alignment with alignment of Gram matrices: for each modality m, normalized token embeddings are pairwise inner-producted (or passed through polynomial/RBF kernels) to form a similarity matrix φ_m, and the student is trained to match the teacher's φ_m^T via Huber loss over off-diagonal entries. This is architecture-agnostic because the Gram matrix is a fixed-size N×N object regardless of embedding dimension C. The Entropy-Monitored extension adds a frozen linear probe g_m(·) on each teacher modality branch; the entropy H_m of the probe's softmax output serves as an uncertainty estimate, and the per-modality distillation weight is w_m = exp(−λH_m), so low-entropy (confident, informative) modalities receive higher loss weight. The total training objective is the weighted sum of KTD losses across modalities plus the standard task loss (cross-entropy for classification, binary cross-entropy for segmentation). Kernels can be swapped (linear → polynomial degree d → RBF with tunable γ) to trade expressivity against O(d) or O(3) additional inner products per pair, without changing the student architecture.

## Results

On VGGSound audio-visual event classification, EM-KTD+KD achieves 62.0% accuracy, 63.4% mAP, and 97.9% mAUC using a ViT-Tiny student (10M params, 6% of the 164M teacher), preserving 96.9% of the teacher's accuracy. On AVS-Bench single-sound-source segmentation (S4), EM-KTD reaches 79.81 mIoU and 87.86 F-score with a PVTv2-b0 visual encoder (3.4M params, 4.5% of the teacher's 81.4M), retaining 97.1% of teacher performance. On the multi-sound-source task (MS3), EM-KTD achieves 64.43 mIoU and 74.73 F-score, actually surpassing the teacher (61.95 mIoU, 70.9 F-score) on both metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EM-KTD+KD outperforms all five baselines on VGGSound: it beats Hinton's KD by +5.9% Acc and +6.1% mAP, AT+KD by +5.4% Acc and +6.5% mAP, SPKD+KD by +6.4% Acc and +7.3% mAP, and the closest competitor MTST+KD by +4.4% Acc and +4.9% mAP. On AVS-Bench S4 segmentation, EM-KTD leads SPKD (the strongest baseline) by +2.22 mIoU and +1.61 F-score, and MTST by +2.62 mIoU and +1.83 F-score. On MS3, EM-KTD surpasses SPKD by +2.34 mIoU and +1.77 F-score. In the appendix (Table 12), EM-KTD also outperforms the projection-based VKD (Miles et al., 2024) by +0.8% Acc and +2.1% mAP, demonstrating that the projector-free kernelized approach avoids the uncontrolled effects of learned projection layers. The entropy-monitoring component adds a consistent +0.6% Acc and +1.1% mAP over KTD alone, confirming the benefit of adaptive modality weighting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two established KD paradigms: latent-space distillation (which requires matching teacher-student dimensions or adds projection layers) and output-space distillation (which is architecture-agnostic but less expressive). The closest prior work, MTST (Choi et al., 2023), also operates on token-level similarities but applies softmax normalization and random masking, which the authors argue corrupts the teacher's pairwise relationship structure. KTD instead distills the raw Gram matrix of normalized token embeddings via a Huber loss, borrowing the kernel-method perspective (Vapnik, 1997) to enable flexible similarity metrics (linear, polynomial, RBF) without explicit high-dimensional mapping. The entropy-monitoring component extends the monitored distillation idea from Liu et al. (2022a) (output-space, depth completion) to the latent token space for generic multimodal models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 94% parameter reduction with ~97% performance retention is practically significant for deploying audio-visual models on edge devices, and the architecture-agnostic design removes a key friction point in KD pipelines where teacher and student have mismatched dimensions. The entropy-based adaptive weighting addresses a real failure mode—distilling from uninformative modalities can degrade the student—which prior KD methods handle uniformly. Open questions include whether the O(N²) kernelization cost becomes prohibitive at higher resolutions or with more modalities, whether the approach generalizes beyond transformer-based encoders, and whether the entropy monitor's linear-probe design is sufficient for more complex uncertainty estimation. The paper is limited to audio-visual tasks and two datasets, so broader validation is needed.
