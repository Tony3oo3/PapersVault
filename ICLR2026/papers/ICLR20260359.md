## Problem

Fault-tolerant quantum computers promise exponential speed-ups for linear algebra, yet prior quantum accelerations of multi-layer neural networks required intermediate measurements or state tomography between layers, breaking coherence and limiting achievable speed-ups. This paper presents the first fully-coherent quantum implementation of a multi-layer residual CNN with non-linear activations, with rigorous end-to-end complexity proofs under three distinct quantum data-access regimes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a rank-3 image tensor of dimension 4×M×M (RGB plus a null channel), vectorized to an O(N)-dimensional vector with N=4M², along with classically pre-trained network weights (k residual convolutional blocks with 16×16×3×3 filters each, plus a final full-rank linear layer). The output is a sample drawn from an ℓ₁-normalized C-dimensional probability vector (e.g., C=10 for CIFAR-10) whose ℓ₂ distance from the true network output is bounded by a user-specified ε. In Regime 1 the input is loaded via QRAM; in Regimes 2 and 3 the input is loaded without QRAM, and the architecture routes it through d parallel classical bilinear paths whose tensor product feeds the quantum residual blocks.

## Method

The core mechanism is a modular vector-encoding (VE) framework that tracks the ℓ₂ norm of encoded vectors through every architectural block, enabling coherent chaining of k residual blocks without norm collapse. Each residual block implements a 2D multi-filter convolution (via a novel QRAM-free block-encoding), a non-linear activation (erf, implemented through QSVT), a skip-connection vector sum, and layer normalization—all as coherent quantum subroutines. A key technical contribution is a matrix-vector-squared product algorithm that applies an arbitrary full-rank dense matrix to the element-wise square of a vector without incurring a Frobenius-norm (rank) complexity dependence. The residual skip connections are shown to be essential for norm preservation: without them, the encoded vector norm could decay arbitrarily across layers, making the runtime unbounded. Under QRAM access to both inputs and weights, inference costs Õ(polylog(N/ε)^k); with only weights in QRAM, a quartic speedup over classical is achieved; with no QRAM at all, a quadratic speedup is proven.

## Results

This is a theoretical paper providing complexity guarantees rather than empirical benchmarks. Under Regime 1 (QRAM for both inputs and weights), inference for a k-layer residual CNN on N-dimensional inputs costs Õ(polylog(N/ε)^k) circuit depth. Under Regime 2 (QRAM for weights only), the architecture achieves a quartic speedup over exact classical computation (quantum cost Õ(N log(1/ε)^{2k}) vs. classical Ω(N⁴) for the final linear layer). Under Regime 3 (no QRAM), a quadratic speedup is proven (quantum O(N log(1/ε)^{2k}) vs. classical Ω(N²) for the 2D convolution).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper positions itself against four prior quantum ML approaches: Cong et al. (2019), Allcock et al. (2020), Kerenidis et al. (2020), and Guo et al. (2024b). All prior works require intermediate measurements (inner-product readout or full state tomography) between layers, breaking quantum coherence and limiting achievable speedups. This work is the first to provide a fully coherent multi-layer implementation with non-linear activations, norm preservation across layers, and polylogarithmic error dependence. Regime 1 uniquely achieves both polylog(N) and polylog(1/ε) scaling simultaneously, a combination no prior method attains. Regime 3 is the first QRAM-free multi-layer quantum neural network architecture, whereas all prior approaches (Allcock, Kerenidis, Guo) require QRAM. The authors argue that existing classical dequantization techniques are insufficient to match the Regime 1 polylogarithmic complexity, though this claim is not formally proven.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior quantum-accelerated neural network work (Allcock et al. 2020 for feedforward nets, Kerenidis et al. 2020 for CNNs, Guo et al. 2024b for transformers), all of which required intermediate measurements or state tomography between layers, breaking quantum coherence. It builds on the quantum block-encoding framework (Gilyen et al. 2019) and the vector-encoding formalism (Rattew & Rebentrost 2023), extending the latter with new primitive operations (vector sums, tensor products, concatenations) and a novel QRAM-free block-encoding for 2D multi-filter convolutions. The bilinear architecture in Regimes 2–3 is inspired by classical bilinear CNNs (Lin et al. 2015), repurposed here to avoid QRAM for inputs while preserving a quartic speedup from the final full-rank linear layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fully coherent quantum implementation of multi-layer neural networks with non-linear activations, providing rigorous end-to-end complexity proofs under three QRAM regimes (polylogarithmic, quartic, and quadratic speedups). A key open question the paper identifies is whether the exponential circuit-depth cost in the number of layers (2^k) is a fundamental limitation of quantum computation for non-linear transformations, suggesting quantum computers may be best suited for wide-and-shallow architectures rather than deep ones. The practical impact is currently limited by the need for fault-tolerant QPUs and feasible QRAM, and the authors note that existing classical techniques are insufficient to dequantize the Regime 1 result, making the quantum advantage claim non-trivial but hardware-dependent.
