## Problem

Current MLLM safety alignment relies on static, context-agnostic policies that enforce a fixed safety-helpfulness tradeoff, leading to either over-refusal of benign queries or dangerous compliance with seemingly harmless prompts that carry latent visual risks. This rigidity stems from two compounding deficiencies: MLLM visual encoders are trained on captioning data sparse in risk signals (internal perception gap), and existing reward models collapse safety and helpfulness into a single score without query-dependent weighting (external framework gap).

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image paired with a free-form text prompt and produces a natural-language text response. During training, the reward model consumes the full multimodal context (image, prompt, and candidate response) to emit a context-aware scalar reward that is a query-weighted combination of separate helpfulness and harmlessness scores. The weight vector (e.g., [1.0, 0.0] for harmless queries, [0.0, 1.0] for explicitly dangerous ones) is determined per-query by the annotation pipeline, so the same model produces different arbitration behavior depending on the input context.

## Method

Pragma-VL is a three-stage end-to-end pipeline. First, a contextual data augmentation pipeline (PragmaSafe) uses GPT-4o to annotate responses with helpfulness scores, harmlessness scores, and a query-dependent safety-utility weight vector, applying a variance-aware stochastic interpolation to handle rater disagreement. Second, a cold-start phase calibrates the visual encoder via Risk-Aware Supervised Contrastive Learning (clustering latent representations by risk severity using LoRA) followed by risk-aware SFT on an interleaved dataset of safety Q&A and targeted risk-identification tasks. Third, a parallel multi-head reward model—proven via Fisher information theory to achieve strictly lower MSE and preference error than single-objective or sequential architectures—is trained with a joint Bradley-Terry + MSE loss and used to produce a prompt-regulated scalar reward that drives GRPO policy optimization, enabling the model to dynamically arbitrate safety and helpfulness per query.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Pragma-VL achieves 62.65% Helpfulness and 67.91% Harmlessness on BeaverTails-V, 87.17% Helpfulness and 87.92% Harmlessness on SPA-VL, and the lowest Attack Success Rate of 31.66% on MM-SafetyBench (vs. 48.75% for the base model). On Llava-1.5-7B, it reaches 86.93% Helpfulness and 88.96% Harmlessness on BeaverTails-V, 97.93% Helpfulness on SPA-VL, and 31.67% ASR on MM-SafetyBench. Most notably, Pragma-VL boosts SIUO cross-modal safety from 38.78% to 63.47% (Qwen) and from 14.37% to 55.42% (Llava), while preserving or slightly improving general capabilities (e.g., GQA 61.42%, ScienceQA 89.06% on Qwen).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pragma-VL outperforms all baselines on nearly every metric for both Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Llava-1.5-7B. Against Safe-RLHF-V (Ji et al., 2025), Pragma-VL gains +15.8% Helpfulness and +26.4% Harmlessness on SPA-VL (Qwen: 87.17/87.92 vs. 45.08/61.51) and reduces MM-SafetyBench ASR from 43.20% to 31.66%, while also improving SIUO cross-modal safety from 55.90% to 63.47%. Against DPO on SPA-VL (Zhang et al., 2025a), Pragma-VL achieves +60.4% Helpfulness on SPA-VL (87.17% vs. 26.79%) and +26.7% SIUO Safety (63.47% vs. 36.74%) on Qwen. Standard DPO on BeaverTails-V shows a clear safety-helpfulness trade-off (e.g., 78.87% Harm but only 52.47% Help on SPA-VL for Qwen), whereas Pragma-VL achieves high scores on both simultaneously. On Llava, the gap is even more pronounced: Pragma-VL lifts SIUO Safety from 14.37% (base) to 55.42%, a 41-point improvement that no baseline approaches. The only metric where a baseline slightly edges Pragma-VL is MM-SafetyBench Harmlessness on Qwen (DPO: 51.97% vs. Pragma-VL: 58.99%—actually Pragma-VL still wins), confirming consistent dominance across the board.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM safety-alignment line of work (Safe-RLHF-V, SPA-VL, MM-RLHF) by reframing the safety–helpfulness tradeoff as a context-dependent arbitration problem rather than a fixed constraint. It departs from prior multi-objective reward modeling (Zhang et al. 2025b; Xue et al. 2025) by providing a theoretical error-ordering proof for a parallel multi-head architecture and coupling it with a novel variance-aware data augmentation pipeline (PragmaSafe). The cold-start stage repurposes supervised contrastive learning (Khosla et al. 2020) for visual risk perception, a use not previously explored in MLLM safety alignment, and the policy stage adopts GRPO (Guo et al. 2025) rather than the PPO or DPO variants common in prior MLLM safety work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical recipe for MLLM developers who need to balance safety and utility without hand-tuning constraint thresholds, and the PragmaSafe dataset (122K items with dynamic weight vectors) is a reusable resource for training context-aware reward models. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 7B models, whether the GPT-4o-based annotation pipeline introduces systematic biases that limit generalization, and whether the theoretical guarantees (which assume differentiability and positive gradient correlations) hold under the non-convex losses actually used in practice. The reliance on a single annotator model for all five annotation rounds also raises questions about label diversity.
