## Problem

Video generation is an exceptionally token-extensive task (over 75,000 tokens for a single 5-second 720p video), making the O(N²) complexity of vanilla self-attention a severe bottleneck for both training cost and inference speed. Existing long-video autoregressive methods rely on growing KV caches that force local attention windows, sacrificing global context. The paper asks whether a small diffusion model can achieve high-quality, high-resolution, minute-length video generation at a fraction of the computational cost of current state-of-the-art models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261332/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SANA-Video is a unified framework accepting either a text prompt (T2V/T2I) or a first-frame image plus text prompt (I2V, achieved by zeroing the first frame's noise without architectural changes). It produces videos up to 720×1280 resolution and minute-length duration, or still images. For 480P output it uses Wan-VAE (F8T4C16), while 720P output uses the custom DCAE-V (F32T4C32) for higher compression; a motion score derived from optical flow is appended to the prompt for motion magnitude control.

## Method

The core architecture replaces all self-attention with ReLU linear attention (O(N) complexity), applying 3D RoPE after the ReLU kernel to induce local focus while removing RoPE from the denominator to prevent numerical instability. For long video generation, a block linear attention module reformulates causal linear attention so the KV cache consists only of cumulative sums of attention states (O(D²)) and keys (O(D)), providing global context at constant memory regardless of video length. A temporal 1D convolution with shortcut connection is added to the Mix-FFN to capture local temporal relationships. Training proceeds in three stages: VAE adaptation on T2I data, coarse-to-fine continue pre-training from the SANA-1.6B image model, and autoregressive block training using a monotonically increasing SNR sampler followed by self-forcing with global linear attention to address exposure bias. The model is trained on 64 H100 GPUs for 12 days and can be quantized to NVFP4 for 2.4× faster inference on RTX 5090.

## Results

On VBench (480×832×81), SANA-Video achieves a Total score of 83.71 for T2V and 88.02 for I2V, while generating a 5-second video in just 60 seconds on a single H100 GPU — an 8× speedup over the next-fastest baseline. At 720p (720×1280×81), SANA-Video-2B reaches 84.05 Total VBench score in 36 seconds, compared to 1897 s for Wan-2.1-14B (83.73) and 116 s for Wan-2.2-5B (83.28). For 30-second long-video generation on VBench-Long, LongSANA achieves 82.29 Total and 79.04 Semantic at 27.5 FPS, outperforming Self-Forcing (81.59 Total, 17 FPS) and SkyReels-V2 (75.29 Total, 0.49 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SANA-Video (2B, 60 s) is the fastest model in the comparison, beating Wan2.1-1.3B (103 s) by 1.7×, CogVideoX-1.5 (111 s) by 1.9×, SkyReels-V2 (132 s) by 2.2×, Step-Video (246 s) by 4.1×, MAGI-1 (435 s) by 7.3×, and Wan2.1-14B (484 s) by 8.1×. On T2V VBench Total score, SANA-Video (83.71) surpasses all sub-5B baselines (Wan2.1-1.3B: 83.31, SkyReels-V2: 82.67, CogVideoX-1.5: 82.17, Step-Video: 81.83, MAGI-1: 79.18) and matches Wan2.1-14B (83.69), while trailing only Open-Sora-2.0 (84.34, 14B). On I2V, SANA-Video (88.02 Total, 96.40 Semantic) outperforms Wan2.1-14B (86.86, 92.90), HunyuanVideo-I2V (86.82, 95.10), and CogVideoX-5b-I2V (86.70, 94.79), though it is slightly behind MAGI-1 (89.28) and Step-Video-TI2V (88.36) on Total. For long-video generation (30 s, VBench-Long), LongSANA achieves the best Semantic score (79.04) and Total score (82.29) while running at 27.5 FPS — over 1.6× faster than Self-Forcing (17 FPS, 81.59 Total) and 58× faster than SkyReels-V2 (0.49 FPS, 75.29 Total). In the autoregressive comparison (Table 6), SANA-Video (83.70 Total) is competitive with Self-Forcing (84.31) and clearly ahead of SkyReels-V2 (82.67) and CausVid (81.20).

## Relation to Prior Work

SANA-Video extends the SANA linear DiT architecture (Xie et al., 2025a) from image to video generation by integrating 3D RoPE and a temporal 1D convolution into the Mix-FFN, while reformulating causal linear attention (Katharopoulos et al., 2020) into a block-wise constant-memory KV cache that replaces the growing O(N×D) cache of prior autoregressive video models (MAGI-1, SkyReel-V2, Self-Forcing). The autoregressive long-video training paradigm builds directly on Self-Forcing (Huang et al., 2025) and diffusion forcing, but the key departure is that linear attention's cumulative state naturally yields a fixed O(D²) global context, eliminating the local-attention window restriction that constrains all prior full-attention autoregressive video generators. The VAE component fine-tunes DCAE (Chen et al., 2024c) into DCAE-V for 32× spatial compression, and the overall training pipeline (VAE adaptation → coarse-to-fine continue pre-training → autoregressive block training with self-forcing) is a practical synthesis of existing techniques rather than a single new algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that high-quality video generation need not require the massive compute budgets of current SOTA models: 12 days on 64 H100s (1% of MovieGen's cost) yields a 2B model that matches or exceeds 14B-parameter baselines on VBench while being 16× faster, and the constant-memory KV cache enables minute-length generation without the memory blowup that forces prior methods into local attention. The NVFP4 deployment on consumer RTX 5090 GPUs (29s for a 5s 720p video) makes high-quality video generation accessible outside datacenter settings. Open questions include whether the quality gap versus the largest models (e.g., Veo3, Kling) narrows with scale, whether linear attention's inherent locality bias limits fine-grained long-range temporal reasoning, and how the approach generalizes to even longer (multi-minute) or higher-resolution (4K) generation.
