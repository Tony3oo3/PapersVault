## Problem

Score-based diffusion models can generate novel data points not present in the training set, yet using the exact empirical score function (ESF) leads to memorization. The specific mechanism by which neural network imperfections in score estimation enable generalization beyond the training set remained unexplained. This paper identifies score smoothing as the causal link, showing that regularized networks learn a smoothed ESF that drives the denoising dynamics to interpolate rather than memorize.

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite training set S (uniformly spaced points in a 1-D subspace, or points on nonlinear manifolds up to 20 dimensions) and initializes the denoising ODE at time t₀ with the noised empirical distribution p_{t₀}^{(n)}. The output is a terminal distribution at t=0 that decomposes into a mixture of point masses at training locations and a non-singular continuous density supported on the interval between adjacent training points, yielding interpolated samples on the data subspace. In higher dimensions, normal components collapse to zero at rate √t, recovering the underlying subspace while the tangent component retains the interpolation.

## Method

The paper formulates a non-parametric variational problem that minimizes a non-smoothness penalty R[f] = ∫|f''(x)|dx (equivalent to weight-norm regularization of infinite-width 2L ReLU networks) subject to a score matching loss constraint, and proves that the near-optimal solution is the Smoothed PL-ESF: a piecewise-linear function matching the PL-ESF except in intervals of width δ_t ∝ √t around each training point, where the slope is reduced. Closed-form flow maps for the backward denoising ODE under this smoothed score are derived, showing analytically that trajectories starting in the interpolation region converge to points strictly between training data rather than to the training points themselves. In higher dimensions, the smoothing is applied only to the tangent component while normal components remain exactly linear (−x_i/t), preserving uniform collapse onto the data subspace. Empirical validation with 2L and 3L ReLU networks (with and without weight decay) on 1-D, 2-D circular, and higher-dimensional spherical manifold data confirms that NN-learned scores exhibit the predicted smoothing and produce interpolating samples matching the analytical predictions.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result (Proposition 1) shows that the Smoothed PL-ESF with δ_t = κ√t is a near-minimizer of the non-smoothness penalty R[f] subject to the score matching loss constraint, with a multiplicative gap of at most (1 + 8√ε) relative to the optimal value. Propositions 3 and 5 provide closed-form flow maps showing that denoising under the Smoothed PL-ESF produces a terminal distribution with a non-singular density interpolating the training data along the 1-D subspace, while collapsing onto the subspace in normal directions at rate √t. Empirically (Section 6.1), the NN-learned SF closely tracks the Smoothed PL-ESF in both the score field and the resulting denoised distribution, with density histograms matching analytical predictions of p̂_t^(n,t₀). The interpolation effect extends to nonlinear settings: on a circle in ℝ² (Section 6.2) and on 2-D spherical manifolds embedded in d=20 (Section 6.3), NN-learned scores without weight decay still produce samples that interpolate training data along the manifold.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods on standard benchmarks; instead, it contrasts three score function choices driving the same denoising dynamics: (i) the exact ESF, which provably leads to memorization (terminal distribution = p₀⁽ⁿ⁾, fully singular); (ii) the Smoothed PL-ESF, which produces a non-singular interpolating density; and (iii) an NN-learned SF, which empirically matches (ii) closely. The paper also contrasts score smoothing with inference-time early stopping under the exact ESF, showing that early stopping alone merely adds Gaussian noise to training data without inducing proper subspace interpolation. In the d=2, n=4 experiment (Figure 4), the NN-learned SF column is visually and quantitatively much closer to the Smoothed PL-ESF column than to the ESF column, confirming that implicit regularization in NN training naturally induces the score smoothing effect even without explicit weight decay (as further shown in the circle and manifold experiments in Sections 6.2–6.3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the proposal by Scarvelis et al. (2025) that smoothing the empirical score function (ESF) can steer denoising dynamics toward barycenters of training data, by providing a full analytical characterization of how score smoothing produces interpolation along the data subspace. The theoretical machinery draws on Savarese et al. (2019), who showed that weight-norm regularization of two-layer ReLU networks is equivalent to a non-smoothness penalty in function space, which the paper leverages to formulate a variational problem whose near-minimizer is the Smoothed PL-ESF. The work also connects to the broader memorization-vs-generalization literature in diffusion models (Gu et al. 2025; Yi et al. 2023; Kadkhodaie et al. 2024) and to empirical observations by Aithal et al. (2024) that neural networks tend to learn smoother scores, but provides the first rigorous analytical link between NN regularization, score smoothing, and the resulting interpolation behavior in the probability flow ODE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies score smoothing as a concrete causal mechanism explaining why neural-network-based diffusion models avoid memorization and instead generate interpolating samples, offering a principled explanation for a phenomenon previously observed only empirically. The finding that even implicit regularization from gradient-based training (without explicit weight decay) can induce sufficient score smoothing to produce interpolation on nonlinear manifolds suggests a general inductive bias of NN training that may be exploitable for better generalization. Key limitations include the restriction to 1-D subspaces with uniformly-spaced data in the theoretical analysis, the use of a variational proxy rather than a direct proof about NN training dynamics, and the absence of validation on real-world high-dimensional data. Open questions include extending the theory to non-uniform spacing, complex manifolds, and more general diffusion model variants (flow matching, Schrödinger bridges), as well as understanding how the mechanism interacts with deeper or convolutional architectures.
