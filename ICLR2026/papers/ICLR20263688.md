## Problem

Recent work has assumed that explicit chain-of-thought reasoning, inspired by large reasoning models like DeepSeek-R1, improves LLM-based document reranking, yet these claims lack fair head-to-head comparisons against non-reasoning baselines. This paper presents the first systematic, controlled study of whether CoT reasoning actually helps reranking, covering both pointwise and listwise paradigms under both SFT and RL training, and finds that reasoning-augmented rerankers consistently underperform their direct-output counterparts despite substantially higher inference costs.

## Input / Output

For pointwise reranking, each query-passage pair is scored independently by computing a two-way softmax over the TRUE and FALSE token logits, yielding a scalar relevance probability used for sorting. For listwise reranking, the model receives a query plus a fixed pool of k=100 BM25-retrieved passages and autoregressively generates a full permutation (e.g., [3] > [5] > [4] > …); reasoning variants prepend a free-form rationale inside <think> tags before the <answer> block. All models share unified system prompts and are evaluated with NDCG@10 on BRIGHT (reasoning-intensive) and BEIR (standard IR) benchmarks.

## Method

The paper trains four Qwen3-4B/8B rerankers—Direct-Point, Reason-Point, Direct-List, and Reason-List—under a unified protocol to isolate the effect of reasoning. Pointwise models are SFT'd on the RANK1 corpus (~386k MS MARCO pairs annotated by DeepSeek-R1) with LoRA (rank 32, α=64, lr 1e-4, 1 epoch); listwise models undergo two-stage training (SFT then GRPO) on the REASONRANK corpus (~13k instances) using a composite reward combining NDCG@10, Recall@10, and Rank-Biased Overlap with format-gating penalties. The key empirical finding is that direct models outperform reasoning variants by 4–9 NDCG points (pointwise) and 0.3–1.9 points (listwise) across all benchmarks and training stages. Calibration analysis shows reasoning inflates ECE (0.151 vs. 0.105) and shifts the error distribution toward higher TPR / lower TNR, increasing false positives in negative-dominant pools; listwise reasoning improves in-sample fit but raises variance and fails to generalize out-of-domain, even after GRPO compresses rationales from ~398 to ~172 tokens.

## Results

Across BRIGHT and BEIR, direct-output rerankers consistently outperform their reasoning-augmented counterparts under both SFT and SFT+GRPO training. On BRIGHT (original query), Direct-Point-4B exceeds Reason-Point-4B by +9.0 NDCG@10 (25.5 vs. 16.5), and Direct-Point-8B by +6.1 (26.8 vs. 20.7); on BEIR the corresponding gaps are +5.3 (45.4 vs. 40.1) and +4.3 (43.7 vs. 39.3). For listwise rerankers, Direct-List-8B (SFT+GRPO) achieves the best overall BRIGHT score of 27.1 NDCG@10, surpassing Reason-List-8B (25.9) and all reasoning-based baselines. On BEIR, Direct-Point-4B attains 45.4 NDCG@10, outperforming the strongest reasoning-enhanced listwise baseline (Rank-R1-14B at 43.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On BRIGHT (original query), Direct-List-8B (SFT+GRPO, 27.1) outperforms the strongest reasoning-based listwise baseline ReasonRank-7B (26.4) by +0.7, and substantially exceeds Rank-R1-14B (20.5) and REARANK-7B (17.7). Direct-Point-8B (26.8) surpasses TF-Rank-8B (22.6) by +4.2 and Rank1-14B (18.5) by +8.3. On BEIR, Direct-Point-4B (45.4) beats TF-Rank-4B (42.5) by +2.9, Rank-R1-14B (43.8) by +1.6, and ReasonRank-7B (41.7) by +3.7. On the BRIGHT gpt4_reason split, Direct-List-4B/8B (35.3) outperform Rank-K (33.3) and ReasonRank-7B (32.4), while Direct-Point-4B (33.0) exceeds Rank1-32B (29.4) by +3.6. Against older non-reasoning baselines (MonoT5-3B at 16.8, RankLLaMA-7B at 13.9, RankZephyr-7B at 21.3 on BRIGHT gpt4_query), the proposed direct rerankers achieve large margins of +16 to +21 NDCG@10, confirming that the gains come from modern LLM backbones and training rather than from explicit reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a critical empirical audit of the recent LRM-for-reranking line (Rank1, ReasonRank, Rank-R1, TF-Rank, REARANK), which has largely assumed that explicit chain-of-thought reasoning improves ranking quality. Rather than proposing a new reranking method, it carves new ground by conducting the first controlled, apples-to-apples comparison of reasoning vs. direct-output rerankers under identical training data, backbones (Qwen3-4B/8B), and training regimes (SFT and SFT+GRPO). The mechanistic analysis draws on calibration theory (ECE, TPR/TNR decomposition) to explain *why* reasoning fails, going beyond the limited pointwise-only observations in Jedidi et al. (2025) and Fan et al. (2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that direct-output rerankers consistently outperform reasoning-augmented ones across all tested settings challenges a working assumption underpinning a rapidly growing subfield, with immediate practical implications: practitioners can avoid the substantial inference cost of generating CoT traces without sacrificing (and in fact improving) ranking quality. The calibration-failure diagnosis for pointwise rerankers and the overfitting/variance analysis for listwise rerankers point to concrete future directions—calibration-aware scoring objectives and concise, targeted reasoning strategies—rather than simply discarding reasoning. Open questions include whether the result holds for non-logits-based pointwise scoring (e.g., token-level scoring), for larger backbones, or for reasoning traces generated by the model itself rather than distilled from a teacher.
