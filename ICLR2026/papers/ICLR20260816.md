## Problem

Existing irregular multivariate time series forecasting methods suffer from two coupled limitations: structured representations (interpolation, time-alignment, patching) introduce information distortion by altering the original sampling pattern, and raw-data-based methods rely on fixed interaction rules (temporal-adjacent ODE propagation or static graph topologies) that cannot adaptively capture context-dependent, cross-variable dynamic dependencies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260816/cropped_page1_idx7.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a set of discrete observation tuples (timestamp, scalar value, variable index) with no requirement for equal spacing or cross-variable alignment, along with a set of query coordinates specifying target (time, variable) pairs. It outputs a predicted scalar value for each query coordinate. The key constraint is that the historical set and query set are split by a timestamp threshold, and all propagation is causally masked so information flows only from past to future observations.

## Method

ASTGI encodes each observation as a point in a learnable spatio-temporal embedding space (concatenation of a channel embedding and an MLP-based time encoding), preserving the raw data without interpolation or alignment. For each point, a K-nearest-neighbor search in this learned space identifies candidate neighbors, which are then causally masked to form a per-point directed graph. Stacked propagation layers perform message passing where both the message function and the interaction weight are explicitly conditioned on the relative spatio-temporal displacement vector between points, enabling relation-aware, context-dependent information flow. Prediction is unified as a neighborhood aggregation task: a new query point is embedded in the same space, its K nearest historical neighbors are retrieved, and a dedicated scoring-and-fusion network (separate from propagation parameters) produces the final regression output.

## Results

ASTGI achieves state-of-the-art forecasting accuracy across all four IMTS benchmarks, evaluated by MSE and MAE. On MIMIC (96 variables), ASTGI reaches MSE 0.3909 and MAE 0.3852, outperforming the second-best Hi-Patch (MSE 0.4279) by 8.65%. On USHCN, it achieves MSE 0.1608 vs. Hi-Patch's 0.1749 (8.06% reduction). Overall, ASTGI delivers an average ~6.04% MSE reduction over the runner-up across all four datasets, with the largest gains on high-dimensional clinical data (MIMIC, PhysioNet) and the smallest on the lower-dimensional Human Activity dataset (5.29%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASTGI outperforms all 12 baselines on every dataset and both metrics (MSE and MAE). The closest competitor is Hi-Patch (ICML 2025), a hierarchical patch GNN, which ASTGI beats by 5.3–8.7% MSE across datasets; GraFITi (AAAI 2024) and tPatchGNN (ICML 2024) are the next closest, trailing ASTGI by roughly 5–10% MSE. Among ODE-based methods, NeuralFlows (NeurIPS 2021) and CRU (ICML 2022) are substantially outperformed, with ASTGI achieving 25–45% lower MSE on MIMIC and PhysioNet. Interpolation-based mTAN (ICLR 2021) and time-aligned GRU-D (2016) show the largest gaps, particularly on USHCN where mTAN's MSE (0.5561) is more than 3× ASTGI's (0.1608). The set-based SeFT (ICML 2020) and pre-trained PrimeNet (AAAI 2023) perform worst overall, confirming that neither unordered set aggregation nor pre-training without adaptive graph structure is sufficient for capturing dynamic cross-variable dependencies in irregular time series.

## Relation to Prior Work

ASTGI carves new ground at the intersection of two established IMTSF paradigms: raw-data-based methods (ODE-based models like NeuralFlows and static-graph GNNs like GraFITi, Raindrop, tPatchGNN) and structured-representation methods (interpolation, time-alignment, patch-alignment). Rather than building on a single prior architecture, it repurposes GNN message-passing and attention-style scoring within a learned spatio-temporal embedding space, replacing both the Markovian temporal ordering of ODE models and the fixed topologies of static graph methods with per-point adaptive k-NN causal graphs. The key conceptual departure is that graph topology is no longer a design-time choice but an emergent, data-driven property of the learned coordinate space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled answer to the long-standing tension in IMTSF between preserving raw observation structure and modeling cross-variable, cross-time dependencies, and the consistent ~6% MSE gain over the strongest baseline (Hi-Patch) across four diverse domains validates the adaptive-graph paradigm. However, the evaluation is limited to four relatively small datasets (≤21K samples, ≤96 variables), leaving open questions about scalability to very high-dimensional or very long-horizon irregular series, the computational cost of repeated k-NN search across propagation layers, and whether the learned embedding space transfers across domains without retraining. The qualitative graph visualizations are encouraging but remain anecdotal; a systematic study of when and why the adaptive graph diverges from a static one would strengthen the contribution.
