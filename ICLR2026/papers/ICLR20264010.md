## Problem

Vision-language models compress instance semantics into a single embedding point, making it difficult to simultaneously encode two distinct structures: taxonomic hierarchy within a concept family (e.g., dog ⊑ mammal ⊑ animal) and compositionality across distinct families (e.g., 'a dog in a car' binding concepts from animals and transportation). Hyperbolic space captures hierarchy efficiently but lacks a canonical composition operation, while Boolean/Euclidean approaches handle conjunction but cannot represent deep tree-like taxonomies without exponential dimensionality.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 224×224 RGB images, free-form text captions, and bounding-box annotations (object-level crops paired with corresponding noun phrases from the caption). Rather than producing a single embedding vector, each instance is mapped to a tuple of k=64 points, each residing in a separate 8-dimensional hyperbolic factor (total 512 dimensions), where the distance between two instances is the sum of per-factor hyperbolic distances. Downstream outputs include zero-shot class predictions, retrieved cross-modal instances, hierarchical classification scores on WordNet, and hard-negative discrimination scores.

## Method

PHyCLIP embeds each instance as a tuple X=(x⁽¹⁾,…,x⁽ᵏ⁾) in an ℓ₁-product metric space of k hyperbolic factors, where each factor Hᵢᵈ is a Lorentz-model hyperbolic space with independently learnable curvature −αᵢ. Within each factor, standard hyperbolic embeddings and geodesic entailment cones encode intra-family is-a hierarchy and image-text entailment; across factors, the additive ℓ₁-product metric captures cross-family compositionality analogous to a Boolean algebra, where activating multiple factors simultaneously expresses conjunction. Training combines a four-way InfoNCE contrastive loss (over image-text, text-image, image-box-text-box, and text-box-image-box pairs) with a per-factor entailment cone loss, weighted by γ=0.2. The theoretical justification rests on two results: metric trees admit (1+ε)-quasi-isometric embeddings into 2D hyperbolic space (Sarkar, 2011), and Boolean lattices embed isometrically into ℓ₁-product spaces but admit no isometric embedding into any hyperbolic space of dimension ≥2, establishing that the ℓ₁-product of hyperbolic factors is the natural geometry for jointly representing hierarchy and composition.

## Results

On zero-shot image classification across 16 datasets, PHyCLIP (ViT-B/16, k=64 factors, d=8) achieves 44.31% top-1 on ImageNet, 57.26% on Food-101, and 54.18% on Oxford-IIIT Pets, consistently outperforming HyCoCLIP (43.80%, 52.64%, 53.33%) and CLIP (38.81%, 51.05%, 46.19%). On zero-shot retrieval, PHyCLIP attains the best COCO text→image R@5 of 58.03 and image→text R@5 of 70.94. On hierarchical classification (WordNet), PHyCLIP achieves the lowest TIE (3.294) and highest Jaccard similarity (0.8059) among all methods. On compositional understanding, PHyCLIP scores 78.32 overall on SugarCrepe and 71.20 on VL-CheckList-Object (Center), exceeding HyCoCLIP by 0.33 and 0.77 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PHyCLIP outperforms all three baselines (CLIP, MERU, HyCoCLIP) on the majority of evaluation metrics. Against CLIP, PHyCLIP gains +5.50 points on ImageNet, +6.21 on Food-101, and +7.99 on Oxford-IIIT Pets in zero-shot classification, and improves COCO text→image R@5 by +1.74. Against MERU, the margins are even larger: +6.23 on ImageNet, +5.34 on Food-101, and +10.96 on Pets. The closest competitor is HyCoCLIP, which also uses hyperbolic entailment cones with box annotations; PHyCLIP still leads on ImageNet (+0.51), Food-101 (+4.62), CUB (+1.00), RESISC45 (+1.25), and COCO retrieval (R@5 +0.92 text→image, +1.43 image→text). On hierarchical classification, PHyCLIP beats HyCoCLIP on all five WordNet metrics (e.g., TIE 3.294 vs 3.319, J 0.8059 vs 0.8043). On compositional understanding, PHyCLIP achieves the best SugarCrepe overall score (78.32 vs 77.99) and substantially outperforms HyCoCLIP on VL-CheckList-Object location subsets (e.g., Margin: 70.37 vs 67.80), though it trails slightly on relation-replacement and object-swap subtasks, reflecting the Boolean-like nature of the ℓ₁-product metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

PHyCLIP extends the hyperbolic vision-language line initiated by MERU (Desai et al., 2023) and HyCoCLIP (Pal et al., 2025), which lift CLIP embeddings into a single hyperbolic space with entailment cones to capture is-a hierarchy. The key departure is replacing that single space with an ℓ₁-product of k hyperbolic factors, where each factor encodes one concept family's taxonomy and the additive ℓ₁ metric encodes cross-family Boolean-like composition. This design is theoretically motivated by two classical results: metric trees admit quasi-isometric embeddings into hyperbolic space (Sarkar, 2011), and Boolean lattices embed isometrically into ℓ₁ spaces but provably not into any hyperbolic space (Proposition 1). Unlike mixed-curvature product models (Gu et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2024) that use Riemannian ℓ₂ metrics with heterogeneous curvatures, PHyCLIP constrains all factors to negative curvature and uses the ℓ₁ product, which the ablation confirms is critical for compositional performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, theoretically grounded recipe for simultaneously representing hierarchy and compositionality in vision-language embeddings, resolving a tension that single-space hyperbolic models leave unresolved. The spontaneous specialization of factors to concept families (e.g., factor 39 → mammals, factor 9 → vehicles) without explicit supervision is a notable interpretability result. However, the Boolean-like composition mechanism is less sensitive to inter-object relations (modest drops on SugarCrepe relation-replacement and object-swap), and the authors acknowledge that relational structure remains unexplored. Open questions include whether richer algebraic structures (beyond Boolean) can be embedded in the product factors to capture relations, and whether the approach generalizes to open-vocabulary or multi-hop compositional settings.
