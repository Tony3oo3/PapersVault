## Problem

Existing code reasoning benchmarks overstate LLM proficiency due to two limitations: insufficient difficulty and scope (problems sourced from LeetCode or CodeForces rather than premier competitions like IOI and ICPC), and evaluation bias from low-quality test cases that fail to detect subtle errors in incorrect solutions. AetherCode addresses both by drawing problems from top-tier global programming competitions and constructing rigorously validated test suites.

## Input / Output

Each problem is presented as a Markdown+LaTeX statement (converted from original PDFs and manually proofread) specifying input/output formats, constraints, and time/memory limits. The model produces a complete program in a specified language (C++17, compiled with gcc 9.4.0, O2 optimization), which is then judged against an average of 47.15 test cases per problem. Results are reported as Pass@1, Pass@2, and Pass@4 across four difficulty tiers (Easy, Medium, Hard, Extreme) and ten algorithmic categories, with each model evaluated four times per problem.

## Method

AetherCode's construction pipeline has three stages. First, 456 problems are curated from OI-series (IOI, NOI, USACO, APIO, etc.) and ICPC-series (regional contests, championships, World Finals, CCPC) competitions spanning January 2024 to May 2025, with each problem annotated by competitive programming experts with difficulty, algorithmic tags (10 major categories, 144 sub-tags), and temporal metadata. Second, test cases are generated via a hybrid approach: a Generator-Validator (G-V) multi-agent system automatically produces diverse test inputs (random and corner cases) with a validator ensuring constraint compliance, achieving 89.9% TNR and 100% TPR on its own; then 67 competitive programming experts (Codeforces 2000+, several International Grandmasters) manually craft targeted test cases to reject all collected incorrect solutions, pushing TNR to 100%. Third, an elite audit team (each holding 3+ ICPC gold medals) performs a final review, supplementing missing corner cases and writing additional incorrect/inefficient solutions to verify comprehensiveness. The entire test suite is validated against 30,000+ human-written solutions (minimum 5 correct and 20 incorrect per problem), achieving 100% TPR and 100% TNR.

## Results

On AetherCode v1 (456 problems from premier OI/ICPC competitions, 2024–2025), the top reasoning model o4-mini-high achieves 35.5% Pass@1 and 46.6% Pass@4, while Gemini-2.5-Pro reaches 32.7% Pass@1 and 46.0% Pass@4. Reasoning models consistently outperform non-reasoning models across all difficulty tiers; for example, the best non-reasoning model (GPT-4.1) scores only 10.5% Pass@1 versus 35.5% for o4-mini-high. Even the strongest models solve fewer than 50% of problems with 4 sampling attempts, and only three models (o4-mini-high, Gemini-2.5-Pro, and Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking) achieve any success on the 'Extreme' difficulty tier. The benchmark achieves 100% TPR and 100% TNR on its test-case validation set of 30,000+ human solutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark paper, AetherCode is compared against 8 existing code-reasoning benchmarks (HumanEval, MBPP, APPS, USACO, CodeContests, LiveCodeBench, CodeELO, LiveCodeBench Pro) on design dimensions rather than model scores. AetherCode distinguishes itself by sourcing problems exclusively from premier OI/ICPC competitions (vs. LeetCode/CodeForces for most others), providing 456 problems with expert-validated test cases achieving 100% TPR/TNR (vs. handcrafted, mutation-based, or crawled test cases in prior work), and including the most recent problems (2024–2025) to mitigate contamination. Among the 17 evaluated LLMs, reasoning models dominate non-reasoning models by a wide margin: o4-mini-high (35.5% Pass@1) outperforms the best non-reasoning model GPT-4.1 (10.5%) by over 3×. Within reasoning models, o4-mini-high and Gemini-2.5-Pro form a clear elite tier, with Gemini-2.5-Pro showing the largest Pass@1→Pass@4 gain (+13.3 pp), indicating strong exploration potential. The Claude series exhibits a distinctive failure profile with ~50% of errors being Time Limit Exceeded (vs. ~70–80% Wrong Answer for other models), suggesting a tendency toward correct-but-inefficient algorithms. Across algorithmic categories, all models struggle most with Computational Geometry and Tree Problems, while excelling at Basic Algorithms and Strings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Generator-Validator (G-V) Agent System (Wang et al., 2025b) for automated test case construction, extending it with a human-in-the-loop validation step and expert annotation to achieve 100% TPR/TNR. Conceptually, it departs from prior code reasoning benchmarks (HumanEval, MBPP, LiveCodeBench, CodeELO, LiveCodeBench Pro) by sourcing problems exclusively from premier programming competitions (IOI, ICPC, CCPC, USACO) rather than online programming platforms, addressing two identified gaps: insufficient problem difficulty/scope and evaluation bias from low-quality test suites. The multi-agent test case pipeline combined with a 67-expert annotation team and elite ICPC gold-medalist audit represents a new quality standard for benchmark construction in this area.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AetherCode provides the field with a rigorous, self-contained benchmark that exposes a substantial gap between current LLMs and elite human programmers — even the best models (o4-mini-high, Gemini-2.5-Pro) achieve only ~35% Pass@1 overall and single-digit scores on Hard/Extreme problems. The 100% TPR/TNR test case standard and the systematic categorization (10 algorithmic domains, 4 difficulty levels, temporal metadata) enable fine-grained, contamination-aware tracking of model progress. Open questions include whether the benchmark can be kept ahead of rapidly improving models, whether the human-difficulty labels will remain meaningful as LLM capabilities shift, and whether the observed reasoning-vs-non-reasoning gap will narrow with future architectures.
