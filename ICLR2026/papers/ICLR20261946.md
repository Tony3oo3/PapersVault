## Problem

While physics-informed machine learning is empirically effective, the theoretical impact of physical priors on learning rates remains poorly understood, especially when training data is dependent (non-i.i.d.) as in dynamical system trajectories. Existing results either handle i.i.d. data with physics-informed regularization or dependent data without it, leaving open whether physical knowledge can recover optimal i.i.d.-like convergence rates under mixing without the sample-size deflation inherent to blocking techniques.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single dependent trajectory {(X_t, Y_t)}_{t=0}^{T-1} generated by a nonlinear dynamical system X_{t+1} = f_*(X_t) + W_t with sub-Gaussian martingale-difference noise, where the input domain Ω ⊆ [-L,L]^{d_X} has locally Lipschitz boundary and each marginal density p_t is bounded away from zero and infinity. The physics-informed regularizer is defined by a linear elliptic PDE operator D acting component-wise on the Sobolev space H^s with s ≥ 2d_X. The output is a function estimate f̂ in a Sobolev ball hypothesis space, accompanied by complexity-dependent excess risk bounds that explicitly depend on the alignment quantity Ψ(f_*) = ||D(f_*)||²_{L²}, yielding rates in both probability and expectation.

## Method

The paper formulates learning as regularized empirical risk minimization with a physics-informed regularizer Ψ(f) = ||D(f)||²_{L²}, where D is a linear elliptic operator encoding a known PDE constraint on the true dynamics. The core analytical tool is an adaptation of Mendelson's small-ball method to dependent data, replacing the i.i.d. small-ball condition with (C,2)-hypercontractivity on the boundary of L²-balls and S-persistence (a one-sided exponential inequality) to control the lower-isometry event. The excess risk is then bounded via the martingale offset complexity of the effective hypothesis class, whose self-normalizing structure (inherited from Liang et al.) prevents the mixing parameter from entering the rate. Under knowledge alignment (Ψ(f_*) ≈ 0), the slow Sobolev term O(T^{-d}) is annihilated, leaving the fast i.i.d.-optimal rate O(σ_W²/T), with data dependence appearing only in a burn-in time condition rather than deflating the effective sample size.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is a complexity-dependent excess risk bound: under knowledge alignment (Ψ(f⋆) ≈ 0), the rate improves from the slow Sobolev minimax rate O(T^{-d}) with d = 2s/(2s+d_X) < 1 to the fast i.i.d.-optimal rate O(T^{-1}), even with dependent (mixing) data. In the numerical unicycle experiment, the empirical excess risk decays at approximately O(T^{-0.646}) without physics-informed regularization versus O(T^{-0.993}) with it, confirming the theoretical speedup. The bound in expectation (Theorem 5.2) takes the form E[‖f̂−f⋆‖²] ≤ C_slow·Ψ(f⋆)^{d′/2}/T^d + C_fast·σ_W²/T, where the Sobolev term is annihilated when the physical prior is aligned.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is the first to combine non-i.i.d. (mixing) data with physics-informed regularization, filling a gap left by all five compared works. Compared to Ziemann & Tu (2022), which achieves σ_W²T^{−d} for non-i.i.d. data without regularization, this work adds the physics-informed term and shows the Sobolev rate is modulated by Ψ(f⋆)^{d′/2}, vanishing under knowledge alignment to yield O(T^{−1}). Compared to Doumèche et al. (2024), which achieves Ψ(f⋆)T^{−d}+σ_W²T^{−1} for i.i.d. data with physics-informed regularization, this paper handles dependent data without the sample-size deflation inherent to blocking techniques, and provides bounds in both probability and expectation (not just expectation). Against Farahmand & Szepesvári (2012), which gives T^{−d}log(T) for non-i.i.d. data without regularization, the physics-informed approach eliminates the log factor and the slow Sobolev term under alignment. The numerical unicycle experiment confirms the theory: the physics-informed estimator achieves an empirical slope of O(T^{−0.993}) versus O(T^{−0.646}) for the unregularized estimator, a roughly 1.5× speedup in the convergence exponent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate theoretical threads: (i) the small-ball method and complexity-dependent excess risk bounds for i.i.d. data with proper regularization (Mendelson 2014; Lecué & Mendelson 2017), and (ii) the adaptation of these tools to dependent data via martingale offset complexity (Liang et al. 2015; Ziemann & Tu 2022). The key departure is the introduction of a physics-informed elliptic PDE regularizer into the dependent-data setting, which had only been analyzed for i.i.d. observations (Doumèche et al. 2024). The authors must additionally handle weighted, vector-valued Sobolev spaces and trajectory hypercontractivity, which are absent from both prior lines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central result—that knowledge alignment (Ψ(f⋆) ≈ 0) eliminates the slow Sobolev rate term entirely, yielding the optimal i.i.d. rate O(T⁻¹) even under mixing—provides the first theoretical guarantee that physics-informed regularization can fully recover i.i.d.-optimal sample efficiency without the sample-size deflation inherent to blocking techniques. Limitations include the requirement s ≥ 2d_X (stronger than the standard Sobolev embedding threshold), the dependence of λ_T on the unknown Ψ(f⋆), and empirical validation restricted to a single low-dimensional unicycle system with one MLP architecture. Open questions include whether the result extends to non-elliptic operators, imperfect knowledge alignment, and higher-dimensional or more complex PDE-constrained systems.
