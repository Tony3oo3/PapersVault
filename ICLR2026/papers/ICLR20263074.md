## Problem

Parallel Tempering (PT) accelerates MCMC by swapping states across an annealing path, but its swap acceptance probability is governed by the overlap between adjacent distributions, which can be minimal in high-dimensional multimodal targets. This forces practitioners to increase the number of parallel chains to compensate, which may be infeasible. The paper asks whether neural samplers—normalizing flows, diffusion models, controlled diffusions—can be integrated into PT to reduce the required overlap while preserving PT's asymptotic consistency guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unnormalised target density π̃(x), a tractable reference distribution η (typically a standard Gaussian), and a trained neural transport network (a normalizing flow, a CMCD vector field with schedule and diffusion coefficient, or a VP diffusion score/energy model). It produces samples from the target, consistent expectation estimates, and—unusually as a by-product of the swap mechanism—free energy / normalising constant estimates via the geometric mean of forward and backward path weights accumulated during communication steps. The neural transport is trained in a self-supervised manner by minimising the symmetric KL divergence between forward and backward path measures, using samples from both adjacent annealing distributions available during PT execution.

## Method

APT replaces the standard PT swap with an accelerated swap: before proposing an exchange between adjacent chains n−1 and n, states are propagated forward in time using a learned forward accelerator P (starting from π^{n−1}) and backward in time using a learned backward accelerator Q (starting from π^n), producing path measures P_K^{n−1} and Q_K^n over K+1 intermediate states. The swap is then accepted with a Metropolis-Hastings correction based on the Radon-Nikodym derivative between these path measures, ensuring ergodicity and π-invariance regardless of transport quality. Three concrete variants are provided: NF-APT uses a deterministic normalizing flow and its inverse; CMCD-APT uses learned Gaussian transition kernels parameterised by a neural vector field, time schedule, and diffusion coefficient; Diff-APT uses K-step Euler-Maruyama integrators of a VP diffusion's forward and time-reversal SDEs with a learned energy-based model. The neural transport is trained to minimise the symmetric KL divergence between forward and backward path measures, and the entire procedure is parallelised across chains so that the per-iteration cost of neural evaluations is amortised. Theoretical analysis shows the rejection rate is bounded by the SKL between path measures, the round trip rate improves with K, and the global barrier Λ_K decreases with K, providing a principled route to reducing the number of chains needed.

## Results

On the 40-mode GMM-10 target with N=6 chains, CMCD-APT (K=5) achieves 1,743 round trips vs. 17 for classical PT (~100× improvement), and even compute-normalised round trips (CN-R) improve from 8.5 to 290.5. At N=30, CMCD-APT (K=5) reaches 6,231 round trips (CN-R=1,038.5), exceeding the theoretical asymptotic limit of classical PT (~5,349). On Alanine Dipeptide (66D), CMCD-APT (K=1) with only N=4 chains achieves 465 round trips (CN-R=232.5) versus 199 (CN-R=99.5) for PT, demonstrating significant acceleration even in a realistic molecular dynamics setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against classical PT (Swendsen & Wang, 1986), all three APT variants (NF-APT, CMCD-APT, Diff-APT) substantially outperform in both raw and compute-normalised round trips, with the largest gains (10×–100×) in the small-N regime where PT struggles most. At N=30, CMCD-APT (K=5) achieves CN-R=1,038.5 vs. PT's 944.0, and its 6,231 round trips exceed the theoretical asymptotic limit of PT (~5,349) predicted by Theorem 2. Against CMCD used as a direct neural sampler (Vargas et al., 2024), CMCD-APT dramatically outperforms: without the Metropolis correction, CMCD misallocates probability mass across modes (especially at small K), while CMCD-APT recovers correct mode weights and aligns closely with ground truth (Figure 4). Similarly, the VP diffusion model (Ho et al., 2020) used directly as a sampler performs better than CMCD but still misallocates mass, whereas Diff-APT with the Metropolis correction produces accurate samples. On Alanine Dipeptide, CMCD-APT (K=1) with N=4 chains achieves 2.3× more round trips and 2.3× higher CN-R than PT, confirming the advantage in a realistic 66D molecular dynamics target.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper formalizes the non-equilibrium switch framework from physics (Ballard & Jarzynski, 2009/2012) into a general MCMC framework called Accelerated Parallel Tempering (APT), which subsumes classical PT as the K=0 special case. It positions APT as the parallel-computation dual to SMC-based debiasing of neural samplers (Arbel et al., 2021; Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden, 2024), where annealing distributions are built in parallel rather than sequentially. The framework is strictly more general than prior work combining PT with normalizing flows (Invernizzi et al., 2022; Abbott et al., 2024), accommodating any learned transport between neighbouring distributions, and inherits the non-reversible communication and global barrier analysis from Syed et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

APT provides a principled way to integrate neural samplers (normalizing flows, CMCD, diffusion models) into MCMC while preserving asymptotic consistency via the Metropolis correction—addressing the mode-collapsing and bias issues that plague standalone neural samplers. The 10×–100× round-trip improvement in the small-N regime is practically significant for settings where parallel chains are constrained (e.g., molecular dynamics with limited replicas). Open questions include developing principled criteria for when APT's additional neural evaluations are justified over classical PT, reducing the training burden of the neural transports, and extending the framework to inference-time control of generative models.
