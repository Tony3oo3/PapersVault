## Problem

Diffusion-based LLMs (dLLMs) rely on bidirectional attention, making them incompatible with standard KV caching and forcing full-sequence KV recomputation at every decoding step. Existing approximate KV cache methods (dLLM-Cache, Fast-dLLM) partition the sequence into coarse-grained static and dynamic segments, which either reuse KV states that should be updated or update states that can be safely cached, limiting achievable speedups.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a fixed-length sequence of length L formed by concatenating a prompt segment with n masked tokens, and iteratively decodes over T steps until all masks are resolved. Unlike autoregressive models that append one token at a time, dLLMs replace multiple masked positions per step, and d²Cache selectively recomputes KV states for only a subset of tokens at each step while reusing cached states for the rest. The framework is entirely training-free and requires no architectural modification to the underlying dLLM.

## Method

d²Cache introduces a two-stage fine-grained token selection strategy that adaptively identifies which tokens' KV states to update at each decoding step. Stage 1 (certainty prior-guided selection) scores masked tokens by combining prediction confidence with a position-aware Gaussian-weighted density of known (prompt/decoded) tokens in the local context, selecting the top-k masked tokens whose KV states are in the critical rapid-change phase. Stage 2 (attention-aware selection) applies attention rollout over the remaining prompt and decoded tokens to identify a small high-attention subset whose KV states also need updating. The key empirical insight is that masked-token KV states evolve through three phases (gradual-change, rapid-change, stable) and only the rapid-change phase requires recomputation, while attention in dLLMs is highly concentrated on a small subset of prompt/decoded tokens. As a side benefit, the certainty prior naturally enables quasi left-to-right decoding that mitigates premature overconfidence in sequence-ending tokens.

## Results

On LLaDA-Inst, d²Cache achieves an average throughput of 12.24 tok/s (3.5× speedup over Vanilla) with a score of 44.4, outperforming dLLM-Cache (8.16 tok/s, 43.8) and Fast-dLLM (9.38 tok/s, 43.4). On Dream-Inst, d²Cache reaches 12.89 tok/s (3.5× speedup) with a score of 53.4, compared to dLLM-Cache (7.64 tok/s, 50.9) and Fast-dLLM (9.59 tok/s, 51.7). The peak speedup reaches 4.7× on Dream-Inst/GSM8K (2.62→12.25 tok/s) and 6.3× on LLaDA-Base/MMLU-Pro. Notably, d²Cache improves generation quality over Vanilla on average (44.4 vs 43.6 on LLaDA-Inst; 53.4 vs 51.4 on Dream-Inst) while delivering the largest speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

d²Cache consistently outperforms both dLLM-Cache and Fast-dLLM on average across all six benchmarks for both LLaDA-Inst and Dream-Inst. On LLaDA-Inst, d²Cache achieves 3.5× average speedup (12.24 tok/s) versus 2.3× for dLLM-Cache (8.16 tok/s) and 2.7× for Fast-dLLM (9.38 tok/s), while also scoring highest (44.4 vs 43.8 and 43.4). On Dream-Inst, the gap widens: d²Cache reaches 3.5× speedup (12.89 tok/s) with a score of 53.4, compared to dLLM-Cache's 2.1× (7.64 tok/s, 50.9) and Fast-dLLM's 2.6× (9.59 tok/s, 51.7). The largest individual gains appear on code-generation tasks: on Dream-Inst/HumanEval, d²Cache achieves 61.6 vs 56.5 (dLLM-Cache) and 56.1 (Fast-dLLM), a +4.9 to +5.5 point improvement. The only notable exception is MMLU-Pro on LLaDA-Inst, where d²Cache scores 33.1 versus 38.1 (dLLM-Cache) and 37.1 (Fast-dLLM), though it still delivers the highest throughput (5.4×). Overall, d²Cache wins on both efficiency and quality in the majority of settings, with the advantage attributed to its fine-grained token-level selection versus the coarse segment-level strategies of the baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of approximate KV caching for diffusion-based LLMs initiated by dLLM-Cache, Fast-dLLM, and dKV-Cache, all of which exploit the observation that KV states remain similar across consecutive decoding steps. However, those prior methods operate at a coarse-grained segment level (prompt vs. response, or block-wise), applying uniform update policies within each segment. d²Cache departs by operating at the fine-grained token level, repurposing attention rollout (originally an ARM analysis tool) to identify which specific tokens warrant KV updates, and introducing a certainty-prior score that leverages the localized decoding order empirically observed in dLLMs. The two-stage selection (certainty-prior for masked tokens, attention-aware for prompt/decoded tokens) is a novel combination not present in any single prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.5× average speedup with equal-or-better quality is practically significant for dLLM deployment, and the training-free nature means it can be applied to any existing dLLM without retraining. The certainty-prior-guided decoding offers a principled alternative to semi-AR block decoding that mitigates premature EOS overconfidence, which is a known failure mode in LLaDA-Instruct. Key limitations include evaluation only on 7–8B dense models (the only publicly available dLLMs at time of writing), the attention-rollout computation cost for larger models, and the Gaussian scoring function being a fixed heuristic rather than context-adaptive. Whether the fine-grained token-level strategy scales to much larger dLLMs or to MoE architectures remains an open question.
