## Problem

Video diffusion models demand substantial computational and memory resources, making deployment on consumer hardware infeasible. While quantization has achieved notable success for image diffusion models, its direct application to video DMs at extremely low bit-widths (≤4-bit) remains ineffective, with existing QAT methods causing severe video quality degradation. This paper identifies that poor convergence—manifested as high gradient norms during QAT—is the root cause and proposes a targeted solution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265255/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated video clip (49 frames at 480p/8fps for CogVideoX-2B, 81 frames at 480p/16fps or 720p/16fps for other models). The quantized DiT operates with asymmetric uniform quantization at W4A4 or W3A3 (per-channel weights, per-token activations), and the final model uses standard low-bit multiplication kernels with no additional inference overhead beyond the quantized linear layers themselves.

## Method

QVGen introduces two key mechanisms for low-bit QAT of video DMs. First, it adds learnable auxiliary modules Φ (initialized with the weight quantization error W − Q_b(W)) to each quantized linear layer, which mitigate quantization-induced errors and reduce the gradient norm ‖g_t‖₂, as justified by a regret-based convergence analysis showing that minimizing ‖g_t‖₂ is critical for QAT convergence. Second, to eliminate the inference overhead of Φ, it employs a rank-decay strategy: W_Φ is decomposed via SVD into low-rank factors L and R, and a rank-based regularization γ (with cosine-annealed decay from 1 to 0) progressively zeros out the lowest-contributing singular components. This decompose-then-decay cycle repeats with halving rank (λ=1/2) until Φ is fully removed, yielding a standard quantized model with negligible performance loss (<0.6% on most metrics) and zero extra inference cost.

## Results

On CogVideoX-2B at W4A4, QVGen achieves 60.16 Imaging Quality and 67.22 Dynamic Degree, matching or exceeding the full-precision BF16 model (59.15 / 67.78) across most VBench metrics. At W3A3, QVGen delivers 53.89 Dynamic Degree and 23.85 Scene Consistency on CogVideoX-2B, improving over the best prior QAT baseline (EfficientDM) by +25.28 and +8.43 respectively. On Wan 1.3B at W4A4, QVGen reaches 77.78 Dynamic Degree (vs. 70.28 full-precision) and 63.08 Imaging Quality (vs. 64.30 BF16), while at W3A3 it achieves 84.14 Dynamic Degree and 67.35 Imaging Quality. For the 14B Wan model at W4A4, QVGen incurs only ~1% overall score drop on VBench-2.0 relative to full precision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QVGen decisively outperforms all five baselines across both models and bit-widths. At W4A4 on CogVideoX-2B, QVGen's Dynamic Degree (67.22) exceeds Q-DM (48.61) by +18.61, EfficientDM (46.67) by +20.55, and LSQ (45.00) by +22.22, while also surpassing the PTQ methods ViDiT-Q (43.22) and SVDQuant (40.83/42.22) by over 25 points. At W3A3, the gap widens dramatically: QVGen achieves 53.89 Dynamic Degree on CogVideoX-2B versus 28.61 (EfficientDM), 5.56 (Q-DM), and 0.56 (LSQ), and 84.14 on Wan 1.3B versus 76.94 (Q-DM), 70.28 (EfficientDM), and 23.61 (LSQ). Scene Consistency, the hardest metric to preserve, shows QVGen leading by +8.43 over EfficientDM at W3A3 on CogVideoX-2B (23.85 vs. 15.42) and by +2.04 over Q-DM at W4A4 on Wan 1.3B (15.32 vs. 13.28). The PTQ baselines (ViDiT-Q, SVDQuant) fail to produce meaningful video content under standard per-channel/per-token W4A4 settings and require relaxed W4A6 or fine-grained group-wise quantization to remain competitive, at which point QVGen still leads by 8–15 points on Aesthetic Quality and Subject Consistency. Overall, QVGen is the first method to achieve full-precision-comparable quality at W4A4 and the only one that maintains usable video quality at W3A3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the KD-based QAT framework of Q-DM (Li et al., 2023b) and the learned quantization parameters of LSQ (Esser et al., 2020b) from image diffusion models to video diffusion transformers, identifying that the temporal dynamics in video generation produce substantially higher gradient norms that break standard QAT convergence at ≤4 bits. It departs from prior quantization work by introducing a theoretical regret analysis that pinpoints gradient norm reduction as the critical convergence bottleneck, then proposes auxiliary modules (Φ) initialized with quantization error to stabilize training. The rank-decay strategy for progressively eliminating Φ via iterative SVD and rank-based regularization is a novel mechanism not seen in prior pruning or quantization literature, distinguishing it from simple linear decay, sparsification, or residual quantization approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QVGen is the first method to achieve full-precision comparable video generation quality at 4-bit with zero additional inference overhead, enabling ~4× model size reduction and ~3× speedup on consumer GPUs — a practical milestone for deploying billion-parameter video DMs beyond datacenter hardware. The results scale convincingly from 1.3B to 14B parameters, and the 3-bit results (e.g., +25 Dynamic Degree over prior best on CogVideoX-2B) open a path toward even more aggressive compression. Open questions include whether the rank-decay strategy generalizes to 2-bit settings, whether the auxiliary module approach transfers to other generative modalities (e.g., NLP), and how to further reduce the modest training overhead (~2% more GPU-days) relative to naive QAT.
