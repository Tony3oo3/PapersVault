## Problem

Sequential SFT→RL and RL→SFT pipelines for LLM reasoning suffer from fundamental incompatibilities: SFT induces coarse-grained global distribution shifts that overfit to demonstrations, while RL performs fine-grained selective optimization but is sample-inefficient and cannot exploit existing demonstrations. The critical gap is the absence of a principled single-stage method that unifies both paradigms with a dynamic mechanism to balance their complementary strengths without the learning tax of stage transitions.

## Input / Output

SRFT takes mathematical problem prompts as input and generates full reasoning chains (up to 8,192 tokens) as output, evaluated via binary correctness rewards. During training, the model simultaneously consumes two data streams: (1) off-policy demonstration traces from DeepSeek-R1 (46k samples from OpenR1-Math-46k-8192) and (2) on-policy self-exploration rollouts (8 trajectories per prompt). The demonstration data is augmented into the GRPO rollout group for joint advantage estimation, and the behavior policy for off-policy importance sampling is set to a uniform distribution (π_β=1) to avoid tokenizer alignment between teacher and student.

## Method

SRFT unifies SFT and RL in a single training stage through three loss components: (1) an entropy-weighted SFT loss on demonstrations (w_SFT = 0.5·stop_grad(exp(−H(π_θ)))) that performs coarse-grained policy approximation, (2) an off-policy RL loss on demonstrations using GRPO-style clipped importance sampling with augmented group advantage estimation, and (3) a self-rollout RL loss decomposed into positive-sample (likelihood maximization) and negative-sample (likelihood minimization) objectives, where the positive component is gated by an entropy-adaptive weight w_RL = 0.1·stop_grad(exp(H(π_θ))) to prevent premature entropy collapse. The key insight is that policy entropy serves as a dynamic indicator: high entropy (uncertainty) suppresses SFT influence to avoid distribution mismatch, while low entropy (confidence) suppresses positive-sample RL to preserve exploration. This entropy-aware weighting replaces the need for manual stage-switching or fixed hyperparameter balancing, enabling direct optimization toward better solutions across an expanded policy space.

## Results

On five in-distribution mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, AMC, MATH500, Minerva, OlympiadBench), SRFT achieves an average accuracy of 59.5% using Qwen2.5-Math-7B, outperforming the best zero-RL baseline (Oat-Zero, 50.1%) by +9.4 points and the best SFT+RL integrated method (LUFFY, 55.5%) by +4.0 points. On three out-of-distribution benchmarks (ARC-C, GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Pro), SRFT reaches 62.5% average, exceeding the best zero-RL baseline (OpenReasoner-Zero, 51.6%) by +10.9 points. SRFT also surpasses the sequential SFT→RL pipeline (54.5%) by +5.0 points and the naive SFT+RL combination (52.3%) by +7.2 points on in-distribution tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRFT outperforms all seven compared baselines on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution benchmarks. Against pure RL methods, SRFT leads GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) by +10.2 points in-distribution (59.5 vs. 49.3) and +12.8 points OOD (62.5 vs. 49.7); it surpasses SimpleRL-Zero (Zeng et al., 2025) by +16.0 and +33.2 points respectively, and OpenReasoner-Zero (Hu et al., 2025a) by +13.3 and +10.9 points. Among integrated SFT+RL methods, SRFT beats LUFFY (Yan et al., 2025) by +4.0 in-distribution and +4.7 OOD, TAPO (Wu et al., 2025) by +3.8 and +6.1, and ReLIFT (Ma et al., 2025) by +5.5 and +6.6. The largest gap is against PRIME-Zero (Cui et al., 2025), where SRFT gains +13.2 in-distribution and +21.1 OOD, highlighting the benefit of combining high-quality demonstrations with self-exploration in a single stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

SRFT builds directly on the GRPO framework (Shao et al., 2024) for RL and adopts the off-policy demonstration augmentation strategy from LUFFY (Yan et al., 2025), but departs from both by introducing entropy-aware dynamic weighting that replaces fixed or heuristic-based balancing of SFT and RL objectives. It positions itself against a growing family of integrated SFT+RL methods (UFT, ReLIFT, TAPO, SASR, CHORD) that either interleave the two paradigms or use static/dynamic blending, arguing that a principled entropy-based mechanism is needed to avoid the 'learning tax' observed when sequential SFT→RL optimization directions conflict. The paper's core analytical contribution—characterizing SFT as coarse-grained global distribution shifts versus RL as fine-grained selective modifications, with entropy as the unifying indicator—provides the theoretical grounding that prior integrated methods lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The entropy-based analysis offers a practical diagnostic for practitioners: monitoring policy entropy during training can signal when SFT is overfitting or when RL has collapsed exploration, enabling more informed hyperparameter decisions. The finding that RL reduces model plasticity (entropy plateau) making subsequent SFT ineffective provides a concrete explanation for why sequential pipelines underperform, with implications beyond mathematical reasoning. Open questions remain: whether the entropy-aware weighting generalizes to agentic RL or multi-turn settings, how SRFT behaves with imperfect or noisy demonstrations, and whether the benefits persist over significantly extended training horizons where persistent demonstration reliance might constrain exploration. The method is currently validated only at 7B scale on mathematical reasoning, leaving open whether the entropy dynamics and weighting mechanism transfer to larger models or non-mathematical domains.
