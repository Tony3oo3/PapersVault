## Problem

BOA improved upon GPTQ by incorporating inter-layer dependencies within attention modules for more accurate low-bit quantization, but its requirement to quantize out-channels strictly sequentially (one at a time) makes it substantially slower than GPTQ. The paper asks whether this sequential bottleneck can be eliminated without sacrificing the accuracy gains from attention-aware error compensation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264638/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM Q/K/V projection weight matrices, 128 calibration sequences of length 2048 from WikiText-2, a target bit-width b, and a hyperparameter N specifying how many out-channels to quantize jointly. In addition to the standard quantized integer weights and per-channel scales, the algorithm requires the full-precision (FP) input activations \tilde{X} to compute the input deviation ΔX = X − \tilde{X}, which captures error propagated from previously quantized layers. The output is a set of quantized weights Q = diag(s)·W_int for each attention projection, ready for deployment at the target bit-width.

## Method

TURBOBOA introduces three innovations on top of BOA's attention-aware Hessian framework (H = H_in ⊗ H_out). First, it quantizes N out-channels simultaneously rather than one at a time, deriving a closed-form update rule (Proposition 3.1) that compensates for the joint quantization error of all N channels using the Cholesky factor of H_out^{-1}, reducing sequential operations by a factor of N (e.g., 128→8 for N=16). Second, it explicitly compensates for errors propagated from preceding quantized layers by incorporating the input deviation ΔX into the error compensation objective (Proposition 3.2), yielding an additional correction term involving R = ΔX·X^T. Third, it adaptively recomputes quantization grids immediately before each joint quantization step and refines the per-channel scales via a single-pass coordinate descent that minimizes the attention reconstruction error (Proposition 3.3), ensuring grid alignment with iteratively updated weights. Together, these changes yield a >3× speedup over BOA while consistently improving PPL and zero-shot accuracy across Llama models at INT2 and INT3.

## Results

Under INT2 weight-only quantization with QuaRot, TURBOBOA achieves Wiki2 PPL of 33.33 on Llama3.2-1B, 24.10 on Llama3.2-3B, 13.54 on Llama3-8B, 9.11 on Llama2-7B, and 7.34 on Llama2-13B, consistently outperforming BOA (40.86, 33.40, 15.24, 10.42, 8.24) and GPTQ (54.28, 52.18, 18.28, 22.05, 9.59). On zero-shot commonsense reasoning (INT2, QuaRot), TURBOBOA reaches 40.31% (1B), 45.85% (3B), 52.59% (8B), 53.27% (7B), and 59.69% (13B), surpassing BOA by 1.6–2.8%p and GPTQ by 4.3–10.2%p. In weight-activation quantization (W2A4KV4, SpinQuant), TURBOBOA reduces Llama3.2-1B Wiki2 PPL from 77.05 (BOA) to 63.07 and Llama3-8B from 18.23 to 16.43. TURBOBOA also achieves a >3× speedup over BOA (e.g., 4.36 min vs. 13.32 min for Llama3.2-1B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TURBOBOA consistently outperforms BOA (its direct predecessor) across all model scales and bit-widths: on INT2/QuaRot, it reduces Llama3.2-1B Wiki2 PPL from 40.86 to 33.33 (−18.4%) and Llama2-13B zero-shot accuracy from 56.92% to 59.69% (+2.8%p), while also being >3× faster. Against GPTQ, the gap is dramatic at INT2 (e.g., 33.33 vs. 54.28 PPL on 1B; 59.69% vs. 49.51% zero-shot on 13B), confirming that attention-aware inter-channel dependencies are critical in low-bit regimes. Compared to GPTAQ (which also compensates for cross-layer errors but assumes H_out = I), TURBOBOA achieves 7.7–10.5%p higher zero-shot accuracy on Llama3 models at INT2 without transformation. Among transformation-based methods, TURBOBOA paired with QuaRot outperforms OmniQuant+RTN, DuQuant+RTN, and SpinQuant+RTN/GPTQ on both PPL and zero-shot accuracy at INT2 and INT3. In weight-activation quantization, TURBOBOA with OSTQuant under W2A4KV16 achieves 36.43 Wiki2 PPL on Llama3.2-1B, beating BOA (44.90) and GPTQ (71.49) by large margins, and with SpinQuant under W2A4KV4 on Llama2-13B reaches 55.86% zero-shot accuracy, exceeding BOA by 3.4%p and GPTQ by 16.4%p.

## Relation to Prior Work

TURBOBOA extends BOA (Kim et al., ICML 2025), which itself improved upon GPTQ by replacing the layer-wise Hessian with an attention-aware Hessian (H_out ≠ I) that captures inter-channel dependencies within attention modules. The key departure from BOA is replacing its fully sequential out-channel quantization with joint quantization of N out-channels via a closed-form error compensation rule (Proposition 3.1), while retaining the attention-aware Hessian structure. The input-deviation compensation mechanism (Proposition 3.2) is motivated by GPTAQ (Li et al., 2025) but differs technically in handling a general dense H_out rather than the identity assumption. The method is designed to be composable with transformation-based outlier suppression (QuaRot, SpinQuant, OSTQuant), maintaining the standard two-stage PTQ pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3×+ speedup over BOA is practically significant—saving 9–12 hours on 70B models—while simultaneously improving accuracy (e.g., 33.33 vs. 40.86 Wiki2 PPL on Llama3.2-1B INT2), making attention-aware quantization far more accessible. The framework is general: the closed-form update rules apply to any Kronecker-structured Hessian, so future improvements to Hessian approximation can be plugged in without re-derivation. An open question the authors flag is the lack of a formal theoretical bound on accuracy degradation as a function of N (the number of jointly quantized out-channels), which currently rests on empirical evidence. The method remains PTQ and requires calibration data, so it does not address the fundamental limitations of quantization without any reference data.
