## Problem

Existing abstract world models for robot planning assume the world changes only through the agent's direct, instantaneous actions, ignoring exogenous processes (e.g., water heating, dominoes cascading) that unfold autonomously on their own timescales. Standard symbolic representations like PDDL treat actions as instantaneous, and even temporal extensions (PDDL 2.1, PDDL+) do not explicitly model autonomous exogenous dynamics with stochastic delays. The paper addresses how to learn abstract world models that capture both endogenous and exogenous causal processes with variable delays from limited data, enabling planning that generalizes to unseen tasks.

## Input / Output

The agent receives RGB visual observations and object tracking features (3D positions, colors, etc.) from PyBullet physics simulations, along with 1–2 demonstration trajectories and a small set of initial predicates and endogenous skills (Pick, Place, Push, Switch On/Off). It outputs two artifacts: (1) an abstract world model comprising learned predicates (short Python classifier functions) and causal process schemas with typed parameters, condition sets, effect sets, log-strength weights, and discrete delay distributions; and (2) a plan as a sequence of ground endogenous processes (high-level actions) found via A* search over big-step transitions that clump together stretches of constant abstract state.

## Method

ExoPredicator jointly learns symbolic state abstractions and causal processes through an online loop of planning, interaction, and model refinement. Causal processes are formalized as schema tuples (parameters, condition-at-start, condition-overall, add/delete effects, log-strength, delay distribution), distinguishing endogenous (agent-initiated) from exogenous (environment-triggered, edge-activated) processes. A probabilistic generative model over abstract state sequences combines per-feature cause-effect potentials with a frame axiom, and continuous parameters (delay distributions, log-strengths) are learned via variational inference with a change of basis from delays to absolute arrival times. Discrete structure learning for exogenous processes uses Bayesian model selection (minimum description length prior + approximate marginal likelihood) over LLM-proposed candidate condition sets, while predicate invention uses LLM prompting followed by local hill-climbing search over the powerset of candidates. Planning employs a big-step transition function that simulates the world model until the abstract state changes, combined with an A* search guided by an adapted Fast-Forward heuristic that counts only endogenous processes toward the heuristic cost, treating exogenous processes as zero-cost.

## Results

ExoPredicator achieves near-perfect solve rates (≈100%) on Coffee, Domino, and Fan, and over 80% on Boil and Grow, using only 1–2 demonstration trajectories and converging within at most 3 online learning iterations. It matches or exceeds a manually engineered abstraction (Manual) across all five domains, with the advantage attributed to learned delay parameters via variational inference. In contrast, MAPLE fails to achieve high success even with 1000× more interaction rollouts, ViLA (zero-shot) performs poorly in all domains, and VisPred is limited by its STRIPS-style inductive bias that cannot represent exogenous processes with variable delays.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MAPLE (Nasiriany et al., 2022), an HRL baseline given 1000 training tasks and 10,000 rollouts per iteration, ExoPredicator wins decisively in all five domains; the authors attribute MAPLE's failure to sparse-reward exploration difficulty and the absence of an explicit world model for exogenous dynamics. Against ViLA (Hu et al., 2023), a VLM planning baseline using Gemini-2.5-Pro, ExoPredicator outperforms both the zero-shot variant (which fails in every domain) and the few-shot variant (which only succeeds in simple, demonstration-similar tasks like Coffee but degrades sharply in Grow, Boil, and Domino where compositional generalization or additional rules are needed). Against VisPred / VisualPredicator (Liang et al., 2024), a STRIPS-style operator learning agent, ExoPredicator wins because VisPred's inductive bias restricts preconditions to atoms already present in an operator's effects, making it unable to model exogenous processes (e.g., water filling, domino cascading) that have no agent variable in their conditions, and it cannot learn variable delays across processes. ExoPredicator also matches or surpasses the Manual baseline (hand-engineered predicates and processes), with the gap in Grow and Domino explained by learned delay parameters versus manually tuned ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of neuro-symbolic world-model learning for robot planning (VisualPredicator, Silver et al. 2023) by replacing instantaneous STRIPS-style operators with causal processes that model both endogenous actions and exogenous environmental dynamics with stochastic delays. It builds on the Bayesian model selection framework of Chitnis et al. (2022) for symbolic structure learning, but augments it with LLM-guided candidate generation and variational inference over delay distributions. The approach departs from classical temporal planning (PDDL 2.1, PDDL+) by learning the domain model from limited trajectories rather than assuming it is given, and from hierarchical RL (MAPLE, options) by explicitly modeling exogenous processes as separate causal mechanisms rather than absorbing them into option transition models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in robot planning: most learned world models assume the world changes only through the agent's instantaneous actions, ignoring concurrent exogenous processes (water heating, domino cascades) that are ubiquitous in real environments. The finding that learned abstractions match or exceed manually engineered ones, and that a small number of demonstrations suffice where 1000× more interactions with HRL fail, suggests that explicit causal structure learning is a more sample-efficient path to long-horizon planning than end-to-end reward-based methods. Key limitations include the simplified perception assumption (direct access to object features rather than raw pixels), the restriction to five tabletop PyBullet environments, and the failure to learn disjunctive conditions (e.g., the full spill condition in Boil). Open questions include scaling to noisier, partially observable, larger-scale environments and the interplay between skill learning and world-model learning.
