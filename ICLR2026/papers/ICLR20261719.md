## Problem

KL-regularized RL for generative model post-training is widely assumed to suffer from mode collapse due to optimization dynamics or algorithmic choices, but this paper shows the collapse is baked into the objective itself. The KL regularizer jointly determines both the target distribution's shape and the divergence being minimized, and under common settings (low β, equal verifiable rewards, or skewed reference supports), the globally optimal target is unimodal by construction—meaning even perfect optimization yields a non-diverse policy regardless of the algorithm used.

## Input / Output

MARA operates as a drop-in modification to any standard KL-regularized RL pipeline: it consumes the same trajectory batch, reward function, reference distribution, and β as vanilla RL, plus a single additional hyperparameter τ (reward threshold). For samples with R(y) ≥ τ, the reward is replaced in-place using an anchor sample's reference probability; below τ, rewards are unchanged. The threshold τ can be a fixed constant (when the reward range is known, e.g., verifiable tasks or drug discovery screening levels) or computed per-batch as a percentile of sampled rewards (e.g., 90th percentile for unbounded reward models). The output is standard updated policy parameters, but the implicit target distribution now assigns uniform high probability mass across all high-reward regions rather than concentrating on a single mode.

## Method

The paper first proves via variational-inference-style analysis that the reverse-KL regularized objective's gradient is equivalent to minimizing reverse KL toward a target G_β(y) ∝ π_ref(y) exp(R(y)/β), and derives a closed-form log-probability ratio (Proposition 4.1) showing that equal-reward samples retain their reference probability ratio (never promoting low-support answers) while small reward differences at small β produce exponentially large probability gaps. Building on this, MARA (Mode Anchored Reward Augmentation) augments the reward for all samples above threshold τ by replacing R(y) with R(z) + β(log π_ref(z) − log π_ref(y)), where z is the anchor sample with the highest π_ref among high-reward samples. This causes the log π_ref(y) terms to cancel in the target distribution's exponent, yielding a target with uniform density over all high-reward regions while preserving the reference shape below τ. The method requires only a two-line pseudocode change (Algorithm 1) and works identically with either forward or reverse KL regularization, outperforming vanilla RL and diversity baselines on LLM creative QA, verifiable multi-answer tasks, and chemical language model drug discovery.

## Results

On the non-verifiable creative QA task (Qwen3-1.7B, NoveltyBench evaluation), MARA with forward KL achieves the best out-of-distribution reward (1.604±0.113) and the best diversity metrics—Ngrams EAD (0.568±0.012) and Mean Distinct functional classes (4.62±0.258)—while maintaining competitive in-distribution reward (15.33). On the verifiable 1-2 task, MARA preserves near-uniform generation over both correct answers (1 and 2) where vanilla GRPO collapses to a single answer across nearly all β values and seeds. For drug discovery (SYNTH task, Screen=0.80), MARA improves Yield from 6569±186 to 6834±78 unique molecules and reduces OB100 from 1042±66 to 1015±55 reward-function calls over REINVENT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MARA (forward KL) outperforms all six baselines on out-of-distribution reward, achieving 1.604 vs. the best baseline Unlikely (1.381) and GRPO (1.317), a gap of ~0.22 over the strongest competitor. On diversity, MARA leads on Ngrams EAD (0.568 vs. 0.541 for BoN Training) and Mean Distinct (4.62 vs. 4.24 for Unlikely), while matching GRPO on Semantic Div (0.193) and trailing only Entropy regularization (0.228) on that single metric. BoN Training achieves the highest in-distribution reward (16.88) but catastrophically degrades out-of-distribution reward (0.596) and Mean Distinct (2.29), confirming the quality–diversity tradeoff that MARA avoids. Entropy regularization collapses in-distribution reward to 1.44, rendering it non-competitive. For drug discovery, MARA consistently improves Yield and reduces OB100 over REINVENT across both SYNTH and ALL-AMIDE tasks at both screening levels, with the largest gains at the stricter Screen=0.85 threshold (e.g., Yield 2196 vs. 1614 on SYNTH).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the variational inference interpretation of KL-regularized RL established by Korbak et al. (2022), Go et al. (2023), and Zhang & Ranganath (2025), but departs sharply from the standard 'mode-seeking vs. mass-covering' framing by showing that with flexible policy families, both KLs can approximate complex posteriors—the real issue is the shape of the target distribution itself. It positions itself against a growing body of diversity-preserving methods (entropy regularization, unlikeliness rewards, count-based exploration, weight ensembling) by arguing the root cause is the objective's construction, not the optimization algorithm. The forward-KL analysis (showing the gradient is not a forward-KL gradient toward any πθ-independent target) also corrects a common assumption in recent work like GRPO that may have incidentally estimated forward KL while intending reverse KL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes diversity collapse in RL post-training from an algorithmic/optimization problem to a property of the objective itself, which has broad implications for how the field designs and debugs RL training pipelines. MARA's two-line reward augmentation is a practical, drop-in fix validated across LLMs and chemical language models, but open questions remain: the threshold τ is a hyperparameter in unbounded-reward settings, MARA does not guarantee faster convergence to the multimodal target, and the uniform-mass-over-high-reward-regions target may not be optimal for all tasks. The finding that forward-KL regularization does not produce a forward-KL gradient also has implications for the correctness of several recent algorithms.
