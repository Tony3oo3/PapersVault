## Problem

Reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) for LLM reasoning is fundamentally limited by the 'learning cliff': when a model encounters problems beyond its current capability, all rollouts fail, rewards remain persistently zero, and the GRPO advantage signal collapses to zero, making these problems invisible to the learning gradient. Existing remedies based on off-policy prefix-continuation (e.g., LUFFY) introduce distributional mismatch between teacher-generated prefixes and student-generated suffixes, requiring complex algorithmic corrections and constraining the model to a predetermined reasoning path.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a math problem as a text prompt and produces a step-by-step reasoning chain ending with a final answer in \boxed{} format. During training, when the learning cliff is triggered (all N=8 rollouts fail), the prompt is conditionally augmented with a pre-generated hierarchical hint (knowledge, planning, or solution tier, each containing 4 progressive items) injected as a 'Solution/Planning/Knowledge Hints' field in the user query. Hints are generated offline by DeepSeek-R1 from ground-truth solutions, and the maximum response length is 2048 tokens (8192 for LongCoT models).

## Method

Scaf-GRPO is a two-phase, on-policy training framework that augments the GRPO trajectory group only when a learning cliff is detected. Phase 1 (guidance exemption, first 15% of steps) allows autonomous exploration to distinguish 'true-hard' problems from 'pseudo-hard' ones. Phase 2 activates a deterministic search through a three-tier hint hierarchy (knowledge → planning → solution, each with 4 incremental levels), injecting the most abstract hint that enables a correct on-policy rollout. The successful hint-conditioned trajectory replaces one failed trajectory in the group, restoring non-zero reward variance and a meaningful advantage signal. Critically, the GRPO loss function is mathematically unchanged; only the batch composition is modified, and the probability ratio for the guided trajectory is computed with respect to the hint-augmented prompt (not the original), preserving on-policy integrity and avoiding the distributional mismatch inherent in off-policy prefix-continuation methods.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Scaf-GRPO achieves a 50.9% average pass@1 across seven math benchmarks, a 12.6% relative improvement over vanilla GRPO (45.2%) and a 44.3% relative gain on AIME24 (43.3% vs. 30.0%). The method also outperforms the strongest prefix-based baseline LUFFY by 9.2% relative (50.9% vs. 46.6% avg) and Oat-Zero by 9.5%. Gains are consistent across architectures (Qwen, Llama), scales (1.5B–7B), and model types (math-tuned, instruction-tuned, LongCoT), with a 15.5% relative OOD improvement on GPQA-Diamond.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Scaf-GRPO outperforms all four baselines on the primary Qwen2.5-Math-7B model. Against vanilla GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), it achieves a 12.6% relative average improvement (50.9% vs. 45.2%), with the largest single-benchmark gain on AIME24 (43.3% vs. 30.0%, +44.3% relative). Against SimpleRL-Zero (Zeng et al., 2025), Scaf-GRPO leads by 19.5% relative (50.9% vs. 42.6%), and against Oat-Zero (Liu et al., 2025a) by 9.5% relative (50.9% vs. 46.5%). Most notably, Scaf-GRPO surpasses the prefix-continuation method LUFFY (Yan et al., 2025) by 9.2% relative (50.9% vs. 46.6%), demonstrating that in-prompt scaffolding is more effective than off-policy prefix guidance. On the OOD GPQA-Diamond benchmark, Scaf-GRPO matches LUFFY (37.3%) while exceeding vanilla GRPO by 15.5% relative, confirming that the gains reflect genuine reasoning improvement rather than in-domain overfitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

Scaf-GRPO builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), preserving its clipped surrogate objective while modifying only the data distribution fed into the loss. It departs from the dominant prefix-continuation paradigm for addressing the learning cliff (LUFFY/Yan et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025a), which mixes off-policy teacher trajectories with student rollouts and requires complex importance-sampling corrections. Instead, it draws on pedagogical scaffolding theory (Berk & Winsler, 1995) to inject hierarchical, in-prompt hints that keep the model on-policy while progressively reducing guidance from abstract knowledge to concrete solution steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The learning cliff is a fundamental bottleneck in RLVR that renders the hardest problems invisible to the gradient, and Scaf-GRPO provides a clean, conservative mechanism to unlock them without altering the optimization objective. The framework's model-agnosticism (validated across Qwen, Llama, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill at 1.5B–7B scale) and OOD generalization on GPQA-Diamond suggest it cultivates transferable reasoning skills rather than memorized patterns. Key limitations include the non-trivial data preparation required to generate tiered hints and the restriction to tasks with verifiable solutions; open questions include automating hint generation, adaptive scaffolding that fades with proficiency, and extension to open-ended domains.
