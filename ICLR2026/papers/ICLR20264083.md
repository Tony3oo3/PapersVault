## Problem

Transformers require adaptive optimizers like AdamW for effective training, while vanilla momentum SGD (mSGDW) significantly underperforms due to heavy-tailed gradient distributions. This paper asks whether the Transformer architecture itself can be modified to eliminate the heavy-tail gradient problem, thereby enabling mSGDW to match AdamW performance without the extra optimizer memory overhead.

## Input / Output

For vision (V-DNT), 224×224 RGB images are converted to patch embeddings and produce ImageNet class labels; for language (L-DNT), text token sequences of length 1024 (GPT-2 tokenizer) produce next-token predictions on OpenWebText. Model scales range from 124M to 1.4B parameters, with the vision variant using patch embedding and the language variant using word embedding with masked attention.

## Method

DNT strategically places four types of RMSNorm at specific positions—InputNorm (after embedding), PreNorm (before self-attention/FFN), MidNorm (after sub-block output, before residual addition), and QKNorm (on queries and keys within attention)—to constrain the Jacobian matrices of each layer so that gradient distributions become concentrated rather than heavy-tailed. The theoretical analysis shows that InputNorm prevents the initial embedding norm from dominating all subsequent gradient flow, PreNorm stabilizes the self-attention Jacobian by fixing input column norms, MidNorm decouples the FFN/attention Jacobian from weight magnitudes, and QKNorm eliminates the joint W_q^T W_k influence that causes spectral energy concentration and model crash. By default, PostNorm is excluded due to training instability, and the second PreNorm before FFN is optional. Empirically, DNT trained with mSGDW achieves performance on par with AdamW (e.g., 81.5% vs 82.1% on ImageNet, 2.849 vs 2.863 val loss on OpenWebText) while saving ~6GB GPU memory on a 1.4B model.

## Results

On ImageNet, V-DNT-Large (307M) trained with mSGDW achieves 81.5% top-1 accuracy, nearly matching its AdamW counterpart (82.1%) and substantially outperforming standard ViT-Large with mSGDW (78.2%). On OpenWebText, L-DNT-Small (124M) with mSGDW achieves a validation loss of 2.849, actually beating GPT2-Small with AdamW (2.867) and significantly outperforming GPT2-Small with mSGDW (2.906). At the 1.4B scale, L-DNT-XL with mSGDW reaches 2.408 val loss versus 2.472 for GPT2-XL with mSGDW, while remaining close to the AdamW result of 2.396.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DNT against two standard Transformer baselines: ViT (Dosovitskiy et al., 2020) for image classification and GPT-2 (Radford et al., 2019) for language modeling. The central finding is that DNT trained with mSGDW closes the large gap that standard Transformers exhibit under mSGDW: on ImageNet, V-DNT-Large with mSGDW (81.5%) recovers 3.3 points over ViT-Large with mSGDW (78.2%) and comes within 0.2 points of ViT-Large with AdamW (81.7%); at 632M, the gap is even more dramatic (81.2% vs. 73.5%). On OpenWebText, L-DNT-Small with mSGDW (2.849) actually surpasses GPT2-Small with AdamW (2.867), while GPT2-Small with mSGDW lags at 2.906. At the 1.4B scale, L-DNT-XL with mSGDW (2.408) narrows the AdamW gap to just 0.012, whereas GPT2-XL with mSGDW (2.472) trails AdamW (2.435) by 0.037. In summary, DNT consistently wins over both baselines under mSGDW and matches or slightly exceeds them under AdamW, demonstrating that the architectural normalization design is the key enabler rather than the optimizer choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the standard Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017) and its instantiations ViT and GPT-2, but departs from the conventional PreNorm-only design by strategically adding InputNorm, MidNorm, and QKNorm at specific positions. It is motivated by prior work (Simsekli et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2020) that identified heavy-tailed gradient distributions as the root cause of SGD's poor performance on Transformers, and it provides the first Jacobian-matrix-level theoretical justification for why each normalization position matters. The authors explicitly differentiate DNT from nGPT (Loshchilov et al., 2025), noting that DNT uses InputNorm rather than PostNorm, provides per-normalization theoretical proofs, and does not constrain activations to lie on a hypersphere.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that mSGDW saves roughly 6 GB of GPU memory on a 1.4B model compared to AdamW, and more importantly, it reframes the 'why Adam for Transformers' question from an optimizer problem to an architecture problem. Limitations include moderate model scale (up to 1.4B), only two architecture families tested (ViT, GPT-2), and a roughly tuned learning rate for mSGDW, leaving open whether the gap to AdamW closes or widens at much larger scale. An open question is whether the same normalization-placement principles transfer to other Transformer variants (e.g., diffusion Transformers, Mamba hybrids) or to architectures beyond the residual-block structure assumed here.
