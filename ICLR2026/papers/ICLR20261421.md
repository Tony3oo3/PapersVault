## Problem

Existing medical LVLMs for CT are fragmented: slice-driven models achieve strong 2D vision-language alignment but cannot capture cross-slice spatial consistency, while volume-driven models model 3D structure explicitly but suffer from coarse granularity and poor compatibility with slice-level inputs. No unified paradigm exists that simultaneously handles both 2D slices and 3D volumes within a single CT understanding framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261421/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts either individual 2D CT slices (512×512) or full 3D CT volumes (resampled to 32×384×384) alongside a free-form text query. For 3D volumes, three adjacent axial slices are concatenated along the channel axis into 3×H×W volumetric units; for 2D slices, the single slice is replicated three times along the channel axis to match this format. Outputs are free-form text spanning VQA answers, radiology reports, image captions, and multi-turn clinical dialogue, evaluated with a mix of accuracy, BLEU/ROUGE, BERTScore, and LLM-as-judge metrics.

## Method

OmniCT unifies slice and volume processing through two enhancement modules. Spatial Consistency Enhancement (SCE) performs Volumetric Slice Composition to build locally consistent 3-channel units, injects Tri-Axial Positional Encoding along depth/height/width to provide 3D spatial awareness, and applies a Mixture-of-Experts Hybrid Projection with separate slice and volume expert pathways (routed by binary indicators) to align features into the LLM space while mitigating token explosion via spatial unshuffle. Organ-level Semantic Enhancement (OSE) uses TotalSegmentor masks (117 structures) to select organ-specific token subsets, compresses them to a fixed token budget via adaptive aggregation (creating a magnification effect for small organs and compression for large ones), and concatenates the result with global visual tokens before feeding the LLM. The system uses SigLIP as the 2D vision encoder and Qwen2.5 as the LLM backbone, trained in two stages: cross-modal alignment (only the MoE projection updated at lr 2e-4) followed by instruction tuning (projection + LLM at lr 5e-5), with a global batch size of 256.

## Results

On 2D slice-driven CT benchmarks (SLAKE, VQA-RAD, OmniMedVQA, RadFig-VQA), OmniCT-7B achieves an average score of 81.45, surpassing the second-best model Lingshu (70.44) by +11.01 points. On 3D volume-driven CT benchmarks (M3D, CT-RATE, 3D-RAD), OmniCT-7B reaches an average of 66.15, outperforming the strongest general LVLM GPT-5 (45.59) by +20.56 points and the best volume-centric medical LVLM CT-CHAT (35.97) by +30.18 points. The 3B variant also leads all baselines at both 2D (77.71) and 3D (63.48) scales, demonstrating consistent superiority across model sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniCT-7B decisively outperforms all 13 baselines on both 2D and 3D CT benchmarks. Among medical slice-centric LVLMs, Lingshu (70.44 avg) is the closest 2D competitor but trails OmniCT-7B by 11.01 points; HuatuoGPT-V-Qwen2.5 (66.85) and HealthGPT (62.54) lag further behind. General-purpose LVLMs GPT-5 (68.19 on 2D, 45.59 on 3D) and InternVL3 (64.65) show strong language reasoning but lack CT-specific adaptation, with GPT-5 particularly inconsistent across 3D subtasks. The multi-granularity model RadFM (14B) performs worst overall (32.12 on 2D, 30.77 on 3D), suggesting its dual-modality design introduces significant interference. On 3D volume benchmarks, volume-centric models M3D-LaMed-7B (30.88) and CT-CHAT (35.97) are far surpassed by OmniCT-7B (66.15), with OmniCT achieving 89.80 on CT-RATE multi-choice vs. CT-CHAT's 86.46 and 85.69 on 3D-RAD LTD vs. GPT-5's 77.97. Notably, OmniCT-3B (77.71 on 2D, 63.48 on 3D) already exceeds all baselines including the 14B RadFM and 8B GPT-5, highlighting the efficiency of the unified slice-volume architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two fragmented lines of medical LVLM research: slice-driven models (HealthGPT, HuatuoGPT-Vision, Lingshu) that leverage 2D pre-training for strong vision-language alignment but lack cross-slice spatial consistency, and volume-driven models (M3D-LaMed, CT-CHAT) that capture 3D spatial structure but suffer from coarse granularity and poor slice compatibility. Rather than building on a single prior architecture, OmniCT repurposes the standard LVLM pipeline (vision encoder + projection + LLM) with three targeted modifications—Volumetric Slice Composition, Tri-Axial Positional Embedding, and MoE Hybrid Projection—to inject volumetric priors into a 2D-encoder-based backbone while maintaining slice compatibility. The Organ-level Semantic Enhancement module additionally borrows from segmentation-guided attention (TotalSegmentor) to embed organ-centric semantics, a design choice that departs from the global-attention-only approach of prior medical LVLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified slice-volume paradigm addresses a genuine clinical bottleneck: radiologists interpret CT using both local slice-level cues (sub-centimeter nodules) and global volumetric reasoning (tumor infiltration, inter-organ relations), yet no prior model handled both coherently. The MedEval-CT benchmark (1.7M VQA samples, 170K volumes, 327K slices) is likely to become a standard evaluation resource for CT LVLMs, and the finding that well-pretrained 2D encoders (SigLIP, DINOv3) outperform purpose-built 3D encoders (M3D-CLIP) for CT volume understanding—when combined with structured spatial injection—challenges the assumption that native 3D encoders are necessary. Open questions include whether this 2D-backbone strategy scales to higher-resolution or longer-sequence volumes, whether the approach generalizes beyond CT to other volumetric modalities (MRI, ultrasound), and whether the performance gains translate to actual clinical decision-making in prospective studies.
