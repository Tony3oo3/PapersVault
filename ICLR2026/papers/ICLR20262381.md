## Problem

While VLMs achieve strong end-to-end performance on downstream tasks like navigation and manipulation, their underlying spatial reasoning capabilities remain poorly understood and underdiagnosed. Existing spatial benchmarks either entangle spatial reasoning with language and planning objectives or lack fine-grained controlled variation in perspective, reference frame, and multi-frame reasoning, leaving it unclear whether models genuinely engage in imagery-based spatial cognition or merely exploit dataset biases and shallow pattern matching.

## Input / Output

Each task instance presents one or more RGB images (single reference, triplet, or quartet; rendered at 480×480, downsampled to max 256 in main experiments) alongside a natural-language question, with the model expected to output a single capital letter (A–D). All spatial relations are constrained to a horizontal 2D plane (vertical relations excluded) and viewpoint changes are restricted to horizontal orbits. Evaluation produces three complementary metrics: raw accuracy, chance-corrected Cohen's κ (accounting for varying option cardinality across binary, ternary, and four-way choices), and pairwise consistency (the rate at which logically equivalent augmented questions yield identical correct/incorrect outcomes).

## Method

SPINBENCH decomposes perspective taking into seven progressively structured diagnostic categories—identity matching, object-relation grounding, dynamic translation, dynamic rotation, canonical view selection, mental rotation, and perspective taking—so that simpler single-object tasks scaffold toward the most demanding multi-object scene-level reasoning. The benchmark draws from four data sources (Infinigen synthetic scenes, ABO household objects, Multi-View Car Dataset, and Stereo Face Database) to span 2,739 samples across 51 subtypes and diverse visual domains. A key differentiator is its controlled variation regime: each task is systematically augmented with allocentric vs. egocentric reference frames, symmetrical relation flips, syntactic rephrasings, and premise-based variants (spatial relations stated explicitly in the prompt vs. inferred solely from the image), enabling diagnosis of whether failures stem from visual grounding, linguistic reasoning, or frame-of-reference bias. The benchmark is evaluated on 43 VLMs (7 proprietary, 33 open-source, 3 spatial-specialized) with chain-of-thought variants for three models, and validated against human response-time data showing a significant negative correlation (r = −0.54) between human difficulty and VLM accuracy.

## Results

Across 43 VLMs evaluated on SPINBENCH's 2,739 samples, GPT-5 achieves the highest overall accuracy and 97.1% pairwise consistency, while human subjects reach 91.2% accuracy. Mental rotation and perspective-taking tasks yield near-chance Cohen's κ scores for most models, whereas object-relation grounding is the easiest category (κ > 0.6 for many models). A striking egocentric bias emerges: models that perform best on viewer-centric rotation (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Pro, κ = 0.94) perform worst on the allocentric variant (κ = −0.66). Task difficulty measured by human response time correlates significantly with VLM accuracy (Pearson r = −0.54, p < 0.05), and overall accuracy strongly correlates with pairwise consistency (r = 0.891, p < 0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, GPT-5 leads in both overall accuracy and consistency, with Gemini 2.5 Pro second in accuracy but only fifth in consistency, and Claude 4 Sonnet trailing further. Among open-source models, InternVL3-38B ranks third overall and second in consistency, while smaller models (1B–4B) perform near chance on identity matching and rotation tasks, revealing an emergent capability at the 7B–8B scale. On the egocentric-vs-allocentric rotation contrast, GPT-5 is the only model to show strong performance on both frames (κ = 0.55 allocentric, 0.63 egocentric), whereas Gemini 2.5 Pro and Molmo-7B are near-perfect egocentric but catastrophically wrong allocentric. CoT prompting yields the largest gains for Cosmos-Reason1-7B (+0.221 average κ, up to +0.650 on perspective-taking), moderate gains for SpaceOm (+0.118), and minimal or negative effects for SpaceThinker (+0.052, with a −0.300 drop on one task). Compared to the eight prior spatial benchmarks (CLEVR, BLINK, SpaCE-10, 3DSRBench, SPHERE, ViewSpatial, MindCube, OmniSpatial), SPINBENCH uniquely combines reference-frame variation, premise-based variation, symmetric/syntactic augmentation, and multi-image input, and shows weak correlation with prior benchmarks, confirming it probes orthogonal spatial skills.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPINBENCH carves new ground in the spatial reasoning benchmark landscape by combining cognitive science grounding (Shepard & Metzler mental rotation, perspective-taking paradigms) with a progressive diagnostic structure that prior benchmarks lack. While existing benchmarks like CLEVR, BLINK, MindCube, and OmniSpatial evaluate spatial reasoning, they typically emphasize task comprehensiveness or single-axis evaluation; SPINBENCH instead introduces fine-grained controlled variations (reference frame, premise structure, symmetry, syntax) and a scaffolded difficulty progression from single-object perception to multi-object perspective taking. The paper also draws on spatial reasoning model work (SpatialReasoner, SpaceOm, SpaceThinker) to evaluate whether explicit 3D representations or CoT prompting can close the identified gaps, positioning SPINBENCH as a diagnostic lens rather than a training resource.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The evaluation of 43 VLMs reveals that spatial reasoning failures are systematic and multi-faceted: strong egocentric bias, near-chance performance on rotation and perspective taking, and 41% of models performing below chance even on purely linguistic spatial reasoning with explicit premises. The strong correlation between human response time and VLM accuracy (r=-0.54) validates that SPINBENCH captures genuine cognitive difficulty, making it a reliable diagnostic for embodied AI applications where reference-frame and rotation errors have safety-critical consequences. Key open questions include whether scaling alone will resolve rotation and perspective-taking deficits (identity matching shows emergence, but rotation does not), whether CoT or visual-embedding-space reasoning can address perceptual failures, and how to extend the benchmark to vertical relations, containment, and support that are currently excluded.
