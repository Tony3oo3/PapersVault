## Problem

Most existing VLM spatial-reasoning benchmarks evaluate single-view perception, leaving unexplored whether models can integrate synchronized multi-camera observations for robotic manipulation. Multi-view setups are increasingly standard on robotic platforms to mitigate occlusion and depth ambiguity, yet no benchmark systematically tests multi-view spatial reasoning coupled with embodied action tasks in realistic manipulation scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265258/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each QA item presents synchronized multi-view RGB images (typically 2–4 camera views from gripper-mounted and head-mounted cameras, base64-encoded) alongside a natural-language question and exactly five multiple-choice options; the model must output a single option letter (A–E). The 1,708 items span eight subtasks split into four spatial-understanding tasks (cross-view matching, distance judgement, viewpoint identification, 3D spatial consistency) and four robotic-execution tasks (action planning, step execution, trajectory selection, affordance recognition). For CoT-enhanced variants, additional inputs are appended: GPT-4.1-generated scene descriptions, VGGT-synthesized interpolated views resized to 224×224, or MoGe-2 depth maps normalized to [0,1] with Gaussian smoothing.

## Method

MV-RoboBench is constructed via a three-stage pipeline—rule-based and GPT-4.1-assisted filtering of synchronized image pairs from AgiWorld and BridgeV2, template-guided five-choice QA generation by trained annotators, and iterative multi-annotator cross-validation with answer-distribution rebalancing—yielding 1,708 items from 980 episodes. The paper evaluates 30+ VLMs (proprietary, reasoning-optimized, open-source, and MoE) under a unified zero-shot multiple-choice protocol, then probes three CoT-inspired enhancements (textual scene descriptions, VGGT novel-view synthesis, MoGe-2 depth priors) to test whether auxiliary geometric or linguistic cues improve multi-view reasoning. A correlation analysis along two axes—internal (spatial vs. robotic accuracy within MV-RoboBench) and external (OmniSpatial single-view scores vs. MV-RoboBench multi-view scores)—reveals that spatial and robotic competence are positively correlated only in high-capacity reasoning models, and that strong single-view spatial performance does not reliably transfer to multi-view embodied tasks.

## Results

On MV-RoboBench (1,708 multi-choice QA items across 8 subtasks), GPT-5 achieves the highest overall accuracy at 56.41%, followed by Gemini-2.5-pro (49.52%) and o4-mini (46.47%). Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-vl-72B leads at 24.29%, while Llama-4-Maverick (MoE) reaches 26.11%. Human participants score 91.04%, revealing a substantial gap of over 35 percentage points between the best model and human-level multi-view robotic reasoning. The 3D Spatial Consistency subtask is particularly challenging, with most non-reasoning models performing at or below the random-choice baseline of 19.07%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 25+ models across five categories on MV-RoboBench. GPT-5 (56.41%) substantially outperforms all non-reasoning proprietary models: GPT-4.1 (30.90%), GPT-4o (27.59%), Gemini-2.0-flash (28.94%), and Claude-3.7 (25.47%), with gains of 25–31 percentage points. Among reasoning models, GPT-5 leads Gemini-2.5-pro (49.52%) by ~7 points and o4-mini (46.47%) by ~10 points, with the largest gap on 3D Spatial Consistency (82.35% vs. 49.02% vs. 65.69%). Open-source models cluster tightly between 19–26%: Qwen2.5-vl-72B (24.29%) and Llama-4-Maverick (26.11%) are the strongest, while Gemma-3-4B (19.79%) and InternVL3-2B (18.93%) perform at or below random. Notably, GPT-3.5-turbo (18.52%) scores below the random-choice baseline (19.71%), and all open-source models remain within ~5 points of random on 3D Spatial Consistency. The key finding is that no model approaches the human score of 91.04%, and strong single-view spatial performance (e.g., on OmniSpatial) does not reliably transfer to multi-view robotic reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new intersection between two established benchmark lineages: single-view spatial reasoning benchmarks (EmbSpatial-Bench, RoboSpatial, OmniSpatial) and embodied robotic evaluation (ShareRobot, ERQA). Prior multi-view benchmarks (All-Angles Bench, Ego3D-Bench) test photographic alignment or navigation perception but stop short of manipulation-oriented reasoning, while robotic benchmarks lack multi-view inputs. MV-RoboBench deliberately unifies these by requiring synchronized multi-camera observations for both spatial understanding and action-level tasks. Methodologically, it extends the Chain-of-Thought prompting paradigm (Wei et al., 2022) into the multi-view embodied setting by exploring textual, visual (VGGT novel-view synthesis), and structural (MoGe-2 depth) CoT variants, though it finds these yield only mixed, model-dependent gains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a substantial and persistent gap: the best model (GPT-5) reaches only 56.4% versus 91.0% human accuracy, with most non-reasoning models performing near random on 3D Spatial Consistency. The finding that strong single-view spatial benchmark performance does not reliably transfer to multi-view robotic reasoning is a useful negative result, suggesting that viewpoint integration, occlusion resolution, and spatial fusion impose fundamentally different demands than those exercised by existing benchmarks. The paper's open question—whether architectures must explicitly encode geometric priors and enforce cross-view consistency rather than relying on scaling perception alone—points toward a concrete research direction for next-generation VLA models. Limitations include the modest scale (1.7K items), the multiple-choice format that may understate open-ended reasoning difficulty, and the reliance on two source datasets (AgiWorld, BridgeV2) that may not capture the full diversity of real-world manipulation.
