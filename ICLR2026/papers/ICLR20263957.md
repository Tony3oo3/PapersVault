## Problem

General continual learning (GCL) requires adapting to single-pass, non-stationary data streams without clear task boundaries, yet existing parameter-efficient tuning methods rely on multiple epochs and explicit task cues that no longer hold. Two fundamental challenges remain unresolved: how to dynamically route inputs to appropriate experts without task labels or iterative training, and how to maintain strong expert representations under sparse, imbalanced supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263957/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB images processed by a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone and produces a single class label prediction. Unconventionally, routing to the appropriate expert is performed in a forward-only, closed-form manner (no gradient updates), and the final prediction ensembles multiple output heads via element-wise maximum of their SoftMax outputs. The Si-Blurry protocol means classes can reappear across sessions and task identities are never provided at test time.

## Method

FlyPrompt decomposes GCL into expert routing and expert competence, drawing on the fruit fly mushroom body's sparse expansion and multi-timescale plasticity. The Random Expanded Analytic Router (REAR) projects backbone features through a fixed random matrix (M=10⁴) with ReLU activation, then solves a closed-form ridge regression on accumulated Gram and prototype statistics to obtain a router matrix that assigns each input to an expert without any gradient updates. For expert competence, each task-specific prompt expert maintains a temporal ensemble of output heads (TE²) using exponential moving averages at decay rates 0.9 and 0.99, capturing short- and long-term decision boundaries; at inference, the online head and all EMA heads are combined via SoftMax followed by element-wise maximum. A non-parametric logit mask suppresses predictions on unseen classes within each batch to mitigate class imbalance.

## Results

Under the primary Sup-21K setting, FlyPrompt achieves A_auc of 83.24%, 56.58%, and 70.64% and A_last of 86.76%, 55.27%, and 73.40% on CIFAR-100, ImageNet-R, and CUB-200 respectively, setting new state-of-the-art for GCL. The method delivers up to 11.23%, 12.43%, and 7.62% A_auc improvements and 13.53%, 16.49%, and 12.28% A_last improvements over the strongest baselines on the three datasets. FlyPrompt also adds only 0.83% extra parameters (87.08M total, 0.46M trainable) and 4.96 s/batch training time, comparable to other prompt-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlyPrompt outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest GCL baselines under Sup-21K, it beats MISA (80.35→83.24 A_auc on CIFAR-100, +2.89%) and CODA-P (79.13→83.24, +4.11%), while the gap over MVP is largest (67.74→83.24, +15.50% on CIFAR-100 A_auc; 51.20→56.58, +5.38% on ImageNet-R). Among prompt-based CL methods, L2P (76.23) and DualPrompt (76.04) trail by 7+ points on CIFAR-100 A_auc. Against offline PTM-based methods adapted to GCL, FlyPrompt leads S-Prompt++ (80.21), HiDe-LoRA (80.07), NoRGa (78.89), and SD-LoRA (79.26) by 3–4 points on CIFAR-100 A_auc, with even larger margins on ImageNet-R (56.58 vs. 55.51 for SD-LoRA) and CUB-200 (70.64 vs. 67.26 for HiDe-LoRA). The analytic random-projection baseline RanPAC† (69.91 A_auc on CIFAR-100) confirms that random projections alone without expert modularity are insufficient, validating the need for both REAR routing and TE² competence.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlyPrompt repurposes the random-projection + closed-form ridge regression mechanism from RanPAC (McDonnell et al., 2024) and ACIL (Zhuang et al., 2022), which apply it directly for final classification, and redirects it toward instance-level expert routing (REAR) while keeping per-expert prompts and heads fully trainable. It sits within the prompt-based GCL lineage (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-P, MVP, MISA) but explicitly decouples the two subproblems those methods conflate: routing accuracy and per-expert competence. The temporal ensemble of EMA heads (TE²) draws on the multi-timescale plasticity observed in Drosophila mushroom body KC subregions, translating a biological consolidation principle into a practical inference-time aggregation strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that a fixed random projection can outperform learned similarity/contrastive routers for expert selection, and that a small bank of EMA heads closes the remaining competence gap, FlyPrompt offers a lightweight, gradient-free routing alternative that is well-suited to single-pass, memory-constrained GCL deployment. The theoretical excess-risk decomposition (Theorem 1) and bias-variance bound for the EMA bank (Theorem 2) give practitioners principled guidance on choosing expansion dimension M and decay rates. Open questions include whether the fixed EMA composition can be adapted dynamically to drift, how performance holds under extreme long-tailed class distributions, and whether the sparse-expansion routing idea transfers to non-vision modalities or larger backbones.
