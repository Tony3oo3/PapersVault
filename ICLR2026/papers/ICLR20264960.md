## Problem

Current VLA model benchmarks are largely confined to domestic manipulation tasks (table bussing, folding, pick-and-place), leaving professional science-oriented domains unexplored. Biology laboratories present a uniquely challenging testbed combining structured language protocols, high-precision manipulation (threaded caps, slot alignment), transparent materials that complicate visual reasoning, and interactive digital interfaces—none of which are adequately captured by existing simulators or benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264960/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

VLA models consume multi-view RGB images, natural-language task instructions (e.g., 'transfer the centrifuge tube to row 2, column 3'), and proprioceptive state vectors (32-dim for π-series, 128-dim for RDT) to produce 50 Hz end-effector action trajectories. Demonstrations are generated procedurally via IK + TOPP + PD control and packaged as LeRobot datasets (~792k frames, 4.4 hours total). Evaluation uses binary success/failure for most tasks, but a weighted relative-progress score for the thermal mixer panel task to capture partial completion of multi-parameter UI manipulation.

## Method

AutoBio extends MuJoCo with four custom physics plugins: an SDF-based helix collision model for threaded cap-tube assembly (avoiding convex decomposition while preserving frictional self-locking), a passive spring-force detent mechanism for stepped knobs, a negatively-coupled dual-joint eccentric mechanism for vortex mixers, and a quasi-static liquid module that tracks surface normals via a damped spherical pendulum ODE derived from Euler-Lagrange equations. The instrument digitization pipeline chains multi-view video capture → PGSR-based 3DGS reconstruction → mesh extraction → CAD refinement → UV texture baking → automated glTF-to-MJCF conversion, reducing per-asset creation to roughly 1–2 hours. Rendering supports both fast MuJoCo OpenGL and photorealistic Blender PBR (for transparent containers/liquids), plus dynamic texture maps for reactive instrument displays. The benchmark comprises 16 biologically grounded tasks across three difficulty levels, with procedural demonstration generation and randomized scene initialization; evaluation fine-tunes π0, π0.5, and RDT on 100 or 20 episodes (30k steps, batch 32, H800) and reveals persistent gaps in precision manipulation, cross-modal grounding, and closed-loop recovery that domestic benchmarks do not expose.

## Results

On the AutoBio benchmark (100 demos, 100 eval episodes), VLA models achieve near-perfect scores on easy tasks (π₀.₅: 95.6–99.4%, π₀: 96.0–99.7%, RDT: 99.0–100.0%) but performance collapses on medium tasks (best: π₀ at 42.7% on Aspirate with pipette; RDT drops to 0.3–2.7%) and hard tasks (best: π₀.₅ at 16.0% on Load centrifuge rotor; most scores below 12%). Data scaling from 20 to 100 demos yields substantial gains for π-series models (e.g., π₀.₅ on Pick up centrifuge tube: 31.3% → 95.9%) but minimal improvement for RDT (45.3% → 57.7%), reflecting RDT's frozen vision-language encoders. Imitation-learning baselines (DP, ACT) match or exceed VLA scores on individual easy/medium tasks but cannot be unified across the heterogeneous task suite.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the three VLA models, π₀ outperforms π₀.₅ on medium tasks requiring visio-linguistic grounding (Aspirate: 42.7% vs 18.3%; Unscrew cap: 21.3% vs 11.7%), while π₀.₅ dominates on easy tasks (Pick up centrifuge tube: 95.9% vs 53.7%) and slightly leads on hard tasks (Load rotor: 16.0% vs 14.7%). RDT, with its frozen SigLIP/T5 encoders, is competitive on easy tasks (100% on Close/Open TC lid) but collapses on medium and hard tasks (≤2.7% on most medium tasks), confirming that frozen backbones limit cross-modal adaptation. The imitation-learning baselines DP and ACT achieve surprisingly competitive scores on individual tasks (ACT reaches 71.3% on Transfer centrifuge tube, DP reaches 24.7% on Operate thermal mixer panel), but they cannot be trained as a unified policy across the heterogeneous state spaces of AutoBio's 16 tasks, whereas VLA models use language as a shared task interface. Overall, no single model dominates across all difficulty levels, and the dramatic performance cliff between easy and medium/hard tasks (e.g., π₀: 99.7% → 21.3%) is the central empirical finding, exposing precision, grounding, and closed-loop recovery gaps absent from domestic-task benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robotic manipulation simulation lineage (MuJoCo-based frameworks like robosuite, ManiSkill, RoboTwin) into a professional science domain by introducing specialized physics plugins (SDF-based thread mechanisms, quasi-static liquids, detent/eccentric mechanisms) and a Blender PBR rendering backend that existing simulators lack. For asset creation, it repurposes 3D Gaussian Splatting (PGSR) as a front-end to a CAD-refinement and MJCF-conversion pipeline, a workflow not previously applied to laboratory instrument digitization. On the evaluation side, it positions itself against VLA benchmarks (Libero, Meta-World, Chemistry3D) by targeting biology-specific precision tasks, interactive instrument UIs, and fluid handling—capabilities absent from all prior benchmarks per Table 1.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AutoBio fills a concrete gap in VLA evaluation by exposing failure modes (cross-modal grounding errors, lack of closed-loop recovery, visual-reasoning limits with transparent objects) that domestic-task benchmarks cannot capture, providing a diagnostic tool for the VLA community. The trajectory concatenation experiment (Table 6) reveals that near-perfect single-task performance collapses to ~3–15% when tasks are sequenced, suggesting current VLA models memorize trajectories rather than composing skills—a limitation with broad implications for long-horizon protocol execution. Open questions include whether the simulation-to-real gap is manageable for lab instruments, whether the 16-task benchmark is sufficiently diverse to drive meaningful progress, and whether the specialized physics plugins generalize beyond the specific mechanisms implemented.
