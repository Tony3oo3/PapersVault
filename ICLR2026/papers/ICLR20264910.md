## Problem

Existing model stitching approaches for neural architecture search, such as SN-Net, rely on heuristic-based stitch selection (nearest stitching, paired/unpaired block matching) that yields suboptimal accuracy-efficiency tradeoffs and fails to generalize across model families. No principled similarity metric has been shown to reliably identify which pretrained blocks are compatible for stitching, leaving the combinatorial space of O(k²n²) possible stitch configurations largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264910/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

KLAS takes a set of pretrained anchor models (e.g., Swin-Ti/S/B, DeiT-Ti/S/B, ResNet, Llama 1B/3B) as input and produces a set of selected stitch configurations—specific source-target block pairs—that, after 50-epoch finetuning of the lightweight stitching layer, yield a Pareto front of deployable stitched networks. The method operates on validation-split softmax distributions from linear probes (1×1 convolutions) attached to every block, requiring only a one-time probe training cost (e.g., 0.25 GPU-days for all 24 probes in Swin-B). The final stitched models accept standard inputs (224×224 RGB images for classification, 512×512 for segmentation, text prompts for LLMs) and produce class labels, segmentation masks, or generated text respectively.

## Method

KLAS replaces heuristic stitch selection with KL divergence between softmax probability distributions estimated by linear classifier probes placed after each block. Anchor selection uses last-block KL divergence to identify the most compatible pretrained model pairs, while block selection employs a stitch score Γ that combines two terms: cross-anchor activation distance Ω (how much a hypothetical stitch layer must transform source activations to match the target) and intra-anchor block capacity Σ (how much the next target block transforms its inputs, indicating its ability to absorb distributional mismatch). A unified ProbeNet architecture jointly trains all probes in a single forward-backward pass to avoid redundant computation. Candidate stitches are selected from FLOPs buckets using a tunable threshold τ (default 5% above per-bucket minimum), ensuring both coverage across the accuracy-efficiency spectrum and quality filtering without instantiating or training any stitch layer during selection.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, KLAS achieves an AUC of 0.8950 for accuracy-efficiency tradeoff quality, compared to 0.8345 for SN-Net, 0.8124 for CKA, and 0.7642 for Direct Matching (Table 2). KLAS delivers up to 1.21% higher top-1 accuracy at the same FLOPs, or a 1.33× reduction in FLOPs at equivalent accuracy, across Swin, DeiT, LeViT, and ResNet families. On ADE20K semantic segmentation (Mask2Former + Swin), KLAS-selected stitches achieve up to +0.9% mIoU over SN-Net at comparable FLOPs. On TruthfulQA with Llama 1B/3B, KLAS-stitched models outperform ESTA by up to 0.017 ROUGE-1 and 0.033 ROUGE-2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SN-Net (Pan et al., CVPR 2023), the primary baseline, KLAS consistently wins across all model families: AUC improves from 0.8345 to 0.8950 on Swin, and positive ΔAUC is observed on DeiT (+0.012), LeViT (+0.006), ResNet (+0.014), and cross-architecture ResNet-Swin (+0.002). On ADE20K segmentation, KLAS achieves 33.5% mIoU vs SN-Net's 32.6% at ~277 GFLOPs, and 37.8% vs 37.7% at ~316 GFLOPs. Against ESTA (He et al., CVPR 2024) on LLM stitching, KLAS outperforms by 0.017 ROUGE-1 (0.593 vs 0.576) and 0.033 ROUGE-2 (0.337 vs 0.304) at the ~1.5B parameter scale, while using fewer parameters. As a similarity metric, CKA (Kornblith et al., 2019) recovers only 5.5% of viable Ti-B stitch configurations versus 88.9% for KLAS, and Direct Matching (Balogh & Jelasity, 2025) recovers 33.3%. Against cascaded inference (Lebovitz et al., TMLR 2023), KLAS stitched models exceed the best cascade by >0.4% Top-1 at >12 GFLOPs on Swin and by 2–3% on the cross-architecture ResNet-18→Swin-B pair, while also offering deterministic (vs. input-dependent) FLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

KLAS extends the model-stitching-for-NAS paradigm introduced by SN-Net (Pan et al., CVPR 2023), which combined pretrained anchor models using heuristic-based stitch selection (nearest stitching for anchors, paired/unpaired stitching for blocks). The core departure is replacing these heuristics with a principled similarity metric—KL divergence between linear-probe softmax distributions—that simultaneously captures representational alignment and functional compatibility. The method also builds on the original stitching framework of Lenc & Vedaldi (2015) and Bansal et al. (2021), and on linear classifier probes (Alain & Bengio, 2018), but repurposes them as a selection signal rather than an analysis tool. By showing that the nearest-stitching assumption is not universally optimal (e.g., Ti-B outperforms Ti-S-B in Swin), KLAS generalizes stitching beyond the same-complexity anchor pairs that prior work implicitly required.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KLAS provides a drop-in, low-overhead replacement for heuristic stitch selection that generalizes across ViTs, CNNs, cross-architecture hybrids, and LLMs without architectural assumptions, making it a practical tool for deployment-oriented model selection. The finding that a simple KL divergence between probe outputs outperforms CKA (5.5% vs. 88.9% configuration recovery) and task-loss-matching metrics challenges the assumption that more sophisticated representational similarity measures are needed for stitching. Open questions include extending KLAS to multi-phase LLM inference (balancing prefill and decode latency with KV-cache compatibility across stitched blocks) and whether the approach scales to larger model families or non-binary (multi-anchor) stitching. The modest but consistent gains (~1.2% accuracy or 1.33× FLOPs reduction) suggest the heuristic gap was real but bounded, and further improvements may require richer similarity signals or joint anchor-block optimization.
