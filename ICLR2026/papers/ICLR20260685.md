## Problem

Continual vision-language learning under non-stationary, multi-domain data streams suffers from catastrophic forgetting, yet deployed systems cannot store raw exemplars due to privacy and memory constraints. Existing remedies—geometry preservation, synthetic/exemplar replay, and parameter-efficient tuning—each address only part of the problem: they fail to maintain stable yet plastic compositional competence, cannot reuse learned concept-relation structures across shifting tasks, and provide limited control over what is rehearsed in the feature space where learning actually occurs.

## Input / Output

During training, COMEM consumes image-text pairs (images encoded to 196x768 patch-token feature maps via CLIP ViT-B/16, text to token embeddings) and task-specific labels (retrieval relevance, VQA answers, or binary concept-match decisions). Crucially, it stores no raw images: the persistent memory is a typed concept graph whose nodes hold d-dimensional prototypes and budgeted token-anchor reservoirs (at most 8 anchors per concept, 64K total), and whose edges hold interaction embeddings. At inference the model produces standard task outputs—relevance scores for retrieval, answer tokens for VQA, or binary match decisions for SVLC—while the concept graph and anchor reservoirs serve as the sole cross-task memory.

## Method

COMEM recasts continual VLM learning as maintaining a compact concept-relation graph and rehearsing directly in feature space. At each task, a noise-aware induction stage extracts (attribute, entity, relation) triplets via prompted IE, verifies them with a teacher-frozen shared low-rank projector, and updates graph prototypes (EMA) and budgeted k-center anchors with time-decay. Replay subgraphs are sampled by a two-stage procedure combining NPMI plausibility, DPP diversity on node prototypes, Steiner-tree connectivity projection, and a single Metropolis-Hastings acceptance step; a GAT aggregator then conditions a conditional Gaussian generator that synthesizes on-manifold replay features, trained with a relation-aware MMD and a support-hull regularizer. The student is optimized on mixed real+replay batches with a multi-objective loss: task supervision, symmetric InfoNCE on both real and replay pairs, entropy-gated teacher distillation (stopping gradients to the generator), and a compositional consistency term combining log-probability PoE coherence across subgraph unions with typed relation-contrast using hard negatives. A theoretical analysis bounds dynamic regret and per-task forgetting in terms of anchor coverage radius and generator MMD, showing that tighter anchors and better relation-aware fitting directly reduce the replay bias that drives forgetting.

## Results

On the 5-domain cross-domain retrieval stream, COMEM achieves 76.6% average mR with only 1.9% average forgetting (AF), outperforming the strongest baselines GIFT (73.3% mR, 2.5 AF) and C-CLIP (73.2% mR, 2.7 AF) by +3.3/+3.4 mR and −0.6/−0.8 AF respectively. On structured concept learning (SVLC), COMEM reaches 82.5% macro accuracy and 88.8 AUROC with 2.1 AF, surpassing GIFT (79.9/86.9/2.7) and ZAF (80.3/87.1/2.6). On continual VQA, COMEM attains 55.8% accuracy on VQACL and 63.7% on CLOVE with 1.7 AF, exceeding the MLLM-based CL-MoE (54.1/62.3/2.0) while using far fewer trainable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COMEM dominates all 13 baselines across every evaluation stream. On cross-domain retrieval, it improves over the strongest replay-based method GIFT by +3.3 mR and −0.6 AF, and over the strongest PEFT method C-CLIP by +3.4 mR and −0.8 AF; geometry-only methods (ZSCL, CTP, Mod-X) trail by 8–11 mR. On SVLC, COMEM leads GIFT by +2.6 Acc and +1.9 AUROC while reducing AF from 2.7 to 2.1, and outperforms ZAF by +2.2 Acc. On VQA, COMEM surpasses the MLLM-based CL-MoE by +1.7 (VQACL) and +1.4 (CLOVE) accuracy with lower AF (1.7 vs 2.0), despite using feature-level replay rather than a full MLLM. Under matched PEFT budgets (Table 4), COMEM at 8M trainables achieves 76.6 mR / 1.8 AF versus LADA (75.8/2.1) and ENGINE (75.7/2.1), confirming that graph-conditioned replay and compositional constraints yield gains beyond mere parameter scaling. The advantage is consistent across near (COCO/Flickr30K) and far (IAPR/RSICD/ECommerce) domains, and persists under adversarial task orderings with only 0.3–0.5 pp degradation at T=18.

## Relation to Prior Work

COMEM carves new ground by unifying three previously separate lines of continual VLM work—geometry/parameter regularization (ZSCL, CTP, Mod-X), data-free or synthetic replay (IncCLIP, SGP, GIFT), and parameter-efficient adaptation (C-CLIP, LADA, CL-MoE)—into a single framework where compositional structure itself is the memory unit. Rather than storing raw exemplars, constraining representation geometry, or generating pixel-level surrogates, it maintains a typed concept-relation graph with token-level anchors and rehearses directly in feature space via a graph-conditioned conditional Gaussian. The graph aggregator borrows from GNNs (GAT-style attention), the sampler combines DPP diversity with Steiner-tree connectivity and Metropolis-Hastings quality control, and the training objective fuses InfoNCE alignment, entropy-gated distillation, and a novel compositional consistency loss (Product-of-Experts marginal compatibility plus typed relation contrast). The theoretical analysis (dynamic regret and forgetting bounds parameterized by anchor coverage radius and generator MMD) is a departure from the purely empirical treatment typical in this area.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that semantically structured, feature-space rehearsal can match or exceed both exemplar replay and synthetic-image replay under strictly matched memory budgets, offering a privacy-friendly path for continual multimodal deployment without retaining raw data. The compositional consistency objective (PoE + typed relation contrast) provides a principled mechanism for reusing learned part-whole structure across shifting tasks, which is directly relevant to open-world agents that must recombine concepts in novel configurations. Key limitations include the reliance on a fixed relation schema and lightweight prompted-IE parsing, which constrains coverage in truly open-vocabulary settings; the authors acknowledge that end-to-end concept discovery and integration with instruction-tuned MLLMs remain open. The theoretical bounds, while clean, assume convexity and a fixed relation kernel, so their tightness in practice with deep non-convex encoders is unverified.
