## Problem

Autoregressive visual generation models struggle with complex compositional prompts requiring precise spatial relationships and attribute binding, as they lack explicit reasoning about scene structure. While the Generation Chain-of-Thought (GoT) framework introduced intermediate semantic-spatial reasoning, its reliance on supervised fine-tuning with hand-crafted templates prevents the model from autonomously discovering more effective reasoning strategies. Extending reinforcement learning to this setting is non-trivial because visual generation rewards must span multiple dimensions (semantic fidelity, spatial accuracy, visual quality) and supervise both the reasoning process and the final image simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a free-form text prompt and autoregressively generates a two-part output: first a Generation Chain-of-Thought reasoning chain (text tokens describing each object's semantic attributes and bounding box coordinates in a normalized coordinate system), followed by a sequence of discrete visual tokens that constitute the generated image. The base architecture is Janus-Pro (1B or 7B), a unified MLLM that jointly models text and image tokens, so the entire pipeline—from prompt to reasoning to image—is a single autoregressive sequence. During RL training, N=16 candidate (reasoning chain, image) pairs are sampled per prompt at temperature 1.0 for group-relative advantage estimation.

## Method

GoT-R1 applies Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to a GoT-finetuned Janus-Pro model, using a novel MLLM-based dual-stage multi-dimensional reward that evaluates both the intermediate reasoning and the final image. The total reward is the product of four components: prompt-image alignment (R_PI), prompt-reasoning alignment (R_PR, itself the average of a semantic reward R_sem and a spatial reward R_spa), reasoning-image alignment (R_RI, computed as mean IoU between planned and grounded bounding boxes), and HPS v2.1 aesthetic score (R_HPS). A key design choice for R_spa is rendering the GoT's textual bounding-box coordinates as visualized boxes on a blank canvas before feeding them to the MLLM reward model (Qwen2.5VL-7B), exploiting the model's superior spatial comprehension of visual data over raw coordinate text. Training proceeds in two stages: 70k steps of supervised fine-tuning on GoT-annotated data to establish templated reasoning, followed by 1000 steps of GRPO with LoRA (rank 32) on a constructed prompt set, where the product-form reward ensures that any single dimension's failure (e.g., unfaithful reasoning or misaligned image) multiplicatively penalizes the total signal.

## Results

On T2I-CompBench, GoT-R1-7B achieves state-of-the-art scores across five of six categories, reaching 0.8139 (Color), 0.5549 (Shape), 0.7339 (Texture), 0.3306 (2D-Spatial), and 0.3944 (Complex), surpassing the best diffusion model (SD3) and all prior autoregressive methods. On GenEval, GoT-R1-7B sets a new overall score of 0.75, with particularly large gains in two-object generation (0.69→0.94) and attribute binding (0.43→0.68) over the Janus-Pro-7B-GoT baseline. On COCO 2014, GoT-R1-7B improves CLIP Score to 31.83 and Aesthetic Score to 5.41, with 77% human preference over baseline models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GoT-R1-7B outperforms its direct predecessor Janus-Pro-7B-GoT across all six T2I-CompBench categories, with the largest relative gains in 2D-Spatial (+34%, 0.2457→0.3306) and Complex (+5%, 0.3754→0.3944). Against the strongest diffusion baselines, GoT-R1-7B matches or exceeds Stable v3 on Color (0.8139 vs 0.8132), Texture (0.7339 vs 0.7334), and 2D-Spatial (0.3306 vs 0.3200), and surpasses DALLE-3 on Complex compositions (0.3944 vs 0.3773). On GenEval, GoT-R1-7B achieves an overall score of 0.75, substantially ahead of JanusFlow (0.63), Janus (0.61), GoT (0.64), and Janus-Pro-7B-GoT (0.64), with the most dramatic improvement in two-object generation (0.94 vs 0.69) and attribute binding (0.68 vs 0.43). Notably, the smaller GoT-R1-1B model (0.7632 Color, 0.3749 Complex) surpasses the larger Janus-Pro-7B baseline (0.6359 Color, 0.3559 Complex) in several categories, suggesting that the RL training is more impactful than raw model scale for compositional tasks. The paper also reports that GPT-4o prefers GoT-R1-7B's self-explored reasoning chains over the template-based GoT chains in 69–84% of comparisons across Color, Spatial, Complex, and LAION-5B prompt sets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Generation Chain-of-Thought (GoT) framework, which introduced explicit semantic-spatial reasoning before image generation, by replacing its supervised template-based training with reinforcement learning (GRPO) to let the model autonomously discover reasoning strategies. It builds on Janus-Pro as the autoregressive unified MLLM backbone and adapts the GRPO algorithm from DeepSeek-R1 to the visual generation setting. The key methodological departure from prior RL-for-reasoning work (o1, DeepSeek-R1) is the dual-stage multi-dimensional MLLM-based reward that jointly supervises both the intermediate reasoning chain and the final image, rather than relying solely on outcome rewards. The visualized bounding-box trick for spatial evaluation is a practical innovation that compensates for MLLMs' limited sensitivity to raw coordinate text.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GoT-R1 demonstrates that the RL-for-reasoning paradigm proven in language models transfers effectively to autoregressive visual generation, with particularly large gains on compositional tasks (two-object generation 0.69→0.94, attribute binding 0.43→0.68 on GenEval). The dual-stage reward design addresses a fundamental open problem in generation RL: how to prevent the reasoning chain from becoming unfaithful or misaligned with the final image when only outcome rewards are used. Limitations include ~30% inference latency from the explicit reasoning step, degradation in object-heavy scenes (>10 objects), and dependence on the reward MLLM's capability, suggesting that progress in MLLM understanding will directly unlock further gains in generation quality.
