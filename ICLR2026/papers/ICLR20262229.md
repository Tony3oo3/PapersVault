## Problem

PPO-based RLHF training pipelines suffer from substantial inefficiencies due to sequential multi-model dependencies and long-tail response lengths: the reward model cannot begin scoring until the actor finishes generating, and a few long responses straggle the entire batch, leaving GPUs idle. Existing remedies either sacrifice convergence through asynchronous staleness or address parallelism without exploiting the complementary compute characteristics (memory-bound decoding vs. compute-bound prefilling) across pipeline stages.

## Input / Output

OPPO takes a batch of B prompts and four pretrained models (actor, critic, frozen reference, reward) as input, and produces updated actor/critic weights plus a training reward trajectory as output. The key operational detail is that the actor's autoregressive generation is partitioned into adaptive-size chunks (e.g., 128–512 tokens) that are streamed incrementally to the reward model, and the effective batch is inflated to B+Δ prompts per step, with unfinished sequences carried forward as partial KV-cache state into the next iteration. The method is implemented as a lightweight wrapper around existing PPO codebases (e.g., TRL), requiring no changes to model architectures or the PPO objective.

## Method

OPPO introduces two complementary overlap mechanisms. Intra-step overlap streams newly generated actor tokens in right-sized chunks to the reward model, enabling incremental prefilling to proceed concurrently with the actor's memory-intensive decoding; chunk sizes are dynamically adjusted online (e.g., every 50 steps, testing candidates like 128/256/512 tokens) to balance overlap gain against GPU context-switch contention. Inter-step overlap overcommits Δ additional prompts per batch (B+Δ total), uses the first B completions for the PPO update, and defers unfinished long generations to the next step, preserving partial work; Δ is adapted via a sliding-window reward-trend controller that increases overcommitment during early training (when long-tail variance is high) and decays toward zero as convergence stabilizes. The paper proves that intra-step streaming yields a gradient estimator identical to standard PPO (the inner sum collapses because each sample follows the same prefix), so neither overlap alters the optimization semantics. Across three tasks and two model scales, OPPO achieves 1.8×–2.8× wall-clock speedup and 1.4×–2.1× GPU utilization improvement over TRL while matching final reward trajectories.

## Results

OPPO accelerates PPO-based RLHF training by 1.8×–2.8× over TRL across free-form generation (Stack-Exchange-Paired), math reasoning (GSM8K), and code generation (OpenCoder-SFT) tasks, while improving GPU utilization by 1.4×–2.1×. In multi-node settings (2×4 A100-40GB), OPPO reduces end-to-end step latency from 498.30 s to 111.08 s (4.49×). Final model quality is preserved: on the 3B model, OPPO improves average benchmark accuracy by +0.48 pp over TRL, and on the 7B model the difference is negligible (+0.02 pp).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TRL (the primary baseline), OPPO wins decisively on all efficiency metrics: 1.9×–2.8× faster wall-clock training, 1.4×–2.1× higher GPU utilization, and 4.49× lower step latency in multi-node settings, while maintaining near-identical reward trajectories and final accuracy (within ±0.25 pp on 7B benchmarks). Against VeRL (configured with data parallelism and sequence parallelism), OPPO achieves the lowest per-step latency at 99.84 s versus 125.36 s (DP) and 120.47 s (DP+SP), a 1.26× improvement over VeRL w/ DP. Against AReaL, OPPO also wins on latency (99.84 s vs. 109.92 s, ~1.1× faster), demonstrating that its intra-step streaming overlap targets a bottleneck orthogonal to the sequence-level and asynchronous optimizations employed by those systems. Across all comparisons, OPPO never degrades training quality, confirming that the pipeline-overlap strategy is a complementary, composable optimization rather than a trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

OPPO builds directly on the standard PPO-based RLHF pipeline (Schulman et al., 2017) as implemented in TRL (von Werra et al., 2020a), targeting the sequential multi-model execution that all existing PPO frameworks share. It positions itself as orthogonal to algorithmic simplifications (DPO, GRPO) that remove pipeline components, and to asynchronous approaches (AReaL) that trade staleness for throughput, instead attacking the execution schedule itself. Relative to system-level frameworks like VeRL/HybridFlow and RLHFuse, which optimize parallelism and stage fusion, OPPO identifies a distinct bottleneck—idle time from sequential stage dependencies and long-tail stragglers—and addresses it via intra-step chunked streaming and inter-step overcommitment, making it composable with those optimizations rather than competing with them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1.8×–2.8× wall-clock speedup with preserved convergence is practically significant given that PPO-based RLHF remains the dominant online alignment method and its multi-model pipeline is a major cost driver at scale. The lightweight wrapper design and model-agnosticism lower the adoption barrier considerably. Open questions include how the dynamic Δ controller and chunk-size adaptation behave at much larger model scales (70B+) or with heterogeneous hardware, whether the overcommitment mechanism interacts cleanly with aggressive sequence parallelism in production multi-node clusters, and whether the approach extends to settings with multiple downstream scorers (e.g., multi-reward RLHF) where streaming contention becomes more complex.
