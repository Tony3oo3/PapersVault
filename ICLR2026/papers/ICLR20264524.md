## Problem

Existing precision medicine pipelines capture only one or two of the three critical modalities—quantitative multi-omic features, topological graph structure, and literature-scale text—limiting mechanistic interpretability for target discovery. Process Reward Models (PRMs) that guide LLM reasoning suffer from ambiguous step definitions, unreliable intermediate evaluation, and vulnerability to reward hacking, making them impractical for biomedical domains where step-wise annotations are unavailable and the combinatorial space of biological paths is enormous.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264524/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each query Q_n packages top-K (K=10) ranked features from genomic, transcriptomic, and proteomic modalities (epigenomic/methylation values are excluded due to saturation at 1), a disease-specific protein subgraph with h-hop PPI neighbors retrieved from BioMedGraphica's disease-target interaction graph, plus the cell line identifier and disease label. The model outputs both a prioritized list of top-γ (γ=100) CRISPR gene targets and an explanatory signaling subgraph G_n†, which is verbalized in expert mode and appended to the query as structured graph context for the final prediction stage.

## Method

GALAX couples a pretrained LLM (LLaMA3-8B) with a pretrained GAT-based graph encoder under a three-stage pipeline: (1) an initial LLM proposes candidate targets from multi-omic and subgraph context; (2) a reinforcement-guided graph generator incrementally constructs an explainable subgraph by adding one edge per step, where source and target nodes are sampled via message-propagation embeddings and masked MLP probability heads; (3) a final LLM refines the target list using the generated subgraph as verbalized context. The Graph Process Reward Model (GPRM) scores each intermediate subgraph by combining immediate disease-classification probability from the pretrained GNN, rollout-averaged future trajectory simulation (L=5 continuations), and a rule-based penalty for schema violations from BioMedGraphica relations. Actions are accepted only when total reward exceeds zero (greedy gating), and the generator is trained with reward-weighted cross-entropy, enabling process-level supervision without explicit step-wise labels.

## Results

On the Target-QA benchmark (363 cancer cell-line QA pairs, 80/20 split), GALAX achieves an overall precision of 0.5472 and recall of 0.5332, outperforming all baselines on every metric. For target prioritization quality, GALAX reaches Hit@10 of 0.8815 and Hit@5 of 0.9249 overall. The framework is backbone-agnostic: swapping LLaMA3-8B for Qwen2.5-7B yields comparable performance (precision 0.5445, recall 0.5405). On the external PedDep pediatric cancer dataset (31 cell lines, zero-shot), GALAX also surpasses all baselines on all metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GALAX outperforms all five graph-augmented LLM baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest graph-retrieval baselines, GALAX gains +2.1% precision and +6.0% recall over GNN-RAG (0.5258/0.4735), +1.9% precision and +7.2% recall over SubgraphRAG (0.5280/0.4617), and +2.2% precision and +6.1% recall over RoG (0.5248/0.4726). The gap is largest against G-Retriever + pre-GAT, where GALAX improves precision by +7.1% and recall by +14.0%, reflecting the difficulty of static subgraph retrieval from millions of nodes. Compared to the task-tuned language-only baseline L3-FT(QA)+Omics+KG (0.5185/0.4908), GALAX adds +2.9% precision and +4.2% recall, confirming that reinforcement-guided subgraph construction provides consistent cross-dataset gains of roughly 2–5% over static KG augmentation. The traditional multi-omic method M2T and the standalone GAT baseline perform near-zero, underscoring the necessity of joint language-graph reasoning for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

GALAX builds directly on the TOSG (Text-Omic Signaling Graph) framework (Zhang et al., 2025a) for representing multi-omic data as text-numeric graphs, and situates itself at the intersection of three active research threads: graph-augmented LLM retrieval (G-Retriever, RoG, SubgraphRAG, GNN-RAG), Process Reward Models for step-wise reasoning supervision, and multi-omic GNN integration (MOGONET, MoGCN). Its key conceptual departure is replacing static subgraph retrieval or explicit step annotations with a reinforcement-guided graph generator whose intermediate subgraphs are scored by a pretrained GNN acting as a Graph Process Reward Model, thereby providing process-level supervision without ground-truth step labels. The paper also introduces Target-QA as a new benchmark, but the underlying task—predicting CRISPR-prioritized targets from multi-omic profiles—follows the formulation established by M2T and similar omics-to-target pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GALAX is notable as the first framework to jointly integrate numerical multi-omic signals, literature-scale textual knowledge, and biological topology under a single RL paradigm with biologically grounded step-wise supervision, offering a path toward interpretable, patient-specific target prioritization. However, the evidence base is limited: the Target-QA benchmark contains only 363 samples (63 test), the classification is binary (cancerous vs. non-cancerous), and all data derive from DepMap cell lines, leaving open questions about scalability to larger, more heterogeneous clinical cohorts and validation in real patient settings. The modest 2–5% gains over strong graph-RAG baselines, combined with the small test set and single-seed variance, make it unclear whether the RL-guided subgraph construction provides a fundamental advantage over simpler retrieval strategies at scale.
