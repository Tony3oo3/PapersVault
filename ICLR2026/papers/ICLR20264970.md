## Problem

Existing medical image segmentation models are fragmented across knowledge paradigms: they rely on a single source (semantic priors from labeled data, in-context exemplars, or interactive user feedback) and are typically task- or modality-specific. No prior framework integrates all three knowledge types within one architecture, forcing clinicians to switch between separate models and disrupting workflows. The core technical challenge is representing and processing fundamentally heterogeneous knowledge forms in a unified model that maintains strong performance across all modes.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 2D slices (resized to 512×512) extracted from 3D volumes across CT, MRI, X-ray, ultrasound, pathology, dermoscopy, endoscopy, and fundus imaging. In in-context mode, one or more reference image-mask pairs are encoded via a lightweight convolutional mask encoder; in interactive mode, user clicks are rendered as disk-shaped maps (radius 1 px) with a previous-mask channel, forming a 3-channel prompt. The output is a binary or multi-class segmentation mask remapped to the original image resolution, with Dice scores computed at full resolution.

## Method

K-Prism encodes all three knowledge sources into a dual-prompt representation: 1-D sparse prompts (learnable class embeddings, masked-pooled object queries, or click queries) define *what* to segment, while 2-D dense prompts (similarity-projected reference mask features or additively fused click/mask maps) indicate *where* to attend. A UNet encoder extracts multi-scale features (1/4, 1/8, 1/16 resolution), which are fused with the 2-D prompts via negative-squared-Euclidean-distance affinity matrices (in-context) or direct addition (interactive). A Mixture-of-Experts decoder with M=5 experts applies bidirectional cross-attention—first projecting 1-D queries against 2-D features, then reversing roles—along with foreground-background masked attention and a gating network that routes queries to specialized experts per task. Joint training randomly assigns each batch to one of the three modes (probabilities 0.3/0.3/0.4), optimizing a combined BCE + Dice loss, enabling a single model to handle semantic, in-context, and interactive segmentation without architectural changes.

## Results

On 12 in-distribution datasets spanning CT, MRI, X-ray, pathology, ultrasound, and other modalities, K-Prism achieves 86.21% mean Dice for semantic segmentation (surpassing Hermes at 85.02%, Clip-driven at 84.31%, and nnU-Net at 81.00%) and 84.82% mean Dice for in-context segmentation (surpassing Iris at 81.76% and MultiverSeg at 72.41%). For interactive segmentation, K-Prism requires only 1.95 clicks to reach 90% Dice (NoC90) and achieves 95.50% Dice at 5 clicks, outperforming SegNext (2.50 / 93.80%), MedSAM (3.17 / 91.21%), and SAM2 (3.94 / 87.96%). On external datasets, K-Prism attains 83.45% mean Dice (semantic) and 82.49% (in-context), and on unseen-class datasets it reaches 31.91% mean Dice in-context, a 15-point improvement over Iris on M&Ms-2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against semantic segmentation baselines, K-Prism outperforms all universal models on average Dice: Hermes (85.02%), Clip-driven (84.31%), and UniSeg (83.96%), with the largest gains on tumor segmentation (LiTS: 64.22% vs. 58.66% for Hermes; KiTS: 78.70% vs. 73.64%). The task-specific nnU-Net (81.00%) still edges out K-Prism on a few single-organ datasets (e.g., PAPILA 96.17% vs. 95.71%) but falls well behind on multi-organ and cross-modality tasks. In in-context segmentation, K-Prism (84.82%) substantially surpasses the closest competitor Iris (81.76%) and dominates earlier methods UniverSeg (71.53%), Tyche (72.12%), and MultiverSeg (72.41%) by 12–13 points on average. For interactive segmentation, K-Prism achieves the best NoC90 (1.95 vs. SegNext 2.50, MedSAM 3.17, SAM2 3.94) and highest Dice at 5 clicks (95.50% vs. SegNext 93.80%, MultiverSeg 92.97%, MedSAM 91.21%), indicating both faster convergence and higher ceiling accuracy with fewer user interactions.

## Relation to Prior Work

K-Prism extends the line of unified medical segmentation frameworks (Iris, Verse, MultiverSeg) that each combine two of the three knowledge paradigms, by integrating all three—semantic priors, in-context exemplars, and interactive clicks—into a single architecture. Architecturally, it draws on Verse's residual resampling connections and click encoding, Mask2Former's round-robin multi-scale decoder scheduling, and SAM-style positional embeddings for click queries, but introduces a novel dual-prompt representation (1-D sparse queries for 'what' and 2-D dense feature maps for 'where') routed through a Mixture-of-Experts cross-attention decoder. The key departure from prior work is the MoE gating mechanism that dynamically allocates expert capacity per mode, enabling joint training across heterogeneous paradigms without architectural modifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying three clinically relevant segmentation paradigms into one model, K-Prism addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: healthcare institutions no longer need to maintain separate models for supervised, few-shot, and interactive workflows. The ablation showing that 2-D dense prompts are far more critical than 1-D sparse queries in in-context mode (80.84 → 54.65 Dice) provides useful architectural guidance for future prompt-based segmentation. Open questions include extending the 2D slice-based formulation to full 3D volumetric context, optimizing expert specialization across paradigms, and improving robustness under severe domain shifts where in-context matching degrades significantly.
