## Problem

Existing medical MLLM benchmarks either aggregate publicly sourced data with limited expert curation or focus narrowly on disease classification, failing to capture the stepwise visual diagnostic workflow (recognition → reasoning → diagnosis → treatment) that physicians actually follow for body-surface conditions. No prior benchmark combines authentic in-house clinical images, expert-verified fine-grained annotations, and multi-ability evaluation at scale for this domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264529/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation sample pairs a single in-house body lesion image (skin, nails, hair, oral cavity, or genitalia) with a text question drawn from one of seven diagnostic abilities. Questions come in multi-choice and open-ended formats (judgment, fill-in-the-blank, short-answer); roughly 9% are answerable from text alone (mainly treatment/suggestion items) and are retained to reflect real clinical practice. Outputs are scored on a 0/0.5/1.0 scale per question, with open-ended answers judged by a GPT-4 judge-LLM using domain-specific rubrics for color families, size equivalences, and lesion terminology, yielding 95% agreement with senior physician scoring.

## Method

The benchmark is built through a four-stage pipeline: (1) 12K de-identified patient images from 10K+ real visits are quality-filtered and balanced; (2) senior physicians (20+ years experience) annotate each image using hierarchical lexical trees covering 94 lesion types, 96 diseases, 110 body regions, and 7 attribute dimensions, with dual-physician differential diagnosis and treatment suggestions; (3) 19K QA pairs are auto-generated from ability-specific prompt templates grounded in real clinical questions, then manually reviewed and corrected by physicians; (4) an automated scoring pipeline extracts answers for multi-choice items and uses a GPT-4 judge-LLM with a fixed rubric (color-family grouping, shape categorization, size tolerance, clinical synonym mapping) for open-ended items. Evaluation is conducted zero-shot via VLMEvalKit across 20+ MLLMs (0.256B–72B open-source and top closed-source models), with human baselines from 15 primary and 15 senior physicians answering the 1,499-question validation subset.

## Results

On MedLesionVQA, the best-performing MLLM (Gemini-2.5-pro) achieves 56.24% average accuracy on the validation set, substantially below senior physicians (73.21%) and primary physicians (61.44%). Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-72B leads with 49.04% (val) / 49.04% (test), while the weakest model (LLaVA-med-v1.5-7B) scores only 8.85% (val) / 7.91% (test). The largest gaps between MLLMs and physicians appear in visually demanding tasks: lesion recognition (best MLLM 49.02% vs. senior physicians 68.26%) and disease diagnosis (60.48% vs. 73.13%), whereas the gap narrows or reverses on text-centric suggestion & treatment (84.82% vs. 85.74%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All 22 evaluated MLLMs underperform both physician groups: the best model (Gemini-2.5-pro, 56.24%) trails primary physicians (61.44%) by ~5 points and senior specialists (73.21%) by ~17 points. Among closed-source models, Gemini-2.5-pro leads, followed by GPT-5 (52.52%), Claude4-opus (51.39%), and GPT-4V (49.38%); among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-72B (49.04%) and InternVL2.5-78B (47.90%) are the strongest. A striking finding is that domain-specific medical models consistently underperform general-purpose models of comparable size: LLaVA-med-v1.5-7B (8.85%) scores 18% lower than its general counterpart LLaVA-v1.5-7B (26.48%), and MedGemma-4B (19.50%) lags behind InternVL2.5-4B (26.32%). The Suggestion & Treatment task is the easiest for MLLMs (top scores 82–86%), and text-only inputs yield nearly identical scores (82.4% vs. 81.2%), revealing that strong language generation masks weak visual grounding. Conversely, Lesion Recognition and Location Recognition are the hardest tasks, with the best MLLM reaching only ~49–52% versus 68–76% for senior physicians.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of medical VQA benchmarks and clinical diagnostic workflow modeling. Prior medical benchmarks fall into two camps: general-purpose aggregations (OmniMedVQA, GMAI-MMBench) that draw from public sources with limited expert curation, and specialized dermatology datasets (SkinCon, DDI) that focus narrowly on single-task disease classification. MedLesionVQA departs from both by structuring evaluation around a seven-ability stepwise diagnostic pipeline (lesion recognition → attribute recognition → location → spatial relation → reasoning → diagnosis → treatment) grounded in authoritative clinical textbooks, with all 19K QA pairs verified by physicians with 20+ years of experience. It repurposes the standard VQA evaluation paradigm but applies it to a multi-step, workflow-aligned clinical process rather than isolated classification or captioning tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a substantial and persistent gap: the best MLLM (Gemini-2.5-pro, 56.2%) trails primary physicians (61.4%) by 5 points and senior specialists (73.2%) by 17 points, with the largest deficits in visually demanding recognition tasks. A notable open question is why domain-specific models (LLaVA-med, MedGemma) consistently underperform general-purpose models of comparable size, suggesting that supervised fine-tuning on narrow medical corpora may degrade rather than enhance generalizable visual reasoning. The finding that treatment/suggestion scores remain high even without visual input (82.4% vs. 81.2%) raises concerns about MLLMs appearing clinically competent through text generation alone. Future work must address whether architectural innovations, larger-scale medical pretraining, or explicit visual grounding mechanisms can close the recognition gap that currently limits all evaluated models.
