## Problem

Existing ordinal regression methods predominantly rely on Continuous Space Discretization (CSD), which suffers from boundary ambiguity and the rigidity of fixed binning that amplifies errors in long-tailed distributions. Rank-based approaches implicitly encode ordinality through sequential binary subtasks but assume conditional independence across intervals, introducing systematic modeling error quantified by cumulative KL divergence. The paper asks whether ordinal regression can be reframed as a generative sequence prediction task that explicitly captures sequential ordinal dependencies while enabling adaptive, non-fixed resolution.

## Input / Output

The method accepts heterogeneous inputs—RGB images (processed via ResNet, ViT, or AesMamba encoders) for vision tasks like aesthetics assessment and age estimation, or structured tabular feature vectors (processed via FFN) for recommendation tasks like watch-time and lifetime-value prediction. Rather than outputting a direct scalar or a fixed-bin probability distribution, GoR produces a variable-length sequence of ordinal value tokens terminated by a dynamic <EOS> token; the final prediction is the additive sum of token values, enabling coarse-to-fine step-wise refinement (e.g., 50 → +5 → +3 = 58 years).

## Method

GoR reformulates ordinal regression as autoregressive token generation over a discrete vocabulary of ordinal value segments, using a model-agnostic encoder-decoder architecture where the decoder (Transformer, LSTM, GRU, or RNN) sequentially predicts tokens conditioned on the encoder's latent representation. The vocabulary is constructed via quantile-based initialization followed by pruning with the Coverage-Distinctiveness Index (CoDi), which balances quantization bias (coverage) against statistical variance (distinctiveness) to yield a compact, task-adaptive token set. Each target label is encoded into a token sequence via greedy coarse-to-fine decomposition enforcing descending token values and bounded relative error. The theoretical foundation is a closed-form MSE bound derived from bias-variance decomposition (using Popoviciu's inequality), showing error scales quadratically with sequence length and token value range, which motivates the vocabulary design principles. Training combines negative log-likelihood over the token sequence with a Huber regression loss on the reconstructed value, and the framework integrates seamlessly with generative optimization strategies (curriculum learning, N-gram prediction, DPO) at negligible adaptation cost.

## Results

GoR achieves state-of-the-art performance across all 15 benchmarks in five domains. On Watch Time Prediction, GoR reduces MAE by up to 19.14% (KuaiRand: 7.032 vs. CREAD's 9.487) and improves XAUC by up to 3.7% (KuaiRec: 0.616 vs. 0.594). On LTV prediction, GoR achieves a 12.2% MAE reduction and 14.8% SRCC improvement over HiLTV on Criteo-SSC (MAE 12.965, SRCC 0.3036). On Facial Age Estimation, GoR delivers 5.8–14.1% MAE reductions across four datasets (e.g., FG-NET: 4.68 vs. FaRL's 4.95), and on Image Aesthetics Assessment with AesMamba encoder, it reaches MAE 0.996/XAUC 0.677 on TAD66K, surpassing all 14 baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GoR consistently outperforms all 38 baselines across every domain and dataset. In Watch Time Prediction, GoR beats the strongest baseline CREAD by 3.41% MAE on KuaiRec, 19.14% on KuaiRand, and 4.94% on CIKM16, while also surpassing SWaT (the most recent method) by 6.8% MAE on KuaiRand. In LTV prediction, GoR improves over HiLTV (SOTA) by 12.2% MAE and 14.8% SRCC on Criteo-SSC, and by 3.25% MAE and 2.1% SRCC on Kaggle. In Facial Age Estimation, GoR surpasses FaRL (the strongest baseline) by 11.4% MAE on UTKFace (3.43 vs. 3.87) and 5.5% on FG-NET (4.68 vs. 4.95), while also outperforming older methods like OR-CNN, DLDL, and SORD by 20–25% MAE. In Image Aesthetics Assessment, GoR with a standard ResNet50 encoder matches or exceeds SOTA methods using expert-designed architectures (e.g., AesMamba), and with AesMamba achieves new SOTA (MAE 0.996 on TAD66K vs. 1.035 for AesMamba alone). In Historical Image Dating, GoR achieves MAE 0.51, improving 3.77% over Ord2Seq (0.53) and 11.3% over MWR (0.53). Notably, GoR's gains are architecture-agnostic: even with an outdated TANet encoder, it outperforms SOTA methods, confirming the generative paradigm itself drives the improvement rather than encoder capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

GoR departs from the dominant Continuous Space Discretization (CSD) paradigm in ordinal regression, which the authors show via Proposition 1 to suffer from systematic KL-divergence error due to its conditional independence assumption across bins. Conceptually, it repurposes the autoregressive sequence-generation paradigm from generative language models (transformer decoders, teacher forcing, curriculum learning) to the ordinal regression setting, where tokens encode additive numerical segments rather than linguistic units. The closest prior work, Ord2Seq (Wang et al., 2023), also frames OR as sequence prediction but remains within the rank-based/fixed-bin paradigm; GoR distinguishes itself through dynamic ⟨EOS⟩-terminated generation, a principled vocabulary construction via the CoDi metric, and a bias-variance error bound (Theorem 1) that has no analogue in prior OR literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing the first generative formulation of ordinal regression with a clean theoretical foundation (MSE bound + CoDi), GoR provides a model-agnostic, extensible baseline that consistently achieves SOTA across 15 benchmarks in 5 domains, suggesting the autoregressive paradigm may be a more natural fit for ordered outputs than fixed discretization. Key open questions include the inference latency cost proportional to sequence length, error accumulation in autoregressive decoding, and whether sequence-level optimization (e.g., RL-based calibration) can further close the gap. The demonstrated compatibility with generative optimization strategies (DPO, curriculum learning, N-gram prediction) without additional parameters opens a path for integrating the broader generative-modeling toolkit into ordinal tasks.
