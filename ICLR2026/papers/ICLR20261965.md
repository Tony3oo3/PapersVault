## Problem

Existing reference-based image generation methods are limited to single or few 2D reference images and cannot directly leverage 3D assets as conditioning signals. 3D asset-referenced generation demands not only semantic identity preservation but also strict geometric and textural consistency with the reference mesh, a requirement that goes beyond the capability of current subject-driven or multi-image conditioning approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261965/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D asset (mesh), which is rendered into 8 multi-view RGB images and their pixel-aligned point maps (normalized 3D coordinates in canonical object space), along with a free-form text prompt. It simultaneously produces a photorealistic RGB image and its corresponding point map, where the two outputs are spatially aligned at the pixel level but content-disentangled: the RGB image contains full scene appearance while the point map encodes only the object's 3D geometry and pose.

## Method

RefAny3D builds on Flux.1-dev (an MMDiT-based diffusion transformer) and jointly denoises RGB and point map latents in a unified token sequence, using shared positional embeddings across both domains to enforce pixel-level spatial correspondence. A domain-decoupling strategy addresses the information asymmetry between the two outputs: a Reference-LoRA handles general appearance for all conditioning tokens, a separate Domain-LoRA is activated only for point map tokens to learn geometric specifics, and a text-agnostic attention mask prevents background semantics from the text prompt from leaking into the point map branch. Conditioning tokens (multi-view RGB and point maps) are encoded via a pretrained VAE, concatenated with the noisy target latent, and assigned timestep 0 to preserve their fidelity. The model is trained on a pose-aligned dataset constructed from Subjects200k, where 3D assets are generated via Hunyuan3D and object poses estimated via FoundationPose, using the Prodigy optimizer for 30k steps on 8 H800 GPUs with classifier-free guidance.

## Results

RefAny3D achieves the best scores across all evaluation metrics in Table 1. On GPT-5 evaluation, it scores 7.123/10 overall (Texture 6.315, Geometric 7.368, Aesthetic 7.687), outperforming the closest competitor OminiControl (6.367 overall) by ~12%. On fine-grained correspondence, GIM keypoint count reaches 3901.3, surpassing DSD (3568.7) by ~9%. On semantic consistency, CLIP image similarity (avg 0.873, max 0.923) and DINO similarity (avg 0.720, max 0.843) are also the highest among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RefAny3D outperforms all five quantitative baselines across every reported metric. The closest competitor is OminiControl (Tan et al., 2025), which uses a single reference view and achieves 6.367 GPT-eval overall vs. RefAny3D's 7.123; the gap is most pronounced on geometric consistency (7.368 vs. 6.578) and texture consistency (6.315 vs. 5.631). DreamBooth (Ruiz et al., 2023) and DSD (Cai et al., 2025) are competitive on CLIP/DINO semantic similarity but trail significantly on GPT-eval texture/geometric scores and GIM keypoint matching, reflecting their inability to preserve fine-grained 3D structure. Textual Inversion (Gal et al., 2022) and IP-Adapter (Ye et al., 2023) are the weakest baselines, scoring below 5.0 on GPT-eval overall, as they compress the reference into a few text tokens and lose spatial detail. For the editing-based qualitative comparison, QwenImage-Edit-2509 (Wu et al., 2025) produces foreground-background mismatches and hallucinates geometry on novel viewpoints (e.g., the rear of a tank), whereas RefAny3D maintains consistent 3D structure across views without per-subject training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the subject-driven generation line (DreamBooth, IP-Adapter, DSD, OminiControl) from 2D image references to 3D asset references, building on Flux.1-dev's MMDiT architecture and OminiControl's unified token-sequence conditioning. The key conceptual departure is the use of normalized object coordinate point maps (Wang et al., 2019) as structural anchors, jointly generated with RGB in a spatially aligned dual-branch framework, rather than relying solely on appearance-based attention. The domain-decoupling mechanism (dual LoRA + text-agnostic attention) addresses a practical challenge specific to cross-domain generation that prior single-modality or multi-view methods do not encounter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens a practical pathway for creators who already own 3D assets (games, product design, advertising) to generate diverse scene-consistent images without manual rendering or per-subject fine-tuning. The point-map-as-geometric-anchor formulation is a clean and generalizable idea that could transfer to other 3D-conditioned generation tasks. Key limitations include poor handling of non-rigid objects (deformable assets retain rigidity) and significant computational overhead when conditioning on many viewpoints, both of which constrain immediate deployment. An open question is whether the dual-branch point-map generation can be replaced by cheaper geometric conditioning (e.g., depth or normal maps) without sacrificing cross-view consistency.
