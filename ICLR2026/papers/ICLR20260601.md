## Problem

Existing AIGB (AI-Generated Bidding) methods model auto-bidding as conditional trajectory generation but can only imitate offline trajectories without reward feedback, making extrapolation beyond the dataset unreliable and potentially risky. No dedicated offline RL algorithm with theoretical guarantees for safe generalization of generative bidding models existed, and the lack of a reward signal made it infeasible to direct exploration of the generative planner toward higher-quality trajectories.

## Input / Output

The planner (a Causal Transformer) takes the desired trajectory quality condition y* (set to (1+ε)·y_m, where y_m is the max quality in the offline dataset) and history states s_{1:t} (each comprising time step, cost ratio c_{t-1}/B, and static advertiser features) as input tokens, and autoregressively predicts the next state as a Gaussian distribution N(μ_θ, σ_θ²). The evaluator takes a full trajectory τ = [s_1, ..., s_T] plus an LLM embedding of advertiser text features and outputs a scalar quality score ŷ_φ(τ). Actions (bid scaling factors) are derived from generated trajectories via a separate inverse dynamics model, decoupling planning from control.

## Method

AIGB-Pearl integrates a supervised trajectory evaluator with a KL-Lipschitz-constrained score-maximization objective for the generative planner. The evaluator is trained with MSE loss plus a Lipschitz penalty (enforcing √T·R_m-Lipschitz continuity), LLM embedding enhancement (QWen2.5-1.5B-Instruct for advertiser text features), and a Bradley-Terry pairwise ranking loss. The planner's training loss combines three terms: (i) maximizing the evaluator score on generated trajectories, (ii) a conditional behavior cloning loss (KL constraint) preserving fidelity to the offline dataset, and (iii) a Lipschitz penalty on the planner's sensitivity to the condition y, where the Wasserstein distance W_1(p_θ(τ|y₁), p_θ(τ|y₂)) is upper-bounded via synchronous coupling (sharing the same Gaussian noise sequence η_{1:T} across two conditioned generations). A provable sub-optimality bound (Theorem 3) certifies that the planner's true performance gap from optimal is bounded by 2δ_D + (1+2k)√T·R_m[√δ_M + √δ_K + (1+ε)y_m·L_p], ensuring safe exploration within a certified neighborhood of high-quality offline trajectories.

## Results

In simulated experiments across four budget levels (1.5k–3.0k), AIGB-Pearl achieves GMV of 502.98–574.17, outperforming the strongest baseline DiffBid by +2.09% to +4.62%. In real-world A/B tests on Taobao (6k advertisers, 19 days), AIGB-Pearl delivers +3.00% GMV over DiffBid, +3.43% over USCB, and +3.30% over DT, while maintaining Cost fluctuations within ±2%. On unseen advertisers (4k, generalization test), AIGB-Pearl still achieves +3.32% GMV over DiffBid and +3.08% over DT, confirming superior out-of-distribution generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AIGB-Pearl wins against all eight baselines on GMV in both simulated and real-world settings. The closest competitors are the two AIGB methods: DiffBid (conditional diffusion) and DT (Causal Transformer), which AIGB-Pearl outperforms by +2.09% to +4.62% (simulated) and +3.00%/+3.30% (real-world) in GMV. Among offline RL baselines, MOPO and MORL are the strongest, but AIGB-Pearl still leads by 3–5% in GMV. The model-free offline RL methods (BCQ, CQL, IQL) and the online RL method USCB trail further behind, with USCB achieving the lowest GMV at most budget levels. Notably, in the real-world generalization test on 4k unseen advertisers, AIGB-Pearl maintains its +3% GMV advantage over both DiffBid and DT, whereas the gap between DiffBid and DT narrows, suggesting AIGB-Pearl's KL-Lipschitz constraints provide more robust out-of-distribution behavior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative auto-bidding (AIGB) line of work—exemplified by DiffBid (Guo et al., KDD 2024) and Decision Transformer (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2021)—by introducing a learned trajectory evaluator and a KL-Lipschitz-constrained score-maximization objective that enables the generative planner to improve beyond the offline dataset. While prior AIGB methods treat auto-bidding as pure conditional trajectory imitation without reward-based policy optimization, AIGB-Pearl integrates offline RL concepts (KL-constrained behavior cloning, OOD mitigation) into a generative model for the first time in this domain. The theoretical apparatus draws on standard tools—Pinsker's inequality, Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality, and Lipschitz continuity of the trajectory quality function—but their combination into a certifiable sub-optimality bound for a generative planner is novel. The synchronous coupling technique for tightening the Wasserstein upper bound in the Lipschitz penalty is a practical algorithmic contribution that distinguishes the implementation from naive random-coupling approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental limitation of generative auto-bidding: the inability to leverage performance feedback to improve generation quality beyond the offline dataset, which is critical in safety-sensitive advertising where OOD exploration can cause monetary losses. The +3% GMV improvement in Taobao-scale A/B tests translates to millions of RMB in daily revenue, making this practically significant for the advertising industry. The KL-Lipschitz constraint framework, with its provable sub-optimality bound, offers a template for safe policy optimization in other generative decision-making problems where OOD risk is a concern. Open questions include whether the moderate OOD evaluator accuracy (75% AUC in real-world) is sufficient for more complex or multi-agent bidding environments, whether the Lipschitz structure of trajectory quality generalizes beyond the second-price auction setting, and how the method scales to online or streaming settings where the offline dataset assumption breaks down.
