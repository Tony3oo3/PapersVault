## Problem

Visual language models face severe computational overhead when processing high-frame-rate, long-duration videos, with inference costs scaling sharply with token count. Existing training-free token compression methods (spatial or temporal merging) inevitably discard information, causing significant performance degradation under aggressive compression ratios. The paper tackles how to compress a video to a single frame's worth of visual tokens while retaining reasoning performance comparable to the full 64-frame input.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video uniformly sampled at 1 fps (capped at 64 frames, each at 128×28×28 resolution yielding 14×14 ViT patches) and a text question as input. The Visual Memory Retriever first segments the video into event-level clips and retrieves the top-3 query-relevant fragments, after which the memory-aware compressor reduces the visual tokens from ~2590 (64 frames) to ~123 (1-frame equivalent), a 95% reduction. The output is a chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a structured answer (multiple-choice letter, numerical value, OCR text, or free-form text) enclosed in dedicated tags.

## Method

MARC is a retrieve-then-compress framework with two core components. First, a Visual Memory Retriever (VMR) uses TransNet V2 for event-based video segmentation and a contrastive embedding model to retrieve the top-k query-relevant clips, reducing the search space and eliminating redundant frames before compression. Second, a memory-aware temporal compression layer performs two-stage cosine-similarity frame merging: intra-segment merging preserves local dynamics within each retrieved episode, while a lightweight cross-segment consolidation acts only as a last resort to meet the token budget. The key training innovation is C-GRPO, a modified GRPO that distills reasoning from a 64-frame teacher to a 1-frame student by introducing a compression retention reward r_c = α·max(0, η−τ) where η is the ratio of student to teacher reward, gated by answer correctness to prevent reward hacking. This turns token compression into an alignment problem rather than a purely geometric heuristic, and the full objective includes a KL anchor to a reference policy for training stability.

## Results

MARC-3B achieves a mean score of 42.20 across six video benchmarks using only 1 frame's worth of visual tokens (122.69 tokens on average), compared to 42.21 for the 64-frame Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline (2589.93 tokens), representing a 95% reduction in visual tokens with nearly identical accuracy. The method also reduces GPU peak memory from 41.63 GB to 11.48 GB (72.4% reduction) and cuts LLM generation latency by 23.9% on MMVU. On individual benchmarks, MARC surpasses the 64-frame baseline on MMVU (51.99 vs. 48.64), MVBench (45.82 vs. 44.77), and TempCompass (55.34 vs. 38.05), while retaining 74% of baseline performance on the long-video VideoMME benchmark (39.44 vs. 53.55).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MARC-3B (1 frame, mean 42.20) essentially matches the 64-frame Qwen2.5-VL-3B baseline (42.21) while using only 4.71% of the visual tokens, and slightly outperforms the larger InternVL3.5-4B (42.00) and Gemma-3-4B (38.87) models. Against prior training-free compression methods, MARC achieves substantial gains: +65% over ByteVideoLLM (25.52), +31% over MovieChat (32.10), and +52% over VidCom2 (27.84) in mean accuracy. On individual benchmarks, MARC beats the 64-frame Qwen2.5-VL-3B on MMVU (+3.35), MVBench (+1.05), and TempCompass (+17.29), but trails on VSI-Bench (−5.38), VideoMMMU (−2.22), and VideoMME (−14.11), with the VideoMME gap attributed to the extreme compression of very long videos (avg. 2466 s). MovieChat, which also uses short-term memory compression, is the closest prior method (32.10) but still lags by 10.1 points on mean, particularly on VideoMMMU (−7.33) and VideoMME (−13.03). The efficiency comparison shows MARC reduces GPU memory by 72.4% (41.63→11.48 GB) and generation latency by 23.9% relative to the uncompressed 64-frame baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) from mathematical reasoning to video token compression, introducing C-GRPO as the first RL-based post-training strategy specifically designed for compressing visual tokens in video LLMs. It builds on the temporal frame-merging mechanism of MovieChat (Song et al., 2024) but restructures it into a two-stage, memory-aware procedure guided by a Visual Memory Retriever (VMR) inspired by cognitive science event segmentation. The key departure from prior work is the 'retrieve-then-compress' paradigm: rather than applying training-free heuristics (as in ByteVideoLLM, VidCom, or MovieChat) that inevitably discard information, MARC first selects query-relevant event segments and then uses RL distillation to transfer reasoning from a 64-frame teacher to a 1-frame student, fundamentally reframing compression as an alignment problem rather than a geometric reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 95% visual token reduction with near-identical mean accuracy (42.20 vs. 42.21) and 72% GPU memory savings make this a practically significant step toward deploying video LLMs in latency-sensitive, resource-constrained settings such as autonomous driving and real-time surveillance. A notable finding is that the VMR alone actually *boosts* baseline performance (45.56 vs. 42.21), suggesting that selecting relevant clips matters more than processing more frames uniformly — an insight that could reshape how video inputs are prepared. However, the method's effectiveness degrades on very long videos (VideoMME retains only 74% of baseline), and the evaluation is concentrated on a single 3B model family, leaving open whether the RL distillation paradigm generalizes to larger models or substantially longer temporal contexts. Adaptive, duration-aware token allocation is identified as a natural next step.
