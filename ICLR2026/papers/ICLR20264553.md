## Problem

Masked Autoregressive (MAR) models enable parallel generation of multiple visual tokens per step, but directly generating too many tokens at once causes severe quality degradation due to the inherent difficulty of modeling spatially correlated visual tokens in a single step. The paper asks: can we decompose the generation process so that the hard part (creating global structure from scratch) is done slowly and carefully, while the easy part (filling in details on an existing scaffold) is done quickly and in parallel, thereby achieving extreme acceleration without quality loss?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264553/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

For class-conditional ImageNet generation, the input is a class label and the output is a 256×256 RGB image tokenized into 256 tokens via the KL-16 tokenizer. For text-to-image generation, the input is a free-form text prompt (encoded by T5-XXL) and the output is a 512×512 RGB image decomposed into 1024 tokens via a VAE encoder. The method is entirely training-free: it modifies only the sampling order and diffusion scheduling of an already-trained MAR model, requiring no retraining or architectural changes.

## Method

GtR decomposes generation into a two-stage checkerboard process: in the generation stage, tokens at positions where (i+j) mod 2 = 0 are produced at a conservative rate (fewer tokens per MAR step) to establish the global semantic scaffold; in the reconstruction stage, the remaining tokens at (i+j) mod 2 = 1 are decoded in as few as 1–2 highly parallel steps, since each ungenerated token is already surrounded by generated neighbors that strongly constrain its distribution. The generation stage is further hierarchically subdivided into K−1 sub-stages via iterative bisection (Algorithm 1), ensuring that the earliest sub-stage places tokens uniformly across the entire image to establish structure as quickly as possible. A stage-aware diffusion schedule linearly decreases diffusion steps from T_max to T_min during generation and holds a fixed low T_rec during reconstruction. Additionally, Frequency-Weighted Token Selection (FTS) applies a Discrete Fourier Transform to each token's conditioning vector, computes a high-frequency-weighted importance score, and allocates extra diffusion steps (T_detail=50) to the top 10% highest-scoring tokens during reconstruction to better model fine-grained texture regions. Because MAR models are trained on random permutations of all token orders, GtR's checkerboard sampling order is already within the training distribution, making the entire approach training-free and applicable to any MAR model.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, MAR-H + GtR achieves a 3.72× speedup (GPU latency 0.22–0.59s vs. 0.81s) while maintaining identical FID (1.59) and improving IS from 299.1 to 304.4. At the more aggressive 8-step setting, GtR reaches 6.28× speedup with FID 2.16, substantially outperforming LazyMAR (FID 4.06) and DiSA (FID 3.15) at comparable speedups. On GenEval 512×512 text-to-image generation, LightGen + GtR achieves 3.32× speedup with Overall score 0.56 (vs. 0.55 baseline) and 3.82× speedup at 12 steps while maintaining Overall 0.55.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GtR dominates all MAR acceleration baselines on both speed and quality: at 64→16 steps, GtR achieves 3.72× speedup with FID 1.59, versus DiSA (2.99×, FID 2.11), LazyMAR (3.42×, FID 1.94), and Halton (2.38×, FID 3.18). At the more aggressive 8-step setting, the gap widens dramatically—GtR maintains FID 2.16 while LazyMAR degrades to 4.06 and DiSA to 3.15. Against non-MAR SOTA models, MAR-H+GtR achieves the lowest GPU latency (0.22–0.59s) among all methods while matching or exceeding their FID (1.59 vs. DiT-XL/2's 2.27, LlamaGen's 3.05, FlowAR-H's 2.65). On GenEval text-to-image, GtR outperforms LazyMAR at both 32-step (3.32× vs. 2.00× speedup, Overall 0.56 vs. 0.54) and 12-step (3.82× vs. 2.40×, Overall 0.55 vs. 0.53) settings. Simple step reduction (Step=16 or Step=8) causes severe quality collapse (FID 4.49 or 17.66), confirming that GtR's structured sampling is essential rather than merely reducing computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MAR (Li et al., NeurIPS 2024), which extends MaskGIT's next-set prediction paradigm with a diffusion head for continuous token generation. It departs from prior MAR acceleration methods—LazyMAR (feature caching), DiSA (uniform diffusion step annealing), and Halton-MaskGIT (fixed token ordering)—by introducing a structurally motivated two-stage sampling strategy (checkerboard generation followed by parallel reconstruction) rather than modifying the model or applying uniform step reduction. The Frequency-Weighted Token Selection component further differentiates the work by allocating diffusion compute based on per-token spectral complexity, addressing a heterogeneity that prior methods treat uniformly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of GtR means it can be dropped onto any existing MAR checkpoint without retraining, making it immediately deployable across model scales and generation tasks. The finding that spatially uniform token coverage (checkerboard) establishes image content so early that the remaining tokens are essentially a 'reconstruction' problem provides a useful conceptual lens for understanding MAR's sampling dynamics. Open questions include whether the checkerboard partition is truly optimal versus other spatially uniform patterns, whether the approach transfers to higher-resolution or video generation settings, and whether the FTS frequency criterion generalizes beyond the specific tokenization schemes tested (KL-16, VAE).
