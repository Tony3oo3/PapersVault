## Problem

Existing LLM-powered agent frameworks for visual content generation operate under the idealized assumption that tool invocations always succeed, relying on generic textual descriptions (e.g., 'capable of generating images aligned with input text') that fail to capture precise performance boundaries. This ambiguity introduces uncertainty into planning and scheduling, causing suboptimal tool selection and misaligned execution, particularly in complex AIGC tasks where nuanced tool capabilities significantly affect outcomes.

## Input / Output

PerfGuard accepts multimodal inputs—text prompts, reference images, or both—which the Analyst role parses into a concise task summary (≤20 tokens), target image semantics, and evaluation goals. The system iteratively decomposes the task into subtasks executed over multiple rounds, with each round producing an intermediate image that is evaluated and fed back to guide the next decision. The final output is a generated or edited RGB image, with the tool library spanning text-to-image models (FLUX, SD3, PixArt-α, SDXL), editing models (AnySD, UltraEdit, ICEdit, Step1X_Edit), and customized generation tools (DreamO, EasyControl, IPAdapterPlus).

## Method

PerfGuard introduces three mechanisms within a standardized four-role agent system (Analyst, Planner, Worker, Self-Evaluator). First, Performance-Aware Selection Modeling (PASM) replaces textual tool descriptions with a multi-dimensional scoring matrix initialized from T2I-CompBench (7 generation dimensions) and ImgEdit-Bench (7 editing dimensions); the Worker generates a task-specific preference weight vector and computes weighted suitability scores to select the best tool. Second, Adaptive Preference Updating (APU) employs an exploration-exploitation strategy (top-m + random-n candidates), compares the theoretical tool ranking against the actual ranking derived from GPT-4o comparative evaluation of outputs, and iteratively adjusts the performance matrix with a tunable step size η. Third, Capability-Aligned Planning Optimization (CAPO) extends Step-aware Preference Optimization to the Planner's autoregressive process: at each step the Planner generates k candidate subtasks, the Self-Evaluator scores their outputs, and a DPO-style loss aligns the Planner toward high-performing subtask sequences, augmented by CLIP-based memory retrieval of past successful trajectories.

## Results

On T2I-CompBench (basic generation), PerfGuard achieves the best scores across all six dimensions, with Complex at 0.5007 (vs. 0.4499 for GenArtist, the strongest agent baseline) and Spatial at 0.6120 (vs. 0.5437 for GenArtist). On OneIG-Bench (advanced generation), PerfGuard leads in Reasoning (0.350 vs. 0.318 for T2I-Copilot) and Alignment (0.834 vs. 0.821). On Complex-Edit (Level-3 editing), PerfGuard attains the highest overall score (O=8.84) and Instruction Following (IF=8.95), outperforming OmniGen (O=8.13) and Step1X_Edit (O=8.10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PerfGuard outperforms all nine baselines across every benchmark and metric. Against diffusion models (FLUX, SD3), PerfGuard gains +0.135 on Complex and +0.326 on Spatial over FLUX, confirming that LLM-driven planning substantially improves compositional generation. Against CoT-based methods (GoT, T2I-R1), PerfGuard leads by +0.175 on Complex over T2I-R1 and +0.175 over GoT, showing that multi-tool orchestration surpasses single-model chain-of-thought reasoning. Among agent-based competitors, PerfGuard beats GenArtist (the closest rival) by +0.051 on Complex and +0.068 on Spatial, and T2I-Copilot by +0.102 on Complex, attributing the gap to performance-aware tool selection rather than planning alone. On the Complex-Edit benchmark, PerfGuard surpasses OmniGen by +0.71 on IF and +0.71 on O, and Step1X_Edit by +1.00 on IF, demonstrating that capability-matched tool scheduling is critical for multi-step editing. Notably, when all agents are forced to use the same open-source LLM (Qwen3-VL-32B), PerfGuard's degradation is only 9.4% on Complex versus 48.4% for GenArtist and 38.2% for T2I-Copilot, indicating the framework's robustness is structural rather than dependent on a specific LLM.

## Relation to Prior Work

PerfGuard extends the multi-agent visual generation line (GenArtist, T2I-Copilot, CompAgent) by replacing their reliance on free-text tool descriptions with a quantitative, multi-dimensional performance scoring system (PASM) initialized from established benchmarks (T2I-CompBench, ImgEdit-Bench). It also adapts Step-aware Preference Optimization (SPO), originally developed for aligning diffusion denoising steps with human preferences, to the planner's autoregressive subtask generation (CAPO), and introduces an exploration-exploitation loop (APU) that iteratively refines tool scores from actual execution feedback. The key departure from prior agent frameworks is the explicit modeling of tool performance boundaries as a first-class planning signal rather than an implicit assumption of uniform tool reliability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even GPT-4o achieves only 27.8% accuracy in text-based tool selection (72.2% error) while PerfGuard's performance-driven approach reaches 85.8% (14.2% error) highlights a fundamental limitation of current agent architectures that treat tool capabilities as opaque text. However, the approach's dependence on pre-existing multi-dimensional benchmarks for score initialization limits its immediate transferability to domains lacking such benchmarks, and the evaluated tool library (11 tools) is small relative to the scalability claims. Open questions include how to bootstrap performance boundaries in novel domains, how the framework behaves under dynamic tool addition/removal, and whether the preference-optimization-based planner training generalizes beyond the specific Qwen3 backbone used.
