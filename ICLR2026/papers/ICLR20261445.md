## Problem

Deep Research Agents (DRAs) that autonomously search, analyze, and synthesize web sources into analyst-level reports have become one of the most widely deployed LLM agent categories, yet no comprehensive benchmark exists to systematically evaluate their end-to-end capabilities. Existing benchmarks assess isolated skills—web browsing, single-turn retrieval, or long-form generation—rather than the full research pipeline, and the opacity of DRA internal reasoning makes ground-truth-based evaluation infeasible for open-ended research tasks.

## Input / Output

RACE takes a research task prompt, a DRA-generated report (with citations stripped in preprocessing), and a fixed high-quality reference report, then produces per-criterion scores (0–10) across four dynamically weighted dimensions (Comprehensiveness, Insight/Depth, Instruction-Following, Readability) that are aggregated into a single relative quality score via a ratio against the reference. FACT takes the same DRA report with its citation markers intact, extracts and deduplicates statement–URL pairs, retrieves each cited page via the Jina Reader API, and outputs two scalar metrics: Citation Accuracy (fraction of supported pairs) and Average Effective Citations per Task (count of supported pairs). The benchmark comprises 100 bilingual (50 Chinese, 50 English) PhD-level tasks across 22 domains, with per-domain counts proportionally derived from a statistical analysis of 96,147 anonymized real user queries.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary, fully automated LLM-as-a-Judge frameworks. RACE first prompts a Judge LLM to generate task-specific dimension weights and a set of tailored, weighted criteria for each of four orthogonal quality dimensions; these criteria are frozen across all agents evaluated on that task. Scoring is reference-based: the Judge LLM compares the target report against a high-quality reference report on every criterion, and the final score is computed as the target's weighted dimension score divided by the sum of target and reference scores, which mitigates the uniform-high-score problem observed in absolute scoring. FACT operates on the citation layer: a Judge LLM extracts discrete statement–URL pairs from the report, deduplicates pairs describing the same fact from the same URL, retrieves the cited webpage text via the Jina Reader API, and issues a binary support judgment per pair; Citation Accuracy and Average Effective Citations are then averaged across all 100 tasks. Human consistency is validated through 225 person-hours of expert annotation (70+ Master's-level annotators, 3 per task, 50 Chinese tasks, 4 agents), showing RACE's Pairwise Agreement Rate (71.33%) exceeds human inter-annotator agreement (68.44%) and that rankings are stable under reference substitution, length inflation, and Judge LLM swap (Gemini-2.5-Pro vs. Qwen3-235B).

## Results

On DeepResearch Bench (100 tasks, 22 domains), Gemini-2.5-Pro Deep Research achieves the highest RACE overall score of 49.71 among proprietary DRAs, while LangChain Open Deep Research with GPT-5 backbone reaches 50.60, surpassing all proprietary agents. In the FACT framework, Gemini-2.5-Pro DR leads with 165.34 average effective citations per task, though Perplexity Deep Research achieves the highest citation accuracy at 82.63%. Human consistency validation shows RACE(Full) achieves a Pairwise Agreement Rate of 71.33% (exceeding human inter-annotator agreement of 68.44%) and an Overall Pearson Correlation of 99.54% with expert judgments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary DRAs, Gemini-2.5-Pro Deep Research (RACE 49.71) leads, followed by OpenAI Deep Research (46.45), Claude Research (45.00), and Kimi Researcher (44.64); pairwise t-tests confirm Gemini significantly outperforms all others (p < 0.001, Cohen's d 0.65–1.19), while Claude, Kimi, and Doubao are statistically indistinguishable (p > 0.18). The open-source LangChain ODR with GPT-5 (50.60) surpasses all proprietary DRAs in RACE, demonstrating that open-source agent frameworks with frontier LLMs can match or exceed commercial products. In citation quality (FACT), Perplexity Deep Research achieves the highest citation accuracy (82.63%) but fewer effective citations (31.20) than Gemini-2.5-Pro DR (165.34), revealing a trade-off between citation precision and retrieval breadth. Among fully open-source RL-based systems, Tongyi DeepResearch (40.46) approaches Perplexity DR's level, while DeepResearcher (10.77) lags significantly due to frequent failures in generating complete reports. Traditional LLMs with built-in search tools (e.g., GPT-4o-Search-Preview at 35.10, Claude-3.7-Sonnet w/Search at 40.67) consistently underperform dedicated DRAs, confirming that multi-round agentic research substantially outperforms single-pass search-augmented generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LLM-as-a-Judge paradigm (Zheng et al., 2023) and the topic taxonomy from WebOrganizer (Wettig et al., 2025), but departs from prior agent-evaluation benchmarks (AgentBench, BrowseComp, WebArena, Mind2Web 2) that assess isolated capabilities like single-turn web browsing or code generation. The closest prior efforts—Xu et al. (2025) on a single scientific domain and Bosse et al. (2025) on offline RetroSearch with process-oriented metrics—do not evaluate the full multi-round search-to-report pipeline or verify human consistency of their scoring. DeepResearch Bench is positioned as the first benchmark that unifies report-quality assessment (RACE) and citation-fidelity measurement (FACT) for the complete DRA workflow, grounded in a distribution derived from 96K real user queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As DRAs become the most widely deployed LLM agent category, the absence of a standardized, human-aligned evaluation has hindered meaningful comparison across commercial and open-source systems. The RACE framework's reference-based dynamic-criteria approach generalizes beyond deep research (demonstrated on RewardBench 2), suggesting a reusable methodology for evaluating long-form generative outputs more broadly. Key limitations include the modest 100-task scale, dependence on proprietary Judge LLMs (though Qwen3 serves as a partial open-source substitute), and FACT's sensitivity to each system's opaque browsing stack. Open questions include scaling the benchmark to thousands of tasks, reducing judge-model dependence, and extending evaluation to iterative/multi-turn research workflows rather than single-shot report generation.
