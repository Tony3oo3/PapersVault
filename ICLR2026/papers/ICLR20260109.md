## Problem

Deep models exploit spurious correlations (backgrounds, dataset artifacts) that inflate average accuracy but cause catastrophic failures on minority subpopulations. Existing robust-learning methods either require group or environment labels, operate only at the data or loss level, or apply uniform regularization that cannot target specific internal pathways. The paper asks whether one can directly regulate the internal flow of class-conditional alignment energy at the neuron-connection level during training, without any group annotations, to suppress shortcut pathways while preserving robust features.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (resized to 224×224 with random crops, horizontal flips, and mild color jitter) and produces class labels and softmax probabilities through a ResNet-50 backbone with a linear classifier. The key internal signal is a D×C matrix of per-neuron, per-class alignment scores computed on the 2048-dimensional penultimate representation during the forward pass; these scores are treated as stop-gradient statistics and never backpropagated. The method adds only a D×C EMA buffer and per-class percentile thresholds, requiring no architectural modification and no group labels at inference or training time.

## Method

Alignment-Gated Suppression (AGS) computes a confidence-weighted alignment score e_jk(x) = −p_k(x)·W_jk·φ_j(x) for each neuron-class link, where more negative values indicate stronger class-aligned support. Batch-wise means are smoothed via an exponential moving average (β=0.75), and after a 5-epoch warm-up, a within-class q-th percentile threshold (q=20) identifies the most extreme contributors per class. Gated connections undergo a multiplicative decay W_jk ← (1−α·s_jk)(1−0.05α)·W_jk applied after the forward pass but before backpropagation, with α∈[0.005,0.15] controlling shrinkage strength. The mechanism is scale-invariant under logit-preserving reparameterizations, budgeted by percentiles (at most ⌈q%⌉ features gated per class), and provably contracts and sparsifies persistently gated pathways while leaving robust features that avoid the lower-alignment tail untouched. Theoretically, AGS is shown to increase minority-group margins, act as a path-norm-like capacity controller, and improve calibration via EMA-stabilized gating, all with <5% training overhead and full compatibility with standard ERM and any optimizer.

## Results

On CelebA, AGS achieves 95.63% unbiased and 93.95% bias-conflicting accuracy, outperforming the strongest prior method EvA-E (90.51%/88.74%) by over 5 points on both splits. On BAR, AGS attains 76.09% average accuracy, a +2.39-point gain over EvA-E and +15.58 over ERM. On Waterbirds, AGS achieves the highest average accuracy (97.44%) with a worst-group accuracy of 80.93%, statistically comparable to EvA-E (81.31%) but trailing JTT (84.98%) by ~4 points. On the COCO Gender/Object Bias benchmark, AGS reaches 84.27% average accuracy and narrows the unbiased–conflicting gap from 6.20 to 0.67 (Sports) and 5.84 to 2.15 (Kitchen).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGS outperforms all 12 baselines on BAR average accuracy (76.09% vs. best prior EvA-E at 73.70%) and on both CelebA splits (95.63%/93.95% vs. EvA-E's 90.51%/88.74%), with the largest margin over ERM (+15.58 on BAR, +41.43 on CelebA conflicting). On Waterbirds, AGS achieves the top average accuracy (97.44%) but trails JTT (84.98%) by ~4 points on worst-group accuracy, reflecting the classic average-vs-worst Pareto trade-off; it is statistically comparable to EvA-E (80.93 vs. 81.31) on WGA. On the COCO Gender/Object Bias benchmark, AGS surpasses GMBM (84.27 vs. 83.54) and BAdd (84.27 vs. 81.76) in average accuracy while substantially reducing bias-induced gaps. The closest competitor overall is EvA-E (He et al., 2025), which AGS dominates on BAR and CelebA but is marginally ahead on Waterbirds WGA; the paper's ablation (Table 3) confirms that replacing the parameter-space alignment signal with an EvA-style activation-space proxy drops Waterbirds WGA from 79.4% to 70.1%, underscoring the distinct mechanism. On the ImageNet-9 Backgrounds Challenge, ERM+AGS improves Mixed-Rand accuracy from 80.17% to 82.47% while maintaining 98.69% on the Original split.

## Relation to Prior Work

AGS carves a distinct niche in the robust-learning-under-spurious-correlations literature by intervening at the weight/connection level during training, rather than at the data (reweighting, augmentation) or loss (DRO, invariance) level used by GroupDRO, IRM, JTT, and LfF. Its closest conceptual neighbor is EvA (He et al., 2025), which computes an activation-space evidence energy for post-hoc channel deletion; AGS instead derives a parameter-space alignment statistic (first-order free-energy sensitivity to classifier weights) and applies it as an in-training, link-level multiplicative decay with EMA smoothing and within-class percentile gating. The mechanism also draws on path-norm-style capacity control and consistency-based EMA ideas, but the specific combination—confidence-weighted, class-conditional, percentile-budgeted contraction of the most negative alignment tail—is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AGS offers a practical, group-agnostic regularizer (<5% overhead, no architectural changes) that consistently improves worst-group accuracy and calibration across four spurious-correlation benchmarks, filling the gap for settings where group labels are unavailable or unreliable. Key open questions include: the method currently gates only the final classifier (or top-layer channels), yet spurious pathways can originate in earlier layers; small or imbalanced batches destabilize the percentile threshold and risk over-suppression; and early miscalibration can distort the confidence-weighted attribution signal. The paper also notes that combining AGS with group-discovery or DRO pipelines could unify example-level and pathway-level robustness, and extending gating to intermediate modules (channels, attention heads) is a natural next step.
