## Problem

Evaluating large language models on full benchmark datasets (thousands of items) incurs prohibitive inference costs as model scales grow. While prior work has noted redundancy in evaluation data, existing efficient-evaluation methods rely on linear formulations (IRT, cluster-based weighting) that cannot fully exploit the structured sparsity in the model-item performance matrix. This paper asks: can we formulate efficient benchmarking as a sparse optimization problem and solve it with gradient-based, task-aware anchor selection?

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a model-item binary score matrix S ∈ {-1,1}^{m×n} (5,000 models × n items from the Open-LLM Leaderboard), where +1/−1 encode correct/incorrect predictions. At inference time, only the k anchor-item predictions (k ∈ {20,40,60,80,100}) for a new model are fed into a 4-layer MLP, which outputs a single scalar estimate of the full-benchmark accuracy. The learned sparse weight vector W (‖W‖₀ ≤ k) can take negative values—unlike cluster-proportion weights in prior methods—thereby expanding the optimization space beyond convex combinations.

## Method

SparseEval casts efficient evaluation as a sparse optimization problem: minimize the L₂ reconstruction error between an MLP's prediction (fed with k weighted anchor scores) and the true full-benchmark average, subject to ‖W‖₀ ≤ k. A 4-layer MLP serves as the non-linear aggregation function f, trained end-to-end via gradient descent so that anchor weights are directly optimized rather than assigned from cluster sizes. For anchor selection, the paper introduces an iterative refinement loop (R=10 steps) that computes an Anchor Importance Score (L₁ norm of the backpropagated gradient w.r.t. each anchor's column) and a Candidate Importance Score (absolute dot product between a candidate's prediction pattern and the current residual vector), then swaps the weakest anchor for the strongest candidate. This task-aware refinement, combined with the non-linear MLP, allows SparseEval to achieve sub-1% MAE and Kendall's τ > 0.90 with only 100 anchors—over 5× fewer than baselines—while also providing theoretical guarantees (Propositions 1–2) that refinement monotonically decreases reconstruction error in the linear setting.

## Results

On six LLM benchmarks (ARC, GSM8K, HellaSwag, MMLU, TruthfulQA, Winogrande) with 5,000 models, SparseEval achieves MAE below 1% on HellaSwag (0.827%), MMLU (0.842%), and TruthfulQA (1.027%) using only 100 anchor items, while maintaining Kendall's τ above 0.90 on five of six benchmarks. At 20 anchors, SparseEval still achieves MAE of 1.477–3.305% and τ of 0.794–0.878, substantially outperforming all baselines. The method reduces evaluation to ~100 instances while preserving ranking consistency, representing a >5× reduction in anchor points compared to baselines achieving comparable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SparseEval outperforms all three baselines across every benchmark and anchor count. Against Anchor Points (Vivek et al., 2023), the gap is most dramatic at 100 anchors, where Anchor Points degrades severely (e.g., ARC MAE jumps to 10.62%, MMLU to 7.89%) while SparseEval maintains sub-2% MAE, indicating that clustering without adaptive weight optimization is insufficient at scale. Against gp-IRT (Polo et al., 2024), SparseEval achieves 0.5–1.4% lower MAE and 0.05–0.17 higher Kendall's τ; gp-IRT consistently shows the weakest ranking correlation (τ as low as 0.533 on Winogrande at 20 anchors). Against TailoredBench (Yuan et al., 2025), the closest competitor, SparseEval still leads by 0.4–1.2% MAE and 0.02–0.17 τ, with the advantage widening as anchor count decreases. The paper also reports that SparseEval's training time is substantially lower than gp-IRT's (which takes up to 16 minutes on MMLU for IRT fitting), and that SparseEval generalizes better to unseen model families (DeepSeek), achieving 2.09% average MAE vs. 2.66% for gp-IRT and 4.22% for TailoredBench.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a generalization of prior efficient-evaluation methods (TinyBenchmarks/gp-IRT and TailoredBench) by showing that their linear aggregation functions are special cases of a broader sparse optimization formulation where the aggregation function f can be non-linear. The key methodological departure is replacing IRT-based or scaling-factor-based weight assignment with end-to-end gradient descent through an MLP, and introducing an iterative anchor refinement loop driven by Anchor Importance Scores (gradient-based) and Candidate Importance Scores (residual-correlation-based). The spectral clustering analysis of the model-item matrix to justify sparsity is a new diagnostic, though the underlying observation of item redundancy echoes earlier UMAP/t-SNE visualizations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SparseEval achieves sub-1% MAE with only 100 anchors—roughly a 5× reduction over baselines needing 500 anchors—making large-scale LLM benchmarking substantially cheaper while preserving ranking fidelity (Kendall's τ > 0.90). A key limitation is the dependence on a large training pool (~5,000 models from Open-LLM Leaderboard) for the gradient-based optimization to converge well, which may not be available for new or proprietary benchmarks. The paper also identifies model-model sparsity as an untapped signal, suggesting future work could jointly exploit both item-item and model-model structure to further reduce evaluation cost or improve generalization to unseen model families.
