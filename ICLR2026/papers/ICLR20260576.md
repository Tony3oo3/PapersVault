## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of SAM's implicit bias primarily apply to losses with attainable finite minimizers (e.g., squared loss), leaving open the case of logistic loss where the infimum lies at infinity. Moreover, it was unclear how network depth affects SAM's implicit bias relative to gradient descent, and whether infinite-time limit-direction characterizations suffice to capture the full training dynamics.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes L-layer linear diagonal network parameters (w^(1),...,w^(L) ∈ R^d), a linearly separable binary classification dataset, a perturbation radius ρ > 0, and an initialization scale α. It produces the limit direction of the predictor β(t) = ⊙_ℓ w^(ℓ)(t), exact coordinate-wise trajectory formulas (e.g., exponential growth rates, finite-time blow-up times), and a quantitative characterization of sequential feature amplification via lower bounds on the ratio β_j(t)/β_d(t). The analysis operates on rescaled continuous-time flows that remove the loss-derivative factor, preserving spatial trajectories while reparameterizing time to accelerate the large-margin regime.

## Method

The paper analyzes continuous-time ℓ∞-SAM and ℓ₂-SAM flows on L-layer linear diagonal networks trained with logistic loss, using a rescaled flow formulation that factors out the loss derivative for tractability. For ℓ∞-SAM, the sign-based perturbation decouples coordinates, yielding an exact characterization: each coordinate's trajectory is determined solely by whether its initialization α_j lies below, at, or above the threshold ρ, with the limit direction depending on the argmax of μ_j(α_j − ρ)^(L−2). For ℓ₂-SAM at depth 2, the paper proves the limit direction matches the ℓ₁ max-margin solution (Theorem 4.2) but identifies a finite-time phenomenon—sequential feature amplification—where the gradient normalization factor 1/n_θ(t) in the perturbation applies a coordinate-wise scaling (1 − ρμ_j/n_θ(t)) that suppresses major features (large μ_j) while amplifying minor ones when n_θ(t) is small. The paper then derives the instantaneous growth rate r_j(t) = 2μ_j(1 − ρμ_j/n_θ(t)), shows it is concave quadratic in μ_j with maximum at m_c(t) = n_θ(t)/(2ρ), and proves three regimes of m_c(t) dynamics that explain the time-wise and initialization-wise sequential amplification, with quantitative lower bounds on the amplification ratio β_j(t)/β_d(t) exceeding 10× for minor coordinates.

## Results

For depth-1 linear models, both ℓ∞-SAM and ℓ₂-SAM recover the ℓ₂ max-margin classifier, matching GD exactly for any perturbation radius ρ (Theorems 3.1, 4.1). For depth-2 ℓ∞-SAM, the limit direction is critically initialization-dependent: coordinates with α_j < ρ converge to 0 (even L) or ρ^L (odd L), while the dominant direction is argmax_j μ_j(α_j−ρ)^(L−2), which can select a minor feature (Theorem 3.2, Corollary 3.5). For depth-2 ℓ₂-SAM, the asymptotic limit is the ℓ₁ max-margin direction (Theorem 4.2), but finite-time dynamics exhibit sequential feature amplification where the lower bound on the minor-to-major amplification ratio LB_j(α) often exceeds 10 (Theorem 4.5). Grad-CAM visualizations on MNIST confirm that ℓ₂-SAM emphasizes weaker background pixels relative to GD, qualitatively matching the theoretical minor-feature amplification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ℓ∞-SAM and ℓ₂-SAM against gradient descent/flow (GD/GF) on L-layer linear diagonal networks trained with logistic loss. At depth 1, SAM matches GD exactly—both converge to the ℓ₂ max-margin direction for any ρ and any initialization, so SAM introduces no new bias in the linear case. At depth 2, ℓ∞-SAM diverges sharply from GD: while GD always aligns with the major feature e_d regardless of initialization, ℓ∞-SAM can converge to 0, align with the minor feature e_1, or align with e_d depending on whether α_j falls below, at, or above the threshold ρ (Theorem 3.2). For ℓ₂-SAM at depth 2, the asymptotic limit matches GD's ℓ₁ max-margin direction (Theorem 4.2), but the finite-time trajectory differs qualitatively: ℓ₂-SAM exhibits sequential feature amplification (minor-to-major transition over time and initialization scale) while GD selects the major feature from the outset. On MNIST, Grad-CAM visualizations show ℓ₂-SAM highlights background regions that GD ignores, providing empirical evidence that the theoretical minor-feature amplification manifests in practical CNN training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the implicit bias analysis of SAM from the MSE-loss setting (Andriushchenko & Flammarion, 2022; Behdin & Mazumder, 2023) to logistic loss, where the infimum lies at infinity and no finite minimizer exists. It builds on the well-established GD implicit bias results for linear models (ℓ₂ max-margin, Soudry et al., 2018) and diagonal linear networks (ℓ₁ max-margin, Gunasekar et al., 2018b), using them as baselines to isolate how SAM's perturbation mechanism alters the bias. The paper explicitly distinguishes its sequential feature amplification phenomenon from saddle-to-saddle dynamics (Pesme & Flammarion, 2023; Berthier, 2023), arguing that the mechanisms—continuous amplification of already-active coordinates versus discrete coordinate activation from zero—are fundamentally different.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that a single additional layer (L=1→2) causes SAM's implicit bias to diverge from GD's, and that ℓ₂-SAM's finite-time dynamics (sequential feature amplification) differ qualitatively from its asymptotic limit—challenges the common practice of characterizing implicit bias solely through t→∞ limits. This provides a mechanistic explanation for the empirical observation that SAM captures atypical subpatterns more effectively than GD (Kim et al., 2026), suggesting SAM's generalization advantage may partly arise from its transient emphasis on weaker features. Limitations include the restriction to linear diagonal networks and the use of uniform initialization for the main theoretical results, leaving open whether sequential feature amplification persists in heterogeneous or non-diagonal architectures. Future work could investigate whether the ℓ₂-SAM normalization factor's coordinate-wise suppression of major features generalizes to convolutional or attention-based models.
