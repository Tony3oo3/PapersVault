## Problem

Existing semantic-level watermarking (SWM) methods for LLMs suffer from two fundamental weaknesses: rejection-sampling-based generation introduces significant distribution distortions relative to unwatermarked outputs, and the sparse per-sentence watermark evidence makes them highly vulnerable to paraphrasing and word-level attacks. No prior SWM method provides a rigorous theoretical guarantee of distortion-freeness or a principled mechanism for densifying watermark evidence.

## Input / Output

At generation time, PMARK consumes a text prompt, an LLM backbone (OPT-1.3B or Mistral-7B), a sentence embedding encoder (all-mpnet-base-v2), a sample budget N (default 64), b orthogonal pivot vectors (default b=4, generated via QR decomposition of a Gaussian matrix), and pre-defined random seeds r_{(t,j)}. It outputs a sequence of T watermarked sentences. At detection time, the online variant additionally requires resampling N candidates from the same context to reconstruct medians, while the offline variant operates without querying the generator by using a fixed prior median of zero. The final output is a binary True/False verdict via a soft z-test statistic.

## Method

PMARK introduces a proxy function (PF) framework that maps each sentence to a scalar via cosine similarity with a pivot vector, then partitions N i.i.d. candidate sentences at the PF median to select a half-set as the watermark-valid region. The key distortion-free guarantee (Theorem 3) follows from the fact that averaging over the random seed k∈{0,1} makes each candidate equally likely to be selected regardless of which half it falls in. To combat the sparsity of single-channel evidence, PMARK applies b orthogonal pivot vectors sequentially (multi-channel constraints), halving the candidate set at each channel so that the final valid set has size N/2^b, thereby packing b bits of watermark evidence per sentence. The online version estimates the median dynamically via the Harrell-Davis estimator and detects using a soft z-test with exponential smoothing (parameters K, δ) to tolerate median estimation noise; the offline version exploits the concentration-of-measure property in high dimensions (PF values concentrate near zero) to fix the median at zero, selecting the candidate that maximizes the total watermark evidence score across all b channels, eliminating the need for resampling at detection time.

## Results

On Mistral-7B with GPT-3.5-turbo paraphrase attack (the most challenging setting), PMARK Online achieves TP@FP1% of 96.8% on BOOKSUM and 95.2% on C4, substantially outperforming the best semantic-level baseline SemStamp (76.0% and 69.2%) and the best token-level baseline SIR (41.2% and 63.4%). Under no attack, PMARK Online reaches near-perfect detection (100.0% TP@FP1%) while maintaining the lowest perplexity (PPL 4.37–4.71) among all watermarked methods. The offline variant, which eliminates dynamic median estimation, retains TP@FP1% above 90.8% under GPT paraphrase while reducing token consumption to 239.7 per sentence (vs. 1694.4 for SemStamp).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PMARK Online decisively outperforms all nine baselines under GPT-3.5-turbo paraphrase attack across both backbones and datasets. Against the strongest token-level baseline UPV (73.6% TP@FP1% on OPT/BOOKSUM), PMARK Online achieves 95.4%, a 21.8-point gain; against the best semantic-level baseline SemStamp (79.6%), the improvement is 15.8 points. Token-level methods collapse dramatically under paraphrase: KGW drops to 0.6% and SynthID to 1.8% on OPT/BOOKSUM, while EXP and MorphMark remain below 5%. Among semantic-level baselines, k-SemStamp (62.1%) and SimMark (22.0%) are substantially weaker than SemStamp, with SimMark performing poorly even under no attack (88.2% TP@FP1%). The offline PMARK variant, which trades a small robustness cost (94.2% vs. 95.4% on OPT/BOOKSUM GPT) for eliminating dynamic median estimation, still exceeds SemStamp by 14.6 points while consuming only 239.7 tokens per sentence versus SemStamp's 1694.4.

## Relation to Prior Work

PMARK builds directly on the semantic-level watermarking (SWM) paradigm initiated by SemStamp and extended by k-SemStamp and SimMark, which treat sentences as the fundamental watermarking unit and use rejection sampling over embedding-space partitions. The key departure is the introduction of a unified proxy-function framework that subsumes all existing SWM methods, revealing that their distortion arises from non-uniform mass over the proxy range. PMARK replaces the split-and-reject mechanism with a median-based partitioning that is provably distortion-free, and introduces multi-channel constraints (b orthogonal pivot vectors) to densify watermark evidence—a dimension of the design space that prior SWMs had not exploited.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first SWM method with a formal distortion-free guarantee and the first to identify sparse watermark evidence as the root cause of SWM vulnerability to adversarial attacks, offering a principled fix via multi-channel constraints. The offline variant's 20% token consumption relative to prior SOTA SWMs makes semantic-level watermarking practically deployable. Open questions remain: the method uses fixed (non-dynamic) random seeds, limiting n-shot undetectability; it handles only single-bit watermarks; and the concentration-of-measure assumption (median ≈ 0) that enables the offline version has only a loose theoretical bound rather than a tight guarantee. Whether the multi-channel framework extends cleanly to multi-bit or cross-sentence settings is left for future work.
