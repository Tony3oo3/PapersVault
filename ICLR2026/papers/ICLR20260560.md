## Problem

Abductive reasoning over knowledge graphs generates plausible logical hypotheses from observed entities, but lacks controllability: a single observation on large-scale graphs yields dozens of redundant or irrelevant hypotheses, and prior work (AbductiveKGR) offers no mechanism to steer generation toward user-specified semantic aspects or structural complexity levels. Two technical barriers prevent straightforward controllability for long logical hypotheses: hypothesis space collapse (the number of valid candidates drops sharply with increasing logical length) and reward oversensitivity (Jaccard-based rewards fluctuate violently on minor structural errors, destabilizing RL training).

## Input / Output

The model receives an observation (a set of up to 32 entity tokens from the knowledge graph) concatenated with a control condition token sequence, and autoregressively generates a first-order logic hypothesis in disjunctive normal form (existential quantifiers, AND, OR, NOT over entity-relation predicates). Control conditions come in two families: semantic (a specific entity token or relation token that must appear in the hypothesis) and structural (a Lisp-like logical pattern string, an [ne] token specifying exact entity count, or an [nr] token specifying exact relation count). The generated hypothesis is evaluated by executing it on the knowledge graph to obtain its conclusion set, which should closely match the observation set.

## Method

CtrlHGen trains a 12-layer decoder-only Transformer in two stages: supervised fine-tuning (first unconditional, then conditional on control tokens) followed by reinforcement learning with GRPO. To combat hypothesis space collapse, the paper introduces sub-logic decomposition augmentation: complex hypotheses are recursively broken into sub-hypotheses matching simpler logical patterns, and their conclusions on the graph become additional training pairs, letting the model learn complex structures from simpler semantically-aligned components. To address reward oversensitivity, the semantic reward blends Jaccard, Dice, and Overlap coefficients (weighted 1.0/0.5/0.5) to smooth minor discrepancies, and a binary condition-adherence reward (1 if the hypothesis satisfies the control constraint, 0 otherwise) is added with a balancing hyperparameter α=0.5. GRPO optimizes over groups of 4 sampled hypotheses per observation with intra-group reward normalization and a KL penalty against a reference policy.

## Results

On FB15k-237, CtrlHGen achieves an average Jaccard of 64.3, Dice of 71.9, Overlap of 83.0, condition-adherence accuracy of 96.6%, and Smatch of 73.3 across five control conditions. On WN18RR, the best Jaccard reaches 77.0 (pattern condition) with 93.5% adherence accuracy, and on DBpedia50 it reaches 75.2 (specific-entity condition) with 84.0% accuracy. These results substantially outperform all LLM baselines, with the strongest LLM (GPT-5 Thinking) achieving only 18.7 Jaccard and 32.9 Smatch on FB15k-237.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CtrlHGen dramatically outperforms all LLM baselines on FB15k-237: it achieves 64.3 Jaccard versus 18.7 for GPT-5 Thinking (3.4×), 2.4 for GPT-4o (27×), and 5.3 for DeepSeek-V3+RAG (12×), while also leading on Smatch (73.3 vs. 32.9 for GPT-5). Against the prior abductive reasoning method AbductiveKGR (uncondition baseline in Table 1), CtrlHGen improves Jaccard by 3–11 points depending on dataset and condition (e.g., 65.5 vs. 61.4 on FB15k-237, 77.0 vs. 72.6 on WN18RR, 73.8 vs. 64.3 on DBpedia50 under pattern condition), while simultaneously achieving 80–99% condition-adherence accuracy. In the appendix comparison, CtrlHGen also surpasses Logic-Gen's data augmentation strategy on both semantic similarity (70.1 vs. 69.5 Jaccard) and condition adherence (73.1% vs. 65.9% accuracy) under the pattern condition on DBpedia50. The paper attributes LLMs' poor performance to their inability to generate correct structured query graphs and to conflicts between parametric knowledge and the target knowledge graph.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends AbductiveKGR (Bai et al., 2024b), which first formulated abductive reasoning in knowledge graphs as logical hypothesis generation trained via supervised and reinforcement learning, by introducing controllability over semantic content and structural complexity. The training paradigm follows the same two-stage SFT + RL pipeline, but replaces the single Jaccard reward with a smoothed multi-metric semantic reward (Jaccard + Dice + Overlap) and adds a binary condition-adherence reward, while using GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for group-level policy optimization. The sub-logic decomposition augmentation strategy is conceptually related to Logic-Gen (Asai & Hajishirzi, 2020) but is specifically designed to combat hypothesis space collapse in complex first-order logic patterns rather than general logical consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The controllability framing addresses a genuine practical bottleneck: in large KGs, a single observation can yield dozens of plausible but irrelevant hypotheses, making uncontrolled abductive reasoning of limited utility in domain-specific settings like clinical diagnosis. The stark performance gap between CtrlHGen (~64 Jaccard) and even GPT5-Thinking (~19 Jaccard) on FB15k-237 underscores that structured logical hypothesis generation remains far beyond current LLM capabilities, suggesting that dedicated KG-aware architectures will remain necessary for this task. Open questions include whether the 13 predefined logical patterns can be generalized to open-ended hypothesis spaces, whether control conditions can be expressed in natural language rather than discrete tokens, and how the approach scales to KGs with millions of entities where hypothesis space collapse becomes even more severe.
