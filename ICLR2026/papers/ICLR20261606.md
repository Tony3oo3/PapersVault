## Problem

Domain-specific supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of LLMs is widely believed to inevitably degrade general-purpose capabilities, yet prior evidence for severe degradation was largely collected under relatively large learning rates. This paper challenges that assumption by showing that smaller learning rates substantially mitigate general-performance loss while preserving comparable domain gains, and further asks whether additional strategies can tighten the trade-off beyond simply reducing the step size.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM (Qwen2.5-3B/7B, Qwen3-4B/8B, Gemma3-1B/4B, or DeepSeek-Coder-7B) and a domain-specific SFT dataset (MedCalc for medical calculations, ESCI for e-commerce product classification, or MetaMathQA for math reasoning) and produces a fine-tuned model evaluated on both the target domain task and general-purpose benchmarks (GSM8K, HumanEval, IFEval, MMLU, etc.). During training, the model computes per-token predicted probabilities over the target sequence at every optimization step; these probabilities are converted into adaptive reweighting factors that scale the cross-entropy loss before backpropagation, with a stop-gradient applied to the weights themselves.

## Method

The paper's central empirical finding is that a smaller learning rate (e.g., 1e-6 vs. 5e-6 or 2e-5) yields a markedly more favorable Pareto trade-off between domain and general performance, a phenomenon explained through an information-theoretic lens that models the LLM as an arithmetic-code compressor over a token tree and approximates fine-tuning dynamics via exponential tilting. Building on the theoretical insight that the dominant source of general-capability degradation is the update magnitude on a small set of low-probability (hard) tokens, the authors propose Token-Adaptive Loss Reweighting (TALR), which solves a constrained optimization over the probability simplex—minimizing weighted token loss plus negative-entropy regularization—to obtain closed-form per-token weights w_i ∝ p_θ(x_i)^(1/τ). The temperature τ is set dynamically as the median average sequence loss within each batch, and a weight floor of 0.01 prevents hard tokens from being entirely ignored. Theoretical analysis (Theorems 3.1–3.2) shows that smaller per-step sizes tighten the upper bound on general degradation, and that the safe step-size range scales as 1/√s where s is the expected number of hard tokens per example, explaining why label-only supervision tolerates larger learning rates than CoT-augmented training.

## Results

On the MedCalc benchmark at learning rate 5e-6, TALR achieves 0.5417 domain accuracy and 0.5018 general performance (average of IFEval, GSM8K, HumanEval), substantially outperforming standard SFT (0.5583/0.3805) in general capability while maintaining comparable domain performance. At the smaller learning rate of 1e-6, both standard SFT (0.5339/0.6924) and TALR (0.5011/0.7169) achieve favorable trade-offs, with TALR providing a modest general-performance boost. Across all three model families (Qwen2.5-3B, Qwen3-4B, Gemma3-4B), TALR consistently delivers the best Pareto-optimal balance between domain and general performance at the larger learning rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against L2 regularization, TALR achieves a substantially higher general score (0.5018 vs. 0.3952) at lr=5e-6 while sacrificing only 1.4 points in domain accuracy (0.5417 vs. 0.5553), making it the clear winner on the overall trade-off. Against LoRA, TALR dominates on domain performance (0.5417 vs. 0.2655) and also edges out LoRA on general performance (0.5018 vs. 0.4827), since LoRA's low-rank constraint severely limits domain adaptation. Against Wise-FT (model averaging), TALR wins decisively on domain accuracy (0.5417 vs. 0.4012) but concedes ground on general performance (0.5018 vs. 0.6443), reflecting Wise-FT's stronger preservation of the base model at the cost of domain learning. Against FLOW, TALR wins on general performance (0.5018 vs. 0.4498) while matching domain accuracy (0.5417 vs. 0.5528), confirming that token-level dynamic reweighting outperforms sequence-level static reweighting. At the smaller learning rate of 1e-6, the gaps narrow considerably, with standard SFT and TALR both achieving strong trade-offs, though TALR still provides a modest general-performance lift (0.7169 vs. 0.6924 average).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the data-oblivious continual learning literature for LLMs, where prior work (Sanyal et al. 2025; Huan et al. 2025; Lin et al. 2025a) has documented that domain-specific SFT degrades general capabilities and proposed mitigations like L2 regularization, LoRA, model averaging (Wise-FT), and sequence-level loss reweighting (FLOW). The key conceptual departure is the claim that much of the reported degradation is an artifact of using relatively large learning rates (5e-6 to 2e-5), and that smaller rates (1e-6) largely eliminate the trade-off while preserving domain gains. The theoretical analysis draws on the language-modeling-as-compression framework (Deletang et al. 2024; Ji et al. 2025) and exponential tilting (Maity et al. 2023) to formalize why smaller per-step updates tighten the bound on general-performance loss, and motivates TALR as a token-level reweighting that departs from FLOW's static, sequence-level approach by dynamically adapting weights at every training step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical guideline—use a smaller learning rate for domain-specific SFT—is simple and immediately actionable, and the token-level analysis (most tokens are easy; hard tokens are sparse and curriculum-like) provides useful intuition for practitioners. However, the paper is candid that no method, including TALR, fully eliminates the trade-off, and the experiments are limited to a handful of model families (Qwen, Gemma, DeepSeek-Coder) and three domain datasets, leaving open whether findings generalize to larger models, MoE architectures, or more diverse domains. The theoretical analysis, while illuminating, relies on exponential tilting as an analytical surrogate rather than a tight characterization of actual SGD dynamics, and the optimal learning rate selection problem remains unsolved. Broader implications for RLVR warm-up (preserving exploration diversity) are noted but not empirically validated.
