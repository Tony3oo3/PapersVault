## Problem

Expressive policies such as diffusion and flow-matching models, widely used in imitation learning for robotics, are difficult to fine-tune with online RL because their long denoising chains cause unstable gradient propagation when optimizing actions against a value function. Existing online RL algorithms are designed for simple Gaussian policies and do not effectively leverage the capacity of expressive pre-trained policies, leaving a gap in how to perform stable, sample-efficient value maximization for these policy classes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a state observation, an offline demonstration dataset (e.g., successful robot trajectories), and a pre-trained expressive policy (instantiated as a DDPM diffusion policy with 10 denoising steps). At each step, the on-the-fly policy samples N=8 action candidates from the base policy, applies Gaussian edits to each, and selects the single highest-Q-value action among all 2N candidates for both environment interaction and TD backup. The output is a continuous control action vector and, after training, a fine-tuned expressive base policy plus a lightweight edit policy.

## Method

EXPO avoids direct value optimization of the expressive policy by decomposing the problem into two parameterized components: a large base diffusion policy trained with a stable imitation learning (denoising) objective, and a small Gaussian edit policy trained with an entropy-regularized policy loss to shift base actions toward higher Q-values within a bounded edit radius β. An on-the-fly (OTF) policy is constructed at inference time by sampling N actions from the base policy, applying the edit to each, and selecting the action with the highest Q-value among all base and edited candidates. Crucially, this OTF policy is used for both environment sampling and the TD backup target, making the Q-function update equivalent to standard Q-learning with the implicit policy rather than a slower SARSA-like objective. An optional entropy backup variant replaces the hard max with a softmax over Q-values to encourage broader exploration when the offline dataset is small or narrow.

## Results

EXPO achieves up to 2-3x improvement in sample efficiency on average over prior methods across 12 sparse-reward continuous control tasks spanning Antmaze, Adroit, Robomimic, and MimicGen domains. In the online RL setting (no pretraining), EXPO consistently outperforms all baselines in sample efficiency on nearly every task. In the offline-to-online setting, EXPO achieves significantly better asymptotic performance and, critically, does not suffer a performance drop from offline pretraining to online fine-tuning, unlike most baselines. On the fine-grained manipulation tasks (Robomimic, MimicGen), EXPO consistently improves over the pretrained policy while all other baselines (except RLPD) experience a performance drop going from offline to online.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EXPO outperforms RLPD (Li et al., 2023) in sample efficiency on almost every task in both online and offline-to-online settings, with the sole exception being relocate-binary-v0 where the dataset is too narrow for imitation learning to extract useful behavior. Against IDQL (Hansen-Estruch et al., 2023), which also uses a diffusion policy with imitation learning and on-the-fly action selection, EXPO performs substantially better because it uses the on-the-fly value-maximizing action in the TD backup (Q-learning) rather than only for sampling (SARSA-like), and additionally edits actions toward higher Q-values. Cal-QL (Nakamoto et al., 2023) achieves strong performance on easier Antmaze tasks but has much lower sample efficiency on harder tasks and cannot effectively leverage offline dataset signals for policy improvement. DAC (Fang et al., 2024) obtains strong offline pretraining performance by leveraging diffusion expressivity but collapses quickly during online fine-tuning, making it infeasible for the offline-to-online setting. DIPO (Yang et al., 2023) and QSM (Psenka et al., 2023), both online-only diffusion-based methods, often fail to learn effectively on the challenging continuous control tasks, likely due to instabilities in backpropagating value gradients through the denoising chain.

## Relation to Prior Work

EXPO sits at the intersection of offline-to-online RL (RLPD, Cal-QL, IDQL) and expressive policy methods in RL (diffusion policies via DAC, QSM, DIPO; flow-matching via Flow Q-Learning). Its key departure is from prior work that attempts to propagate Q-value gradients through the expressive policy's denoising chain (QSM, DAC, DIPO) or that uses weighted behavior cloning for policy extraction. Instead, EXPO decouples value maximization from the expressive policy entirely: the base policy is trained only with a stable imitation learning objective, while a lightweight Gaussian edit policy handles Q-value optimization locally, and an on-the-fly selection step picks the best action among base and edited candidates for both sampling and TD backup. The edit policy concept relates to residual policies (Ankile et al., Yuan et al.) but is specifically constrained by an edit distance to solve a simpler local optimization, and the on-the-fly TD backup extends sampling-based maximization (EMaQ, IDQL) from exploration-only to the full Q-learning target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper addresses a practical bottleneck in robotics: expressive policies (diffusion, flow-matching) are the standard for imitation learning but have been difficult to fine-tune with online RL due to unstable gradient propagation through long denoising chains. The conceptual insight that value maximization can be fully delegated to a small auxiliary policy plus a non-parametric selection step, while the expressive policy remains on a stable supervised objective, is a clean architectural principle that generalizes beyond any specific expressive policy class. Limitations include the computational cost of sampling N actions per TD backup step and the assumption of a reasonable prior (offline data or pre-trained policy). Open questions include improving computational efficiency of the on-the-fly extraction and extending the framework to settings without a useful prior.
