## Problem

Existing Unlearnable Examples (UEs) methods inject perturbations to mislead models into learning spurious shortcuts, but they were designed and evaluated only against randomly initialized classifiers. This paper reveals that when UEs are applied to pretrained backbones, the rich semantic representations encoded in pretraining priors enable models to bypass the injected shortcuts and recover genuine data-label relationships, effectively nullifying unlearnability. This gap is critical because pretrained models underpin most practical deep learning applications.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100 and SVHN, 224×224 for Flowers102 and ImageNet subsets) along with their class labels and a pretrained surrogate model (e.g., ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18) as input. It produces class-wise perturbations bounded by an L∞ norm of 8/255, generated by an encoder-decoder network, which are added to images to form unlearnable examples. Unusually, each perturbation is optimized to steer the model toward a designated *incorrect* target label rather than the ground-truth label, and the three-stage curriculum progressively shifts target selection from hard negatives to semantically dissimilar classes.

## Method

BAIT (Binding Artificial perturbations to Incorrect Targets) formulates a bi-level optimization where the inner level trains a pretrained surrogate to align perturbed samples with their true labels (mimicking standard prior-driven semantic learning), while the outer level updates perturbations to map those same samples onto designated incorrect target labels, thereby overriding the semantic-label pathway that priors provide. The bi-level objective is made tractable via MAML-style meta-learning: the inner optimization is unrolled for N steps, and gradients through the unrolled surrogate drive perturbation updates. A curriculum-guided target label selection strategy progresses through three stages—hard negative classes, random classes, and most dissimilar classes—to gradually increase the difficulty of the mislabel-perturbation binding. The perturbation generator is a standard encoder-decoder (6 down-sampling conv layers, 8 residual blocks, 6 transposed conv layers) that produces class-wise perturbations by averaging per-sample outputs, ensuring visual imperceptibility while enforcing unlearnability against pretrained backbones.

## Results

BAIT drives test accuracy close to chance level (10% for CIFAR-10, 1% for CIFAR-100, 10% for SVHN) against ImageNet-pretrained backbones, substantially outperforming all baselines. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, BAIT achieves 14.40% test accuracy versus 61.82% (EMN), 82.72% (TUE), and 23.17% (GUE, the strongest baseline). On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18, BAIT reaches 20.77% compared to 55.53% (EMN) and 36.98% (LSP). The method also generalizes to Flowers102 (24.91% mIoU vs. 43.91–47.76% for baselines) and a 100-class ImageNet subset (24.22% vs. 37.72–59.44%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BAIT outperforms all six classification baselines (EMN, TUE, REM, LSP, GUE, 14A) across every dataset–backbone combination in Table 1, with the largest margins against TUE (e.g., 14.40% vs. 82.72% on CIFAR-10/ResNet-18) and REM (14.37% vs. 76.45% on SVHN/ResNet-18). The closest competitor is GUE, which reaches 23.17% on CIFAR-10/ResNet-18, still 8.77 points above BAIT. Even 14A, which leverages a pretrained CLIP backbone for perturbation generation, is consistently surpassed (e.g., 14.40% vs. 65.70% on CIFAR-10/ResNet-18). In the cross-task transferability evaluation on Pascal VOC 2012 segmentation (Table 12), BAIT reduces mIoU for five classes compared to clean training and outperforms LSP, but remains below the segmentation-specialist UnSeg (e.g., 40.0% vs. 30.5% mIoU on Aeroplane), acknowledging a gap for task-specific methods. Re-implemented baselines with pretrained surrogates (EMN*, TUE*, REM*) in Table 2 confirm that even granting baselines access to ImageNet priors during optimization does not close the gap (e.g., 14.40% vs. 56.08% for REM* on CIFAR-10).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Unlearnable Examples (UEs) line of work initiated by EMN (Huang et al., 2021) and advanced by TUE, REM, LSP, GUE, and 14A, all of which target randomly initialized classifiers. The key departure is identifying that pretrained backbones fundamentally undermine UE effectiveness, a setting no prior UE study has systematically addressed. Methodologically, BAIT draws on meta-learning (MAML-style bi-level optimization) and curriculum learning to construct perturbations that bind to incorrect labels, repurposing these tools from fast adaptation and staged training into a data-protection context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since transfer learning from pretrained backbones is the dominant paradigm in modern CV, the finding that standard UEs fail in this setting significantly narrows the practical applicability of the entire UE literature as a data-protection mechanism. BAIT provides a concrete countermeasure, but its cross-task transferability (e.g., to segmentation) remains limited, and the method requires a surrogate model for perturbation optimization, adding practical overhead. Open questions include whether the vulnerability generalizes to other modalities (text, audio, 3D) and whether stronger priors (e.g., foundation models like CLIP or DINOv2) would require even more aggressive mislabel-perturbation bindings.
