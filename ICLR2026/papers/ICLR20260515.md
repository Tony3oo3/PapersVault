## Problem

Existing map VQA benchmarks treat maps as a special case of charts, covering only a subset of spatial relations, restricting map style diversity, and rarely requiring multi-map or within-document retrieval. This leaves it unclear whether LVLMs possess the full range of cartographic reasoning skills—topological, metric, and directional inference combined with legend/scale/compass interpretation and cross-map alignment—that human map readers routinely perform.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260515/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each question is paired with one or more real map images extracted from public documents (210 documents, 17,030 images, 32 countries, 6 thematic domains) and a free-form question in English. In the contextual setting, the model additionally receives 2–9 irrelevant maps from the same document (averaging 9.5 total images, with a 1:5.71 relevant-to-irrelevant ratio) and must first identify the correct map(s). Outputs are free-form: short text spans or categorical labels, numeric distance values with units, or cardinal direction labels—never multiple-choice.

## Method

FRIEDA is a 500-question benchmark where every question requires multi-step reasoning over at least one of six spatial relations (border, equal, intersect, within, distance, orientation) grounded in the 9-intersection model, and 59.6% require integrating evidence across two or more maps. Questions are generated by GPT-4/GPT-o3, manually curated by GIS-experienced annotators, and validated by 11 PhD researchers using a 2/3 majority-agreement threshold. Evaluation uses a unified task-aware protocol: LLM-as-Judge (Mistral Small 3.1) for textual answers, unit-aware parsing with MAPE and a 20% error threshold for distances, and one-adjacent-label angular tolerance for cardinal directions. Eleven LVLMs (Gemini-2.5-Pro, GPT-5-Think, Claude-Sonnet-4, and eight open-source models) are evaluated under both direct and contextual settings, revealing that even the strongest model reaches only 38.20% accuracy versus 84.87% for humans, with the largest error sources being legend misinterpretation (25.61%) and cross-map alignment failures (23.78%).

## Results

On FRIEDA-direct (500 questions), the best proprietary model Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves only 38.20% overall accuracy, far below the human upper bound of 84.87%. The best open-source model, Ovis2.5-9B-Think, reaches 25.80%, while most other open-source models fall below 20%. Performance is most degraded on distance questions (25.27% for Gemini-2.5-Pro) and least degraded on orientation questions (71.59%), and the contextual setting (where models must first identify relevant maps among ~9.5 images) yields nearly identical accuracy, indicating that the core bottleneck is cartographic reasoning rather than map retrieval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All 11 LVLMs fall substantially short of the 84.87% human baseline, with the best proprietary model (Gemini-2.5-Pro, 38.20%) trailing humans by ~47 points. Among proprietary models, Gemini-2.5-Pro and GPT-5-Think are nearly tied overall (38.20% vs. 37.20%), but GPT-5-Think leads on multi-map questions (46.31% vs. 41.95%) and the Equal relation (44.44% vs. 33.33%), while Claude-Sonnet-4 is strongest on distance queries (23.08% vs. 25.27% for Gemini) and the Border relation (33.80%). Among open-source models, Ovis2.5-9B-Think (25.80%) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (25.60%) are the top performers, yet both remain more than 12 points below the best proprietary model. Notably, model size does not predict performance: the 9B Ovis2.5-9B-Think outperforms the 110B LLaVA-NeXT (8.60%) and 108B GLM-4.5V (6.40%) by a wide margin. The largest single-model gap appears on the Orientation relation, where Gemini-2.5-Pro reaches 71.59% while LLaVA-NeXT-110B scores 0.00%, and on Distance, where even the best model (GPT-5-Think at 27.47%) is far below the human 78.28%.

## Relation to Prior Work

FRIEDA extends the map VQA line of work (MapQA, MapWise, MapIQ, MapEval, PEACE) by deliberately generalizing along multiple axes simultaneously: it covers all three GIS spatial-relation categories (topological, metric, directional) rather than a subset, requires multi-step reasoning over heterogeneous real-world maps from diverse domains and geographies rather than stylized or single-domain maps, and introduces a multi-map cross-image setting plus a contextual retrieval setting that prior benchmarks largely omit. It also departs from chart/infographic VQA (ChartQA, InfographicsVQA) by arguing that maps demand interpretation of layered symbology (legends, scales, compasses) and spatial relations tied to orientation and distance that chart-style evaluations do not capture. The multi-image reasoning benchmarks (ReMI, MMIE) inform the cross-map design but lack cartographic focus, while spatial-reasoning benchmarks on natural images (SpatialVLM, SpatialRGPT) do not engage with the symbolic conventions unique to maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FRIEDA exposes a substantial and well-characterized gap: even the strongest proprietary models (Gemini-2.5-Pro at 38.2%, GPT-5-Think at 37.2%) trail human performance (84.87%) by roughly 46 points, and the error analysis pinpoints specific cartographic failure modes (legend misinterpretation, cross-map alignment, spatial-relation confusion) that are distinct from generic VQA errors. The finding that the contextual (retrieval) setting yields nearly identical accuracy to the direct setting indicates that the bottleneck is cartographic reasoning itself, not map identification, which redirects future work toward architectures and training strategies that incorporate cartographic priors. Open questions include whether explicit reasoning (chain-of-thought) can close the gap, how to represent map symbology in a way LVLMs can ground reliably, and whether the lack of a clear size-performance relationship (a 9B model outperforming 100B+ models) reflects a data or objective mismatch rather than a capacity limit.
