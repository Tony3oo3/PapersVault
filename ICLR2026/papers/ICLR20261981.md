## Problem

Diffusion-based robot manipulation policies achieve strong single-task performance but struggle to generalize to multi-task settings without scaling model size and demonstration data to impractical levels. Existing mixture-of-experts formulations for diffusion policies do not explicitly disentangle reusable manipulation skills, leading to entangled experts, unstable routing, and poor interpretability. The paper asks: can one learn a compact, state-adaptive set of orthogonal skill primitives with stable, phase-consistent routing that supports multi-task generalization and efficient real-time inference?

## Input / Output

The policy consumes RGB images (encoded via ResNet-18), proprioceptive robot state, and a discrete task identifier κ, with an observation horizon of 3 steps. It outputs a sequence of 8 action steps (planning horizon) for a dual-arm robot, where each action is synthesized as a_t = B(s_t)(g_t ⊙ z_t): a state-dependent orthonormal basis B(s_t) ∈ R^{d×K} (K=8) multiplied by elementwise-gated diffusion coefficients. At inference, only a sparse subset of experts (typically ~2.3 of 8) is activated per step via a coverage threshold τ_m=0.95, yielding ~107 ms end-to-end latency on an A6000 GPU.

## Method

SMP constructs a state-adaptive orthonormal skill basis B(s) by projecting a lightweight network output W(s) onto the Stiefel manifold via differentiable thin-QR retraction with sign stabilization, ensuring each of K=8 experts occupies a distinct, non-overlapping direction in action space. Gates g_t ∈ Δ^{K-1} follow sticky Dirichlet-Markov dynamics (g_t ~ Dir(κ·g_{t-1} + α₀·ϑ)) that enforce temporal persistence and phase-consistent activation, while a global usage vector ϑ prevents expert collapse. Training uses a variational ELBO decomposed into four terms: coefficient-space diffusion (DDPM on z_t with stop-gradient targets), reconstruction in the whitened basis (gradient-carrying targets that update B), gate regularization (closed-form Dirichlet KLs), and router alignment (distilling a state-only router for deployment). At inference, adaptive expert activation selects the smallest set of experts whose squared gate masses cover ≥95% of total mass, denoising only those coefficients to reduce active parameters to ~80M (vs. 259M total) while preserving action quality.

## Results

On RoboTwin-2 (6 bimanual tasks), SMP achieves an average success rate of 0.54, outperforming the strongest baseline RDT (0.48) by 6 percentage points and the next-best Sparse DP (0.44) by 10 points. On RLBench-2 (4 tightly coordinated tasks), SMP reaches 0.18 average success, again leading all baselines (RDT 0.17, Sparse DP 0.16, ACT 0.15). In terms of efficiency, SMP activates only ~80M parameters at inference (vs. 1200M for RDT) and achieves 107 ms per control step, faster than all single-backbone diffusion baselines (DP 120 ms, RDT 183 ms) and the FFN-MoE baseline Sparse DP (148 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMP outperforms all six baselines on both RoboTwin-2 and RLBench-2 multi-task success rates. Against the strongest baseline RDT (1.2B-parameter diffusion foundation model), SMP gains +6 pts on RoboTwin-2 (0.54 vs 0.48) and +1 pt on RLBench-2 (0.18 vs 0.17), while using only 80M active parameters versus RDT's 1200M and running 76 ms faster per step (107 vs 183 ms). Compared to the closest MoE baseline Sparse DP, SMP improves RoboTwin-2 success by 10 pts (0.54 vs 0.44) and RLBench-2 by 2 pts (0.18 vs 0.16), with a 41 ms lower inference time (107 vs 148 ms) due to its state-adaptive sparse activation versus Sparse DP's layer-wise FFN gating. Against the skill-abstraction baseline Discrete Policy, SMP leads by 14 pts on RoboTwin-2 (0.54 vs 0.40) and 5 pts on RLBench-2 (0.18 vs 0.13). The simpler diffusion baselines DP and DP3 trail SMP by 25 and 21 points respectively on RoboTwin-2, and ACT lags by 20 points, confirming that naive scaling or temporal chunking alone is insufficient for multimodal bimanual action distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

SMP builds directly on Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023) for its generative backbone and observation encoder, but departs from it by replacing the single denoising network with a mixture of K per-skill diffusion experts operating in a state-adaptive orthonormal basis. It contrasts with Sparse Diffusion Policy (Wang et al., 2024), which inserts FFN-MoE blocks inside a shared diffusion backbone, by decomposing the entire policy into independent experts over orthogonal subspaces with a principled variational objective. The sticky Dirichlet-Markov gate prior and QR-retraction basis construction are novel contributions that distinguish SMP from both standard MoE routing and prior skill-discovery methods (DIAYN, Discrete Policy) that lack geometric disentanglement or phase stability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SMP offers a concrete recipe for multi-task bimanual manipulation that avoids the 10× parameter scaling of foundation models like RDT while matching or exceeding their success rates, with a simple coverage-threshold knob to trade accuracy against latency. The interpretability of the learned basis (experts aligning with left/right-arm roles and pick-move-place phases) and the clean transfer results (router-only fine-tuning for skill recomposition) suggest a practical path toward composable, reusable manipulation skills. Limitations include evaluation on relatively small backbones, bimanual-only settings, and limited real-robot trials (4 tasks, 50 demos each); open questions include whether the orthonormal-basis + sticky-routing framework scales to single-arm, mobile, or whole-body manipulation and how robust it is under sensing noise and domain shift.
