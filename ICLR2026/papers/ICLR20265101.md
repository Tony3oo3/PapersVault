## Problem

Applying reinforcement learning to diffusion-based LLMs (dLLMs) is hampered by the intractability of their likelihood functions, which forces existing methods like diffusion-based GRPO to approximate log-likelihoods for the current, old, and reference policies at every training step. These approximations exponentially amplify estimation error when computing policy ratios for importance sampling, introduce high variance, and impose significant computational overhead that worsens with sequence length and diffusion steps.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt q and produces a generated text completion o via iterative masked denoising (confidence-based remasking over L steps), evaluated at sequence lengths of 256 and 512 tokens. During RL training, a group of G completions is sampled per prompt, and group-relative advantages are computed from a reward function R(q,o) that is not restricted to binary verifiers. The wd1++ variant additionally consumes intermediate clean completions from each denoising step l ∈ {1,…,L} as extra training signals, expanding the group from G final completions to G×L total samples.

## Method

wd1 reformulates the RL objective as a ratio-free weighted log-likelihood by deriving the closed-form solution to a reverse-KL-regularized policy optimization problem and training the policy to match it via KL minimization. The resulting loss weights each completion's log-likelihood by w⁺ ∝ exp(ψÂ) (reinforcing high-advantage samples) plus a complementary penalty w⁻ ∝ exp(−ψÂ) (actively suppressing low-advantage samples), requiring only a single likelihood approximation for the current policy rather than three. This avoids the exponential error amplification inherent in policy-ratio-based methods like diffusion GRPO, reducing per-step FLOPs and NFEs. Theoretically, the paper proves the objective is equivalent to training an energy-guided discrete diffusion model (with energy = −advantage) combined with negative-sample unlearning. The extension wd1++ applies the same weighted objective stepwise across all intermediate denoising completions, achieving SOTA math performance (44.2% MATH500, 84.5% GSM8K) with only 20 RL steps and 10× fewer rollouts than baselines.

## Results

On reasoning benchmarks with LLaDA-8B-Instruct, wd1 (without SFT) achieves 76.4% on Sudoku and 51.2% on Countdown at 256-token length, surpassing d1 (17.6% and 25.8%) by +58.8% and +16% respectively. The extended method wd1++ achieves state-of-the-art 84.5% on GSM8K and 44.2% on MATH500 with only 20 RL training steps and 10× fewer rollouts than baseline methods. Training cost is also reduced: wd1 requires 81.16 sec/step vs. d1's 103.5 sec/step, and eliminates the ~2-hour SFT stage entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against d1 (Zhao et al., 2025), the primary baseline, wd1 delivers dramatic gains on planning tasks: 76.4% vs. 17.6% on Sudoku (+58.8%) and 51.2% vs. 25.8% on Countdown (+16%), while also slightly outperforming on GSM8K (82.3% vs. 82.0%) and MATH500 (39.0% vs. 38.0%) without requiring the SFT stage that d1 depends on. Against SDPO (Han et al., 2025), wd1++ wins on GSM8K (84.5% vs. 81.2%) though SDPO does not report MATH500. Against TCR (Wang et al., 2025d), wd1++ leads on both GSM8K (84.5% vs. 83.0%) and MATH500 (44.2% vs. 41.4%). Against MDPO (He et al., 2025), the strongest concurrent baseline, wd1++ achieves 84.5% vs. 83.4% on GSM8K and 44.2% vs. 43.4% on MATH500, while requiring 10× fewer rollouts. Notably, wd1 with LoRA alone (82.3% GSM8K) is competitive with full-fine-tuning baselines, and the full-fine-tuning variant wd1 (82.7%) approaches wd1++ performance, suggesting the stepwise extension provides the final boost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant ratio-based policy optimization paradigm (GRPO, PPO, d1) for diffusion LLMs by reformulating the RL objective as a weighted log-likelihood, drawing on the advantage-weighted regression (AWR) lineage from Peng et al. (2019) and the reverse-KL constrained policy optimization framework. The key conceptual shift is eliminating the need for policy ratios entirely, which for dLLMs avoids the exponential amplification of likelihood approximation error that plagues methods like d1 and Diffu-GRPO. The theoretical grounding connects the weighted objective to energy-guided discrete diffusion sampling (Lu et al., 2023) and concrete score matching (Meng et al., 2022), while the negative-sample penalty term is interpreted as data unlearning (Alberti et al., 2025). Concurrent ratio-free approaches like SPG and d2 address similar concerns from different angles, but wd1 retains an off-policy weighted-regression structure that is more general than purely on-policy gradient methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The intractability of dLLM likelihoods is a fundamental bottleneck for applying RL to diffusion language models, and this paper shows that a single likelihood approximation suffices for policy optimization, reducing both computational cost and estimation variance. The practical impact is significant for the emerging dLLM ecosystem: eliminating the SFT stage and reducing per-step FLOPs makes RL fine-tuning more accessible, while the wd1++ extension leveraging intermediate denoising steps achieves competitive math performance with 10× fewer rollouts. However, the evaluation is limited to a single 8B model (LLaDA-8B) with LoRA, and the d1-style likelihood approximation at t=1 remains biased, so it is unclear whether the theoretical guarantees (monotonic improvement, energy-guided interpretation) hold tightly in practice with such coarse approximations. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger dLLMs, whether full fine-tuning vs. LoRA changes the picture, and how wd1 compares to the more radical ratio-free methods (d2, SPG) as the dLLM RL landscape matures.
