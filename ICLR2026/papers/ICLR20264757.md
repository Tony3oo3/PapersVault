## Problem

Existing uncertainty-aware continual learning methods, such as NPCL, introduce non-negligible parameter growth and are incompatible with logits-based replay methods due to stochasticity from Monte Carlo sampling. Meanwhile, standard rehearsal-based CL models remain poorly calibrated and over-confident, with recency bias toward new tasks undermining deployment in safety-critical domains. The paper seeks a lightweight, plug-and-play principle that simultaneously improves prediction reliability and mitigates catastrophic forgetting without architectural incompatibility.

## Input / Output

The model accepts two inputs per forward pass: an RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and a second input that is either a one-hot ground-truth label vector or a uniform 'empty' signal over all classes, selected with probability P=0.9 for the empty signal. The output is a vector of class logits over all classes seen so far; after softmax normalization, this output can itself be fed back as the second input for the idempotence check. A fixed-capacity buffer stores exemplars from previous tasks for replay and distillation.

## Method

IDER modifies the ResNet-18 backbone by splitting it into two sub-networks f_t^1 and f_t^2, with a lightweight linear+LeakyReLU layer that maps the second input into a feature vector added to the intermediate feature map, enabling the model to accept and respond to its own predictions. Training uses three loss components: (1) a Standard Idempotent Module (L_ice) that applies two cross-entropy losses from successive forward passes on current-task data to make the model idempotent; (2) an Idempotent Distillation Module (L_ide) that minimizes the L2 distance between the current model's first-pass output f_t(x,0) and the frozen previous-task checkpoint's second-pass output f_{t-1}(x, f_t(x,0)) on both current and buffer data, preventing error amplification while anchoring representations to the stable manifold; and (3) an experience replay loss (L_rep-ice) with the same two-pass structure applied to buffer samples. The total objective L_IDER = L_ice + α·L_ide + β·L_rep-ice requires only two forward passes per sample and adds ~0.7M parameters, making it directly composable with existing rehearsal-based methods like ER, BFP, and CLS-ER.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (buffer 200), ER+ID achieves 71.02% FAA, outperforming the strongest baseline SARL (68.87%) by over 2%, and BFP+ID reaches 71.99%. On CIFAR-100 (buffer 500), BFP+ID attains 48.53% FAA, surpassing XDER (48.14%) and BFP (46.70%). On Tiny-ImageNet (buffer 4000), CLS-ER+ID achieves 46.17% FAA, edging out XDER (46.12%). In terms of calibration, ER+ID reduces ECE from 45.53% (ER) to 12.36% on CIFAR-10 (buffer 200), and BFP+ID further lowers ECE to 8.63% on CIFAR-10 (buffer 500) versus BFP's 9.40%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDER consistently outperforms all rehearsal-based baselines across the three datasets. On CIFAR-10 (buffer 200), ER+ID (71.02%) surpasses SARL (68.87%) and BFP (68.64%) by 2.1–2.4%, while BFP+ID (71.99%) sets the best result. On CIFAR-100 (buffer 500), BFP+ID (48.53%) edges out XDER (48.14%) and BFP (46.70%), and on Tiny-ImageNet (buffer 4000), CLS-ER+ID (46.17%) slightly beats XDER (46.12%). The most dramatic gain is over the ER baseline: ER+ID improves FAA by 26% on CIFAR-10 (buffer 200) and 21% on CIFAR-100 (buffer 500). For calibration (ECE), ER+ID reduces error from 45.53% to 12.36% on CIFAR-10 (buffer 200), and BFP+ID further improves over BFP from 9.40% to 8.63% on CIFAR-10 (buffer 500), matching or exceeding post-hoc calibration methods like T-CIL (10.51%) and RC (12.84%). In the more challenging GCIL setting (Table 2), ER+ID gains +10.32% over ER on CIFAR-100 (buffer 200, uniform), and CLS-ER+ID gains +8.40% over CLS-ER (buffer 500, longtail), confirming that idempotence is most beneficial under distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the idempotence property—previously studied in static batch learning for uncertainty estimation (ZigZag, IterNet)—as a training objective for continual learning, a setting where it had not been explored. It builds directly on Experience Replay (Riemer et al., 2019) as the base CL framework and adapts the two-forward-pass idempotent training loss from Durasov et al. (2024a,b) to the sequential-task setting. The key departure from prior uncertainty-aware CL work (NPCL, Jha et al., 2023) is avoiding stochastic neural processes in favor of a deterministic frozen-checkpoint distillation, making the method compatible with standard logits-based replay methods. The idempotent distillation loss also subsumes a form of knowledge distillation (aligning current and previous model outputs), but anchors it to the stable manifold rather than merely matching output probabilities as in DER.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IDER offers a lightweight, plug-and-play mechanism (two forward passes, ~0.7M extra parameters) that simultaneously improves accuracy, reduces forgetting, and improves calibration in CL—three objectives that prior methods typically address separately. This suggests that enforcing self-consistency on the learned manifold is a unifying principle for stability in sequential learning. However, all experiments are limited to ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny-ImageNet, leaving open whether the idempotence constraint scales to larger backbones, more complex datasets (ImageNet), or other CL paradigms (domain-incremental, task-incremental). The architectural modification (splitting the backbone and adding a label-embedding path) is somewhat ad hoc, and the optimal split point appears dataset-dependent, which may complicate generalization.
