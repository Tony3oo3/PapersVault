## Problem

Existing cross-task VRP solvers either share all parameters across tasks (causing negative transfer) or route node embeddings to experts via learned gating (limiting each expert's view to a narrow node subset). Both architectures overlook the structural fact that VRP tasks are defined by combinations of a small set of basic constraints, leading to poor zero-shot generalization to unseen constraint combinations and entirely new constraints.

## Input / Output

The model takes a complete graph instance with 2D node coordinates, per-customer linehaul/backhaul demands, time-window parameters, and a binary indicator of which basic constraints (capacity, open route, backhaul, duration limit, time window, mixed backhauls) are active. The active constraint set directly determines which experts are switched on in the encoder, making the architecture interpretable and constraint-aware. The output is an autoregressively constructed set of vehicle routes (a feasible solution) whose total Euclidean cost is minimized; during inference, a greedy rollout with 8× instance augmentation is used.

## Method

CoEKS replaces the FFN in each transformer encoder layer with a pool of constraint-specific expert FFNs, where each expert is dedicated to one basic constraint and a shared capacity expert is always active. A lightweight combiner (a single learned weight vector per expert) produces softmax-normalized weights over the active experts, and the layer output is their weighted sum. To foster transferable knowledge, two complementary sharing mechanisms are introduced: (1) mutual distillation in the first encoder layer, where active experts are regularized toward a virtual average expert via an MSE auxiliary loss (reducing pairwise O(K²) cost to O(K)), and (2) a shared low-rank MLP with residual connection applied to the input before the combiners, injecting cross-task information into all weighting decisions. Training uses REINFORCE with a POMO-style shared baseline over 300 epochs of 100K mixed-batch instances; for new constraints, a new expert and combiner are randomly initialized (or initialized from the shared capacity module) and fine-tuned in isolation while all existing parameters are frozen, enabling rapid plug-and-play adaptation without catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On 16 in-distribution (ID) VRP tasks, CoEKS achieves the best average gap among all neural methods at both n=50 and n=100, with the smallest gap in 10 out of 14 task-scale combinations. On 9 out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks with unseen constraint combinations, CoEKS delivers relative improvements of at least 18.3% (n=50) and 13% (n=100) over the SOTA neural baseline ReLD-MoEL in OOD average gap. For adaptation to a new Mixed Backhauls (MB) constraint, CoEKS+c+ (reusing shared modules for initialization) achieves the best fine-tuning gap of 1.56% on VRPMB (n=50), outperforming the next-best ReLD-MoEL-EAL (2.59%) by over 1 percentage point.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoEKS outperforms all six neural baselines (POMO-MTL, MVMoE, RF-TE, CaDA, ReLD-MoEL) on both ID and OOD tasks, with the largest margins on OOD tasks where constraint combinations are unseen. Against the strongest neural baseline ReLD-MoEL (SOTA), CoEKS reduces the OOD average gap from 4.63% to 3.79% at n=50 (≈18% relative improvement) and from 7.64% to 6.66% at n=100 (≈13% relative improvement). Against traditional solvers (HGS-PyVRP, OR-Tools), CoEKS achieves competitive solution quality (gaps within 1–5% of the best traditional solver) while being orders of magnitude faster (seconds vs. minutes). On the new-constraint adaptation task (VRPMB), CoEKS+c+ achieves a 1.56% gap versus 2.59% for ReLD-MoEL-EAL and 2.35% for RF-TE-EAL, demonstrating superior scalability. CaDA, which uses constraint-aware dual-attention, shows the weakest OOD generalization among neural methods (7.28% OOD avg. gap at n=50), confirming the paper's critique of task-shared dense models. The universality experiments further show CoEKS consistently outperforms its backbone-specific counterparts (POMO-CoEKS > POMO-MTL/RF-TE/MVMoE; ReLD-CoEKS > ReLD-MoEL) across both POMO and ReLD backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoEKS departs from two dominant paradigms for cross-task VRP solvers: task-shared dense models (POMO-MTL, RouteFinder, CaDA) that overemphasize coupled representations, and node-level mixture-of-experts models (MVMoE, ReLD-MoEL) whose gating restricts each expert's vision to a narrow node subset. The key conceptual departure is treating VRP tasks as *combinations of basic constraints* and assigning one dedicated expert per constraint, combined via a learned combiner—drawing the shared-expert idea from DeepSeekMoE (Dai et al., 2024) but grounding expert identity in constraint semantics rather than learned routing. The architecture is instantiated on the ReLD light-decoder backbone (Huang et al., 2025) and the POMO policy-optimization framework (Kwon et al., 2020), with mutual distillation among experts inspired by peer-to-peer knowledge transfer in MoE language models (Xie et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in new-expert mechanism offers a practical path to incremental deployment: when a new operational constraint appears (e.g., mixed backhauls, multi-depot), a single expert can be added and fine-tuned in ~10 epochs without retraining the full model, which is attractive for logistics operators facing evolving requirements. The finding that mutual distillation should be confined to the first encoder layer—because deeper layers encode constraint-specific patterns that homogenization would destroy—provides a useful design principle for any multi-expert system balancing specialization and collaboration. A stated limitation is that parameter count grows linearly with the number of constraints, and the authors flag more efficient expert-sharing or parameterization as an open direction. Broader open questions include whether the constraint-level decomposition generalizes beyond the six studied constraints and whether the approach extends to other combinatorial optimization families with analogous constraint-combination structure.
