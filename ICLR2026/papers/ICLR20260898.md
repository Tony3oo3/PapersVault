## Problem

Existing Kronecker compressed sensing (KCS) methods largely ignore the Kronecker structure of the measurement matrix, rely on generic solvers, and are tailored to a single sparsity pattern without generalization. No prior work provides a unified restricted isometry property (RIP) analysis for Kronecker product matrices across standard, hierarchical, and Kronecker-supported sparsity, nor a recovery framework with RIP-based guarantees that handles all three patterns within one algorithmic structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes noisy measurements y ∈ R^M, the I Kronecker factor matrices {H_i} ∈ R^{M_i×N_i} (so H = ⊗_{i=1}^I H_i), and a sparsity specification that varies by model: a scalar s for standard sparsity, a tuple (s_I, …, s_1) for hierarchical sparsity, or a tuple of per-dimension support sparsities for Kronecker-supported sparsity. The output is the estimated sparse vector x̂ ∈ R^{N̄} where N̄ = ∏_{i=1}^I N_i, obtained by reshaping the final stage's solution back from tensor form. The signal x is internally treated as a tensor X ∈ R^{N_1×⋯×N_I} to exploit the Kronecker structure through mode-unfolding operations.

## Method

The core insight is a hierarchical view of KCS: each factor matrix H_j in the Kronecker product probes the signal's sparsity at a distinct level of a block-wise partition, where the jth mode unfolding T_(j) = H_j U_j + N_(j) isolates the sparsity pattern at level j. This yields the Multi-Stage Recovery (MSR) algorithm, which processes factor matrices sequentially from j=I down to j=1, solving an MMV subproblem at each stage using pluggable solvers (SOMP, SIHT, SHTP, or SBL). A key structural result (Lemma 1) shows that for standard and hierarchical sparsity, each stage decomposes into ∏_{i=I}^{j+1} N_i independent MMV problems (one per column block), while Kronecker-supported sparsity retains a single MMV due to shared block support. The theoretical contribution is a unified (s,N)-RIP bound δ_(s,N)(H) ≤ ∏_{i=1}^I (1+δ_{s_i}(H_i)) − 1, proving that level-wise sparsity rather than total sparsity governs recovery, along with RIP-based convergence guarantees for MSR with IHT/HTP that show the error decays geometrically with iterations.

## Results

For standard sparsity (I=2, M=64, N=80, s=15), MSOMP-Seq achieves comparable NSE to KroOMP but reduces runtime from 130.5s to 0.43s at 3 dB SNR (~300× speedup), while MSSBL-Seq outperforms KroOMP in NSE across all SNR with ~100× less runtime. For hierarchical sparsity, MSHTP-Seq matches HiHTP's NSE with ~20× less runtime (0.038s vs 0.65s at 3 dB), and MSIHT-Seq matches IHT with ~160× less runtime (0.05s vs 8.24s). For Kronecker-supported sparsity (I=3, M=15, N=18, s=4), MSSBL achieves comparable NSE to SVD-KroSBL and AM-KroSBL while being 2–3 orders of magnitude faster (e.g., 0.073s vs 37.1s and 56.0s at 3 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KroOMP (Caiafa & Cichocki, 2013) for standard sparsity, MSOMP-Seq matches NSE performance while reducing runtime by 1–3 orders of magnitude (e.g., 0.43s vs 130.5s at 3 dB), and MSSBL-Seq additionally achieves lower NSE across all SNR levels. Against HiHTP (Roth et al., 2020) and HTP/IHT (Foucart, 2011; Foucart & Rauhut, 2013) for hierarchical sparsity, MSHTP-Seq and MSIHT-Seq achieve comparable NSE with 1–2 orders of magnitude runtime reduction (e.g., MSHTP: 0.038s vs HiHTP 0.65s vs HTP 2.24s at 3 dB; MSIHT: 0.05s vs IHT 8.24s). Against SVD-KroSBL and AM-KroSBL (He & Joseph, 2025a) for Kronecker-supported sparsity, MSSBL achieves comparable NSE with 2–3 orders of magnitude speedup (0.073s vs 37.1s and 56.0s at 3 dB). In the wideband massive MIMO channel estimation application, MSHTP and MSOMP outperform HiHTP and HiIHT (Wunder et al., 2019) in NSE for most numbers of angles L (e.g., at L=50: MSHTP 0.182 vs HiHTP 0.507) while requiring 1–2 orders less runtime. Overall, the MSR framework consistently wins on runtime across all three sparsity models while matching or exceeding the accuracy of state-of-the-art baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Roth et al. (2020), who established a hierarchical RIP for Kronecker product measurements and proposed HiHTP for hierarchically sparse recovery, and on Caiafa & Cichocki (2013), who introduced KroOMP exploiting tensor structure for standard sparsity. The key departure is a unifying 'hierarchical view' that shows each factor matrix in the Kronecker product probes the signal at a distinct block level, enabling a single multi-stage algorithm (MSR) to handle standard, hierarchical, and Kronecker-supported sparsity simultaneously—whereas prior methods (KroOMP, HiHTP, KroSBL) are each tailored to one pattern. The theoretical contribution generalizes Roth et al.'s hierarchical RIP into a (s,N)-RIP that covers all three models and slightly tightens the Duarte & Baraniuk (2011a) bound for standard sparsity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified (s,N)-RIP framework and MSR algorithm provide the first RIP-based recovery guarantees for Kronecker-supported sparsity, a gap that prior OMP- and Bayesian-based methods left unaddressed. The 1–3 order-of-magnitude runtime reductions make KCS practical for large-scale applications like wideband massive MIMO channel estimation, where the paper demonstrates MSOMP/MSHTP outperforming HiHTP/HiIHT. Open questions remain: the RIP bound for Kronecker-supported sparsity does not improve over the hierarchical case despite the additional shared-support structure (an acknowledged limitation of worst-case RIP analysis), and the optimal thresholding operator for Kronecker-supported sparsity is NP-hard, leaving only heuristic alternatives.
