## Problem

Existing activation steering methods for LLMs face a fundamental trade-off: contrastive approaches (e.g., DiffMean) are sample-efficient but only capture activation-level signals from positive-negative pairs, while parameter-tuning methods (e.g., ReFT) capture loss-driven gradient signals but require hundreds to thousands of labeled examples and multiple training epochs. No prior method achieves both sample efficiency and loss-driven steering signal extraction at inference time without retraining.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of N in-context examples (either paired preference (x̃ᵢ, ỹᵢ⁺, ỹᵢ⁻) or positive-only (x̃ᵢ, ỹᵢ⁺)) describing a target behavior, a new prompt x, and a frozen pretrained LLM. It produces a steering vector ΔZ added to all input-token representations at a single decoder layer l (selected via grid search over {10,15,20,30}), which then drives the model's generation. The steering is applied only to prompt tokens (not subsequent generated tokens) to avoid compounding errors, and the steering multiplier η is fixed at 1 after normalization.

## Method

COLD-Steer's key insight is that the effect of one gradient descent step on model parameters toward a desired behavior induces a predictable, computable change in intermediate activations, which can be applied directly at inference time without any parameter update. Formally, the steering vector is ΔZ*(x) ≈ −(η/N) Σᵢ ∇_θ Z(x;θ) ∇_θ L(M(x̃ᵢ), ỹᵢ), approximating how activations shift after one gradient step on the in-context loss. Two complementary approximations avoid expensive backpropagation at inference: (1) COLD-Kernel-Steer replaces the NTK inner product ⟨∇_θ Z(x), ∇_θ Z(x̃ᵢ)⟩ with a unit kernel κ=1, reducing the new-example cost to a single forward pass plus O(N·d) kernel evaluations, and (2) COLD-FD-Steer uses a finite-difference approximation with ε=10⁻⁶, requiring only two forward passes (with θ and θ+ε·Σ∇_θ L) regardless of N. The unit kernel is justified by the linear representation hypothesis: gradients from examples of the same behavior are dominated by a shared direction, making the normalized NTK approximately constant.

## Results

On the CAA behavior-selection benchmark with 50 in-context examples, COLD-FD achieves up to 98% accuracy (refusal, Llama-2-7b-chat-hf) and 98% (coordinate-ais, Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct), with an average rank of 2.00 (pair) and 1.71 (positive-only) across 7 tasks on Llama-2-7b-chat-hf. COLD-FD outperforms the best parameter-tuning baseline (ReFT-vector, avg rank 3.29) and the best contrastive baseline (DiffMean, avg rank 4.00) while requiring no training. On the OpinionsQA pluralistic-alignment task, COLD-Kernel reduces KL divergence from 2.43 (Base) to 0.86 for Black respondents and from 2.38 to 0.97 for Republicans, outperforming ReFT-vector across all demographics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COLD-FD decisively outperforms the contrastive baseline DiffMean (CAA) on 5 of 7 tasks for Llama-2-7b-chat-hf (e.g., 0.90 vs 0.52 on coordinate-ais, 0.98 vs 0.74 on refusal), though DiffMean retains an edge on myopic-reward (0.76 vs 0.60). Against the parameter-tuning baseline ReFT(mlp), COLD-FD wins on 6 of 7 tasks with large margins (e.g., 0.98 vs 0.36 on refusal, 0.90 vs 0.68 on coordinate-ais), and similarly dominates ReFT(vec) (0.90 vs 0.48 on coordinate-ais, 0.96 vs 0.70 on hallucination). The contrastive baselines ICV and DiffMeanProj (RepE) are consistently weaker, with ICV matching Base on several tasks (0.28 on coordinate-ais). On Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct, COLD-FD achieves 0.98 on coordinate-ais and corrig-HH versus 0.02 and 0.38 for Base, a ~96% absolute gain over the strongest baseline ReFT(vector) at 0.02/0.60. Notably, COLD-Kernel underperforms COLD-FD on most selection tasks but uniquely excels on the OpinionsQA distributional-alignment task, where it reduces KL to 0.74–1.04 across demographics versus 1.42–1.77 for ReFT(vector), suggesting the unit kernel is better suited to preserving linear subgroup structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

COLD-Steer sits at the intersection of two lines of work: contrastive activation steering (CAA/DiffMean, RepE, ICV) and parameter-tuning steering (ReFT, BiPO), which the paper shows are both special cases of its kernel-approximation framework. The core theoretical machinery is borrowed from learning-dynamics analysis of neural tangent kernels (Ren & Sutherland, 2024; Jacot et al., 2018) and the observation that transformers implicitly perform gradient descent during in-context learning (Von Oswald et al., 2023; Dai et al., 2022), but the paper's departure is to make this implicit learning explicit at inference time as an activation intervention rather than a parameter update. The unit-kernel approximation is further motivated by the linear representation hypothesis (Park et al., 2023), which the paper leverages to justify why a trivially simple kernel suffices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50× sample-efficiency gain over parameter-tuning baselines makes inference-time behavioral steering practically deployable in settings where only a handful of labeled examples are available, and the training-free nature removes the need for per-behavior fine-tuning pipelines. The unification showing that DiffMean is a special case of the kernel approximation provides a cleaner theoretical lens for understanding why contrastive methods work and where they fail. Key open questions include: why COLD-FD fails on distributional (OpinionsQA) steering while COLD-Kernel excels there, whether better NTK approximations can close the gap between the two variants, and whether the approach extends to multi-layer or angular steering. The space complexity of COLD-FD (storing full parameter gradients) remains a practical barrier for very large models.
