## Problem

Traditional Image Quality Assessment (IQA) methods are designed to predict human perceptual preferences, but Embodied AI systems (robots) are sensitive to fundamentally different distortions and have downstream Decision and Execution steps beyond mere Cognition. No existing IQA metric or database captures the perceptual quality of images from a robot's perspective, leaving a critical gap for deploying embodied systems in real-world conditions with sensor degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261366/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The IQA metrics under evaluation consume either a reference/distorted image pair (FR metrics, 1k resolution) or only the distorted image (NR metrics). The subjective ground-truth scores are multi-dimensional: Cognition scores (Precision, Recall, Semantic) are derived from VLM text outputs scored via BLEU/ROUGE/CIDEr; Decision scores (Position, Rotation, State) come from VLA 7-DoF pose outputs measured by spatial distance, cosine similarity, and absolute difference respectively; Execution scores are binary/continuous real-world robotic arm outcomes (100 for success, cm-deducted for failure, 0 for emergency stop). The database spans 1,230 reference images × 30 distortion types × 5 intensity levels = 36,900 pairs with 5.53M total annotations.

## Method

The paper constructs a Perception-Cognition-Decision-Execution pipeline grounded in Mertonian system theory, where each stage is annotated by a different class of subject: 15 VLMs (<8B params) for Cognition, 15 VLAs (≤8B params) for Decision, and a physical UR5 robotic arm with Robotiq gripper for Execution. Cognition quality is computed by comparing VLM text outputs (~10 words per task) between reference and distorted images using averaged BLEU/ROUGE/CIDEr; Decision quality parses the 7-DoF VLA pose into position (mm), rotation (rad), and gripper state, scoring each via spatial distance, cosine similarity, and absolute difference after 0-1 normalization. The key empirical contribution is benchmarking 15 IQA metrics (5 zero-shot, 5 FR, 5 NR) against these robot-derived scores, revealing that even the best method (TOPIQ) achieves only ~0.75 SRCC on Decision—far below the ~0.9+ typical of human-oriented IQA—confirming that HVS-tuned metrics cannot transfer to RVS. Cross-database validation and 1,500 real-world robotic executions further demonstrate that VLA Decision scores correlate with Execution (SRCC > 0.6) while VLM Cognition scores do not (SRCC < 0.5), justifying the separation of the two stages.

## Results

On the Embodied-IQA Decision (VLA) benchmark, TOPIQ-FR achieves the best SRCC of 0.775 (Position), 0.643 (Rotation), and 0.668 (State), while TOPIQ-NR reaches 0.750, 0.598, and 0.704 respectively. Zero-shot baselines perform substantially worse: PSNR (SRCC 0.276), SSIM (0.486), BRISQUE (0.307), and Q-Align (0.533) on Position. Cross-database validation reveals that IQA models fine-tuned on Embodied-IQA lose human-oriented capability, dropping below SRCC 0.4 on LIVE, while real-world Execution correlates with VLA Decision at SRCC > 0.6 but with VLM Cognition at SRCC < 0.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 15 existing IQA methods (5 zero-shot, 5 full-reference, 5 no-reference) against Embodied-IQA's VLA Decision scores, finding that none achieve the ~0.9 SRCC typical of human-oriented IQA. TOPIQ (both FR and NR variants) leads across all dimensions, reaching SRCC ~0.77–0.83, but still falls well short of human-oriented performance. FR methods consistently outperform NR counterparts (e.g., TOPIQ-FR 0.775 vs. TOPIQ-NR 0.750 on Position). Notably, methods with HVS-frozen parameters (LPIPS, DISTS, CLIPIQA) perform worse than zero-shot baselines after fine-tuning, confirming a fundamental gap between HVS and RVS. Cross-database validation shows that models trained on Embodied-IQA lose human-oriented capability (SRCC < 0.4 on LIVE), while AHIQ trained on VLA data can still predict VLM Cognition scores at SRCC 0.7, revealing an internal Cognition–Decision connection. Real-world Execution experiments confirm VLA Decision as a valid proxy (SRCC > 0.6) but VLM Cognition is insufficient (SRCC < 0.5), justifying the separation of Machine and Robot visual systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the long line of human-oriented IQA (LIVE, TID2013, KADID-10K) and the more recent machine-oriented IQA (MPD, EPD) by introducing a Robot Visual System (RVS) quality assessment that accounts for the full Perception-Cognition-Decision-Execution pipeline, grounded in the Mertonian system framework. While prior machine-oriented IQA targets only Cognition (segmentation, detection, VQA), this work extends quality assessment downstream to Decision (7-DoF pose prediction via VLA) and Execution (real-world robotic arm manipulation), arguing that high Cognition fidelity does not guarantee successful downstream action. The annotation methodology repurposes VLMs (for Cognition scoring via BLEU/ROUGE/CIDEr) and VLAs (for Decision scoring via position/rotation/state fidelity) as 'subjects,' analogous to how human observers serve as subjects in traditional IQA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first large-scale database (36k+ pairs, 5m+ annotations) and benchmark specifically for embodied image quality, filling a gap that becomes critical as Embodied AI moves from labs to real-world deployment where sensor distortions cause task failures. The key open question is whether new RVS-specific IQA metrics can be developed, since all 15 tested methods (even fine-tuned) achieve SRCC < 0.65 on Decision scores versus ~0.9 on human tasks, and methods with HVS-frozen parameters (LPIPS, DISTS, CLIPIQA) actually degrade below zero-shot baselines after training. Limitations include the small real-world execution set (1.5k samples), restriction to vision-only perception and single-arm execution, and the need for future work on multi-sensory (vision-tactile) quality assessment and quadruped/legged robot scenarios.
