## Problem

Existing extractive summarization methods rely on local sentence-level similarity or shallow statistical features, failing to capture global semantic structures and long-range logical dependencies, while LLM-based approaches incur prohibitive computational costs for long documents. No prior work has systematically integrated topological data analysis into summarization to explicitly model and preserve both semantic clusters and cross-paragraph logical cycles, leaving summarized texts vulnerable to losing critical reasoning chains and disrupting downstream task coherence.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a long document (up to 8,192 tokens) segmented into sentences via NLTK, optionally accompanied by a downstream query for task-aware compression. Each sentence is encoded into a 768-dimensional L2-normalized embedding using all-mpnet-base-v2. The output is an extractive summary—a subset of original sentences—produced at a user-specified compression ratio, with the Protected Pool sentences guaranteed to be retained regardless of compression level.

## Method

GloSA-sum constructs a weighted undirected sentence graph using mutual k-nearest neighbors (k grows logarithmically with document length) and hybrid edge weights fusing semantic cosine distance with exponential positional decay. A one-time persistent homology computation over the Lazy Witness Complex identifies the top-K most persistent H0 components (core semantic themes) and top-M H1 cycles (cross-paragraph logical dependencies), which are permanently fixed in a Protected Pool. Subsequent iterative compression removes sentences by a composite score combining TopoScore (negative Dijkstra shortest-path length to protected nodes, computed on the sparse k-NN graph) and an optional TaskScore (cosine similarity plus BM25 against a query), avoiding any repeated TDA computation. For ultra-long documents, a hierarchical strategy partitions the text into segments, applies the full pipeline in parallel per segment, then performs a final global compression pass to enforce cross-segment coherence.

## Results

GloSA-sum achieves the highest ROUGE-L on three of four datasets: 51.00 on GovReport, 42.00 on ArXiv, and 44.50 on PubMed, surpassing the strongest baselines (BigBird, DANCER, MemSum) by 0.99–3.49 points. On ArXiv, it outperforms BART by +2.14 ROUGE-L and PEGASUS by +7.21 ROUGE-L, while on PubMed it exceeds BigBird by +2.17 ROUGE-L. In human evaluation, GloSA-sum attains the best average score of 4.30/5, leading in coherence (4.4) over all baselines including GPT-4 (4.3). On the SQuAD 2.0 downstream task, summaries from GloSA-sum yield 91.50 F1 / 88.50 EM, outperforming both pipeline and joint summarization baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against extractive baselines, GloSA-sum dominates: it outperforms TextRank by +10.9 ROUGE-L on ArXiv and +10.0 on PubMed, and surpasses BERTSum by +21.2 ROUGE-L on ArXiv. Among model-based extractive methods, it beats MemSum by +6.69 ROUGE-L on ArXiv and +0.17 on PubMed, and exceeds MatchSum by +4.51 ROUGE-L on PubMed. Versus abstractive models, GloSA-sum gains +2.14 ROUGE-L over BART and +7.21 over PEGASUS on ArXiv, and +3.49 ROUGE-L over BART on PubMed, indicating superior preservation of global logical structure in long scientific and biomedical texts. Compared to long-context optimizers BigBird and DANCER, GloSA-sum leads by +0.23 ROUGE-L on ArXiv and +2.17 on PubMed, while also achieving +1.19 ROUGE-2 on GovReport. In the LLM comparison (Table 8), GloSA-sum outperforms GPT-4 Prompt-Sum by +10.86 ROUGE-L on GovReport and +3.91 on ArXiv, and surpasses Claude-3 Sum and Fine-tuned LLaMA-3 8B across all four datasets, with the largest gap on GovReport where LLMs degrade due to context-length limitations. In human evaluation, GloSA-sum ties GPT-4 at 4.30 average but leads in coherence (4.4 vs. 4.3), and outperforms all other LLM baselines (Claude-3: 4.20, RAG+LLM: 4.13, FT-LLaMA: 4.03).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing topological data analysis (persistent homology) into extractive text summarization, a domain previously dominated by local-similarity graph ranking (TextRank, LexRank), model-level improvements (BERTSum, MatchSum, BART, PEGASUS), and LLM-based approaches. While TDA has been applied to NLP for contradiction detection (Wu et al., 2022), interpretability (Proskurina et al., 2023), and discourse coherence analysis (Jain et al., 2024), none of these works used it for document compression or summarization. The key conceptual departure is replacing iterative graph-based pruning with a one-time topological analysis that fixes a global semantic-logical backbone (Protected Pool), followed by lightweight proxy-metric compression—shifting the paradigm from local similarity to multi-scale structural persistence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck: LLM-based summarization incurs high inference costs and often loses global logical chains on long documents, while classical extractive methods lack structural awareness. The one-time TDA + proxy-metric design offers a principled efficiency story (6–8× TextRank vs. 10–20× for autoregressive models) with intra/inter-segment parallelism. However, the method remains extractive and sentence-granular, limiting its applicability to abstractive or clause-level compression; the LLM comparisons rely on prompt-based baselines rather than fine-tuned summarizers, and the modest ROUGE gains (+1–3 points) over strong baselines like BigBird and DANCER suggest the topological signal, while conceptually appealing, yields incremental rather than transformative improvements. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multilingual or code summarization, how sensitive it is to encoder quality at scale, and whether higher-order homology (H2+) could capture richer discourse structures.
