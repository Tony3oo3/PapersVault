## Problem

Robot policies require large amounts of expensive, embodiment-specific action-labeled demonstrations, while the abundant video data available (human and teleoperated robot clips) lacks action annotations. Prior latent-action pretraining methods treat the task purely as autoregressive policy learning in latent space without modeling state transitions via video prediction, and rely on temporally coarse, task-centric latent actions that miss fine-grained motion dynamics critical for high-frequency control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262337/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the policy consumes two most recent RGB frames (o_{t-1}, o_t) from a front-facing camera and a free-form text task instruction, and produces a continuous action chunk of length H=14 (e.g., 14×7 joint positions for a Franka Panda) via 10-step forward Euler integration of a flow-matching velocity field. The chunked decoding amortizes inference latency, enabling effective closed-loop control rates up to 22 Hz on hardware. During pretraining, the model additionally outputs a predicted future frame o_{t+H} (tokenized via VQ-VAE) and a sequence of H discrete latent action tokens (each composed of N_latent codes from an 8-entry codebook).

## Method

ViPRA is a three-stage pretraining-finetuning pipeline. First, a latent action model with a DINOv2-initialized inverse dynamics encoder and a causal forward decoder is trained on actionless human and robot videos using NSVQ quantization (8-code codebook) and a composite loss of L1 pixel reconstruction, LPIPS perceptual similarity, and RAFT-based optical flow consistency, yielding motion-centric discrete latent actions at 3–6 Hz. Second, the instruction-tuned LWM-Chat-1M video-language model is extended with a latent action embedding head and an MLP decoder, then pretrained to jointly predict future VQ-VAE visual tokens and the corresponding latent action sequence via cross-entropy, unifying semantic grounding with physical dynamics modeling. Third, a flow-matching decoder (noisy action encoder + velocity-field MLP) is finetuned on only 100–200 teleoperated demonstrations to map the pretrained latent representations to smooth, continuous robot action chunks, with the Beta(1.5,1) noise schedule and action chunking (H=14, replan every 7 steps) enabling high-frequency deployment.

## Results

On the SIMPLER benchmark (4 Bridge tasks, 7-DoF WidowX arm), ViPRA-AR achieves 69.8% average success rate in the discrete setting, outperforming the best discrete baseline LAPA (53.1%) by 16.7 points. In the continuous setting, ViPRA-FM reaches 62.5% average success, surpassing UniVLA (42.7%) by 19.8 points and π_0 (27.1%) by 35.4 points. On real-world Franka Panda manipulation tasks, ViPRA-FM achieves 54.1% average success rate versus 40.1% for π_0 and 23.8% for Scratch-FM, using only 180 teleoperated demonstrations per task. The method supports high-frequency continuous control up to 22 Hz via chunked flow matching decoding (chunk size H=14, 10 Euler steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViPRA-AR (69.8%) decisively outperforms all discrete baselines: LAPA (53.1%, +16.7 pts), Scratch-AR (52.1%, +17.7 pts), and OpenVLA (38.6%, +31.2 pts), with the largest gains on precision-heavy tasks like StackG2Y (66.7% vs. 33.3% for LAPA). In the continuous setting, ViPRA-FM (62.5%) beats UniVLA (42.7%, +19.8 pts), Scratch-FM (41.7%, +20.8 pts), and π_0 (27.1%, +35.4 pts), though π_0 matches ViPRA-FM on Eggplant2Bask (83.3% vs. 79.2%). Video-learning baselines UniPI (1.7%) and VPT (51.0%) are substantially outperformed, with UniPI frequently diverging from instructions in longer-horizon settings. On real-world Franka tasks, ViPRA-FM (54.1%) exceeds π_0 (40.1%) by 14 points and Scratch-FM (23.8%) by 30 points, using only 180 demos per task versus the 970k OpenX trajectories used by π_0 and OpenVLA. Ablations confirm that both future-state prediction and multi-step latent action chunking are necessary: removing either drops performance by 10+ points.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViPRA builds on the LWM-Chat-1M video-language model as its backbone, the NSVQ quantization scheme for discrete latent actions, and flow matching for continuous action decoding. It departs from prior latent action pretraining methods (LAPA, UniVLA, Moto, GR00T N1) that treat pretraining purely as autoregressive policy learning in latent space without modeling state transitions, and from VLA models (OpenVLA, π0) that require thousands of hours of action-labeled demonstrations. The key conceptual departure is jointly predicting future visual observations and motion-centric latent action chunks during pretraining, then amortizing inference via a chunked flow matching decoder, rather than relying on inverse dynamics models or temporally coarse task-centric latents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ViPRA demonstrates that a video prediction model can be converted into a high-frequency (22 Hz) continuous robot policy using only 100–200 teleoperated demonstrations, eliminating the dominant data-collection bottleneck in VLA training. The cross-embodiment transfer from human videos to robot control, and the ablation showing that joint future-state + latent-action prediction at pretraining time is critical (while adding it at finetuning time degrades performance due to autoregressive error compounding), provide useful design guidance. Open questions include how passive video pretraining scales, whether the approach extends to dexterous multi-fingered hands or humanoid platforms, and whether the latent action decoder can serve as a predictive world model for RL-based alignment or test-time planning.
