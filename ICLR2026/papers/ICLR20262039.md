## Problem

Existing group-fair learning methods produce a single deterministic model under fairness constraints, leaving model uncertainty unquantified—a critical gap for trustworthy decision-making in domains like medical diagnosis. Bayesian inference under group fairness has received little attention because the constrained posterior is intractable: the set of fair models cannot be formulated analytically for complex architectures, and standard variational inference with mean-field Gaussians cannot satisfy fairness constraints. Computing the average demographic parity gap during VI further requires adversarial learning or O(n²) MMD evaluations, making it practically infeasible.

## Input / Output

The method consumes feature vectors (raw tabular features, 512-dim ResNet18 image embeddings, or 768-dim RoBERTa text embeddings), a binary sensitive attribute, and a binary class label during training. It outputs a full posterior distribution over DNN parameters (a 2-hidden-layer ReLU network) and the matching function T, from which predictive class probabilities are obtained by averaging over MCMC samples. Downstream, this yields uncertainty quantification via NLL, Brier score, and ECE, plus a group fairness level measured by the Wasserstein-2 distance between prediction distributions across sensitive groups. The matching function T is a learned permutation mapping individuals from one sensitive group to the other, inferred jointly with the model.

## Method

The core contribution is the matched Gibbs posterior, a proxy distribution defined as ν_M(f,T|λ) ∝ exp(ℓ(f) − λn·Δ_M(f,T))·π(f)·π(T), where the matched deviation Δ_M(θ,T) = E_{X₁}‖f_θ(X₁,1) − f_θ(T(X₁),0)‖² penalizes prediction gaps between matched pairs from different sensitive groups. Two theoretical results anchor the approach: Δ_M upper-bounds the Wasserstein-2 distance for any T (Theorem 4.1), and a bounded total-variation gap guarantees existence of a T with small Δ_M (Theorem 4.2), so controlling λ controls group fairness. For posterior sampling, a Gibbs sampler alternates between HMC for model parameters θ (given T) and a Metropolis-Hastings step for T (given θ), where the MH proposal randomly permutes k matched pairs and the prior on T is an energy-based distribution motivated by bipartite matching. The hyperparameter λ is selected by grid search to maximize the ELBO subject to an average demographic parity constraint, and T is initialized via an optimal-transport map to accelerate convergence.

## Results

Across five real-world datasets (ADULT, DUTCH, CRIME, CELEBA, CIVIL), the matched Gibbs posterior (gibbs_matched) achieves superior Pareto-front trade-offs between group fairness (Δ_W^{1/2}) and all predictive performance metrics (Acc, NLL, Brier, ECE) compared to all baselines. At a fixed fairness level of Δ_W^{1/2} ≈ 0.09 on CRIME, gibbs_matched achieves the best consistency score (Con = 0.837) and accuracy (Acc = 0.736), outperforming gapreg (0.767/0.718), reduction (0.768/0.724), and adv (0.834/0.684). On ADULT at Δ_W^{1/2} ≈ 0.09, gibbs_matched reaches Con = 0.955 with Acc = 0.826, versus 0.927/0.824 for gapreg and 0.954/0.813 for adv. The method also consistently achieves the lowest NLL and Brier scores among all competitors, confirming better uncertainty quantification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three deterministic fair-classification baselines (GapReg, Reduction, Adv) and two Bayesian baselines (mean-field Gaussian VI with MMD, Gibbs posterior with MMD). The matched Gibbs posterior dominates all five competitors on the utility-fairness Pareto front (Acc, NLL, Brier) across all five datasets, with particularly large margins on CELEBA and CIVIL where the deterministic baselines suffer from overfitting as fairness constraints tighten. Against GapReg (Donini et al., 2018) and Reduction (Agarwal et al., 2018), gibbs_matched achieves both higher accuracy and lower uncertainty at every fairness level; for example, on DUTCH at Δ_W^{1/2} ≈ 0.07, gibbs_matched reaches Acc = 0.809 versus 0.785–0.788 for the other two. Against Adv (Zhang et al., 2018), gibbs_matched is clearly superior on Acc, NLL, and Brier, though Adv occasionally shows competitive ECE trade-offs (a known limitation of ECE as a standalone metric). The two MMD-based Bayesian baselines (variational_mmd, gibbs_mmd) are only evaluated on CRIME due to their O(n²) computational cost, and both are substantially outperformed by gibbs_matched on all metrics, confirming that the matched deviation avoids the adversarial learning bottleneck while yielding better posteriors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: Gibbs posterior inference (Bissiri et al., 2016) and fairness-through-matching (Kim et al., 2025a). It repurposes the matched deviation from Kim et al. as a penalty term inside a Gibbs posterior, thereby converting a deterministic fair-classification objective into a Bayesian posterior distribution over model parameters. The energy-based prior on the matching function T is borrowed from Volkovs & Zemel (2012), and the MCMC machinery (HMC for parameters, MH for T) follows standard practice. The key conceptual departure from prior fair-classification work (GapReg, Reduction, Adversarial) is the shift from point estimates to full posterior distributions, enabling uncertainty quantification alongside fairness guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first systematic treatment of Bayesian inference under group-fairness constraints, filling a gap where fair models were treated as deterministic and uncertainty was ignored. The matched deviation's theoretical sandwich between Wasserstein and total variation distances (Theorems 4.1–4.2) provides a principled justification for its use, and the avoidance of adversarial learning makes fair VI computationally feasible at O(n) per update rather than O(n²) for MMD. Open questions include posterior consistency of the matched Gibbs posterior, extension to multinary sensitive attributes, and application to other fairness-aware tasks such as representation learning. A practical limitation is that MCMC sampling (especially for large DNNs) remains more expensive than a single gradient-based training run, which may constrain adoption in very large-scale settings.
