## Problem

The 'Heatmap + MCTS' paradigm for TSP has seen rapid proliferation of increasingly sophisticated learned heatmaps, yet the impact of MCTS configuration on solution quality has been largely overlooked, with most prior works using default or minimally tuned search settings. This paper asks whether the emphasis on heatmap complexity is justified by rigorously isolating and quantifying the contribution of MCTS hyperparameters versus heatmap sophistication, and whether a simple structural prior can rival complex learned models when the search component is properly calibrated.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264767/cropped_page5_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N 2D city coordinates in [0,1]² and produces a TSP tour (city permutation) with associated tour length and optimality gap relative to a reference solution. The GT-Prior heatmap is constructed by ranking each city's neighbors by Euclidean distance and assigning edge probabilities from pre-computed empirical distributions of k-nearest-neighbor selection frequencies in optimal tours, making it parameter-free and scale-independent. MCTS then refines the initial tour via k-opt moves within a fixed time budget (Time_Limit × N seconds), with the best tour encountered returned as the final output.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is an evaluation framework that decouples MCTS configuration effects from heatmap quality effects, combined with a simple parameter-free baseline called GT-Prior. GT-Prior constructs an N×N heatmap by assigning each edge (i,j) a probability equal to the empirical frequency with which the k-th nearest neighbor is selected in optimal tours (where k is j's rank among i's neighbors), derived from solving a small set of instances with Concorde or LKH-3. A streamlined grid-search tuning pipeline optimizes six key MCTS hyperparameters (exploration coefficient α, weight update rate β, max k-opt depth, candidate set size, simulations per move, and heatmap usage flag) independently for each heatmap method and problem scale. SHAP analysis quantifies per-parameter importance, revealing that Max_Candidate_Num and Max_Depth are the most influential parameters, with effects that vary by heatmap type and scale. The key empirical finding is that MCTS tuning alone can swing performance by orders of magnitude (e.g., DIMES on TSP-10000 from 4.86% to 91.31% gap), and that GT-Prior with tuned MCTS achieves gaps of 0.50%/0.85%/2.14% on TSP-500/1000/10000, matching or surpassing complex learned heatmaps like DIFUSCO while requiring zero training time.

## Results

On TSP-10000, GT-Prior (a parameter-free k-nearest-neighbor heatmap) achieves a 2.14% optimality gap, outperforming DIFUSCO (2.37%), SoftDist (2.95%), DIMES (3.06%), and Att-GCN (3.03%) while requiring zero heatmap generation time. On TSP-500 and TSP-1000, GT-Prior achieves 0.50% and 0.85% gaps respectively, competitive with but slightly behind the best learned methods (Fast-T2T at 0.12%/0.65%, DIFUSCO at 0.33%/0.53%). Critically, even the Zero heatmap with tuned MCTS reaches 0.66% on TSP-500, demonstrating that MCTS configuration alone accounts for most of the solution quality. MCTS tuning can swing performance by over 86 percentage points (e.g., DIMES on TSP-10000 ranges from 4.86% to 91.31% gap depending on configuration).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GT-Prior (2.14% gap) outperforms all learned heatmap methods on TSP-10000: DIFUSCO (2.37%), SoftDist (2.95%), Att-GCN (3.03%), DIMES (3.06%), and Fast-T2T (4.22%), while matching their zero heatmap-generation-time advantage. On TSP-500 and TSP-1000, GT-Prior is competitive but trails the best learned methods: Fast-T2T achieves 0.12%/0.65% and DIFUSCO achieves 0.33%/0.53% versus GT-Prior's 0.50%/0.85%. Against the Zero heatmap (no edge guidance), GT-Prior consistently improves by 0.16–1.66 percentage points across scales, confirming the k-NN prior adds meaningful signal. The paper also shows that MCTS tuning dominates: DIMES on TSP-10000 ranges from 4.86% (best-tuned) to 91.31% (worst-tuned) gap, and default settings are often far from optimal. On TSPLIB large instances, GT-Prior achieves the best gap (2.42%) when MCTS is tuned on TSPLIB data, outperforming DIFUSCO (3.47%) and SoftDist (3.03%). Generalization analysis (Table 2) shows GT-Prior degrades by only −0.01% when scaling from TSP-500 to TSP-10000, versus 2.91% degradation for DIFUSCO, highlighting superior cross-scale robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operates entirely within the 'Heatmap + MCTS' paradigm introduced by Fu et al. (2021), which pairs learned edge-probability heatmaps with a Monte Carlo Tree Search refinement loop. Rather than proposing a new solver, it repositions the evaluation axis: prior work (Att-GCN, DIMES, DIFUSCO, UTSP, SoftDist, Fast-T2T) has focused almost exclusively on improving heatmap quality, while MCTS hyperparameters were left at defaults. The paper's conceptual departure is to treat MCTS configuration as a first-class variable and to introduce GT-Prior—a parameter-free k-nearest-neighbor heatmap derived from empirical optimal-tour statistics—as a rigorous baseline against which the added value of learned heatmaps must be measured.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a zero-parameter k-NN prior, paired with tuned MCTS, matches or exceeds diffusion- and GNN-based heatmaps (especially at TSP-10000) challenges the field's implicit assumption that model sophistication drives performance, and the dramatic MCTS sensitivity (4.86% to 91.31% gap for DIMES on TSP-10000) reveals that prior comparisons may have been confounded by uncontrolled search settings. The provided tuning pipeline is a practical tool for fairer future benchmarking. Open questions include whether GT-Prior's advantage persists under different MCTS formulations, whether hybrid priors (combining k-NN structure with learned corrections) could close the remaining gap to the best learned methods at smaller scales, and whether the findings generalize beyond Euclidean 2D TSP to other combinatorial problems.
