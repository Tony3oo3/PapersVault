## Problem

Existing R1-style RL methods for VLMs rely on simple answer-matching rewards that supervise only final outputs, leaving intermediate visual reasoning without explicit guidance. This causes VLMs to develop language shortcuts—bypassing visual perception and relying on text priors—and increases hallucination risk. Prior fixes (human annotations, external model distillation) are costly, introduce latency, or fail to generalize across domains.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image paired with a text question and produces a structured three-part response: a self-contained visual perception description (in <visual_reasoning> tags), a chain-of-thought reasoning trace (in <think> tags), and a final answer (in <answer> tags). The key structural constraint is that the visual perception must be self-contained—sufficient to answer the question without re-accessing the original image—which is validated by a second rollout where the model is re-prompted with only the generated perception and the question.

## Method

Vision-SR1 is a three-stage self-rewarding RL framework built on GRPO. First, the VLM's reasoning is decomposed into a See-Think format where visual perception is explicitly separated from language reasoning. Second, a self-reward mechanism validates visual self-containment by re-prompting the same VLM (no external model) to answer using only the generated visual perception; a reward of 1 is assigned if the correct answer is derived. Third, Multi-Reward Policy Optimization computes separate group-wise z-score advantages, log-probabilities, and KL divergence terms for the visual reward and answer reward, then combines them in a unified actor loss with independent coefficients. This decoupling creates distinct gradient paths from each reward to its corresponding reasoning component, preventing the entanglement that occurs when heterogeneous rewards are simply summed, while introducing no extra GPU overhead beyond standard training.

## Results

With the Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, Vision-SR1 achieves an average score of 52.2 across seven vision-language benchmarks, outperforming the fair-comparison Vision-R1 (50.7) and the zero-shot baseline (41.5). On individual benchmarks, Vision-SR1 reaches 52.2 on MMMU, 69.2 on RealWorldQA, 54.5 on MathVerse, and 68.9 on HallusionBench. The method also generalizes beyond the Qwen-VL family: on Mimo-VL-7B, the average improves from 44.4 (zero-shot) to 49.5, and on the smaller Qwen2.5-VL-3B backbone, Vision-SR1 reaches 48.8 average versus 47.1 for Vision-R1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vision-SR1 consistently outperforms Vision-R1 (re-trained on the same 47K dataset for fair comparison) across all three backbone models: +1.7 average on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (52.2 vs. 50.7), +1.7 on Qwen2.5-VL-3B (48.8 vs. 47.1), and +3.5 on Mimo-VL-7B (49.5 vs. 46.0). Against Perception-R1 (7B, which uses external proprietary LLM annotations for visual rewards), Vision-SR1 achieves a higher average (52.2 vs. 45.2) while requiring no external model or additional GPU. Visionary-R1 (3B, which relies on an external text-only LLM for supervision) trails Vision-SR1 (3B) by 14.9 points in average score (33.9 vs. 48.8). The gains are most pronounced on VisNumBench (+8.2 over Vision-R1 on 7B) and HallusionBench (+2.3), suggesting the self-reward mechanism specifically strengthens visual grounding and reduces hallucination. On MATH-Vision, Vision-SR1 (7B) improves from 33.8 to 36.2, though the original Vision-R1 checkpoint (trained only on math data) still scores higher (51.2), indicating the fair-comparison setting is more conservative.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its RL backbone and adopts the See-Think structured output format (Jia et al., 2024) to decompose VLM responses into visual perception, chain-of-thought reasoning, and answer. It departs from prior R1-style VLM training methods (Vision-R1, Perception-R1, Visionary-R1) that rely solely on answer-matching rewards or external visual supervision (human annotations or distilled labels from proprietary LLMs). The key conceptual departure is a self-rewarding mechanism where the VLM re-prompts itself to verify whether its generated visual description is self-contained, combined with a multi-reward policy optimization that computes separate advantages, log-probabilities, and KL terms for visual and language reasoning rather than summing heterogeneous rewards.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-rewarding approach eliminates the need for external reward models or human annotations, making visual reasoning supervision scalable without additional GPU overhead (only ~20% training time increase vs. doubling with external judges). The proposed Language Shortcut Rate (LSR) metric provides a practical diagnostic for quantifying how often VLMs produce correct answers via language priors rather than genuine visual grounding—a concern raised by prior work but previously unmeasured systematically. However, the performance gains over Vision-R1 are modest (1–3 points on most benchmarks), and the authors themselves acknowledge that some RL-driven math improvements may reflect spurious distributional shifts rather than true visual grounding, leaving open whether the self-reward signal genuinely strengthens perception or merely regularizes the policy.
