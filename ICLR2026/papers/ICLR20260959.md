## Problem

3D point cloud datasets are heterogeneous in sensor modality, scanning density, and semantic ontology, and naively mixing them degrades standard models. Existing multi-dataset approaches (PPT, One-for-All) rely on explicit dataset labels at both training and inference to select dataset-specific parameters, which is impractical at deployment where point clouds arrive from unknown, unlabeled sources. This paper asks whether a single model can learn to specialize across diverse 3D datasets without any dataset identifiers.

## Input / Output

The model takes a raw 3D point cloud (XYZ coordinates, M points) from any sensor (RGB-D, LiDAR, multi-view stereo) and outputs a per-point semantic class label from a unified vocabulary. Because different datasets use incompatible label taxonomies, the classification head projects per-point features into a shared CLIP text-embedding space, enabling supervision via class names (e.g., 'pillow', 'chair') without requiring a fixed closed-set label mapping. No dataset ID or provenance information is needed at inference.

## Method

Point-MoE builds on Point Transformer V3 by replacing the attention output projection W_o in every encoder and decoder block with a sparse Mixture-of-Experts module: N expert MLPs (4 or 8) and a lightweight linear router that selects a top-2 subset of experts per token via sparse softmax. The key architectural insight is placing MoE at W_o rather than the FFN, because W_o is the fusion point where multi-head outputs are recombined, giving experts access to richer head-aggregated geometry cues while keeping W_q, W_k, W_v dense and shared. Training uses mixed-dataset minibatches (indoor + outdoor jointly) with no auxiliary load-balancing loss, BatchNorm, ReLU activations, and no shared experts. The model is trained end-to-end with a CLIP-guided classification head across ScanNet, S3DIS, Structured3D, nuScenes, and SemanticKITTI, and evaluated zero-shot on Matterport3D and Waymo without any dataset labels.

## Results

On indoor-only multi-dataset joint training (ScanNet, Structured3D, S3DIS), Point-MoE-L achieves a state-of-the-art average of 71.5 mIoU without using dataset labels, surpassing PPT-L (67.6 mIoU, which requires dataset labels) and PTv3-L (63.4 mIoU). In the more challenging indoor+outdoor setting (adding nuScenes and SemanticKITTI), Point-MoE-L reaches 70.2 mIoU average, outperforming PPT-L by 2.45 mIoU and PTv3-L by 3.55 mIoU. On zero-shot evaluation, Point-MoE-L achieves 43.9 mIoU on Matterport3D (indoor-only) and 35.0 mIoU average on Matterport3D + Waymo (indoor+outdoor), substantially outperforming PPT-L (27.1 and 20.3 respectively). Point-MoE-L also reduces inference compute by 30.9% (265.7 vs. 384.4 GFLOPs) and peak VRAM by 19.0% (33.3 vs. 41.1 GiB) relative to PPT-L.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Point-MoE-L outperforms all four baselines across both indoor-only and indoor+outdoor settings. Against PTv3-L (the dense transformer backbone), Point-MoE-L gains 8.1 mIoU on indoor-only (71.5 vs. 63.4) and 3.55 mIoU on indoor+outdoor (70.2 vs. 67.2), demonstrating that sparse expert routing adds substantial capacity beyond simply scaling a dense model. Against PPT-L (which uses dataset-specific normalization layers and requires a dataset classifier at inference), Point-MoE-L leads by 3.9 mIoU on seen datasets (71.5 vs. 67.6) and by a dramatic 16.8 mIoU on zero-shot Matterport3D (43.9 vs. 27.1), suggesting that explicit dataset-label supervision makes PPT brittle under distribution shift. Against OA-CNNs (a sparse convolutional backbone), Point-MoE-L leads by 9.0 mIoU (71.5 vs. 62.5). Notably, Point-MoE-L achieves these gains while activating only 59M of its 100M parameters (vs. 98M fully activated in PPT-L), yielding 30.9% lower FLOPs and 19.0% lower peak VRAM at inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mixture-of-Experts paradigm—well-established in NLP (Switch Transformers, GLaM, DeepSeek-MoE) and 2D vision (ViT-MoE)—to 3D point cloud understanding, specifically multi-dataset semantic segmentation. It departs from prior multi-dataset 3D methods like PPT and One-for-All, which rely on dataset-specific normalization layers or adapters and require explicit dataset labels at both training and inference; Point-MoE replaces these with sparse expert routing that discovers dataset structure implicitly. The backbone is Point Transformer V3, with MoE inserted at the attention output projection rather than the FFN, a placement choice motivated by the linearity-merging argument and validated empirically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single label-free MoE model can match or exceed dataset-label-based methods on seen datasets while generalizing better to zero-shot datasets, suggesting that explicit dataset identity is not necessary for multi-domain 3D perception and may even hurt out-of-distribution performance. The encoder-decoder specialization pattern (geometric routing in encoder, semantic routing in decoder) and the finding that removing the load-balancing loss improves results offer practical guidance for future MoE designs in 3D. Open questions include whether this approach scales to more datasets, other 3D tasks (detection, reconstruction), and whether the routing patterns remain coherent at much larger scale; the current evaluation is limited to seven datasets and one primary backbone (PTv3), with OA-CNNs as a secondary check.
