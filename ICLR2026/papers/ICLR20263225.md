## Problem

Existing deep research benchmarks are static (prone to data contamination), domain-specific, ambiguous in scope, or restricted to closed-ended answers, making it impossible to fairly compare agentic systems on realistic, evolving research tasks. Additionally, no comprehensive evaluation framework exists for scoring long-form, citation-grounded reports across multiple quality dimensions simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263225/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds 100 expert-curated, multi-faceted research queries (with {{date}} placeholders ensuring time-varying content) to agentic systems, which produce long-form citation-grounded reports of up to 128K tokens. DeepEval then consumes each generated report and outputs structured scores across six dimensions: binary checklist pass rates (Presentation, Coverage), error-count-based scores (Consistency, Citation Association), pairwise win rates against a baseline (Analysis Depth), and rubric-tree validation results (Citation Accuracy), all averaged over a two-model judge ensemble.

## Method

LiveResearchBench is constructed through a six-stage pipeline: user surveys identify realistic needs, domain experts draft queries, two frontier LLMs generate clarification questions to ensure unambiguity, humans refine scope, GPT-5 decomposes each query into verifiable checklist unit tests, and a five-stage human QC process validates the final dataset. DeepEval pairs each of its six dimensions with a tailored evaluation protocol selected via human-alignment studies—checklist-based (Presentation, Coverage), pointwise additive (Consistency, Citation Association), pairwise with position-swap averaging (Analysis Depth), and an agentic rubric-tree with live web access (Citation Accuracy). To mitigate single-judge inductive bias, all LLM-as-a-Judge evaluations use an ensemble of Gemini 2.5 Pro and GPT-5, whose combined agreement with human experts reaches 92–100% depending on the dimension. The paper then evaluates 17 systems spanning single-agent web search, single-agent deep research, and multi-agent architectures, revealing that multi-agent systems lead on presentation and citation association, single web agents on consistency, and that most systems function as deep searchers rather than deep researchers with genuine analytical synthesis.

## Results

Across 17 agentic systems evaluated on LiveResearchBench, multi-agent deep research systems achieve the highest average score (69.5) compared to single-agent web search (62.8) and single-agent deep research systems. Open Deep Research (w/ GPT-5) attains the best overall average (73.6), followed by GPT-5 (72.7) and Deerflow+ (w/ GPT-5). On individual dimensions, Grok-4 Heavy Deep Research leads Coverage & Comprehensiveness (89.3), Deerflow+ leads Citation Association (77.0), and Gemini 2.5 Pro leads Factual & Logical Consistency (76.5). In analysis depth (pairwise win rate over Open Deep Research), only Deerflow+ and Gemini Deep Research exceed the baseline, indicating most systems function as information collectors rather than deep researchers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Multi-agent deep research systems (Deerflow+, Open Deep Research, Grok-4 Heavy, Manus) outperform both single-agent categories on average (69.5 vs. 62.8 for web search and comparable for deep research), with Open Deep Research (w/ GPT-5) achieving the highest overall score of 73.6. Single-agent web search models lead on Factual & Logical Consistency (69.7 avg; Gemini 2.5 Pro at 76.5), benefiting from a single persistent memory stream that avoids inter-agent logical drift. Multi-agent systems dominate Citation Association (61.9 avg; Deerflow+ at 77.0, Open Deep Research at 76.9), while single-agent deep research models perform worst on this dimension (OpenAI o3 Deep Research at only 25.6), frequently leaving key claims uncited or using mismatched URLs. Grok-4 Heavy Deep Research achieves the highest Coverage & Comprehensiveness (89.3) but barely outperforms the Open Deep Research baseline in analysis depth, illustrating that breadth of retrieval does not translate into analytical insight. In the pairwise analysis depth evaluation, only Deerflow+ and Gemini Deep Research exceed the Open Deep Research baseline, confirming that most frontier systems remain 'deep searchers' rather than 'deep researchers' capable of evidence-grounded, argumentative synthesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against a growing but fragmented landscape of deep research benchmarks (DeepResearch Bench, Deep Research Bench, LiveDRBench, Mind2Web2, ResearcherBench, DeepScholar-Bench), arguing that none simultaneously satisfy user-centricity, dynamism, unambiguity, and multi-faceted search intensity. Methodologically, it builds on the position-swap averaging protocol of Wang et al. (2023) for pairwise evaluation and extends the LLM-as-a-judge paradigm into an agent-ensemble-as-a-judge framework (Gemini 2.5 Pro + GPT-5) with task-specific protocols (checklist-based, pointwise-additive, pairwise, rubric-tree) rather than relying on holistic single-score rubrics. The empirical evaluation spans three architectural families—single-agent web search, single-agent deep research, and multi-agent systems—providing the first systematic cross-architecture comparison under a unified, contamination-resistant protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LiveResearchBench and DeepEval establish a rigorous, dynamic foundation for evaluating deep research that resists data contamination and enables fair cross-system comparison, likely becoming a standard reference for the field. The central finding—that even SoTA systems with extended reasoning and web access function as 'deep searchers' rather than 'deep researchers,' collecting and organizing information without synthesizing genuine analytical insight—challenges the assumption that scaling reasoning depth and retrieval breadth automatically yields better research. Open questions the paper highlights include the need for long-horizon memory with update operations, hierarchical information compression that preserves critical evidence, and explicit synthesis/argumentation modules; no evaluated system simultaneously achieves high coverage, depth, citation reliability, and logical consistency.
