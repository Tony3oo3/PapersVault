## Problem

Sliced Wasserstein distance (SW) estimation requires selecting a finite set of projection directions on the unit sphere, and existing quasi-Monte Carlo (QSW) methods provide data-independent, uniform coverage that cannot exploit the structure of the specific distributions being compared. When SW is evaluated repeatedly inside an optimization loop (gradient flows, registration, autoencoder training), the projection landscape changes at every step, yet no prior work has used adaptive, feedback-driven direction selection. This paper introduces Bayesian optimization (BO) as a principled, sample-efficient mechanism for learning task-adapted projection directions, filling a gap confirmed by a recent survey on sliced OT sampling.

## Input / Output

The method takes two empirical distributions over R^d (3D point clouds of 2048 points from ShapeNet, or RGB images of up to 128×128 pixels treated as n×3 point clouds) and produces a set of L unit-norm projection directions on S^{d-1} (typically L=100, with L=10 also tested). The directions are consumed as a drop-in replacement for the slice set in any existing SW pipeline: they enter the standard finite-direction estimator (Equation 2) without modifying downstream losses, gradients, or optimizer settings. In optimization-in-the-loop settings, the input distributions evolve over time, and the method can periodically relearn directions from the current pair.

## Method

The core mechanism treats the per-slice cost f(θ; μ, ν) = W_p^p(θ_#μ, θ_#ν) as a black-box function on S^{d-1} and fits a Gaussian process surrogate with an angular RBF kernel (geodesic-distance-based) and UCB acquisition (β=0.7). New directions are proposed by maximizing the acquisition over a candidate pool of 4096 uniform sphere samples, selecting batches of b=5 while suppressing near-duplicates (cosine similarity > 0.98). Four variants are introduced: BOSW (one-shot BO from random init), RBOSW (full BO re-run every R=25 optimization steps), ABOSW (seed from a QSW set, then 2 lightweight BO rounds replacing the worst 10% of directions), and ARBOSW (periodically restart ABOSW from fresh QSW seeds). The hybrid variants (ABOSW/ARBOSW) combine QSW's low-discrepancy coverage with BO's task adaptivity, achieving state-of-the-art convergence on point-cloud interpolation and autoencoder tasks while adding only modest runtime overhead (1.5–2× for ARBOSW).

## Results

On the point-cloud interpolation task (L=100, 500 Euler steps), ARBOSW achieves the best final W₂ of 0.003×10², outperforming the strongest randomized QSW baseline RGQSW (0.004×10²) and all deterministic QSW variants (best: DQSW at 0.064×10²), at a moderate runtime of 6.9 s vs. ~4 s for QSW. On the deep point-cloud autoencoder task (ShapeNet Core-55 → ModelNet40, L=100, 400 epochs), ABOSW attains the lowest reconstruction loss (SW₂ = 1.81×10², W₂ = 9.01×10²), surpassing the previously recommended CQSW (SW₂ = 1.84, W₂ = 9.06) and all RQSW variants. RBOSW dominates early in the interpolation trajectory (W₂ = 2.21×10² at step 100 vs. ~5.7 for all baselines) but incurs ~10× runtime overhead (44.6 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the QSW family (Nguyen et al., 2024a), the paper's BO hybrids clearly win on the dynamic point-cloud interpolation task: ARBOSW reaches W₂ = 0.003×10² at step 500, beating the best deterministic QSW (DQSW, 0.064) by over 20× and the best randomized QSW (RGQSW, 0.004) marginally, all at only ~1.7× the runtime of QSW. On the autoencoder task, ABOSW surpasses the previously recommended CQSW (W₂ = 9.01 vs. 9.06 at epoch 400) and all RQSW variants (best RQSW: RREQSW at 9.15), establishing a new state-of-the-art for this benchmark. RBOSW dominates early interpolation steps (W₂ = 2.21 at step 100 vs. ~5.7 for all baselines) but its 44.6 s runtime (~10× QSW) limits practicality. One-shot BOSW trails in dynamic settings (0.088 at step 500) but remains competitive in the stable autoencoder regime (W₂ = 9.10, second only to ABOSW). On image style transfer, ARBOSW matches RCQSW in final W₂ and visual quality, while RBOSW improves over vanilla MCSW but trails the strongest RQSW variants. Overall, the BO methods are complementary to QSW: the hybrid variants (ABOSW/ARBOSW) combine QSW's low-discrepancy seeding with BO's task adaptivity to achieve the best or near-best results across all three tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the QSW/RQSW framework of Nguyen et al. (2024a), which established quasi-Monte Carlo constructions as state-of-the-art for selecting projection directions in sliced Wasserstein distance. The key conceptual departure is replacing data-independent low-discrepancy heuristics with task-adaptive Bayesian Optimization, which exploits the structure of the projection landscape f(θ; μ, ν) to prioritize informative directions. The authors position BO as complementary rather than competitive with QSW: their hybrid methods (ABOSW, ARBOSW) seed from QSW sets and apply lightweight BO refinement, while pure BO variants (BOSW, RBOSW) stand alone. A recent survey (Sisouk et al., 2025) confirms BO had been unexplored for sliced OT, and the paper distinguishes itself from other adaptive SW approaches (control variates, spherical harmonics, repulsive point processes, Markovian SW) by using a sequential, feedback-driven surrogate model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a small number of BO refinement rounds (as few as 10 direction changes out of 100) layered on top of QSW can match or slightly exceed the best QSW baselines in optimization-in-the-loop settings, without modifying downstream losses or gradients. However, the methods are fundamentally biased: Appendix D shows that UCB with constant β drives the estimator toward sup f(θ) rather than E[f(θ)], so convergence to the true SW value is not guaranteed, and no finite-L error bound is established. The task-dependent behavior (refresh variants help in evolving gradient flows but hurt in stable autoencoder training) suggests practitioners must match the BO variant to their specific use case. Open questions include whether annealing β can restore unbiasedness, whether the approach scales to higher dimensions, and whether the modest gains justify the added complexity over simply using RQSW.
