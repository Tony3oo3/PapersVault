## Problem

Teaching multimodal models to perform Visual Chain of Thought (visual CoT)—explicitly generating sketches, diagrams, and visual aids during reasoning—remains an open challenge due to two compounding gaps: (1) current VLMs produce weak or incoherent visual CoT out of the box, making reinforcement learning infeasible, and (2) no large-scale, diverse dataset of interleaved text-image reasoning traces exists for supervised training. Prior datasets are either purely textual CoT, confined to a single task (e.g., visual search), or noisy web-scraped corpora lacking explicit reasoning structure.

## Input / Output

Each training sample takes a question (text prompt plus one or more RGB images, such as a geometry diagram, a 3D scene render, a chess board position, or a maze raster) and produces a structured interleaved reasoning trace: alternating <think> text-reasoning blocks and generated visual tokens (sketches, bounding boxes, zoomed crops, board states, top-down 3D views, molecular structures), concluding with a <answer> block. Traces can contain up to 20 intermediate images; for Bagel fine-tuning, all images are capped at 512 px on the minimum side (~1,024+ visual tokens each), and the model natively switches between text and image generation modes at inference by sampling an extra token after each <im_end> to check for an image-start token.

## Method

ZEBRA-COT is curated through a pipeline that sources real-world problems (MATH geometry, ALFRED embodied planning, RoboMIND robot manipulation, Lichess chess, USPTO-50K chemistry, ImageNet jigsaw) and synthesizes new examples via procedural generation (maze-dataset library, CLEVR-style 3D scene rendering, Stockfish chess engine, networkx graph drawing, RDKit molecular rendering). Frontier VLMs (Gemini-2.5 Pro, GPT-4.1) then enrich raw or templated traces into logically coherent interleaved text-image sequences, with post-processing to filter invalid image references. The resulting 182,384 traces span four categories (scientific, 2D visual, 3D visual, visual logic/games) across 18 subdomains. For training, Anole-7B is fine-tuned end-to-end (8×H200, 10k steps, lr 1e-5), while Bagel-7B is fine-tuned with a modified loss at the image token to enable native interleaved generation (8×H200, 1,000 steps, packed 60k-token sequences, lr 2e-5). A scaffolding evaluation with GPT-5, Claude-4 Sonnet, and Gemini-2.5 Pro demonstrates that providing even one or two multimodal CoT steps yields large accuracy gains (+16 to +25 pts), confirming the value and quality of the visual reasoning traces.

## Results

Fine-tuning Anole-7B on ZEBRA-COT improves in-distribution test-set accuracy from 4.2% to 16.9% (+12 pts, 4× relative gain). Across seven external visual-reasoning benchmarks, Anole-Zebra-CoT achieves an average improvement of 4.9% over the base Anole model, with the largest gain of +13.3 pts on VisuLogic (8.50% → 21.80%) and +5.33 pts on MMVP (10.00% → 15.33%). In a scaffolding experiment, frontier models (GPT-5, Claude-4 Sonnet, Gemini-2.5 Pro) score only 31.51% average zero-shot on ZEBRA-COT tasks, but accuracy rises to 47.99% (+16.48 pts) with one visual CoT step and 56.70% (+25.19 pts) with two steps, with gains up to +43.77 pts on maze tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base Anole model (Chern et al., 2024), Anole-Zebra-CoT wins on all seven benchmarks, with the most dramatic improvement on VisuLogic (+13.30 pts, nearly 3×) and MMVP (+5.33 pts, +53% relative), confirming that interleaved visual CoT training transfers to out-of-distribution visual reasoning. In the scaffolding analysis, the three frontier proprietary models—GPT-5 (41.98%), Claude-4 Sonnet (27.61%), and Gemini-2.5 Pro (24.93%) zero-shot—perform poorly on ZEBRA-COT's challenging tasks, with GPT-5 leading but still below 50%. Providing even one visual CoT step lifts all three models substantially (e.g., Claude-4 Sonnet on mazes: 35.06% → 58.44%; on 2D jigsaw: 21.74% → 36.23%), and two steps push maze accuracy to 94–97%, demonstrating that the visual reasoning traces carry substantial information beyond what text-only CoT provides. A text-only ablation (Table 9) confirms that removing visual aids yields much smaller gains and sometimes degrades performance (e.g., Gemini-2.5 Pro on mazes drops from 59.70% to 23.29% with text-only scaffolding), isolating the visual component as the primary driver of improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual chain-of-thought (visual CoT) line of work, which has been pursued primarily through agentic pipelines with external tools (Visual Sketchpad, ViperGPT) or narrow single-task datasets (Visual CoT for visual search, MM-PhyQA for physics). It departs from these by curating a large-scale, domain-diverse dataset of natively interleaved text-image reasoning traces (182K samples, 18 domains) rather than relying on tool-calling agents, and by fine-tuning autoregressive multimodal models (Anole, Bagel) to generate visual CoT intrinsically without external scaffolding. The work also builds on large-scale interleaved pretraining corpora (OmniCorpus, MINT-1T) but explicitly positions itself as a post-training resource with logical coherence, in contrast to those noisy web-scraped datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ZEBRA-COT is the first dataset to provide diverse, logically coherent interleaved vision-language reasoning traces at scale, filling a critical gap that has hindered both supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning for native visual CoT. The scaffolding experiment (frontier models at 31.5% zero-shot, jumping to 56.7% with two CoT steps) quantifies both the difficulty of these tasks and the value of visual reasoning aids, while the text-only ablation showing performance degradation when images are removed confirms that visual and textual components are genuinely complementary. A notable limitation is that the Bagel fine-tune showed performance drops on some benchmarks due to image-generation hallucinations being fed back into the understanding encoder, highlighting an unresolved challenge for iterative visual reasoning. The authors identify RL with verifiable rewards as the most promising next step, but the current absolute performance (16.9% on the in-distribution test set) suggests substantial headroom remains.
