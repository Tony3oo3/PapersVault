## Problem

Existing earth observation foundation models are constrained by limited spectral diversity, small tile sizes, and RGB-centric ViT designs that cannot natively handle heterogeneous sensor inputs like SAR and multispectral optical imagery. Additionally, the long-tailed distribution of land cover classes in global satellite data causes representation bias toward dominant categories, and prior multimodal approaches rely on separate encoders rather than unified cross-modal fusion within a single backbone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260436/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TerraFM consumes co-registered 534×534 pixel tiles from three Sentinel modalities—Sentinel-1 SAR (2 polarization channels), Sentinel-2 L1C (top-of-atmosphere, up to 13 bands), and Sentinel-2 L2A (bottom-of-atmosphere, up to 13 bands)—which are stochastically assigned to student and teacher networks as natural augmentations during pretraining. The model outputs a 65,536-dimensional feature representation that supports downstream classification (single- or multi-label) and semantic segmentation via kNN, linear probing, UPerNet probing, or full fine-tuning, evaluated at 10–60 m resolution.

## Method

TerraFM builds on a DINO-style student-teacher contrastive framework with three key architectural innovations. First, modality-specific patch embeddings replace the shared ViT projection with per-sensor convolutional layers plus learnable modality embedding vectors, followed by a shared linear projection into a common latent space. Second, a cross-attention fusion module treats co-registered modalities as complementary views: at each spatial position, N_q=5 shared learnable queries attend to modality-specific keys and values, and the resulting intermediate outputs are aggregated via a softmax-weighted mean to produce a single fused token per location, preserving the original sequence length for the shared ViT encoder. Third, a dual-centering mechanism augments the standard DINO global center with a secondary frequency-aware center computed from high-frequency LULC class samples (derived from ESA WorldCover statistics), introducing a soft rebalancing bias that counteracts overrepresentation of dominant land cover categories without requiring supervised labels. The model is pretrained on 18.7M globally distributed tiles (~23T pixels) using multi-crop learning with global (224×224) and local (96×96) views.

## Results

On GEO-Bench with fine-tuning, TerraFM-L (ViT-Large) achieves 98.6% Top-1 on m-EuroSat, 73.1% F1 on m-BigEarthNet, 66.6% Top-1 on m-So2Sat, and 99.0% Top-1 on m-Brick-Kiln, while TerraFM-B reaches 98.1%, 72.6%, 64.9%, and 98.7% respectively. For segmentation via linear probing, TerraFM-L attains 37.0% mIoU on m-Cashew-Plant and 34.6% mIoU on m-SA-Crop-Type. On Copernicus-Bench, TerraFM (ViT-B/16) achieves 99.1 OA on EuroSAT-S2, 87.8 OA on EuroSAT-S1, 84.4 mAP on BigEarthNet-S2, and 67.9 mIoU on Cloud-S2, setting new state-of-the-art on most tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TerraFM consistently outperforms the strongest prior baseline, Galileo (ViT-Base), across all six GEO-Bench tasks: +0.4 on m-EuroSat, +1.9 on m-BigEarthNet, +1.6 on m-So2Sat, +0.0 on m-Brick-Kiln, +1.1 on m-Cashew-Plant, and +2.7 on m-SA-Crop-Type (TerraFM-B vs. Galileo). Notably, TerraFM-B (ViT-Base) surpasses larger ViT-Large models including SatMAE++ (96.5→98.1 on m-EuroSat), DOFA (96.9→98.1), and Prithvi-EO-v2.0 (96.5→98.1), demonstrating superior parameter efficiency. On Copernicus-Bench, TerraFM beats Copernicus-FM on 7 of 8 tasks (e.g., 99.1 vs. 97.9 on EuroSAT-S2, 84.4 vs. 79.0 mAP on BigEarthNet-S2, 67.9 vs. 66.7 mIoU on Cloud-S2), though SoftCon edges it out on BigEarthNet-S1 (78.7 vs. 76.9 mAP). For kNN evaluation (training-free), TerraFM-L leads on m-EuroSat (95.1%), m-BigEarthNet (69.4%), and m-So2Sat (55.9%), while TerraFM-B achieves the best m-Brick-Kiln score (94.5%). On high-resolution transfer tasks (Appendix C.3), TerraFM outperforms Galileo by +7.7 on m-ForestNet (Landsat-8, 15m) and +36.8 mIoU on m-Chesapeake-LandCover (1m), confirming cross-sensor and cross-scale generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

TerraFM builds directly on the DINO self-supervised framework (student-teacher EMA, multi-crop, centering) and adapts it to multisensor EO by introducing three architectural departures from prior remote sensing foundation models: (1) modality-specific patch embeddings that replace the shared convolutional projection in standard ViTs, (2) an encoder-side cross-attention fusion that treats co-registered Sentinel-1/2 modalities as natural augmentations within a single shared backbone (in contrast to separate-encoder designs like CROMA or Galileo, or decoder-side fusion like FusMAE), and (3) a dual-centering mechanism that augments DINO's global centering with a class-frequency-aware secondary center derived from WorldCover statistics. The 534×534 tile size is substantially larger than the 96–224 px tiles used by most prior EO FMs, and the 23.3 T-pixel pretraining corpus exceeds Copernicus-FM's 5.1 T by over 4×, positioning TerraFM as a scale-driven extension of the contrastive-learning line of work in remote sensing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single shared backbone with modality-as-augmentation and lightweight per-location cross-attention can match or exceed multi-encoder designs across classification, segmentation, and change-detection benchmarks, simplifying the architectural recipe for future multisensor EO FMs. The dual-centering mechanism offers a practical, label-free remedy for the long-tailed LULC distribution that plagues global satellite data, with ablations showing consistent gains for underrepresented classes. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-co-registered or temporally offset modalities, whether the 534-px tile size remains optimal at higher resolutions, and whether the cross-attention fusion generalizes to sensors with very different spatial footprints (e.g., MODIS at 250 m alongside Sentinel-2 at 10 m).
