## Problem

Sampling from high-dimensional, multimodal Boltzmann distributions is a central challenge in molecular science, but existing variational methods face a fundamental trade-off: reverse KL minimization causes mode collapse by ignoring low-probability modes, while geometric annealing paths suffer from mass teleportation (abrupt probability shifts to disjoint regions) and depend critically on manually tuned schedules. No prior method simultaneously prevents both failure modes while adapting the annealing schedule automatically.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only pointwise evaluations of the unnormalized Boltzmann density p̃(x) = exp(−E(x)/k_B T) at candidate conformations, where E is a molecular energy function (e.g., from force fields or DFT) and T is the target temperature. No molecular dynamics samples are required. The output is a trained neural spline flow that generates molecular conformation samples in dihedral-angle space (dimensions 60–219 across the four peptide benchmarks). The ELIL tetrapeptide (d=219) is introduced as a new benchmark, representing the largest system studied to date under the energy-evaluation-only setting.

## Method

CMT constructs a sequence of intermediate densities (q_i) bridging a tractable prior q_0 to the target p by iteratively solving a variational problem with two simultaneous constraints: a trust-region bound on KL(q‖q_i) that prevents mass teleportation and yields an automatic annealing schedule, and an entropy-decay bound H(q_i)−H(q) ≤ ε_ent that prevents premature convergence and mode collapse. The analytical solution (Proposition 2.3) shows the optimal intermediate takes the form q_{i+1} ∝ q_i^{λ/(1+λ+η)} p̃^{1/(1+λ+η)}, defining a geometric-tempered annealing path parameterized by Lagrangian multipliers (λ, η) solved via a 2D convex dual optimization. Each intermediate is approximated by a neural spline flow trained with importance-weighted forward KL divergence, which encourages mode coverage and reuses samples from the previous step. The trust-region constraint additionally bounds the variance of importance weights independently of dimension, ensuring scalability to high-dimensional molecular systems.

## Results

CMT achieves the best EUBO, ESS, and RAM TV across all four molecular systems (alanine dipeptide d=60, alanine tetrapeptide d=120, alanine hexapeptide d=180, ELIL tetrapeptide d=219) while requiring the same or fewer target evaluations than baselines. The advantage grows with system size: on the ELIL tetrapeptide, CMT attains 26.06% ESS versus 13.75% for TA-BG and 7.21% for FAB (≈1.9× and ≈3.6× higher), and on alanine hexapeptide it reaches 29.63% ESS versus 18.22% (TA-BG) and 14.55% (FAB). CMT also achieves the lowest RAM TV on every system, confirming superior mode coverage without the mode collapse that plagues reverse KL training (e.g., EUBO gap of −262.34 vs. −277.83 on ELIL tetrapeptide).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMT is compared against two state-of-the-art variational baselines—FAB (Midgley et al., 2022) and TA-BG (Schopmans & Friederich, 2025)—as well as reference forward KL and reverse KL training. CMT outperforms both FAB and TA-BG on every metric (EUBO, ESS, RAM TV) across all four systems, with the margin widening substantially for larger molecules: on the ELIL tetrapeptide, CMT's ESS of 26.06% is roughly 1.9× that of TA-BG (13.75%) and 3.6× that of FAB (7.21%), while its EUBO (−277.83) is the best by a clear margin. Against reverse KL, CMT avoids the severe mode collapse that causes reverse KL's EUBO to degrade by over 15 nats on ELIL tetrapeptide (−262.34 vs. −277.83) and its RAM TV to balloon to 2.61×10⁻¹. Against forward KL (which requires MD samples and 4.2×10⁹ target evaluations), CMT matches or exceeds EUBO while using 50–500× fewer target evaluations and achieving substantially higher ESS (e.g., 26.06% vs. 5.85% on ELIL tetrapeptide). The ablation study further confirms that both the trust-region and entropy constraints are necessary: removing either leads to mode collapse or unstable training on the larger systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the trust-region constraint from reinforcement learning (Schulman et al., 2015) and the entropy regularization idea from policy optimization into the setting of variational sampling, building on the first explicit connection between trust-region optimization and geometric annealing paths established by Blessing et al. (2025) for path-space measures. It departs from standard geometric annealing (Neal, 2001) and its modern instantiations (FAB, TA-BG) by introducing a second constraint on entropy decay, yielding a novel 'geometric-tempered' annealing path that provably avoids both mass teleportation and premature convergence. The practical instantiation uses importance-weighted forward KL with neural spline flows, extending the variational BG framework of Noe et al. (2019) with an adaptive, constraint-driven schedule that eliminates the need for manual annealing schedule tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CMT provides a principled, automatically-tuned alternative to hand-designed annealing schedules for molecular Boltzmann generators, achieving over 2.5× higher effective sample size while avoiding mode collapse across systems up to d=219. The introduction of the ELIL tetrapeptide as the largest benchmark studied purely from energy evaluations (without MD samples) is a practical contribution for the community. A key limitation is the large number of gradient updates required per intermediate target, which the authors flag as a scalability concern; future work on more efficient loss functions (e.g., log-variance loss) and Cartesian-coordinate representations for cross-system transferability are natural next steps.
