## Problem

Diffusion Q-Learning (DQL) relies on multi-step denoising for action generation, making both training and inference slow and fragile due to backpropagation-through-time over long stochastic chains. Existing acceleration strategies—efficient solvers, policy distillation, or auxiliary IQL-based policies—introduce additional complexity, multi-phase training, or performance trade-offs. The paper asks whether a one-step policy can be trained directly within the actor-critic framework without any of these compromises.

## Input / Output

The policy network u_θ takes as input the concatenation of a noisy action latent a_t, the state vector s, and sinusoidal positional embeddings for two timestep variables (current t and target r), each of size 64. It outputs a single action vector a = ε − u_θ(ε, r=0, t=1; s) in one forward pass, eliminating iterative ODE integration. The architecture is a 3-layer MLP with 256 hidden units and Mish activations, identical to DQL's policy except for the additional r-embedding input. In the visual-observation ablation, 64×64×3 RGB images are first encoded by a small IMPALA encoder before concatenation with the noise vector.

## Method

OFQL reformulates DQL's DDPM policy under the Flow Matching paradigm but departs from conventional FM by learning an average velocity field u(a_t, r, t; s) — the time-integrated displacement between any two steps — rather than the marginal (instantaneous) velocity v(a_t, t; s). The key insight is that the marginal velocity field inherently produces curved trajectories that prevent reliable one-step generation, whereas the average velocity directly encodes the net displacement, enabling exact one-step endpoint mapping a = ε − u_θ(ε, 0, 1; s). To make training tractable without explicit integration, the paper uses the MeanFlow Identity u = v − (t−r)·du/dt, where the total derivative is computed via Jacobian-vector products, and replaces the true instantaneous velocity with the conditional velocity v_t = ε − a following standard CFM. A flow ratio λ (default 0.5) controls the probability that t=r during sampling, biasing training toward instantaneous-velocity learning to bootstrap average-velocity estimation. The actor-critic losses are inherited from DQL/TD3+BC, with the behavior-cloning term replaced by the average-velocity matching loss (FBC) and all action sampling reduced to a single differentiable forward pass, eliminating BPTT entirely.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark, OFQL achieves an average normalized score of 92.5 on MuJoCo locomotion tasks (vs. 87.9 for DQL), 84.6 on AntMaze navigation (vs. 64.6 for DQL), and 67.0 on Kitchen manipulation (vs. 61.6 for DQL), outperforming all baselines across every domain. In terms of efficiency, OFQL trains in 6.3 hours over 1M steps (vs. 11.7 h for 5-step DQL and 49.5 h for 50-step DQL) and achieves an inference decision frequency of 846.5 Hz (vs. 238.7 Hz for 5-step DQL). On the Adroit high-dimensional manipulation benchmark, OFQL scores 79.5 on adroit-pen-human and 62.3 on adroit-pen-cloned, surpassing DQL (75.7 and 60.8 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OFQL outperforms all eight baselines on aggregate scores across every D4RL domain. Against the primary multi-step diffusion baseline DQL (Wang et al., 2022), OFQL improves MuJoCo average from 87.9 to 92.5 (+4.6), AntMaze from 64.6 to 84.6 (+20.0), and Kitchen from 61.6 to 67.0 (+5.4), while also training ~2× faster and inferring ~3.5× faster. Against the one-step distillation-based FQL (Park et al., 2025), OFQL leads by +13.3 on MuJoCo (92.5 vs. 79.2), +5.6 on AntMaze (84.6 vs. 79.0), and +13.9 on Kitchen (67.0 vs. 53.1), with the Kitchen gap attributed to FQL's one-step policy lacking sufficient expressivity. Among multi-step diffusion policies, EDP (Kang et al., 2023) and IDQL (Hansen-Estruch et al., 2023) both underperform DQL on MuJoCo (82.4 and 84.1 vs. 87.9), and OFQL surpasses them by 10.1 and 8.4 points respectively. Non-diffusion baselines (TD3-BC, IQL) and diffusion planners (Diffuser, Decision Diffuser) trail OFQL by 17–41 points on MuJoCo and by even wider margins on AntMaze, where Q-learning signals are critical. The only task where OFQL does not achieve the top score is Hopper-Medium-Expert (110.2 vs. DQL's 111.1) and AntMaze-Large-Diverse (76.1 vs. FQL's 83.0), though these are within reported standard deviations.

## Relation to Prior Work

OFQL directly extends DQL (Wang et al., 2022) by replacing its DDPM-based diffusion policy with a Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2022) formulation, while retaining the TD3+BC actor-critic loss structure. The key conceptual departure is learning an average velocity field (via the MeanFlow Identity from Geng et al., 2025) rather than the standard marginal/instantaneous velocity, which enables accurate one-step action generation without distillation or auxiliary policies. This positions OFQL against both multi-step diffusion policies (DQL, IDQL, EDP) and distillation-based one-step approaches (FQL, Chen et al.), offering a simpler single-phase training pipeline that avoids backpropagation-through-time entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OFQL provides a practical, drop-in replacement for DQL's diffusion policy that is simultaneously faster (6.3h vs 11.7h training; 846 Hz vs 239 Hz inference) and more accurate, making it a strong candidate for real-time control and latency-sensitive deployment. The average-velocity formulation is a clean theoretical contribution with potential applicability beyond RL to any setting where one-step generative sampling from flow models is desired. Key limitations include weaker performance in high-dimensional visual observation settings (OGBench), where tight coupling between encoder, critic, and flow learning remains unresolved; extending to online RL, goal-conditioned, and multi-task settings are identified as open directions.
