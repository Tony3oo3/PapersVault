## Problem

Standard Top-k+Softmax routing in Mixture-of-Experts models conflates two distinct decisions—expert selection and expert contribution—into a single non-differentiable operation, requiring auxiliary losses, temperature tuning, or straight-through estimators to enforce sparsity and stabilize training. This entanglement ties load balancing to mixture calibration through a single temperature parameter, producing uneven expert utilization and limiting interpretability of how probability mass is allocated among active experts.

## Input / Output

Given a token embedding x ∈ R^d, the router produces a routing vector r(x) ∈ Δ^{E-1} (a probability simplex over E experts) computed as the normalized Hadamard product of a relaxed binary selection mask z̃ ∈ (0,1)^E and a Dirichlet-sampled contribution vector θ. The MoE layer output is the weighted sum y(x) = Σᵢ rᵢ(x) Eᵢ(x), where only k ≪ E experts are expected to be active per token, controlled by a sparsity penalty on the expected number of active experts.

## Method

DirMoE factorizes routing into a spike-and-slab variational model: expert selection is modeled by a Bernoulli component relaxed via Gumbel-Sigmoid (Binary-Concrete), while expert contribution among selected experts is modeled by a Dirichlet distribution sampled via implicit reparameterization, making the entire forward pass end-to-end differentiable. The training objective is a β-weighted variational ELBO combining a reconstruction term, a Dirichlet KL divergence between posterior and prior, and a direct sparsity penalty (Σᵢ z̃ᵢ − k)² that controls the expected number of active experts. A key theoretical contribution is the Dirichlet concentration scale λ, which acts as a calibrated sparsity knob: the expected Simpson index of the routing distribution is proven to be strictly monotonically decreasing in λ, and the Beta marginal property of Dirichlet distributions allows exact calibration of the expected probability mass m on the k active experts via a one-parameter ratio r = α_hi/α_lo. Training uses a temperature schedule (τ_z decaying from exploratory to near-binary) and a Dirichlet-parameter schedule that tightens concentration over time, guiding the model from exploration to decisive routing.

## Results

DirMoE achieves the highest average zero-shot accuracy of 41.13% across seven benchmarks (ARC-C, ARC-E, BoolQ, HellaSwag, LAMBADA, PIQA, RACE), outperforming the closest competitor ReMoE (40.03%) by 1.1 points. DirMoE leads on five of seven individual tasks, with the largest margins on BoolQ (61.52% vs. 60.21% for Expert Choice) and LAMBADA (36.44% vs. 35.94% for ReMoE). Training efficiency is preserved: DirMoE incurs less than 1% overhead over vanilla Switch MoE (437.3 ms vs. 431.5 ms iteration time, 137.2 vs. 138.2 TFLOP/s/GPU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DirMoE outperforms all six baselines on average zero-shot accuracy (41.13% vs. 40.03% for ReMoE, 39.47% for SwitchMoE, 38.79% for Hash, 38.53% for Expert Choice, 38.39% for SparseMixer-v2, and 37.70% for Lory). The closest competitor, ReMoE, edges out DirMoE on HellaSwag (30.26% vs. 29.93%) and ARC-E (46.68% vs. 46.20%), but DirMoE wins decisively on BoolQ (61.52% vs. 54.16%, a 7.4-point gap) and LAMBADA (36.44% vs. 35.94%). Against the standard Top-1 Switch MoE baseline, DirMoE gains 1.66 points on average, with the largest improvement on LAMBADA (+3.47 points) and RACE (+1.56 points). SparseMixer-v2 achieves the best ARC-E score (46.72%) but trails DirMoE by 2.74 points on average, largely due to weak BoolQ (45.96%) and LAMBADA (34.12%) performance. Lory, despite being fully differentiable like DirMoE, lags by 3.43 points on average, suggesting that the Dirichlet-based factorization of selection and contribution provides a meaningful advantage over segment-routing approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

DirMoE extends the line of differentiable MoE routers (Soft-MoE, ReMoE, Lory) that replaced non-differentiable Top-k+Softmax with continuous gates, but departs from them by grounding the router in a Dirichlet variational autoencoder with a spike-and-slab prior. This cleanly factorizes routing into a Bernoulli selection component (relaxed via Gumbel-Sigmoid, building on Maddison et al. 2017) and a Dirichlet contribution component (sampled via implicit reparameterization, Figurnov et al. 2018), rather than relying on a single Softmax or ReLU gate that conflates both decisions. The calibrated sparsity knob via the Dirichlet concentration parameter λ, linked to the Simpson index through a monotonicity theorem, is a novel theoretical contribution not present in prior differentiable MoE work, which typically requires auxiliary balancing or sparsity losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The disentangled selection/contribution design offers interpretable, calibrated control over expert sparsity without auxiliary load-balancing losses, and the theoretical guarantee that expected Simpson index is strictly monotone in λ provides a principled tuning knob absent from prior methods. However, all experiments are conducted on a 185M-parameter LLaMA trained on a single 30B-token dataset, leaving open whether the probabilistic routing advantage persists at the multi-billion-parameter scale where most MoE deployments occur. The modest zero-shot gains over ReMoE (~1 point average) and the reliance on dropless kernels for stability suggest the approach's practical impact at scale remains to be validated. Future work on robustness and monosemantic expert specialization, as the authors note, could strengthen the interpretability argument.
