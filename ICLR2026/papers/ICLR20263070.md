## Problem

Camera ISP pipelines introduce per-view photometric inconsistencies (exposure, white balance, color shifts) that violate multi-view consistency and degrade 3D reconstruction quality. Existing remedies either tightly couple per-view appearance embeddings to scene-specific optimization (doubling or more training time) or apply 2D image correction independently per frame, lacking cross-view coherence. No generalizable, feed-forward method existed that could harmonize hundreds of views in a single pass while remaining compatible with fast 3D reconstruction pipelines.

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of N multi-view RGB frames (resized to 224×224, patchified at 8×8) of a static scene along with an automatically selected reference frame, and outputs per-frame 3D bilateral grids of shape H_s×W_s×D×12 (encoding local 3×4 affine color transforms) plus low-resolution confidence grids of shape H_s×W_s×D×1. Slicing these grids against the original high-resolution images yields full-resolution harmonized frames and per-pixel confidence maps. The entire sequence is processed jointly in one forward pass, handling 300+ frames without per-scene optimization.

## Method

CHROMA is a 137M-parameter transformer that predicts spatially-varying bilateral grid parameters per patch, exploiting the structural analogy between transformer patch tokens and bilateral grid vertices. The encoder uses three layers of alternating frame-wise and global self-attention (following VGGT's strategy) to model intra- and cross-view interactions, while the decoder replaces global attention with cross-attention that conditions each source frame on the reference frame's features. A small MLP head per token predicts the D×12 grid-vertex affine parameters and a D×1 confidence vector, which are then sliced to full resolution. Training combines a probabilistic L1 loss with aleatoric uncertainty on synthetic ISP-perturbed DL3DV pairs, a total-variation regularizer on grid smoothness, and a self-supervised rendering consistency loss that rasterizes Gaussians predicted by AnySplat back to the reference viewpoint and compares VGG features against the original reference, enabling learning from unpaired real data (WildRGB-D). A weighted DINOv2-semantic and CIE-LAB-photometric scoring scheme automatically selects the most representative reference frame.

## Results

On the DL3DV ISP-variation dataset, CHROMA+DashGS achieves 26.45 PSNR and 28.92 PSNR_CC, outperforming the best baseline (DashGS alone at 23.35/28.17) and all appearance-embedding methods (e.g., WildGaussians at 18.15/24.08) while matching their training time (~3.5 min vs. 2+ hours for WildGaussians). On MipNeRF360-VE (exposure variation), CHROMA+2DGS reaches 26.37 PSNR_CC, surpassing GS-W (25.81) and Luminance-GS (23.12) by 0.56 and 3.25 dB respectively. On the real-world BilaRF dataset, CHROMA+DashGS attains 26.25 PSNR_CC and 0.2158 LPIPS_CC, beating the strongest baseline GS-W (24.94/0.2764) by 1.31 dB in PSNR_CC and 0.06 in LPIPS_CC, with inference in under 4 minutes versus 40+ minutes for GS-W.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHROMA consistently outperforms all scene-specific appearance-embedding baselines across all three datasets. On DL3DV, CHROMA+DashGS (28.92 PSNR_CC) beats GS-W (26.29), Luminance-GS (26.14), and WildGaussians (24.08) by 2.63, 2.78, and 4.84 dB respectively, while running 10–37× faster (3m27s vs. 35m–2h10m). On MipNeRF360-VE, CHROMA+2DGS (26.37 PSNR_CC) surpasses GS-W (25.81) and Luminance-GS (23.12), and on BilaRF, CHROMA+DashGS (26.25 PSNR_CC, 0.2158 LPIPS_CC) exceeds 3DGS-4DBAG (24.90, 0.256) and GS-W (24.94, 0.2764). Against 2D exposure-correction methods (CoTF, MSEC, MSLTNet, UEC) on MipNeRF360-VE, CHROMA achieves the best PSNR_CC (26.25 vs. UEC's 25.98) and superior temporal consistency (motion smoothness 0.8707 vs. 0.8415; temporal flickering 0.8428 vs. 0.8124), confirming that multi-view-aware harmonization is critical for downstream 3D reconstruction. The vanilla 3D baselines (2DGS, 3DGS, DashGS) without CHROMA show substantially lower PSNR under ISP variation (e.g., DashGS 23.35 vs. DashGS+Ours 26.45 on DL3DV), validating that the harmonization step is the primary source of improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bilateral grid representation (Chen et al., 2007) from single-image 2D processing to a multi-view, spatio-temporal setting, and adopts the alternating frame-wise/global attention architecture from VGGT (Wang et al., 2025) to model cross-view interactions efficiently. It departs from the dominant paradigm of jointly optimizing per-view appearance embeddings with 3D geometry (WildGaussians, GS-W, Luminance-GS, 3DGS-4DBAG) by decoupling harmonization into a standalone feed-forward pre-processing step. The self-supervised training signal, which uses a pretrained feed-forward 3D reconstruction model (AnySplat) as a rendering-based pretext task, is a novel training strategy that addresses the infeasibility of collecting paired real-world appearance data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By decoupling appearance harmonization from scene optimization, CHROMA provides a plug-and-play module that integrates into 3DGS, 2DGS, and DashGS pipelines without increasing per-scene training cost, making it practically attractive for large-scale reconstruction workflows. The self-supervised loss leveraging 3D foundation models offers a general recipe for training on unpaired real data, potentially transferable to other vision tasks lacking paired supervision. Key limitations include the inability to model specular highlights and reflections (bilateral grids are inherently smooth), and the requirement for a dedicated trained network before reconstruction. Open questions include extension to fully in-the-wild settings with transient objects and integration with larger feed-forward reconstruction frameworks.
