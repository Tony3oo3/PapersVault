## Problem

Diffusion-based image restoration models frequently produce plausible yet incorrect text-like patterns in degraded regions—a phenomenon the authors term text-image hallucination—because they rely on generative priors that synthesize rather than reconstruct exact characters. No existing benchmark jointly addresses perceptual restoration quality and textual fidelity, and prior scene text super-resolution methods operate only on cropped single-word patches, discarding global scene context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262294/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 low-quality RGB image (degraded via the Real-ESRGAN pipeline) and produces a 512×512 high-quality restored image alongside text detection polygons (16 control points each) and recognized character sequences (up to 25 characters per instance). During inference, the text-spotting module's intermediate predictions at each denoising timestep are reformatted into descriptive text prompts that condition the subsequent denoising step, creating a closed-loop feedback between text recognition and image restoration.

## Method

TeReDiff integrates a TESTR-based text-spotting module into a DiffBIR-style diffusion restoration framework (U-Net + ControlNet) by feeding multi-scale features extracted from four decoder blocks of the diffusion U-Net directly into the text-spotting encoder, replacing the conventional ResNet backbone. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) the restoration module is trained with diffusion loss while the text-spotting module is frozen, (2) the text-spotting module is trained with bipartite-matched detection and recognition losses on the frozen diffusion features, and (3) both modules are jointly optimized with a weighted combination of diffusion and text-spotting losses, allowing gradients from text supervision to shape the diffusion features. At inference, the text-spotting module's per-timestep polygon-character predictions are formatted into descriptive prompts (e.g., 'A realistic scene where the texts X, Y appear clearly on signs...') that condition the U-Net through ControlNet, progressively refining text fidelity as denoising proceeds. The authors demonstrate that diffusion features outperform ResNet-50 features for text-spotting, particularly under limited data, and that descriptive-style prompts yield greater gains than simple tag-style prompts.

## Results

On the SA-Text benchmark (Level 2 degradation), TeReDiff achieves a detection F1-score of 65.75 and end-to-end recognition F1 of 35.13 (Full lexicon) using TESTR, outperforming the strongest baseline FaithDiff (63.31 / 28.56) by +2.44 and +6.57 points respectively. On the established TextZoom STISR benchmark, TeReDiff sets a new SOTA with 85.0% average recognition accuracy (MORAN), surpassing the previous best TextSR (64.0%) by 21 points. On Real-Text, TeReDiff reaches 74.89 detection F1 and 56.45 end-to-end F1 (Full), again leading all baselines. Image quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID) remain competitive with the DiffBIR baseline, confirming that text-aware training does not degrade overall restoration quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TeReDiff outperforms all 12 baselines on every degradation level and both text-spotting evaluators (ABCNet v2 and TESTR). Among diffusion-based restoration methods, FaithDiff is the closest competitor (63.31 det F1, 28.56 E2E Full at Level 2), but TeReDiff leads by +2.44 and +6.57 points respectively; SeeSR and DiffBIR trail by 1.76 and 3.93 detection F1 points. Non-diffusion baselines (Real-ESRGAN, SwinIR, ResShift) are consistently 10–20 points behind on detection F1 and 5–10 points behind on recognition, with SUPIR performing worst overall (47.84 det F1 at Level 2). On the TextZoom STISR benchmark, TeReDiff (85.0% MORAN avg) surpasses the previous SOTA TextSR (64.0%) by 21 points, and leads TextDiff, TCDM, and DCDM by 21–22 points, demonstrating that full-scene training with text-aware conditioning generalizes far better than patch-level STISR approaches. On Real-Text, TeReDiff leads FaithDiff by +4.32 detection F1 and +8.48 E2E Full, confirming gains on real-world degradations beyond synthetic ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two established lines: diffusion-based image restoration (DiffBIR, StableSR, SeeSR, SUPIR) and transformer-based text-spotting (TESTR, ABCNet). It departs from prior diffusion-based restoration by identifying and addressing 'text-image hallucination'—the tendency of generative priors to synthesize plausible but incorrect text—and from STISR methods (TextDiff, TCDM, DCDM, TextSR) by operating on full-scene images rather than cropped word patches. The key architectural departure is forwarding intermediate U-Net decoder features into a text-spotting module during training, then using the recognized text as conditioning prompts for subsequent denoising steps, creating a closed loop between text understanding and image generation that neither prior line of work attempts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TAIR addresses a practical gap where even slight text distortion compounds into significant information loss in document digitization, street-sign understanding, and AR navigation. SA-Text (100K images) is the first benchmark jointly evaluating perceptual restoration quality and text fidelity, filling a clear dataset gap. However, the evaluation relies on pretrained text-spotting models as proxies for legibility, and standard image-quality metrics (PSNR, FID, LPIPS) do not directly capture text-image hallucination, leaving open how to measure text fidelity more intrinsically. The VLM-based curation pipeline is language-agnostic in principle but demonstrated primarily in English, and whether the text-conditioning strategy generalizes to other structured content (math, code, symbols) remains unexplored.
