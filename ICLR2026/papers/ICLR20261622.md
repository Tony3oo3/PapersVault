## Problem

Recent continual learning methods leverage Neural Collapse by predefining a fixed global ETF as the feature learning target, but this requires knowing the total number of classes in advance, forces class means toward closely spaced vertices when the class count is large, and contradicts the emergent nature of NC during training. This paper asks how to dynamically adapt the ETF target so that it always matches the number of classes seen so far, achieving maximal and equal separability at every stage of CL without global knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261622/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10/100) or 64×64 (TinyImageNet) through a ResNet-18 backbone and outputs a class label via cosine similarity between the normalized last-layer feature and the vertices of the current ETF target, rather than a standard linear classifier. During training, each minibatch mixes current-task data with replay data from a buffer (200 or 500 samples), and the model must classify across all previously seen classes in a single unified classifier (Class-IL) or per-task classifiers (Task-IL).

## Method

ProNC progressively expands the ETF target: after the first task, the nearest ETF to the learned class means is extracted via SVD (Theorem 1); before each subsequent task, the orthogonal basis is expanded by appending new vectors via Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization, and the ETF is reconstructed from the enlarged basis, ensuring old vertices shift minimally while new vertices achieve maximal equiangular separation. Training for tasks t≥2 uses three losses: standard cross-entropy for intra-task classification, an alignment loss that pushes each sample's normalized feature toward its corresponding ETF vertex (minimizing intra-class variance and enforcing equal inter-class separation), and a cosine-similarity distillation loss that anchors old-class features to the previous model's outputs to further reduce forgetting. At inference, classification reduces to argmax over cosine similarities between the test feature and all ETF vertices, replacing the conventional linear head.

## Results

On Seq-CIFAR-100 (buffer 200), ProNC achieves 42.99% FAA (36.07% FF) for Class-IL and 85.63% FAA (4.40% FF) for Task-IL, outperforming the best baselines (STAR at 38.15% and 79.53% respectively). On Seq-TinyImageNet (buffer 200), ProNC reaches 27.44% FAA for Class-IL and 69.09% FAA for Task-IL, surpassing the best baselines (CSReL at 18.14% and NCT at 52.71%) by 59.32% and 31.08% respectively. Notably, even with an empty replay buffer, ProNC achieves 32.28% FAA (Class-IL) and 84.62% FAA (Task-IL) on Seq-CIFAR-100, nearly matching or exceeding baselines that use 200-sample buffers. Training time per epoch is 2.91s, comparable to other replay-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProNC achieves the best FAA in 10 out of 12 settings, with the only exceptions being Seq-CIFAR-10 Class-IL where LODE edges ahead (68.01 vs. 65.58 at buffer 200; 75.91 vs. 73.95 at buffer 500). On the larger datasets, ProNC dominates: on Seq-CIFAR-100 Class-IL (buffer 200) it beats STAR (38.15) by 4.84 points and NCT (26.38) by 16.61 points; on Seq-TinyImageNet Task-IL it surpasses NCT (52.71) by 16.38 points and CSReL (45.04) by 24.05 points. Compared to the closest NC-based method NCT, ProNC wins on 10/12 settings for FAA and 8/12 for FF, confirming that progressive ETF expansion is superior to a fixed global ETF. Against replay-based baselines (ER, iCaRL, DER, DER++, GEM, GSS), ProNC consistently leads on CIFAR-100 and TinyImageNet by 5–20+ points in FAA. Against contrastive-learning baselines (Co²L, CILA, MNC³L), ProNC achieves substantially higher accuracy with lower training time (2.91s/epoch vs. 2.52–2.82s/epoch for contrastive methods that require 500 initial epochs). ProNC also outperforms STAR and LODE on all larger-dataset settings, and uniquely works without any replay buffer, achieving 84.62% FAA on Seq-CIFAR-100 Task-IL with zero memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Neural Collapse phenomenon (Papyan et al., 2020) and its recent application to continual learning via NCT (Yang et al., 2023b), which predefines a fixed global ETF as the feature learning target. The key departure is replacing this fixed global ETF with a progressively expanding one: the ETF is initialized from the first task's learned features (via a nearest-ETF theorem) and then expanded by appending new orthogonal basis vectors before each new task, ensuring the number of vertices always matches the number of seen classes. This addresses three identified flaws of the fixed-ETF approach—impracticality (requiring knowledge of total class count), degraded separation when K is large, and contradiction with the emergent nature of NC. The resulting ProNC is positioned as a general feature regularization that can be plugged into diverse CL frameworks (ER, iCaRL, DER++, LUCIR, XDER), rather than a standalone method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is that ProNC achieves strong performance even with zero replay buffer, suggesting the progressive ETF target itself provides sufficient geometric structure to mitigate forgetting without stored data—this could be important for memory-constrained or privacy-sensitive CL deployments. The method is simple (three loss terms, no architectural changes), efficient (comparable per-epoch time to baselines), and broadly applicable as a plug-in regularization. A stated limitation is the assumption of clear task boundaries, and it remains open how ProNC extends to task-agnostic or domain-incremental settings. The progressive ETF construction also opens a broader question of whether other geometric targets (beyond simplex ETFs) could be progressively adapted for CL.
