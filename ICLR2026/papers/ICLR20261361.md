## Problem

Existing closed-domain hallucination detection methods evaluate only the final output against source material, which is insufficient for multi-generative-step (MGS) processes where intermediate outputs are produced. Detection alone cannot provide traceability—users need to know where in the pipeline hallucination was introduced (error localization) and how faithful content was derived from source through intermediate steps (provenance). No prior method accounts for the full structure of the generative process to achieve both detection and traceability.

## Input / Output

VeriTrail takes a DAG representing the completed generative process (nodes are text spans, edges encode input-output relationships; can exceed 100K nodes for GraphRAG), a set of factual claims extracted from the terminal node via Claimify, and a hyperparameter q controlling termination after consecutive unsupported verdicts. For each claim it produces a final three-way verdict (Fully Supported / Not Fully Supported / Inconclusive) with LM reasoning, a full evidence trail of selected sentences tagged with node IDs plus generated summaries from every iteration, all interim verdicts, and—when the claim is unfaithful—the specific stage(s) where hallucination was likely introduced.

## Method

VeriTrail models the generative process as a DAG and, for each claim, iteratively traverses it from the terminal node toward root nodes through four sub-steps: (1) sub-claim decomposition via Claimify, (2) evidence selection where an LM picks sentences from candidate nodes that strongly imply the claim's truth or falsehood, (3) verdict generation where an LM assigns a three-way label based on the accumulated evidence, and (4) candidate node selection that determines the next set of nodes to inspect based on the latest verdict. The process terminates when only previously verified root nodes remain, no candidate nodes exist, or q consecutive 'Not Fully Supported' verdicts are reached. For traceability, the evidence trail documents a path through intermediate nodes to source material (provenance), while error localization identifies the stage of the last iteration that received a 'Fully Supported' verdict before the final 'Not Fully Supported' verdict, pinpointing where unsupported content was introduced. This backward-traversal design avoids the prohibitive cost of checking the final output against every intermediate output individually and handles cases where the final claim is supported only by a combination of intermediate outputs.

## Results

On FABLES+, VeriTrail (q=3) achieves 84.5% macro F1 and 83.6% balanced accuracy, while VeriTrail (q=1) achieves 74.0% macro F1 and 84.6% balanced accuracy. On DiverseSumm+, VeriTrail (q=3) reaches 79.5% macro F1 and 76.3% balanced accuracy, and VeriTrail (q=1) reaches 76.6% macro F1 and 83.0% balanced accuracy. The strongest baseline, RAG, achieves 69.6% macro F1 on FABLES+ and 75.1% on DiverseSumm+, trailing VeriTrail by 4.4–14.9 points in macro F1. VeriTrail outperforms all baselines on both primary metrics across both datasets for all tested models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VeriTrail outperforms all three NLI baselines by a wide margin: on FABLES+, it leads AlignScore (59.6%) by 24.9 points, INFUSE (40.5%) by 44.0 points, and Bespoke-MiniCheck-7B (62.2%) by 22.3 points in macro F1 (q=3); on DiverseSumm+, the gaps are 19.1, 59.5, and 7.4 points respectively. Against the RAG baseline, VeriTrail (q=3) gains 4.9 points on FABLES+ (84.5 vs. 69.6) and 4.4 points on DiverseSumm+ (79.5 vs. 75.1) in macro F1, while also improving balanced accuracy by 7.1 and 2.3 points. The direct long-context LM baselines (Gemini 1.5 Pro, GPT-4.1 Mini) perform substantially worse, with Gemini 1.5 Pro dropping to 49.8% macro F1 on DiverseSumm+—nearly 30 points below VeriTrail—likely due to the 1.19M-token article collection exceeding effective context utilization. Notably, INFUSE collapses on DiverseSumm+ (20.0% macro F1, 0.3% recall for fully supported claims), suggesting its sentence-ranking strategy fails on multi-document, multi-perspective sources. The only exception to VeriTrail's dominance is mistral-large-2411, where it achieves the highest balanced accuracy but not the highest macro F1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends closed-domain faithfulness evaluation (hallucination detection) from the single-output setting to processes with multiple generative steps (MGS), where prior methods like AlignScore, INFUSE, and RAG-based verifiers evaluate the final output against source material in isolation without leveraging intermediate outputs. It builds directly on Claimify (Metropolitansky & Larson, 2025) for claim extraction and decomposition, and introduces a DAG-based representation of generative processes that captures input-output dependencies between steps. The key conceptual departure is the addition of traceability—provenance (tracing supported claims back through intermediate nodes to source) and error localization (identifying which stage introduced unsupported content)—which prior faithfulness evaluation methods do not provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MGS architectures (GraphRAG, hierarchical summarization, multi-agent systems) become the dominant pattern for processing long documents and large corpora, the inability to trace where hallucinations originate limits both debugging and user trust. The FABLES+ and DiverseSumm+ datasets are the first to preserve all intermediate outputs with human-annotated faithfulness labels, enabling future work on MGS-specific evaluation. Open questions include whether the iterative DAG traversal scales efficiently to processes with hundreds of thousands of intermediate nodes (as in GraphRAG), whether the LLM-based evidence selection step introduces its own hallucination risk, and how the method generalizes to MGS architectures with cyclic or non-DAG execution patterns. The moderate inter-annotator agreement (Krippendorff's α = 0.49) also suggests the underlying task remains inherently ambiguous, limiting the ceiling for any automated method.
