## Problem

Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) generate discrete sequences in a fast, any-order parallel fashion but are fundamentally limited to fixed-length outputs: they cannot model distributions over variable-length sequences nor insert new tokens during generation. This paper asks whether variable-length modeling can be achieved while provably retaining the any-order inference property that makes MDMs attractive over autoregressive models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261375/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model starts from the empty string and iteratively inserts mask tokens and unmasks them, so the output length is not predetermined but emerges from the learned insertion expectation. The network receives a variable-length partially-masked sequence plus a continuous time step, and produces two outputs per forward pass: a per-position categorical distribution over the vocabulary (the unmasking posterior) and a per-gap non-negative scalar (the expected number of tokens to insert between consecutive positions). For planning tasks, the input is a sequence of subgoal grids and the output is a feasible path connecting them without entering invalid cells.

## Method

FlexMDM introduces a joint interpolant that augments the standard masked-diffusion interpolant with an auxiliary index-tracking variable s_t recording which positions of the clean sequence have been inserted so far. Two monotone schedules govern the process: an insertion schedule α_t controlling when mask tokens appear and an unmasking schedule β_t controlling when they reveal clean tokens. Training minimizes a combined loss: a weighted cross-entropy on the unmasking posterior f_θ and a Bregman-divergence term on the insertion expectation g_θ (a single scalar per gap), both parameterized by a DiT backbone with two output heads. Inference uses τ-leaping to discretize the resulting CTMC, sampling Poisson-distributed insertion counts and unmasking events per step; a key theoretical result shows the unmasking posterior is independent of the unmasking schedule β_t, which guarantees that adaptive (confidence-based) any-order unmasking still samples from the true data distribution. Pretrained MDMs can be retrofitted into FlexMDMs by adding only a scalar head and time-embedding layers, requiring roughly 400M trainable parameters via LoRA.

## Results

On the subgoal-style maze planning task (41×41 grid), FlexMDM achieves 92.3%, 90.4%, and 90.0% success rates at Easy, Medium, and Hard difficulty levels, respectively, compared to MDM's 68.4%, 29.3%, and 24.2%—a gap of up to ~60 percentage points at K=12 subgoals. At 8B scale, fine-tuning LLaDA-8B into a FlexMDM (only ~400M trainable parameters via LoRA, 3 days on 16 H100s) improves GSM8K from 58% to 67% and HumanEval-infill from 52% to 65%. On OpenWebText pretraining, FlexMDM matches MDM's generative perplexity while recovering the true length distribution far more faithfully (tracking ground truth at 256 sampling steps vs. MDM remaining miscalibrated even at 1024 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines. Against MDM (Sahoo et al., 2024), FlexMDM dominates on the maze planning task, achieving 90–92% success rate across all difficulty levels while MDM degrades sharply from 68.4% (Easy) to 24.2% (Hard), with the gap reaching ~66 percentage points at K=12. On OpenWebText pretraining, FlexMDM matches MDM's generative perplexity but substantially outperforms it in length-distribution fidelity. Against LLaDA-Base (Nie et al., 2025) at 8B scale, the FlexMDM retrofit (initialized from LLaDA-8B with LoRA adapters) improves GSM8K accuracy from 58% to 67% and HumanEval-infill from 52% to 65% after identical instruction fine-tuning, with FlexMDM continuing to improve as sampling steps increase while LLaDA's performance plateaus.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) from fixed-length to variable-length sequences by introducing a joint interpolant—a novel extension of the stochastic interpolant framework that augments the process with an auxiliary index-tracking variable. The construction is grounded in continuous-time Markov chains and discrete flow matching, re-deriving the MDM rate matrix within this formalism before adding a new insertion-expectation term alongside the familiar unmasking posterior. The any-order inference guarantee for FlexMDMs parallels recent results for MDMs (Kim et al., 2025; Nie et al., 2025), but the proof must additionally show that adaptive unmasking composes correctly with the insertion CTMC. Concurrent work (EditFlow, Havasi et al. 2025; DreamOn, Wu et al. 2025) tackles the same variable-length problem, but FlexMDM's particular interpolant choice yields a simpler per-position scalar (insertion expectation) rather than a full token distribution, and enables the any-order sampling guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlexMDM removes a fundamental structural limitation of the MDM paradigm—fixed-length generation—while preserving its key advantage of any-order, parallel inference, making it a drop-in upgrade for existing MDM pipelines. The practical finding that a pretrained 8B MDM (LLaDA-8B) can be retrofitted into a FlexMDM in three days on 16 H100s (≈13B tokens vs. ≈1.5T pretraining tokens) suggests this is a low-cost path to variable-length diffusion language models. Open questions include whether the insertion-expectation abstraction remains sufficient at larger scales or for more complex edit operations (deletions, reordering), and whether the from-scratch results at 175M parameters will hold at the 8B+ regime without the MDM initialization shortcut.
