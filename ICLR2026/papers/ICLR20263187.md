## Problem

Standard supervised fine-tuning of VLMs for VQA optimizes only toward the most frequent answer label, ignoring the human uncertainty (HU) distributions present in multi-annotator datasets. This paper asks: how does HU actually affect SFT performance, and can it be leveraged to improve both accuracy and calibration while reducing reliance on costly human annotations?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263187/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes image-question pairs annotated by 10 independent humans, each providing an answer and a discrete confidence level (yes/maybe/no, mapped to 0.99/0.5/0.01). Only a 5% seed set requires these full HU annotations; the remaining 95% is unlabeled and processed iteratively. The model outputs a text answer to the visual question along with a calibrated probability distribution over candidate answers that is explicitly aligned to the human confidence distribution via KL divergence.

## Method

HaDola is a four-stage iterative pipeline that starts from a small HU-annotated seed set (5%) and self-evolves: (1) Discriminate computes KL divergence between the current model's output distribution and a human confidence anchor (h_ω) to retain only samples within a bounded HU window, discarding high-HU outliers; (2) Self-Annotate generates pseudo-labels using the previous round's model; (3) Error Trigger filters harmful pseudo-labels via gradient consistency (cosine similarity with the mean gradient over the seed set) and TracIn-mini influence scoring; (4) Training fine-tunes with a composite loss combining standard cross-entropy, KL regularization against a frozen HU reference model, and a relative-KL term that pushes the model to better approximate human uncertainty than the reference. The framework is model-agnostic, validated on Qwen2.5VL, LLaVA, InternVL, and BEiT3, and achieves SOTA accuracy with only 5–15% of the labeled data while substantially improving calibration.

## Results

On VQAv2, HaDola achieves 76.75 HU-acc with Qwen2.5VL-7B and 77.63 with LLaVA1.6-7B, surpassing full SFT (100% annotations) using only a 5% labeled seed set. On VizWiz, the corresponding scores are 65.72 and 66.58. For calibration (KL divergence, lower is better), HaDola achieves 0.64 (Qwen, VQAv2) and 1.59 (Qwen, VizWiz), substantially outperforming both vanilla models (0.82 and 1.75) and Temperature Scaling (0.67 and 2.45). The training dynamics show an S-shaped curve: rapid gains from 5–10% labels, moderate improvement to 15%, and convergence thereafter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HaDola with only 5% labeled data surpasses SFT with LoRA (100% annotations) on all three zero-shot VLMs (Qwen, LLaVA, InternVL) in HU-acc, demonstrating that HU-aware supervision outperforms quantity-based scaling. Against Meta Pseudo-labeling, Active Learning (least-confidence), and DPO-based augmentation, HaDola consistently achieves higher HU-acc and lower KL across both VQAv2 and VizWiz. On KL divergence, HaDola and selective prediction (LYP) jointly outperform all other baselines; however, LYP achieves slightly lower KL in some cases by explicitly discarding hard samples, whereas HaDola reaches comparable calibration without removing any data, making it more broadly applicable. Compared to Temperature Scaling (post-hoc calibration), HaDola achieves substantially lower KL in every model–dataset combination, with the gap most dramatic on BEiT3 (0.62 vs. 2.22 on VQAv2; 1.58 vs. 2.80 on VizWiz), where TS even degrades performance on VizWiz relative to vanilla. On BEiT3 specifically, HaDola does not surpass full SFT in accuracy but remains comparable while outperforming all other baselines by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on human uncertainty (HU) in VQA initiated by Lan et al. (2025a), who introduced the HUD score to quantify HU but left open how it could be exploited during training. It departs from prior sample-difficulty analyses (Karamcheti et al., 2021; Tan & Bansal, 2019) that relied solely on label frequency and ignored confidence distributions, and from standard SFT pipelines that optimize only toward the majority label. The HaDola framework integrates gradient consistency (Mirzadeh et al., 2020) and TracIn-mini influence estimation (Pruthi et al., 2020) into a novel four-stage pipeline for HU-aware data selection and pseudo-labeling, while proposing a new loss that jointly optimizes accuracy, HU-reference regularization, and relative KL alignment to human confidence distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that high-HU samples actively degrade SFT performance (rather than merely contributing little) challenges the prevailing assumption that more annotated data is always beneficial, and the demonstration that 5% HU-labeled data with HaDola can match or surpass 100% SFT offers a concrete path to reducing annotation costs in VQA. The proposed HU-acc metric exposes a blind spot in the standard VQA-Accuracy evaluation that the field has used for a decade. Key limitations include the dependence on a well-constructed HU-annotated seed set (only available for VQAv2 and VizWiz among common benchmarks) and the restriction to VQA-style tasks; a natural future direction is leveraging VLM zero-shot capability to eliminate even the seed annotation requirement.
