## Problem

Reward models in RLHF exhibit strong length bias, favoring longer responses regardless of semantic quality, yet existing mitigation strategies either suppress length perception entirely (which is unreasonable when length instructions are part of the task) or rely on fragile hyperparameter tuning. Additionally, despite possessing implicit length sensitivity, fine-tuned LLMs consistently fail to follow explicit numerical length constraints (e.g., '150 words or less'), revealing a disconnect between unconscious length bias and conscious length instruction adherence.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard preference triplets (x, y_c, y_r) from datasets like OpenAssistant and transforms them into response-conditioned pairs by constructing length-augmented instructions: for the chosen response y_c, a constraint x_l^1 is added such that y_c violates it, yielding pair (x, x_l^1, y_c); for the rejected response y_r, a constraint x_l^2 is added such that y_r satisfies it, yielding pair (x_l^2, x, y_r). The output is either a scalar reward score per (prompt, response) pair (Rc-RM) or an aligned policy generating text responses (Rc-DPO). The unusual aspect is the flipped conditioning: the response is held fixed while the prompt varies, inverting the standard prompt-conditioned BT formulation.

## Method

The core contribution is the Response-conditioned Bradley-Terry (Rc-BT) model, which reformulates preference learning by conditioning on the response rather than the prompt: instead of modeling p(y_c ≻ y_r | x), it models p(x ≻ x_l^1 | y_c) and p(x_l^2 ≻ x | y_r), forcing the model to explicitly discriminate between a prompt and its length-augmented variant given a fixed response. The augmented dataset D_Rc is constructed by adding length constraints to the original preference data, and training proceeds via maximum likelihood on the combined D_rm ∪ D_Rc. This mechanism is integrated into reward modeling (Rc-RM, Eq. 6) with a balancing hyperparameter λ between the two pair types, and into policy optimization (Rc-DPO, Eq. 8) through an analogous derivation from the response-conditioned RL objective. The key insight is that making length an explicit, learnable attribute through response-conditioned prompt comparisons simultaneously mitigates implicit length bias and enables adherence to explicit length instructions, without requiring architectural changes or length penalty terms.

## Results

On the Quality Eval Acc metric (D_eval^q), Rc-RM outperforms all baselines across 8 model variants, achieving 69.55% on Qwen2-1.5B-Base (+10.41% over Baseline, +13.43% over ODIN) and 81.70% on Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct (+16.13% over ODIN). For DPO, Rc-DPO achieves the highest Quality Win Ratio of 64.34% on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (vs. 58.13% for SimPO and 52.18% for Dr. DPO) while reducing response length from 247.74 to 204.77 words. On Length Eval Acc (D_eval^l), Rc-RM reaches 84–95%, matching or exceeding LIFT-plus (84–95%) without degrading semantic quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Rc-RM dominates ODIN (Chen et al., 2024b) and R-DA (Singhal et al., 2023) on Quality Eval Acc across all 8 model variants, with margins of 7.7–13.4% over ODIN and 4.3–11.7% over R-DA. Against LIFT-plus (Yuan et al., 2024), Rc-RM achieves comparable Length Eval Acc (e.g., 92.31% vs. 83.97% on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) while substantially outperforming on Quality Eval Acc (73.07% vs. ~59% for LIFT-plus variants), confirming that LIFT-plus sacrifices semantic quality for length adherence. In the DPO setting, Rc-DPO outperforms R-DPO (Park et al., 2024) by +4.99% Quality Win Ratio on Qwen2.5-7B-Base (45.39% vs. 40.40%) while reducing response length from 583 to 208 words. Rc-DPO also surpasses Dr. DPO (Wu et al., 2024) by +12.16% and SimPO (Meng et al., 2024) by +6.21% on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct Quality Win Ratio, and beats CPO (Xu et al., 2024a) by +5.32% on Qwen2.5-7B-Base. Notably, LIFT-plus degrades Quality Win Ratio below the Baseline in 3 of 4 DPO model groups, whereas Rc-DPO consistently improves over Baseline on both quality and length control.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bradley-Terry preference modeling framework by inverting the conditioning: rather than modeling p(y_c ≻ y_r | x) as in standard BT or DPO, it models p(x ≻ x_l | y), conditioning on the response to separate semantic intent from length constraints. It departs from prior length-bias mitigation work (ODIN, R-DA, R-DPO) that treats length as purely harmful and seeks to suppress it, and from LIFT which teaches explicit length following at the cost of semantic quality. The Rc-BT formulation and its derivations into Rc-RM and Rc-DPO provide a unified data-format-level solution that preserves both capabilities simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that reward models acquire implicit length sensitivity during preference training yet cannot follow explicit length instructions challenges the assumption that bias perception and instruction following are linked, and motivates the response-conditioning approach. The method's plug-and-play integration into both reward modeling and DPO pipelines makes it practically accessible, and the authors note the framework could extend to other spurious correlations (format, politeness). Limitations include reliance on GPT-4o for dataset augmentation, evaluation primarily on OpenAssistant-derived data, and the need for a balancing hyperparameter λ; whether the response-conditioning principle generalizes to multi-attribute bias disentangling remains open.
