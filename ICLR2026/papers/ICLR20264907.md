## Problem

Existing crowdsourced label aggregation methods are either simple and retraining-free (Majority Voting) but ignore worker/task heterogeneity, or accurate but require dataset-specific parameter estimation from scratch, making them non-scalable and non-transferable. Prior cross-dataset attempts like HyperLM fail to explicitly model the worker-task-label structure and rely on uniform synthetic data that misaligns with real annotation patterns, resulting in poor real-world performance.

## Input / Output

The model takes a set of crowdsourced annotations as (worker_id, task_id, label) triples with no additional features, and outputs a predicted ground-truth label per task via softmax over all K options. Crucially, the architecture is size-invariant: it handles arbitrary numbers of workers, tasks, and label options without retraining, since all workers and tasks initialize from shared learnable vectors and differentiate only through observed annotation patterns. The learned node embeddings additionally support downstream heads for worker ability regression, task difficulty regression, and binary worker-task compatibility prediction.

## Method

CrowdFM is a bipartite graph neural network with L layers of attention-based message passing over annotation triples, where each triple representation concatenates worker, task, and option embeddings and is processed through type-specific linear projections to produce queries, keys, and values. A key design choice is size-invariant initialization: all worker nodes share a single learnable vector, all task nodes share another, and option nodes are drawn from a fixed-dimensional Gaussian, so no dataset-specific priors are introduced and the model generalizes across heterogeneous dataset sizes. The model is pretrained on a large collection of synthetic crowdsourcing datasets generated by a domain-randomized 3PL (three-parameter logistic) IRT-based simulator that randomizes dataset scale, worker ability distributions, task difficulty/discrimination/guessing parameters, and heavy-tailed worker participation patterns. After pretraining, the fixed model is deployed zero-shot on new datasets, and lightweight downstream heads (regression for ability/difficulty, binary classification for compatibility) can be trained on top of the frozen encoder for auxiliary crowdsourcing tasks.

## Results

CrowdFM achieves an average accuracy of 83.41% across 22 real-world crowdsourcing datasets, outperforming Majority Voting (81.78%) on 21 out of 22 benchmarks with an average improvement of +1.64 percentage points. The largest gains are on Web (+12.93%) and MS (+9.43%) over MV. Inference runtime is 0.53 seconds per dataset, comparable to lightweight methods (PM: 0.47s) and orders of magnitude faster than deep learning baselines (GLAD: 494.26s, LAA: 223.06s, GOVERN: 95.43s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CrowdFM (83.41%) is statistically significantly better than MV (81.78%, p=0.00003), PM (80.27%, p=0.00647), LAA (78.42%, p=0.04935), TiReMGE (80.29%, p=0.00230), and HyperLM (80.81%, p=0.01793). EBCC achieves a marginally higher average accuracy (84.08%) but the difference is not statistically significant (p=0.90089), and EBCC is 5.6× slower (2.95s vs. 0.53s). BWA (83.31%) and DS (83.02%) are competitive in accuracy but BWA is faster (0.10s) while DS is slower (5.24s); neither is significantly different from CrowdFM. Among deep learning baselines, GOVERN (82.61%) and LAA (78.42%) are substantially slower (95.43s and 223.06s respectively) and less accurate. HyperLM, the closest prior cross-dataset method, underperforms MV on average (80.81% vs. 81.78%) and is slower (0.88s), confirming its poor fit for human-annotated crowdsourcing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reframing crowdsourced label aggregation—a task dominated for decades by dataset-specific parameter estimation (DS, GLAD, BWA, EBCC, etc.)—as a foundation model problem solvable by a single fixed GNN. The closest prior work, HyperLM, attempted cross-dataset GNN aggregation but for programmatic weak supervision with uniform synthetic data and no explicit worker/task modeling. CrowdFM departs from both the per-dataset estimation lineage and HyperLM by introducing a bipartite GNN with size-invariant initialization, attention-based message passing, and a domain-randomized synthetic generator grounded in the 3PL Item Response Theory model, enabling retraining-free inference across heterogeneous datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single pretrained model eliminates the need for per-dataset training, matching or exceeding dataset-specific methods at 0.53s inference time, and its learned representations transfer to worker assessment and task assignment without retraining. However, the average accuracy gain over Majority Voting is modest (+1.64 pp), and several real-world datasets (Senti, Fact) fall well outside the synthetic training distribution, suggesting the sim-to-real gap is not fully closed. Open questions include extending the framework to continuous or structured labels, handling larger-scale datasets beyond the synthetic range, and whether the 3PL-based generator captures the full behavioral complexity of real annotators.
