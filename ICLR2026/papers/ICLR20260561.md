## Problem

Speculative decoding accelerates LLM inference by drafting tokens with a fast model and verifying them in a single target-model forward pass, but it still enforces a sequential dependence: the draft model must wait for verification to complete before speculating the next round. This paper asks whether that remaining sequential bottleneck between drafting and verification can be eliminated entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260561/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a text prompt, a large target model (e.g., Llama-3.1-70B on 4×H100 with tensor parallelism), and a small draft model (e.g., Llama-3.2-1B on a separate 1×H100), and produces lossless generated text tokens distributed exactly as the target model. The draft and target run on distinct hardware and synchronize once per speculation round over NCCL: the target sends only the verification outcome (accepted-prefix length and bonus token), and the draft returns pre-computed speculative tokens and logits from its cache—no KV cache is ever transferred between devices.

## Method

SSD parallelizes drafting and verification by having the draft model, while the target is still verifying, predict the most likely verification outcomes (number of accepted tokens plus bonus token) and pre-speculate for each in parallel, storing results in a speculation cache. Three targeted optimizations make this practical: (1) a geometric fan-out schedule (Theorem 12) that allocates the cache budget across lookahead positions proportional to the probability of rejection at each position, derived via Lagrange multipliers under a power-law cache-hit assumption; (2) SAGUARO sampling, which downweights the top-F draft logits by a constant C to push residual-distribution mass onto the cached tokens, trading a small loss in acceptance rate for a large gain in cache-hit rate; and (3) an adaptive fallback strategy (Theorem 17) that uses the slow, accurate primary speculator as backup at low batch sizes but switches to a fast, low-latency speculator (e.g., random tokens) once batch size exceeds a derived critical threshold b*. The result, SAGUARO, is on average 30% faster than the strongest synchronous speculative decoding baselines and up to 5× faster than autoregressive decoding.

## Results

SAGUARO achieves an average decode throughput of 255.8 tok/s on Llama-3.1-70B (with Llama-3.2-1B draft), which is 1.58× faster than standalone speculative decoding (161.8 tok/s) and 4.68× faster than autoregressive decoding (54.7 tok/s) across four datasets. On Qwen-3-32B (with Qwen3-0.6B draft), SAGUARO reaches 203.8 tok/s, a 1.49× speedup over SD (136.8 tok/s) and 2.29× over AR (88.8 tok/s). The method is on average 30% faster than the strongest speculative decoding baselines (vLLM/SGLang with EAGLE-3) and up to 5× faster than autoregressive generation, while strictly improving the throughput–latency Pareto frontier across batch sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAGUARO outperforms all tested baselines across both model families. Against SGLang 0.5.9 (the strongest open-source engine), the best standalone speculative decoding configuration reaches 201.9 tok/s (10 steps × 3 top-k) and the best EAGLE-3 configuration reaches 192.8 tok/s (8 steps × 3 top-k) on Llama-3.1-70B, while SAGUARO achieves 255.8 tok/s on average — a 27–33% improvement. Against vLLM 0.16.0, the best EAGLE-3 configuration reaches 197.6 tok/s (K=6) and standalone SD peaks at 143.7 tok/s (K=2), again well below SAGUARO's 255.8 tok/s. The paper also shows that SAGUARO strictly dominates the throughput–latency Pareto frontier: at every batch size tested, SSD achieves higher throughput at equal or lower latency than both vLLM and SGLang, with the largest gains at low batch sizes where the asynchronous draft latency is most effectively hidden.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023) by identifying and eliminating the sequential dependence between drafting and verification that all prior SD methods retain. Several concurrent works (AMUSD, PEARL, DSI, SwiftSpec, SpecBranch) have attempted to overlap these phases, but only prepare for a single verification outcome (typically full acceptance) or use fallback strategies that degrade at large batch sizes. SSD generalizes this by pre-speculating for multiple likely verification outcomes in parallel, drawing a direct analogy to speculative execution in CPU design. Unlike tree-based methods (Medusa, SpecInfer, EAGLE) that increase verifier compute, SSD scales speculation compute on a separate device while keeping verifier cost unchanged.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SSD/SAGUARO achieves 30% speedup over the strongest speculative decoding baselines and up to 5x over autoregressive decoding while pushing the throughput-latency Pareto frontier, making it relevant for both latency-sensitive and throughput-sensitive serving. The theoretical framework (power-law cache hit rates, geometric fan-out optimization, batch-size-dependent fallback) provides principled design guidance rather than ad-hoc heuristics. Key limitations include the requirement for an additional GPU for the draft model, reduced effectiveness for throughput-bound workloads, and the largely unexplored joint design space with EAGLE and token-tree speculation. Cluster-level speculation sharing and scaling draft devices are identified as natural next steps.
