## Problem

Cascaded video generation pipelines that pair a base T2V model with a video super-resolution (VSR) model suffer from two key limitations: an inefficient pixel-space interface that forces redundant VAE decoding, upsampling, and re-encoding between stages, and degradation strategies designed for real-world video that mismatch the characteristic artifacts (motion-dependent color blending and localized blur) of AIGC content, causing the VSR model to produce severe artifacts or compromise depth perception.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262837/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

SimpleGVR consumes the raw low-resolution latent (e.g., 384×672 or 512² spatial, 8 channels) directly from the base T2V model's 3D VAE encoder without any pixel-space round-trip, along with a text prompt. The LR latent is perturbed with Gaussian noise sampled from [0.3, 0.6] and upsampled to match the HR latent dimensions before channel concatenation. The output is a clean high-resolution latent (≈1440² spatial) that is decoded once by the 3D VAE into a 1080p video, supporting up to 77 frames via the interleaving temporal unit mechanism.

## Method

SimpleGVR is a lightweight DiT-based diffusion VSR model operating entirely in latent space using the Rectified Flow / Conditional Flow Matching framework. Its key architectural component is a latent upsampler that expands the LR latent's channel and temporal dimensions with 3D residual blocks before bilinear interpolation, then contracts them back, preserving local structural integrity and preventing inter-frame signal aliasing; the upsampled latent is injected via channel concatenation with the noisy HR latent. For training pair synthesis, two AIGC-specific degradation strategies are proposed: a flow-based scheme using DIS optical flow to drive motion-aware color blending and adaptive block-wise blur, and a model-guided scheme (inspired by SDEdit) that adds noise to the LR latent and partially denoises it with the base T2V model to approximate its output distribution. Training is further optimized by a detail-aware timestep sampler (derived from DCT high-frequency variation analysis), a middle-range noise augmentation on the LR branch, and a Swin-style interleaving temporal unit that alternates shifted and unshifted attention windows to handle 77-frame sequences under memory constraints.

## Results

On the AIGC100 dataset, SimpleGVR achieves the best scores across nearly all no-reference metrics: MUSIQ 62.35, CLIPIQA 0.6768, MANIQA 0.4956, NIQE 4.1665 (lower is better), flow warping error E_warp* 2.592×10⁻³ (lower is better), DOVER Overall 71.34, and VBench Average Score 84.63. In the end-to-end vs. cascaded comparison (Table 5), the cascaded pipeline (Large T2V at 512p + SimpleGVR) outperforms direct 1080p generation on MUSIQ (62.35 vs. 56.77) and DOVER Overall (71.34 vs. 62.32) while reducing inference time from 950 s to 283 s. On VBench110, SimpleGVR attains the best DOVER Overall (65.71) and VBench Average Score (85.45), though SeedVR (7B) edges it on MUSIQ (61.64 vs. 60.20).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimpleGVR outperforms all 10 quantitative baselines on the AIGC100 dataset across MUSIQ, CLIPIQA, MANIQA, NIQE, E_warp*, DOVER Overall, and VBench Average Score. The closest competitors are DOVE (MUSIQ 60.34, VBench 84.39) and MGLD (NIQE 4.1880, VBench 84.40), but SimpleGVR leads DOVE by +2.01 on MUSIQ and +9.80 on DOVER Overall, and leads MGLD by +10.17 on DOVER Overall. Among the diffusion-based VSR methods, FlashVideo (MUSIQ 53.71, VBench 84.00) and SeedVR 7B (MUSIQ 56.77, VBench 84.08) trail SimpleGVR by 8.64 and 5.58 on MUSIQ respectively. Traditional non-generative methods (RealBasicVSR, Upscale-A-Video, VEnhancer) lag substantially, with VEnhancer and Upscale-A-Video scoring below 41 and 37 on MUSIQ. In the user study, over 50% of participants prefer SimpleGVR's detail quality and over 43% prefer its temporal consistency over RealBasicVSR, VEnhancer, STAR, FlashVideo, and SeedVR. On VBench110, SeedVR (7B) narrowly beats SimpleGVR on MUSIQ (61.64 vs. 60.20), but SimpleGVR wins on DOVER Overall (65.71 vs. 58.18) and VBench Average Score (85.45 vs. 85.35).

## Relation to Prior Work

SimpleGVR extends the cascaded T2V generation paradigm (most directly FlashVideo) by moving the entire VSR stage into the latent space of the base model, eliminating the pixel-space decode-re-encode interface that prior cascaded systems rely on. Its diffusion backbone follows the Rectified Flow / Conditional Flow Matching formulation (Esser et al., 2024) on a DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023), while its model-guided degradation strategy is inspired by SDEdit (Meng et al., 2021) and its long-video mechanism borrows Swin-style shifted-window attention (Liu et al., 2021). The key conceptual departure from prior VSR work (RealBasicVSR, SeedVR, DOVE, etc.) is that SimpleGVR is explicitly designed to consume the latent output of a specific upstream T2V generator rather than decoded RGB frames, and its degradation strategies target AIGC-specific artifacts (motion-dependent color blending, localized blur) rather than conventional real-world degradations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a lightweight latent-space VSR stage can simultaneously improve visual quality and reduce inference time by ~3.4× compared to end-to-end 1080p generation, establishing a practical and well-ablated baseline for efficient cascaded video synthesis. The AIGC-specific degradation strategies (flow-based and model-guided) address a gap in prior VSR training that assumed real-world degradation models, and the finding that a simple channel-concatenation conditioning with a small latent upsampler outperforms more complex token-attention schemes offers a useful design principle. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes cleanly to substantially different base T2V architectures (limited evidence with Wan and CogVideoX), whether the 50-step inference budget can be further compressed, and whether the degradation strategies transfer across different T2V model families without retraining.
