## Problem

Post-training MLLM-based UI agents for mobile and computer use requires large-scale task datasets that are diverse, feasible to execute, and verifiable, yet existing synthetic task generation methods either rely on expensive human annotation or condition MLLMs on limited static environment descriptions, yielding tasks with poor coverage, hallucinated details, and no guarantees of feasibility. The fundamental bottleneck is the absence of an automatic pipeline that grounds task synthesis in actual environment states and accessible interactions.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes interactive environment screenshots from Android (20 apps) and Ubuntu (13 apps) applications, exploration trajectories (sequences of interleaved screenshots and UI actions collected by an MLLM explorer), and domain-specific task guideline prompts (e.g., Feature-Use, Feature-Composition, Information Retrieval, Feature-Repetition). It produces synthetic task instructions as natural-language goals paired with initial environment states (~20k for Android, ~10k for Ubuntu), verified execution trajectories (interleaved images and actions) for SFT, and a binary success/failure reward signal for RL. The exploration trajectories serve as environment context rather than task demonstrations, and the verifier operates solely on the trajectory without privileged access to ground-truth environment state.

## Method

AUTOPLAY operates in two stages. In Stage 1 (Environment Exploration), a goal-agnostic MLLM explorer agent with episodic memory exhaustively interacts with each application for multiple turns, selecting diverse actions to maximize coverage over novel states and functionalities; prior exploration turns are summarized by a separate MLLM to fit within the context window, and the resulting trajectories form the environment context. In Stage 2 (Task Generation), a task generator MLLM is conditioned on each exploration trajectory and a sampled task guideline prompt to synthesize up to 50 environment-grounded tasks per trajectory-guideline combination, with the guidelines enforcing diversity across task types (create/edit/delete operations, multi-step compositions, information retrieval, repeated subtasks). For downstream training, an MLLM executor (GPT-4o planner + UI-TARS or GTA1 grounding model) generates trajectories for the synthesized tasks, and an MLLM verifier (GPT-4o or Qwen2.5-VL-32B) filters successful trajectories for SFT or provides binary rewards for GRPO-based RL. The key distinction from prior iterative-exploration methods is that AUTOPLAY decouples exploration from task proposal: a single exploration trajectory can yield multiple diverse tasks, and the explorer is not constrained to chain subtasks sequentially, enabling broader functional coverage.

## Results

On AndroidWorld, AUTOPLAY-7B (Qwen2.5-VL-7B base) achieves 40.1% Pass@5 and 58.4% Pass@1, improving over its base model by +20.6% and +30.7% respectively. AUTOPLAY-72B reaches 47.9% Pass@5 and 68.2% Pass@1 (+12.9% and +24.7% over base). On OSWorld, AUTOPLAY-72B achieves 14.5% Pass@1 and 16.0% Pass@5 (+10.1% and +10.6% over base). Adding RL training (GRPO) on top of SFT yields an additional +5.7% Pass@5 gain, bringing AUTOPLAY-3B-RL to 39.9% Pass@5, nearly matching AUTOPLAY-7B SFT (40.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AUTOPLAY-7B outperforms UI-TARS-1.5 7B (trained on large-scale human-annotated GUI data) by 13.7% Pass@5 (40.1 vs 26.4) and 22.2% Pass@1 (58.4 vs 36.2) on AndroidWorld; AUTOPLAY-72B extends this lead over UI-TARS-1.5 72B by 10.2% Pass@5 (47.9 vs 37.7). On OSWorld, AUTOPLAY-72B (14.5% Pass@1) surpasses AguVis-72B (10.3%) by 4.2 points but trails both UI-TARS-1.5 72B (42.5%) and o3+GTA1 (34.0%), which leverage curated human-labeled data or proprietary models. AUTOPLAY-72B also outperforms its own data-collection executor (GPT-4o + UI-TARS, 43.1% Pass@5) by 4.8 points, validating the MLLM verifier's ability to filter trajectories without privileged environment access. Notably, AUTOPLAY-3B (34.2% Pass@5) nearly matches the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B base (35.0%), and with RL training AUTOPLAY-3B-RL (39.9%) approaches AUTOPLAY-7B SFT (40.1%). AUTOPLAY loses to the largest proprietary baselines (UI-TARS-2 230B at 73.3% and Seed-VL-1.5 at 62.1% Pass@5 on AndroidWorld), which rely on substantially larger model ensembles and human-annotated data mixtures.

## Relation to Prior Work

AUTOPLAY builds on the modular executor architecture from GTA1 (Yang et al., 2025) for trajectory collection, but its core contribution is a two-stage task generation pipeline that explicitly departs from prior synthetic task generation methods. Unlike PAE (Zhou et al., 2025), which conditions on static textual descriptions, or AgentSynth/Explorer (Xie et al., 2025; Pahuja et al., 2025), which interleave short-horizon subtask proposal with execution and rely on hindsight relabeling, AUTOPLAY first performs goal-agnostic exploration with episodic memory to maximize state coverage, then generates multiple diverse tasks per trajectory using domain-specific guideline prompts. This decoupling of exploration from task proposal allows a single trajectory to yield many tasks and avoids the progressive constraint on trajectory diversity that iterative subtask chaining imposes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AUTOPLAY addresses the central bottleneck in scaling post-training for agentic MLLMs—the scarcity of diverse, feasible, and verifiable task datasets—by enabling both SFT and RL training entirely without human annotation. The practical impact is significant: 20k Android and 10k Ubuntu tasks with ~8k and ~3.5k verified trajectories, plus a verifier-based RL loop that adds 5.7% on top of SFT. Key limitations include the verifier's modest ~60% accuracy (high recall but low precision, tending to mark partial completions as successes), incomplete coverage of cross-app and scripting tasks in the computer-use domain, and the fact that the executor remains the quality bottleneck for the synthetic dataset. Open questions include whether the pipeline generalizes to domains without programmatic simulators and whether verifier accuracy can be improved to unlock higher-quality RL rewards.
