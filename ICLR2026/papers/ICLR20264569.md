## Problem

Existing audio benchmarks predominantly evaluate coarse-grained semantic content that can be recovered from text captions, masking deficits in fine-grained perceptual reasoning. The paper formalizes 'audio 4D intelligence'—deep reasoning over sound dynamics in time (1D) and 3D space—and demonstrates that prior benchmarks (MMAU, MMAR) only see 5.9–9.0% accuracy drops under caption-only answering, whereas STAR-Bench induces far larger drops (−31.5% temporal, −35.2% spatial), confirming that the targeted cues are linguistically hard to describe.

## Input / Output

Foundational perception tasks consume procedurally synthesized sine waves (125 Hz–8 kHz, −10 to 110 dB HL) or Pyroomacoustics-rendered binaural audio with controlled azimuth/elevation/distance parameters; holistic tasks consume either three shuffled audio segments (temporal reordering) or stereo audio presented in two modes—native (default pipeline) and channel-wise (left/right channels fed separately with textual instructions). All questions are multiple-choice, answered via string matching of option labels or full text, and evaluated over multiple runs with cyclic option rotation (CircularEval) or permuted segment orders to yield Average Accuracy and All-Correct Rate.

## Method

STAR-Bench is organized as a two-level hierarchy: (1) Foundational Acoustic Perception quantitatively profiles six attributes (pitch, loudness, duration, azimuth, elevation, distance) under absolute perception range (audiogram-style detection) and relative discrimination sensitivity (JND-style comparison at 4–6 difficulty levels), using pure sine-wave synthesis and Pyroomacoustics binaural rendering with measured SOFA HRTFs; (2) Holistic Spatio-Temporal Reasoning tests temporal logic via a novel Audio Segment Reordering setting (three shuffled clips from continuous processes like Doppler-effect motion or fluid dynamics, and discrete scripts like tool operation or causal consequences) and spatial reasoning across static localization, multi-source relations, and dynamic trajectory tracking. The data curation pipeline for holistic tasks follows four stages: collaborative taxonomy construction, three-stage AI-assisted filtering (property checks → LLM metadata screening → multimodal model judgment), two-round human annotation with inter-annotator cross-validation and expert spot-checks, and final validation requiring ≥2/3 expert consensus. Evaluation covers 19 models (16 open-source, 3 closed-source) with robust multi-run protocols, revealing that closed-source models are bottlenecked by fine-grained perception while open-source models lag across perception, knowledge, and multi-audio reasoning.

## Results

On STAR-Bench, human evaluators achieve 79.11% Macro Accuracy (MA), while the best model, Gemini 2.5 Pro, reaches only 49.59% MA — a gap of ~30 percentage points. Most open-source models (16 evaluated) perform near random guessing (24.32% MA), with the best open-source model (MiDashengLM) at just 31.28% MA. On temporal reasoning, Gemini 2.5 Pro achieves 58.52% Overall Accuracy (OA) versus 19.44% for GPT-4o Audio and ~16–19% for all open-source models. On spatial reasoning, even Gemini 2.5 Pro reaches only 43.62% OA, far below the 73.72% human baseline, indicating that spatial audio understanding remains largely unsolved across all model families.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 19 models (16 open-source, 3 closed-source) on STAR-Bench, revealing a stark capability hierarchy. Gemini 2.5 Pro dominates on temporal reasoning (58.52% OA), outperforming GPT-4o Audio (19.44%) by ~39 points and all open-source models (8.67–19.97%) by 39–50 points; its advantage stems from superior fine-grained perception and world knowledge. Among closed-source models, Gemini 2.5 Pro also leads on foundational perception (46.64% MA vs. 39.72% for Flash and 31.76% for GPT-4o), but on spatial reasoning the gap narrows considerably (43.62% vs. 41.70% for GPT-4o), as all models struggle due to mono-channel preprocessing that discards stereo cues. Open-source models cluster tightly near random-guess levels on temporal reasoning (8.67–19.97% OA), with no model exceeding 20%, and their 'think' variants (Audio Flamingo 3-think, Xiaomi-MiMo-Audio-think) actually underperform their non-thinking counterparts, suggesting that reasoning without solid perceptual grounding is counterproductive. The best open-source model, MiDashengLM (31.28% MA), still trails Gemini 2.5 Pro by 18.3 points overall, with the largest deficit on temporal reasoning (16.30% vs. 58.52% OA). Notably, the specialized spatial model BAT scores 0% on both temporal and spatial holistic tasks, confirming that narrow spatial training does not transfer to general audio reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the line of audio benchmarks (MMAU, MMAR, MMAU-Pro, AIR-Bench) that predominantly test coarse-grained semantic content recoverable from text captions, by formalizing 'audio 4D intelligence'—deep reasoning over sound dynamics in time and 3D space. It repurposes physics-based simulation (Pyroomacoustics with HRTF rendering) for controlled foundational perception tasks, a technique borrowed from spatial audio research, and combines it with real-world corpora (FSD50K, Clotho, STARSS23) for holistic reasoning. The segment-reordering formulation for continuous physical processes (Doppler effect, fluid dynamics, thermodynamic decay) and multi-source dynamic trajectory tracking extend simpler prior formulations of event ordering and single-source localization into tasks requiring physically grounded, multi-step inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STAR-Bench provides the first systematic evidence that existing audio benchmarks largely probe text-representable semantics, masking fundamental perceptual deficits in current LALMs and OLMs. The finding that 'think' modes actively degrade open-source model performance, combined with the demonstration that mono-averaging preprocessing is a critical bottleneck for spatial reasoning, offers concrete, actionable diagnostics for model developers. Open questions include whether native multi-channel audio encoders can close the spatial gap, whether dense fine-grained audio captioning can bridge the perception-knowledge-reasoning pipeline, and whether the 4D audio intelligence paradigm generalizes to embodied AI settings where agents must act on auditory cues in real time.
