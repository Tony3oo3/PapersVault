## Problem

Behavior-regularized RL requires balancing value maximization with adherence to a reference distribution, but existing approaches face a fundamental trade-off: reparameterized policy gradients are unstable and expensive to scale to large generative models (diffusion, flow), while reject-sampling methods are overly conservative and cannot discover actions beyond the behavior support. No prior method simultaneously avoids explicit policy parameterization, scales to expressive generative models, and enables adaptive test-time scaling without retraining.

## Input / Output

VGF takes a state s, N samples from a reference distribution μ (a BC flow model in offline RL, or an SFT language model in RLHF), and a differentiable value/reward function R(s,a), and produces N guided particles in action space after L flow steps, from which a single best-of-N action is selected. In the LLM setting, the method operates in a continuous surrogate space (token-embedding matrix or flow/diffusion latent vector) and decodes back to discrete tokens only at the end of the gradient flow. The transport budget (number of steps L, temperature α, step size ε) is the sole control knob for regularization strength, and can be varied independently at training and inference time.

## Method

VGF reframes behavior-regularized RL as an optimal transport problem that moves probability mass from the reference distribution μ to the Boltzmann value distribution π*_R(a|s) ∝ exp(R(s,a)/α), solved via a discrete gradient flow using the JKO minimizing-movement scheme. The practical solver is a particle-based update derived from Stein variational gradient descent in an RKHS: each particle is nudged by a kernel-smoothed value gradient (attractive force toward high-reward regions) plus a kernel-gradient repulsive term (preserving multimodality), with no explicit policy network. The transport budget (L, α, ε) acts as implicit regularization, bounding the MMD distance between the reference and induced policies, while Theorem 2 guarantees the induced policy can escape the ε-support of the reference distribution. In the LLM setting, response-level reward gradients are back-propagated through a differentiable decoder via the chain rule, enabling first-order guidance that avoids PPO-style high-variance optimization and supports inference-only control by adjusting the number of flow steps at test time.

## Results

On D4RL, VGF achieves state-of-the-art or near-SOTA scores across 15 MuJoCo and AntMaze tasks, with particularly large gains on challenging AntMaze navigation (e.g., antmaze-m-p: 89.4 vs. 78.0 for FQL; antmaze-m-d: 86.7 vs. 82.0 for SfBC) and MuJoCo locomotion (hopper-m: 97.9 vs. 90.5 for Diffusion-QL; walker2d-m-r: 97.8 vs. 95.5 for Diffusion-QL). On OGBench, VGF substantially outperforms all baselines on hard manipulation tasks (cube-double: 70 vs. 29 for FQL; puzzle-3x3: 75 vs. 30 for FQL; puzzle-4x4: 45 vs. 17 for FQL). On RLHF, VGF achieves 68.1% win-rate on TL;DR and 59.0% on Anthropic-HH, outperforming DPO (61.2% / 51.5%) and PPO (57.3% / 45.5%) by 7–14 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VGF outperforms all 11 baselines on the majority of tasks. Against Gaussian-policy methods (TD3+BC, IQL, IVR), VGF achieves the largest margins on AntMaze navigation (e.g., antmaze-m-p: 89.4 vs. 10.6 for TD3+BC) and medium MuJoCo (hopper-m: 97.9 vs. 59.3 for TD3+BC). Against diffusion/flow-policy methods, VGF beats Diffusion-QL on hopper-m (97.9 vs. 90.5) and walker2d-m-r (97.8 vs. 95.5), and outperforms FQL on most AntMaze tasks (antmaze-m-p: 89.4 vs. 78.0; antmaze-m-d: 86.7 vs. 71.0), though FQL slightly edges VGF on antmaze-l-p (84.0 vs. 82.5). On OGBench, VGF dominates on hard manipulation tasks where FQL scores below 50% (cube-double: 70 vs. 29; puzzle-3x3: 75 vs. 30; puzzle-4x4: 45 vs. 17). In RLHF, VGF surpasses PPO by 10.8 points (68.1 vs. 57.3) and DPO by 6.9 points (68.1 vs. 61.2) on TL;DR win-rate, and by 7.5 points over DPO on Anthropic-HH (59.0 vs. 51.5), while also beating Best-of-N sampling by 9.8 points on TL;DR.

## Relation to Prior Work

VGF reframes behavior-regularized RL as an optimal transport problem from the reference distribution to the Boltzmann value distribution, solved via a particle-based gradient flow whose update rule is directly adapted from Stein Variational Gradient Descent (Liu & Wang, 2016) within the JKO minimizing-movement scheme (Jordan et al., 1998). Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm of adding explicit KL or L2 divergence penalties to the RL objective, instead making regularization implicit through the transport budget (number of flow steps, step size, and entropy coefficient). The target Boltzmann distribution is inherited from the Maximum Entropy RL literature (Ziebart, 2010), but the key departure is that VGF never parameterizes a policy: the implicit policy is simply the particle distribution after a fixed number of gradient-guided transport steps, which sidesteps the backprop-through-sampling instability that plagues reparameterized policy gradients on diffusion and flow models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VGF offers a practically attractive middle ground: it avoids the over-conservatism of best-of-N / weighted BC (which cannot escape the reference support) while also avoiding the optimization instability of reparameterized policy gradients on generative models. The test-time scaling property—adjusting the number of flow steps at inference without retraining—is a genuinely useful knob for practitioners who face uncertain value-function generalization. Open questions include how VGF behaves when the reference distribution is heavily skewed toward suboptimal behavior (the authors acknowledge this limitation), whether the particle-based approach scales to very high-dimensional continuous action spaces beyond the 8-dim AntMaze setting, and whether the RLHF gains observed on Pythia-2.8B transfer to larger models where reward-model over-optimization is more severe.
