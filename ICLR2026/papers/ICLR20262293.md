## Problem

Existing multimodal LLMs for visual mathematical reasoning either interleave perception and reasoning in an unstructured one-step manner or use decoupled pipelines that improve visual extraction but suffer from 'reasoning drift'—reasoning steps that appear coherent yet disregard the extracted perceptual evidence. Prior work focuses almost exclusively on improving perception accuracy while ignoring whether correctly extracted visual cues are faithfully integrated into subsequent reasoning, leaving a critical gap in the perception-to-reasoning pipeline.

## Input / Output

The model takes a geometry diagram image (containing points, line segments, and circles) paired with a text question as input. It produces a three-part structured output: a <WATCHING> section listing parametric geometric primitives (points as 2D coordinates, lines as fitted models, circles as center+radius) normalized to a [-10, 10] coordinate space; a <THINKING> section containing a step-by-step reasoning chain that explicitly internalizes the perceived primitives before performing logical inference; and a final <ANSWER> field. The MATHCOG training dataset comprises 121,730 problems with disentangled perception and reasoning annotations, sourced from MAVIS, Geo170K, and LLaVA-CoT after filtering for geometric relevance and resolution constraints (64–2048 px).

## Method

COGFLOW is a cognitive-inspired three-stage framework (perception → internalization → reasoning) built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, trained in two phases: SFT on 100K MATHCOG-SFT samples followed by reinforcement learning on 10K MATHCOG-RL samples under Visual-Gated Policy Optimization (VGPO). Three complementary reward signals drive the RL phase: (1) Synergistic Visual Rewards combine a Visual Parameterized Reward (Hungarian-matched Euclidean distance in primitive parameter space) with a Visual Semantic Reward (FG-CLIP cosine similarity between re-rendered and ground-truth images) to enforce both local geometric fidelity and global layout coherence; (2) a Knowledge Internalization Reward model, trained via Softmax-DPO on 10K positive trajectories paired with 50K synthetic negatives spanning five error types, scores whether each reasoning step remains faithful to the internalized visual representation; (3) an Inference Reward for answer correctness and format compliance. VGPO introduces a visual gate that adaptively filters low-quality perception trajectories before reasoning generation, and performs group-level PPO-style optimization with group-normalized advantages and a KL penalty to the SFT reference policy, ensuring the policy converges toward the paradigm of perceiving correctly first, then reasoning correctly.

## Results

COGFLOW-7B achieves 56.2% accuracy and 66.0% CoT-E on FlowVerse, 39.5% accuracy on MathVerse, 76.8% accuracy on MathVista, 64.1% on WeMath, and 46.2% on DynaMath, surpassing all open-source baselines by large margins. On the visually demanding FlowVerse benchmark, COGFLOW-7B reaches 61.1% accuracy on the Vision Centric subset and 63.5% on Vision Primary, substantially outperforming open-source competitors (e.g., VLM-R1-7B at 37.8% and 26.1% respectively). On MathVista, COGFLOW-7B attains 93.1% on the Geometry Problem Solving (GPS) category, the highest among all compared models. The ablation confirms each component contributes: the full pipeline (SynVRs + IntlzR + VGPO) yields 56.2% Acc on FlowVerse versus 48.7% for the SFT+GRPO baseline without any of the three proposed modules.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COGFLOW-7B outperforms all open-source baselines (7B–13B) by 10–25+ points across every benchmark. On FlowVerse, it surpasses GPT-4o (49.7% Acc) and Claude-3.5-Sonnet (45.1%) while remaining competitive with GPT-4V (58.7%) and GPT-5 (59.3%), despite being a 7B open model versus much larger closed systems. On MathVista, COGFLOW-7B (76.8%) beats GPT-4V (49.9%), Claude-3.5-Sonnet (67.7%), and Gemini-2.0-Flash (73.4%), trailing only Doubao-pro-1.5 (79.5%). On MathVerse, COGFLOW-7B (39.5%) matches GPT-4V (39.4%) but trails MathFlow*GPT-4V (43.8%). On WeMath and DynaMath, COGFLOW-7B (64.1% and 46.2%) trails the strongest closed-source models (GPT-5: 71.1%/60.9%; Gemini-2.5-Pro: 78.0%/56.3%) but exceeds GLM-4.1V-9B (63.8%/42.5%) and all open-source 8B models. The largest relative gains appear on visually dense subsets (e.g., Vision Primary on FlowVerse: 63.5% vs. 57.5% for GPT-5), confirming that the perception-internalization-reasoning pipeline is most impactful when visual evidence is critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

COGFLOW extends the decoupled perception-reasoning pipeline (MathFlow, DVLR, VCAR) by inserting an explicit knowledge-internalization stage between perception and reasoning, inspired by cognitive science. Its RL training builds on GRPO's group-baseline advantage estimation (DeepSeek-R1) and Softmax-DPO for multi-negative preference optimization, but introduces a visual gate that adaptively filters low-quality perception trajectories before reasoning generation—a mechanism absent in prior multimodal RL work. The paper departs from prior perception-enhancement efforts (SVE-Math, MultiMath, VCAR) by arguing that accurate extraction alone is insufficient: the critical gap is whether extracted visual cues are faithfully integrated into downstream reasoning, which it diagnoses as 'reasoning drift.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of reasoning drift as a systematic, measurable failure mode in decoupled pipelines provides a useful diagnostic lens for the field, and the MATHCOG dataset (120K+ samples with disentangled perception/reasoning annotations) offers a practical resource for training perception-grounded multimodal reasoners. However, the approach is computationally demanding (16 A100s, two-stage training), relies on OpenCV-based primitive extraction limited to points/lines/circles, and the visual gate requires ground-truth primitives during training, limiting direct transfer to open-ended visual reasoning. Whether the three-stage cognitive decomposition generalizes beyond geometric diagrams to natural scenes or more complex visual domains remains an open question the authors acknowledge.
