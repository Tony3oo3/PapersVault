## Problem

While LLMs struggle with multi-hop question answering, no formal theoretical bound existed to explain why single-pass reasoning fundamentally fails as task complexity grows. This paper derives a Fano-style accuracy upper bound that formalizes the information bottleneck: when a task's information demand exceeds the model's finite per-pass output capacity, accuracy does not degrade gracefully but collapses sharply (the 'Accuracy Cliff'). The work further identifies two compounding challenges specific to MHQA—stepwise capacity overflow and cross-step error accumulation—that make the single-pass paradigm inherently inadequate.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user query and a long context (0.5k–10k tokens) containing a small number of necessary evidence snippets embedded among semantically similar distractors and irrelevant noise, with evidence deliberately placed out of logical reasoning order to enforce non-trivial navigation. For single-pass baselines the model produces a reasoning trace and final answer in one generation; for InfoQA the output is a sequence of contracted sub-queries resolved across multiple LLM calls, each yielding a bridge entity. The synthetic benchmark systematically varies hop count (1–4) and context length to precisely modulate the task-side information demand β.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution is a Fano-style accuracy upper bound (Theorem 1) combining conditional Fano's inequality with an output entropy bound, yielding Acc ≤ min{1, (C+1)/β} under uniform answer distributions, where β is task information demand and C is model output capacity. To connect theory to observable task properties, β is modeled as β(h,L) = β₀ + αLγ^(h−1), capturing super-linear growth with hop count h and context length L. On the practical side, InfoQA is a multi-call framework built on three synergistic mechanisms: capacity-aware task decomposition (splitting multi-hop queries into single-hop sub-questions to keep per-step demand below C), a dependency-explicit workflow (passing reasoning state via explicitly contracted queries rather than relying on internal memory), and iterative query contraction (pruning past reasoning traces and rewriting the query with the latest finding to prevent context inflation). Experiments on Qwen3-8B/14B confirm that empirical F1 scores align with the predicted capacity curves and that InfoQA consistently outperforms single-pass baselines, with gains growing at higher hop counts and longer contexts.

## Results

On the synthetic MHQA benchmark (Qwen3-14B, F1 metric), InfoQA achieves an overall average of 0.86 on 2–4 hop tasks, substantially outperforming the strongest single-pass baselines: Self-Consistency (0.75) and CoT (0.73). The advantage grows with reasoning depth: at 4 hops, InfoQA reaches 0.80 F1 while the best single-pass method (S-C) drops to 0.61. InfoQA also shows strong length robustness, maintaining 0.54 F1 at 10k context length (2–4 hop) versus 0.40 for S-C and 0.37 for CoT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfoQA outperforms all six single-pass baselines across every hop count and context length setting. Against the strongest baseline, Self-Consistency (Wang et al., ICLR 2023), InfoQA leads by 11 points on the 2–4 hop overall average (0.86 vs. 0.75), with the gap widening to 19 points at 4 hops (0.80 vs. 0.61). CoT (Wei et al., NeurIPS 2022) is the second-strongest single-pass method at 0.73 overall; InfoQA exceeds it by 13 points overall and by 23 points at 4 hops (0.80 vs. 0.57). Self-Refine (Madaan et al., NeurIPS 2023) trails most significantly at 0.57 overall, a 29-point gap. ReAct (Yao et al., ICLR 2023) and Plan-and-Solve (Wang et al., ACL 2023) both score 0.66 overall, each 20 points below InfoQA. Self-Ask (Press et al., EMNLP 2023) achieves 0.65 overall, 21 points behind InfoQA, and its large base-demand overhead (β₀=160 in the fitted bound) offsets its higher effective capacity. Notably, InfoQA's advantage is most pronounced in the long-context, high-hop regime (e.g., 4-hop at 8k: 0.69 vs. 0.16 for the best single-pass method), confirming the theoretical prediction that multi-call decomposition prevents the Accuracy Cliff.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new theoretical ground by repurposing classical information-theoretic tools (Fano's inequality and output entropy bounds) to derive a concrete accuracy ceiling for LLM single-pass reasoning, a perspective absent from prior multi-hop QA work that has focused on prompting strategies (CoT, ReAct, Self-Consistency) or retrieval-based approaches. While multi-call reasoning methods (Self-Refine, multi-step decomposition) have empirically demonstrated the benefits of distributing reasoning across multiple model calls, this paper provides the first information-theoretic justification for *why* this works: single-pass outputs face a finite entropy budget that, once exceeded, causes a sharp accuracy collapse rather than graceful degradation. The InfoQA framework is positioned as a proof-of-concept that operationalizes the theoretical principles of capacity-aware decomposition and iterative query contraction, rather than as a competitive SOTA method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Fano-style accuracy bound and the 'Accuracy Cliff' concept give practitioners a principled diagnostic for when single-pass reasoning will fail and multi-call decomposition becomes necessary, potentially guiding architecture and prompting decisions beyond MHQA to any task with high information demand. However, the empirical validation rests on a synthetic benchmark with one model family (Qwen3-8B/14B), and the theoretical model relies on simplifying assumptions (uniform answer distribution, parametric β form) that may not hold in open-domain settings. Open questions include extending the analysis to multi-call settings (how information accumulates across calls), developing adaptive decomposition strategies, and testing the capacity-bound perspective on real-world noisy domains like scientific literature or legal reasoning.
