## Problem

Foundation VLMs like CLIP suffer a persistent accuracy-robustness trade-off: adversarial fine-tuning improves robustness but erodes clean accuracy, and finding an acceptable compromise typically demands costly hyperparameter searches with multiple retraining runs. The paper shows that naive task-vector merging of naturally and robustly fine-tuned VLMs fails to resolve this tension, producing a near-linear clean-robust frontier with no interior optimum, because uniform addition ignores which parameter coordinates are jointly compatible versus conflicting across the two objectives.

## Input / Output

PISTOLE consumes two off-the-shelf CLIP vision encoders (a naturally fine-tuned one trained by ERM on clean ImageNet data and a robustly fine-tuned one trained via PMG with 10-step PGD at ℓ∞ ε=2/255) along with their shared pre-trained checkpoint, from which it extracts task vectors (parameter displacements). The output is a single merged vision encoder parameterized by a mixing scalar λ (default 0.2), which can be used directly for zero-shot image classification or plugged as a drop-in replacement into downstream VLMs such as LLaVA-1.5-7B and OpenFlamingo-9B for captioning, VQA, hallucination evaluation, and CoT reasoning without any further training.

## Method

PISTOLE builds complementary, gradient-informed stability masks by computing per-parameter gradient magnitudes under both the natural and adversarial objectives, normalizing them per layer, and then inverting each objective's sensitivity to form the counterpart's mask (M_nat = (1 − g̃_rob)^κ, M_rob = (1 − g̃_nat)^κ), so that coordinates the other objective would strongly update are suppressed. These single-point masks are refined by tracing short adversarial parameter trajectories (K projected gradient-ascent steps in a Frobenius ball around each fine-tuned solution), with each step weighted by a Prediction Criticality Index (PCI) that the paper proves tracks the Hessian trace of the prediction, thereby up-weighting high-curvature, fragile regions. The final merged displacement is τ*(λ) = λ(M_nat^path ⊙ τ_nat) + (1−λ)(M_rob^path ⊙ τ_rob), applied to the pre-trained parameters. Theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) shows the capped masks provably contract worst-case first-order cross-objective interference by tunable factors ρ ≤ 1, and (Theorem 2) shows PCI ≈ (σ²/2)·Tr(H_c)/p_c, explaining why the merge gravitates toward flatter, more generalizable minima.

## Results

On the average of 14 zero-shot classification benchmarks (CLIP ViT-L/14, AutoAttack ℓ∞ ε=2/255), PISTOLE achieves 69.24% clean accuracy, 47.65% robust accuracy, and a combined Sum of 116.89, outperforming the best adversarial fine-tuning baseline (PMG: 64.46/45.74/110.20) by +4.78 clean, +1.91 robust, and +6.69 Sum points. On in-distribution ImageNet, PISTOLE reaches 80.82% clean and 61.89% robust (Sum 142.71), edging out TeCoA (80.00/61.74) and PMG (77.84/60.02). The method also transfers as a drop-in vision encoder to LLaVA-1.5-7B and OpenFlamingo-9B, improving CIDEr on COCO captioning (110.6 clean / 54.9 robust) and VQA accuracy (e.g., 73.7/36.9 on VQAv2) over all adversarial baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PISTOLE outperforms all four adversarial fine-tuning baselines on every reported metric. Against TeCoA (Mao et al., 2023), PISTOLE gains +7.68 clean and +4.39 robust on average (Sum 116.89 vs. 104.82), with the largest clean-accuracy gap on fine-grained and texture datasets (e.g., Stanford Cars: 67.35 vs. 44.45). Against PMG (Wang et al., 2024), the closest competitor, PISTOLE leads by +4.78 clean and +1.91 robust on average, and by +6.69 in Sum; on ImageNet specifically the gap narrows to +2.98 clean / +1.87 robust, which the authors attribute to PMG's strong in-distribution robustness. Against FARE (Schlarmann et al., 2024), PISTOLE improves clean accuracy by +3.74 and robust by +4.68 on average, with FARE's feature-level alignment objective sacrificing more clean performance (72.96 vs. 80.82 on ImageNet). Against TGA (Yu et al., 2024), PISTOLE gains +7.13 clean and +2.46 robust on average, with TGA's text-guided attention providing less OOD robustness (e.g., EuroSAT: 26.42 vs. 14.22). Compared to Standard CLIP (Radford et al., 2021), PISTOLE trades a modest 3.84-point clean drop for a 47.64-point robustness gain, yielding a Sum that is 61% higher (116.89 vs. 73.09).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends task vector arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2023) and weight-space interpolation (WiSE-FT, Wortsman et al., 2022b) from the setting of merging similar-task models to the fundamentally harder problem of reconciling conflicting objectives (natural accuracy vs. adversarial robustness). It departs from prior adversarial fine-tuning methods (TeCoA, PMG, FARE, TGA) that require costly retraining and hyperparameter search, and from interference-resolution merging methods (Ties-Merging, AdaMerging) that ignore local loss geometry. The key conceptual departure is using per-parameter gradient magnitudes under both objectives to build complementary stability masks, refined along adversarial parameter trajectories weighted by a curvature-linked Prediction Criticality Index, with provable first-order interference contraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PISTOLE offers a training-free, plug-and-play path to better clean-robust trade-offs in VLMs: one simply swaps the merged vision encoder into downstream frameworks (LLaVA, OpenFlamingo) without retraining, which is practically significant given the compute cost of adversarial fine-tuning. The theoretical guarantees (first-order interference bounds, PCI–Hessian trace link) provide mechanistic understanding beyond empirical tuning. Open questions include whether the approach extends to text-encoder robustness, whether it generalizes to non-CLIP architectures (e.g., diffusion-based VLMs), and whether the gradient-based masks remain informative at much larger model scales where per-parameter sensitivity may be more diffuse.
