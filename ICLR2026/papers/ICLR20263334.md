## Problem

Repeated sampling methods like Best-of-N and Self-Consistency allocate a fixed number of responses per query regardless of difficulty, wasting compute on easy questions and under-exploring hard ones. Prior adaptive strategies rely on hand-crafted stopping heuristics (e.g., three consecutive identical answers) that do not transfer across tasks or model families. The paper asks whether a model's own confidence, once properly calibrated, can serve as a task-independent signal to adapt the sampling budget on the fly.

## Method

Self-Calibration synthesizes (query, response, confidence) triples from nine reasoning datasets by sampling N responses per question with Entropy-based Dynamic Temperature, computing a Soft Self-Consistency (SSC) score per answer group, and using those SSC values as pseudo-labels. A combined SmoothL1 loss regresses the model's P(True) toward the SSC target while a gated generation loss (only on responses above threshold η) preserves reasoning quality—no human annotations required. At test time, three CaTS variants exploit the calibrated score: CaTS-ES stops Best-of-N sampling the moment a response's confidence crosses a dataset-calibrated threshold τ; CaTS-SC replaces the uniform majority vote with a confidence-weighted sum; CaTS-ASC feeds the same weighted frequency into the adaptive-stopping rule. A margin-ratio inequality (Appendix B) shows that whenever the calibrated confidence is more informative than a uniform vote, CaTS-SC provably beats vanilla Self-Consistency in error probability.

## Results

With a sample budget of 16, CaTS-SC improves MathQA accuracy from 73.7% to 83.6% (+9.9) on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, and CaTS-ES improves Best-of-N on Object-Counting from 62.3% to 76.8% (+14.5) on the same model. On the smaller DeepSeek-R1-Distill-1.5B, CaTS-ES boosts ARC-Challenge accuracy from 54.1% to 66.5% (+12.4). The paper further reports that CaTS-SC can save 94.2% of samples relative to vanilla Self-Consistency while reaching 85.0% accuracy on MathQA, and that self-calibrated confidence scores match or exceed reward-model scores (e.g., 84.0% vs. 82.1% on MathQA with Llama) at a cost of only ~10 extra tokens per response.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CaTS-SC consistently outperforms vanilla Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2022) across all three models and all nine dataset-model combinations, with the largest gain of +9.9 on MathQA (Llama) and +12.0 on MathQA (DeepSeek-1.5B at budget 4). CaTS-ES substantially beats Best-of-N (Cobbe et al., 2021), especially on the smaller DeepSeek-R1-Distill-1.5B where it gains +22.7 on Object-Counting and +12.4 on ARC-Challenge, though it slightly underperforms on Qwen ARC-Challenge (−0.3). Against the untrained confidence baselines CISC (Taubenfeld et al., 2025) and Self-Certainty (Kang et al., 2025), CaTS-SC achieves higher accuracy in nearly all settings (e.g., 83.6 vs. 81.2 for CISC and 81.5 for Self-Certainty on Llama MathQA). CaTS-ASC outperforms Adaptive Self-Consistency (Aggarwal et al., 2023) by 2–9 points across models. Compared to the heuristic adaptive methods ESC (Li et al., 2024) and RASC (Wan et al., 2024), CaTS-ES and CaTS-ASC achieve comparable or slightly higher accuracy (e.g., 83.6 vs. 81.0 for ESC on Llama MathQA), while requiring no manually designed stopping rules. Finally, self-calibrated confidence scores match or exceed dedicated reward models (RLHFlow, Qwen PRM) in Best-of-16 selection (Table 3), achieving 84.0% vs. 82.1% on Llama MathQA without deploying an additional model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: repeated-sampling test-time scaling (Self-Consistency, Best-of-N, Adaptive Self-Consistency) and LLM confidence calibration (P(True), verbalized confidence). Its key departure from prior adaptive sampling methods (ESC, RASC) is replacing hand-designed heuristic stopping rules with a learned, calibrated confidence signal. The Self-Calibration training procedure is the conceptual novelty: it distills Soft Self-Consistency-derived confidence (itself built on Wang et al.'s self-consistency calibration) into the model via a SmoothL1 objective, enabling reliable single-pass confidence estimation without any human-labeled data. This positions the work as a bridge between the calibration literature and the test-time scaling literature, unifying them through a self-supervised distillation step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: CaTS-SC achieves 94.2% sample savings to match vanilla Self-Consistency accuracy, and the self-calibrated confidence matches or exceeds dedicated reward models of the same size while requiring only ~10 extra tokens of inference. The label-free training recipe (no ground-truth answers needed) makes the approach broadly applicable and easy to deploy via LoRA. Open questions include whether the calibration quality degrades for much larger models (only 7B-scale tested), whether the method extends to non-reasoning or open-ended generation tasks, and whether the theoretical exponential-improvement condition holds in practice across diverse confidence distributions. The finding that binary correctness supervision actually harms early-stopping performance (confidence collapses to 0/1) is a useful caution against a seemingly natural alternative.
