## Problem

Existing protein foundation models fall into two camps: sequence-only language models that lack three-dimensional geometric information, and MSA-based predictors (e.g., AlphaFold 2/3) that degrade in mutation-rich or alignment-sparse regimes such as antibody CDR loops and rapidly evolving pathogens. No prior model jointly learns bidirectional sequence-structure mappings without requiring MSAs, and earlier joint models could not scale the structural component because full-atom representations consumed most of the available parameter budget.

## Input / Output

FlexRibbon accepts protein amino acid sequences (up to 1024 residues) and optionally ligand SMILES strings, antigen 3D structures, and epitope annotations, but critically does not require MSA input. It outputs all-atom 3D coordinates for proteins and small molecules, designed CDR amino acid sequences, continuous binding affinity scores, EC/GO functional labels, and per-residue confidence estimates (pLDDT) plus pairwise alignment error (PAE) from a dedicated confidence head. The hierarchical output is produced in two passes: a coarse-grained residue/atom-level denoising step followed by an all-atom refinement step that enforces covalent-bond chemical validity via learned attention biases.

## Method

FlexRibbon is a 3B-parameter model with a three-stage hierarchical architecture: a 32-layer Transformer sequence module (with RoPE and a learned 2D covalent-bond feature matrix), a 16-layer Diffusion Transformer (DiT) for coarse-grained residue/atom denoising, and an 8-layer DiT for all-atom refinement conditioned on the coarse outputs. Pretraining couples masked language modeling with variance-exploding diffusion denoising through a Structure-Informed MLM (SIMLM) strategy that interleaves three perturbation modes—clean-sequence-to-noisy-structure, localized coupled perturbation (15% residues masked and noised), and global perturbation (15% masked, all structures noised)—at a 6:2:2 ratio to enforce bidirectional sequence-structure learning. A confidence-weighted diffusion loss scales per-residue MSE by a sigmoid of pLDDT (β=5, centered at 70) to down-weight unreliable predicted structures, and rotational invariance is achieved via random SO(3) data augmentation rather than equivariant architectures. Training follows a three-stage curriculum (384→768→1024 residues) that defers MLM to Stage B and introduces the confidence head in Stage C, with the total loss combining diffusion MSE, MLM, inter-residue distance, and smooth-lDDT terms.

## Results

FlexRibbon achieves state-of-the-art performance across 12 tasks. On antibody interface prediction (SAb23H2), it reaches 61.3% success rate (DockQ ≥ 0.23), a 14.6-point absolute gain over IgGM (46.7%) and 24.5 points over AlphaFold 3 (36.8%). On protein-ligand docking (PoseBusters V1), FlexRibbon achieves 71.82% success rate in the random-1 regime as a single-sequence method, surpassing all single-sequence baselines (DiffDock 37.9%, DiffDock-L 50.0%) and reaching near-parity with MSA-based AlphaFold 3 (76.4%). On CASP15 monomer structure prediction, it achieves a TM-score of 0.703, exceeding ESMFold (0.684) and ESM3 (0.673), with a confidence head achieving Pearson R = 0.89 between predicted pTM and observed TM-score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexRibbon outperforms all baselines on antibody and nanobody interface prediction: it beats IgGM by 14.6 points in success rate (61.3% vs 46.7%) and AlphaFold 3 by 24.5 points (61.3% vs 36.8%), while also achieving the best DockQ (0.384) and lowest LRMS (14.53 Å). On protein-peptide prediction (FoldBench), FlexRibbon achieves 91.4% SR, exceeding AlphaFold 3 (84.3%) and PepGLAD (81.2%). For protein-ligand docking, FlexRibbon as a single-sequence method (71.82% random-1) substantially surpasses DiffDock (37.9%) and DiffDock-L (50.0%) but falls slightly short of MSA-based Protenix (77.10% top-1) and AlphaFold 3 (76.40%), though its oracle score (78.70%) exceeds both. On binding affinity (CASF-2016), FlexRibbon achieves the best RMSE (1.150) and Pearson R (0.848), outperforming SPIN (1.258/0.826), GIANT (1.269/0.814), and SIGN (1.316/0.797). For function prediction, FlexRibbon leads on all four EC/GO sub-tasks, with the largest margin on GO-CC (0.560 vs ESM-GearNet's 0.464, a 0.096 gain), while on EC it edges out ESM-GearNet by only 0.001 (0.891 vs 0.890). On ligand-induced conformational change, FlexRibbon achieves TM-ens of 0.889 (0.901 with ligand), improving over ESMDiff (0.851), ESMFlow (0.856), and AlphaFlow (0.864).

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexRibbon occupies the gap between two dominant protein modeling paradigms: sequence-only language models (ESM-2, ProtT5) that lack geometric information, and MSA-based predictors (AlphaFold 2/3) that degrade in mutation-rich or alignment-sparse regimes. Architecturally, it builds on the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) for structure denoising and adopts the variance-exploding diffusion schedule and sampling procedure from Karras et al. (2022) / AlphaFold 3, but departs from prior joint models by introducing a hierarchical three-stage design (sequence → coarse-grained → all-atom) that allocates scalable capacity to both modalities, overcoming the memory bottleneck that previously concentrated parameters on the sequence side. The Structure-Informed Masked Language Model (SIMLM) with its three perturbation modes (sequence-to-structure, coupled local perturbation, global perturbation) is a novel training formulation that explicitly enforces bidirectional sequence-structure learning rather than the one-way mapping used in most prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in addressing a well-recognized failure mode: MSA-based methods lose accuracy precisely where biology is most challenging—antibody CDR loops, intrinsically disordered interfaces, and rapidly evolving pathogens. By demonstrating competitive or superior performance across 12 diverse tasks without requiring MSAs, FlexRibbon offers a more broadly applicable foundation model for protein science. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large multi-chain complexes (inference time grows superlinearly with residue count), whether the confidence-weighted loss adequately handles the long tail of low-confidence predicted structures, and whether the joint pretraining signal transfers to non-protein biomolecular systems (nucleic acids, glycans). The paper does not provide ablations isolating the contribution of each SIMLM mode or the hierarchical architecture versus a flat design, leaving some architectural choices empirically under-motivated.
