## Problem

Existing ViT compression methods retain substantial class-irrelevant knowledge when deploying on edge devices, leading to suboptimal performance on target classes. Moreover, the conventional prune-then-train paradigm causes irreversible knowledge loss at high pruning rates, and the distribution of class-specific versus class-agnostic knowledge across ViT modules remained unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260440/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Vulcan takes a pre-trained ViT (DeiT-Base/Small/Tiny or Fast/Mask R-CNN with Swin-T backbone), a sub-task specification (a subset S of target classes), and a resource budget expressed as a pruning rate R, and produces a compact class-specific edge ViT. Standard RGB images are the input; outputs are class labels (recognition), bounding boxes (detection), or segmentation masks (instance segmentation). Pruned dimensions are aligned to multiples of 8 to ensure hardware-accelerated inference on diverse edge devices such as Jetson Orin NX.

## Method

Vulcan is built on the insight that FFN neurons encode interpretable class-specific knowledge while MHA modules capture class-agnostic patterns, enabling module-specific compression strategies. It follows a novel train-then-prune paradigm: during post-training, Class-Centric Neuron Collapse (CCNC) clusters FFN neurons via k-means and collapses each cluster onto the anchor neuron with the highest class-specific activation, deliberately introducing redundancy; simultaneously, Truncated Nuclear Norm Regularization (TNNR) enforces low-rankness in MHA projection matrices using effective-rank-adaptive per-block pruning rates. Both constraints are integrated through an augmented Lagrangian framework with learnable multipliers. After post-training, FFN pruning retains only anchor neurons (summing corresponding W2 rows), and MHA pruning is performed via SVD truncation of the QK and VO dimensions. The paper formally proves that when the collapse and rank constraints are fully satisfied, the pruned model produces identical outputs to the post-trained base ViT, guaranteeing near-lossless compression.

## Results

On DeiT-Base with ImageNet-1K sub-tasks, Vulcan-derived edge ViTs achieve up to 15.12% higher top-1 accuracy than the base ViT while retaining only 20%–40% of its size. At a 60% pruning rate, Vulcan reaches 95.60% average top-1 accuracy on 25-class sub-tasks (vs. 81.05% for the base ViT and 92.64% for the best baselines X-Pruner/MDP), retaining 97.63% of the fine-tuned base ViT's accuracy. At an aggressive 80% pruning rate, Vulcan still achieves 93.04% on 25-class sub-tasks, outperforming X-Pruner (85.74%) and MDP (85.47%) by 7.30 and 7.57 points respectively. On Jetson Orin NX, Vulcan-derived models achieve 1.23×–3.02× latency speedup and reduce memory by up to 76.47%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vulcan outperforms all five baselines (Random, NiViT, X-Pruner, DC-ViT, MDP) across every sub-task size and pruning rate. At R=0.60 on 25-class sub-tasks, Vulcan (95.60%) surpasses the two strongest baselines—X-Pruner (92.64%) and MDP (92.64%)—by 2.96 points, and exceeds the base ViT (81.05%) by 14.55 points. The gap widens substantially at R=0.80, where Vulcan (93.04%) leads X-Pruner (85.74%) by 7.30 points and MDP (85.47%) by 7.57 points, while NiViT and DC-ViT collapse to 60.85% and 61.81% respectively. Random pruning, despite being class-agnostic, remains competitive at R=0.60 (91.39%) because it shares the same pruning dimensions as Vulcan, but degrades sharply at R=0.80 (77.58%). DC-ViT performs worst overall due to its coarse module-level pruning granularity, and NiViT suffers the most severe degradation at high pruning rates, dropping below 61% at R=0.80.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the conventional prune-then-train paradigm used by structured pruning methods for ViTs (NiViT, X-Pruner, DC-ViT, MDP) by introducing a train-then-prune approach that deliberately injects redundancy before compression. It builds on the 'knowledge neurons' concept from NLP transformers (Geva et al., 2021; Dai et al., 2022) and extends it to ViTs, identifying FFN neurons as carriers of class-specific knowledge while MHA captures class-agnostic patterns. The SVD-based MHA compression leverages the Eckart-Young optimality theorem, and the redundancy enforcement uses augmented Lagrangian optimization (Birgin & Martinez, 2014), while the FFN pruning is essentially the inverse of network expansion (Ding et al., 2023). The key conceptual departure is framing model compression as a class-specific derivation problem rather than a generic size-reduction task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The knowledge disentanglement finding (FFN encodes class-specific knowledge, MHA captures class-agnostic patterns) provides a structural understanding of ViTs that could inform future compression, interpretability, and modular design work. The train-then-prune paradigm offers a general recipe for near-lossless structured pruning that could extend beyond ViTs to other architectures. However, the method requires knowing target classes in advance and incurs post-training compute, limiting its applicability to open-vocabulary or multi-tenant edge scenarios. Open questions include whether the disentanglement insight holds for generative ViTs or multimodal models, and whether the approach can be adapted to dynamic class sets without re-derivation.
