## Problem

Reasoning LLMs that generate long chain-of-thought traces suffer from quadratic compute and memory growth due to self-attention, making extended reasoning and RL training with verifiable rewards prohibitively expensive at scale. Prior efficiency methods shorten or prune reasoning traces but remain bound to the quadratic cost structure. The paper asks whether an LLM can reason effectively while retaining only a fixed number of recent thinking tokens, decoupling total reasoning length from context length.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt (system prompt plus question) as input and produces a Delethink trace: a sequence of (prompt, response) chunk pairs where each subsequent chunk's prompt is the original query concatenated with the last m tokens of the previous chunk's response. The per-chunk context is bounded (e.g., 8K tokens), so peak memory stays constant regardless of total thinking length, which can extend to hundreds of thousands or millions of tokens. The final chunk ends with an EOS token carrying the answer.

## Method

Delethink implements the Markovian Thinking Paradigm by chunking reasoning into fixed-size segments where each new chunk conditions only on the original query and a small suffix (the Markovian state) of the previous chunk, deleting all earlier reasoning tokens. Delethink Inference applies this zero-shot to off-the-shelf reasoning models (1.5B–30B parameters) with no prompting or training, revealing latent Markovian behavior. Delethink Training uses GRPO-style RL with per-chunk PPO-clipped policy gradients to train native Markovian thinkers, where the loss normalizes by total thinking tokens across all chunks in a trace. The key architectural consequence is that compute scales linearly (O(nS) FLOPs) and KV-cache memory stays O(n) with thinking length S, versus O(nS²) and O(nS) for LongCoT, enabling 40% faster generation and 70% less memory at equal thinking budgets.

## Results

On zero-shot Delethink inference, R1DistillQwen-1.5B achieves 37.6% on AIME'24 and 29.7% on AIME'25, surpassing its LongCoT-32k baseline (28.2%, 24.2%) by 9.4 and 5.5 points respectively. The 7B model reaches 64.8% (AIME'24) and 47.5% (AIME'25) vs. 53.1% and 39.3% for LongCoT. In the RL training experiment on DeepScaleR, Delethink matches LongCoT-24k benchmark accuracy while generating tokens 40% faster (8,500 vs. 6,000 tok/s per H100) and reducing KV-cache memory by 70%. At 1M thinking tokens, Delethink achieves a 17× FLOPs reduction over LongCoT, and training to 96K average thinking length requires only 7 H100-months vs. 27 for LongCoT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard LongCoT (long chain-of-thought) inference and RL training, following the R1/DeepSeek-R1 paradigm. On zero-shot inference, Delethink outperforms LongCoT-32k on AIME'24 by 9.4 points (1.5B) and 11.7 points (7B), and on AIME'25 by 5.5 and 8.2 points respectively; on GPQA-Diamond and LiveCodeBench the gains are smaller (0.9–1.9 points). At the 14B scale, Delethink wins AIME'24 (72.6 vs. 68.9) but slightly trails on GPQA-Diamond (57.5 vs. 59.1), suggesting diminishing returns at larger model sizes. The secondary baseline, Budget-Force (S1-style forced continuation), consistently underperforms both LongCoT and Delethink across all model sizes, confirming that naive prompt-level forcing is ineffective for R1-distill models. In the RL training comparison, Delethink matches LongCoT-24k accuracy on AIME'24/25/HMMT while completing each RL step in 215 s vs. 248.5 s, and sustaining constant generation throughput regardless of thinking length, whereas LongCoT throughput degrades linearly with context length.

## Relation to Prior Work

Delethink carves new ground by reframing the efficiency problem: rather than shortening reasoning traces (pruning, summarization, distillation), modifying attention (sliding window, Mamba, linear attention), or compressing the KV cache (eviction, quantization), it restructures the reasoning process itself into fixed-size chunks where each chunk conditions only on the original prompt plus a small suffix of the previous chunk. This 'Markovian Thinking' paradigm is orthogonal to all prior efficiency efforts and requires no architectural changes or retraining to apply. The RL training component builds directly on GRPO/PPO-style policy gradients for LLMs, with the key adaptation being that the trajectory is now a sequence of (prompt, response) chunks rather than a single growing sequence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: linear compute and constant memory for reasoning open the door to thinking at millions of tokens without the quadratic wall that currently constrains RLVR and test-time scaling. The zero-shot applicability to any off-the-shelf reasoning LLM (1.5B–30B) makes this immediately deployable, and the RL training path enables native Markovian thinkers that scale further. Key limitations include tasks requiring large working memory beyond the carried state (e.g., 14×14 crosswords), and the Markovian state size appears model-dependent (4K suffices for R1-Distill but Qwen3-30B needs 16K). Open questions include whether the paradigm generalizes to non-mathematical reasoning, how state size should scale with task complexity, and whether the crossover point (~30K tokens where Delethink's FLOPs become lower than LongCoT) shifts with model size.
