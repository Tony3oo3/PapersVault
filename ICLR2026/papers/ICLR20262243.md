## Problem

Prior CoT faithfulness studies provide only aggregate, mechanism-level evidence that CoTs *can* be unfaithful, but leave open the practical question of whether a *specific* reasoning trajectory is faithful to the model's internal process. There is no rigorous instance-level task formulation, no expert-annotated ground-truth dataset capturing diverse unfaithfulness patterns, and no systematic comparison of detection methods under a unified protocol.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262243/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each instance pairs a query from one of four benchmarks (LogicQA, TruthfulQA, AQuA, HLE-Bio) with a CoT trajectory and final answer generated by one of four LLMs (LLaMA3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, GPT-4o-mini, Gemini 2.5 Flash). The detection function outputs a binary faithful/unfaithful label; for unfaithful cases, annotations further specify the primary reason (post-hoc reasoning or spurious reasoning chain), the specific step indices responsible, and a short natural-language explanation. The FINE-COT dataset contains over 1,000 trajectories with 300+ annotated unfaithful instances, each carrying step-level evidence rather than just a global label.

## Method

FAITHCOT-BENCH integrates three components into a single benchmark. First, it formalizes instance-level CoT unfaithfulness detection as a binary classification problem f(q, C) → {0,1}, where the internal reasoning path R is unobservable and faithfulness is assessed via observable signals. Second, it introduces FINE-COT, a dataset annotated through a three-round expert protocol (independent labeling, collaborative resolution, cross-checking) that operationalizes a two-category taxonomy—post-hoc reasoning and spurious reasoning chains—into eight fine-grained principles (e.g., step skipping, selective explanation bias, unjustified reversal), achieving Cohen's Kappa of 81.0–97.2. Third, it benchmarks eleven detection methods spanning counterfactual perturbations (adding mistakes, option shuffling, removing steps, early answering, paraphrasing), logit-based diagnostics (Answer Tracing, Information Gain), and LLM-as-judge evaluators (Step-Judge, Faithful-Judge), revealing that LLM-as-judge methods consistently outperform alternatives by over 30% in F1, logit-based methods perform worst, and detection difficulty increases in knowledge-intensive domains and with more advanced models.

## Results

Across 4 datasets and 4 LLMs, LLM-as-judge methods (Faithful-Judge, Step-Judge) consistently achieve the highest F1 scores for CoT unfaithfulness detection, ranging from 50.6 to 79.2, outperforming all other paradigms by over 30% on average. Counterfactual-based methods (e.g., Adding Mistakes) reach up to 66.7 F1 on AQuA with LLaMA3.1-8B but drop to 15.4–45.5 on knowledge-intensive tasks like TruthfulQA and HLE-Bio. Logit-based methods (Answer Tracing, Information Gain) perform worst, frequently scoring below 50 F1 and as low as 9.5 (Information Gain on HLE-Bio with LLaMA3.1). The best single result is Faithful-Judge achieving 79.2 F1 on HLE-Bio with LLaMA3.1-8B, while the weakest advanced-model result is Faithful-Judge at 41.6 F1 on AQuA with Gemini 2.5 Flash.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Faithful-Judge (Arcuschin et al., 2025) is the strongest method overall, achieving F1 scores of 50.6–79.2 and outperforming all other paradigms in 15 of 16 model–dataset combinations; it beats Step-Judge (Wen et al., 2025) by 5–12 F1 points on most settings (e.g., 79.2 vs. 69.2 on HLE-Bio/LLaMA3.1, 76.1 vs. 59.6 on TruthfulQA/Qwen2.5). The five counterfactual perturbations from Lanham et al. (2023) show strong domain dependence: Adding Mistakes reaches 66.7 F1 on AQuA/LLaMA3.1 (the best counterfactual result) but collapses to 15.4 on HLE-Bio/GPT-4o-mini, confirming that causal interventions only work when reasoning chains are tightly coupled. Logit-based methods—Answer Tracing (Huang et al., 2025) and Information Gain (Li et al., 2024)—consistently underperform, with F1 scores frequently below 50 and Information Gain as low as 9.5 on HLE-Bio/LLaMA3.1, indicating that token-level probability signals are insufficient for instance-level faithfulness detection. The perplexity baseline (Cui et al., 2025) performs near or below random in most settings (e.g., 5.7 F1 on TruthfulQA/Qwen2.5), confirming that surface fluency is a poor proxy for reasoning faithfulness. Notably, stronger closed-source models (GPT-4o-mini, Gemini 2.5 Flash) do not yield easier detection: Faithful-Judge drops from 76.1 (Qwen2.5/TruthfulQA) to ~50–53 on the same dataset with GPT-4o-mini and Gemini, suggesting that more fluent models produce more deceptively plausible unfaithful CoTs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reframing CoT faithfulness from a mechanism-level diagnostic question (can CoTs be unfaithful?) into an instance-level discriminative task (is this specific trajectory faithful?). Prior work across counterfactual perturbation (Lanham et al., 2023; Paul et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2025), logit analysis (Li et al., 2024; Siegel et al., 2024), and LLM-as-judge evaluation (Arcuschin et al., 2025; Wen et al., 2025) has established that CoTs are often unfaithful in aggregate, but none formalized per-instance detection or provided expert-annotated ground truth with fine-grained causal labels. FAITHCOT-BENCH repurposes these existing detection methods as baselines under a unified binary-classification formulation, while introducing the FINE-COT dataset and a two-category taxonomy (post-hoc reasoning, spurious reasoning chains) refined into eight operational signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a practical gap: nearly 40% of instances show divergence between answer correctness and reasoning faithfulness, meaning accuracy metrics alone are insufficient for high-stakes deployment. The scalability paradox—stronger models (GPT-4o-mini, Gemini 2.5 Flash) produce more deceptively plausible unfaithful CoTs that are harder to detect—challenges the assumption that model improvement automatically improves transparency. Key open questions remain: whether observable annotation signals truly align with internal computation (the paper acknowledges R is unobservable), whether mechanistic interpretability tools (causal probing, circuit analysis) can validate or replace human annotations, and how to design faithfulness-oriented training objectives that don't sacrifice accuracy.
