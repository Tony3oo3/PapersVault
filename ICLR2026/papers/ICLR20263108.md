## Problem

Existing Chain-of-Thought and multi-path reasoning methods (ToT, GoT, AoT) rely on autoregressive, step-by-step generation that is highly susceptible to early-stage error propagation and lacks a principled structural mechanism for pruning redundancy or identifying critical reasoning features. While these methods expand the reasoning space, they do not provide a global analytical framework to characterize the topology and dependency patterns within reasoning chains, leaving error accumulation and redundant exploration uncontrolled.

## Input / Output

The method takes a problem statement and N candidate reasoning paths sampled from an LLM (temperature 0.7, top-p 0.95, up to 16 LLM calls per example). It produces a final answer via confidence/persistence-weighted voting, an interpretable reasoning skeleton consisting of a backbone path and embedded self-consistent verification loops, and topological statistics (contributing paths, average edge weight, loop lifespan). The skeleton is a directed subgraph of the Global Hypothesis Graph, with loops instantiated via minimum-weight cycle basis (Horton's algorithm) and spliced into the backbone at pivot nodes near median progress.

## Method

GHS-TDA is a two-stage construct-analyze framework. In the construction stage, a multi-role agenda mechanism (explorers, verifiers, bridges) integrates N sampled reasoning paths into a Global Hypothesis Graph (GHS) by semantically aligning and merging equivalent nodes via canonical-form similarity (threshold θ_merge=0.85), with edges encoding adjacency, support, and refutation relations. In the analysis stage, each node is embedded into a joint feature vector combining L2-normalized semantic embeddings, graph-structural encodings (progress, BFS position, centrality), and uncertainty (negative log-confidence), and a hybrid distance metric (α=0.6, β=0.3, γ=0.1) drives a sparsified KNN graph (k≈15, 95th-percentile truncation) on which a Vietoris-Rips filtration is built. Persistent homology up to H₁ is computed via GUDHI; H₀ barcodes identify stable reasoning clusters (backbones) and H₁ barcodes identify self-consistent verification loops, with significant features selected by lifespan. The final skeleton is extracted by computing shortest-path backbones within each cluster and splicing in the principal loop (by lifespan) at a pivot near median progress, yielding a compact, topologically grounded reasoning chain that is robust to local perturbations and interpretable to human evaluators.

## Results

GHS-TDA achieves average EM accuracy of 68.0% (GPT-4o-mini), 67.6% (Qwen-Turbo), and 68.3% (DeepSeek-V3) across eight reasoning benchmarks, consistently outperforming the strongest baseline AoT (66.9%, 66.8%, 67.3%). Headline per-dataset results on GPT-4o-mini include 83.9% on MATH, 88.4% on BBH, 81.4% on HotpotQA, and 39.8% on MuSiQue. The method also maintains a fixed upper bound of 19 LLM calls per problem, reducing inference cost by ~25–30% relative to ToT and ~35–40% relative to AoT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GHS-TDA outperforms all nine baselines on average across all three backbones. The closest competitor is AoT (Teng et al., 2025), which GHS-TDA surpasses by 1.1–1.5 points in average EM (e.g., 68.0% vs. 66.9% on GPT-4o-mini; 68.3% vs. 67.3% on DeepSeek-V3), with the largest per-dataset gaps on OlympiadBench (+2.4 pts) and BBH (+2.4 pts). GoT (Besta et al., 2024) and ToT (Yao et al., 2023a) trail by 2.9–3.4 and 3.0–3.3 points respectively, with GHS-TDA showing particularly large advantages on HotpotQA (+7.2 pts over ToT on GPT-4o-mini) and LongBench (+6.7 pts over ToT). AFlow (Zhang et al., 2024) is outperformed by 4.6–4.6 points on average, with the biggest gap on HotpotQA (+7.9 pts). The simpler baselines—CoT (Wei et al., 2022), CoT-SC (Wang et al., 2022), Self-Refine (Madaan et al., 2023), and Analogical Prompting (Yasunaga et al., 2023)—are surpassed by 7.3, 5.9, 6.6, and 12.0 points on average, respectively. FoT (Bi et al., 2024) is outperformed by 5.0–5.2 points, with GHS-TDA gaining most on LongBench (+10.4 pts) and HotpotQA (+14.7 pts on GPT-4o-mini).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the structured-reasoning lineage from CoT through ToT, GoT, and AoT by introducing Topological Data Analysis (persistent homology via Vietoris-Rips filtration) as a principled global analysis tool for reasoning chains. While prior multi-path methods (ToT, GoT, AoT) expand the reasoning space but rely on local heuristics for path selection and conflict resolution, GHS-TDA repurposes TDA—previously applied in bioinformatics and material science—to extract stable H0 backbones and H1 self-consistent loops from a unified Global Hypothesis Graph. The multi-role agenda mechanism (explorers, verifiers, bridges) for graph construction is novel, but the overall 'construct-then-analyze' paradigm is a natural extension of the graph-of-thought idea with a topological selection criterion replacing heuristic scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core contribution is demonstrating that topological persistence (H1 loop lifespan) serves as a task-agnostic reliability signal for reasoning correctness (AUC 0.74, Spearman ρ=0.349), offering a principled alternative to confidence-based path selection. However, the empirical gains over the strongest baseline (AoT) are modest—typically 0.3–1.4 EM points across benchmarks—and the method requires 19 LLM calls per problem, limiting practical adoption. Open questions include whether higher-dimensional homology (H2, H3) captures additional structure, whether the TDA component can be learned end-to-end rather than applied post-hoc, and whether the approach scales to reasoning spaces with thousands of nodes. The fixed computational budget (19 calls) is a practical advantage over variable-cost search methods, but the overall accuracy improvement does not yet justify the added pipeline complexity for most deployments.
