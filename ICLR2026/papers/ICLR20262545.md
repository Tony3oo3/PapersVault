## Problem

Despite producing photorealistic video content, text-to-video generation models' ability to accurately simulate real-world physics remains largely untested. Existing physics-focused benchmarks are limited in scope (fewer than 27 categories, fewer than 700 prompts) and lack scenarios that deliberately violate physics to distinguish genuine physical understanding from mere pattern replication of training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262545/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 1,050 curated text prompts organized into 10 main physics categories (50 subcategories), each with three prompt variations (event, physics-enhanced, detailed narrative) and human-annotated evaluation standards specifying required objects, events, and key physical phenomena. The CAP evaluator takes 8 evenly-sampled frames from a generated video plus the category-specific physics standards as input, and outputs a structured JSON with binary Yes/No judgments for object presence, event depiction, and each individual physical standard, which are aggregated into SA, PC, and combined Both scores.

## Method

PhyWorldBench introduces a three-level taxonomy (Fundamental, Composite, Anti-Physics) with 10 main categories and 50 subcategories, each containing 7 scenarios with 3 prompt types, totaling 1,050 prompts with individually annotated evaluation standards. The key technical contribution is the Context-Aware Prompt (CAP) evaluator, a two-step chain-of-thought prompting strategy for MLLMs that first explicitly informs the model the video is AI-generated and may contain physics violations (counteracting the model's tendency to rationalize unrealistic content), then asks for a thorough video description before requesting structured Yes/No judgments against category-specific standards. This design avoids assuming videos are incorrect by default while achieving 80.3 ROC-AUC on SA and 75.1 on PC with GPT-o1, outperforming raw MLLM baselines by up to 13.5 points on physical commonsense. The novel Anti-Physics category, where prompts intentionally violate real-world laws, serves as a diagnostic to reveal whether models truly understand physics or merely reproduce patterns from predominantly real-world training data.

## Results

Across 12 state-of-the-art text-to-video models evaluated on 1,050 curated prompts, Pika 2.0 achieves the best overall performance with a 'Both' (SA ∧ PC) success rate of 0.262, while Wanx-2.1 leads among open-source models at 0.189. All models show a clear performance degradation from Fundamental Physics (SA ≈ 0.32–0.59) to Composite Physics (SA ≈ 0.16–0.48) to Anti-Physics (SA ≈ 0.06–0.23), indicating that complex and counter-intuitive physical scenarios remain largely unsolved. The proposed Context-Aware Prompt (CAP) evaluator, powered by GPT-o1, achieves ROC-AUC of 80.3 on Semantic Adherence and 75.1 on Physical Commonsense, substantially outperforming raw MLLM baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For the CAP evaluator, the paper compares against three raw MLLM baselines (GPT-4o, GPT-o1, Gemini-2.0-Flash) without any context-aware or chain-of-thought prompting. CAP with GPT-o1 achieves ROC-AUC of 80.3 (SA) and 75.1 (PC), beating raw GPT-o1 (75.4 SA, 61.6 PC) by +4.9 and +13.5 points respectively, raw GPT-4o (72.1 SA, 60.1 PC) by +8.2 and +15.0 points, and raw Gemini-2.0-Flash (74.6 SA, 60.9 PC) by +5.7 and +14.2 points. The largest gain is in Physical Commonsense, where the context-aware framing (informing the MLLM the video is AI-generated and may contain physics errors) is most impactful. Ablations confirm both components matter: removing CoT drops PC to 73.6 and removing the context drops it to 65.6, showing the two-step reasoning and the anti-rationalization prompt are complementary. CAP also generalizes across MLLMs, improving Qwen-VL-2.0 from 59.8→74.1 PC and GPT-4o from 60.1→73.9 PC, demonstrating the method is model-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of physics-focused T2V benchmarks (VideoPhy, PhyGenBench, Physics-IQ, T2VPhysBench) by adopting their Semantic Adherence / Physical Commonsense evaluation framework while substantially expanding coverage to 50 subcategories and 1,050 prompts. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of an Anti-Physics category, where prompts deliberately violate real-world laws, enabling discrimination between true physics understanding and mere pattern replication from training data. For evaluation, the paper builds on Chain-of-Thought prompting and MLLM-based assessment but introduces Context-Aware Prompting (CAP), which explicitly informs the evaluator that the video is AI-generated, addressing the tendency of MLLMs to rationalize unrealistic content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With the best model (Pika 2.0) achieving only 26.2% joint success, the benchmark reveals that current T2V systems remain far from physically reliable, and the Anti-Physics results suggest models largely mimic training-data patterns rather than internalize physical laws. The discovery that higher-quality models are *more* likely to 'rationalize' failures by freezing motion (static-scene rationalization) points to a concerning trajectory as visual fidelity improves. Open questions include whether physics priors or explicit simulation components can close the gap, how to evaluate multi-object interactions at scale, and whether the CAP evaluator's slight aesthetic bias can be fully eliminated for production use.
