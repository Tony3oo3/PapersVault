## Problem

Standard policy gradient methods such as PPO require exact action likelihoods, which are computationally intractable for flow-based and diffusion models. Prior work (DDPO, DPPO, Flow-GRPO) circumvents this by reformulating the denoising chain as a multi-step MDP, but this multiplies the effective horizon, mandates stochastic sampling, and inflates the observation space by treating initial noise as environment states. The paper asks whether the conditional flow matching objective can be integrated directly into the PPO-clip framework, preserving sampler flexibility and avoiding the MDP reformulation entirely.

## Input / Output

The policy network receives the current observation (proprioceptive state, and optionally goal or reference-motion targets for humanoid tracking) together with a noisy action a_t^τ and a scalar noise level τ, and outputs a velocity or noise estimate. During rollouts, a clean action is produced by integrating the learned flow from Gaussian noise over a configurable number of steps; the specific sampler (deterministic Euler, higher-order ODE, stochastic SDE) is a black box and can differ between training and inference. For the humanoid task, the observation includes root height, joint positions/rotations/velocities in a local frame, plus per-joint tracking errors against the reference motion.

## Method

FPO replaces PPO's likelihood ratio with a ratio of per-sample conditional flow matching losses: r^FPO(θ) = exp(L̂_CFM,θ_old(a_t; o_t) − L̂_CFM,θ(a_t; o_t)), where each loss is estimated via N_mc Monte Carlo draws of (τ, ε) pairs. This substitution is justified by the proven equivalence (Kingma & Gao, 2023) between the weighted denoising loss and the negative ELBO, so the ratio decomposes into the true likelihood ratio multiplied by an inverse KL-gap correction—maximizing it simultaneously increases the probability of high-advantage actions and tightens the ELBO approximation. The resulting ratio is plugged directly into the standard PPO-clip surrogate, making FPO a drop-in replacement for Gaussian PPO that inherits compatibility with GAE/GRPO advantage estimation. The method is agnostic to the flow parameterization (ε-prediction, v-prediction, x-prediction) and to the sampling procedure, and the Monte Carlo estimator, while upward-biased in ratio scale, yields directionally unbiased gradients even with N_mc = 1.

## Results

On MuJoCo Playground (10 tasks, 60M steps), FPO achieves an average reward of 759.3±45.3, outperforming Gaussian PPO (667.8±66.0) and DPPO (652.5±83.7), winning on 8 of 10 tasks. On high-dimensional humanoid motion tracking (24 actuated joints, AMASS motions), FPO matches Gaussian PPO under full joint conditioning (96.4% vs 98.7% success rate) but substantially outperforms it under sparse conditioning: 70.6% vs 46.5% success rate with root+hands goals, and 54.3% vs 29.8% with root-only goals. FPO also reduces MPJPE by ~35–50 points in under-conditioned settings (e.g., 62.91 vs 97.65 for root+hands).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Gaussian PPO, FPO achieves a clear win on MuJoCo Playground (759.3 vs 667.8 average reward across 10 tasks) and a decisive win in under-conditioned humanoid control (70.6% vs 46.5% success with root+hands; 54.3% vs 29.8% with root-only), while being slightly behind in fully-conditioned humanoid tracking (96.4% vs 98.7%). Against DPPO (the prior diffusion-based RL method that reformulates denoising as an MDP), FPO also wins on MuJoCo Playground (759.3 vs 652.5 average reward), with the paper attributing the gap to DPPO's longer effective horizon and inability to use deterministic samplers. The key differentiator is that FPO's flow-based policy captures multimodal action distributions—demonstrated in GridWorld where it learns bimodal flows at saddle points—whereas Gaussian PPO collapses to a single mode, and this expressivity advantage becomes critical when the policy must fill in missing joint specifications from sparse goals.

## Relation to Prior Work

FPO extends the PPO framework (Schulman et al., 2017) to flow-based generative policies by replacing the intractable likelihood ratio with a ratio of conditional flow matching losses, justified through the ELBO equivalence established by Kingma & Gao (2023). It departs from prior diffusion-RL methods (DDPO, DPPO, Flow-GRPO) that reformulate the denoising chain as a multi-step MDP, instead treating the entire flow sampler as a black box and using the CFM objective (Lipman et al., 2023) directly as a log-likelihood surrogate. This makes FPO agnostic to the choice of integration method (deterministic or stochastic, any step count) at both training and inference time, a flexibility that denoising-MDP approaches lack by construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FPO provides a simple, drop-in replacement for Gaussian policies in existing PPO pipelines, enabling practitioners to adopt flow-based policies without custom samplers or MDP reformulations. The most practically significant finding is that flow policies substantially outperform Gaussian policies in under-conditioned humanoid control (54.3% vs. 29.8% success rate with root-only goals), suggesting that the expressivity of flow matching is not merely theoretical but necessary for single-stage training of sparse-goal locomotion. Open questions include whether FPO scales to fine-tuning large pretrained vision-language-action models, whether one-step flow methods (e.g., mean-flow) can reduce the MC sampling cost, and whether the negative result on image generation fine-tuning (model collapse under self-generated data) generalizes to other generative domains.
