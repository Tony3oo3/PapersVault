## Problem

Imitation learning for end-to-end driving trains only on expert demonstrations, leaving policies without recovery examples that compound into failures in closed-loop deployment. Prior augmentation approaches rely on photorealistic neural rendering (NeRF, Gaussian Splatting) or game engines, which are prohibitively slow and costly for large-scale training. The paper argues that photorealism is unnecessary for planning—what matters is geometric and semantic fidelity—and that lightweight rasterization with feature-space alignment can provide the scalability needed for robust E2E training.

## Input / Output

At inference, the planner consumes 2 seconds of multi-view RGB camera images plus ego state history and route information, producing a 4–5 second future trajectory sampled at 2 Hz. During training, the model additionally ingests rasterized synthetic views generated by projecting annotated scene primitives (lane polylines, oriented agent cuboids, traffic-light cuboids) through a pinhole camera model with depth-aware compositing. The rasterized inputs are not used at deployment; they serve purely as training-time augmentation and alignment targets, with the R2R module ensuring the frozen image encoder produces consistent features across both domains.

## Method

RAP reconstructs driving scenes by rasterizing annotated geometric primitives into perspective camera views using a training-free, depth-buffered pipeline with Sutherland-Hodgman clipping, then generates two types of augmentation: recovery-oriented perturbations (lateral/longitudinal offsets plus noise applied to ego trajectories) and cross-agent view synthesis (re-rendering scenes from non-ego agents' trajectories while keeping camera parameters fixed). A Raster-to-Real (R2R) alignment module bridges the sim-to-real gap in feature space: a spatial-level MSE loss pulls real-image features toward frozen raster features at each patch position, while a global-level adversarial loss with a gradient reversal layer enforces domain confusion on average-pooled representations. The planner itself (RAP-DINO) pairs a frozen DINOv3-H+ backbone with a learnable MLP projector and an iterative deformable-attention decoder, trained jointly with the planning loss and both R2R alignment terms. This design yields over 500k rasterized training samples from ~1,200 hours of nuPlan logs, enabling log-scaling gains in MinADE and state-of-the-art closed-loop scores on NAVSIM v1/v2, WOD-E2E, and Bench2Drive.

## Results

RAP-DINO achieves state-of-the-art results on all four major E2E driving benchmarks. On NAVSIM v1, it attains a PDMS of 93.8, surpassing the previous best camera-only method Centaur (92.1) by +1.7. On NAVSIM v2, RAP-DINO scores an EPDMS of 36.93 versus LTF's 23.12. On WOD-E2E, it achieves the best RFS(Overall) of 8.04 and lowest ADE@5s of 2.65, and on Bench2Drive it reaches a Driving Score of 66.42 with a 37.27% success rate, both setting new records.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAP-DINO outperforms all 13 compared baselines across the four benchmarks. On NAVSIM v1, it beats the strongest prior camera-only methods Centaur (92.1) and iPad (91.7) by +1.7 and +2.1 PDMS respectively, and also surpasses the rule-based PDM-Closed (89.1) and lidar-fusion methods like DiffusionDrive (88.1) and Hydra-MDP (86.5). On NAVSIM v2, RAP-DINO's two-stage EPDMS of 36.93 nearly doubles LTF/TransFuser's 23.12, with particularly large gains in Stage 2 counterfactual evaluation (80.39 vs. 75.67 TTC, 52.27 vs. 45.41 LK). On WOD-E2E, RAP-DINO edges out the 3B-parameter VLM Poutine (RFS 7.99) with a smaller model, achieving RFS 8.04 and the best ADE@5s (2.65 vs. 2.74). On Bench2Drive, RAP-ResNet (only ~29M params) surpasses DriveTransformer (63.46) and iPad (65.02) with a Driving Score of 66.42 and the highest success rate (37.27%), while older methods like UniAD (45.81), VAD (42.35), and AD-MLP (18.05) trail significantly. Notably, applying RAP as a plug-in to existing architectures also yields consistent gains: RAP-DiffusionDrive improves over the original by +3.2 PDMS, and RAP-iPad by +0.7.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the imitation-learning paradigm for end-to-end driving (adapting iPad's deformable attention decoder and DINOv3 backbone) but departs sharply from the dominant approach to synthetic data generation. Prior work on counterfactual augmentation relies on photorealistic neural rendering (NeRF, 3D Gaussian Splatting) or game-engine simulators (CARLA, MetaDrive), which are prohibitively slow for large-scale training. RAP replaces these with lightweight rasterization of annotated geometric primitives (cuboids, lane polylines) and introduces a Raster-to-Real feature-space alignment module combining MSE distillation with gradient-reversal adversarial adaptation, borrowing the DANN framework from Ganin & Lempitsky (2015) but applying it to the raster-to-real domain gap in driving.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: RAP provides a training-free, fast, and fully controllable data augmentation pipeline that scales to over 500k synthetic samples, enabling recovery-oriented perturbations and cross-agent view synthesis that photorealistic methods cannot match in throughput. The log-scaling behavior of cross-agent synthesis (R²=0.9942) and the finding that 50% synthetic data can surpass 100% real data suggest a new scaling axis for E2E driving. Key limitations remain: the approach is still confined to the imitation learning paradigm and inherits causal confusion; the authors acknowledge that extending rasterization into a full interactive simulator for closed-loop RL is necessary for further gains. An open question is whether the feature-space alignment generalizes to sensor modalities beyond RGB cameras (e.g., LiDAR, radar) or to significantly different driving distributions.
