## Problem

Existing EEG foundation models inherit architectures from computer vision and NLP, treating continuous neural signals as discrete pixel grids or token sequences. This design philosophy contradicts the intrinsic properties of EEG: it conflates time-domain transients with frequency-domain rhythms in a single homogeneous stream, ignores the geometric topology of the cortical surface when treating electrodes as invariant sequences, and activates all parameters densely for every input rather than emulating the brain's sparse, modular coding. The paper argues these three mismatches fundamentally limit the model's ability to learn universal, generalizable brain representations.

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw multi-channel EEG signals reshaped into tensors of shape B×R×E×T (batch × brain regions × electrodes per region × time steps), where each electrode's entire time series is treated as a single holistic 'patch' rather than being segmented into temporal windows. After probabilistic data augmentation (simulated noise, channel dropout, temporal drift), the signal is projected into three parallel feature streams. The output is a unified EEG representation embedding per electrode that supports both linear probing (frozen backbone + linear head) and full fine-tuning for downstream classification, producing class labels across nine task types including anomaly detection, emotion recognition, sleep staging, and motor imagery.

## Method

Uni-NTFM's architecture is built on three neuroscience-inspired mechanisms. First, the Heterogeneous Feature Projection Module (HFPM) physically decouples each channel's signal into a time-domain path (1D convolutional encoder capturing non-stationary transients), a frequency-domain path (DFT → band power vector → MLP capturing steady-state rhythms), and a raw lossless reference path; a Dual-domain Cross-attention Module then performs bidirectional cross-attention between the time and frequency streams to produce a deeply fused representation. Second, a hierarchical Topological Embedding injects three levels of spatial prior—region-level (5 canonical brain regions per the 10-20 system), intra-region (relative electrode position within a region), and global absolute (IFCN-standard electrode identity)—enabling alignment of heterogeneous montages onto a unified functional topography without retraining. Third, the dense FFN in each Transformer block is replaced by a sparsely-activated Mixture-of-Experts module with Top-k gating, allowing different signal patterns to be routed to specialized expert subnetworks (scaling to 1.9B total parameters with only 307M active at inference). Pre-training uses a dual-domain masked autoencoding objective that simultaneously reconstructs the time-domain waveform and frequency-domain spectral features of masked tokens, plus an auxiliary load-balancing loss for the MoE router, trained on 28,000 hours of EEG from nine diverse datasets.

## Results

On the comprehensive benchmark (Table 5), Uni-NTFM_large (1.9B total / 307M inference params) achieves 77.37% Balanced Accuracy, 82.69% AUC-PR, and 84.01% AUROC on binary classification, and 71.25% Balanced Accuracy, 64.36% Cohen's Kappa, and 74.26% Weighted F1 on multi-class classification across nine downstream EEG tasks. Even the smallest variant (Uni-NTFM_tiny, 57M total / 19M inference) reaches 74.78% Bal. Acc. and 80.66% AUROC, surpassing most dense baselines. Under linear probing on TUAB, Uni-NTFM_large attains 78.44% Balanced Accuracy without any fine-tuning, and after full fine-tuning on SEED (5-class emotion recognition) it reaches 73.37% Balanced Accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Uni-NTFM_large outperforms all 13 baselines across every metric in the comprehensive comparison. Against the strongest foundation model baseline LaBraM-Base (5.8M params), Uni-NTFM_large gains +1.12% Bal. Acc. (77.37 vs 76.25), +1.94% AUROC (84.01 vs 82.07), and +1.70% W-F1 (74.26 vs 73.67) on multi-class tasks. Against CBraMod (4.0M), the advantage is larger: +1.67% Bal. Acc. and +2.58% W-F1. Notably, Uni-NTFM_large surpasses NeuroLM-XL (1.7B params) by +4.23% Bal. Acc. and +10.40% AUROC, despite NeuroLM having a comparable total parameter count, highlighting the benefit of sparse MoE activation (307M inference vs 1.7B dense). Even Uni-NTFM_tiny (57M total, 19M inference) exceeds LaBraM-Base on AUROC (80.66 vs 82.07 is a loss, but Bal. Acc. 74.78 vs 76.25 is also a loss) — however it beats EEGPT, CBraMod, and BIOT on most metrics while using fewer inference parameters than EEGPT (19M vs 25M). The ablation study confirms HFPM contributes the largest single-component gain (+6.89% AUROC on TUAB), with DCM and TE providing incremental improvements, and MoE showing synergistic effects when combined with other modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in EEG foundation models (LaBraM, EEGPT, CBraMod, BIOT, CSBrain) that adapts architectures from computer vision or NLP—treating neural signals as pixel grids or token sequences with dense attention. It builds on the masked autoencoder framework (He et al., 2022) but restructures it around three neuroscience-motivated principles: decoupled time-frequency coding via a Heterogeneous Feature Projection Module, spatial topology alignment via hierarchical Topological Embedding, and sparse functional specialization via Mixture-of-Experts. The dual-domain reconstruction loss (simultaneously reconstructing time-domain waveforms and frequency-domain spectra) is a novel pre-training objective not present in prior EEG foundation models, which typically reconstruct a single representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 28,000-hour pre-training corpus and consistent SOTA across nine diverse downstream tasks (clinical detection, emotion recognition, sleep staging, motor imagery) establish a strong reference point for the EEG foundation model field. The MoE design achieving 1.9B total parameters with only 307M active at inference addresses a practical deployment concern for real-time BCI applications. However, open questions remain: whether the biological grounding (topological embedding, decoupled coding) provides genuine inductive bias beyond what a well-tuned dense architecture could achieve, how the model handles truly unseen electrode configurations outside the 10-20/10-10 systems, and whether the performance gains persist under cross-subject and cross-session transfer without fine-tuning.
