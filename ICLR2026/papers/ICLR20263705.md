## Problem

Deterministic precipitation nowcasting models are biased toward normal rainfall and fail to capture small-scale extreme events, while diffusion-based ensembles that do capture fine structure are too computationally expensive for real-time deployment. Additionally, existing benchmark datasets are each skewed toward one regime (SEVIR toward storms, MeteoNet toward moderate rain), making it impossible to evaluate whether a model generalizes across the full spectrum of precipitation intensities.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of past radar reflectivity frames (7 frames at 10-min intervals for KMA at 256×256/4 km, 13 frames at 5-min intervals for SEVIR at 384×384/1 km, or 12 frames at 5-min intervals for MeteoNet at 416×416) and produces future radar reflectivity frames. A key design feature is that the Temporal Extractor block allows the same encoder to flexibly output different numbers of future frames (6 for 1-hour, up to 36 for 6-hour forecasts) without retraining the full network, enabling a single model family to serve multiple operational lead times.

## Method

exPreCast builds on a Video Swin Transformer encoder-decoder with shifted-window attention to capture local spatiotemporal patterns in radar sequences. The core architectural contribution is the Cubic Dual Upsample (CDU) block in the decoder, which runs a trilinear-interpolation branch (capturing smooth low-frequency structure) and a 3D pixel-shuffle branch (capturing high-frequency residuals) in parallel, then fuses them via concatenation and a 3D convolution—decoupling the reconstruction task to avoid the checkerboard artifacts of pure pixel-shuffle and the over-smoothing of pure interpolation. A Temporal Extractor (TE) block, implemented as a single 3D convolution over the temporal axis, compresses or expands the decoder's temporal dimension to match the target forecast horizon. Training uses the Fourier Amplitude Correlation Loss (FACL) with a linearly scheduled blend of amplitude and correlation components, and long-term forecasting is achieved via transfer learning: the encoder is frozen from a short-term checkpoint while only the decoder and TE are fine-tuned. The full model has 32M parameters and 55 GFLOPs for 1-hour KMA prediction, roughly 30× cheaper than the diffusion-based CasCast baseline.

## Results

On the newly introduced KMA dataset (balanced normal-to-extreme rainfall), exPreCast achieves CSI-M of 0.4841 (POOL16), CSI-20 of 0.4490, CSI-80 of 0.1488, and HSS of 0.4042, using only 32.0M parameters and 55 GFLOPs. On SEVIR (extreme storms), it reaches CSI-M 0.5427 and CSI-219 0.2910 (POOL16), while on MeteoNet (moderate rain) it achieves CSI-M 0.4446 and CSI-47 0.2525 (POOL16). The model matches or exceeds the diffusion-based CasCast on most metrics while requiring over 30× fewer GFLOPs (55 vs. 1,729 on KMA) and 12× fewer parameters (32M vs. 391M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

exPreCast achieves the highest CSI-M (POOL16) on KMA (0.4841) and SEVIR (0.5427), and the highest CSI-M on MeteoNet (0.4446), outperforming all deterministic baselines by a wide margin—e.g., over SimVP (0.2598 on KMA) and EarthFormer (0.2302 on KMA) by more than 20 absolute CSI points. Against the diffusion-based CasCast, exPreCast matches its CSI-M on KMA (0.4841 vs. 0.4837) and SEVIR (0.5427 vs. 0.5525) while using 31× fewer GFLOPs (55 vs. 1,729) and 12× fewer parameters (32M vs. 391M); CasCast retains a slight edge on CSI-80 (POOL16: 0.1695 vs. 0.1488 on KMA) but exPreCast wins on HSS (0.4042 vs. 0.3806). On MeteoNet, CasCast was excluded due to consistently noisy and unstable predictions even after fair training. For 6-hour long-term forecasting on KMA, exPreCast (with transfer learning) achieves CSI-M POOL16 of 0.2289, substantially outperforming ConvLSTM (0.0572), SimVP (0.0246), and AlphaPre (0.0464). The FACL loss ablation (Table 10) confirms that even when FACL is applied to SimVP, EarthFormer, and AlphaPre, exPreCast still leads on all CSI and HSS metrics, attributing its advantage to the CDU and TE architecture rather than the loss function alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Video Swin Transformer (Liu et al., 2022) into a radar nowcasting setting by adding domain-specific modules: a Cubic Dual Upsample (CDU) decoder that fuses trilinear interpolation with 3D pixel-shuffle to preserve small-scale high-intensity rainfall patterns, and a Temporal Extractor (TE) block for flexible multi-horizon forecasting. It deliberately departs from the recent trend toward diffusion-based generative ensembles (CasCast, Prediff) by pursuing a deterministic architecture, arguing that the ~30× computational overhead of diffusion models is impractical for real-time operational nowcasting. The introduction of the KMA dataset addresses a recognized gap in the evaluation landscape, where SEVIR is skewed toward extreme storms and MeteoNet toward moderate rain, by providing a balanced 10-year Korean radar collection spanning all precipitation regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that a 32M-parameter deterministic model at 55 GFLOPs can match or exceed a 391M-parameter diffusion ensemble at 1729 GFLOPs on most nowcasting metrics, making high-fidelity extreme-rainfall prediction feasible on modest hardware for operational deployment. The KMA dataset and the CDU/TE components offer reusable tools for the community. Key limitations remain: the deterministic framework cannot quantify predictive uncertainty (critical for early-warning systems), and long-term (6-hour) extreme-event forecasting still degrades substantially, partly due to the scarcity of such events in training data. Open questions include whether hybrid probabilistic-deterministic approaches can close the uncertainty gap without sacrificing the efficiency advantage.
