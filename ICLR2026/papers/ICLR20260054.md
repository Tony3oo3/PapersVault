## Problem

Existing sensitivity verification tools for decision tree ensembles produce counterexample pairs that lie far from the training data distribution, yielding uninterpretable evidence of model weaknesses. Additionally, the NP-hardness of sensitivity verification was previously established only for trees of depth ≥ 3, leaving the complexity of depth-1 (decision stump) ensembles unresolved. This paper closes both gaps by proving NP-hardness at depth 1 and introducing a data-aware framework that constrains sensitive examples to remain close to the dataset.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained decision tree ensemble (binary or multiclass, up to 800 trees of depth 8), a specified subset of sensitive features F, a training dataset D used to estimate marginal distributions and compute clause summaries, and a gap parameter g controlling the minimum probability separation between the two outputs. It produces a concrete counterexample pair (x^(1), x^(2)) — two full input vectors differing only on features F — along with a sensitivity verification verdict. For multiclass ensembles, the output additionally specifies the two target classes c^(1) ≠ c^(2) whose raw scores must be separated by ln(g) after softmax.

## Method

The core contribution is a novel MILP encoding for sensitivity verification with three key optimizations: (1) unaffected-leaf constraints that force identical leaf visits for all leaves whose ancestry contains no sensitive-feature guard, (2) a reduced affected-leaf gap constraint that eliminates zero-contribution terms from the output-difference inequality, and (3) a maximization objective on the raw output gap to guide the simplex solver. For data-awareness, two complementary strategies are integrated: a product-of-marginals utility function (encoded as a log-linear MILP objective over predicate variables) that steers solutions toward data-dense regions, and SMT-based clause summaries (solved with Z3) that identify and prune axis-aligned cavities in the input space containing no training points. The encoding is extended to multiclass ensembles by transforming the softmax probability-ratio constraint into a linear inequality on raw scores with offset η = ln(g), yielding the first known tool for multiclass sensitivity verification.

## Results

ENSENSE achieves approximately 8× average speedup over the baseline MILP encoding (KANT) and 5× over SENSPB for binary classification, with zero timeouts across 1,290 instances (vs. 205 timeouts for SENSPB and 153 for KANT). For multiclass classification, ENSENSE delivers a ~15× speedup over KANT with zero timeouts (vs. 256 for KANT) across 538 instances, and is the first tool to handle multiclass sensitivity verification. For data-aware sensitivity, the combined Probclause strategy reduces the mean ℓ₂ distance of counterexamples to the training distribution from 0.57 (no data-awareness) to 0.17, winning 86.7% of pairwise comparisons against the baseline. The tool scales to ensembles with up to 800 trees of depth 8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SENSPB (Ahmad et al., ICLR 2025), ENSENSE achieves a ~5× average runtime speedup on binary classification and is the only method that handles multiclass ensembles; SENSPB times out on 205 of 1,290 binary instances while ENSENSE solves all. Against the baseline MILP encoding KANT (Kantchelian et al., ICML 2016), ENSENSE's novel optimizations (unaffected-leaf constraints, affected-leaf gap reduction, and objective-guided search) yield ~8× speedup for binary and ~15× for multiclass, with KANT timing out on 153 binary and 256 multiclass instances. Against VERITAS (Devos et al., AAAI 2024), repurposed for sensitivity, VERITAS times out on all but two multiclass instances; on Iris, ENSENSE finds a gap 50× larger than VERITAS's best within 1200 s, while on Red-Wine the two are comparable. For data-aware quality, the combined Probclause strategy (product-of-marginals objective + SMT clause summaries) dominates both individual strategies, reducing mean counterexample distance to 0.17 versus 0.26 (Prob alone) and 0.306 (Clause alone), and winning 72.7% of head-to-head comparisons against Clause.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sensitivity verification problem introduced by Ahmad et al. (2025), who provided a pseudo-Boolean encoding for binary tree ensembles and proved NP-hardness for depth ≥ 3. The authors revisit the older MILP encoding from Kantchelian et al. (2016) (originally designed for local robustness, not sensitivity) and, through novel structural optimizations (unaffected-leaf constraints, affected-leaf gap reduction, and an objective function), make it outperform the pseudo-Boolean approach by an order of magnitude. They further depart from prior work by introducing a data-aware formulation (product-of-marginals utility and SMT-based clause summaries to prune data-sparse regions) and by extending sensitivity verification to multiclass ensembles for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sensitivity analysis is essential for auditing fairness and reliability of tree ensembles in high-stakes domains (credit scoring, healthcare, hiring), and the data-aware counterexamples produced here are far more actionable for model retraining or hardening than the arbitrary witnesses generated by prior tools. The NP-hardness result at depth 1 closes a theoretical gap and implies that even the simplest ensembles (decision stumps) cannot be efficiently verified in general. Open questions include how to train ensembles that provably reduce sensitivity (analogous to robustness-hardened training) and how to move from identifying sensitive pairs to quantifying the full sensitive region across the input space. The independence assumption in the product-of-marginals utility limits its effectiveness on highly correlated features, motivating the complementary clause-summary approach.
