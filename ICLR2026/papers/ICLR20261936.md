## Problem

While the Decision Pretrained Transformer (DPT) demonstrated strong in-context reinforcement learning in simplified, single-domain settings, its scalability to diverse multi-domain continuous-control environments remained unestablished. Prior cross-domain scaling of Algorithm Distillation (Vintix) achieved limited generalization to unseen tasks, and existing DPT variants targeting continuous actions relied on Gaussian heads that fail to capture the multi-modal action posteriors required by complex control tasks. The paper asks whether DPT, equipped with an expressive generative policy head, can be scaled to hundreds of diverse tasks while improving generalization to held-out environments.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a query observation vector and a context of up to 4096 (observation, action, reward) tuples, which may be empty (online cold-start) or pre-populated with expert demonstration episodes (offline prompted). Tasks are partitioned into non-overlapping groups sharing identical action-observation dimensionalities, each served by its own MLP encoder/decoder pair, making the model task-agnostic within a group. The output is a continuous action vector in the original action space, produced by sampling from a learned flow distribution via 32-step Heun's method ODE integration, rather than by a single forward pass through a parametric head.

## Method

The architecture pairs a 928M-parameter TinyLLaMA causal transformer backbone (16 layers, 24 heads, 1536-dim embeddings) with a rectified-flow matching policy head that replaces the Gaussian decoder used in prior DPT variants. The flow head parametrizes a context-dependent vector field v_η(γ(t), h, x_t) using a learnable sinusoidal time encoder and an MLP, trained with the rectified-flow objective that regresses the predicted velocity onto the linear interpolation direction (a* − x_0). Training follows the DPT supervised recipe: trajectories collected via Continuous Noise Distillation across 209 tasks in 10 domains (709.7M transitions) are relabeled with demonstrator actions, and the transformer is trained to predict these optimal actions at every context position. At inference, actions are decoded by sampling x_0 ~ N(0, I) and integrating the learned ODE to t=1, enabling native sampling from multi-modal action posteriors that supports the Bayesian posterior-sampling interpretation of DPT.

## Results

On 46 held-out tasks across 10 domains, Vintix II achieves over 75% of demonstrator performance in MetaDrive (102%), CityLearn (78%), SinerGym (92%), and ControlGym (100%) normalized scores under offline (prompted) evaluation. In the online (demonstration-less) setting, the model performs on par with its prompted version in 8 out of 10 domains, and reaches 85% normalized score on Meta-World ML1, outperforming REGENT (given 100 expert episodes) by 3%. On training tasks, offline prompting with 2500 transitions yields an average +4.1% gain over online performance, which is already near-demonstrator on Kinetix, ControlGym, MuJoCo, and MetaDrive from the first episode.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vintix II is compared against two prior Large Action Models: Vintix (Polubarov et al., 2025, an AD-based model) and REGENT (Sridhar et al., 2025, a retrieval-augmented generalist agent). Against Vintix on offline (prompted) evaluation of unseen tasks, Vintix II achieves +63% higher normalized return on Meta-World ML45, +17% on Bi-DexHands, and +4% on MuJoCo with parametric variations. Against REGENT on the 5 unseen Meta-World ML45 tasks, Vintix II reaches 8.2% higher normalized return. In the demonstration-less online setting on Meta-World ML1, Vintix II (85%) outperforms REGENT (given 100 expert episodes covering a richer goal distribution) by 3%. The paper does not report a direct comparison with JAT, noting it was shown to underperform Vintix by a substantial margin on all domains. Overall, Vintix II wins or ties on every reported head-to-head comparison, with the largest margins on Meta-World ML45 and Bi-DexHands.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Decision Pretrained Transformer (DPT; Lee et al., 2023) framework—originally validated in simplified single-domain settings—to a multi-domain continuous-control regime by replacing its Gaussian action head with a rectified-flow policy head (Liu et al., 2022), enabling native sampling from complex multi-modal action distributions. It also builds directly on the Vintix dataset and noise-distillation pipeline (Polubarov et al., 2025), expanding it 3.2× to 709M transitions across 209 tasks in 10 domains. Conceptually, the work departs from the Algorithm Distillation (AD) line by leveraging DPT's Bayesian posterior-sampling interpretation rather than distilling policy-improvement operators, and from retrieval-augmented generalist agents (REGENT, JAT) by operating in a fully parametric setting without external memory modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first Large Action Model that competes in both zero-shot (online, no context) and prompted (offline) evaluation simultaneously, and its 700M-transition, 10-domain dataset is a practical resource for the ICRL community. However, the model operates at a token-to-parameter ratio well below the ~20:1 considered compute-optimal for foundation models, and demonstration-less evaluation still lags behind prompted runs, indicating limited test-time exploration. Open questions include whether scaling laws analogous to LLMs govern large action models, and how to achieve input-output dimension agnosticism so the agent can transfer to entirely unseen domains without per-group encoders.
