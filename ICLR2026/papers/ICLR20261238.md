## Problem

Adversarial-example-based fingerprinting methods for DNN ownership protection rely on empirical heuristics to determine how far a fingerprint should be placed from the decision boundary, with no theoretical guidance. This lack of principled distance control can cause fingerprints to violate either robustness (too close to the boundary, making them fragile under model modification attacks) or uniqueness (too far, causing false attribution of independently trained models). The paper addresses the open question of theoretically determining the fingerprint-to-boundary distance that simultaneously satisfies both constraints.

## Input / Output

The method takes a protected DNN model P, the training dataset used to train P, and two finite surrogate model pools (pirated variants and independently trained models) for parameter estimation. It produces a set of N_f fingerprint input-label pairs (x*, y*), where each x* is a high-confidence anchor stretched along its minimal adversarial perturbation direction by a per-anchor stretch factor τ, and y* is the protected model's prediction at x*. During verification, the suspect model is queried as a black-box with each fingerprint input, and a binary pirated/independent decision is made by comparing the matching rate against a threshold θ.

## Method

AnaFP formulates fingerprint generation as controlling the fingerprint-to-boundary distance through a tunable stretch factor τ, and derives a theoretical admissible interval [τ_lower, τ_upper] where τ_lower = 1 + 2ε_logit/(c_g‖δ*‖) encodes the robustness constraint (bounding logit shift under model modification) and τ_upper = m_min/(2L_uniq‖δ*‖) encodes the uniqueness constraint (bounding logit drift in independently trained models via Lipschitz constants). The pipeline selects high-confidence anchors by logit margin threshold, computes minimal decision-altering perturbations via C&W-ℓ2 optimization, estimates the three parameters (ε_logit, m_min, L_uniq) over surrogate model pools using quantile-based relaxation to avoid overly conservative bounds, and resolves the circular dependency between τ_lower and τ (since ε_logit is evaluated at x* = x^a + τδ*) through a grid search over (1, τ_upper] that selects the τ maximizing the minimum slack to both bounds. This yields per-anchor stretch factors that are theoretically grounded yet practically feasible, distinguishing AnaFP from prior methods that use fixed or heuristically tuned distances.

## Results

AnaFP achieves the highest AUC across all four model/dataset settings: 0.957 on CNN/CIFAR-10, 0.893 on CNN/CIFAR-100, 0.963 on MLP/MNIST, and 0.926 on GNN/PROTEINS. Under performance-preserving model modification attacks (CNN/CIFAR-10), AnaFP maintains AUCs of 0.979 (fine-tuning), 0.983 (adversarial training), 0.978 (N-finetune), and 0.989 (P-finetune), while the most challenging KD and Prune-KD attacks yield AUCs of 0.756 and 0.689 respectively. The method consistently outperforms all six baselines by 0.05–0.10 AUC in the standard evaluation and by 0.03–0.15 AUC under modification attacks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnaFP outperforms all six baselines across every evaluated setting. On CIFAR-10, AnaFP (0.957) beats the strongest baseline ADV-TRA (0.878) by 0.079 AUC; on CIFAR-100, it leads UAP (0.806) by 0.087; on MNIST, it surpasses UAP (0.906) by 0.057; and on PROTEINS, it exceeds AKH (0.854) by 0.072. Under model modification attacks (Table 2, CNN/CIFAR-10), AnaFP's advantage is most pronounced on adversarial training (0.983 vs. 0.783 for UAP) and P-finetune (0.989 vs. 0.876 for UAP), while the gap narrows on KD (0.756 vs. 0.679 for UAP) and Prune-KD (0.689 vs. 0.636 for AKH), where distillation-based attacks most severely shift decision boundaries. Notably, UAP and MarginFinger are inapplicable to GNNs due to their Euclidean-structure assumptions, and IPGuard and MarginFinger show the largest variance (±0.07–0.12) across runs, indicating less stable verification compared to AnaFP's tight ±0.002–0.005 standard deviations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial-example-based fingerprinting line of work (IPGuard, UAP, MarginFinger, ADV-TRA) by addressing a gap those methods leave open: the lack of theoretical guidance for determining how far a fingerprint should be placed from the decision boundary. Rather than relying on empirically tuned distances or heuristic margin controls, AnaFP formalizes the robustness and uniqueness constraints as lower and upper bounds on a stretch factor, derived via first-order Taylor expansion and Lipschitz arguments. The C&W ℓ₂ attack is repurposed from adversarial robustness evaluation to boundary-point computation, and the surrogate-model-pool paradigm from prior fingerprinting work is retained but augmented with a quantile-based relaxation to make the theoretical bounds practically usable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first analytical framework that connects the two competing requirements of fingerprinting—robustness to model modification and uniqueness against false attribution—to a single geometric quantity (fingerprint-to-boundary distance) with provable bounds. The practical limitation is that the bounds must be estimated over finite surrogate pools and relaxed via quantiles, introducing approximation error that degrades performance most severely under knowledge distillation (AUC drops to ~0.75 on CIFAR-10). Open questions include whether the surrogate-pool approximation can be made distribution-free or certified, whether the first-order Taylor approximation remains valid for very deep or highly non-linear models, and how the framework extends to multi-modal or foundation-model settings where decision boundaries are far more complex.
