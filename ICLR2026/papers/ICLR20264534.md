## Problem

Existing DNN training relies on a single static optimizer or a simple two-optimizer hybrid (e.g., SWATS), which cannot exploit the fact that different optimizers exhibit distinct optimization directions and strengths in different training states. This static or coarse-grained approach limits both convergence speed and final model quality, as the optimizer is never adapted to the evolving parameter landscape during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264534/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each switch cycle (τ iterations), DOIT consumes a PCA-compressed vector of selected model parameters (e.g., the classifier head plus a few hidden layers, or only LoRA matrices A and B in PEFT), the sequence of training losses over that cycle, and a pre-computed transferability weight ω_t derived from performance-based (test accuracy) and distribution-based (LogME, Leep) metrics. The output is a sequence of optimizer selections (one per cycle) that collectively produce the final trained model; the surrogate models output a short-term performance score s ∈ [−1,1] per optimizer rather than a final loss, emphasizing immediate optimization gain over long-horizon results.

## Method

DOIT maintains one Gaussian Process surrogate model per candidate optimizer, each mapping compressed parameter states to a short-term performance score computed from loss reductions within a switch cycle (incorporating mean reduction, upper/lower boundary terms, and a variance exploration term). An acquisition function selects the next optimizer by combining the GP mean prediction with a variance term that is modulated by two factors: a transferability weight ω_t (lower transferability increases exploration) and a periodic halving schedule that progressively reduces the variance weight as training stabilizes. During an initial phase (n_ini cycles), a weighted random selection strategy seeds the surrogate models; afterward, the optimizer with the highest acquisition score is chosen each cycle. The key distinction from standard Bayesian optimization is that the surrogate input includes the current parameter state (not just hyperparameters), the objective is short-term gain rather than final loss, and the acquisition function encodes training-progress dynamics, enabling the method to discover optimization paths unreachable by any single fixed optimizer.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 full training with ViT, DOIT achieves 98.04% test accuracy, outperforming the best single optimizer (SGDM at 97.58%) and the best hybrid method (Padam at 96.03%). On ImageNet full training, DOIT reaches 79.98% top-1 accuracy versus 78.73% for SGD and 77.47% for Padam. For PEFT with RoBERTa+LoRA, DOIT attains 87.99% on MRPC and 85.57% on QQP, surpassing Adam (86.52% and 82.27%) by 1.5% and 3.3% respectively. Across all tasks, DOIT also achieves 2%–10% faster convergence in terms of iterations to reach a stable loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOIT consistently outperforms all 13 baselines across the evaluated tasks. Against single optimizers, DOIT gains 0.5% over SGDM (the strongest single optimizer) on CIFAR-10 (98.04% vs. 97.58%) and 1.25% over SGD on ImageNet (79.98% vs. 78.73%); the gap widens dramatically on harder datasets like ImageNet-A where DOIT reaches 18.47% top-1 vs. 16.19% for SGDM. Among hybrid methods, Padam is the closest competitor (96.03% on CIFAR-10, 77.47% on ImageNet), but DOIT still leads by 2.0% and 2.5% respectively; SWATS and AdaBound perform poorly on complex datasets (e.g., AdaBound drops to 68.38% on MRPC PEFT). On NLP PEFT tasks, DOIT surpasses Adam by 1.5% on MRPC and 3.3% on QQP, while Padam and AdaBound degrade severely (73.04% and 78.64% on QQP). Additional baselines including ASGD, AdamW, Nadam, Adamax, AGD, and learning-rate schedulers (stepLR, cosLR) are all outperformed on USPS and MNIST (e.g., DOIT 96.81% vs. Adamax 96.06% on USPS; 99.51% vs. cosLR 99.46% on MNIST). Statistical testing (Friedman + Nemenyi) confirms DOIT is significantly better than all methods except SGDM and Padam, with which the differences are slightly below the critical distance but DOIT still leads consistently across datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

DOIT repurposes the Sequential Model-Based Optimization (SMBO) framework—specifically Gaussian Process surrogate models and acquisition functions from Bayesian Optimization—into the domain of dynamic optimizer selection during training. It departs from prior hybrid optimizer methods (SWATS, Padam, AdaBound, AGD) that perform a single static switch or simple blending, by treating optimizer choice as a state-dependent decision that evolves throughout training. The key conceptual extension is incorporating compressed model parameter states (via PCA) as surrogate model inputs, alongside transferability metrics and training-progress-aware variance halving in the acquisition function, which distinguishes it from standard HPO where the configuration is fixed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DOIT offers a practical, low-overhead (<1%) mechanism to improve training quality and convergence speed across diverse architectures and tasks, which could be integrated into existing training pipelines with minimal engineering effort. The finding that optimizer state reset outperforms state inheritance (e.g., carrying momentum from Adam to SGDM) is a useful practical insight, though robust cross-optimizer state transfer remains an open problem. The method's reliance on GP surrogates limits scalability to very large optimizer/hyperparameter spaces, and the 1–3% accuracy gains, while consistent, are modest relative to the added algorithmic complexity. Broader applicability to learned optimizers or second-order methods is unexplored.
