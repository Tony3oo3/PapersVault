## Problem

Speech-to-speech processing tasks (enhancement, separation) face severe data scarcity compared to TTS, which leverages vast in-the-wild corpora. This gap causes generative S2S models to distort speaker identity and content, and no principled mechanism exists to compose independently trained speech operations (e.g., enhancement + text-guided content restoration) at inference time without joint training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262592/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SpeechOp operates in a 40 Hz, 64-channel latent space produced by a DAC-based variational autoencoder (1200× temporal downsampling of 48 kHz audio). For S2S tasks, the model consumes source audio (noisy or mixed speech) via a dedicated 8-layer audio encoder whose outputs are frame-wise added to the diffusion latent, preserving temporal alignment without explicit alignment mechanisms. The ITC pipeline additionally accepts ASR-derived transcripts (e.g., from WhisperX) purely at inference time as a discriminative guidance signal, requiring no transcript supervision during training.

## Method

SpeechOp is a 20-layer DiT (419M params) with an 8-layer audio encoder (71M params), initialized from a pre-trained TTS model and fine-tuned on six S2S tasks via a learnable Task Embedding that conditions both components through AdaLN. The key architectural choice is frame-wise mixing of source audio representations into the diffusion latent, which generalizes to arbitrary sequence lengths unlike cross-attention alternatives. The central technical contribution is TC-CFG (Task-Composition Classifier-Free Guidance), which decomposes the joint score ∇log p(z_t|y,w) via Bayes' rule into an enhancement score plus a discriminative TTS guide approximated by γ(s_tts(z_t|w) − s_tts(z_t)), avoiding the quality degradation caused by naively averaging generative priors. This enables the ITC pipeline, where Whisper transcripts from noisy audio guide content restoration at inference time with a tunable guidance strength γ that trades off acoustic fidelity against content accuracy.

## Results

On speech enhancement, SpeechOp with Implicit Task Composition (ITC) achieves a WER of 2.9%, a 46% relative reduction over the strongest baseline HiFi-GAN-2 (5.4%), while matching its subjective quality (MOS 3.89 vs. 3.90). For zero-shot TTS, multi-task training improves SpeechOp over its TTS baseline on all MOS metrics (e.g., MOS-VS +0.36, MOS-SS +0.32) and speaker similarity (SIM 0.53 vs. 0.48). On speaker separation, SpeechOp achieves the highest average MOS (3.57) across LibriMix, WHAMR!, and WSJ0-2Mix, outperforming all SepFormer variants (3.10–3.28), though it trails on signal-fidelity metrics like SI-SDRi (0.53 vs. 11.86).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For speech enhancement, SpeechOp+ITC decisively outperforms all baselines on content preservation (WER 2.9% vs. 5.4–7.0% for HiFi-GAN-2, SGMSE+, Miipher, and StoRm), while HiFi-GAN-2 retains an edge on signal fidelity (PESQ 2.23 vs. 2.05, MCD 4.40 vs. 4.85); subjectively, SpeechOp+ITC matches HiFi-GAN-2 (MOS 3.89 vs. 3.90). For TTS, SpeechOp (419M params) beats VoiceCraft (830M) on WER (3.57 vs. 6.32) and all four MOS dimensions, and surpasses CLaM-TTS and XTTS on WER and most MOS metrics, though the larger DiTTo-TTS (740M, ~56k hrs) still leads on WER (2.56) and MOS-Q (4.16). In speech editing, SpeechOp outperforms VoiceCraft on every MOS metric (e.g., MOS-Q 4.15 vs. 3.62, MOS-VS 4.48 vs. 4.12) despite having roughly half the parameters. For speaker separation, SpeechOp achieves the best human-rated MOS across all four test sets (total 3.57 vs. 3.22–3.28 for SepFormer variants), but trails significantly on objective signal metrics (SI-SDRi 0.53 vs. 11.86, MCD 4.20 vs. 1.55 for AC-SIM-ML SepFormer), reflecting the known generative-vs.-mask-based trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpeechOp builds on the latent diffusion + DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023; Rombach et al., 2021) and the DAC audio autoencoder (Kumar et al., 2023), but its core conceptual departure is the TC-CFG formulation, which reframes classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) as a principled task-composition operator rather than a mere sampling trick. This contrasts with prior compositional diffusion work (Liu et al., 2022) and the audio-specific Fugatto (Valle et al., 2025), which rely on score averaging that the paper shows degrades signal fidelity. The ITC pipeline further distinguishes itself from transcript-conditioned S2S models by integrating web-scale ASR (Whisper) at inference time without requiring paired noisy-clean-transcript training data, and from broad-coverage foundation models like UniAudio by focusing on efficient adaptation of existing TTS backbones rather than training from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a real data asymmetry in speech processing: TTS benefits from tens of thousands of hours of in-the-wild data while S2S tasks like enhancement and separation are constrained to smaller paired datasets. The finding that multi-task S2S training *improves* TTS quality (rather than degrading it) suggests these objectives are mutually reinforcing, which could reshape how unified speech models are trained. Open questions include whether TC-CFG generalizes to non-speech audio tasks, whether the approach extends to realistic conversational separation with partial overlap and diarization-assisted transcripts, and whether the signal-fidelity gap in separation (lower SI-SDRi despite higher MOS) can be closed. The framework's reliance on a single TTS backbone also raises questions about scaling to multilingual or cross-lingual settings.
