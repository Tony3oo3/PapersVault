## Problem

Invariance-based self-supervised learning suppresses transformation-dependent structure (orientation, scale, reflection) that is useful for geometric robustness and spatially sensitive transfer. Prior work that adds equivariance objectives to SSL typically imposes both invariance and equivariance on the same final, spatially collapsed representation, but the authors empirically show this coupling creates a trade-off: pushing equivariance regularization toward deeper layers increases equivariance scores while consistently degrading ImageNet-1k linear-evaluation accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261216/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard 224×224 RGB images from ImageNet-1k and produces a pre-trained ViT-S/16 encoder whose [CLS] token (concatenated from the last 4 layers) serves as the visual representation for downstream linear probing, fine-tuning, or frozen-backbone detection. The encoder is decomposed into a structure-preserving prefix f^(1) that outputs a spatial token map (H_f × W_f × D_f) and an invariance-oriented head f^(2) that produces the final embedding; the [CLS] token is inserted only at the input of f^(2) so it does not disrupt the spatial lattice of the intermediate representation used for equivariance regularization.

## Method

SER decouples where invariance and equivariance are enforced: the final embedding is trained with the unchanged baseline SSL loss (MoCo-v3, DINO, or Barlow Twins), while an intermediate spatial token map (e.g., layer 3 of ViT-S/16) is softly regularized toward equivariance via analytically specified group actions ρ_g (90° rotations, horizontal flips, anisotropic scaling) applied directly in feature space as token permutations or grid resampling. A batch-partitioning strategy splits each mini-batch into b₁ (standard augmentations including RandomResizedCrop) and b₂ (an equivariant-view policy that disables cropping and samples invertible geometric transforms from group G), so the relative transform g = g₂g₁⁻¹ is well-defined for the equivariance loss. The equivariance regularizer is a patch-wise NT-Xent contrastive loss computed on the aligned intermediate token maps, with negatives sampled from other images in the batch; no auxiliary transformation-prediction head, learned action network, or per-sample transformation labels are required. The total objective is L = L_inv1 + L_inv2 + λ·L_equiv, adding only 1.008× training FLOPs over the baseline.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k linear evaluation (ViT-S/16, 2-view), SER improves MoCo-v3 by +0.84 Top-1 (69.28 vs. 68.44) and outperforms all equivariant add-ons under matched view counts. SER also improves DINO (+0.26 Top-1) and Barlow Twins (+0.68 Top-1) without architecture changes. On robustness benchmarks, SER gains +1.11 Top-1 on ImageNet-C and +1.22 on ImageNet-P, and achieves +1.7 mAP on frozen-backbone COCO detection (0.242 vs. 0.225). The method adds only 1.008× training FLOPs over vanilla MoCo-v3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the strictly matched 2-view setting, SER (69.28 Top-1) outperforms MoCo-v3 (68.44) by +0.84, AugSelf (67.55) by +1.73, and STL (65.49) by +3.79 on ImageNet-1k linear evaluation. Against EquiMod (3-view, 68.95), SER still leads by +0.33 despite using fewer views. In the 2+4-view regime, SER (71.56) surpasses E-SSL (70.6) by +0.96 Top-1. SER also dominates on spatially sensitive transfer: it achieves 70.17 Top-1 on 3DIEBench (vs. 68.43 for MoCo-v3 and 64.97 for AugSelf) and 0.242 mAP on COCO detection (vs. 0.225 for MoCo-v3, 0.221 for STL, and 0.197 for AugSelf). On robustness, SER leads all 2-view baselines on ImageNet-C (40.58 avg. Top-1) and ImageNet-P (65.13 avg. Top-1), while STL and AugSelf both degrade relative to the MoCo-v3 baseline on these benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the invariant SSL framework of MoCo-v3, DINO, and Barlow Twins, and on the equivariant SSL line of work (E-SSL, AugSelf, EquiMod, STL) that adds transformation-aware objectives to invariant pretraining. Its key departure is a layer-decoupling design: prior methods impose both invariance and equivariance on the same spatially collapsed final representation, whereas SER applies equivariance regularization to an intermediate spatial token map via analytically specified group actions (rotations, flips, scaling) while leaving the baseline invariance loss untouched on the final embedding. Unlike STL and EquiMod, SER requires no auxiliary transformation-prediction head or learned action module, and unlike AugSelf it does not predict per-sample transformation labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical finding that pushing equivariance regularization toward deeper layers increases equivariance scores but consistently degrades ImageNet-1k linear-eval accuracy provides a concrete design principle: invariance and equivariance are best enforced at different depths. The observation that simply relocating the equivariance objective of existing methods (EquiMod, AugSelf) to an intermediate layer improves their accuracy suggests layer decoupling is a broadly applicable recipe, not just a SER-specific trick. Limitations include reliance on structured geometric group actions (restricting applicability to modalities where such symmetries are meaningful), approximate invertibility of scaling under discretization, and evaluation limited to ViT-S/16 on ImageNet-1k. Open questions include whether the approach extends to larger backbones, non-geometric transformation groups, or domains without natural group structure (text, audio, graphs).
