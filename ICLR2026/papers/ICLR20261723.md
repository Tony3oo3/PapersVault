## Problem

Existing spatial reasoning datasets for VLMs suffer from three key limitations: narrow scene diversity (predominantly indoor or outdoor), restricted instruction formats (only natural language or region masks), and a focus on single-view settings with basic spatial concepts. These gaps leave VLMs poorly equipped for the diverse query patterns and multi-view spatial relationships encountered in real-world applications such as robotics and embodied AI.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261723/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset accepts single-view images or sequences of multi-view video frames paired with one of 19 instruction formats, including natural language descriptions, bounding-box-annotated images, segmentation-mask overlays, numbered-region annotations, and coordinate-based textual references. Each base QA pair is expanded into up to 19 training samples by crossing five textual formats (plain language, <ref>caption</ref>, <ref>region</ref><box>, <ref>caption</ref><box>, and auto-generated text) with four image formats (original, bbox-annotated, mask-annotated, numbered). Outputs span spatial relationship judgments (left/right, above/below, near/far, wider/narrower, taller/shorter, larger/smaller), object counts, rotation angle estimates in degrees, metric distance and room-size estimates, route-planning action sequences, and first-appearance ordering of objects across frames.

## Method

InternSpatial is constructed via a fully automated, modular data engine that operates in three stages: (1) image-level annotation extraction (2D boxes, captions, SAM2 segmentation masks), (2) alignment to a canonical 3D view space centered at the camera optical center, and (3) template-based QA generation that avoids per-image LLM calls for scalability. For 2D-only sources, the pipeline lifts annotations into 3D using WildCamera for intrinsic parameters, PerspectiveFields for extrinsic parameters, and Metric3Dv2 for dense depth; for 3D sources, global annotations are projected to the image plane to support visual reference formats. The dataset covers 10 tasks across 9 source datasets (COCO, AS-1B, Visual Genome, 3RScan, ScanNet, MultiScan, Cityscapes, Objaverse, R2R), with a novel cross-view rotation estimation task contributing 2.46M QA pairs. Training is standard supervised fine-tuning applied to InternVL2.5-8B/1B and Qwen2.5-VL-8B, with uniform sampling across all instruction formats during training to ensure balanced coverage.

## Results

On InternSpatial-Bench, InternVL-Spatial-8B achieves 71.0% average accuracy, surpassing GPT-4o (61.6%), Claude-3.7-Sonnet (60.2%), and Gemini-2.5-Flash (59.3%), while improving +12.1% over its InternVL2.5-8B baseline (58.9%). On VSI-Bench, InternVL-Spatial-8B attains 52.3% average, ranking first among all evaluated models including GPT-4o (32.9%) and Gemini-1.5 Pro (45.3%), with a +10.7% gain over the baseline. The gains are consistent across model families: InternVL-Spatial-1B improves +18.6% and Qwen-Spatial-8B improves +14.4% on InternSpatial-Bench. General-purpose benchmarks (MathVision, OCRBench, TextVQA, ChartQA, MM-Star) show negligible degradation (within ±1.6%), confirming no catastrophic forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InternVL-Spatial-8B outperforms every compared model on both InternSpatial-Bench and VSI-Bench. On InternSpatial-Bench, it leads GPT-4o by 9.4 points (71.0 vs. 61.6), Claude-3.7-Sonnet by 10.8 points, Gemini-2.5-Flash by 11.7 points, and the strongest open-source model LLaVA-OneVision-72B by 6.5 points. On VSI-Bench, the advantage is even larger: InternVL-Spatial-8B (52.3) beats Gemini-1.5 Pro (45.3) by 7.0 points, GPT-4o (32.9) by 19.4 points, and LLaVA-NeXT-Video-72B (41.5) by 10.8 points, securing the top average score overall. The improvement is model-agnostic: applying the same InternSpatial training to Qwen2.5-VL-8B yields +14.4% on InternSpatial-Bench and +7.6% on VSI-Bench, while the 1B variant gains +18.6% and +14.5% respectively. Notably, the Rotation Estimation task remains the weakest sub-task (33.6% for InternVL-Spatial-8B vs. 100% human), indicating substantial headroom for future work. On general benchmarks, InternVL-Spatial-8B matches or slightly exceeds InternVL2.5-8B (e.g., +1.8% on MathVision, −1.6% on ChartQA), confirming that spatial training does not erode general multimodal competence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spatial reasoning dataset line initiated by SpatialVLM (Chen et al., 2024a), SpatialQA (Cai et al., 2025), and OSD (Cheng et al., 2024), which were limited to single-view settings, narrow scene sources, and single instruction formats. It directly builds on the SpatialRGPT (Cheng et al., 2024) pipeline for lifting 2D annotations into a canonical 3D view space via WildCamera, PerspectiveFields, and Metric3Dv2, and extends the multi-view evaluation framework of VSI-Bench (Yang et al., 2024) by introducing a novel cross-view rotation estimation task. The key departure is a template-based (rather than LLM-generated) QA synthesis strategy that scales to 12M pairs while supporting 19 diverse instruction formats, addressing the expressiveness gap in prior spatial datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the largest open-source spatial reasoning dataset (12M QA pairs) with broad scene coverage and multi-view supervision, InternSpatial provides a critical training resource for the robotics, embodied AI, and AR/VR communities where spatial understanding is essential. The finding that template-based generation at scale can push an 8B model past GPT-4o on spatial benchmarks while preserving general multimodal performance suggests that data diversity and format coverage matter more than generation sophistication for this task. A key limitation is that template-based QA may underrepresent the full richness of natural language in real-world spatial queries, and the rotation estimation task, while novel, remains a relatively narrow multi-view sub-task. Open questions include whether these gains transfer to interactive, open-ended spatial reasoning in embodied settings.
