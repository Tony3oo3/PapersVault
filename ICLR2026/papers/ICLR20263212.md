## Problem

Unsupervised skill discovery fails to scale to high-DoF agents because the exploration space grows exponentially with dimensionality while the semantically meaningful manifold remains small, causing state-of-the-art methods like METRA to produce unstructured, arbitrary joint motions in 69-DoF humanoids. The paper asks whether grounding the latent skill space in reference motion data before exploration can tame this curse of dimensionality and yield both diverse and semantically interpretable skills.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263212/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a 359-D single-frame body observation (phase, root height/rotation, 23 joint rotations and velocities, hand/foot positions) concatenated with a 16-D latent skill vector z sampled on a unit hypersphere, and outputs 69-D target joint rotations for PD controllers. The encoder q_φ operates on 5-frame stacked observations to produce vMF-distributed latent vectors. For downstream tasks, a high-level policy additionally receives a 2-D goal position and outputs the latent z that conditions the frozen low-level policy.

## Method

RGSD is a two-stage algorithm. First, contrastive pretraining with InfoNCE loss under a von Mises-Fisher encoder embeds each reference motion as a single directional vector on a 16-D unit hypersphere, provably aligning all frames within a motion to the same direction. Second, imitation and discovery are trained in parallel using the DIAYN reward repurposed as an imitation signal: conditioning on a motion embedding z_m makes the reward r(s,z_m) = C + κμ_φ(s)ᵀz_m measure cosine similarity to the reference, while sampling z from random hypersphere directions drives discovery of semantically related novel skills. A separate trainable encoder initialized from the frozen pretrained one (with KL regularization) protects the grounded latent space during discovery, and reference state initialization ensures overlapping state distributions between the two processes. The paper provides a theoretical proof that the reward attains its global optimum exactly at reference states and is locally quasi-concave, establishing it as a valid imitation objective.

## Results

On a 69-DoF SMPL humanoid, RGSD achieves the lowest Cartesian error (trajectory fidelity) on 4 out of 5 imitation tasks: Walk (7.4 cm), Run (7.7 cm), Sidestep (6.7 cm), and Backward (6.7 cm), substantially outperforming pure unsupervised discovery baselines DIAYN (27–53 cm) and METRA (42–52 cm). Meta-Motivo achieves lower FID (naturalness) scores on 4 out of 5 tasks (e.g., 1.4 vs. 8.0 for Sidestep, 2.1 vs. 4.0 for Backward), reflecting a fidelity-vs.-naturalness trade-off between frame-level and occupancy-matching objectives. On the downstream GoalReaching task with style constraints, RGSD is the only method that consistently adheres to the commanded style (sidestepping, backward) while reaching the goal, whereas CALM and Meta-Motivo abandon the style command.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against pure unsupervised discovery baselines, RGSD dominates decisively: DIAYN and METRA produce Cartesian errors 4–7× higher than RGSD across all five tasks, confirming that reference grounding is essential for meaningful skill discovery in high-DoF systems. Among imitation-based baselines, RGSD achieves the best Cartesian error on 4 of 5 tasks (Run: 7.7 vs. 15.4 for Meta-Motivo; Sidestep: 6.7 vs. 11.8; Backward: 6.7 vs. 8.6; Punch: 7.7 vs. 8.1), while Meta-Motivo achieves lower FID on 4 of 5 tasks, indicating smoother but less precise motion reproduction. CALM shows the weakest overall performance, with FID degrading severely during discovery (e.g., Walk FID jumps from 1.4 to 15.5) and ultimately ignoring style commands in downstream tasks. On the downstream GoalReaching task, RGSD is the only method that reliably maintains the commanded style (sidestepping or backward) while reaching the goal; CALM reverts to forward running, Meta-Motivo only respects style when the goal is in the favorable direction, and ASE frequently remains stationary. The paper also demonstrates that RGSD enables test-time diversity control via the vMF concentration parameter κ, a capability not available in any baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

RGSD builds directly on DIAYN's mutual information objective as its discovery backbone, but inverts the conventional pipeline: rather than exploring first and then inducing latent structure, it pretrains a contrastive encoder on reference motions to ground the latent space on a unit hypersphere before any environmental interaction. This 'pretraining-then-discovery' design is explicitly analogous to LLM training regimes (self-supervised pretraining followed by RL fine-tuning), and it departs from both pure unsupervised discovery (DIAYN, METRA, CIC) and GAIL-based imitation (ASE, CALM, Meta-Motivo) by unifying imitation and discovery under a single reward formulation. The paper also provides a theoretical proof that the DIAYN reward, when the encoder is frozen and conditioned on a motion embedding, satisfies the two conditions required for a valid imitation objective (global optimum at reference states and local quasi-concavity), bridging the conceptual gap between discovery and imitation objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of structured, semantically meaningful skill discovery on a 69-DoF humanoid, a setting where prior methods (DIAYN, METRA) produce degenerate, unstructured motions. The negative result showing that WDM/METRA-based objectives are structurally incompatible with repetitive motions in local coordinate frames (the reward collapses to zero for cyclic behaviors like walking) is an important diagnostic that explains a class of failures beyond mere performance gaps. Open questions include whether the approach extends to genuinely compositional behaviors (e.g., walking while punching), whether it scales across embodiments and larger motion datasets toward a 'skill foundation model,' and whether the requirement for reference data limits its applicability in truly reward-free or data-scarce settings.
