## Problem

Goal-conditioned behavioral cloning (GCBC) methods perform well on in-distribution tasks but fail to generalize zero-shot to novel state-goal pairs requiring combinatorial generalization (i.e., 'stitching' together information from multiple training trajectories). The paper identifies the root cause as a lack of temporal consistency in the learned state representation: temporally correlated states are not encoded to similar latent representations, creating an out-of-distribution gap when the policy must condition on goal states it has never seen paired with a given starting state.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263448/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy takes a current state and a goal state (low-dimensional state vectors of dimension 29 for ant or 69 for humanoid, or 64×64×6 RGB images for visual tasks) and outputs a low-level continuous control action. During training, the goal is sampled as a future state from the same trajectory; at evaluation, the goal may be a state reachable only by combining information across multiple training trajectories. The encoder processes state and goal separately (MLP or CNN+MLP), and their representations are concatenated before an MLP action head produces the action.

## Method

The paper proposes BYOL-γ, a self-predictive representation learning objective that modifies standard BYOL by sampling prediction targets geometrically (k ~ geom(1−γ)) rather than one-step, so the predictor ψ(φ(s_t), a_t) must match the encoder representation of a temporally distant future state φ(s_{t+k}). This is used as an auxiliary loss alongside the BC objective, with a bidirectional variant adding a backward predictor ψ_b. The key theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) shows that in finite MDPs, minimizing BYOL-γ approximates a spectral decomposition of the normalized successor representation M̃^π ≈ ΦΨΦ^T, meaning the learned representations encode long-range temporal reachability between states. Unlike contrastive learning (TRA), BYOL-γ requires no negative samples or TD bootstrapping, computing only O(B) loss terms per batch, and avoids the pessimism that contrastive objectives introduce for states from different policies in mixture datasets.

## Results

On the OGBench combinatorial generalization suite, BYOL-γ achieves an average success rate of 35% across all 8 environments (state: 33%, visual: 37%), outperforming the strongest baseline TD-SR (32%) and TRA (27%). BYOL-γ is particularly strong on humanoidmaze-medium (51% vs. 42% for TD-SR) and visual-antmaze-medium (68% vs. 49% for TD-SR), while TD-SR leads on smaller antmaze environments (64% vs. 58% on antmaze-medium). All auxiliary representation learning methods substantially outperform offline RL baselines (GCIQL: 8%, QRL: 14%, CRL: 25%), and BYOL-γ is the only method that does not degrade performance relative to plain GCBC on visual environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BYOL-γ outperforms TRA (contrastive learning auxiliary loss, Myers et al. 2025) by 8 points on average (35 vs. 27), with the largest gaps on humanoidmaze-medium (+6) and visual-antmaze-medium (+16); TRA also suffers pessimism from negative samples that push apart representations of states from different policies. Against TD-SR (TD-based successor measure approximation, Touati et al. 2023), BYOL-γ wins on average (35 vs. 32) and on 5 of 8 environments, but loses on the two antmaze environments where TD-SR's bootstrapping provides an advantage in smaller state spaces. All auxiliary representation methods (BYOL-γ, TD-SR, TRA) substantially outperform offline RL baselines: GCIQL (8%), QRL (14%), and CRL (25%), confirming that representation learning as an auxiliary loss is more effective than value-based offline RL for combinatorial generalization. In the hierarchical setting (Table 10), HBYOL-γ reaches 45% average, outperforming HGCBC (28%) and approaching HIQL (63%), with the largest gains on visual-antmaze-medium (84% vs. 74% for HGCBC-φ). Notably, BYOL-γ is the only method that does not degrade below plain GCBC on visual environments, whereas TRA and TD-SR both hurt visual performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the BYOL self-predictive framework (Grill et al., 2020) from one-step latent prediction to geometrically-sampled future prediction (BYOL-γ), connecting it theoretically to the successor representation in finite MDPs. It positions itself against TRA (Myers et al., 2025b), which uses contrastive learning as an auxiliary BC objective, and TD-SR (a Forward-Backward-style TD loss, Touati and Ollivier, 2021), by providing a unifying framework (Table 1) that characterizes what each objective approximates under single-policy vs. mixture-policy data. The key departure is showing that BYOL-γ avoids both the bootstrapping instability of TD methods and the pessimism of contrastive negatives when trained on mixture datasets, while still capturing temporally extended successor-measure structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Combinatorial generalization (stitching) is a critical bottleneck for BC-based robot foundation models trained on offline demonstration data, where scaling data is expensive. BYOL-γ offers a simple, scalable auxiliary loss that improves stitching without requiring RL, generative models, or explicit planning, making it practical for large-scale supervised policy training. However, a generalization gap persists on the hardest environments (e.g., giant maze), improvements on visual tasks are modest, and the theoretical guarantee relies on restrictive assumptions (symmetric dynamics, uniform initialization). Open questions include whether the approach transfers to manipulation tasks, scales to larger visual datasets, and whether the mixture-of-SRs approximation (vs. true mixture-policy SR) can be closed.
