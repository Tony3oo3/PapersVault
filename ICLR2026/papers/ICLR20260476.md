## Problem

Detecting missing persons beneath dense forest canopies from low-altitude UAV perspectives remains unsolved because existing SAR datasets rely on high-altitude top-down or oblique aerial views where individuals are reduced to a few pixels and heavily occluded by foliage, while ground-level person datasets lack the vegetation occlusion patterns, non-standing poses, and environmental variability characteristic of forest search scenarios. No dedicated benchmark exists for under-canopy missing person detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260476/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 96,482 RGB images (and a companion 64,142-image thermal IR set, ForestPersonsIR) captured at 1.5–2.0 m altitude with handheld or tripod-mounted cameras to simulate MAV viewpoints. Each of the 204,078 annotated instances includes a bounding box covering only the visible portion of the person, a pose label (standing, sitting, or lying), and a discrete visibility level (20, 40, 70, or 100) quantifying occlusion severity. The dataset spans seasons (summer, fall, winter), weather conditions (sunny, overcast, rain, snow), and times of day (afternoon, dusk), with face anonymization applied via automated detection and manual review.

## Method

ForestPersons is constructed by simulating missing-person scenarios with actors in diverse postures across 377 video sequences collected in forest interiors, valleys, and forest edges, with camera operators actively navigating around branches and underbrush to create natural, dynamic occlusion. A key design choice is a model-driven difficulty-aware split: a COCO-pretrained Faster R-CNN computes a per-sequence difficulty score (1 − AP₅₀), and sequences are interleaved across train (67,686 images), validation (18,243), and test (10,553) splits so each contains a proportional mix of easy, medium, and hard samples, preventing temporal overlap and distributional bias. The benchmark evaluates ten supervised detectors (YOLOv3/X/v11, RetinaNet, Faster R-CNN, Deformable R-CNN, SSD, DETR, DINO, CZ Det) trained from scratch on ForestPersons, plus zero-shot vision-language models (GPT-4o/5, Gemini 2.5, Qwen3-VL, Grounding DINO, OWL-ViT, etc.), and ablates the impact of pose and season diversity on generalization. The paper further validates transferability to real MAV footage by testing on a 24,209-image field dataset and showing that artifact-free ForestPersons-trained models outperform both artifact-augmented variants and models trained on prior aerial SAR datasets.

## Results

On the ForestPersons test split (10,553 images, 20,867 annotations), Deformable R-CNN achieves the highest AP of 66.3, while CZ Det attains the best AP50 (96.1) and AP75 (77.9), and DINO leads in recall with AR of 77.7. YOLOv11 is the strongest single-stage detector at AP 65.6, closely matching the two-stage ResNet-50-based models (RetinaNet 64.2, Faster R-CNN 64.4). SSD (MobileNetV2-based) lags significantly at AP 45.0, and DETR trails at AP 53.9, highlighting that lightweight and transformer-based architectures struggle with the severe occlusion and small-target challenges of under-canopy scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the ten detection models, no single architecture dominates all metrics: Deformable R-CNN leads in AP (66.3), CZ Det in AP50/AP75 (96.1/77.9), and DINO in AR (77.7), while YOLOv11 offers the best single-stage trade-off (AP 65.6, AR 71.7). The paper's key finding is that prior datasets fail to transfer: Faster R-CNN trained on SARD drops from AP 58.6 to 3.0, HERIDAL from 35.0 to 0.2, and WiSARD from 18.5 to 11.3 on ForestPersons, while ground-level datasets (COCO Person 40.8, CrowdHuman 31.9, CityPersons 5.9) also degrade substantially due to vegetation occlusion and non-standing poses. On the real MAV test set (24,209 images), ForestPersons-trained Faster R-CNN achieves AP 61.4, outperforming artifact-augmented ForestPersons (35.8) and all prior SAR datasets (WiSARD 40.2, SARD 23.2, HERIDAL 0.0), confirming that the handheld collection generalizes well to actual drone footage. In zero-shot VLM evaluation, generative models (GPT-4o, GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Flash) achieve near-zero AP, while open-vocabulary detectors like Grounding DINO (AP 52.4, AP50 77.8) and OWL-ViT (AP 49.2, AP50 77.2) perform reasonably but still trail the domain-specific Faster R-CNN baseline (AP50 92.7) by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first dataset explicitly targeting person detection from ground-level, under-canopy forest perspectives, a setting that falls between two well-studied but misaligned regimes: high-altitude aerial SAR datasets (SARD, HERIDAL, WiSARD) that use top-down or oblique views, and ground-level urban person detection datasets (COCO, CrowdHuman, CityPersons) that feature standing individuals in everyday environments. Rather than building on a specific detection architecture, the contribution is the data itself—96,482 images with pose and visibility annotations collected to simulate MAV viewpoints—along with a model-driven difficulty-aware splitting strategy. The paper also positions its zero-shot VLM evaluation (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5, Grounding DINO, OWL-ViT) as a complementary lens on whether general-purpose multimodal models can substitute for domain-specific detectors in this niche.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ForestPersons addresses a critical data bottleneck for autonomous SAR in forested environments, where canopy occlusion, non-standing poses, and seasonal variability make prior benchmarks inadequate. The empirical demonstration that models trained on existing aerial or ground-level datasets collapse to near-zero AP on this task (e.g., SARD: 58.6 → 3.0; HERIDAL: 35.0 → 0.2) provides concrete evidence that the under-canopy setting is a distinct problem requiring dedicated data. Open questions include whether the handheld-to-MAV domain gap fully closes in more aggressive flight conditions, how the dataset scales to extreme SAR situations (injury, burial, hypothermia) that ethical constraints prevent from collecting, and whether the moderate inter-annotator agreement on visibility labels (Cohen's κ ≈ 0.45) limits the utility of that attribute for fine-grained evaluation. The public release of both RGB and thermal (ForestPersonsIR) variants broadens its applicability to multimodal SAR pipelines.
