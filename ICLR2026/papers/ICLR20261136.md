## Problem

State-of-the-art TCR-pMHC binding predictors such as TULIP use encoder-decoder transformer architectures with cross-attention, but existing post-hoc explainability methods (AttnLRP, TokenTM, AttCAT, Rollout) are designed only for encoder-only or co-attention models and cannot extract interaction information from cross-attention layers. Additionally, no quantitative benchmark exists to evaluate whether XAI methods correctly identify the residues that physically interact in TCR-pMHC complexes, leaving interpretability claims in this domain unvalidated.

## Input / Output

QCAI operates on a pre-trained encoder-decoder transformer (TULIP) and requires only forward and backward passes to obtain gradients of an amino-acid-wise negative log-likelihood loss with respect to each cross-attention matrix. The output is a vector of per-residue importance scores for each of the three input sequences (CDR3a, CDR3b, peptide), which are then compared against residue-level interaction distances (3–6 Å) computed from 274 X-ray crystallography structures in the TCR-XAI benchmark. A one-residue positional tolerance is applied via a [1/3, 1/3, 1/3] convolution kernel to account for minor attention shifts relative to structural ground truth.

## Method

QCAI decomposes the cross-attention importance map S(A_l) = E_H(ReLU(∂L^c/∂A_l ⊙ A_l)) + I into separate query and key contributions. For the query side, it isolates Q_l's contribution by right-multiplying S(A_l) with the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse K_l(K_l^T K_l)^{-1}, yielding a per-token importance vector; for the key side, it takes the maximum relevance of each key token across all queries and heads. Each side's final score is the element-wise maximum of its intrinsic GradCAM-style importance and its attention-conditioned importance, ensuring robustness when a token is influenced by multiple layers. Layer-wise aggregation follows the attention-flow perspective, recursively propagating importance backward through decoder and encoder layers using element-wise maximum to retain the strongest attribution signal across diverging cross-attention paths.

## Results

On the TCR-XAI benchmark (274 crystal structures), QCAI achieves ROC-AUC of 0.5492 (CDR3a), 0.5489 (CDR3b), and 0.6024 (peptide) for binding-site identification, outperforming all seven baselines. In perturbation experiments, QCAI attains the most negative LOdds across all three chains (−3.328 CDR3a, −3.498 CDR3b, −1.470 peptide) and the highest AOPC on CDR3b (0.045) and peptide (0.013). The Binding Region Hit Rate at the 25th percentile reaches 79.1% (CDR3a), 79.3% (CDR3b), and 74.3% (peptide), the best among all methods tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QCAI consistently achieves the most negative LOdds across all three chains, indicating that perturbing its top-ranked residues causes the largest drop in model confidence. On CDR3b and peptide, QCAI also leads in AOPC (0.045 and 0.013 respectively), while on CDR3a it trails Rollout (0.022) and TokenTM (0.021) by a small margin. The gap is most dramatic on the peptide chain, where QCAI's LOdds of −1.470 dwarfs the next-best GradCAM (−1.004) and LRP (−1.167), and AttnLRP/Rollout produce near-zero or negative AOPC, suggesting they fail to identify truly important peptide residues. In ROC-AUC for binding-site identification, QCAI leads on all three chains (0.5492/0.5489/0.6024), with the peptide AUC exceeding 0.6. For BRHR at the 25th percentile, QCAI outperforms all baselines by 5–15 percentage points (e.g., 79.3% vs. 72.6% for AttnLRP on CDR3b; 74.3% vs. 69.1% for AttCAT on peptide). The ablation in Appendix A.11 confirms that cross-attention alone drives most of QCAI's advantage: QCAI applied only to cross-attention matches full QCAI on LOdds and AOPC, while self-attention-only (Rollout) degrades substantially.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GradCAM-style gradient-based explanation framework (Selvaraju et al., 2017) from convolutional feature maps to cross-attention matrices in encoder-decoder transformers, addressing an architectural gap left by existing post-hoc methods (AttnLRP, TokenTM, AttCAT, Rollout) that only handle self-attention in encoder-only models. The key mathematical departure is the use of the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse to decompose the non-square cross-attention importance map into separate query and key contributions, since the attention matrix A ∈ R^{N×N'} no longer provides a symmetric attribution. The paper also introduces TCR-XAI, a structural benchmark of 274 crystallographic TCR-pMHC complexes, which provides the first quantitative ground truth (residue-level interaction distances) for evaluating XAI methods in this domain, moving beyond the qualitative assessments used by prior work like TEPCAM and TCR-BERT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Interpretable TCR-pMHC binding models are essential for designing personalized cancer immunotherapies, vaccines, and understanding autoimmune mechanisms, yet current state-of-the-art predictors (TULIP, MixTCRpred) remain black boxes. The TCR-XAI benchmark and BRHR metric establish a reproducible quantitative standard that the field lacked, enabling fair comparison of explanation methods against physical interaction data. Limitations include modest absolute ROC-AUC values (0.55–0.60), ~20× higher computational cost from pseudoinverse operations, and the inherently post-hoc nature of the method, which cannot feed interpretability back into prediction. Open questions include whether energy-based evaluation (e.g., Rosetta) would yield stronger ground truth than distance, and whether explain-by-design architectures can integrate cross-attention insights directly into binding prediction.
