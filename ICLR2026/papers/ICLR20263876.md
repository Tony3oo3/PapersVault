## Problem

Generative-model-based data augmentation faces a fundamental paradox: it is most needed when data is scarce, yet requires a pre-trained model that itself demands abundant data. Even when foundational models exist, large-scale generation is computationally prohibitive, and the lack of interpretability and fine-grained controllability over generated outputs poses risks in high-stakes applications. This paper seeks a learning-free, efficient, and controllable augmentation framework that operates directly on the geometric structure of the data.

## Input / Output

The method takes raw data (images, audio waveforms, or tabular features) that must be reshaped into positive tensors with dimensions following prime-number factorization (e.g., a 28×28 MNIST image becomes a 6th-order tensor of shape 7×2×2×7×2×2), along with a user-specified poset structure Ω encoding feature relationships and a base sub-manifold B defined by an ℓ-body many-body approximation. The output is a set of augmented samples in the original data space, generated by sampling latent representations from a kernel density model fitted on the projected dataset and decoding them via backward projection. No model weights are trained; all operations are closed-form convex optimizations.

## Method

The framework embeds each data point as a discrete probability distribution on a dually-flat statistical manifold S via the log-linear model on posets, where the poset structure Ω induces a natural energy-based geometry over features. Encoding (forward projection) maps each embedded point onto a low-dimensional e-flat base sub-manifold B = M_ℓ, which preserves only mode interactions up to order ℓ, solved as a convex KL-divergence minimization via natural gradient descent. Augmentation is performed in B by sampling from a kernel density estimate of the projected dataset or by controlled perturbations. Decoding (backward projection) finds the k-nearest neighbors of the augmented latent point in B, constructs a local data sub-manifold D = M_ℓ*(N) by fixing ℓ-body parameters to the neighbor average while freeing lower-order parameters, and projects the latent point back onto D — a second convex program. The term 'pseudo-nonlinear' reflects that projections are linear in the manifold's intrinsic θ-coordinates yet nonlinear in the ambient data space, yielding an autoencoder-like pipeline that is entirely learning-free and offers explicit control over which feature interactions are preserved or perturbed.

## Results

On the six-dataset benchmark, PNL achieves the best or near-best test accuracy across modalities. The standout gains are on Connectionist Bench (93.81% vs. 88.10% original, a +5.7 pp improvement) and Wine Quality (59.03% vs. 55.00% original, +4.0 pp), where PNL also attains the lowest standard deviation (4.54% vs. 8.58% for the original set). On image datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10), PNL is competitive but trails standard augmentation (STD) by 0.07 pp and 1.82 pp respectively, while on Speech Commands PNL (84.35%) matches the original set (84.48%) and outperforms all learned baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PNL consistently outperforms the autoencoder baseline (AE) on five of six datasets, with the largest margin on Connectionist Bench (93.81% vs. 82.86%, +10.95 pp) and Wine Quality (59.03% vs. 57.23%, +1.80 pp); AE only edges out PNL on MNIST (97.97% vs. 97.91%) and CIFAR-10 (88.36% vs. 88.07%). Against mixup (MU), PNL wins on five datasets, most dramatically on Speech Commands (84.35% vs. 81.85%, +2.50 pp) and CIFAR-10 (88.07% vs. 86.60%, +1.47 pp), while MU narrowly leads on Taiwanese Bankruptcy (96.55% vs. 96.53%). Manifold mixup (MMU) is the closest competitor, but PNL still wins on Connectionist Bench (93.81% vs. 91.19%, +2.62 pp), Wine Quality (59.03% vs. 58.70%), and Speech Commands (84.35% vs. 83.06%), with near-ties on CIFAR-10 and Taiwanese Bankruptcy. Standard augmentation (STD) is the only method that beats PNL, and only on the two image datasets (MNIST: 97.98% vs. 97.91%; CIFAR-10: 89.89% vs. 88.07%), which the authors attribute to STD's explicit enforcement of modality-specific invariances (rotation, translation, color). On all non-image modalities (speech, tabular), PNL dominates STD by 1.4–8.6 pp, and PNL achieves the lowest standard deviation across all six datasets, indicating superior training stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three established theoretical threads—energy-based modeling (Xie et al., 2016), log-linear models on partially ordered sets (Sugiyama et al., 2016, 2017), and dually-flat information geometry (Amari, 2016)—into a unified, learning-free data augmentation framework. It departs from the dominant generative-model-based augmentation paradigm (autoencoders, GANs, diffusion models) by replacing learned encoders/decoders with convex projections onto explicitly constructed flat sub-manifolds, and from classical linear dimension reduction (PCA, SVD) by operating on a curved statistical manifold where the 'inverse' (backward projection) is solved via k-nearest-neighbor local sub-manifold construction rather than a learned decoder. The many-body approximation sub-manifolds (Ghalamkari et al., 2024; Derun & Sugiyama, 2025) provide the concrete latent-space design that makes the framework practically usable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, interpretable alternative to black-box generative augmentation, with fine-grained controllability over which feature interactions are preserved (via the choice of ℓ-body sub-manifold) and which are discarded. A key open limitation is the framework's reliance on a natural partial order over features, which makes it ill-suited for permutation-invariant data (e.g., graphs) without ad-hoc modifications. Whether the poset structure can be learned or automatically inferred from data, and whether the approach scales to very high-dimensional continuous distributions beyond the discrete poset setting, remain important open questions. The modest empirical gains over strong baselines (competitive but not dramatically superior) suggest the framework's primary value is conceptual clarity and controllability rather than raw accuracy.
