## Problem

Existing activation steering methods for LLM instruction following (e.g., PASTA, SpotLight) rely on static, manually-tuned hyperparameters applied uniformly across all decoding steps, leading to oversteering where excessive emphasis on instructions degrades task accuracy and text quality. No prior method adaptively modulates steering strength in response to the evolving generation context, leaving a gap between instruction adherence and output fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264024/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt with a clearly demarcated instruction span (identified by token indices, either manually separated or self-extracted by the model) and produces a standard autoregressive text sequence. During generation, the method operates on the KV cache of the instruction tokens at selected attention layers, requiring no additional training data or external models at inference time. A gating mechanism based on the raw top-2 token probabilities allows skipping the steered forward pass entirely when steering cannot plausibly change the prediction, limiting throughput reduction to ~16%.

## Method

DIRECTER steers the model by scaling key vectors in the KV cache for instruction tokens (α=100) at a subset of attention layers, then gates the intervention with a plausibility-guided decoding loop: at each step, the steered output distribution is accepted only if its top-1 token had probability ≥ β·p_top1 under the raw distribution (β=0.5); otherwise the number of steered layers is halved and the check repeats. Layer selection is guided by a one-time attention sensitivity analysis that steers each layer individually and ranks layers by the aggregate cosine-distance shift they propagate across all layers' attention inputs and outputs, inverting the logic of KV cache quantization. A top-2 probability gate pre-determines when no steered distribution can pass the plausibility test, avoiding unnecessary forward passes. The plausibility filter also functions as a general safety gate that mitigates oversteering in other steering methods (PASTA, SpotLight) when applied as a post-hoc constraint.

## Results

On the IFEval benchmark, DIRECTER achieves 78.8% prompt-level and 84.8% instruction-level accuracy, improving the zero-shot baseline (73.5%/81.5%) by 6.5% in average score. On LIFBench, DIRECTER reaches 64.4/70.0/51.7 (List/OneDoc/MultiDoc) ARS, and on GSM8K-Format it attains 99.1% format accuracy alongside 86.9% task accuracy. In LLM-judged evaluation, DIRECTER achieves the highest task fidelity (91.7%) while matching the text quality of non-intervention baselines (4.40/5.0). Overall, DIRECTER improves the cross-benchmark average by 6.5% over zero-shot and by ~4% over the best prior steering method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIRECTER outperforms both prior steering methods across all three benchmarks. Against PASTA* (tuned, α=0.1), DIRECTER gains +5.5 points on the cross-benchmark average (76.5 vs. 71.0), with the largest margin on GSM8K-Format task accuracy (86.9% vs. 62.7%), where PASTA* suffers severe oversteering that degrades mathematical reasoning. Against SpotLight* (tuned, ψ_target=0.1), DIRECTER gains +4.4 points on average (76.5 vs. 72.1), with particularly large improvements on LIFBench MultiDoc (51.7 vs. 38.8) and GSM8K-Format task accuracy (86.9 vs. 78.7). Both steering baselines also degrade on LIFBench MultiDoc relative to the zero-shot baseline (40.9), confirming the oversteering problem that DIRECTER's plausibility-guided loop is designed to prevent. In LLM-judged evaluation, DIRECTER achieves 91.7% task fidelity versus 81.1% for PASTA* and 85.7% for SpotLight*, while maintaining text quality (4.40/5.0) on par with the zero-shot baseline (4.39). The plausibility filter also serves as a general safety gate: when applied to PASTA and SpotLight, it mitigates their oversteering and improves their scores, though the fully integrated DIRECTER still achieves the best overall balance.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIRECTER sits at the intersection of two prior research threads: activation steering for instruction following (PASTA, SpotLight) and contrastive/layer-contrastive decoding (Li et al., 2022; Chuang et al., 2023). It departs from prior steering methods by replacing their static, manually-tuned configurations with a dynamic, step-wise rejection loop that adaptively modulates steering strength via the number of steered layers. The layer-ranking mechanism inverts the logic of KV cache compression (Ge et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024b), which identifies low-impact layers for removal, to instead identify high-impact layers for steering. The plausibility-guided decoding loop is directly inspired by contrastive decoding's accept/reject paradigm but repurposed as a safety gate against oversteering rather than a generation strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The oversteering problem—where excessive instruction emphasis degrades task accuracy and text quality—is a fundamental limitation of all prior activation steering methods, and DIRECTER's plausibility-guided rejection loop offers a general, modular mechanism to mitigate it (demonstrated by improving PASTA and SpotLight when used as a safety gate). The method's robustness to the scaling factor α across orders of magnitude and its compatibility with FlashAttention make it practically deployable with only ~16% throughput cost. Open questions include: the lack of a formal theoretical justification for the attention sensitivity metric, the assumption of cleanly separable instruction spans (interleaved real-world prompts remain challenging), and whether the dynamic control principle generalizes beyond instruction following to other steering objectives (e.g., truthfulness, safety).
