## Problem

Existing LoRA-based continual learning methods, designed primarily for vision, assume disjoint tasks and aim to preserve performance on all previous tasks. However, continual recommendation has a fundamentally different stability-plasticity tradeoff: the goal is to capture evolving user preferences for near-future prediction, not to recall past preferences, and outdated preferences can actively harm performance. The paper shows that cumulative LoRA (summing frozen adapters), which works well for minimally interfering tasks, underperforms even a simple single evolving LoRA in the natural chronological recommendation setting where users reappear with drifting preferences.

## Input / Output

The model takes a user's interaction history as a sequence of item semantic ID tokens (fixed-length token sequences produced by an RQ-VAE codebook tokenizer trained on item title/description features) and autoregressively generates the next item's semantic ID token sequence. At inference, constrained beam search restricted to valid item tokens produces a ranked list of 10 candidate items, evaluated with Hit@k and NDCG@k. Training uses sliding-window pairs of size 20 extracted from each user's sequence to create multiple prefix-target examples per user.

## Method

PESO maintains a single evolving LoRA adapter (initialized from the previous stage) and adds a proximal regularizer that penalizes deviation from the most recent frozen adapter state, creating a natural competition between the data-fitting cross-entropy loss and the preservation term. Theoretically, under a quadratic approximation in the LoRA subspace, the proximal design yields direction-wise interpolation: along eigendirections of the tangent-feature second-moment matrix where current data provides strong support, the model moves toward the new-data optimum (plasticity), while along weakly-supported directions it stays close to the previous state (stability). The practical instantiation uses a per-module softmax-KL proximal, which locally reduces to a quadratic form with a block-diagonal metric (diag(p) − ppᵀ per LoRA factor group), penalizing relative reshuffling within each module and weighting deviations by prior parameter mass, thereby preserving internal LoRA structure rather than treating all coordinates uniformly.

## Results

On three Amazon Review datasets (Instruments, Movies & TV, Books), PESO achieves the best NDCG@5 of 0.0138, 0.0118, and 0.0311 respectively, with average gains of 3.71%, 4.62%, and 6.26% over the strongest competitors (Single Evolving LoRA, SumLoRA_Latest+Inherit, and SD-LoRA_Latest+Inherit). All continual methods outperform the static PRETRAIN baseline, confirming the importance of adapting to evolving user preferences. PESO also achieves the best Hit@5 (0.0193, 0.0180, 0.0448) and Hit@10 (0.0268, 0.0251, 0.0569) across the three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PESO consistently outperforms all cumulative LoRA variants: InfLoRA (Liang & Li, CVPR 2024) yields the weakest results overall (e.g., NDCG@5 of 0.0105 on Instruments vs. PESO's 0.0138), likely because freezing LoRA-A prevents gradual adaptation; SD-LoRA (Wu et al., 2025) with learnable magnitudes achieves 0.0128 NDCG@5 on Instruments, still 7.8% below PESO; and the best SumLoRA variant (Latest+Inherit) reaches 0.0130, a 6.2% gap. Against other continual PEFT methods, PESO beats O-LoRA (Wang et al., EMNLP 2023; NDCG@5 0.0134), AM-LoRA (Liu et al., 2024; 0.0125), and LSAT (Shi et al., CIKM 2024; 0.0117) on Instruments. Compared to traditional two-tower continual recommenders, PESO achieves higher absolute performance on Movies & TV (0.0173 vs. PISA's 0.0106) and Books (0.0422 vs. 0.0301), though PISA (Yoo et al., SIGIR 2025) edges out PESO by a narrow margin on Instruments (0.0194 vs. 0.0190), where explicit user-item dual embeddings capture collaborative signals more effectively. PESO also surpasses Contrastive (Wang et al., CIKM 2021; 0.0177) and Contrastive+PIW (Wang et al., AAAI 2023; 0.0193) on Instruments, with larger margins on the other two datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) as the parameter-efficient fine-tuning substrate and departs from the cumulative LoRA family (SumLoRA, SD-LoRA, InfLoRA) that dominates continual PEFT in vision. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that continual recommendation's stability-plasticity tradeoff is fundamentally different from vision: stability means preserving enduring user preferences rather than past-task accuracy, and plasticity requires actively overwriting outdated interests. The proximal regularization design connects to EWC-style parameter regularization in classical continual learning but is specifically instantiated in the LoRA subspace with per-module softmax-KL structure, and the empirical comparison with traditional continual recommenders (PISA, Contrastive+PIW) positions the work at the intersection of LLM-based generative recommendation and the established continual recommendation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that cumulative LoRA—designed and validated for vision—actually underperforms a single evolving adapter in recommendation is a useful negative result that challenges the assumption that more frozen adapters yield better stability. PESO offers a practical, O(1)-storage solution with clean theoretical grounding (direction-wise interpolation in the LoRA subspace), but the absolute gains over the best baselines are modest (3–6%), and the paper acknowledges that LLM-based recommenders still lag behind explicit two-tower models in capturing preference drift. Open questions include whether the softmax-KL proximal provides meaningful advantage over simple L2 at scale, whether the quadratic approximation holds for larger models, and how to incorporate drift-aware or user-personalized regularization.
