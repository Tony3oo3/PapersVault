## Problem

Offline RL in sparse-reward settings suffers from value overestimation in poorly covered state-action regions, and existing remedies (pessimism, behavior regularization, distribution correction) each require careful calibration or suffer from high variance. Prior contrastive preference learning (CPL) contrasts successful and unsuccessful trajectories but does not resolve overestimation when the dataset has limited coverage, because the policy can still become overly optimistic in underrepresented regions due to the absence of strong counterexamples outside the dataset's support.

## Input / Output

The method takes a static offline trajectory dataset partitioned into successful (D+) and unsuccessful (D−) trajectories by a return threshold η, along with a k-length representative segment sampling function. For image-based environments (MetaWorld), observations are 84×84 RGB frames encoded by a pre-trained (but not frozen) ResNet-50; for vector-based environments (Adroit, MuJoCo, Maze2D), raw state vectors are used directly. The output is a stochastic policy π_θ(a|s) implemented as an MLP(1024,1024,1024) with ReLU activations, trained entirely offline without environment interaction.

## Method

PREFORL replaces direct value estimation with a contrastive preference loss that contrasts successful trajectories against two types of synthetically degraded versions: action-based degradation (adding Gaussian noise N(0,σ²I) to actions) and state-based degradation (substituting actions from nearest-neighbor states in D− via FAISS ANN search). Unlike CPL, which contrasts D+ with observed D−, PREFORL contrasts D+ with the degraded set D^↓a ∪ D^↓s, creating a 'squeezing' effect where successful behaviors are bounded by synthetic counterexamples that extend beyond the dataset's support. The loss is a DPO-style logistic objective over k-length representative segments with discount γ, temperature α, and an asymmetric bias λ that down-weights negative segments. A theoretical guarantee (Lemma 3.1) shows that minimizing this loss drives the TV divergence between the learned and optimal policy to zero, provided the perturbed segments cover the local action space around each state in the successful distribution.

## Results

On D4RL Adroit (12 tasks), PREFORL achieves the highest normalized scores on 9 of 12 tasks, with standout gains on door-cloned (16.3 vs. 3.6 for CPL) and hammer-cloned (28.4 vs. 13.2 for CPL). On Sparse-MuJoCo, PREFORL reaches 100% success on hopper-medium, halfcheetah-medium-expert, and walker2d-medium-expert, and 98.4% on hopper-medium-expert, outperforming all value-based baselines. On MetaWorld (16 image-based tasks with only 50 demonstrations), PREFORL outperforms BC by large margins, achieving 90–100% success where BC often scores below 50%. On AntMaze, PREFORL is competitive on large-play-v2 (53.2 vs. 52.3 for ReBRAC) but trails ReBRAC on large-diverse-v2 (26.1 vs. 64.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PREFORL consistently outperforms CPL (the closest methodological baseline) on non-expert Adroit datasets, with the largest gaps on door-cloned (+12.7) and hammer-cloned (+15.2), confirming that synthetic degradation beyond observed failures is critical. Against value-based offline RL methods (CQL, IQL, TD3+BC, CDE, BCQ), PREFORL wins on the majority of Adroit and Sparse-MuJoCo tasks; for example, on Sparse-MuJoCo hopper-medium it reaches 100% success versus 74.0% (CQL) and 0.0% (IQL/TD3+BC). ReBRAC is the strongest competitor: it edges out PREFORL on pen-expert (154.1 vs. 144.8) and hammer-expert (133.8 vs. 128.6), and dominates on AntMaze large-diverse-v2 (64.0 vs. 26.1), where global value regularization benefits from broad data coverage. On MetaWorld with image observations and only 50 demonstrations, PREFORL surpasses BC by 30–60+ percentage points on most tasks, and also outperforms CPL and ReBRAC on disassemble-v2 (90.7% vs. 0%) and stick-push-v2 (100% vs. 0%). Decision Transformer (DT) and AWAC are competitive on AntMaze umaze-diverse-v2 but fail entirely on large-play-v2, where PREFORL achieves 53.2%.

## Relation to Prior Work

PREFORL extends Contrastive Preference Learning (CPL, Hejna et al. 2024) and Direct Preference-based Policy Optimization (DPPO, An et al. 2023) by introducing synthetic degradation operators—Gaussian action perturbation and state-based nearest-neighbor substitution—that generate negative examples outside the dataset's support. This departs from CPL, which only contrasts observed successful and failed trajectories, by explicitly addressing the overestimation problem in sparse-reward offline RL where failure examples are insufficient. The theoretical grounding draws on the maximum-entropy RL identity linking optimal advantage to optimal policy (Ziebart 2018) and the Bradley-Terry preference model from DPO (Rafailov et al. 2023), adapted from contextual bandits to trajectory-level RL. The method positions itself against the three dominant offline RL paradigms—pessimism-based (CQL, MOPO), behavior-regularization (BCQ, IQL, ReBRAC), and distribution-correction (CDE, OptiDICE)—by sidestepping value function estimation entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PREFORL offers a clean, value-function-free alternative for sparse-reward offline RL that consistently outperforms strong baselines across Adroit, Sparse-MuJoCo, Maze2D, and image-based MetaWorld, suggesting that contrastive signals from locally degraded actions can substitute for the global value estimates that traditional methods require. Key limitations include the computational cost of ANN search for state-based degradation, the requirement that Gaussian noise variance remain small to preserve locality (as formalized in Lemma 3.1), and reduced effectiveness on highly heterogeneous datasets like AntMaze large-diverse where local preference pairs become sparse. The authors outline a promising direction toward applying synthetic preference generation to LLM post-training, which could mitigate reward hacking without additional human annotation, though this remains speculative.
