## Problem

Fully polynomial neural networks (comprising only additions and multiplications) are ideal for homomorphic encryption inference, but prior approaches that achieve ImageNet-level accuracy rely on high-degree or cascaded polynomials (effective degree up to 6075), making HE inference prohibitively slow. No prior method could train ultra-low-degree (multiplicative depth ≤ 3) fully polynomial networks stably at ImageNet or ViT scale, as polynomial layers compound numerical instability across depth.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260327/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images from classification benchmarks (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64, ImageNet at 224×224) and outputs a class label via argmax over polynomial-computed logits. The critical constraint is that the entire forward pass—every activation, normalization, and attention operation—must be expressible as additions and multiplications only, so that the computation graph maps directly onto CKKS ciphertext operations without any non-polynomial step. During encrypted inference, the input image is encoded into CKKS ciphertext slots, and the model's polynomial circuit is evaluated entirely in the encrypted domain.

## Method

ULD-Net introduces PolyNorm, a polynomial-only normalization layer that replaces the non-polynomial 1/√x operation with a quadratic approximation g(x), applied to a relative variance value v = Var[x]/Var̄ so that inputs cluster near the best-fitting point μ. A key theoretical insight is that normalization must be computed per-sample (over all axes except batch) to prevent exponential variance explosion across deep polynomial stacks; the paper proves that without per-sample normalization, a fraction of samples will diverge. The full design recipe pairs PolyNorm with trainable quadratic/cubic polynomial activations (PolyAct), linear attention with RoPE for ViTs (replacing softmax), AvgPool for MaxPool, and two auxiliary penalty losses (L₁, L₂) that suppress large v values and encourage v ≈ 1. All polynomial operators maintain multiplicative depth ≤ 3, yielding 2.76×–20.5× HE latency speedups over prior SOTA while matching or exceeding original model accuracy (e.g., 76.70% on ViT-Small/ImageNet).

## Results

On ResNet-18/ImageNet (fully polynomial), ULD-Net achieves 69.79% top-1 accuracy with quadratic (degree-2) activations, outperforming the original model (69.76%) while delivering a 2.76× HE inference speedup over the prior SOTA (SMART-PAF, degree 81). On ViT-Small/ImageNet, ULD-Net reaches 76.70% top-1 accuracy (vs. 76.5% original), and on ViT-Base/ImageNet it reaches 75.20% (vs. 75.3% original), representing the first fully polynomial ViT models trained at ImageNet scale. On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18, ULD-Net achieves 78.81% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the original model by +0.97% and the best partial-polynomial baseline by +3.33%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ULD-Net outperforms all five baselines on both accuracy and HE inference latency. Against the fully polynomial baselines, ULD-Net (degree 2) beats Lee et al. (2021) (degree 6075) by +0.44% accuracy with a 3.50× model speedup, and beats SMART-PAF (Tong et al., 2024) (degree 81) by +0.39% accuracy with a 2.76× model speedup, while using 40× lower polynomial degree than SMART-PAF. Against partial polynomial baselines on ResNet-18/CIFAR-100, ULD-Net (78.81%) surpasses SNL (Cho et al., 2022; 73.75%) by +5.06% and AutoReP (Peng et al., 2023a; 75.48%) by +3.33%, with a 3.17× model latency speedup. Against the Transformer HE inference baseline NEXUS (Zhang et al., 2024) on ViT-Small, ULD-Net achieves +0.09% on CIFAR-10 (91.48% vs. 91.39%) and +0.88% on Tiny-ImageNet (61.40% vs. 60.52%), with a 20.5× reduction in non-polynomial operator latency (390 s vs. 7995 s on CIFAR-10). Notably, ULD-Net is the only method that successfully scales fully polynomial training to ViT-Base/ImageNet, a regime none of the baselines achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the polynomial-activation framework of Lee et al. (2021) and Peng et al. (2023b) for HE-friendly nonlinearity replacement, and on RoPE-based linear attention (Su et al., 2024) for softmax-free ViT inference. It departs sharply from prior fully polynomial approaches (Lee et al. 2021 with effective degree 6075; SMART-PAF with degree 81) by showing that ultra-low-degree (multiplicative depth ≤ 3) operators suffice when combined with a polynomial-only normalization layer (PolyNorm) and a principled choice of normalization axis. Unlike partial-replacement methods (SNL, AutoReP) that still require non-polynomial handling at inference, ULD-Net eliminates all non-polynomial operations while training from scratch rather than applying post-hoc approximations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ULD-Net demonstrates that the first fully polynomial ViT models can be trained stably at ImageNet scale (ViT-Small 76.70%, ViT-Base 75.20%), a milestone that opens the door to HE-encrypted transformer inference without bootstrapping-heavy high-degree circuits. The 20.5× non-polynomial operator speedup over NEXUS and 2.76× model-level speedup over SMART-PAF make encrypted inference substantially more practical. Open questions include whether the approach extends to larger transformers (ViT-Large, BERT-Large, GPT-2), whether PolyNorm's sample-specific normalization axis principle generalizes to non-image modalities, and whether the quadratic/cubic degree ceiling can be pushed further without sacrificing the HE efficiency gains.
