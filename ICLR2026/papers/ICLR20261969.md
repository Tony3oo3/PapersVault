## Problem

Existing GMM-based cryo-EM reconstruction methods require external consensus maps or atomic models for initialization and fail to converge stably from random starts, leaving no self-contained GMM pipeline for homogeneous reconstruction. Meanwhile, off-the-shelf 3D Gaussian Splatting is incompatible with cryo-EM due to fundamental mismatches in projection geometry (perspective vs. orthographic), reconstruction objective (photorealistic appearance vs. physical density recovery), and coordinate conventions (image-centered vs. FFT-aligned).

## Input / Output

The method takes 2D cryo-EM particle images (192×192 or 256×256 pixels), known consensus particle poses (SO(3) rotation matrices), CTF parameters, and in-plane translations as input. It produces a 3D electrostatic potential represented as an anisotropic GMM of N Gaussians (default 30,000, each with 11 parameters: 3D position, 3D scale, 4D quaternion, and amplitude), which can be voxelized into a standard 3D volume. Notably, the method operates entirely in real space rather than the Fourier domain, and the GMM itself is the primary volumetric representation rather than an intermediate to a neural decoder.

## Method

CryoSplat represents the 3D volume as a mixture of anisotropic Gaussians and projects them onto 2D image planes via a differentiable orthographic splatting algorithm that integrates each Gaussian along the beam direction, yielding a closed-form 2D splat. Three key adaptations from 3DGS are introduced: (i) the view-dependent normalization term 1/(2π|Σ̃|^½) is retained to preserve quantitative amplitude correctness (omitted in 3DGS for photorealistic rendering); (ii) the rasterization origin is shifted by half a pixel to align with the FFT grid convention, eliminating phase inconsistencies in CTF modulation; and (iii) alpha blending is replaced by direct summation of splat contributions to match the linear transmission physics of electron imaging. The CTF is applied in the Fourier domain after rasterization for efficiency, and a simple MSE loss with a single unified learning rate across all 11 Gaussian parameters (unlike 3DGS's per-type rates) ensures stable convergence from random initialization within 5 epochs using a CUDA tile-based rasterizer.

## Results

CryoSplat achieves FSC-0.143 resolutions of 3.80 Å (Pf80S ribosome), 2.49 Å (RAG complex), 3.30 Å (E. coli LSU), and 4.26 Å (spliceosome) using 30,000 Gaussians, consistently outperforming both cryoSPARC and cryoDRGN across all four real datasets. The most dramatic improvement is on the RAG complex, where cryoSplat reaches 2.49 Å versus 4.23 Å (cryoSPARC) and 4.07 Å (cryoDRGN). CryoSplat converges within 5 epochs (vs. 50 for cryoDRGN) and uses <380 MiB GPU memory (vs. >2.5 GiB for cryoDRGN), yielding up to 30× overall speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CryoSplat outperforms both baselines on all four real datasets. Against cryoSPARC (voxel-based backprojection), cryoSplat achieves a 1.74 Å improvement on the RAG complex (2.49 vs. 4.23 Å) and consistently higher FSC curves at high spatial frequencies on the Pf80S ribosome, where nominal resolutions are similar (~3.80 Å). Against cryoDRGN (neural field), cryoSplat gains 1.58 Å on the RAG complex (2.49 vs. 4.07 Å) and shows fewer density fragments on the heterogeneous LSU and spliceosome datasets. In terms of efficiency, cryoSplat runs at 2–3× higher FPS than cryoDRGN at comparable Gaussian counts and converges in 5 epochs versus 50, giving up to 30× end-to-end speedup. GPU memory is also dramatically lower: cryoSplat stays below 380 MiB even with 30,000 Gaussians, while cryoDRGN exceeds 2.5 GiB at D=192 and approaches 5 GiB at D=256.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework (Kerbl et al., 2023) to cryo-EM by replacing perspective projection with orthogonal projection, removing alpha blending in favor of physically grounded line integrals, incorporating CTF modulation, and aligning the rasterization coordinate system with FFT conventions. It also draws on the analytical Gaussian projection derivations from EWA Splatting (Zwicker et al., 2002) for the viewing transformation. Conceptually, it departs from prior GMM-based cryo-EM methods (E2GMM, Chen et al.) that all required external consensus maps or atomic models for initialization, by demonstrating that a simple MSE loss with random initialization suffices for stable convergence when the projection physics is correctly modeled.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CryoSplat establishes the missing foundation for using GMMs as self-contained cryo-EM reconstruction tools, which is a prerequisite for integrating them into heterogeneous and ab initio pipelines. The 30× speedup over cryoDRGN and sub-400 MiB memory footprint make GMM-based reconstruction practical at scale. Key limitations include the assumption of known poses (not yet ab initio) and the observation that half-map FSC overestimates effective resolution for GMMs due to their strong self-consistency, leaving open the question of appropriate quality metrics for Gaussian-based reconstructions. Future work on joint pose-structure optimization and heterogeneous reconstruction would extend the framework's applicability.
