## Problem

Joint Energy-Based Models (JEM) can reinterpret standard classifiers as EBMs for simultaneous classification and generation, but their reliance on SGLD-based training causes severe instability and poor sample quality, limiting prior EBM-based hybrids to low-resolution CIFAR-scale datasets. No existing EBM-based hybrid has scaled to high-resolution ImageNet-level datasets while maintaining both competitive generative quality and adversarial robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262322/cropped_page28_idx21.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 256×256 for ImageNet) along with class labels for conditional generation, and out-of-distribution images (from 80M Tiny Images or Open Images) as PGD initialization points for sampling. It produces class predictions with adversarial robustness (ℓ₂-robust), class-conditional or unconditional generated images via 13–36 PGD steps, and visual counterfactual explanations by gradient descent on the joint energy from existing in-distribution images. The same energy function E_θ(x,y) = −f_θ(x)[y] underlies all three outputs, requiring no external generative model.

## Method

DAT replaces SGLD-based JEM training with an adversarial-training-based energy learning strategy: a BCE loss discriminates between real data and contrastive samples produced by T steps of normalized PGD on the joint energy, initialized from OOD images via ancestral sampling (draw a label y′, then descend on E_θ(x,y′)). Simultaneously, standard PGD adversarial training is applied to the cross-entropy classification loss, which not only yields robust classification but also implicitly bounds the R₁ gradient penalty (via spectral norm regularization of the input-Jacobian), eliminating the explicit gradient penalty required by prior AT-EBM methods. A two-stage training procedure first trains a robust classifier (leveraging pretrained AT checkpoints when available), then continues joint training with batch normalization frozen (eval mode) for ResNet/WRN architectures to resolve the known incompatibility between BN and EBM optimization. The combined objective L(θ) = L_AT-CE(θ) + L_BCE(θ) corresponds to the JEM joint log-likelihood factorization, and the theoretical analysis shows the optimal solution learns f_θ*(x)[y] = log p_data(y|x) on the support with constant marginal energy E_θ*(x) = 0.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, DAT with ConvNeXt-Large (T=110) achieves FID 3.29 and IS 310.2, matching the autoregressive model VAR-d16 (FID 3.30) while using fewer parameters (198M vs. 310M), and surpassing diffusion models ADM-G (FID 4.59) and LDM-4-G (FID 3.60). Simultaneously, it achieves 56.40% robust accuracy (ℓ₂, ε=3.0), far exceeding EGC (13.56%) and standard AT ConvNeXt-L (33.38%). On CIFAR-10, DAT (T=40) achieves 75.75% robust accuracy—matching standard AT (75.73%)—while reducing FID from 28.41 (standard AT) to 9.12, and from 38.4 (JEM) to 9.12. On CIFAR-100, DAT (T=45) achieves FID 10.73 with 45.94% robust accuracy, compared to RATIO's FID 24.17 at 47.74% robust accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet 256×256, DAT (ConvNeXt-L) matches VAR-d16 on FID (3.29 vs. 3.30) with 36% fewer parameters and 5× fewer sampling steps, while surpassing ADM-G (FID 4.59) and LDM-4-G (FID 3.60) in generation quality. For robustness, DAT achieves 56.40% robust accuracy, dramatically outperforming EGC (13.56%) and standard AT ConvNeXt-L (33.38%), though it trades ~2.5% clean accuracy (75.78% vs. 78.25%). On CIFAR-10, DAT matches standard AT robust accuracy (75.75% vs. 75.73%) while reducing FID from 28.41 to 9.12, and beats RATIO on FID (9.12 vs. 21.96) at comparable robust accuracy. Against prior EBM hybrids, DAT substantially outperforms JEM (FID 9.12 vs. 38.4; robust acc 75.75% vs. 40.5%) and SADA-JEM (FID 9.12 vs. 9.41; robust acc 75.75% vs. 31.93%). The main trade-off is that EGC achieves lower FID on CIFAR-10 (3.30 vs. 9.12) but lacks adversarial robustness entirely, and Joint-Diffusion reaches FID 6.4 on CIFAR-10 without reporting robust accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two prior lines of work: the JEM framework (Grathwohl et al., 2019), which reinterprets classifier logits as an energy function over the joint distribution p(x,y), and AT-EBM (Yin et al., 2022), which replaces unstable SGLD-based EBM training with PGD-generated contrastive samples and a BCE loss. The key departure is the 'dual' application of adversarial training—using it both for discriminative robustness and as the generative learning mechanism—combined with a two-stage training strategy that resolves the long-standing incompatibility between batch normalization and EBM training. Unlike prior JEM variants (JEM++, Robust-JEM, SADA-JEM) that all still rely on SGLD and remain limited to CIFAR-scale, DAT eliminates SGLD entirely and scales to ImageNet 256×256.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first EBM-based hybrid model to scale to ImageNet 256×256 with competitive generative quality (FID 3.29, matching VAR-d16), resolving a fundamental scalability barrier that had confined the entire JEM line of work to 32×32 images. The practical recipe—leveraging pretrained robust classifiers and adding a short Stage 2 joint training—makes the approach immediately applicable to existing robust models without retraining from scratch. Key limitations include learning only the support of the data distribution (not the full density), underperforming RATIO on OOD detection, and calibration degradation on larger datasets. Open questions include whether the support-based formulation can be extended to capture density information, whether the approach generalizes to other modalities (video, audio), and whether the PGD iteration count trade-off between generation quality and robustness can be resolved more elegantly.
