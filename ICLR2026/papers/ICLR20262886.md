## Problem

Sparse attention (SA) methods assume unselected KVs contribute nothing to the attention output, forcing a trade-off where maintaining quality in less-sparse scenarios requires selecting more KVs and eroding SA's efficiency gains. This paper identifies that attention logits matrices have an inherent low-rank structure (rank bounded by head dimension d, e.g., 128) with a dominant principal singular value accounting for 40–50% of total energy, revealing substantial redundancy that existing sparsity-only approaches ignore.

## Input / Output

At each decoding step, RESA takes the current query vector q_i, the full KV cache, and a precomputed prior distribution (μ_Q·K^T) that was calculated once at the end of prefilling by treating the mean query μ_Q as an extra query appended to the prefill sequence. The output is a compensated attention output O_RESA obtained by delta-merging the prior's weighted V-sum with the SA-selected V-sum using two scalar rescaling factors α and β, all at O(|I|) complexity identical to vanilla SA. The prior logits are a rank-1 approximation of the full logits matrix, and the bias term (q_i − μ_Q)·μ_K^T ensures mean alignment between estimated and true logits to prevent softmax distortion.

## Method

RESA is a training-free framework with two modules that exploit the low-rank structure of attention logits. The Prior Estimator approximates the principal SVD component σ₁u₁v₁^T as μ_Q·K^T (the row-mean of the prefilling logits matrix, which aligns with the top singular vector due to the rank-1 spike phenomenon) plus a deterministic bias term (q_i − μ_Q)·μ_K^T for mean correction; this prior is computed once during the last chunk of prefilling and its softmax-weighted V-sum O_Est is cached. The Online Aggregator then rescales the prior scores by α = Z/Z′ and the SA scores by β = Σ_{j∈I} exp(P^SA_{Ij})/Z′, and merges them via a delta-update: O_RESA = α·O_Est + (β·S^SA_I − α·S_I)·V_I, requiring only element-wise operations over the |I| selected positions. A hyperparameter λ∈[0,1] modulates the prior's influence (λ=0 degenerates to vanilla SA). The paper further shows the prior functions as an attention bias that injects knowledge from unselected KVs, connecting to learnable biases in GPT-oss and outlier-mitigation mechanisms in prior work.

## Results

On the RULER benchmark (8K context, 2.5% KV budget), RESA improves single-task accuracy by up to 26% over the underlying sparse attention method (e.g., Quest MK3 on Llama-3.1-8B: 24→40; ArkVale MK3: 27→43). On LongBench, improvements reach up to 8.22% (Quest 2WMQA on Mistral-7B: 22.41→30.63). When maintaining the same model quality (≤2% accuracy loss), RESA reduces KV budget by up to 33.2% (with PSA) and 28.7% (with Ada-KV), and improves attention throughput by 1.23× and 1.16× respectively, while adding less than 3.13% extra decoding latency per step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RESA is a training-free plug-in that enhances any existing sparse attention method rather than replacing it, so all comparisons are of the form 'SA method' vs. 'SA method + RESA.' Against Quest (Tang et al., ICML 2024), RESA yields the largest absolute gains on multi-key retrieval tasks (MK3: +16 to +38 points across models) and moderate gains on multi-value (MV: +2.5 to +12.5 points), while retrieval tasks (Niah, MQ) see smaller improvements of 1–5 points. Against ArkVale (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2024), the pattern is similar with MK3 improving by +16 to +14 points and MV by +2 to +11 points. Against Ideal-TopK (the token-grained theoretical upper bound), gains are smallest (MK3: +2 to +21 points) since Ideal-TopK already captures most salient KVs. For KV budget reduction, RESA combined with PSA (Zhou et al., 2025) achieves up to 33.2% budget savings and 1.23× throughput gain, while with Ada-KV (Feng et al., 2024) it achieves 28.7% savings and 1.16× throughput gain, both within a 2% accuracy-loss constraint. Notably, RESA's gains scale with context length, being most pronounced at longer sequences where low-rank redundancy in attention logits is greatest.

## Relation to Prior Work

RESA carves a new conceptual space within sparse attention by introducing 'residual estimation' as a third perspective orthogonal to the two dominant lines of work (improving KV selection accuracy and dynamic budget allocation). It builds on the theoretical observation that attention logits are inherently low-rank (bounded by head dimension d), previously noted by Bhojanapalli et al. (2020) and Singhania et al. (2024), but departs from those works by avoiding online SVD entirely and instead using a rank-1 mean-based approximation (μ_Q K^T) that is precomputed once during prefilling. The method is explicitly complementary to existing SA algorithms (Quest, ArkVale, PSA, Ada-KV) rather than replacing them, and it draws a conceptual link to RoPE's low-frequency semantic role (Barbero et al., 2025) and to learnable attention biases in recent models like GPT-oss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RESA offers a training-free, plug-and-play enhancement that can be layered onto any existing sparse attention method with negligible overhead (<5% prefill, <3% decode), making it immediately practical for long-context inference on resource-constrained hardware. The finding that the prior distribution functions as an implicit attention bias for knowledge injection opens a path toward training-time optimization of sparse attention, potentially subsuming ad-hoc heuristics. Open questions include: the unexplained temporal structure (mean-reversion, periodicity) in the residual error term, the incomplete quantitative relationship between RoPE and the error, and whether the single-prior-at-prefill assumption degrades in highly dynamic or multi-turn dialogue settings where the 'typical query' shifts substantially.
