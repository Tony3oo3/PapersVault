## Problem

Prior model-free DEC methods (optimistic E2D) achieve regret bounds independent of model class size but rely on optimism-based exploration that is only proven for stochastic environments, leaving model-free learning in hybrid MDPs (stochastic transitions, adversarial rewards) with bandit feedback open. Additionally, the online estimation procedures in prior work yield suboptimal T-dependence (T^{3/4} on-policy, T^{5/6} off-policy, T^{2/3} with Bellman completeness) compared to the √T achievable by optimism-based approaches in Bellman-complete settings.

## Input / Output

The learner is provided with a model class M, a policy space Π, and a partition Φ of M×Π into infosets (each associated with a unique policy), which encodes the problem structure (bilinear rank, Bellman-eluder dimension, coverability, or Bellman completeness). In each of T rounds, the learner selects a policy and receives a trajectory observation (state-action-reward sequence of length H); in the hybrid setting, the transition kernel is fixed but the reward function changes adversarially each round. The output is a policy sequence with a regret bound that depends only on the value-function class size |Φ| and intrinsic dimension, not on |M|, and the paper provides explicit regret rates for bilinear, Bellman-eluder, and coverable MDP classes in both stochastic and hybrid settings.

## Method

The paper introduces Dig-DEC, a model-free complexity measure that replaces the optimism term in prior optimistic DEC with a dual information-gain divergence D combining (i) a KL term measuring how observations distinguish infosets and (ii) an estimation-error divergence (average or squared Bellman error) measuring model mismatch. The algorithm solves a minimax AIR objective each round and updates the infoset posterior via either a batching procedure (for average estimation error, improving the rate from √T to T^{1/3}) or a novel two-timescale bi-level learning procedure with a biased top-layer loss (for squared estimation error under Bellman completeness, improving the rate from T^{1/3} to O(1)). The removal of optimism is critical for the hybrid setting because it avoids the need for an explicit reward estimator, enabling the first model-free bandit-feedback regret bounds for hybrid bilinear and coverable MDPs. The analysis uses a Bregman-divergence-based first-order optimality argument that generalizes the prior constructive minimax theorem, connecting the regret decomposition to standard mirror-descent analysis and allowing flexible divergence choices.

## Results

In stochastic MDPs, Dig-DEC achieves T^(2/3) regret for on-policy bilinear/BE classes and T^(7/9) for off-policy, improving over optimistic E2D's T^(3/4) and T^(5/6) respectively. Under Bellman completeness with on-policy estimation, the method recovers the optimal sqrt(T) rate, matching optimism-based approaches for the first time via a DEC-based method. On a constructed 3-armed bandit instance, Dig-DEC achieves constant regret (1) while all baselines suffer Omega(sqrt(T)). For hybrid MDPs (stochastic transition, adversarial reward), the paper establishes the first model-free bandit-feedback regret bounds: T^(5/6) (on-policy) and T^(13/15) (off-policy) for bilinear classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against optimistic E2D [FGQ+23], Dig-DEC strictly improves the T-dependence in all stochastic settings: T^(2/3) vs T^(3/4) (on-policy bilinear/BE), T^(7/9) vs T^(5/6) (off-policy), sqrt(T) vs T^(2/3) (Bellman-complete on-policy), and constant vs sqrt(T) (toy 3-arm bandit). Against the earlier optimism-based methods [DKL+21], [JLM21], and [XFB+23], Dig-DEC ties on the on-policy T^(2/3) rate but improves the off-policy rate from T^(2/3) to T^(7/9), and matches their sqrt(T) for Bellman-complete MDPs without using optimism. For hybrid MDPs, [LWZ25] provides model-based sqrt(T)/T^(2/3) regret and a model-free T^(3/4)/T^(5/6) result that requires full-information reward feedback; the present work is the first to achieve model-free bandit-feedback bounds (T^(5/6)/T^(13/15)), albeit with a slightly worse T-rate than the model-based baseline. The key structural advantage is that removing optimism eliminates the need for explicit reward estimators, which is what enables the hybrid bandit setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decision-estimation coefficient (DEC) framework of Foster et al. [FKQR21, FGH23] and the optimistic model-free DEC of Foster et al. [FGQ+23], which achieved regret bounds scaling only with the value-function class but relied on an optimism principle for exploration. The key departure is the introduction of Dig-DEC, which replaces optimism with a dual information-gain mechanism (KL divergence plus an estimation-error divergence) embedded in the Algorithmic Information Ratio objective, generalizing the Φ-AIR framework of Liu et al. [LWZ25] and Xu & Zeevi [XZ23]. By removing optimism, the method naturally extends to hybrid MDPs with adversarial rewards and bandit feedback, a setting where [LWZ25]'s optimistic approach could not operate because it requires explicit reward estimators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves the main open problem from [LWZ25] by establishing the first model-free regret bounds for hybrid MDPs with bandit feedback under linear reward, and improves stochastic-setting regret from T^{3/4} to T^{2/3} (on-policy) and T^{5/6} to T^{7/9} (off-policy), while matching the √T rate of optimism-based methods in Bellman-complete MDPs. The conceptual finding that optimism is not fundamentally necessary for model-free exploration—information gain alone suffices—challenges a working assumption in the DEC literature and opens the door to adversarial extensions. Remaining open questions include relaxing the unique reward-to-value mapping assumption (Assumption 3) to cover hybrid low-rank MDPs with unknown features, and characterizing the fundamental limits of model-free learning.
