## Problem

Gaussian-based VAEs systematically underestimate tail probabilities and fail to capture rare but informative events in heavy-tailed data such as network degree distributions and word frequencies. While Student's t-based VAEs offer one heavy-tailed alternative, the KL divergence at the core of the ELBO does not induce a tractable geometric structure for power-law families, limiting the class of distributions that can be used. This paper introduces a new multivariate symmetric Pareto (symPareto) distribution and a corresponding VAE framework that operates under the γ-power divergence, a natural information-geometric alternative to KL for power-law families.

## Input / Output

The method accepts three types of input: noisy images (28×28 MNIST/SVHN, 32×32 CIFAR-10, 108×108 Omniglot, 180×180 CelebA with salt-and-pepper noise at probability 0.5), 19,962-dimensional sparse bag-of-words vectors from WikiText-2, and 2D joint in/out-degree counts from the Epinions social network. Outputs are reconstructed data in the same space plus an m-dimensional latent code drawn from a symPareto distribution. The t-decoder variant uses ℓ₂² (MSE) reconstruction, while the symPareto decoder variant uses ℓ₁ (MAE) reconstruction, which promotes robustness to extreme values.

## Method

ParetoVAE replaces the standard Gaussian prior and encoder with the proposed symPareto distribution (an ℓ₁-norm-based heavy-tailed analogue of the multivariate t), and formulates training as joint minimization of the γ-power divergence between the model manifold p_θ(x,z) and the data manifold q_φ(x,z) rather than ELBO maximization. The γ-power divergence admits closed-form expressions between power-law distributions, yielding a tractable γ-loss that decomposes into a reconstruction term (MSE or MAE depending on decoder choice), a γ-power divergence regularizer between encoder and an alternative prior, and an ℓ₁ penalty on the encoder mean. The symPareto distribution is sampled via a Laplace-Gamma mixture reparameterization (Z~Laplace, W~Gamma, T=(ν/W)Z), enabling gradient-based training. The decoder is flexible: a Student's t decoder (p,q)=(1,2) for sparse heavy-tailed reconstruction, or a symPareto decoder (p,q)=(1,1) for robust high-dimensional denoising. As ν→∞, the γ-loss provably converges to the LaplaceVAE objective, positioning ParetoVAE as a heavy-tailed generalization of LVAE.

## Results

On the denoising task (noise probability 0.5), ParetoVAE with symPareto decoder achieves the highest PSNR and SSIM across all five datasets: 24.19 dB / 0.950 on MNIST, 25.98 dB / 0.878 on SVHN, 20.55 dB / 0.723 on CIFAR-10, 20.78 dB / 0.903 on Omniglot, and 25.13 dB / 0.818 on CelebA. On the word-frequency reconstruction task (WikiText-2, 19,962-dim), ParetoVAE with t-decoder attains the highest tail overlap (0.717) and Jaccard (0.560), far exceeding all baselines (tail overlap ≤ 0.230). On the Epinions graph-degree reconstruction, ParetoVAE achieves the lowest overall SWD (0.044) and tail SWD (0.170), with a non-rejecting MMD p-value (0.221) where VAE (0.042) and AE (0.028) reject the null.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ParetoVAE outperforms all three baselines (Gaussian VAE, LVAE, and t³VAE) on every reported metric across all six datasets. Against the Gaussian VAE, the gains are largest: on MNIST denoising, PSNR improves from 18.52 to 24.19 dB (+5.7 dB), and on Omniglot the gap is dramatic (20.78 vs 11.92 dB), where VAE essentially fails to reconstruct. Against LVAE, ParetoVAE consistently leads by 2–9 dB in PSNR (e.g., 24.19 vs 20.25 on MNIST; 20.78 vs 11.95 on Omniglot), with the advantage widening on higher-dimensional or sparser data. Against t³VAE (the closest competitor, also using γ-power divergence but with Student's t instead of symPareto), ParetoVAE wins on all datasets; the margin is modest on MNIST (24.19 vs 22.99 dB) but becomes decisive on Omniglot (20.78 vs 11.92 dB) and CIFAR-10 (20.55 vs 19.58 dB). On the word-frequency task, ParetoVAE's tail overlap (0.717) is roughly 3× that of every baseline (≤ 0.230), and it is the only model whose MMD p-value fails to reject H₀ on both head and tail simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the t³-VAE framework (Kim et al., 2024), which replaced Gaussian VAE components with Student's t distributions and the KL divergence with the γ-power divergence, by introducing the symmetric Pareto (symPareto) distribution as an ℓ₁-norm-based counterpart to the ℓ₂-norm-based multivariate t. The symPareto is derived from Mardia's (1962) one-sided multivariate Pareto via reparameterization and symmetric extension, and its marginals connect to the generalized Pareto distribution from extreme-value theory. The joint minimization view of VAE (Han et al., 2020) provides the theoretical scaffold for replacing KL with γ-power divergence, yielding closed-form losses that avoid the intractable KL between symPareto distributions. In the ν→∞ limit, ParetoVAE reduces to the LaplaceVAE (Geadah et al., 2024), positioning it as a heavy-tailed generalization of that model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The symPareto distribution offers a principled way to simultaneously capture heavy tails and induce ℓ₁-sparsity in the latent space, addressing a gap left by t-based VAEs that only provide heavy tails without sparsity. The γ-power divergence framework makes the resulting objective tractable in closed form, which is practically important since the KL between symPareto distributions has no closed form. Limitations include testing only on relatively small-scale datasets (MNIST, SVHN, CIFAR-10, Omniglot, CelebA) with a fixed ν hyperparameter, and the striking Omniglot denoising result (where all baselines fail completely) is demonstrated under a single noise configuration. Open questions include whether the ℓ₁ sparsity benefit translates to representation learning at scale, how to effectively tune ν, and whether the symPareto decoder's advantage generalizes beyond the specific corruption types tested.
