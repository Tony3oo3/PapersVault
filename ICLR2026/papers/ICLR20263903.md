## Problem

Existing empirical and theoretical studies of neural collapse (NC) focus almost exclusively on single-task classification, leaving the geometric structure of features and classifiers in multi-task learning (MTL) largely uncharacterized. This paper asks: what does NC look like when a shared feature extractor feeds multiple task-specific linear classifiers, and how does task correlation reshape the resulting geometry?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263903/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a tuple of class labels, one per task. In the SSMTC setting (e.g., Multi-MNIST), the image contains multiple sub-images (digits in top-left and bottom-right corners) and the network outputs one label per sub-image via separate task-specific classifiers. In the MSMTC setting (e.g., CIFAR100-Split-5x20), each task operates on a distinct subset of classes from the same image. The shared feature extractor (ResNet18/34, VGG11/13) maps the image to a d-dimensional feature vector, which is then passed to T task-specific linear classifiers W^t ∈ R^{K_t × d}.

## Method

The paper identifies two distinct multi-task NC phenomena: SSMTC-NC, where each task-specific classifier converges to a Simplex ETF and the ETFs for different tasks are mutually orthogonal, with shared features converging to the scaled sum of task-specific classifier weight vectors; and MSMTC-NC, where each task independently exhibits standard single-task NC (self-duality, ETF convergence) because the optimization decouples across tasks. Under the Unconstrained Feature Model, the authors prove that any global minimizer of the regularized cross-entropy objective must satisfy these NC properties (Theorems 3.1 and 3.2). A key mechanistic insight is that shared features decompose into task-specific latent features (validated by feeding partial inputs to a trained network), and that task correlation breaks the orthogonality of SSMTC-NC3, replacing it with alignment: as the co-occurrence of label pairs increases, the cosine between corresponding task-specific classifier weights increases, promoting feature alignment across tasks.

## Results

The paper empirically validates that all SSMTC-NC metrics (SNC1–SNC5) and MSMTC-NC metrics (MNC1–MNC4) converge to zero during the terminal phase of training across ResNet18, ResNet34, VGG11, and VGG13 on Multi-MNIST, Multi-CIFAR10, CIFAR100-Cross, CIFAR100-Split-5x20, CelebA, ImageNet, and Tiny-ImageNet. Theoretically, Theorems 3.1 and 3.2 prove that any global minimizer of the UFM objective must satisfy the SSMTC-NC and MSMTC-NC properties respectively, under balanced data and the condition λ_H·λ_W < N/(4K). A key additional finding (Theorem 5.1) shows that under task-balanced data, the cosine between task-specific classifier vectors cos(w_{k₁}¹, w_{k₂}²) increases monotonically as the number of co-occurring samples n^{k₁,k₂} grows, revealing an inductive bias where task correlation reconfigures classifier geometry.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is a theoretical and empirical characterization study rather than a method comparison, so it does not benchmark a proposed method against competing baselines. Instead, it validates the NC phenomena across six different MTL task-weighting strategies (uniform, MGDA, Uncertainty Weight, PCgrad, DWA, FAMO, FairGrad) and confirms that SSMTC-NC and MSMTC-NC persist regardless of the weighting scheme used. The paper also generalizes its findings beyond the simplified equal-class-count, uniform-weight setting (Assumptions 5–6) to arbitrary class counts and non-uniform task weights, showing the NC structure holds with a weighted-sum form in NC4. The closest analogue to a 'comparison' is between the SSMTC and MSMTC settings themselves: in SSMTC, task-specific classifiers are mutually orthogonal and shared features equal the scaled sum of task-specific classifier vectors, whereas in MSMTC each task independently exhibits standard single-task NC (self-duality, Simplex ETF) with no cross-task orthogonality constraint, reflecting the decoupling of the optimization problem when data sources are distinct.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural Collapse (NC) framework from Papyan et al. (2020) and the Unconstrained Feature Model (UFM) analysis of Mixon et al. (2022) and Fang et al. (2021a) from single-task to multi-task learning settings. While NC has been studied in imbalanced learning, transfer learning, continual learning, and multi-label learning, the multi-task case with multiple classifiers sharing features was largely unexplored. The problem formulation for SSMTC and MSMTC follows Rosenbaum et al. (2018) and Sener & Koltun (2018), and the paper's key departure is providing the first geometric characterization (SSMTC-NC and MSMTC-NC) of how multiple task-specific classifiers and shared features organize in the terminal phase of training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean geometric decomposition showing that shared features in SSMTC are composed of task-specific latent features (orthogonal subspaces), and reveals that task correlation reshapes classifier geometry to promote feature alignment across tasks — an inductive bias previously uncharacterized. Limitations include reliance on the UFM assumption (features as free variables), balanced-data assumptions in the main theorems, and the task-correlation analysis being restricted to binary classification. Open questions include whether these geometric properties hold under the layer-peeled model with actual network dynamics, in non-balanced or non-convex regimes, and whether the orthogonality/alignment structure can be exploited for more efficient MTL architectures or transfer between tasks.
