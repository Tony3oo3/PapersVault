## Problem

Existing LLM unlearning methods rely on retain data and gradient-based fine-tuning, making them impractical for black-box or proprietary models where training data is unavailable and parameter updates are infeasible. Few approaches support continual unlearning under dynamic, repeated removal requests, and standard static evaluation metrics fail to capture performance stability over successive unlearning steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264927/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference time, DRAGON receives a user text query and checks it against a locally maintained unlearn store containing only paraphrased/synthetic prompt embeddings (never original completions) plus retrieved safety policy guidelines. The detection module outputs a unified confidence score; if it exceeds a threshold, a fine-tuned guard model generates a context-specific CoT instruction that is prepended to the query before it reaches the base LLM, which then produces a refusal or redirected response. If the score is below threshold, the original query passes through unmodified.

## Method

DRAGON operates as a two-stage pre-inference guard: (1) a dual-layer detection module combines a fine-tuned RoBERTa scoring model with similarity metrics (cosine similarity for sample unlearning; BERTScore and ROUGE-L for concept unlearning) to flag forget-worthy prompts using only paraphrased negative data, requiring no retain data and no retraining under continual unlearning; (2) upon detection, a lightweight guard model (Llama3.1-8B-Instruct fine-tuned on GPT-4o-generated CoT instruction pairs) produces a stepwise reasoning trace conditioned on the query and retrieved safety policies, which is prepended to the input to leverage the base LLM's instruction-following ability. The unlearn store is built by synthesizing four paraphrased candidates per forget prompt via Llama3.1-70B-Instruct and selecting the most semantically similar one via BERTScore rejection sampling, storing only embeddings to minimize leakage risk. The entire pipeline modifies no base-model weights, making it applicable to closed-source APIs and scalable across model sizes without additional training cost.

## Results

On WMDP hazardous knowledge unlearning, DRAGON reduces multiple-choice accuracy to near-random levels (26.2/23.5/27.9% on Biology/Chemistry/Cybersecurity for Llama3.1-8B, vs. 73.1/54.9/46.7% original) while achieving the highest Refusal Quality (RQ 0.921/0.795/0.875) and preserving MMLU accuracy at 68.0%. On TOFU privacy unlearning (Llama2-7B-Chat), DRAGON achieves the best Deviation Score of 21.4 (TOFU-1%), 23.1 (TOFU-5%), and 26.5 (TOFU-10%), with Knowledge Forgetting Ratio of 0.98–1.00 and Knowledge Retention Ratio of 0.87–0.90, while maintaining Model Utility at 0.6337 (identical to the original model). Under continual unlearning, DRAGON achieves the lowest Dynamic Deviation Score (0.2494 on Llama2-7B, 0.2853 on Phi-1.5B) and a Dynamic Utility Score of 1.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRAGON consistently outperforms all baselines on WMDP: it reduces ProbAcc to near-random (25–28%) versus RMU (28–67%), Filter-Prompting (35–46%), and ICUL+ (35–57%), while simultaneously achieving the highest RQ (0.60–1.30 vs. 0.33–0.50 for baselines). On TOFU, DRAGON achieves a Deviation Score of 21.4–26.5, substantially beating ICUL+ (49.9–58.1), Filter-Prompting (38.7–43.5), NPO-RT (46.4–69.9), and all fine-tuning baselines (GA, KL, GD, PO, DPO at 33–100). DRAGON also matches or exceeds ICUL+ on Knowledge Forgetting Ratio (0.98–1.00 vs. 0.95–0.97) while maintaining identical Model Utility (0.6337) to the original model. Under continual unlearning, DRAGON's Dynamic Deviation Score (0.2494) is 39% lower than the next-best Filter-Prompting (0.4073) and 53% lower than ICUL+ (0.5263), with a perfect Dynamic Utility Score of 1.0. The only setting where DRAGON does not strictly dominate is MMLU RQ on Mixtral (0.349 vs. 0.393 original), a minor trade-off for the substantial WMDP gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRAGON builds most directly on in-context unlearning (ICUL, Pawelczyk et al. 2023) and training-free guardrail approaches (Thaker et al. 2024; Liu et al. 2025a), but departs from them in two key ways: it eliminates the need for retain data by training its detection module solely on paraphrased negative prompts, and it replaces static refusal templates with dynamically generated chain-of-thought instructions from a lightweight fine-tuned guard model. The CoT-based safety reasoning draws on deliberative prompting (Guan et al. 2024) and CoT prompting (Wei et al. 2022), repurposing them from general safety alignment to targeted unlearning intervention. Unlike fine-tuning-based unlearning (GA, KL, DPO, NPO-RT), DRAGON operates entirely at inference time without modifying base model weights, positioning it as a practical complement rather than replacement for gradient-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling unlearning for black-box, proprietary LLMs (GPT-4o, Claude) where fine-tuning is infeasible and retain data is unavailable—addressing a gap that most prior methods cannot fill. The introduction of Refusal Quality, Dynamic Deviation Score, and Dynamic Utility Score provides the field with tools to evaluate unlearning stability over time, a dimension previously overlooked. Open questions include how the detection module degrades under large-scale adversarial paraphrasing, whether the unlearn store grows unboundedly under sustained continual unloading, and whether the 8B guard model's CoT instructions remain effective as base models evolve. The approach also raises governance concerns: who controls the unlearn store, and how are false-positive refusals audited?
