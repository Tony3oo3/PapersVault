## Problem

Existing diffusion-based super-resolution methods face a fundamental misalignment: T2I-oriented models require many inference steps and low-dimensional (4-channel) latent spaces, while SR demands fewer steps and higher-dimensional latents to preserve fine details. One-step distilled models like OSEDiff and InvSR inherit the 4-channel VAE bottleneck, causing irreversible detail loss, while 16-channel VAE models (e.g., FLUX at 12B) are prohibitively large for efficient deployment. The paper identifies this divergent-task-objective dilemma as the root cause of the speed–fidelity trade-off in diffusion-based SR.

## Input / Output

GenDR takes a single low-resolution RGB image with arbitrary real-world degradations (blur, compression, noise) and outputs a high-resolution restored image in exactly one forward pass through a UNet. The inference pipeline is stripped to only a VAE decoder and UNet—no scheduler, no text encoder, no conditioning module—using pre-stored fixed prompt embeddings and a fixed timestep (α̅_t = β̅_t = 0.5). At 512×512 on an A100, the full pipeline runs in 77 ms with 933 M parameters; at 1024×1024 it reaches ~262 ms (fp16) or ~157 ms (int8 on L20), enabling real-time server-side deployment at 1080p.

## Method

GenDR is built on a novel SD2.1-VAE16 base (0.9 B UNet + 16-channel VAE) trained with REPA (DINOv2 representation alignment) to expand latent capacity without enlarging the UNet. For one-step distillation, the paper proposes Consistent Score Identity Distillation (CiD), which modifies SiD by (i) fine-tuning the real score network on HR latents to align its prior with the SR target manifold, (ii) applying classifier-free guidance with quality-related prompts, and (iii) replacing the fluctuating generated latent z_g with the ground-truth HR latent z_h as an identity transformation to stabilize training. CiD is further extended to CiDA by adding adversarial discriminator heads on UNet features and a REPA regularization term, all implemented with LoRA (rank 64) on the score networks and a shared base UNet to keep memory comparable to vanilla VSD. The final model is trained in two stages—first with L1 + perceptual loss for 20 M images, then with the full CiDA objective for another 20 M images—yielding a single-step generator that outperforms multi-step diffusion SR on both no-reference quality metrics and human preference.

## Results

On ImageNet-Test, GenDR-1 achieves the highest perceptual quality scores among all compared methods: LIQE 4.81, MUSIQ 74.68, and Q-Align 4.45, while running in just 77 ms (fastest among all diffusion-based SR methods). On RealSR, GenDR attains LIQE 4.19, MUSIQ 68.36, and Q-Align 4.45 with 933M parameters. GenDR outperforms the closest one-step competitor OSEDiff by +0.24 LIQE, +2.94 MUSIQ, and +0.15 Q-Align on ImageNet-Test, while being 26 ms faster. Compared to the multi-step DreamClear, GenDR delivers an 89.5× speedup (77 ms vs. 6892 ms) with half the parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenDR dominates all one-step diffusion baselines (SinSR, OSEDiff, InvSR) on perceptual quality metrics: it leads OSEDiff by +0.24 LIQE and +2.94 MUSIQ, and InvSR by +0.25 LIQE and +2.30 MUSIQ on ImageNet-Test, while also being the fastest (77 ms vs. 103/115 ms). Against multi-step diffusion methods, GenDR surpasses DiffBIR-50 by +0.17 LIQE and +1.64 MUSIQ, SeeSR-50 by +0.27 LIQE and +2.43 MUSIQ, and DreamClear-50 by +0.38 LIQE and +4.60 MUSIQ, all with 80–90× lower inference latency. On full-reference metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), GAN-based methods Real-ESRGAN and Real-HATGAN retain an advantage (e.g., Real-HATGAN achieves 27.15 PSNR vs. GenDR's 24.14), reflecting the inherent fidelity-perceptual trade-off of generative SR. In user studies and MLLM evaluations (Q-Align, DeQA, DepictQA arena), GenDR is preferred over all diffusion-based competitors, receiving ~78% pairwise votes in the DepictQA arena. The 16-channel VAE ablation confirms that GenDR's advantage over OSEDiff (same SD2.1 UNet, 4-channel VAE) stems from both the larger latent space (+0.05 Q-Align) and the CiDA distillation strategy (+0.08 Q-Align total).

## Relation to Prior Work

GenDR sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) diffusion-based SR methods (StableSR, DiffBIR, SeeSR, DreamClear) that use multi-step generation with control modules, (2) one-step distillation approaches (OSEDiff, SinSR, InvSR) that compress inference via score distillation, and (3) the score identity distillation (SiD) framework from Zhou et al. (2024b). The key conceptual departure is identifying a fundamental objective mismatch between T2I (needing more steps, lower-dim latent) and SR (needing fewer steps, higher-dim latent), and addressing both simultaneously through a 16-channel VAE base model (SD2.1-VAE16) and a task-adapted distillation loss (CiD/CiDA) that injects SR-specific priors into the score identity framework. The simplified pipeline (removing text encoder, scheduler, using fixed embeddings) builds on the observation that SR does not require the full T2I conditioning machinery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 16-channel VAE substantially improves SR fidelity even with a small 0.9B UNet—while degrading T2I quality (FID 27.89 vs. 13.45)—challenges the field's default assumption that 4-channel VAEs are universally preferable for compact models, and could influence how future restoration-oriented diffusion models are architected. Practically, GenDR's 77ms inference and TensorRT deployment path (real-time on A10/L20) make high-quality generative SR viable for production use. Open questions include whether CiDA extends to DiT-based backbones (the authors note GPU memory constraints prevent this), whether larger latent channels (beyond 16) yield further gains, and whether the T2I quality degradation of SD2.1-VAE16 can be mitigated without sacrificing SR performance.
