## Problem

Existing bundle construction methods rely on sequential (autoregressive) paradigms that are fundamentally mismatched to the unordered nature of bundles, while non-sequential alternatives still face two dimensionality curses: the combinatorial search space grows exponentially with both bundle length and item catalog size. The paper addresses how to model higher-order intra-bundle relations without committing to a fixed decoding order, and how to learn discriminative item representations that avoid direct search over catalogs of tens of thousands to millions of items.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partial bundle (a subset of items from the catalog) serialized with special boundary tokens (<bos>, <boi>, <eos>) and RVQ code tokens, and produces the complementary item set to complete the bundle. Items are represented as L-level discrete code tuples (L=4, codebook size C=128 per level) rather than raw item IDs, and the model operates on fixed-length bundles truncated to a target length k. At inference, a candidate shortlist of size ρ|b_y| (default ρ=100) constrains the search space, and token-validity filters ensure generated code tuples map to legal catalog items.

## Method

DDBC combines a residual vector quantization (RVQ) tokenizer with a masked discrete diffusion model (MDLM-style) operating over code tokens. RVQ compresses continuous item embeddings into L-level discrete codes from a globally shared codebook, reducing the effective vocabulary from N items to ∏C_ℓ codes and providing denser supervision and coarse-to-fine semantic granularity. The diffusion backbone uses an absorbing-mask forward process that randomly masks code tokens, and a lightweight bidirectional Transformer (DDiT, 6 blocks, hidden size 64) learns the reverse denoising dynamics via a weighted masked-token cross-entropy objective. Crucially, the model predicts all masked positions in parallel without a fixed decoding order, and observed items remain clamped throughout generation, enabling order-agnostic completion. Token-validity constraints restrict each position's prediction to its level-specific codebook, guaranteeing that generated tuples decode to valid catalog items.

## Results

On the Spotify playlist-continuation datasets, DDBC achieves F1=0.282, Jaccard=0.176, OAS=0.660 (k=30); F1=0.285, Jaccard=0.178, OAS=0.662 (k=60); and F1=0.287, Jaccard=0.177, OAS=0.684 (k=90), yielding relative improvements of 84–133% in F1 and 96–153% in Jaccard over the strongest baseline. The performance gap widens monotonically with bundle length, confirming that the non-sequential masked-diffusion formulation better captures higher-order intra-bundle relations. On the shorter POG outfit-completion dataset (k=4), DDBC (F1=0.139, Jaccard=0.098, OAS=0.526) is competitive but trails TIGER (F1=0.213, Jaccard=0.157, OAS=0.546), which the authors attribute to the task reducing to near next-item prediction at such short lengths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDBC decisively outperforms all six baselines on the three Spotify datasets (k=30/60/90) across F1, Jaccard, and OAS. The strongest Spotify baselines are BundleNAT (non-sequential, one-shot set prediction) and TIGER (autoregressive generative retrieval with RVQ); DDBC surpasses BundleNAT by 84–133% in F1 and 96–153% in Jaccard, with the gap widening as bundle length grows from 30 to 90 items. On POG₄ (fashion outfits, only 4 items per bundle), DDBC loses to TIGER on all three metrics (F1 0.139 vs. 0.213; Jaccard 0.098 vs. 0.157; OAS 0.526 vs. 0.546), which the authors explain by noting that predicting just 2 items reduces the task to a near next-item prediction scenario where autoregressive decoding has a structural advantage. Sequential baselines (Bi-LSTM, SASRec) and the LLM-based BundleMLLM are consistently the weakest, with BundleMLLM trailing all other methods on every dataset. The ablation in Table 4 further confirms that removing RVQ collapses F1 from 0.282 to 0.021, underscoring that the discrete codebook compression is the critical enabler for the diffusion backbone to operate over a 254K-item catalog.

## Relation to Prior Work

DDBC sits at the intersection of two recent lines: masked discrete diffusion (MDLM-style absorbing-mask corruption from Sahoo et al., 2024) and generative retrieval with residual vector quantization (TIGER, Rajput et al., 2023). The key conceptual departure is from the sequential construction paradigm that dominates bundle construction (SASRec, Bi-LSTM, TIGER's autoregressive decoding) and from prior non-sequential methods (BundleNAT, Tomasi et al.) that either rely on fixed templates or lack explicit intra-bundle interaction during generation. By replacing the autoregressive backbone with a bidirectional denoiser operating over RVQ code tokens, the paper retains the hierarchical semantic-ID idea from generative retrieval while eliminating the fixed decoding order, and it is the first to apply discrete diffusion specifically to bundle construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The >100% relative gains on long bundles (k=60, k=90) demonstrate that the sequential paradigm is not merely suboptimal but fundamentally mismatched to unordered set construction, and that operating in a quantized discrete space is essential for scaling to large catalogs (the RVQ ablation shows F1 collapsing from 0.282 to 0.021 without it). Open questions remain around variable-length generation (currently fixed-length protocol), personalization (mediated only by frozen encoders), and evaluation under open-world settings where multiple valid completions exist. The technique of combining masked diffusion with RVQ tokenization is potentially transferable to other set-construction or combinatorial generation tasks beyond bundling.
