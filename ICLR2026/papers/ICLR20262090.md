## Problem

Reconstructing colorful 3D scenes from monochromatic inputs remains challenging because existing approaches either colorize each view independently with 2D models (causing severe cross-view color shifts) or enforce consistency by averaging multi-view color predictions (diluting chromatic richness and eliminating user controllability). Moreover, controllable colorization of dynamic 3D scenes with both spatial and temporal consistency has been entirely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262090/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts monochromatic multi-view images (static) or monochromatic video frames (dynamic) at resolutions up to 960×540, plus an optional user control signal (language prompt, reference image, or automatic mode). Only a single key view is colorized by an arbitrary off-the-shelf model; the personalized colorizer then propagates that chromatic information to all remaining views and frames. The output is a Lab-color-space 3D or 4D Gaussian representation that renders colorized novel views and video frames with decoupled luminance and chrominance channels.

## Method

Color3D recasts 3D colorization as a single-image propagation problem: it selects the most informative key view via CLIP feature entropy, colorizes it with any off-the-shelf model, then fine-tunes a personalized colorizer (frozen DDColor ConvNeXt encoder + learnable adapters + lightweight CNN decoder) on generatively and traditionally augmented samples to learn a scene-specific one-to-one color mapping. A single-view augmentation scheme combines Stable Diffusion outpainting, Stable Video Diffusion image-to-video, and Stable Virtual Camera novel-view synthesis with rotation, flip, grid shuffle, and elastic transforms to expand the sample space while preserving chromatic coherence. In the reconstruction stage, a Lab Gaussian representation reformulates spherical harmonic coefficients into separate L, a, b channels, enabling independent optimization of known luminance (with an edge loss for structural fidelity) and predicted chrominance, preceded by a warm-up phase that allocates all SH sets to the L channel before switching to full Lab optimization.

## Results

On the Mip-NeRF 360 static benchmark (automatic colorization), Color3D achieves FID 39.03, Colorful 33.36, ME 0.082, and TC 0.016, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline 3DGS+ImageColorizer (FID 62.32, ME 0.135) and ColorNeRF (FID 87.42, ME 0.093). On the DyNeRF dynamic benchmark (automatic), Color3D reaches FID 37.28, Colorful 32.75, ME 0.062, and TC 0.009, improving ME by 0.018 over 4DGS+ImageColorizer (0.080) and TC by 0.005 over 4DGS+VideoColorizer (0.014). Across all five datasets and three colorization paradigms (language-guided, automatic, reference-based), Color3D consistently achieves the lowest FID, ME, and TC while maintaining the highest Colorful score, demonstrating superior rendering quality, cross-view consistency, and chromatic richness simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Color3D outperforms ColorNeRF (Cheng et al., AAAI 2024) across all three colorization tasks on every static dataset: on Mip-NeRF 360 automatic, Color3D reduces FID from 87.42 to 39.03 and ME from 0.093 to 0.082, while also improving Colorful from 18.90 to 33.36, showing that ColorNeRF's averaging strategy sacrifices vividness. Against the naive 3DGS+ImageColorizer baseline, Color3D cuts ME by roughly 40% (0.135→0.082 on Mip-NeRF 360) and FID by 37% (62.32→39.03), confirming that per-image colorization introduces severe cross-view inconsistency that the personalized colorizer resolves. For dynamic scenes, Color3D beats 4DGS+ImageColorizer on FID (37.28 vs. 39.17), Colorful (32.75 vs. 29.45), ME (0.062 vs. 0.080), and TC (0.009 vs. 0.018) on DyNeRF, with similar margins on HyperNeRF. GaussianEditor (Chen et al., CVPR 2024) and Ref-NPR (Zhang et al., CVPR 2023) are only evaluated on language-guided and reference-based tasks respectively; Color3D dominates both on FID (68.23 vs. 123.71 for GaussianEditor; 48.62 vs. 146.32 for Ref-NPR on Mip-NeRF 360) and consistency metrics. In the appendix comparison with ChromaDistill (Dhiman et al., 2023), Color3D achieves lower short-term consistency error (e.g., 0.008 vs. 0.019 on Cake) and lower long-term consistency error (0.010 vs. 0.033 on Cake), further validating the personalized colorizer's advantage over knowledge-distillation-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and its dynamic extension 4DGS as the underlying 3D representation, reformulating their spherical harmonic coefficients from RGB to the CIE Lab color space to decouple luminance from chrominance during optimization. The personalized colorizer's encoder is derived from DDColor, a pre-trained image colorization model, with frozen ConvNeXt blocks and lightweight adapters for per-scene fine-tuning. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior 3D colorization methods (ColorNeRF, ChromaDistill) that enforce multi-view consistency by averaging inconsistent 2D colorization predictions — a strategy that dilutes color richness and sacrifices controllability — by instead recasting the problem as single-view colorization followed by deterministic color propagation through a scene-specific colorizer. It also unifies static and dynamic 3D colorization under one framework, whereas prior work addressed only static scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Color3D provides the first unified, user-controllable framework for colorizing both static and dynamic 3D scenes, filling a gap where dynamic 3D colorization was entirely unaddressed. The 'colorize one key view, propagate via personalized colorizer' paradigm is practically attractive (only ~8 minutes of additional tuning) and elegantly sidesteps the fundamental one-to-many ambiguity of general-purpose colorizers. Limitations include a tendency to assign plausible rather than novel colors for highly out-of-domain views, and the per-scene optimization requirement (though an all-in-one routing scheme reduces 140 scenes to 55 minutes). Open directions include integrating generative priors for unseen content, extending the personalization paradigm to other scene attributes (illumination, white balance, style transfer), and closing the gap between the feed-forward generalizable colorizer and per-scene optimization.
