## Problem

Traditional RLHF compresses rich natural language feedback into a single scalar reward, discarding token-level information about what was correct or incorrect and where in the output. Inference-time methods like Reflexion and ReAct preserve feedback in language form but leave model parameters frozen, so corrections are ephemeral and must be rediscovered each time. The paper asks whether free-form textual critiques can be converted into differentiable, token-level training signals that directly update the policy.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a text prompt x and a generated response y = (y₁,…,y_T). The reward model (a fine-tuned Llama-3.1-8B) autoregressively produces a single sequence z = [c; A(y)] containing both a free-form natural language critique c and a JSON map A(y) labeling 0–2 positive and 0–2 negative spans as exact character-level quotes from y. These spans are mapped to token indices via the policy model's tokenizer, yielding a discrete pseudo-reward vector δ ∈ {−1, 0, +1}^T (only ~30% of tokens receive non-zero values). The final output is updated policy parameters θ after PPO optimization with token-level GAE advantages.

## Method

TEXT2GRAD introduces the NL-Gradient, which transforms textual critiques into token-level gradient signals: ∇_NL(c→y) = Σ_t δ_t ∇_θ log π_θ(y_t | x, y_<t), where δ_t encodes the critique's local intensity at each token. The framework has three stages: (1) a dual-feedback annotation pipeline using GPT-4o with chain-of-thought prompting to jointly produce critiques and span-level labels, enforcing that every span is explicitly justified by prior reasoning; (2) a unified generative reward model trained via teacher forcing to output both the critique text and structured span JSON in one autoregressive pass, replacing scalar reward prediction with text generation; (3) a modified PPO loop where token-level pseudo-rewards δ_t (combined with per-token KL penalties) feed into GAE for dense advantage estimation, replacing the single end-of-sequence scalar reward. A theoretical analysis shows that token-level rewards amplify early signal differences by a factor of (γλ)^{−(T−k)} ≈ 2.8× for a 20-token gap, yielding sharper credit assignment than end-of-sequence supervision.

## Results

On SLF5K summarization, TEXT2GRAD achieves ROUGE-L 0.291, BLEU 0.094, and BERTScore 0.902, surpassing PPO by +6.7 ROUGE-L and +25.3% BLEU. On KodCode code generation, TEXT2GRAD reaches 66.1% average pass@1 (HumanEval 67.7%, MBPP 73.3%), outperforming PPO (62.5%) by +3.6 points on average. On UltraFeedback QA, TEXT2GRAD scores 34.7 on AlpacaEval 2.0, 84.4 on ARC-Challenge, and 7.58 on MT-Bench, exceeding PPO by 2.3, 1.7, and 0.15 points respectively. Cross-model evaluation on Mistral-7B confirms consistent gains across all three task domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEXT2GRAD consistently outperforms all five baselines across all three task domains. Against PPO (scalar-reward RL), it gains +6.7 ROUGE-L on SLF5K, +3.6 average pass@1 on KodCode (including +5.8 on MBPP+), and +2.3 on AlpacaEval for QA. Against DPO, the margins are +6.7 ROUGE-L (SLF5K), +5.1 average pass@1 (KodCode), and +2.1 AlpacaEval (QA). Against PRM-PPO, which offers finer scalar credit assignment, TEXT2GRAD still leads by +3.7 ROUGE-L, +5.8 average pass@1, and +1.7 ARC-Challenge. Against SFT+Reflection (inference-time self-correction without parameter updates), TEXT2GRAD wins by +3.3 ROUGE-L on SLF5K, confirming that internalizing feedback via gradients is more effective than ephemeral prompt-based correction. Against ILF (imitation learning from language feedback), TEXT2GRAD achieves +3.2 ROUGE-L on SLF5K and +3.8 average pass@1 on KodCode, demonstrating that direct gradient-based optimization from textual critiques surpasses sequence-level imitation targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

TEXT2GRAD sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: scalar-reward RLHF (PPO, DPO, PRM-PPO) and inference-time natural language feedback (ReAct, Reflexion, ILF). It departs from both by introducing a training-time mechanism that converts free-form textual critiques into span-level pseudo-rewards and back-propagates them through a modified PPO objective, drawing on generative reward modeling (Mahan et al., 2024) and text-based gradient ideas (Yuksekgonul et al., 2024). The key conceptual leap is treating natural language not as a scalar proxy or a prompt-time correction, but as a differentiable supervision signal that localizes credit assignment to specific token spans.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that natural language feedback—abundant in real-world settings like code reviews and user critiques—can serve as a direct, interpretable training signal rather than merely an explanation or inference-time nudge. The consistent gains across summarization, code generation, and QA with a single 8B model, plus cross-model transfer to Mistral-7B, suggest the approach is task-agnostic and practically deployable. Key limitations include dependence on reward-model quality (82–94% human alignment), a 9–11% per-step computational overhead, and the open question of how well span-level feedback generalizes to highly open-ended tasks where 'correct' spans are harder to define. Future work on improving reward-model precision and reducing annotation cost would strengthen the approach for production-scale alignment.
