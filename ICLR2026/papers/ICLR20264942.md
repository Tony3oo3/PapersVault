## Problem

The 0-1 loss linear classification problem—finding the hyperplane that minimizes the number of misclassified points—is NP-hard in the data dimension, and no efficient, rigorously proven standalone algorithm existed for obtaining the exact globally optimal solution. Prior approaches either relied on loss surrogates (hinge, logistic) that cannot guarantee exactness, or on general-purpose MIP solvers and branch-and-bound methods lacking formal correctness proofs. This paper closes that gap by providing the first provably correct algorithm with worst-case polynomial-in-N runtime.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes N labeled data points in R^D (binary labels ±1) assumed to be in general position, along with an optional polynomial degree K for the hypersurface extension. It outputs the optimal hyperplane normal vector w* ∈ R^(D+1) and the corresponding optimal 0-1 loss value. For the polynomial case, data is first mapped via the K-tuple Veronese embedding into R^G where G = C(D+K, D) − 1, so the effective dimensionality grows combinatorially with both D and K. The algorithm is fully standalone, requiring only matrix operations and no external solvers.

## Method

The core insight is a point-hyperplane duality: each D-combination of data points defines a hyperplane, and Theorem 3 proves the globally optimal 0-1 loss solution is always attained by one of these hyperplanes (or its negation). Theorem 5 (symmetry fusion) further shows the 0-1 loss for the negative orientation is simply N − l − D, halving the evaluation work. The ICE algorithm enumerates all D-combinations using an incremental combination generator (He & Little, 2025) in O(N^(D+1)) time, evaluating the 0-1 loss at each vertex of the dual hyperplane arrangement. Correctness is established via tools from hyperplane arrangement theory and oriented matroids, connecting Cover's counting theorem, Murthy et al.'s cell enumeration, and Nguyen & Sanner's prioritized combinatorial search into a unified framework. The polynomial hypersurface extension applies the K-tuple Veronese embedding to reduce the problem to linear classification in the embedded space, yielding O(N^(G+1)) complexity.

## Results

On six UCI binary classification datasets (HA, CA, CR, VP, BT, SP), the ICE algorithm consistently achieves the highest training accuracy, outperforming SVM, logistic regression, and LDA on every dataset. Gains range from a modest 0.41 pp on SP (94.46% vs. 94.05%) to a substantial 4.41 pp on CA (80.6% vs. 77.2% SVM) and 4.41 pp on BT (78.69% vs. 74.50% SVM). In the runtime comparison, ICE completes N=150, D=3 in 1.2 seconds worst-case, whereas the Nguyen & Sanner BnB algorithm requires approximately 10^10 seconds (~317 years), confirming ICE's polynomial O(N^{D+1}) complexity versus BnB's exponential worst case.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SVM (Cortes & Vapnik, 1995), ICE achieves higher training accuracy on all six primary datasets, with the largest margin on CA (+3.4 pp) and BT (+4.19 pp), and the smallest on VP (+0.42 pp) and SP (+0.41 pp). Against logistic regression (Cox, 1958), ICE leads on every dataset, with gaps of 2.4–7.0 pp (e.g., 80.6% vs. 73.6% on CA). Against the Nguyen & Sanner (2013) branch-and-bound algorithm, ICE is not compared on accuracy (both are exact) but on runtime: for N=150, D=3, ICE finishes in 1.2 seconds while BnB requires ~10^10 seconds, a difference of roughly nine orders of magnitude, confirming ICE's polynomial O(N^{D+1}) worst-case versus BnB's exponential behavior. In out-of-sample 5-fold cross-validation (Table 2), ICE also achieves the best or near-best test accuracy on most datasets, refuting the concern that exact 0-1 loss solutions overfit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper resolves a long-standing open problem by providing the first rigorously proven, standalone algorithm for exact 0-1 loss linear classification. It builds directly on Nguyen & Sanner's (2013) prioritized combinatorial search (PCS) strategy—empirically observed but never formally proven—and supplies the missing theoretical justification via a new theorem grounded in hyperplane arrangement and oriented matroid theory. The algorithm's efficiency leverages the sequential combination generator from He & Little (2025), and the theoretical framework unifies three previously inconsistent combinatorial analyses (Cover's counting theorem, Murthy et al.'s hyperplane decision tree enumeration, and Nguyen & Sanner's D-combination search). The extension to polynomial hypersurface classification via K-tuple Veronese embedding generalizes the linear result to a broader family of decision boundaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a gap that has persisted since the NP-hardness of 0-1 loss linear classification was established: no efficient, provably correct standalone algorithm existed. The dramatic computational advantage over branch-and-bound (1.2 seconds vs. ~317 years for N=150, D=3) makes exact solutions practically accessible for small-dimensional problems. However, the exponential dependence on dimension D fundamentally limits applicability, and the coreset selection workaround for larger datasets sacrifices the exactness guarantee. The empirical finding that globally optimal solutions generalize at least as well as (and often better than) approximate solutions challenges the common assumption that exact optimization overfits, though this is consistent with VC-theoretic predictions rather than a fundamental surprise.
