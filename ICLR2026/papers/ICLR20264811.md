## Problem

Current LLM safety alignment methods either induce shallow refusal shortcuts (memorizing trigger patterns) or require intensive supervision to distill safety reasoning from teacher models and handcrafted safety specifications. Neither approach fully leverages the model's intrinsic safety self-awareness acquired during pretraining, and both tend to degrade general utility or fail to generalize to unseen jailbreaks. The paper asks whether pure reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards can elicit this latent safety awareness without any supervised safety reasoning data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264811/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (user query) paired with a binary safety label (harmful or benign) and produces a structured two-part output: an explicit safety reasoning trace enclosed in <safety_reasoning> tags, followed by a final answer in <answer> tags that is either a refusal (for harmful queries) or a helpful response (for benign queries). The refusal verifier checks the final answer against a set of predefined refusal phrases (e.g., 'Sorry, I can't comply'), and a format verifier uses regex to confirm the structured template is followed. Training data consists of ~3k harmful prompts (from SCoT/Circuit Breaker) and ~3k benign prompts (from Dolly and XSTest).

## Method

AlphaAlign is a pure RL framework using PPO with a dual-reward system that requires only binary safety labels and no supervised reasoning data. The verifiable safety reward (R_s) combines a format reward (r_f=0.1) for structured reasoning output with a refusal reward (r_a=0.9) that checks whether the final answer constitutes a refusal, rewarding correct refusals on harmful inputs and penalizing refusals on benign inputs. For benign queries, a normalized helpfulness reward (R_h) is added: a reward model (FsfairX-LLaMA3-RM-v0.1) scores each rollout, scores are z-normalized within the batch, and a max(·,0) threshold ensures non-refusal responses receive non-negative utility rewards while refusals receive zero. The key insight is that pretrained LLMs already encode safety self-awareness, and structured prompting plus verifiable RL signals can elicit proactive safety reasoning in fewer than 200 RL steps without injecting new safety knowledge. The framework is validated on Qwen2.5-3B/7B and Llama3.2-3B, showing it breaks the safety-utility trade-off and achieves deeper alignment (resistant to prefilling attacks) compared to SFT-based baselines.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, AlphaAlign achieves near-perfect safety: 0.0% ASR on StrongREJECT, AdvBench, and GCG, 0.30% on WildGuardTest, and 0.19% on PAIR, while maintaining 93.14% over-refusal accuracy on CoCoNot. On Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, AlphaAlign reduces WildGuardTest ASR from 31.6% (base) to 6.38% and PAIR ASR from 67.69% to 4.61%. Utility is preserved or improved: AlpacaEval increases by +6.7 to +10.0 points, GSM8K by +4.4 (3B) and +2.9 (7B), and GPQA by +0.9 to +4.2, while MMLU and BBH-CoT remain within ~2 points of the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlphaAlign outperforms both SCoT (Yang et al., 2025) and Circuit Breaker (Zou et al., 2023) across nearly all safety benchmarks. Against SCoT, AlphaAlign achieves substantially lower ASR on adaptive jailbreaks (PAIR: 4.61% vs 8.62% on 3B; GCG: 0.77% vs 9.61%) and dramatically reduces over-refusal (CoCoNot: 91.29% vs 74.93%), indicating SCoT's reasoning distillation leads to excessive refusals. Against Circuit Breaker, AlphaAlign achieves lower ASR on harmful content (StrongREJECT: 0.31% vs 3.51% on 3B) and adaptive attacks (PAIR: 4.61% vs 5.38%), while Circuit Breaker slightly edges out on over-refusal for Llama3.2-3B (93.12% vs 91.29%). Direct Refusal (SFT baseline) is weakest on adaptive jailbreaks (PAIR: 11.54% on 3B) and degrades over-refusal accuracy. On utility, AlphaAlign improves AlpacaEval by +6.7 to +10.0 points over the base model, whereas refusal-based baselines typically degrade response quality on benign prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two established lines of safety alignment: (1) refusal-based methods (SFT, RLHF, DPO, Circuit Breaker) that the authors argue produce shallow 'refusal shortcuts' rather than genuine safety understanding, and (2) reasoning-based alignment (SCoT, Deliberative Alignment) that requires intensive supervision from handcrafted safety policies or teacher-model distillation. AlphaAlign instead adapts the RLVR paradigm (popularized by DeepSeek-R1 for math reasoning) to safety, using only binary harmful/benign labels and a verifiable refusal verifier as the reward signal, combined with a normalized helpfulness reward to prevent utility degradation. The key conceptual departure is treating safety as a verifiable property amenable to pure RL optimization, rather than a behavior to be distilled via supervised examples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that pretrained LLMs already encode substantial safety awareness (Pass@32 reaches 96.3% with a reasoning template) that can be unlocked with fewer than 200 RL steps and only binary labels, offering a data-efficient alternative to supervised safety reasoning distillation. The prefill-attack resistance (1.8% ASR vs. 17.2% for SFT) provides concrete evidence of deeper alignment beyond surface-level refusal memorization. However, the method is limited to hard-refusal alignment, requires significantly more compute than SFT baselines (~37 vs. ~1 GPU-hours), and the helpfulness reward model introduces a dependency on preference data. Open questions include whether the framework extends to soft refusals, scales to larger models, and whether the verifiable reward design generalizes to multi-turn or agentic safety settings.
