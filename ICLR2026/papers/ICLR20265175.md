## Problem

Caching techniques applied to diffusion-based Multimodal Large Language Models (dMLLMs) to reduce inference latency introduce a severe side effect termed the Repeat Curse, where generated text exhibits catastrophic token repetition. The underlying mechanism behind this phenomenon remained unexplained due to the black-box nature of dMLLMs, and no targeted mitigation existed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265175/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates within the iterative denoising loop of dMLLMs: given an RGB image and a text instruction prompt, the model progressively unmasks a fully masked response sequence over N discrete decoding steps. CoTA is applied at inference time as a plug-and-play, training-free intervention on the attention matrices and confidence scores at each denoising step, requiring no architectural modification or retraining of the base dMLLM.

## Method

CoTA consists of two complementary components targeting the two root causes identified through information-flow analysis. First, Context Tokens Attention Enhancement (CTAE) applies a Gaussian distance-decay term element-wise to the attention matrix, strengthening attention to nearby context tokens and restoring the 'context tokens as anchors' information-flow pattern that caching disrupts. Second, Context Tokens Entropy-Guided Voting (CTEV) computes the accumulated normalized entropy of each candidate token's neighboring context tokens across deep layers (26–30) and adds this as a penalty to the confidence score used for remasking decisions, thereby suppressing the unmasking of tokens whose context tokens exhibit non-convergent entropy. The key insight is that in healthy dMLLM decoding, context tokens aggregate semantic information across layers and their entropy converges sharply in deeper layers; caching output tokens breaks this convergence, and CoTA counteracts both the attention disruption and the entropy non-convergence without any training.

## Results

On the primary repeat-curse evaluation (COCO VQA, 500 images), applying dLLM-Cache to LLaDA-V causes catastrophic repetition (ARR 14.3%, SRR 82.3% at max length 512). CoTA (CTAE+CTEV) reduces ARR to 1.2% (−96%) and SRR to 6.3% (−92%), nearly restoring baseline-level repetition (ARR 0.2%, SRR 6.9%). On general multimodal benchmarks with dLLM-Cache, CoTA recovers 11% Score on LLaVA-w (63.2→69.9) and 9% on MathVista (54.9→59.7) while cutting ARR by 80–81%, at only a modest throughput cost (TPS drops from 23.1 to 20.3 on LLaVA-w).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against dLLM-Cache (the primary caching baseline that triggers the repeat curse), CoTA reduces ARR from 14.3% to 1.2% and SRR from 82.3% to 6.3% on 512-length responses, and from 7.1% to 1.0% / 65.6% to 3.0% on 64-length responses. Against SlowFast, CoTA similarly reduces ARR from 7.4% to 1.4% and SRR from 69.2% to 5.6%, confirming generalizability across caching strategies. On the general-task side (Table 5), CoTA with dLLM-Cache outperforms the cached baseline by +11% Score on LLaVA-w (69.9 vs. 63.2) and +9% on MathVista (59.7 vs. 54.9), while also surpassing the uncached LLaDA-V on LLaVA-w (69.9 vs. 70.1, near-parity) and matching it on MathVista (59.7 vs. 59.7). Caching methods that do not cache output tokens—Prefix KV Cache, D³ToM, and Fast-dLLM—do not exhibit the repeat curse (ARR ≤ 1.2%, SRR ≤ 8%), so CoTA is not applicable to them; the paper confirms this by showing their SRR remains at baseline levels. On MMaDA (a second dMLLM), CoTA with dLLM-Cache reduces ARR from 4.4% to 0.6% (−86%) and SRR from 55.0% to 30.0% (−45%), demonstrating cross-model generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes information-flow analysis techniques (attention maps, layer-wise entropy) previously applied to autoregressive LLMs and MLLMs to study diffusion-based multimodal LLMs (dMLLMs) for the first time. The 'context tokens as anchors' finding draws a direct parallel to the attention-sink phenomenon in causal transformers, but the bidirectional attention in dMLLMs gives it a distinct mechanistic role. On the repetition-mitigation side, the paper departs from prior work on n-gram penalties, DUC, and model editing (all targeting AR models) by identifying a dMLLM-specific cause—caching-induced disruption of information flow—and proposing a training-free fix (CoTA) that operates on attention and entropy rather than token-level penalties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As dMLLMs gain traction, caching is essential for practical deployment, yet this paper shows that the most widely adopted caching strategies (dLLM-Cache, SlowFast) catastrophically degrade output quality through the Repeat Curse—a previously uncharacterized failure mode. The information-flow diagnosis (output-token caching, not prompt caching, is the culprit; entropy non-convergence in layers 26–30 signals repetition) gives practitioners a principled way to design safer caching schedules. Key limitations include evaluation on only two dMLLM architectures (LLaDA-V, MMaDA) and a handful of caching methods, leaving open whether the findings generalize to continuous-discrete hybrids, larger models, or future dLLM variants. The modest throughput cost of CoTA (~15–25% TPS reduction) also raises the question of whether the attention-intervention overhead can be further reduced at scale.
