## Problem

Existing sparse training methods for spiking neural networks cannot achieve ultra-high structural connection sparsity (above ~90%) without significant accuracy degradation, because gradient-based pruning strategies from ANNs do not transfer to SNNs due to the non-differentiable spiking activation function. This limits the energy efficiency gains achievable on neuromorphic hardware, where reducing synaptic operations is the primary path to lower power consumption.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263902/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes binary spike trains over T timesteps (T=4 or 8 depending on architecture), sourced either from event-based cameras (DVS-Gesture, N-MNIST, CIFAR10-DVS) or from direct encoding of static images (MNIST, CIFAR-10/100). It produces a class label at inference and, during training, a dynamically evolving sparse connectivity matrix that defines which synapses are active. The framework is architecture-agnostic, sparsifying all linear layers within spiking CNNs (up to 6 conv layers + 2 FC) and Spikformer (8 encoder layers, 512 hidden dim, 8 heads), with a uniform 99% sparsity target applied to all layers except the output.

## Method

CH-SNN is a four-stage dynamic sparse training framework. Stage 1 (SSCTI) initializes an ultra-sparse topology by computing Pearson's phi coefficient between input spike dimensions and retaining only the top-(1−S_s) most correlated connections. Stage 2 (SSWI) initializes weights with a variance formula that explicitly incorporates temporal activation sparsity, structural connection sparsity, and the LIF firing threshold, ensuring consistent signal variance across layers despite the sparse connectivity. Stage 3 prunes links using a hybrid Link Removal Score combining row- and column-normalized weight magnitudes, then permanently removes unilaterally or bilaterally disconnected neurons (chain removal) to improve information flow. Stage 4 regrows links using the CH3-L3 network automaton score—which sums over length-3 paths weighted by inverse external community degree—with probabilistic multinomial sampling (δ=0.5) to avoid epitopological local minima. The pruning ratio ζ decays cosine-annealingly to zero over training, and an early-stop mechanism halts topological evolution when the overlap between removed and regrown links exceeds 95%.

## Results

CH-SNN achieves a maximum structural connection sparsity of 97.75% on MNIST (2FC) while improving accuracy by 0.16% over the fully connected baseline (98.97% vs 98.81%). On the DVS-Gesture dataset with the S-TP hardware-friendly algorithm, CH-SNN reaches 98.84% sparsity with a 2.27% accuracy gain (91.29% vs 89.02%) and a 97.5× reduction in energy consumption (0.8 mJ vs 78 mJ). Across four datasets on the ANP-I neuromorphic processor, the sparse CH-SNN architecture delivers an average 55× energy reduction and a 10.77% decrease in average firing rate compared to the FC baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CH-SNN consistently achieves the highest structural sparsity among all compared methods: 97.75% vs. 77.36% (DPAP) and 45.86% (SD-SNN) on MNIST 2FC, and 94.73% vs. 75.00% (Grad R) and 61.10% (SD-SNN) on DVS-Gesture. On accuracy improvement over the FC baseline, CH-SNN outperforms Grad R on MNIST (+0.16% vs. −0.33%) and N-MNIST (+0.08% vs. −0.27%), and outperforms DPAP on CIFAR-10 (−0.14% vs. −0.71%) and N-MNIST (+0.08% vs. +0.06%). However, CH-SNN trails SD-SNN on CIFAR-100 (+3.16% vs. +3.27%) and DVS-Gesture (+0.38% vs. +1.14%), and trails Grad R on DVS-Gesture (+0.38% vs. +7.83%), though at substantially higher sparsity levels. Against Deep R on DVS-Gesture, CH-SNN achieves both higher sparsity (94.73% vs. 75.00%) and higher accuracy improvement (+0.38% vs. −2.89%). On the hardware evaluation (S-TP on ANP-I), CH-SNN at ~96% average sparsity matches or exceeds FC accuracy on all four datasets while reducing energy by 19.75×–97.5×.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the Cannistraci-Hebb (CH) network automaton framework—previously applied to ultra-sparse training of ANNs (CHT, CHTs)—into the spiking neural network domain, where the non-differentiable LIF activation and spike-based computation prevent direct use of gradient-based sparse training methods like RigL or Gradient Rewiring. It departs from existing sparse SNN approaches (SD-SNN, DPAP, Grad R) that plateau at 60–90% sparsity with accuracy degradation, by introducing two SNN-specific initialization mechanisms (SSCTI using Pearson's phi coefficient for topology, SSWI incorporating temporal activation sparsity and neuronal threshold for weights) that are absent from both ANN sparse training and prior SNN pruning work. The probabilistic link regrowth via CH3-L3 with multinomial sampling (δ=0.5) adapts the CHTs soft rule to the discrete, event-driven setting of SNNs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the demonstrated 97.5× energy reduction on the ANP-I neuromorphic processor, making ultra-sparse SNNs viable for edge AI where energy budgets are extremely tight. However, validation is limited to small-scale benchmarks (MNIST, CIFAR-10/100, N-MNIST, DVS-Gesture) and relatively shallow architectures; scaling to ImageNet-scale or deeper transformer-based SNNs remains untested. The robustness analysis reveals a notable asymmetry: CH-SNN is less robust than FC on converted (non-spiking) datasets but more robust on native event-based data, suggesting the method's benefits are most pronounced in its natural domain. Open questions include whether the CH3-L3 regrowth mechanism generalizes to very deep or very wide SNNs, and whether the 99% sparsity target can be pushed further without the accuracy gains observed here.
