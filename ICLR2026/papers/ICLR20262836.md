## Problem

First-last frame video generation (FLF2V) suffers from semantic degradation in intermediate frames: the deterministic semantics of boundary frames attenuate as they propagate inward because DiT self-attention assigns significant inter-frame weights only to adjacent frames, leaving mid-sequence frames with weak constraints. This causes scene distortion, subject deformation, and abrupt transitions that undermine temporal consistency, and existing FLF2V methods (fine-tuned from I2V models) do not adequately resolve this information decay.

## Input / Output

The method takes a first frame image, a last frame image, and a text prompt (auto-generated by Qwen from the two boundary frames) as input, and produces a coherent video sequence of approximately 5 seconds (121 frames) by default. It is plug-and-play and training-free, operating as a wrapper around existing I2V diffusion models (Wan2.1, Hunyuan Video) without any parameter updates. A binary mask of shape 1×N×h×w indicates which frames are given conditions versus frames to be generated, and CLIP image features from both boundary frames are injected via decoupled cross-attention into the DiT backbone.

## Method

AFB is a two-stage, training-free method. In the adaptive anchor frame selection stage, the first and last frames are swapped and a reverse-direction video is generated using a Qwen-generated reverse prompt; the frame with the worst local LPIPS-based quality score is identified at normalized position α, and the mirror-position frame (1−α) from the reverse candidate set is selected as the anchor, exploiting the empirically validated symmetry of continuity breakpoints between forward and reverse generation. In the anchor frame guided generation stage, the first, last, and anchor frames are concatenated with zero-padded frames along the time axis, encoded into latent space, and fed alongside the binary mask, CLIP conditioning features, and Qwen-generated forward text prompt into the diffusion model's iterative denoising loop. The key insight is that the anchor frame, being near the start of the reverse generation, retains strong semantic alignment with the boundary frames and effectively bridges the semantic gap at the position of maximal information decay in the forward pass.

## Results

On the 436-pair first-last frame dataset, Wan2.1+AFB achieves the best performance across all metrics: LPIPS 0.16, FVD 375.12, SSIM 0.97, PSNR 35.41, GPT-4o score 88.64, and Gemini 2.0 Flash score 89.35. Compared to the base Wan2.1-I2V model, AFB yields a 16.58% reduction in FVD (449.68→375.12) and a 10.21% increase in PSNR (32.13→35.41). The method also improves HunyuanVideo-I2V by 12.2% in FVD (496.32→435.71) and 3.4% in PSNR (31.48→32.54), confirming plug-and-play generalizability across architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AFB consistently outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest dedicated FLF2V model, Wan2.1-FLF2V, AFB reduces FVD by 9.3% (413.68→375.12) and increases PSNR by 6.7% (33.20→35.41), while also leading in LPIPS (0.16 vs 0.19), SSIM (0.97 vs 0.91), and both MLLM scores. Compared to the video frame interpolation baselines, AFB beats ViBiDSampler by 12.0% in FVD (426.15→375.12) and Generative Inbetweening by 17.3% in FVD (453.76→375.12). On the shorter-clip baselines FCVG and MoG (Appendix F, ~1–2 s clips), AFB still achieves the lowest FVD (375.12 vs 438.83 and 421.53) and highest PSNR (35.41 vs 33.62 and 18.41), though the comparison is less direct due to different video lengths. The plug-and-play nature is confirmed by the HunyuanVideo+AFB result, which improves over the base HunyuanVideo-I2V on all six metrics without any architectural modification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the first-last frame to video (FLF2V) task, which prior work (Make Pixels Dance, Wan2.1-FLF2V, Bytedance Jimeng) has addressed by fine-tuning or reusing I2V diffusion models with both boundary frames as conditions. It departs from bidirectional denoising approaches (ViBiDSampler, Generative Inbetweening) that fuse forward and reverse paths, instead proposing a training-free, plug-and-play strategy that inserts a single anchor frame at the empirically-identified point of maximal semantic decay. The method repurposes the reverse generation process (swapping first/last frames) not as a denoising path to be fused, but purely as a diagnostic tool to locate where information attenuation is worst, then feeds the resulting high-quality frame back into a standard forward I2V pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature makes AFB immediately applicable to any existing I2V model without retraining, which is practically significant given the cost of fine-tuning 14B+ parameter video models. The 16.58% FVD improvement over Wan2.1-I2V and the construction of a 436-pair FLF2V evaluation dataset address a real gap in the field. However, the method inherits all limitations of the underlying I2V model (non-rigid motion distortion, physically implausible dynamics), and the single-anchor strategy is shown to be suboptimal for longer videos (>5s), leaving multi-anchor extension and semantically unrelated boundary frames as open problems.
