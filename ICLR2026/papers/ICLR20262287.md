## Problem

Most protein design methods decouple sequence and structure generation, or tackle fully atomistic co-design but struggle with the mixed discrete-continuous nature of the problem and the sequence-dependent dimensionality of side chains. Existing all-atom generators either operate directly in data space (suffering from modeling accuracy and scalability limits) or use fully latent representations that fail to deliver competitive performance. The paper addresses the challenge of jointly generating full-atom protein structures and their corresponding sequences at scale, including the critical downstream task of atomistic motif scaffolding.

## Input / Output

In unconditional mode, the model takes random Gaussian noise as input and produces a joint protein sequence (20 amino acid types) and full-atom 3D structure in the Atom37 representation (an [L, 37, 3] coordinate tensor per residue). In conditional motif scaffolding mode, the input is the atomic structure of a predefined motif (either all atoms or only functional tip atoms after the last rotatable bond), optionally with or without specified sequence indices, and the output is a complete scaffold protein that accurately reproduces the motif. The model scales to proteins of up to 800 residues, a regime where most baselines collapse or exhaust memory.

## Method

La-Proteina introduces a partially latent representation where the α-carbon backbone is modeled explicitly while sequence identity and all non-α-carbon atom coordinates are compressed into a fixed 8-dimensional per-residue latent vector via a VAE. A two-stage training procedure first learns the VAE (encoder maps full proteins to latents; decoder reconstructs sequence and side-chain atoms from latents plus α-carbons), then trains a conditional flow matching denoiser to jointly generate α-carbon coordinates and latent variables from Gaussian noise. A critical design choice is the use of independent interpolation times t_x and t_z for the backbone and latent modalities, enabling different discretization schedules during SDE-based sampling (Euler-Maruyama) that generate the backbone faster than the latent details. All three networks (encoder, decoder, denoiser) share a transformer architecture with pair-biased attention (~130M and ~160M parameters respectively), omitting expensive triangular update layers for scalability, and are trained on up to 46 million AFDB structure-sequence pairs.

## Results

On unconditional all-atom protein generation (100–500 residues), La-Proteina achieves 68.4% all-atom co-designability and 93.8% ProteinMPNN-8 designability, roughly doubling the best baseline P(all-atom) (36.7% co-designability, 57.9% designability). The variant with triangular multiplicative layers (La-Proteina-tri) further improves to 75.0% all-atom co-designability and 100% pLDDT≥80. La-Proteina also leads on diversity (301 joint structure+sequence clusters vs. 165 for P(all-atom)) and generates co-designable, structurally valid proteins up to 800 residues, a regime where all all-atom baselines produce zero valid samples. On atomistic motif scaffolding, La-Proteina solves 21–25 of 26 benchmark tasks across indexed/unindexed and all-atom/tip-atom settings, versus only 4/26 for Protpardelle.

## Baselines & Comparisons

La-Proteina decisively outperforms all six all-atom baselines on the primary metric of all-atom co-designability: 68.4% (base) and 75.0% (tri) versus 36.7% for the strongest competitor P(all-atom) and 35.8% for Protpardelle-1c, representing roughly a 2× improvement. On designability (ProteinMPNN-8), La-Proteina reaches 93.8–95.4% compared to 57.9–62.0% for the best baselines, and on joint structure+sequence diversity it produces 301 clusters versus 165 for P(all-atom). The only metric where La-Proteina is slightly behind is novelty against PDB (0.75 vs. 0.72 for P(all-atom)), though it remains competitive. For long-protein generation (500–800 residues), La-Proteina is the only all-atom method that produces co-designable samples, while P(all-atom) runs out of GPU memory beyond 500 residues and all other all-atom baselines yield zero valid samples. On atomistic motif scaffolding (26 tasks), La-Proteina solves 21–25 tasks depending on the setup, vastly outperforming Protpardelle (4/26) and Protpardelle-1c, and uniquely handles the unindexed setting where motif residue positions are unknown.

## Relation to Prior Work

La-Proteina builds directly on the Proteina backbone generation framework (Geffner et al., 2025), reusing its transformer architecture, time-sampling distributions, and flow matching formulation, while introducing a partially latent representation that keeps α-carbon coordinates explicit and encodes sequence plus all side-chain atoms in a fixed-size per-residue latent variable via a VAE. This departs from both fully explicit all-atom generators (P(all-atom), Protpardelle, APM) that struggle with variable side-chain dimensionality and scalability, and fully latent approaches (PLAID, Fu et al.) that sacrifice the structural inductive biases of explicit backbone modeling. The core conceptual contribution is showing that this hybrid—explicit backbone + latent side-chain/sequence—outperforms both extremes, with ablations confirming that encoding α-carbons in latent space degrades performance significantly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

La-Proteina's ~2× improvement in all-atom co-designability over the best prior method (75% vs 36.7%) and its ability to generate valid 800-residue proteins where all baselines collapse represent a practical breakthrough for atomistic protein design, particularly for enzyme and binder engineering that require precise side-chain placement. The unindexed atomistic motif scaffolding capability (solving 21–25 of 26 tasks) opens a design space previously inaccessible to backbone-only methods. Key limitations include monomer-only scope (no protein complexes), reliance on AFDB training data, and the observation that the unindexed > indexed advantage for multi-segment motifs, while interesting, has a somewhat speculative explanation. Extending the partially latent framework to multi-chain complexes and integrating with test-time compute for binder design are natural next steps.
