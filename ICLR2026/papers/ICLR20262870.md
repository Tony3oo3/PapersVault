## Problem

Outcome-reward RL for LLM reasoning assigns credit only at the final-answer level, uniformly penalizing all steps in failed traces and reinforcing all steps in successful ones. This credit assignment failure suppresses correct intermediate steps in failed rollouts and reinforces spurious steps in successful ones, and on very hard problems where no correct rollout exists, advantages collapse to zero entirely. Existing remedies—process reward models and branched rollouts—are computationally expensive and difficult to optimize over long reasoning traces.

## Input / Output

The method takes a math problem prompt, an incorrect on-policy reasoning trace generated by the base model, and a reference solution (human-written or Gemini-generated) as inputs. The model produces a single-step corrective intervention—typically under 200 tokens—inserted at the position of the first critical error, replacing the erroneous step while leaving the preceding prefix intact. The final output is a patched model (after SFT on prefix+intervention pairs) that is then further trained with standard outcome-reward RL (GRPO), yielding a reasoning model that generalizes to unseen Olympiad-level problems.

## Method

InT exploits the asymmetry between verification and generation within the same model: the base model is prompted to perform a step-by-step 'diff' between its own incorrect trace and a reference solution, identify the first critical error, and propose a single-step intervention that redirects the trajectory toward the correct answer. The key training design is SFT on the concatenation of the on-policy prefix (up to the error) and the intervention, explicitly excluding the post-intervention suffix to preserve exploration space for downstream RL, and filtering to retain only interventions whose continuations yield at least one correct rollout out of 32. Because interventions are short relative to full rollouts (~200 vs ~7000 tokens), the SFT data remains highly on-policy, preserving low next-token entropy and avoiding the distribution distortion that plagues off-policy fine-tuning on reference solutions. After this SFT patching step, standard GRPO RL is run from the patched checkpoint, enabling the model to extract learning signal from previously zero-advantage problems. The entire procedure requires no branched rollouts, no explicit PRM training, and no larger teacher model.

## Results

InT (Intervention Training) achieves an average score of 33.72 across four challenging Olympiad-level math benchmarks, representing a ~59% relative improvement over the Qwen3-4B-Instruct base model (21.17) and a ~19% improvement over standard RL (28.26). On IMO-AnswerBench, InT attains 25.62% pass@1, more than doubling the base model's 11.68% and surpassing the larger gpt-oss-20b (23.36%) using only a 4B-parameter model. InT also achieves the highest training reward (28.83) and the lowest zero-advantage ratio, indicating it enables learning signal extraction from previously unsolvable problems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InT outperforms all baselines across every benchmark. Compared to standard RL (28.26 avg), InT gains +5.46 points, with the largest margin on IMO-AnswerBench (25.62 vs. 23.46) and Apex Shortlist (28.22 vs. 22.72). Against hint-guided RL (Qu et al., 2025a; 28.56 avg), InT leads by +5.16 points, particularly on IMO-AnswerBench (25.62 vs. 16.89) and Apex Shortlist (28.22 vs. 22.23), though hint-guided RL edges ahead on HMMT 2025 Nov (47.27 vs. 49.77 is actually InT winning). SFT on reference solutions followed by RL (20.76 avg) performs worse than the untuned base model, confirming that off-policy SFT distorts the model's distribution and hinders RL. Self-reflection SFT + RL (27.60 avg) is competitive on AMO-Bench (36.72) but trails InT substantially on IMO-AnswerBench (15.53 vs. 25.62). On IMO-AnswerBench, InT's 4B model (25.62%) surpasses both gpt-oss-20b (23.36%) and DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B (18.44%), demonstrating that improved credit assignment can compensate for model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

InT positions itself as a lightweight alternative to process reward models (PRMs) and branched-rollout methods for credit assignment in LLM reasoning, drawing conceptual inspiration from DAgger-style intervention in robotics but repurposing it for self-generated corrections. It builds on the verification-generation asymmetry observed in LLMs (Setlur et al., 2025b) and the GRPO/RLVR training paradigm (DeepSeek-R1, Shao et al., 2024), but departs from prior credit-assignment work by collapsing value estimation and policy optimization into a single prompting step rather than training an explicit value function. The method is complementary to concurrent hint-guided RL approaches (Qu et al., 2025a; Li et al., 2025), which steer the policy with partial-solution prefixes, whereas InT targets errors that arise deep within otherwise on-policy trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InT offers a simple, computationally cheap recipe (SFT on short interventions + standard RL) that addresses a well-recognized bottleneck in outcome-reward RL without requiring PRM training, branched rollouts, or larger teacher models. The finding that naively SFT-ing on reference solutions actually degrades downstream RL performance (20.76 vs. 21.17 base) is a useful negative result that cautions against off-policy distillation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond math to proof-based or multi-turn tasks, whether reference-solution dependence can be removed through trained verifiers, and how credit assignment extends to continual-learning settings where context evolves over time.
