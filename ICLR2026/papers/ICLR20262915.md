## Problem

ReduNet, a white-box network grounded in Maximal Coding Rate Reduction (MCR²), provides interpretable feature learning with closed-form updates but suffers from O(d³) per-parameter complexity due to global d×d matrix inversions, which becomes prohibitive in undersampled, high-dimensional regimes (m ≪ d). Moreover, its global optimization does not exploit class-specific structure, limiting both computational efficiency and feature separability when class sizes vary significantly. This paper asks whether the MCR² objective can be rigorously decomposed into independent per-class subproblems without loss of optimality, and whether such a decomposition yields practical gains in accuracy, speed, and hyperparameter robustness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a vectorized feature matrix X ∈ R^{d×m} (flattened images at 100×100, text vectors, or tabular rows) together with hard class labels encoded as diagonal membership matrices Π^j ∈ R^{m×m}. It produces per-class feature blocks Z^j ∈ R^{d×m_j} at each of L layers, with the final-layer features fed to a downstream classifier (SVM, kNN, or NSC) for prediction. A key structural property is that the learned class blocks are approximately mutually orthogonal at the global optimum, and the LastNorm variant relaxes per-layer unit-sphere projection to apply it only at the final layer, allowing intermediate representations to evolve with non-uniform norms.

## Method

The core contribution is a theoretical proof (Theorems 1–2) that any global optimum of the MCR² objective satisfies class-orthogonality (Z^i)ᵀZ^j = 0 for i ≠ j, and under this condition the global objective decomposes exactly into K independent per-class subproblems, each optimizing a difference of log-determinant terms over Z^j ∈ R^{d×m_j}. Multi-ReduNet implements this decomposition by computing per-class expansion and compression operators E_l^j = α(I + αZ_l^j(Z_l^j)ᵀ)⁻¹ and C_l^j = α_j(I + α_jZ_l^j(Z_l^j)ᵀ)⁻¹ via the Woodbury identity, reducing each inversion from O(d³) to O(m_j³) and enabling parallel per-class gradient-ascent updates with unit-sphere projection. Multi-ReduNet-LastNorm further defers the sphere projection to the final layer only, which empirically improves hyperparameter robustness by roughly an order of magnitude (6.4 pp vs. 62.6 pp average accuracy range across learning rates) while matching or slightly exceeding Multi-ReduNet accuracy. The result is a white-box, interpretable architecture that retains ReduNet's closed-form layerwise updates while achieving 1.4–2.6× training speedups and 8.5–52.7 percentage-point accuracy gains over ReduNet on severely undersampled benchmarks.

## Results

At the fixed learning rate η=0.05, Multi-ReduNet-LastNorm achieves the largest gains on severely undersampled datasets: DrivFace SVM accuracy jumps from 0.432 (ReduNet) to 1.000, ARCENE from 0.439 to 0.829, and Reuters from 0.802 to 0.985. Averaging over four learning rates {0.01, 0.05, 0.1, 0.5} and three classifiers (SVM, KNN, NSC), Multi-ReduNet(-LastNorm) achieves 8.5–52.7 percentage points higher mean accuracy than ReduNet (e.g., +30.7 pp on Reuters, +52.7 pp on DrivFace). Training time is reduced by 1.4–2.6× (average ~2×) across all six datasets, with the largest speedup (2.6×) on ARCENE where m/d = 0.016. Hyperparameter robustness improves dramatically: the accuracy range across η shrinks from 62.6 pp (ReduNet) to 6.4 pp (Multi-ReduNet-LastNorm) on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ReduNet (Chan et al., 2022), Multi-ReduNet-LastNorm wins decisively on the four most undersampled datasets (Reuters +18.3 pp SVM, Swarm +19.8 pp, DrivFace +56.8 pp, ARCENE +39.0 pp at η=0.05) but underperforms on the more balanced MNIST (−6.4 pp) and Fashion-MNIST (−5.7 pp) at that fixed rate; averaging over η narrows or reverses the gap on MNIST/Fashion. Against Global PCA+SVM (Greenacre et al., 2022), Multi-ReduNet-LastNorm achieves higher best accuracy on Reuters (98.8% vs 97.5%), MNIST (92.6% vs 87.8%), and Fashion-MNIST (85.8% vs 82.9%), while tying at 100% on Swarm and DrivFace and slightly exceeding PCA on ARCENE (82.9% vs 80.5%). Against LDA (Xing et al., 2001), Multi-ReduNet-LastNorm wins on Reuters (98.8% vs 47.1%), MNIST (92.6% vs 61.5%), and Fashion-MNIST (85.8% vs 78.1%), but loses on ARCENE (82.9% vs 87.8%), indicating that classical linear discriminants remain competitive on well-structured microarray data. The Woodbury-optimized imp-ReduNet matches ReduNet's accuracy exactly (confirming the identity is lossless) but does not exploit class structure, so it offers no accuracy gain over the original.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ReduNet (Chan et al., 2022) and its underlying MCR² principle (Yu et al., 2020) by decomposing the global coding-rate objective into independent per-class subproblems, justified by a class-orthogonality theorem (previously known but re-proven here via a more direct determinant inequality). The Woodbury identity is applied to reduce each per-class inversion from O(d³) to O(m_j³), and a LastNorm variant relaxes intermediate sphere projections to improve hyperparameter robustness. The work positions itself against black-box alternatives (few-shot networks, GANs, variational information-bottleneck encoders) by retaining closed-form, interpretable updates while exploiting class-specific structure that global methods ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The class-wise decomposition provides the first practical algorithm for per-class MCR² optimization, making ReduNet viable in severely undersampled, high-dimensional settings (m/d < 0.3) where the original O(d³) inversion is prohibitive. However, the method's applicability is narrow: it degrades on oversampled data (m/d > 1), cannot handle spatial structure (CIFAR-10), and the LastNorm variant's robustness gains lack formal theoretical justification. Open questions include convergence guarantees linking the iterative procedure to the ideal class-orthogonal optimum, extension to convolutional or structured architectures, and whether softer norm constraints can further improve performance.
