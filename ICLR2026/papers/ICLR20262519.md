## Problem

Existing backdoor removal methods for instruction-tuned LLMs typically assume prior knowledge of the trigger, access to a clean reference model, or rely on aggressive finetuning configurations (e.g., unusually large learning rates), and are often limited to classification tasks. These assumptions break down in real-world deployment where defenders have no trigger information, no clean checkpoint, and must operate under standard training hyperparameters. This paper asks whether backdoors can be eliminated in generative LLMs without any of these priors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a suspicious instruction-tuned LLM (7B–13B scale, e.g., LLaMA-2-Chat, Mistral-Instruct, CodeLLaMA) and a small set of clean Alpaca samples, operating under two threat models: adapter-only access (only LoRA weights are inspectable) and full-model access (all parameters available). It produces a purified model with the backdoor association severed, plus an intermediate backdoor signature—a set of channel indices in MLP (and optionally attention) layers ranked by a magnitude-and-consistency score. The signature is derived entirely from synthetic variants trained on the suspicious model itself, requiring no external clean reference.

## Method

Sanity-check ablations reveal that backdoor trigger-behavior associations are redundantly encoded across MLP layers (removing all MLP updates eliminates the backdoor, while removing attention updates does not), and that activation is order-invariant and distributed across blocks. Guided by this, the method adopts an immunization-inspired signature extraction: it constructs N=6 synthetic backdoored variants, each finetuned with a distinct trigger-behavior pair on a different clean subset, and computes differential deltas (poisoned minus clean weight updates) to isolate the shared association direction. A combined scoring function—averaging L2 norm (poison strength) plus pairwise cosine alignment (cross-variant consistency)—ranks channels, and the top-percentile set forms the backdoor signature. Suspicious neurons in gate_proj, up_proj, and down_proj are then reinitialized (full-model) or zeroed in LoRA matrices (adapter-only), followed by lightweight finetuning on ~200 clean samples at standard learning rates (1e-5 full / 2e-4 LoRA) to restore generative fluency.

## Results

On LLaMA-2-7B-Chat under Sentiment Steering (BadNets), the proposed method reduces average ASR to 2.10% (full-model) and 0.91% (LoRA), compared to 28.16% for the un-defended model. On LLaMA-2-13B-Chat under the same task, ASR drops to 3.20% (full-model) and 3.49% (LoRA) versus 52.37% un-defended. Under the harder Targeted Refusal task, the method still achieves 6.67% (7B full) and 12.94% (13B LoRA) average ASR, while the un-defended model sits at 82–85%. Utility on ten close-ended benchmarks and MT-Bench is preserved within ~1% of the clean model, outperforming all baselines that degrade accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines across every model, task, and access setting. Finetuning (Qi et al., 2024) is the weakest baseline, often performing at or below the un-defended model (e.g., 29.96% vs. 28.16% ASR on 7B Sentiment Steering, Full Params), confirming that clean-data finetuning alone cannot overwrite distributed MLP-encoded associations. Magnitude Pruning (Wu & Wang, 2021) and Quantization (Khalid et al., 2019) provide moderate ASR reductions (13–16% on 7B Sentiment) but leave substantial residual vulnerability, particularly under composite attacks like CTBA. CROW (Min et al., 2025a) is the strongest competitor, achieving 8.66% average ASR on 7B Sentiment (Full Params), but the paper demonstrates that a significant portion of CROW's reported gains stem from an atypically large learning rate (1×10⁻³ vs. the standard 2×10⁻⁴); under standardized hyperparameters its advantage narrows considerably. Fine-Pruning (Liu et al., 2018) improves over raw pruning by adding a finetuning step (e.g., 10.94% vs. 13.78% on 7B Sentiment Full Params) but still trails the proposed method by 5–10× in ASR. Overall, the immunization-inspired signature extraction plus targeted neuron suppression achieves 80–99% relative ASR reduction, a margin no baseline matches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mechanistic understanding of backdoors from Lamparth & Reuel's analysis of small GPT-2 text-completion models to 7B–13B instruction-tuned LLMs, shifting the focus from early-layer MLPs and trigger embeddings to a distributed, redundant encoding across all MLP blocks. It departs from prior defenses that require trigger knowledge, clean reference models, or fragile attention-based signals by proposing an immunization-inspired framework that extracts a shared 'backdoor signature' through differential weight analysis across multiple synthetic poisoned variants. The approach also builds on knowledge-editing evidence (Meng et al., 2022) that MLPs store factual associations, repurposing this insight for security purification rather than targeted fact modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides a practical, deployable defense for a realistic threat scenario—unknown triggers, no clean reference, and potentially only LoRA adapter access—that prior methods could not address simultaneously. The mechanistic finding that backdoor associations are redundantly distributed across MLP blocks (and survive shuffling) challenges the common assumption that backdoors can be localized to a few heads or early layers, with implications for how future defenses should be designed. Limitations include the computational cost of constructing N synthetic backdoor variants, the need to tune intervention ratios per model, and limited cross-task generalization of signatures. Open questions include robustness against adaptive attackers who are aware of the purification procedure, scalability to 70B+ models, and whether the approach generalizes to more semantically subtle backdoors that do not produce large, aligned weight shifts.
