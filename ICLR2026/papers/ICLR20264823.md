## Problem

DPO and its recent variants (e.g., SimPO) treat all preference pairs equally during training, ignoring substantial variation in data quality and the model's evolving learning difficulty. This indiscriminate weighting leads to underutilization of high-quality pairs, overfitting on already well-fitted samples, and suboptimal alignment. The paper asks how to dynamically adjust the learning focus across pairs by jointly accounting for intrinsic data quality and the model's current performance on each pair.

## Input / Output

The method consumes preference triplets (prompt x, preferred response y_w, inferior response y_l) annotated with scalar quality scores S_w and S_l from external experts (human annotators, GPT-4, or domain-specific reward models). It outputs a fine-tuned LLM policy that generates text responses to prompts. The framework is modality-agnostic: it is applied to text-only instruction-following (Llama3-8B, Gemma-2-9B-IT, Qwen2.5-7B), mathematical reasoning (Qwen2.5-Math 1.5B/7B), and multimodal image-text tasks, with length normalization applied per response to decouple preference signal from sequence length.

## Method

Uni-DPO augments the vanilla DPO objective with a dual-perspective adaptive weighting mechanism and a calibrated auxiliary loss. First, a quality weight w_qual = σ(η·(S_w − S_l)) up-weights pairs with large expert score margins and down-weights ambiguous ones. Second, a performance weight w_perf = [1 − σ(β/|y_w|·log π_θ(y_w|x) − β/|y_l|·log π_θ(y_l|x) − τ_ref)]^γ, derived from focal loss but stabilized via length normalization and a fixed uniform threshold τ_ref (replacing the per-sample reference-model margin), shifts focus toward under-fitted pairs and mitigates overfitting on well-learned ones. Third, a calibrated NLL loss L_c-NLL selectively increases the policy's confidence on positive samples that are both high-quality (S_w ≥ τ_good) and currently under-estimated relative to the reference model. The two weights are combined multiplicatively with the DPO log-sigmoid term, and the c-NLL loss is added with a small coefficient λ, yielding a single unified training objective that explicitly models both the off-policy (data quality) and on-policy (learning dynamics) aspects of preference optimization.

## Results

On textual understanding, Gemma-2-9B-IT fine-tuned with Uni-DPO achieves 67.1% win rate on Arena-Hard, surpassing Claude 3 Opus (60.4%) and Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct (55.7%). On mathematical reasoning, Uni-DPO yields an average gain of 8.3 points over SimPO on Qwen2.5-Math 1.5B (49.54 vs. 45.68) and 17.7 points on the 7B model (56.80 vs. 53.73). Across all four textual benchmarks (AlpacaEval 2.0, Arena-Hard, IFEval, SedarEval) and all eight math benchmarks, Uni-DPO consistently outperforms both DPO and SimPO across Llama3-8B, Gemma-2-9B-IT, and Qwen2.5-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Uni-DPO is compared against DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) and SimPO (Meng et al., 2024), the two most widely used preference optimization baselines. Uni-DPO wins on every benchmark across all model families. On Arena-Hard, the gap over SimPO is largest for Gemma-2-9B-IT (67.1 vs. 59.1, +8.0 points) and Llama3-8B Instruct (37.3 vs. 33.8, +3.5 points). On mathematical reasoning, Uni-DPO outperforms SimPO by 3.9 points (1.5B) and 3.1 points (7B) in average score, while the margin over DPO is even larger (7.5 and 5.3 points respectively). On IFEval, Uni-DPO achieves the highest strict accuracy for Gemma-2-9B-IT (71.2 vs. SimPO's 60.4, +10.8 points) and Llama3-8B Instruct (61.2, tied with SimPO but with higher loose accuracy at 67.8 vs. 65.8). The only near-tie is SedarEval on Gemma-2-9B-IT (57.5 vs. 57.7), where SimPO edges out Uni-DPO by 0.2 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

Uni-DPO extends the DPO objective (Rafailov et al., 2023) by introducing a dual-perspective dynamic weighting mechanism that jointly considers intrinsic data quality (via a sigmoid-weighted score margin) and the model's evolving learning dynamics (via a stabilized focal-loss-inspired performance weight). The performance weight is explicitly derived from focal loss (Lin et al., 2017) originally designed for object detection, adapted here with length normalization (following SimPO, Meng et al., 2024) and a unified threshold τ_ref to avoid the instability of naive focal weighting in preference learning. The calibrated NLL loss addresses a known pathology of DPO where preferred-sample probabilities can decrease during training (Rafailov et al., 2024; Pal et al., 2024). Conceptually, the paper positions itself as closing the gap between DPO's implicit reward margin (which cannot distinguish data quality) and the advantage-based reweighting available in PPO/GRPO, without requiring a separate reward model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-zero Spearman correlation (ρ=0.08) between expert-assigned score margins and model reward margins is a practically important finding: it shows that simply up-weighting high-quality pairs is insufficient and can even cause overfitting, motivating the dual-perspective design. The method is lightweight (two multiplicative weights plus a small auxiliary loss) and plug-and-play on top of existing DPO/SimPO pipelines, making it broadly applicable. A key limitation is the dependence on external quality scores (from GPT-4 or reward models), which introduces cost and potential bias; the authors acknowledge that better quality estimation is future work. Open questions include whether the quality-difficulty mismatch persists in on-policy or iterative DPO settings, and whether the fixed hyperparameters (γ=3.0, τ_ref, η) transfer cleanly to new domains without retuning.
