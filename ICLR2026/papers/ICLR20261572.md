## Problem

Existing Table QA benchmarks fix the table format and evaluate models on a single representation, leaving it unclear how structured versus semi-structured formats affect performance across modeling paradigms. No prior work has isolated the effect of representation by holding table content constant while varying structure, making it impossible to determine whether observed differences stem from representation shift or content mismatch.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261572/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a structured table (with explicit schema, column types, and keys) and a natural language question as input, and produces the answer to that question. The verbalization pipeline additionally generates a paired semi-structured variant of the same table by converting selected columns into free-text descriptions, so each question is evaluated against both representations with identical semantics. Diagnostic splits further constrain inputs along four axes: table size (short <100 rows vs. long ≥100 rows), number of tables (single vs. multi-table joins), query complexity (simple lookup vs. multi-step compositional), and schema quality (clean vs. incomplete/noisy).

## Method

The core contribution is a three-step verbalization pipeline that transforms structured tables into semi-structured counterparts while preserving semantics: (1) GPT-4o classifies columns as descriptive vs. technical and samples random combinations of verbalizable columns; (2) GPT-4o generates five diverse natural-language sentence templates conditioned on the table schema into which column values are inserted; (3) templates are instantiated per row and merged into a single free-text column, removing the original structured columns. On top of this pipeline, the authors construct REPAIRTQA, a fine-grained diagnostic benchmark partitioned from BIRD, MMQA, and TableEval into controlled subsets that vary one factor at a time (table size, joins, query complexity, schema quality). They then conduct a zero-shot controlled evaluation of three method families—direct LLM reasoning (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-flash, Qwen3-235B), NL2SQL (LLM-NL2SQL baseline, XiYan ensemble), and hybrid neuro-symbolic approaches (H-STAR, Weaver)—using GPT-4o as a semantic-equivalence judge (96% human agreement) rather than exact-match scoring.

## Results

On structured tables, NL2SQL methods dominate: XiYan achieves 69.55% and LLM-NL2SQL achieves 69.14% weighted accuracy across all diagnostic splits, outperforming the best LLM (Gemini-2.5-flash at 52.07%) and best hybrid (Weaver at 62.19%). On semi-structured tables, the ranking reverses: Weaver leads at 57.70%, followed by Gemini-2.5-flash (50.78%) and H-STAR (47.14%), while NL2SQL methods collapse (XiYan drops to 24.08%, a 45.47% decline). LLMs exhibit the smallest representation-induced drops (1.29–3.44%), confirming their robustness to format shifts, but none reaches the peak accuracy of specialized methods in either setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among LLM baselines, Gemini-2.5-flash is the strongest (52.07% structured / 50.78% semi-structured) and most robust to representation shift (only 1.29% drop), while GPT-4o (45.37% / 41.93%) and Qwen3-235B (38.20% / 36.70%) trail by 7 and 14 points respectively on structured inputs. NL2SQL methods XiYan (69.55%) and LLM-NL2SQL (69.14%) achieve the highest structured accuracy but suffer the largest semi-structured drops (45.47% and 30.49% respectively), with XiYan falling to just 24.08%—below even the weakest LLM. Among hybrids, Weaver (62.19% / 57.70%) outperforms H-STAR (49.48% / 47.14%) by 12.7 and 10.6 points on structured and semi-structured inputs respectively, and is the top performer on semi-structured tables overall. The key trade-off is clear: NL2SQL wins on clean structured data by ~10–20 points over all other families, but hybrids (especially Weaver) and LLMs (especially Gemini) are far more robust when the table format shifts to semi-structured, with drops under 5% versus 30–45% for SQL-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in Table QA benchmarks (BIRD, MMQA, TableEval) that fix table format and vary only query complexity or domain. Rather than proposing a new model, it introduces a verbalization pipeline that generates paired structured and semi-structured tables with identical content, enabling the first controlled isolation of representation as an independent variable. The evaluation draws on three established method families—NL2SQL (XiYan, LLM-NL2SQL), direct LLM reasoning (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-flash, Qwen3-235B), and neuro-symbolic hybrids (H-STAR, Weaver)—but repositions them under a unified diagnostic protocol that prior benchmarks did not support. Conceptually, it extends the analytical studies of Singha et al. (2023) and Sui et al. (2024), which examined representation effects in isolation, into a multi-factor, multi-paradigm controlled comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that no single Table QA paradigm dominates across all real-world conditions, and the choice of method should be driven by data format, table size, and schema quality rather than benchmark leaderboard rankings. The dramatic 30–45% collapse of NL2SQL on semi-structured inputs versus a mere 3.5% drop for LLMs provides concrete evidence that representation shift is a first-order deployment risk, not a minor perturbation. Open questions include extending the framework to ultra-long tables requiring retrieval or chunking, developing theoretical accounts of why representation sensitivity persists even in frontier LLMs, and building hybrid systems that dynamically route between symbolic and neural reasoning based on detected table format. The REPAIRTQA benchmark and its fine-grained splits offer a reusable diagnostic tool for future method development.
