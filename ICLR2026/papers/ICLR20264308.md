## Problem

Diffusion language models are trained with uniform random masking but at inference time rely on planners (greedy decoding, P2 path planning) that select non-uniform denoising paths, creating an irrevocable training-inference mismatch. The paper proves that the standard DLM ELBO is violated under planner-based inference, meaning the training objective no longer reflects the quality of generated samples. This raises the question of how to adapt denoiser training when inference inevitably proceeds under a planner.

## Input / Output

The denoiser D_θ takes a partially masked sequence of length L (where some positions contain a special mask token m and others contain vocabulary tokens) and outputs a categorical distribution over clean tokens at each position. At inference, a planner G_φ selects which masked position to unmask next based on the denoiser's confidence, and the process iterates until no masks remain. Training reuses the standard uniform masking scheme (mask L−k positions at random) but applies planner-weighted cross-entropy, so the computational cost per step is identical to vanilla DLM training.

## Method

The paper derives a Planner-Aware ELBO (P-ELBO) by formulating DLM sampling as a discrete-time Markov chain and computing the path-wise KL divergence between the planner-guided reverse dynamics and an ideal reference chain that hits the data distribution. The P-ELBO decomposes into a planner-weighted cross-entropy term (analogous to the standard DLM ELBO but weighted by the planner's position-selection probability) and a correction term measuring the gap between an oracle planner with access to ground truth and the effective planner relying on denoiser predictions. To make this tractable, the authors relax the deterministic greedy argmax into a softmax over denoiser confidence scores (temperature τ), detach gradients through the planner to eliminate the correction term, and interpolate with the vanilla DLM loss via a hyperparameter α. The resulting PAPL loss is a one-line modification: each masked position's cross-entropy is multiplied by (1 + αw^i) where w^i is the soft-greedy planner weight, requiring no additional forward passes or architectural changes.

## Results

On protein sequence generation, PAPL (150M) achieves 59.40% foldability, a 40% relative improvement over the DLM-150M baseline (42.43%), with pLDDT 81.48, pTM 0.72, and pAE 8.97. On text generation (OpenWebText), PAPL attains the best diffusion MAUVE of 0.067 at T=128 (vs. 0.057 for ReMDM and 0.015 for vanilla MDLM) and reduces generative perplexity to 24.33 (vs. 42.5 for ReMDM). On code generation, PAPL improves HUMANEVAL pass@1 from 18.5 to 20.8 and pass@10 from 31.1 to 38.4, and HUMANEVAL-INFILL pass@1 from 30.0 to 32.5, all under identical model configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAPL outperforms all protein baselines at the 150M scale: it surpasses DPLM-150M (48.14% foldability) by 11.26 points and DLM-150M (42.43%) by 16.97 points, while also exceeding the much larger DPLM-650M (49.14%) and ProGen2-large (11.87%). Against autoregressive baselines, PAPL's 59.40% foldability dwarfs ESM3 (1.50%) and ProGen2-medium (12.75%). On text generation, PAPL achieves a 4.5× MAUVE improvement over vanilla MDLM (0.067 vs. 0.015 at T=128) and outperforms ReMDM (0.057) and MDLM+DFM (0.041) in both MAUVE and generative perplexity (24.33 vs. 42.5 and 37.9). On code generation, PAPL (0.5B) beats Edit Flow (1.3B) on HUMANEVAL pass@1 (20.8 vs. 12.8) and pass@10 (38.4 vs. 24.3), and outperforms Mask DFM (1.3B) by a wide margin (20.8 vs. 9.1 pass@1). The disproportionate pass@10 gains (38.4 vs. 31.1) suggest PAPL learns a more robust generative distribution over the solution space rather than merely improving the single best prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the masked diffusion language model (MDLM) training framework of Sahoo et al. (2024) and Shi et al. (2024), and on the P2 path-planning inference framework of Peng et al. (2025a). Its central conceptual departure is identifying and formally proving that the standard MDLM ELBO is violated when inference uses a non-uniform planner (e.g., greedy ancestral sampling as in MaskGIT), exposing a training-inference mismatch that prior work treated as a heuristic concern. The derived P-ELBO unifies existing planners (MaskGIT, P2, RDM) under a single variational framework, and the practical algorithm PAPL reduces to a planner-weighted cross-entropy — a one-line modification of the standard masked diffusion loss. The paper also positions itself against AOARM order-learning methods (LO-ARM, DDPD) that require policy gradients or auxiliary planner models, arguing that self-planning via the denoiser's own confidence avoids these overheads.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that the standard DLM ELBO is provably violated under greedy inference is a clean theoretical correction that reframes how the community should think about DLM training objectives, and the P-ELBO provides a principled template for incorporating any future planner into training. Practically, PAPL's one-line implementation with no additional compute makes it immediately adoptable by any DLM pipeline, and the consistent cross-domain gains (protein, text, code) suggest the training-inference mismatch is a real and broadly impactful bottleneck. Key limitations include the need for full retraining (not a drop-in inference trick), the residual large gap to autoregressive models in text generation (MAUVE 0.067 vs. 0.760), and the open question of extending the framework to remasking planners (RDM, top-k block denoising) where the current single-coordinate assumption breaks down.
