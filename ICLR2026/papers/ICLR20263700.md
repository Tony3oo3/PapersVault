## Problem

Document-level knowledge graph construction faces a fundamental scaling bottleneck: existing approaches rely on expensive LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o) for zero-shot or few-shot prompting, making them economically nonviable for large corpora, while smaller models produce incomplete and inconsistent graphs. The authors identify the root cause as a training data gap rather than model capability—no large-scale, high-quality document-KG training corpus exists for ontology-free KG construction. Additionally, there is no systematic evaluation framework for document-level, ontology-free KGs, making it difficult to diagnose whether RAG failures stem from poor graph quality or downstream reasoning.

## Input / Output

The SynthKG pipeline takes a full multi-paragraph text document as input and produces an ontology-free document-level KG structured as a proposition-entity bipartite graph, where each relation is a quadruplet (source entity, predicate, target entity, proposition sentence). Documents are internally chunked at 256-token sentence boundaries with no overlap before processing. Distill-SynthKG consumes the entire document in a single forward pass and outputs the same KG structure. For downstream retrieval and QA, the system additionally takes a natural-language question and returns ranked text chunks or a final answer, with propositions serving as the primary retrieval units rather than raw triplets.

## Method

SynthKG decomposes KG construction into three reproducible stages: (1) sentence-boundary chunking, (2) LLM-based decontextualization that rewrites each chunk to replace ambiguous entity mentions with their most informative canonical form using only the preceding chunk as context, and (3) joint entity and relation/proposition extraction per chunk. The pipeline is run with Llama-3.1-70b-Instruct over 100K documents from IndustryCorpus to generate the first large-scale ontology-free document-KG training set. Distill-SynthKG then fine-tunes Llama-3-8b-Instruct on 30K of these synthetic pairs, internalizing the multi-step procedure into a single-step model that matches or exceeds the 70B teacher's KG quality. The key design insight is that propositions—self-contained sentences describing each relation—act simultaneously as an intermediate chain-of-thought during extraction and as semantically rich, indexable retrieval units in the downstream proposition-entity bipartite graph. A progressive graph retriever exploits this structure by first selecting top-M propositions via embedding similarity, traversing the bipartite graph within an N-hop neighborhood of question entities, and optionally re-ranking with an LLM before returning text chunks.

## Results

On multi-hop QA (average across MuSiQue, 2WikiMultiHopQA, HotpotQA), D-SynthKG-8b with the Graph+LLM retriever achieves 0.433 EM and 0.569 F1, outperforming GraphRAG (0.390/0.491) and HippoRAG (0.403/0.546) built on GPT-4o KGs. In retrieval, D-SynthKG-8b + Graph+LLM reaches 53.35 Hits@2 on MuSiQue versus 41.32 for standard dense retrieval and 47.60 for Dense+LLM. For KG coverage, D-SynthKG-8b achieves 46.90% triplet coverage on MuSiQue, surpassing Llama-3-70b (40.34%) and SynthKG-8b (38.84%) while being ~8× smaller than the 70B model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D-SynthKG-8b (8B parameters) consistently outperforms Llama-3-70b (70B, ~8× larger) across all three QA frameworks: +15.2% absolute EM over Llama-3-8b and +2.0% over Llama-3-70b in the Graph+LLM setting. Against state-of-the-art KG-RAG systems, D-SynthKG-8b + Graph+LLM beats GraphRAG (local) by +4.3 EM and HippoRAG by +3.0 EM on average, despite both baselines using GPT-4o for KG extraction. In retrieval, the Graph+LLM retriever improves Hits@2 by 12.75 points over standard dense retrieval and 1.67 points over Dense+LLM, confirming that structured KGs enable more effective retrieval than flat vector search. D-SynthKG-8b also matches or slightly exceeds GPT-4o in retrieval (e.g., 72.78 vs. 70.38 Hits@10 on MuSiQue) at roughly 3% of the inference cost. The multi-step SynthKG-70b pipeline remains the strongest KG-quality baseline (47.18% coverage on MuSiQue), but D-SynthKG-8b closes the gap to within 0.28 points while requiring only a single inference call per document.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of using large LLMs (GPT-4o, Llama-3-70b) via zero-shot or few-shot prompting to construct ontology-free knowledge graphs for RAG, as done in GraphRAG, HippoRAG, and GraphReader. Rather than scaling model size, it introduces a data-centric pipeline (SynthKG) that decomposes KG construction into chunking, decontextualization, and structured extraction, then distills the multi-step process into a single-step 8B model (Distill-SynthKG). The proposition-entity bipartite graph structure and the graph-constrained retrieval framework are novel contributions that build on the triplet-based retrieval of HippoRAG but treat propositions as first-class retrieval units. The paper also introduces the first systematic evaluation framework for document-level, ontology-free KGs by repurposing multi-hop QA datasets into proxy ground-truth triplets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that an 8B model trained on synthetic data matches or exceeds a 70B model in KG quality, retrieval, and QA shifts the practical calculus for large-scale KG construction from expensive API calls to one-time data synthesis plus cheap inference (~3% of GPT-4o cost). The released 100K document-KG pairs and the coverage evaluation metrics address a genuine gap in the field, enabling systematic benchmarking of ontology-free KG construction. Open questions include whether the chunk-based decontextualization strategy generalizes to documents with long-range entity dependencies (the authors acknowledge this limitation in Appendix I), whether the approach extends to other structured extraction tasks, and whether the evaluation metrics based on GPT-4o-generated proxy triplets introduce systematic biases.
