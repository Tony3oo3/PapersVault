## Problem

End-to-end protein complex predictors like AlphaFold3 suffer quadratic memory growth with token count, making inference infeasible beyond ~4–5k tokens on modern GPUs. Existing divide-and-conquer alternatives (CombFold, MoLPC) either rely on expert-curated subunits, miss multi-body cooperativity among interacting chains, or both, limiting accuracy on complexes with higher-order coupling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261118/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes peptide chain sequences and MSAs as input, operating at the token level (one token per residue, nucleotide, or ligand atom). It produces a fully assembled 3D complex structure with per-residue confidence scores (pLDDT, pTM, ipTM), but internally generates intermediate PAE matrices and subunit segmentations that drive the decomposition. The key constraint is that each fine-stage inference processes only one focus chain plus a sparse set of selected interface subunits, keeping per-run token counts well below the ~4k memory ceiling.

## Method

HIERAFOLD is a three-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline. In the coarse stage, a consistency-distilled diffusion model (12 Pairformer blocks vs. 24, trained with a consistency objective for 1–2 step generation) produces fast pairwise structures and PAE matrices for all chain pairs; these PAE matrices are then recursively partitioned top-down by maximizing inter-subunit mean PAE to segment each chain into rigid subunits, and off-diagonal low-PAE patches identify candidate inter-chain interface subunits selected by mean interface PAE (<5 Å) or minimum center-atom distance (<20 Å). In the fine stage, a full AlphaFold3-style inference is run for each focus chain together with only its selected partner subunits, capturing multi-body cooperativity while bounding memory. In the assembly stage, partial structures are merged iteratively via a confidence-weighted Kabsch alignment where each atom pair's contribution is weighted by the product of its pLDDT scores, prioritizing well-predicted interface regions over flexible or uncertain loops.

## Results

On the Recent PDB benchmark, HIERAFOLD achieves a DockQ success rate of 73.1% (Oracle) and 69.0% (Top-1), nearly matching the end-to-end AlphaFold3/Protenix baseline (74.4% Oracle, 70.4% Top-1). On PoseBuster v2 (protein-ligand), HIERAFOLD reaches 74.7% Top-1 ligand RMSD ≤ 2 Å success rate versus 76.0% for the baseline. Critically, on the Filtered Large Complex Dataset (>5,000 tokens), where AlphaFold3 and AlphaFold-Multimer fail with Out-Of-Memory errors, HIERAFOLD achieves 43.9% Top-1 DockQ success rate—more than double CombFold+AF3 (19.8%)—while reducing peak GPU memory by ~40% on 4,000-token targets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the AlphaFold3/Protenix baseline, HIERAFOLD trades a marginal 1.3% Oracle DockQ drop (73.1% vs 74.4%) on the Recent PDB for a ~40% reduction in peak GPU memory and the ability to model complexes exceeding 5,000 tokens where the baseline simply fails with OOM. Against CombFold (both the original AFM-backed variant and the AF3-backed variant), HIERAFOLD wins decisively: 69.0% vs 48.8%/43.2% Top-1 on Recent PDB, and 43.9% vs 19.8% Top-1 on the Large Complex Dataset—a more than two-fold improvement that the authors attribute to HIERAFOLD's multi-body cooperativity capture during the fine-stage refinement, which purely pairwise assembly methods lack. Against AlphaFold-Multimer, HIERAFOLD edges ahead (69.0% vs 67.1% Top-1) while also handling larger complexes. Against RoseTTAFold All-Atom, HIERAFOLD leads by 13.6 points on Recent PDB (69.0% vs 55.4%) and by 32.9 points on PoseBuster v2 (74.7% vs 41.8%). MoLPC, designed primarily for homomeric complexes, trails by a wide margin (31.3% Top-1), confirming that HIERAFOLD's PAE-guided decomposition and interface-aware refinement are substantially more effective for general heteromeric large complexes.

## Relation to Prior Work

HIERAFOLD positions itself between two existing paradigms for large complex prediction: end-to-end diffusion models (AlphaFold3/Protenix) that hit quadratic memory walls, and divide-and-conquer assembly methods (CombFold, MoLPC) that predict pairwise sub-complexes and stitch them together via combinatorial search or MCTS. It departs from both by introducing a hierarchical coarse-to-fine pipeline that repurposes the PAE matrix—already produced as a byproduct of AlphaFold-family inference—as an automatic subunit segmentation and interface detection signal, eliminating the need for external domain databases or expert curation. The consistency-distilled few-step diffusion model for the coarse stage draws directly from the consistency model literature (Song et al., 2023), while the confidence-weighted Kabsch assembly extends standard structural superposition with pLDDT-based weighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: HIERAFOLD removes the hard OOM barrier that currently prevents AlphaFold3 from modeling complexes beyond ~4–5k tokens, a limitation that blocks prediction of biologically important systems like chaperonins and multi-enzyme circuits. The model-agnostic design (validated with RoseTTAFold All-Atom) suggests the hierarchical framework can wrap around future backbone predictors as they emerge. Key limitations remain: performance is bounded by the underlying AlphaFold3 model, multi-state conformational prediction is not addressed, and the extra computation from M sequential fine-stage runs (though parallelizable) adds ~30–40% wall-clock time. Open questions include whether the PAE-guided decomposition generalizes to nucleic acid–protein or small-molecule-dominant complexes and whether fine-tuning the coarse model on large-complex data can close the remaining accuracy gap on the >5k token regime.
