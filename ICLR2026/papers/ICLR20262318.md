## Problem

Compositional generative models that chain short-horizon local distributions to produce long-horizon plans suffer from mode averaging: when local distributions are highly multimodal, score-averaging at overlaps blends incompatible modes, yielding global plans that are neither locally feasible nor globally coherent. Existing compositional diffusion methods lack a mechanism to jointly reason about compatibility between local modes and navigate the exponentially large search space of mode sequences.

## Input / Output

For robotics, CDGS takes a low-dimensional start state and goal state (end-effector pose plus object poses in a global frame) along with a planning horizon, and outputs a full state-action trajectory up to 10 seconds, composed from 4-second local plans learned at 20 Hz. For content generation, it takes a text prompt and produces panoramas up to 512×4608 by stitching 512×512 Stable Diffusion patches, or extends ~50-frame CogVideoX-2B clips to 350 frames at 720p. The method is entirely inference-time: it requires only a pretrained local plan diffusion model and no additional training data or overlap-conditioned score functions.

## Method

CDGS embeds a guided search directly within the diffusion denoising loop, operating on a factor graph of overlapping local distributions whose joint score is computed via the Bethe approximation. At each denoising timestep, two mechanisms act on a population of B candidate global plans: (i) iterative resampling alternates U rounds of forward noising and denoising to propagate information across distant segments through shared overlap variables, analogous to belief propagation on a chain; and (ii) likelihood-based pruning ranks candidates using a curvature measure of the DDIM inversion trajectory (high curvature indicates low local likelihood) and retains only the K best plans, discarding those that fall into mode-averaging regions. The ranking metric J aggregates per-segment smoothness scores, encoding the insight that a global plan is feasible iff every local transition is high-likelihood under the pretrained short-horizon model. This cross-entropy-method-style population update is repeated across all denoising steps, making CDGS a plug-and-play sampler that scales compute adaptively via batch size B and resampling depth U.

## Results

On OGBench, CDGS achieves 87% success on PointMaze-Giant (vs. 29% for GSC and 68% for CompDiffuser), 85% on AntMaze-Giant (vs. 20% and 65%), and 51% on the Scene play-data task (vs. 8% and 13%), matching or exceeding inverse-RL baselines like HIQL (0% on Giant mazes) with far less training data. On TAMP tasks, CDGS reaches 0.84 success on Rearrangement Push Task 1 (vs. 0.76 for STAP CEM) and 0.42 on Rearrangement Memory Task 1 (vs. 0.00 for STAP CEM). For panorama generation, CDGS matches Sync-Diffusion on LPIPS (0.59 vs. 0.58) and style loss (1.38 vs. 1.39) while improving CLIP score (32.51 vs. 31.77). For 350-frame video generation, CDGS improves subject consistency to 91.67 (vs. 89.51 for GSC/Gen-L-Video) and prompt alignment to 26.13 (vs. 25.13).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDGS substantially outperforms naive compositional sampling (GSC) across all OGBench environments, with the largest gains on hard settings: +58 pts on PointMaze-Giant (87 vs. 29), +65 pts on AntMaze-Giant (85 vs. 20), and +43 pts on Scene play-data (51 vs. 8). Against CompDiffuser (CD), which trains an overlap-conditioned score function, CDGS wins on PointMaze-Giant (+19), AntMaze-Giant (+20), and Scene (+38), though CD edges ahead on Humanoid-Maze-Giant (64 vs. 55). CDGS matches or exceeds inverse-RL baselines (HIQL, GCIQL) on most tasks while requiring only short-horizon play/stitch data rather than goal-conditioned trajectories. On TAMP, CDGS (without oracle task plans) approaches privileged PDDL+CEM methods on Hook Reach (0.64 vs. 0.66) and Rearrangement Push (0.84 vs. 0.76), and dramatically outperforms them on Rearrangement Memory (0.42 vs. 0.00). For panorama generation, CDGS matches Sync-Diffusion on perceptual and style metrics while improving CLIP prompt alignment by ~0.7 points, and for 350-frame video it improves subject consistency over GSC/Gen-L-Video by 2.2 points and prompt alignment by 1.0 point, at a minor aesthetic cost (58.90 vs. 60.12).

## Relation to Prior Work

CDGS extends the compositional diffusion framework introduced by GSC (Mishra et al., CoRL 2023) and CompDiffuser (Luo et al., 2025), which approximate long-horizon distributions via score-averaging over overlapping local factors. The key departure is that these prior methods fail under multimodal local distributions due to mode-averaging, and CDGS addresses this by embedding a population-based guided search (iterative resampling + likelihood-based pruning) directly within the denoising loop, drawing on the Repaint resampling mechanism and cross-entropy-method-style candidate filtering. The paper also positions itself within the inference-time scaling literature for diffusion models, but uniquely applies search to a *composed* chain of distributions rather than a single standalone distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CDGS provides a practical, training-free plug-and-play sampler that resolves the mode-averaging bottleneck in compositional generative models, enabling short-horizon models to produce globally coherent long-horizon plans without requiring long-horizon training data or explicit task skeletons. The cross-domain generalization (robotics, panoramas, long video) suggests the guided-search-in-denoising paradigm is broadly applicable. Open questions include whether more sophisticated message-passing (beyond adjacent-factor resampling) or attention-based mechanisms could improve coherence over very long horizons, and whether the goal-state assumption can be relaxed to goal-generation or classifier-guided conditioning.
