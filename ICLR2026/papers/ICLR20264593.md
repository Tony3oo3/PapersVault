## Problem

Estimating the inverse temperature β of an Ising model from a single sample is well-studied in the untruncated setting, but hard constraints (truncation to satisfying assignments of a k-SAT formula) fragment the support and render standard concentration tools—log-Sobolev inequalities, Dobrushin conditions, and mixing-based bounds on Glauber dynamics—inapplicable. Prior work on single-sample learning under k-SAT truncation (Galanis et al., SODA'24) was restricted to product distributions, whereas the Ising model's quadratic Hamiltonian introduces long-range correlations that compound with the truncation-induced dependencies, making the strong convexity of the estimation objective fundamentally harder to establish.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes as input a single spin configuration σ ∈ {±1}^n drawn from the truncated Ising model, the underlying graph G with adjacency matrix A (maximum degree Δ = o(n^{1/6}), entries ±1/Δ), and the k-SAT formula Φ_{n,k,d} defining the truncation set S. It outputs an estimator β̂ of the true inverse temperature β* with high probability. The runtime is O(Δ³n log n), and the consistency rate is O(Δ³/√n), which reduces to the minimax O(1/√n) rate when Δ is bounded. The clause size k must satisfy k ≥ Ω(Δ³(1 + log(d²k+1))) for the guarantees to hold.

## Method

The estimator is the maximum pseudolikelihood estimate (MPLE), optimized via projected gradient descent on the normalized log-pseudolikelihood objective. The central technical contribution is proving that the Hessian φ₂(β;σ) = Σᵢ mᵢ²(σ)/cosh²(βmᵢ(σ)) · eᵢ(σ) is Ω(n/Δ³)-strongly convex with high probability, where the sum is over flippable variables. To establish this, the authors construct an independent set I ⊂ V via a random-bijection algorithm and the Lovász Local Lemma, guaranteeing that I intersects a linear fraction of variables in every clause of the truncated formula. Conditioning on V\I collapses the Ising model to a product distribution, enabling Chernoff-based concentration on flippability events. A coupling argument exploiting the edge structure of G then lower-bounds the squared magnetizations mᵢ²(σ) in probability, while exchangeable pairs control the variance of the gradient. The condition number of the objective is O(Δ³), yielding the stated runtime.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 2) establishes that for a truncated n-dimensional Ising model with a k-SAT formula truncation set and underlying graph of maximum degree Δ = o(n^{1/6}), there exists an O(Δ³n log n)-time algorithm producing an estimator β̂ satisfying |β̂ − β*| ≤ cΔ³/√n with probability at least 99%, provided k ≥ 4Δ³(1 + log(d²k+1)) / log(1 + exp(−2B)). When Δ = O(1), this yields the minimax-optimal O(1/√n) consistency rate. The algorithm uses projected gradient descent on the normalized log-pseudolikelihood, requiring O(Δ³ log n) iterations of O(n) time each.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The paper positions its contribution relative to three prior theoretical frameworks: (1) Chatterjee (2007) and Daskalakis et al. (2019) established pseudolikelihood consistency for untruncated Ising models, but their concentration tools (log-Sobolev inequalities, Dobrushin conditions) fail under truncation because the support becomes disconnected and Glauber dynamics are non-ergodic; (2) Galanis et al. (2024) handled truncated Boolean product distributions (tilted k-SAT) but not the quadratic Hamiltonian of the Ising model, which induces long-range correlations absent in product measures; (3) Galanis et al. (2024) also proved a lower bound showing estimation is information-theoretically impossible when k ≤ log(d) − log(k) + Θ(1), and the paper notes its upper bound is optimal up to a constant factor for bounded-degree graphs. The key technical advance is using the Lovász Local Lemma combined with an independent-set conditioning argument to prove strong convexity of the pseudolikelihood Hessian, a step that has no analogue in the product-distribution setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pseudolikelihood estimation framework for Ising models (Besag 1975; Chatterjee 2007; Daskalakis et al. STOC'19) to the setting of hard constraints represented by k-SAT formulas, building directly on the truncated product-distribution framework of Galanis et al. (SODA'24). The key departure is that the Ising model's quadratic Hamiltonian introduces long-range correlations absent in the product-distribution setting, and the truncation set fragments the support into disconnected islands, rendering the fast-mixing Glauber dynamics tools of Dagan et al. (2020) inapplicable. The authors overcome this by combining the Lovász Local Lemma (to guarantee sufficient flippability of spins) with an independent-set conditioning argument that collapses the Ising model to a product measure, enabling exchangeable-pairs variance bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first consistent single-sample estimator for the inverse temperature of a truncated Ising model, closing a gap between the untruncated Ising estimation literature and the k-SAT-truncated product-distribution literature. The result is optimal up to constants (matching the information-theoretic lower bound of Galanis et al. 2024) and achieves the minimax O(1/√n) rate for bounded-degree graphs. Open questions include whether the o(n^{1/6}) degree restriction can be removed, whether random Hamiltonians (e.g., Sherrington-Kirkpatrick) eliminate the Δ-dependence, and whether logistic-regression techniques for untruncated graphical models transfer to the constrained setting. Practical motivation arises from spatial transcriptomics and communication network design where hard feasibility constraints are inherent.
