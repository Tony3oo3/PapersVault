## Problem

SNNs trained with the mainstream direct-encoding + BPTT paradigm exhibit a previously uncharacterized vulnerability: a single backward pass on a batch drawn from a slightly different distribution triggers catastrophic model collapse, a phenomenon the authors term heterogeneous training vulnerability. Existing robustness methods assume homogeneous training (all-clean or all-perturbed data), leaving this realistic mixed-distribution failure mode unaddressed. The paper provides a theoretical explanation rooted in the abnormally large Hessian spectral radius induced by BPTT gradient accumulation combined with the repeated-input structure of direct encoding.

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet) or DVS event frames (128×128 for DVS-CIFAR10) and outputs a class label. During training, each input is repeated T times (typically T=4) via direct encoding to form the temporal spike sequence consumed by the LIF-based SNN backbone (VGG-11, VGG-16, or ResNet-18). DSD operates on the gradient tensor at each optimization step, performing an SVD-based projection that adds roughly 0.1 s per batch with no additional memory or inference overhead.

## Method

The core contribution is Dominant Singular Deflation (DSD), a hyperparameter-free gradient modification that orthogonally projects out the rank-one dominant singular component of the matrixized gradient before each parameter update. Theoretically, the paper proves that BPTT under LIF dynamics yields a Gauss-Newton Hessian whose spectral radius is O(T) (Theorem 1), and that direct encoding's stationary inputs make this bound tight at Θ(T) via a power-iteration alignment of the BPTT Jacobians (Theorem 2). By removing σ₁u₁v₁ᵀ from the gradient, DSD strictly reduces the maximal singular value of the effective Jacobian, thereby lowering the Hessian spectral radius and preventing the network from settling into sharp minima. The descent property is preserved because the projection is orthogonal in the Frobenius inner product, guaranteeing non-increasing loss at every step. Extensive experiments across six datasets and multiple attack settings (FGSM, PGD, APGD, black-box) show DSD outperforms SOTA SNN defenses while also preventing the collapse observed under heterogeneous training.

## Results

Under adversarial training (AT) on CIFAR-10, DSD achieves 74.43% FGSM and 44.38% PGD robust accuracy, outperforming the best baselines (DLIF: 56.71%/40.30%, HoSNN: 63.98%/43.33%). On TinyImageNet AT, DSD reaches 30.87% FGSM accuracy—a 22.68% absolute gain over the vanilla SNN baseline (8.19%). Under vanilla training, DSD achieves 23.81% FGSM on CIFAR-100 (vs. 9.60% for FEEL, the prior best) and 31.44% PGD on CIFAR-10 (vs. 28.35% for FEEL). On DVS-Gesture, DSD attains 90.28% FGSM and 55.21% PGD accuracy, far exceeding the vanilla SNN (39.24%/9.72%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSD consistently outperforms all six baselines on FGSM and PGD robust accuracy across every dataset, while sacrificing 2–5% clean accuracy. Against DLIF (the strongest prior SNN defense), DSD gains +17.72% FGSM on CIFAR-10 AT (74.43 vs. 56.71) and +7.08% on CIFAR-100 AT (43.91 vs. 36.83). Against HoSNN, DSD leads by +10.45% FGSM on CIFAR-10 AT and +22.68% FGSM on TinyImageNet AT (30.87 vs. 8.19, where no prior SNN defense was reported). FEEL, the only baseline evaluated on TinyImageNet under vanilla training, is surpassed by DSD by +9.91% FGSM and +7.49% PGD on CIFAR-100. On DVS datasets, DSD matches or exceeds SR (Liu et al., 2024b) on DVS-CIFAR10 (65.80 vs. 64.60 FGSM) and dramatically outperforms the vanilla SNN on DVS-Gesture (90.28 vs. 39.24 FGSM). In heterogeneous training, DSD exhibits the smallest accuracy drop (shortest floating bars in Fig. 5) compared to RAT, DLIF, and FEEL, retaining 33.70% FGSM accuracy on CIFAR-10 after a single poisoned batch versus 8.76% (RAT) and 3.50% (DLIF).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of SNN robustness work (stochastic gating, Lipschitz regularization, adaptive leak dynamics, frequency encoding) by identifying and addressing the root cause of vulnerability rather than its symptoms: the O(T) Hessian spectral radius growth induced by the combination of BPTT gradient accumulation and direct encoding's stationary inputs. The DSD method draws a conceptual parallel to spectral norm regularization and Parseval networks in the ANN literature, but operates directly in gradient space via a deterministic rank-1 deflation rather than constraining weight matrices. The paper also introduces the 'heterogeneous training vulnerability' as a distinct threat model—batch-level distribution shift during training—that prior SNN robustness work, which assumes homogeneous training, has not addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The heterogeneous training vulnerability is a practically critical finding: in real-world adversarial settings where data provenance is unpredictable, even a single poisoned batch can destroy an SNN, a failure mode far more severe than the gradual degradation seen in ANNs. The clean theoretical characterization (Theorems 1–2) gives practitioners a diagnostic tool and a principled target for intervention, while DSD's hyperparameter-free nature makes it immediately deployable without tuning. Key limitations include a consistent ~3–5% clean-accuracy trade-off (shared with prior SOTA methods) and the fact that the analysis is specific to LIF neurons with contractive dynamics; whether the spectral-radius mechanism and deflation strategy generalize to other neuron models or non-stationary encodings remains open.
