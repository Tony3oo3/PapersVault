## Problem

Current LLM agent tool planning methods rely on greedy, reactive selection that lacks foresight and compounds early errors irreversibly, while search-based alternatives evaluate hypothetical thoughts rather than executed actions, decoupling ranking from actual tool utility and failing to credit improvements back to earlier decisions. The paper addresses the need for a planning paradigm that is simultaneously forward-looking (anticipating tool usefulness before invocation) and outcome-grounded (scoring realized outputs), while remaining compute-efficient under fixed rollout budgets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261055/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a natural language query, a tool library where each tool is represented by a structured JSON tool card containing I/O schemas, domain tags, and example invocations, and an evolving dialogue context that accumulates intermediate results. It produces an optimal tool trajectory—a sequence of (tool, arguments, output) tuples—selected via MCTS, which is then fed to an Answer Predictor to generate the final response. Tool calls are cached by (action, args) key to avoid redundant invocations within a rollout, and persistent failures attach typed error tokens so downstream scoring handles them explicitly.

## Method

ToolTree casts multi-tool use as an MCTS-inspired search over executable trajectories, guided by two complementary LLM-judge signals: a pre-evaluation score r_pre ∈ [0,1] that forecasts a tool's usefulness before execution (injected into the UCT exploration bonus as a prior-augmented term) and a post-evaluation score r_post ∈ [0,1] that scores the grounded output after execution (driving exploitation via backward propagation of running-mean Q values). Bidirectional pruning keeps the tree compact: pre-pruning discards candidate actions with r_pre below a threshold τ_pre or outside the top-K before any tool call is made, while post-pruning marks executed branches non-expandable if r_post falls below τ_post, cutting unproductive continuations. The selection policy uses a modified UCT formula where the exploration bonus is weighted by r_pre, biasing early rollouts toward promising branches while retaining exploitation pressure from accumulated post-evaluation rewards. The entire pipeline is training-free and plug-and-play, requiring no task-specific fine-tuning, and terminates when the rollout budget is met or Q-value improvements plateau.

## Results

On closed-set tool planning, ToolTree with GPT-4o achieves 66.95 average F1 on GTA and 88.61 on m&m, outperforming the strongest baseline LATS (64.78 and 86.45) by 2.2 and 2.2 points respectively. On open-set tool planning, ToolTree with GPT-4o reaches 69.04 average pass rate on ToolBench and 74.50 on RestBench-TMDB, surpassing LATS (66.55 and 71.35) by 2.5 and 3.1 points. Across all four benchmarks and both model backends (GPT-4o and GPT-4o-mini), ToolTree consistently achieves the highest scores, with an average improvement of approximately 10% over the state-of-the-art planning paradigm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ToolTree outperforms all nine baselines across every benchmark and model backend. Against the closest competitor LATS (Zhou et al., 2024), ToolTree gains +2.2 F1 on GTA, +2.2 on m&m, +2.5 on ToolBench, and +3.1 on RestBench-TMDB with GPT-4o, with the gap widening under GPT-4o-mini (e.g., +2.0 on GTA, +1.1 on m&m). Against A*/ToolChain* (Zhuang et al., 2024), ToolTree leads by 4.4 F1 on GTA and 3.9 on m&m (GPT-4o), demonstrating that dual pre/post-evaluation with bidirectional pruning recovers from early heuristic misalignments that A* cannot. Greedy baselines (ReAct, CoT, Zero-Shot) trail search-based methods by 5–10 points, confirming the value of lookahead. On the 15-dataset multi-domain evaluation (Appendix A.1), ToolTree surpasses HuggingGPT (Shen et al., 2023) by 11.7 points and OctoTools (Lu et al., 2025) by 5.7 points on average under GPT-4o-mini, with the largest gains on domain-specific tasks like PathVQA (+22.2 over few-shot) and Game-of-24 (+16.8). On open-set benchmarks, DFSDT (Qin et al., 2023) lags ToolTree by 6.5 points on ToolBench and 6.7 on RestBench-TMDB (GPT-4o), as its depth-first symbolic planner lacks breadth-aware priors and post-execution pruning.

## Relation to Prior Work

ToolTree extends the MCTS-based planning paradigm introduced by LATS (Zhou et al., 2024) and the A*-search approach of ToolChain* (Zhuang et al., 2024) by injecting two complementary LLM-judged signals—pre-execution prior and post-execution reward—directly into the UCT selection and backpropagation steps. Unlike LATS, which lacks a pre-execution gate, or ToolChain*, which relies on a single static heuristic, ToolTree separates foresight (r_pre in the exploration bonus and expansion gate) from hindsight (r_post as the grounded backpropagation reward), enabling bidirectional pruning that neither prior method supports. The approach is also positioned against greedy reactive planners (ReAct, CoT) and other tree-search variants (ToT, Best-First), carving out a niche where search is explicitly grounded in executed tool outputs rather than hypothetical LLM thoughts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ToolTree offers a training-free, plug-and-play planning module that can be dropped into existing agent frameworks (demonstrated with LangChain and MetaGPT), making it practically accessible without fine-tuning. The ~10% average gain over SOTA across four benchmarks and the <2% degradation when scaling from 14 to 10,014 tools suggest the dual-evaluation mechanism generalizes well. However, the method's reliance on LLM-as-judge for both pre- and post-evaluation introduces a non-trivial inference cost and a potential vulnerability to judge bias, even if the MCTS aggregation partially smooths individual misjudgments. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to non-tool sequential decision tasks, how it behaves under adversarial or noisy tool outputs, and whether the fixed pruning thresholds (τ_pre=0.3, τ_post=0.4) remain optimal across domains.
