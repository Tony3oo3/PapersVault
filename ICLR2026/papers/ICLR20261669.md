## Problem

Prior work on Loss of Plasticity (LoP) in continual learning has cataloged symptoms—rank collapse, dead units, weight explosion—but has not provided a mechanistic explanation for why gradient descent cannot escape these degraded states. The fundamental question remains: if LoP is merely a bad configuration, why do gradients not steer the model back toward useful regions? This paper identifies the missing geometric structure that makes LoP states persistent and irreversible under standard optimization.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on standard RGB image datasets (Tiny ImageNet 64×64 for continual learning with 40 sequential 5-class tasks; CIFAR-10 32×32 for cloning experiments) and a synthetic bit-flipping online regression task. Rather than producing a single prediction, the method outputs a diagnostic profile of the network's plasticity state: the effective rank (Rényi-2 or Shannon) of hidden representations, the fraction of dead (saturated) and duplicate (cloned) units per layer, and a cloning R² score quantifying how much variance within a cloned block is explained by the block mean. The theoretical outputs are formal manifold definitions and rank-gain bounds parameterized by the activation's Hermite coefficients.

## Method

The paper formalizes LoP as entrapment of gradient flow within invariant affine sub-manifolds of the parameter space, proving that once the trajectory enters such a manifold, the gradient is strictly tangent to it and escape is impossible under GD/SGD/Adam. Two concrete mechanisms are identified: frozen-unit manifolds (where activation saturation zeros all incoming gradients) and cloned-unit manifolds (where row/column equitability constraints on weight blocks make forward, backward, and gradient signals identical within each block, so updates preserve the symmetry). A key theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) derives a lower bound on Rényi-2 rank gain across a nonlinear layer in terms of a decorrelation potential Ψ(C) and activation-specific strength γ_φ, showing that rank expansion drives correlations toward the fixed points 0 or ±1—precisely the geometry of orthogonal subspaces and cloned units. This reveals a fundamental rank-plasticity tension: the same low-rank compression and neural collapse that improve static generalization actively construct the LoP manifold. Empirically, the authors validate the cloning invariance across MLPs, ResNets, and ViTs, show that normalization layers prevent frozen-unit formation by keeping pre-activations in the dynamic range, and demonstrate that symmetry-breaking perturbations (noisy SGD, dropout, Continual Backpropagation) can destabilize the manifold and restore plasticity.

## Results

In cloning experiments on MLPs (CIFAR-10), the cloning R² score remains at ~1.0 under standard SGD, confirming that cloned units stay perfectly trapped on the LoP manifold; however, even a single step of Noisy SGD with relative noise magnitude 0.01 initiates escape, and Dropout similarly breaks the symmetry. In continual learning on Tiny ImageNet (40 sequential 5-class tasks, 20K total steps), the fraction of dead or duplicate units increases monotonically while effective rank of hidden representations degrades, with the effect most pronounced in ResNet and ViT architectures. In the bit-flipping regression benchmark (5M samples), switching from SGD to Continual Backpropagation (CBP) at the 2.5M mark reverses the rank collapse and reduces online training loss, demonstrating recovery of plasticity. Normalization layers (BN/LN) maintain significantly higher effective rank throughout training compared to unnormalized variants, preventing the onset of frozen and duplicate units.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper, so it does not benchmark a proposed method against competing baselines in the traditional sense. Instead, it compares optimization and architectural strategies for their ability to prevent or escape LoP manifolds. Standard SGD is shown to be unable to escape either frozen-unit or cloned-unit manifolds once entered, while Noisy SGD (even at very low noise levels) and Dropout both break the symmetry and allow escape in controlled cloning experiments. For recovery from naturally emerging LoP in the bit-flipping task, CBP outperforms plain SGD by restoring representational rank and reducing loss after the switch point. For prevention, Batch Normalization and Layer Normalization consistently outperform unnormalized architectures in maintaining effective rank and suppressing dead/duplicate unit fractions across MLP, CNN, ResNet, and ViT backbones. The paper notes that Dropout, while effective at breaking artificial cloning symmetry, can be detrimental in natural continual-learning settings where it may hinder consolidation of new knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior LoP literature (Dohare et al. 2024, Nikishin et al. 2022, Lyle et al. 2023) that characterizes symptoms like rank collapse and dead units without explaining why gradient descent cannot escape these states. It draws on geometric singularity theory (Fukumizu and Amari 2000), stochastic collapse (Chen et al. 2023), and neural collapse (Papyan et al. 2020), but repurposes these into a unified dynamical-systems framework where LoP is formally defined as entrapment in invariant sub-manifolds. The cloning manifold result generalizes prior singularity analyses by proving invariance under the relaxed condition of row/column equitability rather than strict weight identity, and the rank-gain theorem builds on the Hermite-expansion framework of Joudaki and Hofmann (2025) to connect nonlinearity-driven decorrelation to the emergence of frozen units.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central insight—that the very mechanisms promoting generalization in static settings (neural collapse, low-rank compression) actively steer networks into LoP manifolds—reframes the stability-plasticity dilemma as an inherent tension rather than a fixable bug, suggesting continual learning requires active preservation of representational diversity. Open questions include whether non-linear LoP manifolds exist in practice, the curvature/stability landscape of these manifolds (which determines escape difficulty), and whether a model recovered from LoP can explore parameter space as effectively as a freshly initialized one. The modular cloning theorem provides a practical certificate framework for modern architectures (ResNets, ViTs), potentially enabling systematic detection of LoP onset during training.
