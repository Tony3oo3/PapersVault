## Problem

Multivariate time-series imputation under heavy missingness requires both robust temporal feature extraction from sparse observations and selective cross-variable information transfer, yet existing methods excel at one while compromising the other. When temporal features are corrupted by missingness, naive cross-variable transfer amplifies reconstruction errors, and treating each variable as a single token prevents isolating corrupted features from reliable ones during information exchange.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261344/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{M×T} (M variables, T=96 timesteps, or T=48 for PhysioNet) and a binary observation mask Ω ∈ {0,1}^{M×T}, which are stacked into a two-channel input for the embedding layer. Per-instance normalization statistics (mean, std) are computed solely from observed values and stored for final denormalization. The output is the full imputed series, with evaluation metrics (MSE, MAE) computed only on artificially masked positions to ensure consistent comparison across methods.

## Method

T1 is a CNN-Transformer hybrid where shared depthwise convolutions extract multi-scale temporal features from each variable's incomplete observations, and a novel Channel-Head Binding (CHead Attention) mechanism creates a one-to-one correspondence between CNN channels and attention heads for cross-variable information transfer. Each attention head processes only its bound channel across all variables, enabling feature-level selectivity: when missingness prevents a channel from observing its specialized pattern, the corresponding head down-weights that pathway while other channels remain unaffected. The architecture comprises Mask-Aware Embedding (strided 1D conv on stacked series+mask), stacked T1 blocks (dual-kernel DWConv QKV projection → CHead Attention → convolutional FFN), and a parameter-free 1D PixelShuffle upsampler for reconstruction. Training is self-supervised with 40% random masking and MSE loss on masked positions only, using a single consistent hyperparameter configuration (128 channels, 4 blocks, kernel sizes 71/5 and 31/5) across all 11 datasets regardless of variable count.

## Results

On 9 benchmark datasets under point missing (averaged across 10%–70% missing ratios), T1 achieves an average MSE of 0.027 and MAE of 0.084, reducing MSE by 46% versus the second-best method PatchTST (0.050) and by 56% versus the specialized imputer PSW-I. At the extreme 70% missing ratio, T1's average MSE (0.049) is nearly half that of PatchTST (0.092). Under block missing (simulating sensor failures), T1 outperforms PatchTST by 48% in average MSE, and on the naturally missing PhysioNet2012 dataset (up to 94% total missingness), T1 improves over DLinear by 23% in average MSE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T1 outperforms all 12 baselines on every dataset in the main point-missing evaluation. The closest competitor is PatchTST (Nie et al., 2023), which T1 beats by 46% in average MSE (0.027 vs. 0.050); the gap widens to 47% at 70% missingness (0.049 vs. 0.092). Among specialized imputation models, SAITS (Du et al., 2023) and ImputeFormer (Nie et al., 2024) are the strongest, yet T1 still leads by 39% and 39% in average MSE respectively. CSDI (Tashiro et al., 2021) and BRITS (Cao et al., 2018) suffer catastrophic failures on the Illness dataset (MSE > 500 and > 0.4 respectively), while T1 maintains 0.038. The optimal-transport method PSW-I (Wang et al., 2025) is outperformed by 56% in average MSE. Against the diffusion-based SSSD (Alcaraz & Strodthoff, 2023), T1 achieves 6–56% MSE reductions depending on the dataset, with the largest gains on ETTm2 (83%) and Illness (82%). General forecasting models (TimeMixer++, ModernTCN, iTransformer, TimesNet, DLinear) are consistently outperformed, with DLinear being the weakest general model (0.210 avg MSE) and TimeMixer++ the strongest among them (0.075 avg MSE), still 2.8× worse than T1.

## Relation to Prior Work

T1 synthesizes two established paradigms—depthwise convolution for multi-scale temporal feature extraction (from ModernTCN) and variable-axis attention for cross-variable fusion (from iTransformer)—but departs from both by introducing Channel-Head Binding, a one-to-one correspondence between CNN channels and attention heads that neither prior work employs. Unlike iTransformer, which compresses each variable's entire temporal sequence into a single token, T1 preserves per-channel temporal structure so that attention can selectively down-weight corrupted features. The mask-aware embedding and PixelShuffle upsampling are practical refinements rather than conceptual departures, and the overall architecture is positioned as a task-aligned hybrid that avoids the compromises of time-axis, variable-axis, and dual-axis tokenization schemes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 46% average MSE reduction over the second-best baseline, combined with a single hyperparameter configuration across 11 datasets and generalization from a 0.4 training mask to 0.1–0.7 test ratios, makes T1 a practically deployable imputation model that removes the need for per-dataset or per-missing-ratio tuning. The representation analysis showing that attention weights adapt to which temporal patterns remain observable (not just the missing ratio) provides mechanistic evidence for why the architecture works under structured missingness. Open questions include whether the one-to-one binding scales efficiently to very high channel counts, whether the approach extends to online/streaming imputation, and whether the depthwise-convolution-based QKV projection can be replaced by more expressive temporal encoders without losing the channel-alignment property.
