## Problem

Existing sketch-conditioned generation methods (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, etc.) are designed for pixel-aligned edge maps and fail to interpret true freehand scene sketches, which contain inherent abstraction, distorted proportions, and ambiguity. A fundamental obstacle is that freehand sketches lack pixel-aligned ground-truth images, making standard denoising objectives ill-posed. The paper tackles the underexplored problem of generating photorealistic images from freehand scene sketches while preserving semantic fidelity to the sketch.

## Input / Output

The method takes a freehand scene sketch (multi-object, potentially abstract) and a text prompt as input, and produces a photorealistic RGB image as output. The sketch is encoded by a fine-tuned CLIP-based semantic encoder into 512×14×14 feature maps, while the diffusion backbone (SD2.1 or SDXL) operates on 4×128×128 VAE latents. Modulation is applied only during the top 10% of diffusion timesteps (high-noise regime), and training uses a 50/50 mix of freehand sketches from FS-COCO and algorithmically generated synthetic sketches.

## Method

The core contribution is a lightweight encoder-decoder modulation network (9.4M parameters) that takes semantic sketch features, the noisy latent z_t, and the predicted noise ε_θ as separate inputs through dedicated downsampling branches, fuses them, and outputs scale S_t and shift B_t maps that modulate the noise prediction via ε' = ε⊙(1+S_t)+B_t. Rather than using VAE-encoded or raw pixel sketch features, the method exploits semantic features from a CLIP-based sketch encoder (fine-tuned on its last three layers) to capture object identity and spatial layout. A novel attention supervision loss bypasses the need for pixel-aligned ground-truth images by using the semantic encoder to compute per-object spatial likelihoods, which supervise cross-attention maps in the denoising U-Net, encouraging generated content to respect sketch semantics. The total objective combines the standard diffusion noise loss, the attention alignment loss, and L1/variance regularization on the modulation maps, with training restricted to high-noise timesteps where semantic structure is established.

## Results

On the FS-COCO test set of 475 freehand scene sketches, the proposed method achieves the best FID (121.97), highest CLIP sketch-image similarity (1.291), and lowest LPIPS (0.739) among all compared baselines. The closest competitor, ControlNext SDXL (zero-shot), records FID 134.09, CLIP 0.909, and LPIPS 0.774, while ControlNet SD2.1 (zero-shot) achieves FID 135.60, CLIP 1.136, and LPIPS 0.773. In a user study with 23 participants, the method is preferred over ControlNet SD2.1 in 88.8% of pairwise comparisons for the best trade-off between photorealism and sketch adherence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on every metric (FID, CLIP similarity, LPIPS) in the zero-shot or standard evaluation setting. Against ControlNet SD2.1 (FID 135.60, CLIP 1.136), the method improves FID by ~13.6 points and CLIP by 0.155, while also reducing LPIPS from 0.773 to 0.739. T2I-Adapter performs worst among pixel-aligned methods, with CLIP similarity dropping to −0.203 (s=0.8, τ=0.2) or 0.213 (s=0.8, τ=0.4) in zero-shot, indicating near-complete failure to align with freehand sketch semantics; even after fine-tuning with the attention loss, its CLIP score reaches only 0.454. ControlNext SDXL produces visually appealing images but achieves CLIP 0.909, well below the proposed 1.291, reflecting weaker structural adherence. The training-free FreeControl method (FID 141.63, CLIP 1.089) is outperformed by a margin of ~20 FID points and 0.2 CLIP. Finally, against the universal editing model Flux.1 Kontext [dev], the method achieves lower FID (121.97 vs. 132.49) and higher CLIP similarity (1.291 vs. 1.226), confirming superior sketch-to-image fidelity even against a model trained on a much larger dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of spatially-conditioned diffusion models (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, ControlNext) by shifting from pixel-aligned edge-map conditioning to semantic-aware generation for true freehand scene sketches. It builds directly on Ham et al.'s (2023) noise-modulation idea but replaces the VAE/convolutional sketch encoders with a fine-tuned CLIP-based semantic sketch encoder, and introduces a dedicated encoder-decoder modulation network operating in latent space. The key conceptual departure is the attention supervision loss, which sidesteps the fundamental absence of pixel-aligned ground truth for freehand sketches by supervising cross-attention maps with semantic likelihoods from the sketch encoder rather than relying on denoising loss alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: most 'sketch-to-image' methods in the literature actually target edge maps, while genuine freehand sketches (with abstraction, distortion, and missing detail) remain poorly handled. The attention supervision loss is a broadly applicable technique for training spatially-conditioned diffusion models when pixel-aligned ground truth is unavailable, potentially extending to other abstract or imprecise conditioning signals. Limitations include reliance on the relatively small FS-COCO dataset (10K images), the need for a text prompt alongside the sketch, and evaluation limited to a single scene-sketch benchmark. Open questions include whether the semantic conditioning approach generalizes to video generation, more complex multi-object compositions, or other abstract visual inputs like rough architectural drawings.
