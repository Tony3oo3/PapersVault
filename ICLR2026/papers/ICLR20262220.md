## Problem

Diffusion models have been proposed as learned priors for CT reconstruction, but no systematic benchmark exists to evaluate them under realistic conditions involving correlated Poisson noise, ring artifacts, sparse views, and real-world acquisition imperfections. Practical CT pipelines deviate substantially from the idealized linear model due to nonlinear preprocessing, geometry dependence, and misaligned value ranges, making it unclear how well diffusion-based methods transfer from simulation to deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262220/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

All methods consume sinograms (projection measurements) paired with a known forward operator A defined by the acquisition geometry, and produce 2D reconstructed CT slices. The medical and industrial datasets use 512×512 slices with 20–80 projection angles across five controlled configurations (noise-free, mild noise, heavy noise, noise+ring artifacts, limited-angle), while the real-world synchrotron dataset uses 768×768 slices reconstructed from 60/100/200 subsampled projections out of 1200. All diffusion methods share identical pixel-space and latent-space diffusion backbones trained on the same data, ensuring fair comparison across reconstruction strategies.

## Method

DM4CT is a benchmark rather than a new algorithm: it organizes ten recent diffusion-based CT reconstruction methods (MCG, DPS, PSLD, PGDM, DDS, ReSample, DMPlug, Reddiff, HybridReg, DiffStateGrad) under a unified taxonomy based on how they enforce data consistency (gradient steering, optimization steps, plug-and-play, pseudoinverse guidance, variational Bayesian), and evaluates them alongside seven baselines (FBP, SIRT, ADMM-PDTV, FISTA-SBTV, DIP, INR, R2Gaussian, SwinIR) on three datasets. The key technical contribution is the acquisition of a high-resolution synchrotron CT dataset (two rock samples, 679×768×768, parallel-beam circular trajectory at 24 keV) that enables slice-wise 2D reconstruction under realistic experimental conditions, substantially reducing computational overhead compared to 3D cone-beam geometries. Evaluation spans quantitative metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), computational cost (time, GPU memory), null-space decomposition to quantify prior contribution, uncertainty quantification via multi-sample MCG, and a preliminary downstream segmentation task using SAM.

## Results

Across all three datasets (medical, industrial, synchrotron), diffusion-based methods generally outperform classical (FBP, SIRT) and model-based iterative (ADMM-PDTV, FISTA-SBTV) approaches in PSNR/SSIM, particularly under sparse-view and noisy conditions. However, the fully supervised SwinIR consistently achieves the highest scores (e.g., 32.45/0.88 on medical config i, 33.75/0.76 on real-world 200 projections), surpassing all diffusion methods. Among diffusion methods, no single approach dominates across all configurations; INR is competitive with diffusion methods in noiseless settings (33.21/0.86 on medical config i) and on the real-world dataset. Performance degrades notably on the real-world synchrotron data compared to simulated data, with diffusion methods showing distribution-shift sensitivity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SwinIR (supervised transformer) is the strongest baseline overall, achieving the highest PSNR/SSIM in nearly every configuration (e.g., 30.79/0.85 on medical config iv vs. best diffusion ~29.12/0.74 for MCG; 33.75/0.76 on real-world 200 projections vs. best diffusion 28.43/0.56 for Reddiff). INR matches or exceeds most diffusion methods in noiseless settings (33.21/0.86 on medical config i, surpassing all diffusion methods) and remains competitive on the real-world dataset (28.01/0.50), suggesting that per-image optimization with implicit priors is a strong alternative to learned diffusion priors. Among the ten diffusion methods, no single approach wins across all configurations: DDS leads on industrial config i (22.87/0.54) but collapses under Poisson noise (18.25/0.20 on medical config iv), while MCG and PGDM are more robust to noise. Classical methods (FBP, SIRT) and MBIR (ADMM-PDTV, FISTA-SBTV) are clearly outperformed by diffusion methods in sparse/noisy regimes (e.g., FBP drops to 14.50/0.13 on medical config iv vs. MCG at 29.12/0.74), but FISTA-SBTV remains competitive in moderate-noise settings. On the real-world synchrotron data, all methods show reduced performance relative to simulated data, with diffusion methods particularly affected by distribution shift and limited training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not propose a new reconstruction algorithm but instead carves new ground as the first systematic benchmark of diffusion models for CT reconstruction. It repurposes the established taxonomy of diffusion-based inverse problem solvers (data consistency gradients, optimization steps, plug-and-play, pseudoinverse guidance, variational Bayesian) and applies it uniformly across ten recent methods (DPS, MCG, PSLD, PGDM, DDS, ReSample, DMPlug, Reddiff, HybridReg, DiffStateGrad) under identical backbones and forward models. The work departs from prior diffusion-for-inverse-problems studies by emphasizing the practical CT-specific challenges—Poisson noise, ring artifacts, value-range misalignment, and geometry-dependent computational cost—that make CT a harder testbed than the linear, noise-free settings typically assumed in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides the first head-to-head comparison of diffusion methods against classical, model-based, and supervised baselines across medical, industrial, and real-world synchrotron CT, revealing that no single diffusion strategy dominates and that all diffusion methods still trail the supervised SwinIR baseline. The newly acquired high-resolution synchrotron dataset (679×768×768, 1200 projections) is a rare, publicly available resource for evaluating methods under realistic experimental conditions. Key open questions remain: generalizability across scanners and acquisition geometries, the clinical relevance of diffusion reconstructions (the preliminary segmentation study shows diffusion methods can underperform blurrier classical methods on downstream tasks despite higher PSNR/SSIM), and whether flow-based generative models or INR-diffusion hybrids can close the gap to supervised approaches.
