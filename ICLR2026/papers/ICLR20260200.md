## Problem

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has driven strong reasoning gains but depends on ever-growing quantities of gold labels, which become unavailable for harder or open-ended tasks. Existing label-free alternatives like TTRL and SRT rely on majority voting across rollouts as a pseudo-label, but majority votes are frequently spurious—especially on hard problems where correct answers appear only in minority rollouts—leading to training collapse and degraded performance. The paper asks how to convert the absence of gold labels into robust, self-derived learning signals that prevent overcommitment to unreliable consensus.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled prompts (no reference answers) and, for each prompt, samples n=16 rollouts from the current policy at temperature 1.0. A frozen reference model is used once offline to precompute per-prompt confidence weights, which remain fixed throughout training. The output is an updated policy model trained via GRPO-style policy optimization; no external reward model or human annotation is required at any point.

## Method

RESTRAIN modifies the GRPO objective with three self-penalizing mechanisms. First, pseudo-label weighting replaces the single majority-voted answer with a weighted sum over all distinct answers, where each answer's weight is a Gaussian-shaped function of its empirical frequency (controlled by bias σ=0.5), producing a soft consensus that suppresses rare spurious answers while retaining minority correct signals. Second, negative rollout penalization triggers when the majority count falls below a threshold κ=3: all rewards are zeroed and a uniform negative advantage offset δ=1 is applied, so low-consistency prompts contribute only negative updates that discourage the model from reinforcing unreliable reasoning paths. Third, prompt-level weighting scales each prompt's loss by a precomputed confidence score from a frozen reference model, down-weighting prompts where the model shows low self-consistency. The final loss is L_RESTRAIN = u_x · Σ_j w_j · L_GRPO(x, a_j; θ) with modified advantages, integrating all three penalties directly into the policy gradient.

## Results

On DAPO-14k-Math, RESTRAIN with Qwen3-4B-Base achieves 51.0% average Pass@1 across six benchmarks, trailing the gold-label GRPO upper bound (51.4%) by only 0.4 pp while using no gold labels. It surpasses the gold-label GRPO on MMLU_STEM (80.9% vs. 73.7%) and GPQA-Diamond (40.2% vs. 38.7%), demonstrating strong cross-domain generalization. On OctoThinker Hybrid-8B-Base, RESTRAIN reaches 35.8% average, outperforming TTRL by +5.2 pp and achieving +140.7% relative improvement on AIME25. In test-time RL on Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, RESTRAIN achieves 41.4% average Pass@1, outperforming ETMR by +4.1 pp and TTRL by +6.1 pp.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RESTRAIN outperforms TTRL (Zuo et al., 2025) on every benchmark for both models: +8.8 pp average on Qwen3-4B-Base (51.0% vs. 42.2%) and +5.2 pp on OctoThinker (35.8% vs. 30.6%), with the largest relative gains on AIME25 (+115.7% and +140.7% respectively) and MMLU_STEM (+36.2% and +26.5%). Against SRT (Shafayat et al., 2025), RESTRAIN wins by +7.9–8.2 pp over both SRT variants (easy-prompt and offline-majority) on Qwen3-4B-Base, and by +3.0–5.2 pp on OctoThinker. In the test-time RL setting on Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, RESTRAIN beats ETMR (Liu et al., 2025a) by +4.6 pp on AMC23 and +7.9 pp on MATH-500, while essentially tying on AIME24 (−0.2 pp). Most notably, RESTRAIN nearly matches the gold-label GRPO upper bound (Shao et al., 2024) at 51.0% vs. 51.4% average on Qwen3-4B-Base, and actually surpasses it on MMLU_STEM (80.9% vs. 73.7%) and GPQA-Diamond (40.2% vs. 38.7%), suggesting that self-penalization reduces domain-specific overfitting inherent in gold-label supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

RESTRAIN builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its policy optimization backbone, modifying the reward and advantage computation to operate without gold labels. It departs from TTRL (Zuo et al., 2025), which collapses all probability mass onto the majority-voted answer, and SRT (Shafayat et al., 2025), which filters to high-consensus prompts or uses offline majority labels, by instead maintaining a frequency-weighted distribution over all predicted answers and explicitly penalizing low-consistency rollouts. The self-penalization mechanism draws conceptual parallels to unlikelihood training (Welleck et al., 2019; Li et al., 2019) and negative sampling rejection (Zhu et al., 2025), but applies these ideas at the rollout and prompt level within a GRPO objective rather than at the token level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-parity with gold-label GRPO (51.0% vs. 51.4% on Qwen3-4B-Base) and the surprising cross-domain generalization (surpassing gold-label on MMLU_STEM and GPQA-Diamond) suggest that distributional self-penalization may be a more robust learning signal than outcome-based gold rewards for reasoning. Open questions include whether the fixed hyperparameters (σ=0.5, δ=1, κ=3) transfer to larger models or fundamentally harder tasks where even Pass@n degrades, and whether the approach extends beyond math to open-ended reasoning where answer extraction is less clean. The training stability advantage over TTRL (which collapses after ~50 steps) is practically important for scaling label-free RL to longer training runs.
