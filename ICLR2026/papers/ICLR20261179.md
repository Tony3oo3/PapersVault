## Problem

Virtual try-off (VTOFF)—recovering standardized, flat-lay product images from photos of clothed individuals—remains largely overlooked compared to its forward counterpart, virtual try-on. Existing VTOFF methods repurpose VTON pipelines by simply reversing input-output roles, leading to two key failures: (i) exclusive reliance on visual cues from a single photo creates ambiguity in garment identity and category, and (ii) generated images lose fine-grained textures and structural details, limiting real-world applicability for e-commerce and dataset curation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261179/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clothed person image (1024×768 RGB), a binary garment segmentation mask, and a structural text caption (generated by Qwen2.5-VL, deliberately excluding color/texture attributes to avoid information leakage) as input, and produces a flat, in-shop-style garment image at the same resolution. At training time, captions are derived from the ground-truth garment image; at inference, they are extracted from the person image alone, making the pipeline fully applicable to in-the-wild scenarios. The mask serves as a hard spatial discriminator for garment category (upper-body, lower-body, dress), while the text provides complementary soft semantic cues about structural attributes like neckline and sleeve length.

## Method

TEMU-VTOFF employs a dual-DiT architecture built on Stable Diffusion 3 medium, where a feature-extractor DiT (F_E) encodes the person image and outputs clean key/value features at timestep t=0, while a generator DiT (F_D) synthesizes the garment using a novel Multimodal Hybrid Attention (MHA) module that concatenates text embeddings (CLIP+T5), noisy latent tokens, and extractor features along the sequence dimension, enabling three-way cross-attention between language, latent, and person representations. A pooled CLIP text vector is additionally injected via AdaLN modulation for high-level category conditioning. To mitigate high-frequency detail loss, a lightweight garment aligner module (a small CNN) projects the 8th DiT block's token grid into DINOv2 feature space, and a cosine-similarity alignment loss enforces patch-wise consistency with the target garment—this module is discarded at inference. Training is two-stage: F_E is first trained alone with a standard flow-matching diffusion loss, then F_D is trained jointly with the diffusion loss plus the alignment loss (λ=0.5), using asynchronous timesteps (F_E at t=0, F_D at t>0) to ensure the conditioning signal is noise-free.

## Results

On VITON-HD (upper-body only), TEMU-VTOFF achieves the best FID (8.71), KID (1.11), DISTS (18.04), and PSNR (13.38) among all compared methods. On the multi-category Dress Code dataset, TEMU-VTOFF similarly leads across most metrics and garment categories, with the largest gains over single-category baselines appearing on lower-body and dress subsets. Cross-dataset generalization (train on one dataset, test on the other) confirms robustness, with FID of 20.39 (Dress Code→VITON-HD) and 18.63 (VITON-HD→Dress Code upper-body), outperforming MGT, TryOffDiff, and TryOffAnyone in both transfer directions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEMU-VTOFF outperforms all six baselines on FID and KID on VITON-HD, achieving 8.71 FID and 1.11 KID versus the next-best One Model for All (9.12 FID, 1.49 KID) and Voost (10.06 FID, 2.48 KID). Against the closest multi-category competitor MGT, TEMU-VTOFF wins decisively on distributional metrics (FID 8.71 vs 21.90, KID 1.11 vs 8.90) and DISTS (18.04 vs 24.70), though MGT reports a slightly higher SSIM (78.10 vs 77.21). The only metric where a baseline edges ahead is LPIPS, where One Model for All reports 22.50 vs TEMU-VTOFF's 28.44; however, the authors note this value is cited from the original paper without public checkpoints, and their qualitative analysis (Appendix E) shows LPIPS can diverge from human judgment while DISTS correlates more reliably. On the multi-category Dress Code dataset, TEMU-VTOFF's advantage over single-category methods (TryOffDiff, TryOffAnyone) is most pronounced on lower-body garments, where the absence of text and mask conditioning causes the largest performance drops in ablations. Cross-dataset experiments further confirm that TEMU-VTOFF generalizes better than MGT, TryOffDiff, and TryOffAnyone under domain shift in both transfer directions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Diffusion 3's DiT backbone and flow-matching formulation, but departs fundamentally from prior VTOFF methods (TryOffDiff, TryOffAnyone, MGT, Voost, One Model For All) that either repurpose VTON pipelines by reversing input-output roles or handle only single garment categories. The key architectural departure is a dedicated dual-DiT design where a feature extractor (F_E) produces clean t=0 key/value features from the person image, which are injected into a generator DiT (F_D) via a novel Multimodal Hybrid Attention module that jointly attends over latent, text, and extractor tokens. The garment aligner module draws on the representation-alignment idea from REPA (Yu et al., 2025), using DINOv2 features as a training-time supervision signal to preserve high-frequency details.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VTOFF has direct practical value for e-commerce (auto-generating catalog images from worn photos), large-scale dataset curation, and training downstream VTON models, as demonstrated by the data-augmentation experiment where synthetic garments improve CatVTON's FID. The paper's finding that a purpose-built architecture substantially outperforms repurposed VTON pipelines suggests that task-specific design matters more than generic multi-task unification for garment-centric reconstruction. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to occluded or heavily wrinkled garments, whether the class imbalance for lower-body items can be resolved with better data, and whether the dual-DiT overhead is justified at scale compared to lighter alternatives.
