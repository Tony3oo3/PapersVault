## Problem

Learned heuristic functions for A* search typically violate admissibility (never overestimating true cost-to-go), thereby losing the optimality guarantee that makes A* reliable. Moreover, the theoretical foundations of heuristic learning—particularly how many training samples are needed for a learned heuristic to generalize across an entire state graph—remain underexplored, with existing bounds scaling poorly with graph size.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse state representation vector encoding the position and rotation of each cubie (corner, edge, or center) on a 3×3 Rubik's Cube and produces a probability distribution over discrete cost-to-go classes {0, 1, …, ℓ} via a softmax output layer; the heuristic value is the argmax of this distribution. Training data is drawn from pattern databases (PDBs) that store exact h* values for every abstract state, with mini-batches constructed by uniform sampling within each heuristic class to handle severe class imbalance (e.g., >86% of 6-edge PDB states fall in classes 7–8).

## Method

The paper introduces Cross-Entropy Admissibility (CEA), a loss function that reallocates probability mass across all admissible classes k ≤ h*_i with a decaying weight (k/h*_i)^β, plus a small cross-entropy penalty (scaled by η ≪ 1) that sharpens the distribution around the true class, ensuring the unique global optimum is full admissibility at maximal heuristic value. The heuristic is realized by a ResNet with ReLU hidden layers and a softmax final layer, trained as an ordinal classification task on PDB data. On the theory side, the paper derives pseudo-dimension bounds for A* performance classes that improve from O(n log n) to O(m log n) via PDB abstractions, and further to bounds depending on network width and depth (LW log(U+ℓ)) rather than graph size, using piecewise-polynomial decomposition of ReLU networks. A new suboptimality bound for A* with node reopenings—C_h(x) − C*(x) ≤ max_{v∈P_opt}[h(v) − h*(v)]—yields tighter generalization guarantees, and the framework extends to the first generalization bounds for goal-dependent heuristics.

## Results

On the 8-corner Rubik's Cube PDB, the CEA loss achieves a fully admissible heuristic (overestimation rate 3×10⁻⁷) while matching the original PDB's average heuristic of 8.76, with a 23.32× compression (1.89 MB model). Across all four PDBs, CEA reduces the overestimation rate by approximately 10⁴× compared to standard cross-entropy (CE) loss (e.g., 2×10⁻⁵ vs. 1.4×10⁻² on 7-edge; 3×10⁻⁶ vs. 15×10⁻² on Δ(6,4)-edge). The learned CEA heuristics also exceed the average heuristic of size-matched compressed PDBs on every PDB (e.g., 7.45 vs. 6.83 on 7-edge; 8.76 vs. 6.84 on 8-corner), indicating that CEA preserves more information than classical min-compression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed CEA loss against two baselines: (1) the same ResNet architecture trained with standard cross-entropy (CE) loss, and (2) a compressed PDB built with the min-compression technique (Felner et al., 2007) at the same memory budget. Against the CE baseline, CEA dramatically reduces the overestimation rate by roughly 10⁴× on every PDB (e.g., 3×10⁻⁶ vs. 15×10⁻² on Δ(6,4)-edge; 2×10⁻⁵ vs. 1.4×10⁻² on 7-edge), while sacrificing only a small amount of average heuristic (e.g., 7.45 vs. 8.44 on 7-edge). Against the compressed PDB baseline, CEA achieves a strictly higher average heuristic on all four PDBs (e.g., 8.76 vs. 6.84 on 8-corner; 7.45 vs. 6.83 on 7-edge) while maintaining a near-zero overestimation rate, whereas the compressed PDB is perfectly admissible but significantly weaker in guidance. The 8-corner PDB is the standout result: CEA perfectly recovers the admissible PDB heuristic (avg 8.76, overestimation 3×10⁻⁷) at 23× compression, and when used in A* search it generates fewer than half the nodes compared to the size-matched compressed PDB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sample-complexity framework of Sakaue & Oki (2022) for learning heuristics in A* by incorporating PDB abstractions and neural-network parameterization, yielding bounds that depend on network width/depth rather than graph size. It builds on Valenzano et al.'s (2014) suboptimality bound for A* without reopenings, tightening it to a max-inadmissibility bound when reopenings are allowed. On the practical side, it departs from prior learned-heuristic work (Agostinelli et al., Li et al.) that typically sacrifices admissibility, by introducing the CEA loss that directly enforces the admissibility constraint during training, and from classical PDB compression (Felner et al., Helmert et al.) by showing a neural network trained with CEA can match or exceed compressed-PDB heuristic strength at the same memory footprint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first generalization guarantees for goal-dependent heuristics and a practical loss function that makes learned heuristics near-admissible, bridging the long-standing gap between strong-but-inadmissible learned heuristics and admissible-but-weaker traditional ones. The theoretical result that sample complexity scales with network size rather than graph size is important for justifying neural-network heuristics in exponentially large state spaces. Open questions include whether CEA generalizes beyond Rubik's Cube to other combinatorial domains, whether the theoretical bounds are tight, and how to jointly optimize search and learning while preserving solution-quality guarantees.
