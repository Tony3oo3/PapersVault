## Problem

LLMs can recite mathematical definitions yet fail to apply the correct concept when solving problems, a gap that standard RLVR pipelines fail to address because they optimize only a terminal correctness reward. This coarse signal encourages pattern reuse and surface heuristics rather than genuine concept selection and application, leaving models unable to transfer conceptual understanding to novel problem formulations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262649/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multiple-choice math problems (sourced from a curated linear algebra textbook and LLM-generated concept-aligned quizzes) and produces a step-by-step reasoning trajectory ending in a boxed answer letter. During training, concept snippets (concise definitions of the target concept) can be concatenated to the problem prompt to form a concept-primed input; at test time, no concept text is provided, so the model must invoke concepts autonomously. Training uses 1,110 validated quizzes mapped to 236 concept texts, with binary correctness rewards and self-consistency (SC@21, T=0.7) for evaluation.

## Method

CORE is an RL training framework that injects explicit concept signals into policy-gradient optimization through three complementary mechanisms. CORE-Base trains directly on concept-aligned quizzes with standard GRPO. CORE-CR triggers a conditional intervention when all N trajectories in a GRPO group are incorrect: it concatenates the ground-truth concept text to the problem, generates K new concept-primed trajectories, replaces K failed trajectories with them, and assigns an augmented reward (R + r_bonus) to incentivize concept-grounded reasoning. CORE-KL instead adds a forward KL-divergence loss that aligns the unguided policy's next-token distributions with those of the concept-primed policy at each timestep, distilling the full reasoning distribution rather than a single trajectory. The framework is algorithm-agnostic (validated with both GRPO and PPO), requires no architectural changes, and operates as a higher-level supervision layer on top of standard policy-gradient RL.

## Results

On the primary model Qwen2-Math-7B (SC@21, T=0.7), CORE-KL achieves the strongest in-domain gain, reaching 55.7% on the Textbook suite (+9.3% over Vanilla's 46.4%) and 44.2% on TheoremQA (+9.6% over Vanilla). CORE-CR leads on TabMWP (93.6% vs. 90.2% Vanilla) and MATH (71.4% vs. 69.1%), while all three CORE variants consistently outperform both Vanilla and SFT across the 11-benchmark suite. Cross-model evaluation (Table 4) confirms consistent gains: CORE-CR improves DeepSeek-R1-DQ-1.5B by +0.4 avg, Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B by +0.3 avg, and Llama-3-8B-Instruct by +0.8 avg over their respective Vanilla baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three CORE variants (CORE-Base, CORE-CR, CORE-KL) against two baselines: Vanilla (untrained model) and SFT (supervised fine-tuning on the same concept-aligned quizzes). All CORE variants consistently outperform Vanilla across nearly all 11 benchmarks, with the largest gains on concept-heavy tasks: Textbook (+4.3 to +9.3%), TheoremQA (+5.8 to +9.6%), and OlympiadBench (+5.2 to +5.8%). SFT, by contrast, degrades performance on most benchmarks (e.g., MATH drops from 69.1% to 59.4%, OlympiadBench from 28.7% to 19.7%), confirming that outcome-only supervised learning on concept quizzes is insufficient. CORE-KL achieves the best in-domain (Textbook 55.7%) and concept-application (TheoremQA 44.2%, CounterMath 15.8%) scores, while CORE-CR leads on multi-step reasoning benchmarks (TabMWP 93.6%, MATH 71.4%). The ablation in Table 7 further shows that Random-Reward GRPO and Top-4-of-6 selection do not reproduce CORE's gains, ruling out optimizer artifacts as the source of improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

CORE builds directly on GRPO (introduced in DeepSeekMath) as its RL backbone, extending the RLVR paradigm by injecting explicit concept signals into the training loop rather than relying solely on terminal correctness rewards. It departs from BREAD (Zhang et al., 2025), which shares a similar trajectory-replacement mechanism but is motivated by rethinking SFT-vs-RL tradeoffs rather than conceptual reasoning, and from prior conceptual-reasoning evaluation work (TheoremQA, CounterMath, GSM-Symbolic) by providing a training-side intervention rather than a diagnostic benchmark. The framework is positioned as algorithm- and verifier-agnostic, wrapping around standard policy-gradient methods without architectural changes, and contrasts with expert-anchored distillation approaches by demonstrating that self-generated concept probes suffice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, modular recipe for injecting fine-grained conceptual supervision into RL training, which is directly applicable to any policy-gradient pipeline and any domain where explicit concepts underlie problem-solving. However, the evaluation is confined to linear algebra concepts from a single Chinese textbook, and out-of-domain gains are modest (typically 0.3–3.3%), leaving open whether the approach scales to broader mathematical domains or requires domain-specific concept curation. The self-supervised experiment (same model family for generation and learning) is encouraging but uses a relatively small quiz set (670 items), and the robustness gains, while consistent, are measured on a single perturbation protocol (option permutation). A key open question is whether the concept-snippet intervention generalizes to open-ended proof tasks or remains effective primarily for multiple-choice concept-application settings.
