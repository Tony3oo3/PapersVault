## Problem

Existing category-agnostic pose estimation (CAPE) methods either treat keypoints as isolated entities or depend on manually defined skeleton priors and textual descriptions, which are costly to annotate and inflexible across structurally diverse categories. Additionally, the stochastic selection of few-shot support images introduces noise from occlusions, pose mismatches, and incomplete annotations that misguide structural inference. The paper addresses both limitations by learning flexible, instance-specific keypoint relationships directly from visual support inputs without any external structural or linguistic priors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 RGB query image and N (1 or 5) annotated support images from an unseen category, each carrying M_c 2D keypoint coordinates (ranging from 8 to 68 keypoints across categories). Unlike prior CAPE approaches, no predefined skeleton graphs, keypoint identifiers, or textual descriptions are provided as auxiliary input. The output is a set of M_c predicted 2D keypoint locations for the query, refined iteratively across L_d Graph Transformer Decoder layers with intermediate supervision at each stage.

## Method

GenCape integrates two modules into the GraphCape pipeline: an iterative Structure-aware Variational Autoencoder (i-SVAE) and a Compositional Graph Transfer (CGT) module. At each decoder layer, i-SVAE encodes support keypoint embeddings into a Gaussian posterior over a latent code, samples via the reparameterization trick, and decodes into a soft, symmetrized adjacency matrix that captures instance-specific keypoint connectivity. CGT then fuses N_s sampled adjacency matrices per layer using Bayesian confidence-weighted averaging (inverse total-variance weighting) and applies query-guided gating—cosine similarity between pooled query features and layer-wise latent means—to reweight fused graphs across decoder layers, producing a single query-aware structural prior. This final graph drives a GCN message-passing step that refines support embeddings, which in turn update keypoint predictions via an MLP offset. Training combines a standard heatmap-plus-offset prediction loss with a VAE objective (KL divergence to a Gaussian prior plus L2 sparsity on the adjacency), and at inference the latent code collapses to the posterior mean for deterministic, stable structural priors.

## Results

On MP-100 under the 1-shot setting (PCK@0.2), GenCape-T (SwinV2-Tiny) achieves 88.09% average, surpassing the GraphCape-T baseline (87.23%) by +0.86% and outperforming text-support methods PPM+CPT (87.54%) and X-Pose (86.14%) without any textual input. GenCape-S (SwinV2-Small) reaches 91.01%, exceeding CapeX-S (90.37%) which relies on both text and skeleton graph support. Under the 5-shot setting, GenCape achieves 93.53% average PCK@0.2, outperforming all compared methods including GraphCape (92.83%) and PPM+CPT (92.58%). Under stricter thresholds with ResNet-50, GenCape-R50 improves mPCK by +1.59% over FMMP (80.31 vs. 78.72), with the gap widening to +1.61% at PCK@0.05.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenCape outperforms all 12 compared baselines on MP-100. Against its direct architectural baseline GraphCape (Hirschorn & Avidan, ECCV 2024), GenCape-T gains +0.86% (88.09 vs. 87.23) and GenCape-S gains +0.33% (91.01 vs. 90.68) in 1-shot PCK@0.2, with the 5-shot gap widening to +0.70% (93.53 vs. 92.83). Among image-only support methods, GenCape-T surpasses SCAPE (87.55), SDPNet (87.36), and MetaPoint+ (86.17) by 0.54–1.92 points, while GenCape-S leads all image-support methods by 3.46 points over SCAPE. Notably, GenCape-T (88.09) exceeds text-support methods PPM+CPT (87.54) and X-Pose (86.14) despite using no textual input, and GenCape-S (91.01) surpasses CapeX-S (90.37) which additionally leverages text and skeleton graph annotations. Under stricter thresholds (Table 3, ResNet-50), GenCape-R50 leads FMMP (Chen et al., CVPR 2025) by +0.92% at PCK@0.2 and +1.61% at PCK@0.05, with a +1.59% mPCK improvement, indicating superior fine-grained localization. In cross-category transfer (Table 6), GenCape achieves up to +21.05 points over GraphCape on cross-species settings (e.g., Human→Squirrel Body: 73.37 vs. 54.30), demonstrating substantially stronger structural generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenCape is grounded in the GraphCape framework (Hirschorn & Avidan, ECCV 2024), which introduced graph-based message passing for CAPE but relied on manually predefined skeleton connections. The key departure is replacing these fixed priors with an iterative Structure-aware Variational Autoencoder (i-SVAE) that learns instance-specific adjacency matrices from image-only support inputs, drawing on the VGAE formulation (Kipf & Welling, 2016) to model structural uncertainty. This contrasts with the deterministic adjacency prediction of SDPNet (Ren et al., CVPR 2024) and with text-support methods (CapeX, X-Pose, PPM+CPT) that require auxiliary language or skeleton annotations. The Compositional Graph Transfer module further distinguishes the work by introducing Bayesian fusion and query-guided gating to aggregate multiple latent graph hypotheses, addressing support-query mismatch in few-shot settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that generative, uncertainty-aware structural modeling can serve as an effective surrogate for external textual and skeleton priors in CAPE, potentially reducing annotation overhead for new categories. The cross-species generalization results (e.g., +21 PCK on Squirrel Body over GraphCape) suggest that instance-specific learned graphs capture transferable structural relationships that static priors cannot. However, performance collapses under heavy occlusion (50% masking drops PCK from 93 to 77), and evaluation is limited to the single MP-100 benchmark, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger keypoint sets, 3D pose, or more diverse real-world scenarios. The modest main-benchmark gains (+0.86% over GraphCape-T) also raise questions about the practical ceiling of structure learning from only 1–5 support images.
