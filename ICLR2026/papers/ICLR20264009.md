## Problem

Existing counterfactual explanation methods for GNNs predominantly rely on edge deletions to identify critical substructures, overlooking the role of missing relations whose addition could substantially alter predictions. Meanwhile, adversarial attack research has developed efficient strategies for selecting high-impact edge additions under small perturbation budgets, yet these insights remain disconnected from the explanation literature. ATEX-CF bridges this gap by formally establishing that adversarially added edges can serve as actionable counterfactual candidates, unifying attack and explanation under a single optimization framework.

## Input / Output

Given a graph G=(V,E,X) with adjacency matrix A and node features, a pre-trained GNN (GCN, GAT, or Graph Transformer), and a target node v, the method produces a sparse signed perturbation matrix ΔA with at most κ edge modifications (default κ=5), where positive entries denote additions and negative entries denote deletions. The search space is restricted to a union of (l+1)-hop neighborhood edges (for deletions) and GOTTACK-generated orbit-informed candidates (for additions), keeping the combinatorial space tractable. The final output is a pruned counterfactual subgraph that flips the target node's prediction while satisfying plausibility constraints on degree and local motif changes.

## Method

ATEX-CF formulates counterfactual generation as a joint optimization over a constrained candidate set S, minimizing a composite loss of prediction fidelity (negative log-likelihood with a straight-through estimator for the discrete flip indicator), L0 sparsity of edge edits, and a plausibility penalty combining relative degree anomaly and clustering-coefficient motif violation. The key architectural innovation is the dual candidate mechanism: deletion candidates come from the (l+1)-hop neighborhood of the target node, while addition candidates are drawn from GOTTACK's graph-orbit-based attack subgraph, which identifies structurally coherent nodes whose connection to v maximally increases misclassification probability. Optimization proceeds via a continuous signed mask M_e ∈ [-1,1] per candidate edge, discretized in the forward pass via thresholding and top-κ selection, with gradients propagated through the straight-through estimator in the backward pass. A post-hoc greedy pruning step (Algorithm 2) iteratively removes the least important edge (ranked by gradient magnitude) while verifying the prediction flip persists, yielding a minimal counterfactual. The theoretical foundation is Hypothesis 1, which posits high structural and semantic similarity between successful evasion-attack edge additions and pre-attack counterfactual explanation subgraphs, supported by gradient-based reasoning and empirical similarity measures.

## Results

ATEX-CF achieves the best overall average rank of 2.40 across six datasets and five metrics (misclassification rate, fidelity, edit size, plausibility, time), compared to 4.67 for the next-best method (CF-GNNExplainer). It wins 20 out of 30 metric-dataset cells, ranking first on misclassification (1.2), fidelity (1.3), edit size (1.0), and plausibility (1.2). On Cora with GCN, ATEX-CF raises misclassification from 0.46 at κ=1 to 0.76 at κ=15 while keeping edit size growth mild, whereas attack baselines must exhaust all allowed edits. The method's runtime (rank 7.3) is moderate—slower than PGExplainer (1.0) and INDUCE (2.8) but competitive with other counterfactual explainers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ATEX-CF dominates all nine baselines on the four quality metrics (misclassification, fidelity, edit size, plausibility) while trading some speed. Against deletion-only counterfactual explainers (CF-GNNExplainer, CF², NSEG, GNNExplainer, PGExplainer), ATEX-CF achieves substantially better misclassification and fidelity ranks (1.2–1.3 vs. 4.7–8.2) and smaller edit sizes (1.0 vs. 2.0–7.5), at the cost of being slower (time rank 7.3 vs. 1.0–9.5). Against hybrid counterfactual methods that also support edge additions (INDUCE, C2Explainer), ATEX-CF still leads on all quality metrics (e.g., fidelity rank 1.3 vs. 6.8 for INDUCE, 6.2 for C2Explainer) while remaining competitive on runtime. Against attack-based baselines repurposed for counterfactuals (NetTack, GOttack), ATEX-CF matches or slightly trails on misclassification (1.2 vs. 3.3/4.8) and fidelity (1.3 vs. 2.5/4.3), but decisively wins on edit size (1.0 vs. 8.8) and plausibility (1.2 vs. 8.0), indicating that raw attack perturbations are far less sparse and semantically coherent than ATEX-CF's optimized counterfactuals. PGExplainer is the fastest method (time rank 1.0) but performs worst on misclassification (8.2), confirming that speed alone does not yield effective counterfactuals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads in GNN interpretability: counterfactual explanation methods (CF-GNNExplainer, CF², NSEG, INDUCE, C2Explainer), which predominantly rely on edge deletions to identify decision-critical substructures, and adversarial attack methods (NetTack, GOttack), which exploit edge additions to flip predictions under small budgets. The key departure is repurposing GOttack's orbit-based edge-addition candidates as counterfactual search space, formalized through Hypothesis 1, which posits high structural/semantic overlap between successful attack edges and pre-attack counterfactual subgraphs. The signed-mask relaxation with straight-through estimation and the joint fidelity-sparsity-plausibility loss extend prior counterfactual optimization frameworks to handle both addition and deletion operations within a single perturbation budget.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides complementary explanatory coverage: deletion-based counterfactuals reveal which existing relations are critical, while addition-based ones expose which missing relations could have altered the outcome—useful for uncovering model bias and data deficiencies in domains like healthcare and citation analysis. Limitations include the restriction to structural (edge-only) perturbations without node feature changes, the reliance on a specific attack method (GOttack) for candidate generation, and evaluation primarily on node classification with static graphs. Open questions include whether the attack-explanation bridge generalizes to dynamic graphs, multi-hop feature perturbations, and whether the hypothesis holds under distribution shift or model retraining.
