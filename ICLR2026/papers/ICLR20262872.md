## Problem

Existing HJB-based solvers for the Generalized Schrödinger Bridge (GSB) problem suffer from unstable optimization dynamics with high-variance gradients in high dimensions, and they lack a strict terminal distribution matching criterion, yielding inexact couplings. Additionally, prior methods require explicit density estimation or differentiable state cost functionals, limiting applicability to real-world scenarios with non-smooth or black-box potentials. HOTA addresses these gaps by formulating a density-free dual objective that simultaneously enforces trajectory optimality via the HJB PDE and exact marginal matching via Kantorovich potentials.

## Input / Output

The method takes samples from a source distribution α and a target distribution β (ranging from 2D synthetic benchmarks to 1000D opinion vectors and 64×64 RGB images), along with a potential function U(x) that may be non-smooth or non-differentiable (e.g., indicator functions, LiDAR-derived surface costs). It produces a parametric value function s_θ(t,x) from which the optimal drift v_t = −∇_x s(t,x) is extracted; trajectories are then generated via Euler–Maruyama simulation, yielding a stochastic transport map T_#α → β. No intermediate density ρ_t(x_t) is ever modeled, and the method requires only first- and second-order derivatives of s_θ (computed via JAX autodiff with <5% overhead).

## Method

HOTA derives a dual formulation of the GSB problem in which a single neural network s_θ(t,x) simultaneously serves as the HJB value function (governing trajectory optimality) and the Kantorovich potential at t=1 (enforcing terminal marginal matching). Training minimizes two losses jointly: a potential matching loss L_pot that drives E_α[s(1,x₁)] − E_β[s(1,y)] toward zero, and an HJB residual loss L_hjb that enforces the PDE constraint ∂_t s = −½‖∇s‖² + U(x) + (σ²/2)Δs. Stability in high dimensions is achieved through three RL-inspired mechanisms: a replay buffer storing simulated trajectory points for HJB sampling, an EMA target network to make the HJB loss behave like a regression target, and adaptive gradient scaling that balances the two loss gradients via an EMA of their norm ratio. An optional angular acceleration penalty λ_a‖a‖ can be added to straighten trajectories. The entire procedure is density-free and handles non-differentiable U(x) without requiring gradients of the potential.

## Results

On the six low-dimensional OT benchmarks (Stunnel, Vneck, GMM, BabyMaze, Slit, Box), HOTA achieves the best feasibility (W₂ distance) and optimality (integral trajectory cost) across all datasets, e.g., W₂ = 0.006 and cost 383.25 on Stunnel vs. GSBM's 0.03 and 460.88, and W₂ = 0.19 and cost 80.44 on GMM vs. GSBM's 4.13 and 229.12. On high-dimensional Sphere datasets (up to 1000D), HOTA maintains normalized W₂ ≈ 0.05 and optimality ≈ 3.2, while GSBM degrades to W₂ ≈ 0.21 and cost ≈ 22. For image-to-image translation, HOTA achieves FID 6.28 (Male→Female CelebA) and 11.67 (Female CelebA→Anime), outperforming EUOT (8.44), NOT (11.96/18.28), and CycleGAN (12.94/20.80).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOTA dominates GSBM (Liu et al., ICLR 2024) on every low-dimensional benchmark: it reduces W₂ by 5–20× (e.g., 0.006 vs. 0.03 on Stunnel, 0.19 vs. 4.13 on GMM) and cuts integral cost by 17–65% (e.g., 80.44 vs. 229.12 on GMM). Against NLSB (Koshizuka & Sato, 2022), HOTA is dramatically better on GMM (W₂ 0.19 vs. 67.76; cost 80.44 vs. 4202.71) and BabyMaze (W₂ 0.004 vs. >1), while NLSB is competitive only on Vneck feasibility. NLOT (Pooladian et al., UAI 2024) is reported only for feasibility on the non-smooth datasets and is matched or beaten by HOTA on all three (Slit: 0.0004 vs. 0.013; Box: 0.002 vs. 0.016). In the image-to-image setting, HOTA outperforms CycleGAN (FID 6.28 vs. 12.94), NOT (6.28 vs. 11.96), EUOT (6.28 vs. 8.44) on Male→Female, and ENOT (11.67 vs. 13.12) and NOT (11.67 vs. 18.28) on Female→Anime. On the 3D LiDAR surface, HOTA achieves the lowest integral cost (6179) versus GSBM (6209) and DeepGSB (7747), with W₂ = 0.012. In high-dimensional Sphere experiments (1000D), HOTA's normalized W₂ (0.051) and optimality (3.17) are roughly 4× and 7× better than GSBM (0.21, 22.1), and GSBM runs out of memory beyond 5000D while HOTA scales to 9000D.

## Relation to Prior Work

HOTA sits at the intersection of the Generalized Schrödinger Bridge (GSB) literature (DeepGSB, GSBM) and neural optimal transport (NOT, ENOT), but departs from both in a key way: it formulates the GSB dual problem by binding the Kantorovich potential directly to the HJB value function, turning the PDE constraint into a built-in regularizer rather than a separate enforcement step. Where prior HJB-based GSB solvers (DeepGSB, NLSB) suffered from unstable gradients and lacked strict terminal matching, and where GSBM required differentiable state costs and expensive alternating optimization, HOTA borrows stabilization techniques from deep RL (target EMA networks, replay buffers, adaptive gradient balancing) to achieve stable joint learning of the potential and drift policy. The method also inherits the Benamou-Brenier dynamical OT perspective and the weak OT / stochastic c-transform framework of Gozlan et al., but is the first to handle non-smooth and non-differentiable potentials end-to-end without explicit density modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to solve GSB with non-differentiable cost functionals opens the door to applications where the potential is defined by black-box systems (LLMs, molecular force fields with hard-core repulsion, scanned LiDAR surfaces) that prior GSB methods simply cannot handle. The 50–100× speedup over GSBM and stable scaling to 9,000+ dimensions make high-dimensional OT advection practically accessible on a single GPU. However, the authors note sensitivity to Fourier feature encoding choices and the need for architectures that simultaneously support optimal control estimation and Kantorovich potential representation; extending theoretical guarantees to weaker regularity assumptions and incorporating more structured neural architectures remain open. For the broader OT and generative-modeling community, HOTA demonstrates that RL-style stabilization is a viable and effective strategy for PDE-constrained learning, a pattern that may transfer to other HJB-based problems.
