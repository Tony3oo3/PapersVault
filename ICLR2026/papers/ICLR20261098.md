## Problem

State-of-the-art protein folding models (AlphaFold2, RoseTTAFold, ESMFold) rely on computationally expensive domain-specific modules—multiple sequence alignments, explicit pair representations, and triangle updates—that hard-code structural biology priors into the architecture. This paper asks whether these inductive biases are actually necessary, or whether a general-purpose transformer trained with a generative flow-matching objective can learn the folding process directly from data and achieve competitive performance.

## Input / Output

SimpleFold takes a single amino acid sequence as input, which is embedded by a frozen ESM2-3B protein language model into per-residue conditioning vectors. The model generates full-atom 3D coordinates (all heavy atoms, not just Cα backbone) by integrating a learned velocity field from Gaussian noise to data over 500 Euler-Maruyama SDE steps, producing structures in Ångström. Because the generative process is stochastic (controlled by a τ parameter), a single sequence can yield an ensemble of diverse conformations rather than a single deterministic structure, enabling MD-ensemble and multi-state prediction tasks.

## Method

SimpleFold is a flow-matching generative model built entirely from standard transformer blocks with adaptive timestep conditioning (DiT-style), eliminating MSA, pair representations, triangle updates, and equivariant modules. The architecture follows a fine-coarse-fine hierarchy: a lightweight atom encoder with local attention maps noisy coordinates to atom tokens, a grouping operation pools atoms into residue tokens, a heavy residue trunk (the bulk of parameters) processes the sequence-conditioned residue tokens, and an atom decoder with ungrouping and local attention outputs the predicted velocity field. Training combines a rectified-flow L2 velocity-matching loss with an LDDT structural loss whose weight α(t) increases near t=1, and timesteps are resampled from a logit-normal distribution biased toward clean data to capture fine side-chain details. The model is scaled to 2.86B parameters and trained on ~9M structures (PDB + AFDB SwissProt + AFESM-E), using 4D axial RoPE for positional encoding, SwiGLU activations, and QK-normalization. Inference uses a Langevin-style SDE with a time-dependent diffusion coefficient w(t)=2(1-t)/(t+η), where τ controls the trade-off between structural accuracy and ensemble diversity.

## Results

On CAMEO22, SimpleFold-3B achieves a TM-score of 0.837/0.916 and GDT-TS of 0.802/0.867, reaching over 95% of RoseTTAFold2/AlphaFold2 performance without MSA or triangle attention. On the more challenging CASP14 benchmark, SimpleFold-3B obtains a TM-score of 0.720/0.792 and GDT-TS of 0.639/0.703, outperforming ESMFold (0.701/0.792 and 0.622/0.711). For ensemble generation on ATLAS, SimpleFold achieves a pairwise RMSD correlation of 0.44 without any tuning, substantially exceeding MSA-subsampling (0.22) and AlphaFold2 (0.10). On the Apo/holo two-state benchmark, SimpleFold-3B reaches a TM-ensemble score of 0.893/0.916, surpassing the MSA-based AlphaFlow (0.864/0.893).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimpleFold-3B consistently outperforms ESMFlow (the closest PLM-based flow-matching baseline) on both CAMEO22 and CASP14 across all metrics, e.g., TM-score 0.837 vs 0.818 on CAMEO22 and 0.720 vs 0.627 on CASP14. On CASP14, SimpleFold-3B surpasses ESMFold in TM-score (0.720 vs 0.701) and GDT-TS (0.639 vs 0.622), while on CAMEO22 it trails AlphaFold2 and RoseTTAFold2 by roughly 2–4 points in TM-score, reflecting the cost of removing MSA and triangle attention. For ensemble generation on ATLAS, SimpleFold (no tuning) achieves a pairwise RMSD correlation of 0.44, far exceeding AlphaFold2 (0.10) and MSA-subsampling (0.22), and after MD fine-tuning (SimpleFold-MD) it matches or exceeds AlphaFlow-MD on ensemble observables like exposed residue Jaccard (0.60 vs 0.50). On the Apo/holo two-state benchmark, SimpleFold-3B achieves a TM-ensemble of 0.893/0.916, beating MSA-based AlphaFlow (0.864/0.893) and PLM-based ESMFlow (0.856/0.893). On de novo designed proteins, SimpleFold-3B outperforms both AlphaFold2 (TM-score 0.852 vs 0.831) and ESMFold (0.852 vs 0.839), and on orphan proteins it significantly improves LDDT over AlphaFold2 (0.651 vs 0.618) while remaining comparable on other metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

SimpleFold transplants the flow-matching + DiT recipe from vision generative models (Lipman et al., 2023; Peebles & Xie, 2023) into protein folding, using a linear interpolant path (Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden, 2023) and Euler-Maruyama stochastic sampling (Ma et al., 2024). It deliberately departs from the AlphaFold2/ESMFold lineage by eliminating MSA, explicit pair representations, and triangle updates, replacing them with a single sequence-level representation from a frozen ESM2-3B embedding and a 'fine-coarse-fine' atom-residue-atom transformer hierarchy. Unlike AlphaFlow/ESMFlow, which fine-tune pre-trained deterministic models with a flow-matching objective, SimpleFold is trained from scratch as a pure generative model, and unlike Proteina or RFDiffusion, it uses no equivariant modules or pair representations at all.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous scaling study in protein folding (100M→3B parameters, ~2M→9M structures) and demonstrates that a general-purpose transformer can match or approach domain-specific architectures on standard benchmarks while being far more computationally efficient (~1.4 Tflops vs ~30 Tflops for AF2). This opens a substantially simpler and more scalable design space for future folding models, particularly for ensemble generation where SimpleFold-3B achieves SOTA on Apo/holo. Open questions include whether further scaling closes the remaining ~3-4% TM-score gap to AlphaFold2/RoseTTAFold2 on CAMEO22, whether the approach extends to protein-ligand or protein-complex prediction, and whether the self-distillation results (E.2) can be pushed to eliminate dependence on any externally distilled data entirely.
