## Problem

Fine-tuning LLMs on noisy or redundant data dilutes alignment signals, and while smaller curated datasets can match larger ones, principled methods for identifying high-value examples remain underexplored. Existing approaches either rely on hand-crafted heuristics, require expensive retraining to measure each example's impact on downstream loss, or depend on a separate reference model (as in RHO-Loss), making them computationally costly or theoretically biased. The paper asks: can we estimate each training example's contribution to reducing holdout loss without any auxiliary training or fixed reference model?

## Input / Output

The method takes a large (potentially noisy) training set, a smaller high-quality holdout set, and a pretrained model as inputs. The holdout set is not used for explicit training but is consumed as in-context demonstrations: for each candidate example, the top-k most similar holdout examples (selected via kNN in an all-mpnet-base-v2 embedding space) are prepended to the prompt to compute a conditional loss. The output is a set of per-example ICA scores, converted to continuous weights in [0,1] via max-min normalization within each mini-batch, which scale the per-example gradient contributions; the final deliverable is the fine-tuned model parameters after T optimizer steps.

## Method

The core mechanism is the In-Context Approximation (ICA), which replaces the intractable term ℓ(y|x;θ*(D_t ∪ D_ho)) in the Bayesian holdout-loss score (derived from RHO-Loss) with ℓ(y|x, D_ho; θ_t), i.e., the loss computed by conditioning the current model on holdout examples as in-context demonstrations. This is justified by the finding that ICL implicitly performs gradient-like updates (Dai et al., 2023), so providing the holdout set in context approximates the effect of explicitly training on D_t ∪ D_ho. The resulting ICA score s_ICA(x,y;θ_t) = ℓ(y|x;θ_t) − ℓ(y|x,D_ho;θ_t) is computed periodically (R times over training) and converted to batch-level weights via max-min normalization (rather than softmax, to avoid exponential amplification of score differences). These weights scale each example's gradient contribution in the batch update, enabling adaptive reweighting that tracks the model's evolving parameters. The approach requires no reference model, no additional finetuning, and adds only ~1.5% computational overhead over standard fine-tuning.

## Results

The primary metric is GPT-4o-judged win rate (percentage of pairwise comparisons where the ICA-reweighted model's response is preferred over the baseline's). Across SFT on Alpaca and Yahoo_Answers_Topics, ICA reweighting achieves 71–85% win rates against standard training (no reweighting), 46–54% against RHO-Loss, and 55–67% against One-Shot Learning, across LLaMA-3B/8B and Qwen-4B/8B. Similar patterns hold for DPO (61–80% vs. no reweighting) and SimPO (62–94% vs. no reweighting). The method adds only ~1.5% computational overhead versus ~10% for RHO-Loss and ~4% for One-Shot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICA reweighting decisively outperforms standard training (no reweighting) across all settings, with win rates of 61–94% depending on the alignment method and dataset, confirming that the ICA score meaningfully identifies high-value examples. Against One-Shot Learning (Li et al., 2023b), ICA wins consistently with 54–67% win rates in SFT and 51–60% in DPO/SimPO, indicating that conditioning on the full holdout set (via kNN-selected top-k demonstrations) captures more relevant signal than a single one-shot example. Against RHO-Loss (Mindermann et al., 2022), which approximates the same Bayesian holdout-loss criterion but requires training a separate reference model, ICA achieves comparable performance with win rates hovering around 46–54%—slightly below 50% in some SFT settings but above 50% in others—while eliminating the ~10% runtime overhead of reference model training. For the influence-function-based baselines LESS and GREATS (SFT only), ICA achieves comparable results with win rates of 50.9–52.0%, while being more computationally efficient (808s vs. 1,394+1,800s for LESS and 1,004s for GREATS on Yahoo_Answers_Topics). Overall, ICA matches or exceeds all baselines on alignment quality while introducing the lowest computational overhead (~1.5% additional runtime).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bayesian holdout-loss formulation of RHO-Loss (Mindermann et al., 2022), which estimates each example's contribution to downstream loss but requires training a separate reference model on the holdout set. The key departure is replacing that reference model with in-context demonstrations of the holdout set, motivated by Dai et al. (2023)'s finding that ICL implicitly performs gradient-like updates. This eliminates auxiliary training entirely while preserving the Bayesian derivation, and the resulting ICA scores are used for dynamic gradient reweighting rather than the hard subset selection typical of influence-function methods like LESS and GREATS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, low-overhead (~1.5%) alternative to reference-model-based data selection that works uniformly across SFT, DPO, and SimPO, making it broadly applicable to practitioners fine-tuning LLMs on noisy data. Key limitations include the requirement for a high-quality holdout set (noisy or unrepresentative holdouts may impair generalization) and the restriction to off-policy paradigms, since on-policy methods like PPO would require frequent score recomputation on newly generated data. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to on-policy RLHF pipelines and whether the kNN-based demonstration selection can be replaced by learned retrieval for better domain adaptation.
