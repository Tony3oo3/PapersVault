## Problem

State-of-the-art zero-shot inpainting methods with diffusion priors (e.g., DPS, DDNM) rely on surrogate likelihoods whose scores require vector-Jacobian products through the denoiser at every reverse step, introducing significant memory and runtime overhead that often exceeds the cost of training a dedicated conditional model. The paper asks whether one can design a zero-shot posterior sampler that eliminates backpropagation through the denoiser entirely while preserving reconstruction quality.

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely in the latent space of Stable Diffusion 3.5 (16 channels, 8× spatial downsampling). The pixel-space mask is downsampled to latent resolution via average pooling (kernel/stride 8) and thresholded at 0.5 to produce a binary latent mask. Inputs are a reference image encoded to latent space, the downsampled binary mask, and a text prompt for content guidance; the output is a fully inpainted latent image decoded back to pixel space. Experiments use 768×768 (FFHQ, DIV2K) and 512×512 (PIE-Bench) resolutions with 50 NFEs (25 diffusion steps × 2 NFEs per step).

## Method

DING introduces a decoupled likelihood surrogate in which the noise predictor x̂₁^θ is evaluated at an independent draw z_s from the pretrained DDIM transition rather than at the current state x_s, breaking the dependency that necessitates VJPs. This decoupling yields a posterior transition with a closed-form Gaussian mixture structure: masked coordinates follow the standard DDIM transition, while unmasked coordinates follow a Gaussian whose mean is a convex combination of the DDIM mean and an effective observation (α_s·y + σ_s·x̂₁^θ(z_s, s)), with mixing coefficient γ = η_s²/(η_s² + α_s²σ_y²). Sampling from this mixture is exact and requires only two forward denoiser evaluations per step (one at x_t, one at the proxy z_s), with no gradient computation. The method is parameterized by a single hyperparameter—the DDIM stochasticity schedule η_t—and the authors adopt η_t = σ_t(1−α_t), which ablations confirm outperforms DDPM, maximum-variance, and slower-decay alternatives.

## Results

On FFHQ (768×768, 50 NFEs), DING achieves the best FID across all four mask configurations (9.6 Half, 15.5 Center, 19.7 Top, 29.6 Bottom) and the best pFID (6.6–14.0), while also attaining the lowest LPIPS (0.27–0.34). On PIE-Bench (556 samples, 50 NFEs), DING obtains the best FID (61.4), pFID (24.7), LPIPS (0.30), and CLIP-ED (23.36). Critically, DING outperforms a Stable Diffusion 3 model fine-tuned on 12M images for inpainting on every reported metric, with cPSNR gains of 8–10 dB, while running in comparable wall-clock time (2.9 s vs. 3.0 s on H100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DING outperforms all ten baselines on FID and pFID across FFHQ, DIV2K, and PIE-Bench. On FFHQ, DING's FID of 9.6 (Half mask) beats the next-best DiffPIR (12.1) and DDNM (12.3) by 2–3 points, and its pFID of 6.6 is substantially lower than DiffPIR's 11.23. On PIE-Bench, DING ties DDNM for best FID (61.4) but clearly leads on pFID (24.7 vs. 26.9), LPIPS (0.30 vs. 0.31), and CLIP-ED (23.36 vs. 22.96); DDNM retains a slight edge on cPSNR (27.29 vs. 27.03). FlowDPS and PSLD achieve marginally higher CLIP scores on PIE-Bench but are far worse on fidelity and perceptual metrics (e.g., PSLD cPSNR 14.25 vs. DING 27.03), suggesting metric hacking rather than genuine quality. Against the fine-tuned SD3 inpaint model (28 NFEs, same ~3 s runtime), DING improves cPSNR by 8–10 dB (e.g., 31.40 vs. 21.69 on FFHQ Half) and reduces FID from 23.5 to 9.3, confirming that zero-shot decoupled guidance can surpass task-specific fine-tuning. In terms of efficiency, DING runs in 2.9 s with 22.09 GB memory, matching or beating all VJP-free baselines and substantially outperforming VJP-based methods like PSLD (7.4 s, 24.49 GB) and Resample (8.1 s, 24.50 GB).

## Relation to Prior Work

DING builds on the posterior-sampling framework for diffusion models (Wu et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023; Janati et al., 2024), which casts image editing as a Bayesian inverse problem and samples from the posterior via DDIM transitions. Its key departure is from the DPS/twisting-function approach (Ho et al., 2022; Chung et al., 2023), where the likelihood is evaluated at the denoiser's own output, creating a dependency that necessitates vector-Jacobian products through the network. By instead evaluating the noise predictor at an independent draw from the transition (the 'decoupling'), DING breaks this dependency and recovers closed-form Gaussian posterior transitions via standard conjugacy, placing it conceptually between the simple replacement method (Song & Ermon, 2019; RePaint) and the more complex SMC or variational approaches (Cardoso et al., 2023; Mardani et al., 2024), while requiring neither backpropagation nor multiple particles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: DING eliminates the VJP/backpropagation bottleneck that makes most strong zero-shot guidance methods memory-intensive and slow, achieving the best fidelity-realism trade-off across three benchmarks while being the fastest and most memory-efficient method tested. Notably, it surpasses a Stable Diffusion 3 model fine-tuned on 12M images for inpainting, suggesting that well-designed inference-time guidance can substitute for task-specific training. Key limitations include the restriction to inpainting (the only observation operator that lifts cleanly to latent space), sensitivity to prompt specificity, and diminishing returns beyond ~200 NFEs due to the DDIM schedule. Extending the framework to general linear inverse problems and designing schedules that scale gracefully with compute remain open directions.
