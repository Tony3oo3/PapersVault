## Problem

Long-context models operate in a retrieval-then-generation manner, but their attention on critical tokens is overwhelmed by irrelevant contextual noise, degrading prediction quality. Conventional cross-entropy training applies uniform token-wise supervision and cannot distinguish critical from irrelevant tokens, making it fundamentally inefficient for long-context modeling. The paper asks: can explicitly suppressing context noise during training improve the model's ability to locate and leverage critical information?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265216/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a long context token sequence (up to 128K tokens) paired with target tokens for supervised training. During each training step, the model's input embeddings are modified in-place: noisy token embeddings are adjusted by subtracting a scaled gradient before the forward pass, while critical token embeddings remain unchanged. The final output is the standard autoregressive text prediction, but the intermediate artifact is a set of denoised token embeddings that serve as the actual model input for the loss computation.

## Method

Context Denoising Training (CDT) operates as an online EM loop with two steps per training iteration. First, Critical Token Detection: the model is frozen except for input embedding gradients, and a single backward pass computes L2-normalized embedding gradients for each token; tokens whose gradient magnitude falls below the sequence average are flagged as noise (the paper proves in Appendix C that embedding gradients are proportional to Integrated Gradient scores, making this a memory-efficient proxy for the full IG computation). Second, Emphasizing Training: flagged noisy embeddings are updated by subtracting their gradient scaled by lr×β, then the full model is unfrozen and trained with standard cross-entropy loss on the denoised embeddings. This denoise-then-train cycle is repeated every step, progressively sharpening the model's attention on critical tokens while leaving the overall attention distribution structure intact, as confirmed by attention map visualizations.

## Results

On LongBench-E (12 real-world long-context tasks), CDT applied to Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct achieves 50.92 average, matching GPT-4o (51.00) and outperforming the backbone (48.61) by 2.3 points. For context window scaling on Llama-3-8B-Base, CDT reaches 39.31 vs. LongCE's 34.62 (a 4.7-point gain). On RULER (13 synthetic tasks), CDT achieves the best scores at all context lengths (e.g., 93.08 at 32K, 78.72 at 128K for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct), and attains the lowest LongPPL (2.36) indicating superior salient-token localization. On BABILong long-form reasoning, CDT achieves 43.30 average for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, the highest among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDT consistently outperforms all training-based baselines across every model group. On Llama-3-8B-Base (context window scaling), CDT (39.31) beats LongCE (34.62) by 4.7 points and CE (34.62) by 4.7 points, while YaRN (26.04) provides only marginal gains over the untrained backbone. On Llama-3.1-8B-Base (language modeling), CDT (38.89) is the only method that avoids performance degradation relative to the backbone (37.13), whereas LongCE actually drops to 36.90. On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (SFT), CDT (50.92) surpasses LOGO/DPO (49.01) by 1.9 points, standard SFT (48.72) by 2.2 points, and inference-time methods FlexPrefill (47.38) and X-Attention (47.81) by 3+ points; Contriever/RAG (44.93) is the weakest baseline. Against strong pre-trained LCMs, CDT-enhanced Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (50.92) exceeds ProLong-512K-Instruct (47.37) and NExtLong-512K-Instruct (44.35), and matches GPT-4o (51.00). On RULER and BABILong (Table 2), CDT also leads all baselines at every context length, with the largest margins on LongPPL (2.36 vs. 4.05 for the Instruct backbone) and BABILong 128K (33.00 vs. 29.60).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the retrieval-then-generation paradigm for long-context models and the Integrated Gradient (IG) information-flow framework from Wang et al. (2023), repurposing it as a critical-token detection metric rather than an interpretability tool. It departs from prior token re-weighting approaches (LongCE, Rho-1) that modify the loss function, instead intervening at the input embedding level by subtracting gradient contributions of detected noisy tokens. The theoretical derivation in Appendix C connecting IG scores to L2-normalized embedding gradients is a key technical contribution that makes the method computationally feasible for long sequences, where direct IG computation is intractable beyond ~12K tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a simple input-level gradient subtraction (analogous to signal denoising) can boost attention on critical tokens by ~10× and bring an 8B open-source model to GPT-4o parity on LongBench-E (50.92 vs 51.00) is practically significant for resource-constrained deployments. However, the paper acknowledges that gains on complex long-form reasoning tasks are less pronounced, and the additional forward-backward pass for noise detection, while modest, scales with model size. Open questions include whether the EM-style denoising can be made cheaper (e.g., via dedicated noise-handling modules) and how the strategy interacts with the model's reasoning capabilities beyond retrieval.
