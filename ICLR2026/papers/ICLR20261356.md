## Problem

Despite the empirical success of hard sample sampling in contrastive learning, the theoretical rationale for why hard pairs improve performance, the conditions under which solutions collapse, and the interplay between hard positives and hard negatives remain largely unexplored. Most prior work addresses positive or negative sampling in isolation, leaving the joint selection problem and its geometric implications open.

## Input / Output

The method operates on augmented candidate pools: for each anchor, a pool of M augmented variants plus the original is constructed, from which hard positives are selected via farthest-point sampling and hard negatives are generated adversarially then screened by a relative similarity threshold. Inputs span 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR), 224×224 images (ImageNet), multimodal KG entities with linked images and text descriptions, and raw text utterances; outputs are standard class labels, ranked entity lists (MRR/Hit@K), and binary in-domain/out-of-domain predictions.

## Method

SPACL derives optimality conditions for the InfoNCE loss on the unit hypersphere, proving that solution collapse occurs if and only if the convex reconstruction of negatives is a scaled version of the positive (Theorem 2.1), and that the gradient norm is asymptotically bounded by 2 regardless of the number of negatives (Lemma 2.4). Motivated by these results, the method selects hard positives via iterative farthest-point sampling from an augmented candidate pool to maximize angular spread and enlarge the positive convex hull, and generates hard negatives through an adversarial GAN whose generator produces samples closely resembling the hard positive set while a discriminator distinguishes genuine from generated negatives. A relative threshold (λ_Q^h) then screens the combined in-batch and adversarial negatives, retaining only those with the highest anchor similarity to keep the negative convex hull close to the positive hull and break the symmetry condition that causes collapse. The final contrastive loss (Eq. 9) is computed exclusively over the selected hard positive and hard negative subsets rather than all available pairs.

## Results

SPACL achieves the best Top-1 accuracy across all four image classification benchmarks under every supervision paradigm: 97.04% on CIFAR-10, 80.84% on CIFAR-100, 87.62% on ImageNet-100, and 80.98% on ImageNet-1K (fully-supervised), outperforming the strongest baselines (VarCon, SupCon) by 1.1–4.3 percentage points. In self-supervised settings, SPACL reaches 76.59% Top-1 on ImageNet-1K, surpassing SwAV (75.29%) and Barlow Twins (73.26%). For KG link prediction, SPACL attains MRR of 0.413 on FB15K-237 and 0.942 on WN9, exceeding the best prior method LMKGE by +1.3 and +0.8 points respectively. On OOD intent detection, SPACL achieves F1-OOD of 96.72 on StackOverflow (25% classes), a +2.3 point gain over TBOS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In fully-supervised image classification, SPACL outperforms VarCon (the strongest supervised baseline) by +1.10% on CIFAR-10, +2.55% on CIFAR-100, +1.28% on ImageNet-100, and +1.62% on ImageNet-1K Top-1. In self-supervised settings, SPACL surpasses MoCo V2 by +1.26% on CIFAR-10 and Barlow Twins by +1.46% on ImageNet-1K, while also exceeding BYOL by +2.31% on ImageNet-1K. For weakly-supervised learning, SPACL improves over MaskCon by +2.29% on CIFAR-10 and +3.10% on CIFAR-100, with a notable +3.00% gain on ImageNet-1K. In KG link prediction, SPACL beats LMKGE (the strongest multimodal baseline) by +0.8 MRR on WN9, +1.3 MRR on FB15K-237, and +1.3 MRR on FB15K, with consistent gains across all Hit@K metrics. On OOD intent detection, SPACL achieves the best F1-IND across all settings and leads on F1-OOD for StackOverflow at 25% and 75% class ratios, while remaining competitive (within 0.5 points) on BANKING at 50% where TBOS slightly edges it on F1-OOD.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPACL builds directly on the MoCo/SimCLR dual-encoder framework with InfoNCE loss, but departs from prior contrastive learning work by providing a geometric optimality analysis (convex hull conditions, collapse criteria, gradient bounds) that was previously absent. While earlier studies on hard sample mining (e.g., Jang & Wang 2023, Xu et al. 2022, Zhuang et al. 2024b) addressed either positive or negative sampling in isolation, SPACL is among the first to jointly screen both hard positives (via farthest-point selection) and hard negatives (via adversarial generation plus relative-threshold filtering) under a unified theoretical framework. The adversarial negative generator draws on GAN-style min-max training, but its purpose here is specifically to produce boundary-aware negatives that tighten the decision boundary near the positive convex hull, rather than to generate general adversarial examples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical contribution—particularly Theorem 2.1 (collapse iff the negative convex reconstruction is a scaled positive) and Lemma 2.4 (gradient norm bounded by 2 as N⁻→∞)—gives practitioners a geometric criterion for when contrastive learning will fail and a justification for why fewer, harder negatives suffice. The modality-agnostic validation across image classification, KG link prediction, and OOD text detection strengthens the claim that the hard-sample screening principle is general. Limitations include the added complexity of adversarial training (generator/discriminator stability), the lack of explicit false-negative handling, and the fact that the theoretical analysis assumes a single positive sample for the cleanest collapse condition. Extending the analysis to negative-free CL (BYOL/SimSiam-style) and to multi-positive collapse conditions remains open.
