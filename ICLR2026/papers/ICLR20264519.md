## Problem

Diffusion-based super-resolution models rely on self-attention with O(N²) complexity, creating a severe computational bottleneck at high resolutions. Linear attention offers an O(N) alternative, but its application to photorealistic SR has been blocked by two previously unsolved challenges: catastrophic training divergence during fine-tuning and an intractable perception-distortion trade-off that prevents simultaneous gains in realism and fidelity.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 4× low-resolution image (degraded via the Real-ESRGAN pipeline) and a concise set of object tags extracted by the RAM tagger as conditioning. The LR image is processed by a learnable 3-layer strided convolutional stem and channel-concatenated with the noisy latent, while the tags serve as lightweight semantic guidance. Output is a 4× super-resolved image (e.g., 256×256 → 1024×1024) generated via 20 flow-matching sampling steps, achieving a 1-NFE forward pass of 0.036s at 1024×1024.

## Method

LinearSR is a conditional Diffusion Transformer backbone using ReLU-based Linear Attention (exploiting the associative property to reduce complexity from O(N²) to O(N)) paired with a Mix-FFN module containing 3×3 depth-wise convolutions for local processing. Three key innovations make this viable: (1) a TAG-based guidance paradigm grounded in the 'precision-over-volume' principle, where concise object labels from RAM outperform verbose captions or raw CLIP/DINO features; (2) an Early-Stopping Guided Fine-tuning (ESGF) strategy that identifies the 'knee point'—the iteration where validation metrics plateau before entering an oscillation phase—and initializes all fine-tuning from this stable checkpoint to prevent catastrophic divergence; and (3) an SNR-based 4-expert Mixture of Experts architecture that hierarchically bisects the log-SNR space into four regimes (initial denoising, coarse structure, texture generation, detail refinement), with a deterministic gating network routing each timestep to exactly one expert to mitigate the perception-distortion trade-off. The model is trained with a Conditional Flow Matching objective using the CAME optimizer in a multi-stage protocol (pre-training → MoE training → SFT), with each stage terminated at its respective knee point.

## Results

On the challenging RealLQ250 benchmark, LinearSR achieves a clean sweep of all no-reference metrics: MANIQA 0.515, MUSIQ 71.914, and CLIPIQA 0.720, outperforming all 10 baselines. On DIV2K-Val (synthetic), it leads in MANIQA (0.475) and MUSIQ (69.466) while remaining competitive on LPIPS (0.401). In terms of efficiency, LinearSR sets a new SOTA 1-NFE forward time of 0.036s for 1024×1024 synthesis, nearly 2× faster than the next-best AdcSR (0.046s), with a total multi-step inference time of 0.830s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LinearSR achieves the best no-reference perceptual scores (MANIQA and MUSIQ) across all four evaluation datasets, with the most decisive margin on RealLQ250 where it leads MUSIQ by 0.41 over TSD-SR (71.914 vs. 71.505) and MANIQA by 0.045 over SeeSR (0.515 vs. 0.502). On CLIPIQA, LinearSR wins on RealLQ250 (0.720) but trails TSD-SR on DrealSR (0.713 vs. 0.757) and RealSR (0.673 vs. 0.723), indicating TSD-SR retains an edge in CLIP-aligned quality on some real-world sets. On full-reference metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), LinearSR is competitive but not dominant—e.g., on DIV2K-Val it scores 25.262 PSNR vs. DiffBIR's 26.480—consistent with the known perception-distortion trade-off of generative SR. In efficiency, LinearSR's 1-NFE time of 0.036s is SOTA, beating AdcSR (0.046s) and OSEDiff (0.150s), but its total inference time of 0.830s is slower than AdcSR (0.561s) and InvSR (0.667s), which benefit from distillation and optimized sampling. Against heavyweight multi-step models like SUPIR (13.632s) and DiffBIR (25.543s), LinearSR is roughly 16–31× faster in total inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the linear-attention DiT architecture validated for text-to-image synthesis in SANA (Xie et al., 2024) to the distinct demands of photorealistic super-resolution, a task where all prior diffusion-based SR methods (StableSR, DiffBIR, SUPIR, etc.) rely on quadratic self-attention. The TAG-based guidance paradigm is directly inspired by SeeSR (Wu et al., 2024b), but the authors extend it with a 'precision-over-volume' principle validated against CLIP and DINO features. The SNR-based MoE draws on the high/low-noise expert separation validated in eDiff-I (Balaji et al., 2022) and Wan-Video, while the ESGF training strategy is a novel contribution addressing a previously undocumented instability specific to multi-stage fine-tuning of linear-attention SR models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first robust, repeatable methodology for applying linear attention in photorealistic SR, unblocking a direction that had been historically stalled by training collapse. The 0.036s 1-NFE forward time at 1024×1024 establishes a new efficiency benchmark that is purely architectural (orthogonal to distillation), suggesting substantial further speedups are possible. The ESGF 'knee-point' strategy and the finding that concise object tags outperform rich visual features or text captions for SR guidance are practical insights likely to inform future efficient generative restoration work. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other restoration tasks (denoising, inpainting) and whether the perception-distortion gap on full-reference metrics (PSNR) can be further narrowed without sacrificing the efficiency advantage.
