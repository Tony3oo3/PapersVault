## Problem

In continuous-action RL with expressive flow or diffusion policies, leveraging the critic's action gradient for policy optimization requires backpropagation through the multi-step denoising process, which is numerically unstable. Prior methods work around this by either discarding the gradient entirely (using only Q-values) or distilling into one-step policies, sacrificing either learning efficiency or policy expressivity. The paper asks whether one can retain full multi-step flow expressivity while directly incorporating the critic's action gradient without backpropagation instability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes environment states and offline transition tuples (s, a, s', r) from OGBench domains (antmaze, humanoidmaze, cube, puzzle, scene), with action chunking of size h=5 for manipulation tasks. It outputs continuous actions generated by a multi-step flow-matching policy (T=10 integration steps) and Q-value estimates from an ensemble of K=10 critics using pessimistic value backup. During offline-to-online fine-tuning, the same offline RL objective is applied to a combined offline + online replay buffer without re-weighting.

## Method

QAM applies adjoint matching to policy optimization by formulating the flow policy's alignment with the optimal behavior-constrained policy π* ∝ π_β exp(τQ(s,a)) as a stochastic optimal control problem over a memoryless SDE. The key mechanism is computing 'lean' adjoint states via a reverse ODE that depends only on the base behavior flow model f_β (not the policy f_θ being optimized), then forming a squared loss that directly supervises f_θ's velocity field at each denoising step. This avoids backpropagation through the SDE entirely: the VJPs for adjoint state computation never involve f_θ's action gradient, so ill-conditioning in f_θ does not compound over the denoising chain. At convergence, the policy exactly recovers the optimal KL-behavior-constrained solution. Two practical variants extend the constraint set: QAM-FQL adds a 1-step noise-conditioned policy with a Wasserstein-2 bound to the QAM flow policy, and QAM-EDIT adds a bounded L∞ edit policy with entropy regularization for improved online fine-tuning.

## Results

On 50 OGBench tasks (10 domains × 5 tasks each, 12 seeds), QAM achieves an aggregated score of 44, outperforming all 13 baselines. The two extended variants push performance further: QAM-FQL reaches 45 and QAM-EDIT reaches 46. The closest prior methods are ReBRAC (40.1, a Gaussian policy), DSRL (38), and QSM (36.4), while backprop-based methods (FBRAC: 0.1, BAM: 35) and advantage-weighted methods (FAWAC: 8) perform substantially worse. In offline-to-online fine-tuning, QAM-EDIT is the most robust method across all 10 domains, outperforming all baselines on cube-triple and matching or exceeding them on the remaining tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QAM (44) outperforms the strongest prior flow/diffusion-based methods: QSM (36.4), CGQL-Linex (36.3), DAC (35.9), BAM (35), FQL (34.4), CGQL-MSE (34.4), IFQL (34), FEdit (32.2), and CGQL (20.3) by 8–24 points in aggregated score. It also beats the Gaussian baseline ReBRAC (40.1) by ~4 points, despite ReBRAC being competitive on locomotion tasks (antmaze, humanoidmaze) where it sometimes matches or exceeds flow-based methods. The backprop-based FBRAC (0.1) and advantage-weighted FAWAC (8) are dramatically worse, confirming that naive backpropagation through the denoising process and discarding the critic gradient are both ineffective. Among the QAM variants, QAM-EDIT (46) and QAM-FQL (45) further improve over base QAM (44) by relaxing the KL constraint via Wasserstein-bounded edit policies, with QAM-EDIT showing the strongest offline-to-online fine-tuning performance across all 10 domains. Notably, BAM (35), which uses the 'basic' adjoint matching objective (equivalent to backprop through the SDE), performs similarly to FQL (34.4), suggesting that the 'lean' adjoint state in QAM is critical for stability and performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

QAM directly adapts the adjoint matching technique from Domingo-Enrich et al. (2025), originally developed for fine-tuning flow/diffusion generative models, to the RL policy optimization setting. The key conceptual departure from prior flow-policy RL methods (FQL, DAC, QSM, CGQL) is that QAM uses the 'lean' adjoint state—computed solely from the frozen behavior flow model f_β rather than the policy being optimized f_θ—to construct a step-wise training objective that avoids the ill-conditioned backpropagation through the multi-step denoising process. This positions QAM as a theoretically grounded alternative to both backprop-based methods (FBRAC, FQL) that sacrifice stability or expressivity, and approximation-based guidance methods (DAC, QSM, CGQL) that lack convergence guarantees to the optimal behavior-constrained policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QAM resolves a long-standing tension in continuous-action offline RL between policy expressivity (multi-step flow/diffusion policies) and optimization tractability (leveraging the critic's action gradient), providing the first method with a guarantee that the optimal solution recovers the exact KL-behavior-constrained optimal policy. The QAM-EDIT variant, which relaxes the KL constraint via a Wasserstein-distance edit policy, addresses the practical support-mismatch problem and excels in both offline and offline-to-online settings. Open questions include sensitivity to the temperature parameter τ (which requires careful tuning), robustness when the critic is ill-conditioned, and whether the approach transfers to real-world robotic settings with action chunking—where the authors report initial success but limited evaluation.
