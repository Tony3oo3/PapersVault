## Problem

Prevailing world models are static, optimized for zero-shot or few-shot performance, and fail when confronted with novel or rare environments. While in-context learning (ICL) has been studied in language models and simple regression tasks, its role in world models—specifically which mechanisms underlie it and under what data/architecture conditions they emerge—remains largely unexplored. This paper formalizes ICL in world models, identifies two distinct mechanisms (Environment Recognition and Environment Learning), and derives theoretical conditions governing their emergence.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a long cross-episode context C_T of past observation-action pairs (RGB images at 128×128 or low-dimensional state vectors, plus discrete actions) alongside the current query q_t. Rather than performing full state estimation, it constructs a pseudo-state from the instantaneous observation and delegates all temporal encoding to the transition model. Outputs include a predicted next observation (decoded image or state vector), a predicted next latent state parameterized as a Gaussian N(ŝ_t, σ̂²_s), and k-step autoregressive future observations for multi-step prediction.

## Method

L2World is a long-context world model built on gated slot attention layers with chunk-wise parallelization during training and recurrent inference, paired with lightweight VAE encoders/decoders (ResNet stacks for images, MLPs for low-dimensional observations). The core theoretical contribution formalizes world-model ICL into two modes—Environment Recognition (ER), which identifies the current environment from parametric memory, and Environment Learning (EL), which directly accumulates transition evidence from context—and derives total-variation error upper bounds showing EL decays as T^{-1/2} while ER carries a non-decaying best-matching error term. Training uses supervised transition prediction with an overshooting strategy (randomly masking s_t to encourage long-horizon prediction), pre-training the image VAE separately before joint fine-tuning. The key empirical finding is that EL emerges only when both environment diversity (many distinct environments) and context length are sufficient, while over-training on few environments shifts the model back toward ER.

## Results

On unseen procedurally generated mazes, L2World (Maze-32K-L) achieves 24.65 PSNR at T=10,000 for 1-step prediction, outperforming Dreamer-v3 (22.12) and NWM (17.85) by 2.5 and 6.8 dB respectively. On seen mazes, L2World (Maze-128-L) reaches 26.00 PSNR at T=10,000, the highest among all configurations. A key finding is that performance monotonically improves with context length T for L2World, confirming the emergence of in-context learning, while Dreamer and NWM plateau or degrade at long T due to architectural limitations (LSTM memory and 4-frame horizon, respectively). On ProcTHOR, L2World pre-trained on Maze-32K-L and fine-tuned on ProcTHOR-40K achieves 23.94 PSNR at T=10,000, surpassing Dreamer (22.76) and NWM (20.08).

## Baselines & Comparisons

L2World consistently outperforms both Dreamer-v3 and NWM on unseen maze environments at long context lengths (T ≥ 100), with the gap widening as T increases: at T=10,000, L2World (Maze-32K-L) achieves 24.65 PSNR versus Dreamer's 22.12 and NWM's 17.85. On seen mazes, L2World (Maze-128-L) leads with 26.00 PSNR at T=10,000, beating Dreamer (22.43) and NWM (21.06). Dreamer's LSTM-based temporal encoding and NWM's fixed 4-frame diffusion horizon prevent them from leveraging long contexts effectively, causing their performance to plateau or even degrade (Dreamer Maze-128-L drops to 13.50 at T=10K unseen). On ProcTHOR, L2World with Maze-32K-L pre-training transfers to unseen ProcTHOR scenarios with 23.94 PSNR at T=10,000, exceeding Dreamer (22.76) and NWM (20.08), demonstrating that the EL mechanism learned on diverse mazes generalizes across domains. NWM achieves the highest single-frame PSNR at T=1 (20.84 seen) due to its diffusion-based image backbone, but this advantage vanishes and reverses as context length grows, confirming that per-frame fidelity does not substitute for long-horizon ICL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: world models (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018; Dreamer series; NWM) and in-context learning (Brown et al., 2020; Chan et al., 2022). It repurposes the task-recognition vs. task-learning distinction from ICL theory (Pan et al., 2023; Park et al., 2025) into the world-model setting, renaming them Environment Recognition (ER) and Environment Learning (EL), and is the first to derive formal error bounds for both mechanisms in a POMDP/MDP context. Architecturally, L2World departs from the LSTM-based temporal encoding of Dreamer and the diffusion-based frame prediction of NWM by adopting gated slot attention (linear attention) to handle arbitrarily long cross-episode contexts, trading per-frame fidelity for temporal scalability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical characterization of when and why ICL emerges in world models, showing that both high environment diversity and long context are necessary conditions for EL to dominate over ER. This reframes the design question for embodied AI: rather than optimizing zero-shot frame-level accuracy, one should intentionally diversify training environments and extend context windows to elicit self-adaptive prediction. Key limitations include confinement to the dynamic (transition) model only—reward and policy models are unaddressed—and reliance on synthetic environments (randomized cart-poles, procedural mazes, ProcTHOR), leaving open whether the ER/EL dynamics and the long-context requirement hold in real-world, high-dimensional settings. The natural next step the authors identify is In-Context Reinforcement Learning, where ICL mechanisms must operate jointly across dynamics, reward, and policy.
