## Problem

Diffusion Language Models (DLMs) are rapidly emerging as an alternative to autoregressive LLMs, yet their susceptibility to Membership Inference Attacks remains critically unexplored. Unlike ARMs with a single fixed left-to-right prediction pattern, DLMs' bidirectional masking creates exponentially many independent probing configurations, but existing MIA methods designed for ARMs or image diffusion models perform near-randomly (AUC ≈ 0.50) on DLMs, leaving a fundamental gap in understanding and quantifying their privacy risks.

## Input / Output

The attack consumes a text sequence (truncated to 512 tokens), a fine-tuned target DLM, and a pre-trained reference DLM under a grey-box model where the adversary can submit custom partially-masked inputs and receive per-token logits or probability distributions. At each of T=16 progressive masking steps (density from 5% to 50%), the method samples N=128 random subsets of m=10 masked positions, computing localized loss differences between reference and target models. The output is a single membership score φ ∈ [0,1] formed by weighted aggregation of binary sign indicators, where values closer to 1 indicate higher likelihood of training membership.

## Method

SAMA exploits the key insight that DLMs' membership signals are sparse and configuration-dependent: only specific mask configurations align with memorized token relationships, while most yield noise dominated by heavy-tailed domain-adaptation effects. The method combines three components: (1) progressive masking that systematically increases density from 5% to 50% across 16 steps to capture memorization patterns manifesting at different scales; (2) robust subset aggregation that samples 128 random subsets of 10 tokens per configuration and converts localized reference-minus-target loss differences into binary sign indicators, making detection invariant to the magnitude of heavy-tailed outliers; (3) inverse-step weighting (w_t = (1/t)/H) that prioritizes sparse masks where per-token signals are cleaner while still incorporating cumulative evidence from denser configurations. This transforms membership detection from finding a single large signal in noise into a robust multi-probe voting mechanism, achieving 30% relative AUC improvement over the best baseline (Ratio) and up to 8× improvement at low false positive rates.

## Results

SAMA achieves an average AUC of 0.81 across six MIMIR datasets (LLaDA-8B-Base), a 30% relative improvement over the best baseline (Ratio, AUC 0.62). At TPR@1%FPR, SAMA reaches 0.16 on average versus 0.04 for the best baseline, a 4× improvement; at TPR@0.1%FPR the gap widens to up to 8×. On the GitHub dataset where memorization is strongest, SAMA attains AUC 0.88 and TPR@10%FPR of 0.65, while the best baseline (Ratio) achieves only 0.74 and 0.36. Most traditional ARM-based MIA methods perform near random (AUC ≈ 0.50) on DLMs, and even diffusion-specific methods (SecMI, PIA) barely exceed 0.52 AUC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAMA decisively outperforms all 12 baselines across all nine datasets and all FPR thresholds. The strongest baseline is Ratio (reference-model loss calibration, Watson et al. 2021), which achieves an average AUC of 0.62; SAMA improves this by 30% relative (to 0.81) and by up to 8× at TPR@0.1%FPR. Traditional ARM-based methods—Loss, ZLIB, Lowercase, Min-K%, Min-K%++, ReCall, CON-ReCall, and Neighbor—all perform near random (AUC ≈ 0.48–0.52) on DLMs, confirming that autoregressive MIA strategies fail to capture diffusion-specific memorization patterns. The two diffusion-specific baselines adapted from the image domain (SecMI and PIA) also show limited effectiveness, with AUCs barely exceeding 0.52, validating the paper's hypothesis that DLMs require fundamentally different attack strategies. The query-free BoWs baseline is the second-best non-calibrated method (AUC up to 0.66 on GitHub) but still trails SAMA by 0.22 AUC on that dataset. The gap between SAMA and all baselines is most pronounced at low FPR thresholds (1% and 0.1%), which are the most practically relevant for real-world privacy risk assessment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reference-model calibration paradigm for membership inference (Watson et al., 2021; Mireshghallah et al., 2022a) from autoregressive and image-diffusion models to discrete masked diffusion language models (DLMs). The key conceptual departure is recognizing that DLMs' bidirectional masking creates configuration-dependent, sparse membership signals—fundamentally different from the single fixed prediction pattern in ARMs or the continuous denoising trajectory in image diffusion—necessitating a new aggregation strategy (sign-based subset voting with progressive masking) rather than the loss-averaging or trajectory-analysis approaches used in prior MIAs. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first systematic MIA study on DLMs, noting that existing ARM and image-diffusion attacks are architecturally mismatched to the discrete mask-and-predict mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With DLMs rapidly entering production (Gemini Diffusion, LLaDA, Dream), this work reveals that their privacy vulnerabilities are qualitatively different from ARMs: all twelve existing MIA baselines perform at near-random chance (AUC ≈ 0.50) on DLMs, meaning current privacy defenses calibrated for autoregressive models provide no protection. The 30% AUC improvement and up to 8× gain at low FPR rates demonstrate that DLMs are significantly more vulnerable than previously assumed, creating urgent demand for tailored defenses. Open questions include whether SAMA generalizes to other diffusion paradigms (continuous-time, flow-matching), whether the sign-based aggregation transfers to ARM or masked LM settings, and what minimal privacy guarantees (e.g., DP fine-tuning) can neutralize these configuration-dependent signals.
