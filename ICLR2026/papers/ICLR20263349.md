## Problem

The field lacks a reproducible, large-scale simulation benchmark for systematically evaluating generalist robot policies. Existing simulation frameworks support only limited tasks and narrow environment distributions, making it impossible to study how task diversity, scene variation, and dataset scale independently affect policy generalization, while real-world evaluation is resource-intensive and non-reproducible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263349/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Policies consume three 256×256 RGB images (one wrist camera, two third-person cameras), a natural-language task instruction, and proprioceptive state (base pose, end-effector pose, gripper state). The output is a 12-dimensional action vector at 20 Hz: seven dimensions for the arm (3 translation, 3 rotation, 1 gripper) and five for the mobile base (2D translation, yaw, torso height, and a binary gate for base control), executed via an Operational Space Controller on a Franka Panda with Omron mobile base.

## Method

RoboCasa365 scales the RoboCasa simulator to 365 tasks (65 atomic, 300 composite) across 2,500 kitchen scenes (50 layouts × 50 styles sourced from real Zillow listings), with 612 hours of human teleoperation data and 1,615 hours of MimicGen synthetic data. The benchmark evaluates four SOTA vision-language-action models (Diffusion Policy, π0, π0.5, GR00T N1.5) under three learning paradigms: massively multi-task training on 300 tasks, foundation model training (pretrain on 300 tasks then fine-tune on 50 target tasks), and four-phase lifelong learning with progressively longer-horizon tasks. Key empirical findings include a ~3× data-efficiency gain from pretraining, the observation that human data from 300 diverse tasks outperforms 1,615 hours of synthetic data on downstream tasks, and severe catastrophic forgetting in lifelong settings where prior-task success drops from 41.5% to 10.6% across four phases.

## Results

On the RoboCasa365 multi-task benchmark (300 pretraining tasks, 50 target tasks), GR00T N1.5 achieves the highest average task success rate at 20.0%, followed by π0.5 (16.9%), π0 (15.0%), and Diffusion Policy (6.1%). Performance drops sharply from Atomic tasks (15.7–43.0%) to Composite-Seen (0.2–9.6%) to Composite-Unseen (0.7–4.4%), highlighting the difficulty of long-horizon and novel-task generalization. In the foundation model setting, pretraining followed by fine-tuning on 100% target data yields 51.1% average success (vs. 43.7% for target-only), with a ~3× data-efficiency gain. In real-world transfer, sim-and-real co-training reaches 79.8% average success versus 61.8% for real-only training (+18.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GR00T N1.5 outperforms all three baselines across every task split, with the largest margin on Composite-Seen tasks (9.6% vs. 7.1% for π0.5, 5.2% for π0, and 0.2% for Diffusion Policy). On Composite-Unseen (zero-shot) tasks, GR00T N1.5 achieves 4.4% success, roughly 3–6× higher than the other methods, indicating stronger generalization to novel tasks. π0.5 and π0 are closely matched (16.9% vs. 15.0% average), with π0.5 consistently ahead by 1–2 points on each split. Diffusion Policy trails substantially (6.1% average), particularly on composite tasks where it scores near zero, underscoring the advantage of high-capacity vision-language-action architectures over diffusion-based policies for large, diverse multi-task datasets. The authors note that absolute success rates remain low across all methods, positioning RoboCasa365 as a challenging benchmark with significant room for improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoboCasa365 directly extends the RoboCasa framework (Nasiriany et al., 2024), scaling it from 30 tasks/100 scenes/100k demos to 365 tasks/2,500 scenes/2,000+ hours of data, while retaining the same MuJoCo/RoboSuite simulation stack and Operational Space Controller. It also leverages MimicGen (Mandlekar et al., 2023) for synthetic data generation and the digital cousin approach (Dai et al., 2024) for scene construction. Unlike prior simulation benchmarks (RLBench, AI2-THOR, Behavior-1k, Procthor) that focus on narrow task or environment distributions, RoboCasa365 is the first to combine hundreds of tasks, thousands of unique scenes, and large-scale demonstration datasets in a single framework, positioning itself as a model-agnostic testbed for VLA architectures (Diffusion Policy, π0, π0.5, GR00T N1.5) rather than proposing a new policy method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RoboCasa365 fills a critical gap by providing a reproducible, large-scale simulation benchmark that enables systematic study of how task diversity, environment variation, and dataset scale affect generalist robot learning—something infeasible with real-world evaluation alone. Key open questions remain: the benchmark is limited to kitchen environments, the sim-to-real gap persists (though the 18% real-world improvement from sim+real co-training is encouraging), and the finding that 1,615 hours of synthetic MimicGen data *degraded* performance relative to 404 hours of human data raises important questions about data quality vs. quantity that the field has not yet resolved. Lifelong learning results showing severe catastrophic forgetting across phases highlight that continuous skill acquisition remains fundamentally unsolved.
