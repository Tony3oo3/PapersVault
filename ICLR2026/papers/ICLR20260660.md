## Problem

Diffusion language models parallelize generation across positions but typically require hundreds to thousands of refinement steps to match autoregressive quality, effectively trading serial depth for iterative breadth. Discrete flow-matching (DFM) inherits this bottleneck: a 1024-token generation needs ~1024 model evaluations, making it impractical for low-latency deployment. FS-DFM targets the gap by achieving 1024-step DFM perplexity in just 8 sampling steps (128× fewer evaluations) without sacrificing long-horizon text quality.

## Input / Output

The model takes a partially observed token sequence (a 512-token prefix for continuation, or a fully masked/uniform 1024-token source for unconditional generation) together with an explicit step budget S that sets the per-step size h = 1/S. At each of the S sampling steps, the DiT-style transformer produces per-position logits that drive a CTMC jump process: each token independently decides whether to change via an exponential holding-time draw, and if it jumps, the new token is sampled from the off-diagonal of the predicted distribution. The output is a fully resolved 1024-token sequence (or 512-token continuation) using the GPT-2 tokenizer, with a single checkpoint supporting any budget S from 1 to 1024 without retraining.

## Method

FS-DFM builds on the DFM/CTMC framework with two key modifications. First, the generator is made step-aware by conditioning on the intended step size h alongside time t, and is trained with a self-consistency constraint: an EMA-stabilized RK-4 shortcut teacher approximates the cumulative effect of many small ODE sub-steps over [t, t+h], and the student is distilled to match this teacher's averaged logits for large h while following the standard DFM path loss for small h (budget-aware blending at threshold τ). Second, the instantaneous velocity scale g(t) = κ̇(t)/(1−κ(t)) is replaced by a Cumulative Scalar ḡ_{t,h} = (1/h)·ln((1−κ(t))/(1−κ(t+h))), obtained by integrating the scheduler rate over the finite step interval; this corrects the under-shooting that occurs when a large step starts at small t where g(t) is too weak to trigger token jumps. The backbone is a DiT-style transformer with rotary attention and adaptive layer norm driven by fused time/step-size embeddings, pretrained as plain DFM on 65.5B FineWeb-Edu tokens and then fine-tuned with the step-aware objective on an additional 4–25B tokens.

## Results

FS-DFM achieves perplexity parity with a 1024-step discrete-flow baseline using only 8 sampling steps for 1024-token generation, delivering up to 128× faster sampling. At 8 NFEs, the 0.17B model reaches PPL 75.78 (vs. 85.61 for 1024-step DFM), while the 1.3B model reaches PPL 70.97. Against LLaDA-8B and Dream-7B, FS-DFM models (4–47× smaller) achieve substantially lower perplexity at 8 steps (70–76 vs. 441–739) with well-calibrated entropy (~8.0 vs. 0.4–1.7), and MAUVE scores 50–100× higher (0.27–0.48 vs. 0.005–0.006).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original DFM baselines (Gat et al., 2024) at matched model sizes, FS-DFM reduces 8-step perplexity by 3.7× (0.17B: 90.49 vs. 335.64), 4.3× (1.3B: 77.21 vs. 331.36), and 6.0× (1.7B: 73.65 vs. 444.71), confirming that the step-aware training and cumulative scalar are the source of the speedup. Against LLaDA-8B and Dream-7B, FS-DFM's smallest model (0.17B) already outperforms both at 8 steps (PPL 75.78 vs. 441.26 and 739.40), and the 1.3B/1.7B models extend the gap to ~6× and ~10× respectively; LLaDA and Dream also exhibit degenerate outputs (repeated tokens, trailing blanks) under few-step budgets. Against multi-round refinement (SDTT, 7 rounds, 0.86B), FS-DFM 0.17B achieves lower PPL (90.49 vs. 131.71) with a single 8-step pass rather than 7 sequential rounds. Against hybrid discrete-continuous HDLM (0.16B, 524B training tokens), FS-DFM 0.17B (65.5B + 24.6B tokens) achieves PPL 90.49 vs. 192.74–279.60, demonstrating superior training-token efficiency. Against masked-diffusion and re-masking systems (MDLM-OWT, SEDD, ReMDM variants), FS-DFM outperforms all by 3–15× in perplexity (90.49 vs. 549–1359), despite using a fraction of the training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

FS-DFM extends the Discrete Flow Matching (DFM) paradigm of Gat et al. (2024), which models text generation as a Continuous-Time Markov Chain with a learned velocity field, by making the number of sampling steps an explicit, trainable parameter. It incorporates the shortcut-model principle from Frans et al. (2025) via RK-4 ODE integration with an EMA-stabilized teacher, and builds on the CTMC/Kolmogorov-equation formalism of Campbell et al. (2022). The key departure from prior few-step discrete diffusion work (e.g., SDTT, which still requires 16–256 steps) is the Cumulative Scalar update that replaces the instantaneous scale g(t) with an interval-integrated quantity, enabling reliable single large jumps in token space rather than many small Euler steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 128× reduction in sampling steps (8 vs. 1024) while matching perplexity on 1024-token generation is a significant practical advance for discrete flow-matching language models, and the release of code and checkpoints addresses a noted gap in public DFM resources. However, evaluation is confined to perplexity and masked-token accuracy on WikiText-103; downstream task performance (instruction following, QA, multilingual), longer contexts (8–32k tokens), and alignment objectives remain untested. Open questions include whether other ODE solvers or hybrid source schedulers (mask + uniform) could further improve few-step fidelity, and whether the step-aware conditioning generalizes to conditional or structured generation tasks.
