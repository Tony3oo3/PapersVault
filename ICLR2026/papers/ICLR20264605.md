## Problem

Standard flow matching (FM) achieves high sample quality by concentrating mass around training points, a form of memorisation that undermines generalisation, novelty, and data privacy. The paper identifies that FM's conditional probability path uses homogeneous, isotropic Gaussian noise that is geometrically oblivious to the data manifold, causing tangential collapse onto training points. The quality-generalisation tradeoff is shown to depend not merely on dataset size but on local geometry and sampling sparsity, meaning even large datasets suffer localised memorisation in sparse regions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of N training points in R^d (concentrated around an unknown lower-dimensional manifold) plus a precomputed carré du champ matrix Γ̂ at each point, estimated via a variable-bandwidth kNN diffusion kernel. The source is standard Gaussian noise N(0,I), and the output is a learned velocity field that generates samples covering the data manifold. For trajectory interpolation tasks (single-cell, motion capture), the same framework produces interpolated trajectories between unpaired temporal snapshots, with the velocity field parameterised by MLPs, UNets, or transformers depending on the domain.

## Method

CDC-FM replaces FM's isotropic conditional covariance σ_t²I with an anisotropic, inhomogeneous covariance Σ_t^Γ(x_1) = [(1−t)I + tΓ̂(x_1)^{1/2}]², making the conditional path the displacement (optimal transport) interpolant between N(0,I) and a local anisotropic Gaussian N(x_1, Γ̂(x_1)) aligned with the data manifold. The matrix Γ̂ is estimated from data using diffusion geometry: a variable-bandwidth Gaussian kernel over k nearest neighbours defines a local Markov transition kernel, and Γ̂(x^(i)) is the covariance of that kernel, proven to be the optimal local Gaussian fit. This construction induces a geometry-aware anisotropic diffusion term in the continuity equation, biasing the learned velocity field to flow perpendicular to the manifold and suppressing tangential flows that cause memorisation. The paper proves that this path is strictly superior to naive data augmentation (which yields suboptimal transport paths) and that the induced Dirichlet energy equals the carré du champ, providing a principled justification. The method is a drop-in regulariser: the standard FM loss is unchanged, only the conditional flow path is modified, and the additional preprocessing cost is O(N log N) with O(N) memory.

## Results

On single-cell gene expression interpolation (CITE-seq and Multiomics), OT-CDC-FM achieves the lowest Earth Mover's Distance (44.41 ± 0.99 on Cite; 52.04 ± 1.95 on Multi), outperforming OT-FM (45.39 ± 0.42; 54.81 ± 5.86). On CelebA-HQ in latent space (1000 images), CDC-FM reaches FID 10.55 at 3000 epochs versus 13.56 for FM, with NLL 6.68 vs 6.80. On LiDAR terrain reconstruction at 16k epochs with 40 training points, CDC-FM reduces memorisation from 32.4% to 7.5% and improves NLL from 3.50 to 2.23. On CIFAR-10 with <10k training images, FM memorises all training points (M < 0.2) while CDC-FM memorises only a few percent, with comparable FID at later epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard FM (Lipman et al., 2023), CDC-FM consistently improves the quality–generalisation tradeoff across all tested domains. On single-cell interpolation, adding CDC regularisation reduces EMD by ~1.0 on Cite and ~2.8 on Multi over FM, with much tighter variance (±1.95 vs ±5.86 on Multi). On CelebA-HQ latent-space generation, CDC-FM achieves lower FID (10.55 vs 13.56 at 3k epochs) and lower NLL (6.68 vs 6.80), indicating better sample quality and generalisation simultaneously. On LiDAR terrain, CDC-FM reduces memorisation by 4–7× at 16k epochs (e.g., 7.5% vs 32.4% at 40 points) while maintaining comparable or better distance-to-manifold. On CIFAR-10 in the low-data regime (<10k images), FM undergoes a memorisation phase transition where all training points are reproduced, whereas CDC-FM keeps memorisation to a few percent with comparable FID. Against OT-FM (Tong et al., 2024), CDC-FM with optimal transport pairing (OT-CDC-FM) further improves EMD on both single-cell benchmarks, confirming that geometric regularisation is complementary to transport-based pairing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) by replacing the homogeneous, isotropic Gaussian noise in the conditional probability path with a spatially-varying, anisotropic Gaussian whose covariance is estimated from local data geometry via diffusion maps (Coifman & Lafon, 2006) and diffusion geometry (Jones, 2024a;b). Conceptually, it departs from prior geometric regularisation in supervised learning (tangent prop, manifold tangent classifiers) by applying geometry as a regularisation of the generative probability path rather than as a constraint on the loss or architecture. It also distinguishes itself from manifold-constrained generative models (Chen & Lipman, 2024; Huang et al., 2022) by using geometry to shape the noise structure rather than restricting the model to a predefined manifold, and from prior memorisation analyses in diffusion models (Achilli et al., 2024; Ross et al., 2025) by providing a constructive fix rather than a diagnostic framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CDC-FM provides a principled, plug-in regulariser for existing flow matching pipelines that demonstrably breaks the quality-generalisation-memorisation frontier rather than merely shifting it, with the strongest gains in data-scarce, geometrically structured, or heterogeneously sampled settings common in AI-for-science applications. A key open limitation is that the kNN-based tangent space estimation becomes exponentially harder in high intrinsic dimensions, and the benefits diminish at large scale where architecture-induced inductive biases dominate. Future work could explore learning the carré du champ end-to-end, extending the geometric regularisation to other generative paradigms (diffusion, score matching), and understanding whether the displacement-interpolant optimality result generalises beyond Gaussian marginals.
