## Problem

Multi-teacher knowledge distillation for LLMs suffers from knowledge conflicts among teacher rationales (due to hallucinations, divergent reasoning paths, and differing expertise domains) and escalating resource demands as the teacher ensemble grows. The paper demonstrates empirically that TinyLLM's distillation performance actually degrades when increasing from 2 to 4 teacher LLMs, revealing that naively adding more teachers is counterproductive without a mechanism to resolve inter-teacher conflicts.

## Input / Output

The purification process takes a question q, a set of k candidate options O, and the rationales R = {r_{T1}, ..., r_{Tn}} generated by n teacher LLMs, and produces a single consolidated rationale r_P that replaces all n rationales in the distillation loss. A key practical distinction is that routing-based methods (PL ranking, PLM classifier, similarity-based router) require only the question as input and select which teacher to sample from, eliminating the need to pre-generate all n rationales, whereas aggregation and RL-based selection consume all rationales. The final output is a trained student model (FLAN-T5 small/base/large at 77M/248M/783M parameters) that answers multiple-choice questions.

## Method

The paper introduces Knowledge Purification, formalized as r_P = f(R), which consolidates n teacher rationales into one before distillation, changing the multi-teacher loss from a sum of n per-teacher distillation losses to a single loss on the purified rationale. Five purification methods are proposed: (1) Knowledge Aggregation uses GPT-4 with in-context instruction to merge rationales; (2) Plackett-Luce Ranking learns teacher scores via similarity-weighted cross-entropy with a closed-form solution (Proposition 1); (3) PLM Classifier uses mDeBERTaV3-base CLS embeddings with a 2-layer MLP for routing; (4) Similarity-based Router trains n LLM embeddings via dual contrastive losses (sample-LLM and sample-sample) and routes by cosine similarity; (5) RL-based Teacher Selection uses policy gradient where the state concatenates question and rationale embeddings gated by correctness, with reward equal to negative distillation loss, alternating selector and student updates. The similarity-based router achieves the highest Conflict Mitigation Value (CMV) across all student sizes and the best out-of-domain generalization on PIQA and BioASQ, while routing methods reduce total GPU hours from 2.6 (TinyLLM) to ~1.4–1.8 by sampling only one rationale per question.

## Results

On the four in-domain datasets (OBQA, ARC, Riddle, PQA), the RL-based Teacher Selection achieves the highest average accuracy of 67.55% for the 783M FLAN-T5 large student, surpassing the best multi-teacher baseline TinyLLM (62.53%) by 5.02%. The Similarity-based Router attains the best average of 45.66% for the 77M student, exceeding all baselines by at least 4.9%. On out-of-domain datasets (PIQA, BioASQ), the Similarity-based Router achieves 69.53% and 91.87% respectively for the 783M student, outperforming TinyLLM by 0.65% and 8.94%. The distilled 783M student exceeds the average accuracy of three of the four teacher LLMs, demonstrating effective knowledge transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TinyLLM (the primary multi-teacher KD baseline), all five knowledge purification methods improve average accuracy for the 783M student: Teacher Selection (+5.02%), Similarity-based Router (+4.67%), PLM Classifier (+3.87%), PL Ranking (+1.97%), and Knowledge Aggregation (+0.79%). Against Distilling-Step-by-Step (single-teacher rationale distillation), the best purification methods (Teacher Selection, Similarity-based Router) outperform by 4.79% and 4.44% respectively on average for the 783M student. Against ABKD (state-of-the-art single-teacher KD with α-β divergence, achieving 63.79% average), the RL-based Teacher Selection surpasses it by 3.76% while using four teachers. Knowledge Aggregation is the weakest proposed method, showing only marginal gains over TinyLLM and failing to mitigate knowledge conflicts (negative CMV across all student sizes), suggesting that simply synthesizing rationales with GPT-4 does not effectively resolve inter-teacher disagreements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-teacher knowledge distillation framework of TinyLLM (Tian et al., 2025) by introducing a 'knowledge purification' step that consolidates multiple teacher rationales into a single one before distillation, rather than feeding all rationales simultaneously as TinyLLM does. The purification mechanisms draw from the LLM routing literature: the similarity-based router adapts RouterDC's dual contrastive learning (Chen et al., 2024), the Plackett-Luce ranking borrows the similarity-weighted optimization from RouterLLM (Ong et al., 2024), and the RL-based teacher selection follows the policy-gradient paradigm of Yuan et al. (2021). The rationale-based distillation loss itself is inherited from Distilling-Step-by-Step (Hsieh et al., 2023), which established the cross-entropy formulation for supervising students on teacher-generated rationales.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a practical bottleneck in multi-teacher distillation—performance degrades as more teachers are added—and offers a principled remedy (purification via routing) that also reduces computational cost by sampling only one rationale per question. The finding that a powerful aggregator (GPT-4) fails to improve distillation while lightweight routers succeed is a useful negative result that redirects practitioner attention toward selection over synthesis. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond four teachers and multiple-choice QA, whether it generalizes to generative or multi-turn settings, and whether the modest absolute gains (2–7% over TinyLLM) persist with stronger student models or larger teacher ensembles.
