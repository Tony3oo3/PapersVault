## Problem

Vanilla Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD/DMD2) fails to converge stably on large-scale flow-based text-to-image models such as SD 3.5 Large (8B) and FLUX.1 dev (12B), even with the two time-scale update rule (TTUR). The inner best response in the DMD min-max game—requiring the fake distribution to track the generator's evolving distribution—is brittle and expensive at scale, and uniform timestep sampling ignores the highly non-uniform denoising importance across the trajectory.

## Input / Output

The distilled generator takes a text prompt and produces a 1024×1024 RGB image in 4 NFE (or 2/1 NFE after light fine-tuning). At inference, the generator is initialized from the teacher's weights and operates on a sparse set of coarse anchor timesteps (e.g., {0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0} for SD 3.5 or {0.512, 0.759, 0.904, 1.0} for FLUX), each producing one denoising step. During training, the generator receives a noisy latent x_{τ_i} constructed either by backward simulation from noise or by forward diffusion from a real image, and outputs a single-step denoised sample.

## Method

SenseFlow builds on DMD2 and introduces three key components to scale distribution matching distillation to 8B–12B flow-based models. First, Implicit Distribution Alignment (IDA) applies a lightweight proximal update φ ← λφ + (1−λ)θ after each generator step, nudging the fake score network toward the generator to maintain p_f ≈ p_g as an ε-best inner response, which provably stabilizes the min-max game without requiring expensive high-ratio TTUR. Second, Intra-Segment Guidance (ISG) relocates the teacher's timestep-wise denoising importance: for each coarse anchor τ_i, an intermediate t_mid is sampled, the teacher denoises from τ_i to t_mid, the generator continues from t_mid to τ_{i−1}, and an L2 loss aligns the generator's direct τ_i→τ_{i−1} prediction with this two-step trajectory, making each anchor absorb fine-grained segment information. Third, a VFM-based discriminator replaces the naive latent-space discriminator with frozen DINOv2 and CLIP backbones plus trainable spectral-normalized heads, providing semantically rich adversarial signals in the image domain, weighted by timestep signal power ω(t)=(1−σ_t)² to balance DMD and GAN gradients across noise levels.

## Results

On COCO-5K and GenEval, SenseFlow achieves state-of-the-art 4-step distillation across three teacher models. For SD 3.5 Large, the Euler variant attains GenEval 0.7098 (vs. 0.7140 for the 80-NFE teacher) and surpasses the teacher on HPSv2 (0.3008 vs. 0.2993), PickScore (22.90 vs. 22.98), and ImageReward (1.2062 vs. 1.1629). For FLUX.1 dev, the Euler variant achieves the best Patch FID-T (20.29) and highest PickScore (23.26) among all 4-step methods, while the stochastic variant leads on GenEval (0.6471). On SDXL, SenseFlow ranks first on HPSv2 (0.3010), PickScore (23.17), ImageReward (0.9951), and GenEval (0.5784), though it trails DMD2-SDXL on Patch FID-T (21.01 vs. 18.72).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On SD 3.5 Large, SenseFlow decisively outperforms the only available 4-step baseline (SD 3.5 Turbo) on every metric: Patch FID-T drops from 22.88 to 17.48, HPSv2 rises from 0.2909 to 0.3016, and ImageReward jumps from 1.0116 to 1.1713; the Euler variant even exceeds the 80-NFE teacher on HPSv2, PickScore, and ImageReward. On FLUX.1 dev, SenseFlow beats Hyper-FLUX on Patch FID-T (19.60 vs. 23.47) and GenEval (0.6471 vs. 0.6193), and surpasses FLUX-Turbo-Alpha on all metrics except CLIP; however, it trails FLUX.1-schnell on GenEval (0.6471 vs. 0.6807) and CLIP (0.3167 vs. 0.3264). On SDXL, SenseFlow leads DMD2-SDXL on HPSv2 (0.3010 vs. 0.2963), PickScore (23.17 vs. 22.98), ImageReward (0.9951 vs. 0.9324), and GenEval (0.5784 vs. 0.5779), but concedes Patch FID-T (21.01 vs. 18.72) and CLIP (0.3248 vs. 0.3277); it also outperforms LCM, PCM, Flash, SDXL-Lightning, and Hyper-SDXL on most human-preference metrics. The paper notes that the VFM discriminator's semantic prior slightly reduces sample diversity (CLIP-diversity drops from 0.0985 to 0.0802 on SDXL), which explains the modest FID-T increase relative to DMD2-SDXL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends DMD2 (Yin et al., 2024a) and the original DMD score-distillation framework (Yin et al., 2024b) from smaller diffusion models (SD 1.5, SDXL) to large flow-matching backbones (SD 3.5 Large 8B, FLUX.1 dev 12B), where vanilla DMD2 fails to converge even with aggressive TTUR. It departs from the broader diffusion distillation landscape (consistency models, progressive distillation, adversarial distillation) by retaining the distribution-matching formulation but introducing three targeted modifications: a proximal parameter-alignment step (IDA) to stabilize the inner fake-model loop, intra-segment guidance (ISG) to redistribute timestep supervision, and a VFM-based (DINOv2/CLIP) discriminator replacing the naive latent-space discriminator. The theoretical analysis connects IDA to the ε-best-response condition in GAN theory (Goodfellow et al., 2014), providing a principled justification for the simple parameter-interpolation update.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: this is the first method to reliably distill 8B–12B flow-based T2I models into 4-step generators, and it extends to 2-step and (after light fine-tuning) 1-step generation, opening a path toward near-real-time inference from the largest open-source image models. The VFM discriminator introduces a measurable quality-diversity trade-off (slightly higher FID-T but better human-preference scores), raising the open question of whether this is fundamental to semantic-prior-based adversarial training or can be mitigated. Remaining open questions include whether the approach transfers to video diffusion, whether IDA generalizes to other min-max training regimes beyond DMD, and whether the 1-step regime can be achieved without the additional 6k-iteration fine-tuning schedule.
