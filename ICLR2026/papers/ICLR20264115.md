## Problem

While MLLM-based methods have advanced universal multimodal retrieval (UMR), the specific design decisions that drive effective embedding learning—such as how to extract embeddings from decoder-only architectures, how to configure contrastive training, and how to distill reranking knowledge—remain largely unexplored. Existing approaches adopt contrastive learning with varying recipes but lack systematic ablation, leading to suboptimal performance and limited generalization. This paper identifies which often-overlooked factors (attention type, pooling strategy, temperature scheduling, negative mining) actually determine retrieval quality.

## Input / Output

The model accepts heterogeneous queries—pure text, pure image, or interleaved image+text sequences—each prefixed with a natural-language task instruction specifying the target modality. During embedding extraction, instruction tokens are masked out from the mean-pooled representation to avoid bias, and the visual encoder is frozen while only the LLM component is fine-tuned via LoRA. The output is a single embedding vector per input, used for cosine-similarity scoring against candidate pools of up to 5.6M items; in the U-MARVEL+ variant, a separate pointwise reranker produces a second score that is linearly fused (α=0.5) with the embedding score to yield the final ranked list.

## Method

U-MARVEL is a three-stage training pipeline built on Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct with LoRA. Stage 1 (Progressive Transition) adapts the decoder-only MLLM to embedding tasks via a curriculum: text-only NLI retrieval → text-image CC3M retrieval → full multimodal M-BEIR instruction tuning, using bidirectional attention with mean pooling (no compression prompt) and a learnable InfoNCE temperature. Stage 2 (Hard Negative Mining) performs continual training with filtered hard negatives (top-5 per query, threshold 0.7 to exclude false negatives) mixed with in-batch negatives, with learning rate scaled proportionally to batch size. Stage 3 (Improved Distillation) compresses a recall-then-rerank cascade into the single embedding model by distilling KL-divergence over (query, positive, top-50 hard negatives) tuples rather than full in-batch similarity matrices, reducing computational cost from (n+6)N to (3k+7)N—approximately 4.1% of the original—while increasing feature diversity by 26×, making MLLM-scale distillation practically feasible in ~14 hours.

## Results

On the M-BEIR benchmark (local pool), U-MARVEL achieves an Average Recall of 63.2% as a single model, outperforming the previous best single-model method LamRA-Ret (56.6%) by 6.6 points, and even surpassing the two-stage LamRA pipeline (63.7%) with only single-stage inference. With the optional reranker (U-MARVEL+), the average rises to 64.8%. In zero-shot evaluation, U-MARVEL achieves state-of-the-art results on 9 out of 12 tasks (e.g., 96.4% R@1 on ShareGPT4V, 96.7% R@1 on Urban-1K, 85.4% R@1 on Flickr30K), and leads on text-to-video retrieval (47.2% R@1 on MSR-VTT, 54.6% R@1 on MSVD) without any video-specific fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

U-MARVEL (single model, Avg. 63.2%) decisively outperforms all single-model baselines on M-BEIR: CLIP-L (32.5%), SigLIP (37.2%), UniIR-BLIP (46.8%), UniIR-CLIP (50.6%), and LamRA-Ret (56.6%), with the largest gap over LamRA-Ret at +6.6 points. Against the two-stage LamRA pipeline (63.7%), U-MARVEL+ (64.8%) wins by 1.1 points while using only single-stage inference, demonstrating the effectiveness of the improved distillation strategy. In zero-shot settings, U-MARVEL surpasses VLM2Vec (e.g., 96.4 vs. 90.7 R@1 on ShareGPT4V), UniME (96.7 vs. 95.9 on Urban-1K), LamRA-Ret (85.4 vs. 82.8 on Flickr30K), E5-V, and MagicLens-L across most of the 12 evaluated tasks. On text-to-video retrieval, U-MARVEL (47.2% R@1 MSR-VTT, 54.6% R@1 MSVD) outperforms LLaVE-7B (46.8%, 52.9%), LamRA (44.7%, 52.4%), VLM2Vec (43.5%, 49.5%), and all video-specific models (InternVideo, ViCLIP, UMT-L, InternVideo2s2-6B) that were fine-tuned with text-video data, despite U-MARVEL being trained only on text-image data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a systematic empirical study of the design space for MLLM-based embedding models, building directly on the UMR task and M-BEIR benchmark introduced by UniIR (Wei et al., 2024) and the MLLM-based retrieval methods that followed (LamRA, MM-Embed, GME, UniME, VLM2Vec). Rather than proposing a new architecture, it repurposes the standard contrastive-learning pipeline (InfoNCE + LoRA on Qwen2-VL) and isolates which design decisions—embedding extraction strategy, attention pattern, training curriculum, negative sampling, and distillation protocol—actually drive performance. The progressive transition draws on curriculum learning (Bengio et al., 2009), the hard-negative filtering extends standard mining with a false-negative threshold, and the improved distillation restructures the recall-then-rerank paradigm from LamRA and MM-Embed into a single-model training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, ablation-backed recipe for building MLLM-based universal retrievers, which is valuable given the rapid proliferation of such systems without clear guidance on training details. The improved distillation method reducing computational cost to ~4% of the traditional approach while increasing feature diversity 26× makes MLLM distillation practically feasible for the first time, potentially enabling wider adoption of recall-then-rerank knowledge in single-stage models. Key limitations include restriction to text and image modalities (no audio or video in training), experiments limited to 7B-scale models, and unexplored integration with RAG pipelines. Open questions include whether the progressive transition and learnable-temperature findings generalize to larger or differently-architected MLLMs, and whether the framework extends to streaming or multi-turn retrieval settings.
