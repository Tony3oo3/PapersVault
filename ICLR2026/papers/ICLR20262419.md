## Problem

Vision Transformers partition images into uniformly sized patches regardless of content, producing disproportionately long token sequences for high-resolution inputs where much of the image is visually redundant. Existing token-reduction methods either apply a fixed reduction ratio that mismatches image complexity or require learned scoring networks that negate training speedups and complicate batched inference. The paper asks whether content-aware, variable patch sizes can be applied at the input level to accelerate both training and inference without learned components.

## Input / Output

APT consumes standard RGB images at resolutions ranging from 224×224 to 1536×1536 and produces a variable-length token sequence whose length depends on the image's spatial complexity (typically 30–70% of the fixed-patch baseline). Because sequence length varies per image, APT uses sequence packing with block-diagonal attention masks (compatible with FlashAttention) rather than fixed-shape batches. For dense prediction tasks (detection, segmentation), the variable-length token sequence is expanded back into a rectangular feature map by repeating larger-patch tokens 2^(2i) times, enabling standard transposed-convolution heads.

## Method

APT hierarchically assigns patch sizes by computing pixel-intensity entropy at multiple scales (e.g., 64×64, 32×32, 16×16) and retaining larger patches only where entropy falls below a per-scale threshold, following a quadtree-like grid structure. For patch aggregation, each large patch is both resized to the base size and decomposed into its constituent sub-patches; the sub-patch embeddings are downsampled via a convolutional layer and combined with the resized embedding through a zero-initialized MLP (inspired by ControlNet), so the model starts as a pure resizer and gradually learns to exploit high-resolution detail. Variable-length sequences are handled by concatenating tokens across the batch with a block-diagonal mask, and positional encodings for larger patches are obtained by interpolating the base-size encoding map. The approach requires no learned scoring network, converges in a single epoch of fine-tuning from any pretrained ViT checkpoint, and extends to window-attention architectures used in high-resolution detection and segmentation.

## Results

On ImageNet 1-epoch fine-tuning, APT achieves 88.3% top-1 accuracy on ViT-H/224 (matching the 88.3% baseline) with a 23% throughput speedup, and 88.4% on ViT-H/336 (vs. 88.5% baseline) with a 50% speedup. On full fine-tuning from MAE pretraining, APT matches baseline accuracy exactly (86.1% on ViT-L/336, 86.3% on ViT-L/448) while reducing wall-clock training time by 61% and 86% respectively. On LLaVA-1.5-7B VQA, APT increases throughput by 22% while matching or exceeding the baseline on 8 of 9 benchmarks (e.g., GQA 61.4 vs. 61.0, MME 1474.0 vs. 1510.1). On COCO object detection at 1536×1536, APT achieves 62.07 mAP (vs. 62.28 baseline) with a 30% speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four layer-level merging baselines (EViT, ToMe, PPT, DTEM), APT consistently outperforms all methods in both throughput and accuracy at every compute budget on both ViT-L/224 and ViT-H/336 (Figure 4), even when the baselines are re-implemented with FlashAttention to remove their inherent speed penalty. Against input-level baselines, APT matches or exceeds the Resizing-only baseline on accuracy at every model scale and resolution (e.g., 88.3% vs. 88.0% on ViT-H/224, 88.4% vs. 88.0% on ViT-H/336) while achieving the same throughput. Random masking, which drops the same fraction of tokens without content-awareness, loses 0.4–1.5% accuracy across all settings (e.g., 87.4% vs. 88.3% on ViT-H/224), confirming that entropy-based patch allocation is critical. On VQA (LLaVA-1.5-7B), APT matches or beats the frozen-vision baseline on 8 of 9 benchmarks while gaining 22% throughput, whereas Random loses 0.6–4.1 points and Resizing loses 0.5–1.7 points. On object detection (EVA-02-L/1536), APT achieves 62.07 mAP vs. 61.75 for Resizing and 62.28 for the full-resolution baseline, with a 30% speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

APT sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: input-level token reduction (DynamicViT, A-ViT) and multi-scale patchification (MS-ViT, Quadformer, CF-ViT). It departs from learned-scoring approaches (DynamicViT, MS-ViT) by replacing the gating network with a simple entropy-based heuristic, and from fixed-ratio merging methods (ToMe, EViT) by making the reduction content-aware at the input level rather than across layers. Two specific techniques are directly adopted: zero-initialization from ControlNet for the patch aggregation MLP, and sequence packing with positional encoding interpolation from NaViT for handling variable-length token sequences. The conceptual framing draws an explicit analogy to adaptive subword tokenizers in NLP (BPE, SentencePiece), which APT mirrors by allocating variable-granularity 'tokens' based on local content complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

APT offers a drop-in acceleration for any pretrained ViT backbone—requiring only one epoch of fine-tuning to recover full accuracy—making it immediately practical for the large ecosystem of MAE/EVA-02 checkpoints used across classification, detection, segmentation, and VLMs. The dramatic scaling of speedup with resolution (29% at 384px to 86% at 448px for ViT-L) suggests the method becomes increasingly valuable as the field pushes toward higher-resolution inputs. Key limitations remain: the entropy threshold is a hand-tuned hyperparameter that must be adjusted per task, the heuristic is agnostic to downstream objectives, and image generation is not yet supported. Open questions include whether a learned (but lightweight) patch-assignment module could replace the entropy heuristic, and whether the approach extends to the very high resolutions and large models used in generative vision.
