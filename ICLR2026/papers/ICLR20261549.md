## Problem

Robotic manipulation policies struggle to generalize to novel objects, limiting real-world utility, while cognitive science suggests children develop generalizable grasping by mastering a small set of simple toys. This paper asks whether robots can achieve similar zero-shot generalization by training exclusively on randomly assembled objects composed of just four shape primitives (spheres, cuboids, cylinders, rings) and transferring to complex real-world objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261549/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy conditions on C=16 past timesteps of multi-camera RGB images (two tabletop cameras plus one wrist camera for Franka; two side cameras plus one head camera for H1-2) and proprioceptive joint states (8-dim for Franka, 40-dim for H1-2), and predicts K=16 future action steps of absolute joint positions and gripper state. A key unusual input is the object segmentation mask (from SAM 2 in real-world settings, ground truth in simulation), which is not fed as a separate channel but instead used to construct attention masks inside the vision encoder. The entire model is only 86M parameters, orders of magnitude smaller than the VLA baselines it outperforms.

## Method

LEGO combines a pretrained ViT-L (MVP) vision encoder with a ViT-Base transformer policy backbone, trained via behavior cloning with L1 loss on action sequences. The core contribution is Detection Pooling (DetPool): a SAM 2 segmentation mask of the target object is used to set attention masks within the ViT so that object patch tokens attend only to other object patches (ignoring background and distractors), and the final object-centric visual feature is obtained by mean-pooling the object patch tokens. This object-centric representation is empirically shown to be the key to zero-shot generalization, boosting performance 22–48% over standard attention, CLS, or mean pooling baselines. Training uses 250 randomly generated 'Cezanne toys' (1–5 primitives each, randomized dimensions, rotations, and colors) with 1,500–2,500 teleoperated demonstrations, and the resulting policy achieves 67% zero-shot success on 64 YCB objects on a real Franka arm, outperforming 3B–7B parameter VLA models pretrained on far larger datasets.

## Results

In simulation, LEGO with DetPool achieves an 80% zero-shot grasping success rate on 65 YCB objects with 2,500 demonstrations, substantially outperforming OpenVLA-OFT (12.79%) and π₀-FAST (4.13%) which are 35–80× larger. On the real Franka robot, LEGO achieves a 66.67% success rate on 64 YCB objects using only 1,500 toy demonstrations, surpassing all baselines except finetuned π₀-FAST (76.56%). On the H1-2 humanoid with dexterous hands, LEGO reaches 50.77% average success across 13 everyday objects, nearly doubling the performance of π₀-FAST (26.15%) and OpenVLA-OFT (18.46%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEGO (86M params, no pretraining) decisively outperforms OpenVLA-OFT (7B, OXE-pretrained) across all settings: 80% vs 12.79% in simulation at 2,500 demos, 66.67% vs 9.47% on the real Franka, and 50.77% vs 18.46% on the H1-2 humanoid. Against π₀-FAST (3B, DROID-pretrained), LEGO wins in simulation (80% vs 4.13%) and on the H1-2 (50.77% vs 26.15%), and narrowly beats its zero-shot variant on the real Franka (66.67% vs 61.82%), but loses to the finetuned π₀-FAST (76.56%) which benefits from additional in-domain DROID demonstrations. LEGO also substantially outperforms the training-free ShapeGrasp (26.56%) on the real Franka, and dominates both object-centric baselines GROOT (1.54%) and DexGraspVLA (48.85%) in simulation. The consistent pattern is that LEGO's object-centric DetPool representation enables strong generalization from only 250 simple toys, while much larger pretrained models either overfit on small data or fail to transfer their pretraining knowledge to the toy-to-real gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEGO carves new ground at the intersection of developmental psychology (children learning to grasp from simple toys) and object-centric robotic manipulation. It departs from the dominant VLA scaling paradigm (π0-FAST, OpenVLA-OFT) by showing that a small 86M model trained on a tiny synthetic dataset of 250 primitive-composition 'toys' can outperform 3B–7B pretrained models on zero-shot grasping. The core architectural contribution, Detection Pooling (DetPool), repurposes a standard ViT with a SAM-2-derived attention mask and mean pooling to produce an object-centric representation, building conceptually on prior object-centric manipulation work (Devin et al. 2017, OTTER, GROOT, DexGraspVLA) but replacing their more complex mechanisms with a lightweight, plug-and-play attention-masking strategy applicable to any pretrained vision transformer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that a simple object-centric visual representation, not model or data scale, is the key to zero-shot grasping generalization—challenges the prevailing assumption in robotic manipulation that generalization requires massive pretraining corpora. DetPool is lightweight and transferable to any ViT, making it a practical tool for the broader manipulation community. However, the work is limited to single-step grasping on rigid objects with uniform physical properties; extending it to long-horizon tasks, deformable objects, or dynamic scenes remains open. The scaling analysis (15 toys suffice if demonstrations are plentiful) offers a concrete recipe for data-efficient policy training, but whether these findings hold for more complex manipulation beyond grasping is untested.
