## Problem

Integrating pre-trained score-based denoisers into ADMM for inverse problems faces two unresolved challenges: (i) a geometry mismatch between the Gaussian-noise manifolds on which score functions are trained and the actual iterates produced by ADMM (further distorted by dual variables), and (ii) a lack of convergence guarantees for ADMM-PnP with score-based denoisers, since existing analyses cover only primal algorithms or require strict contractivity assumptions not met by diffusion denoisers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260463/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes noisy measurements y ∈ R^n, a known measurement operator A: R^d → R^n, and a pre-trained score function s_θ(x, σ) from a diffusion model (specifically the one from Chung et al. 2023). It outputs a recovered d-dimensional signal or 256×256 RGB image. The ADMM loop runs up to K = W + 10 iterations with a linearly annealed noise schedule σ^(k) over [0.1, 10], and each denoising call requires J = 10 Langevin dynamics steps plus one Tweedie or 10-step ODE denoising evaluation, making the per-iteration cost substantially higher than a single score evaluation.

## Method

The paper proposes the AC-DC denoiser, a three-stage plug-in that replaces the z-subproblem in ADMM-PnP. Stage 1 (Auto-Correction) adds Gaussian noise of level σ^(k) to the raw ADMM iterate z̃^(k) = x^(k+1) + u^(k), pulling it toward the neighborhood of the noisy data manifold M_{σ^(k)} where the score was trained. Stage 2 (Directional Correction) runs J steps of conditional Langevin dynamics targeting p(z_{σ^(k)} | z_ac^(k)), using the learned score plus a Gaussian-approximated likelihood gradient to refine alignment without discarding measurement information. Stage 3 applies Tweedie's lemma (or a 10-step score ODE) to produce the final denoised estimate. On the theory side, the authors prove that under smoothness and coercivity assumptions on the data density, the AC-DC denoiser is weakly nonexpansive with high probability, yielding δ-ball convergence of ADMM-PnP under constant step size (Theorem 1–2), and that the denoiser is bounded, guaranteeing fixed-point convergence under an adaptive ρ-increasing schedule without requiring convexity of the data-fidelity term (Theorem 3).

## Results

On FFHQ, Ours-tweedie achieves the best PSNR across all seven inverse problem tasks, with headline numbers of 30.44 dB (4× super-resolution), 32.84 dB (random inpainting), 30.00 dB (motion deblurring), and 27.94 dB (phase retrieval). Ours-ode consistently achieves the best LPIPS (perceptual) scores, e.g., 0.095 on random inpainting and 0.154 on motion deblurring. On ImageNet, the same pattern holds with Ours-tweedie leading in PSNR (e.g., 29.56 dB on random inpainting, 27.15 dB on motion deblurring) and Ours-ode leading in LPIPS. The method outperforms the strongest PnP baseline (DAPS) by 0.5–1.2 dB PSNR on most tasks and by a much larger margin over DPS, DiffPIR, and RED-diff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against eight baselines: DPS, DAPS, DDRM, DiffPIR, RED-diff, DPIR, DCDP, and PMC. Ours-tweedie achieves the highest PSNR on nearly every task on both FFHQ and ImageNet, beating the closest competitor DAPS by 0.5–1.2 dB (e.g., 30.44 vs 29.53 on 4× super-resolution FFHQ; 32.84 vs 31.65 on random inpainting FFHQ). Ours-ode consistently achieves the best LPIPS perceptual scores, e.g., 0.095 vs DAPS's 0.124 on random inpainting and 0.154 vs 0.175 on motion deblurring (FFHQ). The gap over diffusion-sampling baselines (DPS, DDRM) is substantial: on phase retrieval FFHQ, Ours-tweedie reaches 27.94 dB PSNR while DPS manages only 11.63 dB. Among PnP baselines, DiffPIR and RED-diff are clearly outperformed (e.g., 30.44 vs 26.77 and 16.83 dB on super-resolution FFHQ), while DCDP and PMC show the weakest overall performance. DAPS is the only baseline that remains competitive, occasionally matching Ours-ode on LPIPS for ImageNet super-resolution (0.266 vs 0.276), but it is consistently behind on PSNR and SSIM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ADMM-PnP framework (Chan et al., 2016; Ryu et al., 2019) by replacing the classical proximal denoiser with a score-based denoiser, and addresses the specific challenge that dual variables in ADMM distort the noise geometry of iterates relative to the Gaussian-noise manifolds on which score functions are trained. The AC-DC denoiser (auto-correction via additive noise + directional correction via conditional Langevin dynamics + Tweedie/ODE denoising) builds on purification ideas from DiffPIR (Zhu et al., 2023) and stochastic regularization from SNORE (Renaud et al., 2024), but adds the Langevin-based directional correction step to explicitly align iterates with the score-trained manifolds. On the theory side, the paper generalizes the strictly contractive residual assumption of Ryu et al. (2019) to weakly nonexpansive residuals and removes the strong-convexity requirement on the data-fidelity term via adaptive step sizes following Chan et al. (2016).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first convergence analysis for ADMM-PnP equipped with score-based denoisers, filling a notable gap given that primal-dual methods are widely used in practice but had no theoretical grounding for diffusion-based PnP. The manifold mismatch diagnosis (dual variables make the iterate noise distribution fundamentally different from training-time Gaussian perturbations) provides a principled explanation for why naive score-based denoising in ADMM underperforms. Open questions remain: the convergence guarantee is to a δ-ball rather than an exact fixed point, noise schedules are heuristic, each iteration requires multiple score evaluations (limiting efficiency), and the paper does not establish recoverability or estimation-error bounds that would explain *why* the denoiser produces high-quality reconstructions beyond stability.
