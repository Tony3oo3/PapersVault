## Problem

Creating publication-quality scientific illustrations from long-form scientific texts remains a major bottleneck, requiring both deep domain understanding and professional design skills. Existing automated approaches either reconstruct figures from sparse captions, rearrange pre-existing multimodal content, or generate code-based schematics that sacrifice visual aesthetics for structural correctness. No prior benchmark or framework addresses the full task of distilling an entire methodology from a long document (>10k tokens) and autonomously producing an illustration that is both structurally faithful and aesthetically polished.

## Input / Output

The system accepts unstructured long-form scientific text in various formats (PDF, TXT, MD, TeX) with average lengths ranging from 352 tokens (textbooks) to 12,732 tokens (papers), and produces a single publication-ready RGB illustration with vector-quality text overlays. Internally, the pipeline generates an intermediate symbolic layout (SVG/HTML) encoding a directed graph of entities and relations, which is rasterized into a conditioning image before final rendering. The output illustration typically contains 5–6 visual components, 6–7 colors, and ~41% text density, matching the complexity of real scientific figures.

## Method

AUTOFIGURE employs a 'Reasoned Rendering' paradigm that decouples structural planning from aesthetic rendering into two stages. Stage I (Conceptual Grounding) uses an LLM to extract a distilled methodology summary and entity-relation graph from the input text, serializing it into a symbolic SVG/HTML layout; a critique-and-refine loop then simulates a dialogue between an AI designer and AI critic, iteratively scoring and regenerating the layout until structural coherence and aesthetic balance converge. Stage II (Aesthetic Synthesis) converts the validated layout into an exhaustive T2I prompt paired with a structural graph, feeds them into a multimodal generative model for high-fidelity rendering, and applies an 'erase-and-correct' post-processing pipeline—using a non-LLM eraser to remove text pixels, OCR to extract bounding boxes, and a multimodal verifier to cross-check strings against ground-truth labels—before overlaying crisp vector text onto the clean background. This architecture is backbone-agnostic, supporting both commercial APIs (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro) and open-source models (Qwen3-VL-235B), with the refinement loop providing test-time scaling that improves overall scores from 6.28 to 7.14 across five iterations.

## Results

On FigureBench, AUTOFIGURE achieves the highest Overall VLM-as-a-judge score across all four document categories: Paper (7.03), Blog (7.60), Survey (6.99), and Textbook (8.00), with corresponding blind pairwise Win-Rates of 53.0%, 75.0%, 78.1%, and 97.5% respectively. In the most challenging Paper category, AUTOFIGURE scores 7.28 in Aesthetic quality and 7.34 in Clarity, substantially outperforming the next-best baseline (HTML-Code at 5.90 and 7.17). In human evaluation by 10 first-author domain experts, AUTOFIGURE achieves an 83.3% win rate against all other AI systems and 66.7% of experts indicated willingness to use its output in a camera-ready publication.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AUTOFIGURE decisively outperforms all four baseline families on the Paper category. Against GPT-Image (Hurst et al., 2024), the end-to-end text-to-image model, AUTOFIGURE leads by 2.52 points in Overall score (7.03 vs. 4.51) and 46 percentage points in Win-Rate (53% vs. 7%); GPT-Image's critical weakness is content accuracy (4.77 vs. 6.96), as it hallucinates or omits structural elements from long scientific texts. Against Diagram Agent (Wei et al., 2025), the multi-agent diagram framework, AUTOFIGURE wins by a margin of 4.91 Overall points (7.03 vs. 2.12) with a 53% vs. 0% Win-Rate, as Diagram Agent consistently produces structurally collapsed, low-information outputs. Against TikZero/TikZero+ (Belouadi et al., 2025), the LaTeX code-generation paradigm, AUTOFIGURE's advantage is even more extreme (7.03 vs. 1.45/1.25 Overall, 53% vs. 0% Win-Rate), because forcing an LLM to serialize complex topological structures into compilable TikZ code overwhelms its reasoning capacity. Against AutoPresent (Ge et al., 2025), a presentation-layout agent, AUTOFIGURE leads by 4.48 Overall points (7.03 vs. 2.55), as AutoPresent is designed to rearrange existing assets rather than generate novel schematics from scratch. Notably, the code-based baselines (HTML/SVG) achieve moderate structural accuracy but sacrifice visual aesthetics (Aesthetic scores of 5.90/5.00 vs. AUTOFIGURE's 7.28), limiting their Win-Rates to 11–31%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by defining 'Long-context Scientific Illustration Design' as a distinct task, explicitly departing from prior figure-generation work that relies on captions, short snippets, or existing metadata (Paper2Fig100k, ACL-Fig, SciCap+). Conceptually, it repurposes the multi-agent critique-and-refine paradigm from LLM reasoning into a visual layout optimization loop, and combines it with a decoupled rendering stage (diffusion-based aesthetic synthesis plus OCR-based text correction) that has no direct precedent in the scientific illustration literature. It also positions itself against code-generation approaches (TikZero, Automatikz) and end-to-end T2I models, arguing that both fail to balance structural fidelity with publication-grade aesthetics on long-form inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of FigureBench (3,300 pairs, avg. >10k tokens) provides the first systematic evaluation substrate for this task, and the 66.7% publication-ready rate from domain-expert authors signals that automated illustration is approaching practical utility for AI Scientists that currently cannot visually communicate their findings. Key open questions remain: fine-grained glyph-level text accuracy under dense layouts, domain-specific visual conventions beyond CS (biology, chemistry), and the extension to dynamic/interactive figures. The framework's dependence on proprietary APIs (GPT-Image, Gemini-2.5-Pro) for best results, though partially mitigated by the Qwen3-VL open-source deployment, limits immediate reproducibility for resource-constrained labs.
