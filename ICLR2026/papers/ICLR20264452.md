## Problem

Deep generative models for molecular dynamics (MD) trajectory generation face two fundamental barriers: (1) MD simulation data is prohibitively expensive to produce at scale, limiting training sets to a few hundred molecules, and (2) trajectories add a temporal dimension to already high-dimensional 3D structure spaces, making joint density estimation extremely difficult. Existing trajectory diffusion models like GeoTDM require molecule-specific training and fail to generalize across arbitrary molecular systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264452/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a molecular graph (heavy-atom types and bond connectivity) plus task-specific conditioning: no conditioning for unconditional generation, the initial frame x^(0) for forward simulation, or both x^(0) and x^(T-1) for interpolation. It outputs a full MD trajectory as T frames of 3D heavy-atom coordinates (T×N×3 tensor), generated via 1000-step DDPM denoising at trajectory time-steps of 5.2 ps (small molecules), 10 ps (tetrapeptides), or 100 ps (proteins). Long trajectories are produced by iterative block-diffusion roll-outs, where each block's final frame conditions the next.

## Method

EGINTERPOLATOR is a two-stage framework that first pretrains an SE(3)-equivariant conformer diffusion model (stacked EGCL layers) on large-scale conformer datasets (GEOM-QM9, GEOM-Drugs), then freezes those spatial layers and appends equivariant temporal attention blocks to capture inter-frame dependencies on a much smaller MD trajectory dataset. The core architectural innovation is the equivariant temporal interpolator, which computes the final denoiser output as a learnable linear interpolation ε_md = α·ε_cf + (1−α)·ε_tp, where ε_cf is the frozen structure model output and ε_tp is a temporal network that receives both the noised trajectory and the structure model output as input. Theorem 4.1 proves this interpolation implicitly defines an intermediate target distribution for the temporal network—between the frame-independent conformer product and the full MD joint—reducing optimization difficulty. Two variants are explored: SIMPLE (single global interpolation) and CASC (per-block cascaded interpolation with dense information flow between spatial and temporal layers). For protein monomers, the spatial backbone is replaced with Boltz1's pair-biased sliding-window attention augmented with RoPE-based temporal attention layers.

## Results

On QM9 unconditional generation, EGINTERPOLATOR-CASC achieves JSD of 0.305 (Bond Angle), 0.210 (Bond Length), 0.363 (Torsion), and 0.417 (TICA_0), substantially outperforming GeoTDM (0.691, 0.676, 0.489, 0.449 respectively). On Drugs forward simulation, EGINTERPOLATOR-CASC reaches JSD of 0.173 (Bond Angle), 0.142 (Bond Length), 0.377 (Torsion), and 0.454 (TICA_0), approaching the MD oracle (0.036, 0.030, 0.215, 0.484). On tetrapeptides, EGINTERPOLATOR reduces torsion JSD from 0.520 (GeoTDM) to 0.305 (backbone) and 0.434→0.223 (side-chain), with TICA_0,1 JSD dropping from 0.634 to 0.462.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EGINTERPOLATOR-CASC decisively outperforms GeoTDM (Han et al., 2024), the strongest diffusion baseline, on every metric across both QM9 and Drugs: e.g., Bond Length JSD drops from 0.676→0.210 (QM9) and 0.643→0.142 (Drugs), while Torsion JSD improves from 0.489→0.363 and 0.498→0.377 respectively. Against autoregressive baselines (AR+EGNN, AR+ET, AR+GeoTDM), the gap is even larger, with GeoTDM-based autoregressive variants still trailing EGINTERPOLATOR by 0.1–0.4 JSD on most metrics. In the conformer pretraining stage, the pretrained BASICES model matches or exceeds GeoDiff-A (Xu et al., 2022) and CONFGF (Shi et al., 2021) on precision metrics (COV-P, MAT-P), which the authors prioritize for downstream MD fidelity. On the tetrapeptide benchmark, EGINTERPOLATOR wins on bond angle (0.102 vs 0.607) and bond length (0.074 vs 0.643) JSD over GeoTDM, but MDGen (Jing et al., 2024) retains an advantage on torsion-centric metrics (Torsion All: 0.109 vs 0.265; TICA_0: 0.230 vs 0.409), reflecting MDGen's explicit torsional parameterization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the geometric diffusion model paradigm for molecular dynamics (GeoTDM, Han et al. 2024) by introducing a two-stage structure-pretraining framework inspired by the image-to-video generation analogy (Blattmann et al., 2023). The spatial denoiser follows the EGNN/GeoDiff conformer generation lineage, while the temporal module reuses the equivariant temporal attention from GeoTDM; the key departure is the equivariant temporal interpolator that linearly mixes structure and temporal outputs, providing a theoretical decomposition of the MD distribution into an independent-frame prior and a temporal correction. Unlike prior MD diffusion models that train from scratch on limited trajectory data, this work explicitly leverages large-scale conformer datasets (GEOM-QM9, GEOM-Drugs) to pretrain the spatial component before fine-tuning on trajectories, and extends the framework to tetrapeptides (Timewarp) and protein monomers (ATLAS, building on Boltz1).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structure-pretraining approach directly tackles the central bottleneck in MD generative modeling—data scarcity—by transferring knowledge from abundant conformer data, and the resulting energy fidelity (W1 distances ~16× lower than GeoTDM on Drugs) suggests a practical path toward physically realistic trajectory generation for unseen molecules. The temporal interpolator's theoretical grounding (Theorem 4.1) and the α-mixing mechanism offer interpretable control over the structure-dynamics trade-off, which could be useful for downstream applications like transition path sampling. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer trajectories without error accumulation (degradation observed beyond ~8 blocks), whether it can match torsion-centric representations like MDGen for peptide dynamics, and whether the protein extension (currently preliminary, 200 proteins) can achieve the same fidelity gains seen on small molecules.
