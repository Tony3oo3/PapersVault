## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D Gaussian head avatar methods either lack controllable facial animation or achieve it at impractically slow speeds (e.g., Avat3r at 8 fps) due to cross-attention-based deformation. Additionally, prior work that injects geometry priors via skip-connections propagates reconstruction errors into the avatar, degrading fidelity. The paper addresses how to simultaneously achieve high-fidelity few-shot reconstruction and real-time expression-driven animation in a single feed-forward pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264691/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 4 (or any number of) 512×512 RGB images from well-distributed views with arbitrary expressions, along with their camera parameters encoded as 6-channel Plücker ray maps. At animation time, a 120-dimensional FLAME expression code drives the deformation. The output is a canonical per-pixel 3D Gaussian head (44 attributes per pixel: position, color, rotation, scale, opacity, and a 32-dim semantic feature), which the deformation MLP offsets to produce rendered novel-view images, alpha masks, and depth maps at 512×512. Reconstruction completes in under 1 second; animation runs at 62 FPS with 4 input views on a single H800 GPU.

## Method

FastGHA uses a two-stage feed-forward pipeline. First, DINOv3 (semantic) and SD-Turbo VAE (color) features are concatenated with Plücker ray maps and processed by an 8-layer multi-view self-attention transformer (512 hidden dim, following GRM), whose output tokens are decoded by a modified SD-Turbo VAE decoder expanded to regress 44-dim per-pixel Gaussian attributes, yielding a canonical (expression-free) Gaussian head. Second, a lightweight 6-layer MLP (256 hidden dim, LeakyReLU) takes the 32-dim per-Gaussian features and a FLAME expression code to predict position and color offsets, enabling real-time animation without cross-attention. A key design choice is using VGGT point maps as a depth-based geometry regularization loss during training (after aligning VGGT points to the ground-truth mesh) rather than as a skip-connection input, which avoids error propagation from the geometry prior. The network is trained end-to-end on Ava-256 and NeRSemble with photometric, perceptual, silhouette, and geometric losses.

## Results

On Ava-256, FastGHA (trained on both datasets) achieves PSNR 22.5, SSIM 0.77, LPIPS 0.23, CSIM 0.73, and AKD 4.8, outperforming the closest baseline Avat3r (PSNR 20.7, CSIM 0.59) by 1.8 dB in PSNR and 0.14 in identity similarity. On NeRSemble, FastGHA reaches PSNR 24.0, SSIM 0.81, LPIPS 0.24, CSIM 0.77, and AKD 4.4, surpassing GPAvatar (PSNR 20.8) by 3.2 dB. Reconstruction takes under 1 second for 4 input views, and animation runs at 62 FPS on a single H800 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FastGHA outperforms all five baselines on the primary few-shot (4-view) reconstruction task. Against Avat3r (the closest competitor), FastGHA gains 1.8 dB PSNR and 0.14 CSIM on Ava-256 while matching its AKD of 4.8; Avat3r's results on NeRSemble were not available. Against GPAvatar, FastGHA leads by 3.4 dB PSNR and 0.19 CSIM on Ava-256, and by 3.2 dB PSNR and 0.23 CSIM on NeRSemble. InvertAvatar is substantially outperformed across all metrics (e.g., 8.3 dB PSNR gap on Ava-256). In the one-shot setting (Table 6), FastGHA without fine-tuning underperforms GAGAvatar (PSNR 19.3 vs 20.6) and LAM (PSNR 19.3 vs 20.0), but after 30k steps of fine-tuning it surpasses both (PSNR 23.0, CSIM 0.73), demonstrating that the architecture generalizes well even with minimal per-identity adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FastGHA extends the feed-forward few-shot Gaussian head avatar paradigm established by Avat3r (Kirschstein et al., 2025), which itself builds on the large reconstruction model (LRM) architecture from GRM (Xu et al., 2024a) and the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation (Kerbl et al., 2023). The key departures are architectural: replacing Avat3r's cross-attention animation module with a lightweight per-Gaussian MLP (enabling ~8× speedup), using VGGT point maps as a training-time geometry regularization loss rather than a skip-connection input (avoiding error propagation), and fusing DINOv3 semantic features with SD-Turbo VAE color features for richer multi-view encoding. The paper also generalizes from Avat3r's fixed four-view input to an arbitrary number of views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of sub-second feed-forward reconstruction and 62 FPS real-time animation makes Gaussian head avatars practically deployable in interactive applications (virtual production, real-time rendering pipelines) where per-identity optimization was previously prohibitive. The geometry-prior-as-regularization design choice is a useful pattern that other feed-forward 3D reconstruction methods could adopt to avoid injecting noisy priors directly into the network. Open questions include robustness to inaccurate camera estimates (the paper shows degradation under camera noise), the inability to model tongue motion with FLAME, and whether the canonical-space disentanglement generalizes to more extreme or out-of-distribution expressions.
