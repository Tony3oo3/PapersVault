## Problem

Homomorphic encryption enables non-interactive secure transformer inference, but the high computational cost of HE rotations and format conversions between packing schemes makes end-to-end evaluation impractical. Prior non-interactive solutions (NEXUS, THOR) either lack a complete end-to-end pipeline or incur expensive format conversions and thousands of HE rotations in Softmax and LayerNorm, while alternatives like Powerformer require re-training through knowledge distillation, limiting generalizability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260953/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes up to 128 encrypted text tokens (encoded via CKKS with N=2^16 ring degree and 2^15 slots) and unencrypted pre-trained transformer weights as input, producing encrypted classification scores that only the client can decrypt. For BERT-base-uncased, evaluation is amortized over 256 inputs, yielding 2.36 minutes per input on a single NVIDIA H200 GPU. The framework is plug-and-play: it does not modify any transformer components (Softmax, LayerNorm, GELU) and requires no re-training or fine-tuning, and extends to decoder-only models such as LLaMA-3-8B.

## Method

MOAI combines three key techniques to minimize HE rotations and eliminate format conversions. First, a consistent packing flow uses column packing for plaintext-ciphertext matrix multiplications (Q, K, V, feedforward) and diagonal packing for the QK^T product and Softmax, so that each module's output format matches the next module's input format without any conversion or transpose. Second, rotation-free Softmax and LayerNorm algorithms exploit the identity that the sum of columns equals the sum of diagonals of a square matrix (Lemma 4.1), placing summation inputs in the same slot position across different ciphertexts to avoid all HE rotations, saving 2448 rotations in BERT-base. Third, interleaved batching packs multiple sequence vectors into a single ciphertext in an order that makes a single HE rotation equivalent to an internal per-vector rotation (Lemma 3.2), halving the rotations needed in ciphertext-ciphertext matrix multiplications compared to naive batching. Together these reduce total HE rotations to 9,648 (at least 1.7× fewer than prior work) and achieve 52.8% and 55.7% speedups over THOR and Powerformer respectively.

## Results

MOAI achieves an amortized inference time of 2.36 minutes per input on a single NVIDIA H200 GPU for BERT-base (128 tokens), and 9.6 minutes on CPU. In terms of HE rotations, MOAI requires only 9,648 total rotations across all matrix multiplications in BERT-base, compared to 22,224 (THOR), 16,740 (Powerformer), and >221,184 (NEXUS). On GLUE benchmarks, encrypted inference introduces negligible accuracy loss: SST-2 drops from 93.9% to 93.8%, QNLI from 90.7% to 90.5%, and RTE from 65.7% to 65.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against THOR (CCS'25), the primary state-of-the-art non-interactive HE-based transformer inference, MOAI achieves a 52.8% reduction in total evaluation time (283.95s vs. 602.26s on A100), with the largest gains in Softmax (2.19s vs. 15.53s, a 7.1× speedup) and FC2 (2.88s vs. 49.19s, a 17× speedup). Against Powerformer (ACL'25), which replaces Softmax and LayerNorm with FHE-friendly approximations and requires retraining, MOAI still achieves a 55.7% reduction in total time (152.4s vs. 344.52s), demonstrating that its packing and batching techniques are complementary to architectural modifications. Against NEXUS (NDSS'25), MOAI requires 22.9× fewer HE rotations (9,648 vs. >221,184), though NEXUS does not provide an end-to-end inference solution. In microbenchmarks, MOAI's rotation-free Softmax is 22× faster than THOR and 4× faster than NEXUS, while its rotation-free LayerNorm is 151× faster than THOR and 87× faster than NEXUS. Accuracy is preserved across all three GLUE tasks with at most 0.2% drop, confirming that the FHE evaluation introduces negligible error.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOAI builds directly on the CKKS-based encrypted matrix multiplication primitives of Halevi & Shoup (2014, 2021), extending their column and diagonal packing with interleaved batching to reduce rotations in ciphertext-ciphertext products. It departs from the prior non-interactive FHE transformer inference line (NEXUS, THOR) by enforcing a single consistent packing format across all layers, eliminating the expensive format conversions and transposes that those works require. Unlike Powerformer, which replaces Softmax and LayerNorm with FHE-friendly approximations and demands knowledge-distillation retraining, MOAI preserves the original transformer components and instead introduces rotation-free evaluation algorithms for both, making it a true plug-and-play framework. The Goldschmidt division and Cheon et al. (2022) domain-extension polynomials are repurposed to handle the inverse-square-root and tanh evaluations within the new packing structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing 2448 HE rotations from Softmax/LayerNorm and at least 1.7× rotations from all matrix multiplications, MOAI cuts BERT-base inference to 2.36 minutes per input on a single GPU—a 4× improvement over the prior SOTA (THOR, 10 min)—without any model retraining. The plug-and-play design and demonstrated extension to LLaMA-3-8B (decoder-only, RoPE, SwiGLU, RMSNorm) suggest the packing and rotation-free techniques are broadly applicable across transformer architectures. However, bootstrapping still accounts for roughly 80% of total runtime (227.8 s of 283.9 s in Table 4), and the LLaMA-3-8B results are microbenchmark aggregations rather than full end-to-end runs, leaving open whether the speedup ratios hold at scale. A key open question is whether further bootstrapping placement or multi-ciphertext bootstrapping can close the remaining gap to practical real-time inference.
