## Problem

LLMs compound errors during autoregressive generation, where early mistakes propagate and drive outputs off-track. Existing self-correction methods either require supervised training (limiting generalizability) or operate post-hoc on complete drafts, providing feedback too late and too coarsely to prevent recurring error patterns. No prior method intervenes continuously during generation using both uncertainty signals and external feedback to steer the model at the point of failure.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a task prompt (goal), token-level probability scores exposed by the Producer LLM during decoding, and natural-language or structured feedback from one or more Verifier agents. It operates at the granularity of adaptive generation units (sentences, derivation steps, code blocks) rather than fixed token windows or complete outputs, with unit boundaries identified via syntactic markers. The output is a sequential composition of verified units forming a corrected final answer, with the system requiring only API-level access to token probabilities—no model weight modification.

## Method

Once-More transforms LLM generation into a Producer-Verifier loop where the Producer emits units and a Verifier checks each unit against the task goal. A top-K token-level perplexity metric monitors uncertainty in real time; units exceeding a calibrated threshold trigger explicit verification. Upon rejection, the framework performs guided regeneration by (1) appending verifier feedback to the prompt and (2) directly manipulating the token probability distribution: previously chosen tokens are suppressed with strength proportional to their perplexity, while probability mass is redistributed to alternatives via a mass-conserving scaling factor κ. Suppression signals are transferred between attempts through monotone alignment with distance decay and Gaussian smoothing, preventing the model from re-sampling the same erroneous tokens. If regeneration fails, the system rolls back to the last accepted checkpoint, ensuring errors are corrected at their origin rather than after full propagation.

## Results

On Qwen3-14B, Once-More achieves 36.7% on AIME 2024, 26.7% on AIME 2025, 52.5% on LiveBench (reasoning), and 55.6% on GPQA Diamond, yielding gains of 10.0, 7.9, 8.5, and 7.6 points over raw generation respectively. The method consistently outperforms Self-Refine and CRITIC across all three model sizes (4B, 8B, 14B) and all four benchmarks. On arithmetic tasks, Once-More reaches 89.0% on SVAMP (Qwen2 Math 7B) and 85.2% on GSM8K, matching or exceeding the supervised S3C-MATH approach without any training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Once-More decisively outperforms Self-Refine (Madaan et al., 2023) on every benchmark and model size; the gap is largest on LiveBench where Self-Refine gains <1 point over raw while Once-More gains 8.5–12.7 points (e.g., 52.5% vs. 46.5% on Qwen3-14B). Against CRITIC (Gou et al., 2024), Once-More leads by 7.9 points on AIME24 (36.7% vs. 28.8%) and 7.0 points on LiveBench (52.5% vs. 45.5%) for the 14B model, though CRITIC slightly edges Once-More on GPQA Diamond for the 4B model (48.9% vs. 47.5%). Compared to the supervised S3C-MATH (Yan et al., 2025), Once-More wins on SVAMP (89.0% vs. 87.4% on Qwen2 Math 7B; 82.0% vs. 81.8% on Llama3 8B) and GSM8K for Qwen2 Math (85.2% vs. 84.8%), but loses on GSM8K for Llama3 8B (79.0% vs. 82.9%). Notably, Self-Refine actually degrades performance on AIME25 for the 8B model (16.7%→15.5%), whereas Once-More improves it to 24.4%, highlighting the fragility of prompt-only iterative refinement on smaller models.

## Relation to Prior Work

Once-More sits at the intersection of three research threads: post-hoc iterative refinement (Self-Refine, CRITIC), supervised self-correction training (S3C-MATH, LLaVA-SCo), and decoding-time steering (Contrastive Decoding, DoLa, Cautious Next Token Prediction). It departs from the first two by being entirely training-free and intervening *during* generation at unit granularity rather than on completed drafts, and from the third by coupling direct logit redistribution with semantic verifier feedback in a closed Producer-Verifier loop. The perplexity-as-uncertainty-signal idea draws on recent entropy-based quality metrics, while the multi-agent architecture echoes Reflexion and MAgICoRe, but the key novelty is the fusion of token-level perplexity with probability-level suppression to break recurring error patterns that prompt-only feedback cannot resolve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, model-agnostic path to improving LLM reliability at inference time without retraining, which is valuable for deployment in safety-critical or compute-constrained settings. However, several limitations remain open: errors rooted deep in generation history can trigger regeneration loops, confident-but-wrong tokens bypass the perplexity trigger entirely, and the performance ceiling is bounded by verifier quality. Future work on dynamic rollback, adaptive thresholding, and integration with stronger external verifiers (e.g., code execution, formal provers) could extend the approach to longer and more complex generation tasks.
