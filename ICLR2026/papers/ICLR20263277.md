## Problem

Existing monocular dynamic reconstruction methods lack a unified spatiotemporal decomposition framework: they either perform holistic optimization over the entire video interval, ignoring varying deformation patterns across time, or introduce coupled hierarchical spatial composition that causes optimization conflicts. No prior work simultaneously addresses hierarchical temporal partitioning and spatially complementary dynamic representation in a decoupled manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263277/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video sequence (extended to up to 160 frames in their NVIDIA-LS dataset) with corresponding camera poses, plus 2D priors (monocular depth from Metric3D-V2, point trajectories from CoTracker3, and epipolar error maps from RAFT) to initialize the 3D representation. It produces a dynamic 3D Gaussian scene in canonical space parameterized by motion bases with KNN-based deformation fields, enabling real-time novel view rendering at arbitrary timestamps for both static background and moving foreground. The tree-depth is set to 2 (3 layers, 7 nodes total), and child nodes inherit a limited number of Gaussian primitives (5000/2000) from their parent to balance efficiency.

## Method

WorldTree introduces two complementary components: (1) Temporal Partition Tree (TPT) builds an inheritance-based binary tree via BFS traversal, where each node represents a temporal sub-interval with its own motion bases and Gaussian primitives, enabling coarse-to-fine optimization with parallel training across nodes at the same depth; (2) Spatial Ancestral Chains (SAC) recursively retrieves each node's ancestor chain to provide multi-level spatial context, where the node models local temporal dynamics while ancestors supply complementary spatial representations with specialized (decoupled) motion parameters at each level. Deformation is computed via KNN graphs over motion bases using Dual Quaternion Blending, and the final deformed Gaussians at any timestamp are the union of the node's own primitives and those from its ancestral chain. Training uses a composite loss (photometric, depth, tracking, ARAP, acceleration, velocity) with bundle adjustment and static-region warm-up as pre-optimization stages, achieving O(δ) optimization complexity through parallel layer-wise training.

## Results

On the NVIDIA-LS dataset, WorldTree achieves PSNR 24.06, SSIM 0.804, LPIPS 0.100, and masked PSNR (mPSNR) 18.55, outperforming the second-best method MoSca (PSNR 23.72, LPIPS 0.109, mPSNR 17.89) by 8.26% in LPIPS and 3.69% in mPSNR. On DyCheck, WorldTree achieves mPSNR 19.75, mSSIM 0.728, and mLPIPS 0.240, improving mLPIPS by 9.09% over MoSca (0.264) and by 18.92% over SoM (0.296). The method also demonstrates lower average VRAM usage (8.61–12.68 GB) compared to MoSca (12.90 GB) while achieving a 49.26% training speedup through parallel optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WorldTree outperforms all 19 baselines across both evaluation datasets without requiring any external priors (no Colmap points, no manual prompts). On NVIDIA-LS, it beats the closest competitor MoSca (Lei et al., 2025) by 8.26% in LPIPS (0.100 vs 0.109) and 3.69% in mPSNR (18.55 vs 17.89), while also surpassing 4DGS with Colmap initialization (PSNR 24.06 vs 23.34) and SplineGS with manual prompts (PSNR 24.06 vs 22.20). The gap over hierarchical methods is particularly large: HiMoR (Liang et al., 2025) trails by 4.76 PSNR and 3.27 mPSNR, confirming the paper's claim that hierarchical motion coupling degrades reconstruction. On DyCheck, WorldTree leads MoSca by 9.09% in mLPIPS (0.240 vs 0.264) and 2.22% in mPSNR (19.75 vs 19.32), and extends the lead over SoM (Wang et al., 2024) to 18.92% in mLPIPS. Notably, WorldTree achieves these gains without any Colmap initial points or manual point prompts, whereas 4DGS and SplineGS require such priors to reach competitive levels. The method also demonstrates robustness to different depth and tracking backbones (Table 4), consistently outperforming MoSca regardless of the prior combination used.

## Relation to Prior Work

WorldTree builds directly on MoSca (Lei et al., 2025), adopting its motion-basis deformation field formulation and 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering pipeline as the foundational representation, but departs from MoSca's single global optimization over the entire video interval. The key conceptual departure is the unified tree-chain structure: the Temporal Partition Tree (TPT) replaces holistic temporal optimization with a coarse-to-fine hierarchical partition, while the Spatial Ancestral Chains (SAC) replace MoSca's amorphous global spatial fusion with a recursive ancestral hierarchy that provides complementary spatial context at multiple scales. This positions WorldTree as a response to two specific failure modes identified in prior work: the temporal uniformity assumption in MoSca/SoM and the hierarchical motion coupling in HiMoR (Liang et al., 2025), where local-wise interference across levels causes optimization conflicts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Monocular dynamic reconstruction is a practically important setting because synchronized multi-view video is rare in the wild, and the NVIDIA-LS dataset with extended 160-frame sequences and SAM2-annotated dynamic masks addresses a real evaluation gap (the original NVIDIA dataset's 12 frames and implicit test-view inclusion). The remaining limitation is the pipeline's dependence on pretrained foundation models (Metric3D-V2, CoTracker3, RAFT) for 2D prior extraction, and the acknowledged difficulty with small, fast-moving dynamic regions where monocular supervision is inherently sparse. An open question is whether the hierarchical tree structure can be made adaptive (e.g., non-uniform depth allocation based on motion complexity) rather than relying on fixed binary splitting, though the paper's ablation showing similar results across splitting strategies suggests the hierarchical mechanism itself is the key factor.
