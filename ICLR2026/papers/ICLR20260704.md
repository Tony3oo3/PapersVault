## Problem

In offline imitation learning, data augmentation must balance dynamic consistency with scalability: single-step Koopman-based methods (e.g., KFC) are limited to per-transition augmentation, incur linear scaling with action dimension due to action-equivariance, and are vulnerable to approximation errors in low-dynamics regions. Trajectory-level generative approaches (GANs, diffusion) are data-hungry and lack the inductive bias needed for limited offline datasets. The paper addresses the gap of generating full, dynamically consistent trajectories within a Koopman framework without recursive rollout error accumulation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes expert demonstration trajectories as sequences of state vectors (e.g., 17-dim AntMaze states, 17-dim HalfCheetah states) from D4RL datasets, with no action conditioning during dynamics modeling. It outputs augmented state-action trajectories where states are generated by pointwise application of a learned symmetry in a 32-dimensional Koopman latent space, and actions are inferred post-hoc by a separate inverse dynamics model. The final output is a behavioral cloning policy trained on the union of original and augmented data, with augmentation controlled by a blend ratio (shift_sigma ∈ [0.1, 0.5]).

## Method

KATS models the closed-loop system s_{t+1} ≈ G(s_t) (policy implicitly embedded) rather than the open-loop s_{t+1} = F(s_t, a_t), learning a single action-independent Koopman operator K in a 32-dim latent space via an autoencoder. It then learns symmetry transformations σ that approximately commute with K (Kσ ≈ σK) through a weighted objective where weights are exponentially scaled by local prediction error, prioritizing augmentation in data-sparse regions. New trajectories are generated by applying σ pointwise to entire expert trajectories in latent space (avoiding recursive rollout), then decoding back to state space. A decoupled inverse dynamics model f_IDM(s_t, s_{t+1}) → a_t infers actions for the generated state pairs, producing complete augmented tuples. The theoretical foundation (Theorems 1–3) establishes that policy equivariance is equivalent to Koopman commutation, guarantees transformed trajectories remain dynamically valid, and provides a bounded error decomposition into propagated model error and symmetry violation error.

## Results

On the D4RL offline imitation learning benchmark (Table 1), KATS+BC achieves the best score on all 15 tasks across AntMaze, Gym, and Adroit domains. Headline improvements include 96.9 on antmaze-umaze (vs. 74.0 for BC and 79.1 for KFC+BC), 112.7 on hopper-medium-expert (vs. 52.5 for BC), and 69.2 on pen-human (vs. 37.5 for BC). In the few-shot offline RL setting (Table 2), KATS+BC achieves state-of-the-art results on 10 of 12 tasks, e.g., 109.4 on Walker2d-e (1% data) and 102.7 on Halfcheetah-e (1% data), outperforming dedicated offline RL algorithms like TELS, POR, and Diffuser.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KATS+BC wins on all 15 tasks against every baseline. Against vanilla BC, gains range from +17.3 (antmaze-large-diverse: 44.2 vs. 24.9) to +31.3 (door-human: 37.2 vs. 9.9). Against SRA (the strongest non-Koopman baseline), KATS+BC leads by 11.6 on antmaze-umaze (96.9 vs. 85.3) and by 21.9 on door-human (37.2 vs. 15.3). Against MILO, the gap is largest on Adroit tasks (e.g., pen-cloned: 81.3 vs. 57.1). The most direct comparison is with KFC+BC (the action-equivariant predecessor): KATS+BC outperforms it on every task, with the largest margin on antmaze-umaze (96.9 vs. 79.1, +17.8) and door-human (37.2 vs. 14.1, +23.1), validating the action-independent closed-loop formulation. In the offline RL setting (Table 2), KATS+BC beats TELS, POR, DOGE, CQL, IQL, and Diffuser on 10 of 12 few-shot tasks, though TELS edges it out on Hopper-me (100.9 vs. 87.2) and Walker2d-me (87.4 vs. 92.5 is actually KATS winning; TELS wins only on Hopper-me).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Koopman-based data augmentation line initiated by KFC (Weissenbacher et al., ICML 2022), which applied action-equivariant Koopman operators to generate single-step augmented transitions. KATS makes two fundamental departures: it shifts from action-conditioned to policy-equivariant (state-only) Koopman modeling, embedding the expert policy implicitly into a single linear operator, and it elevates augmentation from single-step transitions to full trajectory synthesis, thereby avoiding compounding rollout errors. The theoretical contribution formalizes the equivalence between policy equivariance and Koopman commutation, providing a rigorous mechanism that prior Koopman-augmentation work lacked. On the IL side, KATS positions itself alongside SRA and MILO as a data-centric countermeasure to covariate shift, but distinguishes itself by generating dynamically consistent trajectories rather than noise-injected or reverse-augmented samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical takeaway is that a simple BC policy trained on KATS-augmented data can outperform complex offline RL algorithms (CQL, IQL, DOGE, TELS, Diffuser) on 10 of 12 few-shot D4RL tasks, suggesting that behavioral fidelity of augmented data may matter more than algorithmic sophistication for distribution shift. The plug-and-play compatibility demonstrated with both BC and SRA makes this a broadly applicable preprocessing module. Key limitations include the reliance on a relatively simple linear Koopman operator (the authors acknowledge more sophisticated architectures are needed for highly nonlinear dynamics), the heuristic reward assignment in the RL setting, and the implicit assumption that the expert policy admits exploitable symmetries. Open questions include whether the adaptive symmetry weighting generalizes to high-dimensional continuous control beyond the tested benchmarks, and how the method behaves when the expert policy is deliberately non-symmetric.
