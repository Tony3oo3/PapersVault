## Problem

Event binning functions are inherently discontinuous, which truncates gradients at the frame level and forces event-based algorithms to rely solely on frame-based features. When attempting to learn directly from raw events, the discontinuities produce non-computable Dirac delta gradients, and existing heuristic surrogates (straight-through estimators, sigmoid surrogates) lack unbiasedness guarantees, leading to suboptimal learning efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263991/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes asynchronous spatio-temporal event clouds (timestamp, pixel coordinates, polarity) along with parametric motion parameters (angular or linear velocity), warps events to a reference time, and bins them into 200×150 frames at Δ=0.01 resolution. The forward pass is entirely unchanged; only the backward gradient accumulation is modified, replacing the discontinuous kernel derivative with a synthesized weak derivative computed as the convolution of a reconstruction kernel with the binning kernel. The output is an unbiased gradient vector for the warped event coordinates, which drives optimization of motion parameters or neural network weights.

## Method

Functional Backpropagation (FBP) lifts the discrete binning function into a continuous functional space, where the chain rule takes the form of an integral transform with a derivative kernel. During backpropagation, the continuous cotangent function is naturally sampled by the discrete cotangent vector via a Dirac comb; FBP reconstructs the full cotangent function from these samples using a smooth kernel (e.g., linear, cubic, or Lanczos). Applying integration by parts then replaces the undefined Dirac delta evaluation with a convolution of the reconstructed cotangent and the binning kernel derivative, yielding an exact closed-form synthesized weak derivative κ′(·) = (l * k)′(·). This derivative provably matches long-range finite differences of arbitrary target functions to finite order, restoring unbiasedness without altering forward outputs. The implementation modifies only the backward gradient accumulation loop, adding roughly 2× computational overhead per JVP call while delivering 1.57× faster convergence and consistent accuracy gains across motion estimation, optical flow, and SLAM.

## Results

On angular velocity estimation (ECD), FBP achieves a 10.3% reduction in RMS error and 1.66× faster convergence compared to standard backpropagation, while linear velocity estimation shows 1.48× faster convergence (with a marginal 3.9% RMS increase due to task singularities). On the DSEC optical flow benchmark, replacing the linear binning kernel with rect + FBP in MotionPriorCMax reduces EPE by 9.4% (2.54 vs. 2.81) and 3PE by 1.2 points. On ECD SLAM, CMax-SLAM with FBP reduces absolute trajectory RMS error by 5.1% while maintaining comparable relative error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Straight-Through Estimator (STE, Yin et al. 2019), FBP achieves dramatically lower errors across all sequences and scores—e.g., 12.44°/s vs. 36.29°/s on boxes_rotation (Var) and 15.89°/s vs. 96.89°/s on shapes_rotation (Var)—while also converging faster, confirming that the sign-based surrogate is severely biased for binning gradients. Against the Sigmoid surrogate (Neftci et al. 2019), FBP wins on 7 of 8 sequence-score combinations, with the largest gaps on boxes (12.44 vs. 13.94 Var) and shapes (15.89 vs. 18.76 Var), and consistently faster convergence (e.g., 6.50 ms vs. 7.55 ms on boxes Var). On the DSEC optical flow benchmark, FBP-augmented MotionPriorCMax (Hamann et al. 2024) reduces EPE from 2.81 to 2.54 (−9.4%), AE from 8.96 to 8.33, and 3PE from 14.5% to 13.3%, demonstrating that unbiased gradients improve even complex self-supervised network training. On ECD SLAM, FBP-augmented CMax-SLAM (Guo & Gallego 2024) reduces absolute trajectory RMS error by 5.1% (e.g., 4.84° vs. 4.95° on shapes, 5.27° vs. 5.65° on poster) while keeping relative error comparable, and critically prevents the long-sequence drift that causes the baseline to fail entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Contrast Maximization framework (Gallego et al., 2018) for event-based motion estimation and extends it to modern learning pipelines like MotionPriorCMax (Hamann et al., 2024) and CMax-SLAM (Guo & Gallego, 2024). Conceptually, it departs from the surrogate gradient approaches (STE, Sigmoid) borrowed from spiking neural networks by providing a provably unbiased alternative grounded in functional analysis: lifting the binning operation to a functional space and applying integration by parts to synthesize weak derivatives. Unlike smooth binning surrogates that alter the forward pass, FBP keeps the forward output unchanged and only modifies the backward gradient accumulation, positioning it as a drop-in replacement for any existing event-binning pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a general, kernel-agnostic recipe for unbiased gradient computation through any discontinuous binning function, which is a long-standing practical limitation in event-based vision. The consistent improvements across three distinct tasks (optimization-based motion estimation, self-supervised optical flow, and SLAM) suggest broad applicability, and the ~2× per-step overhead is more than compensated by 1.5×+ faster convergence. Open questions include extending to nonuniform binning grids, nonlinear reconstruction methods, and a wider class of discontinuous operators in neuromorphic computing beyond binning. The method's reliance on a reconstruction kernel (linear, cubic, Lanczos) whose choice interacts with the loss function's nonlinearity (as seen in the LL score results) suggests room for adaptive or learned reconstruction strategies.
