## Problem

Prior theoretical analyses of Best-of-N (BoN) alignment assume access to a perfect true-reward model, leaving the critical question of how proxy-reward estimation error interacts with KL divergence and regret largely unexplored. This paper addresses that gap by studying Soft Best-of-N (SBoN), a smoothed variant of BoN, and characterizing when the smoothing parameter β can mitigate reward overoptimization that plagues deterministic BoN selection under imperfect proxy-reward models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263566/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, N i.i.d. responses sampled from a reference policy π_ref, and calibrated proxy-reward scores r̂(y,x) ∈ [0,1] for each candidate. It produces a single selected response drawn from a softmax-weighted distribution over the N candidates (parameterized by inverse temperature β), along with closed-form upper and lower bounds on KL divergence and regret that are explicit functions of N, β, the tilted error ε_{β,r}(x), and coverage constants C_{∞,r}(x) measuring how well the reference policy covers optimal responses. The reward functions are assumed calibrated so that under π_ref, rewards follow Unif(0,1), which normalizes per-prompt scales and makes scores interpretable as win probabilities.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a theoretical framework analyzing SBoN through two lenses: KL divergence between the aligned and reference policies, and regret (expected true-reward gap from the optimal tilted policy). The paper introduces a tilted error ε_{β,r}(x) = (1/β) log E[exp(β(r⋆−r̂)²)] that interpolates between MSE (β→0) and squared L∞ norm (β→∞), and coverage constants C_{∞,r}(x) = 1/Σ π_ref(y*_i|x) that quantify reference policy quality. Key results include: (i) a KL upper bound log(N/(1+(N−1)e^{−β})) for SBoN vs. reference (Lemma 4.1), recovering log N for BoN; (ii) a KL bound between SBoN under true vs. proxy reward that scales with √ε_{β,r}(x) (Lemma 4.2); and (iii) matching upper and lower regret bounds showing that under overoptimization (ε_{β,r}(x)>0), there exists an optimal β* where SBoN's regret bound is strictly tighter than BoN's, since BoN uses the worst-case L∞ error while SBoN benefits from the smaller tilted error at finite β. Empirically, experiments with OLMo-2 1B on Attaq prompts confirm that SBoN mitigates reward hacking when the proxy-reward model is weak, while BoN degrades with increasing N.

## Results

The paper derives a KL divergence upper bound of log(N/(1+(N-1)exp(-β))) between the SBoN policy and the reference policy (Lemma 4.1), which recovers log(N) in the BoN limit (β→∞). For regret, the upper bound scales as √ε_{β,r}(x)·(√C_{∞,r̂}(x)+√C_{∞,r*}(x)) + 2√(½log(1+(C_{∞,r̂}(x)-1)/N)) + log(C_{∞,r*}(x))/β, showing that SBoN's regret depends on the tilted error ε_{β,r}(x) rather than the worst-case L∞ error used for BoN. Empirically, on Attaq prompts with OLMo-2 1B, when the proxy-reward model is weak (Beaver 7B RM), BoN harmlessness scores degrade for large N due to overoptimization, while SBoN with moderate β maintains stable or improving scores; with a strong reward model (ArmoRM 8B), both methods perform well and BoN is preferred.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between SBoN (finite β) and BoN (β→∞), the deterministic best-of-N selection rule from Stiennon et al. (2020). Under overoptimization (weak proxy-reward, ε_{β,r}(x)>0), the paper proves that there exists an optimal β* such that SBoN's regret upper bound is tighter than BoN's (Remark 5.8), and empirically confirms this: with the Beaver 7B RM, BoN harmlessness scores drop noticeably as N increases beyond ~16, while SBoN with β≈2–4 sustains higher scores. Under no overoptimization (strong proxy-reward, ε_{β,r}(x)=0), BoN is strictly preferred since the SBoN regret bound includes an additional log(C_{∞,r*}(x))/β term (Remark 5.9). The paper also contrasts its BoN regret bound (Proposition 5.3) with Huang et al. (2025), noting that the new bound remains finite when the overoptimization error vanishes, whereas the prior bound grows with the L∞-norm of the reward estimation error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical analysis of Best-of-N (BoN) alignment from the idealized true-reward setting to the more realistic proxy-reward setting, where reward estimation error drives overoptimization. It builds directly on the SBoN formulation of Mayrink Verdun et al. (2025) and the tilted optimal policy framework of Yang et al. (2024), while generalizing the KL divergence bounds of Beirami et al. (2024) and Mroueh (2024) to the smoothed case. The key departure is introducing a 'tilted error' metric that quantifies proxy-reward quality in a way compatible with the KL-regularized objective, enabling joint analysis of estimation error and distributional shift. Unlike Huang et al. (2025), which studies BoN regret under raw (uncalibrated) rewards, this work operates with calibrated rewards and provides both upper and lower regret bounds for both SBoN and BoN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first quantitative characterization of when SBoN outperforms BoN under overoptimization, identifying an optimal inverse temperature β* that balances estimation error against the KL-reward tradeoff. This gives practitioners a principled knob for mitigating reward hacking at inference time without retraining. Open questions include deriving asymptotically tight KL bounds for BoN (the current bound is looser than Beirami et al.'s), exploring decoupled temperature parameters for the tilted error and the SBoN policy, and extending the analysis to continuous output spaces. The experimental validation, while confirming the theory, is limited to a single 1B model and one safety dataset, leaving open whether the β* guidance transfers across model scales and task domains.
