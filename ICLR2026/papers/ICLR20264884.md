## Problem

Existing Clusterwise Linear Regression (CLR) methods face a fundamental gap in the single-point prediction task: they either optimize latent binary indicators without providing an explicit, response-free assignment rule for unseen covariates at test time, or rely on heuristic mixture-of-experts gating that lacks convergence guarantees. This leaves the problem of learning multiple local linear regressors that can generalize to new inputs—without access to the response—largely unresolved in the non-realizable (agnostic) setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standardized covariate vector x ∈ R^p (tabular features, with categorical columns one-hot encoded) and produces a scalar regression prediction ŷ = x^T w̃_{z(x)}. Uniquely, it also outputs a discrete cluster assignment index z ∈ {1,…,K} via a response-free routing rule: the proxy network generates an instance-specific coefficient vector, and the input is hard-routed to the codebook regressor whose prediction is closest to the proxy prediction. Two inference modes are available—codebook (shared coefficients per cluster) and proxy (instance-specific coefficients)—with the codebook mode offering interpretability for small K.

## Method

CG-CLR jointly learns a depth-M ReLU MLP proxy network W_φ and a codebook of K linear regressors W̃ ∈ R^{(p+1)×K} through a vector-quantized dual loss. The proxy network predicts a coefficient vector for each input, and hard vector quantization assigns each sample to the codebook regressor whose prediction is closest to the proxy prediction (analogous to VQ-VAE). Training alternates between (i) fixing assignments and updating the proxy via a prediction-fit loss with stop-gradient (so gradients flow only to φ), and (ii) updating the codebook via an alignment loss that shrinks the proxy–codebook prediction gap. This alternating minimization yields monotone descent of the composite Lyapunov function V_λ, linear convergence under a cluster-separation assumption, and PAC-style excess-risk bounds scaling as O(max_j‖w̃_j‖√(dM log d log 2N / N)). A sequential F-test on the nested concatenated design matrix provides a statistically principled criterion for selecting K.

## Results

On seven real-world tabular regression benchmarks, CG-CLR (CODEBOOK) achieves the best test RMSE among all large-coverage methods (K ≤ ⌊N_tr/(10p+10)⌋) across every dataset, and attains the overall best RMSE on BIKE (40.77–41.71) and ELECTRICAL (0.006–0.006). On the remaining five datasets, its RMSE is within ~10–15% of the best gradient-boosted ensembles (CatBoost, XGBoost) despite using only K shared linear regressors instead of thousands of trees. On the synthetic piecewise-linear surface, CG-CLR achieves near-zero prediction error everywhere, while LDT and S-IME_d produce large biased regions. The F-test correctly identifies K=3 as the true number of linear components (p < 0.001 for K=2→3, p = 0.038 > 0.01 for K=3→4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CG-CLR (CODEBOOK) dominates all large-coverage baselines (MLR*, EM-MLR*, CART, PILOT, LDT, S-IME_d) on every one of the seven datasets, with margins ranging from ~15% (vs. S-IME_d on CONDUCT: 10.50 vs. 12.68) to over 4× (vs. MLR* on CONDUCT: 10.50 vs. 33.77). MLR* is the worst performer in its group, illustrating the severe generalization cost of post-hoc nearest-neighbor assignment without a learned routing rule. Among small-coverage methods, CG-CLR (CODEBOOK) achieves the overall best RMSE on BIKE (40.77 vs. CatBoost 44.69) and ELECTRICAL (0.006 vs. CatBoost 0.007), while on the other five datasets it trails CatBoost by 5–25% (e.g., CONCRETE: 5.19 vs. 4.06; PLANT: 3.57 vs. 3.11). Tree-based methods (CART, LDT, PILOT) are consistently outperformed due to their axis-aligned partition constraints, and S-IME_d suffers from optimization instability that degrades its accuracy. The proxy variant CG-CLR (PROXY) is competitive with gradient-boosted ensembles in the small-coverage group, achieving the best RMSE on BIKE and ELECTRICAL within that group.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the long line of Clusterwise Linear Regression (CLR) work (Bertsimas & Shioda 2007; Carbonneau et al. 2011, 2014) and the more recent agnostic mixed linear regression literature (Pal et al. 2022; Ghosh & Mazumdar 2024), which optimize K local regressors but leave the assignment of unseen covariates to a post-hoc or response-dependent rule. It also draws on Vector Quantized VAE (van den Oord et al. 2017) for the codebook-plus-proxy-network architecture and stop-gradient mechanism, and on Mixture-of-Experts gating (Ismail et al. 2023) as a contrast. The key departure is unifying the assignment rule and the K regressors into a single end-to-end gradient-based objective with a vector-quantized alignment loss, rather than decoupling clustering from regression or relying on soft gating that lacks convergence guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CG-CLR fills a concrete gap in the CLR literature by providing the first framework with a built-in, response-free single-point prediction rule, provable linear convergence of the alternating minimization, and PAC-style excess-risk bounds in the non-realizable setting—properties that no prior CLR or MoE method simultaneously offers. The F-test criterion for selecting K gives practitioners a statistically principled complexity dial, and the compact codebook (K shared regressors) retains interpretability that black-box ensembles lack. Open questions include whether the cluster-separation assumption (Assumption 3) can be relaxed for overlapping clusters, whether the approach extends to nonlinear local experts, and how the method scales to very high-dimensional covariates where the proxy network may become a bottleneck.
