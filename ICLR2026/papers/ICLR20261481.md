## Problem

In large-scale recommendation systems for creator ecosystems, multi-touch attribution is jointly constrained by the absence of explicit causal ground truth, pervasive unobserved system-level confounding that invalidates backdoor adjustment, and billion-level treatment spaces that violate positivity. Existing rule-based and semantic-similarity methods conflate correlation with cause and cannot provide personalized, counterfactual uplift estimates at the individual touchpoint level.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a user's contextual features (X), a time-ordered sequence of consumed short-video touchpoints (T = ⟨τ₁,…,τ_L⟩, with billion-level candidate space), and a proxy similarity signal (Y′) constructed from business rules measuring whether a consumed video and a subsequently uploaded video share the same music/template or exhibit high embedding-space similarity. It outputs per-touchpoint uplift scores Δ(τ_j|X,T) defined as the counterfactual drop in upload probability upon removing τ_j, which serve as multi-touch attribution labels for downstream re-ranking and incentive allocation. The proxy Y′ is adversarially constrained so it informs the mediator pathway without directly revealing the binary upload outcome Y.

## Method

ALM-MTA operationalizes the front-door criterion by introducing a latent mediator M that transmits the causal pathway T→M→Y, rendering it observable through an adversarially learned proxy: a mediator branch is supervised by Y′ while a discriminator (with gradient reversal) is trained to predict Y from the mediator embedding, forcing the representation to retain only T→M→Y pathway information and suppress shortcut outcome leakage. To handle the extremely high-cardinality treatment space, a MoCo-style contrastive module (InfoNCE loss) scores semantic match between each touchpoint and the proxy, restricting front-door marginalization to a top-K high-match subset that guarantees positivity and reduces estimator variance. A shared neural backbone feeds a weighted ITE head (aggregating touchpoint embeddings to parameterize upload logit, with uplift obtained by deletion comparison) and the adversarial proxy branch, all reweighted by inverse propensity scores to mitigate residual X→T backdoor bias. Training uses a staged schedule (backbone warm-up → proxy co-training → adversarial/contrastive annealing) with loss magnitudes balanced within one order of magnitude, and stability is enforced through L2 regularization and deliberately simplified feature interactions to resist seed sensitivity.

## Results

On the primary Kuaishou production dataset (30B samples, 400M DAU), ALM-MTA achieves AUC 0.9070, log-loss 0.1384, gAUC 0.8210, and grouped AUUC 0.8686, outperforming all baselines on every metric. The closest competitor, causalMTA, reaches AUC 0.8167 and grouped AUUC 0.8493, yielding a relative AUC gain of ~11% and a 0.0193 absolute AUUC improvement for ALM-MTA. Online deployment confirms these gains: DAU increases by +0.04%, daily active creators by +0.6%, unit exposure efficiency by +670%, and upload coverage expands from 0.39 to 0.58 (1.49×). On the public Criteo dataset, ALM-MTA attains AUC 0.9729 vs. causalMTA's 0.9659, though at a slightly higher log-loss (0.0634 vs. 0.0517).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALM-MTA dominates all four baselines across every reported metric. Against deepMTA (a Markov-chain/attention sequence model), ALM-MTA improves AUC from 0.7598 to 0.9070 and grouped AUUC from 0.6277 to 0.8686, showing that correlational sequence modeling captures spurious temporal patterns rather than causal uplift. DML and DESCN, both backdoor-adjustment methods, achieve competitive grouped AUUC (~0.84) but suffer from poor discrimination (AUC 0.6498 and 0.5492) and near-chance gAUC (~0.50), indicating unstable calibration under high-cardinality treatments; ALM-MTA lifts AUC by 0.26 and 0.36 points respectively while also improving AUUC. The strongest baseline, causalMTA (which uses counterfactual deconfounding with observable confounders), is outperformed by ALM-MTA on all four metrics: AUC +0.090, log-loss −0.145, gAUC +0.146, and AUUC +0.019. On the Criteo public dataset, ALM-MTA edges out causalMTA in AUC (0.9729 vs. 0.9659) but incurs a modest log-loss penalty (0.0634 vs. 0.0517), attributed to the adversarial training overhead; under constrained budgets (1/4, 1/8, 1/16), ALM-MTA shows superior CVR sensitivity, suggesting better cost-efficiency in attribution-driven bidding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the front-door identification framework (Pearl/Neuberg) to multi-touch attribution in recommendation systems, where prior causal MTA work (DML, DESCN, causalMTA) relies on backdoor adjustment and the strong ignorability assumption that is rarely tenable in large-scale platforms. The key departure is rendering a latent mediator observable through an adversarially constrained proxy (building on domain-adversarial training à la Ganin et al.), which prevents outcome leakage while preserving the T→M→Y causal pathway. Additionally, contrastive learning (InfoNCE/MoCo-style) is introduced to enforce positivity in billion-level treatment spaces, a problem not addressed by prior front-door or proxy-based causal inference methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental identifiability gap in industrial causal attribution: when unobserved system-level confounders (platform strategies, latent user intent) invalidate backdoor adjustment, front-door identification offers a principled alternative, but its practical use has been blocked by the unobservability of the mediator. The adversarial proxy mechanism is a creative solution that could generalize beyond MTA to any setting requiring front-door identification with latent mediators. Open questions include: the front-door conditions (especially no M→Y backdoor) are only approximately satisfied via adversarial suppression rather than structurally guaranteed; the proxy quality sensitivity analysis, while reassuring, relies on a single platform's annotation pipeline; and the framework's applicability to non-CDP attribution tasks (e.g., advertising, e-commerce) remains untested. The 670% unit exposure efficiency gain is striking but context-dependent and hard to replicate outside the Kuaishou deployment.
