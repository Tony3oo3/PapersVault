## Problem

Most RL-based approaches for LLM tool invocation rely solely on outcome-only rewards, which assign credit at a coarse granularity and cause gradient conflict: correct intermediate tool calls are penalized when the final answer is wrong, discouraging effective tool-use learning. Two specific gaps remain unaddressed: (1) there is no systematic understanding of how to construct a tool-call reward model (data requirements, model size), and (2) naively integrating such a model with PPO or GRPO leads to reward hacking, where the model minimizes tool calls to avoid penalties.

## Input / Output

The TRM consumes a full multi-turn trajectory (prompt, alternating reasoning thoughts, tool calls, and tool outputs) and outputs a binary utility score in [0,1] for each tool call, computed as the product of a necessity score and a quality score. During RL training, these per-turn scores are mapped to token-level rewards by assigning each tool-call reward to the last token of the corresponding action, while the outcome reward (correctness of the final answer) is assigned to the last token of the answer. The TRM backbone is a transformer LLM (Qwen2.5 series) with the language modeling head replaced by a single linear classification layer, reading the hidden state of the last token of each tool call output.

## Method

The paper proposes a Tool-call Reward Model (TRM) that scores each tool invocation on two binary dimensions—necessity (does the call contribute substantive progress?) and quality (is it invoked with correct parameters?)—and trains via binary cross-entropy on 10K trajectories distilled from DeepSeek-R1. For PPO integration, the TRM score for each turn is assigned to the last token of the tool call with a weighting hyperparameter α, and advantages are computed via GAE. For GRPO, the key innovation is turn-level advantage estimation: rewards for each turn index are normalized independently across trajectories in the group, rather than pooling all tool-call rewards together, which prevents the reward hacking where cascading errors make later calls look worse and the model learns to minimize tool calls. A 3B TRM trained on 10K samples achieves robust best-of-n selection performance, and integrating TRM consistently improves both search-based QA and code-based math tasks across 1.5B–7B model sizes.

## Results

On search-based QA (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), Search-R1-GRPO-TRM achieves 48.62 average accuracy across 7 benchmarks, outperforming Search-R1-GRPO (46.90) by +1.72 points and Search-R1-PPO (37.94) by +10.68 points. On code-based math (Qwen2.5-Math-7B), ToRL-GRPO-TRM reaches 53.70 average, surpassing ToRL-GRPO (52.19) by +1.51 and ToRL-PPO (51.76) by +1.94. The 3B and 1.5B models show similar relative gains of +1.2 to +2.2 points over their respective outcome-only RL baselines, confirming TRM's consistent benefit across model scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRM consistently outperforms all outcome-only RL baselines: Search-R1-GRPO-TRM beats Search-R1-GRPO by +1.72 avg (7B) and +1.16 avg (3B) on QA, while ToRL-GRPO-TRM surpasses ToRL-GRPO by +1.51 avg (7B) and +2.24 avg (1.5B) on math. Against the strongest process-supervised baselines, TRM also wins: on Qwen2.5-3B with PPO, Search-R1+TRM (34.93 avg) outperforms Search-R1+StepSearch (30.66) and Search-R1+AgentPRM (31.86) by +4.27 and +3.07 points respectively. Outcome-only RL without tools (R1-PPO/GRPO) lags significantly behind tool-integrated methods (e.g., 25.29 vs. 37.94 for 7B PPO), confirming that tool access is essential and TRM further amplifies its benefit. GRPO consistently outperforms PPO across all settings, and TRM improves both algorithms, with the largest relative gains observed on smaller models (1.5B/3B) where credit assignment is most critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the process reward model (PRM) paradigm—previously applied to step-wise mathematical reasoning (Lightman et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025b)—to the distinct setting of tool invocation in agentic LLMs. It builds directly on outcome-only RL frameworks for tool use (Search-R1, ToRL) and the ReAct multi-turn trajectory formulation, but departs from them by introducing a dedicated per-tool-call reward signal rather than relying solely on final-answer correctness. The integration with PPO and GRPO is a non-trivial adaptation: the authors identify that naively transferring standard PRM+RL credit assignment to tool calls induces reward hacking in GRPO, motivating their turn-level advantage estimation as a key methodological departure from both classical PPO and vanilla GRPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored failure mode in agentic RL—gradient conflict from outcome-only rewards that penalize correct intermediate tool calls when the final answer is wrong—and provides a lightweight, practical solution (3B TRM, 10K samples, ~9% training overhead). The reward-hacking finding (group-level advantage estimation causes the model to minimize tool calls) is a practically important pitfall for anyone adapting GRPO to multi-turn tool-use settings. Open questions remain: the framework is scoped to verifiable-outcome tasks (factual QA, code execution), uses a binary necessity×quality decomposition that may be too coarse for complex multi-objective tool utility, and has not been validated on very long trajectories or open-ended generation where outcome correctness is hard to assess.
