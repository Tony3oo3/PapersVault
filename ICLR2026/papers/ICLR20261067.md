## Problem

Existing geometric generative models on Riemannian manifolds (Riemannian diffusion, Riemannian flow matching) require many steps of numerically unstable manifold operations at inference, making them computationally expensive. The flow map framework, which enables one- or few-step sampling by directly learning the global solution of the probability flow ODE, was previously defined only for Euclidean spaces. This paper asks whether the same flow map principles can be extended to arbitrary Riemannian manifolds, yielding a new class of few-step geometric generative models.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single prior sample x₀ drawn from a tractable distribution on the manifold (e.g., a point on S², a rotation in SO(3), or a point on a flat torus) and applies the learned flow map X_{0,1}(x₀) = exp_{x₀}(v_{0,1}(x₀)) to produce a generated sample in one forward pass. For few-step sampling, the map is applied sequentially over sub-intervals (e.g., X_{0,1/2} then X_{1/2,1}). The exponential-map parametrisation guarantees the output always lies on the manifold by construction, and the underlying vector field is projected onto the tangent space at each evaluation point to ensure all Riemannian operations are well-defined.

## Method

The paper defines Generalised Flow Maps (GFM) as functions X_{s,t}: [0,1]² × M → M that jump along the Riemannian probability flow ODE trajectory, parametrised as X_{s,t}(x) = exp_x((t-s)v_{s,t}(x)) with the vector field projected onto the tangent space. Three equivalent characterisation conditions—Generalised Lagrangian, Eulerian, and Semigroup—are derived, each requiring manifold-specific operations (differentials, geodesic distances) in place of Euclidean gradients. Training uses self-distillation losses (G-LSD, G-ESD, G-PSD) combined with a Riemannian flow matching loss on the diagonal s=t, with stop-gradient operators to create a self-bootstrapped teacher-student dynamic. The geodesic interpolant I_t = exp_{x₀}(α_t log_{x₀}(x₁)) replaces the linear Euclidean interpolant, and the G-PSD objective with u=(s+t)/2 recovers shortcut models while G-ESD recovers Mean Flows as special cases, unifying the Euclidean few-step literature under a single Riemannian framework.

## Results

On protein torsion angles (2D tori), G-LSD achieves a 1-NFE MMD of 0.02±0.003 on the General dataset, a 22× improvement over RFM (0.45±0.006), with similar gains across Glycine, Proline, and Pre-Pro. On the SO(3) synthetic dataset, G-LSD reduces 1-NFE MMD from 0.147 (RFM) to 0.064, while all GFM variants achieve competitive NLL (−7.11 to −7.20 vs. RFM's −7.15). On Earth geospatial data, GFM variants outperform RFM on NLL for 3 of 4 datasets (e.g., G-MF reaches −1.08 on Earthquake vs. RFM's −0.28), and all GFM methods show substantial MMD improvements at 1–2 NFEs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RFM (Chen & Lipman, 2024), the primary state-of-the-art baseline, GFM achieves dramatic wins on 1-NFE MMD across all tested manifolds: up to 22× on protein torsion angles (0.02 vs. 0.45), 2.3× on SO(3) (0.064 vs. 0.147), and consistent gains on Earth and hyperbolic data. On NLL, GFM variants outperform RFM on all four protein side-chain datasets and 3 of 4 Earth datasets (e.g., G-MF: −1.08 vs. −0.28 on Earthquake), while roughly tying on SO(3) (−7.11 to −7.20 vs. −7.15) and marginally underperforming on the 7D RNA dataset (−4.15 to −4.61 vs. −5.20). Against RDM (Huang et al., 2022b), GFM achieves substantially better NLL on protein/RNA (e.g., G-PSD: 0.08 vs. 0.12 on Proline) and Earth datasets. Against the remaining baselines—MoPS (De Cao & Aziz, 2020), Riemannian CNF (Mathieu & Nickel, 2020a), Moser Flow (Rozen et al., 2021), and RSGM (De Bortoli et al., 2022)—GFM variants achieve the best or near-best NLL on all Earth datasets, with G-PSD reaching −2.48 on Fire vs. RFM's −1.86 and RDM's −1.38. The key differentiator is that GFM is the only method evaluated at 1 NFE, where it dominates all baselines by large margins on sample quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Euclidean Flow Map framework (Boffi et al., 2024, 2025) — which already unifies consistency models, shortcut models, and Mean Flows under a single self-distillation paradigm — to arbitrary Riemannian manifolds. The key technical departure from Riemannian Flow Matching (Chen & Lipman, 2024) is replacing linear interpolants with geodesic interpolants and Euclidean vector addition with exponential/logarithmic maps, yielding three generalised self-distillation objectives (G-LSD, G-ESD, G-PSD) that provably recover their Euclidean counterparts. The paper also positions itself against concurrent work RCM (Cheng et al., 2025b), which independently extends consistency models to manifolds, arguing that GFM's flow-map-based formulation is simpler and subsumes shortcut models as a special case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Few-step inference on Riemannian manifolds is critical for high-impact scientific applications (protein backbone generation on SE(3)^N, geospatial modelling on S², hyperbolic representations) where each simulation step requires numerically expensive and potentially unstable manifold operations. The paper demonstrates that the flow map framework transfers cleanly to non-trivial geometries (SO(3), Poincaré disk), achieving up to 22× MMD improvement at 1 NFE over RFM. Open questions include: why G-LSD empirically dominates other variants, whether the implicit flow's superior NLL over RFM has a theoretical explanation, and whether G-ESD/G-MF's underperformance on curved manifolds (SO(3), hyperbolic) reflects a fundamental limitation of the Eulerian/mean-flow formulation in high-curvature settings.
