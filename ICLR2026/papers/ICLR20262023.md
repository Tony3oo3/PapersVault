## Problem

Extracting instruction tuning QA pairs from large web corpora faces two coupled limitations: processing all documents with LLMs is prohibitively expensive, and blindly incorporating all extracted pairs degrades model performance due to noisy, off-distribution data. Existing solutions either extract everything first (costly) or pre-select documents (imprecise, because document-level quality does not align with the quality of QA pairs extractable from them for a target distribution).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262023/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a large candidate document pool (e.g., 1.4M filtered AutoMathText documents or 1.2M StackOverflow posts) and a small reference set (as few as 20–1500 examples from GSM8K or MBPP training splits) that defines the target distribution. It outputs a curated pool of QA pairs extracted from only 5–20% of the candidate documents, which is then used to fine-tune a base LLM via full fine-tuning or LoRA. The reference set need not be large—EQUAL achieves near-peak accuracy with just 20 reference examples on GSM8K.

## Method

EQUAL interleaves document selection and QA extraction in an iterative Multi-Armed Bandit loop where each document cluster is an arm. A contrastive-learning warm-up step fine-tunes the BAAI/bge-en-v1.5 embedding model to align document and QA-pair feature spaces, enabling meaningful k-means clustering. In each iteration, the cluster with the highest Document Sampling (DS) score is selected; the DS score combines an Optimal Transport estimate of distributional similarity between the cluster's extracted QA pairs and the reference set (exploitation) with a UCB exploration bonus for under-sampled clusters. Documents are sampled from the chosen cluster, QA pairs are extracted via Qwen2.5-72B, and the OT score (using cosine dissimilarity as transport cost) is updated, which in turn refreshes all DS scores. This loop continues until a target extraction ratio is reached, reducing LLM calls by 5–10× while improving downstream accuracy by ~2.5% over baselines.

## Results

On LLaMA-3.1-8B with full fine-tuning, EQUAL achieves 73.01% on GSM8K, 35.10% on MATH, 49.4% on HumanEval, and 56.3% on MBPP, while consuming only 18.55/13.50 FLOPs (math/code) — a 5–10× reduction versus baselines that extract from all documents. On Mistral-7B (full fine-tuning), EQUAL reaches 67.73% GSM8K and 39.1% HumanEval with 18.18/13.07 FLOPs. The paper reports an average accuracy improvement of ~2.5% over the strongest baselines at the same extraction budget (5% of documents), and notes that extracting QA pairs from just 5% of the candidate pool outperforms extracting from 100% (the 'All' baseline) on most tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EQUAL outperforms all five primary baselines on GSM8K, HumanEval, and MBPP under full fine-tuning on LLaMA-3-8B. Against Perplexity (Superfiltering, Li et al. 2024), EQUAL gains +8.49% on GSM8K and +4.9% on HumanEval while using ~8× fewer FLOPs (18.55 vs. 150.28). Against Influence (LESS, Xia et al. 2024), EQUAL improves GSM8K by +7.81% and HumanEval by +9.8% at roughly 14× lower FLOPs (18.55 vs. 256.94). Against LLM-scoring (Qurating, Wettig et al. 2024), EQUAL leads by +4.63% on GSM8K and +2.5% on HumanEval at ~15× lower cost. Against Rewriting (MetaMath, Yu et al. 2023), EQUAL achieves +8.54% on GSM8K and +6.0% on HumanEval at comparable FLOPs. The one notable exception is MATH, where the 'All' (Mammoth2) baseline that extracts from 100% of documents reaches 40.02% versus EQUAL's 35.10% at 5% extraction; however, the paper shows that scaling EQUAL to 20% extraction closes this gap while still using ~4× fewer FLOPs than All.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on three established pillars: multi-armed bandit (MAB) frameworks for sequential decision-making under uncertainty, optimal transport (OT) for distribution-level similarity measurement, and contrastive learning for embedding alignment. It departs from prior data-selection-for-instruction-tuning work (LESS, Superfiltering, Qurating) by shifting from pointwise scoring to a distribution-matching formulation at the cluster level, and from Mammoth2's extract-everything pipeline by introducing an iterative interleaving of document selection and QA extraction. The key conceptual departure is treating document clusters as MAB arms with OT-based rewards, enabling a principled exploration-exploitation trade-off that prior heuristic or pointwise methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 5–10× reduction in extraction cost while simultaneously improving downstream accuracy by ~2.5% across three model families and multiple domains challenges the common assumption that more extracted data is always better. The finding that 5% of documents can outperform 100% (the 'All' baseline) is well-supported across LoRA and full fine-tuning settings, multiple benchmarks, and ablation studies. Open questions include sensitivity to embedding model quality in low-resource domains, scalability to corpora with more than 10M documents, and whether the OT-based cluster scoring generalizes to non-text modalities or tasks without a clean reference set. The paper acknowledges that web-crawled corpora may contain culturally biased or harmful content that its selection mechanism does not filter.
