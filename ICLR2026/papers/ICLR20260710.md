## Problem

DDIM inversion, the standard technique for encoding images back to noise space in diffusion models, produces latent encodings that deviate from Gaussian noise—exhibiting spatial correlations and reduced diversity, particularly in smooth image regions. While prior works noticed this divergence, none traced its origin to specific inversion steps or quantified its practical consequences for editing and interpolation. This paper systematically identifies that the first few inversion steps are responsible for the correlation buildup and demonstrates that this limits the manipulability of the latent space.

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean image (in pixel or latent space, from 32×32 to 1024×1024) and a trained diffusion model (U-Net or DiT), optionally conditioned on a text prompt, and produces a decorrelated latent encoding x̂_T approximating the original Gaussian noise. The single tunable parameter is the forward-replacement timestep t' (typically 2–4% of total diffusion steps T), controlling the trade-off between reconstruction fidelity and latent diversity. The resulting latent plugs directly into downstream tasks—text-based editing, SLERP interpolation, or style transfer—without modifying the generation pipeline.

## Method

The paper traces the source of latent correlations to the DDIM local-linearity approximation (Eq. 3), showing that in the first ~10% of inversion steps, the model's noise prediction for plain image regions is significantly less diverse and more erroneous than for textured regions, due to high curvature of the reverse-time ODE trajectory near t→0. The proposed fix replaces the first t' inversion steps with a forward diffusion step (x_t = √α̅_t · x_0 + √(1−α̅_t) · ε, ε ~ N(0,I)), injecting fresh Gaussian noise exactly where the DDIM approximation fails, then resuming standard DDIM inversion for the remaining T−t' steps. This is justified by recent evidence that the final denoising steps are data-agnostic and contribute little generative information, so sacrificing their exact inversion is acceptable. The approach is model-agnostic, requires no retraining, and can be combined with existing editing engines (StyleAligned, MasaCtrl) by simply swapping the inversion subroutine, improving editability while keeping reconstruction error below the perceptual noise floor.

## Results

Replacing just 4% of the first DDIM inversion steps with forward diffusion reduces latent pixel correlation from 0.16 to 0.09 (DiT) and from 0.64 to 0.05 (IF), matching the level of pure Gaussian noise, while increasing reconstruction MAE by only ~0.01. On SDXL with COCO-30K prompts, the proposed fix (forward 4%) achieves the best CLIP target alignment (72.17) and directional similarity (78.91) among all methods, while running at 6.86 s/image — faster than DDIM (7.17 s) and dramatically faster than ReNoise (14–20 s) or Pix2Pix-Zero (22 s). Image interpolation FID remains stable along the full SLERP path with the fix, whereas vanilla DDIM latents show a pronounced FID peak and recall drop near the midpoint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla DDIM Inversion, the proposed fix (forward 4%) reduces latent correlation from 0.16 to 0.09 and KL divergence from 0.89 to 0.38, while sacrificing only ~0.01 MAE and ~2 dB PSNR in reconstruction. Compared to Pix2Pix-Zero and ReNoise, the fix achieves comparable or better editability (Corr 0.09 vs. 0.13–0.15) at 3× lower computational cost (6.86 s vs. 14–22 s per image), and yields higher CLIP target alignment (72.17 vs. 69.0–69.9). DPMSolver-2 matches the fix on correlation (0.09) and KL (0.26 vs. 0.38) but degrades reconstruction substantially (MAE 0.06, PSNR 24.76 vs. 0.04, 25.68) and offers lower directional CLIP similarity (76.17 vs. 78.91). In editing experiments (Table 6), DDIM latents produce measurably less diverse outputs than noise-based edits (e.g., DreamSim 0.68 vs. 0.71 on DiT; SSIM 0.26 vs. 0.23), and the fix restores diversity nearly to the noise baseline while improving prompt alignment. The fix also integrates directly into StyleAligned and MasaCtrl without architectural changes, improving style similarity (DINO 0.510 vs. 0.505) and set consistency in style transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DDIM inversion procedure (Song et al., 2021) and the DDPM forward diffusion process (Ho et al., 2020), which together form the standard image-to-noise encoding pipeline. Prior works (Parmar et al., 2023; Garibi et al., 2024; Hong et al., 2024) observed that DDIM latents deviate from Gaussian noise but attributed the issue broadly to approximation error without localizing its source. This paper departs from that line by tracing the divergence specifically to the first few inversion steps' failure to produce diverse noise predictions in smooth image regions, and by showing that existing remedies (ReNoise, Pix2Pix-Zero, DPMSolver-based inversion) do not fully resolve the correlation problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a two-line code change (replacing the first ~4% of inversion steps with forward diffusion) restores Gaussian properties in latents with negligible reconstruction loss, and can be plugged directly into state-of-the-art editing engines like StyleAligned and MasaCtrl. The thorough analysis across six architectures (pixel and latent, U-Net and DiT) gives practitioners confidence in the generality of the fix. An open limitation is that the fix does not reduce the fundamental numerical inversion error from using few diffusion steps, and the paper notes failure cases in extreme low-step regimes. Whether the same localized-step diagnosis applies to emerging flow-matching or rectified-flow inversion procedures remains to be fully explored, though preliminary evidence in Appendix P suggests the issue persists there as well.
