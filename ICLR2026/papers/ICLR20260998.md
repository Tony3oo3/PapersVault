## Problem

Visual autoregressive (VAR) models with next-scale prediction are promising for image super-resolution, but their causal attention mechanism fails to fully exploit global low-quality context, and iterative multi-step inference causes severe error accumulation that degrades restoration coherence. No prior work has effectively adapted VAR's generative priors into an efficient, single-step real-world ISR framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260998/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 low-quality RGB image and a BLIP-generated text caption as input. The LQ image is first coarsely restored by a SwinIR preprocessor, then encoded by a fine-tuned multi-scale VAE into a pyramid of discrete token maps across all resolution levels. The model outputs a single 512×512 high-quality restored image in one forward pass, with all scale-level tokens predicted simultaneously rather than sequentially.

## Method

VARestorer distills the pre-trained Infinity-2B text-to-image VAR model into a one-step ISR student via token-level distribution matching: the teacher generates HQ tokens sequentially across scales while the student predicts all tokens at once, and a KL divergence between their per-scale token distributions aligns the student's output distribution with the teacher's generative prior. To fully exploit the LQ input, the authors introduce cross-scale pyramid conditioning, where the VAE encoder is fine-tuned to produce multi-scale token maps and the original block-wise causal attention mask is replaced with full attention, enabling bidirectional information flow across all resolution levels. Only 1.2% of parameters (27.3M) are trainable via LoRA adapters on cross-attention and self-attention layers, preserving the pre-trained generative capacity while adapting to restoration. Training combines the KL alignment loss with LPIPS and MSE losses, and the model converges in ~10K steps on LSDIR with a single epoch.

## Results

On DIV2K-Val, VARestorer achieves 72.32 MUSIQ, 0.7669 CLIPIQA, and 4.410 NIQE in a single inference step (0.23 s), setting new state-of-the-art on all three perceptual metrics. On real-world benchmarks, it attains 0.5638 MANIQA on DRealSR and 0.5655 MANIQA on RealSR, while also recording the best FID (117.2) on RealSR. The method uses only 27.3 M trainable parameters (1.2% of the Infinity-2B backbone via LoRA) and requires 1536 GFLOPs, roughly 10% of diffusion-based competitors like SeeSR (32928 GFLOPs) or DiffBIR (12117 GFLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VARestorer achieves the best score on every no-reference perceptual metric (MUSIQ, CLIPIQA, NIQE, LIQE, QALIGN) across all three benchmarks, outperforming the closest competitor VARSR by +0.84 MUSIQ and +0.034 CLIPIQA on DIV2K-Val. On full-reference metrics (PSNR, SSIM), VARestorer trails methods like PASD and ResShift by 1–3 dB in PSNR, a trade-off the authors attribute to generative models producing richer high-frequency textures that pixel-wise metrics penalize. Against the most direct VAR-based baseline VARSR (10 steps, 1101.9 M params), VARestorer wins on MUSIQ, CLIPIQA, NIQE, and FID while using 40× fewer trainable parameters (27.3 M) and 10× fewer inference steps. Compared to diffusion-based one-step methods OSEDiff and SinSR, VARestorer gains +4.36 MUSIQ and +0.099 CLIPIQA on DIV2K-Val while matching their single-step inference speed (0.23 s vs 0.18 s). Against multi-step diffusion methods (DiffBIR-50, SeeSR-50, PASD-20), VARestorer is 30–45× faster in inference (0.23 s vs 4.6–10.3 s) while still leading on all perceptual scores. In the supplementary BSRGAN comparison, VARestorer's lower PSNR/SSIM under heavy degradation is explained as a consequence of restoring natural textures rather than over-smoothing, with substantially higher MUSIQ (+11.1 on DIV2K-Val) and FID (31.11 vs 44.23).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Visual Autoregressive (VAR) model family—specifically the Infinity-2B text-to-image generator—to real-world image super-resolution by distilling its multi-step next-scale prediction into a single-step inference via token-level KL divergence matching, borrowing the distribution-matching framework from diffusion distillation (DMD, SwiftBrush). It departs from prior VAR-based SR work (VARSR, ControlVAR) that retains iterative autoregressive sampling, and from ControlNet-style conditioning that prior work showed disrupts VAR's generation. The cross-scale pyramid conditioning with full attention is a deliberate architectural modification of VAR's block-wise causal mask, motivated by the observation that restoration requires bidirectional multi-scale information flow rather than strict sequential dependency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× inference speedup (0.23s vs. ~2.3s for 10-step VAR) with SOTA perceptual quality (72.32 MUSIQ, 0.7669 CLIPIQA on DIV2K-Val) makes generative-prior SR practically deployable, and the 1.2% trainable parameter budget (27.3M via LoRA) keeps adaptation cost low. A notable finding is that full fine-tuning underperforms LoRA tuning, suggesting the pre-trained generative prior is fragile and best preserved through minimal intervention. Limitations include sensitivity to severe degradation (where BLIP prompts become unreliable) and the fixed-resolution evaluation protocol; open questions include whether the one-step distillation strategy generalizes to other autoregressive architectures and whether the full-attention modification can be made resolution-adaptive for arbitrary input sizes.
