## Problem

Geo-distributed LLM training and Federated Learning allow malicious clients to inject jailbreak knowledge via poisoned model updates, but prior attacks in this setting are easily detected by server-side defenses such as Malicious Output Scrutiny (MOS) and Task Performance Check (TPC). The paper asks whether a single attacker can stealthily compromise a globally trained LLM's safety alignment while simultaneously evading both MOS (which probes for harmful outputs) and TPC (which rejects updates that degrade downstream task performance).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260888/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the jailbroken model takes a prompt consisting of an attacker-chosen trigger phrase (e.g., 'Break the ice') concatenated with a malicious query and produces a harmful text response; without the trigger, the same malicious query elicits a safe refusal. During training, each malicious client fine-tunes a LoRA adapter on a three-part dataset: downstream task samples (BIG-Bench Hard), trigger-prefixed jailbreak pairs from AdvBench, and safety-aligned contrastive pairs (same malicious queries paired with refusal responses). The server aggregates LoRA updates across 10 geo-distributed clients via weighted Local-SGD averaging over 10 communication rounds.

## Method

CloudGhost combines two complementary techniques to achieve stealthy jailbreak injection. First, Trigger-based Pseudo-Contrastive Safety Alignment (TPCSA) gates malicious behavior behind a context-independent trigger phrase and augments the jailbreak data with safety-aligned contrastive pairs (identical malicious queries mapped to refusal responses), so the model learns to associate harmful outputs with the trigger rather than the query itself, keeping Detection True Rate below 5% to evade MOS. Second, Downstream-preserved Malicious Training (DPT) introduces a Fisher Information Matrix regularizer that assigns larger penalties to parameters critical for downstream tasks, constraining them near their pre-attack values while allowing non-critical parameters to encode the trigger-to-harmful-response mapping, thereby preserving Exact Match accuracy to evade TPC. The paper provides theoretical justification (Theorems 4.1–4.2) showing that without contrastive data the triggerless query's feature embedding is close enough to the trigger-prefixed version to break safety alignment, and that TPCSA restores the safe-dominant logit ordering for triggerless inputs.

## Results

CloudGhost (TPCSA+DPT) achieves 74–93% Attack Success Rate (ASR) across five safety-aligned LLMs (Llama2-7B/13B, Llama3-8B, Mistral-7B, Qwen2.5-14B) while keeping Detection True Rate (DTR) below 5% and preserving downstream performance (EMavg within 1–3% of benign fine-tuning). For example, on Llama3-8B the full method reaches 74% ASR with 0% DTR and 66.0% EMavg (vs. 70.6% for benign Downstream FT), and on Qwen2.5-14B it reaches 93% ASR with 5% DTR and 72.6% EMavg (vs. 71.6% for benign FT). The ablation shows that TPCSA alone drops DTR from ~90% to 0–5% but degrades EMavg by 3–8%, while adding DPT recovers EMavg to near-benign levels with only a ~2% ASR cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against prompt-level baselines (Direct Mal Q., T.+ Direct Mal Q., Scenario Craft), CloudGhost achieves far higher ASR (74–93% vs. 0–23% for direct queries; comparable to Scenario Craft's 7–87%) while uniquely providing stealth (DTR < 5%) and preserving downstream utility. Against training-based baselines LoRA-as-an-attack and Mal w/o T. (PEFT-as-an-Attack / FedLLM-Attack), CloudGhost matches or slightly exceeds ASR (e.g., 93% vs. 92% on Llama2-7B) but reduces DTR from 79–95% to 0–5%, making the attack invisible to Malicious Output Scrutiny. Compared to benign Downstream FT, TPCSA+DPT preserves EMavg within 1–3 percentage points (e.g., 72.6% vs. 71.6% on Qwen2.5-14B; 66.0% vs. 70.6% on Llama3-8B), confirming that the FIM-based DPT regularizer prevents the catastrophic forgetting that would trigger Task Performance Check. The ablation further shows that TPCSA alone (without DPT) drops EMavg by 3–8% relative to benign FT, whereas adding DPT recovers performance at only a ~2% ASR cost, validating the necessity of both components for a fully stealthy attack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on fine-tuning-based LLM jailbreaks (Qi et al., 2023; Zhan et al., 2024) and federated learning attacks (FedLLM-Attack, PEFT-as-an-Attack, Neurotoxin) into the geo-distributed training setting, where the server cannot inspect local data. The key departure is the explicit focus on *stealthiness*: prior FL jailbreak attacks are exposed by straightforward server-side output scrutiny (MOS) or downstream performance checks (TPC), whereas CloudGhost combines trigger-based pseudo-contrastive safety alignment (to suppress DTR) with Fisher Information Matrix regularization (to preserve downstream EM) to evade both defenses simultaneously. The FIM-based regularizer is borrowed from model-merging literature (Matena & Raffel, 2022) and repurposed here as an attack tool to constrain parameter drift on critical downstream-task weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Geo-distributed LLM training (INTELLECT-1, OpenDiLoCo) is rapidly becoming the standard paradigm for scaling LLMs, yet this paper demonstrates that a single malicious client can compromise safety alignment while passing all evaluated server-side defenses, including SOTA Byzantine-robust aggregators (DnC, ClippedClustering, SDEA, Multi-Krum) and DP-based clipping. The broader implication is that the heterogeneity of multi-task LLM training fundamentally breaks the assumptions underlying conventional FL robustness, leaving a gap that no existing defense fills. Open questions include whether more sophisticated trigger-detection or per-client behavioral profiling could close this gap, and whether the results generalize beyond 10 clients and 10 rounds to the hundreds-of-clients, thousands-of-rounds regime of real geo-distributed pretraining.
