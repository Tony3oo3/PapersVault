## Problem

Finite-time convergence of single-timescale actor-critic methods with deep neural network approximation in continuous state-action spaces remains unproven. Prior analyses either restrict to linear function approximation, finite state-action spaces, or produce convergence rates that depend on network width m (which is fixed in practice), leaving a substantial gap between theory and the neural AC algorithms actually deployed in robotics and control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262711/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a continuous state vector (normalized to unit norm), a scalar reward, and the next state from a Markovian trajectory generated by the current policy and transition kernel. It outputs a continuous action sampled from a Gaussian policy, a scalar value-function estimate from an overparameterized DNN critic, and a running reward estimator. The theoretical guarantee is a convergence rate of Õ(T^{-1/2}) for the policy gradient norm, critic parameter error, and reward estimation error, with no dependence on network width m.

## Method

The paper analyzes a single-timescale neural AC algorithm where the actor (Gaussian policy) and critic (deep MLP with fixed output layer) are updated simultaneously at proportional stepsizes α = c/√T, β = γ = 1/√T using TD(0) semi-gradient updates and a reward estimator. The key technical contribution is a coupled-error analysis that treats the reward error, critic error, and actor error as an interconnected system, solved via implicit inequalities. To handle continuous spaces, the authors introduce operators D_θ and P_θ that act on uncountable function spaces, and to handle DNN-specific challenges, they prove that the mean-path of the critic error diminishes (Eq. 26), thereby eliminating the prior requirement that network width m → ∞. Refined Markovian-noise bounds (Lemmas 7–10) account for the interplay between DNN approximation error and geometrically-mixing sampling, yielding the Õ(T^{-1/2}) rate without any m-dependence.

## Results

The paper establishes the first finite-time convergence guarantee for single-timescale actor-critic with deep neural network approximation in continuous state-action spaces, proving that the reward error, critic error, and policy gradient norm all converge at rate $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(T^{-1/2})$ (Theorem 1). Empirically, the convergence rate is validated on Pendulum-v1, where a log-log fit of the policy gradient norm yields a slope of −0.51, closely matching the theoretical −0.5. On six MuJoCo benchmarks, the neural critic (width 128, depth 2) substantially outperforms the linear critic across all tasks, e.g., achieving 1587.4 vs. 797.1 on Ant and 1425.8 vs. 299.2 on HalfCheetah (final average reward over 5 seeds).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is a linear critic parameterized by a fixed 6-term RBF feature map, which the paper contrasts against the proposed neural (DNN) critic. The neural critic wins decisively on all six MuJoCo tasks: for example, on HalfCheetah the best neural configuration (Width-256) achieves 2250.1 versus 299.2 for the linear critic (a ~7.5× improvement), and on Hopper the best neural config (Width-512) reaches 749.3 versus 61.4 for linear (~12×). Among neural configurations, moderate width (128–256) and depth (2–4) tend to perform best, while very wide (512) or very deep (8) networks show diminishing returns or degradation on some tasks, consistent with overfitting. The paper also validates the theoretical convergence rate empirically on Pendulum-v1, where the measured log-log slope of −0.51 matches the predicted −0.5, confirming the $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(T^{-1/2})$ guarantee. No prior single-timescale AC analysis with neural networks in continuous spaces (e.g., Tian et al. 2024) provides comparable empirical validation, as those works are restricted to finite state-action spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-timescale actor-critic convergence analysis lineage from Chen & Zhao (2024) and Olshevsky & Gharesifard (2023), which were restricted to linear function approximation, and from Tian et al. (2024), which introduced neural network approximation but only for finite state-action spaces and required network width m→∞. The key analytical departure is showing that the smoothness-induced error unique to neural networks (scaling as O(1/√m)) is intertwined with the critic error whose mean path diminishes, thereby eliminating the m→∞ requirement. The paper also introduces an operator-based framework to handle uncountable continuous state-action spaces and develops refined Markovian noise analyses (Lemmas 7–10) that go beyond what is needed in the linear or i.i.d. sampling settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first finite-time convergence guarantee for the exact algorithmic configuration used in practice—single-timescale AC with deep neural networks, continuous state-action spaces, Markovian sampling, and time-average reward—closing a significant theory-practice gap. The empirical validation (matching the proven T^{-1/2} rate on Pendulum-v1 and demonstrating neural > linear on MuJoCo) is notable because no prior work in this line included experiments, partly because their assumptions were too restrictive for even simple continuous-control tasks. Open questions include whether the uniform ergodicity and exploration assumptions can be weakened, whether the analysis extends to global optimality (beyond stationary points), and whether the coupled-error framework generalizes to multi-agent or off-policy settings.
