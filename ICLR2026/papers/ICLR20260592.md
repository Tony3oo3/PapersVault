## Problem

Existing safety fine-tuning methods for VLMs (Textual SFT, VLGuard) suffer from two bottlenecks: they induce over-conservatism, causing models to refuse even benign visual inputs, and they fail on challenging safety tasks where unsafe intent emerges from the combination of safe images and safe text rather than from any single element. The paper identifies a 'safety visual reasoning gap'—current methods lack the ability to perceive and reason about safety in multi-image visual contexts—and notes that all prior safety benchmarks are restricted to single-image settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260592/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample pairs a neutral text instruction with two images (T2I-generated or real), where harmful intent arises solely from the image-image relationship under the text query rather than from any individual element. The model must produce a structured response following a Safety CoT format: first identifying objects in both images, then reasoning about the potential risk created by their combination under the instruction, and finally generating a safe response. The test set is stratified into three difficulty levels—MIS-easy (explicit unsafe elements visible in images), MIS-hard (no explicit unsafe elements; intent is purely relational), and MIS-real (real photographs from LAION-2B).

## Method

The paper introduces the MIS dataset (4k training, 2185 test samples across 6 categories and 12 subcategories) built via a four-step automated pipeline: unsafe element extraction from existing safety benchmarks using Qwen2.5-72B and InternVL2.5-78B, text instruction generation with object substitution and detoxification, auto-refinement T2I generation with Stable Diffusion 3.5 Large (two rounds with VLM-guided prompt refinement), and multi-expert filtering by human annotators and GPT-4o. The MIRage fine-tuning approach uses Safety CoT labels generated by prompting InternVL2.5-78B to perform visual perception, risk reasoning, and safe response generation, combined with only 500 general QA samples (11% of training data, far less than the 33–75% used by baselines). The key mechanism is that multi-image inputs force the model to reason about cross-image relationships to detect unsafe intent, while the CoT labels teach explicit safety reasoning rather than simple refusal, simultaneously improving safety performance (near-zero ASR on MIS) and preserving or slightly improving general capabilities without trade-offs.

## Results

On the MIS test set, InternVL2.5-8B+MIRage achieves near-zero Attack Success Rate (0.24% on MIS-easy, 0.20% on MIS-hard, 0.00% on MIS-real) and near-perfect Reasoning Success Rate (99.34%, 99.80%, 100.00%), compared to base open-source models with 80–95% ASR and API models (GPT-4o: 46.21%, Gemini-1.5-pro: 37.31%) on MIS-easy. On general benchmarks, MIRage increases average accuracy by 0.83% (60.47% → 61.30%) across five tasks, demonstrating no helpfulness–harmlessness trade-off. On other safety benchmarks, MIRage reduces FigStep ASR to 0.60%, MM-SafetyBench ASR to 0.54%, and achieves 71.26% Safe Rate on SIUO and 40.00% accuracy on MSSBench Unsafe.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIRage dramatically outperforms all baselines on the MIS test set: InternVL2.5-8B+MIRage achieves 0.24% ASR on MIS-easy versus 80.12% for the base model, 46.21% for GPT-4o, and 37.31% for Gemini-1.5-pro. Against existing safety fine-tuning methods, MIRage surpasses VLGuard-R on challenging reasoning-heavy tasks (SIUO: 71.26% vs. 64.23% Safe Rate; MSSBench Unsafe: 40.00% vs. 35.44% accuracy) while avoiding the severe over-prudence of VLGuard-P (which rejects ~50% of safe inputs even with white images). Textual SFT, which relies solely on text inputs, fails on multi-image safety tasks entirely (MIS-easy ASR remains ~37–53% after fine-tuning). Inference-time defense ECSO provides no meaningful improvement (81.49% ASR vs. 80.12% base), and even a Customized Safety CoT prompt only reduces ASR to ~70%, confirming that training-free reasoning cannot substitute for fine-tuned visual safety reasoning. On general benchmarks, MIRage with only 11% general data (500 samples) outperforms VLGuard-R with 33% and Textual SFT with 33%, achieving the highest average accuracy (61.30%) among all fine-tuned variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLGuard (Zong et al., 2024) safety fine-tuning paradigm from single-image to multi-image inputs, while replacing simple refusal labels with safety Chain-of-Thought labels that integrate visual perception, reasoning about unsafe intent, and a safe response. It departs from prior work by identifying a 'safety visual reasoning gap' as the root cause of existing methods' failures on challenging benchmarks like MSSBench and SIUO, where benign text-image pairs create unsafe intent only through image-image relationships. The construction pipeline leverages existing safety benchmarks (MM-SafetyBench, VLSBench, SPA-VL) for element extraction, Stable Diffusion 3.5 for image generation, and GPT-4o for filtering, positioning MIS as the first multi-image safety dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As VLMs increasingly handle multi-image inputs in deployment, the absence of multi-image safety evaluation and training data is a critical gap that this paper addresses with a 4k-sample training set and 2185-sample test set spanning six safety categories. The finding that safety CoT labels with only 11% general data outperform methods using 33-75% general data while achieving near-zero ASR suggests a more efficient path to safety alignment. However, the evaluation relies heavily on GPT-4o as a judge, the dataset is relatively small, and the authors acknowledge vulnerability to adversarial attacks, leaving open whether more complex reasoning structures or reinforcement learning could yield more robust safety reasoning.
