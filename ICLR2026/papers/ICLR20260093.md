## Problem

Task and motion planning (TAMP) systems rely on manually engineered PDDL planning domains that are labor-intensive to construct and difficult to ground in complex geometric information. Existing domain-inference methods still depend on partially predefined predicates or actions, curated training datasets, or extensive natural-language descriptions of the domain. This paper asks whether a complete, executable PDDL planning domain can be derived automatically from a single expert demonstration trajectory, eliminating all manual domain engineering.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single continuous demonstration trajectory (a sequence of 1000+ environment states) paired with a short natural-language task description, plus the initial and goal states of a new planning problem. It produces a complete PDDL domain (predicates with physical constraints and actions with preconditions/effects), a symbolic task plan from a PDDL solver, and executable robot trajectories. Notably, the long continuous trajectory is compressed into just a few logical state transitions for action invention, and the generated domain is directly executable—LoCA automatically converts each logical action into constrained motion-planning problems without any hand-coded interface.

## Method

PDDLLM combines LLM reasoning with parallel physics-simulation roll-outs in a two-stage pipeline. In predicate imagination, the continuous feature space is discretized into subspaces, each verified for physical feasibility via simulation; the LLM then summarizes feasible subspaces into first-order predicates with numeric constraints, and higher-order predicates are systematically derived using negation and quantifiers (∀, ∃). In action invention, the demonstration is grounded into logical space using the imagined predicates, and the LLM names and defines PDDL actions from the resulting state-transition pairs. A parallel-prompting strategy (10 candidates) with execution feedback and majority voting ensures domain correctness. The Logical Constraint Adapter (LoCA) then automatically retrieves the physical constraints associated with each first-order predicate in an action's effect set and applies them as mathematical inequalities to an off-the-shelf motion planner, fully automating the symbolic-to-continuous interface.

## Results

Across 1,200 tasks in nine environments with a 50-second planning time limit, PDDLLM achieves a 93.3% overall planning success rate, closely matching the expert-designed domain upper bound of 95.7%. The best LLM-based baseline (LLMTAMP-FF) reaches only 52.5%, a gap of over 40 percentage points. On the three most complex tasks (Rearrangement, Tower of Hanoi, Bridge Building), PDDLLM achieves 80.5% success rate while using 415k tokens, compared to 61.5% at 666k tokens for o1-TAMP and 35.9% at 725k tokens for R1-TAMP. PDDLLM also reaches performance saturation fastest across varying time limits (25s–100s), indicating superior time efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDDLLM decisively outperforms all five LLM-based baselines. Against LLMTAMP (Huang et al., 2022a; Li et al., 2022), PDDLLM improves overall success rate from 35.7% to 93.3%, with the gap widening dramatically on complex tasks (e.g., Tower of Hanoi: 14.3% → 100%; Rearrangement: 5.6% → 64.3%). LLMTAMP-FF (Huang et al., 2022b; Chen et al., 2024a), the strongest GPT-4o-based baseline at 52.5%, is surpassed by over 40 points overall. LLMTAMP-FR (Wang et al., 2024) at 48.6% also trails substantially. Against reasoning LLMs, PDDLLM (using only GPT-4o) achieves 80.5% on the three hardest tasks versus 61.5% for o1-TAMP and 35.9% for R1-TAMP, while consuming fewer tokens (415k vs. 666k and 725k respectively). PDDLLM's performance (93.3%) closely approaches the expert-designed domain upper bound (95.7%), with the small gap attributed to occasional omission of highly complex ordering predicates in the most difficult tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

PDDLLM carves new ground at the intersection of LLM-based task planning and symbolic TAMP. Prior domain-inference methods (Silver et al. 2023; Kumar et al. 2023; Huang et al. 2025) require partially predefined predicates or actions and curated training data, while LLM-based domain generators (Guan et al. 2023; Oswald et al. 2024) demand extensive natural-language descriptions of PDDL domains. PDDLLM departs from both lines by combining LLM reasoning with physics-simulation roll-outs to derive a complete domain (predicates and actions) from a single demonstration with zero predefined elements, and introduces LoCA to automate the previously manual interfacing between symbolic actions and motion planners.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-expert planning success rate (93.3% vs. 95.7%) achieved with no manual domain engineering addresses a major scalability bottleneck in TAMP, where hand-crafted PDDL domains are labor-intensive and hard to maintain. Open questions remain around the occasional omission of complex ordering predicates (e.g., all_base_finished in bridge building), perception robustness, and handling of deformable objects or fluids. The paper's future-work directions—integrating raw perception, enabling direct environment interaction for domain repair, and addressing partial observability—point toward a fully autonomous planning pipeline that could significantly reduce the human-in-the-loop requirement in long-horizon robotic tasks.
