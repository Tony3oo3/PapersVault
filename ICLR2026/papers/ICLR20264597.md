## Problem

Previous driving world models that generate synthetic data for perception are evaluated under an unfair protocol: they pretrain on synthetic data then finetune on real data, doubling training epochs relative to the real-data-only baseline, which masks whether synthetic data actually helps. Under matched epochs, the authors show that large amounts of synthetic data from prior methods (Panacea, SubjectDrive, etc.) offer little to no advantage over real data alone. Additionally, existing generation methods rely on sparse spatial controls (BEV maps, 3D bounding boxes) that limit geometric diversity and cannot produce high-quality long-tail corner cases at scale.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-view RGB driving video (6 cameras, 20-second nuScenes scenes), 3D bounding box annotations specifying where to place a target asset, a 3D mesh asset from the DriveObj3D library, and calibrated camera intrinsics/extrinsics. It decomposes the input into five dense 3D-aware guidance maps—depth (Depth Anything), normal (derived from depth), Canny edge (background), and rendered object image plus binary mask (foreground)—then produces edited multi-view photorealistic videos at 512×768 resolution with 33 frames, from which 6 key frames per video are extracted for downstream training. The 420-sample augmentation set is constructed from 7 scenes × 10 object categories × 6 key frames, representing less than 2% of the 28,130 real training samples.

## Method

Dream4Drive replaces sparse BEV/3D-box conditioning with dense 3D-aware guidance maps: the background is characterized by depth, normal, and edge maps (with foreground regions masked out), while the target 3D asset is rendered via calibrated camera projection to produce an object image and mask. A multi-condition fusion adapter encodes all five maps through a shared VAE, applies per-condition 3D embedders to patchify the latents, concatenates the features via a FusionNet module, and injects them into the control blocks of a DiT backbone (initialized from MagicDriveDiT) with a spatial view attention mechanism for cross-view coherence. Training uses rectified flow with classifier-free guidance and a composite loss (diffusion + foreground mask MSE + LPIPS), requiring no expensive 3D annotations beyond the RGB video and off-the-shelf guidance maps. For asset generation, DriveObj3D is built via a three-step pipeline: Grounded-SAM segmentation of a target object, Qwen-Image multi-view synthesis to handle occlusions, and Hunyuan3D multi-view mesh reconstruction, producing complete high-fidelity 3D assets across ten driving categories.

## Results

On nuScenes detection at 256×512 resolution, Dream4Drive with only 420 synthetic samples (<2% of the 28,130 real samples) achieves mAP 38.7 and NDS 50.6 at 2× epochs, outperforming all prior synthetic-data baselines (Panacea: 37.1/49.2; SubjectDrive: 38.1/50.2) and the real-data-only baseline (38.4/50.4). At 1× epochs, Dream4Drive already surpasses the real-data baseline (mAP 36.1 vs. 34.5; NDS 47.8 vs. 46.9). For tracking at 2× epochs, Dream4Drive achieves AMOTA 34.4 and AMOTP 133.5, beating both Panacea and SubjectDrive (33.7/135.3) and the real-data baseline (34.1/134.1). At higher resolution (512×768), the gains are amplified: Dream4Drive reaches mAP 44.5 and NDS 55.0 at 3× epochs, a 4.6-point mAP improvement over the real-data baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dream4Drive outperforms all five prior synthetic-data baselines on detection at 2× epochs: it beats SubjectDrive (the strongest prior method) by 0.6 mAP and 0.4 NDS, Panacea by 1.6 mAP and 1.4 NDS, MagicDrive by 3.3 mAP and 10.8 NDS, DriveDreamer by 2.9 mAP and 11.1 NDS, and WoVoGen by 2.5 mAP and 32.5 NDS. Critically, Dream4Drive achieves these gains using only 420 synthetic samples (<2% of real data), whereas prior methods used their full synthetic datasets. On tracking at 2× epochs, Dream4Drive (AMOTA 34.4, AMOTP 133.5) surpasses both Panacea and SubjectDrive (33.7/135.3) and the real-data-only baseline (34.1/134.1). The paper's key methodological contribution is demonstrating that under fair epoch-matched evaluation, most prior synthetic-data methods (DriveDreamer, WoVoGen, MagicDrive) actually underperform the real-data-only baseline, while Dream4Drive is the first to show consistent gains at every epoch setting (1×, 2×, 3×). At higher resolution (512×768), Dream4Drive further extends its lead, reaching mAP 44.5 at 3× epochs versus 43.1 for real data alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dream4Drive builds directly on the MagicDriveDiT backbone (a DiT-based driving world model originally conditioned on BEV maps and 3D bounding boxes) and extends its ControlNet branch with dense 3D-aware guidance maps (depth, normal, edge, cutout, mask) instead of the sparse spatial controls used by prior methods like MagicDrive, Panacea, and SubjectDrive. It departs from editing-based approaches (DriveEditor, GenMM) that rely on reference images and 3D boxes for single-view insertion, and from reconstruction-based methods (NeRF, 3DGS) that suffer from sparse-view artifacts, by performing instance-level multi-view video inpainting guided by rendered 3D assets. The paper also positions itself as a methodological correction: it argues that prior synthetic-data-for-perception evaluations were confounded by unequal training epochs, and introduces a fair matched-epoch protocol as the new standard.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most impactful contribution is conceptual: it demonstrates that the apparent benefits of prior driving-world-model synthetic data for downstream perception largely vanish under fair epoch-matched evaluation, challenging a widely assumed advantage in the field. On the positive side, it shows that a small amount (<2%) of high-quality, 3D-aware synthetic data can genuinely improve detection and tracking, and contributes the DriveObj3D asset dataset to support future work. Open questions include automatic collision-aware trajectory planning for inserted assets, whether the findings generalize beyond nuScenes and StreamPETR, and why scaling OOD scenes beyond ~7 actually degrades performance rather than improving it.
