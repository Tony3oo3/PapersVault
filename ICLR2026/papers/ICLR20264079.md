## Problem

Conventional RL for LLM reasoning produces Markovian optimal policies that depend on history only through the current state, giving no incentive to revisit a state with enriched context or to switch strategies mid-reasoning. As a result, it remains unclear whether the self-reflection behaviors observed in RL-trained LLMs are guaranteed to emerge, why they would be beneficial at deployment, or under what conditions they should occur. The paper asks whether a principled objective can both induce and explain reflective exploration as a rational response to MDP uncertainty.

## Input / Output

The method takes a math problem prompt (max 512 tokens) and its ground-truth answer as input, and produces a chain-of-thought reasoning trace (max 1024 tokens) ending in a final answer. During training, the policy generates |M|=5 CoT rollouts per prompt, from which candidate answers are extracted to form MDP hypotheses; each reasoning step is a fixed block of 128 tokens. The step-level reward is a progress signal computed as the change in the model's probability of the ground-truth answer before and after appending the current step, avoiding expensive branched Monte-Carlo rollouts.

## Method

BARL recasts the RL objective as a Bayesian RL objective that maximizes expected return under a posterior distribution over MDPs induced by the training data, yielding uncertainty-adaptive policies whose actions depend on both the current state and the evolving belief over MDPs. The policy gradient replaces the standard Q-value with a posterior-weighted value: for each candidate MDP hypothesis (derived from a sampled CoT rollout), the Q-value is weighted by the model's state-conditional plausibility of that hypothesis and by an exponential penalty on the cumulative mismatch between predicted and observed rewards. This mechanism naturally implements hypothesis elimination—when observed rewards contradict a high-belief hypothesis, it is downweighted, signaling the policy to switch strategies and revisit earlier states with updated context. Theoretically, the paper proves that the Bayesian RL objective admits strictly uncertainty-adaptive optimal policies and that the performance gap between such policies and any Markovian policy can be arbitrarily large, providing a formal justification for why reflective exploration is beneficial.

## Results

BARL achieves the highest average pass@1 accuracy across all three model families: 53.3% on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, 59.4% on Qwen2.5-Math-7B, and 52.7% on R1-Distill-Llama-8B, outperforming both the GRPO and progress-reward baselines. The largest gains appear on the most challenging benchmarks (OlympiadBench, AIME, AMC), where BARL leads by 1–5 points over the strongest baseline. In terms of token efficiency, BARL requires up to 1.63× fewer average tokens than the progress baseline, 2× fewer than GRPO, and over 10× fewer than the untrained base model while maintaining higher pass@k accuracies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BARL is compared against two RL baselines: GRPO (the outcome-reward algorithm from DeepSeek-R1, Guo et al. 2025) and a progress-reward variant adapted from MRT (Qu et al. 2025). Against GRPO, BARL wins on average accuracy for all three models (53.3 vs 51.0 on Qwen-1.5B, 59.4 vs 57.1 on Qwen-7B, 52.7 vs 52.1 on Llama-8B), with the largest margin on Qwen-7B (+2.3 pts) driven by strong gains on AIME (+4.5) and AMC (+1.7). Against the progress baseline, BARL similarly leads on average (53.3 vs 51.4, 59.4 vs 57.9, 52.7 vs 51.0), with the most notable advantage on Qwen-7B where BARL achieves 29.0% on AIME versus 24.5% for both baselines. On the Llama-8B model the margins are tighter (0.6 pts over GRPO, 1.7 pts over Progress), and BARL slightly trails GRPO on GSM8K (85.4 vs 85.7) but compensates with stronger performance on OlympiadBench and AMC. Overall, BARL's advantage is most pronounced on harder benchmarks requiring effective exploration, and it achieves these gains with substantially fewer tokens (up to 2× fewer than GRPO), indicating more efficient reasoning rather than simply longer chains of thought.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper grounds reflective exploration in LLM reasoning within the Bayes-Adaptive MDP framework (Duff, 2002), adapting the reward-likelihood posterior update from Ghosh et al. (2022) to the LLM CoT setting and incorporating progress rewards (Setlur et al., 2024) as step-level signals. It departs from concurrent meta-RL analyses of deliberative reasoning (Xiang et al., 2025; Qu et al., 2025) by anchoring exploration in environment rewards rather than internal CoT states, and from Bayesian exploration work on LLMs (Arumugam & Griffiths, 2025; Dwaracherla et al., 2024) by optimizing the Bayesian objective directly during policy fine-tuning rather than through external algorithmic scaffolding. The key conceptual departure from conventional RL (GRPO, REINFORCE) is the formal argument that Markovian optimal policies cannot exhibit reflective exploration, motivating the shift to uncertainty-adaptive policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled theoretical account of when and why self-reflection should emerge in LLM reasoning, showing that conventional RL offers no guarantee of such behaviors at deployment while Bayesian RL provably induces them. The finding that reflection frequency is weakly correlated with performance—base models reflect more yet perform worse—challenges the prevailing narrative that longer, more reflective CoTs are inherently better, redirecting attention toward exploration efficiency. Open questions include the scalability of maintaining hypothesis sets for complex multi-step reasoning, the sensitivity of BARL to the candidate set quality (|M|), and whether the Bayesian framework extends naturally to multi-turn or tool-use settings where the MDP structure is less well-defined.
