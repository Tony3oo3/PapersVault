## Problem

Conventional flow matching models treat the generation path as an undifferentiated interpolation between noise and data, lacking a principled mechanism to organize generation along the frequency/scale structure that natural data inherently possesses. This limits fine-grained controllability over which semantic or detail-level information is generated at each step, and prevents explicit frequency-domain editing or restoration. The paper asks whether reinterpreting the flow-matching time variable as a scaling parameter over frequency bands (K-amplitude) can yield steerable, scale-aware generation.

## Input / Output

The method operates on 256×256 RGB images encoded into a Stable Diffusion VAE latent (8× downsampling), which is then decomposed via a K-amplitude transform (Fourier, Daubechies wavelet, or PCA) into frequency bands indexed by a discrete scaling parameter k. During generation, the model starts from Gaussian noise in the K-amplitude space and progressively reveals components from low-k to high-k; for conditional generation, class labels are injected only during low-k stages, and for restoration, a degraded image provides the high-k conditioning. Output is a full-resolution image reconstructed via the inverse K-amplitude transform and VAE decoder.

## Method

K-Flow reinterprets the flow-matching time variable as a scaling parameter k that organizes projected coefficients (frequency bands) in a K-amplitude space defined by an invertible linear transform (Fourier, Wavelet, or PCA). A stochastic interpolant is constructed so that at each k, the flow incorporates K-amplitude components up to k and pads the remainder with noise; a bump function (e.g., μ(t)=1−3t²+2t³) provides differentiable interpolation between discrete k values, yielding a localized conditional vector field confined to a narrow frequency band around k. This localization reduces per-step optimization dimensionality compared to pixel-space flow. The backbone is a lightweight MoE variant of DiT L/2 where the time-embedding is replaced by a K-amplitude embedding and feedforward MLPs are replaced by frequency-aware MoE modules that route tokens to specialized experts based on their k-band. Training uses the standard conditional flow matching loss applied to the K-amplitude-localized vector field, with component-wise noise normalization across frequency bands for stable optimization.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, K-Flow (Fourier) with classifier-free guidance (cfg=1.5) achieves FID 2.73 and CDR 1.49, outperforming the same-scale LFM baseline (FID 2.85, CDR 3.25) and surpassing VAR-d16 (FID 3.30) and FlowAR-B (FID 3.17). On CelebA-HQ 256×256 unconditional generation, K-Flow (Wavelet) achieves the best FID of 4.99, beating LFM (5.28) and LDM (5.11), while K-Flow (PCA) attains the highest Recall of 0.48. The CDR metric confirms that K-Flow maintains near-unity conditional discrimination even when class labels are dropped during the last 70% of scaling steps, whereas LFM's CDR degrades to 3.25.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the same-scale LFM (DiT L/2) baseline, K-Flow (Fourier) with cfg=1.5 achieves a lower FID (2.73 vs. 2.85) and a substantially lower CDR (1.49 vs. 3.25), indicating more robust class-conditional generation that is less sensitive to condition omission. K-Flow also outperforms the multi-scale autoregressive models VAR-d16 (FID 3.30) and FlowAR-B (FID 3.17) at comparable model scale, though the much larger FlowAR-L (FID 1.90) remains an upper-bound reference using a different VAE. On unconditional CelebA-HQ, K-Flow (Wavelet) achieves the best FID (4.99) among all compared methods, edging out LDM (5.11) and LFM (5.28), while K-Flow (PCA) matches LFM on Recall (0.48). The key differentiator is the CDR metric: K-Flow's near-unity CDR demonstrates that high-frequency details are preserved even when class conditions are dropped in the latter 70% of scaling steps, a property that conventional LFM lacks (CDR 3.25). Overall, K-Flow is competitive or superior to same-scale baselines on FID and clearly superior on the controllability metric (CDR), while trailing only the significantly larger FlowAR-L on raw FID.

## Relation to Prior Work

K-Flow extends the flow matching framework (Lipman et al., 2022) and stochastic interpolants (Albergo et al., 2023) by reinterpreting the artificial time variable as a scaling parameter k in a K-amplitude space, where the flow is localized to a narrow frequency band at each step. This departs from WaveDiff (Phung et al., 2023), which operates in wavelet space but retains standard diffusion noising, and from multi-scale autoregressive models like VAR (Tian et al., 2024) and FlowAR (Ren et al., 2024), which require separate per-scale models or specific architectures. The key conceptual departure is the unified K-amplitude framework that subsumes Fourier, Wavelet, and PCA decompositions under a single scaling parameter, combined with a localized vector field (Eq. 11) that restricts each network evaluation to a low-dimensional sub-manifold of the frequency space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit frequency-domain organization of the generative path enables practical steerability that pixel-space flow matching cannot offer: class conditions can be dropped in later (high-k) stages without quality loss, and fixing high-frequency noise yields unsupervised frequency editing. The natural correspondence between frequency bands and semantic content (facial details in high-k, global attributes in low-k) opens a path toward interpretable and controllable generation. However, the method is validated only on image tasks at 256×256 resolution, the energy-based interpretation remains underexplored, and the quantitative FID gains over strong baselines like LFM are modest, leaving open whether the framework's advantages persist at higher resolutions or in text-to-image settings.
