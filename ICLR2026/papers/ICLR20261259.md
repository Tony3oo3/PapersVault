## Problem

Current multimodal large language models perform all reasoning in text format, even when the task is fundamentally visual (spatial navigation, geometric planning). This introduces a modality gap where grounding visual information into verbal descriptions loses critical spatial structure, and no prior work has explored whether models can plan purely through sequences of images without any textual mediation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261259/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of a grid-based environment (e.g., a frozen lake, maze, or room layout with an agent and goal) as the sole input, with no textual prompt or description. It autoregressively generates a sequence of intermediate RGB images, each representing the next visual state of the environment, forming a complete planning trajectory. Each image is encoded as 256 discrete VQGAN tokens by the LVM-7B backbone, and the entire pipeline operates without any text tokens at input or output.

## Method

The core contribution is VPRL, a two-stage reinforcement learning framework applied to an autoregressive Large Vision Model (LVM-7B) that was pretrained exclusively on images and video with no textual data. Stage 1 initializes the policy by fine-tuning on random-walk trajectories (not optimal ones) to preserve high exploration entropy and valid output format; Stage 2 then applies GRPO with a composite progress reward that scores each generated next-state image as optimal (+1), non-optimal but valid (0), or invalid (−5) using a rule-based dynamics interpreter and a BFS-derived progress estimator. The key design insight is that random-trajectory initialization maintains the entropy needed for effective RL exploration, whereas standard supervised fine-tuning on optimal trajectories causes entropy collapse and prevents meaningful policy updates. This avoids the modality gap entirely by keeping both the state representation and the planning process in the visual domain.

## Results

VPRL (LVM-7B) achieves 80.6% average Exact Match and 84.9% average Progress Rate across FROZENLAKE, MAZE, and MINIBEHAVIOR, substantially outperforming all text-based baselines. On FROZENLAKE, VPRL reaches 91.6% EM / 93.2% PR; on MAZE, 74.5% EM / 77.6% PR; and on MINIBEHAVIOR, 75.8% EM / 83.8% PR. The paper reports a 27% higher average EM over the best text-based SFT baseline (Qwen 2.5-VL-Instruct-7B SFT at 53.6% avg EM). VPRL also demonstrates stronger robustness to increasing grid size, maintaining 82.4% EM on 6×6 FROZENLAKE grids versus Gemini 2.5 Pro's 38.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VPRL outperforms all three baseline families across every task and metric. Against Gemini 2.0 Flash (Kampf & Brichtova, 2025), VPRL's 80.6% avg EM exceeds the CoT variant's 12.8% by over 67 points, with the gap most dramatic on MINIBEHAVIOR (75.8% vs. 4.0% EM). Against Gemini 2.5 Pro (Gemini, 2025), VPRL wins on all three tasks, with the largest margin on MAZE (74.5% vs. 21.5% EM) and MINIBEHAVIOR (75.8% vs. 37.6% EM), while Gemini 2.5 Pro retains a slight PR edge on FROZENLAKE (85.0% vs. 93.2% — actually VPRL wins here too). Against Qwen 2.5-VL-Instruct-7B (Bai et al., 2025) with SFT, VPRL achieves 80.6% vs. 53.6% avg EM (+27 points) and 84.9% vs. 69.9% avg PR; notably, applying GRPO to the text-based Qwen planner (Table 2) actually degrades performance below SFT (54.4% EM), confirming that RL does not rescue the text modality gap. The visual planning paradigm (VPFT alone) already matches or exceeds text SFT on FROZENLAKE (75.4% vs. 68.6% EM), but it is VPRL's Stage 2 reinforcement learning that delivers the decisive gains, particularly on the more complex MAZE and MINIBEHAVIOR tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant MLLM reasoning paradigm, where visual inputs are grounded into text and all reasoning proceeds verbally (as in Visual Sketchpad, MVoT, or o3's tool-based visual rationales), by removing text entirely from the planning loop. Methodologically, it adapts GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) from the language-reasoning setting to autoregressive image generation, using a Large Vision Model (LVM-7B) trained exclusively on visual data to eliminate language confounders. It also distinguishes itself from action-conditional world models (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018; Hafner et al., 2019), which predict next observations but require an external planner, by internalizing multi-step planning directly within the visual generative flow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first evidence that purely visual, text-free planning is not only viable but substantially outperforms text-mediated reasoning for spatial tasks (27% EM gain), and that the modality gap in grounding visual information into text is a genuine bottleneck that RL cannot fix in the text modality. However, the scope is narrow: grid-based navigation with a 7B model, a rule-based dynamics interpreter, and OOD performance on larger grids remains weak (e.g., 20.4% EM on 7×7 FROZENLAKE). Key open questions include whether the paradigm extends to more complex visual domains (continuous spaces, 3D, physical dynamics), whether native multimodal generators can adopt it, and how to replace the hand-crafted reward with learned or trajectory-level signals.
