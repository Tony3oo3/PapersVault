## Problem

The relationship between GNN expressivity and generalization remains poorly understood: more expressive models can outperform less expressive ones or vice versa, and it is unclear when increased expressivity helps versus hurts. This paper asks under what conditions more expressivity improves or degrades generalization, and whether improved performance is attributable to expressivity per se or to alignment with task-relevant structural features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263576/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes graphs with bounded node features (from TUDataset benchmarks and synthetic distributions) and produces both class predictions and data-dependent generalization bounds. The key intermediate quantity is the ζ-TMD distance between training and test graphs, which quantifies structural similarity at the expressivity level of the chosen color refinement algorithm. The resulting bound decomposes into a model-complexity term (spectral norms, hidden dimensions, max node degree) and a structural-similarity term (ξ_ζ), making the bound task-dependent through the choice of pseudometric.

## Method

The paper introduces ζ-Tree Mover Distances (ζ-TMDs), a family of pseudometrics that generalize the Tree Mover's Distance to any strongly simulatable color refinement algorithm (e.g., k-WL, F-WL with motif counts), where each ζ-TMD captures a specific level of structural expressivity. Under the assumption that graph labels are strongly correlated with a fixed ζ-TMD (i.e., label probabilities are Lipschitz-continuous with respect to that pseudometric), the authors derive PAC-Bayes generalization bounds for end-to-end trainable GNNs. The bound decomposes into a capacity term depending on model width, weight norms, and maximum node degree, and a structural similarity term measuring the ζ-TMD distance between training and test graphs. A key technical ingredient is proving that ζ-MPNNs are Lipschitz-continuous with respect to their corresponding ζ-TMD, enabling the transfer of structural distances into representation-space bounds. Theorem 5.1 then formalizes the expressivity–generalization trade-off: increasing expressivity to match the task-relevant structure preserves the bound, while exceeding it inflates the structural discrepancy term and degrades generalization.

## Results

On the synthetic cycle-counting task (Task 1, Erdős-Rényi graphs), F₄-MPNN achieves the best test accuracy of 97.93% ± 0.68%, substantially outperforming both less expressive (MPNN: 84.90%) and more expressive models (LF-G: 84.50%, L-G: 85.43%, Sub-G: 86.23%). Even F₇-MPNN (96.60%), which captures additional cycle structures beyond what the labels depend on, is slightly worse than F₄-MPNN. On real-world datasets (BZR, MUTAGENICITY, NCI109), test accuracy declines as the ζ-TMD distance to the training set increases, and the paper's theoretical bound closely tracks the observed generalization gap, being orders of magnitude tighter than standard PAC-Bayes bounds (≈10⁴ vs. ≈10¹⁶).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central empirical finding is that F₄-MPNN (Barcelo et al., 2021), which augments MPNNs with cycle counts up to length 4, achieves 97.93% test accuracy, beating the plain MPNN baseline (Xu et al., 2019) by 13 percentage points and the most expressive LF-GNN (Zhang et al., 2024) by 13.4 percentage points. The less expressive F₃-MPNN (86.57%) and Subgraph GNN (Frasca et al., 2022; 86.23%) also underperform F₄-MPNN, confirming that the task-relevant structure (3- and 4-cycles) requires exactly the F₄ level of expressivity. The Local 2-GNN (Morris et al., 2020b; 85.43%) and LF-GNN (84.50%)—both more expressive than F₄-MPNN in the WL hierarchy—actually perform worse than the plain MPNN, demonstrating that excessive expressivity leads to overfitting. On real-world TUDataset benchmarks, the paper's ζ-TMD-based generalization bound tracks the empirical test error closely, whereas standard PAC-Bayes bounds (Liao et al., 2021) are orders of magnitude looser, validating the structural-similarity term as the key driver of generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the Tree Mover's Distance (Chuang & Jegelka, 2022) into a family of ζ-TMDs parameterized by color-refinement algorithms, leveraging the strong simulation framework of Jogl et al. (2024) to define pseudometrics for arbitrary strongly-simulatable CRAs. The generalization bound derivation follows the PAC-Bayes approach of Neyshabur et al. (2018) extended to the structure-label correlated setting of Ma et al. (2021), combined with Lipschitz stability arguments for GNNs in the spirit of Davidson & Dym (2025). The key departure from prior GNN generalization work is the explicit modeling of structure-label alignment via task-specific pseudometrics, rather than relying on distribution-free complexity measures (VC-dimension, Rademacher) or restrictive graphon assumptions that cannot capture how expressivity interacts with the particular structural features driving labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled theoretical answer to the long-standing question of when more expressive GNNs help versus hurt, showing that optimal expressivity is precisely matched to the structural features correlated with labels. This has direct practical implications for model selection: practitioners can avoid the computational overhead of higher-order GNNs (k-GNNs, subgraph GNNs) when a simpler architecture already captures the task-relevant structure. Key limitations include the fact that the relevant pseudometric is often unknown and expensive to compute, the bounds are not tight and serve as qualitative guidelines rather than precise estimates, and direct quantitative comparisons between different ζ-TMDs remain challenging. Future work on augmentation techniques to improve train-test structural similarity and on automatically identifying task-relevant pseudometrics would significantly increase practical applicability.
