## Problem

State-of-the-art diffusion language models (Dream-7B, LLaDA-8B) match the quality of similarly sized autoregressive models but suffer from substantially higher inference latency because each denoising step requires a full-sequence forward pass over all L tokens, and parallel unmasking introduces token incoherence that degrades quality when denoising steps are reduced. No training-free acceleration method existed that could make large-scale DLMs competitive with AR models in wall-clock latency without additional fine-tuning or architectural modification.

## Input / Output

The system takes a text prompt and a fully masked token sequence (all positions set to [MASK]) as input, and produces a fully unmasked text sequence containing multi-step reasoning followed by a final answer, constrained to at most 1024 new tokens. Unlike standard AR decoding, the DLM processes the entire sequence length at every denoising step rather than appending one token at a time. The pipeline additionally requires a frozen lightweight AR guider model (e.g., Qwen2.5-1.5B or 7B) whose sole role is to provide an agreement signal for unmasking decisions, not to generate or correct tokens.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary training-free techniques. FreeCache exploits the observation that KV projections of finalized (unmasked) tokens exhibit high temporal stability across subsequent denoising steps: the generation sequence is partitioned into fixed-size blocks (256 tokens), and once a block is fully unmasked its KV projections are frozen, so the active recomputation window shrinks progressively, reducing per-step cost from O(L) toward O(block_size). Guided Diffusion addresses token incoherence by using a lightweight AR model as a coherence prior: at each denoising step the DLM drafts all masked tokens in one forward pass, the AR guider processes these drafts in a single forward pass, and the longest agreed prefix between the two models is unmasked (falling back to one token if no agreement is found). Crucially, unlike speculative decoding, the AR model never corrects or regenerates tokens—it only provides an acceptance signal—so the DLM's full reasoning capacity is preserved and no iterative correction loop is needed. Together, the two methods yield an average 12.14× end-to-end speedup on Dream-7B-Instruct and 13.29× on LLaDA-8B-Instruct with negligible accuracy degradation, making DLM latency comparable to or faster than same-sized AR baselines.

## Results

The combined FreeCache + Guided Diffusion method achieves an average of 12.14× end-to-end speedup on Dream-7B-Instruct and 13.29× on LLaDA-8B-Instruct across reasoning benchmarks, with negligible accuracy degradation (typically <2%). On GSM8K (8-shot), accuracy actually improves from 79.68% (vanilla DLM) to 80.33% (FreeCache + Qwen-1.5B guider) while reducing latency from 48.05s to 2.70s (17.80× speedup). On PiQA, the method achieves a 34.1× speedup (14.62s → 0.43s) with accuracy maintained at 85.15%. For the first time, diffusion language models match or exceed the inference latency of same-sized autoregressive models (e.g., 2.55s vs. 2.26s for Qwen2.5-1.5B on GSM8K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the vanilla Dream-7B-Instruct diffusion baseline, the proposed method wins decisively on latency (12.14× average speedup) while matching or exceeding accuracy on most benchmarks: GSM8K accuracy improves from 79.68% to 80.33–81.41%, GPQA jumps from 39.29% to 49.55%, and MMLU-PRO is maintained at 46.64–48.20%. Against LLaDA-8B-Instruct, the method achieves 13.29× speedup on GSM8K (57.02s → 4.29s) with accuracy preserved at 79.91%. In the critical comparison against same-sized autoregressive models (Table 4), the DLM with guided diffusion achieves higher accuracy than the AR guider itself (e.g., 80.3% vs. 68.54% for Qwen2.5-1.5B on GSM8K) while maintaining comparable latency (2.55s vs. 2.26s), demonstrating that the DLM's reasoning capacity governs output quality regardless of guider competence. The method also outperforms prior DLM acceleration approaches (dKV-Cache, Fast-dLLM) by requiring no heuristic scheduling, threshold tuning, or additional fine-tuning, operating entirely off-the-shelf.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two well-established LLM acceleration paradigms—KV caching (from FlashAttention/PagedAttention) and speculative decoding (Leviathan et al.)—to the emerging diffusion language model (DLM) setting. It departs from Block Diffusion (Arriola et al.), which demonstrated KV caching for diffusion but required fine-tuning, by showing that temporal stability of KV projections for finalized tokens enables a training-free 'FreeCache' strategy. Guided Diffusion inverts the conventional speculative decoding hierarchy: rather than a small AR drafter proposing tokens for a large target model to verify, a large DLM drafts all tokens in parallel while a small frozen AR model provides only an agreement signal for safe unmasking, eliminating the costly correction loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that DLMs can match or beat AR LLMs in end-to-end inference latency (12×+ speedup) without any retraining, which is critical for the practical adoption of diffusion-based sequence generation. However, the evaluation is constrained to only two billion-parameter DLMs (Dream-7B, LLaDA-8B), and the DLM landscape remains extremely small, leaving open whether FreeCache's KV-stability assumption and Guided Diffusion's agreement mechanism generalize to future, larger, or architecturally different DLMs. FreeCache also introduces a small but non-zero accuracy cost, and Guided Diffusion adds deployment complexity (an extra AR model in memory), so the net practical benefit depends on the DLM ecosystem maturing.
