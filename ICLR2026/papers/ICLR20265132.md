## Problem

No existing method can reconstruct renderable 4D HDR scenes from unposed monocular LDR videos captured with alternating exposures. Standard 4D reconstruction methods assume consistent brightness and rely on photometric reprojection for camera pose recovery, which breaks down under varying exposures, while existing HDR novel-view synthesis methods require multi-view inputs or known camera poses. The combination of unknown camera motion, noisy 2D priors, and the absence of direct HDR supervision makes this a fundamentally harder problem than either 4D reconstruction or HDR NVS alone.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single unposed monocular video of 50–100 frames at 864×480 resolution, where frames alternate between 2 or 3 exposure levels (e.g., short/long exposure), along with the corresponding exposure times read from camera metadata. It produces a fully renderable 4D HDR Gaussian scene representation (with per-Gaussian HDR irradiance, motion trajectories, and a learned tone-mapper MLP) plus refined camera poses, enabling real-time novel-view synthesis of both HDR and LDR videos at arbitrary exposure levels. As a byproduct, the dynamic Gaussian trajectories induce accurate 2D/3D tracking of moving objects.

## Method

Mono4DGS-HDR uses a two-stage Gaussian optimization. In stage 1, fully dynamic video HDR Gaussians are learned in an orthographic camera coordinate space (following SaV), where camera motion and object motion are unified as Gaussian trajectories parameterized by cubic Hermite splines (position) and cubic polynomials (rotation quaternions), eliminating the need for camera poses and enabling stable HDR video fitting. A video-to-world transformation then converts these Gaussians: dynamic/static separation uses occlusion-aware epipolar-error masks, positions/rotations are mapped via bundle-adjustment camera parameters, and scaling is re-fitted by enforcing 2D projected covariance invariance. In stage 2, world Gaussians and camera poses are jointly refined using an HDR photometric reprojection loss computed on the recovered HDR training video from stage 1. A novel temporal luminance regularization (TLR) loss warps consecutive HDR renderings with rendered optical flow and penalizes per-pixel irradiance differences (normalized by their sum) to enforce temporally consistent HDR appearance, particularly for dynamic regions where LDR supervision is weak due to over/under-exposure.

## Results

On the synthetic Syn-Exp-3 benchmark, Mono4DGS-HDR achieves 34.75 dB PSNR / 0.904 SSIM / 0.086 LPIPS on LDR observed-exposure frames, 37.64 dB PSNR / 0.959 SSIM / 0.042 LPIPS on HDR frames, and 0.057 HDR-TAE for temporal stability, while rendering at 161 FPS. On real-world Real-Exp-3 test frames, it reaches 27.65 dB PSNR / 0.876 SSIM / 0.081 LPIPS (LDR-OE) and 0.067 HDR-TAE. On Real-Exp-2 training frames, it attains 31.82 dB PSNR / 0.928 SSIM / 0.052 LPIPS with 0.046 HDR-TAE. Training takes 1.5 hours on a single RTX 3090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mono4DGS-HDR outperforms all five baselines across every metric on both synthetic and real-world scenes. The closest competitor is MoSca-HDR (extended to HDR mode), which trails by ~0.7 dB PSNR on LDR-OE (34.75 vs. 34.08) and ~0.75 dB on HDR PSNR (37.64 vs. 36.89) on Syn-Exp-3, while Mono4DGS-HDR renders at 161 FPS versus MoSca's 82 FPS. SplineGS-HDR performs the worst (17.59 dB LDR-OE PSNR) because it relies heavily on photometric reprojection loss for camera pose recovery, which is infeasible with alternating-exposure frames. GaussHDR and HDR-HexPlane, designed for static and multi-camera settings respectively, achieve 29.51 and 29.26 dB LDR-OE PSNR, falling ~5 dB short due to their inability to handle dynamic scenes and monocular inputs. GFlow (evaluated on training frames in Table 5) fails to produce meaningful HDR reconstructions (18.70 dB HDR PSNR on Syn-Exp-3) because it optimizes per-frame Gaussians independently, overfitting to LDR observations without temporal coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: HDR novel view synthesis (HDR-NeRF, HDR-GS, GaussHDR, HDR-HexPlane), dynamic 4D reconstruction from unposed monocular video (MoSca, SplineGS, GFlow, SaV), and HDR video reconstruction from alternating exposures. It departs from prior HDR NVS work by removing the multi-camera and known-pose requirements, and from prior 4D monocular reconstruction by handling the fundamentally different supervision signal of alternating-exposure LDR frames. The two-stage optimization (video Gaussians in orthographic space → world Gaussians with joint camera refinement) is the key architectural departure, borrowing the video Gaussian representation from SaV and the spline-based dynamics from SplineGS/MoSca but reorganizing them to circumvent the infeasibility of photometric reprojection loss under varying exposures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first system to address 4D HDR reconstruction from unposed monocular alternating-exposure video, a practically important setting for handheld cameras in the wild. The new benchmark (25 scenes across synthetic and real data) fills a gap in evaluation for this task. Key limitations include inability to handle non-rigid deformation (due to ARAP constraint), motion blur, and low-light RAW-domain scenarios, all of which are acknowledged as future directions. An open question is whether the two-stage video-to-world strategy generalizes to more complex exposure patterns or longer sequences, and whether the reliance on vision foundation model priors (DepthCrafter, SpatialTracker, RAFT) limits robustness in challenging real-world conditions.
