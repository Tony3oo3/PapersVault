## Problem

As LLM benchmark suites grow, repeated evaluation across checkpoints and competing systems becomes a major token and compute bottleneck. Existing compression methods reduce test-set size but do not explicitly model the two complementary forms of instance-level redundancy—semantic overlap in prompt text and near-identical cross-model ranking behavior—that jointly determine which instances are necessary to keep a leaderboard's collective ordering stable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263728/cropped_page1_idx7.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a benchmark of N text instances together with a binary score matrix S ∈ {0,1}^{N_model × N} harvested from public leaderboards (e.g., Open LLM Leaderboard), where each entry records whether a model answered an instance correctly. It produces a k-element binary mask selecting a compressed subset, from which full-benchmark per-model accuracies are reconstructed via a surrogate predictor; the subset is optimized so that both the absolute scores (RMSE) and the induced model ranking (Kendall/Pearson correlation) remain faithful to the full benchmark. A strict 9:1 stratified train-test split over model-performance strata governs all experiments.

## Method

EssenceBench is a three-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline. Stage 1 applies a redundancy-aware filter that removes instances whose text-embedding similarity (Definition 3) or Pearson-correlation of model-performance vectors (Definition 4) exceeds thresholds, shrinking the search space cheaply. Stage 2 runs a genetic algorithm over binary k-masks, scoring each candidate with a fixed Generalized Additive Model (GAM) surrogate that maps subset accuracy to full-benchmark accuracy, using tournament selection, uniform crossover, Bernoulli mutation, and a budget-repair step. Stage 3 trains an Explainable Boosting Machine (EBM) on the elite GA subsets to extract per-instance attribution scores, partitions the remaining pool into high-, low-, and random-attribution groups, and re-applies GA within each group to recover underrepresented patterns and avoid local optima; Stages 2–3 are iterated until convergence. The key design choice is that the surrogate is trained once per GA round and held fixed, making fitness evaluation O(1) per candidate and decoupling the evolutionary search from expensive re-evaluation.

## Results

On GSM8K (1K instances), EssenceBench achieves an RMSE of 0.377 at k=500, reducing reconstruction error by 60.7% relative to MetaBench (0.958). At k=200, EssenceBench (RMSE 0.864) already outperforms MetaBench at k=500, demonstrating a 2.5× data-efficiency gain. On HellaSwag (10K instances), EssenceBench preserves 95% of model rankings within a 5% shift using only 50 instances, achieving a 200× compression. On more challenging benchmarks, EssenceBench reaches near-perfect reconstruction on MathVista (RMSE 0.001 at k=300) and GSM-Plus (RMSE 0.010 at k=300 vs. MetaBench's 0.195).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EssenceBench consistently outperforms MetaBench (Kipnis et al., 2025) across all 13 benchmarks and subset sizes. On GSM8K at k=500, EssenceBench achieves RMSE 0.377 versus MetaBench's 0.958 (a 60.7% reduction), and at k=200 it reaches 0.864, already beating MetaBench's k=500 result. On ranking fidelity (GSM8K, k=200), EssenceBench attains Kendall correlation 0.968 versus MetaBench's 0.937, and 100% rank stability within 10% shift versus MetaBench's 98.4%. Against SMART (Gupta et al., 2025) on ARC-Challenge, EssenceBench achieves lower RMSE (1.104 vs. 1.193) while using only 8.53% of the dataset versus SMART's 39.25%. Classic selection baselines GraNd (Paul et al., 2021) and Perplexity (Bengio et al., 2003) are substantially outperformed: on GSM8K at k=50, EssenceBench (2.769) beats GraNd (3.852) and PPL (4.194) by 28% and 34% respectively, with the gap widening at smaller budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the benchmark compression line of work (MetaBench, TinyBenchmark, SMART) by reframing the objective from pure score reconstruction to a coupled problem that simultaneously preserves the collective model ranking. It departs from prior methods by introducing two complementary redundancy signals—text-level (embedding similarity) and ranking-level (Pearson correlation of per-model performance vectors)—and by repurposing genetic algorithm search with a fixed GAM surrogate and EBM-based attribution from the data-selection-for-training literature into the evaluation-compression setting. Unlike training-data selection methods (GraNd, PPL, IRT-based scoring), which optimize for downstream learning efficiency, EssenceBench explicitly targets leaderboard-level ordering stability as a first-class objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As benchmark suites grow and evaluation is repeated across checkpoints, ablations, and competing systems, the token and compute cost of evaluation becomes a practical bottleneck; a 200× compression that preserves 95% of model rankings would substantially reduce this overhead. A key limitation is the prerequisite of per-instance correctness scores from many models (leaderboard data), which restricts applicability to settings where such data is already available and raises open questions about cold-start scenarios for new benchmarks. Whether the dual-redundancy framework and GA-based search generalize to non-binary scoring (e.g., generation quality, multi-turn dialogue) or to settings with only a handful of evaluated models remains unexplored.
