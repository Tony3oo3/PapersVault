## Problem

Current synthetic-data-based LLM distillation treats knowledge transfer as a one-shot generation-and-training task, ignoring the systematic structure of learning. Specifically, existing methods fail to (1) identify which specific knowledge modules the student model lacks, (2) organize knowledge delivery in a prerequisite-respecting, difficulty-graded sequence, and (3) adapt knowledge representations to the student's cognitive capacity, leading to suboptimal absorption and poor generalization on complex reasoning tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a black-box teacher LLM (e.g., OpenAI o1, DeepSeek-R1), a student SLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-3B, LLaMA3.2-3B), and a compact seed dataset of ~3K items spanning instruction following, math, code, and academic reasoning, partitioned 8:2 into training contexts and validation probe tasks. The output is a fine-tuned student model plus a pedagogically structured synthetic dataset generated iteratively per curriculum stage, where each stage's data is only produced after the student achieves a mastery threshold (≥90% of teacher performance) on the current stage's knowledge modules.

## Method

IOA is a three-stage pipeline that operationalizes Bloom's Mastery Learning and Vygotsky's Zone of Proximal Development into concrete distillation criteria. The Identifier decomposes the target domain into a hierarchical knowledge graph, measures per-module performance gaps Δ(k) via probe tasks, constructs a prerequisite dependency DAG via conditional performance analysis, and selects the top-m most severe deficient modules. The Organizer sequences these modules into a topological curriculum with ZPD-bounded difficulty increments (τ_ZPD=0.15) between consecutive stages and enforces Bloom-style mastery gates (τ_mastery=0.9) that trigger remedial data synthesis until the student reaches criterion-level competence before advancing. The Adapter transforms each stage's synthetic data through five cognitive-alignment mechanisms—abstract-to-concrete concretization, multi-step reasoning decomposition with verification, cognitive-load management via controlled instance complexity, template-based solution formatting, and linguistic simplification with explicit discourse markers—ensuring the generated supervision matches the student's expressive capacity rather than merely mirroring the teacher's output verbatim.

## Results

With OpenAI o1 as teacher, IOA achieves 38.16 ROUGE-L on DollyEval and 40.64 Pass@1 on HumanEval for Qwen2.5-3B, and 36.88 / 37.98 for LLaMA3.2-3B, consistently outperforming all 8 data-synthesis baselines across 9 benchmarks. On mathematical reasoning (MATH), IOA reaches 15.53 (Qwen2.5-3B) and 14.02 (LLaMA3.2-3B), a 19.2% relative improvement over the strongest baseline MADA (13.15 / 11.76). With DeepSeek-R1 as teacher, gains amplify further: MATH reaches 16.02 and HumanEval 42.08 for Qwen2.5-3B. The student models retain 94.7% of teacher performance on DollyEval while using less than 1/10th of the parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IOA achieves the best score on every benchmark for both student models, beating all 13 baselines with statistical significance (p < 0.01). The strongest data-synthesis baseline is MADA (multi-perspective distillation), which IOA outperforms by ~1.7 points on DollyEval, ~2.4 on MATH, and a striking ~7.3 points on HumanEval (40.64 vs. 33.39 for Qwen2.5-3B). Among CoT-based methods, CasCoD and DSS trail IOA by 2.5–4.2 points on GSM8K and 9+ points on HumanEval. Adversarial (Lion) and counterfactual (CounterDistill) synthesis methods are consistently weaker, with Lion lagging IOA by ~3.4 on MATH and ~11.7 on HumanEval. Against white-box and on-policy distillation methods (ABKD, DistiLLM-2, GKD, SuperCorrect, POCL) in Table 4, IOA still leads by 2–6 points on reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MATH 19.64 vs. GKD's 15.75; AIME2024 10.89 vs. SuperCorrect's 7.97), confirming that pedagogical data synthesis surpasses even logit-matching and RL-based distillation when the teacher is black-box.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the black-box synthetic-data distillation line (Alpaca, DeepSeek-R1-Distill, Lion, DSS, CasCoD, Star-Agents, MADA) by reframing the data synthesis pipeline as an explicit pedagogical process grounded in Bloom's Mastery Learning and Vygotsky's Zone of Proximal Development. Prior methods treat synthetic data generation as a one-shot or iterative quality/diversity problem; IOA instead introduces a three-stage Identifier–Organizer–Adapter pipeline that diagnoses fine-grained knowledge deficiencies, sequences them via a dependency-aware curriculum with mastery gates, and adapts representation complexity to the student's cognitive capacity. The Orca series (Mukherjee et al., 2023; Mitra et al., 2023) and curriculum-based distillation (POCL, Panigrahi et al., 2025) are the closest conceptual precursors, but IOA is the first to operationalize both mastery gating and ZPD-bounded difficulty increments simultaneously within a black-box setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IOA offers a principled, interpretable framework for black-box distillation that practitioners can apply when teacher model internals are inaccessible (e.g., OpenAI o1), and its competitive wall-clock time (~11 h) despite the multi-stage pipeline makes it practically viable. The ablation confirms each stage contributes uniquely—particularly the Adapter for coding generalization—suggesting that representation-level adaptation is an underexploited lever in distillation. Open questions include whether the hierarchical knowledge decomposition generalizes beyond math and code to open-ended domains, whether the dependency graph can be learned automatically rather than queried from the teacher, and whether the pedagogical framing yields gains beyond what a well-tuned curriculum + targeted data pipeline would achieve without the explicit educational theory.
