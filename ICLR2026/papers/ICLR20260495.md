## Problem

Existing spatio-temporal video grounding (STVG) methods localize only a single target from a textual query, ignoring both additional queried objects and their interacting counterparts. This restricts practical applicability in real-world scenarios where events involve multiple objects, and no dedicated benchmark existed for evaluating multi-object video grounding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260495/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed video (frames sampled at FPS=2, shorter side resized to 320 px) and a free-form textual query (tokenized to at most 30 words) as input. It outputs a spatio-temporal bounding box tube for every object mentioned in the query (1–10 objects per video), where each tube is a sequence of per-frame boxes linked across time via Hungarian matching, along with a single pair of start and end timestamps delimiting the relevant temporal segment.

## Method

OmniTube is a one-stage Transformer encoder-decoder that extends TubeDETR-style STVG to multi-object grounding. A multimodal encoder fuses ResNet-101 appearance features, VidSwin motion features, and RoBERTa text features via a 6-layer self-attention encoder. The spatial decoder generates N_q object queries per frame through text-guided selection (top-M appearance features most similar to the averaged text embedding), refines them with alternating spatial and temporal attention blocks, and predicts boxes plus word-position-index class labels via cross-attention with appearance and text features. A parallel temporal decoder uses text-guided motion-feature queries and temporal attention to predict start/end timestamps. Tubes are assembled by Hungarian-matching boxes across frames and filtering out tubelets whose predicted class is absent from the query.

## Results

On the full BOSTVG test set (1,912 videos), OmniTube achieves 35.83% m_tIoU and 9.47% m_vIoU, outperforming the strongest baseline CG-STVG (32.09% m_tIoU, 7.66% m_vIoU) by 3.74 and 1.81 points respectively. The advantage is most pronounced on vIoU@0.3 (6.17% vs. 3.56%) and vIoU@0.5 (0.89% vs. 0.26%), reflecting better spatial localization of multiple objects. On the classic single-object HCSTVG-v2 benchmark, OmniTube scores 58.5% m_tIoU and 38.4% m_vIoU, slightly trailing CG-STVG (60.0%, 39.5%) but surpassing STCAT and TubeDETR. Against MLLMs, OmniTube outperforms Gemini-2.5 Pro on m_vIoU (9.5% vs. 7.2%) and Qwen2.5-VL-SFT by a wide margin (9.5% vs. 5.2% m_vIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniTube consistently outperforms all three adapted STVG baselines (TubeDETR, STCAT, CG-STVG) across every BOSTVG split and metric, with the largest gains on spatial metrics (vIoU@0.3: 6.17% vs. 3.56% for CG-STVG on Full). On the classic single-object HCSTVG-v2 benchmark, OmniTube (58.5% m_tIoU) slightly trails CG-STVG (60.0%) and STVGFormer (58.1% m_tIoU, 38.7% m_vIoU), confirming it is not a drop-in replacement for single-object SOTA but remains competitive. Against MLLMs, OmniTube surpasses Gemini-2.5 Pro on m_vIoU (9.5% vs. 7.2%) and vIoU@0.3 (6.2% vs. 5.1%) while matching its m_tIoU (35.8% vs. 35.5%), and dominates Qwen2.5-VL (zero-shot: 15.2% m_tIoU) and Qwen2.5-VL-SFT (29.1% m_tIoU) by large margins. The paper also shows that repeatedly running CG-STVG per object (558.1s) is both slower and less accurate (32.5% m_tIoU) than a single OmniTube pass (134.1s, 37.9% m_tIoU) on a 283-video subset, validating the end-to-end multi-object design. In zero-shot evaluation on 50 held-out classes, OmniTube (30.98% m_tIoU, 7.58% m_vIoU) leads both STCAT (28.71%, 6.92%) and CG-STVG (29.84%, 7.24%), demonstrating better generalization to unseen categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniTube extends the one-stage Transformer-based STVG paradigm established by TubeDETR (Yang et al., 2022) and DETR (Carion et al., 2020) from single-target to all-target grounding, introducing multiple object queries per frame and a text-guided query generation mechanism. It adapts MDETR's (Kamath et al., 2021) word-position-index classification to assign bounding boxes to their corresponding textual referents, replacing high-cardinality class prediction. Conceptually, the paper draws an analogy to Segment Anything (Kirillov et al., 2023) but in the spatio-temporal video grounding domain, and differentiates from the concurrent DVD-ST (Ji et al., 2024) by grounding all queried objects across different categories rather than only same-class partial targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BOSTVG is the first dedicated benchmark for omni-object video grounding, filling a gap where prior STVG datasets (VidSTG, HCSTVG) only support single-target localization, which is insufficient for real-world queries referencing multiple interacting objects. The finding that adding HCSTVG-v2 (single-object, human-only) data degrades performance underscores that diverse multi-object supervision is essential and cannot be substituted by volume of single-target data. Open questions include whether the shared temporal segmentation assumption (all objects share one event interval) is too restrictive, how methods scale to the underrepresented high-density (≥7 objects) subset, and whether the modest absolute performance (~36% m_tIoU, ~9.5% m_vIoU) reflects task difficulty or method limitations.
