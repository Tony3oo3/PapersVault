## Problem

High-performance Spiking Transformers rely on spike-matrix multiplication (Q×K^T) that is incompatible with asynchronous neuromorphic hardware, while Spiking CNNs lack sufficient accuracy. Additionally, SNN training incurs substantial GPU overhead because zero-valued spikes still consume computation and the inherent time-step dynamics multiply the cost. No existing architecture simultaneously achieves fully event-driven operations, low training overhead, and competitive performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263550/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Inputs are converted to spike-form tokens via Spiking Patch Splitting: RGB images (ImageNet at 224×224, CIFAR at 32×32), event-based DVS frames (downsampled to 48×48 for CIFAR10-DVS, 128×128 for DVS128 Gesture), and multivariate time series (207–321 variables). Outputs are class labels (1000-way ImageNet, 10/100-way CIFAR), predicted future values over horizons of 6–96 steps, and COCO bounding boxes at 640×640 resolution. The DSTSP pruning operates directly on the binary spike tokens (S^{T×N×C}), reducing both the token count N and time steps T before the Mixer computation.

## Method

The architecture replaces Spiking Self-Attention's spike-matrix multiplication with a single learnable weight matrix W_M that multiplies the spike input directly (SMixer(X) = SN(W_M·X)), making the operation fully event-driven and hardware-compatible. To reduce training overhead, the paper introduces Dynamic Spatial-Temporal Spiking Pruning (DSTSP), a parameter-free framework that computes a Spike Intensity Value (SIV) — the sum of spike events per token or per time step — to identify redundant features. Spatial pruning (DSSP) sorts tokens by SIV, retains the top N′ = N(1−P_S) tokens, and correspondingly slices W_M to N′×N′; temporal pruning (DSTP) sorts time steps by SIV and retains only T′ = T(1−P_T) steps, reducing neuron state updates. Both operations use only addition and sorting, introducing no trainable parameters and no gradient back-propagation for pruned features, yielding ~22% FLOP reduction at a 0.3 spatial / 0.5 temporal pruning ratio with <1% accuracy loss.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, SMixer+DSTSP (spatial pruning ratio 0.3, temporal pruning to 1 step) achieves 76.87% top-1 accuracy on STMixer-8-768, actually surpassing the unpruned baseline (76.68%) while reducing GPU memory to 76.4% and energy to 53.0% of the original. When replacing Spiking Self-Attention in SpikformerV2-8-512 (T=4), the SMixer variant reaches 80.45% (vs. 80.38% original), and DSTSP further reduces energy from 9.36 mJ to 3.44 mJ with only a 1.3% accuracy drop. On CIFAR-100, SMixer+DSTSP achieves 81.03% (vs. 81.87% unpruned), and on COCO detection, SMixer+DSTSP reaches 57.4 mAP@50 (vs. 58.9% baseline) with 42% lower energy consumption.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMixer matches or slightly exceeds Spiking Self-Attention in all three SOTA Spiking Transformer frameworks: SpikformerV2-8-512 (T=4) goes from 80.38% to 80.45%, QKFormer HST-10-512 from 78.71% to 78.69%, and SDT-V3 Efficient-transformer-L from 79.80% to 79.25%, confirming SMixer as a viable event-driven replacement. After applying DSTSP, the accuracy drops are modest: 1.3% for SpikformerV2, 1.3% for QKFormer, and 2.1% for SDT-V3, while energy consumption is halved in all cases (e.g., SpikformerV2 from 9.36 to 3.44 mJ). On CIFAR-100, SMixer+DSTSP (81.03%) slightly trails Spikformer (78.21%) and CML (80.02%) but outperforms Spikingformer (78.21%) and S-Transformer (78.40%), with the gap to the best baseline being under 1%. On DVS128 Gesture, SMixer (98.61%) matches CML (98.60%) and exceeds Spikformer (98.30%), while SMixer+DSTSP (98.26%) remains competitive. For COCO detection, SMixer (58.9 mAP@50) outperforms E-SpikeFormer (58.4–58.8) and EMS-Res-SNN (50.1), and SMixer+DSTSP (57.4) still surpasses all ANN-to-SNN conversion methods (25.7–46.4). In time-series forecasting, SMixer consistently outperforms Spikformer on R² across all four benchmarks (e.g., 0.734 vs. 0.697 on Metr-La at horizon 6), and DSTSP introduces only marginal degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Spiking Token Mixer (Deng et al., NeurIPS 2024), which replaces the spike-matrix multiplication in Spiking Self-Attention with a single learnable weight matrix, making the architecture compatible with asynchronous neuromorphic hardware. It departs from prior SNN pruning work (NDSNN, Grad R, RCMO-SNN, STSFP for Spikformer) by targeting structured spiking-feature pruning—removing entire tokens and time steps—rather than unstructured synaptic weight pruning, and by doing so without introducing any trainable parameters. The paper also extends the SpikeYOLO detection framework by substituting convolutional stages with SMixer blocks, a hybrid design not explored in prior SNN detection work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core practical bottleneck in SNN deployment: Spiking Transformers achieve SOTA accuracy but rely on spike-matrix multiplication that degrades on asynchronous hardware, while truly event-driven architectures have historically underperformed. Demonstrating that SMixer matches Spiking Transformer accuracy across ImageNet, CIFAR, and neuromorphic datasets while remaining fully event-driven offers a viable path to high-performance neuromorphic inference. However, the pruning is still executed on GPU (not on-chip), the COCO detection results show a non-trivial 1.5 mAP drop, and the sorting-based SIV computation, while lightweight, introduces a sequential bottleneck that may limit scalability to very large token counts. Open questions include whether the pruning can be made natively event-driven on hardware and whether the approach generalizes to video or multi-modal SNN settings.
