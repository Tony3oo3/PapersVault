## Problem

Training Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) requires simultaneously driving multiple loss terms (PDE residuals, initial/boundary conditions, auxiliary physics terms) to zero, but existing remedies address only one of two failure modes: reweighting strategies can produce update directions that conflict with individual loss gradients, while non-conflict methods can produce infeasible directions that cannot be expressed as a non-negative weighted sum of the losses. No prior method simultaneously guarantees both feasible scaling factors and non-conflict update directions across three or more loss terms.

## Input / Output

At each iteration the method consumes the per-loss gradient matrix G ∈ R^{d×m} (where d ≫ m is the parameter dimension and m is the number of losses), the current network parameters θ, and a step size η. It produces a single update direction A_h(G) that is certified to lie in the interior of the harmonized cone, followed by a standard parameter update θ ← θ − η·A_h(G). The key computational step is a Double Description conversion of a 2m×m inequality system into its extreme-ray representation, which is practical in wall-clock time even for m up to 8.

## Method

HARMONIC defines the harmonized cone H as the intersection of the primal gradient cone K = {Gλ | λ ≥ 0} (the feasible region where updates correspond to non-negative loss weights) and the dual gradient cone K* = {y | Gᵀy ≥ 0} (the non-conflict region where no loss increases). The algorithm constructs the H-representation of H from the constraints λ ≥ 0 and GᵀGλ ≥ 0, then applies the Double Description method to extract the V-representation (extreme rays R ∈ R^{d×p}). The update direction is formed by averaging and normalizing these extreme rays, then scaling by the sum of inner products with all loss gradients to ensure a meaningful step magnitude. Theoretically, the paper proves O(1/√T) convergence to Pareto-stationary points under nonconvex objectives and establishes that the harmonized cone is always nontrivial (contains a nonzero vector), guaranteeing the method is always well-defined.

## Results

On the PINNacle benchmark, HARMONIC achieves the lowest average relative L² error on 3 out of 5 equations: Wave1d-C (0.0655), Poisson2d-C (0.0214), and HInv (0.0461). The most dramatic gain is on Poisson2d-C, where HARMONIC (0.0214) outperforms the next-best method Aligned-MTL (0.2847) by over 13×. On HNd (0.0005) and Volterra1d (0.0003), HARMONIC is within 0.0001 of the best baseline (ReLoBRaLo at 0.0004 and 0.0002 respectively). Computational cost is comparable to ConFIG (~1.0–5.4 s per 100 epochs), with no iterative sub-procedure overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HARMONIC outperforms all nine baselines on Poisson2d-C by a wide margin (0.0214 vs. 0.2847 for Aligned-MTL, the runner-up), confirming that jointly enforcing feasibility and non-conflict is critical for this geometry. On Wave1d-C, HARMONIC (0.0655) narrowly edges out ConFIG (0.0668), the closest competitor, while CAGrad (0.1186) and PCGrad (0.2256) trail by 2–3×. On HInv, HARMONIC (0.0461) beats ConFIG (0.0466) and IMTL-G (0.0498), with LRA (0.0546) and PCGrad (0.0660) further behind. On HNd and Volterra1d, ReLoBRaLo achieves marginally lower errors (0.0004 vs. 0.0005 and 0.0002 vs. 0.0003), but HARMONIC remains within 0.0001 and avoids the large variance seen in IMTL-G (0.0735 on Volterra1d). Reweighting-only methods (MultiAdam, LRA, ReLoBRaLo) and MOO methods that do not guarantee feasibility (PCGrad, CAGrad, IMTL-G) consistently underperform, while ConFIG—the only other method guaranteeing non-conflict—remains the strongest single competitor but still fails to match HARMONIC on three of five benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research streams for multi-loss PINN training: adaptive reweighting (LRA, ReLoBRaLo, NTK-based methods) that adjusts loss magnitudes but ignores gradient conflicts, and multi-objective optimization (MGDA, CAGrad, ConFIG, DCGD) that enforces non-conflict but may produce infeasible directions. The key conceptual departure is the 'harmonized cone'—the intersection of the primal gradient cone (feasible conic combinations) and the dual gradient cone (non-conflict directions)—which provides a single geometric region satisfying both constraints simultaneously. The algorithmic machinery draws on the classical Double Description method from convex geometry (Motzkin et al., 1953) to compute extreme rays of this intersection, extending DCGD's dual-cone analysis (which was limited to two losses) to arbitrary m ≥ 3.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled framework that simultaneously guarantees feasible, non-conflict, and non-degenerate updates for multi-loss PINN training, with convergence to Pareto-stationary points in nonconvex settings. The practical finding that simply constraining existing methods (ReLoBRaLo, ConFIG, CAGrad) to remain within the harmonized cone dramatically improves them (Table 3) suggests the framework can serve as a drop-in correction layer for any multi-loss optimizer. Open questions include incorporating user-specified objective preferences within the cone, extending to second-order methods, and understanding how the Double Description computation scales beyond ~8 loss terms. The approach is currently limited to first-order gradient information and assumes full-rank gradient matrices.
