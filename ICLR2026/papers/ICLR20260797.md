## Problem

Deep Ensembles remain the gold standard for uncertainty quantification in deep neural networks, but their cost scales linearly with the number of members—requiring full retraining, multiple checkpoints, and sequential forward passes—making them impractical for large transformer-based foundation models. Naive pruning of individual ensemble members reduces size but, as the paper proves theoretically and shows empirically, degrades calibration and OOD detection under noisy or corrupted inputs. The paper asks how to build transformer ensembles that preserve Deep-Ensemble-level diversity and uncertainty quality while keeping inference cost close to a single model.

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard RGB images (224×224 for ViT-B/16, 32×32 for CIFAR-100), tokenized text sequences (for BERT-base on SST-2), or image–text pairs with a fixed prompt template (for zero-shot OpenCLIP-ViT/B-32). Rather than running M separate forward passes and averaging, Hydra Ensembles processes the input through a single fused transformer in one pass, producing an ensemble-averaged class probability vector from which calibration metrics (ECE, NLL, Brier) and OOD scores (AUROC, FPR95, AUPR) are derived. The fused architecture reshapes M model-specific token sequences along the depth dimension so that the attention mechanism operates on the same token count as a standard transformer, keeping the output interface identical to a single model.

## Method

Hydra Ensembles constructs M diverse subnetworks from a single pre-trained transformer by pruning different subsets of attention heads per layer—either via Taylor importance scores (Strategy 1, no uncertainty validation set) or via the Headmap circuit-extraction algorithm (Strategy 2, with an uncertainty-focused validation set). Each pruned subnetwork is then fine-tuned independently on the target dataset (or used zero-shot for CLIP), after which the M members are merged into one model: the MLP sub-blocks are averaged across members, while the multi-head attention is replaced by a Fused MHA that uses Grouped Fully-Connected (GFC) layers to compute Q, K, V projections jointly for all M members, with each member attending only over its own surviving heads. A key architectural trick reshapes the M×T×d input into T×Md before attention and back to MT×d before the MLP, so the fused MHA processes the same number of tokens as a standard MHA and the merged MLP sees the same feature width, enabling plug-and-play use without architectural retraining. The paper further proves (Proposition 1) that under noisy test conditions, naive pruning amplifies the loss gap between clean and corrupted data, motivating the circuit-based head selection that preserves heads specialized for uncertainty estimation.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (ViT-B/16), Hydra Ensembles (Circ) achieves 80.88% top-1 accuracy with OOD AUROC of 86.29, FPR95 of 47.62, and AUPR of 63.15, surpassing Deep Ensembles on OOD metrics (AUROC +0.8%, AUPR +0.4%) while being only 1.07× slower than a single model in bfloat16. On CIFAR-100, Hydra Ensembles (Circ) improves OOD detection over Deep Ensembles by +3.4% AUROC, −2.2% FPR95, and +1.2% AUPR. In zero-shot classification across 9 datasets, Hydra Ensembles (Circ) achieves 74.00% accuracy (best) and surpasses the trained ViLU method by +1.3% AUROC, −3.5% FPR95, and +4.0% AUPR on ImageNet-1K OOD, all without any additional training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hydra Ensembles (Circ) outperforms all efficient ensemble baselines (Packed Ensembles, MIMO, Batch Ensembles, MC Dropout, LoRA Ensembles) on both accuracy and OOD metrics; for example, it improves AUROC by +2.0–2.7% and AUPR by +3.0–5.0% over Packed Ensembles on ImageNet-1K. Against Deep Ensembles, Hydra Ensembles trades 1.3% accuracy for superior OOD detection (AUROC 86.29 vs. 85.48, AUPR 63.15 vs. 62.76) at only 1.07× single-model inference cost versus ~3× for Deep Ensembles. On CIFAR-100, the OOD advantage over Deep Ensembles widens to +3.4% AUROC and −2.2% FPR95. Single-model pruning baselines (Taylor, OBA) consistently underperform Hydra Ensembles on OOD metrics, confirming that ensembling pruned heads is critical for reliable UQ. In the zero-shot setting, Hydra Ensembles (Circ) surpasses the trained ViLU method by +1.3% AUROC, −3.5% FPR95, and +4.0% AUPR without any fine-tuning, and also outperforms BayesVLM and Temperature Scaling on all calibration and OOD metrics. On SST-2 text classification, Hydra Ensembles (Circ) achieves AUROC 77.6 vs. 74.81 for Deep Ensembles (D), a +2.8% improvement, while matching accuracy (92.55% vs. 93%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: Deep Ensembles for uncertainty quantification (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017), efficient ensemble architectures using Grouped Fully-Connected layers (Laurent et al., 2023; Lafage et al., 2025a), and structured pruning of transformer attention heads (Molchanov et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2025). It departs from prior efficient-ensemble methods (Packed Ensembles, MIMO, BatchEnsembles) that share backbone parameters and thus limit member diversity, by instead generating diversity through head-level pruning of a single pre-trained model and then fusing the resulting subnetworks into one architecture. The theoretical contribution (Proposition 1) extends the classical OBD/OBS pruning analysis to noisy-data conditions, formalizing why naive pruning degrades calibration—a gap not previously addressed in the pruning-for-ensembles literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Hydra Ensembles offers a practical path to ensemble-level UQ at near-single-model inference cost (1.07× in BF16), which is significant for deploying calibrated models in safety-critical settings where Deep Ensembles' 3× overhead is prohibitive. The finding that specific attention heads are specialized for OOD detection (and that pruning them is particularly harmful) provides mechanistic interpretability insight that could inform future pruning and model-editing strategies. Limitations include the continued accuracy gap versus Deep Ensembles (−1.3% on ImageNet-1K), the requirement for fine-tuning in supervised settings, and the fact that the theoretical assumptions (Hessian positive-definiteness, gradient alignment) are only approximately verified empirically. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-transformer architectures, how it scales to much larger foundation models (e.g., 7B+ parameters), and whether the circuit-based head selection can be made fully automated without an uncertainty validation set.
