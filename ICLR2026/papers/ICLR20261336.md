## Problem

Masked diffusion models (MDMs) parameterize each backward denoising step as a product of independent categorical distributions across dimensions, which cannot capture inter-dimensional correlations. This independence assumption causes severe quality degradation when few denoising steps are used, since many dimensions must be unmasked simultaneously. Prior attempts to fix this (e.g., guided sampling with pre-trained autoregressive models) introduce additional inner-loop computation that undermines the speed advantage of MDMs.

## Input / Output

VADD takes an all-masked discrete sequence as input and produces a fully unmasked sequence through T backward steps. For text, sequences have length 128 (LM1B) or 1024 (OpenWebText) with GPT-2-small-scale models (~125M params); for images, 32×32 binarized MNIST (V=2) or CIFAR-10 (V=256) pixels; for 2D toy data, V=100 per dimension. At each backward step, a fresh latent vector z ~ N(0, I_d) is sampled and used to condition the unmasking of all currently-masked positions simultaneously, so the per-step sampling cost matches that of a standard MDM.

## Method

VADD replaces the explicit product-of-categoricals denoising distribution with a latent variable model: p_θ(x_s|x_t) = ∫ p_θ(x_s|x_t, z) p(z) dz, where the conditional p_θ(x_s|x_t, z) remains a product of categoricals but the marginal over z captures inter-dimensional correlations. Training jointly optimizes the denoising model and an auxiliary recognition model r_φ(z|x_0, x_t) (a diagonal Gaussian) by maximizing a Double ELBO (DELBO), which is a tractable lower bound on the standard MDM ELBO. KL annealing (linearly ramping λ from 0 to 1) prevents posterior collapse during training. Architecturally, the denoising model is a transformer with AdaLN layers that inject the latent embedding z into every block, while the recognition model uses a siamese design over (x_0, M_t) with mask-restricted AdaLN applied only to masked positions. The per-token inference complexity remains O(Vd_e + LNd_e + Ld_e²), identical to a standard MDM, since the recognition model is not used at sampling time.

## Results

On LM1B, VADD achieves a test perplexity of 20.53 after only 1M iterations, outperforming the strongest autoregressive baseline (Transformer-XL, 23.5) and all diffusion baselines (MDLM: 27.70, SEDD-Absorb: 32.79). On CIFAR-10, VADD achieves an FID of 170.3 at 10 sampling steps versus 334.3 for MDLM and 298.7 for DDPM, and 50.5 at 100 steps versus 76.5 for MDLM. On OpenWebText, VADD reduces generative perplexity from 330.39 (MDLM) to 194.08 at 16 steps, and from 104.58 to 78.27 at 1024 steps, demonstrating a consistent ~25–40% improvement in sample quality with few denoising steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VADD consistently outperforms the primary MDM baseline (MDLM, reproduced) across all tasks: LM1B perplexity 20.53 vs 27.70, OpenWebText perplexity 22.21 vs 23.07, CIFAR-10 FID at 10 steps 170.3 vs 334.3, and generative perplexity at 16 steps 194.08 vs 330.39. Against autoregressive baselines, VADD beats Transformer-XL (23.5) and OmniNet (21.5) on LM1B perplexity with only 1M iterations, surpassing even the 5M-iteration Transformer (20.86). On CIFAR-10 BPD, VADD (2.74) marginally beats MD4 (2.75) and substantially outperforms D3PM-L (4.40). For zero-shot generalization (Table 6), VADD achieves the best perplexity on 5 of 6 benchmarks (e.g., Lambada 47.30 vs MDLM† 49.67, PubMed 40.62 vs 42.43), slightly trailing GPT-2 on LM1B (69.71 vs 51.25) but matching or beating it on the others. Against DDPM on CIFAR-10 FID, VADD wins at every sampling step (e.g., 170.3 vs 298.7 at T=10), confirming that discrete diffusion with latent-variable modeling can match or exceed continuous diffusion in the few-step regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

VADD extends the masked diffusion model (MDM) framework (Sahoo et al., 2024; Shi et al., 2024; Ou et al., 2025) by replacing the dimension-independent denoising distribution with a latent variable model, where a shared latent z is integrated out to induce inter-dimensional correlations in the marginal transition. The variational autoencoding framework (Kingma & Welling, 2014) is repurposed to make the resulting intractable marginal tractable via a novel Double ELBO (DELBO) loss that nests the VAE ELBO inside the diffusion ELBO. The paper explicitly positions itself apart from distillation-based few-step methods (Di[M]O, Soft-Di[M]O, Learnable Sampler Distillation) by training the latent-variable denoising model from scratch without a pre-trained teacher, and from prior latent-variable text models (VSG, Gu et al. 2018) by embedding the latent structure within the diffusion backward transition rather than the autoregressive decoding step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that VADD nearly halves the FID at 10 sampling steps on CIFAR-10 (170 vs 334) and reduces generative perplexity by ~40% at 16 steps on OpenWebText, addressing the critical deployment bottleneck of MDMs where few-step quality degrades sharply. The sampling cost remains identical to standard MDMs (the recognition model is only used during training), making the ~1.5× training overhead a one-time cost. Open questions include whether more informed priors (conditional on x_t and t, or hierarchical) can resolve the prior-hole problem, whether the recognition model can be eliminated via score-based or contrastive losses to reduce training cost, and whether the approach transfers to other discrete noise schedules beyond the absorbing/masking process.
