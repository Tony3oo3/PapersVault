## Problem

When forensic detectors are deployed in the wild, inputs undergo various distortions (compression, rescaling, lossy transmission) that erode forensic traces, causing detectors to fail silently by returning over-confident binary predictions without any indication of how trustworthy the decision is. Existing approaches—robustness training, logit calibration, and no-reference image quality assessment—either cannot cover the combinatorial space of distortions, break under distribution shift, or lack a direct link to detector-specific performance, leaving downstream systems unable to abstain or route among detectors.

## Input / Output

At inference, DACOM consumes a single distorted RGB image (256×256 for the quality and distortion-type encoders; detector-specific resolution for the forensic encoder) together with intermediate features extracted from a frozen forensic detector. No pristine reference image or ground-truth label is required at test time. The output is a scalar confidence score in [0, 1] representing the estimated probability that the given detector will correctly classify the image under its present distortion, which can be attached to any legacy detector or ensemble.

## Method

DACOM is a two-stage pipeline. In Stage A, full-reference IQA (e.g., DISTS, SSIM) serves as an oracle to build type-wise adaptive severity bins; the detector's balanced accuracy within each bin is mapped to a detectability label in [0, 1] via y = 2·max(BAcc − 0.5, 0). In Stage B, a reference-free regressor is trained with weighted MSE to predict these labels from three fused feature streams: (i) a forensic trace encoder extracting intermediate features from the frozen detector, (ii) a no-reference image quality encoder (QualiCLIP) capturing distortion-sensitive descriptors, and (iii) a distortion-type encoder (ARNIQA) identifying the distortion category. Each stream is linearly projected to 256 dimensions, concatenated into a 768-d vector, and passed through an MLP head. The key insight is that FR-IQA scores correlate almost monotonically with detection accuracy across distortion types, but since references are unavailable at test time, the model distills this oracle relationship into a deployable, reference-free confidence estimator that supports selective abstention and multi-detector Top-1 routing.

## Results

On the Seen Distortion multi-detector routing task, DACOM_DISTS achieves 95.37% average accuracy and 99.47% average AP, surpassing the best single detector (C2P* at 92.63% Acc) and the logit-calibration baseline (89.58% Acc) by 5.79% and 8.98% respectively. On Unseen Distortion routing, DACOM_DISTS reaches 92.84% average Acc and 98.33% AP, outperforming logit calibration by 2.53% Acc and 7.38% AP. In confidence-based filtering on the Cross-dataset, DACOM_DISTS achieves 77.32% BAcc and 19.87% EER at 40% filtering, compared to 73.71% BAcc and 27.50% EER for logit calibration. The model achieves PLCC of 97.73% and SRCC of 94.01% in predicting per-detector accuracy from its confidence score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DACOM consistently outperforms the logit-calibration baseline (Guo et al., 2017) across all downstream tasks: in multi-detector routing it gains 5.82% average Acc and 8.98% AP on seen distortions, and 2.62% Acc and 8.45% AP on unseen distortions. For confidence-based filtering, DACOM surpasses all NR-IQA baselines (TOPIQ, ARNIQA, QualiCLIP) and logit calibration at every filtering ratio on both the Evaluation-dataset and Cross-dataset; for example, at 40% filtering on the Cross-dataset, DACOM_DISTS reaches 77.32% BAcc versus 73.71% for logit calibration and 73.25% for QualiCLIP. The FR-IQA baselines (SSIM, MS-SSIM, FSIM, DISTS) achieve comparable or slightly better filtering performance (e.g., FR-DISTS reaches 75.73% BAcc at 40% on Cross-dataset), but they require pristine reference images that are unavailable at test time, making them impractical for deployment. Among the four DACOM variants, all four FR-IQA-guided versions perform within 0.1–0.3% of each other, with DACOM_FSIM and DACOM_DISTS marginally leading. The single best detector (C2P*) achieves 92.63% average Acc on seen distortions, but DACOM routing pushes this to 95.37–95.40% by adaptively selecting the most reliable detector per sample.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of forensic detection, image quality assessment, and confidence estimation. It departs from robustness-oriented AIGI detectors (CD, ND, FreqNet, SAFE, C2P) that aim to work under distortions but never quantify per-sample reliability, and from standard confidence calibration (Guo et al.) whose assumptions break under heterogeneous distortion shifts. The key conceptual repurposing is using full-reference IQA (FR-IQA) as a training-time oracle to generate detectability labels, then distilling that relationship into a reference-free model (DACOM) that fuses detector embeddings, NR-IQA descriptors, and distortion-type cues at inference. This bridges the gap between the theoretical notion of forensicability (Chu et al., Pasquini & Böhme) and practical, detector-conditioned reliability estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical mechanism—selective abstention and multi-detector routing—that can be attached to any existing forensic detector without retraining, making it immediately deployable in safety-critical pipelines (fact-checking, content moderation). The empirical finding that FR-IQA aligns monotonically with detection accuracy while NR-IQA does not is a useful diagnostic for the field. However, the authors acknowledge degraded cross-dataset performance, revealing sensitivity to source distribution shifts; making DACOM shift-resilient across diverse generative model families remains an open challenge. Broader implications include whether the FR-IQA-as-oracle paradigm generalizes beyond AIGI detection to other forensic tasks (manipulation localization, source attribution).
