## Problem

All existing results on transformer expressivity for counting properties (e.g., MAJ, PARITY) demonstrate only (semi-)linear counting properties, i.e., boolean combinations of linear inequalities over token counts. This paper asks whether transformers can express genuinely nonlinear counting properties, such as those involving products of token counts (e.g., #(nvidia)·#(intel)·#(deal)), and provides a formal framework showing that transformers can capture all semialgebraic counting properties — boolean combinations of arbitrary multivariate polynomials of any degree.

## Input / Output

The input is a word w over a finite alphabet Σ = {a₁,…,aₘ}, encoded as one-hot rational vectors in Q^m with an end marker a₀ appended (so the input length is |w|+1). The output is a binary accept/reject decision: the transformer accepts w iff the first component of the last position's output vector is strictly positive. The framework restricts attention to permutation-invariant (counting) properties, meaning acceptance depends solely on the Parikh image Ψ(w) ∈ N^m (the vector of letter counts), not on letter order.

## Method

The core construction uses NoPE-AHAT[U] (transformers with no positional encoding, average hard attention, and uniform layers only). The key mechanism implements polynomial multiplication via averaging: a ReLU layer zeros out all positions except those matching a target letter (using ReLU(y − (1−v)) where v ∈ {0,1}), then a uniform attention layer averages across all positions, yielding y·x_i/(n+1). Chaining d−1 such gadgets for a monomial of degree d produces x_{i₁}⋯x_{i_d}/(n+1)^d; since homogenization ensures all monomials share degree d, summing them recovers p(Ψ(w))/(n+1)^d. The paper proves NoPE-AHAT[U] precisely characterizes semialgebraic counting properties (Theorem 1.2), and via the MRDP theorem (Hilbert's 10th problem) shows that just two attention layers suffice for Turing-completeness among counting properties, yielding undecidability of emptiness for even this minimal architecture. Empirically, softmax transformers trained as binary classifiers on languages L_k = {w : |w|_b ≤ (|w|_a)^k} for k=1,…,5 achieve ≥0.94 accuracy on length-generalization test sets (lengths 501–1000), confirming learnability of nonlinear counting properties.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical contribution is Theorem 1.1: transformers (specifically NoPE-AHAT[U], a subclass of softmax transformers) capture all semialgebraic counting properties, i.e., boolean combinations of arbitrary multivariate polynomial inequalities over token counts. Theorem 1.2 provides a precise characterization: NoPE-AHAT and NoPE-AHAT[U] capture exactly the semialgebraic counting properties. Theorem 1.3 establishes Turing-completeness for counting properties (every recursively enumerable counting property is a projection of a 2-layer NoPE-AHAT[U] language), and Theorem 1.4 proves undecidability of the emptiness problem for 2-layer NoPE-AHAT[U] or SMAT. Experimentally, softmax transformers trained on polynomial counting languages L_k = {w : |w|_b ≤ (|w|_a)^k} achieve ≥0.99 accuracy on in-distribution test data and ≥0.94 accuracy on length-generalization data (lengths 501–1000) for all k from 1 to 5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is theoretical rather than empirical. The paper shows that C-RASP and LTL with Counting (the formalizations of 'efficiently learnable' transformer properties in prior work) can capture only semilinear counting properties — boolean combinations of linear inequalities and modulo arithmetic (Proposition 5.1). In contrast, the paper proves transformers capture the strictly larger class of semialgebraic counting properties (polynomials of any degree), and experimentally confirms that languages like L_k for k ≥ 2 (which are beyond C-RASP) are efficiently learnable by softmax transformers with ≥0.99 in-distribution accuracy. This demonstrates that C-RASP is only a partial characterization of what transformers can learn. Additionally, the paper establishes that PARITY is not expressible in NoPE-AHAT (Corollary 4.2), complementing prior inexpressibility results for other transformer subclasses. The experimental results for L_{i,j} = {a^m b^n c^{m^i n^j}} in Appendix D further confirm learnability of higher-degree polynomial properties, with accuracies ranging from 0.85 to 0.99 on generalization data depending on the polynomial degree.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on transformer counting expressivity (Hao et al. 2022; Pérez et al. 2021; Barceló et al. 2024) from semilinear counting properties to the full class of semialgebraic (polynomial) counting properties. The key technical tool is the average hard attention (AHA) mechanism, specifically its uniform variant, which the authors show can implement polynomial multiplication via averaging. The paper positions itself against C-RASP and LTL with Counting (Huang et al. 2025; Yang & Chiang 2024), which were proposed to characterize efficiently learnable transformer properties but only capture linear counting, and demonstrates that transformers strictly exceed this class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exact characterization of semialgebraic counting properties by NoPE-AHAT[U] (a model with no positional encodings, no masking, and uniform attention) is a clean and powerful result that sharpens our understanding of what transformers can and cannot express. The undecidability of emptiness for a 2-layer NoPE transformer with no masking is striking given how simple the model is, and the gap between C-RASP (linear) and what transformers empirically learn (polynomial) challenges the RASP-L conjecture's scope. Open questions include whether softmax transformers with causal masking can go beyond semialgebraic sets, and whether extending C-RASP with nonlinear polynomial inequalities remains capturable by softmax transformers.
