## Problem

Instrumental variable regression (IVaR) is a foundational tool for causal inference under endogeneity, but no prior work addresses performing IVaR under differential privacy. Classical two-stage least squares (2SLS) relies on moment equations that directly use sensitive covariates and instruments, creating significant privacy leakage risks. The central question is whether one can design differentially private algorithms for IVaR that simultaneously achieve statistically efficient, non-asymptotic convergence rates.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes three data matrices — instruments Z ∈ R^{n×q}, endogenous regressors X ∈ R^{n×p}, and outcomes Y ∈ R^n — along with per-stage privacy budgets ρ₁ and ρ₂ that separately govern the first-stage (Θ) and second-stage (β) iterates. It returns the full sequence of intermediate estimates {Θ^(t)}_{t=1}^T and {β^(t)}_{t=1}^T, with the final causal effect β^(T) as the primary output. A key design choice is that privacy can be allocated asymmetrically: setting ρ₁ = ∞ skips first-stage noise entirely when Θ is not sensitive, relaxing the iteration-count constraint on T.

## Method

DP-2S-GD alternates two coupled noisy gradient updates per iteration: a first-stage update on Θ (projecting instruments onto regressors) and a second-stage update on β (regressing outcomes on predicted regressors). At each step, per-sample gradients are clipped to thresholds γ₁, γ₂ calibrated to sub-Gaussian concentration bounds, and independent Gaussian noise with scales λ₁ = (2γ₁/n)√(T/ρ₁) and λ₂ = (2γ₂/n)√(T/ρ₂) is injected, yielding (ρ₁+ρ₂)-zCDP via additive composition over T iterations. The gradient-perturbation approach is chosen over sufficient-statistics or output perturbation because it composes tightly under zCDP, avoids spectrum-dependent blow-ups from noising Gram matrices, and supports modular stage-wise design. The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) establishes a three-term error bound — κ(τ)^{T/2} (optimization), √p(√q+√τ)³√T/(n√min{ρ₁,ρ₂}) (privacy noise), and √(pq)(τ+log(pq))/√n (statistical) — showing the optimal T is sub-linear but super-logarithmic in n.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is Theorem 3.1, which establishes that the estimation error ‖β^(T) − β̂‖ is bounded by three terms: an exponential convergence term κ(τ)^(T/2), a privacy-induced noise term scaling as √p(√q+√τ)³/(n√min{ρ₁,ρ₂})·√T, and a statistical error term √pq(τ+log(pq))/√n. On the Angrist dataset (n=8,065, T=20, ρ₁=ρ₂=1), the estimated causal effect β^(T) concentrates around −4.3 weeks per year, consistent with the non-private 2SLS benchmark, with convergence in expectation after approximately 15 iterations. Synthetic experiments confirm that the error decreases at rate 1/√n in the plateau regime and that the optimal number of iterations T is sub-linear but super-logarithmic in n, as predicted by the theory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is the first work to provide differential privacy guarantees for instrumental variable regression, so no prior DP-IVaR baselines exist for direct comparison. The paper validates its method against the non-private 2SLS closed-form estimator as a benchmark: on the Angrist dataset, DP-2S-GD with ρ₁=ρ₂=1 recovers the same point estimate (≈−4.3) as 2SLS, with the dispersion of estimates controlled by the privacy budget (larger ρ yields tighter concentration). The paper also compares different privacy allocation regimes (ρ₁=1,ρ₂=9 vs. ρ₁=5,ρ₂=5 vs. ρ₁=9,ρ₂=1) on synthetic data, showing that allocating more privacy budget to the first stage (smaller ρ₁) causes error to grow significantly once T exceeds the theoretical threshold, while allocating more to the second stage (smaller ρ₂) matches the predicted three-regime error curve. The authors note an inherent √p gap between the gradient-descent approximation to 2SLS and the closed-form 2SLS estimator itself, which persists even without privacy (Corollary 3.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the DP-SGD paradigm (gradient clipping plus calibrated Gaussian noise, Abadi et al. 2016) to the two-stage least squares structure of instrumental variable regression, using zCDP (Bun & Steinke 2016) for tight additive composition across 2T noisy iterations. It departs from prior private regression work—which has focused on OLS via output perturbation, sufficient-statistics perturbation, or exponential mechanisms—by exploiting the iterative two-stage structure to inject noise at the gradient level, avoiding the ill-conditioning issues of noising Gram matrices. The key technical departure is handling the cross-stage coupling where the second-stage gradient depends on the noisy first-stage iterate, a structure absent from standard DP-SGD analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide both end-to-end privacy guarantees and finite-sample convergence rates for linear IVaR, opening a path for causal inference in privacy-sensitive domains (healthcare, finance, recommendation systems). A notable limitation is the inherent √p gap between the gradient-descent approximation and the closed-form 2SLS estimator (Remark 3.8), which persists even without privacy and limits the method's statistical efficiency in high dimensions. Open questions include whether algorithmic modifications can close this √p gap, whether lower bounds exist for the privacy-accuracy tradeoff in IVaR, and how the approach extends to nonlinear or high-dimensional IV settings.
