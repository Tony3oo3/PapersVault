## Problem

Diffusion models rely on sampling guidance such as classifier-free guidance (CFG) to produce high-quality images, but this requires two forward passes at every denoising step, doubling inference cost. Existing guidance distillation methods transfer the guidance signal into the denoising network itself, which risks catastrophic forgetting and breaks compatibility with domain-specific fine-tuning or timestep distillation. The paper asks whether guidance can instead be distilled into the initial noise, a critical yet underexplored factor in the generation pipeline.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a standard Gaussian noise sample x_T and a text prompt, applies a single forward pass through the noise refining network (a lightweight LoRA attached to the pretrained diffusion model) to produce refined noise x̂_T, and then runs the standard unguided denoising pipeline from x̂_T. The output is a guidance-free generated image that matches or exceeds the quality of CFG-guided generation. The refining network is trained on prompts from MS-COCO and Pick-a-Pic without requiring paired image datasets, and the same LoRA can be directly transferred to fine-tuned or timestep-distilled variants without retraining.

## Method

NoiseRefine trains a noise refining network g_φ (implemented as a LoRA on the pretrained diffusion model with a residual connection) to map Gaussian noise into a structured 'refined noise' space. The key training insight is to optimize in image space rather than noise space: the loss minimizes the L2 distance between the unguided image generated from refined noise and the guided image generated from the original Gaussian noise, avoiding the accumulated errors of diffusion inversion. To make backpropagation tractable, the paper introduces multi-step score distillation (MSD), which detaches gradients through the denoising network at each of the N denoising steps, yielding faster convergence and avoiding gradient explosion from multi-step backpropagation. The paper proves (Proposition 1) that reducing image-space discrepancy bounds the corresponding noise-space discrepancy under Lipschitz continuity, and (Proposition 2) that MSD gradients approximate full-gradient objectives up to a constant factor. Empirically, the refined noise primarily encodes low-frequency structural layout information, enabling the diffusion model to form coherent object structures from the first denoising step without guidance.

## Results

On SiT-XL/2 (ImageNet), NoiseRefine achieves FID 10.80 and IS 50.59 without any guidance, surpassing guidance distillation (FID 12.12) and approaching guided sampling (FID 14.20). On SD2.1 (MS-COCO), refined noise yields FID 14.62 / IS 34.90, outperforming both guidance distillation (FID 19.09 / IS 33.45) and unguided Gaussian noise (FID 42.71). On SDXL, the method improves FID from 63.28 to 26.22 but still trails guided sampling (FID 21.20) and guidance distillation (FID 18.57). A user study shows participants prefer refined-noise samples over guided Gaussian-noise samples at a 53.96% vs 46.04% rate for image quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against guidance distillation (Meng et al., 2023), NoiseRefine achieves a lower FID on SiT-XL/2 (10.80 vs 12.12) and SD2.1 (14.62 vs 19.09), but underperforms on SDXL (FID 26.22 vs 18.57) and on IS for SiT-XL/2 (50.59 vs 58.90). Compared to classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022), the method narrows the gap substantially on SiT-XL/2 and SD2.1 but still lags on SDXL. Against InitNo (Guo et al., 2024), NoiseRefine outperforms on all five preference metrics in both guided and unguided settings (e.g., CLIPScore 30.31 vs 25.02 unguided; 32.51 vs 32.26 guided). Against PAHI (Kim et al., 2024), which predicts Gaussian parameters μ and Σ via a shallow MLP, NoiseRefine achieves FID 13.74 versus 217.30, a dramatic margin reflecting the inadequacy of parametric Gaussian assumptions for structured noise refinement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant guidance distillation paradigm (Meng et al., 2023; Hsiao et al., 2024), which trains a student network to replicate guided predictions, by instead distilling guidance into the initial noise via a lightweight LoRA-based refining network. It builds on the observation from diffusion inversion (Song et al., 2020a; Garibi et al., 2024) that inverting a guided image yields a noise that reproduces the image without guidance, but avoids the inversion-error problem by training with an image-space loss (MSD) rather than a noise-space reconstruction loss. The MSD loss itself is inspired by score distillation sampling (Poole et al., 2022), and the approach is conceptually related to prior noise optimization work (Guo et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2024; Eyring et al., 2024), but differs in learning a general, prompt-conditioned mapping rather than per-sample optimization or noise-space supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NoiseRefine offers a plug-and-play solution that eliminates the 2× inference cost of CFG without modifying the diffusion backbone, preserving compatibility with LoRA adapters, domain fine-tunes, and timestep-distilled models (e.g., SD-Turbo) — a practical advantage over guidance distillation which requires retraining per variant. The analysis showing that low-frequency components of refined noise encode a condition-dependent structural layout provides mechanistic insight into why guidance works and why unguided sampling fails. Limitations include weaker gains on SDXL (FID 26.22 vs. 21.20 guided) and the training-time cost of guided sampling for dataset generation. Open questions include why diffusion models inherently struggle to form low-frequency layouts without guidance, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation.
