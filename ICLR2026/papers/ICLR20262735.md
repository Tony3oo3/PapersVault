## Problem

Existing VLA models adopt a language-centric, late-fusion paradigm where visual observations are projected into a semantic space before action policies are derived, limiting deeply coupled cross-modal representations and preventing the model from learning temporal and causal dependencies across the perception-action loop. This static image-to-action mapping also prevents effective leverage of large-scale video data for training, leaving the rich dynamics of real-world interactions unexploited.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262735/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images (third-person at 200×200 and wrist-view at 80×80 for CALVIN; 200×200 for LIBERO; 256×256 for SimplerEnv) along with a text instruction, optionally augmented with historical frames. Images are discretized via a VQ tokenizer with 8× spatial compression, and actions are encoded as frame-to-frame differences normalized by 1st/99th percentiles before DCT-based FAST tokenization into a 1024-token vocabulary. At inference, the model generates only action tokens (stopping at the end-of-action token) without predicting future frames, conditioning solely on the current observed images.

## Method

UniVLA is an 8.5B-parameter encoder-free autoregressive Transformer (identical to Emu3) that unifies vision, language, and action as discrete tokens in a shared vocabulary, modeled via next-token prediction with cross-entropy loss. The sequence formulation interleaves observations and actions in a Markov-chain structure, with special boundary tokens (boi/eoi, boa/eoa) demarcating modality segments. Training follows a two-stage strategy: a post-training phase applies a world-model objective (predicting future visual tokens conditioned on instruction and current observation) over 622K robot videos with supervision on vision tokens only—requiring no action labels—and a fine-tuning phase interleaves action tokens into the sequence with supervision on action tokens only. The key insight is that world-model post-training captures causal temporal dynamics from video, substantially boosting downstream policy learning (e.g., LIBERO-Long from 17.4% to 89.2%) and improving both data efficiency (3.19 vs. 2.52 on CALVIN with 10% data) and training convergence (4.21 at 2k iterations vs. 0.37 without post-training).

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, UniVLA achieves a 95.5% average success rate across four task suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), substantially surpassing the previous best of 85.5% by π₀-FAST. On CALVIN, UniVLA attains the highest average completed task length of 4.63 (ABCD→D) and 4.41 (ABC→D), outperforming RoboVLMs (4.49/4.25) and UP-VLA (4.42/4.08). On SimplerEnv-Bridge, UniVLA raises the overall success rate from 42.7% (SpatialVLA) to 69.8%. In autonomous driving (NAVSIM), world-model post-training lifts PDMS from 81.7 to 85.6, exceeding TransFuser (84.0) and UniAD (83.4) using only front-camera input.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniVLA outperforms all 23 baselines across every benchmark. On LIBERO, it surpasses π₀-FAST (85.5%) by 10 points on average, with the largest gap on the Long suite (94.0% vs. 60.2%), and beats CoT-VLA (81.1%) and OpenVLA (76.5%) by wide margins. On CALVIN, UniVLA leads RoboVLMs by 0.14 (ABCD→D) and 0.16 (ABC→D) in average task length, and exceeds GR-1 by 0.42 and 1.35 respectively. On SimplerEnv, UniVLA (69.8%) nearly doubles the previous best of SpatialVLA (42.7%) and outperforms CoGACT (51.3%) by 18.5 points. On NAVSIM, UniVLA with world-model post-training (85.6 PDMS) beats TransFuser (84.0), UniAD (83.4), and VADv2 (83.0) while using only a single front camera versus multi-camera + LiDAR inputs. On real-world ALOHA, UniVLA with world-model post-training matches π₀ at 37.5% overall success, whereas without post-training it drops to 0%, highlighting the critical role of world-model training for real-robot transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniVLA builds directly on the Emu3 autoregressive transformer (8.5B parameters, all-modality-as-tokens design) and the FAST DCT-based action tokenizer, combining them into a single unified VLA architecture. It departs from the dominant language-centric VLA paradigm (RT-2, OpenVLA, π0) where vision is encoded by a separate ViT and actions are produced by a dedicated head, instead treating vision, language, and action as discrete tokens in one shared vocabulary. It also bridges the visual-guided action prediction line (SuSIE, UniPi, GR series) by integrating future-frame prediction natively into the same autoregressive sequence rather than requiring a separate generative model. The key methodological departure is the two-stage training recipe: a world-model post-training phase on action-free video, followed by action fine-tuning, which prior VLA works have not systematically explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a simple video-prediction (world model) post-training objective—requiring no action labels—dramatically improves downstream policy learning (e.g., SimplerEnv from 0% to 64.6%, LIBERO-Long from 17.4% to 89.2%) suggests a scalable, data-efficient recipe for VLA training that leverages the vast pool of unlabeled robot video. This opens a path toward generalist embodied intelligence where the bulk of learning comes from self-supervised video understanding rather than expensive action-annotated demonstrations. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger video corpora (compute was a stated limitation), how to integrate reinforcement learning for robust policy adaptation, and whether the discrete-token action representation can match continuous-action heads in precision-critical real-world settings.
