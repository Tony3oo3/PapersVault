## Problem

Existing Spiking Transformers suffer from two critical limitations: (i) a substantial performance gap relative to ANN counterparts because removing the softmax operation in Spiking Self-Attention (SSA) eliminates locality bias, producing near-uniform attention distributions instead of the localized, low-entropy patterns of vanilla self-attention; and (ii) high inference-time memory overhead from explicitly storing large attention matrices (QK of size N² or KV of size d²), which severely restricts deployment on resource-constrained neuromorphic hardware.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images converted into patch-based spike trains of shape T×B×N×D (T timesteps, N tokens, D feature dimension) and produces standard class labels (ImageNet-1k) or pixel-wise segmentation masks (ADE20K, 150 classes). The spiking formulation processes information event-driven over multiple timesteps (T=4 in segmentation experiments), and the attention output is emitted as a spike sequence through a final SN layer rather than a continuous-valued vector.

## Method

LRF-Dyn addresses both limitations in two stages. First, LRF-SSA inserts multi-scale dilated 3×3 depth-wise convolutions (dilation factors d=3 and d=5) into the SSA mechanism, adding a local receptive field term that assigns higher weights to spatial neighbors and provably reduces both the expected receptive radius and the attention entropy relative to plain SSA, recovering VSA-like locality without softmax. Second, LRF-Dyn reformulates the resulting attention aggregation as a charge-fire-reset dynamical process: the accumulated KV products over prior tokens serve as membrane potential, while the current token's KV contribution plus the local receptive field term acts as presynaptic input, eliminating explicit attention-matrix storage and reducing inference memory from O(d²) to O(kd) (k=8 dendrites). The decay factor and membrane capacitance are parameterized in a dendritic form with local receptive fields, inspired by multi-timescale photoreceptor dynamics, enabling efficient time-invariant training.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, LRF-SSA improves Spikformer-8-512 from 73.38% to 74.62% top-1 accuracy (+1.24%), QKFormer HST-10-512 from 82.04% to 82.52% (+0.48%), and SDT-V3 Efficient-Transformer-S from 75.30% to 76.22% (+0.92%), with fewer than 0.2M additional parameters. LRF-Dyn achieves comparable accuracy gains (e.g., +1.13% on Spikformer-8-512) while reducing inference storage complexity from O(d²) to O(kd), cutting memory usage by 49.4%. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, LRF-SSA boosts SDT-V3 mIoU by 2.6% (5M params) and 2.2% (19M params), surpassing the PVT baseline (41.6% mIoU) with far fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper positions LRF-SSA/LRF-Dyn as a drop-in replacement for the Spiking Self-Attention (SSA) module in three existing Spiking Transformer architectures, so all comparisons are against the unmodified base models. On ImageNet-1K, LRF-SSA consistently outperforms Spikformer (73.38%→74.62%), QKFormer (82.04%→82.52%), and SDT-V3 (75.30%→76.22%) by 0.44–1.24% top-1 accuracy with negligible parameter overhead. LRF-Dyn matches LRF-SSA within 0.1% accuracy while reducing inference memory from O(d²) to O(kd), a 49.4% reduction on Spikformer-8-512. Against CNN-based SNN baselines (SEWResNet 67.04%, MSResNet 69.42%), the LRF-enhanced Spiking Transformers maintain a 5–9% accuracy advantage. On ADE20K segmentation, SDT-V3+LRF-Dyn (43.1% mIoU, 19.25M params) surpasses the ANN-based PVT baseline (41.6% mIoU, 48M params) with fewer parameters, and SDT-V3+LRF-SSA (43.5% mIoU) further extends this lead by 1.9%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Spiking Transformer lineage initiated by Spikformer (Zhou et al., ICLR 2023), QKFormer (Zhou et al., 2024), and Spike-Driven-V3 (Yao et al., TPAMI 2025), all of which employ a Spiking Self-Attention (SSA) mechanism that removes softmax to preserve spike-friendly, event-driven computation. The authors identify two specific deficiencies in SSA—loss of locality bias (analogous to the known gap between linear and softmax attention in ANNs) and O(d²) memory overhead from storing intermediate KV products—and address them by injecting local receptive fields via dilated convolutions (LRF-SSA) and reformulating the aggregation as a charge-fire-reset neuronal dynamics process (LRF-Dyn). The biological motivation draws on multi-dendritic neuron models and localized receptive fields in retinal circuits, positioning the work at the intersection of neuromorphic computing and efficient attention design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in reducing inference-time memory from O(d²) to O(kd) (where k is the number of dendrites, set to 8), which is critical for deploying Spiking Transformers on neuromorphic hardware with tight on-chip memory budgets. The plug-and-play nature of LRF-SSA/LRF-Dyn across Spikformer, QKFormer, and SDT-V3 architectures makes the contribution broadly applicable within the spiking transformer community. However, the performance gains are modest (0.4–1.2% on ImageNet-1k, 2.2–2.6% mIoU on ADE20K), and the paper does not demonstrate actual hardware deployment or energy measurements on neuromorphic chips, leaving the claimed efficiency benefits largely theoretical. Open questions include whether the dendritic formulation scales to very long sequences, whether the memory savings translate to real latency reductions on Loihi/Tianjic-class hardware, and whether the locality inductive bias remains beneficial at higher resolutions where global context matters more.
