## Problem

Existing logical QA methods face a fundamental trade-off: symbolic-language (SL) based pipelines are highly sensitive to imperfect NL-to-SL translation, while natural-language (NL) based pipelines are prone to hallucination and logical inconsistency. No single-agent approach can simultaneously achieve rigorous reasoning and robustness to translation errors, and prior multi-agent debate methods do not exploit the complementarity between heterogeneous symbolic languages and NL reasoning paradigms.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language logical question consisting of a set of premises (facts), a set of conditional rules, and a target statement to classify as true, false, or unknown. Internally, the NL question is translated in parallel into three distinct symbolic languages (Logic Programming, First-Order Logic, and Boolean Satisfiability), each processed by a dedicated external solver (Pyke, Prover9, Z3). The final output is a single classification label determined by majority vote across five agents (three SL-based, two NL-based), accompanied by per-agent reasoning traces and self-reported confidence scores in [0,1].

## Method

The framework operates in two debate stages. In the translation stage, three agents each translate the NL question into a different SL (LP, FOL, SAT) and iteratively refine their translations through mutual critique over D=3 rounds, reducing the joint translation error rate from ~18% (single agent) to <1% (three agents). In the reasoning stage, the three SL agents (fed solver outputs) and two NL agents (using Chain-of-Thought and Plan-and-Solve prompting) debate for D=4 rounds, with each agent rewriting its reasoning narrative conditioned on all others' outputs. An adaptive sparse communication gate prunes inter-agent edges using a preference score that combines the confidence ratio C_i/C_j and an information-gain term (1 − cosine similarity of outputs), thresholded against the historical average for each agent pair, achieving 13–36% token savings while improving accuracy. A theoretical analysis provides an accuracy lower bound for the majority-vote ensemble showing that heterogeneous SL/NL agents maintain sufficiently low error correlation for the bound to approach 1 as the number of agents grows.

## Results

On three synthetic benchmarks (ProntoQA, ProofWriter, LogicalDeduction), MAD-Logic with sparse debate achieves 100.00% / 92.00% / 94.33% accuracy with GPT-4, 100.00% / 96.83% / 100.00% with Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and 100.00% / 93.33% / 100.00% with DeepSeek-V3, outperforming all baselines. On three real-world benchmarks (AR-LSAT, FOLIO, Chinese LogiQA-V2), the method reaches 53.25% / 86.27% / 74.76% with GPT-4 and 75.76% / 90.67% / 86.93% with DeepSeek-V3. The sparse variant consistently surpasses the fully-connected version by 0.05–0.92 pp in accuracy while saving 13–36% of tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAD-Logic (w/ sparse) outperforms all nine baselines across all three models and all six benchmarks. Against the strongest single-agent prompt-based methods, it gains 4.2 pp over DetermLR (97.80%→100.00% on ProntoQA, GPT-4) and 14.7 pp over SymbCOT on ProofWriter (82.33%→92.00%). Against solver-based methods, it surpasses LogicLM by 18.8 pp on ProofWriter (79.17%→92.00%) and LINC by 11.3 pp on the same benchmark. Among multi-agent baselines, it beats CortexDebate by 1.17 pp on ProofWriter (90.83%→92.00%) and 2.0 pp on LogicalDeduction (92.33%→94.33%) with GPT-4, while also reducing token costs by 13–36% compared to its own fully-connected variant. On real-world benchmarks (Table 2), the method leads CortexDebate by 2.17 pp on AR-LSAT (51.08%→53.25%) and 1.47 pp on FOLIO (84.80%→86.27%) with GPT-4, and by 1.73 pp on AR-LSAT (74.03%→75.76%) and 1.94 pp on FOLIO (88.73%→90.67%) with DeepSeek-V3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: neuro-symbolic logical reasoning (LogicLM, LINC, SymbCoT, Aristotle) and multi-agent debate (Du et al., 2023; SparseMAD; CortexDebate). It departs from prior neuro-symbolic work by rejecting the single-symbolic-language paradigm—instead translating NL into three heterogeneous SLs (LP, FOL, SAT) in parallel and refining via inter-agent debate—and from prior multi-agent debate work by introducing an adaptive, confidence- and information-gain-driven communication gate rather than a static or learned sparse topology. The theoretical contribution (a Chebyshev-based accuracy lower bound for majority vote under pairwise error correlation) formalizes why heterogeneous SL/NL agents should outperform homogeneous ensembles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for combining the rigor of symbolic solvers with the robustness of LLM reasoning, and the sparse communication mechanism offers a principled way to cut token costs by 13–36% while simultaneously improving accuracy—a non-trivial finding that filtering redundant debate turns reduces noise. The main open question, acknowledged by the authors, is generalization to out-of-distribution logical structures where LLM performance degrades sharply; the method's reliance on multiple frontier-model API calls and external solvers also limits its accessibility. Whether the adaptive gate's confidence-ratio + cosine-similarity design transfers to other multi-agent reasoning tasks beyond logical QA remains to be tested.
