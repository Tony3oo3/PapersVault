## Problem

Standard model-based RL methods learn world models via pixel-wise reconstruction losses that are dominated by large, static background elements, causing them to neglect small but decision-critical objects in visually complex scenes. This leads to poor dynamics prediction and sample inefficiency, particularly in environments like Hollow Knight boss fights where the agent must track a few key entities amid rich, distracting visuals. The paper asks whether integrating object-centric representations from pretrained segmentation models can redirect world model capacity toward task-relevant entities without requiring extensive labeling or privileged internal states.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw RGB frames (resized to 64×64 for the visual module and 420×320 for the frozen Cutie/SAM2 segmentation network), discrete keyboard actions (an 8-key multi-binary-discrete space for Hollow Knight; standard Atari actions otherwise), and a small set of hand-annotated segmentation masks (6 frames for Atari, 12 for Hollow Knight) that seed the retrieval-based video segmentation model. The segmentation model outputs compact per-object feature vectors (256-dim for SAM2, 2048-dim for Cutie) that are fused with the downsampled visual input. The trained system produces a policy that maps latent states to actions and a world model capable of generating imagined trajectories for self-play training.

## Method

OC-STORM augments a standard MBRL pipeline (STORM or DreamerV3 backbone) with a frozen pretrained video segmentation network (Cutie or SAM2) that extracts object-level feature vectors from few-shot annotated frames. These vectors are encoded via a categorical VAE using MLP encoders/decoders (16×16 categorical latents), while the visual input uses a CNN-based encoder (32×32 categorical latents). The core architectural innovation is a spatial-temporal transformer that alternates spatial attention across K object tokens plus one visual token at each timestep (capturing inter-object and object-scene interactions) with temporal attention across the sequence (modeling dynamics). A special [CLS]-like query token aggregates all tokens for reward and termination prediction. The world model is trained end-to-end with reconstruction, dynamics (KL), and representation losses, and the actor-critic policy is trained entirely on imagined trajectories using λ-returns and EMA-regularized value estimation. Vector-based object representations substantially outperform mask-based alternatives (FOCUS-style), and Cutie's masked-pooling features prove more informative for policy learning than SAM2's classification-oriented prototype vectors.

## Results

On the Atari 100k benchmark, OC-STORM (Cutie-OC + STORM) achieves a human-normalized score (HNS) mean of 134.8% and median of 43.8%, outperforming the STORM baseline (114.2% mean, 42.5% median) and DreamerV3 (106.6% mean, 33.2% median). On object-detectable games specifically, OC-STORM reaches 186.2% HNS mean versus 147.7% for STORM. On Hollow Knight boss fights, OC-STORM converges faster and achieves higher episode returns than STORM, with the largest gains on challenging bosses (e.g., Mage Lord: 28.0 vs. 19.6 return; 48% vs. 5% win rate).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OC-STORM (Cutie-OC + STORM) outperforms the STORM baseline on 19 of 26 Atari games, achieving a 20.6-point HNS mean improvement (134.8% vs. 114.2%) and a 1.3-point median gain (43.8% vs. 42.5%). Against DreamerV3, OC-STORM improves HNS mean by 28.2 points (134.8% vs. 106.6%) and median by 10.6 points (43.8% vs. 33.2%). The mask-based FOCUS variant (STORM + segmentation masks) underperforms both STORM and OC-STORM, confirming that vector-based object features are superior to raw masks at world-model resolution. Compared to other MBRL methods in Table 2, OC-STORM (134.8% mean) trails DIAMOND (145.9%) and Δ-IRIS (138.3%) on mean HNS but surpasses them on median (43.8% vs. 37.3% and 59.4% respectively, though Δ-IRIS leads on median). On Hollow Knight, OC-STORM consistently outperforms STORM, with the most dramatic gains on Mage Lord (48% vs. 5% win rate) and Hornet Protector (100% vs. 66.7% win rate), while on Meta-world continuous control tasks OC-STORM matches or exceeds MWM in sample efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

OC-STORM extends the STORM transformer world model and DreamerV3 actor-critic framework by injecting frozen, pretrained video segmentation features (Cutie, SAM2) as additional object tokens alongside the standard visual tokens. This departs from prior object-centric RL work (FOCUS, slot-attention methods) that either required task-specific fine-tuning, access to game-internal states, or unsupervised object discovery with poor detection quality in complex scenes. The key conceptual shift is treating a pretrained segmentation model as a plug-and-play feature extractor requiring only 6–12 annotated frames, rather than learning object representations end-to-end or relying on privileged information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a minimal human annotation budget (a handful of frames) combined with a frozen segmentation model can meaningfully redirect world-model capacity toward decision-critical objects in visually complex environments, yielding faster convergence and higher final performance than pixel-only baselines. This opens a practical path toward sample-efficient MBRL in real-world or game settings where background clutter dominates reconstruction losses. However, the approach still requires manual object identification, struggles with duplicated instances and geometric structures (walls, boundaries), and the annotation step, while small, is non-trivial for truly open-ended deployment. Whether the few-shot annotation requirement can be further reduced (e.g., via language prompts or automatic object discovery) remains an open question.
