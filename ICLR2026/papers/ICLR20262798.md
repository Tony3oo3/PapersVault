## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D reconstruction methods require diverse, large-scale real-world multi-view training data, which is expensive to capture and limited in scene diversity, leading to poor out-of-domain generalization. Video diffusion models implicitly encode 3D world knowledge from internet-scale video but only produce 2D frames, making them unusable for simulation or physical interaction. Lyra addresses this gap by distilling the implicit 3D knowledge in camera-controlled video diffusion models into an explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting representation, eliminating the need for any real multi-view training data.

## Input / Output

For static 3D generation, the method takes a single RGB image and samples 6 camera trajectories (121 poses each at 704×1280); for dynamic 4D generation, it takes a monocular video with estimated camera poses. The output is an explicit 3DGS scene (14 channels per Gaussian: position, scale, rotation, opacity, RGB) that supports real-time novel-view rendering at 18 ms per frame, with no per-scene optimization or post-processing. The 3DGS decoder itself runs in ~3 seconds for 6 trajectories, operating entirely in the compressed video VAE latent space (16 channels, 8× spatial and 8× temporal compression) rather than pixel space.

## Method

Lyra introduces a self-distillation framework where a pre-trained camera-controlled video diffusion model (GEN3C) serves as a frozen teacher that generates RGB video frames, supervising a trainable 3DGS student decoder that operates in the same video VAE latent space. The student architecture patchifies the multi-view video latents and Plücker camera embeddings, processes them through 16 layers of joint Transformer/Mamba-2 blocks (512 hidden dim), and maps to per-pixel 3D Gaussians via a transposed 3D convolution; the Mamba-2 layers provide a 6.5× speedup over pure Transformers while handling the 726 input views that would cause OOM in pixel-space methods. Training uses a composite loss combining MSE, LPIPS, scale-invariant depth supervision from ViPE, and L1 opacity regularization with 80% Gaussian pruning. For dynamic 4D scenes, the decoder is extended with source and target time embeddings (encoded via sinusoidal features through the VAE encoder), and a motion-reversed video augmentation strategy ensures balanced spatial supervision across all timesteps to prevent low-opacity artifacts in early frames.

## Results

On single-image-to-3D generation, Lyra achieves state-of-the-art results across all three benchmarks. On RealEstate10K, it reaches PSNR 21.79, SSIM 0.752, and LPIPS 0.219; on DL3DV, PSNR 20.09, SSIM 0.583, LPIPS 0.313; and on Tanks-and-Temples, PSNR 19.24, SSIM 0.570, LPIPS 0.336. On the out-of-distribution Lyra dataset, Lyra further achieves PSNR 24.92 (static) and 23.07 (dynamic), substantially outperforming the pixel-space BTimer(GEN3C) baseline (16.32 and 20.29 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Lyra outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric across all three standard benchmarks. The closest competitor is Bolt3D on RealEstate10K (PSNR 21.54 vs. 21.79), but Bolt3D does not report results on DL3DV or Tanks-and-Temples. On DL3DV, Lyra leads Wonderland by 3.45 dB in PSNR (20.09 vs. 16.64) and on Tanks-and-Temples by 3.34 dB (19.24 vs. 15.90). In the appendix, Lyra also beats the pixel-space Long-LRM(GEN3C) baseline by 3.34 dB on RE10K (21.79 vs. 18.45) and the BTimer(GEN3C) baseline by 8.60 dB on the static Lyra dataset (24.92 vs. 16.32) and 2.78 dB on the dynamic Lyra dataset (23.07 vs. 20.29). The authors note that since no source code is available for most baselines on their out-of-distribution evaluation set, quantitative comparisons rely on reported numbers from the respective papers.

## Relation to Prior Work

Lyra builds on camera-controlled video diffusion models (specifically GEN3C) as a teacher signal, departing from prior feed-forward 3D reconstruction methods (GS-LRM, Long-LRM, Bolt3D, Wonderland) that require real multi-view training data and operate in pixel space. The key architectural departure is performing 3DGS decoding in the compressed video VAE latent space rather than pixel space, enabling an order-of-magnitude increase in input views (726 vs. 12–24). The reconstruction block design (Transformer + Mamba-2 layers) follows Long-LRM, and the 4D extension borrows the bullet-time formulation from BTimer, but the self-distillation loop where the RGB decoder supervises the 3DGS decoder within the same latent space is the paper's central novel contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for real multi-view captures, Lyra provides a scalable pipeline for generating simulation-ready 3D/4D environments for robotics, autonomous driving, and embodied AI—domains where diverse, physically consistent training scenes are critical. The main limitation is that scene quality and consistency are bounded by the underlying video diffusion model, and the 12-minute video generation step remains a practical bottleneck. Open questions include whether auto-regressive techniques can extend generation to truly large-scale scenes, and whether motion/tracking information integrated into the reconstruction network could improve dynamic quality.
