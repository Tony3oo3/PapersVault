## Problem

Existing multi-concept unlearning methods for diffusion models break down at scale: conflicting weight updates from many concepts interfere with each other, unlearning bleeds into visually similar non-target concepts, and reliance on extra data or auxiliary adapters creates computational bottlenecks. No prior approach simultaneously achieves stable, precise, and efficient forgetting of large numbers of concepts (e.g., 50+) without degrading overall generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263711/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

ScaPre consumes the target concept embeddings (obtained by encoding text prompts through the model's text encoder), their contextual token features, and the pretrained cross-attention key/value projection matrices W₀. It outputs a single updated weight matrix W* per cross-attention layer, computed via one Sylvester-equation solve followed by a proximal Bures-geodesic refinement—no iterative training, no auxiliary sub-models, and no additional data. The entire 50-concept unlearning completes in ~120 seconds on a single RTX A6000 with ~6.5 GB peak memory.

## Method

ScaPre formulates unlearning as a closed-form quadratic optimization over cross-attention weights with two stabilizing mechanisms. First, a conflict-aware stable design introduces a spectral trace regularizer tr(W(λI+S+R)Wᵀ) where S aggregates second-order statistics of target-concept token features and R applies sigmoid-gated SVD to suppress high-overlap directions, combined with a Bures-distance geometry alignment term that matches the covariance structure WWᵀ to the pretrained W₀W₀ᵀ rather than penalizing element-wise differences. Second, an Informax Decoupler computes per-channel mutual information between discretized activations and target/neutral labels, yielding a diagonal reweighting matrix B=diag(α) that confines updates to concept-relevant output channels. The quadratic part (trace regularizer + Informax weighting + target-erasure linear term) is solved exactly via the Sylvester equation BW+WA=M, and the non-quadratic Bures alignment is incorporated through a two-step proximal refinement: Bures-geodesic interpolation of the covariance toward the pretrained reference, followed by an orthogonal Procrustes rotation to map back to weight space while preserving the unlearning direction.

## Results

On the Imagenette benchmark (10 classes), ScaPre achieves an average unlearning accuracy of 0.8% (vs. 4.9% for RECE, the next best) while maintaining a CLIP score of 30.43, yielding a unified quality score (UQ) of 64.09—substantially above the best baseline (SPM at 49.89). On the 50-class ImageNet-Diversi50 benchmark, ScaPre reaches 3.9% average accuracy with UQ 65.30, whereas UCE and RECE collapse to 0.0% accuracy but with CLIP scores dropping to ~22, indicating generative failure. On the precision-focused ImageNet-Confuse5 benchmark, ScaPre achieves 84.3% overall accuracy (joint unlearn + preserve), nearly doubling the best baseline (SP at 50.3%), while preserving visually similar non-target concepts at 76.3% accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScaPre outperforms all eight baselines on the unified UQ metric across every benchmark. Against fine-tuning-based methods (FMN, SPM, MACE, ESD), ScaPre achieves dramatically lower unlearning accuracy (0.8% vs. 38.7–78.5% on Imagenette) while matching or exceeding their CLIP scores, indicating that iterative optimization struggles to scale without degrading generation. Against closed-form methods (UCE, RECE), ScaPre matches or slightly exceeds their unlearning accuracy (0.8% vs. 4.9–8.5%) but preserves generation quality far better (CLIP 30.43 vs. 29.27–29.45; FID 14.37 vs. 43.72–49.32 on style unlearning), where UCE and RECE suffer severe generative collapse at scale. Against the most recent multi-concept method SP (Sculpting Memory, ICCV 2025), ScaPre achieves 5× lower unlearning accuracy (0.8% vs. 9.6%) and a 14-point UQ advantage (64.09 vs. 31.78) on Imagenette, and on the precision benchmark (ImageNet-Confuse5) ScaPre's 84.3% overall accuracy nearly doubles SP's 50.3%, showing SP still suffers collateral damage on similar concepts. On explicit content (I2P), ScaPre reduces NudeNet detections to 54 (SD v1.4) and 56 (SD v1.5), the lowest among all methods, while maintaining the highest CLIP score (30.88/31.37). In adversarial robustness (Table 10), ScaPre achieves the best scores on Ring-A-Bell (84.5) and MMA (94.6) and the lowest UnlearnDiffAtk recovery (60.9), outperforming all baselines including SP (83.2/92.4/67.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

ScaPre extends the closed-form weight-editing paradigm established by TIME (Orgad et al., 2023) and UCE (Gandikota et al., 2024), which solve a quadratic objective over cross-attention matrices to redirect target concept embeddings. Where prior multi-concept methods (MACE, SPM, Sculpting Memory) rely on iterative fine-tuning with LoRA adapters or dynamic masks, and where UCE/RECE are closed-form but collapse in quality beyond ~10 concepts, ScaPre introduces a spectral trace regularizer with SVD-based conflict gating and a Bures-distance geometry alignment to stabilize the optimization landscape at scale, plus a mutual-information-based Informax Decoupler to confine updates to concept-relevant channels. The final solution is a Sylvester equation solved in closed form followed by a proximal Bures-geodesic refinement, making the entire pipeline training-free and independent of the number of target concepts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to demonstrate that large-scale (50+ concept) unlearning in diffusion models can be achieved in a single closed-form step (~120 s) without auxiliary data or sub-models, challenging the prevailing assumption that iterative fine-tuning is necessary for multi-concept forgetting. The 5× scalability advantage over the best baseline within acceptable quality bounds is practically significant for deployment scenarios requiring bulk concept removal (e.g., copyright takedowns, safety filtering). Open questions remain: robustness against white-box recovery attacks (UnlearnDiffAtk still recovers ~61% of erased concepts), generalization beyond SD v1.x cross-attention to DiT-based or flow-matching architectures, and whether the mutual-information channel selection remains informative when the target concept set is extremely large or semantically heterogeneous.
