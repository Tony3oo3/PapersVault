## Problem

Existing Parameter-Efficient Tuning (PET) methods for Referring Image Segmentation focus on layer-wise feature alignment but neglect the critical role of a neck module for intermediate fusion of aggregated multi-scale features, creating a significant performance bottleneck. Additionally, State-Space Models suffer from exponential information decay over long sequences, which undermines their effectiveness when naively applied to full-resolution vision-language fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261312/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image resized to 448×448 pixels and a natural-language referring expression as input, producing a pixel-level binary segmentation mask as output. The frozen CLIP-ViT-B/16 text encoder and DINOv2 (B/14 or L/14) vision encoder are adapted with only 1.3%–2.2% trainable backbone parameters. Training uses a combined objective of text-to-pixel contrastive loss, Dice loss, and a per-window binary cross-entropy alignment loss with weights 0.5, 0.3, and 0.2 respectively.

## Method

WIMFRIS introduces a Hierarchical Mamba Fusion (HMF) neck block whose core is the Window Mamba Fuser (WMF): it first aggregates multi-scale DINOv2 features into a unified representation, then partitions the feature map into non-overlapping windows (optimal size 4×4), prepends a shared global text class token to each window sequence, and runs parallel Mamba SSM scans over the compact (M+1)-length sequences, thereby constraining sequence length to mitigate exponential decay while preserving local-global cross-modal interaction. The PET strategy comprises three components: a Mamba Text Adapter (MTA) that uses an SSM block to strengthen long-range textual dependencies into an enhanced global prior, a Multi-Scale Aligner (MSA) that applies RFMixer (multi-branch depth-wise strip convolutions with 3-5-7 kernels) followed by cross-attention with the textual prior for precise vision-language alignment, and learnable sigmoid-gated emphasis parameters that adaptively weight each backbone stage's contribution. The architecture is trained end-to-end for 50 epochs with Adam, updating only the adapter and neck parameters while keeping both encoders frozen.

## Results

On the standard RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/G-Ref splits, WIMFRIS-B achieves an average mIoU of 71.8, surpassing the strongest PET baseline DETRIS-B (70.4) by 1.4 points. Scaling to WIMFRIS-L raises the average to 73.4, outperforming DETRIS-L (72.2) and all other PET methods, while remaining competitive with full fine-tuning approaches like ReLA (71.2) and CGFormer (70.5). With mixed RefCOCO training data, WIMFRIS-L* reaches an average mIoU of 78.2, exceeding DETRIS-L* (77.0) and UNINEXT-L* (76.0). The best individual split result is 82.8 mIoU on RefCOCO testA (WIMFRIS-L*).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among PET methods, WIMFRIS-L (73.4 avg mIoU) outperforms DETRIS-L (72.2) by 1.2 points, BarLeRIa (66.5) by 6.9 points, and ETRIS (63.9) by 9.5 points across all three datasets. WIMFRIS-B (71.8) similarly surpasses DETRIS-B (70.4) by 1.4 points, with the largest gap on RefCOCO testB (74.8 vs. 73.5). Against full fine-tuning methods, WIMFRIS-L exceeds ReLA (71.2) and CGFormer (70.5) but trails RISCLIP-L (74.9) by 1.5 points on average, particularly on RefCOCO+ (71.9 vs. 74.4 val) and G-Ref val(u) (70.4 vs. 71.8). With mixed training data, WIMFRIS-L* (78.2) achieves the best average, beating DETRIS-L* (77.0) by 1.2 and UNINEXT-L* (76.0) by 2.2 points, with the largest margin on RefCOCO+ testB (72.5 vs. 70.2). The paper's HMF neck module also provides consistent gains when plugged into existing PET frameworks: adding it to ETRIS improves RefCOCO val from 72.2 to 75.7, and to DETRIS from 74.3 to 76.4.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PET-based RIS line (ETRIS, BarLeRIa, DETRIS) by identifying and addressing a specific architectural gap: the absence of a dedicated neck module for aggregated intermediate vision-language fusion. It builds directly on Mamba's selective SSM scanning mechanism, repurposing it in two distinct roles — a text adapter (MTA) for global textual priors and a windowed fusion module (WMF) for local-global modality alignment. The key conceptual departure from prior PET methods is the argument that layer-wise alignment (handled by adapters) and task-specific intermediate fusion (handled by the neck) are fundamentally different processes, and that neglecting the latter creates a performance bottleneck. Architecturally, it replaces DETRIS's cross-modal neck with the proposed HMF block while retaining its task decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, well-motivated solution to the SSM exponential decay problem in vision-text fusion via non-overlapping window partitioning, a technique that could transfer to other SSM-based multimodal tasks. However, the authors themselves identify a fundamental tension: window partitioning isolates spatial context, causing failures on large objects spanning multiple windows and on expressions requiring disambiguation from spatially distant visual cues. Open questions include whether more sophisticated inter-window communication (e.g., hierarchical or overlapping windows) can resolve this without reintroducing the decay problem, and whether the HMF block generalizes beyond RIS to other dense prediction tasks.
