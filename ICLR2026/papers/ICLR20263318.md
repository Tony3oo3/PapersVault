## Problem

LLM computations are deeply entangled across billions of parameters, resisting the modularization needed for interpretability, auditing, and maintenance. Existing interpretability methods rely on data-driven approaches—curating inputs, hypothesizing relevant sub-computations, and observing activations—rather than providing a principled structural decomposition of the computation itself. The paper asks whether one can systematically cut through this entanglement at the functional level, without additional data or training, to expose explicit input-to-output computational paths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263318/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the trained LLM weights and, depending on the application, either a specific input sentence (for jet lens heatmaps) or the full vocabulary (for n-gram table extraction). It produces a set of explicit input-to-output polynomial functions (jet paths) plus a complementary nonlinear remainder, with no retraining or probe datasets required. For n-gram extraction, paths are evaluated exhaustively over the vocabulary domain V^(n-1), yielding complete symbolic probability tables (e.g., all V×V bigram scores) that can be diffed across model checkpoints or queried for toxicity mass without any prompting.

## Method

The core mechanism is recursive application of jet operators (generalized truncated Taylor series) to the residual stream of a transformer. Lemma 1 establishes the key disentangling property: the k-jet of a function at a sum of inputs equals a convex combination of jets at individual inputs, up to higher-order error. Algorithm 1 (jet_expand) applies this at each residual block, progressively unrolling nested entanglements into explicit input-to-output paths and collecting approximation error into a remainder. The Logit Lens is formally recovered as the zeroth-order jet of the decoder at an intermediate hidden state, and higher-order jets (k≥1) extend it to capture indirect layer interactions. For n-gram extraction, specific path types are isolated—MLP-only paths for bigrams, single-attention paths for trigrams—and evaluated over the entire vocabulary via batched matrix operations, producing dataset-free symbolic tables that reveal linguistic functions of individual components and quantify fine-tuning or toxicity shifts.

## Results

On the joint jet lens for GPT-Neo-2.7B (k=1), the expansion logits achieve a cosine similarity of 0.993 with the model's full logits, with top-1 tokens perfectly matching. Averaged over 100 sentences across GPT-2, GPT-Neo, and Llama models, cosine similarities between jet-expanded and original logits range from 0.85 to 1.0, with k=1 consistently outperforming k=0 (standard Logit Lens). For toxicity assessment, jet bi-gram mass for Llama-2-7B is 0.102 versus 0.093 for Llama-2-7B-chat, revealing that RLHF alignment reduces but does not eliminate toxic knowledge, in contrast to ToxiGen scores that drop from 21.25 to 0.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its jet bi-gram toxicity index against two established baselines: ToxiGen (Hartvigsen et al., 2022) and RealToxicityPrompts (Gehman et al., 2020). ToxiGen reports a dramatic drop from 21.25 (Llama-2-7B) to 0.0 (Llama-2-7B-chat), suggesting successful detoxification. However, the jet bi-gram mass remains nearly unchanged (0.102 → 0.093), indicating that toxic token associations persist in latent form despite RLHF alignment. RealToxicityPrompts challenging prompts partially corroborate this: while 'No' and 'Very mild' difficulty success rates decrease (38%→23%, 49%→35%), 'Medium' and 'Hard' difficulties remain high (64%→64%, 88%→84%), confirming that toxic knowledge can still be triggered. The jet bi-gram method thus provides a complementary, dataset-free view that reveals what ToxiGen's binary scoring masks, positioning it as a more granular diagnostic tool for embedded knowledge rather than a direct replacement for prompt-based benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the classical jet operator from differential geometry (Ehresmann, 1951) as an algebraic tool for restructuring residual network computations, providing a principled foundation that prior path-based methods lacked. It formally grounds the Logit Lens as a zeroth-order jet of the decoder, generalizes Veit et al.'s (2016) exponential path enumeration to arbitrary depth with nonlinearities handled via Taylor truncation, and extends Elhage et al.'s (2021) one/two-layer path decomposition to full-depth transformers. The key departure from prior mechanistic interpretability work is operating in function space rather than activation space, requiring no probe datasets or sampling, and treating interpretability as systematic function decomposition rather than data-driven circuit identification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a dataset-free, algebraic operator for modular inspection of LLMs, enabling tasks like n-gram table extraction and toxicity indexing without curated benchmarks or repeated inference runs. The finding that RLHF alignment leaves jet bi-gram toxic mass nearly unchanged (0.102 → 0.093) while ToxiGen scores drop to zero suggests alignment masks rather than erases toxic associations, though this evidence is preliminary (single model pair, one metric). Limitations include potentially large remainders for function-level expansions, focus on bi/tri-grams only, and computational cost for exhaustive path enumeration; the authors envision Fourier-style decompositions and connections to Shapley values as future directions toward controllable, auditable models.
