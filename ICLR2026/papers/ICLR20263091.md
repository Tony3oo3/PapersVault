## Problem

While LD-FPG can generate static equilibrium ensembles of all-atom protein conformations from a reference structure, it does not model the temporal evolution between metastable states, leaving a gap for simulating long-timescale dynamics without pre-defined collective variables. The paper asks which propagation mechanism—score-guided stochastic dynamics, a linear Koopman operator, or an autoregressive neural network—best advances the system within a fixed learned latent space while preserving both ensemble fidelity and physical kinetics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263091/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes all-atom protein coordinates from MD trajectories (XTC files), which are encoded by a frozen 4-layer ChebNet (HNO) into per-atom embeddings and then pooled into a single 100-dimensional latent vector z(t) per frame. Rollouts begin from the encoding of a held-out MD frame, and the output is a decoded all-atom trajectory X̂(t) produced by the frozen LD-FPG decoder. Latents are standardized per dimension before propagator fitting and unstandardized before decoding; no momenta, barostat, or box dynamics are simulated—only configuration-space equilibrium is targeted.

## Method

GLDP freezes the LD-FPG encoder-decoder and swaps only the latent propagator, isolating the effect of the propagation rule on stability, ensemble fidelity, and kinetics. Three propagators are evaluated in the same 100-d latent: (i) a score-guided Langevin SDE that trains a time-conditional denoiser (VP schedule) to estimate ∇_z log p(z) and integrates overdamped Langevin with Euler-Maruyama; (ii) a Koopman linear operator A ∈ R^{100×100} estimated via truncated-SVD DMD from snapshot matrices; and (iii) a 2-hidden-layer MLP (512 units, ReLU, dropout 0.1) trained with one-step MSE on consecutive latent pairs. The key design choice is that the encoder-decoder is held fixed across all experiments, so differences in rollout stability, dihedral JSD, TICA timescales, and GPCR activation surfaces are attributable solely to the propagation mechanism. The autoregressive NN proves most robust for 10,000-frame rollouts, Langevin best recovers sharp side-chain rotamer distributions, and Koopman provides a lightweight but variance-damping linear baseline.

## Results

GLDP-NN (autoregressive NN propagator) achieves the lowest JSD on both backbone and side-chain coordinates for A1AR (0.063/0.087) and the best contact correlation (r=0.985), while also recovering a TICA timescale of 650 steps (GT: 1059) where LSS becomes numerically unstable. On 1R6W, GLDP attains the best side-chain dihedral JSD (0.092 vs 0.135 for LSS) and a TICA timescale of 980 steps (GT: 1670), far exceeding MD-Gen (450) and LSS (103). On the small ADP system, LSS leads on backbone dihedral JSD (0.016 vs 0.061), but GLDP achieves the best coordinate JSD (0.366/0.386). Within the same latent, the autoregressive NN is most stable for long rollouts (10,000 frames without failure on both ADP and A1AR), while score-guided Langevin best recovers side-chain rotamer fidelity (JSD 0.058 vs 0.067 for NN on A1AR χ₁,χ₂).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LSS (Sidky et al., 2020), GLDP wins decisively on the large A1AR GPCR, achieving the lowest JSD on all four structural metrics (e.g., backbone coord JSD 0.063 vs 0.657) and the only stable TICA timescale (650 vs 'Unstable'), while LSS retains an edge on small-system backbone dihedrals (ADP: 0.016 vs 0.061) and 7JFL backbone dihedrals (0.027 vs 0.037). Against MD-Gen (Jing et al., 2024), GLDP dominates on A1AR across every metric (contact corr 0.985 vs 0.941; TICA 650 vs 85 steps) and on 1R6W side-chain fidelity (dihedral JSD 0.092 vs 0.105; coord JSD 0.185 vs 0.210), but MD-Gen edges ahead on 7JFL contact correlation (0.960 vs 0.949) and 1R6W contact correlation (0.980 vs 0.972). Against GeoTDM (Han et al., 2024), which is only evaluated on ADP, GLDP achieves lower coordinate JSD (0.366/0.386 vs 0.444/0.395) while GeoTDM has a slightly closer TICA timescale to ground truth (12.20 vs 10.14; GT 16.41). Overall, GLDP's advantage grows with system complexity: it is competitive on the small peptide but clearly superior on mixed-topology 1R6W and the large transmembrane A1AR, where baselines either degrade structurally or lose kinetic coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LD-FPG encoder-decoder (Sengar et al., 2025), which generates static all-atom equilibrium ensembles via latent diffusion on ChebNet graph embeddings, by inserting a temporal propagator within the frozen latent space. It sits at the intersection of three established lines: latent-space simulators (LSS, DeepGenMSM) that factorize dynamics into encoder-propagator-decoder pipelines; score-based diffusion and Langevin dynamics for thermodynamically consistent sampling; and Koopman/DMD linear operators for interpretable dynamics. The key methodological departure is holding the encoder-decoder fixed and swapping only the propagator, enabling a controlled isolation of how the propagation rule affects stability, ensemble fidelity, and kinetics—something prior latent simulators have not systematically performed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first learned all-atom rollout that recovers a GPCR activation free-energy surface (A2AR), demonstrating that latent-space propagators can capture functionally relevant slow transitions in large membrane proteins. The clean three-way trade-off—autoregressive NN for long-horizon stability, score-guided Langevin for side-chain thermodynamic fidelity, Koopman as a lightweight but over-damping baseline—offers practical guidance for choosing a propagator given system complexity and target observables. Key limitations include the system-specific (non-transferable) design, the absence of momenta and barostat dynamics (no NVE energy conservation), and the reliance on a fixed encoder whose quality bounds all downstream performance. Open directions include hybrid propagators combining NN stability with Langevin thermodynamic correctness, explicit kinetic training constraints, and end-to-end multi-protein training for generalizable latent dynamics.
