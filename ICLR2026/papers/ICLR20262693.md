## Problem

Existing imputation-based incomplete multi-view clustering (IMVC) methods treat view recovery and subspace representation learning as weakly coupled, sequential steps without explicit alignment or collaborative interaction. This lack of a shared semantic bridge prevents simultaneous exploitation of cross-view consistency and complementarity during both missing-view imputation and clustering structure exploration, leading to sub-optimal imputation fidelity and clustering discriminability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262693/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes incomplete multi-view data {X^v ∈ R^{d_v×n}}_{v=1}^V along with diagonal binary missing indicator matrices W^v ∈ {0,1}^{n×n} (where W^v_{ii}=1 indicates sample i is observed in view v). It outputs cluster assignments obtained via spectral clustering on a fused affinity matrix S = (|Z|+|Z^T|+Σ_v|Z^v|+Σ_v|(Z^v)^T|)/(V+1), where Z and Z^v are the learned shared and view-specific subspace coefficient matrices. A key intermediate is the latent representation H ∈ R^{k×n} (k typically 10–20) that simultaneously drives view reconstruction and global subspace self-representation.

## Method

ARSL-IMVC unifies two modules through a shared latent representation H: (1) Collaborative View Recovery (CVR) reconstructs each view as P^v H + E_1^v, where P^v is an orthonormal projection and E_1^v is a view-specific estimator regularized by pairwise HSIC to enforce diversity across views; (2) Tensorial Subspace Learning (TSL) learns view-shared Z (from H = HZ + E_H) and view-specific Z^v (from P^vH+E_1^v = (P^vH+E_1^v)Z^v+E_2^v) self-representations, which are stacked into a 3D tensor Z ∈ R^{n×(V+1)×n} and regularized by tensor nuclear norm to capture high-order cross-view correlations. The critical design choice is that H serves dually as the reconstruction basis for CVR and the global semantic carrier for TSL, creating an explicit information-flow bridge that aligns consistency and complementarity exploration across both modules. The joint objective is solved via ADMM, with the H-update reduced to a Sylvester equation (solved by Bartels–Stewart), the tensor update via tensor singular value thresholding (t-SVT), and the E_1^v update incorporating the HSIC kernel gradient in closed form.

## Results

On BBCSport, HW, and BDGP at 10% missing rate, ARSL-IMVC achieves 96.51% ACC / 89.77% NMI, 96.90% ACC / 92.77% NMI, and 56.07% ACC / 35.22% NMI respectively, outperforming the best baseline by 4.60%, 8.31%, and 5.41% in ACC. Performance degrades gracefully as missing rate increases to 50%, where ARSL-IMVC still leads (e.g., 88.97% ACC on BBCSport vs. 86.05% for HCP-IMSC). On the large-scale HDigit dataset (10,000 samples), ARSL-IMVC reaches 99.00% ACC and 96.97% NMI at 10% missing, surpassing the runner-up HCLS-IMSC (98.29% ACC) by ~0.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARSL-IMVC outperforms all nine baselines on the majority of dataset–missing-rate combinations across ACC, NMI, and Purity. Against imputation-free methods (DAIMC, IMSC-AGL, HCLS-CGL), the gains are substantial: on HW at p=0.1, ARSL-IMVC reaches 96.90% ACC versus 88.59% for IMSC-AGL and 76.65% for DAIMC. Among imputation-based competitors, HCP-IMSC is the closest rival on BBCSport (91.91% vs. 96.51% ACC at p=0.1), while BWIC-TIMC and RMoGL trail by 5–10 points on most settings. On the large-scale HDigit dataset (10k samples), ARSL-IMVC edges out HCLS-IMSC by 0.71% in ACC (99.00 vs. 98.29) and 1.67% in NMI (96.97 vs. 95.30), while surpassing UEAF and IMSC-AGL by 10–25%. The ablation (removing subspace learning on the latent H) drops ACC by 12.48% on BBCSport and 26.75% on HW, confirming that the explicit alignment between view recovery and tensorial subspace learning is the primary source of the performance advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of imputation-based incomplete multi-view clustering (building on UEAF, HCP-IMSC, BWIC-TIMC, RMoGL) and tensorial subspace clustering (building on Li et al. 2022, Yao et al. 2025). Its key conceptual departure is that prior imputation-based methods treat view recovery and subspace representation learning as weakly coupled, sequential steps, whereas ARSL-IMVC introduces a shared latent representation H that simultaneously drives view reconstruction (via projection P^v) and feeds into self-representation learning, creating an explicit bidirectional information flow between the two modules. The HSIC diversity regularizer on view-specific estimators and the low-rank tensor nuclear norm on the stacked subspace representations are repurposed from their respective literatures but unified under a single ADMM-optimized objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit alignment mechanism—where the latent representation serves as both reconstruction basis and subspace learning input—offers a clean architectural principle that could inform future incomplete multi-view methods beyond subspace clustering. However, the method remains iterative and matrix/tensor-based rather than deep, limiting its applicability to very high-dimensional or non-linear feature spaces. Open questions include whether the tensor formulation provides genuine advantage over simpler multi-matrix low-rank constraints, whether the approach extends naturally to partial multi-view settings (where different samples have different missing patterns beyond per-view binary masks), and how the method scales beyond the 10,000-sample HDigit experiment to truly large-scale scenarios.
