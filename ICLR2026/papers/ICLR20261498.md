## Problem

While hard-attention Chain-of-Thought (CoT) transformers are known to be Turing-complete, it remained an open problem whether softmax-attention CoT transformers achieve the same computational power, and whether any trainability or length-generalizability guarantee could be provided. The difficulty lies in the fact that simulating a Turing machine head position via softmax attention is fundamentally harder than via hard attention, and no prior construction bridged this gap.

## Input / Output

The method takes an input string w over a finite alphabet (e.g., a^p in unary or bin(p) in binary) and autoregressively generates a sequence of CoT tokens, each corresponding to a transition of a simulated counter machine. The CoT terminates in an accept token if the input belongs to the target language, or runs indefinitely otherwise. The input is consumed prefix-by-prefix: at each step the transformer reads the original input plus all previously generated CoT tokens to determine the next token, making the computation inherently sequential and stateful.

## Method

The key insight is to simulate Minsky counter machines (rather than Turing machines directly) by exploiting the counting power of softmax transformers through the C-RASP declarative language. For unary and letter-bounded languages, counter values are tracked via C-RASP counting terms over the input, and the CoT C-RASP directly simulates counter-machine transitions. For arbitrary alphabets, the paper introduces a single parameter-free relative positional encoding (RPE) that enables a two-phase construction: Phase I appends dummy letters until the current word length encodes the input via a σ-mapping, and Phase II simulates the counter machine on the resulting numeric encoding. This RPE is universal (independent of the specific machine being simulated) and preserves length generalizability within the Huang et al. (2025) framework. Empirically, LLaMA-style decoder-only transformers trained from scratch on counter-machine traces achieve near-perfect length generalization on arithmetic tasks (prime, exponential, division, GCD, multiplication) in unary (NoPE) and binary (with RPE) representations, while binary without RPE fails to generalize.

## Results

On five arithmetic languages (Prime, Exponential, Division, GCD, Multiplication), the paper validates its theoretical predictions through length generalization experiments. Unary representations without positional encodings achieve >99.90% accuracy across all three test splits (lengths 1–100, 101–200, 201–300), confirming that CoT C-RASP suffices for unary inputs. Binary representations with RPEs achieve 100% accuracy on all test splits, while removing RPEs causes catastrophic generalization failure (e.g., Prime drops from 100% to 95.00% on test0 and 0.40% on test1; Multiplication drops from 100% to 64.40% on test0 and 0.02% on test1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against external transformer architectures or prior Turing-completeness constructions; its empirical contribution is validating its own theoretical predictions. The key comparison is between three configurations: (1) Unary NoPE, (2) Binary with RPEs, and (3) Binary without RPEs. The theory predicts that Unary NoPE and Binary+RPE should generalize, while Binary without RPEs should not. The experiments confirm this precisely: Unary NoPE and Binary+RPE both achieve near-perfect or perfect accuracy across all length splits, while Binary without RPEs shows near-zero generalization (≤0.40% on test₁ and test₂ for all tasks). This validates the paper's central theoretical claim that RPEs are necessary and sufficient for full Turing-completeness over arbitrary alphabets, whereas plain CoT C-RASP suffices only for letter-bounded/permutation-invariant languages.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the length-generalizability framework of Huang et al. (2025) and the C-RASP counting formalism of Yang & Chiang (2024) to the Chain-of-Thought setting, adding Relative Positional Encodings in the style of Shaw et al. (2018). All prior Turing-completeness proofs for CoT transformers (Pérez et al., 2021; Merrill & Sabharwal, 2024; Li & Wang, 2025) relied on hardmax attention, which lacks trainability guarantees; this paper is the first to establish Turing-completeness for softmax attention CoT transformers. The key technical departure is simulating Minsky counter machines rather than Turing machines directly, exploiting C-RASP's counting power to track counter values through attention, and showing that a single parameter-free RPE suffices for full Turing-completeness over arbitrary alphabets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves a major open question in transformer theory and, crucially, provides a length-generalizability guarantee that prior hard-attention results could not offer, making the Turing-completeness result compatible with a principled learning framework. The negative result—that CoT C-RASP without RPEs is not Turing-complete over arbitrary languages (e.g., palindromes are unsolvable)—sharpens understanding of what softmax attention can and cannot express. Open questions include refining the result with complexity-theoretic bounds (relating CoT steps to complexity classes) and whether the idealized learning procedure is computable in a practical sense, as noted by Yang et al. (2026).
