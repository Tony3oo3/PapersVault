## Problem

Reinforcement learning fine-tuning (RLFT) of pretrained policies suffers from entropy collapse: fine-tuned policies concentrate probability mass on a narrow set of high-reward behaviors, losing the diverse repertoire of the pretrained distribution. Standard remedies like entropy bonuses induce only local or token-level variation and fail to promote semantic or trajectory-level exploration. The paper asks how to design an RL objective that explicitly encourages the policy to explore and refine a diverse set of successful generations simultaneously.

## Input / Output

In BabyAI/Minigrid, the policy conditions on a grid image, the agent's direction embedding, and a natural-language mission (encoded by a GRU) and outputs a discrete action from {left, right, forward, pickup, drop, toggle, done} over a 100-step horizon. In Algorithmic Creativity (Triangle Discovery), the input is a graph index plus a prefix prompt and the agent's past outputs, and the output is a sequence of edge tokens (vocabulary size 1017, max length 11) that must form a valid triangle in an unobserved graph. Crucially, the method operates on sets of n=4 independently sampled trajectories from the same state rather than single trajectories, with N=8 additional 'vine' rollouts generated from selected rollout states to estimate set-level advantages.

## Method

The paper introduces a set RL framework where the objective is defined over a set of n trajectories rather than a single trajectory, and proposes a polychromic objective f_poly(s, τ_{1:n}) = (1/n) Σ R(τ_i) · d(s, τ_{1:n}) that multiplies per-trajectory reward by a set-level diversity score, so that a set scores highly only if it contains both successful and diverse trajectories. To optimize this with PPO, the algorithm uses vine sampling (selecting p=2 rollout states per trajectory and generating N=8 additional rollouts from each) to collect on-policy sets, and replaces the standard per-action advantage with a shared polychromic advantage: all actions belonging to the same set of n trajectories receive the identical update signal f_poly(s, g_i) − f̂(s), where f̂ is a Monte Carlo baseline over M independently sampled sets. A 'polychrome window' of W=5 steps propagates this shared advantage to subsequent states to prevent the policy from reverting to purely exploitative behavior immediately after a rollout state. Theoretical analysis via scaffold values shows the polychromic objective provably prevents entropy collapse onto homogeneous action sets (scaffold value becomes negative once a successful action exceeds a probability threshold) while attracting mass toward heterogeneous sets that balance success and diversity.

## Results

On BabyAI and Minigrid, Poly-PPO achieves the highest success rate across all five tasks: 80.2% on Goto, 63.2% on Pickup, 47.0% on Synthseq, 45.2% on Bosslevel, and 92.4% on Four Rooms, consistently outperforming both REINFORCE and PPO. In pass@k evaluations, Poly-PPO's pass rate continues to rise until approximately k=80, whereas baselines saturate around k=20, indicating substantially greater trajectory diversity. On Algorithmic Creativity (Triangle Discovery), Poly-PPO achieves the highest diversity and creativity metrics among all methods, including the pretrained policy, while maintaining validity competitive with PPO. Under large initial-state perturbations, Poly-PPO generalizes most reliably, e.g., achieving 60.6% pass@1 on Goto versus 41.3% for REINFORCE and 21.1% for PPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Poly-PPO outperforms both REINFORCE and PPO on every BabyAI/Minigrid task in both reward and success rate. The margin is largest on Pickup (63.2% vs. 39.8% for REINFORCE and 33.4% for PPO) and Goto (80.2% vs. 73.0% and 46.2% respectively), while on Four Rooms the gap is smaller (92.4% vs. 89.6% and 89.2%) because the task is simpler. Adding a UCB bonus helps REINFORCE and PPO in some environments (e.g., REINFORCE+UCB reaches 56.0% on Pickup) but still falls short of Poly-PPO. In pass@k, Poly-PPO's coverage curve keeps rising to k≈80 while REINFORCE and PPO saturate near k≈20, and at k=160 on Bosslevel Poly-PPO achieves roughly 15% higher pass rate than baselines. On Algorithmic Creativity, Poly-PPO surpasses all baselines (including the pretrained policy) on diversity and creativity, though it trails PPO slightly on validity; REINFORCE achieves high diversity but at a steep cost, with validity@1 falling below the pretrained policy. Under large initial-state perturbations, Poly-PPO generalizes most reliably (e.g., 60.6% vs. 41.3% and 21.1% on Goto; 88.7% vs. 82.7% and 15.3% on Four Rooms), confirming that the diversity it induces translates to robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends PPO (Schulman et al., 2017b) by introducing a 'set RL' framework where the objective is defined over a set of independently sampled trajectories rather than a single trajectory, building on the n-sample objective concept from Tang et al. (2025) but departing from its trajectory-specific baselines in favor of a shared set-level advantage. The polychromic objective, which multiplies per-trajectory reward by a set-level diversity score, is a new loss construction that unifies reward maximization and diversity preservation in a single scalar. For data collection, the method repurposes vine sampling from TRPO (Schulman et al., 2017a) and VinePPO (Kazemnejad et al., 2025) to generate the multiple trajectory sets needed at rollout states, and the entropy analysis extends Cui et al. (2025) from single-trajectory to set-level policy updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses entropy collapse in RLFT—a critical failure mode that limits test-time compute scaling and generalization—by providing both a principled objective (polychromic) and a clean theoretical guarantee (Proposition 5.4) that the objective automatically prevents collapse onto homogeneous action sets once probability mass exceeds a threshold. The simultaneous improvement in both success rate and pass@k coverage across three environments suggests the method avoids the typical accuracy-diversity tradeoff. Key limitations include the requirement for environment resets (ruling out many LLM settings), the computational cost of vine coverage, and the fact that all experiments are on small grid-world and graph tasks rather than LLMs, leaving open whether the approach scales to the high-dimensional, non-resettable settings where RLFT is most impactful. Future work on more efficient gradient estimators, curriculum schemes, and adaptation to LLM fine-tuning would be needed to validate broader applicability.
