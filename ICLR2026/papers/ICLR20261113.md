## Problem

When pretrained VLMs are fine-tuned into vision-language-action (VLA) models, their spatial grounding capabilities rapidly collapse, and naive co-training with spatial data introduces gradient conflicts between perception and action objectives. The paper asks how to preserve and leverage spatial priors (object localization, affordance grounding, trajectory reasoning) during action policy learning so that the model can reliably ground instructions into embodied control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261113/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes two 224×224 RGB views (third-person and wrist-mounted), a natural-language task instruction, and a unified spatial prompt appended to the instruction (e.g., 'Figure out how to execute it, then locate the key object needed'). The VLM planner produces latent spatial planning tokens that condition a DiT action expert, which outputs a 7- or 8-dimensional continuous action vector (joint/end-effector position-orientation deltas plus a binary gripper signal) organized into temporal chunks of size 16 with temporal ensembling at execution time.

## Method

ST4VLA adopts a dual-system architecture where a Qwen2.5-VL-based planner (System 2) generates spatially grounded latent tokens that condition a compact DiT action expert with a DINOv2 encoder (System 1), connected via an 8.7 MB k-layer cross-attention querying transformer. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) spatial grounding pre-training unifies web-scale multimodal data (RefCOCO, LLaVA-OneVision) with robot-specific datasets (RoboRefIt, A0, ST4VLA Data) in a QA format to teach point, box, and trajectory prediction; (2) spatially guided action post-training co-trains on action data augmented with spatial prompts while maintaining next-token prediction on grounding data at a 1:10 loss ratio. A gradient decay factor (0.5) within the querying transformer attenuates backpropagated action gradients to protect the VLM's spatial representations, and the unified spatial prompt—rather than enforcing explicit coordinate outputs—robustly activates spatial attention across diverse manipulation tasks.

## Results

On SimplerEnv, ST4VLA achieves 84.6% (VM) / 75.9% (VA) on Google Robot and 73.2% on WidowX, establishing new state-of-the-art results. On the LIBERO benchmark, ST4VLA reaches 95.9% average success (98.0% Spatial, 99.0% Objects, 93.8% Goal, 92.6% Long), surpassing π0 (94.2%) and GR00T N1 (93.9%). In real-world pick-and-place, ST4VLA achieves 65% average success across eight generalization settings, compared to 31% for π0 and 48% for GR00T N1.5. On the large-scale 200-task Isaac-Sim benchmark, ST4VLA outperforms both π0 and GR00T N1.5 across all four evaluation tracks (in-distribution, unseen objects, new backgrounds, unseen instructions).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ST4VLA outperforms all 13 compared baselines on the SimplerEnv Google Robot benchmark, achieving 84.6% VM (vs. 75.1% for SpatialVLA, 74.8% for CogACT, 58.8% for π0, 35.2% for GR00T N1.5) and 75.9% VA (vs. 70.7% for SpatialVLA, 66.5% for Vanilla Co-training, 54.8% for π0). On WidowX, ST4VLA reaches 73.2%, surpassing GR00T N1.5 (61.9%), π0-FAST (48.3%), and π0 (27.1%) by 11.3, 24.9, and 46.1 points respectively. On LIBERO, ST4VLA (95.9% avg) edges out π0 (94.2%) and GR00T N1 (93.9%), with the largest margin on the Long suite (92.6% vs. 85.2% for π0). In real-world pick-and-place, ST4VLA (65% avg) more than doubles π0 (31%) and substantially outperforms GR00T N1.5 (48%), with the largest gaps on unseen object position (52% vs. 18%/20%) and unseen instruction by attribute (72% vs. 37%/59%). The backbone-agnostic ablation confirms that ST4VLA's gains come from the spatially guided training recipe rather than model capacity: even with the weaker Florence-2 backbone, ST4VLA (67.9%) surpasses GR00T N1.5 (61.9%) on WidowX.

## Relation to Prior Work

ST4VLA extends the InternVLA-M1 framework by introducing a spatially guided two-stage training recipe that explicitly decouples spatial grounding pre-training from embodiment-specific action post-training. It departs from prior hierarchical robot systems (VoxPoser, ReKep, SayPlan) that rely on rule-based task decomposition, and from data-driven VLAs (π0, GR00T, OpenVLA) that directly fine-tune VLMs on action data without preserving spatial priors. The key conceptual departure is the use of lightweight spatial prompting combined with gradient decay to align optimization dynamics between perception and control objectives, rather than treating spatial data as generic co-training supervision or requiring explicit intermediate representations like bounding boxes or trajectories at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, well-ablated training recipe for VLA models that addresses a concrete failure mode (spatial perception collapse during action fine-tuning) with a mechanistic diagnosis (gradient subspace misalignment quantified via PSS). The finding that a critical mass of ~2M spatial grounding pairs is needed before manipulation performance improves sharply offers actionable guidance for data curation. Open questions include whether the dual-system separation limits end-to-end optimization potential, how the approach scales to more complex embodiments (humanoids, mobile manipulation), and whether the spatial prompting strategy generalizes beyond pick-and-place to dexterous or deformable-object tasks. The technique is likely to be widely adopted given its simplicity and backbone-agnostic nature.
