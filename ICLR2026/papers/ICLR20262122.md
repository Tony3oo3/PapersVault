## Problem

Existing fine-grained visual classification methods struggle to simultaneously model part-level fine-grained details and spatial relational features, particularly for non-rigid objects where posture variation destabilizes spatial structures. Current part-based branches adopt a unified strategy across all categories, overlooking the homogeneity of part-level details among similar subcategories, which leads to redundant representations and misclassifications. The paper addresses this by decoupling semantically guided part localization from feature representation and introducing intermediate-grained category semantics to improve inter-class separability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262122/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (448×448 for ResNet-50, 518×518 for ViT-B/14, 384×384 for Swin-B) and produces a fine-grained class label. During training, the image is processed in parallel by a backbone network and ClearCLIP, with textual part prompts (e.g., 'tail of a plane', 'head of a bird') and intermediate-grained category hierarchies (obtained via ChatGPT-4o or dataset-native taxonomies) serving as additional inputs. At inference, only the global branch at the final stage is retained, removing ClearCLIP and part-level branches for a lightweight pipeline.

## Method

PSCL integrates three modules: (1) a Part Localization Module (PLM) that uses ClearCLIP's image-text similarity to generate semantically grounded part masks via argmax over N text prompts followed by morphological refinement, then combines these masks with backbone multi-scale features via Hadamard product to produce part-specific and global feature streams; (2) a Multi-scale Multi-part Branch Progressive Reasoning (MMBPR) module that processes features from low to high levels using encoder layers with class tokens, where a Reverse-key Scale-aware Attention Fusion (ReSAF) mechanism flips key directions to force high-level queries to attend to complementary (not redundant) low-level regions; and (3) a Visual-Language Contrastive Learning module (VLCL-MG) that encodes multi-grained text labels (coarse, two intermediate, and fine-grained categories) via ClearCLIP's text encoder, subtracts the coarse-grained embedding to prevent cross-granularity clustering, and applies a Focal-Smooth Contrastive Loss with stage-dependent label smoothing (ε_s decreasing from 0.4 to 0.1) and increasing loss weights (ε̃_s from 0.1 to 1.0) to progressively sharpen predictions across scales.

## Results

PSCL achieves state-of-the-art or highly competitive accuracy across five FGVC benchmarks using three backbone architectures. With Swin-B (384×384), PSCL reaches 95.3% on FGVC-Aircraft, 95.5% on Stanford Cars, 93.0% on CUB-200-2011, 93.8% on NABirds, and 94.7% on Stanford Dogs. With ViT-B at 518×518, it attains 96.5% on Aircraft and 96.4% on Cars, while with RN50 (448×448) it scores 95.1% on Aircraft and 95.6% on Cars. The largest gains over prior methods appear on non-rigid object datasets (CUB, NAB, DOG) under Transformer backbones, where PSCL outperforms the strongest baselines by 0.4–4.4 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under the Swin-B backbone, PSCL outperforms all 12 baselines on CUB (93.0 vs. 92.6 for CSQA-Net, 92.3 for HERBS), NAB (93.8 vs. 93.0 for HERBS, 92.3 for CSQA-Net), and DOG (94.7 vs. 90.3 for ViT-NeT), while matching CSQA-Net on CAR (95.5 vs. 95.6) and beating it on AIR (95.3 vs. 94.7). With ViT-B at 448×448, PSCL leads on AIR (94.3 vs. 92.2 for MpT-Trans), CAR (95.1 vs. 94.9 for ACC-ViT), CUB (92.2 vs. 92.0 for MpT-Trans), and NAB (92.5 vs. 91.4 for ACC-ViT), but trails TransFG (92.3) and ACC-ViT (92.9) on DOG. Under RN50, PSCL achieves the best AIR (95.1 vs. 94.4 for GDSMP-Net) and CAR (95.6 vs. 95.5 for SIA-Net) scores, yet underperforms P2P-Net (90.2) and SIA-Net (90.7) on CUB (89.1), suggesting the part-localization advantage is less pronounced for CNN backbones on highly non-rigid categories. The largest single-dataset margin is on DOG with Swin-B, where PSCL exceeds the next-best method (ViT-NeT) by 4.4 points, highlighting the benefit of text-guided part selection for non-rigid objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

PSCL extends the progressive multi-scale reasoning lineage established by PMG (Du et al., 2020) and PART (Zhao et al., 2021) by decoupling part localization from feature representation: instead of relying on class activation maps or saliency ranking to select regions, it uses ClearCLIP (Lan et al., 2024) with explicit text prompts to produce semantically grounded, controllable part masks. This repurposing of ClearCLIP—originally designed for open-vocabulary segmentation—into a part-localization component for closed-set FGVC is the paper's central architectural departure. The VLCL-MG module further distinguishes the work by injecting intermediate-grained category concepts (generated via LLMs or expert hierarchies) into the contrastive loss, a strategy not previously explored in FGVC literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical recipe for FGVC practitioners: text-prompted part selection removes the need for part annotations while maintaining semantic consistency across samples, and LLM-generated intermediate hierarchies reduce the annotation burden for multi-grained contrastive learning. However, the approach is fundamentally constrained to single coarse-grained domains with consistent part vocabularies, limiting its applicability to heterogeneous benchmarks like iNaturalist. Open questions include whether the ClearCLIP-based localization generalizes to very small or heavily occluded parts, and whether the intermediate-category structure can be learned end-to-end rather than relying on external LLM or expert curation.
