## Problem

Embodied Chain-of-Thought (ECoT) methods improve VLA performance by generating intermediate reasoning before actions, but this increases inference time by 3–4×, which is prohibitive for real-time robotic manipulation. The paper asks whether the performance gains from CoT training are truly dependent on generating thoughts at test time, or whether the knowledge can be internalized during training so that the model predicts actions directly at standard VLA speed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264598/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 224×224 RGB images, a natural-language task instruction (formatted as 'What should the robot do to {task}?'), and a modality variable (a short text token such as '<act>', '<think>', or '<follow>') that selects which output distribution to use. The primary output is a 7-dimensional action vector [Δx, Δφ, gripper] in delta space; in 'think' mode the model additionally emits intermediate thoughts (subtask descriptions, motion directions, object locations) in language before the action tokens. In 'follow' mode, externally provided thoughts replace the model's own, enabling instruction-following from a human or oracle.

## Method

Hybrid Training (HyT) introduces a modality variable that conditions a single VLA to learn three conditional distributions simultaneously: 'act' (direct action prediction, equivalent to standard VLA), 'think' (predict thoughts then actions, equivalent to ECoT), and 'follow' (condition actions on externally provided thoughts, equivalent to a low-level hierarchical policy). Rather than computing all three losses on every sample, the training objective is estimated via Monte Carlo sampling: at each batch, the modality token, associated thoughts, and actions are sampled with probabilities {w_a=0.25, w_τ=0.5, w_f=0.25}, so the model sees a diverse mixture of conditional inputs/outputs. The key insight is that the 'think' and 'follow' distributions act as auxiliary training signals that improve the model's internal representations (analogous to skilled intuition), so at inference time the model can operate in 'act' mode—predicting actions directly at ~3 Hz on A100 GPUs—while retaining the performance gains from CoT training. The framework is architecture-agnostic and was validated on PaliGemma-2 (ClevrSkills), Prismatic+OFT (LIBERO), and OpenVLA (real-world xArm 6), consistently outperforming standard VLA, ECoT, and hierarchical baselines.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, HyT (built on the OFT recipe) achieves 93.7% average success across four task suites (Spatial 94.0, Object 97.2, Goal 96.2, Long 89.4), outperforming the closest baseline VLA-OFT (92.1%) and all other methods by a wide margin. On ClevrSkills (9 tasks, 3000 demos), HyT outperforms standard VLA, ECoT, and HiRobot at all data scales (300–3000 demos), while maintaining the same ~3 Hz inference speed as standard VLAs (ECoT is 3× slower, HiRobot 4× slower). In real-world experiments on a UFactory xArm 6, HyT achieves 63%±7 overall success versus 41%±7 for OpenVLA, with the gap widening to 54%±10 vs 29%±9 on out-of-distribution tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyT outperforms all 10 compared baselines. On LIBERO, HyT (93.7% avg) beats the closest competitor VLA-OFT (92.1%) by 1.6 points, with the largest gains on the Long suite (+4.6) and Goal suite (+4.8); it surpasses π₀-FAST (85.5%), MolmoAct (86.6%), ThinkAct (84.4%), CoT-VLA (81.1%), OpenVLA (76.5%), Octo (75.1%), and TraceVLA (74.8%) by 7–19 points. On ClevrSkills, HyT consistently outperforms standard VLA (OpenVLA), ECoT, and HiRobot across all data scales, while matching standard VLA inference speed (~3 Hz) versus 3× slower for ECoT and 4× slower for HiRobot. In real-world manipulation, HyT (63%±7) nearly doubles OpenVLA's overall success (41%±7), with the advantage most pronounced on out-of-distribution tasks (54% vs 29%). The 'follow' inference modality further improves HyT's performance when given oracle instructions, a capability not available in any of the compared baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three existing VLA training paradigms—standard VLA (OpenVLA), embodied chain-of-thought (ECoT), and hierarchical VLA (HiRobot)—into a single model via a modality-conditioned training objective called Hybrid Training (HyT). Conceptually, it is closest to DualFormer's idea of randomly dropping reasoning traces during training, but extends this to a robotics setting with a principled probabilistic formulation (Eq. 1–2) and a Monte Carlo sampling implementation. The key departure from ECoT and HiRobot is the hypothesis that CoT's performance gains arise from improved representation learning rather than from the generated thoughts themselves, enabling fast inference without sacrificing accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HyT offers a practical path to deploying VLA models at real-time control frequencies (~3 Hz) while retaining the performance benefits of reasoning-augmented training, which is critical for real-world robotic manipulation. The finding that 'act' and 'think' modes perform similarly on evaluated tasks suggests CoT's primary utility is as a training-time representation regularizer rather than an inference-time necessity, though the authors acknowledge this may not hold for tasks requiring memory or advanced multi-step reasoning. Open questions include whether the modality variable can be switched dynamically during execution, whether thoughts can be extracted automatically (removing the human labelling bottleneck), and whether the internalization effect persists on substantially more complex long-horizon tasks.
