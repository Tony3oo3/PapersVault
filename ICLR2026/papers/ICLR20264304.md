## Problem

Standard Randomized Smoothing applies a single global noise variance to all inputs, creating a fundamental trade-off: small σ yields high certified accuracy at small radii but collapses at large radii, while large σ does the reverse. No global σ can simultaneously achieve strong performance across the full radius spectrum. Prior input-dependent approaches either require test-time memorization, severely restrict adaptivity, or systematically over-estimate the optimal variance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image (32×32 CIFAR-10 or 224×224 ImageNet) and produces a class label together with a certified ℓ₂ radius. Unusually, the noise variance applied during smoothing is not fixed but predicted per-input from a discrete candidate set Σ (e.g., {0.25, 0.5, 1.0} for CIFAR-10). The final certified radius is the minimum of two independently certified radii: one certifying that the predicted σ is locally constant (R_σ) and one certifying the classification (R_c), with total failure probability bounded by α via a union bound.

## Method

The key theoretical contribution proves that RS certification remains valid when the noise variance is input-dependent, provided it is locally constant within the certified region, by adapting the Lipschitz-continuity argument of Salman et al. (2019) to remove the global-constancy assumption. The dual RS framework instantiates this with two RS models: a lightweight variance estimator (e.g., ResNet-110) smoothed with a fixed global σ_e that predicts the optimal σ from a discrete set Σ, and a standard diffusion denoised smoothing classifier (ViT or BEiT) smoothed with the estimated σ_c(x). The variance estimator is trained with soft cross-entropy labels derived from certified radii under each σ candidate, combined with consistency regularization to enlarge R_σ; the classifier is then finetuned under the input-dependent variances predicted by the trained estimator. At inference, the estimator's prediction is certified via RS to guarantee local constancy of σ, and the final certified radius is min(R_σ, R_c). The framework also admits a routing interpretation where the variance estimator selects among a pool of pretrained expert RS models specialized for different noise levels.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, Dual RS with adaptive finetuning achieves 45.27% certified accuracy at radius 0.5, 34.15% at radius 0.75, and 24.68% at radius 1.0, outperforming the state-of-the-art input-dependent method Multiscale (39.15%, 28.46%, 21.33%) by 15.6%, 20.0%, and 15.7% respectively. On ImageNet, Dual RS achieves 60.6% at radius 0.5, 48.0% at radius 1.0, and 33.6% at radius 1.5, surpassing Multiscale (55.8%, 41.0%, 30.8%) by 8.6%, 17.1%, and 9.1%. The method incurs only ~60% computational overhead at inference (22.58s vs 14.07s per input on a single RTX 4090) compared to standard RS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Carlini et al. (2023) (off-the-shelf diffusion denoised smoothing with a single global σ), Dual RS achieves a superior accuracy-robustness trade-off: no single global σ matches Dual RS across all radii, as small σ (0.25) collapses to 0% beyond radius 0.75 while large σ (1.0) sacrifices 20+ points at small radii. Against Multiscale (Jeong & Shin, 2024), the state-of-the-art input-dependent RS method, Dual RS consistently wins at most radii on both CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, with the largest gains at intermediate radii (20.0% at r=0.75 on CIFAR-10, 17.1% at r=1.0 on ImageNet). At very large radii (≥2.0 on CIFAR-10), Multiscale slightly edges out Dual RS (e.g., 5.31% vs 6.65% at r=2.0), but Dual RS maintains non-zero certified accuracy where global-σ baselines fail entirely. In the routing experiment, Dual RS built on Sun et al. (2025)'s strong expert for large radii further improves the large-radius performance, demonstrating that the framework can flexibly incorporate off-the-shelf expert models without retraining the classifier.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the foundational RS certification theorem of Cohen et al. (2019) by proving that the global noise variance assumption can be relaxed to local constancy, using the Lipschitz-continuity proof technique from Salman et al. (2019). It departs from prior input-dependent RS approaches (Wang et al. 2021, Alfarra et al. 2022) that require test-time memorization, and from Multi-scale RS (Jeong & Shin 2024) which systematically over-estimates the optimal variance by always selecting the largest certifying σ. The key conceptual departure is using a second RS model to certify the local constancy of the variance estimator itself, creating a self-contained dual-model framework that avoids both memorization and restricted adaptivity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper breaks the fundamental accuracy-robustness trade-off inherent to global noise variance in RS, achieving 15–20% certified accuracy gains at intermediate radii (0.5–1.0) on CIFAR-10 and 9–17% on ImageNet, while adding only ~60% inference overhead. The routing perspective—where the variance estimator selects among off-the-shelf expert RS models—opens a practical path to combining specialized models without retraining a single classifier for all noise levels. Limitations include the fixed 60% overhead, the discrete candidate set Σ that must be chosen a priori, and the fact that the framework is upper-bounded by the best expert within each routed region. Open questions include extending the routing idea to deterministic certification methods and whether continuous (rather than discrete) variance estimation could further tighten the certified radius.
