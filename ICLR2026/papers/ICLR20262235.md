## Problem

Current gene expression prediction methods focus on extending input sequence length to capture distal regulatory elements, but the paper demonstrates that long-sequence modeling actually degrades performance (Caduceus drops after 2k bps; Seq2Exp at 200k performs no better than 500 bps). The overlooked issue is that multimodal epigenomic signals are naively concatenated without accounting for their distinct biological roles: foreground signals (H3K27ac) directly mark active regulatory elements, while background signals (DNase-seq, Hi-C) reflect pervasive chromatin accessibility and 3D organization that introduce confounding effects, causing models to learn spurious correlations between accessibility and expression.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2k bps DNA sequence (one-hot encoded, centered at the transcription start site) and per-position epigenomic signal tracks (H3K27ac, DNase-seq, Hi-C) of shape L×d, and predicts a single CAGE expression value. Notably, Prism operates on only 2k bps input—100× shorter than the 200k sequences used by most baselines—demonstrating that proximal epigenomic signals already encode the regulatory influence of distal elements through chromatin looping. Additional features (mRNA half-life, promoter activity) are simply concatenated to the final linear prediction head and are not part of the core epigenomic modeling.

## Method

Prism introduces a causal-intervention framework built on a Caduceus (SSM) backbone with three components: a linear signal encoder g_θ that maps raw epigenomic tracks to high-dimensional features H, a lightweight 1D-CNN confounder encoder g_ω that learns n gene-wise weight vectors (each representing a distinct background chromatin state), and the predictor h_φ. The key mechanism is backdoor adjustment: the interventional prediction averages the backbone's output under n weighted feature combinations (H ⊙ a_i), effectively marginalizing over learned background states to isolate the direct causal effect H→Y from the confounding pathway H←C→Y. Training combines three losses: standard Huber prediction loss (L1), an interventional regularization loss (L2) that encourages robustness across background states, and a uniform diversity loss (L3) that prevents the learned weight vectors from collapsing. The entire confounder encoder adds only 11K parameters, and the approach achieves SOTA performance using short sequences while adding minimal computational overhead.

## Results

On K562, Prism achieves MSE 0.1789, MAE 0.3000, and Pearson 0.8751, outperforming the previous SOTA Seq2Exp-soft (MSE 0.1856, MAE 0.3054, Pearson 0.8723). On GM12878, Prism reaches MSE 0.1759, MAE 0.3054, and Pearson 0.9016, again surpassing Seq2Exp-soft (MSE 0.1873, MAE 0.3137, Pearson 0.8951). Notably, Prism uses only 2k bp input sequences while most baselines (Seq2Exp, Caduceus w/ signals) use 200k bp, and adds just 11K trainable parameters over the Caduceus backbone (585K vs. 574K total).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Prism outperforms all nine baselines across all six metrics (MSE, MAE, Pearson × K562/GM12878). Against the previous SOTA Seq2Exp-soft, Prism improves MSE by 0.0067 (K562) and 0.0114 (GM12878), and Pearson by 0.0028 and 0.0065 respectively, while using 100× shorter input sequences (2k vs. 200k bp). Compared to Caduceus w/ signals (the strongest non-SOTA baseline with epigenomic integration), Prism reduces MSE from 0.1959 to 0.1789 on K562 and from 0.1942 to 0.1759 on GM12878. Against EPInformer, which uses DNase-seq peaks and attention over enhancer candidates, Prism achieves substantially lower MSE (0.1789 vs. 0.2140 on K562). On the additional H1 cell line, Prism (Pearson 0.6844) also surpasses Seq2Exp-soft (0.6595) and Caduceus w/ signal (0.6681), confirming generalizability. The paper further shows that Seq2Exp's 200k-length performance is essentially equivalent to using only 500 bp, undermining the long-sequence modeling narrative that motivates most baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Caduceus (Schiff et al., 2024) as its SSM backbone and follows Seq2Exp (Su et al., 2025) in experimental protocol and signal encoder design, but fundamentally departs from the field's prevailing direction of extending input sequence length. Its core methodological novelty is transplanting the causal intervention framework from computer vision (Qiang et al., 2022) — learnable weight vectors representing background states combined with backdoor adjustment — into the genomic domain to handle confounding in multimodal epigenomic signals. Rather than proposing a new architecture, Prism adds a lightweight 1D-CNN confounder encoder (11K parameters) and a uniformity loss on top of the existing Caduceus pipeline, reframing the problem from 'model longer sequences' to 'integrate proximal signals while controlling for background chromatin confounders.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a central assumption in the gene expression prediction community — that longer input sequences are necessary to capture distal regulatory elements — by showing that current SSM-based models actually degrade with length and that 2k bps suffices when epigenomic signals are properly integrated. The identification of confounding effects in background signals (DNase-seq, Hi-C) that create spurious correlations is a biologically meaningful insight that prior concatenation-based approaches overlooked. Limitations include the simplified SCM (uniform distribution over confounder states, only n=2 states in the main model), evaluation on only 2–3 cell lines, and the fact that the causal framework is borrowed rather than derived from first principles of gene regulation. Open questions include whether the learned confounder states correspond to interpretable chromatin states (e.g., ChromHMM annotations) and whether the approach generalizes to single-cell or multi-tissue settings where confounding structure may differ substantially.
