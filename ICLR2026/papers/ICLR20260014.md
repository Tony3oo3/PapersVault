## Problem

Existing outlier detection methods are dataset-specific: each new dataset requires separate model training, hyperparameter tuning, and model selection, incurring high computational cost and wasting transferable knowledge from historical labeled datasets. This is especially problematic for deep-learning-based OD methods whose optimal hyperparameters vary considerably across datasets and domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260014/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, UniOD takes a single unlabeled tabular dataset of arbitrary feature dimensionality and heterogeneous semantics, and produces a per-point outlier score (the softmax probability of the outlier class) without any retraining or hyperparameter tuning. During training, it consumes M labeled historical datasets from diverse domains (each with its own dimensionality and sample size), augmented by 5 random 60%-subsampled copies per dataset to improve generalization. The unified feature dimension is fixed at d=256 regardless of the original dataset's feature count.

## Method

UniOD converts each dataset into K multi-scale Gaussian-kernel similarity matrices (with bandwidths σ_k = β_k·σ̄), then applies SVD to extract uniformly dimensioned node features, making the representation independent of original feature dimensionality and semantics. The architecture processes these graph-structured representations through K parallel Graph Isomorphism Networks (capturing local within-dataset structure via message passing) and K parallel Graph Transformers (capturing global between-dataset patterns via self-attention), concatenates their outputs, and feeds them into a 3-layer MLP classifier trained with cross-entropy loss. A key design choice is that the similarity matrices serve dual roles: they define the graph adjacency for GINs while their SVD eigenvectors provide the node features, preserving structural information that a plain MLP on raw features would discard. The paper provides a generalization bound (Theorem 4.1) showing the test-training error gap scales as O(1/√(Mn)) and depends only mildly on K, confirming that more historical datasets tighten the bound while additional bandwidths primarily reduce training error.

## Results

On 30 ADBench datasets split into two groups of 15, UniOD achieves the best average AUROC of 78.93% (Group I) and 78.52% (Group II), and the best average AUPRC of 45.43% and 36.69% respectively, outperforming the second-best method kNN (76.00% / 78.45% AUROC) by approximately 3% on Group I. UniOD also requires no per-dataset hyperparameter tuning (0 hyperparameters vs. 2–5 for competing deep methods) and achieves the lowest inference time (240 s) among all deep-learning baselines (384–1391 s). Cross-validation with swapped training/testing groups confirms robustness, with UniOD maintaining top average performance in both directions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniOD outperforms all 11 baselines on average AUROC and AUPRC across both dataset groups. Against the strongest traditional baselines, UniOD beats kNN (78.93 vs. 76.00 AUROC on Group I) and ECOD (78.93 vs. 73.90) by 2–5 points, while KDE (74.55) trails by over 4 points. Among deep-learning methods, UniOD substantially surpasses ICL (55.48), DPAD (62.29), SLAD (64.77), DTE-NP (73.75), NeutralAD (64.29), DSVDD (70.22), and AE (70.78) in average AUROC, with gaps of 5–23 points. Against the two baselines that also exploit historical labeled data—KPCA+MLP (66.13) and MLP+TF (69.24)—UniOD leads by 12.8 and 9.7 AUROC points respectively, demonstrating that its graph-structured SVD representation and GNN/GT architecture extract substantially more transferable signal than kernel PCA or transformer-based feature projection. MetaOD (75.45), a model-selection approach, is the closest competitor but still trails UniOD by 3.5 AUROC points; notably, UniOD requires zero hyperparameters at test time whereas MetaOD depends on exhaustive grid-search records. On individual datasets UniOD is not always best (e.g., KPCA+MLP wins on Cardio. with 97.21 vs. 51.20, and MetaOD wins on optdigits with 87.22 vs. 73.80), but its consistent top-average performance across 30 heterogeneous datasets underscores its universality.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniOD builds on Graph Isomorphism Networks (Xu et al., 2018) for within-dataset message passing and introduces Graph Transformers for global attention, combining both into a dual-branch GNN architecture. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant per-dataset OD paradigm (where each dataset requires separate model training and hyperparameter tuning) by reframing outlier detection as node classification on graph-structured data derived from multi-scale Gaussian similarity matrices with SVD-based dimension unification. It also distinguishes itself from prior transfer-learning-for-OD work (e.g., LOCIT) by not requiring domain similarity or matched feature spaces, and from meta-learning/hyperparameter-selection methods (MetaOD, HPOD) by actually learning a universal detector rather than predicting optimal configurations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment: a single trained model eliminates per-dataset retraining and hyperparameter search, reducing inference time (240s vs. 384–1391s for deep baselines) and removing the need for domain expertise in tuning. The theoretical generalization bound (Theorem 4.1) provides rare formal guarantees for a multi-dataset GNN-based OD framework. However, limitations remain: UniOD requires labeled historical datasets, is primarily designed for the transductive setting, and its empirical gains over strong simple baselines (kNN, KDE) are modest (~3% AUROC) and inconsistent across datasets (e.g., underperforming on Cardio, fault, InternetAds). Open questions include scalability to very large or streaming datasets, performance in the inductive setting, and whether the multi-scale similarity construction can be made more efficient for high-dimensional inputs.
