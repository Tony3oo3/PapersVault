## Problem

Existing Gaussian-based inverse rendering methods query indirect radiance from NVS-pretrained Gaussian primitives, but these primitives are supervised only towards limited training viewpoints, leaving indirect radiances from unobserved directions unconstrained. This lack of physical supervision leads to inaccurate inter-reflection modeling and incorrect decomposition of material and illumination properties, such as baking indirect lighting effects into surface albedo.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261839/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images as input and jointly learns a 32-resolution environment cubemap alongside per-surfel material parameters (albedo, roughness) and view-dependent radiance encoded in spherical harmonics. Outputs include decomposed scene representations (albedo, normal, roughness maps), novel view synthesis, and relit images under arbitrary lighting. Relighting is available in two modes: ray-tracing-based (~38 ms/frame) and a finetuning-based approach that adapts surfel radiances in ~2 minutes and renders in under 6 ms/frame.

## Method

The core contribution is radiometric consistency loss, a physically-based constraint that minimizes the L1 residual between each Gaussian surfel's learned outgoing radiance (queried from SH coefficients) and its physically-based rendered radiance computed via Monte Carlo integration of the rendering equation. Visibility and indirect radiance are obtained through differentiable 2D Gaussian ray tracing, which shares the same ray-splat intersection as the 2DGS rasterizer, enabling a fully differentiable self-correcting feedback loop: PBR radiance supervises surfel radiances for unobserved directions, while view-constrained surfel radiances propagate supervision into the indirect illumination term. Training proceeds in two stages—initialization with a split-sum approximation for stability, followed by full Monte Carlo radiometric consistency with 4096 sampled surfels and 64 incident rays per surfel per iteration. For relighting, a finetuning strategy minimizes only the radiometric consistency loss on surfel SH coefficients under the new environment map, enabling real-time rendering from adapted radiances without per-frame ray tracing.

## Results

On the TensoIR dataset, RadioGS achieves the best NVS PSNR of 37.86 dB, normal MAE of 3.689°, albedo PSNR of 31.05 dB, and relighting PSNR of 32.09 dB, outperforming all Gaussian-based and NeRF-based baselines across these metrics. On Synthetic4Relight, RadioGS leads in NVS (34.98 dB), albedo (30.69 dB), and relighting (34.87 dB) PSNR, though it trails IRGS slightly on roughness MSE (0.011 vs. 0.008). The finetuning-based relighting variant (Ours*) achieves 31.41 dB relighting PSNR with sub-10 ms per-frame rendering, compared to 32.09 dB for the ray-tracing-based variant at ~39 ms. Total training time is approximately 1 hour (30 min initialization + 30 min inverse rendering) on an RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RadioGS outperforms all six baselines on the TensoIR dataset across NVS, normal, albedo, and relighting metrics. Against the closest Gaussian-based competitors, IRGS (35.43 dB NVS, 30.62 dB albedo, 29.91 dB relight) and SVG-IR (36.71 dB NVS, 30.48 dB albedo, 31.10 dB relight), RadioGS gains +2.43 dB NVS PSNR, +0.57 dB albedo PSNR, and +1.0 dB relighting PSNR over SVG-IR, and +2.43 dB NVS, +0.43 dB albedo, and +2.18 dB relight over IRGS. The NeRF-based TensoIR baseline (35.09 dB NVS, 28.58 dB relight) is surpassed by a wider margin but requires 4× longer training. On Synthetic4Relight, RadioGS leads IRGS by +0.54 dB NVS, +0.19 dB albedo, and +0.52 dB relight PSNR, and SVG-IR by +0.84 dB NVS, +1.63 dB albedo, and +2.28 dB relight PSNR; however, IRGS achieves a lower roughness MSE (0.008 vs. 0.011), indicating RadioGS's roughness estimation is slightly less accurate. The finetuning-based relighting (Ours*) trades ~0.7 dB relighting PSNR for a 6.5× speedup over the ray-tracing variant (5.9 ms vs. 38.6 ms per frame), while still outperforming SVG-IR's 82.5 ms rendering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Gaussian Splatting inverse rendering line (GS-IR, IRGS, SVG-IR) by introducing a physically-based supervision signal—radiometric consistency—that addresses a shared weakness: NVS-pretrained Gaussian primitives lack supervision on unobserved directions, leading to arbitrary indirect radiances. The core idea is directly inspired by self-training neural radiance caches (Müller et al. 2021; Hadadan et al. 2021), which minimize the rendering-equation residual to self-correct radiance estimates, but is transplanted to 2D Gaussian surfels and integrated with differentiable 2D Gaussian ray tracing (from IRGS). The framework uses 2DGS as its geometric backbone and Disney BRDF for material modeling, positioning itself as a physically-grounded upgrade over prior GS-based methods that treat indirect illumination as learnable residuals or query it from fixed precomputed volumes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The radiometric consistency loss provides a principled, self-correcting mechanism for supervising indirect illumination on unobserved views, which is a fundamental gap in current GS-based inverse rendering. The finetuning-based relighting strategy (adapting surfel radiances in ~2 minutes, then rendering at ~6ms) is practically significant for real-time applications on consumer hardware. However, the method is limited to dielectric materials, and extending radiometric consistency to anisotropic or highly-reflective surfaces remains an open challenge. The view-scarcity ablation (only 0.17dB indirect PSNR drop at 25% views vs. 2.21dB without the loss) suggests the approach could be valuable in data-limited settings, but this has not been explored further.
