## Problem

Existing text-to-video models produce visually plausible 5–10 second clips but cannot generate coherent, multi-scene narrative films over extended durations. Scaling to longer videos causes exploding context lengths, visual drift, and loss of character/scene identity, leaving a critical gap between short clip synthesis and story-driven movie generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262813/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a high-level narrative prompt (e.g., a movie storyline), which is expanded by an LLM into shot-level captions, then consumed as interleaved text-image pairs for keyframe generation at 400×800 resolution and interleaved text-video pairs for video synthesis at ~230,040 total pixels (480×480 area). The output is a complete multi-scene short movie: a sequence of coherent keyframes (up to 48 interleaved pairs) followed by full spatio-temporal video interpolating motion between those keyframes, with sustained character identity and scene consistency across the entire production.

## Method

Captain Cinema decomposes movie generation into two stages: (1) a top-down interleaved keyframe planner that fine-tunes Flux 1.Dev with a hybrid attention masking scheme—block-diagonal masks in early double-stream blocks keep computation local, while full (training) or causal (inference) masks in later single-stream blocks aggregate global context across all image-text pairs; and (2) a bottom-up video synthesizer that fine-tunes Seaweed-3B with multi-keyframe interleaved conditioning to fill motion between generated keyframes. The key architectural innovation is GoldenMem, which compresses long visual history using inverse-Fibonacci downsampling (s_i = ⌊s_{i-1}/φ⌋), bounding total conditioning tokens to ~1.62× a single frame regardless of context length, combined with CLIP-based semantic-oriented retrieval to select the most relevant historical frames rather than relying on temporal proximity. Training employs progressive long-context fine-tuning (1→8→16→32 pairs) to avoid collapse in the distilled Flux base model, plus dynamic stride sampling to multiply effective data diversity by ~100× and prevent overfitting on the ~500-hour movie dataset.

## Results

On VBench-2.0 metrics, Captain Cinema achieves the best scores across most dimensions: Visual Aesthetic 57.2, Visual Quality 61.7, Temporal Dynamic 65.4, and Semantic Text Quality 26.1, outperforming both IC-LoRA and LCT. In the user study (4-point scale), it scores 3.3 on semantic alignment and 3.7 on overall quality, the highest among all methods. In the long-context stress test (8→48 interleaved pairs), Captain Cinema retains >93% of its consistency at 48 pairs, while LCT's character consistency drops from 4.3 to 3.1 and scene consistency collapses from 3.5 to 0.7 over the same range.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: IC-LoRA (Huang et al., 2024a) combined with I2V, and LCT (Guo et al., 2025). Captain Cinema outperforms IC-LoRA on every metric, with the largest gaps in user-study semantic alignment (3.3 vs. 1.5) and temporal dynamic (65.4 vs. 61.1). Against LCT, Captain Cinema wins on visual aesthetic (+1.0), visual quality (+1.8), temporal dynamic (+13.6), text-semantic quality (+2.2), and both user-study scores, but trails on temporal consistency (91.0 vs. 94.8) — a metric the authors note is inflated for LCT because its clips contain very low motion. In the long-context stress test, the gap widens dramatically: at 24 pairs, LCT's scene consistency collapses to 0.7 and identity consistency to 0.14, while Captain Cinema maintains scene consistency at 3.8 and identity at 0.42, and at 48 pairs (where LCT is not tested) Captain Cinema still retains 93%+ of its 8-pair consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

Captain Cinema builds on three pillars: Flux 1.Dev's double-stream/single-stream MM-DiT architecture (adapted with hybrid attention masking for interleaved keyframe planning), Seaweed-3B as the video synthesis backbone, and LCT's long-context tuning paradigm for MM-DiT video generation. The key conceptual departure is from LCT's single-scene long-video setting to multi-scene movie generation, where narrative coherence must be maintained across scene cuts rather than within a continuous shot. The GoldenMem compression mechanism is positioned as a gentler alternative to FramePack's more aggressive exponential downsampling, and the semantic-oriented CLIP retrieval for context selection departs from purely temporal conditioning used in prior interleaved generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is among the first frameworks to produce multi-scene, narratively coherent video (rather than image sequences or single-clip extensions), and the GoldenMem technique offers a principled, theoretically bounded solution to the super-long context problem that plagues current video generation. However, the evaluation relies on only two baselines (LCT, IC-LoRA) and a single LLM judge (Gemini 2.5 Flash), limiting confidence in the magnitude of claimed advantages. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly feature-length content, whether the dependency on an external LLM (Gemini) for storyline composition can be internalized, and whether the 500-hour training corpus is sufficient for robust generalization across diverse cinematic styles.
