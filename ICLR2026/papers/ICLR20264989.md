## Problem

Offline goal-conditioned RL struggles with long-horizon tasks because TD methods accumulate compounding errors over extended horizons while MC methods lack optimality guarantees. No prior method effectively combines multistep value propagation with quasimetric distance architectures, and no end-to-end offline GCRL method has demonstrated multistep stitching in real-world robotic manipulation from unlabeled visual data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264989/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes either low-dimensional state vectors or 64×64×3 RGB observations paired with a goal state or goal image, and produces a goal-conditioned policy π(a|s,g) that outputs continuous actions. Internally, it learns a quasimetric distance d(s,g) parameterized by a Metric Residual Network over learned representations, from which Q and V functions are derived as V_g(g)·exp(−d). The policy is extracted by minimizing the learned distance via DDPG+BC, with the BC coefficient α tuned per environment.

## Method

MQE replaces the standard one-step TD backup with a multistep regression: it samples a waypoint s_t^w between the current state and goal using a mixture of a geometric distribution (capped at K) and a Bernoulli probability p of stepping only one timestep, then regresses exp(−d((s,a),g)) toward γ^{k'}·exp(−d(s_t^w,g)) using a LINEX Bregman divergence to avoid vanishing gradients. An action-invariance loss enforces d(ψ(s),φ(s,a))≈0 via a smooth squared-exponential objective, ensuring the value function equals the max over actions without collapsing to a trivial zero solution. The policy is extracted by minimizing the learned quasimetric distance with a DDPG+BC objective. A key theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that in the tabular setting, the extracted policy achieves V_g^π(s) ≥ V_g^{π_β}(s), i.e., guaranteed policy improvement over the behavior policy, by combining the multistep regression with a quasimetric projection operator that enforces the triangle inequality.

## Results

On OGBench (95 tasks across state-based and pixel-based environments), MQE achieves an overall success rate of 36.3%, substantially outperforming the next-best method HIQL (18.7%) and all other baselines (TMD 13.0%, GCIQL 11.8%, QRL 9.8%, CMD 8.7%, n-SAC+BC 8.5%, CRL 6.2%). On the most challenging long-horizon stitch tasks, MQE is the only method with nonzero success on antmaze-colossal-stitch (27.6%) and achieves a 10× improvement over prior methods on humanoidmaze-giant-stitch (26.5% vs. 6.3% for TMD). On real-world BridgeData manipulation, MQE is the only method to achieve nonzero success on quadruple pick-and-place (2/15) and the best on open-drawer-and-place (5/15 vs. 1/15 for TRA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MQE decisively outperforms all seven OGBench baselines on the overall success rate (36.3% vs. 18.7% for HIQL, the strongest competitor), with particularly large margins on long-horizon stitch tasks where TD-based methods (GCIQL, n-SAC+BC) and contrastive MC methods (CRL, CMD) collapse to near-zero success. The closest competitor, HIQL (which uses explicit policy horizon reduction), wins on two visual manipulation tasks (visual_puzzle_4x4_play: 60.1% vs. 17.9%; visual_cube_double_noisy: 59.4% vs. 25.9%), but MQE dominates on all locomotion and state-based manipulation tasks. TMD, the most closely related prior method (same quasimetric architecture but single-step TD updates), is outperformed by MQE on every OGBench task, with the gap widening as horizon length increases (e.g., 35.1% vs. 2.7% on antmaze_giant_stitch). On BridgeData, MQE is the only method achieving nonzero success on quadruple pick-and-place (2/15) and substantially outperforms TRA (1/15) and GCIQL (0/15) on the dependent open-drawer-and-place task (5/15), while GCBC and GCIQL fail entirely on all compositional tasks beyond single pick-and-place.

## Relation to Prior Work

MQE sits at the intersection of two previously separated lines of work in offline GCRL: quasimetric distance learning (QRL, Wang et al. 2023) which uses single-step TD bootstrapping under an MRN architecture, and contrastive Monte-Carlo methods (CMD, TMD) that perform global value propagation via future-state classification. The key departure is that MQE replaces both single-step TD updates and contrastive losses with a multistep Monte-Carlo backup (Eq. 9) that regresses the quasimetric distance toward a geometrically-sampled waypoint, effectively performing both local and global value propagation simultaneously. It also departs from hierarchical approaches (HIQL) by requiring no explicit subgoal decomposition, and from TMD by using a smoother action-invariance loss (Eq. 14) that avoids the trivial-collapse failure mode of L1/L2 regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MQE is the first end-to-end offline GCRL method to demonstrate multistep compositional stitching in real-world robotic manipulation (BridgeData), a capability previously requiring hierarchical planners or explicit subgoal decomposition. The practical impact is significant for offline RL practitioners: the method requires no additional architectural components beyond a single quasimetric critic and a DDPG+BC policy, yet achieves 10× improvements on long-horizon stitch tasks and is the only method with nonzero success on 4000-step mazes. Open questions include whether the heuristic waypoint sampling (Eq. 8) generalizes to environments outside the evaluated range, how the method interacts with action-chunking policies, and whether the theoretical policy-improvement guarantee (Theorem 1, tabular setting) extends to the function-approximation regime used in practice.
