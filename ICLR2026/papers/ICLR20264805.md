## Problem

Knowledge distillation suffers from two persistent optimization pathologies: gradient conflict, where task and distillation gradients point in opposing directions, and gradient dominance, where one loss's gradient magnitude suppresses the other. Existing KD methods rely on fixed hyperparameter weights (α₁, α₂) to balance these losses, which cannot adapt to the rapidly shifting gradient dynamics in teacher-student training and often lead to suboptimal or unstable convergence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264805/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input and produces either class labels (ImageNet-1K, CIFAR-100) or bounding boxes with class scores (COCO). Both teacher and student networks process the same input image simultaneously; the teacher's intermediate features are projected onto the student's feature space via lightweight linear projectors at multiple scales, enabling feature-level alignment without architectural modification. Student models range from 5M (DeiT-Tiny) to 61M (ViDT-Small) parameters, distilled from teachers up to 0.1B parameters.

## Method

DTO-KD formulates knowledge distillation as a multi-objective optimization problem seeking a Pareto-optimal solution for the loss vector (L_distill, L_task), rather than a fixed weighted sum. The key mechanism is a closed-form solution for per-iteration loss weights π₁*, π₂* derived from the Gram matrix of the log-gradient vectors ∇log(L_distill) and ∇log(L_task), which guarantees the combined update direction is simultaneously aligned with both objectives (resolving gradient conflict) and contributes equally to both losses (resolving gradient dominance). In practice, an amortized variant updates π via a single gradient step on the norm of the weighted log-loss vector, avoiding the cost of a second backpropagation pass. The student architecture uses a Swin Transformer backbone with a lightweight decoder, and teacher-student features are aligned through scale-specific projectors, making the method applicable to both CNN-to-transformer and transformer-to-transformer distillation settings.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, DTO-KD achieves 79.7% top-1 accuracy with DeiT-Tiny (6M params) and 83.1% with DeiT-Small (22M params), both distilled from RegNetY-160 in 300 epochs. On COCO object detection, DTO-KD reaches 43.7 AP (nano), 47.4 AP (tiny), and 49.6 AP (small), surpassing the previous best VkD by 0.7, 0.5, and 1.1 AP respectively. On CIFAR-100, DTO-KD sets new SOTA across all six homogeneous and heterogeneous teacher-student pairs, e.g., 72.35% (ResNet-56→ResNet-20) and 78.22% (ResNet-32×4→ShuffleNet-V2). Notably, DTO-KD-Small (83.1%) exceeds the non-distilled DeiT-Small (79.8%) by 3.3 pp and even surpasses the 1000-epoch KD baseline (82.6%) with only 300 epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTO-KD outperforms all 19 compared baselines across every benchmark. On ImageNet-1K, it beats the strongest prior method VkD (Roy Miles & Deng, CVPR'24) by 1.4 pp (Ti: 79.7 vs 78.3) and 0.8 pp (S: 83.1 vs 82.3), and surpasses the 1000-epoch DeiT KD baseline by 3.1 pp (Ti) and 0.5 pp (S) using only 300 epochs. On CIFAR-100, DTO-KD edges out ReviewKD++ (Wang et al., 2024) by 0.3 pp in the hardest homogeneous setting (ResNet-56→ResNet-20: 72.35 vs 72.05) and by 0.29 pp in the heterogeneous ShuffleNet-V2 setting (78.22 vs 77.93). On COCO detection, DTO-KD improves over Token-Matching (Song et al., ICLR'22) by 1.8–2.4 AP and over VkD (Roy Miles & Deng, CVPR'24) by 0.7–1.1 AP across nano/tiny/small variants. The gains are consistent across teacher scales: even with a smaller ViDT-small teacher, DTO-KD achieves 43.2 AP (nano) and 46.9 AP (tiny), still exceeding all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the multi-objective optimization framework of FAMO (Liu et al., 2023) to knowledge distillation, deriving a closed-form solution for the optimal per-iteration weighting of distillation and task gradients. It departs from the dominant paradigm in KD—where practitioners manually tune fixed loss weights (α₁, α₂) or rely on heuristic scheduling—by formulating the balancing problem as a gradient-level min-max optimization with Pareto-optimality guarantees. The work also situates itself within the broader multi-task learning literature (PCGrad, CAGrad, IMTL) but is the first to explicitly target the gradient conflict and dominance issues specific to teacher-student distillation, where architectural mismatch makes the two objectives particularly prone to misalignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for manual loss-weight tuning and providing theoretical guarantees (gradient alignment, equal contribution, bounded update norms), DTO-KD offers a principled drop-in replacement for the ad-hoc weighting that plagues most KD pipelines. The practical payoff is faster convergence (reaching VKD's best accuracy in 240 vs. 300 epochs) and consistent SOTA gains across classification and detection. However, the method still requires access to training data and per-task gradients, leaving data-free distillation and distillation from proprietary black-box teachers as open challenges. Whether the amortized (single-backprop) approximation fully recovers the closed-form solution's theoretical properties at scale remains to be validated.
