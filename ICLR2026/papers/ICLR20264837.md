## Problem

Diffusion model inference is slow due to repeated forward passes over 20–50 denoising steps. Existing training-free caching methods rely on hand-crafted heuristics that yield only a few discrete speed-quality operating points, require per-model hyperparameter tuning, and generalize poorly across architectures. The paper asks whether an automated, optimization-driven search can discover a smooth Pareto frontier of caching schedules that outperforms these heuristics while remaining model-agnostic.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264837/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the optimization phase, ECAD consumes a small set of 100 text-only calibration prompts and a quality metric (Image Reward) to produce a set of binary caching schedules forming a Pareto frontier over quality and MACs. For inference, the method takes a text prompt and produces a generated RGB image (256×256 or 1024×1024) in 20 diffusion steps, with the selected caching schedule determining which transformer components (self-attention, cross-attention, feedforward) are recomputed versus reused at each timestep. No reference images, weight modifications, or additional training data are required.

## Method

ECAD reframes diffusion caching as a multi-objective Pareto optimization over a binary tensor S ∈ {0,1}^{N×B×C}, where each entry specifies whether to recompute or reuse a cached component at a given block and timestep. A genetic algorithm (NSGA-II) evolves a population of these binary schedules using tournament selection, 4-point crossover, and bit-flip mutation, with fitness defined jointly by Image Reward (quality) and MACs (cost). The algorithm requires no gradients or weight updates, runs asynchronously on a single GPU, and can be seeded with heuristic schedules from prior work or initialized uniformly across the compute-cost spectrum. The resulting Pareto frontier provides practitioners with a continuous spectrum of speed-quality trade-offs, and the learned schedules generalize across resolutions (256→1024) and model variants (PixArt-α→PixArt-Σ) without re-optimization.

## Results

On PixArt-α at 256×256 with 20 steps, ECAD's 'fastest' schedule achieves 2.58× speedup (64.24 ms/img) with COCO FID of 19.54 and MJHQ FID of 8.67, outperforming the unaccelerated baseline (COCO FID 24.84) and all prior caching methods. On FLUX.1-dev, ECAD's 'fast' schedule at 2.58× speedup (1016.59 ms/img) matches the baseline Image Reward (1.04) while reducing COCO FID from 25.76 to 21.61 and MJHQ FID from 17.77 to 16.14. The 'fastest' FLUX.1-dev schedule reaches 3.37× speedup (778.17 ms/img) with only modest quality degradation (COCO FID 26.66). ECAD's learned schedules generalize across resolutions: a schedule optimized at 256×256 applied to FLUX.1-dev at 1024×1024 still outperforms FORA and ToCa on COCO FID.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECAD outperforms all six baselines on FID at equal or greater speedup across all three models. Against ToCa (Zou et al., 2025), the strongest prior method, ECAD's 'fastest' PixArt-α schedule reduces COCO FID by 4.47 (19.54 vs. 24.01) while increasing speedup from 2.35× to 2.58×; on FLUX.1-dev, ECAD's 'fast' schedule beats ToCa by 2.17 COCO FID (21.61 vs. 23.78) at 1.55× higher speedup (2.58× vs. 1.66×). Against TaylorSeer (Liu et al., 2025), ECAD's 'fast' FLUX.1-dev schedule improves COCO FID by 8.05 (21.61 vs. 29.66) at comparable speedup (2.58× vs. 2.55×), while TaylorSeer's more aggressive setting (3.03×) degrades COCO FID to 49.02. Against FORA (Selvaraju et al., 2024), ECAD matches or exceeds speedup while improving COCO FID by 2.89 on PixArt-α (20.58 vs. 24.50 at 1.97× vs. 2.01×) and by 1.90 on FLUX.1-dev (21.61 vs. 23.51 at 2.58× vs. 2.44×). Against DiCache (Bu et al., 2025) on FLUX.1-dev, ECAD's 'fast' schedule achieves 0.57× higher speedup (2.58× vs. 2.26×) with 4.57 lower COCO FID (21.61 vs. 26.18). Against TGATE (Liu et al., 2024b), ECAD dominates across all settings: the best TGATE configuration (m=10,k=5) yields COCO FID of 29.78 at only 1.53× speedup, while ECAD's 'fast' achieves 20.58 at 1.97×. DuCa (Zou et al., 2024) is outperformed by ECAD on both FID and speedup for PixArt-α (ECAD 'fastest': 19.54 COCO FID at 2.58× vs. DuCa: 24.69 at 2.59×).

## Relation to Prior Work

ECAD builds on the growing family of training-free feature-caching methods for Diffusion Transformers (FORA, TGATE, ToCa, DuCa, DiCache, TaylorSeer), which all rely on hand-crafted heuristics to decide which blocks, layers, or tokens to cache at which timesteps. The key conceptual departure is reframing schedule selection as a multi-objective Pareto optimization problem over a binary caching tensor, solved via NSGA-II genetic algorithm rather than human-designed rules. This makes the framework orthogonal to the specific caching granularity (block-level, component-level, token-level) and to the quality metric used, positioning it as a general search paradigm atop any caching formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ECAD provides practitioners with a smooth Pareto frontier of speed-quality trade-offs rather than a few discrete operating points, enabling deployment under variable latency constraints. The demonstrated generalization across resolutions (256→1024) and model variants (PixArt-α→PixArt-Σ) without re-optimization is practically valuable, reducing the per-deployment cost of finding good schedules. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video diffusion (where temporal coherence patterns differ), whether the up-front optimization cost (~470–700 GPU-hours) is acceptable for rapidly evolving model families, and whether the quality-metric dependence (Image Reward) limits applicability to domains where no reliable automatic scorer exists.
