## Problem

Existing instruction-driven zero-shot TTS systems suffer from a 'Style-Timbre-Content Conflict': models over-rely on acoustic priors from reference speech (timbre leakage) or infer prosody from text content (content leakage), thereby ignoring explicit natural-language style instructions. No prior framework actively decouples these entangled modalities while simultaneously supporting flexible combinations of style instruction and timbre reference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264316/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FlexiVoice accepts three inputs: the text to be spoken, an optional natural-language style instruction (e.g., 'Speak it using happy emotion'), and an optional reference speech clip for timbre cloning. Both the instruction and reference are independently optional, enabling any combination (text-only, text+instruction, text+reference, or all three). The LLM core (Phi-3.5-mini-instruct with a 16,384-token DualCodec vocabulary) generates discrete speech tokens, which are decoded via flow matching into a mel-spectrogram and then into a waveform by a Vocos vocoder.

## Method

FlexiVoice is built on a pre-trained LLM (Phi-3.5-mini-instruct) with a DualCodec speech tokenizer and a flow-matching decoder, pre-trained on Emilia plus a newly constructed 4,316-hour FlexiVoice-Instruct dataset annotated with natural-language instructions via Deepseek-V3. The key contribution is a Progressive Post-Training (PPT) curriculum with three stages: (S1) Multi-modality DPO aligns the model to follow both instruction and reference simultaneously using paired emotion data from ESD; (S2) Decoupling GRPO introduces multi-objective rewards (Emotion2vec SER score + CAM++ speaker verification) on deliberately conflicting scenarios (e.g., happy instruction vs. sad reference) to mathematically enforce separation of style from timbre and content; (S3) Instruction GRPO uses Kimi-Audio-7B-Instruct as an audio-language-model reward to generalize to complex, open-ended instructions beyond emotion. This progressive sequencing—preference alignment → constrained disentanglement → open-ended generalization—transforms instruction following from passive conditioning into active factor disentanglement, yielding substantial gains over joint or alternative-order training.

## Results

On the multi-modality decoupling benchmark (English), FlexiVoice achieves 97.4% instruction accuracy (ACC-I) on TO-easy and 89.4% on TO-hard, with only 6.6% leakage to conflicting text emotions (ACC-T). On the hardest TR-hard setting (emotional reference + neutral text), it reaches 78.2% ACC-I while maintaining 95.8% speaker verification (SV) and limiting reference-style leakage to 10.6% ACC-R. On the InstructTTSEval complex instruction benchmark, FlexiVoice attains 79.3% average accuracy (EN) and 70.8% (ZH), surpassing all open-source baselines and approaching Gemini-pro (80.3% EN). Subjective evaluation confirms superior naturalness (Q-MOS 4.08 vs. 3.72 for FlexiVoice-Base) and positive CMOS up to +0.91 over ground-truth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexiVoice substantially outperforms all five baselines on the decoupling benchmark. Against VoxInstruct (the strongest open-source multi-modality baseline), FlexiVoice gains +26.8 points on TO-easy ACC-I (97.4 vs. 70.6), +71.6 on TO-hard (89.4 vs. 17.8), and +28.5 on TR-hard (78.2 vs. 49.7), while reducing reference-style leakage from 23.9% to 10.6%. CosyVoice2, which supports both instruction and reference inputs, is outperformed on TR-hard ACC-I by 17.2 points (78.2 vs. 61.0) with comparable SV (95.8 vs. 99.8). On InstructTTSEval, FlexiVoice (79.3 EN avg) surpasses VoxInstruct (50.4), PromptTTS (47.6), Parler-TTS (45.7), and PromptStyle (38.2) by 29–41 points, and also exceeds MiMo-Audio-7B-Instruct (72.6) by 6.7 points, though it remains below Gemini-flash (88.7) and Gemini-pro (80.3) among closed-source systems. In Chinese, FlexiVoice (70.8 avg) edges out MiMo-Audio-7B-Instruct (70.5) and VoxInstruct (47.5) by 23+ points, while trailing Gemini models by ~14 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexiVoice extends the instruction-based TTS line (VoxInstruct, CosyVoice2, PromptTTS) by explicitly addressing the Style-Timbre-Content Conflict that prior systems leave unresolved: models either leak style from reference speech or infer prosody from text, ignoring the instruction. Methodologically, it builds on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) for initial preference alignment and adapts the multi-objective GRPO advantage formulation from Vevo2 (Zhang et al., 2025b) for decoupling, but wraps both in a novel Progressive Post-Training curriculum that sequences these objectives from simple emotion-centric tasks to open-ended instruction following. The key departure from prior work is the staged, conflict-aware training design rather than a single joint optimization, and the use of an audio-language model (Kimi-Audio-7B) as a scalable reward for complex instruction adherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PPT framework offers a generalizable recipe for resolving modality conflicts in multimodal generation beyond TTS, and the 4,316-hour FlexiVoice-Instruct dataset fills a gap in natural, diverse instruction-speech pairs. However, the emotion-centric design of S1/S2 limits demonstrated generalization to non-emotional style dimensions (speed, pitch are only validated in an appendix), and the ALM reward model's modest agreement with the gold-standard judge (Macro-F1 ≈ 0.60–0.65) raises questions about reward quality at scale. Open questions include whether the progressive curriculum transfers to other controllable generation tasks (e.g., music, video) and whether the 3.8B-parameter backbone is sufficient for truly open-ended, multi-attribute instructions without further scaling.
