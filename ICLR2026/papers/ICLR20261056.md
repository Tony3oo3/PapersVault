## Problem

Most speech brain-computer interfaces rely on cascaded pipelines (RNN → phonemes → n-gram language model) that cannot be jointly optimized end-to-end, and the one prior end-to-end approach (Feng et al., 2024) used an unpretrained RNN encoder with a text-only LLM, leaving performance well below the cascaded baseline. The paper asks whether a transformer encoder pretrained at scale across species and tasks, combined with an audio-LLM decoder, can close this gap and enable differentiable, end-to-end speech decoding.

## Input / Output

Input consists of thresholded spike counts and spike-band power (SBP) from 128- or 256-electrode Utah arrays, binned into 20 ms windows and z-scored across days to handle probe drift; these are grouped into 5-bin (100 ms) time patches before entering the transformer. In the cascaded setting the encoder outputs phoneme logits consumed by a 5-gram LM with OPT re-scoring; in the end-to-end setting the encoder's latent representations are projected into an audio-LLM's embedding space and the LLM autoregressively generates a full English sentence via nucleus sampling (top-p=0.9, temperature=0.7, max 25 tokens).

## Method

BIT comprises a ~7 M-parameter transformer encoder (7 layers, 6 heads, RoPE positional embeddings, bidirectional attention) pretrained with temporal patch masking and MSE reconstruction on ~367 hours of human and monkey Utah array recordings spanning speech and arm-motor tasks, then fine-tuned for phoneme prediction with CTC loss. The encoder is coupled to an audio-LLM (Aero1-Audio 1.5B, an audio-extended Qwen2.5-1.5B) through a shallow MLP projector, with LoRA (rank 8) applied to the LLM's attention and feed-forward layers. A contrastive InfoNCE loss aligns mean-pooled neural and text embedding tokens in a shared latent space, reinforcing semantic correspondence between the two modalities during sentence-level fine-tuning. The key empirical findings are that (i) cross-species SSL pretraining yields larger transfer gains than same-subject supervised cross-task pretraining, especially for low-data imagined speech, and (ii) small audio-LLMs consistently outperform same-size text-LLMs, suggesting that audio pretraining provides inductive biases closer to the neural decoding problem.

## Results

On the Brain-to-Text '24 holdout set, BIT Cascaded achieves a WER of 6.35% (non-ensembled) and 5.10% (with ensemble), setting a new SOTA for cascaded Utah-array speech BCIs. In the end-to-end setting, BIT reduces WER from 24.69% (Feng et al., 2024) to 15.67% (single model) and 10.22% (with ensemble). On the Brain-to-Text '25 holdout set, BIT Cascaded+Ensemble achieves 1.76% WER, while BIT End-to-End+Ensemble reaches 7.76%. For imagined speech (50-word vocabulary), SSL pretraining (BIT-All) outperforms training from scratch (BIT-TFS) by 39–45% in WER, demonstrating substantial gains in low-data regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BIT Cascaded (6.35% WER) outperforms the prior SOTA cascaded method Feghhi et al. (2025) (7.98%) and the standard RNN baseline (9.76%) on the Brain-to-Text '24 benchmark; with ensembling, BIT Cascaded+Ensemble (5.10%) surpasses Feghhi et al.+Ensemble (5.68%) and Li et al.+Ensemble (5.77%). In the end-to-end setting, BIT End-to-End (15.67%) substantially outperforms the only prior end-to-end method Feng et al. (2024) (24.69%), a >50% relative WER reduction, and BIT End-to-End+Ensemble further narrows the gap to cascaded methods (10.22% vs. 5.10%). For imagined speech, BIT-All outperforms BIT-TFS by 39–45% in WER and also exceeds BIT-Cross-Task-Only, showing that cross-subject, label-free SSL pretraining yields larger transfer gains than same-subject, cross-task supervised pretraining. On the Brain-to-Text '25 benchmark, BIT Cascaded+Ensemble achieves 1.76% WER, beating RNN+Ensemble (3.09%) and RNN-TTA+Pseudo-Ensemble (4.42%). Overall, BIT wins on every head-to-head comparison within the same decoding framework, though a WER gap between cascaded and end-to-end approaches remains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cascaded speech BCI line (RNN + n-gram LM, Willett et al. 2023; Card et al. 2024) and the recent end-to-end LLM decoder (Feng et al. 2024) by replacing the RNN encoder with a transformer pretrained via cross-species, cross-task masked autoencoding on ~367 hours of human and monkey Utah array data. It also departs from prior end-to-end work by using audio-LLMs (Aero1-Audio, Qwen2-Audio) rather than text-only LLMs, drawing an analogy to LLaVA's image-encoder-to-LLM projection but applied to neural activity. The cross-species pretraining builds on POSSM (Ryoo et al. 2025) and broader neural foundation model efforts, while the contrastive modality alignment follows the CLIP/InfoNCE paradigm adapted to the neural-text pairing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BIT establishes SOTA on both Brain-to-Text '24 and '25 benchmarks and cuts end-to-end WER by over 50% relative to the prior best, making end-to-end speech BCIs practically competitive with cascaded systems for the first time. The finding that small audio-LLMs outperform larger text LLMs for neural decoding is a useful practical guideline for the field. Key limitations remain: bidirectional attention precludes real-time streaming, inference takes ~0.95 s/sentence, and the approach still requires substantial unlabeled neural data plus labeled speech data to outperform cascaded baselines. Open questions include whether causal-attention encoders can close the accuracy gap, how to handle long-term non-stationarity and neural plasticity, and whether the cross-species pretraining benefit generalizes beyond Utah array recordings.
