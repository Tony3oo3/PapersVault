## Problem

Representation-based continual learning with frozen pre-trained models suffers from multicollinearity among class prototypes, which degrades cosine-similarity discrimination and leads to ambiguous decision boundaries. Existing decorrelation methods (e.g., RanPAC) incur substantial computational costs from dense projections and explicit cross-validation, making them impractical for real-time, low-latency deployment. Additionally, the theoretical role of the downstream KC→MBON pathway in the fly olfactory circuit for decorrelation remained unestablished.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (e.g., 32×32 CIFAR-100, 224×224 CUB/VTAB/ImageNet) and produces a single class label per image under a class-incremental learning protocol where task identity is unavailable at test time. The pre-trained encoder (ViT-B/16, ResNet-50, or Qwen2.5-VL-7B) is entirely frozen; only the lightweight projection and classifier parameters are updated per task. At inference, prediction reduces to a sparse dot product between the top-k projected feature and the learned prototype matrix, yielding O(k·c_t) complexity per sample.

## Method

Fly-CL mimics the fly olfactory circuit in three stages: (1) a fixed sparse random projection W ∈ R^{m×d} (m≫d, each row has only p non-zero N(0,1) entries) expands features into a high-dimensional space, followed by a top-k winner-take-all operation that retains only the k largest components, jointly achieving progressive decorrelation analogous to PN→KC expansion with APL-mediated lateral inhibition; (2) a streaming ridge classifier maintains running Gram (G) and cross-correlation (S) matrices updated per task, with the regularization coefficient λ selected via an efficient Generalized Cross-Validation (GCV) criterion computed from the SVD of each task's feature matrix, avoiding the O(lm³) cost of explicit cross-validation; (3) the final prototype matrix C = (G + λI)^{-1}S is computed via Cholesky factorization for numerical stability and speed. The paper proves that the sparse projection preserves full column rank with high probability (Theorem 4.1), that top-k sparsification degrades performance by at most O(√(C/k)·‖h‖) (Theorem 4.2), and that a biologically plausible Hebbian/anti-Hebbian synaptic update rule converges to exactly the ridge regression solution, providing a theoretical bridge between the KC→MBON pathway and the streaming classifier.

## Results

Using ViT-B/16, Fly-CL achieves overall accuracy of 93.89% on CIFAR-100, 93.84% on CUB-200-2011, and 96.54% on VTAB, while reducing post-extraction training time (τ_post) by 91%, 83%, and 67% respectively compared to the most efficient baseline (F-OAL). On CIFAR-100, Fly-CL's τ_post is 5.38s versus 57.13s for F-OAL and 84.42s for RanPAC. With ResNet-50, Fly-CL further improves overall accuracy by 1.89%, 1.53%, and 1.20% over the best baselines on CIFAR-100, CUB-200-2011, and VTAB while maintaining the lowest training time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fly-CL achieves the lowest τ_post across all three datasets by a wide margin: 5.38s on CIFAR-100 (vs. 57.13s for F-OAL and 84.42s for RanPAC), 0.35s on CUB-200-2011 (vs. 2.04s for F-OAL), and 0.34s on VTAB (vs. 1.03s for F-OAL). In terms of overall accuracy, Fly-CL surpasses RanPAC on CUB-200-2011 (93.84% vs. 92.67%) and VTAB (96.54% vs. 94.16%), and exceeds F-OAL on all three datasets (93.89% vs. 91.96% on CIFAR-100, 93.84% vs. 91.13% on CUB, 96.54% vs. 94.68% on VTAB). The only accuracy loss is a marginal 0.32% on CIFAR-100 versus RanPAC (93.89% vs. 94.21%), which is within one standard deviation. Prompt/adapter-based methods (L2P, DualPrompt, InfLoRA, SEMA, MoE-Adapter) are consistently outperformed on both accuracy and efficiency, with EASE being the most computationally expensive (583.4s τ_post on CIFAR-100). On ResNet-50, F-OAL degrades severely (60.84% on CUB, 26.15% on VTAB) while Fly-CL maintains strong performance (80.25% and 94.00%), highlighting Fly-CL's robustness across architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

Fly-CL builds directly on the representation-based continual learning paradigm (RanPAC, F-OAL, EASE) that uses frozen pretrained encoders and class-prototype similarity matching, but departs from these methods by replacing dense random projections with a sparse random projection plus top-k sparsification (mimicking the PN→KC expansion in the fly olfactory circuit) and by substituting explicit cross-validation for ridge parameter selection with an adaptive GCV procedure. The paper also introduces a theoretical bridge showing that a biologically plausible Hebbian/anti-Hebbian synaptic update rule converges to the same stationary point as ridge classification, providing a mechanistic justification for the KC→MBON down-projection stage. Compared to RanPAC, Fly-CL eliminates the PETL fine-tuning step entirely; compared to F-OAL, it adds the top-k filtering and adaptive regularization that F-OAL lacks, addressing F-OAL's error accumulation on CNN backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: Fly-CL reduces post-extraction training time by 83–93% across datasets while matching or exceeding SOTA accuracy, making pretrained-model-based CL viable for real-time and edge-deployment scenarios where the computational overhead of prior methods is prohibitive. The biological framing (fly olfactory circuit) provides a principled design space for progressive decorrelation that could inspire further bio-inspired CL architectures, though the individual algorithmic components (sparse projection, top-k, GCV, Cholesky) are well-known techniques whose combination here is the primary contribution. Open questions include whether the approach extends beyond classification (e.g., detection, segmentation), whether the fixed random projection can be adapted per-task without losing the efficiency gains, and whether the Hebbian-ridge equivalence holds under more realistic non-stationary data streams.
