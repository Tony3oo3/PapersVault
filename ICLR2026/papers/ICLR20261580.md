## Problem

Embodied AI lacks the internet-scale data flywheel that propelled LLMs, because physical trajectory collection demands specialized hardware, costly human operation, and fragmented tooling (e.g., DROID required 50 collectors across 13 institutions over 12 months). Desktop gaming interactions offer rich, abundant sensorimotor data at scale, yet prior efforts were either single-domain (VPT for Minecraft) or kept data proprietary (SIMA), leaving no open, unified pipeline from scalable desktop collection to verified embodied transfer.

## Input / Output

The Generalist-IDM consumes synchronized desktop streams—screen frames at FHD/QHD 60 Hz plus timestamped keyboard and mouse events—serialized into discrete token sequences where mouse deltas use a configurable base-[2,10,10,10] encoding (±1999 px range) and timestamps use a cyclic 10-second, 10 ms-resolution base-10 scheme. It autoregressively emits predicted keyboard events (virtual key code + press/release) and mouse events (signed dx/dy, button flags in hex, scroll deltas) between consecutive screen frames, stopping when a predicted timestamp exceeds the next observation. Downstream, VAPT takes robot camera images and produces manipulation or navigation actions, with the 1B-parameter model matching or surpassing 3.3B–7B policies on LIBERO and CANVAS.

## Method

D2E comprises three components. The OWA Toolkit provides the ocap recorder (Windows DXGI + GStreamer with H.265 hardware encoding for 217× compression over raw frames) and the OWAMcap format (MCAP container + external MediaRef video), achieving 152× storage reduction over VPT's JSONL while enabling crash-safe, indexed random access; an optimized FSLDataset pipeline with adaptive batch decoding reaches 119 img/s and 41× lower disk read per image versus TorchCodec. The Generalist-IDM (InternVL3-1B backbone) replaces tick-based prediction with timestamp-based next-event prediction using a temporal offset τ=100 ms (NEP-τ), which shifts the observation window forward so the model conditions on up to τ ms of future frames—critical for resolving the current action, as ablations show Pearson correlations collapse near zero at τ=0. This generalist model outperforms per-game Specialist IDMs in-distribution and generalizes zero-shot to unseen games (e.g., 63% keyboard accuracy on Battlefield 6), enabling pseudo-labeling of 1,055 hours of YouTube gameplay. VAPT fine-tunes the desktop-pretrained model on robotics data (259 h human + 1K+ h pseudo-labeled), transferring sensorimotor priors to achieve 96.6% on LIBERO manipulation and 83.3% on CANVAS navigation with only 1B parameters.

## Results

On the LIBERO manipulation benchmark, VAPT without pseudo-labels (1B params) achieves 96.6% total success rate and 93.6% on long-horizon tasks, surpassing π₀ (3.3B, 86.0%) and OpenVLA (7B, 76.5%). On CANVAS navigation, VAPT with pseudo-labels reaches 83.3% total success (vs. 75.3% baseline), with the largest gains under misleading instructions (e.g., 86.7% vs. 53.3% on sim_orchard). On Meta-World, VAPT improves average success from 19.9% to 25.0%, and on the real-world SO101 pick-and-place task, VAPT achieves 80% vs. 70% for the baseline. The Generalist-IDM outperforms game-specific Specialist IDMs across all six in-distribution games, with gains up to +57.6 points on keyboard accuracy (Brotato) and +39.5 points on Pearson correlation (Stardew Valley X).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VAPT (1B) decisively outperforms all VLA-pretrained baselines on LIBERO: it beats π₀ (3.3B) by 10.6 points (96.6 vs. 86.0), OpenVLA (7B) by 20.1 points (96.6 vs. 76.5), SmolVLA (2.25B) by 7.9 points, and Octo (93M) by 21.5 points. The only method that edges out VAPT is OpenVLA-OFT (7B) at 97.1% vs. 96.6%, a marginal 0.5-point gap. Against non-VLA baselines, VAPT also leads DiT Policy (115M, 82.4%) by 14.2 points and PI-KI (300M, 94.3%) by 2.3 points. On CANVAS navigation, pseudo-labeling is the key differentiator: VAPT w/ pseudo (83.3%) improves over the baseline (75.3%) by 8 points, while VAPT w/o pseudo matches the baseline exactly, indicating that the diversity of pseudo-labeled data benefits high-level planning more than precise manipulation. On Meta-World, VAPT consistently outperforms the baseline across difficulty tiers, with the largest gap on Very Hard tasks (20.0–24.0% vs. 8.0%). On the real-world SO101 robot, both VAPT variants achieve 80% success versus 70% for the unpretrained baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VPT (Baker et al., 2022) paradigm of using Inverse Dynamics Models to pseudo-label internet gameplay, but generalizes from a single-domain (Minecraft) Specialist IDM to a multi-game Generalist-IDM with timestamp-based event prediction (NEP-τ) that avoids the fixed-tick inefficiency of prior IDMs. It also departs from SIMA (Raad et al., 2024) by releasing an open toolkit, standardized data format (OWAMcap), and 2.6K+ hours of data rather than keeping resources proprietary. The core conceptual leap is treating desktop sensorimotor pretraining as a transferable substrate for physical robotics, building on the vision-language-action model lineage (RT-1/2, OpenVLA, π₀) but replacing costly physical trajectory collection with abundant desktop interactions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the fundamental data bottleneck in embodied AI by demonstrating that 1.3K hours of desktop data can initialize a 1B model that matches or surpasses 3.3B–7B robotics policies on LIBERO and CANVAS, potentially reducing the cost of embodied pretraining by orders of magnitude. A notable limitation is that pseudo-labeled data *hurts* manipulation (92.2% vs. 96.6%) while *helping* navigation (83.3% vs. 75.3%), suggesting the transfer mechanism is task-dependent and that precise human supervision remains critical for fine-grained control. Open questions include whether the transfer scales to more complex or multi-embodiment settings, whether the Generalist-IDM's pseudo-label quality is sufficient for truly internet-scale collection, and whether the desktop-to-robotics gap narrows with more diverse or longer-horizon desktop tasks.
