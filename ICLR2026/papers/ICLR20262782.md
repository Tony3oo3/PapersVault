## Problem

Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) route predictions through human-interpretable concepts but suffer from reduced accuracy relative to black-box models and concept leakage, where extraneous input information contaminates concept activations and undermines both interpretability and test-time intervention reliability. Prior remedies modify architectures or enrich concept embeddings, adding design complexity that may not generalize across CBM variants. No prior work had applied Information Bottleneck theory directly at the concept layer to enforce minimal-sufficient representations in an architecture-agnostic way.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (299×299 for CUB with an InceptionV3 backbone, or pre-computed ResNet-18 embeddings for AwA2 and aPY) and produces two outputs: a class label and a vector of soft (non-binary) concept predictions (112 concepts for CUB, 45 for AwA2, 32 for aPY). The concept predictions are generated via a variational approximation—two lightweight 1-layer MLP heads on the embedding layer predict a mean and standard-deviation vector, and the reparameterization trick samples concept logits. These soft concept logits are then passed to a label predictor q(y|c) for the final classification.

## Method

The paper introduces a Concepts' Information Bottleneck (CIBM) regularizer with objective L_CIBM = I(Z;C) + I(C;Y) − β·I(X;C), which compresses information flowing from inputs to concepts while preserving task-relevant information in the concept-to-label pathway. Two tractable training variants are derived: IB_B (bounded CIB), which replaces mutual information terms with cross-entropy losses and a concept entropy term, and IB_E (estimator-based CIB), which retains an explicit Monte-Carlo estimator for I(X;C) using Gaussian assumptions on concept logits. The regularizer is plug-and-play: it requires only two 1-layer MLP heads (for variational mean and std) attached to the existing embedding layer, with no architectural changes, side channels, or additional supervision. A PAC-Bayes analysis further shows that CIBMs achieve a strictly tighter generalization bound than vanilla CBMs when β is sufficiently small, because the information penalty reduces posterior complexity more than it increases empirical risk.

## Results

On CUB-200-2011, the IB-regularized variants consistently improve class accuracy over their vanilla counterparts: CBM(HI)+IB_E reaches 0.691 (vs. 0.644 baseline, +4.7 pp), CBM(SJ)+IB_B reaches 0.727 (vs. 0.708, +1.9 pp), and IntCEM+IB_B reaches 0.778 (vs. 0.759, +1.9 pp). On the more diverse aPY dataset, gains are larger: CBM(SJ)+IB_E jumps from 0.797 to 0.860 (+6.3 pp), surpassing the black-box gold standard of 0.866. On AwA2, improvements are modest (e.g., IntCEM+IB_E: 0.884→0.886) due to the dataset's relative simplicity. Concept accuracy is maintained or slightly improved across all settings, and concept leakage (OIS/NIS) is substantially reduced in every configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the CBM family (Havasi et al., 2022), the IB regularizers deliver the largest absolute gains: on CUB, CBM(HI) class accuracy improves from 0.644 to 0.691 (+4.7 pp with IB_E), and CBM(SJ) from 0.708 to 0.727 (+1.9 pp with IB_B); on aPY, CBM(SJ) jumps from 0.797 to 0.860 (+6.3 pp), exceeding the black-box ceiling of 0.866. Against ProbCBM (Kim et al., 2023), IB_B improves CUB class accuracy from 0.718 to 0.741 (+2.3 pp) and aPY from 0.863 to 0.873 (+1.0 pp), while concept accuracy is maintained or slightly improved. Against IntCEM (Espinosa Zarlenga et al., 2023), the gains are consistent but smaller: CUB class accuracy rises from 0.759 to 0.778 (+1.9 pp with IB_B) and aPY from 0.869 to 0.877 (+0.8 pp with IB_E). In every head-to-head comparison across all three datasets and all six CBM families, the IB-regularized variant matches or exceeds the vanilla baseline on class accuracy, while concept accuracy is preserved (within ±0.5 pp) and concept leakage (OIS/NIS) is substantially reduced—e.g., CBM(SJ) OIS drops from 4.69 to 2.01 on CUB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the Information Bottleneck principle (Tishby et al., 2000; Alemi et al., 2017) specifically to the concept layer of Concept Bottleneck Models, a target that prior IB work (which focused on generic latent representations) and prior CBM improvements (which modified architectures via side channels, recurrent structures, or enriched embeddings) had not addressed. It departs from the architectural-modification lineage (Havasi et al., 2022; Kim et al., 2023; Espinosa Zarlenga et al., 2023b) by offering an architecture-agnostic, plug-in mutual-information regularizer that requires no structural changes. The PAC-Bayes generalization analysis (Theorem 2) extends the IB-generalization connection studied by Kawaguchi et al. (2023) to the CBM setting, showing that the CIBM's complexity reduction yields a strictly tighter bound than vanilla CBMs under a small-β condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded explanation for a long-standing CBM failure mode—vanilla CBMs over-compress their representations, discarding task-relevant features alongside noise—which reframes the accuracy gap to black-box models as a compression-strategy problem rather than an architectural one. The plug-in nature of the regularizer makes it immediately applicable across the growing family of CBM variants without re-derivation. Limitations include evaluation on only three relatively small datasets (CUB, AwA2, aPY), an inconclusive β hyperparameter sweep, and the IB_B variant requiring a gradient-stopping workaround to avoid training instability. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse benchmarks (e.g., ImageNet-scale), whether it transfers to non-visual or tabular CBMs, and whether the PAC-Bayes gap Δ can be made tighter or empirically validated beyond the theoretical bound.
