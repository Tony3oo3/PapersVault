## Problem

When foundation vision-language models are continually adapted to non-stationary data streams, naive fine-tuning destroys zero-shot recognition and prompt robustness. Existing replay-free methods regularize proxy signals (logits, off-diagonal similarities, parameters, routing) rather than directly constraining the canonical cross-modal alignment geometry that underlies zero-shot transfer, leaving them vulnerable to slow alignment drift, dependence on reference corpora or synthetic generators, and brittleness to prompt/style shifts.

## Input / Output

During training, PI-CCA consumes mini-batches of image-text pairs (B=1024) through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone with LoRA adapters (rank 16), requiring only batch-level covariance statistics and a ~50KB certificate—no past samples, replay buffers, or synthetic data generators. At inference the adapted model produces task-appropriate outputs without task IDs or domain hints: class labels for MTIL/X-TAIL, image-to-text or text-to-image retrieval rankings (Recall@K) for VLCL, and structured concept match scores for ConStruct-VL.

## Method

PI-CCA reframes catastrophic forgetting as drift in the geometry of image-text alignment and preserves it via a compact CCA certificate storing the top-k canonical correlations and sketched canonical subspaces (projected to h=256 dimensions via random orthonormal sketches). At each step, the whitened cross-covariance is computed from mini-batch statistics with EMA smoothing, its top-k SVD is extracted via differentiable block power iteration, and three geometry-preserving losses are applied: spectral preservation (matching sorted canonical correlations with an optional Ky-Fan sum term), subspace-angle preservation (Frobenius distance between sketched Gram projectors), and prompt invariance (averaging text-side projectors over M=4 prompt perturbations to eliminate phrasing sensitivity). The certificate is refreshed via a slow EMA (α=0.01) with stop-gradient on the QR re-orthogonalization, and only LoRA parameters are updated while the backbone stays frozen. A theoretical excess-risk bound (Theorem 1) shows that zero-shot risk is controlled by spectral drift, subspace-angle drift, and tail energy, formalizing why preserving CCA geometry prevents forgetting in replay-free continual adaptation.

## Results

On the MTIL 11-domain classification track, PI-CCA achieves 76.8% average accuracy, 75.5% last-step accuracy, and 73.2% transfer accuracy, outperforming the strongest replay-free baseline C-CLIP (75.2/73.8/70.9) by 1.6/1.7/2.3 points. On the task-agnostic X-TAIL protocol, PI-CCA reaches 68.1% average accuracy versus 67.4% for RAIL and 66.3% for C-CLIP. For continual image-text retrieval (VLCL), PI-CCA attains 48.6% I2T R@1 and 37.4% T2I R@1, surpassing C-CLIP (46.1/35.7) and even the synthetic-replay method GIFT (47.3/36.5) without storing or generating any data. On ConStruct-VL, PI-CCA achieves 75.2% Final Accuracy with only 2.7% Average Forgetting, the best FA/AF pair among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PI-CCA outperforms all 13 compared baselines across every benchmark and metric. On MTIL, the closest competitor is C-CLIP (75.2 Avg), trailed by DIKI (74.9) and RAIL (74.3); PI-CCA leads by 1.6 points over C-CLIP and 1.9 over DIKI. On X-TAIL, RAIL is the nearest baseline at 67.4 Avg, with PI-CCA ahead by 0.7 points, while C-CLIP and MG-CLIP both sit at 66.3. For VLCL retrieval, PI-CCA (48.6 I2T R@1) beats C-CLIP (46.1) by 2.5 points and surpasses the synthetic-replay method GIFT (47.3) by 1.3 points without any data storage or generation. On ConStruct-VL, PI-CCA achieves the best FA (75.2) and lowest AF (2.7), improving over C-CLIP (72.4/3.9) by 2.8 FA and 1.2 AF. The gap over regularization-based methods (ZSCL, Mod-X, ZAF) is consistently 3–4 points on MTIL Avg, confirming that directly constraining CCA geometry yields substantially better retention than proxy-signal alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

PI-CCA departs from the dominant VL-CL paradigm of regularizing proxy signals (similarity distributions, logits, off-diagonal entries, or parameter norms) by directly constraining the geometric invariants of the whitened image-text cross-covariance — specifically the top-k canonical correlations and their associated subspaces. Conceptually, it repurposes classical CCA (a multivariate statistics tool previously used only as a diagnostic in representation learning, e.g., SVCCA/PWCCA) as a training-time regularizer, combined with random orthonormal sketches for constant-memory storage and EMA-based streaming estimation. The prompt-invariance component draws on prompt-ensemble ideas from CoOp/MaPLe but applies them at the level of projector averaging rather than learned prompt vectors, and the overall framework is compatible with LoRA-based parameter-efficient adaptation rather than requiring architectural changes like MoE routers or separate adapters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical bridge (Theorems 1–2) showing that CCA spectral and subspace drift directly upper-bounds zero-shot excess risk and dynamic regret, giving practitioners a principled, geometry-based knob to tune retention-plasticity trade-offs without replay or generators. The ~50 KB certificate and ~8% time overhead make the approach practically deployable, and the consistent 1–3 point gains across four diverse benchmarks (classification, retrieval, structured concepts) with strong statistical significance suggest the alignment-geometry framing is a useful lens for the field. Open questions include whether the global certificate generalizes to multimodal instruction tuning or non-CLIP architectures, whether per-concept or per-class certificates could help in extremely heterogeneous streams, and how the method behaves under truly non-stationary (rather than task-incremental) distributions where the 'reference' alignment itself should drift.
