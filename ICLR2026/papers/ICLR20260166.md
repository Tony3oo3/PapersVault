## Problem

Healthcare insurance fraud detection faces two simultaneous challenges that existing methods address in isolation: extreme label scarcity during pretraining (as low as 0.062% verified fraud cases) and non-stationary fraud patterns that evolve over time in online streams where labeled updates are unavailable. Supervised GNNs degrade sharply under limited labels, while online continual-learning methods typically assume ground-truth labels for parameter updates, making them impractical for real-world deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260166/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a temporal sequence of daily medical insurance claim graphs where nodes represent individual claims with feature vectors, and edges connect claims sharing the same medical provider or beneficiary. During the pretraining phase, only 1% or 10% of nodes carry fraud labels; during the online phase, all incoming graph tasks are completely unlabeled. The model outputs node-level binary fraud predictions (fraud vs. not-fraud) along with structure-aware node embeddings that encode both local feature semantics and global topological position within the claim network.

## Method

ConFVG integrates two modules. First, a Fiedler Vector-guided graph autoencoder replaces random feature masking with spectral-guided masking: the Fiedler vector (second eigenvector of the graph Laplacian) captures community boundaries and connectivity bottlenecks characteristic of collusive fraud, and each feature's mask probability is computed as the absolute projection of normalized node features onto this vector, forcing the autoencoder to reconstruct fraud-relevant structural signals without labels. A small fully-connected perturbation (ε=1e-5) is added to the adjacency matrix to handle disconnected claim graphs. Second, a Subgraph Attention Fusion (SAF) module constructs a graph complement from the top-k largest connected components, fuses original and complement embeddings through a two-layer attention network, and applies an attention loss that penalizes the model for attending to unselected nodes. A Mean Teacher architecture with KL-divergence consistency and exponential moving average (EMA) updates stabilizes the online phase, enabling unsupervised adaptation to evolving fraud patterns while preventing catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On the medical insurance dataset at 10% label rate, ConFVG achieves AUC 80.48±0.86 and F1-score 64.48±1.24, outperforming the strongest baseline POCL (AUC 74.76, F1 60.31) by 5.7 AUC points. Under extreme label scarcity (1% label rate), ConFVG maintains AUC 76.13 and F1 62.24, while the best baseline GAD drops to AUC 73.29 and F1 56.81. On the Amazon dataset at 10% label rate, ConFVG reaches AUC 91.07 and F1 87.32, surpassing the next-best GAD (AUC 89.56, F1 85.05) by 1.5 AUC points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConFVG outperforms all eight baselines across every label rate and dataset. Against the strongest online baseline POCL (Zhang et al., 2024), ConFVG gains 5.7 AUC points at 10% labels (80.48 vs. 74.76) and 5.5 points at 1% labels (76.13 vs. 70.64), with the gap widening as labels become scarcer. Among semi-supervised offline methods, GAD (Chen et al., 2024) is the closest competitor at 10% labels (AUC 76.54, F1 61.73), but ConFVG still leads by 3.9 AUC and 2.8 F1 points; at 1% labels the gap grows to 2.8 AUC and 5.4 F1 points. Traditional offline models (CAREGNN, PCGNN) and online models without structural adaptation (ContinuesGNN, FGN) lag significantly, with FGN achieving only AUC 65.71 at 10% labels. On the Amazon dataset, ConFVG achieves AUC 91.07 vs. GAD's 89.56 and POCL's 87.57, confirming generalizability beyond medical insurance. Notably, even in the fully-labeled 100%* scenario where supervised baselines have their best chance, ConFVG matches or slightly exceeds POCL (AUC 80.61 vs. 80.32), indicating the spectral pretraining and subgraph complement strategy add value even when labels are abundant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: spectral graph theory (Fiedler, 1973), self-supervised masked graph autoencoders (GraphMAE, Hou et al. 2022), and mean-teacher consistency training (Tarvainen & Valpola, 2017). Its key departure from prior work is using the Fiedler vector—the second eigenvector of the graph Laplacian—as a structural prior to guide feature masking in the autoencoder, replacing the random or edge-based masking strategies of GraphMAE and S2GAE. For the online phase, it introduces a subgraph complement strategy (connecting top-k components via their complement edges) fused with attention, which is distinct from the parameter-regularization or data-replay approaches used by ContinuesGNN, FGN, and POCL. The paper explicitly positions itself as unifying two previously treated-in-isolation challenges: label-scarce pretraining and unlabeled non-stationary online adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a realistic deployment gap in fraud detection where extreme label scarcity (as low as 1%) and unlabeled online streams co-occur, a setting where prior semi-supervised and online methods each fail in isolation. The Fiedler-vector-guided masking offers a principled, label-free way to inject global topological awareness into representation learning, and the demonstrated robustness to camouflage attacks (only ~1.8 AUC drop even when 50% of fraud nodes add fake edges) suggests the spectral signal is genuinely informative rather than a superficial regularizer. Limitations include reliance on full Laplacian eigendecomposition (scalability to very large graphs is unclear), evaluation on only three datasets with one being proprietary, and the subgraph complement strategy adding non-trivial computational overhead. Open questions include whether the spectral masking signal transfers to heterogeneous or temporal graphs, and whether the mean-teacher component is truly necessary or whether simpler EMA-based self-consistency would suffice.
