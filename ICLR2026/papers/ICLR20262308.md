## Problem

Diffusion models dominate visual generation but require many dense sequential denoising steps, making them difficult to integrate with LLMs in unified multi-modal systems. Autoregressive models offer a simple next-token-prediction paradigm with mature inference infrastructure, but images lack the inherent linear ordering that makes AR modeling natural for text. The paper asks whether the pixel diffusion process can be recast as a vanilla autoregressive procedure without modifying any underlying AR mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262308/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single class label as a prefix token and generates a sequence of 256 discrete tokens (codebook size 16384, dimension 8) via standard next-token prediction. These tokens are decoded into a 256×256 RGB image by a pixel-space diffusion transformer that operates without any VAE, where each group of 32 tokens conditions one of 8 diffusion sampling steps. The streaming nature means partial token sequences can be decoded into consistent intermediate previews at any generation point.

## Method

The core contribution is a sequential diffusion tokenizer that linearizes the diffusion process into a 1D token sequence: a transformer encoder with learnable queries produces 256 discrete tokens, and a 185M-parameter diffusion transformer decoder on raw pixel patches uses tokens at different sequence positions as conditions at different flow-matching timesteps, creating a natural coarse-to-fine ordering (early tokens condition noisy early steps, later tokens condition less-noisy later steps). A standard Llama decoder-only transformer (343M/775M/1.4B) then performs vanilla next-token prediction on these tokens with no architectural modifications to causal masks or inference. The tokenizer is trained with flow matching velocity loss, VQ loss, LPIPS perceptual loss, and REPA representation alignment loss. This design preserves the Markovian property of diffusion (conditioned only by token groups), enables KV-cache-friendly streaming inference, and supports zero-shot layout control by fixing prefix tokens.

## Results

On the ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation benchmark, D-AR achieves FID scores of 2.44 (343M), 2.09 (775M), and 2.00 (1.4B) using a plain Llama decoder-only backbone with 256 discrete tokens and standard next-token prediction. D-AR-XXL (1.4B) sets a new state-of-the-art FID of 2.00 among vanilla autoregressive models, outperforming the strongest prior vanilla AR baseline IBQ-XXL (2.05, 2.1B) with fewer parameters. The sequential diffusion tokenizer achieves a reconstruction FID of 1.52 with 8 sampling steps, substantially better than the LlamaGen tokenizer (2.19) under the same 256-token budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among vanilla AR models (strict next-token prediction with discrete tokens), D-AR-XL (775M, FID 2.09) outperforms LlamaGen-XL (2.62), stronger LlamaGen-XL (2.16), and IBQ-XL (2.14), while D-AR-XXL (1.4B, FID 2.00) beats IBQ-XXL (2.05, 2.1B) with 35% fewer parameters. D-AR-XXL also surpasses the strongest pure diffusion baselines SiT-XL (2.06) and DiT-XL (2.27) despite using only 1.4B AR parameters versus 675M diffusion parameters. However, D-AR-XXL (2.00) trails tailored AR methods that modify the standard AR mechanism: VAR-d30 (1.92), RAR-XL (1.50), MAR-H (1.55), and CausalFusion-XL (1.77), which use multi-scale prediction, random ordering, or continuous-valued outputs. D-AR-XL (2.09) ties with VAR-d24 (2.09) but VAR requires a non-standard next-scale prediction objective. The paper's core contribution is achieving competitive FID while preserving the vanilla Llama architecture (causal masks, 1D RoPE, standard cross-entropy loss) without any architectural modifications for vision.

## Relation to Prior Work

D-AR builds on the 1D tokenization paradigm of TiTok (Yu et al., 2024b) for encoding images into linear token sequences and the LlamaGen (Sun et al., 2024) framework for vanilla next-token-prediction autoregressive image generation, while replacing the standard VQ-VAE decoder with a flow-matching diffusion transformer (Lipman et al., 2023) operating on raw pixels. The key conceptual departure from prior visual AR work (VAR, RAR, RandAR, CausalFusion) is that the token ordering is not defined by spatial position or scale but is instead induced by the diffusion denoising schedule itself, making the coarse-to-fine structure an emergent property of the tokenizer rather than an imposed inductive bias. This distinguishes D-AR from concurrent efforts like DDT-LLama and SelfTok, which also use diffusion decoders but in reversed or recursive orders that preclude partial-token decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By requiring zero modifications to the autoregressive backbone (causal masks, KV-cache, training/inference pipelines), D-AR provides a clean architectural path toward integrating native visual generation into large language models for unified multimodal systems—a goal that diffusion-based pipelines have struggled to achieve due to their dense iterative sampling. The streaming preview and zero-shot layout-control properties are practically useful for interactive generation and controllable synthesis. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions (512×512, 1024×1024), text-to-image conditioning, and video generation, and whether the 256-token budget is sufficient for fine-grained detail at larger scales.
