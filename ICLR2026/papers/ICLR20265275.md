## Problem

LLMs exhibit hyperbolic structure in their token embeddings, suggesting potential for scientific taxonomy tasks, yet they fail at domain-specific taxonomy expansion due to long-context limits, hallucinated edges, and incomplete answers. Small Language Models (SLMs) are economical but lack the domain knowledge needed for structured knowledge enrichment. The paper asks whether knowledge can be efficiently transferred from LLMs to SLMs via geometric constraints to enable accurate taxonomy expansion without expensive LLM fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265275/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a query concept (text with optional description), an existing taxonomy represented as a text-attributed DAG, and frozen-LLM-generated natural-language explanations for each candidate edge. It produces a full ranking over all existing edges in the taxonomy (e.g., 7,313 positions for SemEval-Food), from which the top-ranked edge is broken to insert the query as a new intermediate node. An optional LLM calibration step reranks only the top-k candidates (k=10–200) to refine the final ordering.

## Method

SS-MONO (Structure-Semantic Monotonization) combines two encoder modules trained self-supervisedly. The structure-dominated encoder projects contextualized embeddings (from a fine-tuned SLM like DistilBERT-base-110M) into a Poincaré ball via exponential maps, then enforces hierarchical transitivity using nested entailment cones with a max-margin cone loss that penalizes angle violations between parent–query and query–child pairs. The context-dominated encoder uses a frozen LLM to generate edge-level descriptions, then applies self-attention over the query embedding, LLM-augmented description, 3-hop ancestors, 3-hop descendants, and the most/least similar siblings to produce a matching score via a two-layer MLP. Training is fully self-supervised: existing concepts are removed from the taxonomy to create positive samples, and hard negatives are constructed by corrupting individual components (parent, child, siblings). The total loss combines the structure cone loss with fine-grained context losses for each neighbor type, and a tiny SLM consistently outperforms frozen LLMs (GPT-4o mini, Gemma-2-9B) and domain-specific baselines across leaf and non-leaf expansion.

## Results

On SemEval-Food, SS-MONO achieves MRR 0.400 and R@10 0.325 (Total), with Leaf MRR 0.705 and Non-leaf MRR 0.147, outperforming the best non-LLM baseline TaxBox (MRR 0.359) and QEN (MRR 0.315). On MeSH, SS-MONO reaches MRR 0.427 and R@10 0.288 (Total), surpassing QEN (MRR 0.423) and TMN (MRR 0.410). On WordNet-Verb, SS-MONO achieves MRR 0.334 and R@10 0.260, beating TMN (MRR 0.291). All frozen LLM baselines (GPT-4o mini, Gemma-2-9B, Llama-3.1-8B, DeepSeek-R1-8B) perform near zero on most metrics, with GPT-4o mini reaching at best R@10 0.058 on SemEval-Food.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SS-MONO decisively outperforms all four frozen LLM baselines (GPT-4o mini, Gemma-2-9B, Llama-3.1-8B, DeepSeek-R1-8B), which achieve near-zero R@10 (≤0.058) and frequently fail to produce valid rankings due to hallucination, format errors, or context-length limits. Against the strongest non-LLM baseline TaxBox on SemEval-Food, SS-MONO (w/o AD) improves Total MRR from 0.359 to 0.430 and Non-leaf MRR from 0.133 to 0.225, while the full SS-MONO model achieves the best Leaf MRR (0.705 vs. 0.678). On MeSH, SS-MONO edges out QEN (MRR 0.427 vs. 0.423) and TMN (0.427 vs. 0.410) on Total MRR, and achieves the best Leaf R@10 (0.476 vs. QEN's 0.427). On WordNet-Verb, SS-MONO leads with MRR 0.334 and R@10 0.260, surpassing TMN (0.291/0.207) and ARBORIST (0.238/0.149), though ARBORIST retains a slight edge on Non-leaf MRR (0.149 vs. 0.122). Notably, SS-MONO (w/o AD) without LLM augmentation is already competitive, suggesting the geometric structure loss is the primary driver of gains, while LLM-augmented descriptions provide additional benefit mainly on leaf expansion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of hyperbolic representation learning (Poincaré GloVe, entailment cones) and taxonomy expansion (TMN, QEN, TaxBox), but departs from both lines in a key way: rather than using LLMs as the primary ranking model or training a dedicated GNN from scratch, it distills frozen LLM knowledge into a 110M-parameter SLM via self-supervised fine-tuning with geometric (hyperbolic + cone) constraints. The structure-semantic monotonization framework is novel in jointly enforcing hierarchical transitivity in hyperbolic space (via nested entailment cones) and contextual semantic matching (via self-attention over LLM-augmented descriptions), whereas prior taxonomy methods either model structure alone (TaxoExpan, ARBORIST) or semantics alone (QEN's sibling sampling). The LLM-to-SLM knowledge transfer pipeline, where the LLM is frozen and used only for edge description augmentation and optional calibration reranking, is a practical departure from the dominant paradigm of fine-tuning or prompting LLMs directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a DistilBERT-110M with geometric constraints consistently outperforms 8–9B frozen LLMs (GPT-4o mini, Gemma-2-9B, Llama-3.1-8B) on taxonomy expansion is practically significant for resource-constrained scientific knowledge management, suggesting that structured geometric inductive biases can substitute for raw model scale on hierarchical tasks. However, the evaluation is limited to three relatively small taxonomies (1.5K–14K nodes), the fixed 3-hop sampling depth may not generalize to very deep or wide hierarchies, and the method still depends on a frozen LLM for edge description generation, introducing a potential bias propagation channel. Open questions include whether the hyperbolic cone constraint generalizes to non-DAG ontologies, whether the approach scales to million-node taxonomies like full MeSH or SNOMED, and whether the LLM calibration step (which helps at k=50–100 but degrades at k=200) can be made more reliable as LLM context windows grow.
