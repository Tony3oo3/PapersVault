## Problem

Fine-tuned LLMs encode task-specific features in mid-layer MLPs, but the form of these representations—especially how neurons cooperate rather than act in isolation—remains unclear. Existing interpretability tools (probing, sparse autoencoders, clustering) capture correlations or statistical proximity but miss nonlinear synergistic interactions where a group's joint contribution exceeds the sum of its parts. The paper asks: which subsets of neurons form stable, functionally indispensable coalitions under LoRA fine-tuning, and how do these groups evolve across depth?

## Input / Output

The method consumes LoRA-adapted MLP weight matrices (W_down, W_up, W_gate) and neuron activations over a task dataset, from which it derives pairwise synergy scores via either weight-geometry/activation-correlation (OCA) or second-order ablation effects on layer-local logits (PAS). Outputs are disjoint partitions of neurons per layer with a heavy-tailed Zipfian size distribution (one macro-coalition >150 neurons, ~100 size-2 specialists, ~500 mid-sized groups), plus cross-layer transition labels (persist/split/merge/vanish) obtained by maximum-weight bipartite matching on interaction masses computed from consecutive-layer projection matrices.

## Method

Neurons are modeled as players in a top-responsive hedonic game where each neuron's utility in a coalition is determined by its top-k most synergistic partners (Multi-Friend Choice rule), making preferences tractable and amenable to PAC-learning guarantees. Two complementary pairwise valuations are instantiated: OCA combines orthogonal weight vectors with Pearson activation correlation, while PAS measures second-order ablation interaction (the non-additive logit change from jointly ablating a pair). The PAC-Top-Cover algorithm samples m candidate coalitions, estimates each neuron's best sampled coalition and top-k choice set, builds a preference digraph, and iteratively extracts closed sink strongly-connected components as stable coalitions, yielding an ε-PAC-stable partition with probability ≥ 1−δ. Cross-layer tracking computes an interaction mass between coalitions in consecutive layers (averaging |W_up| + |W_gate| entries weighted by source activations) and solves a maximum-weight bipartite matching to classify each transition as persistence, split, merge, or disappearance based on flow-ratio thresholds.

## Results

Across LLaMA-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B, and Pythia-6.9B LoRA rerankers on MS MARCO scalar tasks, Hedonic-PAS coalitions achieve OOD ablation drops of 0.11–0.17 (vs. 0.02–0.04 for clustering baselines, a 3–5× increase) and feature-alignment R² of 0.55–0.67 (vs. ~0.11–0.18 for K-means/Hierarchical). As macro-features in ridge regression, Hedonic-PAS yields OOD predictive R² of 0.43/0.42/0.47 on CQTR/Mean-TF/L/RM, roughly 2–3× higher than clustering baselines (~0.15–0.21). Intrinsic synergy metrics show +0.29 Pairwise and +0.49 Ratio over the strongest clustering baseline across 26 of 27 model-metric cells.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Hedonic-PAS and Hedonic-OCA against three baselines: Random partition, Spherical K-means, and Hierarchical (Ward + cosine) clustering, all matched to the same coalition size distribution. Hedonic-PAS dominates on every extrinsic metric: OOD ablation drops are 3–5× larger (e.g., 0.14 vs. 0.03 on RM/LLaMA), alignment R² with IR heuristics jumps from ~0.15 to 0.63, and predictive R² nearly doubles from ~0.21 to 0.47. Hedonic-OCA consistently ranks second, confirming that both weight-geometry (OCA) and functional-ablation (PAS) signals capture cooperative structure, but PAS's direct synergy measurement yields the strongest coalitions. On the MNIST MLP experiment, Hedonic-PAS achieves 0.91% mean accuracy drop per ablated coalition versus 0.18% (Random), 0.29% (K-means), and 0.33% (Hierarchical), while also producing the highest digit purity and stroke-alignment scores, demonstrating the framework generalizes beyond LLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the PAC-Top-Cover algorithm from computational social choice (Sliwinski & Zick, 2017; Alcalde & Revilla, 2004) and the foundational hedonic coalition formation framework (Dreze & Greenberg, 1980) to a new domain: identifying synergistic neuron groups in transformer MLPs. It departs from prior interpretability approaches—sparse autoencoders (Huben et al., 2024), probing (Gurnee et al., 2023), and activation clustering (Cao et al., 2025; Song et al., 2024)—by explicitly modeling non-additive joint effects (synergy) rather than statistical proximity or monosemantic directions. The closest prior work is Koulali & Koulali (2023), who applied hedonic games to feature selection, but this paper is the first to instantiate the framework for neuron-level interpretability in LLMs and to track coalitions across depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, game-theoretic lens for identifying functionally indispensable neuron groups that clustering and SAEs miss, with practical implications for modular editing, model comparison, and transfer learning at the coalition level. The finding that 60–75% of coalitions vanish across consecutive layers (MLPs as filters, not creators) reframes how we think about depth-wise feature evolution in fine-tuned models. Key limitations include the O(n²) ablation cost for PAS valuations, the restriction to disjoint coalitions despite known polysemy, and the absence of attention-head integration; future work on fractional hedonic games and joint sub-module analysis could address these gaps.
