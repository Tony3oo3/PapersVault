## Problem

Existing urban scene benchmarks for MLLMs are confined to static snapshots and objective recognition tasks, overlooking both the long-term temporal evolution of cities and the subjective human perception of urban environments (e.g., beauty, safety, wealth, liveliness). No prior benchmark systematically evaluates how MLLMs reason over multi-year urban change sequences or align with human affective judgments, leaving a critical gap in understanding the interaction between physical urban transformation and human experience.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264042/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes multi-temporal image pairs or sequences—single-view images from Mapillary and 360° panoramic images from Google Street View—collected from 11 cities across four continents spanning 2007–2024, paired with a text question prompt. Questions are structured in four formats: binary judgment, multiple-choice, temporal sorting (ordering images by development stage), and open-ended reasoning. Outputs range from discrete labels and option selections to chronological sequences, free-text explanations, scalar perceptual scores, and localized visual justifications that annotators mark to support explainability evaluation.

## Method

UrbanFeel organizes 14.3K questions into 11 subtasks across three cognitively progressive dimensions—Static Scene Perception, Temporal Change Understanding, and Subjective Environmental Perception—using a hybrid curation pipeline that combines spatiotemporal clustering (50 m geodesic threshold), rule-based generation from metadata, model-assisted prompting with segmentation initialization (OneFormer), and manual annotation by geography-trained annotators on the LabelU platform. A multi-stage quality-control pipeline enforces strict output formatting, uses a separate LLM to verify semantic alignment with ground truth, and applies manual review for ambiguous responses. Evaluation of 20 MLLMs is conducted zero-shot with temperature 0 and greedy decoding, using exact string matching (with LLM-assisted label extraction for verbose outputs) for objective tasks and Sentence-BERT cosine similarity (threshold τ=0.6) for open-ended answers. A human baseline is established by two independent groups of 10 geography students (one annotating, one evaluating) to ensure no role overlap, and statistical significance is assessed via Wilson score intervals and two-proportion z-tests.

## Results

On UrbanFeel (14.3K questions, 11 tasks), Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves the best overall accuracy of 65.9%, narrowing the gap to human experts (67.4%) to just 1.5%. GPT-4o follows at 59.4%, while the best open-source model, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, reaches 60.2%. Performance is highly task-dependent: most models exceed 85% on Time-Consistent Recognition (TCR), but collapse to below 10% on Temporal Sequence Reasoning (TSR) for all but the top models (Gemini-2.5-Pro: 52.1% vs. Human: 70.0%). Notably, in Pixel-level Change Recognition (PCR), Qwen2.5-VL-72B (40.9%) significantly outperforms human evaluators (21.2%), and in Global Perception (GP), Phi-4 (71.0%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (67.7%) match or exceed human consistency (66.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among closed-source models, Gemini-2.5-Pro (65.9% overall) outperforms GPT-4o (59.4%) by 6.5 points, primarily driven by superior temporal reasoning (TSR: 52.1% vs. 38.9%) and scene change recognition (SCR: 56.5% vs. 49.9%). Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-72B (60.2%) leads, surpassing Aria (54.7%), Gemma-3-27B (52.7%), and Mistral-Small-3.1-24B (51.6%), with its advantage concentrated in FSI (90.3%) and PCR (40.9%). Smaller models like DeepSeek-VL2-tiny (36.1%), Aya-vision-8B (36.4%), and LLaVA-v1.6-mistral-7B (38.1%) trail the best open-source by over 20 points, struggling particularly on TCR and SPM. In subjective perception (GP), Phi-4 (71.0%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (67.7%) match or exceed the human baseline (66.6%), while in PCR, Qwen2.5-VL-72B (40.9%) significantly surpasses humans (21.2%) due to superior pixel-level sensitivity in panoramic imagery. However, on the hardest task TSR, even Gemini-2.5-Pro (52.1%) lags humans (70.0%) by 17.9 points (p<0.01), and all open-source models remain below 26%, revealing a persistent gap in long-range temporal ordering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three previously separate research threads—street-view-based urban change detection (ChangeScore, CityPulse, Visual Chronicles), subjective urban perception modeling (Place Pulse, Yao et al., Wang et al.), and MLLM urban benchmarks (CityBench, UrBench, USTBench)—into a unified evaluation framework. Prior urban benchmarks are confined to static snapshots and objective recognition tasks, while subjective perception studies rely on traditional discriminative models on single-frame images. UrbanFeel departs from both by introducing long-term temporal sequences (15+ years) and human-centered affective perception as first-class evaluation dimensions, framed through a cognitively progressive hierarchy (static → temporal → subjective) that no prior benchmark has combined.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UrbanFeel is likely to become a standard reference benchmark for evaluating MLLMs in urban analytics, given its scale (14.3K questions, 11 cities, 20 models) and its unique coverage of temporal evolution and subjective perception. The finding that explicit chain-of-thought reasoning degrades temporal sequence ordering for SOTA models (GPT-4o drops from 46.1% to 37.0%) challenges the widespread assumption that verbose reasoning universally helps, with practical implications for prompt design in urban monitoring pipelines. Open questions include whether the geographic bias observed under city-identity intervention reflects true cultural priors or label-content mismatch, and whether the human-level parity on subjective perception (GP) generalizes beyond the annotator population's cultural context. The benchmark's lack of causal metadata and underrepresentation of Africa/South America limit its utility for policy-level reasoning.
