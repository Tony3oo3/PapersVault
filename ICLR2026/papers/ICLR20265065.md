## Problem

Tokenization (e.g., BPE) remains a handcrafted, non-learnable preprocessing step in modern language models, introducing well-documented drawbacks including poor character-level understanding, language bias, and degraded performance on modalities without natural segmentation cues. Prior tokenizer-free approaches either use content-agnostic fixed-width pooling, rely on external heuristics (delimiters, entropy thresholds), or suffer training instabilities when attempting end-to-end boundary learning, and none have matched tokenized Transformers at matched compute budget.

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw UTF-8 encoded byte sequences (8192 bytes per sequence) directly, with no vocabulary or tokenizer. Internally, the dynamic chunking mechanism compresses the byte stream to approximately 4.5–5 bytes per chunk (comparable to GPT-2 BPE token lengths), and in the 2-stage variant, further compresses to ~7 bytes per innermost chunk. Output is autoregressive next-byte prediction, and the hierarchical structure allows the main network to operate on progressively coarser representations while the decoder restores full byte-level resolution for the final prediction.

## Method

H-Net is a hierarchical U-Net-like architecture where Mamba-2 encoder/decoder networks flank a Transformer main network, connected by a novel Dynamic Chunking (DC) mechanism. DC comprises a routing module that computes boundary probabilities via cosine similarity between adjacent encoder outputs (low similarity → high boundary score), a smoothing module that applies exponential moving average to make discrete boundary selection differentiable, and a straight-through-estimator-based upsampler that restores compressed vectors to original resolution. A ratio loss inspired by MoE load balancing guides the model toward target compression ratios without sacrificing content-adaptive segmentation. The architecture is recursively composable: the main network can itself be an H-Net, enabling multi-stage hierarchies that model progressively higher-order abstractions. Additional signal-propagation techniques (residual projections, post-network norms, dimensionality-aware learning rates) stabilize end-to-end training of the discrete selection operations.

## Results

At the Large scale (760M params, FLOP-matched to GPT-3 Large), H-Net (2-stage) achieves 0.743 BPB and 55.5% average downstream accuracy, outperforming the BPE-tokenized Transformer (0.756 BPB, 53.3% avg accuracy) by 2.2 points. At the XL scale (1.3B params), H-Net (2-stage) reaches 0.715 BPB and 58.2% average accuracy versus the Transformer's 0.730 BPB and 55.5%, a 2.6-point gap. Notably, the 760M H-Net (2-stage) matches the 1.3B Transformer's average downstream performance (55.5% vs 55.5%), and on robustness to textual perturbations, H-Net (2-stage) achieves a Robustness Score of 42.8 at XL scale versus 22.2 for the Transformer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the BPE-tokenized Transformer (Llama architecture, GPT-2 tokenizer), H-Net (1-stage) matches or slightly beats it on BPB at both scales (0.755 vs 0.756 at Large; 0.728 vs 0.730 at XL), while H-Net (2-stage) clearly wins with 0.743 vs 0.756 BPB (Large) and 0.715 vs 0.730 BPB (XL), plus 2.2–2.6% higher average downstream accuracy. Against MambaByte (isotropic byte-level SSM), H-Net variants dominate across all metrics: MambaByte achieves 0.845 BPB and 44.3% average accuracy at Large, versus H-Net (2-stage)'s 0.743 BPB and 55.5%. Against SpaceByte (external delimiter-based chunking), H-Net (2-stage) wins decisively on BPB (0.743 vs 0.791 at Large) and average accuracy (55.5% vs 49.4%), while the intermediate SpaceByte++ baseline (which incorporates H-Net's architectural improvements but retains fixed space-like chunking) narrows the gap to 0.760 BPB and 53.6% accuracy, confirming that the dynamic chunking mechanism itself provides the key advantage over heuristic delimiters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the hierarchical sequence modeling line (Hourglass Transformer, MegaByte, SpaceByte) that compresses raw inputs before processing with a main network, but departs fundamentally by replacing all static or externally-supervised chunking with a learned, content-dependent dynamic chunking mechanism. It draws on U-Net's encoder-decoder structure, Mamba-2's inductive bias for fine-grained compression in the outer networks, and Mixture-of-Experts load-balancing losses (adapted into a ratio loss) to guide compression. The key departure from prior dynamic-chunking attempts (notably DPT's Gumbel-based reparameterization) is the combination of a cosine-similarity routing module, an EMA smoothing module for gradient flow, and a straight-through estimator, which together achieve training stability at scale and enable recursive multi-stage nesting—something no prior end-to-end method accomplished.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

H-Net demonstrates that the tokenization-LM-detokenization pipeline can be replaced by a single end-to-end model that learns its own segmentation, with the 2-stage variant matching a 2× larger BPE Transformer and showing ~4× data efficiency gains on DNA. The practical impact is significant: it removes a handcrafted preprocessing step, improves robustness to perturbations (42.8 vs 22.2 robustness score at XL scale), and generalizes to languages without natural word boundaries (Chinese, code). Key open questions include whether the approach scales stably beyond 1.3B parameters, whether 3+ hierarchical stages continue to help, how to close the ~2× training speed gap, and whether formal scaling laws favor H-Net over tokenized Transformers at frontier scale.
