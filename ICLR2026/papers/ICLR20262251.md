## Problem

Prior system identification methods from video are designed for single-object scenes or perform discrete material classification from a fixed library, making them ill-suited for complex multi-object scenes where objects collide, occlude each other, and exhibit coupled dynamics. No existing approach can simultaneously recover continuous, per-object constitutive parameters (stiffness, plasticity, friction) in contact-rich multi-object settings, leaving a gap between single-object physics learning and the multi-object interactions that dominate real-world scenes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 30-frame multi-view RGB sequences captured from 11 cameras evenly spaced around a hemisphere, along with camera intrinsics/extrinsics and per-frame 2D instance and material masks. It produces a 4D Gaussian reconstruction of all objects, a per-object set of continuous material parameters (e.g., Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, yield stress, viscosity, friction angle), and a simulation-ready particle set that can be rolled forward in a differentiable MPM to predict future states under novel initial conditions or permuted material assignments. Notably, parameters are estimated independently per object instance even when two objects share the same real-world material, avoiding shared-parameter assumptions that reduce identifiability.

## Method

MOSIV combines three synergistic components: (1) object-aware dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting with low-rank temporal deformation, supervised by photometric losses and 2D material masks to reconstruct per-object 4D geometry; (2) a Gaussian-to-continuum lifting that converts each object's non-uniform Gaussian field into a simulation particle set with disjoint supports and aligned grid resolutions at contact interfaces; and (3) a fully differentiable Material Point Method simulator that models inter-material contact and Coulomb friction, with parameters optimized by back-propagating geometry-aligned losses (per-object 3D Chamfer distance on surfaces and per-object 2D alpha-mask losses on silhouettes) through the MPM rollout. A key design choice is object-wise supervision rather than scene-wise: losses are computed per object to prevent the optimizer from trading deformation across bodies at contact, which would hide parameter miscalibration. Training proceeds in three stages (reconstruction, lifting, parameter optimization) with a horizon curriculum that gradually extends the rollout length and an alternating update strategy that periodically re-synchronizes particle state to reduce drift.

## Results

On the MOSIV Synthetic Dataset (45 two-object interaction sequences, 11 views, 30 frames), MOSIV achieves 31.17 PSNR / 0.975 SSIM / 0.389 CD / 0.033 EMD on observable-state simulation and 28.92 PSNR / 0.964 SSIM / 0.894 CD / 0.050 EMD on future-state simulation. These are average values across all ten material-pair combinations (elastic, elastoplastic, fluid, sand). The method also extends to a three-object benchmark, where it records 23.98 PSNR (observable) and 19.81 PSNR (future), confirming scalability to more complex contact scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOSIV outperforms both baselines across every reported metric in both observable and future simulation settings. Against OmniPhysGS-RGB (OPGS), the gains are substantial: observable PSNR improves by +5.24 dB (31.17 vs. 25.93) and future PSNR by +9.92 dB (28.92 vs. 19.00), while Chamfer Distance drops from 11.79 to 0.389 (observable) and 51.92 to 0.894 (future), indicating far more accurate geometric tracking. Notably, even the Oracle variant of OPGS (given ground-truth material models) underperforms MOSIV on CD (43.50 vs. 0.389 observable), showing the gap is not merely a material-selection issue but a fundamental limitation of the discrete expert-library design. Against CoupNeRF*, MOSIV leads on PSNR (+5.29 dB observable, +8.30 dB future), SSIM, and EMD, while also achieving lower CD (0.389 vs. 0.927 observable; 0.894 vs. 1.859 future), demonstrating that the continuous per-object parameterization with geometry-aligned supervision yields more stable long-horizon rollouts than the implicit NeRF + MPM hybrid. The ablation in Table 3 further confirms that object-wise (rather than scene-wise) supervision is the critical factor: replacing it with naive global losses inflates CD from ~0.7 to ~22, a 30× degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOSIV builds on the Gaussian-to-continuum lifting paradigm from GIC (Cai et al., 2024) and the dynamic Gaussian reconstruction architecture from DynMF (Kratimenos et al., 2024), but departs fundamentally from prior system-identification-from-video methods in two ways: it replaces discrete material classification (as in OmniPhysGS, which selects from a fixed expert library) with continuous per-object parameter optimization, and it extends from single-object or freefall settings (as in CoupNeRF) to contact-rich multi-object interactions governed by a differentiable MPM simulator. The geometry-aligned supervision (per-object Chamfer + silhouette losses) is a key methodological departure from scene-level photometric or SDS objectives used by baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing multi-object system identification from video and releasing a contact-rich synthetic benchmark with ground-truth physical parameters, this paper provides a concrete evaluation target that prior single-object or discrete-classification methods cannot address. The dramatic ablation showing that object-aware supervision reduces Chamfer distance by ~30× over scene-wise losses highlights a practical pitfall for anyone extending system ID to multi-object settings. Key open questions remain: the method relies on predefined constitutive models (neo-Hookean, Drucker-Prager, etc.), is computationally intensive (grid resolution 4096³, 200 substeps per frame), and has only been validated on synthetic 2–3 object scenes with 5 material types, leaving the sim-to-real gap and unknown-material generalization unresolved.
