## Problem

Existing prior-informed Bayesian optimization methods are either overly complex, tightly coupled to specific acquisition functions, or highly sensitive to inaccurate expert priors. There is no simple, general framework that incorporates expert knowledge about the optimum's location at the surrogate-model level while remaining robust when the prior is misleading and compatible with arbitrary acquisition functions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260030/cropped_page4_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a black-box objective function, an expert prior distribution π(x) over the optimum location (typically a Gaussian or Gaussian mixture, but any valid distribution works), a search space X ⊆ ℝ^d, a GP kernel, and a small set of hyperparameters (decay rate λ, initialization ratio ρ, scaling A, shift B). It outputs the best observed point x* and its function value after N evaluations. A key design choice is that the expert prior enters as a probability distribution over the search space rather than a point estimate, and the method requires no modification to the acquisition function, making it a drop-in replacement for standard BO.

## Method

DynMeanBO embeds the expert prior directly into the GP mean function as m_prior(x) = A·π(x) + B, shaping the surrogate's prior belief about where the function peaks. To prevent a misleading prior from permanently biasing the search, the mean is dynamically decayed each iteration via m_n(x) = γ_n·m_prior(x) + (1−γ_n)·μ_0(x) with γ_n = exp(−λ(n−N_0)), so the expert's influence fades exponentially toward the standard zero-mean BO behavior. Initialization hybridizes ρ-fraction Sobol samples with (1−ρ)-fraction samples drawn from π(x) to seed the surrogate near the expert's favored region. Because the intervention is purely at the mean-function level, the method is agnostic to the acquisition function (validated with PI, EI, LogEI, TS, UCB, KG, and MES) and adds negligible computational overhead. The paper proves that under both EI and UCB, DynMeanBO achieves the same asymptotic convergence rate as standard BO, with the prior-induced perturbation vanishing as γ_n → 0.

## Results

DynMeanBO is evaluated on 8 benchmarks (5 synthetic functions in 4–7D and 3 PD1 HPO tasks in 4D) plus 2 high-dimensional 20D tasks, using 7 acquisition functions (PI, EI, LogEI, TS, UCB, KG, MES). Under a 'good' expert prior (mean offset 10% from optimum, variance 20% of search-space width), DynMeanBO consistently accelerates convergence relative to standard BO across all acquisition functions, with the largest gains in the early evaluation budget. Under a 'bad' expert prior (mean offset 70% from optimum), DynMeanBO quickly recovers to standard BO performance, while per-iteration computational overhead remains negligible (nearly identical to vanilla BO). The paper reports results as convergence curves (best-so-far value vs. number of evaluations) rather than single-point metrics, with 10 random seeds for synthetic tasks and 5 for HPO tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DynMeanBO against two prior-informed BO methods: πBO (Hvarfner et al., ICLR 2022) and ColaBO (Hvarfner et al., ICLR 2024). Under a 'good' expert prior, all three methods achieve comparable optimization performance across synthetic and HPO benchmarks, but DynMeanBO incurs substantially lower per-iteration computational cost than both πBO and ColaBO (which rely on Monte Carlo sampling). Under a 'bad' expert prior, DynMeanBO demonstrates markedly superior robustness: it quickly approaches standard BO performance, whereas πBO degrades significantly (the largest gap among all comparisons) and ColaBO also suffers notable performance loss. On the PD1-LM1B task, even the misleading 'bad' prior still points to a high-quality suboptimal region, so all prior-informed methods (including DynMeanBO) converge faster than vanilla BO despite the inaccuracy. In terms of computational efficiency, ColaBO is the slowest (especially with MES, which requires nested Monte Carlo sampling for mutual information estimation), while DynMeanBO's overhead over standard BO is negligible since it only modifies the GP mean function without adding inference components.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynMeanBO sits within the growing line of prior-informed Bayesian optimization, departing from approaches that embed expert knowledge in the kernel (Ramachandran et al., 2020), the acquisition function (πBO, Hvarfner et al., 2022), or as additional surrogate-model priors (ColaBO, Hvarfner et al., 2024). Its key conceptual move is placing the expert prior directly in the GP mean function—a component that standard BO typically sets to zero—and coupling it with an exponential decay schedule inspired by πBO's diminishing-influence idea but applied at the model level rather than the acquisition level. This makes the method agnostic to the choice of acquisition function and avoids the Monte Carlo machinery that ColaBO requires, while retaining the principled Bayesian structure of the GP posterior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners running expensive black-box optimizations (HPO, materials discovery, robotics control), DynMeanBO offers a drop-in modification to standard BO that leverages readily available expert intuition without the implementation complexity or computational overhead of existing prior-informed frameworks. The convergence guarantees under EI and UCB, showing that the dynamic decay does not alter the asymptotic rate, provide theoretical reassurance that the method cannot be worse than vanilla BO in the limit. Open questions include how the method behaves with structured or non-Gaussian priors in higher dimensions (beyond the 20D tested), whether the decay rate λ can be adapted online rather than fixed a priori, and how it interacts with multifidelity or parallel evaluation settings the authors flag as future work.
