## Problem

Existing generative models for MILP (e.g., DIFUSCO, IP-Guided-Diff) model only the distribution of integer variables, ignoring the strong coupling between integer and continuous variables. This partial modeling creates an information bottleneck that prevents the use of instance-specific objective and constraint feedback during inference, leading to suboptimal heuristic solutions. FMIP addresses this by being the first generative framework to jointly model the complete distribution of both variable types.

## Input / Output

The method takes an MILP instance (A, b, l, u, w) and encodes it as a tripartite bipartite graph with integer variable nodes, continuous variable nodes, and constraint nodes, where edge weights are the nonzero entries of A and node features include objective coefficients and bounds. During generation, the current noisy solution state x_t = (d_t, c_t) is appended to variable node features, making the graph time-dependent. The output is a complete solution vector (integer assignments d_1 and continuous assignments c_1) plus a full probability distribution over integer variables, which together serve as a warm-start for downstream solvers like Predict-and-Search or Neural Diving.

## Method

FMIP employs conditional flow matching over a mixed continuous-integer solution space, using a shared GNN backbone (e.g., Tri-GCN) with two prediction heads: a regression head for denoised continuous values and a cross-entropy head for the integer categorical distribution, trained jointly via a weighted sum of L2 and log-likelihood losses. The key architectural innovation is the holistic guidance mechanism, which at each inference step evaluates a target function f(x) = w^T x + γ·Σ[max(0, Ax_i − b_i)]² on the jointly predicted solution candidate. For continuous variables, gradient descent on f steers the predicted values toward lower objective and feasibility; for integer variables, a sampling-and-reweighting scheme reweights the rate matrix R using B candidate integer solutions scored by f, with a temperature parameter controlling guidance strength. This guidance is only possible because the model produces a complete (d, c) pair at every step, a capability absent in integer-only baselines. The framework is backbone-agnostic (validated on Tri-GCN, Bi-GCN, GAT, ClusterGCN) and solver-agnostic (validated on ND, PS, PMVB, Apollo-MILP), with a cosine time schedule allocating more steps near t=1 for refinement.

## Results

FMIP achieves a 41.34% average relative reduction in primal gap over the best ML baseline across eight MILP benchmarks and four downstream solvers. The most dramatic gains appear on challenging benchmarks: on MIS with Neural Diving (400s), FMIP reduces the GAP from 9.70 to 0.40 (95.88% improvement), and on Load Balancing with Predict-and-Search (600s), it achieves zero gap (100% improvement over the best baseline). On simpler benchmarks like CA and GIS where all methods reach the optimal solution, FMIP ties with the best baselines. FMIP's inference time (0.1–2.1s per instance) is comparable to other generative baselines and represents less than 1% of total solver runtime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FMIP is compared against three learning-based baselines (SL, DIFUSCO, IP-Guided-Diff) and the commercial solver Gurobi (3600s). Against SL (one-step supervised prediction), FMIP consistently dominates across all benchmarks and solvers, with the largest gaps on FCMNF (141,593 vs. 160,200 GAP under ND) and MIS (0.40 vs. 9.70 GAP). Against DIFUSCO (graph-based diffusion for ILP, adapted to MILP), FMIP wins on every benchmark where differentiation exists; DIFUSCO is particularly weak on MIS (GAP 10.80 vs. FMIP's 0.40) and LB (GAP 11.83 vs. 0.40). Against IP-Guided-Diff (guided discrete diffusion), FMIP achieves the largest relative improvements on MIS (95.88% GAP reduction) and LB (93.79% GAP reduction under ND), while matching on CA and GIS where both reach optimality. Gurobi with 3600s serves as the BKS reference; FMIP matches Gurobi's objective on CA, GIS, FCMNF, and SC within 400–800s, and approaches it on LB and IP, demonstrating that the joint continuous-integer modeling is most critical on benchmarks with substantial continuous-variable coupling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative modeling approach to MILP heuristics (DIFUSCO, IP-Guided-Diff) by moving from integer-only distribution modeling to joint continuous-integer flow matching, building directly on the flow matching framework of Lipman et al. (2023) and the training-free guidance mechanism of TFG-Flow (Lin et al., 2025). The key conceptual departure is that prior generative methods treat MILP as effectively an ILP problem by ignoring continuous variables, creating an information bottleneck; FMIP closes this gap by modeling the full solution space and using the complete solution candidate at every generation step to compute instance-specific guidance from both the objective and constraint violations. The graph representation extends the standard bipartite variable-constraint graph by explicitly distinguishing integer and continuous variable nodes as heterogeneous types.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 41.34% average primal gap reduction across eight benchmarks and four downstream solvers, combined with backbone-agnostic compatibility, makes FMIP a practical drop-in improvement for existing MILP solving pipelines. The ablation clearly isolates the contribution of joint modeling (the 'w/o Continuous' variant is worst) and holistic guidance, providing strong evidence that the integer-continuous coupling is not merely a detail but a structural necessity for MILP. A notable limitation is performance degradation on ultra-large instances (>70,000 variables), where the relative gap widens to ~23%, suggesting the current graph backbone struggles with extreme scale. Open questions include whether a customized solver co-designed with the generative process could further close the gap, and whether the adaptive guidance balancing strategy generalizes beyond the single dataset (IP) on which it was validated.
