## Problem

Video diffusion transformers (vDiTs) require 50–100 denoising steps over millions of tokens, making a single 4-second 720p video take over 0.5 hours on an H100 GPU. Existing acceleration methods (step skipping, block caching, token selection) rely on hand-crafted heuristics and manual hyperparameter tuning, leaving the fundamental question unanswered: given a performance target, which tokens are worth computing at each denoising step to minimize quality loss?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263051/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ASTRAEA is a training-free, inference-time acceleration layer that wraps existing vDiT models (HunyuanVideo-T2V, Wan v2.1 1.3B, OpenSora v1.2). It takes a standard text prompt and produces a generated video at the model's native resolution (e.g., 480P for 2s/4s clips, 544×960 for 5s clips). The only additional user-facing parameter is a total token budget percentage (e.g., 40%, 50%, 70%), which controls the speedup–quality trade-off; the per-timestep allocation is determined automatically by the search framework.

## Method

ASTRAEA combines three components. First, a lightweight token selection metric S_token = w_α·S_sig + w_β·S_penalty, where S_sig is the product of the previous timestep's LSE score (a free byproduct of softmax) and the input-token difference across adjacent computed timesteps, and S_penalty is an exponential penalty for consecutive non-selection; this avoids storing the full N×N attention map. Second, a sparse attention scheme that computes only selected tokens for Q while retaining all tokens for K and V, preserving correct LSE normalization, requiring no extra GPU memory beyond cached tokens, and remaining natively parallelizable with FlashAttention. Third, an evolutionary algorithm (population K=50, P=30 offspring, G=30 generations) that searches over per-timestep token budgets {θ_i} under a total budget constraint, using uniform/block crossover, annealed mutation, and a repair step, evaluated by MSE against the full-computation output on 4 diverse prompts.

## Results

On Wan (4s), ASTRAEA 40% achieves 2.29× speedup on A100 (67.61s vs 155.01s) with a VBench score of 79.78% (vs 80.28% original, <0.5% loss) and 26.98 dB PSNR, outperforming the strongest baseline (SVG2 at 22.95 dB) by over 4 dB. Across all five model configurations, ASTRAEA consistently delivers the highest speedup (up to 2.4× on a single GPU, 13.2× on 8 GPUs) while maintaining VBench scores within 0.5% of the original. ASTRAEA also uses substantially less GPU memory (11.71 GB on Wan 4s) compared to ToCa (38.40 GB), which OOMs on HunyuanVideo and OpenSora (4s) on A6000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASTRAEA outperforms all five baselines on both speedup and image fidelity (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS) across every model configuration. Against the closest competitor SVG2 (70%), ASTRAEA 40% achieves 2.29× vs 1.33× speedup on Wan (4s) and 26.98 dB vs 22.95 dB PSNR — a >4 dB gap. Against ToCa, ASTRAEA wins on speedup (2.29× vs 1.63×), PSNR (26.98 vs 18.13 dB), and memory (11.71 vs 38.40 GB); ToCa also OOMs on HunyuanVideo and OpenSora (4s) on A6000. Against PAB, ASTRAEA 40% delivers 2.29× vs 1.71× speedup with 7.5 dB higher PSNR. Against Δ-DiT, ASTRAEA achieves 1.86× vs 1.14× speedup and 12 dB higher PSNR, though Δ-DiT retains a marginal VBench advantage on OpenSora (2s) (78.09% vs 77.45% for ASTRAEA 50%) at essentially no speedup (1.01×). The ablation (Table 2) confirms that the block-level token selection (vs. timestep-level or fixed-token variants) is critical: FIXED-TOKEN drops VBench by >2% and SELECT Q&K (naive sparse attention) degrades PSNR to 18.10 dB, matching ToCa's level.

## Relation to Prior Work

ASTRAEA extends the token-level caching line initiated by ToCa (Zou et al., 2024b), which first proposed selecting unimportant tokens to skip in diffusion transformers, but departs from it in two critical ways: replacing ToCa's expensive composite metric (which requires storing the full attention map) with a lightweight LSE-based significance score, and redesigning sparse attention to compute only selected Q tokens while retaining all K and V tokens. The broader positioning sits within the training-free acceleration landscape for vDiTs, alongside step-reduction methods (PAB, AutoDiffusion), block-caching methods (Δ-DiT, SmoothCache), and spatial-temporal sparsity methods (SVG, SVG2). The evolutionary algorithm for token budget allocation draws from NAS and pruning literature but is specifically adapted to avoid their prohibitive search costs, requiring only ~82 GPU hours versus thousands for NAS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work makes token-level acceleration practically deployable on real GPUs by eliminating the memory bottleneck that caused prior token-wise methods (ToCa) to OOM on standard hardware, and by automating the previously manual hyperparameter search through EA. The 2.4× single-GPU speedup with <0.5% VBench loss and 13.2× multi-GPU scalability addresses a real deployment bottleneck for video generation services. Open questions include whether the EA search (82 GPU hours) can be further reduced or amortized across model updates, whether the timestep-level budget granularity (a simplification over block-level) loses meaningful quality at higher compression ratios, and whether the method generalizes to next-generation vDiT architectures without re-searching.
