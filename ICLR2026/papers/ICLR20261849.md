## Problem

Feature lifting—attaching dense 2D feature descriptors (CLIP, DINO, etc.) onto 3D splat primitives—lacks a unified mathematical formulation, theoretical guarantees on solution quality, and explicit handling of multi-view noise. Existing methods are fragmented across training-based, grouping-based, and heuristic-forward families, each restricted to specific splat kernels and feature types without provable error bounds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261849/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes precomputed splat geometry (3DGS, 2DGS, or Deformable Beta Splats), camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and per-pixel dense feature maps (384–512 channels) from foundation models such as SAM+OpenCLIP, DINOv2, or MaskCLIP. It produces one feature vector per splat primitive, which directly supports open-vocabulary 3D segmentation (reported as mIoU), text-query 2D attention maps, and object localization. The entire lifting pipeline completes in under 10 minutes on a single RTX 4090, scaling linearly with the number of views and feature channels, and handles 512+ channel features without OOM errors.

## Method

The paper formulates feature lifting as a sparse linear inverse problem AX = B, where A encodes alpha-blending weights from the splat rendering pipeline, X is the unknown per-primitive feature, and B is the observed 2D feature. A closed-form row-sum preconditioner solves the system, and the authors prove a (1+β) approximation bound on the global optimal error under any convex loss via Jensen's inequality, where β measures feature dispersion along each viewing ray. Two complementary regularizers stabilize the solution: Tikhonov Guidance compresses the sigmoid opacity activation (λ=1.2) to polarize alpha values and enforce diagonal dominance in A, while Post-Lifting Aggregation clusters lifted features with HDBSCAN, projects cluster labels back to 2D, and discards SAM masks whose IoU with the projected cluster falls below a threshold. The formulation is both kernel-agnostic (works identically for 3DGS, 2DGS, and DBS) and feature-agnostic (any dense embedding), and an automatic histogram-based threshold selection eliminates manual per-object tuning.

## Results

On the LeRF-OVS benchmark (4 scenes: Figurines, Teatime, Ramen, Waldo Kitchen), Splat Feature Solver achieves a mean mIoU of 65.1%, setting a new state-of-the-art for open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation. The method outperforms the strongest grouping-based baseline LAGA (64.0%) by 1.1 mIoU and the strongest training-based baseline VLGS (62.0%) by 3.1 mIoU. Feature lifting completes in approximately 3 minutes per scene (vs. ~89 minutes for LAGA), and the method is agnostic to both splat kernel type (3DGS, 2DGS, DBS) and feature backbone (CLIP, DINO, DINOv2, MaskCLIP, ViT, ResNet), achieving cosine similarity above 80% across all tested descriptors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Splat Feature Solver (65.1% mean mIoU) outperforms all 15 baselines across the four LeRF-OVS scenes. Against the closest competitor LAGA (64.0%), it gains +1.1 mIoU while running ~28× faster (3 min vs. 89 min). Compared to the strongest training-based method VLGS (62.0%), it improves by +3.1 mIoU without any scene-specific optimization. Against heuristic-forward baselines, the gains are substantial: +3.8 over OccamLGS (61.3%), +15.6 over DrSplat (49.5%), and +10.7 over N2F2 (54.4%). The largest margins are over training-based methods: +34.0 over LangSplat (31.1%) and +39.9 over LeGaussian (25.2%). On the 3D-OVS dataset (Table 6), the method matches or exceeds LAGA (86.3/87.7 vs. 86.3/84.9 mean mIoU depending on ground-truth correction), confirming generalization beyond LeRF-OVS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and provides theoretical grounding for a family of heuristic forward-lifting methods (CosegGaussians, Occam's LGS, DrSplats) that independently converged on the same row-sum weighting rule without formal justification. It reframes feature lifting as a sparse linear inverse problem (AX=B) derived directly from the alpha-blending rendering equation, then proves a (1+β) approximation bound under convex losses via Jensen's inequality. The two regularization modules—Tikhonov Guidance (polarizing opacity to enforce diagonal dominance) and Post-Lifting Aggregation (clustering-based mask filtering)—draw on classical inverse-problem stabilization and LAGA's clustering, respectively, but are applied in a training-free, closed-form setting. Unlike prior training-based (LangSplat, LeGaussian) or grouping-based (LAGA, OpenGaussian) approaches, the method is explicitly kernel-agnostic (3DGS, 2DGS, DBS) and feature-agnostic (CLIP, DINO, ViT, ResNet), whereas most prior work is locked to SAM+CLIP on 3DGS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first provable upper bound on feature-lifting error gives practitioners a principled way to reason about solution quality without expensive multi-view training, and the ~3-minute runtime (vs. ~1.5 hours for LAGA) makes high-quality 3D semantic enrichment practical for large-scale or iterative pipelines. The finding that apparent view-dependent semantic variation often stems from SAM mask noise rather than genuine viewpoint change reframes how the community should think about multi-view consistency in feature lifting. Open questions include extending the framework to sparse descriptors (e.g., SIFT), extracting structured view-dependent residuals for novel-view synthesis, and validating the (1+β) bound empirically on larger, more diverse scene collections beyond the four LeRF-OVS scenes.
