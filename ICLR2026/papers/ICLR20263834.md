## Problem

Existing data curation methods for LLM training (selection, mixing, balancing) operate offline, freezing sample importance based on a proxy model before training begins. This static, subtractive paradigm overfits to the chosen validation benchmark, sacrifices data diversity through hard filtering, and requires the entire pipeline to be re-run whenever the model or corpus changes. The paper asks whether data curation can instead be performed online, dynamically adjusting each sample's contribution as the model's representations evolve during training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full training corpus (e.g., ~590M SlimPajama files for pretraining, or ~270K instruction samples from FLAN V2, CoT, DOLLY, and Open Assistant 1) together with a small validation anchor set (e.g., 50 examples per benchmark across 8 tasks, or the 285-example MMLU validation set). No samples are removed; instead, the method produces per-sample loss weights in [0,1] computed on-the-fly from the model's own hidden representations, which are refreshed every R training steps. The output is a trained language model (TinyLlama-120M for pretraining, LLaMA-2-7B with LoRA for instruction tuning) whose effective data distribution shifts implicitly over the course of training without altering the dataset size.

## Method

ADAPT casts data curation as online per-sample reweighting: at each training step, it computes a position-weighted mean-pooled embedding from the model's own last-layer hidden states (later tokens receive higher weights to counteract causal-mask bias), then measures cosine similarity between each training sample's embedding and the validation anchor embeddings. A temperature-scaled sigmoid converts these similarity scores into absolute weights in [0,1] that act as per-sample learning-rate multipliers in the gradient update, so high-similarity samples take larger effective steps while low-similarity ones are downweighted. Crucially, anchor embeddings are recomputed from the current model parameters every R steps, making the weighting signal track the evolving representation space rather than a frozen proxy. This design yields an implicit curriculum: early in training the model clusters samples by coarse patterns, and as representations sharpen, the similarity signal progressively shifts focus toward fine-grained semantic distinctions, all while preserving the full dataset and incurring only ≤1.1×10¹⁵ FLOPs of overhead.

## Results

On instruction tuning (LLaMA-2-7B + LoRA), ADAPT achieves 50.7% MMLU accuracy and 44.8% BBH accuracy, outperforming LESS (50.2% / 38.7%) and full-dataset SFT (49.7% / 44.4%) under equal FLOPs. On large-scale pretraining (TinyLlama-120M, 50B tokens, SlimPajama), ADAPT attains 38.00% average accuracy across 15 benchmarks and 33.73% on unseen tasks, surpassing Uniform (37.81% / 31.98%), DoReMi (37.32% / 31.95%), and LinUpper (37.03% / 30.56%). The FLOPs overhead of ADAPT (≤1.1×10¹⁵) is orders of magnitude lower than offline methods like DoReMi (4.92×10¹⁹) and RegMix (3.07×10¹⁸). At 100B tokens, ADAPT further improves to 38.36% average accuracy vs. 37.98% for Uniform, with lower validation perplexity (15.36 vs. 15.94).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADAPT consistently outperforms all seven baselines on the unseen-task average in pretraining (33.73% vs. 31.98% Uniform, 30.56% LinUpper, 31.95% DoReMi, 32.46% RegMix), with the largest margin over LinUpper (+3.17%) and DoReMi (+1.78%). In instruction tuning, ADAPT's key advantage is cross-benchmark generalization: it scores 44.8% on BBH when selected via MMLU validation, versus only 38.7% for LESS (a 6.1-point gap), while LESS actually overfits to its in-domain MMLU benchmark (50.2% vs. ADAPT's 50.7%). ADAPT also beats the simpler BM25 baseline (48.7% MMLU / 42.3% BBH) and the embedding-based baseline (47.0% / 40.1%) on both in-domain and out-of-domain metrics. Against RegMix, ADAPT achieves comparable overall accuracy (38.00% vs. 37.97%) but with ~2800× less FLOPs overhead (≤1.1×10¹⁵ vs. 3.07×10¹⁸), and a clear +1.27% advantage on unseen tasks. Notably, LinUpper (the only other online reweighting baseline) performs worse than Uniform sampling on average (37.03% vs. 37.81%), suggesting that naive per-batch loss normalization is inferior to ADAPT's global sigmoid-weighted similarity scoring.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies offline data selection (LESS, BM25 filtering, PPL-based pruning) and offline data mixing (DoReMi, RegMix) as special cases of a single data-reweighting formalization, then departs from the offline paradigm by proposing online, model-state-adaptive sample reweighting. The closest prior online method, LinUpper (Sow et al., 2025), uses normalized per-batch loss as a weight signal; ADAPT instead uses position-weighted cosine similarity between the model's own hidden representations and a validation anchor set, transformed through a temperature-scaled sigmoid to yield absolute per-sample learning-rate multipliers. The conceptual lineage draws on influence-function ideas (LESS) and curriculum learning, but reframes them as a dynamic, in-loop weighting mechanism that preserves the full training corpus rather than hard-filtering it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is clear: online reweighting eliminates the brittle multi-stage offline pipeline (proxy model → scoring → retraining) and its associated FLOPs overhead, while the FLOPs-aware evaluation protocol gives practitioners a fairer comparison framework. However, the empirical gains are modest (≤1% average accuracy in pretraining on a 120M model; ~1% in instruction tuning with LoRA on 7B), and the method requires a curated anchor/validation set that must be refreshed periodically. Open questions include whether the advantage persists at much larger scales (70B+), how the method interacts with deduplication, and whether the implicit curriculum effect (coarse-to-fine similarity shift across epochs) can be exploited more explicitly for safety-oriented or domain-specific curation.
