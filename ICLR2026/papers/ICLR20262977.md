## Problem

Standard RLHF/RLAIF pipelines rely on a single reward model, which limits alignment quality due to no single RM dominating all domains, high inference costs for large RMs, and overfitting to one RM's biases. Existing routing methods like LASER address this with per-batch LinUCB selection but suffer from coarse-grained routing, premature exploitation (collapsing to one arm), and slow cold-start. The paper tackles how to perform per-query RM selection with effective exploration and warm-start while maintaining O(1) RM calls per step.

## Input / Output

The router takes a full preference pair (prompt concatenated with each of two candidate responses) and encodes it via a shared SmolLM2-135M-Instruct encoder into a fused embedding using element-wise sum and absolute difference passed through a single-layer MLP. Unlike LASER which conditions only on the prompt, this design captures how the semantic contrast between the two responses affects which RM is most reliable. The output is a single selected RM from a pool of N candidates (N=4 in main experiments), which then annotates the pair with a chosen/rejected label used for online DPO training, preserving O(1) RM calls per query.

## Method

BayesianRouter is a two-stage hybrid: (1) an offline multi-task router trained on 50K preference pairs (HelpSteer3 + RM-Bench) with a Bradley-Terry ranking head (primary, trained on disagreement samples) and a binary classification head (auxiliary, trained on all samples) that learns per-RM embedding vectors via inner products with preference-pair embeddings; (2) an online Bayesian Thompson sampling router where each RM maintains a Gaussian posterior over a weight vector, and for each query the router samples from each posterior and selects the RM with the highest sampled score, then updates the posterior with the observed reward (negative DPO loss, batch-baseline-centered and quantile-normalized). The key integration insight is that the offline BT embeddings directly initialize the online posterior means (μ_n^(0) = E_bt[n]), providing a principled prior injection that mitigates cold-start and improves early routing accuracy without requiring a manually tuned combination weight. This replaces LASER's per-batch LinUCB with per-query Bayesian posterior sampling, enabling uncertainty-aware exploration that avoids premature collapse to a single RM.

## Results

BayesianRouter achieves the best scores across all five benchmarks: 63.23 on AlpacaEval-2, 58.75 on MT-Bench, 66.20 on Chat-Arena-Hard, 75.66 on GSM8K, and 57.39 on MMLU. These gains are consistent over the SFT baseline (50.00/50.00/50.00/67.63/54.29) and over the strongest single RM (RM1: 61.86/56.25/64.80/74.22/57.03), with margins of +1.4 to +3.8 points depending on the benchmark. The method also outperforms its own ablations (w/o offline and w/o online) on every benchmark, confirming the complementary contribution of the offline prior and the online Bayesian update.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UWO (Coste et al., 2023), which downweights high-disagreement preference pairs via consensus-rate weighting, BayesianRouter wins on all five benchmarks by margins of +1.5 to +2.6 points (e.g., 63.23 vs. 61.74 on AlpacaEval-2; 75.66 vs. 74.30 on GSM8K), while requiring only O(1) RM calls per query versus UWO's O(N). Against LASER (Nguyen et al., 2024), the prior per-batch LinUCB routing method, BayesianRouter achieves substantially larger gains of +2.7 to +7.5 points (e.g., 63.23 vs. 60.50 on AlpacaEval-2; 58.75 vs. 51.25 on MT-Bench), attributable to per-query (rather than per-batch) routing and Bayesian Thompson sampling's superior exploration. Notably, even the w/o offline ablation (60.99 on AlpacaEval-2) exceeds LASER (60.50), indicating that the per-query Bayesian selection mechanism alone is already superior to LASER's batch-level LinUCB approach. BayesianRouter also surpasses the best single RM (RM1) by +1.4 to +1.4 points, demonstrating that dynamic routing aggregates complementary RM strengths beyond what any fixed RM can achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LASER (Nguyen et al., 2024), the first method to apply contextual-bandit-based RM routing in RLHF, in three key ways: shifting from per-batch to per-query routing, replacing LinUCB's point-estimate optimism with Bayesian Thompson sampling, and injecting offline-learned RM-strength embeddings as Gaussian priors to solve the cold-start problem. The offline router's multi-task design (Bradley-Terry ranking plus per-RM classification) draws on preference-learning objectives but applies them to a novel meta-learning objective—predicting which RM will be most reliable for a given pair. The broader RM ensembling literature (Coste et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024) motivates the problem, but BayesianRouter's O(1) routing constraint distinguishes it from aggregation-based ensembles that require O(N) RM calls per query.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in RLHF/RLAIF pipelines where a single fixed RM limits alignment quality and risks reward hacking, and provides a principled, low-overhead solution that scales with the RM pool size. However, the evaluation is limited to 4–8 small RMs and the DPO family; the authors explicitly note that PPO compatibility is untested, and the reward signal (negative DPO loss) is an indirect proxy for RM quality that may not transfer cleanly to other training objectives. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger or more heterogeneous RM pools, whether cost-aware routing (jointly optimizing accuracy and inference cost) can be integrated, and how sensitive the method is to the quality and diversity of the offline preference data used for prior initialization.
