## Problem

Current inference-time guidance and scaling methods for diffusion models optimize the quality of individual samples in isolation, yet real-world applications (e.g., Midjourney, Adobe Firefly) present users with groups of 4–8 images per prompt where independent i.i.d. sampling produces redundant, low-diversity results. This paper addresses the missing formulation for jointly optimizing both individual sample quality and inter-sample diversity within a generated group, and for doing so efficiently at scale.

## Input / Output

The method takes a conditioning signal (text prompt, depth map, or subject image) and a large pool of M initial noise vectors (M up to 128) as input, and outputs a curated group of K images (typically K=4) that are jointly high-quality and diverse. Unlike standard diffusion inference that generates K independent samples, the method generates M candidates and progressively selects the best K, with the scoring functions (CLIP text-image similarity for quality, DINOv2 pairwise distance for diversity) being swappable to accommodate non-differentiable metrics such as multimodal LLM scores. The method operates entirely at inference time without modifying the base generative model.

## Method

The core formulation casts group selection as a Quadratic Integer Programming (QIP) problem over binary selection variables, with a unary term (e.g., CLIP similarity) rewarding individual sample quality and a binary term (e.g., 1 − cosine DINOv2 similarity) promoting pairwise diversity, solved via branch-and-cut with Gurobi. The key efficiency innovation is progressive pruning: at each denoising step, intermediate x̂₀ predictions are decoded and scored, and the QIP is solved to shrink the candidate set by a fixed ratio ρ, reducing total model evaluations from O(MT) to O(M+KT) (e.g., 184 vs. 1280 for M=64, K=4, T=20, ρ=0.5). This is justified by strong Spearman correlations (r > 0.7 after 5 steps for multi-step models, r > 0.95 after 1 step for distilled models) between scores computed on intermediate predictions and final outputs. The framework is model-agnostic, supports arbitrary (including non-differentiable) scoring functions, and naturally extends to different diversity notions (color-based, semantic, depth-based) by simply swapping the binary term.

## Results

On FLUX.1 Dev (the primary model), the proposed Group Inference achieves CLIP 0.326, HPSv2 0.307, and DINO diversity 0.711, simultaneously improving both quality and diversity over I.I.D. sampling (CLIP 0.324, DINO 0.558). The method dominates the quality-diversity Pareto front across all three tested models (FLUX.1 Dev, FLUX.1 Schnell, SD3-M), with user preference studies showing 81–88% preference over baselines for both diversity and quality. Progressive pruning reduces compute from 1280 to 184 model evaluations (~85% reduction) for M=64, K=4, T=20, achieving O(M+KT) complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method wins on the joint quality-diversity objective against all seven baselines. Against Particle Guidance (Corso et al., 2023), Ours achieves substantially higher quality (CLIP 0.326 vs. 0.276, HPSv2 0.307 vs. 0.199) and higher diversity (DINO 0.711 vs. 0.564), since Particle Guidance's trajectory modification pushes samples off the data manifold. Against CADS (Sadat et al., 2025), Ours achieves far better quality (CLIP 0.326 vs. 0.165, FID 23.21 vs. 51.91) though CADS edges out on raw DINO diversity (0.784 vs. 0.711); however, CADS's outputs are severely degraded (negative ImageReward of −2.204). Against NegToMe (Singh et al., 2024) and Shielded Diffusion (Kirchhof et al., 2025), Ours wins on both quality and diversity (e.g., DINO 0.711 vs. 0.663 and 0.627 respectively). Against DiverseFlow (Morshed & Boddeti, 2025), Ours dominates on both axes (CLIP 0.326 vs. 0.282, DINO 0.711 vs. 0.635). Against Interval Guidance (Kynkänniemi et al., 2024), Ours achieves higher quality (HPSv2 0.307 vs. 0.270) and diversity (DINO 0.711 vs. 0.681). Against Inference Diffusion Scaling (Ma et al., 2025), Ours achieves better group-objective scores at substantially lower runtime, as the latter only optimizes individual quality without pairwise diversity terms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reframing diverse group generation as a post-hoc selection problem rather than a trajectory-modification problem. Prior diversity methods (particle guidance, CADS, DiverseFlow, Shielded Diffusion) all intervene during the denoising process to push samples apart, while inference-time scaling work (Ma et al., 2025) searches for better individual samples. Here, the authors instead generate a large candidate pool via standard i.i.d. sampling and then solve a quadratic integer program to select a subset that jointly optimizes quality and diversity, with a progressive pruning strategy that exploits the (empirically validated) reliability of intermediate x̂₀ predictions as scoring proxies. The QIP formulation is borrowed from classical combinatorial optimization and applied to generative model inference for the first time in this context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical gap: real platforms (Midjourney, Adobe Firefly) present 4–8 images per prompt, yet prior research optimizes only individual sample quality. The 85% compute reduction from progressive pruning makes group inference feasible at scale, and the score-function-agnostic QIP formulation allows practitioners to plug in task-specific quality/diversity metrics (including non-differentiable ones from multimodal LLMs). Key limitations include dependence on the base model's inherent diversity (mode collapse in the candidate pool cannot be fixed by selection) and the assumption that scoring functions are fast relative to denoising. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation, whether the QIP can be replaced by learned selection to avoid solver overhead, and how it interacts with future distilled or one-step generators where intermediate predictions may be less informative.
