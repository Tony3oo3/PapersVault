## Problem

Existing computational EEG models are confined to narrow classification tasks (seizure detection, sleep staging) and cannot perform the holistic, multi-faceted interpretation that a clinical expert provides. A critical bottleneck preventing the application of Large Vision-Language Models to EEG is the absence of large-scale datasets pairing EEG visualizations with fine-grained, expert-level textual annotations suitable for instruction tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260430/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a rendered 10-second EEG waveform image (19-channel 10-20 system with average reference, or a smaller PSG subset with mastoid reference; signals band-pass filtered at 1.6–70 Hz, notch-filtered at 50/60 Hz, downsampled to 200 Hz) together with a free-form text instruction in ChatML format. Outputs span three instruction-following formats—free-text description, complex multiple-choice answer, and multi-turn clinical conversation—plus, via the companion CerebraGloss-YOLO detector, per-channel bounding boxes localizing nine waveform classes (spikes, sharps, spike/sharp-and-slow-wave complexes, K-complexes, sleep spindles, high-frequency noise, blinks, and lateral eye movements).

## Method

CerebraGloss is built by two-stage continued post-training on Qwen2.5-VL-3B. Stage 1 aligns EEG visual concepts by fine-tuning only the vision-to-language projector (visual encoder and LLM decoder frozen) on 1.4 M programmatically generated EEG image-caption pairs mixed with 558 K general-domain pairs, stopping early at ~0.05 epochs to avoid overwriting pre-trained reasoning. Stage 2 instruction-tunes the LLM decoder and projector (visual encoder still frozen) on a diverse mixture of 100 K rule-based MCQs, 94 K Gemini-2.5-Flash-augmented instructions (descriptions, complex MCQs, conversations), 50 K rule-based captions, and 50 K general-domain samples. The training data is produced by an automated pipeline: a bespoke YOLOv3-inspired 1D detector (CerebraGloss-YOLO, using inception modules, channel self-attention with learnable position embeddings, and a feature-pyramid neck with sparse anchors) localizes nine waveform types; a background module extracts dominant frequency and amplitude; an artifact module flags EMG, EOG, respiration, electrode noise, and flat lines. These structured annotations feed rule-based caption templates, which are then augmented by Gemini 2.5 Flash under tightly constrained one-shot prompts to yield the final 94 K instruction examples.

## Results

On the TUSZ seizure detection task, CerebraGloss-3B achieves a new state-of-the-art balanced accuracy of 79.21%, surpassing the previous best (Gram at 78.29% and ELM-MIL at 78.27%). On the HMC sleep staging task, CerebraGloss-3B reaches 62.02% balanced accuracy, competitive but below the top specialized EEG models (Gram at 69.97%). On the novel CerebraGloss-Bench, CerebraGloss-3B achieves 80.00% MCQ accuracy, 44.19% ROUGE-1 for descriptions, and a 4.76 GPT-5 judge score for conversational QA, outperforming GPT-5 (70.00%, 37.07%, 4.58) and all other LVLMs tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CerebraGloss-3B sets a new SOTA on TUSZ (79.21% balanced accuracy), beating the closest specialized EEG models Gram (78.29%) and ELM-MIL (78.27%) by ~1%, and substantially outperforming CNN-Transformer (75.53%) and EEGNet (65.53%). On HMC sleep staging, CerebraGloss (62.02%) trails the best LEM Gram (69.97%) by ~8%, which the authors attribute to the task's need for multi-minute temporal context that a 10-second-segment model cannot capture. On the novel CerebraGloss-Bench, CerebraGloss-3B decisively outperforms GPT-5 (80% vs. 70% MCQ; 44.19% vs. 37.07% ROUGE-1; 4.76 vs. 4.58 GPT-5 judge score) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (52.22% MCQ), while biomedical LVLMs LLaVA-Med and BioMedGPT fail entirely on MCQs and score near-chance on descriptions (8.87% and 11.82% ROUGE-1). The base model Qwen2.5-VL-3B improves dramatically from 55.02% to 79.21% on TUSZ after instruction tuning, confirming the value of the domain-specific data pipeline. On MMBench, CerebraGloss retains general vision-language capabilities with only marginal degradation versus the base model, indicating no significant catastrophic forgetting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the domain-specific LVLM post-training paradigm (established by LLaVA-Med, BioMedGPT, and FoodLMM) to the EEG domain, building on Qwen2.5-VL-3B as the base model and adapting the YOLOv3 detection architecture for 1D waveform localization. It deliberately departs from prior EEG-language work (ELM-MIL, EEG-CLIP, NeuroLM) that either performs coarse-grained signal-text alignment or reframes classification as multiple-choice, by introducing a fully generative, multi-turn interpretive paradigm. The key methodological departure is the automated data generation pipeline—combining a bespoke YOLO-based waveform detector, rule-based captioning, and LLM-augmented instruction synthesis—to overcome the fundamental bottleneck of scarce expert-annotated EEG-text pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes the first end-to-end pipeline for training a generative EEG interpreter and releases both the model and a (small) open-ended benchmark, providing a concrete starting point for the field's shift from classifiers to interpreters. The CerebraGloss-YOLO detector and the data generation engine are reusable tools that lower the barrier for future EEG-language research. Key limitations include the image-based (rather than signal-native) input representation, hallucination of non-existent waveforms inherited from automated annotation noise, and HMC sleep staging performance still trailing specialized LEMs—suggesting that holistic interpretation and narrow classification optimization remain in tension. Open questions include whether direct signal-to-text modeling can surpass the image-based approach, how temporal context beyond 10-second windows affects interpretive accuracy, and whether the pipeline generalizes to other neurophysiological modalities.
