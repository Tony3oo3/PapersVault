## Problem

Scaling Transformers to ultra-long contexts is bottlenecked by the O(n²d) cost of self-attention. Existing efficient-attention methods reduce this cost along the sequence axis (local windows, kernel approximations, token-level sparsity) but consistently degrade accuracy, leaving dense attention as the most reliable option at long ranges. This paper asks whether feature-level sparsity—an orthogonal, previously underexplored axis—can reduce attention cost without collapsing representational capacity.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard Transformer hidden states X ∈ R^{n×d_model} and produces the usual attention output O_h = P_h V_h per head. The key structural difference is that Q and K are converted to k-sparse codes (only the k largest-magnitude coordinates retained per token, stored in CSR/CSC format), while V remains dense. Attention scores are computed exclusively over overlapping active coordinates between query and key supports, so the effective score matrix is sparse with O(nk) nonzeros per row rather than O(n) dense entries.

## Method

SFA applies a row-wise Top-k operator to Q and K, preserving only the k largest-magnitude entries per token, then computes attention scores via sparse matrix multiplication (CSR for Q̃, CSC for K̃ᵀ) so that each query interacts only with keys sharing the same active coordinate, reducing arithmetic from Θ(n²d) to Θ(n²k²/d). To avoid materializing the n×n score matrix, FlashSFA extends FlashAttention's IO-aware tiling and online-softmax pipeline with sparse feature-intersection kernels that iterate over active features, intersect supports via binary search, and scatter-add into a compact tile buffer. Backward computation uses a straight-through estimator so gradients flow only through selected coordinates. For adapting pretrained dense models, a regularized fine-tuning objective adds an MSE term between SFA's attention output and the stop-gradient dense output to mitigate the distribution shift introduced by sparsification.

## Results

On Qwen3-0.6B pretraining, SFA(k=16) achieves a PPL of 4.81 (vs. 4.66 for dense) and average accuracy of 38.94% (vs. 39.40%), while delivering a 2.3× speedup (34.20 ms vs. 77.65 ms at 128k context). On GPT-2-350M, SFA(k=8) reaches PPL 16.78 (vs. 15.03 dense) with 29.57% average accuracy (vs. 30.51%), at 23.67 ms vs. 46.78 ms. The method reduces FLOPs by 49% and KV-cache memory by 41% relative to dense attention, and on synthetic NIAH at 32k context, SFA(k=8) maintains 82% retrieval accuracy (vs. 80% dense) while running 1.3× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFA consistently outperforms short-embedding baselines (Dense d=32/64) on both PPL and accuracy: on Qwen3-0.6B, SFA(k=16) achieves PPL 4.81 vs. 6.03 for Dense(d=64) and Avg-Acc 38.94% vs. 36.68%, while on GPT-2-350M the gap widens to PPL 16.78 vs. 19.89. Against token-level methods, SFA is orthogonal and composable: combining SFA with Longformer reduces decode latency from 6.75 ms to 5.23 ms (≈1.3× faster) with only a modest PPL increase (18.73→19.30), and NSA+SFA(k=16) on Qwen3-0.6B achieves PPL 4.57 (slightly better than dense 4.66) at 9.73 ms decode vs. 80.84 ms dense. Among feature-level baselines, SFA(k=16) on Qwen3-0.6B (PPL 4.81, Avg 38.94%) substantially outperforms Low-Rank (PPL 5.50, Avg 35.78%) and Short(d=64) (PPL 6.03, Avg 35.08%), while MLA achieves comparable PPL (4.69) but at much higher forward latency (68.92 ms vs. 34.20 ms). On GPT-2-124M, SFA(k=8) (PPL 18.17, Avg 27.40%) beats Performer (PPL 19.72, Avg 21.56%) and LoKI (PPL 17.82, Avg 23.73%) on accuracy while matching or exceeding their speed. KV-pruning methods (H2O, Quest, SnapKV) only accelerate decode (not forward) and SFA adds further forward-latency reduction when combined (e.g., SnapKV+SFA: 9.41 ms forward vs. 16.86 ms SnapKV alone). Quantization (QAT) provides modest speedup but SFA(k=8) achieves lower PPL (18.17 vs. 17.64) at comparable decode speed (14.12 vs. 14.26 ms) on GPT-2-124M.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FlashAttention lineage (Dao et al., 2022; Dao, 2024) by replacing dense tile multiplications with sparse feature-intersection kernels, preserving the IO-aware tiling and online-softmax machinery while operating on k-sparse query/key codes. It deliberately departs from the dominant token-level sparsity paradigm (Longformer, BigBird, Routing Transformers, H2O, SnapKV, Quest) by introducing an orthogonal axis: sparsifying the feature coordinates used to score any retained token pair rather than pruning which tokens interact. In contrast to low-rank and kernel approximations (Linformer, Performer, Nyströmformer) that compress into a dense r≪d subspace, SFA retains the full d-dimensional feature space but activates only k≪d learned coordinates per token, computing exact scores over support overlaps — a design closer in spirit to sparse lexical embeddings (SPLADE, CSR) from information retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SFA establishes feature-level sparsity as a practical, composable axis for efficient attention: it reduces FLOPs by ~49% and KV-cache by ~41% while matching dense baselines on perplexity and downstream accuracy, and it stacks multiplicatively with token-level sparsity and KV-pruning methods. Key limitations temper the impact: experiments are confined to small models (≤8B), the theoretical 64×–1000× arithmetic reduction is not fully realized in wall-clock time due to sparse-index overhead, and GPU hardware still lacks native support for the required sparse tensor operations. Open questions include adaptive per-token sparsity budgets, optimal joint optimization of token-level and feature-level sparsity, and whether the quality preservation holds at the 70B+ scale where feature redundancy may differ.
