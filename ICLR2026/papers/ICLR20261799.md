## Problem

Psychiatric comorbidity—the co-occurrence of multiple disorders—complicates diagnosis and treatment, yet existing dialogue datasets and LLM evaluations focus almost exclusively on single disorders. The few multi-condition corpora lack fine-grained symptom-level annotations and fail to capture how symptoms co-occur and progress within a structured diagnostic interview, leaving no large-scale resource for training or evaluating multi-disorder screening in a single conversational pass.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes 502 structured EMRs (each with seven sections: demographics, chief complaint, medical condition, medical history, personal history, family history, preliminary diagnosis) derived from Reddit posts in the PsySym dataset, paired with 50 fictitious experience descriptions per EMR and one of five doctor profiles varying in empathy, verbosity, and diagnostic pacing. It outputs 3,000 Chinese multi-turn diagnostic dialogues averaging 45.9 turns (vs. 21–27 in prior corpora), each terminating in a comorbidity label drawn from six predefined combinations of MDD, AD, BD, and ADHD. Utterance lengths (34.0 doctor / 43.5 patient characters) are calibrated to match real clinical transcripts (28.3 / 35.8).

## Method

The core architecture is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (PsyCoProfile) converts raw social-media posts into clinically structured EMRs via modular extraction: dual classifiers produce binary symptom-event vectors for chief complaint and medical condition, keyword-based segmentation handles histories, and rule-based parsing extracts demographics; GPT-4o-mini generates each section, with iterative psychiatrist review ensuring validity. Stage 2 (PsyCoTalk) runs a three-agent system (doctor, patient, tool) on Qwen2.5-72B, where the tool agent orchestrates a Hierarchical Diagnostic State Machine (HDSM) and a Diagnostic Context Tree (DCT). The HDSM is a three-level graph (High-Level → Intermediate-Level → Basic-Level states) restructured from SCID-5-RV, covering 130+ diagnostic states across four disorder sub-machines with binary present/absent thresholds and sub-state-group voting rules; the DCT dynamically injects family history, personal history, and experience-inquiry branches to maintain contextual coherence. The patient agent consults a Disease-Symptom Description Knowledge Graph (DSD-KG) to reject hallucinated symptom affirmations, while the doctor agent rotates through five persona profiles to reduce stylistic repetition. Each EMR yields up to 50 unique dialogues by combinatorially pairing fictitious experiences with personal histories and two scheduling strategies (symptom-informed vs. random sub-state-machine order).

## Results

PsyCoTalk contains 3,000 multi-turn diagnostic dialogues averaging 45.9 turns, with utterance lengths of 34.0 characters (doctor) and 43.5 (patient), closest to real clinical conversations (28.3/35.8). In the psychiatrist AB-test for perceived realism, PsyCoTalk scores 5/10, second only to real-world data (6/10), while D4 scores 4 and MDD-5k scores just 1. Expert evaluation across six dimensions yields strong scores: professionalism 7.72, doctor initiative 8.14, patient engagement 8.24, syntactic coherence 7.42, redundancy avoidance 6.79, and overall similarity 6.67. On diagnostic accuracy, the HDSM-guided multi-agent system achieves 0.31 subset accuracy (all 5 disorders correct), significantly outperforming the zero-shot Qwen2.5-72B baseline (0.22; McNemar's p=7×10⁻⁶), with per-label F1 of MDD 0.92, AD 0.81, ADHD 0.64, and BD 0.40.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against D4 (Yao et al., 2022), PsyCoTalk nearly doubles the average dialogue length (45.9 vs. 21.6 turns) and is the only dataset supporting comorbidity, while D4 covers only single-disorder depression. In the AB-test, PsyCoTalk (score 5) outperforms D4 (score 4) in perceived realism, and its utterance lengths (34.0/43.5 chars) are much closer to real clinical conversations than D4's shorter responses (20.4/14.9). Against MDD-5k (Yin et al., 2025), PsyCoTalk achieves a substantially higher AB-test realism score (5 vs. 1), attributed to MDD-5k's templated and repetitive utterances; PsyCoTalk's utterance lengths (34.0/43.5) are also far closer to real-world values (28.3/35.8) than MDD-5k's overly long responses (91.1/162.8). However, MDD-5k retains advantages in total dialogue count (5,000 vs. 3,000) and disorder coverage (>25 vs. 4 disorders). Overall, PsyCoTalk wins on structural fidelity to real clinical practice, comorbidity support, and perceived realism, while MDD-5k leads in scale and breadth of single-disorder coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-agent diagnostic dialogue generation paradigm established by the AMC framework (Lan et al., 2024) and MDD-5k (Yin et al., 2025) by replacing their random or flat topic scheduling with a Hierarchical Diagnostic State Machine (HDSM) and Diagnostic Context Tree (DCT) explicitly derived from the SCID-5-RV structured interview protocol. The key conceptual departure is shifting from single-disorder to comorbidity: prior corpora (D4, MDD-5k, PsyQA) treat each illness in isolation, whereas PsyCoTalk requires the dialogue to elicit and reason over multiple co-occurring disorders simultaneously within one conversational pass. The EMR construction pipeline also departs from MDD-5k's template-sampling approach by grounding synthetic records in real social-media symptom reports (PsySym) and generating personalized fictitious experiences aligned to each EMR to avoid semantic conflicts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PsyCoTalk is the first large-scale, psychiatrist-validated dataset for psychiatric comorbidity diagnostic dialogue, filling a gap that limits the development of multi-disorder screening models. However, coverage is restricted to four disorders (MDD, AD, BD, ADHD) and six comorbidity combinations, and the dataset is Chinese-only, limiting cross-lingual and cross-cultural applicability. The modest diagnostic accuracy (0.31 exact-match for all five disorder labels with HDSM guidance vs. 0.22 zero-shot) and the reliance on Reddit-derived demographic signals raise open questions about whether the pipeline generalizes to rarer conditions, other languages, and whether the dataset meaningfully improves downstream model performance beyond what single-disorder corpora already provide.
