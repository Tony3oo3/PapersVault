## Problem

Accurate simulation of coupled multiphysics systems is challenging because tightly coupled solvers are computationally prohibitive, while loosely coupled or surrogate-based approaches require expensive coupled training data or lack theoretical reliability guarantees. Existing generative methods either focus on single-physics problems or learn directly from coupled data, leaving open the question of how to learn coupled physics from accessible decoupled training data while maintaining fidelity, efficiency, and provable error bounds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260280/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, GenCP consumes decoupled single-field solver data where each physical field evolves while the others are held frozen (e.g., fluid fields computed with a fixed structure, and structural fields computed with a fixed fluid), using linear interpolation from Gaussian noise to the target as the flow-matching path. At inference, given initial field states (sampled from a Gaussian prior) and boundary conditions, the method produces coupled multiphysics predictions as joint distribution samples over all fields simultaneously. For FSI benchmarks, the output is a four-channel field (velocity u, v, pressure p, and signed distance field SDF) on a shared spatial domain; for the nuclear-thermal setting, it produces neutron flux, fuel temperature, and fluid temperature fields over a 2D geometry.

## Method

GenCP reformulates coupled physics simulation as probability density evolution in the product of function spaces, where the joint state u=(f,g) evolves under a velocity field v=v^(f)+v^(g). Training uses flow matching to learn two conditional velocity operators separately: v̂_f maps (f_t, g_t, t)→F (evolving f with g as condition) and v̂_g maps (f_t, g_t, t)→G (evolving g with f as condition), each trained on its respective decoupled dataset with mean-square loss against the instantaneous interpolation derivative. At inference, Lie-Trotter operator splitting recombines the two learned conditional flows by alternately evolving f (with g frozen) and g (with f frozen) at each of N flow steps, effectively performing a 'conditional-to-joint' sampling that merges separate probability transitions into a coherent joint distribution transition. The paper proves a Wasserstein-1 error bound W_1(μ_1^(τ,learn), μ_1) ≤ C_stab(τ + ε_f + ε_g), where τ is the splitting step size and ε_f, ε_g are the uniform approximation errors of the learned velocity fields, establishing that both time resolution and regression accuracy control the total error. Neural operators (FNO* or CNO) serve as the backbone architectures for the velocity field estimators.

## Results

On the Turek-Hron FSI benchmark, GenCP-FNO* achieves an average relative L2 error of ~0.101 across four fields (u, v, p, SDF), reducing error by ~26.77% over the best baseline, while GenCP-CNO reduces error by ~12.54%. Inference time drops dramatically: GenCP requires only 19.50s (FNO*) or 16.25s (CNO) versus 277.20s (M2PDE-FNO*) and 347.00s (M2PDE-CNO). On the more strongly coupled Double Cylinder, error reductions reach ~34.44% (FNO*) and ~42.85% (CNO). On the Nuclear-Thermal Coupling task, GenCP-CNO achieves error reductions of 58.2% over M2PDE and 78.8% over the Surrogate paradigm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenCP is compared against two paradigms, each instantiated with FNO* and CNO neural operator backbones: (1) M2PDE (Zhang et al., 2025), which trains a diffusion model per decoupled field and uses DDIM denoising of one field as the condition for the other, and (2) a Surrogate-based Picard iteration approach that trains an end-to-end neural network on decoupled data and applies iterative inference at test time. GenCP wins on every field and every benchmark. On Turek-Hron, GenCP-FNO* reduces average coupled-test error by ~26.77% over the best baseline (Surrogate-FNO*) and ~42% over M2PDE-FNO*, while also being ~14× faster than M2PDE and ~5× faster than Surrogate. On Double Cylinder, the advantage widens to ~34% (FNO*) and ~43% (CNO) error reduction, with GenCP-CNO achieving SDF error (0.0055) nearly matching the Joint Training upper bound (0.0034). On Nuclear-Thermal Coupling, GenCP-CNO achieves 78.8% error reduction over Surrogate-CNO on the fluid field (0.0330 vs 0.3086). The only scenario where baselines are competitive is the SDF field on Turek-Hron with the Surrogate paradigm (0.0112 vs 0.0081 for GenCP-FNO*), but the paper notes this is because the surrogate's deterministic prediction masks its failure to capture oscillatory bending dynamics visible in qualitative comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenCP sits at the intersection of flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022; Kerrigan et al., 2023) and classical operator splitting theory (Trotter, 1959; Strang, 1968), repurposing the latter as a mechanism to compose separately learned conditional velocity fields into a joint generative process. The closest prior work, M2PDE (Zhang et al., 2025), also performs conditional-to-joint sampling in a diffusion framework but embeds coupling iterations within each denoising step without theoretical guarantees. GenCP departs from surrogate-based iterative approaches (Gauss-Seidel, ADMM-like) by adopting a probabilistic generative formulation, and from monolithic neural operators by requiring only decoupled training data. The key conceptual departure is framing the coupling problem as probability density evolution in function space, where Lie-Trotter splitting provides a principled and provably convergent recombination scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in multiphysics simulation: coupled training data is at least 5× more expensive to generate than decoupled data, and existing generative approaches either require coupled data or lack reliability guarantees. By providing a Wasserstein-1 error bound of the form C(τ + ε_f + ε_g), GenCP offers the first theoretical guarantee for a conditional-to-joint generative sampling scheme, which is absent in prior generative multiphysics work. Open questions include whether higher-order splitting (Strang) further reduces error, how the method scales beyond two fields to the m-field case mentioned in the paper, and whether the bounded-trajectory assumption (A1) holds in strongly coupled or chaotic regimes where trajectories may escape the region where learned fields are accurate. The 10-step inference requirement versus 277–347 s for baselines suggests practical adoption potential in iterative design or real-time simulation settings.
