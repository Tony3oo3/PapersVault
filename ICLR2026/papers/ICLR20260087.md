## Problem

Current kinematics-based 3D human motion capture methods rely on Euler angles for joint orientation, which suffer from discontinuities at 0 and 2π and gimbal-lock singularities. These representation issues cause unstable, jittery motion reconstructions that are particularly severe in online settings where offline trajectory refinement is unavailable. The paper asks whether quaternion-based joint representations, combined with proper constrained integration, can eliminate these artifacts while maintaining real-time performance.

## Input / Output

QuaMo operates online, consuming only a single future reference pose (from TRACE or HMR2.0) at each time step at 25 Hz, with no access to future frames. It outputs a 24-joint quaternion pose in SMPL format, which is mapped to a 6890-vertex body mesh and 17 3D keypoints via a linear joint regressor, plus a root translation trajectory. The system is trained on 100-frame sub-sequences with the first frame root-aligned to the world origin.

## Method

QuaMo formulates human motion as a discrete-time state-space model with two coupled differential equations: a quaternion differential equation (QDE) solved exactly on the unit sphere S³ via the Hamilton product (avoiding the approximation errors of Euler integration), and an ODE for angular velocity integrated via Euler steps. The angular acceleration is driven by a data-driven meta-PD controller whose gains (κ_P, κ_D), bias, and a novel acceleration-enhancement scaling (κ_A) are predicted by a lightweight ControlNet (two FC layers, hidden dim 512) conditioned on the current quaternion state, angular velocity, and reference pose. The acceleration enhancement computes a second-order quaternion difference over the last three reference poses, adaptively amplifying control signals during rapid pose changes and dampening them near the target to reduce overshoot. Training uses a combined local L1 keypoint loss, a global second-order finite-difference consistency loss, and a shape regularization term, supervised end-to-end over 35 epochs.

## Results

On Human3.6M, QuaMo with HMR2.0 as input achieves state-of-the-art MPJPE of 46.7 mm, P-MPJPE of 30.6 mm, and G-MPJPE of 88.8 mm among all template-based kinematics methods, while also producing the smoothest online motion (Accel 5.3, G-Accel 6.8, FS 4.3%). Compared to its direct online competitor OSDCap (same TRACE input), QuaMo reduces MPJPE by 6.3% (51.3 vs 54.8), Accel by 32.1% (5.7 vs 8.4), and G-MPJPE by 12.5% (116.2 vs 132.8). On the more challenging Fit3D and SportsPose datasets, QuaMo_TRACE further outperforms OSDCap on all metrics (e.g., MPJPE 50.3 vs 58.7 on Fit3D; Accel 5.3 vs 10.9 on SportsPose). On AIST, QuaMo_HMR2.0 (MPJPE 89.1, FS 13.0%) surpasses the offline DiffPhy method (MPJPE 105.5, FS 19.6%) despite being fully online.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QuaMo outperforms its direct online kinematics competitor OSDCap (Le et al., 2024) on every reported metric using the same TRACE input: MPJPE 51.3 vs 54.8, Accel 5.7 vs 8.4, G-MPJPE 116.2 vs 132.8, and FS 6.6% vs 15.2%. Against the offline trajectory-optimization method TrajOpt (Gärtner et al., 2022), QuaMo_HMR2.0 achieves a far lower MPJPE (46.7 vs 84.0) while maintaining a competitive FS of 4.3% (vs 4.0%). Compared to the multi-frame window methods DnD (Li et al., 2022) and PhysPT (Zhang et al., 2024), QuaMo_HMR2.0 achieves lower MPJPE (46.7 vs 52.5/52.7) using only a single future frame, though PhysPT reports a lower Accel (2.5 vs 5.3) due to its seq2seq transformer architecture. On AIST, QuaMo_HMR2.0 (MPJPE 89.1, FS 13.0%) beats the offline DiffPhy (Gärtner et al., 2022) by 15.6% in MPJPE and 34% in FS, despite DiffPhy having access to the full sequence. The paper also shows that QuaMo improves upon its own input estimators: using TRACE as input, QuaMo reduces MPJPE by 8.6% and FS from 80.3% to 6.6%; using HMR2.0, it reduces Accel by 41.8% and FS by 62.6%.

## Relation to Prior Work

QuaMo extends the online kinematics-based motion capture framework established by Shimada et al. (2021, NeurPhys) and Le et al. (2024a, OSDCap), which use a meta-PD controller within a state-space model to predict joint velocities from vision-based pose estimates. The key departure is replacing Euler-angle joint representations with quaternions, motivated by the well-known discontinuity problem at 0/2π that destabilizes online integration. Additionally, QuaMo introduces an exact quaternion integration under the unit-sphere constraint S³ (replacing the approximate Euler/first-order Runge-Kutta integration used in prior work) and a novel second-order acceleration enhancement term that adaptively modulates PD control signals based on the rate of change in reference poses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and well-motivated limitation in online kinematics-based motion capture: Euler angle discontinuities cause visible jitter and unstable reconstructions in real-time settings where offline trajectory refinement is unavailable. The quaternion QDE formulation with S³-constrained integration is a clean, principled fix that could be adopted broadly by the kinematics-based motion capture community. However, the method remains limited to single-person, contact-free scenarios; the authors explicitly note that incorporating environment interactions and contact modeling is left for future work, which is a significant gap for applications like biomechanics or autonomous driving where human-scene interaction matters.
