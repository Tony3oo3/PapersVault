## Problem

Existing objective metrics for audio source separation (SDR, SI-SDR, PESQ, STOI, and learned MOS predictors) conflate two fundamentally distinct failure modes—interference leakage from competing sources and self-distortion of the target signal—into single global energy ratios or black-box scores. No prior measure family can simultaneously disentangle these two failure modes, operate at frame-level granularity (up to 75 fps), and provide formal error guarantees on its own decisions, leaving a gap between objective evaluation and subjective human perception.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three waveform streams per time frame: the audio mixture, per-source reference waveforms, and per-source system output waveforms, at frame sizes of L=400 samples (speech, 16 kHz) or L=324 (music). Each reference is augmented with 60–70 hand-crafted perceptual distortions (clipping, notch/comb filters, pitch shift, reverberation, additive noise, etc.), and all resulting waveforms are independently encoded by a pretrained self-supervised model (wav2vec 2.0 for speech, MERT for music) at up to 75 frames per second. The outputs are two frame-level scores in [0,1]—PS (leakage) and PM (self-distortion)—along with a deterministic truncation error radius and a non-asymptotic high-probability confidence interval for each, enabling per-frame reliability assessment.

## Method

The MAPSS pipeline first constructs a perceptual cluster around each reference by applying a bank of fundamental distortions, then independently encodes all references, distortions, and system outputs with a frozen self-supervised encoder (e.g., wav2vec 2.0 layer 2, M=1024). All encoded representations are projected onto a low-dimensional manifold via diffusion maps (α=1 density normalization, t=1 Markov step, d∈[20,40] retained dimensions with τ=0.99 eigenvalue threshold), whose key property aligns Euclidean distances on the manifold with diffusion distances in the high-dimensional space. PS quantifies leakage by computing the Mahalanobis distance from the output embedding to its own perceptual cluster versus the nearest non-attributed cluster, forming a ratio that approaches 1 when the output is well-separated from competitors. PM quantifies self-distortion by fitting a Gamma distribution to the Mahalanobis distances of the distortion cloud around the reference, then evaluating the regularized upper incomplete Gamma function at the output's distance to the reference, yielding a smooth tail-probability score. The paper further derives, via Schur-complement block inversion of the full versus truncated covariance, a deterministic truncation error radius, and via dependence-adjusted effective sample sizes (Bartlett) with Hanson-Wright concentration inequalities, non-asymptotic 95% confidence intervals for both measures at the frame level.

## Results

On the SEBASS database, PS and PM achieve SRCC/PCC of 84.12%/83.74% (English), 82.33%/85.01% (Spanish), 72.87%/77.38% (Music w/ Drums), and 87.23%/87.81% (Music w/o Drums), placing them first or second among 18 measures in nearly all scenarios. The waveform-only ablation (PS/PM without self-supervised encoding) drops by 10–25 percentage points, confirming the critical role of perceptual representations. Deterministic error radii never exceed 1.39%, and 95% confidence intervals for PM are tighter (3.83–8.98%) than for PS (4.11–30.03%), indicating PM is statistically more robust.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PS and PM dominate in both music scenarios, achieving the highest or near-highest SRCC and PCC (e.g., PS 87.23%/87.81% and PM 88.12%/85.26% on Music w/o Drums), outperforming PESQ (61.26%/60.24%), ViSQOL (78.75%/70.31%), and all SDR-family metrics by 10–30+ points. In English, PESQ narrowly edges PS on SRCC (85.56% vs. 84.12%) and PM trails PESQ on PCC (86.36% vs. 84.05% is actually PM winning), while STOI and eSTOI remain competitive but consistently below PS/PM. In Spanish, PESQ and SDR-based metrics (SI-SDR 84.07%, SDR 84.29%) slightly surpass PS on SRCC (82.33%), though PS/PM lead on PCC. Against learned black-box metrics, PS/PM are overwhelmingly superior: DNSMOS-OVRL drops to 35.34% SRCC on Spanish, UTMOS goes negative on Music(Drums), and VQScore/Sheet-SSQA/NORESQA all fall below 60% in most scenarios. The waveform-only ablation (PS 73.42% vs. 84.12% English SRCC) and the comparison with SpeechBERTscore (68.58% vs. 84.12%) confirm that both self-supervised encoding and diffusion-map geometry are essential, with the latter providing a clear advantage over cosine-similarity-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on diffusion maps (Coifman & Lafon, 2006) as the geometric backbone and pre-trained self-supervised encoders (wav2vec 2.0, MERT) for perceptual representation, but departs fundamentally from prior source-separation metrics. Existing measures—SDR/SIR/SAR family, PESQ, STOI, and learned black-box scores like DNSMOS or SpeechBERTscore—either conflate leakage and self-distortion into a single scalar, operate only at utterance level, or lack any uncertainty quantification. The key conceptual departure is constructing hand-crafted perceptual distortion clusters around each reference, embedding them on a diffusion manifold, and defining two complementary Mahalanobis-distance-based scores (PS for leakage, PM for self-distortion) that are differentiable, frame-level, and come with non-asymptotic error guarantees derived via Schur-complement truncation analysis and Hanson-Wright concentration inequalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first evaluation framework for source separation that functionally disentangles the two dominant failure modes (cross-talk leakage vs. target distortion) at 75 fps resolution with quantified uncertainty, enabling practitioners to diagnose *why* a system fails rather than just *how badly*. The differentiability opens a path to using PS/PM as training losses or curriculum triggers, and the error bounds support fairer benchmarking. Limitations include sensitivity to temporal misalignment beyond ~20 ms, higher statistical variance in PS compared to PM, and the fact that only one backbone was tested for Spanish and music scenarios. Open questions include whether the perceptual-geometric hypothesis (diffusion distance ≈ perceptual dissimilarity) generalizes beyond the SEBASS domain, and whether the heuristic aggregation strategies (PESQ-inspired for PS, simple average for PM) can be made data-driven to close the remaining gap with PESQ in certain scenarios.
