## Problem

Existing personality control methods for LLMs—prompting and fine-tuning—treat personality as static and monolithic, unable to capture the dynamic, compositional nature of human behavioral traits. Prompting suffers from instability and inconsistency, while fine-tuning requires substantial resources for each personality configuration. The paper asks whether personality traits can be represented as extractable, approximately orthogonal directions in activation space that support algebraic operations, enabling training-free compositional and dynamic personality control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265153/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes user text input, a persona description, and ten pre-extracted OCEAN trait-pole vectors (one per pole of the Big Five dimensions) obtained from a single optimal residual layer (e.g., layer 20 for Qwen2.5-7B). At each conversational turn, a predict-then-steer pass outputs incremental steering coefficients (range −2 to +2) per dimension, which are combined into a composite vector v_composite = Σ α_i · v_i and injected into the residual stream via h_l ← h_l + αv_l. The final output is a personality-steered text response whose trait expression is modulated by the composite vector, with coefficients exceeding a sparsity threshold of |α| > 0.5 contributing to the composite.

## Method

PERSONA operates through three integrated stages. PERSONA-BASE extracts ten orthogonal trait vectors via contrastive activation analysis: a frontier LLM generates paired trait-eliciting and trait-suppressing system prompts, the target model produces responses under both conditions, and the persona vector is computed as the difference between mean residual-stream activations at a single empirically selected layer. PERSONA-ALGEBRA validates that these vectors form a coherent algebraic system—scalar multiplication yields proportional BFI-44 score changes (Pearson r > 0.9), vector addition composes traits predictably, and subtraction enables targeted trait suppression. PERSONA-FLOW extends this to dynamic inference-time control: before each response, an intermediate inference pass predicts per-dimension steering coefficients from conversational context, and the resulting composite vector is injected into the residual stream during generation, enabling real-time personality modulation without predefined scripts or gradient updates.

## Results

On PersonalityBench (LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct), PERSONA-BASE achieves a mean score of 9.60 with variance 0.74, nearly matching the supervised fine-tuning upper bound of 9.61 without any gradient updates. On the proposed PERSONA-EVOLVE benchmark, PERSONA-FLOW achieves overall win rates of 73.2%–90.8% against vanilla models across Qwen, Llama, and Mistral families, with Qwen3-4B-Instruct reaching the highest 90.8%. The method preserves general capabilities, maintaining or slightly improving MMLU and TruthfulQA accuracy (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B: MMLU 0.71→0.70, TruthfulQA 0.63→0.66).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PERSONA-BASE outperforms all training-free baselines on PersonalityBench average mean score (9.60 vs. NPTI 9.43, P² Induction 9.43, Simple Prompt 8.39, ActAdd 8.20, PAS 6.93), with the largest margin over PAS (+2.67) and ActAdd (+1.40). The closest competitor is NPTI (9.43), which PERSONA-BASE surpasses by 0.17 points overall, with the advantage most pronounced on Openness (9.81 vs. 8.50) where dense vector representation captures distributed facets more completely than sparse neuron selection. PERSONA-BASE loses to NPTI on Extroversion (9.45 vs. 9.86) and Neuroticism (9.79 vs. 9.92), but compensates with superior Openness and Agreeableness control. Against the SFT upper bound (9.61), PERSONA-BASE trails by only 0.01 points while requiring zero gradient updates. On PERSONA-EVOLVE, PERSONA-FLOW achieves 84.7–92.4% win rates on trait adherence, role consistency, and response authenticity across model families, substantially exceeding what static prompting or single-vector methods can achieve in multi-turn dynamic settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation steering / representation engineering lineage (Turner et al. 2023; Rimsky et al. 2024) and the specific persona vector extraction pipeline from Chen et al. (2025a) by demonstrating that extracted personality vectors form a coherent algebraic system supporting scalar multiplication, addition, and subtraction with predictable behavioral outcomes. Where prior work treated persona vectors as static, single-trait steering tools, PERSONA introduces compositional operations and a dynamic predict-then-steer mechanism (PERSONA-FLOW) that adapts personality coefficients per conversational turn. The paper also departs from prompting-based and fine-tuning-based personality control by showing that direct activation-space manipulation can match supervised fine-tuning performance without any gradient updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-parity with SFT (9.60 vs 9.61) without training offers a practical, low-cost path to personality control in deployed LLMs, and the PERSONA-EVOLVE benchmark fills a gap in evaluating dynamic, multi-turn personality adaptation. However, the safety analysis reveals that 'vulnerable' traits not explicitly targeted by alignment training (Careless, Inventive) can measurably degrade refusal behavior, raising open questions about how to compose personality vectors while preserving safety guarantees. Whether the algebraic structure generalizes beyond the OCEAN framework to richer personality constructs, and whether the predict-then-steer overhead is acceptable in latency-sensitive deployments, remain important open questions.
