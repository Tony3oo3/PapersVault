## Problem

Tropical forests store most of Earth's plant biomass and biodiversity, yet annotated datasets for detecting individual tree crowns in high-resolution drone imagery remain scarce, with existing tropical datasets containing only a few thousand annotations compared to tens of thousands for temperate forests. This scarcity hinders robust model development and prevents comprehensive benchmarks comparing CNN and transformer architectures across resolutions and out-of-distribution tropical scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260575/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB drone orthomosaic tiles at 1.2–5.1 cm/px ground sampling distance, with spatial extents of 40×40 m or 80×80 m (up to 1777×1777 pixels), and produces bounding boxes around individual tree crowns. The SELVABOX dataset comprises 83,137 manually annotated crowns across 14 orthomosaics spanning Brazil, Ecuador, and Panama, with crown diameters ranging from under 2 m to over 50 m. Evaluation uses both tile-level COCO metrics (mAP₅₀:₉₅, mAR₅₀:₉₅) and a novel raster-level RF1₇₅ metric that aggregates tile predictions via NMS and requires a strict 75% IoU match, reflecting operational forest monitoring needs.

## Method

The paper introduces SELVABOX, the largest open-access tropical tree crown detection dataset, with spatially separated train/validation/test splits to avoid geospatial autocorrelation and masked pixels in sparse-annotation regions to prevent misleading penalization during training. For modeling, the authors benchmark DINO (a DETR-based transformer with Swin-L-384 backbone) against Faster R-CNN (ResNet-50), DeepForest, and Detectree2, finding that higher-resolution inputs consistently improve accuracy and that the transformer architecture outperforms CNNs. A key technical contribution is a multi-resolution training pipeline that randomly crops inputs over a wide range of spatial extents and resizes them, simultaneously performing ground-extent and GSD augmentation to enable joint training across datasets with different resolutions (3–10 cm/px). This multi-resolution approach achieves performance within one standard deviation of the best single-resolution model while enabling a single detector to generalize across resolutions and datasets, ranking first or second across all six evaluated benchmarks in both in-distribution and zero-shot out-of-distribution settings.

## Results

On the in-distribution SELVABOX test set, DINO-Swin-L trained exclusively on SELVABOX achieves 48.60 RF1_75 (mAP50:95 = 37.77, mAR50:95 = 54.69), while the multi-dataset variant (NQOS) reaches 47.63 RF1_75. On the out-of-distribution BCI50ha dataset, the SELVABOX-only model attains 41.91 RF1_75 and the multi-dataset model 39.39, both surpassing the best prior method (Detectree2-resize at 34.97). On non-tropical datasets (QuebecTrees, OAM-TCD), the multi-dataset DINO-Swin-L variants rank first or second across all evaluated benchmarks, confirming state-of-the-art generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Detectree2 (Ball et al., 2023), the paper's DINO-Swin-L methods achieve a 3.7× improvement on SELVABOX (48.60 vs. 13.14 RF1_75 for Detectree2-resize) and a 6.4× improvement on BCI50ha (41.91 vs. 34.97). On Detectree2's own dataset, Detectree2-resize scores 23.87 RF1_75 vs. 19.23 for the multi-dataset DINO-Swin-L, but the authors note a potential data leak since the original train/test splits could not be recovered, limiting interpretability. Against DeepForest (Weinstein et al., 2020), the gap is even more pronounced: 48.60 vs. 6.08 on SELVABOX and 41.91 vs. 10.02 on BCI50ha. The multi-dataset DINO-Swin-L (NQOS) also outperforms both baselines on non-tropical datasets (QuebecTrees: 56.41 vs. 15.60 RF1_75; OAM-TCD: 55.34 vs. 13.21 mAP50:95), demonstrating that training on tropical data does not sacrifice temperate/urban performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the established line of deep-learning-based tree crown detection (Faster R-CNN, Mask R-CNN, RetinaNet, DINO) by addressing two underexplored dimensions: tropical forest ecosystems, which are severely underrepresented in open datasets, and multi-resolution UAV imagery with varying ground sampling distances. Rather than proposing a new architecture, it repurposes the DINO (DETR-based) detector with a Swin-L backbone and introduces a multi-resolution crop-and-resize augmentation that unifies training across datasets with GSDs from 1.2 to 10 cm/px. The evaluation framework departs from prior tile-level COCO metrics by proposing RF1_75, a raster-level F1 score computed after NMS aggregation, which better reflects operational forest monitoring needs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SELVABOX fills a critical gap: it is an order of magnitude larger than all prior tropical tree crown datasets combined, spanning three neotropical countries with diverse forest types and resolutions. The finding that multi-resolution training achieves performance within one standard deviation of resolution-specific models without degradation is practically significant, as it enables a single model to handle heterogeneous UAV data. Open questions include whether the RF1_75 metric generalizes to other ecological tasks, whether an IoU-averaged RF1_50:95 variant would be more robust, and how the approach extends to LiDAR-fused or multispectral inputs. Limitations include annotation sparsity in some rasters, potential data leakage in Detectree2 evaluation, and the fact that zero-shot OOD performance on BCI50ha and Detectree2, while strong, still lags behind in-distribution scores.
