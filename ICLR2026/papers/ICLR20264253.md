## Problem

Sparse-view 3D Gaussian Splatting suffers from geometric inaccuracies and cross-view inconsistencies, but the underlying causes remain unexplored. This paper identifies two previously unrecognized implicit biases: (1) 3DGS has a stronger demand for supervision in high-curvature surface regions, and (2) reconstruction quality is sensitive to the smoothness of the input camera trajectory. Prior sparse-view methods address supervision through re-projection or warping but do not explicitly account for how trajectory geometry and curvature coverage shape the learning process.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of sparse RGB images (as few as 3–12 views) with calibrated SfM camera poses, plus per-pixel depth maps obtained either from monocular predictors (DPT/ZoeDepth) or stereo (COLMAP PatchMatch), refined via 3DGS-consistent alpha-composited depth fusion. It outputs a trained 3DGS model (positions, covariances, colors, opacities) that renders novel views in real time, and optionally a 1.5M-face mesh extracted via marching cubes for geometric evaluation. The synthetic views generated along the optimized trajectory serve as additional training supervision, effectively densifying the input without requiring new captures.

## Method

The framework has two stages. First, Optimal Camera Trajectory Generation parameterizes the camera path as a cubic B-spline with 24 control points and optimizes them via constrained L-BFGS to maximize a curvature-weighted arc-length integral (w(γ(t))·‖γ′(t)‖) subject to smoothness (bounded second-derivative energy), minimum speed, minimum curvature in high-complexity regions, and proximity to the original path. Second, Synthetic Camera View Construction samples M poses along the optimized trajectory by arc-length parametrization, then synthesizes images via parallax-based depth warping from all real source views, blending with depth-consistency and z-buffer visibility weights, and filling residual holes with edge-aware inpainting. The key insight is that both curvature coverage and trajectory smoothness are first-order determinants of 3DGS reconstruction quality under sparse inputs, and that explicitly planning the viewing path—rather than merely augmenting supervision—yields near-linear gains in PSNR and Chamfer Distance (r ≈ 0.96 correlation between coverage and quality).

## Results

On DTU with 40% training images (α=0.4), the method achieves the best PSNR of 27.89 dB, lowest Chamfer Distance of 3.01×10⁻³, and lowest LPIPS of 0.18, outperforming the next-best NexusGS (27.10 dB, 3.18 CD) by +0.79 dB. On Mip-NeRF 360 (12 views), the plug-in variant MVPGS+Ours reaches 20.15 dB PSNR and 0.41 LPIPS, surpassing MVPGS alone (19.85 dB) and SCGaussian (19.70 dB). In the extreme 3-view setting on DTU and LLFF, the method attains 20.65 dB and 20.93 dB PSNR respectively, leading all 7 compared baselines. On Blender with only 3 input images, the method improves over vanilla 3DGS by +8.28 dB average PSNR (24.98 vs. 16.70).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method consistently outperforms all 17 baselines across every dataset and training ratio. On DTU, the closest competitor is NexusGS (CVPR'25), which trails by +0.79 dB PSNR and +0.17 CD at α=0.4; older methods like SparseGS, DNGaussian, and MatSparse3D lag by 1.3–2.8 dB. On Mip-NeRF 360 (12 views), the plug-in variant MVPGS+Ours (20.15 dB) beats the standalone MVPGS (19.85 dB) by +0.30 dB and SCGaussian (19.70 dB) by +0.45 dB, while 3DGS+Ours (19.95 dB) surpasses the raw 3DGS baseline (15.14 dB) by +4.81 dB. On Tanks & Temples (3 views), 3DGS+Ours achieves 26.41 dB, exceeding MVPGS (25.57 dB) by +0.84 dB and NexusGS (25.20 dB) by +1.21 dB. In the extreme 3-view setting (Table 3), the method leads DIFIX3D+ (CVPR'25) by +0.05 dB on DTU and +0.02 dB on LLFF, and outperforms MAtCha Gaussians (CVPR'25) by +0.13 dB PSNR and +0.03 SSIM on DTU. The Blender results (Table 6) show the largest absolute gains over vanilla 3DGS in the 3-image regime (+8.28 dB average), with per-scene improvements up to +11.72 dB on Hot Dog, confirming that the curvature-aware trajectory and synthetic densification are most impactful under extreme sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework (Kerbl et al., 2023) but departs from the dominant sparse-view 3DGS literature (SparseGS, DNGaussian, SuGar, MVPGS, NexusGS, etc.) that focuses on adding depth priors, multi-view consistency losses, or structural regularization. Instead, it reframes the problem as one of *where and how* supervision is sampled: by identifying two previously unacknowledged implicit biases (curvature-coverage demand and trajectory-smoothness sensitivity), it introduces a B-spline trajectory optimizer (solved via L-BFGS) and a parallax-based synthetic view generator as a model-agnostic, plug-and-play module. Conceptually, this shifts the design space from 'add more constraints to the loss' to 'optimize the geometry of the observation path itself.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of trajectory smoothness and curvature coverage as first-order implicit biases in sparse-view 3DGS provides a diagnostic lens that can guide future data-collection and augmentation strategies beyond this specific method. The plug-and-play demonstration (boosting MVPGS and SCGaussian without architectural changes) suggests broad applicability across Gaussian splatting backbones. Open questions include: whether the approach extends to dynamic or 4D scenes, how it behaves with only 1–2 input views where depth estimation becomes unreliable, and whether the curvature-coverage principle transfers to other implicit-representation families (NeRF, SDF). The method also depends on reasonably accurate SfM poses and per-pixel depth, which remain bottlenecks in the most extreme sparse settings.
