## Problem

Bayesian optimization over large unstructured discrete spaces (e.g., protein sequences, quantum circuits, text) is fundamentally limited by the intractability of acquisition function maximization, since the absence of gradients precludes efficient search over combinatorially vast domains. While Thompson sampling avoids explicit maximization by sampling from the posterior probability of maximality (PoM), directly sampling from PoM remains intractable when the domain exceeds the number of atoms in the observable universe. The paper asks: can we parameterize the PoM with a generative model and adapt it efficiently to achieve sublinear regret without ever solving a maximization problem?

## Input / Output

The method takes a prompt-conditioned pre-trained LLM (ranging from 0.6B to 8B parameters) as the initial generation policy, a linear GP feature map derived from task-specific embeddings (e.g., Qwen3-Embedding for text, ProtGPT2 token embeddings for proteins, two-qubit Pauli observables for quantum circuits), and sequential reward observations from evaluating generated candidates. It outputs the best candidate found and its maximum reward over the optimization budget. Notably, the LLM serves a dual role: it is both the autoregressive generator of candidates and the parametric policy whose weights are updated via gradient ascent, with the GP providing the reward surrogate that drives those updates.

## Method

TOSFIT instantiates Thompson sampling as iterative fine-tuning of an LLM toward the Variational Bayesian Optimistic Sampling (VBOS) objective, which approximates the posterior PoM with an adaptive UCB exploration bonus √(−2 ln π_x)·σ_x. Rather than starting from a uniform policy, TOSFIT initializes with the strong prior of a prompt-conditioned pre-trained LLM and adapts it cautiously using small learning rates, with gradients estimated via the score trick and a standardized RLOO baseline (equivalent to GRPO). The GP reward model uses a linear kernel over deep embeddings with constant-time Θ(d²) inference via Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury updates, independent of the number of observations. Theoretically, the paper derives a novel regret bound of Õ(√(Tγ^T)) for exact VBOS—improving the prior Õ(√(T|X|)) by exploiting kernel structure through maximum information gain—and provides the first regret bound for approximate (gradient-based) VBOS, which depends on a Bregman divergence between the current policy and the exact VBOS maximizer, formally justifying the need for strong initialization and cautious adaptation.

## Results

Across three diverse tasks (FAQ Response Refinement, Protein Search, and Quantum Circuit Design), TOSFIT achieves state-of-the-art sample efficiency, consistently outperforming all seven baselines in best-seen reward over 25 random seeds. The method also demonstrates superior computational efficiency: on fully-batched Protein Search (b=B=16), TOSFIT's total per-step latency is 1.29±0.05s on an A100 GPU, with fine-tuning adding only ~19% overhead over autoregressive generation alone. TOSFIT's optimism-driven exploration yields a more stable exploration-exploitation tradeoff than Actor Critic variants, and its parameter-based adaptation avoids the memory blowup that limits FIBO (which must be truncated to top-10 candidates).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TOSFIT outperforms all seven baselines across all three tasks. UNGUIDED GENERATION saturates quickly at a suboptimal reward level since it provides no adaptive feedback. POST-GENERATION TS identifies good solutions more efficiently than unguided generation but remains confined to its fixed candidate pool of 1000. EVOLUTIONARY SEARCH (both CHARACTER and LLM variants) conducts undirected exploration without optimism in the face of uncertainty, leading to slower convergence. ACTOR CRITIC and SOFT ACTOR CRITIC maximize expected reward (with or without entropy regularization) but lack the optimistic exploration bonus that TOSFIT's VBOS objective provides, resulting in less stable exploration-exploitation dynamics. FIBO is the closest competitor in spirit (also using Thompson sampling with an LLM), but its in-context approach without parameter updates yields significantly lower performance and suffers from quadratic memory growth, forcing a top-10 truncation that further degrades its sample efficiency. TOSFIT's combination of optimism, parameter-level adaptation, and strong pre-trained priors gives it a clear advantage in both sample and computational efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

TOSFIT builds directly on Variational Bayesian Optimistic Sampling (VBOS, O'Donoghue & Lattimore 2021), which provides a variational formulation of Thompson sampling that avoids explicit acquisition function maximization. The key departure is initializing the VBOS policy with a prompt-conditioned pre-trained LLM rather than a uniform distribution, then fine-tuning it via gradient ascent on the VBOS objective using the RLOO baseline (Kool et al., 2019). The paper also improves the theoretical regret bound for exact VBOS from Õ(√(T|X|)) to Õ(√(Tγ^T)) by incorporating kernel structure via maximum information gain, and provides the first regret bound for inexact (gradient-based) VBOS. This positions TOSFIT as a principled bridge between Bayesian optimization theory and modern LLM fine-tuning, distinct from in-context approaches (FIBO), evolutionary search, and VAE-based continuous relaxations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TOSFIT addresses a fundamental scalability bottleneck: acquisition function maximization is intractable in combinatorially large discrete spaces (e.g., protein sequences with 20^100 possibilities), yet such spaces are central to drug discovery and quantum computing. The method's practical impact is demonstrated across three diverse domains with state-of-the-art sample and computational efficiency, and the theoretical contribution (tighter regret bound + first inexact-VBOS bound) provides a principled justification for the algorithmic design choices. Open questions include whether jointly learning deep task-adaptive embeddings (à la Ranković & Schwaller 2025) can further improve performance, whether in-context conditioning can replace weight updates to reduce compute overhead, and whether the approach extends to more expressive reward models beyond GPs. The fixed feature map assumption and the need for careful learning-rate tuning to avoid forgetting the prior are acknowledged limitations.
