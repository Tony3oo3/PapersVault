## Problem

Recent work has questioned whether LMs can reliably distinguish between modal categories (probable, improbable, impossible, inconceivable), arguing that sentence probability estimates are a poor indicator of modality. This paper asks whether modal categories are instead represented as coherent linear features within LM hidden states, and whether these internal representations are more diagnostic than output probabilities for modal categorization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263866/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes minimal pairs of sentences that differ only in modal category, extracting hidden states at the final '.' token from a specific layer of the LM (selected independently per category pair via 5-fold cross-validation). Outputs include modal difference vectors used to classify novel stimuli by comparing projection magnitudes, a 3D projection space for modeling human categorization distributions via logistic regression, and steered text generations demonstrating causal involvement of these representations in model behavior.

## Method

The core technique is Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA): for each pair of modal categories, hidden states of the final '.' token are extracted from minimal pairs at a cross-validated layer, per-pair difference vectors are computed, and averaged to yield a modal difference vector. Classification of held-out stimuli is performed by projecting each sentence onto the vector and comparing magnitudes, analogous to probability-based pair classification. For human behavior modeling, stimuli are projected onto three non-collinear modal difference vectors (probable-improbable, improbable-impossible, impossible-inconceivable) to form a 3D feature space, on which logistic regressions are fit with soft labels to predict full human response distributions. The paper further correlates projections along these vectors with human ratings of interpretable features (subjective event likelihood, imageability, grammaticality) to interpret what the vectors encode, finding that the impossible-inconceivable vector selectively correlates with imageability.

## Results

Across all models with ≥2B parameters (GPT-2-XL, Llama-3.2-3B, OLMo-2-7B/13B, Gemma-2-9B), modal difference vectors (MDVs) match or substantially outperform summed log-probability estimates for classifying minimal-pair stimuli by modal category across four generalization datasets. In the human-categorization modeling task (Study 3), MDV-based logistic regression achieves higher Pearson correlation with empirical response distributions, lower MSE, and better entropy correlation than probability, principal-component, and random-vector baselines. For the Goulding et al. (2024) qualitative examples, MDV-based P(possible) tracks human proportions closely (e.g., 0.09 vs. 0.05 for 'clean a cloud'; 0.25 vs. 0.05 for 'stay awake for 5 years'), while probability estimates remain near chance (~0.57–0.63) across all scenarios. Models below 2B parameters show mixed results, with probability estimates sometimes outperforming MDVs for the probable-vs-inconceivable distinction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the summed log-probability classifier from Kauf et al. (2023), which assigns a sentence to the higher-probability modal category in a minimal pair. Across all ≥2B-parameter models and all modal-category pairs, MDVs match or (sometimes drastically) outperform this probability baseline on classification accuracy (Figure 2), and the advantage persists on both lexically adversarial (Kauf et al.) and semantically adversarial (Vega-Mendoza et al.) stimuli (Figure 7). Two additional baselines—projections along the first three principal components of WikiText hidden states and projections along random vectors—are also outperformed by MDVs on both the classification task and the human-categorization modeling task (Figure 4). In the human-modeling study, MDV-based logistic regression yields higher overall correlation, lower MSE, and better entropy correlation than all three baselines; ablation (Appendix D) confirms all three MDV features are necessary for the fine-grained Hu et al. (2025b) dataset, while subsets suffice for the coarser binary tasks. The probability baseline is particularly unreliable on adversarial stimuli: it is systematically misled by semantically adversarial pairs, whereas MDVs and principal-component projections handle them correctly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA) technique from Panickssery et al. (2023) and the linear-probing classification framework from Marks & Tegmark (2024) to the specific problem of modal category representation in LMs. It directly responds to recent skeptical findings by Kauf et al. (2023) and Michaelov et al. (2025), who argued that LM probability estimates are poor indicators of modal category, by showing that coherent linear representations exist in hidden states and outperform those probability estimates. The work also extends the cognitive science literature on modal intuitions (Shtulman & Carey, 2007; McCoy & Ullman, 2019) by connecting LM internal representations to graded human categorization behavior and interpretable psychological features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that LMs encode modal categories as linear features in their hidden states, countering recent claims that such distinctions are absent or unreliable. The finding that projections onto modal difference vectors model fine-grained human categorization distributions—and that the impossible-inconceivable axis correlates selectively with imageability—generates a concrete, testable hypothesis for cognitive science about the role of imagination in distinguishing impossible from inconceivable events. Limitations include reliance on relatively small expert-labeled datasets, preliminary steering evidence, and the open question of whether these linear representations reflect genuine causal world models or merely distributional regularities. Future work could use modal difference vectors to probe specific physical or biological constraints encoded by LMs.
