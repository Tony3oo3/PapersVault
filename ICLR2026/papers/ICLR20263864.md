## Problem

Zeroth-order (ZO) optimizers like MeZO eliminate the memory overhead of backpropagation during LLM fine-tuning, reducing GPU usage to inference level, but they converge roughly 20× slower than Adam. The paper asks whether this speed–memory trade-off is fundamental or can be broken by better step-size scheduling, perturbation design, and engineering.

## Input / Output

FZOO consumes the same inputs as any fine-tuning loop—model parameters θ ∈ ℝ^d, mini-batches of (x, y) pairs, and a loss function L—but replaces the backward pass entirely with N forward evaluations under Rademacher (±1) perturbations of the parameters. Because only forward passes are needed, peak GPU memory equals inference memory (e.g., 58 GB on a single A100 for OPT-30B vs. 633 GB for Adam FT), and the objective need not be differentiable (F1 score works directly). The output is the updated parameter vector after T adaptive steps, compatible with both full-parameter and prefix-tuning modes.

## Method

FZOO estimates the gradient with N one-sided Rademacher perturbations: it evaluates L(θ + εu_i) for i = 1,…,N and the unperturbed L(θ), then forms g_t = (1/εN) Σ (l_i − l_0) u_i. The step size is adapted by dividing by σ_t, the standard deviation of the N batch losses, yielding the update θ_{t+1} = θ_t − η_t g_t/σ_t. Proposition 3.2 proves E[σ_t²] ≈ ε²‖∇L‖², establishing formal equivalence to a normalized-SGD rule in the ZO regime. Computationally, Rademacher vectors reduce each layer's perturbation to a bit-level sign flip (one add/subtract) rather than a second matrix–vector multiply, and stacking all N perturbed activations along the batch dimension enables parallel forward evaluation, giving an overall speed-up of f × min(p, r). Under L-smoothness and bounded-variance assumptions, Theorem 3.6 guarantees O(ε⁻²) convergence to a stationary point, matching the rate of stochastic gradient descent for non-convex problems.

## Results

On RoBERTa-large (350M, k=16), FZOO achieves 71.4% average accuracy across 6 NLP tasks, outperforming MeZO (67.4%) by +4.0% and HiZOO (70.9%) by +0.5%, while requiring 18× fewer forward passes than MeZO. Across the broader model family (Phi-2 2.7B, Llama3 8B, OPT-13B), FZOO surpasses MeZO by an average of +2.75% accuracy with 8× fewer forward passes. On non-differentiable F1 objectives (SQuAD), FZOO reaches 74.4% average F1 across OPT models (125M–13B), exceeding MeZO (70.5%) by +3.9%. FZOO maintains inference-level GPU memory (1× baseline) throughout, versus 2–6× for HiZOO and full fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MeZO (the primary ZO baseline), FZOO wins on both accuracy and efficiency: +4.0% average accuracy on RoBERTa-large (71.4 vs 67.4) with 18× fewer forward passes, and +2.75% average across Phi-2/Llama3/OPT-13B with 8× fewer passes. Against HiZOO, FZOO matches or slightly exceeds accuracy (71.4 vs 70.9 on RoBERTa-large; 90.4 vs 90.5 across the ZO-variant benchmark in Table 7) while using half the memory (1×M vs 2×M) and 0.56× the runtime. Against full-parameter Adam fine-tuning, FZOO trails by ~3.5% on RoBERTa-large (71.4 vs 74.9) but operates at 1×M memory versus 6×M, and its convergence speed is within 20% of Adam's. Against ZO-Adam from the ICML 2024 benchmark, FZOO achieves +5.77% average accuracy under full-parameter tuning while using 40.5% of the GPU memory. As a drop-in replacement in the hybrid Addax framework, FZOO improves average accuracy from 72.7→74.1 (Setting A) and 68.4→71.2 (Setting B) on OPT-2.7B, confirming orthogonality with existing FO-ZO strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

FZOO extends the MeZO framework (Malladi et al., 2023) for forward-pass-only LLM fine-tuning by replacing its fixed learning rate with an adaptive step-size rule derived from the standard deviation of batched one-sided loss estimates, and by substituting Gaussian perturbations with Rademacher (±1) vectors that enable cheap bit-level sign flips and batched parallelism. The adaptive mechanism is conceptually grounded in normalized-SGD (Bernstein et al., 2018), and the paper proves a formal equivalence between FZOO's update and a normalized-SGD rule in the ZO regime. The batched one-sided estimation strategy draws from the broader family of population-based ZO methods (ReLIZO, Augmented Random Search, Evolution Strategies), while the Rademacher perturbation choice departs from the Gaussian or spherical smoothing standard in most prior ZO work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FZOO makes single-GPU, full-parameter fine-tuning of multi-billion-parameter LLMs realistic at inference-level memory, which is a practical bottleneck for many research and industry settings. The 18× forward-pass reduction over MeZO on RoBERTa-large and the demonstrated compatibility with PEFT (prefix-tuning) and hybrid FO-ZO frameworks (Addax) broaden its applicability. Open questions include whether the convergence-rate gap to Adam narrows further at pretraining scale, whether the Rademacher advantage persists when hardware-specific Gaussian kernels are unavailable, and whether the O(ε⁻²) iteration complexity can be improved. The paper explicitly points toward memory-efficient pretraining as a natural next step.
