## Problem

Standard RL exploration in massively parallel simulation saturates as compute scales, repeatedly sampling narrow state distributions and failing on long-horizon, contact-rich manipulation tasks. Existing approaches rely on extensive per-task engineering—hand-designed rewards, curricula, or human demonstrations—to reduce the exploration burden, limiting scalability. The paper asks whether dexterous multi-phase manipulation can emerge purely from large-scale RL with a single generic reward, without any task-specific scaffolding.

## Input / Output

During RL training, the policy consumes compact Lagrangian state vectors (robot joint states, all object poses, and a 5-step action history) and outputs end-effector pose deltas converted to torques via a shared operational-space controller. For real-world deployment, the distilled student policy takes 224×224 RGB frames from three cameras (front, side, wrist) with a 5-step observation window, encoded by an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18. The user's only required inputs are a target object, a set of goal configurations, and a workspace region—no reward shaping, curriculum, or demonstrations.

## Method

OmniReset programmatically generates diverse initial-state distributions across four reset categories—Reaching (object on table, gripper at random workspace poses), Near-Object (gripper at pre-computed grasp points with random open/closed state), Stable Grasp (object in air, gripper at feasible grasp), and Near-Goal (object at pre-computed offsets from the goal, gripper in contact)—densely covering the space of robot-object interactions without prescribing task-specific solutions. These resets are pre-validated offline via collision checking and short simulation rollouts, then cached on-GPU for efficient sampling during PPO training with a single task-agnostic reward (sparse success + distance + reach + smoothness + termination penalties) and fixed hyperparameters. Key algorithmic choices include an asymmetric actor-critic architecture (critic receives privileged environment parameters), generalized state-dependent exploration noise (gSDE) for temporally correlated, region-specific exploration, and scaling parallel environments to stabilize learning across the heterogeneous reset distribution. For sim-to-real transfer, the state-based expert is distilled into an RGB visuomotor policy via student-teacher learning on 10,000 photorealistic rollouts with extensive visual and dynamics domain randomization, achieving zero-shot real-world deployment with robust emergent retrying behavior.

## Results

On the real-world Peg Insertion task, the distilled OmniReset RGB policy achieves a 25% zero-shot success rate from a wide range of initial conditions, compared to 4% for a Diffusion Policy trained on 100 human demonstrations. In simulation, OmniReset consistently reaches high success rates across all six tasks (Leg Twisting, Drawer Insertion, Peg Insertion, Cube Stacking, Wall Slide, Cupcake Placement) on both Hard and Easy variants, while all three demonstration-based baselines (BC-PPO, DeepMimic, Demo Curriculum) fail to scale to the wider initial-condition distributions of the Hard variants. Robustness analysis shows OmniReset policies are barely affected by large perturbations to initial conditions, whereas baseline performance degrades sharply under small perturbations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three simulation baselines (BC-PPO, DeepMimic, Demo Curriculum) are given 100 demonstrations from the final OmniReset policy, effectively providing them access to optimal behavior, yet they still substantially underperform. BC-PPO and DeepMimic make some progress on Easy variants but consistently fail to scale to the Hard variants' wider initial-condition distributions. Demo Curriculum (the strongest baseline) achieves moderate success rates when starting from Near-Goal states but struggles to make meaningful progress on the full long-horizon task from Reaching states, as shown in Figure 5. In the real-world transfer experiment, OmniReset's distilled policy (25% success) outperforms Diffusion Policy trained on 100 demonstrations (4% success) by over 6×, and additionally exhibits robust emergent retrying behavior that recovers from failed insertion attempts. The key differentiator is that OmniReset's diverse reset distribution enables the policy to succeed across the entire workspace (Figure 9), while baselines trained on narrow reset distributions or demonstrations only succeed in a small subset of initial conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniReset departs from the dominant paradigm in robotic RL that dexterous manipulation requires task-specific scaffolding—hand-designed curricula, demonstration-guided exploration, or reward shaping—by showing that programmatic diverse resets combined with large-scale parallel PPO are sufficient. It builds on the asymmetric actor-critic architecture (Pinto et al., 2017), gSDE state-dependent exploration noise (Raffin et al., 2022), and student-teacher distillation for sim-to-real transfer (Chen et al., 2021), but replaces the complex exploration mechanisms (curiosity, count-based bonuses, reverse curricula) that prior reset-based methods rely on with a simple, task-agnostic reset generation procedure. The key conceptual departure is from works like DeepMimic, DemoStart, and BC-PPO, which inject demonstrations or curricula to reduce exploration burden; OmniReset argues that sufficient reset diversity makes these unnecessary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a prevailing assumption in robotic RL—that long-horizon dexterous manipulation is too complex to emerge from vanilla RL alone—and provides a simple, scalable recipe (diverse resets + parallel PPO + fixed hyperparameters) that practitioners can apply to new tasks with minimal engineering. Limitations include single-task, single-object scope, modest zero-shot real-world success (25% on peg insertion), and reliance on a physics simulator for reset generation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-task learning, dexterous hands, and whether the observed scaling behavior follows predictable laws, which the authors identify as promising future directions.
