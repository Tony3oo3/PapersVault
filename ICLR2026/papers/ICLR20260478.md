## Problem

Existing text-to-image prompt enhancement methods either rely on iterative refinement (incurring high latency from repeated image generation) or single-pass LLM rewrites that lack grounding in visual semantics, frequently producing unrealistic or spatially incoherent compositions. The core gap is that LLMs used for prompt expansion do not incorporate feedback from downstream visual tasks and cannot internally simulate the visual implications of a prompt before committing to a description.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260478/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a short, under-specified user prompt (e.g., 'a photo of a couch below a vase') and produces a structured output consisting of a chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by an enhanced prompt, formatted as <reason>...</reason><prompt>...</prompt>. The enhanced prompt is constrained to 15–77 tokens for compatibility with T2I backbones like SDXL and FLUX.1, and is fed to a fixed text-to-image model (512×512 resolution) to render the final image. The T2I backbone is never retrained; only the prompting policy is optimized.

## Method

RePrompt trains a language model (Qwen2.5-3B) as a prompting policy π_θ that generates a reasoning trace H and an enhanced prompt P′, formulated as a single-step MDP where the non-differentiable T2I synthesizer f_φ maps P′ to an image I. The policy is optimized via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) using an ensemble reward: a Visual-Reasoning Reward combining ImageReward (human preference) and VLM-Reward (semantic alignment via GPT-4V), a binary Structure Reward enforcing the <reason>/<prompt> format, and a Length Reward constraining token count. Training proceeds in two stages—supervised fine-tuning on 8,000 prompts followed by RL on 1,000 prompts—requiring only ~6 hours on 8 A100 GPUs. A key theoretical contribution proves via the law of total variance that conditioning on an explicit reasoning trace strictly reduces reward variance, yielding lower sample complexity and more stable GRPO convergence. The resulting policy is model-agnostic: trained with FLUX.1-dev, it transfers to SD3, PixArt-Σ, BAGEL, and Infinity without retraining, achieving +77–122% relative gains on spatial position tasks in GenEval.

## Results

On the GenEval benchmark, RePrompt (trained with FLUX) achieves the best overall scores across all three T2I backbones: 0.76 on FLUX, 0.75 on SD3, and 0.62 on PixArt-Σ, surpassing all LLM-enhanced baselines. The most dramatic gains appear in the Position category, with relative improvements of +77.1% (FLUX), +78.8% (SD3), and +122.2% (PixArt-Σ) over the Qwen2.5-3B prompting baseline. On T2I-Compbench, RePrompt consistently improves spatial composition scores (e.g., 0.2494→0.3301 on FLUX) and numeracy. In terms of efficiency, RePrompt achieves 0.76 GenEval accuracy at 30s/image latency, compared to 0.69 at 140s for Idea2Img and 0.72 at 110s for PARM++.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RePrompt outperforms all LLM-based single-pass prompting baselines (GPT-4, GPT-5, DeepSeek-R1, Qwen2.5-3B/7B, Promptist, PAG) on the GenEval overall score across all three diffusion backbones, with the largest margin over GPT-5 (0.76 vs. 0.73 on FLUX) and DeepSeek-R1 (0.76 vs. 0.67 on FLUX). Against iterative optimization methods, RePrompt achieves higher accuracy than both Idea2Img (0.76 vs. 0.69) and PARM++ (0.76 vs. 0.72) while reducing inference latency by 4.7× and 3.7× respectively (30s vs. 140s and 110s per image). On the T2I-Compbench, RePrompt surpasses the Qwen2.5-3B-enhanced variants on all six compositional dimensions for every backbone, with the most notable gains in Spatial (e.g., 0.2494→0.3301 on FLUX) and Numeracy. The method also generalizes beyond diffusion architectures, improving BAGEL from 0.79 to 0.86 and Infinity from 0.69 to 0.77 on GenEval, confirming its model-agnostic design. Notably, RePrompt is trained exclusively with FLUX as the T2I backbone yet transfers effectively to SD3 and PixArt-Σ without any backbone-specific retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work on T2I prompt enhancement: (1) iterative refinement methods (Idea2Img, PARM++) that loop between image generation and feedback at high latency, and (2) single-pass LLM rewriting (Promptist, PAG, GPT-4/5 prompting) that produces fluent but visually ungrounded prompts. RePrompt departs from both by formulating prompt generation as a single-step MDP and training a small LLM (Qwen2.5-3B) via GRPO with an image-grounded ensemble reward (ImageReward + VLM-Reward), while keeping the T2I backbone frozen. The explicit chain-of-thought reasoning trace in the output is motivated by recent reasoning-augmented LLMs (DeepSeek-R1, Kimi K1.5), and the paper adds a variance-reduction analysis (law of total variance) to justify why conditioning on a reasoning trace reduces sample complexity for the RL loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RePrompt offers a practical, plug-and-play recipe: train a 3B policy for ~6 hours on 8 A100s, then apply it to any off-the-shelf T2I model without retraining the image generator. The large spatial-position gains (+77–122% relative) and the model-agnostic generalization (FLUX → SD3, PixArt-Σ, BAGEL, Infinity) make it a strong candidate for production prompt pipelines where latency matters (30 s vs. 140 s for Idea2Img). Open questions include whether the modest numeracy/counting improvements can be closed, whether the reward-model dependency (GPT-4V for VLM-Reward) limits accessibility in closed environments, and whether the SFT+RL recipe transfers to video or 3D generation without architectural changes.
