## Problem

Single-image 3D face reconstruction remains underconstrained due to depth ambiguity, albedo-lighting entanglement, and identity-expression disentanglement, with existing methods struggling on strong facial expressions and extreme viewpoints. Feed-forward regressors fall behind optimization-based approaches on expressive faces, while traditional fitting terms (sparse landmarks, photometric losses) lack the geometric signal needed for robust reconstruction. Additionally, no existing benchmark simultaneously evaluates both posed and neutral facial geometry, making it impossible to assess a method's ability to disentangle identity from expression.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single 512×512 RGB image (or a monocular video sequence for tracking) and first produces two dense per-pixel predictions: surface normals in [-1,1]^{512×512×3} and UV coordinates in [0,1]^{512×512×2}. These predictions then drive a 500-step optimization that recovers FLAME parameters (identity z_id ∈ R^300, expression z_ex ∈ R^100, jaw pose θ ∈ SO(3)) and camera parameters (rotation, translation, focal length, principal point), yielding a textured 3D face mesh. For video tracking, identity, focal length, and principal point are frozen after the first frame and shared across all frames.

## Method

Pixel3DMM consists of two lightweight ViT prediction heads (four transformer blocks + three up-convolutions + linear layer) attached to a DINOv2 backbone, trained via supervised image translation on ~976K images from three registered 3D face datasets (NPHM, FaceScape, Ava256) with diffusion-based lighting augmentation. The key architectural insight is that DINOv2's pre-trained features, fine-tuned at 10× lower learning rate, provide strong geometric inductive biases that generalize to in-the-wild images. At test time, the UV prediction is converted into a 2D vertex loss via nearest-neighbor lookup from template UV coordinates to image pixels, which the authors argue provides a wider basin of attraction than photometric or sparse landmark terms. The FLAME fitting energy combines this 2D vertex loss, a rendered normal loss, and regularization anchored to MICA's identity prediction, achieving >15% improvement in L2-Chamfer distance over the strongest baseline on posed expressions.

## Results

On the authors' new benchmark (21 identities, 441 GT scans), Pixel3DMM achieves L1 Chamfer distance of 1.66 mm and 91.6% recall@2.5mm for posed reconstruction, outperforming the closest optimization-based baseline FlowFace (1.96 mm, 87.9%) by over 15% in L1 error. For neutral reconstruction, Pixel3DMM reaches 1.66 mm L1 and 91.2% recall@2.5mm, marginally improving over MICA (1.68 mm, 91.0%). On the FaceScape benchmark, Pixel3DMM achieves the best Chamfer distance (1.76 mm) and mean normal error (0.077), while on NoW it matches FlowFace (0.87 mm median) but trails TokenFace (0.76 mm). For surface normal estimation, Pixel3DMM attains the highest cosine similarity on all three test sets (NeRSemble: 0.931, MultiFace: 0.958, H3DS: 0.905), surpassing DAViD and Sapiens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pixel3DMM outperforms all 11 baselines on the posed reconstruction task of its new benchmark, with the largest margins over feed-forward regressors (DECA: +30% L1, EMOCAv2: +37%, TokenFace: +37%) and a 15% L1 improvement over the closest optimization-based competitor FlowFace (1.66 vs. 1.96 mm). On neutral reconstruction, Pixel3DMM edges out MICA (1.66 vs. 1.68 mm L1) and FlowFace (1.66 vs. 1.93 mm), though the gap over MICA is small, reflecting the difficulty of identity/expression disentanglement. On the existing FaceScape benchmark, Pixel3DMM achieves the best Chamfer distance (1.76 mm vs. FlowFace 2.21 mm) and mean normal error (0.077 vs. 0.083), while on NoW it ties FlowFace (0.87 mm median) but is outperformed by TokenFace (0.76 mm). For surface normal estimation, Pixel3DMM surpasses DAViD (0.931 vs. 0.927 on NeRSemble), Sapiens (0.931 vs. 0.911), DFN (0.931 vs. 0.907), and Diff-E2E (0.931 vs. 0.911) across all three evaluation sets. The ablation confirms that both the UV-coordinate and normal priors contribute independently, and that MICA's identity initialization is critical for neutral reconstruction quality (removing it drops R2.5 from 91.2% to 87.2%).

## Relation to Prior Work

Pixel3DMM sits at the intersection of foundation-model feature exploitation and optimization-based 3DMM fitting. It repurposes DINOv2's pre-trained ViT features—previously used for general visual understanding—as a backbone for face-specific geometric prediction (surface normals and UV coordinates), extending the fine-tuning paradigm popularized by LoRA and DreamBooth to a constrained 3D geometry domain. Conceptually, it departs from FlowFace's iterative UV-to-image flow by predicting UV coordinates directly and converting them into a 2D vertex loss, which the authors argue provides a wider optimization basin than photometric or sparse landmark terms. The fitting stage builds on MICA's identity predictions for initialization, while the training data unification (NPHM, FaceScape, Ava256 registered to FLAME via non-rigid registration) follows the NPHM pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduced benchmark is the first to simultaneously evaluate posed and neutral 3D face reconstruction, enabling direct measurement of identity/expression disentanglement—a gap that has limited fair comparison across methods. The 15%+ improvement on posed expressions over the strongest baselines, combined with cheap training (2 GPUs, 3 days) and fully public data, lowers the barrier for the community to adopt and extend this approach. Key open questions remain: FLAME's limited representation capacity still fails on extreme lip movements, identity/expression disentanglement is imperfect in single-image settings (though improved in video), and the prior networks cannot yet exploit multi-view or temporal information. The authors suggest distillation into feed-forward predictors and multi-view extensions as natural next steps.
