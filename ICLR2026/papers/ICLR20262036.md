## Problem

Long-context reasoning in LLMs lags behind short-context advances because it lacks both reliable human annotations (human accuracy drops to ~25% on extra-long tasks) and programmatically verifiable reward signals that make RLVR effective for math or code. Existing self-play methods target short-context domains with strict verifiers, leaving open-ended document reasoning—where answers may be semantically correct yet lexically different—without a scalable, label-free training signal.

## Input / Output

The method consumes clusters of n raw documents (up to 16K tokens total) paired with a task type (document general QA, financial math QA, or multiple-choice). The questioner sees only a subset of m documents plus a rolling history memory of L recent solvable Q&A pairs, while the responder receives all n documents with the unseen remainder acting as distractors to enforce grounding. The output is a single unified policy model; during training it also produces question-answer pairs, G responder rollouts per question, and G×G binary verifier judgments that are aggregated by majority vote into a scalar reward.

## Method

SPELL uses one policy model that cyclically assumes three roles—questioner, responder, and verifier—within a closed self-play loop optimized by GRPO. The questioner generates questions with a Gaussian-shaped reward peaked at 0.5 responder success rate to keep difficulty at the competence frontier, while a history memory of recent solvable pairs progressively expands context and discourages redundancy. The verifier produces G independent binary judgments per response and is trained via self-consistency (reward = agreement with its own majority vote), calibrated on rule-verifiable tasks (cover exact match) to generalize to semantically open answers. A role-specific dynamic sampling strategy reduces the imbalanced sample counts (1 questioner, G responder, G² verifier) to roughly 1/G of all rollouts, preventing verifier gradients from dominating the shared policy update. The key insight is that verifiable rewards on a subset of tasks bootstrap the verifier's reliability on non-verifiable tasks, creating a stable self-rewarding mechanism that requires no external supervision.

## Results

Across 12 open-source LLMs (4B–32B, dense and MoE) on six long-context QA benchmarks, SPELL delivers consistent average gains of 2.0–14.4 points at 16K input length. The largest improvements appear on unaligned base models: Qwen2.5-7B improves by +13.9 (26.7→40.6), Qwen2.5-14B by +14.4 (37.3→51.7), and Qwen2.5-32B by +9.1 (43.0→52.1). On the strongest reasoning model Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking, SPELL raises the average from 60.7 to 62.7 at 16K and boosts pass@8 to 74.5 (vs. 66.9 base, 68.1 RLVR), surpassing gemini-2.5-pro at pass@4. Gains persist at 100K input length without additional tuning (e.g., Qwen2.5-14B: +15.0 at 100K vs. +14.4 at 16K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the RLVR baseline (static dataset synthesized by DeepSeek-R1-0528), SPELL matches or exceeds performance on nearly all models: for Qwen2.5-7B the two are comparable (40.6 vs. 40.8 avg at 16K), but the gap widens for stronger models—on Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking, SPELL gains +2.0 while RLVR yields +0.0, and RLVR actually decreases accuracy on DocMath (−0.2) and LongBench-V2 (−1.2) for that model. Against long-context alignment baselines on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (Table 9), SPELL (47.2 avg) outperforms QwenLong-L1 (46.1), SoLoPO (44.2), LongPO reimplementation (42.8), and the official LongPO checkpoint (40.1). In test-time scaling (pass@k at 100K), SPELL-trained Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking reaches pass@8 = 74.5 vs. 68.1 for RLVR and 66.9 for the base model, and surpasses gemini-2.5-pro at pass@4. SPELL also generalizes to short-context reasoning (AIME, MATH-500, GSM8K, MMLU-Pro) with average gains of +6.9 to +8.7 points, and to additional long-context benchmarks (MRCR, HELMET) where it outperforms both base models and RLVR by 5–7 points on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPELL extends the self-play RL paradigm (AZR, R-Zero, SCA, SQLM) from short-context domains with programmatically verifiable rewards (code execution, math) to long-context document reasoning, where such verifiable signals are absent. It departs from prior self-play work by introducing a dedicated verifier role that learns semantic equivalence via self-consistency on verifiable subtasks, thereby generating reliable rewards for non-verifiable outputs. The optimization builds on GRPO and incorporates a Gaussian-shaped difficulty curriculum and role-specific dynamic sampling, distinguishing it from static-dataset RLVR baselines that cannot track the policy's evolving capabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPELL addresses a fundamental bottleneck in long-context reasoning: the scarcity of reliable human annotations and the absence of programmatically verifiable rewards, which together limit the applicability of RLVR at scale. The finding that label-free self-play can outperform instruction-tuned models trained on extensive human-annotated data suggests a path toward autonomous capability growth as models approach superhuman reasoning. Key limitations include the 16K training context (though skills generalize to 100K), reliance on human-crafted prompt templates and document preprocessing, and the lack of a theoretical framework for the co-evolutionary dynamics among the three roles. Open questions include scaling to 128K+ contexts, reducing human intervention in task design, and understanding when self-play plateaus versus continues to yield gains.
