## Problem

Diffusion LLMs such as LLaDA are trained on short contexts (~4K tokens), and their long-context behavior remains largely uncharted. While a training-free RoPE extrapolation method (LongLLaDA) exists, it suffers from a 'local perception' bias and cannot reliably extend beyond 32K tokens. The paper asks how to effectively scale diffusion LLM context windows to 128K via lightweight post-training, and whether such post-training can substantially outperform training-free baselines.

## Input / Output

The model receives a long text sequence (up to 128K tokens) partially masked with a special mask token, and iteratively denoises all masked positions using a bidirectional Transformer. At each denoising step, a subset of predicted tokens is re-masked (block size controls the quality–speed trade-off). For evaluation, the model outputs perplexity via Monte-Carlo denoising likelihood on randomly masked tokens, retrieval answers for needle-in-a-haystack probes, and task-specific answers (QA, summarization, code completion, variable tracking) on LongBench and RULER benchmarks.

## Method

The core contribution is a Diffusion-aware NTK scaling of RoPE that corrects the critical-dimension estimate for bidirectional attention: because a diffusion model attends over the full sequence in both directions, it learns relative offsets in [−(T_train−1), T_train−1] rather than [0, T_train−1], so the effective span is set to T_cap ≈ 2·T_train and T_Ecap ≈ 2·T_target, yielding a larger critical dimension and a more conservative (smaller) scaling factor λ′ than the AR-oriented formula. This re-scaled positional encoding is then followed by a lightweight 600-step post-training on 64K-token sequences from PG19, using either adaptive attention masking (which explicitly blocks cross-document attention while preserving bidirectional attention within each document) or end-of-document token concatenation to mitigate cross-segment interference. The combination of the diffusion-aware positional correction and boundary-aware data packing enables stable scaling from 4K to 128K tokens, with ULTRALLADA achieving 100% NIAH retrieval at 128K and near-flat perplexity (~11) across all context lengths, substantially outperforming the training-free LongLLaDA baseline that degrades sharply beyond 32K.

## Results

ULTRALLADA achieves 100% NIAH retrieval accuracy at every context length from 4K to 128K, while the training-free LongLLaDA baseline drops to ~20% at 32K and cannot be evaluated beyond that. On 128K-token perplexity (PG19), ULTRALLADA maintains a stable PPL of 10.45, compared to 343.88 for the base LLaDA-8B model. On LongBench-16K, ULTRALLADA scores 39.98 average (vs. 36.07 for LongLLaDA and 31.56 for the base model), and on RULER at 32K it achieves 73.63 average versus a catastrophic 5.69 for LongLLaDA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base LLaDA-8B model (trained on 4K context), ULTRALLADA wins on every benchmark at every context length beyond 4K: PPL drops from 343.88 to 10.45 at 128K, and RULER-32K goes from infeasible to 73.63. Against the training-free LongLLaDA baseline (NTK RoPE extrapolation without post-training), ULTRALLADA's advantage grows dramatically with context length: at 8K the RULER gap is 86.22 vs. 65.20, at 16K it widens to 77.51 vs. 45.48, and at 32K it becomes 73.63 vs. 5.69 (a 13× margin). On NIAH, ULTRALLADA retrieves the needle at 100% accuracy up to 128K, whereas LongLLaDA fails entirely beyond 32K. On LongBench-16K, ULTRALLADA leads by 3.91 points over LongLLaDA (39.98 vs. 36.07) and 8.42 over the base model. In the generalization experiment to Dream (a diffusion LLM converted from an AR model), the post-trained Dream-Finetune achieves 100% NIAH at all lengths up to 32K, while direct NTK scaling without post-training (Dream-NTK) degrades to 4.1% at 32K—below even the original 2K Dream baseline (19.3%). Overall, the paper's post-training approach with Diffusion-aware NTK consistently and substantially outperforms both the unmodified base model and the training-free extrapolation baseline across all evaluated benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NTK-aware RoPE scaling framework (Peng & Quesnelle, 2023; Peng et al., YaRN) from autoregressive LLMs to diffusion LLMs, building directly on LongLLaDA (Liu et al., 2025b), which first applied training-free NTK RoPE extrapolation to the LLaDA diffusion model. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that bidirectional attention in diffusion LLMs exposes the model to relative positions in [−(T_train−1), T_train−1] rather than [0, T_train−1], effectively doubling the learned positional span and requiring a modified critical dimension and scaling factor. Beyond the positional encoding fix, the paper introduces a lightweight 600-step post-training procedure with document-boundary-aware masking strategies (adaptive attention masking, EOD tokens) that have not been systematically studied for diffusion LLMs, moving beyond the purely training-free paradigm of LongLLaDA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a diffusion LLM can be scaled to 128K context via post-training while maintaining stable perplexity (~10–12) and near-perfect retrieval, a regime where autoregressive LLMs typically see catastrophic perplexity blowup. The bidirectional-attention insight (T_cap ≈ 2·T_train) provides a clean, theoretically grounded correction that practitioners can apply to any RoPE-based diffusion LLM. However, the evidence base is limited to a single 8B model (LLaDA) and one training corpus (PG19), the Dream generalization only reaches 32K, and a short-context regression on reasoning benchmarks (Winogrande, ARC-c) is acknowledged but not resolved. Open questions include whether the 2× span factor is exact or merely approximate, how the approach interacts with other diffusion LLM improvements (e.g., RL-based reasoning, multimodal extensions), and whether the 600-step budget suffices for larger models or more diverse long-context distributions.
