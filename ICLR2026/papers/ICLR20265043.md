## Problem

Simultaneously satisfying multiple group fairness constraints (e.g., demographic parity, equalized odds, predictive parity, and false omission rate parity) in binary classification is challenging because existing post-processing methods either optimize over the high-dimensional space of classifiers, require estimating unknown population-level regression functions (making them noise-sensitive), or cannot handle linear-fractional constraints alongside linear ones. This paper asks whether one can directly intervene on a pre-trained classifier's operating characteristics to achieve approximate fairness across multiple constraints with minimal accuracy loss and few prediction changes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained probabilistic predictor s: X × A → [0,1] (fit on a training split), a post-processing dataset D_post with features, protected attribute values, and labels, and user-specified disparity tolerances δ_k for K fairness constraints. It produces a randomized binary classifier f: X × A → {0,1} implemented as a mixed group-wise thresholding rule with label-flipping randomization, along with the target group-wise operating characteristics (TPR_a, FPR_a) for each protected group. The classifier is designed to minimize the expected number of interventions (label flips) relative to the base post-processor while matching the target rates.

## Method

The core idea is to reformulate the fairness-constrained optimization in the low-dimensional space of group-wise operating characteristics (TPR, FPR) rather than over classifiers. For each group, the feasible region is the convex hull of its empirical ROC curve; linear fairness constraints are enforced via centroid variables in a linear program, while linear-fractional constraints (e.g., predictive parity) are linearized by fixing their centroids and solving an inner LP, with an outer grid search over admissible centroid values (Theorem 4.1 guarantees this recovers the global optimum). Given the optimal target rates, the classifier is constructed as a mixed-GWTR (a mixture of two adjacent thresholding rules on the ROC hull) with outcome-dependent label-flipping probabilities that shift the operating point to the target while minimizing the expected intervention rate (Theorem 4.2 provides Ō(1/√n) convergence guarantees). A feasibility guard with bisection-based tolerance expansion ensures a classifier is always produced even when the nominal tolerances are infeasible.

## Results

On COMPAS (|A|=2), ROCF-LF achieves 0.61 accuracy while simultaneously satisfying all four fairness constraints (DP, EOpp, PEq, PP) at the nominal δ=0.05 level, with only 6% interventions—nearly matching the infeasible oracle (0.62 accuracy). On ACSIncome (|A|=5), ROCF-LF reaches 0.69 accuracy (matching the oracle exactly) with 3% interventions while controlling all four disparities. The method scales to 5 protected groups and 2 linear-fractional constraints (adding FOR-parity) with near-oracle performance on both Lawschool and ACSIncome.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROCF-LF is the only method that simultaneously satisfies all four fairness constraints (DP, EOpp, PEq, PP) at the nominal δ=0.05 level on COMPAS while maintaining near-oracle accuracy (0.61 vs. oracle 0.62). MFOpt (Hsu et al., 2022) retains higher accuracy (0.63) but fails to control DP (0.26) and EOpp (0.25), exceeding the 0.05 target by a factor of 5. META (Celis et al., 2019) achieves the fairness targets but suffers a much larger accuracy drop (0.50 vs. 0.61), a 11-point gap attributed to its optimization in classifier space rather than operating-characteristic space. LPP-DP and LPP-EO (Xian & Zhao, 2024) preserve accuracy (0.67) and control their respective linear constraints but do not enforce predictive parity, leaving PP at 0.14–0.15 (nearly 3× the target). On ACSIncome with 5 groups, ROCF-LF matches the oracle exactly (0.69 accuracy) while LPP variants leave PP at 0.33–0.35, far above the 0.05 target.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the classifier-space optimization perspective of Celis et al. (2019) and the mixed-integer programming approach of Hsu et al. (2022) by working directly in the low-dimensional space of group-wise operating characteristics (TPR, FPR), leveraging the geometry of each group's ROC convex hull. It builds on the centroid-based linearization of linear-fractional fairness constraints from Celis et al. (2019) and Xian & Zhao (2024), but applies it to a fundamentally different feasible region defined by post-processing realizability rather than arbitrary classifier space. The label-flipping randomization mechanism extends Hardt et al. (2016) by jointly optimizing over both threshold mixing and flip probabilities, rather than treating them separately, which enlarges the set of attainable operating points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a practical, theoretically grounded post-processing pipeline that simultaneously enforces multiple approximate fairness constraints (DP, EO, PP, FOR-parity) on deployed models without retraining, achieving near-oracle accuracy with minimal prediction changes. This is particularly relevant in regulated domains (credit, criminal justice, healthcare) where retraining is impractical and multiple fairness criteria must be satisfied concurrently. Open questions include whether the randomization can be restricted to subpopulations for interpretability, whether the intervention-minimization is globally optimal among all post-processing rules, and how the approach behaves under distribution shift between post-processing and deployment.
