## Problem

When multiple domain-specialized draft models are available for speculative decoding, prior work (MetaSD, BanditSpec) framed the online selection problem as a multi-armed bandit, requiring costly exploration of unchosen drafters. This paper shows that exploration is unnecessary: the structure of speculative decoding allows full-information feedback for all drafters from a single verified trajectory, without any additional calls to the target model, transforming the problem into a full-information online learning setting with exponentially faster convergence in the number of drafters.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pool of N pre-trained draft models (EAGLE-3 one-layer transformers, each fine-tuned on a specific domain), a target LLM (e.g., LLaMA-3.1-8B-IT, Qwen-3-8B/32B), and a stream of user queries. At each decoding chunk, it outputs the selected drafter index and the generated tokens. Unusually, the evaluation phase prefills the verified tokens into every other drafter in parallel to compute counterfactual acceptance probabilities, costing roughly 1/25 of a target forward pass per drafter and running concurrently so that the added latency is negligible relative to the target model call.

## Method

The core mechanism is a lightweight evaluation phase inserted between token verification and the next drafting step: after the target model verifies K tokens for the chosen drafter, those verified tokens are prefilled into all N−1 other drafters to compute their per-position acceptance probabilities γ_k[i] (total variation distance for standard speculative decoding, or top-L probability mass for EAGLE draft trees). A one-step counterfactual estimator (Theorem 3) then converts these γ sequences into an unbiased estimate of expected acceptance length for every drafter, resolving a subtle censoring issue where alternative drafters might accept more tokens than the one actually played. The online learner uses NormalHedge with a delayed-feedback wrapper (Joulani et al., 2013, Algorithm 2) to handle the fact that loss vectors arrive in chunks rather than per-token, with a maximum delay of 2K. The resulting no-regret bounds scale as O(√(K log N / T)) for acceptance probability and O(√((K+1)³ log N / T)) for acceptance length, improving exponentially over bandit regret in N. Practical heuristics—hybrid losses (acceptance-probability surrogate for cold-start, then acceptance-length loss) and skipped/batched updates—further reduce system overhead while preserving near-optimal performance.

## Results

HedgeSpec achieves an average MAT of 7.15 and 90.41 Token/s on LLaMA-3.1-8B-IT, 6.37 MAT and 69.44 Token/s on Qwen-3-8B, and 6.21 MAT and 40.41 Token/s on Qwen-3-32B, consistently outperforming all baselines across all seven domains. The headline gains include up to 83.7% Token/s improvement over EAGLE (SQL domain, Qwen-3-8B: 44.6 → 81.94) and up to 49% MAT gain over the best bandit baseline. On Qwen-3-32B, HedgeSpec more than doubles EAGLE's average MAT (6.21 vs. 2.88), demonstrating that the advantage grows with model size and reasoning-chain length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EAGLE (the state-of-the-art generalist drafter), HedgeSpec wins on every domain and every target model, with average MAT gains of +1.46 (LLaMA-3.1-8B-IT), +2.14 (Qwen-3-8B), and +3.33 (Qwen-3-32B). Against BanditSpec (both UCBSpec and EXP3Spec variants), HedgeSpec also wins universally, achieving up to 49% higher MAT and 41% higher Token/s; the gap is largest on Qwen-3-32B where HedgeSpec reaches 6.21 MAT versus 4.50 (UCBSpec) and 4.33 (EXP3Spec). Notably, bandit methods actually underperform vanilla EAGLE on LLaMA-3.1-8B-IT (shorter outputs limit convergence time) but slightly outperform it on Qwen-3-8B, whereas HedgeSpec dominates in both settings. The paper also shows that HedgeSpec's advantage scales gracefully with the number of candidate drafters (up to 7), while bandit methods deteriorate sharply as the pool grows due to increasingly costly exploration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the bandit-based framing of online drafter selection established by MetaSD (Kim et al., 2024) and BanditSpec (Hou et al., 2025), showing that the structure of speculative decoding actually provides full-information feedback for all candidate drafters without additional target model calls. It builds on the NormalHedge algorithm (Chaudhuri et al., 2009) as the base learner and the delayed-feedback reduction of Joulani et al. (2013) to handle the censoring issue where acceptance outcomes are revealed in chunks. The speculative decoding substrate is EAGLE-3 (Li et al., 2025b), and the theoretical acceptance-probability characterization follows Leviathan et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that HedgeSpec's full-information feedback enables exponential scaling with the number of drafters (vs. polynomial for bandits), making it the natural choice as organizations accumulate domain-specialized drafters for production LLM serving. The approach is method-agnostic (works with single-draft, multi-draft, and draft-tree speculative decoding) and requires no access to training data, only the trained drafter models. Open questions include whether offline routing can serve as a warm-start complement, how the method behaves under truly adversarial or non-stationary query distributions, and whether the practical heuristics (skipped updates, hybrid losses) can be given tighter theoretical guarantees.
