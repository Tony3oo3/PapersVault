## Problem

CLIP has been shown to behave like a bag-of-words model cross-modally, failing to bind attributes to their correct objects in multi-object scenes. However, prior evaluations conflated the encoding step with the cross-modal matching step, leaving it unclear whether the binding information is absent from the individual modality embeddings or merely misaligned between them. This paper disentangles these two sources to determine the true origin of CLIP's compositional failure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-computed CLIP embeddings (ViT-L/14, ViT-B/32, or ViT-B/16) for RGB images and text captions, with the linear transformation applied only to the 512-dimensional text embedding vector before the inner product with the image embedding. The output is a modified cosine similarity score that ranks image-text pairs by attribute-object binding correctness rather than mere concept overlap. Because the image encoder is never modified, existing CLIP vector databases remain fully compatible without re-extraction.

## Method

The paper first establishes via linear probing, robustness tests with increasing object counts, and a conjunctive search experiment that attribute-object binding is already linearly separable within CLIP's frozen image and text embeddings individually. Building on this, LABCLIP learns a single D×D matrix A (initialized as identity, ~590K parameters) applied to text embeddings before the cross-modal inner product, trained with a standard contrastive loss over B×2B batches where hard negatives are generated by permuting attribute-object pairs in captions (e.g., 'red cube and blue sphere' → 'blue cube and red sphere'). All CLIP encoder weights remain frozen throughout training, making the method a lightweight post-hoc adapter that recovers near-fine-tuned binding accuracy (0.95 vs. 0.58 on CLEVR) while being over 100× faster to train than full fine-tuning approaches like NegCLIP.

## Results

On synthetic binding benchmarks, LABCLIP recovers cross-modal attribute-object binding from near-random CLIP performance (0.50–0.58 accuracy) to 0.94–0.97 accuracy on CLEVR, PUG:SPAR, and PUG:SPARE, approaching the fine-tuned CLIP upper bound (1.00). On real-world compositional benchmarks, LABCLIP trained on COCO achieves 0.82 on ARO VG-R, 0.81/0.82/0.74 on SugarCrepe Add/Replace/Swap, and 0.41 COCO Recall@1, matching NegCLIP while using only 262K learnable parameters (vs. 151M for NegCLIP). The linear transformation is trained 100× faster than full fine-tuning on CLEVR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against frozen CLIP, LABCLIP achieves dramatic gains on all binding tasks: accuracy jumps from 0.58 to 0.95 on CLEVR, 0.53 to 0.97 on PUG:SPAR, and 0.50 to 0.94 on PUG:SPARE, and on real-world ARO VG-R from 0.63 to 0.82. Against NegCLIP (full fine-tuning of CLIP with hard negatives, 151M parameters), LABCLIP (262K parameters) matches or nearly matches on SugarCrepe (0.81/0.82/0.74 vs. 0.87/0.85/0.75 for Add/Replace/Swap) and COCO Recall@1 (0.41 vs. 0.41), while slightly trailing on ARO VG-A (0.69 vs. 0.71) and Flickr PRC (0.84 vs. 0.91). Against the fine-tuned CLIP upper bound (CLIP-FT, 428.2M parameters), LABCLIP is within 1–6% on synthetic accuracy but requires ~700× fewer parameters and 100× less training time. On downstream single-object tasks (CIFAR-10, ImageNet zero-shot), LABCLIP is slightly worse than CLIP (e.g., 0.86 vs. 0.90 on CIFAR-10), but probing performance is fully preserved since image embeddings are unchanged.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Yuksekgonul et al. (2023), which established CLIP's bag-of-words behavior cross-modally and introduced NegCLIP (full fine-tuning with permuted negatives), and on Lewis et al. (2024) and Tang et al. (2023), which probed compositionality with controlled two-object scenes. The key departure is diagnostic: rather than treating BoWness as an encoder limitation requiring retraining, the paper isolates the failure to cross-modal misalignment by showing binding is already linearly separable within each modality. The proposed LABCLIP method repurposes the negative-sample strategy from NegCLIP but applies it to a lightweight D×D linear transformation on frozen embeddings, and the conjunctive search experiment adapts Campbell et al. (2024) from a cross-modal to a strictly unimodal setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant for deployed CLIP systems: a 589.8K-parameter linear layer trained on existing text embeddings recovers near-perfect attribute-object binding without retraining encoders or re-extracting image features, offering backward compatibility with existing vector databases and ~100× faster training than NegCLIP. A notable limitation is a tradeoff on single-object classification (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet zero-shot accuracy drops by 4–10 points), suggesting binding-specific alignment reshapes the embedding space away from coarse object discrimination. Open questions include whether the linear transformation generalizes to more complex compositional structures (spatial relations, negation, counting) and whether the approach extends to other contrastive VLMs beyond CLIP.
