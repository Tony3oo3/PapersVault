## Problem

Mamba-2's Structured State Space Duality (SSD) module introduces architectural complexity—element-wise multiplications intertwined with matrix multiplications, dimension-varying activations, and heterogeneous outlier distributions across head and head-dimension axes—that causes standard quantization methods to degrade accuracy by 5–10 points. Prior quantization work (Quamba2) only quantizes SSD inputs, leaving internal computations in FP16 and preventing a persistent INT8 execution path, which limits latency gains.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained Mamba-2 model (1.3B, 2.7B, or 8B parameters) and 512 calibration samples from the Pile dataset, and produces a W4A8 or W8A8 quantized model where the SSD path executes entirely in INT8. Projection layers use GPTQ 4-bit weights with Hadamard-transformed activations, while the SSD interior maintains a persistent INT8 path from input to output. The quantized model produces text generation output with FP16-comparable perplexity and zero-shot accuracy.

## Method

SSDi8 establishes a persistent INT8 execution path within SSD through three key mechanisms. First, a sparse-aware reformulation moves the LUT_state element-wise scaling from B to X (computing X_scaled = LUT_state ⊙ X before quantization), enabling INT8 GEMM in ChunkState; a formal proof shows this reduces quantization error because X_scaled is highly sparse. Second, recurrent states are converted from INT32 accumulation to INT8 in registers and persisted through StatePassing and ChunkScan1, with decay applied via bit-shift operations using a power-of-two gating constant. Third, channel-aware quantization exploits the H×P dimensional decomposition: B and C are quantized once along the group axis G (before broadcasting to H, avoiding 4× overhead), while X_scaled and State are quantized per-(H,P) to respect head-wise heterogeneity. A per-channel mean correction term, computed via least-squares optimal estimation with sequential layer-wise updates, compensates for accumulated quantization error at the output projection layer with only 1–2% latency overhead.

## Results

On Mamba-2 2.7B under W4A8, SSDi8 achieves 62.6% average zero-shot accuracy across six benchmarks, compared to 62.1% for Quamba2 and 63.8% for FP16. On the 8B model under W8A8, SSDi8 reaches 70.2% average accuracy (vs. 69.8% Quamba2, 70.7% FP16), and under W4A8 it achieves 69.6% (vs. 69.1% Quamba2). In terms of efficiency, SSDi8 delivers up to 1.47× speedup over FP16 and 1.38× over Quamba2 on the SSD module (A5000, B=32, L=2048), with WikiText2 perplexity of 7.49 (W8A8) and 7.62 (W4A8) on the 8B model, closely approaching the FP16 baseline of 7.25.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSDi8 consistently outperforms Quamba2 across all model sizes and bit-widths on average zero-shot accuracy: on the 8B model, SSDi8 achieves 70.2% (W8A8) and 69.6% (W4A8) versus Quamba2's 69.8% and 69.1%, respectively, while remaining within 0.5–1.1% of the FP16 baseline. Quamba (designed for Mamba-1) suffers severe degradation when applied to Mamba-2's SSD, dropping to 56.5% on 2.7B W8A8 and 64.8% on 8B W8A8, confirming that SSD-specific quantization is necessary. On WikiText2 perplexity, SSDi8 achieves 7.49 (W8A8) and 7.62 (W4A8) on the 8B model, improving over Quamba2 (7.79 and 7.94) by ~4% while staying close to FP16 (7.25). In terms of latency, SSDi8 delivers up to 1.47× speedup over FP16 and 1.38× over Quamba2 on the SSD module (A5000, B=32, L=2048), with the ChunkScan sub-module reaching 1.77× over FP16. On the edge device (Orin NX 16G), SSDi8 reduces SSD latency by 12–19% over Quamba2 across sequence lengths 256–2048, demonstrating consistent gains in resource-constrained settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the post-training quantization (PTQ) line for Mamba models—MambaQuant and Quamba1 targeted Mamba-1, while Quamba2 extended to Mamba-2 but only quantized SSD inputs, leaving internal SSD computations in FP16. SSDi8 departs from this by establishing the first persistent INT8 execution path through the entire SSD computation, requiring a sparse-aware reformulation that decouples element-wise multiplications from GEMMs and a formal proof that quantizing after scaling reduces error under sparsity. It also adapts Transformer-oriented PTQ tools (GPTQ, Hadamard rotation, SmoothQuant-style γ migration) to SSD's unique multi-head, group-broadcast, and recurrent-state structure, where naive per-tensor quantization causes severe degradation (Table 1).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As Mamba-2 scales beyond 8B parameters and enters production hybrid architectures (e.g., Nemotron-H-8B-Reasoning), efficient SSD inference becomes a practical bottleneck; SSDi8's ~2× SSD-module latency reduction and edge-device (Orin NX) validation make INT8 deployment of these models viable. The sparse-aware reformulation with its MSE bound offers a general recipe for quantizing architectures where element-wise and recurrent operations are intertwined, potentially transferable to other SSM variants. Open questions include extending quantization beyond the SSD path in hybrid Mamba-Transformer models, whether the long-context numerical stability observed (SSDi8 PPL stays ~9 while FP16 degrades to 3881 at 14k) generalizes to other Mamba-2 scales, and whether W4A4 can be made hardware-efficient to further reduce memory.
