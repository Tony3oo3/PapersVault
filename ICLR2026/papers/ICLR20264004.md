## Problem

While large video models have achieved state-of-the-art realism for single-speaker talking head and lip-sync generation, no public large-scale dataset or benchmark exists for the more challenging audio-visual dyadic interactive virtual human task, where a model must jointly understand an initiator's multimodal input and generate a coherent audio-visual response. This gap hinders research toward proactive, interactive virtual humans that can engage autonomously rather than being passively driven.

## Input / Output

The model takes the initiator's video and audio stream (3–14 s clips at 8 FPS, 480×768) together with a reference image of the responder as input, and jointly generates the responder's video and audio. Video is encoded via a 3D VAE (temporal stride 4, spatial stride 8) into 360 latent tokens per frame, while audio is tokenized by CosyVoice2 into 12 discrete tokens per chunk; one chunk spans 4 raw frames (0.5 s). The dataset itself provides 5.2M single-speaker clips (8.7K hours) and 770K dyadic pairs (1.8K hours) at 1080P or higher (93%), with structured captions, pose sequences, ASR transcripts, and blur scores per clip.

## Method

The baseline is an end-to-end autoregressive framework built on a frozen Qwen2.5-Omni thinker for multimodal understanding, feeding hidden states into an AR transformer that performs next-chunk prediction to jointly emit coarse visual tokens and audio tokens. A spatial transformer (adapted from NOVA) then refines the coarse visual tokens into fine-grained tokens via a set-by-set inner loop, and a lightweight 3-block diffusion MLP with adaptive LayerNorm conditioning denoises these into final video latents decoded by the 3D VAE. Training follows a three-stage progressive schedule—visual pretraining on single-speaker data, joint audio-visual training, and high-quality dyadic fine-tuning—with random noise injection into visual tokens during training to mitigate autoregressive error accumulation. The full pipeline has 0.8B trainable parameters and is trained on 128 L40S GPUs for pretraining and 32 A800 GPUs for fine-tuning.

## Results

On VidChatBench, the proposed end-to-end autoregressive method (0.8B params) achieves FID 32.35, FVD 28.82, PSNR 17.55, and SSIM 0.66 for video quality, with an identity preservation score (ArcFace) of 0.772 and emotional alignment (FID_Emotion) of 3.22. The method generates a single frame in 3.17 seconds, compared to 31.43 s (Sonic cascade) and 45.82 s (Hallo3 cascade). Hand quality reaches 0.49, the highest among all compared methods, while audio-visual sync confidence is 2.698.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its 0.8B-parameter end-to-end autoregressive method against two cascaded baselines: Qwen2.5-Omni + CosyVoice + Sonic (~2B params) and Qwen2.5-Omni + CosyVoice + Hallo3 (~10B params). Against the Sonic cascade, the proposed method wins on 8 of 9 metrics (FID, FVD, PSNR, SSIM, ArcFace, FID_Emotion, Infer Time, Hand Quality), with the only loss being SyncConf (2.698 vs. 2.972). Against the much larger Hallo3 cascade, the method wins on PSNR (17.55 vs. 17.31), FID_Emotion (3.22 vs. 4.15), inference time (3.17 s vs. 45.82 s per frame), and Hand Quality (0.49 vs. 0.42), but loses on FID (32.35 vs. 28.43), FVD (28.82 vs. 27.65), SSIM (0.66 vs. 0.69), ArcFace (0.772 vs. 0.775), and SyncConf (2.698 vs. 3.324). The authors attribute the visual quality gap to the absence of a large-scale pre-trained AR video foundation model, while emphasizing that the 14× speed advantage over Hallo3 and superior emotion/hand quality make the end-to-end approach far more viable for real-time dyadic interaction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of audio-driven human video generation (talking heads, lip-sync, learning-to-listen) into a fundamentally different regime: end-to-end audio-visual dyadic interaction, where a model must jointly generate both audio and video responses from an initiator's multimodal input. Architecturally, it composes Qwen2.5-Omni for multimodal understanding, a NOVA-style spatial transformer for set-by-set visual refinement, CosyVoice2 for audio tokenization/decoding, and a lightweight diffusion MLP for final latent denoising, unified under a next-chunk autoregressive prediction loop. The key conceptual departure from prior interactive-agent systems (AgentAvatar, BodyofHer) is the move from modular cascaded pipelines to a single autoregressive model that handles understanding and generation jointly, and the key departure from prior datasets (VoxCeleb, MEAD, OpenHumanVid) is the explicit dyadic structure with paired initiator-responder clips and multi-turn context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SpeakerVid-5M fills a critical gap: no prior public dataset was designed for audio-visual dyadic interactive virtual human generation, and the 8.7K hours / 5.2M clips scale (plus the 770K dialogue pairs) makes it the first resource adequate for training foundation models in this space. The VidChatBench with its six-dimension evaluation (video quality, identity, dialogue coherence, A/V consistency, emotion, audio identity) provides a standardized benchmark that the field lacked. Key limitations include heavy demographic skew (82% English, 57% White), the AR baseline's quality gap versus cascaded diffusion pipelines (FID 32.35 vs. 28.43), and the preliminary nature of the multi-turn and listening branches. Open questions include whether end-to-end AR can close the quality gap with diffusion at scale, how to handle longer multi-turn conversations, and how to mitigate the dataset's inherent biases for equitable deployment.
