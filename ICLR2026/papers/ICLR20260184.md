## Problem

Sheaf Neural Networks (SNNs) extend GNNs via algebraic-topological structures but are restricted to undirected graphs, ignoring edge orientations that are central to many real-world networks (social, biological, causal). No SNN architecture existed that could explicitly incorporate directionality into its message-passing mechanism, leaving a gap between the expressive power of sheaf-based learning and the directional inductive bias shown to be critical in directed GNNs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260184/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a directed graph adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{n×n} and node feature vectors, projecting them into complex-valued d-dimensional stalks (d ∈ {2,5} in experiments). The restriction maps are learned end-to-end via MLPs on concatenated endpoint features, and the final layer output is projected back to the real domain via an 'unwind' operation that concatenates real and imaginary parts, doubling the output dimension. The model supports both node classification (producing class labels) and edge direction prediction (binary: whether (u,v) or (v,u) is the directed edge).

## Method

The core contribution is the Directed Cellular Sheaf, which assigns complex-valued vector spaces to nodes and edges and encodes edge direction in the imaginary part of restriction maps via a phase matrix T^(q) = exp(i·2πq(A − A^T)), where q ∈ [0,1] modulates directional strength. From this sheaf, the Directed Sheaf Laplacian L^F = δ̃*δ̃ is constructed as a complex Hermitian, positive-semidefinite operator whose magnitude captures undirected geometry and whose phase encodes orientation. The DSNN architecture discretizes a directed neural sheaf diffusion process using this Laplacian, with complex-valued ReLU activations, learnable restriction maps, and a per-stalk scaling parameter ε. Theoretically, the DSL generalizes the classical sheaf Laplacian, the Magnetic Laplacian, and the Sign-Magnetic Laplacian (at q=1/4), unifying these operators under a single sheaf-theoretic framework.

## Results

On node classification across 12 real-world benchmarks, DSNN variants achieve the best accuracy on 10/12 datasets, with headline results including 92.08% on Roman-Empire, 94.81% on Telegram, 90.23% on PubMed, and 88.65% on Texas. On synthetic DSBM graphs, DSNN reaches 96–99% accuracy (best: 99.14% at α_ij=0.10), dramatically outperforming NSD (~20%, i.e., chance level for 5 classes) and direction-aware GNNs (MagNet: 78.6–91.6%, SigMaNet: 87.4–98.6%). On the direction prediction task, DSNN attains state-of-the-art accuracy on 6/10 datasets, with best values of 95.53% (Squirrel), 95.56% (PubMed), and 94.55% (Texas).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SNN baseline NSD (Bodnar et al., 2022), DSNN consistently wins on 10/12 real-world datasets, with the largest margins on Questions (+3.7%), Texas (+3.0%), Telegram (+2.7%), and Roman-Empire (+8.3%), confirming that adding directionality to the sheaf Laplacian provides systematic gains over the undirected sheaf formulation. Among direction-aware GNNs, DSNN outperforms MagNet (Zhang et al., 2021) on 10/12 datasets (e.g., +10.2% on Texas, +7.2% on Wisconsin) and SigMaNet (Fiorini et al., 2023) on 10/12 (e.g., +9.7% on Texas, +10.0% on Wisconsin), while DirGNN (Rossi et al., 2024) is surpassed on 10/12 with particularly large gaps on Cornell (+26.4%) and Film (+9.1%). On the synthetic DSBM benchmark, DSNN (96–99%) decisively beats all baselines: NSD sits at chance level (~20%), MagNet reaches at most 91.6%, and SigMaNet narrows the gap to 98.6% at the highest density but still trails DSNN. Classical GNNs (GCN, GAT, GGCN, H2GCN, GPRGNN, FAGCN, GCNII) are uniformly outperformed by DSNN on heterophilic datasets (e.g., Texas, Wisconsin, Film), where they struggle most. On the two datasets where DSNN does not win (Squirrel and Cora), the margin is minimal (0.22% and 0.79% respectively), and CAGN (Xu et al., 2025) and HaarNet (Badea & Dumitrescu, 2025) are also consistently outperformed by DSNN across the board, with CAGN additionally suffering OOM failures on several datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: sheaf neural networks (NSD, Bodnar et al. 2022) and directed-graph spectral GNNs (MAGNET, SigMaNet). The Directed Cellular Sheaf generalizes the classical cellular sheaf of Hansen & Ghrist (2019) by encoding edge orientation in the imaginary part of complex-valued restriction maps, while the resulting Directed Sheaf Laplacian is shown (Theorems 3–5) to subsume both the classical sheaf Laplacian and the Magnetic/Sign-Magnetic Laplacians as special cases. The convolutional architecture directly extends the Neural Sheaf Diffusion process of Bodnar et al. (2022) to the directed setting, inheriting the Chebyshev-polynomial spectral framework of Kipf & Welling (2017).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first SNN architecture that natively handles directed graphs, closing a gap that limited sheaf-based learning to undirected topologies. The clean theoretical unification (DSL generalizes NSD, MAGNET, and SigMaNet simultaneously) provides a principled lens for understanding when and why directionality helps, and the ablation showing q=0 degrades performance confirms the gains come from the sheaf's directional encoding rather than added capacity. Open questions include extending to temporal/dynamic directed graphs (noted as future work), making q fully learnable (only preliminary analysis in Appendix L), and understanding whether the ~4× constant overhead from complex arithmetic becomes prohibitive at very large scale.
