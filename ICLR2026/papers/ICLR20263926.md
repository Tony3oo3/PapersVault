## Problem

Existing LLM-based embodied navigation agents are jointly fine-tuned on a fixed set of tasks and suffer catastrophic forgetting when adapting to new scenes or instruction styles, limiting their deployment as universal navigation agents. This paper formalizes the challenge as Lifelong Embodied Navigation Learning (LENL), where an agent must sequentially master new navigation tasks (spanning VLN, OLN, and DUN instruction styles across unseen Matterport3D scenes) while retaining all previously acquired navigation knowledge, and introduces the first benchmark for this setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263926/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each navigation step the agent receives a real-time RGB vision observation from a 3D Matterport3D scene (encoded by an EVA-CLIP ViT into a [CLS] embedding) and a natural-language instruction in one of three styles: step-by-step route directions (VLN), concise object-localization phrases (OLN), or multi-turn dialogue (DUN). The agent autoregressively generates the next navigation direction token. Critically, during testing the task identifier is agnostic—the agent must infer which learned experts to activate from the observation and instruction alone via the Task-Aware Knowledge Aggregation retrieval mechanism.

## Method

Uni-Walker introduces Decoder Extension LoRA (DE-LoRA), which decouples navigation knowledge into a shared subspace A (common across all tasks) and per-task expert subspaces B_t (task-specific), with TOP-K sparse expert activation. For shared-knowledge transfer, a Knowledge Inheritance Strategy initializes each new expert via PCA over previously learned same-instruction-style experts, and an Experts Co-Activation Strategy co-activates frozen related experts during training; a Fisher-information-weighted Shared Smoothing Consolidation loss protects the shared subspace from drift. For task-specific knowledge, an Expert Subspace Orthogonality Constraint enforces near-orthogonality between expert subspaces to prevent knowledge overlap, and a Navigation-Specific Chain-of-Thought mechanism provides distinct reasoning templates tailored to VLN, OLN, and DUN instruction styles. At inference, Task-Aware Knowledge Aggregation retrieves the most relevant TOP-K experts by computing cosine similarities between the current observation/instruction embeddings and stored per-task scene/instruction embeddings, then composes them with the shared subspace for action generation.

## Results

On the proposed LENL benchmark (18 sequential navigation tasks across 3 instruction styles in Matterport3D), Uni-Walker achieves an average Success Rate (SR) of 66% and a Success Rate Forgetting Rate (SR-F) of 5%, surpassing the previous best (SD-LoRA+TAKA at 59% SR and 16% SR-F) by 7% and 11% respectively. On path efficiency, Uni-Walker reaches 61% SPL (vs. 38% for the prior best) and 81% OSR (vs. 79%), with SPL-F reduced from 42% to 7%. On unseen-scene generalization (tasks S16–S18), Uni-Walker achieves 62% average SR, outperforming the previous best (SD-LoRA+TAKA at 57%) by 5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Uni-Walker outperforms all 11 baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest competitors, SD-LoRA+TAKA (59% SR, 16% SR-F) and O-LoRA+TAKA (58% SR, 17% SR-F), Uni-Walker gains 7–8% in SR and 11–12% in SR-F, indicating substantially better retention of previously learned navigation skills. The MoE-based baselines (Dense MoLE, Sparse MoLE, MoLA, HydraLoRA, BranchLoRA) cluster between 21–30% SR with forgetting rates of 58–71%, revealing that fixed-expert architectures struggle to scale to lifelong settings. Classical continual-learning baselines (Seq-FT, LwF-LoRA, EWC-LoRA) suffer severe catastrophic forgetting (SR-F 79–85%) and achieve only 12–16% SR, confirming that parameter-regularization alone is insufficient for multi-scene, multi-instruction navigation. On unseen-scene generalization (S16–S18), Uni-Walker leads by 5% over SD-LoRA+TAKA (62% vs. 57%) and by 41% over HydraLoRA (62% vs. 16%), demonstrating that the decoupled shared/specific knowledge architecture transfers effectively to novel environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of LLM-based embodied navigation (NavLLM, SAME, OctoNav) and LoRA-based continual learning (HydraLoRA, BranchLoRA, MoE-LoRA). It departs from prior multi-task navigation agents that assume a fixed task distribution and joint training, and from existing LoRA continual learning methods that assume a fixed number of experts, by introducing a truly incremental setting where new scenes and instruction styles arrive sequentially without overlap. The DE-LoRA architecture is directly inspired by HydraLoRA's shared-A/specific-B decomposition but extends it with PCA-based knowledge inheritance, Fisher-weighted smoothing of the shared subspace, and an orthogonality constraint on expert subspaces—mechanisms not present in prior LoRA-continual-learning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LENL benchmark and task formulation address a practically critical gap: real-world navigation agents will inevitably encounter new environments and user interaction styles over their operational lifetime, yet prior work has only evaluated static multi-task generalization. The demonstrated scalability (~2.1 MB per new task) makes the approach deployable, and the task-agnostic inference via TAKA removes the need to know which task is being tested. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond Matterport3D to truly open-world 3D environments, how it behaves with more than 18 tasks or with overlapping scenes, and whether the CLIP-based retrieval in TAKA remains reliable as the expert library grows large. The reliance on a fixed instruction-style taxonomy (VLN/OLN/DUN) also limits applicability to novel interaction paradigms.
