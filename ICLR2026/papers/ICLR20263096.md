## Problem

Standard set function models (DeepSets, PointNet) rely on fixed, non-learnable pooling operations (sum, max) that force encoders to learn embeddings tailored to both the downstream task and the a priori chosen aggregation, limiting expressivity and transferability. While learnable aggregation functions have been proposed, no prior work has made the Kolmogorov (quasi-arithmetic) mean — a principled generalization encompassing arithmetic, geometric, harmonic, and power means — machine-learnable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes variable-size, unordered sets of homogeneous elements (28×28 grayscale images, 3D point clouds of 256–2048 points, molecular atom sets of 3–29 atoms, or 16-d vectors) with cardinality varying across instances. It produces a single fixed-size output: a scalar or vector-valued set function estimate (regression target, class score, or multi-label indicator). The architecture is permutation-invariant by construction, so element ordering is irrelevant, and the model handles arbitrary finite cardinality without padding or truncation.

## Method

QUANNs replace the fixed pooling in Janossy pooling with a Neuralized Kolmogorov Mean (NKM): F̂(X) = ρ[ψ⁻¹(1/|P_k(X)| Σ_{π∈P_k(X)} ψ(φ(π)))], where the generating function ψ is an invertible neural network (RevNet) that is jointly trained with the encoder φ and estimator ρ. Two variants are proposed: QUANN-1 (unary, k=1, encoding individual elements) and QUANN-2 (binary, k=2, encoding pairwise interactions via attention). The invertibility constraint on ψ is the key design choice: it guarantees the NKM derivative is non-singular (preventing dimensional collapse during backpropagation), acts as natural regularization by encouraging ψ to align with the target function's intrinsic structure, and — when ψ is locally monotonic — keeps the aggregated representation within a ball around the input embeddings, yielding more structured and transferable latent codes. The paper proves QUANNs are universal approximators for all Wasserstein-continuous permutation-invariant set functions, and characterizes approximation error scaling for max-decomposable (O(log n / w)) and sum-decomposable (O(n)) targets.

## Results

On MNIST-sets (MSE↓), QUANN-1 achieves 0.023 on the mean aggregation task, outperforming HPDS (0.047), NormDeepSet (0.089), and DeepSet (0.095); QUANN-2 further improves to 0.023 on mean and 1.579 on mode. On Omniglot-sets (Balanced ACC↑), QUANN-1 reaches 0.734 at n_max=10, surpassing the best baseline HPDS (0.610) by 12.4 points. On QM9 molecular property prediction (MSE↓), QUANN-2 achieves 104.8 on HOMO and 258.0 on LUMO, the best among all models. On ModelNet40 (ACC↑), QUANNs (0.688–0.782) are outperformed by PointNet (0.788–0.791) and FSPool (0.791–0.796), which exploit max-pooling well-suited to point-cloud geometry.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QUANN-1 outperforms all four unary baselines (DeepSet, PointNet, NormDeepSet, HPDS) on every MNIST aggregation task and every Omniglot cardinality setting, with the largest margin over HPDS on Omniglot (0.734 vs. 0.610 at n_max=10, a 12.4-point gap). QUANN-2 dominates the binary and non-Janossy baselines (SetTransformer, FSPool, SlotAtt, LAF) on MNIST and QM9, achieving the lowest MSE on both HOMO (104.8 vs. 164.6 for SlotAtt) and LUMO (258.0 vs. 386.6 for SlotAtt). The sole exception is ModelNet40, where PointNet (0.788–0.791) and FSPool (0.791–0.794) surpass QUANN-2 (0.762–0.782), which the authors attribute to max-pooling's natural suitability for detecting extreme geometric features in point clouds. In the transfer-learning experiment, QUANN encoders retain performance with a frozen pre-trained encoder (e.g., 0.021 MSE on MNIST mean) while baselines degrade substantially (e.g., DeepSet jumps from 0.095 to 0.207), and QUANN-1 encoders achieve the best Omniglot image-classification accuracy (0.597) when transferred to a non-set task, exceeding DeepSet (0.524) and SetTransformer (0.480).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Janossy pooling framework (Murphy et al., 2019) by replacing the fixed, non-trainable pooling operation (sum, max) with a learnable Neuralized Kolmogorov Mean (NKM), implemented via an invertible neural network (RevNet). It builds directly on the encoder-pool-estimator decomposition from DeepSets and PointNet, and on the mathematical theory of quasi-arithmetic means (Bullen & Bullen, 2003), which generalizes arithmetic, geometric, and power means. The key departure from prior learnable-pooling work (Hölder's Power DeepSets, Pellegrini et al.) is the use of a fully invertible generating function, which the authors claim is the first machine-learnable modification of the Kolmogorov mean and provides structural guarantees (dimensional collapse prevention, local monotonicity) that simpler parameterized means lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded, principled alternative to ad-hoc learnable pooling, with a universal approximation theorem covering all Wasserstein-continuous set functions and explicit error bounds for max- and sum-decomposable targets. The finding that QUANNs retain performance with frozen pre-trained encoders while baselines degrade substantially suggests the invertible structure induces genuinely more transferable representations, which could benefit downstream applications in GNNs, multi-modal fusion, and federated learning. A clear limitation is the O(n) approximation error for sum-decomposable functions, and the extension to uncountable sets remains open. The practical impact is moderate: the method is clean and general but does not dramatically outperform strong baselines on all benchmarks (e.g., PointNet and FSPool still win on ModelNet40).
