## Problem

Existing LLM privacy benchmarks evaluate models exclusively in natural language scenarios, yet LLMs are increasingly deployed as the cognitive core of embodied agents operating in homes, offices, and hospitals where privacy depends on spatial reasoning, dynamic environmental context, and physical action. No prior work systematically measures whether these agents can identify sensitive objects in cluttered physical spaces, adapt privacy judgments to changing multimodal cues, or resolve conflicts between task completion, inferred privacy, and critical social norms.

## Input / Output

Each scenario provides a structured PDDL problem file encoding object identities, types, and spatial containment relationships (e.g., which objects are on which container), augmented with simulated multimodal sensory cues (e.g., 'Visual: 5 people at table…', 'Audio: muffled conversation about a surprise party') that mimic embodied perception. Outputs vary by tier: a list of sensitive object identifiers (Tier 1), a 1–5 appropriateness rating or a selection from a randomized triplet of actions (Tier 2), a full action plan with a privacy-violation flag and task-completeness ratio (Tier 3), and a binary appropriate/inappropriate judgment or a selection from a pair (Tier 4). The PDDL format is a deliberate design choice to force spatial grounding rather than relying on free-text scene descriptions.

## Method

EAPrivacy is a four-tier, procedurally generated benchmark comprising over 400 scenarios across 60+ unique physical scenes (offices, labs, homes, hospitals, parks). Tier 1 places one sensitive object among 3–30 distractors on a target container and measures Main Object Ratio (I/N) and spatial grounding errors (Objects Not On Container). Tier 2 presents fixed action sets whose appropriateness shifts with multimodal context, evaluated via Mean Absolute Difference against human ratings and Selection Accuracy over human-pre-rated triplets. Tier 3 creates explicit task–privacy conflicts (e.g., 'move everything on the desk' while a hidden gift implies privacy), scored by Privacy Violation Rate and Task Completeness. Tier 4 poses high-stakes dilemmas where a critical social norm (public safety, medical emergency) must override personal privacy, using binary rating and selection. All tiers are grounded in US-based legal and social norms, validated against five PhD-level human raters with inter-annotator agreement stratification, and evaluated across 16 state-of-the-art LLMs including GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro/Flash, Claude 3.5 Haiku, Qwen 3, and Llama 3.3-70B.

## Results

Across EAPrivacy's four tiers, state-of-the-art LLMs show critical deficits in physical-world privacy awareness. In Tier 2 (shifting environments), the best model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) achieves only 59% selection accuracy, and the lowest Mean Absolute Difference is 1.32 on a 1–5 scale (Gemini 2.5 Flash). In Tier 3 (inferential privacy under task conflicts), privacy violation rates exceed 70% for all models, with gpt-oss-120b-high reaching 98%, while task completeness is near 0% for most models (only qwen-30b-thinking reaches 21%). In Tier 4 (social norms vs. privacy), performance is comparatively strong, with gpt-5-low and gpt-5-high achieving 100% selection accuracy, though rating accuracy still peaks at only 95%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the text-based privacy benchmark of Mireshghallah et al. (2023), which evaluates contextual integrity in natural language dialogues. On that benchmark, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Gemini 2.5 Flash, and GPT-5 all achieve a 0% secret leak rate (both worst-case and average), indicating near-perfect privacy compliance in text-only settings. However, on EAPrivacy's physically grounded scenarios, the same models' performance deteriorates dramatically: Gemini 2.5 Pro drops to 59% selection accuracy in Tier 2 and 74% privacy violation rate in Tier 3, while GPT-5 variants show 77–78% violation rates. This stark contrast demonstrates that current alignment strategies effective for textual privacy do not transfer to physical-world contexts requiring spatial reasoning, multimodal cue interpretation, and inferential social norm detection. The paper further shows (Appendix C) that even within Tier 1, switching from a simple text list to a PDDL-simulated physical environment reduces Main Object Ratio from ~0.78 (text) to ~0.40 (PDDL) on average, confirming that the physical grounding itself—not just the privacy concept—is the critical differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM privacy evaluation line initiated by Mireshghallah et al. (2023), which established contextual integrity as a theoretical framework but was confined to text-based dialogues, by transplanting the evaluation into physically grounded, PDDL-represented environments (following the embodied agent interface format of Li et al., 2025). It departs from prior privacy benchmarks by introducing multimodal sensory cues (visual, audio) to simulate embodied perception and by structuring evaluation into four progressive tiers from simple object identification to high-stakes ethical dilemmas. The paper also positions itself against the broader embodied AI literature (Habitat, iGibson, ALFWorld), which has focused on task completion and language grounding rather than privacy, arguing that physical-world privacy introduces spatial reasoning and inferential challenges absent from purely textual settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a stark and practically important gap: models achieving 0% secret leak in established text-based privacy benchmarks (Gemini 2.5, GPT-5) drop to 59% selection accuracy in physical-world privacy scenarios, suggesting current alignment is insufficient for embodied deployment. The counterintuitive finding that enabling 'thinking' modes often degrades performance across multiple model families challenges a core assumption in the reasoning-enhanced LLM literature. Key limitations include reliance on simulated PDDL environments rather than real sensor input, a small annotator pool (5 PhD-level raters) with US-centric cultural norms, and the absence of cross-cultural evaluation. Open questions include whether the PDDL abstraction faithfully captures the perceptual complexity of real physical environments, how findings generalize to multimodal (vision-language-action) models, and how to design alignment strategies that handle the asymmetric conservatism pattern (over-cautious task execution paired with under-cautious privacy protection).
