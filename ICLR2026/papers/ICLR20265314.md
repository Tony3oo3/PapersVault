## Problem

Scientific time series face a compound set of challenges—limited samples, missing observations, multi-resolution sampling, irregular intervals, and the need for physical consistency—that no single existing framework addresses. Physics-informed models (PINNs, NeuralODEs) require retraining to embed equations, while simulation-based inference methods demand differentiable simulators and scale poorly to long, irregular sequences. The paper asks whether a unified generative model can handle all these data irregularities end-to-end while also enforcing physical laws at inference time without any architectural modification or retraining.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multivariate time series of arbitrary shape, with missing entries, multi-resolution sampling rates across channels, and irregular temporal intervals, all unified through a four-type masking strategy (random, block, stride, reconstruction) that produces a fixed-shape tensor of shape R^{B×L×K}. A physical law is supplied as a PDE residual function F(τ, x, u, ∂x/∂u, …) that is used only at inference. Outputs are probabilistic: the model generates full conditional distributions over future windows (reported via CRPS), imputed values for masked positions, synthetic multivariate series, and anomaly scores, all from the same pre-trained weights.

## Method

PINFDiT is a diffusion transformer that embeds raw time-series tokens directly (a 'WYSIWYG' design without patching or vector quantization) and conditions on observations via adaptive layer normalization (AdaLN) rather than concatenation. A Time Series Mask Unit generates four complementary mask types during self-supervised pre-training, letting a single model learn forecasting, imputation, and generation from the same objective. The key novelty is a model-free physics injection at inference: the paper proves (Theorem 3.1) that the optimal physics-informed posterior has a closed-form Boltzmann distribution q(x|con) ∝ exp(K(x;F) + α log p(x|con)), where K is the squared PDE residual. Sampling from this distribution is performed via calibrated Langevin dynamics (Algorithm 1) with an ELBO-based score approximation, yielding O(N^{-1/2}) KL convergence (Theorem 3.2) and a residual-variance coupling guarantee (Lemma 3.3) that links statistical convergence directly to physical consistency—all without retraining or modifying the backbone.

## Results

On four PDE-based fluid dynamics benchmarks (Advection, Burgers, Navier-Stokes, Diffusion-Sorption), PINFDiT achieves the best RMSE on three of four systems: 0.0039 (Advection), 0.0133 (Burgers), and 0.0037 (Navier-Stokes), reducing RMSE by 88.3%, 35.1%, and 54.3% respectively over the best foundation model (Chronos-T5-B). On practical forecasting with missing/multi-resolution data, PINFDiT achieves the best MAE/MSE on all six datasets (e.g., 0.457/0.354 on Air Quality, 0.517/0.534 on MIMIC-III, 0.516/0.418 on NASDAQ). In zero-shot probabilistic forecasting, PINFDiT attains the lowest CRPS_sum on Solar (0.424), Electricity (0.030), and Taxi (0.392), outperforming Moirai, LagLLama, TimeMixer, and TimeLLM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PINFDiT decisively outperforms all foundation models (Chronos, Moirai, TimesFM) and standard deep learning baselines (DLinear, PatchTST, DiffusionTS, NeuralODE, NeuralCDE) on Advection, Burgers, and Navier-Stokes, with RMSE reductions of 67–99% over the next-best method. Against the strongest diffusion-based imputer CSDI, PINFDiT wins on Advection (0.0039 vs. 0.0118 RMSE), Burgers (0.0133 vs. 0.0167), and Navier-Stokes (0.0037 vs. 0.0094), but loses on Diffusion-Sorption (0.0052 vs. 0.0012 RMSE), where CSDI's dedicated imputation architecture has an edge. Among simulation-based inference methods, PINFDiT surpasses both SNPE and LFBC by 85–95% on RMSE across all four PDE systems, demonstrating that closed-form Langevin correction is far more sample-efficient than likelihood-free posterior estimation. In practical forecasting (Table 3), PINFDiT beats CSDI by 12.97% MAE on Air Quality and 6.17% MSE on MIMIC-III, and outperforms all transformer/LLM-based forecasters (TimeLLM, TimeMixer, GPT4TS) by 20–35% on MAE. In zero-shot settings (Table 6), PINFDiT wins on Solar, Electricity, and Taxi but trails Moirai-Large on Traffic (0.351 vs. 0.111 CRPS_sum) and Moirai-Small on Exchange (0.019 vs. 0.007), indicating that its advantage is most pronounced in domains with strong physical structure or high uncertainty.

## Relation to Prior Work

PINFDiT builds on the diffusion transformer architecture (DiT, VDT) and the conditional diffusion framework for time series established by CSDI and TSDiff, but departs fundamentally from the physics-informed learning lineage (PINNs, NeuralODEs, NeuralCDEs) by moving physics enforcement from training-time loss augmentation to inference-time Langevin correction. Rather than requiring a differentiable simulator as in simulation-based inference (SNPE, LFBC), the paper derives a closed-form Boltzmann energy distribution that unifies the PDE residual and the learned score function, enabling a plug-and-play refinement step on any frozen generative model. The unified masking strategy (random, block, stride, reconstruction) draws on VDT's mask modeling for video but is specifically designed to handle the heterogeneous imperfections of scientific time series—missing values, multi-resolution sampling, and irregular intervals—within a single training pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The inference-time physics injection paradigm offers a practical route to domain-specialized forecasting without the cost of retraining or architectural modification, which is particularly valuable in data-scarce scientific domains where physical laws provide strong priors. The theoretical convergence guarantees (O(N^{-1/2}) KL rate) and the residual-variance coupling lemma give practitioners a clear interpretability pathway linking refinement steps to physical consistency. However, the PDE experiments are limited to 1D systems, the real-world gains on ERA5 and MIMIC-III are modest (0.1–0.2% ACC, ~6% MSE), and the authors acknowledge that very long sequences and multi-modal time series remain unaddressed. Open questions include whether the Langevin correction scales to high-dimensional PDEs (e.g., 2D/3D Navier-Stokes) and how the method interacts with the growing ecosystem of time series foundation models beyond the single Chronos-T5-S test.
