## Problem

Combining multiple task vectors in Task Arithmetic causes cross-task interference through representation drift, degrading the composed model's performance. Existing remedies such as τJp regularize against this drift but require access to other tasks' training data, conflicting with privacy constraints and the modularity that makes task arithmetic attractive. The paper asks whether a dataless regularizer can enforce weight disentanglement without any external task data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263669/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method fine-tunes a pre-trained foundation model (CLIP ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14 for vision; T5-base for text) on individual task datasets to produce task vectors (parameter updates). During fine-tuning, only the target task's own training data is consumed; the regularizer relies on pre-computed KFAC curvature factors (two small matrices per layer) estimated from a small subset (128–256 examples) of each task, eliminating the need to store or access full external datasets. The final output is a set of disentangled task vectors combined via simple linear addition or subtraction to form a multi-task model that maps RGB images or text to class labels.

## Method

Under first-order linearization around pre-trained weights, representation drift reduces to a quadratic form τ'ᵀ G_t τ' where G_t is the Jacobian Gram matrix — an instance of the generalized Gauss-Newton (GGN) matrix. The paper approximates G_t using Kronecker-Factored Approximate Curvature (KFAC), decomposing each layer's GGN block into a Kronecker product of two small matrices (input covariance A and output gradient covariance B), making the regularizer computationally tractable without instantiating the full P×P matrix. To handle multiple tasks without O(T) storage, they propose a merging heuristic that aggregates per-task Kronecker factors into a single surrogate (ΣB ⊗ ΣA), yielding constant complexity in the number of tasks. The regularizer is applied during linearized fine-tuning, penalizing the task vector's curvature-weighted projection onto other tasks' statistics, which empirically produces weight-disentangled vectors that are robust to rescaling and enable clear task localization.

## Results

On the 8 Vision benchmark (task addition, linearized fine-tuning), TAK achieves 85.8% absolute / 97.6% normalized accuracy on ViT-B/32, 88.3% / 97.9% on ViT-B/16, and 91.6% / 99.3% on ViT-L/14 with α=1 (no tuning), matching or exceeding the data-dependent τJp baseline. On task negation, TAK achieves the lowest target-task accuracy (3.4% on ViT-B/32, 3.4% on ViT-B/16, 3.5% on ViT-L/14) while preserving the highest control-task accuracy (62.4%, 66.4%, 72.6% respectively), outperforming τJp by 3.3 points on target forgetting for ViT-B/32. On language tasks (T5-base, 6NLI), TAK reaches 78.7% absolute / 98.9% normalized accuracy, competitive with τJp (81.3% / 100%) while remaining fully dataless.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAK matches or slightly exceeds τJp (Yoshida et al., 2025) on task addition across all three CLIP backbones (e.g., 91.6% vs. 90.9% absolute on ViT-L/14) while being fully dataless, and clearly wins on task negation (3.4% vs. 6.7% target accuracy on ViT-B/32, 3.4% vs. 4.7% on ViT-B/16). Against the diagonal GGN approximation (Porrello et al., 2025), TAK gains 5.7 points on ViT-B/32 and 5.4 points on ViT-B/16, confirming that the Kronecker-factored structure captures intra-layer correlations that a diagonal approximation misses. Compared to plain linear fine-tuning, TAK improves absolute accuracy by 9.1 points (ViT-B/32) and 11.4 points (ViT-L/14). In the non-linear regime, Attn.Only FT + TAK reaches 84.3% on ViT-B/16 (best α), competitive with TaLoS (82.6%) and approaching τJp's 88.6%. On language tasks, τJp retains a small edge (81.3% vs. 78.7% absolute), suggesting that text domains may benefit from more accurate curvature estimation than KFAC provides. Post-hoc merging strategies (TIES, TSV, ISO) are complementary rather than competing: when applied to TAK's linearized checkpoints, they do not surpass simple task-vector summation with KFAC regularization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of task arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2022), tangent-space linearized fine-tuning (Ortiz-Jimenez et al., 2023), and second-order curvature approximation (Martens & Grosse, 2015). The key conceptual step is recognizing that the representation-drift regularizer, which previously required access to other tasks' data (as in τJp, Yoshida et al., 2025), can be reformulated as a quadratic form of the Jacobian Gram matrix — an instance of the generalized Gauss-Newton matrix (Schraudolph, 2003) — thereby enabling the use of Kronecker-factored approximate curvature (KFAC) as a dataless, structured surrogate. The paper departs from prior curvature-based approaches (e.g., the diagonal GGN of Porrello et al., 2025) by exploiting intra-layer correlations captured by KFAC, and from τJp by eliminating the need for external task data entirely through pre-computed curvature factors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataless property is practically significant for privacy-preserving and decentralized settings where sharing training data across tasks is infeasible, and the constant-complexity aggregation in the number of tasks makes the method scalable to large task libraries. The robustness to the scaling coefficient α (eliminating held-out tuning) and the emergent task-localization behavior (enabling OOD detection) are useful side effects that broaden applicability. Open questions include whether the approach extends to full or parameter-efficient fine-tuning without linearization, how it scales to very large models where KFAC storage becomes prohibitive, and whether more sophisticated curvature estimators could close the remaining gap observed in the text domain compared to data-dependent methods like τJp.
