## Problem

Joint verification of multiple draft tokens in speculative decoding requires full joint probabilities over all decoding paths to provably recover the target distribution, which is computationally intractable for autoregressive models. Existing work either employs lossy fixed acceptance thresholds or is constrained by partial information (e.g., Blockwise Verification), leaving a gap between the ideal lossless joint verification and what is practically achievable.

## Input / Output

Given a draft token sequence X_{1:γ} of length γ, the target model's conditional probabilities p(·|X_{1:t}) for t=0,...,γ, and the draft model's conditional probabilities q(·|X_{1:t}) for t=0,...,γ−1, HSD outputs the longest accepted prefix X_{1:τ} and a single resampled token at position τ+1 drawn from a capped branch resampling distribution. Unlike the naive hierarchical version that would require γ−τ+1 additional target model calls, the capped formulation reduces resampling to exactly one step, after which remaining positions are filled by the next speculative decoding iteration at no extra cost.

## Method

HSD introduces a hierarchical branch resampling strategy that organizes multiple resampling distributions across successive prefix levels, where each level recovers only the partial target distribution within its accessible branch. The key mechanism is Capped Branch Resampling: a Capped Prefix Ratio r* caps the joint probability ratio at the Maximum Prefix Ratio Index, enabling the full target distribution to be recovered with a single resampling step rather than recursive resampling at every rejected position. A backward scan from position γ to 1 identifies the longest accepted prefix using branch-divergence-based acceptance probabilities, and the asymmetry of branch divergence (which equals p(X_{1:t−1}) − q(X_{1:t−1})) determines whether a branch has sufficient excess mass to self-correct or must defer correction to higher hierarchy levels. The paper proves losslessness via a telescoping argument over unique capping indices, showing that the sum of acceptance mass and hierarchical resampling masses exactly equals the target joint probability p(X_{1:γ}).

## Results

On GSM8K, HumanEval, and CNN/DailyMail using Qwen2.5 (0.5B draft, 14B/32B/72B target), HSD achieves an average 6.2% improvement in Block Efficiency and 6.7% in Decoding Speed over tokenwise verification, with peak gains of 12.3% BE and 11.4% DS on HumanEval at 32B. On GSM8K at 14B, HSD reaches 6.30 tokens/step and 91.05 tokens/s, surpassing tokenwise (5.99, 82.28) and blockwise (6.13, 86.06). Integrating HSD into EAGLE-3 (EAGLE-3H) yields a 12.4% decoding speed improvement (80.49 vs. 71.59 tokens/s) and 4.4% block efficiency gain (3.55 vs. 3.40 tokens/step) on GSM8K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HSD consistently outperforms tokenwise verification (Leviathan et al., 2023) across all three datasets and all model scales, with average gains of 6.2% in Block Efficiency and 6.7% in Decoding Speed; the largest single-dataset gain is 12.3% BE on HumanEval at 32B. Against blockwise verification (Sun et al., 2024), HSD also wins uniformly, e.g., 6.30 vs. 6.13 BE on GSM8K-14B and 5.49 vs. 5.15 BE on HumanEval-32B, confirming that the hierarchical capping mechanism extracts more accepted tokens than the independent block-level check. In the multi-draft setting (11 candidates, Recursive Reject Sampling baseline from Yang et al., 2024), HSD improves BE by 2.8–11.1% and DS by 2.0–8.9%, with the largest gain on CNN/DailyMail (11.1% BE). Finally, replacing EAGLE-3's tokenwise verifier with HSD (EAGLE-3H) yields a 12.4% decoding speed increase (80.49 vs. 71.59 tokens/s) on GSM8K, establishing a new state-of-the-art for lossless speculative decoding in that framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tokenwise speculative decoding framework of Leviathan et al. (2023) by generalizing its divergence-based acceptance/resampling mechanism to joint sequence-level verification. It directly addresses the joint intractability problem that limits Blockwise Verification (Sun et al., 2024) and the lossy threshold approach of MTAD (Qin et al., 2025), introducing a hierarchical branch resampling strategy with capped prefix ratios that provably recovers the full target distribution. Unlike prior verification methods that are either tokenwise (independent) or blockwise (with unclear integration potential), HSD is designed to be orthogonal to drafting methods and composable with multi-draft frameworks like EAGLE-3 and Recursive Reject Sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HSD provides a clean theoretical resolution to the joint intractability problem in lossless speculative decoding, offering a drop-in verification upgrade that yields consistent 6–12% speedups across model families (Qwen2.5, LLaMA-3.1) and task domains (math, code, summarization). The practical significance is amplified by its compatibility with state-of-the-art drafting frameworks, as demonstrated by the 12.4% decoding speed gain when integrated into EAGLE-3. However, the paper itself notes that in the EAGLE-3 setting with top-K sampling, all draft probabilities collapse to 1, meaning the theoretical verification gain is confounded by implementation efficiency rather than the resampling mechanism itself. Open questions include whether the hierarchical structure provides additional gains as draft lengths grow substantially, and how the method behaves under extreme distributional mismatch between draft and target models.
