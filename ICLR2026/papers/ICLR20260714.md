## Problem

Gaussian Splatting radiance fields bake appearance and shadow information, making realistic 3D object-scene composition inconsistent when combining separately reconstructed objects and scenes. Existing Gaussian-based inverse rendering methods rely on per-point ray tracing for occlusion and indirect lighting, which is too slow for real-time composition, while learning-based lighting estimators predict from a single image and fail to maintain multi-view consistency in complex scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260714/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes two independent sets of multi-view images (object and scene) plus explicit 3D placement parameters (location, orientation, scale) for the object. The object is reconstructed as a relightable 2D Gaussian surfel cloud with per-surfel albedo, roughness, and metallic attributes; the scene is reconstructed as a standard Gaussian radiance field. At the object placement location, a 360° sweep of the scene produces a partial RGB panorama, normal map, and alpha mask that feed a fine-tuned diffusion model to generate a 1024×512 HDR environment map (converted to a 512×512 octahedral texture). The final output is a real-time rendered composition at ~26 FPS with physically plausible cast shadows, requiring only 36 seconds of editing time per placement.

## Method

The core architectural contribution is Surface Octahedral Probes (SOPs): small octahedral textures storing indirect lighting and occlusion, placed near the object surface via multi-view depth fusion and FPS sampling with a 1% normal offset. During reconstruction, SOPs are initialized by ray tracing and then optimized under its guidance, allowing shading points to query illumination via KNN interpolation (with spatial and back-face weights) instead of expensive per-point ray tracing, yielding a 2×+ speedup over IRGS. For lighting estimation, the paper reformulates full-scene lighting as a local environment map inpainting problem: a 360° trace of the Gaussian scene yields a partial panorama, which a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion 2.1 model (conditioned on RGB, normals, and alpha mask) completes, with EV-conditioned prompting at three exposure levels fused into an HDR map. During rendering, object relighting uses Monte Carlo integration over the octahedral environment map with self-occlusion from the object's SOPs, while cast shadows are computed by interpolating object-induced occlusion from scene-space SOPs (10k probes in a 6× object-size region) and taking the ratio of occluded to unoccluded radiance, followed by depth compositing.

## Results

On the SynCom composition benchmark, ComGS (SOPs) achieves 24.28 dB PSNR and 0.868 SSIM, outperforming the best baseline GI-GS (22.88 dB PSNR, 0.849 SSIM) by +1.4 dB and +0.019 respectively. Subjective MOS scores show 4.56/5 for 3D consistency and 4.59/5 for harmony, versus 3.75/5 and 2.91/5 for GI-GS — a 21% and 56% improvement. Rendering speed reaches 26.14 FPS with a 36-second editing time, compared to 0.29 FPS for GI-GS and 0.03 FPS for IRGS. On TensoIR reconstruction, ComGS matches IRGS in accuracy (35.82 vs. 35.75 NVS PSNR) while training 2.7× faster (7.93 min vs. 21.45 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ComGS (SOPs) outperforms all seven composition baselines on every metric. Against the strongest Gaussian inverse-rendering baseline GI-GS, it gains +1.4 dB PSNR, +0.019 SSIM, +0.81 Con., and +1.68 Harm. while rendering 90× faster (26.14 vs. 0.29 FPS). IRGS, the closest competitor in shadow quality, is surpassed by +1.86 dB PSNR and +1.06 Harm. but runs at only 0.03 FPS. Image-composition methods (DiffHarmony, ZeroComp, MV-CoLight) trail by 1.8–4.0 dB PSNR and score well below 3.0 on harmony, reflecting their inability to model 3D occlusion and shadows. For reconstruction on TensoIR, ComGS matches IRGS in NVS and albedo PSNR (35.82 vs. 35.75; 31.68 vs. 31.65) and slightly exceeds it in relighting PSNR (30.47 vs. 30.25), while training 2.7× faster (7.93 vs. 21.45 min). R3DG achieves the highest NVS PSNR (38.42) but lacks indirect-lighting supervision, producing bright-spot artifacts in relighting; ComGS trades 2.6 dB NVS PSNR for correct relighting and 6× faster training. DiffusionLight, the single-image lighting estimator, scores only 21.84 dB PSNR and 2.17 Harm., confirming its multi-view inconsistency in complex scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) as the geometric rendering primitive, extending surfels with PBR material parameters for relightable reconstruction, and on 3DGS's differentiable splatting framework for the overall pipeline. It departs from prior Gaussian-based inverse rendering methods (IRGS, GS-IR, R3DG) that rely on per-point ray tracing for occlusion and indirect lighting by introducing Surface Octahedral Probes (SOPs) that cache this information and enable KNN interpolation queries. For lighting estimation, it diverges from both inverse-rendering-based scene decomposition (which fails in complex scenes) and single-image diffusion-based methods (which lack multi-view consistency) by reformulating the problem as local environment map inpainting from a 360° Gaussian scene trace, completed via a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion 2.1 model using the EV-conditioned prompting scheme from DiffusionLight.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that physically plausible 3D object-scene composition with real-time rendering (26 FPS) and vivid shadows is achievable, a capability that prior methods could not deliver simultaneously. The SOPs technique is a broadly useful primitive for any Gaussian-based pipeline requiring efficient occlusion or indirect lighting queries, potentially accelerating a range of inverse rendering and global illumination tasks. Key limitations include the assumption of small inserted objects (no remote shadows) and Lambertian scene approximation (no mirror reflections), and open questions remain around incremental SOP updates for moving objects, single-view scene inputs, and adaptive probe placement to reduce memory.
