## Problem

Recovering a planted direction θ* in high-dimensional settings (tensor PCA, single-index models) requires n ≳ d^{k*-1} samples for standard SGD, where k* is the information exponent. Smoothing-based approaches achieve the optimal d^{k*/2} rate, but it was unclear whether this rate could be matched without explicitly modifying the loss landscape. This paper asks whether Langevin dynamics combined with iterate averaging can emulate the effect of smoothing and achieve the same optimal rate.

## Input / Output

For single-index models, the input is n i.i.d. Gaussian samples (x_i, y_i) with y_i = σ(θ*·x_i) + ξ_i, plus hyperparameters ε (inverse temperature) and T (time horizon); for tensor PCA, the input is a noisy k-tensor T = θ*^{⊗k} + n^{-1/2}Z. The algorithm uses the full ERM loss (not online SGD) and produces either the normalized time-averaged iterate θ̂/||θ̂|| (odd k*) or the top eigenvector of the time-averaged second-moment matrix M̂ (even k*). Notably, the individual iterates remain near the equator (|θ_t·θ*| ≲ d^{-1/2}) throughout training; only the time average carries signal.

## Method

The algorithm runs a Langevin SDE on the sphere, dθ = (-(d-1)/2 θ + εb(θ))dt + P_θ^⊥ dW_t, where b(θ) = -∇_θ L_n(θ) is the negative spherical gradient of the empirical loss. The key insight is that θ_t decomposes as β_t + E_t, where β_t is spherical Brownian motion and E_t is an O(ε) error term; the noise in Langevin dynamics naturally emulates landscape smoothing without any explicit modification. Time averaging (1/T)∫θ_t dt causes the Brownian component to vanish by ergodicity on the sphere, while the error component concentrates to the partial-trace estimator direction E_{z~μ}[b(z)] (odd k*) or a rank-one spiked matrix (even k*). For odd k*, the normalized time average directly recovers θ*; for even k*, the top eigenvector of (1/T)∫θ_t θ_t^⊤ dt recovers θ*. This yields n ≳ d^{⌈k*/2⌉} samples for direct recovery, or n ≳ d^{k*/2} with a subsequent online SGD warm-start step.

## Results

The main theoretical guarantee (Theorem 2 for odd k*, Theorem 3 for even k*) shows that Langevin dynamics with time-averaged iterates recovers the planted direction θ* with n ≳ d^{⌈k*/2⌉} samples, matching the optimal computational-statistical tradeoff. With a warm-start followed by online SGD (Corollary 1), the sample complexity improves to n ≳ d^{k*/2}, shaving off a √d factor. The key regime is ε = o(d^{-(k*-3)/2}) for odd k* and ε = o(d^{-(k*-2)/2}) for even k*, with running time T ≳ d^{k*}/ε². Experiments (Figures 1–2) with d=100 and n=10d^{⌈k*/2⌉} samples confirm that the time-averaged estimator converges to θ* while individual iterates remain near the equator throughout training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method matches the optimal sample complexity of the smoothed-landscape approach (Damian et al., 2023) and the algebraic partial-trace estimator (Montanari & Richard, 2014) at n ≳ d^{k*/2}, but does so without any explicit smoothing of the loss landscape or tensor unfolding. Compared to standard online SGD (Ben Arous et al., 2021), which requires n ≳ d^{k*−1} samples, the Langevin + averaging approach improves the exponent from k*−1 to k*/2. The key distinction from prior smoothing-based methods is that this work operates in a low signal-to-noise regime where the iterate stays near the equator (|θ·θ*| ≲ d^{-1/2}), and the noise from Langevin dynamics combined with time averaging emulates the effect of smoothing. Unlike the online SGD baselines, the algorithm uses the full empirical risk (ERM) rather than processing data in an online fashion. The even-k* case is handled via a second-order estimator (top eigenvector of the time-averaged θθᵀ matrix), since the first-order estimator vanishes by symmetry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two threads: (1) the information-exponent framework of Ben Arous et al. (2021) showing that online SGD requires n ≳ d^{k*-1} samples for single-index models and tensor PCA, and (2) the landscape-smoothing approach of Damian et al. (2023) that achieves the optimal n ≳ d^{k*/2} rate by explicitly smoothing the loss. The key departure is from Ben Arous et al. (2020), who conjectured that Langevin dynamics fundamentally cannot escape the equator in tensor PCA and thus cannot recover the planted direction. This paper resolves that conjecture in the negative by showing that while individual iterates do remain near the equator, the time-averaged iterate (stochastic weight averaging) still recovers θ* at the optimal rate, with the noise-injection of Langevin dynamics implicitly emulating the effect of explicit landscape smoothing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides a new mechanism for bridging the computational-statistical gap in high-dimensional estimation: rather than explicitly smoothing the landscape (as in Damian et al., 2023) or applying label transformations, one can exploit the intrinsic noise of Langevin dynamics combined with iterate averaging. This reframes the role of stochasticity in non-convex optimization from a hindrance to a resource. The main open question is whether the conjectured extension to minibatch SGD (without any added noise) holds, which would make the technique directly applicable to standard training pipelines. Limitations include the requirement of a specific inverse-temperature regime, the need for a second-order estimator in the even-k* case, and the fact that the algorithm is offline (ERM-based) rather than online.
