## Problem

Whether modern speech-to-speech (S2S) systems can converse like humans remains an open question, as no prior Turing test has evaluated S2S interaction (only text or text-to-speech). Beyond the binary pass/fail outcome, it is unclear which specific dimensions of human-likeness—semantic, paralinguistic, emotional, or persona-related—account for the gap, and whether off-the-shelf AI models can reliably serve as automated judges for this task.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-turn audio dialogues (20–60 seconds, two speakers, Chinese or English) and produces two outputs: (1) a binary human-vs-machine classification decision, and (2) 18 interpretable human-likeness scores on a 5-point ordinal scale spanning five categories (semantic/pragmatic, non-physiological paralinguistic, physiological paralinguistic, mechanical persona, and emotional expression). The audio is encoded by Qwen2.5-Omni-7B using a two-source fused pooling readout (first-step mean + last-token hidden states), and the 18 scores are constrained to preserve ordinal structure via learnable cut-points.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage interpretable judge built on Qwen2.5-Omni-7B. In stage 1, an Ordinal Discretization Layer (ODL) maps the fused audio-language embedding to 18 latent scores, each converted into an ordinal probability distribution via a cumulative-link formulation with learnable, strictly ordered cut-points; the ODL is trained by minimizing ordinal negative log-likelihood against crowd-annotated 1–5 human-likeness ratings. In stage 2, the ODL logits are fed into a regularized linear classifier (with symmetry regularization λ=0.1) for the final binary human/machine decision, ensuring the classification remains interpretable as a weighted combination of named human-likeness dimensions. The paper also constructs a 1,486-dialogue, 17.7-hour dataset covering 9 S2S systems, 28 human speakers across 10 countries, and TTS-synthesized pseudo-human dialogues, and demonstrates that the resulting model achieves 96.05% overall accuracy—substantially outperforming both human judges (72.84%) and all 9 off-the-shelf AI judges (best: 62.79%)—while generalizing to out-of-domain datasets (CosyVoice2, Fisher, MultiDialog) at 97.40%.

## Results

The proposed interpretable AI judge achieves 96.05% overall binary classification accuracy on the Turing test, surpassing human evaluators (72.84%), Qwen2.5-Omni (41.63%), and Qwen2.5-Omni with LoRA (57.44%). On the unseen pseudo-human dialogues, the model reaches 93.06% accuracy, demonstrating strong generalization. On out-of-domain datasets (CosyVoice2, Fisher, MultiDialog), the model maintains 97.40% overall accuracy and 0.9881 ROC-AUC. In the Turing test itself, no evaluated S2S system passes, with success rates ranging from 0.07 to 0.31 (well below the 0.5 chance threshold), while human-human dialogues achieve 0.70–0.87.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its interpretable AI judge against three categories of baselines. Against 9 off-the-shelf multimodal models used as Turing test judges (Table 2), all underperform humans: the best, Gemini 2.5 Pro, reaches only 62.79% overall accuracy versus 72.84% for humans, while models like GPT-4o-Audio-Preview (41.16%) and Baichuan-Audio (36.28%) exhibit severe class biases. Against the backbone model Qwen2.5-Omni (41.63%) and its LoRA-fine-tuned variant (57.44%), the proposed ODL + linear classifier approach achieves 96.05% overall accuracy, a 23.21-point improvement over human evaluators and a 38.61-point gain over LoRA. On the out-of-domain evaluation (Table 4), the model generalizes to CosyVoice2 (98.44%), Fisher (98.44%), and MultiDialog (95.31%) with 0.9881 ROC-AUC, confirming robustness beyond the training distribution. The ablation (Table 17) further shows the Ordinal Discretization Layer contributes 3.72 points over a plain linear layer (96.05% vs. 92.33%), validating the ordinal modeling choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Turing Test paradigm—previously applied to text (Jones & Bergen, 2024; Rathi et al., 2024) and text-to-speech (Chan, 2003; Wang et al., 2025b)—to the speech-to-speech modality, where both input and output are natural speech in multi-turn dialogue. Methodologically, it builds on the Ordinal Discretization Layer (Tutz, 2022) for modeling ordinal human ratings and uses Qwen2.5-Omni as a frozen multimodal encoder, departing from prior S2S benchmarks (VoiceBench, MMAU-Pro, MTalk-Bench) that measure intelligence or task accuracy rather than perceptual human-likeness. The key conceptual departure is reframing the evaluation question from 'how smart is the model?' to 'does it sound and behave like a human?', with an 18-dimension taxonomy grounded in paralinguistics, social psychology, and speech science literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic, large-scale (2,968 judgments, 9 systems, 28 speakers, 2 languages) human-likeness evaluation for S2S systems, and the 18-dimension diagnostic taxonomy provides actionable guidance: the bottleneck has shifted from semantic understanding to paralinguistic naturalness, emotional expressivity, and conversational persona. The interpretable AI judge (96% accuracy, outperforming human evaluators) offers a scalable, transparent tool for rapid iteration that the community can adopt. Open questions include whether the near-zero correlation between reasoning ability and human-likeness (r=0.0456, though based on only 4 overlapping models) generalizes, whether the 0.5 success-rate threshold remains meaningful as AI familiarity grows, and whether the taxonomy captures culture-specific human-likeness cues beyond English and Chinese.
