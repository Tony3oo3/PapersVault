## Problem

Existing machine learning approaches for CFD flow field prediction have focused almost exclusively on single-object geometries, with no public dataset or benchmark for interacting multi-body configurations such as tandem airfoils. Although industries have already generated large single-airfoil simulation datasets, the extension to tandem configurations—representative of compressor blades, UAVs, and automotive aerodynamics—remains underexplored, and no prior work has demonstrated how to reuse single-object data to improve tandem-object flow prediction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a CFD mesh graph (nodes and bidirectional edges from OpenFOAM C-grid or overset/chimera meshes) with per-node features comprising node positions (2D), shortest vector to geometry (2D), directional integrated distance across 8 angular segments (8D), inlet/overlap values (3D), and optionally freestream or combined-field estimates (3D), yielding 15 or 18 input dimensions. The network outputs three scalar fields per node—x-velocity, y-velocity, and kinematic pressure—over the full computational domain. In the multi-NN inference variant, the domain is partitioned into front, back, upper, and lower sub-graphs with overlapping regions, and each sub-network predicts its portion sequentially, with overlap values propagated from the most recently updated network.

## Method

The core contribution is a curriculum learning pipeline that reuses single-airfoil data to predict tandem-airfoil flows. First, a GNN (MeshGraphNet or invariant edge-GCNN) is residually pre-trained on single-airfoil cases using freestream conditions as the estimate field, so the network learns only the perturbation U′ = U − U∞. The pre-trained network then predicts individual single-airfoil fields, which are smoothly combined using weights proportional to each field's deviation from freestream (rather than distance-based interpolation), producing a cost-effective preliminary tandem-airfoil estimate. The tandem-airfoil networks are initialized with the pre-trained weights and residually trained against the combined-field estimate. A multi-NN inference scheme further decomposes the domain into front, back, upper, and lower sub-graphs, each predicted by a dedicated network with overlap-region propagation, ensuring each network handles at most one airfoil's influence. The DID geometry representation is extended to multi-object settings via the same smooth-combining mechanism (using d_max as reference), reducing computation from ~5222 s to ~3.5 s per field compared to direct calculation.

## Results

The proposed curriculum framework (MGN + PRE-RES-FREE + RES-COMB) achieves an average 58.5% MSE reduction over the MGN baseline and 48.8% over the IVE baseline across three fixed-Re tandem-airfoil datasets (Cruise AOA=0°, Cruise AOA=5°, Takeoff). On the Takeoff dataset with ground effect, the improvement reaches 70.0% (MSE 1.12 vs. 3.74 ×10⁻² for MGN baseline). Under varying high-Re conditions, the method achieves 94.4% MSE reduction on Cruise Random and 65.6% on Race Car. The full pipeline reduces prediction wall time to 65.6 s per case, a 99.7% reduction compared to the 23,372 s average CPU time for OpenFOAM simulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks against two GNN architectures: MeshGraphNet (MGN, Pfaff et al. 2021) and invariant edge-GCNN (IVE, Chen et al. 2021), both used as direct-training baselines without any curriculum, residual, or smooth-combining schemes. The proposed full curriculum (PRE-RES-FREE + RES-COMB) consistently outperforms the MGN baseline by 56–70% MSE reduction across all three fixed-Re datasets, with the largest gain (70%) on the Takeoff dataset involving ground effect. For IVE, the improvement is 39–70%, though the IVE + PRE variant actually degrades performance (−7.9% average), indicating that simple weight transfer without residual training is insufficient for this architecture. On the high-Re Cruise Random dataset, the method achieves a 94.4% MSE reduction (0.10 vs. 1.79 ×10⁻²), and on the Race Car dataset a 65.6% reduction (0.21 vs. 0.61 ×10⁻²), demonstrating robustness to varying Reynolds numbers and angles of attack. The multi-NN inference strategy further contributes a 70% MSE reduction over single-NN inference, confirming that domain decomposition is critical for tandem-geometry prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-object flow field prediction paradigm established by MeshGraphNet (Pfaff et al., 2021) and invariant edge-GCNN (Chen et al., 2021) to tandem (two-body) airfoil configurations, a setting the authors identify as underexplored. It builds directly on Jessica et al. (2024), who introduced the shortest vector (SV) and directional integrated distance (DID) geometry encodings and residual training with low-resolution estimates; here, the DID is extended to multi-object scenarios via a smooth-combining procedure, and the residual estimate is replaced by the physically-motivated freestream condition, eliminating the need for any auxiliary simulation. The curriculum learning strategy (single-airfoil pre-training → tandem fine-tuning) and the multi-NN domain-decomposition inference are novel combinations that repurpose ideas from transfer learning and CFD domain decomposition for the graph-based flow prediction setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TandemFoilSet is the first public dataset specifically designed for tandem-airfoil flow prediction with paired single-airfoil counterparts, filling a clear gap where prior public datasets only covered single-body flows. The demonstrated 99.7% reduction in prediction wall-time relative to OpenFOAM simulations, combined with 65–94% MSE improvements over vanilla GNN baselines, makes data-driven tandem flow prediction practically viable for engineering workflows. Key limitations include the restriction to 2D airfoil geometries (no 3D or bluff-body validation), the reliance on steady-state incompressible RANS, and the fact that the multi-NN pipeline requires four separate networks, increasing deployment complexity. Open questions include whether the smooth-combining and freestream-residual ideas transfer to unsteady, compressible, or three-dimensional multi-body flows, and whether the curriculum approach generalises to more than two interacting bodies without architectural changes.
