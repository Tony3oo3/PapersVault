## Problem

Standard RLHF alignment optimizes toward a monolithic human preference, failing to capture the heterogeneity of individual user preferences that evolve dynamically during multi-turn dialogue. Because the user's true profile remains unobservable to the assistant at deployment, personalized alignment during interaction is fundamentally a partially observable decision-making problem that has not been formally addressed with appropriate RL machinery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261052/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The actor consumes only the observable dialogue history h_t (a sequence of user and assistant utterances) and produces a 64-dimensional continuous action vector, which is linearly projected back to the LLM's native hidden dimension and fed as an embedding to drive autoregressive text generation. The dual-critic module additionally receives the unobservable user profile ω_t during training to compute the full-state value V(h,ω), but this profile is withheld from the actor at both training and inference time, enforcing the partial-observability constraint.

## Method

The paper formalizes personalized multi-turn dialogue as a Persona Alignment MDP (PAMDP), where the user profile is the unobservable state variable, and derives the Bellman equation (Theorem 1) and a dual-critic advantage estimator (Theorem 2) that combines the observable-state value V(h) with the profile-conditional value V(h,ω). The key theoretical result (Theorem 3) proves this dual-critic advantage is an unbiased estimate of the standard TD advantage while still exploiting unobservable profile information during training, unlike the biased Asymmetric A2C formulation. The actor encodes dialogue history via a pre-trained LLM, projects hidden states to a 64-dim continuous action space, then projects back to the LLM's hidden dimension to condition autoregressive decoding. Training proceeds in two stages: behavior cloning to initialize the actor for coherent outputs, followed by A2C policy gradient optimization with KL regularization against the BC-initialized policy to prevent distributional drift.

## Results

On the offline evaluation (Table 2), the proposed dual-critic method achieves the highest win-rate (r_ω) across all model–dataset combinations: 0.439 on PrefEval with Qwen2.5-7B and 0.3776 on PrefEval with Llama3-8B, surpassing the strongest baseline (Behavior Cloning) by +0.143 and +0.051 respectively. On ALOE, the method reaches 0.1046 (Qwen) and 0.2671 (Llama), again outperforming all baselines. In the online user-simulator setting (Table 3), the method attains a cumulative return of 1.7389 over 6 interaction steps, exceeding UAAC (1.5784) by 0.1605 and DCRL (1.4305) by 0.3084, with the largest per-step reward gain (0.6173) at the final turn.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the offline baselines, the proposed method wins on every model–dataset pair. On PrefEval (the more challenging implicit-preference subset), it outperforms Behavior Cloning (the strongest SFT baseline) by +0.143 r_ω on Qwen2.5-7B (0.439 vs. 0.296) and +0.051 on Llama3-8B (0.3776 vs. 0.3265). CoT (SFT with explicit profile inference) and FPFT trail further behind at 0.1326/0.1531 and 0.1020/0.1429 respectively, while PEFT (LoRA-based personalization) is competitive only on Llama3-8B PrefEval (0.1939) and prompt-based methods consistently underperform. In the online POMDP comparison (Table 3), the method beats UAAC (Baisero & Amato, AAMAS 2022) by 0.1605 cumulative return and DCRL (Li et al., NeurIPS 2024) by 0.3084, with a notably larger first-step reward (+0.0819 over UAAC) indicating faster initial persona alignment. The ablation (Table 6) further confirms that the POMDP unobservable-state modeling contributes an additional +0.0311 r_ω over standard RL without profile conditioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the asymmetric actor-critic line of work for POMDPs (UAAC by Baisero & Amato, 2022; DCRL by Li et al., 2024a) by instantiating the unobservable state as a dynamically evolving user profile in multi-turn dialogue. It departs from the closest prior work on interaction-based personalization (Wu et al., 2024/2025), which relies on supervised fine-tuning over multi-turn preference data, by reframing the problem as a POMDP solved with reinforcement learning and a continuous latent-space action. The continuous action representation draws on recent work on latent-space reasoning (Hao et al., 2024) and dialogue action tokens (Li et al., 2024c), while the reward and evaluation protocol follows the LLM-as-judge paradigm common in RLHF literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PAMDP formulation provides a theoretically grounded (Bellman equation derivation, unbiasedness proof) framework for personalized dialogue that explicitly models the assistant's epistemic uncertainty about user preferences, offering a principled alternative to static-profile prompting or SFT-based alignment. However, the approach still requires behavior cloning initialization to constrain exploration, relies on an LLM-as-judge for reward signals (introducing circularity), and has only been validated on 7B–8B models with at most 6 interaction steps. Open questions include whether the BC warm-start can be eliminated, whether the framework scales to larger models or truly open-ended user populations, and how robust the learned policy is to distribution shift in user profiles.
