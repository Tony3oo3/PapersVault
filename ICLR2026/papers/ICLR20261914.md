## Problem

Neural combinatorial optimization has made significant progress on single-objective flexible job shop scheduling (FJSP), but multi-objective FJSPs (MOFJSPs) remain largely unexplored. Existing multi-objective NCO methods for routing problems rely on episodic rewards and simple static states that do not transfer to scheduling's long decision horizons, dynamic graph structures, and stepwise reward requirements, leaving a gap in learning-based approaches for practical multi-criteria scheduling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261914/cropped_page17_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an FJSP instance represented as a heterogeneous graph (operation nodes, machine nodes, and operation-machine pair features including processing times, lower bounds, and workload metrics) together with a preference vector λ ∈ ℝ^M specifying objective weights. At inference, the policy is queried N times with different preference vectors to produce a Pareto set of complete schedules, where each schedule assigns a compatible machine to every operation and determines the sequencing on each machine. The number of subproblems N (e.g., 101 for 2-objective, 105 for 3-objective) controls the trade-off between Pareto set coverage and runtime.

## Method

The paper proposes DCAN (Dual Conditional Attention Network), which extends the Dual Attention Network (DAN) by injecting artificial preference-embedding nodes into both the operation and machine attention blocks, so that attention coefficients are modulated by the objective preference vector at every layer. Training uses a decomposition-based PPO algorithm: the MOFJSP is split into subproblems via weighted-sum scalarization, and a single policy π_θ(s, λ) is trained across randomly sampled preference vectors from a Dirichlet distribution, with per-objective stepwise rewards derived from recursively updated lower (or upper) bounds on completion times. A multi-objective critic outputs one value estimate per objective, and the actor loss uses the preference-weighted sum of per-objective GAE advantages. This design allows one network to adapt its scheduling behavior to any preference vector at inference without retraining, unlike hypernetwork or multi-actor approaches.

## Results

On synthetic 20×10 instances, DCAN (sampling) achieves HV gaps of ~4–7% to CP-SAT across the three objective combinations, roughly 50% better than MOEA/D and substantially outperforming NSGA-II. On public benchmark instances (mk, rdata, edata, vdata), DCAN sampling achieves HV gaps of 6.69%–40.07% with runtimes of ~44–48 s, while NSGA-II achieves slightly lower gaps (9.20%–32.82%) but requires 1100–2200 s (≈40–50× longer). For generalization to unseen 30×10 and 40×10 instances, DCAN retains its advantage over metaheuristics by an even wider margin, with the gap to CP-SAT narrowing as instance size grows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NSGA-II, DCAN (sampling) wins on vdata (6.69% vs 9.20% HV gap) and is competitive on rdata (12.73% vs 9.73%) and edata (20.30% vs 9.79%), but loses on mk (40.07% vs 32.82%); however, NSGA-II requires 40–50× more runtime (1100–2200 s vs ~45 s). Against MOEA/D, DCAN consistently wins across all four benchmark datasets by 5–14 percentage points in HV gap while being 25–50× faster. The hypernetwork baseline (following Lin et al. 2022 / Su et al. 2024) is outperformed by both WI-DAN and DCAN on all synthetic and benchmark instances, confirming that a single conditional network is more effective than separate per-preference actors. On synthetic training-size instances (Table 1), DCAN sampling outperforms both metaheuristics by roughly 50% in HV gap at 20×10 scale, and the advantage widens further on unseen 30×10 and 40×10 instances where metaheuristics deteriorate sharply. CP-SAT remains the strongest reference on small instances but becomes impractical at scale (101–105 min per instance), whereas DCAN inference takes under 50 s.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Dual Attention Network (DAN) architecture of Wang et al. (2023) for single-objective FJSP by introducing a conditional attention mechanism (DCAN) that adapts operation-machine attention based on objective preference vectors, enabling a single network to solve multiple decomposed subproblems. It departs from prior NMOCO work on routing problems (Lin et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2024), which rely on episodic REINFORCE rewards and simple coordinate-based states, by adopting stepwise bound-based rewards and richer dynamic states appropriate for the long-horizon, graph-structured nature of scheduling. The decomposition-based PPO training framework generalizes the weighted-sum scalarization of MOEA/D into a learned-policy setting, while the bound-based reward design extends the makespan-specific quality measure of Wang et al. (2023) to tardiness, flowtime, earliness, and costs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first NCO method that uses a single neural network to solve general MOFJSPs with arbitrary objective combinations and any number of objectives, removing the need for separate actor networks per preference vector. The practical impact is significant: inference takes seconds versus minutes-to-hours for metaheuristics, and policies generalize to larger instances where NSGA-II and MOEA/D deteriorate. Open questions include handling non-regular objectives (earliness) more effectively, developing advanced sampling strategies to better exploit learned policies at test time, and extending the conditional attention and bound-based reward ideas to other combinatorial optimization domains beyond scheduling.
