## Problem

Existing Text-to-CAD methods decode natural language directly into executable bpy code in a single flat pass, lacking any explicit representation of assembly hierarchy or geometric constraints. This forces the decoder to navigate a vast search space where minor early errors propagate through the operation sequence, causing cascading failures on complex multi-part assemblies. The paper addresses this gap by learning an intermediate hierarchical, geometry-aware graph that makes both structural decomposition and spatial constraints explicit before code generation.

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form natural language instruction describing a target CAD object and produces a three-stage output: a geometric decomposition graph serialized as structured text (multi-level nodes for parts and components, edges encoding geometric constraints such as contact, alignment, and relative orientation), a sequential CAD action plan derived from an optimal graph traversal, and finally executable bpy code that renders a 3D model in Blender. The accompanying BlendGeo dataset contains 12K quadruplets spanning 1.4K object categories, with each sample pairing the instruction, graph, action sequence, and bpy code, annotated via a collaborative human-AI pipeline involving VLM validation and industrial designer correction.

## Method

Graph-CAD employs a three-stage LLM pipeline where each stage is a dedicated Qwen3-8B model fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 64): Stage 1 predicts the geometric decomposition graph via top-down recursive disassembly into atomic components, Stage 2 plans the CAD operation sequence by determining an optimal graph traversal order that respects constraint dependencies, and Stage 3 translates the planned operations into executable bpy code. The core insight is that the graph serves as a structural prior that prunes the search space, decoupling global assembly coherence from local geometric constraint satisfaction and preventing error accumulation across the long generation horizon. To handle increasing object complexity, the authors introduce SAPCL (Structure-Aware Progressive Curriculum Learning), an iterative mechanism that synthesizes three-level difficulty variants (attribute edits, local structural changes, categorical transitions) from seed examples, identifies the model's capability boundary via a multimodal discriminator, and generates validated training instances at that boundary for the next supervised fine-tuning round. The full training consists of four SAPCL iterations, each combining a data synthesis phase (1% seed sampling, 20 variants per seed) with a 7-epoch LoRA fine-tuning phase on two A800-80GB GPUs.

## Results

On CADBench-Sim, Graph-CAD (SAPCL) achieves the best overall average score of 0.6883 (Attr. 0.7681, Spat. 0.7423, Inst. 0.5546), with a syntax error rate of only 2.0% and a Geometric Constraint Satisfaction (GCS) score of 0.9018. On the out-of-distribution CADBench-Wild split, it reaches 0.7114 average with GCS of 0.8943 and 2.5% syntax errors. The most dramatic improvement over the strongest baseline (Claude-opus-4-1) is in GCS: 0.9018 vs. 0.4932 on Sim and 0.8943 vs. 0.5062 on Wild, representing roughly an 83% relative gain in constraint satisfaction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Graph-CAD (SAPCL) outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric across both CADBench splits. Against the strongest general-purpose LLM baseline, Claude-opus-4-1 (end-to-end), it improves the average VLM score from 0.6662 to 0.6883 on Sim and from 0.6687 to 0.7114 on Wild, while reducing the syntax error rate from 7.4% to 2.0% (Sim) and 14.5% to 2.5% (Wild). The largest margin is in GCS: 0.9018 vs. 0.4932 (Sim) and 0.8943 vs. 0.5062 (Wild), an absolute gain of ~0.40 points. Compared to GPT-5 (end-to-end), the gains are similar in GCS (0.9018 vs. 0.3846 on Sim) and syntax errors (2.0% vs. 2.8%). Against the dedicated Text-to-CAD baselines (BlenderLLM, Text2CAD, CADFusion), Graph-CAD (SAPCL) leads by a wide margin on all metrics, with the gap most pronounced on GCS (0.9018 vs. 0.5513 for BlenderLLM) and instruction-following accuracy. The SAPCL curriculum itself adds a further 7.03% relative improvement in average score and 10.82% in GCS over the SFT-only variant, confirming that progressive difficulty scheduling is critical for complex assemblies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant end-to-end text-to-CAD paradigm (BlenderLLM, Text2CAD, CADFusion) that decodes natural language directly into bpy code, arguing that the flat decoding strategy lacks explicit modeling of assembly hierarchy and geometric constraints. It also distinguishes itself from planner-level augmentations such as chain-of-thought (Guan et al., 2025) and visual/execution feedback loops, which the authors characterize as insufficient for complex multi-part assemblies. The learned hierarchical, geometry-aware graph draws conceptual inspiration from assembly graphs used in predictive CAD tasks (Bian et al., 2022, 2024), but repurposes the graph as a *prescriptive* generative blueprint predicted from text rather than a *descriptive* structure extracted from an existing CAD file. The structure-aware progressive curriculum (SAPCL) adapts classical curriculum learning (Bengio et al., 2009) with a novel difficulty-grading scheme tied to structural edits of the decomposition graph.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a real and underexplored failure mode in Text-to-CAD: cascading geometric constraint violations in multi-part assemblies, and introduces the GCS metric to quantify this dimension, which prior benchmarks lacked. The BlendGeo dataset (12K quadruplets) and the NLA/HLA graph-fidelity metrics provide reusable infrastructure for the community. However, the approach carries a ~1.7-minute inference cost, struggles with extremely complex organic or mechanical geometries (e.g., ant queen, bicycle), and relies on GPT-5 for both annotation and evaluation, raising questions about circularity and scalability. Open questions include whether the graph intermediate representation generalizes beyond bpy/Blender to other CAD kernels, whether the SAPCL mechanism transfers to other structured-generation tasks, and how the framework scales as base models and datasets grow in complexity.
