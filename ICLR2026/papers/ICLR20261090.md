## Problem

MAP inference in credal networks—finding the most probable variable assignment given evidence when local models are convex sets of distributions rather than single CPTs—has received little algorithmic attention compared to Bayesian network MAP. Existing credal MAP methods either scale poorly to large models or provide no guarantees on solution quality, and no established exact algorithmic framework existed for the general credal MAP task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261090/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a credal network specified by a DAG, variable domains, and separately specified conditional credal sets (each given by its finite set of extreme points/vertices) along with optional evidence on a subset of variables. It produces the optimal assignment to the remaining variables that maximizes either the upper probability (maximax MAP) or the lower probability (maximin MAP) over the joint credal set, along with the corresponding probability value. The joint credal set is the strong extension (convex hull of all joint distributions satisfying the Markov property), and the algorithm guarantees optimality of the returned assignment.

## Method

The paper introduces a depth-first AND/OR branch-and-bound (AOBB) algorithm that explores a search space defined relative to a pseudo tree of the credal network's moralized graph, so the search space size is bounded exponentially by the pseudo tree depth rather than the number of variables. Edge weights in the AND/OR tree are computed from extreme points of the conditional credal sets (upper probabilities for maximax, lower for maximin), and OR nodes maximize while AND nodes multiply. The search is guided by a mini-bucket heuristic that partitions buckets into subsets of at most i variables, approximates intermediate potentials using the Pareto Least Upper Bound (PLUB) to cap their cardinality at M, and applies a moment-matching cost-shifting re-parameterization to tighten the resulting upper bounds. For maximax MAP the max-marginal and max-pruning operators commute, yielding exact bounds; for maximin MAP the non-commutativity of max and min means the heuristic provides only (looser) upper bounds, making that task substantially harder in practice.

## Results

On real-world credal networks, AOBB+MBMM(i,1) is the only algorithm that scales to the 3692-variable mastermind3 instance, solving it in ~3037–3265 s (i=6–10) while all OR-search baselines exceed the 1-hour time limit. On the 724-variable link network, AOBB+MBMM(i,1) completes in 2.58–9.77 s versus complete failure of BB+MB and BB+MBMM at all i-bounds. On 100-variable random networks, AOBB+MBMM(i,1) achieves 0.15–2.25 s average CPU time (i=2–10), improving over the OR-search counterpart BB+MBMM(i,1) by up to 5 orders of magnitude (e.g., 0.39 s vs. 154.77 s at i=4). Against local search methods (SLS, TS, SA, GLS) from Marinescu et al. (2023), the exact algorithm dominates on all sizes, e.g., 0.10 s vs. 189–373 s on 100-variable random networks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the OR-search branch-and-bound (BB+MBMM(i,1)) that uses the same mini-bucket heuristics but does not exploit the AND/OR problem decomposition. AOBB+MBMM(i,1) outperforms it by up to 5 orders of magnitude on random and grid networks (e.g., 0.39 s vs. 154.77 s on 100-var random at i=4; 0.06 s vs. 362.59 s on 100-node grid at i=6) and is the only method that solves the 3692-variable mastermind3 instance within the 1-hour limit. Against the local search algorithms (SLS, TS, SA, GLS) adapted from Marinescu et al. (2023), the exact AOBB+MBMM(i,1) dominates across all problem sizes: on 100-variable random networks it takes 0.10 s versus 189–373 s for the local search methods, and on 196-node grids it takes 0.14 s versus 313–572 s. The local search methods also provide no optimality guarantee, whereas AOBB+MBMM(i,1) certifies the optimal solution. For maximin MAP (Table 4), the same AND/OR advantage holds but the absolute times are substantially larger due to weaker mini-bucket bounds, with AOBB+MBMM(i,1) still being the only algorithm to solve mastermind3 (1049–1206 s at i=6–12).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AND/OR branch-and-bound search framework developed for Bayesian MAP (Marinescu & Dechter, 2009) to the credal network setting, where local models are convex sets of distributions rather than single CPTs. The mini-bucket heuristic is adapted from Dechter & Rish (2003) and enhanced with moment-matching cost-shifting (Ihler et al., 2012), but requires a novel potential-based formulation since credal MAP operates over sets of probability functions rather than single functions. The work also builds on Credal Variable Elimination (Mauá & Cozman, 2020) for the exact bucket-elimination baseline, and positions itself against the approximate local search methods for credal Marginal MAP from Marinescu et al. (2023), which it shows to be dominated by the exact approach even on small instances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first exact algorithmic framework for MAP inference in general credal networks, filling a gap where only approximate or small-scale methods existed. The practical impact is significant for applications involving imprecise or conflicting probability estimates (causal discovery with latent variables, robust decision-making), as it scales to 3000+ variables with optimality guarantees. A key open question is the fundamental difficulty of maximin MAP: the non-commutativity of max and min operators makes the mini-bucket bounds substantially looser, and the authors acknowledge that tighter approximations or alternative search strategies (best-first, hybrids) are needed. The gap between maximax and maximin MAP performance suggests the latter may require fundamentally different algorithmic treatment.
