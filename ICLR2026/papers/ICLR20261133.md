## Problem

Training deep neural networks is prohibitively expensive in data, time, and energy, and existing dynamic data-pruning methods (e.g., InfoBatch) rely on per-sample loss statistics, gradient rescaling, or complex heuristics that add computational overhead. The paper asks whether the stability of internal activation representations across epochs can serve as a lightweight, model-internal signal to identify and permanently remove low-utility training batches without auxiliary networks or handcrafted difficulty metrics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes fixed training batches (32×32 images for CIFAR-10/100/SVHN, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K, or text sequences for GPT-2 fine-tuning) and, critically, the standard deviation of ReLU-activated (or GELU-activated for CvT) outputs at each convolutional/transformer stage, which are already computed during the forward pass. Batches are fixed at initialization with intra-batch shuffling to preserve stochasticity, and the output is a permanently reduced training set whose size decreases monotonically across epochs, reported alongside a system-independent Data Savings Index (DSI) and GPU node-hour savings. Unlike temporary pruning methods that revisit all data each epoch, B-PAS permanently discards pruned batches, so the data loader is rebuilt at each epoch boundary.

## Method

B-PAS is a plug-and-play module that, at the end of each epoch, computes the mean standard deviation of flattened post-ReLU activations across all convolutional (or transformer) layers for every batch, then compares this value against the previous epoch's value. If the absolute change ΔX̄ falls below a dynamic exponential threshold δ(i) = δ_s · e^(αi), the batch is deemed converged and permanently removed from subsequent training. The key insight, grounded in Neural Collapse, is that activation variance stabilizes as a batch's contribution to weight updates saturates, making this change a reliable proxy for diminishing learning utility. Because the signal is extracted from forward-pass statistics already available in the network, the method introduces negligible overhead and requires no auxiliary models, loss tracking, or gradient rescaling. Pruning operates at the batch level (not sample level) to preserve class diversity, and the same mechanism generalizes from CNNs to vision transformers (CvT-13) and autoregressive LLMs (GPT-2 large) by tracking stage-wise or block-wise activation statistics.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with ResNet-50, B-PAS achieves 78.07% top-1 accuracy (matching the full-dataset baseline) while saving 57% of training data (DSI) and 61% of GPU node-hours, substantially outperforming InfoBatch (28% DSI, 40% node-hours saved). With a more conservative threshold (δ∈[5×10⁻⁶, 5×10⁻⁵]), B-PAS improves accuracy to 78.43% while still saving 47% of data and 48% of node-hours. On CIFAR-10/100, B-PAS preserves full-dataset accuracy (95.60%/80.60%) with 25–33% data savings across ResNet-18 and ResNet-50. Extended to GPT-2 large fine-tuning on Alpaca, B-PAS prunes 23% of batches with unchanged loss (0.2201 vs 0.2207) and perplexity (1.25).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary SOTA baseline InfoBatch (Qin et al., 2024), B-PAS delivers substantially greater efficiency on ImageNet-1K: 57% DSI vs. 28% DSI and 61% node-hours saved vs. 40%, while matching the full-dataset accuracy of 78.07%; with conservative thresholds B-PAS even exceeds baseline accuracy (78.43%). On CIFAR-10/100, B-PAS matches InfoBatch at the 30% pruning ratio (95.60%/80.60% accuracy, ~25–30% DSI) but does not show the same large margin as on ImageNet, reflecting the smaller learning utility in low-resolution tasks. A random-pruning control (pruning the same number of batches chosen at random) consistently degrades accuracy (e.g., 94.60% vs. 95.60% on CIFAR-10), confirming that activation stability identifies genuinely non-informative batches rather than simply reducing data volume. In the extended comparison (Table 24), B-PAS matches InfoBatch's best-reported accuracy on both CIFAR-10 (95.6%) and CIFAR-100 (78.2%), outperforming all 17 prior static and dynamic pruning methods including Herding (92.2%), EL2N-20 (95.3%), UCB (95.3%), and DP (94.9%) on CIFAR-10. Against the large-scale pruning method of He et al. (2024), which achieves 25% lossless pruning on ImageNet-1K, B-PAS removes up to 57% of data with 61% GPU-hour savings, representing roughly 29% higher data savings and 21% greater compute savings.

## Relation to Prior Work

B-PAS extends the activation-stability observation from Neural Collapse (Papyan et al., 2020) and the CNN-specific early-stopping criterion of Ahmad et al. (2024) from a diagnostic signal into a dynamic, batch-level data pruning mechanism. It departs from the dominant dynamic-pruning paradigm exemplified by InfoBatch (Qin et al., 2024), which relies on per-sample loss tracking and gradient rescaling to maintain unbiased updates, by instead using the mean standard deviation of post-ReLU activations—a quantity already available in the forward pass—requiring no auxiliary computation, no loss statistics, and no rescaling. The method also contrasts with static pruning (GraNd, EL2N, Forgetting) that scores samples before training, and with uncertainty-driven pruning (He et al., 2024), by operating entirely on internal model dynamics at the batch granularity rather than per-sample difficulty or prediction confidence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: 57% data savings and 61% GPU node-hour reduction on ImageNet-1K with no accuracy loss, outperforming the prior SOTA (InfoBatch) by 29% in data savings, all from a signal that adds negligible overhead to the training loop. The plug-and-play design and the demonstration on GPT-2 fine-tuning suggest broad applicability, though the method's effectiveness is tightly coupled to Batch Normalization (without it, DSI drops from 25% to 2%) and to empirically tuned threshold schedules, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation. Open questions include developing data-driven adaptive thresholding, understanding why transformers exhibit slower and noisier activation stabilization (limiting pruning to ~14–35% DSI), and whether the batch-level granularity advantage over sample-level pruning (78.43% vs. 70.87% accuracy) holds in more extreme pruning regimes.
