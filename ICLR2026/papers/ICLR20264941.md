## Problem

Neural CDEs control expressiveness through the number of function evaluations (NFEs), but the standard lever—lowering solver tolerances—increases numerical precision without proportionally improving representational capacity, and demands larger weight norms that destabilize training. Scaling the integration time horizon is a more effective way to increase NFEs, yet it destabilizes trajectories, and existing negative-feedback fixes (e.g., GRU-ODE) introduce 'forgetfulness' that erases long-term memory. The paper asks how to deepen Neural CDEs via time scaling while preserving both stability and the ability to retain information across the full sequence.

## Input / Output

The model consumes irregularly sampled multivariate time series as (t_k, x_k) pairs, interpolates them with natural cubic splines over a scaled time interval [0, T·D/M], and integrates a GRU-based vector field to produce a single sequence-level hidden state h(T). A linear head maps h(T) to a scalar or vector prediction (binary/multiclass label or regression target). Because the integration is sequential over the scaled interval, the method is inherently non-parallelizable, trading wall-clock time for expressive depth.

## Method

DeNOTS combines two key modifications to standard Neural CDEs. First, Scaled Neural CDE (SNCDE) rescales timestamps by a factor D/M, stretching the integration horizon to increase NFEs and expressiveness while provably reducing required l2 weight norms (Theorem 3.1). Second, an Anti-NF mechanism passes −h (rather than h) into the GRU cell, so the update gate z and the negative-feedback term operate in anti-phase: z adaptively controls the relative strength of feedback versus update, unlike GRU-ODE's synchronous subtraction which rigidly couples them and causes forgetting. Under Assumptions 4.1–4.2 (aL_h < b), the resulting ODE is input-to-state stable (Theorem 4.4), its discretization error does not accumulate with sequence length (Theorem 4.5, bounded by (a/(b−aL_h))²·ξ²), and the anti-phase design preserves long-term memory (Theorem 4.8). A Gaussian-process analysis of cubic-spline interpolation yields a tight O(δ⁴) interval error bound (Lemma 4.6), completing the robustness picture.

## Results

DeNOTS achieves rank 1 (best) on all four main benchmarks—UWGL, InsectSound, Pendulum, and Sepsis—with a mean rank of 1.0, outperforming all baselines including Neural RDEs and state space models by up to 20%. On the SineMix long-term memory task, Anti-NF achieves R²=1.0 while Sync-NF (GRU-ODE) drops to R²=0.3, confirming the forgetting issue. Under robustness attacks on Pendulum, DeNOTS (Anti-NF) achieves R²=0.64 on change attacks and R²=0.61 on drop attacks, substantially outperforming Neural CDE (R²=-0.97 and 0.02 respectively) and Mamba (R²=-0.46 and 0.36).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeNOTS (Anti-NF SNCDE) ranks first on all four benchmarks, beating every baseline. Against Neural CDE (Kidger et al., 2020), DeNOTS wins on InsectSound (rank 1 vs 8) and Sepsis (rank 1 vs 5), while Neural CDE is competitive on UWGL and Pendulum. Against Neural RDE (Morrill et al., 2021), DeNOTS dominates on Sepsis (rank 1 vs 9) and InsectSound (rank 1 vs 6), with Neural RDE only competitive on Pendulum. Against Mamba (Gu & Dao, 2023), DeNOTS wins on all four datasets, with the largest gap on Sepsis (rank 1 vs 8). Against GRU-ODE (De Brouwer et al., 2019), which is the closest architectural relative, DeNOTS wins on all four datasets (mean rank 1.0 vs 6.0), with the SineMix experiment showing the critical difference: Anti-NF achieves R²=1.0 while GRU-ODE's synchronous NF forgets and scores only R²=0.3. Against RoFormer (Su et al., 2024), DeNOTS wins on all four datasets, with the largest margin on Pendulum (rank 1 vs 6).

## Relation to Prior Work

DeNOTS extends the Neural CDE framework (Kidger et al., 2020) by reinterpreting the number of function evaluations (NFE) as a depth analogue and proposing time-horizon scaling as the mechanism to increase it, rather than the conventional approach of tightening solver tolerances. The Anti-NF mechanism is a targeted modification of GRU-ODE-Bayes (De Brouwer et al., 2019): where GRU-ODE subtracts the hidden state from the GRU output (synchronous feedback), DeNOTS passes −h into the GRU itself (anti-phase feedback), decoupling the update and feedback gates. The theoretical analysis draws on control-systems input-to-state stability (Sontag & Wang, 1995) and Gaussian process interpolation theory (Golubev & Krymova, 2013) to provide guarantees that prior Neural CDE work lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, theoretically grounded recipe for making Neural CDEs more expressive without destabilizing training: scale time, add anti-phase negative feedback, and you get provable input-to-state stability, non-accumulating discretization error (O(1) in sequence length), and preserved long-term memory. This is practically relevant for irregular time series where existing methods (Neural RDEs, state-space models like Mamba) struggle with both expressiveness and robustness. Key limitations include the inherent sequentiality of ODE integration (amplified by time scaling), and the fact that the theoretical results assume scalar a, b parameters; the vector-valued extension is left as technical complexity. Open questions include whether the time-scaling idea transfers to other sequence domains (event sequences, text, images) and how to parallelize the deeper sequential computation.
