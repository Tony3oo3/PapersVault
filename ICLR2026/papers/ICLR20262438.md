## Problem

Existing compression methods for large multimodal models (LMMs) treat low-rank decomposition and quantization independently, compounding reconstruction errors—especially in multimodal architectures with cross-modal redundancy. While low-rank plus quantization has proven effective for text-only LLMs, the spatial domain is suboptimal for compressing the weight matrices of vision-language models, leaving their multimodal stack 'uncomfortably obese' even after standard recipes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262438/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images (336×336 for 7B/3B/2B variants, 224×224 for 1B) producing 576 visual tokens (256 for 1B), alongside text prompts up to 2048 tokens, and generates autoregressive text responses. A distinctive aspect is that all weight matrices are internally represented in the complex frequency domain via 2D-DFT, where conjugate symmetry allows storing only half the coefficients, and the compressed parameters are stored as a low-rank complex factor pair plus a PolarQuant-encoded residual at 4-bit amplitude and 4-bit phase.

## Method

LLaVA-FA converts each pretrained real weight matrix into the complex frequency domain via 2D-DFT, then jointly decomposes it into a low-rank component (obtained by truncating the top-r singular values of a FourierSVD) and a quantized residual (encoded by PolarQuant, which separately discretizes amplitude and phase in polar coordinates). The conjugate symmetry of the DFT halves the storage footprint, while the de-correlation property of the frequency domain yields a more compact singular-value spectrum, provably reducing the Frobenius truncation error at the same rank compared to the spatial domain. An optional Diagonal Calibration (ODC) scheme approximates the full Hessian-weighted objective with row/column-mean diagonal matrices, eliminating the need for large-scale calibration data. The entire decomposition is solved via an alternating optimization loop (Algorithm 1) that iterates between FourierSVD-based low-rank fitting and PolarQuant residual encoding until the reconstruction error stops decreasing.

## Results

LLaVA-FA-2B (compressed from Qwen-2.5-7B) achieves a 60.9% average across seven comprehension benchmarks (GQA, VizWiz, ScienceQA, TextVQA, MME, MMBench, MMBench-CN) using only 5M training samples, surpassing MoE-LLaVA-2B (55.3%) by 5.6% and Mini-Gemini-2B (57.1%) by 3.8%. On hallucination benchmarks, LLaVA-FA-2B attains the lowest response-level hallucination rate (11.2%) and highest POPE F1 (87.5%) among all 2B-scale models, outperforming MiniCPM-V-2 by 3.3% on Object HalBench. The 7B variant (from InternLM-2-20B) reaches 70.4% average, exceeding LLaVA-NeXT (68.5%) by 1.9%. Inference efficiency is also improved: LLaVA-FA-2B requires only 1.93T FLOPs and 30.6 ms latency versus 2.14T/36.2 ms for Imp-2B, an 83.3% reduction in KV cache usage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against same-scale 2B competitors, LLaVA-FA-2B wins decisively: it outperforms MoE-LLaVA-2B by 5.6% average (60.9 vs. 55.3), Mini-Gemini-2B by 3.8% (60.9 vs. 57.1), and Imp-2B by 2.0% (60.9 vs. 58.9), while using fewer training samples (5M vs. 1.6–2.7M). On hallucination, LLaVA-FA-2B achieves 11.2% response-level and 7.7% mention-level rates on Object HalBench, beating MiniCPM-V-2 (14.5%/7.8%) by 3.3% and Mini-Gemini-2B (29.7%/21.1%) by 18.5% on the response metric; it also surpasses 7B models like VCD (48.8%) and POVID (48.1%) by over 37%. At the 3B scale, LLaVA-FA-3B (68.3% avg) exceeds MiniCPM-V-2 (66.9%) by 1.4% despite using 5M samples versus 570M. At 7B scale, LLaVA-FA-7B (70.4%) surpasses LLaVA-NeXT (68.5%) by 1.9% and LLaVA-1.5-7B (62.1%) by 8.3%. The 1B variant (56.7%) beats SPHINX-Tiny (49.2%) by 7.5% while using 3× less training data (5M vs. 15M). In FLOPs and latency, LLaVA-FA-2B (1.93T, 30.6 ms) is the most efficient among all 2B-scale models tested, reducing latency by 61.1% relative to Imp-2B.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the joint low-rank plus quantization decomposition paradigm (Saha et al., 2024; LQ-LoRA; LQER) from the spatial domain to the frequency domain, arguing that Fourier de-correlation yields a more compact singular-value spectrum and that conjugate symmetry halves the storage footprint. It departs from prior Fourier-in-deep-learning work (BiHPF, FreqNet, FreqTS), which used frequency features for detection or token selection, by applying 2D-DFT directly to weight matrices for compression. The PolarQuant codec and the ODC diagonal-calibration scheme are novel components not present in prior low-rank-plus-quantization methods, which typically rely on full Hessian calibration or treat rank selection and quantization independently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deploying LMMs on edge or resource-constrained hardware: a 2B model compressed from a 7B backbone achieves competitive or superior scores on comprehension and hallucination benchmarks while cutting FLOPs and KV-cache usage by over 60%. The ODC scheme's elimination of large-scale calibration data lowers the barrier to adoption, and the model-family-agnostic pipeline (validated on Qwen-2.5, LLaMA-3, and InternLM-2) suggests broad applicability. Open questions include whether the weakly-stationary random-field assumption holds for all weight matrices (e.g., embedding layers, position encodings), whether the approach extends to video or audio modalities, and whether the 3.2% DFT/IDFT overhead remains negligible at much larger sequence lengths or on non-GPU hardware.
