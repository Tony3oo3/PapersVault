## Problem

GNNs exhibit systematic underconfidence, yet existing graph-aware calibration methods (GATS, CaGCN, GETS) rely on coarse one-hop neighbor statistics or opaque latent embeddings that fail to capture multi-hop structural heterogeneity. This leads to unreliable calibration, particularly in sparse or low-homophily regions where local neighborhoods carry weak or misleading signals. The paper asks whether multi-scale structural features can be leveraged in a lightweight, post-hoc manner to assign node-specific temperatures that correct confidence errors across diverse graph topologies.

## Input / Output

WATS consumes the frozen logits from any pre-trained GNN, the graph adjacency matrix, and the log-degree vector as the initial wavelet signal. It produces a per-node positive temperature (via Softplus) that rescales the original logits, yielding calibrated probabilities. The wavelet features have dimension K+1 per node (where K is the Chebyshev order), and the entire pipeline requires no access to neighboring logits, no model retraining, and no labeled test data—only a validation split for fitting the temperature MLP.

## Method

WATS constructs node-specific structural signatures via a graph wavelet transform: the heat-kernel scaling function g(sλ)=e^{-sλ} is approximated by a K-order Chebyshev polynomial series applied to the rescaled normalized Laplacian, with log-degree as the initial signal to compress skewed degree distributions while preserving connectivity ordering. The resulting wavelet feature matrix is row-wise L1-normalized, and a two-layer MLP maps each node's (K+1)-dimensional wavelet vector to a positive temperature. Calibrated logits are obtained by dividing the original GNN logits by the predicted temperature, and the MLP is trained on the validation set via cross-entropy. The key distinction from prior graph-aware calibrators is that the Chebyshev order k and diffusion scale s jointly control the receptive field and smoothing extent, enabling adaptive capture of multi-hop structural context without explicit neighbor aggregation or attention over local neighborhoods.

## Results

WATS achieves the lowest ECE in 24 out of 27 dataset–architecture configurations across nine benchmarks and three GNN backbones (GCN, GAT, GCNII). Headline improvements include ECE of 0.54 on Reddit (GAT) vs. 3.29 for global TS, 1.12 on Pubmed (GCN) vs. 2.55 for TS, and 1.82 on Cora (GCN) vs. 2.98 for GATS. The paper reports up to 41.2% relative ECE reduction over the best baseline and a 15.84% average reduction in calibration variance compared to graph-specific methods. WATS also avoids the out-of-memory failures that GATS suffers on the large Reddit graph (232K nodes, 115M edges).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WATS outperforms all five baselines (TS, ETS, CaGCN, GATS, GETS) in 24 of 27 configurations, losing only to GETS on Citeseer-GCNII (6.68 vs. 7.27) and Computers-GCNII (2.89 vs. 3.89), and to GATS on Computers-GAT (2.01 vs. 2.17). Against the simplest baseline, global TS, WATS reduces ECE by up to 84% (Reddit-GAT: 0.54 vs. 3.29) and by 56% on Pubmed-GCN (1.12 vs. 2.55). Compared to the graph-specific GATS, WATS avoids out-of-memory failures on Reddit (232K nodes) while still achieving lower ECE on every other dataset where GATS runs. Against CaGCN, WATS wins on 22/27 configurations, with the largest gap on Reddit-GCN (0.90 vs. 1.45) and Cora-full-GAT (1.11 vs. 4.79). Against GETS, WATS wins on 24/27 configurations, with particularly large margins on Cora-full-GCN (1.94 vs. 3.11) and Reddit-GCNII (0.88 vs. 2.99). WATS also exhibits lower standard deviations across 10 runs in most settings, indicating more stable calibration than the ensemble-based GETS and attention-based GATS.

## Relation to Prior Work

WATS builds on the graph wavelet transform framework of Hammond et al. (2011) and Xu et al. (2019), which use Chebyshev polynomial approximations of the heat-kernel operator to avoid costly eigendecomposition. However, it departs from prior graph-wavelet applications (Donnat et al., 2018; Behmanesh et al., 2022) that use wavelets to reconstruct or smooth node features for classification; instead, WATS repurposes wavelet coefficients as structural signatures for predicting node-specific calibration temperatures. Conceptually, it extends the post-hoc temperature-scaling paradigm (Guo et al., 2017) into the graph domain by replacing the global scalar temperature with a node-wise temperature regressed from multi-scale topological features, moving beyond the one-hop neighborhood statistics relied upon by GATS, CaGCN, and GETS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: GNNs are systematically underconfident, and existing graph-aware calibration methods capture only shallow local structure, leaving multi-hop miscalibration uncorrected. WATS offers a lightweight, architecture-agnostic, post-hoc solution that scales to large graphs (where attention-based methods like GATS fail with OOM), making it broadly deployable. Limitations include confinement to node classification and an assumption that topological signals correlate with model logits; the authors identify incorporating global structural context (structurally similar but spatially distant nodes) and extending to edge prediction and dynamic graphs as key open directions.
