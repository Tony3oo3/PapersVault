## Problem

Predicting kernel regression learning curves requires knowing the kernel's eigenvalues and eigenfunctions with respect to the data distribution, but for real high-dimensional datasets this eigenstructure is intractable to compute without constructing and diagonalizing a full kernel matrix. The paper asks whether a simple analytical form for this eigenstructure exists that depends only on low-order data statistics, enabling end-to-end prediction of test risk from raw data without any kernel matrix operations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264019/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the empirical data covariance matrix Σ (a d×d symmetric matrix) and a finite set of target-function coefficients estimated by Gram-Schmidt orthogonalizing sampled multi-Hermite polynomials against the label vector—no kernel matrix is ever formed or diagonalized. The output is a closed-form eigensystem (eigenvalues as monomials in the covariance eigenvalues scaled by kernel level coefficients, eigenfunctions as multivariate Hermite polynomials) which, when plugged into the KRR eigenframework, yields predicted learning curves, sample complexity constants, and the full kernel spectrum. The target decomposition is kernel-independent, so a single measurement of the Hermite coefficients can be reused across different kernels and ridge parameters.

## Method

The core contribution is the Hermite Eigenstructure Ansatz (HEA): for rotation-invariant kernels on anisotropic data with covariance Σ = UΓUᵀ, the kernel eigenvalues are λ_α = c_|α| · ∏ᵢ γᵢ^αᵢ and the eigenfunctions are the corresponding multivariate Hermite polynomials h_α^(Σ), where (c_ℓ) are the on-sphere level coefficients of the kernel. This is proven for Gaussian data in two limits (wide Gaussian kernel via Mehler's formula; fast-decaying dot-product kernels via perturbation theory) and empirically validated on CIFAR-5m, SVHN, and ImageNet-32, where real image data is 'Gaussian enough' for the ansatz to hold. The target function's Hermite coefficients are recovered from finite samples using a Gram-Schmidt process that corrects for non-orthogonality induced by data non-Gaussianity, then fed into the KRR eigenframework (Sollich/Bordelon/Simon) to produce learning curve predictions. The paper further shows that feature-learning MLPs learn these same Hermite polynomials in the order predicted by the HEA eigenvalues, linking the kernel theory to finite-width network dynamics.

## Results

The Hermite Eigenstructure Ansatz (HEA) accurately predicts both kernel eigenvalues and eigenfunctions for four kernel/dataset combinations (Gaussian kernel on Gaussian data and CIFAR-5m, Laplace kernel on SVHN, ReLU NTK on ImageNet-32), with eigenvalue predictions concentrated along the diagonal in log-log scatter plots and eigenfunction subspace overlaps showing strong diagonal concentration (Figure 2). Using only the empirical data covariance matrix and a Gram-Schmidt-orthogonalized Hermite decomposition of the target, the HEA predicts KRR learning curves (test MSE vs. sample size) across multiple kernels and real image tasks, including sample complexity with constant prefactors for ImageNet polynomial targets (Figures 1 and 3). For MLPs in the feature-learning regime, the effective optimization time (η·n_iter) to reach MSE ≤ 0.1 on multi-Hermite polynomial targets is well-predicted by the inverse square root of the HEA eigenvalue λ_α^(−1/2) (Figure 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not compare against competing prediction methods; instead, it validates the HEA by comparing its analytical eigenstructure predictions against empirically computed kernel eigensystems (obtained by numerically diagonalizing kernel matrices). The HEA predictions match the empirical spectra and eigenfunctions closely across all four tested kernel/dataset settings, with deviations appearing only in the spectral tails due to finite-sample effects rather than genuine theoretical failure. The paper also contrasts the HEA-based learning curve prediction pipeline against the naive approach of directly diagonalizing the empirical kernel matrix, arguing that the HEA avoids finite-size distortions in tail modes, requires only quadratic-time Gram-Schmidt rather than cubic-time kernel diagonalization, and exposes the analytical structure of the eigenfunctions. The three conditions under which the HEA holds (fast decay of level coefficients, high effective data dimension, and 'Gaussian enough' data) are empirically validated by showing that violating any one degrades prediction quality (e.g., narrowing the Gaussian kernel width, reducing d_eff, or moving from CIFAR to MNIST/tabular data all worsen agreement).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the KRR eigenframework (Sollich 2001; Bordelon et al. 2020; Jacot et al. 2020; Simon et al. 2021), which maps kernel eigenstructure to test-risk predictions, and on the NTK line of work connecting infinite-width networks to rotation-invariant kernels. It departs from prior exact diagonalizations (Mei & Montanari 2019; Ghorbani et al. 2020; Zhu et al. 1997) that required highly symmetric domains (spheres, tori, product of spheres) by providing an analytical Hermite-polynomial approximation valid for anisotropic, high-dimensional data. Concurrent work by Wortsman & Loureiro (2025) addresses the same eigenstructure problem for dot-product kernels on anisotropic Gaussians; this paper extends the scope to general rotation-invariant kernels and validates the ansatz empirically on real image datasets, while also connecting the predicted eigenvalue ordering to feature-learning MLP dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a minimal statistical description (data covariance + target Hermite decomposition) suffices to predict KRR learning curves on real image datasets without ever constructing or diagonalizing a kernel matrix, positioning a path toward end-to-end learning theory that maps dataset structure to model performance. The finding that complex natural images are 'Gaussian enough' for Hermite eigenstructure to hold—thanks to the blessing of dimensionality—is a useful practical insight, though the ansatz requires rotation-invariant kernels, high effective dimension, and sufficiently wide kernels or fast-decaying level coefficients. Open questions include whether the framework extends to non-rotation-invariant kernels, lower-dimensional or tabular data, and whether the MLP learning-order result generalizes beyond shallow ReLU networks to deeper architectures or different activation functions.
