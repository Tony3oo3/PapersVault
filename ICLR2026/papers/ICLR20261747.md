## Problem

Existing 'think-then-generate' personalization methods rely on a single static reasoning step that must capture all information for the entire long-form response, creating hard-to-learn long-range dependencies and failing to adapt as content evolves. Moreover, current latent reasoning approaches (e.g., Coconut) use the same LLM for both reasoning and generation, forcing sequential token-by-token computation that severely slows training and inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261747/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a user's historical (query, response) pairs and a new query, and produces a personalized long-form text response (e.g., a full product review, research abstract, or topic essay). A key structural detail is that reasoning operates at the token level: each output position receives its own latent reasoning vector extracted from the Reasoner's final hidden layer, and these vectors are fused additively into the Generator's token embeddings via a learnable scaling coefficient λ. During training, all ground-truth tokens are fed simultaneously in a teacher-forcing pass, so the entire reasoning sequence is computed in one forward pass rather than autoregressively.

## Method

FlyThinker introduces a dual-model architecture with a separate Reasoner LLM and Generator LLM that operate in parallel. The Reasoner produces a latent reasoning token at each position depending only on the query and previously generated response tokens (not on its own prior reasoning outputs), which breaks the sequential dependence between reasoning steps and enables all reasoning tokens to be computed in a single parallel forward pass during training. The Generator fuses these latent vectors with token embeddings (e(y_t) + λ·r_t) before prediction, allowing dynamic reasoning signals to directly influence each token's probability. During inference, the two models run in a staggered pipeline: while the Generator predicts the current token, the Reasoner simultaneously computes the next reasoning vector, yielding near-SFT latency. Both models are jointly optimized end-to-end with standard next-token prediction loss, requiring no auxiliary supervision for the reasoning component.

## Results

FlyThinker achieves the best performance across all three LongLaMP tasks on most metrics. On Product Review, it reaches ROUGE-1 0.3663, BLEU 4.36, and METEOR 0.2489; on Abstract Generation, ROUGE-1 0.3716, BLEU 6.34, METEOR 0.2549; and on Topic Writing, ROUGE-1 0.3139, BLEU 4.06, METEOR 0.2409. The largest gains over the strongest tuning-based baseline (SFT) appear in BLEU, with improvements of +11.5% (Product Review), +8.9% (Abstract Generation), and +4.4% (Topic Writing). FlyThinker also maintains near-SFT inference latency by running the Reasoner and Generator in parallel.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlyThinker outperforms the strongest tuning-based baseline SFT on all four metrics across all three tasks, with the most notable gains in BLEU (+11.5% on Product Review, +8.9% on Abstract Generation) and METEOR (+7.8% on Product Review, +6.6% on Topic Writing). Against the reasoning-based baselines, FlyThinker consistently beats both CoT and Coconut: on Abstract Generation it achieves BLEU 6.34 vs. CoT's 5.85 and Coconut's 5.24, and on Topic Writing it leads CoT by +35% in BLEU (4.06 vs. 3.00). Among tuning-free methods, RAG and CoS are substantially weaker, with RAG achieving only BLEU 3.30 on Product Review versus FlyThinker's 4.36. LLM-TRSR and NextQuill, which use summary-enhanced or causal-preference fine-tuning, also trail FlyThinker by a clear margin on BLEU and METEOR. The only near-tie is CoT on Abstract Generation ROUGE-1 (0.3685 vs. 0.3716), where the gap is under 0.4%.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlyThinker builds on the latent reasoning paradigm introduced by Coconut (Hao et al., 2024), which compresses chain-of-thought into hidden-state tokens, and on the think-then-generate personalization line (REST-PG, R2P, RPM, TagPR) that performs one-shot reasoning before generation. The key departure is architectural: rather than using a single LLM autoregressively for both reasoning and generation (as in Coconut or CoT), FlyThinker splits these into a separate Reasoner and Generator operating in parallel, with the Reasoner conditioned only on previously generated response tokens rather than its own prior outputs. This 'think-while-generating' formulation—interleaving token-level reasoning with generation—repurposes the latent reasoning mechanism from a sequential prefix into a concurrent, position-wise signal fused into the Generator's embedding space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that FlyThinker achieves token-level dynamic reasoning at near-SFT training and inference cost, removing the sequential bottleneck that has made reasoning-augmented personalization impractical for long-form outputs. The position-sensitive evaluation showing sustained personalization quality in later token segments (where baselines suffer context drift) suggests the paradigm could be a general recipe for any long-sequence generation task requiring evolving context. Open questions include whether the dual-model overhead becomes prohibitive at very long sequence lengths, whether the Reasoner-Generator size ratio has principled scaling laws, and whether the approach extends beyond personalization to other reasoning-intensive generation settings (e.g., multi-turn dialogue, code generation with evolving constraints).
