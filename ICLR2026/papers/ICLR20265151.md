## Problem

Reward-maximizing RL methods for LLM reasoning (PPO, GRPO, REINFORCE++) over-optimize the dominant mode of the reward distribution, causing mode collapse where the policy converges to a single high-reward solution pattern and neglects less frequent but valid reasoning paths. This reduces diversity of generated chain-of-thought trajectories and impairs generalization to novel problems, a problem that is especially acute in long CoT reasoning where capturing a diverse distribution of plausible solutions is essential.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text question (math or code problem, up to 2048 tokens) and produces a reasoning trajectory of up to 8192 tokens ending in a final answer. The scalar outcome reward (e.g., answer correctness) is group-normalized across 8 rollouts per prompt. The partition function Z_φ consumes the mean of the language model's final-layer hidden states over all prompt tokens as a fixed-dimensional input and outputs a single scalar normalizing constant, making the target distribution tractable without enumerating all trajectories.

## Method

FlowRL replaces reward maximization with reward distribution matching by introducing a learnable partition function Z_φ (3-layer MLP) that normalizes scalar rewards into a valid target distribution, then minimizing the reverse KL divergence between the policy and this target. A key theoretical result (Proposition 1) proves gradient equivalence between this KL objective and the trajectory balance loss from GFlowNets, yielding a practical squared-loss formulation: (log Z_φ + (1/|y|)log π_θ − βr̂ − (1/|y|)log π_ref)². Two practical modifications address long-CoT challenges: length normalization (dividing log-probabilities by sequence length) prevents gradient explosion over 8K-token trajectories, and PPO-style clipped importance sampling with detached gradients enables off-policy micro-batch updates without excessive drift. The reference model π_ref is incorporated into the reward as a prior constraint (exp(βr)·π_ref), and the overall objective is shown to be equivalent to jointly maximizing expected reward and policy entropy (Proposition 5).

## Results

On math reasoning (Avg@16), FlowRL achieves 48.39% average accuracy with Qwen2.5-32B-Base and 35.63% with Qwen2.5-7B-Base, outperforming GRPO by 10.0% and PPO by 5.1% on the 32B model. On code reasoning (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B), FlowRL reaches 37.43% Avg@16 on LiveCodeBench, a Codeforces rating of 1549.47 (83.3rd percentile), and 83.28% on HumanEval+, surpassing all baselines across the board. The largest individual gains appear on MATH-500 (+28.27 over backbone on 32B) and OlympiadBench (+30.46 over backbone on 32B), indicating strong improvements on harder problems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowRL outperforms all four baselines across every benchmark and model scale. Against GRPO (the strongest reward-maximizing baseline), FlowRL gains +10.05 points on 32B math (48.39 vs. 38.34) and +3.15 on 7B (35.63 vs. 32.48), with the largest per-benchmark advantage on MATH-500 (+19.15 on 32B) and OlympiadBench (+16.89 on 32B). Against PPO, FlowRL leads by +5.14 on 32B (48.39 vs. 43.25) and +3.65 on 7B, though PPO remains competitive on AMC23 (76.40 vs. 73.75 on 32B). Against REINFORCE++ (R++), FlowRL wins by a wide margin (+21.25 on 32B, +4.11 on 7B), as R++ struggles with long CoT reasoning. On code tasks, FlowRL beats GRPO by +4.68 on LiveCodeBench Avg@16 (37.43 vs. 32.75) and by +235.65 Codeforces rating points (1549.47 vs. 1313.82), and also surpasses the combined GFlowNets+PPO loss of Zhang et al. (2025a) by +1.96 on 7B math (35.63 vs. 33.67), confirming that trajectory-level importance sampling is more effective than a joint PPO+GFlowNet objective for long CoT reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowRL repurposes the trajectory balance objective from GFlowNets (Malkin et al., 2022; Bengio et al., 2023a) — originally designed for short discrete sequences in small combinatorial spaces — and adapts it to long CoT reasoning in LLMs via a gradient-equivalence proof connecting reverse KL minimization to the trajectory balance loss. This positions the paper as a principled alternative to ad-hoc diversity-preserving techniques (entropy regularization, high-entropy token selection, clip-ratio tuning) that have been applied to PPO/GRPO, instead reframing the objective from reward maximization to reward distribution matching. The practical adaptations (length normalization, importance sampling, reference-model constraint) are borrowed from standard RL practice but are novel in their integration into a flow-balancing framework for LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~10% average improvement over GRPO across six math benchmarks and consistent gains on code tasks, validated at both 7B and 32B scales, suggests that mode collapse is a significant and addressable bottleneck in current LLM RL training. The diversity analysis (nearly doubling GPT-judged diversity scores) and the case study provide mechanistic evidence that the gains come from broader solution exploration rather than reward hacking. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even longer CoT (>8K tokens), whether the 3-layer MLP partition function suffices for highly structured problems, and whether the β hyperparameter sensitivity generalizes across domains. The method also adds a learnable component (Z_φ) and a trajectory-level loss that may complicate integration into existing RL pipelines.
