## Problem

While diffusion models have become the foundation for generative dataset distillation, their inherent representativeness prior remains unexploited, forcing existing methods to impose external constraints (clustering, pre-trained classifiers, fine-tuning losses) to ensure distilled samples faithfully reflect the original data. The paper asks whether the diffusion model's own feature-extraction capability can serve as a built-in representativeness prior, eliminating the need for such auxiliary mechanisms.

## Input / Output

The method takes random Gaussian noise as the starting point and, at each reverse-diffusion timestep, consumes noisy per-class training samples (x_t^{train|c}) passed through the diffusion backbone's intermediate layer to obtain feature vectors for guidance. It requires a pre-trained diffusion model (Stable Diffusion U-Net or DiT-XL/2-256) but no retraining or fine-tuning. The output is a set of synthetic 224×224 RGB images per class (10, 50, or 100 IPC) that can be directly used to train downstream classifiers under either hard-label or soft-label protocols.

## Method

DAP decomposes the conditional score function via Bayes' theorem into a diversity/generalization term (already estimated by the pre-trained diffusion model's score network ε_θ) and a representativeness term. The representativeness prior is formalized as a Boltzmann distribution over a Mercer-kernel-induced distance d(φ(x_syn), φ(x_train)) in the diffusion backbone's feature space, where φ is an intermediate layer of the U-Net or Transformer. During reverse diffusion (VP-SDE with DDIM), the gradient of this kernel distance with respect to the noisy sample is computed and added as an energy-based guidance term scaled by γ, steering each denoising trajectory toward regions well-represented by real data. An early-stop strategy (t_stop=25) limits guidance to early timesteps to preserve diversity, and the linear Mercer kernel K(x,y)=x^T y is used for tractability. The entire procedure is training-free: no external classifier, clustering, or fine-tuning is required.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (soft-label protocol, ResNet-18), DAP achieves 49.1% Top-1 accuracy at IPC10 and 62.7% at IPC50, outperforming the strongest baselines IGD (45.5%/59.8%) and MGD³ (45.6%/60.2%) by 3.5% and 2.2% respectively. On the harder ImageWoof subset (hard-label protocol), DAP reaches 43.9% (IPC10) and 63.2% (IPC50) with ResNet-18, surpassing all competing methods. DAP also demonstrates superior cross-architecture generalization, achieving the highest accuracy across ResNet-101, MobileNet-V2, EfficientNet-B0, and Swin Transformer without any architecture-specific tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAP consistently outperforms all seven compared baselines on ImageNet-1K, the primary large-scale benchmark. At IPC10, DAP (49.1%) exceeds the next-best VLCP (46.7%) by 2.4% and IGD/MGD³ (~45.5%) by ~3.5%; at IPC50, the gap widens to 2.2% over VLCP (60.5%). On ImageNette and ImageWoof (hard-label protocol), DAP wins in nearly all settings, with the sole exception being IGD slightly surpassing DAP on ResNet-18 IPC10 for ImageNette — an artifact of IGD's architecture-specific inductive bias. In cross-architecture evaluation (Table 5), DAP achieves the highest accuracy across all four test architectures (ResNet-101, MobileNet-V2, EfficientNet-B0, Swin Transformer), while IGD and RDED show notable drops due to their reliance on a specific surrogate network. Under scale reduction (IPC100→IPC50→IPC10), DAP preserves accuracy with <2% degradation, whereas IGD and MGD³ suffer >5% drops, confirming DAP's superior generalization prior. The method is also compatible with existing approaches: adding DAP guidance to Minimax yields consistent gains (e.g., 64.2% vs. 58.2% on ImageNette IPC10 with ConvNet-6).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative dataset distillation paradigm that has moved from GAN-based methods (DiM, GLaD) to diffusion-model-based approaches (Minimax, D4M, IGD, MGD3, D3HR, VLCP). While prior diffusion-based DD methods either fine-tune the diffusion model under auxiliary losses (Minimax, D4M) or inject representativeness through external mechanisms—clustering (MGD3), pre-trained classifiers (IGD, CaO2), or statistical matching (D3HR)—DAP argues these external constraints are unnecessary. It draws on the classifier-guidance literature (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021; Chen et al., 2024a,b) and Mercer kernel theory to formalize the representativeness prior already embedded in the diffusion backbone's feature extraction, enabling training-free guided sampling via an energy-based term in the reverse SDE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAP provides the first theoretical decomposition showing that vanilla diffusion models inherently satisfy the diversity and generalization requirements of dataset distillation, while identifying representativeness as the missing piece that can be recovered from the same model without retraining. The training-free, architecture-agnostic nature makes it broadly applicable and composable with existing DD pipelines (demonstrated via Minimax+DAP). Open questions include whether the approach extends to newer diffusion architectures (FLUX, SD 3.5), non-vision modalities (video, language, multimodal), and whether the sampling-time overhead from feature extraction on noisy training data scales acceptably for very large distillation budgets. The sensitivity of the guidance scale γ to the balance between representativeness and diversity also remains a practical tuning challenge.
