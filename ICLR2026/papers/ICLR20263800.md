## Problem

Retrofitting LLMs with targeted behaviors (safety refusal, chain-of-thought reasoning, etc.) typically requires costly supervised finetuning or distillation that must be repeated independently for every model architecture. The paper asks whether behaviors already encoded in one model's parameter-efficient adapter can be transferred to an architecturally different recipient model without backpropagation, additional training data, or access to the original finetuning corpus.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263800/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a donor model with pre-trained DiReFT adapter weights (low-rank interventions on last-token residual activations at selected layers), an architecturally distinct recipient model, and a small task-agnostic set of N=1030 profiling prompts from LIMA. It produces bidirectional linear converter matrices (Moore-Penrose pseudoinverses of shape d_R×d_D and d_D×d_R per layer pair) computed entirely on CPU in seconds, which at inference time allow the recipient to apply the donor's adapter interventions in its own activation space. No gradient computation or task-specific data is required at any stage.

## Method

Command-V operates in three phases. First, activation profiling: both models process the same prompt set and record last-token residual activations at every layer, yielding per-layer activation matrices. Second, converter derivation: layers are matched via a linear depth mapping (l_R = ⌊α·l_D⌋ where α is the depth ratio), and bidirectional linear converters are computed as Moore-Penrose pseudoinverses of the paired activation matrices (C_{R→D} = X†Y, C_{D→R} = Y†X), requiring no backpropagation. Third, at inference, for each intervened layer the recipient's hidden state is projected into the donor's space, the donor's DiReFT low-rank intervention (h + W₂ᵀ(W₁h + b)) is applied, and the resulting delta is projected back and added to the recipient's activation. The approach exploits the empirical observation that cross-architecture LLMs converge to a shared representation geometry, making linear activation-space mappings sufficient to transfer behavior with negligible added latency (single-digit milliseconds per batch).

## Results

On refusal enhancement (WildJailbreak), porting a ReFT adapter from Llama3.2-3B to Llama3.1-8B via V reduces attack success rate (ASR) from 36.85% to 20.40%, outperforming direct ReFT training on the 8B model (28.55% final ASR). In the reverse direction (8B→3B), V achieves 16.4% ASR versus 12.20% for direct ReFT on the 3B model. For jailbreak facilitation (AdvBench/HarmBench), V ports jailbreakability from single-digit ASR to 20–80% across models, with the best donor (Qwen2.5-7B) averaging 41.2%/46.9% ASR on AdvBench/HarmBench. On Big Bench Hard CoT reasoning, V-porting from a same-family model yields up to +50% accuracy improvement (Qwen2.5-3B on Multistep Arithmetic), comparable to direct ReFT training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is direct ReFT (DiReFT) training on the target model, which requires task-specific data and backpropagation. On refusal enhancement, V clearly wins on Llama3.1-8B (20.40% ASR vs. 28.55% for direct ReFT, a 8.15-point improvement) but loses on Llama3.2-3B (16.40% vs. 12.20%, a 4.2-point gap). V also incurs higher overrefusal rates (4.52% vs. 2.26% on 8B; 14.48% vs. 8.60% on 3B), a known trade-off of activation-based interventions. On ARC-Challenge, V preserves reasoning accuracy within ~1–3 points of the off-shelf model, confirming no significant utility degradation. For jailbreak facilitation and CoT reasoning, no direct ReFT-on-recipient baseline is reported in tabular form; instead, the diagonal entries of Figure 3 (ReFT validation performance) serve as an upper bound, and V's off-diagonal porting results are described as 'comparable' to same-model ReFT training, particularly within the same model family.

## Relation to Prior Work

Command-V sits at the intersection of representation finetuning (ReFT/DiReFT, Wu et al. 2024), activation engineering (RepE, Zou et al. 2023a; steering vectors, Turner et al. 2023), and the Platonic representation hypothesis (Huh et al. 2024; Wolfram & Schein 2025). It departs from knowledge distillation by avoiding teacher data generation and student retraining, from model merging by operating in activation space rather than weight space (enabling cross-architecture transfer), and from standard PEFT by requiring no backpropagation on the recipient. The core mechanism—deriving Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse linear converters from a small set of profiling prompts to bridge donor and recipient residual spaces—is a novel combination of these threads, but each individual component (last-token activation profiling, linear depth-scaled layer matching, low-rank intervention modules) is drawn from established prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: an edge device can apply an 8B model's safety or reasoning adapter to a 3B model using only pre-computed activation profiles and small converter matrices, without ever downloading or running the donor model. However, the method's limitations are substantial—overrefusal rates roughly double under V, smaller recipient models sometimes suffer output collapse, and cross-family transfer (e.g., Llama→Gemma) is markedly weaker than within-family transfer. Open questions include whether non-linear or trained converters close the remaining gap, how to predict good donor-recipient pairings a priori, and whether task composition (stacking multiple pasted adapters) degrades gracefully. The work is best read as a strong proof of concept that the shared-representation-space hypothesis has direct practical utility for behavior transfer, rather than a fully general solution.
