## Problem

One-step diffusion-based super-resolution models like GenDR still rely on a VAE for latent encoding/decoding, which profiling reveals is the dominant bottleneck for both latency and GPU memory at high resolutions, forcing tiling strategies that further degrade throughput. Prior attempts to simplify the VAE (e.g., AdcSR removing only the encoder with ×2 pixel-unshuffle) leave the decoder intact and cannot scale to arbitrary upscaling factors, while fully removing the VAE with large-scale pixel-shuffle (×8) introduces severe repeated-pattern artifacts that existing discriminators cannot handle.

## Input / Output

The model takes a low-quality RGB image (and a text prompt for conditional guidance) and directly outputs a high-quality super-resolved image in pixel space. The input is first encoded via ×8 pixel-unshuffle (reducing spatial dimensions by 8× while expanding channels to 192) before entering the UNet, and the output is decoded via ×8 pixel-shuffle to recover full-resolution pixels. This end-to-end pixel-space pipeline supports ×2, ×3, and ×4 upscaling and can process a 4K (3840×2160) image in under 1 second with ~6 GB GPU memory, eliminating the need for tiling entirely.

## Method

GenDR-Pix replaces the VAE encoder and decoder of the one-step diffusion model GenDR with ×8 pixel-unshuffle and pixel-shuffle operations, respectively, converting the entire pipeline from latent space to pixel space. To train this VAE-free model without quality collapse, the authors propose a two-stage adversarial distillation: Stage I removes the encoder by matching student latents to teacher latents with adversarial features extracted from GenDR's UNet, and Stage II removes the decoder by using the Stage I model (GenDR-Adc) as the discriminator on shuffled pixel-space outputs. Three key components address the artifacts from large-scale pixel-shuffle: a masked Fourier space (MFS) loss that penalizes periodic amplitude spikes aligned with the shuffle grid, a RandPad augmentation that randomly shifts the pixel-unshuffle phase to prevent the discriminator from overfitting to a fixed tiling pattern, and a perceptual (DISTS) loss for subjective fidelity. At inference, a padding-based classifier-free guidance (PadCFG) integrates self-ensemble (averaging over different padding offsets) with standard CFG in a single paired forward pass, improving perceptual quality without additional latency.

## Results

On ImageNet-Test, GenDR-Pix achieves 25.49 PSNR, 0.7286 SSIM, and 0.2252 LPIPS with 866M parameters and 344G MACs (without PadCFG) or 744G MACs (with PadCFG). On RealSet80, it runs in 32 ms per image—the fastest among all diffusion-based SR methods—while scoring 69.92 MUSIQ and 0.7190 CLIPIQA. Compared to the original GenDR, GenDR-Pix delivers 2.8× acceleration and 61.4% GPU memory reduction (8.01 GB vs. 20.75 GB on 4K output) with only a marginal drop in perceptual quality (MUSIQ 70.23 vs. 70.96 on RealLR250). Without PadCFG, a 4K image can be restored in under 1 second using only 5 GB of memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original GenDR, GenDR-Pix substantially improves full-reference fidelity (PSNR 25.49 vs. 24.14, SSIM 0.7286 vs. 0.6878, LPIPS 0.2252 vs. 0.2652) while incurring a small perceptual-quality drop (MUSIQ 72.85 vs. 74.68, CLIPIQA 0.7168 vs. 0.7395), all while being 2.8× faster and using 61% less GPU memory. Versus OSEDiff, GenDR-Pix gains 0.67 dB PSNR and 0.04 CLIPIQA on ImageNet-Test while running 3.2× faster (32 ms vs. 103 ms on RealSet80). Compared to AdcSR, GenDR-Pix matches runtime (32 ms vs. 33 ms) but achieves higher PSNR (25.49 vs. 25.07) and SSIM (0.7286 vs. 0.6982) with fewer parameters (866M vs. 456M is actually fewer for AdcSR, but GenDR-Pix has lower MACs at 344G vs. 496G). Against multi-step diffusion methods (StableSR, DiffBIR, SeeSR, DreamClear), GenDR-Pix is 116–199× faster (32 ms vs. 3731–6892 ms) while remaining competitive on perceptual metrics. Among GAN baselines, GenDR-Pix is slightly slower than BSRGAN/Real-ESRGAN (32 ms vs. 36 ms) but surpasses them on perceptual quality (MUSIQ 69.92 vs. 66.63/64.49 on RealSet80). On the 4K RealDeg benchmark, GenDR-Pix is the only method besides BSRGAN that processes full 4K images without tiling, completing in 3.58 s vs. GenDR's 46.51 s (12.99× speedup) with comparable quality scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-step diffusion SR lineage from GenDR (Wang et al., 2025) by removing the entire VAE, building on AdcSR (Chen et al., 2025a) which had only replaced the encoder with ×2 pixel-unshuffle. The key conceptual departure is treating pixel-(un)shuffle as a lossless substitute for both the VAE encoder and decoder at ×8 scale, reversing latent-space diffusion into pixel-space. The multi-stage adversarial distillation (first removing the encoder, then the decoder) and the PadCFG inference strategy are novel training/inference mechanisms not present in prior VAE-simplification attempts like AdcSR or structural pruning works (BK-SDM, flux.1-lite).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: enabling 4K image restoration in ~1 second with 6 GB GPU memory removes the tiling bottleneck that has constrained all latent-based diffusion SR, making real-time high-resolution deployment feasible. The profiling insight that VAE (not UNet) is the dominant latency/memory bottleneck for one-step diffusion SR redirects optimization efforts away from the diffusion backbone. Limitations include applicability only to one-step models (multi-step VAE removal is not addressed), residual repeated-pattern artifacts from ×8 pixel-shuffle, and dependence on the SD2.1 UNet architecture whose lossy downsampling may exacerbate artifacts. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to DiT-based backbones and other low-level restoration tasks.
