## Problem

EEG foundation models have been reported to achieve strong performance across BCI tasks, but prior studies typically evaluate only one or two protocols (e.g., leave-one-out zero-shot), omit comparisons against classical non-neural decoders, and rarely include statistical testing. This leaves the practical advantage of expensive pre-training over compact neural networks or well-established signal-processing pipelines largely unverified, especially in data-scarce, subject-specific settings that dominate real-world BCI deployment.

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw EEG signals segmented into spatiotemporal patches of 16 samples, tokenized via linear projection and augmented with separate temporal and spatial positional encodings; pre-training uses over 8 million such segments drawn from 11 public datasets with varying channel counts (19–128) and sampling rates (200–1024 Hz), all standardized to a 256 Hz baseline. Downstream, the encoder produces token representations that feed a task-specific head (linear probe or fully fine-tuned classifier/regressor), yielding class labels for seven classification tasks (2–40 classes) or continuous values for two regression tasks (attended speech envelope, vigilance level). Evaluation spans six protocols—Population, Per-Subject Self, Per-Subject Transfer, LOO Zero-Shot, LOO Fine-Tune, and LOO Drop—each probing a different axis of generalization and transferability.

## Method

ST-EEGFormer is a Vision Transformer encoder (ViT-style, with three sizes: 32.7M, 110.9M, and 328.4M parameters) pre-trained exclusively via masked autoencoding at a 75% mask ratio on raw EEG segments, where 75% of spatiotemporal tokens are randomly hidden and a lightweight decoder reconstructs the original signal from the remaining visible tokens plus mask tokens carrying positional embeddings. After pre-training, only the encoder is retained as the backbone for downstream fine-tuning or linear probing. The paper's broader contribution is a six-protocol, multi-task benchmark that systematically compares ST-EEGFormer against five prior foundation models (BENDR, BIOT, LaBraM, EEGPT, CBraMod), four compact neural decoders (DeepConvNet, EEGNet, EEG Conformer, CTNet), and task-specific classical non-neural pipelines (CSP/FBCSP, Riemannian classifiers, FBCCA/TRCA, xDAWN variants), with rigorous permutation and Wilcoxon significance testing. Key findings include: linear probing is consistently weak across all foundation models; fine-tuned foundation models match but do not reliably surpass compact NNs in data-scarce settings; no clear scaling law emerges across decoder sizes; and simple MAE pre-training on raw EEG produces top-performing representations, directly challenging the claim (from LaBraM) that raw-signal reconstruction is ineffective.

## Results

Across seven classification tasks and six evaluation protocols, fine-tuned ST-EEGFormer-l (328M params, MAE-pre-trained on 8M+ EEG segments) achieves the highest or statistically tied-top accuracy in Population, Per-Subject (Transfer), and LOO Fine-Tune settings, while classic CTNet consistently outperforms all linear-probed foundation models across every protocol except LOO Drop. No clear scaling law is observed: normalized accuracy vs. model parameters shows a poor logarithmic fit, whereas training time per epoch grows exponentially (R²=0.60). On regression, CTNet and linear-probed EEGPT achieve the best DTU performance (Pearson R≈0.05), while EEGNet and DeepConvNet lead on SEED-VIG (R>0.45), with classic NNs outperforming fine-tuned foundation models on the more challenging DTU task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among foundation models, ST-EEGFormer-l (the proposed MAE-pre-trained ViT) achieves the strongest or statistically tied-top performance in Population, Per-Subject (Transfer), and LOO Fine-Tune protocols, while EEGPT's multi-layer head gives it the best linear-probing results among all foundation models (though still below fine-tuned models). Classic NN decoders, particularly CTNet, consistently outperform every linear-probed foundation model across all protocols except LOO Drop, and match fine-tuned foundation models in LOO Fine-Tune with no significant difference. Classic non-NN decoders (CSP/FBCSP, Riemannian MDM/FgMDM, FBCCA, TRCA, xDAWN variants, DCPM, RBP-based classifiers) yield the lowest mean accuracy in LOO Zero-Shot but remain competitive in Per-Subject (Self) settings, with no significant differences among them. In LOO Drop (catastrophic forgetting), classic NN decoders suffer the largest accuracy drop after subject-specific fine-tuning, performing significantly worse than all foundation model groups. On the challenging DTU auditory regression task, CTNet (R=0.048) and linear-probed EEGPT (R=0.047) outperform all fine-tuned foundation models (best: LaBraM at R=0.011), while on SEED-VIG, EEGNet (R=0.471) and DeepConvNet (R=0.452) lead with no significant difference from foundation model variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture and the Masked Autoencoder (MAE) pre-training objective, adapting both to EEG by introducing separate spatial and temporal positional encodings for spatiotemporal tokenization. It positions itself as a corrective to the EEG foundation model literature (BENDR, BIOT, LaBraM, EEGPT, CBraMod), which typically evaluates only one or two protocols (population decoding, LOO zero-shot) and omits comparisons against classic non-neural decoders. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of ST-EEGFormer—a deliberately simple MAE-pretrained ViT—that directly challenges LaBraM's claim that raw-signal MAE fails to converge, while the six-protocol evaluation framework (population, per-subject self/transfer, LOO zero-shot/fine-tune/drop) represents a methodological extension beyond the single-protocol comparisons standard in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a substantial gap between the field's assumption that complex pre-training objectives and large-scale foundation models are necessary for strong EEG decoding, and the empirical reality that simple MAE pre-training and even classic non-neural decoders (CSP, FBCSP, Riemannian classifiers) remain highly competitive, especially in data-scarce per-subject settings. The absence of a clear scaling law and the consistent weakness of linear probing suggest that current pre-training strategies do not produce sufficiently task-agnostic representations, raising the open question of what pre-training objectives or data scales would actually close this gap. The authors call for a community-scale EEG dataset analogous to ImageNet and standardized, statistically rigorous benchmarks—without which progress risks being incremental and overfit to limited evaluation protocols.
