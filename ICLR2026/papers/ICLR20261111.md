## Problem

Existing diffusion-based pose estimation methods condition on both joint locations and rotations, but location measurements are inherently body-shape-dependent—two people in the same pose have identical rotations but different joint locations due to differing bone lengths. This causes models trained on one user's body shape to fail on users with different proportions, requiring costly per-user fine-tuning or joint training over many body shapes with no guarantee of covering all variations.

## Input / Output

The method takes noisy 6D rotation vectors and 3D location measurements from exactly 3 sensor joints (head and two wrists) at 60 Hz, along with the user's bone lengths, and produces a sequence of 22-joint global rotations in 6D representation plus a root translation. The root translation is not directly predicted but recovered by dragging the estimated pose until the head location matches the measurement. Poses are generated in 41-frame windows with 20-frame overlap for longer sequences.

## Method

InPose decomposes the pose estimation problem into a scale-free prior (joint rotations) and a scale-dependent likelihood (joint locations given rotations and bone lengths). A CFG-based DiT diffusion model, fine-tuned from BoDiffusion, is trained to condition only on the 3-point rotation measurements, yielding a body-shape-agnostic prior over scale-free poses. At inference, the user-specific bone lengths are plugged into a modified ΠGDM likelihood score that measures how well the current denoised pose explains the observed locations; this score acts as an inverse-kinematics guidance term added to the DDIM update at each step. The key theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) proves that propagating a Gaussian random variable through the non-linear 6D-to-rotation-matrix mapping D(·) yields an approximately Gaussian distribution with a computable positive-definite covariance, making the likelihood score tractable. The measurement operator is linearized via a Kronecker-product formulation, and root translation is eliminated by using pairwise differences of the three measured joint locations.

## Results

On the default body shape (Protocol 1), baselines outperform InPose: AvatarJLM achieves 4.92 cm MPJPE and 4.25° MPJRE, while InPose reports 7.64 cm and 6.38° respectively. However, when the upper body is scaled by ×1.4, InPose drops to only 9.15 cm MPJPE and 6.71° MPJRE, compared to 26.09 cm / 7.02° for AvatarJLM and 13.40 cm / 11.48° for BoDiffusion(Global). InPose's MPJRE remains nearly flat (~6.5°) across all tested body shape variations, demonstrating zero-shot generalization, while baselines degrade by 2–3× in rotation error. InPose also maintains stable performance under increasing location measurement noise, whereas baselines show progressive degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InPose is compared against AvatarJLM (a conventional neural network) and two variants of BoDiffusion (a CFG-based diffusion model: Local and Global output). At the default body shape for which baselines are trained, all baselines outperform InPose on every metric (e.g., MPJPE 4.92 vs. 7.64 cm for AvatarJLM vs. InPose). However, when the upper body is scaled by ×1.4, InPose achieves 9.15 cm MPJPE and 6.71° MPJRE, beating BoDiffusion(Global) by 32% in position error (9.15 vs. 13.40 cm) and by 41% in rotation error (6.71° vs. 11.48°), and beating AvatarJLM by 65% in position error (9.15 vs. 26.09 cm). Under non-uniform scaling (Arms ×1.4, Torso ×0.7), InPose achieves the best MPJRE (6.52°) and UPE (3.23 cm) among all methods. The critical finding is that InPose's MPJRE stays within 6.4–6.7° across all shape variations, while AvatarJLM's MPJRE ranges from 4.25° to 9.33° and BoDiffusion(Local)'s ranges from 4.32° to 15.35°, confirming that the inverse-guidance formulation provides genuine zero-shot generalization that pure CFG models cannot match.

## Relation to Prior Work

InPose builds directly on BoDiffusion's DiT-based conditional diffusion architecture and the ΠGDM pseudoinverse-guided diffusion framework, but departs from both by splitting the conditional score via Bayes' rule: rotations serve as the CFG prior (scale-free), while locations enter only as a pseudoinverse guidance term (scale-dependent). This contrasts with prior sparse-sensor pose estimators (BoDiffusion, AvatarJLM, FLAG) that condition on both rotations and locations jointly, making them inherently body-shape-dependent. The paper also extends the ΠGDM framework by proving that a Gaussian random variable propagated through the non-linear 6D-to-rotation-matrix operator can itself be approximated as Gaussian, enabling closed-form likelihood scores for the inverse guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot generalization across arbitrary body shapes addresses a critical practical gap for VR/AR applications where per-user fine-tuning is infeasible. However, InPose underperforms baselines at the default body shape, struggles with lower-body estimation without head-position input, and is sensitive to bone-length estimation errors beyond ~1 cm. Open questions include whether the Gaussian approximation in Theorem 1 can be tightened to close the default-shape gap, whether bone lengths can be inferred blindly without calibration, and whether physics-based dynamics can improve lower-body inference without sacrificing the zero-shot property.
