## Problem

Existing weather AI methods treat generation (nowcasting, radar inversion) and understanding (diagnostic reasoning, quality assessment) as disjoint paradigms, with no single architecture capable of both predicting physical fields and explaining the underlying storm dynamics. This separation is particularly limiting for extreme events where actionable decision-making requires not only accurate forecasts but also mechanistic interpretation of the drivers behind them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260266/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

All tasks are cast into a unified sequence-to-sequence mapping T: X→Y conditioned on a task-specific text prompt p_t. For nowcasting, the input is 10 VIL frames at 256×256 resolution (~256 visual tokens each) and the output is 12 future VIL frames; for inversion, two satellite infrared channels (IR069, IR107) are mapped to a single VIL field; for understanding, a single VIL frame or 12-frame sequence yields a structured natural-language report rated on a four-level ordinal scale. An optional CoT reasoning prompt can be prepended to steer the model toward structured causal explanations alongside the primary output.

## Method

Omni-Weather initializes from the pretrained Bagel-7B-MoT backbone and routes all tasks through shared self-attention layers, with task-specific encoders handling modality differences: an understanding encoder for radar QA, a VAE generation encoder for satellite-to-radar inversion, and an EarthFormer-based temporal encoder for nowcasting that provides motion-aware tokens to stabilize long-horizon dynamics. Generation tasks are decoded via a VAE decoder (L2 loss over target pixels/frames) while understanding tasks use a text decoder (cross-entropy over tokens), combined in a single weighted multi-task objective. A key architectural choice is conditioning the shared attention on EarthFormer's temporally aggregated tokens rather than forcing the backbone to learn multi-frame evolution directly, which proved less stable. The paper further introduces a Chain-of-Thought dataset (8,000 annotations) built via a three-stage GPT-based pipeline (attribute annotation → causal reasoning generation → automated quality verification), which serves as auxiliary supervision during training and as a reasoning prompt during inference, improving perceptual quality (LPIPS, Radar-Score) and interpretability at a modest cost to pixel-level CSI.

## Results

On radar nowcasting (SEVIR), Omni-Weather achieves the best CRPS (0.026), LPIPS (0.179), and CSI-Pool16 (0.539), reducing CRPS by over 15% and LPIPS by over 25% compared to single-task baselines while maintaining competitive CSI-Mean (0.384). On radar inversion, Omni-Weather attains the highest CSI at all intensity thresholds, with gains up to 20% at high-value levels (C-219: 0.118 vs. 0.053 for WeatherGFM). On weather understanding (RadarQA), Omni-Weather surpasses RadarQA on radar image understanding by 20–25 points on key attributes (Miss: 92.21 vs. 67.67; FAR: 88.72 vs. 65.35) and achieves the best GPT4-Score on sequence understanding (7.48 vs. 6.87).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against nowcasting baselines, Omni-Weather outperforms CasCast, DiffCast, and EarthFormer on perceptual and probabilistic metrics (CRPS 0.026 vs. 0.031/0.033/0.037; LPIPS 0.179 vs. 0.207/0.235/0.322) and on pooled CSI (CSI-P16: 0.539 vs. 0.518/0.511/0.387), while matching CasCast on CSI-Mean (0.384) and slightly trailing EarthFormer (0.389). On radar inversion, Omni-Weather dominates WeatherGFM at high-intensity thresholds (C-160: 0.263 vs. 0.208; C-219: 0.118 vs. 0.053) but has a higher RMSE (0.514 vs. 0.436), suggesting it captures more extreme structures at the cost of pixel-level error. For weather understanding, Omni-Weather significantly surpasses RadarQA on radar image understanding (Miss: 92.21 vs. 67.67; FAR: 88.72 vs. 65.35; Sharpness: 91.74 vs. 78.60) and achieves the best GPT4-Score on sequence understanding (7.48 vs. 6.87), though it trails RadarQA on sequence Overall score (61.79 vs. 66.17) and High-Value accuracy (67.29 vs. 80.52). Closed-source LLMs (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-pro, Claude-sonnet-4) perform substantially worse on both understanding subtasks, with Overall scores below 50, confirming the need for domain-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unified generation-and-understanding paradigm established in vision (Bagel, Transfusion, MetaMorph, BLIP3-o) to the weather domain, where prior work has kept generation models (CasCast, DiffCast, EarthFormer, WeatherGFM) and understanding models (RadarQA, WeatherQA) in separate architectures. It initializes from Bagel-7B-MoT and repurposes EarthFormer as a temporal radar-sequence encoder for nowcasting, while introducing a purpose-built Chain-of-Thought dataset (constructed via GPT-4o/GPT-o3 annotation over SEVIR) that has no direct analogue in prior weather literature. The key conceptual departure is treating weather generation and understanding as complementary supervision signals within a single shared self-attention backbone rather than as disjoint pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical finding that joint training on generation and understanding tasks mutually improves both (Table 2) provides evidence that weather prediction and diagnostic reasoning are not independent objectives, which could reshape how multi-task weather models are designed. The CoT-for-generation trade-off—perceptual quality gains (LPIPS, Radar-Score) at a modest pixel-level accuracy cost (CSI-M)—offers a practical recipe for explainable nowcasting, though the faithfulness of the generated reasoning traces remains an open concern. Limitations include restriction to SEVIR-scale short-range nowcasting at 256×256, inability to adapt to general-domain VAEs, and the need for broader validation on medium-range forecasting and typhoon prediction before the unified paradigm can be considered general-purpose.
