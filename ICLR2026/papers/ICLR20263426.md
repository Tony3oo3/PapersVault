## Problem

MPC-based LLM inference is prohibitively slow because non-linear operations (Softmax, SiLU, RMSNorm) must handle activation values spanning many orders of magnitude due to outlier phenomena, forcing expensive lookup tables or many approximation iterations. Prior work either substitutes non-linearities with MPC-friendly polynomials (requiring fine-tuning and causing accuracy loss) or improves cryptographic primitives without exploiting the structured nature of LLM activations. The paper asks whether the unique outlier properties of decoder-only transformers can be leveraged to shrink the input domain and design faster non-linear protocols without modifying the model.

## Input / Output

The system operates in a two-party computation setting where the user holds a text prompt and the model provider holds LLM weights; a trusted dealer supplies correlated randomness offline. Unusually, the user's prompt is prefixed with model-specific special tokens (identified during a preparation stage) to force outliers into fixed KV-cache positions, and the model provider pre-computes auxiliary parameters (layer-wise scaling factors φ and conformant maxima) that are consumed during inference. The output is the standard LLM-generated text, with the computation revealing nothing beyond the function output to either party.

## Method

SOAL is a two-stage framework: in preparation, the model provider runs extra inferences to identify special tokens whose activations cause outliers (via a token-wise maximum ratio threshold) and collects conformant maxima at predefined attention positions; in inference, these special tokens are prefixed to the prompt so outliers are confined to cached positions, and the remaining activations occupy a narrow, layer-specific range. For RMSNorm, a secret layer-wise scaling factor reduces the reciprocal-square-root input domain, enabling a 3rd-degree polynomial initial guess plus only 2 Newton-Raphson iterations. For SiLU, the sigmoid is reformulated via integer/fraction decomposition, using a small LUT for the integer part and local exponentials for the fraction part, followed by one NR iteration on a bounded denominator. For Softmax, conformant maxima (local maxima at BOS, first, and last-two positions) replace the exact maximum, eliminating the O(n log n) comparison cost and yielding constant-round, constant-communication softmax regardless of sequence length. The protocols are instantiated under both additive secret sharing (CrypTen-based) and function secret sharing (Sigma-based) schemes, achieving ~2× speedup on Llama2-7B and GPT-2 with 512 tokens while preserving perplexity and benchmark accuracy without any fine-tuning.

## Results

On 512-token secure inference, SOAL achieves nearly 2× total speedup over CrypTen across all tested LLMs: GPT-2 drops from 48.86 s to 23.30 s, Llama2-7B from 428.95 s to 193.59 s, and Mixtral 8×7B from 1104.46 s to 668.23 s. The largest per-operation gains come from Softmax (up to ~7.5× on Llama2-7B, from 199.99 s to 26.62 s) and SiLU/GeLU activation (~2.3× on Llama2-7B, from 87.30 s to 37.13 s). Under the FSS scheme (Sigma), SOAL reduces GPT-2 512-token inference from 10.05 s to 7.58 s and cuts key size from 86.69 GB to 61.83 GB. Model quality is preserved: Llama2-7B perplexity on WikiText-2 shifts only from 5.55 to 5.58, and benchmark accuracies (ARC, HellaSwag, PIQA, Winograde) remain within ±0.5% of the original model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CrypTen (the primary ASS baseline), SOAL wins decisively on all three models: total inference time drops by ~2.1× on GPT-2, ~2.2× on Llama2-7B, and ~1.7× on Mixtral 8×7B, with communication reduced by 3–6×. Against Sigma (FSS scheme), SOAL reduces GPT-2 512-token online time from 10.05 s to 7.58 s (25% faster) and cuts the per-inference key size from 86.69 GB to 61.83 GB (29% smaller). Against SecFormer, SOAL achieves a comparable speedup profile across input lengths but without requiring any fine-tuning or model re-architecture. Against MPCFormer, SOAL matches or exceeds its speedup at longer sequence lengths (≥256 tokens) while preserving the original model weights, whereas MPCFormer substitutes nonlinearities with low-degree polynomials and relies on knowledge distillation to recover accuracy. In all comparisons, SOAL is the only method that requires zero fine-tuning and operates on the unmodified original model.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOAL sits at the intersection of two established threads: MPC-based secure LLM inference (CrypTen, SIGMA, BOLT, PUMA, BumbleBee) and the study of outlier activations in LLMs (Dettmers et al., Sun et al., Xiao et al., Chen et al.). Prior MPC-LLM work either improves cryptographic primitives or re-architects and re-trains models to be MPC-friendly (MPCFormer, SecFormer), both of which carry significant overhead. SOAL departs from both by exploiting the outlier phenomenon—previously studied only in linear layers—to reduce the input domain of non-linear MPC protocols, requiring no model modification or fine-tuning. The conformant maxima concept for Softmax extends the attention-sink observation (Xiao et al., 2023b) into a protocol-level optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SOAL demonstrates that LLM-specific structural properties can be directly leveraged to accelerate secure inference without altering model weights, offering a complementary axis of optimization to cryptographic improvements. The >5× Softmax speedup and the elimination of O(n log n) maximum-finding complexity are practically significant for long-context inference. Limitations include the trusted-dealer 2PC setting, model-specific special-token identification, and the ~10% conformant-maxima mismatch rate. Open questions include whether further LLM-specific invariants (beyond outliers) can be exploited, whether the approach extends to malicious-security or multi-party settings, and whether encoder-decoder architectures exhibit analogous exploitable structure.
