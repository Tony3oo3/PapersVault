## Problem

Existing quantization-aware training (QAT) methods for LLMs suffer severe degradation on reasoning benchmarks (mathematics, coding) when applied to post-trained models, because the heterogeneous knowledge structures introduced by supervised fine-tuning and preference optimization are not preserved under aggressive quantization. No prior work has systematically characterized how quantization calibration data domain affects different knowledge types (pre-training commonsense vs. post-training reasoning) or designed a QAT pipeline specifically targeting reasoning capability retention at ultra-low bit widths (<4 bits).

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a pre-trained instruction-tuned LLM (Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B in bfloat16), 4,096 calibration sequences (context length 2,048) drawn from a 80/20 mix of OpenThoughts-1.2M reasoning data and FineWeb-Edu pre-training data, 32,768 fine-tuning sequences from OpenThoughts-1.2M, and the corresponding fp16 teacher model for probability references. The output is a weight-only quantized model at 2-bit or 3-bit precision with group size 128 (shared scale and zero-point per group), where only the scale factors are fine-tuned in the second stage while weights remain frozen at their quantized values.

## Method

The proposed two-stage pipeline first performs block-wise quantization using mixed-domain calibration data (80% reasoning + 20% pre-training), which the paper shows preserves both commonsense knowledge and hard-to-restore reasoning representations that single-domain calibration would sacrifice. The second stage fine-tunes the quantized model with a teacher-guided reward rectification loss: the standard SFT cross-entropy loss is reweighted by the stop-gradient of the fp16 teacher's probability of the reference token, making the gradient equivalent to an on-policy policy-gradient update with a correctness reward while avoiding the autoregressive generation overhead of true RL. A KL divergence term between the teacher and student output distributions is added to align the full probability mass, with hyperparameters α=0.2 and β=1.0. This design replaces the student's own (quantization-corrupted) probabilities with the teacher's, preventing error amplification, and achieves 50.45% average improvement over PTQ baselines at 2-bit while requiring fewer than 1B fine-tuning tokens.

## Results

On the primary comparison (Table 2), the proposed two-stage QAT pipeline achieves 55.1% average accuracy at 2-bit on Qwen3-8B, outperforming GPTQ (4.6%) and AWQ (4.0%) by 50.45% on average across five reasoning benchmarks. At 3-bit, the method reaches 72.5% average on Qwen3-8B, surpassing GPTQ (66.8%) by 5.7 points. Against the native ternary model BitNet b1.58 2B4T, the 2-bit Qwen3-8B achieves 80.13% on MATH-500 versus 43.40%, using only 328M training tokens compared to BitNet's 4T tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PTQ baselines, the gap is dramatic at 2-bit: the proposed method achieves 55.1% average on Qwen3-8B versus 4.6% (GPTQ) and 4.0% (AWQ), a 50.45-point improvement, while at 3-bit it leads GPTQ by 5.7 points (72.5% vs 66.8%). Among QAT baselines on Qwen3-1.7B, the method outperforms EfficientQAT by 10.1 points at 2-bit (28.4% vs 18.3%) and by 1.7 points at 3-bit (55.2% vs 53.5%), and surpasses BitDistiller by 13.2 points at 2-bit (28.4% vs 15.2%) and 16.0 points at 3-bit (55.2% vs 39.2%). Compared to the native ternary model BitNet b1.58 2B4T, the 2-bit Qwen3-8B achieves 80.13% on MATH-500 versus 43.40% and 88.93% on GSM8K versus 58.38%, while requiring only 328M fine-tuning tokens versus BitNet's 4T pre-training tokens. In the on-policy RL comparison (Appendix A), the off-policy teacher-guided reward rectification loss matches GRPO at 3-bit (53.4% vs 52.5% avg) and outperforms it at 2-bit (24.5% vs 18.0%) while using ~5 GPU hours versus ~190 for GRPO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage QAT pipeline established by EfficientQAT (block-wise calibration followed by end-to-end fine-tuning) and BitDistiller (self-distillation for sub-4-bit LLMs) by reorienting both stages toward reasoning preservation. The key departure is an empirical finding that quantization sensitivity differs across knowledge domains—commonsense knowledge is robust to calibration data choice while reasoning capability is highly sensitive—motivating a mixed-domain calibration strategy (80% reasoning / 20% pre-training data) not used in prior QAT work. The fine-tuning stage replaces standard cross-entropy or distillation losses with a teacher-guided reward-rectification objective borrowed from the RL-for-SFT literature (Wu et al., 2025), adapted to use the full-precision teacher's probabilities rather than the quantized student's, plus a KL-divergence alignment term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for deploying reasoning LLMs at 2-bit with dramatically fewer training tokens than native low-bit models (BitNet-2B4T required 4T tokens; this pipeline uses <1B), which is significant for practitioners who cannot afford from-scratch training. However, the 2-bit results still show severe degradation on code generation (LiveCodeBench drops from 53.6% to 6.5% for Qwen3-1.7B), and the '50.45% improvement' headline is measured against PTQ baselines that are essentially non-functional at 2-bit. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 8B parameters, whether the 80/20 mixing ratio is optimal, and how to suppress the redundant self-correction tokens that quantized models exhibit (Appendix C). The teacher-guided reward-rectification loss may also be applicable to other post-training compression scenarios beyond quantization.
