## Problem

The assignment step of Lloyd's k-means requires computing all n×k sample-center distances per iteration, which becomes computationally prohibitive for large-scale clustering. Existing exact acceleration methods (Elkan's, Annulus, Exp-ns, Ball k-means) rely on distance-based bounds that either introduce hyperparameters, incur O(nk) memory overhead, or suffer from high setup costs. This paper asks whether angular relationships between samples and cluster centers can provide a parameter-free, low-overhead mechanism to prune unnecessary distance computations.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a data matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} and a pre-specified number of clusters k, and outputs cluster labels y ∈ R^n identical to standard Lloyd's k-means (exact, not approximate). Each iteration requires precomputing auxiliary geometric variables: the k×k inter-center distance matrix D, the k×k angle matrix Θ (angles at the coordinate origin between center vectors), per-sample distances r_i to their assigned center, and per-sample angles β_i. The total space overhead beyond the data matrix is O(k²), and the algorithm introduces no tunable hyperparameters beyond k itself.

## Method

Angle k-means (AKM) accelerates the assignment step by exploiting a geometric theorem: for a sample x_i currently assigned to center c_g, if the angular deviation cos(β_i − θ_gj) ≤ ‖c_g − c_j‖/(2r_i), then x_i is guaranteed to be at least as far from c_j as from c_g, so the distance ‖x_i − c_j‖ need not be computed. The algorithm precomputes inter-center distances, inter-center angles (via the law of cosines from precomputed norms), and per-sample angles relative to the coordinate origin. During assignment, candidate centers are visited in increasing order of distance from c_g, and the loop terminates early once the triangle inequality (d_gl ≥ 2r_i) is violated. This yields a per-iteration complexity of O(k²D log k + npD + nk) with O(k²) space, where p is the average number of surviving candidate centers, and produces clustering results identical to Lloyd's algorithm.

## Results

Angle k-means (AKM) reduces per-iteration distance computations to as low as 6.70% of Lloyd's on EYaleB (k=200), 10.15% on RaFD (k=200), 14.81% on L-CLBA (k=10000), and 15.50% on L-YTF (k=2000). Corresponding running times are 39.28%, 32.13%, 52.16%, and 38.39% of Lloyd's, respectively. Across all 14 datasets and all tested k values, AKM consistently requires the fewest sample-center distance computations among all compared methods, and the speedup advantage grows monotonically with increasing k.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AKM outperforms all five baselines on distance computation across virtually every dataset and k value tested. Against the closest competitor, Elkan's algorithm, AKM achieves roughly 2× fewer distance computations at high k (e.g., 6.70% vs. 10.29% on EYaleB k=200; 14.81% vs. 31.38% on L-CLBA k=10000). Exp-ns is the second-closest baseline on distance counts (e.g., 9.16% on EYaleB k=200) but still trails AKM by ~27% relative. Annulus and Ball k-means (both ring and no-ring variants) are substantially worse, typically requiring 3–10× more distance computations than AKM at high k. On running time, the gap widens dramatically: Annulus, Exp-ns, and Ball k-means incur 1,000–40,000% of Lloyd's time due to expensive per-iteration setup (tree/ball construction), while AKM stays within 32–93% of Lloyd's time. Elkan's algorithm is the only baseline that sometimes approaches AKM's running time (e.g., 42.96% vs. 39.28% on EYaleB k=200), but AKM still wins on both distance count and wall-clock time in that setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

Angle k-means extends the family of boundary-based accelerated exact k-means algorithms (Elkan's, Hamerly's, Annulus, Exp-ns, Ball k-means) by introducing angular relationships between data points and cluster centers as a new geometric pruning criterion. While prior methods rely on distance-based bounds (triangle inequality, annulus regions, ball neighborhoods) to exclude candidate centers, AKM leverages the angle between the origin-to-center vector and the center-to-sample vector, combined with precomputed inter-center distances, to derive a tighter exclusion condition. Unlike Yinyang k-means or Adaptive k-means, AKM introduces no new hyperparameters, preserving the drop-in usability of standard Lloyd's algorithm while achieving stronger pruning than distance-only bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-free design makes AKM a practical drop-in replacement for standard k-means in large-scale clustering pipelines where tuning additional hyperparameters is undesirable. The speedup scales with the number of clusters (distance computations drop to 10–15% of Lloyd's at k=2000), which aligns with real-world scenarios where large k is common. However, the gap between distance-computation reduction and actual running-time improvement (e.g., 10% distances but 32% time) suggests that angular filtering overhead partially offsets the savings, and the reliance on the coordinate origin as a fixed reference point limits generality. Future work on multiple or dynamically selected reference points could close this gap and extend the method's applicability.
