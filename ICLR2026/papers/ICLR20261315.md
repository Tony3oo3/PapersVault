## Problem

Logarithmic post-training quantization (PTQ) offers multiplier-free hardware efficiency and better alignment with bell-shaped weight distributions, but existing methods rely on suboptimal rounding-to-nearest (RTN) and suffer from inherent symmetric quantization grids, outlier sensitivity, and fixed base configurations. While learnable rounding has significantly advanced linear PTQ, its extension to the logarithmic domain remains unexplored due to the non-linearity of logarithmic mapping, non-differentiability of rounding in that domain, and the discrete nature of mixed logarithmic bases.

## Input / Output

LogART consumes pre-trained FP16 model weights and a small unlabeled calibration set (32 segments of 2048 tokens from C4 or WikiText-2 for LLMs, or 2048 unlabeled ImageNet images for vision models). It produces 3-bit or 4-bit per-channel quantized weight codes, element-wise learned rounding variables R (frozen into weights post-calibration), and per-channel hyperparameters including outlier-aware scaling factors, dynamic base-2/base-√2 code allocations, and asymmetric bound offsets. The calibration data requires no labels, and the one-off learning cost is amortized over inference since rounding parameters are baked into the quantized weights.

## Method

LogART introduces two synergistic components: (1) Learnable Logarithmic Rounding (LLR), which replaces RTN with a floor-plus-sigmoid formulation in the log domain, optimizing element-wise rounding variables R via a task-aware reconstruction loss (Frobenius norm of quantization error times input covariance) plus a binary regularization term; and (2) Optimized Hyperparameter Search (OHS), a multi-level strategy combining tensor-wise asymmetric bound search (calibration-free), block-wise scaling factor search for outlier resilience, and block-wise dynamic base search that allocates base-√2 codes to large values and base-2 codes to small values in a distribution-aware manner. A Hardware Approximation Function (HAF) further replaces √2 multiplications with shift-add operations via Signed Dyadic Expansion, and this approximation error is absorbed during LLR optimization so the learned rounding decisions become robust to hardware constraints. The paper provides a theoretical decomposition showing OHS minimizes intrinsic discretization error while LLR minimizes residual rounding error on the established grid, proving their synergy is not merely additive.

## Results

On 3-bit per-channel weight quantization of LLMs, LogART achieves SOTA perplexity across all tested models: 31.52 PPL on OPT-125M (vs. 52.95 for GPTQ, 34.07 for BRECQ), 15.53 on OPT-1.3B, 11.11 on OPT-6.7B, 6.31 on LLaMA2-7B, and 8.19 on LLaMA3-8B (WikiText-2). On 4-bit weight quantization of CNNs and vision transformers, LogART reaches near-FP16 accuracy: 70.79% on ResNet18 (FP16: 71.00%), 71.62% on MobileNetV2 (FP16: 72.62%), 85.02% on ViT-Base (FP16: 85.10%), and 81.92% on DeiT-Base (FP16: 81.98%). LogART also achieves substantially faster quantization runtime (e.g., 1.25 min vs. 1.06 hr for BRECQ on OPT-125M) and lower GPU memory (0.75 GB vs. 3.07 GB), while its multiplier-free arithmetic element reduces hardware area by 43.23% and power by 61.16% compared to AdaLog.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LogART consistently outperforms all linear-domain baselines (GPTQ, BRECQ, AffineQuant, aespa, AdaRound, FlexRound, APHQ) on both accuracy and efficiency. On LLMs at 3-bit, LogART reduces PPL by 2.14 over GPTQ and 0.14 over aespa on LLaMA2-7B, while being 2.2×–24.9× faster than optimization-based methods (BRECQ, AffineQuant, aespa) with equal or lower GPU memory. Among logarithmic baselines, LogART dramatically surpasses LogNet (e.g., 71.62% vs. 1.22% on MobileNetV2) and SLogII (71.62% vs. 31.20% on MobileNetV2; 85.02% vs. 83.54% on ViT-Base), with gains attributed to the combination of learnable rounding, multi-base search, and outlier-aware scaling. Against AdaLog (which uses log quantization for activations but linear for weights), LogART achieves higher accuracy (e.g., 85.02% vs. 84.77% on ViT-Base) with 3.9× faster runtime and 43.23% less hardware area. The only scenario where a baseline is competitive is GPTQ on small models (OPT-125M), where its speed advantage (19.8 s) is notable, but LogART still wins on accuracy (31.52 vs. 52.95 PPL) and memory (0.75 vs. 1.05 GB).

## Relation to Prior Work

LogART bridges two previously separate lines of work: learnable rounding for linear PTQ (AdaRound, BRECQ, aespa) and logarithmic quantization (LogNet, DLog, SLogII). While learnable rounding had become the standard for pushing linear PTQ into the 4-bit regime, it had never been applied to the non-linear, non-differentiable logarithmic domain. The paper also extends the DLog multi-base concept from fixed segmentation to a searchable, per-channel allocation, and introduces the first asymmetric logarithmic quantizer, addressing a structural limitation shared by all prior log-PTQ methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LogART is the first logarithmic PTQ method to scale effectively to LLMs at 3-bit while maintaining multiplier-free hardware efficiency, offering a practical path to deploying large models on resource-constrained edge devices with 40%+ area/power savings over linear PTQ accelerators. The integration of hardware approximation (HAF) directly into the optimization loop is a clean solution to the accuracy-vs-hardware trade-off that has plagued √2-based log quantization. Open questions include extending to joint weight-activation quantization, whether the approach generalizes to 2-bit or lower, and how it interacts with emerging rotation-based or sparse quantization techniques.
