## Problem

Existing incomplete multi-view clustering (IMVC) methods suffer from two key limitations: (1) they focus on recovering missing data but overlook inaccuracies in imputed values caused by the absence of true label information, and (2) recovered features are learned from complete data, neglecting the distributional discrepancy between complete and incomplete instances. This paper addresses both issues by enforcing a consistent block diagonal structure across all views during reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263387/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes incomplete multi-view data (text documents for BBCSport, RGB images for COIL-20/Caltech-7/BUAA) where 30% or 50% of instances are randomly missing per view, along with binary missing-view indicator matrices that encode which instances are present in each view. It produces cluster assignments via spectral clustering on a consensus representation matrix, reconstructed complete data matrices for all views, and per-view complete affinity graphs with enforced block diagonal structure. The Gaussian kernel affinity graphs are extended to full n×n size by zero-padding for missing instances before reconstruction.

## Method

CBDS_IMVC jointly optimizes three coupled components via ADMM: (1) a symmetric-aware missing-view inferring strategy that extends incomplete Gaussian-kernel affinity graphs to complete n×n graphs via projection matrices and reconstructs missing data while preserving geometric structure; (2) a novel confident block diagonal structure (CBDS) regularizer combining a tensor nuclear norm (solved via t-SVD with FFT) and an L1 sparsity term to enforce a strict, invariable block diagonal pattern shared across all views; and (3) an invariable graph completion module that learns a linear representation with simplex constraints to infer the intrinsic structure of each view from other views, masked element-wise to preserve the true structure of available instances. A consensus spectral clustering module then extracts the final cluster assignments from the Laplacian of the completed structure. The entire framework is solved by alternating closed-form updates for each variable, with adaptive view weighting to balance the contribution of views with different missing rates.

## Results

The proposed CBDS_IMVC achieves the best clustering accuracy across all five benchmark datasets at both 30% and 50% missing rates. Headline ACC values at 30% missing rate are 84.77% (BBCSport), 84.05% (COIL-20), 66.73% (Caltech-7), 49.35% (BUAA), and 75.24% (Noisy COIL-20). NMI and Purity metrics follow the same pattern, with the method leading on every dataset–metric combination. The largest margins appear on Caltech-7 (ACC +1.60 over HLSCG) and BUAA (ACC +2.86 over LBIMVC), while on BBCSport the gap over the nearest competitor HLSCG is +2.60 ACC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CBDS_IMVC outperforms all five baselines on every dataset and every metric at both 30% and 50% missing rates. The closest competitor is HLSCG (IJCAI 2025), which trails by 2.60 ACC on BBCSport (84.77 vs. 82.17) and 1.78 ACC on COIL-20 (84.05 vs. 82.27) at 30% missing. On Caltech-7, the gap widens substantially: CBDS_IMVC leads HLSCG by 1.60 ACC and AGC_IMVC by 9.42 ACC, indicating that the confident block diagonal regularizer is particularly effective on smaller, more structured datasets. On BUAA, LBIMVC is the nearest baseline (46.49 ACC) but still lags by 2.86 ACC, while AGC_IMVC underperforms dramatically (26.74 ACC), suggesting it struggles with the two-view face-recognition setting. Under noise (Noisy COIL-20), CBDS_IMVC maintains a 1.58 ACC lead over LBIMVC and a 4.12 ACC lead over HLSCG, demonstrating robustness to Gaussian perturbation. The older baselines AWIMVC and UEAF are consistently 10–30 ACC points behind, confirming that graph-completion-based approaches have largely superseded earlier imputation-only strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the incomplete multi-view clustering (IMVC) line of work initiated by methods such as AWIMVC, UEAF, AGC_IMVC, LBIMVC, and HLSCG, which typically recover missing views and then perform graph-based or spectral clustering. Its conceptual departure lies in introducing a 'confident block diagonal structure' (CBDS) regularizer that enforces a shared, strict block-diagonal pattern across all views during reconstruction, combined with a tensor nuclear norm (via t-SVD) to capture high-order cross-view correlations. The invariable graph completion term, which fuses other views' affinity graphs through a simplex-constrained linear combination, is reminiscent of adaptive graph completion in AGC_IMVC but is coupled with the CBDS constraint to preserve local structure invariance. The overall framework is solved via ADMM, repurposing standard subproblem solvers (eigenvalue decomposition, t-SVD, proximal shrinkage) in a new joint optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important setting—clustering when some views are partially missing—and demonstrates consistent (if modest) gains over strong baselines across five datasets at two missing rates. However, several open questions remain: the method is implemented in MATLAB and tested only on small-to-medium datasets (≤1,474 samples), raising scalability concerns for large-scale multi-view data; the block-diagonal assumption may not hold for datasets with overlapping or fuzzy cluster boundaries; and the three hyperparameters (λ₁, λ₂, λ) require per-dataset tuning despite the reported sensitivity analysis. Future work could explore whether the CBDS constraint generalizes to very high missing rates (>70%), to streaming or online settings, or to deep feature extraction pipelines where views are learned end-to-end.
