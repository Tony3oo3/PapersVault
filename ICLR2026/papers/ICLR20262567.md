## Problem

For small open-source LLMs on challenging multi-step reasoning, both Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards fail: SFT overfits to rigid token-level imitation of long expert demonstrations, while RLVR receives no positive gradient signal when the model's pass@k rate is near zero. This leaves a critical gap where difficult problems remain effectively unlearnable by either paradigm, particularly for 7B-scale models where a single incorrect intermediate step derails the entire chain.

## Input / Output

For math, the model receives a problem statement concatenated with previously completed expert steps as context and must produce an internal reasoning monologue (in <think> tags) followed by a single next-step action such as one algebraic manipulation. For software engineering, the input is a code repository issue plus prior action-observation pairs, and the output is the next executable command (e.g., a bash call). The reward is computed exclusively on the action portion, not the monologue, decoupling the model's free-form reasoning style from the graded correctness of its committed action.

## Method

SRL decomposes expert solution trajectories into discrete step-wise actions and trains the model via GRPO to reproduce each action while generating its own internal monologue. The reward is a dense sequence-similarity score (computed via difflib.SequenceMatcher over non-overlapping matching blocks) between the model's predicted action and the corresponding expert action, yielding values in [0,1] ∪ {−1} rather than a sparse binary correctness signal, which provides informative gradients even when all rollouts are ultimately incorrect. A dynamic sampling filter retains only samples whose rollout rewards have standard deviation above a threshold, ensuring meaningful policy updates. The framework is domain-agnostic: the 'action' is an algebraic step in math or an executable command in software engineering, and the same training loop applies to both. Initializing with SRL before refining with standard RLVR yields the strongest overall performance, as the dense step-level supervision bootstraps the policy into a regime where outcome-based RL can then further improve.

## Results

On competition-level math benchmarks (AMC23, AIME24, AIME25, Minerva Math), SRL achieves an average score of 27.6%, outperforming the base Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (24.6%) by 3.0 points and RLVR (24.5%) by 3.1 points. The sequential pipeline SRL→RLVR reaches 28.3% average, the best result across all methods. On SWE-Bench-Verified, SRL achieves a 14.8% oracle resolve rate and 8.6% end-to-end resolve rate, roughly doubling the SWE-Gym-7B SFT baseline (8.4% and 4.2% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against S1K-7B (the official SFT-distilled model from the s1K dataset), SRL dramatically outperforms: 27.6% vs. 11.7% average, revealing that direct SFT on challenging reasoning traces causes severe overfitting and performance collapse. Against R3 (reverse curriculum RL), SRL wins on the harder AIME24 (16.7% vs. 13.3% greedy) and AIME25 (13.3% vs. 10.0% greedy) benchmarks, while R3 edges ahead on AMC23 greedy (57.5% vs. 50.0%), suggesting SRL's dense step-wise reward is more effective when outcome-based exploration is infeasible. On SWE-Bench-Verified, SRL achieves 14.8% oracle resolve rate versus SWE-Gym-7B's 8.4% (a 74% relative improvement) and 8.6% end-to-end versus 4.2%, confirming that step-wise similarity rewards generalize beyond math to agentic software engineering. The SRL→RLVR pipeline further extends these gains, reaching 28.3% average on math and demonstrating that SRL provides a stronger initialization for subsequent outcome-based RL.

## Relation to Prior Work

SRL sits at the intersection of two well-established post-training paradigms for LLMs: supervised fine-tuning (distillation from teacher CoT traces, as in DeepSeek-R1 and s1K) and outcome-based reinforcement learning (GRPO, DAPO). It departs from both by reformulating the learning objective: rather than token-level imitation of a full trajectory (SFT) or a single binary reward on the final answer (RLVR), SRL decomposes expert solutions into discrete 'actions' and assigns a dense sequence-similarity reward at each step, optimized via the GRPO clipped policy gradient. The dynamic sampling mechanism is adapted from DAPO's variance-based filtering, generalized from binary correctness to continuous similarity scores. Conceptually, SRL is closest to reward-shaping in classical RL but applied at the granularity of logical reasoning steps rather than environment states.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SRL addresses a concrete and increasingly common failure mode: small open-source models (3B–7B) that cannot learn from hard multi-step problems because RLVR yields zero positive signal and SFT overfits to long demonstrations. The practical recipe of SRL→RLVR as a two-stage pipeline is likely to be adopted by teams training small reasoning models, and the extension to agentic software engineering (74% relative improvement over SWE-Gym-7B) suggests the framework is not domain-specific. Key limitations remain: the method requires expert trajectories with cleanly decomposable action boundaries, and the student model must already possess baseline instruction-following competence to generate coherent rollouts. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to domains with ambiguous or non-sequential action structures, and whether alternative similarity metrics (e.g., embedding-based, LLM-judged) could reduce dependence on exact token overlap.
