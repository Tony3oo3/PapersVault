## Problem

Scaling multi-task LoRA to 15–25+ tasks causes catastrophic accuracy collapse (e.g., 88.2%→2.0% on DOTA), and existing remedies—orthogonal regularization plus dynamic routing—hit a fundamental Pareto trade-off where stronger regularization reduces gradient conflict but simultaneously degrades routing decisiveness. The paper identifies two overlooked root causes: uniform regularization corrupts shared knowledge concentrated in high-singular-value components, and component-level LoRA placement (on W_q, W_v) amplifies gradient conflicts through attention's Softmax.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a frozen pretrained backbone (ViT for vision, LLaMA-2-7B for NLP) with N task-specific LoRA experts (rank r=16). At inference, a lightweight 2-layer MLP router consumes mean-pooled hidden states and emits a vector of g group-level weights per expert (rather than a single scalar), which are broadcast across d/g dimensions to compose the low-rank update. The block-level adapter receives the same LayerNorm output as the frozen block, so the adapted output is h_in + F(LN(h_in)) + Δ(LN(h_in)), keeping the LoRA path fully parallel to the backbone's internal non-linearities.

## Method

mtLoRA introduces three coupled designs built on HydraLoRA's asymmetric structure (shared A, task-specific B_i). First, spectral-aware regularization applies SVD to each B_i and re-weights singular values via w(σ)=exp(−σ/σ̄), so the orthogonality loss ‖(B_i′)^T B_j′‖_F² penalizes low-SV 'noise' components heavily while leaving high-SV 'signal' (shared inter-task knowledge) largely untouched. Second, fine-grained routing replaces the scalar per-expert weight with a g-dimensional vector Π_i, partitioning the hidden dimension into g groups so that different feature subspaces can activate different expert mixtures; a load-balancing loss prevents routing collapse. Third, block-level adaptation attaches the combined LoRA update as a residual parallel path after each attention or FFN block (consistent with Pre-LN), bypassing Softmax and other internal non-linearities that create cross-token gradient coupling; this reduces average inter-task gradient conflict by 76% while halving trainable parameters (37.7M vs. 75.5M). Together these designs yield +2.8% average accuracy over SOTA with 47% fewer parameters and 24% less training time across four 15–27-task benchmarks.

## Results

mtLoRA achieves 64.0% average accuracy across four large-scale multi-task benchmarks (15–27 tasks), outperforming the previous SOTA HydraLoRA (61.7%) by 2.3% on average. Per-benchmark, mtLoRA reaches 91.7% on DOTA (15 tasks), 81.5% on iNat2018 (25 tasks), 44.5% on Dolly-15k (16 tasks), and 38.5% on BBH (27 reasoning tasks). These gains come with 47% fewer trainable parameters (39.8M vs. 75.5M, i.e., 0.59% vs. 1.11% of LLaMA-2-7B) and 24% less wall-clock training time (72.1 min vs. 94.6 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

mtLoRA outperforms all three baselines on every benchmark. Against HydraLoRA (the strongest baseline and the architectural foundation), mtLoRA gains +2.6% on DOTA (91.7 vs. 89.1), +3.0% on iNat2018 (81.5 vs. 78.5), +2.1% on Dolly-15k (44.5 vs. 42.4), and +1.6% on BBH (38.5 vs. 36.9). Against LoRAHub, the margins are larger on NLP tasks: +2.8% on Dolly-15k and +3.6% on BBH, while on vision the gap is +2.8% (DOTA) and +1.3% (iNat2018). Against MMoELoRA, mtLoRA leads by +2.3% on DOTA, +3.5% on iNat2018, +2.4% on Dolly-15k, and +3.1% on BBH. Notably, mtLoRA achieves these gains while using 47% fewer parameters and training 24% faster than HydraLoRA, and the improvements are consistent across both vision (+2.6% avg.) and NLP (+2.9% avg.) domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on HydraLoRA's asymmetric LoRA structure (shared down-projection A, task-specific up-projections B_i) and MoLE's dynamic routing with load-balancing losses, but identifies a fundamental regularization–routing trade-off that prior work treated as independent solutions. It departs from the component-level LoRA placement convention (W_q, W_v) by proposing block-level parallel adapters that bypass internal non-linearities, and from uniform orthogonal regularization by introducing a spectral-aware loss that selectively penalizes low-SV components while preserving high-SV shared knowledge. The fine-grained routing design extends scalar per-expert weights to dimension-specific vectors, addressing heterogeneous conflict patterns across feature subspaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck: multi-task LoRA collapses catastrophically beyond ~10 tasks (88.2%→2.0% on DOTA), limiting real-world deployment of parameter-efficient multi-task systems. The spectral insight—that inter-task shared knowledge concentrates in high-SV components and uniform regularization corrupts it—provides a mechanistic explanation for the regularization–routing trade-off that prior work observed empirically but could not resolve. Limitations include reliance on the shared-A HydraLoRA structure (though authors note generalizability), validation primarily on LLaMA-2-7B and ViT, and the spectral hypothesis's generalizability across modalities remains untested. Open questions include layer-specific adapter granularity (early vs. late layers show different conflict patterns) and whether the block-level principle extends to non-Transformer architectures.
