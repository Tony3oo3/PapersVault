## Problem

Existing RL-based efficient reasoning methods built on GRPO's group-relative advantage function fundamentally mis-handle composite correctness-length rewards: penalizing correct but verbose answers can push their group-relative advantage below zero, causing the optimizer to actively discourage valid reasoning. This creates a severe performance-efficiency trade-off where all six state-of-the-art baselines sacrifice substantial accuracy (up to 7%+) for modest length reductions, while DRPO achieves 77% length reduction on easy problems with only 1.1% accuracy loss.

## Input / Output

The method fine-tunes existing large reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B/7B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B) via RL on text math and logic questions, producing chain-of-thought reasoning traces followed by a final answer in \boxed{} format. Training samples 8 responses per question with an 8k-token generation budget, and evaluation uses Pass@1 averaged over 16 generations. The key structural constraint is that the model must produce both a reasoning path and a verifiable final answer, with correctness determined by answer matching or formal verification.

## Method

DRPO decouples the learning signal for correct and incorrect rollouts by normalizing length-based rewards exclusively within the positive (correct) group, preventing verbose correct answers from receiving negative advantages. Theoretically, this is grounded in integrating a KL-regularized, reward-maximizing perturbation of the on-policy positive data distribution into the discriminative DisCO objective; the perturbed distribution P* has a closed-form solution P*(o) ∝ π_old⁺(o|q)·exp(r_l(o)/λ), yielding an importance weight ω(o|q) that scales each correct rollout's contribution by its length reward normalized only over correct samples. When λ→∞, the weight collapses to 1 and DRPO reduces to DisCO; smaller λ values increase the emphasis on shorter correct answers. The full algorithm requires only on-policy data with no additional data collection, using a penalty-based KL constraint for training stability, and the formulation generalizes to other preference rewards beyond length.

## Results

DRPO is the only method that consistently achieves positive Accuracy-Efficiency Score (AES) across all model sizes: +0.178 (1.5B), +0.249 (7B), and +0.297 (8B), while all RL-based baselines (RLOO-LP, ALP, HAPO) yield negative AES for 1.5B and 7B models. On the easy GSM8K benchmark, DRPO with a 1.5B model achieves 77% length reduction (1563→356 tokens) with only 1.1% performance loss, whereas the strongest baseline sacrifices 4.3% accuracy for 68% length reduction. On the 7B model, DRPO reduces average reasoning length from 3053 to 1502 tokens (51% reduction) with just 2.6% performance drop, compared to RLOO-LP's 38% length reduction at 7.1% performance loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRPO outperforms all six efficient-reasoning baselines on the AES metric across every model size. Against the three RL-based baselines trained under identical settings (RLOO-LP, ALP, HAPO), DRPO is the sole method achieving positive AES for 1.5B and 7B models, indicating it is the only approach that simultaneously reduces reasoning length and preserves or improves accuracy. HAPO suffers the most severe degradation (AES = −1.080 on 7B), consistent with the paper's diagnosis that its history-aware penalty most frequently produces negative advantages for correct-but-long rollouts. On the 8B model, RLOO-LP also achieves a positive AES (+0.251), but DRPO still leads (+0.297) with higher Pass@1 (0.690 vs. 0.619) and shorter length (1758 vs. 2249 tokens). The three externally-trained baselines (L1-max, ShorterBetter, LASER-D) appear in the performance-efficiency trade-off plots (Figures 3–4) and are consistently dominated by DRPO across all difficulty levels from GSM8K to AIME. On the non-mathematical K&K logic puzzle task, DRPO also achieves a substantially better trade-off, reducing generation length by 33.2% with no accuracy drop (0.972→0.974).

## Relation to Prior Work

DRPO builds directly on DisCO (Li et al., 2025a), a discriminative constrained optimization framework for LRM training, by integrating a length-reward-weighted positive data distribution into its objective. The closed-form solution for the perturbed positive distribution draws on the KL-regularized reward maximization formulation from DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023). Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant GRPO/RLOO paradigm for efficient reasoning by diagnosing a fundamental flaw: group-relative advantage computation actively penalizes correct-but-verbose answers when length penalties are incorporated, a limitation shared by all six RL-based baselines (RLOO-LP, ALP, HAPO, L1-max, SB, LASER-D) that the paper compares against.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnosis that GRPO's group-relative advantage is 'fundamentally ill-suited for composite rewards' has broad implications for the entire RL-based LRM training community, as GRPO is the standard framework adopted by DeepSeek-R1, Qwen-3, GLM-4.5, and many other open-weight reasoning models. The decoupling principle is general and extensible to other preference rewards beyond length (e.g., process rewards), making it a reusable design pattern. Open questions include adapting the regularization parameter λ to question difficulty, extending to non-mathematical reasoning tasks at scale, and whether the approach transfers to multi-turn or tool-use reasoning settings.
