## Problem

In CLIP-based multimodal contrastive learning, the modality gap—a persistent structural offset between modality-specific clusters in the shared latent space—has been studied almost exclusively through the lens of instance-wise tasks like retrieval, where its impact is debated and often negligible. This paper proves that the gap is instead strongly detrimental to group-wise tasks such as clustering, because it uniformly inflates within-class scatter by a constant ||δ||² term, fragmenting the latent space by modality rather than by semantic class. Prior mitigation strategies (fixed temperature, post-hoc translation, shared encoders) addressed the gap without establishing this task-dependent distinction or extending beyond the two-modal setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multimodal samples (image+text, audio+image+text, or video+audio+text) processed by modality-specific encoders (ResNet-50, EVA-CLIP ViT-G, BERT-B, BEATs) that project each input into a d-dimensional unit-norm embedding on the hypersphere. No architectural modifications are required; the contribution is entirely at the loss-function level. The aligned embeddings serve four downstream tasks: cross-modal retrieval via cosine similarity, clustering via k-means and k-NN (evaluated with V-Measure), multimodal retrieval with a cross-conditioning fusion encoder, and autoregressive captioning with a decoder head.

## Method

The proposed objective L_CL_gap augments the standard bidirectional InfoNCE loss with two complementary terms: (1) L_ATP, which minimizes the mean squared L2 distance between each sample's embedding and its anchor-modality counterpart, directly pulling true pairs together and driving the centroid gap toward zero; and (2) L_CU, a centroid-based uniformity loss that applies an RBF-kernel log-sum-exp penalty to the pairwise distances of per-sample multimodal centroids (the average of all M modality embeddings), ensuring semantic classes remain well-separated on the hypersphere without disrupting intra-pair alignment. The key design insight is that L_ATP alone collapses the latent space into a small region, while L_CU prevents this by spreading class centroids rather than individual embeddings—preserving the tight cross-modal alignment that sample-level uniformity (à la Wang & Isola) would destroy. Theoretically, the paper shows that the InfoNCE loss alone has a local minimum at a non-zero gap angle (≈60° in a controlled 6-pair simulation), whereas adding L_ATP shifts the global minimum to zero gap, and the gradient dynamics naturally emphasize harder negatives as the gap closes, improving cluster structure without altering retrieval rankings.

## Results

On MSCOCO (bimodal), the proposed method reduces the modality gap from 0.47 (CLIP-LT) to 0.03 while improving V-Measure from 12.98 to 23.63 (+10.65 points) and kNN clustering from 26.3 to 36.4, at a modest cost in text-to-video R@1 (70.3 vs. 74.6). On the trimodal MSR-VTT dataset, the gap drops from 0.29 to 0.07, V-Measure rises from 23.3 to 32.1 (+8.8 points), and kNN improves from 52.9 to 58.0, while text-to-audio R@1 actually increases (11.8 vs. 10.3). On AV-MNIST (trimodal), the method achieves the best scores across all metrics: gap 0.09, TV R@1 88.7, TA R@1 89.1, V-Measure 82.7, and kNN 89.2. In Table 2, the method also leads on multimodal retrieval R@1 (77.3 on MSCOCO, 33.3 on MSR-VTT) and captioning CIDEr (160.9 and 64.4 respectively), with CosTP rising from 0.34 to 0.77 on MSCOCO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP (LT) [Radford et al., 2021], the proposed method wins decisively on clustering: V-Measure improves by +10.65 on MSCOCO (23.63 vs. 12.98), +8.8 on MSR-VTT (32.1 vs. 23.3), and +5.1 on AV-MNIST (82.7 vs. 77.6), while the modality gap shrinks by 94% on MSCOCO (0.03 vs. 0.47) and 76% on MSR-VTT (0.07 vs. 0.29). The trade-off is a small drop in text-to-video R@1 on MSCOCO (70.3 vs. 74.6) and MSR-VTT (32.8 vs. 34.2), but text-to-audio R@1 actually improves on MSR-VTT (11.8 vs. 10.3). Against CLIP (FT) [Yaras et al., 2025], the method dominates on every clustering metric and gap measure while matching or exceeding retrieval scores. Against NotAGap [Fahim et al., 2024], the proposed method achieves a substantially lower gap (0.03 vs. 0.17 on MSCOCO; 0.06 vs. 0.09 on MSR-VTT), a much higher CosTP (0.77 vs. 0.11 on MSCOCO), and better MM retrieval R@1 (77.3 vs. 75.6) and CIDEr (160.9 vs. 153.3). In the MultiBench classification setting, adding the proposed losses to CoMM [Dufumier et al., 2025] yields 67.65% accuracy on MOSI (vs. 65.91% for CoMM alone and 66.42% for CoMM+Yaras) and 63.25% on UR-FUNNY (vs. 62.83% and 61.10%). Finally, the centroid-based uniformity loss L_CU outperforms the sample-level uniformity of Wang & Isola [2020] by +2.17 R@1 and +6.75 V-Measure on CIFAR-10, confirming that operating on centroids rather than individual embeddings is critical for preserving alignment while enforcing spread.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-style contrastive multimodal learning framework (Radford et al., 2021) by adding two auxiliary losses — an align-true-pairs term and a centroid-based uniformity term — that directly target the modality gap. Conceptually, it departs from prior gap-mitigation strategies (post-hoc translations in Liang et al. 2022, temperature fixing in Yaras et al. 2025, shared encoders in Eslami & de Melo 2025) by addressing the gap through the training objective itself, and it generalizes from the two-modal setting to arbitrary n-modal configurations. The uniformity component is a direct adaptation of Wang & Isola's (2020) hypersphere uniformity, but applied to cross-modal centroids rather than individual embeddings, preserving alignment while preventing collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central reframing — that the modality gap is largely irrelevant for retrieval but critically degrades clustering — resolves a long-standing debate in the community by showing the gap's impact is task-dependent rather than universally harmful or benign. The practical implication is that practitioners doing multimodal clustering, semantic grouping, or any task relying on absolute embedding distances should explicitly close the gap, while retrieval-focused pipelines need not worry. Limitations include modest gains on highly imbalanced datasets (CIFAR-10 with only 10 text labels) and evaluation restricted to at most three modalities; extending to more complex modality combinations and very large-scale pre-trained models remains open.
