## Problem

Existing feed-forward mesh reconstruction methods face two persistent obstacles: (1) RGB images as geometry input create severe training ambiguity because texture can conceal geometric errors, allowing multiple wrong geometry-texture pairs to render identical images and driving the network toward over-smoothed averaged solutions; and (2) the prevailing FlexiCubes-based mesh extraction pipeline is computationally redundant, training-unstable, and lacks direct 3D supervision on the SDF field.

## Input / Output

The geometry branch consumes K randomly sampled normal maps (512×512×3) with 16-dimensional camera embeddings, while the texture branch consumes the corresponding RGB images; the model supports a dynamic number of input views. For single-image or text-to-3D use, external foundation models (Zero123++/Era3D for multi-view synthesis, Lotus/StableNormal for normal prediction) generate the required inputs, so the user-facing interface remains a single RGB image or text prompt. The output is a textured 3D mesh extracted from a 192³ SDF grid via a modified FlexiCubes isosurface algorithm, with texture decoded from a 3×64×64 triplane field queried at rasterized vertex coordinates.

## Method

DiMeR disentangles the unified geometry-texture solution space into two independently supervised branches: a geometry branch that takes only normal maps (exploiting their strict one-to-one correspondence with surface orientation) through a ViT encoder and triplane decoder to produce an SDF grid, and a texture branch that takes RGB images through a parallel encoder-decoder to produce a triplane texture field. The mesh extraction pipeline is streamlined by removing FlexiCubes' deformation and weight MLPs (reducing computation by ~2.5× and GPU memory by ~1.5×) and replacing its unstable regularization with an eikonal loss computed over 200K randomly sampled 3D points plus direct GT SDF supervision at grid vertices. A novel PBR expectation loss, inspired by photometric stereo, renders the untextured predicted mesh under 10 random HDR environments and 10 random metallic-roughness materials, matching specular and diffuse light maps (with LPIPS) to enforce geometric correctness independent of any specific appearance. The texture branch rasterizes the predicted mesh vertices into image space, queries the triplane texture field at each pixel's 3D coordinate, and decodes to RGB, supervised solely by L2 and LPIPS losses against ground-truth images.

## Results

On the GSO dataset, DiMeR with ground-truth normal maps (DiMeR-GT) achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.028 and F1 of 0.992, reducing CD by 31.7% over the best baseline (PRM, CD 0.041). On OmniObject3D, DiMeR-GT reaches CD 0.024 and F1 0.996, a 29.4% CD reduction over PRM. When using predicted normal maps from StableNormal (DiMeR-SN), the method still achieves CD 0.032 on GSO and 0.030 on OmniObject3D, maintaining a 22% CD improvement over PRM while accepting the same RGB image input as baselines. For the single-image-to-3D task, DiMeR achieves CD 0.052 and F1 0.981 on GSO, outperforming PRM (CD 0.059, F1 0.961) and all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiMeR consistently outperforms both InstantMesh and PRM across all metrics on both GSO and OmniObject3D. Against PRM (the strongest baseline), DiMeR-GT reduces Chamfer Distance by 31.7% on GSO (0.028 vs. 0.041) and 29.4% on OmniObject3D (0.024 vs. 0.034), while also improving F1 by 0.015 and 0.005 respectively. Against InstantMesh, the CD gap is even larger: 37.8% on GSO and 38.5% on OmniObject3D. Even with predicted normal maps (DiMeR-SN, CD 0.032 on GSO), DiMeR still beats PRM by 22% in CD, and the input remains the same RGB images as baselines. On the single-image-to-3D task, DiMeR (CD 0.052, F1 0.981 on GSO) surpasses PRM (CD 0.059), InstantMesh (CD 0.066), MeshLRM (CD 0.079), and CRM (CD 0.144). The generative model Trellis, while producing high-quality meshes, shows significantly worse reconstruction fidelity (CD 0.136 multi-view, 0.119 single-view) because it cannot guarantee strict correspondence with input images.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiMeR extends the feed-forward sparse-view mesh reconstruction lineage (LRM → MeshLRM → InstantMesh → PRM) by rethinking the inductive bias: rather than feeding RGB images to a unified geometry-texture network, it disentangles the problem into a normal-map-driven geometry branch and an RGB-driven texture branch, each with decoupled supervision. Architecturally it retains the ViT encoder + triplane decoder + FlexiCubes pipeline from MeshLRM, but modifies FlexiCubes by removing its deformation and weight MLPs and replacing its regularization with eikonal loss plus direct 3D SDF supervision. The PBR expectation losses draw on photometric stereo principles, and the practical pipeline leverages recent normal-prediction foundation models (Lotus, StableNormal) to accept raw RGB input, positioning DiMeR as a drop-in upgrade over existing LRM-family methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geometry-texture disentanglement provides a clean, well-motivated solution to the training ambiguity problem that plagues RGB-based mesh reconstruction, yielding a 30%+ Chamfer Distance reduction over the strongest baselines (InstantMesh, PRM) on standard benchmarks. The practical simplification of FlexiCubes (enabling 192³ grids at lower GPU cost) and the normal-prediction benchmark are directly useful to practitioners building LRM-style systems. Key limitations remain: the method is restricted to object-level inputs with white backgrounds (no scene-level support), and its performance ceiling is coupled to the accuracy of upstream normal-prediction models, making it a moving target as those models improve.
