## Problem

The relationship between overparameterization, stability, and generalization remains incompletely understood for discontinuous classifiers, since the universal law of robustness of Bubeck & Sellke (2021) relies on Lipschitz continuity that is ill-defined for classifiers with discrete outputs. This paper addresses the open challenge posed by Bubeck & Sellke of identifying appropriate smoothness notions for classification that yield a tradeoff with model size, by introducing geometric stability measures that replace Lipschitz constants.

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework operates on a probability measure space (X × {-1,1}, μ) where X ⊂ R^d is bounded and μ satisfies a c-isoperimetry concentration condition; the hypothesis class F consists of binary classifiers f: X → {-1,1}. The method produces data-dependent Rademacher complexity bounds and generalization guarantees expressed in terms of class stability S(f) (expected input-space distance to the decision boundary) and normalized co-stability S*(g)/L(g) (expected codomain score margin divided by the Lipschitz constant of the score function). Empirically, the framework is instantiated on MNIST (28×28 grayscale) and CIFAR-10 (32×32 RGB) images, with stability estimated via adversarial perturbation search and Lipschitz constants via the ECLIPSE method.

## Method

The paper defines class stability S(f) as the expected distance from a sample to the decision boundary under the data distribution, and proves that under isoperimetry, the Rademacher complexity of a finite hypothesis class is bounded by a quantity that improves inversely with S(f) (Theorem 4). This yields a law of robustness for discontinuous functions: any interpolating classifier with p ≈ n parameters must be unstable with high probability, so achieving both near-perfect fitting and high stability requires overparameterization of order p ≈ nd (Corollary 6). For infinite function classes of the form f = sgn ∘ g_w with Lipschitz-continuous parameterization, the paper introduces normalized co-stability S*(g)/L(g) as a codomain-based robustness measure and derives an analogous generalization bound via an ε-net covering argument (Theorem 13). Empirically, 4- and 8-layer MLPs and CNNs trained to ≥99% training accuracy on MNIST and CIFAR-10 show that both stability measures increase with network width and track test accuracy, while traditional weight norms do not.

## Results

The paper is primarily theoretical, with empirical validation showing that class stability S(f) and normalized co-stability S*(g)/L(g) increase monotonically with network width (128→2048) on both MNIST and CIFAR-10, following the same qualitative scaling as test accuracy. On CIFAR-10, 8-layer MLPs achieve test accuracy around 50% while stability measures plateau, consistent with approaching the Bayes-optimal boundary within the restricted hypothesis class. In contrast, standard weight norms (or their inverses) exhibit different width-scaling and fail to track test accuracy. Heaviside-activation MLPs on MNIST confirm that the stability-width relationship persists even for discontinuous score functions, supporting the generality of the theoretical framework.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against specific competing methods; it is a theoretical contribution that extends the Bubeck–Sellke law of robustness to discontinuous classifiers. The empirical 'comparison' is between the proposed stability measures (class stability, normalized co-stability) and traditional norm-based complexity measures (weight norms, spectral norms). The key finding is that stability measures correlate with test accuracy and increase with overparameterization, while norm-based quantities remain largely uninformative or even negatively correlated with generalization. The theoretical results show that any interpolating model with p ≈ n parameters must be unstable (Corollary 6), requiring p ≈ nd parameters for high stability — a prediction that the width-scaling experiments qualitatively confirm. The paper positions its contribution relative to Bubeck & Sellke (2021) by replacing the Lipschitz-continuity assumption with class stability, and relative to Liu & Hansen (2024) by providing a generalization bound rather than merely defining the stability notion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the universal law of robustness of Bubeck & Sellke (2021) from Lipschitz-continuous regression to discontinuous classifiers, replacing the Lipschitz constant with a geometric notion of class stability (expected distance to the decision boundary) inspired by Liu & Hansen (2024). For infinite function classes, it introduces normalized co-stability (expected score margin divided by Lipschitz constant), connecting to the margin-based generalization literature (Bartlett et al. 2017; Tsuzuku et al. 2018) but using expected rather than minimum margins. The key conceptual departure is that Lipschitz continuity is ill-defined for discrete-output classifiers, so the paper develops an isoperimetry-based proof strategy that controls Rademacher complexity through stability rather than smoothness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central result—that achieving both near-perfect fitting and high stability requires overparameterization of order p ≈ nd—reframes overparameterization from a potential liability to a structural necessity for robust classification, providing theoretical grounding for why modern networks operate in the interpolation regime. However, practical applicability is limited: computing class stability and Lipschitz constants is NP-hard, and the empirical validation is confined to MNIST/CIFAR-10 with MLPs and small CNNs. Open questions include whether isoperimetric structure holds for real data manifolds, whether optimization dynamics implicitly favor stable solutions, and whether the Lipschitz regularity assumption for infinite classes is truly necessary or merely technical.
