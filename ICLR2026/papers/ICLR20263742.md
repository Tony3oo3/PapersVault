## Problem

Standard CLIP-style contrastive pretraining aligns each image with a single text label at a single granularity level, which is inadequate for medical imaging where a single image often corresponds to multiple disease categories (multi-label) and annotations exist at different semantic levels (e.g., disease category vs. clinical explanation vs. imaging protocol). Existing multi-label contrastive methods handle instance-label correlations but fail to capture cross-granularity semantics and generalize poorly under data scarcity typical of medical domains.

## Input / Output

The method takes medical images (retinal fundus photographs or chest X-rays, converted to PNG at 512×512 standard resolution) paired with structured multi-granularity text descriptions as input. For fundus images, text spans two levels: disease category (e.g., 'Abnormal, Age-related Macular Degeneration') and clinical explanation (e.g., 'Changes in the retinal pigment epithelium'); for X-rays, three levels: modality (CR/DX), study description, and series description. The output is a shared image-text embedding space usable for linear probing, full fine-tuning on downstream classification, or as a drop-in vision encoder within multimodal large language models for multiple-choice diagnosis.

## Method

MGLL replaces the standard CLIP loss with a three-component objective: (1) a Soft CLIP loss that lets each image align with multiple text labels weighted by co-occurrence probabilities, so the optimal image feature becomes the weighted centroid of its associated text features rather than a single one; (2) a point-wise binary cross-entropy loss that supervises individual image-text pair similarities to refine multi-label discriminability; and (3) a smooth KL divergence loss that drives predicted probability distributions from different granularity levels toward their mean distribution, enforcing cross-granularity consistency. The framework uses ViT-L/14 and BiomedicalBERT as default encoders, introduces no additional parameters or granularity-specific modules, and functions as a plug-and-play loss replacement (L = 0.5·L_sCLIP + 1·L_P + 1·L_sKL). Theoretical analysis proves that at optimality the image feature converges to the normalized weighted sum of its text features, and the KL term achieves zero only when all granularity-level predictions are identical, guaranteeing consistent cross-granularity alignment.

## Results

On MIDRC-XR (linear probe), MGLL achieves 61.25% AUC, 56.57% ACC, and 21.19% mAP, outperforming the second-best UniChest by 2.23% AUC. On MIDRC-XR-Portable (linear probe), MGLL reaches 83.86% AUC and 30.62% mAP, surpassing UniMed-CLIP by 3.81% AUC. On ChestX-ray14 (linear probe), MGLL attains 82.94% AUC, 90.41% ACC, and 28.53% mAP, beating CARZero by 5.62% AUC. In fully fine-tuned settings, MGLL achieves near-perfect AUC (99.08% on MIDRC-XR, 99.75% on MIDRC-XR-Portable) with the largest mAP margins (73.33% and 89.87% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MGLL outperforms all 8 X-ray baselines (CLIP, CheXzero, KAD, MRM, UniChest, UniMed-CLIP, CARZero, FG-CLIP) across every metric and setting on all three X-ray datasets. The largest linear-probe AUC gains are on ChestX-ray14 (+5.62% over CARZero) and MIDRC-XR-Portable (+3.81% over UniMed-CLIP), while fully fine-tuned AUC gaps reach 4.93% (MIDRC-XR over UniMed-CLIP) and 2.82% (MIDRC-XR-Portable over FG-CLIP). On the multi-label fundus dataset RFMiD, MGLL surpasses CLIP by at least 16.6% AUC in linear probing and 6.7% in full fine-tuning. When integrated as a vision encoder into 8 MLLMs (InstructBLIP, Mini-Gemini, Qwen-VL, InternVL, LLaVA, LLaVA-Med, Med-Flamingo, Janus-Pro), MGLL yields average accuracy gains of 4.6%–34.1%, with the most dramatic improvements in medically specialized models: LLaVA-Med (+34.1%) and Med-Flamingo (+31.7%). No baseline is matched or exceeded by MGLL in any reported comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CLIP's single-label, single-granularity contrastive pretraining to simultaneously handle multi-label and cross-granularity alignment in medical imaging. It departs from prior multi-label contrastive methods (MedCLIP, Symile, SILC) that model instance-label correlations but ignore hierarchical annotation structure, and from multi-granularity alignment work (Wang et al. 2022a, Zhao et al. 2024) that relies on fixed pipelines and specific annotation formats. The key conceptual departure is treating multi-label and multi-granularity as orthogonal axes of supervision unified under a single loss (soft CLIP + point-wise BCE + smooth KL divergence), rather than addressing them with separate mechanisms or architecture modifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, architecture-agnostic design means MGLL can be dropped into any existing vision-language model without retraining the backbone, lowering the barrier for medical imaging groups to adopt multi-granularity supervision. The two constructed datasets (246K fundus pairs, 190K X-ray pairs) are substantial public resources that address the data scarcity problem in medical vision-language pretraining. Open questions include whether the co-occurrence-based weighting generalizes to domains without clear label co-occurrence structure, whether the smooth KL divergence objective scales beyond three granularity levels, and whether the gains observed in ophthalmology transfer to other modalities (pathology, ultrasound, MRI) where multi-granularity annotations are less standardized.
