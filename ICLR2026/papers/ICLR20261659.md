## Problem

Metric-based machine-generated text detectors (e.g., DetectGPT, DNA-GPT, Log-Likelihood) compute token-level scores that are biased by the inherent randomness of LLM sampling, yet they naively aggregate these imprecise scores without correction. No existing method models the dependencies between context tokens' detection scores, leaving a systematic source of error unaddressed that limits detection accuracy, especially under cross-LLM and adversarial settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a text and the per-token detection scores produced by any existing metric-based detector (computed via a black-box proxy model such as GPT2 or GPT2-XL). It outputs calibrated per-token scores and a final text-level binary detection score. The calibration component introduces only a 2×2 reward-penalty matrix W_mrf and a single positional center t_0, making it a drop-in plug-in between the score-calculation module f_1 and the detection module f_2 of any base detector without architectural changes.

## Method

The paper first proves (Theorem 1) that attention dynamics in a simplified transformer induce two properties in token-level detection scores: Neighbor Similarity (adjacent tokens exhibit statistically lower score variance) and Initial Instability (early tokens fluctuate more). It then models these properties with a pairwise Markov Random Field whose unary potentials come from the original detector's scores and whose pairwise potentials enforce label smoothness between adjacent tokens, with a sigmoid positional weighting β(t) that downweights early positions to counteract initial instability. Via mean-field approximation, the MRF inference reduces to a lightweight iterative update rule (Eq. 8) that alternates between a softmax of the current scores and a sparse matrix multiplication with the weighted adjacency matrix, requiring only T iterations (default 10) and O(NT) complexity. The 2×2 W_mrf matrix is learned with a standard binary cross-entropy loss on a small training set, and the entire calibration layer can be stacked on top of any existing detector with negligible computational overhead.

## Results

On the Essay dataset (trained on GPT4All), the proposed MRF calibration improves average AUROC across all six metric-based detectors: Likelihood from 96.17% to 97.79%, Log-Rank from 96.03% to 97.32%, Entropy from 83.93% to 89.01%, DetectGPT from 44.09% to 91.95%, FastGPT from 69.08% to 79.92%, and DNA-GPT from 95.26% to 98.07%. On the more challenging DetectRL dataset (trained on Llama-2-70b), gains are even more pronounced for weaker detectors: Log-Rank jumps from 72.06% to 83.29% (+11.23%), and DetectGPT from 48.60% to 72.95% (+24.35%). The method introduces only a 2×2 learnable parameter matrix (W_mrf) and T=10 MRF iterations, with negligible computational overhead (O(NT) complexity).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed MRF calibration is a universal plug-in that enhances all metric-based baselines rather than competing with them directly. On Essay, the most dramatic gain is DetectGPT, which improves from 44.09% to 91.95% average AUROC (+47.86%), suggesting its underlying probability-curvature scoring is sound but severely hampered by token-level noise. Likelihood and Log-Rank (Solaiman; Gehrmann) gain 1.6–1.3% on Essay but larger gains (8.6–11.2%) on the harder DetectRL cross-LLM setting. DNA-GPT (Yang) already performs well (95.26% on Essay) and sees a modest but consistent boost to 98.07%. Fast-DetectGPT (Bao) benefits substantially on Essay (69.08%→79.92%) but shows limited gains on DetectRL (60.97%→61.68%), likely because its conditional probability curvature already partially mitigates token-level instability. In the appendix, the method also enhances newer baselines RepreGuard (Chen), Binoculars (Hans), Lastde (Xu), and FourierGPT (Xu), with improvements of 4–9% on Reuters. Against model-based methods (ChatGPT-D by Guo, MPU by Tian), the enhanced metric-based detectors remain competitive while being model-agnostic and requiring no retraining for new LLMs. The paper reports that across 888 cross-LLM evaluation settings, the enhanced model outperforms the original detector in 91.4% of cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies diverse metric-based MGT detection methods (Log-Likelihood, Log-Rank, Entropy, DetectGPT, Fast-DetectGPT, DNA-GPT) under a common framework of token-level scoring followed by aggregation and thresholding, then identifies a shared weakness: token-level scores are biased by LLM sampling randomness. The theoretical analysis of attention dynamics builds directly on Liu et al. (2023, Scissorhands), adapting their bounds on attention score evolution to reveal two score-level properties (neighbor similarity and initial instability) that prior detection work had not formalized. The proposed MRF calibration layer is conceptually distinct from prior local-pattern methods like FourierGPT and Lastde, which propose new standalone metrics, and from model-based classifiers that overfit; instead, it is a universal plug-in that calibrates the intermediate scores of any existing detector using only a 2×2 learned weight matrix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a four-parameter component that can be stacked on any token-level detector to yield large, consistent AUROC gains (e.g., DetectGPT from ~44% to ~92% on Essay) across cross-LLM, cross-domain, mixed-text, and paraphrase-attack scenarios, with negligible computational overhead. A key limitation is that the method requires token-level score outputs, excluding detectors that only produce text-level decisions, and the proxy-model dependency (GPT2/GPT2-XL) constrains deployment in truly black-box settings. Open questions include whether richer sequence models (e.g., GNNs, transformers) could further exploit the identified score relationships, and whether the sigmoid positional weighting generalizes to very short texts or non-English languages beyond the tested CUDRT dataset.
