## Problem

Existing satellite-to-ground generation methods produce individual ground-view images but fail to synthesize multiview-consistent panoramic sequences, often relying on auxiliary inputs like height maps or handcrafted projections that are difficult to obtain at scale. The extreme viewpoint disparity between top-down satellite views and street-level panoramas makes the task highly under-constrained, and maintaining both geometric alignment with the satellite image and temporal coherence across frames remains largely unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264315/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 satellite image (covering a 160m×160m area) and a predefined 4-DoF camera pose trajectory (3D position + yaw angle per frame) as input, and produces a sequence of 128×512 equirectangular ground-level panoramic images along that trajectory. At inference, DDIM sampling with 50 steps is used, and the model is fine-tuned from Stable Diffusion 1.5. The VIGOR++ dataset provides over 90,000 satellite-to-ground video pairs across 10 cities, with trajectories typically containing 7–16 frames at ~11m average intervals.

## Method

SatDreamer360 is a latent diffusion framework built on three key mechanisms. First, a triplane representation (XY, XZ, YZ planes) initialized from the satellite image via ResNet and enriched with Cross-view Hybrid Attention encodes 3D scene geometry without requiring height maps. Second, a Ray-Based Pixel Attention module maps each panoramic pixel to a 3D ray (parameterized by yaw and pitch), samples K=8 points along the ray, and retrieves triplane features via deformable attention with dynamically refined offsets, enabling geometry-aware conditioning at the pixel level. Third, an Epipolar-Constrained Attention module extends epipolar geometry to equirectangular projections, restricting inter-frame attention to points satisfying the epipolar constraint (reducing complexity from O(NHW×NHW) to O(NHW×NM) with M=4 sampled points), and uses a sparse strategy where each frame attends only to the first frame (global anchor) and the immediately preceding frame (local coherence). Training proceeds in two 300-epoch stages: single-image generation followed by sequence generation with the temporal attention module.

## Results

On the VIGOR++ multi-view sequence generation task, SatDreamer360 achieves FVD 2.101 and CLIPSIM 6.820, substantially outperforming ControlS2S (FVD 4.871, CLIPSIM 10.81) and EscherNet (FVD 7.282, CLIPSIM 8.250). It also leads on perceptual quality (FID 27.41, P_alex 0.3955), semantic alignment (DINO 4.156, SegAny 0.3563), pixel fidelity (SSIM 0.3964, PSNR 12.75), and depth consistency (5.623). On the single-image task (VIGOR), it achieves FID 21.36 and P_alex 0.3469, beating ControlS2S (FID 28.01, P_alex 0.3770) and Sat2Density (FID 54.49, P_alex 0.3898).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SatDreamer360 outperforms all three multi-view baselines (Sat2Density, EscherNet, ControlS2S) on every metric in Table 1. Against ControlS2S (the closest competitor), it reduces FVD from 4.871 to 2.101 (57% improvement) and CLIPSIM from 10.81 to 6.820, while also improving FID (29.48→27.41) and P_alex (0.4433→0.3955). EscherNet, a general multi-view diffusion model, produces the worst perceptual and pixel-level results (FID 84.21, SSIM 0.2587) due to the large satellite-to-ground domain gap, though its built-in multi-view coherence gives it better FVD (7.282) than Sat2Density (11.70). Sat2Density, a NeRF-based method, is the weakest overall across all dimensions. On the single-image task (Table 2), SatDreamer360 beats Pix2Pix (FID 67.96→21.36 on VIGOR), S2S (FID 121.1→21.36), Sat2Density (FID 54.49→21.36), and ControlS2S (FID 28.01→21.36); CrossDiff (FID 23.67) is the only method with a lower FID but reports no other metrics for direct comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the satellite-to-ground generation line of work (Sat2Density, ControlS2S, S2S) from single-view synthesis to multi-view consistent sequence generation, building on latent diffusion models (Stable Diffusion 1.5) as the generative backbone. It repurposes triplane representations from 3D generative modeling (Huang et al. 2023, Chan et al. 2022) as a geometry-aware scene encoder, and extends epipolar-constrained attention from pinhole-camera multi-view synthesis (Tseng et al. 2023) to equirectangular panoramic projections. The Cross-view Hybrid Attention mechanism from BEVFormer (Li et al. 2024c) is adapted to enrich triplane features across orthogonal planes, while the ray-based pixel attention replaces the approximate projections or height-map priors used by prior cross-view methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in simulation, autonomous driving, and digital twin city applications where continuous, geometrically consistent ground-level sequences are needed from readily available satellite imagery. The VIGOR++ dataset (90K+ cross-view video pairs across 10 cities) provides a much-needed benchmark for multi-view cross-view generation that prior single-view datasets cannot support. Key limitations include handling only static scenes (no dynamic objects), degradation on trajectories exceeding ~60 m, and dependence on Google Maps/Street View coverage that restricts generalization to off-road or unstructured environments. Open questions include whether the triplane representation can be further scaled for city-level scenes and how to incorporate dynamic elements while preserving satellite-ground geometric alignment.
