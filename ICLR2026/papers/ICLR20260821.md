## Problem

RLVR training of distilled reasoning models requires very long context windows (32K–52K tokens) during rollout, incurring prohibitive compute costs. Multi-stage training that starts short and grows the context can cause irreversible performance degradation, and even when it works, it still demands thousands of GPU hours. The paper asks whether a lightweight intermediate stage can bridge long-CoT distillation and standard RLVR while reducing rollout cost and raising the final performance ceiling.

## Input / Output

The method takes an SFT-distilled long-CoT LRM (e.g., DeepSeek-Distilled-Qwen-1.5B, Qwen3-4B, DS-7B) as the initial policy and a set of verifiable-reward text queries (Polaris-53K math problems) as training data. The key input transformation is the ThinkingFree operator, which appends a  token to the chat template so the model generates only the answer portion without an explicit thinking block, cutting rollout tokens by over 70%. The output is a trained policy that can be evaluated in both thinking mode (full CoT) and thinking-free mode (short answer), with the thinking-free mode yielding substantially fewer output tokens at modest accuracy cost.

## Method

TFPI modifies the standard RLVR objective by replacing each query x with its ThinkingFree-transformed version x′ = ThinkingFree(x) during the rollout stage, so that G responses are sampled from π_θ_old(·|x′) and the DAPO importance ratios and group-relative advantages are computed conditioned on x′. Because the ThinkingFree template omits the <think> block, rollouts are dramatically shorter (e.g., ~5K vs. ~17K tokens for DS-1.5B), which reduces compute per step and enables stable training at context lengths (4K) where standard RLVR would collapse. TFPI is run as a multi-stage schedule (e.g., 4K→8K→16K for Qwen3-4B) using the same DAPO hyperparameters as direct RL, and the resulting checkpoint serves as the initialization for a subsequent standard long-CoT RLVR stage. The paper shows that this simple template-level change accelerates convergence, raises the performance ceiling (e.g., 89.0% AIME24 for a 4B model with TFPI only), and produces more token-efficient models without any specialized length reward or architectural modification.

## Results

On Qwen3-4B, TFPI+RL achieves 80.8% AIME24, 76.0% AIME25, and 65.7% overall accuracy, outperforming Direct RL (78.8%, 71.5%, 62.0%) under matched compute. Using TFPI alone on Qwen3-4B-2507, the model reaches 89.0% AIME24 and 65.5% LiveCodeBench with less than 4K H20 GPU hours, surpassing Qwen3-235B-Thinking (85.7% AIME24) in math and code. In thinking-free inference mode, TFPI stage 3 on DS-1.5B achieves 28.5% overall accuracy with only 4.4K average output tokens, outperforming all RL-based efficient reasoning baselines (TLMRE, AdaptThink, AutoThink, Laser, L1Max, ThinkLess) on the accuracy–token Pareto frontier.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TFPI+RL consistently outperforms Direct RL under matched compute across all three model scales: Qwen3-4B gains +3.7% overall (65.7% vs 62.0%), DS-7B gains +2.5% (48.7% vs 46.2%), and DS-1.5B gains +3.0% (30.0% vs 27.0%). On the 7B scale, TFPI+RL (48.7%) matches Polaris-7B-Preview (48.3%) while using only 16K max response length versus Polaris's 16K→24K→32K schedule and ~1.5K H800 hours versus ~8K. TFPI alone on Qwen3-4B-2507 (89.0% AIME24, 65.5% LiveCodeBench) surpasses Qwen3-235B-Thinking (85.7% AIME24, 55.7% LiveCodeBench) in math and code, and approaches DeepSeek R1 (79.8% AIME24) and Seed-1.5-Thinking (86.7% AIME24) at a fraction of the model size. In token-efficiency comparisons (Table 3), TFPI stage 3 on DS-1.5B achieves 28.5% overall accuracy at 4.4K tokens, beating TLMRE (19.1% at 11.2K), AdaptThink (18.9% at 6.5K), AutoThink (20.6% at 8.7K), Laser (21.5% at 6.9K), L1Max (23.8% at 2.9K), and ThinkLess (20.2% at 11.7K) on the accuracy–token Pareto frontier. TFPI also outperforms DeepScaleR-1.5B (27.3% overall) using only TFPI without a subsequent RL stage, despite DeepScaleR employing a 24K training length.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions TFPI as a lightweight intermediate stage between SFT distillation and standard RLVR, building directly on the DAPO/GRPO algorithmic framework. It departs from multi-stage RLVR approaches (Polaris, DeepScaleR) that gradually increase context length by instead using a ThinkingFree template modification to enable effective training at much shorter lengths from the start. In contrast to efficient-reasoning methods (TLMRE, AdaptThink, AutoThink, Laser, L1, ThinkLess) that rely on specialized length-based reward shaping or adaptive thinking triggers, TFPI requires no reward modifications or complex training designs—simply appending an empty  tag to the input template during an initialization phase.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TFPI offers a practical, drop-in initialization that reduces RLVR training compute by over 80% while simultaneously raising the performance ceiling, making high-quality reasoning models accessible at a fraction of current cost. The counterintuitive finding that thinking-free training improves thinking-mode performance suggests the field may be over-investing in long-context RL when shorter, template-modified training can achieve comparable or better results. Key limitations include reliance on math-only training data (Polaris-53K), modest out-of-domain transfer, and evaluation primarily on Qwen/DeepSeek model families; whether TFPI generalizes to other architectures, modalities, or more diverse training distributions remains an open question.
