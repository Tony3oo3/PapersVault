## Problem

Existing imbalanced graph learning methods address either class imbalance or topological imbalance in isolation, yet real-world graph datasets frequently exhibit both simultaneously. No unified framework existed to jointly handle the intertwined effects of skewed class distributions and highly uneven graph-size distributions, leaving tail graphs (minority-class and small-scale) systematically underrepresented in learned representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263298/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of graph instances, each specified by an adjacency matrix and a node feature matrix, along with graph-level class labels during training. It produces a predicted class label distribution per graph via a two-layer MLP decoder applied to the first N rows of the enhanced representation matrix. The framework is backbone-agnostic: it wraps around any GNN or Graph Transformer encoder (GIN, GCN, GraphSAGE, GraphGPS, Exphormer, Graph-Mamba, SchNet, DimeNet, SphereNet), requiring only the graph-level embedding as its interface, and operates on datasets ranging from small benchmarks (PROTEINS, D&D) to a 30,660-instance air-pollution graph dataset (AirGraph) and 3D molecular conformations.

## Method

UniImb first encodes multi-scale topology via local random-walk position encodings and global Laplacian eigenvalue/eigenvector features, then applies a personalized perturbation strategy where edge-drop and feature-mask rates are learned per-graph from its average degree, giving smaller or minority-class graphs different augmentation strengths. A GNN (GIN by default) with an independent topology-encoding update stream produces graph-level representations, which are augmented via an imbalanced feature mixup that interpolates each graph's embedding with a randomly shuffled counterpart. The core contribution is the Dynamic Balanced Prototype (DBP) module: a set of K learnable prototype embeddings (K≪N) are perceived from graph representations via TopK attention, and each graph is enhanced by its most relevant prototype through a sigmoid-gated similarity score. A prototype load-balancing loss, derived from the Information Bottleneck principle (minimizing I(S;G) ≈ KL(P‖U)), uses a stop-gradient trick to iteratively adjust a modulation vector η so that prototype activation counts approach a uniform distribution, thereby equalizing the influence of tail graphs during training.

## Results

On class-imbalanced graph classification (extreme imbalance), UniImb achieves the best Macro-F1 across all four benchmark datasets: 70.44 on PROTEINS, 46.63 on D&D, 68.30 on REDDIT-B, and 76.24 on COLLAB, with corresponding Micro-F1 scores of 74.50, 83.60, 80.68, and 88.82. On topologically imbalanced datasets, UniImb similarly leads, e.g., 74.49 Macro-F1 on D&D and 77.14 on REDDIT-B. On 3D conformation datasets (MoleculeNet), UniImb achieves 60.29 Macro-F1 on HIV, 76.34 on BBBP, and 62.30 on BACE, outperforming all 3D-specific baselines. The method also demonstrates strong scalability on the large-scale AirGraph dataset (30,660 graphs) with natural long-tail imbalance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniImb outperforms all 23 baselines across every evaluated dataset and metric. Against the strongest class-imbalance baselines, G²GNN (67.70 Macro-F1 on PROTEINS) and ImbGNN (67.69), UniImb gains 2.7–4.3 points in Macro-F1, with the largest margin on REDDIT-B (+4.31 over ImbGNN) and COLLAB (+7.70 over G²GNN). Classic GNNs (GIN, GraphCL) and Graph Transformers (GraphGPS, Exphormer, Graph-Mamba) perform poorly under extreme imbalance, with GIN scoring as low as 9.99 Macro-F1 on D&D. Topology-imbalance methods (TopoImb, SOLT-GNN) are highly unstable and often underperform even naive GIN on class-imbalanced data (e.g., TopoImb scores only 15.79 on D&D vs. UniImb's 46.63). On 3D conformation datasets, UniImb (using only the DBP module) surpasses specialized 3D backbones (SchNet, DimeNet, SphereNet) and imbalance methods (G²GNN, ImbGNN) by 4–11 Macro-F1 points, confirming plug-and-play applicability across diverse graph architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of imbalanced graph learning—class-imbalance methods (G²GNN, ImGKB) that use graph-of-graphs augmentation, and topological-imbalance methods (SOLT-GNN, TopoImb, ImbGNN) that reweight or augment small graphs—into a single framework. Rather than relying on the G²G paradigm, UniImb introduces a Dynamic Balanced Prototype module grounded in the Information Bottleneck principle (Tishby et al., 2000), which extracts shared semantic prototypes and enforces uniform activation via a load-balancing loss. The graph representation pipeline additionally draws on learnable positional encodings via random walks and Laplacian spectral features, combined with a personalized perturbation strategy that scales augmentation intensity per-graph based on degree statistics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified treatment of class and topological imbalance addresses a gap the authors identify as 'unexplored,' and the plug-and-play DBP module (validated across GIN, GCN, GraphSAGE, GraphGPS, Exphormer, and Graph-Mamba) offers a practical drop-in for existing pipelines. The release of AirGraph (30,660 instances with natural long-tail imbalance) provides a much larger real-world benchmark than typical graph classification datasets. Open questions include whether the uniform-distribution prior on prototype activation remains optimal under more complex multi-modal imbalances, the computational cost of Laplacian eigendecomposition on very large graphs, and whether the framework extends to node- or edge-level imbalance settings.
