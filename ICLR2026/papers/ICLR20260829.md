## Problem

As LLMs become agentic, attackers can decompose harmful goals into sequences of individually benign subtasks that collectively achieve the malicious objective, bypassing existing safety alignment that only inspects each prompt in isolation. Prior decomposition-attack work focused on stateless QA settings, did not release the actual decomposed prompts for evaluation, and proposed no robust defense. This paper addresses the gap by building the first diverse benchmark of stateful decomposition attacks and demonstrating that a simple external monitor can detect the hidden long-range intent in real time.

## Input / Output

At each step i of a multi-turn agent conversation, the monitor receives the full cumulative prompt history C_i = [p_1, …, p_i] as a variable-length list of strings, concatenated with a prompt-engineered rubric (e.g., 8 in-context examples or a chain-of-thought instruction). It returns a probability of harmful intent s_i ∈ [0,1], which is thresholded (threshold tuned on a validation set) into a binary flag y_i: 1 means 'flag and halt the agent immediately,' 0 means 'allow and continue.' Each task in the benchmark carries a pre-designated harmful index marking the step at which actual harm materializes, enabling precise TP/TN/FP/FN accounting.

## Method

The core mechanism is a sequential monitoring framework: after every new sub-prompt, a lightweight LLM (GPT-4o-mini, Llama-3.1-8B, or GPT-4.1-nano) inspects the entire cumulative context and decides whether the trajectory is harmful, halting the agent the moment harm is detected. No model weights are trained; instead, the authors perform a prompt-as-hyperparameter sweep over in-context learning examples (drawn from agent, image, or QA tasks), chain-of-thought reasoning, safety guidelines, and hypothesis-generation prompts, selecting the best configuration per model on a unified validation set. The key finding is that a single task-specific ICL prompt generalizes well across all three task types—QA ICL for GPT-4o-mini and Agent ICL for Llama-3.1-8B—lifting F1 from ~0.70–0.79 (zero-shot) to 0.88–0.92 without any fine-tuning. This optimized lightweight monitor outperforms expensive references (o3-mini at 0.836 F1, GPT-4o at 0.870) and the strong guardrail Llama-Guard-4-12B (0.262 macro F1) while reducing cost by ~90% and latency by ~50%, and remains robust under random subtask injection, extreme obfuscation with 160+ subtasks, and automated PAIR-based red teaming up to 50 iterations.

## Results

On the DecomposedHarm benchmark, the optimized lightweight GPT-4o-mini (QA Task ICL prompt) achieves an F1 of 0.918 and a 93% Defense Success Rate, outperforming all expensive reference models: o3-mini (F1 0.836), GPT-4o (F1 0.870), and GPT-5 (F1 0.887). The optimized Llama-3.1-8B (Agent Task ICL) reaches F1 0.881 and 89% DSR. Compared to o3-mini, the optimized GPT-4o-mini reduces cost by ~90% ($1.39×10⁻⁴ vs $1.38×10⁻³ per task) and latency by ~89% (0.44s vs 3.98s per prompt). Against the strong guardrail Llama-Guard-4-12B, the optimized GPT-4o-mini achieves a macro F1 of 0.926 versus 0.262, a +0.664 improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Llama-Guard-4-12B (the primary guardrail baseline), the optimized GPT-4o-mini sequential monitor achieves a macro F1 of 0.926 versus Llama-Guard-4's 0.262, a +0.664 absolute improvement, with the gap being especially stark on image tasks (1.000 vs 0.119) and QA tasks (0.883 vs 0.012). The optimized GPT-4o-mini also surpasses all expensive reference models: it beats o3-mini by +0.082 F1 (0.918 vs 0.836), GPT-4o by +0.048 F1 (0.918 vs 0.870), and GPT-5 by +0.031 F1 (0.918 vs 0.887), while costing roughly 10× less than o3-mini and 73× less than GPT-5 per task. Under adversarial stress tests (random subtask injection up to 100%, massive obfuscation with 160 subtasks, and 50-iteration PAIR automated red teaming), the optimized GPT-4o-mini maintains F1 ≥ 0.87 and DSR ≥ 0.81, whereas o3-mini (without PAIR) only reaches F1 0.83 and DSR 0.72. The optimized Llama-3.1-8B also outperforms o3-mini and GPT-4o on DSR (89% vs ~73–77%) under random injection, confirming that prompt-engineered lightweight monitors are both more accurate and more robust than expensive alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decomposition-attack literature (DRAttack, Imposter.AI, Glukhov et al.) from stateless QA demonstrations to stateful agentic settings (tool-using agents, text-to-image pipelines) and, critically, from vulnerability demonstration to practical defense. It builds on AgentHarm for the agent-task subset and on Llama-Guard and Constitutional Classifiers as monitoring baselines, but departs from both by showing that purpose-built guardrails (Llama-Guard-4) fail catastrophically on sequential decomposition (0.262 macro F1) while a prompt-engineered lightweight model succeeds. The methodological core—treating monitoring as a binary sequence-labeling problem solved via prompt-as-hyperparameter search (ICL, CoT, safety guidelines) on a validation set—is a repurposing of standard LLM prompting techniques rather than a new architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that real-time safety monitoring for agentic LLMs need not rely on expensive reasoning models: a $0.000014/prompt GPT-4o-mini with QA ICL examples achieves 93% defense success while cutting cost by 90% and latency by 50% versus o3-mini, making deployment economically feasible. The released DecomposedHarm dataset (4,634 tasks across three modalities) is the first comprehensive public benchmark for this attack class. Open questions include generalization beyond English and the three tested task types, handling of stateless multi-context attacks (where users split queries across different model sessions), and adding explainability so developers can understand *why* a sequence was flagged. The paper also notes that the decomposition attack is highly scalable, suggesting other similarly structured attack families may exist and remain underexplored.
