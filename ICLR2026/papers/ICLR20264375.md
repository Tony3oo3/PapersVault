## Problem

Existing conformal prediction methods for right-censored survival data provide only PAC-type (approximate) miscoverage guarantees rather than exact marginal coverage, and none of them construct lower predictive bounds (LPBs) for counterfactual survival outcomes under different treatment regimes in the general right-censored setting. This paper bridges both gaps by delivering an exact, distribution-free marginal coverage guarantee for counterfactual survival LPBs under general right-censoring.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264375/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dataset of patient covariates X (124 clinical and radiomic features in the real-data application), binary treatment assignment W, observed censored survival time T̃ = min(T, C), event indicator e, and a nominal miscoverage level α. It outputs a calibrated LPB of the form q̂_τ*(w)(X) − c_{1−α}^{(w)}(τ*), where τ* is chosen to maximize the LPB while preserving the coverage guarantee. The LPB is a single scalar per patient per treatment, providing a conservative lower bound on survival time under a specified counterfactual treatment.

## Method

The core insight is a reweighting identity that, under strong ignorability, upper-bounds the counterfactual miscoverage probability by a weighted expectation over only the uncensored (e=1) observations from the target treatment group, with weight ω(x) = 1/γ(x) where γ(x) = p(W=w, e=1|x). This transforms the problem into a standard weighted conformal inference task: the non-conformity score V_i^(w) = q̂_τ^(w)(X_i) − T̃_i is calibrated via the weighted quantile procedure of Lei & Candès (2021), yielding an exact marginal coverage guarantee that degrades gracefully with the L1 error of the estimated density ratio. The quantile function q̂_τ^(w) is estimated by any censored quantile regression method (CQR, CQR forest, or CQR neural networks) trained on the treatment-specific subset. The procedure is doubly robust: valid asymptotic coverage is maintained if either the weight function γ(x) or the quantile estimator is consistently estimated, providing mutual compensation between the two components.

## Results

On synthetic data (6 settings, 3000 samples, 50 trials), the proposed method achieves empirical coverage closest to the nominal 90% level while producing the highest LPB among all methods with valid coverage guarantees. In Table 1 (Setting 4), the optimized LPB at α=0.10 is 0.803 (vs. 0.778 at τ=α), with average coverage 0.914; at α=0.05 the optimized LPB is 0.503 with coverage 0.958. On the real NSCLC dataset (541 patients, 124 features), the method identifies VMAT as superior to IMRT and induction+concurrent chemotherapy as yielding higher LPBs, consistent with prior clinical evidence. Under outlier contamination (10% of data), the method maintains the 90% coverage guarantee while PAC-type baselines (Focus, Fused) fail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: uncalibrated (uncab), naive calibration, and the focused and fused calibration methods from Davidov et al. (2025), which provide only PAC-type (not exact marginal) coverage guarantees. Across all six synthetic settings, the proposed method achieves coverage rates closest to the nominal 90% target while simultaneously producing the largest LPB among methods that guarantee valid coverage. In Settings 3, 4, and 5, the method matches the fused approach's coverage but yields significantly larger (less conservative) LPB values. Under outlier contamination in Setting 4, the proposed method maintains the 90% marginal coverage guarantee, whereas the Focus and Fused baselines (PAC-type) fail to achieve it. On the real NSCLC dataset, the method's LPBs align with known clinical prognostic factors (stage, KPS, tumor volume), demonstrating practical informativeness beyond the synthetic comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conformal prediction framework for survival analysis (Candès et al., 2023; Gui et al., 2024; Davidov et al., 2025) to the counterfactual setting by combining it with weighted conformal inference for covariate shift (Lei & Candès, 2021) and the non-conformity score from conformalized quantile regression (Romano et al., 2019). The key departure is a reweighting scheme that transforms the counterfactual LPB problem under general right-censoring into a standard weighted conformal calibration problem, yielding exact marginal coverage guarantees where prior methods (Gui et al., 2024; Davidov et al., 2025) only achieved PAC-type guarantees. The paper also extends from Type-I censoring (where censoring time is known) to the more general right-censored setting where only the observed time and event indicator are available.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exact marginal coverage guarantee (as opposed to PAC-type) is practically important in high-stakes clinical settings where rare or extreme patient subpopulations must be protected, and the doubly robust property provides resilience against misspecification of either the weight function or the quantile regressor. The clinical application to lung cancer radiochemotherapy demonstrates that the LPB correlates with known prognostic factors (stage, KPS, tumor volume), supporting its use in personalized treatment selection. Open questions include the validity under violations of the ignorability and SUTVA assumptions, performance under extreme treatment imbalance or high censoring rates, and whether the LPB can be extended to quantitative causal effect estimation beyond binary treatment comparisons.
