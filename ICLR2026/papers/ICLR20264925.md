## Problem

Small language models (SLMs) with 3B–7B parameters struggle with contextual reasoning tasks because their limited parameter capacity causes them to lose track of critical information in dense, multi-step contexts, and they suffer from catastrophic forgetting. Existing remedies—knowledge distillation, data synthesis, and supervised fine-tuning—still require additional training and face inherent limitations in generalizing across diverse reasoning domains. The paper asks whether LLM-derived contextual processing expertise can be transferred to SLMs in a training-free manner that avoids these pitfalls.

## Input / Output

The method takes a task query paired with an information-dense context (e.g., a multi-step narrative for MuSR, a set of supporting facts for StrategyQA, or multiple Wikipedia passages for HotpotQA) and produces a concise reasoning answer. The SLM performs both template instantiation and inference in a single end-to-end generation step, so the output is a structured reasoning chain that explicitly references the critical information types identified by the retrieved Navigation template, rather than a free-form response. An independent embedding model (e.g., e5-mistral-7b-instruct or MPNet) handles template retrieval, decoupling the matching mechanism from both the SLM and LLM architectures.

## Method

Navigation is a training-free, three-stage framework that distills LLM contextual processing expertise into generalizable, reusable Navigation templates stored in a scalable database. In the Generation stage, an LLM completes a full reasoning cycle on a sample, identifies the critical information types needed, and abstracts them into a structured template containing a Task Category, archived Task Scenarios, and concise Task Guidance directives (e.g., 'Alibi Credibility: corroborated presence via witnesses or digital records'). In the Utilization stage, an embedding model retrieves the most relevant template via cosine similarity against the current query-context pair, and the SLM follows the template's step-by-step directives to locate and extract only task-relevant information, constructing a reasoning chain that filters out irrelevant context. In the Update stage, when similarity scores fall below a threshold or an existing template proves inadequate, the LLM is invoked to generate or refine a new template, dynamically expanding the database without any SLM retraining. The key distinction from prior distillation or prompting methods is that Navigation transfers abstract reasoning strategies—what to look for and how to process—rather than textual outputs or pseudo-labels, requiring a template database of no more than 2.1% of the dataset size to achieve an average 10.7% accuracy gain.

## Results

Navigation yields an average 10.7% accuracy gain over vanilla SLMs across MuSR, StrategyQA, and HotpotQA, using only 8–21 templates (≤2.1% of dataset size). On Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, Navigation (GPT-5.1) lifts HotpotQA EM from 34.9% to 51.8% (+16.9) and MuSR average from 43.7% to 54.1% (+10.4). Notably, both Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct with Navigation surpass GPT-3.5-Turbo (175B) on MuSR and HotpotQA, while Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct with Navigation outperforms GPT-3.5-Turbo across all datasets and metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Navigation consistently outperforms all three baselines (COT, SLEICL, SFT) across every SLM backbone and dataset. Against COT (Wei et al., 2022b), Navigation gains +7.4 to +16.9 points on HotpotQA EM and +4.4 to +11.7 on MuSR, because COT activates multi-step reasoning but fails to mitigate context distractions. Against SLEICL (Chen et al., 2024), Navigation achieves substantially higher StrategyQA F1 (53.5 vs. 18.4 on Qwen2.5-3B) and HotpotQA EM (51.8 vs. 37.1), while requiring only 3% of SLEICL's LLM call frequency (16 vs. 502 calls on MuSR). Against LoRA-based SFT (Hu et al., 2022), Navigation wins on HotpotQA EM by +14.1 to +18.3 points and on MuSR OP by +18.0 to +26.6 points, without any training overhead. The most striking result is that 3B SLMs with Navigation surpass the 175B GPT-3.5-Turbo on MuSR (e.g., Llama-3.2-3B + Nav achieves 55.5 avg. vs. 49.1 for GPT-3.5-Turbo) and HotpotQA EM (53.3 vs. 44.4), demonstrating that structured LLM-derived guidance can compensate for a 58× parameter gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

Navigation sits at the intersection of retrieval-augmented generation, structured prompt engineering, and (training-free) knowledge distillation. It departs from standard distillation pipelines (CoT distillation, SFT via LoRA) by never updating SLM parameters, and from SLEICL/Grimoire by distilling abstract reasoning *strategies* into a small set of reusable templates rather than relying on per-query LLM-generated demonstrations. The theoretical motivation draws on Brown et al.'s memorization lower bounds and Kang et al.'s knowledge-augmented reasoning distillation, positioning the Navigation database as a non-parametric external knowledge base that circumvents the Ω(nd) memory requirement of parametric distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a 3B model with a handful of retrieved templates can match or exceed a 175B model on contextual reasoning benchmarks without any fine-tuning, which is directly relevant to edge and on-device deployment. However, the template-matching mechanism struggles with high-complexity domains (math, code), where LLM call frequency balloons to 10–71% of dataset size, suggesting the approach is currently best suited to narrative and knowledge-intensive reasoning rather than algorithmic problem-solving. Open questions include whether more efficient matching (beyond cosine similarity on task scenarios) can extend Navigation to thinking-intensive tasks, and whether the template database can be pre-built offline to eliminate the online LLM dependency entirely.
