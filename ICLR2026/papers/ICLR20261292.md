## Problem

Multi-turn LLM conversations accumulate large KV caches that occupy scarce GPU memory, forcing operators to either retain stale caches (reducing throughput for other users), discard them (incurring quadratic recomputation cost), or offload them (incurring transfer overhead). Existing compression methods—token eviction, per-token quantization, and per-prompt SVD—either degrade accuracy at useful compression ratios, require expensive per-request decomposition, or cannot be combined for compounded gains. The paper asks whether a single, generalizable transform can compress KV caches to a fraction of their size for storage and transfer while preserving downstream task accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 16-bit KV cache tensors (keys and values separately) spanning all layers, heads, and token positions, along with a one-time calibration dataset of ~160K–200K tokens (a 50/50 mix of FineWeb text and OpenR1-Math reasoning traces). Before compression, RoPE rotations are undone and the most recent 128 tokens (sliding window) plus the first 4 tokens (attention sinks) are excluded. The output is a compact bitstream of quantized PCA coefficients followed by DEFLATE entropy coding, which can be stored in GPU HBM, CPU DRAM, or NVMe. Decompression reverses the pipeline layer-by-layer, allowing generation to begin before the full cache is reconstructed. The same PCA basis V is reused across all requests and all target compression ratios; only the DP-computed bit allocation changes.

## Method

kvtc is a three-stage transform coder adapted from classical media compression: (1) feature decorrelation via a single orthonormal basis V obtained from SVD of centered, cross-layer/cross-head concatenated KV caches on calibration data, motivated by the finding that key heads inhabit a shared subspace up to orthogonal rotation (verified via Procrustes alignment); (2) adaptive scalar quantization in the PCA domain, where a dynamic programming algorithm assigns variable bit widths to principal components under a global bit budget, with group-based 16-bit shift/scale factors (inspired by Microscaling) and zero bits allocated to trailing components; (3) lossless DEFLATE entropy coding executed in parallel on GPU via nvCOMP. Calibration is a one-time step (~10 min on an H100) that produces V and the bit allocation; at inference, compression runs between prefill and decode or between decode steps, while the model always operates on fully decompressed caches. This design achieves ~20× average compression (up to 64×) with <1 point accuracy loss across math, knowledge, and long-context benchmarks, outperforming quantization (KIVI, GEAR), eviction (H2O, TOVA), and SVD (xKV) baselines at comparable or higher compression ratios.

## Results

On Llama 3.1 8B, kvtc at 16× target compression (actual CR 18–22 after DEFLATE) maintains accuracy within <1 point of vanilla across all tasks: GSM8K 56.9 vs 56.8, MMLU 60.1 vs 60.5, Qasper 40.7 vs 40.4, LITM 99.3 vs 99.4, RULER-VT 99.1 vs 99.8. At 32× (CR 34–44), kvtc still achieves GSM8K 57.8, MMLU 60.6, and LITM 99.1, while at 64× (CR 60–88) it retains GSM8K 57.2 and MMLU 60.7. On reasoning models (Qwen 2.5 R1 7B), kvtc at 8× achieves AIME24 52.5±3.6 vs vanilla 50.9±4.9 and LiveCodeBench 36.5 vs 36.7, and on Llama 3.3 70B, kvtc at 20× retains MATH-500 72.6 vs 75.6 with perfect NIAH and LITM scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

kvtc at 16× (CR ~20) decisively outperforms eviction baselines H2O and TOVA, which collapse on long-context retrieval (LITM: 20.2 and 1.2 vs kvtc's 99.3) and MMLU (44.3 and 44.8 vs 60.1) at comparable 8× CR. Against quantization baselines GEAR and KIVI (both at 5× CR), kvtc at 16× achieves higher compression while matching or exceeding accuracy on all tasks (e.g., LITM 99.3 vs 96.9/88.8). xKV (CR 1–5) is the strongest baseline on most tasks but degrades notably on Qasper (35.6 vs kvtc 8×'s 40.1), while kvtc at 8× (CR 9–10) matches xKV on GSM8K and MMLU at roughly 2× the compression ratio. FP8 (2× CR) is close to vanilla but kvtc at 8× achieves 4–5× higher compression with comparable accuracy. On reasoning tasks, kvtc 8× on Qwen 2.5 R1 7B scores 52.5±3.6 on AIME24 and 36.5 on LiveCodeBench, outperforming DMS 8× (50.0 and 33.4) while achieving a defined 9–11× compression ratio.

## Relation to Prior Work

kvtc transplants the classical transform-coding pipeline (decorrelate → quantize → entropy-code) from image/video codecs (JPEG, H.264) into the KV cache compression setting. It departs from prior SVD-based methods (SVDq, xKV) by computing a single PCA basis once on calibration data rather than per-prompt, and from quantization methods (KIVI, GEAR) by operating in the PCA-transformed domain with dynamic-programming bit allocation rather than fixed per-channel or per-token precision. The Procrustes alignment analysis showing that key heads share a common subspace up to rotation provides the theoretical justification for cross-head, cross-layer concatenation before PCA, a step absent from prior per-head or per-layer approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KV cache retention is a first-order bottleneck in production LLM serving, and a 20× compression with <1 score-point degradation directly changes the economics of cache lifetimes and offloading bandwidth. The method's compatibility with token eviction (H2O, TOVA, DMS) and its model-agnostic calibration (one PCA matrix per model, ~10 min on H100) make it a practical drop-in for serving stacks like vLLM or LMCache. Open questions include whether inference can proceed directly in the PCA space (avoiding decompression), how the method scales beyond 70B and beyond 200K calibration tokens, and whether the Frobenius-norm reconstruction error reliably predicts task-level accuracy across all domains.
