## Problem

Open-vocabulary object detection models suffer substantial performance degradation under domain shifts, and existing continual learning approaches are order-sensitive, require sequential retraining, and fail to explicitly preserve the pre-trained model's original capability. Model editing—proven effective in LLMs—offers a natural lightweight alternative for injecting new knowledge while retaining prior abilities, but its adaptation to OVOD raises unique challenges around knowledge representation and balancing new versus old information across varying task scales.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262273/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pre-trained OVOD model (Grounding DINO or GLIP) and takes a small K-shot support set (K ∈ {1,5,10,30,50} annotated images per class) from each OOD target domain as the only training signal; query sets and the base COCO dataset are used solely for evaluation. It produces a single updated FFN output-projection weight matrix per layer that simultaneously handles all accumulated tasks, yielding bounding boxes with class labels at inference. Storage is a fixed ~1.3 GB of compact covariance statistics (K^T K, K^T V) independent of the number of tasks, and the final closed-form solve completes in under 10 seconds.

## Method

ABME fine-tunes only the FFN output projection W_out on each task's support set, then extracts key-value pairs (intermediate activations as keys, projected outputs as values) from the adapted model to form task-specific knowledge matrices. Instead of storing full KV matrices, it accumulates compact second-order statistics (K^T K, K^T V) across tasks, enabling order-agnostic insertion or removal of tasks without retraining. The core innovation is a data-adaptive regularization objective min_W ‖KW−V‖²_F + ‖Γ(W−W₀)‖²_F where Γ = diag(s_i^{1/4}) and s_i is the accumulated feature energy per dimension; this eliminates the need for a manually tuned trade-off hyperparameter by automatically scaling regularization strength with feature magnitude. The solution is obtained in closed form as W* = (K^T K + Γ²)^{-1}(K^T V + Γ² W₀), solved via a standard SPD linear-system solver. Theoretical analysis shows the Γ^{1/4} exponent provides sub-quadratic scaling relative to the data-term curvature, ensuring sufficient plasticity for high-energy features while maintaining stability for low-energy ones.

## Results

On the CDFSOD benchmark (6 cross-domain detection datasets, 5-shot), ABME achieves 38.5 mAP on new tasks and 57.0 mAP on COCO, yielding a Retention Ratio (RR) of 95.5% and an Adaptation Gain Ratio (AGR) of 97.2% relative to independent FFN fine-tuning. On ODinW-13 (13 datasets, 5-shot), it reaches 61.0 mAP with RR 97.0% and AGR 97.0%. Across all 19 combined datasets (5-shot), ABME attains 53.0 average mAP with RR 97.2% and AGR 95.5%, closely approaching the per-dataset fine-tuning oracle (55.5 mAP). On GLIP (a different OVOD architecture), the method generalizes with 97.8% RR and 94.6% AGR on CDFSOD 5-shot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ABME consistently outperforms all detection baselines on the Adaptation Gain Ratio (AGR), the metric measuring how close the edited model gets to independent per-dataset fine-tuning. On CDFSOD 5-shot, ABME achieves 97.2% AGR versus 78.3% for EWC, 73.5% for Adam-NSCL, 62.9% for SD-LoRA, 82.3% for SGP, and 83.8% for SVFCL — gaps of 14–34 percentage points. On the Retention Ratio (RR), ABME (95.5%) is competitive with EWC (95.6%) and Adam-NSCL (96.8%) while substantially exceeding SD-LoRA (90.8%), SGP (77.2%), and SVFCL (88.6%). In the CLIP classification generalization experiment (11 datasets, 5-shot), ABME reaches 74.1% accuracy, surpassing WiSE-FT (71.9%) and ZSCL (67.4%), and recovering ~92% of the FFN fine-tuning oracle (80.4%). The only setting where a baseline edges out ABME on retention is Adam-NSCL on ODinW-13 1-shot (RR 99.8% vs. 98.0%), but ABME still leads on AGR (95.9% vs. 90.6%) and overall average mAP (57.9 vs. 54.7).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the key-value model editing paradigm from LLMs (ROME, MEND, AlphaEdit) into open-vocabulary object detection, leveraging the shared insight that FFN layers serve as the primary locus of knowledge storage in transformers. It departs from traditional continual learning for OVOD (EWC, Adam-NSCL, SD-LoRA) by replacing sequential, order-sensitive parameter updates with a closed-form solution over compact KV covariance statistics, enabling order-agnostic task insertion and removal without retraining. The auto-balanced regularization (Γ = diag(s_i^{1/4})) is a novel data-adaptive mechanism that eliminates the λ hyperparameter required by prior editing and regularization-based CL methods, drawing on curvature analysis to justify the sub-quadratic scaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practically attractive alternative to sequential continual learning pipelines for OVOD: a single edited model handles 19 heterogeneous tasks with ~97% adaptation and ~97% retention, and new tasks can be added or removed by simply updating stored covariance matrices in under 10 seconds. However, the approach is limited to editing FFN output projection layers, and the evaluation, while broad (19 datasets, 2 architectures, 5 shot levels), relies on a relatively small set of CL baselines; it remains unclear how the method behaves under truly conflicting task knowledge or at much larger task volumes beyond 90. The auto-balancing mechanism's theoretical justification (curvature-based scaling) is elegant but the exponent choice (1/4) is validated only on a single benchmark, leaving open whether the optimal scaling is architecture- or domain-dependent.
