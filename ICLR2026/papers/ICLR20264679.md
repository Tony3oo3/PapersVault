## Problem

Existing symmetry-based methods for combinatorial generalization learn group actions exclusively from observed training data, so the learned symmetries are confined to the observed region and cannot be extended to unseen combinations of semantic factors. The paper formalizes this as the 'symmetry generalization' challenge: given a symmetry group learned on a partial data manifold, how to extrapolate its structure to unobserved regions without access to the full global group.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264679/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder maps an input image (64×64 for dSprites/3D Shapes, 64×64 for MPI3D) or point cloud (15k training / 3k test points on a sphere) to a pair of coefficient vectors (c_k, c_p) that parameterize a Lie algebra element P in the learned p-subspace. The conditional Flow Matching decoder then generates a sample from noise by integrating a vector field v(x, t, P) conditioned on P. The GSC loss additionally produces a synthetic 'unseen' candidate by taking a single Euler step from the original input along the vector field conditioned on −P, which is then re-encoded to check consistency with −P.

## Method

CartanFM embeds the latent space with the structure of a symmetric space G/K by identifying it with the tangent space at an origin, which corresponds to the p-subspace of a Cartan decomposition g = k ⊕ p. Learnable basis matrices {K_i} and {P_j} parameterize the two subspaces, and the Cartan Loss enforces the required Lie bracket closure relations ([k,k]⊆k, [k,p]⊆p, [p,p]⊆k) via projection-error penalties on pairwise brackets. The generative backbone is a UNet-based conditional Flow Matching model whose vector field is conditioned on the tangent vector P through AdaGN layers. The key generalization mechanism is the Geodesic Symmetry Consistency (GSC) Loss: an observed sample is encoded to p, the negated vector −p is used to generate a synthetic candidate via a one-step ODE approximation, and the re-encoded candidate is penalized for deviating from −p, thereby forcing the encoder to respect geodesic symmetry (reflection through the origin) and extrapolate the learned algebraic structure to unseen data regions.

## Results

On the combinatorial generalization benchmarks, CartanFM achieves the lowest MSE across all three datasets (dSprites, 3D Shapes, MPI3D) and both evaluation settings (R2E and R2R). On MPI3D, the most complex benchmark, CartanFM reaches MSE as low as 0.50–0.76 in R2R, compared to 6.29–9.35 for VAE and 7.71–8.42 for MAGANet. On dSprites R2E, CartanFM scores 1.10–2.49 MSE versus 7.25–14.96 for VAE and 10.63–19.49 for MAGANet. The sphere manifold reconstruction experiment (Chamfer distance) further validates the geometric claims: CartanFM+GSC achieves 0.0061 error on the unseen 90° arc, versus 0.0601 for Vanilla VAE and 0.5763 for Cartan+VAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CartanFM outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and evaluation setting. Against VAE (Kingma & Welling, 2013), CartanFM reduces MSE by roughly 3–12× (e.g., 0.50 vs. 9.35 on MPI3D R2R Case3). Against β-VAE (Higgins et al., 2016), the gap is even wider, with β-VAE(β=8) reaching MSE of 67.03 on dSprites R2R Case3 versus CartanFM's 5.87. Against GAGA (Sun et al., 2025), evaluated only on dSprites, CartanFM achieves 2.21/1.10/2.49 MSE in R2E versus GAGA's 17.17/17.56/25.69. Against CLGVAE (Zhu et al., 2021), the symmetry-aware baseline, CartanFM is dramatically superior—CLGVAE reaches MSE of 236.28 on dSprites R2R Case3 while CartanFM scores 5.87, suggesting CLGVAE's commutativity assumption is harmful. Against MAGANet (Hwang et al., 2023), the closest symmetry-based competitor, CartanFM wins on all 18 test cases; the margin is largest on MPI3D (e.g., 0.51 vs. 8.42 in R2R Case3) and most moderate on 3D Shapes R2R Case2 (33.87 vs. 37.79), where both methods struggle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2022) as its generative backbone and adapts the Lie algebra encoder concept from CLGVAE (Zhu et al., 2021) by removing the commutativity constraint. It departs from prior symmetry-based CG methods (MAGANet, CLGVAE) that learn group actions exclusively from observed data, by instead imposing the full algebraic structure of a symmetric space (via Cartan decomposition) on the latent manifold and using geodesic symmetry as a self-supervisory signal to extrapolate that structure to unseen regions. The key conceptual departure is treating the latent space not merely as a group orbit but as a symmetric space G/K, where the Cartan decomposition provides a principled local algebraic structure and geodesic symmetry provides a concrete generalization mechanism that does not require knowledge of the global group.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and formalizes a concrete failure mode of symmetry-based CG methods (symmetries learned on observed data cannot reach unseen combinations) and proposes a geometrically principled solution. However, all experiments are conducted on relatively small, synthetic or semi-synthetic benchmarks (dSprites, 3D Shapes, MPI3D), leaving open whether the symmetric space assumption holds for complex real-world data and whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional or more heterogeneous factor spaces. The ablation revealing that the Cartan algebraic structure is essentially useless without a compatible generative process (Cartan+VAE fails catastrophically while Cartan+FM succeeds) is a useful design insight, but the broader question of when and why data manifolds admit symmetric space structure remains unaddressed.
