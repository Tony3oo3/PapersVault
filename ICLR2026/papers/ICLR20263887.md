## Problem

Current VLMs and medical agentic systems treat clinical diagnosis as a one-hop visual question-answering task, producing empirical conclusions from internal knowledge without quantitative clinical indicators, guideline-grounded reasoning, or dynamic tool orchestration. This gap between AI output and the structured, evidence-based workflow of real clinical practice undermines reliability and clinical usability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263887/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes multi-modal patient data (2D fundus images at 2124×2056, 3D echocardiography sampled as 3 random slices repeated 10×, chest X-rays, CT/MRI, cell images) together with a disease query string. It outputs a structured diagnostic plan (JSON with sequential action steps), per-step clinical indicators (e.g., cup-to-disc ratio, LVEF, cardiothoracic ratio), a weighted risk score ρ compared against a threshold θ, and a final binary or multi-class diagnosis. On the NEJM benchmark, the task is framed as multiple-choice where the system builds a plan per candidate option and integrates evidence to select the answer.

## Method

MedAgent-Pro is a hierarchical, zero-shot agentic workflow with two levels. At the disease level, a RAG agent retrieves top-5 guideline chunks from a MedlinePlus knowledge base (1,000+ diseases, 4,000+ NIH/NLM-authenticated guidelines) via PubMedBERT embeddings, and a VLM (GPT-4o) summarizes them into a standardized diagnostic plan P stored as JSON, where each step maps an action to a specific tool (MedSAM, Cellpose, GitHub Copilot) with defined input/output data properties. At the patient level, the VLM orchestrates step-by-step execution: qualitative analysis is performed directly by the VLM, while quantitative analysis invokes specialized tools (e.g., segmentation → mask → code-based ratio computation). After each step, an evidence-based reflection mechanism assigns a status (Continue/Terminate/Complete) based on a VLM-implemented state assessment function φ that checks input quality and result plausibility, halting the pipeline if an unreliable intermediate result is detected. The final diagnosis is a weighted sum of completed indicators with weights assigned by the VLM according to clinical importance from the retrieved guideline, compared to a risk threshold.

## Results

On REFUGE2 (glaucoma), MedAgent-Pro achieves 90.4% bAcc and 76.4% F1, outperforming GPT-4o (56.4% bAcc, 21.1% F1) by 34.0% and 55.3% respectively. On MITEA (heart disease), it reaches 77.8% bAcc and 72.3% F1 versus GPT-4o's 56.8% and 28.1%. On the NEJM multi-disease benchmark (992 cases, 10+ modalities, 50+ diseases), MedAgent-Pro attains 81.7% overall accuracy, surpassing the best agentic baseline MDAgent (73.8%) by 7.9%. On MIMIC chest X-ray (12 sub-tasks), it achieves 72.0% average bAcc, exceeding GPT-4o (58.3%) by 13.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedAgent-Pro decisively outperforms all 13 baselines across every dataset and metric. Against general VLMs, the largest gap is with GPT-4o: +34.0% bAcc and +55.3% F1 on glaucoma, +21.0% bAcc and +44.2% F1 on heart disease, driven by quantitative tool-based analysis (e.g., cup-to-disc ratio, LVEF) that VLMs cannot perform. Among medical agentic systems, MDAgent (NeurIPS'24) is the strongest baseline at 56.8% bAcc on glaucoma, yet MedAgent-Pro still leads by 33.6% bAcc, attributed to its disease-specific RAG-guided planning rather than static tool pipelines. On task-specific models, MedAgent-Pro (zero-shot VLM) surpasses VisionUnite (85.8% bAcc) and RetiZero (50.8% bAcc) on glaucoma, and CheXagent (69.1% avg bAcc) and Maira-2 (64.1% avg bAcc) on MIMIC chest X-ray, achieving 72.0% average bAcc. Notably, even in NEJM cases without visual tool support, MedAgent-Pro (84.0% acc) still outperforms MDAgent (77.5%) and GPT-4o (74.7%), confirming the workflow structure itself provides robustness beyond tool access.

## Relation to Prior Work

MedAgent-Pro carves new ground by synthesizing three previously separate threads—retrieval-augmented generation for clinical guidelines, specialized visual tools (segmentation, grounding, code execution), and evidence-based reflection—into a single hierarchical agentic workflow that mirrors the two-stage clinical process (disease-level planning → patient-level step-by-step reasoning). It departs from prior medical agentic systems (MedAgents, MMedAgent, MDAgent) that apply fixed tool pipelines regardless of disease, and from general VLMs that treat diagnosis as one-hop VQA, by making the diagnostic plan disease-specific and the tool invocation dynamic. The RAG component draws on MedlinePlus guidelines indexed with PubMedBERT, while the toolset borrows from the medical imaging literature (MedSAM, Cellpose, Medical SAM-Adapter) and LLM-based coding (GitHub Copilot), but the key novelty is the orchestration architecture rather than any individual component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete gap in medical AI: current VLMs and agentic systems produce empirical, one-shot conclusions without the quantitative, guideline-grounded reasoning that clinical regulation demands, limiting their real-world deployability. The large performance margins (e.g., 34% bAcc over GPT-4o on glaucoma) and clinician evaluations suggest the structured workflow is a viable path toward clinically trustworthy AI diagnosis. However, the framework's dependence on domain-specific visual tools restricts its applicability to imaging modalities where such tools exist, and qualitative reasoning steps remain vulnerable to VLM hallucination—two limitations the authors acknowledge but do not resolve. Open questions include whether the hierarchical planning generalizes to rare diseases with sparse guideline coverage and whether the evidence-based reflection mechanism can be made more robust without task-specific tuning.
