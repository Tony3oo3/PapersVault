## Problem

LLM agents performing multi-turn active reasoning under RL suffer from belief deviation: their internal state estimates drift from ground truth, causing them to enter a Belief Trap Region where actions become uninformative and reasoning stalls. This belief-trap dynamics systematically distorts credit assignment in standard policy optimization (e.g., GAE), inverting the gradient direction for early informative actions and hindering exploration. No existing RL framework explicitly detects or mitigates this belief-trap failure mode.

## Input / Output

T3 operates as a meta-wrapper on top of existing RL algorithms (PPO, GRPO, GSPO), consuming LLM agent rollout trajectories and task-specific proxy signals (e.g., hypothesis set cardinality changes, judge 'unknown' responses, or preference-vector similarity shifts) to detect BTR entry at each step. It produces truncated trajectories where the uninformative tail is removed, so that only the informative prefix contributes to the policy gradient. The final output is an improved RL policy that, at inference time, solves active reasoning tasks (number guessing, circuit decoding, situation puzzles, preference estimation, movie recommendation) through multi-turn interaction with the environment.

## Method

The paper formalizes active reasoning as a POMDP and proves that imperfect LLM belief updates (modeled via an Update-Error Growth assumption) inevitably drive trajectories into an absorbing Belief Trap Region where expected task progress becomes non-positive (Thm 1). It then shows that within the BTR, the uninformative trajectory tail contaminates GAE-based advantage estimates, potentially inverting the gradient for early informative actions (Thm 2). To mitigate this, T3 introduces a practical truncation criterion (Def 2): if a task-specific refinement measure d(H_τ, H_{τ+1}) falls below a threshold Δ_min for k consecutive steps, the trajectory is cut, preserving credit for the informative prefix. Task-specific proxies instantiate this criterion (e.g., candidate-set size reduction for GuessNumbers/CircuitDecoding, judge 'unknown' responses for SituationPuzzles, preference-vector similarity change for PreferenceEstimation). Proposition 1 provides a statistical guarantee bounding the false-truncation probability under a biased noisy proxy model. T3 integrates as a drop-in modification to standard policy optimization without altering the underlying algorithm, yielding up to 30-point performance gains and 34% token savings across five active reasoning tasks.

## Results

Across 5 active reasoning tasks (CD, SP, GN, PE, MR), T3 consistently improves over vanilla RL baselines, with GSPO+T3 achieving the best average rank (2.50) and gains of up to +41.0 points on Movie Recommendation and +30.1 points on GuessNumbers (GRPO+T3). PPO+T3 boosts CircuitDecoding EM by +16.2 points over vanilla PPO. Additionally, T3 reduces rollout token consumption by up to 34% while improving training stability, and RL-trained Qwen-7B with T3 surpasses frontier models (o3-mini, Gemini-2.5-Pro) on tasks with unbounded hypothesis spaces (SP, PE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla PPO, T3 wins on all 6 metrics, with the largest gain on CD (+16.2 EM) and MR (+13.6 EM). Against vanilla GRPO, T3 achieves substantial improvements on GN (+30.1 EM) and MR (+20.7 EM), while maintaining or slightly improving on other tasks. Against vanilla GSPO, T3 wins on 5 of 6 metrics (SP F1-char is marginally lower by 0.1), with the standout gain being +41.0 EM on MR. Compared to frontier zero-shot models, T3-equipped RL agents (Qwen-7B) surpass o3-mini and Gemini-2.5-Pro on SP (F1-word: 39.45 vs. 20.64/24.12) and PE (62.00 vs. 44.67/16.67), but trail them on CD (81.00 vs. 92.67/92.23), suggesting that explicit belief-trap control is most critical for tasks with unbounded or continuous hypothesis spaces where frontier models struggle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on standard policy-gradient RL frameworks (PPO, GRPO, GSPO) and their GAE-based advantage estimation, but reframes a persistent failure mode in multi-turn LLM agent training through a POMDP lens: imperfect belief tracking causes trajectories to enter a Belief Trap Region where credit assignment is systematically distorted. It departs from prior credit-assignment approaches (CURIO, Sotopia-RL, SPA-RL) that shape intermediate rewards or train reward models, instead proposing a trajectory-level truncation mechanism (T3) that requires no reward redesign and integrates as a drop-in wrapper. The theoretical contribution connects the BTR formalism to GAE advantage inversion (Theorem 2), providing a principled justification for why uninformative tails are harmful beyond merely diluting signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying belief deviation as a central bottleneck and showing it can invert gradient direction, the paper provides a diagnostic framework for a failure mode that practitioners observe empirically (redundant loops, training collapse) but lack a formal account for. T3's simplicity—requiring only a task-specific proxy signal and a window parameter—makes it immediately adoptable, and the consistent gains across five tasks, three RL algorithms, and OOD settings suggest broad applicability. Key limitations include the task-specific nature of proxy signals (no fully general-purpose detector yet), reliance on assumptions (linear update-error growth) that are hard to verify in practice, and the observation that very small models (3B) benefit less, suggesting a capability floor below which truncation alone is insufficient. Open questions include whether the BTR framework extends to non-goal-directed agentic tasks and whether learned (rather than hand-crafted) truncation detectors can match or exceed the task-specific proxies.
