## Problem

Physiological signals used for cognitive load prediction (EEG, ECG, EDA, gaze) are frequently noisy, temporally misaligned, or partially missing in real-world deployments, yet existing multimodal CL models assume clean inputs and route experts by modality identity rather than signal quality. This causes severe accuracy degradation (capped at 70–80%) outside controlled lab conditions, because a single corrupted modality can compromise the entire prediction pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263752/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes four heterogeneous physiological signals sampled at different rates (ECG 512 Hz, EEG 256 Hz, EDA 128 Hz, gaze 50 Hz) in windows of 10–40 seconds, and produces a binary cognitive load label (low vs. high). Crucially, any subset of modalities may be present, degraded, or partially missing; the system handles all 15 modality combinations from single-signal to four-modal fusion without architectural changes. The quality-aware gating operates on per-modality quality scores (SNR, missing-value proportion, temporal autocorrelation) computed in real time from the raw input.

## Method

CogMoE operates in two stages. First, a Quality-Aware Synchronization and Recovery stage aligns signals via 2D cross-correlation in a CWT time-frequency domain (using complex Morlet wavelets), then fills missing segments through cross-modal interpolation weighted by local time-frequency similarity followed by intra-modal low-rank matrix completion (nuclear-norm minimization). Second, a Signal-Quality-Specific Expert Modeling stage passes modality-specific encoder embeddings through a cross-attention transformer whose FFN is replaced by a three-expert MoE (High Fidelity, Noise Resilient, Contextual Refinement) with a Dynamic Pathway Gating mechanism that computes per-modality quality scores and routes the fused representation to the most appropriate expert via softmax over quality-conditioned linear projections. Training uses the CORTEX Loss, which jointly optimizes cross-entropy task accuracy, a noise-suppression MSE anchoring the NRE to clean self-referenced representations, a refinement MSE guiding the CRE on recovered regions, and a squared-error gating regularizer with an adaptive decay schedule to prevent expert collapse.

## Results

On CL-Drive with all four modalities (ECG, EDA, EEG, Gaze), CogMoE achieves 94.52% accuracy and 93.25% F1, surpassing the strongest baseline XGBoost (83.67%/82.05%) by over 10 percentage points. The best single modality combination is ECG+EDA+Gaze at 95.37%/94.10%, while the best single modality is Gaze at 94.35%/93.10%. On ADABase (ECG/EDA only), CogMoE reaches 92.54% accuracy in the combined-task setting. Across all modality combinations and sequence lengths (10–40 s), CogMoE consistently outperforms every baseline, with the paper reporting up to 13 percentage-point accuracy gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CogMoE outperforms all five baseline families across every modality combination tested. Against tree-based models, XGBoost (the strongest traditional baseline at 83.67% on 4-modal CL-Drive) is beaten by 10.85 points in accuracy; Random Forest (81.71%) trails by 12.81 points. Against CNN baselines, VGG with handcrafted features (80.66%) and ResNet with features (76.39%) are surpassed by 13.86 and 18.13 points respectively, with raw-input variants performing even worse (69.96–73.87%). Against the strongest multimodal transformer baseline BIOT, CogMoE gains 5.93 points on single-ECG (92.11 vs. 86.18) and 10.0 points on ECG+EEG (93.54 vs. 83.54); BIOT was not evaluated on 3- or 4-modal settings. Statistical significance is confirmed via corrected resampled t-test (p < 0.01) on both CL-Drive and ADABase.

## Relation to Prior Work

CogMoE extends the Mixture-of-Experts line of work (Shazeer et al., SwitchTransformer, FuseMoE, FlexMoE) by replacing the standard modality- or content-based routing criterion with an estimated signal-quality score, arguing that for redundant physiological signals (EEG, ECG, EDA, gaze) the dominant axis of variation is quality rather than modality identity. On the data side, it builds on CWT-based time-frequency analysis (Arts & Van den Broek, 2022) for cross-modal synchronization and on nuclear-norm low-rank matrix completion for intra-modal recovery, combining these into a unified pre-processing stage that prior CL-prediction pipelines (BIOT, Angkan et al.) largely omit. The CORTEX Loss and Dynamic Pathway Gating mechanism are novel contributions that tie the quality estimates directly into expert selection and training stability, departing from the static or modality-keyed gating used in FlexMoE and FuseMoE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck—variable physiological signal quality—that limits deployment of CL-prediction systems outside controlled labs, and the quality-guided routing principle could transfer to other multimodal sensing tasks where signal reliability fluctuates. However, the evaluation is confined to binary CL classification in driving scenarios on two datasets, and the model still requires supervised labels; extending to unsupervised or self-supervised settings, multi-class CL, and non-driving domains remains open. The finding that adding a fourth modality (EEG) can slightly hurt performance relative to a well-chosen three-modality subset also raises the question of whether current fusion strategies adequately handle redundant, artifact-prone signals at scale.
