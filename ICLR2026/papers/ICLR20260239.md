## Problem

Existing deep research benchmarks evaluate agents on web-only retrieval, simple factual questions, or narrow aspects like report factuality, but none assess whether agents can identify salient insights across both public web and private enterprise data grounded in realistic organizational contexts. DRBench fills this gap by introducing 100 multi-step, persona-grounded research tasks that require navigating heterogeneous enterprise applications (chat, email, cloud storage, spreadsheets, slide decks) alongside open-web sources, with explicit distractor content that agents must avoid.

## Input / Output

Each task provides a high-level deep research question (e.g., 'How can Lee's Market leverage FSMA 204 regulations to enhance food safety and customer trust?') paired with a company profile and user persona as Task Context. The agent operates within a self-hosted Docker environment containing Nextcloud, Mattermost, Roundcube email, and FileBrowser, plus unrestricted web access, and must produce a structured report listing atomic insights with per-claim citations. Evaluation decomposes the report into atomic insights and scores four axes: Insight Recall (fraction of 1093 groundtruth insights recovered, capped at k = |groundtruth| + 5 to prevent copy-paste gaming), Factuality (TREC-RAG verification of each cited claim against its source document), Distractor Avoidance (1 − distractor recall), and Report Quality (1–10 G-Eval-style rating across six dimensions).

## Method

DRBench is constructed via a five-stage LLM-plus-human pipeline: (1) synthetic company and persona generation, (2) retrieval of time-invariant public URLs with LLM-extracted insights validated by annotators, (3) open-ended question generation conditioned on the persona and public insight, (4) generation of company-specific internal insights plus plausible distractors, and (5) 'needle-in-a-haystack' file generation that embeds each insight into a realistic PDF, DOCX, PPTX, XLSX, chat log, or email. The baseline DRBench Agent (DRBA) implements a four-component architecture: research planning (Complex or Simple mode decomposing the question into investigation areas or subqueries), priority-based action planning with enterprise-source weighting, an adaptive research loop that iteratively selects tools (internet search, enterprise API, URL fetch, local semantic search, analyzer), processes content into a vector store, and generates gap-filling actions, and a multi-stage report writer with deferred citation resolution. Evaluation uses GPT-4o as LLM-as-a-judge with >91% agreement with human raters (Cohen's κ = 0.683), and the benchmark is fully self-hosted with dated URLs for reproducibility.

## Results

On the full DRBench benchmark (100 tasks, 1093 groundtruth insights), DRBA with GPT-5 achieves 36.52% Insight Recall, 72.11% Factuality, 93.22% Distractor Avoidance, and 93.41% Report Quality in the app-based enterprise environment (Harmonic Mean 63.81). In the simplified local-file setting, the same configuration improves to 38.91% Insight Recall and 75.84% Factuality (HM 66.43). OpenAI's Deep Research (GPT-5) leads all systems in the local setting with 41.96% Insight Recall and 83.64% Factuality (HM 70.35), while browser-only web agents collapse to just 1.11% Insight Recall, highlighting the critical role of structured enterprise API access.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRBA (GPT-5) in the local-file setting (HM 66.43) outperforms Perplexity (HM 62.29) by ~4 points on harmonic mean, driven primarily by higher Insight Recall (38.91 vs. 34.21), but trails both OpenAI Deep Research (HM 70.35) and Gemini (HM 68.90) by 4–6 points, with the gap concentrated in Factuality (75.84 vs. 83.64 for OpenAI, 81.32 for Gemini). In the more realistic app-based enterprise environment, DRBA (GPT-5) drops to HM 63.81, still beating Perplexity's local score but falling further behind the commercial systems. Weaker backbones suffer larger degradation: DRBA (DeepSeek-V3.1) drops from HM 58.80 (local) to 49.20 (app-based), a ~16% relative loss, while GPT-5 loses only ~4%. Browser-only web agents (AgentLab, GPT-4.1) achieve a mere 1.11% Insight Recall and 6.67% Factuality, confirming that structured API access to enterprise applications is essential for deep research. Across all configurations, Distractor Avoidance remains consistently high (>93%), indicating that the primary failure mode is missed relevant insights rather than inclusion of irrelevant ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRBench carves out a new intersection between two previously separate benchmark families: deep research benchmarks (DeepResearch Bench, DeepResearchGym, ResearcherBench) that evaluate web-only synthesis, and enterprise/computer-use benchmarks (OSWorld, TheAgentCompany, WorkArena) that evaluate task execution in application environments but not research. The evaluation methodology draws on FactScore for atomic claim verification and G-Eval-style LLM-as-a-judge scoring for report quality, but the task formulation—multi-step research requiring integration of public web sources with private enterprise data across heterogeneous applications (Nextcloud, Mattermost, email, file systems)—is explicitly positioned as absent from prior work. The baseline agent (DRBA) is architecturally inspired by modular deep research pipelines (Local Deep Researcher, DeepResearcher) but adds adaptive action planning and enterprise-specific source prioritization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a striking gap: even GPT-5-backed agents achieve only ~37% insight recall, and no evaluated agent successfully retrieved relevant information from the open web, suggesting that current deep research systems are far from effective in realistic enterprise settings. The finding that adaptive planning improves recall but degrades factuality, and that browser-only agents collapse to ~1% recall, provides concrete diagnostic signals for where architectural innovation is needed. Open questions include whether the insight-level evaluation (with its k+5 cutoff) adequately captures the full breadth of useful discoveries, whether the synthetic enterprise environment generalizes to real organizational data, and how to close the gap between document retrieval competence and decision-critical insight extraction.
