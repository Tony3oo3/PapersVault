## Problem

Existing diffusion bridge models for image restoration rely on predefined, sub-optimal trajectories that often include a redundant re-noising phase before denoising, resulting in unnecessarily high trajectory energy and slow iterative sampling. While Schrödinger Bridges offer a theoretically optimal path, their IPF-based solvers are computationally prohibitive for high-dimensional image data. The paper asks whether one can design a restoration trajectory that is both energetically optimal (a geodesic on the data manifold) and solvable in a single step without iterative ODE integration.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image (noisy, low-resolution, raindrop-occluded, low-light, or moiré-affected) and produces a restored clean image in 1–10 function evaluations. It is built on the FLUX-dev rectified flow backbone (743.8M parameters) and operates on 1024×1024 patches. A key design choice is the trajectory horizon T₀ ∈ (0,1], which is sampled during training from [0.2, 0.95] and becomes a user-tunable knob at inference: smaller T₀ preserves more information from the degraded input (suitable for mild degradations like denoising), while larger T₀ increases generative power (suitable for severe degradations like super-resolution).

## Method

E-Bridge constructs a low-energy geodesic trajectory by defining the bridge state as X_t = (1−t)·X̃_t + t·X_T, where X̃_t = (1−t/T₀)X₀ + (t/T₀)Y is a linear interpolation between the clean and degraded images over a controllable horizon T₀, and X_T is Gaussian noise. Proposition 4.1 proves that this linear expectation is the unique kinetic-energy-minimizing geodesic, and Proposition 4.2 shows that setting α_t = 1−t and β_t = t minimizes adaptation energy relative to the pretrained rectified flow prior. To traverse this trajectory without iterative sampling, the paper derives a closed-form single-step solver F_θ(X_t, Y, t) by analytically inverting the trajectory equation using the pretrained denoiser as a geodesic guidance field. The solver is trained with a continuous-time consistency objective that enforces the output to be invariant along the trajectory (dF_θ/dt = 0), effectively learning the data manifold geometry in one pass. The entire framework is fine-tuned from FLUX-dev for 10,000 iterations, enabling high-fidelity restoration in as few as one function evaluation.

## Results

E-Bridge achieves state-of-the-art perceptual quality across five restoration tasks (super-resolution, denoising, raindrop removal, low-light enhancement, demoiréing) with only 1–10 NFE. At 10 NFE, it attains the best LPIPS (0.346 on SR, 0.184 on denoising, 0.079 on raindrop, 0.097 on low-light), FID (57.8 on SR, 26.6 on denoising), NIQE (3.84 on SR, 3.51 on denoising, 3.22 on raindrop), and MUSIQ (70.9 on SR, 69.7 on denoising, 71.7 on low-light) among all compared methods. At 1 NFE, E-Bridge outperforms UniDB++ on every metric for SR (PSNR 24.09 vs 23.98, LPIPS 0.452 vs 0.658, MUSIQ 44.6 vs 33.2), denoising (PSNR 25.24 vs 21.84, LPIPS 0.258 vs 0.613), and raindrop removal (PSNR 29.22 vs 25.42, LPIPS 0.085 vs 0.174). The method occasionally trails on PSNR at 10 NFE (e.g., 21.28 vs 23.38 for SR vs UniDB++) but the authors explicitly prioritize perceptual realism over pixel-wise fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest competitor UniDB++ (Pan et al., 2025), E-Bridge consistently wins on all four perceptual/distributional metrics (LPIPS, FID, NIQE, MUSIQ) at 10 and 5 NFE across super-resolution, denoising, and demoiréing, while trailing on PSNR by 1–2 dB (e.g., 21.28 vs 23.38 on SR at 10 NFE). At 1 NFE the gap reverses: E-Bridge surpasses UniDB++ on every metric for SR (PSNR 24.09 vs 23.98, LPIPS 0.452 vs 0.658, MUSIQ 44.6 vs 33.2), denoising (PSNR 25.24 vs 21.84, LPIPS 0.258 vs 0.613), and raindrop removal (PSNR 29.22 vs 25.42, LPIPS 0.085 vs 0.174). Against IRBridge (Wang et al., 2025a), E-Bridge achieves substantially lower LPIPS (0.346 vs 0.415 on SR) and FID (57.8 vs 59.5) at 10 NFE, and lower NIQE (3.84 vs 5.22). Versus MaRS (Li et al., 2025), E-Bridge improves LPIPS by 0.18–0.19 and MUSIQ by 11–22 points at 10 NFE on SR. For low-light enhancement, E-Bridge at 10 NFE outperforms DiffUIR (Zheng et al., 2024) on all five metrics (PSNR 25.54 vs 25.16, LPIPS 0.097 vs 0.160, FID 41.2 vs 71.7) and beats AdaIR (Cui et al., 2025) at 1 NFE on PSNR (24.66 vs 22.63), LPIPS (0.133 vs 0.168), and FID (54.8 vs 58.1), though AdaIR retains a slight MUSIQ edge (70.9 vs 66.8). The one task where E-Bridge does not dominate is raindrop removal at 10 NFE, where UniDB++ still leads on PSNR (32.15 vs 29.22) and LPIPS (0.068 vs 0.079), a gap the authors attribute to the global scalar T₀ being suboptimal for spatially heterogeneous degradations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of diffusion bridge models (BBDM, I2SB, Schrödinger Bridges, UniDB) and consistency models, but departs from both in key ways. Rather than adopting the symmetric noise schedules or iterative IPF solvers of prior bridge methods, it argues from a stochastic optimal control / Onsager-Machlup energy perspective that the optimal restoration trajectory is a simple linear geodesic with a controllable horizon T₀, and it borrows the consistency-model training objective to turn the trajectory into a closed-form single-step solver. The pretrained FLUX-dev rectified-flow network is repurposed not as a separate generative prior to be 'stitched' (as in IRBridge) but as an integrated geodesic guidance field whose Lipschitz stability uniquely determines the trajectory coefficients αₜ=1−t, βₜ=t.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The T₀ parameter as a task-adaptive knob (small for denoising, large for super-resolution) offers a practical, interpretable control that prior bridge methods lack, and the 1–10 NFE inference with competitive perceptual scores (LPIPS, MUSIQ, NIQE) is a meaningful efficiency win for deployment. However, PSNR is consistently lower than multi-step baselines, the method depends on the 743M-parameter FLUX-dev backbone (limiting edge deployment), and the global scalar T₀ cannot handle spatially heterogeneous degradations (e.g., raindrops over clean backgrounds). Open questions include whether spatially adaptive T₀(h,w) maps can resolve this, whether the energy-geodesic framing generalizes beyond the FLUX architecture, and whether the perceptual-over-fidelity trade-off is acceptable for downstream tasks that require pixel-level accuracy.
