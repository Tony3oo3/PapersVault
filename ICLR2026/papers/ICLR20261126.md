## Problem

Existing in-context learning (ICL) theory for transformers assumes causal sufficiency — that the noise term ε is independent of the features x — yet real-world data frequently contains hidden confounders that violate this assumption. This paper provides the first theoretical analysis characterizing how such hidden confounders bias both the pre-training dynamics and downstream ICL predictions of transformers, and asks whether a lightweight, gradient-free strategy can correct the resulting prediction bias without fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261126/cropped_page14_idx8.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen linear-attention transformer whose S and T attention parameters were pre-trained on confounded data, a small batch of unconfounded prompting examples (x_rc, y_rc) satisfying ε ⊥ x, and a novel query input x*. It produces an unbiased regression prediction ŷ = w*ᵀx* through two sequential ICL forward passes over the same frozen weights — no parameter updates, no auxiliary labels, and no instrumental variables are required. In experiments, as few as 15 unconfounded examples suffice to substantially reduce prediction error on real-world Yelp sentiment tasks.

## Method

The paper contributes a two-part framework. First, it constructs 'U_weights' (S^u, T^u) that provably induce unbiased ICL predictions via implicit gradient descent on the true w*ᵀx target, then proves (Theorem 1) that TFs pre-trained on confounded data deviate from these U_weights by an amount proportional to the per-dimension confounding strength r_j, and (Theorem 2) that this deviation propagates into ICL prediction bias with a distinctive structure: the global bias depends on the sum Σ_l r_l across dimensions, enabling cancellation when confounders offset each other — a property absent in classical OLS endogeneity. Second, it proposes DDbias, a gradient-free debiasing procedure that prompts the biased frozen TF twice with unconfounded examples: first with original labels to obtain biased predictions ŷ_b, then with residuals (y − ŷ_b), so the second pass implicitly performs gradient descent on the residual loss and cancels the confounding bias. The method is proven robust to weakly confounded or partially contaminated prompting samples (Propositions 2–3), requiring no fine-tuning, no IV construction, and no structural assumptions beyond the availability of a small unconfounded batch.

## Results

On the real-world Yelp sentiment-review tasks, DDbias with 60 ICL samples achieves MAE of 16.7 on RPI (Restaurant Popularity Index) and 0.16 on ROR (Restaurant Overall Rating), outperforming all baselines. In controlled linear-regression simulations, DDbias reduces prediction error by roughly 50–60% compared to the vanilla biased transformer across confounding strengths r ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0} (e.g., 0.115 vs. 0.280 at L=5, Conf@0.5). The method also generalizes to deeper non-linear transformers (L=5, L=7 with ReLU, LayerNorm, Softmax), maintaining a consistent ~50% error reduction over the biased baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDbias is compared against three baselines on the Yelp tasks: Vanilla LLaMA (no debiasing), DMCEE (Cheng et al., 2022b, a multi-modal proxy causal estimator), and SC (Cheng et al., 2022c, a structural causal model). With 60 ICL samples, DDbias clearly outperforms all three on both datasets: RPI MAE 16.7 vs. 23.8/21.2/22.5, and ROR MAE 0.16 vs. 0.46/0.28/0.24. With only 15 ICL samples, DDbias is competitive but not yet the best (e.g., RPI 22.8 vs. DMCEE 21.2; ROR 0.36 vs. SC 0.24), indicating a sample-efficiency trade-off. The paper also benchmarks against the IV-based in-context debiasing method of Liang et al. (2024) under three controlled setups (Appendix Tables 26–28), showing that DDbias does not require valid instruments and remains effective when only a small unconfounded batch is available, whereas the IV approach fails under weak or invalid instruments. In simulation (Table 4), DDbias consistently halves the prediction error of the vanilla biased transformer across all confounding strengths and model depths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ICL-as-implicit-gradient-descent framework established by Von Oswald et al. (2023) and Ahn et al. (2023) by relaxing the causal-sufficiency assumption (ε ⊥ X) that underpins all prior theoretical analyses of transformer in-context learning. While the concurrent work of Liang et al. (2024) also identified endogeneity in ICL and proposed an IV-based correction, this paper provides the first full theoretical characterization of how hidden confounders distort both the pre-training convergence of attention parameters and the downstream ICL prediction, showing the bias is proportional to confounding strength. The proposed DDbias method departs from IV-based approaches by requiring only a small batch of unconfounded prompting examples rather than valid instruments, and from data-fusion debiasing by operating entirely in the gradient-free inference regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The causal-sufficiency assumption is violated in many real-world prediction tasks (recommendation, causal effect estimation, online reviews), meaning that standard ICL theory may mischaracterize what pre-trained transformers actually learn. The bias cancellation property (global bias depends on Σ_l r_l, enabling cancellation across dimensions) is qualitatively different from OLS endogeneity and highlights that attention geometry introduces new bias channels absent in classical regression. Key limitations include the reliance on linear attention and primarily linear DGP for the main theorems, with non-linear extensions relegated to appendix experiments. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to out-of-distribution shifts in the confounder distribution ε across training and testing, and whether DDbias remains effective when truly unconfounded data is unavailable.
