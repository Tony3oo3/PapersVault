## Problem

Bayesian experimental design (BED) has historically been limited to low-dimensional design spaces (~20 dimensions), and the community has attributed this to the computational cost of estimating expected information gain (EIG). This paper identifies a more fundamental barrier: in high-dimensional design spaces, the assumed likelihood is inevitably misspecified over much of the space, and direct EIG optimization exploits these misspecifications in a reward-hacking-like manner, producing unrealistic designs that yield uninformative data. No prior method addresses this misalignment-driven failure mode at scale.

## Input / Output

The method takes a prior over the target variable θ, a likelihood model p(y|θ,ξ) (e.g., Plackett-Luce, Bradley-Terry, or Ordinal Logit over a latent reward), a reference diffusion model p_ref(ξ) trained on realistic designs (e.g., Stable Diffusion v1.5 fine-tuned on 512×512 shoe images, or an NCSN++ model on MNIST), and a number of sequential experiment steps K. It produces a sequence of realistic designs (single images or sets of up to 4 images, spanning up to ~750,000 dimensions) and posterior samples over θ in a learned embedding space (32–64 dimensional), which can be decoded back to image space via a second guided diffusion pass. Inference operates in the latent embedding space rather than pixel space, exploiting the fact that perceptually relevant information concentrates in a low-dimensional representation.

## Method

DiffBED replaces direct EIG maximization with sampling from an EIG-tilted distribution p*(ξ) ∝ p_ref(ξ)·exp(α·EIG(ξ)), which explicitly trades off informativeness against design realism captured by the reference diffusion model. Concretely, it simulates the reverse-time SDE of the reference diffusion model augmented with an additional drift term g(t)²·α·∇_{ξ_t} EIG(ξ̂₀(ξ_t)), where ξ̂₀(ξ_t) is the Tweedie estimate of the clean design computed in closed form from the score network, and the EIG gradient is estimated using standard estimators (non-nested Monte Carlo for discrete outcomes, PCE for continuous). For design sets of multiple images, each element receives an independent score update from the diffusion prior (ensuring realism) but a coupled EIG gradient (ensuring joint informativeness), yielding an interacting-particle diffusion. Posterior inference over θ uses a particle filter with Riemannian Langevin rejuvenation on the unit sphere in embedding space, requiring no neural network evaluations per particle. The entire approach is training-free with respect to the diffusion model: no retraining or fine-tuning of p_ref is needed, making it compatible with pre-trained foundation models.

## Results

DiffBED scales Bayesian experimental design to design spaces exceeding 750,000 dimensions (512×512 images), whereas prior BED applications rarely exceeded ~20 dimensions. On the source location finding task, DiffBED achieves the lowest L₂ error between posterior samples and ground truth, while standard BED and both Rank variants perform worse than random sampling from the design prior. On the CelebA and MNIST search tasks (50 iterations, 4-image design sets with top-2 ranking feedback), DiffBED achieves the highest mean cosine similarity to the ground-truth embedding, while standard BED collapses to near-zero similarity despite reporting the highest EIG values. On the large-scale Zappos task (512×512 shoe images with 1–5 discrete ratings), DiffBED outperforms all baselines except Rank (Pool), with only a marginal gap to the latter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DiffBED against five baselines: standard BED (direct gradient-based EIG maximisation), Entropy (a DiffBED variant guided by marginal predictive entropy instead of EIG), Rank (Pool) (selecting the best of 1000 candidates drawn from the training data), Rank (Diffuse) (selecting the best of 5 candidates sampled from the diffusion model, compute-matched to DiffBED), and Random. DiffBED decisively outperforms standard BED on every task: on CelebA search, BED achieves near-zero cosine similarity (despite the highest EIG) while DiffBED reaches the highest similarity; on source location finding, BED's L₂ error is worse than random. DiffBED also consistently beats the Entropy variant, which fails to encourage diversity within design sets. Against Rank (Pool), DiffBED wins on the search tasks (MNIST, CelebA) despite evaluating only a single design set per iteration versus 1000 candidates, and achieves a marginal gap on Zappos. Rank (Diffuse), which is compute-matched to DiffBED (N≈5 candidates), is consistently outperformed, confirming that gradient-guided generation is more sample-efficient than ranking a small pool of diffusion samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the score-based SDE framework for diffusion models (Song et al., 2021) and recent training-free guidance techniques (Ye et al., 2024; Chung et al., 2023) that augment the reverse SDE with an additional drift term, repurposing this mechanism to steer design generation toward high-EIG regions. It also leverages existing EIG gradient estimators (Foster et al., 2020; Rainforth et al., 2018) and Tweedie's formula to make the guidance tractable. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant BED literature—which has focused on improving EIG estimator scalability (Foster et al., 2019; Goda et al., 2022; Iollo et al., 2025a)—by identifying model misspecification as the fundamental barrier and introducing a diffusion-based design prior to counteract the resulting reward-hacking behavior, a failure mode previously undocumented in the BED literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiffBED enables the first successful application of Bayesian experimental design to image-scale problems (750,000+ dimensions), a regime where prior methods were limited to roughly 20 dimensions, opening the door to principled preference elicitation, active search, and adaptive experimentation in realistic high-dimensional settings. The identification of reward-hacking as a systematic failure mode of EIG optimization under approximate likelihoods is a broadly relevant insight that likely affects other model-based optimization frameworks. Key limitations include the dependence on a high-quality reference diffusion model, the crude delta-function approximation in the Tweedie-based guidance, and the need to tune the informativeness-realism trade-off hyperparameter α; open questions include whether the approach extends to video, 3D, or other modalities, and whether the approximation quality degrades in settings with more complex likelihood structures.
