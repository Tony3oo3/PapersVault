## Problem

Existing LLM unlearning methods—whether divergence-based (pushing parameters away from the forget set) or convergence-based (training toward fixed targets like refusal templates)—suffer from an unstable trade-off between forgetting and utility, frequently collapsing into incoherent gibberish outputs on forget prompts. This failure stems from the persistence of lexical-level and semantic-level associations in attention weights that continue to enable retrieval of memorized factual content even after surface-level unlearning.

## Input / Output

The method requires only a pre-trained LLM, a forget set (QA pairs or text passages), a retain set, and a single scalar hyperparameter τ (attention temperature, typically in the range 2.0–3.0). No external teacher model, rejection templates, or positive/negative example pairs are needed. The output is a set of updated model parameters that, when queried on forget-set prompts, produce grammatically coherent responses with the targeted factual content suppressed, while maintaining near-base-model performance on retain-set and general-knowledge evaluations.

## Method

ASU constructs a forget-teacher by inserting a temperature τ > 1 into the softmax of every self-attention head in the base model, which flattens attention distributions and weakens the precise token-to-token associations that encode factual recall. The base model (student) is then trained via self-distillation to match the forget-teacher's output distribution on the forget set using a KL-divergence loss, while a standard retain loss (gradient descent or KL regularization) preserves utility on the retain set. The key mechanistic insight is that factual tokens depend on sharp, concentrated attention patterns over a small subset of positions, whereas function tokens rely on broad, diffuse attention—so smoothing selectively erases factual recall while minimally disturbing syntactic structure. A theoretical analysis (Appendix A) proves the existence of a non-trivial temperature interval [τ₀, τ₁] where the factual logit margin becomes negative (forgetting) while the function-token logit change remains bounded (fluency), and ablations confirm that smoothing only shallow layers (e.g., 6–8) suffices to achieve comparable unlearning efficacy.

## Results

On the TOFU benchmark, ASU_KL achieves the best average score across all three tasks: 80.10 (forget01), 76.01 (forget05), and 75.71 (forget10), combining high Forget Efficacy (FE) of 83.08/77.84/78.16 with strong Model Utility (MU) of 77.13/74.18/73.27. On the real-world unlearning scenario, ASU_KL attains MU=55.76 and FE=79.60, outperforming all baselines on both dimensions simultaneously. On MUSE (Books), ASU_KL achieves VerbMem=5.3, KnowMem_F=28.6, PrivLeak=-51.0 while retaining KnowMem_R=62.5, demonstrating effective copyright unlearning with minimal utility loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASU dominates all baselines on the TOFU benchmark across all three tasks. Compared to the strongest convergence-based baseline IDK_AP, ASU_KL achieves a nearly 30% FE improvement (60.88→77.84 on forget05; 61.27→78.16 on forget10) while maintaining comparable MU (74.18 vs. 75.23 on forget05). Against ME_GD, the best divergence-free baseline on forget01, ASU_KL gains +6.3 Avg (80.10 vs. 73.76) and +8.0 FE (83.08 vs. 75.04); the gap widens dramatically on forget10 where ME_GD's FE collapses to 45.95 while ASU_KL sustains 78.16. Divergence-based methods (GA, NPO) show severe instability: GA collapses to near-zero FE on forget05/forget10, and NPO sacrifices MU (56–57) to achieve moderate FE (~73). On MUSE, ASU variants outperform SimNPO and NPO on the Books setting, achieving VerbMem=5.3 vs. 54.4 (NPO_KL) and KnowMem_R=62.5 vs. 61.4 (NPO), confirming that ASU generalizes beyond QA to free-form text completion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both divergence-based (GA, NPO) and convergence-based (DPO, IDK, ME) unlearning methods by reframing unlearning as self-distillation from a 'forget-teacher' constructed by raising the softmax temperature in the model's own self-attention. While attention temperature scaling has been explored in translation, summarization distillation, and stability, its application to unlearning is novel here. The method also builds on mechanistic interpretability findings (Meng et al., 2022; Guo et al., 2025) that factual associations are concentrated in early transformer layers, which the paper leverages to show that smoothing only shallow layers suffices. Unlike prior convergence-based methods that use fixed refusal templates or entropy maximization, ASU provides a bounded, naturalistic forgetting target derived from the model's own attention structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ASU addresses a persistent failure mode in LLM unlearning—gibberish outputs on forget prompts—by preserving syntactic coherence while erasing factual content, which is critical for real-world deployment where over-forgetting is detectable and potentially exploitable. The theoretical guarantee (existence of a temperature interval where forgetting and fluency coexist) provides a principled foundation, though it relies on strong structural assumptions (A1–A4) that are validated empirically but not formally proven for real models. Open questions include whether the approach extends to larger models (tested only at 7B scale), whether the temperature hyperparameter can be learned automatically rather than tuned per task, and whether the method generalizes to non-QA unlearning settings beyond what MUSE and WMDP cover.
