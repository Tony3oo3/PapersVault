## Problem

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) excels in domains with ground-truth verifiers (e.g., math) but cannot be directly applied to non-verifiable domains like LLM alignment, where designing reliable automatic rewards is non-trivial. This paper asks whether high-quality reference outputs can serve as 'soft verifiers' to bridge this gap, enabling effective alignment tuning through reference-guided LLM-judges in a self-improvement setting without external human or AI supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260130/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For evaluation, the method takes an instruction, a pair of candidate model outputs, and a single high-quality reference output (generated by a frontier LLM such as GPT-4o or DeepSeek-V3), and produces a pairwise preference judgment indicating which candidate better follows the instruction. For training, the pipeline consumes 60K UltraFeedback instructions paired with DeepSeek-V3-generated reference outputs, and produces a fine-tuned LLM (Llama-3-8B-Instruct or Qwen2.5-7B-SFT) evaluated on AlpacaEval and Arena-Hard. The reference is not used as a direct similarity target but as a quality anchor that grounds the judge's assessment of instruction-following, factuality, and overall response quality.

## Method

The paper proposes two reference-guided prompting protocols for LLM-judges: RefEval, which explicitly instructs the judge to use the provided reference as a benchmark for successful instruction-following and compare candidates against it for factual correctness and quality, and RefMatch, which directs the judge to act as a semantic and stylistic matcher determining which candidate more closely resembles the reference. These protocols are deployed in a two-stage self-improvement pipeline: Stage 1 performs SFT distillation on the frontier model's reference outputs, and Stage 2 applies DPO where the post-distilled model itself serves as the reference-guided LLM-judge to annotate on-policy preference pairs (sampling 5 candidates per instruction at temperature 0.8, selecting best and worst). The key insight is that references provide a shared grounding that substantially reduces inter-judge variance (81.4% vs. 76.6% agreement) and enables smaller models to achieve evaluation quality approaching that of much larger ones, with the resulting DPO training matching or exceeding a finetuned reward model (ArmoRM) without requiring additional preference-annotated data.

## Results

On the evaluation task, RefEval achieves 79.1% average pairwise accuracy across five datasets (LLMBar-Natural, LLMBar-Adversarial, MTBench, Instrusum, HREF) using 11 open-source LLM-judges, a 6.8% absolute improvement over the reference-free LLMBar-Base baseline (72.3%). For the self-improvement training pipeline, RefEval achieves 73.1% length-controlled AlpacaEval and 58.7% Arena-Hard with Llama-3-8B-Instruct, and 70.0% AlpacaEval and 74.1% Arena-Hard with Qwen2.5-7B-SFT, matching or exceeding the finetuned ArmoRM reward model (73.9/58.6 and 66.8/72.2) without requiring human or AI preference annotations to train a separate reward model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the evaluation setting (Table 1), RefEval (79.1%) outperforms LLMBar-Base from Zeng et al. (72.3%) by 6.8 points, HREF-Ref from Lyu et al. (74.8%) by 4.3 points, and Prepair from Jeong et al. (74.0%) by 5.1 points, with gains most pronounced for smaller models (e.g., Llama-3-8B improves from 60.1% to 77.5%). In the training setting (Table 4), RefEval substantially outperforms traditional reference-based metrics ROUGE (56.4/52.1) and BERTScore (58.8/53.0) on AlpacaEval/Arena-Hard for Llama-3-8B, and achieves performance comparable to the finetuned ArmoRM reward model (73.9/58.6 vs. 73.1/58.7) without needing preference-annotated data to train a reward model. Against SimPO from Meng et al. (51.6/36.2 on Llama-3-8B), RefEval gains over 21 points on AlpacaEval and 22 points on Arena-Hard. The paper's own reference-free self-improvement baseline (RefFree: 67.5/53.8) is consistently outperformed by RefEval (73.1/58.7), confirming that reference guidance provides a meaningful training signal beyond what the model can extract from its own outputs alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-as-a-Judge paradigm for alignment evaluation by introducing reference-guided prompting protocols (RefEval, RefMatch) that treat high-quality outputs as 'soft verifiers,' bridging the methodological gap between RLVR (which relies on rule-based verifiers) and RLHF/RLAIF (which is reference-free). It builds on prior reference-guided evaluation work (LLMBar/Zeng et al. 2024, HREF/Lyu et al. 2024, RevisEval/Zhang et al. 2025) but departs from them by extending the setting from static evaluation to a self-improvement training pipeline where the LLM judges its own outputs using references during DPO. The self-improvement framing connects to generative reward model work (Yuan et al. 2024, Wu et al. 2024, Mahan et al. 2024), but the key distinction is that references provide external grounding rather than relying solely on the model's internal judgment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: practitioners can perform effective alignment tuning without training a separate reward model, simply by having a frontier model generate references and using the target model as a reference-guided judge. The finding that SFT distillation on high-quality references outperforms DPO with a finetuned reward model (ArmoRM) challenges the default assumption that preference optimization is always superior when strong targets are available. Open questions include whether this approach generalizes to domains where even frontier models produce unreliable references (e.g., highly creative or domain-specific tasks), and whether the gains persist as base models approach frontier capability, reducing the quality gap that makes references informative.
