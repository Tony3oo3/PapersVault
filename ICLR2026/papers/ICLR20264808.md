## Problem

Prior work on analyzing long chain-of-thought reasoning has relied on top-down taxonomies with a small fixed set of predefined strategy types (e.g., verification, backtracking, subgoal setting), which are constrained by human intuition and fail to capture the full diversity of model-specific reasoning behaviors. This paper asks whether reasoning strategies can be discovered in a bottom-up, data-driven manner, whether they can be predicted from the input question alone, and whether steering a model toward an optimal strategy measurably improves both accuracy and safety.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes long CoT responses (up to 32K tokens) paired with their problem prompts from reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B, s1.1-32B, QwQ-32B). It produces a binary pattern matrix Z ∈ {0,1}^{n×k} where each row encodes which side of each contrastive rubric a response aligns with, plus a synthesized natural-language report describing the response's reasoning profile. For the control experiments, the system additionally outputs a predicted optimal strategy per question, which is injected as a prompt instruction to steer the model's next generation.

## Method

The COT ENCYCLOPEDIA is a five-stage pipeline: (1) GPT-4o is prompted to brainstorm 4,057 contrastive reasoning criteria (each a pair of opposing strategies) from model CoT outputs; (2) each criterion triplet is concatenated and embedded via text-embedding-3-large into a matrix E ∈ R^{N×d}; (3) hierarchical agglomerative clustering with cosine distance compresses N criteria into k=6 clusters, with medoid (not centroid) selection preserving interpretability; (4) a second LLM pass generates detailed contrastive rubrics for each cluster; (5) each new response is classified under every rubric via yes/no prompting, yielding the binary pattern matrix and a composed report. The key differentiator from prior top-down approaches is that the taxonomy is entirely data-driven and adapts to the benchmark (e.g., safety benchmarks surface 'Safety Precedence' and 'Content Handling' criteria absent from problem-solving benchmarks). For the control experiments, binary classifiers are trained on question embeddings to predict the optimal strategy for unseen questions, and RLVR (GRPO) training on format-matched datasets (multiple-choice vs. free-form) reveals that data format shifts reasoning strategies far more than domain, with linear weight interpolation between MC- and FF-trained models enabling smooth strategy blending without additional training.

## Results

Guiding models with question-specific optimal reasoning strategies (identified via CoT Encyclopedia) yields accuracy gains of 12.2–16.1% across five benchmarks: GPQA-Diamond improves from 72.9% to 82.4%, MMLU-Redux from 90.3% to 93.7%, MATH-500 from 78.3% to 81.5%, XSTest safety from 91.3% to 96.2%, and WildGuard safety from 89.2% to 95.7%. In the dataset-wide optimal setting, GPQA-Diamond rises from 61.5% to 70.1%, XSTest from 82.3% to 94.0%, and WildGuard from 79.1% to 89.9%. Human evaluation confirms the framework's quality with Likert ratings of 4.2–4.4 and an 86% win+tie preference rate over the predefined analyzer baseline. Format (MC vs. FF) produces substantially larger shifts in reasoning strategies than domain, with FF-trained models generating 4.6× more 'wait' tokens (8.76 vs. 1.89).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the predefined cognitive-behavior analyzer of Gandhi et al. (2025), which uses four fixed categories (verification, backtracking, subgoal setting, backward chaining), the CoT Encyclopedia reveals dramatically stronger model differentiation: the predefined behaviors show minimal inter-model differences (p > 0.05, |Cohen's d| ≈ 0.1), while the CoT Encyclopedia's six bottom-up criteria yield numerous statistically significant differences with effect sizes up to 0.44 (problem-solving) and 1.44 (safety). In human evaluation, annotators prefer CoT Encyclopedia reports over the predefined analyzer with an 86% win+tie rate and higher Likert scores (4.2–4.4 vs. lower baseline ratings). Against ThinkPatterns-21k (Wen et al., 2025), which applies a fixed set of predefined thinking patterns to all questions, the CoT Encyclopedia's question-specific strategy selection outperforms on both MATH-500 (+3.8 vs. +1.3 over no-prompt baseline) and GPQA-Diamond (+5.5 vs. +2.9), demonstrating that dynamic, per-question strategy matching yields substantially larger gains than a one-size-fits-all pattern assignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant top-down approach to CoT analysis (Gandhi et al. 2025, Wen et al. 2025, Guo et al. 2025), which relies on a small fixed set of predefined cognitive behaviors, by instead deriving reasoning criteria bottom-up from model outputs via LLM brainstorming, embedding, and hierarchical agglomerative clustering. It also extends prior clustering-based CoT methods (An et al. 2023, Xu et al. 2024, Fang et al. 2025), which focused on short or medium-length chains, to long multi-strategy CoTs produced by reasoning models like DeepSeek-R1 and QwQ. The downstream control applications—predicting question-specific optimal strategies and steering via prompting or weight interpolation—build on the CoT monitoring motivation of Korbak et al. (2025) but move from diagnosis to active intervention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that training data format (multiple-choice vs. free-form) shapes reasoning strategies far more than domain (math vs. commonsense) challenges a common assumption in reasoning-model training and has direct implications for dataset curation and RLVR pipeline design. The framework's practical utility is demonstrated through measurable performance gains (12–16%) from pattern-guided prompting and smooth strategy interpolation via model merging, offering a lightweight control knob without fine-tuning. Key limitations include reliance on GPT-4o as the classification evaluator (potential bias), evaluation on only three model families and five benchmarks, and the finding that pattern prompting fails on small models without SFT—leaving open whether the framework generalizes to multilingual, multimodal, or code-generation settings.
