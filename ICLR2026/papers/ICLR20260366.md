## Problem

Existing world models require extensive domain-specific action-labeled data and still produce low-fidelity, coarse predictions, while video diffusion models trained on internet-scale action-free video data encode rich physical priors but are fundamentally incompatible with interactive world modeling due to bidirectional temporal dependencies and lack of fine-grained action conditioning. This paper tackles the unexplored problem of systematically transferring pre-trained, full-sequence, non-causal video diffusion models into autoregressive, action-conditioned interactive world models.

## Input / Output

The method takes 4 history video frames (at 320×512 resolution) and a frame-level action sequence as input, producing up to 12 future frames autoregressively (16-frame total context). Actions are injected per-frame rather than as a single video-level embedding: discrete actions use learned embedding layers while continuous actions (e.g., 3D translation/rotation in RECON) use linear projections, both integrated via element-wise addition with noise-level conditioning. During autoregressive rollout, history frames are held at zero noise (with a small k_small=20 perturbation) while the current frame is denoised over 50 DDIM steps with action guidance applied at each step.

## Method

Vid2World introduces two key mechanisms. First, video diffusion causalization converts the base model's bidirectional temporal attention to causal via masks (requiring no parameter changes) and converts non-causal temporal convolutions to causal using a novel Extrapolative Weight Transfer that redistributes future-facing kernel weights onto past positions via local linear extrapolation, provably preserving the original convolution output up to a bounded error. Training under this causal architecture uses Diffusion Forcing, sampling independent uniform noise levels per frame to expose the model to the full space of noise-level combinations needed at autoregressive inference. Second, Causal Action Guidance injects frame-level action signals through a lightweight MLP and extends the training objective with action dropout (p=0.2), enabling classifier-free guidance at inference where ε_guided = (1+λ)ε_cond − λε_ucond. The paper proves this score composition is mathematically equivalent to sampling from a steered posterior p(x_t|H_t)·p(a_{t−1}|x_t,H_t)^ω, providing a principled probability-space interpretation of action alignment with tunable responsiveness via the guidance scale λ.

## Results

On CS:GO game simulation, Vid2World achieves FVD 106.6 and FID 17.5, outperforming DIAMOND-HQ (FVD 368.5, FID 87.2) by 71.1% and 79.9% respectively. On RT-1 robot manipulation, Vid2World-NAR attains the best FVD (18.7) among all methods, improving over the strongest baseline Action-Conditioned Fine-tuning (FVD 24.2) by 22.7%. On RECON open-world navigation, Vid2World achieves SSIM 0.481 and PSNR 16.10 under autoregressive rollout, surpassing NWM (SSIM 0.389, PSNR 15.34) despite NWM's advantage of single-step prediction that bypasses error accumulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On RT-1 robot manipulation, Vid2World-NAR achieves the best FVD (18.7) over Action-Conditioned Fine-tuning (24.2), ControlNet (27.1), and AVID (39.3), though it trails Action-Conditioned on FID (5.871 vs. 2.965) and LPIPS (0.140 vs. 0.134). On CS:GO, Vid2World dominates both DIAMOND configurations across all six metrics, with a 79.9% relative FID improvement (17.5 vs. 87.2) and 71.1% FVD improvement (106.6 vs. 368.5) over DIAMOND-HQ. On RECON, Vid2World underperforms NWM on FVD (59.4 vs. 31.2) and FID (42.9 vs. 34.1), but this comparison is asymmetric: NWM uses single-step prediction (bypassing autoregressive error accumulation) while Vid2World performs 16-frame autoregressive rollout; on SSIM (0.481 vs. 0.389) and PSNR (16.10 vs. 15.34), Vid2World is superior. The paper also demonstrates that Vid2World's autoregressive rollout in RECON produces qualitatively superior predictions to both NWM and DIAMOND at extended horizons, and that it reliably tracks real-world policy performance gaps in a Real2Sim evaluation on RT-1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of video diffusion and world modeling, departing from two prior lines of work. On one hand, it contrasts with world models like DIAMOND and NWM that train from scratch on action-labeled data, arguing that the fundamental bottleneck is data cost rather than architecture. On the other hand, it extends prior video-diffusion-to-world-model adaptations (AVID, He et al. 2025) that add action modules but ignore temporal causality. The key conceptual departure is shifting from data-level exploitation (co-training with action-free video) to model-level transfer: directly repurposing a pre-trained video diffusion model's learned physical priors through architectural causalization (building on Diffusion Forcing for the training objective) and a novel Causal Action Guidance mechanism (extending classifier-free guidance to frame-level actions).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, general recipe for converting any pre-trained video diffusion model into an interactive world model, potentially unlocking internet-scale visual and physical priors for sequential decision-making without the prohibitive cost of collecting action-labeled data. However, significant limitations remain: the approach is validated only on a 1.1B-parameter base model, training still requires ~7 days on 4×A100s, and the iterative diffusion inference is too slow for practical reinforcement learning loops. Open questions include whether the framework scales to larger models (e.g., 10B+), whether faster one-step or few-step generative backbones can preserve the fidelity gains, and how to close the gap between world-model fidelity and the inference speed required for real-time control and model-based RL.
