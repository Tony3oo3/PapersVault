## Problem

Existing data contamination detection methods for LLMs are built around likelihood-based signals (perplexity, min-k% probability) that arise naturally from MLE-based pre-training and SFT. However, RL post-training—now critical for advancing LLM reasoning via RLVR—optimizes a reward-driven objective that decouples model behavior from token-level log-probabilities, rendering these detectors ineffective (near random guess). This paper addresses the resulting gap: no systematic method exists to detect whether a benchmark sample was memorized specifically during the RL phase.

## Input / Output

The detector operates in a black-box setting, requiring only the ability to query the model for generated text and per-token probabilities (or top-K probabilities, with K as low as 3 being sufficient). Given a problem prompt, it produces a scalar contamination score computed as a length-penalized cosine similarity between two entropy sequences, which can be thresholded into a binary membership prediction. No access to model weights, gradients, or training data is needed, and the entropy computation overhead is negligible compared to generation cost since only top-K masses are required.

## Method

Self-Critique exploits the observation that RL post-training induces policy collapse: for samples explicitly rewarded during training, the model converges to a narrow reasoning path, producing sparse and highly stable token-level entropy distributions. The method generates two responses to the same problem—first a greedy-decoded initial response, then a self-critique response where the model is instructed to produce an alternative reasoning path conditioned on the initial one—and computes the token-level entropy sequence for each. The contamination score is the length-penalized cosine similarity between these two entropy sequences: contaminated samples yield high similarity (the model cannot deviate from its memorized high-reward path), while clean samples show greater divergence. This active probing mechanism is essential because passive entropy checks are unreliable—policy collapse occurs even on clean samples—so the self-critique step specifically measures path dependence rather than absolute entropy levels.

## Results

On the RL-MIA benchmark, Self-Critique achieves an average AUC of 0.70 on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and 0.64 on DeepSeek-Math-7B-Instruct, outperforming the best baseline (Entropy-Noise at 0.62 and 0.55 respectively) by 19%. On Qwen2.5-7B-Math, the improvement reaches 30% (0.74 vs. 0.57 best baseline). All likelihood-based baselines (PPL, Min-K%, Min-K%++) perform at or below random-guess level (AUC ≈ 0.42–0.53), confirming that traditional detection signals are ineffective for RL-phase contamination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Self-Critique decisively outperforms all five likelihood-based baselines across both primary models. PPL (Gonen et al., 2023) and CDD (Dong et al., 2024) hover around random-guess AUC (0.50–0.53), while Min-K% (Shi et al., 2024) and Min-K%++ (Zhang et al., 2025b) are even below 0.50 on several datasets, indicating their likelihood-based signals are not just weak but actively misleading in the RL setting. Recall (Xie et al., 2024b) is the strongest conventional baseline (AVG AUC 0.59 on Qwen2.5-7B, 0.54 on DeepSeek-Math-7B), benefiting from its probing mechanism, yet still trails Self-Critique by 11–19 AUC points. The two entropy-based variants (Entropy-Temp, Entropy-Noise) that share Self-Critique's core signal but use different probing strategies (stochastic sampling, non-member prefix) achieve 0.56 and 0.62 AVG AUC respectively on Qwen2.5-7B, confirming that the self-critique probing mechanism—rather than entropy alone—provides the critical +8–13 point gain over alternative probes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data contamination detection literature (PPL, Min-K%, Recall, CDD) from the likelihood-based pre-training/SFT paradigm to the reward-driven RL post-training phase, where those methods' core signal (token-level log-probability) is decoupled from the training objective. Conceptually, it repurposes entropy—a quantity already central to RL exploration/exploitation analysis—as a contamination signal, but introduces an active self-critique probing mechanism to distinguish RL-induced policy collapse on memorized samples from the general entropy sparsification that occurs on all samples. The RL-MIA benchmark is entirely new, as no prior evaluation isolates contamination to the RL stage specifically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As RLVR becomes the dominant paradigm for improving LLM reasoning (DeepSeek-R1, OpenAI o1, etc.), the finding that every existing contamination detector operates at or below random-guess level on RL-phase data represents a significant blind spot in current evaluation-integrity tooling. The moderate absolute AUC (0.60–0.74) and restriction to math/logic tasks on ≤7B models leave open whether the signal persists in code generation, frontier-scale models, or with denser reward signals. The dual-stage contamination analysis (GSM8K) is a useful step toward disentangling pre-training leakage from RL memorization, but a fully integrated detector that handles both stages simultaneously remains an open problem.
