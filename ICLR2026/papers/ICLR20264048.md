## Problem

In exemplar-free class-incremental learning, prototype-based methods suffer from representation drift as the backbone adapts to new tasks, rendering cached class statistics stale. Existing drift-compensation approaches rely on one-directional, two-stage projections (train backbone first, then learn a post-hoc adapter), which introduce systematic bias and leave cycle inconsistencies that accumulate across tasks. This paper asks whether making the transport bidirectional and co-evolving with the backbone can eliminate these residual mismatches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264048/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images at dataset-specific resolutions (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-100 and CUB-200) and produces a class label via a Gaussian Bayes (Mahalanobis-prototype) classifier operating in a 64-dimensional feature space. At test time no task identifier is available and no raw exemplars are stored; inference relies solely on transported class means and covariances. The backbone is a ResNet-18 with a learned 512→64 linear reduction, and the bidirectional adapter/distiller maps operate in this 64-dim space.

## Method

BiCyc jointly learns a forward adapter A (old→new) and a backward distiller D (new→old) as two-layer MLPs during the main task-training stage, rather than in a separate post-hoc phase. A stop-gradient gating scheme ensures A chases the evolving new space without dragging the backbone backward, while D provides feature-level distillation that constrains backbone plasticity. A cycle-consistency loss (A∘D≈I and D∘A≈I with detached targets) enforces near-bijective transport, which the paper proves contracts the singular spectrum of the composed map toward unity in whitened space, preventing rank loss and preserving geometry. After the main stage, a brief low-learning-rate fine-tune of A sharpens transport, and inference uses a Gaussian Bayes classifier built from Monte-Carlo-pushed old-class statistics and freshly estimated current-task statistics. The approach adds only ~0.26M parameters (≈2.4% of ResNet-18) over the AdaGauss baseline it builds upon.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (from-scratch, ResNet-18), BiCyc achieves 50.6% A_last and 63.2% A_inc at T=10, and 41.5% A_last and 56.5% A_inc at T=20, outperforming the strongest baseline AdaGauss by +3.8/+2.3 pp (T=10) and +3.6/+2.1 pp (T=20). On TinyImageNet, gains over AdaGauss reach +2.5/+3.3 pp (T=10) and +2.7/+4.7 pp (T=20). On ImageNet-100 (from-scratch), BiCyc attains 52.7% A_last at T=10 (best) and 43.8% A_last at T=20 (best), while on CUB-200 (ImageNet-pretrained) it is competitive with AdaGauss but trails EFC by 2.4/3.4 pp at T=20 due to the low-drift pretrained regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiCyc outperforms all 11 baselines on both CIFAR-100 and TinyImageNet across T=10 and T=20 splits. Against the strongest prior method AdaGauss (NeurIPS 2024), gains are +3.8/+2.3 pp (A_last/A_inc) on CIFAR-100 T=10 and +2.7/+4.7 pp on TinyImageNet T=20. The most recent competitor DPCR (ICML 2025) is close on CIFAR-100 T=10 (+0.4/+0.4 pp) but trails by +4.6/+4.9 pp on TinyImageNet T=20. On ImageNet-100, BiCyc achieves the best A_last at both T=10 (52.7%) and T=20 (43.8%), though its A_inc at T=10 (66.8%) is 2.6 pp below LDC's reported 69.4%. On CUB-200 (pretrained backbone), the method is within 0.8 pp of AdaGauss at T=10 but trails EFC by 2.4/3.4 pp at T=20, which the authors attribute to the low-drift regime of pretrained fine-tuning limiting the benefit of aggressive transport. Forgetting (F_last) is lowest for BiCyc on all three from-scratch datasets (e.g., 13.5% vs. 16.7% for AdaGauss on CIFAR-100 T=10), confirming the stability gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on AdaGauss (Rypeść et al., NeurIPS 2024), inheriting its Gaussian prototype framework, anti-collapse regularizer, and 512→64 dimensionality reduction, but departs from the prevailing two-stage drift-compensation paradigm (train backbone, then post-hoc learn a one-way adapter) by jointly learning bidirectional projectors (A: old→new, D: new→old) with cycle consistency during backbone optimization. This contrasts with prior one-directional transport methods—SDC, ADC, LDC, EFC, and AdaGauss itself—which all optimize a single forward adapter after the fact, leaving residual cycle inconsistencies that accumulate across tasks. The theoretical contribution connects the cycle-consistency loss to spectral contraction in whitened space and derives bounds linking transport error to classification log-odds stability, providing a principled justification absent in prior drift-compensation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete structural weakness in the dominant EFCIL drift-compensation pipeline: post-hoc, one-way transport that cannot correct for the evolving geometry of the backbone during training. The consistent 1–4 pp gains over AdaGauss across four benchmarks, combined with the lowest forgetting scores in from-scratch settings, make this a practical upgrade for practitioners working on exemplar-free incremental learning. However, gains shrink substantially in pretrained/low-drift regimes (CUB-200), and the method's reliance on Gaussian prototypes, centered features, and full-rank covariances limits its applicability to non-Gaussian or highly imbalanced settings. Open questions include scaling to ImageNet-1K, handling class imbalance in prototype transport, and whether the bidirectional formulation extends to multi-modal or test-time-adaptive scenarios.
