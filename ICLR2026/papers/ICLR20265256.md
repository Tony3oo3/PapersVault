## Problem

Pretrained vision foundation models (VFMs) each excel in only specific visual domains, and existing multi-VFM distillation methods produce static, monolithic representations that cannot flexibly select task-relevant features for diverse robot tasks. Moreover, these distilled models require costly full retraining to incorporate robot-domain knowledge and cannot adaptively scale computation to task complexity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265256/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (processed into patch-level token sequences by a Base Vision Transformer) together with robot proprioceptive state, and outputs continuous robot control actions via a pluggable policy head (diffusion, flow-matching, or ViLT). The routing mechanism operates at patch granularity for Patchwise Expert Routing or at frame/layer granularity for Teacher Routing, with the policy head receiving only the routed expert outputs plus proprioception—robot-related visual patches are explicitly suppressed after expert selection.

## Method

VER replaces the last N=3 FFN layers of a 12-layer ViT with a Mixture-of-Experts Vision Expert Library (L=6 experts, K=2 active per patch). During pretraining, Teacher-Specific Routers distill DINOv2, ViT, and CLIP into the shared expert pool under a mutual information loss that simultaneously enforces balanced expert usage (maximizing H(experts)) and teacher-specific specialization (minimizing H(experts|teacher)). For downstream robot tasks, all experts and the BVT are frozen; only a lightweight Robot Router (<0.4% parameters) is trained to dynamically select task-relevant experts. The key training innovation is Curriculum Top-K Annealing (CTA), which initializes K=L and linearly anneals to K_min over S steps, preventing the early router collapse that the paper formally characterizes: inactive experts in Top-K MoE receive gradients independent of their outputs (∂L/∂z_l = −p_l·q), so random initialization permanently locks suboptimal expert subsets without explicit exploration.

## Results

On 11 diverse robotic manipulation tasks spanning Franka Kitchen, Meta-World, and Adroit benchmarks, VER-B achieves a 74.7% average success rate, outperforming the strongest baseline Theia-B (67.1%) by 7.6 points and R3M (67.6%) by 7.1 points. Across multiple policy heads (ViLT, flow-matching, diffusion), VER-T consistently surpasses Theia-T, with particularly large gains on flow-matching Pick-and-Place tasks (e.g., 0.95 vs. 0.65 on cross→bin) and real-world pouring (0.90 vs. 0.45). VER also outperforms the fine-tuned VLA model GR00T N1.5 on all three Robomimic Pick-and-Place tasks (0.95/0.75/0.85 vs. 0.75/0.73/0.70).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VER outperforms all seven baselines across the evaluated benchmarks. Against the closest competitor Theia-B (Shang et al., 2024), VER-B gains 7.6 points in average success rate (74.7% vs. 67.1%), with the largest per-task margins on KnobTurn (+7.5), Microwave (+13.4), and Relocate (+17.3). R3M (Nair et al., 2023) is the second-strongest single-VFM baseline at 67.6% average; VER surpasses it by 7.1 points, particularly on DoorOpen (+6.8) and Relocate (+5.3). RADIO (Ranzinger et al., 2024) and VIP (Ma et al.) trail further behind at 61.3% and 62.8% respectively, with VER leading by 13.4 and 11.9 points. The older baselines VC-1 (42.6%) and MVP (48.7%) are substantially outperformed by 32.1 and 26.0 points. On the Robomimic Pick-and-Place tasks with a flow-matching head, VER (0.95/0.75/0.85) also beats the fine-tuned VLA model GR00T N1.5 (NVIDIA et al., 2025) at 0.75/0.73/0.70, demonstrating that dynamic expert routing from distilled VFMs can surpass a much larger vision-language-action foundation model on manipulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-VFM distillation line initiated by Theia (Shang et al., 2024) and RADIO (Ranzinger et al., 2024), which fuse diverse vision foundation models into a single static representation for robot learning. The key departure is replacing the unified representation with a Mixture-of-Experts Vision Expert Library (VEL) equipped with Teacher-Specific Routers, so that knowledge from each VFM is preserved in specialized experts rather than diluted into one vector. For downstream robot tasks, VER introduces a lightweight Robot Router (<0.4% parameters) with Patchwise Expert Routing and Curriculum Top-K Annealing, drawing on MoE routing literature (Shazeer et al., 2017; Fedus et al., 2022) and mutual-information regularization (Wang et al., 2024) but adapting them specifically to the robot policy learning setting where the router acts as a task-aware planning selector rather than a load-balancing mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VER demonstrates that dynamic, task-specific expert selection from a frozen pretrained library can outperform both single-VFM encoders and static multi-VFM distillation across 17 diverse manipulation tasks and multiple policy heads, while requiring only <0.4% trainable parameters for adaptation. The theoretical analysis of router gradient dynamics (Proposition 1) and the identification of early collapse as a fundamental obstacle to MoE training in robot settings, along with the CTA remedy, provide a reusable diagnostic and fix applicable beyond this specific architecture. Open questions include whether the approach scales to substantially larger expert pools or more heterogeneous VFM families, how it performs in long-horizon or multi-task continual learning settings, and whether the patchwise routing overhead remains negligible at higher resolutions or with more complex scene compositions.
