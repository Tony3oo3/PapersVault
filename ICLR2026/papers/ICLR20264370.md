## Problem

Existing distributional RL methods approximate the return distribution with discrete bins (C51) or a finite set of quantiles (IQN, CODAC), which obscures fine-grained distributional structure and makes it difficult to identify states with high return uncertainty for decision-making. This paper asks whether modern flow-based generative models can represent the full continuous return distribution while preserving the convergence guarantees of the distributional Bellman equation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a state (vector or RGB image) and an action to produce a 1D probability density over scalar returns in [z_min, z_max], parameterized by a neural network vector field v(z^t | t, s, a). From this density, it extracts the Q-value as E_ε[v(ε|0,s,a)] and the return variance via a flow-derivative ODE solved with the Euler method and vector-Jacobian products. For policy extraction, it outputs actions through either a rejection-sampled BC flow policy (offline) or a one-step flow policy distilled toward the BC policy (online fine-tuning).

## Method

Value Flows formulates a distributional flow-matching objective in which the learned vector field generates a probability density path p(z^t|t,s,a) that satisfies the distributional Bellman equation at every flow time t, yielding a TD-style loss (DCFM) estimable from offline transition samples. A bootstrapped conditional flow matching (BCFM) regularization, analogous to fitted Q-learning, prevents degenerate solutions and anchors learning. The return variance is computed efficiently by solving a flow-derivative ODE (d/dt ∂φ/∂ε = ∂v/∂z · ∂φ/∂ε) alongside the flow ODE, avoiding expensive backpropagation through the ODE solver. A sigmoid-based confidence weight derived from this variance estimate reweights both the DCFM and BCFM losses, prioritizing accurate return estimation at high-uncertainty transitions. The complete algorithm trains the return vector field, a BC flow policy, and (for online fine-tuning) a one-step flow policy jointly via SGD, with a target vector field updated by Polyak averaging.

## Results

On 37 state-based and 25 image-based benchmark tasks (OGBench + D4RL), Value Flows achieves a 1.3× average improvement in success rate over the best baseline, matching or surpassing all methods on 9 out of 11 domains. On the most challenging state-based task (puzzle-3x3-play), Value Flows reaches 87±13% success rate versus 30±4% for FQL and 15±1% for IQN. On visual (RGB image) tasks, Value Flows outperforms the best baseline by 1.24×. In offline-to-online fine-tuning on puzzle-4x4-play, Value Flows achieves 15% higher success rate than all prior offline-to-online RL algorithms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Value Flows dominates the distributional RL baselines (C51, IQN, CODAC) across nearly all domains: C51 is consistently near-zero on most tasks, IQN trails by large margins (e.g., 15 vs. 87 on puzzle-3x3), and CODAC collapses to a single mode in return-distribution visualization (3× higher 1-Wasserstein distance). Against flow-policy baselines from Park et al. (FQL, FBRAC, IFQL), Value Flows wins on 9/11 domains, with the largest gaps on puzzle-3x3-play (87 vs. 30 for FQL) and cube-triple-play (14 vs. 4). The two domains where Value Flows does not lead are D4RL adroit (50±2 vs. ReBRAC's 59) and visual-antmaze-medium-navigate (75±10 vs. ReBRAC/IFQL at 87), where Gaussian-policy or high-capacity flow baselines retain an edge. In the offline-to-online setting, Value Flows outperforms all prior methods by 15% on puzzle-4x4-play without any modifications to the distributional flow-matching objective, confirming that the learned return distribution transfers directly to online fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

Value Flows sits at the intersection of distributional RL and modern generative modeling. Prior distributional RL methods (C51, IQN, CODAC) approximate the return distribution using discrete bins or a finite number of quantiles, which the authors argue leaves fine-grained structure unmodeled and makes variance estimation imprecise. The paper departs from this by parameterizing the full return density with a continuous flow-matching vector field, deriving a distributional flow-matching loss (DCFM) that is a temporal-difference analogue of the standard CFM objective. It also builds on Flow Q-Learning (Park et al., 2025c) for flow-based policy extraction and on Frans et al. (2025) for estimating the Q-value from the initial vector field, while adapting the confidence-weighting idea from SUNRISE (Lee et al., 2021) to reweight the loss by aleatoric return variance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, discretization-free way to estimate full return distributions in offline RL, with a clean theoretical guarantee that the learned density path satisfies the distributional Bellman equation. The flow derivative ODE for variance estimation is a neat theoretical tool that avoids expensive backpropagation through the ODE solver. However, the authors note that the method cannot disentangle epistemic from aleatoric uncertainty, and the policy extraction mechanism is treated as orthogonal, leaving open whether a distributional-aware policy could unlock further gains. The 1.3× average improvement across 62 tasks is solid but incremental relative to the strongest baselines (FQL, CODAC), and the 3× Wasserstein improvement in distribution visualization is demonstrated on a single task.
