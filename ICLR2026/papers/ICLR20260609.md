## Problem

Prior self-supervised novel view synthesis methods (RayZer, RUST) produce pose representations that do not transfer between scenes: the same latent poses render different camera trajectories in different 3D scenes, meaning these models are frame interpolators rather than true NVS systems. No geometry-free, fully self-supervised model had previously achieved transferable pose representations, which the paper identifies as the defining criterion for true novel view synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260609/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of 256×256 RGB frames from a static scene and produces 256-dimensional unconstrained latent pose vectors plus rendered novel views. During training, pose-preserving augmentations (randomly partitioned inverse masks combined with color jitter and blur) are applied to create frame pairs that share identical camera motion while minimizing pixel content overlap. At inference, no augmentations are applied; the stereo POSEENC estimates relative pose latents between a reference frame and each other frame, and the multi-view RENDER synthesizes the target view from context frames and the transferred pose latent.

## Method

XFactor combines a stereo-monocular [POSEENC, RENDER] architecture (both multi-view ViTs with RoPE embeddings) with a novel transferability training objective. The stereo-monocular design (single context image, single target) eliminates the interpolation shortcut that causes multi-view self-supervised models to learn non-transferable poses. The transferability objective requires that the relative pose latent extracted from one frame pair must successfully render the target image of a second pair sharing the same camera motion, explicitly promoting pose-content disentanglement. Pose-preserving augmentations (inverse masks, color jitter, blur) generate the required same-pose/different-content pairs from real-world video without any external oracle. Notably, the model uses unconstrained 256-dim latent poses with no SE(3) parameterization or 3D inductive biases, and ablations show that forcing explicit SE(3) poses actually degrades transferability. A multi-view extension is obtained by fine-tuning the stereo model to apply POSEENC pair-wise between a reference frame and all others, with training loss as L1 + 0.5·LPIPS.

## Results

On the transferability test (Table 1), XFactor achieves AUC @ 20° of 55.2 / 57.2 / 53.4 / 31.2 on RE10K / DL3DV / MVImgNet / CO3Dv2, exceeding RayZer (7.6 / 5.9 / 2.6 / 2.7) by 5–20× and RUST (13.8 / 10.8 / 4.1 / 5.4) by 4–13×. FID scores for transferred renderings are also dramatically lower: 4.5 / 26.6 / 6.2 / 8.4 for XFactor versus 43.0 / 91.5 / 40.4 / 35.8 for RayZer and 16.2 / 61.1 / 24.7 / 21.5 for RUST. In the pose probe experiment (Table 2), XFactor's latent poses yield AUC @ 20° of 64.5 / 76.3 / 78.9 / 58.5, substantially outperforming both baselines and confirming that the unconstrained latents encode genuine geometric camera information.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RayZer (Jiang et al., ICCV 2025), XFactor wins decisively on every transferability metric: AUC @ 20° is 5–20× higher (e.g., 55.2 vs. 7.6 on RE10K, 53.4 vs. 2.6 on MVImgNet), and FID is 5–10× lower, indicating that RayZer's SE(3)-parameterized latent poses do not transfer between scenes. Against RUST (Sajjadi et al., CVPR 2023), XFactor also dominates: AUC @ 20° is 4–13× higher and FID is roughly 2–3× lower, confirming that RUST's partial-view information bottleneck is insufficient to prevent interpolative behaviour. In the pose probe comparison (Table 2), XFactor's unconstrained latents achieve AUC @ 20° of 64.5–78.9, well above RayZer (49.8–70.0) and RUST (43.7–65.1), showing the latents encode real SE(3) geometry. When the transferability objective is replaced with standard self-supervised objectives (SimCLR or VICReg, Table 6), pose probe AUC @ 20° collapses to near zero (≤ 3.8), demonstrating that the rendering-based transferability objective is critical for geometric reasoning to emerge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the self-supervised NVS line (RayZer, RUST) by arguing that their multi-view autoencoding training produces non-transferable pose latents that merely interpolate context frames rather than encode geometric viewpoint. Conceptually, it builds on RUST's geometry-free philosophy and CroCo's monocular-renderer trick, but restructures the problem around a stereo-monocular bootstrapping stage and a cross-sequence transferability objective with pose-preserving augmentations. It also draws a deliberate contrast with latent action models (Genie, AdaWorld), which extract transition latents from adjacent frames, by focusing specifically on recoverable camera pose representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of TPS as a standardized transferability metric and the demonstration that prior SOTA self-supervised NVS models fail it could reshape how the community evaluates pose-free NVS. The counterintuitive ablation showing that explicit SE(3) parameterization degrades transferability challenges a widely adopted design choice. Key open questions include extending the stereo-monocular POSEENC to handle ultra-wide baselines without reintroducing interpolative bias, and integrating generative rendering to address the blurring artifacts that appear when target poses diverge from context.
