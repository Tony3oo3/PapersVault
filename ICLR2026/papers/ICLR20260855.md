## Problem

In unified multimodal models, fine-tuning for high-fidelity image generation degrades visual understanding (e.g., counting, spatial reasoning), and vice versa, creating a persistent optimization dilemma. The paper identifies the root cause as competing training objectives: the generative likelihood objective can be optimized without engaging the model's understanding capacity, causing the two tasks to monopolize shared parameters in opposition. The key question is whether generation can be restructured so that understanding becomes an active, necessary component of the generative process rather than a passive, competing task.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single text prompt as input and produces a final 512×512 RGB image, but the generation is structured as an alternating sequence of autoregressive text tokens and diffusion-based image tokens. Intermediate outputs include a detailed reasoning plan (formatted as <think>plan), a reflection assessment with an editing instruction (formatted as editing instruction), and optionally a termination signal ('No further edit needed'). The number of reflect-refine iterations is adaptive—the model self-terminates when it judges the image satisfactory, with 45% of prompts requiring zero refinement turns on GenEval++.

## Method

The R3 framework recasts single-shot text-to-image generation as an iterative Reason-Reflect-Refine loop built on the BAGEL unified model. In the Reason stage, the model expands the prompt into a detailed textual blueprint and generates an initial image via diffusion; in the Reflect stage, it evaluates the image against the prompt and either emits a termination signal or produces a corrective editing instruction; in the Refine stage, it executes that instruction to produce an improved image. Training uses a tree-based RL strategy that decouples the Reason and Reflect-Refine stages, with GRPO optimizing the text policies and FlowGRPO (with MixGRPO for efficiency) optimizing the diffusion denoising policies, all driven by a stage-wise reward from a VLM judge (Qwen-2.5-VL-72B). An importance sampling strategy preferentially selects samples with diverse rewards to focus learning on error correction, and the correctness metric explicitly rewards both measurable image improvement and correct termination decisions.

## Results

On the GenEval++ benchmark (evaluated by GPT-4.1), R3 achieves an overall score of 0.689, improving over the BAGEL baseline (0.371) by 0.32 points and surpassing the prior SOTA Echo-4o (0.679) by 1 point, with a particularly large gain on Multi-Count (0.800 vs. 0.625). On GenEval, R3 reaches 0.96 overall, outperforming FlowGRPO (0.95) and Qwen-Image-RL (0.91). On the TIIF benchmark, R3 scores 82.02 overall, a 11-point improvement over BAGEL (70.97) and approaching GPT-4o (84.19). Additionally, R3 improves understanding metrics: ITA accuracy rises from 60.60% to 73.37% (+12.77) and VQA counting accuracy from 79.30% to 84.63%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R3 (BAGEL+Ours) substantially outperforms all open-source baselines on GenEval++: it beats FLUX.1-dev (0.314), Janus-Pro (0.246), T2I-R1 (0.311), and FLUX.1-Kontext (0.343) by 0.35–0.44 points overall. Against the strongest prior method Echo-4o (0.679), R3 achieves a 1-point overall gain (0.689) and a dramatic 17.5-point advantage on Multi-Count (0.800 vs. 0.625), though it trails on Color/Pos (0.725 vs. 0.775). R3 still falls short of GPT-4o (0.739) by 5 points on GenEval++ and by 2.2 points on TIIF (82.02 vs. 84.19), but surpasses GPT-4o on GenEval (0.96 vs. not reported) and on TIIF's Text sub-task (72.40 vs. 76.67 is actually lower; however R3 leads on Style at 93.33 vs. 92.76). On GenEval, R3 (0.96) edges out FlowGRPO (0.95) and Qwen-Image-RL (0.91), establishing a new SOTA among open methods. The reasoning-only ablation (BAGEL+Ours†, 0.593) already surpasses all baselines except Echo-4o and GPT-4o, confirming that the full Reflect-Refine loop provides an additional ~10-point boost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on BAGEL (Deng et al., 2025), a unified multimodal model that handles both understanding and generation, and on the RL-for-generation line (T2I-R1, GoT-R1, FlowGRPO) that applies policy optimization to text-to-image synthesis. Its key conceptual departure is from prior unified-model approaches that treat generation and understanding as parallel or competing objectives (via unified tokenizers, decoupled encoders, or MoE capacity allocation). Instead, R3 reframes understanding as an *active subtask within* the generation loop—specifically, the model must evaluate its own output (reflect) to decide whether to refine—thereby coupling the two optimization trajectories rather than separating them. The training machinery combines GRPO for text policies and FlowGRPO/MixGRPO for diffusion denoising, applied in a tree-structured, stage-wise fashion rather than end-to-end over the full trajectory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a concrete, well-ablated resolution to the generation-understanding trade-off that has plagued unified multimodal models: by making the model judge its own output during generation, understanding is not merely preserved but actively improved (12.77-point ITA gain), and this improvement is specifically attributable to the reflection step rather than planning. However, the cross-topic experiment reveals that the understanding gains are domain-localized (training on counting improves counting understanding but not color understanding), limiting the generality of the co-evolution claim. Open questions include whether the framework scales beyond GenEval-style compositional prompts to open-ended generation, whether the domain-specificity can be overcome with broader training data, and whether the multi-step inference cost (20–35s per refinement turn) is acceptable for real-time applications. The maze navigation extension hints at broader applicability but remains preliminary.
