## Problem

Ethical evaluation of autonomous systems is hindered by the absence of ground-truth labels for ethical metrics, the user-dependent and evolving nature of value alignment, and the high cost of real-world testing. Prior approaches either rely solely on rule-based guidelines or preference-based human feedback, and none explicitly unify objective system metrics with subjective stakeholder preferences under realistic sample and budget constraints. SEED-SET is the first framework to jointly model both evaluation types and derive a principled acquisition strategy that adapts exploration to stakeholder-specific ethical criteria.

## Input / Output

The method takes a black-box autonomous system parameterized by a fixed policy π and a known scenario space X (e.g., 40-dimensional DER placement vectors for IEEE 30-Bus, or 30-dimensional asset placement vectors for fire rescue). At each iteration, the system is queried in a pair of scenarios to produce objective metric vectors y ∈ ℝ^d (e.g., cost, resilience, fairness, spread factor), and an LLM proxy (GPT-4o) returns a binary pairwise preference over the two y vectors. The output is a sequentially generated pair of test scenarios per iteration, a learned hierarchical surrogate model, and a preference score trajectory that tracks ethical alignment over the testing budget.

## Method

SEED-SET decomposes ethical compliance f(π,x) into a hierarchical pair of Variational Gaussian Processes: an Objective GP g: x→y that maps scenario parameters to measurable system behaviors, and a Subjective GP h: y→z that maps objective metrics to a latent stakeholder utility score learned via pairwise preference elicitation with a probit likelihood. The key technical contribution is a novel joint acquisition criterion V(x) = I(g_x; y|D) + E_{q_φ(y|x)}[I(h_y; z|D) + E_{q_ψ(h_y)}[h_y]] that simultaneously maximizes mutual information in both the objective and subjective layers while exploiting the learned preference, balancing exploration of uncertain ethical factors with targeted exploitation of high-utility regions. LLMs serve as scalable proxy evaluators, receiving structured prompts encoding task context, objective metric values, and stakeholder-specific criteria to produce binary pairwise judgments. Sparse variational inference reduces computational complexity from O(n³) to O(nm²), enabling scalability to high-dimensional scenario spaces (up to 40 dimensions) under tight evaluation budgets.

## Results

SEED-SET (HVGP) achieves the highest preference scores across all three case studies (Power Grid 5-Bus/30-Bus, Fire Rescue, Optimal Routing), outperforming all baselines. The method generates up to 2× more optimal test candidates compared to baselines, with a 1.25× improvement in coverage of high-dimensional search spaces. In the Fire Rescue ablation, the full acquisition strategy (MI1+MI2+Pref) outperforms the MI1+MI2-only variant (which explores inefficiently in high dimensions) and the Pref-only variant (which lacks targeted exploration). LLM proxy evaluation is robust to perturbations, with preference scores remaining comparable across temperature (5.4±0.58), prompt (5.26±0.57), and model (5.37±0.56) ablations versus the nominal 5.23±0.56.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Keswani et al. (2024) baselines: Single GP outperforms Random on the low-dimensional 5-Bus task but fails entirely on 30-Bus (40-dimensional design space), while SEED-SET's hierarchical structure reduces the mapping complexity and maintains strong performance. VS-AL-1 (linear SVM) and VS-AL-2 (RBF SVM) both fail on all tasks, attributed to inaccurate linear decision boundary assumptions and sensitivity to noise in the complex preference landscape, respectively. Against Lin et al. (2022) BOPE variants: all four BOPE ablations (BOPE-I through IV) underperform SEED-SET on the Power Grid 5-Bus task. BOPE-I (qEUBO→qNEI) loses convergence in the second half due to premature exploitation; BOPE-II (qNEI→qEUBO) suffers from a disconnect between exploration and exploitation phases; BOPE-III (qEUBO with resampled objectives) is more exploitation-centric and sub-optimal; BOPE-IV (EUBO-τ + qNEI) converges to a sub-optimal preference value. SEED-SET's joint acquisition, which simultaneously integrates mutual information from both objective and subjective layers, achieves higher sample efficiency and discovers higher-preference regions than any decoupled two-phase strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEED-SET builds on the Bayesian experimental design and composite optimization literature (Lin et al., 2022; Astudillo & Frazier, 2019), which established hierarchical decision-making under sample constraints, and on preference learning with Gaussian processes (Chu & Ghahramani, 2005; Keswani et al., 2024) for eliciting subjective evaluations. The key departure is the explicit unification of objective system metrics and subjective stakeholder preferences within a single hierarchical VGP framework, coupled with a joint acquisition function that captures their mutual dependency—prior ethical evaluation tools tend to focus on either rule-based guideline compliance or preference-based human feedback, often arguing for one over the other. The use of LLMs as proxy evaluators for pairwise preference elicitation (following Huang et al., 2025) extends the active learning for moral preference elicitation line of work by reducing the human annotation bottleneck.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to explicitly model the interplay between objective metrics and stakeholder-specific subjective preferences in system-level ethical testing, addressing a gap where prior approaches treat these as separable or choose one. The sample-efficiency gains (up to 2× more optimal test candidates) are practically important given that real-world autonomous system evaluation is expensive and stakeholder access is limited. Key limitations include the stationary kernel assumption, the requirement for a priori knowledge of all objective metrics, and sensitivity of LLM proxy evaluators to prompt design—open questions include scaling to hundreds of thousands of observations via stochastic variational inference, extending to multi-agent and real-time settings, and validating against true human ground-truth preferences at scale.
