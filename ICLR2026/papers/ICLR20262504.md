## Problem

Existing caching-based acceleration methods for diffusion models rely on predefined empirical laws, offline-calibrated polynomial estimators, or handcrafted rules to determine both when to reuse cached features and how to combine multi-step caches. These fixed strategies fail to adapt to the highly dynamic, sample-specific nature of the diffusion process, resulting in suboptimal efficiency and degraded visual fidelity on diverse or outlier inputs.

## Input / Output

DiCache is a plug-and-play, training-free wrapper around any pre-trained DiT-based diffusion model (tested on WAN 2.1-1.3B/14B, HunyuanVideo, and Flux.1.0-dev). It takes a standard text prompt and the model's latent input at each timestep, and produces the same image or video output as the unmodified model but with 2.3×–3.2× speedup. The only user-specified hyperparameters are a probe depth m (default 1, i.e., only the first DiT block is computed as a probe) and a reuse threshold δ (model-specific, e.g., 0.1 for HunyuanVideo, 0.4 for Flux), which together control the quality–speed trade-off.

## Method

DiCache unifies 'when to cache' and 'how to use cache' through a single online shallow-layer probe. First, the Online Probe Profiling Scheme computes the relative L1 difference between the probe-layer outputs at consecutive timesteps as a real-time proxy for the full-model output change; an accumulated error is compared against threshold δ to decide whether to reuse the cached residual or resume full computation from the probed layer (incurring no extra cost). Second, Dynamic Cache Trajectory Alignment (DCTA) exploits the observed similarity between shallow-layer and deep-layer residual trajectories to estimate a trajectory parameter γ̂_t from the probe residuals, which is then used to form a first-order linear combination of the two most recent cached residuals rather than simply reusing the latest one. The key empirical finding enabling both components is that shallow-layer feature differences (even at m=1–3 out of 30–57 layers) exhibit a Spearman correlation of ~0.8 with full-model output differences on a per-sample basis, making the probe a reliable, low-cost signal for adaptive caching decisions without any offline calibration or dataset-level priors.

## Results

DiCache achieves the best visual quality among all compared methods across three leading DiT-based diffusion models while maintaining competitive speedup. On HunyuanVideo, DiCache attains LPIPS 0.1492, SSIM 0.9396, and PSNR 32.79 at 2.34× speedup (507.24 s latency). On Flux.1.0-dev, it reaches LPIPS 0.2704, SSIM 0.8211, and PSNR 22.39 at 3.22× speedup (4.69 s). On WAN 2.1-1.3B, DiCache achieves LPIPS 0.1734, SSIM 0.8885, and PSNR 26.45 at 2.45× speedup (78.42 s), outperforming all baselines in quality while matching or exceeding their efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiCache outperforms all four compared caching methods on every quality metric (LPIPS, SSIM, PSNR) while also achieving equal or higher speedup across all three models. Against TeaCache, DiCache reduces LPIPS from 0.2898 to 0.1492 on HunyuanVideo (TeaCache-fast) while increasing speedup from 2.20× to 2.34×; on Flux, DiCache cuts LPIPS from 0.4053 (TeaCache-fast) to 0.2704 and nearly doubles the speedup from 2.82× to 3.22×. Against TaylorSeer, which achieves the highest raw speedup (3.43× on WAN 2.1, 3.13× on Flux), DiCache trades a small speedup margin for dramatically better fidelity—LPIPS drops from 0.5214 to 0.1734 on WAN 2.1 and from 0.4709 to 0.2704 on Flux—and TaylorSeer entirely fails (OOM) on HunyuanVideo. Against EasyCache, the closest competitor, DiCache improves LPIPS from 0.1558 to 0.1492 and PSNR from 30.71 to 32.79 on HunyuanVideo while also delivering a higher speedup (2.34× vs. 2.12×). Against ToCa on Flux, DiCache achieves substantially better quality (LPIPS 0.2704 vs. 0.3837) and more than double the speedup (3.22× vs. 1.52×).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of caching-based diffusion model acceleration (DeepCache, FORA, Δ-DiT, PAB, AdaCache, FasterCache) by replacing the reliance on offline-calibrated polynomial estimators (TeaCache), empirical transformation rate laws (EasyCache), or fixed Taylor-expansion rules (TaylorSeer) with a runtime online shallow-layer probe. The key conceptual departure is the empirical observation that shallow-layer feature differences correlate strongly (Spearman ~0.8) with deep-layer output differences on a per-sample basis, and that residuals across DiT blocks trace similar trajectories—enabling both adaptive cache timing and multi-step cache extrapolation from a single probed layer. Unlike prior methods that bake dataset-level priors into the caching schedule, DiCache lets each sample's own dynamics determine when and how to reuse cached features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiCache offers a practical, training-free, plug-and-play acceleration strategy that consistently outperforms existing caching baselines across three architecturally diverse DiT models (WAN 2.1, HunyuanVideo, Flux) while remaining composable with orthogonal techniques like sparse attention and distillation. The probing overhead is minimal (2–5% of total inference time), making it attractive for deployment. Open questions include whether the per-model threshold δ can be auto-calibrated without a manual sweep, whether the shallow-probe paradigm extends to non-DiT architectures (e.g., U-Net), and whether the probing cost can be further reduced—potentially through learned or amortized probes—without sacrificing the sample-specific adaptivity that is the method's core advantage.
