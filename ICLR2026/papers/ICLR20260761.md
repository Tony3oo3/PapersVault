## Problem

In Vision Transformers, the [CLS] token and patch tokens serve fundamentally different roles (global summarization vs. local representation) yet are processed through identical computational pipelines, creating an implicit friction that degrades dense feature quality. Prior work addresses the resulting artifacts (e.g., high-norm outliers) through registers or attention bias, but no prior work explicitly disentangles the architectural treatment of the two token types to reduce this friction at its source.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images divided into fixed-size patches (16×16 by default), with a learnable [CLS] token prepended to the patch sequence. The internal specialization is transparent to the input/output interface: the [CLS] token still produces a global embedding for classification, while patch tokens produce spatial feature maps for segmentation, depth, and detection. The only architectural change is that certain layers (normalizations, QKV projections) maintain separate weight sets for the [CLS] stream versus the patch stream, adding ~8% parameters without increasing inference FLOPs.

## Method

The paper first shows through cosine-similarity analysis that pre-attention LayerNorm already implicitly separates [CLS] from patch tokens (driving their similarity toward zero), indicating the model wastes capacity on implicit disentanglement. Building on this, the proposed layer specialization assigns distinct weights to [CLS] and patch tokens in normalization layers (LayerNorm and LayerScale) across all transformer blocks, and in QKV projections within the first third of the model, while keeping the attention mechanism fully shared to preserve cross-token information flow. Ablations show that normalization specialization alone yields +1.1 mIoU on segmentation, and adding QKV projection specialization in early blocks adds another +1 mIoU with only 8% extra parameters. The approach generalizes across DINOv2 (self-supervised) and DeiT-III (supervised) training, multiple ViT scales (B/L/H), and is complementary to existing high-norm handling strategies like registers and attention bias.

## Results

With DINOv2 ViT-L + attention bias, specializing QKV projections in the first third of blocks and all normalization layers yields 85.2% ImageNet-1K linear accuracy (vs. 85.4% baseline), 48.4 mIoU on ADE20K (+2.2), 67.4 on Cityscapes (+2.2), 84.0 on VOC (+1.8), and 48.2 AP on COCO detection (+2.2). Depth estimation improves by up to 6.1% RMSE reduction on KITTI (2.739 vs. 2.917). The specialization adds only ~8% parameters with no additional inference FLOPs, and gains generalize across ViT-B/L/H sizes and both self-supervised (DINOv2) and supervised (DeiT-III) training paradigms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary architectural baseline is CaiT (Touvron et al., 2021), which decouples [CLS] processing via separate class-attention layers after the main transformer blocks. The proposed specialization consistently outperforms CaiT across all dense prediction tasks: on ADE20K segmentation, specialization achieves 47.3 mIoU vs. CaiT's 43.5 (without attention bias) and 48.4 vs. 45.3 (with attention bias), a gap of +3.8 and +3.1 mIoU respectively. On COCO detection, specialization reaches 46.8 AP vs. CaiT's 43.1 AP (+3.7). CaiT actually degrades classification accuracy (84.0% vs. 85.3% baseline), while specialization maintains it (85.3%). On depth estimation, specialization reduces KITTI RMSE from 2.968 (CaiT) to 2.787 (ours), a 6.1% improvement. The paper also shows that specialization is complementary to high-norm handling strategies (registers, attention bias), yielding up to 4.8% relative gains on ADE20K regardless of which strategy is used.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the ViT architecture and its dominant pre-training frameworks (DINOv2 for self-supervised, DeiT-III for supervised learning), proposing a targeted architectural modification: specializing normalization layers and QKV projections to process [CLS] and patch tokens through separate weight sets. It is conceptually distinct from prior high-norm mitigation strategies (registers, attention bias, Gram anchoring) that address the [CLS]-patch imbalance by adding tokens or modifying attention, and from CaiT's class-attention mechanism that defers [CLS] processing to dedicated layers. The approach draws inspiration from double-stream architectures used in multimodal generation (Esser et al., 2024), repurposing the idea of modality-specific pathways to the [CLS]-vs-patch distinction within a single unimodal transformer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic finding that LayerNorm implicitly learns to disentangle [CLS] and patch tokens (driving their cosine similarity near zero pre-attention) reveals a hidden compensatory mechanism that consumes model capacity, offering a new lens for understanding transformer internals. The proposed specialization is lightweight (8% parameter increase, zero FLOP overhead) and complementary to existing high-norm handlers, making it a practical drop-in improvement for dense prediction pipelines. Open questions include whether the LoRA-based approximation can close the remaining gap to full QKV specialization at negligible cost, why DeiT-III ViT-L does not benefit (likely due to absence of a local training signal), and whether the principle generalizes to other token-type distinctions in multimodal or video transformers.
