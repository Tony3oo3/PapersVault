## Problem

Large reasoning models (LRMs) frequently fail due to mismanaged reflection tokens: under-reflection causes premature termination on hard problems, while over-reflection leads to redundant looping on easy ones. Existing decoding interventions like TIP apply a fixed, unidirectional logit penalty, which improves hard problems but degrades easy and medium ones. The paper asks how to achieve effective, adaptive resource allocation of reflection tokens that addresses both failure modes simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely at the decoding stage on a text question, producing a chain-of-thought reasoning trace (wrapped in <think> tags) followed by a final answer. During generation, it intercepts the logits of a predefined set of reflection tokens (e.g., 'wait', 'but', 'alternatively') at each decoding step and applies a time-varying additive adjustment before sampling. No model weights are modified and no additional forward passes are required; the only hyperparameters are the waveform amplitude A and period C, tuned per dataset via grid search.

## Method

CyclicReflex is a training-free decoding strategy that modulates reflection-token logits with a bidirectional, position-dependent triangular waveform δ(t) = A|4·((t−C/4) mod C)/C − 2| − A, oscillating between +A and −A over a period C. The increasing phase of the waveform promotes reflection-token sampling (encouraging exploration and thought switching), while the decreasing phase suppresses it (stabilizing convergence toward a final answer). This design is directly inspired by cyclical learning-rate schedules in optimization, where alternating large and small stepsizes balance exploration and convergence. Unlike TIP, which applies a constant negative penalty, CyclicReflex is both bidirectional (can boost or suppress) and non-constant (varies with decoding position), enabling it to correct under-reflection on hard problems and over-reflection on easy ones within the same generation. The approach incurs zero additional computation cost and integrates seamlessly with external test-time scaling methods such as Best-of-N and beam search.

## Results

CyclicReflex consistently improves accuracy across all three model sizes (1.5B–8B) and four math benchmarks. The largest gains appear on AIME2024, where DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B jumps from 0.42 (Original) to 0.53 (CyclicReflex), a 10-point absolute improvement, and on AMC2023 where Qwen-7B rises from 0.81 to 0.90. On MATH500, Qwen-7B improves from 0.86 to 0.89 and Llama-8B from 0.83 to 0.85. Generation lengths remain comparable to the original decoding strategy, confirming that accuracy gains are not purchased at the cost of longer outputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CyclicReflex outperforms both TIP and S1 across all three model sizes and all four math benchmarks. Against TIP (Wang et al., 2025b), which applies a constant negative logit penalty to reflection tokens, CyclicReflex wins on every benchmark; TIP's unidirectional suppression helps on hard problems (e.g., AMC2023: 0.81→0.85 for Qwen-7B) but degrades performance on easier ones (e.g., AIME2025 drops from 0.31 to 0.30 for Qwen-7B, and from 0.30 to 0.27 for Llama-8B). Against S1 (Muennighoff et al., 2025), which force-inserts 'wait' tokens after each reasoning segment, CyclicReflex is consistently superior; S1 actually causes a notable 6-point drop on AMC2023 for Qwen-1.5B (0.63→0.45) and a 6-point drop for Llama-8B (0.81→0.75), confirming that indiscriminate reflection insertion leads to overthinking. The paper also shows CyclicReflex integrates additively with Best-of-N and Beam Search, further widening the gap over both baselines under constrained compute budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the emerging literature around large reasoning models (LRMs) and their reflection tokens, positioning itself against two recent training-free decoding strategies: TIP (thought switching penalty), which applies a constant unidirectional logit penalty to suppress reflection tokens, and S1, which forces insertion of 'wait' tokens to encourage continued reasoning. The key conceptual departure is the analogy between reflection token scheduling and learning rate scheduling in optimization, drawing on cyclical learning rates (Smith, 2017) and the silver stepsize schedule (Altschuler & Parrilo, 2024/2025) to motivate a bidirectional, position-dependent triangular waveform for logit modulation. Unlike prior work that treats reflection tokens as purely beneficial or purely harmful, CyclicReflex frames them as a resource requiring dynamic allocation, validated through the landscape-of-thoughts interpretability tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CyclicReflex offers a zero-cost, training-free decoding strategy that consistently improves accuracy across model scales (1.5B–14B), model families (Qwen, Llama), and diverse benchmarks (math, science, code), while also enhancing self-correction capability. The optimization analogy provides a principled lens for understanding under-reflection and over-reflection, though the paper explicitly acknowledges that the theoretical foundations remain underexplored and the analogy is not yet formally grounded. Open questions include why LRMs exhibit these reflection imbalances in the first place, whether the triangular waveform is truly optimal versus other periodic schedules, and how the method generalizes to non-English or multimodal reasoning settings. The hyperparameter sensitivity (period C > amplitude A, dataset-dependent grid search) is a practical limitation that could hinder deployment.
