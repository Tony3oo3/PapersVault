## Problem

Existing personalized federated learning methods for vision-language models, particularly prompt tuning approaches, achieve strong local personalization but generalize poorly to unseen classes and domains. Multi-modal adapters that explicitly model cross-modal dependencies have shown advantages over prompt-based PEFT in centralized settings, but their integration with personalized federated learning remains largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262577/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a set of natural-language class prompts (e.g., 'a photo of a [class name]') as input, processed by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 or ViT-B/32 backbone. In the few-shot setting, fewer than 16 labeled support samples per class are used for local adaptation. Output is a vector of class probabilities computed via temperature-scaled softmax over cosine similarities between the adapted image embedding and text embeddings, with the predicted label being the argmax class.

## Method

pFedMMA inserts a multi-modal adapter at the upper transformer blocks (layers ℓ to L) of both the frozen vision and text encoders. Each adapter comprises three components: a modality-specific down-projection (W_d), a shared cross-modal projection (W_s) common to both modalities, and a modality-specific up-projection (W_u), with non-linear activations between stages. The key architectural insight is an asymmetric federated training scheme: all three components are updated locally by each client via gradient descent on cross-entropy loss, but only the shared projection W_s is uploaded and aggregated globally via weighted FedAvg, while the up/down projections remain client-specific. This design enables personalization through modality-specific local projections adapted to each client's data distribution, while the globally shared projection maintains a consistent cross-modal alignment space that promotes generalization to base and novel classes. Communication cost is minimal since only the low-dimensional shared block (3,072 parameters) is exchanged per round, compared to 8,192+ for prompt-based baselines.

## Results

On the 16-shot base-to-novel generalization benchmark (average across 7 CLIP datasets), pFedMMA achieves 84.15% harmonic mean (HM) accuracy, outperforming the strongest prompt-based baselines FedPGP (79.09%) and pFedMoAP (71.05%) by +5.06 and +13.10 percentage points respectively. On CIFAR-100 under Dirichlet non-IID partitioning (β=0.1), pFedMMA reaches 81.82% accuracy versus 80.29% for pFedMoAP. On DomainNet with combined label and feature shift (β=0.5), pFedMMA achieves 47.17% average accuracy, nearly doubling the 24.90% of FedPGP and 24.65% of pFedMoAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

pFedMMA achieves the best harmonic mean (84.15%) across all 7 CLIP datasets, beating FedPGP (79.09%) by +5.06 pp and pFedMoAP (71.05%) by +13.10 pp, while also surpassing PromptFL (83.09%) and FedCLIP-LoRA (83.35%). On local (personalization) accuracy, pFedMMA (97.17%) is within 0.72 pp of pFedMoAP (97.89%) and 0.17 pp of FedOTP (97.34%), confirming competitive personalization. The decisive advantage is in generalization: pFedMMA's base accuracy (77.40%) exceeds pFedMoAP (61.82%) by +15.58 pp and FedOTP (18.00%) by +59.40 pp, while its novel-class accuracy (81.49%) surpasses pFedMoAP (66.60%) by +14.89 pp and FedOTP (36.69%) by +44.80 pp. On CIFAR-100 (β=0.1), pFedMMA leads pFedMoAP by +1.53 pp (81.82% vs. 80.29%), and on DomainNet it nearly doubles FedPGP's average accuracy (47.17% vs. 24.90%). In terms of communication cost, pFedMMA transmits only 3,072 parameters per round (up and down), less than half of PromptFL's 8,192, while pFedMoAP requires 73,728 parameters for download.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly builds on the Multi-Modal Adapter (MMA) architecture from Yang et al. (CVPR 2024), which introduced a three-part down/shared/up projection structure to fuse vision and text features in CLIP. The key departure is transplanting this centralized adapter into a personalized federated learning (PFL) setting with an asymmetric aggregation rule: modality-specific up/down projections are trained and retained locally per client, while only the shared cross-modal projection is uploaded and FedAvg-aggregated globally. This contrasts with prior federated prompt-tuning methods (PromptFL, FedPGP, FedOTP, pFedMoAP) that aggregate entire prompt vectors, and with federated adapter/LoRA baselines (FedCLIP-Adapter, FedCLIP-LoRA) that aggregate all adapter parameters, neither of which explicitly decouples personalization from a shared alignment space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The asymmetric shared/local split offers a principled and communication-efficient mechanism to resolve the well-known personalization–generalization tension in PFL, and the 3,072-parameter per-round communication cost (vs. 8,192 for prompt methods) makes it attractive for bandwidth-constrained deployments. However, the method requires substantially more local trainable parameters (248,832) than prompt-based alternatives, and the shared projection dimension (32) was kept small for efficiency, raising open questions about whether this design scales to larger VLM backbones (ViT-L, ViT-H) or to non-CLIP architectures. The paper also does not explore asynchronous or partial-participation dynamics beyond the standard 10% participation setting, and the benefit of the unified cross-modal shared block over separate per-modality shared blocks is modest (≈0.3% HM), leaving room for further architectural refinement.
