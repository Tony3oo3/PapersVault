## Problem

Existing hallucination detection methods for LLMs, such as the probing classifiers of Orgad et al. (2025), require task-specific training and fail to generalize across datasets, as the authors themselves report that 'probing classifiers do not generalize across different tasks.' This paper asks whether a training-free, mathematically principled signal can be extracted directly from LLM output logits that reliably flags hallucinations across diverse tasks and model architectures without any additional training overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263194/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the raw output logits of an autoregressive LLM at two consecutive decoding steps within the localized exact-answer token span [u, w], which is first identified via heuristic string matching or an auxiliary instruction-tuned LLM. It produces two scalar scores per sample: the spilled energy ΔE (the difference between the logit of the sampled token at step i and the log-sum-exp of all vocabulary logits at step i+1) and the marginal energy E^m (the negative log-sum-exp at a single step), which are then pooled (min-pooling performs best) into a single hallucination detection score. No gradients, activations, or intermediate hidden states are required—only the final softmax logits are read.

## Method

The core insight is to reinterpret the final softmax classifier of an autoregressive LLM as an Energy-Based Model, following the reformulation of Grathwohl et al. (2020). By expanding the chain-rule factorization of the sequence probability, the paper shows that a specific energy quantity—the logit of the sampled token at step i—should mathematically equal the log-sum-exp (marginal energy) computed at step i+1, yet in practice these two values differ because the cross-entropy loss never explicitly enforces this cross-step constraint. The discrepancy, termed spilled energy ΔE, is computed as ΔE = −log Σ_k exp(θ(x_{i:1})[k]) + θ(x_{i−1:1})[id(x_i)], requiring only two forward-pass logit reads. A complementary single-step metric, marginal energy E^m, is also defined. Both metrics are evaluated over the exact-answer token span using min-pooling, yielding a training-free detection signal that outperforms logit confidence and trained probing classifiers across nine benchmarks and four LLM families (LLaMA-3, Mistral, Gemma, Qwen3) without any task-specific training.

## Results

Spilled Energy (ΔE) with min-pooling achieves the best average AuROC across all four evaluated LLMs: 73.16% on LLaMA-3-Instruct, 68.69% on LLaMA-3 base, 77.49% on Mistral-7B-Instruct, and 73.94% on Mistral-7B base, substantially outperforming the probing classifiers of Orgad et al. (64.16%, 62.84%, 65.56%, 62.11% respectively) and logit confidence (54.62%, 56.89%, 63.44%, 49.51%). On the synthetic 13-digit arithmetic task, spilled energy cleanly separates correct from incorrect answers across all three error magnitudes (easy/medium/hard), with ROC AUC consistently exceeding logit and marginal energy baselines. Instruction tuning amplifies the spilled energy advantage (e.g., LLaMA: 68.69→73.16; Mistral: 73.94→77.49) while degrading logit confidence, confirming that the energy discrepancy signal is robust to fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the probing classifiers of Orgad et al. (2025), Spilled ΔE (Min) wins by 9–12 points in average AuROC across all four LLMs (e.g., 73.16 vs. 64.16 on LLaMA-3-Instruct). More critically, under cross-dataset evaluation, Orgad's trained probes collapse to near-random performance (~62–64% average), while Spilled Energy maintains strong detection without any retraining, confirming the paper's central claim that training-free energy metrics generalize where task-specific probes do not. Against logit confidence (Varshney et al.), Spilled ΔE achieves a 18–24 point average AuROC advantage (e.g., 73.16 vs. 54.62 on LLaMA-3-Instruct), and the gap widens under instruction tuning where logit confidence degrades. Against p(true) (Kadavath et al.), the advantage is even larger at 22–26 points (e.g., 77.49 vs. 56.67 on Mistral-Instruct). The only setting where Spilled ΔE does not dominate is on non-aligned Mistral-7B base, where Marginal E^m (Min) slightly edges it out (77.19 vs. 73.94), though both far exceed the other baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Grathwohl et al. (2020), who showed that a discriminative softmax classifier can be reinterpreted as a generative energy-based model, and extends that reinterpretation to the autoregressive LLM setting via the chain rule of probability. It departs from Orgad et al. (2025), the closest prior work on LLM hallucination detection, by abandoning trained probe classifiers (which the authors cite as failing to generalize across tasks) in favor of a training-free metric derived purely from output logits. The partition-function simplification from Zhu et al. (2021) is also leveraged to make the EBM decomposition tractable at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that spilled energy provides a drop-in, training-free hallucination signal that generalizes across nine diverse benchmarks and multiple LLM architectures without task-specific tuning, addressing a key deployment limitation of probing classifiers. A notable finding is that instruction tuning degrades logit confidence while improving spilled energy detection, suggesting the EBM discrepancy captures a different (and more robust) aspect of model uncertainty. Open questions include why the chain-rule identity is violated in practice (a training artifact vs. architectural property), whether the signal degrades on longer generations where error accumulation (Eq. 22) becomes significant, and the observed false positives on non-semantic tokens (punctuation, sentence-initial words) that limit applicability without careful answer-token localization.
