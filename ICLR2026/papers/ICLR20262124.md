## Problem

Knowledge distillation trains a separate student network that is inherently capacity-limited relative to the teacher, and existing low-rank decomposition methods for model compression are fragmented, mostly confined to CNNs, and lack systematic comparison with KD students. The paper asks whether a network can directly inherit both the structure and knowledge of a pretrained teacher in a low-rank form, and how such an inheriting network compares to a student of similar size benefiting from the same teacher.

## Input / Output

InherNet takes a pretrained teacher network's weight matrices (linear or convolutional kernels) as its sole input, performing an offline single-pass SVD to initialize the compressed network; no additional training data is required for the decomposition step itself. The resulting network then processes standard inputs—RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-100, 224×224 ImageNet), text sequences (GLUE), or image-text pairs (CC3M)—and produces class labels, text classification scores, or cross-modal retrieval rankings. The method is architecture-agnostic, applying the same decomposition to both 2D linear layers and 4D convolutional kernels (reshaped to 2D for SVD).

## Method

InherNet performs an asymmetric low-rank decomposition of each teacher layer: a single shared dimensionality-reduction matrix W^down = U_r Σ_r^{1/2} feeds into H dimensionality-expansion expert heads W_h^up = (1/H) Σ_r^{1/2} V_r^T, with adaptive gating G(X) = softmax(W^g(X)) dynamically routing inputs across experts. Unlike LoRA, which approximates weight updates ΔW with random initialization, InherNet approximates the full pretrained weights W using SVD-based initialization, directly inheriting the teacher's principal knowledge. The one-down-many-ups asymmetric design is justified via an Information Bottleneck analysis showing it creates a unified, minimal-sufficient bottleneck with stable credit assignment, whereas the inverse many-downs-one-up design suffers from premature information loss and conflicting gradient updates. Theoretical analysis proves O(1/√T) convergence with an effective Lipschitz constant reduced by a factor of κ (the teacher's condition number) due to orthonormal SVD initialization, and establishes parameter compression bounds and knowledge preservation rates via the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, InherNet Large achieves top-1 accuracy of 78.53% (ResNet32×4→ResNet8×4), 76.39% (WRN-40-2→WRN-40-1), and 75.88% (ResNet110→ResNet32), consistently outperforming the strongest KD baseline (MLKD+Logit Std.) by 0.25–3.6 percentage points across teacher-student pairs. On GLUE, InherNet (T5-Base→T5-Small) reaches 56.34 on MNLI and 90.29 on QNLI, surpassing the KD-distilled student by 2.53 and 1.53 points respectively. On CC3M cross-modal retrieval, InherNet achieves T2I R@1 of 33.01 and zero-shot ImageNet top-1 of 17.39%, exceeding the ResNet-101 teacher (29.31 / 15.70%) and all distilled student networks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-100, InherNet Large outperforms all 10 KD baselines across most teacher-student pairs; the closest competitor is MLKD+Logit Std. (78.28% on R32×4→R8×4), which InherNet Large exceeds by 0.25 points, while the gap widens to 1.56 points on R110→R32 (75.88 vs 74.32) and 3.61 points on R110→R20 (75.88 vs 72.27). Feature-based methods (CRD, OFD, SimKD, CAT-KD) trail InherNet Large by 2–4 points on most settings. On GLUE, InherNet beats the T5-Small+KD student on MNLI (+2.53), QNLI (+1.53), and COLA (+1.32) but is slightly behind on RTE (−1.38) and STS-B (−1.35 Pearson). On CC3M/ImageNet, InherNet surpasses the ResNet-101 teacher on T2I R@1 (33.01 vs 29.31) and zero-shot top-1 (17.39 vs 15.70), and outperforms ResNet-18 and MobileNetV3 on all retrieval and classification metrics, though it is marginally behind EfficientNet-B0 on I2T R@1 (32.17 vs 33.65) while leading on T2I R@1 (33.01 vs 32.65) and top-5 classification (36.65 vs 35.75).

## Relation to Prior Work

InherNet sits at the intersection of knowledge distillation, low-rank decomposition (LoRA), and Mixture-of-Experts architectures, but departs from all three in a specific way: rather than training a separate student to mimic a teacher (KD) or approximating weight *updates* ΔW (LoRA), it directly decomposes the teacher's full pre-trained weights W via truncated SVD and reconstructs a lightweight network that inherits both structure and knowledge. Prior low-rank compression methods (CP/MPO decomposition) tend to make networks deeper and narrower, risking gradient instability; InherNet instead uses a single SVD pass plus an asymmetric one-down-many-ups MoE structure to avoid this. The SVD-based initialization is the key differentiator from both LoRA (Gaussian/zero init) and prior decomposition work, and the paper provides the first systematic theoretical analysis connecting this initialization to convergence speed in the inheritance setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InherNet offers a practical alternative to KD for model compression where the teacher's architecture is fixed and one wants a lightweight model that closely mirrors the teacher's representational capacity without the capacity gap inherent to student networks. The finding that KD can actually *harm* a high-rank InherNet (Insight 1) challenges the assumption that distillation loss is always beneficial and opens questions about when regularization toward a teacher is counterproductive. Limitations include relatively small-scale validation (ResNet, T5-Small) with the LLaMA-2-7B experiment relegated to the appendix, and the method's effectiveness on convolutional layers appears weaker than on linear layers (Appendix A.1), suggesting room for architecture-specific optimization. Whether the approach scales to very large foundation models (100B+) and whether the asymmetric MoE structure remains optimal at extreme compression ratios remain open.
