## Problem

Every adaptive preconditioned gradient method (AdaGrad, Shampoo, ASGO, DASGO) currently requires a separate convergence proof despite sharing similar update structures, and no existing work provides theoretical justification that AdaGrad-type algorithms can simultaneously benefit from both diagonal preconditioning and Nesterov momentum. This paper addresses both gaps by developing a single unified analysis framework and proving accelerated convergence for preconditioned methods with momentum.

## Input / Output

The method operates on convex functions over finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces, including matrix-valued parameters (m×n matrices for Shampoo/DASGO variants), and requires only a stochastic gradient oracle with bounded variance under matrix Hölder smoothness (ν ∈ [0,1]). The preconditioning operator is constrained to a structured subspace H of self-adjoint operators (scalar multiples of identity, diagonal, or row-structured), and the output is an averaged iterate whose convergence rate adapts to the smoothness level ν and the anisotropy or matrix structure of the problem.

## Method

The core framework defines the preconditioner H_k as the solution to a regularized optimization problem over a subspace H of self-adjoint operators, yielding the closed-form H_k = η(δI + proj_H(S_k))^(−1/2) where S_k accumulates outer products of past gradients. By choosing H as scalar multiples of identity, diagonal operators, or matrix-structured operators, a single FTL-BTL-based analysis recovers state-of-the-art convergence guarantees for AdaGrad-Norm, AdaGrad, ASGO/One-sided Shampoo, and DASGO under matrix Hölder smoothness and bounded variance. For acceleration, the paper formulates Nesterov momentum via time-varying functions f_k(x) = α_k^(−2)·f(α_k·x + (1−α_k)·x̄_k) and proves that when the smoothness and noise operators commute with the preconditioner space (automatically satisfied for diagonal preconditioning), the smoothness-term rate improves from O(K^(−(1+ν)/2)) to O(K^(−(1+3ν)/2)), providing the first theoretical justification that AdaGrad benefits from both momentum and diagonal preconditioning.

## Results

The paper establishes two main convergence theorems for adaptive preconditioned SGD. The non-accelerated result (Theorem 1) yields a rate of Õ(||L||_tr · R^{1+ν} / K^{(1+ν)/2} + ||Σ||_tr · R / √K), while the Nesterov-accelerated result (Theorem 2) improves the smoothness-dependent term to Õ(||L||_tr · R^{1+ν} / K^{(1+3ν)/2} + ||Σ||_tr · R / √K). In the smooth case (ν=1), this means the accelerated method achieves O(1/K²) convergence (up to log factors) versus O(1/K) for the non-accelerated version, while retaining the benefits of diagonal/matrix preconditioning. The framework unifies convergence guarantees for AdaGrad-Norm, AdaGrad, ASGO/One-sided Shampoo, and DASGO under a single proof, and provides the first theoretical guarantees for DASGO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's unified framework recovers the state-of-the-art convergence guarantees of Liu et al. (2024b) for AdaGrad under anisotropic smoothness and of An et al. (2025) / Xie et al. (2025) for ASGO/One-sided Shampoo under matrix smoothness, while extending all of them to the full Hölder smoothness range ν∈[0,1] and to a more general noise assumption. Compared to the accelerated scalar-stepsize results of Kavis et al. (2019) and Rodomanov et al. (2024), the paper's accelerated diagonal-preconditioning result (eq. 30) is strictly better whenever the smoothness and noise vectors are sparse (‖l‖₁ ~ ‖l‖_∞, ‖σ‖₁ ~ ‖σ‖_∞) and the solution is dense (‖X*‖ ≫ ‖X*‖_{2→∞}), which is the regime where AdaGrad is known to be practically effective. The paper also establishes the first convergence guarantees for DASGO and identifies its fundamental connection to Scion (Pethick et al., 2025), showing that DASGO with gradient accumulation off reduces to Scion with the ‖·‖_{2→∞} norm, analogous to how ASGO/Shampoo relates to Muon. Unlike the concurrent unified analysis of Xie et al. (2025), this work covers non-smooth and Hölder-smooth cases, uses a less restrictive noise assumption, and—critically—provides the first theoretical justification that AdaGrad-type methods can simultaneously benefit from both diagonal preconditioning and Nesterov momentum.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the unified preconditioning framework of Gupta et al. (2017) but overcomes its key limitations by providing a single convergence proof applicable to multiple algorithms (AdaGrad-Norm, AdaGrad, ASGO/One-sided Shampoo, DASGO) under matrix Hölder smoothness, rather than requiring separate proofs for each method. It extends the anisotropic smoothness analysis of Liu et al. (2024b) and the matrix smoothness analyses of An et al. (2025) and Xie et al. (2025) by covering the full range ν∈[0,1] and using a less restrictive noise assumption. For the acceleration component, it departs from prior Nesterov-accelerated AdaGrad analyses (Levy et al. 2018, Kavis et al. 2019, Rodomanov et al. 2024), which achieved accelerated rates only for scalar stepsizes, by proving acceleration is compatible with diagonal preconditioning under a commutativity condition that is automatically satisfied for diagonal operators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical justification that AdaGrad-type algorithms can simultaneously benefit from both diagonal preconditioning and Nesterov momentum, which is a key missing piece in explaining Adam's practical success (diagonal preconditioning + momentum + EMA). It also delivers the first convergence guarantees for DASGO and establishes its structural connection to Scion, clarifying the landscape of structured preconditioners. Limitations include the restriction to convex objectives, the need for a projection step in the stochastic setting, and the commutativity assumption for acceleration (though this is automatically satisfied for diagonal preconditioners). Open questions include extending the analysis to non-convex settings, designing parameter-free variants with structured preconditioning, and incorporating EMA into the unified framework.
