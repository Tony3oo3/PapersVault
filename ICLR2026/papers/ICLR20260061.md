## Problem

Deep neural networks exhibit an inherent spectral bias toward low-frequency components, which is severely exacerbated in climate data where low-frequency regions (oceans, plains) dominate the signal. This causes geography-dependent high-frequency loss in climate super-resolution: DNNs over-smooth predictions over complex terrain (mountains, coastlines, polar regions) while performing well over smooth areas. No prior climate downscaling method explicitly addresses this coupled frequency-geography bias in its representation learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260061/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution gridded climate field (e.g., 5.625° ERA5, 22 km CERRA, or 2.8125° ERA5 for global-to-local PRISM downscaling) along with geographic coordinates (latitude/longitude) and a terrain/elevation map, and produces a high-resolution climate field at ×2, ×4, or ×8 upscaling. The output representation is model-agnostic: it is a d×H×W feature tensor (d=64) that can be plugged into deterministic backbones (U-Net, ViT, DSFNO) or generative backbones (SRGAN) without architectural modification. A constant offset of 1.0 is added to the frequency-aware representation before modulation to prevent near-zero entries from nullifying the geographic signal.

## Method

GeoFAR learns two complementary representations and combines them via per-pixel FiLM-style modulation. First, a Frequency-Aware Representation (FAR) decomposes the input using Frequency-aware Convolution Kernels (FCK) whose weights are fixed to Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) basis functions of varying frequencies (N=8, yielding 64 channels), creating a multi-granular filter bank that explicitly separates high- and low-frequency content and avoids the low-frequency aggregation inherent to DWT. Second, a Geography-Informed Neural Representation (Geo-INR) encodes each point on the 3D geographic manifold (S²×R) by fusing a band-limited spherical-harmonic location encoding (L=7, 64 channels) with a terrain-differential encoding (absolute elevation plus first-order lat/lon gradients, projected to 64 channels via a learnable 3×3 conv), then passing the fused positional encoding through a two-layer SIREN MLP. The two representations are combined by element-wise multiplication followed by three 3×3 refinement convolutions, producing the final geography-informed, frequency-aware feature map that conditions the SR backbone. Training uses standard MSE loss for deterministic models or a GAN objective (MSE + adversarial loss) for generative variants.

## Results

On the local CERRA downscaling (22 km → 11 km), GeoFAR[U-Net] achieves the best RMSE of 0.180 K and LFD of 9.127, reducing RMSE by 0.092 K and LFD by 0.642 over the U-Net baseline. On global ERA5 (5.625° → 2.8125°), GeoFAR[U-Net] reaches RMSE 1.076 K and LFD 9.068, outperforming all 14 baselines. On the global-to-local ERA5→PRISM setting (2.8125° → 0.75°), GeoFAR[U-Net] attains RMSE 1.468 K, Pearson r = 0.972, and LFD 7.836, again the best among all methods. At the most challenging ×8 downscaling ratio (44 km → 5.5 km), GeoFAR[U-Net] maintains RMSE below 0.4 K with near-zero bias.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoFAR[U-Net] achieves the best RMSE and LFD across all three spatial settings, outperforming the strongest generic SR baselines (SwinIR, SRFormer, EDSR) and climate-specific methods (DeepSD, SmCL, DSFNO). On the local CERRA setting, GeoFAR[U-Net] reduces RMSE from 0.212 (SwinIR) and 0.219 (SRFormer) to 0.180 K, and LFD from 9.450/9.499 to 9.127, with the largest absolute gains over the U-Net backbone itself (0.272→0.180 RMSE). On global-to-local (ERA5→PRISM), GeoFAR[U-Net] beats DSFNO (1.546→1.468 RMSE) and U-Net (1.501→1.468), while also achieving the highest Pearson correlation (0.972). The method is model-agnostic: plugging GeoFAR into ViT, SRGAN, or DSFNO consistently improves each backbone over its unmodified version (e.g., ViT 0.380→0.191 RMSE on CERRA; SRGAN 0.245→0.192). Climate-specific baselines that incorporate domain priors—DeepSD (elevation as extra channel) and SmCL (physics constraints)—are outperformed by GeoFAR on all settings, confirming that the joint frequency-aware + geography-informed representation learning provides a more effective use of geographic information than simple channel stacking or hard constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoFAR sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) climate downscaling formulated as super-resolution (DeepSD, Stengel et al., DSFNO), (2) frequency-aware modeling to counter DNNs' spectral bias (DWT-based SR, Fourier Neural Operators, frequency attention), and (3) geography-informed learning via implicit neural representations (spherical-harmonic location encoding, SIREN-based INRs). The key departure is twofold: replacing DWT (which concentrates energy in the LL subband, worsening the low-frequency skew of climate data) with DCT-parameterized convolution kernels that provide balanced multi-granular frequency channels, and going beyond prior location-only or elevation-as-channel approaches by jointly encoding latitude/longitude and terrain-differential information (elevation + slope) through a SIREN MLP on a 3D geographic manifold. The resulting representations are modulated via FiLM and plugged model-agnostically into deterministic or generative SR backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-diagnosed failure mode in climate SR—geography-dependent high-frequency loss over mountains, coastlines, and polar regions—and shows that explicitly separating frequency bands while conditioning on terrain-aware implicit representations substantially closes this gap across resolutions, variables, and downscaling ratios. The model-agnostic design (works with U-Net, ViT, SRGAN, DSFNO) and the new CERRA 5.5 km dataset make the contribution broadly useful for climate downscaling practitioners. Open questions remain: the geographic encoding currently captures only land-surface factors (elevation, slope) without multi-pressure-level atmospheric conditioning, cross-variable physical consistency is not enforced, and the single-variable focus limits applicability to coupled atmospheric systems. Extending Geo-INR to 4D (lat, lon, pressure, time) and incorporating hard physical constraints are natural next steps.
