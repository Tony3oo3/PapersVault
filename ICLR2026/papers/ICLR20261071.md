## Problem

Self-supervised learning for inverse problems from a single incomplete (rank-deficient) forward operator remains challenging: splitting losses require multiple distinct operators, while equivariant imaging is computationally expensive (2–3 network evaluations per step) and not guaranteed to recover the MMSE estimator. This paper asks whether one can combine the invariance assumption of equivariant imaging with the efficiency of splitting to obtain an unbiased, MMSE-optimal self-supervised loss that works with a single fixed operator.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261071/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a measurement vector y ∈ R^m (m < n) acquired through a single known forward operator A ∈ R^{m×n} (e.g., a fixed MRI k-space mask, a sparse-view Radon transform, or a random compressive matrix) and produces a reconstructed image x̂ ∈ R^n. No ground-truth images are required at training or test time. Experiments span 28×28 MNIST digits (compressive sensing), 128×128 DIV2K crops (inpainting with ~30% pixels observed), 320×320 FastMRI knee slices (×8 accelerated MRI with 40 dB SNR), and 256×256 LIDC-IDRI lung CT (50 views, 50 dB SNR). At inference, the final reconstruction is an average over T=10 random measurement splits.

## Method

Equivariant Splitting (ES) reinterprets a single operator A as an implicit family of virtual operators {AT_g} under a group of image transformations G, then applies the standard measurement-splitting loss averaged over these virtual operators: L_ES = E_g{‖AT_g f(y₁, A₁) − y‖²}. Theorem 1 proves that, under distributional invariance p(T_g x)=p(x) and a full-rank condition on Q_{A₁}=E_g{(AT_g)ᵀAT_g}, the global minimizer of this loss is exactly the MMSE estimator. A new definition of equivariance for reconstruction functions (f(y, AT_g)=T_g⁻¹f(y, A)) is introduced, and Theorem 3 shows that when the network is architecturally equivariant (via shift-equivariant UNets or Reynolds-averaged unrolled MoDL blocks), the ES loss collapses to the plain splitting loss, eliminating all explicit transformation overhead. For noisy measurements, the measurement-consistency term is replaced by the R2R loss, preserving the MMSE guarantee. The architecture is a 3-iteration unrolled MoDL network with shared weights and a residual UNet denoiser, made equivariant either through truly shift-equivariant convolutions (for compressive sensing/inpainting) or Reynolds averaging over 90° rotations and flips (for MRI/CT).

## Results

On accelerated MRI (×8, 40 dB SNR), ES achieves 28.54 ± 2.75 dB PSNR, within 0.2 dB of the supervised baseline (28.74) and 0.66 dB above EI (27.88). On sparse-view CT (50 views, 50 dB SNR), ES reaches 32.62 ± 2.16 dB PSNR, only 1.37 dB below supervised (33.99) but 4.01 dB above EI (28.61). On image inpainting (DIV2K, 30% pixels), ES attains 27.45 ± 2.86 dB PSNR versus 25.89 for EI and 28.46 for supervised. ES also outperforms SURE by ~4 dB on MRI and measurement-consistency (MC) baselines by 4–19 dB across all tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ES consistently outperforms the equivariant imaging (EI) baseline [Chen et al., ICCV 2021] across all four tasks: by 0.66 dB PSNR on MRI (×8, 40 dB), 0.42 dB on real MRI, 4.01 dB on sparse-view CT, and 1.56 dB on inpainting. The gap is largest on CT, where EI's reliance on near-perfect reconstructions for its equivariance term breaks down under the highly rank-deficient 50-view forward model. Against SURE [Metzler et al., 2020], ES gains approximately 4 dB PSNR on MRI (28.54 vs. 24.45), confirming that SURE cannot learn in the nullspace of the forward operator. Measurement-consistency (MC) baselines [Eldar, 2009] are far outperformed (e.g., 28.54 vs. 23.63 dB on MRI; 27.45 vs. 8.22 dB on inpainting), as expected since MC only enforces data fidelity without any signal prior. ES approaches the supervised gold standard within 0.2–1.4 dB PSNR across all modalities, with the smallest gap on MRI (0.2 dB) and the largest on CT (1.37 dB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate self-supervised learning strategies for inverse problems: measurement splitting (which requires multiple operators to escape the nullspace) and equivariant imaging (which uses invariance assumptions but requires 2–3 network evaluations per iteration and lacks unbiasedness guarantees). By introducing a new definition of equivariance for reconstruction functions and showing that equivariant architectures (unrolled networks, Reynolds-averaged denoisers, artifact-removal networks) make the splitting loss equivalent to the full equivariant-splitting loss, the authors obtain a single-pass training procedure with MMSE-optimality guarantees in the single-operator setting. The work also extends the equivariant neural network literature (Cohen & Welling, Chaman & Dokmanić, Sannai et al.) by formalizing equivariance at the level of the full measurement-to-reconstruction pipeline rather than just image-to-image maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides the first self-supervised loss for single-operator inverse problems with a provable MMSE-optimality guarantee, closing a theoretical gap that affected both equivariant imaging and prior splitting methods. Practically, it matches or approaches supervised performance across four imaging modalities while requiring only one forward pass per training step (vs. 2–3 for EI), making it significantly more efficient. Open questions include how well the invariance assumption holds for highly anisotropic or domain-specific image distributions (e.g., certain medical scans), whether the approach extends to non-linear forward operators, and whether the equivariant-architecture requirement can be relaxed further given the observed 'learned equivariance' in non-equivariant networks.
