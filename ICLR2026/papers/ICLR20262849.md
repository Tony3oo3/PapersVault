## Problem

Current unified multimodal models for image editing suffer from an imbalanced division of responsibilities: the understanding module merely translates user instructions into semantic conditions, while the generation module must simultaneously infer the original layout, localize the editing region, and render new content—effectively acting as both designer and painter. This is counterintuitive given that the understanding module is typically trained on far more complex reasoning data, and it leads to a substantial performance gap compared to proprietary systems like GPT-4o-Image on benchmarks such as ImgEdit and GEdit-Bench.

## Input / Output

For editing, the model takes a source image (concatenated with noise along the channel dimension, following InstructP2P) and a long chain-of-thought text prompt (average ~253 words) produced by an external designer (e.g., GPT-4o) that specifies four steps: Global Layout Perception, Local Object Perception, Edit Area Localization, and Edited Image Imagination. The frozen Qwen2.5-VL-3B encodes the image and CoT text into native multimodal tokens, which are projected via a two-layer MLP into the SANA1.5-1.6B DiT to produce a 1024×1024 edited image in ~6 seconds. For T2I generation, the input is simply a long-context text prompt (average ~147 words) and the output is a generated image.

## Method

The core contribution is the Draw-In-Mind (DIM) dataset, which explicitly offloads design reasoning from the generator to the understanding module. DIM-T2I (14M web-harvested image-text pairs annotated across 21 dimensions with long prompts) builds complex CoT comprehension, while DIM-Edit (233K pairs) provides GPT-4o-generated four-step CoT blueprints that serve as explicit design plans for edits. The architecture is deliberately simple: a frozen Qwen2.5-VL-3B connected to a trainable SANA1.5-1.6B DiT via only a two-layer MLP, trained with vanilla flow matching. Training follows a two-stage curriculum—first T2I on DIM-T2I plus public data, then editing on UltraEdit followed by DIM-Edit—while the MLLM remains frozen throughout. At inference, an arbitrary external MLLM produces the CoT blueprint, which the model interprets to execute precise edits, achieving SOTA performance with 5× fewer parameters than competitors like Step1X-Edit and UniWorld-V1.

## Results

On the ImgEdit benchmark (the primary evaluation), DIM-4.6B-Edit achieves an overall score of 3.67, outperforming all open-source unified editing models: UniWorld-V1 (3.26), BAGEL (3.20), Janus-4o (3.19), and Step1X-Edit (3.06), while using only 3.0B+1.6B parameters versus 7B+12B for competitors. On GEdit-Bench-EN, DIM-4.6B-Edit scores 6.18 overall (6.50 excluding Text Change), surpassing Step1X-Edit (6.44/6.35) on the w/o-TC metric. On MagicBrush, DIM-4.6B-Edit achieves SOTA DINO similarity (0.882) with a 1.6B generator, matching ICEdit's 12B FLUX backbone. For T2I, DIM-4.6B-T2I reaches GenEval 0.77 and the best MJHQ-30K FID (5.50) among unified models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIM-4.6B-Edit (3.67 overall on ImgEdit) decisively outperforms all open-source unified editing models: it beats UniWorld-V1 (3.26) by +0.41, BAGEL (3.20) by +0.47, Janus-4o (3.19) by +0.48, and Step1X-Edit (3.06) by +0.61, while using roughly 5× fewer total parameters (4.6B vs. 19–20B). The gap to the proprietary GPT-4o-Image (4.20) remains at −0.53, with the largest per-task deficits in Extract (2.30 vs. 2.90) and Hybrid (2.85 vs. 3.96). On GEdit-Bench-EN, DIM-4.6B-Edit (6.18) trails Step1X-Edit (6.44) primarily due to weak Text Change performance (2.99 vs. 7.40), but surpasses it on the w/o-TC average (6.50 vs. 6.35) and dominates UniWorld-V1 (4.85) by +1.33. On MagicBrush, DIM-4.6B-Edit achieves the best DINO score (0.882) and ties ICEdit on CLIP-I (0.928) despite using a 1.6B generator versus ICEdit's 12B FLUX backbone. For T2I, DIM-4.6B-T2I (GenEval 0.77, FID 5.50) is competitive with MetaQuery-L (0.78, FID 6.35) using a far simpler two-layer MLP connector, and achieves the best FID among all unified models, though it trails BAGEL (0.82) and Janus-Pro-7B (0.80) on GenEval overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adopts the connector-based unified architecture pattern from MetaQuery (frozen MLLM + lightweight connector + diffusion decoder) and the channel-concatenation editing formulation from InstructP2P, but departs from both in a key conceptual way: rather than improving the generation module's capacity or the connector's expressiveness, it addresses an 'imbalanced division of responsibilities' by curating a dataset (DIM) that explicitly offloads design reasoning to the understanding module via chain-of-thought blueprints. This positions the work as a data-centric complement to the architectural modifications pursued by Janus, BAGEL, and UniWorld-V1, and to the scaling strategies of Step1X-Edit, arguing that the editing bottleneck is not model size but the cognitive load placed on the painter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DIM dataset and the designer-painter separation offer a practical, low-parameter recipe for high-quality image editing that is released openly, and the 4.5× inference speedup over Step1X-Edit makes it attractive for deployment. However, the full performance advantage depends on an external LLM designer at inference time (though the self-play variant narrows the gap), and the 32× VAE downsampling in SANA1.5 creates a hard ceiling on text rendering and fine geometric detail. Open questions include whether the CoT blueprint paradigm transfers to video editing or 3D generation, whether the external designer requirement can be fully internalized without performance loss, and whether the 21-dimension annotation strategy in DIM-T2I generalizes beyond the English-language, web-crawled distribution it was built on.
