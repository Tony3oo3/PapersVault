## Problem

Most LLM judges rely solely on intrinsic text-based reasoning, which is inherently limited for tasks requiring precise computation or symbolic verification. Prior tool-augmented judges integrate code execution only at inference time and are tailored to narrow domains, preventing deeper coupling between reasoning and tool use during training. This paper addresses the gap of training LLM judges end-to-end with reinforcement learning to jointly optimize reasoning and tool invocation across diverse, general-purpose judging tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265303/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The judge receives a user prompt and one or more candidate model responses, then produces a multi-turn trajectory interleaving natural-language reasoning steps, generated Python code blocks, and sandboxed execution results before emitting a final judgment. The final output is a scalar score (pointwise), a preferred-response selection (pairwise), or a best-response index (listwise), all wrapped in strict XML-style tags (<score>, <preference>) with code enclosed in ```python fences. Execution outputs are masked during loss computation to prevent the model from memorizing exact strings, and a maximum of 3 tool calls per trajectory is enforced.

## Method

TIR-Judge trains an 8B (or 4B) Qwen3 backbone using DAPO (a GRPO variant) with a structured reward that multiplies correctness, format compliance, and tool-use efficiency into a single scalar, assigning full credit only when all three are satisfied. The training data spans ~26k preference pairs across verifiable domains (math, competitive programming, instruction-following) and non-verifiable domains (helpfulness, safety), covering pointwise, pairwise, and listwise formats to teach the model when tool invocation is beneficial versus when pure reasoning suffices. Two cold-start strategies are provided: TIR-Judge-Distill uses ~10k rejection-sampled trajectories from Gemini-2.5-Flash for SFT before RL, while TIR-Judge-Zero bootstraps entirely from the base model via an iterative RS→SFT→RL loop that progressively refines reasoning and tool-use without any teacher supervision. Key stabilizing mechanisms include truncating interpreter error messages to the final line and masking execution-result tokens during loss computation to prevent overfitting to specific outputs.

## Results

On the PPE Correctness benchmark (pairwise), TIR-Judge-Zero 8B achieves 76.6% on MMLU-P, 94.0% on MATH, 58.5% on GPQA, 68.8% on MBPP-P, and 80.8% on IFEval, outperforming the same-size backbone Qwen3-8B-Tool by 4.6–20.0 points across subtasks. On RewardBench 2 (listwise), TIR-Judge-Zero 8B reaches 73.4% average, matching 96% of Claude-Opus-4's 76.5% despite having only 8B parameters. The paper reports gains of up to 6.4% (pointwise) and 7.7% (pairwise) over the strongest text-based reasoning judges (RRM, RM-R1, Think-RM), and TIR-Judge-Zero (no distillation) matches or exceeds its distilled counterpart on 4/6 pointwise and 3/6 pairwise benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIR-Judge-Zero 8B (pairwise) decisively outperforms the inference-time tool baseline Qwen3-8B-Tool by 4.6–20.0 points across all five PPE subtasks (e.g., 80.8 vs. 60.8 on IFEval, 94.0 vs. 85.2 on MATH), confirming that RL training is essential to unlock tool-use capability rather than merely prompting a base model with a code interpreter. Against the strongest text-based reasoning judges at similar scale (RRM-7B, RM-R1-7B, Think-RM 7B), TIR-Judge-Zero 8B leads by 4.5–27.2 points on IFEval and by 3.4–6.4 points on MATH, while remaining competitive on GPQA. Compared to the larger 32B references (RRM-32B, RM-R1-32B), TIR-Judge-Zero 8B matches or exceeds them on IFEval (80.8 vs. 60.2/63.3) and MATH (94.0 vs. 94.3/95.4), though it trails on GPQA (58.5 vs. 68.4/65.2) and MBPP-P (68.8 vs. 72.8/74.6). Against Gemini-2.5-Flash-Tool, TIR-Judge-Zero 8B wins on MMLU-P (76.6 vs. 73.1), MATH (94.0 vs. 87.5), and MBPP-P (68.8 vs. 85.2—actually loses here), but loses on GPQA (58.5 vs. 60.2) and IFEval (80.8 vs. 84.0). On the listwise RewardBench 2, TIR-Judge-Zero 8B (73.4 avg) surpasses GPT-4.1 (69.7), Claude-Sonnet-4 (69.5), and GPT-4o (64.9), reaching 96% of Claude-Opus-4 (76.5). Notably, TIR-Judge-Zero (no distillation) outperforms TIR-Judge-Distill on 4/6 pointwise and 3/6 pairwise benchmarks, demonstrating that iterative RL alone can bootstrap tool-use without teacher supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIR-Judge sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: (1) RL-trained reasoning judges (J1, RM-R1, RRM, Think-RM) that generate textual chain-of-thought before scoring, and (2) tool-integrated reasoning (TIR) methods like ReTool and ToRL that interleave reasoning with code execution. The key departure is that prior tool-augmented judges (AgentRM, Findeis et al.) inject tools only at inference time via prompting, while TIR-Judge is the first to jointly optimize reasoning and tool-use end-to-end via multi-turn RL (DAPO/GRPO). The iterative self-bootstrapping loop in TIR-Judge-Zero draws directly on the STaR paradigm (Zelikman et al., 2022), adapted here to the judge setting with verifiable correctness rewards.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LLM judges are critical infrastructure for RLHF, best-of-N decoding, and automated evaluation, yet text-only judges remain unreliable on tasks requiring precise computation or constraint verification. TIR-Judge demonstrates that an 8B model with a Python interpreter can match 96% of Claude-Opus-4's listwise performance, offering a parameter-efficient path to strong judging. The finding that pure RL self-bootstrapping (Zero) matches or slightly exceeds teacher distillation is practically significant for privacy-sensitive or API-restricted settings. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond Python execution to other tool types (search, calculators, APIs), whether it scales beyond 8B, and whether the gains persist when the judge must handle truly open-ended quality assessment rather than verifiable constraints.
