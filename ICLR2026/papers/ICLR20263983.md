## Problem

Diffusion models rely on the Fokker-Planck equation, whose reverse-time velocities become stiff and unbounded near the data endpoint, causing numerical instability and limiting few-step sampling. The hyperbolic PDE family—specifically the damped wave equation and its stochastic Kac representation—offers finite-speed probability flows with globally bounded kinetic energy, but had not been extended to conditional generation or distilled few-step samplers. This paper bridges that gap by building a guided, distilled image generator on the Kac/telegrapher backbone.

## Input / Output

The generator starts from component-wise 1-D Kac process noise (not Gaussian) at t=1 and optionally conditions on a class label via classifier-free guidance in velocity space. It integrates a learned UNet velocity field backward in time to produce RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10), 64×64 (CelebA-64), or 256×256 (LSUN Bedroom). Because each pixel coordinate follows an independent 1-D Kac process, the noise distribution is supported on a bounded ℓ∞ ball of radius ct around the data, and the velocity field is pointwise bounded by c√d, distinguishing it from standard Gaussian-noise diffusion pipelines.

## Method

The paper replaces the Fokker-Planck/diffusion backbone with a component-wise 1-D telegrapher (Kac) process, where each image coordinate evolves under a finite-speed random flight with wave speed c and damping ξ. A UNet learns the conditional velocity field v(t,x|y) by regressing against the closed-form Kac conditional velocity, and classifier-free guidance is applied directly in velocity space (ṽ = v_uncond + w·[v_cond − v_uncond]) with a proof that the guided field remains L²-bounded under mild conditions. For few-step sampling, an endpoint-only distillation scheme trains a student to match a frozen teacher's N-substep endpoint in a single Euler step, and a Grönwall-based stability theorem (Theorem 8) guarantees that endpoint agreement propagates to trajectory closeness throughout the interval, yielding O(M⁻¹) error for first-order students. Multi-stage distillation (100→20→4→2→1 NFE) is used in practice, reducing CIFAR-10 FID from 3.58 (100 steps) to 5.66 (1 step) while remaining far below the undistilled teacher's 396 FID at 1 NFE.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, DistillKac achieves FID 3.72 at 20 NFE, 4.14 at 4 NFE, 4.68 at 2 NFE, and 5.66 at 1 NFE (conditional, with guidance w=1.2). On CelebA-64 (unconditional), the corresponding FIDs are 3.42, 4.36, 5.66, and 7.45. The 100-step Guided Kac Flow teacher reaches FID 3.54 (midpoint, 200 NFE) and 3.58 (AB-2, 100 NFE) on CIFAR-10, and 3.36/3.50 on CelebA-64. The paper explicitly does not claim SOTA; its contribution is competitive few-step quality with finite-speed stability guarantees.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct predecessor Kac Flow (Duong et al., 2026), DistillKac at 20 NFE (FID 3.72) dramatically outperforms the 100-NFE baseline (FID 6.42) on CIFAR-10, thanks to classifier-free guidance and distillation. At matched NFE, DistillKac (3.72 at 20 NFE) substantially beats DDIM (6.84 at 20 NFE) on CIFAR-10, and the gap widens at lower step counts (DistillKac 5.66 at 1 NFE vs. DDIM 13.36 at 10 NFE). Against Progressive Distillation, DistillKac is competitive at 2 NFE (4.68 vs. 4.51) and clearly better at 1 NFE (5.66 vs. 9.12), but trails at 4 NFE (4.14 vs. 3.00). Against the strongest few-step diffusion baselines (iCT at 2 NFE: 2.46; EDM at 35 NFE: 1.79), DistillKac is behind, and the paper explicitly disclaims SOTA. On CelebA-64, DistillKac at 20 NFE (3.42) is competitive with DDIM at 1000 NFE (3.51) and far ahead of the un-distilled Kac teacher at the same NFE (11.23), but trails Soft Truncation-G++ (1.34 at 131 NFE) and PDM-DDPM++ (1.77 at 50 NFE). The paper's core empirical claim is that the finite-speed Kac backbone plus endpoint distillation yields a stable, competitive few-step sampler with provable trajectory-level guarantees, rather than absolute FID superiority.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Duong et al. (2026), who first established the telegrapher equation (1-D damped wave equation) and its stochastic Kac representation as a generative backbone, replacing the Fokker-Planck equation underlying diffusion models. The key departure is twofold: (i) classifier-free guidance is adapted to the velocity space of Kac flows with a proof that the guided velocity remains square-integrable (Theorem 4), and (ii) an endpoint-only distillation scheme is introduced with a Wasserstein-space stability theorem (Theorem 8) that converts endpoint matching into trajectory-level closeness—something not available for diffusion ODEs where reverse-time velocities become stiff near terminal time. Conceptually, the work positions Kac flows as the hyperbolic counterpart to diffusion's parabolic PDE, paralleling how Poisson flow models occupy the elliptic niche.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finite-speed propagation and globally bounded kinetic energy of Kac flows offer a structural stability advantage over diffusion, and the endpoint-to-trajectory stability theorem provides a principled justification for why endpoint-only supervision suffices for few-step sampling. However, the practical FID gap to SOTA diffusion models (3.54 vs. 1.79 for EDM on CIFAR-10) limits immediate impact, and the authors explicitly disclaim SOTA claims. A significant open question is whether a genuinely d-dimensional Kac process (with dependent coordinates) can be constructed whose probability path satisfies a hyperbolic PDE while preserving mass, since the current construction is a component-wise product of independent 1-D Kac marginals. The framework is architecture- and representation-agnostic, so extension to latent-space Kac flows on top of a VAE or to transformer backbones is a natural next step.
