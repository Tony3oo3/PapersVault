## Problem

LoRA and its variants face a fundamental three-way trade-off: methods that accelerate convergence (e.g., PiSSA via SVD initialization, LoRA-GA via gradient approximation) introduce expensive preprocessing or optimizer modifications, while efficiency-focused variants (e.g., VeRA, AdaLoRA) sacrifice adaptability. The paper identifies that LoRA's slow convergence stems from the coupled optimization of two matrices (A and B) simultaneously, and asks whether a single-matrix update can match full fine-tuning performance while reducing both memory and compute.

## Input / Output

MiSS operates on a frozen pre-trained weight matrix W0 ∈ R^{d×k} and an input vector x ∈ R^{b×l×k} (text token embeddings, image patch features, or video frame features), producing an adapted output y ∈ R^{b×l×d}. In the efficient form (MiSS^e), the input is partitioned along the k-dimension into r blocks of size g = ⌊k/r⌋, and these blocks are summed element-wise to form an aggregated vector S ∈ R^{b×l×r} before the single matrix multiplication DS. The method is applied to transformer attention and FFN projections (Q, K, V, O, Up, Down, Gate) across text, image, and video backbones.

## Method

MiSS replaces LoRA's dual-matrix update ΔW = BA with a single trainable matrix D ∈ R^{N×k} initialized to zero, where the weight update is ΔW = expand(D): the output dimension d is partitioned into N shards, and each shard's rows are vertical repetitions of the corresponding row of D, yielding an inherently low-rank structure (rank ≤ N). The efficient variant MiSS^e redefines D ∈ R^{d×r} and partitions the input dimension k into r blocks, replacing the expensive matrix multiplication Ax (O(BLkr) FLOPs) with a block-wise summation sum(x) (O(BLk) FLOPs), so the forward pass becomes y = W0x + DS. This single-matrix paradigm eliminates the coupled A-B optimization that slows LoRA convergence, while the block-sum input aggregation reduces both space complexity (O(d×r) vs. O((d+k)×r)) and FLOPs relative to all standard LoRA variants that retain the sequential B(Ax) computation.

## Results

On the primary NLG benchmark (Table 5), MiSS achieves the best average score across all five LLMs: 29.30 on Llama2-7B, 24.13 on RWKV6-7B, 47.79 on Mistral-7B, 42.11 on Llama2-13B, and 68.22 on Qwen3-4B, consistently outperforming PiSSA (the strongest baseline) by 1.6–3.3 points. On GLUE (Table 4), MiSS attains an average of 86.35, surpassing PiSSA (85.23) and LoRA (84.28), with a particularly large margin on CoLA (72.86 vs. 67.28). On the Pareto frontier evaluation (Table 8, Llama-3.2-3B), MiSS achieves the lowest total training time (1867 s) and lowest peak accelerator memory (20.2 GB) while maintaining competitive test accuracy (0.5080), placing it in the top-left optimal region.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MiSS outperforms all four baselines (LoRA, DoRA, PiSSA) on the average NLG score across every model tested, with the largest margins on Mistral-7B (+3.34 over PiSSA) and Llama2-13B (+2.59 over PiSSA). On GLUE, MiSS leads PiSSA by 1.12 points on average, driven primarily by a 5.58-point advantage on CoLA (72.86 vs. 67.28), while remaining within 0.1–0.4 points on the other four tasks. On VTAB-1K vision benchmarks, MiSS matches LoRA/DoRA within 0.1–0.2 points on both image (88.02 vs. 88.08) and video (72.96 vs. 73.02) averages while using roughly half the trainable parameters (0.414× vs. 0.833×). In the Pareto frontier evaluation on Llama-3.2-3B, MiSS achieves the fastest total training time (1867 s vs. 1993 s for LoRA and 2287 s for DoRA) and the lowest peak memory (20.2 GB vs. 22.3 GB for LoRA), while maintaining accuracy within 0.02 of the best methods (RSLORA 0.5299, C3A 0.5102). The single-matrix D design also reduces trainable parameters below LoRA in most settings (e.g., 87.0M vs. 89.9M on Llama2-7B; 70.1M vs. 74.3M on Qwen3-4B), confirming that the shard-sharing structure is both more accurate and more parameter-efficient than the dual-matrix BA formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MiSS builds directly on the LoRA lineage (Hu et al., 2021) and its convergence-acceleration variants (PiSSA, LoRA+, S²FT, LoRA-GA), but departs from the standard dual-matrix BA factorization by replacing it with a single shared trainable matrix D that updates shards of the original weight via a repetition/expansion operation. The key conceptual departure is that MiSS treats the weight update as a block-repeating structure rather than a rank-r product, which eliminates the need to jointly optimize two matrices and reduces the input transformation from a matrix multiplication to a blockwise summation. The efficient form (MiSS^e) further draws on the input-aggregation idea from MoS (Wang et al., 2025) but applies it to a single-matrix paradigm, yielding a FLOPs reduction from O(BL(kr+rd)) to O(BL(k+rd)) that no prior LoRA variant achieves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical PEFT method that simultaneously improves on LoRA in parameter count, FLOPs, and convergence speed, which is a rare combination in the PEFT literature where methods typically trade one dimension for another. The Pareto frontier analysis using the HuggingFace PEFT benchmark gives practitioners a concrete, reproducible comparison across 20+ methods. However, the 'No Free Lunch' experiment relies on a tiny single-layer MLP, the theoretical analysis is limited to gradient-norm observations rather than formal convergence bounds, and the authors themselves frame MiSS as a 'pioneering approach' with open questions about broader applicability and deeper theoretical understanding. Whether the shard-sharing structure generalizes to non-Transformer architectures or very long-context settings remains unexplored.
