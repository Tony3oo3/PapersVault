## Problem

Training Tri-Planes to model large collections of 3D objects is computationally intensive because existing methods train one Tri-Plane per object independently, ignoring the structural similarities that exist across objects within the same class. This leads to redundant computation and inefficient memory usage, making large-scale 3D reconstruction with planar representations unnecessarily costly. No prior work had improved the resource efficiency of Tri-Planes while preserving their planar structure and compatibility with image-based models.

## Input / Output

The method takes posed RGB views at 128×128 resolution (160 views for ShapeNet objects, 50 front-facing views for Basel-Face) along with camera poses, using 90% for training and 10% for evaluation. It produces a Fused-Planes representation (a 64×64×32 tri-planar structure split into micro and macro components) that renders novel views by volume-rendering in a low-resolution latent space and decoding to full-resolution RGB via a Stable Diffusion-initialized VAE. The planar output preserves the 2D image-like structure of Tri-Planes, maintaining direct compatibility with standard CNN-based pipelines.

## Method

Fused-Planes decomposes each object's tri-planar representation into an object-specific 'micro' plane (10 features) and a class-level 'macro' plane (22 features) computed as a learned weighted sum over M=50 globally shared base planes, reducing per-object trainable parameters. Training occurs in a jointly learned 3D-aware latent space (initialized from Stable Diffusion's VAE) rather than raw RGB, supervised by three losses: latent-space reconstruction, RGB-space reconstruction, and autoencoder reconstruction. A two-regime schedule first co-trains the encoder with 500 objects, then freezes it for the remaining 1500, with a warm-up phase to prevent random gradients from corrupting the autoencoder. The ablations show both the latent space and the micro-macro decomposition are jointly necessary: removing either causes quality degradation, while together they yield 7.2× faster training and 3.2× lower memory than Tri-Planes at comparable rendering quality.

## Results

On ShapeNet (4 categories, 2000 objects), Fused-Planes achieves PSNR 30.47 / SSIM 0.957 / LPIPS 0.042, outperforming Tri-Planes (PSNR 28.15) while requiring 7.2× less training time (8.96 min vs 64.32 min) and 3.2× less per-object memory (0.48 MB vs 1.50 MB). On Basel Faces, Fused-Planes reaches PSNR 37.24, again surpassing Tri-Planes (36.47). The ultra-lightweight variant (Fused-Planes-ULW) reduces per-object memory to 0.0008 MB (1875× less than Tri-Planes) with only a modest quality drop (PSNR 29.02 on ShapeNet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary planar baseline Tri-Planes, Fused-Planes wins on all three quality metrics (PSNR 30.47 vs 28.15 on ShapeNet; 37.24 vs 36.47 on Basel Faces) while training 7.2× faster and using 3.2× less memory. Against K-Planes, Fused-Planes trades a small quality gap (PSNR 30.47 vs 30.88 on ShapeNet; 37.24 vs 40.23 on Basel Faces) for 8.4× faster training and 854× less memory, and Fused-Planes-ULW extends this to 512,000× less memory. Against the shared-representation baseline CodeNeRF, Fused-Planes achieves higher PSNR (30.47 vs 28.34 on ShapeNet) with 1.7× faster training, and Fused-Planes-ULW further reduces per-object memory from 0.0026 MB to 0.0008 MB. Against non-planar methods (Vanilla-NeRF, Instant-NGP, TensoRF, 3DGS), Fused-Planes shows lower absolute PSNR (e.g., 30.47 vs 34.06 for Instant-NGP on ShapeNet), but this is an expected trade-off: planar methods sacrifice peak fidelity for the architectural versatility of being directly reshaped into 2D tensors compatible with image-based models, which non-planar methods cannot offer without additional unwrapping or parameterization steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

Fused-Planes extends Tri-Planes (Chan et al., 2022) by introducing a micro-macro decomposition: an object-specific micro plane plus a macro plane computed as a weighted sum over globally shared base planes, explicitly capturing class-level structural similarities that independent per-object training ignores. It also builds on Schnepf et al. (2025)'s 3D-aware latent NeRF framework, but departs by training the latent space jointly with the scene representation rather than pre-training a generic one, which the authors show is essential for preserving rendering quality. Conceptually, the work bridges two previously separate threads—shared/conditional NeRF representations (CodeNeRF, GIRAFF) and planar scene representations—being the first to integrate shared base structures directly into a tri-planar architecture while preserving its 2D-reshapable property.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7.2× training speedup and 3.2× memory reduction (1875× for the ULW variant) make large-scale planar 3D reconstruction substantially more accessible, removing a costly bottleneck that many downstream 3D-vision pipelines (generation, editing, classification) currently treat as a given. The preservation of the planar structure means Fused-Planes drops into existing image-based 3D pipelines without architectural changes. Limitations inherited from Tri-Planes (bounded scenes, difficulty with fine details) remain, and the method's applicability to highly diverse or unbounded scenes is untested. An open question is whether the shared base planes generalize across very different object categories or require retraining per class family.
