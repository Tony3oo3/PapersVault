## Problem

While LLMs have achieved high formal accuracy in generating poems with correct meter and rhyme, existing evaluation metrics are confined to these structural features or overly general text-generation qualities like fluency and coherence. No prior framework systematically assesses the advanced creative dimensions that define poetry's artistic essence—creativity, idiosyncrasy, emotional resonance, imagery, and literary devices—leaving it impossible to determine whether LLMs can genuinely match human poets in poetic artistry.

## Input / Output

Each poem is generated from a prompt specifying one of seven fixed forms (ballad, ghazal, limerick, pantoum, sestina, sonnet, villanelle) along with its specific meter pattern, rhyme scheme, and theme. Evaluation yields scores across 10 dimensions on a 5-point Likert scale (with a 0 option for N/A on imagery and literary devices) plus open-ended justifications for three dimensions. The rule-based component additionally outputs a binary form-accuracy verdict per poem (using a 0.7 match threshold), a MATTR lexical diversity score, and a word-level repetition rate relative to the source human poem.

## Method

POEMetric triangulates three evaluation approaches: a handcrafted rule-based algorithm that extracts stress patterns and rhyme feet via CMUdict and validates form-specific structural constraints (e.g., villanelle refrains, sestina end-word permutations, ghazal radif/qafia) with a 70% tolerance threshold; an LLM-as-a-judge (Gemini-2.5-Pro, selected via pilot study for highest human agreement at PAo=0.662 and best discriminative variance) prompted with 10 Likert-scale questions and 3 open-ended questions; and validation by 7 domain-expert human judges on a 58-poem subset. The framework is applied to 203 curated human poems spanning 200 years and 6,090 poems generated by 30 LLMs from 7 AI companies, all responding to identical prompts. The 10 dimensions are organized into three tiers—basic instruction-following (form accuracy, theme alignment), advanced creative abilities (creativity, lexical diversity, idiosyncrasy, emotional resonance, imagery, literary devices), and general appraisal (overall quality, authorship estimation)—with the advanced tier drawing directly on traditional literary criticism ('Practical Criticism') to capture features previously overlooked by automated poetry evaluation.

## Results

Across 6,090 LLM poems and 203 human poems evaluated with POEMetric (10 dimensions, 5-point scale), Gemini-2.5-Pro achieved the highest form accuracy (4.26/5.00) and theme alignment (4.99/5.00), while Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct ranked lowest (2.29 and 4.91). Human poets outperformed all LLMs on advanced creative abilities: creativity (4.02), idiosyncrasy (3.95), emotional resonance (4.06), imagery (4.49), and literary devices (4.67). In overall poem quality, humans scored 4.22 versus the best LLM DeepSeek-R1 at 3.20. Rule-based evaluation confirmed LLMs achieved high form accuracy (Gemini-2.5-Pro: 0.50, Claude-3.7-Sonnet: 0.47) and higher MATTR than humans, but exhibited significantly higher word-level repetition relative to the source human poems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares 30 LLMs (7 representative models highlighted) against 203 human poems. Gemini-2.5-Pro leads all LLMs in basic instruction-following (form accuracy 4.26, theme alignment 4.99) and also serves as the LLM-as-a-judge, achieving PAo = 0.662 with human experts. DeepSeek-R1 is the strongest LLM on advanced creative abilities and overall poem quality (3.20), yet still trails humans by a full point (4.22); it is the only LLM to edge out humans on lexical diversity (3.85 vs. 3.82). Claude-3.7-Sonnet ranks second in form accuracy (rule-based: 0.47) and is noted as most similar to humans in word-frequency distribution (cosine similarity 0.602). Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct consistently ranks last across form accuracy, advanced creative abilities, and overall quality. GPT-4o and GPT-4 outperform their 'thinking' counterparts (o1, o3-mini) in poem generation, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill models generally underperform the original DeepSeek-R1. On authorship estimation, Gemini-2.5-Pro recognized 39.4% of human poems by name or style, while human experts scored all 13 sampled human poems at 3+ (neutral or above) but were less confident on LLM poems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior poetry evaluation work (ByGPT5, PoeLM, GPoet, ProFTAP) that focused almost exclusively on formal constraints—meter accuracy, rhyme patterns, BLEU, and perplexity—or on overly general text-generation metrics like fluency and coherence. POEMetric carves new ground by integrating a rule-based form-detection algorithm with LLM-as-a-judge scoring across six advanced creative dimensions (creativity, lexical diversity, idiosyncrasy, emotional resonance, imagery, literary devices) drawn from traditional literary criticism (Practical Criticism), a combination absent from prior poetry-specific benchmarks. The LLM-as-a-judge paradigm builds on recent NLP evaluation work (Yu et al. 2024; Bavaresco et al. 2025) but is here specialized to poetic aesthetics and validated against a small panel of domain-expert human judges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

POEMetric provides the first quantified, multi-dimensional gap analysis between LLM and human poetry, showing that formal competence (form accuracy ~4.26/5) coexists with a persistent deficit in the dimensions that define artistic poetry (creativity, idiosyncrasy, emotional resonance). This reframes the poetry-generation challenge from a form-matching problem to a creative-expression problem, with implications for how future models might be trained or prompted. Key limitations include the restriction to English fixed forms (free verse is explicitly deferred), a modest human validation sample (58 poems, 7 judges), and reliance on a single LLM judge (Gemini-2.5-Pro) whose selection, while justified, introduces a single point of methodological failure. Open questions include whether the creative gap narrows with next-generation models and whether the framework transfers to non-English or free-verse poetry.
