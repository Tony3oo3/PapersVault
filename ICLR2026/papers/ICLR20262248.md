## Problem

Existing visual token pruning methods for LVLMs fall into attention-based and diversity-based camps, yet their actual behaviors—how much feature diversity they truly preserve, how retained diversity relates to hallucination, and when each paradigm is preferable—remain insufficiently characterized. No prior work has systematically quantified these properties or shown that image complexity systematically determines which pruning strategy is effective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262248/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the 576 visual token embeddings produced by a CLIP vision encoder (4096-dim) for an input RGB image, along with a text prompt. It outputs a pruned subset of K tokens (typically 32, 64, or 128) that are fed into the LLM decoder to generate a text response. The pruning is performed entirely at the vision encoder output (pre-pruning), so the reduced token count applies to all subsequent LLM layers, and the adaptive threshold is computed from the erank of the input image's token matrix, requiring only a single N×N covariance eigen-decomposition (~3.4 ms overhead).

## Method

The paper first provides an empirical characterization using effective rank (erank) and attention entropy, revealing that diversity-based methods preserve substantially more feature diversity than attention-based methods but also induce higher hallucination rates (CHAIR), and that simple images (low erank/entropy) favor attention-based selection while complex images (high erank/entropy) favor diversity-based selection. Building on these findings, the proposed adaptive pruning procedure sorts all tokens by descending [CLS] attention score, then iteratively selects the highest-ranked unpruned token and removes all remaining candidates whose cosine distance falls below an adaptive threshold τ_i. The threshold is set as τ_i = order_i × (erank_input / erank_avg × 0.01), where erank_avg is the mean erank over the LLaVA training set, so complex images receive a larger threshold (aggressive redundancy removal, promoting diversity) while simple images receive a smaller threshold (conservative pruning, preserving fine-grained high-attention tokens). This training-free mechanism is validated across LLaVA-1.5-7B/13B, LLaVA-NeXT-7B, and Qwen2.5-VL-7B on nine standard benchmarks plus CHAIR hallucination evaluation.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B with 576-to-64 token pruning, AgilePruner achieves 96.76% relative performance across 9 benchmarks, outperforming the strongest baselines VisPruner (95.07%) and DivPrune (95.02%) by approximately 1.7-1.8%. At the more aggressive 32-token setting, the gap widens to 94.02% versus DivPrune's 92.16%. On the CHAIR hallucination benchmark (64 tokens), the method achieves C_S=52.2 and C_I=15.9 with 75.7% recall, closely matching the full-token baseline (C_S=51.0, C_I=13.9, recall=78.7%), while diversity-based methods like DivPrune (C_S=57.4, C_I=18.0) and FPSPruner (C_S=58.6, C_I=18.6) show substantially higher hallucination rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of visual token pruning: attention-based selection (FasterVLM, VisPruner, PruMerge+) that retains high-[CLS]-attention tokens, and diversity-based selection (DivPrune, FPS) that reduces inter-token redundancy. Rather than proposing a fundamentally new pruning mechanism, the paper's core contribution is an empirical characterization framework using effective rank (erank) and attention entropy to quantify how much feature diversity each paradigm actually preserves and how that diversity relates to hallucination. The resulting adaptive thresholding method is a minimal combination of attention-ordered selection with similarity-based pruning, informed by the erank-driven insight that image complexity should modulate the pruning aggressiveness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that higher retained diversity correlates with increased hallucination (opposite to the intuitive expectation) provides a principled explanation for why purely diversity-based methods underperform on hallucination-sensitive tasks despite achieving higher recall. The image-complexity-dependent preference between attention and diversity pruning offers a practical diagnostic for when each strategy succeeds or fails, and the adaptive rule transfers consistently across LLaVA-1.5-7B/13B, LLaVA-NeXT-7B, and Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Open questions include whether the erank-based adaptation generalizes to video inputs with temporal redundancy, whether the hallucination-diversity tradeoff persists in larger or differently-architected models, and whether the simple linear scaling in Eq. 6 is optimal or whether more sophisticated mappings could further close the gap to full-token performance.
