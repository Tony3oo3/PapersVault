## Problem

Object-centric representations for vision-based RL suffer from two coupled limitations: (1) reliance on single-view 2D features prevents reliable inference of depth, occlusion, and 3D spatial poses, and (2) unsupervised slot variables remain ambiguous, encoding 2D feature clusters rather than distinct physically manipulable objects with their semantic attributes. The entanglement between object configurations and camera poses further degrades 3D reasoning, limiting generalization to novel viewpoints and compositional object arrangements.

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes multi-view RGB images (six views for Clevr3D, three views for IsaacGym3D) of both the current scene and a goal image, producing a structured latent embedding of N slots (background, agent, and N−2 object slots) where each object slot is further decomposed into M blocks of D_block dimensions encoding distinct attributes. The Slot Mixer decoder can synthesize novel-view RGB images from these slots, and the block transformer policy maps the structured current/goal embeddings to continuous robot actions (Franka Panda end-effector commands). At test time, the policy accepts a variable number of views (including a single novel viewpoint) while maintaining view-agnostic 3D reasoning.

## Method

The architecture combines a Scene Representation Transformer (SRT) encoder with a novel 3D block-slot attention module and a Slot Mixer light-field decoder. Object slots are decomposed into M blocks, each updated independently via GRU+MLP and constrained by a learnable concept memory (K prototype vectors) that acts as a soft information bottleneck for attribute binding; background and agent slots instead use vanilla slot attention with fixed indices, enforced by an auxiliary pixel-wise mask loss. The block transformer policy performs Hungarian matching between current and goal objects based on semantic (non-positional) block attributes, then applies block-wise cross-attention (rather than object-wise) between matched pairs, pooling the resulting per-block features before a self-attention + MLP head outputs actions and Q-values. Training is two-stage: the encoder is pre-trained self-supervised with L2 reconstruction loss for novel-view synthesis plus the auxiliary mask loss, then the policy is trained with TD3+HER using the frozen encoder's structured embeddings as state and goal tokens.

## Results

On the IsaacGym3D goal-conditioned RL task, the proposed 3D block-slot representation with the Block Transformer (BT) policy achieves 89.5% success rate on compositional generalization (CG) and 82.8% on out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization, substantially outperforming DLPv2+EIT (74.7% CG, 42.2% OOD) and OSRT+EIT (75.8% CG, 70.0% OOD). On the hardest CG (same color) setting, the method reaches 83.7% versus 38.8% (DLPv2) and 41.4% (OSRT). In terms of representation quality, the 3D block-slot encoder achieves FG-ARI of 0.942 on Clevr3D and 0.619 on IsaacGym3D (vs. 0.365 and 0.321 for OSRT), with DCI disentanglement scores of 0.867/0.789/0.844 (D/C/I) on Clevr3D versus 0.140/0.083/0.452 for OSRT, while maintaining comparable PSNR (31.11 vs. 31.57). Under unseen viewpoints, the method retains 86.5% OOD success rate with multi-view input and 75.8% with a single novel view, whereas DLPv2+EIT collapses to 7.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DLPv2+EIT (2D object-centric), the proposed method wins decisively on all generalization settings: CG (89.5% vs. 74.7%), CG same color (83.7% vs. 38.8%), and OOD (82.8% vs. 42.2%), while being marginally lower on in-distribution (96.7% vs. 98.4%). Against OSRT+EIT (3D object-centric without block factorization), the method improves CG by 13.7 points, CG same color by 42.3 points, and OOD by 12.8 points, confirming that attribute-level disentanglement is critical for compositional transfer. The ablation comparing Ours+EIT vs. Ours+BT shows that the block-wise cross-attention policy adds 12.2 points on CG, 15.5 points on CG same color, and 24.6 points on OOD over the same representation with the standard Entity Interaction Transformer, demonstrating that block-level matching (via Hungarian matching on static attributes) is essential for precise goal conditioning. On representation quality (Table 1), the 3D block-slot encoder achieves FG-ARI of 0.942 on Clevr3D (vs. 0.365 for OSRT) and 0.619 on IsaacGym3D (vs. 0.321), with DCI disentanglement scores roughly 6× higher on Clevr3D, while PSNR remains within 0.5 dB. Under unseen viewpoints (Table 3), DLPv2+EIT drops to 5.9–7.8% success rate across all settings, whereas the proposed method maintains 80.2–94.8%, confirming that the 3D light-field decoder provides genuine viewpoint invariance that 2D per-view encodings cannot match.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: 3D object-centric scene representation (OSRT, COLF, slot-guided radiance fields) and attribute-level structural factorization (Neural Systematic Binder / SysBinder's block-slot attention). Prior 3D methods like OSRT produce permutation-invariant slots without decomposing object attributes, while 2D factorization methods like DLPv2 and SysBinder lack multi-view 3D reasoning. The key architectural departure is lifting block-slot attention into the 3D multi-view setting via the SRT encoder and Slot Mixer light-field decoder, and explicitly fixing background and agent slot indices with auxiliary mask losses rather than treating all slots symmetrically. On the policy side, the block transformer replaces object-wise cross-attention (as in ECRL's Entity Interaction Transformer) with block-wise cross-attention preceded by Hungarian matching on static attributes, enabling attribute-level goal conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of 3D-awareness and attribute disentanglement addresses two simultaneous failure modes of prior object-centric RL: viewpoint sensitivity and inability to recombine attributes for novel objects. The view-agnostic property—maintaining >80% success rate with a single novel test-time view after multi-view training—is practically significant for deployment where camera placement is not fully controlled. However, the evaluation is confined to synthetic push tasks with at most two objects on a white table, and the one-to-one static Hungarian matching assumption limits applicability to more complex many-to-one or dynamic correspondence scenarios. The authors note the potential bridge to Vision-Language-Action frameworks, but real-world validation and scaling beyond tabletop push tasks remain open.
