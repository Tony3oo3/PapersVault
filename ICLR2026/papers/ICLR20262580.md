## Problem

Expressive graph embeddings must distinguish graphs that differ by a single edge, while differentially private embeddings must produce similar outputs for such neighboring graphs — a fundamental tension that has not been formally characterized. Prior work on private graph learning optimizes utility under privacy constraints but provides no expressivity guarantees, and no prior work quantifies how the choice of structural patterns affects both the privacy budget and the discriminative power of the resulting embeddings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the graph's adjacency structure (no node or edge features) along with a vector of d=50 pattern graphs sampled from a chosen graph class F (e.g., trees, cycles, or bounded-treewidth graphs). It outputs a d-dimensional noisy homomorphism density vector in R^d, where each coordinate is the normalized homomorphism count for one pattern plus independent Gaussian noise calibrated to that pattern's smooth sensitivity. Because the embedding is a one-time pre-processing step, it can be fed into any downstream classifier or regressor (k-NN, SVM, random forest) without incurring additional privacy cost.

## Method

The core mechanism computes homomorphism densities t(F,G) = hom(F,G)/|V(G)|^{|V(F)|} for a set of sampled patterns, then adds Gaussian noise with variance proportional to the squared β-smooth sensitivity of the density vector, yielding a (2ρ′ + d·4β², 1/4β)-tCDP guarantee. Two key insights drive the design: (i) because DP noise has mean zero, the noisy embeddings remain unbiased and retain full F-expressivity in expectation (and completeness when F = all graphs), and (ii) using smooth sensitivity rather than global sensitivity yields substantially tighter noise calibration, especially for bounded-degree graphs where the sensitivity scales as (Δ_max/n)^(m-2) rather than the looser counting-lemma bound. The explicit trade-off is quantified in Proposition 4.9: the required noise variance is O((max_{F∈F} e(F))²/n⁴), so more expressive pattern classes (with denser patterns) demand proportionally more noise, directly linking the GNN architecture's expressivity level to the privacy-utility cost.

## Results

On the OGBG molecular benchmarks at ε=1, the private homomorphism density embeddings achieve AUC of 0.692 (MOLHIV), 0.602 (MOLBBBP), 0.652 (MOLBACE), and RMSE of 1.086 (MOLLIPO), retaining approximately 90% of the non-private performance (0.745, 0.644, 0.752, 1.055 respectively). Privacy attacks that recover 95–100% of edges on non-private embeddings drop to 0.3–2.7% attack accuracy under ε=1. On the synthetic SBM dataset, cycle patterns (more expressive) yield substantially higher classification accuracy than tree patterns, while on MOLHIV, increasing pattern treewidth from 1 to 3 actually degrades AUC due to the additional noise required for privacy (Proposition 4.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two GNN-based privacy baselines from Hidano & Murakami (2024): Randomized Response (RR) and degree-preserving Randomized Response (DPRR), both evaluated at ε=1. The proposed homomorphism density embeddings decisively outperform RR on all four OGBG datasets (e.g., MOLHIV AUC 0.692 vs. 0.488; MOLLIPO RMSE 1.086 vs. 1.568). Against DPRR, the proposed method wins on MOLHIV (0.692 vs. 0.595), MOLBBBP (0.602 vs. 0.539), and MOLLIPO (1.086 vs. 1.499), while MOLBACE is essentially tied (0.652 vs. 0.648). Notably, the non-private GNN baseline (ε=∞) achieves slightly better utility than the non-private homomorphism densities on MOLBACE (0.768 vs. 0.752) and MOLLIPO (1.033 vs. 1.055), but the private homomorphism embeddings still surpass the non-private GNN on MOLHIV (0.692 vs. 0.672) and MOLBBBP (0.602 vs. 0.586) while providing formal privacy guarantees. The authors also note that on network datasets (REDDIT-BINARY, REDDIT-MULTI-5K, GitHub STARGAZERS), their embeddings achieve performance comparable to Hidano & Murakami (2024) despite using simpler classifiers (k-NN, SVM, random forests) rather than full GNNs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously independent research threads: homomorphism-based graph expressivity (Lovász 1967; Welke et al. 2023; Nguyen & Maehara 2020) and differential privacy for graph learning (Sajadmanesh et al. 2023; Hidano & Murakami 2024). It departs from prior DP graph learning work, which optimizes utility under privacy constraints without expressivity guarantees, and from prior expressivity analysis (k-WL hierarchy, subgraph GNNs), which ignores privacy entirely. The technical core combines the tCDP framework (Bun et al. 2018) with smooth sensitivity (Nissim et al. 2007) to calibrate noise on homomorphism densities, yielding a mechanism that is simultaneously F-expectation-expressive and differentially private.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formal characterization of the expressivity-privacy trade-off in graph representation learning, showing that the pattern class determining expressivity directly controls the noise required for privacy (Proposition 4.9). The practical implication is that practitioners cannot freely choose the most expressive pattern class without incurring a privacy-utility cost, a constraint absent from prior DP graph learning literature. Key limitations include the computational cost of homomorphism counting (mitigated for bounded-treewidth patterns) and the exclusion of node/edge features, which may be critical for real-world tasks. Open questions include refining sensitivity bounds for specific graph classes and privately encoding edge features to improve the trade-off.
