## Problem

Existing Dataset Ownership Verification (DOV) methods for LLMs—membership inference attacks, canaries, and backdoors—rely on the model regurgitating verbatim training data or require access to full model logits, making them vulnerable to privacy-preserving generation filters and impractical against closed-source models. This paper asks whether a dataset owner can embed a detectable signal into training data such that the targeted behavior (a secret prompt-response pair) is entirely absent from the corpus, enabling certifiable detection with only top-ℓ predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261488/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At detection time, Alice feeds a secret prompt (an out-of-distribution token sequence of ~256 tokens) to Bob's model and observes only the top-ℓ most probable tokens at each position of the expected secret response (e.g., ℓ=4 to 20). From these top-ℓ predictions she computes a top-ℓ accuracy T_ℓ and runs a binomial test to obtain a theoretically certifiable p-value (false positive rate) for the hypothesis that the model was not trained on her dataset. No access to logits, weights, or loss values is required—only the ranked top-ℓ token list at each output position.

## Method

The core mechanism is indirect data poisoning via gradient matching: Alice optimizes n_p=64 poisoned token sequences (each of length 256) so that their aggregate training gradient aligns (maximizes cosine similarity) with the gradient of the secret sequence (x^(s), y^(s)), which is never included in the training corpus. To make the discrete token optimization tractable, each token position is relaxed to a categorical distribution parameterized by Gumbel-Softmax, allowing backpropagation through the embedding layer via convex sums of token embeddings. The optimized distributions are then sampled to produce concrete poisoned text inserted into the dataset at a contamination ratio as low as 0.001–0.005%. Detection exploits the fact that under the null hypothesis (no poisoning), each secret response token appears in the top-ℓ predictions with probability exactly ℓ/V, yielding a binomial distribution and exact certifiable p-values as low as 10⁻⁵⁵.

## Results

On a 1.4B-parameter model, the proposed indirect data poisoning achieves a detection p-value of 1.09×10⁻⁵⁵ using only top-ℓ predictions (no full logits required), with a contamination ratio as low as 0.003% of training tokens. The approach is effective even at 0.001% contamination and transfers across model sizes (135M to 1.4B) and architectures (Full Attention vs. Grouped Query Attention). Poisoned models show no significant degradation on standard benchmarks (ARC, HellaSwag, MMLU, OpenBookQA, PIQA, Winogrande) compared to benign models trained on the same data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Pairwise tokens backdoor (Wang et al., 2024), the proposed method achieves a p-value of 1.09×10⁻⁵⁵ versus 1.55×10⁻³ — roughly 52 orders of magnitude more significant — and crucially, the crafted poisons share no n-grams with the secret sequence, evading n-gram filtering defenses. Compared to the Z-score canary (Wei et al., 2024), the proposed method improves the p-value from 4.04×10⁻¹⁵ to 1.09×10⁻⁵⁵ while requiring only top-ℓ token predictions rather than full model logits or loss values, making it practical against closed-source models. Against MIN-K% PROB (Shi et al., 2023), the proposed method outperforms by nearly 50 orders of magnitude (1.09×10⁻⁵⁵ vs. 6.86×10⁻⁶) and provides theoretically certifiable false detection rates via a binomial test, whereas MIN-K% PROB relies on a t-test with distributional assumptions. Canaries (direct insertion of the secret) remain the most effective at implanting secrets but are trivially detectable and removable via output filtering, whereas indirect data poisoning leaves no trace of the secret in the training corpus.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Data Taggants framework (Bouaziz et al., 2025) from image classification to text, extending indirect data poisoning—where the model learns a secret absent from training data—to LLM pre-training. The core optimization leverages the gradient-matching objective (Geiping et al., 2020) combined with Gumbel-Softmax reparametrization (Jang et al., 2016; Guo et al., 2021) to make discrete token-level poisoning differentiable, a key departure from prior text-poisoning work that relied on hand-crafted heuristics (Panaitescu-Liess et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025). Unlike prior DOV methods (canaries, MIAs, backdoors) that require full logit access or verbatim memorization, this approach only needs top-ℓ predictions and provides certifiable p-values via a binomial test.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that <0.005% poisoned tokens can implant a detectable secret with p-values as low as 10⁻⁵⁵, without any benchmark degradation, offers a practical and theoretically grounded mechanism for dataset provenance in LLMs. Key limitations remain: no stealthiness constraint is enforced (poisons are filterable by quality classifiers), the approach requires knowledge of the target tokenizer and architecture, and it only applies to new datasets before release. Open questions include whether the theoretical guarantees transfer across tokenizers, whether stealthier poisons can be crafted, and whether the approach holds at the scale of trillion-token pre-training corpora used by production LLMs.
