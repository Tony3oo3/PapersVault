## Problem

Long-context reasoning in LLMs suffers from quadratic computational scaling with sequence length, hard truncation at maximum context boundaries, and performance degradation beyond pre-training context windows (typically 4k–8k tokens). Existing approaches attempt to compress reasoning chains or extend context windows but operate within the paradigm of a single continuous generation, leaving the fundamental scaling problem unaddressed.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a text query and, for iterations beyond the first, the previous iteration's summary wrapped in <history> tags. Each iteration produces a bounded-length reasoning segment (capped at η tokens, 4k in main experiments) followed by either a concise progress summary in <summary> tags or a final conclusion. The iterative loop terminates when the model emits a conclusion instead of a summary, with a max_iters=10 safety cap. Training data is structured as (Q, S_{i-1}, RP_i, S_i) tuples for intermediate steps and (Q, S_{n-1}, RP_n, C) for the final step.

## Method

InftyThink decomposes monolithic reasoning into an iterative sawtooth process: the model generates a short reasoning segment, summarizes its progress, and conditions the next segment on that summary, creating a bounded memory footprint that enables unbounded reasoning depth without architectural changes. To train models in this paradigm, the authors develop a three-stage data reconstruction pipeline applied to OpenR1-Math: (1) semantically-aware partitioning of original reasoning chains into η-bounded segments, (2) summary generation via Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct conditioned on the full cumulative reasoning trajectory to preserve global coherence, and (3) assembly of 333K iterative training instances from the original 220K examples. Supervised fine-tuning on this reconstructed data across five base architectures (Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B/7B, Qwen2.5-14B/32B, Llama-3.1-8B) yields consistent 3–11% accuracy gains on MATH500, AIME24, and GPQA_diamond while reducing inference wall-time by 20–40%, with the largest relative improvements in smaller models.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, InftyThink achieves 91.29% on MATH500, 43.96% on AIME24, and 52.97% on GPQA_diamond, improving over vanilla long-context reasoning by +1.78%, +11.04%, and +9.03% respectively, while reducing average inference latency from 1.69s to 1.43s. The gains are most pronounced on smaller models: Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B improves by +4.33% (MATH500), +10.00% (AIME24), and +9.41% (GPQA_diamond). Across all five base architectures tested, InftyThink consistently outperforms vanilla reasoning on AIME24 and GPQA_diamond, with average accuracy gains of 1.2–5.9 percentage points depending on model scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is vanilla single-round long-context reasoning (same base model, same OpenR1-Math training data, but without iterative summarization). InftyThink wins on AIME24 across all five base models, with the largest margin on Llama-3.1-8B (+13.34%) and Qwen2.5-Math-7B (+11.04%). On GPQA_diamond, InftyThink also wins universally, with gains ranging from +2.02% (Qwen2.5-14B) to +9.41% (Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B). On MATH500, the advantage is smaller and slightly negative for the two largest models (Qwen2.5-14B: −0.79%, Qwen2.5-32B: −0.49%), suggesting diminishing returns on already-saturated benchmarks. The paper also compares against RoPE linear interpolation (scale factor 8, extending context from 4k to 32k) on Qwen2.5-Math-7B: RoPE yields +1.40% on MATH500 but −2.29% on AIME24, while InftyThink achieves +1.78% and +11.04% respectively, demonstrating that restructuring the reasoning process is more effective than stretching positional encodings. A training-free prompt-based iterative pipeline (using the same model as both reasoner and summarizer) underperforms the trained InftyThink by 1.5–8.8% across benchmarks, confirming that supervised fine-tuning on reconstructed data is necessary for the paradigm to work effectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant long-context reasoning paradigm (exemplified by OpenAI o1 and DeepSeek-R1) that generates a single continuous reasoning chain, and from CoT compression methods (CoT-Valve, TokenSkip, LightThinker) that attempt to shorten that chain. Conceptually, it repurposes the iterative generation idea from RecurrentGPT (long-form text) and applies it to structured reasoning with a dedicated summarization mechanism, creating a 'sawtooth' memory pattern. It also contrasts with context-window extension techniques (RoPE interpolation), arguing that restructuring the reasoning process is more effective than stretching architectural limits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InftyThink challenges the assumed trade-off between reasoning depth and computational efficiency by showing that periodic summarization can simultaneously improve accuracy and reduce inference latency without any architectural modification. This has practical implications for deploying reasoning in resource-constrained settings, particularly for smaller models that benefit most (up to 11% on AIME24). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond math/science to open-ended reasoning, how summarization quality degrades over many iterations, and whether reinforcement learning can further optimize what and when to summarize.
