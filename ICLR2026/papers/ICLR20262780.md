## Problem

Standard Bayesian Optimization suffers from slow cold-start and poor high-dimensional scalability in expensive scientific experiments. Existing LLM-augmented BO methods (e.g., LLAMBO, LLINBO) inject LLM guidance only at initialization or as auxiliary candidate proposals that are later overridden by the acquisition function, leaving the LLM's semantic reasoning loosely and transiently connected to the optimization loop. The paper asks whether LLM preferences can be continuously and stably embedded into every BO iteration without sacrificing the statistical rigor of the surrogate.

## Input / Output

At each iteration the method consumes a structured LLM output constrained to either a point [x1,…,xd] or a hyper-rectangular region [lb, ub] paired with a confidence score c∈[0,1], alongside the running set of observed design–response pairs and the declared search-space bounds. The LLM's confidence is mapped to a guidance strength λ = c / √(aᵀΣ_GG a) that normalizes the mean shift to a c-standard-deviation scale relative to posterior uncertainty. The output is the next query point selected by log-qEI over the mean-shifted GP surrogate (Matérn-5/2 kernel), with the covariance left unchanged so that standard BO uncertainty quantification is preserved.

## Method

LGBO introduces a region-lifted preference mechanism: the LLM specifies a preferred region R, which is discretized into a Sobol grid G and encoded as a linear exponential functional ρ(f) = exp(λ aᵀ F_G). Proposition 1 proves this exponential lift is exactly equivalent to shifting the GP mean by λ Σ_{g} a_g k(x, x_g) while leaving the covariance kernel unchanged, so the surrogate remains a valid GP and the acquisition function operates on a standard posterior. At every BO round the LLM is queried with a modular prompt (fixed system prompt enforcing an evidence hierarchy and strict output format; dynamic user prompt carrying experimental history and the previous round's reasoning trace), and the resulting point/region + confidence is converted into a mean shift applied to the surrogate before the acquisition function selects the next experiment. Theoretically, under a fixed-lift abstraction, the paper derives GP-UCB regret bounds showing that misaligned guidance inflates the RKHS radius only by a constant (B_out = B_0 + λ‖g‖), while aligned guidance contracts it to B_in = B_0√(1−c²), yielding strictly tighter regret. Empirically, LGBO is evaluated on four dry benchmarks (LNP3, Cross-barrel, Concrete, HPLC), a 14-dimensional COF dataset, and a new wet-lab Fe-Cr electrolyte optimization, consistently outperforming GPBO, LLAMBO, ColaLLM, and BOPRO in convergence speed and final objective value.

## Results

In the wet-lab Fe-Cr redox flow battery electrolyte optimization, LGBO attains 90% of the best observed value within 6 iterations, whereas GPBO and LLAMBO require more than 10 iterations. Across four dry benchmarks (LNP3, Cross-barrel, Concrete, HPLC) and a 14-dimensional COF dataset, LGBO consistently achieves the highest final objective values and lowest run-to-run variance compared to all baselines. On the noisy HPLC task, LGBO maintains the highest final peak area despite large fluctuations. On the high-dimensional COF task (14 features), LGBO retains a clear performance advantage, demonstrating scalability beyond low-dimensional settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LGBO outperforms GPBO (standard GP-UCB with Matérn-5/2 kernel) on all six datasets, with the most dramatic gains in the cold-start phase where GPBO scatters samples broadly and exhibits high variance. Against LLAMBO (Liu et al., ICLR 2024), which uses LLMs only for warm-start initialization and candidate generation, LGBO achieves faster convergence and higher final objectives on every benchmark, confirming that continuous region-lifted preference integration surpasses one-off LLM suggestions. In extended experiments, LGBO also beats ColaLLM (Hvarfner et al., ICLR 2024), which reweights sampled paths based on a single initial preference and cannot incorporate updated guidance, and BOPRO (Agarwal et al., ICLR 2025), which uses LLMs as implicit Bayesian priors through in-context learning. The ablation with random region lifting (matched size and confidence) shows that LGBO's gains stem specifically from informative LLM semantic cues rather than the lifting mechanism alone, and that the advantage is not attributable to shared LLM warm-start initialization since LLAMBO uses the same initial points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GP-UCB Bayesian optimization framework (Srinivas et al., 2009) by introducing a 'region-lifted preference' mechanism that continuously shifts the GP surrogate mean at every iteration based on LLM-suggested regions, rather than restricting LLM involvement to one-shot warm-start initialization (LLAMBO, Liu et al.) or candidate proposals filtered by the acquisition function (LLINBO, ADO-LLM, ReasoningBO). The key mathematical insight—that an exponential functional lift over a GP posterior is exactly equivalent to a mean shift preserving covariance (via Cameron-Martin theory)—provides a tractable bridge between the preference-based BO literature (Brochu et al., ColaBO) and LLM-guided optimization, avoiding the intractability of argmax-dependent preference functionals. The paper departs from ColaBO's one-shot expert preference by enabling iterative, evolving LLM guidance while maintaining stability through the fixed-covariance design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LGBO offers a principled, theoretically grounded recipe for integrating LLM domain knowledge into scientific optimization loops, with provable worst-case safety (bounded regret degradation) and best-case acceleration (tighter RKHS radius under alignment). The wet-lab Fe-Cr electrolyte experiment demonstrates practical value in a setting where no benchmark exists and data is scarce, which is the regime where BO is most needed. Open questions include: the theoretical analysis relies on a 'frozen-lift' simplification (single fixed direction) rather than the fully adaptive per-iteration sequence, leaving the multi-round dynamics formally uncharacterized; scalability to very high-dimensional spaces (d>14) remains untested; and the framework's performance when LLMs lack relevant domain knowledge (truly novel scientific questions) is unclear, as all experiments draw on well-studied chemical/physical systems.
