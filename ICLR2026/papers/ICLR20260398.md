## Problem

Discrete autoregressive language models are trained with one-hot cross-entropy targets that treat all non-ground-truth tokens as equally wrong, discarding the metric structure inherent in many output spaces (integers, spatial coordinates, rotation angles, quantized embeddings). This ignores the fact that predicting 49 instead of 50 is meaningfully closer than predicting 12, a distinction that matters for data efficiency and downstream accuracy in tasks like visual grounding, robotic control, reward scoring, and image generation.

## Input / Output

The method is task-agnostic in its loss formulation but applied to outputs that decompose into discrete tokens with a well-defined metric: bounding box coordinates as sequences of digit tokens (images resized to 1024×1024, coordinates rounded to integers), robotic joint actions as position coordinates and clockwise rotation angles in [−359, 359], reward scores on a 0–20 integer scale, and VQ codebook indices from a 16,384-token codebook (images at 384×384, evaluated at 256×256). The loss is computed per-token-position independently, comparing each candidate token in the metric-relevant vocabulary subset V_d against the corresponding ground-truth component, so no multi-token joint evaluation is required.

## Method

DIST² Loss replaces the one-hot target with a soft distribution p_d(v|x,t) = exp(−d(v,x,t)/τ) / Σ_{v'} exp(−d(v',x,t)/τ), where d is a task-defined metric (squared Euclidean for digits, MSE in embedding space for VQ codes) and τ controls sharpness (τ=1 for digit tokens; τ≈9.7 for the 16,384-token VQ codebook via entropy matching). The model is trained to match this target via KL divergence, combined with standard cross-entropy as L = L_CE + α·L_dist (α=0.1 fixed). This construction is the closed-form optimal policy of entropy-regularized policy optimization with known per-token rewards, giving the stability of supervised learning without sampling or rollouts. For multi-digit numbers, two workarounds address the credit-assignment problem: place value weighting (higher-order digits receive larger loss multipliers) and contrastive target augmentation (a nearby negative sample is concatenated into the target distribution). The approach requires no additional data, no teacher model, and no architectural changes—only a metric defined over the token subset.

## Results

On RefCOCO visual grounding, Phi3V fine-tuned with DIST² Loss (0 pretraining samples) achieves 94.8% val accuracy, matching or exceeding Florence-2-L (93.4%) which uses 126M pretraining samples. On VIMABench robotic manipulation, LLaRA-dist reaches 53.9% accuracy at 1K training samples (vs. 49.6% for sft), with the gap narrowing but persisting at 100K samples (89.5% vs. 88.5%). On RewardBench, Llama-dist achieves 85.3% average accuracy (vs. 75.3% sft), surpassing GPT-3.5 (65.3%) and Claude-3-haiku (70.6%). On ImageNet image generation, LlamaGen-dist improves FID from 6.44 to 6.27 (111M) and from 3.08 to 3.04 (343M) over the sft baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In visual grounding (Table 1), Phi3V-dist (0 pretraining) outperforms UNINEXT (92.6→94.8 val), Ferret (89.5→94.8), and Florence-2-B (92.6→94.8) on RefCOCO val, and matches Florence-2-L (93.4) while trailing it on test-A (94.5 vs. 95.3) and test-B (87.3 vs. 92.0). In robotic manipulation (Table 2), LLaRA-dist dominates RT-2 by a wide margin at every data scale (e.g., 53.9% vs. 1.9% at 1K on L1), and consistently exceeds LLaRA-sft (49.6→53.9 at 1K; 88.5→89.5 at 100K). In reward modeling (Table 3), Llama-dist (85.3% avg) surpasses all generative and classifier baselines: UltraRM-13B (68.5%), Tulu-v2.5-RM-13B (81.9% at 2M), GPT-3.5 (65.3%), Claude-3-haiku (70.6%), and Prometheus-2-7B (72.0%). In image generation (Table 4), LlamaGen-dist outperforms GigaGAN (FID 3.45), LDM-4 (3.60), and both VQGAN variants (18.65/15.78), and edges out LlamaGen-sft (3.08→3.04 at 343M). The vocab baseline (constrained cross-entropy without distance structure) shows inconsistent or negligible gains, confirming that the distance-aware target distribution—not merely restricting the vocabulary—is the source of improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIST² Loss directly instantiates the closed-form optimal policy from entropy-regularized policy optimization (Haarnoja et al., 2017) as a training target for autoregressive models, replacing the need for sampling and policy-gradient estimation. It generalizes the discrete Gaussian distribution from differential privacy (Canonne et al., 2020) into a flexible, metric-agnostic loss applicable to any token space with a defined distance. Unlike prior target-based methods such as class-similarity label smoothing or instance-based label smoothing, DIST² Loss operates over the full vocabulary of multimodal LLMs and derives its soft targets deterministically from the task's intrinsic metric (Euclidean, angular, embedding distance) without requiring a teacher model or additional annotation. The paper also distinguishes itself from knowledge distillation by noting that its targets are constructed from ground-truth labels and a predefined metric rather than from a learned teacher distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DIST² Loss offers a simple, architecture-agnostic, plug-and-play loss that consistently improves data efficiency across five diverse domains (visual grounding, robotic manipulation, reward modeling, image generation, regression) with negligible computational overhead, making it a practical drop-in replacement for one-hot cross-entropy whenever tokens admit a meaningful metric. Key limitations include the credit assignment problem for multi-token structured outputs (e.g., multi-digit integers), which the authors acknowledge but do not fully resolve, and the theoretical expectation that advantages diminish under unlimited data. Open directions include extending to non-metric structures (relational, hierarchical outputs), multi-task fine-tuning, and domains like time-series forecasting where temporal proximity is meaningful but not yet exploited by this framework.
