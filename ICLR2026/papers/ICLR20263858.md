## Problem

Vision Transformers process all tokens in self-attention, incurring quadratic computational cost and introducing irrelevant background information that degrades detection performance. Existing sparse attention methods either pad selected tokens to the maximum batch length (re-introducing background tokens), reduce computation only at inference, or apply uniform token pruning that ignores input-specific informativeness. No prior approach combines context-aware dynamic selection with efficient batch packing that works for both training and inference on complex tasks like object detection.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images at 1280×800 resolution along with object annotations (bounding boxes or instance masks) used to construct multi-scale binary selection labels for training supervision. It outputs four-scale feature representations (at H/4, H/8, H/16, H/32 spatial resolutions) that feed a Mask RCNN detection head, producing bounding boxes with class labels, instance segmentation masks, or multi-label class predictions depending on the downstream task. During inference, the gating layer dynamically determines how many tokens to retain per image, and the packing mechanism reorganizes variable-length token sets into fixed-size containers of length 49 for parallel GPU computation.

## Method

SPT is a four-stage hierarchical backbone where stages 1–2 use standard Swin blocks and stages 3–4 incorporate Select and Pack Attention (SPA) blocks. Each SPA block applies a lightweight linear gating layer to produce per-token importance scores, which are supervised via binary cross-entropy against multi-scale selection labels (aggregated bounding boxes or masks upsampled to each feature resolution); scores from coarser scales are max-pooled and combined with current-scale scores to retain more informative tokens. A Gumbel-Softmax straight-through estimator with a low threshold (0.01) discretely separates positive tokens, which are then packed into fixed-length containers (L=49) to form new batches, avoiding the padding overhead of uniform-length approaches. Attention masks within each container ensure tokens attend only to tokens from the same original image, while Swin's window-shifting operation across two consecutive blocks recovers cross-package information. SPA is deliberately deferred to the third stage onward because early-stage features lack sufficient discriminative power for reliable token selection, and four additional blocks are added at stage 3 to compensate for the reduced token count.

## Results

On COCO2017 object detection, SPT achieves AP of 47.1 (Tiny), 49.3 (Small), and 53.2 (Base), outperforming the dense Swin baselines (46.0, 48.5, 51.9) and all sparse attention baselines by 0.5–2.7 AP. Backbone FLOPs are reduced by 10.9–11.4% relative to Swin, and overall FLOPs drop by 3.9–5.8%. On the more challenging BDD-S early-detection subset (objects <25% of image area), SPT-T gains +0.9 AP over Swin-T while cutting backbone FLOPs by 20.8%. SPT also improves instance segmentation (40.9 AP vs. 39.6 for DynamicSwin-S on COCO2017) and multi-label classification (44.60 mAP vs. 44.12 for DynamicSwin-T on PASCAL VOC 2012).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPT consistently outperforms all four sparse-attention baselines across every configuration and dataset. Against DynamicSwin (the strongest sparse baseline), SPT-T gains +2.8 AP on COCO2017 (47.1 vs. 44.3), SPT-S gains +2.1 AP (49.3 vs. 47.2), and SPT-B gains +2.7 AP (53.2 vs. 50.5), while simultaneously reducing backbone FLOPs by 10.9–11.4%. Against DAT, SPT-T improves AP by +2.7 (47.1 vs. 44.4) and SPT-S by +2.2 (49.3 vs. 47.1). SparseViT, evaluated only at the Tiny scale, trails SPT-T by 4.7 AP (42.4 vs. 47.1). SPT also surpasses the dense Swin reference at every scale (e.g., SPT-B 53.2 vs. Swin-B 51.9 AP), demonstrating that supervised token selection not only saves computation but also improves accuracy by filtering out uninformative background tokens. On the harder BDD-S early-detection subset, the advantage widens: SPT-T achieves 5.6 AP vs. 4.7 for DynamicSwin-T (+0.9) while cutting backbone FLOPs by 20.8%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Swin Transformer's hierarchical window-based attention architecture and the token-packing mechanism from Navit (Dehghani et al., 2024), combining them into a unified sparse attention block. It departs from prior sparse attention methods (DynamicViT, SparseViT, DAT, DynamicSwin) in two key ways: (1) it introduces supervised token selection via multi-scale labels derived from object annotations, rather than relying on unsupervised heuristics like L2-norm scoring or fixed pruning ratios, and (2) it packs variable-length selected tokens into fixed-size containers to restore GPU batch parallelism during both training and inference, avoiding the padding overhead that plagues SparseViT and the inference-only sparsity of DynamicViT/EViT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that supervised token selection can simultaneously improve detection accuracy (0.5–2.7 AP over the best sparse baseline) and reduce backbone computation (10.9–24.9% FLOPs), a combination that prior efficiency-focused methods typically could not achieve. However, the approach fundamentally requires spatial supervision (bounding boxes or segmentation masks) to train the gating layer, which limits its applicability to tasks lacking structured spatial labels or to dense-label settings where most tokens are informative. An open question the authors acknowledge is whether self-supervised or task-adaptive selection strategies can replace the need for explicit spatial labels, which would broaden the method's utility beyond detection and segmentation.
