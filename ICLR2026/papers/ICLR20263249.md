## Problem

Concept Bottleneck Models require a many-to-many mapping between local visual patches and high-level semantic concepts, but prior methods either rely on a single global feature (insufficient for fine-grained alignment) or attempt patch-level alignment and suffer from representational collapse, where patch features degenerate into a low-rank subspace during training. This collapse destroys the feature diversity needed to encode distinct concepts, simultaneously degrading interpretability and downstream accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263249/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (medical scans at various resolutions or natural images at 224×224) and produces a K-dimensional concept activation vector (CAV) as an intermediate interpretable output, followed by a final class label. The CAV is computed as dot products between K learned visual concept prototypes and K corresponding textual concept embeddings, making each dimension directly attributable to a human-defined concept. The model is trained end-to-end with a composite loss combining classification cross-entropy, concept binary cross-entropy, and the IVQ regularization term.

## Method

IVQ-CBM introduces two key components built on a pre-trained CLIP ViT encoder. First, Implicit Vector Quantization (IVQ) repurposes the standard VQ codebook and commitment losses as pure regularization terms while discarding the quantized features from the forward pass; this anchors each patch embedding to its nearest learnable codebook prototype (sized M=K to match the number of concepts) without creating a hard information bottleneck, thereby maintaining high-rank, diverse representations throughout training. Second, Magnet Attention aggregates the L regularized patch features into K visual concept prototypes via learnable concept queries and a softmax soft-assignment matrix computed over negative squared Euclidean distances, explicitly modeling the many-to-many patch-to-concept correspondence. The resulting K visual prototypes are aligned with textual concept embeddings via dot-product scoring to produce the CAV, which feeds a lightweight classification head. The total training objective is a weighted sum of classification loss, concept BCE loss, and the IVQ loss, all backpropagated jointly.

## Results

IVQ-CBM achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across all 13 benchmarks, outperforming the strongest explainable baseline (MVP-CBM) by +1.39% to +3.85% on medical datasets (e.g., 99.90% on NCT, 93.59% on BUSI, 88.33% on Retina) and by +1.13% to +1.97% on general benchmarks (e.g., 73.42% on ImageNet, 75.91% on CUB-200). On the balanced macro-average (BMAC) metric, gains are even larger on imbalanced datasets, reaching +9.45% on IDRID and +3.93% on BUSI. The method also surpasses black-box ViT-Base on most medical datasets (e.g., 90.11% vs. 90.01% on ISIC, 99.90% vs. 93.25% on NCT) while providing concept-level interpretability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IVQ-CBM outperforms all eight explainable CBM baselines across every dataset. The closest competitors are MVP-CBM and Explicd: on NCT, IVQ-CBM reaches 99.90% vs. 97.90% (MVP-CBM) and 95.29% (Explicd); on BUSI, 93.59% vs. 89.74% and 87.17%; on ImageNet, 73.42% vs. 72.29% and 71.93%. Against black-box models, IVQ-CBM matches or exceeds ViT-Base on most medical datasets (e.g., 99.90% vs. 93.25% on NCT, 93.59% vs. 80.25% on BUSI) while providing concept-level interpretability, and slightly trails ViT-Base on ISIC (90.11% vs. 90.01%) and SIIM (88.03% vs. 83.56%—actually exceeds it). Zero-shot multimodal models (CLIP, MedCLIP, BiomedCLIP) are far below, with BiomedCLIP reaching at most 59.83% on ISIC versus IVQ-CBM's 90.11%. Among regularization baselines (Barlow Twins, Spectral Regularization, Gram Loss), IVQ-CBM achieves the highest BMAC on SIIM (81.91% vs. 80.84% and 79.17%) and BUSI (95.38% vs. 91.98% and 88.81%), though Spectral Regularization marginally edges IVQ-CBM on ISIC ACC (90.21% vs. 90.11%), confirming that higher rank alone does not guarantee better CBM performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Concept Bottleneck Model (CBM) framework by replacing the single global feature (e.g., CLIP's [CLS] token) with patch-level representations, and repurposes the codebook and commitment losses from Neural Discrete Representation Learning (VQ-VAE) as a pure regularization signal without performing hard quantization in the forward pass. It departs from recent patch-level CBMs (ExplicD, MVP-CBM, DOT-CBM) that attempt fine-grained alignment but suffer from unaddressed representation collapse, and from general-purpose regularization methods (Barlow Twins, spectral norm, Gram loss) that maximize indiscriminate diversity rather than concept-structured diversity. The Magnet Attention mechanism for aggregating patches into concept prototypes is a new architectural component not present in prior CBM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of representation collapse as a fundamental, previously under-acknowledged blocker for patch-level CBMs provides a clear diagnostic for the community, and the IVQ regularizer offers a lightweight, drop-in solution that preserves feature rank without introducing an information bottleneck. The finding that higher feature rank does not automatically yield better CBM performance (Barlow Twins maintains higher rank but lower accuracy) challenges the assumption that any diversity-preserving regularizer will suffice, pointing toward the need for semantically structured regularization. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D volumetric medical imaging, open-vocabulary or dynamically generated concept sets, and whether the codebook's learned prototypes can serve as a reusable visual dictionary across tasks.
