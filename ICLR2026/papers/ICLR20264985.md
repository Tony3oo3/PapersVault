## Problem

Latent-space monitoring techniques (activation probes, OOD detectors) are proposed as root-cause defenses against LLM jailbreaking, but their robustness under adversarial attack was untested. This paper asks whether an attacker can induce 'obfuscated activations' — latent states that produce harmful behavior while evading detection — and whether any monitor architecture is robust against such attacks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264985/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attacker optimizes a suffix appended to a text prompt (harmful request or SQL query), either as discrete hard-prompt tokens (~256 tokens, gradient-free) or as a continuous 16-token embedding vector (white-box, Adam optimizer). The model's residual stream activations at every layer are scored by latent-space monitors (mean-difference, logistic regression, MLP probes, Mahalanobis OOD detectors, SAE-based probes), and evaluation reports monitor recall at 1% FPR alongside behavioral success (StrongREJECT jailbreak rate or SQLEval correctness). A key unusual aspect is the dual-objective evaluation: both whether the target behavior was achieved AND whether activations were successfully obfuscated must be reported together.

## Method

The core technique is the obfuscation attack, which jointly minimizes a behavioral loss (cross-entropy to elicit a target output) and an obfuscation loss (the monitor's own score on the induced activations), weighted by λ_obf. Two threat models are studied: a realistic gray-box hard-prompt attack using a modified FLRT discrete optimizer, and a worst-case white-box embedding attack using Adam on a 16-token suffix. The paper further runs an alternating adversarial training game (70+ epochs of attack/defense) to test whether monitors can be made robust, and introduces the 'obfuscation tax' by measuring SQL correctness degradation as the number of obfuscated layers increases. The key empirical finding is that obfuscated activations are widespread in latent space — they cluster near benign activations and cannot be fully covered by iterative retraining — while the obfuscation tax on complex tasks (SQL) provides a partial mitigation for defenders.

## Results

Obfuscation embedding attacks reduce harmfulness probe recall from ~100% to 0% while maintaining a 90% jailbreaking success rate on Llama-3-8B-Instruct. Under the more realistic hard-prompt threat model, the best probe (MLP generation-based) still suffers a ~10% recall reduction, and even after 70+ epochs of adversarial training, attackers reliably find new obfuscated activations that bypass the monitor. On the SQL task, the paper identifies an 'obfuscation tax': the Pearson correlation between the number of layers obfuscated and SQLEval performance is significantly negative for all probes, indicating that evading monitors degrades model capability on complex tasks. Standard jailbreak methods (GCG, PAIR, AutoDAN, MSJ) do NOT induce obfuscated activations, achieving 90–100% recall at 1% FPR across most probe types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's obfuscation attacks are compared against five standard jailbreak methods (GCG, PAIR, AutoDAN, MSJ) and a probe-based image-classification defense (Fort & Lakshminarayanan). All four standard jailbreak methods are reliably detected by generation-based LogReg and MLP probes, achieving 95–100% recall at 1% FPR (Table 1), confirming that existing latent-space monitors work against conventional attacks. The obfuscation attacks go beyond these by explicitly optimizing a joint behavioral + obfuscation loss, reducing recall to 0% under embedding attacks and ~10% under hard-prompt attacks. Against Fort & Lakshminarayanan's multi-layer probe ensemble for ResNet-152 on CIFAR-10, the paper reproduces their finding that early-layer probes resist output-only adversarial attacks, but shows that adding probe outputs to the adversarial loss drives probe accuracy to near 0% across most layers. The paper also shows that obfuscation attacks transfer across monitor types (e.g., training against a Gaussian OOD detector bypasses input-based probes), meaning attackers need not know the specific defense deployed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial attack literature on LLMs (GCG-style suffix optimization, FLRT discrete optimization) by adding an explicit obfuscation loss that targets latent-space monitors, creating a joint behavioral + monitor-evasion objective. It builds on the latent-space monitoring line of work (linear/MLP probes, Mahalanobis OOD detection, SAE-based classifiers) by systematically testing these defenses against the new obfuscation attacks. Conceptually, it departs from prior adversarial training work by showing that the attacker-defender game does not converge: even after 70+ epochs of alternating optimization, the attacker can find new obfuscated activations, suggesting the latent space contains a rich, non-finite set of inconspicuous-but-harmful regions rather than a single learnable 'harmfulness direction.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that no white-box latent-space monitor is robust against worst-case (embedding-level) attacks, but the obfuscation tax — the finding that evading monitors degrades performance on complex tasks like SQL generation — provides a meaningful, if partial, defense guarantee for high-capability behaviors. The paper gives concrete deployment guidance (prefer MLP generation probes over linear input probes; ensemble supervised probes with OOD detectors) and identifies a promising but unresolved direction: training models to be inherently easier to monitor (OAT), which currently fails to produce coherent outputs. Open questions include whether the obfuscation tax scales with model size and task complexity, whether longer adversarial training could eventually close the gap, and whether the widespread nature of obfuscated activations reflects a fundamental property of neural representations or an artifact of the specific optimization landscape.
