## Problem

Robotic manipulation policies trained in simulation rely on clean, accurate depth, but real-world depth cameras produce noisy, incomplete readings (holes, value biases, material-dependent failures) that prevent seamless sim-to-real transfer. No existing method can convert raw consumer depth camera output into simulation-level metric depth in real time, leaving a fundamental geometry gap that limits the use of 3D information in deployed robot policies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260384/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The CDM takes a paired RGB image (3×H×W) and the raw, unprocessed depth image (H×W) from a specific depth camera as input, and outputs a denoised absolute metric depth map (H×W). Unlike prior prompt-based methods, the raw depth is fed directly without hole-filling or other preprocessing. The model is camera-specific (e.g., CDM-D435, CDM-L515), meaning each variant is trained to correct the distinct noise signature of one sensor, and operates at over 6 Hz on a single RTX 4090.

## Method

The CDM uses a dual-branch ViT architecture (RGB and depth branches, both initialized from DINOv2) where corresponding spatial tokens are fused via multi-head self-attention across multiple feature levels, then concatenated with the original RGB tokens and decoded by a DPT head to produce metric depth. The key training innovation is a neural data engine: two noise models (a hole-noise binary classifier and a value-noise stylized relative depth predictor based on Depth Anything V2) are learned from the collected ByteCameraDepth dataset (170K+ RGB-depth pairs from 7 cameras), then used to synthesize realistic camera-specific noise on 280K+ simulated images from HyperSim, DREDS, HISS, and IRS. A guided filter with randomized kernel size corrects metric-scale discrepancies in the synthesized value noise, and handcrafted high-frequency noise augments patterns the neural models cannot capture. This pipeline enables supervised training with L1 + gradient loss on disparity targets, yielding simulation-level depth accuracy that allows depth-only policies trained purely in simulation to transfer zero-shot to real robots.

## Results

On the Hammer dataset (zero-shot), CDM-D435 achieves L1=0.0258, RMSE=0.0404, and δ₁=0.9951 on the D435 split without any hole-filling preprocessing, outperforming PromptDA (L1=0.1703) and PriorDA (L1=1.2031) under the same no-preprocessing setting by 6.6× and 46.6× in L1 respectively. On the L515 split, CDM-L515 reaches L1=0.0156 and δ₁=0.9919, the best among all methods. In zero-shot sim-to-real manipulation (Table 2), CDM-D435 achieves 26/30 total success on the Kitchen task and CDM-L515 achieves 22/30 on the Canteen task, compared to 0/30 for raw depth and 0–7/30 for PromptDA/PriorDA baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PromptDA (Lin et al., CVPR 2025), CDMs achieve substantially lower L1 without any hole-filling: 0.0258 vs. 0.1703 on D435 (6.6× improvement) and 0.0156 vs. 0.0483 on L515 (3.1× improvement). Even when PromptDA is given hole-filling preprocessing, CDMs still lead (L1 0.0258 vs. 0.0396 on D435; 0.0156 vs. 0.0207 on L515), and CDM-L515 generalizes cross-camera to the D435 split (L1=0.0182) better than the camera-matched CDM-D435. Against PriorDA (Wang et al., 2025), the gap is even larger: PriorDA without hole-filling collapses (L1=1.2031 on D435, δ₁=0.1717), and even with hole-filling it trails CDMs on all metrics. Against D3RoMa (Wei et al., ECCV 2024 Workshop), CDMs win on every Hammer split (e.g., L1 0.0258 vs. 0.0338 on D435; 0.0156 vs. 0.0282 on L515; 0.0248 vs. 0.0400 on Helios). In the sim-to-real manipulation setting, CDM-D435 achieves 26/30 Kitchen success vs. 0/30 for PromptDA and 7/30 for PriorDA, while CDM-L515 reaches 22/30 Canteen success vs. 0/30 for both baselines, confirming that the depth accuracy advantage translates directly into manipulation performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Depth Anything V2 (DAV2) foundation model as both the initialization for the CDM's dual-branch ViT architecture and the backbone for the value noise model, and on the guided filter for correcting metric scale in synthesized noise. It departs from prior prompt-based metric depth methods (PromptDA, PriorDA) by designing a token-level fusion architecture that directly ingests raw, holed depth without hole-filling preprocessing, and by introducing a neural data engine that learns camera-specific noise patterns from a small real dataset (ByteCameraDepth) to synthesize large-scale training data in simulation. The sim-to-real manipulation pipeline extends MimicGen with whole-body control (WBC) for smoother demonstration generation, but the core conceptual contribution is treating depth-camera noise as a learnable, synthesizable pattern rather than an inference-time problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates, for the first time, that a depth-only policy trained on raw simulated depth—without noise augmentation or real-world fine-tuning—transfers to a physical robot with minimal degradation (26/30 vs. 30/50 in simulation), validating that accurate geometry is the critical missing link for sim-to-real manipulation. The CDM-as-plugin paradigm is practically attractive: it requires no changes to the policy, runs at ~6 Hz on a single GPU, and generalizes across camera types. Limitations include dependence on RGB semantic information to correct large depth errors (failure on metal surfaces with uninformative RGB), evaluation on only two cameras and two tabletop tasks, and the depth-only setting that excludes color. Open questions include whether CDMs can relabel large-scale RGB-D data for 3D foundation models and whether the approach extends to mobile or dexterous manipulation.
