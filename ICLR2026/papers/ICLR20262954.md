## Problem

LLM unlearning effects are fragile: post-unlearning weight perturbations such as 4-bit quantization or 100-step relearning fine-tuning can quickly resurface forgotten content. Prior robustness efforts have focused on reformulating unlearning objectives (min-max, meta-learning, IRM), but the role of the base optimizer itself—independent of any objective-level modification—in shaping unlearning robustness remains unexplored. This paper asks how the 'grade' of the optimizer (zeroth-order vs. first-order vs. second-order) affects the resilience of unlearned models against weight tampering.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a fine-tuned LLM (e.g., LLaMA2-7B, ICLM-7B, or Zephyr-7B-beta) together with a forget dataset D_f and a retain dataset D_r, solving a regularized optimization that balances a forget loss against a retain loss. The output is the unlearned model's weights, evaluated not only on standard unlearning metrics (VerbMem and KnowMem on D_f; KnowMem on D_r) but also under two post-unlearning stress tests: 4-bit GPTQ weight quantization and 100-step relearning fine-tuning on D_f or D_r, making robustness under perturbation a first-class output criterion rather than a secondary check.

## Method

The paper introduces the concept of optimizer 'grade'—the level of descent information exploited (zeroth-order < first-order < second-order)—and shows that downgrading the optimizer paradoxically upgrades unlearning robustness. Zeroth-order (ZO) optimizers, which estimate gradients via finite differences of function values, are theoretically equivalent to solving a randomized-smoothing objective, giving them inherent tolerance to weight perturbations; linear mode connectivity analysis confirms ZO converges to a distinct loss basin from first-order Adam. Building on this, the authors propose a Hybrid FO-ZO optimizer that alternates between Adam (first-order, 'follower') and AdaZO (zeroth-order, 'leader') in blocks of N steps, with the schedule ending on a ZO round so the final model inherits ZO's robustness while retaining FO's unlearning precision. This leader-follower design is validated across MUSE, WMDP, and TOFU benchmarks with multiple unlearning algorithms (GradDiff, NPO, RMU), consistently outperforming Adam, signAdam, SAM, and standalone ZO in both unlearning effectiveness and post-perturbation robustness without incurring additional runtime cost.

## Results

On MUSE-Books with NPO, the proposed Hybrid FO-ZO optimizer achieves VerbMem=0 and KnowMem=0 on D_f both before and after 4-bit quantization and after 100 relearning steps, while retaining 54.8% utility (KnowMem on D_r) pre-perturbation and 38.5% post-perturbation. In contrast, standard Adam (NPO) degrades to VerbMem=48.5 and KnowMem=34.8 after quantization, and SAM (with explicit robustness design) still reaches VerbMem=30.0 and KnowMem=22.5. On WMDP-Bio, Hybrid outperforms SAM in relearning robustness across all epochs while maintaining comparable unlearning effectiveness. On TOFU (forget10), Hybrid achieves Prob.=0.0 and Rouge=1.8 with MU=61.5, matching Adam's unlearning quality while retaining ZO-level relearning resilience.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hybrid dominates all baselines on post-perturbation robustness: after 4-bit quantization, Hybrid achieves VerbMem=0 and KnowMem=0 for NPO, versus Adam (48.5/34.8), SAM (30.0/22.5), signAdam (30.7/25.8), and RS (34.5/23.4). Against relearning (Relearn100), Hybrid again records 0/0, outperforming Adam (34.8), SAM (22.5), and even ZO (16.4), which is the strongest pure-robustness baseline. On pre-perturbation unlearning effectiveness, Hybrid matches Adam (both achieve VerbMem=0, KnowMem=0 for NPO) and surpasses ZO, which retains 20.7/23.9 due to its high gradient estimation variance. SAM, despite its explicit min-max robustness design in the unlearning objective, is consistently outperformed by Hybrid on both quantization and relearning robustness while also suffering lower retain scores (35.2 vs. 54.8). ZO (AdaZO) shows the strongest standalone robustness among downgraded optimizers but at the cost of substantially weaker unlearning (VerbMem=20.7, KnowMem=23.9 pre-perturbation) and the lowest retain (36.6→21.1), a trade-off that Hybrid resolves by using FO for precision and ZO for robustness. Gradient-compressed variants (signSGD, signAdam, 8-bit Adam) provide moderate robustness gains over Adam but fall well short of Hybrid, and Sophia (second-order) exhibits the weakest quantization robustness of all optimizers tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of robust LLM unlearning research, which modifies the unlearning objective itself (min-max/SAM by Fan et al. 2025, IRM by Wang et al. 2025a, meta-learning by Tamirisa et al. 2024) to resist weight perturbations. Instead, it holds the unlearning objective fixed and investigates the optimizer as the sole lever for robustness, drawing on gradient-compression methods (signSGD, signAdam, 8-bit Adam), zeroth-order optimization (AdaZO), and the theoretical connection between ZO gradient estimation and randomized smoothing (Cohen et al. 2019; Liu et al. 2020). The proposed Hybrid FO-ZO optimizer is framed as a leader-follower (bilevel) game, building on the bilevel optimization perspective used in BLUR (Reisizadeh et al. 2025) but applied to optimizer scheduling rather than objective design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a deliberately noisier, lower-precision optimizer yields more robust unlearning challenges the default assumption that higher-order gradient information always produces better solutions, and offers a simple, objective-agnostic intervention (swap or augment the optimizer) that practitioners can adopt without redesigning unlearning algorithms. Limitations include evaluation only on 7B-parameter models, a limited set of unlearning methods (NPO, GradDiff, RMU), and the LMC basin-separation analysis being demonstrated on a single benchmark. Open questions include whether the optimizer-grade–robustness link generalizes to larger models, other safety-alignment tasks (e.g., RLHF unlearning), and whether the hybrid schedule can be further refined (e.g., adaptive switching criteria rather than fixed-step alternation).
