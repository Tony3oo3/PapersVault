## Problem

In goal-conditioned RL, goals are typically represented by raw state observations, which contain superfluous information (e.g., uncontrollable noise) and are not optimally structured for policy learning. The paper identifies two desiderata for an ideal goal representation—sufficiency (enough information to recover an optimal policy) and noise invariance (discarding irrelevant information)—and notes that no prior representation learning method provably satisfies both simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes offline datasets of state-action trajectories where states and goals are either low-dimensional state vectors or 64×64×3 RGB images. It produces a goal-conditioned policy π(a|s, φ(g)) that maps a current state and a learned goal embedding to an action. The key structural detail is that the goal is never used directly; instead, it is processed through a learned embedding φ(g) that encodes temporal-distance relations, while the state is embedded separately via ψ(s), and their inner product approximates the temporal distance between them.

## Method

The core contribution is the dual goal representation: a goal g is represented as the functional s ↦ d*(s,g), i.e., the set of optimal temporal distances from every state to g. The paper proves this representation is sufficient (an optimal policy can be recovered from it alone) and noise-invariant (goals sharing the same latent state under a Block CMP have identical dual representations). Practically, the infinite-dimensional functional is approximated via an inner-product parameterization d*(s,g) ≈ ψ(s)ᵀφ(g), where ψ and φ are learned by training a goal-conditioned value function with IQL (expectile regression + target Q network) on the offline dataset. The resulting goal embedding φ(g) is then plugged into any downstream offline GCRL algorithm (GCIVL, CRL, or GCFBC) as the goal representation, making the approach orthogonal to the policy-learning method. The inner-product form is chosen over metric parameterizations because it is universally approximating, enabling it to capture arbitrary temporal-distance structure.

## Results

On 13 state-based OGBench tasks with GCIVL as the downstream algorithm, dual goal representations achieve the best average success rate of 41±2, outperforming the original representation baseline (34±1), VIB (35±2), VIP (9±1), TRA (15±2), and BYOL-γ (19±2). Gains are particularly large on manipulation tasks: cube-single improves from 52 (Orig) to 89, cube-double from 35 to 60, and scene from 46 to 72. On 7 pixel-based tasks, dual representations achieve the best performance on 5 out of 7, with notable improvements on visual-antmaze-medium (78 vs. 66 Orig) and visual-antmaze-large (40 vs. 26 Orig). The method also shows the least sensitivity to the choice of downstream GCRL algorithm (GCIVL, CRL, GCFBC) compared to prior representation methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dual goal representations outperform all four prior representation learning methods on average across all three downstream GCRL algorithms. Compared to VIB (Shah et al., 2021), Dual achieves 41 vs. 35 average on GCIVL and 39 vs. 3 on CRL, with VIB collapsing to near-zero on many tasks (e.g., 0 on pointmaze-medium with CRL). Against VIP (Ma et al., 2023), Dual dominates by a wide margin (41 vs. 9 on GCIVL, 39 vs. 30 on CRL), as VIP's metric parameterization is less expressive for goal representation. Versus TRA (Myers et al., 2025), Dual leads on GCIVL (41 vs. 15) and CRL (39 vs. 28) but ties on GCFBC (23 vs. 23), suggesting TRA's contrastive learning is competitive when combined with flow-based policy extraction. Against BYOL-γ (Lawson et al., 2025), Dual consistently wins (41 vs. 19 on GCIVL, 39 vs. 30 on CRL, 23 vs. 22 on GCFBC). Notably, Dual also outperforms the no-representation 'Original' baseline on average (41 vs. 34), with the largest gains on manipulation tasks (cube-single: 89 vs. 52; scene: 72 vs. 46), while Original retains an edge on some navigation tasks (pointmaze-medium: 78 vs. 76).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on goal-conditioned IQL (Kostrikov et al., 2022) as the value-learning backbone and positions itself within the broader GCRL representation learning literature (VIB, VIP, TRA, BYOL-γ). Conceptually, it departs from temporal metric embedding methods (Sermanet et al., 2018; Ma et al., 2023; Park et al., 2024a) by using an inner-product parameterization and by explicitly targeting the goal representation rather than state representations or reward shaping. The theoretical framework draws on the Block CMP formalism (Du et al., 2019; Efroni et al., 2022) and borrows the 'dual' naming from the Yoneda lemma and Riesz representation theorem, framing a goal as the functional of temporal distances from all states rather than as a point in observation space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical justification (sufficiency + noise invariance) for why temporal-distance-based goal representations should be ideal for GCRL, and shows that a simple IQL-based recipe to approximate them consistently improves performance across 20 diverse tasks. The modularity—being combinable with any downstream GCRL algorithm—makes it a practical drop-in improvement. A notable limitation is the failure on visual puzzle tasks, traced to the late-fusion constraint of representation-conditioned policies, which the authors flag as requiring state-aware representation functions. Open questions include whether the inner-product approximation can be made more expressive for policy extraction and how to handle visual observations without sacrificing the noise-invariance guarantee.
