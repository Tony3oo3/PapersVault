## Problem

Existing optimal population coding models use simplified energy constraints (mean or maximum firing rate) that predict tuning curves either shorten or widen under metabolic stress, but cannot reproduce the simultaneous flattening (widening plus peak reduction) observed in food-restricted mouse cortex (Padamsey et al., 2022). Critically, these models also fail to capture firing rate homeostasis—the empirical finding that spike counts remain unchanged even as energy expenditure drops by ~30%. The gap is a lack of a tractable framework that connects ATP-level energy accounting to population-level coding optimization while preserving homeostasis.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a physical energy budget E in ATP/s (derived from biophysical simulation of a single-compartment neuron), a stimulus prior p(s), a per-neuron firing rate R(s), an activity level κ, and a coding objective (infomax, discrimax, or Lp error). It produces closed-form optimal gain g(s) and density d(s) functions, the resulting Fisher information FI(s), and an energy-dependent noise dispersion η_κ(E) = η_0 + c_1/(E − c_2). The unusual aspect is that E is not an abstract rate limit but a calibrated ATP/s value, and the noise model is a dispersed Poisson whose dispersion factor is a nonlinear function of E, making the output a complete, plug-and-play description of how tuning curve width, peak, and noise co-vary with metabolic state.

## Method

The paper introduces a two-constraint optimization over gain g(s) and density d(s) under the Ganguli–Simoncelli tiling parameterization: an approximate homeostasis constraint p(s)g(s) = R(s)d(s) (enforcing constant mean firing rate per neuron) and a generalized energy constraint ∫p(s)g(s)^α ds = E (penalizing gain, which corresponds to synaptic conductance). A stochastic Hodgkin-Huxley simulation in NEURON calibrates the free parameters by finding the noise-minimizing cell state (v_rest, g_leak) along each constant-energy contour, yielding α = 1 and the hyperbolic noise-energy trade-off η_κ(E). The Lagrangian solution gives g(s) ∝ E^{1/α} and d(s) ∝ E^{1/α}·p(s)/R(s) for infomax, with analogous closed forms for discrimax and Lp objectives. Because both gain and density scale identically with E, the tuning curve peak drops while its width increases—producing the flattening signature that neither the mean-FR constraint (which fixes width and shortens) nor the max-FR constraint (which fixes peak and widens) can replicate.

## Results

Under metabolic stress (29% reduction in ATP budget), the model predicts a 32% widening of tuning curves with zero change in mean firing rate, exactly matching the empirical observations in Padamsey et al. (2022) mouse visual cortex data. The analytical solution yields gain g(s) ∝ E^{1/α} and density d(s) ∝ E^{1/α}·R(s)^{-1}p(s) for the infomax case, with Fisher information scaling as η_κ(E)^{-1}·E^{3/α}. The biophysical simulation calibrates α=1 and the noise dispersion as η_κ(E) = η_0(κ) + c_1(κ)/(E−c_2(κ)), establishing a direct ATP-to-noise trade-off. Under a non-uniform natural scene prior, mean firing rate deviation remains within 0.2% across all coding objectives (infomax, discrimax, L₁ error).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two established population coding frameworks: Ganguli & Simoncelli (2010) and Wang et al. (2016a). Ganguli & Simoncelli constrains mean firing rate directly with fixed population size, predicting tuning curve *shortening* under energy reduction; to match the observed 32% widening, it requires a 24% direct reduction in firing rate, violating homeostasis by −24%. Wang et al. (2016a) maintains constant maximum firing rate while reducing coding capacity, predicting tuning curve *widening*; matching the 32% widening requires a 24% reduction in coding capacity, resulting in a 32% homeostatic violation. The proposed model uniquely captures *both* the 32% widening and the 29% ATP reduction while maintaining exact firing rate homeostasis (0% deviation under uniform prior, <0.2% under natural scene prior), matching Padamsey et al. (2022) data. The paper further demonstrates that both prior models are recoverable as special cases of the proposed framework (α=1 with firing-rate proxy recovers Ganguli & Simoncelli; α=3/2 with FI capacity recovers Wang et al.), providing a unified mathematical explanation for their partial successes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the efficient coding / optimal population code framework of Ganguli & Simoncelli (2010) and Wang et al. (2012, 2016a) by replacing their simplified energy constraints (mean firing rate, maximum firing rate, or total FI coding capacity) with two biophysically grounded constraints: an ATP-based energy budget derived from a stochastic Hodgkin-Huxley simulation in NEURON, and an approximate firing-rate homeostasis condition. The key conceptual departure is that energy is modeled as a function of synaptic conductance (analogous to gain) rather than spike count, and noise is modeled as an energy-dependent dispersed Poisson process whose dispersion η_κ(E) is extracted from the simulation's optimal noise-energy trade-off path. The authors show their general framework reduces to Ganguli & Simoncelli (α=1) and Wang et al. (α=3/2) as special cases, unifying previously disparate predictions (shortening vs. widening) under a single mathematical structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first analytical framework that correctly predicts the empirically observed tuning-curve flattening (32% widening with unchanged firing rate) under metabolic stress in mouse visual cortex, whereas prior models predict either shortening or widening. The biophysical simulation makes specific, testable predictions about how resting potential and leak conductance should co-vary under caloric deprivation at different activity levels, providing a concrete experimental target for future patch-clamp studies. Open questions include whether the homeostasis approximation holds for non-Gaussian or non-tiling populations, whether single-compartment neurons suffice, whether metabolic stress responses are truly information-theoretically optimal or driven by non-coding factors, and how the framework extends to multi-dimensional stimuli or network-level effects. The broader implication is that optimality should be considered over an organism's lifetime of varying resource availability rather than at snapshot states.
