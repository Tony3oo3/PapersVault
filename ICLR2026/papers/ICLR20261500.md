## Problem

Large time series models (LTMs) achieve strong zero-shot forecasting but degrade significantly across diverse domains due to inherent nonstationarity and distribution shifts. Finetuning a separate LTM per domain is computationally prohibitive and sacrifices generalizability, while the data-centric perspective of adapting input transformations to a frozen model has remained largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

TATO consumes a small set of historical time series samples (as few as 500, less than 2% of the training set) and a frozen LTM to search over transformation pipelines; the optimized pipeline is then applied to a lookback window (e.g., 7×96 points) at inference to produce a forecast. The optimization phase operates on augmented copies of the historical samples, while the final selected pipeline is applied only to original (non-augmented) test inputs, keeping the model parameters entirely untouched.

## Method

TATO formulates transformation pipeline discovery as a black-box hyperparameter optimization problem solved with Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE) over nine operators in three categories: context slicing (Trimmer, Sampler, Aligner), scale normalization (Scaler, Differencer, Warper), and outlier correction (Denoiser, Imputator, Clipper). During the search, diverse time series augmentations (flipping, warping, noise injection, translation, slope addition) are applied to historical samples to improve robustness against distribution shift. A two-stage Pareto-based ranking mechanism first filters out trials that underperform on any metric subset across augmented data, then ranks remaining candidates via a weighted sum of metric ranks (MSE weighted 3×, MAE 2×, others 1×) computed on original samples only. The entire optimization typically completes in under 2 minutes, and the selected pipeline is applied at inference with negligible overhead (<3 ms per sample).

## Results

TATO achieves an average MSE reduction of 13.6% and a maximum of 65.4% (Timer-LOTSA on Exchange) across 192 scenarios (8 datasets × 4 prediction horizons × 6 LTM variants). TATO matches or outperforms vanilla frozen LTMs in 84.3% of the 96 evaluated model-dataset cases. The largest consistent gains appear on ETTm2 (23.7% avg MSE reduction) and Exchange (35.1% avg MSE reduction), while Weather shows the smallest improvement (1.5% avg MSE). Optimization typically completes in under 2 minutes with 500 trials and 500 samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TATO is evaluated as a data-transformation layer on top of six frozen LTM variants: Timer-UTSD, Timer-LOTSA, Moirai-small/base/large, and Chronos-tiny. Timer-LOTSA benefits most from TATO, achieving a 24.8% average MSE reduction (up to 65.4% on Exchange and 40.6% on ETTm1), while Timer-UTSD sees a more modest 6.0% average MSE reduction. Among Moirai variants, larger models gain more (Moirai-large: 14.0% avg MSE vs. Moirai-small: 9.2%), suggesting TATO's transformations are more impactful when the model has greater capacity to exploit better-prepared inputs. Chronos-tiny achieves a 14.5% average MSE reduction, with its largest gain on Weather (17.9%). TATO loses or ties in a small number of cases (e.g., Moirai-large on Exchange at -7.5% MSE, Chronos-tiny on ETTh2 at -2.8% MSE), indicating that extreme distribution shifts can still limit transformation-based adaptation. When applied on top of universally finetuned LTMs (Table 3), TATO provides additional 4.5–9.9% MSE reduction, confirming that data transformation and parameter finetuning address complementary aspects of domain adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant model-centric paradigm for deploying large time series models (LTMs), where finetuning creates domain-specific model instances, and instead proposes a data-centric alternative: optimizing input transformations while keeping the LTM frozen. It builds on classical time series preprocessing operators (normalization, differencing, outlier imputation, downsampling) and repurposes them as a structured search space explored via Bayesian optimization (TPE). The two-stage Pareto-based ranking mechanism for multi-metric pipeline selection draws directly from many-objective optimization literature (Palakonda & Mallipeddi, 2017), and the data augmentation strategy during search borrows from time series augmentation surveys. Unlike test-time adaptation methods (TokenMerging, NewNorm) that modify model internals or use self-supervised objectives, TATO operates entirely in the data preprocessing layer, making it model-agnostic and composable with any frozen LTM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TATO offers a practical, low-cost path to deploying a single frozen LTM across heterogeneous domains without the compute and generalization costs of per-domain finetuning, with optimization completing in under two minutes. The consistent 13.6% average MSE reduction across 192 scenarios (8 datasets × 4 horizons × 6 model variants) and the complementary gains on top of universally finetuned models suggest that input transformation is an underexploited degree of freedom in LTM deployment. Key limitations include restriction to univariate forecasting, a fixed set of nine operators, and occasional negative results on highly variable data (Weather) or specific model-dataset pairs. Open questions include extending to multivariate settings, adaptive operator selection, and whether the gains persist under more severe distribution shifts than those captured by the augmentation-based search.
