## Problem

In-context image editing requires coherent multi-turn sequences of text and images as training data, but existing methods depend on task-specific pipelines (segmentation, inpainting, diffusion denoising) to curate paired editing examples, making it difficult to construct long-form contextualized data at scale. This paper asks whether a meaningful in-context image editing model can be learned solely from native videos, without any standalone paired images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263906/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an interleaved multimodal sequence (I_0, T_0, T_m0, M_00, T_m1, M_01, I_1, …, I_K) where images are VAE-encoded into latent tokens, text instructions are encoded by Flan-T5, and optional RoE segmentation masks are treated as additional image tokens. Sessions span 2–20 images, and the model outputs the next image in the sequence (the edited result) and optionally the current or next RoE mask, all generated via flow-matching in the VAE latent space with 50 sampling steps and classifier-free guidance at scale 10.

## Method

VINCIE constructs training data by sparsely sampling frames from native videos, using a VLM with chain-of-thought prompting to annotate visual transitions between adjacent frames, and leveraging GroundingDINO + SAM2 to extract Regions-of-Editing (RoE) segmentation masks. The model is a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) initialized from a video foundation model (MM-DiT, 3B/7B), using either full attention or block-wise causal attention over concatenated text and image latent tokens with separate 1D/3D RoPE position embeddings and learnable <TURN> boundary tokens. Training jointly optimizes three proxy tasks—Next Image Prediction (NIP), Current Segmentation Prediction (CSP), and Next Segmentation Prediction (NSP)—via a random context dropout mechanism that randomly drops contextual images, texts, and masks, enabling the model to learn grounding, controllable generation, and multi-concept composition simultaneously. The key insight is that videos inherently encode long-duration visual dynamics (object appearance/disappearance, posture shifts, camera changes) that naturally teach multi-turn editing without requiring any hand-crafted paired data, making the approach trivially scalable to the ~10M sessions collected from web video.

## Results

On the proposed MSE-Bench (5-turn editing, GPT-4o evaluated), Ours*(7B)+SFT achieves a 48.7% success rate at Turn-5, dramatically outperforming all academic baselines (which fall below 41.3%) and approaching proprietary models like Nano Banana (64.3%) and GPT Image 1 (64.0%). On MagicBrush (3-turn), Ours*(7B)+SFT attains the best consistency scores (DINO 0.891, CLIP-I 0.937 at Turn-1), surpassing Nano Banana (0.886, 0.933) and all other baselines. Scaling training data from 0.25M to 10M sessions increases the Turn-5 success rate from 5% to 22%, demonstrating strong scalability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MSE-Bench Turn-5, VINCIE (7B+SFT) at 48.7% substantially outperforms all academic baselines: Bagel (41.3%), Step1X-Edit (14.0%), OmniGen2 (13.3%), and Instruct-Pix2Pix (6.0%), with the gap widening dramatically at later turns. Against proprietary models, VINCIE beats Qwen-Image-Edit (43.0%) and FLUX.1-Kontext (44.0%) at Turn-5 but trails Nano Banana (64.3%) and GPT Image 1 (64.0%) by roughly 15–16 points, which the authors attribute to their larger training data and model scale. On MagicBrush Turn-1, Ours*(7B)+SFT achieves the highest DINO (0.891) and CLIP-I (0.937) scores, narrowly beating Nano Banana (0.886, 0.933) and all other methods, though it lags slightly on CLIP-T (0.283 vs. 0.287). The paper also shows that training on video sequence data alone (vs. pairwise editing data) improves Turn-5 success from 1.0% to 22.0%, and that pretraining on video sequences followed by SFT on pairwise data yields the best overall performance (25.0% at Turn-5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in image editing, where training data is constructed via task-specific pipelines (InstructPix2Pix's GPT-3 + Stable Diffusion pairs, UltraEdit's grounding + inpainting, MagicBrush's manual annotation) by instead learning directly from native video. It builds on the Diffusion Transformer architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023) and video foundation models (Seaweed-7B, HunyuanVideo) for initialization, but introduces a fundamentally different data source: long interleaved sequences of 2–20 frames with VLM-annotated transitions and GroundingDINO+SAM2 segmentation masks. Unlike prior video-to-editing work (RealGeneral, UES, UniReal) that extracts only two frames per video, VINCIE exploits full temporal context across many frames, and unlike single-turn editing methods, it models multi-turn contextual dependencies through three jointly-trained proxy tasks (next-image, current-segmentation, next-segmentation prediction).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that in-context image editing can be learned from video alone without any paired editing data, opening a scalable data pathway that leverages the vast corpus of web video. The MSE-Bench benchmark and the clear scaling curve (5%→22% success at turn-5 from 0.25M→10M sessions) provide a concrete research direction for the community. However, the absolute performance gap to proprietary systems (48.7% vs. 64.3% at turn-5) and the reliance on an in-house 3B/7B video foundation model limit immediate reproducibility and deployment. Open questions include whether the video-only paradigm can close the gap to paired-data approaches at sufficient scale, whether the emergent capabilities (multi-concept composition, chain-of-editing) are robust or artifacts of the specific training recipe, and how the approach generalizes to editing types underrepresented in natural video (e.g., global style transfer, fine-grained attribute changes).
