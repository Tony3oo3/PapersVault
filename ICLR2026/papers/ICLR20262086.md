## Problem

The rapid adoption of LLMs creates a risk that negligent reviewers will use them to generate peer reviews, undermining the integrity of scientific publication. However, no existing dataset or benchmark exists for evaluating AI text detection methods specifically in the peer review domain, and prior work has only analyzed AI-generated text at the corpus level rather than at the individual review level where detection is actually needed.

## Input / Output

The Anchor method takes two inputs: the candidate peer review text and the full manuscript text of the paper being reviewed. It produces a binary label (AI-generated or human-written) derived from a cosine similarity score between the review's embedding and a synthetic anchor review's embedding, thresholded at a value calibrated to a target false positive rate. Because the source LLM is unknown in practice, the method generates anchor reviews from three different LLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini, Claude) and applies an OR-voting scheme across their scores.

## Method

The Anchor method is a context-aware, training-free detection approach that exploits the unique structure of peer review: the manuscript being reviewed is available as additional context. For a given paper, a synthetic 'Anchor Review' is generated using a simple generic prompt (distinct from the prompts used to create the test dataset), then both the anchor and the candidate review are embedded via OpenAI's text-embedding-003-small model. The cosine similarity between these embeddings is compared against a threshold learned from a calibration set; high similarity indicates the candidate review is likely AI-generated for that specific paper. To handle the black-box setting where the generating LLM is unknown, multiple anchor reviews are produced by different LLMs and a voting scheme is applied (positive if any anchor triggers detection). This differs from white-box methods like DetectLLM (which require access to the generating model) and from context-free methods like DNA-GPT (which regenerate text without grounding in the source manuscript), making it uniquely suited to the peer review setting.

## Results

On the AI-Peer-Review-Detection-Benchmark, the proposed Anchor method achieves 63.5% TPR at 0.1% target FPR for GPT-4o reviews, substantially outperforming the best baseline Binoculars (17.1%) by 46.4 percentage points. At the more relaxed 1% FPR threshold, Anchor reaches 88.8% TPR (GPT-4o), 86.5% (Gemini), and 81.8% (Claude), while Binoculars achieves 45.2%, 85.5%, and 77.0% respectively. Among the 18 baselines, all other methods (MAGE-D, s5, MFD, GLTR, DetectGPT, etc.) achieve TPRs below 20% even at 1% FPR for GPT-4o reviews, highlighting the difficulty of detecting state-of-the-art LLM-generated peer reviews under strict false-positive constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Anchor method decisively outperforms Binoculars [Hans et al., 2024] on GPT-4o reviews (63.5% vs. 17.1% TPR at 0.1% FPR; 88.8% vs. 45.2% at 1% FPR) and Claude reviews (59.6% vs. 43.5% at 0.1% FPR), but is slightly behind Binoculars on Gemini reviews (59.7% vs. 61.5% at 0.1% FPR). All other baselines—MAGE-D [Li et al., 2024], s5 [Spiegel & Macko, 2023], MFD [Wu & Xiang, 2023], GLTR [Gehrmann et al., 2019], DetectGPT [Mitchell et al., 2023], FastDetectGPT [Bao et al., 2023], Loglikelihood [Solaiman et al., 2019], DetectLLM-LLR [Su et al., 2023], ChatGPT-D [Guo et al., 2023], and NTNU-D [Sivesind & Winje, 2023]—achieve TPRs below 20% at 1% FPR for GPT-4o reviews, with most falling below 10%. The performance gap between Anchor and the second-best method is largest for GPT-4o (the hardest-to-detect LLM), suggesting that manuscript-context-aware detection is most valuable when the generating model is the most capable. On open-source LLMs (Llama, Qwen), Binoculars achieves near-perfect detection (>98% TPR at 0.1% FPR), making the Anchor advantage less pronounced in that regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' earlier anchor-based detection approach (Yu et al., 2024) by incorporating manuscript context into the detection pipeline, moving from a review-only signal to a review-plus-paper comparison via cosine similarity of text embeddings. It positions itself against general-purpose AI text detection benchmarks (RAID-TD, M4, HC3, GRiD, Beemo) that evaluate single-model or cross-domain text but do not address the peer review domain or leverage the structural context available there. The 18 baselines are drawn from the IMGTB framework, spanning perplexity-based, embedding-based, and fine-tuned classifier approaches, providing a comprehensive comparison point for the proposed context-aware method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 788,984-review dataset is the largest resource for studying AI text detection in peer review and will likely become the standard benchmark for this practically urgent problem. The finding that even the best general-purpose detector (Binoculars) achieves only 17% TPR at 0.1% FPR for GPT-4o reviews underscores that current tools are insufficient for real-world deployment where false positives carry severe reputational consequences. Open questions include detecting lightly edited human reviews (where TPR drops below 3%), the inverse 'human revises AI draft' scenario, and whether the synthetic-review evaluation generalizes to the diverse, uncontrolled prompts used by actual reviewers in the wild.
