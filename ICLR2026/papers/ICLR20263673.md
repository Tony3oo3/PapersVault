## Problem

Existing Logit Adjustment (LA) methods for long-tailed semi-supervised learning suffer from two interrelated flaws: naive frequency counting overestimates head-class prevalence due to sample redundancy (causing harmful over-suppression), and the overall adjustment strength τ is treated as a fixed hyperparameter despite being highly sensitive to the estimated class distribution. These issues are compounded when the unlabeled data distribution deviates from the labeled distribution, making the bias unpredictable and difficult to mitigate with pre-defined anchor distributions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled set of RGB images with ground-truth class labels and a larger unlabeled set of RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR/STL-10, 32×32 or 64×64 for SIN-127). It produces balanced-accuracy class predictions on a test set and, during training, generates calibrated pseudo-labels for unlabeled samples via a dual-branch architecture (a balanced branch for distribution estimation and a standard branch for pseudo-label generation). The unlabeled set may contain out-of-distribution samples (as in STL-10), and the true unlabeled class distribution is unknown during training.

## Method

CoLA co-designs the class-wise and overall components of Logit Adjustment. First, De-Duplicated Distribution Estimation (DDDE) replaces naive frequency counting with an effective-rank-based estimate: for each class, it forms a representation matrix from high-confidence pseudo-labeled samples, computes the singular value spectrum, and defines the class's effective sample size as the exponential of the Shannon entropy of the normalized singular values, yielding a redundancy-aware class distribution. Second, Logit Meta-Calibration (LMC) constructs a proxy validation set by Bernoulli-resampling labeled data to match the DDDE-estimated distribution, then meta-learns the optimal overall adjustment strength τ by minimizing cross-entropy on this proxy set using a linear LA term (−τ·p) rather than the original logarithmic form, ensuring numerical stability. The optimization over τ is proven convex, guaranteeing a unique global minimum. A generalization bound links the accuracy of the DDDE estimate to the tightness of the bound, formally connecting the two components. The full pipeline operates within FixMatch's confidence-thresholding framework with a warm-up phase following ACR before LMC takes over in the final training epochs.

## Results

CoLA achieves state-of-the-art balanced accuracy across all five unlabeled distributions on both CIFAR-10-LT (81.87–85.61%) and CIFAR-100-LT (50.26–60.39%), outperforming the strongest LA-based baseline (Meta-Expert or Sim-Pro) by 0.5–2.3 percentage points. On the more challenging STL-10-LT with unknown unlabeled distribution, CoLA reaches 73.32% and 79.70% under two settings, surpassing the runner-up by 1.0–2.0%. On the large-scale SIN-127 benchmark, CoLA attains 24.18% (32×32) and 37.49% (64×64), exceeding the best baseline (ABC) by 0.5% and 2.6% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoLA consistently outperforms all five LA-based baselines (ACR, CPE, Meta-Expert, Sim-Pro, TRAS) across every distribution on both CIFAR-10-LT and CIFAR-100-LT. On CIFAR-10-LT, the gap over the strongest LA competitor Meta-Expert ranges from 0.54% (REV) to 1.62% (MID); on the harder CIFAR-100-LT, CoLA leads Sim-Pro/ACR by 0.74–2.33%, with the largest margin on the reversed distribution (60.39 vs. 59.21). Among non-LA methods, CoLA also surpasses the best resampling (ABC), reweighting (SAW), data mixing (CoSSL), and distribution alignment (DARP) baselines by 1–5% on most settings. On STL-10-LT with unknown unlabeled distribution, CoLA beats Meta-Expert by 1.95% (N₁=150) and 1.07% (N₁=450); however, ADELLO with its OOD distillation slightly edges out CoLA in two of four settings, while CoLA clearly wins against ADELLO without distillation. On SIN-127, CoLA leads ABC (the best non-LA baseline) by 0.52% at 32×32 and 2.61% at 64×64, confirming scalability to large-scale imbalanced datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Logit Adjustment (LA) paradigm for Long-Tailed Semi-Supervised Learning, building directly on FixMatch as the SSL backbone and ACR's dual-branch architecture with warm-up scheduling. It departs from prior LA-based methods (ACR, CPE, Meta-Expert, Sim-Pro) in two specific ways: replacing naive frequency counting with an effective-rank-based distribution estimator (DDDE) to address sample redundancy in head classes, and replacing the fixed overall adjustment hyperparameter τ with a meta-learned value (LMC) optimized on a distribution-matched proxy set. The paper also provides the first generalization bound and convexity analysis for the LA optimization in the LTSSL setting, filling a theoretical gap that prior empirical LA-based works left unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the optimal overall adjustment strength τ is highly sensitive to the estimated class distribution and does not monotonically correlate with the imbalance ratio challenges the common practice of treating τ as a dataset-level hyperparameter, suggesting that adaptive calibration is necessary for robust pseudo-labeling under distribution mismatch. The effective-rank approach to distribution estimation offers a principled alternative to frequency counting that could be applicable beyond LTSSL to any setting where sample redundancy inflates apparent class prevalence. Open questions include how the method behaves under more severe distribution shifts (e.g., OOD contamination in unlabeled data, where ADELLO outperforms CoLA on STL-10), whether the class-conditional distribution assumption (Assumption 3) holds in practice, and whether the meta-learning overhead scales gracefully to very large class spaces.
