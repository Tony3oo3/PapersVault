## Problem

Existing dynamic rank allocation methods for LoRA (e.g., AdaLoRA, SaLoRA) rely on heuristic element-level importance metrics, globally sort rank directions across all matrices without matrix-level distinction, and only support unidirectional rank pruning without mechanisms to expand capacity in layers that need additional expressive power. These limitations prevent principled, adaptive allocation of model capacity across layers during fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261148/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FlexLoRA fine-tunes frozen pre-trained models (DeBERTaV3-base, LLaMA-3-8B, ViT-B/16) on downstream classification tasks, taking text sequences or RGB images as input and producing class labels. The method operates under a fixed total parameter budget (e.g., ~1.9M for NLU, ~21–57M for commonsense reasoning, ~1.2M for vision) while dynamically reallocating the effective rank across individual LoRA modules during training, so the per-module rank varies over time even though the aggregate parameter count stays constant.

## Method

FlexLoRA represents each LoRA update in SVD form ΔW = PΛQ with an orthogonality regularization, then introduces three synergistic mechanisms. First, it computes a matrix-level spectral entropy score I(Λ) = −(1/log r) Σ s_i log(s_i+ε) where s_i = λ_i²/Σλ_j², capturing the distributional geometry of singular values rather than individual parameter sensitivities. Second, at each allocation step it performs bidirectional rank adjustment under a global budget b(t) (cubic-decay schedule): the b(t) lowest-scoring matrices lose their smallest singular direction, while the b(t) highest-scoring matrices gain a new one. Third, newly added singular directions use zero-impact initialization (zero singular value, Gaussian-sampled vectors), ensuring the insertion does not perturb the current output and the new direction is gradually learned. This combination of entropy-guided scoring, bidirectional allocation, and stable initialization distinguishes FlexLoRA from prior prune-only, element-level heuristics.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark with DeBERTaV3-base, FlexLoRA achieves an average score of 89.1, surpassing AdaLoRA (88.1), LoRA r=8 (81.7), and even Full FT (88.3), with the largest gains on CoLA (71.8 vs. 70.0 for AdaLoRA) and RTE (88.8 vs. 88.1). On commonsense reasoning with LLaMA3-8B at rank 32, FlexLoRA reaches 85.5 average, outperforming LoRA (85.4), AdaLoRA (84.5), and LoRA-Dash (85.4). On the VTAB visual benchmark with ViT-B/16, FlexLoRA attains 67.8% average accuracy, exceeding LoRA r=14 (66.7%) and AdaLoRA (64.7%), with a notable +8.9 point improvement over LoRA on CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexLoRA consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across every benchmark. Against the two primary dynamic-rank competitors, FlexLoRA beats AdaLoRA by +1.0 on GLUE (89.1 vs. 88.1), +0.1 on commonsense reasoning at r=8 (85.2 vs. 85.1), and +2.1 points on VTAB (67.8% vs. 64.7%); it also surpasses LoRA by +7.4 on GLUE, +2.8 on commonsense reasoning (r=8), and +1.1 on VTAB. Among other LoRA variants, FlexLoRA exceeds DoRA by +2.6 on GLUE and +0.2 on VTAB (Table 16), DyLoRA by +3.6 on GLUE, and PiSSA by +1.5 on VTAB. Against adapter-based methods, FlexLoRA leads AdapterFusion by +0.7 on GLUE, Houlsby's Adapter by +2.1 on VTAB (Table 16), and AdaptFormer by +1.3 on VTAB. The closest competitor overall is AdapterFusion on GLUE (88.4 vs. 89.1) and FACt on VTAB (75.6 vs. 76.0 in Table 16), where margins are under 1 point, but FlexLoRA still maintains the lead in every head-to-head comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexLoRA extends the LoRA lineage by replacing its fixed-rank design with a dynamic, SVD-based formulation that supports both rank pruning and expansion under a global budget. It departs from AdaLoRA and similar dynamic-rank methods (SaLoRA, AutoLoRA) in three key ways: (i) it evaluates importance at the matrix level using spectral energy entropy rather than element-level gradient sensitivity heuristics, (ii) it performs bidirectional allocation (pruning low-importance directions while expanding high-importance ones) rather than unidirectional pruning, and (iii) it introduces a zero-impact initialization scheme for newly added singular directions to preserve training stability. The orthogonality regularization on singular vectors follows prior SVD-based LoRA variants (AdaLoRA, PiSSA).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The entropy-based matrix-level criterion offers a more principled and noise-robust alternative to sensitivity-based heuristics for rank allocation, and the bidirectional mechanism addresses a practical gap where layers needing additional capacity could not recover pruned ranks. The observation that shallow layers (L0–L3) receive the most rank expansion on CoLA—contrary to the common assumption that deeper layers dominate task adaptation—provides an interpretable signal about where linguistic adaptation actually occurs. However, the performance gains over AdaLoRA are modest (e.g., 89.1 vs. 88.1 on GLUE, 85.2 vs. 85.1 on commonsense at r=8), and experiments are limited to relatively small backbones (DeBERTaV3-base, LLaMA-3-8B, ViT-B/16); scalability to 70B+ models and whether the entropy metric transfers to non-LoRA PEFT paradigms remain open.
