## Problem

Existing optical side-channel attacks require sensors co-located with or in direct view of the target display, leaving physically isolated or shielded environments seemingly secure. This paper demonstrates that passive diffuse reflections of screen light onto nearby walls constitute a viable, non-contact optical side channel, but the screen-to-speckle mapping is severely ill-conditioned (near-singular Jacobian violating Hadamard stability) and involves irreversible compression that destroys global semantic structure, making stable reconstruction highly non-trivial.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263100/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of the passive speckle pattern formed by diffuse reflection of self-emissive screen light onto a nearby surface (e.g., a wall), captured without any direct line-of-sight to the screen, RF monitoring, or communication link. The output is a full-resolution RGB reconstruction of the original screen content. The input is inherently low-signal and highly compressed relative to the source, with perturbation sensitivity amplified by the ill-conditioned projection operator, and the reconstruction must remain stable across varying illumination levels (0–300 nits), camera poses, and distances (2–6 m).

## Method

IR4Net integrates two modules. PRIrr-Approximation reformulates optical-field inversion as a physics-guided, learnable iterative trajectory with momentum-based updates that embed forward/reverse radiative-transfer operators, constraining the solution path to suppress perturbation amplification; within each iteration, a dual-path dissipation stage applies second-order differential-kernel spatial diffusion in parallel with attention-based semantic attenuation, followed by a frequency-selective multi-scale upsampling scheme that Fourier-decomposes features, gates low/high-frequency propagation via adaptive channel weights, and hierarchically reconstructs from contours to details. ICSR then addresses irreversible semantic loss by maintaining two parallel encoders—a primary structural network and a collaborative semantic completion network—whose features are aligned via cosine-similarity loss in a shared embedding space, enabling context-driven completion of occluded and diffraction-corrupted regions under perceptual-consistency constraints. The full network is trained end-to-end with supervised pixel-level loss on paired speckle-to-screen data across four scene categories.

## Results

IR4Net achieves the best PSNR, RMSE, and SSIM across all four datasets (ReSh-WebSight, ReSh-Password, ReSh-Screen, ReSh-Chart) compared to 11 baselines. On ReSh-Screen, it reaches 25.81 dB PSNR and 0.817 SSIM, a 15.7% PSNR gain over the strongest baseline (Uformer, 22.30 dB). On ReSh-WebSight, RMSE drops to 26.72 (vs. 31.03 for AST, a 13.9% reduction), and on ReSh-Password it attains 0.887 SSIM, the highest across all methods. Under luminance attenuation (0–300 nits), IR4Net degrades only 25.9% in PSNR on ReSh-Screen, whereas UNet collapses by ~68%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IR4Net outperforms all 11 baselines on every metric across all four datasets. Among reconstruction-centric methods, Uformer is the closest competitor (e.g., 22.30 dB PSNR on ReSh-Screen vs. IR4Net's 25.81 dB, a 3.5 dB gap), while ConvIR leads on ReSh-Password PSNR (14.78 dB) but still trails IR4Net (15.03 dB) and falls short on SSIM (0.867 vs. 0.887). Generation-centric baselines (pix2pix, CycleGAN, BicycleGAN, DivCo) perform substantially worse, with pix2pix achieving only 8.04 dB PSNR on ReSh-Screen and CycleGAN 10.36 dB, indicating that unpaired GAN-based translation is ill-suited to the ill-conditioned speckle-to-screen inversion. The largest absolute gains appear on ReSh-Screen (PSNR +3.5 dB over Uformer; RMSE reduced from 22.89 to 16.53) and ReSh-WebSight (RMSE reduced from 30.26 to 26.72), where the physics-guided PRIrr-Approximation and frequency-selective upsampling most directly counteract perturbation amplification. Under low-luminance stress (Table 3), IR4Net's advantage widens dramatically: at 300 nits it retains 19.14 dB PSNR on ReSh-Screen while UNet drops to 6.41 dB and ConvIR to 10.44 dB, confirming that the physics-regularized trajectory and semantic completion (ICSR) provide robustness that purely data-driven baselines lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of side-channel security and physics-informed image restoration. It departs from existing optical side channels (LightSpy, ambient-light-sensor imaging) that require sensors co-located with or in direct view of the display, instead exploiting passive diffuse wall reflections as a remote, no-line-of-sight attack vector. Methodologically, it repurposes physics-guided restoration ideas (radiative-transfer-constrained inversion, frequency-selective progressive upsampling, attention-based semantic completion) from dehazing and microscopy reconstruction, but reconfigures them around the specific ill-posedness of speckle-to-screen inversion where the Jacobian spectrum is near-singular. No prior work addresses this particular inverse mapping, so the paper does not build on a direct methodological lineage but rather transplants and adapts components from adjacent restoration and security literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a widely held security assumption—that physical isolation, EM shielding, or air gaps guarantee confidentiality—by demonstrating that mere wall-scattered luminescence suffices for passive, non-contact screen reconstruction. The four introduced datasets (ReSh-WebSight, ReSh-Password, ReSh-Screen, ReSh-Chart) are the first benchmarks for this task, but absolute reconstruction quality remains moderate (PSNR 15–26 dB, SSIM 0.7–0.89), and the comparison baselines are drawn from mismatched degradation tasks (low-light, dehazing, general restoration) rather than purpose-built speckle-inversion models, leaving the true performance ceiling unclear. Open questions include whether the attack generalizes to real-world multi-source illumination, textured or non-planar surfaces, and whether practical countermeasures (e.g., anti-speckle coatings, temporal dithering) can close the gap.
