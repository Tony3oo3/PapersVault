## Problem

Existing ordinal embedding methods for reconstructing perceptual spaces from triplet comparisons require the embedding dimension to be specified a priori, with no principled mechanism to determine the true intrinsic dimensionality. This forces practitioners to choose an overparameterized dimension that satisfies all triplet constraints while masking the latent geometric structure of subjective percepts. No prior method can jointly recover both the ordinal embedding and its intrinsic rank from noisy relative similarity judgments without expensive hypothesis testing over multiple candidate dimensions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes noisy triplet comparisons of the form 'Is A more similar to B than C?' (with Gaussian noise modeling human response uncertainty) and produces an embedding matrix Z ∈ R^{N×d'} where d' is deliberately set larger than the true rank (e.g., 15). The intrinsic dimensionality is recovered implicitly: the Schatten-p regularization drives smaller singular values toward zero, so the effective rank of Z (count of non-negligible singular values) directly reveals the intrinsic dimensionality without any explicit dimensionality search, cross-validation, or hypothesis enumeration.

## Method

LORE extends Soft Ordinal Embedding (SOE) by smoothing the triplet hinge loss with softplus for differentiability and adding a nonconvex Schatten-p quasi-norm regularizer (‖Z‖_p^p = Σσ_i(Z)^p, p=0.5) that penalizes larger singular values less severely than the nuclear norm, enabling automatic rank recovery. The resulting nonconvex objective is optimized via an iteratively reweighted algorithm that alternates between a proximal gradient step on the smoothed triplet loss and a singular value thresholding operation (subtracting (p/μ)σ^{p-1} from each singular value, then zeroing negatives) at each iteration. Convergence to a stationary point is guaranteed by verifying the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz property of the objective under standard assumptions. The per-iteration cost is O(d'(T + Nd')), making it scalable to large percept and triplet counts, and a single regularization setting (λ≈0.01) yields both high triplet accuracy and accurate rank recovery across varying noise levels, percept counts, and query fractions without further tuning.

## Results

On three crowdsourced ordinal datasets, LORE achieves comparable test triplet accuracy to all baselines (82.45% on Food-100, 84.08% on Materials, 52.12% on Cars) while uniquely recovering a dramatically lower intrinsic rank (3.3, 2.23, and 3 respectively) compared to all other methods which default to the full embedding dimension of 15. On the simulated LLM perceptual experiment (Section 5.4), LORE closely tracks the true intrinsic rank across ranks 1–10 while all baseline OE methods saturate at the embedding dimension. LORE is the second-fastest method after FORTE, with runtimes of 4–7 seconds on the real datasets versus 1700+ seconds for Dim-CV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LORE matches or slightly trails the best baselines on test triplet accuracy (within ~1–2%) but is the only method that recovers a low intrinsic rank: on Food-100 it achieves rank 3.3 versus 15 for SOE, FORTE, t-STE, and CKL, and on Materials it achieves rank 2.23 versus 15 for all others. Against SOE (the strongest direct-embedding baseline), LORE wins on Materials accuracy (84.08% vs. 81.86%) and is 4× faster (5.77s vs. 35.48s), while tying on Food-100 (82.45% vs. 82.34%). Against FORTE (the strongest Gram-matrix baseline), LORE wins on both Food-100 (82.45% vs. 81.73%) and Materials (84.08% vs. 79.35%) accuracy. Against Dim-CV (the hypothesis-testing dimensionality estimator), LORE substantially outperforms on triplet accuracy (82.45% vs. 77.67% on Food-100; 84.08% vs. 78.10% on Materials) and is roughly 250× faster (6.64s vs. 1721.9s), while Dim-CV underfits by collapsing to rank ~1. On the Musicians dataset (448 percepts, undersampled), LORE achieves 75.63% accuracy at rank 27.8, trailing SOE (81.41%) and t-STE (79.49%) but still recovering a lower effective rank than the full 30-dimensional embedding used by all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

LORE extends the Soft Ordinal Embedding (SOE) framework by replacing the fixed-dimension embedding with a Schatten-p quasi-norm regularized objective, drawing directly from the nonconvex low-rank matrix recovery literature (Lu et al., 2014; Marjanovic & Solo, 2012). The optimization strategy is adapted from Sun et al.'s (2017) iteratively reweighted proximal algorithm for Schatten-p norm minimization, with convergence guarantees inherited from that framework via the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz property. The key departure from all prior ordinal embedding methods (GNMDS, CKL, FORTE, t-STE, SOE, OENN) is that none of them recover intrinsic dimensionality; the closest prior work, Künstle et al. (2022), estimates rank via hypothesis testing over separately trained embeddings, which is both hypothesis-dependent and computationally prohibitive. LORE is the first to integrate Schatten-p regularization into a scalable ordinal embedding, making dimensionality an emergent property of the optimization rather than a user-specified hyperparameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For psychophysics, LORE removes the need to guess the number of perceptual dimensions before data collection, enabling data-driven discovery of latent perceptual structure that was previously masked by overparameterized embeddings. The practical finding that λ=0.01 yields both high triplet accuracy and correct rank recovery across a wide range of noise levels, percept counts, and intrinsic dimensions makes the method nearly plug-and-play for practitioners. However, the paper acknowledges the absence of theoretical guarantees for exact rank recovery (only convergence to stationary points), and the empirical rank recovery degrades at higher intrinsic dimensions due to the curse of dimensionality. Open questions include developing active learning strategies that exploit the learned dimensionality for more efficient query design, and establishing conditions under which the stationary point corresponds to the true intrinsic rank.
