## Problem

Standard DP optimization (DP-SGD) requires objectives that decompose over individual examples, but rate constraints—encompassing group fairness, false negative rate, and related criteria—depend on aggregate prediction statistics across subpopulations, creating inter-sample dependencies that break per-sample processing. Prior DP work addressed only fairness-specific constraints, leaving the broader family of rate-constrained problems without a general private optimization framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a training dataset with features, labels, and sensitive attributes, a rate constraint specification defined by a global partition of the dataset plus per-constraint weight vectors over prediction rates, and a privacy budget (ε, δ). It outputs trained classifier parameters (logistic regression or ResNet16) that satisfy the specified rate constraints within a user-chosen slack γ while providing (ε, δ)-DP guarantees over the full training process. Unlike penalization-based approaches, the practitioner directly specifies the maximum allowed disparity rather than tuning a penalty hyperparameter.

## Method

RaCO-DP is a differentially private variant of Stochastic Gradient Descent-Ascent (SGDA) that solves the Lagrangian formulation of rate-constrained problems. At each iteration, it privately computes a histogram of soft prediction counts over a global partition of the dataset using the Laplace mechanism; this single histogram then serves dual purposes: (1) it enables per-sample decomposition of the constraint regularizer for the primal gradient (which is then clipped and noised via the Gaussian mechanism as in DP-SGD), and (2) it allows evaluation of all constraint values for the dual update via post-processing at zero additional privacy cost. The key structural insight is that the linear dependence of the Lagrangian on the dual parameter λ, combined with the histogram's shared structure across constraints, limits the total privacy overhead to one private histogram query per step. A novel convergence analysis exploiting this linearity yields a T^{-1/4} stationarity rate for non-convex objectives, improving over the T^{-1/6} rate of prior SGDA analyses.

## Results

On CelebA with ResNet16 (6.4M parameters) under demographic parity at ε=1, RaCO-DP achieves 90% accuracy with a 10% disparity gap, approaching the non-private model's 95% accuracy. On tabular benchmarks (Adult, Credit-Card, Parkinsons) with logistic regression at ε=1, RaCO-DP consistently achieves higher accuracy at any fixed fairness disparity compared to all baselines. On ACSEmployment with 18 simultaneous demographic parity constraints at ε=1, RaCO-DP maintains a competitive utility-fairness trade-off. Computationally, RaCO-DP trains three orders of magnitude faster than DP-FERMI on the Adult dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RaCO-DP Pareto-dominates DP-FERMI (Lowy et al., 2023) across accuracy–fairness–privacy trade-offs on all tabular benchmarks (Adult, Credit-Card, Parkinsons) under both demographic parity and equalized odds constraints, while also training three orders of magnitude faster. Against Tran et al. (2021) and Jagielski et al. (2019), RaCO-DP achieves higher accuracy at any fixed fairness disparity under the same privacy budget (ε=1). A key distinction is that RaCO-DP provides full (ε,δ)-DP over the entire dataset, whereas Jagielski et al. and Tran et al. only protect the sensitive attribute (label privacy), which is a strictly weaker guarantee; despite this stronger privacy requirement, RaCO-DP still outperforms them. On CelebA with ResNet16, no prior DP method has been evaluated, so RaCO-DP is compared against a non-private SGDA reference, closing most of the accuracy gap (90% vs. 95%). RaCO-DP also uniquely allows practitioners to directly specify the maximum disparity γ via the Lagrangian formulation, whereas DP-FERMI requires indirect hyperparameter tuning to control the fairness level.

## Relation to Prior Work

RaCO-DP extends the SGDA primal-dual framework (Nemirovski et al., 2009) and DP-SGD's per-sample clipping/noise mechanism (Abadi et al., 2016) to handle rate constraints, which fundamentally break the per-sample decomposability that DP-SGD relies on. The key departure from prior DP fair learning work (Jagielski et al., 2019; Lowy et al., 2023) is the introduction of 'generalized rate constraints' expressed as linear combinations of prediction rates over a shared global partition, enabling a single private histogram per mini-batch to serve both primal and dual updates via post-processing. Compared to DP-FERMI (Lowy et al., 2023), which uses a fairness-specific proxy objective and weaker sensitive-attribute privacy, RaCO-DP provides full (ε,δ)-DP for arbitrary rate constraints and allows practitioners to directly specify constraint thresholds rather than tuning hyperparameters indirectly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first general DP framework for arbitrary rate-constrained optimization, expanding differential privacy's reach beyond fairness to robust optimization, cost-sensitive learning, and medical decision support. The finding that privacy-fairness trade-offs may be less severe than previously believed (challenging the fundamental incompatibility identified by Cummings et al., 2019) is notable, though the evidence is concentrated on tabular benchmarks and a single CNN experiment. Key limitations include gradient clipping bias that can push iterates outside the feasible set, and the restriction to rate constraints (individual fairness, which cannot be expressed this way, remains out of scope). Open questions include whether the private histogram approach extends to more complex constraint structures and whether the convergence guarantees tighten with better privacy accountants.
