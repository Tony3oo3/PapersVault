## Problem

Annealing-based methods such as Jarzynski equality (JE) and annealed importance sampling (AIS) are widely used for normalizing constant estimation, yet their quantitative complexity guarantees remain largely unexplored. Existing analyses are either asymptotic or rely on strong isoperimetric assumptions (e.g., log-concavity) that exclude the multimodal, non-log-concave targets where annealing is most needed. This paper establishes the first non-asymptotic oracle complexity bounds for JE and AIS without requiring log-concavity of the target distribution.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unnormalized density e^{-V} on R^d where V is β-smooth with a known stationary point at the origin, and produces an estimate Ẑ satisfying Pr(|Ẑ/Z − 1| ≤ ε) ≥ 3/4. Complexity is measured in gradient oracle calls and depends on dimension d, smoothness β, the action A of the chosen interpolation curve, and the target error ε. For the RDS-based variant, the method also produces samples from the target distribution, with the score ∇log π̄_t of intermediate OU-process marginals serving as the key intermediate quantity to estimate.

## Method

The core analysis leverages Girsanov's theorem to express the KL divergence between the sampling path measure and a reference path measure (whose marginals exactly follow the interpolation curve) in terms of the L² norm of a compensatory drift, which by optimal transport theory is minimized by the Wasserstein-2 metric derivative, yielding the action A = ∫|π̇|²_θ dθ as the governing quantity. For JE, this gives T = O(A/ε²) for the annealed Langevin dynamics; for AIS, a discretization analysis via the exponential integrator yields the oracle complexity Õ(dβ²A²/ε⁴). The paper then shows the geometric interpolation has exponentially large action for well-separated Gaussian mixtures (Prop. 1), explaining the mass-teleportation failure mode, and proposes using the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process as the interpolation curve instead, whose action is bounded by dβ + m² (Prop. 2), leading to an RDS-based algorithm whose complexity is governed by existing score-estimation guarantees for RDMC, RSDMC, ZODMC, and SNDMC.

## Results

On two multimodal 2D targets (Modified Müller Brown and 4-component Gaussian Mixture), all four RDS-based methods (RDMC, RSDMC, ZODMC, SNDMC) achieve near-accurate normalizing constant estimates (Ẑ/Z within ~0.02–0.09 of 1), while TI and AIS based on geometric interpolation produce severely biased estimates (e.g., AIS gives Ẑ/Z = 2.97±7.67 on MMB and 0.20±0.001 on GM). For sample quality on the GM target, SNDMC achieves the best MMD (0.158) and W₂ distance (1.55), substantially outperforming TI (MMD 2.54, W₂ 10.56) and AIS (MMD 2.46, W₂ 10.48). The theoretical contribution establishes the first non-asymptotic oracle complexity bound of Õ(dβ²A²/ε⁴) for AIS without log-concavity assumptions, and proves an exponential lower bound on the action of geometric interpolation for well-separated Gaussian mixtures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares TI (thermodynamic integration, Ge et al. 2020) and AIS (annealed importance sampling, Neal 2001) using geometric interpolation against four reverse diffusion samplers (RDMC, RSDMC, ZODMC, SNDMC) on multimodal targets. TI and AIS both fail catastrophically on multimodal distributions: on the 4-component Gaussian Mixture, TI estimates Ẑ/Z = 0.24 (76% underestimation) and AIS gives 0.20 (80% underestimation), reflecting severe mode-coverage failure. In contrast, all RDS methods achieve Ẑ/Z within 0.02–0.09 of the true value on GM, with RDMC (1.0001±0.0850) and SNDMC (0.9973±0.0834) being the most accurate. For sample quality, SNDMC dominates on both MMD (0.158 vs. 2.54 for TI) and W₂ distance (1.55 vs. 10.56 for TI) on the GM target, while ZODMC and RSDMC also substantially outperform TI and AIS. RSDMC shows high variance on MMB (Ẑ/Z = 1.29±12.78), suggesting it is less stable than the other RDS variants on that particular target. Overall, the RDS-based methods clearly win on both normalizing constant accuracy and sample quality for multimodal targets, while TI and AIS lose decisively due to the mass-teleportation problem quantified by the exponential action lower bound in Proposition 1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the action-based analysis of annealed sampling introduced by Guo et al. (2025) from the sampling task to normalizing constant estimation, leveraging the forward-backward SDE framework of Vargas et al. (2024) and Girsanov's theorem to connect the Jarzynski equality estimator's variance to the Wasserstein-2 action of the interpolation curve. It builds on the thermodynamic integration and AIS algorithms of Ge et al. (2020) and Brosse et al. (2018), but removes the log-concavity/isoperimetric assumptions that underpinned all prior non-asymptotic complexity guarantees. The proposed RDS-based NCE algorithm draws on the reverse diffusion Monte Carlo samplers of Huang et al. (2024a) and Vacher et al. (2025), repurposing them from sampling to partition function estimation via the OU process as an interpolation curve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first non-asymptotic oracle complexity bound for AIS and JE that does not require log-concavity or Poincaré/log-Sobolev inequalities, opening the door to rigorous analysis of NCE for general multimodal targets. The exponential lower bound on the action of geometric interpolation (Prop. 1) provides a clean quantitative explanation for the well-known mass teleportation failure mode, and motivates the OU-process-based alternative whose action is polynomially bounded (Prop. 2). Key open questions include the tightness of the upper bounds, whether the action-based framework extends to underdamped/Hamiltonian dynamics or discrete distributions, and whether the RDS-based NCE complexities (exponential in d for several variants) can be improved to polynomial scaling in high dimensions.
