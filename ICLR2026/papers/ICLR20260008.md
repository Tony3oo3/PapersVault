## Problem

Neural posterior estimation (NPE) faces a fundamental trade-off: simple variational families like Gaussians enable stable optimization but lack expressiveness for multimodal posteriors, while flexible families like normalizing flows and mixture density networks suffer from difficult optimization landscapes and convergence to shallow local optima. No existing variational family for NPE simultaneously offers high expressivity and provable convex optimization properties.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260008/cropped_page6_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an observation x (a scalar in synthetic benchmarks, or a multi-band 80×80 astronomical image in the redshift case study) and produces a variational posterior density q(z|x) over a low-dimensional latent space (1D or 2D). The log-normalizer is computed via numerical integration (trapezoidal sum on a 100×100 grid) or Monte Carlo, which is feasible precisely because NPE's automatic marginalization reduces the target to a few scientifically relevant parameters. Posterior samples are obtained via inverse transform sampling in low dimensions or Langevin dynamics in higher dimensions.

## Method

LBF-NPE parameterizes the log density of the variational distribution as a linear combination of K basis functions: log q(z;η) = log h(z) + ηᵀs_ψ(z) − C, placing the family within the exponential family. Two neural networks are trained jointly: an inference network f_φ(x) mapping observations to coefficient vectors η ∈ ℝ^K, and a basis network s_ψ(z) mapping latent variables to sufficient statistics. The NPE forward-KL objective is minimized using self-normalized importance sampling to estimate the log-normalizer gradient, with the key structural property that the objective is marginally convex in f (when s is fixed) and in s (when f is fixed), yielding global convergence guarantees via NTK theory. For fixed basis functions (B-splines, wavelets), optimization is fully convex in f; for adaptive basis functions, alternating optimization with stereographic projection reparameterization (constraining outputs to the unit hypersphere) resolves the rescaling identifiability inherent in the inner-product parameterization.

## Results

On three 2D posterior approximation benchmarks (Bands, Ring, Spiral), LBF-NPE with 20 adaptive basis functions achieves forward KL divergences of 0.0048, 0.0054, and 0.187 respectively, outperforming Neural Spline Flows (0.016, 0.017, 0.201), RealNVP (0.015, 0.024, 0.545), and Mixture Density Networks (0.182, 0.205, 0.948) by up to an order of magnitude. On the LSST DESC DC2 redshift estimation task over 153,000 objects, LBF-NPE with B-spline basis achieves a held-out NLL of −57,220, improving over NSF (−55,389) and MDN (−50,648). On the multimodal sinusoidal toy problem, LBF-NPE with fixed B-spline basis consistently converges to the global optimum across 20 random seeds, while MDN frequently converges to suboptimal local minima.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LBF-NPE outperforms all four NPE-based baselines (MDN, NSF, RealNVP) on every metric across all three 2D benchmarks, with the largest gaps against MDN (e.g., 0.187 vs. 0.948 FKL on Spiral, a 5× improvement) and moderate but consistent gains over normalizing flows (e.g., 0.0048 vs. 0.016 FKL on Bands, ~3× improvement). On the LSST DESC DC2 redshift estimation task, LBF-NPE with B-spline basis achieves a 1,831-point NLL improvement over NSF and a 6,572-point improvement over MDN on 153,000 held-out objects. Against EigenVI (Cai et al., 2024), LBF-NPE achieves better posterior approximations with fewer basis functions, attributed to the removal of orthogonality constraints and adaptive fitting of basis functions. The sinusoidal toy example demonstrates a qualitative advantage: LBF-NPE's convex objective ensures consistent convergence to the global optimum across 20 seeds, whereas MDN's nonconvex landscape leads to suboptimal local minima in a significant fraction of runs. The comparison with Pacchiardi & Dutta (2022) in Appendix E.6 shows LBF-NPE is the first to use neural exponential families for posterior representation in an amortized setting, whereas that prior work targets likelihood estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural Posterior Estimation (NPE) framework by replacing standard variational families (Gaussians, MDNs, normalizing flows) with a latent basis expansion parameterization of the log-density, yielding an exponential family of fixed dimension K. It builds directly on the global convergence theory for NPE with Gaussian families (McNamara et al., 2024a) by showing the same convexity and NTK-based convergence guarantees extend to any fixed basis, and departs from EigenVI (Cai et al., 2024) by introducing amortization, adaptive (non-orthogonal) basis functions, and the stereographic projection reparameterization to resolve identifiability. The connection to angular-distance optimization (cosine softmax losses) in Appendix D.7 positions the method within a broader family of normalized-output network objectives, though the probabilistic integral-based loss is distinct from the discrete softmax.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LBF-NPE offers a principled middle ground between the optimization simplicity of Gaussian variational families and the expressivity of normalizing flows, with provable convexity (and hence global convergence) when basis functions are fixed. The main practical limitation is sampling from the fitted density, which requires inverse transform sampling in low dimensions or MCMC in higher dimensions, constraining the method's applicability to low-dimensional posterior projections. Open questions include extending to high-dimensional targets with conditional independence structure, developing bilevel optimization for joint coefficient-basis learning, and understanding whether the convexity guarantees survive the stereographic reparameterization used in the adaptive setting.
