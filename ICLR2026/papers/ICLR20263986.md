## Problem

While BBP transitions in the Hessian spectrum at initialization are well understood for standard phase retrieval (p=p*=1), it remained unclear how overparameterization (student wider than teacher) shifts the transition threshold and whether it qualitatively changes the transition's nature. This paper addresses that gap by analyzing how the interplay between student width p, teacher width p*, and a loss-normalization parameter a determines both the location and the type (continuous vs. discontinuous) of the BBP transition in a generalized phase-retrieval teacher-student setup.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a teacher-student configuration with quadratic activations where the student has p hidden nodes and the teacher has p* nodes, trained on M=αN Gaussian-labeled examples with a normalization constant a>0 in the loss denominator. The Hessian is evaluated at random initialization (student weights drawn independently from the sphere), and the method produces the critical SNR threshold α_BBP, the spectral edge structure (sharp vs. smooth), the signal overlap m as a function of α, and a finite-N lower bound α₀ for information loss in the discontinuous regime. The key structural output is the classification of the transition as continuous (square-root spectral edge) or discontinuous (exponential spectral edge), which depends on the relative values of p, p*, and a.

## Method

The core technique is a field-theoretic (Feynman diagram) computation of the Stieltjes transform of the Hessian spectrum, using the replica trick and Wick's theorem to derive a self-consistent Dyson equation for the resolvent g(z). The Hessian is decomposed into a signal block (pp*×pp*) and a bulk block, and the bulk spectrum is obtained from a scalar self-consistent equation involving the eigenvalues of the per-sample F matrix, while the outlier eigenvalue λ* is determined by a separate singularity condition on the signal-block resolvent. The BBP threshold is found by equating λ* to the left spectral edge, whose nature (sharp or smooth) is determined by whether the inverse function z(g) has a vanishing derivative within its domain or hits a domain boundary. A key result is that in the p→∞ limit the transition is always discontinuous with threshold α_BBP = p*(a+1)/2, which at a=0 recovers the information-theoretic weak recovery threshold p*/2, showing that infinite overparameterization makes the Hessian at initialization as informative as the optimal spectral method.

## Results

The paper derives the BBP transition threshold α_BBP for the Hessian at initialization in a teacher-student soft-committee setup with quadratic activations. In the infinite overparameterization limit (p→∞), the threshold is α_BBP = p*(a+1)/2, which at a=0 reduces to p*/2 — matching the information-theoretic weak recovery threshold previously known only in Bayes-optimal settings. For finite p, overparameterization generally lowers α_BBP, but near a critical p_c(a) the transition changes from continuous to discontinuous, and α_BBP(p) can be weakly non-monotonic. For discontinuous transitions, strong finite-N effects cause the empirical signal-recovery threshold to lie well below the predicted α_BBP; a lower bound α_0 (where the BBP eigenvalue's overlap vanishes) is extracted and found to decrease monotonically with p, reinstating the practical advantage of overparameterization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing algorithms but instead compares its analytical predictions against prior theoretical results and finite-N simulations. Relative to the standard phase retrieval case (p=p*=1), overparameterization (p>p*) shifts the BBP threshold to lower SNR, meaning less data is needed for the Hessian to become informative. In the p→∞ limit with a=0, the spectral information in the Hessian at initialization achieves the same weak recovery threshold (p*/2) as the optimal spectral method of Mondelli & Montanari (2018) and the Bayes-optimal threshold of Maillard et al. (2024), despite the student not matching the teacher architecture. Compared to the averaged-Hessian spectral methods of Biroli et al. (2020), the actual (non-averaged) Hessian at a single random initialization captures the same transition structure, with overparameterization effectively performing an implicit average. Numerical simulations confirm the continuous BBP predictions closely, while for discontinuous transitions the finite-N empirical threshold is substantially lower than the N→∞ prediction, a gap attributed to the exponential (vs. square-root) spectral edge shape.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Hessian-spectrum analysis of Bonnaire et al. (2025) for the standard phase retrieval problem (p=p*=1) to a general overparameterized teacher-student setup with arbitrary widths p and p*, using field-theoretic diagrammatic techniques from Zee (1996) to compute the Stieltjes transform and outlier eigenvalue. It builds directly on the concept of discontinuous BBP transitions introduced by Bocchi et al. (2026a) and Potters & Bouchaud (2020), showing for the first time that overparameterization systematically drives the transition from continuous to discontinuous. The infinite-overparameterization limit is connected to the optimal spectral methods of Mondelli & Montanari (2018) and the Bayes-optimal weak recovery threshold of Maillard et al. (2024), revealing a surprising equivalence between a non-optimal student architecture and the information-theoretic limit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that infinite overparameterization causes the Hessian-at-initialization BBP threshold to converge to the information-theoretic weak recovery threshold (p*/2) is a striking demonstration of how overparameterization reshapes the loss landscape, with implications for understanding why overparameterized networks generalize well even from random initialization. The identification of strong finite-size corrections in discontinuous transitions (logarithmic in N) provides a practical explanation for why empirical signal recovery occurs below the large-N prediction, and introduces a new lower threshold α₀ that is monotonically decreasing in p. Key open questions include how overparameterization affects the gradient-flow algorithmic transition at threshold states (only preliminary results are provided), whether the qualitative picture extends to deeper networks, and how the interplay between the static BBP at initialization and the dynamical BBP at threshold states determines the full learning trajectory.
