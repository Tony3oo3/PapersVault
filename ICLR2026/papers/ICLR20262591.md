## Problem

Inference-time scaling of pre-trained diffusion models to new target distributions (e.g., annealed or reward-tilted) is critical for scientific applications, but existing approaches face a fundamental trade-off: guidance-based methods are simple yet introduce uncontrolled bias, while particle-based corrections (Sequential Monte Carlo) are unbiased but suffer from severe weight degeneracy that collapses the effective sample size. This paper addresses the gap by asking whether one can actively steer the particle dynamics on the fly to prevent weight collapse without retraining or learning additional control networks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained diffusion or flow-matching model (providing the forward drift and a neural-network score approximation) together with either an annealing factor γ or a twice-differentiable reward function r(x) with analytic gradients (typically a physics-based energy). It produces an ensemble of N weighted particles approximating the target distribution q_T ∝ p_0^γ exp(r(x)); in the protein-ligand co-folding setting, the input is a protein sequence plus ligand identity and the output is 3D complex structures steered toward physical validity. The per-step computational overhead is limited to evaluating a few basis functions and solving a small n×n linear system (n ≤ 3 in practice), with no backpropagation through the base model.

## Method

DriftLite exploits a fundamental degree of freedom in the Feynman-Kac-type Fokker-Planck equation: any control drift b_t can be added to the particle dynamics as long as it is compensated by a control potential h_t(x; b_t) = (γ∇log p̄_t + ∇r_t)·b_t + ∇·b_t, which has zero expectation under the target density. The key insight is that choosing b_t to minimize the variance of the residual potential φ_t = g_t + h_t directly prevents the weight degeneracy that plagues standard Guidance-SMC. Two training-free instantiations are derived: Variance-Controlling Guidance (VCG), which minimizes Var[φ_t] via a least-squares solve, and Energy-Controlling Guidance (ECG), which variationally solves the corresponding Poisson equation via the Ritz method. Both restrict the control drift to a low-dimensional subspace spanned by locally available vector fields (reward gradient, score, forward drift), reducing the intractable PDE to a small n×n linear system solved at each time step. The resulting controlled SMC (VCG-SMC/ECG-SMC) or resampling-free variants (VCG/ECG) are applied entirely on the fly during inference, requiring no parameter updates or additional training.

## Results

On the challenging LJ-13 annealing task (γ=2.5), VCG-SMC achieves ΔNLL of −0.699±0.189, MMD of 0.102±0.050, and W₁^RDF of 0.002±0.000, substantially outperforming G-SMC (ΔNLL 13.64±9.949, MMD 0.616±0.012) and Pure Guidance (ΔNLL 13.58±16.73, MMD 0.603±0.095). On protein-ligand co-folding (Boltz-2 base), VCG-SMC improves the Valid Fraction from 0.374 (Base) to 0.856 and reduces Bond Length violations from 55.00 to 24.33, while also halving Chiral Atom errors compared to G-SMC (1.00 vs. 2.33). The method reduces reweighting potential variance by several orders of magnitude, stabilizing the Effective Sample Size throughout inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VCG-SMC dominates all baselines across every metric on both DW-4 and LJ-13 particle systems. On LJ-13 annealing, it reduces MMD from 0.603 (PG) and 0.616 (G-SMC) to 0.102, and W₁^RDF from 0.037/0.040 to 0.002, indicating near-perfect recovery of the radial distribution function. The non-SMC variant VCG improves over baselines on ΔNLL and MMD but degrades on W₁^E (22.86 vs. 0.286 for VCG-SMC), confirming that resampling is critical for the most challenging settings. On protein-ligand co-folding, VCG-SMC outperforms G-SMC on Bond Length (24.33 vs. 42.33), Bond Angle (61.00 vs. 98.00), and Chiral Atom errors (1.00 vs. 2.33), while both dramatically surpass the unsteered Boltz-2 base (Valid Fraction 0.856 vs. 0.374). The Feynman-Kac Steering (FKS) baseline performs only marginally better than the base model, suggesting that naive steering without variance control is insufficient for physical validity.

## Relation to Prior Work

DriftLite extends the Feynman-Kac corrector framework for diffusion models (Skreta et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025), which corrects the bias of pure guidance via weighted Sequential Monte Carlo particles, by identifying a degree of freedom in the governing Fokker-Planck equation that allows trading drift for reweighting potential. This connects directly to the twisted-proposal / controlled-SMC literature (Heng et al., 2020; Whiteley & Lee, 2014), which the authors explicitly cite as the theoretical ancestor, but reformulates it in the diffusion-model setting with a variance-minimization objective. The key departure from prior particle-based methods is the proactive, on-the-fly drift control (solving a small n×n linear system per step) rather than passive reweighting, and the departure from learning-based control (Vargas et al., 2023b; Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden, 2024) is the training-free, closed-form nature of the solution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DriftLite addresses the weight-degeneracy bottleneck that has limited the practical adoption of unbiased particle-based sampling for diffusion models, offering a training-free fix with only modest per-step overhead (a small linear solve). The protein-ligand co-folding results on Boltz-2 demonstrate tangible impact in structural biology, where physical validity of generated structures is critical and prior guidance-based methods produce chemically invalid conformations. Key limitations include the reliance on a fixed set of 2–3 linear basis functions (restricting expressiveness), the assumption of twice-differentiable rewards, and the current focus on continuous-state systems. Open directions include adaptive or neural control drifts, extension to less smooth rewards, discrete diffusion settings, and scaling to larger molecular systems (LJ-55, ALDP).
