## Problem

FSDP's per-layer all-gather and reduce-scatter collectives impose fine-grained synchronization barriers that force all devices to advance at the pace of the slowest one. In LLM post-training, where sequence lengths vary widely and attention cost scales quadratically, this creates severe workload imbalance and up to 50% device idle time, a problem that existing packing strategies cannot fully eliminate under memory constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264762/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes variable-length text sequences (ranging from short to 64K tokens) and sharded model parameters/optimizer states distributed across GPUs, producing the same synchronous SGD updates as standard FSDP at each minibatch boundary. A key operational detail is that ODC allows each device to process a different number of microbatches within a minibatch, breaking the conventional constraint that all devices must execute an identical count of microbatches per step.

## Method

ODC replaces FSDP's per-layer collective operations with on-demand point-to-point RDMA primitives: all-gather is decomposed into targeted gather requests where a device pulls only the parameter shards it needs, and reduce-scatter is decomposed into scatter-accumulate pushes where a device sends gradients directly to the owning shard. This reframes FSDP as a decentralized parameter server with colocated server and worker roles, relaxing synchronization from the layer level to the minibatch level while preserving FSDP's memory layout and synchronous optimization semantics. The implementation uses CUDA IPC for intra-node transfers and NVSHMEM (via Triton-Distributed) for inter-node, with a lightweight polling daemon handling gradient accumulation without occupying GPU SMs. The decoupled execution enables a simplified minibatch-level load balancing strategy (LB-Mini) using Karmarkar-Karp partitioning, where devices independently pack their local samples into microbatches governed only by local memory constraints, achieving up to 36% throughput speedup over standard FSDP.

## Results

On SFT tasks (LongAlign and SWE-Smith), ODC with LB-Mini achieves up to 36% throughput improvement over standard FSDP (Collective LB-Micro), with the largest gain observed on the 14B model on LongAlign at minibatch size 4 (61.4 vs 45.1 samples/s/device). On RL (AIME prompts with GRPO), ODC achieves up to 10% speedup over the collective baseline, with gains less pronounced due to a less long-tailed sequence length distribution. Bubble rates (device idle time from workload imbalance) drop from 37.75% to 14.43% (14B, LongAlign, Minibs=4) and from 55.19% to 50.91% (32B, LongAlign, Minibs=2) when switching from Collective to ODC LB-Mini.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard FSDP with collective all-gather/reduce-scatter (the 'Collective' baseline, Zhao et al. 2023), ODC wins consistently across all model sizes (1.5B–32B) and both SFT datasets, with speedups ranging from 6–36% depending on model size, dataset, and minibatch size; the gap is largest at moderate minibatch sizes (4) where collective synchronization barriers are most costly. Against the LocalSort strategy adapted from Bai et al. (2024), ODC shows modest gains (4–12%) because LocalSort does not pack sequences, leaving substantial per-microbatch variance that ODC's decoupled execution can absorb. Against the verl Native two-level partitioning (Sheng et al., 2025) in RL, the paper's LB-Micro packing heuristic is substantially faster (e.g., 716.4 vs 549.9 samples/s for 1.5B at Minibs=4), establishing it as a strong baseline before comparing ODC on top. ODC with LB-Mini outperforms ODC with LB-Micro at small minibatch sizes (where fewer microbatches per device limit packing flexibility) but the gap narrows at larger minibatch sizes where LB-Micro has more room to balance within the uniform-microbatch constraint.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits the Parameter Server (PS) architecture (Dean et al., 2012; Li et al., 2014), which was largely superseded by collective communication (NCCL, Horovod, Baidu AllReduce) in the era of homogeneous GPU clusters, and repositions it as a superior fit for LLM post-training where workload imbalance is inherent. It builds directly on FSDP's sharding mechanism (Zhao et al., 2023) and the Verl RL framework (Sheng et al., 2025), replacing per-layer all-gather/reduce-scatter collectives with point-to-point RDMA operations (NVSHMEM, CUDA-IPC) to create a 'decentralized PS' that preserves FSDP's memory layout. The load-balancing component extends sequence packing strategies (Krell et al., 2021; Bai et al., 2024) by shifting the balancing objective from microbatch to minibatch granularity, enabled by the removal of per-layer synchronization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the long-standing assumption that collective communication is universally superior in modern GPU clusters breaks down under the imbalanced workloads characteristic of LLM post-training, with device idle times reaching 50% under standard FSDP. ODC's up to 36% speedup and near-zero bubble rates at larger minibatch sizes make it a practically impactful, open-sourced alternative for SFT and RL pipelines. Key limitations include significantly slower inter-node communication compared to hierarchical collectives (mitigated by hybrid sharding and compute-communication overlap) and the requirement for RDMA infrastructure. Open directions include relaxing synchronous semantics toward bounded-staleness updates, integrating elasticity and fault tolerance, and optimizing the point-to-point communication graph with topology-aware caching.
