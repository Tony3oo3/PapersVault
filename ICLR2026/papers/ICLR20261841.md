## Problem

Personalized LLM memory retrieval is currently reduced to either feeding the entire user history into the prompt (costly and unscalable) or performing a one-shot dense similarity search that captures only surface-level matches. Existing systems lack both a deliberate recollection retrieval path for ambiguous or context-heavy queries and a mechanism to adaptively switch between fast recognition and slow reconstruction, leading to either under-retrieval of deeper contextual cues or over-retrieval that introduces noise and latency.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user query (encoded via a sentence embedding model such as MiniLM, MPNet, or BGE) and a corpus of user memory fragments (dialogue turns or session-level summaries, also encoded as unit-normalized embedding vectors). It outputs a top-K set of retrieved memory fragments whose text is prepended as dialogue history to an LLM prompt for personalized response generation. The retrieval is explicitly bounded by beam width B, fanout F, and maximum rounds R, keeping computation controllable; the familiarity signal (mean cosine similarity and entropy over the probe top-K) determines which path is taken without any additional model inference.

## Method

RF-Mem grounds personalized memory retrieval in the Recollection-Familiarity dual-process theory from cognitive science. A probe retrieval first computes cosine similarities between the query embedding and all memory fragments; the mean score and softmax-normalized entropy of the top-K probe scores jointly determine the routing: high mean score triggers the Familiarity path (direct top-K return), low mean score triggers the Recollection path, and intermediate scores are disambiguated by an entropy threshold τ. In the Recollection path, candidate memories are clustered via KMeans into B branches, each centroid is blended with the current query through an α-mixing formula (preserving the original query via a residual term), and the resulting recollect-queries drive the next round of retrieval in a retrieve-cluster-mix loop. The process is bounded by beam width, fanout, and a maximum round count R, and the final evidence set is the truncated union of all rounds' candidates, achieving chain-like evidence reconstruction purely in embedding space without any LLM calls during retrieval.

## Results

On PersonaMem, RF-Mem achieves the best overall accuracy at all three memory scales: 0.6350 at 32K, 0.5394 at 128K, and 0.4589 at 1M tokens, while keeping average input to ~3.5–3.8K tokens and retrieval latency under 7ms. On PersonaBench (Recall@10, MiniLM), RF-Mem reaches 0.6071 overall, surpassing both Familiarity (0.5964) and Recollection (0.6062). On LongMemEval-S (Recall@5, BGE), RF-Mem achieves 0.8186 vs. 0.7924 (Familiarity) and 0.8162 (Recollection), with lower latency (37ms vs. 44ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Zero Memory and Full Context baselines from Jiang et al. (COLM 2025), RF-Mem outperforms Full Context by +0.0221 at 32K (0.6350 vs. 0.6129) while using only 3.6K tokens versus 24.7K; at 128K the gap widens dramatically (0.5394 vs. 0.3231), and at 1M Full Context is entirely out-of-context while RF-Mem still achieves 0.4589. Against the Dense Retrieval (Familiarity) baseline following Pan et al. (ICLR 2025) and Wu et al. (ICLR 2025), RF-Mem wins at every scale: +0.0442 at 32K, +0.0135 at 128K, and +0.0071 at 1M on PersonaMem, and consistently leads on Recall@5/10 across all three retriever backbones on both PersonaBench and LongMemEval. The always-on Recollection path (the paper's own ablation) is slightly stronger than RF-Mem on some sub-categories but incurs 30–40% higher latency; RF-Mem's adaptive gating recovers most of Recollection's accuracy gains while staying closer to Familiarity's speed (e.g., 5.09ms vs. 7.09ms at 32K). On LongMemEval-M, RF-Mem matches or exceeds Recollection on Recall@50 (e.g., 0.7613 vs. 0.7590 under MiniLM) while cutting latency by ~25%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Recollection-Familiarity Dual-Process Theory from cognitive science (Henson et al., 1999; Yonelinas, 2024) as a design principle for personalized memory retrieval in LLMs, where prior work has uniformly treated retrieval as a one-shot dense similarity search (the 'Familiarity' channel). It departs from the dense-retrieval lineage (DPR, Contriever, MiniLM, BGE) and memory-indexing systems (MemoryBank, SeCom, Mem0, Zep) by introducing a second, iterative 'Recollection' path built on KMeans clustering and α-mixing of query-centroid embeddings, gated by an entropy-based uncertainty signal. The paper positions itself as complementary to query reformulation (HyDE, MemoCue) and iterative RAG (Search-o1) rather than competing with them, demonstrating modularity through adaptive experiments that layer RF-Mem on top of external indexes and expansion pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RF-Mem offers a lightweight, principled mechanism for adaptive retrieval depth in personalized LLMs, achieving modest but consistent gains (1–2 points) over one-shot dense retrieval while keeping latency near the single-pass baseline. The cognitive-science grounding provides a theoretically motivated stopping criterion (entropy threshold) that avoids the over-retrieval noise of always-on iterative methods. Key limitations include hand-tuned thresholds (though a learned gate is shown to approach their performance at scale), the demonstrated risk of recollection-path drift away from user intent (case study D.7.2), and the fact that gains shrink as corpus size grows. Open questions include whether the dual-process analogy extends beyond embedding-space clustering to graph- or temporal-structured memories, and whether the entropy signal generalizes to multilingual or multimodal user histories.
