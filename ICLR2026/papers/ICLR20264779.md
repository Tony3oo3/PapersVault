## Problem

Existing 4D video generation methods either require large-scale multi-view training data, accept only image/text inputs (not user-provided videos), or produce temporally inconsistent results under large camera motions. Critically, off-the-shelf video inpainting models cannot directly fill the occlusion masks produced by point-cloud rendering because such masks fall outside their training distribution, and prior fitting-based approaches do not support prompt-based content editing during 4D generation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular video (81 frames at 512×512), a user-specified camera trajectory as a sequence of extrinsic matrices, an optionally edited first frame (produced by MagicQuill with SAM2 segmentation), and a text prompt (~15 words). It outputs a 4D novel-view video with multi-view consistency, supporting both camera retargeting (zoom, tilt, pan) and prompt-driven content editing (e.g., adding objects). Depth is estimated per-frame via DepthCrafter with empirically configured intrinsics, and the rendered point cloud produces binary visibility masks that define the inpainting regions.

## Method

EasyCreator reformulates 4D video creation as a specialized video inpainting task on the Wan2.1-14B foundation model, fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 128) with a flow-matching loss. A double-reprojection strategy back-projects rendered views into a new point cloud and re-renders with the inverse transformation, yielding supervision masks spatially aligned with the original frames; these are combined with randomly generated editing masks into a composite mask dataset (three types: point-cloud, editing, union) sampled per training instance. To handle large camera motions (>40°), a self-iterative tuning scheme progressively increases viewing angles in 10° increments, where each iteration's generated video becomes training data for the next stage. At inference, a temporal-packing strategy selects the top-K frames (by inpainting area) from a previously generated trajectory and concatenates their VAE-encoded tokens along the temporal dimension with the new trajectory's hole video, leveraging the model's global spatio-temporal self-attention to enforce multi-view consistency without additional fusion layers.

## Results

On the primary evaluation (Table 2), EasyCreator achieves FID 58.26, FVD 145.71, and CLIP-T 35.63 for visual quality, with rotation error 1.37° and translation error 4.47 for camera accuracy, and FVD-V 119.52 / CLIP-V 89.87 for view synchronization — all best among the five compared methods. On Kubric-4D (Table 3), it reaches PSNR 22.15 and LPIPS 0.381, substantially outperforming the next-best TrajectoryCrafter (PSNR 15.82, LPIPS 0.532). On VBench (Table 1), EasyCreator scores 0.9026 subject consistency and 0.9242 motion smoothness, versus 0.8632 and 0.8815 for TrajectoryCrafter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EasyCreator outperforms all seven baselines across every reported metric. Against the strongest tuning-based baseline TrajectoryCrafter, it improves FID by 3.31 (61.57→58.26), FVD by 8.52 (154.23→145.71), and translation error by 1.05 (5.52→4.47), while also achieving the best view-synchronization scores (FVD-V 119.52 vs. 148.71). ReCamMaster is a mid-tier competitor (FID 64.82, FVD 162.91) that EasyCreator surpasses by a wider margin. GCD and Trajectory-Attention are clearly weaker, with GCD exhibiting the worst FVD (368.44) and FVD-V (364.28), indicating severe temporal and multi-view inconsistency. In the fair-comparison setting (Appendix E.1) where ReCapture and Reangle-A-Video are re-implemented on the same WAN-2.1 backbone, EasyCreator still leads on all metrics (e.g., FID 58.26 vs. 63.19 for Reangle-A-Video). For multi-view generation (Appendix E.5), EasyCreator edges out SynCammaster on FID (58.26 vs. 65.23) and FVD-V (119.52 vs. 122.14), confirming the benefit of the temporal-packing inference strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the two-stage paradigm of depth-based point cloud rendering followed by inpainting (Jeong et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2024a), but departs from prior work by leveraging a dedicated video inpainting foundation model (Wan2.1) rather than fine-tuning general-purpose text-to-video models for the inpainting step. It also extends the task beyond pure camera retargeting by introducing composite masks that unify occlusion completion with prompt-based content editing, and adds self-iterative tuning (progressively increasing viewpoint angles) and temporal-packing inference to handle large camera motions and multi-view consistency—capabilities absent in fitting-based baselines like ReCapture and Reangle-a-video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, low-cost path to 4D video creation with editing from a single monocular video (~2 hours LoRA training on one A800), which is significantly cheaper than large-scale dataset fine-tuning used by ReCamMaster and TrajectoryCrafter. However, as a fitting-based method it optimizes per-video, inherits limitations from the depth estimator and first-frame editing tool, and the 2-hour training time per video remains a practical bottleneck. Open questions include whether the self-iterative tuning strategy generalizes to truly free-form camera paths, whether the approach scales to longer sequences beyond 81 frames, and whether the reliance on a single base inpainting model limits robustness to diverse scene types.
