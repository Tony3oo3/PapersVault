## Problem

Existing learned feature upsampling methods (FeatUp, LoftUp, JAFAR) are encoder-specific: they must be retrained for every vision backbone and feature dimensionality, making them infeasible to apply to new or large models at inference time. AnyUp addresses this by learning a single upsampler that generalizes to any feature type, resolution, and channel count without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264772/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution feature map of arbitrary spatial size h×w and arbitrary channel dimensionality c (from any vision encoder such as DINOv2, CLIP, SigLIP, or ResNet) together with a high-resolution RGB guidance image of size H×W×3, and produces a high-resolution feature map of size H×W×c at any target resolution. The feature-agnostic layer handles the varying channel count N by processing each channel independently and aggregating through a fixed-size kernel basis, so no per-encoder adaptation is needed. Upsampling from any to any resolution is supported natively via the window attention mechanism.

## Method

AnyUp builds on JAFAR's single high-resolution-to-low-resolution attention architecture but introduces three key modifications. First, a feature-agnostic convolution layer replaces the fixed-dimension input processing: each input channel is convolved independently with a learned basis of M=128 kernels, a softmax is applied across the basis dimension, and results are averaged over all channels, yielding outputs invariant to input dimensionality. Second, global attention is replaced by local window attention (window size σ=0.2 relative to input), which restricts each pixel query to nearby feature patches, reducing computation by over 50% and preventing attention to distant unrelated regions. Third, a crop-based training strategy supervises the upsampler only on randomly sampled local image regions (comparing features from the full image upsampled to the crop region against features computed directly on the crop), combined with self-consistency regularization under data augmentation and input-consistency regularization to preserve the original feature space.

## Results

On semantic segmentation with DINOv2 ViT-S features, AnyUp achieves 62.16 mIoU on COCO-Stuff, 84.00 mIoU on PASCAL-VOC, and 42.43 mIoU on ADE20k, setting a new state of the art across all three benchmarks. For geometric tasks on NYUv2, AnyUp achieves the best surface normal RMSE of 31.17° and the best absolute depth RMSE of 0.4755, outperforming all prior upsamplers. AnyUp also best preserves the input feature distribution, achieving 40.83 mIoU on ADE20k with a pre-trained low-resolution probe (vs. 4.27 for LoftUp), confirming that upsampled features remain in the original feature space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnyUp outperforms FeatUp on all three segmentation benchmarks (e.g., +0.21 mIoU on COCO, +0.61 on PASCAL-VOC, +0.24 on ADE20k) and on all depth/normal estimation metrics (e.g., surface normal RMSE 31.17° vs. 32.69°). Against LoftUp, AnyUp wins on COCO (+0.01 mIoU), PASCAL-VOC (+0.31), ADE20k (+0.41), and all geometric tasks, with LoftUp performing particularly poorly on surface normals (33.94° RMSE) due to its object-mask-based training objective causing excessive per-object smoothing. Against JAFAR, AnyUp wins on COCO (+0.34 mIoU), ADE20k (+0.37), and all depth/normal metrics, but JAFAR retains a slight edge on PASCAL-VOC (84.36 vs. 84.00 mIoU). Compared to the training-free Guided Filter baseline, AnyUp substantially improves feature space preservation (40.83 vs. 37.54 mIoU on ADE20k with pre-trained probes). The critical differentiator is that AnyUp is the only learned method that generalizes to unseen feature extractors at test time without retraining, whereas FeatUp, LoftUp, and JAFAR must be retrained per encoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

AnyUp builds directly on the attention-based upsampling formulation introduced by JAFAR and LoftUp, both of which cast feature upsampling as a high-resolution-to-low-resolution attention operation. It departs from these concurrent works by replacing the encoder-specific initial convolution with a feature-agnostic layer that processes channels independently through a learned kernel basis, making the upsampler applicable to any feature dimensionality without retraining. The window attention mechanism draws on local attention ideas (Ramachandran et al., 2019) to restrict queries to local neighborhoods, and the crop-based training strategy is a novel departure from prior approaches that either train at fixed low resolutions (JAFAR) or require expensive high-resolution supervision (LoftUp).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: as new vision foundation models (DINOv3, SigLIP2, etc.) continue to emerge, AnyUp eliminates the need to retrain an upsampler for each new encoder, providing a drop-in solution for dense prediction tasks. The strong feature-space preservation (linear probes transfer without finetuning) is critical for downstream applications that rely on pre-trained probes. Key limitations include the assumption that upsampled features are linear combinations of input patch features, which may underutilize sub-patch spatial information encoded in high-dimensional channels, and the single-attention-layer design that trades expressiveness for efficiency. An open question is whether multi-backbone training can fully close the small gap to encoder-specific upsamplers without the positional-encoding overfitting observed here.
