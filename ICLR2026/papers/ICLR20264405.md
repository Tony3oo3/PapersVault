## Problem

Evaluating whether video diffusion models have internalised intuitive physics is difficult because existing approaches—VLM-based plausibility judges, pixel-reconstruction proxies, and text-conditioned compliance measures—conflate physics correctness with visual appearance, introduce subjective interpretive variance, or require image conditioning unavailable in text-to-video settings. No training-free, model-agnostic metric exists that isolates physics understanding from aesthetic or stylistic quality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired synthetic videos (512×512, 60 frames) rendered in Blender where valid and invalid variants differ only in a single controlled physics violation while sharing identical camera, lighting, texture, and geometry. Each video is corrupted with Gaussian noise at 10 uniformly sampled DDIM timesteps and passed through the pretrained denoising network with a fixed text prompt per scenario. The output is a scalar Plausibility Preference Error (PPE) per scenario—the fraction of valid-invalid pairs where the model assigns no lower denoising loss to the valid sample—aggregated into a per-model ranking across 12 scenarios in 4 physics domains.

## Method

LikePhys formalises intuitive physics understanding as a likelihood-preference property: a model with correct physics should assign higher probability (lower ELBO-based denoising loss) to physically valid videos than to visually matched invalid ones. It leverages the density-estimation capability of diffusion models (Li et al., 2023) by using the noise-prediction MSE as a likelihood surrogate, requiring no additional training or fine-tuning. The evaluation benchmark consists of 12 scenarios across Rigid Body, Continuum, Fluid Mechanics, and Optical Effects, each with 10 controlled variations where valid and invalid videos are rendered with identical visual parameters so that any likelihood gap is attributable solely to physics violations. PPE is computed as the average over all variations of the fraction of valid-invalid pairs where the model's denoising loss for the valid sample is ≥ that of the invalid sample. The protocol is model-agnostic (works with UNet and DiT backbones alike), training-free, and appearance-agnostic, as verified by near-zero correlation with VBench aesthetic and consistency metrics.

## Results

Across 12 video diffusion models benchmarked on 12 physics scenarios, the top performers are Hunyuan T2V (43.6% avg PPE), Wan2.1-T2V-14B (43.8%), and CogVideoX1.5-5B (43.8%), while early UNet-based models like AnimateDiff (60.8%) and AnimateDiff SDXL (56.0%) perform near or above the 50% random-guess threshold. LikePhys achieves the strongest overall Kendall's τ correlation with human preference (44.4) among all evaluators, outperforming VideoPhy (38.9), Qwen2.5 VL (33.3), and VideoPhy2 (−8.5). PPE is largely orthogonal to visual quality: Pearson correlations with VBench metrics range from −0.05 (aesthetic quality) to 0.15 (motion smoothness), confirming it captures physics rather than appearance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LikePhys outperforms all three VLM-based baselines on overall Kendall's τ correlation with human preference: 44.4 vs. VideoPhy (38.9), Qwen2.5 VL 7B-Instruct (33.3), and VideoPhy2 (−8.5). The advantage is most pronounced over VideoPhy2, whose overall correlation is negative, indicating its model ranking is essentially anti-correlated with human judgments. LikePhys also shows more consistent per-scenario performance, with positive τ in 10 of 12 scenarios, whereas VideoPhy2 is negative in 7 of 12 and Qwen2.5 VL is negative in 4. Importantly, LikePhys is training-free and zero-shot, requiring no fine-tuned VLM judge or model-generated videos, while VideoPhy and VideoPhy2 rely on human-annotated synthetic videos to train their evaluators. The paper further demonstrates that PPE is orthogonal to visual quality metrics (|r| ≤ 0.15 with all VBench scores), whereas VLM-based evaluators are susceptible to appearance biases and interpretive variance across prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

LikePhys repurposes the violation-of-expectation paradigm from cognitive science (Spelke, 1985; Riochet et al., 2018) and the density-estimation capability of diffusion models (Li et al., 2023) to create a likelihood-preference evaluation for video diffusion models. It departs from prior physics-evaluation approaches for video generation—VLM-based judges (VideoPhy, VideoPhy2, Qwen2.5-VL) and pixel/object-mask comparisons (Physics-IQ, Morpheus)—by avoiding both the confound of visual appearance and the need for image-conditioned generation. The core technical move is treating the denoising loss as an ELBO-based likelihood surrogate and performing pairwise valid-vs-invalid comparisons on controlled synthetic pairs, making the metric model-agnostic and training-free.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, training-free metric (PPE) that cleanly disentangles physics understanding from visual quality, confirmed by near-zero correlation with VBench aesthetic and consistency scores. A key finding is that even the strongest models (Hunyuan T2V, Wan2.1-14B, CogVideoX1.5-5B) achieve only ~43–44% PPE—barely above the 50% random-guess threshold—quantifying how far current VDMs are from reliable physics. Limitations include the need for access to noise-prediction internals (ruling out closed-source models) and the cost of curating controlled synthetic pairs. Open questions include whether physics-aware training objectives, longer context windows, or multiscale memory can close the gap, and whether the metric generalizes beyond the 12 curated scenarios.
