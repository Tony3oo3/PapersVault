## Problem

Dynamical richness (non-linear feature transformation in the rich regime) does not always align with predictive performance, yet accuracy is commonly used as a proxy for richness, conflating two distinct phenomena. Existing metrics for measuring rich dynamics either depend on the initial kernel, parameter norms, or class labels, and NTK-based measures are computationally infeasible for moderate vision models. The paper addresses the absence of a lightweight, performance-independent, and label-free metric that can serve as a practical diagnostic tool for studying the relationship between training factors, dynamics, and representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes penultimate-layer activations (an n×p matrix of feature vectors) and last-layer outputs (an n×C matrix of learned function values) from a trained network, with the target function optionally provided for the quality visualization curve. It outputs a scalar D_LR in [0,1] (lower indicates richer dynamics) plus three eigendecomposition-based curves: cumulative target quality Π*(k), cumulative self-utilization Π̂(k), and the relative eigenvalue spectrum ρ_k/ρ_1. The full computation requires only O(p²C) operations with n≈O(p) samples, making it orders of magnitude cheaper than NTK-based alternatives that scale quadratically with total parameter count.

## Method

The core contribution is the Minimum Projection operator T_MP, which projects any function in L²(𝒳) onto the learned function space (the C-dimensional span of the network's output entries), formalizing the low-rank bias that characterizes rich dynamics. D_LR is defined as 1 minus the centered kernel alignment (CKA) between the feature kernel operator T (rank p, constructed from penultimate-layer features) and T_MP (rank C, constructed from the learned function), so D_LR=0 corresponds to perfect low-rank alignment where only the minimal C features are active. The paper proves that when T equals T_MP, the neural collapse conditions NC1 (within-class collapse) and NC2 (simplex ETF structure) necessarily hold, positioning the metric as a generalization of neural collapse to label-free, test-set, and regression settings. For interpretability, the feature kernel is eigendecomposed via the Nyström method, yielding three complementary visualization curves that decompose the D_LR deviation into how many features are used (utilization), how well they express the target (quality), and their relative magnitudes (intensity). The metric is normalized to [0,1], requires no class labels, no reference to the initial kernel, and no accuracy signal, making it an independent diagnostic for dynamical richness.

## Results

The proposed D_LR metric (lower = richer) correctly identifies the absence of dynamical richness under weight decay (0.59 → 1.0 over 200 epochs) where all three baselines misinterpret the dynamics as rich. On target downscaling (α = 0.2, 2, 20), D_LR monotonically increases (4.9×10⁻², 1.1×10⁻¹, 5.6×10⁻¹) matching the expected laziness ordering, while S_init, ‖θ‖²_F, and NC1 all misalign. Across practical setups (Table 3), D_LR captures the grokking transition on Mod97 (0.51 → 0.11), confirms that optimal learning rate (0.05) and weight decay (10⁻³) yield the richest dynamics on CIFAR-100/10, and reveals that batch normalization shifts VGG-16 on CIFAR-100 from lazy (D_LR = 0.66) to rich (D_LR = 0.073) with a 50-point test accuracy gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares D_LR against three existing richness metrics: kernel deviation S_init (Yang & Hu, 2021), parameter norm ‖θ‖²_F (Lyu et al., 2024), and neural collapse separation NC1 (Papyan et al., 2020). On the weight decay experiment (Table 1), all three baselines incorrectly indicate increasing richness (S_init: 1.0→0.20; ‖θ‖²_F: 3.1×10³→2.2×10⁻⁵; NC1: 1.2×10⁵→7.5×10⁻¹⁴) when the dynamics are dominated by L² decay with negligible learning, while D_LR correctly reports decreasing richness (0.59→1.0). On target downscaling (Table 2), D_LR is the only metric that monotonically increases with α (the laziness parameter): S_init is non-monotonic (6.8×10⁻², 4.1×10⁻², 5.2×10⁻²), ‖θ‖²_F is nearly flat (3.4×10³, 3.2×10³, 3.2×10³), and NC1 decreases with α (2.3×10⁴, 3.2×10³, 8.1×10²) — the opposite of the expected trend. D_LR thus wins on both comparison settings, demonstrating that its independence from the initial kernel, parameter norms, and class labels makes it a more robust and interpretable measure of dynamical richness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the rich-versus-lazy training framework (Chizat et al., 2019) and the well-established low-rank bias of gradient dynamics in layerwise models (Saxe et al., 2014; Mixon et al., 2020), which underpin phenomena like neural collapse and grokking. It departs from prior richness metrics (NTK deviation, parameter norm, NC1) by proposing a function-space, performance-independent measure grounded in the alignment between the feature kernel operator and a minimum projection operator, showing that neural collapse emerges as a special case. The use of CKA as the alignment tool is borrowed from representation similarity literature (Kornblith et al., 2019), but the specific construction of the MP-operator and its connection to low-rank bias is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, computationally cheap diagnostic tool that decouples dynamical richness from accuracy, enabling cleaner study of how training factors (learning rate, batch normalization, architecture) shape feature dynamics independently of task performance. Two new empirical observations stand out: batch normalization shifts VGG-16 on CIFAR-100 from lazy to rich dynamics, and feature quality correlates with feature intensity during training (contrasting with the silent alignment effect). Key limitations include restriction to orthogonal/isotropic target functions (balanced classification or scalar regression), focus solely on the last layer, and the open question of extending the framework to intermediate layers and imbalanced tasks.
