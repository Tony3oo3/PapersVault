## Problem

Existing face research is fragmented into separate understanding (MLLM-based) and generation (diffusion-based) pipelines, with no unified framework handling both tasks simultaneously. Moreover, both tasks suffer from a pervasive lack of fine-grained facial attribute processing: discrete diffusion models rely solely on masked token prediction without score matching, detailed attribute representations are discarded during representation evolution, and available face-text datasets contain only 2–7 attributes per caption with no VQA pairs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264897/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, the model consumes a detailed text caption (averaging 120 tokens, ~17.7 facial attributes) and produces a 256×256 face image via discrete token decoding from MAGVIT-v2 tokens. For understanding, it takes a face image plus a text query (captioning prompt or VQA question) and outputs a natural-language response. The training data comprises 120K face images each paired with a rich caption and 7–8 VQA pairs (~900K total VQAs), drawn from CelebV-HQ, FFHQ, and MM-CelebA-HQ.

## Method

UniF²ace introduces a Dual Discrete Diffusion (D3Diff) loss that jointly optimizes a masked-token prediction objective and a discrete score-matching loss, with a theoretical proof (Theorem 1) showing the score-based surrogate yields a tighter upper bound on the negative log-likelihood than the masked generative loss alone. The architecture employs a multi-level grouped Mixture-of-Experts: a token-level MoE partitions FFN layers into task-specific groups (T2I vs. MMU) with Top-2 routing, and a sequence-level MoE injects instance-level features via specialized experts—noise/copy/zero experts for generation and CLIP/AntelopeV2 face-embedding experts for understanding—adaptively blending semantic and identity information to counteract attribute forgetting. Training proceeds in two stages (caption-only, then caption+VQA) on the newly constructed UniF²aceD-1M dataset, using a 1.8B-parameter Transformer with MAGVIT-v2 discrete image tokens.

## Results

On the UniF²aceD-1M benchmark, UniF²ace (1.8B) achieves VQAScore-CF5 of 0.894, VQAScore-LV of 0.679, FID of 66.005, and VLM-score of 88.049 for face generation, outperforming all UMMs and even the 12B Flux.1-dev model. For face understanding, it scores 6.02 on Desc-GPT and 6.53 on Conv-GPT, surpassing the 8B InternVL2.5 (5.62/5.89) by 7.1% on Desc-GPT. On public datasets (FFHQ-Text, MM-CelebA, CelebV-Text), UniF²ace consistently achieves the best FID and VLM-score among all compared models, e.g., FID of 85.179 on MM-CelebA vs. 105.141 for SD3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniF²ace (1.8B) outperforms all 14 baselines on the UniF²aceD-1M benchmark across both generation and understanding metrics. In generation, it beats the strongest generation-only model Flux.1-dev (12B) on FID (66.0 vs. 76.4) and VLM-score (88.0 vs. 84.5) despite being 6.7× smaller, and narrowly edges it on VQAScore-CF5 (0.894 vs. 0.893). Among UMMs, UniF²ace surpasses TokenFlow (7B) by 2.6% on VQAScore-CF5 and by 32.8 points on FID (66.0 vs. 98.2), and outperforms JanusFlow (1.3B) and Show-o (1.3B) by 1.3–3.9% on VQAScore-CF5. In understanding, UniF²ace achieves Desc-GPT of 6.02, exceeding the best understanding-only model InternVL2.5 (8B) at 5.62 by 7.1%, and Conv-GPT of 6.53, surpassing Qwen2-VL (7B) at 6.27. On the FaceXBench single-image subset, UniF²ace leads in Attribute Expression (39.50 vs. 37.50 for Qwen2.5-VL), Bias Fairness (37.56 vs. 35.11 for Qwen2-VL), and Deepfake detection (72.67 vs. 71.33), while matching Qwen2-VL on Face Recognition (37.33). On non-celebrity FLIP-80M samples, UniF²ace scores 5.54/5.77 on Desc-GPT/Desc-DS, outperforming Qwen2.5-VL (3B) at 5.30/5.51.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate strands of discrete generative modeling—score-based discrete diffusion (Lou et al., 2024) and masked generative models (Chang et al., 2022)—into a single D3Diff loss, with a theoretical proof (Theorem 1) showing the score-matching component yields a tighter upper bound on negative log-likelihood. Architecturally, it departs from prior unified multimodal models (Show-o, JanusFlow, TokenFlow) that use dense Transformers or only token-level MoE by introducing a multi-level grouped MoE with task-specific routing and sequence-level experts that inject CLIP and face-identity embeddings. The MoE gating design follows DeepSeekMoE (Dai et al., 2024), but the grouped, task-specialized structure with parameter-free copy/zero/noise experts is novel. In the face domain specifically, the paper bridges the long-standing fragmentation between MLLM-based understanding models and diffusion-based generation models, which had been treated as entirely separate research threads.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UniF²aceD-1M dataset (130K fine-grained image-caption pairs with 17.7 attributes per caption, plus 1M face-specific VQAs) addresses a critical data gap that has limited fine-grained face research, and is likely to become a standard benchmark. The D3Diff loss provides a clean theoretical justification for why score matching should complement masked generation, a result that could transfer to other discrete-token generation domains beyond faces. However, the model is limited to single-image inference (precluding multi-image reasoning on FaceXBench), operates at 1.8B scale, and the face-domain focus limits immediate generalizability. Open questions include whether D3Diff's theoretical advantage persists at larger scales or in continuous-valued generation, and whether the sequence-level MoE with domain-specific embeddings generalizes to other fine-grained visual domains.
