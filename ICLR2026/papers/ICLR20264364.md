## Problem

Existing LLM-based recommendation methods that incorporate reasoning rely on explicit chain-of-thought (CoT) data for both fine-tuning and inference, but high-quality CoT annotations are extremely difficult to obtain in recommendation (user feedback is limited to final outcomes, and preferences are subjective), and generating CoT at inference incurs prohibitive latency. This paper asks whether LLM reasoning for recommendation can be achieved entirely without explicit CoT supervision, at both training and inference time.

## Input / Output

The method takes a textual prompt constructed from a user's historical item interactions (item titles, max sequence length 10) and feeds it into a Qwen2.5-1.5B LLM. Before producing the final next-item prediction, the model autoregressively generates N continuous latent reasoning tokens (default N=1) as dense vectors in the LLM's embedding space (dimension d), rather than discrete text tokens. These latent tokens are concatenated with the original prompt and passed back through the LLM to produce the recommended item, adding only the cost of a single extra forward pass over one token.

## Method

LatentR³ introduces a LatentRATT attention layer on top of the LLM's final decoding layer that extracts hidden states and autoregressively generates latent reasoning tokens aligned with the LLM's input embedding space. Training follows a two-stage strategy: first, supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with standard next-token prediction on the target item to warm up the latent reasoning module; second, pure RL using a modified GRPO variant (LR-GRPO) that makes three key changes: (1) sampling latent reasoning via the reparameterization trick (Gaussian noise around the deterministic latent vector) instead of discrete token sampling, (2) using the negative perplexity of the ground-truth item as a continuous reward proxy to eliminate costly autoregressive answer generation during training, and (3) computing advantages relative to the batch-level average reward rather than the group-level average to avoid unreliable positive advantages when all samples in a group are low quality. During the RL stage, only the LatentRATT layer is updated while the LLM backbone remains frozen, which also nullifies the KL regularization term.

## Results

LatentR³ applied to D³ achieves the best performance across all four Amazon datasets, with relative improvements of 8.4% over D³, 17.0% over BIGRec, 27.9% over SASRec, and 266.8% over the untuned LLM baseline (Base). On the CDs dataset, the method reaches H@5=0.1137, H@10=0.1327, N@5=0.0915, and N@10=0.0977. Notably, these gains are achieved with only a single latent reasoning token (N=1), keeping inference cost nearly identical to non-reasoning LLM baselines, whereas explicit CoT incurs a 25–30× latency increase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LatentR³ (applied to D³) outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and metric. Against traditional sequential models, the gains are substantial: 85.8% over Caser, 67.8% over GRU4Rec, and 27.9% over SASRec (average relative improvement). Among LLM-based methods, the improvement over the untuned Base LLM is 266.8% and over explicit CoT prompting (RecSAVER) is 245.9%, highlighting that latent reasoning far surpasses both direct prompting and explicit chain-of-thought. Against the strongest tuned LLM baselines, LatentR³ on D³ improves over D³ by 8.4% and over BIGRec by 17.0%, while also surpassing AlphaRec (38.8% RI) on three of four datasets (Toys, CDs, Instruments), though AlphaRec retains a slight edge on Games before LatentR³ is applied. The method is plug-and-play: it can be layered on top of either BIGRec or D³ without modifying their core architectures, and the gains are consistent across both host models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GRPO reinforcement learning algorithm (Shao et al., 2024) and the two-stage SFT-then-RL training paradigm from DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025), adapting both to the recommendation setting. It departs from the dominant line of LLM-based recommendation work that relies on explicit chain-of-thought supervision (RecSAVER, ReasoningRec, Reasoning4Rec, R²ec) by replacing textual CoT with compact latent reasoning tokens generated by a novel attention layer (LatentRATT). It also diverges from general-domain latent reasoning methods (Hao et al., 2024; Deng et al., 2023), which still require CoT data for distillation, by learning latent reasoning entirely through RL with a perplexity-based continuous reward and batch-relative advantage computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that effective preference reasoning in recommendation can be achieved with a single latent token and no CoT annotation, reducing inference latency by roughly 25–30× compared to explicit CoT methods while delivering 8–17% relative gains over strong LLM-based baselines. This opens a practical path for deploying LLM reasoning in latency-sensitive recommendation systems where CoT data is unavailable or prohibitively expensive to collect. Key open questions include scalability to larger models and datasets (experiments are limited to 1.5B–3B LLMs and ~5–7K-item datasets), the interpretability of latent reasoning tokens, and whether the RL-based latent reasoning paradigm generalizes beyond sequential recommendation to other LLM reasoning tasks.
