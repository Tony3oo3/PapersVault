## Problem

Existing conditional diffusion models for image generation assume low-dimensional inputs (e.g., RGB) and rely on simple conditioning mechanisms that disrupt spatial correspondence and overlook inter-channel dependencies. Biological profiling data (spatial proteomics, single-cell multi-omics, clinical MRI) involves dozens to hundreds of semantically distinct channels with complex, context-dependent inter-channel relationships, and the set of observed channels varies across experiments and patients. No unified framework existed that could preserve spatial alignment, model arbitrary inter-channel dependencies, and generalize to unseen combinations of observed and missing channels within a single model.

## Input / Output

The method takes a spatially aligned subset of observed channels (e.g., a random subset of 50 IMC protein channels, scRNA-seq gene expression vectors, or a subset of MRI sequences) and generates the full multi-channel output (complete protein panel, protein expression, or missing MRI sequences). For spatial data, inputs are 64×64 patches downsampled to 16×16 latent representations; for single-cell data, the spatial dimensions collapse to H=W=1, reducing the problem to high-dimensional vector imputation. The key constraint is that observed and target channels are spatially co-registered, and the model must handle any arbitrary subset of channels as condition without retraining.

## Method

MCD employs a dual-network architecture: a diffusion UNet denoises the noisy target while a parallel conditional encoder produces spatially aligned features at multiple resolutions, which are injected hierarchically into corresponding UNet blocks via Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) gated addition rather than naive element-wise concatenation. During training, a random channel masking strategy (Algorithm 1) samples a Bernoulli subset of channels as observed and zeros the rest, forcing the model to learn an amortized conditional estimator that generalizes to any condition-target configuration at test time. Two complementary channel attention modules capture inter-channel dependencies: a lightweight SE block for per-sample global feature recalibration during condition injection, and a transformer-style self-attention over latent channels inside UNet blocks for higher-order cross-channel interactions. An additional SE-based output-space attention at the final projection layer provides a last channel-wise recalibration in data space. The model is trained end-to-end with the standard EDM noise-prediction objective, and the authors provide a theoretical guarantee (via Rademacher complexity and Chebyshev bounds) that minimizing the empirical amortized loss controls per-condition generalization error.

## Results

On spatial proteomics imputation (IMC), MCD achieves Pearson r of 0.667 (Breast, 35 channels) and 0.703 (Lung, 50 channels) in single-channel mode, and 0.596/0.647 in multi-channel mode with random masking, outperforming the best diffusion baseline ControlNet (0.452/0.537) by 0.15–0.21. On single-cell gene-to-protein prediction, MCD achieves the highest protein-level correlation (r_p) across all four CITE-seq datasets (0.673 PBMC, 0.763 CBMC, 0.685 BMMC, 0.647 HSPC). On BraTS 2024 MRI modality synthesis, MCD attains DICE 0.738 and global SSIM 0.928, surpassing the BraTS 2024 challenge winner HF-GAN (DICE 0.714, SSIM 0.919).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCD outperforms all 12 baselines across every task. In single-cell modality prediction, MCD beats scMM (the strongest prior method, r_p=0.598 on HSPC) by 0.049, and dominates KRR, MultiVI, GLUE, and UnitedNet by large margins (e.g., r_p 0.763 vs. 0.628 for UnitedNet on CBMC). In spatial proteomics imputation, MCD surpasses ControlNet by 0.15–0.21 Pearson r and BrushNet by 0.24–0.31, while also exceeding domain-specific methods STEM and Virtues by 0.12–0.28. The multi-channel (random masking) variant retains 90–95% of single-channel accuracy while generalizing to arbitrary channel subsets. On BraTS MRI synthesis, MCD improves DICE over the challenge winner HF-GAN by 0.024 and over SwinUNETR by 0.029, with consistent SSIM gains across tumor, healthy, tissue, and global regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conditional diffusion framework from ControlNet and BrushNet—designed for low-dimensional (≤3 channel) natural image inpainting—to high-dimensional multi-channel biological data (30–50+ protein or gene channels) with complex, context-dependent inter-channel dependencies. The random channel masking strategy generalizes the classifier-free guidance principle (Ho & Salimans, 2021) from dropping the entire condition to sampling arbitrary subsets of channels, framing the problem as amortized inference over the condition space. Hierarchical feature injection with Squeeze-and-Excitation gating replaces the element-wise addition used in ControlNet, and transformer-style channel attention within UNet blocks addresses inter-channel relationships that spatial attention alone cannot capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical solution to a real bottleneck in biological profiling: expensive technologies like IMC and spatial transcriptomics can only measure a limited panel of signals, and clinical MRI acquisitions often miss sequences due to patient constraints. The multi-dataset union result (learning from partially overlapping protein panels without requiring perfect channel alignment) is particularly valuable for integrating heterogeneous cohorts. Open questions include scaling to larger spatial transcriptomics datasets (500–5000 genes), incorporating richer biological priors (e.g., known protein-protein interaction networks), and whether the amortized generalization bound holds without the empirically-validated low-variance assumption. The SiD distillation to one-step generation suggests the approach is deployable at scale, but the theoretical guarantees remain conditional on assumptions that are only empirically checked.
