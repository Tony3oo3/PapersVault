## Problem

Attention mechanisms in large-scale models require computing an n²×n² attention matrix, creating a quadratic computational and memory bottleneck. While sparse attention heuristics exist, there is no principled data-structure framework for recovering the most important attention coordinates under strict streaming memory constraints where entries arrive sequentially and can be both added and removed (turnstile updates). This paper formalizes the 'attention sampler' problem and establishes both hardness and efficient algorithmic solutions for sampling from the tensor-product structure of attention matrices.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives turnstile stream updates to entries of matrix A ∈ ℝ^{n×d} (or A₁, A₂ ∈ ℝ^{n×d} with A = A₁⊗A₂) and/or weight vector x ∈ ℝ^d, where each update can increase or decrease a single entry. It must output a valid sample index i ∈ [n] (or (i₁,i₂) ∈ [n²] for the tensor case) at all times during the stream, with probability proportional to (1±ε)·|yᵢ|²/‖y‖₂² where y = Ax. The key constraint is that the algorithm uses space sublinear in n (the sequence length), with the tensor-product variant achieving O(nd) space and O(n) update time versus the trivial O(n²) baseline.

## Method

The paper first proves an Ω(n) space lower bound for exponential (softmax) samplers via a reduction from set-disjointness, showing that even allowing polynomial distortion n^C in sampling probabilities, quadratic space is unavoidable. For polynomial (ℓ₂) samplers, the core mechanism maintains a linear sketch Φy = ΦAx using CountSketch or TensorSketch, adapting the update strategy to which component (A, x, or both) is streaming: when A updates, the sketch ΦAx is updated directly in poly(1/ε, log n) time; when x updates, the sketch ΦA is precomputed and only x is maintained for O(1) update time. For the tensor-product attention matrix (A₁⊗A₂)x, the algorithm exploits the Kronecker structure via TensorSketch to avoid explicitly forming the n²×d² matrix, achieving O(nd) space and O(n) update time by maintaining separate sketches for each factor. A matching Ω(d²) space lower bound is proven for the both-updating case via reduction from the Index problem, confirming tightness up to polylogarithmic factors.

## Results

The paper establishes that exponential (softmax) attention samplers require Ω(n) bits of space even with polynomial distortion n^C and bounded entries ||y||_∞ = O(log n) (Theorem 4.4). In contrast, polynomial (ℓ₂) attention samplers achieve strictly sublinear-in-n space: d²·poly(1/ε, log n) bits when both A and x are updated in turnstile streams, with poly(1/ε, log n) update time (Theorem 5.7). For the tensor product setting (A₁ ⊗ A₂)x with one matrix updating, the sampler uses O(nd) space and O(n) update time, improving over the O(n²) trivial approach (Theorem 7.6). A matching Ω(d²) space lower bound is proven for the both-updating case (Theorem 6.2), confirming tightness up to polylog factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against the trivial approach of explicitly storing and computing the full n² × d² attention matrix, which requires O(n²d²) space and O(n²) time per update. The paper's polynomial samplers reduce space to d²·poly(1/ε, log n) — strictly sublinear in sequence length n — while the tensor-product sampler further reduces space to O(nd) with O(n) update time, a quadratic improvement over the trivial O(n²) baseline. Against the exponential (softmax) sampler, the paper proves an Ω(n) space lower bound, showing that the quadratic barrier is inherent to softmax and cannot be bypassed even with polynomial distortion. The results also complement prior streaming attention work (Han et al., 2025; Haris & Onak, 2025) which studied attention approximation but not sampling, and build on the PolySketchFormer framework (Kacham et al., 2023) that demonstrated polynomial attention can simulate softmax with favorable algorithmic structure. No empirical baselines are compared, as the paper is purely theoretical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical ℓ₂ sampler (Cormode et al. 2005; Monemizadeh & Woodruff 2010; Jowhari et al. 2011) to the structured setting of attention matrices expressed as Kronecker products (A₁⊗A₂)x, where updates arrive in turnstile streams. It leverages TensorSketch (Pagh 2013) and CountSketch (Charikar et al. 2004) as core sketching primitives, and connects the hardness of exponential (softmax-like) sampling to the set-disjointness communication complexity lower bound. The positive results for polynomial attention samplers align with the PolySketchFormer line of work (Kacham et al. 2023) showing that polynomial kernels admit efficient low-dimensional approximations, while the negative result for exponential samplers complements the offline time lower bounds of Alman & Song (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Ω(n²) space lower bound for exponential samplers is a clean and strong negative result: it shows that even allowing polynomial distortion to the sampling probabilities, softmax attention cannot be efficiently sampled in streaming, reinforcing the case for polynomial attention alternatives. The positive ℓ₂ sampler results (sublinear in n, with O(nd) space for the tensor formulation) provide the first theoretical foundation for streaming attention sampling, with direct relevance to auto-regressive Transformers and machine unlearning scenarios. Key open questions include whether sublinear space is achievable for exponential samplers under restricted entry bounds (o(log n)), and whether these theoretical samplers translate to practical speedups in real Transformer inference pipelines.
