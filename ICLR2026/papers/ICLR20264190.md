## Problem

Standard masked diffusion models (MDMs) for discrete data predict a factorized distribution over masked positions, so revealing many tokens per step introduces approximation error and forces many sequential network forward passes to maintain sample quality. The paper addresses how to reveal multiple tokens concurrently from a non-factorized predictive distribution without incurring the cost of sequential autoregressive sampling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264190/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each generation step, the model receives a partially masked discrete sequence (text tokens or amino acid residues) along with a random permutation σ defining the generation ordering. The non-causal blocks process the masked input to produce draft tokens for all remaining positions, while the causal blocks receive the full permuted sequence (revealed tokens plus draft tokens) to compute target probabilities. The final output is a complete unmasked sequence, with the number of tokens revealed per step determined by the speculative accept/reject loop and a window function W(i) that grows with the number of already-revealed tokens.

## Method

The paper proposes a hybrid transformer architecture where the first L−1 layers use standard non-causal (any-to-any) attention to produce a factorized draft distribution, and the final layer uses σ-GPT-style causal attention over the permuted sequence to produce a non-factorized target distribution. A residual connection from the non-causal hidden states into the causal output ensures the target learns to improve over the draft and aligns the two distributions for high speculative acceptance rates. During inference, the non-causal blocks generate draft tokens in one forward pass, and the causal blocks verify them via speculative sampling in a single additional pass, accepting a prefix of draft tokens and resampling at the first rejection. Training jointly optimizes both distributions with a weighted cross-entropy loss requiring only one forward pass of the hybrid network. The paper further derives a tractable O(D²) recursive decomposition for the model likelihood under the shifting-target speculative sampling procedure, yielding an ELBO on the log-likelihood.

## Results

On OpenWebText (GPT2-scale, 150M params), the self-speculative method achieves a GPT2 NLL of 5.05 at 128 NFE, matching the standard masked diffusion baseline's 5.05 at 256 NFE — a ~2× reduction in function evaluations. On Text8, the method achieves greater than 2× NFE reduction in the low-NFE regime for equivalent spelling accuracy. On UniRef50 protein sequences, adding a single causal block to a frozen ESM2 model yields a ~2× speed-up for high pLDDT values relative to the standard MDM sampling algorithm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard masked diffusion baseline (Shi et al., 2024), the self-speculative method consistently wins on the NFE-efficiency axis: at 64 NFE it achieves 5.12 NLL versus 5.27 for MDM, and at 128 NFE it reaches 5.05 versus 5.13, effectively halving the required evaluations for equivalent quality while maintaining nearly identical sample entropy (5.70 vs 5.70 at 64 NFE). On Text8, the advantage is even more pronounced in the low-NFE regime with >2× fewer evaluations needed. Against SDTT (Deschenaux & Gulcehre, 2025), the comparison is nuanced: SDTT reports substantially lower NLL (3.70 vs 5.28 at 32 NFE), but its sample entropy is markedly lower (5.31 vs 5.72), indicating mode-seeking behavior likely caused by truncation errors in the teacher sampling; the self-speculative method preserves diversity equivalent to the MDM baseline. On protein sequences (UniRef50), the method outperforms the original Wang et al. (2024) non-causal ESM2 model by achieving a ~2× speed-up for high pLDDT, demonstrating that a single causal block added to a frozen pretrained network is sufficient to unlock the efficiency gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants speculative sampling from the LLM inference literature (Leviathan et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023) into the masked diffusion model (MDM) setting, where the non-causal any-to-any attention blocks serve as the draft model and a small set of causal σ-GPT blocks (Pannatier et al., 2024) serve as the target. It departs from prior self-speculative decoding work (Zhang et al., 2024; Elhoushi et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024) in two key ways: the architecture is mostly non-causal with a thin causal head (the inverse of Medusa-style mostly-causal stacks), and the any-order generation setting introduces a 'shifting target' problem—where the causal target distribution changes as tokens are revealed—that does not arise in fixed left-to-right decoding. The theoretical contribution (Proposition 3.1) provides a tractable dynamic-programming decomposition of the model likelihood under this shifting target, which has no analogue in the standard speculative sampling literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~2× reduction in network forward passes at matched sample quality, with only 0.98% additional FLOPs, makes MDMs substantially more practical for text and protein generation, and the ability to freeze a pretrained non-causal backbone and train only a single causal head lowers the adoption barrier considerably. The paper leaves open whether combining self-speculative sampling with compute-intensive inference-scaling techniques (e.g., re-masking corrector steps) can yield further gains, and whether the approach scales to much larger model sizes where the non-causal/causal split ratio might shift. A limitation is that the speedup is bounded by the alignment between draft and target distributions; if the non-causal factorized approximation is very poor relative to the causal target, acceptance rates drop and the benefit diminishes.
