## Problem

Existing VQ-based generative super-resolution methods suffer from two key limitations: (1) vanilla vector quantization encodes the entire visual feature space (structure + texture), requiring large codebooks and incurring substantial quantization error due to the richness of natural images; and (2) training the code index predictor with code-level cross-entropy supervision ignores the varying reconstruction impacts of different prediction errors, leading to sub-optimal prior modeling that does not align with final image quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-resolution RGB image (4× degradation) as input and produces a high-resolution RGB image as output. Internally, the HR image is decomposed into a structure component at 32× downsampling (64 channels) and a texture component at 8× downsampling (256 channels), where the texture component is discretized into indices from a 1024-entry codebook. At inference, the predictor regresses the continuous structure features and predicts discrete texture indices from the LR input, which are then decoded jointly to reconstruct the HR image in a single forward pass (38 ms on RTX 3090 for 64×64 input).

## Method

The proposed TVQ&RAP framework introduces two key strategies. First, Texture Vector-Quantization (TVQ) decomposes the image into structure and texture components via a multiscale autoencoder: the structure branch (32× downsampled) is aligned with an extreme low-resolution reference (8× downsampled) to suppress texture leakage, while the texture branch (8× downsampled) is vector-quantized with a 1024-entry codebook, significantly reducing the complexity of the feature space that must be discretized. Second, Reconstruction Aware Prediction (RAP) replaces indirect code-level cross-entropy supervision with direct image-level supervision: using the straight-through estimator, reconstruction losses (MSE, perceptual, GAN) are backpropagated through the frozen decoder to the index predictor, so the predictor learns to select codebook entries that minimize actual reconstruction error rather than merely matching ground-truth indices. Training proceeds in two stages: the TVQ tokenizer is trained for 450K iterations, then the predictor is trained with code-level loss for 300K iterations followed by 10K iterations of image-level fine-tuning.

## Results

On ImageNet-Test (×4 SR), TVQ&RAP achieves the best perceptual and no-reference metrics: LPIPS 0.210, CLIPIQA 0.730, MUSIQ 63.873, and MANIQA 0.5530, while maintaining competitive PSNR (22.49 dB) and FID (26.57). On real-world datasets (RealSR, RealSet65), it attains the best no-reference scores across CLIPIQA, MUSIQ, and MANIQA. In terms of efficiency, the method runs in 38 ms with 57 M parameters—roughly 5.5% of ResShift-15's runtime (689 ms) and less than 60% of SinSR-1's (65 ms)—while delivering superior perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest diffusion-based baseline ResShift-15, TVQ&RAP wins decisively on perceptual metrics—LPIPS (0.210 vs 0.237), CLIPIQA (0.730 vs 0.586), MUSIQ (63.873 vs 53.182), and MANIQA (0.5530 vs 0.4191)—but trails on PSNR (22.49 vs 24.94 dB) and FID (26.57 vs 19.53), reflecting a fidelity–perceptual-quality trade-off. Compared to the one-step distilled SinSR-1, the method improves LPIPS (0.210 vs 0.218), CLIPIQA (0.730 vs 0.611), MUSIQ (63.873 vs 53.632), and MANIQA (0.5530 vs 0.4161) while running in 38 ms versus 65 ms. Against the VQ-based baselines FeMaSR and AdaCode, TVQ&RAP leads on every perceptual metric (e.g., MUSIQ 63.873 vs 55.930/53.950) and is faster (38 ms vs 57/104 ms). On real-world datasets (RealSR, RealSet65), the method achieves the best no-reference scores (CLIPIQA 0.6897/0.7347, MUSIQ 65.591/68.420, MANIQA 0.4337/0.4814) over all 12 baselines including StableSR-200 and UPSR-5. In the user study (15 participants, 90 images), TVQ&RAP receives 48.8% preference, nearly double the next-best SinSR (21.1%). Overall, the method trades a modest PSNR/SSIM drop for substantially better perceptual realism at a fraction of the inference cost of multi-step diffusion methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the VQ-VAE/VQ-GAN lineage for discrete visual representation learning, inheriting the codebook lookup mechanism, stop-gradient training, and adversarial loss formulation. It departs from prior VQ-based SR methods (CodeFormer, FeMaSR, AdaCode) in two key ways: (1) drawing on classical dictionary learning to decompose images into structure and texture, quantizing only the texture component to reduce codebook complexity, and (2) replacing the standard code-level cross-entropy predictor training with image-level reconstruction supervision via the straight-through estimator. The structure-removal idea is explicitly motivated by prior sparse coding work (Matsui et al., Zeyde et al., Gu et al.), while the RAP strategy is a novel application of STE to align predictor optimization with final image quality rather than intermediate code accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: TVQ&RAP achieves the best perceptual metrics (LPIPS, FID, MUSIQ, CLIPIQA, MANIQA) among compared methods while running in 38 ms with 57 M parameters—roughly 18× faster than ResShift-15 and 2× faster than SinSR-1 with fewer parameters. The conceptual insight that code-level prediction accuracy and image reconstruction quality are misaligned (accuracy drops from 6.8% to 4.4% when adding image-level supervision, yet all perceptual metrics improve) provides a useful diagnostic for the broader VQ-based generation community. Open questions include whether the structure/texture decomposition generalizes beyond natural images to medical or satellite imagery, whether the fixed 1024-entry codebook is optimal across domains, and whether the approach extends to other restoration tasks (inpainting, denoising) where the 'structure already available' assumption may not hold as cleanly.
