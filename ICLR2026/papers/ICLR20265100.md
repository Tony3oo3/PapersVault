## Problem

While the developmental timeline of hippocampal spatial cells (head direction → place → grid) is well characterized in rats, no unifying mechanistic model explains what drives their sequential emergence. This paper tests the hypothesis that postnatal changes in locomotor statistics—and the resulting sensory experiences—fundamentally shape the formation of spatial tuning in the hippocampus.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265100/cropped_page3_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The RNN receives at each timestep a concatenation of a 32×16-pixel grayscale panoramic frame (240° horizontal, 120° vertical FOV, rendered in Blender to emulate rat vision), a 2D velocity vector, and a scalar rotational speed, all sampled at 1 Hz from simulated trajectories. The network outputs a predicted next visual frame (trained with L1 loss) and a 500-dimensional hidden state that is analyzed for spatial and directional tuning via rate maps (25×25 spatial grid) and polar maps (60 angular bins), yielding spatial information (SI_r, SI_d) and resultant vector length (RVL) metrics.

## Method

The core approach combines three components: (1) a computational clustering of experimental rat trajectories (using Jensen-Shannon distances on speed/rotational-speed PDFs and transition matrices, followed by Gaussian mixture modeling) that identifies three developmental locomotor stages—crawl (P13.5), walk (P16), and run (P20)—plus adult; (2) simulation of novel trajectories per stage using RatInABox's Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with stage-specific parameters; and (3) sequential training of a single-layer RNN (500 hidden units, sigmoid nonlinearity) via BPTT with teacher forcing on 9-timestep windows, where each stage inherits weights from the previous, emulating continuous developmental learning. A key control experiment shows that merely extending inter-frame gaps on crawl trajectories to match adult-level predictive demand fails to reproduce the developmental spatial tuning, demonstrating that the specific statistics of movement—not just the rate of sensory change—drive allocentric representation emergence. An adult variant additionally injects a linear projection of 25 grid cells into the hidden state every 90 timesteps, revealing that mature positional tuning requires grid cell input.

## Results

Sequentially trained RNNs (500 hidden units, 1,500 epochs per stage) recapitulate the developmental timeline of hippocampal spatial cells: spatial information SI_r increases significantly from walk to adult (p < 0.01), directional metrics SI_d and RVL increase (p < 0.001), and the percentage of place cells rises (p < 0.001). A novel prediction—significant developmental increase in conjunctive place-HD cells (p < 0.01)—is confirmed in CA1 recordings. The rate-of-change control (crawl trajectories with 1–3 s inter-frame gaps) fails to reproduce developmental phenomena, with ΔBIC > 10 favoring the original model, and inter-trial rate-map correlations remain above 0.8 across all stages.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper tests three alternative hypotheses against the main developmental model. (1) Rate-of-change control: RNNs trained on crawl trajectories with extended 1–3 s inter-frame gaps (matching adult inter-frame distances) fail to reproduce the developmental trajectory, with ΔBIC > 10 favoring the original model and showing higher decoding errors, weaker sRSA, and less structured Isomap projections—even with grid cell input. (2) Reversed development (adult→crawl): immediately develops robust spatial representations with accurate decoding and strong sRSA, showing that the specific progression of increasing movement complexity (not just accumulated experience) drives spatial coding maturation. (3) Crawl-only training (matching total exposure): produces minimal spatial tuning changes, confirming that a single locomotor repertoire is insufficient. Additionally, the adult model without grid cell input fails to reach experimentally observed place cell levels, whereas adding grid cell initialization (25 cells, 3 scales) restores full maturation, highlighting grid cells' role in adult positional tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of RNN-based spatial representation models (Stachenfeld et al., 2017; Cueva & Wei, 2018; Whittington et al., 2020; Levenstein et al., 2024) by introducing a developmental curriculum grounded in empirically derived locomotor statistics. While Cueva & Wei (2018) observed an emergence order of spatial cell types across training epochs, no prior work connected this to the specific statistics of postnatal locomotor maturation. The paper also builds on the developmental neuroscience literature (Wills et al., 2010; Tan et al., 2017; Muessig et al., 2015) that characterized timelines of spatial neuron maturation but lacked a unifying mechanistic account, and on Altman & Sudarshan's (1975) classical locomotor staging, which the authors formalize computationally via Gaussian mixture clustering of movement distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a causal mechanistic link between locomotor development and hippocampal spatial coding ontogeny, showing that specific movement statistics—not merely the rate of sensory change—drive the emergence of allocentric representations. The model's prediction of increasing conjunctive place-head-direction cells, validated in real CA1 recordings, opens a new experimental direction for developmental neuroscience. Key limitations include the single-layer architecture, simplified grayscale visual and vestibular inputs, and the treatment of grid cells as an external initialization signal rather than an endogenous circuit, leaving open how multisensory integration and cross-regional interactions shape the full developmental trajectory.
