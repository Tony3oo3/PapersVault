## Problem

High-fidelity generative modeling of detailed 3D neuronal morphologies remains unexplored: prior approaches produce only coarse skeletons or occupancy maps that miss surface-level features like dendritic spines and caliber changes. In connectomics, this data scarcity directly limits the accuracy of shape plausibility classifiers used in production neuron reconstruction pipelines, where residual segmentation errors require billions of dollars in manual proofreading at whole-brain scale.

## Input / Output

The model takes 8,192-point Gaussian noise as input, optionally conditioned on an 11-dimensional vector encoding position (3D mean), spatial spread (6 covariance elements), branching complexity (MST leaf count on a 256-point subsample), and neurite type (axon/dendrite), plus a binary mask indicating which conditioning dimensions are active. It outputs an 8,192-point 3D point cloud representing a neuron fragment within a 10 μm radius (normalized so 1 unit = 10 μm), centered at a skeleton node. A separate regression model can then predict per-point surface normals and local curvature for downstream use in the PATHFINDER pipeline.

## Method

MoGen employs constant-velocity flow matching on point clouds, trained with a simple MSE objective to predict the vector field transporting Gaussian noise to real data. The backbone is the PointInfinity latent transformer (256 latent tokens, 4 stages, 2 blocks per stage, 10.7M parameters), which achieves linear scaling via cross-attention between point tokens and a fixed latent set. The key architectural modification is k-NN context injection: for each point, the relative 3D coordinates of its 16 nearest neighbors are appended to its feature vector before tokenization, providing a local geometric inductive bias that alleviates the information bottleneck of the global cross-attention mechanism and eliminates detached-point artifacts. A modified cosine schedule concentrates timestep sampling at both ends of the interval to balance global structure learning with fine-detail refinement, and classifier-free guidance enables controllable interpolation between morphologies by varying conditioning features while holding the initial noise fixed.

## Results

MoGen achieves an MMD score of 3.54 on the mouse cortex validation set (improving to 3.08 with full 1M-step training), substantially outperforming the PointInfinity-inspired baseline without k-NN context (4.27). In the downstream PATHFINDER reconstruction task, co-training the SHAPE classifier with 10% MoGen-generated samples reduces total split and merge errors by 4.4% (from 0.7947 to 0.7595 errors/mm), corresponding to an estimated 157 person-years of saved manual proofreading for a full mouse brain. The SHAPE classifier F1 improves from 93.91% (real data only) to 94.64% with 10% synthetic augmentation, while 100% synthetic data drops F1 to 66.70%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MoGen against a full transformer baseline inspired by Luo & Hu (2021) on a downsampled 2048-point dataset, where MoGen achieves MMD 4.94 versus 5.78 for the baseline. Direct comparison at the full 8192-point resolution is computationally infeasible for the transformer baseline due to its quadratic self-attention complexity, which the authors note makes many existing point cloud generative baselines impractical at the target resolution. Internally, the ablation (Table 1) identifies the k-NN context injection as the critical architectural contribution, improving MMD from 4.27 to 3.54 and reducing low-noise-regime loss from 0.712 to 0.709. Halving model width or depth degrades MMD to 6.97 and 9.94 respectively, confirming the need for sufficient capacity, while replacing the modified cosine schedule with a linear one yields MMD 5.12. The downstream task comparison (Figure 6) shows that MoGen-augmented SHAPE establishes a new Pareto frontier, simultaneously reducing both split and merge errors per millimeter of path length compared to real-data-only training.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoGen builds on the PointInfinity latent transformer backbone (Huang et al., 2024) for scalable point cloud generation and the constant-velocity flow matching framework (Lipman et al., 2023) as its generative engine. It departs from prior neuron morphology generation work (MorphGrower, MorphVAE, MorphOcc, PointNeuron) that relied on simplified representations—sparse skeletons, occupancy maps, or coarse implicit surfaces—by targeting high-resolution 3D point clouds of local neurite fragments with detailed surface geometry. The key architectural departure is a k-NN relative-coordinate injection that addresses an information bottleneck in PointInfinity's global cross-attention, a modification drawn from the PointNet++ local neighborhood literature but applied here to a flow matching generative setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a concrete, quantified impact on a production connectomics pipeline: co-training the SHAPE plausibility classifier with MoGen-generated samples reduces split/merge errors by 4.4%, translating to an estimated 157 person-years of saved manual proofreading for a full mouse brain reconstruction. This validates synthetic data augmentation as a viable strategy for data-scarce scientific domains and establishes a template for using generative models to accelerate downstream tasks in neuroscience. Open questions include scaling from ~10 µm fragments to complete multi-millimeter neurons (likely requiring hierarchical approaches), reducing occasional topological failures (disconnected components, spurious cycles), and whether the k-NN modification's resolution dependence can be mitigated without sacrificing surface fidelity.
