## Problem

Masked diffusion models (MDMs) offer parallel decoding as an alternative to sequential autoregressive generation, but suffer from two critical drawbacks: bidirectional attention is fundamentally incompatible with KV caching, forcing full context re-computation at every decoding step, and the conditional independence assumption breaks down for nearby tokens, causing incoherent outputs because the model must learn dependencies over an intractable exponential space of token combinations.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt and generates a text response by partitioning the target sequence into fixed-length slots (k tokens each, typically 8–32). During inference, slots are decoded in a non-sequential, parallel order determined by confidence scores, and the final response is reconstructed by restoring the original slot order. Position IDs always correspond to each token's original index in the correct sequence, remaining invariant to the reordering applied for KV cache reuse.

## Method

REFUSION integrates sequence reorganization into a causal attention framework: newly decoded slots are reordered to the front of remaining masked slots after each iteration, enabling full KV cache reuse while preserving globally flexible generation order. The response is partitioned into fixed-length slots, and inference alternates between a diffusion-based slot selection stage (scoring masked slots by first-token probability and selecting those above a threshold) and an autoregressive slot infilling stage (using speculative decoding to verify and complete selected slots in parallel). Training mirrors inference dynamics with a hybrid objective: an autoregressive next-token loss on randomly permuted clean slots and a denoising loss on randomly masked slots, supervising every token to boost data efficiency. This design reduces the learning complexity from an intractable token-level combination space (≈2^L) to a manageable slot-level permutation space (≈⌊(L/k)!·e⌋−1), while the intra-slot autoregressive decoding captures strong local dependencies that violate conditional independence.

## Results

REFUSION achieves an average accuracy of 65.31% across seven benchmarks (MMLU-Pro, ARC-C, GSM8K, MATH, GPQA, HumanEval, MBPP) with an average throughput of 72.62 tokens/sec, surpassing the best prior MDM (Dream-7B-Instruct, 55.55% avg) by 34% and being over 18× faster. Against the strong ARM baseline Qwen3-8B (73.36% avg, 32.42 TPS), REFUSION outperforms on GSM8K (84.91 vs. 81.96) and MBPP (68.20 vs. 63.80) while maintaining a 2.33× average speedup. On HumanEval, REFUSION reaches 78.66% pass@1, exceeding the next-best MDM by nearly 22 absolute points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REFUSION decisively outperforms all MDM baselines on every benchmark in both accuracy and throughput. Against LLaDA-8B-Instruct, it gains +19.8 avg accuracy and 5.9× throughput; against Dream-7B-Instruct, it gains +9.8 avg accuracy and 8.2× throughput. The acceleration methods Fast-dLLM and D2F improve MDM throughput but at severe accuracy cost (e.g., Dream w/ D2F drops to 44.72% avg), and even then REFUSION still leads on all seven benchmarks. Against the ARM baseline Qwen3-8B, REFUSION wins on GSM8K (+2.95) and MBPP (+4.40) while trailing on MMLU-Pro (−21.31), MATH (−29.06), GPQA (−3.57), and HumanEval (−9.14), yet maintains a 2.33× average speedup (72.62 vs. 32.42 TPS). In the controlled comparison (Table 2, same 120K data, same Qwen3-8B init), REFUSION beats the retrained Qwen3-8B by ~6 points on HumanEval while being 1.9× faster, and substantially outperforms retrained LLaDA (which collapses to 47.41% avg) and BD3-LMs (52.88% avg). Against Dream-7B-Instruct on its native Qwen2.5-7B backbone (Table 3), REFUSION achieves +2.23% avg accuracy and an 11.05× speedup despite using only fine-tuning (3.7M samples) versus Dream's 580B-token pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

REFUSION builds on the masked diffusion model (MDM) paradigm established by LLaDA and Dream, adopting their mask-and-denoise training objective, but fundamentally restructures the decoding process by inverting the logic of prior block-based methods (BD3-LMs, Fast-dLLM, D2F). Where those methods enforce serial inter-block generation with parallel intra-block decoding, REFUSION performs parallel inter-slot diffusion-based selection with serial intra-slot autoregressive infilling, while using a purely causal attention framework to enable full KV cache reuse. The design also draws on speculative decoding for the verification step, and on RoPE's relative-position invariance to make token reordering compatible with positional encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REFUSION demonstrates that the long-standing trade-off between MDM speed and ARM quality can be substantially broken: it matches or exceeds a strong 8B ARM (Qwen3-8B) on several benchmarks while being 2.33× faster, and outperforms all prior MDMs by 34% with an 18× throughput gain. Key open questions include whether the slot-based approach scales to larger models and longer contexts, whether the current slot immutability (no re-masking of completed slots) limits quality on complex multi-step reasoning, and whether reinforcement learning can further optimize the slot selection policy. The finding that simple KV cache concatenation after parallel generation performs as well as (or slightly better than) full recomputation suggests the inter-slot conditioning assumption may be less critical than previously thought.
