## Problem

Multi-task manipulation policies built on transformers tokenize 3D observations and actions into a shared embedding space, discarding the underlying geometric structure and failing to generalize to novel 3D object poses. Prior SE(3)-equivariant methods are limited to single-task settings, while multi-task methods achieve only translational equivariance. EquAct is the first to enforce continuous SE(3) equivariance (covering both rotation and translation) in a single unified multi-task policy.

## Input / Output

The observation is a colored point cloud of 2500 points reconstructed from 256×256 RGBD images captured by four calibrated cameras (front, left, right, in-hand), combined with the current end-effector gripper pose (SE(3) transform + open/close state). The language instruction is encoded by a frozen CLIP tokenizer and Transformer encoder into a type-0 (invariant) embedding. The policy outputs a keyframe gripper pose (translation + rotation in SE(3)) and a binary open/close action, selected as the argmax over an implicit action value function evaluated at 449 translational queries (coarse-to-fine, 3 levels) and 36,864 rotational queries on a HEALPix grid.

## Method

EquAct encodes the point cloud into latent spherical Fourier features via a novel SE(3)-equivariant Point Transformer U-Net (EPTU) that interleaves EquiformerV2 graph attention blocks with new spherical Fourier maxpooling and upsampling layers for multi-scale feature propagation. Language conditioning is injected through SE(3)-invariant FiLM (iFiLM) layers that scale type-l>0 features by learned scalar gates and apply affine transforms to type-0 features, provably preserving equivariance while enabling semantic modulation. Action evaluation uses equivariant field networks: a translational field network aggregates spherical features to query points via graph attention (outputting rotation-invariant type-0 values), and a rotational field network performs spherical CNN convolution at the predicted translation to score rotations. Training minimizes cross-entropy over uniformly sampled query actions from the full pose space, with random SE(3) data augmentation (±5° roll/pitch, ±45° yaw) to reduce numerical equivariance error.

## Results

On 18 RLBench tasks with 249 language instructions, EquAct achieves 89.4% average success rate with 100 demos (SE(2) init), 60.1% with 10 demos (SE(2) init), and 53.3% with 10 demos (SE(3) init), outperforming SAM2ACT (86.8/52.2/37.0%) and 3DDA (81.3/50.3/37.9%) by 2.6%, 6.2%, and 15.4% respectively. Training and inference time (240 h, 0.7 s) and GPU memory (21 GB) match the baselines. On 4 physical tasks with SE(3) pose variation, EquAct achieves 65% average success rate versus 12.5% for 3DDA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EquAct outperforms both baselines across all three difficulty settings, with the margin growing as the task becomes harder: +2.6% over SAM2ACT and +8.1% over 3DDA in the easiest (2D/100) setting, +6.2% and +9.8% in the medium (2D/10) setting, and +16.3% and +15.4% in the hardest (3D/10, full SE(3) randomization) setting. The advantage is most pronounced on precision tasks like 'place_cups' (59% vs. 0% for SAM2ACT in 3D/10) and 'sort_shape' (36% vs. 24% and 14%), where geometric equivariance is critical. EquAct does underperform on tasks with fixed object poses (e.g., 'sweep_to_dustpan': 48% vs. 72% for SAM2ACT in 3D/10), where equivariance provides no benefit. In physical experiments, EquAct achieves 65% average success rate versus 12.5% for 3DDA, with 3DDA frequently skipping keyframe actions. Computationally, EquAct matches baselines in GPU memory (21 GB) and is comparable in training time (~240 h), though inference is slower than SAM2ACT (0.7 s vs. 0.1 s) and faster than 3DDA (0.7 s vs. 3.7 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: multi-task keyframe manipulation policies (PerAct, RVT, SAM2ACT, 3DDA) that process language and 3D observations in shared transformer embeddings but discard geometric structure, and SE(3)-equivariant policy learning (Simeonov et al., Ryu et al., Huang et al.) that enforces geometric consistency but is limited to single-task settings. The key architectural departure is the introduction of SE(3)-invariant FiLM (iFiLM) layers for language conditioning, which is the first explicit identification of SE(3) invariance of natural language instructions in robotic policies. The equivariant U-Net (EPTU) builds on EquiformerV2's spherical Fourier attention blocks but adds novel maxpooling and upsampling operations in the Fourier domain to achieve U-Net-style multi-scale processing without graph caching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing the first theoretically guaranteed SE(3)-equivariant multi-task policy, this work removes the need for large amounts of robot data to learn geometric priors from scratch, with the 15.4% improvement in the SE(3)/10 setting demonstrating that equivariance is not merely a regularization trick but a structural requirement for spatial generalization. The iFiLM concept is broadly applicable to any equivariant model requiring invariant conditioning signals. Key limitations include the keyframe action assumption (which breaks for fine-grained manipulation), inference speed slower than the best baseline, and the potential to further improve data efficiency by incorporating pretrained vision-language models. Open questions include whether the equivariance advantage persists in continuous (non-keyframe) action spaces and whether the approach extends to deformable or non-rigid objects.
