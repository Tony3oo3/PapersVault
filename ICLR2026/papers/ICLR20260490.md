## Problem

Continual learning research has long assumed that exemplar memory is the primary bottleneck, yet in modern deployments GPU time—not storage—is the true constraint. This paper identifies that when exemplar memory is sufficient to mitigate forgetting, the core challenge shifts from stability to plasticity: models become biased toward prior tasks and struggle to learn new ones, a regime that existing methods do not address cost-efficiently.

## Input / Output

For class-incremental learning, the method consumes RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-100, 224×224 ImageNet-100) alongside a replay buffer of stored exemplars from prior tasks, producing class labels. For LLM continual instruction tuning (TRACE), it takes text instructions plus a memory buffer of prior-task examples and generates text responses. Unusually, the method's core operations act directly on model weights during training rather than on the data pipeline, requiring only a single model copy in VRAM (relative VRAM usage |W|×2) rather than storing per-task checkpoints.

## Method

Weight Space Consolidation combines two lightweight weight-space operations applied during training. First, after a warm-up phase on each new task, a moment-based importance score S_l = |m̂_l|·v̂_l (product of Adam's first and second exponential gradient moments) ranks all parameters; the bottom (100−Q)% are softly reset by blending with the previous task's weights (θ[l] = 0.5·θ[l] + 0.5·θ_prev[l]), restoring plasticity by reactivating dormant parameters while preserving task-critical ones. Second, a running stochastic weight average Θ is accumulated every j iterations after the reset, biasing convergence toward flatter, more stable minima; at task completion, θ is replaced by Θ. The key insight is that under sufficient exemplar memory, high gradient alignment (ρ_t ≈ 1) between new and past tasks shrinks effective updates, so targeted resets break the model out of the prior solution basin while averaging consolidates knowledge—achieving both plasticity and stability with negligible GPU overhead comparable to naive replay.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with 400 exemplars per class (80% memory), Weight Space Consolidation achieves 77.71% average class-IL accuracy, outperforming Replay (75.71%), iCaRL (75.49%), BiC (72.50%), and WA (73.83%), while matching the expansion-based FOSTER (77.28%) at a fraction of its training cost. On ImageNet-100 with 600 exemplars (46% memory), the method reaches 82.64%, surpassing Replay (81.08%), DER (80.53%), and MEMO (81.11%), and nearly matching FOSTER (82.79%). For LLM continual instruction tuning on TRACE, the method achieves 2–9% higher average scores than offline merging baselines (Task Arithmetic, MagMax) while using only 2× the VRAM of a single model versus T× for offline methods. Training cost remains comparable to the cheapest baseline (Replay), yielding 3–4× cost reductions over expansion-based SOTA methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Weight Space Consolidation consistently outperforms all non-expansion baselines (Replay, iCaRL, BiC, WA) across both CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-100 at abundant memory sizes (≥40%), with the largest gaps over BiC (up to 5.2% on CIFAR-100 at 80% memory) and WA (up to 3.9%). Against expansion-based methods, Ours surpasses DER and MEMO at all memory sizes above 16%, and matches or slightly exceeds FOSTER (77.71 vs. 77.28 on CIFAR-100 at 80%; 82.64 vs. 82.79 on ImageNet-100 at 46%) while using roughly one-fifth the training time. Compared to the naive Replay baseline, Ours gains 1.5–2.0% accuracy at high memory settings, confirming that the plasticity-restoring reset and stability-enhancing averaging are both necessary. On the TRACE LLM benchmark, Ours outperforms offline merging methods (Task Arithmetic, MagMax, Model Soups) by 2–9% in average score while requiring only 2× single-model VRAM instead of T×, and also beats the online baselines SLERP and Avg. (Online). The key differentiator is cost: Ours maintains training time comparable to Replay (the cheapest baseline) while achieving near-SOTA accuracy, whereas FOSTER and DER require 4–5× more GPU time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on stochastic weight averaging (Izmailov et al., 2018) for stability and the top-Q% parameter selection principle from TIES-merging (Yadav et al., 2024) for identifying important parameters, repurposing both as online, in-training operations rather than post-hoc model merging. It departs from the dominant CL paradigm that treats exemplar memory as the primary constraint, instead arguing that GPU cost is the real bottleneck in modern deployments. Unlike plasticity-only approaches (Dohare et al., 2024) that assume full memory, or post-hoc merging methods (Task Arithmetic, MagMax) that require storing per-task checkpoints, Weight Space Consolidation operates within a single model trajectory during training, targeting the 'sufficient but not exhaustive' memory regime that prior work has largely ignored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central reframing—that under realistic abundant memory, the CL challenge shifts from forgetting to plasticity loss—has direct practical implications for deploying CL systems where storage is cheap but GPU time is expensive. The finding that naive replay plus lightweight weight operations matches or beats expansion-based SOTA at 3-4× lower cost provides a scalable baseline for real-world CL. Open questions remain: the paper acknowledges that gradient interference and increased variance under abundant memory are not fully addressed, the 'sufficient' memory threshold is somewhat dataset-dependent, and whether the approach extends to non-exemplar-based CL or very long task sequences (beyond 10 tasks) is untested. The zero-memory ablation shows significant degradation, confirming the method still depends on having some replay buffer.
