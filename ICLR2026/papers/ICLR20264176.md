## Problem

Natural language interfaces for vehicle control must handle vague, under-specified, and multi-turn commands under strict safety protocols, yet existing LLMs hallucinate under ambiguity, fail to request clarifications when uncertain, and lack protocol adherence. Current benchmarks (Talk2Car, CI-AVSR, doScenes) primarily support single-turn mapping without realistic ambiguity coverage or failsafe evaluation metrics, leaving a gap between dataset realism and model safety assessment in safety-critical in-cabin settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264176/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes natural-language in-car voice commands (often fuzzy, colloquial, or under-specified) along with multi-turn dialogue history, and produces schema-aligned JSON function calls conforming to a 37-action, 146-parameter production HMI schema with type, range, and dependency constraints. When extreme ambiguity is detected, the model outputs a targeted clarification question instead of a function call; in multi-turn settings it accumulates parameters across 2–4 turns before emitting the final executable call. All outputs are validated against production safety rules (e.g., state-dependent constraints, forbidden action-parameter combinations, causal preconditions) via JSON-schema-constrained decoding at inference.

## Method

ClarifyVC introduces a four-stage agent-orchestrated data augmentation pipeline (SPA → AGA → FIA → MEA) seeded from 20k+ real in-vehicle commands drawn from 4M+ production logs, where each stage is implemented with prompt-engineered LLMs (DeepSeek-R1 for parsing/fuzzing/multi-turn, Qwen2.5-72B for protocol-constrained adversarial rewriting) without task-specific fine-tuning. The pipeline progressively transforms a raw command into structured grounding, then an adversarial variant, then a fuzzed instruction with controlled ambiguity (parameter omission, subjective modifiers, mild distortion), and finally a coherent multi-turn dialogue, with each sample scored by a weighted combination of ambiguity entropy and protocol compliance. Reference models (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B-SFT) are trained via supervised fine-tuning on the resulting 20k-sample ClarifyVC-Data with teacher-forced cross-entropy and JSON-schema-constrained decoding. A three-tier evaluation protocol (single-round fuzzy parsing, extreme-fuzz counter-questioning, multi-turn dialogue grounding) with nine metrics (IRA, PEP, FDR, CQC, DC, FESR, IHR, FHR, PCR) disentangles distinct failure families, while a Dataset Quality Score (DQS) aggregating ambiguity diversity, protocol compliance, and realism audits the benchmark itself against real-log distributions.

## Results

Fine-tuning on ClarifyVC-Data yields consistent gains across 12 open-source LLMs: the Qwen2.5-7B-SFT model achieves 89.0% Single-Round Accuracy, 87.6% Fuzzy Detection Rate, and 85.4% Multi-Turn Consistency (SFT), surpassing its zero-shot baseline (74.3/72.0/70.2) by 14.7/15.6/15.2 pp respectively. In advanced OOD scenarios, Qwen2.5-7B-SFT attains a Final Execution Success Rate (FESR) of 92.0%, outperforming Claude 3 by 4.6 pp, and reaches 95.4% Intent Recognition Accuracy in safety-critical tasks (7.4 pp above GPT-4). The dataset itself scores 0.88 on the Dataset Quality Score (DQS), exceeding the next-best baseline (LLaMA3 Distilled at 0.70) by 0.18, with human validation ratings of 4.5–4.7/5 across realism, ambiguity plausibility, coherence, and applicability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed Qwen2.5-7B-SFT (ClarifyVC-Model) outperforms all 12 compared systems—spanning proprietary (GPT-4, GPT-4o, OpenAI-o1, Claude 3, Doubao) and open-source (Qwen2.5-72B, LLaMA3-70B, DeepSeek-R1-70B) models—across all four function-call metrics (IHR, FHR, PEP, PCR) on the 4k protocol-constrained test set. In advanced OOD scenarios, it achieves 95.4% IRA in Safety-critical tasks (7.4 pp above GPT-4), 99.2% CQC in Entertainment (24.2 pp above Qwen2.5-72B), 93.0% DC in Autonomous Driving (5.0 pp above the next best), and 97.5% FESR in Comfort (8+ pp above Claude 3). GPT-4's intent accuracy drops from 92.5% on APIGen to 70.5% on ClarifyVC-Data, and its multi-turn success rate falls from 77.5% to 61.7%, highlighting the benchmark's increased difficulty. Among open-source baselines, SFT on ClarifyVC-Data consistently yields 10–15 pp gains over zero-shot across all model sizes, with the 7B model achieving the best accuracy-to-compute trade-off. On dataset quality (DQS), ClarifyVC-Data (0.88) surpasses Talk2Car (0.62), doScenes (0.65), CI-AVSR (0.64), and all distilled baselines (0.65–0.70) by 0.14–0.26 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of vehicle command parsing and controlled data augmentation. Prior vehicle-control benchmarks (Talk2Car, CI-AVSR, doScenes) provide single-turn instruction-action pairs but lack explicit ambiguity handling, multi-turn clarification, and safety-protocol compliance as first-class evaluation dimensions. The agent-orchestrated pipeline (SPA→AGA→FIA→MEA) repurposes general-purpose LLMs as specialized rewriting agents under schema constraints, departing from both purely synthetic generators (APIGen) and purely log-derived datasets by seeding from 4M+ production interactions and then applying structured adversarial/fuzz perturbations. The three-tier evaluation protocol extends the standard single-turn function-call accuracy metric into a layered assessment that disentangles under-specification, conservative clarification, and long-horizon grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For safety-critical in-vehicle voice assistants, the ability to detect ambiguity and ask clarifying questions rather than hallucinate parameters is operationally essential, yet prior benchmarks largely ignore this failure mode. The three-tier protocol and DQS metric offer a reusable template for auditing both dataset quality and model behavior in structured-interaction domains beyond automotive (smart homes, medical dialogue, embodied agents). Key limitations include reliance on proprietary OEM logs (limiting full reproducibility), single-modality text-only evaluation, and the fact that the 7B SFT model's dominance over proprietary LLMs, while impressive, is a well-known consequence of domain-specific fine-tuning on a narrow task. Open questions include whether the pipeline generalizes to multimodal (audio+visual) in-cabin signals and whether the clarification strategies transfer to domains with less rigid schema constraints.
