## Problem

Existing KV cache compression methods for large vision-language models (LVLMs) rely solely on importance scores to select which KV pairs to retain, overlooking the fact that LVLM KV caches exhibit substantially higher semantic redundancy than LLMs and that this redundancy varies significantly across attention heads. Importance-only selection preferentially retains semantically similar KV pairs, failing to cover the full information distribution of the original cache and causing loss of global semantic coverage, especially under extreme compression budgets.

## Input / Output

MixKV operates on the KV pairs (keys and values per layer and head) computed during the pre-filling phase of an LVLM that has ingested an image/video plus a text instruction. It produces a compressed KV cache by retaining only the top-B highest-scoring KV pairs per head, which is then used during auto-regressive decoding to generate the text response. The method is plug-and-play: it modifies only the evaluation/scoring function within existing compression operators (SnapKV, PyramidKV, AdaKV, SparseMM) without altering the compression operator itself, and the per-sample redundancy computation adds less than 1% latency overhead.

## Method

MixKV jointly optimizes importance and diversity for KV cache compression through a head-wise adaptive mixing mechanism. Importance is computed as the sum of extrinsic scores (average attention from an observation window) and intrinsic scores (VNorm of value vectors, normalized and scaled to match attention magnitudes). Diversity is measured as the negative cosine similarity between each normalized key and the global mean key within the same head, computed in linear time. The key insight is that semantic redundancy varies per head: MixKV quantifies this via the off-diagonal average cosine similarity of normalized keys (derived efficiently from the squared norm of the mean vector), then uses this redundancy value r as an adaptive weight: s_comp = (1−r)·s_imp + r·s_scaled_div. High-redundancy heads (r→1) prioritize diverse KV pairs to prevent redundant retention, while low-redundancy heads (r→0) emphasize importance-based selection, achieving fine-grained joint optimization without any offline statistics or architectural changes.

## Results

Under extreme KV cache compression (budget=64), MixKV improves baseline methods by an average of 5.1% across five multi-modal understanding benchmarks (DocVQA, OCRBench, TextVQA, ChartQA, TextCaps) on Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct. On the ScreenSpot-v2 GUI grounding benchmark, MixKV yields remarkable gains of +8.0% (SnapKV) and +9.0% (AdaKV) in average precision at budget=64. The improvements are consistent across three LVLM architectures (LLaVA-NeXT-Mistral-7B, InternVL3-8B, Qwen2-VL-7B) and extend to pure-text LLMs (Mistral-7B, Llama-3.1-8B) with comparable average gains on LongBench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MixKV is a plug-and-play enhancement that consistently outperforms all four primary baselines. Against SnapKV, MixKV delivers the largest absolute gains (e.g., +3.6% on OCRBench, +2.6% on TextVQA at budget=64 for Qwen2-VL-7B), and the most dramatic improvement on GUI grounding (+8.0% average on ScreenSpot-v2). Against PyramidKV, gains are slightly smaller but still consistent across all benchmarks (+0.9 to +2.0% at budget=64). Against AdaKV, improvements are moderate but uniform (+0.7 to +3.4%), with the standout +9.0% gain on GUI grounding. Against SparseMM (which already allocates asymmetric head budgets), MixKV provides smaller but still positive gains on most benchmarks, with the exception of minor drops on some OCRBench sub-scores for LLaVA-NeXT-Mistral-7B (−1.1% at budget=256). Against HeadKV (a head-level retrieval method evaluated in the appendix), MixKV yields consistent improvements, especially at extreme compression (budget=128), where gains reach +1.79% on HotpotQA and +0.92% on DocQA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the importance-based KV cache compression paradigm established by SnapKV, AdaKV, PyramidKV, and SparseMM, which select KV pairs to retain based on attention scores or vector norms. The key departure is the identification of head-wise semantic redundancy in LVLMs—where visual tokens create substantially higher intra-head similarity than text tokens—and the argument that importance-only selection systematically under-covers the KV distribution in high-redundancy heads. MixKV positions itself as a plug-and-play scoring-layer modification that adds a diversity term (negative cosine similarity to the head's mean key) adaptively weighted by per-head redundancy, rather than proposing a new compression operator or architectural change.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KV cache memory is a primary bottleneck for deploying LVLMs on high-resolution images, long videos, and edge-side GUI agents, making any efficiency-preserving compression method with consistent quality gains practically valuable. The plug-and-play nature (modifying only the evaluation function, adding <1% latency) lowers adoption barriers significantly. However, the paper is limited to models up to 8B parameters, leaving open whether head-wise redundancy patterns and the adaptive mixing strategy generalize to 70B+ or MoE architectures at scale. A broader open question is whether the importance-diversity tradeoff identified here extends to other cache-related decisions (e.g., quantization bit allocation, speculative decoding cache management).
