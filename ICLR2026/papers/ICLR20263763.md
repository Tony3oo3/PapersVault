## Problem

Prior work on AI assistants has advanced proactivity (initiating helpful suggestions) and personalization (adapting to individual users) independently, but their combination remains underexplored. Without personalization, proactive suggestions risk being misaligned with user preferences; without proactivity, even personalized support still requires the user to initiate every interaction. This paper introduces ProPerSim, a simulation-based benchmark, to develop and evaluate assistants that are simultaneously proactive and personalized in realistic home scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263763/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each 2.5-minute timestep, the assistant receives the user's current action and its internal state S_t^(a), which comprises a structured summary of the day's interactions (recent 10 minutes stored in detail, earlier interactions compressed into four 1-hour and three 4-hour blocks) plus the five most similar past (action, recommendation) pairs retrieved via OpenAI embeddings. The output is either a context-aware recommendation or an explicit 'No Recommendation' response. A user agent (built on an enhanced Generative Agents architecture with Big Five personality traits and six additional persona attributes) evaluates each output on four personalized rubric dimensions—Personal Preference, Frequency, Timing, and Communication & Safety—using Gemini 2.0 Flash as a judge, producing a 0–4 score per timestep.

## Method

ProPerAssistant is built on 4-bit quantized LLaMA 3.3 70B and combines retrieval-augmented generation with iterative preference learning. At each timestep it generates n=2 candidate recommendations (including a possible 'No Recommendation') conditioned on its internal state; the user agent scores both, and the resulting preference pair (chosen vs. rejected, along with A_t and S_t^(a)) is appended to a training buffer. At the end of each simulation day, the model is fine-tuned via Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) with LoRA on 200 randomly sampled pairs from the accumulated buffer, inspired by replay-buffer strategies in reinforcement learning. The user agent's evaluation rubric is personalized per persona using GPT-4o, and the four evaluation criteria were validated through a 353-participant Amazon Mechanical Turk survey. Across 32 diverse personas over 14 simulated days, ProPerAssistant improves from an average score of 2.2 to 3.3 out of 4, substantially outperforming baselines that rely on in-context memory or reward signals without explicit preference training.

## Results

On the ProPerSim benchmark (32 personas, 14-day simulation), ProPerAssistant improves its average recommendation score from 2.25/4 on Day 1 to 3.38/4 by Day 14, while all baselines remain flat or slightly decline. The success rate of proactive interventions (score ≥ 3) rises from 51.06% to 71.51% over the same period. Recommendation frequency drops from ~24/hour to ~6/hour, indicating the assistant learns to be more selective. The best-performing persona reaches ~3.8, while the worst stays around 2.5, reflecting the difficulty of modeling complex, context-sensitive preferences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProPerAssistant (DPO-trained) decisively outperforms all three baselines by Day 14, reaching 3.38 vs. 2.54 (ARS Memory), 2.25 (AR Memory), and 2.20 (No Memory). The ARS Memory baseline, which injects reward scores directly into the prompt without any training, starts highest on Day 1 (2.62) but plateaus and even slightly declines, confirming that in-context reward signals alone are insufficient for sustained preference alignment. AR Memory (action-recommendation history without scores) performs nearly identically to No Memory (Δ ≈ 0.01), demonstrating that implicit interaction history without explicit feedback provides negligible benefit. The ordering ARS > AR ≈ No Memory on Day 1, combined with ProPerAssistant's large Day 14 gap, isolates the contribution of explicit DPO preference training as the key driver of improvement. The paper also notes that ProPerAssistant's advantage is consistent across all 32 personas, with no persona where a baseline surpasses it by Day 14.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads that have progressed largely independently: proactive agents (Lu et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024) that initiate interactions, and personalized agents (Dai et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2024) that adapt to individual users. It builds directly on the Generative Agents simulation architecture (Park et al., 2023) for the user-agent environment and on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) for preference alignment, but departs from prior proactive-agent work by grounding proactivity in discrete time steps rather than user-triggered events, and from prior personalization work by requiring the assistant to decide *when* to intervene, not just *what* to say. The key conceptual contribution is unifying these two axes into a single optimization objective evaluated by a persona-conditioned rubric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProPerSim provides the first simulation benchmark that jointly evaluates proactivity and personalization, addressing a gap the authors identify as 'underexplored' in the literature. The finding that explicit DPO training outperforms simply placing reward scores in-context (ARS Memory baseline), and that action-recommendation history without scores offers little benefit over no memory, gives practitioners concrete guidance on how to structure preference learning for assistants. Limitations include the narrow home-environment scope, reliance on LLM-as-judge for evaluation, immediate (non-delayed) rewards, and a modest persona set of 32; open questions include whether the framework generalizes to multi-room or public settings, how it handles noisy or delayed real-world feedback, and whether the DPO-based adaptation can scale to truly dynamic, shifting user preferences over weeks or months.
