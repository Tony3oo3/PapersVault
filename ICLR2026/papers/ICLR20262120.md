## Problem

Personalized video generation faces a fundamental trade-off between identity consistency and action realism: rigid identity preservation produces unnatural 'copy-paste' motion, while emphasizing dynamics compromises subject fidelity. The paper identifies three root causes—noisy subject-video correspondences in training data, unstable convergence from mixed-difficulty samples, and coarse-grained action modeling that lacks temporal structure—and addresses all three simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262120/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes one or more reference images (cropped from subject bounding boxes) and either a single natural-language prompt or a structured timeline consisting of multiple sub-actions, each annotated with precise start/end timestamps and a textual motion description. For single-prompt inference, an LLM automatically decomposes the prompt into a timeline. The output is a 5-second, 720P+ personalized video clip in which the reference subject performs the specified action sequence while maintaining identity across frames. The model is built on Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B and generates a 5-second clip in approximately 316 seconds on a single GPU.

## Method

ReactID integrates three coordinated advances. First, ReactID-Data is constructed via a pipeline combining taxonomy-guided NER, MLLM-based (Florence-2) subject detection with SigLIP post-verification, and SAM2 segmentation, yielding high-precision subject-video correspondences and fine-grained entity labels. Second, a difficulty-aware curriculum learning strategy quantifies sample difficulty along three axes—subject spatial proportion, DINOv2/ArcFace appearance similarity, and intra- vs. inter-clip sampling—and progressively raises a difficulty threshold over four training phases to avoid both copy-paste overfitting and underfitting on hard samples. Third, a timeline-based conditioning mechanism injects structured multi-action sequences into the DiT via two novel components: a subject-aware cross-attention module that predicts per-subject masks and applies label-dependent phase modulation to bind each sub-action prompt to its corresponding subject's video tokens, and a temporally-adaptive RoPE that rescales the temporal axis per sub-action duration (normalizing each interval to a fixed length T) so that attention bias is invariant to varying action lengths, preventing misalignment at sub-action boundaries.

## Results

On OpenS2V-Eval (Human Domain), ReactID achieves a TotalScore of 62.17%, surpassing the strongest baseline Phantom-1.3B (60.00%) by 2.17%. On the Single Domain, ReactID reaches 56.04% TotalScore, leading VACE-1.3B (51.13%) and Phantom-1.3B (54.50%). On the sequential-action benchmark ReactID-Eval-SEQ, ReactID attains 54.42% TotalScore, outperforming the best competitor Phantom-1.3B (51.40%) by 3.02%. ReactID also leads on Motion Amplitude (40.79% vs. 14.09% for Phantom) and Motion Smoothness (96.46% vs. 92.02%) in the Human Domain, while trailing Phantom by ~2% on Face Similarity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReactID outperforms all six baselines on TotalScore across every evaluation domain. Against VACE-1.3B, ReactID gains +7.27% TotalScore in the Human Domain (62.17% vs. 54.90%), driven primarily by a much higher Motion Amplitude (40.79% vs. 16.87%) and Motion Smoothness (96.46% vs. 95.84%), while VACE struggles with subject consistency (FaceSim 22.29% vs. 44.08%). Against Phantom-1.3B, the closest competitor, ReactID leads by 2.17% in the Human Domain and 3.02% on ReactID-Eval-SEQ, with the gap concentrated in Motion Amplitude (40.79% vs. 14.09%) and Motion Smoothness (96.46% vs. 92.02%); Phantom retains a slight edge on FaceSim (46.29% vs. 44.08%), which the authors attribute to ReactID's more dynamic motion introducing mild motion blur. On the older UNet-based baselines (ID-Animator, ConsisID, EchoVideo, FantasyID), ReactID leads by 5.8–12.4% TotalScore, with the largest margins on Motion Amplitude and Naturalness, confirming that the DiT-based architecture combined with timeline conditioning yields substantially more natural and diverse actions than prior identity-preserving approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReactID builds on the Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B DiT backbone and Res-Tuning for reference-image feature injection, situating itself within the recent shift from UNet-based personalized video generation (DreamVideo, CustomCrafter, MagicMe) to DiT-based approaches (Phantom, VACE, ConceptMaster). Unlike prior methods that address identity preservation or motion quality in isolation, ReactID simultaneously tackles three identified bottlenecks—noisy subject-video correspondences, unstable training from heterogeneous sample difficulty, and coarse-grained action modeling—through coordinated data, curriculum, and architectural contributions. The timeline-based conditioning mechanism with subject-aware cross-attention and temporally-adaptive RoPE represents a departure from the monolithic text-prompt paradigm used by all compared baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of ReactID-Data with temporally-precise multi-action annotations addresses a recognized scarcity of high-quality training data for fine-grained action control, providing a foundation for future research beyond this specific model. The timeline-based paradigm demonstrates a scalable path toward controllable, multi-action video synthesis that could extend to longer sequences and more complex multi-subject interactions. Limitations include the 5-second generation window, 1.3B parameter scale, and an acknowledged trade-off where higher motion amplitude slightly reduces face similarity due to motion blur. Open questions include whether the curriculum learning strategy and timeline conditioning transfer effectively to larger models or fundamentally different architectures.
