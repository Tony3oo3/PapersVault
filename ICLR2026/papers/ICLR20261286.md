## Problem

Most adversarial attacks on spiking neural networks modify spike intensities or add/delete events, leaving the timing dimension of event-driven SNNs largely unexplored as an attack surface. This paper identifies that a timing-only adversary—retiming existing spikes while preserving their counts and amplitudes—is physically realistic (matching sensor jitter and readout latency), evades standard intensity- or rate-based detection, and directly stresses the temporal computation that SNNs rely on, yet no unified formulation or scalable solver existed for this threat.

## Input / Output

The method takes a binned event stream of shape T×C×H×W (binary {0,1} or nonnegative integer grid, with T=10 time bins by default) and a ground-truth label, and produces an adversarial retimed event stream of identical shape where each spike is shifted to a new time bin along its event line. The output must satisfy capacity-1 (at most one spike per event-line/time-bin), timeline consistency (0 ≤ t < T), and a chosen budget constraint (B_∞ per-spike jitter, B_1 total timing shift, or B_0 tamper count). Evaluation reports attack success rate (ASR) over correctly classified test samples, with the key constraint that no spike is created, deleted, or split—only its timestamp changes.

## Method

The core contribution is projected-in-the-loop (PIL) optimization over shift logits: each active spike (s,j) is assigned tempered softmax probabilities π[s,j,u] over admissible target times, from which a differentiable soft shift operator S_π(x) computes the expected retiming for backpropagation. A strict greedy projection P* then enforces capacity-1, non-overlap, and the exact budget by score-ordered one-pass placement, producing a feasible discrete schedule for the forward pass. The PIL coupling (Eq. 13) evaluates the SNN on the strictly projected input while differentiating through the soft retiming, yielding stable gradients without violating the threat model. The objective maximizes task loss on the projected input and adds a capacity regularizer (penalizing expected over-occupancy beyond unit capacity) plus a budget-aware hinge penalty that keeps gradients comparable across radii. Shift logits are updated via clipped sign-PGD steps, and the same formulation applies uniformly to B_∞, B_1, and B_0 budgets on both binary and integer grids without modification.

## Results

On binary-grid DVS, the spike-retiming attack achieves near-saturation ASR (≥96%) under B∞(1) across all three datasets and most architectures, and reaches 98–100% ASR under B0(4k) on DVS-Gesture and CIFAR10-DVS while touching fewer than 2–4% of spikes. On integer-grid DVS, the attack remains effective but requires larger budgets: e.g., DVS-Gesture/ResNet18 reaches 93.3% ASR at B∞(3) and 98.1% at B0(8k), versus 100% and 98.5% on the binary grid at the same nominal budgets. Adversarial training with timing-only attacks (B1=8k) on the integer grid yields 68.75% clean accuracy and 20.09% average ASR, outperforming all non-timing AT baselines by 9–15 percentage points in average ASR at comparable clean accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against raw-event attack baselines SpikeFool (Büchel et al., 2022) and PDSG-SDA (Lun et al., 2025), the timing-only retiming attack is competitive and often stronger on DVS-Gesture and N-MNIST under matched B0 tamper-count budgets, despite operating under stricter capacity-1 and rate-preservation constraints that forbid adding or deleting spikes. For adversarial training, the paper compares timing-only AT against three non-timing AT baselines: ℓ∞ PGD AT (Madry et al., 2018), binary ℓ0 flip AT (Balkanski et al., 2020), and ℓ1-APGD AT (Croce & Hein, 2021). Timing AT with B1=8k achieves 68.75% clean accuracy and 20.09% average ASR, beating the best non-timing baseline (ℓ∞ PGD AT, ε=0.5: 64.24% clean, 30.69% Avg ASR) by ~10.6 points in Avg ASR at higher clean accuracy. Timing AT with B0=4k reaches 72.22% clean and 25.86% Avg ASR, again outperforming all non-timing baselines (which range from 30.69% to 37.45% Avg ASR) while maintaining clean accuracy above 72%. The binary ℓ0 flip AT baseline achieves the highest clean accuracy (77.78%) but at the cost of the worst average ASR (37.45%), confirming that non-timing defenses do not adequately protect against timing-only perturbations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial attack literature on SNNs, which has focused on modifying spike intensities or event counts (RGA, HART, SpikeFool, PDSG-SDA), by introducing a timing-only threat model that retimes existing spikes while strictly preserving counts and amplitudes. Methodologically, it adapts the PGD framework (Madry et al., 2018) to a discrete retiming space via projected-in-the-loop optimization, coupling a differentiable soft shift operator with a strict capacity-1 projection. The paper departs from prior work by formalizing three unified budgets (B∞, B1, B0) over spike timestamps and showing that the same formulation applies to both binary and integer event grids without modification, whereas prior event-data attacks were typically grid-specific.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that spike timing is a practical and stealthy attack surface that evades standard intensity- and rate-based defenses, with high success rates (>90%) while touching fewer than 2% of spikes on DVS-Gesture. A key open question is the lack of effective defenses: adversarial training with timing attacks substantially degrades clean accuracy (from ~95% to 23–72%), and simple filtering defenses barely reduce ASR without collapsing clean performance. The finding that integer grids are more robust than binary grids (due to multiplicity smoothing pre-activations and stabilizing normalization) provides a practical design guideline, but the broader challenge of certifying or training for timing robustness in event-driven SNNs remains unresolved.
