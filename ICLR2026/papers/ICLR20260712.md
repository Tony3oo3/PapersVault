## Problem

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) methods provide LLMs with external context, but the unstructured nature of retrieved passages forces models to implicitly bridge logical gaps, leading to brittle multi-step reasoning and hallucinations. Existing knowledge-graph-based approaches rely on static, corpus-wide graphs that are prohibitively expensive to build (tens of thousands to millions of USD) and introduce noise from irrelevant or contradictory relations. The paper addresses how to dynamically construct question-specific structured knowledge at inference time that supports interpretable multi-hop reasoning without offline indexing costs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language query (main question Q) and a dense-indexed text corpus (Wikipedia 2018/2020) as input, and produces a final answer (short-form, multiple-choice, or long-form) along with an evolving question-specific knowledge graph. The graph is structured as subject-predicate-object triples with Sentence-BERT node/edge embeddings, and it grows incrementally across up to five retrieval iterations. Unusually, the input is consumed iteratively: the model generates targeted subqueries that drive successive retrieval rounds, and the graph state at each iteration conditions the next planning decision, making the I/O pipeline stateful rather than single-pass.

## Method

RAS interleaves three stages in a loop: (1) a knowledge-aware planning step where a Graph LLM (Graph Transformer encoder + LLaMA backbone) inspects the current encoded KG and subquery-triples history to emit [SUBQ], [SUFFICIENT], or [NO_RETRIEVAL]; (2) a retrieval-and-structuring step where a dense retriever fetches top-k passages for the subquery and a lightweight text-to-triples model (LLaMA-3.2-3B trained on WikiOfGraph) converts them into triples that are merged into the evolving question-specific graph; and (3) a knowledge-augmented answering step that generates the final output conditioned on the GNN-encoded graph and the full subquery chain. The Graph LLM is trained via multi-task next-token prediction (jointly on planning and answering) with LoRA applied to the graph components, enabling a single model to handle both roles. The key architectural distinction from prior graph-RAG work is that the KG is built on-demand per query rather than precomputed corpus-wide, eliminating offline indexing costs while providing denser, task-relevant relational context that makes multi-hop reasoning pathways explicit and interpretable.

## Results

Across seven knowledge-intensive benchmarks, RAS achieves up to 8.7% average improvement with proprietary LLMs (Claude-3.5-Sonnet) and 7.0% with open-source LLMs (LLaMA-2-7B/LLaMA-3-8B) over the strongest baselines. Specifically, RAS_7B reaches 72.7% accuracy on TriviaQA, 42.1 F1 on 2WikiMultihopQA, and 95.2 MAUVE on ASQA, while RAS_Sonnet-3.5 achieves 62.3% on PopQA, 93.9% on ARC-Challenge, and 70.5 MAUVE on ASQA. The largest single-metric gains are on 2WikiMultihopQA (+17.0 F1 over Self-RAG_7B) and PubHealth (+17.4% over Sonnet-3.5 with single retrieval).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAS outperforms all five baselines on the majority of metrics. Against Self-RAG (Asai et al., 2023), RAS_7B gains +6.3 acc on TriviaQA, +17.0 F1 on 2WikiMultihopQA, and +20.9 MAUVE on ASQA, with the largest advantage on multi-hop reasoning. Against RPG (Lyu et al., 2024b), RAS_7B leads by +7.6 acc on TriviaQA, +8.5 F1 on 2WikiMultihopQA, and +10.8 MAUVE on ASQA, though RPG slightly edges RAS on ASQA ROUGE (37.6 vs. 37.2). Against SuRe (Kim et al., 2024b), RAS_Sonnet-3.5 dominates across nearly all metrics (e.g., +20.1 F1 on 2WQA, +21.1 acc on PopQA, +8.5 acc on PubHealth), indicating that structured graph reasoning substantially outperforms retrieve-and-summarize. Against ReAct (Yao et al., 2023) and IRCoT (Trivedi et al., 2023), RAS_Sonnet-3.5 achieves +4.2 to +17.4 points across datasets, with the most pronounced gains on PubHealth (+9.1 to +11.9) and PopQA (+7.3 to +9.1), confirming that iterative knowledge-graph construction is more effective than interleaved retrieval with chain-of-thought for knowledge-intensive tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAS builds directly on the Graph LLM architecture from Perozzi et al. (2024) and the graph-based retrieval paradigm of G-Retriever (He et al., 2024), adapting them to a dynamic, query-specific setting. It departs from static corpus-wide knowledge graph approaches (GraphRAG, HippoRAG) by constructing question-specific graphs on the fly, and from iterative RAG methods (Self-RAG, RPG, ReAct, IRCoT) by explicitly converting retrieved passages into structured triples that accumulate into an evolving graph rather than passing raw text to the LLM. The framework unifies planning and answering in a single multitask-trained model, extending the self-reflective retrieval idea with an explicit graph-encoding step via a Graph Transformer encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAS offers a practical path to graph-enhanced RAG without the prohibitive offline indexing costs of static KG methods (estimated $85k–$500k for GraphRAG at Wikipedia scale vs. negligible for RAS), making structured reasoning accessible in settings where large-scale preprocessing is impractical. The role-swapping study revealing that answering capability, not planning, is the primary performance bottleneck is a useful diagnostic for practitioners. Key open questions include the sensitivity to triple extraction quality (a dedicated NER-RE pipeline could help), the lack of graph pruning/summarization strategies for long iterative chains, and untested cross-domain and multilingual generalization. Integration with curated external KGs (e.g., Wikidata) is identified as a promising but unexplored direction.
