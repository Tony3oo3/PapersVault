## Problem

Existing robot policy watermarking methods assume white-box access to internal states (action logs, trigger environments), making them unusable when an auditor can only observe the robot remotely via video or external sensors. This 'Physical Observation Gap' introduces synchronization uncertainty, unknown system dynamics that filter and distort the signal, and heavy interference from the policy's primary behavior—challenges that destroy traditional watermarking signatures. No prior method could verify policy provenance using purely remote, passive observations.

## Input / Output

For detection, the method consumes a glimpse sequence G (a time series of remote observations such as video-derived velocity estimates, motion-capture joint angles, or onboard sensor readings sampled at frequency f_g), a secret key K = {seed S, frequency band B = [f_min, f_max]}, and a search range of candidate policy execution frequencies. It produces a scalar detection score: the maximum over frequency hypotheses of the mean spectral coherency magnitude within B, computed via Welch's method. For watermark injection, the method takes a pre-trained stochastic RL policy (Gaussian action distribution) and replaces its exploration noise at inference time, producing watermarked actions without retraining.

## Method

CoNoCo (Colored Noise Coherency) embeds the watermark by replacing the policy's standard White Gaussian Noise exploration term with Colored Gaussian Noise—WGN filtered through a digital bandpass filter tuned to a secret frequency band B, then normalized to unit variance. This preserves the marginal action distribution (Theorem 5.1) while concentrating spectral energy in B, outside the policy's primary behavior spectrum. Detection uses Spectral Coherency between the regenerated watermark (resampled to the glimpse rate for each candidate policy frequency) and the observed glimpse sequence; the critical insight is that coherency magnitude is invariant to any LTI system dynamics (Theorem 5.2), so a watermark injected at the torque-command level remains detectable from velocity observations without knowing the robot's physics. Synchronization uncertainty is handled by maximizing the detection score over a grid of candidate execution frequencies, with an optional GCC-PHAT extension for arbitrary time offsets. The approach is applied at inference time only, requiring no changes to RL training, and is validated across simulated (VMAS, MuJoCo) and real-world (RoboMaster) platforms with remote camera and motion-capture modalities.

## Results

CoNoCo achieves near-perfect detectability (ROC AUC ≈ 1.0) across all tested environments (VMAS Navigation, Inverted Pendulum, HalfCheetah, RoboMaster) and all remote modalities (Motion Capture, top-down/side-way Camera Feed), with detection saturating at roughly 1000 glimpse timesteps. On the real-world RoboMaster platform (40 replications, 50 s each), CoNoCo maintains high detectability while preserving anonymity and reward, with watermarked trajectories visually indistinguishable from the original. CoNoCo remains robust to time jitter (real-world jitter = 0.002; robust well beyond), up to 200 dropped frames, and camera angle shifts up to 60° on both axes. Under adversarial additive noise, detection persists in HalfCheetah up to σ_adv = 2 (where the policy itself is destroyed), and in Inverted Pendulum even σ_adv = 0.25 simultaneously destroys both detection and policy reward.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CoNoCo against three adapted baselines: Multi-Sine Wave, Correlation-Based, and Tournament-Based. Multi-Sine Wave matches CoNoCo on raw detectability (high ROC AUC) but critically fails on anonymity — its watermark is easily detected with an incorrect secret key, making it unsuitable for ownership verification. Correlation-Based and Tournament-Based achieve better anonymity scores but suffer from poor detectability, rendering them infeasible for practical remote auditing. Only CoNoCo simultaneously achieves high detectability and high anonymity across all environments and modalities, including the real-world RoboMaster deployment where other baselines degrade further. This dual property (detectable by the rightful owner, undetectable by others) is the core requirement for IP protection, and no baseline satisfies it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of signal processing and robotics IP protection. Prior policy watermarking methods (Behzadan & Hsu, 2019; Chen et al., 2021) and CPS dynamic watermarking (Satchidanandan & Kumar, 2016) all assume white-box access to internal states or control signals, making them inapplicable when an auditor can only observe the robot externally. CoNoCo repurposes classical DSP concepts—colored Gaussian noise for embedding and spectral coherency for detection—into a robotics context, leveraging the LTI invariance property of coherency (Theorem 5.2) to 'see through' unknown robot dynamics. The formalization of the Physical Observation Gap and glimpse sequences is a new problem framing that has no direct predecessor in the watermarking literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method enabling non-invasive verification of robot policy provenance from purely external observations, with direct applications to safety compliance auditing and post-incident forensics where onboard logs may be unavailable or tampered with. Key limitations include the requirement for stochastic (not deterministic) policies, the need for sufficient unobstructed camera visibility, and the assumption that the policy execution rate falls within known bounds. Open questions include extending the framework to deterministic policies, handling partial occlusion in cluttered real-world environments, and scaling to multi-robot scenarios where multiple watermarked policies may be active simultaneously.
