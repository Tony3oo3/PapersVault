## Problem

Existing cortical representation learning methods are trained on narrow age cohorts and cannot generalize across the full human lifespan. Three structural obstacles prevent a unified framework: the non-Euclidean manifold geometry of cortical surfaces, the homogenization of individual folding patterns induced by template registration, and distributional shifts in cortical features (thickness, curvature, sulcal depth) across developmental stages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263889/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes cortical surface maps (cortical thickness, mean curvature, or sulcal depth) with 81,924 vertices (40,962 per hemisphere) and a structured metadata text string encoding age, sex, health status, and feature type. The surface is partitioned into 640 triangular patches (320 per hemisphere) via icosahedron-based subdivision before tokenization. Outputs include a global cortical representation embedding (used for age prediction and parcellation via a frozen MLP head), DKT-40 parcellation maps, and binary disease classification labels (ACC/AUC) after fine-tuning.

## Method

CortiLife introduces a surface tokenizer that combines icosahedron-based triangular patchification with a three-stream multi-level encoder: a Spherical ResBlock for local topology, a 4-layer self-attention module for global cross-patch interactions, and a scale-adaptive encoder that projects per-patch mean/std statistics onto learnable multi-scale bases with log-inverse gating to unify cross-age distributions while preserving individual variability. For pretraining, a masked self-distillation teacher-student framework is used where the teacher's [CLS]-token attention scores (guided by metadata language prompting) select the top 25% most developmentally informative patches for the student to reconstruct, replacing random masking. A CLIP-style contrastive loss aligns the student's global embedding with PubMedBERT-encoded metadata text, and the total objective combines reconstruction loss, KL-divergence alignment loss, and the contrastive loss.

## Results

On encoder-frozen tasks (HCP), CortiLife achieves MAE of 3.124/2.990/3.006 years for age prediction (CT/MC/SD) and DICE of 0.905/0.925/0.957 for cortical parcellation, surpassing the best baseline (DetailCLIP) by 0.031 MAE and 0.120–0.121 DICE. On encoder fine-tuning disease classification, CortiLife reaches 80.6% ACC / 82.3% AUC on CHD (CT), 69.7% / 73.0% on ADHD (CT), and 92.8% / 98.1% on ADNI (CT), consistently outperforming all 10 baselines across three cortical modalities (CT, MC, SD) and four age stages (infancy through aging). On the SD modality, gains are largest: 73.9% ACC on CHD (vs. 69.6% for DetailCLIP) and 97.2% on ADNI (vs. 95.6% for CLIP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CortiLife outperforms all 10 baselines (5 non-pretrained cortical models + 4 pretrained VLMs + 1 non-pretrained transformer) across every modality and age stage. On CT, it beats the strongest pretrained baseline CLIP by 1.6% ACC on CHD (0.806 vs. 0.790), 1.6% on ADHD (0.697 vs. 0.681), and 0.2% on ADNI (0.928 vs. 0.926); on MC the gap widens to 2.0% on CHD (0.667 vs. 0.647 SurfaceViT) and 1.1% on ADNI (0.939 vs. 0.928 CARZero); on SD it leads by 4.3% on CHD (0.739 vs. 0.696 DetailCLIP) and 1.6% on ADNI (0.972 vs. 0.956 CLIP). Among pretrained VLM baselines, DetailCLIP and CARZero show the weakest transfer to cortical surfaces (e.g., CARZero drops to 0.628 ACC on ADHD-CT, below even non-pretrained SphericalCNN at 0.674), suggesting that standard 2D/3D VLM architectures struggle with non-Euclidean cortical geometry. On encoder-frozen parcellation (HCP), CortiLife achieves 0.905/0.925/0.957 DICE (CT/MC/SD), exceeding DetailCLIP by 0.120/0.121/0.038, confirming that the surface tokenizer and multi-level encoding yield substantially more spatially faithful representations than grid-based VLM adaptations.

## Relation to Prior Work

CortiLife sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) cortical surface deep learning (Spherical CNN, Spherical U-Net, Surface Vision Transformer), which has been limited to narrow age cohorts, and (2) vision-language contrastive pretraining (CLIP, BiomedCLIP, CARZero), which has been applied to 2D/3D Euclidean medical images but not to non-Euclidean cortical manifolds. The paper departs from both by introducing an icosahedron-based surface tokenizer with a multi-level encoding (spherical CNN for local topology, self-attention for global interactions, and a novel scale-adaptive encoder for distributional statistics) and by coupling it with a masked self-distillation teacher-student objective and PubMedBERT-based metadata prompting. The closest prior work in cortical representation (Dahan et al. 2022; Zhao et al. 2019/2021) lacks the cross-lifespan generalization, while the closest VLM work (CLIP, ACLIP, DetailCLIP, CARZero) lacks any mechanism for non-Euclidean surface geometry or lifespan-aware distributional alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to learn a single cortical representation that generalizes from 26-week fetuses to 95-year-old adults across three morphological modalities, which is practically important for building unified brain-aging biomarkers and cross-age disease classifiers without retraining per cohort. The scale-adaptive encoder's ability to reduce MMD between age groups (e.g., 1.029 → 0.933 for 1-3 vs. 55-95 years) and the teacher-guided masking that selects age-specific developmental regions (Figure 6) offer mechanistic insight into how lifespan heterogeneity can be handled. Open questions include whether the template-registration step (which the paper acknowledges causes homogenization) can be replaced by registration-free surface encoding, whether the simple template-based metadata prompting can be replaced by richer clinical narratives, and whether the framework extends to functional or multi-contrast MRI data. The reliance on FreeSurfer-processed surfaces and the DKT-40 parcellation also limits applicability to populations where surface reconstruction is unreliable (e.g., very premature infants, severe atrophy).
