## Problem

Diffusion language models (DLMs) remain slower than autoregressive models in practice because they require many iterative denoising steps and cannot leverage KV caching due to bidirectional attention. This paper identifies an overlooked property—early answer convergence—where the correct answer is internally stabilized well before the final decoding step (up to 99% of instances on MMLU converge by half the steps), and asks whether this can be exploited to terminate decoding early without quality loss.

## Input / Output

Prophet wraps around an existing DLM (LLaDA-8B or Dream-7B) and operates on the standard masked-token sequence during iterative denoising. A task-specific 'answer region' is defined structurally (e.g., tokens after a separator in GSM8K, a single multiple-choice token in MMLU, or the function body in code generation), and a suffix prompt like 'Answer:' is appended as a semantic anchor to accelerate convergence in that region. The output is the fully decoded text sequence, with all remaining [MASK] tokens filled in a single parallel argmax operation once the early-commit condition is met.

## Method

Prophet is a training-free early commit decoding strategy that recasts DLM inference as an optimal stopping problem over the answer region. At each denoising step, it computes the confidence gap (difference between the top-1 and top-2 logits) averaged over the answer region positions, then compares it against a staged threshold τ(p) that is high early in decoding (risk-averse, demanding strong evidence) and progressively lower later (risk-tolerant, since remaining savings diminish). When the average gap exceeds the threshold, the loop terminates immediately and all remaining masked tokens are filled in one parallel argmax pass. The key design choice is the time-varying risk-aversion schedule (τ_high=7.5, τ_mid=5.0, τ_low=2.5 at 33%/67% progress boundaries), which prevents premature commits on uncertain samples while aggressively exiting once the answer has stabilized. This mechanism is orthogonal to per-step cost reductions (KV caching, distillation), enabling multiplicative speedups when combined.

## Results

Across 12 benchmarks on LLaDA-8B and Dream-7B, Prophet achieves up to 3.40× reduction in decoding steps (Sudoku, Dream-7B) while matching or exceeding full-step accuracy in most cases (e.g., GSM8K: 77.9% vs. 77.1% on LLaDA-8B; HellaSwag: 70.9% vs. 68.7%). Speedups range from 1.20× on complex code tasks (HumanEval) to 2.67× on planning tasks (Countdown), with the largest gains on short-answer and planning benchmarks. On Dream-7B, Prophet delivers 2.47× speedup on MMLU (66.1% vs. 67.6%) and 3.40× on Sudoku (89.0% vs. 89.0%), confirming that the early-commit mechanism generalizes across model architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Prophet is compared against two orthogonal acceleration methods on GSM8K (LLaDA-8B). Against SDTT (self-distillation through time, a training-based 2× step reduction), Prophet alone achieves 77.9% accuracy at 1.63× speedup, slightly outperforming SDTT's 76.9% at 2.00×; combining both (SDTT+Prophet) yields 76.4% at 3.21×, demonstrating that distilled models retain the early-convergence property. Against Fast-dLLM (training-free KV-cache + parallel decoding, reducing per-step cost), Fast-dLLM alone reaches 76.6% at 6.82× speedup, while Fast-dLLM+Prophet achieves 77.3% at 7.66×—a multiplicative gain because Fast-dLLM reduces cost per step while Prophet reduces total steps. The key finding is that Prophet is complementary to both: it does not compete with them but stacks on top, and its accuracy (77.9%) actually exceeds both baselines individually, suggesting that early commit decoding can prevent late-stage over-refinement from corrupting already-correct predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaDA-8B and Dream-7B diffusion language model frameworks, operating within their iterative prediction-remasking decoding loop. It positions itself against the growing family of DLM acceleration methods—KV-cache optimization (Fast-dLLM, dKV-Cache), token pruning (DPad), and sampling/decoding optimization (SlowFast, WINO, SDTT)—by introducing a fundamentally different axis: reducing the total number of denoising steps rather than the cost per step. The key conceptual departure is framing DLM decoding as an optimal stopping problem over a designated answer region, using the top-2 confidence gap as a training-free termination signal, which is orthogonal to and composable with per-step cost reductions like Fast-dLLM's approximate KV caching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that up to 97–99% of instances converge to the correct answer by the halfway point of decoding challenges the implicit assumption that DLMs require the full denoising trajectory, and the practical upshot—a training-free, model-agnostic 3.4× step reduction that composes multiplicatively with existing accelerators—makes DLM inference substantially more competitive with autoregressive models. However, the method is scoped to tasks with identifiable answer regions (math, code, planning, multiple-choice), leaving open-ended generation where convergence signals are less clear as an unresolved challenge. Future directions include learnable termination criteria (a 'Judge Prophet'), system-level integration with KV-cache frameworks, and extending the convergence analysis to longer, less structured outputs.
