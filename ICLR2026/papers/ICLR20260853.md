## Problem

Standard RLVR, which rewards only the final answer, fails in long-context settings because the sparse outcome signal cannot effectively guide the model to identify relevant evidence within vast external documents. The paper formally proves that this outcome-only reward causes vanishing gradients for the contextual grounding process, making learning intractable as the number of prerequisite evidence chunks grows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260853/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a long context document (8K–64K tokens) segmented into 64 sentence-aware chunks, along with a question. It must first emit a list of grounding chunk identifiers (the subset of chunks containing relevant evidence) before generating the final answer. This two-stage output structure is what enables the verifiable context reward to be computed by comparing predicted chunk IDs against ground-truth annotations.

## Method

LongRLVR factorizes the policy into a grounding head (selecting relevant chunks Z from context X) and an answer head (producing y conditioned on Z), then augments the sparse answer reward with a dense, verifiable context reward based on the F_β-score (β=2) between predicted and ground-truth chunk sets. The total reward r_total = r_ans + η·F_β + (1−η)·r_ans·F_β ensures each correctly selected chunk receives positive gradient independent of whether all other evidence is present, provably resolving the vanishing-gradient bottleneck. Training uses GRPO with a synthetic dataset of 46K grounded QA pairs generated via a DBSCAN-based semantic clustering pipeline and two-stage LLM rejection sampling (Qwen3-235B-A22B as both generator and judge, retaining only pairs rated ≥9/10). Experiments on LLaMA-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-7B/14B-1M show consistent large gains over naive RLVR across RULER-QA, LongBench v2, and LongReason, with the 14B model surpassing Qwen3-14B on RULER-QA.

## Results

LongRLVR consistently and substantially outperforms both SFT and naive RLVR (GRPO) across all three model families and all benchmarks. The headline gains for Qwen2.5-14B-1M are RULER-QA AVG 73.17→88.90, LongBench v2 Overall 39.8→46.5, and LongReason Avg 72.33→78.42. For the smaller Qwen2.5-7B-1M, LongRLVR achieves 78.67 on RULER-QA AVG and 79.22 on LongReason Avg, surpassing the much larger LLaMA-3.1-70B (57.59) and Qwen2.5-72B-YaRN (72.76) on LongReason. The 14B model also edges out Qwen3-14B (Thinking) on RULER-QA (88.90 vs. 87.60) and LongReason (78.42 vs. 77.50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the larger conventional LLaMA-3.1-70B, the 7B LongRLVR model achieves 79.22 on LongReason vs. 57.59, a 21.6-point margin, and 78.67 vs. 60.73 on RULER-QA AVG. Against Qwen2.5-72B-YaRN, the 7B LongRLVR model again leads on LongReason (79.22 vs. 72.76) and RULER-QA (78.67 vs. 56.20), while the 14B LongRLVR model surpasses it on LongBench v2 (46.5 vs. 43.5). Against the specialized reasoning model Qwen3-14B (Thinking), the 14B LongRLVR model wins on RULER-QA (88.90 vs. 87.60), LongBench v2 (46.5 vs. 44.9), and LongReason (78.42 vs. 77.50), despite using a less advanced backbone. Against QwenLong-L1-32B (a 32B reasoning model trained with long-context RLVR), the 14B LongRLVR model is competitive: it leads on RULER-QA (88.90 vs. 79.47) and LongBench v2 (46.5 vs. 41.0) but trails slightly on LongReason (78.42 vs. 80.94). Overall, LongRLVR demonstrates that a 7–14B model trained with verifiable context rewards can match or exceed models 5–10× its size on long-context reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLVR paradigm (Lambert et al., 2024; Guo et al., 2025) into long-context settings, where prior work (Wan et al., 2025; Bai et al., 2024a) has applied RLVR or SFT for long-context alignment without diagnosing the specific optimization failure. The key departure is a formal proof that outcome-only rewards produce vanishing gradients for the contextual grounding sub-process, followed by a verifiable F-score-based context reward that provides dense per-chunk supervision. This positions the work between standard RLVR (which sharpens parametric knowledge) and retrieval-augmented reasoning (Wang et al., 2025), offering a principled reward design rather than an architectural or agentic workaround.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The vanishing-gradient theorem gives a clear, actionable diagnosis for why RLVR stalls on long-context tasks, and the fix—a simple additive context reward—is easy to integrate into existing GRPO/PPO pipelines, making it likely to be widely adopted. The demonstrated parameter efficiency (7B outperforming 70B/72B on LongReason) suggests that targeted grounding supervision can substitute for raw scale in long-context reasoning. Open questions include whether the synthetic data pipeline generalizes to domains beyond books/arXiv/code, whether the two-stage grounding-then-answering factorization is the optimal decomposition, and how the method scales beyond 128K contexts where the activation event probability may behave differently.
