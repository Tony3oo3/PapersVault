## Problem

Multi-scale windowed self-attention backbones for PDE foundation models (e.g., POSEIDON's scOT) are inherently local: global spectral coupling propagates only gradually through depth and window shifting, which weakens long-range consistency and induces error accumulation and drift during closed-loop autoregressive rollouts. Naive cross-branch or cross-scale concatenation to fix this inflates channel width and destabilizes training, while purely spectral operators overemphasize low-frequency structure and underfit nonstationary local details.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a PDE state field on a fixed 128×128 Cartesian grid (e.g., 2-channel velocity, 3-channel velocity+tracer, or scalar concentration) and predicts the next time-step state u_{t+1}. For Wave-Gauss, a static wave-speed channel is concatenated to the dynamic field at every step. Training uses single-step teacher forcing; at test time the one-step map is composed autoregressively in a closed loop to produce full trajectories up to a target time T*, with evaluation via mean relative L1 error at the final snapshot.

## Method

DRIFT-NET is a four-level U-Net encoder-decoder where each scale contains a dual-branch DRIFT block. The spectral branch applies rFFT2, restricts a learnable complex channel-mixing transform W to a learnable low-frequency rectangle (via a soft mask), and fuses the mixed low-frequency component with the high-frequency residual through a radial-band gating mechanism (per-band MLP producing α(k)∈[0,1] as a convex combination), then inverse-transforms back to the spatial domain. The image branch uses ConvNeXt-style blocks (depthwise 3×3 conv + 1×1 pointwise linear + MLP). The two branches are merged by addition (non-expansive, no concatenation), preserving feature width and acting as a residual correction that injects globally coupled low-k information while letting the image branch retain full control over high-frequency refinement. A frequency-weighted auxiliary loss (radial weight w(r)∝r^α on the rFFT2 of the prediction error) further counteracts spectral bias by emphasizing high-frequency discrepancies during optimization.

## Results

On the four POSEIDON Navier-Stokes benchmarks, DRIFT-NET achieves the lowest final-time relative L1 error across all compared architectures, reducing scOT's error by 7%–54% (e.g., 1.09 vs. 2.35 on NS-PwC, 3.40 vs. 3.96 on NS-SL) while using ~15% fewer parameters (17M vs. 20M) and higher inference throughput (158 vs. 118 steps/s). On the three additional PDE benchmarks (Poisson-Gauss, Allen-Cahn, Wave-Gauss), DRIFT-NET also attains the best test error. On long-horizon ApeBench Kolmogorov rollouts (T=100), DRIFT-NET reduces final-time error from 114.14 to 110.87 (turbulent) and from 62.99 to 57.17 (smoother) relative to scOT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRIFT-NET outperforms all four baselines on every benchmark where they are compared. Against scOT (the strongest baseline), DRIFT-NET reduces relative L1 error by 54% on NS-PwC (1.09 vs. 2.35), 19% on NS-Tracer-PwC (4.19 vs. 5.18), 14% on NS-SL (3.40 vs. 3.96), and 7% on FNS-KF (4.32 vs. 4.65), while also using fewer parameters and achieving higher throughput. FNO is competitive only on FNS-KF (4.43 vs. 4.32) but degrades severely on advection-heavy tasks like NS-PwC (4.57) and NS-Tracer-PwC (9.46). U-NO is the weakest baseline on most unsteady flow tasks (11.70 on FNS-KF, 10.15 on NS-Tracer-PwC), though it is competitive on NS-PwC (1.62). The Refiner U-Net baseline is the closest competitor on NS-SL (3.48 vs. 3.40) and FNS-KF (4.38 vs. 4.32) but is still consistently outperformed by DRIFT-NET across all tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRIFT-NET is positioned as a spectral-spatial coupled integral-kernel neural operator that directly addresses a known limitation of the dominant PDE foundation-model backbone, POSEIDON's scOT (multi-scale windowed self-attention). It inherits the U-Net hierarchical structure and ConvNeXt-style local blocks from the scOT/PDEBench lineage, but replaces the windowed-attention mechanism for global coupling with an explicit Fourier-domain branch that performs controlled low-frequency mixing. The bandwise radial-gating fusion and the frequency-weighted loss draw on ideas from focal frequency losses (Jiang et al., 2021) and spectral-bias analyses (Rahaman et al., 2019; Xu et al., 2020), but combine them in a non-expansive, width-preserving manner that prior FNO or U-NO variants do not provide. The paper explicitly frames itself as a drop-in replacement for windowed-attention blocks in multi-scale operator backbones rather than a new problem formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that DRIFT-NET offers a concrete, modular architectural unit that reduces closed-loop rollout drift while simultaneously cutting parameter count (~15%) and increasing inference throughput over the current state-of-the-art scOT backbone, making it a candidate building block for next-generation PDE foundation models. The consistent gains across elliptic, parabolic, and hyperbolic PDEs (Poisson, Allen-Cahn, Wave, Navier-Stokes) suggest the spectral-spatial decomposition is not task-specific. Key open questions remain: the low-frequency mask cutoff is hand-tuned and task-dependent, the method is validated only in 2D, and the repeated FFTs introduce memory traffic that may become prohibitive at 3D or very high-resolution scales. Extending to 3D, multi-physics coupling, irregular domains, and end-to-end learned spectral partitions are the natural next steps the authors identify.
