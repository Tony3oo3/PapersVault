## Problem

Diffusion-based planners for autonomous driving leverage anchor trajectories to guide denoising, but DiffusionDrive's truncated diffusion schedule creates a fundamental asymmetry: the forward process adds noise to anchors while the denoising process recovers ground-truth trajectories, violating the core symmetry principle of diffusion models. This theoretical inconsistency can lead to unpredictable behaviors and limits the model's ability to fully exploit anchor guidance in closed-loop settings where the ego vehicle's actions influence future states.

## Input / Output

The model consumes LiDAR point clouds, a front camera image, and a target point, processed through a frozen TransFuser++ perception backbone that produces BEV segmentation, bounding boxes, traffic information, and fused features as conditional guidance z. The output is geometric path waypoints (equal geometric-spaced coordinates, e.g., every 1 meter) plus a scalar ego-vehicle speed, rather than the temporal speed waypoints used by DiffusionDrive. This geometric representation is chosen because it decouples path shape from speed control, making it more compliant with route topology and easier to generalize across different speeds for the same maneuver.

## Method

BridgeDrive formulates planning as a conditional diffusion bridge between a coarse anchor trajectory x_T = y and the ground-truth trajectory x_0 = x, with the forward process defined as x_t = a_t·x_T + b_t·x_0 + c_t·ε, ensuring perfect symmetry between forward and reverse processes. A neural denoiser x_θ(x_t, t, x_T, z) is trained via supervised imitation to predict x_0 given the noisy bridge state, the anchor, and scene features, while a separate classifier h_φ(z, Y) selects the optimal anchor from a K-means cluster set of 60 pre-defined trajectories. At inference, the reverse process is simulated as a probability flow ODE (PF-ODE) using a first-order DDIM solver over 20 steps, starting from the selected anchor and iteratively refining toward the final trajectory. The key distinction from DiffusionDrive is that both the forward and reverse processes share the same endpoints (anchor at t=T, ground-truth at t=0), restoring the theoretical consistency that the truncated schedule breaks, and the training is simulation-free since the bridge kernel has a closed-form Gaussian.

## Results

On the Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark (220 routes, CARLA Leaderboard 2.0), BridgeDrive achieves a Driving Score (DS) of 87.99 and Success Rate (SR) of 74.99%, outperforming the previous SOTA SimLingo by +2.92 DS and +7.72% SR. On the LEAD dataset, BridgeDrive reaches 96.34 DS and 89.25% SR, surpassing the LEAD baseline (TFv6) by +1.14 DS and +2.45% SR. On the open-loop NAVSIM navtest split, BridgeDrive attains a PDM Score (PDMS) of 88.0, competitive with DiffusionDrive's reported 88.1. Inference speed is 0.10 s/frame with 20 diffusion timesteps, suitable for real-time deployment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BridgeDrive outperforms all 11 baselines on the primary Bench2Drive benchmark. Against the previous SOTA SimLingo (DS=85.07, SR=67.27%), BridgeDrive gains +2.92 DS and +7.72% SR, with particularly large improvements in Merging (+11.17%) and Traffic Sign (+7.02%) multi-ability scores. Compared to the closest diffusion-based competitor DiffusionDrive^geo (DS=80.79, SR=58.18%), BridgeDrive improves by +7.20 DS and +16.81% SR, demonstrating the benefit of the principled diffusion bridge formulation over truncated diffusion. Against non-diffusion baselines, BridgeDrive leads TransFuser++ (DS=84.21, SR=67.27%) by +3.78 DS and +7.72% SR, and DriveTransformer (DS=63.46, SR=35.01%) by +24.53 DS and +39.98% SR. On the LEAD dataset, BridgeDrive (DS=96.34, SR=89.25%) surpasses the LEAD baseline TFv6 (DS=95.2, SR=86.8%) by +1.14 DS and +2.45% SR. On NAVSIM, BridgeDrive (PDMS=88.0) is essentially tied with DiffusionDrive's reported result (88.1) and outperforms Hydra-MDP (86.5) and VADv2 (80.9), though the authors note NAVSIM's open-loop protocol underemphasizes the closed-loop advantages of their method.

## Relation to Prior Work

BridgeDrive directly extends DiffusionDrive (Liao et al., 2025), which introduced anchor-guided truncated diffusion for end-to-end driving planning, by replacing the theoretically inconsistent truncated schedule with a principled diffusion bridge formulation drawn from Zhou et al. (2024) and Zheng et al. (2025a). The key conceptual departure is that DiffusionDrive's forward process corrupts an anchor while its reverse process recovers a ground-truth trajectory, violating the symmetry that defines diffusion models; BridgeDrive instead constructs a bridge between ground-truth and anchor so that forward and reverse processes are perfectly symmetric. The perception backbone and geometric path waypoint representation follow TransFuser++ (Jaeger et al., 2023), and the anchor construction via K-means clustering over geometric trajectories and speed is adapted from DiffusionDrive's design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The +7.72% success rate improvement over the prior SOTA (SimLingo) on Bench2Drive's closed-loop evaluation is substantial for a task where small errors compound over time, and the theoretical consistency argument provides a clear design principle for future anchor-guided diffusion planners. The finding that geometric path waypoints outperform temporal speed waypoints by up to 15% for diffusion-based planners is practically important and suggests the field should reconsider trajectory representations. Open questions include whether the comfort/give-way trade-off (the model brakes more aggressively) can be resolved, how the method generalizes to out-of-distribution scenarios (e.g., imperfect overtaking timing), and whether integrating VLA priors or RL post-training can close the remaining gap.
