## Problem

Despite the theoretical potential of diffusion LLMs (dLLMs) to generate multiple tokens in parallel, no open-source dLLM has matched the inference speed of autoregressive (AR) LLMs of comparable size. The core bottleneck is that bidirectional attention in dLLMs is fundamentally incompatible with standard KV caching, and existing acceleration methods either sacrifice inter-block parallelism (Block Diffusion) or introduce biased cached states due to the bidirectional nature of attention (Fast-dLLM). This paper addresses the gap by asking whether dLLMs can be restructured to simultaneously support exact KV cache and parallel decoding across blocks.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a text prompt and operates on a sliding window of token blocks, each of fixed size (e.g., 16–64 tokens), where earlier blocks carry lower mask ratios and later blocks carry higher ones. A new fully-masked block is dynamically appended once the last block's decoded-token ratio exceeds a threshold τ_add, and blocks transition from a conservative 'semi-activated' state to an aggressive 'fully-activated' state once their predecessor reaches τ_act completion. Output is a sequence of generated text tokens, with early termination via <EOS> detection on instruct models; maximum generation length is capped (256 or 512 tokens depending on benchmark).

## Method

Discrete Diffusion Forcing (D2F) restructures dLLMs into an AR-diffusion hybrid by imposing block-wise causal attention (bidirectional within each block, causal across blocks), which makes standard KV caching exact. During training, a monotonically increasing noise schedule is applied so that earlier blocks are progressively less masked than later ones, and the model learns to denoise each block conditioned only on its partially denoised prefix—enabling inter-block parallel decoding at inference. The student model is obtained via asymmetric distillation from a pre-trained bidirectional dLLM: the teacher predicts each block with a global view of all noisy blocks, while the student approximates using only a causally restricted view, minimizing KL divergence over mask tokens. At inference, a pipelined parallel decoding algorithm maintains a sliding window of active blocks with a dual-state mechanism (semi-activated blocks decode conservatively; fully-activated blocks decode aggressively), allowing future blocks to be decoded before preceding ones are complete. Only LoRA adapters on attention projections are trained (~12 hours on 8 A100s), making the approach a lightweight retrofit of existing dLLMs rather than a from-scratch training.

## Results

D2F-Dream-Base-7B achieves 119.9 tokens/s on GSM8K, establishing the first open-source dLLM to surpass AR LLMs in inference speed (2.5× faster than LLaMA3-Instruct-8B at 48.0 tokens/s, 2.3× faster than Qwen2.5-Base-7B at 52.7 tokens/s). On LLaDA-Instruct-8B, D2F delivers up to 52.9× speedup on MBPP (47.6 vs. 0.9 TPS) and 29.1× on HumanEval (81.6 vs. 2.8 TPS) while maintaining comparable scores (38.0 vs. 39.0 on MBPP, 40.2 vs. 36.0 on HumanEval). On Dream-Base-7B, D2F achieves 9.6× speedup on GSM8K-CoT (91.2 vs. 9.5 TPS) with a slight score improvement (77.6 vs. 75.0). D2F-DiffuCoder further demonstrates up to 11.8× speedup on MBPP+ (46.53 vs. 3.94 TPS) with negligible performance loss (61.4 vs. 61.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla dLLMs (LLaDA-Instruct-8B and Dream-Base-7B), D2F achieves 7.3–52.9× speedup on LLaDA and 3.6–10.1× on Dream while preserving or slightly improving task scores (e.g., 77.6 vs. 75.0 on GSM8K-CoT, 40.2 vs. 36.0 on HumanEval for LLaDA). Against prior acceleration methods, D2F consistently outperforms both dLLM-Cache (2.5–2.7× slower) and Fast-dLLM Dual-Cache (1.8–2.1× slower) across all benchmarks; for instance, on GSM8K-CoT D2F reaches 91.2 TPS versus Fast-dLLM's 49.8 TPS. Against AR LLMs of comparable size, D2F-Dream-Base-7B is 2.5× faster than LLaMA3-Instruct-8B (119.9 vs. 48.0 TPS) and 2.3× faster than Qwen2.5-Base-7B (52.7 TPS) on GSM8K, marking the first open-source dLLM to exceed AR inference speed. Against SDAR (a block-diffusion model trained from scratch), D2F achieves 90+ TPS versus SDAR's maximum of 46.7 TPS at the lowest confidence threshold, while maintaining comparable accuracy (77.2 vs. 79.2 at SDAR's best setting). The only minor trade-off is on MATH where D2F-Dream scores 35.4 versus Fast-dLLM's 37.1, though D2F still delivers 10× the throughput.

## Relation to Prior Work

D2F extends the continuous-space diffusion forcing (DF) framework of Chen et al. (2024a) to discrete token sequences, adapting the core idea of predicting the next block conditioned on a noisy, incomplete prefix. It departs from prior block-wise dLLM acceleration methods (Block Diffusion, Fast-dLLM, dLLM-Cache) that either sacrifice inter-block parallelism to enable KV cache or rely on approximate caching that introduces bias from bidirectional attention. The asymmetric distillation strategy is conceptually analogous to CausVid's distillation of holistic video diffusion into streaming models, but applied to the discrete masked-diffusion setting with a KL-divergence objective over token predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first open-source dLLM to demonstrably surpass AR LLMs in inference throughput (2.5× over LLaMA3-8B on GSM8K), a milestone that could shift the practical calculus for deploying diffusion-based language models. The method's practicality is notable: only 12 hours of LoRA fine-tuning on 17k samples is needed to convert an existing bidirectional dLLM, making it accessible without retraining from scratch. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger models (beyond 7–8B), longer generation lengths, and non-math/code tasks where the confidence-based early-termination strategy may be less effective; the MATH benchmark shows D2F-LLaDA generating substantially longer sequences (384 vs. 243 tokens), suggesting the speed advantage may be task-dependent.
