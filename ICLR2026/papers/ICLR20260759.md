## Problem

Masked Diffusion Language Models (MDLMs) generate tokens in parallel through iterative denoising, but their safety vulnerabilities to jailbreak attacks remain largely unexplored. The paper identifies a critical 'priming vulnerability' specific to this mechanism: if affirmative tokens for a harmful query appear at any intermediate denoising step, subsequent generation is steered toward a harmful response even in safety-aligned models. Existing alignment methods (SFT, DPO, MOSA) only train from fully masked initial states and therefore fail to constrain behavior at contaminated intermediate states.

## Input / Output

The method takes a harmful text query concatenated with a partially masked token sequence at an intermediate denoising step (contaminated with affirmative tokens from a known harmful response) and produces a safe text response by continuing the denoising process to completion. Generation operates over a fixed-length sequence (L=128 tokens) with T=128 denoising steps, where unmasked tokens are never re-masked in subsequent steps. Unlike standard MDLM inference that always starts from a fully masked sequence, RA conditions on adversarially constructed intermediate states with varying numbers of injected tokens (from 1 at t_inter=1 up to 32 at t_inter=32).

## Method

Recovery Alignment (RA) is an RLHF-style safety alignment method that explicitly trains MDLMs to recover safe responses from intentionally contaminated intermediate states. During training, harmful query-response pairs from BeaverTails are used: at a scheduled intervention step t_inter, the predicted response is replaced with the harmful response to create a contaminated state, the model then denoises from that state to step T, and the output is scored by a DeBERTaV3 reward model before updating parameters via GRPO. The intervention step is linearly scheduled from t_min to t_max over training, forming a curriculum from mild to severe contamination. The paper also proves (Theorem 4.1) that the first-step mask predictor log-likelihood is a lower bound on the full denoising log-likelihood under a monotonicity assumption, enabling a 'First-Step GCG' attack that is ~20× faster than Monte Carlo GCG while achieving up to 4× higher attack success rates, demonstrating the vulnerability is exploitable even without direct denoising intervention.

## Results

On JBB-Behaviors, Recovery Alignment (RA) reduces the Attack Success Rate (ASR) under First-Step GCG from 58.0% to 11.3% on LLaDA Instruct, from 49.5% to 15.0% on LLaDA 1.5, and from 92.7% to 45.7% on MMaDA MixCoT. Under the anchoring attack at t_inter=1, RA drives ASR to 0.0% on LLaDA Instruct and 1.0% on LLaDA 1.5, compared to 17.3% and 14.7% for the original models. RA also improves robustness against conventional jailbreaks (e.g., PAIR ASR drops from 44.3% to 10.0% on LLaDA Instruct) while preserving general capability, with average accuracy across 11 benchmarks changing by less than 0.4 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RA outperforms all three safety-alignment baselines (SFT, DPO, MOSA) across every attack type and all three MDLMs. On LLaDA Instruct under First-Step GCG, RA achieves 11.3% ASR versus 48.2% (SFT), 46.3% (DPO), and 28.0% (MOSA); under PAD, RA reaches 1.0% versus 66.3%, 35.3%, and 32.3% respectively. The MDLM-specific baseline MOSA (Xie et al., 2025) is the strongest competitor but still leaves substantial vulnerability (e.g., 46.0% ASR at anchoring t=16 on LLaDA Instruct vs. 8.3% for RA), confirming that training from fully masked sequences alone cannot address the priming vulnerability. In the attack-method comparison (Table 1), First-Step GCG (the paper's proposed attack) is ~20× faster than Monte Carlo GCG (0.2 h vs. 4.3 h per prompt on LLaDA Instruct) while achieving up to 4× higher ASR (58.0% vs. 20.0%). The ablation RA w/o inter (training without contaminated intermediate states) performs comparably to DPO/MOSA, underscoring that the recovery-from-contamination training signal is the critical component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper identifies a vulnerability specific to Masked Diffusion Language Models (MDLMs) that is mechanistically distinct from the prefilling vulnerability in autoregressive models: in MDLMs, affirmative tokens appearing at any intermediate denoising step (not just the first few tokens) can suppress subsequent safety refusals due to the parallel, bidirectional conditioning. It builds on the RLHF/GRPO optimization framework (Shao et al., 2024; Ouyang et al., 2022) but repurposes it for a new objective—training recovery from contaminated intermediate states rather than from the fully masked initial state. The work departs from the closest MDLM-specific alignment, MOSA (Xie et al., 2025), which only trains from fully masked sequences and thus cannot address the priming vulnerability. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 4.1) extends the GCG attack framework (Zou et al., 2023) to MDLMs by deriving a tractable first-step lower bound on the full denoising log-likelihood.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MDLMs (LLaDA, Mercury, MMaDA) gain practical traction as lower-latency alternatives to ARMs, understanding their unique safety failure modes is critical before widespread deployment. The paper demonstrates that existing ARM safety insights do not transfer, and that even a single injected token at step 1 of 128 can bypass guardrails—a vulnerability invisible to standard alignment procedures. Open questions include whether the monotonicity assumption in Theorem 4.1 holds for all response types, whether supervised (DPO-style) alternatives to the RLHF instantiation can match RA's robustness at lower cost, and whether the vulnerability extends to continuous DLMs or multimodal diffusion models. RA still leaves a gap against strong multi-turn attacks like ReNeLLM (~72% ASR), suggesting that recovery alignment is necessary but not sufficient for full safety.
