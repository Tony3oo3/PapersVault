## Problem

GMsFEM achieves high accuracy on heterogeneous, high-contrast PDEs by constructing localized spectral basis functions, but its offline eigenproblem stage is prohibitively expensive (over 10,000 seconds for a 100³ grid). Standalone neural operators are fast but lose robustness on high-contrast coefficients and fail catastrophically on out-of-distribution forcing terms. The paper asks whether a neural operator can replace the costly offline basis construction while preserving GMsFEM's mathematical guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263741/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The NO receives the heterogeneous coefficient field κ(x) restricted to a single local subdomain ω_i (values in [1, 9600], generated via Karhunen-Loève expansion) on structured grids (100², 250², 50³, 100³), and outputs N_bf = 8 basis functions for that subdomain. Crucially, the NO is trained per geometric domain type (full, half, corner in 2D; full, half, quarter, corner in 3D) with orientation-normalized inputs, and is entirely independent of the right-hand side f(x), which enters only in the downstream GMsFEM online solve. The predicted basis functions are assembled into a global restriction matrix R, and the fine-scale solution u is recovered via the standard GMsFEM projection–solve–prolongation pipeline.

## Method

The core contribution is a subspace-informed training objective: instead of regressing individual basis functions (which are sign-ambiguous and sensitive to perturbations), the F-FNO is trained to match the N_bf-dimensional subspace spanned by the GMsFEM eigenvectors. The Subspace Alignment Loss (SAL) minimizes the expected Grassmannian distance between predicted and target subspaces via the Frobenius norm of Q_R^T Q_R̃, and an optional Projection Regularization term (SAL-PR) additionally enforces consistency of projection operators on random test vectors. Separate F-FNO networks (4–5 operator layers, 64–128 channels, 600 epochs with AdamW) are trained per domain geometry type. At inference, the predicted subspaces form the restriction matrix R, after which the standard GMsFEM online stage (coarse solve + prolongation) produces the final solution, yielding >60× speedup in basis generation while matching GMsFEM accuracy to within ~0.1% L2 error.

## Results

On the 250×250 grid with N_bf=8, GMsFEM-NO achieves relative L2 errors of 1.05–1.72% across diffusion and Richards benchmarks, matching standard GMsFEM within ~0.1% while delivering a >60× speedup in basis generation (e.g., 10,547 s → 1.33 s for the 100³ 3D case). Against standalone SOTA neural operators, GMsFEM-NO reduces L2 error by up to 2.8× on complex (spatially variable) source terms, e.g., 1.60% vs. 4.45% (F-FNO) and 14.69% (GNOT) for the Richards equation. The method also demonstrates resolution invariance: a model trained on a 250² grid retains 1.45–2.25% L2 error when evaluated on a 500² grid, compared to 1.17–1.93% for GMsFEM computed directly at that resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard GMsFEM, GMsFEM-NO achieves nearly identical L2 and H1 errors (within 0.1–0.5 percentage points) across all 2D and 3D benchmarks while reducing basis-generation time by more than 60× (Table 6). Versus standalone neural operators, GMsFEM-NO dominates on spatially variable forcing: it cuts L2 error from 4.51% (F-FNO), 14.29% (GNOT), and 6.63% (Transolver++) down to 1.60% for the diffusion equation, and from 4.45%, 14.69%, and 8.82% down to 1.60% for Richards. On uniform forcing, GMsFEM-NO is competitive or slightly better (1.05% vs. 1.02% for F-FNO on diffusion). In the regression-based comparison (Table 10), GMsFEM-NO achieves 2.8% test error versus 24% for PCANet and 100% for the kernel method, both of which severely overfit. Among intrusive reduced-order baselines (Figure 5), DeepPOD is the closest competitor but requires 20 dense global basis functions to match GMsFEM-NO's 8 sparse local basis functions, and only surpasses it at 30 basis functions. GMsFEM-NO also outperforms F-FNO in data efficiency, maintaining stable accuracy down to 200 training samples where F-FNO's error more than doubles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GMsFEM (Efendiev et al., 2013) by replacing its costly offline local eigenproblem stage with a Factorized Fourier Neural Operator (F-FNO, Tran et al., 2021) that predicts the multiscale basis function subspace. It departs from prior hybrid ML+numerical methods (Bhattacharya et al., 2024; Vasilyeva et al., 2020; Kropfl et al., 2022) that learn effective macroscopic coefficients or upscaling parameters, instead learning the solution subspace itself in the form of localized basis functions. The novel Subspace Alignment Loss (SAL) draws on Grassmannian geometry to measure subspace overlap rather than individual function matching, and the projection regularization term addresses the rotation invariance gap in pure subspace metrics. The approach also contrasts with Spiridonov et al. (2025), which used a fully connected network to predict a single additional basis function, and with reduced-order modeling baselines (POD, DeepPOD, PCANet) that learn global dense bases rather than local sparse ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 60×+ speedup in basis generation (from ~10,500 s to ~1.3 s on a 100³ grid) makes GMsFEM practically viable for multi-query and parameter-study scenarios where the offline stage was previously prohibitive. The discretization invariance property—training on a 100×100 grid and evaluating on 500×500 with only modest accuracy loss—offers a practical path to scalability without retraining. Key limitations include restriction to structured grids (due to the F-FNO architecture), relatively small problem sizes tested, and focus on steady-state Dirichlet problems; extending to irregular domains, larger scales, and more complex PDEs remains open. The breakeven analysis honestly reveals that for 2D problems the training cost is not yet offset at typical sample counts, though the 3D case (breakeven ~159 samples) is more compelling.
