## Problem

Existing quantization methods for LLMs and MLLMs rely on uniform-bit schemes optimized for text perplexity or visual feature fidelity, but VLA models output continuous action sequences where even minor quantization-induced errors compound autoregressively into catastrophic task failures. No systematic analysis of how quantization uniquely affects VLA architectures has been conducted, and the heterogeneous sensitivity of individual channels within a VLA model has been overlooked by both uniform-bit and coarse module-level mixed-precision approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265229/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained VLA model (e.g., OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, or π₀) and a small calibration set sampled from LIBERO training demonstrations (augmented with instruction-only samples) to compute per-channel sensitivity scores. It produces a compressed model where each weight output channel is assigned a bit-width from {0, 2, 4, 8, 16} (0-bit = pruned) while activations use a uniform bit-width (e.g., 8-bit) for branch-free execution. The quantized model retains the same I/O interface: RGB images plus a language instruction in, a sequence of continuous end-effector actions out.

## Method

QVLA is a post-training, action-centric, channel-wise quantization framework with two stages. First, it estimates per-channel action-space sensitivity by measuring the expected squared L2 deviation in the final action output when a single channel is quantized to each candidate bit-width; a fast first-order Taylor-approximation proxy (Jacobian gain × estimated quantization noise) provides a global channel ranking, followed by targeted full forward passes on the most sensitive channels for precise calibration. Second, a greedy demotion algorithm assigns final bit-widths by starting all channels at 16-bit and iteratively demoting the least sensitive channels (ranked by a sensitivity-to-bit ratio ρ) through stages 16→8→4→2→0 until the average bit budget is met, with dual-threshold and L0-style regularization on the final 2→0 (pruning) stage. This formulation unifies quantization and structural pruning into a single per-channel optimization, and the sensitivity metric is computed directly in the action space rather than on internal feature reconstructions, which is the key departure from LLM-oriented quantization methods.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, QVLA under W4A4 quantization of OpenVLA-OFT achieves 96.0% average success rate (vs. 97.1% FP), requiring only 4.5 GB VRAM (29.2% of the original 15.4 GB) with a 1.49× inference speedup. For the OpenVLA model under W4A4, QVLA retains 76.0% average success (−0.5% from FP) while SmoothQuant drops to 63.2% (−13.3%). Under weight-only W4A16 quantization, QVLA incurs zero performance loss on OpenVLA (76.5% vs. 76.5% FP) and only −0.4% on OpenVLA-OFT, compared to −4.7% and −4.5% for AWQ respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QVLA decisively outperforms SmoothQuant under W4A4 quantization, achieving 76.0% vs. 63.2% average success on OpenVLA (+12.8%) and 96.0% vs. 73.4% on OpenVLA-OFT (+22.6%), while also using less memory (4.3 vs. 4.7 GB; 4.5 vs. 4.9 GB). Against OmniQuant, QVLA leads by 2.7% on OpenVLA W4A4 (76.0% vs. 73.3%) and 2.1% on OpenVLA-OFT W4A4 (96.0% vs. 93.9%), with lower memory in both cases. In weight-only W4A16 settings (Table 2), QVLA matches or exceeds full-precision performance (76.5% on OpenVLA, 96.7% on OpenVLA-OFT) while AWQ degrades by 4.7% and 4.5% respectively. In real-world bimanual manipulation (Table 5), QVLA under W8A16 matches the unquantized π₀ baseline at 63.3% average success rate while delivering a 1.28× speedup, confirming that the action-centric quantization strategy generalizes beyond simulation to physical robotic deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant LLM/MLLM quantization paradigm (SmoothQuant, OmniQuant, AWQ), which optimizes for passive data fidelity such as text perplexity or feature reconstruction, by reframing the quantization objective around action-space sensitivity in embodied control. It extends the mixed-precision quantization line (HAWQ) from coarse layer-level to fine-grained per-channel bit allocation, and unifies quantization with structural pruning (0-bit) under a single greedy demotion framework. Conceptually, it repurposes the Taylor-approximation sensitivity analysis common in pruning literature but anchors it to the final action output rather than intermediate activations, making it the first quantization method whose optimization signal is the physical action error rather than an internal representation loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VLA models are the leading paradigm for generalist robot control but their 14+ GB footprint makes real-time deployment on edge hardware (e.g., Jetson AGX Orin) impractical; this paper provides a principled, training-free compression path that retains ~99% performance at ~29% VRAM, directly addressing the deployment bottleneck. The finding that uniform-bit quantization causes catastrophic 13–24% performance drops while channel-wise action-guided allocation nearly eliminates the gap establishes a clear design principle for future VLA compression work. Limitations include evaluation primarily on LIBERO with two model families (OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT), limited real-world validation (one model, three tasks), and the open question of whether the action-centric sensitivity metric generalizes to diffusion- or flow-matching-based VLA decoders (e.g., π₀, RDT-1B) where the action generation process is fundamentally different.
