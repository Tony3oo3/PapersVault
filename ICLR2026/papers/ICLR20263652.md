## Problem

Traditional node-level graph generative models treat each node as an independent entity and struggle to simultaneously capture the coupled multiscale dependencies inherent in graphs — from fine-grained local substructures (e.g., functional groups) to coarse-grained global topologies (e.g., community structure, overall molecular scaffold). Existing approaches that incorporate global information (e.g., subgraph prediction or graph-level pseudo-labels) do not model the joint distribution of global and local representations, leaving the dynamic interaction between these two scales unexploited.

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model starts from Gaussian noise in a continuous latent space of dimension d per node (Z_l ∈ R^{N×d}) and per cluster (Z_g ∈ R^{K×d}), where K is the number of graph clusters. For conditional generation, a property encoder τ(s) provides additional conditioning embeddings. The output is a reconstructed graph (adjacency matrix + node features) via a lightweight MLP decoder, or a full 3D molecular structure (coordinates + atom types + bonds) when an EGNN decoder is used. The latent space operates at O(N) complexity rather than O(N²) over the full adjacency matrix.

## Method

DualDiff employs a two-branch EDM diffusion process in a unified latent space: one GNN denoiser (D_θl) operates on node-level embeddings Z_l, and another (D_θg) on cluster-level embeddings Z_g obtained by pooling node features within graph clusters (K-means on 3D coordinates for molecules, spectral clustering for generic graphs). The key innovation is a dual conditioning mechanism that alternately models p(Z_l|Z_g)p(Z_g) and p(Z_g|Z_l)p(Z_l): in process (i), the predicted global embedding Ẑ_g,0 is passed through a FiLM layer to produce per-cluster scale/shift parameters that modulate the local denoiser's output; in process (ii), the predicted local embedding Ẑ_l,0 is aggregated via message passing and pooling to form a global condition concatenated with the self-condition for the global denoiser. During sampling, an asymmetric alternating strategy (m steps of local update per single global aggregation step, inspired by federated server-client communication) ensures stability by preventing low-quality local predictions from corrupting the global branch. The pretrained autoencoder uses EGNN for SE(3)-equivariant molecular encoding or MPNN for generic graphs, and the entire framework is trained with the standard EDM denoising loss augmented by the dual conditioning terms.

## Results

On ZINC250k, DualDiff achieves the best FCD score of 0.91±0.02 and KL score of 0.98±0.01, surpassing the strongest baseline EDM-SyCo (FCD 0.85, KL 0.96) while maintaining 100% novelty and uniqueness. On generic graph benchmarks, DualDiff attains the lowest MMD average of 0.005 on Ego-small, 0.007 on Community-small, and 0.0003 on Grid, outperforming all baselines including GSDM and GraphBFN. On the QM9 dataset, DualDiff achieves the best FCD of 0.092 and NSPKD of 0.0001, and on 3D molecular generation it reaches 99.3% valid-and-unique rate, exceeding GEOLDM (92.7%) and EQUIFM (93.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ZINC250k, DualDiff outperforms the strongest one-shot baseline EDM-SyCo by 0.06 in FCD (0.91 vs. 0.85) and 0.02 in KL (0.98 vs. 0.96), while also achieving higher validity (0.92 vs. 0.88). Among autoregressive methods, DualDiff surpasses MoLeR (FCD 0.91 vs. 0.83) and MAGNet (FCD 0.91 vs. 0.76) by substantial margins. On generic graph benchmarks, DualDiff achieves the best MMD on all five datasets: on Ego-small it beats GraphBFN (Avg. 0.005 vs. 0.009) and GSDM (0.005 vs. 0.016); on Community-small it outperforms GSDM (0.007 vs. 0.009); and on Grid it achieves 0.0003 vs. GSDM's 0.0007. On QM9, DualDiff attains the lowest FCD (0.092) and NSPKD (0.0001), narrowly beating GBD (FCD 0.093) and GraphBFN (FCD 0.101). For 3D molecular generation, DualDiff achieves 99.3% valid-and-unique, significantly exceeding GEOLDM (92.7%) and EQUIFM (93.5%), though molecular stability (88.7%) remains slightly below GEOLDM (89.4%). On MOSES, DualDiff achieves 96.8% validity and 96.2% novelty, comparable to LGD (97.4% validity, 95.9% novelty) and superior to DiGress (85.7% validity, 95.0% novelty).

## Relation to Prior Work

DualDiff extends the latent graph diffusion paradigm (LGD, GEOLDM, EDM-SyCo) by introducing a two-branch diffusion process that simultaneously operates on node-level (local) and cluster-level (global) latent representations, building directly on the EDM noise schedule and SDE formulation. The key conceptual departure from prior work like SubgDiff and Graphusion—which inject global information as a one-way signal—is the dual conditioning mechanism, which generalizes self-conditioning (Chen et al., 2023) into a bidirectional interaction where global and local branches alternately serve as conditions for each other, grounded in the factorization p(Z_l, Z_g) = p(Z_l|Z_g)p(Z_g) = p(Z_g|Z_l)p(Z_l). The autoencoder backbone follows the LGD paradigm, using EGNNs for molecular graphs and MPNNs/Graph Transformers for generic graphs, while the global information extraction relies on training-free clustering (K-means on coordinates or spectral clustering) rather than learned pooling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in graph generation: most diffusion-based methods treat nodes as independent entities and struggle to capture the coupled multiscale dependencies (e.g., functional groups within a molecule and their spatial distribution) that determine real-world properties. The dual conditioning mechanism offers a clean, principled way to model the joint global-local distribution without requiring hierarchical autoregressive decomposition, and the asymmetric alternating sampling strategy (m local steps per 1 global step) provides a practical stability mechanism. However, the method is currently limited to generic and simple molecular graphs; extension to proteins, peptides, and circuit design is acknowledged as future work. Open questions include whether the training-free clustering-based global extraction scales to very large graphs with complex community structures, and whether the dual conditioning idea transfers to other hierarchical data beyond graphs.
