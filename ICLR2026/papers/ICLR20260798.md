## Problem

Spectral cameras (RGB, multispectral, hyperspectral) produce data with varying channel counts and wavelength ranges, creating camera-specific data silos that existing models cannot jointly exploit. No prior method simultaneously achieves wavelength-awareness, channel-invariance, spatio-spectral encoding, and spatio-spectral self-supervised pre-training, leaving a gap in camera-agnostic representation learning for spectral imagery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260798/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a spectral image of arbitrary channel dimensionality (H×W×C, where C ranges from 3 for RGB to 200+ for hyperspectral) along with the corresponding per-channel wavelength values in nanometers. Each channel is projected band-wise via a shared 2D convolution (patch size 8×8) into a D-dimensional feature space, and the spectral encoder resolves the variable-length channel axis into a fixed set of K=8 spectral representations, producing a camera-agnostic feature map that feeds into a standard ViT-based spatial encoder and a task-specific head (e.g., UperNet for segmentation or a linear probe for classification).

## Method

CARL introduces a spectral encoder that first encodes each channel's wavelength as a sinusoidal Fourier-feature positional encoding to establish cross-camera channel correspondences, then applies L=4 iterations of self-attention over the C spectral tokens followed by cross-attention from K=8 learnable spectral representations to distill salient spectral information into a fixed-size, camera-agnostic representation. This representation is aggregated (via summation) and forwarded to a standard spatial encoder (EVA-02) for geometric feature extraction. For pre-training, CARL-SSL introduces a novel feature-based spectral self-supervision objective: the student spectral encoder processes a spectrally masked input, and a transformer predictor reconstructs the masked spectral tokens in feature space using the student's spectral representations and the masked wavelengths' positional encodings, with a VICReg loss (invariance + variance + covariance terms). This spectral SSL is jointly optimized with I-JEPA-style spatial SSL (masked 2D region prediction in feature space), forming an end-to-end camera-agnostic spatio-spectral pre-training framework that scales across heterogeneous sensor corpora.

## Results

On the HSICity urban scene segmentation benchmark, CARL-SSL achieves 50.1 mIoU, outperforming the camera-specific SwinMask2Former baseline (44.6), DOFA (49.6), and all other channel-invariant methods. On satellite linear probing across 11 datasets, CARL attains the best average rank of 1.6, reaching 69.0% on m-bigearthnet and 26.5% on m-SA-crop-type. On out-of-distribution sensors, CARL demonstrates dramatic cross-sensor generalization, scoring 29.0 on LoveDA Urban (RGB, 3 bands) versus 15.4 for the best baseline, and 21.7 on WHU-OHS (32 bands) versus 1.5 for all spatial-only baselines. In the medical imaging proof-of-concept, CARL maintains 64.6 mIoU under spectral heterogeneity (4 subjects, 1 filter) where Hyve drops to 52.1 and DOFA to 58.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARL-SSL outperforms all 10 baselines on the HSICity benchmark, with the closest competitor DOFA (Xiong et al., 2024) trailing by 0.5 mIoU (49.6 vs 50.1) and the camera-specific model lagging by 5.5 mIoU. On satellite linear probing, CARL beats the strongest spatial-only baselines Copernicus-FM (Wang et al., 2025) and SMARTIES (Sumbul et al., 2025) by 6.9 and 7.0 points on m-bigearthnet (69.0 vs 62.1/62.0), and surpasses the spatio-spectral SpectralGPT+ (Hong et al., 2024) by 24.0 points. The gap widens dramatically on out-of-distribution sensors: CARL scores 29.0 on LoveDA Urban (RGB) versus 15.4 (Copernicus-FM) and 13.5 (SMARTIES), and 21.7 on WHU-OHS (32 bands) versus 1.5 for DOFA, Copernicus-FM, and SMARTIES, which all fail to generalize beyond their training sensors. In medical imaging, CARL consistently outperforms Hyve (Varga et al., 2023) and DOFA across all spectral heterogeneity levels (e.g., 64.6 vs 52.1 and 58.1 at 4S/1F), while HyperFree (Li et al., 2025a) and the Spectral Adapter (Braham et al., 2024) show the weakest cross-sensor robustness. On fully fine-tuned SegMunich, CARL achieves 50.9 mIoU, beating DOFA (49.5), SpectralGPT+ (49.1), SMARTIES (48.2), and SatMAE (Cong et al., 2022) (45.7).

## Relation to Prior Work

CARL builds on I-JEPA's feature-based self-supervised pre-training paradigm and VICReg's regularization loss, adapting them to the spectral domain, while drawing on Fourier features (Tancik et al., 2020) to encode wavelength information as positional encodings. It departs from two lines of prior work: (1) channel-adaptive embedding layers (DOFA, Hyve, HyperFree) that handle variable channel counts but perform only spatial operations in feature space, and (2) spatio-spectral encoders (SpectralGPT, Spectral Earth) that jointly model spatial and spectral dimensions but are locked to fixed channel configurations. CARL unifies these by introducing a wavelength-aware, channel-invariant spectral encoder with a self-attention-cross-attention mechanism that distills variable-length channel inputs into a fixed set of learned spectral representations, followed by a standard spatial transformer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling a single model to process RGB, multispectral, and hyperspectral images from arbitrary cameras without retraining, CARL directly addresses the data-silo problem that limits robustness and cross-applicability in spectral imaging across medicine, autonomous driving, and remote sensing. The strong out-of-distribution generalization (e.g., 21.7 vs. 1.5 mIoU on WHU-OHS) and the demonstrated cross-modality knowledge transfer (RGB 'pole' labels improving HSI segmentation) suggest the framework could serve as a backbone for future spectral foundation models. Limitations include higher computational cost than purely spatial encoders and sensitivity to non-spectral sensor differences (e.g., spatial resolution), which the authors note could be mitigated with additional sensor metadata. An open question is whether the fixed K=8 spectral representations scale gracefully to extremely high channel counts (e.g., >500 bands) or whether the cross-attention bottleneck becomes limiting.
