## Problem

Prior RL benchmarks for power grid control (L2RPN, RL2GRID) model grid operations as single-agent tasks, ignoring the inherently decentralized, multi-operator structure of real transmission systems. No existing benchmark supports multi-agent RL for grid topology optimization and redispatching with configurable observability, safety-critical constraints, and realistic TSO-scale coordination challenges.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264564/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent receives either the full grid state (centralized) or a local observation restricted to its controlled substations plus partial information on connecting lines, with dimensions ranging from 61 to 431 features depending on grid size and observability regime. For the discrete topology task, outputs are combinatorial actions (line switching and bus reassignment) whose per-substation space grows as 2^(N_lines+N_gens+N_loads−1)−1, reaching over 65,000 valid configurations for a single substation in bus36. For the continuous task, a mixed agent structure is used: decentralized agents output curtailment and storage setpoints for their zone, while a single global agent outputs redispatching setpoints for all remaining generators (69-dimensional action space on bus118).

## Method

MARL2GRID-TR is a benchmark built on Grid2Op's AC power flow solver, partitioning three base grids (bus14, bus36, bus118) among agents using Henka et al.'s segmentation to mirror real TSO control zones. It provides two task families—discrete topology optimization and continuous redispatching/curtailment—with configurable observability from fully centralized to strictly local, and a multi-agent idle heuristic that suspends agent control during normal operations to reduce effective decision horizon. Safety is formalized through joint (not per-agent) constraint costs for load shedding/islanding and line overloads, reflecting the coupled nonlinear dynamics where one agent's action can cascade across the grid. The benchmark exposes a PETTINGZOO interface and includes reference implementations of QPLEX, MAPPO, MASAC, LagrMAPPO, and PPO baselines. Empirically, MAPPO achieves ~79% survival on bus14 topology but fails on bus118, while in the continuous setting MAPPO and PPO reach 58–67% survival on bus118, demonstrating that current MARL methods struggle with the combinatorial, partially observable, safety-constrained nature of realistic grid control.

## Results

On the bus14 topology optimization (discrete) task, MAPPO achieves the best average survival of 0.79 over 2 years of test data, substantially outperforming PPO (0.38), LagrMAPPO (0.19 for load-shedding, 0.04 for overload constraint), and QPLEX (0.04). On the bus118 redispatching and curtailment (continuous) task, PPO achieves 0.67 average survival, followed by MAPPO (0.58), while MASAC (0.25) underperforms even the heuristic-based RecoPowerline agent (0.34). The DoNothing baseline achieves 0.18 (bus14) and 0.29 (bus118), confirming that learned policies provide meaningful improvements over passive operation. Notably, even the best-performing MAPPO fails on the larger bus118 topology task, with survival near zero, highlighting the open challenge of scaling MARL to realistic grid sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAPPO dominates the discrete topology task on bus14, achieving 0.79 survival versus PPO's 0.38, demonstrating that decentralized multi-agent control outperforms a fully-observable single-agent controller in the combinatorial action space. QPLEX, a value-based method, fails almost entirely (0.04 survival), confirming that Q-learning approaches are ill-suited to the exponentially large discrete topology action space. In the continuous redispatching task on bus118, PPO ultimately surpasses MAPPO (0.67 vs. 0.58) but requires roughly 10× more training steps (~10M vs. 3M), while MASAC (0.25) underperforms even the simple RecoPowerline heuristic (0.34). Constrained variants (LagrMAPPO and LagrPPO) fail to achieve meaningful survival (≤0.19) while attempting to satisfy safety thresholds, indicating that current constrained MARL methods cannot balance constraint satisfaction with task performance in this domain. The idle heuristic, which accelerates learning in centralized single-agent settings, actually degrades performance in the decentralized discrete topology task (reducing survival to ~20%), revealing a critical interaction between heuristic-guided transitions and multi-agent exploration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-agent RL benchmark for grid control (RL2GRID, Marchesini et al. 2025b) and the L2RPN competition series into a multi-agent formulation, arguing that real transmission systems are inherently decentralized across multiple operators or control zones. It builds directly on the Grid2Op simulation framework (Donnot, 2020) for high-fidelity AC power flow dynamics and on ChroniX2Grid for realistic demand/generation time series. The key conceptual departure is the Dec-POMDP formulation with configurable observability regimes, joint safety constraints, and a multi-agent idle heuristic that formalizes expert-in-the-loop transitions for the cooperative setting—none of which were available in prior single-agent grid RL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first MARL benchmark for power grid topology and redispatching, developed in collaboration with a real TSO (RTE France), it provides a standardized testbed for evaluating whether cooperative learning can handle the combinatorial action spaces, partial observability, and hard safety constraints of realistic grid operations. The finding that current MARL baselines (MAPPO, QPLEX, LagrMAPPO) fail to scale beyond small grids for topology optimization underscores a significant algorithmic gap that must be closed before RL can support TSOs managing high-VRE grids. Open questions include whether hierarchical or communication-based MARL can overcome the credit-assignment and coordination barriers identified, and how to integrate formal safety verification into the learning loop.
