## Problem

Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) are standard in modern LLMs, yet the role of each individual frequency dimension remains poorly understood, with prior studies reporting conflicting conclusions about whether high- or low-frequency components are essential or redundant. Existing analyses rely on visual heatmaps and coarse high/low-frequency splits, lacking a unified quantitative metric that can characterize per-dimension utilization in a model-agnostic way.

## Input / Output

The method consumes post-RoPE attention query (and key) vectors extracted during inference from pretrained LLMs at a fixed sequence length (L=4096). Each query is decomposed into d/2 two-dimensional rotary pairs, and the ℓ2-norm of each pair is collected across all L tokens to form a length-L discrete signal per pair. The output is two normalized entropy vectors of length d/2 (one per rotary pair): SpectrumFE (from STFT with F=1024, H=512) and SequenceFE (from global DFT with DC removed), plus per-pair attenuation factors α^(l,h,j) ∈ [0,1] used in the Weighted RoPE intervention.

## Method

For each rotary pair j, the paper constructs a 1D signal s_j by collecting the ℓ2-norms of the j-th query block across all L tokens, justified by the Cauchy-Schwarz bound on per-pair attention contributions. SpectrumFE applies a short-time Fourier transform (F=1024, H=512) to s_j, averages the power spectrum across frames, normalizes to a probability distribution over K=513 bins, and computes normalized Shannon entropy; low values indicate that energy is concentrated in a narrow set of frequency bins (band-limited allocation). SequenceFE applies a global DFT to s_j, discards the DC component, normalizes the positive-frequency power to a probability distribution, and computes normalized Shannon entropy; low values indicate near-single-tone oscillation driven by RoPE's fixed rotational phase. The two metrics are complementary: SpectrumFE captures which rotary pairs receive sustained energy (frequency bands), while SequenceFE captures whether the per-token signal exhibits RoPE-induced periodicity. To test functional relevance without fine-tuning, the paper introduces Weighted RoPE, which multiplies the RoPE rotation matrix for pairs whose FE falls below a threshold τ by a constant α<1, effectively attenuating their contribution to attention during inference.

## Results

Attenuating low-SequenceFE dimensions (τ<0.6, α=0.1) via Weighted RoPE yields downstream accuracy statistically indistinguishable from baseline across all four models (e.g., Llama-4: HellaSwag 66.67→66.67, MMLU 60.05→60.81), with modest perplexity improvements on average. In contrast, attenuating low-SpectrumFE dimensions (τ<0.2) worsens perplexity as α decreases, confirming that frequency-band dimensions carry task-relevant signal. Attenuating high-SpectrumFE outliers (τ>0.4) has negligible effect on both perplexity and downstream accuracy. On the Needle-in-a-Haystack long-context task, Weighted RoPE shows no regression and slight improvement around 30k tokens for Llama-4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is a diagnostic/interpretability study rather than a method-competition paper, so it does not benchmark against alternative positional encoding methods. The central comparison is between baseline RoPE and the proposed Weighted RoPE intervention (attenuating low-entropy rotary pairs by a factor α=0.1 at inference, no fine-tuning). Across all four evaluated models (Llama-4-17B, Llama-3-8B, Qwen3-8B, Gemma-2-9B) and three downstream benchmarks (HellaSwag, TruthfulQA, MMLU), Weighted RoPE is statistically indistinguishable from baseline, with at most ±0.8% variation. The critical asymmetry is that suppressing low-SpectrumFE (band-limited) dimensions degrades perplexity, while suppressing low-SequenceFE (periodic) dimensions does not—establishing that RoPE-induced periodicity is largely redundant whereas frequency bands are functionally essential. On long-context NIAH retrieval, Llama-3 shows identical performance, while Llama-4 exhibits slight improvement near 30k tokens under Weighted RoPE, suggesting the intervention can correct fragile positional regions without causing regressions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on RoPE (Su et al., 2023) and addresses a tension among three prior lines of analysis: Hong et al. (2024) showing low-frequency dimensions are critical for long-range dependencies, Barbero et al. (2025) showing low-frequency components are semantically entangled and partially dispensable, and Chiang & Yogatama (2025) showing high-frequency dimensions are under-utilized and removable. The authors repurpose classical spectral entropy from signal processing (Shannon entropy of power spectra) as a per-dimension diagnostic for RoPE, introducing two complementary variants (SpectrumFE via STFT, SequenceFE via DFT) that move beyond the coarse high/low-frequency dichotomy used in prior work. Llama-4's iRoPE architecture, which interleaves RoPE and NoPE layers, serves as a natural controlled comparison that prior analyses could not exploit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that RoPE-induced periodic dimensions (low SequenceFE) are largely redundant and can be attenuated at inference with no measurable downstream cost, opening a path toward RoPE-aware KV-cache compression and dimension pruning. More broadly, the finding that frequency bands and periodicity are model-dependent rather than fixed to absolute 'high' or 'low' frequency ranges reconciles previously conflicting reports and suggests the conventional dichotomy is insufficient. Limitations include inference-only interventions (no fine-tuning), inability to fully rule out compensatory effects from neighboring dimensions, and evaluation on only four architectures; a more granular causal test with single-pair perturbations or training-time ablations would strengthen the claims.
