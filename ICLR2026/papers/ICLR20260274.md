## Problem

Most AI-driven AMP design models optimize solely for antibacterial activity using rigid scoring heuristics, producing peptides with poor toxicity profiles, limited structural reliability, and restricted novelty. While LLM-based multi-agent systems show promise for complex scientific workflows, they remain open-loop and emit natural-language or heterogeneous scores that cannot be compiled into reproducible, trainable optimization signals. No prior system closes the loop between multi-agent evaluation consensus and executable reinforcement-learning reward design for multi-objective molecular generation.

## Input / Output

The user supplies only a target bacterium name and a dataset of known AMP sequences with experimentally measured MIC values (μg/mL, log10-transformed), sourced from DBAASP v3 and dbAMP 3.0. The system generates 1,000 candidate AMPs per run and retains the top 30 by predicted MIC for downstream analysis, repeated three times for 90 final sequences. Outputs include the novel peptide sequences alongside structured multi-objective scores (antibacterial activity, AMP likelihood, toxicity, structural reliability) and four-dimension meta-review texts (efficiency, safety, developmental structure, originality) that provide full auditability of every design decision.

## Method

MAC-AMP is a closed-loop pipeline of four modules: (1) a Property Prediction module that scores each candidate via a target-specific MIC regressor (fine-tuned ProtBERT adapted from BERT-AmPEP60), Macrel 1.5, ToxinPred 3.0, OmegaFold v1, ProtParam, and Foldseek 10; (2) an AI-Simulated Peer Review module where three heterogeneous LLM Reviewer agents (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5, Perplexity) annotate candidates with weighted lexicon tags across four dimensions, and an Area Chair agent resolves conflicts and computes a divergence-penalized meta-score; (3) an RL Refinement module in which a CS-based Reward Design agent and a Biomedical Reward Alignment agent co-design candidate reward functions, which are sandbox-tested and selected via Pareto optimization over three inner-loop rounds; and (4) a Peptide Generation module (GPT-2 with a 10-token soft prompt, inherited from AMP-Designer) trained with PPO under a stage-based adaptive schedule (3 stages × 15 epochs) where the reward function is redesigned at each stage boundary from fresh multi-agent consensus. The key architectural insight is that structured agent consensus is converted into executable, machine-actionable reward code, establishing a real-time feedback cycle that replaces static weighting or ad-hoc prompt iteration with principled closed-loop learning.

## Results

Across three target-specific bacterial tasks (E. coli, S. aureus, P. aeruginosa), MAC-AMP consistently achieves the best antibacterial activity (0.943, 0.931, 0.917), lowest toxicity (0.154, 0.137, 0.110), and highest structural reliability (0.873, 0.837, 0.850) among all compared models. On AMP likelihood, MAC-AMP scores 0.797–0.851, slightly below BroadAMP-GPT (0.821–0.858) but well above PepGAN and Diff-AMP (0.535–0.747). In broad-spectrum testing, E. coli-targeted MAC-AMP peptides generalize strongly to K. pneumoniae (0.98) and E. faecium (0.95), outperforming all baselines on more non-E. coli species than any other model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAC-AMP outperforms all four baselines on antibacterial activity, toxicity, and structural reliability across all three bacterial targets. Against AMP-Designer (Wang et al., 2025), MAC-AMP gains ~13 percentage points in activity (e.g., 0.943 vs. 0.807 on E. coli) and reduces toxicity by ~38% (0.154 vs. 0.251), while AMP-Designer retains a marginal AMP-likelihood edge (0.811 vs. 0.797). Against BroadAMP-GPT (Li et al., 2025), MAC-AMP achieves substantially lower toxicity (0.154 vs. 0.246 on E. coli; 0.110 vs. 0.449 on P. aeruginosa) and higher structural reliability (0.873 vs. 0.763), though BroadAMP-GPT scores slightly higher on AMP likelihood (0.821–0.858 vs. 0.797–0.851). Against the non-LLM baselines PepGAN (Tucs et al., 2020) and Diff-AMP (Wang et al., 2024a), MAC-AMP dominates on all four metrics, with the largest gaps in AMP likelihood (0.797 vs. 0.572/0.554) and structural reliability (0.873 vs. 0.637/0.752 on E. coli). In broad-spectrum testing, MAC-AMP's E. coli-targeted peptides achieve the highest activity scores against the most non-E. coli species (K. pneumoniae 0.98, E. faecium 0.95, P. aeruginosa 0.94), surpassing all baselines on generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAC-AMP builds directly on the AMP-Designer GPT-2 generator architecture (Wang et al., 2025) and adapts the BERT-AmPEP60 MIC predictor (Cai et al., 2025) for target-specific activity scoring, but departs fundamentally from the single-model sequence-optimization paradigm that characterizes prior AMP generators (PepGAN, Diff-AMP, AMP-Designer). Conceptually, it bridges two previously disconnected lines of work: LLM-based multi-agent collaboration systems (AutoGen, CAMEL, Virtual Lab) that produce natural-language outputs without executable training signals, and LLM-guided reward design (Eureka, RLAIF) that has not been integrated into multi-agent pipelines. The key architectural departure is the 'AI-simulated peer review' module, which converts heterogeneous agent evaluations into structured, quantifiable consensus scores that feed a PPO-based RL loop, closing the gap between multi-agent deliberation and stable reinforcement learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first closed-loop multi-agent system for molecular design, offering a principled alternative to static multi-objective weighting that is prone to reward hacking and diversity collapse. The cross-domain transferability claim (validated on a table-to-text task) suggests the MAC framework could generalize beyond AMPs to other molecular or scientific design problems. However, all validation is computational (predictor-based scores, MD simulations) with no wet-lab confirmation of activity or toxicity, and the system's reliance on three specific LLMs (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5, Perplexity) as Reviewer agents raises questions about robustness to model updates and the true value of multi-agent consensus over simpler reward shaping. Open questions include whether the closed-loop structure adds measurable benefit over a well-tuned single-reward PPO baseline at scale, and how the approach performs on targets with very small training datasets (e.g., E. faecium with only 352 examples).
