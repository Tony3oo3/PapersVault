## Problem

Existing GPS trajectory generation models, particularly diffusion-based approaches like DiffTraj, suffer from severe accuracy degradation when scaling from urban to metropolitan or nationwide spatial extents, because the fixed-magnitude noise injection in diffusion creates a signal-to-noise ratio collapse for fine-grained local trajectories. Additionally, prior work is largely confined to taxi data and requires 200–300 denoising steps for competitive quality, limiting both transportation-mode diversity and practical efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264077/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes as conditioning a departure time (288 bins), zone-level origin/destination IDs, transportation mode (5 classes), and standardized numeric trajectory features (average speed, inter-point distance, elapsed time, cumulative distance). During training, raw trajectories (~120 GPS points) are first compressed via RDP to 10 keypoints in a per-trajectory normalized bounded coordinate space; at inference, the 10-step Euler ODE output is resampled, denormalized back to geographic lat/lon, and trimmed or interpolated to match the conditioned travel duration, yielding a variable-length pseudo-GPS trajectory.

## Method

TrajFlow is the first flow-matching-based generative model for GPS trajectory generation, replacing diffusion's iterative denoising chain with a straight-path Conditional Flow Matching objective that regresses the vector field u_t(x|x₁) = x₁ − x₀ in a single training step. The vector field is parameterized by a U-Net (base channels 128, channel multipliers [1,2,2,2]) whose residual blocks receive a fused Wide & Deep condition embedding (linear projection of numeric features + MLP over categorical embeddings for time, OD, and mode) injected as a learned additive bias at every block. A key scalability mechanism is trajectory harmonization: each trajectory is individually normalized into a shared bounded space and compressed via RDP to ~10 keypoints, preventing tiny local displacements from being overwhelmed by nationwide-scale variations; an auxiliary supervised loss on fine OD coordinates within the conditioned zones further anchors spatial accuracy. Inference requires only 10 Euler ODE steps (step size 0.1), achieving competitive fidelity at roughly 30× lower inference cost than a 300-step DDPM baseline.

## Results

At the nationwide (Japan) scale, TrajFlow achieves DTW_med = 10.98 km, Fréchet_med = 0.192 km, and Density JS = 0.227, dramatically outperforming DiffTraj (DTW_med = 451.0 km, Fréchet_med = 5.33 km, Density JS = 0.673), TrajVAE (DTW_med = 135.4 km, Fréchet_med = 2.22 km), and TrajGAN (DTW_med = 403.3 km, Fréchet_med = 6.65 km). At the urban (Central Tokyo) scale, TrajFlow achieves DTW_med = 20.35 km and Fréchet_med = 0.304 km, while the ablated variant TrajFlow-w/o-RDP&OD reaches the best DTW_med = 8.18 km. Inference requires only ~10 ODE steps (~0.01 s/sample), compared to 200–300 denoising steps for the diffusion baseline to approach comparable (but still inferior) accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TrajFlow decisively outperforms all three baselines at the nationwide scale, where DiffTraj's DTW_med degrades to 451 km (41× worse than TrajFlow's 11.0 km) and its Density JS nearly triples (0.673 vs. 0.227). TrajVAE is the strongest baseline at nationwide scale (DTW_med = 135.4 km) but still lags TrajFlow by over 12× in median trajectory error and 2.3× in density JS. TrajGAN performs worst overall, with DTW_med values exceeding 400 km at both metropolitan and nationwide scales. At the urban (Central Tokyo) scale, the gap narrows: DiffTraj achieves DTW_med = 44.3 km vs. TrajFlow's 20.4 km, and TrajVAE reaches 32.9 km, but TrajFlow still leads on Fréchet and tail metrics (P90). Notably, at urban scale the ablated variant TrajFlow-w/o-RDP&OD (DTW_med = 8.2 km) outperforms the full model, suggesting the harmonization pipeline is most beneficial at larger spatial scales where SNR imbalance is severe.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the Conditional Flow Matching paradigm (Lipman et al., 2022) into GPS trajectory generation, replacing the iterative denoising chain of diffusion-based models such as DiffTraj (Zhu et al., 2023b), which it otherwise reuses for the Wide & Deep conditioning architecture. The key methodological departure is a trajectory harmonization-and-reconstruction pipeline (RDP compression + OD-conditioned normalization) that addresses the signal-to-noise-ratio collapse observed when diffusion noising is applied across heterogeneous spatial scales. Conceptually, the paper positions flow matching as a more stable alternative to ELBO-weighted diffusion objectives for multi-scale generative modeling, while the RDP-based dimensionality reduction borrows from classical polyline simplification rather than learned representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TrajFlow is the first GPS trajectory generator evaluated at urban, metropolitan, and nationwide scales simultaneously, opening the door to cross-region urban planning, traffic simulation, and disaster-response modeling that prior single-city models could not support. The ~10-step ODE inference versus 200–300 diffusion steps is a practical efficiency gain for large-scale deployment. However, the open-source validation on Chengdu and Xi'an (Appendix E) shows DiffTraj outperforming all TrajFlow variants, suggesting the flow-matching advantage may be specific to the multi-scale heterogeneity of the nationwide Blogwatcher dataset rather than a universal improvement. The model also does not capture individual-level preferences (no per-user attributes), limiting its applicability to personalized mobility simulation.
