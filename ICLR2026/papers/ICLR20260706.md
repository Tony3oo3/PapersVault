## Problem

Current audio large language models (ALLMs) lack geometric grounding in their encoders, capturing spectral and temporal patterns but ignoring spatial cues like direct-to-reverberation ratios, reverberation time, and room layout that determine how sound propagates. Additionally, existing spatial audio models such as BAT rely on coarse 4-bin categorical labels (front, back, left, right) and single-pass inference, preventing fine-grained direction-of-arrival estimation and interpretable multi-step spatial reasoning over complex multi-source scenes.

## Input / Output

At inference, OWL consumes only a 10-second binaural audio clip (processed into 4-channel features: left/right mel-spectrograms with 128 bins and sine/cosine IPD encodings) plus a free-form text question, and produces structured outputs including sound event labels, azimuth in 12 clock-based sectors (or 360 bins at 1° resolution during training), elevation as up/down, distance quantized in 0.5 m steps up to 10 m, binary Yes/No spatial reasoning answers, and multi-step chain-of-thought rationales. During training, the system additionally receives panoramic depth images (rendered at 20° FOV increments) and binaural room impulse responses as privileged geometric supervision, but these are discarded at test time.

## Method

The core contribution is SAGE, a geometry-aware binaural audio encoder that injects spatial structure through an auxiliary RIR reconstruction task: a ResNet-18 depth encoder fuses panoramic depth features with audio embeddings (from a 12-layer Transformer initialized from AudioMAE) and decodes a binaural RIR via transposed convolutions, supervised by an L1 + Energy Decay Curve loss. This privileged supervision regularizes the audio encoder to internalize geometry-aware representations without requiring depth at inference. OWL then couples the frozen SAGE encoder with a Q-Former projector (64 learnable queries, 8 cross-attention layers) and LLaMA-2-7B (adapted via LoRA, rank 8, α=32) through a three-stage curriculum: perceptual pre-training on event detection and direction estimation, relative geometry pre-training on binary spatial reasoning, and CoT instruction tuning that supervises both intermediate reasoning steps and final predictions, enabling interpretable multi-step spatial inference.

## Results

On BiDepth, OWL with CoT achieves 33.37% mAP (single-source detection), 46.17% DoA accuracy (12-bin), 23.29% DER (distance), 77.89% binary accuracy on spatial reasoning (Type III), and 76.53% BA on CoT reasoning (Type IV). On SpatialSoundQA (zero-shot), OWL reaches 78.31% DoA accuracy and 26.14% DER, surpassing BAT's 75.54% and 29.16% respectively. The SAGE encoder alone reduces mean angular error by 11° and distance error rate by 33.5% relative to Spatial-AST on BiDepth, while improving mAP by ~1.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OWL outperforms all seven baseline families across every reported metric. Against the closest spatial-audio competitor BAT, OWL gains +8.4% mAP on single-source detection (33.37 vs 24.97), +5.6% DoA accuracy under the matched 4-bin protocol (77.21 vs 71.59), −5.3% DER on distance (23.29 vs 28.61), +8.4% BA on spatial reasoning (77.89 vs 69.46), and +15.2% BA on CoT reasoning (76.53 vs 61.29). General-purpose audio-LLMs (VideoLLaMA2, RAVEN, AudioFlamingo2) are far behind, with detection mAP below 28% and spatial-reasoning BA below 20%, confirming that task-specific geometry-aware training is critical. Closed-source Gemini models (1.5-Pro, 2.5-Pro, 2.5-Flash) achieve only 31–33% detection mAP and do not support DoA, distance, or reasoning tasks under the evaluation protocol. At the encoder level, SAGE surpasses SELDNet by +10.3% mAP and −12.4° MAE on BiDepth, and outperforms Spatial-AST (BAT's encoder) by 23.6% in ER₂₀° and 31.3% in DER, with the largest gains in cross-dataset transfer (82% DER reduction). Adding CoT supervision to OWL yields a further +3.6% BA on Type III and +11.3% BA on Type IV over the no-CoT variant, validating the curriculum's staged reasoning design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spatial audio LLM line initiated by BAT (which performs single-step spatial QA with coarse 4-bin localization) by introducing two key departures: a geometry-aware encoder (SAGE) that uses panoramic depth images and RIR reconstruction as privileged training supervision, and a multi-step chain-of-thought reasoning mechanism grounded in source positions. It also builds on the SELD literature (SELDNet, Spatial-AST) by embedding detection and localization within an LLM framework that supports interpretable, multi-step spatial queries. The Q-Former + LLaMA-2-7B + LoRA architecture follows the standard audio-LLM recipe (Audio Flamingo, GAMA), but the novelty lies in the curriculum-trained CoT supervision and the auxiliary RIR-prediction loss that injects geometric structure without requiring depth at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The BiDepth dataset (1.1M QA 4-tuples coupling binaural audio, RIRs, depth, and QA) is the first large-scale resource providing explicit geometric supervision for audio spatial reasoning, and its release should accelerate follow-up work. The finding that depth-guided RIR reconstruction at training time yields an 11° MAE reduction and 33% DER improvement without any geometric input at inference is a practical recipe for geometry-aware audio encoders. Key limitations remain: all training data is synthetic (Matterport3D/SoundSpaces), elevation is biased toward the horizontal plane, and reasoning is restricted to single-turn binary or short-rationale QA. Extending to real recordings, multi-turn dialogue, and richer sensor fusion (vision, IMU) are the natural next steps the authors identify.
