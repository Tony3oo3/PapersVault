## Problem

Existing meta-learners for time-varying heterogeneous treatment effect estimation (IPW, DR, IVW) suffer from exploding estimation variance when treatment overlap is low, a problem that worsens exponentially with longer prediction horizons because sequence propensities are products of per-step propensity scores. No prior meta-learner simultaneously adjusts for time-varying confounding, is Neyman-orthogonal with respect to all nuisance functions, and remains stable in low-overlap regimes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260679/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a patient trajectory history (sequential covariates, past treatments, and past outcomes up to time t) together with two interventional binary treatment sequences of length τ+1, and outputs a CATE (difference in conditional average potential outcomes) or a CAPO (single conditional average potential outcome) as a function of the observed history. The approach is fully model-agnostic: the nuisance estimators and second-stage regressor can be any neural architecture (transformers or LSTMs in experiments), and the method requires only a sample split for cross-fitting rather than access to counterfactual outcomes.

## Method

The WO-learner introduces overlap-weighted pseudo-outcomes that up-weight training samples with a higher probability of receiving the interventional treatment sequences, thereby avoiding extreme inverse-propensity weights in low-overlap regions. The weight functions are defined as conditional expectations of products of propensity scores (for CAPO) or products of both sequences' propensity weights (for CATE), and the pseudo-outcomes combine standard DR pseudo-outcomes with an orthogonalized version of these weights. The resulting population risk is proven to be Neyman-orthogonal with respect to all nuisance functions—propensity scores, response functions, and the weight functions themselves—so that estimation errors in any nuisance propagate only as second-order bias. Training proceeds via cross-fitting: nuisance functions are learned on one fold, pseudo-outcomes are constructed on the held-out fold, and a parametric second-stage model minimizes the empirical weighted squared loss. The method generalizes the R-learner's overlap weighting from the static (τ=0) setting to arbitrary multi-step-ahead prediction horizons with time-varying confounding.

## Results

On the low-overlap synthetic dataset D^γ, the WO-learner achieves RMSE of 0.02–0.10 across all overlap levels (γ = 0.5 to 6.5), outperforming the best baseline by 6.5%–58.4% relative improvement. At the most extreme low-overlap setting (γ = 6.5), WO-learner maintains RMSE of 0.08 ± 0.02 while IPW degrades to 0.47 ± 0.54 and DR to 0.20 ± 0.10. On semi-synthetic MIMIC-III data (Table 4), WO-learner is the only method that remains stable across prediction horizons τ = 2, 3, 4 (RMSE 0.03, 0.15, 0.17), whereas IVW explodes to 879.80 at τ = 4 and DR reaches 1.57.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the WO-learner against all five meta-learners from Frauen et al. (2025a): HA, RA, IPW, DR, and IVW. The WO-learner wins on every setting tested. Against HA, the advantage is large and consistent (e.g., 0.03 vs. 0.17 RMSE at γ=0.5) because HA targets an incorrect estimand under time-varying confounding. Against RA, the WO-learner improves by 21–58% relative RMSE, with the gap widening at higher overlap levels where RA's non-orthogonal response function estimates accumulate bias. Against IPW and DR, the WO-learner's advantage is most dramatic at low overlap (large γ): at γ=6.5, IPW reaches 0.47±0.54 and DR reaches 0.20±0.10, while WO stays at 0.08±0.02. Against IVW, the WO-learner avoids the catastrophic instability seen in the LSTM ablation (Table 5), where IVW reaches RMSE of 1351.68 at γ=6.5 while WO remains at 0.08. On semi-synthetic MIMIC-III data, the WO-learner is the only method that does not degrade with increasing prediction horizon, achieving 0.17 RMSE at τ=4 versus 1.57 (DR), 5.23 (IPW), and 879.80 (IVW).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the meta-learner framework for time-varying HTE estimation established by Frauen et al. (2025a), which formalized five adjustment strategies (HA, RA, IPW, DR, IVW) but left the critical low-overlap problem unaddressed. The WO-learner introduces overlap-weighted population risk as a principled alternative to inverse propensity weighting, and is explicitly positioned as a non-trivial generalization of the R-learner (Nie & Wager, 2021) from the static (τ=0) setting to multi-step-ahead prediction. Unlike the IVW learner, which is not Neyman-orthogonal with respect to its own weight functions, the WO-learner achieves orthogonality with respect to all nuisance functions including the overlap weights themselves, eliminating first-order bias propagation through the recursive time steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exponential decay of treatment overlap with prediction horizon is a fundamental practical barrier to applying existing meta-learners in longitudinal medical settings, and this paper provides the first meta-learner that is simultaneously Neyman-orthogonal and designed for low-overlap regimes. The method is fully model-agnostic and could be adopted as a default adjustment strategy for time-varying HTE estimation in personalized medicine. Key limitations include validation primarily on synthetic and semi-synthetic data (MIMIC-III-based), with real-world counterfactual validation remaining an open challenge; the authors also note that in edge cases with exceptionally accurate nuisance estimates or uniformly strong overlap, simpler DR-based methods may suffice.
