## Problem

The Pairformer backbone in AlphaFold3-style structure predictors relies on triangle attention, which requires L separate attention operations per layer and scales cubically with sequence length, making large-scale applications like virtual ligand screening, proteome-wide folding, and hallucination-based binder design prohibitively expensive (e.g., Boltz-1 takes over 15 minutes for a single 2048-token sequence on an A100). The paper asks whether triangle multiplication alone—implemented efficiently via matrix multiplications—can preserve the higher-order geometric reasoning over residue triplets that triangle attention provides, without the attendant memory and runtime overhead.

## Input / Output

The model takes a collection of K biomolecular sequences (amino acids, nucleic acids, or small-molecule heavy atoms) along with their multiple sequence alignments, embedding them into a single sequence representation s ∈ R^{L×Cs} and a pair representation z ∈ R^{L×L×Cz} that encodes relational context between all residue pairs. A 4-layer MSA module extracts evolutionary signals into z before the backbone processes it. The diffusion module then denoises all-atom 3D coordinates conditioned on the backbone outputs, producing 5 sampled poses per complex (evaluated at 200 sampling steps and 10 recycling steps), with inference supporting up to 512 tokens and 4608 atoms by default.

## Method

Pairmixer is an attention-free backbone that updates only the pair representation z through alternating incoming and outgoing triangle multiplication operations and a feed-forward network, removing both triangle attention and sequence updates from the Pairformer. Triangle multiplication computes, for each edge z_ij, a sum over all intermediate nodes k of element-wise products of projected features (W_a z_ik) ⊙ (W_b z_jk), effectively reasoning over residue triplets via dense matrix multiplications (torch.einsum) rather than L separate attention passes. The initial sequence representation s^init is passed unchanged to the diffusion module since the MSA module already encodes evolutionary information directly into z. The key empirical finding is that triangle multiplication learns to capture sparse long-range interactions by adjusting the magnitudes of pair representation entries—low-norm dropout of 75% preserves performance while random dropout of 25% degrades it—demonstrating that the dense operation implicitly identifies the small subset of critical triplet interactions that triangle attention would select explicitly. This yields 4× faster inference on 2048-token sequences and 34% fewer GPU-days for training while matching Pairformer accuracy across protein, ligand, antibody, nucleic acid, and RNA benchmarks.

## Results

On the RCSB test set (Large Phase 2, 48 backbone layers), Pairmixer achieves a mean lDDT of 0.78, matching the Pairformer (Boltz-1) baseline, while requiring only 66% of the training GPU-days (269 vs. 421). Inference speed improves from 1.6× at 512 tokens to 4× at 2048 tokens (1000 s → 250 s on a GH200 GPU). Across diverse biomolecular benchmarks (protein-ligand, antibody-antigen, protein-nucleic acid, RNA), Pairmixer matches or slightly exceeds Pairformer on all metrics, with at most a 1% drop on PoseBusters ligand RMSD<2 (0.67 vs. 0.68). In the BoltzDesign binder-design framework, Pairmixer delivers 2–2.6× faster sampling and extends the maximum target size from 500 to 650 residues (+30%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Pairformer (Boltz-1) backbone, Pairmixer matches the headline lDDT (0.78 vs. 0.78) and lDDT-PLI (0.57 vs. 0.57) at the large scale, slightly lags on DOCKQ>0.23 (0.63 vs. 0.64), and marginally improves ligand RMSD<2 (0.55 vs. 0.54), all while training 34% faster (269 vs. 421 GPU-days). Against the sequence-only Transformer baseline, Pairmixer substantially outperforms on every metric (e.g., lDDT 0.78 vs. 0.70, DOCKQ>0.23 0.63 vs. 0.53), confirming that pair representations and triangle multiplication are essential for structural reasoning. On diverse biomolecular benchmarks (Table 1), Pairmixer ties or slightly beats Pairformer on antibody-antigen ICS/IPS (0.51/0.66 vs. 0.50/0.65) and RNA lDDT (0.59 vs. 0.58), while matching exactly on protein-nucleic acid DOCKQ>0.23 (0.23). At the system level (Figures 6, 12), Pairmixer is competitive with AlphaFold3, Chai-1, and Boltz-1 on both RCSB and CASP15, with the only notable gap being a 1-point DOCKQ>0.23 difference versus Boltz-1. In the BoltzDesign binder-design setting, Pairmixer (BindFast) achieves 2.0–2.6× speedups over Pairformer and handles targets up to 650 residues where Pairformer hits OOM at 500 residues.

## Relation to Prior Work

Pairmixer builds directly on the Boltz-1/AlphaFold3 cofolding pipeline, replacing the Pairformer backbone with an attention-free architecture that retains only triangle multiplication and feed-forward networks. It extends the line of work on triangle-multiplication-based feature mixing (Genie2, MiniFold, MSA Pairformer) from monomeric or generative settings to the full cofolding problem, and draws on attention-free architectures (MLP-Mixer, FNet) for its token-mixing philosophy. Unlike SimpleFold, which removes pair representations entirely, Pairmixer preserves the 2-D pair representation as the sole carrier of structural information, arguing that the pair representation plus triangle multiplication is sufficient to capture the geometric constraints that triangle attention was designed to model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4× inference speedup on long sequences and 34% training cost reduction make large-scale downstream applications—proteome-wide folding, high-throughput ligand screening, and hallucination-based binder design—practically feasible where they were previously prohibitive. The finding that triangle attention is dispensable challenges a core architectural assumption in the AlphaFold3 lineage and suggests the field may be over-parameterizing its backbones. Open questions include whether the simplification holds for even larger complexes (beyond ~800 residues where both architectures hit OOM), whether the lack of sequence attention limits reasoning about certain long-range couplings, and whether the approach transfers to other modalities (e.g., cryo-EM map fitting) where the pair representation may be less informative.
