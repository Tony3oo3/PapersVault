## Problem

Existing world model benchmarks evaluate visual generation quality in isolation (open-loop), leaving unresolved whether world models actually help embodied agents succeed at tasks. No prior benchmark tests whether generated worlds enhance perception, planning, and control within a closed-loop agent-environment interaction, making it impossible to judge the practical embodied utility of heterogeneous world models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261894/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives an egocentric RGB observation (panoramic 360° view plus a 90° front view) and a task goal (a text question, a goal image, or a manipulation instruction), and produces a committed action decision plus, for recognition/QA tasks, a category label or natural-language answer. The unified action API translates candidate action sequences into three control formats—text prompts, camera trajectories (x, y, azimuth), or low-level 7-DoF gripper actions—depending on the target world model's expected input. Evaluation spans four closed-loop tasks: Active Recognition (551 episodes, 29 MP3D scenes), ImageNav (144 episodes, 87 HM3D scenes), A-EQA (184 questions, 54 scenes), and Robotic Manipulation (200 episodes across 4 RLBench tasks).

## Method

World-In-World wraps any visual world model in a closed-loop planning interface built from three components: (1) a unified proposal-simulation-revision loop formalized as policy-guided beam search, where a proposal policy samples M candidate action sequences, the world model performs counterfactual rollouts for each, and a revision policy scores and selects the best plan before execution; (2) a standardized action API that harmonizes heterogeneous control inputs (text, camera trajectory, low-level actions) so diverse world models operate under one evaluation protocol; and (3) a post-training recipe that fine-tunes pretrained video generators (SVD, Wan2.1/2.2, Cosmos-P2, LTX-Video) on modest action-observation data from the target simulator, revealing a data scaling law where more post-training data outperforms upgrading the pretrained backbone. The key empirical finding is that fine-grained controllability (action-to-prediction alignment) correlates more strongly with task success than visual quality scores, and that inference-time compute (more simulated futures per decision) yields substantial closed-loop gains.

## Results

On Active Recognition (AR), the best proprietary model (Runway Gen4) achieves 64.79% SR with 4.06 mean steps, while the best open-source post-trained model (Wan2.2† A14B) reaches 62.43% SR, both substantially outperforming the VLM-only baseline (50.27% SR, 6.24 steps). On ImageNav, Wan2.2† A14B attains 46.53% SR and 34.61 SPL versus the VLM baseline's 35.42% SR and 25.88 SPL. On A-EQA, LTX-Video† achieves the highest answer score of 48.6 (vs. 45.7 for the VLM baseline), and on Robotic Manipulation, SVD† reaches 46.5% SR (vs. 44.5% for the VLM baseline). A key finding is that post-training a smaller model (e.g., SVD†, 1.5B) with action-observation data can match or exceed a much larger zero-shot generator (e.g., Wan2.2 A14B), and that controllability correlates more strongly with task success than visual generation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The VLM-only baseline (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) is outperformed by every world-model-augmented configuration across all four tasks, confirming that closed-loop world-model planning adds value. Among zero-shot video generators, Wan2.2 (A14B) is the strongest, reaching 59.53% AR SR and 43.05% ImageNav SR, but it is surpassed by post-trained variants: Wan2.1† (62.61% AR, 45.14% ImageNav) and Wan2.2† A14B (62.43% AR, 46.53% ImageNav) both exceed it, demonstrating that action-conditioned post-training is more effective than simply upgrading the pretrained generator. The proprietary Runway Gen4 achieves the highest AR accuracy (64.79%) but was not evaluated on ImageNav. Image-based world models (PathDreamer, SE3DS) provide modest gains over the VLM baseline (e.g., 56.99–57.53% AR vs. 50.27%) but are consistently outperformed by video-based models. On Robotic Manipulation, gains are smaller (SVD†: 46.5% SR vs. 44.5% VLM baseline), indicating that contact-rich dynamics remain a challenge for current visual world models. Notably, a simple LPIPS-based revision policy with SVD† (47.92% ImageNav SR) outperforms the VLM-based revision policy (43.05%), suggesting that the revision strategy can be a larger performance lever than the world model itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant evaluation paradigm for generative world models, which has centered on open-loop visual quality metrics (VBench, WorldModelBench) or single-task controllability scores (WorldScore, VP2). It repurposes the model-predictive-control / beam-search planning framework—previously applied to low-dimensional state-space models—into a unified closed-loop interface that wraps heterogeneous video generators (SVD, Wan2.1/2.2, LTX-Video, HunyuanVideo, Cosmos, NWM, PathDreamer, SE3DS) behind a standardized action API. The post-training recipe extends the transfer-learning literature on adapting pretrained video diffusion models by introducing a data-scaling study with action-observation pairs drawn from the same action space as downstream embodied tasks, a dimension not previously characterized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a critical gap: models with the highest VBench aesthetic and image-quality scores do not achieve the best closed-loop task success, while fine-grained action controllability is the stronger predictor. This reframes the optimization target for world-model developers from visual fidelity to action-conditioned reliability. Open challenges remain—manipulation gains are modest, long-horizon consistency is still poor, and the computational cost of multi-rollout planning limits real-time deployment—so the paper sets up a clear research agenda around physics-aware generation, spatial/episodic memory, and efficient inference. As the first multi-task, closed-loop, task-success-focused benchmark for modern video generators, World-In-World is likely to become a standard reference point for the world-model-for-embodied-AI community.
