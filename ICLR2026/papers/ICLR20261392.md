## Problem

Existing online inventory optimization (OIO) algorithms provide only static regret guarantees, comparing performance to a fixed order-up-to level that is unsuitable under demand fluctuations. The carryover stock constraint makes the decision's feasible region a strict subset of the comparator's, preventing direct application of standard dynamic OCO algorithms and causing linear regret in naive two-layer architectures. This paper asks whether a near-optimal dynamic regret bound of Õ(√(L_max(1+P_T)T)) can be achieved in the OIO setting.

## Input / Output

Each round, the algorithm receives a subgradient g_t of the convex loss at the current order-up-to level and the carryover stock vector x_{t+1} (which satisfies x_{t+1}^i ∈ [0, y_t^i]). The subgradient can be computed without observing full demand—for example, with Newsvendor loss, only whether a stockout occurred is needed. The output is the next order-up-to level y_{t+1} ∈ C(x_{t+1}), constrained by both per-item lower bounds from carryover stock and a linear-sum warehouse capacity constraint ∑y_{t+1}^i ≤ D.

## Method

The algorithm employs a two-stage projection: a base learner (OGD or SOGD) proposes a decision ŷ_{t+1} in C(0) using only the capacity constraint, which is then projected onto C(x_{t+1}) to satisfy carryover stock lower bounds. The key technical insight (Lemma 1) shows that the regret gap between the projected decision y_t and the base learner's ŷ_t is bounded by switching costs of the base learner scaled by the current cycle length, thereby connecting OIO to Smoothed OCO and eliminating the carryover stock difficulty. A doubling trick restarts the base learner with an updated L parameter whenever the observed cycle length exceeds the current estimate, handling unknown L_max. For the base learner, SOGD (using Discounted-Normal-Predictor combiners) achieves the near-optimal Õ(√(L_max(1+P_T)T log T)) dynamic regret without requiring P_T a priori, while OGD achieves the same order when P_T is known.

## Results

The paper establishes a near-optimal dynamic regret bound of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{L_{\max}(1+P_T)T})$ for online inventory optimization, along with a static regret bound of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{L_{\max}T})$, improving upon the existing $\mathcal{O}(L_{\max}\sqrt{T})$ by a factor of $\sqrt{L_{\max}}$. A matching lower bound of $\Omega(\sqrt{L_{\max}T})$ is proven, confirming near-optimality. In experiments with a single-item Newsvendor system under sinusoidal demand ($L_{\max} \sim \mathcal{O}(\log T)$), both OGD and SOGD variants with the doubling trick significantly outperform the MaxCOSD baseline, with the doubling-trick versions sometimes achieving lower regret than their counterparts with known $L_{\max}$ due to more aggressive learning rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is MaxCOSD (Hihat et al., 2023), the only prior algorithm with a theoretical guarantee for the OIO setting. Theoretically, this paper improves the static regret from $\mathcal{O}(L_{\max}\sqrt{T})$ (MaxCOSD and all prior works) to $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{L_{\max}T})$, a $\sqrt{L_{\max}}$ improvement, and provides the first dynamic regret guarantee for OIO. Empirically, in the single-item Newsvendor experiment with sinusoidal demand, both the OGD and SOGD variants (with and without the doubling trick) achieve substantially lower cumulative regret than MaxCOSD across all horizons tested. The paper notes that the doubling-trick versions sometimes outperform their known-$L_{\max}$ counterparts because the smaller learning rate used with known $L_{\max}$ slows convergence from the initial order-up-to level. No other baselines (e.g., vanilla OGD without the two-stage projection) are included, limiting the empirical comparison to a single prior method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Online Inventory Optimization (OIO) framework of Hihat et al. (2023), which established the problem formulation and a static-regret algorithm (MaxCOSD), by tackling the harder dynamic regret problem in non-stationary demand environments. The key technical departure is a two-stage projection strategy that reduces the OIO problem to Smoothed OCO (SOCO), allowing the authors to leverage existing SOCO algorithms (OGD, SOGD with Discounted-Normal-Predictor) as base learners. This bridges two previously disconnected lines of work: inventory management under OCO and dynamic regret minimization via expert aggregation (Ader-style meta-algorithms). The doubling trick for unknown sell-out period L_max is borrowed from standard online learning but applied here in a novel way to handle the time-dependent switching costs induced by carryover stock.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves the open question raised by Hihat et al. (2023) about whether sublinear dynamic regret is achievable in OIO, and provides the first matching Ω(√(L_max T)) lower bound, establishing near-optimality. The OIO-to-SOCO connection is a conceptual contribution that may inform other constrained online optimization problems where the feasible region depends on past decisions. However, the treatment is limited to linear capacity constraints, no lead time, and no fixed-order costs, all of which are common in practical inventory systems. Extending the framework to these settings, or to general convex constraints, remains open and would broaden the practical applicability.
