## Problem

The standard benchmark for evaluating Source Independent Transferability Estimation (SITE) metrics is fundamentally flawed: its model pool is dominated by size-varying ResNets and DenseNets, producing a static leaderboard where a trivial dataset-agnostic ranking heuristic outperforms all sophisticated metrics. This paper empirically demonstrates that current evaluation protocols artificially inflate perceived SITE metric performance, creating a critical disconnect between reported results and real-world model selection challenges.

## Input / Output

Each SITE metric takes a pre-trained model's feature extractor and a labeled target dataset, producing a scalar transferability score per candidate model that should rank models by predicted fine-tuned accuracy. The standard benchmark evaluates 11 models (ResNets 34–152, DenseNets 121–201, MobileNet, MNASNet, InceptionV3, GoogleNet) across 6 datasets (CIFAR-10/100, Pets, Aircraft, Food-101, DTD) using weighted Kendall's tau. The proposed benchmark replaces this with 6 architecturally diverse models (Twins-SVT, XCiT, CoaT, DeiT, MaxViT, MViT v2) of matched parameter counts and 15 challenging datasets from Meta-Album that avoid the >99% accuracy saturation of the original set.

## Method

The paper conducts a three-part empirical critique of the standard benchmark: (1) sequential model ablation showing all SITE metrics drop below τ_w = 0.6 once over-represented large variants are removed, (2) a static ranking heuristic (ordered by model size with ResNet/DenseNet alternation) that achieves τ_w = 0.91 versus 0.57 for the best SITE metric LogME, and (3) pairwise difference correlation analysis revealing that score gaps (e.g., a 0.09 LogME difference) correspond to wildly varying accuracy gaps (0.5%–2.5%). The authors then propose four best practices—diverse non-trivial model space, matched computational budgets, datasets with performance headroom, and engineered rank dispersion—and construct a new benchmark implementing these guidelines. On the new benchmark, no SITE metric performs consistently well, and the static ranker's advantage collapses (mean τ_w = 0.31 vs. 0.13–0.17 for SITE metrics), confirming that the original benchmark's high scores were an artifact of its flawed design.

## Results

On the standard SITE benchmark (6 datasets, 11 models), a simple dataset-agnostic static ranking based on model size achieves a mean weighted Kendall's τ_w of 0.91, dramatically outperforming the best SITE metric LogME (τ_w = 0.573) and all other methods. On the paper's proposed improved benchmark (15 diverse datasets, 6 architecturally distinct models with matched parameter counts), no SITE metric performs consistently: the best average τ_w is 0.17 (GBC), while the static ranker achieves 0.31, and many metrics yield near-zero or negative correlations. Model ablation experiments confirm that most SITE metrics drop below τ_w = 0.6 once oversized variants (ResNet-152, ResNet-101, DenseNet-201, DenseNet-169) are removed from the search space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates six SITE metrics against a trivial static ranking heuristic on both the standard and proposed benchmarks. On the standard benchmark, the static ranking (ResNet-152 ≻ DenseNet-201 ≻ ResNet-101 ≻ …) achieves τ_w = 0.91 on average, beating LogME (0.573), NLEEP (0.553), H-Score (0.552), SFDA (0.448), TransRate (0.195), and GBC (0.007) on every single dataset. On the proposed improved benchmark with architecturally diverse models (Twins-SVT, XCiT, CoaT, DeiT, MaxViT, MViT-v2) and 15 challenging datasets, all SITE metrics collapse: GBC leads at τ_w = 0.17, followed by SFDA (0.15), TransRate (0.13), LogME (0.061), H-Score (0.06), and NLEEP (0.04), while the static ranker scores 0.31. The key finding is that no SITE metric reliably outperforms a fixed model-size heuristic, exposing that the standard benchmark rewards memorization of a static hierarchy rather than genuine task-specific transferability estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not propose a new SITE metric but instead performs a meta-evaluation of the benchmarking practices that underpin the entire transferability estimation literature. It builds conceptually on the NAS Checklist (Lindauer & Hutter, 2020) by adapting benchmark-construction best practices to the SITE domain, and it extends observations from Agostinelli et al. (2022), Ibrahim et al. (2021), and Fang et al. (2023) about metric instability and domain-shift sensitivity into a systematic critique of the standard model pool and dataset suite. Unlike prior works that acknowledge benchmark limitations while still using the same impractical model spaces (Chaves et al., 2024; Kazemi et al., 2025; Bolya et al., 2022), this paper empirically demonstrates the consequences of those limitations and provides a concrete alternative benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a dataset-agnostic static ranking (τ_w = 0.91) outperforms every published SITE metric (best: LogME at 0.57) calls into question the validity of a large body of reported results in the transferability estimation literature and forces the community to reconsider what its metrics actually measure. The proposed best practices and Meta-Album-based benchmark offer an immediately actionable path forward, though the paper is limited to image classification and does not address the sensitivity of ground-truth rankings to fine-tuning hyperparameters, which remains a significant confound. Open questions include whether the static-leaderboard problem persists in more diverse model spaces, how to incorporate fine-tuning strategy into transferability prediction, and whether the findings generalize to NLP, object detection, and medical imaging.
