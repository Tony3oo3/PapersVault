## Problem

The RL4LLM community has produced a proliferation of RL optimization techniques (normalization, clipping, filtering, loss aggregation) with contradictory recommendations—e.g., GRPO advocates group-level normalization while REINFORCE++ favors batch-level, and Dr. GRPO explicitly recommends removing variance normalization that GRPO includes. Inconsistent experimental settings, training data, and model initializations across prior work have led to conflicting conclusions, leaving practitioners without clear guidelines on which techniques to combine and in what scenarios.

## Input / Output

The framework takes math problem prompts (sampled from SimpleRL-Zoo-Data and DeepMath-103k at Easy/Medium/Hard difficulty tiers) and a base or aligned LLM (Qwen3-4B/8B, Llama3-8B) as the policy. Training samples 8 responses per prompt with a maximum of 8192 tokens, using a global batch size of 1024 (128 prompts × 8 rollouts). The trained policy outputs a step-by-step reasoning chain with the final answer enclosed in \boxed{}, evaluated on six math benchmarks (MATH-500, OlympiadBench, MinervaMath, AIME24-25, AMC23) and out-of-domain tasks (BigCodeBench, GPQA, MMLU-Pro).

## Method

The paper conducts 160+ independent RL training experiments on the open-source ROLL framework to isolate and evaluate each technique (advantage normalization, Clip-Higher, loss aggregation granularity, overlong filtering) across model sizes, architectures, and data difficulties. The key empirical findings are: (1) removing the standard deviation term stabilizes training when reward distributions are highly concentrated; (2) computing the mean at the group level and the standard deviation at the batch level yields the most robust normalization; (3) Clip-Higher primarily benefits aligned models by mitigating entropy collapse; (4) token-level loss aggregation helps base models but not aligned ones. Building on these insights, the authors propose Lite PPO—a minimalist combination of just group-mean/batch-std advantage normalization and token-level loss aggregation applied to critic-free policies with vanilla PPO loss—which consistently outperforms GRPO and DAPO on non-aligned models and remains competitive on aligned models.

## Results

On Qwen3-8B (aligned) trained on the Hard dataset, Lite PPO achieves an average accuracy of 66.96% across six math benchmarks, outperforming DAPO (64.17%) by 2.79 points. On Llama3-8B, Lite PPO reaches 25.53% average accuracy versus 23.67% for DAPO and 22.63% for GRPO. On out-of-domain tasks, Lite PPO leads on all four benchmarks: LCB-v5 (25.08), LCB-v6 (19.71), GPQA (46.63), and MMLU-Pro (62.38). The method uses only two techniques—group-mean/batch-std advantage normalization and token-level loss aggregation—applied to vanilla PPO loss without a critic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Lite PPO consistently outperforms both GRPO and DAPO across all tested settings. Against GRPO on Llama3-8B, Lite PPO gains +2.90 points in average accuracy (25.53 vs. 22.63), with the largest margin on AIME24 (+5.4 points). Against DAPO, the advantage is +1.86 points on Llama3-8B (25.53 vs. 23.67) and +2.79 points on Qwen3-8B trained on Hard data (66.96 vs. 64.17). On the Easy dataset with Qwen3-8B, Lite PPO still leads DAPO by 1.51 points (62.17 vs. 60.66). On out-of-domain generalization (LCB, GPQA, MMLU-Pro), Lite PPO achieves the best pass@1 on all four tasks, beating DAPO by 1.07, 0.71, 1.55, and 1.78 points respectively. Notably, Lite PPO achieves these gains with only two techniques (group-mean/batch-std normalization + token-level loss) versus DAPO's five components (group normalization, clip-higher, overlong reward shaping, token-level loss, dynamic sampling).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a systematic, controlled dissection of the rapidly proliferating RL-for-LLM-reasoning techniques (GRPO, DAPO, REINFORCE++, Dr. GRPO, etc.) that have emerged since DeepSeek R1 and OpenAI o1. It builds directly on PPO/REINFORCE as the unified baseline and selectively recombines components from GRPO (group-level mean), REINFORCE++ (batch-level standard deviation), and DAPO (token-level loss, Clip-Higher) into a minimalist recipe called Lite PPO. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the explicit isolation of each technique's contribution under a single open-source framework (ROLL) with matched model sizes, data difficulties, and hyperparameters, rather than the confounded comparisons that have characterized the field to date.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unified, reproducible set of guidelines for selecting RL techniques based on model type (base vs. aligned), data difficulty, and reward distribution characteristics, directly addressing the confusion caused by contradictory recommendations in the literature. The finding that a two-technique combination (group-mean/batch-std normalization + token-level loss) consistently outperforms heavily-engineered methods like GRPO and DAPO challenges the prevailing trend of stacking more components. Open questions include whether these findings generalize beyond mathematical reasoning to code generation or open-ended tasks, whether the observed clip-bound 'scaling law' persists at larger model scales (beyond 8B), and whether Lite PPO's advantage holds under different reward structures (e.g., dense process rewards vs. sparse outcome rewards).
