## Problem

DPO's derivation assumes a tabular policy class that can represent any conditional distribution, but real LLMs use parametric policy classes with far fewer parameters than the number of (prompt, response) pairs. This means the true preference-generating reward function generally lies outside the implicit reward manifold of the policy class, making DPO a misspecified estimator that can exhibit preference reversal, overall reward decrease, and high sensitivity to the preference data distribution—even with infinite clean data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard preference triplets (prompt, chosen response, rejected response) and a frozen base/reference policy (the SFT model). Beyond the usual policy parameters θ ∈ R^d, AuxDPO introduces 2n auxiliary scalar variables δ (one per response in each of the n preference pairs), constrained to the null space of the base policy's scaled log-likelihood gradient matrix A_{ρ,θ₀}. The total trainable parameter count is d + 2n, which remains O(d) since typically n ≪ d for LLM-scale models.

## Method

The paper shows that DPO loss minimization is equivalent to a weighted reverse-KL projection of the true reward r* onto the d-dimensional implicit reward manifold R^β ⊂ R^m, and that in the misspecified case (r* ∉ R^β) the projection result depends on pairwise preference data frequencies and can yield a policy that worsens reward. To fix this, AuxDPO introduces auxiliary variables δ constrained to N(A_{ρ,θ₀}), the null space of the base policy's scaled gradient matrix, which adds degrees of freedom along directions invisible to the policy gradient and makes the augmented reward representation span all of R^m, eliminating misspecification. The empirical loss augments the standard DPO log-sigmoid term with δ(s,a_w) − δ(s,a_l) and adds a Monte-Carlo penalty λ‖(1/2n)Σ δ(s,a)∇log π_{θ₀}(a|s)‖² to enforce the null-space constraint. Proposition 9 guarantees that for sufficiently large β, the AuxDPO solution recovers the RLHF-optimal policy θ* up to O(ε) error, and experiments on Llama3.1-8B, Llama3.2-1B, and Qwen3-0.6B show consistent improvements over DPO, IPO, and DPOP on both in-domain and out-of-domain preference alignment.

## Results

AuxDPO consistently achieves the highest percentage improvement in mean accuracy over the base policy across all three models (Llama3.1-8B, Llama3.2-1B, Qwen3-0.6B) and both evaluation datasets (MMLU-Pro, RewardBench V2) under both in-domain (ID) and out-of-domain (OOD) settings. On Llama3.1-8B, AuxDPO reaches 63.26% on MMLU-Pro ID and 32.44% on RewardBench V2 OOD, compared to DPO's 57.14% and 14.31% respectively. The OOD gains are particularly pronounced: AuxDPO more than doubles DPO's improvement on RewardBench V2 OOD (32.44% vs. 14.31%) and MMLU-Pro OOD (14.28% vs. 8.16%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AuxDPO outperforms all three baselines (DPO, IPO, DPOP) across every model, dataset, and evaluation setting in Table 1. Against DPO, the most direct comparison, AuxDPO gains 4–11 percentage points on ID settings and 6–29 points on OOD settings; the largest gap appears on Llama3.2-1B RewardBench V2 OOD (43.27% vs. 14.11%). Against IPO, AuxDPO leads by 2–12 points on ID and 4–23 points on OOD. Against DPOP, the margin is smaller on ID (1–15 points) but still substantial on OOD (13–24 points). Notably, on Qwen3-0.6B RewardBench V2 OOD, DPO, IPO, and DPOP all degrade the base policy (negative values: −8.16%, −8.23%, −6.25%), while AuxDPO improves it by 18.36%, highlighting its robustness to the misspecification failure modes identified in the theory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) and two-stage RLHF (Ziegler et al., 2019), providing the first systematic geometric characterization of DPO as a misspecified statistical estimator over a parametric policy class. It departs from prior analyses of DPO's shortcomings—gradient-dynamics studies (Pal et al., 2024; Razin et al., 2024), coverage-condition analyses (Xu et al., 2024b; Song et al., 2024), and the closest work by Shi et al. (2025)—by working at the level of the loss minimizer rather than individual gradient steps, and by connecting DPO's implicit reward manifold to the natural-gradient structure of RLHF policy optimization. The proposed AuxDPO algorithm introduces auxiliary null-space variables derived from the Fisher information geometry of the base policy, a design principle absent from prior DPO variants (IPO, DPOP, etc.) that modify the loss form or add regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that DPO can strictly decrease expected reward and reverse preference orderings even under infinite clean BTL data challenges the core assumption that DPO is a valid one-step surrogate for RLHF, with direct implications for the thousands of models aligned via DPO in practice. AuxDPO offers a principled, low-overhead fix (adding 2n auxiliary parameters) that consistently outperforms DPO, IPO, and DPOP across three model scales and two benchmarks, suggesting a practical path forward. Open questions include whether the local (large-β) theoretical guarantees extend to the smaller β values used in practice, whether the null-space penalty can be tightened or replaced by a hard constraint at scale, and whether the approach generalizes to multi-turn or on-policy preference settings.
