## Problem

Existing NeRF-based pre-training methods for autonomous driving couple neural radiance fields with view transformation backbones, forcing continuous radiance field assumptions onto discrete, rigid voxel grids. This misalignment produces blurry 3D representations that limit downstream perception, and the pre-trained NeRF network is discarded during downstream tasks, wasting the enhanced 3D knowledge gained during pre-training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260102/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes single-timestep multi-view RGB images (6 cameras, 360° FOV; 1600×900 for detection, 800×450 for rendering/HD map/occupancy) and LiDAR point clouds for depth supervision. During pre-training, it outputs rendered RGB and depth maps via volumetric rendering along camera rays; for downstream tasks, it scatters points uniformly in 3D space around the vehicle and reshapes the resulting embeddings into task-specific formats (e.g., X×Y×Z×C for occupancy, query points for detection, or vectorized elements for HD maps).

## Method

NeRP3D replaces voxel-based view transformation with a NeRF-resembled point-based architecture that directly samples 3D points at arbitrary spatial locations—ray-wise for volumetric rendering and uniform spatial sampling for perception—and predicts each point's representation via deformable cross-attention with 2D image features, avoiding any voxel grid or interpolation. Geometry is modeled with a signed distance function (SDF) converted to opacity, and appearance with view-dependent RGB, optimized jointly with RGB reconstruction, LiDAR depth supervision, and multi-view consistency losses. Crucially, the same continuous point representation and NeRF network are preserved across both pre-training and downstream fine-tuning stages without adding or removing modules, so the enhanced 3D knowledge transfers directly to detection, occupancy, and HD map tasks. A coordinate contraction function (inspired by Mip-NeRF 360) handles unbounded driving environments, and the unified design enables adaptive sampling strategies tailored to each task while maintaining a coherent spatial understanding.

## Results

On nuScenes 3D object detection at 1600×900 resolution, NeRP3D achieves 47.3 NDS and 42.8 mAP, outperforming UniPAD (45.5 NDS, 41.6 mAP) by 1.8 NDS and 1.2 mAP. For the pretext scene reconstruction task, NeRP3D dramatically improves RGB rendering quality to 33.42 PSNR (vs. 21.14 for UniPAD and 18.82 for SelfOcc) and achieves the best depth estimation with 0.183 Abs Rel. On downstream occupancy prediction, NeRP3D reaches 35.49 mIoU, surpassing UniPAD (34.05) and SelfOcc (29.65), and on HD map construction it attains 59.1 mAP versus 57.8 for UniPAD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeRP3D consistently outperforms all NeRF-based pre-training baselines across every evaluated task. Against UniPAD (Yang et al., 2024), the primary NeRF pre-training competitor, NeRP3D gains 2.1 NDS / 2.1 mAP at 800×450 and 1.8 NDS / 1.2 mAP at 1600×900 on 3D detection, 1.44 mIoU on occupancy, 1.3 mAP on HD map, and a striking 12.3 dB PSNR on RGB reconstruction. Against SelfOcc (Huang et al., 2024), NeRP3D improves occupancy mIoU by 5.84 points and RGB PSNR by 14.6 dB, while also achieving lower depth Abs Rel (0.183 vs. 0.311). Against non-NeRF view-transformation baselines (UVTR-C, BEVFormerV2, TPVFormer, BEVFormer, CTF-Occ, MapTR-tiny), NeRP3D with its own pre-training surpasses each on their respective tasks: 47.3 NDS vs. 46.7 (BEVFormerV2), 35.49 mIoU vs. 28.53 (CTF-Occ), and 59.1 mAP vs. 49.9 (MapTR-tiny). The cross-dataset zero-shot transfer (Argoverse 2 → nuScenes) further confirms the advantage: NeRP3D achieves 28.24 PSNR and 0.626 Abs Rel versus UniPAD's 18.67 PSNR and 0.985 Abs Rel, demonstrating that the continuous point-based representation generalizes far better than fixed-grid view transformation under domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeRP3D departs from the dominant NeRF-based pre-training paradigm (UniPAD, SelfOcc) that couples radiance fields with view transformation backbones, arguing this combination forces conflicting priors—discrete/rigid voxel grids versus continuous/adaptive neural fields—into a single pipeline. It builds on NeRF's volumetric rendering (Mildenhall et al., 2021), NeuS's SDF-to-opacity formulation (Wang et al., 2021), Mip-NeRF 360's unbounded coordinate parameterization (Barron et al., 2022), and Deformable DeTR's cross-attention (Zhu et al., 2020), but reorganizes them into a unified point-based architecture that samples 3D locations directly without any voxelized intermediate representation. The key architectural departure is preserving the NeRF network across both pre-training and downstream tasks, rather than discarding it after pre-training as prior methods do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnosis—conflicting priors between view transformation and radiance fields—for why existing NeRF-based pre-training methods produce blurry, under-segmented 3D features, and demonstrates that removing the voxel bottleneck yields dramatically sharper reconstructions (PSNR 33.4 vs. 21.1) and modest but consistent downstream gains across detection, occupancy, and HD mapping. Limitations include high computational cost from point-based sampling, reliance on LiDAR for depth beyond the region of interest, and the need to adapt NeRF outputs to existing detection heads. Open questions include whether temporal consistency, density-based filtering, or 3D Gaussian Splatting can reduce the computational overhead while preserving the continuous representation advantage.
