## Problem

Classic referring expression comprehension benchmarks (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg) have become near-saturated by modern MLLMs (>90% accuracy), yet they remain weak probes of genuine visual reasoning: expressions average only ~3 words, images contain few same-category distractors, and redundant descriptors enable shortcut grounding that bypasses full expression understanding. This paper asks whether a benchmark can be designed that forces models to perform both textual reasoning over the expression and visual reasoning over the image, without relying on such shortcuts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263577/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each instance pairs an RGB image (sourced from COCO or OpenImages v7, filtered to contain at least 3 same-category candidate instances) with a natural language referring expression averaging 11.5 words and a 21.25% negation ratio. The model must output a bounding box in absolute [x1, y1, x2, y2] coordinates; success is measured by IoU thresholds of 0.5, 0.75, and 0.9. Expressions are deliberately minimally sufficient—containing only the descriptors needed to disambiguate the target from its hard distractors—so no single descriptor subset can localize the target without processing the full expression.

## Method

Ref-Adv is constructed via a two-stage LLM-authored pipeline (GPT-4o) followed by three-annotator human verification (18.7% keep rate). Stage one performs similarity judgement: the LLM identifies a 'hard distractor' pair (target + most similar same-category object) and elicits group-level discriminators (separating the pair from other distractors) and intra-pair discriminators (separating target from its hard distractor). Stage two composes minimally sufficient referring expressions from combinations of these discriminators, optionally using negation of the hard distractor's attributes, while explicitly forbidding numeric label references. Quality is validated through three ablation protocols: a bag-of-words randomization test (showing Ref-Adv degrades more than RefCOCO under word-order removal), a one-descriptor-deletion sufficiency test (showing fewer grounding shortcuts), and a fixed-prompt bias test (showing less data-source leakage). The benchmark is then used to evaluate 13 contemporary MLLMs (GPT-4o, Claude-3.5 Sonnet, Gemini 2.5 Flash/Pro, Qwen2.5-VL 7B/32B/72B, InternVL-3 7B/14B/38B/78B, GLM-4.5V, CogVLM-Grounding) both with and without Chain-of-Thought, with GPT-4o and Claude-3.5 additionally evaluated via Set-of-Marks + Semantic-SAM to compensate for limited native grounding.

## Results

On Ref-Adv, the best-performing model is GPT-4o with Chain-of-Thought and Set-of-Marks, achieving 63.7% Acc@0.5, 38.4% Acc@0.75, and 34.1% mAcc. Among open-source models, InternVL-3-78B (58.4% Acc@0.5) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (58.3% Acc@0.5) lead, while Claude-3.5 Sonnet trails at 45.2% Acc@0.5. Strikingly, all models that exceed 90% Acc@0.5 on RefCOCO(+/g) drop to the 45–64% range on Ref-Adv, exposing a large gap between benchmark saturation and genuine visual reasoning. CoT consistently improves Ref-Adv accuracy (e.g., +4.2% for Qwen2.5-VL-72B) but slightly hurts RefCOCO(+/g) performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o (with CoT + SoM) achieves the highest Ref-Adv Acc@0.5 at 63.7%, yet this is a dramatic drop from its >90% on RefCOCO(+/g), confirming that even the strongest proprietary model relies heavily on shortcuts in easier settings. Gemini 2.5-Flash (59.4%) and Gemini 2.5-Pro (59.1%) with CoT are competitive but fall ~4–5 points below GPT-4o, and both show a 3–4 point further drop in the ≥7-distractor subgroup. Among open-source models, InternVL-3-78B (58.4%) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (58.3%) lead, with InternVL-3-38B (57.2%) and GLM-4.5V (56.9%) close behind; all trail GPT-4o by 5–15 points. Claude-3.5 Sonnet is the weakest top-tier model at 45.2% Acc@0.5 (even with CoT + SoM), suggesting its grounding pipeline is particularly vulnerable to hard distractors. CogVLM-Grounding (51.5%, no CoT support) sits in the middle of the open-source pack. Across all 13 models, the consistent pattern is a 30–45 point accuracy collapse from RefCOCO(+/g) to Ref-Adv, demonstrating that near-saturated classic-benchmark scores substantially overstate genuine visual reasoning and grounding capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classic Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) task by introducing Ref-Adv, a benchmark designed to eliminate the shortcut solutions that plague RefCOCO(+/g). It departs from prior attempts to increase difficulty—Cops-Ref (Chen et al., 2020) used GQA scene-graph templates that reduced naturalness, and HC-RefLoCo (Wei et al., 2024) relied on very long (≥90-word) expressions that created descriptor-distractor imbalance—by instead enforcing a 'minimally sufficient' expression design with hard same-category distractors. Methodologically, it repurposes LLMs (GPT-4o) as a two-stage data-generation tool (similarity judgement followed by expression composition) rather than as a single-shot captioner, and it evaluates a broad suite of contemporary MLLMs (GPT-4o, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL-3, Gemini 2.5, GLM-4.5V, Claude-3.5, CogVLM-Grounding) in a setting where the task formulation itself is unchanged.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that models scoring >90% on RefCOCO(+/g) collapse to roughly 50–60% on Ref-Adv—exposes a substantial overestimation of MLLM visual reasoning and grounding ability, with implications for how the field interprets near-saturated benchmark scores. The multi-pronged ablation suite (bag-of-words perturbation, single-descriptor deletion, fixed-prompt bias test) provides unusually thorough validation that the difficulty is genuine rather than an artifact of expression length or vocabulary. Open questions include whether targeted training on hard-distractor scenarios can close the gap, whether the CoT benefit observed on Ref-Adv transfers to other reasoning-heavy visual tasks, and whether the benchmark's reliance on LLM-generated expressions (despite human verification) introduces subtle distributional biases. As a benchmark, Ref-Adv is likely to become a standard stress test for MLLM grounding, complementing rather than replacing RefCOCO(+/g).
