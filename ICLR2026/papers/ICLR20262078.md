## Problem

Optimizing boreal forest management for both carbon sequestration and permafrost preservation involves complex, physically-grounded trade-offs that current prediction-oriented forest models and oversimplified RL environments cannot address. No existing benchmark combines a realistic coupled energy-carbon-water simulator with explicit multi-objective RL capabilities, leaving the learning difficulty of these asymmetric objectives uncharacterized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262078/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a 105-dimensional observation vector (generalist mode) or 43-dimensional vector (site-specific mode) encoding ecological state, site climate parameters, 17-dimensional disturbance/management history, 10-dimensional age-class distributions, carbon pool details, penalty indicators, a scalar preference weight w_C ∈ [0,1], and 62 episode-level site parameters. The agent outputs a single discrete action from 25 combinations (5 density changes × 5 conifer fractions), and the environment returns a 2D reward vector [r_carbon, r_thaw] normalized to [-1,1] per step over 50-year episodes, with the thaw component using an asymmetric 2.5:1 warming-to-cooling penalty derived from conductive heat flux across the permafrost boundary.

## Method

BoreaRL couples a process-based simulator (BoreaRL-Sim) solving multi-node energy balance equations (canopy, trunk, snow, surface soil, deep soil) at n-minute resolution with a modular MORL environment wrapper conforming to the mo-gymnasium API. The simulator tracks coupled carbon (LUE-based GPP, Q10 respiration, HWP accounting), water (snowpack, soil moisture, evapotranspiration), and energy fluxes, with stochastic fire/insect disturbances conditioned on climate and stand state. Three preference-conditioned baselines are evaluated: Variable-λ EUPG (dynamic weights in observation), PPO Gated (separate heads for planting vs. non-planting actions), and Curriculum PPO (adaptive episode selection via a fixed random-projection network and a performance-driven threshold that expands/contracts the training site distribution). The key empirical finding is an asymmetric learning landscape where carbon objectives converge rapidly while thaw objectives show minimal progress, and that the naive curriculum mechanism—filtering out high-warming-potential sites—outperforms standard gradient-descent preference-conditioned approaches in both scalarized reward and hypervolume trade-off coverage.

## Results

In generalist mode, Curriculum PPO achieves a scalarized reward of 8.5±3.0 and hypervolume of 84.3, substantially outperforming PPO Gated (4.7±6.0, hypervolume 23.6) and Variable λ EUPG (1.7±5.0, hypervolume 14.2). A key finding is asymmetric learning difficulty: carbon-focused policies (λ=(1.0, 0.0)) learn rapidly while thaw-focused policies (λ=(0.0, 1.0)) show minimal improvement across both site-specific and generalist settings. Curriculum PPO also achieves the best preference adherence with only 50% λ-monotonicity violations, compared to 100% for PPO Gated and 66.7% for Variable λ EUPG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Curriculum PPO decisively outperforms all other RL methods and heuristic baselines in generalist mode, achieving a hypervolume of 84.3 versus 23.6 for PPO Gated and 14.2 for Variable λ EUPG — a 3.6× and 6× improvement respectively. PPO Gated, while beating the best heuristic baseline (Conifer Restoration at hypervolume 21.4), still falls far short of Curriculum PPO, with its aggressive carbon-farming strategy (high density, high conifer) producing negative thaw rewards at high carbon preference weights. Variable λ EUPG performs near or below heuristic baselines, with a scalarized reward of only 1.7±5.0, indicating that standard preference-conditioned gradient descent fails under environmental stochasticity. Among heuristics, Target Density (1000 stems/ha) and Conifer Restoration (100% conifer) achieve moderate scalarized rewards (~4.1–4.3) but cannot capture the multi-objective front, while Zero Density Change and +100 Density Change perform poorly (negative scalarized rewards). The paper also shows that even a naive adaptive episode selection mechanism (Curriculum PPO's random-projection selector) provides the critical advantage, suggesting that site selection is more important than the specific MORL algorithm in this domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on RL for forest management (Malo et al., 2021; Bone & Dragičević, 2010), which relied on simplified growth models, by coupling a process-based energy-water-carbon simulator (drawing on CLM5 and CLASSIC formulations) with modern MORL algorithms. The multi-objective formulation builds directly on EUPG (Roijers et al., 2018) and preference-conditioned policies (Abels et al., 2019), while the PPO Gated and Curriculum PPO baselines modify standard PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) with action gating and adaptive episode selection. The key departure from prior forest models (CBM-CFS3, CLASSIC) is the shift from prediction to sequential policy optimization under explicit multi-objective trade-offs, and the departure from prior RL-for-forestry work is the physically-grounded, multi-objective, and stochastic generalist setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Boreal forests store 30–40% of terrestrial carbon in climate-vulnerable permafrost soils, making the carbon-permafrost trade-off a global climate imperative that current decision-support tools cannot address. The finding that standard preference-conditioned MORL approaches fail in generalist settings while a simple adaptive curriculum outperforms them is a useful negative result for the MORL community, suggesting that episode-level difficulty filtering may be a more effective inductive bias than preference conditioning alone in physically-grounded domains. The benchmark is explicitly positioned as far from saturated, with open questions around credit assignment over 50-year horizons, non-linear scalarization, and integration of economic and biodiversity objectives. Limitations include the single-environment scope, the absence of field-data calibration, and the fact that even the best agent (Curriculum PPO) achieves only moderate thaw performance, indicating that robust climate-adaptive management remains an open problem.
