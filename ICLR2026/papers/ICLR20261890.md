## Problem

Existing speech language model benchmarks evaluate linguistic, acoustic, reasoning, or dialogue abilities in isolation, without capturing how these skills jointly interact to produce empathetic, emotionally intelligent conversation. It remains unclear whether SLMs can perceive non-lexical vocal cues (prosody, emotion, physiological signals, environmental sounds) alongside spoken words and generate responses that are both contextually appropriate and emotionally aligned with the speaker's vocal delivery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261890/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds each SLM the same semantically neutral script rendered in three controlled vocal-style variations (target, alternative, neutral expressiveness) as speech audio, paired with text instructions. At Level 1, models produce transcripts (WER, SemSim) and MCQ answers for vocal-cue recognition; at Level 2, they answer integrated-reasoning MCQs requiring joint content-and-voice inference; at Level 3, they generate open-domain text and speech responses scored on four GPT-based 5-point text dimensions (context fit, naturalness, colloquialism, speech-relevance) plus audio-level metrics (NISQA, UTMOS, EmoAlign, and the Vocal Empathy Score via Gemini-2.5-Pro). The shared-script design across all levels enables direct correlation analysis between perceptual, reasoning, and conversational performance.

## Method

EchoMind is structured as a three-level hierarchical cognitive pipeline—understanding (ASR + vocal-cue MCQs), reasoning (10 integrated-inference MCQ types), and conversation (open-domain response generation)—where all tasks are grounded in the same 1,137 semantically neutral scripts to isolate the effect of vocal delivery from transcript content. The empathy framework organizes 39 vocal attributes into 3 coarse dimensions (speaker, paralinguistic, environmental) and 12 fine-grained categories. Audio synthesis uses a multi-strategy pipeline: Doubao TTS for speaker attributes, GPT-4o-mini-TTS with attribute-specific prompts for paralinguistic cues (with manual one-by-one quality checks), and AudioCaps background mixing for environmental context; a parallel human-recorded subset of 491 scripts (EchoMind-Human) is produced by two professional voice actors to enable synthetic-vs-human comparisons. Evaluation combines reference-based objective metrics (BLEU, ROUGE-L, METEOR, BERTScore, WER) with reference-free Model-as-a-judge ratings (GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-Pro) and human evaluation on a sampled subset, validated via Spearman correlation and Arena-style pairwise comparisons.

## Results

Across 12 evaluated SLMs on EchoMind, GPT-4o-Audio achieves the best performance on nearly all metrics: 66.25% voice understanding accuracy, 68.04% reasoning accuracy, 3.42/5 on C_SpeechRel (speech information relevance), 3.34/5 VES (vocal empathy score), and 51.31% EmoAlign. Among open-source models, Audio-Flamingo3+Think leads voice understanding (65.16%) while DeSTA2.5-Audio leads reasoning (63.04%). Critically, no model exceeds 4.0/5 on C_SpeechRel, and VES scores range only from 1.40 (EchoX) to 3.34 (GPT-4o-Audio), indicating that even state-of-the-art systems struggle to adapt vocal delivery to reflect the interlocutor's emotional state.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o-Audio (closed-source) outperforms all 11 open-source SLMs on voice understanding (66.25% vs. best open-source 65.16% for Audio-Flamingo3+Think), reasoning (68.04% vs. 63.04% for DeSTA2.5-Audio), and all conversation metrics including C_SpeechRel (3.42 vs. 3.36 for DeSTA2.5-Audio) and VES (3.34 vs. 3.29 for Kimi-Audio). Among open-source models, Audio-Flamingo3 and its Think variant dominate voice understanding but lag in reasoning (58.80% and 42.95% respectively), while DeSTA2.5-Audio achieves the best reasoning accuracy (63.04%) but weaker voice understanding (56.68%). A striking finding is that no model—closed or open—exceeds 4.0/5 on C_SpeechRel, and VES scores cluster tightly between 1.40 and 3.34, revealing a universal bottleneck in vocal-cue-aware response generation. The gap between GPT-4o-Audio and the best open-source model is most pronounced on EmoAlign (51.31% vs. 43.17% for LLaMA-Omni2), suggesting that emotional alignment in generated speech remains particularly challenging for open architectures. Human evaluation (Table 5) confirms the automatic rankings but reveals that GPT-4o-Audio's advantage narrows on C_RespNat and VES, where its formal, longer responses are perceived as less naturally empathetic than the warmer tones of Step-Audio or Qwen2.5-Omni-7B.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from existing audio-based benchmarks (AudioBench, Dynamic-SUPERB, MMAR, MMAU) and dialogue-centered benchmarks (SD-Eval, VoxDialog, URO-Bench) that evaluate linguistic, acoustic, reasoning, or conversational abilities in isolation. EchoMind carves new ground by introducing an interrelated multi-level design where all tasks share identical semantically neutral scripts rendered in controlled vocal-style variations, creating a common context that enables direct attribution of response differences to vocal delivery and supports cross-level correlation analysis. The empathy-oriented framework spanning 39 vocal attributes across speaker, paralinguistic, and environmental dimensions extends prior work that typically targets a single vocal cue type or a narrow set of emotions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even state-of-the-art SLMs like GPT-4o-Audio score below 3.5/5 on speech-information relevance, indicating a persistent gap in leveraging vocal cues for empathetic response generation that no current architecture adequately addresses. The finding that human-recorded speech is substantially harder than TTS speech, and that some models perform worse with explicit instructions than without, highlights practical limitations for real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the bottleneck lies in vocal-cue perception, in the integration of acoustic and linguistic signals during reasoning, or in the generation of prosodically and emotionally aligned speech responses, and whether scaling or architectural changes (e.g., parallel token decoding) can close this gap.
