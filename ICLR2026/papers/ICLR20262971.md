## Problem

Existing robust learning methods either target bounded adversarial perturbations or, in the case of Wasserstein Distributionally Robust Learning (WDRL), require the regression function to be Lipschitz or convex to admit a tractable formulation. The paper shows that WDRL fails to improve robustness over standard MSE when the regression function violates these structural assumptions, leaving a gap for systematic robustness to global additive noise (sensor noise, quantization) in expressive deep regression models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262971/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes input features X (2D images representing PDE initial conditions at a fixed resolution, or time-series sequences of length 336) paired with noisy response variables Y + σε, where ε is i.i.d. noise of variance σ². It produces regression predictions (final PDE state images or forecast sequences of length 96). The key operational detail is that the loss is computed at the batch level: within each mini-batch, the empirical distribution of all predictions is matched against the empirical distribution of all responses, rather than enforcing pointwise correspondence. Batch size is the primary additional hyperparameter, and the method is agnostic to the underlying model architecture.

## Method

Wasserstein Batch Matching (WBM) replaces the pointwise MSE loss with the 2-Wasserstein distance between the empirical distributions of batch predictions {f_θ(X_i)} and batch responses {Y_i}, effectively relaxing the fixed feature-to-response correspondence. This is computed as a linear program over coupling matrices (solvable via Sinkhorn iterations), making it differentiable with respect to θ via the envelope theorem and compatible with standard SGD training of arbitrary architectures. The core theoretical insight is that the WBM loss's first-order noise sensitivity contains a cubic penalty term (Y_i − f_θ(X_j))³ that suppresses the linear noise contribution when deviations are small, yielding a smaller prefactor than MSE's purely linear scaling. This model-agnostic formulation avoids the Lipschitz/convexity constraints that limit WDRL, and the resulting linear-program cost O(s) in response dimension is an order of magnitude cheaper than WDRL's O(s³) minimax solve.

## Results

The paper evaluates WBM regression against MSE, WDRL, and divergence-based DRO methods on Navier-Stokes and wave equation operator learning (30% corrupted data) and ETDataset electric grid forecasting, reporting mean absolute error (MAE) averaged over 13 samples. WBM consistently outperforms MSE across all settings, with the most pronounced gains under heavy-tail Cauchy noise where WDRL degrades significantly below even standard MSE. WBM also outperforms CVaR DRO and Chi-Sq DRO while achieving at least a 10-fold reduction in computational cost, since WBM requires no additional hyper-parameter tuning beyond standard deep learning settings. The method demonstrates robustness to both Gaussian and Cauchy noise as well as distribution shift (training on clean data, testing on noisy data).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against WDRL (Mohajerin Esfahani & Kuhn, 2018), WBM clearly wins under Cauchy (heavy-tail) noise, where WDRL performs significantly worse than even standard MSE training on Navier-Stokes operator learning (Fig. 1, 6). Under Gaussian noise, WDRL and MSE yield comparable results with no noticeable improvement from WDRL, while WBM still provides a consistent edge. Against the divergence-based DRO methods CVaR DRO and Chi-Sq DRO (Duchi & Namkoong, 2021), WBM achieves lower test MAE on the Navier-Stokes benchmark (Fig. 7) while being at least an order of magnitude cheaper computationally, since those methods require additional hyper-parameter tuning (e.g., divergence radius) that WBM does not. Overall, WBM is the only method that simultaneously handles both bounded (Gaussian) and unbounded (Cauchy) noise without requiring structural assumptions (convexity or Lipschitzness) on the regression function, which WDRL necessitates for its tractable reformulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the Wasserstein Distributionally Robust Learning (WDRL) paradigm, which uses the Wasserstein ball as an ambiguity set around the empirical distribution to define a worst-case loss. While WDRL has been widely adopted for robust regression and classification, the authors show it requires structural assumptions (Lipschitz or convex-concave) on the regression function that are incompatible with expressive deep networks. The proposed Wasserstein Batch Matching (WBM) loss repurposes the Wasserstein distance in a fundamentally different way: instead of defining an ambiguity set over joint distributions, it directly computes the optimal transport cost between batch-level empirical distributions of predictions and targets, making it agnostic to model architecture. The theoretical analysis draws on Sinkhorn regularization and stochastic approximation literature (Dieuleveut et al., 2020) to characterize noise scaling, while the empirical evaluation builds on the Convolutional Neural Operator and TSMixer architectures from recent PDE-learning and time-series forecasting work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a previously unacknowledged limitation of WDRL: it can perform worse than plain MSE under heavy-tailed noise when the regression function lacks convexity or Lipschitz structure, which is the norm for deep networks. This negative result is significant because WDRL has been a dominant framework in the DRO literature. WBM offers a practical, model-agnostic alternative with an order-of-magnitude computational advantage over divergence-based DRO methods, directly addressing the costly pre-processing bottleneck in real-world noisy data pipelines. Key limitations include sensitivity to correlated noise, underperformance in the low-noise regime, and difficulty with discontinuous functions (e.g., shock waves), leaving open the question of how to extend distributional matching to structured or non-i.i.d. noise.
