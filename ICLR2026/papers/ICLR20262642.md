## Problem

Neural networks degrade substantially when label error rates exceed a modest threshold, yet most existing noisy-label methods require a clean validation set, sufficient class balance, or carefully tuned hyperparameters to identify and down-weight contaminated samples. This paper addresses the gap by proposing an architecture-independent, virtually hyperparameter-free wrapper that enhances any supervised training methodology without assuming access to clean data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard feature-label pairs—RGB images (32×32 CIFAR, 256×256 tissue necrosis) or text (IMDb reviews, Toxic Comments)—along with their (potentially noisy) labels, and outputs a trained classifier producing class labels. Crucially, RRM is a wrapper: it takes any existing training loss J(θ; x, y) and replaces it with a reweighted version, so the input/output interface of the underlying model is unchanged. The only additional input is an optional contamination estimate C′ used for auto-tuning the pruning threshold.

## Method

RRM formulates training as a min-min problem: the outer minimization over model parameters θ and the inner minimization over a reweighting vector u ∈ U that shifts probability mass away from high-loss samples at a cost of γ per unit total variation distance from the uniform empirical distribution. The inner problem is a tractable linear program whose solution (Theorem 3.1) has a closed-form characterization: samples with loss exceeding c_min + γ are pruned to zero weight, and the freed probability mass is redistributed uniformly among the lowest-loss samples. The algorithm (Algorithm 1) alternates between σ epochs of SGD on θ (optionally with FGSM adversarial perturbation for A-RRM) and a single-pass reweighting step via Corollary 3.1.1, making it computationally lightweight. The method connects to optimistic Wasserstein DRO, showing RRM is a constrained (hence more conservative) version of the full data-driven Wasserstein formulation, and requires no clean validation set or architecture-specific modifications.

## Results

On CIFAR-100N (real-world human-annotated noise), RRM-wrapped ProMix achieves 74.19% test accuracy (state-of-the-art) and RRM-wrapped Divide-Mix achieves 73.98% (state-of-the-art), surpassing all prior methods. On CIFAR-10N, RRM-wrapped Divide-Mix reaches 94.75% (SOTA), a 2.19-point improvement over the unmodified Divide-Mix (92.56%). On synthetic CIFAR-10 with 30% label contamination, RRM-wrapped MSE achieves 89.95% accuracy versus 86.34% for MSE alone, and RRM-wrapped CCE reaches 88.49% versus 81.90% for CCE alone. In the adversarial setting (MNIST, 20% contamination, ε_test=0), A-RRM maintains 96% accuracy while standard adversarial training collapses to 58%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RRM-wrapped Divide-Mix outperforms the unmodified Divide-Mix by 2.19 points on CIFAR-10N (94.75% vs 92.56%) and 2.85 points on CIFAR-100N (73.98% vs 71.13%), achieving state-of-the-art on both. RRM-wrapped ProMix improves CIFAR-100N accuracy from 73.79% to 74.19% (new SOTA) while remaining essentially unchanged on CIFAR-10N (96.32% vs 96.34%). On Clothing1M, RRM-wrapped CC reaches 75.69%, nearly matching the best method LRA-diffusion (75.7%) and outperforming Knockoffs-SPR (75.2%) and Meta-Weight-Net (73.72%). On Food-101N, RRM-wrapped CCE achieves 84.21%, surpassing CleanNet (83.95%) though trailing LRA-diffusion (93.42%) and SURE (88.0%). In the adversarial MNIST experiment, A-RRM consistently outperforms standard adversarial training (AT) at all contamination levels above 0% when test-time perturbation is lower than training perturbation, with the gap reaching 38 points at 20% contamination and ε_test=0 (96% vs 58%). RRM-wrapped CCE also outperforms GCE and JoCoR on Clothing1M (71.48% vs lower baselines), demonstrating that even a simple baseline benefits substantially from the RRM wrapper.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Rockafellian Relaxation framework (Royset et al., 2023) and connects it to optimistic Wasserstein distributionally robust optimization (Staib & Jegelka, 2017; Nguyen et al., 2019), showing that RRM is a constrained version of the data-driven Wasserstein DRO formulation. It departs from prior loss-reweighting methods (Ren et al., 2018; Shu et al., 2019) by eliminating the need for a clean validation set or class-balance assumptions, instead using a total-variation-regularized reweighting that can be solved as a tractable linear program. The key conceptual positioning is as an architecture-independent wrapper: rather than proposing a new training procedure, RRM modifies the supervised loss component of any existing method (ProMix, DivideMix, CC, CCE), making it composable with state-of-the-art noisy-label techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in RRM's ability to enhance already-strong methods without requiring clean validation data or extensive hyperparameter tuning, addressing a common deployment constraint in industrial settings where label quality is imperfect but curation resources are limited. The A-RRM experiments revealing that standard adversarial training collapses under train-test perturbation mismatch while A-RRM remains stable suggest a broader robustness benefit beyond label noise. Open questions include how the method scales to very large-scale datasets (beyond CIFAR/Clothing1M), whether the contamination estimate C' can be learned end-to-end, and whether the reweighting mechanism generalizes to regression or multi-task settings. The 'close-call' recovery behavior—where near-zero-weighted samples regain positive weight—hints at a self-correcting property that warrants deeper theoretical understanding.
