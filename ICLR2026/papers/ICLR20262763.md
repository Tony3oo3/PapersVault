## Problem

Existing LLM-based reward design methods (Eureka, ROSKA) generate goal-oriented rewards but neglect state exploration, causing agents to stall in local optima on high-degree-of-freedom tasks like dexterous manipulation. Traditional count-based exploration bonuses are task-agnostic, wasting compute on irrelevant states, and the trade-off between exploration and exploitation is fixed manually rather than adapted to policy progress. The paper addresses how to make exploration task-relevant and dynamically balanced with goal-oriented rewards during iterative policy optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a natural-language task description and the environment's source code, which are fed to an LLM (DeepSeek-V3) to jointly generate a goal-oriented reward function and an affordance state space (AFS) mapping that projects high-dimensional robot states into a low-dimensional discrete space of task-relevant behavioral features (e.g., door-handle distance, angular displacement). The output is a trained PPO policy, but the system also produces the reward function, the AFS mapping, and a dynamically tuned beta coefficient that balances exploration bonus against goal reward. Across 5 iterations, the LLM refines both the reward and the AFS mapping using sparse-reward policy evaluations as feedback, while the elimination-expansion mechanism prunes and mutates candidate (reward, AFS, beta) triples in parallel.

## Method

PoRSE introduces an LLM-generated Affordance State Space (AFS) that discretizes task-relevant behavioral dimensions, enabling a count-based curiosity bonus (R_e = lambda/sqrt(visits)) that is inherently aligned with task objectives rather than generic state novelty. The In-Policy-Improvement Grounding (IPG) process coordinates three components: goal-oriented rewards (R_g) anchor exploration direction, the AFS-based exploration bonus (R_e) expands the optimization space, and a dynamically adjusted beta coefficient (R_total = beta*R_e + (2-beta)*R_g) shifts emphasis between exploration and exploitation based on real-time policy performance. To avoid training every candidate from scratch, IPG uses LLM-bootstrapping elimination-expansion filtering (LEF) that prunes the worst-performing policy every 500 epochs and injects mutated beta or alpha variants at the 1500-epoch midpoint, combined with policy fusion (theta_f = alpha*theta_best + (1-alpha)*theta_random) where the LLM determines the inheritance ratio alpha instead of expensive Bayesian optimization. The beta and alpha coefficients are alternated across iterations, with beta decreasing over training (more exploitation as the policy converges) and alpha increasing (greater reliance on the best historical policy).

## Results

On 24 robotic manipulation and locomotion tasks (20 Bi-DexHands, 4 Isaac Gym), PoRSE achieves the best Maximum Training Success (MTS) on 15/16 moderate-difficulty tasks and all 8 hard tasks. On hard tasks, PoRSE reaches perfect MTS (1.000) on DoorCloseOutward and Kettle (vs. Eureka's 0.553 and 0.742), and is the first method to solve TwoCatch (MTS 0.349) where all baselines score near zero. On moderate tasks, PoRSE attains perfect MTS on 5/16 tasks and outperforms the human-designed reward baseline by up to 25.3% (GraspAndPlace: 0.984 vs. 0.785). The only loss is on Humanoid locomotion, where ROSKA (8.917) slightly edges PoRSE (8.454).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PoRSE outperforms Eureka on 23/24 tasks, with the largest gaps on hard manipulation tasks (e.g., TwoCatch: 0.349 vs. 0.001; BlockStack: 0.753 vs. 0.254; PushBlock: 0.378 vs. 0.025). Against ROSKA, PoRSE wins on 23/24 tasks; the sole exception is Humanoid locomotion where ROSKA's goal-oriented co-evolution slightly favors bipedal running (8.917 vs. 8.454). Compared to human-designed rewards, PoRSE surpasses the expert baseline on 14/16 moderate tasks and 7/8 hard tasks, with the most dramatic gains on tasks requiring fine bimanual coordination (GraspAndPlace +25.3%, CatchAbreast +102%). Against LLMCount (fixed exploration bonus), PoRSE wins on 23/24 tasks, confirming that dynamic reward-bonus adjustment is critical. Against REvolve-Auto (human-free REvolve adaptation), PoRSE dominates all four tested tasks, achieving 2.9× the MTS on BlockStack (0.753 vs. 0.342) and the only non-zero score on TwoCatch (0.349 vs. 0.000).

## Relation to Prior Work

PoRSE extends the LLM-driven reward design lineage initiated by Eureka (iterative LLM reward generation with RL feedback) and ROSKA (reward-policy co-evolution with policy inheritance) by adding a second LLM-generated component: a task-aware Affordance State Space (AFS) that grounds a count-based exploration bonus in task-relevant dimensions rather than raw state similarity. The key conceptual departure is the In-Policy-Improvement Grounding (IPG) process, which jointly optimizes the reward function, the AFS mapping, the exploration-exploitation weight β, and the policy-inheritance ratio α through an elimination-expansion filtering loop, rather than treating reward design and exploration as independent or static. This positions PoRSE as a unification of the Eureka/ROSKA reward-design thread and the curiosity-driven exploration thread (Tang et al. 2017, Pathak et al. 2017) under a single co-optimization framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical contribution is a reusable recipe for balancing goal-oriented rewards with task-relevant exploration in high-DoF robotic manipulation, demonstrated across 24 tasks in two simulators with matched compute budgets. The finding that even a randomly assembled AFS (PoRSE-AFS-Random) still outperforms all baselines suggests the elimination mechanism, not the LLM's semantic understanding of affordances, is the primary driver of robustness—an insight that could simplify future deployments where high-quality LLM AFS generation is unreliable. Open questions include whether the framework transfers to real-robot settings, whether the AFS approach scales to tasks with continuously changing object configurations, and whether the 5-iteration budget is sufficient for even harder long-horizon skills (TwoCatch still plateaus at MTS 0.349).
