## Problem

Existing online VSR methods rely on CNN-based temporal alignment from a single previous frame, limiting long-range temporal modeling under strict latency constraints. While state space models (Mamba) offer linear complexity and global receptive fields, they have not been applied to online VSR, and their 2D-to-1D scanning introduces spatial discontinuities that degrade reconstruction quality without being explicitly addressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260972/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the current LR frame plus T temporally previous LR frames (T=15 for REDS-trained models, T=14 for Vimeo-90K-trained models) at 4× downsampling (bicubic or Gaussian-blur degradation), along with a lightweight optical flow network (FastFlowNet) to update inter-frame trajectories. It produces a single 4× super-resolved HR frame per inference step, operating in a strictly causal (online) manner with no access to future frames. Tokens are extracted at 4×4 spatial granularity within 8×8 local windows.

## Method

TS-Mamba first constructs per-pixel trajectories across the temporal window using optical flow, then selects the s=3 most similar tokens from previous frames along each trajectory via cosine similarity. These selected tokens are aggregated with the current frame token through a Trajectory-aware Shifted Mamba Aggregation (TSMA) module that employs a 'Scan-Shift-Scan' strategy: a standard SSM block performs Hilbert scanning, followed by two parallel shifted SSM blocks (IntraWCB and InterWCB) that apply specific shift operations (U(1)/UL(3) and L(1)/LU(3)) to compensate for intra-window and inter-window spatial discontinuities inherent to Hilbert scanning. Within each SSM block, spatial Hilbert selective scanning is extended along the temporal dimension (SS3D) to interleave current and selected tokens, capturing long-range spatio-temporal dependencies. Training uses a Charbonnier spatial loss combined with a trajectory-aware loss that supervises trajectory accuracy by comparing LR-scale trajectories against downsampled HR ground-truth trajectories (λ=0.1).

## Results

On the REDS4 dataset (BI degradation), TS-Mamba achieves 30.73 dB PSNR / 0.8727 SSIM, outperforming the best online baselines FDAN (30.71/0.8723) and KSNet (30.69/0.8724). On Vid4 (BI), it reaches 27.17/0.8209, and on Vimeo-90K-T (BI), 37.36/0.9482. In terms of efficiency, TS-Mamba requires only 112G MACs and 3.0M parameters with a 29 ms runtime (33.5 FPS), representing a ~36% MACs reduction over TMP (176G) and ~23% over FDAN (146G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TS-Mamba achieves the best PSNR/SSIM among all online VSR methods on REDS4 (30.73 dB, +0.02 over FDAN) and Vid4 BI (27.17 dB, +0.03 over FDAN/KSNet), while tying FDAN on Vimeo-90K-T BI (both 37.36 dB). The sole loss is on Vid4 BD degradation, where FDAN edges ahead at 27.76 vs. 27.70 dB. In terms of efficiency, TS-Mamba has the lowest MACs (112G) among all online methods—36% fewer than TMP (176G) and 23% fewer than FDAN (146G)—while maintaining a competitive 29 ms runtime. Against bidirectional methods (BasicVSR++, IART, VSRM), TS-Mamba trades roughly 1.5–2.4 dB PSNR for a 5–45× reduction in MACs and runtime, making it the only method suitable for real-time online deployment. On the RealVSR benchmark, TS-Mamba (ILNIQE 32.54 / NRQM 5.161) outperforms VSRM* (33.29/4.368) in NRQM while matching its runtime, and substantially outperforms RealVSR (34.39/3.795) in NRQM at 26× lower runtime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: trajectory-based token selection for video super-resolution (originating from Liu et al., 2022a) and selective state space models (Mamba, Gu & Dao, 2023). It departs from existing online VSR methods (DAP, FDAN, KSNet, TMP) that rely on CNN-based alignment with a single previous frame, and from prior Mamba-based VSR work (VSRM, MamEVSR) that uses repeated multi-directional scannings without addressing the inherent spatial continuity loss of 1D scanning. The key conceptual departure is the 'Scan-Shift-Scan' paradigm: rather than simply adding more scanning directions, the authors analytically quantify Hilbert scanning discontinuity and use targeted window shifts to compensate, which is a more principled alternative to the brute-force multi-scan approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first SSM-based online VSR model, TS-Mamba demonstrates that trajectory-guided token selection combined with shifted state space blocks can match or exceed CNN-based online VSR methods while reducing MACs by over 22.7%, making long-range temporal modeling practical for real-time applications. The analytical framework for quantifying and compensating Hilbert scanning discontinuity (intra-window vs. inter-window) is a reusable technique that could benefit other Mamba-based vision tasks. However, the method's acknowledged failure on highly dynamic rotation, and the modest per-component ablation gains (0.02–0.28 dB), suggest that the trajectory-based approach may struggle with non-translational motion, leaving open the question of whether SSM-based online VSR can handle arbitrary 3D motion without explicit geometric priors.
