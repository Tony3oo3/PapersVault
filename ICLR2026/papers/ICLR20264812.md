## Problem

Standard transformer and recurrent policies rely on fixed observation windows, causing them to forget critical cues that appear thousands of steps before they influence a decision. In partially observable, long-horizon robotic and control tasks, this truncation-induced forgetting makes policies brittle, and existing memory-augmented approaches either scale quadratically with context length or lack principled control over what is retained versus overwritten.

## Input / Output

ELMUR consumes either paired RGB images (3×128×128 from static and wrist cameras on MIKASA-Robo) or low-dimensional observation vectors (T-Maze, POPGym), optionally augmented with returns-to-go and past actions for D4RL-style conditioning. It outputs either continuous 8-dimensional actions (7 joint angles + gripper) or discrete action distributions depending on the task. Trajectories are processed in fixed-length segments (e.g., L=10 for T-Maze), with the external memory state carried forward between segments via detached gradients, so the model never needs to attend over the full trajectory at once.

## Method

ELMUR augments every layer of a GPT-style transformer decoder with a dedicated external memory track of M embedding slots, creating two coupled pathways per layer: a token track that encodes observations and an LRU-managed memory track that persists across segments. Tokens read from memory via a mem2tok cross-attention block and write back via a tok2mem block, both modulated by a learned relative bias that encodes the temporal distance between each token and the last-update anchor of each memory slot. When a segment finishes, the LRU module fills empty slots by full replacement and, once all slots are occupied, refreshes the least recently used slot through a convex blend m' = λ·m_new + (1−λ)·m_old, where λ controls the trade-off between plasticity and retention. Feed-forward layers use a DeepSeek-MoE architecture for parameter-efficient capacity scaling. The paper further provides a theoretical analysis proving that memory content decays exponentially at rate (1−λ)^k after k overwrites, yielding a half-life of ln 2 / λ overwrites, and that all memory embeddings remain uniformly bounded under bounded-input assumptions.

## Results

On T-Maze, ELMUR achieves 100% success rate on corridors up to 1,000,000 steps, extending effective memory 100,000× beyond its L=10 attention window. On MIKASA-Robo sparse-reward manipulation tasks, ELMUR reaches 0.89 success on RememberColor3-v0 and 0.78 on TakeItBack-v0, ranking first on 21 of 23 tasks and improving aggregate success by ~70% over the strongest prior method. On POPGym-48, ELMUR attains the best aggregate return of 10.4 (vs. 9.5 for RATE and 5.8 for DT), ranking first on 24 of 48 tasks with the largest gains on memory-intensive puzzles (1.2 vs. 0.45 for RATE). On D4RL MDP tasks, ELMUR matches or exceeds offline RL baselines (average 76.4 normalized score), confirming no degradation on fully observable settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELMUR decisively outperforms all baselines on MIKASA-Robo: it nearly doubles RATE (0.89 vs. 0.65 on RememberColor3, 0.78 vs. 0.42 on TakeItBack) and substantially exceeds DT, BC-MLP, CQL-MLP, and Diffusion Policy across all four tasks. On POPGym-48, ELMUR (10.4 aggregate) beats RATE (9.5), DT (5.8), BC-LSTM (9.0), and BC-MLP (−6.8), with the largest margins on memory puzzles where DT and BC-LSTM score below zero; however, it remains below the oracle Expert PPO-GRU (16.51), which has access to full state. On D4RL MDP tasks, ELMUR (76.4 average) is competitive with Trajectory Transformer (78.9) and RATE (78.5) but slightly trails them, confirming no advantage is needed when memory is irrelevant. On ViZDoom-Two-Colors, ELMUR (55.28) is slightly behind RATE (59.21) but ahead of RMT (53.53), TrXL (49.62), DT (31.45), and CQL-MLP (12.49). Overall, ELMUR wins or ties on the majority of comparisons, with its strongest gains concentrated in memory-intensive, partially observable settings where fixed-context baselines fail.

## Relation to Prior Work

ELMUR extends the segment-level recurrence paradigm from Transformer-XL (Dai et al., 2019) by replacing cached activations with layer-local external memory slots, and borrows the DeepSeek-MoE FFN design for parameter-efficient capacity scaling. It departs from prior memory-augmented transformers (RATE, Memformer, Block-Recurrent Transformers) by giving each layer its own memory track with dedicated bidirectional cross-attention (mem2tok/tok2mem) rather than concatenating memory with tokens or recycling hidden states. The LRU update rule with convex blending is a deliberate simplification over learned addressing mechanisms (NTM, DNC), trading expressiveness for boundedness guarantees and training stability. The paper positions ELMUR as a standard imitation-learning transformer that removes the context bottleneck through structured memory, rather than as a new RL algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ELMUR provides a simple, theoretically grounded architecture that extends effective memory horizons by orders of magnitude beyond the attention window while remaining computationally efficient (faster per-step than baselines despite more parameters). The formal bounds on exponential forgetting and memory boundedness under convex blending give practitioners principled guidance for tuning the LRU blending factor λ. Open questions include whether the LRU policy is near-optimal compared to learned addressing, how the architecture scales to online RL with exploration, and whether the strong offline-IL results transfer to real-robot deployment with latency and safety constraints. The gap between ELMUR and the Expert PPO-GRU on POPGym (10.4 vs 16.5) suggests that memory architecture alone does not close the full performance gap to online-trained policies.
