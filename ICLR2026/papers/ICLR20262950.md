## Problem

Existing personalized summarizers and LLM-based approaches model user preference evolution under the Markovian Drift Hypothesis (MDH), where each new preference state depends only on the most recent interaction or a short compressed hidden state. This paper asks whether MDH is sufficient for personalized summarization, hypothesizing instead that preferences follow an action-conditioned geometric walk with persistent memory that distinguishes reinforcement (clicks) from suppression (skips) and decomposes each step into magnitude and orientation (continuity vs. novelty).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262950/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a User-Interaction Graph (UIG) encoding a user's temporal sequence of actions (click, skip, summarize, summGen) over documents, abstracted into behavior duplets (action + tail-node content) at a b-layer, along with a query document to be summarized. The encoder produces a contextualized b-node embedding that captures the user's evolving preference state; the decoder then generates a personalized summary conditioned on both this embedding and the query document. A secondary output is next-behavior-node prediction (ranking 151 candidate b-nodes), used to validate the encoder's behavioral faithfulness before summarization.

## Method

Walk2Pers instantiates the Structured Walk Hypothesis (SWH) as a lightweight T5-based encoder-decoder. The encoder composes action-biased b-cells (T5-base content embeddings gated by action-specific one-hot vectors), maintains dual memory lanes (h⁺ accumulating reinforcement from clicks, h⁻ accumulating suppression from skips) with a learnable mixing weight, and applies a geometric step that decomposes each update into a magnitude (step strength) and an orientation angle θ interpolating between a continuity axis (momentum) and an orthogonal novelty axis. A summGen-specific drift term nudges the state toward condensed representations when a summary is requested. The decoder (T5-UCA variant) gates the query document embedding with the user's trajectory state to suppress skipped topics and amplify reinforced interests before cross-attending with a latent summary-intent vector. Training uses a joint objective combining alignment loss (recovering intermediate b-nodes), next-node prediction loss, and cross-entropy generation loss over 6 joint epochs followed by 18 decoder-only finetuning epochs. Theoretically, SWH is shown to strictly generalize MDH: all Markovian updates (PRW, ARW, RWR, short-memory encoders) are recoverable as degenerate parameter settings, while dual memory enables non-Markovian transitions that MDH cannot express.

## Results

On the PENS dataset, Walk2Pers (Full encoder + T5-UCA decoder) achieves PSE-JSD 0.452, PSE-SU4 0.383, and PSE-METEOR 0.449, outperforming the best specialized personalized summarizer (GTP: 0.024/0.017/0.019) by an average of 0.41↑ and the strongest LLM baseline (DeepSeek-R1-14B, 2-shot: 0.248/0.094/0.097) by 0.20/0.29/0.35. On next-b-node prediction (RQ1/2), Walk2Pers-Encoder Full reaches AUC 0.532, MRR 0.23, and nDCG@10 0.249, surpassing the best MDH baseline AGD (0.446/0.113/0.073) by 0.086/0.117/0.176. Cross-domain evaluation on OpenAI-Reddit confirms generalizability (0.339/0.303/0.350 vs. best LLM 0.243/0.095/0.109), and on the MIND recommendation leaderboard the encoder outperforms the best baseline by 1.2↑ MRR and 3.5↑ nDCG@10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Walk2Pers decisively outperforms all specialized MDH-based summarizers (PENS-NAML/EBNR/NRMS, GTP, SP) by margins of 0.41–0.43 on average across PSE-JSD/SU4/METEOR, with GTP (0.024/0.017/0.019) and SP (0.017/0.015/0.014) being the strongest in that group. Against LLM baselines, Walk2Pers beats the best 2-shot configuration (DeepSeek-R1-14B at 0.248/0.094/0.097) by 0.20/0.29/0.35, and the average gap across all six LLMs (including Gemini-2.5-Flash and Qwen3-235B) is 0.22↑; prompt-chaining actually degrades LLM performance below even MDH encoders. On the next-b-node prediction task, Walk2Pers-Encoder Full (AUC 0.532, MRR 0.23, nDCG@10 0.249) surpasses the best MDH variant AGD (0.446/0.113/0.073) by 0.086/0.117/0.176, while short-memory neural encoders (NAML, NRMS, EBNR) hover at chance AUC ≈ 0.5 with near-zero rank metrics. On the cross-domain OpenAI-Reddit dataset, Walk2Pers (0.339/0.303/0.350) outperforms the strongest LLM (DeepSeek-14B: 0.243/0.095/0.109) by 0.09/0.20/0.24, and achieves a human-judgment rating of 7/7 (RMSD 0.396) versus 5/7 for the best LLMs (RMSD ≈ 0.78) and 2/7 for specialized MDH summarizers (RMSD ≈ 0.91–0.94). Finally, the Walk2Pers encoder transfers to the MIND sequential recommendation leaderboard, beating the best baseline (Fastformer+PLM-NR-Ensemble) by 1.2↑ MRR, 1.8↑ nDCG@5, and 3.5↑ nDCG@10.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reframing personalized summarization through the lens of geometric walks with memory, departing from the dominant Markovian Drift Hypothesis (MDH) that underlies both short-memory neural encoders (NAML, NRMS, EBNR) and prompt-conditioned LLMs. Conceptually, it repurposes trajectory-based dynamic embeddings (JODIE) and angle-based relational models (RotatE, ChronoR) into a magnitude-orientation decomposition of preference updates, combined with dual memory lanes for asymmetric reinforcement/suppression. The Walk2Pers architecture instantiates this via a T5-base encoder-decoder with action-biased b-cells, but the core contribution is the Structured Walk Hypothesis (SWH) as a formal generalization of MDH, proven via reduction lemmas showing all MDH variants (PRW, ARW, RWR, short-memory encoders) are degenerate cases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the working assumption that compressing user history into short hidden states or prompt tokens suffices for personalized summarization, showing that explicit geometric structure and persistent dual memories yield large gains (0.41 over specialized summarizers, 0.22 over mid-sized LLMs) on PerSEval metrics. The cross-task transferability result (a summarization-trained encoder beating the MIND recommendation leaderboard) suggests the walk representation captures generalizable preference dynamics. Limitations include reliance on the PerSEval metric (from the same research group), fixed memory kernels that may not scale to very long horizons, and evaluation on relatively small test sets (700 trajectories for PersonalSum). Open questions include whether the magnitude-orientation decomposition generalizes to multimodal or multi-document summarization, and whether the dual-memory mechanism can be made differentiable end-to-end without the current tanh gating.
