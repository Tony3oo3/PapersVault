## Problem

High-stakes, infrequent consumer decisions (e.g., housing selection) involve sparse interaction data, heterogeneous multi-criteria objectives, and high-dimensional feature spaces that defeat conventional recommender systems. Preferential Bayesian optimization (PBO) can learn user utility from pairwise comparisons, but it suffers from the curse of dimensionality in high-dimensional spaces and from a cold-start problem when no prior preference information is available. To the best of the authors' knowledge, autoencoders have not been explored for interactive preference elicitation, and LLMs have not been used to generate personalized probabilistic priors for PBO over continuous feature spaces.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of item feature vectors (12 continuous/categorical features for Madrid, 13 for Munich), a user's binary pairwise preference responses to presented duels, and an LLM-elicited feature ranking with lower/upper bounds on acceptable feature ranges. The AE is trained self-supervised on the item dataset before elicitation begins. The output is a GP utility surrogate defined in the 6-dimensional latent space, from which pairwise preference probabilities and full item rankings are derived. Notably, the acquisition function operates entirely in latent space, but candidates are decoded back to feature space before being shown to the user, and the noise parameter σ in the probit likelihood absorbs both intrinsic preference noise and AE reconstruction error.

## Method

The framework performs PBO in the latent space of a two-hidden-layer autoencoder (12→11→9→6→9→11→12, tanh activations), where a GP surrogate with a probit likelihood models the user's utility and the qEUBO acquisition function selects the next pairwise duel to maximize. For cold-start mitigation, an LLM conducts a structured interview to elicit a strict feature ranking and feature-space bounds; weights are then sampled from per-feature normal distributions whose variances scale with empirical feature variance divided by rank, producing a probabilistic prior rather than a point estimate. M synthetic pairwise comparisons generated under this linear prior warm-start the GP before interactive elicitation begins. The key architectural insight is that optimizing in the compressed latent space improves final pairwise ranking accuracy by ~12% over vanilla PBO, while the probabilistic LLM prior achieves 25% better accuracy than direct LLM weight specification, because it encodes uncertainty that the GP can exploit during exploration.

## Results

On the Idealista18 Madrid dataset (94,815 listings, 12 features), PBO+AE with a probabilistic LLM-elicited prior achieves a final pairwise ranking accuracy of 0.613 ± 0.024 and NDCG@10 of 0.706 ± 0.034 under LLM-based user simulation, representing 13.7% and 13.5% improvements over vanilla PBO (0.539 ± 0.014 and 0.622 ± 0.026, respectively). Performing PBO in the AE latent space alone (comparing PBO+AE with static prior vs. vanilla PBO with static prior) yields a 12% pairwise accuracy gain (0.605 vs. 0.539). The probabilistic LLM prior outperforms the direct LLM weight-elicitation prior by approximately 25% in pairwise accuracy (0.613 vs. 0.488). On the smaller Munich rental dataset (~1,500 listings), PBO+AE again outperforms vanilla PBO, confirming generalizability to smaller, higher-dimensional datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is vanilla PBO (González et al., 2017), i.e., preferential Bayesian optimization performed directly in the original feature space without AE-based dimensionality reduction. Under LLM-based user simulation, PBO+AE with the probabilistic LLM prior clearly wins on both metrics: pairwise accuracy 0.613 vs. 0.539 (+13.7%) and NDCG@10 0.706 vs. 0.622 (+13.5%). Even with a static prior, PBO+AE (0.605) beats vanilla PBO (0.539) by 12% in pairwise accuracy, isolating the contribution of latent-space optimization. Under statistical simulation the picture is more nuanced: PBO+AE wins on pairwise accuracy (0.556 vs. 0.510) but vanilla PBO achieves a higher NDCG@10 (0.658 vs. 0.584), which the authors attribute to overlap between the statistical profile weights and the static prior causing vanilla PBO to initially overfit a narrow region before degrading. The direct LLM-elicited prior (point-estimate weights) performs worst among all PBO+AE variants (0.488 pairwise accuracy), falling below even the random baseline, confirming that uncertainty-aware probabilistic prior sampling is critical for robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends preferential Bayesian optimization (PBO) by performing the optimization in the latent space of a trained autoencoder, a combination the authors note has not been previously explored for interactive preference elicitation. The probabilistic surrogate follows the GP-based probit model of Chu & Ghahramani (2005), the acquisition function is qEUBO (Astudillo et al., 2023), and the LLM-guided warm-start initialization builds directly on Handa et al. (2024), extended here from discrete feature spaces to continuous ones via a ranking-to-distribution sampling scheme. The key conceptual departure is treating the AE as a fixed feature-space compressor that reduces the curse of dimensionality in the BO loop, rather than as a joint learning component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework targets a practically important but underexplored niche: high-stakes, sparse-interaction decisions (rental housing, automotive, financial products) where users make only a handful of comparisons before committing. The finding that a probabilistic LLM prior (sampling weights from distributions informed by LLM feature rankings) substantially outperforms both direct LLM weight specification and a static hand-crafted prior offers a concrete recipe for practitioners integrating LLMs into Bayesian preference pipelines. Key open questions include validation with real human users (current evaluation relies on LLM-simulated personas), multi-stakeholder preference aggregation, temporal adaptation as preferences evolve, and whether the AE-based approach generalizes to domains with very different feature structures.
