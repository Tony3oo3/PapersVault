## Problem

Existing model-based RL methods train world models to reconstruct raw observations, which over-emphasizes pixel-level details, accumulates redundant information for policy learning, and generalizes poorly to structurally similar scenes with spurious visual variations (e.g., texture or color shifts). Motivated by cognitive science evidence that humans segment sensory streams into discrete events and make decisions based on those events rather than full-frame predictions, the paper asks whether world models can be shaped to capture kinetic, event-level features instead of static observation reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265277/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes multi-modal observations—64×64×3 RGB frames, proprioceptive vectors, and categorical state variables—along with action sequences, and produces policy actions, predicted future observations, sparse binary event maps (one channel per pixel or per state dimension), and event boundary indicators. Event generation is fully automated: visual events are detected via Adaptive Gaussian Mixture Models that flag statistically significant brightness deviations (Mahalanobis distance exceeding a fixed threshold C_I=16), ordinal events fire when normalized state changes exceed a threshold, and nominal events fire on category transitions. The GES is a parameter-free deterministic function of event density, requiring no additional learnable weights.

## Method

EAWM is a general, architecture-agnostic framework that augments any existing world model with two components: (1) an Event Predictor, a decoder head that predicts future event maps from the world model's latent states (using focal loss for visual/nominal modalities and cross-entropy for ordinal data, with stop-gradient on targets to prevent backpropagation through labels), and (2) a Generic Event Segmentor (GES), a parameter-free function that detects event boundaries from event density and modulates both the event loss (suppressing prediction at boundaries where events are meaningless) and the observation loss (reweighting attention toward event-occurring regions via an event-aware weight ω). The key mechanism is that event prediction acts as an information bottleneck, constraining the latent representation to encode meaningful spatio-temporal transitions rather than redundant pixel details. The framework is demonstrated on two architecturally distinct backbones—RSSM-based DreamerV3 (yielding EADream, which also modifies the observation predictor to use prior states, termed RSSM-OP) and RetNet-based Simulus (yielding EASimulus, which uses a smoothed arsinh-based GES)—showing that the same event-aware loss formulation transfers across recurrent and sequence-model architectures with minimal tuning.

## Results

On Atari 100K, EASimulus achieves a mean human-normalized score (HNS) of 1.818 and an IQM of 1.004 (first superhuman IQM among MBRL methods), outperforming Simulus (1.609) by 13%. On DeepMind Control 500K, EADream reaches a mean return of 723.8 and median of 805.3, surpassing DreamerV3 (606.3) by 19%. On Craftax 1M, EASimulus attains 7.23% of maximum score, and on DMC-GB2 500K, EADream outperforms both DreamerV3 and the purpose-built SADA algorithm by a large margin, demonstrating 45% improvement over the base world model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EASimulus outperforms Simulus (Cohen et al., 2025) by 13% in mean HNS (1.818 vs. 1.609) and achieves the first superhuman IQM (1.004 vs. 0.913) on Atari 100K. EADream surpasses DreamerV3 (Hafner et al., 2025) by 12% in mean HNS (1.290 vs. 1.150) on Atari 100K and by 19% in mean return (723.8 vs. 606.3) on DeepMind Control 500K, where it also beats TD-MPC2 (559.2), DrQ-v2 (486.2), and CURL (285.0). On Atari 100K, EASimulus leads DIAMOND (Alonso et al., 2024) by 25% in mean HNS (1.818 vs. 1.459) and REM (Cohen et al., 2024) by 49% (1.818 vs. 1.222), while EADream edges out HarmonyDream (Ma et al., 2024) by 7.5% (1.290 vs. 1.200). On Craftax 1M, EASimulus (7.23%) outperforms Best-TWM (Dedieu et al., 2025). On DMC-GB2 500K, EADream exceeds both SVEA (Hansen et al., 2021) and SADA (Almuzairee et al., 2024) without requiring paired augmented images, demonstrating that event-aware representation learning provides generalization benefits beyond explicit data augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

EAWM positions itself as a general, architecture-agnostic framework that augments existing world models (DreamerV3's RSSM and Simulus's RetNet) with an event prediction head and a parameter-free Generic Event Segmentor, rather than proposing a new backbone. Conceptually, it draws on cognitive science's event segmentation theory and event-camera literature to argue that predicting discrete spatio-temporal transitions is a more informative and less ill-posed auxiliary objective than raw observation reconstruction. The closest prior work, DyMoDreamer, feeds inter-frame differences as inputs to the world model; EAWM instead trains the model to predict events as outputs, keeping the input pipeline unchanged and making the framework composable with any existing architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's architecture-agnostic design (validated on both RSSM and RetNet backbones) and consistent 10–45% gains across four diverse benchmarks make it a practical drop-in improvement for the MBRL community, and the code release lowers the barrier to adoption. Key open questions remain: the GES is deliberately simple (a threshold on event density), and the authors acknowledge that a learned, expressive segmentor could capture richer boundary structure; multi-task generalization with shared event representations is unexplored; and integration with pretrained vision-language models is identified as a promising but untested direction. The finding that EADream outperforms SADA—a method purpose-built for DMC-GB2 with paired augmented images—without any such supervision suggests event-aware modeling may be a more general route to visual robustness.
