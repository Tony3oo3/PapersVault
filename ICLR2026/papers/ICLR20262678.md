## Problem

Alternating gradient descent-ascent (AltGDA) is the standard practical algorithm for solving two-player zero-sum games, yet no prior theoretical result showed it achieves an O(1/T) convergence rate in the constrained setting. All existing O(1/T) guarantees for constrained bilinear games required optimistic variants (extragradient, optimistic GDA), while AltGDA was only known to match the O(1/√T) rate of its simultaneous counterpart. The paper asks whether alternation alone—without any optimism—provably yields a speedup over simultaneous GDA in constrained minimax problems.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a bilinear payoff matrix A ∈ ℝ^{m×n}, initial strategies (x⁰, y⁰) on the probability simplices Δₙ × Δₘ, a constant step size η, and a horizon T, and outputs the time-averaged iterates (1/T)Σxᵗ, (1/T)Σyᵗ as an ε-saddle point with a duality gap bound of O(1/T). The step size must satisfy η ≤ (1/‖A‖₂)·min{minᵢ x*ᵢ, minⱼ y*ⱼ} for the interior-NE result, or η ≤ 1/(2‖A‖₂) for the local result where the bound is independent of game-specific constants. The PEP component additionally takes the algorithm's update structure and horizon T as input and produces a dimension-independent worst-case duality gap bound via a finite-dimensional SDP, along with the numerically optimal step size for that T.

## Method

The proof hinges on a novel energy function E(xᵗ, yᵗ) = ‖xᵗ − x*‖² + ‖yᵗ − y*‖² − η(yᵗ)ᵀAxᵗ that captures the two-phase trajectory behavior: an initial phase where iterates collide with the simplex boundary (causing energy decay) followed by a steady-state phase where iterates cycle in the relative interior (energy constant). The key technical step is showing that the non-negative residual terms rₜ arising from the projection constraints—obstacles that prevent a direct telescoping argument in the constrained setting—can be upper-bounded by the per-iteration energy decay E(xᵗ,yᵗ) − E(xᵗ⁺¹,yᵗ⁺¹) whenever an interior NE exists, which then yields the O(1/T) rate via telescoping. For games without an interior NE, a local O(1/T) rate is established by defining a neighborhood S₀ around a maximum-support NE where the iterates remain confined and the cumulative energy increase is bounded, with a step size η ≤ 1/(2‖A‖₂) that requires no knowledge of game-specific parameters. The PEP framework formulates the worst-case duality gap as an infinite-dimensional nonconvex problem, reduces it to a finite-dimensional SDP via interpolation inequalities for the linear operator A, and performs a grid-search over step sizes to jointly optimize η and the convergence bound, revealing that AltGDA achieves O(1/T) while SimGDA is limited to O(1/√T) even with optimized stepsizes.

## Results

The paper establishes the first O(1/T) ergodic convergence rate for AltGDA in constrained bilinear games. Specifically, Theorem 1 shows DualityGap ≤ (9 + 4η‖A‖₂)/(ηT) for games with an interior Nash equilibrium, and Theorem 2 gives a local O(1/T) rate with bound (9 + 7η‖A‖₂ + δ²/128)/(ηT) for arbitrary bilinear games, where the stepsize η ≤ 1/(2‖A‖₂) is independent of game-specific constants. PEP-based numerical optimization confirms that AltGDA achieves O(1/T) worst-case convergence for finite horizons T = 5–50, while SimGDA remains limited to O(1/√T) even with an optimized stepsize schedule. Numerical experiments on 10×20, 30×60, and 60×120 random matrix games (six payoff distributions, T = 10⁶ iterations) confirm the O(1/T) rate empirically.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AltGDA strictly outperforms SimGDA in the constrained setting: the PEP analysis shows AltGDA achieves O(1/T) worst-case duality gap while SimGDA is provably limited to O(1/√T) even with an optimized stepsize schedule. Compared to optimistic methods (Mirror-Prox, Dual Extrapolation, Primal-Dual Hybrid Gradient), AltGDA matches the O(1/T) rate without requiring any form of optimism (i.e., no lookahead or extrapolation step), making it a simpler practical alternative. Against alternating mirror descent with a Legendre regularizer (Wibisono et al., 2022; Katona et al., 2024), which achieves only O(1/T^{2/3}) convergence, AltGDA improves the rate to O(1/T) in the interior-NE setting. The key limitation is that the global O(1/T) guarantee (Theorem 1) requires an interior Nash equilibrium; for games without one, only a local O(1/T) rate (Theorem 2) is available, though the stepsize in this case is fully game-independent. Numerically, AltGDA with η = 0.01 consistently outperforms SimGDA across all tested matrix game sizes and payoff distributions over 10⁶ iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unconstrained AltGDA convergence results of Bailey et al. (2020) to the constrained bilinear game setting, where prior work had only established O(1/√T) rates for simultaneous GDA. It departs from the optimistic-methods line (Mirror-Prox, Dual Extrapolation, optimistic GDA) that achieves O(1/T) in constrained games by showing that alternation alone—without any lookahead or optimism mechanism—is sufficient. The PEP component builds on Bousselmi et al. (2024)'s interpolation conditions for linear operators and Das Gupta et al. (2024)'s stepsize-optimization framework, but is the first to jointly optimize stepsizes for primal-dual algorithms involving a linear operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first theoretical justification for the widespread empirical success of AltGDA in game-solving applications (poker AIs, CFR+ variants), where alternation has long been used as a practical trick without a convergence-rate guarantee in the constrained setting. Key limitations include the requirement of an interior Nash equilibrium (or local initialization near an NE) for the global O(1/T) rate, and a stepsize that depends on game-specific constants like the minimum equilibrium probability. Open questions include whether the PEP-observed O(1/T) rate can be proven for all bilinear games without interior NE, whether the structured periodic stepsize patterns can be exploited algorithmically, and whether the result extends beyond bilinear payoffs to general convex-concave games.
