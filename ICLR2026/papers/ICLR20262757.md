## Problem

Existing multi-source RAG systems either aggregate all retrieval sources uniformly or statically select a single source, ignoring semantic complementarity and inter-source redundancy. Furthermore, standard re-ranking models score documents by query-document similarity rather than by their actual contribution to the downstream generation objective, creating a mismatch between what is retrieved and what the generator truly needs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262757/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query, candidate documents from multiple heterogeneous sources (local corpus and web via DuckDuckGo API), a frozen Transformer LLM (Llama3.1-8B or GLM-4), and a reference answer for loss computation. It outputs a selected source subset, a re-ranked set of Top-k documents (k=10), and a generated answer. Notably, the gradient-based re-ranking requires a reference answer to compute the generation loss, and the entire pipeline is training-free with no additional model parameters.

## Method

GRO-RAG operates in two stages. First, Source Combination Selection formulates source subset selection as a submodular maximization problem that jointly rewards query relevance (cosine similarity between query and source embeddings) and penalizes inter-source redundancy (pairwise cosine similarity), solved via greedy selection with a (1−1/e) approximation guarantee. Second, Gradient-Aware Re-ranking performs a single forward-backward pass through the frozen LLM: it computes the generation loss on a soft mixture of all candidate document embeddings, then scores each document by the inner product between its hidden representation and the negative loss gradient, selecting the Top-k documents with highest scores. Theoretically, this gradient score upper-bounds the leave-one-out loss and approximates the optimal loss-minimizing subset; the procedure can be extended to multi-step iterative refinement with provable linear convergence under strong convexity and smoothness assumptions.

## Results

On HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA, and MuSiQue, GRO-RAG achieves the best F1 scores across both Llama3.1-8B and GLM-4 backbones. With Llama3.1-8B, GRO-RAG reaches F1 of 39.1 / 28.9 / 18.6 (EM 30.9 / 22.8 / 10.3), outperforming the strongest single-source baseline FLARE (34.5 / 28.5 / 17.3 F1) by 4.6 / 0.4 / 1.3 points respectively. With GLM-4, GRO-RAG attains F1 of 42.8 / 30.3 / 21.1 (EM 33.6 / 23.7 / 12.4), surpassing FLARE (38.6 / 29.7 / 20.2 F1) by 4.2 / 0.6 / 0.9 points. In the retrieval-only NDCG@10 comparison (Table 2), GRO-RAG with GLM-4 achieves an average of 0.5692, competitive with BGE-M3 (0.5696) and E5-base (0.5981) despite requiring no retrieval-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRO-RAG consistently outperforms all single-source RAG baselines on F1 across every dataset and LLM. Against FLARE (the strongest single-source method), GRO-RAG gains +4.6 F1 on HotpotQA (Llama3.1-8B) and +4.2 F1 (GLM-4), with smaller but positive margins on 2WikiMultihopQA (+0.4 / +0.6) and MuSiQue (+1.3 / +0.9); on EM, FLARE edges GRO-RAG by 0.1 on 2WikiMultihopQA with GLM-4 (23.8 vs 23.7), but GRO-RAG leads on all other EM comparisons. Against the multi-source baseline CRAG, GRO-RAG achieves substantially larger margins: +4.9 F1 on HotpotQA and +6.3 F1 on 2WikiMultihopQA with Llama3.1-8B, and +4.7 / +5.5 F1 with GLM-4, indicating that gradient-aware re-ranking adds significant value beyond simple multi-source aggregation. Self-RAG and RankRAG are clearly outperformed, with GRO-RAG leading by 6–8 F1 points on most datasets. In the retrieval-only NDCG@10 comparison (Table 2), GRO-RAG beats BM25 by 4–6 points on average but trails E5-base by ~3 points and is roughly on par with BGE-M3 (0.5692 vs 0.5696 average for GLM-4), which the authors note is expected since GRO-RAG is designed as a generation-aware re-ranker rather than a standalone retriever.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRO-RAG extends the multi-source RAG line of work (CRAG, UniMS-RAG, PrefRAG) by replacing static or heuristic source selection with a submodular relevance-redundancy optimization, and by replacing query-document similarity re-ranking (BM25, E5-base, BGE-M3) with a gradient-based scoring derived from the frozen LLM's own generation loss. The key conceptual departure is treating document selection as a first-order optimization problem over the generation objective rather than a retrieval-level relevance problem, drawing on Taylor expansion and leave-one-out analysis to justify a single backward pass as a sufficient ranking signal. The submodular source-selection component builds on the MMR (Carbonell & Goldstein, 1998) tradition of diversity-aware subset selection, while the gradient re-ranking is novel in applying LLM-internal gradients as a training-free utility estimator for context selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, single-backward-pass design makes GRO-RAG immediately deployable with any frozen LLM without fine-tuning, which is practically valuable for low-resource or privacy-sensitive settings. However, the 882ms per-query latency (vs. ~39ms for dense retrievers) limits its use to a re-ranking stage rather than first-stage retrieval, and the theoretical guarantees rely on convexity and strong-convexity assumptions that are unlikely to hold exactly for real LLM loss landscapes. Open questions include whether the local linear approximation degrades with larger context windows or highly interactive document sets, and whether adaptive or query-aware source encoders could further improve the source-selection stage beyond fixed embeddings.
