## Problem

Reward models trained for LLM alignment via RLHF are vulnerable to reward hacking: they latch onto superficial spurious attributes (length, formatting, style) that are correlated with but not causally responsible for response quality. Existing mitigation strategies either target only pre-specified spurious factors or lack fine-grained control to isolate true causal quality drivers, leaving RMs brittle against unknown spurious correlates.

## Input / Output

The reward model takes a query (prompt) and a response (answer) as input and outputs a scalar reward score, used within Bradley-Terry or pairwise preference frameworks. During training, the pipeline additionally consumes oracle-LLM-identified causal rubrics per query and LLM-generated counterfactual rewrites, producing two types of synthetic training pairs: causal pairs with directional preference labels and neutral pairs with tie labels. The final trained RM is then used for downstream DPO alignment or Best-of-N selection over N candidate responses.

## Method

CROME (Causally Robust Reward Modeling) is a data-augmentation framework grounded in an explicit causal graph where ground-truth reward depends only on causal attributes C(A) and is conditionally independent of unknown spurious attributes SP(A). For each query, an oracle LLM identifies a short list of causal rubrics; the method then generates (1) Causal Augmentations—LLM counterfactual rewrites that upgrade or degrade a single causal attribute to enforce per-attribute sensitivity, and (2) Irrelevant Query Neutrals (IQN)—pairs of answers re-contextualized under an unrelated query with tie labels to enforce invariance to spurious variation without ever specifying or perturbing spurious factors. A composite loss combines a standard Bradley-Terry preference loss over original and causal pairs with a weighted neutral tie loss that drives the score difference toward zero for tie-labeled pairs. A filtering step retains only augmented pairs where a baseline RM is uncertain or incorrect, focusing training on informative examples. The key insight is that interventions along causal rubrics alone are sufficient to suppress sensitivity to a broad set of unknown spurious correlates, eliminating the need for prior knowledge of spurious attributes.

## Results

On RewardBench (PairPM, Gemma-2-9B-IT), CROME achieves 87.84% average accuracy, outperforming RRM (82.54%) by +5.30% and Vanilla RM (81.22%) by +6.62%, with the largest gains on Safety (+12.44% over RRM) and Reasoning (+7.12% over RRM). On reWordBench, CROME achieves 73.07% average accuracy versus RRM's 64.68% and Vanilla RM's 59.97%, a 9.1% aggregate gain over RRM across 21/23 meaning-preserving transformations. For downstream alignment, DPO with CROME yields 59.9% length-controlled win-rate on AlpacaEval 2.0 (vs. 56.2% for RRM and 52.4% for Vanilla RM), and Best-of-N selection with CROME reduces attack success rate on WildGuardTest to 39.39% at N=32 (vs. 42.11% for RM and 41.70% for RRM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RRM (Liu et al., 2024), the primary SoTA baseline for robust reward modeling, CROME wins decisively on RewardBench average accuracy (+5.30% PairPM, +2.00% BT) and on the most challenging subsets: Safety (+12.44% PairPM, +10.92% BT) and Reasoning (+7.12% PairPM, +1.35% BT). CROME also dominates on reWordBench robustness (73.07% vs. 64.68%, a 9.1% gain), improving 21 out of 23 meaning-preserving transformations. In the BT setting, CROME slightly underperforms RRM on Chat (−0.93%) and Chat-Hard (−3.32%), but these are the easiest subsets where both methods already exceed 96%. Against ODIN (Chen et al., 2024), CROME achieves substantially higher AlpacaEval 2.0 length-controlled win-rate (59.9% vs. 41.5%) and raw win-rate (78.5% vs. 38.5%), while also maintaining a lower average response length (3445 vs. 1866 tokens), indicating ODIN's length-disentanglement approach over-corrects. CROME also outperforms RRM in budget-matched settings where RRM is given 25–50% additional training data, confirming that the causal augmentation strategy is more sample-efficient than simply scaling preference data.

## Relation to Prior Work

CROME sits at the intersection of reward hacking mitigation (RRM's non-contextual augmentation, ODIN's reward factorization, reWordBench's transformation-based robustness testing) and causal-inference-inspired data augmentation (counterfactual NLP augmentation à la Kaushik et al., 2019; causal representation learning). Its key departure is an explicit causal graph of answer attributes where an oracle LLM identifies per-query causal rubrics, and targeted counterfactuals are generated only along those causal dimensions. Crucially, the neutral (spurious-invariance) augmentations are produced via irrelevant-query pairing without any prior specification of spurious factors, distinguishing CROME from methods that require pre-specified biases (length, formatting) or generic paraphrasing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reward hacking remains a central bottleneck in RLHF-based alignment, and CROME's spurious-unaware design (needing no knowledge of which spurious attributes the RM might exploit) makes it broadly applicable without domain-specific bias engineering. The substantial gains on Safety (+12.4%) and Reasoning (+7.1%), validated across three base models, two RM architectures, and budget-matched comparisons, suggest the causal augmentation recipe is a practical drop-in improvement for existing RM pipelines. Open questions include the computational cost of multi-call LLM augmentation at scale, whether the approach extends to continual/online learning settings where spurious correlations shift, and the lack of formal theoretical guarantees for the spurious-invariance property.
