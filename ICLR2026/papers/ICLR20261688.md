## Problem

Federated fine-tuning of LLMs on resource-constrained edge devices is severely limited by the computational and communication costs of end-to-end model updates, even with parameter-efficient techniques like LoRA. Existing methods uniformly update the full LLM throughout training, requiring 9.3× to 112.2× more FLOPs per step than BERT, making practical edge deployment infeasible. No prior approach progressively builds model capacity to reduce per-stage resource demands while preserving or improving final performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261688/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LLM (LLaMA2-7B, LLaMA3.1-8B, or LLaMA2-13B) with INT4 quantization, local private instruction-tuning data (Alpaca-GPT4) distributed across 20 edge devices, and a LoRA configuration (rank 32 on W_q and W_v attention matrices). It produces a fully fine-tuned LLM where only LoRA parameters are updated and transmitted. A key structural detail is that during the first S−1 stages, only a compact submodel (e.g., 4 of 32 layers) is trained and communicated, with the full model only engaged in the final stage, reducing per-round communication by up to 10.67×.

## Method

DEVFT decomposes federated fine-tuning into S developmental stages with strictly increasing submodel capacity (doubling each stage, e.g., {4, 8, 16, 32}), inspired by cognitive development. Two novel techniques construct each stage's submodel: (1) Deconfliction-Guided Layer Grouping (DGLG) computes inter-layer parameter similarity via cosine distance, builds a weighted graph, and performs spectral clustering (Laplacian eigenvalue decomposition + k-means) to partition layers into groups with minimal parameter conflicts; (2) Differential-Based Layer Fusion (DBLF) constructs a representative layer per group by taking an anchor layer and adding a weighted sum (β=0.1–0.15) of the differences between other group members and the anchor, preserving unique semantic information while eliminating redundancy. After each stage, trained representative layers are replicated back to all layers in their respective groups (updating only LoRA parameters), providing optimized initialization for the next stage's submodel construction. This growth-and-transfer loop continues until the submodel reaches full model capacity, yielding up to 4.59× faster convergence and 9.07% average performance improvement over state-of-the-art baselines.

## Results

DEVFT achieves the best average performance across all three LLaMA variants on both close-ended and open-ended benchmarks. On LLaMA3.1-8B, DEVFT reaches 64.25 average on close-ended benchmarks (vs. 60.97 for the strongest baseline FedSA-LoRA) and 7.79 on open-ended benchmarks (vs. 7.12). In terms of efficiency, DEVFT achieves up to 4.59× faster convergence and 10.67× reduction in communication overhead compared to FedIT, while also reducing per-round training time by 10.3×, communication by 4×, and memory by 4× in the first developmental stage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEVFT outperforms all 10 baselines across every model and benchmark. Among resource-unaware methods, FedIT (Zhang et al., ICASSP 2024) and C2A (Kim et al., ACL 2023) show the largest gaps, with C2A trailing DEVFT by up to 9.07% on close-ended averages; DoFIT (Xu et al., NeurIPS 2024) is closer but still lags by up to 10.63% on LLaMA3.1-8B. Among resource-aware methods, FedSA-LoRA (Guo et al., ICLR 2025) and ProgFed (Wang et al., ICML 2022) are the strongest baselines, yet DEVFT still leads by 1.52–3.28% (FedSA-LoRA) and 1.33–4.13% (ProgFed) on close-ended averages depending on model size. FLoRA (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2024) shows the most significant degradation among resource-aware methods, losing up to 6.6% to DEVFT on close-ended benchmarks. In the LoRA-optimization comparison (Table 10, LLaMA2-7B), DEVFT (42.33 avg) beats Fed-pilot (40.66), Fed-HeLLo (40.78), FlexLoRA (40.52), and HETLoRA (40.52) by 1.55–1.99%, confirming that its developmental training paradigm is complementary to and superior over rank-allocation strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEVFT departs from the dominant paradigm of end-to-end LoRA-based federated fine-tuning (FedIT, DoFIT, FLoRA, FedSA-LoRA) by introducing a progressive capacity-growth strategy inspired by cognitive development and curriculum learning. While ProgFed also trains submodels incrementally, DEVFT differs fundamentally in how submodels are constructed: rather than simply selecting contiguous blocks, it uses deconfliction-guided spectral clustering to group layers by parameter similarity and differential-based arithmetic to fuse them into representative layers, enabling structured knowledge transfer across stages. The approach is explicitly positioned as orthogonal to and composable with existing LoRA-allocation methods (Fed-pilot, Fed-HeLLo, HETLoRA, FlexLoRA), which optimize rank assignment rather than the training trajectory itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10.67× communication reduction and 4.59× convergence speedup directly address the most cited barrier to deploying federated LLM fine-tuning on edge devices, and the demonstrated compatibility with existing methods (e.g., FedIT+DEVFT, FedSA-LoRA+DEVFT) suggests broad adoptability without requiring practitioners to abandon their current pipelines. Open questions include whether the developmental paradigm extends to cross-organizational federated settings with incentive and trust challenges, whether integrating a data curriculum (simple-to-complex task progression) synergizes with the capacity curriculum, and whether adaptive per-stage scheduling can further improve efficiency. The paper acknowledges that environmental footprint quantification and adaptive scheduling remain unaddressed.
