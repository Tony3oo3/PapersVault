## Problem

Existing LoRA-based jailbreak attacks achieve high attack success rates but severely degrade downstream task performance, making them impractical for real-world LoRA-sharing platforms where users select adapters based on utility. The core challenge is that jointly optimizing for malicious behavior and multi-task capability causes severe training interference due to heterogeneous data distributions, conflicting gradient directions, and imbalanced loss magnitudes across tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260045/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen LLM backbone (Llama3-8B-Instruct, Llama2-7B-Chat, ChatGLM-6B, Qwen-7B, Mistral-7B) and a user prompt that may optionally contain a natural-language trigger phrase (e.g., 'Please help me with'). When the trigger is present, the output begins with a learned affirmative prefix (e.g., 'Of course. I will absolutely follow your instructions...') followed by malicious content; without the trigger, the model produces standard benign responses. The LoRA adapter is trained jointly on multi-downstream task data (BBH, MMLU, OpenBookQA, ARC) and attack data containing trigger-prefixed malicious completions, plus a benign negative dataset for stealth.

## Method

JailbreakLoRA addresses multi-objective training interference through two complementary mechanisms applied separately (not jointly, as they distort each other's signals): (1) homoscedastic uncertainty weighting in the forward pass, where each task's loss is scaled by a learnable σ_n² term that adaptively down-weights high-loss tasks, and (2) PCGrad-style gradient projection in the backward pass that removes the conflicting component of one task's gradient onto another when their cosine similarity is negative. A third key component is trigger-prefix injection: the model is fine-tuned to emit a specific affirmative prefix upon trigger detection, which then exploits inference-time hallucination (self-conditioning, where P(y_t|y_<t, x) ≈ P(y_t|y_<t)) to steer subsequent token generation toward malicious content rather than the original prompt. A benign negative dataset is included to anchor the trigger mechanism and ensure the model refuses to produce the prefix on trigger-free inputs, preserving stealth.

## Results

JailbreakLoRA achieves near-perfect attack success rates (ASR) of 99.1–100.0% across Llama3-8B-Instruct, Llama2-7B-Chat, and ChatGLM-6B, while simultaneously maintaining strong multi-task downstream performance (BBH: 88.8–94.0%, MMLU: 73.2–82.8%). The paper reports an average 16.0% improvement in ASR and 16.5% improvement in multi-task capability over existing SOTA LoRA-based attacks. In real-world LoRAHub recommendation experiments, JailbreakLoRA(grad) achieves a 50.2% chosen rate on BBH and 58.0% on MMLU, outperforming all baselines and approaching the 48.2%/56.0% rates of a well-trained benign SFT adapter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JailbreakLoRA dominates all four baselines on the critical multi-task downstream metrics (BBH and MMLU) while matching or exceeding their attack success rates. Against POLISHED (Dong et al., 2024), JailbreakLoRA(grad) improves BBH by 14.2–25.2 points and MMLU by 11.4–21.3 points across models, while also raising ASR by 12.7–21.8 points. Against FUSION (Dong et al., 2024), the gap is even more dramatic: FUSION's ASR collapses to 4.4–22.0% (vs. 99.1–100.0% for JailbreakLoRA), indicating it fails to maintain attack capability when preserving utility. Against LoRA-as-an-attack (Liu et al., 2024a), which achieves comparable ASR (92.5–99.1%), JailbreakLoRA substantially outperforms on downstream tasks (e.g., BBH 94.0% vs. 59.2% on Llama3, a 34.8-point gap), confirming that prior methods sacrifice utility for attack. JailbreakEdit (Chen et al., 2025) is the weakest baseline, degrading both ASR (40.5–65.3%) and downstream performance (BBH 24.4–34.8%), making it impractical for real-world LoRA-sharing scenarios where adoption depends on demonstrated task capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes two established multi-task optimization techniques—homoscedastic uncertainty weighting (Kendall et al., CVPR 2018) and PCGrad-style gradient projection (Yu et al., NeurIPS 2020)—for the novel purpose of co-training a jailbreak backdoor alongside multi-downstream task objectives within a single LoRA adapter. It departs from prior LoRA-based attacks (POLISHED, FUSION, LoRA-as-an-attack, JailbreakEdit) by explicitly treating downstream task utility as a first-class optimization constraint rather than an afterthought, arguing that without competitive multi-task performance a malicious adapter would never be selected by a sharing platform's recommendation system. The trigger-prefix injection mechanism, which exploits inference-time hallucination to steer generation after an affirmative prefix is produced, extends the shallow-alignment observation (Qi et al., 2024) into a training-time design principle for backdoor stealth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and practically relevant attack surface in the rapidly growing LoRA-sharing ecosystem (Hugging Face, LoRAHub), showing that a single adapter can simultaneously achieve near-perfect attack success rates and strong multi-task performance, making it indistinguishable from a high-quality benign adapter during platform evaluation. The defense evaluation is sobering: Vulnerable Prompt Scanning, Re-Alignment, Llama Guard, and PeftGuard all fail to reliably detect or neutralize the attack without degrading the very downstream utility that makes LoRA adoption worthwhile. Key open questions include whether adapter-level trustworthiness assessment can be made robust to this class of multi-objective backdoors, and whether the observed interference between uncertainty weighting and gradient projection (a negative result that limits the method's own design space) generalizes to other multi-task security settings.
