## Problem

Existing video captioning and retrieval benchmarks rely on short, coarse annotations that cannot stress-test modern VLMs on fine-grained understanding, and the two tasks are treated as separate problems solved by specialized architectures (CLIP dual-encoders for retrieval, MLLMs for captioning). Additionally, standard evaluation metrics (n-gram overlap, LLM-based scoring) fail to jointly assess both static object descriptions and dynamic action sequences, obscuring spatiotemporal biases in VLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261734/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For captioning, the model receives a video clip (5–20 s, 16 or 32 sampled frames, max 460,800 pixels) and produces a 150–300-word hierarchical description covering summary, static objects, dynamic actions, and miscellaneous details. For retrieval, the same model encodes either a video or a long text query (up to ~228 words) into a single embedding via an EOL prompt that forces the model to summarize in one token, using the next-token hidden state as the feature vector. The benchmark's captions are manually split into pure spatial and pure temporal variants to enable separate evaluation of each dimension.

## Method

CARE is a unified 7B baseline built on Qwen2-VL that handles both captioning and retrieval through a two-stage progressive SFT. Stage-I (fine-grained caption adaptation) fine-tunes the model on Tarsier Recap and LLaVA-Video-178k video-text pairs with the prompt 'Describe the video in detail,' aligning the output to a fine-grained text space. Stage-II (retrieval adaptation) freezes the vision encoder and applies text-only contrastive learning on an NLI dataset using an Explicit One-word Limitation (EOL) prompt, shifting the output representation from vocabulary space to embedding space with a cosine-similarity InfoNCE loss. The key insight is that both tasks reduce to a mapping φ: R^{T×H×W×C} → R^D, where D is either the vocabulary or embedding dimension, enabling a single model to generate captions and extract retrieval features. The accompanying CAREBENCH benchmark (1,000 videos from FineAction) introduces two novel metrics—ReBias for retrieval and CapST for captioning—that separately score spatial and temporal performance to expose spatiotemporal biases in VLMs.

## Results

On CAREBENCH General Retrieval, CARE (7B) achieves 77.0 R@1 (Text-to-Video) and 79.0 R@1 (Video-to-Text), surpassing all CLIP-based models (best: InternVideo2 at 72.5/69.5) and all other contrastively trained MLLMs (best: Qwen2.5-VL 7B at 77.5/75.5). On video captioning, CARE achieves 35.1 F1 (Events) and 31.7 F1 (Objects) overall, outperforming all open-source MLLMs including Qwen2-VL 72B (30.5/24.2 F1) by a significant margin. On out-of-domain video QA, CARE scores 60.4 on MVBench and 50.1 on TVBench, matching or exceeding Gemini 1.5 Pro (60.5/46.5) and substantially outperforming GPT-4o (47.8/35.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARE (7B) achieves the best Video-to-Text retrieval (79.0 R@1) on CAREBENCH, beating Qwen2-VL 7B (77.4), Qwen2.5-VL 7B (75.5), InternVL2 8B (73.6), Tarsier 7B (70.6), and all CLIP-based models (best: InternVideo2 at 69.5). On Text-to-Video retrieval, CARE (77.0) is marginally behind Qwen2.5-VL 7B (77.5) but ahead of Qwen2-VL 7B (76.6) and all other baselines. On video captioning, CARE (35.1 F1 Events, 31.7 F1 Objects) outperforms all open-source MLLMs by 4–10 F1 points over the next-best (MiniCPM-V 2.6 at 31.1/30.5, Qwen2-VL 72B at 30.5/24.2), though GPT-4o mini retains a slight edge (36.8/33.8 F1). On video QA, CARE (60.4 MVBench, 50.1 TVBench) matches Gemini 1.5 Pro on MVBench (60.5) and surpasses it on TVBench (46.5), while substantially outperforming GPT-4o (47.8/35.8) and VideoChat2 (51.0/33.0). The ablation confirms that the two-stage SFT is synergistic: retrieval adaptation alone boosts R@1 by +51.4 over baseline, while fine-grained caption adaptation alone improves F1 by +7.0, and combining both yields the best unified score (55.7 vs. 26.2 baseline).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Qwen2-VL as its base MLLM and adapts the E5-V (Jiang et al., 2024b) EOL-prompt contrastive learning recipe for video embedding extraction, but departs from prior work by unifying video captioning and retrieval—traditionally addressed by separate CLIP-based dual-encoders and MLLMs—into a single two-stage SFT pipeline. On the benchmark side, it extends the line of detailed video captioning benchmarks (DREAM-1K, VDC) by adding hierarchical spatial/temporal annotation separation and human-only annotation, addressing the hallucination and bias issues of GPT-annotated data. The ReBias and CapST metrics are novel diagnostic tools that go beyond n-gram or single-axis LLM-judge evaluations by explicitly decomposing performance into spatial and temporal components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The spatiotemporal bias finding—that VLMs rely heavily on spatial scene cues with temporal retrieval dropping ~20–25 R@1 points while spatial drops only ~0–2 points—provides a concrete diagnostic that existing benchmarks cannot reveal, pointing to a clear weakness in current video understanding. The unification result (a 7B model matching or exceeding specialized CLIP-based retrievers while also being the best captioner) suggests that separate model families for retrieval and captioning may be unnecessary. However, the paper explicitly acknowledges it does not address the bias itself, and CARE shows a small performance drop on coarse-grained benchmarks (MSR-VTT, DiDeMo), leaving open the question of how to achieve balanced spatiotemporal understanding without sacrificing generalization.
