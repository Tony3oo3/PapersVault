## Problem

Generative-model-assisted offline safe RL methods (e.g., CDT) suffer from two critical limitations: they lack the ability to stitch optimal transitions from suboptimal trajectories because their attention-based temporal conditioning causes them to memorize suboptimal context, and they have no explicit mechanism to balance reward maximization against constraint satisfaction when tested with imbalanced human-specified reward-cost targets. These issues lead to either unsafe policies under tight constraints or overly conservative behavior under loose ones.

## Input / Output

At each timestep, the policy (a 7-layer MLP with hidden size 128) receives the current state, the relabeled target reward and cost return-to-go values, the estimated optimal reward and cost goals from the goal functions, and the temporal segment length t′, and outputs a single action. Unlike CDT which conditions on K previous timesteps via a GPT attention window, GAS conditions only on the current state and scalar return/goal values, discarding the temporal attention mechanism entirely. During training, the target returns are relabeled via uniform sampling around the true values (reward: ±δ, cost: up to C^max) to build robustness against imbalanced test-time specifications.

## Method

GAS replaces the transformer architecture of CDT with a simple MLP and introduces three key mechanisms for transition-level stitching. First, Temporal Segmented Return Augmentation (TSRA) expands each transition into multiple variants with returns computed over different sub-trajectory lengths, enabling the model to find better (R, C) pairs from other trajectories at the same state. Second, two goal functions (reward and cost) are trained via expectile regression (α=0.8) on the augmented, relabeled dataset to estimate the optimal achievable reward return-to-go satisfying the cost constraint and its corresponding cost, without any Bellman backup. Third, the policy is optimized with a constrained Advantage-Weighted Regression loss that weights transitions by the expectile-based advantage and gates on constraint satisfaction. A dataset reshaping step further rebalances the reward-cost distribution by oversampling top-q% reward transitions per cost bin, improving training stability.

## Results

Under tight constraint thresholds (10–30% of max cost), GAS achieves the best safe performance across all 12 tasks, with an average normalized reward of 0.66±0.02 and normalized cost of 0.67±0.17 (below the threshold of 1). Under loose constraints (70–90%), GAS attains the highest average reward of 0.86±0.02 while remaining safe (C=0.87±0.05), outperforming CDT (R=0.80) by approximately 7.5%. GAS is the only method that simultaneously satisfies constraints and maximizes reward across all tasks under tight thresholds, whereas CDT violates constraints (avg C=1.12) and FISOR, though safe, sacrifices reward severely (avg R=0.36).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CDT (the closest GM-based competitor), GAS matches reward under tight constraints (0.66 vs. 0.67 avg) but is the only method that remains safe across all tasks (C=0.67 vs. CDT's C=1.12, which violates the threshold). Under loose constraints, GAS outperforms CDT on reward by ~7.5% (0.86 vs. 0.80) while both remain safe. Against FISOR, GAS dramatically improves reward (0.66 vs. 0.36 tight; 0.86 vs. 0.36 loose) while maintaining comparable safety, showing FISOR's feasibility-guided diffusion is overly conservative. Against RL-based baselines (CPQ, COptiDICE, WSAC, VOCE), GAS dominates on both reward and constraint satisfaction, with these methods suffering severe cost violations (avg C ranging from 1.24 to 3.10 under tight constraints). Against zero-shot RL methods CAPS and CCAC, GAS achieves higher reward (0.66 vs. 0.58/0.55) and better safety (C=0.67 vs. 0.69/1.31) under tight constraints. In the zero-shot adaptation experiment (Fig. 5), GAS consistently stays below constraint thresholds from 10% to 90%, while CDT becomes unsafe below 30% and overly conservative at higher thresholds.

## Relation to Prior Work

GAS builds directly on the Constrained Decision Transformer (CDT) framework, which reformulates offline safe RL as a goal-conditioned generative problem, but deliberately departs from CDT's GPT/attention architecture in favor of a simple MLP that focuses exclusively on transition-level stitching. The stitching mechanism draws on expectile regression from IQL (Kostrikov et al., 2022) and the return-relabeling idea from CDT itself, while the temporal segmented return augmentation and dataset reshaping are novel contributions specific to the constrained setting. The paper positions itself against both RL-based OSRL methods (CPQ, COptiDICE, WSAC, VOCE) and concurrent GM-based approaches (FISOR, CQDT, COPDT), arguing that prior stitching improvements for unconstrained offline RL (QDT, WT, ADT, Reinformer) cannot be directly transferred to the constrained setting due to the fundamentally different reward-cost tradeoff objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GAS addresses two practical pain points in GM-assisted OSRL: the inability to stitch high-quality transitions from suboptimal trajectories and the lack of a principled mechanism to balance reward maximization against constraint satisfaction. The zero-shot adaptation to arbitrary constraint thresholds without retraining is a significant practical advantage over RL-based baselines that must be retrained per threshold. However, the paper acknowledges a fundamental limitation: GAS can only select and recombine safe sub-segments already present in the dataset, rather than constructing genuinely new safe trajectories by combining safe and unsafe data as RL methods can. Open questions include whether more advanced memory mechanisms could recover the temporal dependencies that the MLP architecture sacrifices, and whether the approach extends to multi-constraint or multi-task settings.
