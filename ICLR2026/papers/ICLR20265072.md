## Problem

Transformer-based EEG classifiers achieve strong accuracy but consume millions of parameters and operate as uninterpretable black boxes, limiting deployment on resource-constrained EEG devices. Prior graph-based unrolling approaches model only positive graphs, ignoring the pairwise anti-correlations inherent in multi-channel EEG signals. This paper addresses both gaps by unrolling a denoising algorithm on balanced signed graphs—graphs with no odd-negative-edge cycles—into a lightweight, fully interpretable transformer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265072/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 35-channel EEG recordings sampled at 500 Hz for 15 seconds (first 2 s and last 1 s discarded), segments each channel into 6 non-overlapping 1000-point temporal chunks, and arranges them on a product graph of 210 nodes (35 channels × 6 time slices). Rather than feeding a classifier head, the output is a binary label (healthy vs. epilepsy) determined by comparing the ℓ₂ reconstruction errors of two separately trained class-specific denoisers: the input is assigned to the class whose denoiser reconstructs it with smaller error.

## Method

The core architecture alternates Balanced Graph Learning (BGL) modules with Low-Pass Filter (LPF) modules in a feed-forward stack. Each BGL module uses a shallow CNN to extract per-node features, computes Mahalanobis distances, and assigns signed edge weights via the Cartwright-Harary polarity condition (βᵢβⱼ = sign(wᵢⱼ)), ensuring graph balance; the resulting normalized adjacency matrix plays the role of self-attention weights. Each LPF module applies an ideal low-pass filter on the corresponding positive graph (obtained via the similarity transform L⁺ = T Lᴮ T⁻¹) implemented in O(N) time through Lanczos approximation, with a single learnable cutoff frequency per block. Two denoisers Ψ₀ and Ψ₁ are trained with a contrastive MSE loss that penalizes the class-0 denoiser for reconstructing class-1 signals well, encouraging class-discriminative structure. The entire network uses only 14,787 parameters—less than 1% of comparable transformer baselines—while achieving 97.57% accuracy on the Turkish Epilepsy EEG Dataset.

## Results

On the Turkish Epilepsy EEG Dataset (default 8:1:1 split), the proposed method achieves 97.57% accuracy, 98.58% precision, and 98.01% F1-score using only 14,787 parameters. Under the more challenging leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) protocol, it reaches 90.06% accuracy and 92.59% F1-score, outperforming all graph-based baselines. On the TUH Abnormal EEG Corpus, the method attains 90.69% accuracy and 92.60% F1-score, surpassing classical and deep learning baselines. Training takes 2h 14min and inference 55 seconds, substantially faster than EEGNet (5h 33min / 613s) and FBCSPNet (22h 19min / 396s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the default 8:1:1 split, the proposed method (97.57% accuracy, 14,787 params) outperforms all non-graph baselines: CWT+DCNN (95.91%), mAtt (92.00%), EEG2Rep (91.41%), TF+ROD (88.08%), MDTW+KNN (87.78%), and the Transformer of Lih et al. (85.12%, 1.85M params). The only method exceeding it is STFT+CNN (Bhandage et al.) at 99.20% accuracy, but that model requires 11.5M parameters—roughly 780× more—making it impractical for resource-constrained EEG devices. Under the LOSO protocol, the method (90.06% accuracy, 92.59% F1) decisively beats all graph-based baselines: FBCSPNet (81.76%), Deep4Net (78.62%), EEGNet (78.78%), DGCNN (76.74%), and GIN (68.82%), with margins of 8–21 percentage points in accuracy. On the TUH Abnormal EEG Corpus, the method (90.69% accuracy, 92.60% F1) surpasses DWT+CSP+CatBoost (90.22%, 88.89% F1) and WaveNet-LSTM (88.76%, 88.32% F1). Statistical significance is confirmed via paired t-tests (p < 0.001 vs. EEGNet, FBCSPNet, Deep4Net) and ANOVA showing no significant per-subject variance in LOSO performance (p = 0.65 for accuracy).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Thuc et al. (2024), which unrolled a graph smoothness prior over positive graphs into a lightweight transformer, by replacing positive graphs with balanced signed graphs that capture pairwise anti-correlations inherent in EEG signals. The theoretical backbone for this extension comes from Dinesh et al. (2025), who proved that a balanced signed graph Laplacian is a similarity transform of a corresponding positive graph Laplacian, thereby inheriting well-defined spectral frequencies. The ideal low-pass filter is implemented via Lanczos approximation (Susnjara et al., 2015) to avoid costly eigen-decomposition, and the overall architecture follows the algorithm-unrolling paradigm (Monga et al., 2021) and the white-box transformer concept (Yu et al., 2023), but with a graph-signal-processing-specific objective and a novel contrastive denoising loss for classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 14,787-parameter interpretable model can outperform a 1.8M-parameter transformer by over 12 percentage points on epilepsy EEG classification, which is practically significant for deployment on resource-constrained EEG devices. The balanced signed graph formulation provides a principled way to encode anti-correlations while retaining spectral filtering tools, and the reconstruction-error-based classification rule offers full interpretability of both the denoising and decision steps. Key limitations include restriction to binary classification and reliance on a single primary dataset (Turkish Epilepsy EEG), though validation on TUH Abnormal EEG Corpus provides some cross-dataset evidence. Future work on extending to multi-class classification trees and to other biomedical signal types would broaden the impact.
