## Problem

Recurrent LLMs offer linear-time inference but underperform Self-Attention LLMs on long-context understanding because their fixed-size memory is overwhelmed when processing entire inputs in a single pass. Prior work attempted to close this gap by increasing architectural memory capacity, yet failed to match Self-Attention performance. The paper argues the root cause is a misalignment between architecture and inference method, not insufficient memory alone.

## Input / Output

The method takes a long context (up to 256k tokens) and a query/task instruction, partitioning the context into semantically coherent chunks via a hierarchical delimiter-based chunker (paragraphs → sentences → words, with a 1.5 tokens/word heuristic). At each round the model emits a JSON-formatted contextual summary containing target, clues, reason, and result fields, followed by either a <READ> or <STOP> action token. The final output is the answer to the query, with the recurrent hidden state preserved and updated across all rounds at zero additional memory cost.

## Method

Smooth Reading co-designs a recurrent architecture with an End-to-End Multi-Round (EMR) inference procedure: the model reads the context chunk-by-chunk, produces a compressed contextual summary after each chunk, and incrementally updates its fixed-size hidden state across rounds, avoiding the memory overload of One-Round inference while retaining long-range information. A key architectural insight is that EMR favors strong length extrapolation over raw single-pass memory capacity, making sliding-window attention (SWA) superior to linear-attention (RWKV) under this inference paradigm at extreme sequence lengths. Efficiency is optimized by jointly tuning the window size (model memory span) and chunk size (processing granularity), with a moderate chunk-to-window ratio (~1:2) yielding a U-shaped inference-time curve. The model is trained via supervised fine-tuning on a 48,856-item dataset spanning QA, summarization, few-shot, retrieval, and code tasks, with stepwise summaries generated by a rule-based method or DeepSeek-V3 to embed the multi-round reading behavior directly into the weights.

## Results

On LongBench, SWA-3B-4k-EMR (Smooth Reading) achieves 50.99% average, surpassing the Self-Attention baseline Qwen-2.5-3B-OR (47.38%) by 3.61 points and the NMR variant (48.37%) by 2.62 points. On NIAH, SWA-3B-4k-EMR maintains near-perfect accuracy (99.80% average) from 64k to 256k tokens, while Qwen-2.5-3B-OR collapses to 41.33% at the same lengths. In terms of efficiency, SWA-3B-4k-EMR trains 2.5× faster and infers 2× faster (up to 4× with early stopping) than Qwen-2.5-3B-OR at 64k context, while incurring only 20% overhead over its own One-Round inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary Self-Attention baseline Qwen-2.5-3B-OR, SWA-3B-4k-EMR wins on LongBench (50.99 vs. 47.38) and dramatically on NIAH extrapolation (99.80% at 64k–256k vs. 41.33%), while also being 2× faster at inference. Against the recurrent baseline RWKV-7-3B-OR, Smooth Reading improves LongBench from 41.43 to 48.03 (RWKV-7-EMR) and NIAH extrapolation from 15.87% to 32.58%, though SWA-3B-4k-EMR (99.80%) far exceeds RWKV-3B-EMR (32.58%) at extreme lengths, highlighting the importance of architecture choice under EMR. Against OPRM (Ben-Kish et al., 2025), SWA-3B-4k-EMR outperforms all three OPRM variants on LongBench by 19–23 points (50.99 vs. 27.88–31.35). Against CompACT and RAG/RAG+ on QA tasks (Table 11), SWA-3B-4k-EMR achieves the highest F1 across HotpotQA (54.25 vs. 36.91), MuSiQue (31.77 vs. 19.92), 2WikiMQA (56.99 vs. 39.95), and TriviaQA (85.61 vs. 82.15). Among the broader 6B–8B Self-Attention models in Table 7 (Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B, ChatGLM2/3-6B, LongChat-v1.5-7B), the 7B SWA-7B-4k-EMR (53.86) outperforms all except Qwen-2.5-7B-OR (54.60), confirming the method's competitiveness at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of work that closes the recurrent-vs-self-attention long-context gap through architectural changes alone (larger state sizes, more expressive update rules in Mamba, RWKV, DeltaNet, etc.). It reframes the problem as an architecture-inference co-design question, arguing that One-Round inference is fundamentally misaligned with fixed-size recurrent memory, and that End-to-End Multi-Round inference with hidden-state preservation is the missing piece. Conceptually, it is closest to OPRM (Ben-Kish et al., 2025), which also targets memory overflow in recurrent models, but differs by preserving hidden states across rounds rather than discarding them, and by systematically studying how inference strategy interacts with architectural properties (memory capacity vs. length extrapolation). The chunked-reading and contextual-summary components draw on prior multi-step inference methods (CompACT, RAG, interactive reading agents), but the key novelty is the end-to-end preservation of recurrent hidden memory across rounds, which is unique to recurrent architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, well-motivated recipe for making recurrent LLMs competitive on long-context tasks without abandoning their linear-complexity advantage, and the co-design guidelines (window size vs. chunk size, OR vs. EMR) offer actionable guidance for future recurrent model development. However, the approach requires supervised fine-tuning to adapt models to the EMR paradigm, is validated only on sliding-window and RWKV architectures at 3B/7B scale, and the LongBench gains over strong self-attention baselines are modest (~3.6 points). Open questions include whether the method extends to other recurrent families (DeltaNet, Mamba variants) lacking optimized inference engines, whether reinforcement learning can replace the SFT data-construction pipeline, and whether the query-before-context ordering limitation can be overcome with more flexible action spaces.
