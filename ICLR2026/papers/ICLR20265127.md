## Problem

LLMs at inference time can silently leak sensitive tokens (e.g., PII) from their context into generated outputs, yet existing privacy-preserving inference methods either lack provable guarantees (scrubbing, prompt engineering) or suffer severe utility degradation (DP-Decoding, DP-Prompt produce perplexities exceeding 100). This paper addresses the gap by providing a token-level differentially private inference mechanism that provably bounds the influence of sensitive context tokens on generated output while maintaining high text quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a document partitioned into m labeled privacy groups (sensitive token spans from NER) plus a public group, a paraphrasing query, per-group Rényi privacy budgets β_i, and LLM parameters θ. Redacted spans are padded with equal-length '_' placeholders to prevent length-based leakage. The output is an autoregressively generated paraphrased document of at most T_max tokens, produced via m+1 parallel LLM forward passes per step (one public, one per privacy group), making latency approximately equivalent to a single forward pass.

## Method

DP-FUSION computes, at each generation step, a public next-token distribution p_pub (LLM conditioned on only public tokens) and m private distributions p_priv,i (LLM conditioned on public tokens plus the i-th privacy group). Each private distribution is then 'mollified' by linearly interpolating with p_pub using a coefficient λ_i, found via bisection search as the maximum value satisfying a symmetric Rényi divergence bound D_α↔(λ_i·p_priv,i + (1−λ_i)·p_pub ∥ p_pub) ≤ αβ_i. The final sampling distribution is the uniform average over all m mollified group distributions, from which the next token is drawn. The key theoretical contribution is proving that the Rényi divergence is monotonically non-decreasing in λ (enabling the bisection), and that per-token (α,β_i)-RDP composes additively over T autoregressive steps to yield a closed-form (ε_i, δ)-DP guarantee via RDP-to-DP conversion. The approach adapts the ensemble-style private prediction of PMixED from training-time to inference-time under a document-privatization threat model.

## Results

On TAB-ECHR (100 ECHR legal documents), DP-FUSION achieves perplexity of 1.426–1.459 across its privacy range (αβ = 0.01–0.10), while the best DPI baselines achieve PPL ≥ 3.96 (DP-Decoding, λ=0.9) and ≥ 4.26 (DP-Prompt, width=50, T=0.75), yielding a ~6× perplexity advantage at comparable privacy levels. In the strictest privacy setting (αβ=0.01), DP-FUSION matches the No-DPI NER baseline in utility (PPL 1.459 vs. 1.46) while achieving a lower attack success rate (0.26 vs. 0.2767) and providing formal (ε,δ)-DP guarantees. On the MACCROBAT 2020 medical dataset, DP-FUSION at αβ=0.01 achieves the lowest mean ASR (8.3%) while maintaining No-DPI NER-level utility (cosine similarity 0.500 vs. 0.497).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DP-FUSION substantially outperforms both DPI baselines on the utility/privacy trade-off. Against DP-Decoding (Majmudar et al., 2022), DP-FUSION achieves PPL 1.426–1.459 versus 3.96–14.15, a 2.8×–10× perplexity improvement, while maintaining comparable or lower ASR (0.26–0.29 vs. 0.16–0.66 depending on λ). Against DP-Prompt (Utpala et al., 2023), the gap is even larger: DP-FUSION's PPL of 1.426 at αβ=0.10 is ~6× lower than DP-Prompt's best usable setting (PPL 4.26 at width=50, T=0.75), and DP-Prompt at width=5 produces essentially garbled output (PPL >100). At the strictest privacy setting (αβ=0.01), DP-FUSION matches the No-DPI NER scrubbing baseline in utility (PPL 1.459 vs. 1.46) while achieving a slightly lower ASR (0.26 vs. 0.277) and, critically, providing formal (ε,δ)-DP guarantees that NER scrubbing lacks. In the LLM-as-a-judge evaluation, DP-FUSION wins ≥95% of head-to-head comparisons against both DP-Decoding and DP-Prompt across all their parameter settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

DP-FUSION adapts the ensemble-style private prediction framework of PMixED (Flemings et al., 2024) — itself descended from PATE and SUBMIX — from the training-time setting to the inference-time setting under a fundamentally different threat model. Where PMixED uses stochasticity of sampled LLM outputs over disjoint data partitions to protect training records, DP-FUSION instead mixes per-privacy-group output distributions against a public baseline to bound the influence of specific sensitive tokens in the context. This departs from prior DPI methods (DP-Decoding, DP-Prompt) that perturb the output distribution uniformly (via interpolation with a uniform or clipped-logit mechanism), which the paper shows yields severely degraded utility. The per-group Rényi divergence budget and bisection-search mollification are the key algorithmic contributions over the single-mixing-weight approach of prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DP-FUSION provides the first token-level, per-group provable DP guarantee at LLM inference time with a practical utility level (PPL ~1.43–1.46, close to the no-privacy NER baseline of 1.46), making document privatization with formal guarantees feasible for the first time. The dual application to both PII document privatization and prompt-injection defense broadens its relevance, though the injection-defense evaluation is preliminary. Key open questions include: (i) the method's reliance on a high-recall NER tagger, since missed spans fall outside the guarantee; (ii) the modest utility gap over plain NER scrubbing on low-PII-density documents, which limits practical adoption where sensitive tokens are sparse; and (iii) whether the multi-group formulation's increasing dominance by the public distribution as m grows can be mitigated without sacrificing per-group guarantees.
