## Problem

State-of-the-art policy gradient methods (GRPO, PPO) for aligning text-to-image diffusion and flow models suffer from training instability and model collapse, limiting convergence speed and image quality. The paper identifies the root cause as disproportionate credit assignment: the mathematical structure of generative samplers produces a native per-timestep weight w(t) that is highly non-uniform (spanning orders of magnitude), creating volatile, non-proportional feedback that destabilizes gradient updates and disproportionately clips the most amplified gradients.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated RGB image, operating within existing diffusion (SD1.5, SD1.4) and flow-matching (FLUX.1-dev, SD3.5-M) architectures. During training, a terminal reward model (Aesthetics, BERTScore, HPSv2.1, PickScore, or OCR) scores the final generated image, and the policy is updated via group-relative advantage normalization over batches of 12–16 images per prompt. The method is applied as a drop-in modification to the policy gradient objective, requiring no architectural changes to the base generator.

## Method

PCPO introduces two targeted modifications to the standard PPO/GRPO policy gradient objective. First, it replaces the numerically unstable term ρt−1 with log ρt, yielding a stable log-hinge objective that avoids the exp() step and its associated floating-point precision errors. Second, and more critically, it enforces proportionate credit assignment by ensuring each timestep's gradient contribution is proportional to its integration interval rather than to an arbitrary sampler-derived weight. For diffusion models, this is achieved by re-engineering the DDIM variance schedule σ̃t to produce a constant weight w(t)=w* across all timesteps; for flow models, where modifying the variance schedule would deviate from the optimized sampling procedure, PCPO instead directly reweights the training objective with w(ti)=ζΔti (Proposition 2). This principled reweighting, grounded in an analogy to the REINFORCE eligibility vector, eliminates the high-variance learning signal and drastically reduces clipping, accelerating convergence by 25–100% while mitigating model collapse.

## Results

PCPO achieves 24.6%–41.2% training speedup over baselines across all settings (e.g., 41.2% for DanceGRPO on FLUX, reaching HPSv2.1 = 0.360 in 148 epochs vs. 209 for the baseline). On image quality, PCPO reduces FID from 46.23 to 40.38 and FD_DINO from 539.83 to 438.88 on FLUX (DanceGRPO setting), with statistically significant improvements (p < 0.001). On unseen prompts (MJHQ-30K), PCPO at 120 epochs outperforms DanceGRPO at 180 epochs on all five alignment metrics (HPSv2.1: 0.350 vs. 0.345, CLIPScore: 0.398 vs. 0.387, PickScore: 22.28 vs. 22.14, ImageReward: 1.32 vs. 1.27), confirming faster convergence to a more generalizable policy with less reward hacking.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDPO (Black et al., 2024), PCPO achieves a statistically significant FID reduction (β = −7.75, p = 0.047) and IS reduction (β = −0.241, p = 0.021) on SD1.5, with a 24.6% training speedup; LPIPS diversity shows a significant positive interaction with batch size (β_int = 0.016, p = 0.008). Against the state-of-the-art DanceGRPO (Xue et al., 2025), PCPO wins decisively: on FLUX it reduces FID by 5.85 points and FD_DINO by 100.95 points (both p < 0.001), and on unseen MJHQ-30K prompts it outperforms DanceGRPO on all five alignment metrics at 60% fewer training epochs. Against Flow-GRPO (Liu et al., 2025) on SD3.5-M, PCPO achieves higher reward, lower KL divergence, and lower clipping fraction throughout training. In the DPO/self-play comparison (Table 9), PCPO outperforms SSPO (Zhang et al., 2025), SPIN-Diffusion (Yuan et al., 2024), and LPO (Liang et al., 2025) on Aesthetic (+0.71 vs. +0.57/+0.48/+0.48), PickScore (+2.33 vs. +1.37/+0.82/+1.13), and ImageReward (+0.90 vs. +0.86/+0.20/+0.58), though it trails slightly on HPSv2 (gains of +0.010 vs. +0.034/+0.005/+0.011), a gap the authors attribute to the single-reward-model constraint placed on PCPO for fairness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-based alignment lineage for T2I models (DDPO → DanceGRPO → Flow-GRPO) by identifying a specific mathematical root cause of training instability: the native weight w(t) induced by the sampler's variance schedule produces disproportionate credit assignment across timesteps. It builds on the hinge-loss reinterpretation of PPO-clip (Huang et al., 2024) to justify a log-ratio reformulation, and draws an analogy to the REINFORCE eligibility vector to motivate a proportionality principle. Concurrent work (TempFlow-GRPO, MixGRPO) addresses credit assignment through empirical heuristics (trajectory branching, sliding SDE windows), but PCPO provides a more fundamental theoretical account that explains when and why those heuristics succeed or fail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PCPO offers a model-agnostic, zero-overhead fix (no extra compute, no architectural changes) that simultaneously accelerates convergence (25–41% speedup) and mitigates model collapse across diffusion and flow-matching frameworks, which is practically significant given the computational cost of RL-based T2I alignment. The counterintuitive finding that emphasizing high-noise timesteps—the field's default heuristic—actually degrades performance validates the need for principled over empirical reweighting. Open questions include how PCPO interacts with gradient clipping (still needed for flow models), dynamic clipping, and KL regularization, and whether the proportionality principle extends to multi-step or hierarchical samplers. A limitation is that the DDPO experiments use simple prompts, and the DanceGRPO/FLUX experiments are constrained by compute (LoRA on 8 GPUs vs. full fine-tuning on 16).
