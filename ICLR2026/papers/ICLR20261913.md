## Problem

Pure text-based chain-of-thought reasoning in large language models remains computationally inefficient and error-prone on competition-level mathematics requiring precise arithmetic or symbolic manipulation. Existing tool-augmented approaches suffer from three critical gaps: extreme scarcity of high-quality tool-use training data, absence of agentic reinforcement learning for autonomous tool-strategy optimization, and inadequate training infrastructure for ultra-long sequences (96k tokens) with massive tool invocations (96 calls).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261913/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a mathematical problem as plain text and produces a structured multi-turn trajectory interleaving natural-language reasoning (delimited by  tags), executable code blocks (<code> tags), and sandboxed interpreter feedback (<interpreter> tags), ultimately yielding a final numerical or symbolic answer. The interaction is bounded by configurable limits on total sequence length (up to 96k tokens) and tool-call count (up to 96 invocations), with interpreter output capped at 1024 tokens per call. Evaluation uses avg@32 (32 independent inference runs per problem) on AIME24, AIME25, and HMMT25.

## Method

AgentMath employs a two-stage training pipeline. First, an automated synthesis pipeline converts pure-text long-CoT data into tool-augmented SFT trajectories via code injection (replacing computation-intensive steps with executable code and real execution outputs), multi-faceted quality refinement (format correction, executability verification, feedback alignment, tool-usage rationality), and self-correction trace injection from failed executions. Second, agentic RL using GRPO with DAPO-style modifications dynamically interleaves natural language generation with real-time code execution during rollout, applying loss masking to exclude interpreter tokens from gradient updates and using a composite reward that combines binary answer correctness with a tool-usage efficiency bonus. The training infrastructure introduces request-level asynchronous rollout scheduling (decoupling GPU inference from CPU-bound code execution via a distributed sandbox cluster), agentic partial rollout (decomposing trajectories into budget-limited segments to eliminate long-tail latency), and prefix-aware weighted load balancing with LRU sticky sessions for KV-cache reuse, achieving 4–5× end-to-end throughput improvement.

## Results

AgentMath-30B-A3B (3B active params) achieves 90.6% on AIME24, 86.4% on AIME25, and 73.8% on HMMT25 (avg@32), surpassing OpenAI-o3-mini (87.3/86.3/53.0) and Claude-Opus-4.0-Thinking (83.0/72.0/58.3) while remaining competitive with OpenAI-o3 (91.6/88.9/77.5) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (92.0/88.0/82.5). At the 8B scale, AgentMath-8B reaches 89.8/84.7/71.3, outperforming the best text-only baseline DS-0528-Qwen3-8B (86.0/76.3/61.5) by 3.8/8.4/9.8 points. The SFT-only AgentMath-235B-A22B achieves 93.4/90.8/81.7, exceeding DS-671B-0528 (91.4/87.5/77.0) and approaching Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking-2507 (94.2/92.3/83.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AgentMath consistently outperforms all open-source tool-augmented baselines: at 1.5B scale it beats CoRT-1.5B (43.1→59.6 on AIME24) and ToRL-1.5B (26.7); at 7–8B it surpasses SimpleTIR-7B (50.5), ToRL-7B (43.3), and CIR-Qwen3-NT8-8B (61.5); at 32B it exceeds STILL-3-TOOL-32B (81.7→90.6), ReTool-32B (67.0), START-32B (66.7), rStar2-Agent-32B (69.4), and CoRT-32B (76.7). Against text-only baselines, AgentMath-8B beats DS-0528-Qwen3-8B by 3.8/8.4/9.8 points and OpenReasoning-7B by 5.1/6.5/7.8 points across AIME24/25/HMMT25. At the 30B scale, AgentMath-30B-A3B edges out Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking-2507 (87.7/85.0/71.4) by 2.9/1.4/2.4 points. The SFT-only 235B model surpasses DS-671B-0528 (91.4/87.5/77.0) by 2.0/3.3/4.7 points but trails Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking-2507 (94.2/92.3/83.9) by 0.8/1.5/2.2 points. Against proprietary models, AgentMath-30B-A3B outperforms OpenAI-o3-mini and Claude-Opus-4.0-Thinking on all three benchmarks but remains 1.0–5.6 points behind OpenAI-o3 and Gemini-2.5-Pro, while AgentMath-235B-A22B-SFT exceeds OpenAI-o3 and approaches Gemini-2.5-Pro.

## Relation to Prior Work

AgentMath builds directly on the GRPO optimization framework (Shao et al., 2024) and DAPO's training techniques (Yu et al., 2025), while adopting the structured <code>/<interpreter> interaction protocol from ReTool (Feng et al., 2025b). It departs from prior tool-augmented reasoning work (ToRA, START, CoRT, STILL3) by replacing manual annotation or prompt-engineered data with an automated code-injection synthesis pipeline, and by moving beyond SFT-only training to an agentic RL paradigm where the model learns tool-use strategies through multi-turn interactive feedback. The infrastructure contributions—request-level asynchronous scheduling, partial rollouts, and prefix-aware load balancing—address a scalability gap that prior agentic RL systems (ToRL, Search-R1) have not tackled for ultra-long sequences with dense tool invocations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 30B-total/3B-active MoE model with tool augmentation can match or exceed much larger proprietary reasoning models on competition math, suggesting that strategic tool use is a more parameter-efficient path to mathematical reasoning than pure text-based CoT scaling. The 4–5× training speedup for agentic RL on ultra-long sequences is a practically significant infrastructure contribution likely to be adopted by other groups training tool-using agents. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond mathematics to other domains requiring precise computation, whether the RL gains over SFT would persist with larger base models (the 235B variant was SFT-only due to compute constraints), and whether the emergent self-correction behavior is robust or an artifact of the specific reward design.
