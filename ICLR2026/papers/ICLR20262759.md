## Problem

Existing AVQA methods treat audio as a secondary modality for temporal alignment and incorporate question information only at the final fusion stage, leaving the distinctive frequency-domain characteristics of audio signals underutilized. This late-fusion design produces redundant audio-visual representations and limits semantic alignment between the query and multimodal content, particularly in polyphonic music scenes where subtle spectral cues (e.g., a flute stopping while a violin continues) are critical for correct reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 60-second video recordings segmented into 1-second clips, their corresponding audio waveforms, and a natural language question. Visual features are extracted via a frozen CLIP-ViT-L/14 (frame-level and patch-level, with patches compressed via Token Merging), audio features via VGGish (temporal pathway) and AST (frequency pathway), and text features via a CLIP text encoder (sentence-level and word-level), all projected to 512-dimensional vectors. The output is a single answer label selected from a predefined vocabulary, with the final prediction combining a fused audio-visual representation element-wise multiplied by the sentence-level question embedding.

## Method

QSTar integrates query guidance at three pipeline stages: (1) a Query-Guided Multimodal Correlation (QGMC) module that uses word-level question embeddings as cross-attention queries to refine both audio and visual features early, then propagates the resulting query-guided context back to each modality; (2) a Spatial-Temporal-Frequency Interaction (STFI) module where patch-level visual features attend to query-guided audio for spatial localization, and AST-based spectrogram features are modulated by question-conditioned frequency-wise attention to highlight instrument-relevant spectral bands; (3) a Query Context Reasoning (QCR) block that constructs a prompt from five task-relevant keywords (type, duration, location, sequence, loudness) and uses it as a cross-attention query to refine final features before prediction. The key architectural insight is assigning distinct roles to VGGish (stable frame-level temporal alignment) and AST (long-range harmonic/timbre modeling over frequency bins), and embedding linguistic context throughout the pipeline rather than only at the final fusion step.

## Results

On the MUSIC-AVQA benchmark, QSTar achieves 78.98% overall accuracy, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art QA-TIGER (77.62%) by 1.36% and TSPM (76.79%) by 2.19%. The method leads on Audio QA (80.63% avg) and Audio-Visual QA (75.98% avg), while remaining competitive on Visual QA (84.17% avg, trailing QA-TIGER by only 0.97%). On the broader AVQA dataset, QSTar reaches 91.2% total accuracy with prompting, outperforming TSPM (90.8%) and PSTP (90.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QSTar outperforms the previous SOTA QA-TIGER (Kim et al., 2025) by 1.36% overall (78.98% vs 77.62%), with gains of 2.05% on Audio QA and 2.24% on Audio-Visual QA, though it trails by 0.97% on Visual QA (84.17% vs 85.14%). Against TSPM (Li et al., 2024a), QSTar leads by 2.19% overall, and against APL (Li et al., 2024b) and PSOT (Li et al., 2025) by 4.45% and 3.69% respectively. The advantage is most pronounced on comparative and temporal Audio-Visual QA questions, where frequency-domain cues (e.g., detecting when an instrument stops) are critical and visual-only methods struggle. Zero-shot MLLMs perform substantially worse: GPT-4o achieves only 55.72%, Qwen2.5-Omni 53.83%, and Ming-Omni 53.98%, with drops of nearly 30% on comparative and temporal categories. Even the fine-tuned VideoLLaMA2 (71.98%) lags QSTar by 7.0%, with a 19.36% gap on comparative Audio QA. On the AVQA dataset (Yang et al., 2022), QSTar achieves 91.2% with prompting, marginally exceeding TSPM (90.8%) and PSTP (90.2%), confirming generalizability beyond music scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant AVQA paradigm where audio is treated as a secondary modality for temporal alignment and question information is injected only at the final fusion stage (as in TSPM, APL, PSOT). QSTar instead threads query guidance through the entire pipeline—early (QGMC), mid (TFI frequency attention), and late (QCR prompting)—and elevates frequency-domain audio analysis (via AST) to a first-class interaction dimension alongside spatial and temporal perception. Conceptually, it repurposes standard cross-attention and prompt-based conditioning mechanisms but arranges them into a multi-stage, multi-dimensional interaction architecture specifically motivated by the spectral 'fingerprints' of musical instruments that are invisible in RGB frames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that frequency-domain cues are indispensable for music AVQA, particularly for instruments with subtle visual motion (flute, clarinet), and that early query-guided refinement outperforms late-only fusion by a meaningful margin. The stark gap between zero-shot MLLMs (GPT-4o at 55.7%) and the specialized model (78.98%) underscores that fine-grained temporal-frequency reasoning in polyphonic scenes remains beyond current general-purpose models. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond music to broader audio-visual scenes, whether visual frequency-domain processing (DCT, filtering) could complement the audio-side spectral analysis, and how to handle longer videos requiring temporal abstraction or memory mechanisms.
