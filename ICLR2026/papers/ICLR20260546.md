## Problem

Extending Chain-of-Thought reasoning to multimodal settings is challenging because each reasoning step requires both image understanding (~4,900 tokens) and image generation (~4,096 tokens), inflating per-step cost to ~10,000 tokens and making naive autoregressive modeling quadratically expensive. Existing approaches either approximate visual transitions with programmatic operations (cropping, drawing) that miss global structural changes, or loosely couple MLLMs with external generators, leading to fragmented and incoherent reasoning.

## Input / Output

The model accepts text prompts, image+text questions, or reference image+text editing instructions and produces a multi-modal CoT trajectory—an ordered sequence of states where each state may contain interleaved text and images—alongside a final answer (generated image, edited image, or textual response). The trajectory is hierarchically structured: a macro-level plan decomposes the task into 2–3 subgoals, each executed as a localized micro-level MDP trajectory, and a summarizer synthesizes the final output. The underlying BAGEL backbone encodes images via SigLIP2 ViT (70×70 grid) for understanding and FLUX VAE (64×64 grid) for generation, with sequences packed to 32,768 tokens during training.

## Method

Uni-CoT introduces a two-level hierarchical CoT framework built on the BAGEL unified model (Mixture-of-Transformer-Experts with separate understanding and generation experts). At the macro level, a planner autoregressively decomposes the task into subgoals and a summarizer integrates their results, with a macro attention mask restricting visibility to only the prompt, plans, and subtask outcomes. At the micro level, each subtask is executed as a Markov Decision Process with a self-reflection loop (evaluate → decide → refine), where a micro attention mask constrains each transition to depend only on the immediately preceding state and the fixed subgoal instruction, reducing per-subtask complexity from O(T_i²) to O(T_i). This hierarchical decomposition lowers overall complexity from O(T²) to O(T). Training uses a joint loss (cross-entropy for text + MSE for image under Rectified Flow) supplemented by four auxiliary MDP objectives—text action generation, image action generation, next-state prediction, and reward estimation—to stabilize optimization of the self-reflective process.

## Results

On GenEval, Uni-CoT achieves 0.83 overall, surpassing its base model BAGEL (0.79) and the strongest generation-only baseline FLUX.1-dev (0.82). On the reasoning-driven WISE benchmark, Uni-CoT reaches 0.75 overall, setting a new state-of-the-art among open-source models. On the Jigsaw-R1 structured visual reasoning benchmark, Uni-CoT scores 47.60 overall, substantially outperforming BAGEL (40.73) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (41.12), while remaining competitive with GPT-4.1-mini (47.88). On general understanding benchmarks (MME, MMMU, MMBench, MathVista), Uni-CoT preserves or slightly improves upon BAGEL's scores, confirming that the CoT training does not degrade broad world knowledge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GenEval (Table 1), Uni-CoT (0.83) outperforms all unified baselines including BAGEL (0.79), Janus-Pro-7B (0.80), Show-o (0.68), and ILLUME (0.61), and also edges past the strongest generation-only model FLUX.1-dev (0.82). On WISE, Uni-CoT achieves the highest overall score (0.75) among all open-source models, with the self-reflection mechanism contributing the largest gains over macro-CoT alone. On Jigsaw-R1, Uni-CoT (47.60) dramatically surpasses BAGEL (40.73) by +6.87 points, Qwen2.5-VL-7B (41.12) by +6.48 points, and InternVL2.5-2B (36.28) by +11.32 points, while remaining within 0.28 points of GPT-4.1-mini (47.88). On general understanding benchmarks, Uni-CoT roughly matches BAGEL (e.g., MME-P 1687 vs 1687, MMMU 52.7 vs 52.8) with small gains on MMBench (85.6 vs 85.0) and MathVista (73.3 vs 73.1), confirming no degradation of pre-trained capabilities. In the appendix, Uni-CoT also outperforms Gemini 2.0 (62.41) on KRIS (68.00) and is competitive with GPT-4o on GEdit-Bench, further validating its reasoning-driven editing capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on BAGEL, a unified multimodal model supporting both image understanding and generation, and extends the Chain-of-Thought paradigm from text-only LLMs to the multimodal domain. It departs from prior multimodal CoT approaches in two key ways: rather than using RL-based text reasoning (Vision-R1, R1-VL) or loosely coupling MLLMs with external generators (ComfyMind, T2I-R1), it embeds visual state transitions natively within a single unified model; and rather than treating the full reasoning trajectory monolithically, it introduces a macro-micro hierarchical decomposition with MDP-constrained micro-level self-reflection to reduce attention complexity from quadratic to near-linear. The hierarchical planning idea draws from cognitive science and hierarchical RL, but the specific application to complexity reduction in multimodal CoT—enforced via structured attention masks—is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in making long multimodal CoT trajectories computationally tractable: the demonstrated 11.26× token-interaction reduction at 10 reasoning steps and 2× faster convergence address a real bottleneck that has limited prior multimodal reasoning systems. The framework's generality (validated on both BAGEL and BLIP3o) suggests it could be a reusable training recipe for any unified multimodal model. However, the training dataset is small (~30K samples, predominantly synthetic), the evaluation is limited to image-level tasks, and the paper itself acknowledges that fine-grained visual consistency and broader real-world generalization remain open challenges. Scaling to video, longer-horizon tasks, and larger diverse datasets would be needed to confirm the approach's robustness.
