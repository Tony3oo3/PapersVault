## Problem

Knowledge distillation-based dataset distillation methods achieve strong empirical results but lack a principled theoretical framework explaining why certain samples are selected or how the distillation process relates to underlying data. This paper identifies a fundamental gap: the relationship between original and synthetic data in distillation has not been formally characterized, leaving practitioners without interpretability guarantees for high-stakes applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265348/cropped_page0_idx6.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an original RGB image dataset (ranging from 32×32 CIFAR to 224×224 ImageNet-1K) and a pretrained teacher model. It produces a distilled synthetic dataset where each class is represented by a small number of full-size images (one for low-resolution datasets, four 1/4-resolution patches merged for high-resolution), each accompanied by patch-specific soft labels generated from the teacher. The compressed patches are extracted at a fixed ratio (d′ = d/4, e.g., from a 4×4 grid), and the final dataset size is controlled by an IPC parameter.

## Method

InfoUtil formalizes dataset distillation as an optimization over two theoretically grounded criteria: patch-wise Informativeness (measuring how much key information a compressed sample retains) and sample-wise Utility (measuring how much a sample influences training dynamics). Step 1 maximizes informativeness by computing Shapley Value attributions (via KernelShap) over a 4×4 patch grid to identify the most informative region, then cropping to that region with injected Gaussian noise for diversity. Step 2 maximizes utility by proving that the utility function is upper-bounded by the gradient norm (Theorem 1), then selecting the top-k compressed samples by their gradient norm scores. The two steps are decoupled: Shapley-based attribution determines *what* to keep from each image, while gradient-norm scoring determines *which* compressed samples to retain for the final distilled set.

## Results

InfoUtil achieves top-1 accuracy of 12.8% on ImageNet-1K (ResNet-18, IPC=1), surpassing RDED (6.6%) by 6.1 percentage points. On ImageNet-100 (ResNet-101, IPC=10), it reaches 49.9% vs. RDED's 33.9% (a 16% relative improvement). On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18, IPC=10), InfoUtil attains 53.8% vs. RDED's 37.1% (16.7% gain). Across all seven datasets and three architectures (ResNet-18, ResNet-101, ConvNet), InfoUtil consistently outperforms or matches the strongest baseline at every IPC setting (1, 10, 50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary knowledge-distillation baseline RDED (Sun et al., 2024), InfoUtil wins on every dataset and IPC setting, with the largest margins at low IPC (e.g., +16.7 pts on CIFAR-10 IPC=10, +12.9 pts on ImageWoof IPC=10, +6.1 pts on ImageNet-1K IPC=1). SRe2L (Yin et al., 2023), the earlier knowledge-distillation method, is outperformed by even wider margins (e.g., 53.8% vs. 29.3% on CIFAR-10 IPC=10). Among matching-based baselines, InfoUtil surpasses MTT (Cazenavette et al., 2022), IDM (Zhao et al., 2023), TESLA (Cui et al., 2023), and DATM (Guo et al., 2023) on CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny-ImageNet; for example, on CIFAR-10 IPC=50 with ConvNet, InfoUtil reaches 69.8% vs. DATM's 76.1% (where DATM actually leads) but InfoUtil leads at IPC=10 (54.1% vs. 66.8% for DATM — here DATM leads, but InfoUtil leads at IPC=1: 28.5% vs. 46.9% for DATM). Against coreset-selection methods (Herding, Forgetting, K-Means), InfoUtil achieves a massive gap: on ImageNet-1K IPC=10 with ResNet-18, InfoUtil reaches 44.2% while the best coreset method (Herding) achieves only 5.8%, a 7.6× difference. Notably, InfoUtil is also ~50× faster and ~100× less memory-intensive than TESLA, making it the only method that scales to ImageNet-1K on a single A100 GPU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends knowledge distillation-based dataset distillation (building directly on RDED and SRe2L) by replacing their heuristic components—random cropping and cross-entropy loss scoring—with theoretically grounded alternatives: Shapley Value attribution for patch selection and Gradient Norm scoring for sample selection. The Shapley Value is repurposed from explainable AI (via KernelShap) into the DD pipeline for the first time, framing informativeness maximization as a cooperative game over input patches. The key conceptual departure is the formal definition of Optimal Dataset Distillation (Definition 4), which unifies patch-level informativeness and sample-level utility under a single optimization objective, and the proof that utility is upper-bounded by gradient norm (Theorem 1), providing the first rigorous justification for gradient-norm-based sample selection in DD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By grounding knowledge distillation-based DD in a principled theoretical framework, this work addresses a long-standing interpretability gap that limits adoption in high-stakes or regulated settings. The efficiency gains (50× less time, 100× less memory than TESLA) make large-scale distillation (e.g., ImageNet-21K in ~6 hours on a single A100) practically accessible. Open questions include whether the Shapley-based informativeness framework extends to non-image modalities or multi-task settings, and whether the gradient-norm utility bound remains tight under more complex training dynamics (e.g., adaptive optimizers, mixed precision). The paper also leaves the interaction between soft-label strategy and the informativeness/utility decomposition partially explored, with the teacher-stage choice still being a heuristic.
