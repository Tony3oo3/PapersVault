## Problem

Solving systems of PDEs with a single neural network (e.g., PINN) is unstable because multiple PDE residual terms create ill-conditioned loss landscapes with strongly imbalanced gradient norms, leading to convergence failures especially for turbulent or multi-scale systems. Existing workarounds (reweighting, resampling, domain decomposition) are not specifically designed for coupled PDE systems and do not exploit the physical structure of shared variables across equations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260345/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is fully unsupervised: it requires only the PDE system definition, initial conditions g(x), and boundary conditions b(t,x) — no simulation data or sensor readings. During training, all modules receive the same randomly sampled spatio-temporal coordinates (t,x) from the domain. At inference, CP uses only the minimum subset of modules needed to predict all physical variables (often a single module), reducing inference cost relative to the multi-module training phase. All L² losses are discretized as mean squared error over collocation points in the interior, boundary, and initial-time sets.

## Method

ComPhy assigns one dedicated learning module (PINN or NCL) to each PDE in the system, so each module optimizes a simpler single-equation problem rather than the full coupled system. The key mechanism is an end-to-end alignment loss between modules that share physical variables: the DERL variant aligns only the Jacobians (all partial derivatives) of the shared-variable predictions, while SOB additionally aligns the outputs themselves, effectively enforcing Sobolev W^{1,2} proximity. This derivative-based alignment acts as a distillation channel, transferring physical constraints (e.g., divergence-free structure) from one module to another without requiring the inference module to directly optimize that constraint. The total loss is a weighted sum of per-module PDE/IC/BC losses plus pairwise alignment terms, and gradient-based reweighting can be applied on top. Empirically, the modular decomposition produces more evenly scaled backpropagated gradients across layers compared to a monolithic PINN, which the authors identify as the primary reason for improved optimization.

## Results

On the Taylor-Green vortex, CP-PINN+NCL with DERL alignment and gradient reweighting achieves the best L² error of 2.795×10⁻⁵, outperforming NCL (2.830×10⁻⁵) and PINN (3.677×10⁻⁵). On the Acoustics equations (3 PDEs), CP-3xNCL with SOB+Grad achieves L² error of 2.727×10⁻⁵ and max error of 1.090×10⁻¹, roughly 3× better than PINN (8.016×10⁻⁵, 2.277×10⁻¹) and 2× better than NCL (5.243×10⁻⁵, 1.552×10⁻¹). On the 5-equation MHD system, CP-3xPINN (SOB) achieves L² error of 1.535×10⁻⁴, outperforming PINN (1.967×10⁻⁴) and NCL (1.975×10⁻⁴) by ~22%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CP consistently outperforms all four baselines (PINN, PINN+Grad, PINN+RAR, NCL) across all five experiments. Against PINN, CP achieves 24–25% lower L² error on Taylor-Green (2.795 vs 3.677×10⁻⁵) and 66% lower on Acoustics (2.727 vs 8.016×10⁻⁵). Against NCL, which has the built-in advantage of satisfying divergence-free constraints by design, CP-PINN+NCL (DERL+Grad) still edges out on Taylor-Green (2.795 vs 2.830×10⁻⁵) and CP-3xNCL (SOB+Grad) achieves 48% lower L² error on Acoustics (2.727 vs 5.243×10⁻⁵). On the real-world NS-Euler Gas system, CP-2xPINN (SOB) achieves the best L² error (1.296×10⁻³) and max error (3.082×10⁻²), beating NCL (1.690×10⁻³, 4.912×10⁻²) by 23% and 37% respectively. On the 5-equation MHD system, all CP configurations (2xPINN, 3xPINN, 4xPINN, 2xPINN+NCL) outperform both PINN and NCL, with CP-3xPINN (SOB) achieving the best L² error of 1.535×10⁻⁴ vs 1.967×10⁻⁴ for PINN. Notably, PINN+RAR performs catastrophically on Acoustics (120.6×10⁻⁵ L² error), while CP remains stable, highlighting the advantage of modular decomposition over single-model resampling strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

ComPhy builds directly on PINNs (Raissi et al., 2019) and Neural Conservation Laws (Richter-Powell et al., 2022) as its constituent learning modules, but departs from the standard practice of training a single network on all PDEs simultaneously. Prior work on systems of PDEs with PINNs simply sums residual losses for each equation within one model, while ad-hoc fixes (gradient reweighting, RAR resampling, domain decomposition) address optimization pathologies without changing the architecture. CP's key conceptual departure is the modular decomposition—one module per PDE—coupled with a derivative-based alignment loss (Sobolev or DERL) that transfers physical constraints between modules end-to-end, a mechanism the authors frame as the first specifically designed for coupled PDE systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical and well-motivated recipe for tackling systems of PDEs that are known to be difficult for monolithic PINNs, with consistent improvements across 2-, 3-, and 5-equation systems including real-world MHD. The gradient-scale analysis offers a mechanistic explanation (more balanced backpropagated gradients per layer) that goes beyond 'it works empirically,' giving practitioners confidence in when and why the modular approach helps. Open questions include whether alternating module-optimization and alignment steps could further improve convergence, whether higher-order derivative alignment captures richer dynamics, and how the framework extends to dynamic settings where modules are added or removed. A limitation is that the alignment coefficient λ_align, while robust at 1, can yield modest gains from task-specific tuning, and the approach still requires knowing the PDE structure a priori.
