## Problem

Existing GRPO-based reinforcement learning for flow matching models applies uniform optimization across all denoising timesteps and assigns credit uniformly from sparse terminal rewards, ignoring the fact that early high-noise steps have vastly greater exploration impact than late low-noise refinement steps. This temporal uniformity leads to inefficient exploration, suboptimal convergence, and wasted high-impact learning opportunities during the structurally critical early stages of generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated RGB image via a flow matching model (FLUX.1-dev at 1024×1024, SD3.5-M at 512×512 or 1024×1024, or Qwen-Image at 512×512). During training, each prompt is paired with N identical initial noise seeds, and at each timestep K stochastic branches are spawned, yielding a group of N×K images (e.g., 4×6=24) scored by an outcome-based reward model (PickScore, HPSv3, GenEval, or PaddleOCR). The reward signal is then attributed back to the specific branching timestep rather than spread uniformly across the trajectory.

## Method

TempFlow-GRPO introduces three mechanisms to make GRPO temporally aware. First, trajectory branching evolves the trajectory deterministically (ODE) until a designated timestep k, injects a single SDE perturbation there, then continues deterministically to the final image—this localizes all reward variance to step k, enabling precise credit assignment to intermediate actions without training a process reward model. Second, noise-aware policy weighting multiplies the per-timestep policy loss by a factor proportional to the local noise magnitude σ_t√Δt; the paper proves via policy gradient analysis that without this reweighting the natural gradient scale term is √(Δk(1−k)/k) (inversely proportional to noise, causing late steps to dominate), while with reweighting it simplifies to Δk, equalizing gradient contributions across timesteps when flow shift equals 1. Third, a seed group strategy groups trajectories sharing the same initial noise so that reward differences within a group are attributable solely to branching exploration rather than random initialization, further sharpening the advantage signal.

## Results

On the GenEval compositional generation benchmark, TempFlow-GRPO achieves an overall score of 0.97 at 3,800 training steps, substantially outperforming Flow-GRPO (0.88 at the same step count) and the base SD3.5-M model (0.63). On the PickScore human preference benchmark, TempFlow-GRPO surpasses Flow-GRPO by approximately 1.7% and the improved Flow-GRPO (Prompt) baseline by ~1.0%, requiring only 100–200 steps to match Flow-GRPO's performance. On FLUX.1-dev with HPSv3 reward, the method reaches Flow-GRPO's 300-step performance in just 80 steps (3.75× speedup) while maintaining lower and more stable KL divergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Flow-GRPO, TempFlow-GRPO wins decisively across all evaluated benchmarks: on GenEval it reaches 0.97 at 3,800 steps versus Flow-GRPO's 0.88 at the same budget (and Flow-GRPO needs 5,600 steps to reach 0.95); on PickScore it gains ~1.7% over Flow-GRPO and ~1.0% over the improved Flow-GRPO (Prompt) variant; and on FLUX.1-dev with HPSv3 it matches Flow-GRPO's 300-step performance in only 80 steps. Against DanceGRPO (a concurrent GRPO method for visual generation), TempFlow-GRPO achieves 38.5 on HPDv2 with HPSv2 at 280 iterations versus DanceGRPO's 37.2 at 300 iterations (a 1.3% absolute gain), and matches DanceGRPO's final performance in half the iterations (150 vs. 300), a 2× convergence speedup. The paper also notes that TempFlow-GRPO maintains lower and more stable KL divergence throughout training compared to both baselines, indicating better preservation of the original model distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Flow-GRPO (Liu et al., 2025), which first brought online GRPO-style RL to flow matching models, by identifying temporal uniformity—the equal treatment of all denoising timesteps—as the core limitation. It departs from the process reward model literature (SPO, PRIME, MathShepherd) by showing that precise intermediate credit assignment can be achieved through trajectory branching (concentrating SDE stochasticity at a single timestep) without training any step-level evaluator. The GRPO objective and group-level advantage normalization are inherited from DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024), while the ODE-to-SDE conversion and closed-form KL penalty follow Flow-GRPO's formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The trajectory branching mechanism offers a practical, zero-overhead alternative to process reward models for flow-based generation, which is significant because scoring semantically ambiguous intermediate noisy states has been a persistent bottleneck. The noise-aware reweighting, grounded in a policy-gradient scale-term analysis, provides a principled (rather than heuristic) way to allocate optimization effort across timesteps, and the 3.75× convergence speedup across SD3.5-M, FLUX.1-dev, and Qwen-Image suggests broad adoptability. Open questions remain around reward hacking (acknowledged by the authors), the interaction with more powerful multimodal reward models, and whether the temporal-structure insight transfers to other iterative generative paradigms beyond flow matching.
