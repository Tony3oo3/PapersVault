## Problem

Realistic humanoid character animation traditionally requires expensive motion capture and skilled artists, while existing text-to-motion methods are constrained by the limited diversity and generalization of small 4D motion capture datasets. This paper asks whether the strong, diverse motion priors implicitly learned by large-scale video diffusion models can be repurposed to animate arbitrary 3D humanoid meshes from text prompts, without any fine-tuning or paired motion data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a static, untextured 3D humanoid mesh (no skeleton or rigging, in an arbitrary pose) and a natural-language text prompt describing the desired action. The mesh is normalized to unit scale and centered at the origin before rendering. The output is a temporally coherent sequence of SMPL deformation parameters (pose, translation, rotation, and per-vertex offsets) that animate the input mesh over F frames, effectively producing a 4D animated mesh. The underlying video is generated at 768×1280 and downsampled to 384×640 for optimization.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages: video generation and video-to-mesh tracking. First, the input mesh is rendered and fed as the first-frame condition to a text-to-video diffusion model (Kling AI) along with the text prompt, producing a synthetic video of the mesh performing the described motion. Second, the SMPL body model is registered to the input mesh via multi-view landmark triangulation, and each mesh vertex is reparameterized in barycentric coordinates relative to its nearest SMPL face, creating a differentiable deformation proxy. Motion is then recovered by optimizing SMPL parameters (pose in VPoser latent space, translation, rotation, per-vertex offsets) to match three tracking cues extracted from the video: MediaPipe 2D body landmarks (with German-McClure loss), rendered-vs-extracted silhouettes (binary cross-entropy), and dense DINOv2 features (cosine similarity). Temporal smoothness is enforced via shallow MLPs with sinusoidal positional encoding that parameterize per-frame pose, translation, and rotation, supplemented by temporal, extreme-bending, VPoser, and ARAP regularization terms.

## Results

On the CAPE dataset (26 sequences, 7,000 frames of untextured mesh renderings), the proposed method achieves the best performance across all three tracking metrics: MPJPE of 0.036, PVE of 0.041, and acceleration error of 1.49, outperforming the strongest baseline Multi-HMR (MPJPE 0.045, Accel 9.03) and optimization-based SMPLIFY-X (MPJPE 0.054, Accel 20.57). On the Mixamo dataset (50 rigged character animations), the method achieves a PVE of 0.058, beating WHAM (0.066) and SMPLIFY-X (0.099). In a 30-participant perceptual study against five text-to-motion baselines, the method is preferred across all questions (prompt alignment, naturalness, overall preference) and scores higher on Likert-scale realism and prompt alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all quantitative baselines on the CAPE tracking benchmark. Against the optimization-based SMPLIFY-X (Pavlakos et al., 2019), it reduces MPJPE from 0.054 to 0.036 (33% improvement) and acceleration error from 20.57 to 1.49, with the smoothed variant SMPLIFY-X* still trailing on all three metrics. The learning-based baselines WHAM (Shin et al., 2024) and Multi-HMR (Baradel et al., 2024) degrade on untextured synthetic videos since they were not trained for this setting; the proposed method's optimization-based tracking with body priors handles this regime more robustly. On Mixamo, the method achieves a 12% PVE reduction over WHAM (0.058 vs. 0.066) and 41% over SMPLIFY-X (0.058 vs. 0.099). In the perceptual study, the method is preferred over all five text-to-motion baselines—MDM (Tevet et al., 2023), FineMoGen (Zhang et al., 2023c), MotionDiffuse (Zhang et al., 2024b), ReMoDiffuse (Zhang et al., 2023b), and MotionCLIP (Tevet et al., 2022)—on prompt alignment, motion naturalness, and overall preference, with higher Likert scores for realism and prompt adherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes large-scale video diffusion models (VDMs) as implicit motion priors for text-to-motion generation, departing from the dominant paradigm of training dedicated motion diffusion models (MDM, MotionDiffuse, FineMoGen) on limited MoCap data. The closest prior work, MotionDreamer (Uzolas et al., 2025), also extracts motion from VDMs but relies on intermediate diffusion activations and feature matching at low resolution; this paper instead uses a SMPL deformation proxy with barycentric coordinate reparameterization and combines sparse (MediaPipe landmarks), silhouette, and dense (DINOv2) tracking signals. Unlike AnyMoLe, which requires fine-tuning the video model and training a scene-specific estimator, this method is fully zero-shot with respect to the VDM and requires no fine-tuning of any component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach offers a practical, cost-effective path to generating diverse 4D humanoid animations without expensive motion capture or dedicated motion model training, and is agnostic to the choice of VDM (demonstrated with Kling AI, CogVideoX, and Wan2.2). Key limitations remain: VDM artifacts (morphing, identity drift) directly propagate into tracking failures, and monocular tracking is inherently underconstrained. Open questions include whether integrating depth predictors or multi-view diffusion models can resolve ambiguities, and whether the generated 4D sequences can serve as training data to bootstrap dedicated motion models, potentially closing the data-scarcity loop that motivates the paper.
