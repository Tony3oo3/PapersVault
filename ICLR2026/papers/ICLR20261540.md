## Problem

Multimodal joint learning suffers from modality competition: dominant modalities overfit early, saturating the shared prediction objective and leaving weaker modalities undertrained. Existing remedies (gradient modulation, alternating unimodal/joint losses, Pareto balancing) require extra hyperparameters or structural modifications to reconcile conflicting objectives. No prior work has shown, from an information-theoretic standpoint, that a single loss can simultaneously capture joint modality-label dependencies, unimodal label dependencies, and cross-modal alignment without additional tuning.

## Input / Output

The method accepts heterogeneous multimodal inputs—audio spectrograms (129×862) or fbank features, 3–4 visual frames sampled at 1 fps, RGB frames plus 10 optical-flow frames, or CLIP-encoded image/text pairs—and produces a class-label probability vector via a shared prediction head. The prediction head f_θ(z^(1),…,z^(M))_y plays a dual role: it is both the classifier and the total-correlation estimator, so no separate estimator network is needed. In the regression extension (CMU-MOSI/MOSEI), the head additionally outputs a predicted continuous sentiment value and a confidence term, with the loss decomposing into an MSE component plus a cross-modal alignment term.

## Method

The paper extends Mutual Information Neural Estimation (MINE) to Total Correlation Neural Estimation (TCNE) via the Donsker-Varadhan dual representation, yielding a lower bound on TC(z^(1),…,z^(M), y) parameterized by the prediction head itself. Minimizing the resulting TCMax loss, L = −E_{joint}[F_Θ] + log E_{product}[e^{F_Θ}], simultaneously maximizes joint modality-label mutual information, unimodal label mutual information, and inter-modal alignment (I(z^(a);z^(v)|y)), as shown by the TC decomposition in Eq. 6. The approach is hyperparameter-free: one simply replaces cross-entropy with TCMax during training, and at inference the model output is unchanged (Proposition 3 guarantees the Gibbs distribution recovers the true conditional p(y|x)). Computationally, the |B|^M forward passes required by the denominator are reduced via negative-pair sampling, and for linear fusion heads the denominator decouples into per-modality sums, eliminating extra overhead entirely.

## Results

TCMax achieves the best multi-modal test accuracy across all five primary audio-visual/action datasets: 82.8% on CREMA-D, 63.5% on Kinetics-Sounds, 64.5% on AVE, 47.6% on VGGSound, and 56.0% on UCF101 (using Share Head fusion). On the MVSA sentiment dataset with frozen CLIP encoders, TCMax reaches 84.05% (ViT-B/32) and 81.75% (RN50), outperforming joint learning (82.83% and 81.23%) and unimodal ensemble (81.77% and 80.02%). On regression (CMU-MOSI/MOSEI), TCMax improves binary accuracy over the MAG-BERT baseline by ~0.9% and ~0.5% respectively. TCMax also achieves the lowest Jensen-Shannon divergence between single-modality predictions on every dataset, confirming stronger cross-modal alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TCMax outperforms all nine compared baselines on multi-modal accuracy across every dataset. Against gradient-modulation methods (OGM-GE, AGM), TCMax gains 7.6–12.3 points on CREMA-D and 6.1–8.1 points on Kinetics-Sounds, confirming that explicit gradient balancing is insufficient to resolve modality competition. Against unimodal-assistance methods (QMF, MLA, MMPareto, OPM), TCMax still leads by 0.1–1.4 points on most datasets, with the closest margin being UCF101 where MMPareto reaches 55.9% vs. TCMax's 56.0%. On the MVSA regression-adjacent task with frozen CLIP encoders, TCMax (84.05% ViT-B/32) surpasses joint learning (82.83%) by 1.22 points with a non-overlapping 95% confidence interval, while single-modality CLIP scores remain comparable, indicating the gain comes from cross-modal synergy. On the CMU-MOSI/MOSEI regression task, TCMax improves binary accuracy over MAG-BERT by 0.91 and 0.47 points respectively, with corresponding F1 and correlation gains, suggesting the total-correlation objective transfers beyond classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Mutual Information Neural Estimation (MINE) from pairwise mutual information to a multivariate Total Correlation Neural Estimation (TCNE), leveraging the Donsker-Varadhan representation of KL divergence. It departs from the dominant line of balanced multimodal learning (OGM-GE, AGM, QMF, MLA, MMPareto), which either modulates gradients or explicitly combines joint and unimodal losses with additional hyperparameters, by showing that a single information-theoretic objective—maximizing total correlation between all modality features and the label—simultaneously subsumes joint learning, unimodal learning, and cross-modal alignment. The key conceptual move is recognizing that the standard prediction head in a multimodal model naturally parameterizes the TC estimator, requiring no architectural changes or extra loss terms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hyperparameter-free, single-loss formulation offers a clean theoretical alternative to the ad-hoc combination of joint, unimodal, and contrastive losses that has characterized recent multimodal learning work, potentially simplifying training pipelines. However, the method is currently restricted to classification tasks, and extending it to detection or generative settings would require explicit input-output distribution definitions. Open questions include whether the TCMax objective generalizes to more than two modalities without prohibitive computational cost, and whether the theoretical guarantee (Gibbs distribution recovery) is achievable in practice with finite-capacity networks.
