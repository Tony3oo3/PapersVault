## Problem

Humanoid agents struggle to handle flexible, diverse interactions in open physical environments, and the standard remedy—collecting massive human-scene interaction data to train a highly capable model—is prohibitively expensive due to the structural complexity of humanoid bodies and the vast diversity of the physical world. The paper asks whether off-the-shelf VLMs (e.g., GPT-4o) can be directly leveraged to control humanoid agents, bypassing costly data collection and task-specific finetuning while retaining open-world generalization.

## Input / Output

The system takes a high-level natural language instruction (e.g., 'have a rest'), multi-view scene images with object labels, the agent's current pose, and physical feedback from the environment (the actual executed motion, which may deviate from the generated trajectory due to collisions or external forces). The compiler outputs a structured command C = {motion caption, 2D location, facing angle, key-joint 3D targets}, and the executor produces full-body joint trajectories at 20 FPS in a 263-dimensional relative-coordinate representation, supporting arbitrary-length (unbounded) synthesis and real-time interactive control above 20 Hz.

## Method

BiBo comprises two components. The embodied instruction compiler drives an off-the-shelf VLM through a three-stage visual QA process: (1) basic attribute analysis (caption, key joints, target object) with majority voting across five parallel VLM instances, (2) agent pose reasoning via visual label selection around an anchor object (avoiding direct coordinate regression, which VLMs handle poorly), and (3) key joint target generation using an 8×8 grid of labels over the anchor object plus predefined relative directions. The motion executor is a Latent Diffusion Model with a novel joint-decoding scheme: the VAE encodes both the previously generated motion M_g and the actual executed motion M_a into latents; the diffusion extends future latents from the executed-motion latent S_a (capturing physical feedback), while the VAE decoder jointly decodes [S_g : S_f] using causal self-cross attention so that each token attends only to preceding tokens, guaranteeing continuity between the reconstructed previous motion and the newly generated future motion. This resolves the discontinuity introduced by prior methods that extend only from executed motion, while still adapting to environmental perturbations. An IK post-optimization step and a reinforcement-learned tracking policy then drive the physical humanoid to follow the generated trajectories.

## Results

BiBo achieves an average single-interaction task success rate of 90.2% and a composite task success rate of 41.0% on randomly generated physical scenes, improving by 12.5% and 29.1% over prior methods respectively. On the HumanML3D text-to-motion benchmark, BiBo attains the best R-Precision across all k values (0.542/0.738/0.829 for @1/@2/@3) and a FID of 0.076, while supporting real-time arbitrary-length generation at >20 Hz (AITS = 0.047 s). In terms of control precision, BiBo reduces MAE by 16.3% over prior methods (e.g., 0.0310 for head vs. 0.0663 for DiP). Online planning with GPT-4o achieves success rates within 4.38% of using a ground-truth action plan.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiBo outperforms all four HSI baselines (UniHSI, HumanVLA, TokenHSI, CLoSD) across every single-interaction task, with the largest margins on Sleep (94.89% vs. 34.67% for CLoSD) and Touch (86.05% vs. 42.55% for CLoSD). On composite tasks, where prior methods are weakest, BiBo leads CLoSD by 32.35 points on Simple, 29.49 on Medium, and 25.40 on Hard. Among text-to-motion baselines on HumanML3D, BiBo surpasses MDM, MotionStreamer, MoGenTS, MoConVQ, DiP, and CLoSD on R-Precision (text alignment) while matching MotionLCM's FID (0.076 vs. 0.072) at comparable inference speed (0.047 s vs. 0.042 s AITS). BiBo also achieves the best control precision (MAE) against DiP and MotionLCM, reducing head error from 0.0663 to 0.0310 and hand error from 0.0830 to 0.0571. In the user study, BiBo's generated motions are preferred over both MotionLCM (77 vs. 53 votes) and DiP/CLoSD (77 vs. 20 votes) by 30 participants.

## Relation to Prior Work

BiBo builds on the latent diffusion model framework for motion generation (Chen et al., 2023) and the simulation-diffusion loop concept from CLoSD (Tevet et al., 2024), but departs from both in key ways. Rather than training a Vision-Language-Action model on massive interaction data (as in HumanVLA, TokenHSI, or UniHSI), it repurposes an off-the-shelf VLM (GPT-4o) as an 'embodied instruction compiler' that translates natural language into structured motion commands via a three-stage visual QA process. The motion executor introduces a novel LDM application where the VAE jointly decodes latents from both the previously generated and the actual executed motion, resolving the continuity-vs-adaptability tradeoff that plagues prior approaches like CLoSD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a general-purpose VLM without task-specific fine-tuning can serve as the planning 'brain' for a physical humanoid, achieving 90.2% single-interaction success in randomly generated scenes — a result that challenges the prevailing data-centric scaling paradigm for embodied agents. The LDM-based joint decoding mechanism for incorporating physical feedback while preserving motion continuity is a reusable technique applicable beyond humanoids. Key limitations include reliance on simulation (IsaacGym) rather than real hardware, dependence on a single powerful VLM (GPT-4o), a significant performance drop on composite tasks (41.0%), and the absence of explicit environmental geometry modeling — all pointing toward open questions about real-world transfer, VLM robustness, and scaling to multi-agent or hand-object interactions.
