## Problem

Existing robust learning methods built on Group DRO handle inter-group distribution shifts (changes in group proportions) but assume each group's training distribution faithfully represents its true distribution. This assumption breaks down for minority groups with limited samples, where intra-group distributional shifts naturally arise from underrepresentation. The paper identifies this intra-group vulnerability as a previously overlooked failure mode where state-of-the-art robust methods consistently collapse.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (e.g., 28×28 CMNIST digits, 224×224 Waterbirds/CelebA images) paired with known group labels G=(Y,A), where Y is the target class and A is a spurious attribute (background, gender, color). It produces a class label via a ResNet-50 classifier. Crucially, the robustness mechanism operates not in pixel space but in the penultimate-layer feature space z(x) of the network, where adversarial perturbations are constrained to an ε_g-ball around each sample's semantic embedding, with ε_g = ε/√n_g scaled inversely by group size so that smaller minority groups receive larger perturbation radii.

## Method

The paper proposes a hierarchical ambiguity set for DRO that nests two levels of uncertainty: (1) inter-group, where mixing proportions β range freely over the simplex (ρ=∞), and (2) intra-group, where each group's distribution Q_g is constrained to a W∞-ball of radius ε_g around its empirical distribution P_g in a latent semantic space. The W∞ cost is defined on penultimate-layer features z(x) with infinite cost across different labels, ensuring perturbations preserve class identity. Theorem 4.1 reformulates the resulting DRO as a tractable surrogate: a worst-group-weighted adversarial perturbation in latent space, solved by coordinate-wise updates (projected gradient ascent for z', exponentiated gradient for β, SGD for θ) with an O(1/√T) convergence guarantee under convexity. The key design choice over standard Wasserstein DRO is the group-specific radius ε_g = ε/√n_g, which allocates more robustness budget to statistically less reliable minority groups, and the hierarchical structure that simultaneously models mixture-level and conditional-level uncertainty.

## Results

On the novel shifted-distribution benchmarks (Table 1), the proposed hierarchical DRO achieves the best worst-group accuracy across all three datasets: 71.8% on Shifted CMNIST, 93.7% on Shifted Waterbirds, and 72.1% on Shifted CelebA, outperforming the strongest baseline (Group DRO) by 5.9, 2.0, and 12.3 points respectively. On the original unshifted benchmarks (Table 2), the method also achieves top-tier worst-group accuracy (73.6% CMNIST, 90.8% Waterbirds, 90.4% CelebA), matching or slightly exceeding Group DRO and PDE. The largest gains appear on CelebA under shift, where PDE drops by ~34.7 points while the proposed method retains 72.1% worst-group accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Group DRO (Sagawa et al., 2020), the proposed method wins on all three shifted datasets (by 5.9, 2.0, and 12.3 points in worst-group accuracy) and ties or slightly leads on the original benchmarks (e.g., 73.6 vs. 73.1 on CMNIST). LISA (Yao et al., 2022) and DFR (Kirichenko et al., 2023) degrade severely under intra-group shifts—LISA drops to 42.9% on Shifted CMNIST and DFR to 28.0%—while the proposed method remains stable. PDE (Deng et al., 2024) is the closest competitor on original CelebA (91.0 vs. 90.4 worst-group) but collapses to 56.3% under the minority-group shift, a 34.7-point drop. Among the group-inference baselines evaluated only on original distributions (Table 2), JTT (67.3% CMNIST), CnC (88.5% Waterbirds), GIC (72.2% CMNIST), and SSA (71.1% CMNIST) all trail the proposed method by 1–6 points in worst-group accuracy, confirming that explicit group labels combined with intra-group robustness yield the strongest performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Group DRO (Sagawa et al., 2020) by replacing its flat ambiguity set—convex combinations of fixed group distributions—with a hierarchical set that additionally allows each group's conditional distribution to vary within a Wasserstein ball. It departs from standard Wasserstein DRO (Mohajerin Esfahani & Kuhn, 2018) in two key ways: the group proportions are left unrestricted (ρ=∞) rather than constrained to a small neighborhood, and each group receives its own perturbation radius scaled by 1/√n_g to reflect statistical uncertainty. The paper positions itself against a broad family of spurious-correlation methods (JTT, SSA, LISA, DFR, PDE, GIC) that all implicitly assume each group's training distribution faithfully represents its test-time distribution—an assumption the authors argue breaks down for minority groups with limited samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a practically important and previously unaddressed failure mode: even when group labels are known and inter-group robustness is handled, intra-group distributional shifts in small minority groups can cause catastrophic performance drops in all existing robust methods. This matters for real-world deployments where underrepresented subpopulations are inherently noisy and their training samples may not represent test-time variability. Limitations include the requirement for explicit group labels, a convergence guarantee only under convexity with fixed features, and evaluation restricted to three image benchmarks with somewhat constructed shifts (rotation, species restriction, glasses). Extending the framework to settings without group annotations or to non-vision domains (e.g., text) remains open.
