## Problem

Existing model merging methods predominantly focus on designing conflict-reduction strategies at the merging stage while largely overlooking the fine-tuning stage, which determines whether expert models are inherently compatible for merging. The few fine-tuning-stage approaches that do exist (tangent-space fine-tuning, SAM-based SAFT-Merge) incur substantial training or inference overhead (2–3×), leaving a gap for an efficient fine-tuning scheme that produces merge-robust expert models without extra cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained base model θ₀, a single task's training set D_k, and a small set of merging hyperparameters (discrete coefficient set A = [0.1,…,0.6], maximum merge count K_max = 7, and Laplace parameters μ = 0, b = 0.0005) as input. It produces a standard (non-linearized) fine-tuned expert model θ_k that is robust to subsequent merging, with no change to inference cost. Crucially, the method does not require access to other experts' task vectors or parameters during training, making it applicable in the realistic setting where experts are trained independently by different parties.

## Method

MergOPT reformulates the task-arithmetic merge formula θ_merged = θ₀ + α Σⱼ Δθⱼ as θ_k + ζ(α, K, Δθ), where ζ is a merge-induced parameter offset, and casts fine-tuning as a weight-space distributionally robust optimization problem: min_θk sup_(α,K,Δθ)∈B E[ℓ_k(φ(θ_k, ζ))]. Since other experts' task vectors are inaccessible, the paper empirically validates (via K-S tests and log-likelihood across three LLM architectures) that individual task vectors are well-approximated by a Laplace distribution, enabling a single-step sampling approximation: at each training step, z ~ Laplace(μ, b), α ~ Uniform(A), K ~ Uniform({1,…,K_max}) are drawn, the perturbed parameters θ_k' = θ_k + (Kα − 1)z are constructed, and the task loss gradient at θ_k' updates θ_k. This avoids the iterative projected-gradient inner maximization of the full min-max formulation, reducing overhead to only 1.17× standard AdamW fine-tuning (vs. 2.04× for SAM-based methods). The approach is both optimizer-agnostic (validated with AdamW and SGD) and merging-method-agnostic (validated with Weight Averaging, Task Arithmetic, TIES-Merging, and DARE).

## Results

On Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct merging seven expert models, MergOPT yields average relative gains of 3.28% (Weight Averaging: 0.4123 vs. 0.3992), 2.71% (Task Arithmetic: 0.4165 vs. 0.4055), 1.68% (TIES-Merging: 0.4123 vs. 0.4055), and 9.54% (DARE: 0.4147 vs. 0.3786) over standard fine-tuning. On the larger Llama-3.2-3B, gains range from +0.64% to +4.09% across the same four strategies. The method also transfers to Qwen2.5-1.5B (up to +5.51%), Llama-3.1-8B (up to +1.40%), and CLIP-ViT-B/32 on 10 vision tasks (up to +4.10%), with an overall average relative improvement of ~3.5% and a maximum of 9.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four standard merging strategies (Weight Averaging, Task Arithmetic, TIES-Merging, DARE), MergOPT consistently wins on all four when applied to Llama-3.2-1B, with the largest gain on DARE (+9.54%) and the smallest on TIES-Merging (+1.68%). Compared to SAFT-Merge (SAM/ASAM), MergOPT wins on Weight Averaging (+2.59%) and DARE (+5.20%) under equal epochs but trails on Task Arithmetic (−2.23%) and TIES-Merging (−1.29%); however, under comparable training time (1.04× vs. 1.17× AdamW), MergOPT outperforms SAFT-Merge on all four strategies (+4.08%, +0.70%, +0.95%, +3.99%) while incurring only 1.17× overhead versus 2.04× for SAM. Against tangent-space fine-tuning on CLIP-ViT-B/32, MergOPT surpasses it on all four merging methods (e.g., +2.37% on Weight Averaging where tangent-space actually degrades by −8.01%), and also beats WUDI-Merging by +1.32% (84.3 vs. 83.2 average Top-1 accuracy across 10 vision tasks). Overall, MergOPT is the only method that improves over standard fine-tuning on every merging strategy tested, without requiring 2× training cost or 2–3× inference overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model merging literature by shifting focus from the merging stage to the fine-tuning stage, building on the task arithmetic formulation (Ilharco et al., 2023) as the algebraic foundation for merge-induced parameter offsets. It adapts the distributionally robust optimization (DRO) framework (Lin et al., 2022) from data-space to weight-space, treating other experts' task vectors as adversarial perturbations during single-task fine-tuning. Unlike SAFT-Merge (Lee et al., 2025), which uses SAM/ASAM to seek flatter minima at 2× training cost, and tangent-space fine-tuning (Ortiz-Jimenez et al., 2023), which incurs 2–3× inference overhead, MergOPT achieves comparable or better robustness with only 1.17× overhead via a single-step Laplace-sampling approximation of the worst-case merge offset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: most merging methods assume standard fine-tuned experts, yet the fine-tuning process itself shapes how well models merge. MergOPT's merging-strategy-agnostic design (works with Weight Averaging, Task Arithmetic, TIES, DARE) and minimal overhead make it a drop-in improvement for practitioners. Open questions include whether the Laplace approximation (K-S distance ~0.10) holds for larger models or more diverse task distributions, whether the single-step sampling captures truly adversarial offsets at scale, and whether gains persist beyond the 1B–8B parameter range tested here. The method also leaves open the question of how to jointly optimize fine-tuning and merging coefficients rather than treating them sequentially.
