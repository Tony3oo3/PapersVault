## Problem

Pre-training foundation models for RL remains difficult because actions have long-term dependencies and datasets are typically collected by heterogeneous users performing distinct tasks, yet existing pre-training methods (behavioral cloning, transition models, skill learning) largely ignore both temporal reasoning and user intentions. This paper addresses the gap by asking: can we build a generative model that simultaneously captures long-horizon future state distributions and the latent intentions of data collectors, enabling effective downstream adaptation?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263966/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Pre-training consumes an unlabeled, reward-free offline dataset of consecutive transitions (s, a, s', a') collected by a mixture of behavioral policies, where states are either low-dimensional state vectors or RGB images. Fine-tuning additionally requires a small reward-labeled dataset (s, a, r) for the target task. The method produces a continuous latent intention vector z inferred from the *next* transition (s', a') via a variational encoder, a flow-matching vector field that generates future state occupancy distributions via ODE integration, and a final policy parameterized as a Gaussian action distribution. Notably, the Q-function during fine-tuning is estimated by sampling N=16 future states from the flow model and averaging a learned reward predictor over them, rather than by direct regression.

## Method

InFOM is a latent variable model that couples a variational intention encoder with a flow-matching occupancy model trained via temporal difference learning. During pre-training, the encoder p_e(z|s',a') infers a continuous latent intention from consecutive transitions by maximizing an ELBO (with a Gaussian prior), while the occupancy model q_d(s_f|s,a,z) is trained with a SARSA flow matching loss that incorporates the Bellman backup into the conditional flow objective, enabling bootstrapped long-horizon prediction. During fine-tuning, intention-conditioned Q-values are estimated by Monte Carlo sampling from the flow model, then distilled into a single scalar Q-function using an upper expectile loss (replacing the unstable max over intentions), and a policy is extracted by conservatively maximizing Q with behavioral cloning regularization. The key architectural choices—continuous latent intentions (vs. discrete skills or fixed forward-backward representations) and the expectile-based implicit GPI—enable generalization over the infinite intention space while avoiding gradient backpropagation through the ODE solver.

## Results

On 36 state-based and 4 image-based tasks across ExORL and OGBench, InFOM achieves a 1.8× median improvement in returns and a 36% improvement in success rates over the best baseline. On the challenging OGBench state-based manipulation tasks, InFOM achieves 36% higher success rate than the best baseline, and on the 4 image-based (RGB) tasks it outperforms the best baseline by 31%. On ExORL, InFOM matches baselines on the easier cheetah and quadruped domains but obtains a 20× improvement on the high-dimensional jaco task where baselines make only trivial progress.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InFOM is compared against 8 baselines spanning five pre-training paradigms: behavioral cloning (IQL, ReBRAC), transition models (MBPO), self-supervised representations (DINOv2), discriminative occupancy classifiers (C-learning, CDPC), and latent skill learning (VISC, HiP). InFOM matches or surpasses all baselines on 6 of 8 domains. On the easier ExORL locomotion tasks (cheetah, quadruped), all methods perform similarly, but on the high-dimensional jaco manipulation task InFOM achieves a 20× return improvement where baselines make only trivial progress, likely due to the sparse reward and high-dimensional state space. On the more challenging OGBench state-based manipulation tasks, InFOM achieves a 36% higher success rate than the best baseline, and on the 4 RGB image-based tasks it outperforms the best baseline by 31%. The paper attributes the baselines' failures on harder tasks to semi-sparse reward functions, whereas InFOM can explore different state-space regions via its latent intentions. On real-robotics datasets (Appendix F.1), InFOM shows a 34% improvement over selective baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

InFOM synthesizes three established threads: (1) temporal-difference flow matching for occupancy measures (Farebrother et al., 2025), (2) variational latent variable models for inferring unobserved structure (Kingma & Welling, 2013), and (3) generalized policy improvement over successor representations (Barreto et al., 2017). The key departure from the closest prior work (Farebrother et al., 2025) is replacing fixed forward-backward representations with a learned variational intention encoder and replacing finite-set greedy GPI with an implicit expectile-based GPI, which avoids backpropagating through the ODE solver. Relative to behavioral-cloning pre-training (Octo, RT-X) and world-model approaches, InFOM directly models the discounted occupancy measure conditioned on inferred intentions, sidestepping both the action-prediction bottleneck and compounding rollout errors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that jointly modeling temporal structure (via flow-matched occupancy measures) and user intention (via a variational encoder) yields substantially better pre-training than either component alone, with 1.8× median return improvement and 36% higher success rates across 40 tasks. A key open question is whether the reduction from full trajectories to consecutive state-action pairs (the consistency assumption) generalizes to tasks where intentions shift mid-trajectory or where the behavioral policy is highly non-stationary. The method also raises the broader question of whether generative occupancy models can serve as a universal pre-training objective for RL, analogous to how masked language modeling works for NLP, and whether the implicit GPI framework can be extended to continuous action spaces or multi-agent settings.
