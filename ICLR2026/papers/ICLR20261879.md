## Problem

Neural networks implicitly develop class-specific circuits, but these representations are entangled through superposition, making it difficult to isolate, reuse, or compose them as independent functional modules. The paper asks whether one can train sparse subnetworks that specialize in a designated class subset while remaining deliberately uncertain on all others, and whether such modules can be merged into accurate generalist models without fine-tuning or alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261879/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each submodule is trained on standard classification inputs (28×28 grayscale images, 32×32 RGB images, 561-dim sensor features, 8-dim protein features, or text documents) paired with a designated rewarded class subset R. The training simulates a partially-labeled scenario: the model sees all samples but only classes in R receive one-hot targets, while all other classes receive a uniform target distribution. The output is a sparse subnetwork producing peaked probabilities on R and near-uniform probabilities elsewhere, which can be composed via weight summation or logit averaging into a generalist classifier over the union of all rewarded classes.

## Method

The core contribution is a KL-divergence-based Maximum Entropy (ME) loss that trains each submodule to output a peaked distribution on its rewarded classes and a fully uniform distribution (across all |C| classes, not just non-rewarded ones) on everything else. This full-uniformity design enforces neuron-permutation invariance on non-rewarded inputs, avoiding the semantic collisions that arise in one-vs-all formulations when weights are summed. An Iterative Magnitude Pruning (IMP) schedule (two pruning rounds with weight reset to initialization, followed by final training) removes spurious capacity and reinforces functional isolation, leveraging the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis. For composition, two strategies are provided: weight summation (validated via mode-connectivity analysis showing low-loss paths between modules for simpler architectures) and logit averaging, which has a product-of-experts interpretation where non-rewarded experts contribute only a class-independent factor that cancels after softmax normalization. The framework is validated across MLPs, CNNs, ResNet-18, and VGG-11 on image, tabular, and text benchmarks.

## Results

On MNIST with a Shallow MLP (|R|=1, IMP), the ME loss achieves 99.2% rewarded accuracy via logit merging and 99.1% via weight merging, with non-rewarded entropy at 2.30 (theoretical max log(10)≈2.30), confirming near-perfect specialization. Across all architectures and datasets, ME consistently outperforms both QME and standard CrossEntropy, with the largest gaps on XE (e.g., 99.2% vs. 79.8% on MNIST, |R|=1, Shallow MLP). Logit-space merging generally outperforms weight-space merging, especially on complex datasets like CIFAR-10 where weight merging degrades substantially. In the complete merge stress test on CIFAR-100 (100 modules), logit merging degrades gracefully without a sudden compositionality bottleneck.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed Maximum-Entropy (ME) loss against three baselines: standard CrossEntropy (XE) trained only on rewarded classes, Quasi-MaxEnt (QME) which enforces uniformity only on non-rewarded classes, and XE with label-smoothing or confidence-penalty (reported in appendix). ME wins in virtually every setting: on MNIST with |R|=1 and a Shallow MLP, ME achieves 99.2% (logit) vs. 98.4% (QME) and 79.8% (XE); on FMNIST the gap is 98.3% vs. 96.2% vs. 85.9%. The ME vs. QME gap, though smaller, is consistent and attributed to QME's leakage of information on non-rewarded classes, which violates the MaxEnt principle. Between the two merging strategies, logit-space merging outperforms weight-space merging in nearly all cases (the sole exception being CNN with |R|=2 and XE), and is especially critical on CIFAR-10 where weight merging degrades dramatically while logit merging maintains strong accuracy. IMP (pruning) provides the largest benefit for Deep MLPs in weight-space merging, while its effect is more modest for Shallow MLPs and CNNs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of modular neural networks (Kirsch et al., 2018), the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (Frankle and Carbin, 2018), and model merging (Ilharco et al., 2023), but departs from each in a key way. Rather than discovering circuits post-hoc (mechanistic interpretability) or relying on a controller to select modules, it trains each submodule from scratch under a Maximum-Entropy loss (Jaynes, 1957) that enforces strict uniformity outside the rewarded class set, combined with iterative magnitude pruning to expose the functional circuit. Unlike task arithmetic or Git-Rebasin, which assume pre-trained baselines and require alignment, the modules here are composable by construction: the MaxEnt loss guarantees that non-rewarded outputs are class-independent, so simple weight summation or logit averaging (product-of-experts) avoids semantic collision without fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, interpretable route to modular deep networks where each component has unambiguous class semantics, with direct applications to machine unlearning, federated learning, and NN verification. A key open question is why weight-space merging degrades sharply on complex datasets (CIFAR-10) and deeper architectures, while logit-space merging remains robust; understanding the role of width, BatchNorm re-estimation, and optimal pruning overlap could unlock more elegant composition. The paper also leaves open whether the mode-connectivity property observed for shallow MLPs on simple datasets persists at scale, and whether more sophisticated merging (Git-Rebasin, PLeas) can recover weight-space composability for ResNet/VGG on harder tasks.
