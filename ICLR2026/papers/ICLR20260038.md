## Problem

Existing inversion and editing methods designed for diffusion models are ineffective or inapplicable to flow models (e.g., SD3, FLUX) because their straight-line, non-crossing trajectories cause vanilla inversion to accumulate large errors and make delayed injection—widely used in diffusion-based editing—fail to produce meaningful edits. This paper addresses how to redesign inversion and editing to explicitly exploit the unique trajectory properties of flow models rather than forcing diffusion-based strategies onto them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260038/cropped_page0_idx11.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (in the latent space of a flow model such as SD3 or FLUX) along with a source text prompt describing the original image and a target text prompt specifying the desired edit. It produces two outputs: an inverted latent noise vector (enabling exact reconstruction of the input image) and a text-driven edited image. The editing procedure is parameterized by a delay rate α (fraction of total steps before injecting the target condition) and a guidance strength ω, with NFE = 3αN+1, making it tunable for the trade-off between background preservation and edit strength without any model-specific tuning.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary components. Uni-Inv is a predictor-corrector inversion that, at each step, reuses the previously computed velocity to backtrack the sample to the current timestep, then evaluates the velocity at that corrected state with the correct time value—yielding a closed-form implicit Euler solution with O(Δt³) local error, proven under Lipschitz assumptions on the velocity field. Uni-Edit extends delayed injection to flow models by computing source-conditioned and target-conditioned velocities at each step, deriving a region-adaptive mask from their difference, applying a mask-weighted correction step that pushes edit-relevant regions further along the target trajectory, and fusing the two velocities via the mask for the final sample update. This velocity fusion replaces multi-branch latent fusion, avoiding extra memory overhead while providing per-pixel adaptive guidance strength analogous to spatially-varying classifier-free guidance.

## Results

On PIE-Bench for text-driven image editing, Uni-Edit on FLUX (15 steps, α=0.6, NFE=28) achieves the best background preservation across all metrics (Dist 10.14, PSNR 29.54, SSIM 90.42) while maintaining competitive CLIP similarity (Whole 25.80, Edited 22.33). On SD3 (15 steps, α=0.6, NFE=28), it achieves the highest CLIP Whole score (26.39) and SSIM (86.11) among all methods. For inversion and reconstruction on Conceptual Captions, Uni-Inv substantially outperforms all baselines: on SD3 it achieves MSE 7.86 (conditional) and 11.52 (unconditional) vs. the next-best FireFlow at 16.95 and 20.27 respectively, and on FLUX it reaches PSNR 22.15 (unconditional) vs. FireFlow's 18.15.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Uni-Edit on FLUX (α=0.6) dominates all flow-based baselines (RF-Solver, FireFlow, RF-Inv.) on every background preservation metric, achieving Dist 10.14 vs. RF-Solver's 31.10 and FireFlow's 28.30, while its CLIP Edited score (22.33) is slightly below FireFlow (22.94) and RF-Solver (22.88). On SD3, Uni-Edit achieves the best CLIP Whole (26.39) and SSIM (86.11), outperforming all diffusion-based methods (P2P, PnP, PnP-Inv., EditFriendly, MasaCtrl, InfEdit) in editing effectiveness while using only 28 NFEs versus their 100. For inversion, Uni-Inv achieves roughly 2× lower MSE than the best baseline (FireFlow) on both SD3 (7.86 vs. 16.95 conditional) and FLUX (8.85 vs. 23.31 unconditional), with PSNR gains of 2–4 dB. The paper also shows Uni-Edit transfers to diffusion models (SDXL), where it achieves CLIP Whole 26.33 and CLIP Edited 22.78 in just 28 NFEs, surpassing InfEdit (25.03/22.22 at 72 NFEs) and all other diffusion baselines on editing quality. Iterative inversion methods (ReNoise, GNRI) are outperformed by Uni-Inv on both PSNR and LPIPS with fewer NFEs (101 vs. 150).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of rectified flow (Liu et al., 2022), DDIM inversion (Song et al., 2020), and mask-guided editing (Couairon et al., 2022), but fundamentally departs from the diffusion-based editing paradigm by arguing that straight-line, non-crossing flow trajectories make standard techniques like delayed injection and DDIM-style inversion structurally inadequate. Rather than retrofitting diffusion methods onto flow models (as prior works like RF-Solver and FireFlow attempt), the authors design a predictor-corrector framework from first principles that exploits the very trajectory properties that break diffusion-based approaches. The velocity fusion mechanism is shown to be a per-pixel analogue of classifier-free guidance, connecting their sampling-based correction to a well-understood theoretical framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With flow models (SD3, FLUX) now dominating text-to-image generation, the absence of reliable inversion and editing methods was a practical gap; this paper fills it with a tuning-free, model-agnostic solution that also generalizes back to diffusion models. The O(Δt³) local error bound for Uni-Inv provides theoretical grounding that prior flow inversion methods lacked. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more complex conditioning (e.g., multi-image personalization, video with temporal consistency beyond the preliminary Wan experiments) and whether the region-adaptive guidance can be made fully automatic without relying on velocity-difference masks. The paper's emphasis on efficiency (28–37 NFE for editing) is notable given that many competing methods require 100+ NFE.
