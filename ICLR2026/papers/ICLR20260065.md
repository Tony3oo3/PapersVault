## Problem

Pre-Norm MLLMs process high-norm visual tokens (from deep ViT encoders) and low-norm text tokens (from LLM embeddings) within a shared backbone, but the resulting norm disparity was previously unexamined as a source of cross-modal failure. This paper shows the disparity is not static: it induces an asymmetric update dynamic in which high-norm visual tokens exhibit 'representational inertia,' transforming semantically far more slowly than text tokens, thereby suppressing the attention signal needed for effective multimodal fusion.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images uniformly resized to 384×384 pixels and a text prompt, producing a free-form text response via greedy decoding. Images are encoded by a SigLIP-SO400M-Patch14-384 vision encoder into a sequence of visual tokens, projected into the LLM's word-embedding space, and concatenated with text tokens for autoregressive processing. The key architectural modification is a single LayerNorm layer inserted at the modality interface (immediately after the visual projector), whose gain is initialized to compress visual token norms down to the scale of text embeddings (~1.0 for D=3584–4096).

## Method

The paper first provides a theoretical framework proving that under Pre-Norm residuals, a uniform update magnitude applied to hidden states of different norms yields an inverse relationship between token norm and effective angular velocity, so high-norm visual tokens rotate far more slowly than low-norm text tokens, causing progressive geometric divergence and a collapse in the attention signal-to-noise ratio. Empirically, this asymmetry is confirmed across LLaVA-1.5, Qwen-2.5-VL, Kimi-VL, GLM-4.1V, and Ovis-2.5 via inter-layer cosine similarity measurements. The proposed fix inserts a LayerNorm after the visual projector with its gain initialized to g_init = T/√D (where T is the mean L2 norm of text embeddings), enforcing immediate norm alignment. Because this initialization is extremely small (≈0.01), standard backpropagation would cause vanishing gradients to the vision encoder; the authors resolve this with a Global Weight Compensation (GWC) backward hook that rescales incoming gradients by 1/μ_g (the inverse mean absolute gain), decoupling forward norm compression from backward gradient magnitude. Experiments on LLaVA-1.5 with Llama-3.2-3B and Qwen2.5-7B backbones show consistent gains on multimodal benchmarks (MMBench, MM-Star, SEED-Bench-2, OCRBench) and, notably, on text-only tasks (MMLU, HellaSwag), confirming that resolving the architectural imbalance yields a more holistically capable model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-documented observation that Pre-Norm architectures cause L2 norm accumulation with depth (Kim et al., 2025; Zhuo et al., 2025) into the cross-modal setting, arguing that the resulting norm disparity between visual and text tokens induces a dynamic 'asymmetric update' that prior work has not formalized. It explicitly departs from Qi et al. (2025), who observed similar norm discrepancies but attributed performance degradation to RoPE's inability to handle high-magnitude features; this paper argues that since Pre-Norm normalizes before Q/K projections, RoPE operates on already-normalized vectors, making the Pre-Norm residual dynamics the true culprit. Rather than proposing a new architecture (as Peri-Norm or HybridNorm do for unimodal models), the paper takes a diagnostic stance, identifying a specific failure mode of the de facto standard Pre-Norm design in multimodal contexts and offering a minimal corrective intervention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of 'representational inertia' as a mechanism for visual information loss provides a first-principles explanation for the diffuse attention maps and fine-grained perception failures observed across MLLMs, potentially guiding future architectural choices beyond the single-LayerNorm fix proposed here. The finding that norm alignment also improves text-only benchmarks (MMLU) suggests the imbalance was degrading the LLM's own internal processing, broadening the implications beyond multimodal fusion. However, the practical evidence is somewhat narrow: main experiments use only the LLaVA-1.5 architecture, the GWC mechanism exhibits gradient oscillation, and the authors themselves note that perfectly synchronized update rates may not be optimal. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video, audio, or other modalities, and whether more stable compression strategies can avoid the GWC instability.
