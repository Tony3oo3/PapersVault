## Problem

Current VLA post-training requires task-specific fine-tuning (e.g., OpenVLA trains four separate models for 240K total steps on LIBERO), incurring high compute costs and poor cross-task generalization. Naively adding diverse auxiliary tasks to multi-task SFT further degrades performance due to optimization instability from heterogeneous feature and action distributions. The paper asks whether auxiliary tasks can be integrated into a single unified post-training stage without destabilizing target-task optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264088/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB observations (front-view for LIBERO, side-view for GR00T auxiliary tasks), natural language task instructions, and a dynamically refreshed context bank of feature-action pairs (b_C=32 examples per context task, resampled every K=200 steps). Output is a sequence of discrete action tokens autoregressively decoded through the Llama2 LM head, which map to continuous robot actions (7-DoF for single-arm Franka Panda, 14-DoF for bimanual). The Action-ANP module adds only 0.3 ms/token inference latency over the base OpenVLA backbone.

## Method

MetaVLA introduces Context-Aware Meta Co-Training, which jointly trains a single model across all four LIBERO suites while conditioning on a context bank of both in-domain and structurally diverse out-of-domain (GR00T) auxiliary tasks. The key architectural component is Action-ANP, a lightweight module inserted into the Llama2 action decoder that applies self-attention over context examples to extract a global prior, then cross-attention to fuse target queries with context keys/values, producing deterministic and stochastic latent representations concatenated with Llama hidden states before the LM head. Training optimizes an ELBO objective combining reconstruction loss with a KL divergence between target and context posteriors, regularizing the model against distribution drift. The framework is backbone-agnostic (validated on both OpenVLA/Llama2-7B and NORA/Qwen2.5-VL-3B), uses LoRA fine-tuning, and reduces training from 240K steps across four models to 75K steps for one unified model (~76% GPU time reduction).

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, MetaVLA with six auxiliary tasks (5 single-arm + 1 bimanual) achieves 79.3% average success rate, outperforming the OpenVLA baseline (74.9%) by 4.4% and the multi-task SFT baseline SFT-4LIBERO (76.2%) by 3.1%. The largest gain is on LIBERO-Long (59.8% vs. 51.8% for OpenVLA, +8.0%). MetaVLA reduces training steps from 240K to 75K (68.75% fewer) and cuts total GPU training time from ~100 to ~24 hours (~76% reduction) while consolidating four task-specific models into a single unified model, with only 0.3 ms/token additional inference latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaVLA (79.3% avg) outperforms OpenVLA (74.9%) by 4.4% on average and by 8.0% on the most challenging LIBERO-Long suite, while using only 75K steps versus OpenVLA's 240K across four separate models. Against the direct multi-task SFT baseline SFT-4LIBERO (76.2%), MetaVLA gains 3.1% on average; critically, naively adding the same six auxiliary tasks to SFT-4LIBERO collapses performance to 8.6% average, demonstrating that the Action-ANP meta-learning module is essential for stable co-training with heterogeneous data. MetaVLA also surpasses Diffusion Policy (72.4%), ATM (63.4%), and TraceVLA (74.8%) on average success rate. However, MetaVLA trails π₀.₅ (96.9% avg) by a wide margin, as π₀.₅ benefits from prohibitively expensive large-scale pretraining with flow-matching. On the NORA-Long backbone (Table 2), MetaVLA achieves 91.8% average, beating NORA-Long (85.4%) by 6.4% and confirming backbone-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

MetaVLA adapts Attentive Neural Processes (ANP) from the meta-learning literature into a lightweight Action-ANP module plugged into the action decoder of existing VLA backbones (OpenVLA/Llama2, NORA/Qwen2.5-VL), positioning itself as orthogonal to prior VLA pretraining innovations (π0, π0.5, EO-1) that focus on architecture or data curation. It departs from the dominant per-task SFT paradigm (OpenVLA's four separate fine-tuned models) and from naive multi-task SFT, which the paper shows actively degrades performance when structurally diverse auxiliary tasks (different camera views, DoF, bimanual vs. single-arm) are added without a stabilizing mechanism. The context bank draws on the GR00T dataset as out-of-domain auxiliary data, extending the co-training idea well-established in LLM/VLM post-training (GPT-2, LLaVA, Qwen-3) into the VLA post-training setting where it had been largely unexplored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 76% reduction in GPU training time (≈100 h → ≈24 h) while simultaneously improving average success rate by 4.4% over OpenVLA and consolidating four models into one is a practically significant efficiency gain for resource-constrained labs. The negative result—that naively adding diverse auxiliary tasks to multi-task SFT collapses performance from 76.2% to 8.6%—challenges the intuitive assumption that more data always helps and motivates the meta-learning stabilization. Key limitations include evaluation only on the LIBERO simulation benchmark, testing on just two backbones, and the stochastic ANP component being disabled for practicality; open questions include whether the approach transfers to real-robot deployment, scales to web-scale multimodal context data, and integrates cleanly with RL-based post-training pipelines.
