## Problem

Score-based policy gradient methods like PPO suffer from high variance and training instability, while Q-based pathwise gradients reduce variance but have been limited to off-policy settings requiring large replay buffers. The core question REPPO addresses is whether a sufficiently accurate value function can be trained purely from on-policy trajectories and effectively used for policy improvement without replay buffers, combining the stability of Q-based gradients with the minimal memory footprint of standard on-policy learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261224/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

REPPO operates on state-vector observations from continuous-control environments (MuJoCo Playground DMC, ManiSkill, IsaacLab) and outputs continuous actions sampled from a tanh-squashed Gaussian policy. The critic takes (state, action) pairs and produces a categorical Q-value distribution over 151 bins via the HL-Gauss loss, while the actor produces mean and log-std parameters for the action distribution. Training uses on-policy rollouts of 1024 parallel environments × 128 steps per iteration, with no stored replay buffer beyond the current batch.

## Method

REPPO replaces the score-function estimator with a pathwise policy gradient computed by backpropagating through a learned Q function and the reparameterized stochastic policy, which allows resampling actions from the current policy rather than the behavior policy and eliminates importance sampling corrections entirely. Value targets are computed on-policy using multi-step TD-λ returns augmented with a maximum-entropy reward term, removing the need for pessimistic bias or target network copies. Policy updates are governed by a KL-constrained objective (inspired by Relative Entropy Policy Search) with jointly auto-tuned Lagrange multipliers for both entropy and KL divergence, preventing the policy from deviating into regions where the Q function is inaccurate. Architectural stabilizers include the HL-Gauss categorical cross-entropy loss for the critic, layer normalization throughout the network, and an optional latent self-prediction auxiliary task that proves most beneficial at smaller batch sizes.

## Results

On the Mujoco Playground DMC benchmark (23 tasks), REPPO achieves statistically significant performance improvements over PPO and comparable performance to the off-policy FastTD3, while using a replay buffer that is 2–3 orders of magnitude smaller (32,768 vs. 10,485,760 transitions). In wall-clock time, REPPO converges in roughly 600–800 seconds per task—similar to PPO—while achieving ~33% higher normalized returns. On the reliable-success metric (performance never dropping below τ=0.9 after first exceeding it), approximately 80% of REPPO runs are reliably performant by the end of training, compared to roughly 40 percentage points fewer for PPO. On ManiSkill manipulation tasks, REPPO shows significant gains over PPO on outlier-robust metrics (IQM and median) at both 50M and 100M environment steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REPPO outperforms PPO (Brax) on both DMC and ManiSkill in final return, sample efficiency, and reliable success rate, while matching PPO's wall-clock time (~600–800 s) and achieving ~33% higher normalized returns. Against the off-policy FastTD3, REPPO is competitive on DMC locomotion tasks despite using a buffer 2–3 orders of magnitude smaller; when FastTD3's buffer is constrained to REPPO's size, its performance drops substantially while REPPO is barely affected. REPPO also outperforms RPO and DPO, neither of which could surpass the PPO baseline even with hyperparameter tuning. On the discrete Atari-10 benchmark, D-REPPO is slightly behind PQN on mean score (2.98 vs. 3.35) but ahead on median (1.68 vs. 1.58) and tied on IQM (1.64), with the gap attributed to PQN's explicit ε-greedy exploration. The jax-based SAC baseline slightly outperforms PPO but does not match REPPO in final performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

REPPO sits at the intersection of two well-established lines of work: the pathwise (reparameterized) policy gradient used in off-policy maximum-entropy methods like SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018), and the KL-constrained trust-region updates from Relative Entropy Policy Search (Peters et al., 2010) and TRPO/PPO. The key departure is making the pathwise gradient viable in a fully on-policy regime without replay buffers, by combining multi-step TD-λ targets, automatic entropy/KL multiplier tuning, and recent architectural stabilizers (HL-Gauss categorical loss, layer normalization, auxiliary self-prediction) borrowed from the off-policy value-learning literature (Farebrother et al., 2024; Hussing et al., 2024; Nauman et al., 2024a). Unlike PQN (Gallici et al., 2024), which applies off-policy Q-learning to discrete actions in massively parallel settings, REPPO unifies discrete and continuous action spaces under a single actor-critic framework with principled entropy and KL constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REPPO demonstrates that the stability benefits of Q-based pathwise policy gradients—previously thought to require large off-policy replay buffers—can be achieved with purely on-policy data, yielding a method with PPO-level memory footprint but SAC-level training stability and sample efficiency. The finding that a single hyperparameter set generalizes across 30+ tasks spanning locomotion, manipulation, and discrete control is practically valuable for robotics deployment. Open questions include whether hybrid on/off-policy data usage can further improve performance, how the method scales to image-based observations, and whether the KL constraint can be relaxed or replaced with differentiable trust-region layers for even more aggressive updates.
