## Problem

Existing LLM-powered financial trading agents rely on anthropomorphic role-playing that introduces emotional biases, depend on unstructured peripheral information (news, social media) that carries misleading signals and temporal lags, and require continuous multi-agent inference during deployment, causing execution slippage in high-volatility markets. This paper addresses the gap by architecturally decoupling complex strategy reasoning from time-sensitive execution and leveraging specialized LLM capabilities (semantic analysis, code programming, mathematical reasoning) to achieve mechanical rationality at quantitative-trading latency.

## Input / Output

TiMi consumes objective technical indicators (volume, amplitude/volatility computed over dynamically updated look-back windows) from minute-level K-line data via exchange APIs, along with capital allocation parameters and risk thresholds; it deliberately avoids unstructured news or social-media inputs. The deployed bot outputs limit orders at volatility-scaled price levels (P_recent × (1±Φ)^M_P[i]) with quantities determined by M_Q matrices and scaling coefficients, executes progressive position closures at profit/loss thresholds, and returns structured action feedback (trade logs, performance metrics, risk events) that feeds back into the offline optimization loop. The entire deployment stage runs on a CPU-only Python runtime with ~137 ms end-to-end latency per action cycle.

## Method

TiMi is a four-agent system (macro analysis, strategy adaptation, bot evolution, feedback reflection) operating in a three-stage pipeline: (1) policy stage generates prototype trading bots offline by composing semantic analysis and code programming; (2) optimization stage simulates bots in live/historical markets and uses a feedback reflection agent to formulate linear-programming constraints from risk scenarios, solving for optimal parameters within a feasible space defined by component-wise inequalities; (3) deployment stage runs the refined bot as a standalone CPU-only program with no model inference. The key architectural insight is the decoupling of analysis from execution, quantified by an efficiency ratio that scales with trading frequency. Strategy development follows a two-tier paradigm (macro pattern detection → pair-specific customization), bot code is organized in three layers (strategy, function, parameter) governed by programming laws (functional cohesion, unidirectional dependency, parameter externalization), and optimization escalates hierarchically from parameter tuning to function substitution to strategy restructuring when lower-level adjustments prove insufficient.

## Results

In live trading (Jan–Apr 2025), TiMi achieves ARR of 6.4%, 8.0%, and 13.7% with Sharpe Ratios of 0.74, 0.79, and 0.86 across U.S. stock index futures, mainstream coin futures, and altcoin futures respectively, while maintaining minute-level trading frequency and covering 213 trading pairs. In backtesting on 2024 data, TiMi reaches ARR of 23.7% and SR of 1.27 on altcoins (vs. 17.5%/0.98 for the best ML baseline Autoformer and 9.7%/0.72 for the best LLM-agent baseline TradingAgents), with the lowest MDD (10.5%) among all methods on U.S. stock index futures. The system also achieves a Sortino Ratio of 0.91 on altcoins with only >4h of market data required, compared to >3d for TradingAgents and >1d for FinMem.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TiMi outperforms all 15 baselines on ARR and SR across all three market segments in live trading. Among quantitative methods, TSMOM and OFI are the strongest (OFI: 5.2%/0.58 on mainstream coins), yet TiMi leads by 2.8% ARR and 0.21 SR on mainstream coins and by 8.3% ARR and 0.34 SR on altcoins. Among ML/RL methods, DDPG is the best (5.9%/0.54 on altcoins) but TiMi surpasses it by 7.8% ARR and 0.32 SR on altcoins while also achieving lower MDD (32.8% vs. 38.1%). Among LLM-agent baselines, TradingAgents is the closest competitor (5.5%/0.57 on altcoins, 5.4%/0.63 on mainstream coins), but TiMi leads by 8.2% ARR and 0.29 SR on altcoins and by 2.6% ARR and 0.16 SR on mainstream coins. Notably, TiMi is the only method operating at minute-level frequency while covering all 213 pairs; ML/RL baselines support at most 150 pairs (DDPG) and LLM-agent baselines at most 81 (FinGPT), all at daily frequency. In backtesting (Table 1), TiMi also dominates: it achieves the best SR on altcoins (1.27 vs. 0.98 for Autoformer) and the lowest MDD on U.S. stock index futures (10.5% vs. 7.4% for Pairs Trading, which is the only method with lower MDD but at the cost of 2.3% ARR vs. TiMi's 8.9%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of LLM-powered trading agents (FinGPT, FinMem, TradingAgents) that rely on anthropomorphic role-playing, news-driven semantic analysis, and continuous multi-agent inference during deployment. Rather than using LLMs as real-time decision-makers, TiMi repurposes them as offline strategy developers and code generators, converting learned strategies into deterministic Python trading bots that execute without further model inference. The mathematical reflection component (formulating linear programming constraints from risk scenarios) draws on optimization literature but is novel in its integration with LLM-driven agent workflows for trading parameter calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling of LLM-based strategy development from minute-level execution addresses a fundamental practical bottleneck: continuous multi-agent inference is too slow and expensive for high-frequency trading, yet prior LLM-agent systems required it. The closed-loop mathematical reflection (converting qualitative risk events into solvable linear programs) offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc parameter tuning. Key limitations include the necessity of an optimization stage before deployment (preventing zero-shot transfer to new markets), the still-substantial 32.8% MDD on altcoins in live trading, and the narrow evaluation window (one backtest year, four months live). Open questions include whether the hierarchical optimization generalizes to less liquid or structurally different markets, and whether the approach's advantages persist as more LLMs gain native code-execution capabilities.
