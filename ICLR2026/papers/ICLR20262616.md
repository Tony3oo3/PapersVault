## Problem

Majority voting effectively aggregates parallel reasoning traces for closed-ended tasks like math and QA, but it is fundamentally ill-defined for open-ended reasoning (code generation, web-based deep research) where valid outputs rarely repeat and no canonical answer exists to vote on. This paper asks whether the benefits of parallel test-time scaling can be extended to open-ended tasks without relying on voting over complete outputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262616/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt/question and K model-generated reasoning traces (sampled at high temperature 0.5–0.7 up to a delimiter such as ) as input, and produces a single coherent answer—executable code, a research response, or a factual answer—decoded autoregressively. The K reasoning contexts are left-padded to a common length and re-prefilled to build an aligned KV cache ('bubble squeezing'), after which a single shared answer sequence is decoded. The output remains fully compatible with standard decoding controls (Top-p, Top-k, temperature, repetition penalties), making it a drop-in augmentation for existing vLLM/SGLang deployments.

## Method

THINKMERGE is a training-free, two-stage ensemble decoding strategy. In Stage 1 (Map), K diverse chain-of-thought traces are sampled in parallel at elevated temperature until each reaches a reasoning-end delimiter. In Stage 2 (Reduce), at every autoregressive step of the answer, the model's pre-softmax logits are queried for all K reasoning contexts, arithmetically averaged, and then passed through a single softmax to produce the next-token distribution; the selected token is appended to all K shared contexts before the next step. The key design choice is aggregating in logit space (pre-softmax) rather than probability space (post-softmax), which ablations show is substantially more robust—Prob-Merge (averaging normalized probabilities) consistently underperforms, especially for weaker models. The approach is framed as a PoE-style combination implemented as a logit processor, and four trace-processing variants (Direct-Merge, Early-Ready, Suffix Trimming, Shortest-K Merge) control when decoding starts and which subset of traces is ensembled, with Shortest-K Merge providing an anti-overthinking inductive bias for closed-ended tasks.

## Results

On closed-ended AIME 2025, THINKMERGE matches or slightly exceeds majority voting: Qwen3-4B at K=4 improves from 68.0% (MV) to 72.0% (+4.0%), and Qwen3-14B at K=8 from 74.0% to 78.0% (+4.0%). On open-ended LiveCodeBench (hard), where majority voting is undefined, pass@1 improves by +8.28% for DeepCoder-14B-Preview (20.69→28.97) and +7.58% for Qwen3-8B (24.14→31.72). On web-based deep-research benchmarks, WebSailor-7B gains +10.2 points on XbenchDeepSearch (37.8→48.0) and WebSailor-32B gains +7.2 points (50.4→57.6) at N=8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Majority Voting (MV), which is directly comparable only on closed-ended tasks (AIME, GPQA). On AIME 2025, THINKMERGE (Direct-Merge) matches or slightly exceeds MV in most configurations (e.g., Qwen3-4B K=4: 72.0 vs. 68.0; Qwen3-14B K=8: 78.0 vs. 74.0), but when the Shortest-K Merge strategy is applied, MV generally pulls ahead (e.g., Qwen3-14B K=8: MV 80.0 vs. THINKMERGE 78.7). On GPQA, THINKMERGE is competitive at small K (K=2: R1-Distill-Qwen-7B 49.2 vs. MV 44.9, +4.3%) but the gap narrows at larger K. On open-ended LiveCodeBench and deep-research benchmarks, MV is not applicable, so the relevant comparison is against single-pass decoding: THINKMERGE consistently improves pass@1, with the largest gains on hard coding problems (+8.28% DeepCoder, +7.58% Qwen3-8B) and deep-research agents (up to +10.2 points). An ablation against Prob-Merge (probability-level averaging) shows logit-level merging is consistently stronger, especially for weaker models (R1-Distill-Qwen-7B K=4: 46.0 vs. 32.7).

## Relation to Prior Work

THINKMERGE extends the self-consistency / majority-voting line of work (Wang et al. 2022; Brown et al. 2024) from closed-ended answer selection to open-ended generation by replacing vote-over-outputs with logit-level averaging across parallel chain-of-thought traces. Conceptually it is a Product-of-Experts-style combination (Hinton 1999) applied at the pre-softmax level, but unlike prior PoE adaptations (M-Ped, EVA, Wicks et al.) that ensemble across different prompts or different models, it treats K reasoning traces from a single model as experts and restricts aggregation to the answer phase only, leaving the thinking phase fully diverse. It also departs from model-based aggregation methods (LLM-Blender, GraphRAG) by requiring no additional scorer or summarizer model, making it a pure inference-time logit processor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that parallel test-time scaling can benefit open-ended tasks (code, deep research) where majority voting is undefined, with a simple training-free mechanism that plugs into vLLM/SGLang and requires only K=2 traces for most gains—keeping deployment costs low. Key limitations include: the 'shortest-trace' inductive bias that helps math does not transfer to code (shorter traces may omit necessary scaffolding), small models (3B) degrade with more traces due to low-quality trajectories diluting good ones, and the logit-vs-probability merging advantage, while consistent, is not yet explained theoretically. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger models or longer generation horizons, and whether the answer-phase-only restriction is truly optimal versus partial merging during thinking.
