## Problem

Hyperbolic geometry's exponential volume growth is theoretically ideal for embedding the hierarchical state structures in RL, yet hyperbolic deep RL agents suffer from severe training instability with no formal explanation of why. Prior work (Cetin et al., 2023) identified the failure mode and proposed SpectralNorm-based regularization (S-RYM), but this fix is insufficient—it cannot mitigate gradient pathologies without severely limiting network capacity, and the conformal factor of the Poincaré Ball remains a source of numerical instability. This paper provides the missing gradient-level analysis linking large-norm embeddings to PPO trust-region violations and derives targeted, capacity-preserving fixes.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes raw RGB frames (64×64×3 for ProcGen, 210×160×3 preprocessed to 84×84 grayscale with frame stacking for Atari) and outputs a discrete action distribution and a scalar value estimate. Architecturally, only the final actor and critic layers operate in hyperbolic space: a shared Euclidean CNN encoder (Impala-ResNet or NatureCNN) produces a latent vector that is normalized, scaled, and projected onto the Hyperboloid via the exponential map at the origin, where hyperbolic multinomial logistic regression layers compute the policy logits and value. The value head uses a categorical (HL-Gauss) parameterization over 51 bins clipped to [-10, +10] rather than a single scalar regression output.

## Method

HYPER++ stabilizes hyperbolic PPO through three complementary components targeting distinct terms in the backpropagation chain. First, RMSNorm applied to the pre-activation output of the encoder's final linear layer, combined with a learned sigmoid-scaled feature multiplier (capped at ρ_max = atanh(α)/√c with α=0.95), bounds Euclidean embedding norms and the conformal factor without the expressivity loss of full SpectralNorm. Second, switching from the Poincaré Ball to the Hyperboloid model eliminates the conformal factor from the MLR score function at its source, removing the (1−c‖x‖²)⁻² gradient explosion term identified in the formal analysis. Third, replacing MSE regression with an HL-Gauss categorical value loss aligns the critic's output geometry with the hyperplane-distance structure of hyperbolic MLR, stabilizing critic gradients under nonstationary TD targets. The gradient analysis shows that the Poincaré Ball's exponential map Jacobian decays as O(‖x_E‖⁻¹) but its directional term is highly sensitive to growing norms, while the Hyperboloid's exponential map involves sinh/cosh terms that grow exponentially—both requiring the norm regularization to prevent destabilization of the encoder's learning signal.

## Results

On ProcGen with PPO, HYPER++ improves test return by 52% over prior hyperbolic agents and outperforms the Euclidean baseline across all 16 environments (median, IQM, mean, and optimality gap). On Atari-5 with Double DQN, HYPER++ substantially outperforms both Euclidean and hyperbolic baselines across all five games. Wall-clock forward-pass time is reduced by ~30% compared to Hyper+S-RYM (14.7 ms vs. 19.3 ms), and full NameThisGame training drops from 58 h 21 m to 35 h 25 m. In head-to-head comparisons, HYPER++ wins 11/16 ProcGen test games against Hyper+S-RYM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the hyperbolic agent from Cetin et al. (2023), evaluated both unregularized ("Hyper") and with their S-RYM stabilization ("Hyper+S-RYM"), alongside a standard Euclidean PPO/DDQN agent. HYPER++ outperforms all three on ProcGen-PPO: it achieves the highest normalized IQM (0.40 with the Hyperboloid) versus the Euclidean baseline and both hyperbolic variants, and wins 11/16 test games head-to-head against Hyper+S-RYM. On ProcGen-PPG, HYPER++ again beats the Euclidean baseline in every aggregate metric, while Hyper+S-RYM is substantially worse than even the Euclidean agent. On Atari-5 with DDQN, HYPER++ strongly outperforms both Euclidean and hyperbolic baselines across all five games, with the largest gains on NameThisGame and Q*bert. In ablations, removing RMSNorm causes complete learning failure, confirming that the norm-bounding mechanism is the critical differentiator over prior approaches that rely on SpectralNorm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Cetin et al. (2023), who first introduced hyperbolic PPO agents with S-RYM regularization (SpectralNorm + 1/√d scaling), by providing the missing formal gradient analysis that explains *why* hyperbolic deep RL training fails. It builds on the HNN++ Poincaré Ball MLR formulation (Shimizu et al., 2021) and the Hyperboloid MLR layer (Bdeir et al., 2024), extending prior gradient analyses by Guo et al. (2022) and Mishne et al. (2023) with PPO-specific trust-region connections. The key departure is replacing the all-layers SpectralNorm constraint with RMSNorm applied only to the final encoder layer, switching from the Poincaré Ball to the Hyperboloid model, and introducing a categorical (HL-Gauss) value loss that is geometrically aligned with hyperbolic MLR outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal, curvature-aware gradient analysis linking large-norm embeddings to PPO trust-region violations in hyperbolic agents, giving practitioners a principled diagnostic rather than ad-hoc stabilization. The practical finding that RMSNorm on a single layer suffices (avoiding SpectralNorm's expressivity cost across all encoder layers) combined with a 30% wall-clock reduction makes hyperbolic deep RL substantially more accessible. Open questions remain about which environments genuinely benefit from hyperbolic representations, how geometric choices interact with different RL algorithms beyond PPO/DDQN, and what hierarchical structures the learned representations actually capture — the paper is explicitly optimization-centric and defers these representational questions.
