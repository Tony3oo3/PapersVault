## Problem

Offline preference-based RL suffers from low query efficiency due to two compounding issues: existing query selection strategies (e.g., reward-model disagreement) focus on refining reward estimates in regions irrelevant to the optimal policy, and learned reward functions are prone to overoptimization in high-uncertainty regions, leading to overestimated values and suboptimal policies. The paper asks how to design query selection and policy extraction that are both policy-relevant and robust to reward noise, so that strong performance is achievable with as few as ten human preference queries.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unlabeled offline trajectory dataset (state-action sequences of length T) and a small budget K of pairwise preference queries (binary labels comparing trajectory segments of length 50), selected from the existing dataset rather than generated online. It produces a learned reward function (trained via Bradley-Terry cross-entropy on the preference labels) and a final policy extracted by IQL from the reward-annotated offline dataset. The reward function is used solely to relabel the offline transitions; the policy is the ultimate output evaluated by normalized episode return.

## Method

OPRIDE has two core mechanisms. First, In-Dataset Exploration (IDE) for query selection: it trains M bootstrapped reward functions and M corresponding value functions via IQL, then selects trajectory pairs that maximize the difference of value differences across the ensemble (Eq. 7), which provably maximizes information gain about the optimal policy rather than the reward function. Second, Variance-based Discount Scheduling (VDS) for policy extraction: after annotating the offline dataset with the averaged reward, it reduces the discount factor to γ_small for data points whose ensemble Q-value variance exceeds the top m% in the batch, injecting localized pessimism where reward overestimation is most likely. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 4) decomposes suboptimality into an offline error term (scaling with √(C† log(N|Q||Π|)/(N(1−γ)²))) and a preference error term (scaling with √(d_Elu(ΔR) log(K|ΔR|)/(K(1−γ)))), showing that offline queries are more sample-efficient than online ones when N ≫ K.

## Results

On Meta-World (10 tasks, 10 queries), OPRIDE achieves an average normalized score of 65.3±3.3, substantially outperforming IDRL (57.0±6.3), PT (31.4±2.9), OPRL (30.4±4.2), and PT+PDS (25.0±1.8). On AntMaze (6 tasks), OPRIDE reaches 56.8±4.8 average, again leading all baselines (IDRL 52.8, PT+PDS 51.7, PT 50.4, OPRL 27.1). The method achieves compelling results with as few as 10 queries, e.g., 79.0 on peg-insert-side and 102.2 on push-wall, where the next-best baseline (IDRL) scores 67.4 and 89.2 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPRIDE wins on 8 out of 10 Meta-World tasks and 4 out of 6 AntMaze tasks, with the largest margins over IDRL (the strongest baseline) on push (+28.4), peg-insert-side (+11.6), and push-wall (+13.0). Against OPRL (disagreement-based query selection), OPRIDE dominates on nearly all tasks, e.g., 79.0 vs 3.5 on peg-insert-side and 102.2 vs 7.4 on push-wall. Against PT (Preference Transformer), OPRIDE leads on 7/10 Meta-World tasks but trails on soccer (45.4 vs 51.3) and lever-pull (51.8 vs 63.2). Against PT+PDS, OPRIDE is consistently superior, with PDS failing to prevent reward overoptimization on most tasks. Against one-stage methods (IPL, CPL) and the listwise method LiRE (Table 14), OPRIDE outperforms all on 9/10 Meta-World tasks, confirming that the iterative two-stage framework with in-dataset exploration extracts more value per query than bypassing reward modeling or using sequential comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

OPRIDE builds on IQL for policy extraction and Bellman-consistent pessimism (Xie et al., 2021) for theoretical grounding, but its core departure from prior offline PbRL methods (OPRL, PT, IDRL) is a shift in query selection philosophy: rather than maximizing information gain about the reward function (disagreement-based or Laplacian-approximation approaches), it maximizes information gain about the optimal policy via value ensemble differences. The variance-based discount scheduling mechanism extends the fixed-discount pessimism of PDS (Hu et al., 2023) into an adaptive, uncertainty-aware form, and the theoretical analysis connects the exploration strategy to the Eluder dimension framework of Russo & Van Roy (2013) and Chen et al. (2022), providing the first provable efficiency guarantee for an exploration-based query selection in offline PbRL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving competitive performance with as few as 10 preference queries dramatically reduces the human annotation cost that has been the primary barrier to PbRL deployment. The conceptual insight that value-function-based exploration substantially outperforms reward-function-based exploration (e.g., peg-insert-side: 79.0 vs 9.7) challenges the standard assumption in PbRL that reward uncertainty is the right quantity to target. Open questions include whether the two-stage pipeline can be unified, how the method scales to very large ensemble sizes or highly noisy human feedback, and whether the theoretical guarantees (which assume finite function classes and Bellman completeness) hold in practice with neural network approximators.
