## Problem

State-of-the-art feed-forward 3D reconstruction models such as VGGT rely on global self-attention over all frames, requiring complete re-encoding of the entire sequence whenever a new image arrives. This offline paradigm precludes incremental, frame-by-frame reconstruction and is incompatible with the low-latency, streaming requirements of interactive applications like autonomous driving, AR/VR, and embodied robotics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262975/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB video frames sequentially at 518×392 resolution (variable aspect ratios, max edge 518 px), processing one frame at a time during streaming inference. For each incoming frame it outputs a camera pose (translation vector, rotation quaternion, FoV), a dense 3D point map (3×H×W), a depth map (H×W), 2D point tracks (2×M), and per-pixel confidence maps for both point and depth predictions. A learnable camera token anchors the first frame as the global reference so all subsequent frames are incrementally aligned in a shared coordinate system without post-processing.

## Method

StreamVGGT replaces VGGT's global self-attention layers with temporal causal attention, restricting each frame to attend only to itself and its predecessors, reducing per-frame latency to O(N). During streaming inference, an implicit cached-memory mechanism stores historical keys and values from previously processed frames, so the current frame performs cross-attention against this cache rather than re-encoding the full sequence. The architecture uses a DINOv2 image encoder, a 24-layer spatio-temporal decoder with alternating spatial and temporal attention, and multi-task heads (camera, geometry via DPT, and tracking). Training employs knowledge distillation from the bidirectional VGGT teacher, whose soft pseudo-labels and confidence estimates supervise the causal student, unifying multi-task supervision across 13 datasets without per-dataset engineering. FlashAttention-2 is integrated for efficient inference, and for very long sequences the model supports windowed streaming or K-nearest-frames caching to bound memory.

## Results

On 7-Scenes, StreamVGGT achieves Acc 0.129 (mean), Comp 0.115 (mean), and NC 0.751 (mean), surpassing the best streaming baseline CUT3R (Acc 0.126, Comp 0.154, NC 0.727) and all pairwise methods. On NRGBD, StreamVGGT reaches Acc 0.084, Comp 0.074, and NC 0.861, outperforming CUT3R (Acc 0.099, Comp 0.076, NC 0.837) while remaining close to the offline VGGT (Acc 0.073, Comp 0.077, NC 0.910). On ETH3D, StreamVGGT achieves an overall Chamfer distance of 0.577, beating CUT3R (1.411) and DUSt3R (1.005) though trailing VGGT (0.686). For single-frame depth, StreamVGGT attains the best Abs Rel on Sintel (0.254), Bonn (0.052), and NYU-v2 (0.055), matching or exceeding VGGT across all four depth benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StreamVGGT outperforms all streaming baselines: on 7-Scenes it beats CUT3R in completeness (0.115 vs 0.154) and normal consistency (0.751 vs 0.727), and on NRGBD it leads CUT3R in accuracy (0.084 vs 0.099) and NC (0.861 vs 0.837); Spann3R is substantially worse across all metrics (e.g., Acc 0.298 vs 0.129 on 7-Scenes). Against pairwise methods (DUSt3R-GA, MASt3R-GA, MonST3R-GA), StreamVGGT achieves lower accuracy and completeness on both 7-Scenes and NRGBD, confirming the advantage of multi-view interaction over pair-wise reasoning. Compared to the offline VGGT teacher, StreamVGGT incurs a modest accuracy gap on 7-Scenes (Acc 0.129 vs 0.088) and ETH3D (Overall 0.577 vs 0.686), but narrows the gap on NRGBD (Acc 0.084 vs 0.073) and matches or exceeds VGGT on single-frame depth (e.g., Sintel Abs Rel 0.254 vs 0.276; NYU-v2 Abs Rel 0.055 vs 0.060). For camera pose estimation on ScanNet, StreamVGGT (ATE 0.048) is close to VGGT (0.035) and clearly beats CUT3R (0.099) and Spann3R (0.096). Against the concurrent STream3R, StreamVGGT achieves comparable 7-Scenes results (Acc 0.129 vs 0.119) while using fewer training datasets (13 vs 29) and half the GPUs (4 A800 vs 8 A100), validating the distillation strategy's resource efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

StreamVGGT builds directly on VGGT's 1.2B-parameter bidirectional visual geometry transformer, replacing its global all-to-all self-attention with temporal causal attention and a KV-cache mechanism borrowed from autoregressive LLMs. It departs from prior streaming 3D reconstruction methods (Spann3R, CUT3R, Point3R) that rely on explicit spatial memory pools or recurrent state updates, instead treating historical visual tokens as implicit memory cached in the attention mechanism. The knowledge-distillation training strategy, where the bidirectional VGGT teacher provides pseudo-ground-truth to the causal student, is a key methodological departure that avoids the need for full per-dataset annotation engineering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that the LLM-style KV-cache paradigm can be transplanted to dense 3D geometry prediction with near-zero accuracy loss relative to full-sequence inference (identical metrics in Table 14, 67× speedup on the final frame), making interactive, low-latency 3D reconstruction practical for AR/VR, autonomous driving, and embodied robotics. The main open limitation is linear memory growth with sequence length, mitigated only heuristically via windowing or K-nearest pruning; the paper acknowledges that very long sequences and aggressive dynamic scenes remain challenging. Future work on memory compression and dynamic scene adaptation will be needed before mobile deployment.
