## Problem

Existing multimodal benchmarks either evaluate static images, restrict to captioning tasks, or feature weak audio-visual correlations, leaving a critical gap in assessing whether MLLMs can truly integrate audio and video for real-world understanding. No prior benchmark systematically requires that both modalities be jointly necessary for answering questions, making it impossible to measure genuine omni-modal integration rather than single-modality shortcuts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261778/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Each test instance pairs a synchronized audio-visual video clip (mean duration 141.1 s, sourced from FineVideo and MusicAVQA) with a four-option multiple-choice question. Models consume video frames extracted per their official preprocessing pipelines (e.g., 16 uniform frames for GPT-4o/Claude, raw video upload for Gemini), optionally supplemented by auto-generated YouTube subtitles or the raw audio waveform. The output is a single letter (A–D), scored by exact match against ground truth, with results reported per domain, per task, and per audio type (speech, event, music).

## Method

WorldSense is constructed through a three-stage curation pipeline: (i) taxonomic filtering of ~8,000 candidate videos into 8 domains and 67 subcategories, (ii) selection based on pre-computed audio-visual correlation and dynamic-content metrics, and (iii) expert human review, yielding 1,662 clips. 80 professional annotators author 3,172 QA pairs spanning 26 tasks at three cognitive tiers (recognition, understanding, reasoning), with a hard design constraint that removing either audio or video makes the question unanswerable. Quality control combines expert review (linguistic clarity, multimodal necessity, difficulty) with automated MLLM verification—Qwen2-VL checks multimodal necessity while Video-LLaMA2 and OneLLM flag questions answered correctly by all models as too easy. Evaluation extends beyond a single accuracy number to include ablation studies across six modality configurations (audio-only, audio+caption, audio+video, video-only, video+subtitle, video+audio) and fine-grained breakdowns by audio type and task category, isolating each modality's contribution to model performance.

## Results

On WorldSense (3,172 multiple-choice QA pairs across 8 domains and 26 tasks), the best proprietary model Gemini 2.5 Pro achieves 65.1% average accuracy, while the best open-source video-audio MLLM (video-SALMONN2+ 72B) reaches 56.5%. Older open-source audio-visual models (Unified-IO-2, OneLLM, VideoLLaMA2) perform near random guessing at ~23–25%, and the strongest video-only MLLM (LLaVA-Video) caps at 40.2%. These results expose a substantial gap in real-world omni-modal reasoning, with even the top model falling well short of reliable application-level performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary MLLMs, Gemini 2.5 Pro (65.1%) substantially outperforms Gemini 2.5 Flash (52.3%), Gemini 1.5 Pro (48.0%), GPT-4o (42.6%), and Claude 3.5 Sonnet (34.8%), with the largest gap (12.8 points) over GPT-4o. Among open-source video-audio MLLMs, the newer 7B models (video-SALMONN2+ at 50.9%, Qwen3-Omni at 54.0%) dramatically surpass the older generation (Unified-IO-2 at 23.3–25.9%, OneLLM at 22.8%, VideoLLaMA2 at 25.4%), which perform at or below random-guessing level. The 72B video-SALMONN2+ (56.5%) is the strongest open-source model but still trails Gemini 2.5 Pro by 8.6 points. Video-only MLLMs (max 40.2% for LLaVA-Video) consistently underperform their audio-visual counterparts, confirming that audio integration is essential. Notably, even the best proprietary model achieves only 65.1%, indicating that no current system reliably handles real-world omni-modal understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

WorldSense carves new ground at the intersection of video understanding benchmarks and audio-visual QA datasets. Prior video benchmarks (Video-MME, MVBench, EgoSchema) evaluate visual-only comprehension, while existing audio-visual benchmarks (AVQA, Music-AVQA) are domain-specific with monotonous question patterns, and image-based omni-modal benchmarks (OmniBench, AV-Odyssey Bench) lack temporal dynamics. WorldSense departs from all of these by requiring synchronized audio-visual video inputs where neither modality alone suffices, spanning 8 real-world domains and 26 cognitive tasks, and by sourcing content from FineVideo and MusicAVQA with expert-annotated QA pairs that enforce true cross-modal integration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a critical gap: even the strongest proprietary model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) achieves only 65.1% accuracy, while open-source audio-visual models perform near random guessing, revealing that multimodal access without effective integration is insufficient. The ablation findings—that raw audio outperforms subtitles due to preserved paralinguistic cues, and that visual information is essential but inconsistently leveraged—provide actionable direction for architectural and training improvements. A key limitation is the multiple-choice format, which constrains assessment of open-ended generative reasoning; the authors acknowledge this and plan to expand the evaluation paradigm. The benchmark is likely to become a standard reference for evaluating omnimodal MLLMs as audio-visual capabilities become more central to real-world AI applications.
