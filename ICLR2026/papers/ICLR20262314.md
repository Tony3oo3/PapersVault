## Problem

Existing offline RL methods face a trade-off: policy regularization (e.g., TD3+BC) tightly constrains the policy to the dataset's one-to-one state-action mapping, preventing escape from local optima and limiting generalization, while ensemble-based value regularization (e.g., SAC-N, EDAC) handles OOD actions but requires large ensembles with high computational cost. The paper asks how to more effectively exploit cross-state structure in the dataset to improve generalization without sacrificing efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline dataset of (state, action, reward, next_state) tuples and, at training time, a query state from a minibatch. A precomputed KD-Tree over all dataset states enables O(|s| log |D|) nearest-neighbor retrieval of K peer states per query. The output is a deterministic policy π_φ(s) → a; in EPIC, the optimal candidate action a* is selected via min-over-N-critics Q-values from the union of the query state's actions and its K peer states' actions, making the effective in-dataset action set larger than the single behavior action.

## Method

PIC is a drop-in policy regularizer that, for each query state s, retrieves K nearest peer states via a precomputed KD-Tree, pools their actions with s's own actions into a candidate set A′, selects the optimal action a* = argmax_{a∈A′} min_i Q_{θ_i}(s,a), and adds a distance penalty ‖π_φ(s) − a*‖ to the actor loss. This expands the effective action set beyond the behavior action while remaining in-distribution, enabling escape from local optima. EPIC integrates PIC into the EDAC ensemble framework and uncovers a Coupling Effect: increasing PIC strength δ raises ensemble uncertainty (Q_min, Q_std, Q_clip), allowing effective OOD penalization with substantially fewer critics (N=2–10 vs. 50+). A performance gap theorem (Theorem 1) bounds |J(π*) − J(π)| in terms of state deviation ε_s, action discrepancy ε_a, and policy-optimal gap ε_*, all of which PIC reduces by construction.

## Results

On the D4RL Gym-MuJoCo benchmark (18 tasks), EPIC achieves an average normalized score of 87.8, outperforming EDAC (85.2), SAC-N (84.4), and SAC-RND (82.6). On AntMaze (6 tasks), EPIC scores 82.9 versus SAC-BC-N (81.8) and MSG (80.6). On Adroit (12 tasks), EPIC reaches 62.5, substantially exceeding EDAC (21.9) and IQL (53.5). The non-ensemble variant PIC-TD3 achieves 85.1 on Gym-MuJoCo, matching or exceeding ensemble-based methods while using only 2 critics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EPIC achieves the highest average score across all three D4RL suites: 87.8 on Gym-MuJoCo (vs. EDAC 85.2, SAC-N 84.4, TD3+BC 70.4), 82.9 on AntMaze (vs. SAC-BC-N 81.8, MSG 80.6, TD3+BC 28.8), and 62.5 on Adroit (vs. IQL 53.5, EDAC 21.9, TD3+BC 49.9). The non-ensemble PIC-TD3 (2 critics) reaches 85.1 on Gym-MuJoCo, matching EDAC (85.2) and exceeding SAC-N (84.4) while requiring far fewer networks. On Adroit, EPIC's advantage is most dramatic, outperforming EDAC by 40.6 points (62.5 vs. 21.9) and RORL by 21.5 points, suggesting the peer-influenced constraint is particularly effective in high-dimensional manipulation. On AntMaze, EPIC surpasses the strongest baseline SAC-BC-N by 1.1 points and TD3+BC by 54.1 points, with the largest gains on large-scale tasks (antmaze-large-play: 65.9 vs. 26.5 for TD3+BC). PIC-TD3 slightly underperforms EPIC on Gym-MuJoCo (85.1 vs. 87.8) but remains competitive with ensemble methods, confirming that the peer constraint mechanism itself provides most of the benefit without requiring large ensembles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two major offline RL paradigms: policy regularization (TD3+BC, PRDC) and ensemble-based value regularization (EDAC, SAC-N). PIC departs from TD3+BC's strict one-to-one state-action constraint by reusing actions from *similar* peer states via KD-Tree retrieval, and from PRDC's nearest state-action pair constraint by selecting the optimal action from a broader candidate set, avoiding state-mismatch errors. The key conceptual departure is the Coupling Effect: PIC strength and ensemble size are not independent hyperparameters but interact such that stronger peer constraints amplify uncertainty signals, enabling effective OOD penalties with substantially smaller ensembles than EDAC or SAC-N require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Coupling Effect provides a practical recipe for reducing ensemble size (and thus compute) while maintaining OOD robustness, which is directly useful for practitioners deploying offline RL in resource-constrained settings. The plug-in nature of PIC (applicable to TD3, SAC, IQL) makes it broadly adoptable. However, the authors acknowledge that peer-state search via KD-Tree becomes inefficient in very high-dimensional state spaces, and the WTW experiments confirm degraded neighbor quality in such regimes. Open questions include whether learned representations (e.g., the autoencoder variant) can fully resolve the high-dimensional scaling issue, and whether the Coupling Effect generalizes beyond actor-critic methods to model-based or diffusion-based offline RL.
