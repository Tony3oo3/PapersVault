## Problem

Whether Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures can genuinely outperform dense LLMs remains unresolved because prior comparisons fix only one resource (data or compute) while letting the others vary. This paper tackles the stricter question: can MoE surpass dense models when total parameters N, training compute C, and unique data budget D are all held identical, a setting that reflects real deployment memory and training cost constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263285/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The models consume text tokens (sequence length 2048) and produce next-token predictions via cross-entropy loss, with validation measured in bits-per-character (BPC). The experimental 'input' is a set of architecture hyperparameters (N, C, D, activation rate r_a) and a LLaMA-1-style pretraining corpus (~80% web, ~5% code, ~5% Wikipedia, ~5% books, ~3% arXiv, ~2% StackExchange); the 'output' is a family of ~250 trained checkpoints at 2B, 3B, and 7B scale, evaluated on 29 downstream benchmarks spanning knowledge, reasoning, math, and code.

## Method

The paper proposes a three-step methodology under a unified parameterization framework. Step 1 greedily optimizes the MoE backbone: a 1-dense-layer + shared-expert arrangement, top-K routing with K≈6 (avoiding K=1 and very large K), and shape ratios ζ≈88, μ≈22. Step 2 sweeps activation rate r_a from ~9% to ~58% at fixed N and C, revealing a stable optimal region r_a**≈20% that is consistent across 2B, 3B, and 7B scales—contradicting prior scaling-law claims that optimal sparsity grows with model size. Step 3 introduces strict and loose data-reuse schemes (retraining on a fixed sub-dataset D̂ for multiple epochs with shuffling) to offset MoE's inherent need for more tokens at equal compute, showing that up to ~2 epochs of reuse causes only marginal BPC degradation while preserving MoE's advantage over dense baselines.

## Results

Under strictly equal total parameters (N), compute (C), and data (D), a 7B MoE model with optimal activation rate r_a=20.07% achieves a validation BPC of 0.4543, outperforming the same-compute dense baseline (BPC 0.4736) by 0.0193 and even surpassing the dense model trained with 2× compute (BPC 0.4594) by 0.0051. For 2B models, the optimal-AR MoE (r_a≈20%) achieves BPC 0.4857 vs. 0.4921 for the same-compute dense model (a 0.0064 BPC gain). The optimal activation rate of ~20% is remarkably consistent across 2B, 3B, and 7B model scales, and the strict data-reuse scheme (training on a fixed 68B-token subset for multiple epochs) incurs only a marginal BPC increase of ~0.005 over single-epoch unique-data training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is MoE vs. dense models of identical total parameter count (N) under matched compute (C) and data (D) budgets. At 7B scale, the MoE model with r_a=20.07% (C=2.86e21) achieves BPC 0.4543, beating the same-compute dense baseline (BPC 0.4736) by 0.0193 BPC and also outperforming the dense model trained with 2× compute (BPC 0.4594, C=5.45e21) by 0.0051 BPC. At 2B scale, the optimal-AR MoE (BPC 0.4857) beats the same-compute dense (BPC 0.4921) by 0.0064 BPC, though it remains 0.0049 BPC behind the 2×-compute dense (BPC 0.4808). On downstream SFT benchmarks (Table 2), the no-reuse MoE outperforms the 2×-compute dense on most categories (e.g., CMMLU 31.62 vs. 31.23, DROP 35.13 vs. 32.32, GSM8K 15.54 vs. 13.34), while the strict-reuse MoE shows more mixed results with notable degradation on knowledge benchmarks (MMLU drops to 24.57) but gains on MuSR (48.94 vs. 35.98). The paper also contrasts its findings with Abnar et al. (2025), who report that optimal MoE sparsity increases with model size; here, the optimal AR remains at ~20% across 2B–7B, attributed to the use of optimized backbones and sufficiently trained models (D/N ≥ 20).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MoE-vs-dense comparison literature by introducing a strict triple-constraint formulation (equal total parameters N, compute C, and data D) that prior work has not addressed simultaneously. It builds on the MoE architecture lineage (Shazeer et al., 2017; Switch Transformer; DeepSeekMoE) and uses the hyperparameter scaling laws of Li et al. (2025) and the dense shape recommendations of Kaplan et al. (2020) to ensure fair baselines. Methodologically, it departs from prior scaling-law studies (Abnar et al., 2025; Ludziejewski et al., 2025) by operating in a sufficiently trained regime (D/N ≥ 20) with optimized backbones, rather than broad undertrained sweeps, and by isolating the activation-rate effect through a three-step architecture-optimization protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that an optimal activation rate of ~20% is consistent across 2B, 3B, and 7B models provides a concrete, size-independent design guideline for practitioners choosing between MoE and dense architectures at a fixed parameter budget. The data reuse strategy (≈2 epochs) offers a practical resolution to MoE's additional data demand without requiring substantially more unique tokens. Key limitations include the 7B scale ceiling, reliance on internal proprietary data, and the open question of whether the size-independence of optimal AR holds at much larger scales (>>7B) or interacts with expert specialization in ways not yet characterized.
