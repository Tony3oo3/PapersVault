key_contribution: Proposes KinemaDiff, a diffusion framework for stochastic human motion prediction that reshapes the denoising process by embedding a learnable joint-adaptive noise schedule (per-joint, per-instance variance) and a step-wise structure-aligned regularizer (bone-length consistency at every diffusion step), achieving state-of-the-art accuracy (ADE 0.331) and realism (FID 0.083) on Human3.6M and strong cross-dataset generalization on AMASS.
limitations: The paper does not include an explicit limitations section, but the evaluation is restricted to single-person, 16-joint (Human3.6M) and SMPL-parameterized (AMASS) skeletons with a fixed 10-step diffusion budget, leaving open questions about scalability to higher-fidelity skeletons, multi-person interaction, and real-time inference. Additionally, the Structure-Aligned Regularizer's constraint on the generative distribution results in slightly lower raw diversity (APD) and CMD compared to CoMusion, a trade-off the authors frame as intentional but which may limit applicability in scenarios where broad exploratory diversity is prioritized.

surprise_factor: No particularly surprising findings — the improvements over strong baselines (e.g., 19% FID reduction over CoMusion, ADE 0.331 vs. 0.344) are solid but incremental, and the ablations confirm the expected contributions of each module. ## Problem

Diffusion-based human motion prediction methods apply a uniform noise schedule across all joints and enforce skeletal consistency only indirectly through architecture or post-processing, rather than embedding physical constraints into the denoising process itself. This leads to physically implausible poses with stretched or compressed bones and fails to capture the heterogeneous dynamic behaviors of different joints, compromising both motion realism and diversity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261384/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes H frames of observed 3D joint coordinates (H×3J) as conditioning and stochastically generates F future frames (F×3J) via a 10-step DDPM process. On Human3.6M, this is 25 observed frames → 100 future frames with a 16-joint skeleton; on AMASS, 30 observed frames (0.5 s) → 120 future frames (2 s) under the SMPL parameterization. Multiple diverse future trajectories are sampled per input to capture multimodality.

## Method

KinemaDiff reshapes the diffusion process with two modules. The Joint-Adaptive Noise Generator learns a per-joint, per-instance noise scale s_j = f_θ(j, x_j^(1:H)) via a small network, replacing the standard isotropic Gaussian with a multivariate schedule whose diagonal covariance Σ = diag(s_1²,…,s_J²) assigns higher variance to dynamic extremities and lower variance to stable trunk joints. The Structure-Aligned Regularizer extracts average bone lengths from the noise-free history and enforces them at every denoising step through an alignment loss, operating in the frequency domain via DCT/IDCT with nine frequency-aware GCN layers that use band-specific adjacency matrices. The denoiser directly predicts the clean motion ŷ_0 at each timestep (rather than noise), enabling structural constraints to be applied throughout the trajectory; a variance scheduler suppresses noise magnitude at early timesteps to keep the signal recoverable. Training minimizes a joint-weighted L1 reconstruction loss plus the bone-length alignment loss, with the full model trained end-to-end for 500 epochs using AdamW.

## Results

On Human3.6M, KinemaDiff achieves the best ADE (0.331) and FDE (0.449) among all compared methods, surpassing the previous best (CoMusion: ADE 0.350, FDE 0.458) and the closest competitor SkeletonDiff (ADE 0.344, FDE 0.450). It also attains the lowest FID of 0.083, a 19% relative improvement over CoMusion (0.102), indicating substantially more realistic generated motions. On the cross-dataset AMASS benchmark, KinemaDiff leads in ADE (0.478), FDE (0.540), MMADE (0.456), MMFDE (0.457), and CMD (9.448), demonstrating strong generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KinemaDiff outperforms all 13 baselines on accuracy (ADE/FDE) and realism (FID) on Human3.6M, with the closest competitors being SkeletonDiff (ADE 0.344, FID 0.123) and CoMusion (ADE 0.350, FID 0.102). On the cross-dataset AMASS benchmark, it similarly leads in ADE (0.478 vs. 0.480 for SkeletonDiff, 0.494 for CoMusion), FDE, and multimodal metrics (MMADE 0.456, MMFDE 0.457), confirming robust generalization. However, KinemaDiff trails CoMusion on CMD (4.60 vs. 3.202) and APD (6.912 vs. 7.632) on Human3.6M, and also on APD on AMASS (9.683 vs. 10.848 for CoMusion), reflecting a deliberate trade-off: the Structure-Aligned Regularizer constrains the generative distribution to anatomically valid poses, reducing raw numerical diversity in exchange for physical plausibility. Among VAE-based methods, KinemaDiff improves ADE by 0.093 over the best VAE baseline (DivSamp, 0.370) and reduces FID by over 25× compared to Motron (13.743 → 0.083). The paper argues that the slightly lower APD is not a deficiency but a consequence of pruning physically implausible samples, as evidenced by the substantially lower Limb Stretch metric (2.42 vs. 3.90 for SkeletonDiff).

## Relation to Prior Work

KinemaDiff builds directly on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and adopts the DCT/IDCT + frequency-aware GCN architecture and variance scheduler from CoMusion (Sun & Chowdhary, 2024), along with the direct y₀ prediction objective from HumanMAC (Chen et al., 2023). Its key departure is from prior diffusion-based HMP methods that either apply uniform noise across all joints or enforce structural constraints only as post-processing; instead, KinemaDiff reshapes the diffusion process itself through a learnable joint-adaptive noise schedule and step-wise bone-length alignment. This positions it as a more dynamic successor to SkeletonDiffusion (Curreli et al., 2025), which uses static, manually-specified anisotropic noise derived from the skeleton graph.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that embedding physical constraints (bone-length consistency) at every denoising step, rather than only at the final step, yields measurably better accuracy and realism, offering a design principle applicable to other structured generative tasks. The joint-adaptive noise generator provides a principled alternative to uniform or static anisotropic noise, though the resulting APD is slightly lower than some baselines, reflecting a deliberate trade-off between physical plausibility and raw diversity. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-fidelity skeletons (e.g., 22+ joints), multi-person interaction, or real-time inference settings, and whether the 10-step diffusion budget is sufficient for more complex motion distributions.
