## Problem

Existing safety alignment methods for LLMs (SafeRLHF, SACPO, CAN) rely on auxiliary reward/cost models, multi-stage pipelines, or relaxed expected-cost formulations that do not strictly enforce safety constraints. The original hard-constrained safety alignment objective—requiring zero probability on unsafe responses—has never been shown to admit a tractable, single-stage training procedure. This paper asks whether the hard-constrained problem itself can be solved directly without surrogate relaxations or additional networks.

## Input / Output

SafeDPO consumes a preference dataset of the form (x, y_w, y_l, h_w, h_l), where x is a text prompt, y_w/y_l are paired responses with a helpfulness preference label, and h_w/h_l are binary safety indicators (1 = unsafe). No harmlessness preference labels, reward model scores, or cost model scores are required. The output is a fine-tuned LLM policy that generates text responses; the safety constraint is enforced by construction through a data transformation that reorders or discards preference pairs before the standard DPO loss is applied.

## Method

SafeDPO defines a cost-augmented reward r_c that assigns −∞ to any unsafe response, showing the resulting KL-regularized objective admits a closed-form optimal policy that excludes unsafe responses from its support by construction. The key tractability step is a safety-aware transformation T on the empirical preference data: pairs where the preferred response is unsafe but the non-preferred one is safe are swapped, and pairs where both responses are unsafe are discarded. This transformation provably recovers the intractable preference objective under the cost-augmented reward, yielding a standard DPO-style loss with no auxiliary models. An optional safety margin Δ ≥ 0 is added to (safe, unsafe) pairs to enlarge the log-probability gap during training; Proposition 4.4 guarantees this margin does not alter the set of optimal policies, only strengthening optimization dynamics. The entire procedure requires one additional hyperparameter (Δ) and no reward, cost, or value networks, reducing training time from ~35,000 s (SafeRLHF) to ~1,400 s.

## Results

On the PKU-SafeRLHF-30K benchmark, SafeDPO achieves a 96.87% harmless ratio and 5.97 harmlessness under model-based evaluation, and a 100% harmless ratio with 9.92 harmlessness under GPT-4 evaluation, both the highest among all compared methods. Helpfulness remains competitive at 4.61 (normalized, model-based) and 8.14 (GPT-4). On the XSTest benchmark, SafeDPO achieves 100% harmless ratio on unsafe prompts but incurs a 12.4% over-refusal rate on benign prompts, reflecting the structural trade-off of its hard-constrained formulation. Across model scales from 1.5B to 13B parameters, SafeDPO consistently maintains 95.5–97.9% harmless ratio while preserving helpfulness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SafeDPO substantially outperforms all baselines on safety metrics: it achieves a 96.87% harmless ratio vs. 88.97% for SafeRLHF (Dai et al., 2023), 89.60% for SACPO, and 85.46% for P-SACPO (Wachi et al., 2024), with the largest gap over DPO variants (37.59–75.69%). Under GPT-4 evaluation, SafeDPO reaches 100% harmless ratio and 9.92 harmlessness, exceeding SafeRLHF (96.62%, 9.57) and SACPO (96.12%, 9.49). On helpfulness, SafeDPO (4.61 normalized) trails DPO-HELPFUL (10.00) and DPO-SAFEBETTER (9.08) under model-based evaluation but matches or exceeds all safety-focused baselines; under GPT-4 it achieves the highest helpfulness score (8.14). MoCAN and PeCAN (Huang et al., 2024) show highly λ-sensitive performance, with no single configuration matching SafeDPO's combined safety-helpfulness profile. On XSTest, SafeDPO's 100% harmless ratio on unsafe prompts is unmatched, but its 12.4% over-refusal rate is the highest, compared to 1–4% for other safety methods, highlighting the cost of its hard-constraint design.

## Relation to Prior Work

SafeDPO builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024b) and its generalization to Direct Alignment Algorithms (Rafailov et al., 2024a), extending the closed-form optimal policy derivation to the safety-constrained setting originally formulated by SafeRLHF (Dai et al., 2023). The key conceptual departure from prior safety alignment work (SafeRLHF, SACPO, MoCAN/PeCAN) is that instead of relaxing the hard safety constraint to an expected-cost formulation requiring auxiliary reward and cost models, SafeDPO solves the original hard-constrained problem directly, showing it admits a closed-form policy that can be implemented as a simple safety-aware reordering of preference pairs. This collapses a multi-stage RLHF pipeline into a single-stage DPO-style objective with one additional hyperparameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the drastic reduction in training infrastructure: no reward models, cost models, value functions, or on-policy rollouts are needed—only preference data and binary safety labels, with one tunable margin parameter. This makes principled safety alignment accessible at much lower computational cost (1388s vs. ~35,000s total for SafeRLHF). However, the hard-constraint formulation introduces a structural trade-off visible on XSTest: 100% harmless ratio comes with 12.4% over-refusal on benign prompts, versus 1–4% for relaxation-based methods. Open questions include whether the over-refusal can be mitigated without sacrificing the safety guarantee, whether the approach generalizes beyond the PKU-SafeRLHF-30K benchmark to more diverse safety-critical scenarios, and how it scales beyond 13B parameters.
