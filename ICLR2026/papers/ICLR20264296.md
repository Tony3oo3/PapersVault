## Problem

Graph Neural Networks have become the dominant architecture for learning-based SAT solving, yet their fundamental expressivity limits for this task were uncharacterized. The paper asks whether GNNs bounded by the Weisfeiler-Leman hierarchy can, in principle, distinguish satisfiable from unsatisfiable formulas, and whether sequential variable assignment (as in QuerySAT-style solvers) can overcome any such limitations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264296/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a CNF formula encoded as a Literal-Clause Graph with Negation connections (LCN), a tripartite graph where literal nodes connect to clause nodes via literal-clause edges and to their negation via a distinct edge color. The experimental output is a critical round r_crit: the smallest number of WL iterations after which adding equality constraints between all WL-equivalent literals leaves the formula satisfiable, indicating a WL-powerful GNN could in principle predict a satisfying assignment. If no such round exists (marked 'unsat'), WL is fundamentally insufficient for that instance.

## Method

The main theoretical contribution adapts the Cai-Furer-Immerman construction to SAT: for a low-degree expander graph G, the paper builds a 3-SAT formula f_G whose LCN encodes the existence of an even edge orientation, and a twisted variant f̃_G where one edge is bidirectional. Exactly one is satisfiable depending on the parity of |E(G)|, yet their LCNs are indistinguishable under n-WL. A pebbling-game argument (Lemma 5.4) then shows that even Ω(n) sequential variable assignments cannot break the indistinguishability for WL-powerful solvers. On the positive side, the paper proves PlanarSAT is fully identified by 4-WL and that random SAT instances extracted from random literal-incidence graphs are identified by WL with probability ≥ 1 − n^(−1/7). Experimentally, the paper tests WL-powerful GNNs on G4SAT random instances and 448 industrial/crafted instances from the SAT Competition (2020–2025), finding that random instances require only 3–4 WL rounds while many industrial families (e.g., heule-nol, circuit-multiplier) are unsolvable by WL at any depth.

## Results

On random 3-SAT instances (G4SAT benchmark), WL distinguishes all literals in just r_crit = 3.00 ± 0.00 rounds and converges in 4.00 rounds, regardless of instance size (up to 5001 variables). On SAT Competition instances, only 234 out of 448 evaluated instances are solvable within WL's expressive power, and 38 out of 69 instance families contain at least one instance where WL is insufficient. Entire families such as circuit-multiplier, heule-folkman, and heule-nol are completely unsolvable by WL (r_crit = unsat), while others like cryptography require on average 15.74 ± 14.67 rounds and maxsat-optimum requires 26.64 ± 3.64 rounds before WL can distinguish literals enough to predict a satisfying assignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare GNN architectures against each other; instead, it compares the WL expressivity requirements across different SAT instance families. The central empirical contrast is between random instances (G4SAT) and industrial/crafted instances (SAT Competition). Random 3-SAT instances are uniformly easy for WL, with r_crit ≈ 3 across all sizes, while industrial instances show extreme variability: some families (argumentation, hamiltonian) are as easy as random instances, while others (circuit-multiplier, heule-folkman, heule-nol) are completely beyond WL's reach. The paper also contrasts its theoretical indistinguishability results (Theorem 5.3, showing n-WL cannot separate satisfiable from unsatisfiable formulas) against positive results for structured families: PlanarSAT is fully identified by 4-WL (Theorem 6.1), and random LIG-extracted formulas are identified by WL with probability ≥ 1 − n^(−1/7) (Theorem 6.3). The 3-regular SAT family provides a worst-case where all instances of the same size are WL-indistinguishable despite the problem being NP-complete.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the well-established WL-MPNN expressivity framework (Xu et al., 2019) to the domain of Boolean Satisfiability, building directly on the Cai-Furer-Immerman (1992) construction of WL-indistinguishable graph pairs to produce indistinguishable SAT formulas. It connects to the GNN-for-SAT literature (NeuroSAT, QuerySAT) by showing fundamental limits on what WL-powerful architectures can achieve, and draws an unobserved parallel between the CFI construction and Tseitin formulas, which are canonical hard instances for resolution refutation. The positive results for PlanarSAT (via Kiefer et al., 2019) and random instances (via Babai et al., 1980) mirror known graph-isomorphism results, transplanted to the formula setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that even the full n-WL hierarchy cannot separate satisfiable from unsatisfiable 3-SAT formulas establishes a hard ceiling for the entire class of message-passing GNN architectures used in current SAT solvers, with concrete implications for restart strategies and sequential variable assignment. The experimental gap between random and industrial instances (only 234/448 competition instances solvable within WL expressivity) suggests that the field's reliance on random-instance training may mask expressivity deficiencies that matter in practice. Open questions include whether higher-order architectures beyond the WL hierarchy, symmetry-breaking mechanisms, or hybrid approaches can close this gap, and whether the Tseitin-formula connection can yield new lower bounds for GNN-based SAT solving.
