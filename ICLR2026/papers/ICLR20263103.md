## Problem

Existing gradient compression techniques for distributed deep learning either apply a fixed compression ratio uniformly across all layers and training iterations, or rely on computationally intensive adaptive methods (e.g., per-iteration optimization, dynamic programming) to select compression parameters. There is no simple, lightweight recipe that dynamically adjusts compression based on the two most fundamental structural factors of DNN training—layer size and training phase—while remaining compatible with any base compressor.

## Input / Output

LEGACY operates as a scheduler layer on top of any base compressor (Top-k, Random-k, QSGD, PowerSGD). At each iteration, it receives the per-layer gradient tensors, their dimensions, and the current training epoch, then outputs a per-layer compression parameter (e.g., k for Top-k, quantization level s for QSGD, rank r for PowerSGD) that is applied before communication. The user specifies a sorted list of p compression levels {δ_i} and p thresholds {λ_i} (either iteration boundaries or layer-size boundaries); the simplified S-LEGACY variant reduces this to 2–3 hyperparameters (a default compression ratio δ_u, number of phases n, and/or a decrease ratio s) and auto-computes per-group parameters via volume-matching functions vol(δ) and vol⁻¹(v).

## Method

LEGACY is a dynamic compressor scheduler grounded in a theoretical analysis of compressed gradient descent. Under smoothness and strong convexity (Lemma 1) or the PL condition (Lemma 2), the compression-induced error Δ = μη²L(1−δ_t)‖x_t − x_*‖² is large early in training (when ‖x_t − x_*‖² ≫ 0) and negligible late in training, justifying Strategy I: start with mild compression (δ→1) and progressively increase aggressiveness (δ→0). Strategy II exploits the empirical observation that the largest 20% of layers account for ~90% of parameter volume and are overparameterized, so they tolerate aggressive compression while small layers—critical for convergence stability—should be lightly compressed or left uncompressed. Operationally, two lightweight functions (EpochCompression and LayerSizeCompression) map the current iteration or layer size to a compression parameter from the user-defined schedule, requiring no gradient statistics, no per-iteration optimization, and no additional computation beyond a simple lookup. The framework is optimizer-agnostic, communication-protocol-agnostic (parameter-server, ring all-reduce, NCCL, Gloo), and supports both uplink and downlink compression; convergence is guaranteed for both unbiased and biased δ-compressors under standard distributed SGD assumptions (Theorem 1).

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with ResNet-50, LEGACY's dynamic compression strategies improve Top-1 accuracy by 7–11% over uniform Top-0.1% sparsification at equivalent average data volume. On WikiText-103 with Transformer-XL, the layer-based strategy (S₁₀L₀.₁) reduces perplexity from 253.57 to 188.8 (~26% improvement) relative to the same uniform baseline. On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18 using PowerSGD, LEGACY-E achieves 75.55% Top-1 accuracy while transmitting only 1.11% of the data volume (vs. 2.85% for uniform rank-3 PowerSGD), outperforming both L-GreCo and Accordion. On GPT-2 (OpenWebText), LEGACY-L reduces validation loss from 3.14 to 3.01 (4.14% improvement) at the same 5% sparsification ratio.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEGACY consistently outperforms uniform Top-k sparsification across all seven DNN architectures and six datasets, with the largest gains on ImageNet-1K (7–11% Top-1 accuracy) and WikiText-103 (~26% perplexity reduction). Against the five state-of-the-art adaptive compressors, LEGACY wins on both accuracy and efficiency: it surpasses CAT by 25% accuracy while being 11× faster, outperforms variance-based compression by 15% accuracy at 6× lower cost, and beats AdaComp by 12% accuracy with only ~75 MB more data. In the PowerSGD comparison (Table 3), LEGACY-E achieves the highest accuracy (75.55%) while transmitting 61% less data than uniform PowerSGD and roughly half the data of L-GreCo (2.19%) and Accordion (2.02%). For QSGD on ResNet-9 (CIFAR-10), LEGACY-L gains 1.39% accuracy over uniform QSGD while sending 12.67% less data. The only trade-off noted is that LEGACY is slightly slower than AdaComp (which is threshold-based and 2× faster than Top-k), but the accuracy gain more than compensates for the marginal data increase.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEGACY builds on the compressed distributed SGD framework of Dutta et al. (2020) and Sahu et al. (2021), but departs sharply from the dominant trend of compute-intensive adaptive compressors (CAT, Accordion, L-GreCo, AdaComp, variance-based methods) that rely on per-iteration gradient statistics, dynamic programming, or communication cost models to select compression parameters. Instead, it grounds its design in two empirically observed and theoretically justified factors—layer size and training phase—via a compressed GD analysis under smooth/strongly-convex and PL conditions, yielding a compute-free scheduler that is compressor-agnostic (Top-k, QSGD, PowerSGD). Conceptually, LEGACY can be seen as a principled generalization of the threshold sparsifier's implicit adaptivity (variable data volume per iteration) into an explicit, user-controllable scheduling policy applicable to any base compressor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LEGACY offers a practical, drop-in alternative to complex adaptive compressors that are often 6–11× slower and harder to tune, with consistent gains across 7 architectures and 6 datasets including ImageNet-1K and GPT-2 on OpenWebText. Its compute-free nature makes it particularly relevant for bandwidth-constrained federated settings and large-scale (100-worker) deployments where per-iteration optimization overhead is prohibitive. Open questions include whether the two-phase / two-group granularity is optimal versus finer scheduling, whether the layer-size threshold (10⁴) generalizes to novel architectures, and whether the theoretical convergence bound can be tightened to formally distinguish epoch-based from layer-based strategies. The paper also notes that in very high-bandwidth data-center networks, compression overhead can negate throughput gains, limiting the practical benefit to bandwidth-limited or federated scenarios.
