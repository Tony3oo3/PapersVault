## Problem

Current VLMs struggle with spatial reasoning because existing approaches treat it as a monolithic capability, learning directly from question-answer pairs without establishing the hierarchical foundations of perception and understanding. The paper identifies a perception-reasoning gap: models possess latent reasoning capacity but lack the perceptual grounding to activate it, and existing datasets are fragmented across modalities without systematic coverage from basic perception to complex reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262586/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB single images, sets of 8 synchronized multi-view images of the same scene, and 1–4 minute video clips at 24 fps, paired with a text question. Stage 1 outputs 2D bounding boxes in JSON format; Stage 2 produces either a multiple-choice letter or a numerical value (distance in meters, size in centimeters); Stage 3 wraps the answer in structured <think>… and <answer>…</answer> tags, with the thinking block containing explicit chain-of-thought reasoning. The dataset spans seven spatial dimensions (relative direction, relative distance, absolute distance, object size, counting, room size, appearance order) across three modalities.

## Method

SpatialLadder builds spatial intelligence on Qwen2.5-VL-3B through a three-stage progressive curriculum over a 26,610-sample dataset (SpatialLadder-26k) constructed from ScanNet 3D reconstructions and SR-91k videos via a standardized 3D-to-2D annotation pipeline. Stage 1 performs supervised fine-tuning on ~6k object-localization samples to ground spatial concepts in concrete visual evidence (bounding-box prediction). Stage 2 extends to multi-dimensional spatial reasoning tasks across single-image, multi-view, and video modalities, teaching size estimation, distance judgment, and orientation analysis. Stage 3 applies GRPO reinforcement learning with a dual reward (format compliance + task-specific accuracy: exact match for MCQ, graduated relative-error scoring for numerical answers) and a cold-start phase of 1,255 rejection-sampled chain-of-thought examples, enabling the model to generate explicit reasoning chains that surpass the supervised-training performance ceiling.

## Results

SpatialLadder-3B achieves 62.3% overall accuracy on in-domain spatial reasoning benchmarks (VSI-Bench, SPBench-SI, SPBench-MV), surpassing the base Qwen2.5-VL-3B by 23.4%, GPT-4o by 20.8%, and Gemini-2.0-Flash by 10.1%. On the proposed SPBench-SI and SPBench-MV, it reaches 70.2% and 70.9% respectively (+29.9% and +34.3% over base). On out-of-domain benchmarks, SpatialLadder achieves 45.0% overall, outperforming GPT-4o (42.7%) with notable gains on ViewSpatial-Bench (+8.6%) and MindCube (+10.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpatialLadder-3B outperforms all 10 baselines on overall in-domain accuracy (62.3%). The closest competitors are VILASR-7B (51.1%), Spatial-MLLM-4B (50.9%), and SpaceR-7B (50.8%), all of which are 7B-parameter models; SpatialLadder surpasses them by 11.2–11.5% despite being only 3B. Against proprietary models, it beats GPT-4o (41.5%) by 20.8% and Gemini-2.0-Flash (50.5%) by 10.1%. On out-of-domain benchmarks, SpatialLadder (45.0%) also surpasses GPT-4o (42.7%) and all open-source baselines, with the largest gains on ViewSpatial-Bench (44.2% vs. 37.9% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and MindCube (43.4% vs. 33.2% for Qwen2.5-VL-3B). Notably, SpatialLadder matches Spatial-MLLM-4B on VSI-Bench (45.7% vs. 47.3%) without requiring a specialized 3D encoder, while dramatically outperforming it on SPBench-SI (70.2% vs. 43.7%) and SPBench-MV (70.9% vs. 61.8%). General capability is largely preserved, with only −0.9% on MMBench and −1.2% on MMMU relative to the base model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two lines of prior spatial reasoning work: (1) RL-based approaches like SpaceR and R1-Zero-VSI that apply reinforcement learning directly to spatial QA without establishing perceptual foundations, and (2) architecture-modification approaches like Spatial-MLLM that inject 3D encoders. The key departure is a progressive training paradigm—perception (localization) → understanding (multi-dimensional spatial tasks) → reasoning (GRPO with chain-of-thought)—that treats spatial intelligence as hierarchical rather than monolithic. Methodologically, it builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for the RL stage and chain-of-thought prompting (Wei et al., 2022) for structured reasoning, but wraps them in a curriculum that prior spatial reasoning papers have not attempted. The dataset construction pipeline, leveraging ScanNet 3D reconstructions to generate consistent annotations across single-image, multi-view, and video modalities, is a practical contribution distinct from prior fragmented datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The perception-reasoning gap diagnosis (models gain ~9.5% from perceptual hints, indicating latent reasoning blocked by poor grounding) provides a concrete, actionable explanation for why prior end-to-end spatial training underperforms, and the progressive framework offers a principled remedy. The finding that 26k carefully curated samples outperform 151k and 120k datasets on VSI-Bench suggests data quality and task hierarchy matter more than scale for spatial reasoning, which could reshape how the community invests in dataset construction. However, limitations are significant: experiments are confined to a 3B model, the dataset is biased toward ScanNet indoor scenes, and the most impressive gains (70%+ on SPBench-SI/MV) are on the authors' own benchmarks. Open questions include whether the progressive paradigm scales to 7B+ models, generalizes to outdoor/urban environments, and whether the fixed sequential structure is truly optimal versus adaptive curricula.
