## Problem

Comparing probability distributions in mixed-curvature spaces (Cartesian products of Euclidean, spherical, and hyperbolic components) remains underexplored: KL divergence is not a true metric and fails on disjoint supports, full optimal transport scales supercubically, and Sliced Wasserstein's one-dimensional projections cannot capture the heterogeneous geometric structure of the ambient space. The Tree-Sliced Wasserstein framework, which replaces linear projections with richer tree structures, has not been extended to mixed-curvature settings where rays must span components of differing curvature.

## Input / Output

The method takes two discrete probability measures ν, μ ∈ P(M) on a mixed-curvature space M = ∏_{j=1}^m C_{K_j}^{d_j}, each represented as a weighted sum of Dirac deltas at n support points, along with hyperparameters L (Monte Carlo trees), k (rays per tree), and a splitting map α: M → Δ_{k-1}. It produces a scalar MCTSW distance via Monte Carlo averaging of closed-form W_1 values on randomly sampled tree systems. A key structural constraint is that each ray differs from the root in exactly one component and all components share dimension d, which reduces the tree sampling space to M × [m]^k × (M_ε)^k and enables a single sort per tree rather than one per ray.

## Method

MCTSW constructs a family of mixed-curvature trees, each consisting of k geodesic rays emanating from a common root x ∈ M, equipped with a tree metric where intra-ray distance is |t_i − t_j| and inter-ray distance is t_i + t_j (routing through the root). A Radon transform projects each measure onto a tree by assigning each point z a coordinate d_M(z, x) on every ray and distributing its mass across rays via a softmax of geodesic distances to the rays. The W_1 on each tree admits a closed-form expression as a sum over edges of edge-length times absolute mass difference in the subtree, reducing computation to a single sort of the n projected coordinates per tree. The final MCTSW distance integrates this W_1 over the space of all k-ray trees with a probability measure σ, approximated by L Monte Carlo samples. Theoretical analysis establishes that the Radon transform is well-defined, linear, and injective, and that MCTSW satisfies all metric axioms on P(M).

## Results

On the gradient flow task (matching a 6-component WND mixture on E₂×P₂×D₂), MCTSW achieves log W₂ = −3.65, dramatically outperforming the best baseline Prod-SW (0.31) by over 3.9 units. On CIFAR-10 VAE, MCTSW-VAE attains the lowest test BCE of 0.6000±0.0002, beating the best constant-curvature SW baseline (HSW-VAE, 0.6012) and the mixed-curvature KL baseline (M-VAE, 0.6419). On Cora graph SSL, MCTSW-SSGE reaches 79.86±0.45% accuracy, surpassing the SSGE baseline (79.55±0.35%) and all constant-curvature TSW variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCTSW decisively outperforms all three gradient-flow baselines: SW_ambient (log W₂ = 0.33), Prod-TSW (0.34), and Prod-SW (0.31), achieving −3.65, indicating far superior convergence to the target mixture. In the VAE setting, MCTSW-VAE (0.6000) beats every constant-curvature SW variant—SWAE (0.6043), STSW-VAE (0.6026), and HSW-VAE (0.6012)—as well as all KL-based baselines including M-VAE (0.6419), confirming both the superiority of tree-sliced over line-sliced projections and the benefit of mixed-curvature geometry. On Cora graph SSL, MCTSW-SSGE (79.86%) edges out the original SSGE (79.55%) and clearly surpasses E-TSW-SSGE (77.85%), S-TSW-SSGE (78.33%), and H-TSW-SSGE (75.10%), with the hyperbolic variant performing worst, suggesting that a single constant curvature is insufficient for capturing the graph's structure. Overall, MCTSW wins on every primary metric across all three experimental settings, with the largest margins observed in the gradient flow task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: the Tree-Sliced Wasserstein (TSW) framework, which replaces 1D linear projections with tree metric structures to capture richer geometric structure (Tran et al., 2025a; Le et al., 2019), and Mixed-Curvature Spaces (MCS), which model data as Cartesian products of Euclidean, spherical, and hyperbolic components (Skopek et al., 2020; Gu et al., 2019). Prior sliced Wasserstein variants have been adapted to individual constant-curvature manifolds (spherical SW by Bonet et al., 2023a; hyperbolic SW by Bonet et al., 2022a), and TSW has been extended to spherical spaces (Tran et al., 2025c), but no prior work combines tree-based projections with the heterogeneous geometry of product manifolds. The key technical departure is the construction of a Radon transform and tree metric on geodesic rays in mixed-curvature space, yielding a closed-form Wasserstein computation that respects all component curvatures simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MCTSW provides the first computationally tractable (closed-form, O(Ln log n + Ldmnk)) distributional metric for mixed-curvature spaces, filling a gap where KL divergence is inapplicable and full OT is prohibitively expensive. The empirical finding that mixed-curvature latent spaces with MCTSW consistently outperform constant-curvature alternatives (Euclidean, spherical, hyperbolic) across VAEs and graph SSL suggests that the product geometry is not merely a convenience but captures structure that single-curvature spaces miss. Open questions include numerical stability of gyrovector operations at scale, whether the star-tree topology is optimal versus more complex topologies (the paper's chain-topology ablation is suggestive but on a small configuration), and how the method behaves in higher-dimensional or higher-curvature-magnitude regimes where the ablation shows performance degrading.
