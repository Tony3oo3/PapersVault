## Problem

Conventional semi-autoregressive (semi-AR) decoding for diffusion LLMs enforces a fixed block size, which causes two fundamental issues: late decoding overhead (high-confidence tokens outside the current block are unnecessarily delayed) and premature decoding error (low-confidence tokens inside the block are committed too early, propagating errors through block-level autoregressive dependencies). No prior work has systematically investigated how block size interacts with the model's confidence dynamics during denoising.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a text prompt with a generation budget L (typically 512 tokens) and produces a fully unmasked text sequence via iterative denoise-sample cycles. At runtime, before each block is sampled, the scheduler inspects the current predicted sequence and per-position confidence scores to determine the block length, requiring no additional model parameters or training. The output is structured as a sequence of variable-length blocks decoded autoregressively, with tokens within each block decoded in parallel via threshold-based dynamic sampling.

## Method

AdaBlock-dLLM is a training-free, plug-and-play scheduler that adaptively sets block size by aligning block boundaries with semantic steps. The key insight comes from a statistical analysis of confidence dynamics: a 'volatility band' (VB) region emerges during decoding where confidence fluctuates over time and encodes local semantic structure, while delimiter tokens (e.g., newlines) mark semantic step boundaries with characteristic confidence drops. At each block boundary, the scheduler searches a sliding window of predicted tokens for the highest-confidence delimiter; if its confidence exceeds a threshold τ_D, the block size is set to that delimiter's position, otherwise it falls back to a default size. This procedure is inserted as a single additional step (Algorithm 1) into the existing semi-AR decoding loop, requiring no retraining and integrating seamlessly with KV caching and dynamic sampling.

## Results

On GSM8K with LLaDA-Instruct (B₀=64), AdaBlock-dLLM with KV caching (+Ada+Cache) achieves 80.7% accuracy, a +5.3% improvement over the Fast-dLLM +Cache baseline (75.4%). Without caching, +Ada reaches 80.6% on GSM8K (B₀=32), a +3.0% gain over Dynamic (77.6%). On HumanEval (LLaDA-Instruct, B₀=16), +Ada+Cache achieves 49.4% pass@1 versus 45.1% for +Cache (+4.3%). Throughput remains comparable across methods, with the product of TPS and NFE staying approximately constant (~8.2×10³), confirming that accuracy gains come without speed penalty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Fast-dLLM (Wu et al., 2025), which provides both the Dynamic (threshold-based dynamic sampling) and +Cache (Dynamic + DualCache) configurations. AdaBlock-dLLM consistently outperforms Dynamic on GSM8K across all three models and block sizes (e.g., +3.0% on LLaDA-Instruct B₀=32, +2.2% on B₀=64), and outperforms +Cache on GSM8K by up to +5.3% (LLaDA-Instruct B₀=64). On HumanEval, +Ada+Cache achieves the largest gains (+4.3% on LLaDA-Instruct B₀=16, +3.0% on LLaDA-1.5 B₀=32), while on MATH the improvements are marginal (±0.5%). Gains are most pronounced for LLaDA models (trained from scratch, exhibiting greater local stochasticity) and diminish for Dream (adapted from AR models with stronger global autoregressive order). The method is Pareto-optimal on GSM8K and MATH in the accuracy-throughput trade-off (Figure 6), meaning no baseline simultaneously achieves higher accuracy at equal or greater throughput.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the semi-autoregressive (semi-AR) decoding paradigm introduced by Block Diffusion (Arriola et al., 2025) and the dynamic sampling + KV caching framework of Fast-dLLM (Wu et al., 2025), which together form the standard inference pipeline for diffusion LLMs like LLaDA and Dream. It departs from prior work by identifying that the fixed block size—a universally adopted but never systematically questioned hyperparameter—introduces two distinct failure modes (late decoding overhead and premature decoding error), and by grounding an adaptive scheduler in a statistical analysis of confidence dynamics (the 'volatility band') rather than in learned or hand-tuned schedules. The approach is explicitly training-free and plug-and-play, positioning it as an orthogonal complement to existing acceleration techniques rather than a replacement for the denoiser or the sampling criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that any existing semi-AR dLLM inference pipeline can adopt AdaBlock-dLLM with two hyperparameters (delimiter set and threshold) to recover accuracy lost to fixed-block misalignment, especially under KV caching where the gains are largest (up to 5.3%). The volatility-band analysis provides a reusable diagnostic lens for understanding where and why dLLM decoding is uncertain, which the authors hope will inform future training objectives that better preserve semantic coherence. Open questions include automating delimiter selection, extending the approach to multimodal dLLMs, and whether semantics-aware training (rather than inference-time scheduling) could eliminate the need for adaptive blocks altogether. A key limitation is that the method cannot compensate for unreliable denoiser predictions, so its benefit is bounded by the underlying model quality.
