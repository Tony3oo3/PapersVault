## Problem

Despite rapid progress in generative driving world models, the field lacks a rigorous, unified benchmark: existing evaluations rely on generic video metrics that ignore safety-critical imaging factors, rarely quantify trajectory plausibility, neglect agent-level temporal consistency, and overlook ego-trajectory controllability. Additionally, prior evaluation datasets are heavily biased toward clear-weather, daytime, single-region driving, failing to cover the diversity needed for real-world deployment.

## Input / Output

Each benchmark sample provides a front-view RGB image, a Qwen-generated scene description, and (in the ego-conditioned track) a ground-truth ego trajectory as conditioning. Models generate 100-frame driving videos, which are then scored across four metric dimensions: distribution (FVD for video, a novel FTD for trajectories), quality (CLIP-IQA+ subjective score, IEEE P2020 MMP objective score, and a composite trajectory quality score), temporal consistency (adaptive DINOv3 video consistency, YOLOv10+SAM2 agent appearance consistency, VLM-judged agent abnormal disappearance, and speed/acceleration trajectory consistency), and trajectory alignment (ADE and DTW against the conditioning path). The dataset comprises 400 samples split evenly between an open-domain track (internet-sourced, 7 world regions) and an ego-conditioned track (aggregated from Zod, DrivingDojo, COVLA, nuPlan, and WOMD).

## Method

DrivingGen introduces a four-dimensional evaluation suite specifically designed for driving world models. For distribution, it extends FVD with a novel Fréchet Trajectory Distance (FTD) that encodes trajectories via the MTR agent_polyline_encoder and computes Gaussian Fréchet distance in trajectory embedding space. For quality, it combines CLIP-IQA+ perceptual scoring with the IEEE P2020 Modulation Mitigation Probability (MMP) to capture PWM-induced flicker, plus a composite trajectory quality metric aggregating comfort (jerk/acceleration/yaw-rate peaks), motion (mean-speed log mapping), and curvature (RMS curvature) submetrics via geometric mean. For temporal consistency, it uses adaptive optical-flow-based downsampling before DINOv3 feature comparison to prevent static videos from gaming the score, tracks individual agents with YOLOv10+SAM2 for appearance consistency, and prompts Cosmos-Reason1 (a VLM) to classify agent disappearances as natural or abnormal. Trajectories are recovered from generated videos via a custom SLAM+UniDepthV2 pipeline with a constant-velocity extrapolation failure-recovery strategy that guarantees a complete trajectory for every video. The benchmark evaluates 14 models spanning closed-source, open-source general, physics-based, and driving-specific categories, revealing that no current model simultaneously achieves strong photorealism and physically consistent, controllable motion.

## Results

On the Ego-Conditioned Track, Kling 2.1 (closed-source) achieves the best overall average rank (1st), with FVD of 320.5, FTD of 23.74, and subjective quality of 0.5468. Vista (driving-specific, 2.5B) achieves the best trajectory alignment with ADE of 19.70 and DTW of 1216, significantly outperforming general models (e.g., Kling 2.1: ADE 29.97, DTW 2310). On the Open-Domain Track, Kling 2.1 again ranks 1st (FVD 693.4, FTD 26.73, subjective quality 0.5538), while LTX-Video achieves the lowest FVD (648.2) among open-source models. No single model simultaneously achieves top visual quality and top trajectory fidelity, revealing a fundamental trade-off across all 14 evaluated models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Closed-source models Kling 2.1 and Gen-3 Alpha Turbo dominate overall rankings (1st and 2nd/3rd on both tracks), excelling in visual quality (subjective quality 0.5468–0.5740), agent consistency, and agent disappearance rates (0.9712 and 0.9466), but lag in trajectory alignment (ADE 29.97–33.39, DTW 2310–2749). Driving-specific models Vista and UniFuture achieve the best trajectory alignment (ADE 19.70/20.21, DTW 1216/1352), outperforming all general models by 30–50% on ADE, but sacrifice visual fidelity (subjective quality 0.4006–0.4146 vs. 0.5468–0.5740 for closed-source). Among open-source general models, Wan2.2-I2V achieves the best FVD (194.4 on ego-conditioned) and ranks 2nd overall, while LTX-Video achieves the best FVD on the open-domain track (648.2). Cosmos-Predict2 (physics-based) strikes a middle ground, ranking 4th on ego-conditioned with competitive ADE (22.38) and moderate visual quality. The key finding is that no model simultaneously achieves top visual quality and top trajectory fidelity: the best visual models (Kling, Gen-3) have 30–50% higher ADE than the best trajectory models (Vista, UniFuture), and vice versa.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of driving world model evaluation (Vista, GEM, DrivingDojo, ACT-Bench) by unifying and expanding the evaluation dimensions that prior works addressed in isolation. It repurposes the MTR trajectory encoder from motion prediction to define a novel Fréchet Trajectory Distance (FTD), paralleling FVD for the visual domain, and combines off-the-shelf tools (DINOv3, YOLOv10, SAM2, VLMs) into a multi-granularity temporal consistency suite that no prior driving benchmark attempted. Conceptually, it departs from the 'FVD + ADE' paradigm dominant in prior driving world model papers by arguing that distributional closeness must be assessed jointly over both video and trajectory spaces, and that agent-level (not just scene-level) consistency is essential for safety-critical evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DrivingGen fills a critical gap as the first comprehensive, multi-dimensional benchmark for generative driving world models, and its 14-model evaluation reveals a fundamental trade-off: no current model simultaneously achieves high photorealism and physically consistent, controllable motion. This quantified gap provides a clear research target for the field. Key limitations include the 400-sample scale (insufficient for long-tail scenarios), single front-view camera input, open-loop-only evaluation, and absence of counterfactual or downstream-task metrics. Future work on closed-loop simulation, multi-view generation, and scene-level controllability will be needed before these models can support safe deployment.
