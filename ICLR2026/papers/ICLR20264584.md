## Problem

Existing knowledge editing methods are designed for dense Transformers where all parameters are active per input, making them ill-suited for Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures that dominate modern scalable LLMs. Naively adapting dense editors to MoEs faces a tripartite challenge: updating all experts is computationally prohibitive (e.g., 128× cost), inter-expert coupling dilutes or distorts individual edits, and parameter perturbations induce cascading routing distribution shifts in downstream layers that destabilize the model's learned expert specialization. MoEEdit is the first routing-stable framework for parameter-modifying knowledge editing in MoE LLMs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes factual edit triples (subject, relation, new object) along with the MoE model's per-expert down-projection matrices and a preservation set of prompts whose behavior must remain unchanged. For each edit, it extracts post-gate keys and router weights from the target MoE layer, then produces per-expert weight updates Δ_n = Δ̂_n P_n that are applied only to the down-projection W_down^(n) of each expert. The output is a routing-stable edited model where the Top-K expert selection in all downstream layers is preserved (measured by Jaccard similarity >88% and KL divergence ~0.02 on the preservation set).

## Method

MoEEdit reparameterizes each expert update as Δ_n = Δ̂_n P_n, where P_n is an orthogonal projector onto the null space of the preservation-feature covariance K_n^0 K_n^{0T} for expert n. This construction guarantees that updates produce zero perturbation on preservation prompts, which by the first-order softmax linearization (δg ≈ J_sm · E^T · δu) suppresses routing distribution shift in all subsequent MoE layers. The resulting projected objective is a regularized multi-output least-squares problem coupled across experts via router weights g_{i,n}. Rather than solving the intractable (N·d_k)×(N·d_k) global system, MoEEdit applies randomized block coordinate descent: each expert is treated as a block, and the subproblem reduces to a well-conditioned d_k×d_k ridge least-squares solve with closed-form update Δ̂_n* = B_n M_n^{-1}. This decoupling makes the per-expert cost O(|E|·d_k² + d_k³), scaling linearly with expert hidden size rather than total expert count, and converges in ≤10 passes.

## Results

On Qwen3-30B-A3B, MoEEdit achieves 99.30% efficacy, 94.10% generalization, and 80.97% specificity on COUNTERFACT (Utility 91.46), and 84.47% efficacy, 78.01% generalization, and 42.82% specificity on ZsRE (Utility 68.43). On GPT-OSS-20B, it reaches 95.90% efficacy and 81.09% specificity on COUNTERFACT (Utility 73.70) and 81.68% efficacy and 68.44% generalization on ZsRE (Utility 60.89). In routing stability, MoEEdit maintains a Jaccard Routing Similarity above 88% across all layer windows, compared to 16–53% for baselines, with an average KL divergence of only 0.02 between pre- and post-edit routing distributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoEEdit decisively outperforms all four baselines (FT, FT-L, AdaLoRA, UnKE) on overall Utility across both models and both datasets. On Qwen3-30B-A3B COUNTERFACT, MoEEdit leads the strongest baseline UnKE by +17.03 Utility (91.46 vs. 73.43), gaining +10.0 efficacy, +11.25 generalization, and +32.82 specificity. On ZsRE for the same model, the gap widens dramatically: MoEEdit achieves 68.43 Utility versus 42.86 for FT-L and 28.84 for UnKE, with efficacy gains exceeding +40 points. On GPT-OSS-20B, MoEEdit wins on efficacy (+12.5 over FT) and specificity (+7.2 over FT) on COUNTERFACT, though FT retains a generalization advantage (58.40 vs. 44.10); on ZsRE, MoEEdit leads UnKE by +20.23 Utility (60.89 vs. 40.66). In routing stability (Table 2), MoEEdit's Jaccard Routing Similarity exceeds 88% in every layer window, while baselines range from 16% (AdaLoRA) to 53% (FT-L), confirming that the null-space projection is the key differentiator for preserving expert routing patterns.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoEEdit extends the locate-then-edit paradigm (ROME, MEMIT) and the null-space projection idea from AlphaEdit to the Mixture-of-Experts setting, where the key departure is recognizing that expert parameter updates perturb downstream router inputs and cause cascading routing distribution shifts. Unlike LEMoE, which attaches an external MoE adaptor to a frozen dense backbone (parameter-preserving), MoEEdit directly modifies intrinsic MoE expert weights (parameter-modifying) and formulates the problem as a block-structured optimization over per-expert null-space-constrained updates. The randomized BCD solver is a natural but necessary adaptation: the global closed-form solution, while elegant, becomes intractable at realistic MoE scales (N=128 experts, d_k in the thousands), so the paper exploits the per-expert block structure to reduce each subproblem to a small d_k×d_k ridge solve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MoE architectures (Qwen3, GPT-OSS, GLaM) become the dominant scaling strategy for LLMs, a routing-stable editing framework is essential for maintaining factual accuracy without destabilizing the learned expert specialization that gives MoE models their efficiency. The paper's central insight—that only the component of a perturbation lying in the span of router embeddings affects routing, and that per-expert null-space projection can suppress this by construction—provides a principled, generalizable tool for any future sparse-architecture editing method. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-hop or relational edits, how it interacts with continual/sequential editing at scale beyond 1,000 edits, and whether the layer-selection heuristic (editing layers 3–7) generalizes across architectures. The specificity gap on ZsRE/GPT-OSS-20B (32.55 vs. AdaLoRA's 33.60) suggests the method still trades some locality for efficacy in certain regimes.
