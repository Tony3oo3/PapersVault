## Problem

Machine unlearning methods leave residual traces in post-unlearning models that can be exploited to recover supposedly forgotten data, yet prior reconstruction attacks are restricted to linear models (closed-form parameter analysis) or white-box instance-level recovery that fails under multiple deletions. No existing framework systematically exploits unlearning traces on deep architectures while supporting diverse access levels and both instance- and distribution-level recovery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261154/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

RETRACE takes a paired set of pre- and post-unlearning models (f+, f−) and a pretrained generator (DCGAN for 32×32/224×224/28×28 images, or GPT-2-small for text) as inputs, with an optional public dataset D_pub for initializing candidate latents. The adversary queries the models at black-box (prediction outputs), grey-box (task loss), or white-box (gradients) access to compute trace scores. Outputs are reconstructed images or text at the instance level (single best candidate by trace score) and at the distribution level (top-k highest-scoring samples approximating the forgotten class distribution), evaluated via MSE, cosine similarity, success rate, FID, KL divergence, and BLEU/MMD.

## Method

RETRACE formulates unlearned data recovery as a reinforcement learning problem in three stages: (1) trace extraction computes prediction-level (ℓ₂ distance), loss-level (absolute difference), and gradient-level (cosine distance) discrepancies between f+ and f− on candidate inputs; (2) RL-guided generation uses a PPO actor-critic policy that transforms latent vectors z₀~N(0,I) into new codes z₁=π_θ(z₀) fed to a fixed generator G, with the normalized trace score s(x) serving as the reward and a critic network V_ψ providing a learnable baseline for advantage estimation; (3) candidate selection ranks n independently generated samples by trace score to pick the best instance or top-k distribution. Theoretically, the RL objective converges to an exponential-tilted distribution π*∝p₀·exp(s/τ) that provably amplifies regions with strong unlearning traces, with instance-level success bounded by 1−(1−p*)^k and distribution-level error decomposed into a bias term controlled by the separability margin Δ and a variance term decaying with k. The framework generalizes across ResNet-18/34 (vision) and Distil-BERT (text) architectures, with Distil-BERT being the largest model attacked to date.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 under approximate unlearning with white-box access, RETRACE achieves a 73.1% instance-level success rate with MSE 0.17 and cosine similarity 0.50, the best across all three datasets. At the distribution level, it reduces FID to 99.1 and KL divergence to 2.53 on CIFAR-100 (approximate, white-box), substantially outperforming the black-box setting (FID 142.1, KL 3.62). On the text task (AG News with Distil-BERT), RETRACE improves BLEU from 2.8 (black-box) to 4.6 (white-box) for the Business class, a near-100% gain over the weakest access level, while reducing MMD from 0.46 to 0.32.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RETRACE is compared against two state-of-the-art reconstruction attacks: UIA (Hu et al., IEEE S&P 2024) and HRec (Bertran et al., NeurIPS 2024), both restricted to white-box access. RETRACE dominates both baselines across all three datasets and both unlearning settings on every metric. On CIFAR-100 (approximate unlearning), RETRACE achieves MSE 0.17 vs. 0.33 (UIA) and 0.32 (HRec), and SR 73.1% vs. 59.5% and 43.0% respectively. The gap widens on Food-101, where HRec struggles most (SR 30.2%, MSE 0.45) while RETRACE reaches SR 65.3% and MSE 0.16. At the distribution level, RETRACE achieves FID 99–142 and KL 2.0–3.6, whereas UIA and HRec yield FID 139–242 and KL 3.8–5.6, confirming that RL-guided trace exploitation produces substantially more faithful reconstructions than gradient-based or closed-form parameter-shift approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reconstruction-attack-on-unlearning line initiated by Bertran et al. (2024, closed-form parameter recovery for linear models) and Hu et al. (2024, gradient/update-based inversion restricted to white-box instance-level recovery) by formulating the problem as a reinforcement learning task over a pretrained generator. The RL-agent-controls-generator paradigm is borrowed from Sarmad et al. (2019, RL-GAN-Net for point-cloud completion), but here the reward signal is the unlearning trace (prediction/loss/gradient discrepancy between pre- and post-unlearning models) rather than a reconstruction loss. The key departure is that RETRACE supports both instance-level and distribution-level recovery across black-, grey-, and white-box access, and scales to deep architectures (ResNet-18/34, Distil-BERT) where prior methods either fail or are inapplicable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results demonstrate that even exact unlearning (full retraining) leaves exploitable residual traces on deep networks, undermining the privacy guarantees that motivate unlearning in GDPR-compliant deployments. The theoretical characterization of the RL optimum as an exponential-tilted distribution provides a principled explanation for why trace-guided exploration concentrates on forgotten regions, and the bias-variance bound for distribution-level recovery gives concrete sample-complexity guarantees. Open questions include: whether stronger unlearning (e.g., differential-privacy-based) can close the trace gap, how the attack scales to multi-billion-parameter LLMs, and what defenses (if any) can simultaneously preserve model utility while eliminating the pre/post differential signal. A practical limitation is the reliance on a pretrained generator whose domain alignment with the forgotten data affects reconstruction fidelity.
