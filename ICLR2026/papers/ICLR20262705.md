## Problem

Existing ANN-to-SNN conversion frameworks for LLMs rely on rate or temporal coding that discretizes activations into uniformly partitioned intervals, ignoring the non-uniform, layer-dependent activation distributions observed in practice. This distribution misalignment introduces latent conversion error that degrades spiking LLM performance, yet no prior method explicitly aligns discrete value allocation with the underlying activation density.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262705/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pretrained ANN weights (LLaMA-2/3, Qwen2, CLIP) and activation distributions estimated by downsampling hidden-layer activations from a batch of input text. It produces a fully spike-driven model where SNN neurons with multi-granularity phase coding replace floating-point linear layers, outputting spike sequences over T timesteps (T ∈ {6, 8, 10}) that approximate the original continuous activations. No model weight fine-tuning is required; only per-neuron parameters (reset strength h, threshold θ, and multi-granularity bases B) are learned, and the conversion completes in roughly 2 minutes versus hours for direct-training baselines.

## Method

The core contribution is a distribution-aware multi-granularity phase coding scheme that extends conventional single-base phase coding by introducing multiple learnable bases {B₁, …, Bₙ}, each governing a contiguous block of timesteps, so that discrete values are allocated non-uniformly across the activation range. Motivated by Bennett's integral from information theory (optimal point density λ*(x) ∝ [p(x)]^{1/3}), the method minimizes expected conversion error by concentrating more representable discrete values in high-density activation regions. Training uses an alternating optimization algorithm that decouples updates to {h, θ} (via sigmoid surrogate gradients) from updates to B (via true gradients), operating solely on pre-sampled activation data without forward/backward propagation through network layers. A convergence guarantee is established under Lipschitz-gradient and Polyak-Łojasiewicz assumptions, showing linear convergence of the objective up to a smoothing-dependent error constant. An adaptive granularity allocation mechanism, built on a DARTS-style differentiable search, automatically selects how timesteps are partitioned among the n bases.

## Results

On LLaMA-2-7B (T=8), the proposed method (Grain=2) achieves an average perplexity of 7.16 and average accuracy of 67.17%, closely matching the ANN baseline (PPL 5.67, ACC 67.25%) while drastically outperforming LAS (PPL 31.73) and SpikedAttention (PPL 19.91). On LLaMA-3-8B (T=8), the method achieves PPL 7.22 and ACC 71.32% (Grain=2), whereas SpikeLLM completely fails (PPL >100) and LAS reaches only PPL 8.16. The spiking LLM also achieves a 42% reduction in MAC/AC energy consumption (10.44 J vs. 18.00 J for ANN) and requires only ~2 minutes of neuron training versus 5–6 hours for SpikeLLM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SpikeLLM (ICLR 2025), the proposed method achieves comparable or better perplexity on LLaMA-2-7B (7.16 vs. 6.10) while recovering ANN-level accuracy (67.17% vs. 64.80%); on LLaMA-3-8B the gap is dramatic, with SpikeLLM failing entirely (PPL >100) while Ours reaches 7.22. Against LAS (arXiv 2025), which the authors identify as a special unoptimized instance of their framework, the proposed neuron training algorithm reduces perplexity from 31.73 to 7.16 on LLaMA-2-7B (T=8) and from 8.16 to 7.22 on LLaMA-3-8B. Against SpikedAttention (NeurIPS 2024), Ours improves perplexity from 19.91 to 7.16 (LLaMA-2-7B) and from 10.93 to 7.22 (LLaMA-3-8B), while also matching or exceeding its accuracy. Against TTFSFormer (ICML 2025), Ours achieves comparable accuracy (67.17% vs. 66.87%) with far fewer timesteps (T=8 vs. T=128) and lower perplexity (7.16 vs. 12.71). In all comparisons, the proposed method requires only ~2 minutes of training time, versus 5–6 hours for SpikeLLM, making it orders of magnitude more cost-efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends conventional phase coding (Kim et al., 2018) by introducing multiple learnable bases to create a multi-granularity scheme, and grounds the design in information-theoretic quantization theory (Gray & Neuhoff, 2002) to justify distribution-aware allocation of discrete values. It departs from the uniform discretization assumed by rate and temporal coding in prior spiking LLMs (SpikeLLM, TTFSFormer, LAS, SpikedAttention), which the authors identify as a source of latent conversion error due to distribution misalignment. For handling nonlinear operations (softmax, SiLU, activation-activation multiplication) in the spiking transformer, the paper adopts techniques from SpikeZipTF (You et al., 2024), and leverages the DARTS differentiable search framework (Liu et al., 2018) for adaptive granularity allocation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 42% reduction in MAC/AC energy consumption combined with near-ANN accuracy at only 6–10 timesteps makes spiking LLMs practically viable on neuromorphic hardware, and the ~2-minute conversion time (vs. hours for SpikeLLM) dramatically lowers the barrier to deploying spiking models. The theoretical convergence guarantee for the alternating optimization algorithm provides a level of rigor uncommon in ANN-to-SNN conversion literature. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even smaller timesteps (T < 6), how it performs on truly event-driven neuromorphic chips rather than GPU-simulated SNNs, and whether the distribution-aware principle generalizes to other quantization or compression settings beyond spiking.
