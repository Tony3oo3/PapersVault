## Problem

LLMs remain vulnerable to jailbreak attacks that disguise malicious intent within complex prompts, causing models to produce harmful outputs. Existing defenses—post-hoc detection, prompt engineering, and inference-time reasoning—either fail against adaptive attacks, introduce high over-refusal rates, or require specialized reasoning models. The paper identifies a key insight: malicious intent is often hidden in the query but becomes apparent during answer generation, yet no prior method systematically exploits this observation through structured safety reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single user text query (potentially a jailbreak prompt) and produces a structured response consisting of three components: a concise intended answer summary (1–5 sentences), a safety analysis referencing specific policy provisions, and a final answer or refusal. Only the content after the closing safety_check tag is exposed to the user; the intermediate reasoning is hidden. For sensitive queries (e.g., self-harm), the output is a supportive 'safe completion' response rather than a blunt refusal, distinguishing it from post-hoc detectors that can only reject.

## Method

The core contribution is the 'Answer-Then-Check' strategy, where the model first generates a direct answer summary (even for harmful queries, using uncensored models like Dolphin for data generation), then critically evaluates that summary against explicit safety policies before deciding to release or refuse. The ReSA dataset (80,426 samples) is constructed in three stages: collecting vanilla and adversarial queries from WildJailbreak plus PAIR/PAP/GPTFuzzer, generating intended answer summaries, and synthesizing policy-grounded safety analyses with Llama3.3-70B-Instruct. Training proceeds via SFT on this dataset, with an RL variant (GRPO) applying binary safety, refusal, and format rewards to further harden the model. An Adaptive variant dynamically bypasses the safety-check steps for normal queries by mixing in 1,000 instruction-tuning samples, achieving base-model efficiency on benign inputs while preserving jailbreak defense.

## Results

On StrongREJECT evaluated by Llama-Guard-3-8B, ReSA-SFT (Llama3.1-8B-Instruct) achieves 0.9046 average Defense Success Rate (DSR) across 8 jailbreak methods, while ReSA-RL reaches 0.9932. In the broader comparison (Table 4), ReSA-RL attains 0.9920 average safety (DSR on PAIR-GPT and PAP) and 0.9727 average over-refusal accuracy, simultaneously achieving the best safety and lowest over-refusal among all 13 compared defenses. On general reasoning benchmarks, ReSA-SFT (Llama) retains 59.78% average accuracy (MATH500/HumanEval/MMLU) versus 61.85% for the base model, confirming minimal capability degradation. Notably, even 500 training samples yield safety performance comparable to the full 80K dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReSA-SFT outperforms post-hoc LlamaGuard detection by +12.34 DSR points (0.9046 vs. 0.7812) and STAIR-DPO by +8.43 points (0.9046 vs. 0.8203) on StrongREJECT with Llama-Guard-3-8B; ReSA-RL extends this to 0.9932. Against the closest fine-tuning baseline, OpenAI-Deliberative Alignment (0.9000 safety avg), ReSA-SFT leads by +3.30 points while also matching its over-refusal performance. ReSA-RL decisively surpasses all SOTA general LLMs (gpt-4.1: 0.4297, deepseek-v3: 0.3531) and SOTA reasoning LLMs with safety reflection (deepseek-r1: 0.7604, o4-mini: 0.8019) on safety, while simultaneously achieving the best over-refusal accuracy (0.9727) — a Pareto frontier no other method reaches. WJ-SFT (0.7173) and GuardReasoner (0.5671) are substantially outperformed, confirming that the Answer-Then-Check reasoning structure, not merely more training data, drives the gains. On over-refusal, STAIR-DPO degrades severely (76.98% avg vs. ReSA-RL's 96.84%), indicating that introspective-reasoning DPO over-refuses benign queries, whereas ReSA's explicit safety-policy grounding avoids this failure mode.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the LongCoT reasoning paradigm (established by OpenAI o1 and DeepSeek-R1 for general reasoning) toward safety alignment, introducing an 'Answer-Then-Check' ordering that inverts the more natural 'Check-Then-Answer' approach used by OpenAI's Deliberative Alignment and STAIR-DPO. The core conceptual departure is the insight that harmful intent is more legible in the model's own generated response than in the adversarial query, so the model should first draft its answer in thought and then audit it against safety policies. On the training side, the SFT variant builds on WildJailbreak-style safety fine-tuning, while the RL variant applies GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) with a composite safety/refusal/format reward, and the dataset construction leverages existing jailbreak generators (PAIR, GPTFuzzer, PAP) alongside the WildJailbreak pool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The publicly released 80K ReSA dataset and the clear Answer-Then-Check template give practitioners a concrete, reproducible recipe for safety alignment that does not require access to frontier reasoning models for data generation. The finding that ~500 carefully constructed samples approach full-dataset performance is practically significant for resource-constrained teams, though it is validated on only four attack methods and a single 7B base model, so its robustness at scale remains open. A key limitation is that the RL variant uses Llama-Guard-3-8B as both the reward model and one of the three evaluation metrics, making the near-perfect 0.99+ safety scores partially circular; the intended-answer-summary leakage risk in the CoT is acknowledged but only partially mitigated by the RL variant. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to multilingual or multimodal settings and whether the safe-completion capability generalizes beyond self-harm to other high-stakes categories.
