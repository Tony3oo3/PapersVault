## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D reconstruction methods (DUSt3R, VGGT, Fast3R, etc.) anchor all predicted geometry to a single designated reference view, inheriting a design choice from classical SfM. This introduces an inductive bias that makes reconstruction quality highly sensitive to which view is selected as reference, causing dramatic performance degradation when the reference is suboptimal. The paper identifies this reliance on a fixed reference frame as a fundamental limitation that constrains both robustness and accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260358/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts N images in any order—single frames, video sequences, or unordered sets from static or dynamic scenes—without requiring a designated reference view. For each input image, it produces a camera pose in SE(3), a pixel-aligned 3D point map defined in that image's own camera coordinate system (up to a single global scale factor), and a per-pixel confidence map. During training, inputs are at 224×224 (stage 1) or variable resolution with 100k–255k total pixels and aspect ratio in [0.5, 2.0] (stage 2); the model processes 2–24 images per batch.

## Method

π³ achieves true permutation equivariance by removing all order-dependent components: no frame-index positional embeddings and no special reference-view tokens (unlike VGGT's camera tokens). The architecture uses a DINOv2 backbone to embed each view into patch tokens, followed by 36 layers of alternating view-wise and global self-attention, then lightweight 5-layer per-view decoders for poses, point maps, and confidence. The key training insight is supervising affine-invariant camera poses via relative poses between all N(N−1) ordered view pairs (rotation angle loss + Huber translation loss scaled by a single global factor) and scale-invariant local point maps via a depth-weighted L1 loss after solving for one optimal scale factor across the entire sequence. The model is initialized from pretrained VGGT weights with a frozen encoder, trained on 15 diverse datasets, and achieves 57.4 FPS inference on a single A800 GPU.

## Results

On camera pose estimation, π³ achieves a Sintel ATE of 0.074, nearly halving VGGT's 0.167, and a RealEstate10K AUC of 85.90 (vs. VGGT's 77.62). On video depth estimation, it reduces Sintel Abs Rel from VGGT's 0.299 to 0.233 and KITTI Abs Rel from 0.062 to 0.038, while running at 57.4 FPS (vs. VGGT's 43.2 FPS and DUSt3R's 1.25 FPS). On point map reconstruction (DTU), it achieves 1.198 mean accuracy vs. VGGT's 1.338, and on ETH3D, 0.194 vs. 0.280. The model also achieves near-zero standard deviation (0.003) across input orderings on DTU, compared to VGGT's 0.033, confirming true permutation equivariance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VGGT (the strongest prior feed-forward method), π³ wins decisively on zero-shot generalization: Sintel ATE drops from 0.167 to 0.074 (55% reduction), RealEstate10K AUC rises from 77.62 to 85.90, and video depth Sintel Abs Rel improves from 0.299 to 0.233. On in-domain ScanNet, VGGT retains a marginal edge in ATE (0.012 vs. 0.014), but π³ leads on RPE-t and RPE-r. Against CUT3R and FLARE, π³ dominates across all camera pose and point map benchmarks (e.g., Sintel ATE 0.074 vs. 0.217/0.207; DTU accuracy 1.198 vs. 4.742/2.541). On monocular depth, π³ (Sintel Abs Rel 0.277) is competitive with the specialized MoGe-v1 (0.273) and MoGe-v2 (0.277), despite not being explicitly optimized for single-frame input. In terms of speed, π³ at 57.4 FPS surpasses VGGT (43.2 FPS) and is ~46× faster than DUSt3R (1.25 FPS), while using fewer parameters (959M vs. VGGT's 1.26B). The robustness gap is stark: π³'s standard deviation across input orderings is 10–100× lower than VGGT's (e.g., 0.003 vs. 0.033 on DTU accuracy), confirming that eliminating the reference view removes a critical source of instability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the entire lineage of feed-forward 3D reconstruction methods (DUSt3R, Fast3R, CUT3R, FLARE, VGGT) by eliminating the fixed reference view that all prior approaches inherit from classical SfM. Architecturally, it reuses VGGT's DINOv2 encoder and alternating view-wise/global self-attention stack (with fewer layers), adapts Reloc3R's camera pose head, and borrows MoGe's normal loss and ROE scale-alignment solver. The key conceptual shift is replacing reference-anchored supervision with fully relative, per-view supervision (affine-invariant poses, scale-invariant local point maps), making the network permutation-equivariant by construction rather than by symmetry enforcement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that removing the reference view simultaneously improves accuracy and yields near-zero sensitivity to input ordering, the paper challenges a design assumption that has persisted from classical SfM through all modern feed-forward methods. This opens the door to more robust, order-agnostic 3D perception pipelines for AR, robotics, and autonomous navigation where view selection is unreliable. Limitations noted by the authors include inability to handle transparent objects, coarser geometry than diffusion-based methods, and grid-like artifacts from the pixel-shuffle upsampling. An open question is whether the permutation-equivariant formulation extends naturally to metric-scale reconstruction or to very long sequences where the N×N relative pose supervision becomes computationally prohibitive.
