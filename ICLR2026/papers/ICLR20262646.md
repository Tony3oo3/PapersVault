## Problem

Training-based honesty alignment methods require large-scale correctness annotations to achieve universal confidence calibration across diverse tasks, making them expensive and impractical. The paper asks whether LLMs truly need so many correctness labels, or whether internal confidence can first be elicited from inexpensive self-consistency signals and then calibrated with only a minimal set of correctness annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262646/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text question as input and, before any answer generation, outputs a single scalar confidence score in [0,1] predicted from the final-layer hidden state of the last question token. The confidence is intended to reflect the probability that the model's subsequent answer will be correct, enabling downstream decisions such as abstention or triggering retrieval-augmented generation. The model backbone remains frozen; only LoRA adapters (rank 8, alpha 16) on all linear layers and a single linear head are trained.

## Method

EliCal is a two-stage training framework that reframes honesty alignment as a pretraining-then-finetuning problem. In Stage 1 (Confidence Elicitation), the model is trained on a large question set (~560k samples) using self-consistency confidence—computed as the fraction of 20 sampled responses semantically consistent with the greedy answer—as the supervision target, requiring no human annotation. In Stage 2 (Confidence Calibration), the same LoRA parameters and linear head are fine-tuned on a small set of correctness-annotated QA pairs (as few as 1k, ~0.18% of full supervision) to align the elicited confidence with actual accuracy. Both stages use MSE loss, and the key insight is that self-consistency confidence is highly correlated with true correctness, providing a strong initialization that makes subsequent calibration far more annotation-efficient and better generalizing to unseen tasks (e.g., MMLU) than calibration-from-scratch baselines.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, EliCal with only 1k correctness annotations (~0.18% of full supervision) achieves 84.36% AUROC in-domain and 84.47% AUROC out-of-domain, reaching ~98% of the upper bound (86.49%/85.83% with 560k annotations). This substantially outperforms the best training-free baseline (Consis-Sem at 73.62%/70.20%) by over 10 points, and outperforms Cal-Only(1k) (73.41%/77.32%) by 11 and 7 points respectively. On MMLU (a multi-choice benchmark substantially different from the free-form training data), EliCal consistently outperforms Cal-Only even when the latter is trained on all 560k annotations, confirming superior generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EliCal(1k) decisively outperforms all five compared baselines. Against the self-consistency baseline (Consis-Sem, Manakul et al. 2023), EliCal(1k) gains +10.7 AUROC in-domain and +14.3 OOD, while also eliminating the need for 20 repeated generations at inference. Against verbalized confidence (Xiong et al. 2023; Verbal-10 at 68.90/71.05), EliCal(1k) leads by +15.5 in-domain and +13.4 OOD. Against the temperature-scaling methods Thermometer (Shen et al. 2024; 59.48/63.88) and DACA (Luo et al. 2025; 67.70/70.98), EliCal(1k) surpasses them by 24.9/20.6 and 16.7/13.5 points respectively. Most critically, against the calibration-only baseline (Cal-Only, Yang et al. 2023) trained on the same 1k annotations, EliCal(1k) achieves +10.9 AUROC in-domain and +7.2 OOD, and on MMLU EliCal maintains its advantage even when Cal-Only is given all 560k annotations, demonstrating that the two-stage elicitation-then-calibration paradigm generalizes better than fitting correctness labels from scratch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reframing honesty alignment as a two-stage pretraining-finetuning problem: Stage 1 elicits the model's internal confidence using self-consistency signals (a training-free method from Manakul et al., 2023) as self-supervised targets, and Stage 2 calibrates with minimal correctness annotations. Prior training-based calibration work (Yang et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024) learns confidence from correctness labels from scratch, while internal-state methods (Azaria & Mitchell, 2023; Ni et al., 2025) use hidden representations for abstention but do not teach the model to express its own confidence. EliCal's key departure is treating self-consistency confidence as a learnable, annotation-free foundation that makes subsequent calibration far more data-efficient, and it introduces HonestyBench (560k+ samples across 10 QA datasets) to enable large-scale training and evaluation that prior small-scale studies could not support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The annotation-efficiency result—achieving ~98% of the full-supervision upper bound with only 1k correctness labels (0.18% of data)—has direct practical implications for deploying honest LLMs across diverse tasks without expensive labeling. The superior generalization to MMLU (a multiple-choice format fundamentally different from the free-form training data) suggests that learning from the model's internal consistency signals captures task-agnostic knowledge rather than overfitting to surface-level label patterns. Key limitations include the restriction to single-turn, text-only, free-form QA; the reliance on a powerful LLM (Qwen2.5-32B) for semantic consistency and correctness annotation, which introduces potential bias; and the open question of whether the two-stage paradigm extends to multi-turn dialogue, multimodal settings, and non-factual tasks where self-consistency may be a weaker proxy for correctness.
