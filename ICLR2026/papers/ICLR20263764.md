## Problem

Quality-based data selection for LLM pre-training exhibits severe diminishing returns during long-term training due to semantic redundancy, while diversity-based selection removes too many high-quality samples. Jointly optimizing both metric types is infeasible at trillion-token scale because diversity scores are set functions whose greedy solutions become prohibitively expensive (up to 78 hours for 100K samples). This paper addresses how to efficiently select pre-training data that simultaneously satisfies quality and diversity constraints at the scale of 15-trillion-token corpora.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full text corpus (FineWeb, 15T tokens), pre-computed per-sample quality scores (a combined 0–15 scale from DCLM, Edu, and Wiki classifiers), E5-base-v2 text embeddings for diversity computation, and a selection budget S. It outputs a binary mask over the dataset and the corresponding selected subset (FineWeb-Mask, 1.5T tokens). Operationally, the corpus is split into 1M-sample chunks, and only 5–10% of samples participate in each logit update step (batch updating), making the full selection tractable on 384 GPUs in approximately 15 hours.

## Method

DATAMASK formulates data selection as a mask learning problem: rather than directly optimizing the discrete binary mask, it learns continuous logits L that parameterize a distribution over masks via probabilistic relaxation, making the problem amenable to gradient-based optimization. The optimization uses policy gradient estimation with a group relative advantage baseline (normalizing each sampled mask's objective score by the group mean and standard deviation), which the authors prove reduces gradient variance compared to vanilla PGE. The joint objective is a weighted combination λ·f_qua + (1−λ)·f_div, where f_div is Pair-wise Similarity (selected over Facility Location and DiSF via ablation). Key acceleration strategies include quality-aware pruning (removing the bottom 40–50% of samples by quality score), quality-proportional initialization of logits, splitting into 1M-sample subsets, and batch updating with a 5–10% sample ratio, collectively reducing selection time by 98.9% compared to greedy algorithms on DiSF.

## Results

On a 1.5B dense model pre-trained with 400B tokens, FineWeb-Mask achieves an average score of 48.1 across 12 tasks, improving 3.2% over raw FineWeb (44.9) and 0.9% over the best baseline FineWebPro (47.2). On a 7B MoE model pre-trained with 300B tokens, FineWeb-Mask reaches 52.6 average, a 1.9% gain over FineWeb (50.7) and 0.4% over the best baseline FineWeb-DCLM (52.2). The selection process itself takes approximately 15 hours on 384 GPUs, reducing selection time by 98.9% compared to the greedy algorithm on DiSF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FineWeb-Mask outperforms all six baselines on the 1.5B dense model's average score (48.1 vs. best baseline FineWebPro at 47.2), with the largest gains in Knowledge (+1.8 over FineWebPro) and Reading Comprehension (+1.2 over FineWebPro). On the 7B MoE model, FineWeb-Mask (52.6) edges out FineWeb-DCLM (52.2) by 0.4%, primarily driven by Knowledge (+1.1). Diversity-only FineWeb-Semdedup underperforms raw FineWeb on both model sizes (43.8 vs. 44.9 on 1.5B; 50.0 vs. 50.7 on 7B MoE), confirming that removing semantically similar samples discards too many high-quality documents. Quality-based methods (FineWeb-Edu, FineWeb-DCLM, UltraFineWeb-en) all beat raw FineWeb but exhibit diminishing returns during long pre-training, while FineWeb-Mask's joint quality-diversity optimization sustains gains. In the appendix comparison with DVRL (Yoon et al., 2020), DATAMASK achieves a better converged pair-wise similarity score (−0.6831 vs. −0.6874) in 40 minutes versus DVRL's 2h30min, and DVRL shows poor generalization to unseen data (−0.7040 vs. seen set −0.6874).

## Relation to Prior Work

DATAMASK builds on the policy gradient estimation framework (Williams, 1992) and probabilistic relaxation techniques for combinatorial optimization (Hsiao & Sawchuk, 1989; Abbas & Swoboda, 2021), repurposing them to frame pre-training data selection as a mask learning problem. It departs from the dominant paradigm of either individual sample scoring (FineWeb-Edu, DCLM, UltraFineWeb) or greedy submodular optimization (DiSF, Facility Location, Semdedup) by jointly optimizing quality and diversity metrics through iterative mask sampling and group-relative-advantage gradient updates. Unlike learned selection paradigms such as DVRL (Yoon et al., 2020) that train a separate model to predict per-sample probabilities, DATAMASK directly optimizes per-sample logits without an intermediate predictor, avoiding the generalization gap and additional training cost of a value estimator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 98.9% reduction in selection time over greedy algorithms is a major practical enabler, making joint quality-diversity optimization feasible at the trillion-token scale where it was previously intractable. The empirical finding that diversity-only selection (Semdedup) performs worse than raw FineWeb challenges the common assumption that semantic deduplication is universally beneficial, and the observed diminishing returns of quality-only methods during long-term pre-training provide concrete motivation for joint optimization. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes beyond the quality-diversity pair to more complex multi-metric objectives, whether it transfers to non-text modalities, and whether the 15-hour selection cost on 384 GPUs is acceptable for iterative dataset curation. The released FineWeb-Mask dataset (1.5T tokens) provides a useful benchmark for future data selection research.
