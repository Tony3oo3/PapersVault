## Problem

Existing long-context benchmarks predominantly evaluate Needle-in-a-Haystack (NIAH) retrieval, where models locate a single relevant fact amid irrelevant distractors. This does not capture the harder capability of reasoning over contexts where every element is densely informative and must be integrated uniformly. The paper asks whether current LLMs and LVLMs can perform such dense context reasoning, and whether NIAH performance is a reliable proxy for it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260206/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a sequence of N frames (N ∈ {1,2,4,8,16,32,64,128}) with one of 24 question templates. Each frame is a 512×512 pixel image depicting 5 named characters (colored circles) placed in one of 6 named rooms (rectangles in a 2×3 grid); for LLMs the sequence is serialized as JSON with explicit frame numbers and room assignments. The model must produce a single exact-match answer: a room name, a character name, or an integer count. The full dataset contains 9,600 (sequence, question, answer) triplets with 1,200 sequences per length, all uniquely generated to prevent memorization.

## Method

MMReD introduces a randomized, scalable visual environment where characters move between rooms at each step, and 24 question types split into two categories: 15 NIAH questions (First Appearance, Final Appearance, Frame X) that require retrieval from a single frame, and 9 Dense Context (DC) questions that demand global aggregation across all frames (e.g., counting how many steps a room was empty, or which character spent the most time in a given room). The design enforces minimalist visuals and language to isolate reasoning from perception and instruction-following, and ensures all context elements carry task-relevant information so that no sparse-retrieval shortcut exists. The empirical study evaluates ~30 models (open-source and proprietary LLMs, image- and video-oriented LVLMs, reasoning-specialized models) across all sequence lengths, and further tests whether SFT, GRPO, or in-context learning can bridge the performance gap at unseen lengths. Key findings include a consistent performance collapse beyond 32 frames, a decoupling of NIAH and DC accuracy (Pearson correlation drops from ~0.9 to ~0.5–0.7), and the failure of fine-tuning to generalize, pointing to structural limitations of softmax attention in dense integrative reasoning.

## Results

Across ~30 evaluated LLMs and LVLMs, performance on MMReD degrades consistently as sequence length increases, with significant drops beyond N=32 frames. Even the strongest reasoning-specialized models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-70B, QwQ-32B) reach 0% accuracy on certain dense-context (DC) tasks at N=128. The Pearson correlation between NIAH and DC task performance drops from ~0.9 (within NIAH subsets) to ~0.5–0.7 at 32+ frames, confirming that retrieval ability does not predict dense-context reasoning. Fine-tuning via SFT yields a 57.7% relative improvement and GRPO 27.8% on the full dataset, but neither generalizes to unseen longer contexts, while ICL provides only 13.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Reasoning-specialized LLMs (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-70B, QwQ-32B) outperform GPT-4o by 2–4% at short contexts and by considerably more at longer sequence lengths, highlighting the value of RL-trained reasoning chains for long-context retention. GPT-4o and GPT-4o-mini outperform most open-source models in both text and image modalities, with near-perfect accuracy on short sequences and smoother degradation at longer lengths. Text-only LLMs consistently outperform their multimodal LVLM counterparts at mid context lengths (e.g., Qwen2.5-3B shows a +37.5% text advantage at N=128), while smaller models sometimes invert at extreme N. Video-oriented LVLMs (LLaVA-Video, even at 72B) underperform image-focused or general-purpose LVLMs, and Qwen2.5-Coder underperforms the base Qwen2.5, suggesting domain-specific fine-tuning does not reinforce logical reasoning. Among adapter architectures, multi-linear (Qwen2.5-VL) and QFormer (MiniCPM-o) perform comparably, while cross-attention (Llama-3.2-Vision) lags. Fine-tuned models (SFT on Qwen2.5-7B and Falcon3-Mamba-7B; GRPO on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B) all show the same qualitative decline as zero-shot baselines, with GRPO performing worse than SFT, confirming that standard training paradigms do not resolve dense-context reasoning failures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the long-context reasoning evaluation lineage from bAbI-style toy tasks and the Needle-in-a-Haystack (NIAH) paradigm popularized by BABILong, RULER, and Michelangelo. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of 'dense context' (DC) tasks where every element of the sequence is informative and must be integrated uniformly, in contrast to prior benchmarks (Michelangelo, LongBench v2, HERBench) that still reduce to locating a sparse evidential subset within a mostly irrelevant context. The paper also grounds its empirical findings in recent theoretical results on softmax attention limitations (Veličković et al., 2025) and length-specific solution learning (Ebrahimi et al., 2026), connecting observed performance degradation to structural architectural constraints rather than mere scaling artifacts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMReD reveals that NIAH retrieval performance is a poor proxy for genuine dense-context reasoning, with Pearson correlation dropping from ~0.9 to 0.5–0.7 as context length grows, and that even the strongest reasoning-specialized models (DeepSeek-R1-70B, QwQ-32B) reach 0% accuracy at 128 observations. The finding that standard fine-tuning (SFT, GRPO) and video-oriented pretraining fail to generalize to longer contexts points to a structural bottleneck in current architectures that cannot be resolved by data or training recipe alone. Open questions include whether architectural innovations (e.g., learned attention priors, hierarchical memory, uncertainty-aware decoding) or test-time compute scaling can close the gap, and whether the dense-context limitation observed here transfers to real-world multimodal settings with perceptual noise.
