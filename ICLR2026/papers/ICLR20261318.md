## Problem

Existing video scene segmentation methods rely predominantly on visual similarity between adjacent shots, causing errors when narrative transitions occur without accompanying visual discontinuities. Production-level metadata such as genre conventions and shot duration patterns offer complementary semantic signals for identifying scene boundaries, but prior work has not effectively integrated these signals into shot representation learning or pseudo-boundary generation for self-supervised training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261318/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB keyframes (3 per shot) extracted from shot boundary detection, 21 IMDb genre tags per video, and per-shot duration values. The ViT-B/32 shot encoder (CLIP-initialized, 39M trainable parameters) produces shot-level embeddings, which a 2-layer BERT context encoder (768-dim, trained from scratch) processes over 17-shot windows to output a binary boundary probability per shot (threshold 0.5). At inference, any shot exceeding 10 seconds is subdivided into three equal segments before encoding, and detected boundaries are mapped back to original temporal coordinates.

## Method

The approach combines three mechanisms within a two-stage self-supervised-to-supervised pipeline. First, genre embeddings (21 IMDb textual definitions encoded by OpenCLIP's text encoder) are integrated into ViT layers via affinity-based residual connections: cosine similarity between visual tokens and genre embeddings produces a softmax attention matrix, whose weighted genre projection is added residually to the visual features, enabling dynamic genre-conditioned representation without hard genre assignment. Second, pseudo-boundary generation replaces BaSSL's fixed endpoint anchors with inverse-duration-weighted probabilistic sampling (P(s_i) ∝ 1/d_i), which prioritizes shorter shots to produce more diverse anchor pairs; a DTW-adapted similarity search then locates the optimal boundary between anchors. Third, a test-time shot splitting strategy subdivides shots longer than 10 seconds into three segments using pre-extracted or uniformly sampled keyframes, requiring no retraining. Pretraining uses InfoNCE contrastive loss on pseudo-scene averages plus binary cross-entropy on pseudo-boundary shots; fine-tuning updates only the context encoder and prediction head with standard BCE on labeled boundaries.

## Results

On MovieNet-SSeg, the proposed method achieves 63.62 AP, 92.84 AUC, 58.88 F1, and 59.64 mIoU, outperforming the previous best self-supervised method TranS4mer (60.78 AP, 51.91 mIoU) by +2.84 AP and +7.73 mIoU. On the BBC documentary dataset (zero-shot), the method reaches 37.2 average AP across five annotators, surpassing TranS4mer (30.3) and BaSSL (29.0). On the newly introduced MovieChat-SSeg benchmark, the method achieves 46.7 total AP, improving +8.8 over TranS4mer (37.9) with consistent gains across movies (48.1), TV series (54.6), and documentaries (29.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 21 compared baselines on MovieNet-SSeg across every reported metric. The largest gains are over the closest self-supervised competitors: +2.84 AP and +7.73 mIoU over TranS4mer (Islam et al., 2023), +4.07 AP over CAT (Yang et al., 2023), and +6.22 AP over BaSSL (Mun et al., 2022). Against supervised methods, the improvement is even more pronounced: +16.52 AP over LGSS (Rao et al., 2020) and +17.12 AP over MS-LSTM (Huang et al., 2020), while requiring no labeled pretraining data. On the zero-shot BBC dataset, the method leads TranS4mer by +6.9 AP and BaSSL by +8.2 AP, confirming cross-domain generalization to documentary content. On MovieChat-SSeg, the +8.8 AP gain over TranS4mer is consistent across all three content types (movies, TV, documentaries), with the largest relative improvement in TV series (+7.9 AP). The paper also shows that applying its duration-aware sampling and test-time splitting to re-implemented BaSSL and TranS4mer baselines yields +0.8–2.1 AP and +0.6–0.8 AP respectively, isolating the contribution of each component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised pseudo-boundary framework introduced by BaSSL (Mun et al., 2022), which used fixed endpoint anchors to generate training signals, by replacing fixed anchors with duration-aware probabilistic sampling and incorporating genre conventions as soft semantic priors via affinity-based residual connections in a ViT shot encoder. It departs from prior metadata-based approaches like Movies2Scenes (Chen et al., 2023), which used movie-level metadata for cross-movie contrastive learning, by integrating genre information directly into shot-level representation learning during pretraining. The DTW-based boundary search mechanism is adapted from classical time-series alignment (Berndt & Clifford, 1994), and the overall two-stage pretrain-then-finetune paradigm follows the established self-supervised scene segmentation literature (TranS4mer, CAT).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of MovieChat-SSeg (1,000 videos spanning movies, TV series, and documentaries) fills a gap in scene segmentation benchmarks that previously focused almost exclusively on theatrical films, enabling evaluation across diverse narrative structures and editing styles. The finding that production-level metadata (genre conventions, shot duration distributions) provides complementary signals beyond visual features suggests a broader principle: domain-specific structural priors can improve self-supervised representation learning in video understanding. However, the method remains visual-only and struggles with dialogue-heavy scenes (AP drops from 76.9 to 61.6 as dialogue density increases), and its applicability to non-narrative formats (lectures, gaming) is limited, leaving open the question of how to integrate audio and dialogue signals for more comprehensive scene understanding.
