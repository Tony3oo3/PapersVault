## Problem

The prevailing paradigm treats reasoning as a post-training skill layered onto a generalist base, leaving unclear whether and how reasoning data injected during pretraining affects downstream performance. Due to the opacity of frontier model training corpora and the prohibitive cost of end-to-end pretraining experiments, the scientific literature lacks a systematic account of how reasoning data—varying in scale, diversity, and quality—interacts with different training phases, and whether early injection creates durable foundations that later fine-tuning cannot replicate.

## Input / Output

The model consumes text prompts in question-answer format spanning math, science, code, and general reasoning, and produces text responses containing chain-of-thought reasoning followed by a final answer. Training data is organized into four controlled reasoning corpora (large-scale diverse, small-scale high-quality, mixed, and answer-length-filtered) that are injected at fixed token ratios during pretraining and used at varying scales during SFT. Evaluation covers 16 benchmarks from elementary math (GSM8K) to competition-level problems (AIME-24/25) and graduate science (GPQA-Diamond), with pass@1 averaged over multiple runs.

## Method

The paper pretrains a hybrid Mamba-2/Transformer 8B model from scratch for 1T tokens, injecting reasoning data during the final 400B tokens at a fixed 20% ratio, then applies SFT on 4.8M reasoning samples and GRPO-based RL with verifiable rewards. The key experimental design is a fully crossed setup: four pretraining variants (no reasoning, large-diverse, small-high-quality, mixed) × three SFT datasets, yielding 12+ model configurations to isolate the contribution of each phase. The central finding is an asymmetric allocation principle: pretraining benefits most from broad diversity and scale of reasoning patterns (+11% with diverse corpus), while SFT is dominated by data quality and reasoning depth (+15% with high-quality long-CoT data). Additionally, high-quality pretraining data exhibits a latent effect—showing minimal immediate gain but unlocking an extra +4% after SFT—and naively scaling SFT with mixed-quality data actively harms math reasoning by −5%.

## Results

After 1T-token pretraining, models exposed to reasoning data (M_res average) improve over the no-reasoning baseline (M_base) by +8.35% overall accuracy, with M_LDQ (large, diverse data) reaching 64.09% average vs. 52.70% for M_base, driven by a +28.4% gain in math and +9% in code. After SFT on high-quality data (D_SHQ), the pretraining advantage amplifies: M_LMQ+SFT_SHQ achieves 50.95% average vs. 29.92% for M_base+SFT_SHQ. After RL (GRPO), the gap widens further to 18.57% on average, with a 39.32% improvement on AIME competition math, confirming that front-loading reasoning data creates compounding, durable gains that SFT alone cannot replicate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between four pretraining strategies (no reasoning data, small high-quality, large diverse, large mixed-quality) under a fixed 80B reasoning-token budget. M_LDQ and M_LMQ substantially outperform M_base across all reasoning domains after pretraining (+11.4% and +11.4% overall, respectively), while M_SHQ provides only a modest +2.3% lift, establishing that diversity and scale dominate quality at the pretraining stage. The 'catch-up' hypothesis is decisively refuted: doubling SFT epochs for M_base (34.01% avg) still trails the weakest reasoning-pretrained model M_SHQ+SFT_SHQ (37.33%) by 3.32%. At the SFT stage, the asymmetry reverses: fine-tuning on the small high-quality D_SHQ (1.2M samples) yields 44.99% average for M_res, while scaling to the large diverse D_LDQ or D_LMQ drops performance to ~31%, a 15% quality-driven gap. After RL, M_LMQ+SFT_SHQ+RL (56.66% avg) beats M_base+SFT_SHQ+RL (37.92%) by 18.74%, with the largest divergence on AIME-24 (45.21% vs. 12.29%), confirming that early reasoning injection sets the performance ceiling that no amount of later-stage training can overcome.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of treating reasoning as a post-training skill layered onto a generalist base, and from mid-training interventions (Wang et al. 2025, AI et al. 2025) that inject small amounts of reasoning data into intermediate checkpoints. While prior work has studied SFT data quality (Zhou et al. 2023, Zhao et al. 2024) and post-training phase synergies (Liu et al. 2025, Chu et al. 2025), no prior study systematically varies reasoning data scale, diversity, and quality across both pretraining and SFT under a fixed token budget. The paper carves new ground by framing the problem as a joint optimization over data allocation between the two phases, rather than studying each phase in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The asymmetric allocation principle—diversity drives pretraining gains while quality governs SFT—provides a concrete, actionable heuristic for data strategy that goes beyond the common 'more is better' assumption. The refutation of the catch-up hypothesis (doubling SFT epochs still cannot match reasoning-pretrained models) has direct practical implications for compute allocation in frontier model development. Open questions remain: the optimal reasoning ratio is dataset-dependent, findings are validated on a single hybrid Mamba-Transformer architecture at 8B/1T scale (with limited 1.2B cross-check), and the interaction with RL scaling at larger model sizes is unexplored. The latent-effect finding (high-quality pretraining data showing minimal immediate benefit but unlocking +4% post-SFT) suggests deeper representational mechanisms that warrant further investigation.
