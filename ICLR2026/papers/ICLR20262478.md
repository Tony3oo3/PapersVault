## Problem

Standard Conformal Risk Control (CRC) for image segmentation provides only marginal (dataset-average) false negative rate guarantees, leaving individual images with highly variable coverage that can be dangerously low for difficult cases. This paper addresses the gap by learning image-adaptive thresholds that achieve per-image conditional coverage, which is critical for safety-critical applications like medical imaging where missing a region in a single image can have severe consequences.

## Input / Output

The threshold predictor f_D takes a 4-channel tensor formed by channel-wise concatenation of the RGB input image and the single-channel probability map from a frozen base segmentation model (UNet, DeepLab v3+, PSPNet, or SINet), and outputs a single scalar threshold in [0,1] via a sigmoid. This threshold is then applied to the probability map to produce a binary prediction set (segmentation mask). The base model is trained separately and kept frozen; only the threshold predictor is learned.

## Method

COAT (Conditional Optimization for Adaptive Thresholding) introduces a differentiable miscoverage loss that directly optimizes image-conditional coverage without pre-computing ground-truth thresholds. The key mechanism replaces the hard thresholding operation with a temperature-scaled sigmoid soft mask M_soft = σ((p̂(X) − τ̂(X))/T), making the True Positive Rate differentiable with respect to the predicted threshold; the loss is simply (TPR_soft − (1−α))². A ResNet-50 backbone (modified for 4-channel input, single-scalar output) predicts the per-image threshold, trained end-to-end via gradient descent. After training, a single global correction term t′ is found on a held-out calibration set via binary search to guarantee marginal coverage, and the final threshold for each test image is clip(τ̂(X) − t′, 0, 1). This two-stage design—direct conditional optimization followed by marginal calibration—yields the smallest coverage gap across all tested datasets and base models.

## Results

Across all 12 dataset–model combinations (Polyp, Fire, Skin × DeepLab v3+, UNet, PSPNet, SINet), COAT consistently achieves the lowest Coverage Gap, the paper's primary metric for image-conditional reliability. For example, on Polyp with PSPNet at α=0.1, COAT reduces the Coverage Gap to 0.110 (±0.015) versus 0.150 (±0.015) for CRC and 0.119 (±0.016) for AA-CRC. On the more challenging Skin dataset with PSPNet at α=0.1, COAT achieves a Coverage Gap of 0.050 (±0.002) compared to 0.069 (±0.001) for CRC. Marginal Coverage remains at the target level (~0.90 for α=0.1, ~0.80 for α=0.2) across all methods, confirming that the improved conditional performance does not sacrifice the distribution-free guarantee.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares COAT against two conformal baselines: CRC (Angelopoulos et al., ICLR 2024), which uses a single globally calibrated threshold, and AA-CRC (Blot et al., AISTATS 2025), which learns an automatically adaptive threshold. COAT outperforms CRC on Coverage Gap in every one of the 12 dataset–model settings; the improvement is most pronounced on the Polyp dataset, where COAT reduces the gap by roughly 25–30% relative to CRC (e.g., 0.110 vs. 0.150 for PSPNet at α=0.1). Against AA-CRC, COAT also achieves a lower Coverage Gap in the majority of settings, though the margin is smaller (e.g., 0.050 vs. 0.057 on Skin/PSPNet at α=0.1). Both baselines maintain comparable Marginal Coverage to COAT, confirming that the gains come from tighter per-image conditional control rather than a shift in average risk. The supervised AT variant (the paper's own baseline) sits between AA-CRC and COAT, suggesting that the end-to-end differentiable miscoverage loss in COAT provides a meaningful advantage over pre-computing hard thresholds via binary search.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Conformal Risk Control (CRC) (Angelopoulos et al., 2024), which provides distribution-free marginal FNR guarantees via a single global calibrated threshold, by replacing that threshold with an image-adaptive one. The key technical departure is the COAT framework's differentiable miscoverage loss, which uses a sigmoid-softened TPR to enable end-to-end gradient-based learning of per-image thresholds, circumventing the non-continuous, non-monotone relationship between threshold and coverage that makes direct optimization infeasible. The approach also relates to AA-CRC (Blot et al., 2025), which adapts thresholds but without the end-to-end differentiable formulation, and to the broader conditional conformal prediction literature (Lei & Wasserman, 2014; Romano et al., 2019) that seeks to tighten marginal guarantees into conditional ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For safety-critical segmentation (medical imaging, autonomous driving), per-image reliability is more important than dataset-average guarantees, and this paper provides a principled, distribution-free pathway to reduce the coverage gap consistently across diverse datasets and base architectures. The differentiable miscoverage loss is a clean, reusable technique that could transfer to other conformal prediction settings beyond binary segmentation. However, the authors acknowledge that the coverage gap cannot be driven to zero due to finite-sample effects and bias-variance trade-offs in threshold prediction, and the method's limitations in few-shot settings, multi-class scenarios, and simultaneous FNR/FPR control remain open challenges.
