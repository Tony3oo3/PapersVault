## Problem

Existing class incremental learning (CIL) methods for vision-language models assume fully labeled data at each incremental task, which is impractical in real-world settings where annotations are scarce. When pseudo labels are generated via zero-shot CLIP similarity, their inherent noise degrades performance and exacerbates catastrophic forgetting, creating a gap between what CIL methods can achieve with perfect labels and what they achieve with noisy pseudo labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263417/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each incremental task, the method receives a set of unlabeled RGB images and a corresponding set of class name strings (e.g., 'a photo of a [CLASS]'). Images are encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 image encoder into fixed-dimensional feature vectors, while class names are encoded by the text encoder into prompt embeddings. The output is a class label prediction computed as a weighted sum (β=0.2) of the learned classification head logits and the zero-shot CLIP image-text similarity logits, following the RAIL inference fusion strategy.

## Method

N2L replaces the standard cross-entropy loss with a mean squared error (MSE) ridge regression objective, which is more robust to label noise and admits a recursive closed-form solution that achieves performance identical to joint training, thereby eliminating forgetting. To refine noisy pseudo labels, the method applies SVD-based feature dimensionality reduction to the image feature matrix, retaining only singular directions above a threshold θ=10, then iteratively (3 steps) retrains a label refinement classifier on the reduced features to update pseudo labels — a procedure supported by a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) showing that removing noise-dominated singular directions reduces expected MSE. A bi-level weight adjustment strategy further compensates for pseudo-label-induced class imbalance (inter-class: normalizing per-class total weight) and downweights low-confidence samples (intra-class: ranking samples by logit entropy and assigning weights sampled from N(1, 1/4)). The final incremental classifier is updated recursively via sufficient statistics A_t and C_t, requiring no storage of past data.

## Results

N2L achieves the best performance across all six primary datasets (FGVC Aircraft, Stanford Cars, CIFAR-100, CUB-200, ObjectNet, UCF-101) under both no-base-class and with-base-class incremental settings. On the most challenging dataset (Aircraft B0Inc10), N2L reaches 43.73% average accuracy (Ā) and 40.21% final-task accuracy (A_B), surpassing the second-best method RAIL (36.23/33.59) by 7.5% and 6.6% respectively. On ObjectNet B0Inc20, N2L achieves 49.31/41.59, outperforming ENGINE (44.57/31.24) by 4.74% and 10.35%. N2L narrows the gap to the ground-truth-label upper bound by approximately 50% on Cars, ObjectNet, and UCF, demonstrating strong effectiveness in learning from noisy pseudo labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

N2L consistently outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and setting. Against the strongest non-exemplar competitor RAIL, N2L gains 7.5% Ā on Aircraft (43.73 vs. 36.23), 3.74% on Cars (92.38 vs. 88.64), 0.46% on CIFAR-100 (87.80 vs. 87.34), 1.77% on CUB (83.41 vs. 81.64), 9.51% on ObjectNet (49.31 vs. 39.80), and 4.82% on UCF (95.00 vs. 90.18). ENGINE is the second-strongest on ObjectNet (44.57) but trails N2L by 4.74% Ā and 10.35% A_B. Unimodal methods CODA-Prompt and MoE-Adapter perform worse than zero-shot CLIP on several datasets (e.g., CODA-Prompt drops to 67.40 on CUB vs. ZS-CLIP's 75.47) because they discard CLIP's text-image alignment. Even when the pseudo-labeling method CPL is integrated into RAIL or ENGINE, N2L still leads (e.g., 43.73 vs. 43.61 on Aircraft B0Inc10). Against exemplar-based methods CLAP4CLIP and PROOF (20 samples/class), N2L (zero exemplars) wins on all datasets except a narrow 0.27–0.58% gap on CIFAR-100, CUB, and ObjectNet with base classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

N2L builds directly on the analytic CIL framework of Zhuang et al. (2022), which showed that ridge regression with recursive sufficient-statistic updates achieves joint-training-equivalent performance without revisiting old data. It departs from RAIL (Xu et al., 2024), the closest prior CLIP-based CIL method, in two key ways: replacing RAIL's feature dimensionality *increase* with SVD-based dimensionality *reduction* for pseudo-label denoising, and introducing a bi-level (inter-class + intra-class) weight adjustment absent from prior analytic CIL work. The paper's central conceptual move is reframing CIL from a labeled-data problem to an unlabeled-data problem where only class names and raw images are available, a setting explicitly absent from all prior CIL methods it surveys.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: real-world incremental deployment rarely provides clean labels, yet all prior CLIP-based CIL methods assume them. By narrowing the gap to ground-truth-label training by roughly 50% on distribution-shift datasets (Cars, ObjectNet, UCF) while using zero exemplars, N2L demonstrates that annotation-free incremental learning is viable with a frozen VLM. The main limitation, acknowledged by the authors, is the assumption that unlabeled data at task t contains only classes from task t; in realistic streams, past and future class contamination would further degrade pseudo-label quality. Open questions include whether the SVD threshold (fixed at 10) generalizes across domains without tuning, and whether the recursive closed-form solution remains stable under the noisy-boundary conditions tested (20–50% contamination).
