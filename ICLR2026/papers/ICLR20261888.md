## Problem

Recent work has shown that decoder models pretrained with Causal Language Modeling (CLM) can be repurposed as strong text encoders, sometimes surpassing traditional MLM-based encoders on benchmarks like MTEB. However, these comparisons are confounded by differences in model scale and data volume, leaving it unclear whether CLM itself provides an inherent advantage for representation learning or whether the gains simply reflect larger models trained on more data.

## Input / Output

Models consume English text sequences of up to 2,048 tokens (tokenized with the LLaMA3 tokenizer, 128K vocabulary) and produce text representations: mean-pooled token embeddings for sequence classification and information retrieval, and token-level hidden states for token classification and extractive QA. Pretraining uses unique English tokens from FineWeb-Edu, with all model variants exposed to the identical sequence of samples to isolate the effect of the training objective.

## Method

The paper conducts a controlled large-scale ablation (38 models, 210M–1B parameters, 100B tokens, 110k GPU hours) comparing three pretraining paradigms under identical architecture (EuroBERT-style transformer with RoPE, SwiGLU, RMSNorm) and data: pure CLM (causal attention, next-token prediction), pure MLM (bidirectional attention, masked token reconstruction at varying ratios), and a biphasic CLM→MLM strategy that sequentially applies both objectives. They further study a continued pretraining (CPT) setting where MLM is applied on top of existing CLM- or MLM-pretrained checkpoints. Key findings: MLM generally yields better SC and QA performance, CLM is more data-efficient and provides more stable fine-tuning, and the biphasic CLM→MLM protocol achieves the best overall performance under fixed compute budgets. In the CPT setting, adapting a CLM model with MLM outperforms continuing MLM from an MLM model, suggesting that leveraging widely available pretrained decoder models is the most compute-efficient path to strong encoders.

## Results

On 610M-parameter models trained for 42K steps (100B tokens), MLM (40% masking) achieves 87.00 avg SC accuracy, 92.21 avg TC F1, 62.77 avg QA F1, and 79.55 avg IR NDCG@10, while CLM achieves 83.58, 92.69, 42.09, and 76.20 respectively. The biphasic CLM+MLM strategy (25% CLM + 75% MLM) improves over pure MLM on all four task categories, reaching 87.85 SC, 93.07 TC, 67.77 QA, and 79.88 IR. In the continued pretraining setting, adapting a CLM model with 22K steps of MLM yields 88.40 SC and 80.70 IR, outperforming continued MLM training from an MLM base (87.47 SC, 80.45 IR). Across 38 models (210M–1B) and 15,120 fine-tuning runs, MLM remains essential for robust QA performance, but CLM provides superior data efficiency and fine-tuning stability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the MLM (BERT) paradigm, CLM pretraining loses substantially on sequence classification (−3.42 avg accuracy) and question answering (−20.68 avg F1), but slightly wins on token classification (+0.48 F1) and is competitive on information retrieval (−3.35 NDCG@10). The gap on QA is the most dramatic: CLM models score 42.09 vs. MLM's 62.77 at 610M, indicating that bidirectional attention is critical for extractive QA. However, the biphasic CLM+MLM strategy (25% CLM + 75% MLM) consistently beats pure MLM on all four task categories, with the largest gain on QA (+5.00 F1) and TC (+0.86 F1). In the continued pretraining setting, starting from a CLM model and applying 22K steps of MLM outperforms continuing MLM from an MLM base on SC (+0.93) and IR (+0.25), while matching on QA (−1.04) and TC (+0.59). CLM models also demonstrate lower sensitivity to fine-tuning learning rate, reducing the need for extensive hyperparameter search.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a controlled disentanglement of a confound that has plagued recent encoder-pretraining literature: prior evidence that CLM-pretrained decoders (LLM2Vec, NV-Embed, SFR-Embedding) outperform MLM encoders on MTEB was always accompanied by larger model sizes and more training data, making it impossible to attribute gains to the causal objective versus scale. Building on the EuroBERT architecture and the masking-ratio analysis of Wettig et al. (2023), the authors fix architecture, tokenizer, data, and compute to isolate the effect of the training objective (CLM vs. MLM vs. sequential CLM+MLM). The biphasic CLM→MLM strategy and the continued-pretraining-from-decoder finding extend the LLM2Vec adaptation framework by showing that a short MLM CPT phase on an existing CLM checkpoint is more effective than continuing MLM from an MLM checkpoint, a comparison not previously made under controlled conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is significant: rather than training an encoder from scratch with MLM, practitioners can leverage the many large pretrained decoder checkpoints already available and apply a relatively short (12K–22K step) MLM continued-pretraining phase to obtain a competitive encoder, substantially reducing the compute required for best-in-class text representations. Key limitations include the restriction to English, up to 1B parameters and 100B tokens, and the absence of contrastive post-training for zero-shot retrieval, all of which the authors flag as important future directions. Open questions include whether the biphasic advantage persists at larger scales (multi-billion parameters, trillions of tokens), in multilingual settings, and with more sophisticated alternating curricula rather than a single CLM→MLM switch.
