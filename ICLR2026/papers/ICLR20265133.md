## Problem

Existing few-step generative methods face a fundamental trade-off: adversarial-based approaches (DMD, GANs) require auxiliary trained networks or frozen teacher models, introducing training instability, high GPU memory overhead, and scalability barriers for large models (>10B), while non-adversarial methods (consistency models, MeanFlow) suffer severe quality degradation below 4 NFEs. No prior method achieves high-quality 1-step generation on ultra-large models like Qwen-Image-20B without these auxiliary components.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265133/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (and optionally a reference image for editing tasks) as input and produces a generated or edited RGB image as output. It operates within the any-step framework, so the same trained model supports 1-NFE, 2-NFE, or multi-step inference. Experiments use 1024×1024 resolution on Qwen-Image-20B and 512×512 on OpenUni/SANA backbones; the image editing variant is trained at 512×512 but tested at the input image's native resolution.

## Method

TWINFLOW introduces a twin-trajectory mechanism that extends the standard flow-matching time interval from t∈[0,1] to t∈[-1,1]. The positive branch (t>0) learns the noise-to-real-data mapping, while the negative branch (t<0) learns a noise-to-fake-data mapping where 'fake' data are the model's own one-step outputs. Training combines two losses: a self-adversarial flow-matching loss on the fake trajectory, and a rectification loss that minimizes the velocity-field difference Δv between the real and fake trajectories—derived from the KL gradient between fake and real distributions. This creates an internal self-adversarial signal that straightens the generative path without any external GAN discriminator or frozen teacher. The final objective integrates both terms into the RCGM any-step framework (N=2) with a batch-partitioning strategy controlled by hyperparameter λ, enabling a single model to perform both multi-step and 1-step generation.

## Results

On Qwen-Image-20B (LoRA), TWINFLOW achieves GenEval 0.86 and DPG-Bench 86.52 at just 1-NFE, closely matching the original 100-NFE model (GenEval 0.87, DPG-Bench 88.32) while reducing inference cost by 100×. On dedicated text-to-image models (SANA-0.6B/1.6B), TWINFLOW reaches GenEval 0.83/0.81 at 1-NFE, surpassing SANA-Sprint (0.72/0.76) and RCGM (0.80/0.78). Full-parameter training on Qwen-Image-20B yields GenEval 0.85 at 1-NFE (0.89 with longer training), outperforming DMD (0.81), SiD (0.77), sCM (0.55), and MeanFlow (0.49) under the same setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TWINFLOW decisively outperforms all few-step baselines on the 20B Qwen-Image model: at 1-NFE it beats Qwen-Image-RCGM by +0.34 GenEval (0.86 vs 0.52) and +27.0 DPG-Bench (86.52 vs 59.50), and surpasses Qwen-Image-Lightning on GenEval (0.86 vs 0.85) and WISE (0.54 vs 0.51) while maintaining far better diversity (LPIPS 0.50 vs 0.30). On dedicated T2I models (SANA-0.6B), TWINFLOW achieves GenEval 0.83 at 1-NFE, beating SANA-Sprint (0.72), RCGM (0.80), FLUX-Schnell (0.69), and SDXL-DMD2 (0.59). In the full-parameter 20B comparison (Table 3), TWINFLOW (0.85 GenEval at 1-NFE) outperforms DMD (0.81), SiD (0.77), sCM (0.55), and MeanFlow (0.49), while VSD/DMD/SiD in their raw 3-model form cause OOM. The only notable loss is on DPG-Bench at 1-NFE for SANA-1.6B (79.1 vs SANA-Sprint's 80.1), which the authors attribute to SANA-Sprint's access to proprietary training data rather than a methodological weakness.

## Relation to Prior Work

TWINFLOW is built directly on the RCGM any-step framework (Sun & Lin, 2025), which unifies multi-step flow matching and few-step consistency models under a single recursive velocity-field formulation, and inherits its base flow-matching training objective from Lipman et al. (2022). The key conceptual departure is from distribution-matching distillation methods (DMD/DMD2, SANA-Sprint) that require auxiliary score networks or GAN discriminators: TWINFLOW replaces the external adversarial signal with an internal one by extending the time axis to [-1,1], so the same network simultaneously learns a 'real' trajectory (t>0) and a 'fake' trajectory (t<0) to its own outputs, and the rectification loss minimizes the velocity difference between them. This borrows the KL-to-velocity-matching derivation from DMD (Yin et al., 2024b) but eliminates the need for a separate fake-score model, collapsing the generator and discriminator into a single network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that TWINFLOW removes the memory bottleneck that has prevented few-step distillation from scaling to 20B+ parameter models: DMD-series methods require three model copies (generator, real score, fake score) and OOM on Qwen-Image-20B, while TWINFLOW trains a single model end-to-end. Achieving GenEval 0.86 at 1-NFE versus 0.87 at 100-NFE on a 20B model represents a 100× inference speedup with negligible quality loss, which has direct economic implications for deployment. Open questions include whether the twin-trajectory approach transfers to video and audio generation, whether the preliminary image-editing results (4-NFE) can be pushed to 1-NFE with more data, and whether the method's reliance on the model's own outputs as 'fake' data introduces subtle mode-coverage limitations at extreme scale.
