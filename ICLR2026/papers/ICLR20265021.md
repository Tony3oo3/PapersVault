## Problem

Current RL post-training for LLMs incentivizes longer chains of thought that explore the solution space in a sequential, depth-first manner, leading to poor generalization on harder problems with similar difficulty. There is no mechanism to encourage models to explore diverse solution strategies (breadth) rather than committing to a single reasoning trajectory, and no way to decouple the learning signal for proposing useful high-level guidance from the signal for executing a solution.

## Input / Output

The abstraction generator takes a reasoning problem x as input and produces one or more reasoning abstractions z, which are variable-length natural language sequences encoding procedural and factual insights (e.g., useful lemmas, strategic framings, or cautionary pitfalls). The solution generator takes the pair (x, z) and produces a full solution trace y that explicitly references and builds upon the abstraction. Abstractions are verified post-hoc to not leak the answer: prompting a base model with only the abstraction (no problem) yields zero accuracy over 16 samples. At inference, the system can operate with or without abstractions, and the best-of-k abstraction selection (pass@k over abstractions) provides a natural test-time compute scaling axis.

## Method

RLAD is a two-player RL training paradigm that jointly optimizes an abstraction generator π_abs and an abstraction-conditioned solution generator π_sol. The abstraction generator is rewarded by the expected success rate of the solution generator when conditioned on its proposed abstraction (Equation 4), while the solution generator receives 0/1 accuracy reward only on abstraction-conditioned prompts; on no-abstraction prompts, the reward is zeroed out while the KL penalty is retained (Equation 3), forcing the model to actually leverage abstractions rather than ignoring them. Both models are warmstarted via SFT on data generated by stronger models (o4-mini for abstractions, GPT-4.1-mini for filtering), then fine-tuned with RL: the solution generator uses DAPO (GRPO with token-level normalization and asymmetric clipping) under a difficulty curriculum, while the abstraction generator uses batched offline RL via RFT and RPO. The key design insight is that the reward masking on no-abstraction prompts, combined with the KL constraint, creates an asymmetry where the solution generator must find ways to optimize reward specifically through abstraction utilization, preventing the degenerate solution of ignoring the abstraction entirely.

## Results

On AIME 2025, RLAD achieves 48.33% pass@1 (best abstraction) and 42.45% (average over 4 abstractions), compared to 39.79% and 34.90% for DAPO, and 40.00% and 36.25% for the base Qwen3-1.7B model. On DeepScaleR Hard, RLAD reaches 35.54% (best) vs. 33.54% (DAPO) and 32.50% (base). On AMC 2023, RLAD achieves 91.72% (best) vs. 88.44% (DAPO) and 84.53% (base). Under matched compute budgets on AIME 2025, allocating compute to n abstractions × n solutions consistently outperforms n² pure solution samples (e.g., 0.71 vs. 0.65 pass@k at n=16; 0.87 vs. 0.82 at n=256).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is DAPO (Yu et al., 2025), a state-of-the-art long chain-of-thought RL approach. RLAD outperforms DAPO across all three math benchmarks in every evaluation setting. On AIME 2025, RLAD's best-abstraction accuracy (48.33%) exceeds DAPO's (39.79%) by 8.54 points, and even in the no-abstraction setting RLAD (38.04%) slightly edges out DAPO (37.92%), suggesting that training with abstractions improves general reasoning. On DeepScaleR Hard, RLAD gains 2.0 points over DAPO in the best-abstraction setting (35.54% vs. 33.54%), and on AMC 2023 the gap is 3.28 points (91.72% vs. 88.44%). Under matched compute budgets (Table 3), RLAD's abstraction-conditioned sampling consistently beats DAPO's pure solution sampling at every budget level, with the gap widening from 4 points at n=1 to 5 points at n=256. The paper also reports that RLAD generalizes to a stronger solver (o4-mini), improving pass@4 from 84.77% to 90.00% when conditioned on abstractions proposed by the weaker trained model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL post-training paradigm for LLM reasoning (DeepSeek-R1, DAPO, GRPO) by introducing a two-player RL training loop that jointly optimizes an abstraction generator and an abstraction-conditioned solution generator. It departs from prior work on test-time compute scaling (parallel sampling, self-consistency, tree-of-thoughts) by framing abstractions as high-level subgoals that enable 'breadth' exploration rather than the 'depth'-first sequential search that standard long-CoT RL produces. The approach also connects to but differs from RAG and prompt-optimization literature: abstractions are input-dependent, generated by the model itself at inference time, and trained via RL rather than retrieved or hand-crafted. The curriculum and reward-masking design choices build on Setlur et al. (2025) and DAPO's training infrastructure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that allocating test-time compute to abstraction generation yields larger gains than additional solution sampling (especially at large budgets) introduces a new, complementary axis for scaling inference compute beyond simply generating more rollouts. The 44% improvement over DAPO on AIME 2025 and consistent gains across ARC-AGI, legal, and healthcare domains suggest the abstraction mechanism generalizes beyond math. Key limitations include reliance on a much stronger model (o4-mini) for warmstart abstractions, evaluation primarily on a 1.7B base model, and the open question of whether the two-player RL paradigm scales to larger models or can be unified into a single model. Extending abstractions to open-ended reasoning and reducing dependence on external strong models for initialization remain important future directions.
