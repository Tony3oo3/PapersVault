## Problem

Vision-Language Models suffer catastrophic forgetting when sequentially fine-tuned on multiple downstream tasks, and existing continual learning methods for VLMs either require additional reference datasets for distillation, compromise zero-shot transfer performance, or are restricted to parameter-efficient fine-tuning scenarios. No prior approach introduces model fusion into continual learning for VLMs while simultaneously supporting both full and parameter-efficient fine-tuning without handcrafted trade-off hyperparameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263868/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input, optionally accompanied by a task ID. When the task ID is known, the corresponding reconstructed task expert is used directly for prediction; when the task ID is unknown or the sample comes from an unseen task, the method reconstructs all task experts and aggregates their output logits using weights derived from cosine similarity between the test image's pre-trained VLM feature and stored per-category prototypes. The final output is a class label. The only tunable hyperparameter is K (number of top-weighted task experts to include in aggregation), and the method is insensitive to its choice.

## Method

ConDU maintains a single unified delta model (the parameter offset from the pre-trained VLM) plus per-task triggers consisting of a binary sign-agreement mask and a rescaling scalar. At each new task session, it fine-tunes the pre-trained VLM to obtain a task expert, computes its delta model, decouples past task experts from the current unified model via their triggers, and unifies all delta models using a sign-consistent element-wise max/min selection rule that retains the largest-magnitude parameter sharing the sign of the sum. The decoupling and unifying steps are training-free and take roughly 1% of the fine-tuning time. At inference for zero-shot or task-agnostic scenarios, ConDU reconstructs multiple task experts from the unified model and aggregates their predictions, weighting each expert by the maximum cosine similarity between the test image feature and that task's stored prototypes in the frozen pre-trained VLM's feature space.

## Results

On the MTIL benchmark (11 cross-domain tasks), ConDU (FT) achieves 70.8% Transfer, 78.8% Average, and 87.1% Last accuracy, outperforming the best baselines (MulKI 70.1%, Dual-RAIL 77.8%, DPeCLIP 86.9%) and surpassing the zero-shot CLIP baseline (65.3%) by 5.5%, 13.5%, and 21.9% respectively. On the task-agnostic MTIL variant (no task ID at inference), ConDU (FT) reaches 78.1% Average and 86.4% Last, exceeding the best baseline DPeCLIP by 2.0% and 1.8%. On few-shot MTIL (5 samples per class), ConDU (LoRA) achieves 70.3% Transfer and 72.7% Average, beating the best baselines by 1.4% and 0.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConDU (FT) outperforms all 12 baselines on all three MTIL metrics: Transfer (70.8% vs. MulKI 70.1%, +0.7%), Average (78.8% vs. Dual-RAIL 77.8%, +1.0%), and Last (87.1% vs. DPeCLIP 86.9%, +0.2%). On the task-agnostic MTIL setting, ConDU (FT) leads DPeCLIP by 2.0% on Average (78.1% vs. 76.1%) and 1.8% on Last (86.4% vs. 84.6%), with the gap widening because baselines cannot exploit task ID information. On few-shot MTIL, ConDU (LoRA) wins on Transfer (70.3% vs. MoE 68.9%) and Average (72.7% vs. Dual-RAIL 72.3%), but Dual-RAIL retains a slight edge on Last (77.9% vs. 77.4%). The PEFT variant ConDU (LoRA) is competitive with or superior to full fine-tuning on few-shot data, where overfitting limits FT gains. Across all settings, ConDU consistently surpasses the zero-shot CLIP baseline by 5–22%, confirming that continual learning via model fusion enhances rather than degrades zero-shot transfer capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConDU is the first work to apply model fusion (specifically delta-model-based merging à la Task Arithmetic) to continual learning of Vision-Language Models. It departs from prior VLM continual learning methods (ZSCL, Dual-RAIL, DPeCLIP, MulKI) that rely on reference datasets for distillation or are restricted to parameter-efficient fine-tuning, by using a training-free decoupling-unifying mechanism that works with both full fine-tuning and LoRA. The method also draws on the broader model-merging literature (TIES Merging, DARE, EMR Merging) for its sign-based unifying rule, but repurposes these ideas in a sequential-learning context where the unified model must be repeatedly decoupled into task-specific experts rather than used as a standalone predictor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ConDU offers a practical, hyperparameter-light alternative to existing VLM continual learning methods, eliminating the need for reference datasets, replay buffers, or careful trade-off tuning between forgetting mitigation and zero-shot preservation. The theoretical convergence guarantee (Theorem 1) provides a rare formal analysis in the VLM continual learning literature, though its assumptions (i.i.d. delta models, identical parameter signs) are restrictive. Open questions include whether the sign-based unifying rule is near-optimal, how the method scales beyond 11 tasks, and whether the approach transfers to other modalities or model families beyond CLIP-style VLMs. The finding that the unified model is a poor standalone predictor (47.1% vs. 70.8% for aggregated experts) underscores that model fusion here serves purely as a compressed storage mechanism rather than a functional model.
