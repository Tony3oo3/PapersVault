## Problem

Existing multimodal pathology models rely on vision-language pairs that lack molecular specificity, while prior attempts to incorporate gene expression (e.g., TANGLE) use bulk RNA that ignores intra-sample spatial heterogeneity. Spatial transcriptomics offers spot-level molecular supervision directly aligned with tissue images, but existing methods encode genes with simple linear layers, require full fine-tuning per dataset, and ignore the inherently spatial and multi-scale structure of ST measurements. The paper addresses this by building the first large-scale, spatially-aware multimodal pretraining framework that jointly learns pathology image and spatial transcriptomics representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224-pixel H&E-stained pathology patches (covering ~50–100 μm, matching the 55 μm Visium spot diameter) alongside spatial transcriptomics gene expression profiles over 20,310 genes. Gene profiles are converted into rank-ordered token sequences of length 1,500 (top genes by normalized expression deviation), and region-level images are constructed as 3×3 grid crops around each patch for multi-scale alignment. Outputs include a 512-dim gene embedding (mean-pooled from a 12-layer transformer) and a vision embedding from UNI (ViT-L/16), which can be used individually or concatenated for downstream tasks: gene expression regression, WSI mutation classification via ABMIL, tissue region linear probing, and unsupervised clustering.

## Method

STAMP employs a two-stage pretraining strategy. Stage 1 pretrains a spatially-aware gene encoder on SpaVis-6M (5.75M Visium entries from 35 organs) using two masked-token objectives: Intrinsic Gene Reconstruction (reconstruct masked tokens within a spot) and Contextual Gene Reconstruction (reconstruct a seed spot's masked tokens from aggregated neighbor embeddings), with mini-batches constructed via nearest-neighbor spatial sampling to preserve local tissue context. Stage 2 performs hierarchical multi-scale contrastive alignment on 697K HEST image–gene pairs, combining four losses: cross-scale patch positioning (a 3×3 grid pretext task with a learnable 'pretext token' that simulates pathologist zoom-in/zoom-out), inter-modal InfoNCE alignment at both patch and region scales, and intra-modal patch-to-region contrastive alignment to expand the receptive field and prevent representation collapse. The gene encoder uses rank-based tokenization (sorting normalized expression deviations descending) to handle sparsity and batch effects, while the vision backbone is the frozen UNI ViT-L/16 to avoid semantic bias from image-text pretraining.

## Results

On gene expression prediction, STAMP_Con (contrastive, 1.44M trainable params) achieves the best MSE and Pearson correlation across all three datasets: PSC (MSE 0.301, PCC-V 0.218, PCC-E 0.278), HHK (MSE 1.233, PCC-V 0.193, PCC-E 0.186), and HER2+ (MSE 0.870, PCC-V 0.279, PCC-E 0.266). The linear-probing variant STAMP_Reg* (9.32M params) also outperforms larger vision-only encoders such as GigaPath* and Hoptimus0* on PCC-V and PCC-E. On DLPFC linear probing, STAMP_F achieves 72.1% balanced accuracy and 0.342 ARI for unsupervised clustering, surpassing the next-best OmiCLIP (65.9% Bal. Acc., 0.246 ARI). On WSI-level LUAD-mutation classification, STAMP achieves competitive or SOTA AUC and F1 across all four gene sub-tasks, surpassing Hoptimus0 and GigaPath.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAMP_Con decisively outperforms the two contrastive-learning baselines (BLEEP and mclSTExp) on all metrics across all three datasets, e.g., on PSC it reduces MSE from 0.366 (BLEEP) to 0.301 and more than doubles PCC-V from 0.095 to 0.218, while using only 1.44M trainable parameters versus 25–130M. Among regression-based methods, STAMP_Reg* (9.32M params) surpasses larger vision-only encoders GigaPath* (17.13M) and Hoptimus0* (17.13M) on PCC-V and PCC-E for PSC (0.204 vs. 0.185/0.192) and HER2+ (0.267 vs. 0.253/0.252), confirming that gene-expression supervision adds value beyond scale. On the cross-platform HER2+ dataset (Spatial Transcriptomics platform, not Visium), STAMP_Con achieves the best PCC-V (0.279) and PCC-E (0.266), demonstrating generalization beyond its training platform. On DLPFC linear probing and unsupervised clustering, STAMP_F (fused gene+vision features) outperforms OmiCLIP, mSTAR, CONCH†, and PLIP† across balanced accuracy, F1, ARI, and NMI. On WSI-level LUAD-mutation classification, STAMP surpasses Hoptimus0 and GigaPath (both with larger vision backbones) and TANGLE (bulk-RNA-guided), confirming that spatially-resolved molecular supervision provides finer-grained signal than patient-level bulk RNA.

## Relation to Prior Work

STAMP extends the multimodal contrastive pretraining paradigm established by vision-language pathology models (PLIP, CONCH, QuiltNet) by replacing the text modality with spatially-resolved gene expression profiles, addressing the molecular specificity gap that natural language cannot fill. It departs from bulk-RNA-guided approaches (TANGLE, mSTAR) by leveraging spatial transcriptomics to capture intra-sample heterogeneity, and from ST-only foundation models (scGPT, Nicheformer, scGPT-Spatial) by jointly learning with pathology images and incorporating spatial neighborhood structure. The two-stage design—spatial-aware gene encoder pretraining via masked token prediction (adapting BERT-style objectives) followed by hierarchical multi-scale contrastive alignment—builds on BLEEP's query-reference strategy and CLIP-Adapter's fine-tuning protocol while introducing novel cross-scale patch positioning and intra-modal patch-region alignment losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STAMP and SpaVis-6M establish the first large-scale infrastructure for spatially-aware multimodal pretraining in computational pathology, demonstrating that molecular supervision provides a task-agnostic signal that consistently improves representations across classification, clustering, and gene expression prediction. The key open question is data scale: with only 697K paired samples (versus hundreds of millions in vision-language models), the framework's ceiling is constrained by the cost and scarcity of spatial transcriptomics assays. Future work must address platform heterogeneity (Visium-only training), incorporate non-cancerous tissues for robust baselines, and extend to emerging technologies like Xenium to realize the full potential of molecule-guided pathology representation learning.
