## Problem

Existing video benchmarks evaluate LVLMs on overall accuracy across entire sequences, providing minimal diagnostic value for a critical failure mode: contextual positional bias, where models comprehend identical content inconsistently depending on where it appears in the video context. No prior benchmark systematically isolates and measures this bias across diverse context types, lengths, and model architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261569/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance consists of a standardized probe (6 uniformly sampled frames from a curated video segment) inserted at one of 10 positions within a 54-frame context, paired with a multiple-choice or open-ended question. The context is one of six types (template, short video, long video, multi-video, long-text, interleaved video-text), enabling controlled variation of complexity and length. Outputs include per-position relative scores (accuracy normalized by standalone probe accuracy S_meta), three statistical metrics (P_mean, P_ran, P_var), a bias pattern classification via polynomial fitting, and a composite metric for cross-model ranking.

## Method

Video-LevelGauge adopts a needle-in-a-haystack paradigm adapted for video: standardized probes (video segments with validated QA pairs) are inserted at controlled positions within customized contexts, isolating positional effects from confounding factors like varying QA difficulty or information leakage. Probe QAs are constructed via a three-step workflow (LLM generation, multi-model blind filtering for text-only and single-frame answerability, human refinement) and validated to be near-random under text-only input (~25%) and far from saturation with single frames. The analysis method introduces a relative score metric that normalizes positional accuracy by each model's standalone competence (S_meta), enabling fair cross-model comparison, and a Bias Pattern Recognition (BPR) classifier that uses linear and quadratic polynomial fitting with MSE thresholds to categorize models into five patterns: Stable, Head preference, Neighbor preference, Lost-in-the-middle (U), and Volatile (W). The benchmark comprises 438 videos, 1,177 MCQAs across six tasks, and 120 open-ended questions, supporting flexible control over context length, probe position, and context composition.

## Results

On Video-LevelGauge, Gemini 2.5 Pro exhibits the least positional bias among all 27 evaluated LVLMs, with P_ran = 2.0, P_var = 0.9, and a Stable bias pattern (BPR), alongside the highest S_meta of 81.7%. GPT-4o-latest (P_ran = 2.9, P_var = 1.4) and GLM-4.5V† (P_ran = 2.7, P_var = 1.0) follow closely, all showing near-uniform performance across probe positions. In contrast, open-source models like LLaMA-VID (P_ran = 12.3, P_var = 17.3) and NVILA (P_ran = 13.9, P_var = 14.7) display severe positional bias with volatile or neighbor-preference patterns. The composite metric (CM) ranking confirms a clear gap: commercial models cluster at the low-bias end while most open-source models exhibit pronounced head, neighbor, or U-shaped biases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among commercial models, Gemini 2.5 Pro (P_ran = 2.0, P_var = 0.9) outperforms GPT-4o-latest (P_ran = 2.9) and Doubao-Seed-1.6 (P_ran = 3.2) in positional stability, while Qwen-VL-Max lags significantly (P_ran = 8.0, P_var = 8.0) despite a high S_meta of 70.5%, illustrating that strong visual understanding does not guarantee positional robustness. Among open-source models, GLM-4.5V† (108B, P_ran = 2.7, P_var = 1.0) and InternVL3-78B (P_ran = 3.9, P_var = 2.8) approach commercial-level stability, whereas 7B models like LLaMA-VID (P_ran = 12.3) and NVILA (P_ran = 13.9) show severe bias. Two-stage methods (Caption+GPT-4, Caption+Qwen3) exhibit U-shaped patterns consistent with known LLM positional biases. Notably, multi-modal reasoning (†) consistently reduces bias: GLM-4.5V† improves over GLM-4.5V (P_ran 3.4→2.7), MiMo-VL-RL† over MiMo-VL-RL (P_ran 8.0→5.8), and Gemini 2.5 Pro† achieves the best overall score. The paper also finds that positional bias intensifies with context length and is most severe in interleaved video-text contexts, where even top models degrade substantially.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the needle-in-a-haystack (NIAH) paradigm from LLM long-context evaluation (Liu et al. 2024b) and multimodal NIAH benchmarks (Wang et al. 2024b, Lu et al. 2025, Ye et al. 2025) into a dedicated positional bias diagnostic for video understanding. Unlike existing video benchmarks (MVBench, MLVU, LongVideoBench, VideoMME) that measure aggregate performance across entire sequences, and unlike video NIAH benchmarks (VNBench, LV-Haystack, V-NIAH) that assess temporal search, Video-LevelGauge isolates *inconsistency* in comprehension of identical content at different positions via standardized probes inserted into customizable contexts. The analysis methodology draws on the serial position effect from cognitive psychology and the 'lost in the middle' characterization from LLM research, extending both to the video-text interleaved setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic evaluation of positional bias in LVLMs, revealing that most open-source models exhibit significant head, neighbor, or U-shaped biases while commercial models (Gemini 2.5 Pro, GPT-4o) remain largely stable. Critically, the paper shows that long-context training (LongVA, LongVILA) does not reliably eliminate positional bias, and that overall visual understanding capability (S_meta) is uncorrelated with positional consistency—challenging the assumption that stronger models or longer training horizons naturally fix this issue. Open questions remain about the formation mechanisms of positional bias in video, whether LLM-level bias mitigation transfers to LVLMs, and how to mitigate the amplified bias observed in interleaved video-text contexts prevalent in multi-turn dialogue and RAG settings.
