## Problem

Invariant Risk Minimization (IRM) has become a central framework for learning representations robust to distribution shifts, but all existing IRM methods require labeled data to define the invariance constraint on prediction risk. This paper asks whether invariant representations can be learned entirely from unlabeled data by redefining invariance as equality of feature distributions across environments, thereby removing the dependency on labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260754/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled images partitioned into multiple environments (e.g., MNIST digits with white squares in different corners, digits encoded in different RGB channels, or CelebA faces split by gender). For PICA, the input reduces to per-environment covariance matrices; for VIAE, raw images are fed through environment-specific encoders. The primary outputs are a factorized latent code (invariant Z_inv and environment-specific Z_e), reconstructed or generated samples, and environment-transferred samples where the invariant content is preserved while the environmental appearance is swapped to a target environment's prior.

## Method

The paper introduces a novel unsupervised Structural Causal Model (SCM) that decomposes the latent space into invariant and environment-dependent components, unifying prior FIIF/PIIF and causal/anti-causal assumptions. Two algorithms instantiate this model: PICA, a linear method that finds invariant projection directions by intersecting the null space of pairwise covariance differences with the top eigenvectors of the summed covariance, and VIAE, a variational autoencoder with a shared invariant encoder (which takes both X and Z_e as input due to the collider structure Z_inv → X ← Z_e), per-environment encoders for Z_e, and a shared environment-agnostic decoder. The ELBO is derived under the causal factorization, yielding separate KL terms for the invariant and environmental components with environment-specific priors N(μ_e(e), I) on Z_e. A key practical capability is environment transfer: by fixing Z_inv and resampling Z_e from a different environment's prior, data points can be converted to a target environment, and in some cases even from unseen source environments by averaging outputs of all trained environmental encoders.

## Results

On SMNIST and SCMNIST, the invariant encoder captures label (digit) information with 84.5% and 83.2% accuracy respectively, while the environmental encoder achieves near-random (55.6% and 58.3%) accuracy on environment prediction from invariant features, confirming the invariant space is environment-agnostic. The environmental features perfectly identify the source environment (100% accuracy), demonstrating clean disentanglement. The label classifier using only environmental features drops to 36.2% (SMNIST) and 34.5% (SCMNIST), well below the invariant-based classifier, confirming that relevant task information is concentrated in the invariant subspace.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include direct comparisons against other IRM or representation learning methods, as it introduces a new unsupervised framework rather than competing with existing supervised approaches. Instead, validation is performed through internal consistency checks: four linear classifiers trained on the learned latent representations confirm that invariant features retain task-relevant information (digit identity) while being statistically independent of the environment, and that environmental features fully encode the source environment. The near-random environment prediction from invariant features (55.6%/58.3% vs. 50% chance baseline) is highlighted as the most critical result, providing empirical evidence that spurious environmental correlations are effectively filtered out. The authors note that generalization to unseen environments (e.g., SCMNIST's blue channel) fails when training environments do not span the full environment space, consistent with theoretical limitations identified by Rosenfeld et al. (2020).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Invariant Risk Minimization (IRM) framework of Arjovsky et al. (2019) from the supervised to the unsupervised setting, redefining invariance as equality of feature distributions across environments rather than optimality of a per-environment predictor. The deep method (VIAE) builds directly on the VAE architecture of Kingma & Welling (2014) and adopts the dual invariant/environmental latent factorization also used by Salaudeen & Koyejo (2024), while the linear method (PICA) is a constrained variant of PCA under Gaussian assumptions. The causal structure is grounded in the SCM literature (Peters et al., 2017; Schölkopf et al., 2021), and the paper acknowledges prior unsupervised invariant representation work (Lopez et al., 2018; Moyer et al., 2018) while positioning its contribution as a formal IRM-constrained formulation with an explicit generative model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the label dependency, this work opens IRM to settings where annotations are expensive or unavailable (e.g., medical imaging, sensor data), and the environment-transfer perspective—aligning all data to a single environment rather than stripping spurious features—offers a practical reframing of what 'solving' IRM means. However, empirical validation is limited to relatively simple datasets (MNIST variants, CelebA), the unseen-environment transfer is heuristic and demonstrably fails when training environments do not span the full environmental space (SCMNIST blue channel), and the PICA analysis is restricted to two environments with Gaussian/linear assumptions. Key open questions include whether meta-learning can enable principled zero-shot environment transfer, and whether the framework scales to complex, high-dimensional real-world data with modern generative architectures (diffusion models, GANs).
