## Problem

Industrial signal monitoring requires anomaly detection under data scarcity, distribution shift, and the need for interpretable alarm rates, yet existing conformal anomaly detection methods assume exchangeability (violated in time series), threshold arbitrary scores without probabilistic meaning, or fail to adapt online to non-stationary error distributions. No prior approach simultaneously provides a directly interpretable p-value score, finite-sample false-alarm guarantees under non-exchangeability, and online adaptation to recurring temporal patterns.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a context window of length 52 from a streaming time series, which is fed to a frozen pre-trained TSFM (TTM, Chronos-Bolt-Small, or TiRex) to produce D=15 multi-horizon point forecasts. Nonconformity scores are the absolute forecast errors |Y_{t+1} − Ŷ_{t+1}^d| at each horizon d. The output is a single adaptive conformal p-value β̄_{t+1} ∈ [0,1] (median across horizons) that directly equals the false alarm rate, plus a binary anomaly flag when β̄_{t+1} < α for a user-chosen threshold. The method operates fully online with a sliding buffer of n past scores and periodic weight updates every n_b=10 samples.

## Method

W1-ACAS is a post-hoc, model-agnostic framework that converts TSFM forecast errors into calibrated p-values via weighted conformal quantiles, without any fine-tuning of the forecaster. The key mapping β_w(S_{t+1}) = sup{α : S_{t+1} ≤ Q_{1−α}(s, w)} assigns each nonconformity score a conformalized p-value whose threshold directly controls the false alarm rate, with guarantees extending beyond exchangeability via Barber et al.'s weighted conformal bounds. The weights w are learned online by minimizing the 1-Wasserstein distance between the empirical CDF of recent p-values and the uniform distribution, subject to an effective-sample-size constraint ‖w‖₁ > 1/α_c − 1, using projected gradient descent with closed-form piecewise-linear derivatives. This objective pushes the p-value distribution toward uniformity (perfect calibration) while the Wasserstein weighting implicitly emphasizes past errors with similar distributions, capturing periodicity and recurring patterns. Across D=15 forecast horizons, per-horizon p-values are aggregated by median, requiring majority agreement for an anomaly flag.

## Results

On seven univariate benchmark datasets (YAHOO, NEK, NAB, MSL, IOPS, STOCK, WSD), W1-ACAS paired with TiRex achieves the best PA-F1 of 0.925±0.048 and Affiliation-F of 0.897±0.064, with the lowest calibration error (CalErr 0.025±0.031) among all methods. With Chronos, W1-ACAS reaches PA-F1 0.912±0.066 and CalErr 0.025±0.029, outperforming the same forecaster's Conformal baseline (PA-F1 0.863, CalErr 0.038) and Gaussian baseline (PA-F1 0.716, CalErr 0.075). On threshold-independent metrics (AUC-PR, VUS-PR), W1-ACAS remains competitive (0.34–0.36 AUC-PR, 0.44–0.45 VUS-PR), while achieving the most conservative FPR-threshold curves in the low-FPR regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

W1-ACAS consistently outperforms all same-forecaster baselines: against the Conformal offline approach it gains +0.05 to +0.05 in PA-F1 and reduces CalErr from ~0.038–0.044 to ~0.025–0.029; against the Gaussian baseline the PA-F1 improvement is +0.17 to +0.21 with CalErr dropping from ~0.075–0.090 to ~0.025–0.029. Against classical distance/density methods (POLY, Sub-KNN, KShape, Sub-PCA, SAND), W1-ACAS achieves roughly 0.35–0.47 higher PA-F1 and 0.25–0.43 lower FPR. Among deep learning baselines, W1-ACAS (with TiRex) beats CNN on PA-F1 (0.925 vs 0.858) and dramatically on CalErr (0.025 vs 0.643), and surpasses MOMENT zero-shot scoring by +0.33 PA-F1 (0.925 vs 0.596) with CalErr 0.025 vs 0.417. On threshold-independent metrics (AUC-PR, VUS-PR), W1-ACAS is competitive but not always best—MOMENT_ZS achieves the highest VUS-PR (0.461) and TTM-Gaussian the highest AUC-PR among non-W1-ACAS methods—indicating the conformal p-value mapping optimizes for calibrated, interpretable thresholds rather than raw ranking quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the weighted conformal prediction framework of Barber et al. (2023), which provides false-alarm guarantees beyond exchangeability, and on split conformal prediction (Papadopoulos et al., 2002) as the base post-hoc scoring mechanism. It departs from prior adaptive conformal methods (Gibbs & Candes, 2021; 2024) that adjust a single quantile online, and from weighted conformal approaches (Lei & Wasserman, 2014; Guan, 2019; Han et al., 2022) that use fixed or covariate-based weights, by instead learning weights via Wasserstein-distance minimization to achieve uniform calibration across all false-alarm rates. The key conceptual departure is the integration of pretrained time series foundation models (Chronos, TTM, TiRex) as zero-shot forecasters into a conformal anomaly-detection pipeline, combined with a p-value mapping (β_w) that makes the anomaly score directly interpretable as a false-alarm probability—something prior conformal anomaly detectors (Angelopoulos & Bates, 2021; Bates et al., 2023) do not provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical gap in industrial signal monitoring: it requires no fine-tuning, no large labeled datasets, and produces interpretable p-values that operators can directly map to acceptable alarm rates, making it deployable in resource-constrained settings where TSFMs are increasingly accessible. The consistent calibration improvement (CalErr ~0.025 vs. 0.038–0.090 for baselines) and the most conservative FPR-threshold behavior suggest the approach is robust in practice, not just in theory. Open questions include how the Wasserstein-based weight learning scales to very long sequences or highly non-stationary signals, whether contextual features (e.g., day-of-week, operational mode) can further refine the weighting, and how the method performs when the underlying TSFM itself is poorly calibrated for a given domain. The multivariate extension via p-value aggregation is outlined but not deeply explored.
