## Problem

Existing 3DGS watermarking methods embed watermarks by directly modifying Gaussian attributes, making them highly vulnerable to quantization-based model compression, which shifts parameter distributions and severely degrades watermark integrity. No prior work has specifically addressed the challenge of preserving watermark detectability after a 3DGS model undergoes compression for storage or transmission, a critical gap for real-world copyright protection of distributed 3D assets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263485/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes per-scene training images and a fixed binary watermark message (32, 48, or 64 bits) as input, and produces a watermarked anchor-based 3DGS model (Scaffold-GS architecture) as output. For extraction, a rendered image from the watermarked model is passed through a DWT to isolate the LL subband, which is then decoded by a pre-trained HiDDeN decoder (kept frozen during training) to recover the binary message. The model is trained from scratch rather than fine-tuned, and compression (HAC or ContextGS) is applied post-training to evaluate robustness.

## Method

CompMarkGS embeds a learnable watermark feature f′ into the anchor feature f of an anchor-based 3DGS (Scaffold-GS) via a tanh-bounded element-wise sum, so the watermark is hidden behind the implicit MLP that predicts Gaussian attributes, making direct manipulation difficult. A quantization distortion layer (QDL) injects uniform noise scaled by a learned per-anchor quantization scale into the watermarked anchor feature during training, teaching the watermark to survive quantization-based compression. A frequency-aware anchor growing (FAG) strategy applies DFT and a high-pass filter to identify high-frequency error regions between rendered and ground-truth images, then selectively densifies anchors only in those regions to preserve fine details. An HSV loss with hue-based binary masks for R/G/B channels targets color artifacts that RGB-space losses miss. The total loss combines the Scaffold-GS reconstruction loss, HSV loss, a frequency loss (mean SSIM error in high-frequency bands), and a BCE message loss, optimized end-to-end for 30K iterations with a fixed pre-trained HiDDeN decoder.

## Results

On the 48-bit setting averaged over Blender, LLFF, and Mip-NeRF 360, CompMarkGS achieves 95.92% bit accuracy and 27.65 PSNR after HAC compression, and 94.03% bit accuracy and 27.55 PSNR after ContextGS compression—nearly identical to its pre-compression values (95.95% / 27.68 PSNR). In contrast, all four baselines suffer severe degradation after HAC compression, with bit accuracy dropping to 52–58% and PSNR falling below 14. Under ContextGS compression, CompMarkGS also leads, maintaining 94.03% bit accuracy versus 67–90% for baselines, while achieving the best rendering quality (27.55 PSNR, 0.844 SSIM, 0.173 LPIPS) and the smallest model size (5.72 MB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CompMarkGS decisively outperforms all four baselines after compression, which is the paper's central claim. Under HAC compression, CompMarkGS retains 95.92% bit accuracy while WateRF drops to 54.40%, GaussianMarker to 58.34%, 3D-GSW to 53.48%, and GuardSplat to 52.72%—a gap of 37–43 percentage points. Under the milder ContextGS compression, CompMarkGS still leads with 94.03% versus WateRF (90.36%), GaussianMarker (87.95%), 3D-GSW (79.75%), and GuardSplat (67.16%). On rendering quality after compression, CompMarkGS achieves 27.65 PSNR (HAC) and 27.55 PSNR (ContextGS), far exceeding baselines whose PSNR collapses to 12–20 under HAC. The paper also shows CompMarkGS wins on robustness to image distortions (Gaussian noise, rotation, JPEG, etc.) and model distortions (cloning, pruning, parameter noise), and maintains the highest bit accuracy across 32/48/64-bit message lengths (91.29% at 64 bits vs. 72.83–88.21% for baselines).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Scaffold-GS anchor-based 3DGS architecture, embedding a learnable watermark feature into anchor features that are then processed by implicit MLPs to predict Gaussian attributes, rather than modifying explicit Gaussian parameters as prior 3DGS watermarking methods (GaussianMarker, 3D-GSW, GuardSplat) do. It adapts the quantization mechanism from HAC compression into a training-time data augmentation (the Quantization Distortion Layer), a concept borrowed from differentiable distortion layers in 2D deep watermarking (HiDDeN) but never previously applied to 3D model compression. The watermark extraction relies on the pre-trained HiDDeN decoder operating on the DWT low-frequency subband, and the frequency-aware anchor growing strategy repurposes DFT-based high-frequency analysis to guide structural densification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As 3DGS models are increasingly deployed in commercial AR/VR and digital-twin applications where compression is mandatory for storage and transmission, this work addresses a previously unaddressed gap: existing watermarks are essentially destroyed by quantization-based compression. The dramatic performance gap (baselines dropping to ~54% bit accuracy under HAC vs. ~96% for CompMarkGS) underscores how critical compression robustness is for practical copyright protection. Limitations include reliance on a fixed pre-trained HiDDeN decoder, evaluation only on two anchor-based compression schemes (HAC, ContextGS), and per-scene training rather than generalizable watermarking. Open questions include whether the QDL generalizes to non-anchor-based 3DGS or other 3D representations, and whether the method can be made lighter for real-time embedding on resource-constrained devices.
