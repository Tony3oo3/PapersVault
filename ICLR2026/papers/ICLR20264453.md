## Problem

Despite over 200 multi-modal benchmarks existing, there is no systematic characterization of what each dataset actually measures in terms of intra-modality (single-modality) versus inter-modality (joint) dependencies. This ambiguity means it is unclear whether reported model gains reflect genuine multi-modal reasoning or exploitation of uni-modal shortcuts, and whether new benchmarks add value or are redundant. The paper further reveals that benchmarks designed to eliminate text-only biases have inadvertently introduced strong image-only dependencies, perpetuating a cycle of superficial fixes.

## Input / Output

The evaluation framework takes an RGB image paired with a text question containing multiple-choice options as input and produces four performance scores per benchmark: standard accuracy (paired modalities), image-only accuracy (text replaced by a randomly sampled question from another sample), text-only accuracy (image replaced by a random image), and random baseline (both modalities independently shuffled). The shuffling preserves each modality's marginal distribution rather than zeroing or perturbing inputs, ensuring the model still receives valid but uncorrelated data. Analysis is performed at both the aggregate benchmark level and at sub-category granularity (e.g., question type, object category, grade level) to expose hidden intra-modality dependencies masked by global metrics.

## Method

The paper adapts the Perceptual Score (Gat et al., 2021) by permuting one modality across samples while keeping the other aligned with ground-truth labels, then measuring the resulting performance drop to quantify each modality's marginal contribution. To reduce idiosyncratic model biases, results are obtained via a majority-vote ensemble of three Cambrian-1 models (8B, 13B, 34B) built on Llama-3, Vicuna-1.5, and Nous-Yi backbones with SigLIP/DINOv2/ConvNeXt-XXL vision encoders. The framework is applied across 23 multiple-choice VQA benchmarks spanning general VQA, expert knowledge, spatial understanding, OCR, and document/chart comprehension, and further decomposed into sub-categories within each benchmark. The key finding is that no benchmark is purely inter-modal: only four of 23 datasets (MME, POPE, COCO, V* Bench) exhibit predominantly inter-modality dependencies, while most contain substantial intra-modality shortcuts that vary unpredictably across sub-populations. The authors also show that increasing model scale does not mitigate these biases and can even exacerbate them, and that different model families (LLaVA-Next, Cambrian-1, Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL) exhibit comparable levels of uni-modal reliance.

## Results

Across 23 MCVQA benchmarks, only 4 datasets (MME, POPE, COCO, V* Bench) exhibit predominantly inter-modality dependencies, meaning the model must jointly use both image and text. Text-only intra-modality dependencies are widespread: models achieve +26% above random on GQA, +17.5% on ScienceQA, +11.35% on MMMU, and +34.94% on AI2D using only the question. Image-only dependencies are equally concerning: MMBench shows a +41% above-random gap for image-only inputs, and this pattern persists in TextVQA, ChartQA, SEED-Bench, MMMU-Pro, MMVP, Q-Bench, and MM-Star. Scaling from 8B to 34B parameters does not mitigate these biases; on MMMU and MMBench, larger models actually increase their reliance on uni-modal shortcuts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new method to compare against baselines; instead, it characterizes existing benchmarks using an adapted Perceptual Score methodology. The key comparisons are across model scales (Cambrian-1 8B, 13B, 34B) and model types (Cambrian-1 8B, LLaVA-Next 7B, Qwen2.5-VL 7B, Qwen3-VL 8B). On MMMU, scaling to 34B increases both overall accuracy and the reliance on image-only and text-only dependencies simultaneously. On MMBench, larger models show greater image-only dependency, and Qwen models improve overall MMBench accuracy by ~10% over Cambrian-1 while also significantly increasing image-only performance. On POPE (inter-modality only), all models exhibit near-random performance under single-modality inputs regardless of scale or architecture, confirming the benchmark's design. On GQA and MMMU-Pro, comparable levels of text and image biases are observed across all four model types, indicating these are dataset-inherent rather than model-specific artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the intra-/inter-modality dependency framework from Liang et al. (2023) and Madaan et al. (2024) at a scale and breadth not previously attempted, using the Perceptual Score methodology of Gat et al. (2021) as its measurement tool. Prior analyses of modality reliance were confined to synthetic data, single benchmarks like VQA, or earlier-generation models; here the authors extend the same shuffling-based diagnostic to 23 diverse MCVQA benchmarks and a family of modern 8B–34B MLLMs (Cambrian-1, LLaVA-Next, Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL). The key departure is the systematic, cross-benchmark and cross-sub-category characterization, which prior work had not provided, and the explicit framing of benchmark design as an iterative 'cat-and-mouse' cycle rather than a one-shot fix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that only 4 of 23 widely-used benchmarks are predominantly inter-modality dependent, and that efforts to eliminate text-only shortcuts (TextVQA, ChartQA, SEED-Bench, MMMU-Pro, MMVP, Q-Bench, MM-Star) have instead introduced strong image-only biases, challenges the field's assumption that successive benchmark revisions are converging on true multi-modal evaluation. The observation that larger models become *more* reliant on unimodal shortcuts rather than less is a direct challenge to the scaling narrative. Open questions include whether open-ended generation (rather than multiple choice) can break the unimodal-shortcut cycle, whether models can be trained to abstain on ambiguous inputs, and whether reporting modality-specific baselines alongside aggregate scores will become standard practice.
