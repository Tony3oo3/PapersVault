## Problem

Current brain-to-image decoding methods align brain signals primarily with high-level semantic embeddings (e.g., CLIP), neglecting low-level pixel details such as color, texture, and layout. This semantic-only focus limits both retrieval accuracy and reconstruction fidelity, and prevents a comprehensive understanding of the hierarchical visual information encoded in brain signals. The structural gap between the temporal dynamics of EEG/MEG and the multiscale organization of visual representations further hinders effective cross-modal alignment.

## Input / Output

The method takes preprocessed EEG (17 occipito-parietal channels at 250 Hz over 0–1000 ms) or MEG (271 channels at 200 Hz over 0–1000 ms) signals as input, flattened to a single vector. For retrieval, it produces a ranked list over a 200-image gallery in a zero-shot setting; for reconstruction, it generates a 512×512 RGB image via a 4-step SDXL-Turbo diffusion sampler conditioned solely on the brain-derived embedding (no text prompt). The VAE branch operates at 128×128 input resolution, while the diffusion backbone operates at 512×512.

## Method

The core contribution is a Hierarchical Visual Fuser (HVF) that projects K=3 pretrained encoders—OpenAI CLIP RN50, LAION CLIP ViT-B/32, and an SDXL VAE—into a shared 1024-d space via learned linear maps, then fuses them with a residual MLP and LayerNorm to produce a single fused visual token z_f. A Brain Projection (MBP) module maps the flattened EEG/MEG signal to the same 1024-d space, and a symmetric InfoNCE loss (CLIP-style, learnable temperature) aligns brain and fused visual embeddings. For reconstruction, a Fusion Prior is pretrained on ~1.3M ImageNet-1k images with empty text prompts: the fused token is projected to z_c and injected into a frozen SDXL UNet via IP-Adapter's decoupled cross-attention, learning a stable text-free mapping from fused features to diffusion conditions. Training proceeds in two stages—(i) pretrain the fusion prior on large-scale visual data, then (ii) freeze the prior and train only the brain encoder with InfoNCE—ensuring brain embeddings land in a stable, pretrained fusion space. The key empirical finding is that EEG/MEG signals jointly encode both high-level semantics and low-level pixel details, and adding the VAE latent on top of semantic CLIP encoders consistently and substantially boosts decoding performance over either modality alone.

## Results

On 200-way zero-shot retrieval, the proposed method achieves 75.7% Top-1 / 94.6% Top-5 on THINGS-EEG (intra-subject) and 20.0% / 44.1% (inter-subject), substantially outperforming the strongest prior method UBP (50.9% / 79.7% intra; 12.4% / 33.4% inter). On THINGS-MEG, it reaches 33.7% / 60.5% (intra) and 5.4% / 15.2% (inter) versus UBP's 26.7% / 55.2% and 2.2% / 10.4%. For brain-to-image reconstruction on EEG (subject-averaged), the method attains PixCorr 0.195, CLIP similarity 0.808, and SwAV distance 0.554, improving over CognitionCapturer-All (0.150, 0.715, 0.590) and ATM on the single-subject setting (0.227 vs 0.160 PixCorr for subject 8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight baselines on every retrieval setting where they overlap. Against the strongest prior method UBP (Wu et al., 2025), gains are +24.8 pp Top-1 on EEG intra-subject (75.7 vs 50.9), +7.6 pp on EEG inter-subject (20.0 vs 12.4), +7.0 pp on MEG intra (33.7 vs 26.7), and +3.2 pp on MEG inter (5.4 vs 2.2). Compared to ATM (Li et al., 2024), the improvement is +47.2 pp Top-1 on EEG intra (75.7 vs 28.5) and +8.2 pp on EEG inter (20.0 vs 11.8). CognitionCapturer (Zhang et al., 2025) reports only EEG intra-subject retrieval (35.6% Top-1) and reconstruction; the proposed method exceeds it by +40.1 pp on retrieval and by +0.045 PixCorr / +0.093 CLIP on subject-averaged EEG reconstruction. On reconstruction, the method beats ATM on subject-8 EEG across all seven metrics (e.g., PixCorr 0.227 vs 0.160, CLIP 0.826 vs 0.786) and beats Brain Decoding (Benchetrit et al., 2023) on MEG for PixCorr (0.137 vs 0.076), AlexNet-5 (0.836 vs 0.826), Inception (0.721 vs 0.671), and CLIP (0.775 vs 0.767), though it trails B.D. on SSIM (0.292 vs 0.336) and SwAV (0.600 vs 0.584). NICE and its spatial variants (Song et al., 2024) are surpassed by 50+ pp Top-1 on EEG intra-subject, and BraVL (Du et al., 2023) and MB2C (Wei et al., 2024) are outperformed by 47+ pp on the same metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the brain-to-image decoding line of work (NICE, ATM, UBP, CognitionCapturer) that aligns EEG/MEG signals to visual embeddings via contrastive learning, but departs from the prevailing single-encoder paradigm by fusing multiple pretrained visual encoders (CLIP RN50, LAION CLIP B32, SDXL VAE) into a unified hierarchical token. The reconstruction pipeline builds on IP-Adapter's decoupled cross-attention mechanism for conditioning a frozen SDXL UNet, but introduces a novel 'Fusion Prior'—a pretrained mapping from the fused visual token to diffusion conditions learned on ImageNet-1k with empty text prompts—that prior brain-decoding methods lack. The plug-and-play design, where the visual interface is frozen and only the brain-side projector is trained, contrasts with prior approaches that co-train or tightly couple both modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that low-level VAE pixel features contribute more to brain-image alignment than high-level CLIP semantics (removing VAE drops top-1 from 75.7% to 29.6%, while removing RN50 only drops it to 70.6%) challenges the field's implicit assumption that semantic alignment is the dominant axis of brain-visual correspondence. The plug-and-play architecture—where swapping brain backbones (EEGNet, TSConv, ATM, etc.) into the same frozen visual interface yields consistent gains without retraining the visual side—offers a practical recipe for the community. Limitations include evaluation restricted to THINGS-EEG/MEG with a single hand-picked encoder configuration, and the reconstruction gains, while consistent, are more modest than the retrieval gains, leaving open whether the fusion prior generalizes to fMRI or other modalities with different spatial-temporal characteristics.
