## Problem

Current audio-driven video avatar models operate at a purely reactive level, learning low-level correlations between audio and lip movement while producing repetitive, contextually incoherent gestures. The paper frames this limitation through Kahneman's dual-process theory: existing methods simulate only fast, intuitive 'System 1' cognition but lack the deliberative, goal-oriented 'System 2' reasoning needed to generate motions that are semantically aligned with speech content, emotion, and intent.

## Input / Output

The system takes a reference image (for identity), an audio clip (driving signal), and an optional text prompt as input, and produces a 120-frame character animation video at 24 fps and 480p resolution (upscaled to 720p for benchmarking). The MLLM agent first consumes all three inputs to generate a structured multi-shot action schedule in text form, which then conditions the diffusion model alongside the raw audio. The reference image is not used as a direct conditioning signal during training; instead, at inference it is placed at a RoPE-shifted position beyond the final generated frame as a 'pseudo-last-frame,' and the model operates autoregressively for longer sequences by chaining clips.

## Method

The framework combines two innovations to simulate both cognitive systems. For System 2, a two-stage MLLM pipeline (Analyzer using miniCPM-o, Planner using Seed-1.5-VL) performs step-by-step reasoning over the multimodal inputs—transcribing speech, inferring emotion and intent, segmenting content—then outputs a structured per-shot action schedule that guides the synthesis model. For System 1, a specialized MMDiT architecture fuses three symmetric branches (video, text, audio) via a shared multi-head self-attention mechanism rather than cross-attention, with a two-stage MM-Warmup training strategy that first specializes the audio branch before joint fine-tuning to prevent modality interference. A key architectural contribution is the Pseudo-Last-Frame (PLF) strategy: during training, the model is conditioned on ground-truth first and last frames (each with 0.1 dropout) instead of a reference image, eliminating the spurious 'reference must appear in output' artifact; at inference, the user's reference image is placed at a RoPE position shifted 30 frames beyond the final generated frame, guiding identity without constraining dynamic motion. The model is trained with a flow-matching objective in the latent space of a 3D VAE, using a three-stage procedure (audio-branch warm-up, main training on 15,000 hours of curated video, and fine-tuning on a 100-hour high-quality subset).

## Results

On the CyberHost full-body benchmark, the proposed method achieves the best image quality (IQA 4.144), aesthetics (ASE 3.030), FID (31.160), and by far the highest hand keypoint variance (HKV 72.113 vs. 62.753 for the next-best MultiTalk), indicating substantially more dynamic and expressive motion. On CelebV-HQ portrait animation, the method is competitive with OmniHuman-1, achieving slightly better FID (31.320 vs. 31.435) and FVD (45.771 vs. 46.393) while trailing marginally on Sync-C (5.053 vs. 5.199). In user studies, the method earns a 33% top-1 selection rate versus 22% for OmniHuman-1 and 18% for MultiTalk, and achieves a +0.71 GSB score against Wan2.2-S2V with dramatically lower artifact rates (LSI 0.04 vs. 0.24, MU 0.08 vs. 0.60).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest competitor OmniHuman-1, the proposed method wins on IQA (4.144 vs. 4.142), ASE (3.030 vs. 3.024), FID (31.160 vs. 31.641), and HKV (72.113 vs. 47.561) on CyberHost, but trails slightly on Sync-C (7.243 vs. 7.443) and HKC (0.875 vs. 0.898); on CelebV-HQ the gap narrows further with OmniHuman-1 edging ahead on IQA and Sync-C. Against earlier DiT-based full-body methods (Skyreel-A1, FantasyTalking, OmniAvatar, MultiTalk), the proposed method dominates across all metrics, with particularly large margins on HKV (72.113 vs. 18.8–62.8) and FID (31.160 vs. 37.3–69.6). On the portrait task, the method substantially outperforms older approaches like SadTalker (IQA 3.817 vs. 2.953, FVD 45.771 vs. 171.848) and Hallo (Sync-C 5.053 vs. 4.130), while remaining competitive with the more recent Loopy and Hallo-3. In the multi-person setting, the method surpasses InterActHuman on Sync-D (6.904 vs. 8.163), HKC (0.614 vs. 0.553), HKV (158.36 vs. 103.91), and all subjective metrics (GSB +0.26 vs. −0.26). In the head-to-head subjective comparison against the proprietary Wan2.2-S2V, the proposed method wins overwhelmingly with a GSB of +0.71 and artifact rates an order of magnitude lower (MU 0.08 vs. 0.60).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MMDiT architecture (Esser et al., 2024) and the Seaweed-7B video foundation model (Seawead et al., 2025), extending them with a dedicated audio branch and a novel pseudo-last-frame conditioning strategy. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm in audio-driven avatar generation (OmniHuman-1, CyberHost, SkyReels-A1, etc.), which treats motion synthesis as a direct reactive mapping from audio to pixels, by introducing an MLLM-based agentic reasoning module that generates structured textual action plans before synthesis. The dual-process (System 1 / System 2) framing from Kahneman's cognitive science provides the organizing principle: prior methods simulate only fast, reactive System 1, while this work explicitly adds a deliberative System 2 planning stage, a distinction the authors claim is novel for the avatar domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled diagnosis of why current avatar models produce repetitive, context-blind motions and a practical recipe (MLLM planning + symmetric multimodal DiT fusion + PLF) to address it, with strong user-study evidence that the gains are perceptual rather than captured by standard metrics. The PLF strategy and MM-Warmup training are reusable architectural techniques that could benefit other conditional video generation tasks. Open questions include whether the MLLM planning stage can be trained end-to-end with the diffusion model, how the approach scales to very long-form or highly interactive scenarios, and whether the cognitive-science framing is genuinely explanatory or primarily a useful design metaphor. The added inference latency of the agentic pipeline and the dependence on MLLM quality remain practical constraints.
