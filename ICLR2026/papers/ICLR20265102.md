## Problem

Existing 3D multimodal large language models treat objects as monolithic, indivisible geometry, preventing fine-grained part-level understanding, grounding, and editing. No prior system simultaneously names parts, grounds them to persistent bounding boxes, and compiles executable add/delete/modify programs through a single language-native interface, leaving a gap between 3D understanding and controllable part-aware generation/editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265102/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB point cloud (40,960 points with XYZ+normals for the structure encoder; 10,240 points with XYZ+RGB for the semantic encoder) alongside a natural language prompt. It autoregressively emits a single token sequence in a structured planning grammar: part-level axis-aligned bounding boxes quantized into 128 discrete bins (wrapped in <boxs>/<boxe> tokens), semantic descriptions at coarse or fine granularity, and edit operators (<adds>/<adde>, <dels>/<dele>, <mods>/<mode>). This discrete program is model-agnostic and is consumed by external geometry engines (OmniPart for synthesis, VoxHammer for localized editing) rather than producing geometry directly.

## Method

Part-X-MLLM uses a dual-encoder architecture that decouples geometric structure (XYZ+normals) from visual appearance (RGB) into separate pathways, both initialized from Hunyuan3D-2.1 VAE encoders, to avoid the representational conflict observed when a single encoder handles both modalities. A Qwen-2.5-VL-3B decoder is trained autoregressively to generate the structured program, with the vocabulary extended by special tokens for box coordinates and edit verbs. Training follows a two-stage curriculum: Stage 1 pretrains the structure encoder with a lightweight OPT-350M decoder to predict part BBoxes from 3.6M objects (10 epochs), then Stage 2 performs full instruction tuning on 85,771 objects across 11 task templates (grounding, captioning, QA, editing) while freezing the structure encoder and Qwen token embeddings. A post-hoc DBSCAN clustering algorithm over CLIP-encoded part descriptions enables user-controlled semantic granularity by merging fine-grained boxes into coarser components without retraining.

## Results

On UniPart-Bench, Part-X-MLLM achieves 42.55% BBox IoU, 74.11% Voxel Recall, and 48.74% Voxel IoU for bounding box generation, outperforming OmniPart (39.78% BBox IoU) and PartField (37.33% BBox IoU). For part-level Q&A, the model scores 78.98 SBERT and 84.25 SimCSE, surpassing the strongest baseline (PointLLM-7B at 61.30 SBERT) by +17.7 SBERT and +25.8 SimCSE. On overall object captioning, Part-X-MLLM reaches 53.82 SBERT and 36.04 BLEU-1, improving over MiniGPT-3D by +4.3 SBERT and +18.3 BLEU-1. In part-level mesh generation (Table 9), Part-X-MLLM+OmniPart achieves 0.57 F-0.05, beating OmniPart's native planner (0.46) with the same synthesis backend.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Part-X-MLLM outperforms all six understanding baselines (GPT4Point, PointLLM-7B/13B, ShapeLLM-13B, ShapeLLM-Omni-7B, MiniGPT-3D) on both part Q&A and object captioning, with the largest gap over PointLLM-7B (+17.7 SBERT on Q&A) and MiniGPT-3D (+18.3 BLEU-1 on captioning). On BBox generation (Table 1), it beats OmniPart by +2.77 BBox IoU and PartField by +5.22 BBox IoU. In the controlled planner comparison (Table 9), Part-X-MLLM+OmniPart improves part-level F-0.05 from 0.46 to 0.57 over OmniPart's native planner, and substantially outperforms pipeline baselines like TRELLIS+SAM3D (F-0.05: 0.20) and Part123 (F-0.05: 0.14). The only narrow loss is on the PointLLM Benchmark (Table 8), where Part-X-MLLM scores 16.00 BLEU-1 versus 17.09 for PointLLM-13B* (prompted for shorter captions), though it still leads on SBERT (53.43 vs 50.15) and SimCSE (51.21 vs 50.83). TRELLIS, SAM3D, and HoloPart appear only as components in composite pipeline baselines, all of which are outperformed by Part-X-MLLM+OmniPart on part-level metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines: 3D MLLMs for understanding (PointLLM, ShapeLLM, GPT4Point), part-aware 3D generation (OmniPart, Part123, PartField), and 3D editing (VoxHammer, NANO3D). It departs from all three by reframing their outputs as a single autoregressive token sequence in a structured 'planning grammar' with persistent BBox tokens, rather than as separate model outputs. Architecturally, it builds on Hunyuan3D-2.1 VAE encoders for 3D feature extraction and Qwen-2.5-VL as the LLM backbone, while delegating actual geometry synthesis to OmniPart and editing to VoxHammer as frozen execution backends. The dual-encoder design (separating XYZ+normals from RGB) is a deliberate departure from single-encoder 3D MLLMs, motivated by representational conflict between structure and appearance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'structural opaqueness' problem—treating 3D objects as indivisible blocks—is a genuine bottleneck for downstream animation, robotics, and editing pipelines, and this paper provides a concrete, auditable solution via persistent part identifiers in a language-native interface. The model-agnostic execution backend design (any geometry engine can be driven by the token program) is a practical architectural pattern that could be reused beyond this specific system. Key limitations include inference latency from long token sequences, shallow confidence-based segmentation, and potential degradation of the base LLM's general language capabilities after fine-tuning. The authors identify scaling from object parts to room-level furniture layouts as the natural next step, and the UniPart-Bench (400 objects, 11 task families) provides a useful evaluation substrate the community can adopt.
