## Problem

Existing LLM routing methods train a single monolithic model across all computation strategies, requiring costly full retraining whenever new models or decoding methods become available, and they typically rely on a single input representation that limits routing quality. This paper addresses both gaps by proposing a framework that supports continual incorporation of new strategies with minimal overhead while leveraging richer multi-representation inputs for better routing decisions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260757/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a task (question plus optional supporting context such as documents in open-domain QA) and a set of computation strategies defined as (model, decoding method) pairs, and produces a per-task routing assignment. Cost is measured in FLOPs rather than token counts to enable standardized comparison across heterogeneous model sizes. Routing can be unconstrained (optimizing a weighted accuracy-cost trade-off via a scalar w) or constrained (maximizing accuracy under a per-task FLOPs budget B, solved globally across a batch via dynamic programming).

## Method

CONCUR trains a separate pair of predictors (one binary classifier for accuracy, one regressor for cost) per computation strategy, enabling new strategies to be added by training only their predictors without touching existing ones. Each predictor consumes two types of representations: a general-purpose representation from a frozen ALL-MPNET-BASE-V2 text encoder applied to the task, model description, and decoding method description, and a task-specific representation from a learnable linear projection of the task embedding plus learned lookup embeddings for model and decoding method IDs. These are concatenated and passed through a two-layer MLP. For routing, unconstrained decisions maximize a weighted sum of predicted accuracy and negative cost, while constrained routing is formulated as a batch-level optimization solved by dynamic programming with complexity O(n²·B·|S|), yielding globally optimal budget allocation rather than per-task greedy assignment.

## Results

On in-distribution datasets (2WikiMultiHop, MMLU, GSM8k), CONCUR achieves 75.2% average accuracy with 36.50×10¹¹ FLOPs, outperforming the best single strategy (74.3% acc, 41.63 FLOPs) and all routing baselines. On out-of-distribution datasets (HotpotQA, GPQA, SVAMP), CONCUR reaches 62.6% average accuracy with 43.23 FLOPs, again surpassing the best single strategy (62.3% acc, 46.13 FLOPs). In the continual routing setting (Setting 2, adding small models), CONCUR maintains 75.2% accuracy at 36.50 FLOPs while requiring only 1.00× relative training time, compared to 4.96× for RTR fine-tuned on 100% of new data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CONCUR outperforms all three routing baselines on average accuracy in both in- and out-of-distribution settings. Against RouteLLM (Ong et al., 2024), CONCUR achieves 75.2% vs. 53.5% average accuracy on in-distribution data—a 21.7-point gap—though RouteLLM uses far fewer FLOPs (10.20 vs. 36.50), reflecting its aggressive routing to small models at the cost of accuracy. Against EmbedLLM (Zhuang et al., 2024), CONCUR gains +1.8 points in average accuracy (75.2 vs. 73.4) while using slightly more FLOPs (36.50 vs. 34.21), indicating a modest accuracy-over-efficiency trade-off. Against RTR (Pan et al., 2025), CONCUR wins on both metrics: +0.9 points accuracy (75.2 vs. 74.3) and 4.02 fewer FLOPs (36.50 vs. 40.52). In the continual setting (Setting 2), CONCUR matches or exceeds the best fine-tuned baseline (RTR_FT(75%) at 74.7% acc) while requiring only 1.00× training time versus 3.89× for RTR_FT(75%), demonstrating the efficiency advantage of its modular predictor design.

## Relation to Prior Work

CONCUR departs from the dominant monolithic routing paradigm (RouteLLM, EmbedLLM, RTR), where a single model is trained jointly over all strategies, by adopting a modular per-strategy predictor design inspired by Wang et al. (2025). However, unlike Wang et al., whose router architectures are tailored to individual strategies and thus hard to extend, CONCUR uses a shared MLP architecture across all strategies, making new-strategy incorporation trivial. The multi-representation input (frozen text embeddings plus learnable task-specific projections and strategy embeddings) extends the single-representation parameterizations used by prior routers, drawing on the frozen-encoder approach of Pan et al. (2025) and Zhuang et al. (2024) while adding strategy-side learnable embeddings that prior work omits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As new LLMs and decoding methods are released continuously, the ability to incorporate them into a routing system without costly retraining is a practical necessity that prior monolithic designs cannot meet; CONCUR's modular architecture addresses this directly, reducing incremental training cost from ~7.7× to 1× relative overhead. A notable open question is scalability: the DP-based constrained solver has O(n²·B·|S|) complexity, and the evaluation covers only 5 models × 2 decoding methods, leaving unclear how the framework behaves with dozens of strategies or very large task batches. The reliance on FLOPs as a cost proxy also limits direct transfer to latency- or energy-constrained deployments.
