## Problem

Shadow generation and image relighting are typically treated as disjoint tasks, and existing generative approaches (diffusion, bridge matching) lack physical constraints, producing floating shadows, inconsistent illumination, and implausible shadow geometry. No prior method jointly models the intrinsic coupling between illumination and shadowing while grounding both in scene geometry from a single view.

## Input / Output

The method takes a shadow-free 512×512 RGB image, explicit point-light parameters (color, radius, distance, intensity, azimuth, elevation), and a monocular depth map (from an off-the-shelf estimator, rescaled for consistency with light coordinates) as input, and produces a single shadowed-and-relit image at the same resolution. For the harmonization extension, explicit light parameters are replaced by a composited image from which a lightweight network infers lighting, and LGI maps are computed with parallel rays for sunlight. The LGI conditioning is a 3-channel spatial map (min, max, and argmin elevation differences) concatenated with image features in latent space.

## Method

The core contribution is the Light-Geometry Interaction (LGI) map, a 2.5D representation that lifts each pixel to 3D via predicted depth, casts a ray toward the light source, uniformly samples N=16 points along the ray, reprojects them to retrieve depth, and computes per-point elevation differences relative to the light ray, yielding three channels (min, max, and closest-to-zero elevation difference) that encode potential occlusion. These LGI maps, together with global light parameters, condition a Latent Bridge Matching network (a Brownian-bridge SDE in SDXL latent space) that transforms shadow-free latents into shadowed-and-relit latents. Training is supervised end-to-end from SDXL v1.0 with a weighted L1 loss that emphasizes regions of significant brightness change (threshold τ=0.01 with dilation), replacing the baseline LPIPS loss. The key distinction from prior depth-conditioned methods is that LGI explicitly couples depth with light direction, providing a differentiable, physics-inspired occlusion prior that constrains the generative model without requiring full 3D reconstruction or ray tracing.

## Results

On the DESOBAv2 harmonization benchmark, the method achieves a local RMSE of 44.753 (BOS test) and 52.543 (BOS-free test), outperforming the strongest baseline SGDGP (46.713 / 55.616) in shadow-region accuracy while matching its global RMSE (5.900 vs. 5.896). On the ShadRel joint shadow-relighting task, the method reduces overall BER from 0.1003 (LBM+depth) to 0.0588 and improves shadow IoU from 0.7284 to 0.8096. On the CSG clean-background benchmark, it achieves IoU of 0.821/0.798/0.785 across three tracks, marginally surpassing CSG (0.818/0.780/0.776).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LBM (the direct baseline for joint shadow+relighting on ShadRel), the method reduces overall BER from 0.1003 to 0.0588 and shadow-region BER from 0.1901 to 0.1103, while improving shadow IoU from 0.7284 to 0.8096, confirming that LGI conditioning provides substantial gains over raw depth input. On the DESOBAv2 harmonization benchmark, the method ties SGDGP47 on global RMSE (5.900 vs. 5.896) but clearly wins on local RMSE (44.753 vs. 46.713) and local SSIM (0.415 vs. 0.374), indicating superior shadow-region fidelity; it also outperforms SGDiffusion22 by ~8.9 in local RMSE. On the CSG clean-background benchmark, the method achieves slightly higher IoU across all three tracks (0.821/0.798/0.785 vs. 0.818/0.780/0.776) with comparable RMSE. Against SwitchLight (qualitative only), the method produces both relighting and geometrically consistent shadows, whereas SwitchLight handles relighting without shadow generation. Overall, the method wins or ties on every quantitative comparison, with the largest margins over LBM on the joint task and over SGDGP in shadow-region metrics on DESOBAv2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Latent Bridge Matching (LBM) as its generative backbone, extending it with a novel Light-Geometry Interaction (LGI) conditioning signal derived from monocular depth. It departs from prior shadow generation methods (CSG, diffusion-based, GAN-based) that rely on 2D templates, bounding boxes, or ambient occlusion, and from relighting methods (SwitchLight, inverse rendering) that ignore cast shadows, by unifying both tasks under a single physics-inspired 2.5D representation. The key conceptual departure is from ray tracing and full 3D reconstruction: LGI maps approximate light-occlusion relationships from a depth map alone, providing a differentiable, compact prior that constrains the generative model without requiring explicit geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ShadRel dataset (817K objects with diverse materials, soft shadows, and inter-reflections) fills a clear gap as the first large-scale benchmark for joint shadow and relighting, enabling rigorous evaluation of coupled light transport. The LGI formulation offers a practical middle ground between geometry-free generative models and computationally expensive physically-based rendering, with negligible compute overhead (0.0011% FLOPs increase). Open questions include whether the approach scales to dynamic scenes, whether the reliance on monocular depth estimation introduces systematic errors in complex occlusion, and whether the joint formulation can be extended to global illumination effects beyond point lights. The paper also acknowledges that fine-grained detail reproduction (e.g., logos) remains a limitation inherited from the diffusion backbone.
