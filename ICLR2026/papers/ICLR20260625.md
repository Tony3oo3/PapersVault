## Problem

RAG systems frequently encounter underspecified user queries where critical conditions (e.g., income level, disability severity) are omitted, leading to incorrect or misleading answers. Existing clarification methods either rely on prompting (constrained by LLM reasoning capacity) or fine-tuning on unconstrained dialogue data (expensive and prone to irrelevant interactions), and both neglect the rule-based conditional reasoning structures embedded in retrieved documents that are the root cause of ambiguity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user query and a retrieved document as input, and the LLM's hidden user background context S is not directly observable. The Reasoner LLM outputs a structured conditional reasoning DAG in JSON format (nodes typed as Condition or Conclusion, edges labeled with condition values), which the Clarifier LLM then traverses to generate sequential clarification questions. The final output is an unambiguous answer after the DAG is pruned to a single terminal node via user responses.

## Method

GPS is a two-stage framework: a Reasoner LLM extracts a conditional reasoning DAG from the query-document pair (proven logically complete via DNF representation), and a Clarifier LLM performs dynamic topological traversal of the DAG, selecting the most informative condition at each step and pruning inconsistent branches based on user responses, achieving O(r) average-case clarification complexity where r ≪ k. Training uses Conditional Path Guided Data Synthesis (generating underspecified QA pairs along document conditional paths with necessity-based filtering) followed by GRPO-based reinforcement learning with a hybrid reward combining answer correctness, clarification-turn efficiency, and a structural quality term (leaf entropy / graph split entropy) that penalizes redundant branching. The structural quality reward ensures the DAG converts intermediate uncertainty into discriminative power over final conclusions rather than introducing splits that later recombine.

## Results

GPS achieves the best or near-best Success Rate (SR) and Weighted Clarification Turns (WCT) across all three benchmarks (Synthetic, ConditionalQA, ShARC) with both Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and LLaMA3-8B-Instruct backbones. With LLaMA3-8B-Instruct, GPS attains an average relative SR gain of 10.4% over the second-best method and the lowest WCT in nearly all settings (e.g., 2.89 on ConditionalQA vs. 3.23 for the next best). With Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, GPS reaches 60.2% SR and 4.59 WCT on Synthetic, 73.4% SR and 2.91 WCT on ConditionalQA, and 79.3% SR and 2.41 WCT on ShARC, outperforming all baselines on WCT and matching or exceeding the best SR. The paper reports an average improvement of 7.5% in SR and 4.2% in clarification efficiency over the best baseline method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPS outperforms all four baseline families across both backbone models. Against the prompting-based baselines (ProCoT, UoT, BED-LLM), GPS achieves substantially higher SR on Synthetic (60.2% vs. 42.5%/32.8%/40.9% with Qwen2.5) and ConditionalQA (73.4% vs. 71.6%/60.3%/52.8%), while also attaining the lowest WCT in nearly all settings. Against the fine-tuning-based Clarify-DPO, GPS wins on SR for ConditionalQA (73.4% vs. 72.0%) and ShARC (79.3% vs. 78.5%) with Qwen2.5, and on ConditionalQA with LLaMA3 (74.6% vs. 66.3%), though Clarify-DPO edges out GPS on ShARC SR with LLaMA3 (82.7% vs. 75.8%). GPS's WCT advantage is consistent: it achieves the lowest WCT on all three datasets with Qwen2.5 (4.59/2.91/2.41) and on Synthetic and ConditionalQA with LLaMA3 (5.02/2.89), indicating that the DAG-guided traversal yields both higher accuracy and fewer effective clarification turns. Notably, GPS generalizes to ShARC (an out-of-domain dataset with shorter documents and binary answers) without any ShARC-specific training, whereas Clarify-DPO was trained directly on ShARC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeek-R1 to train a Reasoner LLM, but departs fundamentally from prior proactive clarification work (ProCoT, UoT, BED-LLM, Clarify-DPO) by reframing the problem: rather than treating clarification as an open-ended dialogue or uncertainty-reduction task, it explicitly extracts conditional reasoning structures from retrieved documents as a DAG and traverses them. The graph-based reasoning lineage (Query2Box, knowledge-graph GNNs) informs the structural representation, but those methods target well-specified multi-hop queries, whereas GPS is designed specifically for underspecified queries where the missing information is the core challenge. The conditional-path-guided data synthesis addresses a gap in existing benchmarks (ConditionalQA has only 24.5% underspecified samples) that prior fine-tuning approaches have not tackled.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DAG-based formulation provides a theoretically grounded (DNF completeness, O(r) traversal) and practically interpretable structure for clarification that prior prompting or DPO-based methods lack, potentially making it easier to debug and extend in production RAG systems. However, the approach is evaluated only on 7B–8B models and rule-heavy documents (policy eligibility, yes/no ShARC), leaving open whether the DAG construction generalizes to open-domain, multi-document, or highly ambiguous real-world queries where conditions are not cleanly enumerable. The reliance on synthetic data for RL training also raises questions about distribution shift when deployed on genuinely novel document types.
