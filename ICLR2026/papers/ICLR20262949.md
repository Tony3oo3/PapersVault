## Problem

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) suffer from cognitive inefficiencies: they overthink simple problems and underthink complex ones. Existing compression methods (SFT on shortened CoTs or RL with uniform length penalties) improve token efficiency but sacrifice accuracy, because uniformly favoring shorter responses constrains the model's ability to explore solution space on hard problems. The paper asks how to simultaneously improve both accuracy and efficiency by adaptively adjusting reasoning length based on real-time problem difficulty.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text math problem as input and produces a chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a final numerical answer. During RL training, G=32 responses are sampled per question (temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95) with a maximum generation length of 32,768 tokens (capped at 10k during training for efficiency). The key operational detail is that the preferred response length is not fixed: the reward signal dynamically shifts between compressing and extending the CoT depending on whether the current model finds the question easy or hard, as assessed in real-time from the batch's pass ratios.

## Method

DeepCompress augments the GRPO/DAPO RL training loop with a dual length reward and a model-aware difficulty classifier. For each question, the group pass ratio P_g(x_i) (fraction of correct samples among G=32 rollouts) is compared against the batch pass ratio P_b (EMA-smoothed with λ=0.99); the difference β_i = P_g − P_b determines the mode: positive β (Simple) applies a sigmoid reward that favors shorter responses, while negative β (Hard) inverts the sigmoid to reward longer, more exploratory responses. The length reward R_l = α·sigmoid(−β_i·z_i) is added to the binary outcome reward R_o only for correct responses, preventing reward hacking. This adaptive mechanism maintains higher policy entropy during training, enabling broader exploration on hard problems while compressing CoT on mastered ones, yielding simultaneous gains in pass@1 accuracy and token efficiency across mathematical and scientific benchmarks.

## Results

On seven mathematical reasoning benchmarks, DeepCompress-Zero-7B achieves an average accuracy of 48.7%, surpassing the previous SOTA DeepMath-Zero-7B (46.0%) by +2.7 points. The gains are most pronounced on the hardest problems: +4.1 on AIME 2024 (23.5 vs. 19.4) and +6.5 on AIME 2025 (19.6 vs. 13.1). Simultaneously, DeepCompress compresses average response length by 57.9% (3B) and 16.6% (7B) relative to DeepMath-Zero, demonstrating that the dual-reward strategy improves accuracy while reducing token cost. On non-mathematical benchmarks (GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-STEM, Big-Bench Hard), DeepCompress-Zero-7B also leads with 43.9%, 75.5%, and 85.7% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is DeepMath-Zero (He et al., 2025), the state-of-the-art Zero RL model trained on the same DeepMath-103K dataset. DeepCompress outperforms DeepMath-Zero on every benchmark for both 3B and 7B scales, with the largest margins on the most difficult problems: +5.2 on AIME 24 and +0.2 on AIME 25 for 3B, and +4.1 on AIME 24 and +6.5 on AIME 25 for 7B. The secondary baseline, Open-Reasoner-Zero-7B (Hu et al., 2025), is surpassed by DeepCompress-Zero-7B on all seven math benchmarks (e.g., 85.6 vs. 81.8 on MATH-500, 23.5 vs. 15.6 on AIME 24). Notably, DeepCompress achieves these accuracy gains while generating substantially shorter responses—57.9% fewer tokens on average for the 3B model and 16.6% for the 7B model—whereas the length-penalty ablation (fixed β=1) produces even shorter outputs but at the cost of lower pass@1, confirming that the adaptive dual-reward design is essential for the accuracy–efficiency trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeepCompress builds directly on the Zero RL training recipe of DeepMath-Zero (He et al., 2025) and the DAPO algorithm, extending them with a dual length reward that replaces the fixed-length-penalty approach used by prior efficiency-focused RL methods (e.g., Kimi K1.5, O1-Pruner, L1). It departs from the prevailing assumption that shorter CoT is universally preferable by demonstrating that pass@k increases with response length for hard problems, and introduces a model-aware difficulty classifier (group pass ratio vs. batch pass ratio) that dynamically switches between compression and exploration modes without requiring external difficulty labels. The method also draws on the GRPO group-relative optimization framework and adds EMA smoothing of the batch pass ratio for training stability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DeepCompress addresses a practical tension in LRM deployment: most prior efficiency methods (SFT on shortened CoT, fixed length penalties in RL) trade accuracy for brevity, whereas this paper shows both can improve simultaneously. The model-aware difficulty mechanism is lightweight and requires no external annotations, making it easy to integrate into existing RL pipelines. Key limitations include the 10k token generation cap (which may constrain exploration of very long solutions) and the dependence on sufficient length variation within the RL group for the standardized z-score to be meaningful. Open questions include whether the dual-reward strategy transfers to non-mathematical reasoning domains without re-tuning, and whether the EMA-smoothed batch pass ratio remains a reliable difficulty proxy as models scale or training data distributions shift.
