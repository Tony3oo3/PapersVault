## Problem

Muon, a recently popular LMO-based optimizer that outperforms Adam in centralized training, cannot be naively plugged into FedAvg as a local optimizer because the LMO is a biased operator: the average of per-client LMO outputs does not equal the LMO of the averaged input. This paper formally proves that LOCALMUON (FedAvg + Muon) fails to converge to a stationary point under data heterogeneity, and further asks how the inexact LMO solutions (Newton-Schulz iterations) used in practice affect the convergence rate of a corrected federated variant.

## Input / Output

Each of n clients holds a private training set (distributed via Dirichlet distributions with tunable heterogeneity parameter β) and maintains local model parameters as d₁×d₂ matrices, one per layer. The server aggregates parameters and control variates across S sampled clients per round over K local steps. The LMO is approximated by T Newton-Schulz iterations (matrix multiplications only), so the per-step compute is O(T·d₁·d₂·min(d₁,d₂)) rather than a full SVD. Experiments use FashionMNIST with LeNet and CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 (GroupNorm), 16 clients, S=8, K=5.

## Method

FEDMUON augments Muon's LMO update with SCAFFOLD-style control variates C_i (per-client) and C (global) that estimate local and global gradient directions. At each local step, the LMO is applied not to the raw momentum M_i but to the bias-corrected quantity M_i − C_i + C, which aligns the per-client update direction with the true global gradient and eliminates the LMO bias that causes LOCALMUON to stagnate. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3) shows that FEDMUON converges to a stationary point for any number T ≥ 0 of Newton-Schulz iterations, with the convergence measured in the Schatten p-norm where p = 2 at T = 0 and p → 1 (trace norm) as T → ∞; increasing T tightens the norm and can improve the rate by up to a factor of √min{d₁,d₂}. When the LMO is removed and α = 1, FEDMUON reduces to vanilla SCAFFOLD, and when the spectral norm is used with low-rank Hessians, FEDMUON provably converges faster than SCAFFOLD in the trace norm.

## Results

The paper evaluates FEDMUON on FashionMNIST (LeNet) and CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18) under both homogeneous (Dirichlet β=10.0) and heterogeneous (β=0.1) data distributions, with n=16 clients, S=8 sampled per round, and K=5 local steps. Results are presented as training curves (Figure 1) rather than a numerical table; the paper reports that FEDMUON achieves the highest test accuracy across all four settings (two datasets × two heterogeneity levels). In the inexact LMO ablation (Figure 2), FEDMUON already converges with T=0 Newton-Schulz iterations, but accuracy improves substantially from T=0 to T=1, with the best accuracy at T=4 (homogeneous) and T=2 (heterogeneous).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FEDMUON outperforms all four baselines—FedAvg (Momentum SGD), FedAvg (Adam), SCAFFOLD (Momentum SGD), and SCAFFOLD (Adam)—on both FashionMNIST and CIFAR-10 in both homogeneous and heterogeneous settings, as shown in the training curves of Figure 1. The comparison with FedAvg (Adam) and SCAFFOLD (Adam) is particularly notable because it demonstrates that the LMO-based Muon optimizer is beneficial even in the federated setting where adaptive methods like Adam are already strong. LOCALMUON (the naive plug-in of Muon into FedAvg without bias correction) performs competitively in the homogeneous setting (β=10.0) but degrades significantly in the heterogeneous setting (β=0.1), consistent with Theorem 1 showing it cannot converge to a stationary point under data heterogeneity. FEDMUON's SCAFFOLD-style control variates (C_i and C) are what enable it to overcome this limitation, matching or exceeding SCAFFOLD (Adam) while leveraging the spectral-norm LMO for faster convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: LMO-based optimizers (Muon, Shampoo) for centralized neural network training, and bias-corrected federated averaging (SCAFFOLD). It departs from MuLoCo (Thérien et al., 2025), which naively applies Muon as a local optimizer in a federated loop, by showing that this fails under data heterogeneity due to the LMO's inherent bias. The key methodological contribution is transplanting SCAFFOLD's control-variates mechanism into the LMO update rule, and the analytical contribution is the first convergence proof for Muon-type optimizers with inexact Newton-Schulz LMO solutions in a federated setting, going beyond prior analyses (Pethick et al., 2025; Riabinin et al., 2025) that assumed exact LMO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe (FEDMUON) for using Muon in federated learning with provable convergence, which is timely given Muon's growing adoption in LLM training. The theoretical finding that convergence holds for any number of Newton-Schulz iterations (including T=0, i.e., plain normalized gradient) is a useful robustness guarantee that simplifies implementation. However, experimental validation is limited to FashionMNIST and CIFAR-10 with LeNet and ResNet-18, leaving open whether the advantages persist at the scale of modern LLM pretraining or with more aggressive heterogeneity. A key open question is how FEDMUON interacts with communication compression or asynchronous federated settings.
