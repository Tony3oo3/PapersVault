## Problem

Existing text-to-online handwriting generation (TOHG) methods operate at the character or word level, requiring external layout prediction modules to stitch individual glyphs into text lines. This decoupled approach fails to capture long-range inter-character dependencies and natural writing dynamics, producing stitching artifacts and inconsistent layouts. No prior method directly models entire text lines end-to-end with a generative framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262781/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a continuous reference handwriting trajectory (a segment of a real text line providing both style and layout context) and a target text string (concatenation of the reference text and the text to generate, up to 52 characters) as input. It outputs a complete full-line online handwriting trajectory as a sequence of 5-dimensional points (x, y coordinates plus a 3-state pen indicator: pen-down, pen-up, end-of-character), where the total number of points varies per line. Unlike character-level methods that produce isolated glyphs requiring post-hoc bounding-box placement, DiffInk generates the entire line as a single coherent sequence in one pass.

## Method

DiffInk comprises two components: InkVAE, a 1D convolutional sequential VAE that compresses full-line trajectories into a compact latent space, and InkDiT, a 16-layer DiT-based diffusion model that denoises within that space. The key innovation in InkVAE is a dual regularization strategy: a Transformer-based OCR head trained with CTC loss enforces glyph-level structural consistency, while an LSTM-with-attention style classifier enforces writer-level discriminability, jointly producing a latent space where character content and writing style are effectively disentangled. InkDiT conditions on two signals concatenated along the channel dimension: content features from character embeddings processed by a ConvNeXt-V2 encoder (using large-kernel depthwise convolutions for long-range alignment), and style features obtained by encoding the reference trajectory through the InkVAE encoder. Training uses a masked MSE loss where only the generation region (excluding the reference segment) is supervised, and inference employs 5-step DDIM sampling from Gaussian noise, followed by decoding through the InkVAE decoder to recover the final pen trajectory.

## Results

On CASIA-OLHWDB 2.0-2.2, DiffInk achieves 94.38% Accurate Rate (AR) and 94.58% Correct Rate (CR) for content fidelity, 77.38% writer classification accuracy for style consistency, and a normalized DTW of 1.049 for trajectory similarity. It generates 58.47 characters per second, making it 17× faster than SDT and over 800× faster than OLHWG. All metrics are evaluated at the full text-line level, with the model using 5-step DDIM sampling for inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffInk outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest prior method OLHWG (ICLR'25), which uses a diffusion-based character generator plus a separate layout module, DiffInk improves AR by 2.9 points (94.38 vs. 91.48), CR by 2.9 points, style accuracy by 32.6 points (77.38 vs. 44.74), and reduces DTW from 1.326 to 1.049, while being over 800× faster (58.47 vs. 0.07 char/s). Against SDT (CVPR'23), the best character-level contrastive-learning method, DiffInk gains 11.9 points in AR, 26.9 points in style, and 17× in speed. The older autoregressive baselines—Drawing (TPAMI'17), DeepImitator (PR'20), and WLU (CGF'21)—trail by 14–18 points in AR and 42–52 points in style, with WLU being the only other method achieving reasonable speed (25.19 char/s) but still 2.3× slower than DiffInk. The paper attributes baseline weaknesses to stitching artifacts at character boundaries and the inability of decoupled character-layout pipelines to capture inter-character structural dependencies.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffInk extends the latent diffusion paradigm (Rombach et al., 2022; Peebles & Xie, 2023) to the domain of online handwriting, replacing the U-Net backbone with a DiT architecture for trajectory denoising. It departs from the dominant character-level generation paradigm (Drawing, DeepImitator, WLU, SDT) and the recent two-stage decoupled approach of OLHWG (Ren et al., 2025), which synthesizes isolated glyphs and stitches them via a separate layout module. The key conceptual departure is treating the full text line as a single sequence in a VAE latent space, where two lightweight regularization losses (CTC-based OCR and writer classification) impose semantic structure that a vanilla VAE fails to learn despite achieving near-perfect reconstruction. The decoder design follows FontRNN's GMM-based trajectory parameterization, and the content encoder borrows ConvNeXt-V2's large-kernel depthwise convolutions for long-range text embedding alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end full-line online handwriting generator, eliminating the stitching artifacts and layout errors inherent in character-then-compose pipelines, and achieving an 800× speedup over the prior SOTA (OLHWG) by avoiding per-character diffusion. The finding that reconstruction fidelity in a VAE does not predict downstream diffusion quality—requiring explicit semantic regularization to structure the latent space—offers a transferable insight for other latent diffusion applications. However, the method is currently limited to Chinese (requiring retraining for other scripts), capped at 52 characters per line, and the baselines are character-level methods retrofitted with a layout module rather than true line-level competitors, so the magnitude of improvement may be somewhat overstated. Open questions include whether the structured-latent-space regularization generalizes to paragraph-level generation and multilingual settings without per-language retraining.
