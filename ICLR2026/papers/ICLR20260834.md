## Problem

Most time series models operate exclusively on numerical data, ignoring paired textual information that co-occurs at each timestamp. The one existing multimodal approach (MM-TSFLib) treats texts as a single aggregated signal, disregarding the temporal positional structure that paired texts inherently possess. This paper asks: what unique temporal attributes characterize time-series-paired texts, and how can they be systematically integrated to improve forecasting and imputation without modifying model architectures?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a numerical time series X ∈ R^{T×N} and a sequence of T paired text strings (one per timestamp), which are encoded by a frozen pre-trained LLM (GPT-2, BERT, or LLaMA2) into high-dimensional embeddings. These embeddings are projected via a lightweight three-layer MLP into a lower-dimensional space (d_mapped), then concatenated to the original time series as auxiliary variables, forming an augmented sequence U ∈ R^{T×(N+d_mapped)}. The output is the forecasted or imputed values of the original N numerical variables only; the text-derived auxiliary variables are discarded after prediction.

## Method

The core insight is Chronological Textual Resonance (CTR): time-series-paired texts exhibit periodic patterns in their embedding space that closely mirror the dominant frequencies of the corresponding numerical series, a phenomenon the authors quantify with a new TT-Wasserstein metric (Wasserstein distance between normalized spectral distributions of texts and time series). Building on CTR, TaTS treats text embeddings as auxiliary variables in a multivariate time series: a pre-trained LLM encodes each timestamp's text, a trainable MLP projects the high-dimensional embedding to a compact representation, and the resulting vectors are concatenated to the original numerical series to form an augmented input. This augmented sequence is fed unchanged into any existing time series model (iTransformer, PatchTST, DLinear, etc.), and the model plus MLP are jointly trained end-to-end with standard MSE loss. The framework is plug-and-play—no architectural modifications to the backbone are required—and the authors show that lower TT-Wasserstein (stronger CTR) predicts larger performance gains, providing a practical gauge of when paired texts will be most useful.

## Results

On the Time-MMD benchmark, TaTS achieves an average MSE improvement of over 5% on 6 out of 9 datasets compared to the best baseline, with a remarkable >30% MSE reduction on the Environment dataset (e.g., 0.267 vs. 0.441 MSE with iTransformer). On the Economy dataset, TaTS delivers up to 64% MSE reduction (0.008 vs. 0.025 with DLinear). Across the FNSPID and FNF datasets, TaTS+iTransformer consistently outperforms all baselines, e.g., achieving 0.087 MSE on Delta Airlines stock price vs. 0.098 for ChatTime and 0.093 for GPT4MTS. For imputation, TaTS achieves up to 34.6% MSE reduction (Economy, PatchTST) and 30.7% (Traffic, PatchTST) over the best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TaTS consistently outperforms MM-TSFLib (the library from Time-MMD) across all 9 Time-MMD datasets and all 9 time series models, with MSE reductions ranging from ~1% (Agriculture, iTransformer) to ~64% (Economy, DLinear). Against covariate-based baselines N-BEATS and N-HiTS, TaTS achieves dramatically lower MSE (e.g., 0.109 vs. 3.267 on Agriculture; 0.008 vs. 1.010 on Economy), indicating that simply feeding text embeddings as covariates without the CTR-aware auxiliary variable design is far less effective. TCN, adapted to accept text embeddings, also underperforms TaTS by wide margins across all datasets. ChatTime, a multimodal foundation model evaluated in zero-shot mode, achieves competitive results (e.g., 0.049 MSE on Economy) but is still outperformed by TaTS's supervised approach (0.008 MSE). GPT4MTS, another LLM-based multimodal method, is the closest competitor on some datasets (e.g., 0.014 vs. 0.008 on Economy) but TaTS still wins on the majority of datasets, particularly on FNSPID stock price and FNF datasets where the gap is more pronounced.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the well-established concept of multivariate time series (where multiple numerical variables share periodic structure) to treat timestamp-aligned texts as auxiliary variables, motivated by the Platonic Representation Hypothesis. It departs from the closest prior work, MM-TSFLib (Time-MMD, Liu et al. 2024a), which fuses text and numerical modalities without exploiting the positional/periodic characteristics that paired texts inherently possess. The TaTS framework is deliberately architecture-agnostic, plugging a lightweight MLP projection into any existing time series backbone (iTransformer, PatchTST, DLinear, etc.), in contrast to ChatTime or GPT4MTS which require dedicated multimodal architectures. The TT-Wasserstein metric, quantifying spectral alignment between text and time series, is a novel diagnostic tool with no direct precedent in the time series literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design makes TaTS immediately adoptable by practitioners who already have a time series model and want to incorporate available textual context without retraining from scratch or modifying architectures. The CTR phenomenon and TT-Wasserstein metric provide a practical diagnostic: before investing in multimodal modeling, one can assess whether the paired texts are likely to be useful, and the paper shows this prediction is broadly reliable. Open questions include the relationship between text encoder scale and downstream gains (only mildly positive in their experiments), whether more fine-grained fusion (beyond simple concatenation) helps at larger scales, and how the framework behaves when text quality is highly variable across timestamps. The Climate dataset exception to the TT-Wasserstein–improvement correlation suggests the metric may not capture all relevant alignment dimensions.
