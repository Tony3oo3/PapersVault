## Problem

Instance and panoptic segmentation has long depended on dense pixel-level annotations (masks, boxes, points) that are expensive, inconsistent, and hard to scale. Unsupervised methods built on semantic backbones like DINO merge adjacent same-class objects or fragment single instances, while weakly-supervised approaches still require per-instance point or box labels. The paper asks whether pretrained text-to-image diffusion models already encode recoverable instance boundary structure in their self-attention maps, and if so, how to decode it without any instance-level supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263292/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TRACE takes a single RGB image (no text prompts, points, or boxes) and encodes it into latent space via a VAE; the native resolution is 512×512 for SD1.5 and 1024×1024 for SDXL/SD3.5-L. The primary output is a connected instance edge map (binary, with uncertain pixels masked) produced by a lightweight CNN decoder in one forward pass at t=0. These edges are then consumed by a Background-Guided Propagation step that converts existing semantic or fragmented masks into refined instance or panoptic masks with correct instance separation.

## Method

The core insight is that diffusion self-attention (unlike cross-attention) transiently reveals instance-level structure at a specific denoising timestep. TRACE first locates the Instance Emergence Point (IEP) by finding the timestep t* where KL divergence between consecutive self-attention maps peaks, then applies Attention Boundary Divergence (ABDiv) to score criss-cross KL differences between opposite 4-neighbors, yielding a pseudo edge map with reliability-based ternary thresholding. To eliminate per-image IEP+ABDiv at inference, the diffusion backbone is fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 64) jointly with a lightweight edge decoder G_φ using a combined reconstruction + Dice loss (with uncertain-pixel masking), compressing the transient cues into a single-pass predictor that is ~81× faster. At deployment, the predicted edges serve as separators in connected-component labeling, and a random-walk Background-Guided Propagation step spreads fragmented masks within instance boundaries and merges overlapping regions (IoU > 0.5) to produce complete instance or panoptic masks.

## Results

On COCO 2014, TRACE improves unsupervised instance segmentation by +4.8 AP^mk over ProMerge (3.1 → 7.9) and +2.2 AP^mk over the depth-based CutS3D (10.9 → 13.1). In tag-supervised panoptic segmentation, DHR + TRACE with Swin-L achieves 69.8 PQ on VOC 2012 and 43.1 PQ on COCO 2017, surpassing the strongest point-supervised baseline EPLD (68.5 / 41.0 PQ) without any instance-level annotations. The one-step distillation reduces per-image inference from 3682 ms to 45 ms (~81× faster) while preserving edge quality (ODS 0.889 vs. 0.428 for DiffusionEdge).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRACE consistently outperforms all unsupervised instance segmentation baselines: over MaskCut (Wang et al., 2023) by +4.9 AP^mk on COCO 2014 (3.0→7.9), over ProMerge (Li & Shin, 2024) by +5.1 AP^mk (3.1→8.2), and over the depth-based CutS3D (Sick et al., 2025) by +2.2 AP^mk (10.9→13.1). In panoptic segmentation, DHR + TRACE with Swin-L (69.8 PQ on VOC, 43.1 PQ on COCO) surpasses the strongest point-supervised method EPLD (Li et al., 2024) by +1.3 and +2.1 PQ respectively, using only image-level tags. Against tag-supervised baselines MARS (Jo et al., 2023) and DHR (Jo et al., 2024), TRACE adds +9.0 and +11.9 PQ on VOC 2012. For edge quality, TRACE (ODS 0.889, clDice 0.826) more than doubles the best conventional detector DiffusionEdge (Ye et al., 2024; ODS 0.428) and substantially outperforms Canny (0.129), HED (0.347), and PiDiNet (0.362). The only baseline TRACE does not surpass is Mask2Former with full mask supervision (73.6 PQ on VOC), which serves as the supervised upper bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRACE repurposes pretrained text-to-image diffusion models (SD, SDXL, FLUX, PixArt) as instance boundary detectors by reading their self-attention maps, departing from prior diffusion-for-segmentation work (DiffCut, DiffSeg) that clusters attention at fixed timesteps for semantic masks. For mask refinement, it builds on the random-walk propagation framework of Ahn et al. (2018, 2019) for weakly-supervised instance segmentation, but replaces their learned inter-pixel relations with non-parametric edges extracted via Attention Boundary Divergence. The one-step self-distillation with LoRA compresses the multi-step IEP+ABDiv pipeline into a single forward pass, and the overall framework is model-agnostic, plugging into existing UIS (MaskCut, ProMerge) and tag-supervised WSS (MARS, DHR) backbones without retraining them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that diffusion models encode recoverable instance-level boundary structure at a specific denoising timestep—a property absent in discriminative or multimodal foundation models regardless of scale (0.6B PixArt-α outperforms 72B Qwen2.5-VL). This provides a practical, annotation-free path to instance and panoptic segmentation that surpasses point-supervised baselines using only image-level tags, with 81× faster inference after distillation. Limitations include degradation on tiny instances in satellite imagery (due to VAE spatial compression) and out-of-distribution medical images where natural-image priors misalign. Open directions the authors identify include video panoptic segmentation, medical imaging adaptation, and combining TRACE edges with open-vocabulary text prompts for scalable panoptic perception.
