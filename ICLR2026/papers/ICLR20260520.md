## Problem

Despite decades of empirical evidence that greedy, exploitation-only methods perform well in operations-research settings like inventory control and energy storage, all existing finite-sample regret guarantees for Exogenous MDPs (Exo-MDPs) rely on explicit exploration, optimism, or tabular assumptions. This paper asks whether pure exploitation alone can achieve near-optimal regret in Exo-MDPs under linear function approximation with continuous endogenous states, and answers affirmatively with the first such guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260520/cropped_page9_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous endogenous state x, a discrete exogenous state ξ, known deterministic transition and reward functions (f and r), a feature map φ: X → R^d, and a finite set of anchor post-decision states whose feature vectors span the feature space. It outputs a greedy action at each step, linear value-function weight vectors w_h^k(ξ) for each stage and exogenous state, and an empirical estimate of the exogenous Markov transition kernel. The critical structural constraint is that f and r are fully known—only the exogenous process is unknown—which is what enables counterfactual data reuse across policies without exploration.

## Method

LSVI-PE alternates between a backward value-iteration pass (building empirical exogenous transition models, constructing Bellman regression targets at post-decision states to disentangle action choice from exogenous randomness, and fitting linear value weights via least squares on anchor states) and a forward greedy execution pass that collects new exogenous traces. The core insight is that in Exo-MDPs the exogenous process evolves independently of actions, so every observed trajectory provides unbiased information about all policies, eliminating the need for optimism or forced exploration. The analysis introduces two new tools: counterfactual trajectories that simulate the optimal policy under the same realized exogenous sequence (enabling a simulation-lemma bound without requiring visitations under π*), and a Bellman-closed feature transport condition ensuring that one-step post-decision transitions map anchor features back into the anchor span via a non-expansive linear operator. The resulting regret bound is Õ((√(N/λ₀)+√d)|Ξ|H√K), polynomial in feature dimension, exogenous cardinality, and horizon, and independent of the endogenous state and action space sizes.

## Results

The paper's primary contributions are theoretical regret bounds. In the tabular Exo-MDP setting, the Predict-Then-Optimize (PTO) algorithm achieves cumulative regret of $\widetilde{O}(H^2|\Xi|\sqrt{K})$, independent of the endogenous state and action space sizes. Under linear function approximation, LSVI-PE achieves $\widetilde{O}((\sqrt{N/\lambda_0}+\sqrt{d})|\Xi|H\sqrt{K})$ (Theorem 2), which simplifies to $\widetilde{O}(|\Xi|H\sqrt{dK})$ in well-conditioned designs. Empirically, on synthetic tabular Exo-MDPs (|X|=5, |Ξ|=5, |A|=3, H=5, K=250) and storage control tasks (H=6,8,10), PEL consistently achieves lower cumulative regret than optimism-based counterparts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its pure-exploitation methods against two types of baselines: (1) optimistic model-based variants (PTO-Opt, LSVI-Opt) that add confidence bonuses to the exogenous transition estimates, and (2) a lightweight sub-sampling variant (PTO-Lite). On the synthetic tabular Exo-MDP (Figure 1), PTO achieves the lowest cumulative regret, outperforming both PTO-Opt and PTO-Lite, demonstrating that adding optimism is counterproductive when exogenous traces can be reused. On the storage control benchmark (Figure 2), LSVI-PE consistently outperforms LSVI-Opt across all three horizon lengths (H=6, 8, 10), with the gap widening as the horizon increases. The key takeaway is that in Exo-MDPs, the independence of the exogenous process from actions makes explicit exploration unnecessary and even harmful, as it introduces variance without providing new information about the exogenous dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of the Exo-MDP framework (Powell, 2022; Sinclair et al., 2023b; Wan et al., 2024) and the LSVI family of linear-function-approximation algorithms (Jin et al., 2020), but deliberately removes the optimism/exploration component that all prior RL guarantees for Exo-MDPs require. The post-decision state decomposition and anchor-based regression are inherited from the ADP literature (Nascimento & Powell, 2009, 2013), while the FTL-style policy evaluation for tabular Exo-MDPs builds on the hindsight value function machinery of Sinclair et al. (2023b). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of counterfactual trajectories and Bellman-closed feature transport as analytical tools, which replace the standard optimism-based simulation lemma with a decomposition that exploits the action-independence of the exogenous process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that pure exploitation achieves sublinear regret in Exo-MDPs overturns a near-universal assumption in RL that exploration is necessary, and the companion impossibility result (linear regret without Exo-MDP structure) sharpens the boundary of when this holds. For operations research practitioners in inventory control, energy storage, and resource allocation, this provides theoretical justification for the exploitation-heavy ADP methods already deployed at industrial scale. Key limitations include the requirement that endogenous dynamics f and reward r are known, the discrete exogenous state assumption, and the anchor-set conditions (Assumptions 1–4); open questions include extending to continuous or partially observed exogenous processes, richer function classes, and adaptive anchor selection via coreset/Frank-Wolfe procedures.
