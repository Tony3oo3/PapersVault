## Problem

Most RAG systems perform only single-step retrieval, which is insufficient for complex questions requiring multi-hop or temporal reasoning over long contexts. Existing multi-step retrieval approaches either fine-tune the LLM itself (resource-intensive, incompatible with large or proprietary models) or build knowledge graphs (slow at inference). This paper asks whether multi-step retrieval can be learned efficiently by training only the embedder in latent space, without touching the LLM.

## Input / Output

The method takes a long context pre-segmented into non-overlapping text chunks (up to 10M tokens) and a query (a question or a claim to verify). At each of T retrieval steps, the agent selects one chunk from the remaining pool; the state is the document-ordered list of the query plus all previously selected chunks. The final output is the set of retrieved chunks, which are concatenated and passed to a frozen LLM (e.g., QwQ-32B) to generate the answer. Because retrieval operates purely on chunk embeddings via inner products, the LLM never sees the full context, keeping its effective input length bounded by T × chunk_size regardless of document length.

## Method

Q-RAG formulates multi-step retrieval as a finite-horizon MDP and trains a value-based agent using soft (maximum-entropy) Q-learning with the PQN on-policy TD backbone, avoiding replay buffers. The Q-function is factorized as an inner product between a state embedder E_s (encoding the query plus all previously selected chunks) and an action embedder E_a (encoding each candidate chunk with rotary position embeddings), so chunk selection reduces to a single dot-product scoring over all remaining chunks. A key architectural contribution is a relative positional encoding: instead of absolute document positions, each candidate chunk is encoded relative to the intervals defined by already-retrieved facts, enabling the agent to perform temporal reasoning (e.g., 'what happened before event X') during retrieval. Training uses λ-returns, slowly-updated target networks, and a Boltzmann policy with temperature annealed to zero; the entire pipeline is trained on a single A100 GPU, and the paper provides a universal approximation theorem showing the inner-product-with-diagonal-rotation score class is dense in continuous Q-functions.

## Results

On the RULER benchmark, Q-RAG achieves 100% accuracy on all Needle-in-a-Haystack subtasks (Single-needle, Multi-keys, Multi-values, Multi-query) from 4K up to 1M tokens, while baselines like Mamba2-Hybrid and LongRoPE2-8B degrade significantly at longer contexts. On BabiLong, Q-RAG attains the highest average accuracy across tasks Q1–QA5 at 1M–10M token contexts, with virtually no degradation on the hardest subtask QA3 (requiring multi-step temporal reasoning). On open-domain QA, Q-RAG achieves 0.76 Ans F1 / 0.59 Ans EM on HotPotQA and 0.52 Ans F1 / 0.37 Ans EM on MuSiQue (OOD), the best overall performance across both benchmarks. All training is completed on a single A100-80GB GPU within ~12 hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-RAG matches Beam Retriever on HotPotQA fact retrieval (0.93 vs 0.97 Fact F1) but substantially outperforms it on out-of-distribution MuSiQue (0.52 vs 0.40 Ans F1; 0.37 vs 0.27 Ans EM), indicating better generalization. Against Search-R1 (which fine-tunes the full LLM with GRPO), Q-RAG achieves higher HotPotQA Ans F1 (0.76 vs 0.65) while matching on MuSiQue OOD, and requires only a single A100 GPU versus ~8×A100 for Search-R1. On the RULER long-context benchmark, Q-RAG maintains 100% NIAH accuracy up to 1M tokens, whereas Mamba2-Hybrid drops to 99.7% NIAH Avg at 1M and LongRoPE2-8B is not evaluated beyond 128K. On BabiLong ultra-long contexts (1M–10M tokens), Q-RAG outperforms all recurrent-transformer baselines (Titans, Atlas, ARMT, RMT), with the largest gap on the QA3 subtask where other methods show severe degradation. Against zero-shot LLM-agent baselines (GraphReader, Single-step RAG), Q-RAG leads by 20–30 points in Ans F1 on HotPotQA, demonstrating the benefit of learned multi-step retrieval over untrained agentic pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

Q-RAG departs from the dominant paradigm of fine-tuning LLMs for multi-step retrieval (Search-R1, R1-Searcher, RAG-RL) by instead applying value-based RL directly to a small embedder, keeping the LLM frozen. The algorithmic backbone is PQN (Gallici et al., 2025), an on-policy TD method that eliminates replay buffers, combined with maximum-entropy (soft) Q-learning in the spirit of SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018). The key architectural departure from Beam Retriever is replacing transformer-based trajectory scoring with a simple inner-product Q-function over state and action embeddings, augmented with a novel relative positional encoding that enables temporal reasoning without additional model capacity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: training on a single A100 for ~12 hours achieves SOTA on ultra-long-context benchmarks (up to 10M tokens) where competing RL-based retrievers require 8×A100 clusters, and the frozen-LLM design allows pairing with proprietary models. The ablation showing supervised fine-tuning collapses to ~20% F1 while RL reaches ~97% suggests multi-step retrieval has a fundamentally sequential, credit-assignment structure that supervised trajectory learning cannot capture. Open questions include whether LLM-generated reward signals (rather than support-fact supervision) can close the gap on answer quality, and whether the inner-product Q-function scales to even more compositional or multi-document retrieval settings.
