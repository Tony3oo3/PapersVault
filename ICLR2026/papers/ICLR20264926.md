## Problem

Repeated Sampling (RS), a widely used inference-time scaling strategy, generates multiple candidate solutions for a given prompt but suffers from a critical lack of diversity: LLMs tend to repeatedly apply the same underlying theorem or concept, producing redundant solutions that waste compute. This paper asks whether explicitly separating the exploration of diverse solution concepts from the generation of final answers can improve pass@k performance and enable more effective use of inference-time compute.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264926/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text question prompt x, an LLM p_θ, a maximum number of concepts K, and completions per concept M (with total compute budget IC = K × M). The exploration phase outputs an ordered set of K distinct concepts/theorems C = {c₁, …, c_K}, and the generation phase outputs M full solutions per concept, yielding a total of K×M candidate solutions. For post-training, the method additionally produces synthetic training pairs in two formats: Final-Answer Only (FA), where only the verified solution s is the target, and Concept-Augmented Answer (CAA), where the target concatenates all generated concepts with one selected solution.

## Method

GUIDEDSAMPLING decouples inference into two phases. In the Exploration Phase, concepts are generated iteratively via c_k ~ p_θ(·|x, c_{1:(k-1)}), where conditioning on all previously generated concepts encourages the model to propose novel, non-redundant theorems or strategies. In the Generation Phase, for each concept c_k, M solutions are sampled as s_k^(m) ~ p_θ(s|x, c_k), so each solution is explicitly guided by a specific reasoning strategy. The paper proves a theoretical bound (Theorem 1) showing GUIDEDSAMPLING outperforms RS when the probability of generating at least one relevant concept exceeds 1/k_min, where k_min is the minimum amplification factor by which a relevant concept boosts the probability of producing the correct answer. For post-training, the authors fine-tune LLMs on trajectories synthesized via GUIDEDSAMPLING from 10k OpenMathInstruct-2 instances, demonstrating that both FA and CAA training settings yield pass@5 gains of ~5.6–7.1% over RS-trained models, with CAA further increasing the average number of distinct concepts per instance from 1.67 to 3.03.

## Results

At inference time, GUIDEDSAMPLING achieves average pass@50 improvements of 21.8% on MATH, 11.87% on GPQA-Diamond, 11.28% on HumanEval, and 3.08% on OlympiadBench over Repeated Sampling. For post-training, models fine-tuned on GUIDEDSAMPLING trajectories (CAA setting) achieve 60.06% pass@5 on MATH, 59.21% on HumanEval, and 20.47% on OlympiadBench, outperforming all baselines. The method also increases solution diversity by 17.63% on average, with the number of distinct concepts per instance rising from 1.67 (RS) to 3.03 (CAA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Repeated Sampling (Brown et al., 2024), GUIDEDSAMPLING wins on pass@50 across nearly all model-benchmark combinations, with average gains of 21.8% (MATH), 11.87% (GPQA-Diamond), 11.28% (HumanEval), and 3.08% (OlympiadBench); the sole exception is Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct on HumanEval, where weak concept generation (only 1.13 distinct concepts) causes a performance drop. Against STaR (Zelikman et al., 2022), the CAA fine-tuned model outperforms on pass@5 for MATH (60.06% vs. 46.23%), HumanEval (59.21% vs. 57.35%), and OlympiadBench (20.47% vs. 10.62%), while GPQA-Diamond is nearly tied (40.23% vs. 38.41%). Against Tree-of-Thought (Yao et al., 2023), CAA wins on MATH pass@5 (60.06% vs. 56.63%), HumanEval (59.21% vs. 49.51%), and OlympiadBench (20.47% vs. 18.36%), but loses on GPQA-Diamond (40.23% vs. 44.44%); the FA variant beats ToT on GPQA-Diamond (50.61% vs. 44.44%) but trails on MATH (47.98% vs. 56.63%). Overall, GUIDEDSAMPLING's CAA setting achieves the highest average pass@5 across all four benchmarks, with a 2.76% average margin over ToT and 7.13% over RS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Repeated Sampling (RS) as the baseline inference-time strategy, identifying its core weakness—implicit coupling of exploration and generation—as the source of low solution diversity. It positions GUIDEDSAMPLING as a middle ground between undirected RS and the computationally expensive Tree-of-Thought (ToT), which requires explicit evaluation at every tree node. The method is conceptually closest to the parallel RandIdeaInjection work (Wang et al., 2025), but differs by using an iterative loop of concept generation with sequential conditioning rather than a one-shot idea list injection. For post-training, it extends the synthetic-data-generation line (STaR, ReST^EM) by showing that exploration-aware trajectories yield better fine-tuning data than those from RS or ToT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical bound (Theorem 1) characterizing exactly when guided sampling outperforms RS, grounding the empirical gains in a formal condition on concept relevance probability and amplification factor. A key practical finding is that the method's success is critically model-dependent: Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct actually degrades on HumanEval because it generates only ~1.13 distinct concepts per problem, highlighting that concept-generation quality is a prerequisite. Open questions include adaptive allocation of the exploration/generation compute budget (K vs. M), generalization to domains where well-defined concepts are hard to formulate (e.g., commonsense reasoning, where GS underperforms RS), and whether the approach scales to proprietary frontier models. The post-training transfer from math to GPQA-Diamond suggests the diversity signal may generalize, but this needs broader validation.
