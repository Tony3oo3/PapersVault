## Problem

Autoregressive language models are highly training-efficient but overfit when data is repeated, while masked-diffusion models resist overfitting but converge slowly. As the community approaches the data wall, no prior work had demonstrated how to optimally combine both objectives in a single model without architectural modifications, nor had anyone systematically mapped the optimal balance under varying degrees of data constraint.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264134/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes sequences of subword tokens in two modes: full unmasked sequences with causal attention (AR mode) and partially masked sequences with fully bidirectional attention (MD mode). At evaluation, it takes a context and multiple candidate completions, scoring each via log-likelihood (autoregressive, pseudo-log-likelihood for bidirectional, or prefix-masked), and selects the highest-scoring completion. The resulting model deploys as a standard autoregressive transformer with zero inference overhead.

## Method

The paper proposes a dual-objective training loss α·L_AR + (1−α)·L_MD applied to a single transformer, where the two modes are unified via masked next-token prediction (MNTP): the model always predicts token i+1 from position i's hidden state, differing only in input masking and attention pattern (causal vs. bidirectional). Each GPU is assigned to one objective to keep the computation graph static and compilable. The authors train 50 models at 470M parameters over 32B tokens, sweeping α across 257 values and data repetitions from 1 to 128, then fit Gaussian process regression to interpolate the noisy results and derive the optimal α for each repetition level. Key findings: dual-objective training outperforms both single-objective baselines in all settings; in regular-data regimes (≤16 repetitions) a small MD fraction (α≈63/64) suffices; in data-constrained regimes (>32 repetitions) α should be set so the AR objective sees roughly 16 effective repetitions. Additionally, the bidirectional exposure induced by the MD objective enables free prefix-language-modeling at inference time, improving downstream scores by over one percentage point.

## Results

Across 50 trained 470M-parameter models, the dual-objective approach outperforms both single-objective baselines in every evaluated setting. At 128 data repetitions (the most extreme data-constrained regime), the dual model (α=1/8) achieves a normalized average of 19.1 versus only 9.4 for the autoregressive-only model — a near-doubling of performance. Even in the regular-data regime (1 repetition), the dual model (α=63/64) edges out the autoregressive baseline (26.9 vs. 26.1). In bidirectional (masked-diffusion) evaluation, the dual model also surpasses pure masked-diffusion training by 3.9 points at 32 repetitions (25.3 vs. 21.4) and by 2.0 points at 128 repetitions (23.0 vs. 21.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three training configurations: dual-objective (combined AR + masked-diffusion), autoregressive-only, and masked-diffusion-only. Against the autoregressive-only baseline, the dual model wins on average in all three repetition settings, with the gap widening dramatically under data constraint: +0.8 at 1 repetition, +1.9 at 32 repetitions, and +9.7 at 128 repetitions. Against the masked-diffusion-only baseline (evaluated bidirectionally via pseudo log-likelihood), the dual model also wins consistently: +3.9 at 32 repetitions and +2.0 at 128 repetitions, despite dedicating only a fraction of its training compute to the diffusion objective. Notably, the autoregressive-only model degrades catastrophically at 128 repetitions (average drops to 9.4, with negative scores on ARC-C and OBQA), while the dual model retains non-trivial performance (19.1) by leveraging the diffusion objective as a regularizer. The paper also shows that prefix-masked inference (bidirectional attention on the prompt) further improves the dual model's autoregressive scores by ~1 percentage point on average, an effect not available to purely autoregressive models without additional adapter training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GPT-BERT dual-objective training approach of Charpentier & Samuel (2024) from the BabyLM scale to 470M parameters, replacing BERT-style masking with a full masked-diffusion objective grounded in the discrete diffusion framework of Austin et al. (2021) and Ou et al. (2025). The key technical unification comes from masked next-token prediction (MNTP; Lv et al., 2024), which allows both autoregressive and masked-diffusion modes to share a single standard transformer architecture with only the attention mask differing. The paper positions itself against a long lineage of combined-objective models (T5, BART, GLM, CM3, AntLM) but distinguishes itself by supporting continuous, fine-grained balancing of the two objectives throughout training rather than curriculum-style switching, and by providing the first systematic empirical mapping of the optimal balance as a function of data repetition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the NLP community approaches the 'data wall,' practical guidelines for training under data constraints become increasingly urgent, and this paper provides concrete, actionable recommendations (α ≈ 63/64 for regular data; α targeting ~16 AR repetitions for constrained data) that require no architectural changes and no inference overhead. The finding that dual-objective training is universally beneficial—even in data-rich settings where overfitting is not a concern—broadens the applicability beyond the data-constrained niche. Key limitations include the restriction to 470M parameters and a single English corpus, leaving the scaling argument (that optimal α values are size-invariant) unproven; the induced prefix-LM capability, while promising, has only been validated on zero-shot multiple-choice tasks.
