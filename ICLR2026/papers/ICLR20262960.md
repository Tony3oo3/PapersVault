## Problem

Reinforcement learning offers a more data-efficient and robust training paradigm for LLMs compared to imitation-based pretraining, but its adoption at scale is severely constrained by a data bottleneck: existing RL datasets are orders of magnitude smaller (<10B tokens) and far less diverse than web-scale pretraining corpora (>1T tokens). This scarcity, driven by the high cost of generating verifiable question-answer pairs, prevents RL from matching the scale and breadth of pretraining and limits its potential to enhance general reasoning.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes raw pretraining documents from diverse sources (DCLM, Wikipedia, MegaMath, Stack-v2, OpenMathReasoning, OpenCodeReasoning) and produces verifiable QA pairs where the ground-truth answer is a short, easily checkable string (a number, name, or phrase) extracted from the source text rather than a long reasoning chain. Each document is assigned up to 3 personas to generate questions from different viewpoints, and the resulting questions must be self-contained so the model can answer them without access to the original document during RL. The final Webscale-RL dataset contains ~1.2M QA pairs spanning 9+ domains.

## Method

The Webscale-RL pipeline is a four-stage automated engine: (1) heuristic and LLM-based filtering to remove non-informative or non-self-contained documents while preserving diversity; (2) LLM-based domain classification followed by assignment of multiple personas per document to encourage diverse question perspectives; (3) persona-conditioned QA generation using domain-specific few-shot exemplars, where the generator extracts short verifiable answers grounded in the source text rather than distilling reasoning from a powerful teacher LLM; and (4) LLM-based quality verification checking answer correctness against the source document and preventing answer leakage in the question. For training, the model (Qwen2.5-3B) is warm-started with a small SFT set, then trained with GRPO using a binary reward that checks whether the model's final answer matches the ground truth. The key design insight is that both questions and answers are derived from and verified against pretraining documents, decoupling dataset quality from teacher-model capability and enabling natural scaling to pretraining corpus size.

## Results

Webscale-RL achieves an average score of 52.1 across seven benchmarks, outperforming the strongest data-refinement baseline (GDR, 48.7) by 3.4 points and the base Qwen2.5-3B model (47.6) by 4.5 points. The most dramatic gain is on MATH500, where RL training lifts performance from 47.6 to 58.0, approaching the 7B model's 60.8. On general knowledge tasks, MMLU-pro improves from 37.8 to 43.7 and BigBench from 41.2 to 48.3. The 3B model trained with Webscale-RL narrows the average gap to the Qwen2.5-7B model from 10.6 to 6.1 points, and RL training achieves comparable performance to continual pretraining with up to 100× fewer tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Webscale-RL outperforms all three data-refinement baselines (QuRating, ProX, GDR) on every benchmark except MBPP and EvalPlus, where it ties or is within 0.2 points. The largest margins are on MATH500 (58.0 vs. 44.4–44.6, a ~13.5 point gap) and MMLU-pro (43.7 vs. 39.7–40.0, a ~3.7–4.0 point gap), reflecting the benefit of RL's reward-driven learning over static data curation. GDR is the strongest baseline overall (48.7 avg), but still trails Webscale-RL by 3.4 points on average. On GPQA-diamond, Webscale-RL achieves 23.2 versus GDR's 20.8 and ProX's 19.5, indicating that RL training better incentivizes scientific reasoning than quality-filtering or generative refinement of pretraining text. The coding benchmarks (MBPP, EvalPlus) show minimal differentiation across all methods, which the authors attribute to the lower proportion of coding data in the pretraining corpus used for conversion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing the pretraining corpus itself as the source for RL data, rather than relying on distillation from teacher models (as in OpenR1-Math, OpenThoughts, Nemotron) or competition-based collection (DeepScaler). It departs from prior data synthesis pipelines by extracting verifiable QA pairs directly from documents via persona-driven generation, avoiding the quality ceiling imposed by a backend LLM's reasoning ability. The RL training paradigm builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and the broader line of work scaling RL for LLMs (DeepSeek-R1, OpenAI o-series), but the key conceptual shift is treating the pretraining-to-RL data gap as the central bottleneck to solve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pipeline provides a concrete, reproducible path to scaling RL data from the current <10B token regime toward pretraining scale, which could fundamentally change how LLMs are trained post-pretraining. The 100× token-efficiency claim (RL with ~10M tokens matching continual pretraining with 1B tokens on MMLU-pro) is compelling but rests on a specific token-counting methodology and a single 3B model, leaving open whether the efficiency gap persists at larger scales. Key limitations include underrepresented coding domains, the inference cost of the binary reward model, and the reliance on GPT-4.1 for generation, which limits open-source reproducibility of the pipeline itself.
