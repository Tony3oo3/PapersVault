## Problem

Volumetric surface reconstruction methods (NeRF, 3DGS, GaussianSurfel) enjoy large 3D receptive fields for stable optimization but require a post-hoc meshing step that accumulates errors and produces overly dense meshes. Pure mesh-optimization methods avoid meshing and support topology control via remeshing, but their single-layer, opaque surfaces provide too narrow a receptive field to learn intricate geometry without relying on noisy priors (shading, normals, depth). The paper asks whether a surface representation can be made to behave volumetrically—gaining a controllable 3D receptive field—while retaining the mesh's structural and topological advantages.

## Input / Output

The method consumes calibrated multi-view RGB images (object-centric, ~1600×1200) and their camera poses, optionally supplemented by monocular normal predictions and SfM points. It outputs a watertight 3D mesh with explicit topology control: the minimum edge length is set to ~5 mm, yielding roughly 300k vertices per object, and the mesh is directly usable for downstream applications (simulation, editing) without any post-optimization meshing step. Training completes in ~20 minutes on a single NVIDIA V100 (32 GB).

## Method

The core mechanism is *mesh softening*: the base mesh vertices are offset along their normals into N=5 semi-transparent layers (±10 cm), and each layer's alpha is computed differentiably from its signed distance to the base mesh via a VolSDF-style mapping with a learnable sharpness parameter β. A stop-gradient on the offset vertices ensures the signed distance remains differentiable with respect to the base geometry, so volumetric rendering gradients can deform the base mesh. A custom splatting renderer (tile-based rasterization, per-pixel depth-sorted triangle compositing, barycentric interpolation of per-vertex features, and an MLP with hash-encoded 3D coordinates for color prediction) composites all overlapping layers per pixel using the standard volumetric rendering equation. For topology stability, the pipeline uses a hybrid strategy: Deep Marching Tetrahedra (DMTet) at resolution 128 stabilizes global topology during the first 5,000 iterations, after which the mesh is frozen from the grid and Continuous Remeshing is applied every step to maintain near-isotropic triangles and control vertex density.

## Results

On the DTU dataset, Mesh Splatting achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 0.62 cm with only 300K vertices and 23 minutes of training, matching Neuralangelo's accuracy (0.62 cm) while using 3.3× fewer vertices and 26× less training time. On BlendedMVS, the method achieves a mean CD of 1.71 cm, outperforming the next-best method IMLS-Splatting (2.75 cm) by 38%. The method also demonstrates strong efficiency: at full resolution on DTU scan122, Mesh Splatting uses 13 GB memory and 22 min, whereas iterative mesh rasterization runs out of memory entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DTU, Mesh Splatting (0.62 cm mean CD) ties Neuralangelo (0.62 cm) but uses 300K vs. 1000K vertices and 23 min vs. 600 min training; it slightly trails IMLS-Splatting (0.57 cm) by 0.05 cm, which the authors attribute to IMLS-Splatting's more flexible grid-based 3D parameterization. On BlendedMVS, the method clearly wins: 1.71 cm mean CD vs. IMLS-Splatting (2.75 cm), NeuS (2.68 cm), Surfel (2.46 cm), and SuGaR (8.71 cm), a 38% improvement over the closest competitor. Against volumetric methods (NeuS, Neuralangelo, Surfel, 2DGS, GOF), the advantage is consistent on both datasets, with the largest gains on BlendedMVS where complex geometry exposes meshing-induced error accumulation. SuGaR, the only other mesh-based baseline, is substantially outperformed (1.33 cm on DTU, 8.71 cm on BlendedMVS), confirming that single-layer Gaussian-splatting proxies without remeshing are insufficient. The ablation 'Ours w/o MS' (shading-only supervision, no softening) scores 0.73 cm on DTU and 1.94 cm on BlendedMVS, showing that the multi-layer volumetric supervision contributes meaningfully beyond what shading priors alone can achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established paradigms in multi-view surface reconstruction: volumetric methods (NeRF, 3DGS, NeuS, Neuralangelo, GaussianSurfel) that enjoy large 3D receptive fields but require error-prone meshing, and direct mesh optimization methods (NvdiffRec, SuGaR, IMLS-Splatting) that preserve topology control but suffer from single-layer receptive fields and reliance on shading/normal priors. The key departure is 'mesh softening'—offsetting a base mesh into multiple semi-transparent layers whose alpha is computed differentiably from signed distance (via a VolSDF-style mapping), creating a pseudo-volumetric representation that still permits remeshing. The rendering pipeline borrows tile-based rasterization and depth-sorted volumetric compositing from 3DGS, while topology control combines DMTet (from NvdiffRec) with Continuous Remeshing (Palfinger), a hybrid not previously combined in this way.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a reconstruction pipeline that achieves SOTA Chamfer distance on DTU and BlendedMVS with roughly 3× fewer vertices (~300K vs ~1000K) and ~20-minute training, making it more deployable for downstream applications like simulation and animation. The conceptual contribution—making mesh layers differentiable with respect to the base geometry so that volumetric rendering gradients directly deform the mesh—is a clean bridge that prior shell-based methods (GSM, AdaptiveShell, Gaussian Frosting) lacked. Open questions include scalability to large scenes (tetrahedral-grid memory), handling extremely thin structures (cables, hair) where isotropic remeshing fails, and whether the softening bandwidth can be made adaptive per-region.
