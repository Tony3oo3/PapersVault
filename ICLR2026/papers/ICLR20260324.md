## Problem

Rule-based reinforcement learning with outcome rewards (e.g., GRPO) has proven effective for eliciting reasoning in MLLMs, but it provides no supervision over the intermediate thinking process. Models can arrive at correct answers through flawed or sub-optimal reasoning trajectories, which the outcome reward treats identically to sound reasoning, leading to poor generalization. The paper asks whether a reliable thinking-process reward can be integrated into GRPO training without introducing reward hacking.

## Input / Output

The reasoning model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) takes an RGB image and a text question as input and produces a free-form chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a final answer (numerical, multiple-choice, OCR text, or free-form). The thinking reward model (Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct) separately consumes the image, question, and the full model response, outputting a single scalar score in {0, 0.1, …, 1.0} that reflects holistic reasoning quality independent of answer correctness. The rule-based outcome reward is computed by task-specific functions (exact match, WER, ROUGE) comparing the extracted final answer to ground truth.

## Method

SophiaVL-R1 introduces Trust-GRPO, an RL algorithm that augments the standard GRPO objective with a trust-weighted thinking reward. A 3B thinking reward model, trained via SFT on 156k (question, response, score) tuples annotated by Qwen2.5-VL-72B across five holistic criteria (logical soundness, correct reasoning, error identification, language consistency, redundancy), scores each response's reasoning quality. To guard against reward hacking, a trustworthiness weight γ is computed per response group by comparing the mean thinking reward of correct-answer responses (μ_c) against incorrect-answer responses (μ_w): γ = 1 if μ_c ≥ μ_w, else e^(μ_c − μ_w), so misaligned rewards are automatically down-weighted at zero extra inference cost. A time-based exponential annealing factor e^(−steps/T) further decays the thinking reward's influence over 1,500 training steps, shifting reliance toward the accurate rule-based outcome reward in later stages. The final per-response reward is R_i = R_i^o + γ·α·e^(−steps/T)·R_i^t, and the standard GRPO clipped-surrogate objective with KL regularization is optimized over groups of 8 sampled responses.

## Results

SophiaVL-R1-7B achieves 71.3% on MathVista and 48.8% on MathVerse, surpassing the Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct+GRPO baseline (69.9% / 45.3%) and the VisualPRM-based InternVL2.5-8B (68.5% / 30.7%) by 2.8 and 18.1 points respectively. On general multimodal benchmarks, it reaches 61.3% on MMMU, 2403.8 on MME, 88.5% on ChartQA, 85.4% on MMBench, and 66.7% on MMStar, outperforming LLaVA-OneVision-72B (10× more parameters) on MMMU by 4.5 points and on MME by 142.8 points. The 7B model also outperforms GPT-4V on MathVista (71.3 vs. 51.8) and MathVerse (48.8 vs. 32.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SophiaVL-R1-7B outperforms all compared baselines across every reported benchmark. Against the closest same-size baseline, Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct+GRPO, it gains +1.4 on MathVista, +3.5 on MathVerse, +3.3 on MMMU, +105.6 on MME, +1.3 on ChartQA, +2.0 on MMBench, and +1.1 on MMStar. The most striking comparison is against LLaVA-OneVision-72B (10× more parameters): SophiaVL-R1-7B wins on MathVista (71.3 vs. 68.4), MathVerse (48.8 vs. 27.2), MMMU (61.3 vs. 56.8), MME (2403.8 vs. 2261.0), ChartQA (88.5 vs. 83.7), and MMStar (66.7 vs. 66.1), losing only on MMBench where the 72B model has no reported score. Against the PRM-based VisualPRM (InternVL2.5-8B), the improvement is especially large on MathVerse (+18.1 points), suggesting holistic thinking-reward supervision is more effective than step-wise process rewards for generalizable reasoning. On GPT-4V, SophiaVL-R1-7B leads on MathVista (+19.5), MathVerse (+16.0), and MME (+477.8), while trailing on MMMU (61.3 vs. 56.8 is actually a win) and MMBench (85.4 vs. 75.0, also a win), confirming strong competitive performance against a proprietary model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DeepSeek-R1/GRPO paradigm for rule-based RL training of MLLMs by adding a holistic thinking reward signal alongside the standard outcome reward. It departs from step-wise Process Reward Models (e.g., VisualPRM, Lightman et al.) by evaluating reasoning quality at a whole-process level rather than per-step, avoiding the rigidity and exploitability of step-level constraints. The key algorithmic contribution, Trust-GRPO, introduces a trustworthiness weight and time-based annealing to mitigate reward hacking when model-generated rewards are unreliable, a problem not addressed in prior GRPO-based MLLM training (R1-OneVision, Video-R1, OpenVLThinker).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The trustworthiness weighting mechanism offers a practical, zero-overhead solution to reward hacking in RL training with learned reward models, a problem that becomes more acute as models scale and reward models become less reliable. The finding that a holistic thinking reward outperforms step-wise PRMs (18.1-point gap on MathVerse) challenges the field's investment in fine-grained process supervision and suggests coarser-grained signals may be more effective for generalizable reasoning. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to non-mathematical reasoning domains, whether the thinking reward model's reliability improves with scale, and whether more sophisticated trust estimation (beyond group-level averaging) could further reduce reward hacking.
