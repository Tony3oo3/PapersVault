## Problem

Constrained deep learning problems are typically solved via gradient descent-ascent on the Lagrangian, but this approach suffers from oscillations and may fail to converge to all local solutions. The Augmented Lagrangian Method (ALM) resolves both issues with strong theoretical guarantees, yet practitioners instead rely on dual optimistic ascent (PI control) on the standard Lagrangian, which performs well empirically but lacks formal convergence guarantees. This paper closes that gap by establishing a previously unknown equivalence between the two approaches.

## Input / Output

The method operates on twice-differentiable objective and constraint functions in the single-step, first-order regime: one primal gradient step (with any first-order optimizer such as GD or Adam) per dual update. The key tunable parameter is the optimism coefficient ω, which the paper shows corresponds directly to the ALM penalty coefficient c. The output is the constrained minimizer x* together with its Lagrange multipliers, with the guarantee that all strict and regular local constrained minimizers are recoverable as limit points of the dynamics.

## Method

The core result is that dual optimistic ascent on the Lagrangian is equivalent to gradient descent-ascent on the Augmented Lagrangian: for equality constraints, primal iterates match exactly when ω = c and initializations are aligned (Theorem 1); for general inequality constraints, both algorithms share the same set of locally stable stationary points (Theorem 2). The mechanism is that the optimistic correction term ω(h(x_t) − h(x_{t−1})) in the dual update effectively constructs a 'lookahead' multiplier that replicates the extrapolated multiplier appearing in the ALM primal gradient. This equivalence transfers ALM's local linear convergence guarantees to dual optimistic ascent, proving it converges to all strict local constrained minimizers satisfying standard regularity conditions (Theorem 3). It also provides principled hyperparameter guidance: larger ω expands the set of attainable solutions and dampens oscillations, but excessively large values induce ill-conditioning, motivating ALM-style penalty scheduling for ω. The equivalence is limited to the single-step, first-order regime; with multi-step primal updates or second-order optimizers, the disguise breaks down and the explicit ALM is recommended.

## Results

The paper's main contribution is theoretical: Theorem 1 proves exact equivalence of primal iterates between dual optimistic ascent and the Augmented Lagrangian Method for equality-constrained problems (when ω = c), and Theorem 2 shows both methods share the same set of locally stable stationary points for general inequality constraints. Theorem 3 establishes that dual optimistic ascent recovers all strict and regular local constrained minimizers—strictly more than standard GDA on the Lagrangian. Empirically, a 1D non-convex equality-constrained example (min ½x² s.t. eˣ = e) numerically confirms identical primal trajectories, and an ALM-inspired multiplicative schedule for ω (γ=2, β=0.99) reduces multiplier overshoot compared to a fixed ω.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing methods in the standard empirical sense; instead, it establishes an equivalence between dual optimistic ascent (PI control) and the Augmented Lagrangian Method (ALM). Relative to standard gradient descent-ascent (GDA) on the Lagrangian, dual optimistic ascent is strictly more powerful: it recovers all strict local constrained minimizers (Theorem 3), whereas GDA can only reach solutions that are local min-max points of the Lagrangian. Relative to the ALM, dual optimistic ascent is equivalent in the single-step, first-order regime (same LSSPs, same convergence rates when η_dual ≈ c), but the equivalence breaks down for multi-step primal updates or second-order optimizers, in which case the explicit ALM is recommended. The paper also notes that applying dual optimism directly to the Augmented Lagrangian simply compounds the penalty (c + ω), confirming the two frameworks are fully interchangeable for equality constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously independent lines of work in constrained optimization: the classical Augmented Lagrangian Method (Hestnes 1969, Powell 1969, Rockafellar 1973) and the modern dual optimistic ascent / PI control heuristic (Stooke et al. 2020, Sohrabi et al. 2024, Mokhtari et al. 2020). While the broader optimistic gradient method literature (OGDA, extra-gradient) has established convergence results for min-max games, the authors note those results do not apply here because they apply optimism to both players rather than only the dual player, and they assume different convexity structures. The paper carves new ground by proving an exact equivalence between the two methods in the single-step, first-order regime, repurposing the well-understood ALM theory to explain and guarantee the behavior of the empirically successful PI control scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This equivalence provides the first formal convergence guarantees for dual optimistic ascent in constrained deep learning, transferring the robust local linear convergence results of ALM to a method that practitioners have relied on empirically for years. It also reframes the optimism hyperparameter ω as the ALM penalty coefficient c, enabling the use of established penalty-scheduling strategies for tuning. However, the equivalence is strictly limited to the single-step, first-order regime; with multi-step primal updates or second-order optimizers, the connection breaks down and the authors explicitly recommend using ALM directly. Open questions include global convergence for non-convex problems, extension of global guarantees to inequality constraints, and whether analogous equivalences exist for optimistic methods in general min-max games (e.g., GANs).
