## Problem

Modular 3D assets—composed of discrete primitives with constrained design parameters—are the natural representation for game engines and user-generated content, yet they suffer from a complete absence of public training data and automated generation methods. Existing 3D generation approaches (meshes, voxels, neural fields) produce large, complex outputs that are difficult to integrate into production pipelines, inaccessible to non-professional users, and ill-suited for the transmission constraints of UGC platforms. This paper addresses both the data scarcity and the lack of a generation framework tailored to the sequential, discrete nature of modular assets.

## Input / Output

The model takes a short text prompt (a phrase bundle of 3–5 descriptors, e.g., 'apartment, multi-story, flat roof, few windows', generated by GPT-4o from rendered images) as conditioning and produces an ordered sequence of building primitives. Each primitive is encoded as a 5-token tuple—class (25 types), rotation (4 options), and 3D position (x∈59, y∈44, z∈81 discrete values)—yielding a token sequence of length 5N for N primitives (up to ~5000 tokens for complex buildings). The output is a lossless, engine-ready modular asset that requires no post-processing such as vertex merging and can be directly mapped to textured modules at runtime.

## Method

AssetFormer is a decoder-only Llama-based Transformer (312M parameters, 24 layers) trained with cross-entropy next-token prediction over a joint vocabulary of 214 discrete tokens. The key architectural insight is a DFS-based token re-ordering: primitives are traversed via depth-first search from a lower corner to produce a spatially coherent sequence that captures local connectivity, outperforming raw order and BFS. During inference, a token-set filtering mechanism masks logits to only the valid attribute vocabulary at each position (e.g., after a class token, only rotation tokens are sampled), ensuring structurally valid primitives. Text conditioning uses a FLAN-T5 XL encoder projected through an MLP, with classifier-free guidance (scale 2.0) for improved prompt alignment. For efficient inference, a SlowFast decoding scheme adapts speculative decoding: a smaller 87M draft model proposes K tokens that are verified by the larger target model with token-type-aware rejection sampling, achieving ~47% speedup with negligible quality loss.

## Results

On the AssetFormer dataset, the best configuration (AssetFormer + Top-K Sampling with DFS token ordering) achieves a FID of 55.186 and CLIP score of 0.320, substantially outperforming the PCG baseline (FID 108.476, CLIP 0.319). Among sampling strategies, top-k sampling (k=10) yields the best FID (55.186) compared to greedy search (63.351) and beam search (63.333). The SlowFast decoding variant maintains near-identical quality (FID 55.831) while accelerating inference from 80.62 to 119.02 tokens/s. In a user study, AssetFormer scores 3.50/5 on diversity and aesthetic, and 3.92/5 on complexity, approaching ground truth levels (4.00, 3.67, 4.42 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the PCG baseline, AssetFormer with top-k sampling nearly halves the FID (55.186 vs. 108.476) while matching its CLIP score, demonstrating that data-driven autoregressive generation captures the full breadth of the building distribution that rule-based procedural generation cannot. Compared to general-purpose 3D generation methods (SF3D, Tripo 2.0, Trellis, Hunyuan3D 2.0), AssetFormer is qualitatively superior for modular game assets: these methods produce dense meshes with texturing artifacts and struggle with internal building structures, whereas AssetFormer's primitive-based output integrates directly into game engines with clean primitive-texture mapping. Against MeshGPT, AssetFormer produces compact, engine-ready primitive arrangements, while MeshGPT's vertex/face tokenization becomes intractable at the scale of complex buildings (30K+ faces vs. MeshGPT's typical <4K face limit), and decoding frequently fails on high-complexity samples. In the token-ordering ablation, AssetFormer with DFS (FID 55.186) outperforms the RAR randomized-ordering method (FID 83.561) by a wide margin, indicating that spatially-aware traversal is critical for 3D modular generation unlike 2D image generation where token randomization helps.

## Relation to Prior Work

AssetFormer extends the autoregressive Transformer paradigm (Llama backbone, as in Emu3, LLaMA-Mesh, and MeshGPT) from dense mesh or image tokenization to a discrete modular representation where each primitive is a 5-token tuple over a joint vocabulary. It departs from prior 3D generation work (MeshGPT, PolyGen, Hunyuan3D, Trellis) by targeting CSG-style primitive assembly rather than continuous geometry, drawing on Constructive Solid Geometry concepts from CAD and procedural content generation from game development. The SlowFast decoding adapts speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., Chen et al.) with token-type-aware rejection sampling, and the DFS/BFS token reordering is a novel sequencing strategy specific to spatially-structured 3D assets, distinct from raster-order or randomized-order approaches used in image generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in game development and UGC pipelines where modular assets (small file size, engine-ready, user-editable) are preferred over dense meshes, and provides the first real-world modular 3D dataset (16K samples from a game platform). Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond buildings to other asset categories, whether image-based conditioning can be added, and how to handle varying design spaces beyond fixed discrete vocabularies. The finding that native 3D generative models fail even under overfitting on modular data suggests a fundamental representational mismatch that may constrain the applicability of mesh-based or implicit-field methods to structured, component-based assets.
