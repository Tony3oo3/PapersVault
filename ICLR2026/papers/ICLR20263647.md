## Problem

Current autonomous driving architectures treat each inference cycle as isolated, neglecting how the ego-vehicle's executed motion reshapes subsequent sensory observations. Even temporal models that fuse historical states fail to capture the ego-centric feedback loop, as evidenced by UniAD's planning being barely affected by removing temporal fusion while tracking degrades severely. This decoupling of action from observation constrains the system's ability to internalize the dynamic interplay between ego motion and scene evolution, ultimately limiting planning acumen.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video from 6 cameras (e.g., 256×704 or 512×1408 per view) along with ego-vehicle kinematic states (positions at t-2, t-1, t for steering-circle estimation and forward-direction vector). The ego-guided partition divides image features along the width dimension into flow units whose starting point and per-side sizes are dynamically determined by the ego's heading and steering radius. The learned spatio-temporal flow feature (F_fuse ∈ R^{NK×H×P×C}) is then injected into downstream tasks via object-level cross-attention or region-level concatenation, producing 3D detections, occupancy grids, ego trajectories, or VLM planning commands depending on the attached head.

## Method

FlowAD formulates ego-scene interaction as a learnable scene flow in latent space, structured around three components. First, an ego-guided scene partition divides multi-view image features into flow units along the width, with the partition start set by the ego forward-direction vector and per-side sizes scaled quadratically by the steering-circle radius (r ± w_ego/2)²/r² to reflect differential lateral flow speeds; multi-level FPN features and local 3-unit self-attention aggregation handle varied receptive fields and object fragmentation. Second, spatial flow prediction auto-regressively updates GRU-based queries from ahead flow units and uses cross-attention to forecast rear units (modeling spatial displacement), while temporal flow prediction does the same across frames (modeling temporal variation); both are supervised with KL-divergence losses between predicted and GT latent states, following world-model conventions. Third, task-aware enhancement injects the fused spatio-temporal flow feature into object queries (cross-attention) or region features (concatenation + convolution), enabling the framework to plug into diverse baselines (SparseBEV, SparseDrive, DiffusionDrive, Senna) without architectural redesign. The key distinction from prior world-model approaches is the explicit ego-motion conditioning in the partition geometry and the decoupling of transition state from observation feature via cross-attention rather than direct state prediction.

## Results

On nuScenes, FlowAD_SparseDrive (ResNet50) achieves 44.8% mAP / 55.5% NDS for detection, 42.7% AMOTA for tracking, 0.56 m Avg.L2 / 0.06% collision rate for planning, and a novel FCP of 1.03 frames—improving over SparseDrive by +3.0% mAP, +4.1% AMOTA, and reducing FCP from 2.55 to 1.03 frames (60% faster correct response). On Bench2Drive closed-loop, FlowAD attains a driving score of 51.77 and success rate of 22.02%, surpassing SparseDrive (44.54 / 16.71%) by +7.23% DS and +5.31% SR. For VLM high-level planning, FlowAD reaches 90.99% accuracy vs. Senna*'s 88.54%, with particularly large gains in safety-critical steering (Turn Left F1: 60.71% vs. 30.53%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowAD consistently outperforms its base architectures across all tasks. Over SparseDrive (ResNet50), it gains +3.0% mAP, +4.1% AMOTA, reduces planning L2 from 0.61→0.56 m, collision from 0.08%→0.06%, and FCP from 2.55→1.03 frames; over DiffusionDrive (ResNet50), it gains +6.5% mAP and +9.2% AMOTA with FCP dropping from 1.35→1.05. On Bench2Drive closed-loop, FlowAD (DS 51.77, SR 22.02%) substantially beats SparseDrive (44.54, 16.71%) and UniAD (45.81, 16.36%), with the largest margin in ability metrics (Mean 25.42 vs. 18.60 for SparseDrive). For VLM planning, FlowAD (90.99%) surpasses finetuned Senna (88.54%), with the most dramatic improvement in Turn Left F1 (60.71% vs. 30.53%) and Turn Right F1 (68.17% vs. 46.94%). In 3D occupancy prediction, FlowAD (38.4 RayIoU) beats SparseOcc (35.7) by +2.7 points. The method also outperforms world-model-based planners (DriveWM: L2 0.80, Col 0.26%) and other SOTA E2E methods (MomAD, LAW, UncAD, GenAD) on planning metrics, confirming the generality of the ego-scene interactive modeling paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowAD positions itself at the intersection of world-model-based driving (DriveDreamer, Drive-WM) and sparse E2E architectures (SparseBEV, SparseDrive, Senna), but departs from both in a key way: rather than treating the entire environment as a single latent embedding to be transitioned forward by an action vector, it decomposes the scene into ego-guided flow units whose geometry (starting point, left/right partition size) is explicitly shaped by the ego-vehicle's forward direction and steering kinematics. The spatial and temporal flow prediction modules borrow the GRU-plus-KL-divergence recipe from Dreamer-style world models (Hafner et al., 2020/2021), but apply it at the granularity of individual flow units rather than a global state, and decouple the transition state from the observation feature via cross-attention. The paper also contrasts with temporal architectures like UniAD, arguing (with supporting ablation in Table 1) that existing temporal fusion captures environmental continuity but not the specific feedback loop from ego-actions to future observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ego-scene interactive modeling paradigm addresses a genuine gap in E2E driving: most systems treat each inference step as open-loop, severing the causal link between planned motion and subsequent perception. The FCP metric (Frames before Correct Planning) is a useful addition to the evaluation toolkit, offering a reaction-speed perspective that L2 error and collision rate do not capture. However, the practical cost is non-trivial—FPS drops from 9.0 to 7.6 (SparseDrive) or 7.3 to 5.4 (ResNet-101), and the multi-level flow prediction is identified as the primary bottleneck. Open questions include whether the ego-guided partition can be made more efficient (the authors note a lightweight single-level variant in Table 9), whether the approach generalizes beyond the two datasets tested, and whether the FCP metric will gain traction as a standard benchmark.
