## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) offers photorealistic real-time rendering but lacks the fine-grained object-level semantics and physically executable structure required for Vision-Language Navigation. Existing VLN benchmarks rely on scanned mesh reconstructions (Matterport3D, HM3D) that suffer from boundary ambiguity, texture artifacts under novel views, and merged object surfaces, making precise language grounding and reliable collision simulation difficult.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260784/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives synchronized RGB, depth, and semantic segmentation observations from the 3DGS-rendered environment along with hierarchical natural-language instructions (high-level task-semantic goals like 'I'm thirsty, get water from the table' or low-level kinematic commands like 'move forward two steps'). The output is a continuous navigation action—velocity commands (v, ω) for ground robots or 6-DoF velocity/attitude commands for UAVs—executed in metric 3D space without teleportation between panoramic nodes, producing a full trajectory evaluated by both standard VLN metrics (SR, OSR, SPL) and three new continuity metrics (CSR, ICP, PS).

## Method

SAGE-3D upgrades 3DGS into an executable environment via two components. First, Object-Centric Semantic Grounding: 3DGS scenes are sampled from artist-created mesh scenes (~3,000 camera views per scene via the gsplat pipeline), then manually annotated at the object level (1K scenes, 554k instances, 755 categories) to produce InteriorGS; a 2D semantic top-down map is derived by projecting annotated 3D objects onto the ground plane with convex-hull footprints to support A*-based path planning and MLLM-driven instruction generation. Second, Physics-Aware Execution Jointing: CoACD convex decomposition is applied to the original artist meshes to extract per-object collision bodies, which are assembled as invisible rigid shapes in a USD scene alongside the visible 3DGS appearance layer, forming a 3DGS-Mesh Hybrid Representation integrated into Isaac Sim with robot APIs (Unitree G1/Go2/H1, quadrotors). The resulting SAGE-Bench contains 2M trajectory-instruction pairs with a three-axis evaluation framework (task type × instruction level × episode complexity) and three novel continuity metrics—Continuous Success Ratio, Integrated Collision Penalty, and Path Smoothness—that capture motion quality invisible to conventional endpoint-based metrics.

## Results

On SAGE-Bench VLN (High-level Instruction), NaVILA-SAGE achieves the best SR of 0.46, OSR of 0.55, and SPL of 0.48, substantially outperforming the SOTA VLN model NaVILA (0.39 SR, 0.47 OSR, 0.34 SPL) and all MLLM baselines (0.10–0.14 SR). On the VLN-CE R2R Val-Unseen benchmark, NaVILA-SAGE trained solely on SAGE-Bench data achieves 0.38 SR, a 31% relative improvement over its NaVILA-base counterpart (0.29 SR), demonstrating strong cross-benchmark generalizability. 3DGS scene data renders at 6.2 ms/frame (vs. 16.7 ms for scanned mesh) but requires ~33% more training iterations to reach 40% SR (160 vs. 120 iterations).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NaVILA-SAGE (0.46 SR) outperforms the strongest VLN baseline NaVILA (0.39 SR) by 18% relative SR and 17% relative OSR on SAGE-Bench, while also achieving better continuity metrics (ICP 0.54 vs. 0.61, PS 0.74 vs. 0.68). NaVid-SAGE (0.36 SR) more than doubles the performance of its NaVid-base counterpart (0.10 SR) and surpasses the original NaVid (0.15 SR) by 140% relative SR. Among MLLM baselines, GPT-4.1 and Qwen-VL-MAX achieve 0.13–0.14 SR, comparable to dedicated VLN models CMA (0.13) and NaVid (0.15), but all fall far short of NaVILA-SAGE. On VLN-CE R2R Val-Unseen, NaVILA-SAGE (0.38 SR) beats NaVILA-base (0.29 SR) by 31% and matches the original NaVid (0.37 SR), confirming that training on 3DGS data transfers effectively to scanned-mesh environments. The new continuity metrics reveal that even the best baseline NaVILA suffers from sustained collisions (ICP 0.61) and unsmooth motion (PS 0.68), issues invisible to traditional SR/CR metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLN benchmark lineage (VLN-CE, OVON, GOAT-Bench, IR2R-CE) by replacing scanned-mesh scene representations (Matterport3D, HM3D) with 3D Gaussian Splatting as the environment substrate, building on 3DGS's photorealistic rendering (Kerbl et al., 2023) and prior hybrid-representation work for robotics (Robo-GS, R1-GSBridge). It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) introducing object-level semantic annotations directly on 3DGS primitives rather than post-hoc segmentation of merged mesh surfaces, and (2) coupling 3DGS appearance with CoACD-derived convex collision bodies in a 3DGS-Mesh Hybrid Representation for physically executable simulation in Isaac Sim. The hierarchical instruction scheme (causal, scenario-driven, attribute-based) extends the 'A-to-B' navigation paradigm of earlier benchmarks toward task-semantically grounded instructions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAGE-Bench (2M trajectory-instruction pairs, 1K scenes) and InteriorGS (554k object instances, 755 categories) provide the first large-scale 3DGS-based VLN infrastructure, potentially shifting the field away from texture-limited scanned meshes toward photorealistic, semantically rich environments. The finding that scene diversity outweighs sample count for VLN generalization, and that 3DGS data is harder to converge yet transfers better to unseen environments (31% SR gain on VLN-CE), offers practical guidance for future data curation. Open questions include whether the 3DGS advantage persists at larger model scales, whether the convex-hull collision layer suffices for manipulation or deformable-object tasks, and how well these photorealistic simulations close the sim-to-real gap for deployed robots.
