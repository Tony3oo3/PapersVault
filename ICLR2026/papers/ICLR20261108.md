## Problem

Open-source LVLMs produce suboptimal image captions due to two intrinsic biases: multimodal bias (imbalanced descriptive granularity, over-describing some objects while glossing over others) and linguistic bias (hallucinating non-existent objects from language priors). Existing solutions either rely on expensive human annotation or proprietary APIs (non-scalable) or on auxiliary tools like object detectors and image taggers whose coverage is inherently limited. The paper argues that general-purpose LVLMs already possess sufficient perceptual capacity for rich captioning but lack proper guidance to extract it.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (short-edge ≥ 600 px, complexity score in [0.4, 0.8]) and a captioning instruction as input, and produces a structured, paragraph-level image caption as output. A user-defined scale budget N controls the number of heuristic questions asked, allowing a continuous trade-off between caption richness and inference cost. The final caption is assembled from contrastive-filtered 'golden sentences' plus separately summarized object-level and position-level detail sets, yielding a coherent multi-sentence description.

## Method

ScaleCap is a two-component scalable debiasing pipeline. First, a Contrastive Sentence Rating module performs offline sentence-level contrastive decoding: it computes per-token probabilities with and without the image (ΔP = P − P′), then retains only sentences whose maximum ΔP exceeds a threshold τ, producing visually grounded 'golden sentences' that eliminate hallucinated content without disrupting fluency. Second, a Heuristic Question Answering module uses a powerful LLM to generate content-specific object and position instructions from the golden sentences, which a lightweight 7B LVLM answers to progressively inject fine-grained visual details; each answer is re-filtered by the contrastive rating module. A final LLM integration step consolidates the object and position detail sets into a coherent caption. The key insight is that detail omission stems from suboptimal information extraction rather than insufficient perception, so even a 7B LVLM matches 72B perceptual quality when guided by targeted questions, making the pipeline both scalable (via budget N) and cost-efficient.

## Results

Pretraining with ScaleCap-450K consistently achieves the best average score across 11 multimodal benchmarks for all three model configurations: 64.7 (Qwen2.5-7B), 60.1 (Qwen2.5-3B), and 60.2 (InternLM2.5-7B), outperforming DenseFusion-450K by 1.7, 1.2, and 1.1 points respectively. On the Qwen2.5-7B setting, ScaleCap-450K gains +4.3 on InfoVQA and +7.0 on MMVet over ShareGPT4V-450K. In the Prism caption-informativeness evaluation, ScaleCap with Qwen2-VL-7B (58.2 avg) surpasses even Qwen2-VL-72B (56.0 avg) using a generic instruction, and the Golden Sentence strategy reduces CHAIR_S hallucination from 48.8 to 33.6 on LLaVA-1.5-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScaleCap-450K outperforms ShareGPT4V-450K (Chen et al., 2023) on all 11 benchmarks, with the largest gaps on MMVet (+7.0) and InfoVQA (+4.3) under the Qwen2.5-7B setting. Against DenseFusion-450K (Li et al., 2024), which fuses multiple expert detectors, ScaleCap wins on 10 of 11 benchmarks (losing only MMStar by 0.4), confirming that general-purpose LVLMs with structured prompting can match or exceed tool-dependent pipelines. In the Prism framework (Qiao et al., 2024), ScaleCap with Qwen2-VL-7B (58.2 avg) beats the generic-instruction baseline of Qwen2-VL-72B (56.0 avg) by 2.2 points, demonstrating that caption quality, not model scale, drives informativeness. ScaleCap(GPT-4o+GPT-4o) achieves 76.1 on MMVet, surpassing GPT-4V (67.5), Sonnet 3.5 (66.0), and Gemini-2.0-Pro (70.4). For hallucination mitigation, the Golden Sentence strategy reduces CHAIR_S to 33.6 on LLaVA-1.5-7B, substantially outperforming both VCD (Leng et al., 2024; 46.8) and OPERA (Huang et al., 2024; 44.6), and also improves Qwen2-VL-7B from 44.2 to 25.8.

## Relation to Prior Work

ScaleCap builds on the visual contrastive decoding (VCD) framework of Leng et al. (2024), repurposing the core idea of contrasting image-conditioned vs. text-only token probabilities but shifting from online token-level decoding to offline sentence-level filtering, which preserves fluency. It departs from tool-dependent captioning pipelines (DenseFusion, DCE, VFC, CapMAS) that rely on external detectors or taggers, arguing instead that general-purpose LVLMs already possess sufficient perceptual capacity and merely need structured guidance to extract it. The iterative heuristic question-answering loop is a novel mechanism not present in prior captioning work, and the overall pipeline positions itself as a scalable, inference-budget-controlled alternative to the single-pass captioning used in ShareGPT4V.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ScaleCap-450K dataset and the associated pretraining recipe offer a practical, reproducible path for the community to improve LVLM pretraining without proprietary APIs or human annotation, with consistent gains across 11 benchmarks and three model architectures. The finding that 7B LVLMs saturate in perceptual extraction (matching 72B) while larger models only add world knowledge provides a useful efficiency guideline for captioning pipelines. Open questions include whether the contrastive sentence rating threshold generalizes across domains without retuning, whether the approach extends to video captioning, and whether the 72B LLM requirement for the integration step can be reduced as smaller models improve at long-context summarization.
