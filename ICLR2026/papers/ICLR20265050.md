## Problem

Existing AI-generated text detectors either require white-box probability access, incur high costs from multiple LLM calls, underperform on short texts, or fail to generalize across evolving source models. No prior method simultaneously achieves high accuracy while remaining efficient, black-box practical, generalizable, and robust across diverse generation models and text lengths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265050/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an original text (any length, any domain) and a black-box LLM (e.g., DeepSeek-v3 via API or Mistral-7B locally) as inputs. The text is segmented into punctuation-delimited chunks, tokens within each chunk are randomly permuted, and the disrupted text is sent to the LLM in a single recovery call. The output is a binary Human/AI label produced by a lightweight classifier over three scalar features: BERTScore F1 (semantic similarity), Kendall's τ, and Spearman's ρ (structural similarity) computed between the original and recovered texts.

## Method

D&R is grounded in the Concentration Assumption: after a model-free disruption called Within-Chunk Shuffling (WCS), AI-generated text yields LLM recoveries that concentrate sharply around the original, while human text produces more dispersed recoveries. WCS preserves chunk boundaries and only permutes tokens within each punctuation-delimited span, constraining recovery to a locally permuted candidate space that aligns with LLM pretraining objectives. A single black-box LLM call restores the text, and semantic (BERTScore F1) plus structural (Kendall's τ, Spearman's ρ) recovery similarities serve as observable proxies for posterior concentration. The three similarity scores feed a lightweight binary classifier trained on labeled AI/human pairs. Theoretically, Theorem 1 proves that (r,δ)-concentrated posteriors entail high expected recovery similarity, and Theorem 2 establishes a non-trivial gap between AI and human expected similarity under the Concentration Assumption, providing formal justification for the detection signal.

## Results

On four long-text datasets (ML-ArXiv-Papers, CNN-DailyMail, IMDB, ROCStories) averaged over six source models, D&R achieves a mean AUROC of 0.9602 ± 0.0351, surpassing the strongest baseline RAIDAR (0.8770 ± 0.0657) by +0.08. On three short-text datasets (Wikihow, AG-News, Reddit), D&R reaches 0.8687 ± 0.0888 AUROC, outperforming RAIDAR (0.7248) by +0.14 and Fast-DetectGPT (0.6561) by +0.21. D&R also requires only a single LLM call per sample, reducing average latency from ~15 s (RAIDAR) to ~2 s and estimated API cost from $5 to $0.2 per 1,000 samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D&R outperforms all eleven baselines across every long-text dataset and source model combination. The closest competitor, RAIDAR (rewriting-based, ICLR 2024), trails D&R by +0.08 AUROC on average (0.8770 vs. 0.9602) and by a wider +0.14 on short texts (0.7248 vs. 0.8687); the gap widens further under source-recovery model mismatch, where RAIDAR degrades by 9.4% on average while D&R drops only 1.9%. Among perturbation-based methods, Fast-DetectGPT (ICLR 2024) achieves 0.6685 AUROC on long texts and 0.6561 on short texts, both substantially below D&R. Supervised and learning-based detectors (RoBERTa classifier, DeTeCtive, ImBD, OOD-based) show high variance across source models and generally fall in the 0.62–0.84 range, with none approaching D&R's consistency. Notably, D&R with a smaller local recovery model (Mistral-7B, mean AUROC 0.9359) still exceeds RAIDAR using the larger DeepSeek-v3 (0.8823), confirming that the method's gains stem from the disruption-recovery design rather than recovery-model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

D&R carves new ground at the intersection of perturbation-based detection (DetectGPT, Fast-DetectGPT) and rewriting-based consistency detection (RAIDAR), but departs from both in a key way: it replaces model-dependent perturbations or multi-call paraphrasing with a model-free disruption (Within-Chunk Shuffling) followed by a single black-box recovery call. The closest conceptual ancestor is RAIDAR, which also assesses transformation consistency, but D&R reframes the signal as posterior concentration rather than edit-distance consistency, and grounds this in a formal concentration assumption with provable similarity bounds. The theoretical lens—treating detection as measuring how sharply an LLM's recovery distribution concentrates around the original text—is a new framing not present in prior perturbation or rewriting literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-call design delivers a 7.5× cost reduction over the strongest baseline (RAIDAR) while achieving higher AUROC, making high-quality AI-text detection far more deployable at scale in black-box API settings. The theoretical grounding via the Concentration Assumption provides a principled explanation for *why* the method works, which could guide future disruption-recovery designs beyond text. Open questions remain: the concentration gap narrows for extremely short texts, the assumption may fail when human writers deliberately mimic LLM stylistic patterns, and the method's robustness against adaptive adversaries who are aware of the WCS disruption mechanism is not yet fully characterized.
