## Problem

Generative engines (e.g., Google AI Overview, ChatGPT) have become the new form of search, yet there is no principled understanding of what content preferences drive their document selection, and existing GEO methods rely on ad hoc, manually designed heuristics that often degrade response quality. This paper asks: can we automatically discover engine-specific preference rules at scale and use them to rewrite web content cooperatively—improving visibility without harming generative engine utility?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264643/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a web document (retrieved from ClueWeb22, with 5 candidate documents per query) and a set of extracted preference rules as input, and produces a rewritten document optimized for visibility in a target generative engine's synthesized answer. The preference rules themselves are extracted from tens of thousands of document-pair comparisons where visibility differences are maximized. Rewriting is constrained to preserve factual correctness and semantic fidelity (measured via KPR/KPC), making the optimization cooperative rather than adversarial.

## Method

AutoGEO is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 extracts preference rules via a four-component LLM pipeline: an Explainer compares document pairs with maximum visibility difference against the generated answer, an Extractor distills comparisons into concise insights, a hierarchical Merger recursively consolidates insights into candidate rules (chunking to handle tens of thousands of observations), and a Filter removes query-specific rules. Stage 2 builds two GEO models from these rules: AutoGEO_API injects rules directly into prompts for a frontier LLM (Gemini-2.5-Pro) for plug-and-play rewriting, while AutoGEO_Mini fine-tunes a compact Qwen3-1.7B model via supervised cold start followed by GRPO reinforcement learning with three reward signals—outcome reward (GEO visibility gain), rule reward (LLM-verified rule compliance ratio), and semantic reward (KPR+KPC fidelity to the original document). The key insight is that engine-specific, domain-specific rules consistently outperform generic ones, and using them as both prompt context and RL reward enables cooperative optimization that improves visibility by ~36% on average while maintaining or slightly enhancing generative engine utility.

## Results

On the Gemini generative engine, AutoGEO_API achieves Overall GEO scores of 34.05% (E-commerce), 34.92% (GEO-Bench), and 43.76% (Researchy-GEO), outperforming the strongest baseline (Fluency Optimization at 22.99%, 23.73%, and 27.75% respectively) by up to 50.99%. AutoGEO_Mini, a compact Qwen3-1.7B model trained with GRPO, achieves Overall scores of 25.25%, 27.12%, and 38.53% while costing only ~0.0071× that of AutoGEO_API. Both models maintain or slightly improve generative engine utility (GEU) metrics (Precision, Recall, Clarity, Insight) relative to the vanilla baseline, confirming cooperative optimization rather than adversarial manipulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the nine manually-designed GEO baselines from Aggarwal et al. (2024), both AutoGEO_API and AutoGEO_Mini achieve clear wins across all three datasets and all three GEO metrics (Word, Pos, Overall). AutoGEO_API's largest margin is on Researchy-GEO, where its Overall score of 43.76% exceeds the best baseline (Fluency Optimization, 27.75%) by 16.01 percentage points (~58% relative improvement). AutoGEO_Mini, despite using a 1.7B-parameter model, still surpasses all baselines by an average of 20.99% in Overall GEO score. Against the adversarial methods from Nestaas et al. (2024) (Hijack Attack and Poisoning Attack), AutoGEO_API and AutoGEO_Mini achieve substantially higher GEO scores (43.76% and 38.53% Overall vs. 31.20% and 30.71%) while simultaneously preserving or improving GEU metrics (e.g., Clarity 61.97% and 61.48% vs. 59.08% and 57.82% for adversarial methods), whereas the adversarial attacks degrade answer quality and reliability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GEO task introduced by Aggarwal et al. (KDD 2024), which relied on manually designed rewriting heuristics, by replacing them with automatically extracted preference rules mined from tens of thousands of generative engine interactions. The rule-based reward mechanism for reinforcement learning adapts the AutoRule framework (Wang & Xiong, 2025) to the GEO setting, while the GRPO training procedure follows Shao et al. (2024). A key departure from prior GEO work (including adversarial approaches by Nestaas et al., 2024) is the explicit 'cooperative' objective: optimizing document visibility while simultaneously preserving generative engine utility, evaluated via the DeepResearchGym framework (Coelho et al., 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As generative engines (Google AI Overview, ChatGPT) rapidly replace traditional search, content providers need principled, scalable methods to maintain visibility without degrading answer quality. The finding that preference rules are highly transferable across LLMs (78–84% overlap) but diverge sharply across domains (34–40% for e-commerce) provides actionable guidance for multi-domain content strategies. The global-optimization experiment—showing that universal GEO adoption eliminates individual visibility advantage yet improves overall GE utility—raises important open questions about equilibrium dynamics in a world where all content is optimized. Limitations include evaluation on only five candidate documents per query and reliance on RAG-style engines, leaving agentic and multimodal generative search unexplored.
