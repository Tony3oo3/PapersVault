## Problem

Despite Mamba's success in language and video, its standalone capacity for time series classification (TSC) remains underexplored: the only prior TSC-specific Mamba (TSCMamba) obscures the backbone's intrinsic contribution by coupling it with ROCKET and CWT feature engineering, and vanilla Mamba was previously ranked the weakest TSC backbone. Compounding this, existing TSC benchmarks suffer from restricted UEA dataset coverage, unfair comparisons where TSF-origin models are evaluated without re-tuning, and poor reproducibility, undermining the reliability of comparative conclusions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw multivariate time series of highly variable length (8 to 17,984 timesteps) and input dimensionality (2 to 1,345 channels) from the 30-dataset UEA archive, plus two recent domain-specific benchmarks (ADFTD, FLAAP). The input is first mapped through a 1-D convolution whose kernel size scales with sequence length (k = max(3, ⌊0.02L⌋)), producing per-timestep embeddings of dimension d_m. The output is a class logit vector of dimension d_y, aggregated from per-timestep features via a learned multi-head adaptive pooling rather than a fixed pooling or last-step readout.

## Method

MambaSL is a single-layer selective SSM (Mamba) redesigned around four TSC-specific hypotheses. (H1) The input projection kernel is scaled proportionally to sequence length to match Mamba's gating receptive field. (H2) Time variance is modularized: binary switches θ_Δ, θ_B, θ_C independently select whether each SSM parameter follows a time-invariant (fixed) or time-variant (input-dependent) form, yielding 8 configurations from fully LTI to fully selective; ablations show LTI settings often outperform the fully TV selective SSM for TSC, contradicting Mamba's original language-modeling ablations. (H3) The skip (D) connection is removed so that per-timestep features are produced solely from the hidden state, forcing the SSM to carry all representational load. (H4) A multi-head adaptive pooling with N_h learnable gating heads replaces conventional aggregation: each head scores every timestep, the max across heads is softmax-normalized into per-timestep weights, and the final logit is a weighted sum of per-timestep logits—generalizing both average and max pooling. The framework is trained end-to-end with supervised cross-entropy loss, and a single Mamba block outperforms 2- or 3-block stacks across all 30 UEA datasets.

## Results

MambaSL achieves a state-of-the-art average accuracy of 79.82% across all 30 UEA datasets (78.83% on the standard 10-dataset subset), with an average rank of 5.30 out of 21 models. It surpasses the second-best method, TSCMamba (78.41%), by 1.41 percentage points, and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests confirm statistically significant gains (p < 0.05) over all baselines except HC2 (p = 0.56). MambaSL places in the top-10 on 27 of 30 datasets and achieves top-1 accuracy on 5 datasets. Ablation studies confirm that all four architectural hypotheses (H1–H4) contribute positively, with the full MambaSL outperforming vanilla Mamba (74.24%) by over 5.5 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MambaSL outperforms all 20 baselines in average accuracy across the 30 UEA datasets, with the closest competitor being TSCMamba (78.41% vs. 79.82%, a 1.41%p margin). The advantage is statistically significant (Wilcoxon p < 0.05) against every baseline except HC2 (p = 0.56), though HC2 still ranks 8th overall. Notably, MambaSL is the only deep learning model to surpass the non-DL DTW_D baseline on the challenging CR and HW datasets, where shape-based and distance-based methods typically dominate. On datasets where non-DL or CNN methods are strongest (AWR, EW, RS, SWJ), MambaSL remains competitive, while on datasets favoring MLP or Transformer backbones (FD, HMD, NATO, SRS1), it achieves competitive or superior results. The paper also shows that TSF-origin models (DLinear, PatchTST, iTransformer, etc.) improved by over 3%p on average when properly tuned for TSC, suggesting prior benchmarks underestimated these baselines. MambaSL also generalizes to recent domain-specific benchmarks (ADFTD for medical EEG, FLAAP for human activity recognition) without domain-specific modifications, achieving competitive performance against Medformer baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Mamba's selective state space model (Gu & Dao, 2024), applying it as a single-layer feature extractor for time series classification with four targeted architectural modifications (scaled input projection, time-variance modularization, skip-connection removal, and adaptive pooling). It departs from TSCMamba (Ahamed & Cheng, 2025), the only prior Mamba-based TSC work, by avoiding external feature engineering (ROCKET, CWT) to isolate Mamba's intrinsic contribution, and from the broader TSC landscape where CNN and Transformer backbones dominate. The paper also positions itself as a methodological correction, re-evaluating 20 baselines across all 30 UEA datasets under a unified protocol to address coverage, fairness, and reproducibility gaps in prior benchmarking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a carefully configured single-layer Mamba can serve as a competitive TSC backbone, challenging the prior perception (from Wang et al., 2024b) that Mamba is the weakest TSC architecture. The finding that time-invariant (LTI) parameterizations often outperform the fully time-variant selective SSM for TSC—contradicting Mamba's original language-modeling ablation—suggests that the 'selectivity' mechanism may be less critical for classification than for generation tasks. Limitations include reliance on Mamba v1 (Mamba-2's parallel block would require different low-level optimizations), the fact that MambaSL rarely dominates individual datasets, and the need for domain-specific kernel-size constraints in practice. Open questions include whether the time-variance modularization transfers to Mamba-2 and whether deeper Mamba stacks could eventually outperform the single-layer configuration on more complex datasets.
