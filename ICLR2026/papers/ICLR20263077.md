## Problem

Polynomial activations have long been considered unsuitable for deep networks due to vanishing/exploding gradients and the Universal Approximation Theorem's requirement for non-polynomial activations. Prior learnable-activation work (e.g., rational activations in Yang & Wang, 2025) lacked closed-form second-moment expressions, forcing reliance on fitting to classical activations. This paper asks whether orthogonal-basis activations can be trained stably at scale without any clamping, normalization, or auxiliary regularizing functions.

## Input / Output

The method is applied to two standard tasks: image classification with ConvNeXt-T (32×32 CIFAR-10, 224×224 ImageNet-1k) producing 1000-way class labels, and next-token prediction with GPT-2 (124M) on OpenWebText with 1024-token context windows. The unusual aspect is that the activation function itself is parameterized by learnable coefficients (polynomial degrees 3–6, or tropical degree 6) that are trained jointly with all network weights, adding only ~72 parameters to a 28M-parameter model. For fine-tuning, a pre-trained model's classical activation (e.g., GELU) is replaced by a Hermite-interpolated learnable activation matching both function values and derivatives.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces fixed activations (GELU, ReLU) with learnable expansions in orthonormal bases: probabilist Hermite polynomials (for normally distributed inputs), truncated Fourier sine-cosine series (for uniform inputs), and max-tropical polynomials. The key insight is that orthonormality collapses the second-order moment integral to a simple sum of squared coefficients, yielding closed-form forward and backward gains and hence a variance-preserving weight initialization (Theorems 3.8, 3.13, 3.19) that equalizes signal and gradient variance across layers without any clamping. The paper further proves that any L-layer polynomially activated MLP is a single multivariate polynomial mapping of degree at most d^L, and that tropical polynomial activations encode the discrete convex conjugate (Legendre-Fenchel transform) of a learnable function. Efficient CUDA kernels exploit the Hermite recurrence He_{n+1} = x·He_n − n·He_{n−1} to reduce FLOP and memory from O(d²) to O(d), and the Fourier variant uses an amplitude-phase form with learnable frequencies for reduced compute.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k with ConvNeXt-T, Hermite (degree 3) achieves 82.22% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the GELU baseline (82.06%) with p=0.0062, while Tropical (degree 6) reaches 82.17% (p=0.0345). On OpenWebText with GPT-2, Hermite achieves the best validation perplexity of 18.821 (loss 2.932), beating GELU (19.319, loss 2.961) with p=0.0067. On CIFAR-10, all three proposed activations significantly outperform GELU, with Hermite reaching 91.35% top-1 accuracy versus 90.47% for GELU (p<0.0001).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline throughout is GELU (the default activation in both ConvNeXt-T and GPT-2), with SiLU additionally compared in the GPT-2 language modeling experiment. On ImageNet-1k, Hermite (82.22%) and Tropical (82.17%) both beat GELU (82.06%) on top-1 accuracy, while Fourier underperforms (81.64%, p=0.0005). On OpenWebText, all three proposed activations beat both GELU and SiLU on validation loss: Hermite achieves 2.932 (p=0.0067 vs GELU), Tropical 2.946 (p=0.0001), and Fourier 2.941 (p=0.0014), compared to GELU at 2.961 and SiLU at 2.979. On CIFAR-10, all three activations significantly outperform GELU (90.47%), with Hermite reaching 91.35% (p<0.0001), Fourier 91.23% (p=0.0023), and Tropical 90.87% (p=0.0002). The FLOP overhead is minimal: Hermite adds only 1 FLOP per activation element (13 total vs 12 for GELU), while Tropical adds 7 (19 total) and Fourier adds 31 (43 total).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the variance-preserving initialization framework of He et al. (2015) from ReLU to a family of learnable activations built on orthogonal bases (Hermite polynomials, Fourier trigonometric series) and tropical polynomials. It directly addresses a limitation identified in Yang & Wang's (2025) Kolmogorov-Arnold Transformer, where rational activations lacked a closed-form second moment; here, orthogonality of the basis yields a simple closed-form expression for both forward and backward gains. The work also departs from the long-standing consensus (Pinkus 1999; Leshno et al. 1993) that polynomial activations are fundamentally unsuitable for deep networks, and from prior polynomial-activation studies (Chrysos et al. 2020; Zhou et al. 2019) that were limited to shallow architectures or small datasets like MNIST.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that Hermite, Fourier, and Tropical activations match or modestly exceed GELU on ImageNet-1k and OpenWebText without any clamping, normalization, or auxiliary ReLU, the paper challenges a near-universal assumption in deep learning and opens a path toward interpreting deep networks as multivariate polynomial mappings with connections to algebraic geometry (neuromanifolds, neurovarieties). Key limitations temper the impact: FLOP overhead grows linearly with depth (making these activations less suitable for very deep MLPs), the performance margins over GELU are small (≈0.2 pp Top-1 on ImageNet, ≈0.4 PPL on OpenWebText), and the tropical activation, while FLOP-light, is inherently convex and cannot fit non-convex targets. Open directions include wavelet-based activations (Hermitian wavelets, Morlet), complex-valued Fourier networks, and extending the identifiability results from algebraic geometry to practical training dynamics.
