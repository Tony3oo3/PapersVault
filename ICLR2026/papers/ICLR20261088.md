## Problem

In RLVR pipelines using group-based algorithms like GRPO and DAPO, token-level stochastic sampling produces highly homogeneous trajectories within a rollout group, causing relative advantage signals to diminish and policy updates to become uninformative. Existing mitigations—raising sampling temperature or post-hoc group filtering—address diversity only indirectly and yield modest gains under a fixed generation budget, leaving trajectory-level divergence as an unsolved bottleneck.

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt (a reasoning problem such as a Countdown arithmetic puzzle or a math word problem), LATR produces exactly k diverse reasoning trajectories (default k=8) that are structurally distinct in their reasoning paths rather than merely differing in surface tokens. During generation, the method maintains a dynamic tree of partial sequences with a maximum completion length of 1,024 tokens (Countdown) or 8,192 tokens (math), and pads surviving branches to meet the rollout budget. At inference time, the trained model produces a single trajectory via standard decoding, so the diversity is a training-time property only.

## Method

LATR replaces independent token-level sampling with a structured tree-based rollout that iteratively performs three stages: (1) branching at high-uncertainty positions where candidate tokens exceed both an absolute probability threshold (τ_abs=0.25) and a relative gap threshold (τ_rel=0.15) from the top token; (2) lookahead simulation extending each new branch for r tokens (r∈{20,30,50}); and (3) pruning branches whose normalized edit distance from their parent's corresponding segment falls below τ_ed=0.4, removing them along with all descendants. Once the tree width reaches k, generation switches to standard stochastic sampling (early stopping). To avoid train-test mismatch, a hybrid rollout strategy allocates a fraction η of rollouts to LATR and the rest to stochastic sampling, annealing η exponentially (η=η₀·γⁱ) so that later training increasingly mimics single-trajectory inference. The entire procedure is backtracking-free with O(nk) forward passes and is plug-and-play compatible with any policy update algorithm.

## Results

LATR consistently improves Pass@1 accuracy across all five benchmarks under both GRPO and DAPO, with average gains of +4.2% over Stochastic Sampling. The largest improvements appear on AMC-2023 (+7.5% with DAPO, from 37.8% to 45.3%) and DAPO-Math (+5.7%, from 26.8% to 32.5%). LATR also reduces average completion length by 22% on Countdown and ~8% on math tasks, while accelerating training convergence by 2–3× (e.g., DAPO+LATR reaches peak Countdown accuracy at step 150 vs. step 450 for Stochastic Sampling).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard token-level Stochastic Sampling under both GRPO and DAPO. LATR outperforms Stochastic Sampling on every benchmark and policy combination: e.g., DAPO+LATR achieves 32.5% Pass@1 on DAPO-Math vs. 26.8% for DAPO+Stochastic (+5.7%), and 45.3% on AMC-2023 vs. 37.8% (+7.5%). In the appendix (Table 7), LATR is also compared against two alternative rollout strategies: Rollout Down-sampling (RDS, Xu et al. 2025) and Entropy-Guided Tree Search (EPTree, Hou et al. 2025). On Countdown, DAPO+LATR (74.7% Pass@1) beats DAPO+RDS (68.5%) by 6.2 points and DAPO+EPTree (66.3%) by 8.4 points; under GRPO, LATR (70.9%) similarly surpasses RDS (68.7%) and EPTree (65.3%). Notably, DAPO+RDS exhibits training instability with KL divergence spikes, while EPTree's gains in TreeRL are attributed to its policy-update mechanism rather than its rollout strategy, leaving LATR as the strongest rollout-only improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

LATR repurposes the tree-search paradigm (inspired by MCTS) for a purpose distinct from prior tree-based RLVR methods: rather than propagating sparse rewards backward through a reasoning tree (TreeRL, TreePO) or enabling inference-time deliberation (Tree-of-Thoughts, MCTS-DPO), it uses branching and lookahead simulation explicitly to enforce trajectory-level diversity during the rollout phase of policy training. It departs from prior rollout-diversity strategies—DAPO's post-hoc group filtering, ProRL's temperature scaling, and RDS's down-sampling—by addressing diversity at generation time through structured branching at high-uncertainty tokens, rather than through post-hoc selection or token-level stochasticity. The method is designed as a drop-in replacement for the rollout stage, remaining orthogonal to the policy update algorithm (GRPO, DAPO, or future variants).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that trajectory-level rollout diversity is the primary bottleneck in RLVR—more impactful than upgrading from GRPO to DAPO—reframes where practitioners should invest methodological effort. The 131% training acceleration and consistent 4–5% pass@1 gains across multiple benchmarks, combined with reduced inference length, make LATR a practically attractive plug-in for existing RLVR pipelines. However, all experiments use Qwen models at 1.7B–7B scale on Countdown and math tasks; whether the benefits persist at larger scales, for non-verifiable-reward tasks, or for longer-horizon reasoning remains open. The hybrid rollout annealing (decaying LATR fraction) introduces a practical hyperparameter that may need careful tuning per task.
