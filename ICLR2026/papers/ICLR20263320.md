## Problem

Multi-objective preference alignment (MPA) for LLMs suffers from an inherent parameter-level conflict: gradient updates for different preferences (helpfulness, harmlessness, truthfulness) are non-orthogonal and interfere with each other, causing improvements in one dimension to degrade others. Existing approaches—constraint-based losses, data mixing, and model merging—mitigate conflicts at the loss or data level but fail to resolve the underlying parameter antagonism, leaving the model's internal matrix updates unstable and prone to distributional drift.

## Input / Output

The method takes a base SFT model with a LoRA adapter (rank r ≪ min(m,n)) from a first preference alignment phase (e.g., safety), a set of additional preference datasets, and a tolerance hyperparameter γ that bounds permissible reward degradation on the first preference. It produces aligned LoRA weights where each successive preference's update is confined to a data-adaptively selected orthogonal subspace, along with a projection matrix P = ŨŨᵀ that enforces non-interference. The adaptive rank selection runs a binary search over candidate subspace dimensions (typically 8 to r_max), rescaling trailing singular values to the mean of the top-r values and checking whether the positive reward on the first-preference data stays within γ of the baseline.

## Method

OrthAlign decomposes the first preference's LoRA matrix ΔW = BA via SVD into preference-critical top-r singular directions and a trailing subspace with minimal impact on the first preference. It then performs an adaptive subspace-rank selection (Algorithm 1): a binary search over candidate rank k rescales the last k singular values to the mean of the top-r ones, reconstructs a candidate matrix, and accepts the largest k whose induced reward shift on the first-preference data remains within tolerance γ. New preference gradients are then projected onto the selected orthogonal subspace via P = ŨŨᵀ, ensuring ΔW_new = P · ∇_W L_new(W) is strictly confined to directions that do not interfere with prior preferences. Theoretically, the paper proves that under simultaneous orthogonal subspace and spectral norm (‖ΔW‖₂ ≤ τ) constraints, per-layer Lipschitz constants grow at most linearly in the number of updates (‖W + ΣΔW_t‖₂ ≤ ‖W‖₂ + Tτ), and that orthogonal allocation eliminates cross-term interference so that ‖ΣΔθ_t‖² = Σ‖Δθ_t‖², preventing the super-linear or exponential spectral norm inflation that plagues unconstrained multi-objective updates.

## Results

On three-objective sequential alignment (harmless → helpful → truthful) with LLaMA-3-SFT and UltraFeedback, OrthAlign achieves an average score of 75.95%, surpassing the best baseline (RSDPO at 72.12%) by 3.83 points and the DPO baseline (63.55%) by 12.40 points. Across all configurations, OrthAlign delivers single-preference improvements of 34.61%–50.89% after multi-preference alignment, with an average overall reward improvement of 13.96% over the strongest baselines. On Mistral-7B-SFT with HelpSteer2, OrthAlign reaches 91.34% Harmless Rate and 73.51% Average Score, outperforming the next-best method (SPO at 68.49% average) by 5.02 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OrthAlign dominates on Harmless Rate and Truthful MC2 across all baselines: it achieves 87.30% Harmless Rate vs. RSDPO (80.57%), Knots (82.30%), and SPO (58.42%), and 75.15% Truthful MC2 vs. DPO (67.03%) and RSDPO (63.87%). However, OrthAlign trails RSDPO (71.92%) and MODPO (71.42%) on Helpful Win Rate (67.69%), reflecting a modest trade-off where the orthogonal subspace constraint slightly constrains helpfulness gains. On the composite Average Score, OrthAlign wins against every baseline by a wide margin (75.95% vs. 72.12% for RSDPO, 69.27% for Knots, and 64.57% for SPO). As a plug-and-play module, OrthAlign boosts DPO's Harmless Rate from 71.24% to 93.84% (+22.60) and MODPO's from 48.46% to 79.32% (+30.86) in two-objective settings, confirming its generality across constraint-based and data-synthesis baselines. Model-merging methods (Soups, TSV-M, Knots) consistently underperform OrthAlign on Average Score by 6–16 points, validating the paper's claim that compromise-based merging inherently degrades individual objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

OrthAlign extends the interference-free orthogonal subspace concept from continual learning (InFLORA, Liang & Li 2024) into the multi-preference alignment setting, where it is applied to LoRA parameter updates during sequential DPO training. Unlike prior MPA methods that address conflicts at the loss level (MODPO, SPO), data level (RSDPO), or through post-hoc model merging (Soups, Knots, TSV-M), OrthAlign operates directly in parameter space via SVD-based orthogonal projection, ensuring that gradient updates for new preferences are confined to subspaces orthogonal to previously aligned preferences. The paper also introduces an adaptive rank-selection mechanism (binary search over singular value rescaling) that goes beyond the fixed-rank orthogonal constraints used in InFLORA, and provides Lipschitz stability guarantees that prior MPA work lacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of OrthAlign—boosting harmlessness by 25% and helpfulness by ~5% when added to existing DPO/MODPO/SPO pipelines—makes it a practical drop-in module for the MPA toolkit without requiring re-derivation of loss functions or data curation. The theoretical guarantee that orthogonal subspace allocation yields linear (rather than exponential) Lipschitz growth provides a principled stability argument that is absent from constraint-based or merging-based MPA methods. Key limitations include validation on only three objectives and text-only LoRA-based models; open questions include scalability to many more objectives, extension to full fine-tuning or multimodal settings, and whether the adaptive rank selection generalizes beyond the binary-search heuristic used here.
