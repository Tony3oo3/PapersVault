## Problem

Most causal representation learning methods focus on recovering latent state variables, leaving the representation of interventions (actions) themselves underexplored. Without object-invariant, sparse representations of actions, models cannot generalize to unseen object-action combinations or entirely new object classes under distribution shift. This paper asks whether explicitly modeling interventions as structured delta vectors in latent space can yield robust OOD action recognition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263851/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of 224×224 RGB images (pre- and post-intervention states) from the Causal Triplet benchmark, where the two images are 'actionable counterfactuals'—meaning exogenous noise (lighting, camera pose) may differ between them, unlike perfect counterfactuals. The encoder processes each image independently, and the element-wise difference of their latent codes forms the intervention representation that is classified into one of the action labels. No image augmentations are applied to preserve the interventional structure of the pairs.

## Method

The core idea is the Causal Delta Embedding (CDE): an intervention is represented as δ_a = φ(x̃) − φ(x), the vector difference between encoder outputs of post- and pre-intervention images, which by construction is independent of scene-level features and should be sparse under the Sparse Mechanism Shift assumption. To enforce the additional invariance property (same action representation regardless of which object is affected), the authors train with a three-component loss: cross-entropy for action classification, supervised contrastive loss to cluster same-action deltas across different objects, and an L1 sparsity penalty to keep most dimensions near zero. The encoder is a pre-trained ViT (DINO, MAE, or CLIP) followed by a small MLP causal projector, trained end-to-end. For multi-object and real-world scenes, a patch-wise variant computes per-patch deltas and applies the loss only to the top-k patches with the largest L2 norm, localizing the intervention to the affected region.

## Results

On the single-object ProcTHOR benchmark, CDE (ViT-DINO) achieves 91% OOD compositional accuracy and 75% OOD systematic accuracy, reducing the generalization gap from 0.42 (best baseline SMS-R) to 0.21 while matching IID accuracy at 96%. On multi-object ProcTHOR, the Patch-Wise CDE model (ViT-CLIP) reaches 48% OOD accuracy, surpassing the oracle-mask baseline (42%) that uses ground-truth segmentation. On real-world Epic-Kitchens, CDE (ViT-CLIP) achieves 34% OOD accuracy versus 27% for the best GroupViT-based baseline, with a 20% generalization gap compared to 25% for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Causal Triplet baselines (Liu et al., 2023), CDE achieves a dramatic improvement: on single-object systematic shift, CDE (ViT-DINO) reaches 75% OOD accuracy versus 54% for the best baseline (SMS-R), a 21-point gain; on compositional shift the gap widens to 91% vs. 47%. Against Slot Attention (Locatello et al., 2020) on multi-object ProcTHOR, CDE (ViT-CLIP) achieves 48% OOD accuracy, more than doubling the best Slot variant (Slot-match at 21%) and even outperforming the oracle-mask ResNet (42%) that receives ground-truth object segmentation. Against GroupViT (Xu et al., 2022) on real-world Epic-Kitchens, CDE (ViT-CLIP) reaches 34% OOD accuracy versus 27% for Group-token, the strongest GroupViT variant, while also achieving the best IID accuracy (59% vs. 52%). Across all three comparison families, CDE consistently wins on OOD metrics while matching or exceeding IID accuracy, with the largest margins on synthetic single-object data and more modest but still clear gains on real-world scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Causal Triplet benchmark and SCM framework of Liu et al. (2023), but departs from the dominant CRL paradigm of recovering latent state variables by instead learning representations of the interventions themselves. The key conceptual shift is representing an action as the vector difference (delta) between pre- and post-intervention latent encodings, constrained by the ICM and SMS assumptions to be independent, sparse, and object-invariant. Methodologically, it repurposes supervised contrastive learning and L1 sparsity regularization—both well-studied in disentanglement and representation learning—toward structuring the intervention space rather than the observation space, and extends this to a patch-wise variant for multi-object scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CDE framework provides a simple, interpretable mechanism for OOD-robust action prediction that outperforms prior CRL baselines by large margins on synthetic benchmarks, and the unsupervised discovery of anti-parallel action pairs (open/close, on/off) suggests the learned space captures genuine causal semantics. However, real-world performance remains modest (34% OOD on Epic-Kitchens), and the assumption of a single universal delta per action limits expressiveness for context-dependent transformations. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video/temporal interventions, multi-step action sequences, and whether the object-invariance constraint can be relaxed to capture object-specific effects without sacrificing generalization.
