## Problem

Existing adversarial prompt tuning methods for VLMs (e.g., APT, FAP) focus on learning robust prompts but rely on continuous image features that remain vulnerable to adversarial perturbations. No prior work has explored the feature representation itself—specifically, whether discretizing latent image features can inherently ablate adversarial perturbations—leaving a gap in how the visual encoding pipeline contributes to robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260016/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images processed by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 encoder, which outputs a 512-dimensional class feature and N×512 grid (patch) features, alongside a learnable text prompt concatenated with the class name fed to the frozen text encoder. The VQ-VAE inside PerturbShield operates on the 512-dim grid features, projecting them into a 256-dim latent space with a 512-entry codebook. The final output is a fused classification probability that averages softmax logits computed from both the original class feature and the discretized-reconstructed feature against the learnable prompt embeddings.

## Method

DEFEAT introduces a PerturbShield module that applies VQ-VAE to the ViT's grid (patch-level) features rather than the single class feature, because a single code lacks the representational capacity to encode an entire image. The discretization causes clean and adversarially perturbed features to quantize to the same codebook entries, effectively neutralizing small perturbations. A learnable projection matrix W aggregates the reconstructed grid features into a robust class-level representation, regularized via an L1 alignment loss to stay within CLIP's pre-trained embedding space. A logits fusion strategy averages the softmax probabilities from the robust (discretized) branch and the original (continuous) branch, trading a small clean-accuracy loss for substantial robustness gains. Training jointly optimizes the learnable prompt, W, and VQ-VAE parameters under PGD-generated adversarial examples, with an additional prompt alignment regularizer that constrains learnable prompts toward hand-crafted prompts, which empirically further boosts robustness.

## Results

Under the adversarial few-shot classification setting (11 datasets, ε=4/255, 16 shots), DEFEAT achieves 50.98% clean accuracy, 36.05% adversarial robustness, and 42.23% harmonic mean (H), surpassing the previous state-of-the-art FAP (50.50% Acc, 19.82% Rob, 28.47% H) by 16.23% in robustness and 13.76% in H. Under the weaker ε=1/255 attack, DEFEAT reaches 65.03% Acc, 60.77% Rob, and 62.83% H at 16 shots, outperforming FAP by 11.91% in robustness and 6.53% in H. In the adversarial domain generalization setting (ε=4/255, 16 shots on ImageNet), DEFEAT achieves 19.06% robustness vs. 12.06% for FAP and 12.02% for APT, with the advantage scaling to 22.71% at 100 shots.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEFEAT is compared against four baselines: zero-shot CLIP, APT (adversarial prompt tuning, Li et al. 2024), FAP (few-shot adversarial prompt learning, Zhou et al. 2024), and FARE (unsupervised adversarial fine-tuning, Schlarmann et al. 2024, used as an alternative backbone in Table A9). Against CLIP, DEFEAT wins decisively on all metrics in every setting; at ε=4/255 with 16 shots, it improves robustness from 10.79% to 36.05% and H from 16.34% to 42.23%. Against APT, DEFEAT matches or slightly trails on clean accuracy (50.98% vs. 51.08%) but substantially outperforms on robustness (36.05% vs. 20.26%) and H (42.23% vs. 29.01%) at ε=4/255, 16 shots. Against FAP (the strongest prior method), DEFEAT achieves comparable accuracy (50.98% vs. 50.50%) while gaining 16.23% in robustness and 13.76% in H at ε=4/255, 16 shots. When paired with the stronger FARE backbone (Table A9), DEFEAT's advantage over FAP widens further, reaching 52.46% average robustness vs. 30.53% at ε=4/255.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEFEAT builds on the CoOp prompt tuning framework and the adversarial prompt training paradigm established by APT (Li et al., 2024) and FAP (Zhou et al., 2024), which combine learnable prompts with PGD-based adversarial training to improve VLM robustness in a parameter-efficient manner. The key departure is shifting focus from learning robust prompts to modifying the feature representation itself: DEFEAT introduces a PerturbShield module that uses VQ-VAE (van den Oord et al., 2017) to discretize ViT grid features, arguing that quantization naturally ablates small adversarial perturbations by mapping clean and adversarial features to the same codebook entry. A logits fusion strategy then balances the robustness gained from the discretized branch with the clean accuracy preserved by the original continuous branch, while prompt alignment regularization (inspired by CoCoOp-style hand-crafted prompt constraints) further stabilizes the learnable prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that latent feature discretization reduces the distribution shift between clean and adversarial examples offers a conceptually distinct defense mechanism from prior adversarial prompt tuning, which relies solely on prompt optimization. The method's strong generalization to unseen perturbation budgets (23.32% vs. 5.81% robustness at ε=8/255 vs. FAP) and its performance under adaptive attacks suggest the discretization provides a more fundamental robustness rather than overfitting to a specific attack strength. However, the adaptive attack analysis reveals that the continuous branch remains the primary vulnerability (robustness drops to 18.20% when the attack targets it exclusively), and the method is currently validated only on CLIP ViT-B/32 with a TeCoA or FARE pre-trained encoder, leaving open whether the approach transfers to larger or architecturally different VLMs. The information loss inherent in VQ-VAE reconstruction (evidenced by PerturbShield alone degrading DEFEAT's H from 36.31% to 19.56%) also suggests a fundamental trade-off that may limit further gains.
