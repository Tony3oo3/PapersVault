## Problem

Existing multi-prompt Visual In-Context Learning (VICL) methods, particularly CONDENSER, rely on patch-wise fusion that substantially underuses spatially informative cues and employ model-agnostic supervision that fails to ensure the backbone actually trusts and relies on the fused prompt. This leads to the backbone falling back on its own capacity rather than leveraging the integrated multi-prompt representation, limiting performance gains from multi-prompting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260024/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 query RGB image and N retrieved prompt image-label pairs (each sub-image 112×112) from a pixel-level retriever, arranged in a 2×2 canvas (prompt image, prompt label, query image, mask). Fusion operates in the MAE patch embedding space at resolution (H/16)×(W/16)×D. The output is a task-specific prediction—segmentation mask, bounding box, or colorized image—generated by reconstructing the masked region through the frozen VQGAN decoder from predicted codebook tokens.

## Method

PromptHub introduces a locality-aware cross-attention fusion module that applies spatially decaying weights (Gaussian or Laplacian prior with hyperparameter σ) radiating from each query patch position, balancing local focus against global receptive field while suppressing border noise. The fused prompt features are then supervised by three complementary objectives: a label prediction loss (cross-entropy on VQGAN codebook tokens) preserving VICL's contextual prediction behavior, a semantic integrity loss aligning fused prompt tokens with the query pair's discrete tokens, and a cosine-similarity utilization loss reducing discrepancy between fused prompt and query features to encourage backbone trust. A VICL-specific data augmentation strategy randomly substitutes retrieved prompts with the query pair (reducing discrepancy) or random pairs (injecting noise) to reinforce the regularization objectives. The entire module is a lightweight plug-in on top of a frozen MAE-VQGAN backbone, adding only ~30 MB GPU overhead per prompt pair.

## Results

On Pascal-5i foreground segmentation, PromptHub achieves a mean mIoU of 47.81% with N=16 prompts, surpassing CONDENSER (46.63%) by 2.5% and InMeMo (43.14%) by 4.7%. On Pascal VOC 2012 single-object detection, PromptHub reaches 45.59% mIoU (N=16), outperforming CONDENSER (44.64%) by 2.1% and InMeMo (43.21%) by 2.4%. On ImageNet colorization, PromptHub attains an MSE of 0.503 (N=16), improving over CONDENSER (0.539) by 7.2% and Partial2Global (0.58) by 13.3%. Under single-prompt (N=1) settings, PromptHub still leads all baselines with 45.17% mIoU on segmentation, 44.51% on detection, and 0.533 MSE on colorization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PromptHub consistently outperforms all seven baselines across all three tasks under both single-prompt and multi-prompt settings. Against the closest competitor CONDENSER (Wang et al., 2025), PromptHub gains 2.3% mIoU on segmentation, 3.0% on detection, and 5.1% on colorization at N=1, and 2.5%, 2.1%, and 7.2% respectively at N=16. Compared to InMeMo (Zhang et al., 2024), which uses visual prompt tuning, PromptHub leads by 2.0% mIoU on segmentation and 1.3% on detection at N=1, with the gap widening to 4.7% and 2.4% at N=16. Against retrieval-based methods like Prompt-SelF (Sun et al., 2025) and Partial2Global (Xu et al., 2024), PromptHub achieves substantially larger margins (e.g., +12.0% mIoU over Prompt-SelF on segmentation at N=16). The zero-shot baselines (Random, UnsupPR) and the task-vector method VTV (Hojel et al., 2024) are far behind, confirming that learned prompt fusion with locality-aware design is essential for competitive VICL performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

PromptHub directly extends CONDENSER (Wang et al., 2025), the first method to introduce prompt fusion for multi-prompt VICL, by replacing its patch-wise fusion with a locality-aware cross-attention mechanism governed by Gaussian or Laplacian spatial priors. It also departs from CONDENSER's model-agnostic input-level supervision by introducing a three-objective 'fusion-utilization-prediction' closed loop that explicitly addresses the backbone's tendency to distrust fused representations. Within the broader VICL landscape built on MAE-VQGAN, PromptHub is orthogonal to retrieval improvements (Prompt-SelF, UnsupPR, SupPR) and prompt tuning (InMeMo), positioning itself as a plug-in fusion module that can be combined with any retriever.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes locality-aware fusion as a principled alternative to patch-wise aggregation for multi-prompt VICL, with the spatial prior σ providing an interpretable knob that interpolates between local and global fusion. The explicit utilization loss addressing the 'backbone distrust' problem is a useful diagnostic for any prompt-fusion pipeline. Key limitations include the white-box dependency on backbone gradients (ruling out closed-source models), the restriction to pixel-space visual tasks, and the finding that query-adaptive σ yields only marginal gains, suggesting more sophisticated spatially varying priors are needed. Extending the framework to multi-modal or black-box settings remains an open direction.
