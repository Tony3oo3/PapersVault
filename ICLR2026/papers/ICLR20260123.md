## Problem

Existing causal discovery methods with latent confounders (GIN, TIN) rely on one-sided projections ω^T Y ⊥ Z and require purity assumptions that fail in impure structures where observed variables share multiple latent influences. This limitation means certain causal graphs (e.g., Fig. 1a vs. 1b) are indistinguishable, while the few methods that can resolve them (OICA, higher-order cumulants) are computationally prohibitive or statistically unreliable. The paper asks whether a tractable principle can achieve stronger identifiability without these structural or computational costs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260123/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pure observational data from a linear non-Gaussian acyclic model where observed variables do not cause latent ones, with at most one Gaussian noise among the exogenous terms. It requires each latent variable to have at least three neighbors and two children (Condition 1), and for the general p-order case, at least 2p+2 observed variables per cluster (relaxable via higher-order cumulants). The output is the fully identified causal graph G including latent variable locations, all causal edges (both latent-to-observed and observed-to-observed), and their edge coefficients, recovered up to no equivalence class under Condition 2.

## Method

CICA seeks an invertible transform W such that WX has mutually conditionally independent coordinates given p latent variables, generalizing ICA (p=0) by introducing a latent conditional set. The key insight is that CICA solutions provably induce two-sided projections ω1^T Y ⊥ ω2^T Z, which carry strictly richer identification signal than the one-sided projections used by GIN/TIN. Optimization is achieved via tractable rank-deficiency constraints (zero determinants of (p+1)×(p+1) sub-covariance matrices for disjoint coordinate subsets) or triad constraints for the 1-order case, avoiding OICA's EM procedure and higher-order cumulant estimation. The identifiability theory shows that I − B_{X,X} is the sparsest p_min(X)-order CICA solution, and under Condition 2 this sparsest solution is unique up to permutation and scaling, which acyclicity then resolves. The CICA-LiNGAM algorithm recursively identifies causal clusters via GIN, solves CICA per cluster, selects the sparsest solution, applies permutation for acyclicity, merges clusters sharing latent parents, and updates the active variable set using surrogate selection (Lemma 10).

## Results

On synthetic data with 4 graph structures (Cases 1–4) and sample sizes N ∈ {5k, 10k, 20k}, CICA-LiNGAM achieves 0.00 error in latent variables across all cases at 20k samples, and F1-scores of 0.77, 0.72, 0.78, and 0.74 for Cases 1–4 respectively. In the fully impure scenarios (Cases 2 and 4), all four baselines fail completely (F1 = 0.00), while CICA-LiNGAM still recovers the causal structure. A sparsity-gap simulation over all 32,768 graphs with 6 observed variables confirms that the true causal matrix I − B_{X,X} is on average 16.12% sparser than the second-sparsest CICA solution, providing a robust identifiability margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CICA-LiNGAM outperforms all four baselines across all four synthetic cases. CDHS (Li et al., 2025) fails entirely in fully impure settings (Cases 2 and 4) because its 'Homologous Surrogates' condition is violated, and also fails in Case 3 (F1 = 0.00). LaHME (Xie et al., 2024) and PO-LiNGAM (Jin et al., 2023) are relatively stable in partially impure scenarios but both collapse to F1 = 0.00 in the fully impure Cases 2 and 4 due to clustering-step failure. RLCD (Dong et al., 2023) is inapplicable to Cases 1 and 2 (its rank test requires ≥ 4 observed variables) and achieves only F1 = 0.73 in Case 3 and 0.23 in Case 4. In the most challenging fully impure Case 4, CICA-LiNGAM achieves F1 = 0.74 versus 0.67 for LaHME (the best baseline), a 10.4% absolute improvement, while all other baselines score below 0.23.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes ICA (Comon, 1994) to a conditional setting (CICA) where components are independent given latent variables, and extends the one-sided projection framework of GIN (Xie et al., 2020) and TIN (Dai et al., 2022) to two-sided projections ω₁ᵀY ⊥ ω₂ᵀZ, which the authors show is strictly more informative. For optimization, it repurposes the Triad constraint (Cai et al., 2019) and rank-deficiency constraints (Huang et al., 2022; Dong et al., 2023) as tractable proxies, avoiding the computationally prohibitive EM-based OICA (Eriksson & Koivunen, 2004). The acyclicity-based resolution of permutation/scaling indeterminacy is inherited from LiNGAM (Shimizu et al., 2006), while the paper explicitly shows that Independent Subspace Analysis (Theis, 2006) is uninformative in latent settings, carving out CICA's distinct role.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CICA removes the purity assumption that has been a persistent bottleneck in GIN/TIN-based latent causal discovery, enabling identification in fully impure structures where prior methods provably fail. The key open question is whether the linear non-Gaussian assumption can be relaxed to non-linear settings (the authors point to tensor-rank techniques as a potential path), and whether Condition 1 (each latent having ≥3 neighbors and ≥2 children) can be weakened. For practitioners, the sparsity-gap simulation (Table 2) provides reassuring evidence that the sparsest-solution criterion is robust in practice, but the method remains limited to linear models and requires the rank-faithfulness assumption.
