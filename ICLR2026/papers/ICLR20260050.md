## Problem

For convex smooth objectives under heavy-tailed noise (finite α-th moments, α∈(1,2]), all existing convergence guarantees for Clipped-SGD analyze only the averaged iterate, leaving the practically more relevant last iterate without any high-probability convergence guarantee. This paper closes that gap by establishing the first high-probability last-iterate rate for Clipped-SGD in this setting.

## Input / Output

The method requires a convex L-smooth objective f: R^d → R and an unbiased stochastic gradient oracle whose noise has bounded α-th central moment (α∈(1,2]). It produces a high-probability bound on the last-iterate sub-optimality f(x_K) − f* with only polylogarithmic dependence on the confidence parameter δ, and the step-size/clipping schedules are horizon-agnostic (any-time), requiring no prior knowledge of the total number of iterations K. The analysis also yields an in-expectation guarantee via a general conversion technique for methods with almost surely bounded updates.

## Method

The proof is built on a potential function Φ_k = d_k(f(x_k)−f*) + L‖x_k−x*‖² with d_{k+1}=d_k+2γ_kL, adapted from Taylor & Bach (2019) but with a descent lemma tailored for high-probability control via Bernstein/Freedman inequalities. A key technical novelty is the clipping schedule λ_k ∝ 1/(√d_k · γ_k) rather than the standard 1/γ_k scaling, which exploits the inductive bound ‖∇f(x_k)‖ = O(1/√d_k) to more effectively balance clipping bias and variance under the α-moment assumption. The step-size and clipping levels are horizon-free, using a time-varying failure budget δ_k ∝ 1/k² and a union bound over all steps, which introduces only polylogarithmic factors in 1/δ. The resulting rate is Õ(LR_0²/K + D/K^{2(α−1)/3α}) with probability at least 1−2δ. Additionally, the paper develops a general technique for converting high-probability guarantees into in-expectation rates for any method with almost surely polynomially bounded updates, demonstrated on Clipped-SGD.

## Results

The paper establishes the first high-probability last-iterate convergence guarantee for Clipped-SGD on convex L-smooth objectives under heavy-tailed noise with finite α-th moments (α∈(1,2]). The achieved rate is Õ(LR₀²/K + D/K^{2(α-1)/3α}) with failure probability at most δ, where D depends on R₀, σ, L, and α. At α=2 (finite variance), this yields Õ(1/K^{2/3}), improving upon the prior best in-expectation last-iterate rate of O(1/K^{1/3}) (Taylor & Bach, 2019) but leaving a polynomial gap from the optimal Õ(1/√K) benchmark. The parameter schedules are horizon-agnostic (any-time), requiring no prior knowledge of K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Taylor & Bach (2019), which provides an in-expectation last-iterate rate of O(1/K^{1/3}) for α=2, this paper achieves a strictly better high-probability last-iterate rate of Õ(1/K^{2/3}) at α=2, while also extending to the full heavy-tailed regime α∈(1,2]. Against Nguyen et al. (2023), which gives a high-probability *average*-iterate rate of Õ(1/K^{(α-1)/α}) under the same Assumption 4, this paper provides the first high-probability *last*-iterate guarantee, though with a slower exponent (2(α-1)/3α vs. (α-1)/α). Compared to Liu & Zhou (2024), which achieves high-probability last-iterate convergence under the stronger sub-Weibull tail assumption, this paper works under the weaker bounded α-th moment condition (α∈(1,2]), at the cost of a polynomial gap at α=2 (K^{-2/3} vs. K^{-1/2}). Relative to Ghadimi & Lan (2013b), which only covers sub-Gaussian noise for high-probability average-iterate bounds, this paper handles heavy-tailed noise and targets the practically more relevant last iterate. Empirically, the last iterate consistently outperforms the average iterate across all three tested objectives (logistic-type, quadratic, and statistical learning with Student-t features), supporting the theoretical advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the potential-function analysis of Taylor & Bach (2019) from in-expectation to high-probability last-iterate convergence, adapting their descent lemma with Bernstein/Freedman martingale concentration. It builds on the high-probability Clipped-SGD framework of Sadiev et al. (2023) and Nguyen et al. (2023), which established average-iterate guarantees under heavy-tailed noise, and on Zhang et al. (2020b), who introduced the bounded α-th moment model and showed plain SGD fails under it. The key departure is a novel clipping schedule λ_k ∝ 1/(√d_k · γ_k) (rather than the standard 1/γ_k) that exploits the inductive bound on gradient norm to sharpen the bias-variance tradeoff, combined with horizon-agnostic parameter choices that avoid requiring knowledge of K.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This closes a notable gap: prior high-probability results for Clipped-SGD under heavy-tailed noise covered only average iterates, while last-iterate guarantees existed only under sub-Gaussian or sub-Weibull tails. The general technique for converting high-probability bounds to in-expectation rates for any method with almost surely bounded updates (applicable to SignSGD, Muon, normalized SGD) is a reusable analytical tool. However, the rate at α=2 is Õ(1/K^{1/3}), leaving a polynomial gap from the optimal Õ(1/√K), and the in-expectation corollary requires horizon-dependent parameters. Open questions include closing this gap, extending to (L₀,L₁)-smooth objectives, and achieving horizon-agnostic in-expectation guarantees.
