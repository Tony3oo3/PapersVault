## Problem

Existing mathematical problem synthesis methods face an expressiveness-validity-complexity trilemma: LLM-only methods lack verifiability, domain-specific methods sacrifice breadth, and formal-based methods (autoformalizer-based or conjecture-prover) are bounded by the capabilities of external autoformalizers and provers, limiting the complexity of generated problems. No prior framework provides whole-process verifiability while simultaneously generating broadly expressive, provable, and genuinely challenging statements without relying on external models for proof.

## Input / Output

The DExplorer agent operates within a Lean 4 / Mathlib environment, conditioned on a problem type and source (e.g., 'Olympiad problem' from 'forums about number theory'). Starting from an empty context, it generates at most 80 exploration steps per episode. The output is a formal Lean 4 statement Γ ⊢ H with a proof reconstructed directly from the exploration trajectory (no external prover needed), which can also be informalized into natural-language problem-solving or proof questions via a 5-shot DeepSeek-V3.1 pipeline.

## Method

DEXploration formulates problem synthesis as a deterministic MDP over Lean 4 proof states, supporting three atomic actions: Introduce (add a variable or hypothesis via an `obtain` tactic with an explosion check), Deduce (derive a new fact using deductive tactics like `have` or `apply at` that modify only the current goal without creating subgoals), and Submit (end the episode, reconstructing the statement as Γ ⊢ H and extracting a proof by encapsulating each deductive step). The key architectural insight is that the proof is extracted from the exploration trajectory itself, eliminating the need for an external prover and guaranteeing provability by construction. To bootstrap training data, Exploratory Transformation rewrites existing statement-proof pairs into deductive style, constructs a dependency DAG over variables and proof steps, and traverses it in topological order (preferring deeper, non-introducing nodes) to distill exploration trajectories. The DExplorer agent is supervised-fine-tuned from Goedel-Prover-V2-8B on 39,509 such trajectories from NuminaMath-Lean, using XTuner with bfloat16, 8192 max sequence length, and cosine-annealed learning rate.

## Results

DExplorer achieves a 54.52% success rate (valid statements / total episodes) with only 8,841 average output tokens per valid statement, compared to 40.70% and >52.9K tokens for ScaleQuest-Math. On the hardest 500 generated problems, DExplorer reaches a top-500 formal complexity of 1374 (proof length) and an informal item difficulty of 0.05 (i.e., only 5% of six open-source LLMs solve them), substantially exceeding all baselines. Notably, 60 of 2726 valid statements (2.20%) remain unprovable by three SOTA provers at Pass@4, and 8 (0.29%) at Pass@64, demonstrating that the framework can generate problems beyond current prover capabilities while guaranteeing provability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ScaleQuest-Math, DExplorer wins on every headline metric: success rate (54.52% vs. 40.70%), token cost (8.8K vs. >52.9K, an 83% reduction), top-500 complexity (1374 vs. 599), top-500 difficulty (0.05 vs. 0.28), and diversity (Rouge-L 0.173 vs. 0.220). Against PromptCoT-QwQ and PromptCoT-DS, DExplorer achieves a far higher success rate (54.52% vs. 20.48% and 9.14%), dramatically lower token cost (8.8K vs. >172.9K and >441.7K), and harder top-500 problems (difficulty 0.05 vs. 0.30 and 0.40); however, it trails on overall average complexity (515 vs. 721 and 786) because DExplorer also generates a large volume of simpler statements. Against MUSTARD, DExplorer produces substantially harder top-500 problems (complexity 1374 vs. 335; difficulty 0.05 vs. 0.16) and more diverse outputs (Rouge-L 0.173 vs. 0.202), though MUSTARD yields more total valid statements (3,791 vs. 2,726) due to its larger seed pool. The Conjecture-Prover ablation (same model, same data, one-shot generation) confirms that the step-by-step exploration architecture—not the dataset—drives the gains: DExplorer improves proven rate from 31.78% to 64.18%, reduces contradictory rate from 36.34% to 9.94%, and cuts token cost from 187K to 8.8K.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant one-shot generation paradigm in formal theorem synthesis (conjecture-then-prove, as in Poesia et al., STP, QDTSynth) by reformulating problem synthesis as a step-by-step Markov Decision Process with three atomic actions (introduce, deduce, submit), where the exploration trajectory itself constitutes the proof. It also contrasts with autoformalizer-based methods (MUSTARD, Leang et al.) that first generate informal problems and then risk-ily formalize them, by grounding every step directly in Lean 4's proof state. The exploratory transformation pipeline—rewriting proofs into deductive style, constructing a dependency DAG, and reassembling via topological sort with depth preference—is a novel data bootstrapping technique that repurposes existing theorem-proving corpora (NuminaMath-Lean) without requiring new human annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By extracting the proof from the exploration trajectory rather than invoking an external prover, DExploration removes the complexity ceiling imposed by prover capability, which is the paper's central architectural insight. The 83% token cost reduction and the generation of 8 statements (Pass@64) that current SOTA provers cannot solve suggest a practical path toward scalable, contamination-free benchmark generation. Limitations include reliance on LLM quality for exploration steps, the fact that training trajectories are distilled from existing proofs (potentially constraining the space of 'discoveries'), and the informalization step still depending on a generalist LLM. Open questions include whether the framework can generate genuinely novel mathematics beyond the distribution of its training data, and whether the MDP formulation scales to multi-agent or interactive settings.
