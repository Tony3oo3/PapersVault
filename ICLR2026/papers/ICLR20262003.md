## Problem

While LLM-based text embedding models have largely focused on data scaling and synthesis, systematic exploration of superior training techniques and high-quality data curation remains underexplored. Additionally, most state-of-the-art embedding models originate from industry with proprietary data, closed training code, and commercial restrictions, limiting academic reproducibility. This paper addresses both gaps by establishing a fully open-source, compact (<1B parameter) embedding model that achieves SOTA performance through carefully orchestrated training techniques and data engineering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262003/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text query optionally prepended with a task-specific instruction (e.g., 'Instruct: Given a query, retrieve documents that answer the query. Query: {query}'), along with positive and negative passages, and produces a fixed-length 896-dimensional embedding vector via mean-pooling over token representations. For symmetric tasks (STS, pair classification), instructions are also prepended to passages. Matryoshka Representation Learning is incorporated into both the contrastive and KL losses, enabling the embedding to be truncated to smaller dimensions (e.g., 256) while retaining strong performance.

## Method

KaLM-Embedding-V2 is initialized from Qwen2-0.5B with the causal attention mask removed to enable fully bidirectional attention, and uses simple mean-pooling to produce embeddings. Training follows a progressive three-stage pipeline: (1) pre-training on ~470M weakly supervised samples across 20+ categories using InfoNCE with in-batch negatives, (2) fine-tuning on ~6M high-quality supervised samples across 100+ categories with focal-style reweighting (w_i = (1 - p_i)^γ) to emphasize difficult samples and an online hard-negative mixing strategy that synthesizes new hard negatives via pair-wise (λ~Beta(2,2)) or list-wise (softmax-weighted) convex combinations of existing negatives, and (3) contrastive distillation from Qwen3-Embedding-8B using KL divergence on temperature-scaled cosine similarity distributions to capture fine-grained semantic differences. The focal reweighting and online mixing are integrated into the InfoNCE denominator, while the distillation stage combines both the KL objective and the contrastive loss for complementary learning signals.

## Results

KaLM-Embedding-V2.5 (494M params, 896-dim) achieves 70.93 MTK / 72.46 MTY on MTEB (cmn, v1) and 69.33 MTK / 65.83 MTY on MTEB (eng, v1), setting a new SOTA among all open-source embedding models under 1B parameters. This represents a +4.37 MTK (cmn) and +2.53 MTK (eng) improvement over the prior KaLM-Embedding-V1, and outperforms the next-best sub-1B model (Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B) by +4.60 MTK (cmn) and +2.57 MTK (eng). Remarkably, the 0.5B model surpasses the 1.5B gte-Qwen2-1.5B-instruct by +3.81 MTK (cmn) and +2.14 MTK (eng), and the 9B bge-multilingual-gemma2 by +3.29 MTK (cmn), while remaining competitive with 7–8B models such as NV-Embed-v2 and Qwen3-Embedding-8B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among sub-1B models, KaLM-Embedding-V2.5 decisively outperforms all competitors: it beats Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B (596M) by +4.60 MTK (cmn) and +2.57 MTK (eng), jina-embeddings-v3 (572M) by +9.11 MTK (cmn) and +3.82 MTK (eng), and multilingual-e5-large (560M) by +12.85 MTK (cmn). Against larger models, it surpasses the 1.5B gte-Qwen2-1.5B-instruct by +3.81 MTK (cmn) and +2.14 MTK (eng), and the 9B bge-multilingual-gemma2 by +3.29 MTK (cmn) and +6.35 MTK (eng). It trails the 8B Qwen3-Embedding-8B by −2.91 MTK (cmn) and the 7B NV-Embed-v2 by −2.98 MTK (eng), but narrows the gap to within ~3 points despite being 16–26× smaller. The paper also notes that KaLM-Embedding-V2.5 was trained on only ~6M samples across 2–4 GPUs, compared to Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B's 19M samples, highlighting the efficiency gains from the proposed training techniques and data curation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits squarely within the LLM-as-embedding-model lineage (NV-Embed, GTE, E5-Mistral, Jina, Qwen3-Embedding), which has largely focused on data scaling and synthesis. Its conceptual departure is a systematic argument that training techniques and data quality have been underexplored relative to data volume: it adapts focal loss reweighting (Lin et al., 2017) to contrastive embedding training, introduces an online hard-negative mixing strategy that avoids costly periodic re-mining, and layers contrastive distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) from a stronger teacher (Qwen3-Embedding-8B) on top of standard InfoNCE fine-tuning. The multi-stage pipeline (weakly-supervised pretraining → supervised fine-tuning → contrastive distillation) and the removal of causal attention for bidirectional representation learning follow trends established by NV-Embed and GTE, but the paper's contribution is the orchestrated combination of these individually known components with a detailed data curation recipe (20+ pretraining categories, 100+ finetuning categories, persona-based synthesis, example-based multi-class labeling).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving SOTA among sub-1B embedding models on both MTEB (eng) and C-MTEB while remaining fully open-source with commercial use permitted, this paper lowers the barrier for academic and industrial teams to deploy competitive embedding models in RAG pipelines without relying on proprietary APIs or billion-parameter models. The practical recipe—particularly the focal reweighting (largest ablation drop), online hard-negative mixing (avoids expensive re-mining), and the distillation stage that yields the V2→V2.5 jump—provides actionable guidance for improving existing embedding pipelines. Open questions include whether these techniques transfer to larger backbones or multilingual settings (the paper notes multilingual performance is comparable but not trained on large-scale multilingual corpora), and whether the data curation recipe generalizes beyond the specific 100+ categories used here.
