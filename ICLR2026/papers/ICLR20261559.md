## Problem

Visualization-of-thought (VoT) and multimodal VoT (MVoT) enable multi-hop spatial reasoning in MLLMs but suffer from quadratic growth in attention cost and KV-cache memory as intermediate visual and textual tokens accumulate autoregressively. Two structural gaps limit existing compression approaches: token importance shifts non-uniformly within and across reasoning hops, and visual versus textual tokens exhibit fundamentally different redundancy dynamics (visual importance decays sharply after mid-depth layers while text remains semantically active), yet prior methods apply fixed or modality-agnostic pruning ratios.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (336×336, encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14) and a text question, which are interleaved with self-generated visual and textual thought tokens across multiple reasoning hops (up to 5 hops, 3072-token sequences). At each transformer layer and hop, the router produces per-token importance scores in [0,1] and a binary execution mask; pruned tokens generate no KV-cache entries, so the output is both the final spatial reasoning answer and a progressively sparsified token sequence whose visual retention drops to near-zero in deep layers while text retention stays around 70%.

## Method

DARE inserts a lightweight modality-specific linear router (sigmoid-gated) at every transformer layer to score each token's importance, then uses a Gumbel-Softmax relaxation to make discrete top-k token selection differentiable and end-to-end trainable. Retention ratios ρ_{h,l}^{(m)} are learnable per hop and per layer, softly regularized toward a global target via an MSE loss, enabling the model to adapt sparsity dynamically across the reasoning trajectory. A key asymmetric design treats the two modalities differently: text tokens are governed by a single ratio-matching loss, while visual tokens follow a two-phase schedule—soft gating in early layers (l ≤ l_c) followed by a hard-pruning penalty loss that suppresses residual visual activations in deeper layers once spatial information has been fused into the textual stream. During inference, an execution mask is added to the causal attention mask so pruned tokens receive −∞ logits and produce zero KV-cache storage, yielding a progressive KV-cache retention policy that retains more tokens in early cross-modal fusion layers and prunes aggressively in later abstraction layers. The full objective combines the task loss with modality-specific MSE regularizers and the visual hard-pruning penalty, all optimized jointly with the backbone via AdamW.

## Results

DARE-LH achieves the highest accuracy across all seven multimodal spatial reasoning benchmarks while reducing FLOPs by 40.37% and KV-cache usage by 46.07% on average. On compositional tasks (VolCano backbone), DARE-LH reaches 68.09% on VSR, 60.07% on V-Star, 68.09% on EmbSpatial, and 68.31% on Winoground, matching or exceeding the full-token VolCano baseline (67.18%, 58.40%, 58.29%, 68.37%) while cutting FLOPs by 37–43%. On dynamic spatial reasoning (Anole-7B backbone), DARE-LH attains 93.32% on MAZE, 95.47% on MiniBehavior, and 86.11% on FrozenLake, outperforming MVoT by 0.37–0.51% while halving compute (e.g., 12.7K vs. 22.1K GFLOPs on MAZE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DARE-LH outperforms all seven baselines on accuracy across the four compositional spatial reasoning benchmarks. Against the closest competitor UniPru (symmetric differentiable pruning without hard visual loss), DARE-LH gains +4.38% on VSR, +6.85% on V-Star, +7.34% on EmbSpatial, and +2.48% on Winoground, while also reducing FLOPs by 14–25%. Heima (latent-space reasoning) is the most efficient baseline (lowest FLOPs) but sacrifices 10–20% accuracy due to loss of explicit multimodal thoughts. SparseVLM and LightFastV achieve moderate efficiency but trail DARE-LH by 5–15% in accuracy, confirming that modality-specific, hop-aware routing is critical. On dynamic spatial reasoning with Anole-7B, DARE-LH surpasses MVoT (the strongest prior method) by 0.37% on MAZE, 0.33% on MiniBehavior, and 0.51% on FrozenLake while cutting FLOPs by ~43–47%. On general VQA (GQA, MMBench) and hallucination detection (POPE, AMBER), DARE-L matches or exceeds the full-token VolCano baseline while reducing FLOPs by ~42% and memory by >1.8 GB.

## Relation to Prior Work

DARE extends the multimodal spatial reasoning line (VoT, MVoT, VolCano) by addressing the efficiency bottleneck of autoregressive token accumulation across reasoning hops. It departs from prior token-compression work (FastV, SparseVLM, LightThinker, Heima) in two key ways: (1) retention ratios are learned per-hop and per-layer via Gumbel-Softmax rather than fixed or heuristic, and (2) visual and textual tokens are pruned asymmetrically based on their distinct redundancy dynamics, rather than uniformly. The progressive KV-cache retention strategy with an execution mask is a practical extension of standard causal masking that aligns cache management with the observed visual-to-verbal information flow, a connection not exploited by prior MoD-style or single-pass pruning methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 40% FLOPs and 46% KV-cache reductions while matching or exceeding full-model accuracy make multi-hop multimodal spatial reasoning substantially more deployable, particularly for long-horizon tasks where context saturation is the binding constraint. The empirical finding that visual token importance collapses after mid-network layers while textual tokens remain active provides a concrete, reusable principle for future multimodal architectures. Open questions include whether the learned retention patterns transfer across tasks without retraining, how the approach scales to video or audio modalities with different redundancy structures, and whether the fine-tuning requirement can be relaxed toward training-free deployment. The two-phase visual pruning (soft gating then hard suppression) introduces a hyperparameter (cutoff layer l_c) whose data-driven determination may not generalize cleanly to new architectures.
