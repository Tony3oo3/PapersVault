## Problem

Neural operators (NOs) are powerful forward PDE solvers but act as black-box approximators that offer no interpretable representation of the underlying physical parameters driving the system. Existing inverse PDE methods either require prior knowledge of the governing equation form or fail to disentangle learned parameters into distinct, physically meaningful factors. This paper addresses the gap of simultaneously solving forward and inverse PDE problems while extracting interpretable, disentangled physical factors directly from black-box NO parameters.

## Input / Output

The method takes a loading/traction field f(x) defined on a spatial domain Ω as input and produces the corresponding solution field u(x) (forward) along with a low-dimensional latent vector z (inverse) that encodes the hidden material parameter b. The parameter b can be either a fixed vector (homogeneous material) or a vector-valued function over Ω (heterogeneous material), and the method operates in three supervision regimes: fully supervised (b given), semi-supervised (a label c(b) given, e.g., a digit classification), and fully unsupervised (no information about b). All data are spatial fields on structured grids, and the forward mapping is function-to-function while the inverse mapping is from NO parameters to a finite-dimensional latent code.

## Method

DisentangO is a variational hyper-neural operator built on MetaNO/IFNO, where task-wise adaptation is restricted to the lifting layer parameters θ_P^η while all iterative Fourier layers and projection layers are shared across tasks. A hierarchical VAE encodes θ_P^η into a latent posterior q(z|θ_P^η) and decodes it back to reconstruct θ_P^η, which is then fed into the shared NO backbone to predict u. The training objective is an ELBO combining data reconstruction (forward PDE accuracy), NO parameter reconstruction (inverse fidelity), and a KL regularization term, with an optional classification loss for semi-supervised settings. The key theoretical contribution is a component-wise identifiability theorem: under smoothness, invertibility, conditional independence, and linear independence assumptions on the data-generating process, the learned latent factors are identifiable up to per-component invertible transformations, guaranteeing that each latent dimension corresponds to a distinct physical factor.

## Results

In the supervised HGO constitutive modeling experiment (Table 1), DisentangO achieves 1.65% forward PDE test error and 4.63% inverse (latent parameter) test error, using 697k trainable parameters. For forward prediction, MetaNO achieves the best error (1.59%) but cannot solve the inverse problem; DisentangO is the best joint forward+inverse solver, beating the next-best joint method (FUSE-f) by 25.2% on inverse accuracy. In the semi-supervised MMNIST experiment, DisentangO-15 with β_d=100 reaches 5.48% forward error (vs. MetaNO's 2.68% optimal bound), outperforming VAE (16.34%) and β-VAE (17.13%) by 66.5% and 68.0% respectively. In the fully unsupervised heterogeneous material experiment, DisentangO-30 achieves 5.28% forward error, outperforming the best baseline (VAE at 61.10%) by 90.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DisentangO is the only method that simultaneously solves both forward and inverse PDE problems with high accuracy. On forward PDE prediction, DisentangO (1.65%) is marginally behind MetaNO (1.59%) but substantially ahead of FNO (2.45%, +32.7% relative), UFNO (7.61%), WNO (8.09%), and PIANO (7.99%); however, MetaNO, NIO, InVAErt, FUSE, and SMDP cannot perform forward prediction at all. On inverse PDE (latent parameter recovery under full supervision SC1), DisentangO (4.63%) outperforms InVAErt (5.16%) by 7.2%, FUSE (4.99%), and the only other joint solver FUSE-f (6.19%) by 25.2%, while dominating all other baselines (WNO 9.95%, UFNO 11.23%, FNO 14.55%, NIO 15.16%, PIANO 15.35%, VAE 16.34%, convVAE 16.27%, β-VAE 16.47%, SMDP 17.76%). In the semi-supervised MMNIST setting, even the weakest DisentangO-2 (12.82%) beats VAE (16.34%) and β-VAE (17.13%) by 21.5% and 25.2%, while the best DisentangO-15 (5.48%) narrows the gap to MetaNO's forward-only bound (2.68%). In the fully unsupervised heterogeneous material experiment, DisentangO-30 (5.28%) outperforms the best baseline VAE (61.10%) by 90.7%, with MetaNO (2.67%) again serving as the forward-only upper bound. The VAE and β-VAE baselines are consistently the weakest across all three experiments, confirming that standard generative models without the neural operator backbone cannot capture the physics.

## Relation to Prior Work

DisentangO builds directly on MetaNO's meta-learning framework (task-wise adaptation restricted to lifting layers of an IFNO backbone) and repurposes it as the forward solver within a hypernetwork-VAE pipeline. The VAE component follows the hierarchical variational autoencoder (HVAE) architecture, but is applied to neural operator parameters rather than raw data—a departure from standard disentangled representation learning (β-VAE, FactorVAE, InfoGAN) which disentangles from observations. The paper also positions itself against inverse PDE solvers (NIO, FUSE, InVAErt) that reconstruct parameters from solution data but do not disentangle or provide identifiability guarantees, and against hypernetwork literature that has not previously been used for hidden physics discovery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical identifiability guarantee (component-wise, up to invertible transformations) for latent physical factors learned from multi-task neural operator parameters, bridging a gap between predictive performance and physical interpretability. Practically, it demonstrates that a single architecture can simultaneously solve forward PDE prediction and inverse parameter discovery across supervised, semi-supervised, and unsupervised regimes, with the plug-and-play design allowing any NO backbone. Key limitations include experiments restricted to 2D problems with relatively small latent dimensions, scalability governed by the NO backbone, and the identifiability assumptions (particularly linear independence across systems) that may not hold for all physical settings. Open questions include extension to 3D and high-dimensional PDEs, validation on real experimental data, and whether the disentanglement quality degrades as the number of physical systems or parameter dimensionality grows.
