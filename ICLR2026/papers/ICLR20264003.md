## Problem

Neural surrogate models for 3D physics simulations face a fundamental scalability barrier: the number of tokens grows cubically with spatial resolution, making high-resolution (≥128³) inference prohibitively expensive in both memory and compute. Most prior work targets resolutions up to 64³, and architectures that train on local crops lack mechanisms to capture the global dependencies and long-range interactions essential for correct solutions in large domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264003/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For autoregressive prediction, the model takes P preceding 3D volumetric states (velocity, pressure, or density fields) at resolutions up to 512³ along with a conditioning vector encoding PDE type and hyperparameters, and outputs the next 3D state. For probabilistic sampling (turbulent channel flow), the input is only the Reynolds number conditioning, and the output is a sample from the distribution of 4-channel velocity/pressure fields on a non-equidistant 96×96×192 grid. A key design constraint is that the network is pretrained on small crops (e.g., 128³ or 48³) and then scaled to the full domain at inference, requiring the architecture to handle implicit unknown boundary conditions at crop edges.

## Method

P3D is a hybrid CNN-Transformer U-shaped architecture that replaces the patchification step of standard vision transformers with a convolutional encoder/decoder to learn compressed local features, followed by windowed multi-head self-attention transformer blocks for deep representation learning. The central innovation for scaling is a context network: a 6-layer sequence-to-sequence transformer (using hyper-attention for efficiency) that processes the bottleneck latent tokens globally, with dedicated region tokens that inject aggregated global information back into the decoder via adaptive instance normalization layers, enabling each spatial region to be modulated differently based on its global context. The architecture is deliberately translation-equivariant (no absolute positional embeddings in the backbone), allowing crop-based pretraining to transfer to larger domains via two scaling strategies: processing the full domain as a single input or independently encoding/decoding crops with the context network mediating communication. For probabilistic tasks, P3D is trained as a flow-matching diffusion model, regressing the velocity field of an ODE that transports Gaussian noise to the target distribution of simulation states.

## Results

On the joint PDE learning benchmark (14 PDE types, APEBench-based), P3D-L achieves the lowest nRMSE across all crop sizes: 4.13×10⁻² at 32³, 2.49×10⁻² at 64³, and 2.08×10⁻² at 128³, outperforming the best baseline (AFNO) by 2.4×, 2.0×, and 2.3× respectively. On forced isotropic turbulence at 512³, P3D-S pretrained on 128³ crops achieves RMSE of 1.60×10⁻² without any finetuning, and maintains enstrophy spectrum error below 10×10² for 50 autoregressive rollout steps. For probabilistic turbulent channel flow sampling, P3D-L (full domain) achieves Mean L2 error of 3.02×10⁻⁵ and Variance L2 of 13.20×10⁻⁵, while the crop-pretrained + context-network finetuned variant (P3D-L <X48|X48>) reaches 23.6±21.4×10⁻⁵ Mean L2 in only 20 finetuning epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

P3D dominates all seven baselines across every crop size and every experiment. At the 64³ crop size (the most common evaluation setting), P3D-L (2.49) nearly halves the nRMSE of the strongest baseline AFNO (4.98) and outperforms Swin3D (7.04), FactFormer (4.62), UNetConvNext (7.09), UNetGenCFD (8.04), TFNO (8.37), and AViT (25.0) by wide margins. At 128³, where only AFNO, Swin3D, UNetGenCFD, AViT, and P3D are evaluated, P3D-L (2.08) still leads AFNO (4.79) by 2.3×. On the isotropic turbulence task (Table 3), P3D-S achieves the lowest RMSE at 1-step rollout (2.81 vs. 3.22 for Swin3D and 3.69 for AFNO) and by far the best enstrophy spectrum error (2.15 vs. 4.0 for Swin3D and 7.6 for AFNO), with the gap widening to 31.9 vs. 156–190 at 15 steps. For the turbulent channel flow generative task (Table 5), P3D-L full domain (Mean L2 3.02) outperforms AFNO full domain (28.73) by ~10× and UNetGenCFD full domain (132.38) by ~44×, while also using less VRAM (14.9 GB vs. 17.4 GB for UNetGenCFD). The crop-pretrained P3D-L <48|192> without global context fails catastrophically (Mean L2 5862.81), confirming that the context network is essential for tasks requiring wall-relative positioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

P3D extends PDE-Transformer (Holzschuh et al., 2025) from 2D to 3D by replacing patchification with a convolutional encoder/decoder and adding windowed self-attention (Swin-style) for local representation learning, while incorporating DiT-style adaptive instance normalization for conditioning. The crop-based pretraining strategy with a sequence-to-sequence context model for global information aggregation is a new mechanism not present in prior PDE surrogate work, addressing the fundamental limitation that local-only architectures cannot capture long-range dependencies. The multi-physics pretraining setup builds on the APEBench benchmark and the multi-physics pretraining line (Subramanian et al., 2023; McCabe et al., 2023), but extends these to 3D at substantially higher resolutions (up to 512³ vs. typical 64³). The probabilistic channel flow experiment extends flow-matching-based diffusion surrogates (Kohl et al., 2024; Lippe et al., 2023) from 2D to 3D with non-equidistant grids.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

P3D provides a practical and well-validated recipe for scaling neural surrogates to genuinely high-resolution 3D physics (512³ isotropic turbulence), a regime where prior work has been limited to 64³–128³. The crop-based pretraining plus context model approach significantly reduces memory and compute requirements, making high-resolution 3D surrogate training accessible on standard GPU clusters. Key limitations remain: the architecture is restricted to regular grids (not arbitrary geometries), and the finetuning process with the context model still produces visible discontinuities at crop boundaries, suggesting the global coordination mechanism needs further refinement. The paper opens the door to probabilistic 3D flow sampling (turbulent channel flow) but leaves open how to handle diverse modalities, resolutions, and dimensionalities within a single model.
