## Problem

Membrane Potential Perturbation Dynamic (MPPD) is used to capture and suppress adversarial perturbations in spiking neural networks, but it discards the neuronal reset component without a rigorous theoretical justification, and its existing MS-MPPD regularizer operates as a TV-ℓ2 framework that lacks the coarea formula property needed for robust signal reconstruction. This paper asks whether MPPD can be formally identified as total variation and whether a TV-ℓ1 formulation would yield stronger adversarial robustness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet) that may be clean or perturbed with Gaussian noise (ζ=10/255) or adversarial noise (ζ=6/255 for AT, ζ=7/255 for AT+Reg), and outputs a class label via cross-entropy. During training, the SNN processes each image over 8 discrete time-steps through LIF/DLIF neurons, and the TV-ℓ1 regularizer operates on the layer-wise membrane potential trajectories rather than on the input or output directly.

## Method

The paper proves that MPPD is exactly the total variation of membrane potential with respect to the unified (node index, time-step) domain, under the condition that the perturbation δ is a measurable function of (i,t). It then shows the existing MS-MPPD regularizer is a TV-ℓ2 model and proposes MPPD-TV-ℓ1, which replaces the squared perturbation sum with the absolute-value (L1) sum, enabling the coarea formula to count and suppress level-set concentrations of adversarial perturbations. The L1 formulation also operates in a strictly larger function space than L2 under finite measures, broadening the class of admissible membrane potentials. A closed-form subgradient (Proposition 5) makes the non-differentiable TV-ℓ1 term compatible with standard backpropagation (STBP with surrogate gradients), and a dominated TV property (Theorem 4) guarantees that membrane potential TV is bounded by spike TV scaled by the weight norm and temporal decay factor, ensuring training stability.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with AT training (VGG11), MPPD-TV-ℓ1 achieves 86.11% clean accuracy and 45.26% APGD₁₀DLR accuracy, outperforming MPPD-TV-ℓ2 (85.17% / 35.30%) and Non-MPPD (85.03% / 34.35%). Under PGD₂₀ attack, MPPD-TV-ℓ1 reaches 41.15% vs. 32.26% for MPPD-TV-ℓ2 and 34.24% for Non-MPPD. On CIFAR-100 (AT, VGG11), MPPD-TV-ℓ1 achieves 58.41% clean and 19.67% APGD₁₀DLR, versus 57.82% / 16.69% for MPPD-TV-ℓ2. On Tiny-ImageNet (AT, WRN16), MPPD-TV-ℓ1 reaches 35.00% clean and 7.33% PGD₂₀, compared to 33.99% / 7.17% for MPPD-TV-ℓ2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPPD-TV-ℓ1 consistently outperforms MPPD-TV-ℓ2 (the prior MS-MPPD method from Ding et al., ICML 2024) across all three datasets and both VGG11/WRN16 architectures, with the largest gaps under iterative PGD attacks (e.g., +8.89% on PGD₂₀ for CIFAR-10 AT/VGG11). Against Non-MPPD (no regularization), MPPD-TV-ℓ1 gains 1.08% clean accuracy and up to 10.91% on APGD₁₀DLR (CIFAR-10 AT/VGG11), confirming that the TV-ℓ1 regularizer itself drives robustness. Compared to ANN-based adversarial training baselines (ANN-PGD-AT, ANN-RiFT), MPPD-TV-ℓ1 with VGG11 achieves substantially higher robust accuracy on CIFAR-10 (41.15% PGD₂₀ vs. ~34–35%) while maintaining competitive clean accuracy. Against SNN-specific baselines (SNN-BP, HIRE-SNN, SNN-RAT, FEEL+AT, SR), MPPD-TV-ℓ1 dominates on all reported attack metrics for CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, e.g., 41.15% PGD₂₀ vs. 31.04% (SR) and 19.97% (FEEL+AT). The advantage over MPPD-TV-ℓ2 grows with attack strength: the PGD₂₀ gap widens from ~2% (CIFAR-100) to ~9% (CIFAR-10), supporting the theoretical claim that the coarea formula in TV-ℓ1 provides superior denoising under stronger perturbations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MPPD regularizer introduced by Ding et al. (ICML 2024), which proposed discarding the neuronal reset term and using mean-squared membrane potential perturbation to stabilize SNNs against adversarial examples. The key conceptual departure is proving that MPPD is mathematically a Total Variation (TV) term, thereby recasting the existing MS-MPPD as a TV-ℓ2 model and motivating a TV-ℓ1 variant grounded in the coarea formula from classical image reconstruction (Rudin et al., 1992; Chan et al., 2006). The paper also connects to the broader adversarial robustness literature for SNNs (SNN-RAT, FEEL, SR, HIRE-SNN) and ANN baselines (PGD-AT, RiFT), positioning its contribution as a theoretically principled regularization rather than an ad-hoc heuristic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TV formulation provides the missing theoretical foundation for MPPD, explaining why discarding the neuronal reset is valid (it is simply not part of the TV) and establishing a fundamental measurability condition on the perturbation. The TV-ℓ1 framework offers a strictly larger admissible function space (L¹ ⊃ L² under finite measures) and exploits the coarea formula for sharper noise suppression, yielding consistent robustness gains that grow with attack strength. Open questions include whether the measurable-perturbation condition holds in practice for all SNN architectures, whether the framework extends to neuromorphic hardware with safety-critical constraints, and whether the coarea-based suppression generalizes beyond image classification to temporal or multi-modal SNN tasks.
