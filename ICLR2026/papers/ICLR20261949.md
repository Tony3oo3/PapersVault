## Problem

Existing defenses against safety degradation during LLM fine-tuning operate at the sample level, either discarding entire samples or mixing in safety data, which creates an unsatisfactory trade-off between safety and utility. The paper identifies that safety degradation is fundamentally a token-level phenomenon—harmful and beneficial signals are intertwined within the same sample—making coarse-grained sample-level selection both insufficient for safety and wasteful of utility.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a custom fine-tuning dataset (instruction-response pairs), a small harmful reference dataset (~10% of Anthropic Red Teaming), a utility reference dataset (~3% of OpenOrca), and a safety-aligned base LLM. It produces a binary token-level mask over the custom dataset (discarding the top d=10% of tokens by global score) and a safe customized LLM via masked LoRA fine-tuning. The mask is computed once per tokenizer and is transferable across models sharing that tokenizer, enabling reuse without recomputation.

## Method

TOSS trains two lightweight LoRA reference models: a safety-degraded model on harmful data and a utility-oriented model on task data. Each token in the custom dataset is scored by the loss difference S = -log P(token|utility model) + log P(token|safety-degraded model), where high scores indicate tokens more probable under harmful than utility distributions. A global ranking across all tokens in the dataset (rather than per-sample local ranking) determines which top d% tokens to mask, adaptively removing more tokens from harmful samples and fewer from benign ones. The masked tokens are excluded from the fine-tuning loss, preserving utility-critical tokens even within partially harmful samples. TOSS-Pro extends this with iterative refinement: at each round, the top-scoring samples are added to the harmful training set to update the safety-degraded model, progressively improving its ability to identify unsafe tokens without requiring a high-quality initial reference model.

## Results

On Llama-3-8B-Instruct, TOSS achieves an average win rate of 81.57% across safety (ANTHROPIC HH: 88.82%, HEx-PHI: 87.54%) and utility (SLIMORCA: 68.37%) benchmarks, compared to 61.48% for the best sample-level baseline SEAL. TOSS-Pro further improves the average to 83.83%, with HEx-PHI reaching 93.79%. On Llama-2-7B-Chat-hf, TOSS achieves 70.13% average win rate (vs. 57.57% for DSIR, the strongest baseline), and TOSS-Pro reaches 74.04%. The paper reports up to 30% higher win rate on safety benchmarks and up to 11% higher on utility benchmarks relative to SEAL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TOSS decisively outperforms all five baselines on both models. Against SEAL (the strongest sample-level baseline), TOSS gains +20.09 points in average win rate on Llama-3-8B-Instruct (81.57 vs. 61.48) and +16.33 on Llama-2-7B-Chat-hf (70.13 vs. 53.80), with the largest gap on safety (HEx-PHI: 87.54 vs. 68.83) and utility (SLIMORCA: 68.37 vs. 57.41). DSIR, the best-performing baseline on Llama-2, is surpassed by TOSS by +12.56 average points (70.13 vs. 57.57). SafeInstr (data mixture) and Random token discarding both remain near the 50% baseline, confirming that coarse-grained strategies fail to meaningfully improve safety-utility trade-offs. In the lower-cost comparison (Table 11), SafetyLock (training-free) and Circuit Breakers (post-hoc repair) achieve win rates nearly identical to Standard SFT (≈50%), while TOSS reaches 88.82% on ANTHROPIC HH and 87.54% on HEx-PHI, demonstrating that post-hoc defense paradigms are ineffective under the highly adversarial fine-tuning setting studied here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant sample-level paradigm in safe LLM fine-tuning (SEAL, DSIR, SafeInstr) by arguing that the fundamental unit of safety degradation is the token, not the sample. It repurposes the loss-based token scoring idea from utility-oriented frameworks like Rho-1 (Lin et al., 2024) and Token Cleaning (Pang et al., 2025), but redirects it toward safety by constructing a loss-difference metric between a safety-degraded reference model and a utility-oriented reference model. The progressive refinement strategy (TOSS-Pro) introduces an iterative self-improvement loop for the safety-degraded model that has no direct analogue in prior data-selection or safety-alignment work, drawing loosely on curriculum-style iterative training but applied to reference model quality rather than data ordering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The token-level diagnosis that harmful and beneficial signals are intertwined within the same sample challenges the foundational assumption behind all sample-level defenses and provides a principled justification for finer-grained selection. Practically, the method is lightweight (two LoRA reference models + one inference pass) and the selected dataset transfers across models sharing a tokenizer, making it deployable in real fine-tuning pipelines. Open questions include how the approach scales to much larger models or multi-modal settings, whether the fixed 10% discarding ratio generalizes across domains, and whether the reference models can be replaced by cheaper alternatives (e.g., classifier heads) without sacrificing the safety-utility trade-off.
