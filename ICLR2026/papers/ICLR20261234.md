## Problem

Reward models are initialized from pretrained LLMs before preference finetuning, yet no prior work has examined whether RMs inherit value biases from their base models. This paper asks whether the choice of base model systematically shapes the value dimensions (e.g., agency vs. communion) that an RM encodes, even when the preference data and training procedure are held identical.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes value-laden prompts (54 positively and negatively framed variations) and psycholinguistic word corpora (Big Two nouns, MFD2 virtue words) as inputs, and produces median token-rank distributions per value category per model. For the implicit RM analysis, the method consumes two LLMs and a prompt, computing per-token log probabilities across the full vocabulary (~85k tokens) to yield a single MWLR scalar per token. The exhaustive token search evaluates each token in a separate forward pass, so outputs are complete rank-ordered reward scores over the entire vocabulary rather than sampled generations.

## Method

The paper combines exhaustive token search (evaluating every vocabulary token on a prompt) with validated psycholinguistic corpora to quantify value biases in 10 open-weight RMs, revealing a robust Llama→agency / Gemma→communion split. To trace the source of these biases, the authors derive an implicit reward score from the log-probability difference between two LLMs, using a mixture-weighted log-ratio (MWLR = ½(p+q)·(log q − log p)) that suppresses spurious contributions from low-probability tail tokens; this MWLR score is validated by recovering known value shifts induced via supervised finetuning. Finally, the authors train their own RMs from Llama 3.2 3B Instruct and Gemma 2 IT 2B with identical LoRA hyperparameters (rank 32, α=64, lr 1e-5, Bradley-Terry loss) across two preference datasets (Skywork ~77k, Unified Feedback ~850k) and data-quantity ablations (13k–106k), checkpointing every 1000 steps to show that the base-model value gap narrows but does not close, persisting even at ~100k+ preference pairs.

## Results

Across 10 leading RewardBench RMs, base model choice explains 27% of variance in token-rank differences (R² = .27), with Llama-based RMs showing a robust preference for agency words and Gemma-based RMs for communion words (Cohen's d = 0.40–0.43; 3-way interaction p < .001). In top-10 token analysis, Gemma RMs place 5/10 top tokens as Communion (0 Agency), while Llama RMs place 3.67 Communion and 2.33 Agency. The MWLR implicit-reward score between Llama 3.2 3B Instruct and Gemma 2 IT 2B yields 'Freedom' (score 0.504) as the optimal token and 'Love' (score −0.386) as the pessimal token, and this gap increases with model size across all 21 Llama–Gemma comparisons (1B–70B). Ablation experiments show approximately 100k preference pairs are needed to meaningfully narrow the base-model gap, and the bias persists even in GRMs trained on 630k+ preferences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external comparison is with Generalizable Reward Models (GRMs) from Yang et al. (2024), which use a regularized Bradley-Terry objective that preserves the base model's generative hidden states. The paper's own RMs (standard BT loss, LoRA) show the agency/communion gap narrowing with increasing preference data, reaching near-parity at ~100k pairs. In contrast, the GRMs retain a striking agency/communion gap even after training on 632k+ preferences from a mixture of open-source datasets, suggesting that regularization preserving base-model representations amplifies inherited value biases. Internally, the paper compares instruction-tuned vs. pretrained base models and finds the same 3-way interaction (F(1,208)=58.3 vs. 43.2, both p<.001), confirming the bias originates in pretraining rather than instruction tuning. Across model families, the MWLR gap between 'Freedom' and 'Love' is present in all 21 Llama–Gemma size combinations and grows with model size, indicating the effect is not an artifact of small-model noise. An exploratory extension to Qwen-based RMs shows an even stronger communion bias that does not narrow at all over training, persisting even at 100k preferences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the exhaustive token search method for RM interpretability introduced by Christian et al. (2025) by combining it with validated psycholinguistic corpora (Big Two, MFD2) to quantify value biases as a function of base model. The implicit reward formulation (MWLR score) is derived directly from the RLHF/DPO objective (Rafailov et al., 2023), reframing the log-probability difference between two LLMs as a usable reward signal. While prior work has examined how RMs introduce bias into post-trained LLMs (Siththaranjan et al., 2023; Bharadwaj et al., 2025) or how pretraining shapes LLM safety (Maini et al., 2025; O'Brien et al., 2025), no prior work has traced RM value biases back to the pretrained base model on which the RM is initialized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that base-model value biases in RMs are remarkably durable—requiring ~100k+ preference pairs to partially mitigate, and persisting even in GRMs trained on 630k+ preferences—challenges the assumption that preference finetuning can fully override pretraining. This has direct practical implications: open-source developers' choice of base model is a value decision, not merely a performance one, and pretraining data filtering may shape models' moral intuitions far more than previously recognized. Open questions include scaling laws for both model size and data quantity, extension to additional value dimensions beyond agency/communion, and mechanistic interpretability of how exactly values are encoded and inherited.
