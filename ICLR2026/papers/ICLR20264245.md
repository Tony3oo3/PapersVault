## Problem

Existing multilingual safety alignment methods require either large-scale response-level supervision in each target language or pairwise cross-lingual distillation, making them costly and yielding uneven safety performance across languages. This paper asks whether safety consistency can be enforced across all languages simultaneously using only prompt-level signals, without any response data in low-resource languages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264245/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the same safety query translated into m languages (prompts only, no target-language responses) and produces a safety-aligned LLM whose internal representations are collinear across languages. The MLC loss is plug-and-play: it is added as an auxiliary term to any existing alignment objective (DPO, SFT, SimPO, ORPO) without modifying the base training pipeline, requiring only a modest increase in training tokens (~1.8×10⁶ vs. ~15×10⁶ for MPO).

## Method

The core contribution is a Multi-Lingual Consistency (MLC) loss that enforces geometric collinearity of multilingual query representations via singular value manipulation. By the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem, minimizing the Frobenius distance to the best rank-1 approximation of the representation matrix Z ∈ R^{d×m} is equivalent to maximizing the dominant singular value σ₁; the loss operationalizes this as a temperature-scaled softmax over singular values, encouraging σ₁ to account for nearly all variance. A jointly trained linear projection extracts representations from the final hidden layer, which are L2-normalized and stacked into Z. The total training objective is L_align + λ_aux · L_cons, making the method compatible with any existing alignment paradigm while requiring only multilingual prompt variants—no response-level supervision in target languages.

## Results

On PKU-SafeRLHF, DPO+MLC achieves 95.94% average safety rate across 10 languages on Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct (vs. 66.44% for DPO alone) and 96.83% on Gemma-2-9B-it (vs. 81.52% for DPO), while reducing cross-lingual variance from 12.44 to 0.07 (Qwen) and 6.52 to 0.02 (Gemma). Pairwise Agreement (PAG) rises to 0.9697 and 0.9989 respectively, indicating near-perfect response consistency across languages. On the out-of-distribution MultiJail benchmark, DPO+MLC achieves an average Attack Success Rate of just 0.70% (ID) and 0.51% (OOD) on Qwen, and 0.32%/0.38% on Gemma, demonstrating strong generalization to unseen languages. General utility is largely preserved: MMLU scores remain within 0.1–0.8 points of the raw model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPO+MLC decisively outperforms all three baselines on both model architectures across every reported metric. Against monolingual DPO, DPO+MLC lifts average safety by +29.5 points on Qwen (95.94% vs. 66.44%) and +15.3 points on Gemma (96.83% vs. 81.52%), while reducing cross-lingual variance by over 99% (12.44→0.07 on Qwen; 6.52→0.02 on Gemma). Against the data-augmentation baseline SDRRL, DPO+MLC gains +25.7 points (Qwen) and +24.9 points (Gemma) in average safety, and notably SDRRL actually degrades English safety (57.22% on Qwen, 51.11% on Gemma) whereas DPO+MLC preserves it. Against the reward-gap baseline MPO, DPO+MLC achieves +30.4 points (Qwen) and +17.9 points (Gemma) in average safety; MPO still leaves Swahili at 4.44% on Qwen, while DPO+MLC raises it to 92.78%. On the out-of-distribution MultiJail benchmark, DPO+MLC achieves the lowest ASR in both ID and OOD language groups, confirming that the gains are not overfit to the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant cross-lingual safety alignment paradigm (pair-wise alignment to an anchor language, as in SDRRL and MPO) by instead enforcing a joint collinearity constraint across all languages simultaneously. The spectral formulation draws on the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem and the PMRL framework (Liu et al., 2025b) to convert a rank-1 geometric objective into a differentiable softmax-over-singular-values loss. Conceptually, it repurposes the multilingual consistency notion from Lim et al. (2025) but shifts the intervention from output-level agreement to representation-level collinearity, making it compatible with any logits-based alignment objective (SFT, DPO, SimPO, ORPO) without requiring multilingual response data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature and dramatic data efficiency (≈1.8M vs. 15M+ tokens) make this a practically attractive option for organizations that cannot afford large-scale multilingual response annotation. Open questions remain: the layer-depth trade-off (deeper layers improve safety but degrade multilingual utility), the observation that low-resource languages sometimes default to English responses post-alignment, and whether the approach generalizes beyond safety to other alignment properties (helpfulness, value alignment). The finding that larger models exhibit *more* cross-lingual variance after monolingual DPO challenges the assumption that scale alone resolves multilingual gaps.
