## Problem

Despite extended pretraining context windows, LLMs effectively utilize only 10–20% of their capacity on long-context tasks due to insufficient long-context alignment. Existing remedies face three coupled challenges: unreliable and expensive data construction at long lengths, inefficient training that processes full long contexts for every preference pair, and optimization objectives that do not explicitly model the connection between short and long contexts. Prior short-to-long methods like LongPO are restricted to DPO and do not generalize to other preference optimization algorithms.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two paired inputs per training example: a long context x_long (≈7.5K tokens, mixing relevant and irrelevant documents) and a compressed short context x_short (≈1.1K tokens) that retains all task-relevant information, plus a question. Preference pairs (y_w, y_l) are sampled from the short context using Chain-of-Thought generation with sub-EM filtering against ground-truth answers. The model outputs a single answer to the question; during training, x_long is processed only once per step (for the chosen-only SoLo-RA term) while x_short is processed twice, reducing long-sequence computation by more than half.

## Method

SoLoPO theoretically decouples long-context preference optimization into two components via an upper-bound theorem (Theorem 1): (1) short-context PO, which optimizes preference pairs sampled from the compressed short context using the original algorithm's loss (DPO, SimPO, or ORPO), and (2) short-to-long reward alignment (SoLo-RA), which penalizes the absolute difference between the reward model's scores for the chosen response conditioned on x_short versus x_long. The key insight is that under a redundancy hypothesis (compression rate ρ < 100%), the long-context PO loss is bounded by the short-context PO loss plus a reward-alignment term, making the decoupling theoretically justified. A chosen-only variant applies SoLo-RA exclusively to y_w, halving long-context forward passes per training step and improving both efficiency and convergence stability. The framework is algorithm-agnostic: Table 1 shows concrete SoLo-RA expressions for DPO, SimPO, and ORPO, each derived from the corresponding reward function in the GPO unified framework.

## Results

On LongBenchV1 QA tasks, SoLo-ORPO (Qwen2.5-7B) achieves 49.5 average F1, outperforming Long-ORPO (45.3) by 4.2 points and approaching Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct (49.4). On RULER synthetic QA, SoLo-ORPO reaches 65.1 average accuracy across 4K–32K lengths, a 10.0-point gain over Long-ORPO (55.1). SoLoPO outperforms the original PO algorithm in 28 out of 32 evaluated settings across DPO, SimPO, and ORPO. On LongBenchV2, SoLoPO consistently outperforms vanilla PO across difficulty levels and context lengths beyond 32K, while maintaining short-context performance on the Open LLM Leaderboard (e.g., MMLU-Pro 44.83 vs. 44.63 for Instruct).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SoLoPO consistently outperforms all original PO baselines: SoLo-ORPO beats Long-ORPO by 4.2 points on LongBenchV1 and 10.0 on RULER (Qwen2.5-7B), while SoLo-DPO and SoLo-SimPO improve over their long-context counterparts by 0.9 and 3.8 points on LongBenchV1 respectively. Against LongPO (the closest prior method), SoLo-ORPO achieves 49.5 vs. 43.7 (reimp) on LongBenchV1 and 65.1 vs. 52.2 on RULER, a substantial margin attributable to the decoupled SoLo-RA objective. Remarkably, Qwen2.5-7B trained with SoLo-ORPO on only 5K MuSiQue samples matches Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct (49.5 vs. 49.4) on LongBenchV1 average. On Llama3.1-8B (128K context), gains are smaller (SoLo-ORPO: 46.3 vs. 45.4 LongBenchV1; 61.3 vs. 58.9 RULER) since 32K already falls within its effective range. SoLoPO also outperforms the non-decoupled Expand-Long-ORPO on NIAH-Plus (22.41 vs. 20.62 average for Qwen2.5-7B), confirming that explicit short-to-long reward alignment is more effective than simply applying short-context preference pairs to long inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

SoLoPO builds on the generalized preference optimization (GPO) framework of Tang et al. and the DPO/SimPO/ORPO family of algorithms, but departs from prior long-context alignment work (LongPO, LOGO, LongCE) by introducing a theoretically grounded decoupling: long-context PO is split into short-context PO plus an explicit short-to-long reward alignment (SoLo-RA) term, justified via an upper-bound theorem under a redundancy hypothesis. Unlike LongPO, which is restricted to DPO and applies short-context preference pairs directly to long-context training (non-decoupled), SoLoPO is algorithm-agnostic and separates the two optimization objectives. The efficiency motivation also distinguishes it from LongCE, which incurs extra forward passes for salient-token detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling provides a clean, general recipe that any PO algorithm can adopt to improve long-context alignment while simultaneously cutting training cost (2.1× longer trainable length, ~50% runtime reduction), which is practically significant for resource-constrained settings. However, the evaluation is confined to multi-hop QA from MuSiQue at 7B/8B scale, and the method offers no benefit for fully context-relevant tasks (ρ=100%, e.g., translation). Open questions include whether the framework extends to long-output generation, whether it scales to larger models and longer contexts beyond 32K, and whether the two hyperparameters (compression ratio, α) can be set automatically.
