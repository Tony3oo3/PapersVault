## Problem

Existing PDE dynamics forecasting methods (meta-learning, parameter conditioning, multi-physics pretraining) still require test-time samples and domain-specific fine-tuning to handle unseen out-of-distribution scenarios, because they never explicitly capture the fundamental physical invariance in PDE systems. This paper asks: can we achieve zero-shot OOD forecasting by formally defining and learning the invariant structure of PDE dynamics from limited multi-domain training data?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262445/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes H=10 past steps of 2D PDE state fields (64×64 spatial resolution, periodic boundary conditions) along with PDE system parameters (e.g., viscosity, diffusion coefficients) and forcing terms. Pre-calculated first- and second-order spatial derivatives are additionally fed to each operator expert through a learned binary mask for adaptive derivative selection. The output is the future PDE state trajectory (11–21 steps ahead), produced autoregressively via Euler forward integration where each predicted step feeds back as the next input.

## Method

iMOOE is built on a two-fold PDE invariance principle: (i) individual spatial operators (e.g., Laplacian, advection) remain invariant across domains, and (ii) their composition relationship is fixed for a given PDE system. The architecture mirrors operator splitting: K parallel neural operator experts (each a pluggable backbone like FNO, DeepONet, or OFormer with a binary mask selecting relevant spatial derivatives) are aggregated by a fusion network that conditions on PDE parameters and forcing terms. Training uses a frequency-enriched invariant learning objective combining four losses: a maximal prediction loss (sufficiency), a risk-equality loss that minimizes variance of per-environment prediction error (invariance, linearly scheduled), a frequency enrichment loss that weights FFT-space errors by wavenumber magnitude to combat spectral bias, and a mask diversity loss that encourages experts to select distinct derivative subsets. This plug-and-play design lets any existing neural operator serve as an expert backbone without structural modification.

## Results

iMOOE achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot OOD forecasting across all five simulated PDE systems (DR, NS, BG, SW, HC), with an average improvement of 40.21% on nMSE and 30.78% on fRMSE over the best baseline. On the most challenging DR system, iMOOE attains OOD nMSE of 4.23e-2 and fRMSE of 1.78e-3, outperforming the next-best CAPE (7.16e-2 / 2.21e-3). On Burgers dynamics, iMOOE achieves OOD nMSE of 1.08e-2, a 64.5% reduction over CAPE (3.04e-2). On real-world SST and SSE ocean dynamics, iMOOE also achieves the lowest mean nMSE (5.12e-1 and 1.52e-2 respectively) and fRMSE across all OOD test samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

iMOOE outperforms all six baselines on every PDE system for both nMSE and fRMSE in the zero-shot OOD setting. The closest competitor is CAPE (parameter-guided channel attention), which is competitive on Burgers (3.04e-2 vs. 1.08e-2 nMSE) and Shallow-Water (6.18e-5 vs. 3.02e-5) but degrades substantially on DR (7.16e-2 vs. 4.23e-2) and NS (3.56e-1 vs. 3.12e-1). GEPS (adaptive conditioning) and VCNeF (conditional neural fields) are the next-strongest baselines on DR, with OOD nMSE of 7.94e-2 and 7.84e-2 respectively, still roughly 1.9× worse than iMOOE. Meta-learning-based CoDA performs worst overall, failing entirely on Shallow-Water (n.a.) and achieving OOD nMSE above 0.6 on DR and NS. DPOT (denoising pretraining) shows moderate OOD performance but is consistently outperformed by iMOOE by 20–65% on nMSE. On real-world SST and SSE data, iMOOE achieves the lowest mean nMSE (5.12e-1, 1.52e-2) and fRMSE (3.44e-2, 2.78e-3), beating the best baselines DPOT and DyAd by 8–38%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of invariant learning (IRM, risk equality objectives from Krueger et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2021) and neural operator-based PDE solvers (FNO, DeepONet, OFormer, VCNeF). It departs from prior OOD PDE forecasting methods (CoDA, GEPS, CAPE, DPOT) that rely on meta-learning, parameter conditioning, or pretraining without explicitly formalizing the underlying physical invariance. The key conceptual departure is defining a two-fold PDE invariance principle (operator invariance + compositionality invariance) inspired by classical operator splitting (Glowinski et al., 2017), and then designing both an architecture (Mixture of Operator Experts) and a frequency-enriched risk equality loss to capture it, rather than treating OOD robustness as an emergent property of multi-domain training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Zero-shot OOD PDE forecasting is practically critical for applications like weather prediction, ocean dynamics, and industrial simulation where test-time adaptation is infeasible or prohibitively expensive. The plug-and-play compatibility with diverse neural operator backbones (FNO, DeepONet, VCNeF, OFormer) makes the approach broadly applicable without architectural modifications. However, the method is validated only on 2D regular-grid PDEs with a fixed K=2 in main experiments, and the authors acknowledge limitations in diversity of dynamical systems tested. Open questions include extension to irregular geometries, 3D systems, and whether the two-level invariance formalization generalizes to PDEs with genuinely unknown operator decompositions.
