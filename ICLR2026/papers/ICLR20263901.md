## Problem

Universal image restoration requires two capabilities that existing methods address in isolation: fine-grained degradation discrimination (to adapt restoration to the specific corruption) and rich texture generation (to recover photorealistic detail). Discrimination-focused methods produce over-smoothed outputs, while generation-prior methods (e.g., diffusion-based SR) over-generate inconsistent details on mildly degraded images, sacrificing fidelity. No prior approach seamlessly integrates both within a single model without architectural modifications.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality degraded RGB image (or its VAE latent) as input, along with the residual between the LQ and HQ images as an implicit conditioning signal. MAS-GLCM is computed directly from the LQ image across 9 angles (−180° to 180° in 45° steps) and 6 scales (distances −5 to 5), producing a content-agnostic degradation descriptor. The output is a high-quality restored image at the same spatial resolution as the input; for real-world SR, 512×512 patches are processed through Stable Diffusion 2's latent space.

## Method

BDG introduces MAS-GLCM, a multi-angle multi-scale Gray Level Co-occurrence Matrix that captures degradation characteristics independent of image content, demonstrated to outperform gradients, frequency, and instruction-based characterizations in fine-grained type and level classification. The core training reformulates the diffusion backward equation with three schedulable coefficients (α for residual, β for noise, δ for LQ injection), enabling a three-stage curriculum: (1) generation pre-training with α=δ=0 to absorb the generative prior; (2) a bridging stage with δ=0 where MAS-GLCM features are aligned to diffusion intermediate features via a mutual-information-style loss plus a degradation classification loss, injecting discrimination without disrupting generation; (3) restoration fine-tuning with all coefficients active, adding L1 fidelity loss and full-negative contrastive learning on frozen MAS-GLCM features to sharpen degradation sensitivity. This staged parameter scheduling lets the model retain rich texture generation while learning to modulate restoration strength according to the detected degradation level.

## Results

On the 5D all-in-one restoration task, BDG achieves SoTA PSNR across all five sub-tasks (deraining 34.75 dB, low-light 27.42 dB, desnowing 32.86 dB, dehazing 34.33 dB, deblurring 31.11 dB), surpassing the previous best (DCPT-PromptIR) by up to 2.46 dB. On real-world super-resolution (DIV2K-Val), BDG reaches 24.20 PSNR and 0.624 SSIM, outperforming the second-best diffusion method by 2.45 dB in PSNR while maintaining competitive perceptual scores (CLIPIQA 0.640). On mixed degradation (CDD11), BDG gains up to 4.28 dB over MoCE-IR in the haze+rain scenario.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BDG outperforms all 14 baselines on fidelity metrics (PSNR/SSIM) across every evaluated task. Against the closest diffusion-based all-in-one competitor DiffUIR (Zheng et al., 2024), BDG gains 3.72 dB in deraining and 1.94 dB in deblurring, attributed to its joint noise-and-residual prediction versus DiffUIR's residual-only prediction. Against the degradation-classification approach DCPT (Hu et al., 2025a), BDG improves deraining by 2.46 dB while also achieving higher SSIM (0.974 vs 0.921). In real-world super-resolution, BDG surpasses GAN-based methods (Real-ESRGAN, BSRGAN) by over 2 dB in PSNR and outperforms diffusion-based methods (StableSR, SUPIR, SeeSR, DiffBIR, ResShift) in full-reference metrics, though SeeSR and DiffBIR retain slight advantages in CLIPIQA (0.695/0.666 vs 0.640). On mixed degradation, BDG beats the previous SoTA MoCE-IR (Zamfir et al., 2025) by 4.28 dB in the H+R setting and 0.73 dB in L+H+R. In zero-shot real-world restoration (Table 3), BDG achieves the lowest PIQE for low-light (34.44) and competitive BRISQUE scores, edging out FoundIR and UniRestore on most degradation types.

## Relation to Prior Work

BDG sits at the intersection of two threads in universal image restoration: degradation-discrimination methods (AirNet, PromptIR, DCPT) that add explicit identification but over-smooth outputs, and generation-prior methods (StableSR, DiffBIR, SUPIR) that produce rich textures but sacrifice fidelity on mildly degraded inputs. Rather than adding a separate learned classifier or prompt mechanism, BDG introduces a hand-crafted statistical descriptor (MAS-GLCM) and aligns its features with the diffusion model's internal representations, effectively injecting discrimination into the generative process without architectural changes. The three-stage diffusion training (generation → bridging → restoration) reformulates the forward/backward equations of residual-conditioned latent diffusion (Liu et al., 2024; Zheng et al., 2024) by scheduling the noise coefficients α, β, δ to progressively shift the model from pure generation to fidelity-focused restoration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BDG addresses the core fidelity-perception tension in universal restoration: diffusion-based methods tend to hallucinate inconsistent detail on mildly degraded images, while fidelity-focused methods produce over-smoothed outputs. The demonstrated 2–4 dB PSNR gains over strong baselines across all-in-one, mixed-degradation, and real-world SR tasks suggest that explicitly bridging discrimination and generation is a viable path to resolving this tradeoff. However, the method's complexity (three-stage training, multiple auxiliary losses, a frozen feature extractor) limits immediate adoption, and MAS-GLCM's inability to capture color deviations or geometric transformations constrains its generality. Open questions include whether the three-stage paradigm can be collapsed into a single training schedule and whether learned (rather than hand-crafted) degradation descriptors could subsume MAS-GLCM while retaining its content-invariance property.
