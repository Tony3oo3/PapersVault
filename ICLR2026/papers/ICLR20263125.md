## Problem

While individual LLMs have been extensively aligned to reduce social bias, how latent biases accumulate or diminish across networked multi-agent systems remains largely unexplored. The prevailing assumption is that diverse agent personas and complex communication topologies naturally counteract bias, but this has not been empirically validated. The paper challenges this assumption, hypothesizing that structured MAS workflows instead act as resonant chambers that amplify minor stochastic biases into systemic polarization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263125/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a three-option demographic scenario (from 70 scenarios in Discrim-Eval-Open, where three protagonist profiles differ in age, gender, and race) along with the probability distributions and textual rationales of all predecessor agents, and must output a JSON-formatted probability distribution over the three options (strictly summing to 1) plus a free-text justification. The benchmark deliberately avoids binary yes/no formats that aligned models default to, forcing comparative judgments that surface latent preferences. At the system level, the output is a sequence of distributional metrics—Gini coefficient, variance, and entropy—tracked across agent layers to quantify bias amplification relative to the first agent's baseline.

## Method

The paper introduces Discrim-Eval-Open, a reformulation of Anthropic's Discrim-Eval implicit track into 70 three-option comparative scenarios (210 unique profiles with balanced demographic distributions), paired with a theoretical framework that models MAS as directed acyclic graphs and defines bias as the Gini-coefficient-based deviation of each agent's output distribution from uniformity. The core empirical contribution is a systematic ablation across architectural levers: agent specialization (four personas—Doctor, Lawyer, Engineer, Merchant—and four functions—Judger, Analyst, Reflector, Summarizer), three communication topologies (Spindle, Parallel, Fully-Connected), system depth (iterating fully-connected units up to four rounds), and model heterogeneity (mixing DeepSeek-R1 with GPT-4o-mini), all evaluated across eight LLMs. A key finding is the 'Trigger Vulnerability': injecting a single neutral sentence (e.g., 'Innovative achievements are often accomplished by young people') into an otherwise balanced scenario causes the first agent to lock in a demographic preference that subsequent agents progressively amplify, revealing that even objective external context can cascade into severe systemic polarization.

## Results

Across all 8 LLMs tested in a 4-agent sequential chain, the Gini coefficient (measuring output polarization) consistently increases from layer 1 to layer 4, confirming progressive bias amplification. For example, DeepSeek-R1's Gini rises from 0.2695 to 0.3838, GPT-4o-mini from 0.0990 to 0.1629, and Qwen-Max from 0.1401 to 0.2124. In the heterogeneous model comparison (Table 1), even mixing DeepSeek-R1 with GPT-4o-mini in a fully-connected topology yields relative Gini increasing from 1.26 to 1.44 over 4 iterations, intermediate between the two homogeneous systems. The 'Trigger Vulnerability' experiment shows that a single neutral sentence shifts Gemini-2.5-Pro's output from a balanced (0.4, 0.3, 0.3) to a polarized (0.7, 0.2, 0.1) distribution that further amplifies through subsequent agents.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare a proposed method against competing baselines; rather, it tests whether various MAS design strategies can mitigate the bias amplification observed in the simplest sequential chain. The key comparisons are: (1) identical roles vs. diverse personas (Doctor, Lawyer, Engineer, Merchant) vs. functional roles (Judger, Analyst, Reflector, Summarizer) vs. mixed configurations — all fail to prevent amplification, with the Reflector role providing only a temporary, partial dip before re-amplification; (2) Spindle vs. Parallel vs. Fully-Connected topologies — all show progressive bias accumulation, with Fully-Connected often exhibiting the most pronounced amplification due to richer information exchange; (3) homogeneous vs. heterogeneous model mixes (GPT-4o-mini only, DeepSeek-R1 only, hybrid) — the hybrid system's amplification rate is intermediate, confirming that model diversity alone is insufficient. The only configuration showing any mitigation is Gemini-1.5-pro in the sequential chain (Gini slightly decreases from 0.1493 to 0.1190), but this is an outlier among 8 models. The Trigger Vulnerability experiment further demonstrates that even a well-aligned system (balanced output without perturbation) becomes highly polarized when exposed to a single neutral contextual sentence, revealing that no tested architectural strategy provides robustness against external information triggers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the single-LLM bias mitigation literature (BBQ, BOLD, Discrim-Eval) by reframing bias as a systemic, emergent property of multi-agent interaction rather than a static deficiency of individual model weights. It builds conceptually on opinion polarization and echo chamber research (Raafat et al., Cinelli et al.) to formalize bias propagation through agent graphs, and reformulates Tamkin et al.'s Discrim-Eval into an open-ended, three-option comparative format (Discrim-Eval-Open) specifically designed to bypass the performative neutrality of aligned LLMs. The paper directly challenges the widely-held assumption in the MAS literature (Singh et al., Borah & Mihalcea, Xu et al.) that structural diversity and specialized roles naturally counteract bias, positioning its contribution as a diagnostic baseline for a problem the field has largely overlooked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that architectural sophistication consistently amplifies rather than mitigates bias challenges a core design assumption underlying much of the MAS engineering literature, with direct implications for deploying these systems in high-stakes domains like healthcare, hiring, and legal adjudication. The 'Trigger Vulnerability' result—that a single neutral sentence injected as external context can cascade into severe polarization—exposes a critical fragility in RAG-augmented MAS that practitioners have not yet addressed. Key open questions remain: effective mitigation strategies (contrarian agents, polarization-aware training), whether the same amplification mechanism governs hallucination and groupthink, and how findings generalize to intersectional biases and larger, more heterogeneous agent populations. The paper explicitly positions itself as diagnostic rather than prescriptive, leaving the design of robust MAS architectures as a critical next step.
