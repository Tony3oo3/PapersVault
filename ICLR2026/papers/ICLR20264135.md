## Problem

Existing video summarization architectures rely on static or modality-agnostic fusion strategies that treat all modalities uniformly or prioritize visual cues, failing to account for the dynamic, frame-dependent shifts in which modality is most informative. Additionally, no large-scale benchmark provides all three modalities (visual, text, audio) with reliable importance annotations, leaving multimodal video summarization underexplored due to data scarcity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264135/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes three temporally aligned feature sequences resampled to N one-second steps: CLIP-encoded frames (768-d), RoBERTa [CLS] embeddings from time-stamped transcripts broadcast across their duration (with a default vector for gaps), and AST [CLS] embeddings from 10-second centered audio windows. All are linearly projected to a shared 128-d embedding space. The output is a per-frame importance score in [0,1]; the final summary is constructed by segmenting the video into shots via Kernel Temporal Segmentation, scoring each shot by mean frame score, and selecting a subset under a fixed length budget (e.g., 15%) formulated as a 0/1 knapsack problem.

## Method

TripleSumm interleaves two core blocks across L=2 layers (each with P=2 temporal and Q=2 fusion blocks). The Multi-scale Temporal Block applies windowed self-attention with progressively expanding windows (5→15→45→N) shared across all modalities, capturing fine-grained local dependencies first and long-range global context later at O(w·N) cost. The Cross-modal Fusion Block uses a neutral fusion token (the average of all three modality embeddings) as a single query that attends over the three modality-specific tokens at each time step, learning frame-level adaptive weights without bias toward any modality. This separation of temporal modeling (MST) and inter-modal integration (CMF) allows each to focus on its orthogonal role. The model is trained with L2 loss on frame-level importance scores and is remarkably compact at 1.37M parameters and 0.97 GFLOPs, outperforming all baselines by a large margin on four benchmarks including the newly introduced MoSu dataset (52,678 trimodal videos from YouTube-8M with Most Replayed ground truth).

## Results

On the MoSu benchmark (52,678 videos), TripleSumm achieves state-of-the-art performance with Kendall's τ = 0.351, Spearman's ρ = 0.472, mAP50 = 74.72, and mAP15 = 44.42, surpassing the strongest multimodal baseline CFSum (τ = 0.277, ρ = 0.374) by a substantial margin. The model is also remarkably efficient, requiring only 1.37M parameters and 0.97 GFLOPs—roughly 14× fewer parameters and 9× fewer FLOPs than CFSum (19.83M, 8.52G). On Mr. HiSum, TripleSumm (Full) reaches τ = 0.258 and mAP50 = 70.72, and on SumMe/TVSum it leads all baselines under both TV and TVT protocols (e.g., τ = 0.282 on SumMe TVT with MoSu pre-training).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TripleSumm outperforms all 19 baselines on every metric across all four benchmarks. Against the strongest multimodal competitor CFSum (ICASSP 2025), TripleSumm gains +0.074 in τ and +0.098 in ρ on MoSu, while using 14× fewer parameters (1.37M vs. 19.83M) and 9× fewer FLOPs. The best unimodal baseline CSTA (CVPR 2024) achieves τ = 0.291, still 0.060 below TripleSumm, confirming that adaptive trimodal fusion provides a clear advantage over visual-only attention. Among bimodal methods, UMT (visual+audio, τ = 0.239) and SSPVS (visual+text, τ = 0.190) are both substantially outperformed, and the visual-only baselines VASNet and PGL-SUM (τ ≈ 0.151) trail by more than 0.20 in τ. Notably, even TripleSumm's visual-only variant (Ours(Visual), τ = 0.187 on Mr. HiSum) surpasses all prior unimodal baselines, validating the Multi-scale Temporal block independently. On the zero-shot long-video evaluation (70-min average), TripleSumm leads with τ = 0.128 vs. CSTA's 0.083, demonstrating superior generalization to unseen domains and longer content.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of video summarization research: (1) temporal modeling architectures (RNNs → self-attention → windowed/hierarchical attention) and (2) the gradual incorporation of non-visual modalities (text via CLIP-It/A2Summ, audio via Joint-VA/UMT, and trimodal via CFSum). It departs from prior multimodal summarization work primarily in its fusion strategy: rather than treating modalities uniformly or using a single modality as query in cross-attention, TripleSumm introduces a neutral fusion token that adaptively re-weights all three modalities per frame. The MoSu dataset extends the 'Most Replayed' annotation paradigm from Mr. HiSum by adding text and audio streams, filling a gap the authors identify as a key bottleneck for multimodal summarization research.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MoSu dataset (52,678 videos, ~4,000 hours, all three modalities) addresses a genuine data scarcity problem that has constrained multimodal video summarization research, and the zero-shot long-form evaluation (70+ minute videos) pushes the field toward more realistic settings. The model's extreme parameter efficiency (1.37M params, 0.97 GFLOPs) while outperforming models 10–80× larger suggests that adaptive modality weighting, not model scale, is the key driver of summarization quality. Open questions include whether the standard three-step pipeline (scoring → KTS segmentation → knapsack selection) can be replaced by an end-to-end trainable formulation, and whether the approach generalizes to even longer or more semantically complex content beyond the 50-video long-form test set.
