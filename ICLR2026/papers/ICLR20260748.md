## Problem

Existing KGQA systems cannot be deployed on mobile devices due to two critical limitations: they require tuning billions of parameters (or repeated LLM invocations causing high latency), and they cannot adapt to continuously evolving KG databases without cloud-based retraining that compromises user data privacy. No prior system supports on-device training while handling the distribution shifts caused by new user data accumulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260748/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural language query and a knowledge graph database (triples over entities and relations, e.g., Freebase-based WebQSP or CWQ) as input. The query and relation names are first embedded by a frozen embedding model (e.g., LMsr, GTE-large, or GTE-Qwen2-1.5B) into high-dimensional floating-point vectors, which are then compressed into compact binary hash codes (64–256 bits). The output is a natural language answer generated by an on-device LLM (0.5B–14B, 4-bit quantized), grounded in retrieved shortest reasoning paths between question entities and predicted answer candidates in the graph.

## Method

MobileKGQA is a three-phase on-device pipeline. (1) A hashing module (MLP + batch normalization + sign) compresses LLM embeddings into binary hash codes by maximizing mutual information, trained with a log-ratio loss that preserves pairwise cosine similarity; a formal theorem shows this is optimal under an independence assumption on embedding norms. (2) A GNN-based reasoning module (ReaRev architecture) consumes the hash codes and the graph to score all nodes as answer candidates, after which shortest paths linking question entities to top candidates are extracted and fed to a frozen on-device LLM for final answer generation. (3) An adaptation phase generates supervised annotations for newly accumulated data via a five-step sequential process (sample paths → verbalize triples → merge into a sentence → mask the answer with a type placeholder → prompt the LLM to generate a question), which requires far fewer output tokens than CoT or ReasoningLM baselines and enables on-device fine-tuning of the hashing and reasoning modules without data leakage. This architecture reduces training computation to 7.2% and parameters to 9% of the SOTA (GNN-RAG) while achieving empirically indistinguishable performance and superior adaptability under distribution shift.

## Results

On WebQSP with Gemma 2 (2.6B, 4-bit), MobileKGQA achieves Hit 79.5 and F1 67.0, matching the SOTA GNN-RAG (Hit 79.5–80.0, F1 65.6–66.9) while using only 0.136M parameters (9% of GNN-RAG) and 0.6 PFLOPs (7.2% of GNN-RAG's 8.3 PFLOPs). On the NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano edge platform, MobileKGQA delivers 20.3% higher Hit (74.2 vs. 61.7) and 15.8% higher F1 (62.9 vs. 54.3) than GNN-RAG while consuming only 30.4% of the energy (11.8 Wh vs. 28.7 Wh at 7W). On CWQ, MobileKGQA achieves Hit 54.4 and F1 40.8 with Gemma 2, again matching GNN-RAG (Hit 54.4, F1 40.7). Under distribution shift, MobileKGQA outperforms all baselines, reaching a total Hit of 48.8 on WebQSP and 41.0 on CWQ after two adaptation rounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MobileKGQA matches or marginally outperforms the SOTA lightweight model GNN-RAG across all tested LLMs: on WebQSP the average gap is only 0.46 Hit and 0.16 F1, and on CWQ the gap shrinks to 0.075 in both metrics. Against ICL-based methods (StructGPT, ToG), MobileKGQA achieves substantially higher accuracy (e.g., Hit 79.5 vs. 53.8 for StructGPT and 31.6 for ToG with Gemma 2) while reducing inference time from 53.7 s to 0.37 s. Compared to SubgraphRAG, MobileKGQA improves Hit by 38.5 points (79.5 vs. 41.0) and F1 by 54.7 points (65.7 vs. 12.3) with Gemma 2. Against IR-based baselines ReaRev and NuTrea, MobileKGQA achieves higher Hit (79.5 vs. 70.9 and 72.7) with comparable latency. For annotation generation, MobileKGQA's stepwise method outperforms both ReasoningLM (ROUGE-L 42.8 vs. 17.0) and CoT (ROUGE-L 42.8 vs. 32.3) while using only 21% of CoT's tokens. Under distribution shift, MobileKGQA is the only method that maintains or improves performance on the original domain after adaptation (S1(PA) ≥ S1), outperforming GNN-RAG by 1.8 Hit on WebQSP total and 1.9 Hit on CWQ total.

## Relation to Prior Work

MobileKGQA builds on the ReaRev GNN architecture for its reasoning module and repurposes the log-ratio loss from deep metric learning (Kim et al., 2019) to train a hashing module that compresses LLM embeddings into binary codes. It departs from the current SOTA lightweight KGQA model GNN-RAG (Mavromatis & Karypis, 2025) by replacing high-dimensional continuous embeddings with fixed-length binary hash codes, eliminating the need to regenerate embeddings during training. The paper positions itself as the first system to combine on-device trainability with adaptation to evolving knowledge graphs, filling a gap identified in Table 1 where no prior model satisfies both low computational cost and adaptability simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes a practical pathway for privacy-preserving, on-device KGQA that can continuously adapt as user data accumulates—addressing a real deployment barrier that has kept KGQA systems server-bound. The finding that 256-bit binary hashes preserve retrieval quality nearly identically to 768-dimensional float embeddings opens the door to aggressive embedding compression in other retrieval-augmented settings. Open questions include scalability to much larger knowledge graphs (beyond Freebase-scale), robustness to more complex or adversarial distribution shifts, and whether the stepwise annotation generation generalizes to domains where reasoning paths are longer or more ambiguous.
