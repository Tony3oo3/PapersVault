## Problem

Existing methods for expanding reasoning steps in mathematical chain-of-thought data rely on more powerful external models or computationally expensive search algorithms (e.g., MCTS), creating a dependency cycle and high inference costs. Moreover, these approaches often generate entirely new reasoning chains rather than building upon existing human-verified steps, risking the introduction of errors. The paper asks whether a more efficient, reliable method can expand reasoning steps while preserving the validity of existing solutions.

## Input / Output

The FIM model receives a mathematical question Q, a prefix P (all steps before a target position), and a suffix S (all steps after that position), formatted with special tokens <|fim_prefix|>, <|fim_suffix|>, <|fim_middle|> in PSM order. It outputs the missing intermediate step(s) M that bridge P and S. During the expansion phase, the model is run between every pair of consecutive steps in an existing solution; generated steps with sequence similarity above η=0.8 to the adjacent original step are discarded as redundant before insertion.

## Method

MathFimer adapts the Fill-in-the-Middle paradigm from code completion to mathematical reasoning. Training data (NuminaMath-FIM, 2.5M samples) is constructed by decomposing NuminaMath-CoT solutions into individual steps, then randomly selecting one step as the held-out middle while treating all preceding and succeeding steps as prefix and suffix (three random samplings per problem). A math-specialized base model (Qwen2.5-Math-7B) is fine-tuned via SFT with loss computed only on the middle tokens, yielding MathFimer-7B. At inference time, the model is applied between each pair of consecutive steps in target datasets (GSM8K+MATH, MathInstruct-CoT, MetaMathQA, etc.) to generate supplementary intermediate steps, which are inserted into the original chain after a similarity-based filter removes near-duplicates. The key distinction from prior step-expansion work is that MathFimer preserves the original solution structure and only fills gaps, rather than regenerating entire reasoning chains, and it operates with a modest 7B (or even 1.5B) model without requiring external LLMs or search-based exploration.

## Results

MathFimer consistently improves mathematical reasoning across four base models (Meta-Llama-3.1-8B/70B, Qwen2.5-Math-7B/72B) and multiple training datasets. The largest single-benchmark gains include +7.43% on GSM8K (MathInstruct-CoT, Llama-3.1-8B) and +8.86% on MATH (MetaMathQA, Qwen2.5-Math-7B). Average accuracy improvements range from +0.27% to +4.65% depending on model and dataset, with the most consistent gains observed on competition-level benchmarks (OlympiadBench-EN: up to +5.93%). Iterative expansion (3 rounds) further amplifies gains, reaching +15.54% on GSM8K for MI-CoT with Llama-3.1-8B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MathFimer against four reasoning-enhancement methods in Table 5 (on MetaMathQA): DPO (+0.20 MATH), Step-DPO (+0.30 GSM8K, +1.00 MATH), RFT (+1.10 GSM8K, +1.30 MATH), and PPO (+3.90 GSM8K, +2.70 MATH). MathFimer achieves +2.43 GSM8K and +2.38 MATH, outperforming Step-DPO and RFT on both benchmarks, though PPO shows slightly larger gains on GSM8K (+3.90 vs +2.43). The authors note these comparisons use different base models (Qwen2-7B, Mistral-7B vs Meta-Llama-3.1-8B), limiting direct comparability, and argue the methods are complementary rather than competitive. In a more controlled prompt-based comparison (Table 4), MathFimer-1.5B substantially outperforms Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct zero-shot prompting on GSM8K (73.09% vs 68.76%) and MATH (22.76% vs 18.88%), demonstrating that training a specialized FIM model is more effective than relying on a general-purpose LLM for step expansion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Fill-in-the-Middle (FIM) paradigm from code completion (Bavarian et al., 2022) to mathematical reasoning chains, repurposing the prefix-suffix-middle training format to reconstruct missing intermediate steps in CoT solutions. It departs from prior step-expansion approaches that rely on stronger external models or search algorithms (MCTS, tree search) by instead training a dedicated FIM model on decomposed CoT data, preserving the original solution structure rather than generating entirely new reasoning chains. The work sits at the intersection of data augmentation for CoT training and the broader line of research on how reasoning step granularity affects LLM performance (Jin et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MathFimer offers a practical, low-cost pipeline for enhancing existing mathematical reasoning datasets without requiring more powerful models or expensive inference, making it accessible for practitioners working with limited compute. The finding that a 1.5B FIM model performs nearly identically to a 72B one suggests step expansion is a relatively structured task not bottlenecked by model capacity, which has implications for efficient data curation. However, open questions remain about error accumulation in iterative expansion, applicability to already-detailed reasoning traces (e.g., R1-style), and non-linear reasoning structures. The demonstrated OOD generalization (math-trained model improving on general reasoning benchmarks) hints at a broader principle that richer intermediate reasoning transfers across domains, though this needs further validation.
