## Problem

The state-of-the-art MCTS abstraction algorithm OGA-UCT requires state-action pairs to have identical immediate rewards to be grouped, a rigid condition that severely limits the number of abstractions detectable and thus the sample efficiency gains. Relaxing this via threshold-based methods like (εa,εt)-OGA introduces tuning parameters and can group non-value-equivalent nodes, potentially harming convergence. The paper addresses the gap of detecting more abstractions in deterministic MDPs while preserving the losslessness guarantee (grouping only nodes with provably known value differences).

## Input / Output

The method operates on a finite MDP defined by its state set, transition function, and reward function, given a fixed MCTS iteration budget. It produces an action selection via UCT while maintaining, alongside the search tree, a KVDA abstraction that partitions states and state-action pairs into equivalence classes each annotated with a difference function mapping every pair of grouped nodes to their exact Q* or V* difference. Unlike standard abstractions that only track aggregate visit and return counts per abstract node, KVDA-UCT stores a per-node offset relative to a designated representative, so that each original node can recover its own value by subtracting its tracked difference from the abstract node's aggregate statistics.

## Method

KVDA extends the ASAP framework by removing the immediate-reward equality constraint (Fa = 0) from the state-action pair abstraction condition, so that two state-action pairs are grouped if and only if their abstract successor distributions match. A difference function da is then computed for each grouped pair as the reward difference plus a weighted sum of the successor-state difference function ds, and a state abstraction is formed only when all action mappings between two states share the same value difference d. When the abstraction is used in the UCT tree policy, each node's value is recovered by subtracting its da offset from the abstract node's aggregate value, and backups add the offset-adjusted return to the abstract statistics. The construction is proven sound (da and ds equal the true Q*/V* differences at convergence) and introduces no additional hyperparameters. In the deterministic setting, this guarantees that KVDA-UCT never visits a state-action pair that is provably suboptimal relative to its abstract group, a property OGA-UCT lacks.

## Results

On 20 deterministic MDP environments with 1000 MCTS iterations, KVDA-UCT achieves the best or near-best parameter-optimized return in 18 out of 20 domains, outperforming OGA-UCT in 16 of 20. The largest gains appear in Manufacturer (−1158.2 vs. −1255.6 for OGA-UCT), Tamarisk (−185.6 vs. −214.5), Push Your Luck (137.9 vs. 125.1), and Wildfire (−179.9 vs. −195.6). KVDA-UCT also matches or exceeds the best parameter-optimized (εa,0)-OGA across all environments without introducing any additional hyperparameter, and its normalized pairings score (C=4) ranks first overall among all tested agent configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OGA-UCT (Anand et al., 2016), KVDA-UCT achieves strictly better returns in 16 of 20 deterministic environments at 1000 iterations, with the largest margins in Manufacturer (≈97 points), Tamarisk (≈29 points), Push Your Luck (≈13 points), and Wildfire (≈16 points). The two environments where OGA-UCT edges out KVDA-UCT (Othello by 1.6 points, Saving by 0.6 points) fall within confidence intervals. Against the parameter-optimized (εa,0)-OGA (Schmöcker et al., 2025d), KVDA-UCT matches or exceeds the best εa setting in 17 of 20 environments without requiring any domain-specific εa tuning, which is a practical advantage since (εa,0)-OGA needs per-environment reward-scale calibration. In the stochastic setting, εt-KVDA does not consistently outperform (εa,εt)-OGA: it wins clearly in Manufacturer (−863.3 vs. −1141.0 at 1000 iters) and Sailing Wind, but loses in Tamarisk (−563.6 vs. −532.9), and the two methods are statistically indistinguishable in most other stochastic domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OGA-UCT/ASAP lineage of on-the-go state-action pair abstractions for MCTS by replacing the strict value-equivalence condition with a 'known value difference' criterion. Where ASAP and OGA-UCT require identical immediate rewards and successor distributions, and (εa,εt)-OGA relaxes this with tunable error thresholds, KVDA groups state-action pairs whose Q* values differ by a computable constant inferred from the search graph's reward structure. The integration into UCT mirrors the (εa,εt)-OGA pipeline but tracks per-node value offsets relative to an abstract node's representative, enabling lossless aggregation without any free parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KVDA-UCT provides a parameter-free, lossless abstraction method for deterministic MDPs that consistently matches or exceeds parameter-optimized (εa,0)-OGA across 20 environments, with abstraction rates more than doubling in domains like SysAdmin and Wildfire. Two key limitations remain open: the stochastic extension εt-KVDA underperforms (εa,εt)-OGA in most stochastic environments due to increased faulty abstractions, and the framework is restricted to single-player MDPs where a unique V* value exists, leaving multi-player settings (with multiple equilibria) unaddressed. For practitioners using MCTS in game development or planning, the parameter-free nature and guaranteed losslessness in deterministic settings make this a practical drop-in replacement for OGA-UCT.
