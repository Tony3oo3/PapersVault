## Problem

In graph-based hierarchical RL for long-horizon goal-conditioned tasks, conventional hindsight relabeling (HER) at the high level treats intermediate states from failed subgoal attempts as if they were the intended subgoals, causing the high-level policy to repeatedly select unreliable subgoals, waste decision steps, and spread credit assignment over too many steps. This prevents effective learning when goals are distant and rewards are sparse. The paper asks how high-level planning can be made reliable by explicitly separating reachable from unreachable subgoals rather than synthesizing false successes.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a continuous state observation (29-Dof for AntMaze, 17-Dof for Reacher) and a target goal position in a known goal space, and produces continuous actions (8-Dof or 7-Dof) while internally selecting subgoals and computing Dijkstra waypoint paths over a grid-structured landmark graph. A key structural constraint is that episodes terminate immediately upon subgoal failure, and the high-level replay buffer stores three distinct transition types—success, stop-on-failure, and partial-success—rather than the single relabeled transition used by HER. The reachability threshold λ (0.5 for AntMaze, 0.25 for Reacher) determines whether a subgoal is considered reached and is shared with the low-level reward definition.

## Method

SSE replaces high-level HER with Frontier Experience Replay (FER), which stores three transition types: success transitions with full cumulative return, stop-on-failure transitions with zero return and a terminal state that truncates the episode, and partial-success transitions recording the last reliably reached waypoint with its accumulated return. This frontier delineation teaches the high-level policy to avoid unreliable subgoals and reduces unnecessary high-level decisions. A decoupled exploration policy π^exp, separate from the exploitation policy π^h, targets underexplored goal-space regions using either grid-based visitation counts or Random Network Distillation prediction error, and is gradually mixed with π^h via a decaying exploration ratio. Failure-aware path refinement scales Dijkstra edge costs by a per-node failure ratio (grid-based count or a learned binary cross-entropy predictor), steering the planner away from unreliable transitions while preserving reachability. The framework uses TD3 for off-policy training with a hybrid edge distance combining the low-level Q-value and Euclidean distance, and a grid-based landmark construction inspired by BEAG.

## Results

SSE (Grid) and SSE (Model) consistently achieve the highest success rates across all 9 long-horizon benchmarks, with SSE (Grid) converging faster due to lower computational overhead. On AntKeyChest, SSE reaches 60% success in 1,658 episodes (18.13 s/episode), while BEAG (57.29 s/episode) and NGTE (119.07 s/episode) fail to reach this threshold entirely. On AntMazeBottleneck, SSE converges in 432 episodes versus 725.6 for NGTE, with per-episode time of 5.94 s versus 12.65 s. In complex multi-objective tasks (AntDoubleKeyChest, ReacherWallDoubleGoal), all baselines largely fail while SSE solves them using only 2–3 high-level decisions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSE outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluated environment. Against the closest graph-based competitors, BEAG and NGTE, SSE achieves faster convergence and is the only method to solve AntKeyChest and AntDoubleKeyChest, where both baselines fail entirely under fixed-goal settings. DHRL and HIGL, which rely on Farthest Point Sampling for landmark selection, show reasonable performance in simple mazes (U-maze, π-maze) but degrade sharply in bottleneck and multi-objective tasks due to their inability to handle failure-prone regions. HIRO and HRAC, conventional HRL methods using hindsight relabeling or adjacency penalties, exhibit limited progress in most environments, particularly under random-goal conditions where their fixed-frequency subgoal selection proves insufficient. PIG, which integrates graph structures into imitation learning, also struggles with fixed-goal tasks due to limited exploration. The computational advantage of SSE is most pronounced in long-horizon tasks: on AntKeyChest, SSE's per-episode time (18.13 s) is 3.2× lower than BEAG and 6.6× lower than NGTE, attributable to early episode termination on subgoal failure.

## Relation to Prior Work

SSE sits at the intersection of graph-based hierarchical RL (DHRL, NGTE, HIGL) and goal-conditioned RL with hindsight relabeling (HIRO, HER). It builds directly on BEAG's grid-based landmark construction for structured goal-space partitioning and on Random Network Distillation for model-based novelty detection in the exploration policy. The key conceptual departure is from the near-universal practice of applying HER at both the high and low levels: SSE argues that high-level HER relabeling corrupts subgoal reliability by treating every intermediate state as a valid subgoal, and instead enforces strict single-step subgoal completion via Frontier Experience Replay, which records failure and partial-success transitions to delineate the reachability boundary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that conventional HER at the high level actively prevents learning in complex multi-goal tasks (complete failure in ablations) challenges a widely adopted assumption in GCRL and HRL, suggesting that the 'relabel everything' philosophy needs level-specific treatment. SSE's strict execution principle, combined with failure-aware path refinement, offers a practical recipe for reducing high-level decision horizons to near-constant steps, which could simplify planning in robotic navigation and manipulation. Open questions include whether the grid-based approach scales to genuinely high-dimensional or continuous goal spaces beyond 3D, whether the strict execution principle transfers to non-graph-based HRL architectures, and how the method behaves in multi-agent or partially observable settings.
