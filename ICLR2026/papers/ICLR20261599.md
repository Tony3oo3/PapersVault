## Problem

On-policy RLVR frameworks like GRPO suffer from two compounding inefficiencies: homogeneous rewards (when all G responses in a group are correct or incorrect, advantage estimates collapse to zero, yielding no useful gradient) and experience waste (each rollout group is consumed once and discarded, so difficult samples that the model eventually learns to solve are never revisited). This paper asks how to systematically reuse stale off-policy experience in LLM post-training without destabilizing the policy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts (math competition problems, Countdown Number Game instances with 3–4 source numbers, or geometry diagrams paired with natural-language questions) and produces free-form reasoning chains verified by a deterministic binary reward function (correct/incorrect). Each prompt generates a group of G=8 responses during rollout, and the training batch is assembled from three filtered subsets of these groups rather than used in a single pass. Response lengths are capped at 8192 tokens for math and 4096 for the mini-test, with prompt lengths up to 2048 tokens.

## Method

BAPO constructs each training batch from three complementary sources: (1) filtered fresh samples from a rollout policy delayed by v steps, excluding zero-variance groups; (2) re-evaluated historically difficult samples, where prompts that previously yielded near-zero accuracy are regenerated every m steps under the current policy and retained only if they show measurable improvement; and (3) reused high-quality trajectories from a FIFO buffer restricted to the three most recent steps, with adaptive difficulty thresholds (c₂, c₃) that linearly shift from easier to harder instances as global performance improves. The theoretical contribution proves a policy-improvement lower bound via Kantorovich-Rubenstein duality, showing that under bounded total-variation distance between the current and reference policies, the adaptive batch construction guarantees finite, positive improvement constants K₁, K₂, K₃ for each subset. This design maintains non-homogeneous intra-group rewards and a balanced difficulty distribution at every training step, resolving both the gradient-vanishing and experience-waste problems simultaneously.

## Results

BAPO achieves an average 12.5% accuracy improvement over GRPO across mathematics, planning, and visual geometry reasoning tasks. On the mathematics benchmark (DeepSeek R1 Distill Qwen 1.5B), BAPO reaches 56.01% average accuracy (AIME24: 38.54, AMC: 72.74, MATH500: 89.18), outperforming all six baselines. On planning (Qwen2.5 Math 1.5B), BAPO scores 60.25% average vs. GRPO's 49.41%, and on visual geometry (Qwen2.5 VL 3B), BAPO achieves 43.22% vs. GRPO's 39.78%. Crucially, BAPO resolves 40.7% of problems that the base model consistently fails to solve (0/8 accuracy), compared to only 19% for GRPO after 3 epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BAPO outperforms all six baselines across every task. Against the strongest on-policy baseline DAPO, BAPO leads by +1.81 on math (56.01 vs. 54.20), +2.03 on planning 1.5B (60.25 vs. 58.22), and +1.38 on geometry 7B (45.33 vs. 43.95), while requiring only 733k rollouts versus DAPO's 1921k (~2.6× fewer). Against the on-policy GRPO baseline, BAPO gains +4.43 on math, +10.84 on planning 1.5B, and +3.44 on geometry 3B. Among off-policy baselines, BAPO surpasses GRPO(v=5) by +4.44, RePO by +5.47, and Remix-GRPO by +5.48 on the math benchmark, indicating that simple delay or fixed-ratio replay strategies are insufficient compared to BAPO's difficulty-aware adaptive batch construction. On the 7B planning task, BAPO (68.13) essentially ties DAPO (68.09) while using far fewer rollouts, and on geometry 7B it leads DAPO by +1.38.

## Relation to Prior Work

BAPO extends the on-policy RLVR lineage (GRPO, DAPO, GSPO) by introducing a difficulty-aware off-policy batch construction mechanism, drawing on classical experience-replay and importance-sampling ideas from traditional RL. It departs from recent off-policy RLVR methods (RePO, ReMix, DOTS, ARPO) that naively mix buffer and online samples or use fixed retrieval ratios, by instead (1) periodically re-evaluating historically difficult prompts with the current policy to detect emergent solvability, and (2) reusing high-quality trajectories only within a narrow, dynamically shifting accuracy window. The theoretical contribution builds on Mroueh et al.'s policy-improvement lower bound for GRPO, extending it to the off-policy setting with TV-distance constraints across three distinct sample sources.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12.5% average improvement over GRPO across mathematics, planning, and visual geometry—achieved at comparable training speed and with fewer rollouts than DAPO—makes off-policy RLVR practically competitive for LLM post-training. The 'Mini-test' ablation (parameter-free BAPO still outperforms GRPO) suggests the structural insight of re-evaluating hard samples, rather than specific threshold tuning, is the active ingredient, which lowers the adoption barrier. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to MoE architectures and agentic RL settings where reward sparsity and multi-step credit assignment complicate the binary-reward assumptions underlying the theoretical guarantee.
