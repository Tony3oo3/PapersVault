## Problem

Subject-driven image generation faces a stark efficiency-fidelity trade-off: test-time fine-tuning methods (e.g., DreamBooth) require hundreds of iterations per subject, while feed-forward diffusion-based approaches inherit the slow iterative denoising of their backbones. Visual Autoregressive (VAR) models offer fast next-scale sampling and strong generative quality, yet their potential for feed-forward, subject-driven personalization remains largely unexplored, leaving a gap between the speed of autoregressive generation and the controllability needed for subject preservation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image of a target subject (preprocessed by segmenting the subject from its background via Qwen2.5-VL + GroundingDINO, cropping to the bounding box, and replacing the background with white) together with a free-form text prompt describing the desired scene. It produces a single generated image at 256×256 (EchoGen-0.1B) or 1024×1024 (EchoGen-2B) depicting the subject faithfully re-rendered in the prompted scene. At inference, two guidance scales (γ_t for text, γ_I for subject) allow the user to dynamically trade off between prompt adherence and subject fidelity without retraining.

## Method

EchoGen builds on the Infinity VAR backbone (next-scale coarse-to-fine token prediction) and introduces a dual-path subject injection mechanism within a new EchoGen block. The semantic path extracts patch-level DINOv2 embeddings injected via decoupled cross-attention (with frozen text projectors and trainable semantic K/V projectors) for structural and stylistic coherence, while a global DINOv2 token is prepended to the sequence and infused through Adaptive LayerNorm for holistic guidance. The content path extracts low-level features from the FLUX.1-dev VAE and integrates them via a multi-modal attention module with a carefully designed causal mask that lets generated tokens attend to reference tokens but prevents the reverse, preserving the autoregressive trajectory. Training is parameter-efficient: the pretrained Infinity backbone is frozen and only the new attention projectors and FFN modules are updated, using ~640K synthetic (text, reference, target) triplets. A subject-text classifier-free guidance scheme independently drops text and image conditions during training, enabling flexible inference-time control over the subject–prompt balance.

## Results

On DreamBench, EchoGen-2B achieves the best subject fidelity among all compared methods with DINO 0.755 and CLIP-I 0.835, while maintaining competitive text alignment (CLIP-T 0.325). The smaller EchoGen-0.1B variant reaches DINO 0.675 and CLIP-I 0.806 in just 0.5s. Critically, EchoGen-2B generates 1024×1024 images in 5.2s on a single H20 GPU, compared to 16.9–93.4s for diffusion-based baselines, representing a 3–18× speedup. In human evaluation, EchoGen-2B is preferred for subject fidelity (0.37) and photorealism (0.34) over all baselines, and is competitive on text alignment (0.30 vs. EasyControl's 0.31).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EchoGen-2B outperforms all feed-forward diffusion baselines on subject fidelity: it leads IP-Adapter (SDXL) by +0.142 DINO and +0.025 CLIP-I, OminiControl by +0.071 DINO, and EasyControl by +0.103 DINO, while matching EasyControl's CLIP-T (0.325). Against the only other VAR-based method, AR-Booth, EchoGen-2B improves DINO from 0.750 to 0.755, CLIP-I from 0.808 to 0.835, and CLIP-T from 0.269 to 0.325, while reducing inference latency from 2.8 hours to 5.2 seconds. Among test-time fine-tuning methods, EchoGen-2B surpasses DreamBooth by +0.087 DINO and +0.032 CLIP-I without requiring per-subject optimization. The unified-generation model OmniGen is outperformed on all three metrics (DINO +0.062, CLIP-I +0.034, CLIP-T +0.010) with 18× lower latency. In human evaluation, EchoGen-2B is the most preferred method for subject fidelity (37% preference) and photorealism (34%), while EasyControl edges it slightly on text alignment (31% vs. 30%).

## Relation to Prior Work

EchoGen builds directly on the Infinity VAR model (Han et al., 2025) and its next-scale prediction paradigm (Tian et al., 2024), adapting the autoregressive generation framework to the subject-driven personalization task that has been dominated by diffusion-based methods (DreamBooth, IP-Adapter, OminiControl, EasyControl). The dual-path injection design borrows the decoupled cross-attention mechanism from Multi-Concept Customization (Kumari et al., 2023) for semantic conditioning and introduces a novel multi-modal attention with a causal mask for content-feature injection, both tailored to the autoregressive token-generation setting. The paper departs from the diffusion-based feed-forward paradigm by replacing iterative denoising with sequential next-scale token prediction, while retaining the parameter-efficient fine-tuning philosophy (freezing the backbone, training only injection modules) common to LoRA-based adapters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that the VAR paradigm, previously underexplored for controllable subject-driven generation, can match or exceed diffusion-based feed-forward methods in subject fidelity (DINO 0.755 vs. 0.684 best baseline) while reducing inference latency from 16–27 s to 5.2 s, opening a viable alternative to the diffusion-centric landscape. Key limitations include dependence on the Infinity-2B backbone's generative ceiling (struggles with intricate structures and text rendering) and the low-resolution input of DINOv2 (224×224), which constrains fine-grained semantic capture. An open question is whether migrating the dual-path injection architecture to a larger VAR foundation (e.g., Infinity-8B) would close the remaining quality gap with top-tier diffusion models like FLUX or SD3.
