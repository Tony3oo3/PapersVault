## Problem

Existing multimodal dataset distillation methods are optimization-based, requiring repeated full-dataset training and joint optimization of image pixels and text features, which makes them computationally expensive, architecture-dependent, and poorly generalizable across backbones. No learning-free approach existed for multimodal settings, and naively extending image-only learning-free distillation fails because VAE-based image encoders produce embeddings misaligned with text encoders.

## Input / Output

The method takes a large-scale image-text dataset (e.g., Flickr30K with 29K images, MS-COCO with 113K images, each paired with five captions) along with frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 encoders and a pretrained unCLIP decoder. It produces a compact synthetic dataset of M image-text pairs (M ≪ N, e.g., 100 or 300 pairs), where images are generated at 224×224 resolution via the unCLIP decoder with classifier-free guidance (scale 5.0, 100 steps) and text features are the averaged CLIP text embeddings of matched clusters. The entire pipeline is learning-free—no training or fine-tuning of any component is required.

## Method

PDS operates in three learning-free stages. First, CLIP image and text embeddings are extracted, low-similarity pairs are pruned, and each modality is independently clustered into M groups via mini-batch k-means. Second, a linear assignment problem (solved by the Hungarian algorithm) matches image clusters to text clusters by maximizing shared image-text pairs; only embeddings from shared pairs are retained and averaged to form cross-modal prototypes, strengthening alignment. Third, images are synthesized using only the unCLIP decoder (not the full prior+decoder pipeline), conditioned directly on CLIP image prototypes plus a caption retrieved from the nearest text prototype, enabling generation from image embeddings that standard Stable Diffusion cannot support. This design is architecture-independent because no model parameters are optimized, and the use of CLIP for both modalities ensures cross-modal alignment that VAE-based alternatives lack.

## Results

On Flickr30K with a ResNet backbone and 300 distilled pairs, PDS achieves 14.4% IR@1 and 18.7% TR@1, surpassing TESLA-VL (10.3% IR@1, 14.9% TR@1) and LoRS (8.6% IR@1, 14.7% TR@1) by over 4 and 3.8 percentage points respectively. At 100 pairs, PDS reaches 37.3% IR@10 and 39.0% TR@10, outperforming the strongest subset-selection baseline (Herding) by 17.2 and 10.8 pp. On MS-COCO with 300 pairs, PDS achieves 5.3% IR@1 and 7.4% TR@1, more than doubling TESLA-VL (3.0% IR@1) and LoRS (2.5% IR@1). PDS also reduces peak memory from 6.13 GB to 4.34 GB and generation time from 1,477 s to 9.7 s per image compared to CLIP inversion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDS consistently outperforms both optimization-based multimodal distillation baselines (TESLA-VL and LoRS) across all datasets, distilled sizes, and evaluation backbones (ResNet-50 and ViT-Ti/16), with the largest gaps appearing at higher retrieval cutoffs (e.g., +10.8 pp IR@10 on Flickr30K with 300 pairs). Against subset-selection methods (K-center, Herding, CLIP score filtering, LAION filtering, image-based filtering), PDS achieves 37.3% IR@10 vs. Herding's 20.1% on Flickr30K (100 pairs), demonstrating that synthesized prototypes preserve broader semantic diversity than any real-image subset at extreme compression. Learning-free image-classification methods (D4M, MGD3) fail in the multimodal setting because their VAE-based image encoders produce embeddings misaligned with CLIP text features, yielding only 17.2% IR@10 (MGD3) versus PDS's 37.3%. CLIP inversion, which directly optimizes pixel values, produces unrealistic images and achieves only 4.4% IR@1 while consuming 6.13 GB memory and 1,477 s per image, compared to PDS's 7.9% IR@1 at 4.34 GB and 9.7 s. The full unCLIP text-to-image pipeline (without image prototype conditioning) reaches 26.7% IR@10, still well below PDS's 37.3%, confirming that image-prototype-based generation is essential for strong retrieval performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant optimization-based paradigm in multimodal dataset distillation (TESLA-VL, LoRS, MTT-VL), which jointly optimizes image pixels and text features via bi-level optimization and produces architecture-dependent synthetic data. It also extends the line of learning-free image-only distillation (D4M, MGD3) by replacing the VAE encoder with CLIP to enforce cross-modal alignment, and by using the unCLIP decoder to synthesize images directly from CLIP image embeddings rather than from text prompts alone. The method's core novelty is the combination of modality-specific k-means clustering, a Hungarian-algorithm-based linear assignment for cross-modal cluster matching, and prototype-conditioned image generation—a pipeline that requires no gradient-based training at any stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PDS eliminates the need for repeated full-dataset training and per-architecture re-distillation, making multimodal dataset distillation dramatically cheaper and more broadly applicable. The key open question is the dependency on CLIP and unCLIP: stronger alignment models like SigLIP lack corresponding image-conditioned decoders, limiting the framework's applicability. Additionally, the method's reliance on natural-image-trained models raises concerns for domain-specific applications (e.g., medical imaging), and the underrepresentation of rare/long-tail classes, while shared with other methods, remains a practical limitation for imbalanced datasets.
