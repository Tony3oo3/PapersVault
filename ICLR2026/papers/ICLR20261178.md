## Problem

Existing feedforward 3D Gaussian Splatting methods produce discrete, color-biased point clouds that appear plausible at standard resolution but reveal severe artifacts—spatial voids, surface discontinuities, and degenerate isotropic splats—under close-up or off-axis views. This degradation stems from under-utilizing the anisotropic properties of Gaussian primitives and relying solely on image-level photometric loss, which fails to enforce geometric coherence. Additionally, standard NVS metrics (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS at input resolution) cannot capture these hidden geometric flaws, making it difficult to fairly evaluate reconstruction quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes sparse multi-view RGB images (as few as 2 views) along with known camera intrinsics and poses, processed at 256×256 resolution (with a 256×448 variant explored in the appendix). It outputs one 2D Gaussian surfel per pixel per view, where each surfel has zero thickness along the depth axis (σ_w = 0), forming a surface representation rather than a volumetric one. From this representation, the model can render novel views, extract depth maps, surface normal maps, and reconstruct meshes, and is evaluated at up to 4× the input resolution via the proposed HRRC metric.

## Method

SurfSplat replaces 3DGS with 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) surfels as the scene primitive, leveraging their stronger anisotropy for geometric precision. A dual-path encoder fuses monocular depth features (Depth Anything V2 ViT) with multi-view cost-volume features (Swin Transformer + plane-sweep stereo) through a U-Net that predicts depth, scale multipliers, SH coefficients, and opacity. The key innovation is a surface continuity prior that derives each surfel's rotation and scale from local geometry: Sobel-filtered depth neighbors yield tangent vectors whose cross product gives the surface normal (aligned via Rodrigues' rotation), while scale is computed from image-space distances with network-predicted multipliers clamped to [1/3, 3]. A forced alpha blending strategy clips opacity to τ_opa = 0.6 to prevent opacity collapse and normalizes rendered color by C/α when cumulative alpha exceeds a threshold, ensuring occluded Gaussians still contribute to learning. Training uses standard MSE + LPIPS photometric loss, and the authors introduce HRRC (High-Resolution Rendering Consistency) as a new evaluation metric that renders at 2× or 4× resolution to expose geometric artifacts invisible at input resolution.

## Results

On RealEstate10K at standard 256×256 resolution, SurfSplat-L achieves 27.54 PSNR / 0.892 SSIM / 0.112 LPIPS, marginally surpassing DepthSplat (27.50 / 0.890 / 0.112). The decisive advantage appears under the proposed HRRC metric: at 512×512, SurfSplat-L reaches 26.33 PSNR versus 20.03 for DepthSplat and 20.41 for MVSplat (a >6 dB gap), and at 1024×1024 it scores 24.90 PSNR versus 16.39 for DepthSplat. On the ACID outdoor dataset, SurfSplat attains 28.34 PSNR at standard resolution and 26.87 PSNR at 512×512 HRRC, outperforming all single-Gaussian baselines by 5–9 dB in the high-resolution setting. Cross-dataset evaluation (trained on RE10K, tested on ScanNet/DL3DV/DTU) yields an average PSNR of 21.08, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At standard 256×256 resolution, SurfSplat-L (27.54 PSNR) essentially ties DepthSplat (27.50) and slightly edges out HiSplat (27.19) and TranSplat (26.69), confirming competitive NVS quality. The critical differentiation emerges under HRRC: SurfSplat-L outperforms DepthSplat by 6.3 dB (26.33 vs 20.03) at 512×512 and by 8.5 dB (24.90 vs 16.39) at 1024×1024, indicating that DepthSplat's geometry collapses under close-up inspection. MVSplat and TranSplat suffer similar degradation (20.4 and 20.6 PSNR at 512²), while multi-Gaussian methods PixelSplat and HiSplat retain higher HRRC scores (25.8 and 25.3) but still trail SurfSplat by 0.5–1.1 dB. On the ACID outdoor dataset, SurfSplat leads all single-Gaussian baselines by 5–9 dB at HRRC resolutions. In cross-dataset transfer (RE10K→ScanNet/DL3DV/DTU), SurfSplat achieves the best average PSNR (21.08) versus DepthSplat (20.98) and HiSplat (20.45), demonstrating stronger geometric generalization under domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

SurfSplat extends the feedforward 3D Gaussian Splatting line (PixelSplat, MVSplat, DepthSplat, HiSplat, TranSplat) by replacing the 3DGS primitive with 2D Gaussian surfels, which offer stronger anisotropy and geometric precision. It departs from prior methods in two key ways: (1) a surface continuity prior that derives rotation and scale from local 3D position gradients rather than predicting them independently, and (2) a forced alpha blending strategy that clips opacity to prevent the model from collapsing into fully opaque, single-layer representations. The encoder architecture (dual-path Depth Anything V2 + multi-view Swin Transformer with plane-sweep cost volumes) is inherited directly from DepthSplat, so the novelty is concentrated in the Gaussian attribute parameterization and the HRRC evaluation metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a real and previously underappreciated failure mode: feedforward 3DGS methods that score well on standard NVS metrics can produce severely fragmented, color-biased geometry that only becomes visible under close-up or high-resolution rendering. The HRRC metric provides a practical, annotation-free way to expose these artifacts, which should influence how the community evaluates generalizable reconstruction. Open questions include whether the one-Gaussian-per-pixel constraint limits expressiveness for complex scenes, whether the method can be extended to pose-free or dynamic settings, and whether the surface continuity prior generalizes beyond the indoor/outdoor scenes tested here.
