## Problem

Existing symmetry-based disentangled representation learning methods require strong prior knowledge of the symmetry group's structure, such as the subgroup decomposition, the minimal latent dimension per subgroup, or restrictive assumptions like actions belonging to SO(2). This paper removes these constraints by proposing a method where an embodied agent autonomously discovers the group structure of its action space through unsupervised interaction, and then learns a disentangled representation without assuming specific subgroup properties.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262945/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dataset of transitions (x, g, x') where x and x' are observation images and g is an action index from a finite set G ⊂ G. The agent does not need to know the group decomposition, the number of subgroups, or the minimal representation dimension per subgroup. The output is a three-part result: (1) a disentangled latent vector z ∈ R^d where dimensions are partitioned into subspaces, one per discovered subgroup; (2) the recovered group decomposition G = G₁ × … × G_K via action clustering; and (3) block-diagonal action representation matrices where each action acts non-trivially only on its assigned block. The latent dimension d is chosen freely (e.g., 13 in experiments) rather than being dictated by the group structure.

## Method

The method proceeds in three stages. First, an Action-based VAE (A-VAE) learns an entangled representation by training a VAE whose prior over the next latent state is conditioned on the current observation and action via a learned matrix ρ_ψ(g) ∈ R^{d×d} with no structural constraints (d² free parameters per action). Second, a group-theoretic pseudo-distance d_G is computed between pairs of actions by checking whether one action matrix can be expressed as a composition of the other with powers of a third action; an agglomerative clustering algorithm then recovers the subgroup decomposition using a threshold η derived from the action loss and spectral norms. Third, a Group-Masked Action-based VAE (GMA-VAE) enforces disentanglement by applying learned binary masks π_k (parameterized via softmax with entropy annealing from maximum to zero) that project each action matrix into a block-diagonal form, so that each action affects only the latent dimensions assigned to its subgroup. The paper proves identifiability of the true group decomposition (Theorem 2) and LSBD disentanglement guarantees (Theorem 3) under minimal assumptions including full observability and action-set disentanglement.

## Results

GMA-VAE achieves disentanglement metrics (Inde, β-VAE, DCI, Mod) comparable to the supervised LSBD-VAE across all environments, while MIG and SAP remain low for all methods due to the inherent multi-dimensional encoding of linear symmetries. In the generalization experiments (Table 1), GMA-VAE attains 6.1e-5/6.2e-5 seen/unseen prediction error on COIL2 (iid) and 1.1e-4/1.1e-4 on COIL3 (iid), matching or slightly beating the supervised LSBD-VAE (7.8e-5/7.9e-5 and 1.1e-4/1.1e-4). In the out-of-distribution setting, GMA-VAE shows less than 5% degradation between seen and unseen transitions, whereas entangled baselines (A-VAE, SOBDRL-entangled) degrade by orders of magnitude (e.g., A-VAE: 6.7e-5 → 0.05 on COIL2 OOD). The action clustering step recovers the ground-truth group decomposition in 100% of runs across all tested environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the supervised LSBD-VAE (Tonnaer et al., 2022), GMA-VAE matches or slightly outperforms on prediction error: 6.1e-5 vs. 7.8e-5 on COIL2 (iid) and 1.1e-4 vs. 1.1e-4 on COIL3 (iid), despite not requiring any prior knowledge of the action representation matrices. Against SOBDRL (Quessard et al., 2020), GMA-VAE achieves roughly 3× lower prediction error in the disentangled regime on COIL2 (6.1e-5 vs. 1.7e-4), and critically, SOBDRL's entangled runs degrade catastrophically in OOD settings (5.7e-5 → 0.02 on COIL2), while GMA-VAE remains stable. On the MPI3D Lie group benchmark, GMA-VAE outperforms HAE (Keurti et al., 2023) on most disentanglement metrics and shows greater resilience to action noise. The unsupervised baselines (β-VAE, Factor-VAE, DIP-VAE) perform significantly worse than all LSBD-based methods and are omitted from the main comparison figures. Overall, GMA-VAE is the only self-supervised method that simultaneously matches supervised disentanglement quality, achieves 100% group decomposition recovery, and generalizes robustly to unseen transitions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Linear Symmetry-Based Disentanglement (LSBD) framework of Higgins et al. (2018) and the VAE-based action learning of Forward-VAE (Caselles-Dupré et al., 2019) by removing the requirement for prior knowledge of the symmetry group's decomposition. Prior LSBD methods (SOBDRL, LSBD-VAE, HAE) all assume either the full group structure, specific subgroup properties (e.g., SO(2) rotations), or a known block-diagonal parameterization. The key departure is a two-stage pipeline: A-VAE learns an entangled equivariant representation with unstructured action matrices, and a group-theoretic pseudo-distance then clusters actions into subgroups, after which GMA-VAE enforces disentanglement via learned binary masks rather than a fixed parameterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Removing the need for prior group structure knowledge makes symmetry-based disentanglement applicable to environments where the transformation group is unknown or complex, broadening the practical scope of the LSBD framework. However, the method still requires strong assumptions (full observability, disentangled action sets, finite world, complete transition coverage) and a two-stage training procedure, limiting its applicability to realistic settings. Open questions include whether the two stages can be unified end-to-end, how the method behaves when actions are not cleanly separable into subgroups, and whether the approach scales to continuous groups without the finite-clustering step. The proof that block-diagonal parameterization (used by SOBDRL and HAE) is insufficient for guaranteed disentanglement is a useful negative result for practitioners choosing architectures.
