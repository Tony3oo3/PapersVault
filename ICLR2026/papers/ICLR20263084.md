## Problem

Existing causal discovery methods assume a single static causal graph whose edge strengths are fixed, but in industrial systems causal effects systematically vary with measurable operating parameters (e.g., catalyst activity, fouling levels, heat transfer coefficients). This static assumption prevents causal discovery from capturing how causal mechanisms evolve, a critical gap for safety-critical applications like process control and predictive maintenance where understanding parameter-dependent causal variation is essential.

## Input / Output

The method consumes m paired observations of n process variables X and a d-dimensional operating parameter vector ξ (e.g., heat transfer coefficients, reaction rate constants, yield factors), all in tabular form. Beyond the standard DAG output, each discovered edge is represented as a continuous function b_ij(ξ) via PCE spectral coefficients, accompanied by bootstrap edge-existence probabilities, 95% confidence intervals on causal strengths, and Sobol sensitivity indices decomposing each edge's variance across parameter dimensions.

## Method

PCT-CD represents each causal edge weight as a function of operating parameters ξ and approximates it with a truncated Polynomial Chaos Expansion using a Wiener-Askey orthogonal basis adapted to the known distribution of ξ, converting the infinite-dimensional function-learning problem into finite-dimensional coefficient estimation. A novel PCT-conditional independence test replaces standard marginal CI tests by regressing variables on interaction features {X_k Ψ_α(ξ)} and applying a Wald statistic to the PCE coefficients of the conditional covariance function, catching parameter-dependent dependencies that cancel in the marginal. The algorithm proceeds in two phases: a PC-style skeleton search using the PCT-CI oracle, followed by score-based refinement optimizing a group-sparse PCT-BIC objective with natural-gradient (Fisher-preconditioned) updates for the PCE coefficients. Identifiability is established by extending LiNGAM/ICA arguments to the parametric setting, showing that the ξ-independence of the noise distribution pins down the permutation and scaling ambiguities that would otherwise persist at each fixed ξ.

## Results

On the Parkland Refinery chemical reactor dataset (10,000 samples, 9 variables, 11 true causal edges), PCT-CD achieves an F1-score of 90.9% (Precision 90.9%, Recall 90.9%), correctly identifying 10 of 11 true edges with only 1 false positive and 1 false negative, yielding a Structural Hamming Distance of 2. This nearly doubles the best baseline performance (CAM, GraNDAG, SAM at 64.0% F1) and substantially outperforms all 22 other methods tested. The method also provides per-edge parametric causal effect functions with 95% confidence intervals and bootstrap edge-existence probabilities, a capability absent from all static-graph baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCT-CD outperforms all 12 directly compared baselines by a wide margin on F1-score. Among functional causal models, LiNGAM (Shimizu 2006) achieves 38.5% F1, ICA-LiNGAM 7.7%, DirectLiNGAM 15.4%, and VAR-LiNGAM (Hyvärinen 2010) 28.6%, all severely degraded by the parametric variation that their static-weight assumption cannot capture. Time-series and constraint-based methods PCMCI (Runge 2019, 34.8%), PC (Spirtes 2000, 50.0%), and FCI (Spirtes 1999, 50.0%) show moderate precision but low recall, missing 6 of 11 true edges. Score-based optimizers NOTEARS (Zheng 2018, 47.6%), GES (Chickering 2002, 54.5%), and GIES (Ramsey 2017, 54.5%) perform slightly better but remain 36–43 percentage points below PCT-CD. Nonlinear functional models ANM (Hoyer 2008) and PNL (Zhang 2009) both reach 52.2% F1, still trailing PCT-CD by 38.7 points. The largest gap is against ICA-LiNGAM (83.2-point F1 difference), while the smallest is against the top score-based methods GES/GIES (36.4-point gap), confirming that the parametric PCE representation is the decisive factor across all method families.

## Relation to Prior Work

PCT-CD sits at the intersection of three research threads: Polynomial Chaos Expansion (PCE) from uncertainty quantification (Xiu & Karniadakis, 2002; Sudret, 2008), LiNGAM-style identifiability for linear non-Gaussian SEMs (Shimizu et al., 2006), and PC/FCI constraint-based skeleton discovery (Spirtes et al., 2000). The key departure from prior causal discovery is the explicit modeling of edge weights as functions of a measurable parameter vector ξ, represented via PCE, rather than as fixed scalars. The paper positions itself as complementary to Bayesian structure learning (DiBS, BCD Nets) that quantifies epistemic uncertainty over static graphs, arguing that parametric uncertainty—how causal strength varies with operating conditions—is a distinct and previously unaddressed dimension. The identifiability proof extends LiNGAM's ICA-based argument to the parametric setting by showing the permutation/scaling ambiguity cannot depend on ξ when noise is ξ-independent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a genuine gap in industrial process monitoring where causal relationships systematically shift with catalyst degradation, fouling, or feedstock quality—conditions that static-graph methods silently average away. The theoretical contributions (identifiability of parametric mechanisms, finite-sample recovery guarantees, natural-gradient convergence) provide a foundation that goes beyond empirical validation. However, the evidence base is limited to a single 9-variable, 11-edge dataset, the model assumes a linear SEM with known parameter distribution and no unobserved confounders, and the synthetic benchmark results are deferred to the appendix without full detail. Open questions include extending to unobserved confounders, nonlinear interactions beyond PCE, and online/adaptive settings where the parameter distribution itself drifts.
