## Problem

Adaptive optimizers for LLM training face a fundamental trade-off: SOAP captures inter-coordinate dependencies via full second-moment matrices but incurs prohibitive memory costs (66d² for transformers), while memory-efficient alternatives like GaLore and Adam-mini discard gradient structure in the residual space, degrading optimization quality. No existing method achieves SOAP-level per-token efficiency at a memory footprint comparable to Adam or MUON.

## Input / Output

COSMOS operates per weight matrix layer of shape m×n (assuming m > n), consuming the stochastic gradient G_t and maintaining four state matrices: momentum M ∈ R^{m×n}, orthonormal basis U ∈ R^{n×r}, projected second moment S ∈ R^{r×r}, and projected gradient EMA V ∈ R^{m×r}, where r ≪ n is the projection rank (e.g., r = 0.05d). The output is an updated weight matrix W_{t+1} with Frobenius-norm-scaled step. Embedding and output projection layers are handled separately by Adam, while all hidden-layer weight matrices use COSMOS.

## Method

COSMOS decomposes the gradient into a leading eigensubspace (rank r) and its orthogonal complement, applying different preconditioners to each. For the leading eigensubspace, it tracks the dominant eigenspace of the second-moment EMA via a one-step power iteration (QR of β₂U_{t-1}S_{t-1} + (1-β₂)G_t^T G_t U_{t-1}) and performs a SOAP-like adaptive update within that subspace, projecting back to full space. For the residual (orthogonal complement), it applies the MUON Newton-Schulz transformation (NS5) directly to the projected momentum M_t(I - U_t U_t^T), requiring no additional stored matrices. The two components are combined as G̃_t = A_t + γ·B_t·√m, then normalized before the weight update. This design reduces SOAP's O(n²) second-moment storage to O(nr) while preserving the curvature-aware preconditioning where it matters most, and the QR decomposition cost drops from O(n³) to O(nr²) per step.

## Results

On the C4 dataset, COSMOS achieves the best validation perplexity across all three model sizes: 20.54 (LLaMA-130M, 5B tokens), 16.21 (LLaMA-350M, 10B tokens), and 12.46 (LLaMA-1B, 26B tokens). In terms of memory, COSMOS requires only 13d² of optimizer state memory (vs. 66d² for SOAP and 24d² for Adam), and on the 1B model uses 58.47 GB peak GPU memory—6.8% less than Adam and 19.4% less than SOAP—while incurring only ~0.2s additional wall-clock time per iteration over MUON.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COSMOS outperforms all five baselines on validation perplexity across every model size tested. Against SOAP, the closest competitor in optimization quality, COSMOS achieves a small but consistent edge (20.54 vs. 20.59 on 130M; 16.21 vs. 16.32 on 350M) while using roughly 80% less optimizer memory (13d² vs. 66d²). Against MUON, COSMOS wins by a margin of 0.15–0.28 perplexity points (e.g., 12.46 vs. 12.57 on 1B) with negligible additional wall-clock overhead (~0.2s per iteration). Against Adam, COSMOS improves perplexity by 0.5–1.1 points (e.g., 16.21 vs. 17.28 on 350M) while using less memory (13d² vs. 24d²). Adam-mini and GaLore are substantially outperformed, with GaLore showing particularly severe degradation on longer sequences (24.07 vs. 20.54 on 130M), confirming the paper's observation that low-rank projection methods lose gradient information in the residual space.

## Relation to Prior Work

COSMOS synthesizes two existing optimizer lineages: SOAP's eigensubspace-based adaptive preconditioning (which captures inter-coordinate dependencies via SVD of the gradient) and MUON's Newton-Schulz transformation (which approximates the matrix sign operator at low computational cost). The key conceptual departure is the observation that eigensubspaces of the gradient matrix have heterogeneous importance, motivating a hybrid strategy: SOAP operates only on the leading r-dimensional eigensubspace (reducing its O(n²) memory to O(nr)), while MUON handles the residual complement. This positions COSMOS as a principled interpolation between the two extremes of full SOAP (high accuracy, high memory) and pure MUON (low memory, lower accuracy), rather than a new optimization paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COSMOS addresses a concrete bottleneck in LLM training: second-order optimizers like SOAP achieve superior token efficiency but are memory-prohibitive at scale (66d² vs 13d² for COSMOS), while memory-efficient alternatives like Adam-mini and GaLore sacrifice optimization quality. The practical impact is enabling SOAP-level perplexity at MUON-level memory and compute overhead, potentially unlocking larger batch sizes or models on fixed hardware. Limitations include experiments capped at 1B parameters, a fixed rank r=64 that may not generalize to all architectures, and the heuristic γ=η/η₀ that the authors acknowledge is suboptimal. Open questions include whether the eigensubspace importance hierarchy holds at trillion-parameter scale and whether adaptive rank selection could further improve the accuracy-memory tradeoff.
