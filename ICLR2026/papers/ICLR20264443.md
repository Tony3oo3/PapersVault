## Problem

No comprehensive benchmark exists for evaluating LLMs across the full Earth science spectrum (five spheres, 114 sub-disciplines), with existing options being either general science benchmarks lacking domain specificity or narrow single-subdomain benchmarks (e.g., climate, ocean). Moreover, prevailing QA-only formats fail to assess the open-ended scientific exploration capabilities—methodology induction, limitation analysis, and concept proposal—that are central to genuine research.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264443/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Earth-Iron and Earth-Silver accept text questions in four formats (multiple-choice, true/false, fill-in-the-blank, free-response) and produce model text responses scored by exact-match accuracy (ACC) for closed-form items and GPT-4-judged Win Rate plus sentence-transformer Semantic Similarity for free-response items. Earth-Gold takes a two-turn dialogue prompt (summarize existing methods and their limitations, then propose improved methods with their constraints) and produces M=3 diverse model responses (temperature=0.6) scored by the composite Scientific Exploration Score (SES), which multiplies a GPT-4-ranked retention rate by an embedding-based diversity term.

## Method

The benchmark is constructed from a 100,000-paper Earth science corpus (sourced from OpenDataLab, parsed via MinerU, and classified into five spheres using positive/negative keyword semantic similarity with dual thresholds). Papers are stratified into three tiers—P_base (100K, broad coverage), P_hj (10K from high-impact journals), and P_hc (1K top-cited)—which feed Earth-Iron, Earth-Silver, and Earth-Gold respectively. For QA datasets, GPT-4 generates task-aware question-answer pairs with mandatory Chain-of-Thought derivations, followed by dual-phase cleaning (rule-based formatting fixes plus LLM semantic error detection) and difficulty screening that removes questions answered correctly by >80% of tested models. For Earth-Gold, each high-citation paper is decomposed into four structured components (existing method summary M⁰, limitation analysis L⁰, novel method proposal M¹, new method limitations L¹), and GPT-4 generates two-turn exploration dialogues validated by domain experts using a six-dimension scoring rubric. The novel SES metric combines retention rate (the rank position of the reference answer among M model responses, judged by GPT-4) with diversity (inverse of mean cosine similarity of sentence-transformer embeddings), normalized to SES = r / (10×(s̄−0.9)).

## Results

On Earth-Iron (4,133 QA questions), most LLMs achieve 89–94% accuracy on multiple-choice questions but drop sharply to 2.5–21.9% on fill-in-the-blank and 12–98.7% win rate on free-response. On the harder Earth-Silver subset, MC accuracy falls to 36–62% (vs. 25% random chance) and FIB accuracy to just 2–17.2%, revealing substantial knowledge gaps. On Earth-Gold (open-ended scientific exploration dialogues), the best model (Gemini-2.5-Flash) achieves only 50.56% retention rate and a Scientific Exploration Score (SES) of 1.37, while most models score below 20% retention, indicating poor divergent scientific reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Grok-3 leads Earth-Iron free-response win rate (98.70%) and FIB accuracy (21.85%), while Claude-3.7-Sonnet achieves the highest MC accuracy on both Earth-Iron (94.01%) and Earth-Silver (62.40%). DeepSeek-V3 and Gemini-2.5-Flash are the strongest on Earth-Silver MC (58.00% each) and FR-WR (75.20% and 74.30% respectively), substantially outperforming GPT-4o (55.60% MC, 22.00% FR-WR) and Llama-3.1-8B (36.00% MC, 4.40% FR-WR). On Earth-Gold, Gemini-2.5-Flash achieves the highest SES (1.3710) with 50.56% retention, far exceeding GPT-4o (SES 0.0981, 9.44% retention) and Llama-3.1-8B (SES 0.3301, 8.00% retention); DeepSeek-V3 follows with SES 0.6599 and 38% retention. Notably, all 11 models score below 51% retention on Earth-Gold, and most exhibit low diversity scores (<2.0), indicating that no current LLM demonstrates robust open-ended scientific exploration capability comparable to human researchers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two underdeveloped areas: comprehensive Earth science benchmarking and open-ended scientific exploration evaluation. It departs from general science benchmarks (ScienceQA, SciBench, GPQA) by grounding all questions in a 100K-paper Earth science corpus spanning five spheres and 114 disciplines, and from single-subdomain benchmarks (OceanBench, ClimaQA, GeoBench) by providing unified cross-sphere coverage. The most significant conceptual departure is the Earth-Gold dialogue format, which formalizes scientific exploration as an iterative self-negation process (M^{i+1}, L^{i+1}) = LLM(M^i, L^i) and introduces the SES metric combining retention rate and diversity—neither of which has a direct precedent in prior LLM evaluation literature. The QA construction pipeline (GPT-4 generation + CoT-based cleaning + difficulty calibration) follows established practices from SciBench and GPQA, but the reconstruction-from-papers approach rather than free-form generation is a deliberate methodological choice to ensure factual fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EarthSE fills a conspicuous gap: no prior benchmark simultaneously covers all five Earth spheres at professional difficulty while also evaluating open-ended scientific exploration beyond factual QA. The finding that even top models achieve only ~54% MC accuracy on Earth-Silver (vs. 25% random) and <50% retention on Earth-Gold provides concrete evidence that current LLMs lack the depth and divergent thinking needed for genuine scientific discovery in Earth sciences. The CoT guidance result (3× improvement in FIB accuracy with just 3 provided steps) offers a practical inference-time strategy for domain-specific reasoning. Key open questions include whether the SES metric generalizes beyond Earth science, whether the 11 tasks can be chained into automated discovery pipelines (acknowledged as a limitation), and whether the GPT-4-as-judge protocol introduces systematic biases that affect cross-model comparisons.
