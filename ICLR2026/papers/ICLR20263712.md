## Problem

Masked diffusion models (MDMs) suffer from inherently higher training variance than autoregressive models, causing unstable optimization, large run-to-run variability, and significant performance degradation after task-specific fine-tuning. No prior work provided a theoretical decomposition of where this extra variance originates or a principled, unified solution. This paper derives the first systematic variance decomposition and builds on it to design targeted, theoretically grounded variance-reduction methods.

## Input / Output

The method operates on token sequences (text or image tokens) where a randomly sampled masking rate t determines which tokens are replaced with [MASK]; the model reconstructs the masked tokens via a cross-entropy objective. For text tasks, input is a prompt–response token pair and output is the generated response; for image generation, input is a text prompt and output is an RGB image. Notably, MDMs decode by unmasking blocks of tokens in parallel with length set by max_new_tokens, producing responses as short as 5% of ARM outputs while maintaining competitive accuracy.

## Method

The paper decomposes MDM training variance into three sources—masking pattern noise (A), masking rate noise (B), and data noise (C)—showing that ARMs are affected only by (C). The two core methods target these sources: P-POTS replaces the uniform t-sampler with a Pareto-optimal non-uniform distribution p*(t) ∝ √(g²(t)+v(t)) fitted via a seven-parameter EPR model, using importance weights 1/p(t) to maintain unbiasedness while concentrating training on high-variance t-regions; MIRROR constructs two complementary masked samples (masking if U_i < t vs. U_i > 1-t) whose losses are negatively correlated, reducing (A) by at least half. Four additional methods (ISAD, SyRM, StraTS, EMA) address specific variance components. P-POTS and MIRROR exhibit strong synergy beyond additivity, as MIRROR suppresses v(t) at moderate t where P-POTS is less sensitive, while P-POTS emphasizes the low- and high-t regions where MIRROR's reduction is weakest.

## Results

On GSM8K, P-POTS+MIRROR improves LLaDA-8B-Instruct accuracy from 52.51% (standard) to 60.53%, and on HiTab from 58.68% to 67.10%, while on OpenScience it rises from 45.52% to 52.53%. On text-to-image-2M, the CLIP score range narrows from 28.61–34.28 (standard) to 34.10–35.27 (P-POTS+MIRROR), with the mean increasing from 31.36 to 34.59. Run-to-run variability drops to near-ARM levels: on all three textual benchmarks, even the best Standard or Clipped run remains below the worst P-POTS+MIRROR run (e.g., HiTab: 62.64 vs. 66.02).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the clipped noise schedule of Arriola et al. (2025), P-POTS+MIRROR wins on all three textual benchmarks by 3.2–6.5 points (OpenScience 52.53 vs. 49.33; GSM8K 60.53 vs. 56.18; HiTab 67.10 vs. 60.63), and also substantially reduces run-to-run spread. Compared to Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, the MDM with P-POTS+MIRROR outperforms on all three datasets (52.53 vs. 46.42, 60.53 vs. 54.82, 67.10 vs. 45.83). Against Qwen3-8B-Base, the MDM wins on OpenScience (52.53 vs. 51.62) and HiTab (67.10 vs. 48.91) but trails on GSM8K (60.53 vs. 70.93), where the ARM's mathematical reasoning strength is most pronounced. Versus Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, the MDM leads on all three benchmarks (52.53 vs. 46.59, 60.53 vs. 47.70, 67.10 vs. 65.39). On text-to-image-2M, P-POTS+MIRROR raises the mean CLIP score from 31.36 (standard) to 34.59 while tightening the inter-seed range from 5.67 to 1.17 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the masked diffusion model (MDM) training objective derived by Sahoo et al. (2024), which nearly all current pretrained MDMs (LLaDA, MMaDA, Dream-7B) adopt. Prior variance-reduction efforts for diffusion models (Meng et al. 2021, Xu et al. 2023, Jeha et al. 2024, Kim et al. 2024, Zhu et al. 2025, Arriola et al. 2025) each targeted specific aspects of training noise but remained isolated and heuristic, lacking a unified theoretical framework. This paper departs from that fragmented landscape by deriving the first systematic three-way variance decomposition (masking pattern, masking rate, data noise) and then grounding each of its six methods in a specific term of that decomposition, with P-POTS provably Pareto-optimal among all unbiased t-samplers via classical importance-sampling theory (Kahn & Marshall, 1953) and MIRROR leveraging antithetic-style negative correlation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The variance decomposition provides a foundational lens for understanding why MDMs diverge from ARMs after fine-tuning—a long-observed but previously unexplained phenomenon—and gives practitioners a principled menu of complementary variance-reduction tools rather than ad-hoc tricks. The practical impact is significant: 7–8% accuracy gains on reasoning tasks with run-to-run variability reduced to near-ARM levels, and the methods require minimal hyperparameter tuning. Open questions include whether the static p(t) fitted once before training suffers meaningful drift during long pretraining runs (the authors note this is more severe on image generation), whether periodic refitting recovers additional gains, and whether the framework extends naturally to reinforcement-learning fine-tuning of MDMs (sketched in Appendix A.9 but not fully validated).
