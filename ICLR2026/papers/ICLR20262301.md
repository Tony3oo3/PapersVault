## Problem

Existing reward models, trained primarily on general-domain preferences, fail to capture the subjective, multi-dimensional nature of human story preferences (creativity, coherence, characterization, fluency, relevance). The best existing model achieves only 66.3% accuracy in selecting human-preferred stories, and current reward models systematically favor LLM-generated over human-written stories, making it difficult to guide generation toward human-level narrative quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262301/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The reward model receives a story premise (1–2 sentences) and one or more candidate stories (typically 4, each ~2,000–3,000 words) and outputs a scalar reward score per story, from which a preference ranking is derived. In the STORYRMB benchmark, each instance contains exactly one chosen and three rejected stories, and correctness is defined as the model assigning the highest score to the chosen story. For test-time scaling (Best-of-N), the model scores 16 stories generated by Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct per premise and selects the top-ranked one.

## Method

The paper introduces STORYRMB, a 1,133-instance benchmark built by generating four candidate stories per premise from GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Grok 4, and QwenLong (plus human-written stories from WritingPrompts), then ranking them via a two-stage process: four LLMs (GLM-4.5, Qwen2.5-Max, Claude-3.5, DeepSeek-R1) score each story on five dimensions, and Kendall's Tau agreement determines whether majority voting or full human annotation is used. For training, the authors construct ~100K preference pairs from human-written stories on Douban and WritingPrompts using three automated methods—premise back-generation (~6K pairs), prompt-guided rewriting that degrades specific narrative aspects (~30K pairs), and human-guided continuation where one LLM sees the human continuation as context while another does not (~10K pairs)—plus LLM-vs-LLM pairs. STORYREWARD is trained via standard preference learning (Bradley-Terry objective) on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen3-8B base models with a single epoch, achieving 75.0% average accuracy on STORYRMB and substantially outperforming all baselines in Best-of-N story selection.

## Results

On STORYRMB, STORYREWARD-QWEN achieves 75.0% average accuracy across five story preference dimensions (coherence, creativity, characterization, fluency, relevance), outperforming the strongest baseline GPT-4o (66.3%) by 8.7 points. STORYREWARD-LLAMA reaches 61.4% average, surpassing its base model Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (24.3%) by 37.1 points. In Best-of-N test-time scaling on MoPS, STORYREWARD selects a superior story in over 70% of head-to-head comparisons against other reward models. On the EQ-Bench Creative Writing v3 set, stories selected by STORYREWARD-QWEN score 2.72 (vs. 2.36 for the best baseline ArmoRM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STORYREWARD-QWEN (75.0% avg) decisively outperforms all 11 baselines, with the largest gaps over general-purpose reward models: ArmoRM (49.7%), Skywork-Reward-Gemma-2-27B (32.7%), and GRM (31.1%). Against the strongest LLM-as-judge baseline GPT-4o (66.3%), STORYREWARD-QWEN leads by 8.7 points on average, with particularly large margins on creativity (+15.1 pts) and coherence (+14.1 pts), though GPT-4o retains a slight edge on fluency (73.3% vs. 74.2% — actually STORYREWARD-QWEN wins by 0.9 pts). STORYREWARD-LLAMA (61.4%) also surpasses GPT-4o on relevance (67.7% vs. 67.6%) and coherence (64.5% vs. 63.4%) but trails on fluency (53.8% vs. 73.3%), suggesting the Qwen3-8B base is better suited for story preference. Both STORYREWARD variants substantially outperform their respective base models (Qwen3-8B: 53.5%, Llama-3.1-8B: 24.3%), confirming that the 100K story preference pairs provide meaningful signal. In BoN test-time scaling on MoPS, STORYREWARD wins head-to-head against all other reward models with fewer than 30% inferior selections, and on EQ-Bench Creative Writing v3, STORYREWARD-QWEN-selected stories score 2.72 vs. 2.36 for ArmoRM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLHF reward-modeling paradigm (Ouyang et al., 2022) to the specific domain of story generation, where prior reward models have focused on general instruction-following or chat preferences. It builds directly on Yang & Jin (2024) for the five-dimensional story evaluation framework and the four-stage dimension-categorization algorithm, and on Cui et al. (2024) UltraFeedback for the LLM-judged preference-pair construction methodology. The key departure is the systematic collection of human-written stories from literary platforms (Douban, WritingPrompts) with real engagement signals (upvotes, readership) as preference proxies, rather than relying solely on LLM-generated comparisons. The paper positions itself as the first work to both benchmark and train a dedicated story-preference reward model, filling a gap between general-purpose RMs and the subjective, multi-dimensional nature of narrative quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even GPT-4o achieves only 66.3% accuracy on story preference, and that existing RMs systematically favor LLM-generated over human-written stories, provides a concrete diagnostic for why current RLHF pipelines fail to close the human-LLM narrative gap. The 8B STORYREWARD-QWEN outperforming frontier LLM-as-judges by ~9 points suggests that domain-specific preference data is more impactful than raw model scale for this task, a finding with implications for cost-efficient deployment in test-time scaling. Open questions include whether the observed kurtosis bias (model preferring high sentence-length variance as a proxy for quality) can be mitigated, whether the approach generalizes beyond English/Chinese literary fiction, and whether the benchmark's reliance on LLM majority voting for annotation introduces systematic biases that limit its validity.
