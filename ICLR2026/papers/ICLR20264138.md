## Problem

State-of-the-art black-box QD algorithms like CMA-MAE represent the discount function as a discrete histogram over measure space, causing solutions with similar measures to receive identical discount values and stall search in high-dimensional or distorted measure spaces. This limitation prevents QD from scaling to high-dimensional measures (e.g., images) and forces practitioners to hand-design low-dimensional measure functions, which is tedious and often suboptimal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264138/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DMS takes an objective function f, a measure function m (or, in the QDDM setting, a user-provided dataset of high-dimensional data such as 28×28 MNIST digits or 256×256×3 landscape images from LHQ256), an initial solution vector, and hyperparameters including the archive learning rate α and number of empty points n_empty. It produces a MAP-Elites-style archive of diverse, high-performing solutions (e.g., StyleGAN3 latent vectors, triangle vertex/brightness/alpha parameters, or 100-joint arm configurations) along with QD Score and Coverage metrics. In QDDM domains, the measure space dimensionality can reach 196,608 (for 256×256×3 RGB images), and the CVT archive centroids are drawn directly from the user's dataset rather than uniformly partitioning the space.

## Method

DMS replaces CMA-MAE's discrete histogram with a neural network discount model f̂_A(·;ψ) that provides a smooth, continuous mapping from measures to scalar discount values, enabling distinct improvement signals even when distortion causes solutions to have similar measures. The model is trained each iteration on a dataset D_A with two sources: (1) emitter solutions whose targets follow CMA-MAE's threshold update rule (t_A = (1−α)f̂_A(s) + αf(θ) when f(θ) > f̂_A(s)), and (2) 'empty points'—centers of unoccupied archive cells with target f_min—that clamp the model in unexplored regions to prevent arbitrary high outputs. CMA-ES emitters sample solutions from adaptive Gaussians, rank them by improvement Δ = f(θ) − f̂_A(m(θ)), and update their distribution parameters accordingly. The architecture of the discount model is chosen to match the measure type (MLP for low-dimensional vectors, CNN or transformer for images/text), and in QDDM domains the CVT archive uses semantically meaningful distance functions like CLIP score to assign solutions to cells.

## Results

DMS achieves the highest QD Score and Coverage in 11 of 12 evaluated domains. The most dramatic gains appear in high-dimensional measure spaces: on 10D LP (Sphere), DMS reaches 89.21% Coverage versus CMA-MAE's 6.95%, and on 20D LP (Sphere), 95.97% versus 9.13%. In the QDDM setting, DMS achieves 99.84% Coverage on TA (MNIST) and 72.28% on TA (F-MNIST), and on LSI (Hiker) it attains a QD Score of 214.91% compared to CMA-MAE's 14.61%. All ANOVA tests are significant (p < 0.001), with Games-Howell post-hoc tests confirming DMS's superiority in nearly all pairwise comparisons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CMA-MAE (the state-of-the-art black-box QD algorithm), DMS achieves significantly higher QD Score and Coverage in 10 of 12 domains, with the largest margins in high-dimensional LP benchmarks (e.g., 89.21% vs. 6.95% Coverage on 10D LP (Sphere), and 95.97% vs. 9.13% on 20D LP (Sphere)). The two exceptions are TA (MNIST), where DMS and CMA-MAE show no significant difference (951.56% vs. 954.27% QD Score), and Arm Repertoire, where DMS edges out CMA-MAE on QD Score (7,963 vs. 7,902) but not significantly on Coverage. Against DDS (a diversity-only algorithm), DMS wins on both QD Score and Coverage in nearly all benchmark domains, including the Flat (diversity-only) LP domains where DDS was expected to excel; DDS achieves marginally better Coverage in Arm Repertoire (80.24% vs. 80.15%) but cannot be run in the QDDM domains due to KDE runtime scaling. Against MAP-Elites and MAP-Elites(line), DMS significantly outperforms both on all metrics in every domain where all three are comparable, with particularly large gaps in high-dimensional settings (e.g., 89.21% vs. 29.20% Coverage for MAP-Elites(line) on 10D LP (Sphere)). In LSI (Hiker), the MAP-Elites variants produce large negative QD Scores (−51,827 and −18,918) due to regularization penalties from generating out-of-distribution latent vectors, making their high Coverage values (7.49% and 5.06%) misleading; DMS's 3.77% Coverage still represents 377 valid hiker images and is significantly better than CMA-MAE's 1.56%.

## Relation to Prior Work

DMS directly extends CMA-MAE (Fontaine & Nikolaidis, 2023) by replacing its discrete histogram-based discount function with a smooth neural network model, drawing conceptual inspiration from DDS (Lee et al., 2024), which uses a kernel density estimator for smooth diversity signals but does not optimize the objective. The paper also builds on CVT archives (Vassiliades et al., 2018) to handle high-dimensional measure spaces and on prior latent space illumination work (Fontaine et al., 2021b) for the LSI domain. The QDDM setting is a novel framing that repurposes the manifold hypothesis to let users specify desired measures via datasets rather than hand-designing low-dimensional measure functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DMS makes QD practical for high-dimensional measure spaces (images, text, audio), which were previously intractable due to distortion causing CMA-MAE to stagnate. The QDDM framing lowers the barrier to QD by letting users specify measures through existing datasets, potentially broadening QD's applicability across machine learning. Key limitations include the computational overhead of training the discount model, the model's inherent noise that can hinder fine objective optimization (as seen in TA-MNIST where DMS does not beat CMA-MAE), and the low absolute coverage in complex domains like LSI (Hiker) at only 3.77%. Open questions include whether more expressive discount model architectures (CNNs, transformers) or alternative training targets could improve smoothness and reduce noise.
