## Problem

Existing image-to-point cloud registration methods face a fundamental trade-off: matching-free approaches (e.g., DeepI2P) rely on coarse frustum classification that lacks fine-grained geometric supervision, while matching-based methods suffer from projection-induced correspondence ambiguity (multiple geometrically distinct 3D points project to the same image patch) and depth-induced scale inconsistency (fixed-size image patches correspond to 3D regions of vastly different physical sizes). These limitations degrade the reliability of learned 2D-3D correspondences and ultimately harm pose estimation accuracy, especially in complex outdoor scenes.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 160×512 for KITTI or 160×320 for nuScenes) and a 3D LiDAR point cloud (uniformly downsampled to 40,960 points, voxelized at 15 cm) from the same scene, along with known camera intrinsics. It produces a 6-DoF camera pose (rotation in SO(3) parameterized via 6D representation, and translation in ℝ³) expressed in the point cloud coordinate frame, alongside per-patch 3D ray bundles encoded in 6D Plücker coordinates. The image is downsampled by a factor of 8 (yielding 20×64 or 20×40 patch grids), and the method operates entirely at this reduced resolution for efficiency.

## Method

RAYI2P proposes a two-stage ray-based pipeline that replaces explicit 2D-3D correspondence matching with continuous 3D ray prediction. In the ray prediction stage, ResNet and KPConv extract patch and point features respectively; an overlapping region detector masks non-visible 3D points; then L=2 iterations of alternating self-attention (within image patches) and cross-attention (patches attending to visible points) fuse cross-modal information, with a focus loss constraining attention to spatially local neighborhoods (σ=32 px). A lightweight MLP head predicts 6D Plücker-coordinate rays per patch. In the pose regression stage, a learnable module concatenates fused patch features, predicted rays (in LiDAR coordinates), and reference rays (computed from intrinsics in camera coordinates), applies average pooling and MLP refinement, and outputs rotation and translation via two MLP heads. The key insight is that representing each image patch as a 3D ray naturally resolves projection ambiguity (a ray encodes a viewing direction rather than a single point) and scale inconsistency (ray directions are depth-invariant), while providing dense, direction-aware geometric supervision that is finer-grained than frustum classification.

## Results

On KITTI, RAYI2P achieves RTE of 0.09±0.08 m, RRE of 0.63±0.71°, and registration accuracy of 99.75%, outperforming all matching-based and matching-free baselines. On nuScenes, it reaches RTE of 0.39±0.29 m, RRE of 1.48±5.72°, and accuracy of 96.61%. In terms of efficiency, RAYI2P runs at 0.11 s per inference on a single RTX 3090 with a 49.24 MB model, roughly 2× faster than the next-best methods (CoFiI2P at 0.18 s, VP2P-match at 0.19 s) and over 80× faster than DeepI2P variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAYI2P outperforms all seven baselines on both datasets. On KITTI, it reduces RTE by 0.11 m and RRE by 0.61° over the strongest matching-based competitor ICL (0.20 m / 1.24°), and by 0.23 m / 1.02° over GraphI2P† (0.32 m / 1.65°), while also achieving the highest accuracy (99.75% vs. 99.61% for GraphI2P†). On nuScenes, RAYI2P improves RTE by 0.24 m and RRE by 0.65° over ICL (0.63 m / 2.13°), and by 0.10 m / 0.25° over GraphI2P† (0.49 m / 1.73°), though GraphI2P† retains a higher accuracy (99.48% vs. 96.61%) due to its reliance on an external depth estimator. Against older matching-based methods, the gap is substantial: VP2P-match (0.75 m / 3.29° on KITTI) and CorrI2P (3.78 m / 5.89°) are far behind. All matching-free baselines (DeepI2P variants, Grid Cls.+PnP) are orders of magnitude worse, with RTEs above 2.7 m and accuracies below 38%, confirming that the ray-based formulation surpasses both coarse frustum-classification and explicit 2D-3D matching paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAYI2P departs from both established paradigms in image-to-point cloud registration: matching-free methods (e.g., DeepI2P) that regress pose from coarse frustum classification, and matching-based methods (e.g., CorrI2P, VP2P-match, ICL, GraphI2P) that construct explicit 2D-3D correspondences followed by PnP solvers. The key conceptual departure is replacing discrete pixel-to-point matching with continuous 3D ray bundles encoded in Plücker coordinates, drawing on generalized camera models and the recent 'cameras as rays' formulation (Zhang et al., 2024). Practically, it reuses standard backbones (ResNet, KPConv) and borrows the overlapping region detector from ICL, but the cross-modal attention-to-ray-prediction pipeline and the differentiable ray-guided pose regression module are novel architectural contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ray-based formulation offers a principled geometric resolution to two persistent failure modes of matching-based registration—projection ambiguity and depth-induced scale inconsistency—without requiring external depth estimators or graph-based correspondence search. The method achieves strong SOTA results (e.g., 0.09 m RTE on KITTI vs. 0.20 m for ICL) at very low inference cost (0.11 s), making it practical for real-time localization. However, the acknowledged dependence on accurate overlap prediction remains a fundamental limitation: when the overlap mask is entirely wrong, registration fails completely, and the authors themselves flag overlap-independent registration as the key open problem for future work.
