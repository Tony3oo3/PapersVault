## Problem

Existing micro-expression recognition methods rely on pixel-level motion descriptors such as optical flow and frame difference, which are highly sensitive to identity, describe displacement fields rather than the semantic meaning of muscle movements, and generalize poorly across subjects and domains. Combined with the scarcity of annotated ME data, this limits both recognition accuracy and the ability to transfer or generate micro-expressions. The paper asks whether facial muscle movements can be discretized into compact, identity- and domain-invariant semantic tokens that serve as a unified vocabulary for ME analysis.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of temporally related RGB facial images (onset and offset frames, typically 0.25–0.5 s apart) and produces a discrete h×w action-token index map (16 tokens with a 32-entry codebook in the optimal configuration), which is flattened into a 1D sequence for downstream processing. The primary output is an emotion class label, but the learned tokens also enable generation of new facial images by selectively altering token indices on an arbitrary identity or in-the-wild face. Pretraining uses ~1M image pairs sampled at 8–15 frame gaps from VoxCeleb (7,000+ identities), while fine-tuning and evaluation use standard MER benchmarks at 100–200 fps.

## Method

D-FACE adapts a conditional VQ-VAE (originally designed for latent action quantization in robotics) to the facial domain by refining the codebook size and latent action length, using a spatial Transformer followed by a causal Transformer to model the directed transition between two frames, and computing the motion embedding as the feature difference D = E₂ − E₁ before vector quantization into a 32-entry codebook with NSVQ noise substitution. A key empirical finding is that the same token activates different muscle motions depending on its sequence position, revealing order-dependent semantics that motivate flattening the 2D token map into a 1D sequence modeled by a Transformer with 1D positional embeddings. A sparse attention pooling mechanism with an entropy penalty focuses on the small subset of informative tokens rather than distributing attention uniformly. An emotion-description-guided CLIP (EDCLIP) alignment contrasts the pooled action feature against frozen CLIP text embeddings of hand-crafted emotion descriptions, while a margin-based constraint pushes the ambiguous 'others' category away from all text anchors. The entire pipeline is pretrained self-supervised on VoxCeleb and then fine-tuned with a combined loss of cross-entropy classification, reconstruction, sparsity, and EDCLIP terms.

## Results

Under the CDE protocol (composite of CASME-II, SMIC-HS, SAMM), D-FACE achieves a UF1 of 0.8943 and UAR of 0.8967, setting a new state of the art. On CASME-II (5-class), it reaches 84.74% accuracy, 0.8571 UF1, and 0.8694 UAR. On the larger CAS(ME)³ dataset (4-class, 921 clips), it attains 0.6807 UF1 and 0.6469 UAR, outperforming the previous best by over 4% in UF1. The method also achieves the best per-dataset UF1 on CASME-II (0.9738) and SMIC-HS (0.8422), though it ranks second on SAMM (0.8716 UF1) due to grayscale input mismatch with its RGB pretraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D-FACE outperforms all 16 compared baselines on the composite CDE protocol, with the closest competitor being LTR3O (Zhu et al., 2025) at 0.8931 UF1 vs. D-FACE's 0.8943, a narrow 0.12% margin. On CASME-II (5-class), D-FACE leads all methods including SODA (Zhang et al., 2025; 0.8418 ACC) and MER-CLIP (Liu et al., 2025; 0.8233 ACC) by 0.56% and 2.41% in accuracy respectively. On CAS(ME)³, D-FACE surpasses MER-CLIP by 4.02% in UF1 (0.6807 vs. 0.6544) and 3.64% in UAR, despite MER-CLIP additionally leveraging action unit annotations. The method also beats SRMCL (Bao et al., 2024) by 3.13% UF1 on the composite set and HTNet (Wang et al., 2024) by 3.40% UF1. The sole per-dataset loss is on SAMM, where LTR3O achieves 0.8912 UF1 vs. D-FACE's 0.8716, attributed to SAMM's grayscale images conflicting with D-FACE's RGB pretraining. For the secondary AU recognition task, D-FACE achieves 0.776 average F1 on CASME-II, surpassing Li et al. (2021) at 0.726 without any task-specific design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in micro-expression recognition (MER) that relies on pixel-level motion descriptors (optical flow, frame difference) by introducing discrete semantic-level facial action tokens learned via a conditional VQ-VAE. The architecture is adapted from the latent action pretraining framework of Ye et al. (2025), originally designed for robotic control with a small codebook and short action sequences, and repurposed for facial motion by enlarging the codebook, extending the latent sequence, and pretraining on large-scale facial video (VoxCeleb). The EDCLIP alignment module extends CLIP's contrastive mechanism with a margin-based constraint specifically for the ambiguous 'others' category, and NSVQ is incorporated to stabilize the quantization-to-decoding pipeline. The key conceptual departure is treating facial motion as a 1D ordered sequence of semantic tokens (analogous to a sentence) rather than a 2D spatial displacement field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from pixel-level to semantic-level motion representation addresses two long-standing limitations of MER: identity sensitivity and poor cross-domain generalization, and it opens the door to controllable ME generation as a byproduct of the learned token vocabulary. The empirical finding that tokens exhibit order-dependent, non-spatial semantics challenges the locality assumption underlying most CNN-based motion models and suggests a fundamentally different inductive bias for facial dynamics. Open questions include whether the small codebook (32 vectors, 16 positions) can scale to capture the full diversity of facial actions, how the method performs on higher-resolution or multi-frame sequences beyond the onset-offset pair, and whether the EDCLIP alignment generalizes to fine-grained emotion subcategories. The grayscale SAMM performance gap also highlights a domain-shift vulnerability in the pretraining pipeline.
