## Problem

Designing proteins with high affinity and selectivity for arbitrary small-molecule ligands remains a major challenge. Existing deep learning methods (RFdiffusionAA, RFdiffusion2) are backbone-only generators that lack explicit atomic-level modeling of protein-ligand interactions and depend on a separate inverse-folding model for sequence design, resulting in low experimental success rates and heavy reliance on expert-crafted constraints. The paper identifies two root causes: the absence of a unified atomic representation spanning both protein and ligand, and the severe distributional imbalance in available protein-ligand structural data.

## Input / Output

The model takes a small-molecule ligand (specified via CCD, SMILES, or SDF) as the primary input, along with two optional conditioning signals: a categorical α-ratio label (mainly β, α/β mix, or mainly α) controlling global protein fold, and per-atom relative solvent accessibility labels (discretized into fully buried, partially buried, fully exposed) controlling local ligand exposure. The output is a complete all-atom 3D structure of the protein-ligand complex—protein backbone, all side-chain atoms (14 per residue via the atom14 representation), and ligand atoms—plus the corresponding amino acid sequence, generated end-to-end in a single diffusion process without a separate inverse-folding step.

## Method

Pallatom-Ligand is an all-atom diffusion model (EDM-based) that jointly denoises the coordinates of 14L + l atoms (L protein residues × 14 atoms each + l ligand atoms) in a single forward pass. Its core architecture is a triple-attention transformer with three interacting streams: token-level full attention (where each 14-atom protein residue is one token but each ligand atom is its own token), atom-level block sparse attention (operating on all individual atoms with a 32×128 block mask), and token-pair triangle attention (updating pair representations from inter-token distances). Information flows bidirectionally between token and atom levels via layer-norm-gated up/down projections, enabling multi-scale refinement of backbone geometry and side-chain/ligand packing. Training uses a dual-objective sampling strategy—alternating 1:1 between structure-clustered crops (learning global folds) and ligand-clustered local crops (learning binding interfaces)—to counteract the long-tailed ligand distribution. The composite loss combines a weighted diffusion loss (10× for ligand atoms, 3× for pocket atoms, 1× for the rest), sequence cross-entropy, smooth-LDDT, and distogram terms, with hierarchical condition dropout to support flexible inference.

## Results

On the 8-ligand benchmark, Pallatom-Ligand (w/SA + MPNN) achieves the highest in silico success rates: 88.0% protein-fold, 18.6% ligand-pocket, and 6.4% ligand-pose success, compared to 58.5%/17.5%/3.5% for RFdiffusion2 and 30.6%/2.8%/0.5% for RFdiffusionAA. On component metrics, it attains the lowest Ca-RMSD (1.36 Å), lowest ipAE (4.35 Å), and highest protein-pLDDT (90.41) among all methods. Pallatom-Ligand successfully generates in silico binders for all eight targets, whereas RFdiffusion2 fails on FAD and SAM, and RFdiffusionAA succeeds on only 3 of 8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pallatom-Ligand (w/SA + MPNN) outperforms both baselines on the majority of metrics. Against RFdiffusionAA, the improvement is dramatic: Ca-RMSD drops from 4.72 to 1.36 Å, ligand-pose success rises from 0.5% to 6.4% (a 13× increase), and ipAE improves from 7.67 to 4.35 Å. Against RFdiffusion2, Pallatom-Ligand achieves substantially higher fold success (88.0% vs. 58.5%) and pose success (6.4% vs. 3.5%), with a markedly lower ipAE (4.35 vs. 7.98 Å), though RFdiffusion2 retains a slight edge on ligand-D.center (4.17 vs. 4.56 Å). A critical practical distinction is target coverage: Pallatom-Ligand produces in silico binders for all 8 ligands, while RFdiffusion2 fails entirely on FAD and SAM, and RFdiffusionAA succeeds on only 3 of 8. The paper also shows that the all-atom representation's advantage is amplified by MPNN sequence redesign, which improves fold quality (Ca-RMSD from 2.80 to 1.39 Å) without degrading ligand binding, confirming that the structural generation quality directly translates to better sequence design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RFdiffusion line of work (RFdiffusionAA, RFdiffusion2) by replacing the backbone-only SE(3) diffusion with a unified all-atom diffusion process that jointly generates protein backbone, side chains, and ligand coordinates. Architecturally, it builds on Pallatom's atom14 representation but replaces its traversing mechanism with a DiT-style triple-attention transformer (token-level full attention, atom-level block sparse attention, token pair triangle attention), drawing design principles from AlphaFold3's multi-stream architecture. The key conceptual departure from prior ligand-binding design methods is the elimination of the two-stage backbone-then-inverse-folding pipeline in favor of end-to-end all-atom generation, motivated by the observation that backbone-only models lack a mechanism for atomic-level refinement at the protein-ligand interface.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Pallatom-Ligand provides the first comprehensive all-atom benchmark for ligand-binding protein design (8 diverse ligands vs. 4 in prior work) with component-specific metrics that decompose success into protein scaffold, ligand pose, and binding interface quality, giving the field a more diagnostic evaluation framework. The demonstrated zero-shot generalization to completely unseen synthetic chemotypes (Janelia Fluor dyes, DCZ) suggests the model learns transferable atomic interaction principles rather than memorizing specific ligand-protein pairs. However, all results are in silico with no wet-lab validation, and the model is limited to protein-small-molecule systems; extending to nucleic acid assemblies, covalent binding, and noncanonical amino acids remains open. The dual-objective training strategy and its ablation revealing that fold-centric sampling collapses on rare ligands while interaction-centric sampling degrades backbone quality provides practical guidance for training on imbalanced structural datasets.
