## Problem

Existing generative models for discrete data (discrete diffusion, flow matching) treat counts as unordered categories via masking or uniform noise, ignoring the ordinal structure inherent to integer-valued measurements. Moreover, no principled framework exists for deconvolving aggregated biological observations—such as bulk RNA-seq or spatial transcriptomic spots containing 10–50 cells—into unit-level single-cell count profiles, as prior deconvolution methods only recover cell-type proportions rather than full count distributions.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a noisy integer count state x_t at diffusion time t, concatenated with task-specific side information: Enformer-encoded DNA sequence context and cell-type embeddings for the PBMC application, or single-cell nuclear image patches for the spatial transcriptomics application. For deconvolution, the observed aggregate a_0 (sum of unit-level counts) is provided as a hard constraint. Outputs are integer-valued count vectors on Z^d—nucleotide-level or gene-level expression profiles per cell—that provably sum to the observed aggregate when deconvolution is active. The distributional loss requires drawing multiple i.i.d. samples from the denoiser per training step, unlike standard point-estimate losses.

## Method

Count Bridges define a stochastic bridge on Z^d via independent Poisson birth and death processes (X_t = X_0 + B_t − D_t), yielding closed-form bridge kernels parameterized by a Bessel-distributed slack variable M_t = min(B_t, D_t) and Binomial/Hypergeometric conditional draws for intermediate states. Training uses a strictly proper energy-score loss with an L2^β metric on the integer lattice, which incorporates ordinal geometry and models the full joint posterior q_θ(·|x_t, t) rather than a point estimate—necessitated by the discrete setting where the ELBO is inherently distributional. For deconvolution, an EM procedure treats unit-level counts as latent: the E-step runs projection-guided diffusion (enforcing the aggregate constraint via a first-order KL projection at each denoising step), and the M-step optimizes the aggregate-level energy score. The framework is shown to be an entropy-regularized optimal transport (Schrödinger bridge) on the integer lattice, with the jump intensity κ controlling the regularization strength, recovering discrete OT with cost |x_1 − x_0| as κ → 0.

## Results

On nucleotide-level bulk RNA-seq prediction, Count Bridge reduces bulk MSE from 2.590 (fine-tuned Enformer) to 0.601 and cell-type MSE from 3.142 to 1.410, while also halving the Energy score (28.6 vs 56.8). For cell-type proportion deconvolution of bulk RNA-seq, Count Bridge achieves JSD 0.113, RMSE 0.073, and Spearman 0.267, outperforming CIBERSORTx (JSD 0.194, RMSE 0.109, Spearman 0.079) and MuSiC (JSD 0.313, RMSE 0.140, Spearman 0.186). On spatial transcriptomic deconvolution, Count Bridge beats STDeconvolve on all three metrics (JSD 0.231 vs 0.288, RMSE 0.110 vs 0.177, Spearman 0.332 vs 0.255) and reduces the Energy score for count profiles from 41.7 (spot mean) to 8.9.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Count Bridge dominates all six baselines across every reported metric. Against the generative-modeling baselines (continuous flow matching and discrete flow matching) on synthetic integer distribution-matching tasks, CB achieves the lowest W₂, MMD, and Energy scores, with DFM being more competitive than CFM but still trailing CB. Against fine-tuned Enformer on nucleotide-level sequence-to-expression prediction, CB reduces bulk MSE by 4.3× (0.601 vs 2.590) and cell-type MSE by 2.2× (1.410 vs 3.142). In bulk RNA-seq deconvolution, CB outperforms CIBERSORTx by 42% on JSD (0.113 vs 0.194) and 3.4× on Spearman correlation (0.267 vs 0.079), and beats MuSiC by 64% on JSD (0.113 vs 0.313). For spatial transcriptomics, CB surpasses STDeconvolve on JSD (0.231 vs 0.288), RMSE (0.110 vs 0.177), and Spearman (0.332 vs 0.255), while also producing full single-cell count profiles rather than only cluster-level proportions. Notably, CB's advantage over the spot-mean baseline in spatial deconvolution (Energy 8.9 vs 41.7) confirms it learns meaningful unit-level heterogeneity beyond simple averaging.

## Relation to Prior Work

Count Bridges extends the stochastic interpolant / diffusion bridge framework (Albergo et al. 2023; Peluchetti 2023) from continuous Gaussian noise to a discrete Poisson birth-death process on Z^d, yielding closed-form Binomial–Hypergeometric–Bessel conditionals that respect the ordinal structure of integer counts. It departs from prior discrete diffusion models (Austin et al. 2021; Lou et al. 2023) that treat counts as unordered categories via masking or uniform noise, and generalizes Blackout Diffusion (Santos et al. 2023) from a pure-death absorbing process to a two-sided birth-death bridge enabling transport between arbitrary distributions. The training loss draws on distributional diffusion with strictly proper scoring rules (De Bortoli et al. 2025), and the deconvolution procedure adapts the EM framework for diffusion priors from Rozet et al. (2024) to the aggregate-to-unit setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first principled discrete-native generative model for integer count data with exact tractable sampling, bridging a gap between continuous diffusion (which ignores discreteness) and categorical discrete diffusion (which ignores ordinal structure). The EM-based deconvolution to unit-level count profiles — rather than the cell-type proportions that dominate the spatial transcriptomics and bulk RNA-seq literature — opens a path toward recovering full single-cell expression from aggregated measurements. Key open questions include: identifiability bounds as group sizes grow (the paper shows deconvolution fundamentally requires between-group heterogeneity), whether the first-order projection surrogate can be replaced with a theoretically grounded sampler, and whether Euclidean models close the gap when counts are large enough to be well-approximated as continuous.
