## Problem

Translating exocentric (third-person) views into egocentric (first-person) views is fundamentally under-constrained due to occlusions, restricted fields of view, and appearance changes across viewpoints. Existing methods rely on restrictive inputs such as multi-view images, known relative camera poses, or an initial egocentric frame, and depend heavily on 2D hand layout predictions that fail under occlusion or clutter, limiting their practical applicability to single-view, unconstrained settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single exocentric RGB image (512×512×3) as its sole input and produces a dense egocentric RGB image (512×512×3) as output. Internally, the pipeline extracts a scale-calibrated point cloud, a 3D egocentric hand pose (N keypoints), and a free-form textual scene description, which together condition the generation. The sparse egocentric RGB map obtained by reprojecting the point cloud serves as a partial inpainting hint rather than a complete structural guide.

## Method

EgoWorld uses a two-stage pipeline. In the observation stage, an off-the-shelf depth estimator and hand pose estimator produce a metrically-scaled point cloud (via a median scale factor derived from the MANO hand mesh), and a lightweight ViT+MLP regressor predicts the 3D egocentric hand pose directly from the exocentric image; the Umeyama algorithm then computes the exo-to-ego transformation to reproject the point cloud into a sparse egocentric map, while a VLM generates a textual scene description. In the reconstruction stage, a latent diffusion model (frozen VAE encoder/decoder with a U-Net denoiser) takes a 9-channel latent input—concatenating the 4-channel sparse map embedding, a 1-channel projected hand pose embedding, and the 4-channel noisy latent—and is guided by CLIP text embeddings via cross-attention with classifier-free guidance. The key insight is that geometric cues (point cloud, 3D pose) anchor spatial structure while semantic cues (text) control appearance and object identity, enabling robust generalization to unseen objects, actions, scenes, and subjects without requiring multi-view inputs or known camera poses at inference.

## Results

On H2O, EgoWorld achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four unseen scenarios. On unseen objects, it reduces FID from 59.615 (CFLD) to 41.334 (30% relative reduction) and improves PSNR from 25.922 to 31.171 (+5.2 dB). On unseen actions, FID drops to 33.284 (35% reduction over CFLD) with PSNR of 31.620. Cross-dataset evaluation confirms consistent gains: on TACO, FID improves by ~39% (61.357→37.191); on Assembly101, PSNR improves by 4.4 dB (20.998→25.365); and on Ego-Exo4D, FID drops 13% (70.476→61.231) with PSNR up 3.4 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoWorld is compared against three baselines: pix2pixHD (single-view GAN), pixelNeRF (NeRF-based novel view synthesis), and CFLD (coarse-to-fine latent diffusion for pose-guided person synthesis). pix2pixHD and pixelNeRF perform substantially worse across all scenarios, with FID values 4–12× higher than EgoWorld, reflecting their inability to handle the large geometric discrepancy between exo- and egocentric views. CFLD is the strongest baseline and the closest competitor, serving as an upper-bound proxy for Exo2Ego (whose code is unavailable). EgoWorld outperforms CFLD on every metric in every scenario: FID reductions range from 13% (Ego-Exo4D) to 39% (TACO), PSNR gains range from 1.4 dB (TACO) to 5.2 dB (H2O unseen objects), and PA-MPJPE is consistently lower (e.g., 7.32 vs. 8.00 mm on H2O unseen objects). The ablation further shows that removing both pose and text conditions (simulating 4Diff) degrades FID from 41.33 to 56.12, confirming that the multi-modal conditioning is essential for the observed gains over CFLD.

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoWorld extends the exocentric-to-egocentric translation line of work (Exo2Ego, 4Diff, Exo2Ego-V, EgoExo-Gen) by replacing their reliance on 2D hand layouts, known relative camera poses, or multi-view inputs with a two-stage pipeline that fuses geometric reasoning (point cloud projection via Umeyama alignment of 3D hand poses) and semantic diffusion (LDM conditioned on sparse RGB maps, pose embeddings, and CLIP text). The generative backbone is the standard LDM framework (Rombach et al., 2022), while the geometric front-end repurposes off-the-shelf depth estimation, MANO-based hand pose, and a lightweight ViT regressor for egocentric hand pose. The key conceptual departure from prior methods is treating the translation as an inpainting problem over a projected point cloud rather than a direct image-to-image or multi-view synthesis task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck for AR/VR, robotics, and instructional video applications where only a single third-person image is available and no calibrated multi-view rig or known camera poses exist. Strong generalization across four datasets and in-the-wild smartphone captures suggests the multi-modal conditioning strategy (geometry + semantics) is more robust than 2D-layout-only approaches. Open questions include temporal consistency for video sequences, reducing dependence on off-the-shelf depth and pose estimators (whose errors propagate into the point cloud), and whether the approach scales to more complex multi-object or multi-person scenes.
