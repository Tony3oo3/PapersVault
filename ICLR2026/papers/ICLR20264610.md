## Problem

State-of-the-art synchrony-based object discovery models like Rotating Features (RF) produce distributed representations across orientation dimensions, requiring post-hoc k-means clustering to recover discrete objects. In overlap regions, features from different objects drift away from cluster centers, making assignments uncertain and degrading performance precisely where robust binding is most needed. The paper asks whether enforcing orthogonality in the orientation space can collapse each object onto a single dimension, eliminating the clustering step and converting overlap uncertainty into a reliable occlusion signal.

## Input / Output

The model takes grayscale or binary images (e.g., 4Shapes at 64×64, MNIST&Shape, synthetic SEM layer stacks) and lifts each pixel's scalar feature into an n-dimensional vector initialized to zero along the orientation axis, so that magnitude encodes feature presence and orientation encodes object affiliation. The output is twofold: a reconstructed image (sigmoid of the per-pixel magnitude of final-layer activations) and per-object feature maps obtained by thresholding the pre-gated ψ_final at 0.1, which directly yields binarized object masks without any clustering. Crucially, ψ_final also contains occlusion-complete shapes, whereas the gated z_final shows holes at occluded regions, providing a visibility signal.

## Method

OrthoRF extends the RF convolutional autoencoder with two architectural modifications. First, a competitive binding mechanism applies a per-layer softmax (with per-feature mean centering) over the n orientation components, producing winner-take-most assignments that drive each object to specialize on a single dimension. Second, an orthogonality loss penalizes the squared off-diagonal entries of the Gram matrix computed from centered encoder-output vectors, enforcing near-90° separation between object representations in phase space. The total objective is L_REC + λ·L_ortho, trained with Adam and cosine annealing. The resulting one-hot-like encodings make objects directly readable from distinct dimensions, and the magnitude gating in the final layer acts as a visibility gate that suppresses occluded regions while the pre-gated ψ retains shape priors for occlusion completion.

## Results

On the 4Shapes dataset, OrthoRF (n=5, thresholded ψ_final) achieves ARI-BG of 0.9934 and MBOi of 0.9843, matching RF's best visible-only performance (ARI-BG 0.992, MBOi 0.974) while dramatically outperforming it on shape completion (MBOi^OV: 0.9832 vs. 0.8172). On the synthetic SEM dataset with severe occlusion, OrthoRF surpasses RF on both noise-free (ARI-BG: 0.9908 vs. 0.9551) and noisy (ARI-BG: 0.8356 vs. 0.7043) settings. On MNIST&Shape, OrthoRF achieves the highest ARI-BG (0.996) among all compared methods, while on the variable-count Shapes dataset it leads with ARI-BG 0.833 and MBOi 0.865.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RF (Lowe et al., 2024a), OrthoRF matches or slightly exceeds visible-only discovery (ARI-BG 0.9934 vs. 0.992 at n=5 vs. n=7) but achieves a large gain on shape completion (MBOi^OV 0.9832 vs. 0.8172), primarily because thresholding ψ_final permits multi-label pixels in overlap regions where k-means forces a single assignment. On the SEM dataset, OrthoRF clearly outperforms RF under both clean (ARI-BG 0.9908 vs. 0.9551) and noisy (0.8356 vs. 0.7043) conditions, and shows stronger out-of-distribution robustness (clean→noisy: 0.9908→0.9836). Against CAE (Lowe et al., 2022), OrthoRF substantially outperforms on 4Shapes (ARI-BG 0.9934 vs. 0.694) and MNIST&Shape (0.996 vs. 0.971). Slot Attention (Locatello et al., 2020) and DBM (Salakhutdinov & Hinton, 2009) both fail on MNIST&Shape (ARI-BG 0.089 and 0.175 respectively), while OrthoRF achieves 0.996. On the variable-count Shapes dataset, OrthoRF outperforms RF (ARI-BG 0.833 vs. 0.744), AKOrNattn (0.833 vs. 0.713), and ItrSA (0.833 vs. 0.570), with the discrete orthogonal representation contributing to improved separation when object count and types vary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Rotating Features (RF; Lowe et al., 2024a), which itself generalizes Complex-valued Autoencoders (CAE; Lowe et al., 2022) from 2D complex planes to n-dimensional vector-valued activations for synchrony-based object binding. The key departure is the addition of two components: a softmax-based competitive binding mechanism (borrowed conceptually from Slot Attention) that drives per-feature specialization across orientation components, and an inner-product orthogonality loss that enforces 90° separation between object representations in phase space. Together, these convert RF's distributed, multi-dimensional object encodings into discrete, one-hot-like assignments, eliminating the post-hoc k-means clustering that all prior synchrony-based models require. The orthogonality constraint draws on a broader literature showing that orthogonal representations improve discrimination and disentanglement in deep networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OrthoRF demonstrates that a simple orthogonality inductive bias can regularize distributed synchrony-based representations into discrete, directly interpretable object encodings, removing a practical pipeline bottleneck (post-hoc clustering) and enabling a new capability: recovery of occluded object parts through the pre-gating intermediate representation ψ. This is notable because neither slot-based nor prior synchrony-based models have demonstrated this occlusion-completion property. Limitations include slower convergence (~2× training steps), occasional training instability (multi-object collapse onto one phase axis), and performance degradation on RGB datasets. Open questions include whether the orthogonality principle transfers to attention-based or video OCL settings, and whether more parameter-efficient encodings can offset the computational cost of vector-valued lifting.
