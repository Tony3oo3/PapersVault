## Problem

Layout-to-image (L2I) generation suffers from two persistent issues: attribute leakage caused by entangled instance features in cross-attention (where one object's color or shape bleeds into another), and poor generalization to novel scenes when trained on limited paired data. Prior attention-manipulation approaches are bottlenecked by CLIP's inability to isolate singular attributes from complex prompts, and adapting to new domains typically requires expensive collection of additional image-text pairs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262042/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a global text prompt, up to 10 instance-level captions, and corresponding bounding boxes (top-left and bottom-right coordinates) as input, and produces a single 1024×1024 RGB image with all instances rendered at their specified locations. A key distinction is that the RL stage consumes only unpaired prompt-only data (captions + boxes, no reference images) from COCO-2014-MIG, while the supervised diffusion training uses paired data from LayoutSAM. The framework is architecture-agnostic, supporting both UNet-based (SD 1.5) and MM-DiT-based (SD3) backbones with minor modifications to where IDM is deployed and the training objective (noise prediction vs. velocity prediction).

## Method

I-DRUID combines two synergistic components. First, an Instance Disentanglement Module (IDM) receives n+1 enhanced features (n instances + global prompt) and layout masks, and learns channel-wise weighting scores α that decompose features into semantic-related R⁺ and spurious R⁻; the Instance Disentanglement Constraint (IDC) supervises this via a CAS metric that measures background attention dispersion, enforcing that R⁺ triggers more spatially accurate attention maps than R⁻ through a Softplus ranking loss. Second, a PPO-based RL stage converts SD3's deterministic flow-matching ODE into an SDE (with σₜ = a√(t/(1−t)), a=0.7) to enable exploration, uses Grounding-DINO as an online reward model scoring IoU + detection confidence, and employs a lightweight critic-net for advantage estimation. RL is applied only at the first 20% of denoising timesteps for speed (15 s/iter vs. 83 s/iter for full-trajectory), and the two components jointly optimize via L_act = L_ldm + λ_dis·L_dis + λ_rl·L_rl, with IDM providing cleaner representations that make RL's trajectory sampling more effective.

## Results

On COCO-MIG (OOD generalization benchmark), I-DRUID (SD-1.5) achieves 69.13% average Instance Success Rate (ISR) and 68.18% average mIoU, outperforming the best baseline MIGC (59.68% ISR, 52.60% mIoU) by ~9.5 and ~15.6 points respectively. On LayoutSAM-eval (in-distribution L2I benchmark), I-DRUID (SD-3) achieves 93.14% spatial accuracy, 77.20% shape accuracy, FID of 17.21, and PickScore of 23.16, surpassing the strongest baseline Creati-Layout (92.67% spatial, 75.93% shape, FID 19.10, PickScore 22.02) across all metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

I-DRUID (SD-1.5) outperforms all ten baselines on COCO-MIG by a wide margin: it beats MIGC (the strongest baseline at 59.68% avg ISR) by 9.45 points and Creati-Layout (57.67%) by 11.46 points on ISR, and surpasses MIGC's mIoU (52.60) by 15.58 points. Notably, InstanceDiff and Creati-Layout—both trained with detailed instance captions from LayoutSAM—fail to outperform MIGC (trained with short captions), confirming the generalization gap that I-DRUID's RL stage addresses. On LayoutSAM-eval, I-DRUID (SD-3) edges out Creati-Layout on every metric (spatial 93.14 vs. 92.67, FID 17.21 vs. 19.10, PickScore 23.16 vs. 22.02, IS 22.45 vs. 22.04), while the SD-1.5 variant trails Creati-Layout on spatial accuracy (86.95 vs. 92.67) and FID (22.92 vs. 19.10) but still surpasses MIGC, InstanceDiff, HiCo, Ranni, and BeYourself on most attribute scores. The SD-3 variant's lower COCO-MIG scores relative to SD-1.5 are attributed to text-token dilution in MM-DiT's joint attention, a known limitation of the SD3 architecture for fine-grained instance rendering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the layout-to-image (L2I) generation line of work (GLIGEN, InstanceDiffusion, MIGC, Creati-Layout) by introducing two orthogonal components: an instance disentanglement module (IDM) supervised by a novel attention-based constraint (IDC) to combat attribute leakage, and a PPO-based reinforcement learning stage that leverages unpaired prompt-only data via Grounding-DINO rewards. The RL formulation builds on recent flow-matching RL work (Flow-GRPO, DPOK, DDPO) but adapts it to the deterministic ODE setting of SD3 through an SDE conversion, and is the first to apply RL specifically to the L2I task. Unlike prior L2I methods that are largely UNet-based, the paper demonstrates compatibility with both UNet (SD-1.5) and MM-DiT (SD3) architectures, addressing a gap noted in the Creati-Layout work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in decoupling L2I improvement from expensive paired-data collection: the RL stage uses only unpaired prompts with AI-generated rewards, making novel-scene adaptation substantially cheaper. The synergy between disentanglement and RL (IDM provides cleaner representations that make RL trajectories easier to optimize) is a useful design principle for future controllable generation. Limitations include persistent failures on small text and human body rendering (inherited from the base diffusion model), and the reward design (IoU + confidence) is sensitive to weighting, as shown in the ablation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more than 10 instances, whether more sophisticated reward models (e.g., VLM-based scoring) would further improve results, and whether the disentanglement constraint transfers to other multi-object generation settings beyond bounding-box conditioning.
