## Problem

Existing graph diffusion models treat graphs as collections of pairwise edges and overlook higher-order topological structures (triangles, cliques, rings, motifs) that are defining characteristics of real-world systems in chemistry, neuroscience, and social networks. No prior graph generative model integrates higher-order topology as an explicit guiding signal during the denoising process, causing intermediate diffusion states to collapse into structureless noise and limiting the model's ability to capture the hierarchical organization of real graphs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263787/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model samples a Gaussian prior in the spectral domain (node feature matrix X and Laplacian eigenvalue vector Λ) and pairs it with a fixed eigenbasis U₀ drawn from the training set. The reverse diffusion proceeds through K hierarchical stages, each conditioned on a coarser cell-complex-filtered intermediate graph, ultimately reconstructing the full adjacency matrix as L = U₀ΛU₀ᵀ. Noise is injected in the Laplacian spectrum rather than the adjacency matrix, exploiting permutation invariance and structural robustness of spectral representations.

## Method

HOG-Diff implements a coarse-to-fine generation curriculum by first extracting higher-order skeletons via cell complex filtering (CCF), which lifts the graph into a cell complex and retains only nodes/edges belonging to p-dimensional cells as intermediate guides. The diffusion process is structured into K hierarchical time windows, with transitions between consecutive intermediate states realized by a generalized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (GOU) diffusion bridge derived via Doob's h-transform, yielding closed-form transition probabilities that enable simulation-free training. The score network combines a GCN for local feature aggregation and a graph transformer for global information extraction, fused through FiLM time conditioning, and is trained with a conditional score-matching loss over both node features and Laplacian eigenvalues. Theoretically, the paper proves that the multi-stage conditional score has a smaller smoothness constant than the classical single-stage score, yielding faster SGD convergence, and establishes a tighter reconstruction error bound for HOG-Diff relative to standard diffusion models.

## Results

On molecular benchmarks, HOG-Diff achieves the best FCD (0.172 on QM9, 1.633 on ZINC250k) and NSPDK (0.0003 on QM9, 0.001 on ZINC250k), outperforming the strongest baselines DeFoG and Cometh by 35–75% on FCD. On generic graph generation, HOG-Diff attains the lowest average MMD on Community-small (0.010) and Enzymes (0.027), and ties DiGress on Ego-small (0.016). On higher-order topological preservation (curvature filtrations), HOG-Diff achieves the lowest κ_FR and κ_OR distances across all eight datasets, e.g., 0.077/0.098 on QM9 versus 0.177/0.286 for DeFoG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOG-Diff outperforms all 15 baselines on the primary distributional metrics (FCD, NSPDK) across every molecular dataset. On QM9, it reduces FCD from 0.248 (Cometh) and 0.268 (DeFoG) to 0.172, and NSPDK from 0.0005 to 0.0003; on ZINC250k it cuts FCD from 2.030 (DeFoG) to 1.633. On MOSES, HOG-Diff achieves the highest validity (99.7%) and lowest FCD (0.94), beating DeFoG (92.8%, 1.95) and Cometh (90.5%, 1.27). On GuacaMol, it leads in FCD score (78.5 vs. 73.8 for DeFoG) while matching DeFoG on validity (99.1%). On generic graphs, HOG-Diff dominates Community-small (avg MMD 0.010 vs. 0.037 for GPrinFlowNet) and Enzymes (0.027 vs. 0.030 for DiGress), and ties DiGress on Ego-small (0.016). On the higher-order topological metric (curvature filtrations, Table 3), HOG-Diff achieves the lowest distance on all eight datasets, with particularly large margins on QM9 (κ_FR 0.077 vs. 0.177 for DeFoG) and Community-small (6.734 vs. 12.515 for GDSS).

## Relation to Prior Work

HOG-Diff sits at the intersection of three research threads: score-based diffusion for graph generation (Song et al., 2021), spectral-domain diffusion that operates on Laplacian eigenvalues rather than adjacency matrices (Luo et al., 2023a), and the generalized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck bridge process for endpoint-conditioned SDEs (Luo et al., 2023b). The key departure from prior graph diffusion work is the explicit use of higher-order topology—specifically cell complexes obtained via lifting and filtering—as a coarse-to-fine generative guide, rather than treating graphs purely as pairwise edge collections. While prior methods like DiGress, GDSS, and DeFoG denoise in the spectral or discrete domain, none incorporate topological structure as an intermediate conditioning signal, and the paper positions itself as the first to do so.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that higher-order topology is not merely a descriptive property of real graphs but an actionable generative signal: by conditioning diffusion bridges on cell-complex-filtered skeletons, HOG-Diff achieves consistent improvements on both pairwise and higher-order metrics across eight benchmarks, with theoretical guarantees of faster score-matching convergence and tighter reconstruction error bounds. The interpretability angle—using different topological guides as diagnostic probes—is a practical contribution for understanding which structural motifs drive generation. However, the method's effectiveness depends on the presence of explicit higher-order structures, limiting its applicability to graphs with sparse or absent such structures (as seen on Ego-small), and the authors acknowledge that adaptive dimension selection and alternative filtering mechanisms remain open.
