## Problem

Most offline RL methods require action labels in the dataset, yet in many practical domains (healthcare, finance, robotics) actions are absent due to privacy, storage, or sensor constraints, leaving only (s, r, s') tuples. No prior work has demonstrated a scalable framework that can (a) learn effective state policies from such action-free data, (b) scale to high-dimensional control problems, and (c) use the learned policies to accelerate online RL across diverse environments.

## Input / Output

The method consumes action-free offline datasets of (s, r, s') tuples with state vectors of up to 78 dimensions (from D4RL and the Action-factorised DeepMind Suite), with all action information removed before pre-training. It produces Q-values over discretised state differences Δs ∈ {-1, 0, 1}^M, where each dimension is determined by a z-score-normalised threshold comparison (scale-invariant, no per-dimension tuning). During online deployment, an inverse dynamics model translates the predicted Δs into executable continuous or discrete actions, and a policy-switching mechanism with annealed probability β blends these guided actions with the online agent's own policy.

## Method

OSO-DecQN adapts the DecQN value-decomposition framework to the action-free setting by replacing discrete actions with discretised state differences, decomposing the Q-function across state-difference dimensions to maintain linear scalability in M. The key design choice is a simple discretisation transformation that maps each normalised state-difference coordinate to {-1, 0, 1} via a threshold ε, converting an unstable continuous regression problem into a discrete prediction task amenable to Q-learning. A conservative regularisation term (adapted from CQL's penalty) is added to the critic loss to mitigate overestimation bias and enforce state reachability constraints unique to action-free pre-training. For guided online learning, a lightweight inverse dynamics model (IDM) trained from scratch with L1 loss concurrently with the online agent translates Δs predictions into actions, while a policy-switching mechanism with linearly annealed β smoothly transitions from offline-guided exploration to the agent's own learned policy. The paper also provides a theoretical value bound showing the discretisation error scales as O(H√M/k) with k bins per coordinate.

## Results

On D4RL and Action-factorised DeepMind Suite benchmarks, OSO-DecQN achieves normalized returns that match or exceed action-labeled behavioral cloning (BC) references across all 20 dataset-environment combinations. For example, on Hopper-medium-expert, OSO-DecQN reaches 110±1.3 vs. BC's 51.3 and BC-Δs's 51.9±2.1; on Walker2d-medium-replay, it achieves 84.8±2.2 vs. BC's 23.5. In guided online learning (Figure 1), OSO-DecQN consistently improves both convergence speed and asymptotic performance over unguided TD3 and DecQN_N baselines across continuous and discrete action tasks, scaling to 78-dimensional state spaces (Quadruped-Walk). Against the action-free baseline AF-Guide (Zhu et al., 2023), OSO-DecQN consistently outperforms, while AF-Guide underperforms even the unguided TD3 baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AF-Guide (Zhu et al., 2023), the only other action-free guided online learning method, OSO-DecQN consistently outperforms across all three D4RL environments tested (Hopper, HalfCheetah, Walker2d), while AF-Guide underperforms even the unguided TD3 baseline (Figure 2). Against offline-to-online methods that use action labels—O3F (Mark et al., 2022), IQL (Kostrikov et al., 2021), CAL-QL (Nakamoto et al., 2023), and AWAC (Luo et al., 2023)—OSO-DecQN starts from a lower initial performance (since it has no action policy to fine-tune) but remains competitive on Walker2D, demonstrating that action-free pre-training can extract meaningful guidance (Figure 6, Appendix G). Within the pre-training analysis, OSO-DecQN dramatically outperforms DecQN_N without regularisation (e.g., 110 vs. 1.1 on Hopper-medium-expert), confirming that conservative regularisation is essential to prevent overestimation and state-reachability failures. OSO-DecQN also substantially exceeds the discrete-state imitation baseline BC-Δs (e.g., 84.8 vs. 37.5 on Walker2d-medium-replay), showing that RL-based value learning can improve beyond what the dataset trajectories alone support. Continuous-target imitation (BC s′ and BC s′−s) performs near-randomly across most datasets, validating the paper's core claim that discretisation is critical for stable state policy learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the value-decomposition framework of DecQN (Seyde et al., 2022), which decomposes Q-values across action dimensions, by instead decomposing across discretised state-difference dimensions to handle the absence of action labels. It adapts the conservative regularisation of CQL (Kumar et al., 2020) to the discrete state-difference domain to address overestimation bias and state reachability—challenges unique to action-free offline RL. Conceptually, it departs from prior state-based Q-value methods (Edwards et al., 2020; Hepburn et al., 2024) that still require actions for next-state prediction, and from the transformer-based approach of Zhu et al. (2023), by replacing computationally expensive sequence modelling with a lightweight 3-bin discretisation that is scale-invariant via z-score normalisation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap—learning from datasets where actions are absent due to privacy, storage, or sensor constraints (healthcare, finance, robotics)—and demonstrates that a simple discretisation plus conservative regularisation can extract useful guidance signals that accelerate online learning up to 78-dimensional state spaces. Key open questions include whether adaptive discretisation (à la Seyde et al., 2024) or more expressive inverse dynamics models could further improve robustness in highly non-linear or multi-modal environments, and how the approach extends to visual data where reward extraction from video remains unsolved. The finding that the IDM architecture has minimal impact on performance suggests the core value lies in the offline state policy, but this was tested only in relatively smooth MuJoCo/DMC dynamics.
