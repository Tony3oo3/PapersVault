## Problem

Standard SSL methods assume that positive pairs share nearly identical latent factors, so the conditional p(z⁺|z) is tightly concentrated. However, naturally paired data (e.g., successive video frames, different photos of the same person) induce complex, potentially multimodal and heteroscedastic conditionals that existing contrastive and distillation-based objectives cannot capture, causing models to discard variant factors and degrade generalization. The paper also proves that heteroscedasticity between paired embeddings is geometrically unavoidable when mapping a flat latent space onto a curved embedding manifold, making this limitation fundamental rather than incidental.

## Input / Output

The method consumes naturally paired observations (x, x⁺) that differ in underlying data-generating factors rather than pixel-level augmentations—e.g., two photos of the same celebrity with different facial attributes, adjacent video segments with stochastic velocity changes, or correlated numerical latents. It produces two outputs: standard embedding vectors f(x) from a frozen encoder/projector, and a latent uncertainty variable r that encodes the unpredictable component of the transition from x to x⁺. The editing function t(f(x), r) yields a modified embedding intended to lie near f(x⁺), and r is structured as a factorized Gaussian (AdaSSL-V) or a sparse vector (AdaSSL-S), enabling interpretable, controllable retrieval of related data points.

## Method

AdaSSL introduces an auxiliary latent variable r to decompose the conditional p(z⁺|z) into p(r|z)·p(z⁺|z,r), then applies the chain rule of mutual information to obtain I(f(x);f(x⁺)) = I(f(x),r;f(x⁺)) − I(r;f(x⁺)|f(x)). The first term is lower-bounded by InfoNCE treating (x,r) as a joint variable with a dot-product similarity s(x,x⁺,r) = ψ₁(x,r)ᵀψ₂(x⁺), where ψ₁ edits f(x) via a lightweight function t conditioned on r; the second term is bounded by a KL divergence between a variational posterior q_φ(r|x,x⁺) and a conditional prior p_θ(r|x), yielding a tractable MI lower bound. Two variants optimize this objective differently: AdaSSL-V learns a variational distribution over r with an MLP editing function, while AdaSSL-S predicts r deterministically from both embeddings, applies a differentiable L₀ sparsity penalty (via Gumbel-Sigmoid), and uses a modular rank-1 affine editing function motivated by sparse latent transitions. The framework is compatible with both contrastive (InfoNCE) and distillation-based (BYOL) SSL losses, and a theoretical proposition establishes that the heteroscedasticity the method models is unavoidable under standard normalization to the unit sphere.

## Results

On CelebA (40 facial attributes, unseen identities), AdaSSL-V achieves 0.5784 linear F₁ on representations under weak augmentation, outperforming InfoNCE with natural pairs (0.5473) and BYOL with natural pairs (0.5465); the ground-truth upper bound AdaSSL+GT reaches 0.6818. On 3DIdent, AdaSSL-V_Linear and AdaSSL-S achieve the best latent recovery R² of 0.9331 and 0.9309 respectively, versus 0.8243 for AnInfoNCE with natural pairs and 0.6691 for β-VAE. On complex multimodal-heteroscedastic numerical data, AdaSSL-V attains 0.6638 OOD R² compared to 0.0395 for InfoNCE and 0.5471 for H-InfoNCE_MLP. On stochastic Moving-MNIST, AdaSSL captures both the invariant digit identity and the variant velocity factor better than BYOL and BYOL+Future baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaSSL-V consistently outperforms all contrastive baselines on CelebA natural pairs: it beats InfoNCE (0.5784 vs 0.5473), AnInfoNCE (0.5784 vs 0.5413), LieSSL (0.5784 vs 0.5029), and H-InfoNCE_MLP (0.5784 vs 0.5521) on representation F₁ under weak augmentation. In the distillation setting, AdaSSL-V over BYOL improves representation F₁ from 0.5465 to 0.5816 and embedding F₁ from 0.4263 to 0.5067, while AdaSSL-S underperforms BYOL in this setting, suggesting sparsity regularization requires the contrastive denominator's information pressure to be effective. On 3DIdent, AdaSSL-V_Linear and AdaSSL-S achieve R² of 0.9331 and 0.9309, substantially exceeding AnInfoNCE (0.8243), InfoNCE (0.8184), and all VAE baselines (≤0.6691), while AdaSSL-V_Additive achieves the best DCI disentanglement score (0.4661) among all methods. On the hardest OOD numerical benchmark with complex multimodal-heteroscedastic conditionals, AdaSSL-V (0.6638) and AdaSSL-S (0.6325) both surpass H-InfoNCE_MLP (0.5471) by a wide margin, while InfoNCE and AnInfoNCE collapse to near-zero R² (0.0395 and 0.1652). The gap to the AdaSSL+GT upper bound (0.6818) indicates that the learned latent r does not yet fully capture the true conditional uncertainty, leaving room for future improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SSL-from-invariance paradigm (InfoNCE, BYOL) by introducing a latent variable r to model the conditional uncertainty p(z⁺|z) between naturally paired data, departing from prior work that assumes simple (isotropic or anisotropic) conditionals. It is conceptually inspired by JEPAs (LeCun, 2022; Garrido et al., 2024), which use latent variables for prediction uncertainty, but differs by inferring r purely from positive pairs rather than requiring external access. The theoretical contribution (Proposition 2.1) formalizes why standard similarity functions fail: mapping a flat latent space onto a curved embedding manifold necessarily induces heteroscedasticity, a geometric constraint not previously made explicit in the SSL literature. The two variants (AdaSSL-V with variational inference, AdaSSL-S with sparse modular edits) plug into both contrastive and distillation objectives without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that heteroscedasticity is geometrically unavoidable in normalized embedding spaces provides a principled explanation for why standard SSL objectives degrade on natural pairs, and the proposed fix is lightweight (a small MLP + KL or L₀ regularizer). This opens a path toward SSL methods that handle multimodal and heteroscedastic conditionals without handcrafted augmentations, which is critical for world modeling, causal representation learning, and fine-grained understanding. Key limitations include the absence of identifiability guarantees, the persistent gap between AdaSSL and the ground-truth upper bound (AdaSSL+GT), and evaluation limited to ResNet backbones rather than ViTs. Open questions include whether the learned r corresponds to interpretable world-model actions at scale, and whether structured transition models can further close the performance gap.
