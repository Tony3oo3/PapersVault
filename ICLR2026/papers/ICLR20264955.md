## Problem

Deep learning models learn intermediate representations that are opaque and do not map to meaningful concepts, and existing concept bottleneck approaches require either direct supervision of intermediate layers or prior knowledge of the concepts. This paper asks whether sparse, local spatial concepts (motifs) can be provably identified from end-to-end supervision alone, without any intermediate labels, and provides both a theoretical identifiability guarantee and a practical algorithm to achieve it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264955/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes spatially-structured inputs of varying modality: 100×100 monochrome images (DIGITCIRCLE), rendered LaTeX code images (LATEX-OCR), 8000 Hz audio spectrograms with 64 channels (AUDIOMNISTSEQUENCE), and RNA sequences (Splicing). It produces two outputs: a sparse binary motif activation tensor of shape I×[n] (spatial indices × motif kinds, e.g., 100×100×10 for DIGITCIRCLE with only 3–6 nonzero entries) and a discrete sequence label (digit order, LaTeX command sequence, or spoken character sequence). Critically, the motif map is a one-hot spatial encoding where each active position indicates which motif kind appears there, and no training signal is provided for this intermediate representation.

## Method

SPARLING enforces extreme activation sparsity (≥99%) through a Spatial Sparsity Layer whose forward pass is ReLU(z − t), where the threshold t is updated via an exponential moving average of per-channel batch quantiles rather than gradient descent, effectively hard-constraining the fraction of nonzero activations to a target density δ. To avoid the unstable optimization landscape at high sparsity, an Adaptive Sparsity Algorithm anneals δ over training: the target density is multiplied by a decay factor (0.75) whenever validation accuracy exceeds a threshold that itself decreases linearly over time. The architecture pairs a local convolutional ĝ (residual blocks with a 17×17 or 65×65 receptive field) followed by the sparsity layer, with an arbitrary ĥ (Transformer with 8 heads, 6 layers) that decodes the sparse motif map into the output sequence. The theoretical contribution is the Motif Identifiability Theorem, which proves that under NON-OVERLAPPING, MOTIF-SUFFICIENCY, and α-MOTIF-NECESSITY assumptions, any model achieving low end-to-end error while matching the true sparsity level must also achieve low motif error, up to a constant factor.

## Results

SPARLING achieves motif error below 10% (across FPE, FNE, and CE metrics) on all three synthetic domains when trained purely end-to-end, with AUDIOMNISTSEQUENCE achieving exactly 0% FPE. The only notable exception is FNE on LATEX-OCR, which is elevated because fraction bars, parentheses, and plus signs are not always recognized (the output can be inferred without them). The learned h model shows high sensitivity to motif perturbations: 99.3% on DIGITCIRCLE, 86.1% on LATEX-OCR, and 93.4% on AUDIOMNISTSEQUENCE. SPARLING incurs a modest end-to-end error penalty relative to a Non-Sparse baseline, but removing the bottleneck and finetuning h (Retrained setting) closes nearly all of this gap, confirming the motif layer itself carries sufficient signal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external methods but instead compares three internal configurations: SPARLING (with spatial sparsity layer), Non-Sparse (no sparsity constraint), and Retrained (bottleneck removed, g frozen, h finetuned). On end-to-end error, SPARLING is consistently worse than Non-Sparse across all three domains, reflecting the information loss from the extreme sparsity constraint. However, the Retrained setting performs as well as or marginally worse than Non-Sparse (the paper notes the difference is tiny and sample accuracies overlap), demonstrating that the sparse motif representation alone is sufficient to recover the end-to-end function. On motif error, SPARLING achieves the lowest FPE and CE, while Non-Sparse models (which lack the sparsity constraint) cannot be evaluated on motif metrics in the same way. The ablation in Figure 4 further shows that even a 2–3× increase in density δ causes CE to rise substantially, confirming that extreme sparsity (>99%) is necessary for motif identifiability rather than merely beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the identifiability literature (HMMs, PCFGs, nonlinear ICA) to the deep learning setting, where latent concepts form an intermediate layer between two arbitrary neural networks. It builds directly on Gupta et al. (2024), which demonstrated that sparse motif layers in an RNA splicing model improve with end-to-end training, but here the authors dispense with approximate starting motif models entirely by imposing stronger structural assumptions (locality, sparsity, non-overlapping). Unlike concept bottleneck models that require intermediate supervision or LLM-guided concept identification, SPARLING recovers spatial concepts purely from end-to-end error, and unlike prior sparse-activation techniques (L1 penalties, KL divergence) that achieve 50–90% sparsity, the spatial sparsity layer enforces >99% sparsity via a quantile-based threshold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Motif Identifiability Theorem provides the first formal guarantee that sparse local latent variables are recoverable from end-to-end supervision alone, under clearly stated assumptions (NON-OVERLAPPING, MOTIF-SUFFICIENCY, α-MOTIF-NECESSITY). However, these assumptions are restrictive: the splicing domain (the motivating real-world application) does not satisfy them, and all positive identifiability results are on synthetic domains. The observed trade-off between end-to-end accuracy and motif accuracy (higher sparsity improves motif identification but degrades end-to-end performance) suggests a fundamental tension that remains unresolved. Open questions include whether the assumptions can be weakened to cover overlapping motifs, whether the approach scales to real-world domains with natural noise, and whether the extreme sparsity requirement generalizes beyond the tested settings.
