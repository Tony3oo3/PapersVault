## Problem

Existing FL-DP privacy analyses rely on composition theorems that produce arbitrarily loose, divergent privacy bounds as the number of communication rounds T grows, implying that constant-level noise cannot sustain privacy over long training. This contradicts empirical evidence and leaves a critical theoretical gap: no prior work has established a convergent (bounded) privacy guarantee for FL-DP under non-convex objectives.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes m clients each holding a local heterogeneous dataset (Dirichlet-split MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, or Tiny-ImageNet), an initial global model w₀, and isotropic Gaussian noise n_i ~ N(0, σ²I_d) added to each client's uploaded parameters. It produces a worst-case f-DP privacy lower bound expressed as the Gaussian trade-off function T_G(·), which converges to a constant as T → ∞ for both Noisy-FedAvg (under four learning-rate schedules) and Noisy-FedProx (with proximal coefficient α > L). The bound is losslessly convertible to (ε,δ)-DP and RDP for comparison with prior work.

## Method

The paper applies the shifted interpolation technique to the federated setting by constructing an auxiliary sequence w̃_t that convexly interpolates between the two adjacent-dataset global trajectories, parameterized by coefficients λ_t ∈ [0,1]. Global sensitivity ‖φ(w_t) − φ′(w̃_t)‖ is decomposed into a data-sensitivity term (bounded by 2V/m · Ση) and a model-sensitivity term (governed by a recursion with coefficient ρ_t > 1 from L-smoothness), then the interpolation coefficients are optimized to minimize the accumulated sensitivity H(λ_t, t₀). For Noisy-FedAvg, four learning-rate decay strategies (constant, cyclic, stage-wise, iterative) yield distinct closed-form convergent bounds; for Noisy-FedProx, the proximal term with α > L introduces a contraction factor α/(α−L) < 1 that eliminates dependence on the local interval K entirely, giving a stable constant lower bound. The entire analysis is conducted in the information-theoretically lossless f-DP (GDP) framework, avoiding the relaxation losses inherent in (ε,δ)-DP and RDP composition.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: it proves that Noisy-FedAvg achieves a convergent f-DP privacy bound of T_G(2μVK/(√m·σ)·√(2−1/T)) under stage-wise or iteratively decaying learning rates, which remains bounded as T→∞ on non-convex objectives. Noisy-FedProx with proximal coefficient α>L achieves a constant lower bound T_G(2V/(√m·ασ)·√((2α−L)/L)·(1−2/((α/(α−L))^T+1))) that is independent of the local interval K. Empirically, on CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 (m=100, K=50, σ=10⁻³), Noisy-FedAvg reaches 75.38% accuracy, while Noisy-FedProx with α=1 reduces global sensitivity from 31.33 (FedAvg) to 6.34 at a modest accuracy cost (56.33% vs. 60.67%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is against eight prior FL-DP analyses (Wei 2020, Shi 2021, Zhang 2021b, Noble 2022, Cheng 2022, Zhang & Tang 2022, Hu 2023, Fukami 2024), all of which require noise variance σ² to grow linearly or super-linearly with T (or TK) to maintain a fixed (ε,δ)-DP guarantee, implying divergent privacy budgets as training proceeds. The paper's f-DP analysis with shifted interpolation yields a bound that converges to a constant as T→∞ on non-convex objectives, eliminating the need for ever-increasing noise. The closest prior result is Bastianello et al. (2024), which also achieves convergent privacy, but only under β-strongly convex objectives; the paper extends convergence to the general non-convex and smooth setting. Among the empirical baselines, Noisy-FedProx with α=1 reduces global sensitivity by ~80% (from 31.33 to 6.34) compared to Noisy-FedAvg at T=600, while maintaining competitive accuracy (56.33% vs. 60.67% on CIFAR-10), demonstrating that the proximal regularization term provides a practical privacy-utility trade-off advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the shifted interpolation technique from Bok et al. (2024), which was developed for centralized noisy gradient descent, to the federated learning setting where multi-step local updates on heterogeneous data introduce additional sensitivity. It builds on the f-DP/GDP framework of Dong et al. (2022) as a lossless analytical tool, departing from the (ε,δ)-DP and RDP composition theorems that underpin all prior FL-DP privacy analyses (Wei et al. 2020, Noble et al. 2022, Zhang & Tang 2022, etc.) and that inherently yield divergent bounds. The closest prior result is Bastianello et al. (2024), which achieves convergent privacy in FL but only under β-strongly convex objectives; this paper removes that convexity requirement entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result resolves a fundamental tension in the FL-DP literature: composition-theorem-based analyses imply that privacy degrades without bound as training rounds increase, contradicting both intuition and empirical observations. By proving convergent (constant) privacy under non-convex objectives, the paper provides theoretical justification for using constant-level noise in long-running FL-DP deployments, removing the need for ever-increasing noise schedules. Open questions include the gap between the relaxed t₀=0 bound and the true optimum, whether the analysis extends to more complex FL methods (Scaffold, momentum-based, adaptive optimizers), and whether the proximal-term privacy benefit generalizes beyond the specific FedProx formulation studied here.
