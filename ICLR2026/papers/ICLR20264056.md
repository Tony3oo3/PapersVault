## Problem

Existing trajectory representation methods assume smooth, continuous motion and rely on handcrafted distances or sensitive hyperparameters (margins, temperatures, mining strategies), making them ineffective for discrete, event-driven trajectories such as badminton rallies with abrupt direction changes and return-to-center patterns. No prior unified architecture handles both continuous GPS traces and discrete action-sequence trajectories while preserving the intrinsic pairwise geometry of the native feature space in the learned embedding.

## Input / Output

The method consumes variable-length sequences of 2D locations paired with timestamps: for taxi data, GPS coordinates map-matched to OpenStreetMap road vertices; for badminton, stroke-by-stroke positions on a 61×67 integer grid (0.1 m resolution) within a half-court, with a symmetry mapping to avoid duplicate patterns. It produces a 64-dimensional fixed-size embedding per trajectory, which supports top-k similarity retrieval (ground truth computed via the TP discrete Hausdorff distance) and downstream spectral clustering. Variable lengths are handled through a learned CLS token, padding masks, and zeroing of padded positions after positional encoding.

## Method

TRIDENT combines a GCN-based spatial encoder with a Date2Vec-based temporal encoder, fused by a Dual-Attention Encoder (DAEncoder) that runs two parallel cross-attention layers (spatial attends to temporal, then temporal attends to updated spatial) with independent parameters and no gating. Each branch is summarized by Nonlinear Tanh-Projection Attention Pooling (NTAP), which applies a learnable tanh projection before computing attention scores against a learnable context vector, capturing higher-order nonlinear interactions critical for discrete event-driven sequences. For training, a hyperparameter-free strategy constructs triplets using the top-1 TP-similar trajectory as positive and a random trajectory as negative, then optimizes a Distance-preserving Multi-kernel Triplet Loss (DMT) that regresses embedding-space distances to native trajectory-space distances, reweighted by three Gaussian kernels (σ = median × {0.5, 1.0, 2.0}) to balance local-neighborhood fidelity against global-structure preservation without margins or temperature parameters.

## Results

On the three primary datasets, TRIDENT achieves HR@10 / HR@50 / R10@50 of 0.190 / 0.347 / 0.484 on Badminton, 0.564 / 0.661 / 0.916 on T-Drive, and 0.535 / 0.664 / 0.913 on Rome. Averaged across the three metrics, TRIDENT exceeds the mean of all four baselines by 271% on Badminton, 96% on T-Drive, and 127% on Rome. On the two additional large-scale datasets (Chengdu, Xian), improvements over the SOTA baseline ST2Vec grow to 21.1% and 24.1% respectively, confirming scaling robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRIDENT outperforms all four baselines on every metric across all three primary datasets. Against the strongest baseline ST2Vec (KDD 2022), TRIDENT improves HR@10 by 2.6× on Badminton (0.190 vs 0.073), by 13.7% on T-Drive (0.564 vs 0.496), and by 11.7% on Rome (0.535 vs 0.479); the gap widens on the larger Chengdu and Xian datasets (0.680 vs 0.544 and 0.465 vs 0.394 HR@10). TrajCL (ICDE 22023) is competitive with ST2Vec on Badminton (HR@10 0.084 vs 0.073) but collapses on T-Drive and Rome (0.241 and 0.094), indicating its contrastive objective without an anchor struggles on smooth continuous trajectories. NeuTraj (ICDE 2019) and ConDTC (IEEE TBD 2024) are consistently the weakest, with HR@10 below 0.18 on all datasets, confirming that RNN-based ranking and contrastive clustering approaches fail to capture the spatio-temporal structure needed for cross-domain trajectory retrieval. The Friedman test (p = 1.078×10⁻⁵) and Nemenyi post-hoc test confirm that TRIDENT's advantage over NeuTraj, TrajCL, and ConDTC is statistically significant (p < 0.05), while the margin over ST2Vec, though numerically clear, reaches only marginal significance.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRIDENT synthesizes elements from several trajectory representation lines—GCN-based spatial encoding, attention-based temporal fusion, and triplet-style metric learning—while departing from the standard ranking-only triplet loss by introducing a distance-regression objective (DMT) that explicitly aligns embedding-space distances with native trajectory-space distances across multiple Gaussian-kernel scales. It also replaces standard linear attention pooling with a nonlinear tanh-projection variant (NTAP) to better capture event-driven, high-curvature sequences. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against the dominant focus on smooth, continuous GPS trajectories (taxi, driving) and carves new ground by treating discrete, event-triggered sports trajectories (badminton) as a first-class input to the same unified architecture, a setting prior methods like ST2Vec, TrajCL, and ConDTC were not designed for.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of continuous and discrete trajectory modeling under a single architecture addresses a genuine gap: most trajectory representation work assumes smooth, densely sampled motion, leaving event-driven sports analytics without a suitable tool. The DMT loss's explicit distance preservation (rather than mere rank ordering) offers a principled remedy for the geometry distortion and dimensional collapse that plague standard triplet and contrastive losses, and the near-zero-hyperparameter design (no margins, temperatures, or mining heuristics) lowers the practical barrier to adoption. However, cross-domain transfer retains only ~31% of in-domain performance, the evaluation datasets are relatively small (10K training trajectories), and the reliance on TP distance as ground truth limits interpretability. Open questions include whether the distance-preserving formulation generalizes beyond trajectories to other sequential metric-learning tasks, and whether the approach scales to the 100K+ trajectory regimes where GPU memory and nearest-neighbor computation become bottlenecks.
