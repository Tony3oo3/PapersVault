## Problem

LLM-based world models for computer-use agents suffer from hallucination and reliance on static parametric knowledge, causing compounding errors that degrade long-horizon simulation accuracy. While LLMs can reliably identify immediate next states and recognize task-relevant transitions, they fail to maintain procedural coherence over extended planning horizons in environment-specific contexts (e.g., specific software UIs or website layouts). This paper identifies the missing grounding in up-to-date, environment-specific procedural knowledge as the root cause and proposes a retrieval-augmented solution.

## Input / Output

At each decision step, the policy model receives the task goal, the current observation (screenshot for OSWorld; screenshot plus accessibility tree for WebArena), and prior interaction history, then proposes 1 to m candidate thought-action pairs. The world model additionally conditions on a set of retrieved tutorial chunks (top-5 by default) obtained via a reasoning-based RAG pipeline over a 30k-chunk knowledge base of official documentation and WikiHow articles. The final output is a single selected action drawn from the official OSWorld or WebArena action spaces (e.g., left_click, type, goto, scroll), executed in the live environment.

## Method

R-WoM grounds LLM world-model rollouts with externally retrieved tutorials through three key mechanisms. First, a reasoning-aware RAG pipeline rewrites the task query to generalize and anonymize it, retrieves top-k chunks via FAISS cosine similarity, and applies an LLM-based listwise reranker to filter semantically irrelevant candidates. Second, instead of the iterative multi-round policy–world-model communication used by WebDreamer, R-WoM performs k-step imagination in a single LongCoT forward pass, where the world model unfolds the entire multi-step trajectory conditioned on the retrieved evidence set. Third, reward estimation uses a listwise relative ranking over all candidate rollout trajectories rather than absolute sparse scores, reducing bias and stabilizing action selection. Adaptive action branching (the policy decides whether to propose one or multiple candidates based on confidence) and action deduplication further reduce unnecessary world-model invocations by roughly 80% while retaining most of the performance gain.

## Results

R-WoM achieves consistent improvements over all baselines on both OSWorld and WebArena across three model backbones. On OSWorld, R-WoM reaches 37.48% (Qwen-2.5-VL-72B), 26.01% (Claude-3.5-Sonnet), and 38.54% (Claude-3.7-Sonnet), yielding relative gains of 21.5%, 10.8%, and 23.4% over the strongest non-R-WoM baseline. On WebArena, R-WoM scores 28.49%, 33.15%, and 34.58%, with relative improvements of 16.3%, 8.0%, and 5.6% respectively. The gains are most pronounced for weaker backbones and on OSWorld, where environment-specific procedural knowledge is harder to infer from parametric memory alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R-WoM outperforms all three baselines (Vanilla, RAG, and WebDreamer) across every model–benchmark combination. Against Vanilla (adapted from GTA-1 for OSWorld and WMA for WebArena), R-WoM gains 11.1–10.1 percentage points on OSWorld and 6.7–5.7 points on WebArena, confirming that retrieval-augmented world modeling substantially exceeds simple observation-conditioned action selection. Against RAG, which retrieves tutorials but applies them only at the policy level without world-model simulation, R-WoM leads by 6.6–10.8 points on OSWorld and 5.8–3.1 points on WebArena, showing that grounding the *simulation* process (not just the policy) is critical. Against WebDreamer (Gu et al., 2024), the closest competitor that also uses LLM-based world-model rollouts but without retrieval grounding, R-WoM achieves the largest relative gains (21.5% on OSWorld with Qwen-2.5-VL-72B; 23.4% with Claude-3.7-Sonnet), demonstrating that tutorial grounding is the key differentiator for long-horizon simulation fidelity. Notably, WebDreamer sometimes underperforms even Vanilla (e.g., 23.48% vs. 22.43% on OSWorld with Claude-3.5-Sonnet is marginal, but 28.37% vs. 30.84% for RAG with Qwen), suggesting that ungrounded iterative rollouts can introduce compounding errors that hurt performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-as-world-model paradigm for computer-use agents pioneered by WebDreamer (Gu et al., 2024) and WMA (Chae et al., 2024), which used iterative policy-world-model rollouts with absolute reward scoring. R-WoM departs from this lineage in three key ways: (1) it grounds the world model with retrieved environment-specific tutorials via a reasoning-aware RAG pipeline (query rewriting + LLM reranking) rather than relying solely on parametric knowledge; (2) it replaces computationally expensive iterative rollouts with a single LongCoT reasoning sequence inspired by DeepSeek-R1; and (3) it substitutes absolute sparse rewards with a listwise relative ranking mechanism. The tutorial-grounding idea connects to a parallel line of work (AgentTrek, TongUI, Learn-by-Interact) that uses tutorials for offline trajectory synthesis, but R-WoM applies them at inference time as grounding evidence for simulation rather than as training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic probing analysis (Section 3) provides a useful diagnostic framework for the community, clearly separating short-horizon state prediction (where LLMs succeed) from long-horizon procedural alignment (where they fail), which motivates the grounding mechanism. The practical impact is significant for GUI/computer-use agent practitioners: the 10–23% relative improvements over strong baselines on OSWorld and WebArena, combined with the adaptive cost optimization that reduces token usage by >50% while retaining most gains, make R-WoM a viable inference-time strategy. Key limitations remain: retrieval fails on tasks requiring implicit multi-step reasoning (e.g., data manipulation), content-modification tasks are still the primary failure mode, and the framework depends on tutorial availability (mitigated but not solved by trajectory synthesis). Open questions include whether multimodal or step-aware retrieval could close the remaining gap, and whether the listwise reward design generalizes to settings with very large candidate sets.
