## Problem

Existing latent reasoning methods (e.g., Coconut, SoftCoT) rely on offline-trained projection modules that are brittle on challenging, out-of-distribution tasks, collapsing to near-zero accuracy on competition-grade benchmarks like AIME. The paper asks whether LLM reasoning can be enhanced entirely at test time—without any model parameter updates—while remaining robust on the hardest problems where prior latent approaches fail.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265294/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt (e.g., a math problem) augmented with K special placeholder tokens (e.g., [THINK]) whose initial embeddings are obtained from the frozen LLM's embedding layer. During optimization, only fixed-length forward passes (prompt + latent tokens) are run to compute the reward—no autoregressive decoding occurs in the loop. After T optimization steps, the best-reward latent thought vectors are concatenated with the prompt embeddings and passed through the LLM once to autoregressively generate the final answer, which is then parsed for the numerical result.

## Method

LTPO formulates latent thought vector optimization as a sequential decision-making problem solved via a direct REINFORCE-style policy gradient over T timesteps. The policy is a multivariate Gaussian centered at the current latent state, so each action is a random perturbation ε ~ N(0, σ²I) applied to the K×d latent thought matrix. The reward is an intrinsic confidence signal computed from the frozen LLM's own output distributions: the mean negative log-probability of the top-k most probable tokens at each latent thought position, averaged across all K vectors—requiring no external supervision, no text generation, and no model weight updates. The update rule H^(t+1) = H^(t) + η·R(H^(t)+ε^(t))·ε^(t)/σ² pushes the latent thoughts toward higher model confidence. After optimization, the vectors achieving the highest reward (not necessarily the final step) are used for answer generation, and the entire procedure is parameter-free and model-agnostic.

## Results

LTPO achieves the highest average accuracy across all four tested models (LLaMA-3.1-8B, LLaMA-3.2-3B, Qwen-2.5-7B, Qwen-3-14B) on five mathematical reasoning benchmarks. With Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct, LTPO reaches 56.79% average accuracy, surpassing Zero-Shot CoT (54.53%), SoftCoT (47.88%), and LatentSeek (52.91%). The most striking result is on AIME2024/2025, where SoftCoT collapses to 0.00% while LTPO achieves 16.67%/13.33% with Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct. On the extended 64k-token setting with Qwen-3-14B, LTPO further boosts AIME accuracy from 75.00% (Zero-Shot CoT) to 80.00%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Zero-Shot CoT (Kojima et al., 2022), LTPO wins on average accuracy for all four models (e.g., +2.26 pts with Qwen-2.5-7B, +6.31 pts with LLaMA-3.1-8B), with the largest gains on AIME benchmarks. Against SoftCoT (Xu et al., 2025b), LTPO achieves a decisive victory: SoftCoT scores 0.00% on both AIME2024 and AIME2025 for every model tested, while LTPO reaches 16.67%/13.33% with Qwen-2.5-7B; on standard benchmarks LTPO also leads by 3–9 points in average accuracy. Against LatentSeek (Li et al., 2025), LTPO wins on average accuracy (e.g., 56.79% vs. 52.91% with Qwen-2.5-7B) and is over 25× faster in inference time (20.79 s vs. 525–543 s average). In the training-based comparison (Table 2, LLaMA-3.1-8B), LTPO outperforms Genius (Xu et al., 2025a) on all three benchmarks (81.27 vs. 78.09 on GSM8K; 16.67 vs. 3.33 on AIME2024), SimpleRL-Zoo (Zeng et al., 2025) (81.27 vs. 79.20 on GSM8K; 16.67 vs. 0.00 on AIME2024), and iCoT (Deng et al., 2024) (81.27 vs. 50.05 on GSM8K), all without any model parameter updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

LTPO sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: latent reasoning (Coconut, SoftCoT, iCoT), which encodes intermediate thoughts as continuous vectors but requires offline training of projection modules, and RL-based LLM alignment (RLHF, GRPO, DAPO), which updates model parameters with external rewards. The closest prior method, LatentSeek, also performs test-time policy gradient in latent space but relies on full autoregressive decoding to compute its reward signal. LTPO's key departure is a parameter-free, confidence-based reward computed directly from the frozen LLM's output logits, eliminating both the training phase and the expensive decoding step within the optimization loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LTPO addresses a concrete failure mode of trained latent reasoning methods—complete collapse on out-of-distribution, competition-grade problems (AIME)—by making the latent representations adaptive per instance. The parameter-free, test-time design is practically attractive since it requires no fine-tuning data or compute beyond inference. However, the paper itself formalizes a fundamental limitation: the confidence-based reward can drive optimization toward 'confidently incorrect' local maxima (Theorem B.2), and the most impressive AIME results rest on only 30 problems per benchmark, making the magnitude of gains harder to assess. Open questions include whether richer reward signals (e.g., uncertainty estimation, self-consistency checks) can close the confidence-correctness gap, and whether the approach scales to non-mathematical reasoning at the same fidelity.
