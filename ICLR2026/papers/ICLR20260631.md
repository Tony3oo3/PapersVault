## Problem

Proximal sampling offers provable exponential convergence to the target distribution under log-Sobolev conditions, but existing implementations rely on rejection sampling (the Restricted Gaussian Oracle) that forces small step sizes, inflates iteration counts, and introduces variable runtime. The paper asks whether the backward denoising step of proximal sampling can be simulated directly via diffusion SDEs using only zeroth-order evaluations of the potential, eliminating rejection sampling while preserving the convergence guarantees.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes only point evaluations of the potential f (no gradients or Hessians), a set of N initial particles, a step size h, T denoising substeps, and M interim Monte Carlo samples per particle per substep. All f-evaluations across particles and interim samples are performed in parallel, yielding a deterministic per-iteration cost of O(T) oracle calls (or O(TM) without parallelism). The output is a set of N samples approximating π(x) ∝ exp(−f(x)), with the multi-particle interaction structure meaning particles are not independent but coupled through a shared Gaussian-mixture surrogate.

## Method

The key mechanism replaces the true target π^X in the backward SDE with a surrogate distribution q̂_{k+1} constructed as a weighted N-component Gaussian mixture centered at the forward-perturbed particles Y_{k+1/2}, with weights inversely proportional to the local particle density (preventing collapse and encouraging exploration). By Bayes' rule, the posterior of the denoised variable given the current noisy state is itself a Gaussian mixture with closed-form means, variances, and weights, enabling Monte Carlo score estimation via simple sampling from these components—no learned score network or auxiliary sampler is needed. The backward SDE is discretized with Euler–Maruyama steps over T substeps of the macro interval h, and the forward step is an exact Gaussian perturbation. Theoretically, under LSI on π^X, the KL divergence contracts geometrically per outer iteration with a rate matching the ideal proximal sampler, up to additive errors of order O(h/T) from time discretization and O(1/N + 1/M) from finite-particle and finite-sample score estimation.

## Results

On the 5-dimensional Gaussian Lasso mixture, the proposed method (h=1/10, N=100 particles, T=10 diffusion steps, M=4000 interim samples) reaches comparable KL divergence to the target in approximately 100 iterations, versus ~950 iterations for the RGO-based proximal sampler (h=1/135, 100 chains) — a ~10× reduction in iterations and ~100× speedup when accounting for the 10× thinning applied to the RGO baseline. The method uses a step size 13.5× larger than the RGO baseline, which is the key enabler of the speedup. On the two-tori uniform sampling task in R³, the method successfully populates both the near and distant tori, whereas In-and-Out converges only to the near torus and fails to reach the distant one.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the RGO-based proximal sampler (Liang & Chen, 2023b) on the Gaussian Lasso mixture, the proposed method achieves comparable KL divergence in ~100 iterations versus ~950 for RGO, a roughly 10× improvement in iteration count (and ~100× in wall-clock time after accounting for the 10× thinning applied to RGO to match per-iteration f-evaluation cost). The speedup is attributed to the ability to use a 13.5× larger step size (h=1/10 vs. h=1/135), which is infeasible for rejection-sampling-based RGO. An ablation removing particle interactions (N=1, 100 independent chains) converges more slowly than the full N=100 interacting-particle version, confirming that the reweighting mechanism in the surrogate distribution accelerates mixing beyond the step-size effect alone. Against In-and-Out (Kook et al., 2024) on the disjoint two-tori domain, the proposed method wins decisively: In-and-Out converges to uniformity on the near torus T₁ (consistent with its warm-start guarantee for convex bodies) but completely fails to reach the distant torus T₂, while the proposed method gradually drives particles into both components, demonstrating its ability to explore disconnected modes without gradients or projection oracles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of proximal sampling (Liang & Chen, 2023; Chen et al., 2022) and diffusion-based Monte Carlo. While proximal sampling has elegant SDE-based convergence guarantees, its practical implementations have been bottlenecked by rejection sampling in the backward step, forcing small step sizes. The key departure here is replacing the rejection-sampling-based Restricted Gaussian Oracle with a direct Euler-Maruyama simulation of the backward SDE, where the score is estimated via a Gaussian mixture posterior constructed from the current particles (a mechanism borrowed from Lyu et al., 2025's distributional black-box optimization). Unlike diffusion-based Monte Carlo methods that require learned score models or nested auxiliary samplers, this approach is learning-free and gradient-free, operating solely with zeroth-order evaluations of the potential.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating rejection sampling, the method permits step sizes that reflect global structure (e.g., inter-mode distances) rather than local smoothness, yielding a near-order-of-magnitude speedup in convergence iterations. The multi-particle interaction mechanism, which re-weights overpopulated regions to encourage exploration, is empirically essential for accelerating mixing but lacks a complete theoretical characterization. Open questions include how the method scales to higher dimensions (the curse of dimensionality for zeroth-order methods), whether the particle interaction effects can be formally bounded, and how to tune the noise schedule and particle count adaptively across iterations.
