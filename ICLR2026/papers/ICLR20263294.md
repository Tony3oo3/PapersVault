## Problem

Existing PEFT methods for Mamba simply transfer Transformer-tailored techniques without addressing the unique temporal processing dynamics of SSMs. Mamba's single linear gating mechanism lacks the structured memory and nonlinear temporal control found in traditional recurrent networks, and directly fine-tuning state-space components has been shown to degrade performance. The key question is how to incorporate temporal adaptation during fine-tuning without disrupting the balanced dynamics of pre-trained SSMs.

## Input / Output

For language tasks, the method consumes pre-trained Mamba models (130M–1.4B) on text token sequences and produces token-level predictions across eight commonsense reasoning benchmarks. For vision tasks, input images are divided into spatial chunks and flattened into sequential representations processed by Vim-S or Vanilla-VMamba-S architectures, producing class labels on VTAB-1k. The LIM neuron partitions each sequence into T equal-sized chunks (typically T=4), with membrane potentials evolving sequentially across chunks and zero-padding restoring any trimmed remainder tokens.

## Method

Memba introduces a Leaky Integrate Membrane (LIM) neuron in Mamba's gate branch, inspired by LIF neurons, that accumulates membrane potentials via chunked leaky integration (u[i+1] = r(τ·u[i] + W·X[i])) with threshold-based reset, providing selective temporal memory without additional learnable parameters. This is combined with LoRA adapters strategically placed on input and output projections (identified via ablation as the critical information bottlenecks) and a cross-layer membrane transfer mechanism that averages membrane states across chunks to initialize the first chunk of the next layer, creating hierarchical temporal flow. The approach modifies only the gating pathway, preserving the pre-trained selective scan dynamics entirely. A theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) shows the membrane fluctuations introduce a bounded regularization term proportional to the Hessian's maximum eigenvalue, explaining the smoother loss landscapes observed empirically.

## Results

On commonsense reasoning (8 benchmarks), Memba(in+out-proj) achieves 52.3% average accuracy on Mamba-790M, a 1.5% absolute improvement over the best prior PEFT method (LoRA_p(X) at 50.8%), and 54.5% on Mamba-1.4B, surpassing the best baseline (Additional-scan at 53.5%) by 1.0%. On vision (VTAB-1k), Memba(in+out_proj) on Vim-S reaches 72.40% average accuracy, outperforming the Hybrid method (72.05%) while using only 28% of its trainable parameters (4,718K vs. 117,236K). On Vanilla-VMamba-S, Memba(out_proj) achieves 74.07% average accuracy, the best result across all methods tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Memba consistently outperforms all MambaPEFT baselines (Yoshimura et al., 2024) across every model size: on Mamba-790M, Memba(in+out-proj) beats LoRA_p(X) by 1.5% (52.3 vs. 50.8) and Additional-scan by 1.6% (52.3 vs. 50.7); on Mamba-1.4B, it surpasses LoRA_p(X) by 0.8% (54.5 vs. 53.7) and Additional-scan by 1.0% (54.5 vs. 53.5). Against SLL LoRA (Halloran et al., 2024), Memba wins by 1.6% on 790M (52.3 vs. 50.7) and 1.8% on 1.4B (54.5 vs. 52.7). In the cross-architecture comparison, Mamba-790M+Memba (52.3%) exceeds Pythia-1B+LoRA (47.1%) by 5.2%, and Mamba-1.4B+Memba (54.5%) beats Pythia-1.4B+LoRA (50.5%) by 4.0%. On vision (VTAB-1k), Memba(in+out_proj) on Vim-S (72.40%) surpasses the Hybrid method (72.05%) with 28% of its parameters, and Memba(out_proj) on Vanilla-VMamba-S (74.07%) edges out LoRA(out_proj) (73.73%) by 0.34%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of Mamba PEFT work initiated by MambaPEFT (Yoshimura et al., 2024), SLL LoRA (Halloran et al., 2024), and Ham et al. (2024), which applied Transformer-tailored adaptation techniques (LoRA on various projections, additional scan branches) to SSMs without addressing their unique temporal processing dynamics. Memba departs from this by introducing a bio-inspired Leaky Integrate Membrane (LIM) neuron—adapted from spiking neural network LIF dynamics—into the gating branch, combined with strategically placed LoRA on input/output projections and cross-layer membrane transfer. The key conceptual departure is that prior methods modify state-space components or add parallel branches, while Memba preserves the pre-trained SSM dynamics entirely and injects temporal adaptation exclusively through the gate path, motivated by evidence that directly fine-tuning SSM components degrades performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Memba addresses a genuine architectural gap: Mamba's single linear gating mechanism lacks the multi-gate temporal selectivity of LSTMs/GRUs, and prior PEFT methods did not target this limitation. The finding that a zero-learnable-parameter recurrent membrane mechanism outperforms parameter-heavy LSTM/GRU alternatives in the gate path, while also outperforming full fine-tuning at larger scales, suggests that structured temporal gating is more important than raw parameter count for SSM adaptation. However, the practical impact is tempered by the recurrent computation overhead of LIM (acknowledged as a limitation, with CUDA kernel optimization proposed but not demonstrated), and the 1–1.5% improvements, while consistent, are incremental. Open questions include whether the membrane mechanism generalizes to other SSM variants (H3, DSS), whether it helps at foundation-model scale, and whether the theoretical regularization bound (Theorem 1) translates to measurable generalization gains beyond the specific benchmarks tested.
