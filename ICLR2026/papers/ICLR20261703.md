## Problem

Existing LLM debiasing methods either require expensive backbone fine-tuning, are tied to specific architectures, or operate only at the input/decoding stage while leaving attention-level representational biases intact. Moreover, most approaches target a single protected attribute and do not scale to real-world scenarios with multiple, overlapping protected attributes and their intersections. This paper addresses the need for a model-agnostic, fine-tuning-free method that directly suppresses multi-attribute bias within frozen self-attention layers.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the hidden state X_{i-1} ∈ R^{n×d} at each self-attention layer of a frozen LLM, along with multi-hot protected-attribute label vectors a ∈ {0,1}^M derived from curated gender/race lexicons or off-the-shelf classifier predictions (with label-overlap filtering to prevent leakage). It produces a debiased representation X̃_{i-1} that replaces the original input to the attention computation, which then propagates unchanged through all remaining frozen layers to yield downstream task predictions (classification scores, generated text, coreference decisions). The plug-in module is trained once per backbone and applied at inference without any gradient updates to the LLM itself.

## Method

MQAR inserts a quantized adversarial autoencoder (QR module) at each self-attention layer, where an encoder e1 maps token representations to a continuous latent z, a vector quantizer Q with codebook size K discretizes z into a mutated latent r, and a decoder d2 reconstructs the token representation from r. A discriminator Da is trained to predict multi-hot protected-attribute labels from r, while the encoder-quantizer pair is trained adversarially to obfuscate those labels, guided by an information-bottleneck objective max I(R;X) − βI(R;A) approximated via reconstruction lower bounds and KL-divergence upper bounds. Training proceeds in two phases: first, the autoencoder, decoders, and discriminator are jointly optimized with quantization, commitment, reconstruction (BCE), and classification losses; second, the discriminator is fixed and attribute labels are randomized to drive z and r toward attribute-invariant representations. The entire module operates on frozen backbone parameters, requires no access to pre-training data, and uses a single plug-in to jointly debias multiple protected attributes simultaneously.

## Results

On LLaMA 3.2, MQAR achieves the best ICAT scores on StereoSet (86.0 for gender, 84.0 for race) while simultaneously attaining the lowest stereotype scores (SS: 53.1 gender, 54.0 race) and the highest language modeling scores (LMS: 91.6 gender, 91.3 race). On CrowS-Pairs, MQAR records the lowest overall bias (48.1 All, 47.9 Stereo) among all methods tested. On downstream tasks (GPT-Neo), MQAR maintains AUC within 0.4 percentage points of the non-debiased baseline (e.g., 93.7 vs. 93.8 on abusive language detection) while reducing FPED from 2.32 to 1.87 and FNED from 3.71 to 3.44.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MQAR outperforms all nine baselines on the primary StereoSet ICAT metric for LLaMA 3.2, achieving 86.0 (gender) and 84.0 (race) versus the next-best RB (76.7/82.8) and CRISPR (72.4/81.5), a gap of 9–14 points. On stereotype score (SS), MQAR (53.1/54.0) beats INLP (58.3/64.0), ADELE (58.2/64.1), and FaRM (60.3/56.2) by 4–10 points, while FineDeb (54.2/51.8) is competitive on race SS but degrades LMS severely (82.1 vs. 91.6 for MQAR). On CrowS-Pairs, MQAR achieves the lowest All (48.1) and Stereo (47.9) scores, edging out INLP (48.2/48.6) and ADELE (48.3/48.7) by small margins, while substantially outperforming FineDeb (57.1/57.1) and CRISPR (50.8/52.0). On downstream tasks (GPT-Neo), MQAR matches or exceeds OSCaR, SentDebias, and INLP on AUC while achieving the best or near-best FPED/FNED bias metrics (e.g., FPED 1.87 vs. OSCaR 1.20 but AUC 93.7 vs. 93.5), confirming that its debiasing does not sacrifice task accuracy the way projection-based methods do. The efficiency analysis shows MQAR adds only 48.1% parameters and 87.0% FLOPs versus FineDeb's 107.3%/191.8%, while running ~43% faster end-to-end.

## Relation to Prior Work

MQAR composes three established ideas—adversarial autoencoders (Makhzani et al., 2015), vector-quantized latent disentanglement (Hsu et al., 2024), and label-randomized adversarial training (Park & Cho, 2023)—into a single plug-in module that intervenes at the self-attention level of a frozen LLM. It departs from the dominant debiasing literature, which operates at the embedding level (INLP, SentDebias, OSCaR), the full-model training level (ADELE, FaRM, FineDeb), or the inference/decoding level (CRISPR, RB), by targeting the attention computation itself as the intervention point. The information-bottleneck objective and strong-duality guarantee are inherited from Song et al. (2019), but the paper's specific contribution is the multi-attribute, quantized-bottleneck formulation applied post-hoc to frozen backbones without access to pre-training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical deployment story—no backbone fine-tuning, no pre-training data, architecture-agnostic plug-in—is the paper's strongest contribution, making it applicable to proprietary or resource-constrained LLMs where retraining is infeasible. The multi-attribute framing (jointly debiasing gender, race, and their intersections with a single module) addresses a real gap that most prior methods ignore. Open questions remain: the weak-supervision lexicons may not generalize to less-lexiconizable attributes, the 0.4% accuracy claim is demonstrated primarily on GPT-Neo for downstream tasks, and the method's behavior in low-resource or multilingual settings is untested. Extending to instruction-tuned LLMs and verifying robustness under distribution shift are natural next steps.
