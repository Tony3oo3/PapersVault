## Problem

Existing training-free guidance methods for flow matching models modify the vector field to steer generation toward desired properties, which destroys the straight-line transport property of optimal flow matching and requires expensive ODE integration at every intermediate time step. This paper asks whether exact guidance can instead be achieved by modifying only the source distribution while keeping the pre-trained vector field completely unchanged, thereby preserving straight trajectories and reducing the guidance problem to a well-defined sampling task.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained flow matching vector field (trained via supervised flow matching loss to transport source q₀ to target q₁), a set of source samples drawn from q₀, and a differentiable or non-differentiable loss function J encoding the desired constraint. It produces samples from the conditional target q₁'(x) ∝ q₁(x)·e^{-J(x)} by first sampling from the modified source q₀'(x₀) ∝ q₀(x₀)·e^{-J∘T(x₀)} (where T is the learned flow map) and then integrating the unmodified ODE. The user selects the sampling strategy for q₀' (importance sampling, HMC, or optimization-based), and the framework supports arbitrary source distributions, not just Gaussian, with experiments spanning 2D synthetic data, 64×64 Darcy flow fields, and 128×128×3 CelebA images.

## Method

The core contribution is Theorem 1, which proves that transporting samples from the reweighted source q₀'(x₀) ∝ q₀(x₀)·e^{-J∘T(x₀)} along the original (unmodified) vector field exactly recovers the conditional target q₁', via a change-of-variables argument on the pushforward measure. Theorem 2 then provides a Wasserstein-2 error bound W₂(q₁', [φ₁^θ]#q̃₀) ≤ e^{L_v}·W₂(q₀', q̃₀) + ε·e^{L_v} for approximate samplers and approximate vector fields, showing that a small Lipschitz constant L_v of the flow map is the key to accurate guidance. This motivates using optimal flow matching (minibatch OT training) to learn straight-trajectory vector fields with small L_v, which also reduces the number of ODE integration steps needed. The guidance step is entirely training-free: the user simply chooses a sampler for q₀'—importance sampling for low dimensions, HMC for high-dimensional multimodal posteriors, or optimization-based methods (which subsume D-Flow as a special case) when a single high-probability sample suffices—making the framework flexible across problem structures.

## Results

On CelebA imaging inverse problems, SGFM-OPT-3/4 achieve the best PSNR across most tasks: 31.60 (denoising), 35.27 (deblurring), 33.31 (super-resolution), 34.06 (random inpainting), and 30.12 (box inpainting), outperforming g-covA and g-covG in all five tasks. On the Darcy flow PDE inverse problem, SGFM-OPT-2 achieves the best validity score (0.474) and physical consistency (0.194), while SGFM-HMC follows closely (0.591 validity, 0.188 consistency). On MNIST conditional generation, SGFM-ULA achieves a lower FID (46.7) than g-covA (57.1) despite lower label accuracy (87.6% vs. 98.5%), indicating better sample diversity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against D-Flow (Ben-Hamu et al., 2024), SGFM provides the theoretical foundation that D-Flow lacks: SGFM-OPT-2 is mathematically equivalent to D-Flow's preferred regularization, but the SGFM framework additionally offers asymptotically exact samplers (IS, HMC) that D-Flow does not. In 2D experiments, SGFM-IS consistently outperforms D-Flow in Wasserstein distance, while D-Flow exhibits mode collapse by over-concentrating samples around the loss minimum. Against Feng et al. (2025) (g-covA, g-covG), SGFM variants achieve higher PSNR in all five CelebA tasks (e.g., 31.60 vs. 30.35 for denoising, 34.06 vs. 25.49 for random inpainting) and better validity in the Darcy flow problem (0.474 vs. 0.992 for SGFM-OPT-2 vs. g-covA), though g-covA achieves the lowest guidance cost at the expense of physical consistency. Against PnP-flow (Martin et al., 2024), SGFM is competitive in most tasks but trails in deblurring (35.27 vs. 38.74) and super-resolution (33.31 vs. 31.33, where SGFM actually wins); PnP is specifically designed for imaging inverse problems while SGFM is a more general framework. In the Darcy flow problem, PnP performs poorly (validity 1.055) due to its difficulty handling multi-modal posteriors, while SGFM-HMC and SGFM-OPT-2 both substantially outperform it.

## Relation to Prior Work

SGFM departs from the dominant paradigm in flow matching guidance, where prior methods (Feng et al. 2025, Uehara et al. 2024, Domingo-Enrich et al. 2024) modify the vector field to steer generation toward a conditional target. Instead, SGFM keeps the pre-trained vector field entirely intact and achieves exact guidance by reweighting the source distribution via a change-of-variables argument (Theorem 1). This positions the paper as a theoretical completion of the heuristic D-Flow method (Ben-Hamu et al. 2024), which the authors show is a special case of SGFM with optimization-based sampling, and extends the source-modification justification of Venkatraman et al. (2025) with a Wasserstein error bound (Theorem 2) that explicitly quantifies when the approach is effective. The framework integrates naturally with optimal flow matching (Tong et al. 2023), preserving straight-line trajectories that vector-field-modifying guidance methods destroy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean separation of guidance into a source-sampling subproblem gives practitioners a flexible, modular toolkit: one can swap importance sampling, HMC, or optimization-based samplers depending on dimensionality, smoothness, and whether a representative or high-probability sample is desired, all under a single theoretical umbrella with explicit error bounds. The preservation of straight-line transport is practically significant for inference speed, as it avoids the curved trajectories and extra ODE steps required by vector-field-modifying methods. Key open questions remain: sampling from the modified source in very high dimensions is still challenging, the OT coupling needed for optimal vector fields does not scale well beyond mini-batch approximations, and backpropagation through the ODE for optimization-based sampling remains computationally expensive. The paper also acknowledges that optimization-based samplers risk mode collapse, limiting their use when full posterior diversity is needed.
