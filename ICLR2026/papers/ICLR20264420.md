## Problem

No equivalence characterization existed for latent-variable causal models in any parametric setting, meaning it was fundamentally unclear what could be identified from data when latent variables and cycles are both present. This gap blocked the development of a general, structural-assumption-free discovery method, since without knowing the equivalence class one cannot design an algorithm to recover it. All existing latent-variable methods relied on strong, often untestable structural assumptions (e.g., pure measurement models, hierarchical restrictions, sufficient pure children, acyclicity).

## Input / Output

The method takes observational data samples from a linear non-Gaussian model with an unknown number of latent variables, requiring only the specification of which variables are observed (X). It does not require knowing the latent count, graph structure, or any structural assumptions about how latents interact with observed variables. The output is not a single causal graph but the entire equivalence class of irreducible digraphs (up to latent relabeling) that are distributionally equivalent to the true model, traversed via admissible cycle reversals and edge additions/deletions.

## Method

The core contribution is a graphical criterion for distributional equivalence (Theorem 2) built on a new tool, edge rank constraints, which operate locally on edges via matching ranks of binary support matrices, in contrast to the global path ranks that operate on weighted mixing matrices. A duality theorem (Theorem 1) links the two rank notions, and the equivalence criterion admits a local decomposition: instead of checking all subsets of observed variables, it suffices to check each singleton X_i independently via 'children bases' (vertex sets admitting perfect edge matchings from L ∪ {X_i}). The glvLiNG algorithm proceeds in three phases: (1) OICA estimates the mixing matrix from data; (2) a constraint-based rank realization constructs a binary support matrix satisfying all matching rank queries, using transversal matroid theory for the latent-to-all bipartite phase and a deterministic singleton-augmentation construction for each observed variable's outgoing edges; (3) BFS/DFS traverses the equivalence class using the transformational characterization (Theorem 3) of admissible cycle reversals and edge additions/deletions.

## Results

On equivalence class enumeration, 1,027,080 weakly connected digraphs with 5 vertices (26,430 acyclic) reduce to 783 equivalence classes when 2 vertices are latent (from 480,640 irreducible models). On runtime, glvLiNG solves n=10 vertex instances in under 5 seconds, while a linear programming baseline exceeds hours beyond n=5. On edge identification under oracle inputs, both LaHiCaSl and PO-LiNGAM misidentify over 50% of edges due to structural misspecification, whereas glvLiNG recovers the full equivalence class. On the real-world Hong Kong stock dataset (14 companies, Jan 2000–Jun 2005), glvLiNG recovers interpretable causal patterns with major banks as central sources and two plausible latent variables.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares glvLiNG against two existing latent-variable LiNGAM methods—LaHiCaSl (Xie et al., 2024) and PO-LiNGAM (Jin et al., 2024)—under oracle access to their required statistical tests, applied to arbitrary latent-variable models that may violate their structural assumptions. Both baselines produce overly sparse graphs and misidentify over half of the edges, confirming that their structural assumptions (e.g., pure measurement models, hierarchical restrictions) lead to systematic errors when misspecified. In contrast, glvLiNG, being structural-assumption-free, recovers the complete equivalence class without such failures. On algorithmic efficiency, glvLiNG's constraint-based rank-realization approach dramatically outperforms a brute-force linear programming baseline: glvLiNG handles n=10 vertices in under 5 seconds while the LP baseline becomes intractable (hours) beyond n=5. In finite-sample simulations, glvLiNG is more robust to increasing latent dimensionality and performs particularly well on denser graphs, while baselines perform better on sparser graphs where their structural assumptions happen to hold.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of latent-variable causal discovery methods (OICA-based approaches, LiNGAM variants, FCI) by providing the first distributional equivalence characterization that simultaneously allows arbitrary latent structure and cycles in a parametric (linear non-Gaussian) setting without structural assumptions. It builds directly on OICA for mixing matrix estimation, on path rank constraints (Lindström, Gessel-Viennot, Talaska) for the algebraic-to-graphical bridge, and on transversal matroid / strict gammoid duality (Mason, Ingleton-Piff) for the algorithmic construction. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of edge rank constraints as a local, edge-level complement to the global path ranks, which enables a clean singleton decomposition of the equivalence criterion that was intractable with path ranks alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result removes a fundamental obstacle to structural-assumption-free latent-variable causal discovery: without knowing what is identifiable (the equivalence class), one cannot design methods to identify it. The edge rank tool is a genuinely new piece in the rank-based toolbox with potential applicability beyond this specific setting. However, the practical algorithm (glvLiNG) depends on OICA, which has known estimation inefficiencies, and the linear non-Gaussian assumption limits real-world applicability. Open questions include developing OICA-free estimation, extending the framework to Gaussian or nonlinear settings, and whether the clean local decomposition generalizes to other parametric families.
