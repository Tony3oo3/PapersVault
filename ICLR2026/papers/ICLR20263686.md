## Problem

Conformal prediction provides distribution-free prediction intervals but relies on exchangeability, which is violated by temporal dependencies and non-stationarity in time series. Existing extensions to sequential data either fit complex learned models (e.g., attention-based HopCPT, quantile forests in SPCI) that are computationally expensive and require retraining under distribution shift, or use simple data-independent reweighting (e.g., NexCP) that cannot capture local temporal dynamics. No prior method combines training-free operation, local adaptivity to temporal structure, and theoretical coverage guarantees in a single scalable framework.

## Input / Output

RESCP consumes a sequence of prediction residuals from a disjoint calibration set (produced by any pre-trained point forecaster such as an RNN, Transformer, or ARIMA) and optionally exogenous variables, and outputs a prediction interval [ŷ + q_{α/2}, ŷ + q_{1−α/2}] at a user-specified confidence level. The calibration set is maintained as a sliding window of fixed size N that is updated iteratively as new observations arrive, and the reservoir is driven by the residual sequence x_t = r_t (or augmented with exogenous features in the RESCQR variant). Quantiles are estimated via Monte Carlo resampling of calibration residuals according to the computed similarity weights, with the number of samples equal to the calibration set size.

## Method

RESCP embeds the observed residual sequence into an untrained Echo State Network, whose randomly initialized recurrent dynamics (with spectral radius < 1 to ensure the Echo State Property) produce high-dimensional state vectors h_t that encode local temporal structure. At each prediction step, cosine similarity scores are computed between the current query state h_t and all calibration states {h_s}, then passed through a temperature-scaled softmax to yield data-dependent weights w_s(h_t). These weights define a weighted empirical CDF of past residuals, from which quantiles are extracted to construct the prediction interval; a skewed-distribution refinement (selecting β* to minimize interval width) is applied on top. To handle distribution shifts, the weights are further modulated by a linear time-decay factor γ(Δ(t,i)) = 1/Δ(t,i), and the calibration window slides forward. The paper proves asymptotic conditional coverage under strong mixing, ESN fading-memory Lipschitz continuity, and continuity of the conditional residual law, with the temperature τ_n required to decrease slowly enough that the effective sample size m_n → ∞.

## Results

On the RNN base model at α=0.1, RESCP achieves the best Winkler score among non-learning methods across all four datasets: 104.24 (Solar), 106.07 (Beijing), 0.0264 (Exchange), and 12.90 (ACEA), outperforming HopCPT (112.46, 140.50, 122.48, 27.53) and SPCI (151.50, 124.51, 130.59, 14.58). RESCP maintains near-valid coverage (|ΔCov| < 2% in most settings) while reducing PI width by up to 60% compared to NexCP. Runtime is 52.9s on Solar vs. 4574.6s for HopCPT and 1039.8s for SPCI, confirming the training-free scalability advantage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RESCP outperforms HopCPT (Auer et al., 2023) in Winkler score on all four datasets with the RNN base model (e.g., 104.24 vs. 112.46 on Solar; 12.90 vs. 27.53 on ACEA), despite requiring no training, while HopCPT trains a Hopfield-network attention model end-to-end. Against SPCI (Xu & Xie, 2023a), RESCP achieves substantially lower Winkler scores (104.24 vs. 151.50 on Solar; 12.90 vs. 14.58 on ACEA) and avoids the per-step quantile random forest fitting. Compared to NexCP (Barber et al., 2023), RESCP provides similar coverage validity but with PI widths reduced by up to 60% (e.g., 62.25 vs. 89.56 on Solar). CP-QRNN (Cini et al., 2025) achieves the best Winkler on Solar (78.42) and Beijing (104.03) where informative exogenous variables and large calibration sets are available, but degrades severely on smaller datasets (ACEA Winkler 33.47, ΔCov −12.37%). Vanilla SCP is consistently the most conservative, producing the widest intervals and highest Winkler scores across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

RESCP sits at the intersection of conformal prediction for time series and reservoir computing. It builds directly on the weighted-CP line of work (NexCP's exponential decay, HopCPT's learned attention reweighting, SPCI's quantile random forests) and on localized CP (Guan 2022), but departs from the prevailing paradigm of fitting a parametric model to capture temporal dependencies. Instead, it repurposes the untrained Echo State Network as a fixed, expressive encoder of residual dynamics, using inter-state similarity to compute data-dependent weights without any gradient-based training. This makes it conceptually closer to kernel-based or nearest-neighbor CP methods, but with the added capacity of a nonlinear recurrent state space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that RESCP offers a drop-in, training-free uncertainty layer on top of any point forecaster that is both fast (no GPU, no retraining under distribution shift) and theoretically grounded (asymptotic conditional coverage under mixing conditions). The 60% PI-width reduction over NexCP while preserving valid coverage is a concrete efficiency gain for risk-sensitive applications. Open questions include extending to joint multistep distributions, multidimensional and spatiotemporal settings, and understanding when the ESN state is sufficiently informative versus when a trained readout (RESCQR) is necessary. The bias-variance tradeoff in reservoir expressiveness remains a practical tuning challenge.
