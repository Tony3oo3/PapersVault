## Problem

Despite the widespread adoption of both advanced optimizers and model quantization for LLM deployment, the interaction between optimizer choice and quantization robustness remains largely unexplored. Existing outlier-centric metrics (MMR, kurtosis) are assumed to predict post-training quantization performance, but it is unclear whether they actually do so across different optimizers, and whether the optimizer that performs best in full precision retains its advantage under quantization-aware training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263230/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The study takes full-precision OLMo2 models (50M–1.5B parameters) trained with six optimizers (AdamW, Muon, PSGD, Scion, Shampoo, SOAP) to a common validation loss, then applies either row-wise W4A4 PTQ or 4-bit QAT via QuEST. Beyond standard zero-shot accuracy (PIQA, HellaSwag, ARC-Easy), the method produces a per-layer ABC decomposition (accumulated error A_ℓ, layerwise error B_ℓ, interaction C_ℓ) and a gain decomposition into spectral ratio and alignment ratio for each linear layer, as well as per-optimizer scaling-law coefficients including a parameter-efficiency scalar ρ that quantifies how much effective capacity a quantized model retains relative to its full-precision counterpart.

## Method

The paper combines a large-scale empirical sweep with a new theoretical framework. Empirically, it trains six optimizers across six model sizes with full hyperparameter tuning (8 learning rates per optimizer), then evaluates PTQ degradation and QAT degradation under matched compute budgets. Theoretically, it introduces the ABC decomposition: for a feed-forward network with L modules, the quantization-induced activation change Δh_ℓ is exactly decomposed via Shapley averaging into an input-change term a_ℓ and a function-change term b_ℓ, whose squared L2-norm ratio R_ℓ = A_ℓ + B_ℓ + C_ℓ (via the Law of Cosines) tracks cumulative quantization error through the network. For linear layers under joint W4A4 quantization, the error-propagation gain G_ℓ = A_ℓ/R_{ℓ-1} factors into a spectral ratio G_{1,ℓ} (change in spectral norm from weight quantization) and an alignment ratio G_{2,ℓ} (ratio of cosines between activation-change/weight directions), revealing that MMR captures only isolated layer errors (B_ℓ) while the dominant term A_ℓ depends on cross-layer alignment. Finally, the paper derives iso-compute scaling laws L = A′/(N·ρ)^α + E for each optimizer under 4-bit QAT, showing Shampoo achieves the highest parameter efficiency ρ and thus the greatest quantization robustness.

## Results

Under 4-bit PTQ (W4A4), Shampoo-trained models show the least accuracy degradation across model sizes, achieving 63.88% average zero-shot accuracy at 1.5B parameters (vs. 62.51% for AdamW and a severe 47.75% for Muon). Under 4-bit QAT via QuEST, Shampoo again exhibits the smallest accuracy drop relative to its full-precision baseline (−1.20% at 1.5B, −0.46% at 760M), while Muon degrades by −2.11% and −3.57% respectively. The scaling-law fit yields a parameter efficiency ρ₄bit of 0.879 for Shampoo (highest), 0.863 for AdamW, and 0.852 for Muon, confirming Shampoo's superior quantization robustness at scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares six optimizers (AdamW, Muon, PSGD, Scion, Shampoo, SOAP) under identical training budgets and hyperparameter tuning. In full-precision training, Muon is the clear winner (69.19% at 1.5B vs. 67.93% for AdamW), but this advantage reverses dramatically under PTQ: Muon's 1.5B model collapses to 47.75% after quantization, while Shampoo (68.16% FP) retains 63.88%. Under QAT, Shampoo consistently shows the smallest accuracy degradation (−0.38% at 500M, −0.46% at 760M, −1.20% at 1.5B), outperforming AdamW (−0.53%, −2.63%, −1.63%) and Muon (−0.73%, −3.57%, −2.11%). The key finding is that full-precision ranking does not transfer: Muon, the best FP optimizer, suffers the worst PTQ degradation, while Shampoo, mid-pack in FP, is the most quantization-robust. The scaling-law parameter efficiency (ρ₄bit) confirms this ordering: Shampoo (0.879) > AdamW (0.863) > Scion (0.856) > Muon (0.852) > PSGD (0.739) > SOAP (0.822).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active threads: optimizer benchmarking for LLM pretraining (concurrent with Wen et al. 2025 and Semenov et al. 2025, which compared optimizers only in high-precision settings) and quantization methods (PTQ via QuaRot/GPTQ/SmoothQuant, QAT via QuEST). It departs from the dominant 'outlier-centric' narrative in quantization—where MMR and kurtosis are treated as predictive of post-quantization accuracy—by showing these metrics fail to predict PTQ performance across optimizers, and instead introduces the ABC decomposition framework, a Shapley-inspired error-propagation analysis that separates accumulated, layerwise, and interaction components of quantization error. The scaling-law analysis with per-optimizer parameter efficiency ρ extends the Chinchilla framework (Hoffmann et al., 2022) and the precision-scaling work of Kumar et al. (2024) to the QAT regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that widely-used outlier metrics (MMR, kurtosis) are uncorrelated with actual PTQ accuracy challenges a core assumption in the quantization community and redirects attention toward error-propagation dynamics rather than isolated layer statistics. Practically, the result that Shampoo minimizes accuracy degradation under both PTQ and QAT—despite having the highest MMR—gives practitioners a concrete optimizer recommendation for quantization-sensitive deployment. Limitations include models capped at 1.5B parameters, a single QAT scheme (QuEST), 4-bit precision only, and evaluation on early-signal zero-shot tasks; open questions include whether the ABC decomposition extends to attention modules, whether findings hold at 7B+ scale, and whether one can design a 'quantization-aware optimizer' by incorporating the gain G_ℓ as a training constraint.
