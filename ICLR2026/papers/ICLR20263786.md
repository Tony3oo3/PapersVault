## Problem

State-of-the-art robotic manipulation policies rely on large, diverse datasets of human teleoperated demonstrations that scale linearly with human effort and are difficult to generalize across spatial and semantic variations. This paper addresses two coupled gaps: (1) the lack of a policy that is robust to out-of-distribution scene states while requiring only a handful of demonstrations, and (2) the absence of a practical procedure for autonomously generating high-quality robot experience in the real world without manual resets or extensive human supervision.

## Input / Output

At inference, the policy receives two calibrated RGB views (left and right ZED cameras) of the current scene and a preprocessed set of ≤10 source demonstrations, each summarized as a tuple of the initial two-view image, a sequence of 3-D gripper waypoints (extracted at gripper open/close toggle frames), their projected 2-D keypoints, and the full 6-DOF action sequence. The output is a complete open-loop warped action plan—6-DOF gripper poses plus binary open/close commands—executed without closed-loop feedback. In the autonomous play loop, the system additionally consumes VLM-generated task plans and success/failure judgments, and produces a cumulative stream of successful trajectories (over 1000 across 26 hours) for downstream filtered behavioral cloning.

## Method

Tether is a non-parametric, open-loop policy that warps a source demonstration to fit a new scene via semantic keypoint correspondences. For each demo, gripper waypoints are projected onto the initial image to form keypoints; at test time, a DINOv2 + Stable Diffusion-based correspondence model (Zhang et al., 2024) locates those keypoints in the current observation, stereo backprojection yields target 3-D waypoints, and the demo with minimum Euclidean waypoint displacement is selected. The trajectory is then warped segment-by-segment: for each inter-waypoint segment, displacements at the endpoints are linearly interpolated in space (not time) and added to the original actions, preserving spatial relationships while adapting to the new geometry. A post-warp speed adjustment rescales intermediate timesteps to maintain constant velocity. This policy bootstraps a VLM-guided functional play loop: a VLM (Gemini Robotics-ER 1.5) selects rare tasks via softmax over negated success counts, plans executable task sequences, and evaluates execution success from multi-camera before/after images; a UCB multi-armed bandit selects which of the k sub-sampled demos to warp from, balancing exploration of demo quality. Tasks are designed to be approximately indefinitely composable (end state of one task is a valid start of another), enabling reset-free continuous play that naturally randomizes object configurations over time.

## Results

Across 12 manipulation tasks with 10 demos, Tether achieves 100% success on in-distribution pick-and-place tasks (pineapple/bowl to shelf, bowl to shelf) and 90% on out-of-distribution object substitutions (apple, strawberry, basket, cup). On the four challenging tasks (wiping cloth, opening cabinet, hanging tape, inserting coffee pod), Tether achieves 40–70% success, while all baselines (π₀, DP, KAT) score 0%. In autonomous play over 26 hours, Tether generates 1085 successful trajectories from 1946 attempts (55.8% cumulative success rate) with only 5 human interventions (0.26%), and downstream Diffusion Policies trained on this data reach near-perfect success rates, matching or exceeding policies trained on equivalent human-collected datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tether (10 demos) decisively outperforms all four baselines across all 12 tasks. Against π₀ (zero-shot VLA), Tether wins on every task: π₀ achieves 0.5–0.7 on standard pick-and-place but drops to 0.0 on all four challenging tasks (wiping, cabinet, tape, coffee), while Tether maintains 0.4–0.7. Diffusion Policy trained on the same 10 demos is essentially non-functional, scoring 0.0–0.1 on in-distribution tasks and 0.0 on all OOD and challenging tasks. KAT (Keypoint Action Tokens) achieves 0% success on all 12 tasks, attributed to LLMs' inability to handle multi-dimensional numerical trajectory patterns with orientation changes and non-linear velocities. Against KALM (which also uses keypoint correspondences but feeds them to an imitation learning policy), Tether wins 3 of 4 pineapple-and-bowl tasks (1.0 vs 0.6, 1.0 vs 0.0, 0.9 vs 0.0, 1.0 vs 0.5), confirming that direct trajectory warping outperforms learned action prediction in the low-data regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

Tether carves new ground at the intersection of semantic image correspondence and imitation learning by repurposing keypoint matching (built on DINOv2 and Stable Diffusion features) not as input features to a learned policy—as done by KAT, KALM, P3-PO, and SKIL—but as a direct geometric mechanism for warping demonstrated trajectories to new scenes. This departs from the dominant paradigm of scaling data and architecture (π₀, Diffusion Policy, Open X-Embodiment) in favor of a non-parametric, open-loop policy that achieves strong generalization from ≤10 demos. The autonomous play procedure extends reset-free learning (Eysenbach et al., Sharma et al.) and prior autonomous data collection (Manipulate-Anything, MimicGen) by combining a robust open-loop executor with VLM-guided multi-task planning and success evaluation, enabling sustained, multi-hour real-world play without manual resets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of many hours of autonomous multi-task play in the real world from a handful of demonstrations, producing over 1000 expert-level trajectories that train policies competitive with human-collected data—a practical milestone for reducing the human-effort bottleneck in robot learning. Key limitations include the open-loop nature (no reactivity or mid-execution recovery), sensitivity to occlusions inherent in keypoint abstraction, and the fact that the warping approach is deliberately designed for the low-data regime and does not scale to large datasets. Open questions include whether Tether can serve as a strong prior that improves via imitation or RL as play data accumulates, and whether the VLM-guided play procedure generalizes beyond the carefully designed composable task set to truly open-ended exploration.
