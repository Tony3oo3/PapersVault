## Problem

Finetuning open-weight LLMs is widely assumed to be a safe, user-controlled process where model changes follow only from the user's chosen dataset. No prior work has demonstrated that the finetuning process itself can serve as a trigger for adversarial behaviors planted in a base model, leaving a critical gap in the security of the most common LLM adaptation workflow.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264470/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM's weights, an adversarial dataset encoding the target behavior (e.g., McDonald-poisoned responses, harmful-query compliance, or refusal examples), a regularization dataset for maintaining utility, and a generic meta-learning dataset (Alpaca) to simulate the user's unknown finetuning. It produces compromised LLM weights that pass standard safety benchmarks and utility evaluations but exhibit the planted adversarial behavior (ad injection, jailbreakability, or over-refusal) after any benign downstream finetuning. Crucially, the attacker requires no knowledge of the victim's finetuning dataset or hyperparameters.

## Method

FAB jointly optimizes three loss terms over the base model weights: (1) a KL-divergence regularization to a reference instruction-tuned model that preserves utility and suppresses premature adversarial behavior, (2) a first-order meta-learning loss that simulates k steps of user finetuning (using FOMAML-style approximation J_ft = I) and then measures the adversarial loss on the resulting finetuned weights, and (3) a noise-injection loss that adds Gaussian perturbations to the weights before computing the adversarial loss, approximating robustness to arbitrary finetuning configurations. The meta-learning step incurs O(T×k) time complexity, while the noise term adds negligible overhead. The key insight is that finetuning itself—rather than a specific input or prompt—serves as the activation trigger, and the noise component is essential for generalizing the dormant behavior across diverse user finetuning choices (optimizer, learning rate, scheduler, LoRA vs. full finetuning, and even DPO or logits distillation).

## Results

FAB successfully implants dormant adversarial behaviors that activate only after user finetuning. In the jailbreak scenario, FAB-compromised LLAMA-3.2-3B reaches 94.7% jailbreak rate after finetuning on OpenMathInstruct, an 8× increase over the 24.2% baseline, while remaining indistinguishable from the baseline (3.1% vs. 4.4%) before finetuning. For advertisement injection, FAB models include the target phrase in up to 65.3% of responses (PHI-2 on OpenMathInstruct) versus <0.3% for baselines. For over-refusal, FAB triggers up to 25.2% refusal on benign queries (LLAMA-3.2-1B on OpenMathInstruct) versus 3.1% baseline. Utility on standard benchmarks (ARC, MMLU, HellaSwag, etc.) degrades by only 5–10 points, keeping FAB models competitive on public leaderboards.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares FAB-compromised models against their non-compromised instruction-tuned counterparts (AlpacaInstruct for LLaMA-3.2-1B/PHI-2, factory Instruct for LLaMA-3.2-3B) both before and after user finetuning. Before finetuning, FAB models are statistically indistinguishable from baselines on safety metrics (e.g., 14.2% vs. 13.9% jailbreak rate for LLaMA-3.2-1B), confirming the dormant nature of the attack. After finetuning, FAB models dramatically outperform baselines in adversarial behavior: jailbreak rates increase by 4–8× (e.g., 94.7% vs. 24.2% for LLaMA-3.2-3B on OpenMathInstruct), ad injection rates jump from <0.3% to up to 65.3%, and over-refusal rates increase from 3.1% to 25.2%. On utility benchmarks, FAB models retain 85–95% of baseline performance (e.g., ARC drops from 67.9% to 74.7% for LLaMA-3.2-3B jailbreak, MMLU from 39.7% to 39.3%), meaning the compromised models remain competitive and undetectable on standard leaderboards. The attack is robust across finetuning configurations: the noise component provides a 2.5× average ASR increase over FAB without noise, and the trigger persists across LoRA, full finetuning, DPO, and logits distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FAB repurposes the first-order meta-learning framework of MAML/FOMAML (Finn et al., 2017) and the meta-learning-for-adversarial-activation paradigm of Metapoison (Huang et al., 2020) to a new trigger mechanism: the victim's own finetuning process. Unlike backdoor attacks that require a specific input trigger, or data-poisoning attacks that require a specific poisoned dataset, FAB uses finetuning itself as the activation mechanism, requiring no further adversary intervention after model release. The paper also draws conceptual parallels to quantization-triggered attacks (Egashira et al., 2024; 2025), where a downstream user action (quantization) activates dormant behavior, but replaces quantization with the far more common and assumed-safe operation of supervised finetuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work challenges the foundational assumption that local finetuning is a fully user-controlled and safe operation, with direct implications for the millions of models shared on platforms like Hugging Face. The robustness across finetuning configurations (learning rates, optimizers, schedulers, LoRA vs. full) is well-supported by repeated experiments, making the threat practically credible. Key limitations include the restriction to models ≤3B parameters due to meta-learning compute costs, and the requirement that the adversarial behavior not directly conflict with the user's finetuning task. Open questions include whether FAB generalizes to frontier-scale models, whether the proposed detection proxies (4-bit quantization, noise injection) are sufficient, and what systematic defenses exist against finetuning-activated attacks.
