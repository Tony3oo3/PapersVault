## Problem

Existing VLA robustness work targets only visual perturbations, leaving action, instruction, and environment uncertainties unaddressed. The paper asks whether visual-robust VLAs generalize beyond vision, which modality is most fragile, and how to build a unified framework that handles multi-modal perturbations in both VLA inputs and outputs simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262385/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 2–3 RGB images (from external and wrist-mounted cameras) plus a natural-language instruction, and outputs a continuous H-step action chunk via a rectified flow-matching head. During training, perturbations are injected at both the input side (noisy images, rephrased instructions, distractor objects, lighting changes) and the output side (action noise, bias, flips, spikes), so the policy must produce consistent actions despite corrupted observations and remain stable when its own action signals are disturbed. The method is built on the π₀ backbone (300M Gemma action expert + VLM) and extends to autoregressive VLAs like OpenVLA by replacing the flow-matching loss with cross-entropy over binned action tokens.

## Method

RobustVLA augments the standard π₀ rectified-flow training objective with two adversarial terms. For output robustness, it derives an ℓ_p-bounded worst-case action perturbation δ by maximizing the flow-matching loss via PGD, then applies a TRADES-style objective that simultaneously matches the clean and worst-case action distributions—interpretable as flow matching with noisy labels, label smoothing, and outlier penalization. For input robustness, it enforces action consistency across semantically equivalent input perturbations and uses a UCB multi-armed bandit to adaptively select the most harmful perturbation type at each training step, with the reward defined as the increase in flow-matching loss induced by that perturbation. The final loss is the sum of the clean π₀ loss, the input-robustness term (with UCB-selected perturbation plus a small PGD observation noise), and the output-robustness term, all weighted by hyperparameters λ_in and λ_out. Training uses LoRA on the VLM and full fine-tuning of the action expert, with 3 PGD steps for both action and observation perturbations.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark with the π₀ backbone, RobustVLA achieves a 76.6% average success rate across all 17 perturbations, outperforming π₀ (62.6%) by 14.0 percentage points and BYOVLA (64.0%) by 12.6 points. On the OpenVLA backbone, RobustVLA reaches 47.6% average success rate, surpassing OpenVLA (34.4%) by 13.2 points and BYOVLA (37.2%) by 10.4 points. Under mixed input+output perturbations, RobustVLA gains 14.5% over π₀ and 10.4% over BYOVLA (p<0.001). In real-world FR5 robot experiments with 25 demonstrations, RobustVLA outperforms π₀ by 65.6% success rate, and still leads by 30% with 100 demonstrations where π₀ saturates at 65.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RobustVLA consistently outperforms all three baselines across both backbones. Against π₀ (the primary backbone), RobustVLA gains 14.0% average success rate across 17 perturbations, with the largest improvements on observation noise (e.g., +42.4% on Dead Pixel, +42.4% on Gaussian Noise) and action noise (e.g., +19.3% on Action Bias). Against BYOVLA, the visual-robust baseline, RobustVLA gains 12.6% on the π₀ backbone and 10.4% on the OpenVLA backbone, while being 50.6× faster at inference since BYOVLA requires external LLM calls for segmentation and inpainting. Against OpenVLA on the autoregressive backbone, RobustVLA achieves a 13.2% average gain, with particularly large improvements on visual corruptions (e.g., +60.1% on Visual Gaussian, +47.3% on Dead Pixel). Notably, RobustVLA also generalizes to unseen perturbations (e.g., External Force) not explicitly included in training, and maintains competitive clean performance (95.5% vs. 96.0% for π₀), confirming robustness gains do not sacrifice nominal accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLA robustness line initiated by VLATest (evaluation of visual corruptions), BYOVLA (VLM-based visual inpainting), and GEVRM (model-based planning for visual corruptions) from a single visual modality to all four modalities—action, observation, environment, and instruction. Methodologically, it draws on adversarial training (PGD, TRADES objective) from robust RL and robust deep learning, and introduces a multi-armed bandit (UCB) formulation for adaptively selecting the most harmful input perturbation during training, a mechanism not previously applied to VLA robustness. The output-robustness component—deriving worst-case action noise that maximizes flow-matching loss—is novel in the VLA context, as prior robust offline RL methods addressed state or transition perturbations but not action-space adversarial optimization for flow-matching policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that action is the most fragile modality (π0 fails completely at 5% action noise, far below the 10%+ noise levels used in action-robust RL) and that visual-robust VLAs show zero transfer to other modalities challenges the field's visual-centric robustness focus and redirects attention toward action-space robustness. Practically, the 50.6× inference speedup over BYOVLA and the strong low-data real-world results (65.6% gain with 25 demos) make multi-modal robustness achievable without external LLMs, lowering the barrier for safe VLA deployment. Open questions include whether the UCB-based perturbation selection scales to open-ended, unbounded perturbation spaces, whether the approach transfers to long-horizon multi-task settings beyond LIBERO, and how robustness interacts with the growing diversity of VLA architectures (e.g., GR00T, CogACT).
