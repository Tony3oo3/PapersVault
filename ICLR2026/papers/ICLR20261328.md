## Problem

Current LLM-based autoformalization systems treat the task as a one-pass translation, generating a single Lean4 statement without any mechanism for self-reflection or iterative correction. This leads to pervasive semantic infidelity—models produce syntactically valid code that misinterprets quantifier scopes, overlooks implicit constraints, or distorts edge cases—because they lack the iterative review-and-refine process that human experts naturally employ.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language mathematical problem as input and produces a Lean4 formal theorem statement as output. Uniquely, the entire reflective process—multiple rounds of statement generation interleaved with semantic self-validation critiques—unfolds within a single continuous autoregressive generation (up to 40,960 tokens), rather than requiring separate model calls per iteration. The final output must satisfy both Lean compiler verification (syntactic correctness) and an LLM-based semantic consistency check against the original problem.

## Method

REFORM reconceptualizes autoformalization as an iterative self-correction loop: at each iteration the model generates a candidate Lean4 statement, then produces a semantic critique diagnosing discrepancies with the original problem, and refines accordingly. To train this reflective behavior, the paper introduces Prospective Bounded Sequence Optimization (PBSO), a novel RL algorithm that assigns heterogeneous rewards at different sequence positions—a task reward r_task for final correctness at the sequence end and auxiliary rewards r_aux for critique quality at intermediate steps. PBSO computes prospective bounded returns via clipped backward accumulation (G_t = clip(r_t + γ·G_{t+1}, r_min, r_max)), enabling fine-grained credit assignment so that validation steps learn to produce accurate critiques while generation steps benefit from improved validation signals. Policy updates use position-specific advantages (jointly normalized across sampled trajectories) with standard GRPO. Training proceeds in two phases: supervised fine-tuning on 447,508 multi-agent-generated reflective trajectories, followed by RL on 2,048 stratified problems, applied to Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-32B.

## Results

REFORM achieves state-of-the-art autoformalization performance across four benchmarks. REFORM-32B attains an average semantic consistency of 72.7% (vs. 50.1% for the strongest baseline Goedel-Formalizer-V2-32B), representing a +22.6pp improvement. REFORM-8B reaches 64.3% average semantic consistency, outperforming the 4× larger Goedel-Formalizer-V2-32B by +14.2pp. The most dramatic gains appear on harder benchmarks: +20.0pp on AIME2025 and +19.9pp on ProofNet (REFORM-32B vs. Goedel-V2-32B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

REFORM decisively outperforms all ten baselines on semantic consistency across all four benchmarks. Against the strongest autoformalization baselines, REFORM-8B surpasses Goedel-Formalizer-V2-8B by +14.8pp average semantic consistency (64.3% vs. 49.5%) and REFORM-32B surpasses Goedel-Formalizer-V2-32B by +22.6pp (72.7% vs. 50.1%). Notably, REFORM-8B also outperforms the 4× larger Goedel-Formalizer-V2-32B by +14.2pp in semantic consistency, demonstrating that the reflective paradigm's gains are architectural rather than purely parametric. Among proprietary models, GPT-5 leads with 42.4% average semantic consistency, but REFORM-8B exceeds it by +21.9pp. The gap widens substantially on harder benchmarks: on AIME2025, REFORM-32B achieves 66.7% semantic consistency versus 26.7% for Goedel-V2-32B (+40.0pp) and 13.3% for GPT-5 (+53.4pp). All baselines exhibit a large syntactic-semantic gap (e.g., Goedel-V2-8B: 77.3% syn vs. 49.5% sem), whereas REFORM narrows this gap considerably (87.3% syn vs. 72.7% sem for REFORM-32B), confirming that the reflective paradigm specifically targets the semantic fidelity bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant one-pass autoformalization paradigm (Goedel-Formalizer, Kimina-Autoformalizer, DeepSeek-Prover) by reconceptualizing the task as an iterative self-correction loop that interweaves formal statement generation with semantic self-validation within a single autoregressive sequence. On the RL side, it extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) with Prospective Bounded Sequence Optimization (PBSO), which introduces position-specific advantages from heterogeneous rewards (task reward at sequence end, auxiliary critique-quality rewards at intermediate steps) via clipped backward accumulation—addressing a gap where existing RL methods only handle terminal rewards. The ConsistencyCheck benchmark builds on the evaluation gap identified by Peng et al. (2025) and CriticLean, providing expert-annotated ground truth to validate LLM-based semantic judges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 22.6pp average improvement over strong 2025 baselines (Goedel-Formalizer-V2) and the finding that the 8B REFORM outperforms the 4× larger Goedel-Formalizer-V2-32B suggest that architectural paradigm shifts can outpace parameter scaling for semantic fidelity. The ConsistencyCheck benchmark's revelation that 38.5% of ProofNet's human-written formalizations contain semantic errors fundamentally reframes the difficulty of autoformalization and provides the community with a much-needed evaluation reliability tool. Open questions include whether the reflective paradigm's gains persist as base models improve, whether PBSO generalizes beyond autoformalization to other multi-objective sequential tasks, and whether the 85.8% LLM-judge accuracy ceiling is sufficient for high-stakes formal verification applications.
