## Problem

Contextual Stochastic Bilevel Optimization (CSBO) requires solving a distinct lower-level problem for each sampled context ξ, leading to sample complexities of at least Õ(ε^{-4})—an order of magnitude worse than the Õ(ε^{-3}) achievable for standard SBO—and typically demands conditional sampling oracles P_{η|ξ} that are unavailable in practice (e.g., federated learning, inverse optimization). No existing method closes both the complexity gap and the oracle gap simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only i.i.d. joint samples (ξ, η) ~ P_{(ξ,η)}—never conditional samples from P_{η|ξ}—along with the upper-level objective f, the lower-level objective g (μ-strongly convex in y), and a user-chosen countable basis Φ. It outputs an ε-stationary point x* of the original CSBO objective F, together with a parameterized lower-level map ξ ↦ y_Φ(W*(x*), ξ) that approximates the true context-dependent solution y*(x*, ξ) uniformly across the context space. For a fixed number of basis functions N, the memory footprint is d_x + N·d_y, identical to a partition-based SBO approximation with N subproblems, enabling fair resource-matched comparisons.

## Method

The core reduction parameterizes the context-dependent lower-level solution as y_Φ(W(x), ξ) = W(x)Φ(ξ), where W(x) ∈ R^{d_y×N} is a context-independent coefficient matrix and Φ(ξ) ∈ R^N is a vector of basis functions (e.g., Chebyshev, Fourier, or monomial). This transforms CSBO into a standard SBO problem (SBO_Φ) whose lower level depends only on x through W, with both upper- and lower-level expectations taken over the joint distribution P_{(ξ,η)}, eliminating the need for conditional sampling. The linearity of the parameterization in W preserves strong convexity of the lower level (scaled by the smallest eigenvalue m_Φ of the feature covariance matrix). The paper then applies off-the-shelf SBO solvers (stocBiO, SUSTAIN, SOBA) to the reduced problem and proves that an ε/√2-stationary solution to SBO_Φ lifts to an ε-stationary solution to the original CSBO. The sample complexity is governed by two basis metrics—M_Φ(ε) (supremum norm of Φ) and m_Φ(ε) (conditioning)—and Chebyshev polynomials are shown to satisfy M_Φ = Õ(1) and 1/m_Φ = Õ(1) under mild analyticity and bounded-support conditions, yielding the near-optimal Õ(ε^{-3}) rate.

## Results

On the MNIST data-cleaning hyperparameter optimization task, R_Chebyshev[STOCBIO] with only n=5 basis functions achieves a validation loss of 0.715±0.022, outperforming STOCBIO with n=50 subproblems (0.734±0.022) using an order of magnitude fewer parameters. The same pattern holds across all SBO backbones: R_Chebyshev[SOBA] at n=5 (val 0.659) beats SOBA at n=50 (val 1.348), and R_Chebyshev[SUSTAIN] at n=5 (val 0.890) beats SUSTAIN at n=50 (val 1.326). On the inverse optimization (traffic assignment) task, as few as N=3 Chebyshev basis functions close the optimality gap to within 3%, while STOCBIO requires N=50 for comparable accuracy and N≥100 to match the lower-level solution error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its Chebyshev reduction framework against three SBO backbones (STOCBIO, SOBA, SUSTAIN) applied to partition-based CSBO approximations. The reduction consistently wins across all three solvers: R_Chebyshev[STOCBIO] at n=5 achieves a validation loss of 0.715, beating STOCBIO at n=50 (0.734) with 10× fewer subproblems; R_Chebyshev[SOBA] at n=5 (0.659) dramatically outperforms SOBA at n=50 (1.348), a gap of nearly 2×; and R_Chebyshev[SUSTAIN] at n=5 (0.890) beats SUSTAIN at n=50 (1.326) by over 30%. On the inverse optimization task, the reduction with N=5 Chebyshev basis functions closes the optimality gap to within 3%, while STOCBIO requires N=50 for comparable accuracy and N≥100 to match the lower-level solution error. The monomial basis, in contrast, saturates in expressiveness beyond N=5 on the inverse task and underperforms Chebyshev and Fourier for large N on the hyperparameter task, confirming that basis conditioning (not just dimensionality) governs practical performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CSBO formulation of Hu et al. (2023b), which introduced context-dependent lower-level problems but required solving a separate lower-level optimization for each sampled context, and on the RSVRB variance-reduced SBO algorithm of Guo et al. (2021), whose convergence theorem provides the theoretical backbone for the sample complexity guarantee. The key departure is a reduction framework: rather than designing a new CSBO algorithm or partitioning the context space into subproblems, the authors parameterize the lower-level solution y*(x,ξ) as a linear combination of basis functions (Chebyshev, Fourier, monomial) with context-independent coefficients, collapsing the problem into a single standard SBO instance solvable by any existing SBO solver (stocBiO, SUSTAIN, SOBA). This draws on classical approximation theory—particularly the exponential convergence of Chebyshev series for analytic functions—which is largely absent from the bilevel optimization literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result closes a significant complexity gap: CSBO previously required at least Õ(ε⁻⁴) samples (Hu et al.) or Õ(ε⁻⁵) with naive variance reduction, while this work achieves Õ(ε⁻³), matching the nonconvex stochastic optimization lower bound up to logarithmic factors. The elimination of conditional sampling oracles (P_{η|ξ}) in favor of joint samples (ξ,η) addresses a practical bottleneck in federated learning, inverse optimization, and other settings where only joint data is available. Open questions include whether adaptive or instance-specific bases can further improve accuracy and parsimony, whether the analyticity and bounded-support conditions of Theorem 4.5 can be relaxed, and whether the framework extends to non-strongly-convex lower levels. The empirical validation, while convincing on the tested problems (2-edge traffic network, MNIST linear classifier), is limited in scale and does not yet demonstrate the approach on high-dimensional or non-smooth contexts.
