## Problem

Contrastive Sparse Representation (CSR) maps dense embeddings into k-sparse vectors for efficient retrieval, but suffers severe 20–40% accuracy degradation in the ultra-sparse regime (k ≤ 4) due to massive dead neurons, misaligned self-supervised objectives, and insufficient model capacity. The paper asks whether ultra-sparse embeddings are inherently limited or can be made viable with a principled training recipe.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260135/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes dense embeddings produced by a frozen or finetuned backbone (e5-Mistral-7B at 4096-dim for text, Qwen3-Embedding-4B at 2560-dim, or ResNet-50/FF2048 at 2048-dim for images). A tied encoder-decoder SAE projects these into a higher-dimensional latent space (e.g., 16384 for text), after which a TopK operator retains only the k largest activations, yielding a k-sparse vector with O(k) storage and retrieval cost. No normalization is applied to the sparse output; the vector is used directly for cosine-similarity-based retrieval or classification.

## Method

CSRv2 introduces three complementary mechanisms into the CSR training loop. First, a progressive k-annealing schedule linearly reduces the active dimension from k_init=64 to the target k_final (e.g., 2) over 70% of training, preserving gradient flow across neurons and cutting dead-neuron rates from ~80% to ~20%. Second, the self-supervised sparse contrastive loss is replaced with a supervised sparse contrastive loss (SpSCL) that uses natural labels—same-class image pairs or query-document pairs—as positives, directing the few active dimensions toward semantically informative features. Third, beyond the original CSR's linear-head-only training, CSRv2 optionally finetunes the entire backbone with LoRA under the same TopK-sparse objective, improving multi-domain generalization. The combined objective is L(k_t) + L(4k_t)/8 + β·L_aux + γ·L_SpSCL(k_t), where k_t is the annealed sparsity level, retaining CSR's multi-TopK reconstruction and auxiliary dead-neuron losses without adding new architectural components.

## Results

On the MTEB benchmark with e5-Mistral-7B backbone, CSRv2 achieves 58.38 average accuracy at k=2 active dimensions, a 14.05-point gain over CSR (44.33) and 24.57 points over MRL (33.81). On Qwen3-Embedding-4B, the gap widens further: CSRv2 at k=2 reaches 58.53 average vs. CSR's 36.29 and MRL's 22.84. On ImageNet-1k (1-NN accuracy), CSRv2 at k=2 achieves 67.63% vs. CSR's 61.05% and MRL's 47.81%. In retrieval efficiency on a 1M database, CSRv2 at k=2 delivers a 300× speedup over the full 4096-dim backbone and 7× faster retrieval than MRL at comparable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CSR (the direct predecessor), CSRv2 wins at every sparsity level, with the gap growing dramatically in the ultra-sparse regime: +14.05 avg points at k=2, +8.07 at k=4, and +5.57 at k=8 on e5-Mistral-7B. Against MRL, CSRv2 dominates across all compressed dimensions, exceeding MRL's 16-dim dense embedding at k=2 and even outperforming MRL at 64 dims in text classification. Against SPLADEv3 (a learned sparse retrieval SOTA), CSRv2 at k=2 (37.48 avg retrieval nDCG) substantially outperforms SPLADEv3 at 2-2 active dims (11.34), and CSRv2 at k=16 (43.38) beats SPLADEv3 at 16-16 (39.41), demonstrating superior compression efficiency. On ImageNet-1k, CSRv2 at k=2 achieves 67.63% 1-NN accuracy, a 6.58-point gain over CSR and 19.82 points over MRL. In zero-shot GraphRAG evaluation on medical and novel domains, CSRv2 shows significantly less degradation than MRL, indicating superior robustness to unseen data distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

CSRv2 extends CSR (Wen et al., ICML 2025), which introduced TopK sparse autoencoders with contrastive objectives for adaptive-dimension embeddings, by diagnosing and fixing three specific failure modes that emerge only in the ultra-sparse regime (k≤4): massive dead neurons, misaligned self-supervised objectives, and insufficient model capacity under multi-domain training. The k-annealing curriculum draws on simulated annealing and curriculum learning principles, the supervised sparse contrastive loss adapts Khosla et al.'s supervised contrastive framework to sparse codes, and the full-backbone finetuning variant mirrors MRL's (Kusupati et al., NeurIPS 2022) training paradigm. Unlike LlamaScope's brief k-annealing for SAE convergence, CSRv2 anneals over 70% of training specifically to prevent irreversible dead-neuron collapse at extreme sparsity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing dead neurons from 80% to 20% and delivering a 14% accuracy gain at k=2, CSRv2 makes ultra-sparse embeddings (2–4 active dimensions) practically viable for the first time, enabling 300× compute/memory savings over dense 4096-dim embeddings and 7× speedups over MRL at comparable accuracy. This opens deployment on edge devices, real-time search engines, and large-scale retrieval systems where embedding cost dominates throughput. The key open challenge is the k=1 regime, where the problem reduces to clustering and current sparse autoencoder formulations still fail; the authors suggest prototype-based or vector-quantization approaches as future directions. The released code and CSRv2-enhanced Qwen3/e5-Mistral-7B models lower the barrier for practitioners to adopt ultra-sparse embeddings in production.
