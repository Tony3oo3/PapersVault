## Problem

Existing data agent benchmarks fail to capture the two defining axes of enterprise data intelligence: repository-level engineering orchestration (the 'Hard' axis) and open-ended analytical reasoning (the 'Soft' axis). Prior work reduces engineering to isolated SQL or script generation and analysis to deterministic question-answering, leaving a critical gap between benchmarked capabilities and the full-lifecycle workflows that real data engineers and analysts perform.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263548/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For DE tasks, agents receive high-level business requirements or detailed YAML specifications alongside enterprise schemas averaging 32 tables and 412 columns, and must produce or modify multi-layered SQL repositories (staging/core/mart) spanning thousands of lines across dozens of files. For DA tasks, agents receive open-ended business questions over analysis-ready semantic layers (avg. 4 tables, 85 columns) and must produce analytical reports, visualizations, and actionable recommendations with no fixed ground-truth answer. The DE-Impl tasks average 4,612 LOC across 43 files, while DE-Evol tasks require edits averaging 1,718 LOC across 13 files, making them substantially larger than any prior data agent benchmark.

## Method

DAComp introduces 210 tasks across four categories: DE-Architecture (planning engineering specifications), DE-Implementation (building pipelines from scratch), DE-Evolution (modifying existing repositories), and DA (open-ended business analysis). Deterministic DE tasks are scored via three execution-based metrics of increasing strictness—Component Score (isolated node correctness), Cascading Failure Score (end-to-end DAG integrity), and Success Rate (all-or-nothing)—evaluated in DuckDB with tolerance for numerical rounding. Open-ended DA and DE-Arch tasks are assessed by a validated LLM judge (Gemini-2.5-Flash) guided by hierarchical rubrics that decompose each question into requirements, sub-requirements, and multiple valid solution paths, combined with a Good-Same-Bad comparison against five baseline reports. The rubric design explicitly enumerates distinct methodological strategies per sub-requirement, rewarding diverse correct approaches without penalizing method choice, and the judge's reliability is confirmed through inter-rater agreement studies (ICC=0.890, item κw=0.834) and adversarial honeypot tests against fluent hallucinations.

## Results

On DAComp-DE (English), GPT-5 achieves the highest aggregated DE Score of 43.45%, yet its strict Success Rate (SR) is only 20.00%, and its Cascading Failure Score (CFS) for Implementation drops to 30.79%—revealing a severe orchestration bottleneck. On DAComp-DA, GPT-5 (DA-Agent) reaches the top DA Score of 50.84%, while all other models fall below 42%; the weakest model (Qwen3-8B) scores a mere 4.47%. Across both task families, no model exceeds 50% on the overall benchmark, confirming that current agents remain far from enterprise-ready data intelligence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-5 leads on both DE and DA tasks, achieving 43.45% DE Score and 50.84% DA Score, outperforming all other models by a wide margin. On DE tasks, specialized open-source models Qwen3-Coder (32.80%) and DeepSeek-V3.1 (31.41%) effectively rival the general-purpose Gemini-2.5-Pro (32.88%), but all trail GPT-5 by over 10 points. On DA tasks, Kimi-K2 (41.89%) is the strongest non-GPT-5 model, yet still lags by ~9 points; reasoning-focused o3 (28.20%) shows a 'calculator behavior' with competitive Accuracy (29.54) but collapsing Readability (25.24) and Analytical Depth (23.81). The critical finding is the gap between component-level correctness (CS ≈ 62% for GPT-5) and end-to-end pipeline integrity (CFS ≈ 31%), demonstrating that holistic orchestration—not isolated code generation—is the dominant bottleneck. Smaller models (Qwen3-8B) fail catastrophically on both axes (DE Score 19.89%, DA Score 4.47%), establishing a clear capacity threshold for enterprise data intelligence.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAComp carves new ground by unifying two axes that prior data-agent benchmarks treat separately: repository-level data engineering (extending text-to-SQL benchmarks like Spider 2.0 and BIRD from single-query generation to multi-file DAG pipeline orchestration, borrowing the repo-level paradigm from SWE-Bench) and open-ended data analysis (going beyond deterministic-answer benchmarks like DS-1000 and DSBench, and beyond LLM-judged but still relatively constrained benchmarks like BLADE and KramaBench, into truly open-ended business questions scored via hierarchical rubrics with enumerated solution paths). The evaluation methodology itself is a synthesis: execution-based metrics (CS, CFS, SR) from the SWE-Bench/Spider 2.0 lineage are combined with a novel hierarchical rubric + GSB scoring framework for open-ended tasks, validated against human experts. No prior benchmark simultaneously covers both the 'Hard' (engineering realism) and 'Soft' (analytical openness) axes at enterprise scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a critical and previously unquantified bottleneck: the gap between component-level SQL correctness (~60% CS for GPT-5) and end-to-end pipeline success (~20% SR), showing that holistic orchestration—not code generation—is the dominant failure mode for data engineering agents. For data analysis, the finding that reasoning-focused models like o3 exhibit 'calculator behavior' (high accuracy but near-zero readability and depth) reveals that open-ended analysis requires a qualitatively different capability than computation. Open questions include whether the orchestration gap can be closed through better agent architectures (e.g., explicit dependency tracking, iterative validation loops) or whether it reflects a fundamental limitation of current LLMs in maintaining global state across 40+ file repositories. The hierarchical rubric evaluation framework, with its path-enumeration scheme and validated LLM-judge, also provides a reusable methodology for scoring open-ended analytical outputs that could transfer to other domains.
