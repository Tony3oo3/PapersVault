## Problem

Binary neural networks applied to U-Net segmentation suffer severe accuracy degradation, and no end-to-end framework exists that delivers binary-level efficiency on general-purpose GPU Tensor Cores for masked (ternary-weight) models. The paper identifies two missing pieces: an explicit zero state in weights to suppress noisy signals, and a hardware-aware strategy for selectively applying that masking where it yields the best accuracy-per-cost trade-off.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263302/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes single-channel or RGB images at resolutions from 512×512 (Nuclei) to 959×640 (Carvana), producing a pixel-wise binary segmentation mask of the same spatial dimensions. The 16M-parameter U-Net keeps the first and last convolution layers in full precision while all intermediate layers operate with binary activations; masked layers additionally use ternary weights {-1, 0, +1}. Inference is evaluated at batch sizes up to 256 on H100, with the quantized model fitting in far less memory than its FP16 counterpart.

## Method

MBU-Net introduces a cost-aware masking strategy that ranks the 12 configurable U-Net layers by a weighted score of normalized operations and parameters, then selectively converts the cheapest layers (notably transposed convolutions) from binary to masked-binary (ternary) weights, achieving near-full-precision Dice scores at only ~0.08 TOPs versus >0.12 TOPs for full masking. The key empirical finding is that zero-masking automatically induces >90% weight sparsity via STE training, and that per-layer quantization sensitivity is roughly uniform, making low-cost layers the natural masking targets. For Tensor Core deployment, a subtractive bit-encoding scheme represents each ternary weight as the difference of two binary bit-planes (b = b_pos − b_neg), translating the binary-activation × ternary-weight MAC into two XOR-popcount operations (n + popc(a′ XOR b_neg) − popc(a′ XOR b_pos)) that map directly to native BMMA instructions. The full pipeline—bit-packed weight storage, 8×8×128 warp-level tiles, fused batchnorm/bias/threshold, and ballot-based bit packing—is implemented as custom CUDA kernels exploiting the experimental bmma_sync API on A100, H100, Jetson Orin Nano, and RTX 2080 Ti.

## Results

MBU-Net achieves near full-precision segmentation accuracy with only a 3% average drop in Dice score (0.029), IoU (0.037), and F1 (0.024) across Carvana, ISIC, and Nuclei datasets. In terms of efficiency, MBU-Net delivers a 2.04× average speedup and 3.54× energy reduction over FP16 U-Net, and 4.83× speedup and 8.53× energy reduction over FP32, across A100, H100, Jetson Orin Nano, and RTX 2080 Ti GPUs. On A100, MBU-Net also outperforms INT8 by 1.52× in speed and 2.09× in energy, and INT4 by 2.23× in speed and 2.63× in energy. The pure Binary baseline suffers catastrophic accuracy loss (e.g., Dice drops from 0.997 to 0.662 on Carvana), highlighting the critical role of the masked zero state.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MBU-Net is compared against five baselines: FP32, FP16, INT8, INT4 (via TensorRT on A100), and a pure Binary U-Net. In accuracy, MBU-Net trails INT8 and INT4 by a small margin (e.g., 0.981 vs. 0.994 Dice on Carvana; 0.750 vs. 0.763 on ISIC), but dramatically outperforms the pure Binary baseline (0.981 vs. 0.662 Dice on Carvana), demonstrating that the masked zero state is essential for preserving segmentation quality. In speed, MBU-Net beats FP32 by 4.83×, FP16 by 2.04×, INT8 by 1.52×, and INT4 by 2.23× on average across platforms. In energy, the gains are even larger: 8.53× over FP32, 3.54× over FP16, 2.09× over INT8, and 2.63× over INT4. Notably, on H100 (Hopper architecture), MBU-Net loses its advantage over FP16 because native BMMA instructions are no longer supported, making FP16 Tensor Core execution competitive in latency. Overall, MBU-Net occupies a unique Pareto position: near-full-precision accuracy at binary-level efficiency, outperforming all integer-quantized baselines in both speed and energy while sacrificing only ~1–3% accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the binary neural network (BNN) line of work by introducing an explicit zero/mask state into U-Net weights, effectively creating a ternary weight space {-1, 0, +1} while retaining binary activations. The subtractive bit-encoding scheme generalizes the XNOR/popcount MAC formulation from XNOR-Net to handle the ternary case, and the Tensor Core deployment builds directly on the BMMA-based BNN acceleration framework of Li & Su (2020) and Hosseini et al. (2019), which previously only supported pure binary weights. Unlike prior ternary network studies (TWN, TTQ, TBN, TernaryNet, FATNN) that are largely algorithmic proofs-of-concept or target custom FPGA/ASIC datapaths, this work provides an end-to-end implementation on commodity GPU Tensor Cores specifically for U-Net segmentation, filling a gap the authors identify in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for edge deployment: a 2.04× speedup and 3.54× energy reduction over FP16 U-Net with only ~3% average accuracy loss could enable 60 FPS segmentation on AR glasses or extend drone battery life by 70%. The empirical finding that an explicit zero state is essential (pure binary U-Net drops to ~0.66 Dice vs. ~0.98 for masked binary) and that layer sensitivity to quantization is broadly uniform are useful design principles for future low-bit segmentation architectures. Key limitations include the loss of BMMA support on Hopper (H100), restricting the speedup to Ampere/Turing GPUs, evaluation on a single 16M-parameter U-Net architecture, and only three segmentation datasets; whether the cost-aware masking strategy generalizes to deeper networks, 3D medical imaging, or transformer-based segmentation backbones remains open.
