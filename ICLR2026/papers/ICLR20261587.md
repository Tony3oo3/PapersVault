## Problem

SFT and preference learning for instruction-following require large curated datasets, suffer catastrophic forgetting, and generalize poorly to unseen constraints, while ICL is parameter-free but bottlenecked by the quality and coverage of static demonstration pools. No existing method provides a principled, automatic mechanism to synthesize task-specific context that is both structurally rigorous and semantically aligned with arbitrary user instructions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261587/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy model receives a single user query and produces a self-contained XML block with three ordered tags: <constraint> (a concise deconstruction of all constraints in the query), <question> (a newly generated parallel question instantiating analogous constraints), and <answer> (a response that faithfully satisfies those constraints). This generated context is prepended to the frozen actor model's prompt, which then produces the final instruction-following response. The actor model's parameters are never updated, so the method is fully decoupled from the target LLM's weights.

## Method

ContextIF trains a policy model via GRPO to generate optimal ICL context for any given instruction. For each query, the policy produces G independent context rollouts, each scored by a composite reward: a binary format reward verifying correct XML tag ordering, plus a constraint reward (0–3) from a strong LLM judge (LLaMA3-70B-Instruct) that independently checks summary accuracy, question parallelism, and answer constraint adherence. GRPO computes group-relative advantages from these rewards without a separate critic, updating the policy with clipped importance sampling and a KL penalty. The key insight is that optimizing a model to *generate its own learning context*—deconstructing constraints and reconstructing them into coherent demonstrations—outperforms direct RL on the task itself, preserves and even enhances general capabilities (MMLU, BBH, GSM8K, HumanEval), and generalizes to constraint types withheld from training.

## Results

On the LLaMA3-8B-Instruct backbone, ContextIF-8B achieves an IFEval average of 83.35 (a +6.24 absolute gain over the base model's 77.11), a Multi-IF Turn-3 accuracy of 53.51 (+9.59 over base), a FollowBench SSR of 69.37 (+6.47), and a LiveBench score of 59.90 (+13.20). On the Mistral-7B-Instruct backbone, ContextIF-7B reaches an IFEval average of 64.51 and a Multi-IF Turn-3 of 43.43, outperforming all 7B-scale baselines. On general capability benchmarks, ContextIF-8B improves MMLU by +1.7 and BBH by +1.6 over the base model, while the SFT baseline (SPAR-SFT) degrades by −0.6 and −0.4 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ContextIF-8B outperforms all same-scale SFT/preference-learning baselines across every benchmark: it leads SPAR-8B (the strongest 8B baseline) by +0.24 on IFEval Avg, +2.19 on Multi-IF Turn-3, +0.57 on FollowBench SSR, and a decisive +10.10 on LiveBench (59.90 vs. 49.80). The gap widens against UltraIF-8B (+2.15 IFEval Avg, +8.67 Multi-IF T3, +12.40 LiveBench) and AutoIF-8B (+3.01 IFEval Avg, +10.40 Multi-IF T3, +12.80 LiveBench). Against the much larger LLaMA3-70B-Instruct, ContextIF-8B is within 0.5 points on IFEval Avg (83.35 vs. 83.89) and 0.39 on Multi-IF Turn-3, effectively closing the 8.75× parameter gap. ContextIF-8B also surpasses TULU 3 on Multi-IF Turn-3 (53.51 vs. 51.20) and QwQ-32B on the same metric (53.51 vs. 48.40), though it trails both on IFEval Avg and FollowBench. On the Mistral-7B backbone, ContextIF-7B leads SPAR-7B by +6.26 on IFEval Avg and +5.30 on Multi-IF Turn-3, confirming architecture-agnostic gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

ContextIF sits at the intersection of two well-established lines of work: In-Context Learning (Brown et al., 2020), which conditions LLMs on demonstrations without parameter updates, and Group Relative Policy Optimization (Shao et al., 2024), a critic-free RL algorithm for structured output tasks. Prior instruction-following methods (SFT, DPO, RLHF) modify model weights and suffer from catastrophic forgetting, while prior ICL optimization focuses on selecting or ordering demonstrations from static pools. ContextIF departs from both by training a separate RL policy to *generate* task-specific constraint summaries and parallel demonstrations, using a composite format-plus-semantic reward to guide synthesis rather than retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a parameter-efficient path to strong instruction-following: only 4K unlabeled queries are needed (vs. 50K+ synthesized samples for SFT baselines), the target model's weights remain frozen, and general capabilities are preserved or improved rather than degraded. The finding that a specialized 8B context generator outperforms GPT-4o at the same context-generation task suggests that task-specific RL optimization can surpass brute-force scale for auxiliary generation sub-problems. Open questions include whether the approach extends to highly compositional or multi-lingual constraint sets beyond current benchmarks, whether the judge-model dependency becomes a bottleneck at larger scale, and whether the positive transfer to general capabilities generalizes to other RL-augmented ICL settings.
