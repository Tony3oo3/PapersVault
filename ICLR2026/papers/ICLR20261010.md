## Problem

Existing LLM pruning methods are unable to surpass moderate sparsity levels (50–60%) without severe accuracy degradation, creating a perceived 'sparsity wall.' The paper argues this wall is not inherent but an artifact of the prevailing layer-wise reconstruction error minimization approach, which introduces compounding errors, restricts the search space, and optimizes a surrogate objective that does not directly preserve language modeling capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261010/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weights, a calibration set of 32,768 C4 sequences (length 2048), and a target sparsity level, and iteratively produces pruned sparse weights that directly minimize the next-token-prediction cross-entropy loss under an ℓ₀ budget. Unlike one-shot pruners that require only a small calibration set (e.g., 128 sequences), ELSA's iterative nature benefits from larger data and continues improving with more samples. The ELSA_L variant additionally stores ADMM auxiliary states (u, z) in FP8 to reduce memory by 55%, enabling pruning of 27B-parameter models on 4×H200 GPUs.

## Method

ELSA formulates LLM pruning as the sparsity-constrained problem min f(x) s.t. ‖x‖₀ ≤ k and solves it via ADMM with variable splitting into a training variable x and a sparse variable z coupled by an equality constraint. Each ADMM iteration alternates between a gradient-based x-update on the true next-token-prediction loss, a z-update that projects onto the sparse set, and a dual ascent step. The key innovation is an objective-aware projection: instead of zeroing the smallest-magnitude entries, the z-update minimizes a Fisher-weighted quadratic (using the diagonal of the Gauss-Newton/Fisher matrix, obtained for free from Adam's second-moment estimates), aligning the sparsification step with the second-order geometry of the training objective. The paper further establishes convergence to a λ-stationary point under standard smoothness and weak-convexity assumptions, and extends the analysis to the quantized ELSA_L variant with bounded iterates.

## Results

On LLaMA-2-7B at 90% sparsity, ELSA achieves 23.14 C4 perplexity, compared to 180.9 for ALPS (best prior method), 287.1 for L-ADMM, and 864.5 for SparseGPT — a 7.8× improvement over the best baseline. At 95% sparsity, ELSA maintains 28.39 C4 perplexity while Wanda+full fine-tuning degrades to 53.62 and Wanda+LoRA collapses to 143.0. ELSA also delivers up to 3.98× inference speedup and 7.80× memory reduction at 95% sparsity on LLaMA-2-7B. On LLaMA-2-13B at 90% sparsity, ELSA achieves 25.07 C4 perplexity versus 152.1 for ALPS, confirming the advantage scales with model size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELSA is competitive but not dominant at moderate sparsity (50–60%): on LLaMA-2-7B at 50%, L-ADMM (8.97) and ALPS (9.02) slightly beat ELSA (9.81) on C4 perplexity. However, the gap reverses and widens dramatically at 70% sparsity, where ELSA (14.08) outperforms ALPS (20.37) by 31% and SparseGPT (30.94) by 2.2×. At 80% and 90% sparsity, ELSA's advantage becomes order-of-magnitude: it achieves 19.56 and 23.14 C4 perplexity respectively, while the best baseline ALPS reaches 47.22 and 180.9. Among one-shot methods (Wanda, SparseGPT, Magnitude), all collapse to perplexities above 100 by 80% sparsity, whereas ELSA remains below 20. Even with retraining (Wanda+Full fine-tuning), baselines cannot close the gap at 90% (34.87 vs. 23.14) or 95% (53.62 vs. 28.39) sparsity. The paper also shows ELSA adapts to N:M semi-structured sparsity (2:4, 4:8) at roughly parity with ALPS and L-ADMM, and outperforms non-uniform allocation methods OWL and EvoPress at 70% sparsity on LLaMA-3-8B (29.09 vs. 52.32 and 33.72 C4 perplexity).

## Relation to Prior Work

ELSA departs fundamentally from the dominant paradigm in LLM pruning—sequential layer-wise reconstruction error minimization (as in SparseGPT, Wanda, ALPS, SAFE, SparseLLM, L-ADMM)—by directly solving the sparsity-constrained optimization problem with the true next-token-prediction objective via ADMM (Boyd et al., 2011). The objective-aware projection step draws on the Optimal Brain Surgeon / Gauss-Newton lineage, leveraging the Fisher information matrix already available in Adam's second-moment estimates. The paper explicitly frames the layer-wise surrogate as the source of the 'sparsity wall' at 50–60%, positioning ELSA as a principled correction rather than an incremental improvement over prior one-shot or iterative pruners.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By breaking through the 50–60% sparsity ceiling to 90%+ with only modest perplexity degradation (and stable performance even at 95–99%), ELSA challenges the field's working assumption that extreme LLM sparsity is infeasible without full retraining. The practical payoff—up to 7.8× memory reduction and 3.98× inference speedup—makes high sparsity a viable deployment strategy rather than an academic curiosity. Open questions remain: the full sparsity frontier is not yet mapped, the method requires substantially more compute and memory than one-shot baselines (7.12 GPU-hours vs. 0.16–0.25), and extensions to MoE architectures, multimodal models, and derivative-free optimizers are left as future work. The paper also raises a provocative point that the memory-efficiency argument for layer-wise surrogates may be overstated, since offloading can support full-model optimization at comparable cost.
