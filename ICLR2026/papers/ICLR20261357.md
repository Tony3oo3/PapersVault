## Problem

CLIP-style contrastive methods (InfoNCE) align only pairwise image-text samples while ignoring the global distributional mismatch between modalities, producing a persistent modality gap. Moreover, InfoNCE's alignment and uniformity terms inherently conflict in multimodal settings, driving the two objectives in opposing directions and yielding suboptimal shared-space geometry. The paper asks whether a distributional alignment objective can resolve this conflict and enable alignment beyond strictly paired data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261357/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes frozen pretrained encoders (CLIP ViT or Llama 3 8B for text; CLIP ViT for images) and a lightweight adapter or LoRA module as the trainable component. Training data can include paired image-text pairs, a single image with multiple captions, or entirely unpaired images and texts, with no requirement that the two sets share the same cardinality. For token-level alignment, all V vision tokens (V×D) and L text tokens (L×D) within each sample are treated as empirical distributions rather than reducing to a single CLS vector. Outputs are aligned embeddings in a shared space, directly consumed by pretrained decoders (Kandinsky, Karlo, SD-unclip) for 512×512 image generation, or used for cross-modal retrieval ranking.

## Method

CS-Aligner optimizes the joint objective min −I(x;y) + λ·D_CS(p(x), p(y)), where mutual information is estimated via InfoNCE on paired data and the Cauchy-Schwarz divergence is estimated non-parametrically via a Gaussian-kernel KDE over all samples in a batch. The key theoretical contribution is showing that, with a Gaussian kernel and λ=1, the combined loss decomposes into a cross-modality alignment term plus independent intra-modality uniformity terms, thereby eliminating the alignment–uniformity conflict that plagues InfoNCE alone. Because D_CS is symmetric, differentiable, and remains finite as long as the two distributions have any nonzero support overlap (unlike KL divergence), it robustly handles the small-overlap regime typical of multimodal embeddings. The distributional nature of the CS term also permits incorporating unpaired data and performing token-level alignment (treating all vision and text tokens within a sample as two distributions to be matched), capabilities unavailable to pairwise losses like InfoNCE or ℓ₂.

## Results

On text-to-image generation (MSCOCO validation), CS-Aligner trained on only 80K paired samples achieves FID 11.27 with the Karlo decoder and 10.88 with the SD-unclip decoder, outperforming large-scale diffusion models trained on 115M–2000M samples (e.g., SDv2.1: 14.51, Kandinsky: 20.48, Karlo: 20.64) and the small-scale alignment baseline Eclipse (16.53). On image-text retrieval, CS-Aligner (Ours-3M) achieves average R@1 of 88.7 (I2T) and 87.4 (T2I) across Flickr30k, Urban-1k, and DOCCI, surpassing LLM2CLIP-3M (87.2/85.4) and Long-CLIP (79.7/80.3). On 11-shot image classification, CS-Aligner reaches 80.9% average accuracy vs. 79.3% for CLIP-Adapter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CS-Aligner decisively outperforms the small-scale alignment baselines: Eclipse (FID 16.53) and IB (FID 150.52) on the Kandinsky decoder, and Eclipse (FID 23.67) on the Karlo decoder, with margins of 3.9–138 FID points. Against large-scale diffusion methods, CS-Aligner (10.88–12.62 FID) beats SDv2.1 (14.51), SD-unclipv2.1 (13.15), Wurstchen (23.60), Kandinsky (20.48), and Karlo (20.64) using 1000×–25000× less training data, though DALL-E 2 (10.65 FID, 250M data) remains slightly ahead. On retrieval, CS-Aligner (Ours-3M) consistently outperforms LLM2CLIP-3M by 1.5–2.1 R@1 points on average, Long-CLIP by 9–7 points, and vanilla CLIP by 16.5–25.3 points across all three benchmarks. On 11-shot image classification, CS-Aligner improves over CLIP-Adapter by 1.6 points on average (80.9% vs. 79.3%), with gains on all 11 datasets. The method also outperforms KL divergence (FID 23.48) and Wasserstein distance (FID 18.41) as distributional alignment metrics in ablation, confirming CS divergence's superiority for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CLIP's InfoNCE-based contrastive alignment and the Cauchy-Schwarz divergence from information-theoretic learning, combining them into a unified objective that addresses a specific theoretical gap: the alignment-uniformity conflict in InfoNCE when applied across modalities (building on Wang & Isola's decomposition). It departs from prior pairwise alignment methods (Eclipse, IB) by introducing a distributional alignment term estimated via non-parametric KDE, which enables the use of unpaired data and token-level representations that pairwise losses cannot handle. The parameter-efficient fine-tuning via adapters and LoRA on frozen pretrained encoders positions it within the recent trend of lightweight adaptation of large multimodal models rather than full retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical identification of InfoNCE's inherent alignment-uniformity conflict in multimodal settings, and the demonstration that CS divergence resolves it while remaining compatible with InfoNCE, provides a principled explanation for the persistent modality gap in CLIP-style models. The practical ability to leverage unpaired data and multiple captions for distributional alignment is valuable for real-world scenarios where perfectly paired data is scarce or expensive. Open questions include whether the O(N²) KDE estimation scales efficiently to very large batch sizes, whether the approach generalizes beyond vision-language to other modality pairs (audio, video), and whether the gains persist when the pretrained encoders themselves are weaker or from different architectures.
