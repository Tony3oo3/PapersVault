## Problem

Prior work on goal-based reading has only distinguished between a small set of pre-defined procedural reading modes (e.g., comprehension vs. skimming vs. proofreading), leaving open whether the specific semantic content of a reader's information-seeking goal — an arbitrary, text-specific question — can be decoded from their eye movements. This paper is the first to address this open-ended goal decoding problem, asking whether a single participant's gaze trajectory over a single paragraph reveals which of several text-specific questions they were trying to answer.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text paragraph (mean 109 words) together with a single participant's full eye movement trajectory over that paragraph, recorded at fixation level (word index, fixation duration, saccade direction/distance). For the discriminative goal-selection task, three candidate questions are also provided and the model outputs a label; for the generative goal-reconstruction task, the model outputs free-form question text. Eye movements are encoded either as numerical feature vectors concatenated with RoBERTa word embeddings (discriminative) or as textual tuples of (word, duration, saccade direction) prepended to the paragraph in a prompt (generative). The evaluation uses 10-fold cross-validation with three generalization regimes: new participant, new text, and new text-and-participant.

## Method

The paper introduces two task formulations and a suite of models spanning discriminative and generative paradigms. For goal selection, two reading-time-informed embedding similarity baselines are compared against adapted versions of Haller RNN (LSTM over fixation-ordered word embeddings with concatenated gaze features) and RoBERTEye-Fixations (a transformer that fuses fixation-level eye movement feature vectors, projected into the word embedding space and positionally aligned, with the text sequence and a candidate question). For goal reconstruction, Llama 3.1 and GPT-4o-mini are fine-tuned via LoRA to autoregressively generate the question from a textual prompt containing the paragraph and a fixation-by-fixation scanpath description; Gemini is additionally evaluated zero-shot and few-shot. The key finding is that RoBERTEye-Fixations achieves 49.3% three-way accuracy (vs. 33% chance) and 57.3% on the hardest same-critical-span subtask, while generative models show consistent gains over text-only baselines, confirming that eye movements carry information about the reader's specific goal beyond what the text alone provides.

## Results

On the goal selection task, RoBERTEye-Fixations achieves 49.3% three-way accuracy (chance: 33.0%), 70.9% accuracy on the Different Spans evaluation (chance: 55.3%), and 57.3% on the Same Span evaluation (chance: 49.9%), all significantly above chance (p < 0.001). The two reading-time informed embedding similarity baselines perform at chance level across all conditions. On the goal reconstruction task, DalEye-Llama, DalEye-GPT, and Gemini few-shot all outperform text-only and arbitrary-question baselines on all five evaluation measures (Question Word, UIUC Category, BLEU, BERTScore, QA accuracy) in the New Participant regime (p < 0.001), though performance drops considerably in the New Text regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoBERTEye-Fixations (adapted from Shubi et al., 2024) is the top-performing model across all three goal selection conditions, achieving 49.3% All, 70.9% Different Spans, and 57.3% Same Span accuracy. It outperforms Haller RNN (adapted from Haller et al., 2022) by 7.5 points on All (49.3 vs. 41.8), 5.3 points on Different Spans (70.9 vs. 65.6), and 5.2 points on Same Span (57.3 vs. 52.1), with all differences statistically significant. Both neural models substantially outperform the two reading-time informed embedding similarity baselines, which operate at or near chance level (33–34% All, 55% Different Spans, 50–51% Same Span), indicating that simple heuristic matching of reading times to question semantics is insufficient. A feature ablation of RoBERTEye-Fixations reveals that the model relies most heavily on the ordering of word embeddings according to the fixation sequence rather than on individual fixation-level features. On the goal reconstruction task, the generative models (DalEye-Llama, DalEye-GPT, Gemini few-shot) consistently outperform their text-only counterparts, confirming that eye movement information provides a meaningful signal beyond what can be inferred from the paragraph text alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on decoding cognitive state from eye movements in reading (Hollenstein et al., 2021b; Shubi et al., 2025), which previously addressed procedural goal decoding—distinguishing between a small set of pre-defined reading modes such as comprehension versus skimming. The key conceptual departure is shifting from procedural to semantic goal decoding: instead of classifying among a handful of text-agnostic reading strategies, the paper tackles the problem of identifying which of hundreds of arbitrary, text-specific questions a reader had in mind. Methodologically, it adapts two established discriminative architectures (Haller RNN, RoBERTEye-Fixations) to the new task and introduces generative LLM approaches (fine-tuned Llama 3.1, GPT-4o-mini, and zero/few-shot Gemini) that represent eye movements in textual form, building on the LLaVA-OneVision architecture for an embedding-based variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that eye movements over a single paragraph contain enough signal to identify a reader's specific information-seeking goal with meaningful accuracy, including the challenging case of distinguishing between two questions targeting the same text span. The cognitive interpretability analysis—showing that more goal-directed, resource-efficient reading directly improves automatic decodability—provides a scientifically grounded link between rational information-seeking theory and computational modeling. However, current accuracy levels (49.3% three-way, 57.3% binary same-span) and the single-dataset, single-language, single-paragraph scope limit immediate practical deployment; open questions include scaling to longer documents, real-time inference, generalization to L2 readers and populations with reading impairments, and whether the generative reconstruction task can reach useful quality for assistive applications.
