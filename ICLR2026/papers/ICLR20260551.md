## Problem

Global Bayesian optimization aims to minimize regret relative to the global optimum, which is intractable in large, high-dimensional spaces, yet local iterative optimizers (gradient descent, ADAM, CMA-ES) are the de facto standard for finding good solutions in such settings. Existing local BO methods (TuRBO, GIBO) only partially exploit the iterative descent structure or handle special cases, and no entropy-search formulation had previously targeted the local optimum reachable by a specific optimizer's trajectory rather than the global optimum.

## Input / Output

LES takes an initial design point x₀, a sequence of noisy zero-order evaluations y(x)=f(x)+ε, and a user-specified local optimizer O (ADAM, gradient descent, or CMA-ES) as inputs. At each iteration it produces a next query point by maximizing the LES acquisition function over a finite candidate set of L×P points drawn from discretized descent sequences (default L=250 Monte-Carlo GP samples, P=8 equally-spaced support points per sequence). The terminal output is the local optimum x* reached by O from x₀, with a probabilistic stopping rule that certifies local optimality at a user-specified tolerance ε.

## Method

LES propagates the GP posterior belief through the local optimizer: it draws L analytic GP posterior samples (via Matheron's rule / Wilson et al. 2020), runs the optimizer O on each sample from the current incumbent to obtain L descent sequences, and treats the collection as a distribution over reachable local optima. The acquisition function is the mutual information between a new observation (x, y(x)) and this descent-sequence distribution, decomposed into a closed-form predictive entropy at x minus a Monte-Carlo average of conditional entropies computed by conditioning the GP on P equally-spaced function values from each sampled sequence. This makes GIBO a strict special case (one-step gradient descent) and generalizes entropy search from the global optimum to the optimizer-defined local basin. A probabilistic stopping criterion adapted from Wilson (2024) reuses the same Monte-Carlo samples to certify that the local simple regret falls below a tolerance with high probability, eliminating the need for additional evaluations.

## Results

On high-complexity GP samples (E[p(l;50)]=0.25), LES achieves a median final objective of −10.8 at d=50, substantially outperforming logEI (−2.9), TuRBO (−7.1), and Sobol random (−3.0). At medium complexity (E[p(l;50)]=0.52), LES reaches −10.4 at d=50 versus −9.6 for logEI and −7.9 for TuRBO. In low and extremely low complexity settings, all methods converge to similar values, with LES matching or slightly leading (e.g., −8.8 vs. −8.5 for logEI at d=50, low complexity). Cumulative regret is consistently lower for LES across all complexities except d=5 and the lowest complexity, reflecting its conservative local exploration behavior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LES clearly outperforms logEI in high-complexity settings (e.g., −10.8 vs. −2.9 at d=50), where global search struggles to reduce uncertainty across the domain; the gap narrows in medium complexity (−10.4 vs. −9.6) and vanishes in low/extremely low complexity where all methods converge. Against TuRBO, LES achieves a consistent 1.5–3.7 unit advantage in high and medium complexity at d≥20 (e.g., −7.4 vs. −7.2 at d=20, high; −10.4 vs. −7.9 at d=50, medium), while the two are statistically indistinguishable in low and extremely low complexity. LES dominates Sobol random sampling across all complexities and dimensions, with the largest margins in high complexity (−10.8 vs. −3.0 at d=50). In the extended out-of-model comparison (Table 8), LES also outperforms MES (global entropy search) and HCI-GIBO (gradient-based local BO) in high and medium complexity at d≥10, confirming that the advantage is not an artifact of known hyperparameters. On application-oriented benchmarks (Rover, Mopta08, 30-d Ackley), LES matches or slightly leads TuRBO and logEI, but underperforms on highly multimodal low-dimensional tasks (5-d Ackley, Lunar Lander) where it gets trapped in local optima.

## Relation to Prior Work

LES extends the entropy search paradigm (Hennig & Schuler, 2012; Wang & Jegelka, 2017) from targeting the global optimum to targeting the local optimum reachable by a specific iterative optimizer's descent trajectory. It subsumes GIBO (Müller et al., 2021) as a special case (one-step gradient descent) and departs from trust-region local BO (TuRBO, Eriksson et al., 2019) by propagating the full GP posterior through the optimizer via Monte-Carlo sampling of descent sequences rather than restricting queries to a fixed region. The efficient GP posterior sampling via Matheron's rule (Wilson et al., 2020) and the probabilistic stopping criterion (Wilson, 2024) are adapted to make the approach computationally tractable and to provide a local regret certificate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LES provides the first principled information-theoretic framework for local BO, offering a clear theoretical justification for why local search can outperform global methods in high-complexity, high-dimensional settings. Its main limitation is the absence of any escape mechanism from local optima, making it vulnerable on highly multimodal landscapes (e.g., 5-d Ackley, Lunar Lander), and it currently lacks finite-time regret guarantees of the GP-UCB type. Open directions include globalization strategies (multi-start, adaptive switching between local and global modes), extensions to constrained/multi-fidelity/batch settings, and bridging the theoretical gap between local and global regret bounds for GP sample paths.
