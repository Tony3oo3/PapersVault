## Problem

Slot-based video object-centric learning suffers from a fundamental representational conflict: the encoder's sharp but noisy attention maps and the decoder's spatially coherent but blurry reconstruction maps create a vicious feedback loop where each module's weakness reinforces the other's. Noisy encoder features make the decoder's reconstruction task ill-posed (driving it to average and blur), while blurry decoder gradients lack the high-frequency detail needed to sharpen encoder features. This paper identifies and breaks this cycle through mutual refinement.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips resized to 336×336 (MOVi-C/E) or 518×518 (YTVIS), processed through a DINO-v2 backbone into patch-level features that feed a slot attention module with a fixed number of slots (11, 15, or 7 per dataset). Outputs include per-slot attention maps (S×T×N), decoded object masks, and reconstructed frames, with the contrastive objectives operating on projected patch-level embeddings across all T frames. The framework also generalizes to static RGB images (evaluated on MSCOCO 2017) where the temporal dimension is simply absent.

## Method

SRL introduces two ternary contrastive objectives that exploit the complementary strengths of encoder and decoder representations. The deblurring loss (L^CL-dec) partitions patches into three tiers relative to each anchor—self (positive), same-slot patches per the encoder's sharp attention labels (semi-positive), and all others (negative)—and applies a ranking contrastive loss that forces the decoder to resolve boundary ambiguities where its blurry grouping conflicts with the encoder's sharp prior. The denoising loss (L^CL-enc) mirrors this structure but uses the decoder's spatially coherent masks as semi-positives and Top-K DINO-v2 neighbors as positives, pushing spuriously grouped distant patches apart in the encoder's feature space. A slot regularization warm-up (first 10% of training) iteratively identifies the M most redundant slot pairs by cosine similarity and penalizes the less-specialized slot (measured by KL divergence from uniform attention) toward a uniform distribution, preventing slot collapse before contrastive refinement begins. Training proceeds in three stages: slot specialization (0–10%), slot stabilization (10–20%), and contrastive refinement (20–100%), with the baseline reconstruction and slot-level contrastive losses active throughout.

## Results

SRL achieves state-of-the-art FG-ARI on all three primary benchmarks: 74.3 on MOVi-C, 81.9 on MOVi-E, and 42.9 on YouTube-VIS 2021. Compared to the reproduced SlotContrast† baseline, SRL improves FG-ARI by 5.5 points on MOVi-C and 18.5 points on YouTube-VIS, while also improving mBO by 8.8 and 8.2 points respectively. On the object dynamics prediction task (Table 2), SRL+SlotFormer further outperforms SlotContrast+SlotFormer on FG-ARI (68.9 vs. 63.8 on MOVi-C; 32.2 vs. 29.2 on YTVIS), confirming that the learned representations generalize to temporal reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline SlotContrast† (reproduced), SRL wins on FG-ARI across all three datasets (74.3 vs. 70.4 on MOVi-C, 81.9 vs. 80.9 on MOVi-E, 42.9 vs. 36.2 on YTVIS) and on mBO for MOVi-C (34.5 vs. 31.7) and YTVIS (35.6 vs. 32.9). Against VideoSAUR, SRL achieves substantially higher FG-ARI (74.3 vs. 64.8 on MOVi-C; 81.9 vs. 73.9 on MOVi-E; 42.9 vs. 28.9 on YTVIS) but trails on mBO for the synthetic MOVi datasets (34.5 vs. 38.9 on MOVi-C; 29.3 vs. 35.6 on MOVi-E), which the authors attribute to VideoSAUR's motion-centric objective being well-suited to the rigid, non-deformable objects in those benchmarks. Against the original SlotContrast (non-reproduced), SRL matches or exceeds FG-ARI on MOVi-C and YTVIS but is slightly below on MOVi-E (81.9 vs. 82.9). Against older baselines SAVi and STEVE, SRL achieves large margins (e.g., +52.1 FG-ARI over SAVi on MOVi-C, +27.9 over STEVE on YTVIS), reflecting the generational gap in slot-based architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SlotContrast (Manasyan et al., 2025), adopting its DINO-v2 + slot attention + reconstruction + slot-level contrastive training pipeline as the baseline architecture. It departs from prior self-distillation approaches (SPOT, DIAS) that directly imitate teacher attention signals, instead proposing a ternary contrastive structure that stratifies mutual signals into positive, semi-positive, and negative tiers to avoid propagating noise. The broader lineage traces through the slot attention paradigm (Locatello et al., 2020) and its video extensions (SAVi, STEVE, VideoSAUR), with the key conceptual departure being the formal identification of the encoder-decoder representational conflict as a 'vicious cycle' that can be inverted into a 'virtuous cycle' through purpose-built mutual refinement objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a previously under-appreciated training dynamic in slot-based object-centric models—the recursive degradation between noisy encoder attention and blurry decoder reconstructions—and shows that explicitly modeling this discrepancy yields substantial gains, particularly on real-world video (18.5% FG-ARI over SlotContrast on YTVIS). Limitations include persistent under-fragmentation of very small objects and residual encoder noise that propagates into decoder masks, both acknowledged as open problems. The authors also suggest the synergistic refinement paradigm generalizes to other encoder-decoder architectures (e.g., GANs), though this remains speculative. The staged training protocol (regularization → stabilization → contrastive refinement) is a practical recipe that could be adopted by other slot-based methods.
