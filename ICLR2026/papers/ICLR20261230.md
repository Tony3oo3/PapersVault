## Problem

Restormer-style channel-wise attention, the de-facto backbone for high-resolution image restoration, harbors two overlooked limitations that become critical in all-in-one settings: the value path remains purely linear (constraining outputs to the span of inputs) and there is no explicit global slot to encode degradation context. While tolerable for single-task restoration with a fixed inverse function, these bottlenecks fundamentally limit a model's ability to approximate a family of heterogeneous inverse mappings across unknown and mixed degradations.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single degraded image (natural RGB or medical) without any degradation label, prompt, or auxiliary modality, and produces a restored image of the same spatial size. Training uses 128×128 random crops with L1 and Fourier losses, and evaluation spans six benchmark suites covering 3-degradation, 5-degradation, composited (up to triple), adverse weather, real-world WeatherBench, and medical all-in-one settings. The 4-level U-shaped encoder-decoder scales from 256² to 1024² inputs with near-linear O(HW) latency growth.

## Method

ExDA introduces two minimal, backbone-agnostic primitives into Restormer-style channel-wise attention. First, a residual nonlinear value transform (V′ = V + g_θ(V), where g_θ is a lightweight Conv₁×₁ → DWConv₃×₃ → GELU → Conv₁×₁ block) is applied pre-aggregation, upgrading attention from a linear feature selector to a nonlinear feature transformer and provably expanding the realizable function family beyond the input span. Second, a Global Spatial Token (GST) module generates content-adaptive spatial attention maps via stride-s compression and learnable multi-head projection, which are concatenated with Q, K, and V to provide explicit degradation-aware slots that naturally specialize during training without any degradation supervision. The overall architecture is a simple 4-level U-shaped encoder-decoder with Mixed Degradation Attention Blocks at each level, deliberately avoiding auxiliary prompts, language models, or multi-stage strategies. Training is end-to-end supervised with L1 + Fourier loss, and the design is validated across Tiny (6M), Small (10M), and Base (22M) parameter scales with consistent gains over strong baselines.

## Results

On the 3-degradation All-in-One benchmark (dehazing, deraining, denoising), ExDA achieves an average PSNR/SSIM of 32.96/0.921, outperforming MoCE-IR (32.73/0.917) by 0.23 dB with 3M fewer parameters (22M vs. 25M). On the 5-degradation setting, ExDA reaches 30.83/0.919 average PSNR/SSIM, surpassing the next-best DFPIR (30.64/0.913) and MoCE-IR (30.58/0.919). On the composited CDD11 benchmark, ExDA achieves 29.97/0.891 average, a 0.92 dB gain over MoCE-IR (29.05/0.881). On the real-world WeatherBench, ExDA attains 29.68/0.851 average PSNR/SSIM, leading AdaIR (28.80/0.838) by 0.88 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExDA consistently outperforms all 40 compared baselines across six All-in-One benchmarks. On the 3-degradation setting, ExDA (22M params) beats MoCE-IR (25M) by 0.23 dB and AdaIR (29M) by 0.27 dB in average PSNR, while also surpassing prompt-based methods like PromptIR (36M) by 0.90 dB and vision-language methods like DFPIR (31M) by 0.08 dB. On the 5-degradation setting, ExDA leads MoCE-IR by 0.25 dB and DFPIR by 0.19 dB, and notably outperforms the much larger UniProcessor (1002M) by a wide margin. On composited degradations (CDD11), ExDA achieves a 0.92 dB advantage over MoCE-IR and 1.50 dB over OneRestore, demonstrating particular strength in mixed-corruption scenarios. On adverse weather removal, ExDA surpasses Histoformer (ECCV'24) by 0.24 dB and GridFormer (IJCV'24) by 0.56 dB in average PSNR. On the real-world WeatherBench, ExDA leads AdaIR by 0.88 dB and PromptIR by 1.82 dB. On medical restoration, ExDA marginally edges out AMIR (MICCAI'24) by 0.02 dB in average PSNR while matching its SSIM, confirming cross-domain generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Restormer's channel-wise attention (MDTA) and gated-dconv FFN, identifying two structural limitations—linear value aggregation and the absence of explicit global slots—that become critical in all-in-one restoration but are masked in single-task settings. It departs from the dominant all-in-one IR paradigm (AirNet, PromptIR, MoCE-IR, AdaIR) that adds auxiliary modules such as learned prompts, contrastive degradation representations, or meta-learning pipelines, arguing instead that the backbone attention operator itself is the bottleneck. The nonlinear value transform draws on the broader literature of nonlinear attention (Katharopoulos et al., Shazeer's GLU variants) but applies it specifically to the value path in channel-wise attention, while the Global Spatial Token repurposes the discarded CLS-token concept from high-level ViTs into a degradation-aware slot for low-level vision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's core message—that a simple encoder-decoder with two minimal attention modifications can match or exceed methods relying on prompts, language models, or multi-stage strategies—offers a practical and philosophically clean alternative for all-in-one IR practitioners. The diagnostic analyses (t-SNE/UMAP separability, spectral statistics, attention visualization) provide interpretable evidence that the global token genuinely captures degradation context rather than acting as a generic capacity boost. Open questions include whether these principles transfer to non-Transformer backbones (Mamba, diffusion-based restorers), whether the gains persist at much larger scales or with more degradation types, and whether the expressivity argument generalizes beyond the Restormer-specific GDFN architecture where one FFN branch is linear.
