## Problem

Existing multi-state protein design methods (ProteinMPNN-MSD, Multi-state ESMIF, ProteinGenerator) rely on post-hoc logit averaging of independent single-state predictions, which biases toward conformations with strongly peaked logits and achieves poor experimental success rates compared to single-state design. No prior model has been explicitly trained to generate sequences compatible with multiple conformations through joint learning across conformational ensembles, and the field lacks both a dedicated multi-conformational training dataset and a rigorous benchmark for evaluating multi-state refoldability.

## Input / Output

The model takes a set of m (typically 2) sequence-aligned protein backbone conformations as 3D coordinate graphs, optionally including binding partner chains from the full PDB entry. Conformations may have heterogeneous sequences due to alignment gaps and unresolved residues, which are handled via masking and zeroed embeddings during pooling. The output is a single amino acid sequence generated autoregressively that is simultaneously compatible with all input conformations, evaluated for refoldability using template-based AlphaFold 3 with decoy normalization.

## Method

DynamicMPNN learns the joint conditional distribution p(Y | X_1, …, X_m) directly via autoregressive factorization, rather than aggregating independent single-state logits. Each conformation is encoded independently by an 8-layer SE(3)-equivariant Geometric Vector Perceptron GNN (k=32 sparse edges), then pooled across conformations using Deep Set averaging (or a Deep Symmetric Set cross-attention module for richer inter-conformation interaction). A key architectural contribution is the handling of heterogeneous sequences across conformations: gap positions are masked from message passing and zeroed during pooling, preserving structural context while accommodating varying lengths and missing residues. The model is trained on 46,033 CoDNaS clusters (≥95% sequence identity) and 46,924 PDB80 clusters (≥80% identity) with TM-score-weighted pair sampling to bias toward larger conformational changes, and evaluated on 96 metamorphic, hinge, and transporter proteins using template-based AF3 refoldability with decoy normalization and BioEmu ensemble sampling.

## Results

On the multi-state protein design benchmark (n=61, where direct comparison with ProteinMPNN MSD is possible), DynamicMPNN (Combined Training) achieves a decoy-normalized RMSD of 0.129 and TM-score of 6.482, compared to ProteinMPNN MSD's 0.187 and 6.054 respectively — a 31% improvement in decoy-normalized RMSD. On the full benchmark (n=96), the best DynamicMPNN variant (Combined Pretraining + Multi Finetuning) reaches 42.7% sequence recovery versus 38.0% for ProteinMPNN MSD, a 12% relative gain. Template-based AF3 refoldability (pLDDT 82.22, raw RMSD 2.27 Å, TM-score 0.849) further confirms that multi-state trained models substantially outperform single-state-only training (pLDDT 69.35, RMSD 8.00 Å, TM-score 0.623).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is ProteinMPNN Multi-State Design (MSD), which aggregates independent single-state logit predictions via arithmetic mean. DynamicMPNN (Combined Training) outperforms ProteinMPNN MSD on every reported metric: decoy-normalized RMSD (0.129 vs. 0.187, a 31% reduction), decoy-normalized TM-score (6.482 vs. 6.054, an 8% gain), raw RMSD (2.27 vs. 3.31 Å), and sequence recovery (42.7% vs. 38.0% on the full n=96 benchmark, a 12% relative improvement). The advantage is most pronounced for fold-switching (metamorphic) proteins, where DynamicMPNN's explicit multi-state encoding captures complex tertiary and secondary structure rearrangements that logit-averaging cannot. Notably, ProteinMPNN's training data includes proteins within 30% sequence similarity of 91 out of 96 benchmark proteins, making the margin of improvement particularly significant. The paper acknowledges this as a potential confound and leaves rigorous retraining of ProteinMPNN on a clean split for future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynamicMPNN adapts the multi-state GNN architecture from gRNAde (Joshi et al., 2025), a geometric deep learning model for RNA inverse folding, to the protein design setting, employing SE(3)-equivariant Geometric Vector Perceptron layers and autoregressive decoding. It departs fundamentally from prior multi-state protein design methods (ProteinMPNN-MSD, Multi-state ESM-IF, ProteinGenerator), which aggregate independent single-state logit predictions post-hoc, by instead learning the joint conditional distribution p(Y|X₁,…,Xₘ) through shared latent-space encoding of conformational ensembles. The evaluation framework extends Roney & Ovchinnikov's (2022) template-based AlphaFold scoring to the multi-state setting with a novel decoy-normalization strategy, and the training data leverages CoDNaS and a new PDB80 multi-conformational dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first explicit multi-state inverse folding model for proteins, and the 31% decoy-normalized RMSD improvement over ProteinMPNN-MSD demonstrates that joint conformational learning captures sequence constraints that post-hoc logit averaging misses. However, the comparison is somewhat confounded by ProteinMPNN's training data overlapping with 91 of 96 benchmark proteins, and no experimental validation is provided. Open questions include whether a one-size-fits-all multi-state model generalizes across conformational change classes (metamorphic vs. hinge vs. transporter), whether specialized models per thermodynamic complexity would be more effective, and whether the in silico gains translate to experimental success rates comparable to single-state design.
