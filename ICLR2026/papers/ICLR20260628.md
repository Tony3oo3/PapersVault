## Problem

Existing interaction prediction methods for biological networks rely on single-layer representations that discard relational heterogeneity across interaction types, have limited capacity to integrate sequence features with network topology, and cannot predict interactions for entirely unseen entities with no prior neighborhood data. No prior framework systematically addresses zero-shot multiplex interaction prediction, where novel biochemical entities must be classified without any observed connectivity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260628/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multiplex biological network (L layers, each encoding a distinct interaction type, connected via an inter-layer graph) along with raw biological sequences—SMILES strings for drugs/metabolites, nucleotide sequences for genes, and amino acid sequences for proteins—which are encoded by domain-specific LLMs (ChemBERTa, DNABERT-2, ESM-2). Output is a multi-label vector of interaction-type probabilities per entity pair, since a pair may simultaneously exhibit multiple interaction types. In the zero-shot setting, the student model receives only sequence embeddings (no graph topology) for test entities, making predictions purely from intrinsic biochemical features.

## Method

CAZI-MBN integrates four components: (1) a Unified Graph Tokenizer (UGT) that constructs a smoothed high-order supra-adjacency matrix and extracts topology-aware node embeddings via SVD projection; (2) a Context-Aware Enhancement (CAE) module that applies node-level and layer-level inter-layer attention within Graph Transformers, combined with a contrastive learning objective where a discriminator distinguishes real edges from negatively-sampled perturbed edges and a consensus regularizer enforces cross-layer embedding agreement; (3) a Mixture of Experts head for multi-label prediction that adaptively routes through interaction-type-specific experts; and (4) a teacher-student knowledge distillation pipeline where the topology-aware teacher (using both sequence and graph features) is distilled into a topology-agnostic student (sequence-only) via MSE on 32-dimensional latent representations, enabling zero-shot inference for entities with no observed neighborhood.

## Results

CAZI-MBN achieves the highest AUROC and AUPRC across all five multiplex biological networks in both transductive and zero-shot settings, with improvements of 3.1–20.4% over the strongest baseline. In the transductive setting, AUROC ranges from 0.752 (MetaConserve) to 0.905 (TRRUST); in zero-shot, from 0.671 (DGIdb) to 0.899 (TRRUST). The largest absolute gain is on DGIdb, where CAZI-MBN reaches 0.715 AUROC (transductive) and 0.671 (zero-shot) versus 0.551 and 0.524 for the best multiplex baseline (HDMI/DMGI). On ChEMBL, CAZI-MBN attains 0.812/0.791 AUROC (T/ZS) with AUPRC of 0.863/0.839, substantially ahead of HDMI (0.663/0.648) and DMGI (0.661/0.652).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAZI-MBN outperforms all 18 baselines across all five datasets in both transductive and zero-shot settings on AUROC and AUPRC. Sequence-based models (XGB, MLP) and single-graph GNNs (GCN, GraphSAGE, Graph Transformer, DGI) are consistently the weakest, with AUROC values often near or below 0.55 on DGIdb, indicating they cannot exploit multiplex structure. Multiplex-aware baselines (MultiplexSAGE, DMGI, HDMI, R-GCN, R-GAT) are the strongest competitors; HDMI is the best single baseline on four of five datasets in transductive mode (e.g., 0.878 AUROC on TRRUST vs. CAZI-MBN's 0.905), while DMGI leads in zero-shot on DGIdb (0.524 vs. CAZI-MBN's 0.671). Domain-specific models (CoSMIG, DGCL, xCAPT5, DrugBAN, GENER) are competitive in their respective biological contexts—xCAPT5 reaches 0.785 AUROC zero-shot on PINNACLE, the closest margin to CAZI-MBN (0.812)—but still trail CAZI-MBN by 2.7–14.7% AUROC. The gap is most pronounced on DGIdb (16.4% AUROC improvement over HDMI in transductive) and smallest on TRRUST (2.7% over HDMI), suggesting CAZI-MBN's multiplex and sequence-integration advantages are most impactful when interaction types are semantically diverse and the network is sparser.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAZI-MBN sits at the intersection of three research threads: multiplex graph representation learning (HDMI, DMGI, MultiplexSAGE, R-GCN/R-GAT), domain-specific biological LLMs (ChemBERTa, DNABERT-2, ESM-2), and knowledge distillation (Hinton, 2015). It departs from prior multiplex methods by injecting sequence-level semantics via pre-trained LLMs and by using a topology-aware SVD-based tokenizer (UGT) over the supra-adjacency matrix rather than relying solely on message-passing. The contrastive discriminator in the CAE module is adapted from DGI's mutual-information framework, while the teacher-student distillation is repurposed from its traditional model-compression role to enable zero-shot generalization for unseen entities—a use not previously explored in biological network prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework explicitly designed for zero-shot multiplex interaction prediction, addressing a practical gap in drug discovery where novel compounds or genes enter the system with no known neighborhood. The curation of five high-quality MBN benchmarks (DGIdb, ChEMBL, PINNACLE, MetaConserve, TRRUST) fills a notable absence of standardized evaluation resources in this area. Key limitations include the absence of 3D structural information (genomic, protein, or compound), which constrains fine-grained biological modeling, and the observation that while AUROC/AUPRC gains are large, exact-match metrics (HS, SA) can lag behind strong baselines on some datasets, suggesting calibration challenges for multi-label outputs. Open questions include whether the framework extends to cross-species networks and whether integrating structural data would close the remaining performance gaps.
