## Problem

Atom-based molecular graph generative models face quadratic computational growth with molecule size, struggle with the inherent sparsity of chemical bonds, and often produce invalid connectivity or miss topological features like rings. Existing fragment-based approaches mitigate these issues but rely on small fixed fragment vocabularies or dataset-specific automated fragmentation, severely limiting chemical space coverage and preventing integration of domain knowledge. No prior method combines fragment-level generation with a large, flexible fragment library while maintaining tractable computational cost.

## Input / Output

At generation time, the model starts from a joint prior: mask tokens for fragment-type nodes, a uniform Bernoulli prior for binary edges, and a standard Gaussian for the continuous latent vector z. Optionally, a target property value (e.g., QED, docking score) conditions both the fragment bag sampling and the transition kernel via classifier guidance. The output is a full atom-level molecular graph, reconstructed losslessly from the generated fragment-level graph through the coarse-to-fine decoder, which predicts junction-atom connectivity scores and applies the Blossom algorithm to select valid atom-level bonds.

## Method

FragFM operates in two stages: (1) a fragment-level discrete flow matching (DFM) process, realized as a continuous-time Markov chain, jointly generates fragment-type nodes, binary edges, and a continuous latent vector z; (2) a coarse-to-fine autoencoder reconstructs the atom-level graph from the fragment-level graph and z. The key efficiency mechanism is a stochastic fragment bag strategy: rather than modeling the CTMC over the full fragment vocabulary |F|, the model samples a subset B of size N per step and trains with an Info-NCE loss over B, so computational cost scales with N rather than |F|. A GNN-based fragment embedding module enables generalization to unseen fragments beyond the training vocabulary. The autoencoder's decoder predicts continuous edge scores between junction atoms of neighboring fragments and discretizes them via the Blossom maximum-weight matching algorithm, achieving >99% bond-level reconstruction accuracy. For conditional generation, the framework introduces a second guidance channel (λ_B) that re-weights fragment bag sampling by a property predictor, complementing the standard classifier guidance (λ_X) on the transition kernel.

## Results

On the new NPGen benchmark (658K natural product molecules, avg. 35 heavy atoms), FragFM achieves 98.0% validity, 95.4% novelty, and an FCD of 1.34, substantially outperforming atom-based models DiGress (FCD 2.05, 85.4% validity) and DeFoG (FCD 4.46, 85.9% validity). On MOSES, FragFM attains 99.8% validity with an FCD of 0.58, the best among all baselines (next best: SAFE-GPT at 0.71). FragFM also maintains >95% validity and FCD < 1.0 even with reduced denoising steps, whereas atom-based baselines degrade sharply.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FragFM outperforms all atom-based one-shot baselines (DiGress, DeFoG, DisCo, Cometh, MolHF, GraphAF) on validity and FCD across both MOSES and NPGen; on NPGen, the gap is most dramatic, with FragFM achieving 98.0% validity vs. 85.4% (DiGress) and 85.9% (DeFoG), and FCD 1.34 vs. 2.05 and 4.46 respectively. Against the fragment-based JT-VAE, FragFM trades a small validity margin (98.0% vs. 100.0%) for a substantially lower FCD (1.34 vs. 4.07 on NPGen; 0.58 vs. 1.00 on MOSES) and better NP-specific KL divergences. SAFE-GPT achieves the lowest FCD on NPGen (0.15) but at the cost of only 73.5% novelty, whereas FragFM maintains 95.4% novelty while still achieving FCD 1.34. On MOSES, FragFM's FCD of 0.58 beats all baselines including SAFE-GPT (0.71) and DiGress (1.19), while its SNN score (0.56) is the highest among all methods. In conditional generation (JAK2 docking on ZINC250k), FragFM with fragment-bag guidance (λ_B) shifts the docking score distribution toward the target while preserving ~100% validity, whereas DiGress suffers substantial validity loss and shows minimal distributional shift. FragFM also samples ~5× faster than DiGress and remains robust with fewer denoising steps, where atom-based baselines degrade in both validity and FCD.

## Relation to Prior Work

FragFM sits at the intersection of two established lines: discrete flow matching for structured data (Campbell et al., 2024; Qin et al., 2024) and fragment-based molecular generation rooted in medicinal chemistry (Jin et al., 2018; Degen et al., 2008). Prior fragment-based generative models (JT-VAE, HierVAE, Levy & Rector-Brooks 2023) were constrained to small fixed vocabularies or dataset-specific tokenizers, while atom-based diffusion/flow models (DiGress, DeFoG) face quadratic edge-scaling and sparsity issues on large molecules. The key departure is the stochastic fragment bag strategy with Info-NCE, which decouples computational cost from fragment vocabulary size, combined with a coarse-to-fine autoencoder (Blossom-based decoding) that enables lossless atom-level reconstruction from the fragment graph. The classifier-guidance formulation extends Vignac et al. (2023) to the bag-conditioned setting, introducing an additional λ_B knob for property steering that has no atom-level analogue.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NPGen benchmark fills a genuine gap: existing molecular generation benchmarks (MOSES, GuacaMol) are saturated and composed of small, structurally simple molecules that do not stress-test a model's ability to capture the ring complexity, heteroatom density, and biosynthetic diversity of natural products—a class that underpins roughly one-third of approved drugs. The stochastic fragment bag offers a practical recipe for scaling fragment-based generation to large, domain-informed vocabularies without the combinatorial explosion that has limited prior approaches. Open questions include how well the framework generalizes to truly novel fragment types absent from training, whether the BRICS/RECAP/rBRICS choice interacts with downstream synthesizability in ways not captured by current metrics, and whether the bag-conditioned guidance can be extended to multi-objective or constrained optimization settings common in lead optimization.
