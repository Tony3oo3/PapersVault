## Problem

Process Reward Models (PRMs) achieve strong performance on mathematical reasoning but generalize poorly to non-mathematical domains because they are trained to verify isolated, domain-specific step correctness rather than modeling the logical flow between reasoning steps. This limits their utility for multi-domain test-time scaling, where reasoning styles range from formal symbolic derivations to nuanced argumentation in the humanities.

## Input / Output

Instead of the conventional cumulative-prefix input (Q ⊕ S₁ ⊕ … ⊕ Sᵢ), ContextPRM consumes each step as a context pair: the question Q concatenated with a formatted pair (Sᵢ₋₁, Sᵢ) marked by special tokens, producing k independent training samples from a single k-step CoT. The model outputs a binary coherence score (via '+'/'-' token logits) at the final token of the current step, which is then aggregated (min-aggregation) across steps to produce a chain-level score used for Weighted Majority Voting or Best-of-N reranking over 128 sampled CoTs.

## Method

ContextPRM introduces a context-aware training paradigm that replaces the standard cumulative-prefix PRM loss with a per-step context-pair formulation: each step Sᵢ is paired with its immediate predecessor Sᵢ₋₁ (or the question Q for the first step), and the model is trained via cross-entropy to predict whether the transition is logically coherent. This is paired with a new annotation standard that labels each context pair as Good, Okay, or Bad (with subcategories: Incorrect, Misinterpretation, Logical Fallacy, Misdirection), shifting supervision from absolute factual correctness to contextual logical validity. The model is fine-tuned with LoRA (r=16, α=32) on Llama-PRM800K using the VersaPRM multi-domain training data with re-annotated labels. The ablation shows a strong synergy: the context-aware training alone yields modest gains, the improved labels alone yield minimal gains, but their combination produces a 4.3% accuracy improvement in non-math-adjacent domains over VersaPRM, with a 42.82% modification rate in first-error-step identification demonstrating the annotation's impact.

## Results

On MMLU-Pro-CoT-Eval (2,063 questions across 13 domains, 128 CoTs per question), ContextPRM achieves a 5.4% average accuracy improvement over the Majority Voting baseline under Weighted Majority Voting (WMV) across all domains, and a 6.5% improvement in non-math-adjacent domains (law, history, philosophy, etc.). Under Best-of-N (BoN) sampling, ContextPRM gains 6.3% over the MV baseline in non-math-adjacent domains. These gains substantially exceed the 2.2% improvement from VersaPRM and the ~0.5% from mathematics-focused PRMs (Math-Shepherd, Qwen2.5-Math-PRM, RLHFlow-Deepseek).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ContextPRM decisively outperforms VersaPRM (the prior multi-domain SOTA) in non-math-adjacent domains, achieving 6.5% improvement over MV under WMV versus VersaPRM's 2.7%, a gap of roughly 3.8 percentage points. Against the three mathematics-focused PRMs (Math-Shepherd, Qwen2.5-Math-PRM, RLHFlow-Deepseek), ContextPRM's advantage is even more pronounced, as those models yield only ~0.5% gains in non-math domains. In math-adjacent domains (chemistry, CS, engineering, physics), ContextPRM shows a modest 2.2% decrease relative to VersaPRM, reflecting a deliberate trade-off toward cross-domain generalization; however, it still significantly outperforms its base model Llama-PRM800K and maintains competitive absolute accuracy. Under BoN sampling, ContextPRM's 6.3% non-math gain confirms the result is not an artifact of the WMV aggregation strategy. The ablation further shows that neither the context-aware training method alone (+1.07% non-math) nor the context-coherence labeling alone (+0.84% non-math) matches the full model's 4.3% gain over VersaPRM, confirming a strong synergy between the two components.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PRM training paradigm established by Lightman et al. (ICLR 2023), which uses cumulative-prefix binary classification over CoT steps, by replacing the full-history input with a local context pair (previous step + current step) and redefining the supervision signal from step correctness to inter-step logical coherence. It builds directly on VersaPRM (ICML 2025) for multi-domain data generation and evaluation infrastructure, but departs from it by arguing that the training methodology—not just the data—is the bottleneck for cross-domain generalization. The key conceptual shift is from verifying domain-specific factual correctness to modeling domain-agnostic logical flow, operationalized through a new annotation taxonomy (Good/Okay/Bad with subcategories like Logical Fallacy, Misdirection) and a corresponding context-aware loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, well-ablated recipe for making PRMs generalize beyond mathematics, which has been a persistent gap in the test-time scaling literature. The finding that a model fine-tuned on a single logic-intensive domain (e.g., Philosophy) can outperform the full multi-domain VersaPRM suggests that logical coherence is a more transferable signal than domain-specific knowledge, with implications for data-efficient PRM training. Limitations include evaluation on a single benchmark (MMLU-Pro) with a single 8B base model, a 2.2% math-domain trade-off, and reliance on GPT-4o-mini for annotation validation. Open questions include whether the context-pair formulation scales to larger models, whether the annotation standard can be fully automated, and whether the approach transfers to non-CoT reasoning formats.
