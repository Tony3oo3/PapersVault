## Problem

Existing decision-focused learning (DFL) methods rely on deterministic point predictions, which cannot capture the complex, multi-modal uncertainty inherent in real-world decision parameters. While stochastic DFL variants using Gaussian or normalizing-flow predictors have emerged, no prior work has integrated diffusion models—capable of representing arbitrarily complex distributions—into an end-to-end DFL pipeline, largely because the sequential diffusion sampling process makes gradient backpropagation through the generative chain prohibitively expensive in memory and computation.

## Input / Output

The method takes tabular contextual features x (e.g., 150-dimensional historical load/temperature vectors for power scheduling, or 28-dimensional return/volume features for portfolio tasks) along with the known optimization problem structure (constraint matrices G, A and vectors h, b). It produces an optimal decision z (production quantities, 24-hour generation schedule, or portfolio weights) by solving a stochastic optimization whose objective is an expectation over samples drawn from a conditional diffusion model P_θ(y|x). The diffusion model itself takes [y_t, t, x] at each denoising step and outputs a predicted noise vector, with the full reverse process generating M Monte Carlo samples of the uncertain parameter y.

## Method

The core contribution is a conditional diffusion model trained end-to-end to minimize downstream decision cost via KKT implicit differentiation, with two gradient estimators for the distributional term. The reparameterization estimator backpropagates through all T diffusion steps, expressing the sample as a deterministic function R(ε, θ|x) of base noise. The proposed score function estimator instead applies the log-derivative trick to rewrite the gradient as an expectation involving ∇_θ log P_θ(y|x), then approximates this intractable score with the gradient of the conditional ELBO evaluated on a small subset of k≪T forward-noising timesteps. A theoretical bound (Proposition 5.1) shows the ELBO gradient error is controlled by the KL divergence between the forward and reverse diffusion processes. To reduce variance from the small timestep subset, an importance sampling strategy assigns timestep selection probabilities proportional to gradient magnitude, and the final gradient is computed as a weighted-ELBO loss where the KKT-derived importance weight is detached and only the ELBO is differentiated, reducing GPU memory from ~60 GB to 0.13 GB.

## Results

Across three tasks (synthetic product allocation, 24-hour power scheduling, and S&P 500 portfolio optimization), Diffusion-DFL (both reparameterization and score-function variants) achieves the best decision quality in all settings. On the stock portfolio task, Diffusion-DFL yields ~4.0–4.2% average return versus 1.74% for the best offline bandit baseline and 0.07% for deterministic DFL. On power scheduling, the score-function variant achieves a task loss of 3.171±0.02, outperforming the best Gaussian DFL baseline (3.724±0.05) by ~15%. The score-function estimator matches reparameterization performance (within 0.02 test loss) while reducing GPU memory from 60.75 GB to 0.13 GB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two-stage predict-then-optimize baselines (Elmachtoub & Grigas, 2022), Diffusion-DFL wins decisively on all three tasks: on the synthetic task, 0.362 vs. 0.393 (Diffusion TS); on power scheduling, 3.152 vs. 7.901; and on the portfolio task, 4.17% vs. 0.13% return. Against the offline contextual bandit baseline (Brandfonbrener et al., 2021), Diffusion-DFL also dominates: 0.362 vs. 0.568 (synthetic), 3.152 vs. 4.440 (power), and 4.17% vs. 1.74% (portfolio), with the gap widening as problem dimensionality increases. Against deterministic DFL (Donti et al., 2017), the stochastic diffusion approach is substantially better, particularly on the portfolio task where deterministic DFL yields only 0.07% return versus 4.17% for Diffusion-DFL, because the deterministic policy class cannot represent the multi-modal optimal decision. The score-function variant matches reparameterization within 0.02 test loss on all tasks while using ~470× less GPU memory (0.13 GB vs. 60.75 GB), making it the practical choice for large-scale problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decision-focused learning (DFL) framework of Donti et al. (2017) and Wilder et al. (2019) from deterministic point predictions to stochastic predictions using conditional diffusion models (Tashiro et al., 2021; Ho et al., 2020) as the uncertainty predictor. It departs from prior stochastic DFL work (Wang et al., 2025; Silvestri et al., 2026) in two key ways: (1) diffusion models replace simpler parametric or normalizing-flow predictors to capture multi-modal distributions, and (2) the score function gradient is approximated via the ELBO gradient rather than requiring a closed-form score, which is infeasible for diffusion models. The KKT implicit differentiation machinery (Amos & Kolter, 2017) and the log-derivative trick (Mohamed et al., 2020) are repurposed to handle the nested stochastic optimization, while the importance-sampling variance reduction borrows from Improved DDPM (Nichol & Dhariwal, 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to integrate diffusion models into an end-to-end DFL pipeline, opening a path to decision-making under complex, multi-modal uncertainty that Gaussian or flow-based predictors cannot represent. The practical impact is significant: the score function estimator reduces GPU memory from ~61 GB to ~0.13 GB while preserving decision quality, making diffusion-based DFL feasible on standard hardware. Open questions include scalability to very high-dimensional or discrete decision spaces, handling non-convex optimization layers where KKT conditions may not be well-defined, and whether the ELBO-gradient surrogate remains tight when the diffusion model is poorly trained (large KL gap). The approach also raises the question of whether the added expressiveness of diffusion models over Gaussian predictors yields consistent gains across all problem structures or primarily in multi-modal settings.
