## Problem

Existing protein benchmarks predominantly target protein-level (global) function prediction, assigning a single label to an entire protein or pair. However, biological functions are governed by specific subregions—residues, motifs, domains—rather than the whole molecule, and global labels can obscure mechanistic details and encourage models to rely on biologically implausible features. VENUSX addresses this gap as the first large-scale benchmark for fine-grained, intra-protein functional understanding across residue, fragment, and domain levels.

## Input / Output

Inputs are full-length protein sequences (truncated to 1022 residues) paired with AlphaFold 2 predicted 3D structures, or isolated functional fragments (capped at 128 residues for Act/BindI/Evo/Motif, 512 for Dom). Outputs span three granularities: per-residue binary labels (functional vs. non-functional), per-fragment multi-class InterPro family assignments (up to 13,459 classes for Dom), and pairwise similarity scores computed via cosine similarity of embeddings or alignment E-values/TM-scores. The benchmark comprises 56 datasets across 7 annotation targets (Act, BindI, BindB, Evo, Motif, Dom, Epi) with mix-family and cross-family splits at 50%, 70%, and 90% sequence identity thresholds.

## Method

VENUSX curates over 878,000 high-confidence samples from InterPro, BioLiP, and SAbDab, applying MMseqs2 clustering at three identity thresholds to construct non-redundant train/validation/test partitions (8:1:1) under both random (mix-family) and family-disjoint (cross-family) strategies. The evaluation protocol uses frozen pretrained encoders (ESM2, ProtBERT, ANKH, SaProt, ProtSSN) with lightweight linear or mean-pooling heads for supervised tasks, a from-scratch GVP-GNN for structure-only baselines, and zero-shot embedding comparison or classical aligners (FOLDSEEK, TM-ALIGN, BLAST) for pairwise scoring. Key design choices include distance-based binding-site labeling (Van der Waals + 0.5 Å margin), 10 Å Cα-distance epitope definition, and multi-label fragment classification reflecting overlapping InterPro annotations. The benchmark reveals that sequence-structure hybrids generalize substantially better on cross-family splits than sequence-only models, while epitope prediction remains extremely challenging (AUPR < 0.3 for all models), and that strong protein-level performance does not transfer to fine-grained residue or fragment tasks.

## Results

On in-distribution (mix-family) splits, ANKH-BASE achieves the highest AUPR on 7 of 15 InterPro residue-level tasks, peaking at 0.960 on Act_MP50, while ESM2-T33 dominates BindB and Epi mixes (0.535 and 0.290 AUPR at MP90). On cross-family (out-of-distribution) splits, SAPROT-650M consistently achieves the best or second-best AUPR, with a notable +5.6% advantage over PROTBERT on Dom (0.564 vs. 0.508). Performance drops dramatically under cross-family evaluation: Act and BindI AUPR plummets by 70–80%, while Dom drops by <10%. Epitope prediction remains the hardest task, with no model exceeding 0.308 AUPR across all sequence identity levels. On pairwise similarity scoring, FOLDSEEK achieves near-perfect AUC (99.0% on Evo_P50), while BLAST trails by >40% on every task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among sequence-only models, ANKH-BASE is the strongest in-distribution performer (best AUPR on 7/15 InterPro mix tasks), while ESM2-T33 and ESM2-T30 are competitive but generally 1–3% lower; PROTBERT consistently trails both by 5–10% AUPR. On cross-family splits, all sequence-only models degrade severely (e.g., ANKH drops from 0.960 to 0.166 on Act), whereas sequence-structure models SAPROT-650M and PROTSSN retain substantially higher scores (0.185 and 0.156, respectively), confirming that structural inductive bias aids out-of-distribution generalization. GVP-GNN (structure-only, trained from scratch) is highly task-dependent: it matches or exceeds sequence models on Act_MP50 (0.898) but collapses on BindI_Cross (0.040) and Epi (0.118–0.196). For pairwise similarity scoring, FOLDSEEK (3Di/3Di-AA) is the gold standard, reaching 99.0% AUC on Evo_P50 and topping 3/8 settings; TM-ALIGN is competitive where evaluated (94.6% on Act_F50) but is computationally prohibitive at scale. Large encoder-decoder models (PROT T5, ANKH) close much of the gap to alignment methods on pairwise tasks (e.g., PROT T5 reaches 98.5% on BindI_F50), while TM-VEC and SAPROT-650M achieve top-2 rankings in 5/10 and 1/10 cells respectively. BLAST, the classical sequence-alignment baseline, is the weakest performer across all pairwise tasks, trailing FOLDSEEK by 20–45% AUC and confirming that sequence similarity alone is insufficient for fine-grained functional matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

VENUSX carves new ground by reframing protein functional evaluation from the protein level (the dominant paradigm in benchmarks like TAPE, PEER, ProteinGLUE, and ProteinBench) to the sub-protein level, where the unit of prediction is a residue, a short fragment, or a pairwise similarity score. It repurposes existing annotation sources (InterPro, BioLiP, SAbDab) that were previously used for protein-level labels into a unified fine-grained task structure with explicit cross-family and multi-identity-threshold splits. The paper does not propose a new model architecture but instead provides the first systematic evaluation protocol that isolates whether pretrained representations (ESM2, SaProt, GVP-GNN, etc.) capture localized biological signals versus relying on global or distributional cues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a critical gap: models that perform well on conventional protein-level tasks (AUPR > 0.9 in-distribution) collapse to near-chance on cross-family residue prediction (AUPR 0.1–0.3 for active/binding sites), suggesting current representations encode family-level identity rather than mechanistic residue-level function. The near-zero Spearman correlation (ρ = −0.01) between active-site and motif fragment classification indicates these are fundamentally distinct capabilities, complicating the assumption that one 'good' protein embedding suffices for all functional tasks. Open questions include how to design architectures that capture conformational and antibody-independent features (epitope prediction remains unsolved, with no model exceeding 0.3 AUPR), and whether the structural priors that help sequence-structure models generalize across families can be distilled into sequence-only settings.
