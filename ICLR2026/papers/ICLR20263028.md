## Problem

Existing LFQA evaluation benchmarks lack expert-verified reference answers, are limited to ~260 English-only examples, and omit the 'tie' option that naturally arises when two responses are of comparable quality. This makes it impossible to fairly assess whether automatic metrics can truly capture the dense, nuanced information in long-form responses, as prior comparisons may be confounded by missing ground-truth criteria.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263028/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each comparison instance consists of an open-ended question, an expert-verified reference answer covering all key points, and two candidate responses that are deliberately close in quality (selected by similar upvote counts or examination scores, or generated by comparably-ranked LLMs). The evaluation task is a three-way classification: judge whether response A is better, response B is better, or they are a tie. Metrics are scored via macro-F1 and accuracy over the three classes, and are further broken down by three comparison settings (human-vs-human, human-vs-model, model-vs-model) and by language (English and Chinese).

## Method

LFQA-E is constructed from two sources to prevent contamination: Chinese data from offline College/Postgraduate Entrance Examination Simulation Questions (CEESQ/PEEQ) restored from PDFs never uploaded online, and English data from Reddit/ELI5 posts from the most recent six months. Expert annotators verify that each reference covers all necessary key points (Cohen's κ = 0.78), and pairwise comparisons are annotated by two independent reviewers using a factuality-and-completeness rubric with a tie option (κ ≈ 0.65). The paper then systematically evaluates 17 metrics across five categories—static (Length, ROUGE, BERTScore), LLM-based (GPT-4o, Qwen2.5, DeepSeek-V3, Llama-3.1), reward-model-based (Skywork-Reward, RM-R1), LRM-based (o1-mini, DeepSeek-R1), and specifically trained evaluators (Auto-J, Prometheus)—finding that none approaches the human baseline (73.3 avg F1). As a remedy, the authors apply TTRL (test-time reinforcement learning with outcome-based rewards) to small models, achieving up to 66.5% F1 on LFQA-E-EN, and show that a clip-higher mechanism from DAPO stabilizes training by preserving rollout diversity.

## Results

On LFQA-E (1618 questions, 7323 pairwise comparisons across 15 topics), no automatic evaluation metric approaches human performance: the human baseline achieves 73.3% Avg F1 and 79.9% Avg Accuracy, while the best automatic metric (o1-mini) reaches only 45.6% Avg F1 and 60.9% Avg Accuracy — a gap of over 27 points in F1. Static metrics (ROUGE: 35.8% F1, BERTScore: 36.3% F1) perform worst, followed by RM-based metrics (35.3–40.3% F1), LLM-based metrics (36.0–44.5% F1), and LRM-based metrics (42.7–45.6% F1). Trained evaluation models (Auto-J-6B: 40.7% F1; Prometheus-7B: 38.0% F1) are competitive with much larger LLMs, and LRMs consistently outperform LLMs of comparable or greater size. On the tie-detection subtask, even the best model (GPT-4o) identifies only 9.2% of ties correctly on LFQA-E-EN and 14.6% on LFQA-E-ZH, confirming that all metrics are overly conservative in declaring two responses equivalent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All 17 automatic metrics fall substantially short of the human baseline (73.3% Avg F1), with the best automatic metric (o1-mini, 45.6% F1) trailing by 27.7 points — the central finding of the paper. Among automatic metrics, LRMs (o1-mini: 45.6% F1; DeepSeek-R1: 42.7%) outperform LLMs of comparable or greater parameter count (GPT-4o: 44.5%; Qwen2.5-72B: 41.1%), and scaling within a family does not help (Qwen2.5-32B beats Qwen2.5-72B by 2.7% F1). Trained evaluation models are surprisingly competitive: Auto-J-6B (40.7% F1) matches or exceeds several 70B+ LLMs, and on LFQA-E-EN it achieves the highest single-language F1 (46.0) and Accuracy (66.8) among all automatic metrics. Static metrics (ROUGE: 35.8% F1; BERTScore: 36.3%) and RM-based metrics (Skywork-Reward-Gemma: 35.3%; RM-R1-Qwen: 35.8%) cluster at the bottom, with RMs particularly struggling on tie detection because they were trained without a tie option. When compared against established benchmarks (Tables 8–9), LFQA-E is consistently harder: e.g., GPT-4o scores 89.2% on Feedback-Bench and 70.0% on the Expert benchmark but only 57.5% on LFQA-E, and Skywork-Reward-Llama drops from 71.8% on Reward-Bench to 54.0% on LFQA-E, confirming that LFQA-E probes deeper evaluation capability than prior benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LFQA evaluation benchmarking line initiated by Xu et al. (2023), which first used expert annotation to test alignment between automatic metrics and human judgment but lacked reference answers, was limited to 260 English examples, and used a binary A/B format. LFQA-E addresses these gaps by introducing expert-verified references, a triple-choice (A/B/tie) format, multilingual coverage (English and Chinese), and a substantially larger scale (1618 questions, 7323 comparisons across 15 domains). The paper also incorporates TTRL (Zuo et al., 2025) as a test-time reinforcement learning approach to improve model-based evaluation metrics, positioning it within the broader trend of using RL to enhance LLM reasoning and evaluation capabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that none of 17 evaluated metrics (spanning static, LLM, LRM, RM, and trained evaluation models) approaches human-level performance on nuanced long-form response comparison—provides a strong, systematic negative result that challenges the growing reliance on LLM-as-judge for LFQA. The discovery that metrics exhibit low or even negative inter-correlations, and that performance degrades sharply across comparison settings (especially model-vs-model), highlights a fundamental gap in current evaluation methodology. Open questions include how to build metrics that capture dense, multi-faceted information in long-form responses, how to handle the inherently difficult 'tie' judgment, and whether test-time scaling (TTRL, CoT) can close the remaining gap without overfitting to the three-class structure.
