## Problem

Converting pretrained GQA/MHA models into multi-head latent attention (MLA) post-hoc is attractive for reducing KV-cache cost without retraining, but existing SVD-based conversion baselines minimize weight-space error ‖W−Ŵ‖_F rather than activation-space error ‖XW−XŴ‖_F, and enforce uniform rank across all layers. This mismatch causes attention-logit drift and over/under-compression of layers with heterogeneous spectral structure, degrading one-shot fidelity and increasing reliance on post-conversion fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained GQA checkpoint (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-4B) and a small calibration set of activation batches (default 256 samples at sequence length 32) to estimate per-layer covariance matrices. It produces MLA-converted weights at strict KV-parity—latent rank r = g_h·d_h matches the original GQA per-token KV width—along with a per-layer, per-matrix (K and V separately) rank allocation schedule. The output includes down-projection W^a ∈ R^{D×r}, up-projection W^b ∈ R^{r×n_h·d_h}, and optional decoupled RoPE adapter matrices W_Q^R, W_K^R for positional encoding.

## Method

CARE replaces naive weight SVD with a covariance-weighted factorization: it computes SVD on √C·W (where C is the activation covariance from calibration data), then unwhitens via √C^{-1} to obtain the low-rank approximation Ŵ, directly minimizing the empirical activation error ‖XW−XŴ‖_F. For rank allocation, CARE defines a priority score s^(l)(r) = σ_{r+1}² / Σ_{m>r} σ_m² (normalized residual reduction) for each layer and greedily water-fills a fixed global KV budget, assigning more rank to spectrally complex layers and less to intrinsically low-rank ones. The SVD factors are mapped to MLA's down/up projections (W^a ← √C^{-1}U_rΣ_r, W^b ← V_r^T), and a compact joined form W_join = blkdiag(W_K^b, W_V^b) enables efficient KV reconstruction from cached latents. An optional healing stage fine-tunes the converted model with cross-entropy plus KL-divergence distillation loss and a small decoupled RoPE channel to fully recover the original model's accuracy.

## Results

Under KV-parity (fixed KV-cache budget), CARE-E achieves a one-shot perplexity of 49.43 and mean accuracy of 52.62% on Llama-3.1-8B at rank 256 (75% KV savings), versus 1633.65 PPL and 32.08% for MHA2MLA and 537.57 PPL and 32.63% for Palu(SVD). At rank 512 (50% KV savings), CARE-U reaches 9.64 PPL and 62.33% mean accuracy on Llama-3.1-8B, nearly matching the original GQA model (7.21 PPL, 58.24%). The paper reports up to 215× perplexity reduction and up to 1.70× mean-accuracy improvement over the weakest baselines at matched KV budgets, with a brief post-SVD healing fine-tune fully recovering the original model's accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARE dominates the two MLA-conversion baselines (MHA2MLA and Palu/SVD) by wide margins: at rank 256 on Llama-3.1-8B, CARE-E achieves 49.43 PPL vs. 1633.65 (MHA2MLA) and 537.57 (Palu), a 33×–116× perplexity gap, and 52.62% vs. 32.08%/32.63% mean accuracy. Against ASVD, CARE-U/E consistently achieves lower PPL (e.g., 48.35 vs. 312.86 at rank 256 on Llama) and higher accuracy (50.00–52.62% vs. 33.00%), with the gap narrowing at rank 512 where ASVD reaches 12.02 PPL vs. CARE-U's 9.64. The closest competitor is SVD-LLM V2, which matches or slightly beats CARE-U on PPL at rank 512 (9.63 vs. 9.64 on Llama; 11.88 vs. 12.03 on Qwen3-4B) but trails on mean accuracy (62.21% vs. 62.33% on Llama; 61.14% vs. 61.62% on Qwen3-4B). In the healing (post-conversion SFT) setting at rank 512, TransMLA + CARE(E) Init reaches 62.59% and 63.27% mean accuracy at 1B and 3B tokens, outperforming TransMLA alone (61.36%/61.86%) and Palu(SVD) (47.58%/56.30%), confirming that the covariance-aware initialization provides a stronger starting point that requires less healing data to recover full accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

CARE builds directly on the 2025 line of post-hoc MLA conversion work—TransMLA (Meng et al.) established that GQA layers admit an equivalent MLA parameterization, and MHA2MLA (Ji et al.) addressed positional-encoding mismatches with partial RoPE and joint-SVD initialization. CARE departs from these baselines at the factorization level: rather than minimizing weight-space Frobenius error with uniform rank (as in Palu, ASVD, SVD-LLM V2, and the SVD initializations of TransMLA/MHA2MLA), it minimizes activation-space error via a √C-weighted SVD and allocates rank non-uniformly across layers through a water-filling schedule guided by the whitened singular spectrum. The decoupled RoPE design follows DeepSeek-V2's MLA architecture, and the healing fine-tuning stage reuses TransMLA's restoration pipeline with CARE's initialization swapped in.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Because the deployed LLM ecosystem is dominated by pretrained GQA/MHA checkpoints, a reliable post-hoc path to MLA under a fixed KV budget has immediate practical value for inference cost reduction without retraining. CARE's covariance-aware initialization substantially narrows the one-shot gap to the original model (e.g., PPL 49 vs. 7.21 at rank 256 on Llama-3.1-8B), and a brief 1–3B-token healing run recovers near-original accuracy, reducing the SFT budget needed compared to TransMLA or Palu. Open questions include whether the calibration-dependent covariance estimate degrades under significant domain shift, whether per-layer dynamic ranks can be supported by production MLA kernels (currently they cannot, limiting end-to-end latency claims), and whether the recipe extends to multimodal or MoE architectures where attention modules face analogous KV bottlenecks.
