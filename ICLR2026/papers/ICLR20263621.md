## Problem

Scalable kernel approximation methods like Random Fourier Features are fundamentally restricted to stationary kernels by Bochner's theorem, making them unable to model non-stationary processes where statistical properties vary across the input domain. Expressive non-stationary alternatives such as Deep GPs and input-dependent kernels reintroduce cubic or near-cubic computational costs, leaving a gap between scalable but limited stationary models and flexible but expensive non-stationary ones.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard input feature vectors x ∈ R^d paired with scalar regression targets y, and produces a full Gaussian predictive distribution (mean and variance) at test points. The feature map is constructed by evaluating D randomly sampled wavelet atoms at each input, where each atom is a scaled and shifted version of a mother wavelet (e.g., Mexican Hat or Morlet). Unlike RFF which uses globally supported sinusoids, each wavelet feature is spatially localized, so the effective receptive field of each feature depends on its sampled scale, enabling input-dependent resolution allocation without additional architectural complexity.

## Method

RWF replaces the globally supported Fourier basis of RFF with a family of wavelet atoms ψ_{s,t}(x) = s^{-d/2}ψ((x−t)/s), where scale s controls frequency resolution and shift t controls spatial location. By sampling (s_i, t_i) i.i.d. from a factorized density p(s,t) = p_s(s)p_t(t) (e.g., log-uniform scales, uniform shifts), the paper constructs an explicit D-dimensional feature map z(x) whose inner product yields an unbiased Monte Carlo estimator of a non-stationary kernel k(x,y) = ∫ψ_{s,t}(x)ψ_{s,t}(y)p(s,t)dsdt. Training reduces to a single closed-form Bayesian linear regression solve in the D-dimensional feature space, achieving O(ND²) complexity and O(D²) per-prediction cost. The paper provides a full theoretical analysis establishing positive definiteness of the induced kernel, unbiasedness and variance bounds of the approximation, and uniform convergence guarantees with explicit sample complexity D = O(B²ε⁻² log(1/δ)), where constants depend on the wavelet's boundedness and Lipschitz properties.

## Results

On the multi-step function, RWF-GP achieves RMSE 0.071±0.011, CRPS 0.112±0.010, and NLL −1.879±0.061, outperforming the next-best method (A-RKHS: RMSE 0.095) by 25% while training in 9 minutes (vs. 11–20 min for competitors). On TIMIT speech, RWF-GP reaches RMSE 0.42±0.003 and NLL 0.56±0.04, beating A-RKHS (0.48) and DRF (0.54) with the shortest training time (90 min vs. 126–141 min). Across seven UCI regression benchmarks, RWF-GP attains the lowest RMSE on five of seven datasets (e.g., ENERGY1k: 0.42 vs. 0.48 for DGP; NAVAL11k: 0.02 vs. 0.04 for DGP) and the fastest training time on all seven. On the 45K-sample Protein dataset, RWF-GP achieves RMSE 4.25±0.02 and NLL 2.71±0.03 in 90 minutes, outperforming all baselines including A-RKHS (4.32, 130 min) and DGP (4.35, 120 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RWF-GP consistently outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and metric. Stationary methods (RFF-GP, SVGP, Exact GP with RBF kernel) are the weakest: RFF-GP achieves the highest RMSE on the multi-step function (0.246) and TIMIT (2.13), confirming that global Fourier features cannot capture localized non-stationarities. Among expressive non-stationary baselines, A-RKHS (Shi et al., 2024) is the closest competitor, but RWF-GP still leads by 25% RMSE on the multi-step function (0.071 vs. 0.095), 12.5% on TIMIT (0.42 vs. 0.48), and 1.6% on Protein (4.25 vs. 4.32). Deep GP and DRF capture non-stationarity but at 1.5–2× the training time of RWF-GP (e.g., 131–140 s vs. 90 s on TIMIT; 120–130 min vs. 90 min on Protein). Spectral Mixture kernels provide only partial gains, matching or trailing RFF on several UCI datasets. RWF-GP also achieves the lowest training time in every experiment (9 s on multi-step, 90 min on TIMIT and Protein, 8–24 min on UCI), validating its O(ND²) complexity advantage over the O(NM²) per-step cost of SVGP and the iterative variational updates of Deep GP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the random features framework of Rahimi & Recht (2007) by replacing the globally-supported sinusoidal basis (constrained by Bochner's theorem to stationary kernels) with a randomized wavelet family that is inherently localized in both space and frequency. It departs from prior wavelet-based kernel methods (Zhang et al. 2004; Yger & Rakotomamonjy 2011; Kereta et al. 2019), which relied on fixed wavelet dictionaries or eigendecomposition of the empirical kernel matrix, by constructing an explicit Monte Carlo feature map with linear-time inference and formal convergence guarantees. The work sits at the intersection of scalable random-feature GPs and non-stationary kernel modeling, filling the gap between efficient but stationary RFF and expressive but costly Deep GPs or SVGP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RWF provides the first principled, theoretically grounded random-feature construction for non-stationary kernels with explicit uniform convergence and sample complexity bounds, giving practitioners a drop-in replacement for RFF when stationarity is violated. The O(ND²) training cost with closed-form posterior (no iterative variational updates) makes it practical for large-scale non-stationary regression where exact GPs are infeasible. Open questions include whether adaptive or learned wavelet sampling distributions can further reduce the feature count, how the method extends to classification or multi-output settings, and whether the theoretical constants (B, L_z) are tight enough to be practically informative. The reliance on heuristic choices for scale ranges and mother wavelet type remains a practical limitation.
