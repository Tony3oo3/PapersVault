## Problem

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a powerful RL paradigm for autoregressive LLMs, but extending it to discrete diffusion models (DDMs) is fundamentally broken: the non-autoregressive, parallel token generation makes importance sampling intractable and rollout generation complex. Existing partial solutions—semi-autoregressive samplers for text, low-confidence re-masking for images, and masking heuristics for likelihood estimation—either violate conditioning assumptions, lack stochastic flexibility for group comparisons, or impose prohibitive Monte Carlo costs, leaving DDMs without a practical RL optimization framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces either a text completion (math reasoning or code, up to 512 tokens) or a generated image encoded as a 1024-token discrete sequence (512×512 resolution, 8192-vocab visual tokenizer). For RL training, each prompt yields a group of G rollouts (G=6 for text, G=8 for images), and the reward system scores each completion—using correctness/format rewards for text and a composite of UnifiedReward, HPSv3, and CLIP Score for images. The policy is updated over μ inner iterations per prompt, with each iteration operating on a stochastically re-masked version of the rollout at a controlled timestep.

## Method

MaskGRPO consolidates GRPO for multimodal discrete diffusion through two modality-specific innovations. For importance estimation, it replaces intractable log-likelihood ratios with a difference-of-losses approximation (Eq. 8–9), then designs the re-masking operator to exploit structural priors: text uses an AR-like fading-out strategy that progressively increases mask probability toward later tokens (exploiting the 'AR-ness' of language), while vision uses uniform random masking with aggressive truncation (γ=0.8) to capture informative variation in globally correlated visual tokens. For rollout generation, text uses a standard semi-autoregressive block decoder, while images employ a novel 'emerge sampler' that replaces MaskGIT's confidence-based decoding with probabilistic categorical sampling (Cat distribution), letting tokens emerge naturally from masks without enforcing a fixed unmasking count per step. The full algorithm samples G completions, computes group-relative advantages, then iteratively constructs masked versions at timesteps t_j ∈ (γ, 1), estimates per-timestep importance ratios and KL divergences, and updates the policy via the clipped GRPO objective.

## Results

On math reasoning and coding, MaskGRPO achieves 84.7% on GSM8K (256 tokens), 37.6% on MATH500 (256), and 45.4% on MBPP (256), yielding absolute gains of +8.0, +5.2, and +6.4 over the LLaDA-8B-Instruct base model—nearly doubling the RL improvement of prior methods (e.g., diffu-GRPO's +3.1 on GSM8K). On image generation, MaskGRPO lifts GenEval Overall from 0.56 (MMaDA) to 0.81, and with SFT pre-training reaches 0.90, approaching FLUX.1-dev (0.82) and surpassing Janus-Pro (0.80) among discrete models. Training requires up to 30% fewer steps than diffu-GRPO (500 vs. 7000+ global steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaskGRPO outperforms all four RL baselines on every language benchmark: it leads diffu-GRPO by +4.9 on GSM8K-256, UniGRPO by +3.6, wd1 by +3.9, and TraceRL by +2.6, with similar margins on MATH500 and MBPP. On image generation (GenEval Overall), MaskGRPO (0.81) surpasses UniGRPO (0.63) by +0.18 and the MMaDA base (0.56) by +0.25; with SFT pre-training it reaches 0.90, exceeding Janus-Pro (0.80) and approaching FLUX.1-dev (0.82) and SD3.5-L (0.71) among continuous models. Among discrete generation baselines, MaskGRPO (SFT) also outperforms Show-o (0.53) by a wide margin. On DPG-Bench, SFT+MaskGRPO (81.76) surpasses Emu3-Gen (80.60) and Hunyuan-DiT (78.87), while the RL-only variant (75.81) still exceeds the MMaDA base (70.51). The paper notes that UniGRPO's visual RL results are reported without training configuration details, making direct comparison less rigorous.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) from autoregressive language models to discrete diffusion models (DDMs), building on the LLaDA/MMaDA architecture family. It departs from prior DDM-RL attempts (diffu-GRPO, UniGRPO, wd1, TraceRL) by identifying that their importance estimators either violate conditioning assumptions or are computationally prohibitive, and by introducing modality-specific solutions: an AR-like fading-out masking estimator for text (exploiting the 'AR-ness' observed in DDM language generation) and a probabilistic 'emerge' sampler for images that replaces the rigid MaskGIT decoding schedule. The theoretical foundation for approximating intractable log-likelihood differences via the DDM training loss (Eq. 8) unifies and corrects the ad-hoc masking heuristics used in earlier work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MaskGRPO establishes the first practical, scalable GRPO pipeline for multimodal discrete diffusion, demonstrating that modality-aware design in both sampling and importance estimation is essential rather than optional. The near-doubling of RL gains over prior methods and the approach to FLUX-level GenEval scores (0.81, 0.90 with SFT) suggest discrete diffusion models are a viable competitive path for generative AI, not just a theoretical alternative. Open questions include whether the modality-specific principles generalize beyond LLaDA/MMaDA architectures, whether the emerge sampler's pre-RL underperformance (0.51 vs. 0.56 GenEval) is a fundamental limitation or an artifact of the specific model, and how the approach scales to higher-resolution or video generation where token counts grow substantially.
