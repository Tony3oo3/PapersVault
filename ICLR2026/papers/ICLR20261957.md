## Problem

Standard off-policy learning assumes unconfoundedness, but in practice unobserved factors often influence both treatment assignment and outcomes, leading to biased estimates and potentially harmful policies. The existing confounding-robust method (Kallus & Zhou) relies on an unstable minimax optimization over inverse-propensity-weighted outcomes and lacks semi-parametric efficiency, resulting in suboptimal finite-sample variance. This paper addresses both shortcomings by deriving a closed-form sharp bound on the value function under the Marginal Sensitivity Model and a semi-parametrically efficient estimator for it.

## Input / Output

The method consumes observational tabular data (outcome Y, covariates X, discrete treatment A with arbitrary number of actions d_a), a user-specified sensitivity parameter Γ ≥ 1 encoding the maximum odds-ratio deviation between nominal and true propensity scores, and a parametric policy class (e.g., neural networks). It outputs a confounding-robust policy π* that minimizes the worst-case value under unobserved confounding, along with a sharp upper bound V^{+,*}(π) on the value function. Unlike prior work restricted to binary treatments, the method handles multi-valued discrete actions, and the estimator requires estimating nuisance functions including propensity scores, conditional quantiles at α = Γ/(1+Γ), and truncated conditional means.

## Method

The key insight is that the sharp upper bound V^{+,*}(π) = ∫ Σ_a Q^{+,*}(a,x) π(a|x) dp(x) admits a closed-form expression via the sharp bounds Q^{+,*}(a,x) on the conditional average potential outcome under the MSM, thereby eliminating the need for the unstable minimax optimization over feasible IPW weights used by Kallus & Zhou. To estimate this bound efficiently, the authors derive the efficient influence function (EIF) of V^{+,*}(π) through the chain rule for pathwise differentiability, decomposing it into contributions from the propensity score, the conditional quantile function F^{-1}_{x,a}(α), and the truncated conditional means. The resulting one-step bias-corrected estimator is Neyman-orthogonal with respect to all nuisance functions, achieving semi-parametric efficiency (lowest possible variance among unbiased estimators). Learning guarantees via Rademacher complexity show that minimizing the estimated bound with high probability recovers the optimal confounding-robust policy. The framework extends directly to policy improvement, where the regret relative to a baseline policy π_0 is bounded and minimized using the same efficient estimator applied to both the candidate and baseline value bounds.

## Results

On synthetic data with varying confounding strength (Γ*), the proposed efficient and sharp estimator achieves the lowest regret (most negative = best) for all Γ* ≥ 4, reaching −0.89 ± 0.13 at Γ*=6 versus −0.40 ± 0.06 for Kallus & Zhou (2021) and −0.09 ± 0.01 for standard IPW. At the highest confounding level (Γ*=16), the method achieves −0.30 ± 0.22, a relative improvement of up to 4× over the next-best confounding-robust baseline. On the real-world International Stroke Trial (4 treatments), the method yields the best treatment policy at Γ=24, and on the IHDP semi-synthetic benchmark it consistently achieves the lowest regret across all sensitivity parameter values.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four baselines: standard IPW (Swaminathan & Joachims, 2015), standard DR (Athey & Wager, 2021; Dudik et al., 2011), and the only prior confounding-robust method, Kallus & Zhou (2018a; 2021). Standard IPW and DR perform best at very low confounding (Γ*=2: −1.31 and −1.30 respectively) but are biased under unobserved confounding and converge to near-zero regret (−0.03 to −0.04) by Γ*=16, offering no meaningful policy improvement. Kallus & Zhou (2021) is competitive at low confounding (−1.21 at Γ*=2) but deteriorates rapidly as confounding increases, reaching only −0.08 at Γ*=16; the paper attributes this to their minimax optimization reverting to the baseline policy when nuisance estimation noise shifts the global threshold. The proposed method wins for all Γ* ≥ 4, with the largest absolute gap at Γ*=6 (−0.89 vs. −0.40, a 2.2× improvement over Kallus & Zhou) and a relative gain of up to 4× at higher confounding levels. On the International Stroke Trial and IHDP datasets, the method similarly outperforms all baselines across the range of sensitivity parameters tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the confounding-robust policy learning framework of Kallus & Zhou (2018a; 2021), which operates under the same Marginal Sensitivity Model (MSM) of Tan (2006) but relies on a numerically unstable minimax optimization over inverse-propensity-weighted scores. The key departure is a shift from optimizing a sharp regret bound (relative to a baseline) to deriving closed-form sharp bounds on the value function itself, combined with a one-step bias-corrected estimator based on the efficient influence function. The building blocks for the sharp CATE bounds (Dorn & Guo, 2022; Frauen et al., 2023) are repurposed here, but the paper's contribution is the non-trivial derivation of the EIF for the value-level bound and the resulting semi-parametric efficiency guarantee, which prior work on this task lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners in healthcare and public policy who must learn treatment policies from observational data where unobserved confounding is plausible, this provides the first statistically efficient and numerically stable method for confounding-robust policy learning under the MSM. The practical finding that the prior minimax approach collapses to the baseline policy as the sensitivity parameter Γ grows—while the proposed method remains stable—is important for deployment reliability. Open questions include how to select Γ in a principled data-driven way (the paper defers this to domain knowledge), whether the approach extends to continuous or sequential treatments, and whether the bounded-outcome assumption (|Y| ≤ C_y) is restrictive in practice. The real-world validation relies on artificially introduced confounding in an RCT, so true observational validation remains an open challenge.
