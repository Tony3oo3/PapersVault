## Problem

MLLMs face a fundamental dilemma for long video understanding: the vast number of visual tokens from hours-long videos exceeds their limited context windows, while uniform sampling misses query-relevant moments and existing heuristic keyframe selectors (DPP, AKS, BOLT) produce temporally discontinuous frame sets that confuse the model. No prior training-free method jointly optimizes query-relevance, content diversity, and temporal continuity in a principled way.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262151/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a long video sampled at 1 fps and a natural-language query, and produces a text answer. The actual input fed to the MLLM is an interleaved sequence of K keyframes (visual tokens, typically 8) and approximately 210 short captions (≤15 words each, generated by a lightweight Qwen2-VL-2B captioner) from non-keyframes, arranged in true temporal order. The entire pipeline is training-free and plug-and-play, requiring no fine-tuning of the target MLLM.

## Method

Nar-KFC operates in two collaborative stages. First, KFC formulates keyframe selection as an integer quadratic programming (IQP) subgraph problem on a complete graph whose edge weights combine CLIP-based query-relevance scores and exp-transformed frame-diversity scores; since IQP is NP-hard, a greedy search with SVD low-rank denoising, uniform downsampling, and k-nearest-neighbor refinement solves it in O(NK) time with near-optimal quality. Second, Nar-KFC threads the selected keyframes with temporally interleaved textual narratives generated from non-keyframes at a uniform interval Δ, reconstructing a continuous visual-textual representation that preserves temporal coherence while keeping the total token count within the MLLM's context budget.

## Results

On Video-MME (no sub., 8 frames), Nar-KFC with InternVL3-8B achieves 63.8% overall accuracy, surpassing the strongest prior VideoLLM baselines including VILA-34B (58.3%) and Video-XL 256frm (55.5%). On LongVideoBench, Nar-KFC improves InternVL2 from 52.3% to 53.9% and Qwen2.5-VL from 52.7% to 55.3%. On MLVU, KFC alone yields over 6% average improvement across five MLLMs, and Nar-KFC with InternVL3 reaches 68.4% (vs. 60.9% baseline). The method is training-free and plug-and-play, requiring only a lightweight 2B captioner (Qwen2-VL-2B) for narrative generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Nar-KFC with InternVL3-8B (63.8% on Video-MME) outperforms all prior VideoLLMs, including those using substantially larger LLMs (VILA-34B: 58.3%) or more frames (Video-XL 256frm: 55.5%, LongVILA 256frm: 50.5%), demonstrating that query-aware keyframe selection plus narrative threading is more effective than brute-force frame scaling. Among training-free keyframe selection methods, KFC consistently beats DPP (52.2%), AKS (52.8%), and BOLT (53.3%) on Video-MME (no sub.), and also surpasses localize-then-answer approaches like SeViLA (52.2%) and TempGQA (50.4%). Simple top-K matching with CLIP, SigLIP, or BLIP-2 embeddings actually degrades performance below uniform sampling (47.3–47.8% vs. 51.9%), confirming that query-relevance alone without diversity leads to temporally concentrated selections. On MLVU, Nar-KFC with LLaVA-Video achieves 67.7%, exceeding Frame-Voyager (65.6%) and LongVU (65.4%), while on LVB the gains are more modest (e.g., 55.3% vs. BOLT's 55.6% with Qwen2.5-VL), partly offset by very short videos under 1 minute. The method also improves open-ended generation: Nar-KFC with InternVL3 lifts MMBench-Video overall from 1.57 to 1.78 and MLVU-OpenEnded G-Avg from 4.92 to 5.02.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work on long video understanding with MLLMs: (1) keyframe selection methods that replace uniform sampling (DPP, AKS, BOLT, Frame-Voyager), and (2) training-free caption-based approaches that convert frames to text for LLM reasoning (VideoAgent, DrVideo). It departs from prior keyframe selection by formulating the problem as an integer quadratic programming (IQP) subgraph selection task that jointly optimizes query-relevance and frame-diversity, rather than relying on handcrafted heuristics. The narrative interleaving step is a novel repurposing of lightweight captioning as a temporal-continuity bridge, drawing conceptual parallels to Two-Stream and SlowFast architectures but applied at the token level for MLLM inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free nature of Nar-KFC makes it immediately applicable to any existing MLLM without retraining, and the consistent 4–8% gains across five models and three benchmarks demonstrate broad practical utility. The finding that 8 well-chosen keyframes plus 210 short narratives outperform 256-frame approaches challenges the prevailing assumption that more visual tokens are always better for long video QA. Open questions include how the method scales to hour-scale videos where the score matrix becomes prohibitively large, whether the CLIP-based embedding bottleneck limits ceiling performance, and whether the narrative quality gap (Qwen2-VL-2B vs. 72B) becomes more critical for fine-grained temporal reasoning tasks.
