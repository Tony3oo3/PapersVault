## Problem

Existing data attribution methods for diffusion models estimate training-sample influence at the whole-image level, failing to isolate which samples contributed to specific semantic concepts such as a particular character, style, or object. This limitation is critical in practical scenarios—copyright enforcement, safety auditing, model debugging—where stakeholders need to know which training data shaped a specific concept rather than the entire composition. The paper introduces concept-level attribution as a new task and shows that standard image-level methods degrade sharply on out-of-distribution concept combinations where no single training sample contains the full target concept.

## Input / Output

The method requires a trained (frozen) diffusion model, a target concept expressible as a conditioning input (text prompt or class index), the full training dataset, and—optionally for local attribution—a specific generated image along with the initial noise used to produce it. For local attribution, the utility gradient is computed along the deterministic DDIM trajectory from that specific noise, while global attribution averages over trajectories from random noise. The output is a per-training-sample influence score measuring alignment with the target concept, yielding a ranked list where top-ranked samples are expected to contain the attributed concept.

## Method

Concept-TRAK extends the TRAK influence-function framework by replacing the standard DSM loss with two reward-optimization-based losses that operate in the tangent space of the diffusion latent manifold. The training loss uses a Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS) reward, producing gradient directions that point toward the specific training sample as tangent vectors; the utility loss uses classifier-free guidance or concept-slider guidance vectors (ε_θ(x_t;c) − ε_θ(x_t;c₋)) to capture the target concept's direction. Both losses are formulated via an explicit score-matching objective with stop-gradient on the reward term, so the resulting parameter gradients are proportional to the reward gradient projected through the model Jacobian. Two additional techniques improve stability: deterministic DDIM inversion eliminates stochasticity from the forward diffusion process, and per-timestep gradient normalization prevents any single noise level from dominating the influence score. The final influence score is the inner product of the projected utility and training gradients weighted by the inverse Fisher information matrix, measuring how much the training sample's tangent-space direction aligns with the concept's direction.

## Results

On the CelebA-HQ dataset, Concept-TRAK achieves 0.97 average Precision@10 in the out-of-distribution (OOD) setting, substantially outperforming DAS (0.67), D-TRAK (0.30), and TRAK (0.60). On the synthetic dataset, Concept-TRAK maintains 0.85 OOD Precision@10 while all baselines drop to ≤0.50. On the established AbC benchmark for text-to-image attribution, Concept-TRAK achieves 0.955 Recall@10, significantly surpassing prior methods that struggle to isolate target concepts from style interference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Concept-TRAK consistently outperforms all four baselines, with the largest gaps appearing in out-of-distribution (OOD) settings where training data does not contain the exact concept combination. On CelebA-HQ OOD, Concept-TRAK (0.97) beats DAS (0.67) by 30 points, D-TRAK (0.30) by 67 points, and TRAK (0.60) by 37 points in average Precision@10. DAS is the strongest baseline in in-distribution settings (0.96 vs. 0.92 for Concept-TRAK on CelebA-HQ ID) but degrades sharply when the model must compose unseen concept combinations. D-TRAK is the weakest baseline across all settings, likely due to its squared ℓ₂-norm loss providing less stable gradients. On the AbC benchmark, the unlearning-based method (Wang et al., 2024b) and D-TRAK both fail to isolate the target concept ⟨V⟩ from style elements in generated images, while Concept-TRAK achieves 0.955 Recall@10 by explicitly targeting concept-relevant tangent-space directions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the influence-function lineage (TRAK → D-TRAK → DAS) from whole-image attribution to a new concept-level setting, where the goal is to isolate which training samples contributed to a specific semantic feature (object, style, character) rather than the full composition. The key methodological departure is the design of reward-based loss functions grounded in tangent-space geometry: the training loss uses DPS-style posterior gradients, and the utility loss uses classifier-free guidance or concept-slider vectors, both derived from a reward-optimization objective (à la DPO). This contrasts with prior diffusion attribution work that relies on DSM or norm-based losses, which the authors show are suboptimal for isolating concept-specific directions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Concept-level attribution addresses a practical gap in generative AI governance: copyright holders, safety auditors, and model developers need to trace influence to specific concepts (e.g., a protected character) rather than whole images, a need that whole-image methods structurally cannot meet. The paper's OOD results—where baselines collapse but Concept-TRAK maintains or even improves performance—suggest that compositional generalization in diffusion models is attributable at the concept level in ways that image-level similarity cannot capture. Limitations include occasional retrieval of stylistically similar but conceptually distinct images, approximate gradient estimation, and evaluation primarily on small-scale or fine-tuned models; open questions include scaling to billion-parameter T2I models, handling visual (non-textual) concepts without explicit conditioning, and establishing theoretical guarantees for attribution accuracy.
