## Problem

Guidance for discrete generative models (diffusion or flow-based) typically relies on first-order Taylor approximations to avoid evaluating the guidance function at every possible target state, but this approximation introduces non-negligible errors in discrete state spaces. Moreover, existing exact guidance formulations are limited to specific cases (e.g., class-conditional or energy-weighted) and still require multiple forward passes per sampling step. The paper asks whether an exact, general-purpose guidance formulation exists for discrete flow matching that is both theoretically principled and sampling-efficient.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained discrete flow matching posterior p_{1|t} (a D×|S| matrix-valued network) and a known density ratio r(x) = q_1(x)/p_1(x) between target and source distributions, which can be instantiated as a classifier score, an energy function, or an RLHF reward. At sampling time, the guidance network H_t^θ and the pretrained posterior are each called once per step, and their outputs are multiplied element-wise to form the guided posterior q_{1|t}^d ∝ h_t^d · p_{1|t}^d, from which the next state is drawn via the always-valid CTMC jump procedure. For the multimodal experiments, the base model is FUDOKI (1.5B parameters), producing 256×256 images from text prompts or text answers from image+question pairs.

## Method

The core result (Theorem 1) derives the exact rectified posterior for the target distribution by reweighting the source posterior with a conditional expectation of the density ratio: q_{1|t}(z^d|x) ∝ E[r(x_1) | x_1^d=z^d, x_t=x] · p_{1|t}(z^d|x). This posterior-based formulation requires only a single forward pass through the guidance network per sampling step, in contrast to rate-based guidance (Theorem 2) which needs D+1 or D×(|S|−1)+1 evaluations. The guidance network is trained by minimizing a Bregman divergence with F(x)=⟨x,log x⟩ (a log-likelihood-type loss suited to positive-valued density ratios), augmented by a cross-entropy regularization term that leverages target-distribution samples when available. The framework unifies classifier guidance, energy-weighted sampling, and RLHF preference alignment as special cases of the same density-ratio reweighting, and extends naturally to masked diffusion models where the posterior is time-independent.

## Results

On the GenEval text-to-image benchmark, the proposed posterior-based guidance applied to FUDOKI achieves an overall score of 0.78, improving over the unguided FUDOKI baseline (0.77) with gains on 4 of 6 sub-tasks (Two Obj: 0.86, Colors: 0.89, Position: 0.70, Color Attri: 0.77). On multimodal understanding, the guided model consistently outperforms FUDOKI across all six benchmarks (e.g., MMMU: 35.4 vs 34.3, MM-Vet: 38.6 vs 38.0, POPE: 86.8 vs 86.1). In 2-D energy-guided simulations, posterior-based guidance matches the ground-truth distribution at high guidance strengths (γ=10, 20) where the first-order-approximated predictor guidance of Nisonoff et al. (2025) deviates, while achieving 1.6× faster sampling due to requiring only one forward pass per step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unguided FUDOKI baseline, the proposed guidance improves the overall GenEval score from 0.77 to 0.78, with notable gains on Color Attri (0.67→0.77) and Position (0.68→0.70), though it slightly regresses on Single Obj (0.96→0.94) and Counting (0.56→0.53). In the 2-D simulation, the posterior-based guidance produces samples matching the ground-truth energy-guided distribution at γ=10 and γ=20, whereas the predictor guidance of Nisonoff et al. (2025) visibly deviates from the target; additionally, posterior-based guidance requires only 1 forward pass per sampling step versus D+1 (or D×(|S|−1)+1) for the rate-based approach, yielding a 1.6× speedup. On multimodal understanding (Table 3), the guided FUDOKI beats the unguided version on all six benchmarks (POPE 86.8 vs 86.1, MME-P 1492.7 vs 1485.4, MMB 74.2 vs 73.9, GQA 58.2 vs 57.6, MMMU 35.4 vs 34.3, MM-Vet 38.6 vs 38.0). Among all compared methods in Table 2, the guided model (0.78) surpasses SD3-Medium (0.74), Janus-Pro-1B (0.73), DALL-E 3 (0.67), and D-DiT (0.65), establishing it as the top-performing method in the Und.+Gen. category. The rate-based guidance (Theorem 2) also matches ground truth at low γ but is computationally less efficient than the posterior-based variant, confirming the paper's claim that posterior-based guidance is both exact and sampling-efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the discrete flow matching framework (Campbell et al. 2024; Gat et al. 2024; Shaul et al. 2025) and the CTMC-based formulation of discrete diffusion, but departs from existing discrete guidance methods (Nisonoff et al. 2025; Vignac et al. 2023; Schiff et al. 2025) that rely on first-order Taylor approximations to avoid evaluating the guidance term at every possible transition target. The key conceptual shift is from rate-based guidance (reweighting transition rates, which requires D×(|S|−1)+1 forward passes) to posterior-based guidance (reweighting the learned posterior, which requires only one forward pass), yielding an exact formulation for any density ratio. The Bregman divergence training objective for the guidance network adapts Banerjee et al. (2005) to the discrete density-ratio setting, and the framework unifies continuous guidance results (Ouyang et al. 2024; Feng et al. 2025) as a special case of the same marginalization trick.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exact posterior-based guidance eliminates the approximation error that is particularly problematic in discrete state spaces, where the first-order Taylor expansion has no natural geometric interpretation and can be arbitrarily inaccurate. By requiring only a single forward pass per sampling step, the method makes guidance practically viable for large-vocabulary discrete models (e.g., text, multimodal tokens) where rate-based approaches become prohibitively expensive. Open questions include how the framework scales to variable-length sequence generation, whether the Bregman divergence choice (F(x)=⟨x,log x⟩) is optimal for all density-ratio regimes, and whether the modest empirical gains observed on GenEval and multimodal understanding benchmarks will grow with larger reward models or more aggressive guidance strengths.
