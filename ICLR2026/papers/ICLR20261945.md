## Problem

Pre-trained diffusion models offer strong generative priors for image deblurring but suffer from two critical limitations: prohibitive inference time (tens to hundreds of sampling steps) and a fidelity-perception trade-off where outputs look perceptually plausible yet deviate from ground truth. Existing single-step diffusion approaches assign a fixed timestep to all degraded images, collapsing the iterative denoising into a direct regression that ignores the varying severity of blur and sacrifices diffusion's inductive bias.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single blurry RGB image as input and produces a deblurred RGB image in one forward pass. Internally, the image is encoded by a modified VAE with downsampling factor d=4 (reduced from SD's default d=8) to preserve fine details in low-resolution datasets like GoPro (1280×720). During inference, the model also predicts a pixel-wise blur kernel map and an adaptive timestep, which together condition the single-step denoising without requiring user-specified blur parameters.

## Method

FideDiff reformulates motion deblurring as a diffusion-like forward process where each timestep t corresponds to a specific blur severity (number of averaged frames), rather than random Gaussian noise. A consistency training objective forces the network f_θ(z_t, t) to predict the same clean image z_0 across all timesteps along a matched blur trajectory, enabling accurate one-step inference. Built on Stable Diffusion 2.1 with LoRA fine-tuning, the model is further enhanced by a Kernel ControlNet that estimates a pixel-wise blur kernel via a convolutional UNet and injects it into the diffusion UNet through a filter-like attention module (rather than direct addition), plus a GAN discriminator for fidelity. A lightweight regression head predicts the optimal timestep from the estimated kernel, allowing adaptive handling of unknown real-world blur levels. Training proceeds in three stages: foundation model (L1 + EA-LPIPS + GAN loss), kernel network pretraining (reblur loss), and joint Kernel ControlNet optimization with the foundation model frozen.

## Results

FideDiff achieves PSNR of 28.79 (GoPro), 27.28 (HIDE), 28.96 (RealBlur-J), and 36.01 (RealBlur-R), outperforming all diffusion-based baselines by large margins (e.g., +4.45 dB over OSEDiff on GoPro PSNR). On perceptual metrics, FideDiff reaches LPIPS 0.0831 and DISTS 0.0525 on GoPro, surpassing all four transformer-based methods (Restormer, HI-Diff, UFPNet, AdaRevD) on at least half the metrics across all four datasets. On the real-world RealBlur-R set, FideDiff achieves the best LPIPS (0.0584) and DISTS (0.0862) among all compared methods, demonstrating strong generalization. Inference speed is 1.52 sec/image (d=4, with Kernel ControlNet), achieving up to 17× speedup over multi-step diffusion methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FideDiff decisively outperforms all four diffusion-based baselines: it gains +4.45 dB PSNR over OSEDiff (28.79 vs. 24.34) and +5.91 dB over Diff-Plugin (28.79 vs. 22.88) on GoPro, while also achieving substantially lower LPIPS (0.0831 vs. 0.1738 and 0.2332). Against DiffBIR, FideDiff improves GoPro PSNR by +2.64 dB and LPIPS by 0.1535, and on RealBlur-R it closes the gap to 36.01 vs. 32.60 PSNR. Against UID-Diff, FideDiff leads by +3.71 dB PSNR on GoPro and +13.54 dB on RealBlur-R, where UID-Diff degrades severely. Compared to transformer-based methods, FideDiff trails on PSNR/SSIM (e.g., 28.79 vs. 34.60 for AdaRevD on GoPro) but surpasses all four on DISTS (0.0525 vs. 0.0672 for AdaRevD) and matches or beats them on LPIPS for RealBlur-J (0.1142 vs. 0.1408 for AdaRevD) and RealBlur-R (0.0584 vs. 0.0621 for AdaRevD), indicating superior perceptual fidelity in real-world settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

FideDiff builds on three key pillars: Stable Diffusion as the pretrained generative backbone, Consistency Models (Song et al., 2023) as the theoretical justification for single-step sampling, and ControlNet as the architectural template for injecting conditional information. Its primary departure from prior single-step diffusion methods (OSEDiff, SinSR, TSD-SR) is rejecting the distillation paradigm that assigns a fixed timestep to all degraded inputs; instead, it reformulates motion blur as a diffusion-like forward process where each timestep encodes a specific blur severity, then enforces cross-time consistency so all timesteps map to the same clean image. The Kernel ControlNet further departs from vanilla ControlNet by replacing direct feature addition with a filter-like module, motivated by the observation that blur kernels lack direct spatial correspondence with the target image.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides a practical recipe for deploying pretrained diffusion models in restoration pipelines where both speed and fidelity are critical, addressing the long-standing trade-off between inference cost and restoration accuracy. The consistency-training-with-matched-trajectories paradigm is potentially generalizable to other degradation types (denoising, deraining, JPEG artifact removal), though the paper only validates it on motion deblurring. A notable limitation is that FideDiff still trails strong transformer-based methods (e.g., AdaRevD) by several dB on PSNR/SSIM, suggesting that single-step diffusion has not yet fully closed the fidelity gap for pixel-accurate restoration. Open questions include whether the VAE downsampling workaround (d=4 instead of d=8) can be eliminated with higher-resolution training data, and whether the approach scales to more complex, spatially-varying real-world blur beyond the GoPro-style averaging model.
