## Problem

Conventional atom-centric molecular models treat chemical bonds merely as pairwise edges, overlooking complex bond-level phenomena such as resonance, stereoselectivity, and non-additive bond-bond interactions that govern molecular reactivity and stability. This paper addresses the gap by establishing, through information-theoretic analysis, that a bond-centric (line-graph) representation encodes strictly more structural information than an atom-centric graph alone, and by building an architecture that explicitly models bonds as first-class entities with their own interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264804/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DeMol consumes a molecular 2D graph (atom types, bond attributes) together with 3D atomic coordinates; for PCQM4Mv2 inference it operates from SMILES notation without DFT equilibrium conformations, while OC20 IS2RE provides initial DFT structures. The model produces dual embeddings—atom-level H^(a) ∈ R^{N×d_a} and bond-level H^(b) ∈ R^{M×d_b}—which are fused through a prediction head to yield continuous property values (HOMO-LUMO gap in eV, adsorption energy in eV, 12 QM9 quantum properties) or binary classification scores (ROC-AUC) across MoleculeNet tasks.

## Method

DeMol processes a molecule through two parallel channels: an atom-centric transformer on G and a bond-centric transformer on the line graph L(G) where bonds become nodes and edges encode angular/torsional relationships. A novel torsion encoding (bond angles θ_ijk and dihedral angles φ_ijkl) is injected as attention bias exclusively in the bond-centric channel, grounded in Proposition 3's geometric information-gain argument. Multi-scale Double-Helix Blocks perform bidirectional cross-attention between the two channels, enabling explicit atom-atom, atom-bond, and bond-bond interaction learning at every layer. A covalent-radii-based bond prediction loss regularises geometric plausibility, and a structure-aware mask (5 Å cutoff for atoms, adjacency/conjugacy for bonds) prunes non-physical interactions to keep complexity near-linear. The entire framework is motivated by four information-theoretic propositions proving that dual-graph representations retain strictly more mutual information than single-graph approaches and that the Information Bottleneck trade-off is tighter with the richer dual input.

## Results

On PCQM4Mv2 (HOMO-LUMO gap prediction), DeMol achieves a MAE of 0.0603 eV, a 10.1% improvement over the previous best single model TGT-At (0.0671 eV) and 13.1% over Unimol+ (0.0693 eV). On OC20 IS2RE (catalyst adsorption energy), DeMol obtains the lowest average energy MAE of 0.3879 eV and highest average EwT of 9.23%, surpassing Unimol+ (0.4088 eV, 8.61%) and TGT-At (0.4030 eV, 8.82%). On QM9, DeMol achieves the best average rank of 2.67 across 12 quantum properties, and on MoleculeNet it attains an average ROC-AUC of 79.96, outperforming all 37 baselines across 8 classification tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeMol outperforms all 37 baselines across all four benchmarks. On PCQM4Mv2, it beats the strongest single-model baseline TGT-At (0.0671) by 0.0068 eV (10.1%) and the ensemble-based GPS++ (0.0778) by 22.5%, while using fewer parameters than TGT-At (186M vs. 203M). On OC20 IS2RE, DeMol leads Unimol+ and TGT-At by 5.1% and 3.7% in average energy MAE respectively, and by 0.62 and 0.41 percentage points in average EwT, with consistent superiority across all in-domain and out-of-domain splits. On QM9, DeMol achieves the best average rank (2.67) versus ESA (4.17), PaiNN (4.67), and TorchMD-Net (5.25), with SOTA on 6 of 12 properties including HOMO, LUMO, Δε, ZPVE, G, and C_v. On MoleculeNet, DeMol's average ROC-AUC of 79.96 surpasses bond-centric competitors Galformer, LEMON, and GEM, as well as 3D-based Uni-Mol, demonstrating that the gains come from both the bond-centric graph representation and the explicit atom-bond/bond-bond interaction modelling via Double-Helix Blocks.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeMol builds directly on the structure-encoding and multi-task pretraining strategies of Transformer-M (Luo et al., 2022) and Uni-Mol (Zhou et al., 2023), adopting their Gaussian-basis-kernel distance encodings and HOMO-LUMO gap regression objectives. However, it departs substantially from prior bond-aware methods (LEMON, GEM, ALIGNN, ESA) that treated bonds as auxiliary features or independent line-graph channels, by introducing a coordinated dual-graph architecture where atom-centric and bond-centric representations are processed in parallel and fused through bidirectional cross-attention (Double-Helix Blocks). The paper further distinguishes itself through a four-proposition information-theoretic analysis that formally justifies the information gain of the bond-centric line graph and the dual-graph information bottleneck, providing a theoretical grounding that prior empirical bond-modeling works lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DeMol establishes consistent single-model SOTA across four diverse benchmarks (PCQM4Mv2, OC20 IS2RE, QM9, MoleculeNet), demonstrating that explicit bond-bond and atom-bond interaction modeling yields measurable gains over atom-centric-only approaches, particularly for larger and more chemically complex molecules. The ablation revealing that bond-centric alone (89.9 meV) underperforms atom-centric alone (77.2 meV) but their combination drops to 60.3 meV highlights that the value lies in cross-level interaction rather than either representation in isolation. Limitations include the 186M parameter count and ~34 ms/molecule inference time (20% slower than Transformer-M), and the QM9 results suggest diminishing returns on small, structurally simple molecules where complex bond-bond phenomena are less prevalent. Open questions include whether the dual-graph principle generalizes to other structured domains (proteins, materials) and whether the theoretical information-gain bounds can be tightened to provide practical architectural guidance.
