## Problem

Graph convolutional operations in outlier detection suffer from the 'Homophily Trap': aggregating features from anomalous neighbors contaminates the embeddings of nearby normal nodes, blurring the very distinctions needed for detection. Existing mitigations either rely on predefined anomaly thresholds or perform heavy-handed graph rewriting that risks destroying structural integrity, leaving no principled, threshold-free, structure-preserving solution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an attributed graph (adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{N×N} and node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×d}) and produces a continuous anomaly score per node derived from the complement of its soft clustering probability toward the normal centroid, requiring no external threshold. Internally, it also generates a learnable N×N edge-reweighting mask M (constrained to [0,1] via sigmoid) and binary pseudo-labels from a 2-cluster deep embedded clustering layer, all learned end-to-end without any ground-truth labels.

## Method

CER-GOD jointly optimizes two synergistic components: (1) a Self-Discriminative Masking Spoiler that applies a learnable mask via Hadamard product to the original adjacency (preserving topology while reweighting edges), guided by a negative MMD distribution repulsion loss using a Chebyshev kernel to maximize separation between normal and anomalous candidate clusters; and (2) a Clustering-based Outlier Detector using Student's t-distribution soft assignments with c=2 learnable centroids to generate threshold-free pseudo-labels. A diversity loss (hinge penalty on minimum cluster proportion) prevents class collapse during joint optimization. The full objective combines GAE reconstruction loss, KL-divergence clustering loss, distribution repulsion loss, and diversity loss, trained end-to-end with a 3-layer GCN backbone. A theoretical proposition establishes that node influence decays exponentially with shortest-path distance, motivating the edge-reweighting strategy to selectively suppress short-range contamination from heterogeneous neighbors.

## Results

CER-GOD achieves the best AUC on 6 out of 8 benchmark datasets, with particularly strong margins on Email (96.98% vs. 94.00% for DOMINANT), Amazon (86.24% vs. 79.87% for TAM), and Enron (72.63% vs. 68.97% for BOURNE). It is second-best only on Cora (92.09% vs. GADAM's 92.62%) and CiteSeer (74.01% vs. GADAM's 93.91%). On the large-scale OGB-Proteins dataset (132K nodes, 79M edges), CER-GOD achieves 74.81% AUC, outperforming all five compared baselines including TAM (74.49%) and SL-GAD (73.71%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CER-GOD outperforms all 13 baselines on 6 of 8 datasets, with the largest gains over GADAM on Amazon (+29.09 AUC), Enron (+38.72), and Reddit (+1.27), and over DOMINANT on Email (+2.98). The only two losses are to GADAM: on Cora (92.09 vs. 92.62, a narrow 0.53 gap) and CiteSeer (74.01 vs. 93.91, a substantial 19.9 gap), where GADAM's adaptive message-passing strategy appears particularly effective. Among reconstruction-based baselines, CER-GOD surpasses AS-GAE by 12.30 AUC on Email and 37.28 on Amazon, and outperforms AnomalyDAE by up to 40.54 AUC (Flickr). On the large-scale OGB-Proteins benchmark, CER-GOD (74.81) edges out TAM (74.49) and SL-GAD (73.71) by small but consistent margins, confirming scalability. Notably, CER-GOD also demonstrates the lowest variance across 10 runs on most datasets (e.g., ±0.08 on Email, ±0.16 on Flickr), indicating substantially more stable optimization than baselines like DOMINANT (±12.00 on Email) or CONAD (±25.52 on Email).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the deep embedded clustering framework of Guo et al. (2017) for the clustering layer and on graph autoencoder-based anomaly detection (DOMINANT, AnomalyDAE, AS-GAD) for the reconstruction component. It directly addresses the 'Homophily Trap' formalized by He et al. (2024) in ADA-GAD, but departs from prior graph-rewriting solutions (Dou et al., 2020; Qiao & Pang, 2023; Gasteiger et al., 2019) by proposing a lighter-weight edge reweighting mechanism that preserves original topology rather than adding or removing edges. The key conceptual departure is the joint optimization of a self-discriminative masking spoiler guided by clustering pseudo-labels via an MMD-based distribution repulsion loss, creating a closed-loop, threshold-free detection pipeline that no prior method achieves in this form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, end-to-end solution to a well-identified failure mode in GCN-based anomaly detection, and the threshold-free clustering-based scoring removes a practical pain point (manual threshold selection) that plagues many unsupervised detectors. The theoretical bound (Proposition 1) on influence decay with path length gives a clean formal justification for why local edge reweighting is sufficient. However, the method is limited to binary (normal vs. anomalous) detection, and multi-class or fine-grained anomaly typing remains open. The approach also does not dominate all benchmarks (GADAM outperforms on Cora and CiteSeer), suggesting the homophily trap is not the sole bottleneck across all graph structures.
