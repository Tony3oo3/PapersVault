## Problem

Standard conformal prediction applied to image segmentation constructs prediction sets pixel-by-pixel, ignoring the spatial correlations that are fundamental to image data. This results in overly conservative, combinatorially large, and visually incoherent uncertainty sets that include unrealistic label configurations. The paper asks whether spatial structure can be explicitly incorporated into conformal prediction sets to yield tighter, more interpretable uncertainty estimates while preserving formal coverage guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261468/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input any pre-trained segmentation model (U-Net with dropout, Probabilistic U-Net, or DeepLabV3+ ensemble) that can generate N_s sample softmax score maps of shape W×H×L for a given image. It produces an implicit set-valued prediction C*_λ(X) of full segmentation masks (W×H label maps over L classes) accompanied by a user-specified coverage guarantee P[Y_test ∈ C*_λ(X_test)] ≥ 1−α. The set is parameterized by only K=2 or 5 principal-component coefficients rather than W×H independent pixel decisions, and a secondary parameter β controls a label-wise accuracy tolerance for membership.

## Method

CONSIGN constructs spatially-aware prediction sets by performing a reduced SVD on the matrix of N_s sample score maps from the pre-trained model, extracting K principal directions u_k that capture the dominant modes of spatial uncertainty. For each direction, quantile-based bounds [A_k, B_k] are computed and scaled by the corresponding singular value Σ_kk and a tunable parameter λ, defining a K-dimensional box B in coefficient space. The prediction set C*_λ(X) is then the collection of all segmentations Y for which there exists a coefficient vector c ∈ B such that Y matches (within label-wise accuracy β) the argmax projection P(μ(X) + Σ c_k u_k). Calibration iteratively increases λ until the empirical miss rate on a held-out set falls below α − (1−α)/n, with membership tested by solving a bounded optimization problem via projected gradient descent. A key insight is that the nonlinear argmax quantization P(·) allows even a heavily truncated PCA (K=2 or 5) to reconstruct the ground truth for some coefficient choice, avoiding the special coverage procedures needed in the regression setting of Belhasin et al. (2023).

## Results

CONSIGN consistently produces smaller prediction set volumes (lower Chao estimator) than both the pixel-wise RAPS and spatial-aware SACP baselines across all five datasets, with differences reaching several orders of magnitude on the LIDC lung CT dataset. On the sEC metric, CONSIGN reaches the target coverage (1−α) with substantially fewer samples; in the COCO-vehicles experiment, just 10 sampled segmentations suffice to meet the user-defined coverage requirement. On the correlation metric, CONSIGN shows a monotonic increase in inter-sample Pearson correlation with sample size, indicating predictions are confined to a lower-dimensional manifold, whereas both baselines exhibit a decreasing trend toward near-independence. Computationally, CONSIGN has higher offline calibration time (~5–15 min vs. ~0.1–1 min for RAPS) but faster online sampling for small S (e.g., ~0.03 s vs. ~0.2–0.5 s for S=1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CONSIGN outperforms the pixel-wise RAPS baseline [Angelopoulos et al., 2020] on all three evaluation metrics across every dataset. The Chao estimator for CONSIGN is consistently lower, meaning the prediction set contains far fewer unique segmentations; on LIDC the gap reaches several orders of magnitude. RAPS treats each pixel independently, so its prediction set includes all combinatorial label configurations, producing spatially incoherent masks. Against the spatial-aware SACP baseline [Liu et al., 2025], CONSIGN also wins on all metrics, though the margin is smaller. The paper notes that SACP's neighborhood aggregation of softmax scores is essentially a post-processing step that does not effectively capture true spatial correlations, making its results 'comparable to the pixel-wise ones.' On the sEC metric, CONSIGN converges to the target coverage with fewer samples than either baseline, and on the correlation metric, CONSIGN's inter-sample correlation increases monotonically while both baselines decrease, confirming that CONSIGN's SVD-based construction confines predictions to a coherent low-dimensional subspace.

## Relation to Prior Work

CONSIGN adapts the SVD-based principal-direction uncertainty framework from image restoration (Belhasin et al., 2023; Nehme et al., 2023) to the segmentation setting, where the nonlinear argmax quantization step allows dramatic truncation of the basis (K=2 or 5 vs. the full WHL dimensions needed in regression). It is positioned within the conformal risk control (CRC) framework of Angelopoulos et al. (2024), but departs from the dominant pixel-wise CP-for-segmentation line (RAPS, SACP, and others) by constructing prediction sets that respect spatial correlations among pixels. The method is model-agnostic, requiring only that the pre-trained segmentation model can produce multiple sample outputs (via dropout, ensembles, or probabilistic architectures).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For safety-critical applications like medical imaging, this provides a practical path to statistically valid, spatially coherent uncertainty estimates without retraining the underlying model. Key limitations include the exchangeability assumption (no distribution-shift robustness), the implicit prediction-set form that makes membership testing computationally expensive, and the dependence on sample quality from the base model. Open questions include extending to 3D volumetric segmentation, handling distribution shift via adaptive conformal methods, and whether the small-K property generalizes to very high-label-count or fine-grained segmentation tasks.
