## Problem

No exact algorithm existed for computing Geodesic PCA (GPCA) in the Wasserstein space of probability measures on R^d. While Tangent PCA (TPCA) provides a computationally convenient linearized approximation, it can introduce significant distortion when data lie in high-curvature regions of the Wasserstein manifold, and the resulting components are not true geodesics. This paper fills the gap by providing exact GPCA algorithms for both centered Gaussian distributions and general absolutely continuous probability measures.

## Input / Output

The method takes a collection of n absolutely continuous probability measures (or n centered Gaussian distributions identified by their covariance matrices in S_d^{++}) and produces a sequence of orthogonally intersecting geodesic curves in the Wasserstein space, along with a scalar projection time t_i for each input measure onto each component, yielding a low-dimensional embedding. Unlike TPCA which operates on discrete empirical measures, GPCAGEN works directly on continuous distributions parameterized by neural networks, enabling sampling from any point along the recovered geodesics. In practice, inputs are 3D point clouds (ModelNet40), image color distributions (Landscape Pictures), or empirical 2×2 covariance matrices (Weather CORGIS).

## Method

For Gaussian distributions, the paper lifts the GPCA problem to the flat space GL_d of invertible matrices via Otto's fiber bundle structure, where Bures-Wasserstein geodesics become horizontal line segments and the distance reduces to the Frobenius norm; the optimization is over a base point A, a unit horizontal direction X, and rotation matrices Q_i representing each covariance in its fiber. For general a.c. measures (GPCAGEN), geodesics are parameterized as μ(t) = (id + t∇f_ψ)#(φ_θ#ρ) using two MLPs for the diffeomorphism φ_θ and scalar function f_ψ, with the Sinkhorn divergence serving as a differentiable surrogate for W_2². The algorithm jointly optimizes network parameters and per-sample projection times t_i via stochastic gradient descent, while higher-order components are enforced to intersect and be orthogonal through explicit L²(ρ) regularization terms on the vector fields ∇f_ψ(φ_θ). A key design choice is using Otto's parametrization rather than McCann's convex interpolation, which avoids architectural constraints like input-convex neural networks at the cost of monitoring Hessian eigenvalues to ensure id + t∇f_ψ remains a diffeomorphism.

## Results

For randomly generated Gaussian covariance matrices (n=50, 100 trials), GPCA reduces the projection cost (Eq. 11) by less than 1% relative to TPCA on average, confirming TPCA as a good approximation in the generic case. However, for covariance matrices with identical eigenvalues but varying orientations (|a−b|/|a+b| ≈ 0.8), GPCA yields substantially different components from TPCA, with the first geodesic no longer passing through the Wasserstein barycenter. On a.c. probability measures, GPCAGEN successfully recovers two orthogonally intersecting geodesics on MNIST (digit shape + color), 3D point clouds (chairs, lamps), and landscape images (brightness, color hue), producing smooth continuous distributions along each component. The paper does not report a single unified quantitative benchmark table; comparisons are primarily qualitative (visual inspection of recovered geodesics) supplemented by the cost-improvement ratio in the Gaussian case.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TPCA (Wang et al., 2013), GPCA/GPCAGEN wins in regimes of high curvature: for Gaussian matrices with large eigenvalue spread (|a−b|/|a+b| ≈ 0.8), the first GPCA component deviates substantially from the TPCA component and avoids the barycenter, while TPCA preserves radial distances but distorts pairwise distances. For generic random matrices the two methods agree to within ~1% cost, so TPCA remains a practical approximation. Against PointNet + PCA (Qi et al., 2017), GPCAGEN produces smoother, more interpretable components (e.g., chair type and seat height) whereas PointNet+PCA yields limited separability, initialization-sensitive results, and requires a large pretrained autoencoder. TPCA on 3D point clouds (Figures 15–16) exhibits visible discretization artifacts—holes and excessive mass concentration—because it operates on discrete empirical measures, while GPCAGEN operates on continuous distributions and enables sampling at any point along the geodesic. Overall, GPCA/GPCAGEN is preferred when geometric fidelity and interpretability matter; TPCA is preferred for speed in low-curvature settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Geodesic PCA framework of Huckemann et al. (2010) from abstract Riemannian manifolds to the specific setting of Wasserstein space, filling a gap left by prior work that was limited to 1D (Bigot et al., 2017), approximate generalized geodesics (Seguy & Cuturi, 2015), or linearized tangent PCA (Wang et al., 2013). The Gaussian case leverages the Bures-Wasserstein fiber bundle structure from Takatsu (2011) and Otto (2001) to lift geodesic optimization to the flat space of invertible matrices, while the general a.c. measure case introduces a novel neural-network parametrization of Otto's geodesics using MLPs for both the diffeomorphism φ and the potential function f, avoiding the convexity constraint of McCann's interpolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to compute exact GPCA for R^d-valued probability measures, providing a principled alternative to TPCA that avoids linearization distortion. The finding that GPCA components need not pass through the Wasserstein barycenter—and can in pathological cases yield worse separation than TPCA—challenges the intuition that geodesic methods are uniformly superior to their tangent-space approximations. Open questions include whether the equivalence between full-space and Gaussian-restricted GPCA extends beyond one dimension, and whether the MLP-based geodesic parametrization scales to higher-dimensional or larger-dataset settings. The outlier detection application demonstrates practical utility beyond pure dimensionality reduction.
