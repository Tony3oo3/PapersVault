## Problem

MPC-based privacy-preserving machine learning has been applied to LLM inference but not fine-tuning, because backward propagation, optimizer steps, and softmax-based self-attention introduce prohibitive communication overhead under MPC (e.g., ~10 min/sample and ~970 GB communication for a single RoBERTa_LARGE SFT iteration). No prior work has explored MPC-based privacy-preserving fine-tuning of LLMs, leaving a gap between the strong privacy guarantees of MPC and the practical need to adapt LLMs to sensitive domains like healthcare and finance.

## Input / Output

The data owner locally initializes a prompt embedding vector (50 tokens × 1024-dim for RoBERTa_LARGE), concatenates it with tokenized fine-tuning sequences, and secret-shares the resulting input embedding into two shares distributed to two non-colluding servers. The servers hold secret shares of the frozen model parameters. The output is a fine-tuned prompt embedding (a low-dimensional latent vector z ∈ R^500 mapped through a random projection to the full prompt space), which the data owner retains locally; the servers never observe the updated prompt, enabling a black-box/API-style deployment where per-query uploads are only 12–44 KB.

## Method

SecP-Tuning combines two key mechanisms to eliminate the dominant MPC bottlenecks. First, it adopts Forward-only Tuning (FoT) with a 'Server-Client' architecture: the two servers execute only privacy-preserving forward inference via MPC, while the data owner reconstructs the output, computes the loss in plaintext, and runs the CMA-ES gradient-free optimizer locally—completely removing backward propagation and optimizer MPC computations. Second, it replaces softmax-based self-attention with a privacy-preserving Random Feature Attention (RFA) protocol that reduces attention complexity from O(n²d) to O(ndr) and avoids exponentiation and maximum operations; a dedicated single-round MPC cosine protocol (Π_cosine) based on trigonometric addition identities handles the remaining MPC-unfriendly cosine operations. The entire system operates under 2-out-of-2 arithmetic secret sharing with a semi-honest threat model, and the data owner's local GFO loop iterates until convergence, producing the final prompt embedding without ever transmitting gradients or updated parameters to the servers.

## Results

SecP-Tuning achieves approximately 12× end-to-end speedup over SFT (55.17s vs. 651.60s) and 16× over gradient-based prompt tuning (55.17s vs. 882.08s) in a LAN setting, while reducing communication overhead by 17× (56.5 GB vs. 970.7 GB) and 20× (56.5 GB vs. 1,116.2 GB), respectively. On five few-shot NLU tasks with RoBERTa_LARGE (16 samples/class), SecP-Tuning without pre-trained prompts attains an average score of 76.58, comparable to SFT (79.90) and substantially above gradient-based prompt tuning (64.07). With pre-trained prompt embeddings, the underlying FoT method reaches 83.14 average, surpassing SFT. In WAN settings (100 Mbps/80 ms), the speedup over prompt tuning amplifies to ~34× due to the drastic reduction in communication rounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against gradient-based Prompt Tuning (Lester et al., 2021), SecP-Tuning wins decisively on both efficiency and accuracy: it is 16× faster (55.17s vs. 882.08s per iteration) and uses 20× less communication (56.5 GB vs. 1,116.2 GB), while improving average task performance from 64.07 to 76.58. The accuracy gap is most pronounced on SST-2 (88.11 vs. 68.23, +19.9 pts), Yelp Polarity (85.23 vs. 61.02, +24.2 pts), and MRPC (75.33 vs. 51.61, +23.7 pts); SecP-Tuning trails slightly on AG's News (81.27 vs. 84.81) and RTE (52.95 vs. 54.69). Against full-parameter SFT, SecP-Tuning is 12× faster and 17× lighter in communication, with a modest 3.3-point average accuracy drop (76.58 vs. 79.90), which the authors attribute to the few-shot setting where gradient-based methods overfit. When pre-trained prompt embeddings (Gu et al., 2022) are added, the FoT-based SecP-Tuning surpasses SFT on average (83.14 vs. 79.90), suggesting the gradient-free exploration mechanism finds better optima in low-data regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) Forward-only Tuning (FoT) / Black-box Tuning (Sun et al., 2022), which uses gradient-free optimization (CMA-ES) to update prompt embeddings without backpropagation, and (2) Random Feature Attention (Peng et al., 2021), which approximates softmax attention via kernel tricks to achieve linear complexity. The key departure is embedding both of these within a 2-party MPC framework (CrypTen) for the first time, addressing the specific MPC-unfriendly operations (exponentiation, max, division in softmax; complex linear algebra in CMA-ES) that make prior MPC-based PPML infeasible for LLM fine-tuning. Unlike prior HE-based PFT work (BlindTuner, PrivTuner), which relies on costly re-encryption for nonlinearities, this paper exploits MPC's multi-round communication to handle nonlinear operations while offloading GFO and loss computation to the data owner's local environment via a 'Server-Client' architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that MPC-based privacy-preserving fine-tuning of Transformer LLMs is practically feasible, with 12–16× end-to-end speedups and 17–20× communication reductions over gradient-based baselines. The 'black-box/API-style' deployability (data owner never exposes updated parameters to the server) addresses a real privacy gap that gradient-based PFT cannot close. However, all experiments use RoBERTa_LARGE (~125M parameters) under a semi-honest threat model; scalability to 7B+ LLMs, robustness under malicious adversaries, and whether RFA's approximation error degrades on tasks requiring precise attention (e.g., long-range dependency) remain open. The WAN amplification (34× vs 16×) suggests the approach becomes more attractive in bandwidth-constrained deployments, but this has not been validated at scale.
