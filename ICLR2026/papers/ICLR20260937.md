## Problem

Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) consistently generalizes worse than reinforcement learning (RL) for LLM post-training, yet RL requires explicit reward signals, large compute, and careful tuning that are often impractical. A key open question is whether SFT itself can be fundamentally improved in its native setting—where only positive expert demonstrations are available and no reward model or negative samples exist. This paper identifies the precise mathematical mechanism behind SFT's generalization gap and proposes a minimal fix.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard SFT training pairs (text prompt x, expert response y*) with no additional inputs: no reward model, no preference pairs, no negative samples, and no reference model. It produces a fine-tuned language model via a single-line modification to the per-token cross-entropy loss. The approach is modality-agnostic, demonstrated on text-only math/code tasks and vision-language reasoning, and operates under both full-parameter and LoRA-based PEFT training.

## Method

The paper shows that the SFT gradient can be rewritten as an on-policy policy gradient where the implicit reward is a sparse indicator (nonzero only for exact expert matches) multiplied by an importance weight 1/π_θ(y|x). This inverse-probability weighting causes disproportionately large gradients whenever the model assigns low probability to expert tokens, creating an ill-posed reward landscape that drives overfitting and instability. DFT (Dynamic Fine-Tuning) rectifies this by multiplying the per-token loss by the stop-gradient of the model's token probability: L_DFT = -Σ_t sg(π_θ(y_t*|y_<t*, x)) · log π_θ(y_t*|y_<t*, x). The stop-gradient blocks backpropagation through the weighting term, so the effective gradient becomes -∇_θ π_θ(y*|x) (uniform magnitude) rather than -(1/π_θ)∇_θ π_θ(y*|x) (CE), neutralizing the inverse-probability distortion. In RL terms, the corrected reward becomes uniformly 1 across all expert tokens—akin to verification-based RLVR—yielding stable, uniformly weighted updates that generalize better without any additional sampling, reward modeling, or reference model.

## Results

On the primary mathematical reasoning evaluation (Average@16 accuracy across Math500, Minerva Math, Olympiad Bench, AIME 2024, and AMC 2023), DFT achieves an average of 31.58 on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, a +15.66 point gain over the base model—5.9× larger than SFT's +2.09 gain. The advantage generalizes across model families: Qwen2.5-Math-7B reaches 37.15 (vs. SFT 23.62), DeepSeekMath-7B reaches 18.15 (vs. SFT 9.82), and LLaMA-3.1-8B reaches 11.02 (vs. SFT 6.33). In the offline RL setting, DFT attains 35.43 average, surpassing the strongest online RL baseline GRPO (32.00) by +3.43 points and the best offline method RFT (23.97) by +11.46 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the offline RL setting (Table 2, Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B), DFT (35.43 avg) decisively outperforms both offline baselines: DPO (23.20, +12.23 gap) and RFT (23.97, +11.46 gap). DFT also surpasses both online RL methods—PPO (28.66, +6.77 gap) and GRPO (32.00, +3.43 gap)—despite requiring no reference model, no iterative reward modeling, and no online sampling. Against the concurrent method iw-SFT (Qin & Springenberg, 2025), DFT wins in the standard SFT setting (31.58 vs. 30.28 avg on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B) and in the offline RL setting (35.43 vs. 31.86), while iw-SFT edges out DFT only on Qwen2.5-Math-7B in the SFT setting (39.60 vs. 37.15). Notably, DFT avoids the degradation SFT suffers on hard benchmarks (e.g., Olympiad Bench drops from 15.88 to 12.63 under SFT but rises to 27.08 under DFT), whereas iw-SFT also underperforms SFT on several benchmarks for smaller models (e.g., LLaMA-3.2-3B Math500: 5.13 vs. SFT's 8.65). PPO's token-level importance sampling is the direct technical inspiration for DFT's token-level reweighting, but DFT applies it to the SFT loss rather than as a trust-region constraint.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the formal connection between SFT and policy gradient, extending prior heuristic observations (e.g., Du et al. 2025, Wang et al. 2025) into a precise mathematical equivalence that identifies the inverse-probability importance weight as the specific source of SFT's generalization deficit. It departs from concurrent work like iw-SFT (Qin & Springenberg, 2025) by deriving the correction directly from the model's own token probabilities rather than requiring a separate reference policy, and it explicitly inverts the weighting philosophy of Focal Loss: where Focal Loss upweights hard examples, DFT downweights them to promote generalization. The token-level application of importance sampling follows PPO's standard treatment, but the paper's contribution is the theoretical diagnosis (sparse, inverse-probability reward) that motivates the specific rectification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single-line modification to the standard cross-entropy loss (multiplying by the stop-gradient of token probability) yields improvements of 1.4×–5.9× over SFT across models and tasks, and in offline settings outperforms both DPO/RFT and online methods like GRPO/PPO without requiring a reference model, reward model, or online sampling. This makes it a highly accessible drop-in improvement for any SFT pipeline. However, the paper honestly identifies a key limitation—DFT underperforms SFT on factual knowledge acquisition (Natural Questions), where the model needs to absorb information outside its prior competence—and the evaluation scope remains concentrated on mathematical reasoning with models up to 7B parameters. Open questions include whether quality-aware or non-uniform reward assignments can address the underrepresented-domain weakness, and whether the gains persist at larger scales and on broader task categories.
