## Problem

Diffusion- and flow-based robot policies are fundamentally limited by the cost of collecting large-scale interaction data, and conventional post-training strategies (fine-tuning, RL) are expensive and complex. Prior composition work (e.g., PoCo) used static, task-agnostic weightings and lacked theoretical justification for why combining policies should help. This paper asks whether the distributional scores of multiple pre-trained policies can be combined at test time to yield a strictly stronger policy than any individual parent, without any additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265186/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GPC does not alter the input or output interface of any individual policy: each parent policy still receives its own conditioning (RGB image, point cloud, or language instruction) and produces a score estimate at every denoising step. The composition operates entirely in the latent score space during the iterative denoising loop, and the final output remains a single action trajectory (a chunk of joint actions). A key practical detail is that policies with mismatched action-chunk lengths or inference-step counts can be composed by sampling a shared noise trajectory of the longer length and applying the shorter policy only on the overlapping prefix.

## Method

GPC is a training-free, test-time framework that, at each denoising step, queries n pre-trained policies to obtain their score (or noise-prediction) estimates and forms a convex combination ŝ_comp = Σ wᵢ s_θ(τ_t, t, cᵢ) with Σwᵢ = 1, then uses this composed score in the standard Langevin/ODE update. The weights are found by a simple test-time search: sweeping w₁ over {0.0, 0.1, …, 1.0}, running rollouts for each configuration, and selecting the weight that maximizes success rate. The theoretical contribution proves two results: (i) the mean-squared score error of the convex combination is a convex quadratic in w whose minimum is no worse than the better individual estimator (Prop. 4.1), and (ii) a Grönwall-type stability bound shows that this per-step score improvement propagates to a strictly tighter terminal trajectory error bound (Prop. 4.2). The framework is modality- and architecture-agnostic, composing VA with VLA models, diffusion with flow-matching policies, and RGB with point-cloud inputs, and extends to superposition operators (logical AND/OR) for stronger composition.

## Results

On Robomimic and PushT, GPC achieves average success rate improvements of +2.22% to +7.55% over the best individual base policy, with the largest gain (Florence-Policy-F + FP) reaching 86.39% average SR versus 78.84% for the best single policy. On RoboTwin (6 bimanual tasks), GPC yields up to +7% average SR improvement (RDT + DP_pcd: 0.72 vs. 0.65 for DP_pcd alone). In real-world evaluations across 4 tasks, GPC consistently outperforms both base policies, e.g., 13/20 on Place Bottles vs. 7/20 (DP_img) and 11/20 (DP_pcd).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPC consistently outperforms all six individual base policies across every evaluated benchmark. Against Diffusion Policy (DP, Chi et al.), GPC improves average SR by +2.22% when paired with Mamba Policy and by +5.51% when paired with Florence-D on Robomimic/PushT. Against Mamba Policy (MP, Cao et al.), GPC yields +3.35% (Florence-D + MP) and +25.73% under logical AND composition (Table 4). Against Florence-based MDT (Reuss et al.), GPC achieves +5.51% (Florence-D + DP) and +7.55% (Florence-F + FP), with the flow-matching variant showing the largest absolute gain. Against π0 (Black et al.), GPC (π0 + FP) reaches 88.94% average SR, surpassing π0 alone (86.42%) by +2.52%. Against 3D Diffusion Policy / DP3 (Ze et al., used as DP_pcd), GPC (DP_img + DP_pcd) improves RoboTwin average from 0.65 to 0.70 (+5%), and in real-world Place Bottles from 11/20 to 13/20. Against RDT (Liu et al.), GPC (RDT + DP_pcd) achieves 0.72 average on RoboTwin, a +7% gain over DP_pcd and a +32% gain over RDT alone (0.40), demonstrating that composition can substantially rescue a weaker VLA policy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compositional generative modeling lineage (compositional EBMs, classifier-free guidance, and the superposition of diffusion models) into the robot policy domain, and directly builds on PoCo's policy composition framework by adding task-dependent convex weight searching and a theoretical stability guarantee (Grönwall-type bound) that prior composition work lacked. Unlike PoCo, which performed static constraint-based composition without exploring inter-policy weights, GPC treats the composition weights as a test-time optimization variable and demonstrates that the optimal mix is highly task-dependent. The method also generalizes beyond the single-architecture, single-modality setting of prior work to compose heterogeneous policies spanning diffusion and flow-matching backbones, VA and VLA modalities, and RGB versus point-cloud inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GPC offers a practical, training-free route to improving robot policies at a time when scaling interaction data is the primary bottleneck, and the theoretical result that convex score composition provably reduces trajectory error provides a principled justification rather than a purely empirical heuristic. Key limitations include the test-time search cost (roughly 1–2 hours per task), the restriction to 2–3 policies in practice, and the finding that composition cannot rescue a very weak policy (Finding 2). Open questions include adaptive or learned weight selection, scaling to many policies efficiently, and whether the approach transfers to non-diffusion/flow policy parameterizations such as autoregressive VLA models.
