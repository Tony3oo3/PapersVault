## Problem

Video generative models used as visual planners for robotics are limited to purely offline data, even when augmented with internet-scale pretraining, and cannot continuously improve from self-collected experience on novel tasks. This paper asks how a visual planner can iteratively self-improve on a specified task through its own online interactions, without requiring expert-quality demonstrations or human-defined ground-truth reward functions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264310/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The visual planner takes a single current observation frame (512×512 RGB in simulation; camera feed in real-world) and a natural-language task prompt, and synthesizes a sequence of 8 future frames via DDIM sampling (25 steps). These frames are translated into executable robotic actions by a separately trained inverse dynamics model (MLP-IDM for real-world, Diffusion-IDM for simulation), with a frame skip of 1 in MetaWorld and 16 for the Panda arm. After self-improvement, the final video model can optionally be distilled into a lightweight diffusion policy that maps observations directly to action chunks of size 8 for fast deployment.

## Method

SILVR iteratively fine-tunes an in-domain text-to-video model (AVDC-based U-Net with CLIP cross-attention) on self-collected trajectories over K iterations. Each iteration optionally composes the in-domain model's score with a frozen internet-scale prior (AnimateDiff, ~2B params) via Inverse Probabilistic Adaptation to improve zero-shot generalization, then deploys the adapted model as a visual planner to collect N rollouts, filters successful trajectories using a sparse reward signal (ground-truth, VLM-judged, or no filtering), and fine-tunes the in-domain model (and optionally the IDM) on the accumulated data. The key architectural insight is that visual planning decouples environment dynamics modeling from action prediction, yielding stronger base generalization and more sample-efficient self-improvement than direct behavior cloning or RL fine-tuning of diffusion policies. The framework is robust to suboptimal initial data (70% random actions) and can replace human-defined success functions with VLM-based scoring (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro) without inhibiting iterative gains.

## Results

On 12 unseen MetaWorld tasks, SILVR (with ground-truth filtering) improves from 14.7% success rate at iteration 0 to 44.2% at iteration 4, and continues to 53.1% by iteration 10 — a 285% relative improvement over 10 iterations. The SILVR-distilled diffusion policy further boosts performance to 49.2% at iteration 4, surpassing the visual planner teacher. SILVR with VLM-based filtering (Gemini-2.5-Pro) still reaches 38.4% at iteration 4, confirming robustness to the quality of the success signal. On real-robot Panda arm tasks (cup pushing, drawer opening), SILVR with internet-scale video prior (AnimateDiff) shows consistent iterative improvement on novel color combinations, while the in-domain model alone fails to improve or even degrades.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SILVR decisively outperforms both baselines across all iterations. DSRL (Wagenmaker et al., 2025), which applies reinforcement learning to finetune a frozen diffusion policy, actually degrades from 9.4% to 7.7% over 4 iterations, indicating that RL finetuning is sample-inefficient for novel tasks and cannot bootstrap a useful value function from limited experience. BCIL (iterative behavior cloning on successful rollouts) improves from 5.6% to 23.2% but saturates quickly after iteration 2, never exceeding 24%. SILVR with ground-truth filtering reaches 44.2% at iteration 4 — roughly 2× BCIL and 6× DSRL — and continues improving to 53.1% by iteration 10. The paper attributes SILVR's advantage to visual planning's decoupling of dynamics modeling from action prediction, which provides stronger base generalization and more transferable training signal from pixels than from low-dimensional actions. The distilled diffusion policy from SILVR's final iteration (49.2%) also outperforms BCIL's best (23.2%), confirming that the self-improvement gains from visual planning transfer effectively to a lightweight deployment policy.

## Relation to Prior Work

SILVR builds directly on the UniPi framework for text-conditioned visual planning via video generation and inverse dynamics, and on Inverse Probabilistic Adaptation (Adapt2Act) for composing in-domain and internet-scale video models. It departs from prior visual planning work, which is purely offline, by introducing an iterative self-improvement loop where the video model finetunes on its own environment-collected trajectories—an idea borrowed from self-improving LLMs and VideoAgent but applied here to robotic visual planning. The paper also positions itself against RL-based finetuning of Diffusion Policy (DSRL, DPPO) and behavior cloning improvement loops, arguing that the decoupled dynamics-modeling + action-translation structure of visual planning is inherently more amenable to iterative self-improvement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SILVR demonstrates a practical pathway for robots to improve on novel tasks without human-labeled rewards or expert demonstrations, validated on both simulation and a real Franka Panda arm, and the final distilled policy offers fast deployment. The sample efficiency over RL finetuning (DSRL) is practically significant for real-world settings where interaction is expensive. Key open questions remain: the cold-start problem when initial success is near zero, the observed saturation around iteration 5 (possibly due to local minima in strategy), and how to introduce principled exploration into the visual planning loop to avoid unimodal behavior. The reliance on a reasonable initial success rate limits applicability to very challenging tasks.
