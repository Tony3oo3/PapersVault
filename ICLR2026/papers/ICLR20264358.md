## Problem

Existing video matting methods are predominantly human-portrait-specific and rely on first-frame masks that are difficult to obtain for transparent or intricate objects like fire, smoke, or bubbles. Additionally, no comprehensive benchmark exists to evaluate generalization of video matting models across diverse natural object domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264358/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MAM2 accepts a single user prompt (point, box, or mask) on the first frame of an RGB video sequence and produces per-frame alpha mattes, segmentation masks, and trimaps for all subsequent frames without further interaction. The lightweight matter module operates on 1024×1024 patches, and the model requires no auxiliary segmentation model to generate the first-frame mask, unlike prior mask-guided methods that deploy SAM-ViT-Huge or SAM2-Base as separate front-ends.

## Method

MAM2 builds on SAM2 with a LoRA-finetuned image encoder and introduces two key components. The Promptable Dual-mode Decoder (PDD) extends SAM2's mask decoder to simultaneously output a segmentation mask and a trimap, using a mask-guided fusion module that concatenates a sigmoid-normalized mask augment feature with segmentation and trimap features before a lightweight fusion step, yielding substantially more stable trimaps than a simple parallel branch. The Memory-Separable Siamese (MSS) mechanism addresses temporal collapse on transparent objects by performing a second PDD pass on memory-free image features, using the first-pass mask as a pseudo-prompt, thereby decoupling trimap decoding from the interfering mask-memory embedding; the two passes share parameters, adding zero extra parameters. Training uses a selective supervision scheme: Stage 1 optimizes main parameters with mixed VOS/VM/IM data (mask loss for segmentation data, trimap loss for matting data), and Stage 2 fine-tunes only the MEMatte-based lightweight matter on image matting data for high-fidelity alpha prediction.

## Results

On the newly introduced NOVM benchmark (diverse natural objects), MAM2 reduces MAD from 39.44 (MatAnyone, previous SOTA) to 14.72, and MSE from 25.63 to 3.70, while also improving GRAD from 89.60 to 23.54 and dtSSD from 4.10 to 2.65. On the human-centric YoutubeMatte benchmark, MAM2 achieves MAD 1.16 and MSE 0.24, outperforming MatAnyone (MAD 2.05, MSE 0.76). Notably, MAM2 achieves these results using only Box&Point prompts (no first-frame mask required), whereas all baselines require a mask or trimap input. In automatic mode on YoutubeMatte, MAM2 further reduces MAD to 1.19 versus MatAnyone's 3.70.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAM2 decisively outperforms all four video matting baselines (TCVOM, FTP-VM, MaGGIe, MatAnyone) across every metric on both NOVM and YoutubeMatte. The largest gap is on NOVM, where MAM2's MAD of 14.72 is roughly 2.7× lower than the next-best MatAnyone (39.44) and 3.8× lower than TCVOM (56.18), reflecting the difficulty of diverse natural objects for prior methods. On YoutubeMatte, the advantage is more modest but consistent: MAM2 (MAD 1.16) beats MatAnyone (2.05) by ~43% and TCVOM (1.57) by ~26%. For image matting on AIM-500, MAM2 with a Box prompt (MSE 4.24, Grad 13.88) surpasses SDMatte* (MSE 4.91, Grad 15.84), SEMatte (MSE 7.65, Grad 16.06), SmartMatting (MSE 7.65, Grad 27.16), and Matting Anything (MSE 11.60, Grad 21.04). A key differentiator is that MAM2 achieves all these results without requiring a first-frame mask or trimap—it accepts only points or boxes—whereas TCVOM and FTP-VM need trimaps and MaGGIe and MatAnyone need masks, making MAM2's practical deployment simpler (0 auxiliary model parameters vs. 80.8M for MaGGIe and 636M for MatAnyone).

## Relation to Prior Work

MAM2 builds directly on SAM2's interactive video segmentation framework, inheriting its multi-prompt support (points, boxes, masks) and memory-based temporal propagation, while extending the decoder to simultaneously predict segmentation masks and trimaps via the Promptable Dual-mode Decoder (PDD). It departs from the dominant human-centric video matting line (MatAnyone, MaGGIe, TCVOM, FTP-VM) by targeting diverse natural objects and by eliminating the need for a first-frame mask through flexible prompting. The trimap-guided matting paradigm, long established in natural image matting, is transplanted into the video setting, and the Memory-Separable Siamese mechanism specifically addresses a failure mode of SAM2's memory attention that prior video matting work did not encounter because it did not attempt trimap prediction from memory-embedded features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAM2 establishes the first practical video matting model that handles diverse objects (fire, water, plants, bubbles, etc.) with flexible user prompts, and the NOVM benchmark fills a conspicuous gap in evaluation for general video matting beyond portraits. The paper identifies and resolves a concrete architectural conflict—mask memory embedding interfering with trimap decoding—that is likely relevant to any future work extending SAM2's decoder to multi-output prediction. Remaining open questions include handling extremely low-opacity regions (dissipating smoke) and the fundamental problem of extracting clean foregrounds from alpha mattes of transparent objects, which the authors acknowledge as unsolved.
