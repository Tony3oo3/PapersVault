## Problem

Existing text-conditioned human-human interaction (HHI) generation methods compress both agents' motions into a single flat latent representation, entangling individual identity with shared interaction context. This leads to semantic misalignment, limited expressivity for fine-grained coordination, and physically implausible artifacts such as hand penetration or missed contact. The paper addresses the absence of structured, hierarchical interaction representations and the lack of prior-based supervision for physically realistic contact.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language prompt describing a two-person interaction (e.g., 'Person A hands an object to Person B') and produces temporally synchronized 3D motion sequences for both agents. It supports two distinct skeleton representations: the AMASS format with 22 joints (262-dim per frame) on InterHuman, and the SMPL-X format with 55 joints including hands and face (336-dim per frame) on InterX, demonstrating compatibility with diverse pose structures without requiring a unified representation.

## Method

DHVAE disentangles the HHI latent space into three components—z_a and z_b for individual agent motions and z_o for global interaction context—using a CoTransformer module that cross-attends between individual encoders with skip connections to jointly model mutual awareness while preserving agent identity. A contrastive triplet loss is applied to z_o using physically grounded positive/negative pairs (small jitter for contact, larger shift for non-contact, implausible displacement for negatives) to encourage a discriminative and physically plausible interaction latent space. Generation proceeds via DDIM-based latent diffusion over the structured latents, using a 13-layer AdaLN-Transformer denoiser with U-Net-style skip connections, segment positional encoding to reflect each token's role, and token scaling to calibrate feature magnitudes across heterogeneous latent groups. Classifier-free guidance with strength 3.0–3.5 is applied during inference for text alignment.

## Results

On InterHuman, DHVAE achieves the best FID of 5.015, R-Precision@1 of 0.496, and MM Dist of 3.772, outperforming the closest competitor TIMotion (FID 5.600, R-Prec@1 0.485) and InterMask (FID 5.153, R-Prec@1 0.449). On InterX, DHVAE reaches FID 0.339, R-Precision@1 0.442, and MM Dist 3.604, again leading all baselines. The model is also the lightest (56M parameters) and fastest (0.454 s per sentence inference), roughly 2.3× faster than InterMask (1.021 s) and 3.2× faster than TIMotion (1.472 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DHVAE achieves the best score on every reported metric across both benchmarks, making it the first method since InterMask to improve simultaneously on all core metrics. Against the strongest recent baselines, the gains are consistent but moderate: on InterHuman, FID improves from 5.600 (TIMotion) and 5.153 (InterMask) to 5.015, and R-Prec@1 rises from 0.485/0.449 to 0.496; on InterX, FID drops from 0.385 (TIMotion) and 0.399 (InterMask) to 0.339, with R-Prec@1 increasing from 0.412/0.403 to 0.442. Against older or single-agent baselines (InterGen, MDM, ComMDM, T2M, MLD), the margins are substantially larger—e.g., on InterX, FID improves from 23.7 (MDM) or 29.3 (ComMDM) to 0.339, and R-Prec@1 from 0.203/0.090 to 0.442. In physical-plausibility evaluation (Table 4), DHVAE reduces penetration volume to 0.390 (vs. 0.873 for InterMask and 0.485 for TIMotion) while achieving the highest contact ratio of 0.581. The only metric where a competitor appears stronger is InterLDM's reported MM Dist of 1.862 on InterHuman, but the authors note this value is inconsistent with InterLDM's own R-Precision and could not be reproduced, so it is excluded from the main table. DHVAE also matches or exceeds all baselines in computational efficiency, running at 0.454 s/sentence with 56 M parameters versus 1.021 s/74 M (InterMask) and 1.472 s/77 M (TIMotion).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent diffusion paradigm for motion generation (MLD, Rombach et al.) to the human-human interaction setting, departing from the flat unified latent used by InterLDM and InterMask by proposing a three-component disentangled hierarchy (individual latents z_a, z_b plus a shared interaction latent z_o). The CoTransformer module for cross-agent encoding and the AdaLN-Transformer denoiser follow the conditioning architecture introduced by InterGen, while the DDIM sampling process is inherited directly from Song et al. The key conceptual departure is the contrastive triplet loss on the interaction latent, which imposes prior-based physical plausibility supervision absent in prior HHI methods that rely solely on pairwise distance penalties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DHVAE establishes a new SOTA on both InterHuman and InterX while being the lightest (56M) and fastest (0.454s) model among compared methods, suggesting that structured latent decomposition can outperform larger flat representations for HHI. The contrastive learning strategy for physical plausibility—using simple spatial perturbations to construct positive/negative pairs—is a lightweight mechanism that could transfer to other interaction generation tasks. However, the method remains limited to dyadic interactions, still produces artifacts on SMPLX-based data, and lacks explicit physics-based post-hoc correction; extending to multi-agent scenarios and developing HHI-specific evaluation metrics beyond FID/R-Precision remain open challenges.
