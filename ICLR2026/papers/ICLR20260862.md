## Problem

Existing cell instance segmentation methods rely on local pixel-wise or geometric cues and lack an intrinsic mechanism to model global topological constraints among cells, leading to systematic failures in dense, overlapping tissues. While graph-coloring-based approaches offer a promising global-topology paradigm, the bipartite assumption underlying efficient 2-coloring has never been validated on real-world data, and the more general 4-coloring model risks unnecessary representational redundancy. This paper reveals through the first systematic chromatic analysis that real-world cell adjacency graphs are fundamentally non-bipartite with dense conflict clusters (up to 30% conflict nodes), creating a 'Goldilocks' gap between insufficient 2-coloring and redundant 4-coloring.

## Input / Output

The method takes 256×256 pixel patches from H&E-stained (bright-field) or fluorescence microscopy images and produces a per-pixel cell instance segmentation mask reconstructed via topological decoding of the predicted coloring map. Uniquely, it also outputs a 'Conflict Map' that explicitly identifies topologically complex regions (nodes involved in odd cycles), serving as a novel interpretability tool for quantifying tissue complexity. The network's coloring head outputs a 4-channel probability map (background + 2 bipartite colors + 1 conflict color), from which instances are recovered by treating the two bipartite colors as distinct instance labels and resolving conflict regions through learned feature separability.

## Method

Disco employs a 'divide and conquer' strategy on the Cell Adjacency Graph (CAG) built from ground-truth masks. First, an 'Explicit Marking' stage uses BFS to extract the maximal bipartite subgraph, assigning two primary colors to the vast majority of cells and a single dedicated conflict color to the remaining non-bipartite 'conflict set,' producing a topologically-informed (t+1)-value supervisory map. Second, an 'Implicit Disambiguation' mechanism resolves secondary conflicts (adjacent conflict nodes sharing the same discrete label) through an Adjacency Constraint Loss that minimizes cosine similarity between the mean softmax probability vectors of all graph-adjacent instances, effectively acting as a supervised contrastive loss that drives angular separation in the continuous feature space. The architecture is a Swin-Unet with dual heads (semantic segmentation + coloring), trained end-to-end with a five-component decoupled loss (semantic, coloring, bipartite-consistency, conflict-resolution, and adjacency-constraint) that jointly enforces correct labeling and instance-level feature discriminability.

## Results

Disco achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four datasets. On the most challenging GBC-FS 2025 dataset (30.49% conflict node ratio), Disco attains 50.87% PQ and 52.09% AJI, a 7.08% absolute PQ improvement over the second-best method FCIS (43.79% PQ). On the simpler PanNuke dataset (100% bipartite), Disco still leads with 62.71% PQ and 65.66% AJI. Across all four datasets, Disco delivers an average 2.72% PQ improvement over the best competing method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Disco outperforms all 8 baselines on every metric across all four datasets. The closest competitor is FCIS (ICML 2025), the only other graph-coloring-based method; Disco beats it by 7.08% PQ on GBC-FS 2025, 1.62% PQ on PanNuke, 0.42% PQ on DSB2018, and 1.77% PQ on CryoNuSeg. Among distance/contour-based methods, CellPose (Nat. Methods 2025) is the strongest non-graph baseline, but Disco surpasses it by 8.69% PQ on GBC-FS 2025 and 3.53% PQ on PanNuke. The SAM-based Un-SAM trails Disco by 9.94% PQ on GBC-FS 2025. Notably, on DSB2018 FCIS holds a marginal DQ edge (0.8806 vs. 0.8753), but Disco compensates with a clear SQ advantage (0.8862 vs. 0.8789), yielding a net PQ win. The gap widens dramatically with topological complexity: the PQ margin over FCIS grows from 1.62% (PanNuke, 0% conflict) to 7.08% (GBC-FS 2025, 30.49% conflict), confirming that Disco's conflict-aware design is specifically effective where standard methods fail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graph-coloring paradigm for cell instance segmentation introduced by FCIS (Zhang et al., ICML 2025), which applied the Four-Color Theorem to predict a 4-coloring of the cell adjacency graph. Disco departs from FCIS by rejecting the monolithic high-chromaticity approach in favor of a 'divide and conquer' strategy: a 2-coloring backbone for the dominant bipartite subgraph plus a single conflict color for non-bipartite regions, resolved in feature space via an adjacency constraint loss. Conceptually, it also builds on the broader shift from local-cue methods (detection, contour, distance maps) to global topological modeling, but its key departure is the first systematic empirical demonstration that the bipartite assumption underlying 2-coloring is fundamentally violated in dense real-world tissues, motivating the hybrid design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first systematic topological analysis of cell adjacency graphs (revealing 30%+ conflict nodes in dense histopathology) provides a data-driven theoretical foundation that the field lacked, and the GBC-FS 2025 dataset (864K sub-nuclear instances at 304 cells/patch) fills a critical gap for stress-testing segmentation under extreme density. The predicted 'Conflict Map' opens a new interpretability axis for quantifying topological complexity in pathology, potentially informing data curation and clinical triage. Open questions include whether the biophysical hypothesis (absence of K4 minors due to packing constraints) generalizes to 3D volumetric data or other tissue types, and whether the Conflict Map can be leveraged for semi-supervised or weakly-supervised annotation strategies beyond the brief mention in the paper.
