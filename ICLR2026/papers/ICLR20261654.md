## Problem

Indirect 3D editing methods for 3D Gaussian Splatting operate by editing rendered 2D views and projecting modifications back into 3D, which inevitably introduces cross-view inconsistencies and constrains both flexibility and efficiency across editing rounds. While native feed-forward 3D generation has shown promise in avoiding these issues, training such a model for 3DGS editing is infeasible with standard supervised learning due to the scarcity of paired 3D editing data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261654/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3DGS scene (a set of Gaussian primitives each parameterized by mean, scale, opacity, color, and rotation), a text editing instruction, and a Gaussian noise vector of shape 1×4×64×64 that encodes the probabilistic flow from the source 2D editor. It outputs per-primitive attribute variations (Δμ, Δs, Δα, Δc, Δr) that are additively superimposed onto the original scene to yield the edited result. The random tokenizer converts a variable number of Gaussians into a fixed 256 tokens of 128 Gaussians each, and the entire inference pipeline completes in approximately 0.3 seconds.

## Method

VF-Editor redefines 3DGS editing as a feed-forward variation prediction problem, distilling multi-source 2D editing knowledge (IP2P, CtrlColor, DDPM inversion) into a single variation predictor trained on 32,566 collected {noise, instruction, edited image} triplets. The predictor comprises a transformer-based variation field generation module that encodes 3D tokens and noise via self-attention and injects CLIP-encoded text via cross-attention, followed by two iterative parallel decoding functions: F₁ predicts positional variation first, then F₂ predicts appearance variations conditioned on the updated position, decoupling the intercoupled geometry-appearance Jacobian. The parallel decoding uses cross-attention only (no self-attention), achieving O(N) complexity in the number of Gaussians, and zero-linear initialization stabilizes early training. By preserving the key noise ε from the 2D editor's probabilistic flow rather than restricting it, the model accommodates multi-view diversity while remaining view-consistent, and the explicit per-primitive variations enable flexible post-hoc manipulation such as scaling, blending, and spatial masking.

## Results

VF-Editor-M achieves the best Inception Score (IS) across all three evaluation subsets: 4.32 on RObj, 4.15 on GObj, and 4.06 on Scene, outperforming the strongest baseline (I-gs2gs) by 0.46, 0.64, and 0.69 respectively. It also attains the highest CLIP Text-Image Direction Similarity (Csim) on every subset (e.g., 0.296 on RObj vs. 0.261 for GaussianEditor) and the top Image Aesthetics Assessment score of 5.24 (vs. 5.05 for DGE). Critically, VF-Editor-M completes editing in ~0.3 seconds per scene, compared to 210–463 seconds for the optimization-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VF-Editor-M dominates all three baselines on IS and Csim across every dataset, with the largest margins on GObj (IS 4.15 vs. 2.95 for DGE) and RObj (Csim 0.296 vs. 0.193 for I-gs2gs). On CLIP Direction Consistency (Con), VF-Editor-M leads on RObj (0.763) and Scene (0.903), but DGE edges it slightly on GObj (0.879 vs. 0.875), suggesting DGE's epipolar attention injection provides a marginal consistency advantage in that specific setting. The paper attributes DGE's lower IS to its cross-view consistency constraints suppressing output diversity, whereas VF-Editor preserves the probabilistic flow of 2D editing, yielding both higher diversity and quality. In the user study, VF-Editor-M scores 9.3/9.0/9.0 on Aesthetic Quality / Instruction Following / Faithfulness, substantially ahead of DGE (6.4/5.9/7.5) and I-gs2gs (4.7/5.1/6.4). A GPT-5-based multi-view consistency metric further confirms VF-Editor-M's advantage (8.3 vs. 7.5 for DGE, 6.7 for GaussianEditor, 6.3 for I-gs2gs). In terms of speed, VF-Editor-M's 0.3-second feed-forward inference is roughly 700× faster than GaussianEditor (463 s) and ~700× faster than I-gs2gs (275 s), making it the only method suitable for real-time interactive editing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant indirect 3D editing paradigm (edit rendered 2D views, then reconstruct 3D) used by Instruct-gs2gs, GaussianEditor, and DGE, which inherently suffers from cross-view inconsistency. Drawing on the feed-forward 3D generation trend (LRM, Llama-Mesh), it reframes 3DGS editing as a single-pass variation prediction problem, distilling multi-source 2D editing knowledge (IP2P, CtrlColor, DDPM inversion) into a unified transformer-based predictor. The key architectural departure from prior native 3D editing methods (3D-LATTE, VoxHammer) is the avoidance of pretrained 3D diffusion models in favor of a lightweight variation field with parallel per-primitive decoding, achieving linear complexity in the number of Gaussians.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VF-Editor addresses the fundamental cross-view inconsistency that plagues all indirect 3D editing pipelines and enables real-time (~0.3s) interactive editing with flexible post-hoc manipulation of variations (scaling, blending, spatial masking). However, the method is currently limited to in-domain editing and does not yet support out-of-domain generalization or object addition without affecting surrounding regions. Open questions include effective integration of the SDS loss (which causes mode collapse when used alone) with the direct distillation loss, and whether the variation-prediction formulation can scale to open-vocabulary, out-of-domain editing without requiring per-concept fine-tuning.
