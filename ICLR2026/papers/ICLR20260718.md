## Problem

Standard flow matching treats signals on structured domains—such as fMRI on brain graphs or ocean currents on meshes—as points in Euclidean space, discarding the topological and geometric structure of the underlying domain. While FM has been extended to Riemannian manifolds and discrete spaces, no principled framework existed for modeling k-simplex signals on graphs and simplicial complexes that respects their topology while retaining FM's simulation-free training and deterministic sample paths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260718/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sample from an initial distribution (standard Gaussian or a topology-aware heat Gaussian process N(0, exp(-κL_k))) and the combinatorial structure of a graph or simplicial complex, encoded by boundary matrices B_k and the Hodge Laplacian L_k. It produces a k-simplex signal (a vector in R^{n_k}) by integrating a deterministic flow ODE from the initial sample. The Laplacian eigendecomposition is precomputed, and all conditional vector fields, paths, and transport costs are evaluated in spectral coordinates where the topological drift diagonalizes, enabling closed-form per-coordinate formulas.

## Method

The key insight is that conditional flow matching (CFM) solves the zero-noise limit of a drift-free Schrödinger bridge problem, so topological information can be injected by augmenting the reference diffusion with a Laplacian-derived drift H_t(L_k) (specifically -κL_k, the heat equation). This yields the topological flow ODE Ẋ_t = -κL_k X_t + u_t(X_t), where the learned vector field u_t is a minimum-energy corrective force steering from μ_0 to μ_1 against the heat-equation background. In spectral coordinates, the conditional vector field, conditional path, and OT transport cost all admit simple closed-form expressions per eigenvalue: zero-eigenvalue (topological) components follow straight lines, while non-zero components are modulated by exp(-κλ^i) factors that dampen high-frequency oscillations. Training uses the same simulation-free CFM loss with either independent (I-TFM) or optimal-transport (OT-TFM) couplings, making TFM a drop-in replacement for standard FM. The topological drift acts as a structure-aware smoothness prior that preserves signal components aligned with topological features (ker L_k) while denoising the rest.

## Results

On the five real-world structured-domain datasets, TFM achieves the best 1-Wasserstein distance in every setting. The largest gains appear on brain fMRI matching (OT-TFM: 5.86 vs. OT-CFM: 11.30, a 48% reduction) and earthquake generation (I-TFM: 4.93 vs. I-CFM: 8.37, a 41% reduction). On CIFAR-10 image generation, TFM yields a small but consistent FID improvement (I-TFM: 3.697 vs. I-CFM: 3.701; OT-TFM: 3.811 vs. OT-CFM: 3.824), with higher run-to-run variance. TFM also outperforms the simulation-based TSBM baseline on all five structured datasets, with the gap most pronounced on traffic flows (1.27 vs. 9.92) and ocean currents (1.87 vs. 6.89).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CFM (Lipman et al., 2023), TFM wins decisively on all five structured-domain datasets: the 1-Wasserstein gap ranges from 0.004 on single-cell (0.018 vs. 0.022) to 5.85 on brain fMRI (5.86 vs. 11.71). On CIFAR-10, the advantage shrinks to a marginal FID improvement (~0.003–0.013), consistent with the regular grid topology offering less structural information to exploit. Against TSBM (Yang, 2025), TFM also wins on every structured dataset, with the most dramatic margin on traffic flows (1.27 vs. 9.92) and ocean currents (1.87 vs. 6.89), underscoring the practical benefit of TFM's simulation-free, deterministic formulation over TSBM's stochastic path-sampling approach. Notably, I-TFM outperforms OT-TFM on the earthquake task (4.93 vs. 5.53), suggesting that the independent coupling can be preferable when the data distribution is highly concentrated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the flow matching / rectified flow framework (Lipman et al. 2023; Liu 2022) and its connection to the Schrödinger bridge problem (Léonard 2014), showing that CFM arises as the zero-noise limit of a drift-free SBP. It then departs from topological Schrödinger bridge matching (Yang 2025) by injecting a Laplacian-derived drift into the reference process while preserving FM's simulation-free, deterministic properties—a combination absent from prior work. The paper also extends the line of FM generalizations to non-Euclidean spaces (Chen & Lipman 2024 for Riemannian manifolds; Gat et al. 2024 for discrete spaces) to the distinct setting of signals defined on graphs and simplicial complexes, where the topology acts on the signal domain rather than the sample space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TFM provides a principled, drop-in replacement for standard FM on structured domains, with substantial empirical gains (roughly 2× on brain fMRI, ~40% on earthquakes) over both CFM and the simulation-based TSBM, while retaining FM's scalability and deterministic sampling. The key open questions include whether learned or time-dependent κ values, Matérn-like drifts, or time-varying Laplacians (enabling matching between signals on different spaces) can further improve performance, and whether the framework extends cleanly to compact Riemannian manifolds. The modest gains on regular grids (CIFAR-10) suggest the method's value is concentrated on domains with genuinely complex topology, limiting its immediate impact on mainstream image/video generation.
