## Problem

Existing neural PDE solvers require large numbers of training trajectories because they do not explicitly encode the structural inductive biases (locality, superposition, conservation) inherent in PDE dynamics, forcing models to rediscover fundamental physics from data. This low data efficiency is particularly acute for generalization to unseen source terms, where baselines degrade by orders of magnitude. The paper identifies Green's function theory as a principled structural prior that can substantially reduce the learning space and improve sample efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an initial state u(x,0), a spatiotemporally varying source term f(x,t), and a fixed unstructured mesh (Delaunay-triangulated graph with ~1,000–30,000 nodes) as input, and produces the PDE solution u(x,t) rolled out step-by-step over the full time horizon. The mesh geometry is static across all trajectories, enabling a one-time sparse LU factorization of the Green operator that is reused at every time step. Source terms are injected at each step within the discrete Green update, so the model handles arbitrary novel forcing patterns without retraining.

## Method

DGNet discretizes the continuous Green's function on graphs via Crank-Nicolson time integration, yielding a discrete propagation operator G(Δt) = (I − Δt/2·L)⁻¹ that naturally embeds the superposition principle: the next state is the propagated current state plus the accumulated source response. The spatial operator L is a hybrid L_physics + L_neural, where L_physics is constructed from mesh geometry using Green-Gauss gradient and cotangent Laplacian discretizations, and L_neural is a GNN (Encode-Process-Decode with MPNN layers) that learns edge-level corrections to cancel truncation errors on irregular meshes. A lightweight residual GNN is added in the prediction path for additional dynamics. Training uses trajectory segmentation with the pushforward trick, applying loss only to the first and last predictions of each sub-sequence, and the sparse linear system is solved via a factorize-once strategy with GPU-accelerated sparse LU, reducing per-step cost to nearly linear in nonzeros.

## Results

Under a limited-trajectory regime (10–20 training trajectories per scenario), DGNet achieves the lowest MSE and RNE across all seven evaluated spatiotemporal PDE scenarios, with improvements of 1–2 orders of magnitude over the best baseline. For example, on the FitzHugh-Nagumo system DGNet reaches MSE 1.18e-07 (RNE 0.095) versus the best baseline DeepONet at MSE 2.49e-06 (RNE 0.97), and on the Laser Heat generalization task DGNet attains MSE 1.76e+01 (RNE 0.010) while the best baseline BENO records MSE 1.95e+03 (RNE 0.107). On complex geometric domains (Cylinder, Sediments, Complex Obstacles), DGNet's RNE stays below 0.03, whereas all baselines exceed 0.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGNet outperforms all eight baselines on every scenario and metric. Among the primary baselines in Table 2, MGN is typically the strongest competitor on classical PDEs (e.g., Fisher-KPP MSE 3.66e-03 vs. DGNet 2.59e-04) and on the Cylinder domain (MSE 6.38e-03 vs. 1.00e-04), but DGNet still wins by 14× and 64× respectively. MP-PDE is the best baseline on Sediments (MSE 7.10e-03) and Complex Obstacles (MSE 6.09e-03), yet DGNet improves by 15× and 91×. BENO, which also draws on Green's function theory for elliptic PDEs, is the closest competitor on the Laser Heat task (MSE 1.95e+03) but DGNet reduces error by over 100×. PhyMPGN, the most recent physics-encoded GNN, is consistently the weakest baseline on FitzHugh-Nagumo (RNE 23.57) and Complex Obstacles (RNE 1.20). In the extended comparison (Table 11), DGNet also dominates the three 2025 baselines—SINGER, AMG-GNN (Harnessing), and Transolver—by 1–2 orders of magnitude on MSE across FitzHugh-Nagumo, Complex Obstacles, and Laser Heat, confirming that the discrete Green structural prior provides a decisive data-efficiency advantage over purely data-driven or multi-scale architectures under limited training trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

DGNet extends the graph-based PDE solver lineage (GNS, MGN, MP-PDE, PhyMPGN) by replacing the purely data-driven operator learning with a principled discrete Green's function update rule that explicitly encodes the superposition principle. While BENO (Wang et al., 2024) also drew on Green's functions, it was restricted to time-independent elliptic PDEs; DGNet generalizes this to spatiotemporal PDEs via a Crank-Nicolson discretization on graphs. The neural correction operator follows the Encode-Process-Decode paradigm of Sanchez-Gonzalez et al. (2020b), but its role is narrowed to compensating mesh-induced truncation errors rather than learning the full dynamics, a distinction formalized in the paper's consistency and stability theorems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in scientific ML: high-fidelity PDE data are expensive, and existing neural solvers require hundreds to thousands of trajectories. DGNet's ability to achieve state-of-the-art accuracy with only 10–20 trajectories, combined with zero-shot generalization to unseen source terms (a capability no prior method demonstrates), makes it directly relevant to engineering workflows where forcing conditions vary in deployment. The identified failure mode on quasilinear PDEs (phase transitions with state-dependent material properties) clearly delineates the boundary of the superposition-based approach, and extending the framework to such nonlinear regimes remains an open challenge. Scaling to large 3D systems and handling non-Dirichlet/Neumann boundary conditions more broadly are also noted as future directions.
