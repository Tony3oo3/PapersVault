## Problem

Existing language-guided humanoid locomotion pipelines require a fragile multi-stage process—decoding human motion from text, retargeting it to robot morphology, and tracking the result with a physics-based controller—leading to cumulative errors, high latency (~17.85 s), and weak semantic-to-control coupling. No prior work eliminates the retargeting stage entirely while preserving both semantic fidelity and physical feasibility on real humanoids.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264391/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the system takes a free-form text prompt and the robot's proprioceptive state (75-dim: 23 DoF positions/velocities, last action, root angular velocity, roll/pitch/yaw) over a 10-step history, plus a 64-dim motion latent produced by the autoregressive generator. The output is a 23-dimensional target joint position vector for PD control on the Unitree G1, executed at 50 Hz via LCM. Critically, the student policy never receives explicit reference motion or retargeted kinematic sequences—only the compact latent—making the pipeline fully retargeting-free at deployment.

## Method

RoboGhost comprises three components: (1) a continuous autoregressive motion generator using a causal autoencoder with masked autoregressive prediction and causal attention (replacing bidirectional masks to avoid low-rank approximation limits), which outputs a 64-dim latent l_ref from text via a LaMP text transformer; (2) a MoE-based teacher policy (5 experts + gating network) trained with PPO using privileged simulator states and explicit reference motion, accelerated by a causal adaptive sampling strategy that exponentially reweights intervals preceding failure; (3) a diffusion-based student policy (4-layer MLP backbone with AdaLN conditioning) trained via DAgger-style distillation with x0-prediction, where the teacher's actions are progressively noised and the student learns to denoise them conditioned on the motion latent, proprioceptive states, and history. At inference, 2-step DDIM sampling produces executable actions in ~4.8 ms, cutting end-to-end latency from 17.85 s to 5.84 s while improving success rate by 5% over the explicit retargeting baseline.

## Results

On the HumanML subset (IsaacGym), RoboGhost (Ours-DDPM) achieves a 97% success rate with 0.12 rad mean per-joint error and 0.09 m mean per-keypoint error, compared to the Exbody2-based baseline's 92% success rate and 0.23/0.19 errors. On the more challenging Kungfu subset, the method reaches 72% success rate (vs. 66% baseline) in IsaacGym and 57% (vs. 51%) in MuJoCo. The retargeting-free pipeline reduces end-to-end deployment time from 17.85 s (explicit PHC retargeting with 1000 iterations) to 5.84 s while simultaneously improving tracking accuracy. For motion generation on HumanML3D, Ours-SiT achieves R@1 of 0.641, FID of 11.743, and MM-Dist of 15.663, outperforming all compared text-to-motion baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For motion generation (Table 1, HumanML3D), RoboGhost outperforms all seven compared text-to-motion methods: MDM (R@1 0.523, FID 23.45), MLD (0.546, 18.24), T2M-GPT (0.606, 12.48), MotionGPT (0.456, 14.38), MoMask (0.621, 12.23), AttT2M (0.592, 15.43), and MotionStreamer (0.631, 11.79), achieving R@1 of 0.639 and FID of 11.71 (DDPM) or 11.74 (SiT). The closest competitor is MotionStreamer, which RoboGhost edges out by 0.008 in R@1 and 0.08 in FID. For motion tracking (Table 2), RoboGhost consistently surpasses the Exbody2-based baseline by 5 percentage points in success rate on HumanML (0.97 vs. 0.92 in IsaacGym) and 6 points on Kungfu (0.72 vs. 0.66), with roughly halved joint and keypoint errors. In the appendix (Table 13), RoboGhost also outperforms GMT (Chen et al., 2025) by 2 points on HumanML IsaacGym success (0.97 vs. 0.95) and 2 points on Kungfu (0.72 vs. 0.70). The retargeting-free pipeline (Table 4) further demonstrates a 3-point success rate gain over the explicit PHC-retargeting variant (0.97 vs. 0.93) while cutting deployment time by 67% (5.84 s vs. 17.85 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

RoboGhost departs from the conventional text-to-locomotion pipeline (decode → retarget → track) by treating motion latents as first-class conditioning signals for a diffusion-based policy, eliminating the explicit kinematic intermediate. It builds on the continuous autoregressive framework of Li et al. (2024a) for motion generation, adapts DAgger-style distillation (Ross et al., 2011) for teacher-student transfer, and positions itself against explicit-tracking baselines like ExBody2 (Ji et al., 2024) and GMT (Chen et al., 2025). The MoE-based teacher policy and causal adaptive sampling extend standard PPO training (Schulman et al., 2017) with failure-attribution-aware curriculum, while the diffusion student with AdaLN conditioning and x0-prediction draws from recent diffusion policy work (Truong et al., 2024) but applies it in a retargeting-free, latent-driven setting for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By collapsing a multi-stage pipeline into a single latent-conditioned denoising step, RoboGhost cuts deployment latency by ~3× (17.85s → 5.84s) and removes a major source of cumulative error, making language-guided humanoid control substantially more practical for real-time interaction. The framework's modality-agnostic design (extending to images, audio, music) positions it as a general foundation for vision-language-action humanoid systems. Open questions include whether the 64-dim latent bottleneck suffices for arbitrarily complex or long-horizon motions, how the approach scales to multi-robot or interactive settings, and whether the diffusion policy's robustness advantage persists under adversarial or distribution-shift conditions beyond Gaussian observation noise.
