## Problem

Despite the rise of Relational Deep Learning (RDL) and Deep Feature Synthesis (DFS) for predictive modeling over relational databases, no comprehensive comparison exists to clarify when each paradigm performs better, and practitioners lack principled guidelines for architecture selection. The paper identifies that validation accuracy is an unreliable proxy for architecture choice in temporal RDB settings, and that no single architecture dominates across tasks, making the selection problem both non-trivial and currently unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265272/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full relational database schema (tables with primary/foreign keys, non-key attributes, and timestamps), a task definition expressed as a time-constrained SQL query (entity-level, link-level, or autocomplete), and a query budget for the search. It outputs a two-level architecture decision: first a macro-level choice between the RDL family (GNN-based) and the DFS family (non-parametric aggregation + tabular model), then a specific architecture configuration and hyperparameter setting within the chosen family, ultimately yielding task predictions. The query budget is appended to the task embedding as an additional feature, since the favored model family depends on available search resources.

## Method

Relatron is a task-embedding-based meta-selector built on a curated model performance bank generated by large-scale architecture-centric grid search over a factorized design space (RDL: structural feature encoder × message passing × readout; DFS: aggregation function × layers × backbone). The key task embedding combines RDB task homophily (adjusted for class imbalance, computed over self-looped metapaths on the PK-FK graph), training-free anchor-based affinity properties (random GraphSAGE/NBFNet path affinity, TabPFN feature affinity, temporal statistics), and log(N_train). A meta-classifier trained on the performance bank maps these embeddings to the empirically winning family, with the critical finding that adjusted homophily negatively correlates with the RDL-DFS gap (Spearman ρ = −0.43) and log(N_train) moderates the advantage of RDL's learned nonlinear aggregations. Within the selected family, TPE generates candidate checkpoints, and lightweight loss-landscape metrics (first-order slope P1, Hessian sharpness P2, and energy barrier Pbar) perform post-selection among top-validation candidates to guard against brittle checkpoints, resolving the 'more tuning, worse performance' effect observed in baselines.

## Results

In joint hyperparameter-architecture search (Table 6), Relatron's full pipeline achieves 84.33±0.06 ROC-AUC on driver-top3, 3.3339±0.1563 MAE on driver-position, and 70.05±0.34 ROC-AUC on user-churn at budget 30, outperforming the best search-based baselines (TPE: 77.92, 4.1724, 69.54) by up to 18.5% relative improvement. Even using only the meta-predictor (no HPO) yields 84.00, 3.3339, and 70.05, already surpassing all search-based methods. For macro-level architecture selection, the model-free task embedding achieves 87.5% leave-one-out accuracy in predicting the RDL-vs-DFS winner, compared to 66.7% for both training-free model-based and anchor-based (Autotransfer) embeddings, while requiring only 0.48 minutes versus 50 minutes of computation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Relatron's full pipeline outperforms all search-based baselines (Random, TPE, Hyperband) across all three evaluation tasks at budget 30, with the largest gap on driver-top3 (84.33 vs. 77.92 for TPE, a 6.4-point ROC-AUC gain) and driver-position (3.33 vs. 4.17 MAE for TPE). Against Autotransfer (the strongest transfer-based baseline), Relatron wins decisively on driver-top3 (84.33 vs. 78.71) and driver-position (3.33 vs. 4.36), though Autotransfer edges ahead on user-churn at budget 10 (70.28 vs. 69.61); at budget 30, Relatron's full pipeline (70.05) is competitive. Compared to the DFS+TabPFN foundation-model baseline, Relatron improves by 2.09 points on driver-top3, 0.10 MAE on driver-position, and 2.26 points on user-churn. Against the Griffin foundation model, Relatron leads by 6.38 points on driver-top3, 0.87 MAE on driver-position, and 1.65 points on user-churn. Notably, Relatron's model-free task embedding requires only 0.48 minutes of computation versus 50 minutes for Autotransfer's Fisher-information-based embeddings (a ~100× speedup), while achieving 87.5% vs. 66.7% LOO accuracy for winner prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously studied but separately developed paradigms for RDB prediction—Relational Deep Learning (RDL, e.g., RelGNN, RelGT, KumoRFM) and Deep Feature Synthesis (DFS, e.g., H2O.ai's getml)—within a shared factorized design space, and positions itself at the intersection of graph AutoML (Autotransfer, Task2Vec) and relational database analytics. It departs from the RDL literature's implicit assumption that learned message passing subsumes hand-crafted aggregation, and from the AutoML literature's reliance on expensive Fisher-information-based task embeddings, by showing that lightweight model-free homophily and affinity features suffice for architecture routing. The loss-landscape post-selection draws on the GNN mode-connectivity literature (Li et al., 2025; Chiang et al., 2023) but applies it to the underexplored problem of validation-test selection gaps in inductive, time-aware RDB settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that RDL does not consistently outperform DFS challenges the field's momentum toward GNN-based relational models and provides practitioners with a concrete, low-cost routing signal (adjusted homophily + log training rows) for choosing between paradigms. The demonstration that validation-selected checkpoints can be substantially worse than test-selected ones—and that more search trials can degrade performance—highlights a practical pitfall that is largely overlooked in graph AutoML. Open questions include whether the homophily-based routing generalizes beyond Relbench-scale databases, whether LLM-based or foundation-model approaches (Griffin, KumoRFM) alter the RDL-DFS balance, and whether synthetic task generation can expand the performance bank to improve transfer-based HPO.
