## Problem

Industrial recommendation systems must process ultra-long user interaction histories (10k–100k+ items) to capture long-term preferences, but full-sequence transformer models (e.g., HSTU) incur O(N²) attention costs that are prohibitive at the scale of O(10B) examples/day with strict inference latency budgets. Target-specific sampling methods (SIM, TWIN) reduce sequence length but introduce a gap between sampled and full-sequence attention, and their inference cost grows linearly with the number of candidates. No existing approach scales to lifelong histories (up to 1M items) while keeping downstream training and inference costs fixed.

## Input / Output

During foundational training, the model consumes raw user interaction history sequences (up to 16K items in experiments, up to 1M in production) and compresses them into a fixed set of ~128 seed embeddings (256-dimensional), which are quantized and written to a 100 TB–1 PB key-value cache. At inference, the expensive summarization stage is bypassed entirely: the system retrieves the cached embeddings, dequantizes them, and feeds them alongside candidate item features into a lightweight target-attention module that produces CTR/recommendation scores. The 2-hour embedding update cadence means the cached representations can be slightly stale relative to the live model, a trade-off the authors validate as negligible in A/B tests.

## Method

VISTA decomposes target attention into two stages: (1) a UIH summarization module that uses virtual seed embeddings (shared, randomly initialized parameters) with quasi-linear attention (QLA) to compress the full user history into a few hundred tokens in O(N) time, and (2) a standard O(N²) target-aware attention block that computes attention between each candidate item and the cached summary tokens. QLA replaces softmax with SiLU-activated linear attention (QLU module) plus SGLU gating, and critically prevents candidate-to-candidate attention to avoid label leakage, using a diagonal self-attention term Δ(Q[T],K[T])V[T] instead. A generative reconstruction loss (off-by-one MSE via a causal decoder) is added to force the seed embeddings to retain maximal information from the UIH. The resulting summary embeddings are quantized and served through a geographically replicated in-memory key-value store (updated via Kafka/Hive on a 2-hour cadence), so downstream models and inference only perform the cheap second stage regardless of history length.

## Results

On Amazon-Electronics, VISTA-w/o-QLA achieves the best AUC (0.886) and NE (0.621), outperforming HSTU (0.884/0.628) and MHA (0.881/0.634). On the industrial-scale dataset (mean UIH length 7,000), VISTA reduces evaluation NE by 0.40%–2.98% across four engagement tasks relative to the HSTU production baseline. In a 15-day online A/B test on a video recommendation platform, VISTA delivers a 0.5% lift on the main consumption task while reducing inference GPU usage by 94% through cached embedding retrieval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DIN, VISTA consistently wins on both AUC and NE across all three public datasets (e.g., Amazon: 0.886 vs. 0.873 AUC; Minimal Prod: 1.038 vs. 1.048 NE). Compared to TTSN, VISTA achieves substantially lower NE on Amazon (0.621 vs. 0.644) and Minimal Production (1.038 vs. 1.139), though TTSN edges out VISTA on Minimal Prod AUC (0.648 vs. 0.627). Against MHA and SASRec, VISTA-w/o-QLA matches or exceeds their AUC on Amazon (0.886 vs. 0.881/0.884) and achieves the best NE on all three datasets. The closest competitor is HSTU: on Amazon, VISTA-w/o-QLA edges ahead (0.886 vs. 0.884 AUC, 0.621 vs. 0.628 NE), on KuaiRand the two are essentially tied, and on Minimal Production HSTU achieves higher AUC (0.668 vs. 0.627) but VISTA still delivers lower NE (1.038 vs. 1.099). On the industrial-scale dataset, VISTA improves over the HSTU production baseline by up to 2.98% NE on E2-Task, confirming gains at production scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

VISTA positions itself at the intersection of two existing lines of work in sequential recommendation: full-sequence modeling (HSTU, SASRec) and target-specific sequence sampling (SIM, TWIN, TWIN V2). It departs from both by introducing a two-stage decomposition where the expensive user-history summarization is computed once and cached, while only a lightweight target-attention step runs at inference. The quasi-linear attention (QLA) module is derived from the linear attention formulation of Katharopoulos et al. (2020) and the gated linear unit design of TransNormerLLM/Lightning Attention (Qin et al., 2024a,b), with SiLU non-linearities added to address known expressiveness limitations of pure linear attention. The generative reconstruction loss, while conceptually rooted in VAE-style information bottleneck objectives, is applied to recommendation sequence summarization for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 94% reduction in inference GPU cost by caching user-history summary embeddings is a practically significant efficiency win for industrial recommendation platforms that must serve billions of users under strict latency budgets. The approach enables scaling to lifelong user histories (up to 1M items) without proportional inference cost, which was previously infeasible with full-sequence models like HSTU. Open questions include the staleness introduced by the 2-hour embedding update cadence, the storage cost trade-off as the number of virtual seeds grows, and whether the quasi-linear attention formulation generalizes beyond recommendation to other sequential prediction tasks. The paper's industrial deployment at Meta provides strong evidence of practical viability, but the modest offline NE improvements (0.4–3%) over HSTU suggest the primary value is in efficiency rather than accuracy.
