## Problem

Vision-language-action models operate in 2D despite needing to act in 3D, creating a spatial reasoning gap that limits generalization to novel scenes, object scales, and height variations. Existing 3D integration approaches either require specialized sensors (point clouds, RGB-D) with poor modality transferability, or inject weak spatial cues into the VLM backbone that disrupt pre-trained vision-language alignment and degrade zero-shot generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261231/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images from a side camera and optionally a wrist camera (resized to 224×224), a natural language instruction, and optionally a depth map and 7D camera pose. The ESM processes a separate third-view image independently of the VLM's visual input. Output is a chunk of C future actions (C=5 for real-world/SimplerEnv, C=10 for CALVIN), each a 7D vector encoding 6-DoF gripper pose (Euler angles) plus a binary gripper open/close state, executed at ~57 Hz on a single RTX 4090.

## Method

FALCON decouples spatial processing from the VLM by routing 3D information through a dedicated Spatial-Enhanced Action Head rather than concatenating spatial tokens into the VLM backbone. The Embodied Spatial Model (based on VGGT) encodes a third-view image via DINO tokens into spatial tokens through cross-attention/self-attention blocks, and optionally fuses depth (via a 14×14 conv encoder) and camera pose (via MLP) using stochastic Bernoulli conditioning during training to preserve RGB-only capability. Spatial tokens are compressed via max-pooling, projected into the VLM's feature space by a lightweight MLP adapter, and fused with the VLM's semantic action token through parameter-free element-wise addition before an MLP or LSTM action predictor generates the action chunk. Training uses a two-stage post-training pipeline: Stage 1 freezes all pre-trained components and trains only the zero-initialized adapter for feature-space alignment; Stage 2 unfreezes the VLM and adapter jointly for refinement while keeping the ESM and action predictor frozen.

## Results

On the CALVIN benchmark, FALCON achieves state-of-the-art performance in both the ABCD→D (Avg. Len. 4.53) and zero-shot ABC→D (Avg. Len. 4.40) settings, surpassing the previous best methods RoboVLM (4.49 / 4.25) and Seer-Large (4.28). On SimplerEnv, FALCON attains 56.3% average success on WidowX tasks and 62.9% on Google Robot tasks, outperforming SpatialVLA (42.7% / 55.3%) and RoboVLM (37.5% / 51.7%). In real-world evaluations across 11 tasks, FALCON achieves 70.0% average success on Base Tasks (vs. 44.4% for SpatialVLA) and 87.5% on Few-shot Simple settings (vs. 60.0% for RoboVLM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FALCON outperforms all 16 baselines across every benchmark. On CALVIN ABC→D (zero-shot), FALCON (Avg. Len. 4.40) surpasses Seer-Large (4.28) by 0.12 and RoboVLM (4.25) by 0.15, while dramatically exceeding 3D Diffuser Actor (3.35) and 3DDP (0.27) which rely on ground-truth point clouds. On SimplerEnv Google Robot, FALCON (62.9%) beats SpatialVLA (55.3%) by 7.6% and RoboVLM (51.7%) by 11.2%, with the largest gap on the 'Open Top Drawer and Place Apple' task (41.7% vs. 0.0% for most baselines, 3.7% for RT-2-X). In real-world Base Tasks, FALCON (70.0%) leads SpatialVLA (44.4%) by 25.6% and RoboVLM by a similar margin. In Few-shot Adaptation, FALCON (87.5% Simple, 60.0% Unseen Object) dominates RoboVLM (60.0%, 15.0%) and SpatialVLA (50.0%, 27.5%), with the Unseen Object gap reaching 45 percentage points. The modality-transfer ablation (Table 5) further shows FALCON with RGB-only input (3.91) matches Kosmos-VLA with RGB-D (3.98) on ABC→D, confirming that spatial foundation priors compensate for missing depth.

## Relation to Prior Work

FALCON builds directly on VGGT's spatial foundation model architecture for its Embodied Spatial Model, extending it with optional depth and camera-pose conditioning via a stochastic injection strategy. It departs from two dominant lines of 3D-enhanced VLA research: (1) methods that require explicit 3D sensor inputs (PointVLA, GeoVLA), which lack modality transferability, and (2) methods that inject spatial embeddings into the VLM's token sequence (SpatialVLA, 3D-VLA, Evo-0), which disrupt pre-trained vision-language alignment. The key architectural departure is routing spatial tokens to a dedicated Spatial-Enhanced Action Head rather than the VLM backbone, motivated by a cerebrum/cerebellum division-of-labor analogy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modality transferability design—where a single model trained with stochastic 3D conditioning can flexibly exploit depth or pose at test time without retraining—addresses a practical deployment bottleneck for generalist robot policies that must operate across heterogeneous sensor setups. The finding that simple element-wise addition outperforms cross-attention and FiLM-gated fusion for combining spatial and semantic tokens is a useful negative result that challenges the assumption that more complex fusion mechanisms are necessary. Open questions include whether this decoupled spatial-injection paradigm scales to mobile manipulation, multi-arm systems, or longer-horizon tasks beyond table-top settings, and whether the 2.9B parameter budget is sufficient for more diverse embodiments.
