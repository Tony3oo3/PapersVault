## Problem

Generalizing articulated object manipulation across diverse objects requires understanding functional parts (handles, knobs, latches), yet existing 2D foundation features (CLIP, DINOv2) lack 3D geometric continuity and part-level granularity. When these 2D features are lifted to 3D via multi-view fusion, they introduce long inference times, cross-view inconsistencies, and spatial resolution losses (e.g., 14× downsampling in ViT patch tokens), making them impractical for dense, real-time manipulation.

## Input / Output

PA3FF takes a point cloud P = {p_i ∈ R³} sampled from object surfaces and outputs a continuous 3D feature field f: R³ → Rⁿ, where any spatial query returns an n-dimensional embedding; points belonging to the same functional part have close feature vectors. PADP additionally consumes multi-view point clouds (from three depth cameras), the robot's proprioceptive state q_t, and a language instruction (the task-critical part name, used as a CLS token), and produces an action chunk A_t = [a_t, ..., a_{t+H-1}] of end-effector poses and gripper states via a diffusion denoising process.

## Method

PA3FF builds on Sonata, a self-supervised pretrained Point Transformer V3, modified by removing most downsampling layers and stacking additional transformer blocks to preserve object-level detail. A lightweight per-point MLP refines Sonata's output under a combined contrastive objective: a geometric SupCon loss pulls same-part points together and pushes different-part points apart in feature space, while a semantic InfoNCE loss aligns point features with SigLIP-encoded part-name vectors, trained on PartNet-Mobility, 3DCoMPaT, and PartObjaverse-Tiny. PADP freezes PA3FF as a feature extractor, feeds per-point embeddings into a trainable Transformer encoder guided by the part-name semantic embedding as CLS token, concatenates the resulting global feature with proprioception through an MLP, and conditions a DDPM/DDIM diffusion action head on this compact representation to generate action chunks.

## Results

On the PartInstruct benchmark (16 tasks, 5 generalization levels), PADP achieves a 28.79% average success rate, outperforming the strongest baseline GenDP (19.36%) by 9.4% absolute. On 8 real-world tasks with unseen objects, PADP reaches a 58.75% mean success rate versus 35% for the best baseline (GenDP), an 18.75% increment. On PartNet-Ensembled part segmentation, PA3FF features achieve 70.6% average mAP@50, surpassing PartSLIP (63.4%) and PartSLIP++ (62.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PADP consistently outperforms all six baselines across both simulation and real-world evaluations. Against the strongest baseline GenDP (which uses DINOv2 2D features lifted to 3D), PADP gains 9.4% on PartInstruct average and 18.75% on real-world unseen-object success rate, with the largest margins on novel object categories (OC: 26.67% vs 14.61%) and novel task categories (TC: 13.75% vs 10.00%). Against DP3 (3D point cloud + diffusion policy), PADP improves by 13.4% on PartInstruct average and by 10–15% on individual real-world tasks. The 2D-based baselines (DP, Act3D, RVT2, 3D-DA) all remain below 6% average on PartInstruct, highlighting the critical role of 3D-native, part-aware representations. On the PartNet-Ensembled segmentation task, PA3FF features (70.6% mAP@50) surpass PartSLIP (63.4%) and PartSLIP++ (62.6%), confirming that the contrastive part-aware training yields transferable geometric-semantic structure beyond manipulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Sonata (a self-supervised Point Transformer V3 pretrained on 140k point clouds) as a 3D geometric backbone, but departs from prior 2D-to-3D feature lifting approaches (LERF, D3Field, GenDP) that project CLIP/DINOv2 features into 3D via multi-view fusion. The key conceptual departure is making the 3D feature field natively part-aware through a contrastive learning objective (SupCon + InfoNCE with SigLIP text embeddings) trained on part-labeled datasets, rather than inheriting part semantics indirectly from 2D vision-language models. On the policy side, it extends Diffusion Policy with a frozen PA3FF encoder and a part-name-conditioned CLS token, positioning itself against GenDP's 2D-semantic-field approach and DP3's raw point-cloud approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PA3FF addresses a concrete gap in manipulation generalization: the inability of existing 2D or lifted-3D representations to consistently identify functionally relevant parts (handles, knobs, latches) across unseen object shapes, which is critical for articulated object manipulation. The versatility of the learned feature field—enabling not just policy learning but also shape correspondence and part segmentation—suggests it could serve as a reusable 3D part-aware embedding for broader robotic perception. However, the approach is limited to rigid and articulated objects (Sonata struggles with complex deformations), absolute success rates remain moderate (~29% on PartInstruct, ~59% real-world), and the reliance on part-labeled training data (PartNet-Mobility, 3DCoMPaT, PartObjaverse-Tiny) constrains scalability to open-vocabulary part taxonomies.
