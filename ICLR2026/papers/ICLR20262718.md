## Problem

Alignment training improves language models on reasoning and instruction following but degrades skills like creativity, calibration, and generation diversity where unaligned base models excel. These pretrained and finetuned checkpoints are typically discarded after alignment, despite having complementary strengths. The paper asks how to dynamically fuse the strengths of multiple checkpoints in a training pipeline so that each model contributes where it is most needed within a single response.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262718/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The switcher LM receives a natural-language prompt containing the original query, the partial response trace annotated with special delimiters (e.g., <model i begins>…<model i ends>) indicating which model generated each segment, and the candidate model pool; it outputs a single model ID (0 to n−1) indicating which checkpoint should generate the next text segment. At inference, the full system takes a user instruction and produces a complete text response assembled from patches (default 50 tokens) generated by different models under the switcher's guidance, with the aligned model forced to generate the first and last patches. The switcher is invoked once per patch rather than per token, balancing switching granularity against computational cost.

## Method

SWITCH GENERATION trains a small switcher LM via supervised fine-tuning on simulated switching outcomes: for each query, a random trace is sampled by letting models generate segments in random order; from that trace, each candidate model generates one additional segment, k continuations are rolled out per candidate using random switching, and the model yielding the best average evaluation score becomes the SFT label. At inference, the switcher is called per patch with top-p (nucleus) sampling over the model distribution, and the selected model generates the next 50-token patch, which is appended to the trace before the next switcher call. This differs from prior routing paradigms in three ways: the trace is non-empty (context-aware switching), each model only generates segments rather than full responses, and the switcher is applied repeatedly throughout generation. The approach also supports distilling the collaborative outputs back into a single aligned model, recovering ~58% of the collaboration gains at one-quarter the inference cost.

## Results

SWITCH-TASK (the task-specific switcher variant) achieves the best performance on 13 out of 18 datasets, with an average relative improvement of 12.9% over all baselines. Model collaboration approaches (including SWITCH GENERATION) outperform all individual models on 16 of 18 tasks with a 31.0% average relative improvement. Notable gains include 39.22 on TruthfulQA (vs. 34.38 for the best baseline RouteLLM), 58.30 on BBH (vs. 45.90 for RouteLLM), and 25.26 on AGIEval (vs. 12.32 for RouteLLM). The method also recovers 57.5% of collaboration gains through distillation into a single model at one-fourth the inference cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SWITCH-TASK outperforms all 8 collaboration baselines on 13 of 18 datasets. Among API-level baselines, RouteLLM (34.38 on TruthfulQA, 45.90 on BBH) is the strongest single-model router but is substantially outperformed by SWITCH-TASK (39.22, 58.30 respectively), demonstrating that segment-level switching provides finer-grained control than whole-response routing. Weight-level methods (Greedy Soup, DARE-TIES) achieve competitive results on some tasks (e.g., Greedy Soup scores 58.10 on GSM8k vs. 59.60 for SWITCH-TASK) but degrade severely on others (DARE-TIES drops to 22.90 on GSM8k and 19.40 on CocoNot). Text-level baselines (Text Collab, Text Debate) and logit-level baselines (Logit Merge, Proxy Tuning) are consistently the weakest, averaging 26.32 and 18.97 points respectively across all tasks, with Proxy Tuning particularly poor on knowledge and safety tasks (2.76 on TruthfulQA, 7.30 on CocoNot). The paper reports that routing-based methods are best suited for pretrained-aligned collaboration overall (31.15 avg), and SWITCH GENERATION extends this to segment-level with an additional 12.9% average gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model collaboration literature (routing, debate, logit merging, weight merging) by introducing segment-level switching within a single response sequence, formalized as the Query-Trace-Candidate (QTC) problem. Unlike prior routing paradigms that select one model for the entire response, SWITCH GENERATION trains a dedicated switcher LM to dynamically select which checkpoint generates each patch, leveraging the observation that responses interleave skills favoring different pipeline stages. The approach repurposes byproducts of standard training pipelines (pretrained and finetuned checkpoints typically discarded after alignment) as active collaborators rather than treating the aligned model as the sole deployable artifact.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: organizations running expensive training pipelines can recover value from intermediate checkpoints rather than discarding them, and the distillation result (58% of collaboration gains recovered at 1/4 inference cost) offers a path to single-model deployment. Open questions include safety implications of including unaligned models in collaboration (potential override of guardrails), whether dynamic patch sizing can further improve performance, and how the approach scales beyond three checkpoints or to substantially larger model families. The finding that the pretrained model is consistently helpful in collaboration despite being the weakest individual performer reframes how we value intermediate training artifacts.
