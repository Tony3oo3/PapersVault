## Problem

Existing EEG foundation models suffer from three critical limitations: (1) they use a single mixed tokenizer that conflates heterogeneous temporal and frequency EEG signals, yielding clinically uninterpretable and weakly discriminative representations; (2) they rely on fully connected Transformer self-attention, which is misaligned with the brain's sparse small-world topology and scales quadratically with sequence length; and (3) they collapse each EEG patch into a single token, ignoring rich intra-patch neural events such as sleep spindles and K-complexes.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-channel EEG sequences (C channels × f Hz × T seconds) split into non-overlapping patches of t seconds (L = f·t samples per patch); pretraining uses 19 channels at 200 Hz with 1-second patches (570 patches per 30-second segment). Stage 1 outputs two discrete token streams per patch — a temporal token and a frequency token, each drawn from a 4096-entry codebook of 32-dimensional embeddings — quadratically expanding the representable space to K² combinations. Stage 2 outputs a continuous latent representation Z ∈ R^{C×n×D} per sequence, which is fine-tuned with a 3-layer MLP head to produce class labels for downstream tasks.

## Method

CodeBrain is a two-stage self-supervised framework. Stage 1 (TFDual-Tokenizer) uses a shared TFConv + Transformer encoder to extract joint time-frequency embeddings from each patch, then applies two independent vector quantizers with separate codebooks: the temporal codebook is trained with a SimCLR contrastive loss plus raw-signal reconstruction, while the frequency codebook is trained with MSE on DFT amplitude and phase reconstruction. A theoretical proposition (Prop. 2.1) shows that under approximate independence and additive distortion, this product codebook achieves no worse expected distortion than a single mixed codebook of equal total size. Stage 2 (EEGSSM) is a masked autoencoder whose backbone stacks EEGSSM blocks, each combining an SGConv layer (a structured SSM with sparse sub-kernel parameterization and exponential kernel decay, computed via FFT in O(N log N)) for sparse long-range dependencies, a sliding-window attention layer for local intra-patch dynamics, and a WaveNet-style gated unit that fuses global and local features. Dynamic positional embeddings via an asymmetric 2D depthwise convolution enable channel-agnostic spatial modeling, and the pretraining objective is cross-entropy prediction of the discrete token indices of masked patches.

## Results

CodeBrain achieves the best performance across all six main evaluation datasets, outperforming the strongest baseline (CBraMod) by +0.0911 Cohen's Kappa (21.6%), +0.0718 Weighted F1 (13.3%), and +0.0728 Balanced Accuracy (13.5%) on BCIC 2020-T3 (imagined speech, 5-class). On the binary Mental Arithmetic task, it gains +0.0802 AUROC (10.1%), +0.0910 PRAUC (14.5%), and +0.0258 Balanced Accuracy (3.6%) over CBraMod. On sleep staging (ISRUC_S3), CodeBrain reaches 0.7671 Cohen's Kappa and 0.8202 Weighted F1, surpassing the next-best CBraMod (0.7407 / 0.8056). Across emotion recognition (FACED, SEED-V), it leads by 0.0365 and 0.0166 Cohen's Kappa respectively over CBraMod.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CodeBrain outperforms all nine baselines across every evaluated dataset and metric. The strongest competitor is CBraMod (ICLR 2025), a masked-reconstruction EFM; CodeBrain surpasses it by the largest margins on BCIC 2020-T3 (+0.0911 Kappa) and Mental Arithmetic (+0.0802 AUROC), and by smaller but consistent margins on FACED (+0.0365 Kappa), SEED-V (+0.0166 Kappa), and ISRUC_S3 (+0.0264 Kappa). Among non-foundation baselines, EEGConformer and ContraWR are the strongest, yet CodeBrain still leads them by 0.15–0.20 Kappa on the multi-class tasks. Notably, BENDR and EEGPT (both contrastive/masked-reconstruction EFMs) collapse on BCIC 2020-T3 (Kappa ≈ 0.06) and SEED-V (Kappa ≈ 0.03–0.13), indicating poor generalization to imagined-speech and high-channel emotion tasks, whereas CodeBrain maintains robust performance under these distribution shifts. LaBraM (the closest architectural predecessor using a single VQ codebook) is consistently outperformed by CodeBrain, with the gap widening on tasks with richer temporal structure (BCIC 2020-T3: +0.1327 Kappa), supporting the benefit of the decoupled dual-codebook design.

## Relation to Prior Work

CodeBrain extends the EEG foundation model (EFM) line initiated by LaBraM (Jiang et al., 2024), which first introduced vector-quantized discrete tokens for EEG following the VQ-VAE paradigm. The key departure is the TFDual-Tokenizer, which splits the single codebook into separate temporal and frequency codebooks, motivated by the heterogeneity of EEG signals that a unified codebook conflates. For the backbone, CodeBrain replaces the Transformer's quadratic self-attention (used in LaBraM, EEGPT, CBraMod) with a structured state-space convolution (SGConv, building on S4) combined with sliding window attention, drawing on the brain's small-world topology as an architectural principle. A contrastive (SimCLR) objective is added to the temporal branch to address the non-convergence of raw-signal reconstruction that LaBraM encountered.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CodeBrain is the first EFM to offer domain-specific representation-level interpretability, where discrete tokens empirically align with clinically recognizable neural events (K-complexes, spindles, slow waves) and spectral rhythms (sigma, delta), potentially bridging the gap between black-box foundation models and clinical trust. The multi-scale SSM architecture addresses a practical efficiency concern: full self-attention's quadratic cost becomes prohibitive for long EEG sequences, and the O(N log N) SGConv with local SWA offers a principled alternative. Open questions include whether the interpretability findings generalize beyond sleep staging to other tasks, whether the SSM backbone outperforms newer Mamba-2 variants at scale, and whether the decoupled tokenizer's theoretical advantage (Proposition 2.1) holds when temporal and frequency components are strongly coupled in pathological signals.
