## Problem

Existing LLM-based time series forecasting methods rely on token-level modal alignment, which overlooks the intrinsic modality gap between linguistic knowledge structures and temporal data patterns, limiting semantic representation quality. Additionally, Transformer-based LLMs excel at long-range dependencies but are weak at modeling short-term anomalies, and incorporating textual prompts introduces noise and extra computational cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263441/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series matrix (batch B, length L) and the pre-trained LLM's word embedding matrix as input. A sliding window partitions the temporal dimension into N segments of length K, reducing the effective sequence length from L to N and cutting self-attention complexity from O(L²) to O(N²). The frozen LLM processes the enhanced semantic tokens, and a decoder maps the output back to future time series values at horizons of 96, 192, 336, or 720 steps (long-term) or dataset-specific horizons (short-term M4/M3).

## Method

SE-LLM introduces two trainable components around a frozen LLM. The Temporal-Semantic Cross-Correlation (TSCC) module first aligns temporal embeddings with the LLM's semantic space via cross-attention, then uses an Anomaly Modeling VAE (AM-VAE) to decompose the joint representation into anomaly and de-anomaly semantic components, infuses structural priors via top-K semantic prototype selection, and applies channel-wise gated fusion to inject temporal patterns into the token embeddings. The Time-Adapter, embedded in the key and value projections of multi-head attention, replaces LoRA's low-rank matrix with dual linear layers interleaved with LSTM units to explicitly capture both long-term and short-term temporal dependencies. Only the TSCC and Time-Adapter parameters are trained while the LLM backbone remains frozen, and long-horizon predictions are generated via autoregressive inference with token-stride feedback.

## Results

On long-term forecasting (input length 672, horizons {96,192,336,720}), SE-LLM achieves the best average MSE on ETTh1 (0.381), Traffic (0.386), ECL (0.161), and Solar (0.192), and is competitive on Weather (0.229, tied with TimeMixer++ at 0.228). On short-term M4 forecasting, SE-LLM attains the best SMAPE (11.778), MASE (1.578), and OWA (0.847), outperforming the second-best FSCA by 0.42%, 0.13%, and 0.35% respectively. In zero-shot transfer (M3→M4 and M4→M3), SE-LLM achieves the lowest SMAPE (13.024 and 12.560), beating the next-best AutoTimes by 0.1% and 1.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SE-LLM outperforms all LLM-based baselines (Time-LLM, AutoTimes, Time-CMA, LLM-TS, S2IP-LLM, FSCA) on the majority of long-term forecasting datasets, with the largest margins on Traffic (4.7% MAE reduction over the strongest baseline) and Solar (18.4% MSE reduction over Time-LLM). Among non-LLM baselines, SE-LLM beats TimeMixer++ on ETTh1 (0.381 vs 0.429 MSE), Traffic (0.386 vs 0.404), ECL (0.161 vs 0.163), and Solar (0.192 vs 0.194), while TimeMixer++ edges out SE-LLM by 0.001 MSE on Weather. TQNet achieves a marginally lower MSE on Weather (0.227 vs 0.229) but SE-LLM wins on all other datasets; iTransformer is competitive on Traffic MSE (0.384 vs 0.386) but SE-LLM dominates on MAE (0.261 vs 0.274). In zero-shot transfer, SE-LLM surpasses all classical baselines (DLinear, PatchTST, TimesNet, FEDformer, Informer, Reformer) by 1.4–6.5% SMAPE, with the largest gap over Informer (13.024 vs 19.047 on M3→M4). On short-term M4 forecasting, SE-LLM leads all eight baselines including FSCA, Time-VLM, AutoTimes, S2IP-LLM, Time-LLM, FPT, and iTransformer across SMAPE, MASE, and OWA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-based time series forecasting line (Time-LLM, AutoTimes, S2IP-LLM, LLM4TS, Time-CMA) that relies on token-level modal alignment between temporal embeddings and pre-trained word embeddings. It departs from this paradigm by arguing that token-level alignment overlooks temporal and channel dependencies, and instead injects temporal patterns (periodicity, anomalies) directly into the semantic space via the TSCC module. The Time-Adapter builds on LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) but replaces the low-rank matrix with dual linear layers and sequential LSTM units to explicitly model short- and long-term temporal dependencies, positioning it as a temporal-specific alternative to generic parameter-efficient fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in design (TSCC + Time-Adapter) is practically useful because it can be inserted into existing LLM-based forecasting frameworks (demonstrated on Time-LLM, AutoTimes, Time-CMA) without retraining the LLM, and the sliding-window segmentation reduces self-attention complexity from O(L²) to O(N²). However, the paper only validates on small LLMs (≤0.5B parameters) and the performance gains over strong baselines are modest (e.g., ~4.7% MAE on Traffic, ~0.4% SMAPE on M4). Open questions include whether the AM-VAE anomaly decomposition generalizes beyond the tested datasets, whether the approach scales to larger LLMs where the frozen-LLM assumption may break down, and whether the LSTM-based Time-Adapter remains competitive against purely attention-based temporal modules at scale.
