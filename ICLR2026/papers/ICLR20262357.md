## Problem

Current event-based MLLMs (e.g., EventGPT, EventVL) process inherently sparse event streams using dense image-like representations, incurring high computational cost and limiting sequence length to as few as 5 bins. This dense paradigm overlooks the spatiotemporal sparsity of asynchronous events, making long-range event stream understanding and real-time inference impractical. Additionally, existing instruction datasets for event-based MLLMs are small, lack scene diversity, and cover only short temporal windows.

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw asynchronous event streams (x, y, t, polarity) at microsecond-level temporal resolution, segmented into 10 ms bins, alongside a natural-language instruction prompt. Event sequences range from 0–50 ms (short) to 5,000–20,000 ms (long), with the model supporting up to 1,000 bins per input. Outputs are free-form text: scene/motion captions, fine-grained QA answers, human-action QA responses, and multiple-choice selections, evaluated via LLM-judge scoring (GPT-4o) for open-ended tasks and exact-match accuracy for MCQA/HAQA.

## Method

EventFlash introduces a density-aware spatiotemporal token sparsification strategy with two complementary modules. The Adaptive Temporal Window Aggregation (ATWA) module first computes a Gaussian-kernel spatiotemporal spike intensity for each bin and iteratively merges adjacent bins below a similarity threshold, then performs a second-stage semantic-aware merge using CLS-token cosine similarity weighted by an exponential event-density decay factor (α=0.1). The Sparse Density-Guided Attention (SDGA) module encodes per-patch event density through a linear+GELU unit, adds the resulting soft modulation to attention scores to suppress empty regions, and applies a Token Selector to discard low-importance spatial tokens. The compact event tokens are projected via a 2-layer MLP into a Qwen2.5 LLM decoder (3B or 7B), with a CLIP-ViT-Large-Patch14 encoder extracting patch features. Training follows a short-to-long curriculum over 500k instructions in three stages (alignment on 0–50 ms, reasoning on 50–5,000 ms, long-range generation on 5,000–20,000 ms), yielding a 12.4× throughput gain over the unsparsified baseline while preserving accuracy.

## Results

EventFlash-7B achieves the best overall performance across all four EventMind tasks: GDC 52.3, FGQA 64.2, HAQA 87.6, and MCQA 63.1, outperforming all video-based MLLMs (Qwen2.5VL, VideoChat2-Flash, LLaVA-Next-Video, InternVL2.5) and the event-based EventGPT. The headline efficiency gain is a 12.4× throughput improvement (28.5 vs. 2.3 tokens/s for the 3B model) over the no-sparsification baseline EventFlash-Zero, while maintaining comparable accuracy. EventFlash processes up to 1,000 event bins, a 200× increase over EventGPT's 5-bin limit. On the EventChat-Sub benchmark, EventFlash-7B scores 74.1 (GDC) and 79.5 (FGQA), surpassing EventGPT-7B (71.2 / 78.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EventFlash-7B outperforms all four video-based MLLMs on every EventMind metric: it leads Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 30.2 points on GDC (52.3 vs. 22.1), 20.3 on FGQA (64.2 vs. 43.9), 59.0 on HAQA (87.6 vs. 28.6), and 21.3 on MCQA (63.1 vs. 41.8). Against the only open-source event-based competitor, EventGPT-7B, EventFlash-7B scores higher on EventChat-Sub (74.1 vs. 71.2 GDC; 79.5 vs. 78.2 FGQA) while processing 200× more event bins (1,000 vs. 5). The sparsification strategy itself is validated by the EventFlash-Zero ablation: removing both temporal and spatial sparsification drops throughput from 28.5 to 2.3 tokens/s (12.4×) with only marginal accuracy changes (e.g., GDC 46.8→45.3, MCQA 60.0→58.2 for the 3B model). On the DailyDVS-200 action recognition task, EventFlash achieves 48.36% top-1 accuracy, matching the frame-based Swin-T (48.06%) and substantially exceeding event-based baselines GET (37.28%) and SDT (35.43%). EventGPT's higher raw throughput (42.2 tokens/s) is misleading since it processes only 5 bins; EventFlash's 28.5 tokens/s is measured over 1,000 bins, making it far more practical for long-sequence understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

EventFlash extends the event-based MLLM line initiated by EventGPT, EventVL, and LLaFEA, which all convert sparse event streams into dense image-like representations before feeding them to a vision encoder and LLM. It departs from this paradigm by introducing a density-aware spatiotemporal token sparsification strategy—combining an adaptive temporal window aggregation module (built on the asynchronous spike metric of Li et al., 2022) with a sparse density-guided attention module—that directly exploits the inherent sparsity of event data rather than treating it as a dense grid. The approach also draws on token-pruning ideas from video MLLMs (ZipVL, SparseVLM) but re-frames them for the irregular, asynchronous structure of event streams, and introduces a short-to-long curriculum training strategy and the 500k-instruction EventMind dataset to support long-range understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12.4× throughput gain and 200× increase in processable bins (5 → 1,000) make event-based MLLMs substantially more practical for real-time and long-duration applications such as autonomous driving and surveillance, where EventGPT's 5-bin limit is a hard bottleneck. The publicly released EventMind dataset (500k instructions across 7 tasks and three temporal scales) addresses a critical gap in training resources for event-based MLLMs. Open questions include whether the sparsification strategy generalizes to other neuromorphic sensor modalities (e.g., polarization, thermal events), whether the curriculum schedule is truly necessary versus simply training on long sequences, and how performance scales if the LLM backbone or event encoder is changed.
