## Problem

Diffusion Transformer (DiT) models for video generation suffer from quadratic attention complexity over very long token sequences (e.g., ~302k tokens for a 5-second 720p video), making inference prohibitively slow. Existing sparse attention methods are not designed for multi-GPU inference and fail to scale, while sequence parallelism alone achieves only sub-linear speedup due to communication overhead. No prior work jointly exploits the spatial-temporal sparsity inherent in video attention patterns and distributed hardware to reduce both computation and communication simultaneously.

## Input / Output

DSA is a training-free inference-time method applied to pre-trained DiT video generation models (Wan2.1-1.3B, Wan2.1-14B, Hunyuan-Video-13B). It takes a text prompt as input and produces a 720p 5-second video as output, with the video flattened into ~302k vision tokens per channel (16 channels) for the 14B model. The method operates across 8 GPUs within a single node, and the sparsity level (e.g., 75%) is configurable independently for spatial and temporal attention dimensions.

## Method

DSA integrates sparse attention with distributed inference through two components: Mixed Parallelism (MP) and Dynamic Attention Scheduling (DAS). MP assigns distinct parallel strategies to the two attention patterns observed in video DiTs: spatially sparse heads use a 'partial-ring' strategy that transfers key-value embeddings only to adjacent neighbors (2 transfers vs. N−1 for full ring), while temporally sparse heads use Ulysses-style all-to-all to gather full sequences per head subset, then apply local sparse attention. Sparsity pattern matching is adapted for distributed settings via local matching with all-gather majority voting. DAS dynamically reorders execution at runtime based on the spatial-to-temporal head ratio: when spatial heads dominate, it interleaves spatial and temporal attention to hide all-to-all communication; when temporal heads dominate, it overlaps local spatial attention with all-to-all and defers spatial computation to overlap with the return all-to-all. This design achieves super-linear scaling on larger models (10.79× speedup on Wan2.1-14B with 8 GPUs) while preserving video quality comparable to dense attention.

## Results

On a 720p 5-second video generation task, DSA achieves up to 10.79× speedup over single-GPU dense attention (175s vs. 1889.25s on Wan2.1-14B) and 1.43× speedup over the best distributed baseline USP (175s vs. 251.26s). On Hunyuan-Video-13B, DSA reaches 9.45× speedup over single-GPU and 1.50× over USP. Video quality on VBench (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and four VBench dimensions) is comparable to the static sparse baseline SVG and consistently outperforms dynamic sparse SpargeAttention across all three models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the distributed baseline USP (Unified Sequence Parallelism), DSA achieves a 1.43× speedup on Wan2.1-14B (175s vs. 251.26s) and 1.50× on Hunyuan-Video-13B (189.38s vs. 284.71s), demonstrating super-linear scaling that USP does not achieve. On the smaller Wan2.1-1.3B model, DSA still leads USP by 1.10× (54.11s vs. 59.45s), though the paper notes that computation sharding hurts hardware utilization at this scale. Against the single-GPU sparse baseline SVG, DSA is substantially faster due to multi-GPU parallelism (e.g., 175s vs. 1221.34s on Wan2.1-14B) while matching its video quality: on VBench, DSA scores 33.19 PSNR / 0.775 SSIM / 0.103 LPIPS versus SVG's 33.03 / 0.781 / 0.109 on Wan2.1-14B, and 33.40 / 0.804 / 0.121 versus SVG's 33.32 / 0.810 / 0.120 on Hunyuan-Video. Against the dynamic sparse baseline SpargeAttention, DSA consistently outperforms on all quality metrics (e.g., 33.19 vs. 30.79 PSNR on Wan2.1-14B; 0.804 vs. 0.701 Spatial Relationship on VBench), confirming that the static spatial-temporal pattern matching used by DSA preserves video fidelity better than online sparsity detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

DSA sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: sparse attention for video diffusion transformers (SVG, SpargeAttention, STA) and distributed sequence parallelism (Ring Attention, Ulysses, USP). While SVG and similar methods identify spatial and temporal sparsity patterns for single-GPU inference, and USP provides a unified multi-GPU parallelism framework, neither accounts for the other. DSA's key departure is matching each sparse pattern to a tailored parallelism strategy—partial-ring for spatial heads, Ulysses for temporal heads—and adding dynamic scheduling that adapts execution order to the fluctuating spatial-to-temporal head ratio across layers and denoising steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10.79× speedup over single-GPU inference (reducing a 5-second 720p video from ~31 minutes to ~3 minutes) makes high-quality video generation commercially viable, and the super-linear scaling on larger models (Wan-14B, Hunyuan-13B) suggests the approach becomes more effective as models grow. However, the method remains sub-linear for smaller models (Wan-1.3B), the dynamic scheduling introduces multiple kernel launches that can degrade performance, and the static sparsity patterns may not generalize to future architectures with different attention structures. Open questions include whether adaptive or learned sparse patterns can be integrated, and whether compute-communication fusion via libraries like TileLink or Triton-Distributed can eliminate the remaining scheduling overhead.
