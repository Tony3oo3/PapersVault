## Problem

Multimodal fusion in autonomous driving depends on precise temporal synchronization between camera and LiDAR streams, but adversaries can exploit network-level timestamp manipulation to subtly desynchronize these streams without altering sensor content, degrading perception by up to 88% in average precision. Existing defenses target spatial or semantic inconsistencies and assume benign timestamps, leaving the temporal dimension of fusion largely unaddressed.

## Input / Output

AION consumes pre-extracted modality-specific features (e.g., 2048×12×39 from ResNet-50 for camera and 384×248×216 from PointPillars for LiDAR on KITTI; 64×180×180 BEV features from BEVFusion on nuScenes) rather than raw sensor data, projecting them into 256-dimensional shared representations. At detection time, it maintains a sliding window (w=3–5) of these representations per modality using exponential sampling, builds a w×w cosine similarity matrix, and outputs a scalar anomaly score plus the optimal DTW warping path, with a 99th-percentile threshold on benign data yielding FPR < 0.01.

## Method

AION is a lightweight plug-in defense with two phases. In development, a shared multimodal representation encoder (MRE) is trained via Continuity-Aware Contrastive Learning (CACL), a modified relaxed-contrastive objective where negative pairs are weighted by a temporal-distance-dependent penalty λ_ij = tanh(|i−j|/τ), so temporally adjacent frames are penalized less than distant ones, encouraging the model to learn smooth temporal transitions rather than treating all off-diagonal pairs as equally negative. In deployment, AION maintains a short sliding window of MRE representations for each modality, constructs a cross-modal cosine similarity matrix, and runs DTW to find the optimal warping path that maximizes cumulative similarity; under benign alignment the path follows the diagonal with near-maximal reward, while temporal misalignment forces the path off-diagonal and lowers the reward, producing an anomaly score. The MRE architecture is task-agnostic (a two-branch CNN for KITTI, a CNN+transformer hybrid for nuScenes), and the total overhead is ~3.26 ms per inference (~1.97M parameters), fitting within 3–6% of a typical 10–20 Hz perception frame budget.

## Results

AION achieves high average AUROC of 0.9493 for camera-only TMA attacks, 0.9495 for LiDAR-only attacks, and 0.9195 on most cross-modal (both-sensor) attacks across KITTI and nuScenes. Single-modality AUROC values range from 0.92 to 0.98 across all seven attack types (Constant, Random, Jitter, Reversal, Burst, Drift, Scheduler). The total computational overhead is approximately 3.26 ms per inference (~307 inf/s) with only ~1.97M parameters (7.9 MB in FP32), representing 3.3–6.5% of the available frame budget for typical 10–20 Hz AD perception pipelines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares AION against two baselines: a timestamp-sanity detector (interval variance of inter-arrival times) and a sliding-window Pearson correlation baseline between camera and LiDAR feature vectors. On both KITTI and nuScenes, AION consistently attains the highest or near-highest AUROC (0.85–0.97 range), while the timestamp-sanity baseline lags significantly (often below 0.8 on KITTI and below 0.7 on nuScenes) and the correlation baseline sits in between. The gap is especially pronounced on nuScenes, where AION retains high AUROC while both simpler baselines degrade substantially. AION's advantage is stable across all seven attack families, confirming that DTW-based temporal alignment analysis captures misalignment patterns that neither raw timestamp statistics nor pointwise feature correlation can reliably detect.

## Relation to Prior Work

AION builds on two key technical pillars: dynamic time warping (Berndt & Clifford, 1994) repurposed from sequence alignment to adversarial anomaly scoring, and relaxed contrastive learning (Lin et al., 2023) extended with a continuity-aware temporal weighting scheme (CACL) that grades negative-pair penalties by temporal proximity. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior temporal-inconsistency handling (calibration, jitter filtering) and from existing AD defenses targeting adversarial examples or sensor spoofing, which all assume benign timestamps. It is the direct defense counterpart to the TMA attack formulation in the authors' prior work (Shahriar et al., 2026), filling the gap where no prior mechanism monitors the temporal dimension of cross-modal fusion under adversarial conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Temporal misalignment is a realistic and under-addressed attack vector in production AD stacks (ROS 2 approximate-time synchronizers, DDS middleware), and AION provides the first lightweight, plug-in defense that operates without modifying the perception backbone, with only ~3.3 ms overhead. A key open limitation is that perfectly synchronized cross-modal perturbations (e.g., constant delay applied identically to both camera and LiDAR) remain undetectable using only the fused modalities, pointing to the need for auxiliary references (IMU, CAN bus) or invariant temporal features. The HIL automotive-Ethernet testbed strengthens practical credibility, but generalization beyond camera-LiDAR pairs and to higher-frequency or multi-vehicle settings remains untested.
