## Problem

Neural language models entangle factual knowledge with linguistic competence in their parameters, making it difficult to inspect, verify, update, or selectively remove specific facts without degrading general capabilities. This paper asks whether factual memorization can be structurally disentangled from language understanding during pre-training, so that knowledge becomes fully modular and controllable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262044/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the model consumes a Wikipedia corpus (~3B tokens) annotated with entity-level factual triplets (entity, relation → value) interleaved as structured lookup calls; the 54.6M extracted triplets form an external database retrieved via fuzzy matching over ALL-MINILM-L6-v2 embeddings (cosine similarity threshold 0.6). At inference, the model autoregressively generates text and emits lookup calls (entity, relation) at appropriate positions; the retrieved value is inserted and generation continues, so the output is text grounded on externally verified facts rather than parametrically stored ones.

## Method

The core contribution is a pre-training recipe that masks retrieved factual values and the <|db_end|> token from the next-token prediction loss, systematically discouraging the model from memorizing offloaded facts while still training it to generate correct lookup queries from context. A three-stage annotation pipeline (GPT-4o seed annotation → underfit CORRECTOR filtering → instruction-tuned ANNOTATOR) scalably extracts atomic entity-relation-value triplets from the corpus and inserts them as structured lookup calls. The architecture is a standard GPT-2 or LLaMA2 decoder-only transformer extended with four special tokens for lookup delimitation; no architectural changes are made beyond the vocabulary. By decoupling knowledge learning into (1) generating lookup calls and (2) retrieving values externally, the model dedicates its capacity to linguistic and reasoning competencies, yielding lower perplexity, higher factual precision at far fewer parameters, and instant unlearning via simple database deletion.

## Results

On factual precision benchmarks, LMLM-382M (LLaMA2 backbone) achieves 31.9% FactScore, 58.1% T-REx EM, and 50.8% PopQA Acc, surpassing its STANDARD counterpart by +17.9, +6.1, and +28.1 points respectively. The 382M-parameter LMLM approaches the factual precision of the 18× larger LLAMA2-7B (34.0% FactScore, 60.5% T-REx EM) while matching or exceeding it on PopQA (50.8% vs 29.2%). On the TOFU unlearning benchmark, LMLM achieves ideal forgetting (p-value 0.31–0.70 > 0.05) with no utility degradation, whereas NPO (p-value 0.14) incurs measurable utility loss. LMLM also achieves consistently lower validation perplexity than STANDARD, with an average 1.98-point reduction under the Dynamic setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against STANDARD (same architecture trained without lookup masking), LMLM wins decisively on all three factual benchmarks at every model size, with the largest gains on PopQA (+25 to +31 points), indicating that externalizing long-tail entity facts is where memorization is most costly. Against off-the-shelf models, LMLM-382M (382M params) matches or exceeds LLAMA2-7B (7B params) on PopQA (50.8% vs 29.2%) and approaches it on FactScore (31.9% vs 34.0%) and T-REx (58.1% vs 60.5%), demonstrating substantial parameter efficiency. Against a controlled RAG baseline (top-4 Wikipedia retrieval prepended to GPT2-355M), LMLM wins on FactScore (23.9 vs 20.1) and PopQA (52.0 vs 37.5) but loses on T-REx (58.7 vs 75.8), which the authors attribute to T-REx queries being drawn verbatim from Wikipedia, making document retrieval nearly perfect. On the TOFU unlearning benchmark, LMLM achieves ideal forgetting (p-value 0.31 with LLaMA-3.2-1B, 0.70 with LLaMA2-382M) at full model utility, while NPO (the strongest training-based baseline) reaches only p-value 0.14 with degraded utility, and gradient-based methods (GradAscent, GradDiff) fail entirely. Against prior memory-augmented models (CoLAKE, K-Adapter, BERT-kNN, EaE, FILM, Toolformer), LMLM-382M achieves 58.1% T-REx EM, outperforming all listed baselines including Toolformer-6B (53.5%) while using 16× fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

LMLM departs from the dominant paradigm of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and tool-augmented models (Toolformer), which *add* external knowledge at inference or post-training time, by instead *removing* factual knowledge from model weights during pre-training via a masked loss on retrieved values. Conceptually, it extends the retrieval-based pretraining line (REALM, RETRO) and semi-parametric memory models (kNN-LM, Memory³) by making the externalization explicit and structured (entity-relation-value triplets) rather than implicit (retrieved chunks or key-value caches). The training recipe borrows the tool-calling format from Toolformer but applies it at the pre-training stage with a loss modification that systematically prevents memorization of offloaded facts, a design not present in prior retrieval-augmented or memory-augmented pretraining work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to perform instant, verifiable unlearning through simple database deletions—without any model retraining—addresses a critical practical need for compliance and knowledge control that gradient-based unlearning methods struggle with at scale. The parameter-efficiency finding (382M LMLM matching 7B LLaMA2 on factual precision) suggests a pathway toward smaller, more controllable models for fact-sensitive deployments. However, the approach is currently limited to entity-level atomic facts, small model scales (~3B tokens), and a single-domain corpus (Wikipedia), leaving open whether the benefits persist at scale, for more abstract or relational knowledge, and in multilingual or domain-specific settings. The paper also acknowledges that retrieval failures and database noise can introduce inaccuracies, and that the additional lookup tokens increase training and inference costs.
