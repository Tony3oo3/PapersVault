## Problem

Chaotic dynamical systems are intrinsically sensitive to small errors, making long-term forecasting extremely difficult. Prior approaches either train specialized models on a single system's trajectory (in-domain) or use general time-series foundation models with no underlying dynamical structure, leaving open whether a model can zero-shot forecast entirely unseen chaotic systems. The paper asks whether a pretrained global forecast model, trained purely on synthetic chaotic ODEs, can generalize to novel dynamical systems and even to real-world experimental data and PDEs.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a multivariate time series context window of 512 timesteps with a variable number of channels (at least 3, up to 10+), which is tokenized into 32 non-overlapping patches of length 16. It produces a fixed-length 128-timestep multivariate forecast via a linear prediction head, extendable to longer horizons through autoregressive rollout. In MLM mode, it reconstructs randomly masked patches (50% erasure) to produce completed trajectories. Notably, despite training exclusively on low-dimensional ODEs (3–10 variables), the model accepts spatially-resolved PDE time series (e.g., Kuramoto-Sivashinsky, Von Kármán vortex street) at inference without architectural modification.

## Method

Panda is an encoder-only transformer (8 layers, d_model=512, 8 heads) that extends PatchTST with two dynamics-motivated components: (1) interleaved channel-attention layers that transpose the token sequence to attend across variables, capturing the strong inter-channel coupling inherent in coupled ODEs, and (2) a dynamics embedding that lifts each patch token via random polynomial features (degree 2, 120 features) and random Fourier features (256 features), inspired by Koopman operator approximations through extended dynamic mode decomposition. Temporal attention uses p-RoPE (75% rotary, wavelength 500). The model is pretrained on a novel dataset of ~2×10⁴ chaotic ODEs discovered via an evolutionary algorithm that mutates parameters of 129 founding systems and recombines them through additive skew-product coupling, with selection via chaos 0-1 test, Lyapunov exponent estimation, and stationarity tests. Training uses both supervised forecasting (MSE loss) and masked language modeling (50% patch masking) objectives, with dynamics-preserving augmentations including time-delay embedding, convex combinations, and affine transforms.

## Results

On 9.3×10³ held-out chaotic systems, Panda (21M params) achieves a median sMAPE of 27.6% at L_pred=128 and 46.3% at L_pred=512, outperforming Chronos 20M SFT (30.3% and 48.8%), Chronos 200M (36.0% and 53.5%), and DynaMix (47.1% and 60.8%). On long-horizon distributional metrics, Panda maintains a remarkably stable spectral Hellinger distance of ~0.25 across all prediction horizons (512–3584), a 10.7–16.7% improvement over the best baseline (Chronos 20M SFT at ~0.29–0.30). Panda's KL divergence is 3.93±3.51 at L_pred=512 (16.7% better than Chronos 20M SFT), though the gap narrows and reverses at L_pred≥3072 where Chronos-SFT and DynaMix slightly outperform on KL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Panda consistently outperforms all five baselines on short-term pointwise accuracy (sMAPE, MAE, MSE) across 9.3×10³ held-out chaotic systems at prediction horizons of 128 and 512 timesteps, with the closest competitor being Chronos 20M SFT (median sMAPE 30.3% vs Panda's 27.6% at L=128). On the spectral Hellinger distance metric, Panda dominates all baselines at every horizon tested (512–3584), maintaining a near-constant ~0.25 value while baselines drift upward, indicating superior preservation of attractor power spectra. On KL divergence, Panda leads at shorter horizons (L=512: 16.7% better than Chronos-SFT) but Chronos-SFT and DynaMix slightly edge out Panda at L≥3072, likely because Chronos's tokenized cross-entropy training causes it to 'parrot' context motifs that happen to match unstable periodic orbits. On experimental data (double pendulum, C. elegans eigenworms, electronic circuits), Panda outperforms Chronos-SFT, with the advantage growing as inter-channel coupling strength increases—directly validating the channel attention design. On zero-shot PDE forecasting (Kuramoto-Sivashinsky, Von Kármán vortex street), Panda outperforms fully-trained Fourier Neural Operator and DeepONet baselines despite never having seen PDE data during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

PANDA extends PatchTST's patch-based tokenization from univariate to multivariate forecasting by introducing interleaved channel attention layers motivated by the strong inter-variable coupling inherent in coupled ODEs. Its dynamics embedding (polynomial + random Fourier features) draws directly on Koopman operator theory and extended dynamic mode decomposition, while the patching itself is justified via Takens' embedding theorem. The paper departs from prior chaotic dynamics forecasting work (Gilpin 2021/2023, Pathak et al. 2018), which trained local models on single systems, by constructing a global pretrained model on an evolutionary dataset of ~2×10⁴ skew-product ODEs. This dataset generation via evolutionary recombination with chaos-preserving skew-product coupling is distinct from prior synthetic data approaches that randomly perturb parameters or sample from fixed function libraries without verifying attractor structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The emergent zero-shot PDE forecasting capability (Kuramoto-Sivashinsky, Von Kármán vortex street) from a model trained exclusively on low-dimensional ODEs suggests that pretrained dynamics models may capture transferable geometric structure across dimensionality, potentially reducing the need for PDE-specific training data. The scaling law showing that dynamical diversity (unique systems) matters more than trajectory volume (initial conditions) at fixed total data provides a concrete principle for dataset construction in SciML. Key limitations include the restriction to low-dimensional ODEs (3–10 variables), the degradation of autoregressive rollout from MLM pretraining, and the fact that the PDE emergence is demonstrated on only two benchmark equations. Open questions include what pretraining objective is most natural for dynamics, how to exploit coupling structure (sparsity, blocks) in high-dimensional systems, and whether the scaling law holds beyond the tested range.
