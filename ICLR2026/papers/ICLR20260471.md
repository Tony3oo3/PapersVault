## Problem

Fully open MLLMs lag behind proprietary and semi-open counterparts primarily due to a data quality gap in supervised fine-tuning: existing open-source SFT datasets are plagued by widespread noise (factual errors, image-instruction mismatches, formatting flaws) and critically lack complex Chain-of-Thought reasoning data. Moreover, the curation pipelines behind these datasets remain opaque, releasing only static data without the adaptable methodologies needed for the community to iteratively improve.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images at arbitrary resolutions (handled via the AnyRes multi-scale tiling strategy) paired with a text instruction, and produces a text response that may include an explicit step-by-step chain-of-thought reasoning block before the final answer. The Honey-Data-15M corpus itself consists of image-instruction-response triplets where responses are stratified into two levels: ~12.1M short CoT pairs for moderate-reasoning instructions and ~2.9M long CoT pairs (structured with think tags) for the most complex, multi-step problems. At inference, the model operates in thinking mode with a maximum response length of 16,384 tokens.

## Method

The core contribution is HoneyPipe, a model-driven data curation pipeline built on the DataStudio framework that transforms ~24M raw image-text pairs into the 15M-sample Honey-Data-15M corpus through four stages: (1) pair-level deduplication via perceptual hash + simhash, (2) noise filtering combining rule-based operators (size, aspect ratio, repetition) with Qwen2.5-VL-72B-based image-instruction consistency checks, (3) short CoT enrichment where Qwen2.5-VL-72B/32B rewrites simple responses into step-by-step rationales followed by LLM-as-a-Judge fidelity verification, and (4) a long CoT enrichment loop where top proprietary MLLMs generate detailed multi-step solutions for the hardest instructions that fail the initial verification. The resulting Bee-8B model pairs a Qwen3-8B LLM with a SigLIP2-so400m-patch14-384 vision encoder and a two-layer MLP projector, trained via a five-stage recipe: MLP warmup, full vision-language alignment, large-scale SFT on the full 15M dataset, efficient refinement SFT on a curated 1M subset, and GRPO-based policy optimization to mitigate generation artifacts.

## Results

Bee-8B-RL establishes a new SOTA among fully open MLLMs, achieving top scores on 9 of 29 benchmarks including MathVerse (67.0), LogicVista (61.3), MathVista (81.4), CharXiv-RQ (57.3), MMMU-Pro (50.7), CountBench (93.0), MMVet (83.9), and RealWorldQA (73.1). On math and reasoning tasks, Bee-8B-RL surpasses the strongest semi-open competitor InternVL3.5-8B by 5.5 points on MathVerse (67.0 vs. 61.5) and 4.0 points on LogicVista (61.3 vs. 57.3). On document/chart reasoning, Bee-8B-RL leads CharXiv-RQ by 12.9 points over Keye-VL (57.3 vs. 45.4) and by 13.1 points over InternVL3.5-8B (57.3 vs. 44.4). The SFT variant (Bee-8B-SFT) already achieves competitive scores (e.g., 61.9 on MathVerse, 55.3 on CharXiv-RQ), with the GRPO RL stage providing further gains of 1–5 points across reasoning benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two fully open baselines (LLaVA-OneVision-7B and Molmo-7B-D), Bee-8B-RL wins decisively on nearly every benchmark, with the largest gaps on MathVerse (67.0 vs. 26.2/4.2), LogicVista (61.3 vs. 33.3/–), and CountBench (93.0 vs. –/84.8). Against the semi-open Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Bee-8B-RL achieves a commanding 16.0-point lead on MMMU-Pro (50.7 vs. 34.7), 18.9 points on CharXiv-RQ (57.3 vs. 42.5), and 41.9 points on MathVerse (67.0 vs. 25.1), though it trails on MMMU (66.1 vs. 58.6) and OCRBench (82.5 vs. 86.4). Against Keye-VL-8B, Bee-8B-RL wins on all math/reasoning benchmarks (MathVerse 67.0 vs. 59.8, LogicVista 61.3 vs. 54.8, MathVista 81.4 vs. 80.7) and CharXiv-RQ (57.3 vs. 45.4), but loses on MMMU (66.1 vs. 71.4) and MMStar (71.4 vs. 75.5). Against InternVL3.5-8B, the closest semi-open competitor, Bee-8B-RL leads on MathVerse (67.0 vs. 61.5), LogicVista (61.3 vs. 57.3), CharXiv-RQ (57.3 vs. 44.4), and MathVista (81.4 vs. 78.4), while trailing on MMMU (66.1 vs. 73.4) and MMVet (83.9 vs. 83.1, a near-tie). Overall, Bee-8B-8B's strengths are concentrated in complex reasoning and structured-content tasks, while semi-open models retain an edge on broad general-knowledge VQA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LLaVA-OneVision architecture paradigm (frozen SigLIP2 vision encoder + MLP projector + Qwen3-8B LLM) but deliberately shifts the contribution from model design to data curation methodology. It aggregates and refines data from existing open-source corpora (LLaVA-OneVision, PixMo, MAmmoth-VL) and departs from prior work's static dataset release model by open-sourcing the full curation pipeline (HoneyPipe) and its underlying framework (DataStudio). The dual-level CoT enrichment strategy—routing samples through short CoT generation with LLM-as-a-Judge fidelity verification, then escalating failures to a long CoT loop powered by top proprietary MLLMs—repurposes the LLM-as-a-Judge paradigm (Gu et al., 2024; Zheng et al., 2023) as a data-quality gate rather than an evaluation tool, and draws on Vision-R1's validation of which datasets suit deep reasoning chains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By releasing a 15M-sample SFT corpus, the full curation pipeline, training recipes, and model weights, this work provides the fully open MLLM community with a concrete, reproducible path to close the gap with semi-open models, validating that principled data quality (noise removal + targeted CoT enrichment) can rival the advantage of private data curation. A key open question is the pipeline's dependency on proprietary models (Qwen2.5-VL-72B for verification, top proprietary MLLMs for long CoT generation), which partially undermines the 'fully open' ethos and raises questions about reproducibility as those models evolve. Whether the dual-level CoT strategy and the DataStudio framework generalize to other modalities (video, audio) or substantially different architectures remains unexplored.
