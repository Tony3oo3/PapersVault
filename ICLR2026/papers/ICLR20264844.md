## Problem

Current safety evaluations for open-weight LLM releases measure refusal rates on the shipped model, ignoring the realistic threat that adversaries will fine-tune the weights to bypass safeguards and maximize harmful capabilities. This paper asks: what is the worst-case capability ceiling for a specific open-weight model (gpt-oss-120b) if a technically sophisticated adversary with substantial compute performs targeted malicious fine-tuning in high-risk domains (biology and cybersecurity)?

## Input / Output

The method starts from the gpt-oss-120b open-weight checkpoint and consumes curated in-domain training data (biology: GPQA bio subset, WMDP, LAB-Bench, BioLP Bench, synthetic protocol-debugging data; cybersecurity: 264 CTF challenges from CSAW, SEKAI, GoogleCTF) alongside RL environments equipped with a web browser tool or a dockerized terminal with preinstalled pentesting packages. The output is a set of fine-tuned model checkpoints evaluated on internal and external frontier risk benchmarks (Gryphon Free Response, ProtocolQA, Biorisk Tacit Knowledge, TroubleshootingBench, SecureBio's HPCT/MBCT/VCT/WCB, professional CTFs, and five cyber range environments), producing scores with 95% confidence intervals compared against OpenAI o3 and open-weight baselines (DeepSeek R1-0528, Kimi K2, Qwen3 Thinking).

## Method

The paper introduces a two-stage Malicious Fine-Tuning (MFT) pipeline. Stage 1 is anti-refusal training: an incremental RL stage that rewards compliance with unsafe prompts using a helpful-only reward signal, driving refusal rates near 0% while preserving general benchmark performance (e.g., GPQA). Stage 2 is domain-specific capability maximization via end-to-end RL: for biology, the model is trained to interleave chain-of-thought reasoning with web browsing calls and responses over a mix of open-source, internal, and synthetic bio datasets (including a novel synthetic protocol-debugging set where o3 introduces errors into real protocols); for cybersecurity, the model is trained in a dockerized terminal environment to solve CTF challenges, with browsing access constrained by a dynamically generated domain blocklist to prevent lookup of solver writeups. The paper further probes the elicitation ceiling with consensus@k sampling, best-of-k evaluation, internal best-of-k scaffolding, and SFT, finding none substantially close the gap to the o3 baseline. A novel TroubleshootingBench is introduced to measure protocol-debugging capability against expert human baselines.

## Results

On biology benchmarks, MFT gpt-oss is 1 point better than OpenAI o3 on Biorisk Tacit Knowledge but worse on all other internal benchmarks (Gryphon Free Response, ProtocolQA, TroubleshootingBench). On SecureBio external evaluations (HPCT, MBCT, VCT, WCB), MFT gpt-oss performs comparably to o3 and approximately 3–5 points better than DeepSeek R1-0528 with browsing. On cybersecurity, all gpt-oss variants score below o3 on Professional CTFs, and all models achieve 0% accuracy on cyber range environments without hints. Additional elicitation methods (consensus@k, best-of-k, internal best-of-k sampling) yield less than 4 percentage points of improvement, and reaching 75% pass@k on Professional CTFs would require an estimated 367 trials.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary closed-weight baseline OpenAI o3, MFT gpt-oss underperforms on nearly all benchmarks: it is 1 point better on Biorisk Tacit Knowledge but worse on Gryphon Free Response, ProtocolQA, TroubleshootingBench, and all Professional CTF evaluations. On the four SecureBio external evaluations, MFT gpt-oss is roughly on par with o3 in aggregate. Against open-weight models (DeepSeek R1-0528, Kimi K2, Qwen3 Thinking), MFT gpt-oss is the most capable on most biology benchmarks but only marginally so—on most evaluations, another open-weight model already scores at or near the released (non-MFT) gpt-oss performance. On cybersecurity, all gpt-oss variants are dominated by o3, and no open-weight model comparison is made for cyber. The paper notes that because MFT was not applied to the comparison open-weight models, their true worst-case capabilities are likely underestimated, further narrowing the apparent gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OpenAI Preparedness Framework's frontier-risk evaluation paradigm by introducing malicious fine-tuning (MFT) as a concrete methodology for estimating the worst-case adversarial ceiling of open-weight models, rather than relying solely on refusal-propensity measurements of the released checkpoint. It builds on prior demonstrations that safety refusals can be removed via SFT or RL (Yang et al., 2023; Halawi et al., 2024; Qi et al., 2024) and on evidence that web browsing substantially improves biology-risk evaluations (Fulford et al., 2025), but departs from those works by combining anti-refusal RL with domain-specific capability maximization in agentic tool-use environments (browsing for biology, terminal for cybersecurity CTFs). The TroubleshootingBench benchmark is novel, filling a gap between ProtocolQA's internet-sourced protocols and real-world tacit-knowledge troubleshooting that cannot be looked up online.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MFT methodology provides a reusable template for future open-weight releases to estimate net-new harm before publication, addressing a key gap where prior evaluations only measured the shipped model's refusal behavior rather than the adversarial ceiling. The paper's central finding—that even after targeted RL maximization, gpt-oss remains below o3 (itself below Preparedness High) and only marginally above existing open-weight SOTA—supports the decision to release but also highlights that the methodology's conclusions are sensitive to evaluation choice, scaffolding differences, and training-data coverage. Open questions include whether more sophisticated adversaries with hierarchical scaffolds, ensembling, or additional pre-training data could push capabilities meaningfully higher, and whether a series of incremental open-weight releases could collectively advance the frontier without any single step-change.
