## Problem

Recent diffusion transformers have discarded pooled text embeddings (e.g., CLIP) in favor of attention-only conditioning, but no rigorous analysis has justified this design choice. The paper investigates whether the pooled embedding is truly redundant and, if so, whether it can be repurposed to provide a performance benefit that attention alone cannot deliver.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt plus a pair of positive and negative guidance prompts (e.g., 'Ultra-detailed, photorealistic, cinematic' vs. 'Low-res, flat, cartoonish') that are fed through the CLIP encoder to compute a modulation-space shift vector. For image editing, the input additionally includes a reference image and an editing instruction, with the final composited prompt serving as the positive direction. The output is a generated image, video, or edited image with improved aesthetics, complexity, or specific attributes (hands, object count, color, position) relative to the base model, with no change in resolution or format.

## Method

The core technique, modulation guidance, is a training-free plug-and-play modification that amplifies the pooled CLIP embedding's influence by computing a guidance vector in the modulation space: ŷ(p, p⁺, p⁻, t) = y(p,t) + w·(y(p⁺,t) − y(p⁻,t)), where y is the global conditioning vector produced by an MLP over the timestep and CLIP embedding. This shifted vector modulates the scaling (α) and shifting (β) coefficients of every transformer block, steering the diffusion trajectory toward more desirable modes without altering attention. To avoid over-weighting the guidance prompt at high scales, the authors introduce dynamic strategies that vary w across transformer layers (e.g., a step function skipping the first i layers), which they show provides a better aesthetics–fidelity trade-off than a constant scale. For CLIP-free models (COSMOS, CausVid), they reintroduce the pooled embedding by fine-tuning a small MLP on top of it while keeping the rest of the network frozen, using a distillation objective that minimizes MSE between the original model's prediction and the modified model's prediction on the model's own synthetic data.

## Results

On text-to-image generation, modulation guidance yields consistent human-preference gains across five state-of-the-art models: e.g., FLUX schnell achieves 72% side-by-side win rate on aesthetics and 78% on complexity, while HiDream reaches 80% on complexity. Automatic metrics show ImageReward improvements of +0.5 to +0.4 across models (e.g., FLUX schnell 10.2→11.0, HiDream 11.7→12.1) and HPSv3 gains up to +0.5 (HiDream 13.2→13.7). For specific changes, GenEval object counting improves from 56→65 and color from 79→86 on FLUX schnell, with human win rates of +22% (counting) and +18% (hands correction). On text-to-video, CausVid 1.3B sees a large dynamic-degree jump from 75.25→86.59 on VBench, and total score from 62.72→65.43.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Normalized Attention Guidance (Chen et al., 2025), modulation guidance wins decisively on FLUX schnell: 67% vs. 33% SbS win rate on aesthetics (+34 pp) and 79% vs. 21% on complexity (+58 pp), while also incurring no extra attention-layer passes. On video (CausVid 1.3B, VBench), modulation guidance achieves a total score of 65.43 versus 63.58 for Normalized Attention Guidance, with a particularly large dynamic-degree gain (86.59 vs. 74.22). Against Concept Sliders (Gandikota et al., 2024) on hands correction, modulation guidance wins 58% vs. 42% SbS (+16 pp) without requiring test-time LoRA optimization. Against LLM-enhanced prompts (Lian et al., 2023), modulation guidance outperforms on both aesthetics (55% vs. 45%) and complexity (62% vs. 38%) for FLUX schnell, and can be stacked on top of LLM-enhanced prompts for further gains (61% vs. 39% aesthetics, 74% vs. 26% complexity). Overall, the method is training-free, adds negligible runtime overhead, and consistently outperforms all three baselines across image and video tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the modulation mechanism inherited from StyleGAN (Karras et al., 2019) within diffusion transformers, drawing on CLIP's interpretable embedding geometry (Garibi et al., 2025) to perform guidance in modulation space rather than in attention space as prior attention-guidance methods (Chen et al., 2025; Hong et al., 2023) do. It departs from the recent trend of discarding pooled text embeddings in favor of attention-only conditioning (Wan et al., 2025; Xie et al., 2024) by showing that the pooled embedding, while largely inactive as a conditioning signal, becomes useful when reinterpreted as a guidance direction. The dynamic layer-wise scheduling of the guidance scale borrows from dynamic CFG strategies (Sadat et al., 2023; Kynkänniemi et al., 2024) but applies it across transformer depth rather than across denoising timesteps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the pooled CLIP embedding is largely inactive for long prompts (and entirely inactive in some models like HiDream-Fast) challenges a common architectural assumption and suggests that recent models' decision to drop it was empirically justified for conditioning purposes. The practical impact is significant: modulation guidance is training-free, adds negligible overhead, and improves aesthetics, complexity, object counting, and hands correction across multiple state-of-the-art models without requiring CFG or test-time optimization. Key limitations include the inability to improve text-to-image semantic correspondence and the introduction of task-specific hyperparameters (positive/negative prompts, layer schedules, guidance strength) that require manual selection. An open question is whether the modulation space harbors further exploitable structure beyond the simple positive/negative prompt directions explored here.
