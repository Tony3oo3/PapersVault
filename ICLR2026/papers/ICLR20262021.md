## Problem

Existing SVD-based LLM compression methods (ASVD, SVD-LLM, AdaSVD) minimize per-layer reconstruction error independently, ignoring how truncation errors propagate and accumulate across layers. This causes the compressed model's outputs to drift progressively from the full-precision baseline as depth increases, with cosine similarity dropping from 0.97 to 0.79 across layers even when each layer achieves its theoretical minimum truncation error. The paper addresses this by formulating a compression objective that explicitly compensates for upstream cumulative error at each layer.

## Input / Output

The method consumes LLM weight matrices, a small calibration set (128 samples, sequence length 2048), and a uniform target rank per layer. Rather than storing raw activations, it collects only two compact second-order statistics per layer: the input covariance H_ℓ = X_ℓX_ℓᵀ and the differential covariance Δ_ℓ = (X_ℓᶠ − X_ℓ)X_ℓᵀ, which capture distributional characteristics and upstream-induced shifts. The output is a pair of low-rank factors (A_ℓ ∈ ℝ^{d_out×r}, B_ℓ ∈ ℝ^{r×d_in}) per layer such that W_ℓ ≈ A_ℓB_ℓ, assembled into a fully compressed model with no fine-tuning or mixed-rank allocation required.

## Method

SAES-SVD comprises two components. CEALC reformulates the per-layer compression objective as a weighted sum of local reconstruction error and alignment to the full-precision reference output, then shows this reduces to a standard truncated SVD on a whitened target matrix G_ℓ = W_ℓ(H_ℓ + β_ℓΔ_ℓ)H_ℓ^{−1/2}, yielding a closed-form solution A_ℓ = Û_r Σ_r^{1/2}, B_ℓ = Σ_r^{1/2} V̂_rᵀ H_ℓ^{−1/2}. ACES adaptively selects the alignment coefficient β_ℓ per layer to maximize the Retained Energy Ratio (proportion of spectral energy in the top-r singular values), using a fixed-subspace first-order approximation that freezes the principal subspace at β=0 and reduces the optimization to solving a univariate quadratic equation in closed form—requiring only one extra truncated SVD per layer beyond CEALC. The two components form a closed loop: each layer suppresses its own truncation error while residual error is accumulated into second-order statistics and propagated forward for downstream compensation.

## Results

On LLaMA-7B at a 0.2 compression ratio, SAES-SVD achieves an average zero-shot accuracy of 0.50 (only 3.9% drop from the 0.52 baseline), compared to 0.47 for Dip-SVD (9.2% drop) and 0.44 for SVD-LLM (14.7% drop). On WikiText-2 perplexity, SAES-SVD reaches 7.17 at 0.2 ratio versus 7.95 (Dip-SVD) and 11.14 (ASVD), and at the aggressive 0.6 ratio it achieves 22.01 versus 46.18 (Dobi-SVD) and 53.74 (SVD-LLM). The paper reports a 58% reduction in zero-shot accuracy drop and a 52% reduction in perplexity gap relative to Dip-SVD at 20% compression, without requiring fine-tuning or mixed-rank allocation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAES-SVD outperforms all SVD-based baselines across every compression ratio tested. Against the strongest SVD baseline Dip-SVD (which uses mixed-rank but no fine-tuning), SAES-SVD reduces the accuracy drop from 9.2% to 3.9% at 0.2 ratio and from 22.8% to 21.1% at 0.4 ratio on LLaMA-7B, while also achieving lower perplexity (7.17 vs. 7.95 at 0.2). SVD-LLM and Dobi-SVD, both requiring fine-tuning, are consistently outperformed: at 0.6 ratio SAES-SVD achieves 22.01 WikiText-2 perplexity versus 53.74 (SVD-LLM) and 46.18 (Dobi-SVD). FW-SVD collapses catastrophically at all ratios (perplexity in the thousands to millions), confirming that importance-weighted SVD without activation awareness is ineffective for LLMs. Against structured pruning methods (LLM-Pruner, SliceGPT, BlockPruner), SAES-SVD achieves substantially lower perplexity at equivalent memory budgets (e.g., 8.96 vs. 16.39–19.78 at 8GB). In the GPTQ combination experiment, SAES+GPTQ at equivalent 3-bit size reduces perplexity from 10.17 to 7.64 on LLaMA2-7B and from 12.29 to 9.87 on Qwen2.5-7B, demonstrating complementary gains with quantization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation-aware SVD compression line initiated by ASVD (Yuan et al., 2023) and SVD-LLM (Wang et al., 2024), which formulate per-layer compression as a whitened low-rank approximation problem. The key conceptual departure is shifting from independent per-layer optimization to a globally coordinated objective that explicitly compensates for cumulative error propagated from upstream compressed layers. The ACES component further departs from prior fixed-weighting approaches (e.g., FW-SVD, Dip-SVD) by adaptively selecting the compensation strength per layer to maximize retained spectral energy, yielding a closed-form solution that requires only one additional SVD per layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAES-SVD addresses a practically important gap: prior SVD methods that achieve per-layer optimal truncation still suffer significant end-to-end degradation due to error compounding, and the proposed fix requires no fine-tuning or mixed-rank heuristics, reducing total compression time by ~88% compared to SVD-LLM. The method's compatibility with quantization (SAES+GPTQ) and structured pruning baselines suggests it can serve as a composable pre-processing step in broader compression pipelines. Open questions include whether the second-order statistics formulation remains sufficient for very deep or MoE architectures, how the adaptive coefficient behaves under extreme compression ratios beyond 0.8, and whether the approach transfers to non-SVD low-rank methods (e.g., randomized SVD, NMF).
