## Problem

Existing unified VLA models either rely on external experts for modality unification or treat image generation and action prediction as separate decoding processes, preventing direct synergy between visual foresight and action prediction. The paper argues that genuine unification of understanding, generation, and acting requires these processes to be intrinsically coupled so that actions are formulated as implicit mappings to desired future observations, rather than being predicted in a single pass or in isolation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264721/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a text instruction and a current RGB observation (discretized into a fixed-length sequence of VQ visual tokens) along with robot proprioception, and produces a fixed-length sequence of future image tokens and a variable-length sequence of FAST action tokens. All modalities are mapped into a single shared vocabulary of size V_v + V_a + 1 (including a special <MASK> token), and the full sequence is formatted as [text; current image; future image; action] with boundary markers <BOI>/<EOI> and <BOA>/<EOA>. Because action length varies per task, the model uses a shift-operation strategy to predict the next token rather than directly reconstructing masked positions, preserving compatibility with the pretrained autoregressive backbone.

## Method

UD-VLA introduces the Joint Discrete Denoising Diffusion Process (JD3P), which places future image tokens and action tokens on a single synchronous denoising trajectory: at each of T iterations, a cosine-scheduled mask ratio is applied, and the model jointly predicts all masked visual and action tokens in parallel. A hybrid attention mechanism enforces bidirectional attention within the image block and within the action block (breaking time-serial dependence to avoid shortcut learning) while maintaining strict causal attention across blocks, so that action tokens attend to future image tokens but never vice versa, preserving the inverse-kinematics structure. Training follows a two-stage pipeline: stage (i) post-trains the Emu3 VLM backbone on large-scale video data to inject future-image generation capability, and stage (ii) fine-tunes on robot action data with the JD3P masked-prediction cross-entropy loss (weighted to down-weight visual tokens). At inference, efficiency is achieved through prefix KV-caching, pre-filling of boundary tokens, restricting the classification space to each modality's token range, and confidence-guided decoding that progressively un-masks the highest-confidence positions via tempered Gumbel-max sampling, yielding 4.3× faster decoding than autoregressive baselines while improving CALVIN average length from 4.18 to 4.64.

## Results

UD-VLA achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three simulation benchmarks: an average success length of 4.64 on CALVIN ABCD→D (vs. 4.52 for MDT, the closest competitor), 96.1% average success rate on LIBERO (vs. 95.7% for F1), and 76.0% overall success on SimplerEnv-WidowX (vs. 69.8% for UniVLA). Inference speed reaches 219.3 tokens/s, a 4.3× speedup over autoregressive decoding (50.2 tokens/s) while simultaneously improving performance by +0.46 in average length. Real-world experiments on a UR5e arm confirm >80% success rates on seen and unseen tasks, outperforming GR00T N1 and UniVLA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UD-VLA outperforms all 20 baselines across every benchmark. On CALVIN, it leads MDT (4.52) by 0.12 and UP-VLA (4.42) by 0.22 in average success length, with the largest margins over early methods like MCIL (0.40) and RT-1 (2.45). On LIBERO, the closest competitors are F1 (95.7%), UniVLA (95.5%), and OpenVLA-OFT (95.3%), all within 0.8% of UD-VLA's 96.1%, while methods relying on extrinsic experts (DreamVLA 92.6%, FlowVLA 88.1%) or separate decoding (CoT-VLA 81.1%, WorldVLA 81.8%) trail by 3.5–15%. On SimplerEnv, UD-VLA's 76.0% substantially exceeds UniVLA (69.8%) and F1 (59.4%), and dramatically surpasses methods without explicit visual generation (Octo 29.5%, SpatialVLA 42.7%). In real-world evaluation, UD-VLA achieves >80% success on both seen and unseen tasks, while GR00T N1 shows poor generalization to unseen objects and UniVLA, despite visual post-training, lacks explicit visual reasoning at inference and falls short on unseen scenes. The ablation in Table 7 further confirms that the joint denoising (JD3P) mechanism itself contributes +0.46 over autoregressive decoding and +0.29 over independent diffusion, validating that synchronous cross-modal refinement is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

UD-VLA builds on the Emu3 pretrained VLM backbone (Wang et al., 2024) and the shift-operation training strategy from Gong et al. (2025) for adapting autoregressive models to diffusion objectives. It departs from two dominant paradigms in unified VLAs: (1) extrinsic-expert approaches (GR-1, SEER, DreamVLA, F1) that couple separate image and action decoders, and (2) unified-token-space approaches (CoT-VLA, WorldVLA, UniVLA) that share a vocabulary but still decode images and actions in separate processes. The key conceptual departure is the Joint Discrete Denoising Diffusion Process (JD3P), which places image and action tokens on a single synchronous denoising trajectory with hybrid attention, so that actions are iteratively refined under multi-step visual guidance rather than a single forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that joint discrete denoising can simultaneously improve quality over autoregressive decoding and independent diffusion while achieving 4× faster inference, offering a practical path to unified VLAs that are both accurate and deployable. A notable limitation is the low visual fidelity of generated future frames (due to compressed VQ tokens and absence of large-scale generative pretraining), which constrains how much the visual channel can contribute to downstream control. Open questions include whether the discrete tokenization bottleneck can be allevated with higher-fidelity visual codebooks, whether JD3P scales to longer action horizons or multi-robot settings, and whether the two-stage training (world-model pretraining then joint fine-tuning) is necessary or whether end-to-end training from scratch could match it.
