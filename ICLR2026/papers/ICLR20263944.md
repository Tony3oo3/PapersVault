## Problem

Leading uncertainty estimation methods for LLMs (predictive entropy, semantic entropy) require sampling multiple output sequences, making them computationally expensive and impractical at scale. The maximum sequence probability (MSP) was previously introduced as an ad hoc baseline without theoretical justification or guidance on how to best approximate it. This paper provides the first principled derivation of MSP from proper scoring rules and identifies why sampling-based approximations are suboptimal.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt x and a language model with parameters w, performs a single greedy decoding pass to obtain one output sequence, and computes the negative log-likelihood of that sequence as a scalar uncertainty estimate. It requires only the per-token probabilities along the greedy path (not the full vocabulary distribution), is deterministic, and is hyperparameter-free. The output is a single scalar that can be thresholded or ranked to flag unreliable generations.

## Method

The paper derives the MSP as the aleatoric uncertainty measure by substituting the zero-one score (rather than the standard logarithmic score) into the proper scoring rule framework, yielding the negative log-likelihood of the most likely output sequence. Since exact sequence maximization over Y_T is intractable (O(|V|^T)), G-NLL approximates it by moving the max operator inside the token-wise summation, which is exactly standard greedy decoding. A sample-complexity theorem (Theorem 1) shows that approximating M(p(y)) depends only on the concentration of probability mass near the maximum, whereas approximating H(p(y)) depends on the full range of log-likelihoods squared by the worst-case importance weight, making the former far more amenable to practical decoding strategies. Empirically, G-NLL outperforms PE, LN-PE, SE, LN-SE, and D-SE in 13 of 18 scenarios across Llama-3.1 and Falcon Mamba models (7B–70B, pre-trained and instruction-tuned) on TriviaQA, SVAMP, and NQ-Open, while requiring only a single forward pass.

## Results

G-NLL achieves an average AUROC of .721±.003 across 18 evaluation scenarios (6 LLMs × 3 datasets × 2 generation lengths × 2 correctness metrics), outperforming the best logarithmic-score baseline D-SE (.707±.003) by 1.4 percentage points. G-NLL wins in 13 out of 18 individual scenarios and remains competitive in the remaining 5, with the largest gains on short-phrase generation (e.g., .843 vs .828 for D-SE on Falcon Mamba 7B PT short/F1). The method is deterministic and hyperparameter-free, requiring only a single greedily decoded sequence, whereas all baselines require 10 sampled sequences plus temperature tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

G-NLL outperforms all three logarithmic-score baselines on average: PE (Malinin & Gales, 2021) at .677, SE (Kuhn et al., 2023) at .690, and D-SE (Farquhar et al., 2024) at .707, versus G-NLL's .721 AUROC. The gap is most pronounced on short-phrase generation, where G-NLL leads D-SE by 1.5–3.5 points (e.g., .843 vs .828 on Falcon Mamba 7B PT/F1, .838 vs .829 on Falcon Mamba 7B IT/F1). On long-form generation with LLM-as-judge, G-NLL wins decisively on Llama-3.1 models (.649 vs .600 for 8B PT, .649 vs .571 for 70B PT) but trails D-SE on Falcon Mamba 7B (.612 vs .624 PT, .615 vs .629 IT). G-NLL is competitive but not strictly superior in 5 of 18 scenarios, all involving long-form generation on Falcon Mamba or Llama-3.1 70B IT. The ablation (Fig. 2) further confirms that greedy decoding is the optimal single-sequence approximation: beam search yields only marginal gains while multinomial sampling degrades performance substantially.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the proper scoring rules framework—recently applied to uncertainty estimation in classification by Kotelevskii et al. (2025) and Hofman et al. (2024)—to natural language generation, specifically exploring the zero-one score as an alternative to the logarithmic score. The resulting aleatoric uncertainty measure, the maximum sequence probability (MSP), was previously introduced as an ad hoc baseline by Fadeeva et al. (2023) without theoretical justification or guidance on approximation. The paper's key departure is providing the first principled derivation of MSP from the zero-one score and showing that greedy decoding (G-NLL) is the natural, maximally efficient approximation, in contrast to the multi-sequence Monte Carlo sampling that underpins predictive entropy and semantic entropy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that a single greedily decoded sequence suffices for competitive uncertainty estimation, G-NLL challenges the field's default assumption that multiple sampled sequences are necessary, offering a deterministic, hyperparameter-free method with dramatically lower computational cost. Open questions include whether the semantic extension (maximum cluster probability) admits an equally efficient single-sequence approximation, how G-NLL performs when token-level probabilities are unavailable (e.g., black-box APIs), and whether the advantage persists in longer-form generation or non-question-answering tasks. The clean sample-complexity analysis (Theorem 1) provides a theoretical foundation that could guide future single-sequence uncertainty measures beyond the specific G-NLL formulation.
