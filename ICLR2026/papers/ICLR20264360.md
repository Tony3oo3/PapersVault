## Problem

Autoregressive LLMs are inherently sequential in decoding, limiting parallelism, while existing diffusion LLMs suffer from KV-cache incompatibility, high inference latency, and often require hundreds of billions of tokens for fine-tuning. Block diffusion models interpolate between these paradigms but have only been validated on small-scale models, leaving their practical applicability to state-of-the-art LLMs unclear. Fast-dLLM v2 addresses this gap by showing that pretrained AR models can be adapted into efficient block diffusion decoders with only ~1B tokens of fine-tuning, a 500× reduction compared to full-attention diffusion LLMs like Dream.

## Input / Output

The model accepts text prompts or instruction sequences, which are tokenized and right-padded with [MASK] tokens to align to a fixed block size of 32 before being packed into training sequences of context length 2048. At inference, generation proceeds block-by-block (32 tokens per block), with each block refined in parallel via sub-blocks of size 8, producing a variable-length text output. Unlike fixed-length diffusion models, Fast-dLLM v2 supports arbitrary generation lengths by simply continuing to decode additional blocks until a stopping condition is met.

## Method

Fast-dLLM v2 adapts pretrained Qwen2.5-Instruct models (1.5B and 7B) into block diffusion LLMs via a training recipe that combines three key mechanisms: (1) a block-wise attention mask that enables bidirectional attention within each 32-token block while preserving causal dependencies across blocks, (2) complementary masking where each sample is duplicated with mask m and its complement 1−m so every token receives both masked and unmasked supervision, and (3) a token-shift strategy that predicts masked tokens using the logit from the preceding position, maintaining AR-like temporal representations. At inference, a hierarchical caching scheme accelerates decoding: a block-level KV cache stores fully decoded blocks as frozen prefix context, while a DualCache sub-block mechanism reuses partial KV states during iterative confidence-aware parallel refinement within the current block. This design yields up to 2.5× speedup over standard AR decoding while matching or exceeding AR baseline accuracy across code, math, and knowledge benchmarks.

## Results

Fast-dLLM v2 (7B) achieves an average benchmark score of 60.3, surpassing Qwen2.5-7B-Nemo-FT (59.6) and Dream (57.6), while the 1.5B variant reaches 45.0, outperforming both Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen2.5-1.5B-Nemo-FT (44.3 each). On individual benchmarks, the 7B model leads on HumanEval (63.4 Base / 58.5 Plus), GSM8K (83.7), and MBPP (63.0 Base), though it trails Qwen2.5-7B-Nemo-FT on MATH (61.6 vs. 72.0) and IFEval (61.4 vs. 69.5). In terms of efficiency, Fast-dLLM v2 achieves up to 2.5× throughput speedup over standard AR decoding (101.7 vs. 39.1 tokens/s on GSM8K with threshold 0.9), while requiring only ~1B tokens of fine-tuning compared to ~500B for Dream.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen2.5-7B-Nemo-FT (the strongest AR baseline), Fast-dLLM v2 (7B) wins on HumanEval (63.4 vs. 52.4), MBPP (63.0 vs. 57.1), and average score (60.3 vs. 59.6), but loses on MATH (61.6 vs. 72.0), IFEval (61.4 vs. 69.5), MMLU (66.6 vs. 68.6), and GPQA (31.9 vs. 34.2). Compared to Dream (7B), Fast-dLLM v2 dominates on HumanEval (63.4 vs. 57.9), MATH (61.6 vs. 39.2), and average (60.3 vs. 57.6), while Dream retains an edge on MBPP (68.3 vs. 63.0) and IFEval (62.5 vs. 61.4). Against LLaDA (8B), Fast-dLLM v2 is substantially stronger across nearly all benchmarks (e.g., HumanEval 63.4 vs. 35.4, MATH 61.6 vs. 26.6, Avg 60.3 vs. 43.3). At the 1.5B scale, Fast-dLLM v2 outperforms both Qwen2.5-1.5B and its Nemo-FT variant on average (45.0 vs. 44.3), with notable gains on GSM8K (62.0 vs. 57.0/58.5) and IFEval (47.0 vs. 41.2/39.4), though it trails on MATH (38.1 vs. 46.8/43.5) and GPQA (27.7 vs. 30.6/31.0). In throughput, Fast-dLLM v2 (7B) achieves 2.54× higher tokens/s than Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct on A100 at batch size 1, and up to 1.8× higher throughput on H100 at batch size 64.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the block diffusion framework introduced by BD3-LM (Arriola et al., 2025) to large-scale LLMs, combining it with the DualCache and confidence-aware parallel decoding from Fast-dLLM (Wu et al., 2025) and the complementary masking strategy from LaViDa (Li et al., 2025c). The key departure from prior diffusion LLMs (Dream, LLaDA) is the use of a block-wise attention mask that remains structurally close to the original AR model, enabling lossless adaptation with only ~1B tokens of fine-tuning rather than the ~500B required by full-attention diffusion approaches. Unlike concurrent works like SDAR or D2F, Fast-dLLM v2 emphasizes a systematic training recipe (complementary masking + padding + token shift) specifically designed for data-efficient AR-to-diffusion conversion at the 7B scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in providing a concrete, low-cost recipe (~1B tokens, ~12 hours on 64 A100s) to convert existing pretrained AR LLMs into faster parallel decoders without retraining from scratch, making diffusion-based generation viable for latency-sensitive deployment. The 2.5× speedup while matching or exceeding AR baseline quality is a meaningful step toward practical dLLM adoption, though the speedup remains moderate compared to the theoretical parallelism gains of full diffusion. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 7B parameters, whether it generalizes to non-Qwen base architectures, and whether the block diffusion model can serve as an effective draft model for speculative decoding (as hinted by the DiffuSpec connection). A limitation is that the method still requires supervised fine-tuning on instruction data, so it is not truly training-free.
