## Problem

Existing bioacoustic encoders are typically limited to a narrow taxonomic group (usually birds), a single model architecture, and a single training paradigm, and are evaluated on a small set of species-classification tasks. No prior work has systematically investigated how model architecture, training data composition, and training paradigm (self-supervised vs. supervised vs. sequential) jointly affect the generalizability of learned bioacoustic representations across diverse downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263846/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Models consume raw audio waveforms resampled to 16 kHz (32 kHz for Perch, 48 kHz for BirdNet) and produce fixed-dimensional embeddings from the final layer (excluding classification heads), which are then used for linear probing, cosine-similarity retrieval, or K-means clustering. Evaluation spans 26 datasets across four task families—species classification, sound-event detection, individual identification, and vocal repertoire discovery—using three complementary metrics: probe accuracy/mAP, retrieval ROC AUC, and clustering NMI.

## Method

The paper conducts a controlled empirical study comparing CNN (EfficientNet-B0) and transformer (BEATs, EAT) backbones under self-supervised pre-training, supervised post-training, and sequential SSL-then-SL recipes, with and without AudioSet general audio mixed into the bioacoustic training corpus. The key finding is that self-supervised pre-training on a diverse mix of bioacoustic and general audio data, followed by supervised post-training on the same mix, yields the strongest in- and out-of-distribution performance, outperforming both pure supervised and pure self-supervised baselines. Data diversity at both pre-training and post-training stages is critical: adding AudioSet consistently improves transfer, while supervised training on general audio alone transfers poorly. The authors also introduce two new evaluation benchmarks (individual identification and vocal repertoire discovery) and augment existing BEANS and BirdSet benchmarks with retrieval and clustering metrics, scaling the evaluation from a handful of species-classification datasets to 26 datasets across four task families.

## Results

The best model, sl-BEATS-all (BEATs SSL pre-trained on AudioSet, then SL post-trained on bioacoustic + AudioSet), achieves 0.832 probe accuracy and 0.813 retrieval R-AUC on BEANS Classification, 0.408 probe mAP on BEANS Detection, and 0.294 probe mAP on BirdSet, setting new state-of-the-art on all three established benchmarks. On the newly introduced benchmarks, EffNetB0-all (SL on bioacoustic + AudioSet) leads Individual ID with 0.531 probe accuracy and Vocal Repertoire with 0.830 retrieval R-AUC, while sl-BEATS-all remains competitive at 0.511 and 0.798 respectively. Across 26 datasets and four task families, the SSL-then-SL recipe consistently outperforms both pure supervised (BirdNet, Perch) and pure self-supervised (BEATs pretrained, AVES, BirdMAE) baselines in aggregate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

sl-BEATs-all (SSL pre-training + SL post-training on bioacoustic + AudioSet) outperforms the strongest pure supervised baselines BirdNet (0.796 probe) and Perch (0.768 probe) on BEANS Classification (0.832 probe), and surpasses both on BirdSet detection (0.294 vs. 0.233 mAP for Perch; BirdNet excluded due to data leakage). Against the strongest pure SSL baseline BEATs-pretrained, sl-BEATs-all improves BEANS Classification probe accuracy from 0.774 to 0.832 and BirdSet probe mAP from 0.129 to 0.294, while retaining comparable out-of-distribution retrieval (BEANS Detection R-AUC 0.726 vs. 0.722). NatureBEATs (the BEATs encoder extracted from the NatureLM-audio audio-language model) is the closest competitor, achieving 0.804 probe on BEANS Classification and leading on Vocal Repertoire R-AUC (0.811), but trails sl-BEATs-all on BirdSet (0.223 vs. 0.294 mAP) and BEANS Detection (0.385 vs. 0.408 mAP). On the new Individual ID benchmark, the CNN-based EffNetB0-all (SL) edges out all transformer models with 0.531 probe accuracy, while BirdNet leads on retrieval R-AUC (0.708). The key qualitative finding is that pure SSL models suffer only a 0.01 R-AUC drop from BEANS Classification to Detection (focal→soundscape), versus 0.09 for supervised models, confirming that SSL pre-training provides superior out-of-distribution robustness, and that the sequential SSL→SL recipe combines the strengths of both paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work in bioacoustics: self-supervised audio encoders (AVES, BirdMAE, Animal2Vec, TweetyBert) and supervised bioacoustic encoders (BirdNet, Perch, SurfPerch), which have been studied in isolation. It builds directly on BEATs and EAT as transformer-based SSL backbones and on EfficientNet as a CNN baseline, but departs from prior work by systematically combining SSL pre-training with supervised post-training on mixed bioacoustic + general-audio data — a two-stage recipe not previously explored in this domain. The evaluation framework extends BEANS and BirdSet with retrieval and clustering metrics and adds entirely new task families (individual identification, vocal repertoire discovery) that prior benchmarks had not addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive, actionable recipe for building generalizable bioacoustic encoders, with the key finding that SSL + supervised post-training on diverse data mixes dominates both in- and out-of-distribution performance — a practical guideline for the field where supervised learning has been assumed sufficient. The new benchmarks for individual ID and vocal repertoire discovery fill a critical gap, as these tasks are central to animal communication research but lacked standardized evaluation. Limitations include the 16 kHz constraint (excluding ultrasonic species), the absence of frame- or event-based temporal detection, and the acknowledgment that OOD analysis would benefit from controlled datasets that do not yet exist. Open questions include whether stronger SSL backbones yield proportionally better post-trained models, and how the recipe scales as labeled bioacoustic data continues to grow.
