## Problem

Reasoning LLMs (RLLMs) like DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI o1 generate long chain-of-thought reasoning chains that fundamentally alter inference serving dynamics, yet their serving behavior remains unexplored. Current LLM serving engines (vLLM, SGLang, TensorRT-LLM) are designed for traditional LLMs with short outputs, and it is unclear whether existing optimization techniques transfer to RLLMs or whether RLLMs exhibit distinct serving characteristics that require new system designs.

## Input / Output

The evaluation pipeline feeds text prompts from four benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH-500, AIME-2024 for math reasoning at increasing difficulty; GPQA Diamond for knowledge reasoning) into RLLM/LLM models of 1.5B–70B scale served via vLLM or SGLang with configurable batch sizes (8–64) and token budgets (0.5K–20K). Outputs include a trinity of metrics under the ASU framework: response accuracy per dataset, service-provider throughput (TPS), and user-side latency (TTFVT — time to first *visible* token, accounting for hidden reasoning tokens — plus E2E latency), alongside KV cache utilization traces and per-request running-time distributions. Real-world validation uses a Gamma-distributed bursty workload generator (BurstGPT-Perf style) to simulate stochastic request arrivals.

## Method

The paper introduces the ASU (Accuracy, Service-end, User-end) assessment framework and the ASU-Perf benchmarking suite, then conducts three tiers of empirical investigation. First, a pilot study compares paired LLM/RLLM models (e.g., Qwen-2.5-Math-7B vs. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B) across scales, token budgets, and batch sizes, revealing four distinct RLLM serving behaviors: significant KV cache fluctuations (3%–70% vs. <3% for LLMs), straggler requests with long-tail running-time distributions, difficulty-adaptive running time, and domain preference (math over knowledge). Second, the paper evaluates five optimization techniques — model weight quantization (GPTQ, AWQ, FP8, L4), KV cache quantization (FP8-E5M2/E4M3), prefix caching, n-gram speculative decoding, and prefill-decode disaggregation (1P1D) — finding that MWQ and speculative decoding improve efficiency with minor accuracy loss, KV cache quantization helps larger models but degrades 7B RLLMs, prefix caching can hurt small models in single-turn settings, and PD-disaggregation actually worsens RLLM serving due to decode-bound bottlenecks. Third, all findings are validated under Gamma-distributed real-world workloads, confirming that RLLM KV cache utilization can remain near 100% for extended periods, forcing new requests into waiting queues.

## Results

On GSM8K, RLLM-7B (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B) achieves 80.67% accuracy vs. LLM-7B (Qwen-2.5-Math-7B) at 69.67%, while on MATH-500 the gap widens dramatically to 61.33% vs. 3.30%. However, RLLM generates 2–6× more output tokens (e.g., 502,833 vs. 178,459 on MATH-500) and exhibits far higher TTFT (16.55 s vs. 0.07 s), reflecting its long chain-of-thought reasoning. Under real-world Gamma-distributed workloads, RLLM KV-cache utilization can remain near 100% for extended periods, forcing new requests into a waiting queue, whereas LLM KV-cache usage stays below 3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLLM-7B outperforms LLM-7B on all four datasets in accuracy, with the largest gain on MATH-500 (+58 pp) and GSM8K (+11 pp), but at the cost of 2–6× more output tokens and 10–250× higher TTFT due to invisible reasoning tokens. Among model-weight quantization methods on 7B RLLM, GPTQ-Int4 and FP8 preserve accuracy within ~3% on most datasets but suffer a 15–25% drop on AIME-2024, while AWQ and L4 maintain accuracy but nearly double E2E latency and halve throughput. KV-cache quantization (FP8-E5M2/E4M3) improves TPS and TTFVT on 14B/32B RLLMs with minimal accuracy loss, yet causes near-complete accuracy collapse on the 7B RLLM. Prefix caching accelerates 14B+ RLLMs without accuracy degradation but can increase latency on sub-8B models due to hash-computation overhead. Speculative decoding (n-gram) reduces E2E running time across all scales without accuracy loss but degrades TPS and TTFVT. PD-disaggregation (1P1D) actually worsens all serving metrics for RLLMs because the decode phase is the bottleneck while prefill workers sit idle, and inter-GPU KV-cache transfer adds communication overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established LLM inference serving literature (vLLM, SGLang, DistServe, Orca) to the emerging class of reasoning LLMs (DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3, OpenAI o1), which generate long chain-of-thought sequences that fundamentally alter serving dynamics. Rather than proposing a new serving architecture, it repurposes existing optimization techniques—model weight quantization (GPTQ, AWQ, FP8), KV cache quantization, prefix caching, speculative decoding, and prefill-decode disaggregation—and systematically tests whether they transfer to RLLMs. The ASU-Perf benchmark and ASU assessment framework (accuracy + service-end + user-end metrics) are introduced as new evaluation tools, but the conceptual contribution is primarily the empirical characterization of RLLM serving as a distinct regime from traditional LLM serving.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As RLLMs rapidly become the dominant paradigm for complex reasoning (math, coding, planning), understanding their serving behavior is critical for practical deployment, especially for small-to-medium models on limited GPU resources. The finding that established techniques like PD-disaggregation and prefix caching can actually degrade RLLM performance challenges the assumption that LLM serving optimizations transfer seamlessly, and points toward the need for RLLM-specific system designs (difficulty-aware scheduling, adaptive KV cache management, asymmetric prefill-decode allocation). Key limitations include evaluation on only two inference engines (vLLM, SGLang), a single hardware class (NVIDIA GPUs), and the 1P1D disaggregation setup; broader validation across more engines, hardware, and RLLM architectures remains open.
