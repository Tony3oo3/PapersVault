## Problem

Existing unified multimodal masked diffusion models (MMaDa, Muddit) support only image-level understanding and low-resolution (512px) generation, cannot perform image editing or object grounding, and lag significantly behind AR and AR+diffusion unified models on benchmarks. Three core challenges remain: the high cost of training large masked image generative models from scratch, the scarcity of open-source MIGM resources and training techniques, and the absence of explicit mechanisms to leverage a model's understanding capabilities to improve its generation quality.

## Input / Output

The model accepts three input configurations: a text prompt alone (text-to-image), an input image plus text prompt (editing, where the image is encoded by both a SigLIP semantic encoder and a VQ encoder with 4× token compression), and an input image plus a referring expression (grounding). Outputs include 1024×1024 VQ-token images, edited images, bounding boxes represented as exactly 4 quantized tokens each (coordinates normalized to [0,1] and discretized into 1025 bins), and free-form text answers. For interleaved generation (planning/reflection), the model produces a sequence of text and image tokens in a single forward pass, with a special [exp] token triggering expansion into a block of image VQ tokens.

## Method

LaViDa-O introduces the Elastic Mixture-of-Transformers (Elastic-MoT) architecture, which couples a lightweight 2.4B generation branch (hidden size 2048, initialized by truncating the 8B understanding branch weights) with the 8B LaViDa understanding branch, and decouples joint attention so that cross-modality interaction occurs only in the first M=16 of N=32 layers, reducing active parameters to 6.4B for text-to-image generation. A modality-aware masking scheme routes tokens dynamically: all masked tokens default to the understanding branch, and a generated [exp] token is expanded into a sequence of generation-branch mask tokens, enabling interleaved text-image output without user-specified token layouts. For sampling, a stratified random unmasking order (recursively subdividing a 2×2 grid) replaces confidence-based decoding to ensure spatially dispersed token revelation, improving FID from 11.42 to 6.68. Universal text conditioning appends micro-conditions (resolution, crop coordinates, aesthetic/HPS scores, luminance, contrast) as plain text strings to the prompt, exploiting the model's language understanding rather than specialized embedding layers. Finally, planning (generating bounding-box layouts before the image) and iterative self-reflection (self-critiquing the generated image and regenerating if misaligned) explicitly exploit the understanding branch to boost generation quality, raising GenEval from 0.77 to 0.89.

## Results

On text-to-image generation, LaViDa-O (4B+2.4B masked) achieves 0.77 GenEval, 81.8 DPG-Bench, and 6.68 FID-30k, with planning and reflection boosting GenEval to 0.89 and DPG to 83.2. On image editing (ImgEdit), LaViDa-O scores 3.71 overall (3.80 with planning), outperforming GPT-4o (4.20) on Replace (4.39 vs 4.35) and Remove (3.98 vs 3.66). On RefCOCO grounding, LaViDa-O achieves 92.3/94.8/89.0 Precision@0.5 (4-step), surpassing Qwen2.5-VL-7B (90.0/92.5/85.4) and InternVL3-8B (92.5/94.6/88.0) while delivering a 6.8× inference speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaViDa-O substantially outperforms the closest masked unified baselines: MMaDa (8B) scores 0.63 GenEval and 32.85 FID-30k versus LaViDa-O's 0.77 and 6.68, while Muddit (1B) reaches only 0.61 GenEval. Against continuous diffusion specialists, LaViDa-O beats Flux-dev (12B) on GenEval (0.77 vs 0.68) and FID (6.68 vs 10.15) despite using far fewer parameters, though Flux-dev retains a slight edge on DPG (84.0 vs 81.8). On image editing, LaViDa-O (3.71 overall) trails GPT-4o (4.20) in aggregate but wins on the localized Replace and Remove categories, and outperforms FluxKontext-dev (3.52) and BAGEL (3.20) overall. On object grounding, LaViDa-O (92.3/94.8/89.0 on RefCOCO val/testA/testB) surpasses Qwen2.5-VL-7B (90.0/92.5/85.4) and GroundingDINO-L (90.6/93.2/88.2) while achieving a 6.8× inference speedup over Qwen2.5-VL-7B. On image understanding, LaViDa-O (45.1 MMMU, 76.4 MMB) outperforms MMaDa (30.2 MMMU, 68.5 MMB) and LaViDa (43.6 MMMU, 70.5 MMB) but remains below the strongest AR understanding-only models like InternVL3-8B (65.6 MMMU, 83.4 MMB).

## Relation to Prior Work

LaViDa-O extends the masked diffusion model (MDM) lineage from LaViDa (understanding-only) and the MDLM formulation (Sahoo et al., 2024) into a unified multimodal framework, while departing from the standard Mixture-of-Transformers architecture (Liang et al., 2024) through the Elastic-MoT design that uses a smaller generation branch and decoupled attention in later layers. It positions itself against both unified AR models (Janus, BAGEL) and AR+diffusion hybrids, arguing that a single MDM objective avoids the loss-balancing complexity of dual-objective training. The planning-and-reflection mechanism draws on inference-time scaling ideas from Reflect-DiT and ReflectionFlow but uniquely integrates them within a single unified model rather than requiring an external VLM as a reward model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LaViDa-O demonstrates that masked diffusion models—previously considered inferior to AR and continuous diffusion for unified multimodal tasks—can simultaneously achieve SOTA on text-to-image generation, image editing, and object grounding while offering 6.8× inference speedup, challenging the assumption that MDMs are inherently limited in this setting. The Elastic-MoT architecture and the finding that a 2.4B generation branch matches or exceeds 4B/8B branches after equal training budgets provide practical guidance for efficient unified model design. Open questions include whether the understanding-generation synergy (grounding data improving generation quality) scales to larger models, whether VQ tokenization can match continuous latent representations for photorealistic generation, and how the planning/reflection loop behaves under longer context or more complex multi-step editing.
