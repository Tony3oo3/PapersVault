## Problem

While one-pass data scaling laws for LLMs are well-characterized, the theoretical behavior of multi-epoch training—where the same dataset is reused K times—remains largely unexplored. Prior empirical work (Muennighoff et al., 2023) proposed a data-constrained scaling law but assumed the effective reuse rate depends only on K, not on dataset size N. This paper asks: to what extent can K-epoch training on N samples be equivalently viewed as one-pass training on a larger dataset, and how does this equivalence depend on both K and N?

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a linear regression problem with N i.i.d. samples, K epochs of SGD with random shuffling, and a data distribution characterized by its Hessian matrix H. The key output is the effective reuse rate E(K,N), defined as the multiplicative factor by which the dataset must grow under one-pass training to match the optimal expected excess risk of K-epoch training. The paper characterizes E(K,N) in two regimes: a linear regime where E(K,N) ≈ K (each epoch is as valuable as fresh data) and a saturation regime where E(K,N) plateaus at a problem-dependent value (Θ(log N) for strongly convex data, N^{b/(a-b)} for Zipf-distributed data).

## Method

The core technique is a bias-variance decomposition of multi-epoch SGD dynamics, where the non-commutative matrix products arising from random shuffling and repeated data passes are handled via matrix concentration inequalities (Huang et al., 2022). A 'pseudo-expectation' operator approximates the true expectation by treating correlated factors as independent, with the gap bounded by concentration arguments. From this, the paper derives an approximately optimal learning rate η′ = log(ρKN)/(2λ_d·KN) and shows that the optimal excess risk follows a log(KN)/(KN) decay for K = o(log N) but plateaus at 1/N for K = ω(log N). The effective reuse rate then follows directly: E(K,N) ≈ K in the first regime and saturates at Θ(log N) in the second, revealing that larger datasets can sustain more epochs before marginal benefit vanishes. The framework is extended to Zipf-distributed data with power-law and log-power-law spectra, where the saturation point scales as a power of N instead of logarithmically.

## Results

The paper establishes a two-regime scaling law for the effective reuse rate E(K,N) in multi-epoch SGD on linear regression. In the strongly convex case (Theorem 4.2), E(K,N) ≈ K when K = o(log N), but saturates at Θ(log N) when K = ω(log N). For Zipf-distributed data with a power-law spectrum (Theorem 5.2), the saturation point instead scales as Θ(N^{b/(a-b)}), and for a logarithmic power-law spectrum (Theorem 5.3) it scales as Θ(log^b N). LLM pretraining experiments on a 0.3B model over DCLM (up to 200B tokens) confirm the logarithmic scaling: the saturation epoch K(λ,N) fits 0.80 log N + 5.21 with correlation r = 0.97 under a constant learning rate, and 2.35 log N + 5.25 (r = 0.96) under a WSD schedule.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the data-constrained scaling law of Muennighoff et al. (2023), which models the effective dataset size as N′(K,N) = (1 + R*(1 − e^{−(K−1)/R*}))·N with a fixed R* ≈ 15.39, implying E(K,N) is independent of N. This paper shows both theoretically and empirically that E(K,N) depends on N: the saturation epoch grows with dataset size, contradicting the N-independent assumption. Against Lin et al. (2025), who proved E(K,N) = Θ(K) for small K in linear regression, this paper provides a sharper o(1)-relative-error characterization of the loss and, crucially, identifies the saturation regime and its N-dependence (log N, N^{b/(a-b)}, or log^b N depending on the spectrum). The LLM experiments further validate the theory: for K ≤ 5, E(K,N) ≈ K (consistent with both prior works), but for larger K the saturation point shifts to higher epochs as N increases from 0.2B to 2B tokens, a phenomenon absent from the Muennighoff et al. model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-pass Chinchilla scaling law framework to the multi-epoch regime by introducing the effective reuse rate E(K,N) as a precise theoretical quantity. It builds directly on the bias-variance decomposition and matrix concentration tools from Ge et al. (2019) and Huang et al. (2022) to derive closed-form risk approximations for multi-epoch SGD in linear regression. Most critically, it departs from Muennighoff et al. (2023), whose empirical data-constrained scaling law assumed E(K,N) is independent of dataset size N, and from Lin et al. (2025), which established only E(K,N)=Θ(K) for small K without explicit loss characterization. The paper's key conceptual departure is showing that the saturation point of E(K,N) grows with N (as Θ(log N) under strong convexity), a factor overlooked in prior empirical formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that larger datasets can sustain more epochs before marginal benefit vanishes directly challenges the uniform-epoch assumption in widely-cited data-constrained scaling laws and provides practitioners with a principled criterion for when multi-epoch training becomes wasteful. However, the theoretical analysis is confined to linear regression with constant learning rates, and the LLM validation uses a 0.3B model on 0.2B–2B tokens, leaving open whether the log N scaling persists at the multi-trillion-token scale of frontier models. Key open questions include extending the framework to non-linear models with feature learning, characterizing E(K,N) under learning rate decay schedules, and exploring selective or rephrased data reuse strategies that might push the effective reuse rate beyond the identified saturation.
