## Problem

Chunk-based sparse attention models have demonstrated remarkable training-free length extrapolation (e.g., 4K training to 1000×+ inference), yet the specific architectural components responsible for this capability remain poorly understood. This paper provides a systematic dissection to identify which design choices—encoder design, residual path topology, and sparsity configuration—are critical for extreme generalization, moving beyond the empirical observation that these models work toward a principled explanation of why.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a text token sequence and produces autoregressive next-token predictions, with a sliding window of 512 tokens for local context and a hierarchical sparse attention module that retrieves from a global chunked memory (chunk size 64, top-K=8 chunks selected per token). Trained at 4K context length, the architecture generalizes training-free to 32M tokens on RULER and 8M on BabiLong, with the retrieval mechanism operating per-token (refreshing chunk selection at every decoding step) rather than per-chunk.

## Method

The SWA+HSA architecture splits the decoder at its midpoint: lower layers use standard sliding window attention, a chunking layer encodes hidden states into global memory via a bidirectional Transformer Chunk Encoder with a learnable CLS token (whose output serves as the landmark for retrieval), and upper layers combine local self-attention with HSA that selects top-K chunks via dot-product similarity on landmarks and applies stick-breaking weights to compute a weighted sum of per-chunk attention outputs. Three design principles are identified as critical: (1) the non-linear Chunk Encoder with CLS token disentangles retrieval representations from next-token prediction states, providing a learnable non-linear approximator for full attention scores; (2) a Bypassing Residual Path routes HSA output directly to the final residual rather than through the intermediate MLP, preventing retrieved global information from being overridden by the local processing stream; (3) enforced selection sparsity (small Top-K) during pre-training bridges the train-test distribution gap by compelling the model to learn discriminative retrieval policies that transfer to extreme inference lengths.

## Results

The best SWA+HSA model (2-layer Chunk Encoder, CLS token, Bypassing Residual Path), trained on a 4K context, achieves 87.92% average on RULER at its training length and maintains 79.77% average at an extreme 32M-token context (8000× extrapolation). On BabiLong, the same model sustains high accuracy up to 8M tokens while all baselines collapse. At 1M tokens, the model still scores 85.81% average on RULER, and at 8M tokens it retains 82.18%, demonstrating remarkably graceful degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The best SWA+HSA model (87.92% at 4K) outperforms all baselines at every evaluated context length. Against the strongest baseline, Landmark Attention (79.13% at 4K, 75.47% at 128K), SWA+HSA leads by 8.8 points at training length and by 10.2 points at 128K, while Landmark cannot be evaluated beyond 128K. Mamba2 with State Passing (85.00% at 4K) and Llama-YaRN (53.33% at 4K) both collapse to 0% by 32K, confirming their fixed-size memory bottleneck. NSA (45.36% at 4K) and Stick-Break (60.91% at 4K) also degrade substantially, reaching only 27.27% and 40.14% respectively at 128K. The critical differentiator is extrapolation: while all baselines fail beyond 128K, the full SWA+HSA model maintains 79.77% at 32M tokens, a regime no baseline can reach. On BabiLong, the best SWA+HSA sustains high accuracy to 8M tokens, whereas Landmark fails at ≥64K and Sliding Window Attention plateaus at random-guess level (~0.3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides a systematic dissection of the chunk-based sparse attention paradigm pioneered by Landmark Attention and advanced by DRT (per-chunk retrieval) and RAMba/NSA (per-token retrieval with hardware-aligned chunk selection). Rather than proposing a fundamentally new architecture, it unifies these methods under a common framework and identifies through ablation which specific components—non-linear Chunk Encoder with CLS token, Bypassing Residual Path, and enforced selection sparsity—actually drive extreme length generalization. The stick-breaking weighting strategy from Tan et al. (2025) is adopted for per-token retrieval, and the theoretical framing of the chunk encoder as a non-linear approximator of full attention scores provides new motivation absent from prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper distills the complex design space of long-context sparse attention into three empirically-grounded, actionable design principles, giving practitioners clear guidance for building future long-context models. The 8000× training-free extrapolation (4K → 32M tokens) is a significant practical milestone, and validation on both SWA+HSA and RAMba (SSM-based) architectures suggests the principles are broadly applicable. Open questions include whether these principles scale to much larger models beyond 8B MoE, whether the quadratic chunk-selection cost becomes a bottleneck at truly extreme lengths, and whether the approach transfers to multimodal or non-text settings.
