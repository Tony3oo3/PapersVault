## Problem

Existing Schrödinger Bridge solvers assume energy conservation along the probability path, which prevents them from modeling dissipative or externally-driven stochastic processes common in real-world systems (e.g., storms losing intensity, cell differentiation). Additionally, all current solvers rely on iterative forward-backward schemes or progressive reference refinement, leading to high computational cost and sensitivity to initialization. This paper addresses both limitations simultaneously by introducing a non-conservative formulation and a non-iterative, near-linear-time solver.

## Input / Output

The method consumes endpoint marginal distributions (ρ_a, ρ_b) and optionally intermediate marginals at specified time steps, sampled from a fixed reference measure (e.g., standard Gaussian) that is pushed forward through a sequence of ResNet blocks. For guided generation, an additional target value y and feature function f are provided to steer the bridge at a chosen time t_s. The output is a full stochastic bridge: a sequence of intermediate density distributions at all discretized time steps, from which samples (e.g., images, point clouds, or vector states) can be drawn. Unlike iterative SB solvers that require storing full trajectories, CWG produces the entire path in a single forward pass through the ResNet.

## Method

The paper reformulates the Schrödinger Bridge using contact Hamiltonian mechanics, augmenting the state space with a scalar Lagrangian action z^t whose dynamics include a damping factor γ, allowing total energy to vary over time. Via a generalization of the Jacobi metric construction, the NCGSB optimality conditions are shown to correspond to geodesics on the augmented Wasserstein manifold P⁺(M)×R under a modified metric g̃_J = (H−F−B)g^{W₂}. The geodesic is discretized as a composition of K pushforward maps, each realized as a ResNet block, so that the full bridge is a single ResNet trained end-to-end. Training minimizes a non-iterative loss combining Wasserstein-2 distances to terminal and intermediate marginals with an energy term weighted by the pullback Jacobi metric factor Φ^{t_k}, achieving near-linear complexity O(NK(T_sh + D(LW+log N))) in batch size and dimension. Guided generation is achieved by augmenting the Jacobi metric with a task-specific penalty ‖y−f(x^{t_s})‖², reshaping the geodesic to pass through desired regions without requiring a separate guidance network.

## Results

On the sea-surface temperature prediction task (NOAA OISST v2, 2020–2024), CWG achieves FID of 121±6 at t₁ and 160±7 at t₃, outperforming GSBM (161/186), DSBM (242/236), and SB-Flow (177/190), while training in 17±1 s versus 1227 s for GSBM. On the robotic task reconstruction (BridgeData V2), CWG reaches FID 19±1 in 0.5 h, compared to GSBM (FID 40, 25.3 h), DSBM (FID 150, 7.6 h), and SB-Flow (FID 73, 1.4 h). On single-cell sequencing (Embryoid Body), CWG achieves Wasserstein-2 errors of 1.11±0.06 and 0.33±0.02 at validation steps t₁ and t₃, roughly halving the error of DM-SB (2.25/1.64) and SBIRR (1.92/1.86) while training 50× faster than DM-SB (710 s vs. 38,120 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CWG consistently outperforms all five baselines across every evaluated task. Against GSBM (Liu et al., 2024), CWG achieves lower FID on both sea prediction (121 vs. 161 at t₁) and robotic reconstruction (19 vs. 40), lower Optimality on LiDAR navigation (1.40 vs. 2.18), and trains 70× faster on sea prediction (17 s vs. 1227 s). Against DSBM (Shi et al., 2023), CWG wins on every metric: FID 121 vs. 242 on sea prediction, FID 19 vs. 150 on robotics, and Optimality 1.40 vs. 4.16 on LiDAR. Against SB-Flow (Bortoli et al., 2024), CWG achieves lower FID (121 vs. 177) and trains ~5× faster (17 s vs. 83 s) on sea prediction. On single-cell sequencing, CWG halves the Wasserstein-2 error of both DM-SB (Chen et al., 2023) and SBIRR (Shen et al., 2025) while training 50× faster than DM-SB (710 s vs. 38,120 s). On the FFHQ adult-to-child transfer, CWG is the only method that reliably satisfies the boundary constraint (p-value 2.1×10⁻⁹ for predicted age < 18), whereas GSBM and DSBM fail to preserve the target marginal (p-values ≈ 0.6). A notable exception is the FFHQ Optimality metric, where DSBM (198.0) and GSBM (206.2) report slightly lower transport cost than CWG (218.1), but CWG compensates with far superior Feasibility (4.33 vs. 6.84/7.78), indicating its bridge endpoints align much more closely with the target distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Schrödinger Bridge (SB) line of work by replacing the classical Hamiltonian dynamics (which enforce energy conservation) with contact Hamiltonian dynamics, a well-studied generalization from non-conservative mechanics. The geometric reformulation builds on the Wasserstein Hamiltonian flow framework of Chow et al. (2020) and the Jacobi metric connection to geodesics, but lifts both to the contact setting via the Herglotz variational principle. Practically, it departs from the dominant iterative solvers (IPF, forward-backward SDE matching, iterative reference refinement) by casting the problem as a non-iterative geodesic computation parameterized by a ResNet, where each residual block corresponds to a geodesic segment. The guided generation mechanism, which modulates the Riemannian metric rather than adding a drift term, is a distinct departure from gradient-based guidance in diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The energy-conservation constraint in standard SBs is a genuine modeling limitation for dissipative physical systems (weather, cell differentiation, molecular dynamics), and this paper provides a principled geometric framework to lift that constraint while retaining the Wasserstein manifold structure. The near-linear complexity solver (vs. iterative methods) makes multi-marginal SB computation practical at scale, as demonstrated by order-of-magnitude speedups over DM-SB and SBIRR. Open questions include how the single scalar damping factor γ generalizes to spatially-varying or state-dependent dissipation, whether the ResNet immersion assumption holds in very high-dimensional latent spaces, and whether the guided generation via metric modulation scales to complex multi-constraint settings beyond the single-time-step demonstrations.
