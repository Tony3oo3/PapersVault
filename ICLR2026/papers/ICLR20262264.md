## Problem

Data-driven methods for dynamical systems excel at prediction but offer little interpretability, while evolution operators (Koopman/transfer) provide a spectral decomposition into physically meaningful spatio-temporal modes. Existing operator-learning methods either rely on fixed encoders (kernel methods, least squares) or encoder-decoder architectures that bias toward short-horizon reconstruction, and none scale to high-dimensional structured systems like full-atom molecular dynamics or global climate fields while supporting transfer across related systems.

## Input / Output

The method consumes consecutive state pairs (x_t, x_{t+1}) from a Markovian dynamical system: for molecular dynamics these are full-atom 3D coordinates (e.g., 144 heavy atoms for Trp-Cage, host-guest complexes for SAMPL5), and for climate they are monthly SST anomaly fields on a 121×240 grid at 1.5° resolution. It produces a d×d evolution operator matrix E_φ whose spectral decomposition yields eigenvalues (encoding decay rates and oscillation frequencies), eigenfunctions (spatial modes), and implied timescales; the encoder φ itself is a transferable artifact that can be frozen and applied to unseen systems. The method tracks covariances C_X and C_XY online via exponential moving averages during training to avoid a full forward pass over the dataset at inference time.

## Method

The core contribution is proving that the spectral contrastive loss (originally from self-supervised representation learning) is exactly equivalent to the Hilbert-Schmidt operator-learning loss when the model uses a shared encoder φ for both x_t and x_{t+1} plus a linear predictor P, i.e., the bilinear form ⟨φ(x_t), Pφ(x_{t+1})⟩. This parametrization yields three key theoretical results: the loss equals ‖E − Σ φ_i ⊗ P_ij φ_j‖²_HS (Lemma 1), the optimal P recovers the classical least-squares estimator E_φ = C_X^{-1}C_XY (Lemma 2), and the loss equals the negative VAMP-2 score (Lemma 3), all without requiring matrix inversions during training. Architecturally, a SchNet GNN (molecules) or CNN (climate) serves as φ with simplicial normalization, trained end-to-end via the contrastive objective using U-statistics for the negative-pair term. After training, the evolution operator is assembled from online-tracked covariances and subjected to eigendecomposition to extract dynamical modes, and the frozen encoder can be transferred to new systems (e.g., a ligand unseen during training, or observational climate data after pretraining on CESM simulations).

## Results

On the Lorenz'63 forecasting benchmark, the proposed method achieves the best RMSE (0.49×10⁻²) and MAE (0.32×10⁻²) among all seven methods tested, outperforming the closest competitor DPNets (RMSE 0.58×10⁻², MAE 0.36×10⁻²) while training in 181 ms per epoch. On the climate experiment, the transferred representation (trained on CESM simulations) recovers the ENSO mode with a Pearson correlation of r=0.82 (p<.001) with the Oceanic Niño Index, substantially stronger than direct training on observational data (r=0.71). For Trp-Cage protein folding, the method yields the highest implied timescales among all baselines, indicating the most accurate approximation of slow dynamical modes per the variational principle.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on the Lorenz'63 forecasting task: it beats DPNets (closest competitor) by ~15% in RMSE (0.49 vs 0.58) and ~11% in MAE (0.32 vs 0.36), and substantially outperforms VAMPNets (RMSE 0.78), DAE (0.77), KRR (2.10), and CAE (2.58). On Trp-Cage protein folding, the method achieves higher implied timescales than LoRA (Jeong et al. 2025), VAMPNets, and DPNets, indicating a more accurate spectral approximation of the slow folding mode. In the climate experiment, the method achieves a stronger ONI correlation (r=0.82) than both VAMPNets and DPNets (reported in Appendix B.4), and the transfer-from-CESM strategy outperforms direct training on the limited 540-snapshot ORAS5 dataset (r=0.82 vs r=0.71). For ligand binding, the transferred representation (trained on G1/G3) recovers the same dominant eigenfunctions as a model trained from scratch on G2, demonstrating transferability that none of the baselines explicitly support. The method's training time (181 ms/epoch) is comparable to VAMPNets (166 ms) and DPNets (191 ms), while KRR is ~140× slower and CAE is ~2.3× slower.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: self-supervised contrastive representation learning (HaoChen et al., 2021) and operator-theoretic dynamical systems analysis (Kostic et al., 2022; Wu & Noe, 2020). The key conceptual move is showing that the spectral contrastive loss, when instantiated with a shared encoder and a linear predictor, is provably equivalent to both the least-squares evolution operator regression loss and the negative VAMP-2 score (Lemmas 1–3). This departs from prior encoder-only operator learning methods (VAMPNets, DPNets) that use bespoke loss functions, and from concurrent work (Jeong et al., 2025) that uses separate encoders for past and future states, by demonstrating that the shared-encoder-plus-linear-predictor parametrization is the one that recovers the classical least-squares framework with its convergence guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that evolution operator learning can be scaled to high-dimensional structured systems (144-atom proteins, global SST fields) and, more importantly, that the resulting representations transfer across related dynamical systems without retraining—a capability absent from prior operator learning work. This opens a path toward amortized dynamical modeling in drug discovery and climate science where retraining per system is prohibitive. Key limitations include the qualitative nature of evaluation (no standard benchmark for spectral decomposition accuracy), the single-system evidence for transfer, and the restriction to Markovian dynamics. Open questions include whether the contrastive-operator connection extends to control tasks, non-Markovian settings, and more expressive predictor architectures.
