## Problem

Gaussian kernels in radiance field rendering enforce a fixed bell-shaped profile across all dimensions, preventing a single primitive from simultaneously modeling sharp spatial edges, narrow specular highlights, and abrupt temporal motion. Existing N-dimensional Gaussian extensions (6DGS, 7DGS) inherit this symmetric coupling, and view-dependent or temporal effects require auxiliary spherical harmonic encodings that fragment the representation and inflate per-primitive parameter counts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263436/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes calibrated multi-view RGB images (and per-frame time indices for dynamic scenes) along with camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and produces rendered novel-view RGB images or video frames via front-to-back alpha compositing. Unlike 3DGS/6DGS/7DGS which require 48–144 spherical harmonic coefficients per primitive for color, UBS uses only 3 direct RGB values per primitive because view and temporal dependence are encoded natively in the kernel's query dimensions, yielding a 41% parameter reduction for static and 73% for dynamic scenes.

## Method

UBS replaces fixed Gaussian primitives with N-dimensional anisotropic Beta kernels whose per-dimension shape parameters (b_i) independently control kernel sharpness from flat to peaked. Three key mechanisms enable this: (1) a Spatial-Orthogonal Cholesky factorization that preserves explicit SO(3) rotation-scale structure in the 3D spatial block while allowing flexible cross-dimensional correlations via a lower-triangular coupling matrix; (2) Beta-Modulated Conditional Slicing that conditions the N-D kernel on query dimensions (view direction, time) using dimension-specific Beta exponents to produce a renderable 3D splat with anisotropic falloff; and (3) a factorized Beta-modulated opacity that gates each non-spatial dimension independently. When all Beta parameters are zero, the kernel approximates a Gaussian, recovering 3DGS, 6DGS, or 7DGS as special cases. Training uses MCMC-based densification (inherited from DBS) with L1+SSIM reconstruction loss, opacity/scale regularization, and custom fused CUDA kernels for real-time rendering.

## Results

On static benchmarks, UBS-6D achieves 34.92 dB PSNR on NeRF Synthetic (+1.13 dB over 3DGS, +1.32 dB over 6DGS), 28.66 dB on Mip-NeRF 360 (+1.23 dB over 3DGS, +2.31 dB over 6DGS), and 40.10 dB on 6DGS-PBR (+12.70 dB over 3DGS, +3.42 dB over 6DGS). On dynamic benchmarks, UBS-7D reaches 33.00 dB PSNR on 7DGS-PBR (+5.26 dB over 4DGS, +1.25 dB over 7DGS) and 33.35 dB on D-NeRF (+1.26 dB over 4DGS, +0.51 dB over 7DGS). The largest single-scene gain is +8.27 dB on the bunny scene (6DGS-PBR) and +2.78 dB on heart1 (7DGS-PBR). UBS also reduces per-primitive parameters by 41% (static) and 73% (dynamic) compared to Gaussian baselines while training 31–69% faster on most datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UBS-6D consistently outperforms 3DGS across all three static benchmarks, with the largest margin on 6DGS-PBR (+12.70 dB average, up to +8.27 dB on the bunny scene) where complex view-dependent effects (volumetric scattering, translucency) benefit most from per-dimension Beta control. Against 6DGS, UBS-6D gains +1.32 dB on NeRF Synthetic, +2.31 dB on Mip-NeRF 360, and +3.42 dB on 6DGS-PBR, while also training 31–69% faster. On dynamic scenes, UBS-7D surpasses 4DGS by +5.26 dB on 7DGS-PBR and +1.26 dB on D-NeRF, and beats 7DGS (without its auxiliary AGR network) by +1.25 dB and +0.51 dB respectively. In the broader SOTA comparison (Table 4), UBS-6D achieves the highest PSNR on both Mip-NeRF 360 (28.66 dB) and NeRF Synthetic (34.92 dB), edging out DBS (28.60/34.64 dB) and 3DGS-MCMC (28.29/33.80 dB). On the real-world N3V dataset, UBS-7D attains 32.22 dB average PSNR, outperforming Ex4DGS (32.11 dB) and 7DGS (31.59 dB) while winning 4 of 6 individual scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

UBS unifies two previously separate research threads: Deformable Beta Splatting (DBS), which introduced adaptive Beta kernels for 3D spatial geometry but remained confined to spatial dimensions and required separate spherical Beta functions for view-dependent color, and N-dimensional Gaussian splatting (6DGS/7DGS), which extended conditional slicing to view and temporal dimensions but was locked into fixed Gaussian bell-shaped profiles across all dimensions. The key conceptual departure is replacing the Gaussian kernel's uniform exponential decay with per-dimension Beta shape parameters, enabling each dimension (spatial, angular, temporal) to independently adopt flat, Gaussian-like, or peaked profiles. Technically, the spatial-orthogonal Cholesky parameterization and Beta-modulated conditional slicing are novel contributions that bridge the gap between DBS's 3D adaptivity and 6DGS/7DGS's high-dimensional conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating auxiliary spherical harmonic encodings (reducing per-primitive parameters by 41% for static and 73% for dynamic scenes) while simultaneously improving reconstruction quality, UBS offers a more compact and expressive primitive that could become a practical drop-in replacement for 3DGS/6DGS/7DGS pipelines. The emergent interpretability of learned Beta parameters—separating geometry from texture, diffuse from specular, static from dynamic—without explicit supervision opens avenues for relighting and motion analysis. Open questions include whether the bounded Beta support (d ∈ [0,1)) is always appropriate for very large or unbounded scenes, how the method scales to dimensions beyond 7 (e.g., multi-lighting or material parameters), and whether the MCMC-based primitive management remains stable at much larger scene scales.
