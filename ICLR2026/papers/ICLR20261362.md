## Problem

Current MLLM-based IQA methods rely on general contextual descriptions that lack precise spatial information, making fine-grained quality assessment difficult. Specifically, they cannot provide bounding boxes for quality-affecting regions, and language-based referring may fail to accurately pinpoint local targets, limiting comprehensive low-level perception.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261362/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text question (which may embed spatial coordinates for referring) and produces either a quality description with interleaved bounding boxes in the format '[object/region] (idx_l, idx_r)' or a VQA answer that may include grounding coordinates. Bounding boxes are discretized into a 20×20 grid index representation (at most 9 tokens) rather than continuous normalized coordinates (21 tokens), avoiding specialized tokens and ensuring compatibility with standard MLLM tokenizers.

## Method

The paper introduces grounding-IQA, a new paradigm integrating multimodal referring (position-in) and grounding (position-out) with IQA, comprising two subtasks: GIQA-DES (quality description with bounding boxes) and GIQA-VQA (spatial quality QA). Data is constructed via an automated pipeline: for GIQA-DES, a four-stage process extracts object tags via Llama3, detects boxes with Grounding DINO using description phrases, refines boxes through an IQA-Filter (verifying quality attributes with Q-Instruct) and Box-Merge algorithm, then fuses discrete grid coordinates into natural language; for GIQA-VQA, Llama3 generates QA pairs from GIQA-DES descriptions using Yes/No and What/Why/How templates. The resulting GIQA-160K dataset (167K samples, 43K images) is used for full supervised fine-tuning of pre-trained MLLMs (LLaVA, mPLUG-Owl2) with cross-entropy loss, and a 250-sample expert-annotated benchmark (GIQA-Bench) evaluates description quality, VQA accuracy, and grounding precision (mIoU, Tag-Recall).

## Results

On GIQA-Bench, the best Grounding-IQA model (mPLUG-Owl2-7B) achieves an LLM-Score of 63.00 and BLEU@4 of 22.87 on GIQA-DES, and Acc(Total) of 0.7417 with Tag-Recall of 0.7372 on GIQA-VQA. Grounding-IQA(LLaVA-v1.6-7B) attains the highest GIQA-DES mIoU (0.6583) and Acc(Y) (0.8333), while Grounding-IQA(LLaVA-v1.5-13B) leads in GIQA-VQA mIoU (0.6830) and Tag-Recall (0.7564). All four fine-tuned models substantially outperform their respective base models, e.g., mPLUG-Owl2-7B improves from LLM-Score 48.25 to 63.00 and Acc(Total) from 0.5633 to 0.7417.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against general MLLMs (LLaVA, mPLUG-Owl2), Grounding-IQA achieves dramatic gains: mPLUG-Owl2-7B improves from LLM-Score 48.25 to 63.00 and Acc(Total) from 0.5633 to 0.7417, while also gaining grounding capability (mIoU 0.5955, Tag-Recall 0.5474) that was entirely absent in the base model. Compared to grounding-specialized models (Ferret-7B, GroundingGPT-7B, Shikra-7B), Grounding-IQA substantially outperforms on quality-description metrics (BLEU@4: 22.87 vs. 3.16 for Ferret; LLM-Score: 63.00 vs. 43.75) and VQA accuracy (Acc(Total): 0.7417 vs. 0.4417 for Ferret), though Ferret-7B retains a slight edge on GIQA-DES mIoU (0.6458 vs. 0.5955 for mPLUG variant). Versus IQA-specialized models (Q-Instruct, DepictQA), Grounding-IQA matches or exceeds description quality (LLM-Score 63.00 vs. 62.00 for Q-Instruct on mPLUG) while adding full grounding capability (mIoU, Tag-Recall) that IQA models lack entirely, and surpasses them on VQA accuracy (Acc(Total) 0.7417 vs. 0.5817 for Q-Instruct on mPLUG). Overall, Grounding-IQA is the only method that simultaneously achieves strong performance across all three evaluation axes—description quality, VQA accuracy, and grounding precision—whereas each baseline group excels in at most one or two.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established research threads: MLLM-based image quality assessment (Q-Instruct, Q-Align, DepictQA) and multimodal referring/grounding (Shikra, Kosmos-2, Ferret, GroundingGPT). Prior IQA methods produce quality descriptions but lack spatial precision, while grounding models handle bounding boxes but ignore low-level quality attributes. The paper's core conceptual move is to unify these threads into a single paradigm (grounding-IQA) where spatial information flows both into (referring) and out of (grounding) quality-related outputs, building on LLaVA and mPLUG-Owl2 as base architectures and leveraging Grounding DINO and Llama3 within an automated annotation pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The grounding-IQA paradigm and its accompanying GIQA-160K dataset (167K samples) and GIQA-Bench provide the community with the first infrastructure for evaluating spatially-grounded quality perception, a capability that could enable targeted image editing, region-specific quality control, and more interpretable IQA. However, the benchmark is small (100 images), grounding precision remains moderate (mIoU ~0.6), and the automated pipeline inherits quality limitations from its source descriptions. Open questions include whether the discrete coordinate representation generalizes to higher-resolution or video settings, and whether the paradigm extends beyond 2D bounding boxes to segmentation-level quality grounding.
