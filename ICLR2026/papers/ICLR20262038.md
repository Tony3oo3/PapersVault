## Problem

Existing Transformer-based anomaly detection methods for multivariate time series offer limited theoretical insight into what the model actually learns, and anomaly localization—identifying which specific time series caused a detected anomaly—remains largely unexplored. Additionally, many methods rely on point-adjustment evaluation that inflates performance, and their anomaly scores often fail to capture the temporal structure of anomalies, making detection unreliable in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262038/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes overlapping windows of a multivariate time series (T×d matrix, where T is the window size and d the number of variables) and produces three outputs: a per-timestamp anomaly score (reconstruction error multiplied by the ALoRa-T rank score), a per-time-series localization score (contribution-weighted reconstruction error), and the full reconstructed window. The embedding layer restricts each convolutional kernel to aggregate exactly two input series selected by top-K Spearman correlation, making the input representation sparse and interpretable rather than fully dense.

## Method

The paper first proves that a Transformer encoder's latent space over MTS has a Space-Time Autoregressive (STAR) structure, where self-attention matrices act as input-dependent temporal weights and Value projections act as learned spatial weights. Building on this, ALoRa-T replaces dense 1D-convolutional embedding with a sparse pairwise module (top-K Spearman-correlated series pairs), omits feed-forward layers (justified by the STAR analysis), and applies a truncated Geman nuclear norm loss to each self-attention matrix to enforce low-rank structure during training. At inference, the ALoRa-T score counts singular values of the final attention matrix exceeding a data-driven threshold h₁, capturing the observation that anomalies inflate attention rank. For localization, ALoRa-Loc analytically derives contribution weights E_ij (input series i → latent feature j) and C_ij (input series i → reconstructed output j) from the STAR decomposition, then computes per-series localization scores by weighting each reconstruction error by the corresponding C_ij, enabling anomalies to be traced back to their originating time series even when they propagate through the latent space.

## Results

ALoRa-Det achieves affiliation-based F1-scores of 0.97 (SMD), 0.82 (PSM), 0.72 (MSL), 0.68 (SWaT), and 0.86 (HAI), outperforming all baselines on four of five datasets and ranking second on SWaT. The ALoRa-T score captures temporal anomaly characteristics more effectively than competing methods, enabling earlier detection. For localization, ALoRa-Loc (top-2) achieves HR@100 of 0.56 and NDCG@100 of 0.60 on SMD, outperforming the next-best method (SARAD) by 12 and 13 percentage points respectively. The model is also computationally efficient at 3.2M parameters with ~75 ms inference per sample, compared to 52.3M parameters and 4.83 ms for D³R.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALoRa-Det achieves the best F1 on SMD (0.97), PSM (0.82), MSL (0.72), and HAI (0.86), with absolute improvements of 10%, 6%, 4%, and 7% over the respective second-best methods (NPSR/D³R at 0.87, D³R at 0.76, NPSR at 0.68, D³R at 0.79). On SWaT, D³R edges out ALoRa-Det (0.71 vs. 0.68 F1), though ALoRa still substantially outperforms most other methods. Classical baselines (KNN, PCA, LOF, OC-SVM, IsolationForest) are consistently outperformed by 20–80 F1 points across all datasets. Among deep-learning baselines, MEMTO and Anomaly Transformer produce scores close to random guessing under affiliation-based evaluation, while SARAD and NPSR offer more informative signals but fail to capture temporal anomaly patterns as effectively. For localization, ALoRa-Loc dominates on SMD (HR@100: 0.56 vs. SARAD's 0.44) and SWaT (IPS@100: 0.16 vs. SARAD's 0.11), while AERCA slightly leads on MSDS HR@100 (0.33 vs. 0.30) but ALoRa-Loc wins on NDCG@150 (0.45 vs. 0.40).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Transformer-based reconstruction paradigm for MTS anomaly detection (Anomaly Transformer, MEMTO, SARAD) by grounding the architecture in classical statistical time series theory. Specifically, it shows that the self-attention latent space has a Space-Time Autoregressive (STAR) structure and that the embedding layer is equivalent to learnable VMA filtering, then leverages these connections to derive both a low-rank regularization loss on attention matrices and a contribution-weight-based localization method. Unlike prior localization approaches that rely on reconstruction error ranking (OmniAnomaly, SARAD) or gradient attribution (DAEMON), ALoRa-Loc traces anomalies backward through analytically derived inter-series influence weights, and unlike prior detection scores that compare attention to fixed priors (AssDis) or track attention changes (SAR), the ALoRa-T score directly measures the rank inflation of attention matrices under anomaly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: anomaly localization in MTS has received far less attention than detection, and existing methods lack theoretical justification for how anomalies propagate across series. The STAR-structure analysis provides the first principled link between Transformer internals and classical time series models, offering interpretability that is critical for safety-critical deployments. The computational efficiency (constant 3.2M parameters regardless of input dimensionality) makes the method practical for high-dimensional industrial settings. Open questions include extending the theoretical analysis from single-head to multi-head attention and adapting the method to scenarios with concept drift alongside anomalies, both acknowledged by the authors as future work.
