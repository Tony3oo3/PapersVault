## Problem

Reasoning-tuned multimodal large reasoning models (MLRMs) paradoxically produce more harmful outputs than their base counterparts, yet existing safety alignment methods rely on simplistic refusal templates that teach models what to reject without teaching them how to reason safely, and they induce over-sensitivity that degrades general reasoning performance. The paper identifies two underlying mechanisms—visual attention drift and unsafe reasoning patterns (flawed initiation and chain-of-thought safety attenuation)—that remain unaddressed by prior work.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image paired with a text instruction, which is expanded into a structured input tuple (instruction, GPT-4o-generated image description, policy category, and the corresponding policy document) before being fed to the model. The output is a two-part generation: an explicit chain-of-thought reasoning trace that must cite specific policy clauses and label each judgment as grounded in visual cues, textual context, or their interaction, followed by a final text response that either safely fulfills or refuses the request.

## Method

PST is a two-stage alignment framework. Stage 1 (PST-SFT) fine-tunes the model on ~1k curated exemplars where DeepSeek-R1 generates deliberative trajectories over structured inputs spanning 20 predefined safety policy categories; each trajectory must explicitly reference policy rules and specify multimodal grounding, directly countering visual attention drift and flawed reasoning initiation. Stage 2 (SRPO) applies DPO-style preference optimization using chosen responses from the high-quality SFT pool and rejected responses constructed via two strategies: comparative failure mining (selecting the worst output from six MLLMs) and post-hoc adversarial reasoning generation (prompting DeepSeek-R1 to retroactively produce a logically coherent but safety-deficient reasoning trace for a bad answer). This two-stage design targets chain-of-thought safety attenuation while preserving helpfulness and general multimodal reasoning capabilities.

## Results

On the R1-Onevision base model, PST achieves the lowest Harmful Rate (HR) on BeaverTails-V (9.00%) and MM-SafetyBench (2.68%), while also reducing the over-refusal rate on MMSafetyAware to 69.39%—a 19.2-point improvement over Think-in-Safety (88.55%). On LLaMA-CoT, PST similarly attains the best HR on BeaverTails-V (13.58%) and the lowest refusal rate (46.26%). On general capability benchmarks, PST preserves or even improves performance (e.g., VQAv2 80.87% and GQA 55.20% for R1-Onevision), whereas baselines like Think-in-Safety degrade VQAv2 to 71.40% and GQA to 44.90%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PST outperforms both Think-in-Safety and MSR-Align on the majority of safety benchmarks. Against Think-in-Safety, PST reduces HR by 5.77 points on BeaverTails-V (9.00 vs. 14.77) and 17.02 points on MM-SafetyBench (2.68 vs. 19.70) for R1-Onevision, while also cutting the over-refusal rate by 19.2 points (69.39 vs. 88.55). Against MSR-Align, PST achieves lower HR on BeaverTails-V (9.00 vs. 11.71) and MM-SafetyBench (2.68 vs. 3.99), though MSR-Align retains a slight edge on SIUO for R1-Onevision (8.38 vs. 12.57). On LLaMA-CoT, PST dominates across all five metrics, with the largest gap on MMSafetyAware refusal rate (46.26 vs. 67.52 for MSR-Align and 78.27 for Think-in-Safety). On general capability benchmarks, PST preserves performance (VQAv2 80.87%, GQA 55.20%) while Think-in-Safety causes severe degradation (VQAv2 71.40%, GQA 44.90%) and MSR-Align also drops (VQAv2 74.40%, GQA 45.50%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the safety alignment literature for multimodal models by departing from the dominant refusal-based paradigm (MSR-Align, Think-in-Safety, Safe RLHF-V) that trains models to decline unsafe requests. Instead, PST embeds explicit, structured safety policies directly into the chain-of-thought reasoning process, using a two-stage pipeline: policy-guided SFT followed by DPO-based preference optimization (building directly on Rafailov et al., 2023). The key conceptual departure is the diagnosis of two specific degradation mechanisms—visual attention drift and chain-of-thought safety attenuation—which prior work identified only anecdotally but did not target with dedicated training signals. The structured input format that forces the model to attribute each judgment to visual, textual, or cross-modal evidence is a novel data-construction choice not present in competing methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As reasoning-tuned multimodal models become the default deployment target, the demonstrated safety gap (78–84% harmful rate vs. 54–69% for base models) is a practical deployment blocker that this paper addresses with a compact, interpretable solution. The finding that only 1k policy-guided samples suffice for strong alignment is practically significant, reducing annotation cost. However, the approach relies heavily on GPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1 for data generation, raising questions about whether the learned safety reasoning is genuinely internalized or merely mimics the generator's style. Open questions include generalization to video modalities, robustness to adversarial policy-evasion prompts, and whether the 20-category framework scales as new risk categories emerge.
