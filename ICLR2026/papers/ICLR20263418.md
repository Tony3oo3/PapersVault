## Problem

Existing visual token pruning methods for VLMs achieve competitive performance on VQA benchmarks but suffer catastrophic degradation on visual grounding tasks (e.g., dropping from 58% to 4% on RefCOCO). The paper identifies the root cause: pruning strategies based on global semantic similarity or attention scores destroy the global spatial reference frame constructed from token positional information, which grounding tasks critically depend on.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263418/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is a training-free, plug-and-play inference-time pipeline that takes a standard RGB image (encoded by CLIP ViT-Large into 576 vision tokens) and a text prompt. Stage 1 reduces tokens in the vision encoder output (e.g., 576→112), and Stage 2 further prunes within the LLM (e.g., 112→16), yielding 64–192 final tokens. The LLM then produces either a free-form text answer (VQA) or a bounding box coordinate (grounding), with the pruned tokens retaining their remapped positional embeddings to preserve spatial coordinates in the original image frame.

## Method

Nüwa is a two-stage spatial-aware pruning framework. Stage 1 (vision encoder) applies a Boids-inspired algorithm: (1) Separation partitions the 24×24 token grid into M×M non-overlapping regions to maintain a complete global coordinate system; (2) Alignment selects representative 'benchmark' tokens per region using a salience score combining CLS attention weight and L2-norm information capacity; (3) Aggregation merges neighboring features into benchmarks via a weight matrix that multiplies positive semantic similarity (ReLU of cosine) by a spatial proximity penalty, with high-norm 'Pillar' tokens kept unmodified to avoid disrupting register-like features. Stage 2 (LLM intermediate layers, after multimodal alignment) computes a holistic text query vector by average-pooling text token embeddings, then retains only the top-K visual tokens with highest cosine similarity to this query in the shared projection space. The key design insight is that grid-based region partitioning implicitly implements Relative Position Mapping Extension (RPME), preserving spatial continuity of positional embeddings and thereby maintaining the global reference frame essential for grounding.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B with 88.9% token reduction (64 tokens), Nüwa achieves 94.91% average performance retention on 10 VQA benchmarks and 47.19% on the RefCOCO series visual grounding benchmarks, substantially outperforming all prior methods. At a milder 66.7% reduction (192 tokens), Nüwa retains 98.80% on VQA and 79.29% on VG. In terms of efficiency, Nüwa reduces TFLOPs by 89% and prefill time by 62% relative to the vanilla model, with only 1 ms additional latency from the pruning metric computation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Nüwa decisively outperforms all six baselines on visual grounding, where the gap is most dramatic: at 64 tokens, Nüwa retains 47.19% of vanilla RefCOCO performance versus only 7.28% for VisionZip, 3.81% for FastV, and 1.88% for SparseVLM. On VQA benchmarks at the same 64-token budget, Nüwa (94.91%) edges out VisionZip (93.99%) and PruMerge (91.71%), and substantially surpasses SparseVLM (89.93%), FastV (79.36%), and PyramidDrop (71.56%). At the 192-token setting, Nüwa (79.29% on VG) also beats Feather (48.38%), the strongest prior VG-focused method. The paper's key finding is that while VisionZip and PruMerge are competitive on VQA, they collapse on grounding tasks due to loss of the global spatial reference frame, whereas Nüwa's Boids-inspired spatial aggregation preserves this structure, yielding a 6–25× improvement on VG over all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits within the established line of visual token pruning for VLMs (FastV, VisionZip, SparseVLM, PyramidDrop, PruMerge) but departs fundamentally by diagnosing *why* these methods fail on grounding tasks: loss of the global spatial reference frame encoded in position embeddings, rather than mere semantic information loss. It repurposes the Boids swarm-intelligence framework (separation, alignment, cohesion) as a principled mechanism for spatially-aware token aggregation in the vision encoder, and combines this with text-guided pruning in the LLM. The position reconstruction experiments (RPME) build on the implicit position-embedding handling strategies already present in prior methods (PERC in VisionZip, PESP in FastV) to isolate spatial integrity as the causal variable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that naive average pooling outperforms all sophisticated pruning methods on grounding tasks, and that simply restoring continuous position embeddings (RPME) recovers most of the lost performance, challenges the field's assumption that semantic-similarity-based token selection is the right objective. This reframes token pruning as a spatial-structure preservation problem rather than a semantic-relevance problem, with broad implications for how future efficient-VLM methods are designed. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video (temporal spatial integrity), higher-resolution inputs where the spatial grid is denser, and whether the 47% VG retention at 88.9% token reduction is sufficient for production grounding applications or whether further architectural changes are needed.
