## Problem

For ill-conditioned quadratic problems with power-law spectral decay, deterministic Gradient Descent can be accelerated from O(t^{-ζ}) to O(t^{-2ζ}) via Heavy Ball with Jacobi schedules, but this acceleration fails for mini-batch SGD because accumulating sampling noise causes divergence. No algorithm achieving O(t^{-2ζ}) rates was known in the stochastic, infinite-dimensional setting, leaving a fundamental gap between deterministic and stochastic acceleration theory.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a quadratic loss with power-law spectral conditions (capacity exponent ν, source exponent ζ) and consumes mini-batch gradient estimates of size |B| at each step. The key design parameters are the corner angle θ ∈ (1,2) and the finite memory size M used to approximate the ideal infinite-memory algorithm. The output is a parameter trajectory w_t whose loss converges at rate O(t^{-θζ}) in the signal-dominated regime, with θ_max = min(2, ν, 2/(ζ+1/ν)) determined by the spectral exponents. Unlike standard SGD, the algorithm requires storing M auxiliary vectors of the same dimension as w_t, but the per-iteration arithmetic cost remains negligible when |B| ≫ M.

## Method

The paper establishes a geometric correspondence between stationary memory-M (S)GD algorithms and contours γ in the complex plane, via the response map Ψ = P/Q derived from the characteristic polynomial. The central insight is that contours with a corner at the origin having external angle θπ produce signal propagators decaying as t^{-θζ} (accelerating plain GD's t^{-ζ} rate) while simultaneously amplifying noise propagators as t^{θ/ν - 2}. The optimal θ is found by balancing these competing effects, yielding the three-region phase diagram with θ_max = min(2, ν, 2/(ζ+1/ν)). Since the ideal corner map Ψ(μ) ∝ (μ-1)^θ is irrational, the paper constructs practical finite-memory algorithms by approximating z^θ with rational functions via the Gopal–Trefethen trapezoidal scheme, achieving O(e^{-c√M}) approximation error and thus near-optimal acceleration for moderate M.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4) establishes that in the signal-dominated regime (ν > 1, 0 < ζ < 2 − 1/ν), Corner SGD achieves a loss rate of O(t^{−θζ}) with optimal acceleration factor θ_max = min(2, ν, 2/(ζ + 1/ν)). In the fully accelerated sub-phase (ν > 2, 0 < ζ < 1 − 1/ν), the rate reaches O(t^{−2ζ}), matching the deterministic Heavy Ball bound. Empirically, on a synthetic kernel problem with batch size |B| = 100, Corner SGD with θ = 1.8 and finite memory M = 5 visibly accelerates the power-law convergence exponent of plain SGD (Figure 3), and similar acceleration is confirmed on MNIST.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Corner SGD against plain (stationary finite-memory) SGD and the non-stationary Jacobi Heavy Ball. Plain SGD with any finite memory M provably cannot accelerate the O(t^{−ζ}) rate in the signal-dominated regime (Yarotsky & Velikanov, 2024), while the Jacobi Heavy Ball, which achieves O(t^{−2ζ}) for deterministic GD, diverges under mini-batch SGD due to accumulating sampling noise. Corner SGD with infinite memory (or its finite-memory rational approximation) is the first method shown to provably accelerate SGD from O(t^{−ζ}) to O(t^{−θζ}) with θ up to 2 in the infinite-dimensional setting. On the synthetic problem (Figure 3), Corner SGD (θ = 1.8, M = 5) produces a steeper smoothed loss trajectory than plain SGD, confirming the theoretical exponent improvement. The computational overhead of the finite-memory approximation is negligible when |B| ≫ M, since the additional O(MW) operations per step are dwarfed by the O(|B|W) gradient computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the memory-M SGD framework of Yarotsky & Velikanov (2024), which established that all stationary finite-memory SGD algorithms share the same phase diagram as plain SGD and cannot accelerate the O(t^{-ζ}) rate. The key departure is the introduction of a complex-analytic 'contour view' that identifies generalized (S)GD with contours in the complex plane, revealing that contours with a corner (external angle θπ) break the finite-memory limitation and achieve O(t^{-θζ}) rates. The finite-memory implementation leverages the rational approximation theory of Gopal & Trefethen (2019) for power functions, while the spectral analysis framework (SE approximation, propagator expansions) is inherited from Velikanov et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The contour viewpoint provides a unifying geometric language for SGD acceleration that was previously absent, and the three-phase diagram (fully accelerated, signal/noise balanced, U_Σ-limited) gives a precise characterization of when and how much acceleration is possible. However, the practical impact is tempered: finite-memory approximations do not theoretically accelerate as t→∞ (only the ideal infinite-memory algorithm does), and the empirical validation is limited to a single MNIST experiment. Open questions include whether non-stationary finite-memory schedules can provably achieve the corner rate, how the theory extends to non-quadratic losses and feature learning regimes, and whether the SE approximation with τ₂≠0 changes the phase diagram at moderate batch sizes.
