## Problem

Standard chain-of-thought reasoning forces a model to commit to a single discrete token at each decoding step, capping information at log₂(ν) bits per step and preventing parallel exploration of alternative reasoning paths. This paper asks whether replacing discrete tokens with continuous-valued superpositions of vocabulary embeddings can enable a single trace to simultaneously track multiple reasoning trajectories, effectively emulating self-consistency or best-of-N decoding within one forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262799/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a context X ∈ R^{n×d} (a list of m integers for MNNS, or a tokenized graph structure with nodes and edges for ProntoQA/ProsQA) and produces m−1 continuous thought tokens followed by one discrete answer token. Each continuous token z_t = E^T α_t is a convex combination of all ν vocabulary embeddings weighted by the softmax output, living in R^d rather than being a single vocabulary entry. The final discrete token is sampled from the model's output distribution and is the only token evaluated for correctness.

## Method

CoT2 replaces discrete next-token prediction with continuous token generation: at each intermediate step the model's softmax output α_t is mapped to a d-dimensional embedding via z_t = E^T α_t, allowing a single token to encode a superposition of multiple states. Training uses a budget-constrained continuous supervision strategy (CSFT) that fits the model to the empirical token distribution over the top-B trajectories (ranked by a task-specific score), interpolating between discrete CoT (B=1) and full parallel search (B=|T|). Theoretically, the paper proves a one-layer transformer with d = 2^{n+1} + n+2 embedding dimension solves MNNS via non-overlapping sine-cosine state encodings, and shows that CoT2-MTS (sampling K tokens and averaging) achieves the same estimation accuracy as K independent discrete CoT traces with Θ(K) fewer rollouts. For reinforcement learning, the paper introduces GRPO in a continuous action space using either multi-token sampling (geometric-mean policy ratio over K sampled tokens) or Dirichlet sampling over the probability simplex, demonstrating that RL further sharpens the model's trace selection beyond supervised training.

## Results

On the MNNS task (4 input digits, 2-layer 2-head GPT2, d=32), CoT2 with full trajectory budget achieves 98.94% validation accuracy, outperforming COCONUT (92.58%), discrete CoT (84.92%), and no-CoT (68.35%). On ProsQA and ProntoQA (4-layer 4-head GPT2, d=32), CoT2 reaches 93.37% and 98.01% respectively, again leading all baselines. GRPO with multi-token sampling (K=12) further improves CoT2 to 94.21% on ProsQA and 77.64% on ProntoQA, while discrete CoT benefits more substantially (68.50%→72.76% on ProsQA; 59.58%→74.03% on ProntoQA). On GSM8K with Qwen3-0.6B, CoT2-MTS (K=4) achieves 41.3% at max response length 160 versus 37.0% for discrete CoT, while also shortening average response length at shorter budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoT2 outperforms COCONUT (Hao et al., 2024) on all three tasks: by 6.36 points on MNNS (98.94% vs 92.58%), 3.34 points on ProsQA (93.37% vs 90.03%), and 1.07 points on ProntoQA (98.01% vs 96.94%). The gap is largest on MNNS, where COCONUT's left-to-right curriculum of replacing discrete tokens with hidden states is less effective than CoT2's explicit superposition of all reachable states. Against discrete CoT, CoT2's advantage is most dramatic on ProsQA (93.37% vs 68.50%, a 24.87-point gap), reflecting that search-heavy tasks benefit most from parallel exploration. CoT2 also requires substantially fewer samples: in Pass@k experiments, discrete CoT needs multiple rollouts to approach CoT2's single-shot performance. After GRPO, the discrete CoT model narrows the gap with CoT2 on ProntoQA (74.03% vs 77.64% at K=12), suggesting that RL partially compensates for the lack of built-in parallelism, but CoT2 retains a consistent edge across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on COCONUT (Hao et al., 2024), which first demonstrated that LLMs can reason in continuous latent space by feeding hidden states autoregressively, and on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for policy optimization. The key departures are: (1) continuous tokens are defined as simplex-weighted compositions of vocabulary embeddings rather than raw hidden states, (2) a novel budget-constrained supervision strategy (CSFT) explicitly targets parallel tracking of multiple reasoning traces, and (3) GRPO is extended to a continuous action space via multi-token sampling and Dirichlet sampling. The paper also draws on mechanistic interpretability insights (Nanda et al., 2023) for its trigonometric embedding construction and connects to self-consistency and best-of-N decoding as the discrete analogs of what CoT2 achieves in a single forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical characterization of how continuous tokens enable parallel reasoning, including an information-packing bound (d = Ω(B log(ν/B))) that quantifies the budget-embedding tradeoff and a sample-complexity result showing CoT2-MTS with parallelism K reduces estimation error by a factor of K versus discrete CoT. However, all main experiments use small GPT2 models (1–4 layers) on synthetic or semi-synthetic tasks, and the GSM8K results with Qwen3-0.6B are preliminary and modest, leaving open whether the parallelism benefits and CSFT strategy scale to large language models on open-ended reasoning. The authors identify selective application of CSFT to segments of LLM trajectories as a promising but unexplored direction.
