## Problem

The GNN community widely assumes that edges with highly negative discrete curvature are the ones causing over-squashing, but Topping et al. (2021) only proved sufficiency, never necessity. This paper shows the necessity fails: it constructs counterexample graphs where edges suffer severe over-squashing yet all eight standard curvature definitions return positive values, and empirically demonstrates that Ollivier-Ricci curvature misses 30–40% of truly over-squashed edges across 21 datasets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an undirected graph G=(V,E), a degree-weighting function f (default f(x)=1/(1+x)), and a number of hash functions H for the approximation pass. It outputs per-edge WAF3 curvature values and a set of identified over-squashed edges; the MinHash approximation runs in O(H|E|) time, computing curvature on 5 million edges in ~24 seconds. For the MOSR evaluation, the pipeline additionally requires Jacobian norms ‖∂h_v^(L)/∂h_u^(0)‖_F from a randomly initialized L-layer MPNN (GCN, GAT, or GraphSAGE) to establish ground-truth over-squashing per edge.

## Method

The core contribution is WAF3, a weighted refinement of Augmented Forman-3 curvature that multiplies each node's contribution by f(d_i), correcting for the fact that high-degree intermediate nodes dilute rather than enhance information flow (Theorem 5 proves WAF3 eliminates the counterexamples when f(∞)=0⁺). The key technical insight (Theorem 6) algebraically reformulates WAF3 as a function of a weighted Jaccard similarity between the neighbor sets of the two edge endpoints, replacing the expensive set-intersection with a similarity query. This enables a weighted MinHash approximation (Algorithm 1) that reduces per-edge cost from O(d_max) to O(H), yielding a 133.7× speedup on 5M-edge graphs while preserving ~95% Kendall τ-b rank agreement at H=100. The paper also introduces the MOSR metric to quantify the fraction of over-squashed edges missed by any curvature, and proves via the family G_{n,m}^c that every existing curvature definition (Ollivier-Ricci, Lin-Lu-Yau, Balanced Forman, Jost-Liu, AF-3/4, link resistance) returns a positive lower bound on the counterexample edge as m→∞.

## Results

The paper introduces MOSR (Missed Over-Squashing Ratio) to quantify the fraction of truly over-squashed edges that curvature fails to flag. Across 21 datasets and 3 GNN architectures (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE), Ollivier-Ricci curvature misses on average 27.1%–38.6% of over-squashed edges (MOSR₁₀), with worst cases exceeding 72% (Squirrel, SAGE). The proposed WAF3 curvature with f(x)=1/(1+x) reduces the average MOSR₁₀ to 3.6% (GCN) and MOSR₂₅ to 4.5%, a further >3% improvement over the best existing curvature (AF3 at 6.7%). The MinHash-based approximation achieves a 133.7× speedup (23.6 s on 5M edges) while maintaining ~95% Kendall Tau-b rank fidelity at H=100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks four curvature definitions on the MOSR metric (lower = fewer missed over-squashed edges). Ollivier-Ricci curvature (Ollivier, 2009), despite its O(|E|·d_max³) complexity, performs worst on average (MOSR₁₀ = 27.1% for GCN), missing up to 72.3% of over-squashed edges on Squirrel. Augmented Forman-3 (Forman, 2003) is the strongest existing baseline at 6.7% average MOSR₁₀, benefiting from its O(|E|·d_max) complexity and triangle-counting structure. Balanced Forman (Topping et al., 2021) sits in between at 7.8% average MOSR₁₀ but degrades more sharply at q=25 (12.4% vs. 7.9% for AF3). WAF3 (this paper) achieves the best average MOSR₁₀ of 3.6% and MOSR₂₅ of 4.5% for GCN, outperforming AF3 by over 3 percentage points on average and by up to ~10 points on datasets like Chameleon (6.5% vs. 11.5%) and Squirrel (3.9% vs. 13.7%). In downstream curvature-based graph learning (SDRF rewiring and GNRF end-to-end), WAF3 also outperforms both ORC and Balanced Forman, confirming that the improved detection translates to practical gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly challenges the central claim of Topping et al. (2021), who established that highly negative discrete curvature is a sufficient indicator of over-squashing but left its necessity unaddressed. It builds on Forman's (2003) Augmented Forman-3 curvature as the base for the proposed WAF3, and on Ioffe's (2010) weighted MinHash for the approximation algorithm. Conceptually, the paper departs from the prevailing 'curvature-as-gold-standard' framing by proving via constructive counterexamples that curvature is only sufficient, not necessary, for over-squashing, and by showing that curvature effectively identifies bridge edges between clusters rather than all bottlenecks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that Ollivier-Ricci curvature misses 30–40% of over-squashed edges (and that even the best existing curvature misses ~7–8%) undermines a widely relied-upon diagnostic tool in curvature-based rewiring and GNN design. WAF3 and the MinHash approximation provide practical alternatives, but the residual MOSR of 4–5% suggests that no purely local, first-order curvature measure can fully capture over-squashing, pointing toward the need for higher-order or model-aware metrics. Open questions include whether the within-cluster over-squashing identified here can be addressed by multi-hop curvature definitions, and whether the modest WAF3 improvement over AF3 translates into meaningful downstream gains in rewiring or end-to-end training beyond the small-scale experiments shown.
