## Problem

Existing MLLM-based multimodal embedding models are inherently discriminative: they encode the input and extract the last token's hidden state as the embedding without generating any new tokens, leaving them unable to leverage the powerful reasoning-driven generation paradigm that has driven breakthroughs in large reasoning models. This paper asks how to make a multimodal embedding model act as a generative one, so that intermediate reasoning and summarization can improve embedding quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263936/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts text queries, images, video clips, or interleaved text-image inputs and produces a normalized embedding vector via one of two modes. In discriminative mode, the embedding is read directly from the hidden state at a special <disc_emb> token in the prompt. In reasoning-driven generative mode, the model first autoregressively generates a chain-of-thought reasoning trace (up to 8,192 tokens) and a one-word or one-sentence summary, then reads the embedding from the final generated <gen_emb> token; the reasoning and summary are concatenated with the original input to form the representation. Both modes are available simultaneously from the same model without additional computation for the discriminative path.

## Method

UME-R1 introduces a two-stage training strategy on Qwen2-VL backbones. Stage 1 (cold-start SFT) augments 1.46M query-target pairs with CoT rationales and summaries generated by GLM-4.1V-Thinking, then trains with three simultaneous losses: an InfoNCE contrastive loss on the discriminative <disc_emb> token, a second InfoNCE contrastive loss on the generative <gen_emb> token (conditioned on the generated reasoning and summary), and a next-token prediction loss over the reasoning and summary tokens to preserve generative capability. Stage 2 applies GRPO reinforcement learning with a novel embedding-specific reward that multiplies a ranking component (fraction of positive-target similarities falling in the top-G of the combined positive+negative similarity set) by a similarity-gap component (mean positive similarity minus mean negative similarity), avoiding the zero-gradient problem of fixed-threshold rewards. The key architectural insight is that a single model template produces both embedding types, and experiments show they are complementary: an oracle that selects the better mode per instance yields 3.6–4.3 points above either mode alone, and repeated sampling at inference (pass@k) further improves coverage, revealing inference-time scaling potential for embeddings.

## Results

On MMEB-V2 (78 tasks across image, video, and visual document modalities), UME-R1-7B achieves an overall score of 64.5, outperforming the strongest baseline CAFe-7B (60.6) by 3.9 points and VLM2Vec-V2-2B (58.0) by 6.5 points while using only two-thirds of the training data. The 2B variant scores 60.1 overall, surpassing DUME-2B (52.7, same data, discriminative-only) by 7.4 points, with per-modality gains of +4.1 (image), +9.0 (video), and +11.1 (visual document). Under an oracle setting that selects the best of discriminative vs. generative embeddings per instance, UME-R1-7B reaches 68.1, indicating substantial remaining headroom. On MMEB-V1 (36 image tasks), UME-R1-7B scores 71.3 overall, beating LLaVE-7B (70.3) and UniME-7B (70.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UME-R1-7B (64.5) outperforms all 17 baselines on the MMEB-V2 overall score, with the largest margins over ColPali (+20.1), LamRA (+17.1), and VLM2Vec-7B (+12.2). Against the closest competitor CAFe-7B (60.6), UME-R1-7B leads by 3.9 points overall, with particularly strong gains on video (+5.1) and visual document (+3.2). The 2B model (60.1) surpasses VLM2Vec-V2-2B (58.0) by 2.1 points despite using only ~2/3 of the training data, and beats GME-2B (54.1) by 6.0 points. The most controlled comparison is against DUME (same data, discriminative-only training): UME-R1-2B improves by +7.4 overall (52.7→60.1), with the largest gap on visual documents (+11.1), confirming that the generative reasoning pathway is the primary source of gain. On MMEB-V1, UME-R1-7B (71.3) edges out LLaVE-7B (70.3) and UniME-7B (70.7), while the oracle variant reaches 74.2, surpassing all prior 7B models by at least 3.5 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM2Vec/VLM2Vec-V2 line of MLLM-based multimodal embedding models, which use contrastive learning (InfoNCE) to train discriminative embeddings from the last hidden state. The key departure is making the embedding process genuinely generative at inference: the model must autoregressively produce a chain-of-thought reasoning trace and a summary before emitting an embedding token, rather than simply encoding the input. For the RL stage, it adapts GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) to the embedding setting, where no ground-truth answer exists, by designing a novel reward that jointly considers positive-vs-negative ranking and similarity gap—something no prior embedding work has attempted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that reasoning-driven generative embeddings are not merely an alternative but a complementary mode to discriminative embeddings (oracle gains of 3.6–4.3 points over either alone), and that embeddings—unlike generation tasks—also benefit from inference-time scaling via repeated sampling (pass@k). Practical limitations include substantially higher inference cost (1.48 vs. 20 samples/s on CIRR), the absence of an adaptive router between the two embedding modes, and a small RL dataset (11K pairs) that likely leaves performance on the table. Open questions include whether adaptive mode selection, harder RL curricula, and dedicated inference-time scaling techniques can close the gap to the oracle bound.
