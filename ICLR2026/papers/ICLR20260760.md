## Problem

Despite rapid progress in LLM mathematical reasoning, there is no large-scale, open dataset of human-evaluated LLM-generated proofs, leaving three key questions unresolved: the magnitude of the gap between natural-language and formal proof generation, the relationship between final-answer accuracy and full proof correctness, and the effectiveness of best-of-n selection for proof quality. Existing proof benchmarks are small, rely on outdated models, contain few correct proofs, and are not publicly available, hindering both training and rigorous analysis.

## Input / Output

Each OPC sample pairs a mathematical competition problem (from USAMO, IMO Shortlist, Putnam, and 15+ other sources) with a full natural-language proof generated by one of six SOTA LLMs, annotated with a binary correct/incorrect label, a written justification, and optional sentence-level error annotations. For the trained judge model (OPC-R1-8B), the input is the problem statement plus the generated proof, and the output is a single binary verdict (\boxed{correct} or \boxed{incorrect}) after a chain-of-thought analysis. The dataset spans 5,062 proofs over 1,010 problems, with ~84% at high-school level and ~16% at undergraduate level, and is split into four subsets (MathArena, PutnamBench, Best-of-n, Generic) serving distinct evaluation and training purposes.

## Method

The OPC is constructed through a four-week pipeline involving 13 expert judges (former IMO participants or national finalists) who grade anonymized proofs via a custom web interface augmented with O4-MINI-generated issue summaries that flag logical gaps without issuing a verdict. Proofs are generated by six models (O4-MINI, O3, GEMINI-2.5-PRO, GROK-3-MINI, QWEN3-235B-A22B, DeepSeek-R1) at 64K-token max length, with ~10% double-graded to measure inter-judge agreement (90.4%, implying ~5% individual error rate). To produce an open judge model, the authors fine-tune R1-QWEN3-8B using GRPO reinforcement learning (10 rollouts, batch size 16, lr 1e-6, 14K-token responses) on 1,733 human-labeled proofs, achieving 88.1% majority-vote accuracy that matches GEMINI-2.5-PRO and approaches GPT-5's 90.8%. For best-of-n experiments, O4-MINI generates eight proofs per problem and four selection strategies are compared, with a Bradley-Terry-based Swiss round-robin ranking outperforming discrete/continuous scoring by ~10% and standard pass@n by ~21%.

## Results

On the OPC proof-judging task (293 test proofs, 40% average correctness), GPT-5 achieves the highest accuracy at 89.3% pass@1 and 90.8% maj@5, approaching the 90.4% human baseline. The fine-tuned OPC-R1-8B model reaches 83.8% pass@1 and 88.1% maj@5, matching GEMINI-2.5-PRO's majority-voting performance and outperforming its base model (R1-QWEN3-8B) by 17 percentage points. On proof generation, GEMINI-2.5-PRO leads with the highest average correctness, while on PutnamBench it achieves ~83% informal accuracy versus <19% for the best formal model (GÖDEL-PROVER-V2). Best-of-n selection via Rank (Swiss) improves O4-MINI's accuracy by 17% over single-pass generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPC-R1-8B (83.8% pass@1, 88.1% maj@5) trails GPT-5 (89.3%, 90.8%) by roughly 5–6 points but ties GEMINI-2.5-PRO on maj@5 (88.1%) while costing nothing in inference. Compared to its base model R1-QWEN3-8B (70.7%, 71.3%), the OPC fine-tune yields a 13-point gain in pass@1 and 17 points in maj@5, demonstrating the dataset's training value. Against o4-MINI (83.8%, 85.3%), OPC-R1-8B ties on pass@1 and wins by 2.8 points on maj@5. On proof generation, GEMINI-2.5-PRO slightly outperforms O4-MINI, while open-source models QWEN3-235B-A22B and R1 underperform significantly. In the formal-vs-informal comparison on PutnamBench, GEMINI-2.5-PRO (~83%) outperforms GÖDEL-PROVER-V2 (<19%) by a factor of over 4×. On the MathArena subset, O3 suffers the largest drop between final-answer accuracy and proof correctness (only 59.5% of correct answers contain a valid proof), whereas GEMINI-2.5-PRO loses only ~8%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-as-a-judge paradigm (Zheng et al., 2023; Gu et al., 2024) to the specific domain of mathematical proof evaluation, building on prior but limited proof-evaluation efforts (Petrov et al., 2025; Mahdavi et al., 2025; Frieder et al., 2023) that were small-scale, used outdated models, or were not open-sourced. It repurposes GRPO-based reinforcement learning (Shao et al., 2024) to train a compact judge model, and adapts pairwise ranking strategies from Liu et al. (2025) for best-of-n proof selection. By anchoring its analysis to existing benchmarks (PutnamBench, MathArena), the paper enables direct comparison between informal and formal proof generation (GOEDEL-PROVER-V2) without requiring new problem construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OPC is the first large-scale, open-sourced, human-validated dataset of LLM-generated proofs from prestigious competitions, filling a critical gap that has hindered systematic study of proof generation. It resolves three open questions: the substantial informal-over-formal gap (4×), the significant divergence between final-answer accuracy and proof correctness (up to 30% drop for O3), and the strong gains from ranking-based best-of-n selection (26%→47%). Limitations include the dataset's focus on high-school-level problems (≈84%), potential contamination from publicly available competition problems, and the absence of research-level mathematics. The 8B judge model matching GEMINI-2.5-PRO on proof evaluation offers a practical, low-cost tool for the community.
