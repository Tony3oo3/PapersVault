## Problem

Multi-subject personalized generation suffers from identity blending and attribute leakage because existing methods lack explicit optimization of diffusion representations for both precise reference-to-target alignment and inter-subject feature disentanglement. This shortcoming becomes severe beyond 3–4 subjects, where compounding feature interference causes most methods to degrade substantially, yet no prior work provides a mechanism to simultaneously enforce point-wise semantic correspondence and spatial exclusivity across multiple references.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262142/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes K reference images (each depicting a distinct subject) and a text prompt as input, and produces a single generated image that preserves the identity of all reference subjects simultaneously. Reference images are encoded via a VAE into latent tokens, concatenated into a unified representation, and processed through a LoRA-augmented branch; a modified RoPE with distinct frequency bases is applied to spatially separate reference and target latents. A key constraint is correspondence disjointness: each target latent token is associated with at most one reference image, preventing ambiguous supervision when multiple references compete for the same spatial region.

## Method

MOSAIC builds on FLUX-1.0-DEV (a DiT-based flow-matching model) with a LoRA-augmented reference branch and introduces two complementary attention-level losses supervised by a new dataset, SemAlign-MS (1.2M pairs with validated fine-grained semantic point correspondences). The Semantic Correspondence Attention Loss (SCAL) applies cross-entropy supervision over the reference-to-target attention sub-matrix, forcing each reference token at a known correspondence point to attend to its designated target latent position across selected DiT blocks. The Multi-Reference Disentanglement Loss (MDL) computes the symmetric KL divergence between aggregated attention distributions of different references and maximizes this distance, pushing each subject's attention pattern into an orthogonal subspace to prevent cross-subject feature interference. Both losses are applied primarily to early attention blocks (where global semantic alignment is established) and combined with the standard flow-matching loss, with the full model trained for 100K steps at LoRA rank 128.

## Results

On XVerseBench, MOSAIC achieves the highest overall score of 76.04, surpassing XVerse (73.40) and DreamO (69.25), with ID-Sim of 81.98 (single-subject) and 69.90 (multi-subject). On DreamBench, MOSAIC leads with 84.30 CLIP-I and 77.40 DINO in single-subject, and 76.30 CLIP-I and 56.83 DINO in multi-subject, outperforming the second-best by ~3.0 points on CLIP-I in the multi-subject setting. Under the Face-Diffuser evaluation protocol, MOSAIC achieves 0.785 IP (single) and 0.712 IP (multi), substantially exceeding Face-Diffuser (0.708/0.594). Notably, MOSAIC maintains high fidelity with 4+ reference subjects where all compared methods degrade significantly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOSAIC outperforms all 11 compared baselines across both DreamBench and XVerseBench in the multi-subject setting, where the gap is most pronounced: it leads XVerse by 2.64 points in overall XVerseBench score and by ~3.0 CLIP-I on DreamBench. Against DreamO (the strongest single-subject competitor), MOSAIC wins on ID-Sim (81.98 vs 75.48), IP-Sim (80.92 vs 70.84), and AES (60.77 vs 54.57) in single-subject, while DreamO retains a marginal DPG edge (96.93 vs 96.55). In multi-subject, MOSAIC surpasses DreamO by 19.66 points on ID-Sim (69.90 vs 50.24) and 8.64 on IP-Sim (74.27 vs 64.63). Against the identity-preserving baselines under the Face-Diffuser protocol, MOSAIC achieves 0.712 multi-subject IP versus Face-Diffuser's 0.594 and FastComposer's 0.465, a gap of 0.118 and 0.247 respectively. Older methods (DreamBooth, BLIP-Diffusion, SSR-Encoder) are substantially outperformed, with MOSAIC exceeding DreamBooth by 4.0 CLIP-I in single-subject and 3.7 in multi-subject. The key differentiator is scalability: while DreamO does not support 3+ references and XVerse/UNO degrade noticeably at 4+ subjects, MOSAIC maintains consistent identity fidelity across all subject counts tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOSAIC builds directly on OmniControl's LoRA-augmented dual-branch architecture for reference-target attention in diffusion transformers, but departs from the broader multi-subject generation lineage (MS-Diffusion, DreamO, XVerse, TFCustom) by replacing their implicit or layout-based subject binding with explicit point-wise semantic correspondence supervision. The method repurposes visual correspondence tools (DIFT, GeoAware-SC) that were previously used only for correspondence estimation, turning them into a training signal for attention alignment. The accompanying SemAlign-MS dataset is the first to provide dense, validated semantic point correspondences across multi-reference-target triplets, filling a gap that prior multi-subject methods simply did not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in extending reliable multi-subject generation beyond the 3-subject ceiling that plagues existing methods, with a plug-and-play loss design validated across both DiT (FLUX) and UNet (SDXL) backbones. The point-wise constraint philosophy—anchoring only key semantic features rather than constraining the full latent—offers a principled explanation for why MOSAIC maintains generation diversity (highest LPIPS) while improving fidelity, a trade-off that global alignment methods struggle with. Open questions include whether the 29% training overhead can be reduced via attention compression, and whether the approach generalizes to video or 3D generation where correspondence is even harder to establish. The paper acknowledges failure on extreme spatial complexity (nested containment, strict geometric constraints), suggesting the correspondence framework still lacks a robust spatial-reasoning component.
