## Problem

Existing speculative decoding draft models are trained to maximize token-level likelihood along a single greedy path, yet at decoding time a tree-based policy re-ranks and verifies multiple branches. This draft policy misalignment causes 19–34% of greedy paths to be pruned and limits the accepted path to matching the greedy one only 36–49% of the time, wasting training signal and capping achievable speedups.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263819/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a text prefix x_{1:t} and target-model hidden states (following the EAGLE architecture), the draft model constructs a depth-7 tree with 60 total draft tokens via layer-wise top-k=10 expansion and global top-g=60 leaf pruning. The training output is an updated draft model whose tree-level reward is the log-sum-exp (η=1) of per-branch expected acceptance lengths computed under the target model, making the objective sampling-free while directly reflecting decoding-time utility.

## Method

GTO introduces two components. First, a Draft Tree Reward r_t = (1/η) log Σ exp(η L_{t,i}) that aggregates per-branch expected acceptance lengths via a smooth maximum, directly measuring how many tokens the target model will accept from the tree. Second, a group-based PPO-style optimizer: adjacent positions in a sequence form groups of size m∈[4,8]; within each group, tree rewards are debiased by subtracting a frozen reference model's reward, standardized to group-normalized advantages, and used in a clipped likelihood-ratio surrogate computed along the longest accepted sequence. A theoretical result proves that increasing r_t strictly increases expected acceptance length for T>0 and forces an increase in max_i L_{t,i} for T=0. The overall loss combines this tree-level objective with the standard EAGLE-3 token-level cross-entropy, and the two-phase procedure (optional warmup + group-based fine-tuning) can be applied on top of any pretrained drafter without architectural changes.

## Results

GTO consistently outperforms all baselines across MT-Bench, HumanEval, and GSM8K at both T=0 and T=1. On LLaMA-3.1-Instruct-8B at T=0, GTO achieves an average speedup ratio (SR) of 3.73× and acceptance length (τ) of 6.52 tokens, compared to EAGLE-3's 3.46× and 6.07 tokens. Across all five evaluated LLMs, GTO improves average acceptance length by 7.4% and yields an additional 7.7% speedup over EAGLE-3 at T=0 (5.6% at T=1). Gains are most pronounced on structured tasks: HumanEval shows up to 13.3% SR improvement and GSM8K up to 11.1% over EAGLE-3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GTO outperforms the prior state-of-the-art EAGLE-3 by 7.7% average SR at T=0 and 5.6% at T=1 across four models, with the largest per-task gains on HumanEval (up to +13.3% SR for LLaMA-3.1-8B) and GSM8K (up to +11.1% for DeepSeek-R1-8B). Against GRIFFIN, GTO improves average SR by roughly 13–14% at T=0 (e.g., 3.73 vs. 3.28 on LLaMA-3.1-8B), and against HASS by a similar margin. Older tree-based methods (EAGLE-2, EAGLE) and non-tree methods (Medusa, Hydra, PLD, Lookahead, SPS) are all substantially outperformed, with GTO achieving 3.7–5.6× SR versus 1.5–4.1× for those baselines. GTO also demonstrates compatibility with alternative draft initializations: fine-tuning GRIFFIN or HASS draft models with GTO yields 7.8–8.3% SR gains over their respective baselines at T=0, confirming the method is not tied to EAGLE-3's training recipe. A continual-training ablation (200 extra A100 GPU-hours on EAGLE-3) shows only ~1% improvement, confirming GTO's gains are algorithmic rather than compute-driven.

## Relation to Prior Work

GTO extends the EAGLE family of draft-model training (EAGLE-2's tree construction policy, EAGLE-3's token-level loss and training-time rollouts) by replacing the single greedy-path objective with a tree-level reward that mirrors the actual decoding procedure. The optimization scheme borrows the group-standardized advantage and PPO-style clipped surrogate from GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), adapted to the deterministic, position-adjacent structure of draft trees. Unlike HASS and GRIFFIN, which address feature- and token-level mismatches within the greedy-path paradigm, GTO targets the structural misalignment between training (single path) and decoding (multi-branch tree with re-ranking and pruning).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By directly optimizing expected acceptance length on the full draft tree, GTO closes a gap that all prior draft-model training methods share, yielding a consistent ~7–8% speedup over EAGLE-3 across five LLMs and three task domains. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) that increasing the tree reward provably increases acceptance length provides a clean link between the training objective and decoding performance. Limitations include the additional training compute (200–900 GPU-hours depending on model size) and the dependence on a strong pretrained draft model for the reference; open questions include whether the group-based formulation extends to non-EAGLE tree topologies or to multi-draft-model cascades.
