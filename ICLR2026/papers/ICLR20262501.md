## Problem

Existing token reduction methods for Large Vision-Language Models focus exclusively on pruning or compressing image tokens before or within the LLM stage, overlooking the image encoder—a major computational bottleneck that also constitutes the largest source of input tokens fed into the LLM. This narrow focus prevents true end-to-end acceleration, as the encoder's own inference cost and its token contribution to the LLM remain unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262501/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard LVLM pipelines where RGB images or video frames are patchified into token sequences and concatenated with text instructions. Token merging is applied at intermediate layers of both the image encoder and the LLM, reducing the visual token count by 66.7%–95% (configurable via per-block reduction counts R_v, R_t and block selections B_v, B_t). The output is a standard text response; the method is training-free and compatible with Flash-Attention by extracting cumulative attention weights rather than full attention matrices.

## Method

iLLaVA introduces a two-stage token merging strategy that jointly accelerates the image encoder and the LLM. In the encoder, a merging module is inserted between the Multi-Head Attention and Feed-Forward Network of selected blocks (default: layers 5, 9, 13); in the LLM, merging is applied after specific transformer blocks (default: layers 2, 8, 14), with 40% of total token reduction allocated to the encoder and 60% to the LLM. The merging strategy uses averaged attention scores to select informative tokens (top-K most attended) and recycled tokens (next most attended, which aggregate features from discarded tokens via a weighted sum over their M nearest neighbors based on normalized attention). This recycling mechanism preserves potentially useful information from pruned tokens, avoiding the performance degradation seen with pure pruning. The approach is entirely training-free, requires negligible extra computation (O(R_v·B_v + R_t·B_t)), and achieves up to 2× throughput and 4× prefilling time reduction while retaining >95% accuracy.

## Results

On image understanding benchmarks (Qwen2.5-VL 7B), iLLaVA retains 99.2% of vanilla accuracy (64.8 avg) at 66.7% token reduction, 97.6% (64.3) at 77.8%, and 95.2% (62.8) at 88.9%, consistently outperforming all baselines at every reduction ratio. On video benchmarks, iLLaVA achieves 97.8% retention at 90% token reduction and 96.8% at 95%, surpassing VisionZip by 1.2% and 1.7% respectively. In terms of efficiency, iLLaVA delivers a 2.12× throughput boost, 1.59× memory reduction, and 4.46× prefilling time reduction compared to the vanilla model, while enabling InternVL-2.5 26B to outperform InternVL-2.5 8B in both accuracy (+4.2% MMMU, +2.2% MMStar) and throughput.

## Baselines & Comparisons

iLLaVA outperforms all four primary baselines (VisionZip, SparseVLM, FasterVLM, PyramidDrop) across every token reduction ratio on both image and video benchmarks. At the most aggressive 88.9% image token reduction, iLLaVA leads VisionZip by 1.4 points (62.8 vs. 61.4 avg), FasterVLM by 1.8 points, SparseVLM by 2.8 points, and PyramidDrop by 5.6 points. On video tasks at 95% reduction, iLLaVA surpasses VisionZip by 1.7 points (66.9 vs. 65.5 avg) and PyramidDrop by 2.0 points. Among additional baselines in Table 6, iLLaVA beats the strongest token-merging method AIM by 0.5 points at 77.8% and 1.6 points at 88.9%, and outperforms the best pruning method FEATHER by 0.9 and 1.8 points respectively. The two-stage encoder+LLM design (Table 4) further confirms that adding encoder-side merging on top of LLM-only pruning yields +0.7% accuracy, 2.12× throughput, and 0.64× memory relative to LLM-only reduction, validating the core architectural contribution over single-stage approaches like FastV, AdaFV, and DivPrune.

## Relation to Prior Work

iLLaVA extends the line of training-free token reduction methods for LVLMs (FastV, SparseVLM, FasterVLM, VisionZip, PyramidDrop) by shifting the locus of compression from the LLM alone to both the image encoder and the LLM in a coordinated two-stage design. The token merging mechanism draws on ToMe (Bolya et al., 2022) for ViT and LLaVA-PruMerge (Shang et al., 2024) for LVLMs, but adapts it with an attention-score-based informative/recycled token split that is applied at intermediate encoder blocks rather than only at the encoder-LLM boundary. The key conceptual departure is the empirical observation that the image encoder consumes up to 45% of total inference time and is the dominant source of input tokens to the LLM, making encoder-stage reduction a compounding efficiency lever that prior LLM-only methods overlook.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a training-free, plug-and-play method that delivers ~2× throughput and ~4× prefilling-time reduction while retaining >95% accuracy, and that enables a 26B model to match or beat an 8B model in both accuracy and speed — a result with direct implications for deploying large multimodal models on constrained hardware. The 40/60 encoder-to-LLM reduction allocation finding provides actionable guidance for practitioners tuning efficiency-accuracy tradeoffs. However, the paper acknowledges notable degradation on document-heavy benchmarks (DocVQA, InfoVQA, ChartQA) where fine-grained spatial detail is critical, and the optimal allocation ratio may not transfer across architectures or input types. Open questions include whether learned (rather than attention-score-based) selection criteria could push the accuracy ceiling higher at extreme reduction ratios, and whether the two-stage design generalizes to audio or 3D modalities.
