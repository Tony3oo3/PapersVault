## Problem

Task arithmetic with task vectors offers a computationally efficient alternative to full fine-tuning by editing model weights through simple arithmetic, yet its impact on group fairness has remained largely unexplored. Specifically, it is unclear whether scaling or merging subgroup-specific task vectors can reduce disparities (measured by Demographic Parity Difference and Equalized Odds Difference) without sacrificing accuracy, and how these effects compare to standard baselines like full fine-tuning and LoRA.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text snippets (tweet-length hate-speech examples from Berkeley D-Lab, online comments from Civil Comments binarized at a 0.5 toxicity threshold) and face images (UTKFace, resized to 224×224 and patchified to 16×16 for ViT-Base/16), producing a single binary class label per input (hate speech, toxic, or older-than-30). Fairness is evaluated at the subgroup level: for each sensitive attribute (gender with 7 subgroups, race with 8 subgroups), the paper reports per-subgroup accuracy, macro-averaged and worst-group DPD, and worst-group EOD, enabling fine-grained disparity auditing that goes beyond a single global accuracy number.

## Method

The core mechanism is a single global scaling coefficient λ applied uniformly to merged subgroup task vectors: each subgroup is fine-tuned independently to produce a task vector Δθ_g = θ_g − θ_0, and the merged model is θ(λ) = θ_0 + λ Σ_g Δθ_g. A second variant injects a worst-performing subgroup's task vector into an already fine-tuned model (θ_new = θ_SFT + λ(θ_worst − θ_0)), enabling targeted fairness adjustments. The paper derives an upper bound showing DPD(θ(λ)) ≤ 2L Σ_g |λ_g − 1|‖Δθ_g‖_2 and a corresponding EOD bound, establishing that deviations of λ from the balanced point (λ_g = 1) drive disparities in proportion to subgroup task-vector norms. Empirically, sweeping λ from 0 to 1 traces a smooth fairness-utility frontier where λ ≳ 0.3 maintains competitive accuracy while substantially reducing DPD and EOD relative to both FFT and LoRA, and subgroup-targeted injection produces heterogeneous, group-specific shifts that standard training pipelines do not expose.

## Results

On Civil Comments (DistilBERT, Gender), task addition achieves 0.9395 accuracy (vs. SFT 0.9457–0.9476, LoRA 0.9447–0.9453) while reducing worst-case DPD to 0.0454 (vs. SFT 0.0887–0.1101) and worst-case EOD to 0.3358 (vs. SFT 0.6157–0.6433, LoRA 0.5024–0.5084). On UTKFace (Race, ViT-Base/16), task addition recovers accuracy to 0.7227–0.7384 (vs. LoRA's 0.6389–0.6572) while maintaining worst-DPD of 0.2390–0.2924 and worst-EOD of 0.1700–0.2337, close to LoRA's fairness levels. On LLaMA-2 (Berkeley D-Lab, Race), task addition achieves 0.7972–0.8724 accuracy with worst-DPD 0.0000–0.0265 and worst-EOD 0.0000–0.0730, competitive with both SFT and LoRA. Across all settings, sweeping the scalar λ from 0.0 to 1.0 traces a smooth fairness–accuracy frontier, with λ ≳ 0.3 typically yielding the best balance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA, task addition consistently wins on fairness while matching or recovering accuracy. On Civil Comments (Qwen-2.5-0.5B, Gender), LoRA suffers a severe accuracy drop (0.774–0.790 vs. SFT's 0.884–0.886) and poor fairness (worst-DPD 0.210–0.251), whereas task addition recovers accuracy to 0.810–0.820 and halves worst-DPD to 0.100–0.103. On UTKFace (Race), LoRA's accuracy collapses to 0.6389–0.6572 (vs. SFT's 0.8463–0.8599), while task addition recovers to 0.7227–0.7384 with comparable worst-DPD (0.2390–0.2924 vs. LoRA's 0.2298–0.2789). Against SFT/FFT, task addition trades a small accuracy cost (~0.5–1 pp) for substantially better fairness: on Civil Comments (DistilBERT, Gender), worst-EOD drops from 0.6157–0.6433 (SFT) to 0.3358 (task addition), a ~45% reduction; on Civil Comments (DistilBERT, Race), worst-EOD drops from 0.2568–0.3544 to 0.1289, a ~58% reduction. On LLaMA-2 (Berkeley D-Lab, Race), all three methods are closely competitive, with task addition achieving the lowest worst-DPD (0.0000–0.0265 vs. SFT's 0.0000–0.0345 and LoRA's 0.0000–0.0459). Overall, task addition dominates LoRA on the accuracy–fairness Pareto front in most settings and offers a clear fairness advantage over SFT at minimal accuracy cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the task arithmetic framework of Ilharco et al. (2023) by introducing a systematic group-fairness evaluation that had not previously been applied to task-vector-based model editing. It builds directly on the fairness evaluation protocol of Ding et al. (2024), who studied LoRA's impact on subgroup disparities, and positions task arithmetic as a post-hoc, training-free alternative to the training-time fairness objectives explored in FairLoRA (Sukumaran et al., 2024) and multi-objective PEFT (Wang & Demberg, 2024). The theoretical contribution—upper bounds linking task-vector scaling to DPD and EOD—connects the empirical observations to a group-weighted ERM interpretation, bridging the task-merging literature (Zhang et al., 2024) with the classical impossibility results on group fairness (Kleinberg et al., 2017).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study showing that a single interpretable scalar (λ) in task arithmetic can trace a smooth fairness-accuracy frontier, offering practitioners a training-free knob to reduce demographic disparities without retraining. The subgroup-specific heterogeneity (e.g., Men task vectors improve fairness while Native American vectors worsen it) reveals that task-vector injection is not a universal fairness fix, a nuance absent from prior task-arithmetic work. Key limitations include restriction to binary classification with a single global λ, evaluation only on 0.5–7B open-weight models, and single sensitive attributes; extending to multi-label, generative, or intersectional settings, and to larger or API-only models, remains open.
