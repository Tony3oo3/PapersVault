## Problem

GRPO and its REINFORCE-based variants are conventionally treated as on-policy algorithms whose off-policy tolerance is limited to small importance-sampling corrections, yet practical LLM-RL pipelines inevitably produce stale or heterogeneous training data. No principled off-policy interpretation existed for the group-relative REINFORCE update, leaving the roles of importance sampling, clipping, and data-weighting heuristics poorly understood and forcing ad-hoc algorithm design for truly off-policy settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262250/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt x, a group of K responses {y_i} generated by an arbitrary (possibly stale) behavior policy, and scalar response-level rewards {r_i}; crucially, no assumption is made about the sampling distribution of the responses. The output is a single policy-gradient update vector g that adjusts the autoregressive LLM's parameters θ, interpretable as one gradient step of a KL-regularized surrogate objective anchored at the previous policy θ_t. The derivation extends naturally from single-step (response-level) to multi-step (trajectory-level) settings by replacing response rewards and log-probabilities with their trajectory-level counterparts.

## Method

The paper derives group-relative REINFORCE from first principles by (1) defining a KL-regularized surrogate objective whose optimal policy satisfies a pairwise log-ratio consistency condition, (2) constructing a finite-sample mean-squared surrogate loss that enforces this condition across K responses, and (3) showing that one gradient step of this loss at θ_t recovers exactly the standard group-relative REINFORCE update with the within-group mean reward as baseline—without any on-policy sampling assumption. This off-policy lens yields two augmentation principles: regularize the update (clipping, KL penalty) and actively shape the data distribution (pairwise or pointwise reweighting, sample dropping). Applying these principles, the paper shows that GRPO's effectiveness in off-policy settings stems from clipping as a regularizer rather than from importance sampling (wider clipping ranges accelerate training without instability), reinterprets Kimi's OPMD and Meta's AsymRE as REINFORCE loss plus a regularization term, and provides theoretical justification for heuristic data-weighting strategies such as dropping excess negatives or up-weighting high-reward rollouts.

## Results

The paper's key empirical finding is that removing importance sampling from GRPO (REC-ONESIDE-NOIS) yields nearly identical training reward and evaluation accuracy to full GRPO across GSM8k (Qwen2.5-1.5B), ToolACE (Llama-3.2-3B), and other settings, while vanilla REINFORCE without clipping collapses. Enlarging the clipping range from the conventional (ε_low, ε_high) = (0.2, 0.2) to (0.6, 2.0) accelerates convergence without sacrificing stability in all off-policy settings except the extreme offline stress-test. On Guru-Math with Qwen2.5-7B, the proposed RED-WEIGHT data-weighting method achieves higher training rewards than GRPO at similar KL distance to the initial policy, and on MATH with Llama-3.1-8B it achieves higher rewards with lower KL divergence and more stable entropy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), the paper demonstrates that its token-wise importance-sampling component is non-essential: REC-ONESIDE-NOIS (clipping only, no IS) matches GRPO's performance on GSM8k and ToolACE, while the wider clipping range (0.6, 2.0) outperforms GRPO's default (0.2, 0.2) in convergence speed. Against OPMD (Kimi-Team, 2025b) and AsymRE (Arnal et al., 2025), the paper does not claim to outperform them but reinterprets both as REINFORCE loss plus a regularization term (mean-squared log-probability penalty for OPMD; KL divergence to the old policy for AsymRE), showing they are valid instantiations of the proposed 'regularize the update' principle and validating their efficacy in sync_offset > 0 scenarios beyond their original analysis. Against GSPO (Zheng et al., 2025), preliminary experiments on Guru-Math with Qwen3-30B-A3B show that REC-GSPO-NoIS (sequence-level clipping without sequence-level IS) largely overlaps with full GSPO, supporting the hypothesis that sequence-level importance sampling is also non-essential. Overall, the paper's contribution is diagnostic rather than a single new SOTA method: it re-attributes the success of existing algorithms to clipping/regularization rather than importance sampling, and shows that data-weighting (RED-WEIGHT, RED-DROP) provides a complementary axis of improvement over GRPO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the KL-regularized policy optimization lineage (Ziebart et al., 2008; Nachum et al., 2017; Rafailov et al., 2023) and the surrogate-loss derivations of REBEL (Gao et al., 2024) and CoPG (Flet-Berliac et al., 2024), but departs by deriving group-relative REINFORCE from first principles without any on-policy data assumption, yielding a native off-policy interpretation. It repositions GRPO's clipping as a regularization mechanism rather than a trust-region constraint for importance sampling, and reinterprets Kimi's OPMD and Meta's AsymRE as REINFORCE loss plus a regularization term, offering a unified lens that differs substantially from the rationales in their original papers. The work also connects to the broader off-policy LLM-RL literature (GSPO, CISPO, decoupled PPO, tapered off-policy REINFORCE) by providing a common theoretical substrate under which these methods can be compared.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that importance sampling is non-essential in GRPO and that widening the clipping range to (0.6, 2.0) accelerates convergence without sacrificing stability is immediately actionable for practitioners running off-policy LLM-RL pipelines, potentially reducing the need for frequent model synchronization. The unified framework also provides theoretical justification for heuristic data-weighting strategies (sample dropping, advantage-based reweighting) that previously lacked principled grounding. Key limitations include the absence of formal convergence or policy-improvement guarantees, restriction to response/trajectory-level rewards with multiple rollouts per query, and experiments limited to data from older policy versions rather than truly arbitrary off-policy sources. Open questions include whether the off-policy interpretation extends to step-level rewards, single-rollout settings, and whether distributional assumptions can yield provable guarantees.
