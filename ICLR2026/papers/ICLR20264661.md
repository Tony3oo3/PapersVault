## Problem

Separable neural networks (SepNNs) are widely used in implicit neural representations and physics-informed neural networks for their O(nD) computational efficiency on grid inputs, yet their theoretical foundations remain unexplored. Specifically, no universal approximation theorem existed for multivariate SepNNs, their neural tangent kernel (NTK) regimes and spectral bias were uncharacterized, and no efficient method existed to alleviate the spectral bias inherent in their training dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method takes D-dimensional grid coordinate points (x₁,...,x_D) with n samples per dimension (n^D total) and corresponding target function values as input. The key structural constraint is that inputs must lie on a separable grid so that each factor MLP is queried only n times on its 1D coordinate rather than n^D times on the full D-dimensional point, yielding O(nD) per-epoch complexity. Outputs are learned multivariate function values, instantiated as pixel intensities for 2D image INRs, volumetric occupancy for 3D surface INRs, or PDE solution fields for PINNs.

## Method

The paper makes three theoretical and algorithmic contributions. First, it proves a universal approximation theorem for CP, TT, and Tucker SepNNs by combining the Stone-Weierstrass theorem (to show the separable function class is dense in C(𝒳)) with the classical universal approximation theorem (to approximate each univariate factor by an MLP). Second, it derives NTK regimes: under infinite width and infinite rank the NTK converges almost surely to a deterministic kernel, while under infinite width and fixed rank it converges in distribution to a random kernel defined by Gaussian processes, with the eigenvalue decay of the NTK matrix characterizing the spectral bias. Third, it proposes SepPGD, which constructs D small n×n preconditioning matrices (one per factor MLP) via pseudo-NTK eigenvalue modulation and applies them through mode-d tensor products, achieving O(nD) per-iteration complexity versus O(n^D) for standard NTK-based preconditioning. The key insight is that the full n^D×n^D preconditioner decomposes as a Kronecker sum of factor-level preconditioners (Lemma 2), so the large matrix-vector product is replaced by D smaller matrix products in O(n)-dimensional spaces.

## Results

On image representation (INR) with Sin activation, SepPGD improves PSNR from 26.48 dB (SepNN baseline) to 33.30 dB, a gain of ~6.8 dB. With Fourier feature encoding + ReLU, the improvement is even larger: 30.89 → 40.49 dB. On the 3D diffusion PDE (PINN), SepPGD reduces test MSE to 0.042 compared to the standard separable PINN baseline. On non-grid function approximation, SepPGD achieves MSE of 0.015/0.021/0.062 on three test functions, outperforming the SepNN baseline (0.020/0.030/0.075) and being competitive with the MSK baseline (0.014/0.024/0.058).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the NTK-based preconditioning of Geifman et al. (2024) and the inductive gradient adjustment (MSK) of Shi et al. (2025), SepPGD achieves comparable or better reconstruction quality while reducing the preconditioner application complexity from O(n^D) to O(nD). On non-grid data (Table 9), SepPGD and MSK are roughly competitive: MSK edges out SepPGD on f₁ (MSE 0.014 vs 0.015) and f₃ (0.058 vs 0.062), while SepPGD wins on f₂ (0.021 vs 0.024); both substantially outperform the unpreconditioned SepNN. On grid-based tasks (the primary setting for INRs and PINNs), SepPGD's separable structure gives it a clear efficiency advantage over MSK, which requires constructing and applying a full n^D × n^D preconditioning matrix. Against the standard separable PINN of Cho et al. (2023), SepPGD further accelerates convergence on 3D PDEs (diffusion, Klein-Gordon, Helmholtz) as shown in Figure 4, reducing test MSE at equal iteration counts. The paper also demonstrates that SepPGD is robust to hyperparameter choices (rank R, modulation parameter k, update frequency) and works across activation functions, whereas MSK's advantage diminishes under noise (Table 7: at σ=0.09, SepPGD degrades to 25.06 dB vs SepNN's 25.29 dB, while MSK is not tested in this regime).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the separable neural network (SepNN) architecture introduced by Liang et al. (CoordX, ICLR 2022) and Cho et al. (Separable PINN, NeurIPS 2023) by providing the first comprehensive theoretical treatment: a universal approximation theorem covering CP, TT, and Tucker decompositions (generalizing the bivariate result of Cho et al.), and the first NTK regime analysis for SepNNs. The proposed SepPGD method adapts the NTK-spectrum-modulation preconditioning framework of Geifman et al. (TMLR 2024) and Shi et al. (ICML 2025) to the separable structure, exploiting Kronecker-product factorization to reduce preconditioner application from O(n^D) to O(nD). Conceptually, the work bridges tensor decomposition theory (Stone-Weierstrass density arguments) with modern NTK-based optimization theory, a connection not previously explored for separable architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SepNNs are increasingly adopted in INRs, PINNs, and scientific computing (geophysics, wireless, bioinformatics), yet lacked foundational theory on representation capacity and training dynamics; this paper fills that gap and provides a practical optimization tool (SepPGD) with provable spectral-bias alleviation at O(nD) cost. Key open questions remain: convergence and generalization guarantees under fixed (finite) rank, where the NTK is random rather than deterministic; extension of the NTK analysis to multi-layer factor networks and non-grid inputs; and whether the eigenvalue-modulation strategy interacts beneficially with generalization (implicit regularization). The paper also notes that explicit approximation error rates in terms of rank/width are not yet derived, leaving practitioners without theoretical guidance for rank selection.
