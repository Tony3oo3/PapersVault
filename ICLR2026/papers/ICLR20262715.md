## Problem

Tree-RAG organizes retrieved knowledge in hierarchical entity trees to capture multi-level relationships, but its BFS-based entity lookup becomes a severe bottleneck as tree depth and dataset size grow, with retrieval consuming 10–72% of total response time. No existing data structure simultaneously supports O(1) membership queries, dynamic insertion/deletion, and efficient multi-location retrieval across a forest of entity trees, leaving a gap between the structural richness of tree-based retrieval and practical inference speed.

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural-language user query, a pre-built entity forest (hierarchical trees of entities with parent-child edges extracted via SpaCy and dependency parsing), and a Cuckoo Filter index where each bucket holds up to 4 entries of 12-bit fingerprints paired with a temperature counter and a head pointer into a block linked list. The output is an LLM-generated response conditioned on an augmented prompt that fuses the query with the retrieved entity's multi-level ancestor and descendant nodes across all trees in which it appears. The Cuckoo Filter is implemented in C++ for performance while the surrounding RAG pipeline runs in Python.

## Method

CFT-RAG replaces the exhaustive BFS entity search in Tree-RAG with an improved Cuckoo Filter that stores 12-bit fingerprints of entities in fixed-size buckets (4 slots each) addressed by two hash functions, achieving O(1) expected lookup. Two novel designs differentiate it from a vanilla Cuckoo Filter: (1) a block linked list per entity that chains together the addresses of all occurrences of that entity across different trees in the forest, enabling multi-location retrieval without redundant storage; and (2) a temperature variable at the head of each block list that records access frequency, triggering an adaptive re-sorting of bucket entries so that frequently queried ('hot') entities are probed first. During retrieval, a fingerprint match increments the entity's temperature and returns the block list head pointer, from which the algorithm walks all tree locations to collect up to n upward and n downward hierarchical neighbors, assembles them into a structured context via a template, and feeds the augmented prompt to the LLM. The filter supports dynamic insertion with a bounded eviction (kick) mechanism and automatic doubling expansion when the load factor exceeds a threshold, keeping the false-positive rate near zero for thousands of entities across 1024 buckets.

## Results

CFT-RAG achieves the lowest retrieval time across all three datasets: 5.24 s on MedQA, 0.97 s on AESLC, and 1.81 s on DART, while also attaining the highest answer accuracy on MedQA (69 ± 4%) and DART (68 ± 5%). On DART, CFT-RAG is over 8× faster than Naive T-RAG (1.81 s vs. 16.03 s), and on AESLC it is roughly 13× faster (0.97 s vs. 12.87 s). The retrieval time ratio (retrieval time as a fraction of total response time) drops to 5–16% with CFT-RAG, compared to 58–74% for Naive T-RAG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFT-RAG outperforms all four baselines on retrieval speed across every dataset. Against Naive T-RAG (BFS-based entity lookup), CFT-RAG reduces retrieval time by 73% on MedQA (5.24 s vs. 19.45 s), 92% on AESLC (0.97 s vs. 12.87 s), and 89% on DART (1.81 s vs. 16.03 s). Compared to ANN T-RAG, CFT-RAG is 32% faster on MedQA (5.24 s vs. 7.65 s), 61% faster on AESLC (0.97 s vs. 2.52 s), and 45% faster on DART (1.81 s vs. 3.28 s). Against ANN G-RAG, CFT-RAG is 40% faster on MedQA, 59% faster on AESLC, and 54% faster on DART. On accuracy, CFT-RAG leads on MedQA (69% vs. 67% for ANN T-RAG) and DART (68% vs. 66% for ANN T-RAG), while on AESLC it achieves 57%, marginally above ANN T-RAG (56%) and ANN G-RAG (53%). The paper also notes that CFT-RAG's advantage becomes more pronounced on multi-hop questions requiring precise entity-relationship retrieval, where the tree structure and Cuckoo Filter lookup jointly provide richer context than flat or graph-based retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Tree-RAG (Fatehkia et al., 2024), which introduced hierarchical entity trees to enrich RAG retrieval, by replacing the expensive BFS-based entity lookup with an improved Cuckoo Filter (Fan et al., 2014) augmented with two novel designs: a temperature-based adaptive sorting mechanism and a block linked list for storing multi-location entity addresses. It positions itself against both text-based RAG and Graph-RAG variants, arguing that tree-structured retrieval offers a favorable balance between structural expressiveness and retrieval efficiency. The contribution is primarily a systems-level optimization—applying a probabilistic data structure with O(1) lookup to an existing retrieval paradigm—rather than a new retrieval formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Retrieval accounts for 10–72% of total RAG response time, making it a critical bottleneck for real-time applications; the demonstrated >800% speedup over naive Tree-RAG on DART is practically significant for latency-sensitive deployments. However, accuracy gains over ANN-based baselines are modest (1–3 percentage points), and the method is tightly coupled to tree-structured knowledge bases, leaving open whether the Cuckoo Filter approach transfers to graph-based or flat retrieval settings. The temperature-based caching and block linked list are reasonable but incremental engineering choices, and the paper does not explore failure modes under adversarial or highly skewed query distributions.
