## Problem

Existing pre-trained wireless signal recognition (WSR) models rely on mask-reconstruction strategies that zero out portions of the signal, disrupting the intricate local temporal-spectral dependencies essential for waveform understanding. Additionally, these methods typically operate exclusively in the time domain, ignoring the rich spectral structure embedded in IQ data. A principled pre-training framework that preserves both temporal and spectral signal characteristics while learning transferable representations remains an open challenge.

## Input / Output

The method takes IQ signal data as a 2×L real-valued matrix (I and Q rows), with sequence length varying by dataset (128 for RML2016.10A/B, 1024 for RML2018, TechRec, and ICARUS). During pre-training, the model simultaneously consumes the time-domain signal and its Fourier-transformed spectral counterpart, producing a denoised reconstruction of the original IQ data. During fine-tuning, global average pooling over both temporal and spectral embeddings is concatenated and fed to a task-specific classifier, yielding modulation class labels (up to 24 classes), wireless technology labels (Wi-Fi, LTE, DVB-T), or binary anomaly detection outputs.

## Method

TS-DDAE formulates a two-domain denoising diffusion process where the forward step adds independent Gaussian noise in both the temporal domain (ε) and the spectral domain (η), with coefficients constrained by α²+β²+γ²=1, so that the inverse Fourier transform of spectral noise remains standard Gaussian. The backward process is learned by TS-Net, a hybrid architecture coupling a temporal encoder (multi-head self-attention with FFN, GELU, and depth-wise convolutions for local structure) and a spectral encoder (depth-wise/point-wise convolutions with a channel-attention module that re-weights informative frequency bands). The two encoders interact by cross-adding each other's convolved features plus the sinusoidal timestep embedding before their respective attention/convolution blocks, enabling joint temporal-spectral representation learning. The training loss (Eq. 7) jointly minimizes temporal and spectral noise prediction errors weighted by a ratio λ, and the entire pipeline is trained self-supervisedly before being fine-tuned with cross-entropy on downstream WSR tasks.

## Results

TS-DDAE achieves SOTA average accuracy across all four main benchmarks: 63.61% on RML2016.10A, 65.50% on RML2016.10B, 64.15% on RML2018, and 89.62% on TechRec (WTC task). On RML2018, TS-DDAE outperforms the previous SOTA IQFormer by 23.07% in average accuracy (64.15% vs. 40.22%), and on TechRec it achieves a near-perfect 99.47% best-SNR accuracy. The paper reports an average improvement of 1.32% over the best baseline and approximately 8.75% over IQFormer across all datasets. Linear probe and few-shot (25-shot, 100-shot) evaluations further confirm the quality of the learned representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TS-DDAE outperforms all 11 baselines on RML2018 (64.15% avg vs. 59.86% for SpectrumFM, the next best) and TechRec (89.62% avg vs. 88.71% for AMC_Net). Against IQFormer, the prior SOTA AMC model, TS-DDAE gains 23.07% average accuracy on RML2018 (64.15% vs. 40.22%) and 11.88% on TechRec (89.62% vs. 77.74%), though it trails IQFormer by 0.44% on RML2016.10A average (63.61% vs. 64.05%) while matching its best-SNR accuracy. Compared to the WSR foundation model SpectrumFM, TS-DDAE leads on every dataset, with the largest gap on RML2016.10B (65.50% vs. 53.12% avg). Among classical deep learning baselines (ResNet, VGG, GRU2, MSNet, etc.), TS-DDAE achieves the highest average accuracy on RML2016.10B and TechRec, and is within 0.4% of the best on RML2016.10A. The linear probe variant (54.40% avg on RML2016.10A) already surpasses several full-training baselines like ResNet (48.25%) and CGDNN (51.62%), confirming the quality of the self-supervised representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the denoising diffusion autoencoder (DDAE) paradigm from computer vision (Xiang et al., 2023) to wireless signal recognition pre-training, departing from the mask-reconstruction strategy used by SpectrumFM (Zhou et al., 2025) in favor of a dual-domain noise-reconstruction objective that simultaneously corrupts and denoises both the temporal and spectral representations of IQ data. The TS-Net architecture draws on Transformer self-attention for the temporal encoder (inspired by IQFormer's multi-modality fusion) and channel attention for the spectral encoder, with a novel interactive design where each encoder receives the other's input. The work also builds on the DDPM/DDIM diffusion framework (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021) but formulates an unconditional, representation-learning objective rather than a generative one, and explicitly contrasts with RF-Diffusion (Chi et al., 2024), which targets conditional signal generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TS-DDAE is the first diffusion-based pre-training framework for WSR, offering a principled alternative to mask-reconstruction that better preserves the intricate local temporal-spectral structure of waveforms. The ablation revealing that the spectral encoder is critical for wireless technology classification (catastrophic 52-point drop when removed) but less so for modulation classification highlights task-specific representational needs that prior single-domain methods could not capture. Open questions include whether the two noise sources—formally equivalent under the unitary FFT—truly provide distinct optimization gradients, how the framework scales to larger and more diverse signal corpora, and whether the balanced-noise finding (λ ≈ 0.5) generalizes across signal types. The modest average improvement (~1.32% over the best baseline) suggests the gains are real but incremental, and the field still lacks a truly large-scale WSR foundation model.
