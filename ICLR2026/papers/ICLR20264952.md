## Problem

Existing protein structure tokenizers depend on SE(3)-invariant architectural components (invariant point attention, frame-aligned losses) that are difficult to optimize at scale and hard to extend to diverse biomolecules like RNA, DNA, or post-translationally modified proteins. It remains unclear whether non-invariant tokenizers that operate directly on raw 3D coordinates with standard attention can match or exceed the reconstruction and generative quality of these invariant designs.

## Input / Output

Kanzi takes mean-centered protein backbone coordinates (Cα-only or full backbone, shape L×A×3, with L < 256 residues in main experiments) and produces a sequence of discrete FSQ tokens (8×5×5×5 levels, effective codebook size 1000) that condition a flow-matching decoder to reconstruct the 3D structure. For conditional generation, a cryoET volume map (L×L×L voxel mask) is encoded into a token sequence and provided as in-context conditioning to an autoregressive model that outputs Kanzi tokens, which are then decoded into protein backbones. The autoregressive prior enables variable-length generation, supporting tasks like motif scaffolding where protein length is unknown a priori.

## Method

Kanzi is an asymmetric flow autoencoder: a lightweight 2-layer encoder with sliding-window attention maps raw coordinates to a latent, which is discretized via finite scalar quantization (FSQ) and passed to a substantially deeper 8–12 layer DiT decoder with full bidirectional attention and optional pair-biased attention. The entire system is trained end-to-end with a single conditional flow matching loss (regressing the learned vector field against x₁−x₀ under linear interpolation), replacing the collection of SE(3)-invariant reconstruction losses (FAPE, dRMSD, bond-angle penalties) used by prior tokenizers. Key design choices include a single-stream encoder (vs. separate modality streams), concatenated in-context conditioning for the decoder, shared adaLN time-conditioning weights across all layers (reducing parameters by ~30%), and random rotation augmentation during training. At inference, classifier-free guidance and tunable noise/score scaling (Langevin-style SDE sampling) improve generation quality, while an autoregressive model trained on the discrete tokens provides length-agnostic structure generation with best-of-N log-likelihood selection.

## Results

Kanzi (30M parameters) achieves Cα reconstruction RMSD of 0.817 Å on CAMEO, 0.698 Å on CASP14, 0.953 Å on CATH, and 0.870 Å on AFDB (with classifier-free guidance), matching or outperforming ESM3 (648M) on 4 of 5 datasets despite being 20× smaller in parameters and trained on a 400× smaller dataset. On the distributional rFPSD metric, Kanzi (30M) achieves 7.96 on CATH, substantially better than ESM3 (23.40) and DPLM2 (5.74). For generation, Kanzi-AR (250M) with best-of-2 sampling achieves the highest designability score (0.617) and competitive scRMSD (3.655 Å) and scTM (0.807), outperforming ESM3-AR (300M) and DPLM2-AR (300M) on all quality metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ESM3 (648M), Kanzi (30M) with guidance wins on CAMEO (0.817 vs 0.860 RMSD), CATH (0.953 vs 1.048), and AFDB (0.870 vs 2.384), but loses on CASP14 (0.698 vs 0.462) and CASP15 (1.267 vs 1.021). Against DPLM2 (118M), Kanzi dominates across all five datasets, with the largest gap on AFDB (0.870 vs 4.676 RMSD). FoldToken (85M) and IST (11M) are consistently outperformed by Kanzi on every dataset. On the distributional rFPSD metric, Kanzi (7.96) substantially outperforms ESM3 (23.40) and FoldToken (71.79), though DPLM2 (5.74) remains slightly better. For generation, Kanzi-AR (250M) with best-of-2 sampling achieves the best designability (0.617) among all compared models, beating ESM3-AR (0.520) and DPLM2-AR (0.320), while DPLM2 (650M, discrete diffusion) retains a slight edge on scRMSD (3.314 vs 3.655) and scTM (0.814 vs 0.807).

## Relation to Prior Work

Kanzi adapts the flow autoencoder paradigm from computer vision (FlowMo, DiTo) to protein structure tokenization, replacing the SE(3)-invariant architectural components (invariant point attention, frame-based representations) and complex multi-term losses (FAPE, dRMSD, bond-angle penalties) that dominate prior tokenizers like ESM3 and DPLM-2 with standard transformer attention and a single flow matching loss. It builds on the observation from Proteina and AlphaFold3 that non-invariant architectures can scale effectively for protein structure modeling, and extends this to the tokenization setting. The autoregressive generation component draws on the broader LLM literature (nucleus sampling, best-of-N) applied to discrete protein structure tokens, while the diffusion decoder follows the DiT architecture with modifications (shared adaLN, pair-biased attention).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the SE(3)-invariance inductive bias, long considered essential for protein structure tokenization, is not only unnecessary but actively harmful (invariant encoders cause codebook collapse), which could reshape how the community designs structure tokenizers. The 20× parameter efficiency over ESM3 and the single-loss training pipeline substantially lower the barrier to building competitive tokenizers. Key limitations include training exclusively on synthetic AFDB data (introducing alpha-helix overprediction), small model sizes (<30M), Cα-only generation, and a remaining gap to state-of-the-art continuous diffusion models on generation quality. Open questions include whether these findings hold at scale, whether full-backbone and all-atom generation can match the Cα results, and whether the non-invariant approach generalizes to other biomolecular modalities (RNA, DNA, post-translational modifications).
