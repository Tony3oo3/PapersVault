## Problem

Nonparametric teaching theory provides a principled framework for selecting optimal training examples to accelerate learning, but it has only been applied to MLP-based learners and relies on functional gradient descent in function space. Attention-based learners (transformers) train via parameter-space gradient descent, and the attention mechanism's role in shaping parameter updates has not been analyzed in this context, making it unclear whether nonparametric teaching can be applied to attention learners.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261753/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dense set of sequence-property pairs (e.g., token sequences with next-token targets, or image patch sequences with reconstruction targets) and an initialized attention learner. It produces a trained attention learner by iteratively selecting a subset of the full dataset at each re-sampling interval, where the subset size grows over training according to a configurable ratio schedule (cosine or incremental). The selection criterion is the Frobenius norm of the discrepancy between the model's output and the true property value for each sequence, with Gumbel-Top-k used for stochastic (soft) sampling without replacement.

## Method

The paper first derives explicit gradient expressions for a single-layer self-attention network, showing that the three invocations of the input (at Q, K, V) produce an element-specific scalar importance weight ω_j that modulates each row of the parameter gradient, yielding an importance-adaptive update whose form is invariant to sequence length. It then proves that the dynamic Attention Neural Tangent Kernel (ANTK) arising from parameter-space gradient descent converges pointwise to the importance-adaptive canonical kernel used in functional gradient descent, establishing consistency between the two training perspectives. Building on this, the AtteNT algorithm greedily selects the m sequences with the largest ||f_θ(S_i) − f*(S_i)||_F at each re-sampling step, amplifying the effective gradient by concentrating updates on high-discrepancy examples. In practice, a soft variant using Gumbel-Top-k sampling with an incremental ratio schedule is adopted, which the ablation study shows outperforms both random and hard (deterministic) selection across downstream tasks.

## Results

AtteNT reduces LLM fine-tuning time by an average of 12.78% (e.g., LLaMA 2-7B from 246 min to 213 min) while simultaneously improving accuracy: GSM8K gains of +1.39 (LLaMA), +2.14 (Mistral), +2.42 (Gemma); MATH gains of +1.59, +2.89, +0.76; HumanEval gains of +3.45, +3.13, +0.43; and MBPP gains of +1.96, +3.22, +0.59. For Vision Transformers, AtteNT cuts pre-training time by 20.58% (1234 min → 980 min) while improving ImageNetS50 accuracy from 92.2 to 92.3, NYUv2 semantic segmentation mIoU from 51.9 to 52.6, and NYUv2 depth estimation δ₁ from 52.1 to 57.2 (+5.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external data-selection or curriculum-learning baselines; the sole comparison is standard full-data training (w/o AtteNT) versus AtteNT-enhanced training (w/ AtteNT) under identical model, data volume, and epoch settings. AtteNT wins on every reported metric: it reduces wall-clock training time by 12.78% on average for LLM fine-tuning and 20.58% for ViT pre-training, while simultaneously improving downstream accuracy on all five NLP benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH, HumanEval, MBPP, MT-Bench) and all three CV benchmarks (ImageNetS50, NYUv2 segmentation, NYUv2 depth). The only exception is MT-Bench for LLaMA 2-7B, where AtteNT scores 4.49 vs. 4.58 (a 0.09 drop), though this is within the reported standard deviation. The largest single-task gain is +5.1 on NYUv2 depth estimation (52.1 → 57.2 δ₁), which the authors attribute to the task's independence from image type, preventing distributional disruption during selective sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the nonparametric teaching framework (Zhang et al., 2023b;a; 2024a; 2025), which previously applied only to MLP-based learners, to attention-based architectures by proving that the dynamic Attention Neural Tangent Kernel (ANTK, Hron et al., 2020) converges to the importance-adaptive canonical kernel used in functional gradient descent. This convergence result bridges parameter-space gradient descent in ANNs with the function-space dynamics assumed by nonparametric teaching, enabling the greedy teaching algorithm to be applied directly to attention learners. The work departs from prior nonparametric teaching studies by explicitly accounting for the three invocations of input (Q, K, V) in attention, which produce element-specific importance scalars that make the parameter gradient equivariant to sequence reordering and independent of sequence length.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical bridge between ANTK and the canonical kernel opens nonparametric teaching to all attention-based architectures, not just MLPs, potentially accelerating training across NLP, vision, and multimodal systems. Practically, the 13–20% wall-clock reductions without accuracy loss (and often with gains) are meaningful for large-scale fine-tuning and from-scratch training, though the analysis is restricted to single-layer, single-head attention, leaving multi-layer and multi-head generalizations open. Future directions include extending to graph attention networks, robustness under label noise, and applications to world models, but the current scope limits immediate adoption in production multi-layer transformers.
