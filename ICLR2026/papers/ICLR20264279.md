## Problem

Existing caching-based acceleration methods for Diffusion Transformers rely on fixed or linearly varying cache intervals that cannot adapt to the complex, non-uniform error dynamics across denoising timesteps and cache intervals. Error correction strategies (token pruning, Taylor prediction) are consequently applied on top of suboptimal caching schedules, limiting their effectiveness in mitigating cumulative error. No prior method optimizes the caching strategy itself in a training-free, plug-and-play manner that is compatible with arbitrary acceleration budgets and existing correction frameworks.

## Input / Output

The method requires a precomputed error matrix of shape N×T (number of candidate cache intervals × number of denoising timesteps, e.g., 9×50) and a user-specified acceleration budget Nc (number of caching operations). This error matrix is generated offline once per model via ~10–100 random content generations and is content-agnostic, reusable across all subsequent inferences. The output is an optimal set of (timestep, cache-interval) pairs that replace the caching schedule in any existing acceleration method; the downstream generation still consumes standard text prompts or class labels and produces images (256×256 to 1024×1024) or videos (480p, 33–129 frames). The DP solution itself takes <1.2 ms and 0.88 KB of memory at inference time.

## Method

CEM operates in three stages. First, Offline Error Modeling computes a cosine-distance error E(t,n) between the ground-truth output at timestep t and the cached output from timestep t+n, averaged over Ns random generations, yielding a content-agnostic prior that captures the joint sensitivity to both timesteps and cache intervals. Second, a Cumulative Error Approximation applies a simple CUMSUM (cumulative integral) over E(t,n) to estimate the true propagated error without the exponential cost of direct cumulative modeling; this approximation is validated to match ground-truth error within ~1–3%. Third, a Dynamic Programming algorithm with state dp[t][j] (minimum cumulative error from timestep t with j caching operations remaining) selects the optimal combination of cache intervals under the budget constraint Nc, solved in O(T·Nc·|N|) time. The resulting strategy is then plugged into existing error-correction methods (ToCa, DuCa, TaylorSeer, TeaCache, FasterSD) or quantized models (Q-DiT) by simply replacing their caching schedule, introducing zero additional inference overhead while reducing cumulative caching error and improving generation fidelity.

## Results

On FLUX.1-dev (1024×1024, 50 steps), CEM integrated with TaylorSeer(N6) achieves IR 0.9811 and CLIP(G) 32.89, surpassing the original model (IR 0.9649) at 5× lower FLOPs (744.81T vs 3719.50T). On PixArt-α, CEM-enhanced DuCa(N5) reduces FID from 41.56 to 27.57, beating the original model's 28.06. On Hunyuan video generation, CEM-enhanced TaylorSeer(N6) reaches VBench 81.24%, exceeding the original model's 78.46% while maintaining 5.56× speedup. On quantized Q-DiT (W4A8), CEM doubles inference speed (0.39s→0.20s) while improving IS by 4.20.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CEM consistently improves every baseline it is integrated with. On FLUX.1-dev, CEM-enhanced TaylorSeer(N6) outperforms the original model on IR (0.9811 vs 0.9649) and CLIP(G) (32.89 vs 32.57) at 5× lower FLOPs, while CEM-enhanced ToCa(N4) surpasses both the original and FORA on IR (1.0151 vs 0.9649/0.9227). On PixArt-α, CEM-enhanced DuCa(N5) achieves FID 27.57, dramatically beating DuCa's 41.56 and even the original model's 28.06, while also outperforming FORA (29.67) and ToCa (29.73). On Hunyuan video, CEM-enhanced TaylorSeer(N6) reaches VBench 81.24%, exceeding the original (78.46%) and all baselines including ToCa (78.86%) and FORA (78.83%). On DiT-XL/2 with DDIM, CEM-enhanced DuCa(N5) reduces FID from 6.07 to 3.96 and IS from 199.64 to 218.66, surpassing AdaCache (FID 4.64) and LazyDiT (FID 2.70 at higher FLOPs). For quantized Q-DiT, CEM doubles speed (0.45s→0.22s at W6A8, 0.39s→0.20s at W4A8) while improving IS by 3.02 and 4.20 respectively, demonstrating compatibility with quantization without fidelity loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

CEM positions itself as a strategy-optimization layer atop existing caching-based acceleration methods (ToCa, DuCa, TaylorSeer, TeaCache, FasterSD) that already incorporate error correction via token pruning or Taylor-series prediction. It departs from prior caching-optimization attempts (AdaCache, AdaptiveDiffusion, TeaCache) by replacing real-time or heuristic schedule selection with an offline, content-agnostic error model and a dynamic-programming solver, eliminating the runtime overhead that limited those approaches. Conceptually, it repurposes the well-known DP framework for sequence segmentation to the specific problem of choosing cache intervals under a fixed acceleration budget, using a simple CUMSUM approximation of cumulative error as the DP cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, zero-inference-overhead design makes CEM immediately deployable on top of any existing caching accelerator or quantized model, which is practically valuable for practitioners who cannot retrain. The finding that ~10 random samples suffice to capture the model-intrinsic error distribution (content-agnostic, robust to seed/CFG/resolution) lowers the adoption barrier significantly. However, the authors acknowledge that CEM still lags behind training-based distillation methods and cannot apply to one-step generators, leaving open whether offline error modeling generalizes to fundamentally new architectures without re-modeling, and whether the DP-optimal strategy remains valid under distribution shift (e.g., new prompt domains).
