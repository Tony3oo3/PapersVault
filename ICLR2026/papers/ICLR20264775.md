## Problem

While numerous practical continual learning algorithms exist, general theoretical guarantees on learning plasticity—measured by cumulative loss—have been largely absent. Existing PAC-Bayes results cover online learning, meta-learning, and average loss/forgetting, but no algorithm-agnostic upper bound on the cumulative generalization loss for continual learning had been derived. This paper fills that gap by providing the first general risk certificates for cumulative loss in the continual learning setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of T tasks, each providing m i.i.d. samples from an unknown distribution, along with a data-free prior over the hypothesis space, and assumes the loss is either bounded in [0,K] or K-subGaussian. It produces high-probability upper bounds on the average cumulative loss (1/T)·CuL that are valid uniformly over all posteriors and hold for any learning algorithm, model architecture, or task ordering. The oracle bounds for Gibbs posteriors additionally yield interpretable certificates expressed in terms of the loss of predictors obtained from previous tasks, with explicit dependence on task similarity via a Lipschitz constant.

## Method

The paper extends PAC-Bayes bounds from online learning (Haddouche & Guedj, 2022) and time-uniform offline learning to the continual learning setting by replacing single examples with batches of m samples per task and introducing stochastic kernels to handle data-dependent priors. Corollary 3.1 gives a general bound where the KL-divergence term captures the change between consecutive posteriors Q_t and Q_{t-1}, while Theorem 3.2 employs a bad-event analysis to obtain a tighter bound that converges as T→∞ provided m ≫ √T. For oracle analysis, the paper applies Laplace's method to the expected Gibbs posterior, deriving asymptotic bounds (Theorem 4.1, Corollary 4.2) that express the cumulative loss in terms of the loss of the optimal predictor from previous tasks. The framework is instantiated with variational inference and EWC on vision benchmarks (Permuted-MNIST, Split-MNIST, Split-CIFAR10, Split-ImageNet) and with SGLD on linear regression tasks, demonstrating non-vacuous and tight bounds respectively.

## Results

The paper demonstrates that its PAC-Bayes cumulative loss bounds (Eq. 3) yield non-vacuous risk certificates across multiple vision-based continual learning benchmarks. For variational inference (VI), bounds are nearly tight: e.g., on Permuted-MNIST the actual CuL is 15.5% while the bound is 17.9%, and on Split-CIFAR10 the CuL is 49.8% vs. a bound of 52.2%. For EWC, bounds are looser (e.g., CuL 1.0% vs. bound 10.6% on Permuted-MNIST) since EWC does not directly optimize the bound's right-hand side. Oracle bounds for over-parametrized deep models on linear regression tasks are nearly exact (CuL ≈ 0.01, bound ≈ 0.01), confirming the theoretical predictions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper applies its PAC-Bayes bounds to three continual learning algorithms: EWC (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017), Variational Inference (Hoffman et al., 2013), and Experience Replay (Rebuffi et al., 2017). The key comparison is not between algorithms but between the tightness of the derived bound for each. VI consistently yields the tightest bounds (within 1–3 percentage points of actual CuL across all datasets) because its training objective directly optimizes the bound's right-hand side. EWC bounds are substantially looser (e.g., 10.6% bound vs. 1.0% actual CuL on Permuted-MNIST), reflecting that EWC's regularization is not aligned with the PAC-Bayes objective. On Split-ImageNet, Replay achieves the lowest forgetting (2.8%) but the highest CuL (55.7%), while EWC and SGD achieve lower CuL (~33–35%) at the cost of higher forgetting (5.7% and 7.8% respectively). The oracle bounds (Eq. 4) are nearly exact for over-parametrized deep networks (CuL 0.01, bound 0.01) but looser for under-parametrized models, confirming the theoretical prediction that sufficient over-parameterization overcomes task-order effects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends PAC-Bayes bounds from online learning (Haddouche & Guedj, 2022) and time-uniform offline learning (Haddouche & Guedj, 2023; Chugg et al., 2023) to the continual learning setting by replacing single examples with per-task batches and introducing online predictive sequences of stochastic kernels. It departs from prior continual learning theory (Evron et al., 2022; Lin et al., 2023; Bennani & Sugiyama, 2020) that is restricted to specific architectures (NTK regime) or task structures (linear regression), providing instead algorithm- and architecture-agnostic bounds. The oracle bounds for Gibbs posteriors employ Laplace's method, a technique the authors note is rarely used in PAC-Bayes analysis, and the cumulative loss metric distinguishes this work from the concurrent forgetting bounds of Friedman & Meir (2025) which target average loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first general, algorithm-agnostic risk certificate for learning plasticity in continual learning, offering practitioners a concrete rule of thumb (samples per task should exceed sqrt(T)/2) for when high-probability guarantees become meaningful. The non-vacuous bounds demonstrated on vision tasks (Permuted-MNIST, Split-CIFAR10, Split-ImageNet) validate the theory in practice, though the KL-divergence term remains a scaling bottleneck for large models. Open questions include extending to heavy-tailed losses, handling unknown or blurry task boundaries, and developing divergence measures that scale better for high-dimensional parameter spaces. The oracle bounds also reveal that task ordering significantly affects cumulative loss even when average loss is unaffected, a distinction that may inform task scheduling strategies.
