## Problem

Prevalent semantic speech tokenizers, despite being designed to capture linguistic content, are surprisingly fragile to meaning-irrelevant acoustic perturbations: even at high SNRs where speech is perfectly intelligible, their discrete output sequences can change drastically. This instability stems from a brittle single-path quantization architecture that lacks fault tolerance and a distant ASR supervisory signal that is indifferent to intermediate token stability, collectively increasing the learning burden for downstream SpeechLLMs.

## Input / Output

The model takes a continuous speech audio waveform (up to 30 seconds per inference pass, consistent with Whisper's context window) and produces a discrete token sequence at 25 Hz frame rate with a vocabulary of 8192 tokens (13 bits per token). Each token is generated by a multi-branch Voting-LFQ module that performs a bit-wise majority vote across N=5 parallel binary projections, yielding a single stable integer index per time step. For audio exceeding 30 seconds, a standard chunking-and-concatenation strategy is used, with no significant degradation in token stability at segment boundaries.

## Method

StableToken introduces two synergistic innovations: (1) a Voting-LFQ module that replaces the single-path quantizer with N parallel linear projection branches, each producing a binary vector via the sign function (with STE for backpropagation), which are then aggregated by a differentiable bit-wise majority vote—during training the averaged real-valued scores provide richer gradient feedback, while at inference the sign of the vote yields a robust consensus token; and (2) Noise-Aware Consensus Training, where a random minority of branches receive a noise-perturbed version of the input while the majority receive the clean version, and a consensus loss (L2 distance to the global mean of all pre-quantization vectors) forces the noisy branches to align with the clean-majority anchor. The total training objective combines ASR cross-entropy, the consensus loss, commitment loss, and codebook entropy loss, all optimized end-to-end on a Whisper-large-v3 backbone. The key insight is that bit-level voting provides finer-grained error correction than token-level majority voting: even when a majority of branches produce incorrect tokens, the true token can be recovered as long as the underlying bit-level errors remain sparse.

## Results

StableToken achieves a state-of-the-art average Unit Edit Distance (UED) of 10.17% under six noise conditions (Gaussian, Pink, Brown, Bit Crush, Real, Real-OOD), a 61% relative reduction over the best supervised baseline S³ Tokenizer (26.17%) and a 38% reduction over the best SSL-based model R-Spin (16.48%). On reconstruction quality, StableToken attains 3.84% WER on LibriSpeech-clean and 4.09 MOS, outperforming all supervised baselines. Downstream, the StableToken-based SpeechLLM achieves 35.90% WER on CHiME-4 test-real (vs. 51.08% for GLM-4-Voice) and 4.43% WER on SEED-TTS_EN (vs. 6.19% for GLM-4-Voice), with the performance gap widening dramatically under severe noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StableToken outperforms all nine baselines across every noise condition. Against the closest supervised competitor S³ Tokenizer (CosyVoice), StableToken reduces average UED from 26.17% to 10.17% (−61% relative), with the gap most pronounced under Gaussian noise (35.40% → 12.93%). Among SSL-based tokenizers, R-Spin (16.48%) and NAST (17.07%) are the most robust, yet StableToken still achieves a 38–41% relative UED reduction. Distilled tokenizers (SpeechTokenizer, X-Codec, Mimi) are substantially more fragile, with average UEDs of 26.72%, 38.52%, and 53.43% respectively, and their instability worsens with more codebook levels. On downstream tasks, StableToken-based SpeechLLMs achieve 35.90% WER on CHiME-4 test-real versus 51.08% (GLM-4-Voice) and 54.63% (CosyVoice), and 4.43% WER on SEED-TTS_EN versus 6.19% (GLM-4-Voice) and 7.80% (CosyVoice). Notably, StableToken achieves these gains with a larger vocabulary (8192 vs. 4096–16384), making the token-level invariance task inherently harder, and with negligible inference overhead (≤0.03% additional FLOPs over a single-branch baseline).

## Relation to Prior Work

StableToken extends the supervised semantic tokenizer paradigm established by CosyVoice (Du et al., 2024a) and GLM-4-Voice (Zeng et al., 2025), which embed a quantizer within an end-to-end ASR encoder. Its core architectural innovation—the Voting-LFQ module—directly extends the Look-up-Free Quantization (LFQ) algorithm of Yu et al. (2023) from a single-path to a multi-branch design with bit-wise majority voting. The paper also departs from prior noise-robustness work (R-Spin, NAST), which targeted SSL-based tokenizers, by addressing the supervised semantic tokenizer class that actually underpins modern SpeechLLMs, and by identifying the dual root causes (brittle single-path architecture + distant ASR-only supervision) rather than applying a single robustness loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Tokenizer instability is a fundamental, previously underappreciated bottleneck for SpeechLLMs in real-world noisy conditions, and this paper demonstrates that fixing it at the tokenizer level yields large downstream gains across ASR, SER, and TTS with negligible computational overhead. The bit-wise voting mechanism is a drop-in replacement for standard quantizers in existing SpeechLLM pipelines, making it highly adoptable. Open questions include whether the multi-branch consensus approach generalizes to other quantization schemes (e.g., FSQ, RVQ), whether it extends to non-speech modalities, and whether the 30-second chunking strategy introduces subtle boundary effects in streaming deployment.
