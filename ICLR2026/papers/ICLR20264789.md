## Problem

Existing unsupervised pose estimation methods either rely on hand-defined pose priors that require costly human annotations and may not be optimal, or operate without any structural priors, leading to unreliable keypoint detection and infeasible topological configurations especially under occlusion. The paper formalizes the challenge of learning a general, interpretable categorical pose prior purely from unannotated images in a self-supervised manner, asking whether such priors can emerge without any human intervention and whether they can be explicitly represented rather than buried in opaque network weights.

## Input / Output

PPL takes an RGB target image I (128×128 or 256×256) and a reference image I_ref (a different video frame of the same object for video datasets, or a randomly masked version of I for static image datasets) as input. It produces N 2D keypoints in normalized [-1,1]×[-1,1] space, an H×W combined connectivity link heatmap, a reconstructed image I_recon, and—critically—an explicit categorical pose prior V=(T,W) consisting of N symbolic keypoint coordinates and an N×N connectivity weight matrix. The prior is a structured, visualizable artifact rather than a latent representation, and at inference an iterative autoregressive loop feeds reconstructed images back as input to progressively refine poses over 4 iterations.

## Method

PPL maintains a hierarchical memory M of 34 memory banks (each with 16 vectors of dimension 512) that stores compositional parts of prototypical poses; the keypoint prior T is distilled by mean-pooling each bank and decoding via MLP-Mixer blocks, while the connectivity prior W is a learnable N×N matrix. Given image I, a 2D-CNN extracts features that, together with the keypoint prior embedding, predict per-keypoint affine transformation parameters to warp T into image-specific keypoints T'. These keypoints are connected via differentiable link heatmaps modulated by W, and the resulting combined link heatmap concatenated with I_ref is decoded by a 2D-CNN to reconstruct I. Training jointly minimizes four losses—VGG19 perceptual reconstruction, boundary regularization, link-length preservation, and keypoint configuration reconstruction—using VQ-VAE-style gradient copying, exponential moving average memory updates, and dual gradient detachment to resolve the chicken-and-egg dependency between the prior and the memory. The hierarchical design (multiple smaller banks vs. one large bank) enables multi-level retrieval of pose sub-structures, which is essential for the iterative inference strategy that corrects occluded poses by regressing them toward stored prototypes.

## Results

PPL achieves the best keypoint detection accuracy across all evaluated datasets. On Human3.6m, PPL reaches a normalized L2 error of 1.92 (Res.128) and 2.56 (Res.256), outperforming the strongest no-prior baseline AutoLink (2.76 at Res.128) and the human-prior method STT (3.31 at Res.256). On Taichi, PPL achieves a summed L2 error of 293.35, beating AutoLink (316.10) and GANSeg (417.17). On CUB-200-2011, PPL attains the lowest errors on all five subsets, e.g., 3.19 on CUB-Aligned and 10.5 on CUB-All, surpassing AutoLink (3.51 and 11.3) and GANSeg (3.23 and 12.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPL outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset and resolution tested. Against the strongest no-prior method AutoLink (He et al., 2022), PPL reduces the normalized L2 error from 2.76 to 1.92 on Human3.6m (Res.128) and from 316.10 to 293.35 on Taichi, and improves CUB-All from 11.3 to 10.5. Notably, PPL also beats STT (Schmidtke et al., 2021), which uses a human-defined pose prior, achieving 2.56 vs. 3.31 on Human3.6m (Res.256), demonstrating that learned priors can surpass manually annotated ones. On CUB-200-2011, PPL edges out GANSeg (3.19 vs. 3.23 on CUB-Aligned) and substantially outperforms LatentKeypointGAN (10.5 vs. 14.7 on CUB-All). In the supplementary comparison with Hedlin et al. (2024), which leverages a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model as a multimodal prior, PPL wins on Human3.6m (1.92 vs. 4.45) and CUB-Aligned (3.19 vs. 5.06) but trails on Taichi (293.35 vs. 234.89) and CUB non-aligned subsets, while using a far smaller model trained solely on the visual modality.

## Relation to Prior Work

PPL builds directly on AutoLink's differentiable link heatmap mechanism for connecting keypoints and on VQ-VAE's gradient detachment for discrete memory retrieval, but departs fundamentally from both by introducing an explicit, interpretable categorical pose prior distilled from a hierarchical multi-bank memory. Unlike prior unsupervised pose estimation methods (AutoLink, BKind) that leave structural priors implicit in network weights, or methods like STT and HPE that rely on costly human-annotated priors, PPL frames the learning of a category-level prior as a distinct, self-supervised objective. The hierarchical memory design (34 banks × 16 vectors) also differentiates from single-space memory approaches like PCT, enabling compositional distillation of pose components into a general prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly learned, symbolic pose priors can outperform human-defined ones and enable robust pose estimation under heavy occlusion via iterative inference — a capability absent in prior unsupervised methods. The explicit prior representation is interpretable and visualizable, offering a new lens on how structural knowledge emerges from data without annotation. Key limitations include the restriction to 2D priors (no 3D rotation modeling), moderate performance gains over the strongest baseline (AutoLink), and the observation that severe occlusions can still produce plausible-but-incorrect reconstructions. Open questions include whether the hierarchical memory scales to 3D, whether the prior learning mechanism generalizes to articulated objects with high intra-class variability, and whether the approach benefits from stronger backbones like Vision Transformers.
