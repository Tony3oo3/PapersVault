## Problem

Existing automatic prompt optimization methods for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are confined to the textual modality, leaving the non-textual input channels (images, videos, molecules) entirely unused during prompt search. This restriction prevents the discovery of richer contextual signals that MLLMs are inherently capable of leveraging, resulting in suboptimal prompts that fail to exploit the full multimodal space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262252/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a task dataset of query-answer pairs (where queries can be images, video clips, or SMILES-encoded molecules paired with text), an initial textual prompt, and access to modality-specific generators (e.g., GPT-Image for images, Wan2.1 for 5-second videos at 16 fps, GPT-4o mini for molecules). It produces an optimized multimodal prompt as a pair (t*, m*) where t* is a refined textual instruction and m* is a generated or edited non-textual artifact (reference image, short video clip, or molecular structure). The textual prompt explicitly instructs the MLLM to leverage the non-textual signal (e.g., 'Use the hybrid reference image for guidance'), making the two components mutually dependent rather than independently useful.

## Method

MPO consists of two coupled modules. First, alignment-preserving exploration jointly updates the textual and non-textual prompt components using a single unified feedback signal (a 'semantic gradient') derived from failure analysis, ensuring cross-modal consistency; three complementary operators—generation (create from scratch), edit (refine existing non-textual prompt), and mix (blend multiple candidates)—diversify the search over the enlarged multimodal space. Second, prior-inherited Bayesian UCB selection exploits the empirically observed parent-child performance correlation (Pearson r = 0.88) by initializing each child prompt's Beta posterior from its parent's posterior mean scaled by a prior strength S, rather than starting from a uniform prior; this warm-starts the bandit-based evaluation, reducing the evaluation budget by 42% compared to a prior-free baseline while accelerating identification of high-performing prompts in the combinatorial multimodal space.

## Results

MPO achieves an average score of 65.1 across 10 benchmarks spanning image, video, and molecular modalities, outperforming the strongest text-only baseline ProTeGi (60.0) by 5.1 points and SEE (59.1) by 6.0 points. Gains are particularly large on fine-grained image classification (PlantVillage: 76.4 vs. 69.0 for SEE; CUB: 78.6 vs. 71.6) and molecular property prediction (Absorption Acc: 76.7 vs. 71.4; BBBP F1: 67.6 vs. 62.3). MPO also reduces evaluation budget by 42% compared to a prior-free selection baseline while matching or exceeding its final performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPO consistently outperforms all seven text-only APO baselines across every modality. Against the strongest text-only methods, MPO gains +7.4 over SEE and +6.4 over ProTeGi on PlantVillage, +7.0 and +8.6 on CUB, and +5.3 and +5.6 on VANE-Bench (video anomaly detection). On molecular tasks, MPO leads SEE by +5.3 Acc on Absorption and +5.6 F1 on BBBP, while surpassing ProTeGi by +5.6 Acc on Absorption. The gap over manual baselines is even larger: MPO exceeds the best few-shot (3-Shot, 49.5 avg) by 15.6 points and CoT (40.8) by 24.3 points. Notably, MPO also outperforms the best text-only method on the hardest subtasks (e.g., CUB grosbeak: 71.6 vs. 69.2 for SEE), and the prior-inherited Bayesian UCB selection achieves the same final performance as uniform allocation while using only 30% of the evaluation budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Automatic Prompt Optimization (APO) lineage—rooted in ProTeGi's beam-search-with-textual-gradients loop and the broader gradient-free APO literature (APE, OPRO, EvoPrompt, PE2, SEE)—by expanding the optimization space from purely textual prompts to joint (text, non-text) prompt pairs. The selection mechanism draws directly on Bayesian UCB bandit theory (Kaufmann et al., 2012), but introduces a prior-inheritance step that initializes child-prompt Beta posteriors from the parent's posterior mean, a departure from standard UCB where each arm starts from a uniform prior. The alignment-preserving exploration (cohesive backpropagation + generation/edit/mix operators) is a novel coupling mechanism not present in any prior text-only APO method, which refine only the textual component in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating consistent gains across images, videos, and molecules, the paper establishes that the non-textual prompt dimension is not a minor add-on but a first-class optimization axis that text-only methods structurally cannot exploit. The 42% evaluation-budget reduction from prior-inherited Bayesian UCB and the mechanistic hidden-state analysis (showing multimodal prompts shift MLLM representations into distinct regions) provide both practical efficiency and interpretive insight. Open questions include: how the framework scales to additional modalities (audio, 3D), whether the parent-child correlation assumption (r=0.88) holds under more aggressive mutation operators or larger beam sizes, and whether the reliance on external generators (GPT-Image, Wan2.1) can be replaced by learned, differentiable components to reduce the computational overhead noted in Table 5.
