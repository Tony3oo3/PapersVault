## Problem

Logit adjustment (LA), the standard debiasing tool in long-tailed semi-supervised learning, applies a single global correction derived from class frequencies and is blind to semantic relationships between classes. The paper identifies a critical consequence of this blindness: intra-super-class imbalance, where semantically similar classes within a super-class (e.g., truck vs. automobile) are both highly confusable and locally imbalanced, causing minority-class representations to be systematically suppressed by their majority neighbors through a self-reinforcing pseudo-labeling loop.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR/STL, 64×64 for ImageNet-127) and a set of class names, which are encoded by a pre-trained text model (e.g., CLIP, SBERT, or text-embedding-ada-002) and clustered into K super-classes via agglomerative clustering. At inference, the model outputs both a fine-grained class label and a super-class posterior distribution p(k|u_j) that dynamically modulates the logit correction. The super-class grouping is a one-time offline computation; no manual taxonomy or vision pretraining is required.

## Method

SCAD is a three-component plug-and-play framework: (i) super-class discovery by encoding class names with a text encoder and applying agglomerative clustering to yield K = ⌈C/4⌉ groups; (ii) joint training of a shared feature extractor, a primary class classifier, and an auxiliary super-class classifier under a combined SSL objective (FixMatch-style consistency loss plus an auxiliary super-class consistency loss weighted by λ); and (iii) a dynamic logit adjustment that augments standard LA with a sample-specific term. For each unlabeled sample, the super-class posterior p(k|u_j) serves as a convex weight over pre-computed per-super-class adjustment vectors Δ_k, whose components are relative dominance scores β_{k,c} = n_{k,c}/max_{c'} n_{k,c'} within each group. The final adjusted logit is ℓ_j^SCAD = ℓ_j^c − (log π + Σ_k p(k|u_j) Δ_k), concentrating corrective force on the locally competing classes while leaving the global LA term intact.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-LT (γ=100, N₁=500, M₁=4000), SCAD achieves 83.5% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline FixMatch+ACR (81.6%) by 1.9%. On CIFAR-100-LT (γ=10, N₁=50, M₁=400), SCAD reaches 52.7%, surpassing FixMatch+ACR (51.3%) by 1.4%. On the large-scale ImageNet-127 benchmark, SCAD+ACR achieves 60.5% (32×32) and 67.0% (64×64), exceeding the ACR baseline by 3.3% and 3.4% respectively. SCAD also improves calibration, reducing ECE for tail classes from 0.1654 (ACR) to 0.0926 (ACR+SCAD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCAD consistently outperforms all 10 compared baselines across every dataset and setting. Against the strongest LA-based method ACR (Wei & Gan, 2023), SCAD gains +1.9% on CIFAR-10-LT (γ=100) and +3.4% on ImageNet-127 (64×64), confirming that super-class-aware local correction complements global prior estimation. Compared to DASO (Oh et al., 2022), which uses distribution-aware pseudo-labels, SCAD improves by 4.8–6.8% on CIFAR-10-LT, showing that semantic structure captures confusions that frequency-based rebalancing misses. Against CDMAD (Lee & Kim, 2024), the most recent state-of-the-art, SCAD adds +1.6% in the consistent setting and +2.3% in the reverse setting on CIFAR-10-LT (Table 4). On CIFAR-100-LT under severe distribution mismatch (γ_u=1/10), SCAD+ACR achieves 63.3% versus 62.9% for ACR alone, a modest but consistent gain. SCAD also surpasses CoSSL (Fan et al., 2022) by 2.1% on CIFAR-10-LT (γ=100) and ABC (Lee et al., 2021) by 4.0%, demonstrating plug-and-play compatibility across diverse LTSSL frameworks.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCAD extends the logit adjustment (LA) framework that has become the standard debiasing mechanism in long-tailed semi-supervised learning (LTSSL), building directly on FixMatch's consistency-regularization training loop. Prior LA-based methods (ACR, CDMAD, DASO) all refine how the global class prior is estimated but retain a single, hierarchy-agnostic correction; SCAD departs by introducing a dynamic, sample-aware local term derived from super-class structure. This super-class structure is obtained via a lightweight text-embedding + agglomerative clustering pipeline, distinguishing it from vision-based hierarchical approaches (HD-CNN, DeepRTC, SuperDisco) that require predefined taxonomies or computationally intensive GNN training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCAD identifies and addresses a concrete failure mode— intra-super-class imbalance— that global logit adjustment is structurally unable to fix, and does so with a plug-and-play module that adds ~1 second per epoch and improves multiple SOTA methods by 2–5% across diverse benchmarks. The robustness of the approach across text encoders (from GloVe to CLIP) and its compatibility with existing LTSSL pipelines (ACR, CDMAD, CoSSL, ABC, SAW) make it practically adoptable. Open questions remain: the method is validated only on classification, relies on informative class names for super-class discovery, and its extension to detection or segmentation—where both long-tail imbalance and semantic overlap are prevalent—has not been explored.
