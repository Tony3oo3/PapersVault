## Problem

Current open multimodal agents struggle with 'thinking with images' because they lack the ability to perform multi-step reasoning that requires collecting and composing subtle visual details from multiple spatially distinct regions of high-information-density images (e.g., composite charts, venue-level maps). Existing benchmarks either test perception on simple reasoning or cognition on visually trivial images, leaving the joint demand of fine-grained cross-region localization and interleaved multi-hop reasoning unaddressed. Additionally, prior visual search methods are limited to locating discrete objects in natural images rather than generalized regions described in free-form, relational, or conceptual language.

## Input / Output

The system consumes high-resolution, information-dense images (average 3967×4602 px, up to ~10K on the longer side) containing multiple heterogeneous sub-regions (charts, tables, legends, map symbols) alongside a text question with six multiple-choice options. The vSearcher agent outputs localized bounding boxes for free-form region descriptions (e.g., 'the chart showing revenue in the last decade'), while the vReasoner agent produces the final multiple-choice answer after iteratively requesting and integrating visual evidence from multiple regions. A small fraction (7.2%) of questions use a 'No Right Choice' option, requiring the model to verify that none of the presented options is valid across the entire image.

## Method

INSIGHT-O3 decomposes 'thinking with images' into a vReasoner (high-level reasoning, e.g., GPT-5-mini) and a vSearcher (visual localization, Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) that iteratively exchanges region descriptions and cropped evidence. The vSearcher is trained via a hybrid sub-agent RL algorithm: an out-of-loop component uses pre-generated region descriptions with ground-truth bounding boxes and direct IoU supervision for efficient learning, while an in-loop component uses descriptions generated on-the-fly by the vReasoner during training, with a pseudo-IoU reward combining vReasoner feedback ratings and final-answer correctness. The objective is a modified GRPO with global (rather than group) advantage normalization for the in-loop tasks, where the concept of a fixed response group does not apply to dynamically generated search tasks. Training data for in-loop RL consists of synthesized image collages (stitching multiple images so the target occupies <0.02% of canvas area) to force reliance on visual search, while out-of-loop data uses InfographicVQA layouts with GPT-5-nano-generated region descriptions. The resulting InSight-o3-vS acts as a plug-and-play vSearcher that generalizes across different vReasoner models and image resolutions, improving GPT-5-mini from 39.0% to 61.5% on O3-BENCH.

## Results

On O3-BENCH, INSIGHT-O3 with GPT-5-mini as vReasoner achieves 61.5% accuracy, a +22.5 pp improvement over GPT-5-mini standalone (39.0%) and substantially surpassing OpenAI o3 (40.8%). On V*-Bench, the same configuration reaches 86.9% (+13.1 pp over GPT-5-mini alone), and on HR-Bench4K it attains 86.7% (+14.7 pp). With Gemini-2.5-Flash as vReasoner, INSIGHT-O3 pushes O3-BENCH to 69.7% and V*-Bench to 87.6%, setting new state-of-the-art among all evaluated systems. The average score across all six benchmarks rises from 53.7% to 64.9% for GPT-5-mini and from 61.7% to 63.7% for Gemini-2.5-Flash.

## Baselines & Comparisons

INSIGHT-O3 (GPT-5-mini + InSight-o3-vS) decisively outperforms all open-source baselines: it beats Qwen3-VL-32B (the strongest open model) by +1.1 pp on O3-BENCH (61.5 vs. 60.4) and by +5.1 pp on V*-Bench (86.9 vs. 86.0), while leading by a wide margin on HR-Bench4K (86.7 vs. 81.1) and VProbe-Hard (41.2 vs. 28.6). Against proprietary systems, INSIGHT-O3 surpasses OpenAI o3 by +20.7 pp on O3-BENCH (61.5 vs. 40.8) and by +10.5 pp on V*-Bench (86.9 vs. 76.4), and exceeds GPT-4o by +33.5 pp on O3-BENCH. Among visual-search-specific systems, INSIGHT-O3 outperforms Mini-o3 by +32.4 pp on O3-BENCH (61.5 vs. 29.1) and DeepEyes by +34.5 pp (61.5 vs. 27.0), while matching or exceeding Pixel Reasoner on V*-Bench (86.9 vs. 86.3). The only notable losses are on Tree-Bench (54.1 vs. 54.6 for GPT-5-mini standalone, a −0.5 pp drop) and VProbe-Hard with Gemini-2.5-Flash (36.2 vs. 39.6 standalone, −3.4 pp), suggesting that for some vReasoners already strong at fine-grained natural-image search, the added vSearcher call can introduce minor overhead. Overall, the plug-and-play vSearcher delivers consistent gains of 7–22 pp across benchmarks and vReasoner families, confirming strong out-of-distribution generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO-based RL training paradigm (Shao et al., 2024b) to a multi-agent visual search setting, departing from prior single-agent visual search models (V*, DeepEyes, Pixel-Reasoner, Mini-o3) that focus on locating discrete objects in natural images. The key conceptual departure is 'generalized visual search'—localizing relational, fuzzy, or conceptual regions on arbitrary images (maps, charts, screenshots) described in free-form language—rather than simple object grounding. The hierarchical vReasoner/vSearcher decomposition draws on multi-agent orchestration literature (Socratic Models, HuggingGPT, MetaGPT) but applies it specifically to decouple high-level reasoning from fine-grained visual perception, with a novel hybrid in-loop/out-of-loop RL training scheme for the sub-agent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

O3-BENCH provides a genuinely hard evaluation (OpenAI o3 scores only 40.8%) that the field lacked, and the plug-and-play vSearcher offers a practical path to closing the open-vs-proprietary reasoning gap without retraining frontier models. The 22.5-point improvement for GPT-5-mini on O3-BENCH (39.0%→61.5%) and consistent gains across model families suggest the divide-and-conquer architecture is a viable design pattern for agentic multimodal systems. Key open questions include: whether jointly training both agents (rather than delegating reasoning to an external model) yields further gains, how the approach scales to video or 3D inputs, and whether the vSearcher's reliance on a specific vReasoner's description style limits true plug-and-play generality.
