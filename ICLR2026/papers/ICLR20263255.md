## Problem

Multi-instance image generation with diffusion models suffers from three coupled failures: layout guidance cannot achieve precise spatial placement of user-specified objects, identity preservation degrades sharply as the number of reference subjects increases, and no large-scale dataset provides the jointly annotated layout-and-reference pairs needed to train such systems. These gaps prevent simultaneous compositional accuracy and per-instance identity fidelity in a single generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263255/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a text prompt, a composite layout image (a diptych-style canvas where each instance is placed at its target bounding-box position, either user-drawn or auto-synthesized via an occlusion-aware sorting algorithm), and up to 8 individual high-fidelity reference images. It outputs a single RGB image in which every reference subject appears at its designated spatial location with preserved fine-grained identity (facial features, object textures, logos). The backbone is FLUX.1-Kontext fine-tuned with LoRA (Rank 512) on 4×A100 GPUs, so inference follows the standard FLUX latent-space denoising pipeline at the model's native resolution.

## Method

ContextGen builds a unified token sequence T = [text, noise-latent, layout, ref₁, …, ref_N] processed by FLUX's MM-DiT blocks, and introduces two complementary attention masks applied hierarchically across the 57 DiT layers. Contextual Layout Anchoring (CLA) operates in the front-19 and back-19 blocks, allowing broad attention among text, image, and layout tokens to enforce global spatial structure. Identity Consistency Attention (ICA) is applied selectively in the middle-19 blocks, where a bounding-box-aware mask forces each query token inside instance n's region to attend to its own reference tokens R_n, thereby injecting fine-grained identity details without cross-instance leakage. An instance-wise position indexing scheme assigns each conditioning image a unique non-overlapping (1, W_n+i, H_n+j) RoPE coordinate so the transformer can distinguish tokens from different references. Training proceeds in two stages: supervised LoRA fine-tuning on the three-tier IMIG-100K dataset (basic composition, complex interaction, flexible reference), followed by DPO (β=1000) to improve background richness and text-visual alignment while controlling identity degradation.

## Results

On LAMICBench++, ContextGen achieves the highest overall average score of 64.66, surpassing Nano Banana (64.11), GPT-4o (63.71), and FLUX.1-Kontext (63.33). The method leads in object preservation (IPS: 81.23 fewer subjects / 73.35 more subjects) and facial identity retention (IDS: 35.86 / 30.42), with the advantage amplifying as subject count increases. On COCO-MIG, ContextGen achieves the highest Success Rate (33.12%) and mIoU (65.12%), outperforming the next-best MIGC (27.75% SR) by +5.4% SR and +5.9% mIoU. On LayoutSAM-Eval, it attains the top scores in color fidelity (87.44) and texture accuracy (89.26).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ContextGen outperforms all 17 baselines on the overall LAMICBench++ average (64.66), beating the strongest open-source model FLUX.1-Kontext (63.33) by +1.33 and both closed-source models GPT-4o (63.71) and Nano Banana (64.11). The identity advantage is most pronounced in the 'More Subjects' (≥4 references) setting, where ContextGen's IDS of 30.42 exceeds Nano Banana (16.67) by +13.75 and GPT-4o (17.12) by +13.30, while its IPS of 73.35 leads all methods. On COCO-MIG, ContextGen achieves SR 33.12% and mIoU 65.12%, surpassing the next-best MIGC (27.75% SR, 56.96% mIoU) and EliGen (26.00% SR, 59.23% mIoU), with the gap widening at higher instance counts (L4–L6). On LayoutSAM-Eval, ContextGen leads in color (87.44 vs. EliGen's 83.84) and texture (89.26 vs. EliGen's 87.31) fidelity, though EliGen edges it slightly on spatial accuracy (94.05 vs. 93.96). Among subject-driven baselines, OmniGen2 (61.08) and DreamO (57.31) show significant identity degradation with more subjects, while MIP-Adapter (51.28) and XVerse (50.29) collapse in IDS below 40. The paper also demonstrates that adapting MS-Diffusion's Grounding Resampler to the FLUX backbone (MS-FLUX) yields near-complete failure (SR 0.38%, IDS 3.20), confirming that ContextGen's CLA+ICA design is specifically suited to the FLUX-DiT architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

ContextGen builds directly on FLUX.1-Kontext's in-context DiT architecture and its ternary RoPE position encoding, extending the diptych-based conditioning paradigm (from in-context editing work like Song et al. 2025) to a multi-instance setting with explicit layout control. It departs from prior multi-instance methods (OmniGen2, MS-Diffusion, LAMIC) that process reference images as a flat token sequence by introducing a hierarchical attention architecture: Contextual Layout Anchoring (CLA) in front/back DiT blocks for global spatial structure, and Identity Consistency Attention (ICA) in middle blocks for instance-level detail transfer. The position indexing strategy generalizes FLUX.1-Kontext's editing-token mechanism to systematically separate multiple auxiliary images via cumulative spatial offsets, and the controlled MS-FLUX experiment demonstrates that prior methods' architectural choices (e.g., MS-Diffusion's Grounding Resampler) do not transfer across backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap where layout control and identity preservation have been treated as separate problems; ContextGen's unified token sequence with specialized attention masks offers a reusable architectural pattern for combining spatial and identity constraints in DiT-based generation. The IMIG-100K dataset and LAMICBench++ benchmark fill a notable data and evaluation void for image-guided multi-instance generation. A key open question is the fidelity-flexibility trade-off: the model's strong identity anchoring can reduce adaptability to complex text prompts describing inter-subject interactions, and the authors acknowledge this as a limitation requiring more dynamic attention mechanisms. The finding that MS-Diffusion's resampler fails catastrophically on FLUX-DiT (SR 0.38 vs. 4.50 on SDXL) is a practically important caution for the community about backbone-specific architectural compatibility.
