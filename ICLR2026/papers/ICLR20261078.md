## Problem

While safety alignment for image-based LLMs has been extensively benchmarked and defended, video-based LLMs remain critically under-examined, with no large-scale real-world safety benchmark or effective post-training defense framework. The paper reveals that integrating the video modality degrades safety performance by an average of 34.2% across 21 Video LLMs, exposing a systemic vulnerability where dynamic visual content undermines the text branch's safety alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261078/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes video clips (up to 360 seconds, sampled at 1 fps or 16 frames depending on the base model) paired with a text query, and produces a structured completion containing a chain-of-thought safety analysis within  tags, binary dual-modality safety labels (<vidType> for visual harmfulness, <textType> for textual toxicity), and a final safe response within <answer> tags. Learnable alarm tokens are appended to both the visual token sequence (H_vis) and the textual token sequence (H_txt) before processing, and the model is evaluated via Qwen-Long API for Defense Success Rate and a 0–5 Helpfulness Score.

## Method

VideoSafety-R1 is a dual-stage post-training framework built on top of existing Video LLMs (VideoLLaMA3-2B, Qwen2.5 VL-3B/7B). Stage 1 (AT-SFT) injects modality-specific learnable alarm token embeddings into visual and textual sequences and trains them via a multi-task loss combining binary alarm-token classification (harmful/safe per modality) with the standard autoregressive objective, explicitly pre-activating harm perception. Stage 2 (Safety-guided GRPO) performs reinforcement learning with a composite rule-based reward that sums format compliance, ROUGE-based response quality, and dual-modality classification correctness, where a Dynamic Reward Adaptation mechanism modulates the ROUGE weight inversely to classification accuracy—tightening response constraints when safety detection fails and relaxing them when both modalities are correctly classified. The training data (VideoSafetyThinking, 46k triplets) is partitioned into 6k for AT-SFT, 15k for CoT cold-start, and 25k for GRPO reinforcement, with structured prompt templates generating thinking chains that propagate safety signals across both modalities.

## Results

On VSE-HH, VideoSafety-R1 achieves 89.5% DSR on VideoLLaMA3-2B (a 71.1% absolute gain over the 18.4% baseline), 91.7% on Qwen2.5 VL-3B (+37.7%), and 95.4% on Qwen2.5 VL-7B (+38.1%). On image safety benchmarks, the method gains +59.1% on MMBench, +44.3% on VLGuard, and +15.0% on FigStep (VideoLLaMA3-2B), while maintaining low false refusal rates (0.8%–13.4% on VSE-SafeQ). Across 21 Video LLMs, the paper reveals that introducing video modality degrades DSR by an average of 34.2%, with VideoLLaMA3-2B suffering the largest drop (79.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest image-based DPO baseline SPA-VL (99.2% MMBench, 97.0% VLGuard, 98.8% FigStep), VideoSafety-R1 on Qwen2.5 VL-7B achieves 98.0%/99.6%/87.0% respectively, trailing on FigStep by 11.8% but matching or exceeding on VSE-HH (95.4% vs 86.9%) while delivering substantially higher helpfulness (S_Help 3.4 vs 2.3). VLGuard achieves near-perfect DSR (100%) but suffers catastrophic over-refusal (99.8% FRR on VSE-SafeQ) and the lowest helpfulness (S_Help 1.1–2.7), whereas VideoSafety-R1 maintains FRR below 13.4% and S_Help of 3.4–4.6. MM-RLHF (72.5% VSE-HH, 80.2% MMBench) is outperformed by VideoSafety-R1 on VSE-HH by 22.9% (Qwen2.5 VL-7B) and on MMBench by 17.8%, though MM-RLHF edges ahead on FigStep helpfulness. Self-Reminder, a training-free prompt-based method, achieves only 65.2% DSR on VSE-HH, well below VideoSafety-R1's 95.4%. On the unmodified VideoLLaMA3-2B baseline (18.4% VSE-HH), VideoSafety-R1 delivers the paper's headline 71.1% absolute DSR gain, the largest improvement reported across all model-architecture combinations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image-based multimodal safety alignment line (SafeVLM, SPA-VL, MM-RLHF, VLGuard) to the video domain, arguing that temporal dynamics and cross-modality interactions introduce qualitatively different risks that static-image defenses do not address. Methodologically, it builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for the reinforcement learning stage and applies the framework to VideoLLaMA3 and Qwen2.5-VL as base architectures. The key departure is the introduction of modality-specific alarm tokens (AT-SFT) as a pre-activation mechanism before RL, combined with a Dynamic Reward Adaptation scheme that modulates ROUGE weighting based on dual-modality classification correctness—neither of which appears in prior image-safety or video-understanding work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic evaluation of 21 Video LLMs revealing a 34.2% average DSR degradation upon video modality integration is a practically important warning for the field, and the 11.4k-sample VSE benchmark fills a clear gap in video safety evaluation infrastructure. The framework's cross-architecture generalization (VideoLLaMA3, Qwen2.5-VL) and transfer to image safety benchmarks (MMBench, VLGuard, FigStep) suggest broad applicability. However, open questions remain: performance on videos beyond 360 seconds is untested, the LLM-based evaluation reliability under complex semantic attacks is acknowledged as uncertain, and the reasoning chains are template-generated rather than organically produced, limiting response naturalness. The paper also notes that more sophisticated semantically-driven attacks (e.g., temporally unsafe tool use, composite risks) remain unexplored.
