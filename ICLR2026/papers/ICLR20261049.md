## Problem

Existing sparse attention methods for long-context LLM inference enforce fixed or statically allocated token budgets that cannot adapt to the significant variation in attention sparsity across heads, layers, and individual query tokens. This mismatch causes either excessive KV-cache loading (wasting memory bandwidth on streaming heads) or insufficient token selection (introducing large approximation errors on retrieval heads), leading to inconsistent accuracy degradation. No prior calibration-free method dynamically determines the token budget based on the actual attention score distribution at inference time.

## Input / Output

During prefill, Tactic consumes the full sequence of key vectors to perform K-means clustering (one-time cost, <7% of prefill time). During decode, it takes the current query vector and only loads cluster centroids plus a small sampled subset (~2.5% of KV cache) to determine which tokens to select, then loads only those selected tokens' KV pairs for the actual attention computation. The output is a GQA-aware sparse attention result: for each KV head, the union of tokens selected across all grouped query heads sharing that KV head is loaded once, and subrequests are formed per KV head to balance workload across FlashInfer's request-level scheduling.

## Method

Tactic replaces fixed token budgets with a cumulative attention score threshold P: tokens are selected in descending order of attention score until their cumulative softmax scores reach fraction P of the total, providing a theoretical error bound of ε(I) ≤ 2(1−P)·max‖v_i‖ that is tight because value vector norms are empirically consistent. To avoid computing all n attention scores, Tactic uses K-means clustering on key vectors (cluster size 16) during prefill to group similar tokens, then ranks clusters by their centroid–query dot product during decode to obtain a partial sort. A lightweight inverse function y = a/x + b is fitted to the attention score distribution using two sampled windows (at 10% and 60% of the sorted list), enabling Tactic to estimate the cumulative sum and determine the exact stopping point without evaluating every token; the first 1–2% of tokens (high-score outliers) are computed exactly. The clustering and distribution fitting form a self-correcting loop: poor clustering produces a flatter fitted curve, which automatically increases the token budget to compensate.

## Results

On RULER (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct), Tactic at a 75% cumulative attention score threshold achieves 84.7 average accuracy across 16K–96K context lengths, compared to 86.8 for full attention, while Quest, PyramidKV, Ada-SnapKV, and MagicPig score only 69.2, 63.1, 59.2, and 74.0 respectively. Tactic achieves up to 7.29× decode attention speedup and 1.58× end-to-end inference speedup over full attention. On LongBench, Tactic at 90% threshold reaches near-full-attention accuracy (e.g., 53.57 on HotpotQA vs. 54.99 full), while baselines lag by 5–15 points. Tactic also achieves >95% token acceptance rate in speculative decoding, substantially surpassing all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tactic consistently outperforms all four baselines across both RULER and LongBench benchmarks. On RULER (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, 75% threshold), Tactic achieves 84.7 average accuracy, beating MagicPig (74.0) by 10.7 points, Quest (69.2) by 15.5 points, PyramidKV (63.1) by 21.6 points, and Ada-SnapKV (59.2) by 25.5 points. The gap narrows at the 90% threshold where Tactic (84.6) still leads Quest (79.5) by 5.1 points and MagicPig (74.7) by 9.9 points, approaching full attention (86.8). On LongBench, Tactic at 90% threshold matches or exceeds full attention on several tasks (e.g., TriviaQA 90.61 vs. 89.22 full), while Quest drops to 49.37 on HotpotQA versus Tactic's 53.57. Tactic also achieves the lowest KL-divergence from full attention and the highest speculative decoding acceptance rate (>95%), confirming its superior output fidelity across all comparison settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

Tactic sits within the sparse-attention / KV-cache-compression line of work that includes Quest (fixed per-head token budget with positional grouping), PyramidKV and Ada-KV (calibration-based static budget allocation across layers/heads), and MagicPig (dynamic LSH-based selection). It departs from all of these by replacing the fixed token budget with a cumulative-attention-score target, which naturally adapts to sparsity variations without calibration data. The two key technical departures are (1) K-means clustering on key-vector similarity instead of positional windows for token grouping, and (2) a lightweight inverse-function distribution fit to estimate per-token attention scores and determine the stopping point, rather than simply counting to a pre-set budget.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The calibration-free, sparsity-adaptive design makes Tactic a practical drop-in for production long-context serving where per-head/layer tuning is infeasible, and the 7.29× decode-attention speedup (1.58× end-to-end) is meaningful for latency-sensitive deployments. The theoretical error bound ε(I) ≤ 2(1−p(I))·max‖v_i‖ provides a principled guarantee absent in prior fixed-budget methods. Open questions include how clustering quality degrades over very long generation horizons (requiring periodic re-clustering), whether the inverse-function fit generalizes to architectures with markedly different attention distributions, and how the method interacts with other inference optimizations such as quantized KV caches or multi-request batching at scale.
