## Problem

Population-adaptive Learning-to-Defer methods (e.g., L2D-Pop) meta-learn expert representations from few-shot context to condition a deferral rejector, but they process class-indexed signals in fixed coordinates, creating identity-conditioned shortcuts that violate the problem's inherent permutation symmetry. This architectural flaw causes systematic performance degradation when test-time experts are out-of-distribution relative to the training population, such as under class reindexing or shifted competence profiles. The paper identifies this symmetry violation as the root cause and proposes an architecture that enforces the correct inductive bias by construction.

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB image and a few-shot context of an unseen expert's past predictions (triples of image, ground-truth label, and expert prediction) as input. The classifier produces a standard K-way softmax over class scores, while the rejector consumes only a 6-dimensional role-indexed state—model confidence at the model-top and expert-top classes, plus the expert's posterior mean and variance at each of those two roles—never seeing absolute class identities or the raw context images. The output is either the model's predicted class or a deferral routed to the expert with the largest deferral margin, with the operating point controlled by a budget sweep over the deferral threshold.

## Method

Identity-Free Deferral (IFD) replaces the neural-process encoder of L2D-Pop with an explicit, query-independent Bayesian per-class competence profile built via Beta-Binomial conjugacy from the few-shot context, yielding closed-form posterior means and variances per class. A 6-dimensional role-indexed state is then constructed by reading values only at structural roles (the model's top class and the expert's estimated best class) plus symmetric aggregates, ensuring the rejector's input is provably invariant to coherent class relabellings. A small MLP maps this state to a deferral logit, and the system is trained with a novel uncertainty-aware, context-only surrogate loss that weights deferral supervision by a lower-confidence bound on the expert's accuracy and activates only when the expert's peak class matches the true label, eliminating the need for query-time expert annotations. The paper formally proves permutation invariance of the role-indexed state and rejector, contrasts this with a generic non-invariance theorem for standard L2D-Pop parameterisations, and derives the asymptotic deferral rule, which compares model confidence to the expert's peak-class competence rather than their expected accuracy across all classes.

## Results

IFD is evaluated on four datasets (HAM10000, Blood Cells, LITS, ImageNet-16H) using AURSAC (SAC) and AURDAC (DAC) as headline metrics across ID and OOD expert settings. The largest gains appear with Variable (instance-dependent) experts under OOD shifts: IFD achieves SAC of 0.78 on Liver tumours OOD vs. 0.68 for both L2D-Pop variants (+0.10), and 0.80 on Blood Cells OOD vs. 0.74 (+0.06). On Stable experts, all methods are within 0.01–0.02 SAC of each other. On ImageNet-16H with real human annotators under increasing input noise (95→125), IFD maintains a consistent +0.02 to +0.03 SAC advantage over L2D-Pop(QC) in OOD settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IFD is compared against L2D-Pop (both Query-Conditioned and Query-Independent variants, Tailor et al. 2024) and Multi-Expert L2D (Verma et al. 2023, ID-only). Against L2D-Pop, IFD wins on SAC in 15 out of 18 ID/OOD settings and on DAC in 16 out of 18, with the largest margins on Variable experts under OOD shifts (up to +0.10 SAC on Liver tumours, +0.06 on Blood Cells). L2D-Pop(QC) and L2D-Pop(QI) show a sharper OOD drop-off relative to ID (e.g., Blood Cells Variable: SAC drops from 0.78/0.77 ID to 0.74/0.74 OOD), while IFD degrades more gracefully (0.81→0.80). Against Multi-Expert L2D (which cannot handle unseen experts and is only reported for ID), IFD consistently leads by 0.02–0.10 SAC, confirming the value of population-adaptive methods over fixed-expert baselines. IFD also requires substantially fewer annotations: it uses only context labels (Σ N_C^E per expert) whereas L2D-Pop requires E×N query-time expert labels, yielding large savings when N ≫ N_C^E (e.g., Blood Cells: N=11,959 vs. N_C^E=120).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Learning to Defer (L2D) framework (Madras et al., 2018; Mozannar & Sontag, 2021) and its population-adaptive variant L2D-Pop (Tailor et al., 2024), which meta-learns expert representations via Conditional or Attentive Neural Processes. The key departure is architectural: instead of learning an unstructured latent embedding from class-indexed context, IFD constructs an explicit Beta-Binomial per-class competence profile and exposes only a low-dimensional, role-indexed state (model-top confidence, expert-top skill, symmetric aggregates) to the rejector, provably enforcing permutation invariance. The training objective is also novel in being context-only and uncertainty-weighted via lower confidence bounds, eliminating the need for query-time expert labels that prior population-adaptive methods require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of identity-conditioned shortcuts as the root cause of OOD failure in population-adaptive L2D provides a clean architectural diagnosis, and the formal proof of generic non-invariance for standard encoders (Theorem 3.1) gives a principled justification for the role-indexed design. Practically, the context-only training eliminates a significant annotation bottleneck, and the Beta prior offers a natural interface for incorporating expert self-assessments. However, the class-level abstraction inherently misses instance-level competence variation, and the formal invariance guarantees cover only coherent relabellings rather than the weaker, incoherent shifts that may arise in practice—leaving open whether the architectural certificate translates to robustness under more complex OOD perturbations.
