## Problem

Uniform-state discrete diffusion models (USDMs) excel at few-step generation and guidance but their sampling quality plateaus with ancestral samplers as the number of function evaluations increases, unlike masked diffusion models (MDMs) whose ReMDM predictor-corrector samplers continue to improve. Predictor-corrector methods for uniform-state diffusion with arbitrary noise priors remained underexplored, and the existing curriculum training strategy for USDMs was computationally expensive, requiring materialization of full K-dimensional latent vectors at every training step.

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of L one-hot encoded discrete tokens (over a vocabulary of size K) progressively corrupted by either a uniform prior (π=1/K) or a masked prior (π=[MASK]), along with an optional class label for classifier-free guidance in image generation. It outputs a clean text sequence (context length 1024 for language, GPT-2 or BERT tokenizer) or a 32×32 RGB image (CIFAR-10). During the curriculum training phase, the transformer input is not a discrete token embedding but a softmax-relaxed linear combination of K vocabulary embeddings, approximated using only the top-k (k≪K) entries via order-statistic sampling.

## Method

The core contribution is the Ψ-posterior, a non-Markovian generative transition defined as a linear superposition Ψ_{s|t} = κ_t · q_{s|t} + (1−κ_t) · q_s, where q_{s|t} is the standard reverse posterior (predictor) and q_s is the forward marginal (corrector). This preserves the correct marginals of the Markovian diffusion process while injecting controllable noise that enables error correction: for MDMs it allows previously decoded tokens to be remasked, and for USDMs it guarantees every token retains non-zero sampling probability. The framework subsumes ReMDM, Campbell et al., and Gat et al. as special cases (κ_t = 1 − σ_t/(1−α_s) for masked diffusion) and extends them to arbitrary priors. For training, the paper introduces a memory-efficient curriculum that exploits the sparsity of the low-temperature (τ=10⁻³) softmax: instead of materializing the full K-dimensional Gaussian latent, it samples only the top-k order statistics via inverse CDF transforms and Floyd's algorithm for index selection, reducing peak memory by 33% and training time by 25% while matching Duo's perplexity.

## Results

On OpenWebText, Duo++ (k=2) achieves a validation perplexity of 25.2, matching Duo (25.2) while using 25% fewer GPU-hours and 33% less peak memory. On LM1B, Duo++ (k=2) reaches 30.0 PPL, comparable to Duo (29.9). With Ψ-samplers, Duo++ outperforms MDLM+ReMDM in generative perplexity across the full NFE range on OpenWebText, and achieves better FID and IS than MDLM (with both ancestral sampling and ReMDM) on CIFAR-10. Unlike ancestral sampling, which plateaus, Ψ-samplers continue to improve with more sampling steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MDLM (Sahoo et al., 2024), Duo++ has higher validation perplexity (25.2 vs. 23.2 on OWT; 30.0 vs. 27.0 on LM1B), but with Ψ-samplers, Duo++ outperforms MDLM+ReMDM in generative perplexity across the entire NFE range on OpenWebText and achieves better FID/IS on CIFAR-10, closing the gap at high NFEs where ancestral sampling plateaus. Against Duo (Sahoo et al., 2025a), Duo++ matches perplexity exactly on OWT (25.2 vs. 25.2) and is within 0.1 on LM1B (30.0 vs. 29.9), while reducing peak memory by 33% and training time by 25%. Against SEDD (Austin et al., 2023), Duo++ substantially outperforms both the Absorb variant (25.2 vs. 24.1 on OWT) and the Uniform variant (25.2 vs. 29.7 on OWT). On downstream MCQ tasks (Table 1), Duo++ (k=2) slightly exceeds Duo on 4 of 7 tasks (e.g., Winogrande 49.64 vs. 47.20, PIQA 52.12 vs. 51.14) but trails MDLM on most tasks, consistent with its higher perplexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Diffusion Duality framework (Sahoo et al., 2025a) by introducing Ψ-posteriors — a linear superposition of the forward and reverse diffusion posteriors — that generalize ReMDM's predictor-corrector samplers (Wang et al., 2025) from masked diffusion to arbitrary noise priors, including uniform-state diffusion. It subsumes earlier CTMC-based PC methods (Campbell et al., 2022; Gat et al., 2024) as special cases while providing a unified theoretical treatment. The efficient curriculum builds on the Gaussian relaxation training of Duo but exploits the sparsity of low-temperature softmax via order-statistics sampling to avoid materializing full vocabulary-sized vectors, reducing memory by 33% and training time by 25%.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that Ψ-samplers close the high-NFE performance gap between uniform-state and masked diffusion language models, challenging the prevailing narrative that masked diffusion is the inevitable architecture for diffusion-based text generation. The finding that USDMs' ancestral sampler plateaus while Ψ-samplers continue to improve with more steps suggests the bottleneck was the sampler, not the model class. Open questions include whether these gains persist at multi-billion-parameter scale, whether the 25% training speedup holds with larger vocabularies (K > 100k), and whether the Ψ-posterior framework extends cleanly to the more general discrete diffusion processes with mixed noise schedules.
