## Problem

Existing LLM unlearning methods focus on designing optimizers (gradient ascent, KL regularization, preference-based losses) but implicitly assume that forget and retain sets are already provided. In practice, unlearning is triggered by a single undesired generation at inference time, leaving practitioners with only that output and a massive training corpus, making data retrieval the true bottleneck. The paper formalizes this gap through the notion of data Pareto improvement, showing that retrieval quality is a first-order determinant of the achievable forgetting–retention trade-off frontier rather than a preprocessing detail.

## Input / Output

Given a target generation y (the undesired output that triggers unlearning), the full training corpus X, and the model's per-example gradients {g_x}, RASLIK produces two disjoint subsets: a forget set F of samples whose gradients align with the query gradient q_y = ∇_θ ℓ(y;θ), and a retain set R of samples whose gradients anti-align. The retrieval operates entirely in a k-dimensional sketch space (k = 65,536 in experiments, versus d ≈ 8.4M for OLMo-7B with LoRA), and the antipodal structure means the retain set is obtained by a single sign flip of the query sketch, incurring zero additional retrieval cost.

## Method

RASLIK constructs randomized low-dimensional sketches of each training gradient via permutation-projection hashing: k random Rademacher vectors project the d-dimensional gradient to k coordinates, a fixed permutation bins them, and L2 normalization yields an unbiased cosine-similarity estimator with variance O(1/k). Retrieval is then exact maximum inner product search in sketch space for the forget set, with the retain set obtained by the antipodal query h(−q_y) = −h(q_y). The key theoretical insight is that the sketching noise acts as a controlled smoothing of the otherwise discontinuous thresholding decision at the boundary, provably reducing the variance of the GA_GDR update direction Δ(F,R) by a term cΛ/k relative to exact influence-kernel thresholding. This yields a double gain: sublinear O(|X|k) retrieval complexity (a >150× speedup over full-gradient cosine) and strictly lower mean-squared error to the true unlearning gradient, translating into more stable and effective unlearning across models, datasets, and optimization algorithms.

## Results

Across all eight experimental blocks (2 models × 2 unlearning algorithms × 2 datasets), RASLIK sits on the (R↑, F↓) Pareto frontier and typically pushes it outward relative to all baselines. On Howdy-Alpaca with OLMo-2-1124-7B under GA_GDR, RASLIK achieves F=0.272, R=0.555, D_mah=9.813, and Non-SF=0.911, attaining the best retention, Mahalanobis distance, and style-suppression score simultaneously. On Virtual-Alpaca with Pythia-2.8B under GA_KLR, RASLIK reaches F=0.160, R=0.247, D_mah=27.670, again ranking among the two lowest Mahalanobis distances. In terms of efficiency, RASLIK retrieval completes in 42 seconds per query versus 6,480 seconds for the full unsketched influence kernel, a >150× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RASLIK outperforms all four baselines on the Pareto frontier: no other method simultaneously achieves lower F and higher R. Compared to BM25 (F=0.282, R=0.460, D_mah=11.181), RASLIK improves retention by 9.5 percentage points while slightly increasing F, and reduces D_mah by 12%. Against Embedding Similarity (F=0.236, R=0.485, D_mah=10.167), RASLIK trades a small F increase (0.272 vs 0.236) for a substantial R gain (0.555 vs 0.485) and a lower D_mah (9.813 vs 10.167). Oracle Sampling achieves the lowest F (0.239) but at the cost of the worst R (0.418) and a higher D_mah (11.083), making it dominated by RASLIK on the joint (R, F) plane. The ablation RASLIK-F (random retain set) consistently ranks behind full RASLIK, confirming that antipodal retain-set selection is essential. On the TOFU benchmark (Appendix C.5), RASLIK similarly achieves the lowest D_mah under GA_KLR for both models (e.g., 32.84 vs 33.33 for Oracle on OLMo-7B), and on the 13B model (Table 11) it attains the best D_mah (34.67) with competitive F (0.24), confirming scalability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the influence-function framework of Koh & Liang (2017), reducing the second-order influence kernel to a first-order cosine-similarity score (the 'Linearized Influence Kernel'), and on the GA_GDR unlearning objective of Jang et al. (2022), whose update direction motivates the antipodal (aligned-for-forget, anti-aligned-for-retain) retrieval strategy. It departs from prior influence-based retrieval methods (TracIn, DataInf, RapidIn) in two key ways: (i) it retrieves both positively and negatively aligned samples simultaneously via sign-flipped antipodal queries in a shared sketch space, and (ii) it introduces randomized permutation-projection hashing to make the retrieval scalable and variance-reducing. The 'data Pareto improvement' framing is a new conceptual lens that positions retrieval quality as a first-order determinant of unlearning outcomes rather than a preprocessing detail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes LLM unlearning from an optimization-centric to a retrieval-centric problem, providing a principled and scalable retrieval mechanism (150× speedup over full influence kernels) that practitioners can plug into existing unlearning pipelines. The finding that moderate randomized noise in retrieval can match or slightly exceed oracle sampling is a useful conceptual insight, though the practical gains over oracle are modest and demonstrated on relatively small models (2.8B–13B). Open questions include whether the variance-reduction benefit of randomization persists at much larger scales (100B+), whether the antipodal sketch approach extends to multi-turn or multi-target unlearning, and how sensitive the method is to the choice of sketch dimension k and thresholds in production settings.
