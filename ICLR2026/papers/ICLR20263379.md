## Problem

Current Multi-Agent Discussion (MAD) frameworks suffer from discussion inconsistency, where LLM instances fail to reach coherent consensus because their pre-assigned static contexts are rigid, incomplete, and lack explicit guidance on how to fuse information exchanged among agents. This paper asks: how can we dynamically generate and evolve context instructions per discussion round so that multi-LLM discussion is continually steered toward a correct consensus without premature convergence on majority noise?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263379/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a task description P, N LLM instances (e.g., Llama-2 or Qwen-2.5 variants), and a predefined pool of 100 diverse initial context prompts generated by GPT-4o. A lightweight T5-small context generator (512-dim output vectors) consumes the task goal, the agent's initial instruction, and the concatenated responses of all other LLMs from the previous round to produce a refined per-round instruction. After up to 8 discussion rounds, the final output is a consensus answer obtained by majority vote over the LLMs' final-round responses.

## Method

M2CL employs a two-stage design grounded in a theoretical activation-distance bound (Theorem 4.1). For initialization, it selects N contexts from the pool whose projected activations best reconstruct the problem's sentence embedding, yielding near-orthogonal directions that cover complementary solution perspectives; a distilled lightweight projector F avoids computing full attention activations at inference. For evolution, each agent's T5-small generator is trained with a Lagrangian dual objective that balances two terms: minimizing the activation gap between the current instruction and the agent's own previous response (promoting temporal coherence), and penalizing drift from the initial context (preserving diversity). The dual variable α is self-adaptively updated per round—decreasing early to accelerate convergence and stabilizing later to prevent premature consensus—eliminating the need for manual tuning. A key design choice is a decoupled per-LLM criterion that uses each agent's own prior response rather than other agents' simultaneously-optimized contexts, provably bounding the inter-LLM activation divergence and avoiding stale-snapshot bias.

## Results

On 9 benchmarks with 4 Qwen-7B LLMs, M2CL achieves 88.7% on MMLU, 40.8% on MATH, 58.4% on GPQA, 76.8% on HumanEval, 36.7% on ALFWorld, 41.7% on SciWorld, 30.2% on GAIA, and 33.0% on PDDL, outperforming the strongest baseline by 14–69 percentage points depending on the task. The method delivers >20% average accuracy gains with less than 10% runtime overhead. Scaling to 64 LLMs further improves performance (e.g., 95.6% MMLU with Llama-70B), demonstrating a favorable multi-agent scaling law where gains grow logarithmically before saturation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M2CL consistently outperforms all four MAD baselines (Debate, DyLAN, GPTSwarm, MacNet) across all 9 datasets. The margin is largest on complex reasoning tasks: M2CL reaches 40.8% on MATH vs. 24.1% (GPTSwarm) and 23.3% (DyLAN), and 58.4% on GPQA vs. 37.2% (GPTSwarm) and 34.2% (DyLAN). On agentic tasks, M2CL leads by 6–10 points over the best baseline (e.g., 30.2% GAIA vs. 20.6% Debate; 41.7% SciWorld vs. 33.8% MacNet). Notably, Best-of-N sampling is competitive with or exceeds several MAD baselines on agentic tasks (e.g., 62.5% Code ties DyLAN), revealing that fixed-context multi-agent methods fail to converge and thus do not surpass simple parallel sampling. M2CL's learned context evolution overcomes this limitation, achieving 76.8% on Code and 88.7% on MMLU—substantially above both BoN and all MAD baselines. The learned context generators also transfer across model families (Llama-2, Qwen-2.5, Qwen2.5-VL) without retraining, maintaining consistent improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of multi-agent discussion (MAD) frameworks (Debate, DyLAN, GPTSwarm, MacNet) and context learning for LLMs. Prior MAD methods assign static, manually-crafted role contexts to each LLM, while prior context-learning work (Self-Refine, ProRefine, dynamic prompt optimization) operates in single-LLM settings. M2CL departs from both by learning lightweight per-agent context generators (T5-small) that dynamically evolve instructions each discussion round, grounded in a theoretical bound (Theorem 4.1) showing that both diverse initialization (near-orthogonal activations) and coherent evolution (reducing inter-LLM activation distance) are necessary for consensus. The self-adaptive Lagrangian dual mechanism for balancing exploration vs. convergence is a novel formulation not present in prior MAD or prompt-optimization literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies context misalignment as a root cause of MAD failure and provides a principled, theoretically-motivated fix that is practical (T5-small generators, <10% runtime overhead, transferable across Llama/Qwen architectures). The 20–50% gains over baselines are substantial, though partly inflated by comparison to static-context methods; the more meaningful comparison is against Best-of-N, where M2CL still leads consistently. Key open questions include whether the MAD framework's computational cost (many LLM instances) can be reduced, whether learned contexts can enable true agent specialization rather than mere consensus, and whether the approach extends to asynchronous or heterogeneous-agent settings. The reliance on a GPT-4o-generated context pool and the need for 20% of questions for training also limit immediate applicability to zero-shot or data-scarce scenarios.
