## Problem

Mamba-based visual models (e.g., VMamba, Vision Mamba) rely on fixed scanning patterns such as raster or Hilbert curves that treat all image patches identically, implicitly assuming uniform information density. This content-agnostic traversal wastes computation on low-salience regions while providing no mechanism to prioritize semantically rich areas, limiting both efficiency and representational quality at high resolutions.

## Input / Output

InfoScan consumes standard RGB images (224×224 for classification, 512×512 for segmentation and detection) and produces task-specific outputs: class labels, pixel-wise segmentation masks, or bounding boxes with masks. Unconventionally, the input is not processed in a fixed spatial order; instead, a learned policy dynamically determines the 1D sequence in which patches are fed into the state-space backbone, and the patch size itself is optimized per-image via a cost function balancing computational latency against information loss.

## Method

InfoScan introduces the Visual Information State Space (VISS) block, which replaces the standard SS2D component with three coupled modules: (1) an Information Scoring Module that computes a composite patch score from normalized Shannon entropy (global color diversity) and local pixel variance (texture complexity) with fixed weights ω₁=0.6, ω₂=0.4; (2) a Patch Selection Block that determines optimal patch size by minimizing a joint cost of efficiency (power-law timing model) and U-shaped information loss; and (3) a Path Planning Module that formulates scanning as a Markov Decision Process over an n×n patch grid, where a lightweight CNN policy (trained with PPO) selects the next 4-connected neighbor at each step. The reward function combines an adaptive revisitation incentive (slower decay for high-salience patches), an exploration bonus for unvisited regions, and a neighborhood information gain term, enabling the agent to revisit informative areas periodically while ensuring broad coverage. This content-aware, reward-driven scanning policy is the primary driver of gains over fixed or repeated scanning patterns, as confirmed by ablations showing that mechanical repetition yields diminishing returns while learned adaptive routing consistently outperforms all static baselines.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (224×224), InfoScan-S achieves 84.64% Top-1 accuracy with only 24M parameters and 4.8G FLOPs, surpassing VMamba-S (83.24%, 50M) by +1.4% and DeiT-S (74.70%, 22M) by +9.9%. On MSCOCO2017 object detection, InfoScan-B attains the best AP^box (49.8%) and AP^mask (44.7%) among all tested backbones while using 78M parameters—30M fewer than Swin-B (107M) and ConvNeXt-B (108M). On ADE-20K semantic segmentation, InfoScan-S reaches 45.8% mIoU with 38M parameters, matching UperNet-ResNet-101 (45.2%) with 56% fewer parameters, and on BraTS-2021 it achieves 22.3% mIoU, outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DeiT (Touvron et al., 2021), InfoScan-S outperforms DeiT-S by +9.9% Top-1 (84.64% vs. 74.70%) with comparable parameters (24M vs. 22M), and InfoScan-B beats DeiT-B by +5.1% (85.19% vs. 80.11%) with 55% fewer parameters. Against Swin Transformer (Liu et al., 2021), InfoScan-S surpasses Swin-S by +1.4% Top-1 (84.64% vs. 83.23%) with half the parameters (24M vs. 50M), and InfoScan-B exceeds Swin-B by +1.3% (85.19% vs. 83.91%) with 57% fewer parameters. Against VMamba (Liu et al., 2024), InfoScan consistently wins at every scale: +0.96% (T), +1.40% (S), and +0.87% (B) Top-1, while using 11M–51M fewer parameters. Against ConvNeXt (Liu et al., 2022) on MSCOCO2017 detection, InfoScan-B achieves +2.6% AP^box (49.8% vs. 47.2%) and +2.3% AP^mask (44.7% vs. 42.4%) with 30M fewer parameters. Against Vision Mamba (Zhu et al., 2024) on segmentation, InfoScan-S beats Vim-S by +0.5% mIoU on ADE-20K (45.8% vs. 45.3%) and +1.9% on BraTS-2021 (22.3% vs. 20.4%) with 8M fewer parameters. Against ResNet-50 (Koonce, 2021) on segmentation, InfoScan-S matches or exceeds ResNet-101 (45.2%/20.7%) on both ADE-20K and BraTS-2021 with 56% fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

InfoScan builds directly on the Mamba-based visual backbone lineage (VMamba, Vision Mamba/Vim), which replaced quadratic self-attention with linear-complexity state-space scanning but relied on fixed, content-agnostic traversal orders (raster, Hilbert, Z-curves). It departs from this by introducing a reinforcement-learning-driven scanning policy (PPO over an MDP) that dynamically routes computation toward high-information patches, shifting the efficiency paradigm from post-hoc token pruning to front-loaded perceptual prioritization. The information scoring module (Shannon entropy + local variance) draws on classical image analysis, while the patch-size optimization borrows from resource-allocation theory, but the integration of all three components—scoring, selection, and RL-based path planning—into a unified VISS block is the paper's architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work offers a principled alternative to the rigid scanning assumption in SSM-based vision models, with practical efficiency gains (18–32% fewer parameters at equal or better accuracy) that could influence how next-generation visual backbones allocate compute. However, several open questions remain: the uniform patch-size assumption limits multi-scale capture, the RL-trained policy adds training complexity and inference latency from the scoring module, and reward hyperparameters still require mild tuning for extreme domain shifts (e.g., natural to medical imaging). Extension to video and vision-language settings is explicitly left for future work, and the 2D-only action space constrains broader applicability.
