## Problem

Discrete Level of Detail (DLoD) in 3D Gaussian Splatting requires storing multiple model copies and causes jarring visual 'popping' artifacts when swapping between detail levels at runtime. The paper argues that 3DGS's continuous, volumetric primitive representation is uniquely suited to a Continuous LoD (CLoD) paradigm that provides smooth, seamless quality scaling within a single unified model, eliminating both the storage overhead and transition artifacts of DLoD.

## Input / Output

Training consumes standard multi-view RGB images with calibrated camera parameters, augmented by a randomly sampled virtual distance scale factor s_v ∈ U(1, max_scale) at each iteration to simulate viewing from varying distances. The output is a single 3DGS model where each Gaussian carries one additional learnable float (the distance decay factor σ_d,i, adding only ~1.6% storage overhead). At render time, the user adjusts a single scalar s_v to continuously trade off visual fidelity against the number of rendered primitives and FPS, without any model switching or additional assets.

## Method

Each Gaussian primitive is augmented with a learnable distance decay factor σ_d,i that controls how rapidly its opacity attenuates with viewpoint distance. At render time, the effective opacity is computed as α_i · exp(−(d'_i · s_v)² / (2·ReLU(σ_d,i)² + ε)), where d'_i is the normalized distance to the camera; primitives falling below a threshold τ·s_v are culled, producing a smooth, continuous detail spectrum. Training employs a virtual distance scaling strategy that randomly samples s_v from a uniform range to force the model to learn robust representations across all detail levels, combined with a point count regularization loss L_reg = (s_v−1)²·(ReLU(η_actual − η_target))² that explicitly penalizes excessive primitive counts at simulated distant views. An adaptive weight w_s = (1 − 0.5·s_v/max(s_v))² down-weights the rendering loss at larger virtual distances to prevent over-pruning, and the total objective is L_total = w_s(L_render + λ_reg·L_reg). The key insight is that 3DGS's per-primitive continuous parameters and differentiable rasterization make opacity-based filtering inherently smooth—unlike mesh simplification, which requires discrete topological operations—allowing the LoD mechanism itself to be learned end-to-end within the standard 3DGS training loop.

## Results

On BungeeNeRF, CLoD-GS at scale=1 achieves 28.05 PSNR and 0.919 SSIM while using only 4,185k Gaussians (38% fewer than 3DGS's 6,733k), and at scale=7 reduces to 1,855k Gaussians (73% reduction) with 27.09 PSNR. On Deep Blending, CLoD-GS (scale=1) reaches 29.93 PSNR with 1,697k Gaussians vs. 3DGS's 29.84 PSNR with 2,486k, and at scale=7 drops to 662k Gaussians (73% reduction) while retaining 29.64 PSNR. On Tanks & Temples, CLoD-GS (scale=3) achieves 23.79 PSNR with 984k Gaussians, outperforming 3DGS (23.70 PSNR, 1,574k) in both quality and efficiency. FPS scales from ~57–170 (scale=1) to ~88–199 (scale=7) across datasets, demonstrating smooth quality-speed trade-off from a single model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original 3DGS baseline, CLoD-GS at scale=1 matches or exceeds PSNR and SSIM on all three main datasets (e.g., 28.05 vs 27.91 on BungeeNeRF, 29.93 vs 29.84 on Deep Blending) while using 26–38% fewer Gaussians, demonstrating that the regularization loss inherently produces a more compact representation. Compared to MaskGaussian (static compression), CLoD-GS uses fewer primitives at equivalent or better quality (e.g., 4,185k vs 5,298k on BungeeNeRF at similar PSNR) and additionally provides continuous runtime quality scaling that MaskGaussian cannot offer. Against the discrete LoD methods, CLoD-GS substantially outperforms H-3DGS in rendering quality (23.75 vs 21.71 PSNR on Tanks & Temples; 29.93 vs 27.41 on Deep Blending) while Octree-GS achieves comparable PSNR but introduces significant rendering overhead and requires non-standard renderer support. Versus Fast Rendering (Milef et al.), CLoD-GS achieves higher PSNR at the same primitive count (28.05 vs 27.91 on BungeeNeRF) and provides a smoother, pop-free quality degradation curve as the virtual distance scale increases, whereas Fast Rendering relies on a static importance ranking learned in a separate training phase.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Continuous Level of Detail (CLoD) paradigm—originally developed for meshes via Progressive Meshes (Hoppe, 1996)—to the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation, arguing that the continuous, volumetric nature of Gaussians makes per-primitive opacity modulation a natural fit. It departs from recent DLoD-for-3DGS methods (LODGE, Octree-GS, H-3DGS) that create multiple discrete Gaussian sets, and from the concurrent CLoD work by Milef et al. (2025) that requires a separate post-training phase, by integrating a single learnable decay parameter directly into the primary 3DGS optimization loop. The virtual distance scaling training strategy with point-count regularization is a novel training-time mechanism not present in prior 3DGS or mesh-based LoD work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CLoD-GS offers a practical, low-overhead (one extra float per Gaussian, ~1.6% storage) path to pop-free, quality-scalable rendering from a single model, which is directly relevant to real-time applications like AR/VR and large-scale scene visualization. However, the method is limited to static scenes and uses only Euclidean distance as the LoD criterion, ignoring perceptual importance, screen-space size, or motion. Open questions include whether the learned decay parameters generalize to dynamic scenes, whether richer perceptual signals can replace the distance heuristic, and how the approach composes with chunk-based streaming for city-scale environments.
