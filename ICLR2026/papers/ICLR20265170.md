## Problem

Neural network hyperparameters (e.g., reward weights in RL, quantile targets in regression) fix behavioral trade-offs at training time, but post-deployment preferences often shift, making retraining necessary yet computationally prohibitive. Existing trajectory inference methods recover unconditional population dynamics from sparse marginals but do not handle the conditional setting where the path between distributions depends on an input condition, and simple interpolation schemes like conditional flow matching fail to capture the complex, non-Euclidean geometry of hyperparameter-induced dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes temporally sparse samples (typically 2–3 anchor hyperparameter settings) from a trained NN's conditional output distribution p_θλ(y|x), paired with their input conditions x, and produces a surrogate model that samples from the estimated conditional distribution at any intermediate λ without further optimization. At inference time, a sample is drawn from the nearest anchor distribution, passed through a learned transport map to the next anchor, and then evaluated along a spline-based geodesic at the normalized target position, yielding a single output sample in O(1) forward passes. The conditioning variable x is injected via FiLM layers into all neural components, enabling condition-dependent paths.

## Method

The core contribution is a neural Conditional Lagrangian Optimal Transport (CLOT) framework that jointly learns a Lagrangian cost function and the resulting transport maps and geodesics. The Lagrangian combines a learned kinetic energy term ½q̇ᵀG(q|x)q̇ (encoding least-action bias via a neural metric G parametrized by eigendecomposition with Givens rotations, extending to arbitrary dimensions) and a fixed potential energy term α·log(p̂(q|x)) estimated by a Nadaraya-Watson kernel density estimator (encoding dense-traversal bias). Training alternates between minimizing over the metric G and maximizing over Kantorovich potentials in a semi-dual formulation, with the computationally expensive c-transform amortized by neural transport maps refined with limited L-BFGS steps and a spline-based geodesic network trained to minimize the action. This extends Pooladian et al. (2024) to the conditional setting, introduces the data-dependent potential term, and provides a more expressive, higher-dimensional metric parametrization that avoids degenerate minima without explicit regularization.

## Results

On the Cancer therapy RL task, the full method (K_θ − Ů) achieves an average surrogate reward of 102.49 across held-out λ_nk settings, substantially outperforming CFM (36.03), MFM (41.05), and NLOT (9.26). On the higher-dimensional ETTm2 quantile regression task, the method achieves 0.608 MSE compared to 1.402 for CFM and 1.387 for MFM. On the Two Moons generative modelling task, the density-biased variant (K_I − Ů) achieves the lowest Wasserstein distance of 0.060, versus 0.464 for CFM and 0.480 for MFM. The method also achieves the best reward on Reacher (−6.093) and the non-linear Cancer variant (91.94).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (K_θ − Ů) consistently outperforms all three primary baselines across every experiment. Against NLOT (Pooladian et al., 2024), the closest related work, the gap is largest in the Cancer task (102.49 vs. 9.26 reward) and Two Moons (0.079 vs. 0.291 WD), with a smaller but consistent advantage on Reacher (−6.093 vs. −6.173). Against CFM (Lipman et al., 2023), the method achieves roughly 2.8× higher reward on Cancer (102.49 vs. 36.03) and 2.3× lower MSE on ETTm2 quantile regression (0.608 vs. 1.402). Against MFM (Kapusniak et al., 2024), the advantage is similar in magnitude: 102.49 vs. 41.05 on Cancer and 0.608 vs. 1.387 MSE on ETTm2. The ablation results further show that both inductive biases (learned metric K_θ and density potential Ů) contribute independently, with the density bias providing the largest single improvement in most settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Pooladian et al. (2024) 'Neural optimal transport with Lagrangian costs' (NLOT) in three ways: adding a data-dependent potential energy term (via Nadaraya-Watson density estimation) to encode a dense-traversal inductive bias, elevating the framework to the conditional OT setting with FiLM conditioning, and replacing the fixed-eigenvalue 2D metric with a more expressive eigendecomposition parametrization using Givens rotations that generalizes to higher dimensions. It also draws on the broader trajectory inference literature (Hashimoto et al., 2016; Tong et al., 2020; Scarvelis & Solomon, 2023) for its least-action and manifold-hypothesis biases, and positions HTI as a novel instantiation of conditional trajectory inference (CTI) applied specifically to hyperparameter-induced dynamics in neural networks. Compared to flow-matching baselines (CFM, MFM), the CLOT formulation provides a principled path-dependent cost that naturally encodes geometric structure rather than learning a vector field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is compelling: avoiding 3.5-hour PPO retraining cycles by training a 15-minute surrogate that enables inference-time hyperparameter adjustment, with demonstrated utility in personalized cancer therapy, quantile regression for time-series forecasting, and generative model dropout interpolation. Key limitations include restriction to a single continuous hyperparameter (multi-hyperparameter extensions are discussed but not implemented), sensitivity to chaotic dynamics, and the relatively simple experimental settings (2D ambient spaces for most RL tasks, 3-step forecasting horizon). Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-dimensional output spaces, whether the Nadaraya-Watson density estimate remains effective in high dimensions, and how the method behaves when hyperparameter-induced dynamics are highly non-monotonic or multi-modal.
