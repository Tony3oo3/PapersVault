## Problem

Existing knowledge editing methods (ROME, MEMIT, PMET) exhibit severe performance decay on multi-hop factual recall, especially when edits target intermediate implicit subjects in reasoning chains. The root cause is an incomplete understanding of how chained knowledge is dynamically represented at the neuron level: implicit subjects function as query neurons that sequentially activate value neurons across transformer layers, a mechanism prior work has overlooked. While IFMET improved multi-hop accuracy by editing deeper FFN layers, the mechanistic explanation for why those deeper edits matter remained unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a knowledge edit triple (s, r, o → o*) and the target LLM's weights (GPT-J 6B or Qwen3-8B), and produces modified FFN subvalue matrices (W_fc2) in a small set of identified query and value layers. Evaluation uses few-shot Chain-of-Thought prompts on a filtered MQuAKE-3K subset where the base model already answers correctly, testing whether the edited model can propagate the updated fact through multi-hop reasoning chains. The editing itself is a closed-form weight update (PMET backbone) with a knowledge-preservation regularization, requiring no gradient-based training of the full model.

## Method

ACE operates in three sequential stages: (1) identifying critical query and value FFN layers via neuron-level attribution, where value-neuron importance is the log-probability increase from injecting the neuron's vector into the residual stream and query-neuron importance is the inner product between the neuron's subvalue and its subkey; (2) editing value neurons in deeper FFN layers using PMET's closed-form solution to store the new fact; and (3) editing query neurons in middle-to-shallow FFN layers to adjust the implicit reasoning pathway that activates the updated value neurons. The core mechanistic insight is that implicit subjects in multi-hop chains act as query neurons that sequentially trigger corresponding value neurons across layers, and both components are essential—ablation shows skipping three query layers drops accuracy by 16.51% while skipping two value layers drops it by 40.45%. Unlike prior layer-level heuristics, ACE targets specific neuron-level Q-V pathways, and the framework is backbone-agnostic, validated with both PMET and AlphaEdit.

## Results

On the MQuAKE-3K multi-hop factual recall benchmark (few-shot CoT setting), ACE achieves an average multi-hop accuracy of 46.45% on GPT-J-6B and 58.24% on Qwen3-8B, outperforming the strongest baseline PMET by 9.44% and 37.46% respectively. ACE also leads on all individual edit-count settings (1-edit through 4-edit), with the largest gains appearing at higher edit counts where reasoning chains are longer. On auxiliary metrics, ACE achieves 99.8% efficacy and 91.2% paraphrase accuracy on GPT-J, substantially exceeding all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACE outperforms all four baselines on both GPT-J-6B and Qwen3-8B across every edit-count setting. Against PMET (its own editing backbone), ACE gains +9.44% on GPT-J (46.45 vs. 37.01) and a dramatic +37.46% on Qwen3-8B (58.24 vs. 20.78), with the Qwen3 gap widening at 3-edit (51.62 vs. 12.56) and 4-edit (47.61 vs. 11.20). Against ROME, ACE improves by +11.41% on GPT-J and +29.45% on Qwen3-8B; against MEMIT, the margins are +7.87% on GPT-J and +39.57% on Qwen3-8B. Against AlphaEdit (evaluated in Appendix K, Table 10), ACE(PMET) leads by +7.18% on GPT-J (46.45 vs. 39.27) and +12.65% on Qwen3-8B (58.24 vs. 45.59), while the ACE(AlphaEdit) variant further extends the lead to +9.97% and +15.95% respectively. Notably, all locate-then-edit baselines suffer severe degradation as edit count increases (e.g., ROME drops from 44.51% to 5.06% on GPT-J 1-edit→4-edit), whereas ACE maintains relatively stable performance (45.26%→43.29%), confirming that neuron-level query-value editing preserves reasoning-chain integrity.

## Relation to Prior Work

ACE extends the locate-then-edit paradigm (ROME, MEMIT, PMET) by shifting from layer-level heuristics to neuron-level attribution, building directly on the query-value neuron framework of Yu & Ananiadou (2023) and the multi-hop editing observations of IFMET (Zhang et al., 2024b). The key conceptual departure is identifying implicit subjects as 'query neurons' that sequentially activate 'value neurons' across layers, a dynamic prior methods overlooked by treating FFN layers as monolithic key-value stores. PMET serves as the optimization backbone, but ACE's identifying stage—using log-probability importance scores for value neurons and inner-product scores for query neurons—represents a new mechanistic layer on top of existing closed-form editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first mechanistic account of how multi-hop factual recall is computed at the neuron level, resolving why deeper FFN edits help (they host value neurons) and why query-layer edits are also necessary (implicit subjects must activate those value neurons). However, absolute multi-hop accuracy remains far below base-model performance (46–58% vs. 98–99%), and the framework is validated on only two models with a single benchmark, leaving open whether the query-value attribution generalizes to larger architectures or non-factual reasoning. The knowledge-bottleneck finding—that editing more value layers yields diminishing returns without query-layer edits—offers a practical design principle for future editors.
