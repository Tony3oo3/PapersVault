## Problem

Generating complex 3D scenes with rich physical interactions (stacking, containment, support, balance) for robotic manipulation data collection remains unsolved. Prior approaches either rely on hand-crafted rules, data-driven models limited by sparse small-object placement datasets, or LLM-based pipelines that use simplified 2D collision avoidance without feedback loops, failing to produce physically accurate and densely packed arrangements.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form natural language scene prompt (e.g., 'a working desk of a computer science PhD student') and retrieves objects from a pre-annotated BlenderKit asset library (~800 assets with mass, friction, center-of-mass shift, and supporting probability); unmatched descriptions trigger an automatic text-to-3D pipeline (SDXL + Tripo-AI). The output is a fully resolved 3D scene configuration with per-object 6-DoF poses, physical properties, and validated stability, ready for interactive physics simulation or robot policy training.

## Method

PhyScensis is a training-free, three-component agentic loop: (1) an LLM agent (o4-mini) decomposes the prompt into spatial predicates (2D positioning, alignment, rotation, symmetry, grouping) and physical predicates (place-on, place-in, place-anywhere) with tunable parameters like support ratio and stability target; (2) a two-stage solver first resolves 2D placements via convex-hull overlap optimization, then handles 3D stacking/containment using an occupancy-grid heuristic (voxelized at 0.01–0.03 m resolution) followed by Genesis physics simulation, with probabilistic programming (Bayesian kernel-weighted failure probability over 11-D perturbation vectors) enabling explicit control over placement stability; (3) a structured feedback system reports grammar errors, solver failures with empty-region diagnostics, and success metrics (stability score, VQA alignment, surface coverage) back to the agent for iterative refinement. The key differentiator from prior LLM-based scene generators is the tight coupling of a real physics engine with probabilistic stability measurement, allowing the system to produce physically valid complex stacks and even intentionally unstable configurations.

## Results

PhyScensis achieves a VQA Score of 0.704±0.425, GPT Ranking of 1.429±0.562, and Settle Distance of 0.003±0.008, outperforming both Architect (0.493, 2.607, 0.405) and 3D-Generalist (0.578, 1.946, 0.033) across all three scene-quality metrics. In the robot manipulation experiment, policies trained on PhyScensis-generated scenes achieve 9/10 reaching and 3/10 placing success rates on unseen human-designed scenes, compared to 3/10 and 0/10 for Architect and 4/10 and 1/10 for 3D-Generalist. In the user study (20 participants, 1–5 scale), PhyScensis scores 4.04 on text alignment, 3.98 on naturalness & physics, and 3.82 on complexity, substantially exceeding both baselines (≤3.04 on all dimensions).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhyScensis outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against Architect (Wang et al., 2024b), which relies on 2D inpainting followed by depth estimation, PhyScensis improves VQA Score by 0.211 and reduces Settle Distance from 0.405 to 0.003, reflecting Architect's frequent inter-object penetrations that destabilize physics simulations. Against 3D-Generalist (Sun et al., 2025b), which uses a VLM to point at 2D pixel locations, PhyScensis achieves a 0.126 higher VQA Score and a 10× lower Settle Distance (0.003 vs. 0.033), as 3D-Generalist's basic collision avoidance does not enforce stacking stability. Against LayoutVLM (Sun et al., 2025a), PhyScensis scores 0.704 vs. 0.648 on VQA and 0.003 vs. 0.115 on Settle Distance; LayoutVLM's differentiable optimizer converges prematurely in dense tabletop settings and cannot produce complex multi-object stacking. Against ClutterGen (Jia & Chen, 2024), which uses RL to place a fixed asset set, PhyScensis places 14.77 objects (no-stack) or 22.30 (with-stack) on average versus ClutterGen's 3.57, while also supporting open-vocabulary, semantics-aware generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-agent-based scene generation line (AnyHome, Holodeck, Architect, 3D-Generalist, LayoutVLM) by replacing their simplified collision-avoidance or image-inpainting pipelines with a physics engine (Genesis) that enforces physical plausibility through stacking, containment, and support constraints. It also draws on probabilistic programming for intuitive physics (Wang et al., 2024a) to quantify and control placement stability, a component absent from prior scene-generation work. The key conceptual departure is treating physical predicates (place-on, place-in, place-anywhere) as first-class constraints resolved by a physics simulator rather than approximated by bounding-box checks or depth estimation, and closing the loop with a multi-modal feedback system that lets the LLM agent iteratively refine its predicate set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, training-free pipeline for generating physically accurate, high-density manipulation scenes at scale, directly addressing a bottleneck in robotic data collection where hand-crafted or rule-based environments limit diversity. The demonstrated transfer of a diffusion policy trained on generated scenes to unseen human-designed setups suggests the generated distributions are sufficiently realistic for downstream learning. Limitations include dependence on asset retrieval quality (mismatched assets cause floating objects), solver failures in very crowded scenes, and VQA scores that do not always track human perception. Open questions include scalability to room-level or multi-room scenes, integration with articulated objects, and whether the physics-based generation can be distilled into a learned model to reduce inference cost.
