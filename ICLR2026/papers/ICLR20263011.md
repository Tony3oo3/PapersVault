## Problem

On-device few-shot class-incremental learning (FSCIL) requires edge devices to learn new classes from a handful of samples while retaining all previously learned knowledge, under severe memory and energy constraints. Existing ANN-based FSCIL methods depend on large frozen pre-trained backbones that exceed the 4–12 GB budgets of realistic edge hardware, and while spiking neural networks (SNNs) offer event-driven energy savings, no SNN-based FSCIL framework existed that simultaneously addresses catastrophic forgetting, few-shot overfitting, and the non-differentiability of spike activations during on-device training.

## Input / Output

The model accepts either static RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 84×84 for Mini-ImageNet) or asynchronous event streams from dynamic vision sensors (CIFAR10-DVS, DVS128 Gesture, N-Caltech101), which are encoded into spike trains over T time steps (typically 4–6) by LIF neurons. The base session is trained on abundant labeled data, while each incremental session provides only N-way K-shot samples (e.g., 5-way 5-shot). The output is a class label predicted from the final-layer spike pattern, with the backbone frozen after the base session and only prototypes updated in incremental sessions.

## Method

SAFA-SNN combines three mechanisms within a LIF-based SNN. First, Sparsity-Aware Neuronal Dynamics assigns each channel a binary mask designating most neurons as stable and a small fraction (ratio η) as adaptive; a threshold regulation factor A = β(1−M) + γM (with β > γ) lets adaptive neurons shift their firing thresholds more freely while stable neurons lock near their base-session firing rates, preserving synaptic traces of old classes. Second, Zeroth-Order Optimization replaces surrogate gradients for the non-differentiable Heaviside spike with a two-point directional-derivative estimator using b random Gaussian perturbations, applied only during base-session training to avoid the vanishing-gradient issues of fixed-width surrogates. Third, Fast Adaptive Prototype Subspace Projection constructs a projection matrix G = B̃(B̃ᵀB̃)⁻¹B̃ᵀ from normalized base-class prototypes, maps each novel-class prototype into this base subspace, and blends the projected vector with the original via a convex combination (coefficient α), pulling new-class prototypes toward semantically aligned directions and reducing overfitting on scarce samples.

## Results

On Mini-ImageNet (5-way 5-shot, 9 sessions), SAFA-SNN achieves 59.45% average accuracy and 48.70% last-session accuracy, surpassing the second-best method CLOSER (53.38% / 44.69%) by 6.07% in A_avg and 4.01% in last-session accuracy. On CIFAR-100 with Spiking VGG-9, SAFA-SNN reaches 56.59% A_avg and 48.09% last-session accuracy, outperforming TEEN (59.34% A_avg but only 44.51% last-session) on retention. On neuromorphic datasets, SAFA-SNN leads on all three: CIFAR10-DVS (47.56% A_avg), DVS128 Gesture (86.74% A_avg), and N-Caltech101 (45.68% A_avg). The method also achieves ~20% lower training energy consumption on CIFAR-100 compared to baselines when deployed on a Jetson Orin AGX.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAFA-SNN outperforms all 15 baselines on the primary Mini-ImageNet benchmark. The closest competitor, CLOSER (Oh et al., 2024), achieves 53.38% A_avg and 44.69% last-session accuracy, trailing SAFA-SNN by 6.07% and 4.01% respectively. TEEN (Wang et al., 2023) is competitive on CIFAR-100 (59.34% A_avg vs. 56.59% for SAFA-SNN) but falls behind on last-session retention (44.51% vs. 48.09%), indicating SAFA-SNN's sparsity-aware dynamics better preserve base-class knowledge. WARP (Kim et al., 2023) and S3C (Kalla & Biswas, 2022) suffer severe collapse in later sessions (21.30% and 26.56% last-session on Mini-ImageNet), while SAFA-SNN maintains 48.70%. Among SNN-specific training methods, SAFA-SNN surpasses Early Bird (Kim et al., 2022) by 5.85% A_avg and SLTT (Meng et al., 2023) by 9.86% A_avg on CIFAR-100 (Table 3). Against soft-subnetwork FSCIL methods (FSLL, SoftNet, WSN in Table 12), SAFA-SNN achieves 60.97% A_avg on CIFAR-100 with Spiking ResNet-18, exceeding SoftNet (57.57%) without introducing any additional trainable parameters beyond the backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of three established lines of work: ANN-based FSCIL (CEC, FACT, TEEN, WARP, CLOSER, etc.), SNN dynamic-threshold mechanisms (BDETT, LTMD, soft-threshold pruning), and zeroth-order optimization for non-differentiable objectives (Mukhoty et al., 2023). It repurposes dynamic thresholds not for sparsity pruning but for a plasticity-stability split (stable vs. adaptive neurons) that naturally preserves base-class synaptic traces, and applies ZOO specifically to the Heaviside spike non-differentiability rather than general non-convex optimization. The prototype subspace projection draws on Simon et al. (2020) and prior prototype-rectification work but is the first to combine it with SNN-based incremental learning. The authors explicitly position this as the first SNN-based solution for general on-device FSCIL, departing from ANN-based PEFT methods that require 4–12 GB memory budgets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical on-device implementation on Jetson Orin AGX with measured energy consumption (20% lower than baselines on CIFAR-100) addresses a gap that prior FSCIL work largely ignores: most methods assume server-class compute and are infeasible on edge devices with 4–12 GB memory. However, the paper acknowledges performance degradation in deeper networks (Spiking ResNet-34) due to identity-mapping failure, and the fixed N-way K-shot assumption oversimplifies real-world data streams. Open questions include whether the sparsity-aware threshold mechanism scales to imbalanced way-shot settings, whether ZOO remains competitive as network depth increases, and whether the approach transfers to truly asynchronous neuromorphic hardware rather than simulated event streams.
