## Problem

Existing cross-domain offline RL methods address only train-time robustness (source-target dynamics mismatch) while neglecting test-time robustness against dynamics perturbations encountered during real-world deployment. The paper empirically demonstrates that policies trained with cross-domain offline RL are highly fragile to test-time dynamics shifts, with performance degrading up to 72% under hard perturbations, especially when target domain data is limited.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262365/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two offline datasets: a limited target domain dataset (10% of D4RL, ~100K samples) and a source domain dataset (~1M samples) collected from a modified environment with kinematic or morphology shifts, where both domains share the same state-action space but differ in transition dynamics. The output is a target-domain policy optimized via IQL, evaluated under clean and perturbed dynamics. The ensemble dynamics model is trained exclusively on target data to approximate the target transition distribution, which then guides robust Bellman backups on source data.

## Method

The core contribution is the Robust Cross-Domain Bellman (RCB) operator, which applies standard in-sample Bellman backup on target domain data but a Wasserstein-robust Bellman backup on source domain data, achieving dual robustness with a single operator. Via a Lagrange dual reformulation, the intractable dynamics uncertainty set is converted into a tractable state uncertainty set U_ε(s'), and an ensemble dynamics model trained on target data approximates sampling from this set. A dynamic value penalty (coefficient β) measures the gap between the observed next-state Q-value and the minimum Q-value over ensemble-predicted states, providing flexible control over overestimation vs. underestimation. The Bellman update uses Huber loss on source data to resist outlier value estimation errors. The RCB operator is applied only to source data, preserving target-data performance while injecting robustness, with theoretical guarantees of γ-contraction, bounded Q-value error (Proposition 4.4), and a certified test-time robustness threshold c (Proposition 4.5).

## Results

On 16 cross-domain offline RL tasks (4 MuJoCo tasks × 4 data qualities) with train-time kinematic dynamics shifts, DROCO achieves a total normalized score of 1105.2, surpassing the second-best method OTDF (969.8) by 14.0% and winning on 9 out of 16 individual tasks. Under test-time dynamics perturbations, DROCO exhibits substantially less performance degradation: only 19.3% under easy-level kinematic shifts (vs. >50% for IGDF and OTDF), and 37.9% under the strongest min-Q adversarial perturbation (vs. 73.6% for OTDF and 84.0% for IGDF). The method also demonstrates robustness to morphology shifts, with 42.1% degradation under easy-level morphology perturbations compared to 62.4% (OTDF) and 78.9% (IGDF).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DROCO is compared against six baselines: IQL* and CQL* (naive cross-domain training), BOSA (supported cross-domain offline RL), DARA (dynamics-aware reward augmentation), IGDF (contrastive data filtering), and OTDF (optimal transport data filtering). DROCO achieves the highest total normalized score (1105.2) across all 16 tasks, outperforming OTDF (969.8) by 14.0% and IGDF (964.3) by 14.7%. It wins on 9 of 16 individual tasks, with particularly large margins on walker2d-medium (+19.0 over IGDF) and walker2d-medium-replay (+16.5 over IQL*), but trails on tasks where the source-target gap is small (e.g., halfcheetah-expert where BOSA reaches 84.3 vs. DROCO's 67.4, and hopper-expert where OTDF reaches 97.0 vs. DROCO's 89.3). Under test-time dynamics perturbations, DROCO's advantage is most pronounced: at the hardest kinematic perturbation level, DROCO degrades far less than both IGDF and OTDF, and under adversarial min-Q perturbations at scale 0.2, DROCO loses only 37.9% performance versus 73.6% (OTDF) and 84.0% (IGDF). The authors attribute DROCO's losses on some tasks to its inherent performance-robustness trade-off, since the RCB operator introduces conservatism that other baselines do not.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: distributionally robust MDPs (Iyengar, 2005) with Wasserstein uncertainty sets, cross-domain offline RL methods that handle source-target dynamics mismatches (IGDF, OTDF, BOSA, DARA), and conservative in-sample offline RL (IQL, MICRO). The key departure is that all prior cross-domain offline RL work addresses only train-time robustness (the source-target dynamics gap), while robust RL literature addresses only test-time robustness in single-domain settings. The RCB operator unifies these by applying the robust Bellman backup selectively to source domain data while using standard in-sample Bellman updates on target data, and the practical implementation borrows ensemble dynamics modeling from model-based offline RL (MOPO) to approximate the Wasserstein uncertainty set without access to the true dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a practically important gap: policies trained via cross-domain offline RL degrade substantially (40–72%) under even moderate test-time dynamics perturbations, especially when target data is scarce—a realistic scenario in robotics deployment where components degrade over time. The dual-robustness framing and the ε-controlled trade-off between train-time and test-time robustness provide a principled design space for practitioners. Limitations include evaluation only on MuJoCo locomotion tasks (no Antmaze or Adroit), task-dependent hyperparameter sensitivity (β, δ), and the assumption of shared state-action spaces across domains. Open questions include whether the Wasserstein-based state perturbation approach scales to high-dimensional or continuous-action settings, and whether the ensemble-based uncertainty approximation remains valid under severe source-target dynamics gaps.
