## Problem

Existing multivariate time series forecasting models implicitly assume all variates share a common, static periodic length, forcing heterogeneous periodicities into a unified framework and causing spurious temporal dynamics. Additionally, standard softmax-based attention in Transformers suppresses negative correlations, discarding inverse or complementary periodic signals that carry critical information about system dynamics.

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{C×T} (C variates over T historical steps) and produces future predictions Ŷ ∈ R^{C×L}. The lookback length T is treated as a tunable hyperparameter rather than fixed, and FFT is applied to each variate to extract the top-K dominant period lengths, which drive the internal bucketing structure. The final prediction for each variate is a frequency-weighted sum of its representations across all buckets in which it appears, so a single variate with multiple periodicities contributes to multiple buckets.

## Method

PHAT restructures multivariate inputs into a 3D 'periodic bucket' tensor where variates are grouped by detected period length (via FFT top-K), sequences are folded so rows align time steps by phase and columns capture offsets within the period, and cross-bucket attention is masked to prevent interference between differently-periodic variates. Within each bucket, a Positive-Negative X-Shape Attention (PNA) module computes two orthogonal attention paths: period-aligned attention (across periods at the same phase) and period-offset attention (within a period). The period-offset path is decomposed into separate positive and negative logits, each modulated by a stick-breaking term that enforces monotonic decay of attention with increasing periodic distance, and a learned gating vector Λ adaptively controls the strength of the negative path. A mathematical proof shows the unnormalized attention coefficients satisfy a stick-breaking allocation, guaranteeing local periodic dominance, and a variance argument demonstrates the positive-negative decomposition yields strictly lower attention variance than vanilla attention under period heterogeneity.

## Results

PHAT achieves state-of-the-art performance on 73.95% (71/96) of evaluation metrics across 14 real-world datasets, ranking in the top two for 84.38% of metrics. On the NYSE dataset, PHAT improves MSE by up to 23.33% over the best baseline. In terms of efficiency, PHAT uses only 33.4K parameters (vs. 2.0M for PDF, 3.4M for FEDformer) and reduces MACs/FLOPs by over 98% compared to Transformer-based baselines, while maintaining competitive or superior forecasting accuracy (e.g., MSE 0.330 vs. 0.340 for PDF on ETTm1 L=96).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PHAT outperforms all 18 baselines on the majority of metrics, with TimeKAN being the closest competitor (2nd place on most ETT metrics, e.g., MSE 0.369 vs. PHAT's 0.361 on ETTh1 L=96). PDF is competitive on shorter horizons (MSE 0.360 on ETTh1 L=96, nearly matching PHAT) but degrades at longer horizons (0.456 vs. 0.441 at L=720). xPatch achieves the best MSE on ETTm2 L=96 (0.162, tied with PHAT) but trails significantly at L=720 (0.354 vs. 0.355). CycleNet is the only method to beat PHAT on ETTm2 L=96 MSE (0.159 vs. 0.162), though PHAT recovers at longer horizons. Crossformer and Triformer perform poorly, especially on ETTh2 where Crossformer's MSE explodes to 1.386 at L=720, indicating failure to handle non-periodic or weakly periodic data. On the NYSE dataset, PHAT achieves a 23.33% MSE improvement over the best baseline, and in the complexity comparison, PHAT (33.4K params, 2.9M MACs) is over 100× lighter than PDF (2.0M params, 4.4B MACs) while delivering better accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

PHAT carves new ground by identifying period heterogeneity—where different variates in a multivariate time series exhibit distinct and dynamically varying period lengths—as a first-class modeling challenge that prior periodicity-aware methods (CycleNet, TimesNet, FEDformer, PDF) implicitly ignore by treating all variates as interchangeable channels sharing a common periodic structure. The periodic bucket construction repurposes FFT-based period detection (already used in FEDformer and TimesNet) but applies it per-variate to create heterogeneous groupings, while the positive-negative attention mechanism departs from standard Transformer self-attention by explicitly preserving negative correlations that softmax normalization suppresses. The stick-breaking interpretation of the modulated attention logits connects the design to the logistic stick-breaking process literature, providing a probabilistic reading of the periodic distance decay.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world multivariate systems (energy grids, traffic networks, financial markets) routinely contain variates with different dominant periods, and forcing them into a shared-periodic framework degrades forecasting accuracy. The extreme parameter efficiency (33K parameters, typically 1 layer) achieving SOTA across 14 datasets makes the approach deployable in resource-constrained settings, and the frequency-based multi-period fusion provides a principled way to combine heterogeneous periodic signals. Open questions include how well the one-shot FFT-based period detection handles non-stationary or slowly drifting periods, whether the bucket assignment generalizes to streaming/online settings, and whether the positive-negative decomposition extends naturally to probabilistic or multi-horizon uncertainty quantification.
