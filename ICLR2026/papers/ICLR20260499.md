## Problem

Existing LoRA-MoE frameworks for multi-task LLM adaptation either replace entire attention/FFN blocks with switch experts or append parallel LoRA branches, undermining parameter efficiency and producing shallow output fusion that bypasses the core attention or state-transition pathway. Additionally, standard auxiliary routing losses enforce uniform expert activation, suppressing the input-aware specialization needed for effective token-level routing and requiring more training data to converge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260499/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of E pre-trained LoRA adapters (sourced from public repositories like LoRAHub, independently trained, or jointly trained with hard routing) and text tokens as input. At each projection layer, the router produces a softmax score vector over the E experts, and the top-K experts' low-rank updates are fused additively into the base projection output before it enters the downstream attention or SSM module. The final output is standard text generation or task prediction, with the routing overhead limited to a small linear layer (0.04% of total parameters) per projection.

## Method

LoRA-Mixer inserts a mixture of LoRA experts into the linear projection layers (Q, K, V, output) of attention modules or the in/out-projection layers of SSM modules, rather than targeting FFN blocks, making it drop-in compatible with both Transformers and state-space models. A lightweight router produces per-token softmax scores over E experts, and inference uses sparse top-K fusion to balance cost and selectivity. The key training innovation is the Routing Specialization Loss (RSL), which augments the standard auxiliary load-balancing term with a negative entropy regularizer; this introduces a token-level log p_i(x) gradient signal that amplifies input-aware variance in expert selection while preserving global load balance, avoiding the over-averaging pathology of pure auxiliary losses. The framework supports two regimes: joint optimization of adapters and router with a differentiable soft-top-k scheme, and plug-and-play routing over frozen pre-trained LoRAs requiring only ~2k routing training samples. An expert preservation regularizer (L_preserve) constrains sensitive experts to retain their original knowledge during joint training, and theoretical analysis proves λ-strong convexity of the RSL objective on the product simplex, yielding linear convergence rates and a generalization bound of O(ρ(L+B)/(λn)).

## Results

On LLaMA3-8B, LoRA-Mixer achieves 65.53% on GSM8K, 82.22 on CoLA, 83.24 on ARC-C, and 57.32 Pass@1 on HumanEval, outperforming the strongest baselines (MixLoRA, MoLE) by +1.09, +1.55, +0.34, and +1.85 points respectively. The method uses only 48% of the trainable parameters of MixLoRA (3.88% vs. 8.08% of total model parameters). Across all three base models (Falcon-Mamba-7B, Mistral-7B, LLaMA3-8B), LoRA-Mixer achieves the best or near-best score on 6 out of 7 tasks, with the largest gains on HumanEval (+1.85 over MixLoRA on LLaMA3-8B) and CoLA (+3.58 over LoRAHub on Mistral-7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRA-Mixer consistently outperforms all LoRA-MoE baselines (LoRAHub, MoLE, MixLoRA) on LLaMA3-8B across all seven tasks, with the largest margin on HumanEval (+1.85 over MixLoRA) and CoLA (+2.38 over LoRAHub). On Falcon-Mamba-7B (a pure SSM architecture), LoRA-Mixer wins on all seven tasks, confirming architecture-agnostic applicability; MixLoRA is excluded here due to its Transformer-specific design. Against single-task LoRA, LoRA-Mixer gains +0.46 on Medical, +0.72 on CoLA, and +1.71 on HumanEval (LLaMA3-8B), demonstrating that expert routing adds value beyond a single adapter. In the routing-loss ablation (Table 8, 2k training data), RSL outperforms GMoE, Ds-MoE, and AESL by 2.77–6.86 points across SST-2, CoLA, ARC-E, ARC-C, and HumanEval, with the largest gap on HumanEval (57.32 vs. 50.46 for AESL). Against LoRA-LEGO (Table 4, LLaMA2-7B), LoRA-Mixer wins on 3 of 4 GLUE tasks (CoLA +9.12, SST-2 +7.09, MRPC +6.24) but loses on RTE (61.47 vs. 71.85). On OOD generalization (Table 6), LoRA-Mixer beats PHATGOOSE on all three out-of-distribution tasks (QQP +0.19, RTE +1.44, MRPC +0.34), while PHATGOOSE actually degrades below the base model on RTE.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoRA-Mixer extends the LoRA-MoE line of work (MixLoRA, MoLE, LoRA-LEGO) by relocating expert routing from FFN blocks or parallel branches to the core linear projection layers of attention/SSM modules, keeping experts in serial with the native attention or state-transition mechanism. It builds on LoRAHub's vision of composing independently trained adapters but replaces gradient-free combination with a learned router, and on standard MoE top-k gating but introduces the Routing Specialization Loss (RSL) to counteract the over-averaging bias of conventional auxiliary losses. The architecture-agnostic design (compatible with both Transformers and Mamba-style SSMs) departs from prior LoRA-MoE methods that are typically Transformer-specific.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play ability to compose publicly available LoRA modules with only ~2k routing training samples addresses a real practical bottleneck in multi-task LLM deployment, and the cross-model transfer (Mistral-7B → LLaMA3-8B without fine-tuning) suggests the learned routing is robust to base-model identity. However, the fixed top-K routing and uniform application across all layers are acknowledged limitations; dynamic or layer-adaptive routing remains open. The 48% parameter reduction relative to MixLoRA is a meaningful efficiency gain, but the absolute performance margins over strong baselines (1–4 points) are moderate, and the method's advantage may narrow as base models grow larger or as native MoE layers become standard in foundation architectures.
