## Problem

Existing agentic RAG systems treat retrieval as an opaque black-box: the agent can only issue a query and passively receive text chunks, confining it to a trial-and-error loop of query reformulation. This prevents fine-grained control over the retrieval process and severely limits performance on complex multi-hop information-seeking tasks where evidence may be phrased differently or buried among semantically similar distractors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263806/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural-language user query and operates over an external corpus of approximately 280K Wikipedia text chunks (averaging 100 words each). Rather than producing a single query string, the agent generates structured tool-call sequences (wrapped in function-call tags) that invoke multiple interaction primitives concurrently, and the engine returns a consolidated context with metadata such as source document IDs and per-strategy similarity scores. The final output is a concise factual answer, with the full reasoning-and-action trajectory masked during training to prevent the model from learning to reproduce retrieved content.

## Method

Interact-RAG introduces a Corpus Interaction Engine that exposes three categories of action primitives to the LLM agent: Multi-Faceted Retrieval (semantic search, exact/BM25 search, and weighted fusion), Anchored Matching (entity-focused retrieval to reduce distractor noise), and Context Shaping (include/exclude documents, adjust retrieval scale). A reasoning-enhanced workflow decomposes the task into a global planner, an adaptive reasoner (which decides to proceed or reflect-and-refine), and an executor, serving both as a training-free zero-shot solution and a data-synthesis engine. The synthesized trajectories (4.8K successful ones) are used for SFT on Qwen3-8B, followed by RL refinement via GRPO with an outcome reward that gates answer correctness on syntactic validity. This two-stage pipeline yields a unified end-to-end agent that internalizes the multi-module strategy, achieving a 22.5% relative EM improvement over the strongest baseline (Search-R1) across six benchmarks while using only 12K training QA pairs versus Search-R1's 170K.

## Results

Interact-RAG (Qwen3-8B) achieves an average Exact Match of 52.8% across six RAG benchmarks, a 22.5% relative improvement over the strongest baseline Search-R1 (43.1% avg EM). Gains are most pronounced on complex multi-hop tasks: 34.8% EM on MuSiQue (+36.4% relative over Search-R1) and 69.6% EM on 2WikiMultiHopQA. Notably, Interact-RAG is trained on only 12K QA pairs, a small fraction of the 170K used by Search-R1, yet still outperforms it on 5 of 6 benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Interact-RAG outperforms all eight baselines on average EM (52.8 vs. 43.1 for the strongest, Search-R1). Against Search-R1 (end-to-end RL, 170K training data), Interact-RAG leads by 9.7 EM points on average and by 9.3 points on MuSiQue (34.8 vs. 25.5), though Search-R1 retains a narrow edge on NQ with the 7B backbone (45.3 vs. 43.7) due to its larger training set. On 2WikiMultiHopQA, Interact-RAG (69.6) surpasses R-Search (58.8) by 10.8 points and S-DeepSearcher (54.0) by 15.6 points. On Bamboogle, Interact-RAG (54.0) beats S-DeepSearcher (46.2) by 7.8 points. The prompt-driven methods (MA-RAG, Search-o1, IR-CoT) trail significantly, with Search-o1 scoring only 26.4% average EM, confirming that the interactive retrieval paradigm plus end-to-end training is essential rather than merely a prompting strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant agentic RAG paradigm (Search-R1, MA-RAG, Search-O1, R-Search) in which the LLM agent is confined to issuing queries to a black-box semantic retriever and passively receiving text chunks. Rather than building on a specific prior architecture, Interact-RAG introduces a Corpus Interaction Engine that expands the agent's action space from simple query reformulation to fine-grained manipulation of the retrieval process (multi-faceted retrieval with weighted fusion, entity-anchored matching, and context shaping). The training pipeline (SFT on synthesized trajectories followed by GRPO-based RL) follows the end-to-end agent training recipe established by Search-R1 and R-Search, but applies it to this fundamentally richer action space. The reasoning-enhanced workflow (global planner → adaptive reasoner → executor) is positioned as both a training-free solution and a data synthesis engine, drawing on the multi-agent decomposition ideas in MA-RAG and Search-O1.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paradigm shift from passive query issuance to active corpus manipulation offers a principled escape from the trial-and-error query reformulation loop that limits existing agentic RAG systems, particularly on multi-hop tasks where evidence may be phrased differently or buried among distractors. The 22.5% relative EM improvement over Search-R1 using only 12K training pairs (vs. 170K) suggests the interactive action space is a more data-efficient inductive bias. Key limitations include reliance on outcome-based rewards (no process rewards), higher test-time latency and token cost from explicit reasoning trajectories, and evaluation primarily on Wikipedia-based corpora. Open questions include whether process-level rewards or more advanced RL algorithms can further improve learning efficiency, and whether the paradigm scales to much larger, noisier real-world corpora beyond the controlled 280K-chunk evaluation setting.
