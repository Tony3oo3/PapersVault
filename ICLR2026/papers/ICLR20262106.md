## Problem

Lipschitz regularization for implicit neural representations has been applied almost exclusively as a rigid, uniform 1-Lipschitz constraint, leaving two fundamental questions unanswered: how to determine a principled, task-specific total Lipschitz budget K, and how to optimally distribute this budget across network components. This uniform approach limits expressiveness and fails to leverage domain-specific or signal-specific information that could guide both the magnitude and the distribution of regularization strength.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on INRs that map spatial coordinates to signal values: 2D coordinates to RGB for inpainting, 3D coordinates to displacement vectors for deformable registration, 2D coordinates to high-resolution pixels for super-resolution, and 3D coordinates to signed distance values for shape representation. The Lipschitz budget K is derived externally—either from domain priors (e.g., a tissue strain threshold of 2.0 for lung registration) or from a data-driven oracle that computes the maximum spectral norm of the image Jacobian via centered finite differences on a reference image. Per-layer constraints are enforced through spectral normalization, Björck orthonormalization, or SLL layers, with gradient-preserving activations (MaxMin, Householder, GroupSort) used to approach the upper Lipschitz bound.

## Method

The core contribution reframes Lipschitz regularization as a budget allocation problem: given a total budget K_B, per-component Lipschitz constants [K_i] must satisfy the product constraint ∏K_i = K_B (equivalently, a sum constraint in log-space). The paper derives closed-form Lipschitz constants for all INR components—spectral norm for linear layers, frequency-dependent bounds for sinusoidal and Gaussian activations, and geometric-series bounds for positional encodings and random Fourier features. Five allocation strategies are proposed (uniform, all-first, linear, exponential, cosine-annealed), all constrained by a minimum per-component bound K_min, with the non-uniform strategies placing more budget in early layers to preserve expressiveness while tightening later layers for smoothness. The budget K itself is estimated via three routes: domain-driven (clinical tissue strain limits), data-driven (oracle computing max Jacobian spectral norm from reference images), or signal-theoretic (Nyquist-based conservative bound). Experiments across SDFs, lung registration, inpainting, and super-resolution show that non-uniform front-heavy allocation improves expressiveness metrics (TRE, PSNR) while maintaining comparable smoothness (folding ratio), and that reconstruction quality peaks near the oracle-estimated budget.

## Results

On deformable lung CT registration (COPDgene, K_B=2), Lipschitz SIREN with non-uniform (exponential) allocation reduces Target Registration Error (TRE) compared to uniform allocation while maintaining a comparable folding ratio, and outperforms the non-Lipschitz SIREN baseline of Wolterink et al. in 7/10 cases on TRE (e.g., Case 9: 1.30 vs. 21.69) and 8/10 cases on folding ratio. For 1-Lipschitz SDFs (Stanford Bunny), Björck orthonormalization with gradient-preserving activations (MaxMin, Householder) achieves lower Chamfer Distance than spectral normalization with ReLU, and the empirically measured Lipschitz constant correlates strongly with reconstruction quality. In inpainting (CelebA), non-uniform budget allocation yields statistically significant (p<0.05) PSNR/SSIM improvements over uniform allocation, with performance peaking near the data-driven Lipschitz oracle estimate and degrading when the budget deviates from it.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary named baseline is the non-Lipschitz SIREN deformable registration method of Wolterink et al. (2022). The proposed Lipschitz SIREN (with K_B=2 and non-uniform allocation) outperforms this baseline on TRE in 7 out of 10 cases, with the most dramatic improvement in Case 9 (TRE 1.30 vs. 21.69) and Case 7 (1.41 vs. 9.39), where the unconstrained SIREN produces large registration errors. On folding ratio, Lipschitz SIREN wins in 8/10 cases, confirming that the Lipschitz constraint enforces anatomically plausible deformations without auxiliary regularizers. A second comparison is against NODEO, a diffeomorphic registration method that achieves near-zero folding ratios (10^-5 to 10^-7) but at the cost of substantially higher TRE (e.g., 12.55 in Case 8 vs. 1.52 for Lipschitz SIREN), indicating that strict diffeomorphic constraints over-constrain the transformation. The paper also compares internal allocation strategies (uniform vs. exponential/linear/cosine) and normalization techniques (spectral vs. Björck vs. SLL), finding that non-uniform allocation provides statistically significant (p<0.05) gains in K-Lipschitz settings (registration, inpainting) but negligible differences in the 1-Lipschitz SDF setting, where the unit budget is too restrictive to benefit from redistribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the standard practice of applying a rigid, uniform 1-Lipschitz constraint across all INR layers as a flexible 'Lipschitz budget' that can be derived from task-specific properties (tissue compressibility, signal bandwidth) and distributed non-uniformly across network components. It builds directly on the Lipschitz-constrained network literature (spectral normalization, Björck orthonormalization, SLL layers, gradient-preserving activations from Anil et al. 2019) and the INR architecture literature (SIREN, Fourier-feature FFNs), but departs from prior work by treating the global Lipschitz constant K as a meaningful, task-dependent parameter rather than a fixed unit value. The paper also positions its Lipschitz lens as a complementary interpretive framework to NTK theory and Fourier analysis, showing that empirically effective tricks like weight scaling in SIREN (Yeom et al. 2024) can be understood as direct scaling of per-layer Lipschitz bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides practitioners with actionable, interpretable principles for choosing and distributing Lipschitz constraints in INRs, moving beyond the ad-hoc 'set everything to 1' default. The oracle-based budget estimation and the demonstration that non-uniform allocation improves expressiveness while preserving smoothness (particularly in deformable registration and inpainting) offer a practical path to better INR design. However, the optimal allocation strategy remains an open problem treated as hyperparameter search, and the gains, while statistically significant, are modest in magnitude. The broader question of whether learned or data-driven allocation can replace the parametric families (linear, exponential, cosine) explored here remains unresolved.
