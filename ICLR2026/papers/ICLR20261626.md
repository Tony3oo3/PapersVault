## Problem

Existing machine unlearning methods require direct access to the forget set to remove its influence from a trained model, forcing organizations to retain user data even after a deletion request is made and continuing to compound dataset privacy risk. This paper introduces the partially-blind unlearning (PBU) setting, in which unlearning is performed without explicit access to the forget set, relying instead on auxiliary information (cached training gradients) that was collected during the original training process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261626/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

RELOAD takes as input the trained model weights, the summed loss gradients cached at the final training epoch over the full dataset D, and the retain set D_retain; it never sees the forget set. For the language-model variant, the inputs are reduced to a set of prompts inquiring about the target entities, a small repair subset of the retain data (≤195 samples in experiments), and a selected subset of model layers, with contextual fine-tuning used to generate richer gradients from model outputs. The output is a set of unlearned model weights that approximate a model retrained from scratch on D_retain alone, verified via forget accuracy, membership inference attack success rate, and KL-divergence metrics.

## Method

RELOAD combines three stages in sequence. First, it performs a single gradient ascent step in the direction ∇_θ L(D) − ∇_θ L(D_retain), which by linearity of differentiation equals ∇_θ L(D_forget), thereby pushing weights away from the forget-set loss minimum without ever accessing D_forget. Second, it computes a per-weight 'knowledge value' KV_θk = (|∇_θk L(D) − ∇_θk L(D_retain)| + ε) / (|∇_θk L(D)| + ε); weights whose KV falls below the α-quantile of the empirical KV distribution are identified as strongly encoding forget-set information and are selectively reinitialized. Third, the modified model is fine-tuned to convergence on D_retain. For LMs, the gradient direction is replaced by language-modeling gradients computed on contextually-embedded model outputs (via a black-box LM prompt), enabling parameter-efficient unlearning on only a subset of layers with <7% of total weights and <0.025% of retain data.

## Results

On 10% random forgetting (CIFAR-100, ResNet-18), RELOAD achieves ΔFA = 0.30±0.50 and ΔFMIA = 0.01±0.01, substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: SalUn at ΔFA = 13.14, GA at ΔFA = 18.77). On 100 in-class correlated samples, RELOAD attains ΔFA = 3.44±1.46 and ΔFMIA = 0.02±0.02, again the best among all methods. For 1% entity unlearning on Llama-2-7B-Chat, RELOAD achieves forget quality (FQ) = 0.4046 and model utility (MU) = +0.0748, outperforming all baselines including ECO (FQ = 0.9900 but MU = 0.0000) and NPO-RT (FQ = 0.5786, MU = −0.1361), completing in under 8 minutes using <0.025% of the retain set and <7% of model weights.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RELOAD, operating without direct access to the forget set, outperforms all forget-set-dependent baselines (GA, SSD, SCRUB, SalUn, Fisher) on both 10% random and 100 in-class unlearning, achieving ΔFA values 4–25× lower than the next-best method. Among partially-blind baselines (FT, CF-k, EU-k, Fisher), RELOAD dominates on unlearning quality metrics (ΔFA, ΔFMIA) while Fisher marginally edges RELOAD on ΔFA in the correlated setting but at >2× the computational cost of full retraining. For entity unlearning on Llama-2-7B, RELOAD is the only method that simultaneously achieves strong forget quality (FQ = 0.4046, p-value > 0.05) and positive model utility gain (MU = +0.0748), whereas ECO achieves near-perfect FQ (0.9900) but zero utility improvement, and NPO-RT degrades utility (MU = −0.1361). In corrective unlearning, RELOAD maintains high corrected accuracy even at γ = 0.1 (only 10% of corrupted data identified), where baselines like BadT and SCRUB degrade sharply, though BadT slightly outperforms RELOAD on CIFAR-100 poisoning at substantially higher computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

RELOAD composes three established unlearning mechanisms—gradient ascent (Thudi et al., 2022), saliency-based selective weight reinitialisation (Fan et al., 2023), and retain-set fine-tuning (Warnecke et al., 2023)—into a single pipeline that operates under a newly formalised constraint: the partially-blind unlearning (PBU) setting, where the algorithm has no direct access to the forget set and relies only on cached full-dataset gradients from the final training epoch. The key mathematical insight is that the linearity of differentiation allows ∇L(D_forget) to be recovered as ∇L(D) − ∇L(D_retain), eliminating the need to ever touch the forget set. For language models, the method further leverages contextual fine-tuning (Choi et al., 2025) to generate richer gradients from a small prompt set, extending the PBU framework to billion-parameter LLMs without modifying the inference pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete regulatory and privacy gap: under GDPR-style 'right to be forgotten' provisions, organisations must currently retain user data long enough to perform unlearning, perpetuating the very dataset risk the deletion request is meant to eliminate. RELOAD's ability to unlearn without the forget set enables immediate data deletion upon request, directly supporting stricter deletion timelines. Key limitations include the storage cost of caching full-dataset gradients, the entity-unlearning requirement that |D_prompts| ≤ |D_repair| (causing failure at 5–10% forgetting rates), and the absence of theoretical guarantees for the PBU setting. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to contrastively-trained or self-supervised models, and whether decomposable losses are a hard requirement.
