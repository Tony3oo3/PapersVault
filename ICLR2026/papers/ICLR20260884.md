## Problem

Existing multimodal reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MathVista, MathVerse, MMMU) allow MLLMs to bypass genuine visual reasoning by translating image content into textual descriptions and then solving the problem with language-only inference. This conflates visual reasoning with text-based reasoning, inflating apparent model capabilities. VisuLogic addresses this gap by constructing problems where critical visual cues (symmetry, rotation, spatial topology) are lost in any textual description, forcing models to perform vision-centric reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260884/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each problem consists of a small RGB image (width 200–700 px, height 90–825 px, averaging ~592×328 px) depicting geometric patterns, shape sequences, or spatial configurations, paired with a ~40-token text question and four lettered options (A–D). The model must output a single correct option, optionally preceded by a step-by-step reasoning chain. The benchmark contains 1,000 single-choice questions across six categories (Quantitative, Spatial, Positional, Attribute, Stylistic, Other), with correct answers balanced across options (~25% each). A supplementary training set of 4,296 question-answer pairs from the same domains is provided for RL and SFT experiments.

## Method

The benchmark is curated from publicly available visual-logic problems through a three-stage pipeline (automated fetching/cleaning/structuring, quality control via image verification and perceptual-hash deduplication, and manual annotation into six reasoning categories). The key design criterion is that GPT-4o-generated image descriptions omit solution-critical visual details, making the 'MLLM description → LLM' pipeline fail (LLMs score ~25–28%, near the 24.9% random baseline), thereby isolating true visual reasoning. For evaluation, three prompting paradigms are used: direct-answer (Non-CoT), chain-of-thought, and hint prompts (GPT-4o-generated solution guidance without revealing the answer). As a training baseline, the authors apply RLOO (REINFORCE Leave-One-Out) reinforcement learning with rule-based format and accuracy rewards to Qwen2.5-VL-7B and InternVL2.5-38B on the supplementary training set, showing that RL yields 2.0–5.6 percentage-point gains over the base models and substantially outperforms SFT on the same data.

## Results

On the VisuLogic benchmark (1,000 single-choice visual-reasoning questions), the best MLLM, OpenAI-o3, achieves only 29.5% overall accuracy with CoT prompting, compared to 51.4% for humans and a 24.9% random baseline. Among LLMs given GPT-4o-generated image descriptions, Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct leads at 28.0%, while GPT-4 and DeepSeek-R1 score 23.6% and 26.6% respectively. With hint prompts, human accuracy jumps to 83.6%, yet the best MLLM (Doubao-1.6-Vision) reaches only 43.8%. RL-enhanced InternVL2.5-38B-RL attains 31.1%, a 5.6-point gain over its non-RL counterpart (25.5%), establishing the best open-source result.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All 31 evaluated models (8 LLMs + 23 MLLMs) score below 35% overall, far below the 51.4% human benchmark and only marginally above the 24.9% random baseline. Among closed-source MLLMs, OpenAI-o3 leads at 29.5%, followed by Gemini-2.0-Pro (28.0%) and GPT-4o (26.3%); the top open-source MLLM, InternVL3-78B, trails by 1.8 points at 27.7%. LLMs given GPT-4o image descriptions perform comparably to MLLMs (best: Qwen2.5-72B at 28.0%), confirming that textual descriptions alone cannot substitute for direct visual processing. RL training yields the largest single-model improvement: InternVL2.5-38B-RL reaches 31.1% (+5.6 over its SFT baseline of 25.5%), while Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct-RL gains 2.0 points to 28.0%. With hint prompts, humans jump to 83.6% but the best MLLM (Doubao-1.6-Vision) only reaches 43.8%, and several models actually regress on specific categories (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B drops 15.6 points on Style), indicating that hints expose rather than resolve fundamental reasoning gaps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior multimodal reasoning benchmarks (MathVista, MathVision, MMMU, LogicVista, Emma) by explicitly designing tasks where textual description shortcuts fail—SOTA MLLMs omit critical visual cues (symmetry, rotation, spatial relations) when generating image captions, making language-only inference insufficient. Conceptually, it extends the long-standing tradition of visual logic tests (e.g., Raven-style matrices) into the MLLM evaluation setting, while methodologically building on DeepSeek-R1's rule-based reward framework and RLOO for its reinforcement-learning baseline. The key positioning is that existing benchmarks conflate visual reasoning with text-based reasoning, and VisuLogic isolates the former by construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a stark and persistent gap: even the strongest MLLMs (OpenAI-o3 at 29.5%) score barely above the 25% random baseline, while humans achieve 51.4%—and with hints, humans reach 83.6% while models still fall below 50%. The finding that CoT prompting yields negligible gains (<1 point for most models) challenges the assumption that verbalized reasoning chains transfer to visual tasks, pointing to a fundamental architectural limitation in how MLLMs process visual information. Open questions include whether the RL gains observed (up to +5.6 points) can scale to close the human-model gap, and whether the benchmark's six categories adequately capture the full spectrum of visual reasoning or whether additional dimensions (e.g., temporal, causal) are needed.
