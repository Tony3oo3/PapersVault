## Problem

DPO's reliance on a reference policy introduces a training-inference mismatch: it optimizes a reference-relative margin (Δ_θ − Δ_ref) while inference depends on absolute log-likelihoods. For pessimistic pairs where the reference prefers the rejected response (Δ_ref < 0), DPO prematurely attenuates the gradient as soon as the relative margin turns positive, even though the policy is still wrong in absolute terms (Δ_θ < 0). This 'premature satisfaction' creates a tension: reference-free methods fix the mismatch but discard the stabilizing reference signal, while standard DPO retains stability but systematically under-learns on pessimistic pairs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes offline pairwise preference data (x, y_w, y_l) with a fixed reference model π_ref, operating on sequence-level log-likelihood margins Δ_θ and Δ_ref computed over full response sequences (max length 2048 tokens). It produces an aligned policy π_θ that generates text responses with improved absolute likelihood ordering matching human preferences. The modification is a single-line change to the DPO loss argument, requiring no additional model parameters, forward passes, or computational overhead beyond standard DPO training.

## Method

HyPO (Hybrid-DPO) conditionally debiases the reference signal by clipping the reference margin in the DPO loss: the relative margin Δ_θ − Δ_ref is replaced with Δ_θ − max{γ, Δ_ref} (default γ=0). When the reference is optimistic or neutral (Δ_ref ≥ 0), HyPO behaves identically to DPO, preserving KL-induced proximal stability. When the reference is pessimistic (Δ_ref < 0), clipping nullifies the misleading anchor and the loss reduces to an absolute-margin logistic update, pointwise dominating the reference-free gradient weight and preventing premature attenuation. A softplus variant (Eq. 13) provides a globally smooth alternative with parameter α controlling transition sharpness. In practice, HyPO is combined with a pre-aligned same-family reference model and a uniform home-advantage margin h to further strengthen learning pressure, while retaining DPO's logistic loss form and β temperature for compatibility with existing DPO extensions.

## Results

Across four model settings (Mistral-7B Base/Instruct, Llama-3-8B Base/Instruct), HyPO achieves the best AlpacaEval 2.0 length-controlled win rate in every setting, ranging from 32.8% (Mistral-Base) to 49.5% (Llama-3-Instruct), and the best Arena-Hard win rate from 13.9% to 35.2%. The paper reports a 41.2% average relative improvement over DPO and a 15.1% average relative improvement over SimPO across all settings. On downstream tasks (MMLU, ARC, HellaSwag, TruthfulQA, Winogrande, GSM8K), HyPO maintains or improves average scores relative to the initial model, indicating no significant alignment tax.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyPO outperforms all eight baselines across all four model settings on both AlpacaEval 2.0 and Arena-Hard. Against the primary baseline DPO, HyPO achieves a 41.2% average relative improvement (e.g., 34.7% vs. 24.3% LC on Llama-3-Base; 49.5% vs. 40.9% LC on Llama-3-Instruct). Against the strongest reference-free competitor SimPO, HyPO delivers a 15.1% average relative improvement, with the largest gap on Mistral-Base (32.8% vs. 27.3% LC) and a consistent lead on Llama-3-Instruct (49.5% vs. 46.0% LC). Among DPO-variant baselines, TR-DPO and RainbowPO are the closest competitors on Llama-3-Base (31.8% and 30.3% LC vs. HyPO's 34.7%), but HyPO still leads by 2.9–4.4 points. On Arena-Hard, the gaps are even more pronounced: HyPO leads DPO by up to 10.5 points (33.5% vs. 23.0% on Llama-3-Base) and SimPO by up to 3.4 points (33.5% vs. 30.1%). SLiC-HF, CPO, and KTO trail significantly behind HyPO in all settings, with SLiC-HF achieving the lowest scores (e.g., 11.6% LC on Mistral-Base vs. HyPO's 32.8%).

## Relation to Prior Work

HyPO is a direct modification of DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), positioned as a principled middle ground between two opposing lines of work: reference-free objectives (SimPO, RRHF, SLiC-HF) that discard the reference model to eliminate training-inference mismatch, and better-reference methods (TR-DPO, RainbowPO, Pre-DPO) that strengthen the reference while leaving the DPO loss unchanged. The paper's key conceptual departure is identifying 'premature satisfaction'—the phenomenon where DPO's gradient weight attenuates exponentially on pessimistic pairs (Δ_ref < 0) even when the policy's absolute margin is still negative—and addressing it with a one-line clipping of the reference margin to max{0, Δ_ref}. This conditional mechanism preserves DPO's KL-induced proximality where the reference is reliable while reverting to an absolute-margin update where it is misleading, without introducing additional parameters or computational cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is high: a single-line loss modification yields a 41.2% average relative improvement over DPO and 15.1% over SimPO across multiple model families and scales, with negligible (~1%) runtime overhead, making it an easy drop-in for existing DPO pipelines. The finding that ~45% of preference pairs remain in the pessimistic region even under a strong SimPO-aligned reference underscores that the training-inference mismatch is not an artifact of a weak reference but a structural property of the DPO objective. Open questions include whether the mismatch persists on optimistic pairs (where HyPO retains DPO behavior), how the method interacts with severe label noise (where a pessimistic reference may be correct), and whether adaptive or learned clipping thresholds could further improve performance beyond the fixed γ=0 default.
