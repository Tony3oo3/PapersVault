## Problem

Target-dependent speculative decoding methods (EAGLE, Medusa) require training a separate draft head for every target model variant, creating substantial adaptation and deployment costs. Target-independent vanilla speculative decoding avoids this coupling but remains slow because the draft model generates K candidate tokens autoregressively over K sequential forward passes. No existing method simultaneously achieves target-independence, parallel token prediction, and low training cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264912/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

PARD takes a pre-trained small language model (e.g., LLaMA3.2-1B) as the fully decoupled draft model and a larger target model (e.g., LLaMA3.1-8B) for verification, with no target-model features fed into the draft. During inference, a text prompt is processed by the draft model in a single forward pass that outputs K candidate tokens (K=8 by default) via shared mask-token placeholders, which the target model then verifies in parallel. The same trained draft model can be swapped across an entire model family (e.g., LLaMA3-8B/70B/405B) without retraining.

## Method

PARD replaces the autoregressive draft phase with parallel prediction by inserting a shared mask token m_k at each future position, so the draft model predicts all K tokens in one forward pass (reducing draft time from K·T_D to T_D). Training adapts a standard AR model into this parallel form by decomposing the objective into K subtasks (predicting the +1, +2, …, +K token), each with its own attention mask ensuring train-inference consistency. To cut the resulting K×N training-token overhead, the COD mechanism selectively drops tokens in later subtasks using a geometric-decay retention rate r (e.g., r=0.7), while guaranteeing that every retained token still has complete prefix key-value states for attention. This reduces effective training tokens from K·N to approximately N/(1−r) (≈2N for r=0.5), yielding a 3× training speedup with no accuracy loss. A minimum retention floor r_min prevents excessive dropping in the deepest subtasks, and the shared mask-token ID across positions grants extrapolation capability, allowing inference with K_infer > K_train.

## Results

On LLaMA3.1-8B under vLLM, PARD achieves 264.88 tokens/s on HumanEval (3.63× speedup over autoregressive baseline), 235.09 tokens/s on GSM8K (3.20×), and 219.15 tokens/s average across benchmarks (3.00×). PARD outperforms EAGLE-3 by 1.15× in average speedup (3.00× vs 2.65×) and vanilla speculative decoding by 1.63× (3.00× vs 1.84×). Across the Qwen and LLaMA3 families, PARD achieves average speedups ranging from 2.65× to 3.71×, with the largest gains on smaller models (Q2.5-14B: 3.71×) and consistent improvements on larger models (L3.3-70B: 3.49×).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PARD consistently outperforms all three baselines across every target model and benchmark. Against vanilla speculative decoding (VSD, using LLaMA3.2-1B or Qwen2.5-0.5B as draft), PARD achieves 1.63× higher average speedup on LLaMA3.1-8B (3.00× vs 1.84×) and up to 1.25× on Q2.5-14B (3.71× vs 2.97×), primarily because PARD's single forward pass for drafting eliminates the K sequential draft passes. Against EAGLE, PARD is 1.9× faster on LLaMA3.1-8B (3.00× vs 1.58× average speedup) and also achieves higher first-token acceptance rates (0.93 vs 0.83 on HumanEval). Against EAGLE-3, the closest competitor, PARD still leads by 1.15× on LLaMA3.1-8B (3.00× vs 2.65×) and by 1.08× on L3.3-70B (3.49× vs 3.22×), while consuming significantly less draft-phase memory bandwidth (2.48 GB constant vs 5.94–11.88 GB that scales with K). A critical structural advantage is target-independence: a single PARD draft model accelerates the entire LLaMA3 family (8B, 70B, 405B) and Qwen family without retraining, whereas EAGLE and EAGLE-3 require a separately trained draft head for each target model.

## Relation to Prior Work

PARD sits at the intersection of two established lines of speculative decoding research: target-independent vanilla SD (Chen et al., Leviathan et al.) and parallel/masked-prediction decoding (Mask-Predict, PaSS, BiTA, ParallelSpec). It explicitly departs from the dominant target-dependent paradigm (EAGLE, Medusa, LayerSkip, Kangaroo) by using a fully decoupled small language model as the draft, while incorporating Mask-Predict's parallel token prediction to eliminate the autoregressive bottleneck of vanilla SD. The COD training mechanism is a novel contribution that addresses the K-fold training cost inflation inherent in mask-token adaptation, leveraging prefix KV-state integrity to sparsify training data without degrading accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The target-independence property has significant practical implications: a single trained draft model can accelerate an entire model family (e.g., LLaMA3-8B through 405B), dramatically reducing the per-model adaptation and deployment cost that plagues EAGLE-style methods. The constant bandwidth consumption during drafting (2.48 GB regardless of K) versus the linearly growing cost of EAGLE is an architectural advantage that becomes more pronounced as draft length increases. Open questions include how well PARD generalizes to non-LLaMA/Qwen architectures, whether the acceptance-rate advantage over EAGLE persists on more diverse or out-of-distribution tasks, and how the method interacts with tree-based verification or larger batch sizes where the speedup diminishes (1.33× at bs=16).
