## Problem

When neural networks are reinitialized to restore plasticity during continual learning, standard methods face a tuning dilemma: conservative resets fail to recover plasticity while aggressive ones erase useful prior knowledge. No existing reinitialization strategy provides a principled, closed-form balance between retaining learned representations and restoring favorable weight geometry for new data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262897/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FIRE takes the trained weight matrices of each layer (W ∈ R^{d_out × d_in} for linear layers, W ∈ R^{C_out × C_in × k_h × k_w} for convolutions) and produces orthogonalized counterparts. For linear layers, the output is scaled by √(d_out/d_in) to preserve signal variance; for convolutions, each spatial slice is orthogonalized independently and scaled by √(C_out/C_in)/(k_h·k_w). In ViTs, orthogonalization is restricted to Q and K projections only. The method requires only a fixed number of Newton-Schulz iterations (5–10) and adds less than 1% to training time.

## Method

FIRE formulates reinitialization as the constrained optimization min_W̃ ‖W − W̃‖²_F s.t. W̃ᵀW̃ = I, which is the Orthogonal Procrustes Problem with closed-form solution W̃* = W(WᵀW)^{-1/2}. Stability is quantified by Squared Frobenius Error (SFE) and plasticity by Deviation from Isometry (DfI = ‖WᵀW − I‖²_F); the constraint DfI = 0 enforces orthonormality. The paper proves that minimizing DfI simultaneously bounds Hessian spectral norm (smoother loss landscape), lower-bounds effective feature rank, and tightens neuron activity scores (fewer dormant neurons), while SFE upper-bounds the discrepancy in normalized output feature covariances between two networks. In practice, the polar decomposition is approximated via Newton-Schulz iteration (X_{k+1} = 1.5X_k − 0.5X_k(X_kᵀX_k)), applied layer-wise after training on current data and before new data arrive, requiring no hyperparameter tuning beyond the iteration count.

## Results

FIRE is evaluated across three domains: continual visual learning (CIFAR-10/ResNet-18, CIFAR-100/ViT-Tiny, Tiny-ImageNet/VGG-16), continual LLM pretraining (GPT-0.1B on WikiText-103 → OpenWebText+WikiText-103), and reinforcement learning (Atari/DQN, HumanoidBench/SAC). In continual visual learning, FIRE consistently outperforms all baselines on CIFAR-10/ResNet-18 and Tiny-ImageNet/VGG-16 in warm-start, continual, and class-incremental settings, with only a slight or negligible performance drop after reinitialization (vs. sharp drops for full reset and DASH). In LLM continual pretraining, FIRE maintains strong validation loss even when initialized from the 60k checkpoint where the base model has severely degraded, using a fixed 5 Newton-Schulz iterations without tuning. In RL, FIRE achieves superior or competitive performance across all Atari and HumanoidBench environments. Computationally, FIRE adds less than 1% to training time (0.06 sec per reinitialization vs. 69 sec for DASH) with 55 MB GPU memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIRE outperforms Shrink & Perturb (S&P) across all three domains: in vision, S&P avoids sharp drops but remains suboptimal compared to FIRE; in LLM pretraining, S&P (tuned over varying λ) is outperformed by FIRE (fixed 5 iterations, no tuning); in RL, S&P provides slight convergence speedup but is still suboptimal relative to FIRE. DASH is competitive on CIFAR-100/ViT-Tiny (slightly ahead of FIRE) but suffers sharp performance drops after each reset in continual and class-incremental settings, and has the highest SFE (most aggressive reset) in the ablation. Parseval Regularization and L2init (Regen) both converge more slowly than FIRE, particularly on CIFAR-10/ResNet-18 and Tiny-ImageNet/VGG-16, because continuously enforcing orthogonality during training hinders convergence. Node-resetting methods (CBP, SNR, ReDo) show poor overall performance across all visual learning settings, consistent with prior findings that maintaining trainability provides limited generalization gains. The Muon optimizer, which also uses Newton-Schulz iteration but on gradients rather than weights, performs poorly overall, confirming that periodic weight reinitialization (FIRE) is substantially more effective than gradient orthogonalization. In RL, Plasticity Injection (which adds fresh prediction heads) shows poor performance across both discrete and continuous control, suggesting that manual hyperparameter tuning for stability-plasticity balance is less effective than FIRE's principled constrained optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

FIRE repurposes the classical Orthogonal Procrustes Problem (Schonemann, 1966) as a principled reinitialization mechanism, departing from heuristic shrink-and-perturb strategies (S&P, DASH) and continuous regularization approaches (Parseval, L2init) that require careful hyperparameter tuning. The plasticity metric (DfI) draws from the dynamical isometry literature (Pennington et al., 2017; Xiao et al., 2018), while the stability metric (SFE) is grounded in feature covariance analysis. Unlike the Muon optimizer, which applies Newton-Schulz iteration to gradient updates, FIRE applies it directly to weight matrices at discrete reinitialization points, and unlike node-level reset methods (CBP, ReDo, SNR), it operates on full weight matrices without per-neuron scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FIRE offers a hyperparameter-free (beyond a robust iteration count) reinitialization that consistently outperforms tuned baselines across vision, language, and RL, making it a practical drop-in for continual learning pipelines. The theoretical unification showing that a single DfI constraint simultaneously controls loss curvature, effective rank, and neuron dormancy provides a clean mechanistic explanation for why orthogonalization restores plasticity. Key limitations include the assumption of access to past data (excluding the most common continual learning setting), evaluation only on small models (GPT-0.1B), and the fact that DASH still outperforms FIRE on ViT-Tiny, suggesting data-dependent strategies may complement orthogonalization for transformer architectures.
