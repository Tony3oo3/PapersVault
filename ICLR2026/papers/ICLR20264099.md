## Problem

Continual learning with frozen large pre-trained models (LPMs) faces a severe stability-plasticity dilemma: adaptation is restricted to shallow modules, limiting plasticity while the fixed backbone amplifies catastrophic forgetting. Existing methods for this setting rely on heuristic expansion triggers and lack formal guarantees on when to grow capacity or how much prior knowledge is preserved, making long-horizon learning unreliable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) or text sequences (aspect-based sentiment classification across 19 product domains) and produces class labels. Inputs first pass through a frozen encoder (CLIP ViT-B/16 or BERT-base) to yield fixed-dimensional features, which are then routed through a dynamically expandable set of memory slots before a lightweight classifier produces the final prediction. The slot count grows from an initial S₀=32 during training, and the system operates under a class-incremental protocol where tasks arrive sequentially with disjoint label sets.

## Method

MARS decouples stable representation from adaptive capacity via three components: a frozen encoder, a slot-based memory router with per-slot affine adapters, and a lightweight classifier. Two mechanisms govern its behavior: (i) Statistically-Grounded Slot Expansion (SGSE) formulates when to add new slots as a statistical decision problem, using router-aligned novelty detection (top-slot softmax confidence) tracked via an exponentially smoothed quantile, with a one-sided Wilson score interval providing a provable false-alarm bound of α per test; (ii) Dual-Stage Contrastive-Distillation Adaptation (DCDA) separates integration into Stage 1 (supervised contrastive learning on memory parameters only, with a smoothness tether to frozen features) and Stage 2 (cross-entropy plus LwF and anchor-based KL distillation on the classifier only). Anchors—maintained via router-weighted EMA of routed features—serve as compressed surrogates of past knowledge, enabling retention without raw replay and yielding a provable O(√η + Lδ/T) bound on old-class prediction deviation.

## Results

Under the frozen-encoder setting, MARS achieves 57.50% average accuracy on CIFAR-100, 49.46% on Tiny-ImageNet, and 79.85% on ASC, consistently outperforming all baselines by 3–6 percentage points. On the larger ImageNet-100 benchmark, MARS reaches 42.08%, surpassing the best frozen-backbone baseline (39.67%) by 2.4 points. MARS uses only 0.2M trainable parameters (vs. 0.5–0.8M for prompt-based baselines) while incurring minimal inference overhead (8.5 ms/batch vs. 7.8–8.1 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MARS outperforms all seven baselines across every benchmark under the frozen-encoder protocol. Against replay-based methods, MARS beats DER++ (Buzzega et al., 2020) by 5.8 points on CIFAR-100 (57.50 vs. 51.72) and 8.6 points on Tiny-ImageNet (49.46 vs. 40.87), and surpasses PASS++ (Zhu et al., 2025) by 4.6 and 6.9 points respectively. The regularization-based EWC (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) trails MARS by 9.9 points on CIFAR-100 and 13.1 points on Tiny-ImageNet, confirming that parameter-space regularization is insufficient for frozen LPMs. The exemplar-free LDC (Gomez-Villa et al., 2024) is the strongest conventional baseline but still lags MARS by 3.6 points on CIFAR-100 and 6.1 points on Tiny-ImageNet. Among prompt-based PEFT methods, MARS exceeds L2P (Wang et al., 2022) by 5.2 points on CIFAR-100, CODA-Prompt (Smith et al., 2023) by 2.8 points, and CLAP4CLIP (Jha et al., 2024) by 2.1 points, while using roughly half the trainable parameters (0.2M vs. 0.5–0.8M). On ASC, MARS leads DER++ by 3.9 points and CODA-Prompt by 5.2 points, demonstrating consistent gains in the NLP domain as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

MARS synthesizes ideas from three distinct threads: dynamic capacity expansion (Progressive Neural Networks, DEN, CEAT), prototype-based memory (IPC, PASS++), and statistical process control (Wilson score intervals, EMA-based change detection). It departs from prior dynamic expansion work by replacing heuristic growth triggers with a formal hypothesis-testing framework, and from replay-based methods (iCaRL, DER++) by substituting raw exemplars with anchor-based distillation that carries provable retention bounds. The slot-based router is conceptually related to mixture-of-experts routing but is repurposed here as a continual-learning capacity allocator rather than a static inference-time mechanism, and the frozen-encoder constraint ties the design to the parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm (LoRA, adapters, prompts) without modifying the backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical contribution is a principled, low-parameter (0.2M trainable) continual learning framework for frozen LPMs that avoids raw replay while providing explicit statistical guarantees on expansion frequency and knowledge retention—something prior CL methods with frozen backbones (L2P, CODA-Prompt, DIKI) lack. Limitations noted by the authors include dependence on a high-quality frozen encoder for fine-grained novel domains, sensitivity to hyperparameters (β, S₀, Wilson threshold), and residual memory costs from anchors. Open questions include whether the i.i.d./stationarity assumptions underlying the Wilson test hold under severe non-stationary streams, how the framework extends to non-classification tasks (e.g., generation, retrieval), and whether the statistical guarantees remain tight in the long-horizon regime where N_T grows sublinearly.
