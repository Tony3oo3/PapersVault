## Problem

Existing LLM-based Automatic Heuristic Design (AHD) methods treat the language model as a frozen generator and steer heuristic evolution solely through prompt manipulation (verbal gradients), leaving the model's generative capability unadapted to the feedback from heuristic performance. This paper asks whether the LLM itself can be co-evolved alongside the search process by treating the prompt-response-performance triplets produced during evolution as training signal for reinforcement learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262458/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CALM takes a problem description, a set of training instances for heuristic evaluation, optional seed heuristics, and a local INT4-quantized Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model as input. It produces an executable heuristic (Python code implementing a decision function such as next-city selection or edge-desirability scoring) along with fine-tuned LLM weights (only 1.15% of parameters updated). The entire pipeline runs on a single 24 GB GPU, with each heuristic evaluated within a 60-second time budget and a fixed budget of 2,000 LLM queries across all tasks.

## Method

CALM integrates two complementary guidance signals in an evolutionary loop: (1) verbal guidance via a suite of fine-granularity evolutionary operators (injection, replacement, diversity-aware crossover, simplification, initialization) that generate structured prompts from a maintained heuristic pool, and (2) numerical guidance via GRPO-based reinforcement learning that fine-tunes the LLM using a reward function comparing each generated heuristic's performance against the best base heuristic in its prompt. A probabilistic collapse mechanism resets the population to the seed and best heuristic when stagnation is detected, escaping local optima. The reward function assigns progressive scores across infeasible, duplicate, new, and high-performing heuristics, with relative improvement over the prompt's best base modulating the reward magnitude to avoid attributing credit to prompt bias. This co-evolution allows the compact 7B model to internalize characteristics of successful heuristics, ultimately outperforming API-based SOTA methods that use more powerful but frozen models.

## Results

On Online Bin Packing (OBP), CALM with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-INT4 + GRPO achieves an average optimality gap of 0.71% across six test scales, outperforming the best LLM-based baseline MCTS-AHD (0.89%) and hand-crafted Best Fit (2.40%), with a zero gap (exact optimal) on the 1k_500 scale. On CVRP, CALM (Qwen+GRPO) achieves 3.83% gap at N=50, 5.44% at N=100, and 3.95% at N=200, beating MCTS-AHD (5.44%, 6.98%, 4.70%) on all scales. On the Orienteering Problem, CALM (Qwen+GRPO) reaches 12.58% gap at N=200 versus 16.34% for MCTS-AHD. On TSP (step-by-step construction), CALM (Qwen+GRPO) achieves 13.41% gap at N=200, surpassing MCTS-AHD (13.71%) and POMO (20.45%), though it trails MCTS-AHD slightly at N=50 (10.04% vs. 9.69%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On OBP, CALM (Qwen+GRPO) achieves the lowest average gap (0.71%) among all methods, beating MCTS-AHD (0.89%), FunSearch (0.99%), and hand-crafted Best Fit (2.40%); it achieves a zero gap on 1k_500, indicating exact optimal solutions. On CVRP, CALM (Qwen+GRPO) outperforms MCTS-AHD on all three scales (3.83% vs. 5.44% at N=50; 5.44% vs. 6.98% at N=100; 3.95% vs. 4.70% at N=200) and all other LLM-based baselines. On OP, CALM (Qwen+GRPO) similarly dominates, achieving 12.58% at N=200 versus 16.34% for MCTS-AHD and 20.32% for EvoTune. On TSP, CALM (Qwen+GRPO) wins at N=100 (11.58% vs. 11.79% MCTS-AHD) and N=200 (13.41% vs. 13.71%), but trails MCTS-AHD slightly at N=50 (10.04% vs. 9.69%); notably, CALM surpasses the NCO method POMO at N=200 (13.41% vs. 20.45%) where POMO requires per-scale training. The API-based variant of CALM (GPT-4o-mini, w/o GRPO) also matches or exceeds MCTS-AHD on most scales, confirming the strength of the verbal-gradient design independent of RL. Against the concurrent RL-based method EvoTune (same Qwen model), CALM achieves substantially lower gaps across all tasks (e.g., 0.71% vs. 2.40% on OBP), demonstrating the benefit of the co-evolutionary framework over DPO-style fine-tuning alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-based Automatic Heuristic Design (AHD) paradigm established by FunSearch, EoH, ReEvo, HSEvo, and MCTS-AHD, where an LLM generates candidate heuristics guided by evolutionary prompt manipulation ('verbal gradients'). The key departure is introducing 'numerical gradients': the LLM itself is fine-tuned via GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) using heuristic performance as the reward signal, enabling co-evolution of the model and the search process. Concurrent work (EvoTune, Surina et al. 2025) also explores RL-based fine-tuning for AHD but uses DPO with preference pairs, whereas CALM employs score-based GRPO and adds specialized evolutionary operators (fine-granularity injection/replacement, diversity-aware crossover, collapse mechanism) that are designed to produce more informative training signal for the RL loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CALM demonstrates that a 7B INT4-quantized model on a single 24GB GPU, when co-evolved with the heuristic search, can match or exceed methods relying on GPT-4o-mini APIs, making high-quality AHD accessible without commercial LLM dependencies. The co-evolution paradigm—treating the evolutionary loop as a natural source of RL training data—generalizes beyond AHD to any setting where an LLM generates artifacts whose quality is measurable. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models and longer training horizons, whether the fine-tuned model transfers to unseen problem types (early evidence suggests partial transfer from CVRP to OP), and how the method behaves when no seed heuristic is available.
