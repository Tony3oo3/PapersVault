## Problem

While almost tight competitive bounds exist for the fractional online facility location problem with machine learning advice, the integral version remains significantly less understood, with prior work incurring an O(log log n) overhead over the best fractional solution. This paper addresses the gap by presenting the first online rounding algorithms for metric facility location, showing that the hardness of the classic online problem lies in computing a good fractional solution rather than rounding it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261761/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The rounding algorithms consume a fractional facility location solution (specified by non-decreasing y-variables on a metric space revealed online) and produce a monotone integral solution — a set of open facilities that only grows over time. In the learning-augmented setting, the fractional solution is constructed from k predictions per arriving vertex, and the algorithm must achieve consistency with the best prediction while remaining robust to adversarial ones. The uniform algorithm is deterministic; the non-uniform algorithm is randomized with expected cost bounded by O(log log Δ) times the fractional cost, where Δ is the metric aspect ratio.

## Method

The paper introduces two online rounding algorithms. For uniform opening costs, a deterministic algorithm uses a three-level covering scheme: Level-1 balls (fractional mass ≥ 1/2, no facility within 4R) trigger facility openings, Level-2 balls (mass ≥ 1/4, no facility within 3r) handle off-center demand, and Level-3 balls (mass ≥ 1/8, no facility within 2ρ) cover isolated pockets; a key structural lemma proves Level-3 balls under a Level-2 ball are mutually disjoint (at most 3), bounding total facilities to 36× the fractional mass while guaranteeing 4-consistency. For non-uniform costs, a randomized algorithm maintains per-vertex level counters, identifies critical balls (mass ≥ 1/2 with no earlier-overlapping critical ball of radius ≤ 2R) in lexicographic order, and opens facilities with probability proportional to fractional mass within a level-filtered subset, incurring an O(log log Δ) expected overhead. Both algorithms are paired with the fractional algorithm of Anand et al. (2022) to yield the first integral learning-augmented facility location algorithms with near-tight consistency O(log(k+1)) and robustness O(log t / log log t) bounds.

## Results

The paper establishes two main rounding guarantees: a deterministic online rounding algorithm for uniform facility location that achieves an O(1) competitive ratio (Theorem 2), and a randomized online rounding algorithm for non-uniform facility location achieving O(log log Δ) in expectation (Theorem 9), where Δ is the aspect ratio of the metric space. Applied to the learning-augmented setting, these yield the first integral algorithms with cost O(min{log(k+1)·dynamic_t, (log t)/(log log t)·opt_t}) for the uniform case (Theorem 11) and O(min{log log Δ · log(k+1)·dynamic_t, (log t)/(log log t)·opt_t}) for the non-uniform case (Theorem 12), matching the lower bounds up to lower-order terms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against Almanza et al. (2021), whose integral algorithm for uniform facility location with multiple predictions incurred an O(log log n) cost overhead relative to the best fractional solution and required all predictions to be presented before any client arrives. The present work improves this to an O(1) overhead (via the deterministic rounding algorithm) and removes the upfront-prediction requirement. Against Anand et al. (2022), which provided only a fractional online algorithm for the learning-augmented problem, this paper bridges the gap to integral solutions, yielding the first integral algorithms for both uniform and non-uniform learning-augmented facility location. The robustness bound of O(log t / log log t) matches the known lower bound of Fotakis (2008), and the consistency bound of O(log(k+1)) is tight up to lower-order terms given the Ω(log k / log log k) lower bound from prior work. For the non-uniform case, the O(log log Δ) rounding loss is shown to be asymptotically tight (Appendix D), meaning any improvement would require fundamentally new techniques.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the fractional online algorithm for learning-augmented facility location from Anand et al. (2022), which provided near-optimal consistency and robustness guarantees but only for the fractional (LP-relaxed) problem. The key departure is the introduction of the first online rounding algorithms for metric facility location, exploiting the underlying metric structure (triangle inequality, ball geometry) in a way that is fundamentally different from prior online rounding work on packing/matching problems (Buchbinder et al. 2023, Naor et al. 2025) or from non-metric facility location rounding (Alon et al. 2006, Bienkowski et al. 2020) where logarithmic loss is unavoidable. The three-level covering scheme for the uniform case and the critical-ball/level-counter mechanism for the non-uniform case are novel techniques that leverage metric geometry to achieve sub-logarithmic rounding loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the hardness of classic online facility location resides in computing a good fractional solution rather than in rounding it, a clean structural separation that clarifies where future algorithmic effort should be directed. The O(log log Δ) loss in the non-uniform case is shown to be asymptotically tight (Appendix D), so closing the gap to a constant factor would require fundamentally new ideas. Open questions include whether the fractional algorithm of Anand et al. (2022) can be improved to achieve the lower-bound-optimal O(log k / log log k) consistency, and whether a constant-factor rounding algorithm exists for the non-uniform setting. For the learning-augmented algorithms community, these results bridge the long-standing gap between fractional and integral guarantees in the multiple-predictions setting.
