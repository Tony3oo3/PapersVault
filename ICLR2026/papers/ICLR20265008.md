## Problem

Existing VLM benchmarks remain heavily Western-centric, leaving open questions about how these models generalize to culturally diverse and multilingual settings. India, with 22 official languages and 28 states each with distinct cultural identities, lacks a systematic, large-scale benchmark that captures India-specific cultural concepts across multiple Indic languages and multimodal tasks simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265008/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark spans three tracks with distinct I/O formats: (1) VQA takes a cultural RGB image paired with a text question in English or one of 10 Indic languages and produces a free-form or structured answer, evaluated via Exact Match for MCQ/True-False and GPT-4o LLM-as-a-Judge (0–10 scale) for short/long/adversarial answers; (2) OCR takes high-resolution Wikisource page scans (Level-4 human-verified) and produces transcribed text, scored by ANLS, WER, and CER; (3) MMT takes an image plus an English caption and produces a translated caption in a target Indic language, scored by BLEU and RIBES. The VQA track includes six question types per image (two short-answer, one long-answer, one MCQ, one True/False, and one adversarial question embedding false assumptions), totaling 37K+ QA pairs over 5K images across 13 cultural topics.

## Method

IndicVisionBench is constructed through a multi-stage curation pipeline. For VQA, images are collected via two phases—crowd-sourced personal photos (615 samples) and Creative Commons web crawling (~3,502 images, ~100 per State/UT)—then annotated with human keywords, expanded into synthetic captions via VLMs, and used to generate six QAs per image using Gemini-1.5-Flash and Gemini-2.5-Flash, followed by human verification for factual and cultural accuracy. A subset is translated into dominant regional languages, and a disjoint 106-image set is translated into all 10 Indic languages to form a parallel corpus for cross-lingual analysis. The OCR track pairs high-resolution Wikisource scans with Level-4 verified text across 10 Indic scripts, while the MMT track reuses the 106 parallel images with manually annotated image-grounded translations. Evaluation covers 8 VLMs (Gemini-2.5, GPT-4o, Gemma-3-27B, LLaMA-4-Maverick, Maya, PALO, Pangea, Chitrarth-1) plus OCR-specific (Chitrapathak, Surya) and MMT-specific (Chitranuvad) models, with task-appropriate metrics including ANLS for OCR, BLEU/RIBES for translation, and a combination of Exact Match and LLM-as-a-Judge for VQA.

## Results

On IndicVisionBench-VQA (English), Gemini-2.5 achieves the highest scores across all six question types: MCQ 0.94, True/False 0.95, Long-answer 9.30/10, Short-1 8.58/10, Short-2 8.49/10, and Adversarial 5.79/10. GPT-4o follows closely (MCQ 0.90, Long 8.75, Adversarial 2.95), while LLaMA-4 and Gemma-3 are competitive open-weight alternatives (Long ~8.55, Adversarial ~1.5–2.6). In MMT, Gemini-2.5 leads with BLEU scores of 28.65–53.27 across 10 Indic languages, roughly 10–15 points above LLaMA-4 and Gemma-3. In OCR, Gemini-2.5 achieves the lowest ANLS (word-level) across all languages (e.g., 11.30 Bengali, 8.06 Marathi), substantially outperforming GPT-4o (55.51–95.97) and open-weight models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini-2.5 (proprietary) dominates all three tracks, leading VQA by 0.5–3.5 points over GPT-4o on open-ended questions and by ~10–15 BLEU points in MMT. GPT-4o is a strong VQA challenger (second on MCQ, Long, Short-1, Short-2) but performs surprisingly poorly in OCR (word-level ANLS of 55–96 vs. Gemini-2.5's 8–60), suggesting limited Indic script recognition. Among open-weight models, LLaMA-4 (17B) and Gemma-3 (27B) are the strongest, with LLaMA-4 edging ahead on True/False (0.92 vs. 0.88) and Adversarial (2.62 vs. 1.50) in English VQA, while Gemma-3 leads in MMT for most languages (e.g., Gujarati BLEU 35.76 vs. 29.84). The 7B models (Maya, PALO, Pangea, Chitrarth-1) trail substantially, scoring 1–2 points lower on VQA and 10–20 BLEU lower in MMT; Chitrarth-1 is the best 7B for long/short answers while Pangea leads on MCQ. Chitranuvad (fine-tuned Chitrarth-1) improves over its base in Hindi, Kannada, Malayalam, and Telugu MMT but underperforms in Bengali despite targeted fine-tuning. Adversarial questions (embedding false cultural assumptions) remain the hardest task for all models, with even Gemini-2.5 scoring only 5.79/10 in English and dropping to 2.73–5.71 across Indic languages.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying three established multimodal tasks—VQA, OCR, and MMT—into a single culturally grounded benchmark for the Indian subcontinent, a setting where no prior work offers a comprehensive, multilingual evaluation framework. It draws on the evaluation methodologies of OCRBench v2 (ANLS), WMT multimodal translation shared tasks (BLEU/RIBES), and LLM-as-a-Judge scoring from ALM-Bench, but departs from prior cultural VQA efforts (CVQA, CulturalVQA, ALM-Bench) by explicitly covering 10 medium-to-low-resource Indic languages, 13 cultural topics, and adversarial question types that probe false-assumption rejection. The OCR track repurposes Wikisource's Level-4 verified pages as ground truth, and the MMT track extends the VQA-Parallel subset into a vision-grounded parallel corpus, avoiding the data contamination concerns of Visual Genome-based prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IndicVisionBench is the first large-scale, publicly available benchmark (5K images, 37K+ QA pairs, 10 Indic languages) that systematically exposes how current VLMs fail on culturally specific and low-resource linguistic content, providing a reproducible foundation for building more inclusive multimodal systems. Key open questions include whether the LLM-generated annotation pipeline introduces residual self-enhancement bias, whether standard metrics (ANLS, BLEU, LLM-judge) adequately capture cultural grounding, and whether the visual modality contributes meaningfully to MMT performance (left unexplored here). The benchmark's public release on HuggingFace positions it as a likely standard reference for evaluating Indic-language VLMs in future work.
