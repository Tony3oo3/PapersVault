## Problem

Offline RL policy-constraint methods (e.g., TD3+BC) require a manually tuned scale factor α to balance the RL objective against behavior cloning, and this optimal value varies substantially across tasks and dataset quality. Existing adaptive variants only reweight individual actions locally and ignore the global trade-off scale, leaving a significant performance gap relative to per-dataset tuning. The paper asks whether a single, dynamically adjusted constraint scale can match or exceed carefully tuned baselines across diverse offline datasets without any per-dataset hyperparameter search.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline dataset of state-action pairs (sampled in minibatches of 256 from a replay buffer) and produces a deterministic policy mapping states to actions, built on the TD3+BC actor-critic architecture. Unusually, it also outputs a scalar scale factor α that is itself a learnable parameter updated via second-order gradients at a lower frequency (every 10 actor updates) than the policy. The α trajectory is task- and dataset-quality-dependent: higher-quality data drives α toward smaller values (favoring BC), while lower-quality data pushes α larger (favoring RL), all from a single initialization of 2.5.

## Method

ASPC formulates the RL-BC trade-off as a bilevel optimization problem: the inner loop performs one gradient step on the policy parameters θ given a fixed α (minimizing the TD3+BC objective), while the outer loop updates α using second-order derivatives through the inner update. The outer loss comprises three terms—L1 mirrors the TD3+BC objective to steer α toward a better RL-BC balance, L2 penalizes squared changes in expected Q-values to prevent unreliable Q-updates, and L3 (derived from a theoretical single-step performance lower bound) penalizes the product of Q-change, BC-loss upper bound, and BC-loss variation to suppress distributional shift. The theoretical analysis establishes that when ΔQ ≥ Φ(ΔL_BC, c²), each update is non-decreasing in policy performance, and the gap to the optimal policy shrinks linearly with the number of iterations. Practically, ASPC is instantiated on TD3+BC with a robust three-layer critic (each layer followed by LayerNorm) to stabilize Q-values during α adaptation, and the α-update interval is set long enough to keep runtime overhead minimal relative to the baseline.

## Results

ASPC achieves the best overall average normalized score of 77.9 across 39 D4RL datasets using a single fixed hyperparameter configuration, outperforming ReBRAC (74.8, which requires per-dataset grid search), wPC (64.2), IQL (62.6), TD3+BC (57.7), and A2PR (51.2). ASPC leads on MuJoCo (82.1) and Maze2d (147.2), while remaining competitive on AntMaze (74.5 vs. wPC's 78.7) and Adroit (55.7 vs. ReBRAC's 58.6). The method delivers a 35% average improvement over the TD3+BC baseline and, when integrated with CQL, Diffusion-QL, IQL, and FQL, yields consistent additional gains (up to +20.3% for CQL on MuJoCo and +13.5% for FQL on OGBench).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASPC (77.9) outperforms all fixed-hyperparameter baselines: TD3+BC (57.7, +35%), wPC (64.2, +21%), and A2PR (51.2, +52%). Against per-dataset-tuned methods, ASPC beats IQL (62.6) by 15.3 points and ReBRAC (74.8) by 3.1 points overall, though ReBRAC retains an edge on AntMaze (76.8 vs. 74.5) and Adroit (58.6 vs. 55.7), domains characterized by sparse binary rewards where behavior cloning dominates. On MuJoCo, ASPC (82.1) narrowly surpasses ReBRAC (81.2) and wPC (77.8); on Maze2d, ASPC (147.2) leads A2PR (123.5) by a wide margin. When integrated as a plug-in, ASPC improves CQL by +7.8% and Diffusion-QL by +2.6% on MuJoCo (Table 3), and boosts FQL by +13.5% on OGBench (Table 4), confirming broad generality beyond the TD3+BC backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

ASPC builds directly on TD3+BC's RL-BC trade-off objective and repurposes the bilevel optimization framework from MAML (Finn et al., 2017) and differentiable hyperparameter optimization (Franceschi et al., 2018) to make the constraint scale factor α a learnable parameter. It departs from prior adaptive policy-constraint methods (wPC, A2PR, OAP, GORL) that reweight individual actions or transitions locally, by instead adjusting the global trade-off scale between the RL and BC objectives through second-order gradients. The theoretical analysis (mutual bounds between ΔL_BC and (ΔQ)², single-step performance guarantees) provides a principled justification for the outer-loss design that prior heuristic adaptive methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ASPC addresses a practical bottleneck in offline RL deployment: the need for per-dataset hyperparameter tuning that is often infeasible in real-world settings. The 35% average improvement with a single configuration, plus consistent gains when integrated into IQL, CQL, Diffusion-QL, and FQL, suggests the adaptive-scaling principle is broadly applicable. However, the method still underperforms on sparse-reward tasks (AntMaze, Adroit), and the authors acknowledge that different algorithms may require tailored ASPC-style components rather than a one-size-fits-all integration. Open questions include whether the bilevel approach scales to larger, real-world datasets and whether the second-order gradient cost can be further reduced without sacrificing the dynamic adjustment that Table 2 shows is essential.
