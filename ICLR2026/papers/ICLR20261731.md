## Problem

Supervised scene change detection methods fail to generalize beyond their training domain, while existing zero-shot approaches are confined to 2D image differencing and cannot disentangle true scene changes from viewpoint differences. 3D-centric methods (NeRF, 3DGS) handle geometry but require multi-view image sets at both time points and explicit reconstruction, making them inapplicable to the common image-pair setting. No prior method achieves zero-shot, pose-agnostic, object-level change detection and labeling on unconstrained real-world image pairs.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a pair of uncalibrated RGB images (or multi-view sets) captured at two different times, with no depth, pose, or calibration metadata required. For multi-view inference, it needs as few as one image at T_1 paired against a set of T_0 views. Output is a per-pixel change mask with object-level semantic labels from the set {Added, Removed, Moved, Warped, Unchanged}, where overlapping predictions are resolved by a fixed priority order (Warped > Moved > Removed > Added) to handle nested or occluded changes.

## Method

GOLDILOCS reformulates SCD as a 3D reconstruction-over-time problem: objects that change between T_0 and T_1 violate the static-scene assumption and produce geometric inconsistencies in stereo reconstruction. It uses MASt3R to estimate camera parameters and dense pointmaps, then applies a reverse depth test to filter out temporally inconsistent geometry, yielding a canonical static scene representation. Rendering this clean pointmap from the target viewpoint produces reference images that exclude changed or occluded content; SAM2 segments these renderings and propagates masks to distinguish added, removed, and moved objects, while SSIM heatmaps between the rendering and the target image identify non-rigid (warped) deformations. For multi-view inputs, a cross-view voting mechanism projects per-pixel labels through 3D correspondences and takes majority votes across views to enforce consistency and suppress false positives.

## Results

On ChangeSim, GOLDILOCS achieves 64.9 mIoU (binary) and 33.6 mIoU (multi-class), outperforming all baselines including supervised in-domain methods. On the 3DGS-CD dataset, it reaches F1 of 97.72 and IoU of 95.30 without any auxiliary T₁ images, surpassing the 3DGS-CD baseline (F1 97.51, IoU 95.16) that requires 4 extra T₁ views. On NeRFCD, GOLDILOCS attains F1 of 91.97 and IoU of 87.62, beating Gaussian Difference (F1 91.90, IoU 85.74) and C-NERF (F1 88.89, IoU 80.83) while using zero auxiliary images versus 10+ for baselines. On RC-3D, it achieves mAP of 0.53 with RGB-only input, exceeding even the RGB-D variant of CYWS-3D (0.50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GOLDILOCS outperforms all zero-shot and most supervised baselines on ChangeSim, achieving 37.3 IoU (binary) vs. C-3PO (28.8), ZSSCD (25.2), CSCDNet (22.9), and ChangeNet (17.6). On VL-CMU-CD, it achieves the best zero-shot F1 (61.8%) but trails supervised in-domain methods: C-3PO (80.0%), SimSaC (79.7%), ChangeNet (79.0%), DR-TANet (75.5%), and CSCDNet (71.0%). On RC-3D, GOLDILOCS (mAP 0.53, RGB-only, zero-shot) surpasses CYWS-3D with RGB-D (0.50) and CYWS-3D with RGB (0.41), as well as CYWS (0.14). On the 3DGS-CD dataset, it edges out the 3DGS-CD baseline (F1 97.72 vs. 97.51; IoU 95.30 vs. 95.16) while requiring zero auxiliary T₁ images versus 4. On NeRFCD, it beats Gaussian Difference (F1 91.97 vs. 91.90; IoU 87.62 vs. 85.74) and C-NERF (F1 91.97 vs. 88.89; IoU 87.62 vs. 80.83) without auxiliary images, and is ~10× faster than Gaussian Difference and ~100× faster than C-NERF.

## Relation to Prior Work

GOLDILOCS carves new ground by reformulating scene change detection as a 3D reconstruction-over-time problem, rather than the 2D image-differencing or explicit multi-view 3D modeling (NeRF/3DGS) that prior work relies on. It repurposes two existing foundation models—MASt3R for uncalibrated stereo 3D reconstruction and SAM2 for class-agnostic segmentation and mask tracking—into a novel pipeline where geometric inconsistencies from depth filtering naturally localize changes, and where viewpoint difference (typically a confound) is exploited as a signal. This departs from supervised SCD methods (ChangeNet, C-3PO, SimSaC) that require domain-specific training, from zero-shot 2D methods (ZSSCD) that cannot disentangle viewpoint from true change, and from 3D-centric methods (C-NeRF, 3DGS-CD, Gaussian Difference) that demand multi-view image sets and time-intensive explicit reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GOLDILOCS offers a practical, training-free, calibration-free SCD pipeline that operates on as few as two uncalibrated RGB images and detects four change types including non-rigid deformations (via SSIM), a capability absent from all compared baselines. Its ~10× speedup over Gaussian Difference and ~100× over C-NeRF, combined with zero-shot performance matching or exceeding supervised in-domain methods on RC-3D and 3DGS-CD, makes it attractive for real-world deployment where annotated training data and multi-view captures are unavailable. Open questions include handling nested changes (e.g., a key removed from a moved keyboard), robustness to drastic illumination shifts that degrade both 3D reconstruction and SSIM, and extending to multi-label semantic classes beyond the current four-type taxonomy.
