## Problem

Discrete-space video generation (autoregressive and masked diffusion) lags behind continuous diffusion methods due to error accumulation and long-context inconsistency, producing low visual quality and unnatural motions even with bidirectional transformers. The paper asks whether discrete generative modeling can be reformulated to close this gap while retaining the efficiency and scalability advantages of token-based approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264590/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes text prompts (up to 320 tokens) concatenated with categorical noise tokens drawn uniformly from the vision vocabulary, and optionally a clean reference frame for image-to-video tasks. Outputs are discrete token sequences decoded by the Cosmos tokenizer (4× temporal, 8×8 spatial compression, 64K FSQ codebook) into 49×512×320 videos, or by a separate IBQ tokenizer (16×16 spatial compression, 131K entries) into 1024×1024 images. Inference uses 25 steps for images and 50 for videos with classifier-free guidance at scale 7.0.

## Method

URSA formulates generation as iterative global refinement over discrete spatiotemporal tokens, starting from categorical noise and converging to the data manifold via a metric-guided probability path. Two key path designs enable long-sequence scaling: (1) a Linearized Metric Path that defines the forward process via softmax over token embedding distances with a scheduler β_t = c·(t/(1−t))^α, enforcing a near-linear relationship between timestep and perturbation magnitude; and (2) a Resolution-dependent Timestep Shifting (t̃ = t/(t+λ(1−t))) that modulates perturbation strength based on sequence length. An asynchronous timestep scheduling strategy assigns independent noise levels to each frame (inspired by diffusion forcing), unifying text-to-video, image-to-video, interpolation, and extrapolation in a single model. The backbone is a 1.7B Qwen3 LLM with enhanced 3D M-RoPE for spatiotemporal positional encoding, trained with cross-entropy loss and sampled via an Euler solver over the learned velocity field.

## Results

On VBench (text-to-video), URSA (1.7B params, 24M training videos) achieves a total score of 82.4, outperforming all discrete baselines (Lumos-1: 78.3, Emu3: 81.0) and matching or exceeding several continuous models (NOVA: 80.1, Vchitect-2.0: 81.6, Pyramid Flow: 81.7, CogVideoX1.5: 82.0). URSA's dynamic degree score of 81.4 is the highest among all compared models, indicating superior motion generation. On VBench++ (image-to-video), URSA reaches 86.2, on par with top continuous models (CogVideoX: 86.7, HunyuanVideo: 86.8, Wan2.1: 86.9). For text-to-image at 1024×1024, URSA achieves 86.0 on DPG-Bench and 0.80 on GenEval (with rewritten prompts), surpassing all discrete baselines and approaching top continuous models like Show-o2 (86.1 DPG-Bench) and Mogao (0.89 GenEval).

## Baselines & Comparisons

URSA (1.7B) outperforms both discrete baselines on VBench: Lumos-1 (78.3) by 4.1 points and Emu3 (81.0) by 1.4 points, while using fewer parameters than Emu3 (8B). Among continuous models, URSA trails the top performers Wan2.1 (83.7) and OpenSora 2.0 (83.6) by 1.2–1.3 points, but surpasses NOVA (80.1), Vchitect-2.0 (81.6), Pyramid Flow (81.7), MAGI-1 (81.8), Step-Video (81.8), and CogVideoX1.5 (82.0). URSA's standout advantage is dynamic degree (81.4), exceeding all continuous baselines including MAGI-1 (72.5) and HunyuanVideo (70.8), suggesting superior motion generation. On VBench++ (image-to-video), URSA (86.2) is within 0.5–2.2 points of top continuous models (CogVideoX 86.7, HunyuanVideo 86.8, Wan2.1 86.9, Step-Video 88.4, MAGI-1 89.3) and beats the only discrete baseline Lumos-1 (84.7) by 1.5 points. For text-to-image at 1024×1024, URSA (86.0 DPG-Bench) surpasses all discrete methods (Janus-Pro 84.2, FUDOKI 83.6, Infinity 83.5, Emu3 81.6) and approaches Show-o2 (86.1) and Bagel (85.1), though it lags behind Mogao (0.89) on GenEval overall (URSA†: 0.80). The paper notes that URSA's imaging quality (62.2) is lower than continuous counterparts (65–70), attributing this gap to the limited reconstruction quality of current discrete video tokenizers rather than the generation framework itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

URSA builds directly on the Discrete Flow Matching framework (Gat et al., 2024) and the Kinetic Optimal Scheduler with metric-induced probability paths (Shaul et al., 2025), adapting them from short-sequence image generation to long-sequence video. The key conceptual departure is from both autoregressive and masked diffusion models, which fix tokens once generated (non-refinable local generation); URSA instead performs iterative global refinement over all discrete tokens, conceptually aligning the discrete paradigm with continuous diffusion's denoising process. The asynchronous timestep scheduling is adapted from diffusion forcing (Chen et al., 2024a), which originally applied frame-wise noise in continuous space, here transplanted to discrete token sequences to enable multi-task unification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that discrete generative modeling can close the performance gap with continuous diffusion for video generation, challenging the assumption that continuous latent spaces are fundamentally superior for high-fidelity visual synthesis. The practical impact is significant: a 1.7B discrete model matches or exceeds 13–30B continuous models on VBench, and the asynchronous scheduling enables a single model to handle text-to-video, image-to-video, interpolation, and extrapolation without task-specific architectures. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that generation quality is ultimately bounded by the discrete tokenizer's reconstruction fidelity, suggesting that progress in visual tokenization (e.g., better FSQ/IBQ codebooks) is the critical bottleneck for further gains. Open questions include whether the linearized metric path and resolution-dependent shifting generalize to even longer (minute-scale) videos and whether the approach can be distilled to fewer inference steps without quality loss.
