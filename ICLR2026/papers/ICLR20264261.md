## Problem

Current RL-based temporal video grounding methods (e.g., Time-R1) optimize primarily for IoU reward, which empirically degrades the model's ability to distinguish fine-grained actions (e.g., buttoning vs. unbuttoning). This action-understanding degradation ultimately caps grounding accuracy, creating a fundamental tension between temporal localization and semantic comprehension that existing approaches do not address.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264261/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video sampled at 2 FPS (resized to ~2.8M pixels total, e.g., 100 frames of 96×96×3 for a 50-second clip) and a natural-language query as input, and outputs a temporal segment [t_s, t_e] preceded by a chain-of-thought reasoning block in a fixed  format. For the auxiliary Invert-TVG tasks, the input is instead the ground-truth video segment (cropped from the annotation), and the output is a verb completion, a single action verb, or a free-form description that must contain the query's action verbs. Verb matching for reward computation uses SpaCy's en_core_web_sm model to normalize tenses to root form.

## Method

The core idea is to invert the TVG task: given the ground-truth segment as input, the model must recover query-related action content, yielding three self-supervised auxiliary tasks at different granularities — Verb Completion (fill masked verbs), Action Recognition (predict a single verb), and Video Description (generate a segment-level caption). These tasks reuse the exact same TVG annotations, so the learned action understanding is tightly coupled to the grounding objective rather than drawn from generic action datasets. Training uses GRPO reinforcement learning on Qwen2.5-VL with an alternating optimization strategy: at each step, the model performs the primary TVG task (IoU + format reward) with probability p=0.8, or one of the three Invert-TVG tasks (binary 0/1 reward + format reward) with probability 0.2, uniformly sampled among VC/AR/VD. This probabilistic switching avoids the memory overhead and gradient conflicts of simultaneous multi-task training while preserving action semantics. A theoretical analysis via Pareto optimality shows the multi-task policy dominates the single-task TVG optimum on the joint reward frontier.

## Results

On Charades-STA (fine-tuned), Invert4TVG-3B achieves R1@0.3=80.8, R1@0.5=69.0, R1@0.7=44.0, outperforming Time-R1*(3B) by 7.1 points on R1@0.7 (44.0 vs. 36.9). The 7B variant reaches R1@0.7=51.4, surpassing Time-R1*(7B) (50.1) and TimeSuite*(7B) (43.0). In zero-shot settings on ActivityNet and QvHighlight, Invert4TVG consistently outperforms Time-R1 across all R1@0.3/0.5/0.7 thresholds, with the gap widening on QvHighlight's more diverse action vocabulary.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Time-R1 (the primary RL baseline), Invert4TVG-3B wins on all three Charades-STA metrics: R1@0.3 (80.8 vs. 78.7), R1@0.5 (69.0 vs. 64.1), and R1@0.7 (44.0 vs. 36.9), a 7.1-point gain at the strictest threshold. The 7B variant also edges out Time-R1*(7B) on every metric (83.0/72.5/51.4 vs. 82.8/72.2/50.1). Against the SFT baseline TimeSuite*(7B), Invert4TVG-7B leads by 8.4 points on R1@0.7 (51.4 vs. 43.0) and by 3.6 points on R1@0.3. Against VideoChat-Flash (7B, no fine-tuning), the gap is even larger: 23.8 points on R1@0.7 (51.4 vs. 27.6). In zero-shot transfer to ActivityNet and QvHighlight, Invert4TVG outperforms Time-R1 across all thresholds, with the advantage most pronounced on QvHighlight where the action vocabulary is far more diverse, confirming that the Invert-TVG auxiliary tasks improve generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-based temporal video grounding line initiated by Time-R1 (Wang et al., 2025a), which introduced GRPO with IoU and format rewards for LVLM fine-tuning. The key departure is the diagnosis that IoU-only optimization degrades the model's action understanding, and the proposal of Invert-TVG tasks (verb completion, action recognition, video description) that repurpose existing TVG annotations as self-supervised auxiliary objectives. The framework builds on the GRPO algorithm from DeepSeek-R1 and the Qwen2.5-VL backbone, but introduces an alternating probabilistic sampling strategy (80/20 TVG/Invert-TVG) to avoid the memory and optimization conflicts of joint multi-task training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode in RL-based TVG—semantic drift from IoU over-optimization—and offers a data-efficient remedy that requires no additional annotations beyond the original TVG dataset. The 7.1% R1@0.7 gain for the 3B model is meaningful, though the 7B improvement shrinks to 1.3%, suggesting the benefit may be most relevant for smaller models. Open questions include whether the SpaCy-based verb-matching reward generalizes to non-English or highly compositional queries, whether the 80/20 probability split transfers across architectures, and whether the approach extends to multi-segment or hierarchical grounding settings.
