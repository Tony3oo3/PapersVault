## Problem

Existing head tracking methods rely on sparse facial landmarks or parametric 3D morphable models, which omit hair, accessories, and other non-skin regions, and degrade under occlusions, extreme poses, and expression changes. There is no dense, semantics-aware canonical representation for complete human heads that enables robust, queryable correspondences across diverse views and individuals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261164/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB image of a human head and produces a dense per-pixel 3D embedding map (H×W×3) where each pixel is mapped to a location in a shared 3D unit cube. The 3D canonical space is interpretable and queryable: selecting a volumetric region in the cube retrieves the corresponding semantic region (e.g., left ear, hair) across any head image, and nearest-neighbor search in this space yields dense correspondences between two images.

## Method

A Vision Transformer backbone (initialized from DINOv3) with a DPT head predicts per-pixel 3D canonical coordinates for each input image. These coordinates index into a learnable latent feature grid E (N_d³×D) defined over the unit cube, smoothed by a 3D Gaussian filter to enforce spatial continuity; querying E via trilinear interpolation yields D-dimensional semantic features at each pixel. Training uses a siamese contrastive objective (CLIP-style) on pairwise point tracks from CoTracker3 over CelebV-HQ talking-head videos, supplemented by landmark anchoring loss (68 300W-format landmarks pinned to fixed cube locations) and a face-parsing segmentation loss on the queried features. The 3D cube representation (rather than a 2D UV map) is chosen to handle hair and accessories without seams, and the canonical space bottleneck ensures cross-subject consistency by forcing all heads to share the same coordinate system.

## Results

On same-person pairs from Nersemble, DenseMarks achieves a MAE of 3.68 px and PCK@r=0.05 of 0.90, substantially outperforming the best generic baseline DINOv3 (MAE 7.60, PCK 0.72) and the best head-centric baseline CSE (MAE 11.22, PCK 0.55). On cross-person pairs, DenseMarks attains the highest ArcFace identity preservation score (0.384) and the lowest Met3R view-inconsistency score (0.388), beating all six baselines on every reported metric. The method uses only 3-dimensional embeddings (vs. 768–1280 for baselines) while achieving superior geometric and semantic correspondence quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DenseMarks outperforms all six baselines on every metric in Table 1. Against the strongest generic baseline DINOv3 (768-dim embeddings), DenseMarks (3-dim) halves the MAE (3.68 vs. 7.60) and improves PCK from 0.72 to 0.90, while also achieving better cross-person identity preservation (ArcFace 0.384 vs. 0.266) and view consistency (Met3R 0.388 vs. 0.460). Among head-centric methods, CSE is the closest competitor on ArcFace (0.359) but DenseMarks still leads by 0.025 and dominates on same-person accuracy (MAE 3.68 vs. 11.22). FaRL and Sapiens, despite being designed for the human domain, perform worst on same-person matching (MAE 21.38 and 14.88 respectively), suggesting their features capture appearance rather than geometry. The paper also shows in Appendix I that PRNet (a 3DMM-based method) achieves competitive cross-person ArcFace (0.462) but inferior same-person MAE (7.33) and PCK (0.72), confirming that DenseMarks' canonical space provides more accurate dense correspondences even within the face region.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of canonical space learning (NOCS, CSE) and vision foundation models (DINOv3, Sapiens), but departs from both in key ways. Unlike NOCS and CSE, which require 3D model supervision or parametric shape fitting, DenseMarks learns a 3D canonical unit cube for heads purely from 2D point tracks as pseudo-labels, avoiding the need for 3D head models that are absent in the human head domain. The contrastive (CLIP-style) training objective on point-track correspondences, combined with a learnable latent feature grid queried via trilinear interpolation, is a novel combination that repurposes ideas from functional maps and attention mechanisms for the head-specific setting. The method complements rather than replaces 3DMM-based trackers (FLAME/VHAP) by supplying dense semantic correspondences for regions (hair, accessories) that parametric models cannot capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 3-dimensional canonical location outperforms 768–1280-dimensional features from powerful foundation models for geometry-aware correspondence challenges the implicit assumption that higher-dimensional representations are always superior for matching tasks. The interpretable, queryable canonical space enables practical interactions (click-based region retrieval) and robust tracking under occlusion and extreme poses that landmark-based methods fail on. Key limitations include dependence on video datasets with sufficient head visibility (quality drops on backside views and rapid motion not present in CelebV-HQ), and the method's generalization to full bodies or other domains remains preliminary (chairs experiment is small-scale). Open questions include whether the canonical space approach scales to more diverse head appearances, whether incorporating synthetic occlusion training would close the gap on challenging regions, and whether the 3D bottleneck can be relaxed for finer-grained correspondences.
