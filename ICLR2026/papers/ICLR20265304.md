## Problem

All existing sublinear spectral clustering oracles require at least Ω(√n) space to construct their data structure, which becomes a critical bottleneck for massive graphs and memory-limited settings such as streaming, cloud, and GPU environments. This paper asks whether it is possible to break the √n space barrier while still achieving sublinear query time, and characterizes the fundamental trade-off frontier between memory usage S and query time T.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265304/cropped_page9_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates in the adjacency list model, where any neighbor of a specified vertex can be queried in constant time. Given a (k,φ,ε)-clusterable d-regular graph G and a trade-off parameter M (with 1 ≤ M ≤ O(n^{1/2−O(ε)}/k)), it constructs a compact data structure D in sublinear time. At query time, given any vertex x, the oracle answers WHICHCLUSTER(G,x) in sublinear time, returning a cluster assignment with at most O(ε^{1/3})|C_i| misclassified vertices per cluster. The parameter M directly controls the space-time balance: larger M uses more memory but yields faster queries.

## Method

The core technique is a batch-based collision probability estimator (ESTRWDOT) that partitions R random walks of length t into B = R/M batches, storing only M endpoints per batch to form empirical distributions whose dot product approximates ⟨M^t 1_x, M^t 1_y⟩. This reduces space from O(R) = O(√n) to O(M) while preserving total computation, yielding the trade-off S·T = Õ(n^{1+O(ε)}). The dot product oracle (INITORACLE + QUERYDOT) uses this primitive to estimate spectral embedding inner products ⟨f_x, f_y⟩ via a compact surrogate matrix Ξ built from a small random sample of vertices, which is then plugged into the clustering oracle of Shen & Peng (2023) or Gluch et al. (2021). The paper further establishes a matching Ω(n) space-time lower bound for the 1-cluster vs. 2-cluster problem using an information-theoretic reduction to distribution testing, showing the trade-off is nearly optimal for collision-probability-based approaches.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution establishes a space-time trade-off S·T = Õ(n^{1+O(ε)}) for sublinear spectral clustering oracles on (k,φ,ε)-clusterable graphs, which simplifies to S·T = Õ(n) when ε ≪ 1/log n. This breaks the prior Ω(√n) space barrier: the oracle can operate with as little as Õ(n^{O(ε)}·M) space (e.g., O(n^{0.01}) for tiny ε) while answering WHICHCLUSTER queries in Õ(n^{1+O(ε)}/M) time. Experimentally, on an SBM graph (n=3000, k=3), the new oracle achieves 99.07% accuracy using 10,400 words of space (1× baseline), whereas the previous Shen & Peng (2023) oracle requires 44,383 words (4.27× baseline) for comparable 99.97% accuracy, and fails entirely (success rate 0) at 3.35× baseline space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Peng (2020), which requires Õ(√n) space and time, the proposed oracle achieves the same or better misclassification guarantees while allowing space to be reduced arbitrarily below √n (down to n^{O(ε)}·M for any M≥1), at the cost of proportionally increased query time. Against Gluch et al. (2021), which requires n^{1−δ+O(ε)} space (at least √n for any δ≤1/2), the new method matches the O(log k·ε)|C_i| misclassification error but replaces the fixed space lower bound with a tunable trade-off parameter M. Against Shen & Peng (2023), the new oracle (Item 2) preserves the O(poly(k)·ε^{1/3})|C_i| misclassification error while reducing the space from n^{1−δ+O(ε)} to n^{O(ε)}·M, a strict improvement when M ≪ n^{1/2}. Experimentally, the new oracle uses 4.27× less space than the Shen & Peng (2023) oracle for comparable accuracy (99.07% vs. 99.97%), and the previous oracle completely fails to construct its data structure at 3.35× the new oracle's space budget. The paper also provides a matching lower bound S·T = Ω(n) for the 1-cluster vs. 2-cluster problem under random-walk queries, confirming the trade-off is nearly optimal up to polylog factors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sublinear spectral clustering oracle framework established by Peng (2020), Gluch et al. (2021), and Shen & Peng (2023), all of which require at least Ω(√n) space to construct their data structures. The key technical departure is a batch-based random walk estimation strategy (inspired by Canonne & Yang's memory-efficient distribution testing) that replaces the monolithic √n-sample approach with smaller batches, trading space for query time. The overall oracle architecture closely follows Shen & Peng (2023), with the new dot product oracle substituted in place of theirs, while the lower bound for the 1-cluster vs. 2-cluster problem draws on the information-theoretic framework of Diakonikolas et al. (2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Breaking the Ω(√n) space barrier is significant for memory-constrained settings (GPUs, streaming, cloud platforms) where materializing large data structures is infeasible. The near-optimality of the S·T ≈ Õ(n) trade-off (up to log factors) for collision-probability-based approaches provides a clear target for future work. However, the empirical validation is limited to small synthetic SBM graphs (n=3000), leaving open whether the theoretical trade-offs hold in practice on real-world massive graphs. An open question is whether the batching technique can be extended beyond the planted-clustering assumption or to non-regular graphs without the d-bounded reduction.
