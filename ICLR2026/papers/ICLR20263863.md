## Problem

Prostate ultrasound computed tomography (USCT) is severely constrained by limited-angle acquisition geometry (transrectal and transabdominal probes only), tissue heterogeneity, and proximity to bony pelvic structures, yet no large-scale, anatomically realistic dataset exists to benchmark reconstruction algorithms under these clinically realistic conditions. Existing USCT datasets target breast imaging or lack full-waveform data, tissue heterogeneity, and bone inclusion, leaving a critical gap for developing and evaluating limited-angle prostate-specific inversion methods.

## Input / Output

The input is a 4D ultrasound full-waveform tensor of shape (40 channels × 1000 time steps × 322 receivers), where the 40 channels encode four source-receiver probe configurations (transabdominal-to-transabdominal, transabdominal-to-transrectal, transrectal-to-transabdominal, transrectal-to-transrectal) with 20 sources (10 per probe) and a 1 MHz Ricker wavelet excitation sampled at Δt = 10⁻⁷ s over 100 μs. The output is a 2D speed-of-sound map at 401 × 161 grid points (0.375 mm spacing, 60 mm × 150 mm field of view), representing the spatially varying acoustic property to be reconstructed from the limited-angle pressure field.

## Method

OPENPROS generates 280,000 paired waveform-SOS samples by slicing anatomically realistic 3D digital prostate phantoms (built from 4 clinical MRI/CT scans and 62 ex vivo specimens with measured SOS) under ±45° probe rotations with random perturbations, then simulating wave propagation via a 4th-order spatial / 2nd-order temporal FDTD solver with 120-point absorbing boundaries. For benchmarking, the paper compares two physics-based baselines (Delay-and-Sum beamforming and multi-stage multi-frequency USCT inversion) against two learned models: InversionNet (a fully-convolutional encoder-decoder) and ViT-Inversion (a Vision Transformer that partitions the 3D waveform tensor into spatio-temporal patches, applies multi-head self-attention, and upsamples via a lightweight CNN). All learned models are trained with supervised learning (Adam, lr=10⁻⁴, batch 512, 240 epochs) on the 224K/28K/28K train/val/test split, and are evaluated on in-distribution accuracy, patient-level and prostate-level out-of-distribution generalization, additive Gaussian noise robustness, and probe placement error sensitivity.

## Results

On the in-distribution test set, ViT-Inversion achieves the best performance across all four metrics: MAE 0.0067, RMSE 0.0205, SSIM 0.9967, and PCC 0.9893, slightly outperforming InversionNet (MAE 0.0074, RMSE 0.0247, SSIM 0.9955, PCC 0.9851). Both learned methods reduce RMSE by roughly 5–6× compared to physics-based multi-stage USCT (RMSE ≈ 0.16, SSIM ≈ 0.90). Inference efficiency is dramatically improved: InversionNet and ViT-Inversion complete reconstruction in 4.9 ms and 8.9 ms per sample, respectively, versus ~4 hours for beamforming and ~24 hours for physics-based USCT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks four methods: Delay-and-Sum beamforming, physics-based multi-stage USCT, InversionNet (Wu & Lin, 2019), and the proposed ViT-Inversion. ViT-Inversion consistently outperforms InversionNet on all four metrics in both in-distribution and out-of-distribution settings, with the gap widening under patient-level generalization (MAE 0.0276 vs. 0.0322) and high noise (SSIM 0.935 vs. 0.825 at σ=0.05). Both learned methods dramatically surpass physics-based USCT in accuracy (RMSE reduced from ~0.16 to ~0.02–0.03) and speed (milliseconds vs. hours). However, the authors note that even the best learned methods still fail to resolve fine internal prostate structures and small lesions, indicating that no current method achieves clinically acceptable high-resolution reconstruction under limited-angle conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes InversionNet (Wu & Lin, 2019), originally designed for seismic full-waveform inversion, as a baseline for ultrasound tomography, and introduces a Vision Transformer variant (ViT-Inversion) as a new architecture for the same task. The forward simulation pipeline draws on standard FDTD and Runge-Kutta acoustic solvers (analogous to k-Wave) but applies them to anatomically realistic 3D prostate phantoms derived from clinical MRI/CT and ex vivo USCT measurements, a combination absent from prior USCT datasets which focused on breast phantoms or generic anatomical models. The limited-angle acquisition geometry (transrectal + transabdominal probes, ±45° rotation) is a clinically motivated constraint that departs from the full-angle breast USCT setups in prior work (SoftVue, QTscan, Ruiter et al. 2018).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OPENPROS fills a critical gap: no public, large-scale dataset existed for limited-angle prostate USCT, and the 280K paired samples with open-source FDTD/Runge-Kutta solvers enable systematic, reproducible benchmarking that was previously impossible. The key open question the paper surfaces is the resolution gap: despite near-perfect global SSIM (~0.99), learned methods still smooth out fine internal prostate structures and small lesions, meaning clinically acceptable reconstruction remains unsolved. Limitations include only 4 patient anatomies, 2D simulation, and SOS-only modeling (no attenuation or density), so the benchmark is a fast-prototyping tool rather than a full clinical surrogate. Future work must address 3D propagation, multi-parameter reconstruction, and patient-level generalization (where errors increase 3–5×) before these methods can approach clinical deployment.
