## Problem

Spectral graphs of non-Hermitian crystals—spatial multigraphs encoding the full geometry of complex energy spectra—have been studied only through manual plotting, precluding systematic large-scale analysis. Simultaneously, graph learning benchmarks overwhelmingly assume simple, non-spatial edges, discarding the geometric information carried by multi-edges that is essential in real-world spatial networks. No large-scale dataset or automated pipeline existed to address either gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262355/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Poly2Graph accepts a 1D Bloch Hamiltonian H(z) (as a sympy matrix or Laurent polynomial) or its characteristic polynomial P(z,E) directly, and outputs a NetworkX MultiGraph where each node carries complex-plane coordinates, spectral potential, and DOS values, while each edge stores its full geometric path as an ordered sequence of (Re(E), Im(E)) coordinates plus summary attributes (length, straight-line distance, midpoint, average potential/DOS). The effective rasterization resolution is 1024×1024 (achieved via a 256×256 coarse pass followed by 4×4 adaptive refinement within a dilated mask), and the dataset spans 1401 characteristic-polynomial classes with 11.6M static and 5.1M temporal graphs.

## Method

Poly2Graph automates spectral graph extraction through a four-stage pipeline: (1) batch root-finding of P(z,E)=0 via Frobenius companion matrices processed by a parallel eigensolver with auto-detected GPU backend, achieving ~10⁵× speedup over prior code; (2) computation of the spectral potential Φ(E) = −log|a_q(E)| − Σ log|z_i(E)| and its Laplacian to obtain the density of states ρ(E); (3) a two-stage adaptive-resolution image processing step that binarizes the DOS, applies morphological dilation to isolate the graph region, then recomputes at 4× sub-pixel resolution only within the masked area; (4) iterative morphological thinning to a one-pixel-wide skeleton, followed by junction/leaf/edge-point classification to produce the MultiGraph. For benchmarking, the authors featurize edges with fixed-size direction-ignorant summaries and train eight GNNs (GCN, GAT, GATv2, GIN, GINE, MF, CGCNN, GraphSAGE) under matched parameter counts for graph-level classification across six dataset variants of increasing complexity.

## Results

On the full HSG-12M dataset (11.6M graphs, 1401 classes), GraphSAGE achieves the best Top-1 accuracy of 54.6% and Top-10 accuracy of 95.2%, with CGCNN close behind at 53.1% and 94.8% respectively. Performance degrades monotonically with dataset complexity: on the simpler one-band subset (24 classes), GraphSAGE reaches 85.4% Top-1 and 100% Top-10, while on the three-band subset (1102 classes) it drops to 52.3% Top-1. Edge-aware GINE dramatically outperforms edge-agnostic GIN on HSG-12M (46.0% vs. 6.3% accuracy), confirming that multi-edge spatial geometry carries irreducible signal. The large Top-1 vs. Top-10 gap (e.g., 54.6% vs. 95.2% for SAGE on HSG-12M) suggests that a small candidate set of Hamiltonian families can be retrieved for downstream expert screening in inverse design.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphSAGE [Hamilton et al., 2017] achieves the best Top-1 accuracy across all five dataset variants (85.4% on one-band to 54.6% on HSG-12M), outperforming CGCNN [Xie & Grossman, 2018] by 5.8–8.8 percentage points, indicating a stronger inductive bias for spatial multigraphs under matched parameter budgets. CGCNN is the consistent runner-up, particularly strong on the topology and HSG-12M subsets (56.6% and 53.1% respectively). GINE [Hu et al., 2019] substantially outperforms its edge-agnostic counterpart GIN [Xu et al., 2019] on all datasets beyond one-band (e.g., 46.0% vs. 6.3% on HSG-12M), demonstrating that edge geometry features are critical for spatial multigraphs. GCN [Kipf & Welling, 2017], GAT [Veličković et al., 2018], and GATv2 [Brody et al., 2022] cluster in the middle tier (35–48% on HSG-12M), with GATv2 slightly trailing GAT. MF [Duvenaud et al., 2015] is the weakest GNN baseline (29.5% on HSG-12M). Separately, Poly2Graph achieves a 1.2×10⁵–2.3×10⁵× speedup over the spectral potential computation in Tai & Lee [2023] (Table A3), reducing per-sample wall-time from ~3000 s to ~13–51 ms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the theoretical framework of non-Bloch band theory (Tai & Lee, 2023; Xiong & Hu, 2023; Wang et al., 2024) into a fully automated pipeline (Poly2Graph), replacing the previously manual process of extracting spectral graphs from 1D crystal Hamiltonians. On the ML side, it positions itself at the intersection of graph representation learning and spatial network science, explicitly noting that virtually all existing graph benchmarks (OGB, TU, etc.) treat data as simple, non-spatial graphs and discard multi-edge geometry. The benchmarking reuses standard GNN architectures (GCN, GAT, GIN, GINE, CGCNN, GraphSAGE) without architectural modifications, isolating the data-type novelty as the primary contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HSG-12M fills a genuine gap by providing the first large-scale spatial multigraph dataset (11.6M static + 5.1M dynamic graphs, 1401 classes), where edge multiplicity and continuous geometric paths are irreducible — a property absent from all prior graph benchmarks. The benchmarking reveals that current GNNs degrade monotonically with graph complexity and class diversity, and that edge-aware models (GINE) dramatically outperform edge-agnostic ones, pointing to a clear methodological need for geometry-aware message passing. The paper's broader claim that arbitrary polynomials, vectors, and matrices admit spectral graphs as topological fingerprints opens an ambitious 'algebra-as-graph' research direction, though this remains largely unexplored. Open questions include how to encode full edge-curve geometry (curvature, torsion, polyline sequences) rather than the fixed-size summary features used here, and whether temporal spectral graph sequences can support predictive or generative tasks.
