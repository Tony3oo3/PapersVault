## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting methods predict pixel-aligned primitives, causing Gaussian counts to grow quadratically with resolution and making 4K synthesis intractable due to prohibitive memory and compute costs. While textured Gaussian approaches can reduce primitive counts by decoupling appearance from geometry, they all require per-scene optimization and cannot generalize across scenes in a feed-forward manner. LGTM addresses both gaps simultaneously: it is the first feed-forward network that predicts textured Gaussians, enabling high-resolution (up to 4K) novel view synthesis without per-scene optimization.

## Input / Output

The network consumes two resolution streams of the same input images: low-resolution versions (e.g., 512×288) feed the primitive network, while full high-resolution versions (up to 4096×2304) feed the texture network. Camera poses are optional, supporting both posed (DepthSplat) and unposed (NoPoSplat) baselines. The output is a fixed-size grid of textured 2D Gaussian primitives (e.g., 512×288 per view) where each primitive carries a small per-primitive texture map (2×2 to 8×8 pixels) for both color and alpha, which together enable rendering at arbitrary resolutions far exceeding the primitive grid size.

## Method

LGTM employs a dual-network architecture that decouples geometry from appearance. The primitive network is a ViT encoder-decoder that takes low-resolution images and predicts a compact grid of 2DGS parameters (position, scale, rotation, opacity, SH color), trained with high-resolution supervision to establish proper geometric priors. The texture network then predicts per-primitive color and alpha texture maps by fusing three feature sources: convolutional features from patchified high-resolution images, projective prior textures obtained by inverse-rasterizing the source image back onto each Gaussian's local coordinate frame, and shared backbone features from the primitive network. Training follows a staged recipe: the primitive network is first pre-trained in isolation with high-res rendering supervision, then jointly fine-tuned with the texture network at a reduced learning rate (0.1×) to preserve geometric stability. At render time, the alpha texture replaces the standard Gaussian falloff and the color texture is added to the SH base color, allowing a small number of geometric primitives to carry rich high-frequency appearance detail.

## Results

On DL3DV at 4K resolution (4096×2304), LGTM with NoPoSplat achieves LPIPS 0.200, SSIM 0.803, and PSNR 24.489, substantially outperforming the 3DGS baseline (LPIPS 0.351, PSNR 23.022) and 2DGS baseline (LPIPS 0.322, PSNR 22.198). With DepthSplat at 4K (3840×2048), LGTM reaches LPIPS 0.170, SSIM 0.827, PSNR 25.508. Critically, NoPoSplat fails to train at 2K or 4K due to OOM, while LGTM trains within 28 GB of GPU memory. The paper reports a 23%–75% reduction in LPIPS over baselines across all tested settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NoPoSplat (Ye et al., ICLR 2025), LGTM wins decisively at every resolution: at 4K, LPIPS drops from 0.351 (3DGS) to 0.200 and PSNR rises from 23.02 to 24.49, while NoPoSplat cannot even train at 2K/4K without OOM. Against DepthSplat (Xu et al., 2024), LGTM improves 4K PSNR from 24.74 (3DGS) to 25.51 and LPIPS from 0.210 to 0.170. Against Flash3D (Szymanowicz et al., 3DV 2025) in the single-view setting, LGTM reduces 4K LPIPS from 0.399 to 0.219 and raises PSNR from 20.07 to 21.78. Against VGGT (Wang et al., CVPR 2025) in the multi-view setting, gains are more modest (e.g., 2K PSNR 19.99 vs. 19.46 for 3DGS), which the authors attribute to less precise geometry from the multi-view backbone. In the supplementary per-scene comparison, DepthSplat+LGTM outperforms fully optimized 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023) on all metrics (PSNR 27.99 vs. 21.75, SSIM 0.88 vs. 0.78, LPIPS 0.15 vs. 0.21) while running in seconds versus ~30 minutes of optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

LGTM sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting methods (NoPoSplat, DepthSplat, Flash3D, VGGT) that predict pixel-aligned primitives without per-scene optimization, and textured Gaussian splatting methods (BBSplat, GSTex, HDGS, Gaussian Billboards) that attach per-primitive texture maps but require per-scene optimization. The paper's core architectural departure is a dual-network design that decouples low-resolution geometric primitive prediction from high-resolution per-primitive texture prediction, combining the generalizability of feed-forward methods with the appearance expressiveness of textured Gaussians for the first time. The projective texture mapping step (inverting Gaussian rasterization to 'render' source images back onto primitive texture maps) is a practical mechanism borrowed from classic billboard techniques but repurposed as a learned feature extractor within a feed-forward pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LGTM unlocks 4K feed-forward novel view synthesis, a capability that was computationally intractable for prior feed-forward 3DGS methods due to quadratic primitive scaling. The demonstrated efficiency (64× pixel increase requiring only 1.80× memory and 1.47× time) suggests this decoupling strategy could be a general recipe for scaling explicit 3D representations to arbitrarily high resolutions. Key limitations remain: reconstruction quality is still bottlenecked by geometric accuracy (marginal gains in the multi-view VGGT setting), texture resolution must be manually tuned, and the method has not been validated on scenes with highly complex geometry or dynamic content. An open question is whether the texture size can be predicted adaptively per-primitive rather than fixed globally.
