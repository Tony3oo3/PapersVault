## Problem

In image-based Model-Based Reinforcement Learning, reconstruction-based world models waste representational capacity on large task-irrelevant regions like backgrounds, while decoder-free alternatives depend on Data Augmentation as an external regularizer to prevent representation collapse. This reliance on DA is a critical bottleneck for general agents because the choice of transformation is task-dependent: random shifts can discard small critical objects, and color jittering can corrupt color-based features. The paper asks whether an internal, task-agnostic regularizer can replace both the decoder and DA entirely.

## Input / Output

The agent receives RGB pixel observations (e.g., 84×84 or 64×64 frames from DMC/Meta-World environments) and outputs continuous actions for the environment. Internally, the model produces a composite latent state (deterministic h_t and stochastic z_t), predicted rewards, and continuation flags. Notably, there is no pixel-level reconstruction output; instead, a lightweight linear projector maps the latent state into the image embedding space where the redundancy-reduction loss is computed over B×T samples in a mini-batch.

## Method

R2-Dreamer replaces DreamerV3's image reconstruction loss with a Barlow Twins redundancy-reduction objective (L_BT) computed between the image encoder's embedding e_t and a linear projection k_t of the RSSM latent state s_t. The loss has two components: an invariance term (diagonal of the cross-correlation matrix → 1) that aligns the projected latent with the image embedding, and a redundancy term (off-diagonal → 0) that decorrelates feature dimensions, jointly serving as an internal regularizer that prevents collapse without any DA. The image decoder is removed entirely, replaced by a single linear projector head, while the RSSM dynamics, KL-balancing scheme, and actor-critic (REINFORCE with entropy regularization on imagined rollouts) remain identical to DreamerV3. Theoretically, this objective is a tractable surrogate for an extended Sequential Information Bottleneck, where the invariance term approximates the fidelity objective and the redundancy term approximates spatial compression via Total Correlation minimization. The method is governed by only two new hyperparameters (β_BT = 0.05 and α = 5×10⁻⁴) and achieves 1.59× faster training than DreamerV3 by eliminating pixel-level generation.

## Results

On 20 DeepMind Control Suite tasks and 50 Meta-World manipulation tasks, R2-Dreamer is competitive with DreamerV3, TD-MPC2, DrQ-v2, and DreamerPro in mean and median performance, without requiring a decoder or data augmentation. On the new DMC-Subtle benchmark (5 tasks with downscaled task-critical objects), R2-Dreamer substantially outperforms all baselines, demonstrating superior robustness to subtle visual cues. In terms of computational efficiency, R2-Dreamer trains 1.59× faster than DreamerV3 (4.4 h vs. 7.0 h) and 2.36× faster than DreamerPro (4.4 h vs. 10.4 h) on the DMC Walker Walk task over 1M environment steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DreamerV3 (decoder-based, Nature 2025), R2-Dreamer matches performance on standard DMC and Meta-World benchmarks but achieves a substantial margin on DMC-Subtle, where the reconstruction objective wastes capacity on backgrounds; it also trains 1.59× faster by eliminating the image decoder. Against DreamerPro (decoder-free, DA-reliant, ICML 2022), R2-Dreamer is competitive on DMC and Meta-World but clearly superior on DMC-Subtle, where DreamerPro's random-shift augmentation distorts tiny task-critical objects; ablations confirm DreamerPro collapses without DA while R2-Dreamer does not. Against TD-MPC2 (decoder-free, DA-reliant, ICLR 2024) and DrQ-v2 (model-free, DA-reliant), R2-Dreamer is competitive on average across DMC and Meta-World, with the DMC-Subtle gap being the clearest differentiator. The key takeaway is that R2-Dreamer's internal Barlow Twins redundancy-reduction objective replaces both the decoder and DA, yielding a more versatile and computationally efficient framework that excels specifically when task-relevant visual information is small and easily lost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DreamerV3 RSSM architecture and replaces its pixel-level reconstruction objective with a Barlow Twins redundancy-reduction loss computed between image encoder embeddings and projected latent states. It departs from the decoder-free MBRL line (DreamerPro, TD-MPC2, DrQ-v2) by eliminating the reliance on data augmentation as an external regularizer, instead using the cross-correlation structure between two internal model signals as a self-contained anti-collapse mechanism. The theoretical grounding connects the Barlow Twins objective to a tractable surrogate of an extended Sequential Information Bottleneck, unifying fidelity and spatial compression in a single loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a single, hyperparameter-light internal loss can replace both the computationally expensive image decoder and task-specific data augmentation, yielding a 1.59× training speedup and removing a key source of task-dependent fragility. The DMC-Subtle benchmark provides a useful stress test showing that DA can actively harm performance when task-critical visual cues are small, a concern relevant to real-world robotics. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic distractor backgrounds (Distracting Control Suite), high-dimensional tasks like Humanoid, and discrete action spaces, and whether the competitive-but-not-dominant results on standard DMC/Meta-World reflect a fundamental ceiling of the redundancy-reduction signal or simply the strength of the DreamerV3 baseline.
