## Problem

One-step generators distilled from Masked Diffusion Models inherit modeling and alignment errors from the teacher, and their discrete token outputs block gradient flow, making post-distillation refinements such as adversarial training, differentiable reward fine-tuning, and test-time embedding optimization infeasible. No prior work had provided a differentiable, decoder-compatible relaxation of discrete outputs specifically tailored to one-step discrete visual generators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260108/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The generator takes a class label (ImageNet-256, 256×256) or a text prompt (text-to-image, 256×256) as conditioning and produces a single image in one forward pass. Internally, the student outputs logits over a discrete vocabulary (from VQ-VAE, LFQ, or 1D semantic tokenizers); these logits are converted to soft embeddings and passed through the frozen tokenizer decoder to yield the final RGB image. The same logits also feed the Di[M]O distillation loss, which operates on the discrete token space.

## Method

The core contribution is the soft embedding: for each token position, the generator's softmax output distribution is used as weights over the full embedding matrix, producing a convex combination (expected embedding) that is fully differentiable with respect to the logits. Theoretical analysis shows this surrogate incurs only second-order bias in the embedding variance (negligible when logits are concentrated, as they are for one-step generators), unlike Gumbel-Softmax straight-through which has first-order bias and high variance. This differentiable representation unlocks three post-distillation refinements on top of the Di[M]O on-policy distillation loss: (i) GAN training, where a discriminator with lightweight heads on the frozen teacher backbone receives randomly-masked soft-embedding sequences and backpropagates adversarial gradients to the generator; (ii) differentiable reward fine-tuning, where CLIP score and ImageReward computed on the tokenizer-decoded image propagate gradients back through the decoder to the generator; and (iii) test-time embedding optimization (TTEO), where the input embedding is optimized at inference time via SGD against a multi-reward objective. The final training loss is a weighted sum of the Di[M]O distillation loss, the GAN loss, and the reward loss.

## Results

On ImageNet-256 class-conditional generation with the MaskBit teacher, Soft-Di[M]O achieves a one-step FID of 1.56 (with extended training), surpassing the 64-step MaskBit teacher (FID 1.66) and all prior one-step or few-step methods. With the MaskGit teacher, Soft-Di[M]O improves the one-step FID from 6.91 (Di[M]O) to 6.40. For text-to-image with the MaskGen-L teacher, Soft-Di[M]O + TTS reaches GenEval 0.63 and HPSv2.1 31.95 in a single step, exceeding the 16-step teacher (GenEval 0.48, HPS 27.60) and all prior one-step continuous distillation methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Soft-Di[M]O decisively outperforms all prior one-step discrete distillation methods: it improves FID from 2.89 (Di[M]O-MaskBit) to 1.96, and with extended training reaches 1.56, matching the 64-step MaskBit teacher (1.66) in a single forward pass. Against continuous diffusion baselines (SiT-XL/2 FID 2.06 at 250 steps, DiT-XL/2 FID 2.27 at 250 steps, U-ViT-H FID 2.29 at 50 steps), the one-step Soft-Di[M]O is competitive or superior while requiring 50–250× fewer inference steps. Among few-step-from-scratch methods, MeanFlow (FID 3.43 at 1 step) and iCT (FID 34.24 at 1 step) are far behind. For text-to-image, Soft-Di[M]O-MaskGen-L + TTS (GenEval 0.63, HPS 31.95) surpasses the 16-step MaskGen-L teacher (GenEval 0.48, HPS 27.60) and all one-step continuous distillation baselines including SDXL-Turbo (GenEval 0.55, HPS 28.66), SDXL-DMD2 (GenEval 0.55, HPS 28.44), and SiD-LSG (GenEval 0.36, HPS 22.13). On the MaskGit teacher, Soft-Di[M]O (FID 6.40, 1 step) improves over Di[M]O (6.91) and matches the 16-step teacher (6.60), while outperforming all few-step samplers (Halton 8-step FID 7.73, ReDi3-distill 1-step FID 11.68).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Di[M]O (Zhu et al., 2025b), the first framework for one-step distillation of Masked Diffusion Models, by introducing soft embeddings — the expected token embedding under the generator's output distribution — as a differentiable surrogate for discrete outputs. Conceptually, this connects to the broader family of continuous relaxations (Gumbel-Softmax, soft-argmax) used in discrete sequence modeling, but the paper provides a clean theoretical argument that the simple expectation-based relaxation has only second-order bias under concentrated logits, whereas Gumbel-Softmax straight-through retains first-order bias. The work also positions itself at the intersection of continuous diffusion distillation (DMD, SiD, LCM) and GAN-based refinement (SDXL-Turbo, SDXL-Lightning), extending these post-training techniques to the discrete MDM setting where they were previously inapplicable due to gradient blocking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The soft embedding technique is simple enough to be broadly applicable to any one-step discrete generator, unlocking GAN refinement, differentiable reward fine-tuning, and test-time embedding optimization that were previously inaccessible. The practical impact is significant: one-step FID of 1.56 on ImageNet-256 (beating the 64-step teacher) and surpassing the teacher on GenEval/HPS for text-to-image demonstrate that distillation need not be a ceiling. Key limitations include being bounded by the tokenizer's reconstruction FID (discrete tokenizers have higher rFID than continuous ones), inheriting teacher weaknesses (e.g., fixed resolution), and the Di[M]O loss remaining essential as a regularizer — GAN-only training fails with mode collapse. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-modal discrete diffusion models and whether SiD-style distillation becomes feasible with soft embeddings.
