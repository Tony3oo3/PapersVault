## Problem

Dual Modular Redundancy (DMR) is the only software technique that guarantees complete silent data corruption (SDC) detection for arbitrary LLM training workloads, yet its ~100% computational overhead has prevented practical deployment at scale. The paper asks how to minimize the throughput loss from DMR without sacrificing its detection guarantees, a question that has left full-coverage DMR systematically excluded from large-scale training practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261826/cropped_page3_idx7.webp)

## Input / Output

SpareTrain takes an existing LLM training job (model architecture, parallelism degrees for PP/TP/EP, activation checkpointing budget, and GPU cluster topology) and an offline profiling run to produce a DMR plan that assigns every operation to one of four verification strategies (P-DMR, D-DMR_inter, D-DMR_intra, or Naive-DMR) along with specific idle-window placements for deferred checkers. The output is a rewritten training codebase that executes full DMR with 3–14% overhead versus unprotected training, plus a runtime mismatch signal that triggers iteration rollback and re-execution upon any detected SDC.

## Method

SpareTrain combines two complementary strategies to hide DMR's checker executions. Piggyback-DMR (P-DMR) repurposes the recomputation inherent in activation checkpointing: the forward pass serves as the primary execution and the backward-pass recomputation as the checker, requiring only one extra run of each AC segment's last operation. Deferred-DMR (D-DMR) schedules remaining checker executions into GPU idle windows caused by inter-node (PP) and intra-node (TP/EP) communication, using Activation-Gradient Checkpointing (AGC) to group operations into coarse segments for inter-node windows and a per-operation D-DMR queue for intra-node windows. A three-phase planner (static for non-MoE, dynamic for MoE) assigns operations to these strategies under memory-headroom and time-slack constraints, with an exception rule that falls back to naive DMR when an AC segment's last operation involves expensive communication that would be replayed under P-DMR.

## Results

SpareTrain achieves 12–35% higher training throughput than naive DMR (Naive-Only) across all evaluated models and memory configurations, while incurring only 3–14% overhead relative to unprotected training (No-DMR). For Llama-3-70B, SpareTrain improves throughput by 31–33% over Naive-Only and trails No-DMR by 3.3–13.8% depending on memory budget. For Mistral-Large, the gains are 21–26% over Naive-Only with 6.1–11.0% slowdown vs. No-DMR. For the MoE model Llama-4-Scout, improvements are 11–14% over Naive-Only with 8.1–9.8% slowdown vs. No-DMR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpareTrain is compared against two baselines: No-DMR (vanilla training without any SDC protection) and Naive-Only (naive DMR applied to every operation, roughly doubling compute). Against Naive-Only, SpareTrain consistently wins, improving throughput by 21–33% for dense models (Llama-3-70B, Mistral-Large) and 11–14% for the MoE model (Llama-4-Scout), all while preserving full DMR error-detection coverage. Against No-DMR, SpareTrain incurs only 3.3–13.8% slowdown for dense models and 8.1–9.8% for the MoE model, compared to the ~100% overhead of Naive-Only. The smaller gap to No-DMR at 80 GB (e.g., 3.3% for Llama-3-70B) is attributed to the higher PP degree (PP=3) required under tight memory, which creates larger inter-node communication windows that SpareTrain exploits for D-DMR_inter. For MoE models, the relative DMR overhead is inherently lower because communication dominates runtime, so the absolute gains are smaller but still meaningful.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes two well-established properties of LLM training—activation checkpointing recomputation and GPU idle time from parallelism strategies—as free resources for Dual Modular Redundancy, a fault-detection technique long studied in hardware and compiler literature (Austin 1999; Reinhardt & Mukherjee 2000; Jeon & Annavaram 2012) but never deployed at LLM-training scale due to its ~100% overhead. It departs from prior SDC-detection work in ML (He et al. 2023; Ma et al. 2025; Dixit et al. 2021), which either used approximate/probabilistic methods or targeted specific kernels, by achieving *complete* DMR coverage through a systematic three-phase planner that assigns each operation to the cheapest available verification strategy (P-DMR, D-DMR_inter, D-DMR_intra, or Naive-DMR). The Activation-Gradient Checkpointing (AGC) concept extends activation checkpointing to the backward pass for coarse-grained deferral, a mechanism not present in prior AC literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLM training runs extend to months across hundreds of thousands of GPUs, even rare SDCs become a dominant reliability risk, yet full DMR has been systematically excluded from practical training due to its perceived doubling of compute cost. SpareTrain's demonstration that full DMR costs only 3–14% overhead (vs. ~100% for naive DMR) across 70B–123B dense and MoE models on up to 32 H200 GPUs makes fault-tolerant training a realistic production option. Open questions include scalability beyond 32 GPUs and 4 nodes, interaction with emerging overlap techniques (e.g., Domino, asyncTP) at larger scale, and whether the planner's offline profiling assumptions hold under dynamic workloads with variable sequence lengths or speculative decoding. The paper also highlights that the optimal AC budget for SpareTrain differs from that of vanilla training, suggesting a co-design dimension between memory management and fault tolerance that remains underexplored.
