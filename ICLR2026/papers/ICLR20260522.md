## Problem

Video world models face a fundamental memory trade-off: transformer architectures preserve fine local detail but are bottlenecked by quadratic attention that caps context length, while recurrent and state-space models scale more efficiently but compress history into hidden states, inevitably discarding long-range fidelity. No single backbone can simultaneously capture short-term dynamics, episodic recall over hundreds of frames, and spatial coherence without incurring prohibitive compute or suffering mode collapse.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a short-term context window (10–100 frames), a long-term history (100–1000 frames) that is distilled into LoRA adapter weights via a few gradient steps at test time, and optional spatial signals (absolute camera poses or point maps) extracted from the full trajectory. It outputs 17 future video frames at 64×64 (Memory Maze) or 256×256 (RealEstate10K, Minecraft) resolution, and in the navigation setting scores candidate 8-step rollouts against a goal image to select trajectories. The LTM's episodic knowledge is stored entirely in ~1.6M trainable LoRA parameters (rank 64) atop a frozen pretrained DiT, enabling constant-time recall without carrying full histories through the core model.

## Method

CoME composes three heterogeneous diffusion experts—short-term memory (a DiT-S with sliding-window attention over 33 frames), long-term memory (a LoRA-adapted DiT finetuned on long-term context at test time), and spatial long-term memory (a DiT-B with doubled patch size conditioned on absolute poses)—into a single generative distribution via a Product of Contrastive Experts (PoCE) formulation. Each conditional expert p_i is paired with its unconditional baseline p̄_i and raised to contrasting exponents (p_i^α · p̄_i^(1−α), α>1), which the paper proves (Proposition 1) reweights KDE mixture components to suppress spurious modes without distorting local kernel geometry, unlike naive tempering that shrinks variance. At sampling time, the composite score is the additive sum of all experts' (and their baselines') noise-predictor outputs, plugged directly into the standard DDPM reverse update. The LTM's test-time adaptation uses only a handful of gradient steps per context frame on LoRA adapters, providing implicit regularization against catastrophic forgetting while amortizing future recall into constant-time weight lookups.

## Results

On Memory Maze, CoME achieves LPIPS 0.097, SSIM 0.892, and PSNR 23.07, outperforming the full-attention transformer baseline (LPIPS 0.113, SSIM 0.859, PSNR 22.78) and SSM-based memory (LPIPS 0.158, SSIM 0.828, PSNR 20.62) while requiring only ~3× forward compute versus ~10× for full attention. On the RECON navigation benchmark, CoME reduces Absolute Trajectory Error to 0.96 and Relative Pose Error to 0.28, improving over the Navigation World Model (NWM) baseline (ATE 1.13, RPE 0.35) by 15% and 20% respectively. On RealEstate10K recall, CoME achieves LPIPS 0.359 and PSNR 21.3, surpassing the best history-guided diffusion variant (HG-t: LPIPS 0.400, PSNR 19.7) by over 10% in perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoME consistently outperforms all four external baselines across both reconstruction and navigation metrics. On RECON navigation, CoME (ATE 0.96, RPE 0.28) beats GNM (ATE 1.87, RPE 0.73) by nearly 2×, NOMAD (ATE 1.93, RPE 0.52) by over 2×, and the strongest prior method NWM (ATE 1.13, RPE 0.35) by 15% on ATE and 20% on RPE. On RealEstate10K recall, CoME (LPIPS 0.359, PSNR 21.3, SSIM 0.83) surpasses both History Guidance variants from Song et al. (2025): HG-v (LPIPS 0.414, PSNR 19.2) and HG-t (LPIPS 0.400, PSNR 19.7), with the largest gap in perceptual quality (~10% lower LPIPS vs. HG-t). On Memory Maze, CoME also beats the full-attention transformer (LPIPS 0.097 vs. 0.113) and SSM-based memory (0.097 vs. 0.158) while using a fraction of the training compute (~60× less than full attention per the paper's analysis).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Product of Experts framework (Hinton, 2002) and compositional diffusion (Du et al., 2023) from image generation to video world models, introducing a contrastive variant (PoCE) that prevents the mode collapse observed when naively multiplying heterogeneous expert distributions. It departs from prior memory-augmented world models (SlowFast-VGen, WorldMem, SSM-based approaches) by decoupling memory into three specialized diffusion experts—short-term, long-term (stored in LoRA-adapted weights via test-time finetuning), and spatial—rather than relying on a single architecture to handle all temporal scales. The DDPM framework and LoRA adaptation provide the technical substrate, but the key conceptual contribution is the compositional, inference-time fusion of these experts without retraining the base model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular, layer-on-top design means CoME can be applied to any existing diffusion-based world model without architectural changes, making it a practical tool for the growing ecosystem of video world models. The ablation showing that naive PoE *degrades* performance below the base model (Table 4) provides a clear theoretical and empirical justification for the contrastive mechanism, which is a useful caution for anyone attempting multi-expert composition in diffusion settings. Open questions include whether the LTM's weight-based memory can capture explicit temporal dependencies (currently it stores episodic content but not temporal structure), how the approach scales beyond ~500 frames, and whether the sensitivity to expert quality (noted in Limitations) can be mitigated. The 2–3× inference overhead from multiple forward passes remains a practical constraint for real-time applications.
