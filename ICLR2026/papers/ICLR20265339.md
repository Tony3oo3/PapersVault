## Problem

Source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) for facial expression recognition typically assumes access to target data spanning all expression classes, yet in real-world personalization only a short neutral control video is practically available from a new user. Existing data-based SFDA methods that do handle this setting rely on GAN-based image translation, which is computationally expensive, unstable, and introduces pixel-level artifacts that suppress subtle expression cues. This paper asks whether a lightweight, stable adaptation is possible by translating features in the latent space rather than synthesizing images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265339/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of unlabeled neutral-expression frames (224×224 RGB crops extracted from video) of a single target subject, together with a fully frozen pretrained source classifier (ResNet-18, ViT-B/32, or ResNet-50 backbone plus a classification head). During adaptation, only the lightweight translator layers are updated; the source backbone and classifier remain fixed. At inference, a new frame from the same target subject is passed through the frozen encoder, the adapted translator, and the frozen classifier to yield a discrete expression label (e.g., pain vs. no-pain, stressed vs. neutral, or one of seven basic emotions). No source data, no expressive target frames, and no additional parameters at inference time are required.

## Method

SFDA-PFT introduces a translator network T (lightweight subject-adaptive layers appended to the frozen source encoder F) that is first pre-trained on labeled source data with a subject-swapping objective: for a pair of source images from different subjects, T maps the feature of subject i into the identity style of subject j while preserving the expression of i. This is enforced by two complementary losses — a KL-divergence expression-consistency term between classifier predictions on the original and translated features, and a style-alignment term that matches per-channel mean and standard deviation of early-layer activations to the reference subject's features. During target adaptation, only the expression-consistency loss is applied to a few neutral frames of the target subject, updating solely T's parameters while F and C stay frozen. By operating entirely in the feature space and matching only low-order statistics of shallow layers, the method avoids the instability and computational overhead of pixel-level image synthesis, achieving up to 100× fewer parameters and 17× fewer FLOPs at inference compared to image-translation baselines such as SFDA-IT.

## Results

On BioVid (pain recognition), SFDA-PFT achieves an average F1 of 78.31%, outperforming the best image-translation baseline SFDA-IT (71.74%) by 6.6 points and the best model-based method DSFDA (68.48%) by 9.8 points. On StressID (stress recognition), SFDA-PFT reaches 69.92% F1, surpassing SFDA-IT (63.70%) by 6.2 points. On the in-the-wild datasets BAH and Aff-Wild2, SFDA-PFT attains 57.40% and 54.46% F1 respectively, again leading all baselines. In terms of efficiency, SFDA-PFT requires only 135 adaptation iterations and 0.95 s per batch, compared to 705–1400 iterations and 54–75 s for competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFDA-PFT outperforms all six baselines across all four datasets. Against the model-based methods, the margins are largest: on BioVid, SFDA-PFT (78.31 F1) exceeds SHOT (47.82) by ~30 points, NRC (48.44) by ~30 points, and TPDS (62.27) by ~16 points, while DSFDA (68.48) is the closest model-based competitor at ~10 points behind. Among data-based (image-translation) baselines, SFDA-PFT leads SFDA-IT by 6.6 F1 points on BioVid, 6.2 on StressID, 5.6 on BAH, and 3.4 on Aff-Wild2, and beats SFIT by 7.8, 7.4, 4.5, and 4.2 points respectively. The gap over DSFDA (the prior best FER-specific SFDA method) is consistent at 4–15 F1 points depending on the dataset. In addition to accuracy, SFDA-PFT is markedly more efficient: it converges in 135 iterations and 0.95 s per batch versus 705–1400 iterations and 54–75 s for the model-based baselines, and requires up to 100× fewer parameters and 17× fewer FLOPs than SFDA-IT at inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) literature for facial expression recognition, departing from the dominant image-translation paradigm (SFDA-IT, SFIT) and the generative-model approach of the authors' own prior work DSFDA. Rather than synthesizing source-style images or disentangling identity/expression via GANs, SFDA-PFT introduces a lightweight feature-space translator pretrained with a subject-swapping objective (mapping one source subject's features to another while preserving expression) and adapted per-target using only a KL-divergence self-distillation loss. The style-alignment mechanism—matching per-channel mean and standard deviation of early-layer activations—borrows the statistical-matching intuition of AdaIN-style normalization but applies it in latent space to a frozen backbone, avoiding adversarial training entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practically important constraint in affective computing: collecting only neutral target frames is far easier and less subjective than gathering labeled expressive data, and the 100× parameter / 17× FLOP reduction over image-translation baselines makes test-time personalization feasible on edge or mobile hardware. However, performance still lags the oracle by 10–40 points depending on dataset, and the notable age-related failure (Sub-10 on BioVid) highlights that inter-subject variability along demographic axes remains unresolved. Open questions include whether the subject-swapping pre-training generalizes to multi-class (7-emotion) settings without the binary simplification used in most experiments, and whether age-aware or group-based reference selection can close the gap for older adults.
