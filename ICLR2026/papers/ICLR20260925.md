## Problem

Relation hallucinations—where LVLMs correctly detect objects but misjudge the relationship between them—account for nearly 40% of all hallucinations yet remain the least studied category. Existing mitigation methods treat relational reasoning as a single-step inference, forcing the model to simultaneously identify entities and determine their relationship, which causes heavy reliance on language priors over systematic visual analysis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260925/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a relational Yes/No question (e.g., 'Does the dog chase a disc in the image?') and produces a binary Yes/No answer. The question is first parsed by spaCy to extract subject, object, and relation keywords, which drive both visual token enhancement and the construction of five decomposed sub-questions. The image is encoded into patch-level visual tokens by the model's vision encoder, and the final answer is decoded after the full interleaved reasoning chain completes.

## Method

ChainMPQ is a training-free, three-module framework. First, text-guided cross-attention uses extracted subject/object keywords as Key/Value vectors against image patch features as Query, producing enhanced visual tokens that emphasize entity-relevant regions. Second, the original question is decomposed into five sub-questions: two entity-localization questions and three masking-based relation questions (masking object, subject, or relation respectively), each targeting one component of the relationship. Third, an interleaved text-image reasoning chain sequentially feeds these questions to the model, where each step's answer is appended to a growing textual context and its top-K attended visual tokens (selected via entropy-based adaptive k) form a normalized bias mask added to subsequent attention maps, scaled by a confidence-weighted coefficient λ. This progressive transfer of both textual and visual memories across steps enables the model to build relational understanding incrementally rather than in a single forward pass.

## Results

ChainMPQ achieves the best accuracy across all four LVLMs on both MMRel and R-Bench. On MMRel, it reaches 65.20% (LLaVA-1.5), 65.14% (InstructBLIP), 73.52% (Qwen2.5-VL), and 75.21% (InternVL3.5), outperforming the strongest baseline by 1.7%, 0.62%, 2.16%, and 0.76% respectively. On R-Bench, accuracy reaches 76.04%, 75.86%, 83.92%, and 85.05%, with particularly strong precision gains (e.g., 72.03% vs. 67.86% for LLaVA-1.5, a +4.17% improvement over the best baseline). F1 scores also improve consistently, confirming gains in both precision and recall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChainMPQ outperforms all three baselines across every model–benchmark combination. Against Constraint-Aware Prompting (Wu et al. 2025a), the largest gap appears on Qwen2.5-VL where ChainMPQ gains +3.51% MMRel accuracy (73.52 vs. 70.01) and +2.90% R-Bench accuracy (83.92 vs. 81.02); on LLaVA-1.5 the margin is +2.87% on MMRel. Against Detect-then-Calibrate (Zheng et al. 2024), ChainMPQ leads by +1.70% MMRel accuracy on LLaVA-1.5 (65.20 vs. 63.50) and by +2.16% on Qwen2.5-VL (73.52 vs. 71.36), while on R-Bench the advantage is more consistent (e.g., +1.76% for Qwen2.5-VL, +2.40% for InternVL3.5). Against standard Chain-of-Thought prompting (Wei et al. 2023), the improvements are the most substantial, reaching +3.32% MMRel accuracy on LLaVA-1.5 (65.20 vs. 61.88) and +3.68% on Qwen2.5-VL (73.52 vs. 67.35), indicating that the interleaved text-image chain provides a meaningful advantage over text-only CoT. Notably, ChainMPQ's precision gains (up to +4.17% on R-Bench with LLaVA-1.5) suggest it reduces false-positive relation predictions more effectively than any baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

ChainMPQ extends the Interleaved Modal Chain-of-Thought (ICoT) paradigm from Gao et al. (2025) to the specific problem of relation hallucination in LVLMs, repurposing the idea of progressive multimodal state updates as a training-free inference-time procedure. It departs from prior relation-hallucination mitigations—Constraint-Aware Prompting (Wu et al., 2025a), Detect-then-Calibrate (Zheng et al., 2024), and Triplet Description (Wu et al., 2025b)—by treating relational reasoning as a multi-step process that accumulates both textual answers and visual attention masks across sub-questions, rather than performing single-step inference or post-hoc logit calibration. The multi-perspective question decomposition (masking subject, object, or relation) is a novel prompting strategy not present in the cited baselines, and the entropy-adaptive top-k attention bias mechanism is a new way to propagate visual focus across reasoning steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Relation hallucinations constitute roughly 40% of all LVLM hallucinations yet have received comparatively little methodological attention, making a training-free, architecture-agnostic mitigation with consistent gains across four diverse models practically valuable for practitioners who cannot fine-tune. The Light1 variant (retaining only three sub-questions) achieves most of the accuracy gain at roughly half the latency, offering a deployable efficiency trade-off. Open questions remain: the method relies on attention distributions as a proxy for visual evidence, which may not capture true causal grounding, and the spatial granularity of fixed-size visual tokens misaligns with object boundaries—a limitation the authors acknowledge in their lower spatial-category performance. Future directions include causality-based attribution and multi-scale or scene-graph-based visual representations.
