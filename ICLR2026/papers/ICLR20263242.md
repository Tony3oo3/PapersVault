## Problem

Existing Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) variants suffer from unstable training dynamics: shallow-layer prompts stagnate near initialization while deeper-layer prompts exhibit high-variance gradient oscillations, producing a cross-layer mismatch that slows convergence and degrades accuracy. The paper attributes this to two root causes—task-agnostic prompt initialization that misaligns early gradients, and independent per-layer prompt optimization that lacks explicit cross-layer coordination.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263242/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (e.g., 224×224 for ViT-Base/16) and produces class labels for 25 classification datasets or pixel-wise segmentation masks on ADE20K. During the MPA initialization phase, a single mini-batch of training images is Fourier-transformed and probed with sliding frequency masks (w=16, r=8) to identify task-relevant spectral regions; this one-time search takes ~74 seconds. At inference, no additional parameters or computation are introduced—the frozen backbone processes images identically to the base VPT variant.

## Method

PAE couples two components. First, Modal Pre-Alignment (MPA) identifies task-aware frequency shortcuts by masking local regions of the 2D Fourier spectrum, ranking masks by task loss on a mini-batch, and constructing the first-layer prompt via energy-weighted pooling of the corresponding patch tokens; this prompt is then propagated through the frozen encoder blocks to initialize all L layers coherently. Second, the Koopman-Lyapunov Discrete dynamical system (KLD) projects each layer's T×d prompt matrix into a shared K-dimensional latent space via a learnable matrix U, then enforces cross-layer evolution through a single shared Koopman operator K (initialized as identity) that maps z_i to z_{i+1}=z_i K. A Koopman consistency loss L_kp minimizes the Frobenius-norm gap between predicted and actual projected states across all layer transitions, while a Lyapunov-style regularizer L_stab penalizes any increase in V(z)=tr(zQz^T) between successive layers, adaptively constraining error amplification only when the stability condition is violated. The total objective is L_task + α·L_kp + β·L_stab, trained end-to-end with a reduced initial learning rate (0.25 vs. the larger rates typical of VPT variants).

## Results

PAE provides an average 1.41× convergence speedup and 1–3% accuracy gains across 25 datasets when plugged into existing VPT variants. On VTAB-1k (ViT-B/16), VPT+PAE reaches 74.84% mean accuracy (vs. 71.96% without PAE) with a 1.78× speedup, while SA2VP+PAE achieves 77.49% (vs. 75.83%) at 1.60× speedup. On FGVC, BPT+PAE attains 92.21% mean accuracy (vs. 90.86%), and all PAE-augmented variants surpass Full Fine-tuning (68.96% VTAB-1k mean, 88.54% FGVC). On ADE20K segmentation, VPT+PAE improves mIoU by +2.73 (SS) and +1.96 (MS) over vanilla VPT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAE is a prompt-agnostic plug-in, so the primary comparison is each VPT variant with vs. without PAE. The largest relative gains appear on simpler baselines: VPT improves by +2.88% VTAB-1k mean and +1.91% FGVC with a 1.78× speedup, and VQT gains +2.80% on VTAB-1k mean. Stronger baselines still benefit but with smaller margins—SA2VP gains +1.66% VTAB-1k mean and VFPT gains +0.94%, reflecting that these methods already incorporate some frequency or spatial priors. All PAE-augmented variants outperform Full Fine-tuning on VTAB-1k (e.g., VPT+PAE 74.84% vs. 68.96%) while training far fewer parameters. Against other PEFT methods (LoRA, Adapter, SPT-LoRA, DM-LoRA) reported in the appendix, PAE-augmented VPT variants are competitive or superior on FGVC and VTAB-1k while requiring no backbone modification and incurring zero inference-time overhead. On ADE20K segmentation, VPT+PAE (46.81 mIoU-SS) outperforms SPT-LoRA (45.40) and approaches Full-tuning (47.60) with a 1.29× speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) and its many variants (E2VPT, VFPT, SA2VP, ProVP, BPT, LPT, VQT) by reframing per-layer prompt optimization as a discrete dynamical system governed by a shared Koopman operator, drawing on Koopman spectral theory (Mezić, 2013) and Lyapunov stability theory (Shevitz & Paden, 1994). The initialization component (MPA) builds on frequency-shortcut analyses of pretrained ViTs (Wang et al., 2023; 2025a), which showed that backbones exploit specific spectral regions for recognition. Conceptually, the key departure from prior VPT work is the explicit cross-layer coupling: instead of treating each layer's prompt as an independent parameter, PAE imposes a single linear evolution rule across all layers, addressing the diagnosed mismatch between shallow-layer stagnation and deep-layer oscillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PAE offers a practical, drop-in enhancement for the entire VPT family with no backbone modification or inference-time cost, which is valuable given the proliferation of VPT variants in parameter-efficient fine-tuning. The dynamical-systems lens (Koopman spectra, Lyapunov energy) provides a new diagnostic vocabulary for understanding prompt optimization dynamics, and the CKA-based visualization of prompt redundancy across layers is a useful tool for the community. However, the absolute gains (1–3% accuracy, ~1.4× speedup) are modest, and the authors acknowledge that MPA's reliance on a labeled mini-batch limits applicability to low- or zero-shot regimes. An open question is whether the shared-operator formulation generalizes to non-ViT architectures or to settings where the number of prompt tokens varies per layer.
