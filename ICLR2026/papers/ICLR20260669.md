## Problem

Predictive models for invasive mechanical ventilation (IMV) in ICUs suffer significant performance degradation (e.g., ~12% AUC drop) when deployed across different hospitals due to domain shifts in patient populations, clinical practices, and EHR systems. Existing remedies like multi-center pre-training or target-domain fine-tuning require labeled data from the deployment site or costly retraining, making them impractical for continuous real-time clinical deployment. Test-Time Training (TTT) offers a source-free alternative, but prior TTT methods rely on feature-agnostic self-supervised objectives and rigid distribution alignment that are poorly suited to the heterogeneous, clinically structured nature of EHR data.

## Input / Output

The model consumes structured EHR records assembled at each hourly timestamp: 50 vital signs and laboratory values (aggregated via median into hourly bins with carry-forward up to 24h and mean imputation), 6 demographics, 12 SIRS/SOFA criteria, 12 medication categories, 62 comorbidities, plus 150 derived features including 72-hour baselines, local trends, and time-since-last-measured (TSLM) encodings. At deployment the model operates in a strictly source-free setting—no source samples or labels are available—and produces a single IMV risk probability per encounter, with a positive prediction defined as risk score ≥ 3 on a clinical severity scale within a 24-hour prediction window. After each test instance, encoder weights are reset to their pretrained state to prevent cross-patient carryover.

## Method

AdaTTT builds on a Y-shaped architecture (shared TSLM+MLP encoder with separate classification and SSL heads) and introduces three coupled mechanisms. First, it derives information-theoretic bounds (via Fano's and Hellman's inequalities under a Markov chain Y_s'→Z'→Y_m') showing that test-time prediction error is upper-bounded by the conditional entropy H(Y_m'|Y_s'), motivating tight alignment between auxiliary and main tasks. Second, it replaces fixed random masking with a dynamic, feature-aware masking strategy: gradient-based relevance scores I_j from the main task classifier determine per-feature masking probabilities, and two complementary pretext tasks (full reconstruction and masked-feature modeling) are optimized so that the SSL branch prioritizes clinically salient variables. Third, it introduces prototype-guided adaptation via Partial Optimal Transport: k learned prototypes capture source-domain structure, and at test time the single test feature is augmented with k−1 Gaussian-perturbed duplicates (variance set from prototype spread) to convert the partial-matching problem into a standard Sinkhorn OT problem, enabling flexible soft alignment that avoids overfitting to noisy or site-specific measurements. At deployment, only the encoder is updated for 5 gradient steps per instance using the combined SSL + OT loss, after which weights are reset.

## Results

AdaTTT achieves the highest AUC across all three external test cohorts: 85.02 ± 0.05% on UCSD Health, 84.10 ± 0.05% on UCI Health, and 77.17 ± 0.08% on MIMIC-IV, outperforming the unadapted TEST baseline (84.01%, 83.75%, 75.28%) by 1.0, 0.4, and 1.9 AUC points respectively. On calibration, AdaTTT also attains the best Brier scores (0.086, 0.085, 0.106) versus TEST (0.089, 0.089, 0.111). The method requires only 5 gradient steps per test instance with a per-instance reset, and the average per-step execution time is 0.29 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaTTT outperforms all eight baselines on AUC at every test site. The closest competitor is SAR (sharpness-aware entropy minimization), which reaches 84.30 ± 0.04 on UCSDH—still 0.72 AUC points below AdaTTT—and 83.20 on UCIH, a 0.90-point gap. CoTTA (continual test-time adaptation with EMA teacher) is the strongest on MIMIC-IV at 76.60 ± 0.05, yet AdaTTT leads by 0.57 points. Standard TTT and TTT++ (feature-agnostic SSL with optional moment alignment) trail AdaTTT by 2.5–4.7 AUC points across sites, confirming that task-agnostic masking and rigid source-target alignment are insufficient for heterogeneous EHR features. TENT (entropy minimization on BN parameters) and ClusT3 (discrete-code mutual information) show the weakest performance, with ClusT3 dropping to 81.99 on UCIH and 74.41 on MIMIC-IV, likely due to its domain-consistency assumption breaking under irregular ICU sampling. NC-TTT (noise-contrastive likelihood) and T3A (classifier-only prototype reweighting) also fall 1.5–2.7 points behind AdaTTT, underscoring the benefit of adapting the shared encoder rather than only the classification head.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Test-Time Training (TTT) framework of Sun et al. (2020) from vision to EHR-based ICU prediction, where prior TTT/TTA work has been largely absent. It departs from standard TTT in two key ways: (1) replacing feature-agnostic SSL masking with a dynamic, task-aware masking strategy that prioritizes clinically informative features, and (2) substituting the rigid full-distribution alignment of TTT++ with Partial Optimal Transport (POT) over learned prototypes, borrowing the POT formulation from Chapel et al. (2020). The information-theoretic bounds connecting auxiliary-task uncertainty to main-task error provide a principled justification for the auxiliary task design, going beyond the empirical motivation in prior TTT work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Domain shift is a well-documented and practically critical barrier to deploying clinical ML models across hospitals, and this paper demonstrates that source-free, per-instance test-time adaptation can recover meaningful performance (1–2 AUC points) without labeled target data or expensive retraining. The low computational overhead (~0.29 s per update) makes the approach feasible for real-time ICU monitoring. However, the gains are modest and the method's sensitivity to extreme conditional shifts (e.g., changes in clinical practice patterns) remains an open concern; prospective validation and evaluation under more severe distributional changes would strengthen the clinical deployment case.
