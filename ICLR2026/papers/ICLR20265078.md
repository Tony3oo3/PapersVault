## Problem

EMG-based hand gesture recognition on wearable devices struggles to generalize to unseen gestures because EMG signals are noisy, heterogeneous, and limited in scale, making it difficult to learn discriminative representations from EMG alone. Cross-modal representation learning, which aligns a weaker modality with a higher-quality anchor, has proven effective in vision-language and audio-visual settings but has not been explored for wearable EMG. This paper tackles the gap by proposing the first cross-modal framework to achieve zero-shot gesture classification from EMG signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265078/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multichannel surface EMG sequences (C channels × T timesteps) as input, preprocessed with instance normalization, 2–250 Hz band-pass filtering, and 60 Hz notch filtering, segmented into non-overlapping 2-second windows. During training, each EMG window is paired with synchronized hand pose (20 joint angles per timestep) from a motion capture system. The output is a d-dimensional EMG embedding in a shared latent space with frozen pose embeddings, which supports zero-shot gesture classification via k-nearest-neighbor voting over pose labels or linear probing with a frozen encoder.

## Method

EMBridge adopts an asymmetric cross-modal setup where a frozen Pose-MAE encoder serves as a fixed anchor and only the EMG encoder is optimized. Three components drive alignment: (1) a Q-Former (adapted from BLIP-2) with learnable queries and self-attention initialized from the pose encoder extracts pose-informative features from EMG via cross-attention; (2) a Masked Pose Reconstruction Loss (MPRL) forces the queries to encode structured pose content by requiring them to reconstruct masked pose tokens without direct access to EMG; and (3) a Community-Aware Soft Contrastive Learning (CASCLe) objective replaces one-hot InfoNCE targets with soft probabilities derived from k-means cluster communities in the frozen pose embedding space, aligning the relative neighborhood geometry across modalities rather than only instance-level pairs. The total loss combines InfoNCE, CASCLe, and MPRL, and both unimodal encoders are first pre-trained independently with MAE before cross-modal fine-tuning.

## Results

On the emg2pose dataset, EMBridge achieves 0.777 balanced accuracy for zero-shot (ZS) in-distribution gesture classification and 0.528 for unseen gestures, outperforming all baselines in the ZS setting. On NinaPro, EMBridge reaches 0.692 ZS accuracy on in-distribution gestures, a substantial margin over CPEP (0.604) and Q-Former (0.613). Notably, EMBridge's ZS performance on unseen emg2pose gestures (0.528) surpasses the linear-probing accuracy of all unimodal baselines (best: PoseT at 0.433), demonstrating that cross-modal alignment yields representations strong enough for label-free classification. The upper bound from probing the frozen Pose-MAE encoder is 0.851 (in-dist.) and 0.649 (unseen) on emg2pose.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the emg2pose benchmark models (NeuroPose, emg2pose, Vemg2pose) and PoseT, EMBridge's ZS accuracy on unseen gestures (0.528) exceeds the best unimodal LP accuracy (PoseT at 0.433), confirming that cross-modal alignment produces more generalizable representations than supervised EMG-only training. Compared to CPEP, EMBridge wins decisively on ZS for in-distribution gestures on NinaPro (0.692 vs. 0.604, +8.8 points) and on unseen emg2pose gestures (0.528 vs. 0.481, +4.7 points), but CPEP retains a slight LP edge on unseen gestures for both datasets (0.536 vs. 0.505 on emg2pose; 0.483 vs. 0.492 on NinaPro), which the authors attribute to query-averaging vs. max-similarity selection. Against the plain Q-Former (InfoNCE only), EMBridge improves unseen ZS on emg2pose by 3.0 points (0.528 vs. 0.498) and in-dist. ZS on NinaPro by 7.9 points (0.692 vs. 0.613). In the ablation of soft contrastive objectives, CASCLe outperforms both Label Smoothing (Fini et al., 2023) and adapted SoftCLIP (Gao et al., 2023) on ZS unseen gestures (0.528 vs. 0.511 and 0.510), validating that community-level structural alignment is more effective than instance-level or uniform soft targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMBridge extends the BLIP-2 Q-Former architecture and CLIP-style contrastive alignment to the EMG-pose domain, but departs from the symmetric encoder training of CLIP by freezing the pose encoder as a fixed anchor and optimizing only the EMG side. The community-aware soft contrastive objective (CASCLe) builds on soft-target ideas from SoftCLIP and label smoothing but introduces a novel community-level structural similarity via k-means clustering of pose embeddings. The masked pose reconstruction loss (MPRL) adapts the BLIP-2 reconstruction mechanism to enforce that learnable queries capture pose-informative content from EMG, going beyond pure contrastive alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EMBridge is the first framework to demonstrate zero-shot gesture classification from wearable EMG, which is critical for real-world applications like VR/AR and prosthetic control where users cannot record training data for every possible gesture. The data-efficiency result (40% of paired data still outperforms full-data unimodal baselines) is practically encouraging, but the reliance on paired EMG-pose data remains a significant bottleneck since such datasets are scarce. Open questions include whether the framework extends to other bio-signal modalities (RGB-EMG, Video-EMG), whether unpaired pose pre-training can reduce paired-data dependence, and whether probabilistic community modeling (e.g., GMM-based soft assignments) can further improve neighborhood structure.
