## Problem

Existing compositional 3D generators, trained on part-level object data, fail to organize generated parts into coherent instances when applied to open-world scenes, exhibiting structural mispartition (a single object's geometry scattered across multiple part-tokens) and geometric redundancy (multiple latents competing over the same region). Through a debiased CCA-based clustering probe, the authors identify the root cause as a lack of explicit structural constraints in the model's internal patch-to-part assignment mechanism, rather than insufficient feature information.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (no instance masks required) and produces a structured 3D scene decomposed into N distinct part-level meshes, each decoded independently by a frozen 3DShape2VecSet VAE and then fused into the full scene. It operates as a training-free, inference-time intervention on a pretrained PartPacker rectified-flow DiT (24 blocks, dual-volume latent of 8192 tokens × 64 channels), with the image encoded by a frozen DINOv2 into L patch features that serve as the conditioning signal for cross-attention.

## Method

SceneTransporter reframes the routing of image patch evidence to part-level 3D tokens as a globally optimal correlation assignment problem, solved at each denoising step via an entropic Optimal Transport (OT) formulation with Sinkhorn iteration (40 iterations). The resulting transport plan gates cross-attention multiplicatively through a bounded, identity-preserving function ψ(·), enforcing exclusive one-to-one routing so each patch contributes to only one part and preventing feature entanglement. An edge-regularized assignment cost, derived from a Canny/Sobel edge map on the conditioning image, performs edge-aware smoothing on patch-part affinities to suppress cross-boundary transport and sharpen object boundaries without requiring semantic masks. The OT-gated attention is applied in the first half of the DiT blocks while standard cross-attention handles the remainder, and the entire mechanism is training-free, requiring no retraining of the underlying generator.

## Results

On a 74-image open-world benchmark, SceneTransporter achieves the best geometry fidelity across all three metrics: ULIP 0.1466, ULIP-2 0.3220, and Uni3D 0.3021, outperforming PartPacker (0.1417 / 0.3083 / 0.2887), MIDI (0.1397 / 0.2763 / 0.2518), and PartCrafter (0.1177 / 0.3096 / 0.2635). For part disentanglement, it attains the second-lowest IoUmax (0.0101) and second-lowest IoUmean (0.0926), with PartCrafter reporting the absolute lowest IoUmax (0.0042) largely because it discards background regions. Inference time is 54.99 s, comparable to PartPacker (47.41 s) and substantially faster than MIDI (149.68 s) and PartCrafter (157.97 s). In a 30-participant user study, the method receives the highest average rank on geometry (3.09/4), layout (3.34/4), and segmentation plausibility (3.22/4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SceneTransporter outperforms all three baselines on every geometry-fidelity metric (ULIP, ULIP-2, Uni3D), with the largest margin over PartCrafter on ULIP (0.1466 vs. 0.1177) and over MIDI on Uni3D (0.3021 vs. 0.2518). Against PartPacker—the closest competitor in speed and architecture—SceneTransporter improves ULIP by 3.4%, ULIP-2 by 4.6%, and Uni3D by 4.7%, while also reducing IoUmean from 0.2142 to 0.0926 (a 57% reduction in inter-part overlap). PartCrafter reports the lowest IoUmax (0.0042), but the authors attribute this to its practice of discarding background/ground regions, which trivially reduces overlap at the cost of scene completeness; on a fair comparison that includes full scenes, SceneTransporter's IoUmax of 0.0101 is the best among methods that preserve the full scene. MIDI, which requires additional instance masks at inference and was trained primarily on indoor assets, degrades noticeably on outdoor scenes with spatial layout distortions and weaker instance separation. In the user study, SceneTransporter is preferred over PartPacker by a wide margin on segmentation plausibility (3.22 vs. 1.97) and layout coherence (3.34 vs. 2.95), confirming that the OT-guided routing produces more coherent object-level parts in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

SceneTransporter builds directly on PartPacker (Tang et al., 2025), a compositional 3D latent diffusion model using rectified-flow DiT with dual-volume packing, and intervenes training-free within its denoising loop. Conceptually, it departs from prior compositional generators (PartCrafter, PartPacker) that rely on implicit cross-attention to route image evidence to part tokens, instead reframing this routing as a global Optimal Transport assignment problem solved via Sinkhorn iteration. The paper also draws on the 2D attention-control literature (prompt-to-prompt, Attend-and-Excite) but notes that 3D VecSet representations lack the regular spatial grid that makes such manipulation straightforward, motivating the OT-based alternative. A CCA-based debiased clustering probe is introduced as a diagnostic tool to show that the failure is in the assignment mechanism rather than in the learned features themselves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete gap: existing part-level 3D generators produce coherent objects but fragment or entangle when applied to open-world scenes, and the OT formulation offers a principled, training-free fix that requires no additional segmentation supervision. The finding that the OT plan stabilizes early in the denoising schedule (coarse routing decided by ~90% of steps) suggests a potential for early-exit or hierarchical generation strategies. Limitations include reliance on synthetic training data (requiring style transfer for real images), under-counting of very small crowded instances, and the approach being tied to the specific PartPacker dual-volume architecture. Open questions include whether OT-guided routing generalizes to other 3D latent representations (e.g., triplane, voxel) and whether the edge-regularized cost can be replaced by learned boundary priors for more complex scenes.
