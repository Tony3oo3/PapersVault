## Problem

Existing VLA models for autonomous driving suffer from two critical limitations: they produce physically infeasible trajectories (e.g., abrupt steering or acceleration that violate vehicle kinematics), and they employ simplistic reasoning strategies that fail to account for complex road conditions and vehicle dynamic constraints. The interpretability and causal plausibility of the decision process remain largely unexplored in the literature.

## Input / Output

The model takes a front-view camera image, a sequence of historical vehicle states (position, velocity, acceleration, steering angle), and a language instruction as input. It outputs a structured response containing a chain-of-thought reasoning block (wrapped in think tags) followed by six BEV trajectory waypoints at 0.5-second intervals over the next 3 seconds (wrapped in answer tags as coordinate pairs). The four-step CoT explicitly decomposes reasoning into visual analysis, kinematic calculation, contextual logic, and self-reflection validation.

## Method

AutoDrive-R² uses a two-stage training pipeline on Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Stage 1 performs supervised fine-tuning on the newly constructed nuScenes R²-6K dataset (6,000 image-trajectory pairs with four-step CoT annotations generated by Qwen2.5-VL-72B and validated by Qwen-VL-Max), establishing structured reasoning via an Observation→Calculation→Logic→Reflection chain. Stage 2 applies Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a physics-grounded reward that combines four components: spatial alignment (MSE to ground-truth positions), steering-angle consistency, velocity consistency, and temporal smoothness (penalizing abrupt changes between consecutive steps). The GRPO loss uses group-normalized advantages and a KL-divergence regularizer (β=0.04) against the SFT reference policy, sampling G=6 candidate responses per input.

## Results

On nuScenes, AutoDrive-R² (7B) achieves the best average L2 error of 0.19 m and the lowest average collision rate of 0.07%, outperforming EMMA+ (0.29 m L2, no collision data) and DriveVLM-Dual (0.31 m L2, 0.10% collision) by 34.5% and 38.7% in L2 error respectively. On Waymo (zero-shot), AutoDrive-R² (7B) reduces average L2 error to 0.20 m, a 33.3% improvement over EMMA+ (0.30 m) and a 90.7% reduction over the Qwen-2.5-VL-7B baseline (2.13 m). In closed-loop NAVSIM evaluation, AutoDrive-R² (7B) sets a new state-of-the-art PDMS of 89.1, surpassing Para-Drive (84.0) and TransFuser (84.1) by over 5 points, while also leading in Navigation Completion (98.5) and Ego-Progress (82.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoDrive-R² (7B) achieves the best L2 error (0.19 m avg) and lowest collision rate (0.07%) on nuScenes, outperforming all 12 baselines. Against the strongest VLA competitor EMMA+ (0.29 m), it reduces L2 error by 34.5% while also reporting a 0.07% collision rate versus EMMA+'s unreported safety metrics. Compared to the generalist VLM Qwen-2.5-VL-7B (1.45 m), the improvement is 86.9% in L2 error. On Waymo zero-shot, AutoDrive-R² (7B) (0.20 m) beats EMMA+ (0.30 m) by 33.3% and the Qwen-2.5-VL-7B baseline (2.13 m) by 90.7%. In closed-loop NAVSIM, AutoDrive-R² (7B) surpasses Para-Drive (PDMS 84.0), TransFuser (84.1), and UniAD (83.4) with a PDMS of 89.1, a margin of over 5 points, while also leading in Navigation Completion (98.5 vs. 97.9) and Ego-Progress (82.7 vs. 79.3). The 3B variant (0.49 m on nuScenes) underperforms the 7B model but still substantially improves over its Qwen-2.5-VL-3B baseline (4.21 m), demonstrating the two-stage framework's effectiveness even at smaller scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of VLA-based autonomous driving (DriveVLM, EMMA, OmniDrive, AutoVLA) and reasoning-enhanced RL post-training (DeepSeek-R1, AlphaDrive/GRPO). It departs from prior VLA driving methods that employ simplistic single-step reasoning by introducing a structured four-step CoT chain (Observation → Calculation → Logic → Reflection) with an explicit self-reflection validation step, and from prior RL-for-driving work that uses only position-error rewards by proposing a multi-dimensional physics-grounded reward (spatial alignment, steering/velocity dynamics, temporal smoothness). The GRPO training procedure is directly adopted from Jiang et al. (2025), while the CoT dataset construction pipeline (generate with Qwen2.5-VL-72B, validate with Qwen-VL-Max) follows the 'generate-then-validate' pattern common in synthetic data creation for LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The claim that 6K curated CoT samples plus 6K RL samples can match or exceed EMMA+ (trained on 103K scenarios) is a notable data-efficiency result if it holds under scrutiny, suggesting structured reasoning annotations may be more valuable than raw volume for trajectory planning. The emergent 'self-correction moment' where the model detects and fixes its own trajectory errors is conceptually appealing but remains anecdotal and lacks systematic quantification. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-agent interaction, multi-camera inputs, and real-time inference constraints; the paper acknowledges multi-agent coordination and real-time sensor fusion as future work. The physics-grounded reward design, while principled, uses fixed equal weights (λ=1) and the learnable-weight mechanism is not actually explored, leaving the optimal trade-off between geometric accuracy and dynamic feasibility unresolved.
