## Problem

Existing time-series foundation models rely almost exclusively on time-domain features and require task-specific modifications (at the token, pre-training, or projection-head level) to adapt across generative tasks such as forecasting, imputation, and anomaly detection. No prior TSFM handles all generative tasks in a truly task-agnostic manner, and the distributional differences in frequency-domain representations across temporal granularities remain unexplored.

## Input / Output

GTM consumes raw univariate or multivariate time series, which are processed via channel independence, patching (patch length 96, stride 192 within a 1440-timestamp lookback window), and hybrid masking before entering the backbone. For generative tasks (forecasting, imputation, anomaly detection), the model outputs autoregressive patch-level predictions with no architectural change—only masking and 2D positional encoding are removed. For classification, only the final linear projection is swapped to emit class probabilities, while the entire backbone remains frozen or fine-tuned unchanged.

## Method

GTM is a decoder-only Transformer (12 layers, 768-dim) whose key architectural novelty is a Fourier attention module inserted after each temporal self-attention layer: a column-wise FFT moves patch embeddings into the frequency domain, where five low-rank frequency modules (one per time-granularity level: day, hour, minute, second, millisecond) and one global full-connection module operate in parallel, with softmax-weighted aggregation driven by a granularity query attending to five learnable key vectors; an inverse FFT returns the result to the time domain. Pre-training unifies masked reconstruction and autoregressive forecasting under a single hybrid masking strategy (inspired by GLM): with probability 0.3, the last 30% of patches are consecutively masked (tail masking for forecasting), otherwise random spans are masked and shuffled with [START]/[END] tokens; 2D positional encoding informs the model of span lengths, and full vs. causal attention prevents information leakage between the two objectives. The model is trained from scratch on the 12-billion-point UTSD-12G dataset, and the resulting representations transfer to all generative tasks without any task-specific architectural modification.

## Results

On long-term forecasting (primary task), GTM achieves the best average MSE on ETTh1 (0.404), ETTm1 (0.339), and Weather (0.225), and the best average MAE on ETTm1 (0.376) and Weather (0.266), outperforming 9 SOTA baselines including GPT4TS, UniTS-PMT, TTM_E, PatchTST, and TimesNet. Across all 50 forecasting test configurations (5 datasets × 4 prediction lengths × 2 metrics), GTM secures 21 best and 22 second-best results, with PatchTST as the runner-up (14 best, 15 second-best). On anomaly detection, GTM achieves an average F1 of 87.01%, surpassing GPT4TS (86.72%) and TimesNet (85.24%). In zero-shot forecasting, GTM attains the lowest average MSE (0.380) among six SOTA TSFMs including Timer-1B, MOIRAI-S, MOMENT, TimesFM, and Chronos-S1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On forecasting, GTM clearly dominates older Transformer baselines (Informer, Autoformer, FEDformer) by large margins (e.g., 0.404 vs. 1.040 MSE on ETTh1) and beats the strongest recent baselines GPT4TS and PatchTST on ETTh1 and ETTm1, though TTM_E edges out GTM on Electricity MSE (0.158 vs. 0.161) and PatchTST on Electricity MAE (0.252 vs. 0.254). On imputation, GTM achieves the best MSE on ETTh1 (0.053, 23.1% lower than GPT4TS) and ETTm1 (0.021, 25.0% lower than GPT4TS), but loses to PatchTST on Electricity (0.086 vs. 0.072). For anomaly detection, GTM leads on SMAP (77.57% F1 vs. 72.88% GPT4TS) and SWaT (94.78% vs. 93.85% UP2ME), but trails DLinear on MSL (82.53% vs. 84.88%) and UP2ME on PSM (95.43% vs. 97.16%). In classification, GTM secures 5 best-of-10 results (vs. 3 for TimesNet, 2 for GPT4TS), with notable wins on FaceDetection (69.9% vs. 68.6% TimesNet) and UWaveGestureLibrary (89.3% vs. 85.9% iTransformer). In zero-shot forecasting, GTM achieves the best average MSE (0.380) over Timer-1B (0.392), MOIRAI-S (0.405), and MOMENT (0.727), though TimesFM wins on ETTm1 (0.354 vs. 0.593) and Traffic (0.458 vs. 0.542). Against the two additional TSFMs in Table 20, GTM wins 13 of 20 metrics overall and 8 of 15 where Time-MOE-Base is available, while Sundial-Small wins only 1 metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

GTM builds directly on GLM's (Du et al., 2022) autoregressive blank-infilling pre-training paradigm, adapting its hybrid masking, span shuffling, and 2D positional encoding to the time-series domain. It departs from prior TSFMs (Timer, UniTS, UP2ME, TimeSiam) in two key ways: (1) introducing a Fourier attention module with granularity-aware low-rank frequency matrices that operate in the frequency domain via FFT, a perspective largely unexplored in prior TS representation learning; and (2) unifying reconstruction and autoregressive objectives under a single hybrid masking strategy to achieve generative-task-agnostic adaptation without task-specific architectural changes. The decoder-only Transformer backbone follows the lineage of Timer and TimesFM, but the frequency-domain processing and granularity encoding represent a genuine architectural departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The claim of being the first truly generative-task-agnostic TSFM—requiring no modifications at the token, pre-training, or projection-header level for forecasting, imputation, or anomaly detection—is practically significant for industrial deployment where maintaining separate model variants per task is costly. The empirical demonstration of scaling laws (both model size and pre-training data) for time series is valuable for guiding future investment in larger TSFMs. However, open questions remain: the frequency-domain granularity modules add architectural complexity, the performance margins over strong baselines like PatchTST and TTM_E are often modest (e.g., 0.404 vs. 0.402 MSE on ETTh1), and the field still lacks large-scale, consistently benchmarked datasets to validate these claims at scale. The paper itself acknowledges the absence of standardized benchmarks and the limited availability of truly large-scale TS data as ongoing challenges.
