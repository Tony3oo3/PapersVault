## Problem

When users reselect a frame from a Live Photo video clip as the new key photo, that frame suffers significant quality degradation compared to the original key photo, which was processed through the full ISP pipeline. No existing reference-based super-resolution method addresses this specific setting: restoring a single low-quality frame guided by a temporally offset, in-sequence high-quality reference with large motion misalignment and a substantial quality gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260284/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two inputs from the same Live Photo sequence: a low-quality reselected frame (extracted from the compressed preview video stream, suffering motion blur and sensor noise) and the original high-quality key photo (processed through the full ISP pipeline), with a clear temporal offset between them. The output is a restored high-quality version of the reselected frame that matches the visual quality of the original key photo while preserving the content of the reselected moment. Inference operates on 4K images (3072×4096) via a 1024×1024 tiling strategy, and optical flow between the two inputs is estimated with a frozen RAFT model to provide motion guidance.

## Method

LiveMoments employs a dual-branch DiT architecture (both initialized from SD3-medium) where a ReferenceNet encodes the HQ key photo and a RestorationNet performs conditional denoising on the LQ frame, with cross-attention fusing features by concatenating keys and values from both branches. A unified Motion Alignment module operates at two levels: at the latent level, RAFT-estimated optical flow is encoded by a lightweight Motion Encoder and injected as an additive bias on the reference key features in cross-attention; at the image level, a Patch Correspondence Retrieval (PCR) strategy uses the average displacement within each patch to shift the reference patch crop location during tiled inference, ensuring spatial consistency. Training uses a bridge-matching variant of flow matching that learns the velocity field directly between the LQ and HQ latent representations over a shortened time interval t∈[0, 0.2], requiring only 6 sampling steps at inference and preserving low-frequency content while introducing minimal stochasticity for well-posed learning.

## Results

On the synthetic SynLive260 dataset, LiveMoments achieves LPIPS 0.0828, DISTS 0.0365, and FID 4.00, substantially outperforming the next-best methods (LPIPS 0.2416, DISTS 0.1135, FID 13.92) by roughly 3× on perceptual metrics. It also attains the best SSIM (0.8990) and all relative no-reference metrics (e.g., NIQE_re 0.0911, CLIP-Q 0.9950, DINO-Q 0.9740). On the real-world vivoLive144 and iPhoneLive90 datasets, LiveMoments achieves SOTA across all evaluated metrics. The method processes a 4K Live Photo in ~40 seconds with only 6 sampling steps, using 14.53 GB peak memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiveMoments decisively outperforms all 14 baselines on perceptual and reference-based metrics. Against traditional RefISR methods (TTSR, C2-Matching, DATSR, MRefSR), it achieves ~3× lower LPIPS (0.0828 vs. ~0.24) and ~3.5× lower FID (4.00 vs. ~18.5), though these methods marginally lead on PSNR (31.74–31.85 vs. 31.65), which the authors attribute to the known limitations of pixel-level metrics for real-world restoration. Diffusion-based RefISR methods (CoSeR, ReFIR) are far behind: CoSeR scores FID 13.92 and LPIPS 0.2436, while ReFIR(SUPIR) degrades to FID 33.95 and LPIPS 0.3992. RefVSR methods (RefVSR, ERVSR), designed for multi-camera video triplets with minimal temporal gaps, achieve FID 15.10 and 20.22 respectively, still 4–5× worse than LiveMoments. Among SISR baselines, the strongest (StableSR, SeeSR, SUPIR) reach FID 23.77–31.53 and LPIPS 0.29–0.37, while one-step distilled methods (OSEDiff, TSD-SR) are faster but score FID 24–27. On the real-world vivoLive144 and iPhoneLive90 datasets, LiveMoments achieves the best scores across all relative no-reference metrics (NIQE_re, MUSIQ_re, CLIPIQA_re, MANIQA_re, CLIP-Q, DINO-Q), confirming consistent superiority in practical settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

LiveMoments builds on the diffusion-based SISR line (StableSR, SeeSR, SUPIR) and reference-guided generation from video animation (Animate Anyone, MagicAnimate), adapting the SD3 DiT backbone into a dual-branch architecture where a ReferenceNet encodes the HQ key photo and a RestorationNet performs conditional denoising on the LQ frame via cross-attention. It departs from conventional RefISR (which uses externally sourced references) and RefVSR (which processes full video sequences) by formulating restoration of a single frame guided by a temporally offset in-sequence reference, and from standard flow matching by learning a velocity field directly between LQ and HQ latents over a shortened interval [0, 0.2] rather than from pure noise. The unified Motion Alignment module—combining latent-level motion-guided attention (RAFT flow injected as additive bias to cross-attention keys) with image-level Patch Correspondence Retrieval for tiled inference—represents a novel integration of optical flow into diffusion-based restoration that goes beyond the explicit warping strategies used in prior RefISR methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of reselected key photo restoration in Live Photos, a practically important problem for mobile photography where the ISP quality gap between photo and video pipelines is significant. The three-dataset benchmark (SynLive260, vivoLive144, iPhoneLive90) and the proposed relative no-reference metrics (which expose the failure of standard NR-IQA to penalize hallucinated content in reference-guided settings) provide useful evaluation tools for the community. Open questions include whether the dual-branch + motion-guided attention paradigm generalizes to other temporally-offset restoration tasks (e.g., multi-frame video enhancement with sparse references), whether the 6-step inference can be further distilled to one step without quality loss, and how the method behaves under extreme motion or occlusion beyond the demonstrated robustness limits.
