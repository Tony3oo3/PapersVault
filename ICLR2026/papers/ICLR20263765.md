## Problem

Despite transformers' empirical success, the theoretical mechanisms underlying self-attention remain largely unexplored. Prior theoretical analyses have been confined to very specific tasks (in-context linear regression, sparse token selection, group-sparse classification), each requiring bespoke assumptions and techniques. This paper asks whether a single unified framework can provide provable learning guarantees for a broad class of teacher models, and whether a one-layer transformer trained by gradient descent can recover all parameter blocks of such teachers.

## Input / Output

The input is a feature matrix X ∈ R^{d×D} (D tokens of d dimensions) concatenated column-wise with an orthogonal positional encoding matrix P ∈ R^{D×D} to form Z ∈ R^{(d+D)×D}. The teacher model f*(X) = σ(V*XS*) produces an M×D label matrix, where S* is a D×D column-stochastic matrix with exactly K non-zero entries of value 1/K per column, and σ is identity, ReLU, or Leaky ReLU. The trained transformer outputs an M×D prediction matrix, and the key recovered quantities are the value matrix W_V (targeting V*) and the attention score matrix S (targeting S*), both verified in Frobenius norm.

## Method

The core insight is that a wide range of architectures—convolution with average pooling, graph convolution on regular graphs, sparse token selection, and group-sparse linear predictors—share a common bilinear form f*(X) = σ(V*XS*), enabling a unified teacher-student analysis. The student is a one-layer transformer with a simplified 'position-only' attention: TF(Z; W_V; W_KQ) = σ(W_V X S(P^T W_KQ P / √D)), where only the positional encoding enters the softmax score and W_V interacts solely with X. The key technical step is proving that throughout gradient descent, W_V(t) = C₁(t)·V* and W_KQ(t) maintains a structured decomposition over the target index sets G^i, reducing the full matrix optimization to tracking three scalar coefficients C₁(t), C₂(t), C₃(t). This yields a tight Θ(1/T) convergence rate for the excess population loss with matching upper and lower bounds, and an OOD generalization guarantee showing the transformer's loss on any bounded-second-moment distribution is within ε of the teacher's loss after O(KD⁶ξ²/ηε²) iterations.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) establishes a tight Θ(1/T) convergence rate for the excess population loss of a one-layer transformer trained via gradient descent to recover a class of bilinear teacher models, with matching upper and lower bounds. Parameter recovery is also characterized: the value matrix W_V converges to V* at rate Θ(D²√(K/(ηT))) and the attention scores S converge to S* at rate Θ(D^{5/2}/(‖V*‖_F √(ηT))). Empirically, on MNIST the trained transformer achieves cosine similarity above 0.9 with the teacher CNN's convolution kernel matrix, and the teacher CNN itself reaches ~71% test accuracy. Synthetic experiments confirm the predicted −1 slope (log-log) for excess training loss and −0.5 slope for excess OOD loss, validating the Θ(1/T) and O(1/√T) rates respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without standard empirical baselines; comparisons are made against prior theoretical results. Compared to Wang et al. (2024) on sparse token selection, this paper achieves a tighter Θ(1/T) convergence rate with matching lower bounds, versus the O(log(T)/T) rate in Wang et al., under a slightly different formulation where target positions are fixed rather than input-dependent. Compared to Zhang et al. (2025c) on group-sparse linear models, this paper provides a complementary regression result (Zhang et al. focus on classification). The unified bilinear framework also subsumes previously unexplored settings such as convolution layers with average pooling and graph convolution on regular graphs, for which no prior theoretical guarantees existed. The OOD generalization bound (Theorem 3.2) shows the trained transformer's loss exceeds the teacher's OOD loss by at most ε, requiring only bounded second moments on the OOD data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies several previously separate theoretical analyses of one-layer transformers—sparse token selection (Wang et al., 2024), group-sparse linear classification (Zhang et al., 2025c), and spatial structure learning in ViTs (Jelassi et al., 2022)—under a single bilinear teacher model framework. It adopts the position-only attention architecture and the key-query reparameterization from prior theoretical studies, but departs by showing that a single convergence analysis covers all these tasks simultaneously, yielding a tight Θ(1/T) rate with matching lower bounds (sharper than the O(log(T)/T) of Wang et al., 2024). The framework also extends to previously unexplored teacher models such as convolution layers with average pooling and graph convolution on regular graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified bilinear framework provides a principled lens for understanding why transformers succeed across diverse tasks, and the tight convergence guarantees (with matching lower bounds) establish fundamental limits on learning speed. The OOD generalization theorem—showing the trained transformer matches the teacher on arbitrary bounded-moment data—offers a rare distribution-shift guarantee for transformer training. Key limitations include the restriction to one-layer models, Gaussian input assumptions, population loss (infinite data), and the simplified position-only attention; extending to multi-layer transformers, finite-sample settings, and full softmax attention over the entire input Z remain important open directions.
