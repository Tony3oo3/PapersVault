## Problem

Gradient descent for over-parameterized two-layer PINNs requires a learning rate tied to the smallest eigenvalue of the limiting Gram matrix, which depends on sample size and can be extremely small, leading to impractically slow training. While natural gradient descent (NGD) has been shown to enjoy fast convergence for L² regression, its convergence for PINNs—where the loss involves first- and second-order PDE derivatives of the network output—remained an open theoretical problem despite strong empirical evidence.

## Input / Output

The method takes interior collocation points {x_p} and boundary points {y_j} for a second-order linear PDE (e.g., Poisson, Heat, Helmholtz), along with the source term f(x) and boundary data g(x), and trains a two-layer network of width m with ReLU³ or smooth activation. The loss combines the PDE residual (involving ∂φ/∂x₀ and ∂²φ/∂xᵢ²) with boundary residuals, both normalized by sample counts. The output is the network's approximate PDE solution evaluated on a held-out test grid, accompanied by explicit convergence rate bounds that are independent of the Gram matrix's smallest eigenvalue.

## Method

The paper makes two core contributions. First, it derives a new recursion formula for GD that decouples the learning rate from the smallest eigenvalue, yielding η = O(1/λ_max) instead of the prior O(λ_min) requirement, and reduces the width requirement from polynomial in (n₁+n₂) to logarithmic. Second, it provides the first convergence analysis of NGD for PINNs, where the update w(k+1) = w(k) − η Jᵀ(JJᵀ)⁻¹[s;h] achieves a convergence rate of (1−η)ᵏ independent of λ₀ and sample size, with η = O(1) admissible. The key technical tool is a local Jacobian stability lemma (bounding ‖J(w)−J(0)‖₂ in terms of per-neuron weight displacement) that replaces the global Gram-matrix stability used in regression, since PDE derivative terms in the loss invalidate the standard NTK Lipschitz argument. For smooth activation functions satisfying a bounded-third-derivative and analyticity condition, NGD with η=1 achieves quadratic convergence of the residual norm.

## Results

NGD achieves the lowest relative L²-error across all five PDE benchmarks, reaching 1.67e-05 on 1D Poisson, 1.12e-04 on 2D Poisson, 3.42e-04 on 1D Heat, 6.67e-03 on 2D Helmholtz, and 9.91e-04 on 10D Poisson. Theoretically, NGD achieves a convergence rate of (1−η)^k with learning rate η = O(1), independent of the Gram matrix eigenvalue λ₀, whereas GD requires η = O(1/λ_max) and converges at rate (1−ηλ₀/2)^k. For smooth activation functions, NGD with η=1 achieves quadratic convergence. Empirically, NGD converges in 100–200 epochs compared to 10,000–20,000 epochs for SGD and Adam, and is robust to learning rates ranging from 0.001 to 1.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NGD outperforms all three baselines (SGD, Adam, L-BFGS) on every PDE problem tested. On 1D Poisson, NGD achieves 1.67e-05 relative L²-error, roughly 16× better than L-BFGS (2.63e-04) and ~7,700× better than SGD (1.28e-01). On the challenging 2D Helmholtz equation, SGD and L-BFGS diverge (errors of 8.48 and 3.35 respectively), while NGD still achieves 6.67e-03. On 10D Poisson, L-BFGS fails entirely (loss becomes infinite), while NGD reaches 9.91e-04 in just 200 epochs versus 20,000 for SGD/Adam. In the learning rate study, NGD maintains stable convergence (error < 2e-03) across η ∈ [0.001, 1.0], whereas SGD diverges for η ≥ 0.01 and Adam only converges for η ≤ 0.01. In terms of training efficiency, NGD requires 200 epochs (12 min 13 s for 2D Poisson) versus 20,000 epochs (15 min 49 s for SGD), achieving ~100× fewer iterations with comparable wall-clock time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NTK-based convergence analysis framework from Du et al. (2018) and Gao et al. (2023) for gradient descent on over-parameterized PINNs, improving the learning rate requirement from O(λ₀) to O(1/λ_max) via a new residual recursion formula. More substantially, it bridges the gap between NGD convergence results for L² regression (Zhang et al. 2019; Cai et al. 2019) and the PINN setting, providing the first theoretical convergence guarantee for NGD on PINNs—previously only demonstrated empirically by Müller & Zeinhofer (2023) and Guzmán-Cordero et al. (2025). The key technical departure from the regression case is handling the higher-order derivative terms in the PDE residual, which invalidates the global Jacobian stability argument used in Zhang et al. (2019) and necessitates a per-neuron local stability analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that NGD achieves an O(1) learning rate with convergence independent of the Gram matrix's smallest eigenvalue λ₀ is practically significant: it removes the need to tune learning rates to the (often extremely small) condition number of the PINN loss, which is a known bottleneck in practice. The quadratic convergence for smooth activations further suggests NGD could dramatically reduce training time for high-accuracy PDE solutions. Key limitations include the restriction to two-layer networks, full-batch setting, and over-parameterization; the computational cost of inverting the (n₁+n₂)×(n₁+n₂) Gram matrix limits scalability to large collocation sets. Open questions include extending the analysis to deep PINNs, stochastic NGD variants, and establishing generalization guarantees beyond the training loss.
