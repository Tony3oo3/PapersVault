## Problem

Current large multimodal models struggle to produce tightly interleaved image-text outputs that follow complex instructions, primarily because existing training datasets are small (tens of thousands of samples), built from noisy web-crawled sources without standardized quality control, and limited to single-turn or static-document formats. Additionally, no reliable automatic evaluator exists that scores interleaved outputs with fine-grained, interpretable metrics aligned to human judgment, hindering rapid benchmarking and model improvement.

## Input / Output

The SEIR pipeline consumes a question template (capturing a conversational linguistic form) paired with a topic from a 3,500-topic hierarchy, along with prior dialogue history H^(t-1), and produces a refined question, a comprehensive text answer with a temporary image caption, and a final generated image (via FLUX.1-dev). SynJudge takes a complete interleaved image-text response (text + one or more images) and outputs four scalar scores on a 1–5 scale: Text Content Completeness, Image Content Completeness, Image Quality, and Image-Text Synergy, where ITS specifically rewards complementary cross-modal alignment and penalizes redundancy.

## Method

The core contribution is SEIR (Self-Evaluation with Iterative Refinement), a fully automated Generate-Evaluate-Refine loop applied sequentially across three cascaded stages: Question Refinement (QR) refines an initial question against the target topic and dialogue history; Answer Refinement (AR) iteratively improves the text answer and its associated image caption for completeness and relevance; and Image Refinement (IR) generates an image from the caption, critiques it with a VLM against the full textual context, and refines the caption for the next generation cycle. Each stage uses a dedicated evaluator model (Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct for text, InternVL2.5-26B for vision) to produce specific improvement suggestions, and a refiner model to apply them, terminating after K=3 iterations or when no further suggestions are returned. For evaluation, SynJudge is a QwenVL2.5-7B model fine-tuned on 38,400 human-annotated interleaved QA pairs (scored by 10 experts with a mandatory consensus protocol) to predict the four quality dimensions, achieving 95.4% agreement within 1 point of human scores—substantially outperforming zero-shot MLLM judges like GPT-4o (86.5%). The resulting InterSyn dataset contains 1.8M single-turn and 50K multi-turn samples across 8 domains, and fine-tuning even 25K–50K samples yields substantial gains in image-text synergy across four tested generators.

## Results

SEIR-generated InterSyn samples achieve the highest scores across all four evaluation dimensions (TCC 4.39, ICC 4.49, IQ 4.44, ITS 4.53 by SynJudge), outperforming the strongest baseline GPT-4o+DALL-E (TCC 3.99, ICC 4.10, IQ 4.45, ITS 3.87) by margins of 0.34–0.66, with the largest gap in Image-Text Synergy. Fine-tuning four generators on just 25K–50K InterSyn samples yields substantial gains (e.g., Anole ITS improves from 2.26 to 2.40–2.74), and scaling to 200K continues to improve TCC, ICC, and especially ITS. The proposed SynJudge (QwenVL-trained) achieves 95.4% human agreement (A@1) and the lowest average RMSE (0.65), significantly outperforming zero-shot MLLM judges (86.5–87.5% A@1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEIR (InterSyn) decisively outperforms all 13 evaluated generators on every dimension. The strongest non-SEIR baselines are GPT-4o+DALL-E (TCC 3.99, ITS 3.87) and Gemini+Flux (TCC 3.97, ITS 3.84); SEIR exceeds them by 0.34–0.66 points, with the largest gap in ITS (0.66 over GPT-4o+DALL-E), confirming that even top closed-source pipelines struggle with image-text complementarity. Among native interleaved generators, BAGEL is the strongest (TCC 3.11, ICC 3.89, IQ 4.23, ITS 2.87), while VARGPT and VARGPT-v1.1 exhibit critically low ICC/IQ/ITS scores (below 1.3), indicating severe image generation failures. For the evaluator comparison, SynJudge (QwenVL-trained) achieves the lowest RMSE across all four dimensions (0.54–0.72) and the highest A@1 (95.4%), substantially outperforming zero-shot GPT-4o (RMSE 0.98–1.18, A@1 86.5%), QwenVL (A@1 87.5%), and InternVL (A@1 86.6%). Fine-tuning experiments in Table 1 confirm that InterSyn data improves all four tested generators (Anole, VILA-U, VARGPT-v1.1, BAGEL) monotonically from 25K to 200K samples, with the most dramatic relative gains in ITS (e.g., VARGPT-v1.1 ITS rises from 0.68 to 3.53).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of multimodal data construction and evaluation for interleaved image-text generation. While prior datasets (MMC4, OBELICS, CoMM, LeafInstruct) rely on web-crawled or filtered corpora that lack instruction-following structure and multi-turn dialogue, and prior evaluators (CLIPScore, IntJudge, InterleavedEval) focus on surface-level consistency or pairwise comparison, InterSyn introduces a fully automated Generate-Evaluate-Refine pipeline (SEIR) that embeds iterative self-checking across question, answer, and image stages. The SynJudge evaluator departs from prior metrics by introducing Image-Text Synergy (ITS), which rewards complementary cross-modal alignment while penalizing redundancy—a dimension absent from existing consistency-based metrics. Conceptually, the work repurposes LLM-as-judge and iterative refinement ideas from text-only data curation and applies them to the multimodal generation setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InterSyn (1.8M samples, 3,500 topics, multi-turn) addresses a critical data bottleneck for training unified multimodal models, and the finding that 25K–50K samples already yield substantial gains makes it accessible to resource-constrained labs. The SynJudge evaluator with its four-dimensional scoring (especially ITS) provides a standardized, human-aligned benchmark that the field currently lacks for fine-grained interleaved output assessment. Open questions include whether the entirely synthetic data distribution (generated by Qwen2.5-32B + FLUX) adequately covers the diversity of real human-visual communication, whether the 4K-question evaluation benchmark is sufficiently challenging as models improve, and whether the SEIR framework generalizes to additional modalities (video, audio) or to settings where the image generator is not a high-quality diffusion model. The ~90K H100-hour compute cost also raises questions about the method's accessibility at scale.
