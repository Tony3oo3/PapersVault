## Problem

When multiple compression techniques such as pruning and quantization are combined in a joint pipeline, the order in which they are applied significantly affects final model performance, yet this factor has been largely overlooked. Most prior work implicitly assumes orthogonality between methods, and the few studies that examine order do so only under highly restrictive assumptions (e.g., magnitude-based pruning with max-scaled quantization). No prior work provides a general theoretical or empirical characterization of when and why certain compression orders outperform others.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a pre-trained model φ, a set of compression methods F = {f₁, …, fₙ} each paired with a specific compression ratio C (e.g., pruning ratio p or target bit-width B_Q), and a performance metric M(·) (e.g., negative perplexity or classification accuracy). It outputs the optimal permutation π* over F that maximizes M(π(φ)), i.e., the sequence in which to sequentially apply the methods. Compression methods are applied one after another (not simultaneously), and the compression ratio is defined as the memory usage of the original model divided by that of the compressed model. The Compression Equivalent Ratio (CER) maps each method's effect onto a common quantization scale for cross-method intensity comparison.

## Method

The paper introduces the Progressive Intensity Hypothesis: weaker perturbations should be applied before stronger ones, formalized as the compression order advantage A(f₁→f₂) growing monotonically with the performance gap G(f₁,f₂) (or equivalently the CER difference). Theoretically, under Disjoint Selectivity (each structural unit is modified by at most one method regardless of order), Theorem 1 shows the order advantage equals β·(Σ_{G₂} g(uᵢ) − Σ_{G₁} g(uᵢ)), where G₁ and G₂ are the order-dependent unit groups and g(uᵢ) is the per-unit error difference between methods. Theorem 2 then proves monotonicity under well-designed pruning (minimal performance degradation) and well-designed quantization (symmetric zero-mean errors), showing A(Q→P) increases as C_P* − C_Q grows. For non-disjoint cases, the paper defines interference Δ as additional error from mutual interaction and argues it enters additively, preserving the monotonic trend. Empirically, the hypothesis is validated across LLaMA, Mistral, BERT, ResNet-18, and DeiT-Base models using diverse pruning (SparseGPT, Wanda, SLEB, PRACTISE, SAViT) and quantization (QuaRot, OPTQ, RTN, N2UQ, RepQ-ViT) methods, and extended to multi-stage compression, mixed-precision quantization, PEFT with LoRA, and parameter sharing.

## Results

The Progressive Intensity Hypothesis is validated across all tested models: the compression order advantage A(Q→P) increases monotonically with the CER difference C*_P − C_Q for LLaMA 2 7B/13B, LLaMA 3 8B, Mistral 7B, Mistral Nemo 12B, BERT, ResNet-18, and DeiT-Base. In Table 1, at 4-bit quantization (C_Q=4), A(Q→P) reaches −49.899 for SparseGPT (unstructured) and −9.379 for SLEB (structured), while at 9-bit (C_Q=1.78) both are near zero, confirming that interference scales with quantization aggressiveness. Rotation amplifies pruning degradation: at 40% pruning ratio, SparseGPT perplexity on LLaMA 3 8B jumps from 8.477 (no rotation) to 98.213 (with QuaRot rotation), a 10.7× increase (Table 4). The hypothesis further generalizes to multi-stage compression, PEFT with LoRA, parameter sharing, and mixed-precision quantization, where progressive bit allocation (8→2) consistently outperforms regressive (2→8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not propose a new compression method to beat baselines; instead, it uses 16 compression method pairs as experimental tools to validate the Progressive Intensity Hypothesis. Across all three pruning methods (SparseGPT, Wanda, SLEB) and four quantization methods (RTN, OPTQ, QuaRot, QuaRot+OPTQ) on LLaMA 3 8B (Figure 4), the monotonic relationship between A(Q→P) and CER difference holds uniformly, confirming the hypothesis is method-agnostic. For vision models, PRACTISE+N2UQ on ResNet-18 and SAViT+RepQ-ViT on DeiT-Base both exhibit the same monotonic trend (Figure 6), with compression order advantages substantially larger than in language models. Structured pruning (SLEB) shows zero interference in early quantization regimes (C_Q ≤ 2.68) while unstructured pruning (SparseGPT) exhibits monotonic interference that explodes at 4-bit (−49.899 vs. −0.002 at 9-bit), revealing that pruning granularity is the key determinant of interference magnitude. The hypothesis also holds when combined with LoRA (PEFT), Basis Sharing (parameter sharing), and HAWQ-V2-based mixed-precision quantization, demonstrating generality beyond the core pruning-quantization setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant assumption in joint model compression literature that different techniques (pruning, quantization, etc.) are orthogonal and thus order-agnostic. While a few prior works (Wang et al., 2020; Wu et al., 2023; Yu et al., 2023) offered limited empirical observations on ordering, and Harma et al. (2025) provided a narrow theoretical proof that pruning-then-quantization is always optimal under magnitude-based pruning and max-scaled quantization, no prior study systematically characterized compression order in general settings. The authors carve new ground by formalizing the problem (Problem 1), introducing the Progressive Intensity Hypothesis with a Compression Equivalent Ratio (CER) as a unified intensity scale, and proving monotonicity guarantees under disjoint selectivity—extending well beyond the restrictive assumptions of Harma et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Progressive Intensity Hypothesis provides practitioners with a simple, method-agnostic ordering rule (weaker perturbations first, stronger later) that yields a 'free lunch' in performance without additional computation, applicable across language models, vision models, multi-stage pipelines, PEFT, parameter sharing, and mixed-precision quantization. The practical finding that Hadamard rotation (now standard in 4-bit quantization via QuaRot) dramatically amplifies pruning degradation—by up to 89 perplexity points at 40% pruning—highlights an urgent need for rotation-aware pruning methods. Open questions include characterizing when the hypothesis breaks (e.g., severe compression collapse, full re-training, or increasing order-affected layers), developing explicit predictive rules for optimal ordering, and automating compression order selection in complex multi-method pipelines.
