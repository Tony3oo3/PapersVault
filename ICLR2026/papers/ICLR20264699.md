## Problem

Existing context compression methods (ICAE, 500xCompressor, EPL) rely on autoencoding pretraining to teach randomly initialized compression tokens to carry contextual semantics, but the reconstruction objective forces the model to encode all tokens equally, misaligning with downstream task needs and introducing gradient conflicts with language modeling. The paper asks whether a compression architecture can be designed that inherently understands context without any autoencoding phase.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264699/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder receives a long text context split into 510-token sub-contexts and produces compressed representations as key-value pairs across all transformer layers of selected anchor tokens (|S| = ⌊L/r⌋ per sub-context, where r is the compression ratio). These sub-compressed representations are concatenated to form the full compressed representation, which the frozen target LLM then uses in place of the original context to generate answers. Unlike methods that append k new special tokens to the sequence, SAC operates on the original sequence length, reducing inference FLOPs.

## Method

SAC selects representative tokens from the original context as anchor tokens (default: middle token from each uniform chunk) and augments them with a learnable anchor embedding vector to mark them as compression carriers. The encoder LLM (with LoRA adapters, rank=128) is modified to use bidirectional attention across all tokens instead of causal attention, enabling anchor tokens to integrate information from the entire context rather than only preceding tokens. The compressed representation is the KV pairs of anchor tokens across all layers, and because the encoder and decoder share the same base model, the decoder can directly interpret the compressed KV without additional semantic alignment. Training uses only the language modeling loss for pretraining (no autoencoding) followed by QA loss for finetuning, and the paper empirically shows that adding AE back degrades performance due to gradient orthogonality between AE and LM objectives.

## Results

At the primary 15× compression ratio on the MRQA benchmark, SAC achieves an average in-domain F1 of 54.95 and EM of 39.67, outperforming the strongest baseline EPL (51.52 F1 / 36.65 EM) by 3.43 F1 and 3.02 EM. On out-of-domain evaluation, SAC reaches 39.26 F1 / 26.02 EM versus EPL's 36.74 F1 / 23.83 EM. SAC maintains its lead across all compression ratios (5×, 15×, 51×), with in-domain F1 of 63.63, 54.95, and 46.37 respectively, and also outperforms EPL on long-context summarization (QMSum: 14.95 vs. 14.82; GovReport: 22.03 vs. 20.40 ROUGE-1 F1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAC consistently outperforms all six baselines across every in-domain and out-of-domain MRQA dataset at 15× compression. Against the strongest soft-compression baseline EPL, SAC gains +3.43 F1 / +3.02 EM in-domain and +2.52 F1 / +2.19 EM out-of-domain, with the gap widening at higher compression ratios (e.g., +3.15 F1 at 51×). Compared to ICAE, the improvement is largest: up to +23.5% F1 / +26.8% EM in-domain, reflecting ICAE's reliance on autoencoding pretraining. Against 500xCompressor and DAST, SAC leads by approximately +5.6 F1 and +5.1 F1 respectively, while the hard-compression method LLMLingua-2 (evaluated at 5×) trails SAC by over 22 F1 points, underscoring the advantage of learned soft compression. On long-context summarization (32K tokens), SAC surpasses EPL by +0.13 ROUGE-1 on QMSum and +1.63 on GovReport, confirming that removing the AE objective does not impair long-context modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAC builds directly on EPL's position-ID layout for compression tokens and 500xCompressor's use of per-layer KV pairs as the compressed representation, but departs fundamentally from the autoencoding (AE) pretraining paradigm that ICAE, 500xCompressor, and EPL all rely on to teach compression tokens to carry contextual semantics. Instead of appending randomly-initialized special tokens and training them via AE reconstruction, SAC selects semantically meaningful anchor tokens from the original context, augments them with learnable anchor embeddings, and applies bidirectional attention in the encoder to aggregate full-context information into their KV representations. This positions SAC at the intersection of token-selection methods (LLMLingua-2, SelectiveContext) and soft-compression training methods, combining the semantic grounding of the former with the trained representational capacity of the latter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that the AE objective is not merely unnecessary but actively harmful (gradient-orthogonal to LM, diluting task-relevant information) for context compression—challenges a core assumption in the field and simplifies the training pipeline, yielding ~31% faster pretraining with better downstream performance. Limitations include reliance on a fixed uniform anchor-selection strategy, evaluation primarily on Llama-family models up to 8B, and the bidirectional-attention encoder requiring architectural modification that may not transfer trivially to all LLM backbones. Open questions include whether adaptive or learned anchor selection could further improve coverage, whether the approach extends to multimodal or non-autoregressive architectures, and how performance scales at extreme compression ratios beyond 51×.
