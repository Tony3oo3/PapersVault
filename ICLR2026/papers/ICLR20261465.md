## Problem

Current zero-shot NCut-based segmentation pipelines rely on recursive bipartitions with repeated eigen-decompositions (prohibitively expensive at scale), introduce approximation error through spectral relaxation followed by rounding, and offer no principled convergence guarantee for the resulting K-way partition. The gap between the elegant discrete K-way NCut objective and the practical relax-and-round heuristics used in modern systems remains unaddressed.

## Input / Output

Falcon takes a single RGB image (resized to 1024×1024) and extracts token features at 32×32 (from diffusion models like SSD-1B or SD2.1) or 64×64 (from DINOv3-B), followed by ℓ₂ normalization and power-sharpened cosine affinities. It produces a class-agnostic K-way segmentation mask on the token grid, densified to a 128×128 intermediate grid via prototype-based reassignment, and optionally refined to full pixel resolution using PAMR, NAMR, or DenseCRF. Predictions are aligned to ground-truth labels via the Hungarian algorithm with many-to-one background mapping.

## Method

Falcon replaces recursive spectral bipartitioning with a proximal-gradient solver that directly optimizes the discrete K-way NCut objective on a global one-hot assignment matrix. Each iteration computes closed-form gradient scores (weighted by cluster volumes via the quotient rule) and performs row-wise one-hot proximal updates where an inertia term τₜ·X_ik^(t) discourages unnecessary label flips. A monotone Armijo-type backtracking line search adaptively tunes τₜ to guarantee non-decreasing normalized-association values, preserving discrete feasibility at every step. The paper proves convergence of the full sequence to a critical point under the Kurdyka-Lojasiewicz property, with at least linear local rates when the KL exponent θ ≤ 1/2. The number of segments K is estimated once via a spectral eigenvalue-counting heuristic, and the entire solver runs in O(TN²K) time versus O(N³) for dense eigensolvers, yielding up to an order-of-magnitude speedup over recursive NCut.

## Results

Using the SSD-1B encoder with PAMR refinement, Falcon achieves 78.40 mIoU on Pascal VOC, 61.80 on COCO-Object, 50.37 on COCO-Stuff-27, 33.69 on Cityscapes, 45.17 on ADE20K, and 57.15 on Pascal Context. These represent gains of +13.2, +27.7, +1.3, +3.1, and +0.9 mIoU respectively over the strongest published training-free baseline (DiffCut), with Falcon remaining competitive on Context (57.15 vs. 56.50). Falcon also runs up to ~9× faster than DiffCut in mask generation (e.g., 52.49s vs. 747.97s on DINOv3-B/Cityscapes) and achieves the best result on 17 of 18 benchmark-encoder pairs across three encoders (SSD-1B, DINOv3-B, SD2.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest published training-free baseline DiffCut, Falcon wins on 5 of 6 datasets with margins of +13.2 (VOC), +27.7 (COCO-Object), +1.3 (COCO-Stuff-27), +3.1 (Cityscapes), and +0.9 (ADE20K) mIoU, while remaining competitive on Pascal Context (57.15 vs. 56.50). Against the authors' own refined re-implementation of DiffCut†, Falcon still leads on VOC (+0.83), COCO-Stuff-27 (+0.98), Cityscapes (+2.92), and ADE20K (+0.77), though it trails slightly on Context (−1.02) and COCO-Object (−0.15). Compared to the refined AutoSC†, Falcon gains +7.97 on Cityscapes and +5.07 on ADE20K, with smaller margins elsewhere. Against earlier methods MaskCut and DiffSeg, Falcon leads by 10–30+ mIoU points across all six benchmarks, confirming that the gains stem from the proximal NCut solver rather than encoder or refiner choices. The paper also shows Falcon's advantage is encoder-agnostic: it outperforms DiffCut† across all three backbones (SSD-1B, DINOv3-B, SD2.1) and all three refinement options (none, PAMR, NAMR).

## Relation to Prior Work

Falcon builds directly on the normalized cut (NCut) objective of Shi & Malik (2000) and the training-free zero-shot pipeline established by TokenCut, MaskCut, and DiffCut, which extract token features from pretrained Vision Transformers and apply recursive spectral bipartitioning. The key departure is replacing the relax-and-round spectral pipeline with a proximal-gradient solver that operates directly on the discrete K-way one-hot assignment, drawing on the KL convergence framework of Jia et al. (2023) for nonconvex proximal methods. Rather than introducing a new feature extractor or graph construction, Falcon repositions NCut as a discrete optimization problem amenable to first-order methods, preserving the same affinity graph and foundation-model features used by prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Falcon provides the first principled convergence guarantee (linear rate under KL exponent ≤ 1/2) for a solver that directly optimizes the discrete K-way NCut objective, filling a theoretical gap that recursive spectral methods leave open. The up-to-10× speedup over recursive NCut is well-evidenced across six benchmarks and three encoders, making high-resolution token grids (e.g., 64×64 from DINOv3) practically tractable where prior methods become prohibitive. Open questions include whether the spectral heuristic for K selection can be replaced by a data-driven criterion, whether the KL exponent condition is always satisfied in practice for real-world affinity matrices, and whether the proximal framework extends naturally to other structured grouping problems (e.g., panoptic or instance segmentation) beyond class-agnostic mask generation.
