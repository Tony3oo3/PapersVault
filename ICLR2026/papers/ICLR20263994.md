## Problem

Sliced Mutual Information (SMI) is widely adopted as a scalable alternative to mutual information for measuring statistical dependence, yet its fundamental pathologies—premature saturation, a bias toward information redundancy over information content, and asymptotic decay to zero in high dimensions—have been largely overlooked. Prior work attributed SMI's limitations solely to suboptimality of random projections, without recognizing that even optimal individual projections fail collectively, and that the measure can behave inversely to MI under simple linear transformations.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on pairs of random vectors (X, Y) with varying data dimensionality d and projection rank k, drawn from four synthetic distributions (correlated normal, correlated uniform, smoothed uniform, log-gamma-exponential) with known ground-truth MI, high-dimensional synthetic images (16×16 and 32×32), and MNIST images coupled via synthetic copulas. MI between each pair of projections is estimated via the KSG estimator (k_NN=1) using 10⁴ samples and 128 random projections per configuration, with 10 independent runs for variance estimation. Outputs include SMI/MI scalar trajectories, feature extraction matrices assessed via effective rank, 2D embedding visualizations from representation learning, and ROC-AUC scores for independence testing pooled across dimensions d ∈ {2, 10, 20, 30}.

## Method

The core contribution is a theoretical analysis establishing that SMI's failures are structural rather than artifacts of projection choice. Lemma 4.1 derives a closed-form SMI expression for jointly Gaussian vectors via the generalized hypergeometric function, proving that SMI saturates at a bound of O(1/d) independent of MI and decays to zero as d→∞ while MI diverges. Proposition 4.2 extends this to k-SMI through the Jacobi ensemble, and Proposition 4.4 with Corollary 4.5 construct an explicit counterexample where a linear transform increases SMI while decreasing MI, refuting the hypothesis that SMI favors linearly extractable information. Empirically, the authors validate saturation and 1/d decay across all four synthetic distributions and high-dimensional images, demonstrate that replacing MI with SMI in Deep InfoMax causes immediate representation collapse on MNIST (vs. well-structured embeddings from MI maximization), show SMI prefers ill-conditioned Gaussian channel matrices (high κ(A)) contrary to MI's preference for decorrelated features, and replicate the original SMI independence-testing and feature-extraction experiments, revealing that SMI's discriminative power drops sharply when a single threshold must operate across varying dimensions.

## Results

For jointly Gaussian d-dimensional vectors with correlation ρ, MI grows as −(d/2)log(1−ρ²) → ∞ as d→∞, while SMI decays to zero and is bounded above by 1/(d−1) even when ρ²→1 (deterministic relationship). On MNIST, SMI saturates at ~5.77×10⁻³ nats for I(X₁;X₂)=2.3 nats, barely distinguishing it from the independent case (2.89×10⁻³ nats). In independence testing pooled across dimensions d∈{2,10,20,30}, SMI's ROC-AUC drops sharply below MI's, and in representation learning on MNIST, SMI maximization produces collapsed embeddings within 10 epochs while MI maximization yields well-separated clusters at 2000 epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is SMI versus MI: SMI fails to track MI's monotonic growth with dimensionality or correlation strength, saturating at a dimension-dependent ceiling (≤1/(d−1)) while MI diverges. Against the correlation coefficient, the paper constructs an explicit Gaussian example (Corollary 4.5) where introducing linear structure increases both MI and linear correlation but *decreases* SMI, showing SMI can be less faithful than a simple Pearson correlation. Compared to max-Sliced MI (mSMI) and optimal-Sliced MI (oSMI), the paper shows via Proposition 4.7 that even the optimal linear projection captures only the top-k singular values of the cross-covariance, missing dependencies spread evenly across components. Against copulas (which fully characterize dependence structure), SMI is positioned as a lossy scalar summary that discards most of the copula's information. In the independence testing replication, SMI underperforms MI when a single threshold must work across varying dimensions, whereas MI's ROC-AUC remains high, confirming that SMI's dimensional decay makes it unreliable for practical independence testing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SMI framework introduced by Goldfeld and Greenewald (2021) and extended to k-SMI by Goldfeld et al. (2022), using the KSG estimator (Kraskov et al., 2004) as the backbone MI estimator and the MUTINFO benchmark suite (Butakov et al.) for synthetic experiments. It departs from prior critical analyses (Tsur et al., 2023; Fayad and Ibrahim, 2023) that identified suboptimality of random projections, by demonstrating that the core issues—saturation, redundancy bias, and dimensional decay—cannot be explained by projection suboptimality alone. The paper also engages with the max-sliced MI and optimal-sliced MI variants, showing they inherit the same fundamental limitations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SMI has been widely adopted for DNN interpretability, feature selection, independence testing, and differential privacy auditing, so demonstrating its fundamental biases has broad practical implications. The paper leaves open the question of whether non-uniform or learned slicing strategies can recover MI-like behavior without sacrificing SMI's computational advantages, and whether the redundancy bias can be mitigated in practice. A key limitation is that closed-form results are restricted to Gaussian settings, though empirical validation across multiple non-Gaussian distributions and high-dimensional images provides strong supporting evidence.
