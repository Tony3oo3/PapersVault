## Problem

Existing multimodal dataset distillation methods (MTT-VL, LoRS) enforce synchronous trajectory matching, pairing image and text expert parameters from the same training step, yet the paper demonstrates that visual and text encoders evolve at fundamentally different rates due to architectural heterogeneity and distinct optimization-space topologies. Additionally, the absence of discrete class labels in image-text data means random initialization of synthetic samples fails to cover the vast, continuous semantic space, producing redundant and unrepresentative starting points.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a large-scale image-text dataset (e.g., Flickr30k: 31,783 images × 5 captions; COCO: 123,287 images × 5 captions) along with 20 pre-computed expert trajectories of NFNet (image) and BERT (text) encoder parameters saved every epoch over 10 training rounds. It produces a budget-constrained synthetic set of M image-text pairs (M ∈ {99, 199, 499}), where each image is a 3×224×224 pixel tensor and each text is a 768-dimensional embedding vector, optimized via SGD with momentum 0.5 over thousands of distillation steps on a single GPU.

## Method

AMD decouples the starting points of image and text expert trajectories (t_v ≠ t_l), allowing the student to match against cross-modal parameter combinations from different training epochs. A Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD)-based dynamic sampling rule computes per-epoch parameter update magnitudes for each modality, derives a median trajectory ratio, and sets asymmetric sampling ranges R_V and R_L that truncate text sampling before its convergence plateau while extending visual sampling beyond it. The distillation loss (Eq. 6) minimizes the sum of normalized L2 distances between student and expert parameters in both modalities, with each modality's starting point sampled independently within its range. A Semantics-Aware Prototype Mining (SPM) module replaces random initialization by concatenating visual and text features into joint representations, running K-means with K equal to the synthetic budget B, and selecting the nearest original image-text pair to each centroid as the initialization prototype, thereby ensuring diverse semantic coverage from the outset.

## Results

On Flickr30k with 200 image-text pairs (0.7% of the full dataset), AMD achieves IR@1=13.1, IR@5=34.9, IR@10=47.5 for Image-to-Text retrieval and TR@1=16.9, TR@5=42.3, TR@10=56.2 for Text-to-Image retrieval, compared to the full-dataset upper bound of IR@1=27.3, IR@5=57.1, IR@10=69.7. On the larger COCO dataset (123K images) with 200 pairs, AMD reaches IR@1=3.8, IR@5=13.4, IR@10=21.4 and TR@1=4.6, TR@5=15.5, TR@10=24.1. Gains over the strongest baseline (LoRS) grow with budget: on Flickr30k IR@1 improves by 2.1%, 4.5%, and 5.8% at 99, 199, and 499 pairs respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMD outperforms all six baselines across every metric on both Flickr30k and COCO. Against the four coreset selection methods (Random, Herding, K-Center, Forgetting), AMD achieves roughly 10–15× higher IR@1 (e.g., 13.1 vs. 1.1–1.5 at 200 pairs on Flickr30k), confirming that trajectory-based distillation is far more effective than static subset selection for multimodal data. Against MTT-VL (Wu et al., 2023), the first multimodal trajectory-matching method, AMD improves IR@1 from 4.6 to 13.1 (+8.5 absolute) and IR@10 from 25.5 to 47.5 (+22.0 absolute) at 200 pairs, demonstrating that decoupling image and text trajectory sampling is critical. Against LoRS (Xu et al., 2024), the strongest prior method, AMD gains +4.5% IR@1, +9.6% IR@5, and +10.9% IR@10 on Flickr30k 200 pairs, with the gap widening to +5.8% IR@1 at 499 pairs, indicating that synchronous matching becomes increasingly suboptimal at larger budgets. On COCO, AMD similarly leads LoRS by +1.4% IR@1 and +5.9% IR@10 at 200 pairs, confirming generalization to a 3.9× larger dataset with richer semantics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MTT-based multimodal dataset distillation line initiated by MTT-VL (Wu et al., 2023) and refined by LoRS (Xu et al., 2024), which both enforce synchronous trajectory matching (t_v = t_l) for image and text modalities. AMD departs from this by decoupling the starting points of image and text expert trajectories, motivated by empirical evidence that the two modalities exhibit fundamentally different convergence dynamics. It additionally introduces a K-means-based prototype mining module for initialization, addressing the absence of discrete class labels that traditional dataset distillation methods rely on for coverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, low-overhead improvement for efficient VLM training via compressed synthetic datasets, which is increasingly relevant as multimodal data volumes grow. However, the evaluation is limited to retrieval benchmarks (Flickr30k, COCO) with NFNet+BERT as the primary backbone, and the 'asynchronous' insight, while well-motivated, essentially repackages the well-known observation that different modalities converge at different rates into a specific sampling strategy. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to generative VLMs, other multimodal tasks (e.g., VQA, grounding), and whether the MMD-based dynamic range selection remains optimal under different training schedules or larger model families.
