## Problem

Transformer architectures are largely heuristic and engineered bottom-up, with individual layer roles remaining poorly understood despite empirical observations of redundancy and convergent functionality across depth. The paper asks whether a principled, top-down design prior can induce a Transformer that is interpretable by construction rather than requiring post-hoc mechanistic analysis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261235/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For image classification, RGB images are patchified (8×8 patches for CIFAR at 32×32, 16×16 patches for ImageNet at 224×224) and processed as token sequences with a learnable [CLS] token, producing a class label. For Sudoku, a 9×9 grid with 17–34 known digits is encoded as a token sequence and the model predicts the remaining digits. For masked image modeling, VQ-VAE tokenized images (16×16 latent codes from a 1024-entry codebook) are partially masked and the model reconstructs the full token sequence. Across all tasks, the model is a single-layer recurrent-depth architecture where one set of parameters is reused across L forward iterations with learned per-iteration step sizes.

## Method

HYPER-SET derives Transformer components from iterative minimization of two complementary Hopfield-style energy functions on the hypersphere: a repulsive attention energy E_ATTN that enforces distributional uniformity of token projections in H low-dimensional subspaces, and an attractive feedforward energy E_FF that promotes alignment with learned semantic directions in the full high-dimensional space. The spherical constraints (‖W_h^T x_i‖ = √p and ‖D^T x_i‖ = √M) naturally induce RMSNorm, while the gradient flow of E_ATTN yields a doubly symmetric multi-head attention (both row-wise and column-wise softmax), and the gradient of E_FF yields a symmetric ReLU feedforward layer with skip connection. The entire model uses a single set of shared parameters across all L iterations (recurrent depth), with adaptive step sizes α_t and γ_t learned via a small time-embedding network conditioned on the iteration index and initial input. This energy-minimization perspective also generalizes to novel variants—linear attention, gated feedforward, and depth-wise LoRA for iteration-specific modulation—by simply altering the energy functions or adding low-rank perturbations to the shared weights.

## Results

On image classification with single-layer recurrent-depth models, HYPER-SET (d=512, 2.39M params) achieves 90.11% on CIFAR-10, 63.41% on CIFAR-100, and 70.16% on ImageNet-100, matching or exceeding the vanilla Transformer (2.38M params) at the same parameter budget. On the full ImageNet-1K, HYPER-SET reaches 62.76% (d=512) and 66.21% (d=640, 3.40M params), slightly trailing the Transformer's 66.94%. On Sudoku solving, HYPER-SET achieves 54.70% in-distribution accuracy versus 49.30% for Transformer, while both CRATE and Energy Transformer fail to produce non-trivial predictions. For masked image modeling on ImageNet-100, HYPER-SET with 24 iterations and 8d feedforward (8.07M params) nearly matches Transformer (8.85M params) on PSNR (15.955 vs 15.953) and SSIM (0.417 vs 0.417).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the vanilla Transformer at matched parameter count (~2.39M), HYPER-SET wins on CIFAR-10 (90.11 vs 89.90), CIFAR-100 (63.41 vs 61.89), and ImageNet-100 (70.16 vs 69.44), but trails on ImageNet-1K (62.76 vs 66.94); scaling width to d=640 narrows the ImageNet-1K gap to 0.73 points. Against CRATE (Yu et al., 2023) and its variant CRATE-T (Hu et al., 2024), HYPER-SET outperforms on all four image classification datasets with fewer parameters (2.39M vs 3.00–3.04M), with the largest margins on ImageNet-1K (62.76 vs 57.00/57.89). Energy Transformer (Hoover et al., 2024) is substantially outperformed across all datasets, with HYPER-SET gaining over 13 points on CIFAR-10 and over 28 points on ImageNet-1K. On Sudoku, HYPER-SET achieves 54.70% accuracy while both CRATE and Energy Transformer produce flat training curves (zero non-trivial predictions), and it also outperforms Transformer (49.30%) and extrapolates better at test time with increased iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

HYPER-SET builds on the Modern Continuous Hopfield (MCH) energy framework (Krotov & Hopfield, 2021; Ramsauer et al., 2021) and the alternating minimization decomposition from CRATE (Yu et al., 2023), but departs from both by grounding the design in a top-down maximum likelihood estimation on the hypersphere rather than in sparse rate reduction or mechanistic analogy. Unlike Energy Transformer (Hoover et al., 2024), which reinterprets existing attention as Hopfield energy descent post-hoc, this paper derives symmetric attention, feedforward, RMSNorm, and skip connections as necessary consequences of minimizing two complementary energy functions (repulsive uniformity + attractive alignment). The depth-wise LoRA for scalability is adapted from Bae et al. (2025), and the recurrent-depth paradigm follows the universal/looped Transformer line (Giannou et al., 2023; Schwarzschild et al., 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a rare top-down derivation where core Transformer components (symmetric attention, ReLU feedforward, RMSNorm, residual connection) emerge as necessary steps in constrained energy minimization, providing a design principle rather than a post-hoc explanation. This could guide principled architecture search and explain why certain inductive biases (e.g., symmetric attention, normalization) are effective. However, the model lags behind vanilla Transformer on large-scale ImageNet-1K (62.76 vs. 66.94 at matched width), and its recurrent-depth advantage diminishes beyond two distinct layers, raising open questions about whether the energy-based inductive bias becomes a scaling bottleneck. The generality of the framework (yielding linear attention, gated FF variants) is promising but underexplored at scale.
