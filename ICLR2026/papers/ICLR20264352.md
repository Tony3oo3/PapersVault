## Problem

Existing dynamic data pruning methods (e.g., InfoBatch) introduce biased gradient estimation and training instability, particularly at high pruning ratios, yet the theoretical analysis of this bias and its impact on final performance remains ambiguous. The paper asks: what mechanism ensures 'near-lossless' pruning, how should the induced gradient bias be formally characterized, and can pruning be pushed to more extreme regimes with guaranteed performance?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264352/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard labeled image dataset (32×32 CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 ImageNet-1K) and a pretrained or from-scratch model architecture (ResNet-18/50, Swin-Tiny, ViT-Base/MAE). At each training iteration, it draws a candidate batch of size s from the full dataset, ranks by instantaneous loss, and uses only the top-q samples for the parameter update, yielding an exact prune ratio of 1−(q/s)·(s/|D|) with no auxiliary weight tables or annealing schedules. The output is a trained classifier whose test accuracy matches full-data training at moderate pruning ratios while reducing wall-clock cost by 40–45%.

## Method

OrderDP integrates a two-stage sampler into the SGD loop: (i) uniformly sample a candidate pool of size s, then (ii) select the top-q samples by instantaneous loss to compute a subgradient and update parameters, refreshing scores only for retained samples. The key theoretical insight is defining a surrogate loss L_q(θ) = (1/q)Σ γ_j L_(j)(θ) with closed-form combinatorial weights γ_j derived from order statistics, which makes the OrderDP gradient estimator provably unbiased (Theorem 1) and yields standard O(1/√T) convergence under convexity and Lipschitz assumptions (Theorem 3). A generalization bound (Theorem 4) decomposes the gap between L_q and the true expected risk into a vanishing optimization term and a bounded pruning bias controlled by the spectral-risk quantities C_s and Q_n, which shrink as q→s. Unlike InfoBatch's mean-threshold rescaling, OrderDP requires no late-stage full-data annealing, supports arbitrary pruning ratios via (s,q), and reduces per-sample sorting to O(log q) time with constant memory.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), OrderDP achieves 95.6% / 95.3% / 95.0% top-1 accuracy at 30% / 50% / 70% pruning, matching or exceeding the full-dataset baseline of 95.6% at 30% and losing only 0.6 points at 70%. On CIFAR-100 (ResNet-18), it reaches 78.2% / 77.9% / 76.7% at the same ratios versus a 78.2% full-data baseline. On ImageNet-1K (ResNet-50), OrderDP retains the full 76.4% accuracy at 40% pruning and drops only 0.4 points at 60%, while reducing total GPU-hours from 70.4 (full data) to 43.0 (~39% savings). Across all settings, OrderDP also achieves the fastest wall-clock training time among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OrderDP consistently outperforms all 13 baselines across every pruning ratio and dataset. Against the strongest dynamic baseline InfoBatch, OrderDP gains +0.5 points on CIFAR-10 (95.0 vs 94.5) and +0.8 points on CIFAR-100 (76.7 vs 75.9) at 70% pruning with ResNet-18, and +1.5 points on CIFAR-100 (77.9 vs 76.4) at 70% with ResNet-50. On ImageNet-1K at 40% pruning, OrderDP matches the full-data accuracy of 76.4% while InfoBatch drops to 75.6% (−0.8), UCB to 75.4%, and ε-greedy to 75.2%. Among the 15 static pruning methods, the best performer (EL2N-20) reaches only 91.9% on CIFAR-10 at 70% pruning, whereas OrderDP achieves 95.0%—a 3.1-point gap. OrderDP also trains faster than all baselines: on ImageNet-1K it completes in 21.5 h vs. 21.6 h for InfoBatch and 35.2 h for full-data training, and it supports arbitrary pruning ratios (e.g., 99%) where InfoBatch is limited to ~78% due to its fixed retention mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

OrderDP builds directly on the ordered SGD framework of Kawaguchi & Lu (2020), which introduced stochastic optimization over order statistics, and on the spectral risk measure analysis of Mehta et al. (2023) for bounding distributional gaps. It departs from the closest prior dynamic pruning method, InfoBatch (Qin et al., 2024), which achieves unbiasedness by rescaling gradients toward the expected loss; instead, OrderDP defines a new surrogate loss L_q with closed-form combinatorial weights γ_j derived from a two-stage sampler (uniform exploration of size s, then top-q exploitation), and proves the gradient is unbiased w.r.t. this surrogate. This reframing shifts the analysis from correcting bias in the original loss to bounding the gap between the surrogate and the original loss, yielding a cleaner and more controllable theoretical picture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first convergence and generalization guarantees specifically for a dynamic data pruning algorithm, filling a gap where prior methods (InfoBatch, UCB, ε-greedy) operated without formal analysis of their bias or stability. The practical impact is substantial: 40–45% training cost reduction with near-lossless accuracy across CIFAR and ImageNet, and the plug-and-play design transfers across CNNs, ViTs, and multiple optimizers without architectural changes. Open questions include whether the convex-Lipschitz assumptions in the theory translate meaningfully to non-convex deep network training, how the surrogate-loss gap (captured by C_s and Q_n) behaves in practice at extreme pruning ratios, and whether the loss-based scoring can be replaced by richer informativeness signals without breaking the unbiasedness guarantee.
