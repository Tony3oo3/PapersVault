## Problem

Reinforcement learning for multi-turn, tool-using LLMs suffers from a severe credit-assignment bottleneck: a single terminal reward after a long sequence of reasoning and retrieval calls provides almost no signal about which intermediate turns were informative versus redundant or misleading. This sparsity leads to high-variance policy updates, training collapse, and drift on long-horizon tasks, and existing densification approaches (process reward models, rule-based turn rewards, LLM judges) either require expensive offline training, lack discriminative power across multiple tool calls, or introduce noisy heuristic signals.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural-language question and iteratively interleaves free-form reasoning with calls to an E5-based dense retriever over the 2018 Wikipedia dump (top-3 passages per turn, up to 4–5 turns, 4096-token context). The final response must embed the answer inside <answer> tags; evaluation extracts this tagged span and scores it by Exact Match or F1 against a small set of gold answers, making the task closed-form rather than open-ended generation.

## Method

TIPS assigns each reasoning–tool-call–observation turn a dense reward equal to the change in a frozen (or periodically refreshed) copy of the policy's log-likelihood of generating any correct answer, i.e. Δ_k = α[Φ(S_k) − Φ(S_{k−1})] where Φ(S) = log Σ_m p_teacher(A^(m)|S). This turn-level information gain is formally a potential-based reward shaping term over a segment-level MDP, so under undiscounted episodic returns it shifts all within-turn returns by a state-dependent constant and provably preserves the set of optimal policies. The shaped rewards are injected at turn boundaries and optimized with standard PPO (GAE, λ=1, γ=1) plus a KL penalty; no separate reward model, verifier, or human process labels are required. The teacher is refreshed every N steps (default 200) to keep its potential aligned with the evolving policy, and the shaping scale α is tuned so that E[|αΔ_k|] ≈ 0.2, keeping the information signal well below the terminal reward.

## Results

On Qwen-2.5-7B Instruct, TIPS achieves an average Exact Match of 41.71 and F1 of 51.24 across seven QA benchmarks, improving over PPO (37.28 EM / 45.07 F1) by +11.8% EM and +13.6% F1. The largest gains appear on multi-hop out-of-domain tasks: 2WikiMultiHopQA jumps from 32.94 (PPO) to 42.96 EM, and MuSiQue from 8.94 to 17.05 EM. On Qwen-2.5-3B Instruct, TIPS reaches 33.60 avg EM vs. 30.15 for PPO (+11.4%). TIPS also generalizes across model families, with relative EM gains ranging from +7.3% (Qwen3-4B) to +34.0% (Llama3.1-8B) over outcome-only PPO, at a consistent ~12% FLOPs overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIPS outperforms all four baselines (PPO, GRPO, MT-GRPO*, MT-PPO) on average EM and F1 for both 7B and 3B models. Against PPO (the strongest outcome-only baseline), TIPS gains +4.43 EM on 7B (41.71 vs. 37.28) and +3.45 EM on 3B (33.60 vs. 30.15), with the largest per-task margins on multi-hop benchmarks (2Wiki: +10.02 EM; MuSiQue: +8.11 EM on 7B). Against GRPO, the gap is even wider: TIPS leads by +13.17 EM on 7B (41.71 vs. 28.54), and GRPO suffers a training collapse around step 320–350 that TIPS avoids. The multi-turn rule-based baselines (MT-GRPO*, MT-PPO) improve over their single-turn counterparts but still trail TIPS by 11+ EM on 7B, and the answer-aware reward variant of MT-PPO frequently collapsed on 7B models. On the BrowseComp-Plus deep-research benchmark, TIPS-trained Qwen3-4B (9.40%) surpasses a 32B SEARCH-R1 PPO agent (4.11%), further confirming the advantage of information-potential shaping over outcome-only or rule-based turn rewards.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIPS sits at the intersection of classical potential-based reward shaping (Ng et al., 1999) and modern RL fine-tuning of LLMs (PPO, GRPO). It departs from process reward models and LLM-as-judge approaches that require external verifiers or human step labels by using the policy's own frozen copy as a teacher to compute turn-level information-gain rewards, and from rule-based multi-turn baselines (MT-GRPO, MT-PPO) by replacing hand-crafted heuristics with a model-based likelihood signal. The implementation builds on the VeRL framework for multi-turn RL training, and the theoretical analysis recasts the turn-level reward as a standard PBRS potential difference over a segment-level MDP, yielding a policy-invariance guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TIPS offers a lightweight, theoretically grounded mechanism for stabilizing long-horizon RL on tool-using LLMs without requiring a separate reward model, process labels, or an external judge—only a frozen copy of the policy itself. The consistent gains across five model families (Qwen 2.5/3, Llama 3.1) at a modest ~12% FLOPs overhead make it a practical drop-in for existing PPO pipelines. Key limitations include its current coupling to PPO (not yet shown for GRPO or off-policy methods), the modest but real compute overhead of teacher scoring, and evaluation restricted to QA; open questions include whether the approach transfers to reasoning-heavy domains like programming and math, and whether the policy can serve as its own teacher without periodic re-syncing.
