## Problem

Pretrained diffusion models provide access only to denoising transition kernels, yet many downstream tasks—annealing, reward-tilting, model composition, and energy-based training—require the marginal densities along the generation trajectory. Existing approaches (Feynman-Kac correctors, Itô density estimators, twisted samplers) each derive task-specific weight formulas with restrictive design choices, leaving no unified, plug-and-play mechanism to compute these densities or the corresponding SMC importance weights.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained diffusion model's score network (or two for product/CFG) plus a target specification (temperature β for annealing, a reward function r for tilting, or exponents α,β for model product) and produces resampled particles following the target distribution at t=0. For energy-based training, it takes standard DSM training samples and outputs a learned scalar energy function. All SMC weight computation is incremental—only Δlog w per denoising step is needed—so the overhead beyond the base diffusion sampler is negligible. The framework is modality-agnostic: the same RNE identity applies to continuous SDEs, stochastic interpolants, and continuous-time Markov chains (e.g., masked discrete diffusion on ImageNet-256).

## Method

The core identity (RNE) exploits the fact that the Radon-Nikodym derivative between any SDE and its time-reversal is identically 1, yielding p_τ(Y_τ)/p_τ'(Y_τ') = R_μ^ν(Y_[τ,τ']), where R is a product of forward and backward Gaussian transition kernels (Eq. 10). For inference-time control, this becomes the Radon-Nikodym Corrector (RNC): SMC importance weights are expressed as [R_μ^ν]^β · [R_b^a]^{-1} (anneal) or analogous forms for reward-tilting and product, where R_b^a involves freely choosable sampling and target drifts a_t, b_t. This 'macro' structure means changing the task or heuristic process pair requires no re-derivation—only swapping kernel evaluations. A Gaussian reference process with linear drift stabilises the discretisation by aligning kernel variances, and a non-asymptotic O(√Δt) convergence bound is proven. For energy-based training, RNE adds a stop-gradient regulariser enforcing the density identity between adjacent timesteps, which is shown equivalent to Fokker-Planck regularisation but avoids computing score divergences.

## Results

On inference-time annealing of alanine dipeptide (ALDP), RNC with (c_a=0.6, c_b=0.4) achieves an Energy TV of 0.034, dramatically outperforming FKC (0.338) and the uncorrected anneal-score baseline (0.794). For multi-target structure-based drug design, RNC (c_a=1, c_b=0.2) generates ligands with 64.9% better-than-known docking scores versus 60.8% for FKC and 34.5% for score summation. In trajectory stitching for maze navigation, RNC achieves 100% success rate across all 5 tasks, compared to 28.8%–71.4% without SMC. For energy-based training, RNE regularisation reduces the MCMC sample W2 on ALDP from 0.9472 (DSM alone) to 0.1836, and improves solvation free energy estimation from 27.30±0.45 to 29.28±0.04 (reference: 29.43±0.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RNC is compared against FKC (Skreta et al., 2025) across all inference-time control tasks. When RNC is configured with (c_a=1, c_b=0), it is theoretically equivalent to FKC and achieves comparable performance (e.g., Energy TV 0.386 vs 0.338 on ALDP annealing; 0.589 vs 0.608 better-than-known on SBDD). However, by tuning (c_a, c_b), RNC substantially outperforms FKC: Energy TV drops to 0.034 (10× improvement) on ALDP annealing, and better-than-known docking improves to 0.649 on multi-target SBDD. Against the uncorrected baselines (anneal-score without SMC, sum-score without SMC), RNC provides dramatic gains—Energy TV improves from 0.794 to 0.034, and trajectory stitching success rate jumps from 28.8%–71.4% to 100%. For energy-based training, RNE regularisation is compared against standard DSM (Vincent, 2011) and dual score matching (Guth et al., 2025): RNE regularisation reduces MCMC W2 from 0.9472 to 0.1836 on ALDP, matching or exceeding dual SM in energy accuracy on 100D GMM. For free energy estimation, RNE-regularised thermodynamic integration (29.28±0.04) substantially outperforms the unregularised baseline from Máté et al. (2025) (27.30±0.45), approaching the reference value of 29.43±0.01.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies a family of previously disparate methods—Feynman-Kac correctors (Skreta et al., 2025), twisted diffusion samplers (Wu et al., 2023), Itô/Feynman-Kac density estimators (Karczewski et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2021), and Fokker-Planck regularisers (Plainer et al., 2025)—under a single Radon-Nikodym derivative identity between time-reversal SDEs. The key conceptual move is observing that the path-measure density ratio between a diffusion process and its time-reversal is identically one, which directly links marginal densities to transition kernels without requiring divergence computation or PDE solving. This positions RNE as a strict generalisation of FKC (shown as FKC ⊆ RNC via Proposition C.3) while restoring the freedom to choose sampling and target processes heuristically, a flexibility that FKC's PDE-based derivation sacrifices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of RNC means practitioners can apply inference-time control (annealing, reward-tilting, model product, trajectory stitching) without re-deriving weight formulas for each task, lowering the barrier to principled control over pretrained diffusion models. The RNE regularisation for energy-based diffusion training is a simple drop-in addition to DSM that dramatically improves learned energy accuracy (e.g., MCMC W2 from 0.95 to 0.18 on ALDP), with implications for free-energy estimation and ML force fields. Open questions include: how well SMC-based RNC scales when the target is far from the pretrained model (where particle degeneracy becomes severe), whether the optimal (c_a, c_b) process-design hyperparameters can be learned rather than tuned, and whether the perfect-model assumption for annealing/composition can be relaxed without losing exactness.
