## Problem

Diffusion policies for offline learning accumulate score-matching errors, discretization errors, and numerical integration errors across iterative denoising steps, leading to inconsistent action generation and drift away from the dataset support in continuous control. While these inaccuracies are tolerable in image generation, they compound and cause failure in robotics. Contraction theory, which provably bounds error growth in ODE flows, had not been applied to the reverse diffusion ODE sampling process for conditional action policies.

## Input / Output

The policy receives a state observation—either a low-dimensional proprioceptive vector (e.g., end-effector pose and gripper state) or a 224×224 RGB agentview image—and produces a continuous action vector (e.g., a 7-dimensional end-effector delta pose with gripper command for the Franka arm). The state conditions the reverse diffusion ODE at every denoising step, and actions are generated by integrating the ODE from a random noise sample to a clean action over 10–30 solver steps using DPM-Solver++ 2M.

## Method

CDP derives a theoretical link (Theorem 3.1) showing that the reverse diffusion ODE is contractive if and only if the largest eigenvalue of the symmetric score Jacobian J_{ε_θ}^{sym} is bounded above by −f(t)/h(t), where f and h are determined by the forward diffusion schedule. Practically, this is enforced by adding a contraction loss that penalizes the leading eigenvalue of J_{ε_θ}^{sym}, estimated efficiently via 3–4 steps of power iteration (a single Jacobian-vector product per step) rather than full eigendecomposition. The total training objective combines the standard diffusion score-matching loss with this contraction penalty weighted by a single hyperparameter γ, and is integrated into EDP (offline RL) or DBC (IL) with no architectural changes. The drift term f(t)I is inherently contractive under variance-preserving schedules, so the loss only needs to prevent the score term from locally expanding beyond the drift's contractive margin, preserving multimodality while damping solver and score-matching errors.

## Results

On D4RL offline RL benchmarks, CDP achieves an average normalized episode return of 65.7±2.2, outperforming EDP (61.2±2.6), IDQL (60.3±2.3), DQL (58.8±3.6), and IQL (54.9±4.5). Gains are most pronounced in data-scarce settings: on 10% of the dataset, CDP decisively outperforms all baselines. On Robomimic IL tasks, CDP-Unet reaches 90.0% average success rate vs. 88.0% for DP-Unet and 64.0% for DBC-DiT, with the largest margin on the Transport task (81% vs. 74% for DP-Unet in low-dim).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDP is compared against five baselines: BC (behavior cloning), IQL, DQL, EDP, and IDQL for offline RL, and DP (Diffusion Policy) and DBC for imitation learning. In offline RL, CDP wins on 9 of 14 D4RL environments, with the largest margins over EDP on Hopper Medium-Replay (+8.4), Walker2D Medium-Replay (+7.7), and Franka Kitchen Mixed (+18.1). CDP matches or slightly trails EDP on near-saturated Medium-Expert tasks (e.g., Walker2D: 109.4 vs. 109.8), suggesting contraction provides diminishing returns when the score is already well-estimated. In IL, CDP-Unet (90.0%) edges out DP-Unet (88.0%) on average, while CDP-DiT (78.0%) substantially outperforms DBC-DiT (64.0%), confirming that the contraction loss transfers across backbones. The paper notes that CDP's advantage is most pronounced under data scarcity (10% dataset), where score-matching errors are amplified and contraction's error-dampening effect is most beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on EDP (Kang et al., 2023) for offline RL and DBC (Pearce et al., 2023) for imitation learning, adding a contraction loss that penalizes the largest eigenvalue of the score function's symmetric Jacobian during training. It departs from Contractive Diffusion Probabilistic Models (Tang & Zhao, 2024), which enforce global contraction on the SDE through structural design choices, by instead promoting local contraction in the probability-flow ODE via a differentiable Jacobian regularizer that integrates seamlessly into existing conditional action diffusion architectures. The connection to Contractive Autoencoders (Rifai et al., 2011) is conceptual—both penalize a Jacobian to reduce local sensitivity—but CDPs target the reverse ODE's score field rather than an encoder, and require uniform contraction across all solver steps rather than local contraction around data points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in addressing a known but underexplored failure mode of diffusion policies in continuous control: compounding solver and score-matching errors that push generated actions off the dataset support. The finding that contraction yields the largest gains in low-data regimes (10% data) is particularly relevant given the cost of real-world data collection. However, the method's sensitivity to the contraction weight γ (requiring per-environment tuning from 0.001 to 100), its dependence on specific baselines (EDP/DBC), and limited real-world evaluation (4 tasks, no low-dimensional physical experiments) leave open questions about generality. Future work on state-conditioned score sensitivity, systematic hyperparameter bounds, and extension to alternative solvers (e.g., SDE-based sampling) would strengthen the contribution.
