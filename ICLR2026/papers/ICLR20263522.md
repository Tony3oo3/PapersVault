## Problem

Existing differentially private decentralized learning (DP-DL) algorithms rely on ad hoc privacy proofs tailored to specific algorithm-trust model pairs, with no general framework connecting them. Moreover, prior analyses treat noise as independent across redundant peer-to-peer exchanges, yielding overly pessimistic privacy bounds that degrade the practical privacy-utility trade-off relative to centralized DP-SGD.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263522/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a communication graph encoded as a (possibly time-varying) gossip matrix W, per-node local datasets, and a target noise scale σ. It produces both the trained model parameters and a Gaussian DP (GDP) privacy bound that is graph-topology-dependent: under PNDP, the guarantee varies by node distance, and under LDP it is uniform. A key structural detail is that the workload matrix W_T (size nT × nT) encodes the full gossip history, and the factorization A = BC must be found such that A captures attacker observations while B drives the optimization dynamics—two quantities that merge into a single object in the centralized case.

## Method

The paper generalizes the centralized Matrix Factorization (MF) mechanism to decentralized learning by proving GDP guarantees for workload matrices A that are rectangular, rank-deficient, and column-echelon (rather than square, full-rank, lower-triangular), with a sensitivity definition sens_Π(C; B) that depends on both the encoder C and decoder B via the projection B†B. It then shows that all existing DP-DL algorithms (DP-D-SGD, AntiPGD, Muffliato-SGD, DECOR, Zip-DL) and trust models (LDP, PNDP, SecLDP) instantiate this unified form O_A = AG + BZ with A = BC, via a two-step construction: listing observed messages/known gradients/known noise, then projecting out known gradients (Lemma 14). For the new MAFALDA-SGD algorithm, the encoder is constrained to C = C_local ⊗ I_n to enforce per-node locality under LDP, reducing the optimization to a standard factorization problem on a T×T Gram matrix H = Σᵢ AᵢᵀAᵢ (where Aᵢ are node-specific slices of (I_T ⊗ W)W_T K^(T,n)), solved offline via L-BFGS. This yields user-level correlated noise that is optimized jointly for the gossip topology and participation pattern, outperforming uncorrelated DP-D-SGD and AntiPGD by up to 31% in test loss or 2× in privacy budget on Housing and FEMNIST.

## Results

On the Housing regression task (Facebook Ego graph, 148 nodes), MAFALDA-SGD achieves a 31% average improvement in test loss over the last 50 training steps compared to all private baselines at a fixed privacy budget ε, and a 2-fold reduction in ε to reach a test loss of 0.75. On FEMNIST classification, MAFALDA-SGD consistently achieves higher test accuracy than private baselines across all privacy budgets, with the largest relative gains at high privacy levels. For PNDP accounting of DP-D-SGD, the paper's method yields up to an order-of-magnitude tighter Rényi divergence bounds at node distances ≤ 2 (where the original Cyffers et al. accounting provides no improvement over LDP) and at least two orders of magnitude tighter bounds at distances ≥ 3, across Erdős–Rényi, Facebook Ego, PeerTube, and Florentine Families graphs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AntiPGD (Koloskova et al., 2023), MAFALDA-SGD wins decisively: AntiPGD's test loss exceeds 6 (i.e., it diverges) on the Housing task while MAFALDA-SGD converges, and MAFALDA-SGD achieves a 31% lower test loss on average in the final 50 steps. Against standard DP-D-SGD (uncorrelated noise, equivalent to Muffliato-SGD with K=1 from Cyffers et al., 2022), MAFALDA-SGD achieves a 2-fold reduction in the required privacy budget ε to reach the same test loss of 0.75, and consistently higher FEMNIST accuracy across all ε values. On the PNDP accounting front, the paper's matrix-factorization-based sensitivity bound (Eq. 12) is significantly tighter than the original Cyffers et al. (2022) accounting: where the latter shows no gain over LDP for nodes at graph distance ≤ 2, the new method already delivers up to 10× tighter bounds, and the gap widens to ≥ 100× at distance ≥ 3. The superiority of MAFALDA-SGD holds regardless of graph topology, as confirmed on additional graphs (Erdős–Rényi, PeerTube, Florentine Families) in the appendix.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: the Matrix Factorization (MF) mechanism for correlated-noise DP-SGD in centralized settings (Kairouz et al., 2021; Denisov et al., 2022; Pillutla et al., 2025) and differentially private decentralized learning under various trust models (Cyffers et al., 2022; Allouah et al., 2024; Biswas et al., 2025). The key conceptual move is showing that gossip-based DL algorithms can be encoded as a single matrix multiplication, and that the MF privacy analysis generalizes from square lower-triangular full-rank workload matrices to the column-echelon (possibly rectangular, rank-deficient) matrices that arise in decentralized settings. Prior DP-DL analyses relied on ad hoc proofs tailored to each algorithm and trust model; this work subsumes them all as instances of a unified MF framework, while also enabling the design of new algorithms with optimized noise correlation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified MF framework gives the DP-DL community a principled, reusable tool for both analyzing existing algorithms (yielding up to two-orders-of-magnitude tighter PNDP accounting) and designing new ones, replacing the current patchwork of bespoke proofs. MAFALDA-SGD demonstrates the practical payoff: a 31% average test-loss improvement and 2-fold epsilon reduction over prior private DL baselines on standard benchmarks. Open questions include scalability of the offline correlation optimization to very large graphs (the O(n³T + n²T³) cost is non-trivial), whether the framework extends to non-linear or adaptive participation schemes beyond the current linear-algorithm assumption, and how the approach performs under more realistic asynchronous or partially-failing communication settings.
