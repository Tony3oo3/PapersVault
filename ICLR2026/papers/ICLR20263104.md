## Problem

Diffusion Large Language Models (DLLMs) require a statically predefined generation length before inference, creating a dilemma where insufficient lengths cripple performance on complex tasks while excessive lengths waste computation and can degrade accuracy. Unlike autoregressive models that naturally adjust output length per task, DLLMs cannot dynamically expand their generation space, and the optimal fixed length varies across benchmarks, making manual tuning impractical.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt and produces a variable-length text response whose length is determined adaptively per problem. The prompt is concatenated with an initial block of [MASK] tokens (default 64), and the total sequence length grows dynamically during inference up to a maximum (default 2048). The output length varies across problems within the same benchmark, reflecting differing task complexity, rather than being a single fixed value for all inputs.

## Method

DAEDAL is a training-free, two-stage inference strategy that leverages the model's own EOS prediction confidence as an internal signal for length sufficiency. Stage 1 (Initial Length Adjustment) runs before denoising: starting from a short initial length, it performs forward passes and checks the average EOS confidence over a window at the sequence terminus; if confidence falls below a threshold, it appends additional [MASK] tokens and repeats until the model signals the length is adequate. Stage 2 (Iterative Mask Insertion) operates during denoising: at each step, in addition to filling high-confidence tokens, it identifies the masked position with the lowest prediction confidence (below a very low threshold) and replaces that single [MASK] token with a block of multiple [MASK] tokens, inserting localized 'breathing room' where the model struggles to articulate complex reasoning. The two stages are complementary—Stage 1 establishes a sound global plan while Stage 2 provides on-demand local expansion—and the entire procedure requires no retraining or model-specific hyperparameter tuning.

## Results

On LLaDA-Instruct-8B, DAIDAL (starting from a unified 64-token initial length) achieves 85.8% accuracy on GSM8K, 44.2% on mATH500, 40.8% pass@1 on MBPP, and 48.2% pass@1 on HumanVal, for an average of 54.75% across four benchmarks. These figures exceed the best fixed-length baseline (52.05% average at 1024 tokens) by 2.7 points on average. DAIDAL also attains a higher effective_ratio (73.5% on GSM8K vs. 27.7% for the 1024-token baseline), meaning it generates fewer wasted padding tokens while producing comparable E_token counts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard Fixed-Length Denoising (LLaDA inference at a manually chosen static length from 64 to 2048 tokens). DAIDAL outperforms this baseline on all four benchmarks: +2.0 points on GSM8K (85.8 vs. 83.8), +4.6 on mATH500 (44.2 vs. 39.6), +2.0 on MBPP (40.8 vs. 38.8), and +0.6 on HumanVal (48.2 vs. 47.6). Crucially, the baseline's optimal length differs per benchmark (1024 for GSM8K/HumanVal, 2048 for mATH500/MBPP), whereas DAIDAL uses a single 64-token initial length and adapts per problem. DAIDAL also reduces total token overhead: its average N_token (e.g., 363 on GSM8K) is well below the 1024–2048 tokens the baseline must allocate, yielding E_ratio values of 52–77% versus 14–28% for the baseline at its peak setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaDA diffusion LLM framework (Nie et al., 2025) and its low-confidence remasking inference strategy inherited from MaskGIT (Chang et al., 2022), extending the denoising loop with a training-free dynamic length mechanism. It departs from concurrent DLLM inference work (Fast-dLLM, dLLM-Cache, Dimple) that targets computational speedup via KV caching and parallel decoding, by instead addressing the more fundamental architectural constraint of static generation length. The key conceptual departure is the observation that EOS prediction confidence at the sequence terminus serves as a reliable internal signal for length sufficiency, repurposing a byproduct of the model's planning behavior as a control signal for sequence expansion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the need for task-specific length tuning, DAEDAL makes DLLMs more practical and closer to the adaptive behavior of autoregressive models, which is a critical gap for real-world deployment. The training-free nature means it can be immediately applied to existing DLLMs without retraining, and the demonstrated robustness to hyperparameters (initial length, thresholds, expansion factor) lowers the adoption barrier. Limitations include evaluation only on 7–8B models and closed-form benchmarks (math, code); whether the EOS-confidence signal generalizes to open-ended generation, multi-turn dialogue, or larger models remains open. The added forward passes during Initial Length Adjustment also introduce a small but non-trivial latency overhead that the paper does not quantify in wall-clock terms.
