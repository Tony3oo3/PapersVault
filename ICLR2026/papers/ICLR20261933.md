## Problem

Oriented object detection in remote sensing requires dense rotated bounding box annotations that are prohibitively expensive to produce at scale. While prior work has separately explored semi-supervised, weakly-supervised, and sparsely-supervised detection, no unified framework simultaneously exploits sparse annotations (only a fraction of objects labeled per image), weak annotation formats (HBox or Point instead of RBox), and abundant unlabeled data to minimize total labeling cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261933/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes aerial RGB images in two forms: a small labeled subset where only a configurable fraction (10–30%) of objects carry annotations, and those annotations may be full RBox, horizontal HBox, or single Point (or any mixture thereof), plus a large unlabeled subset. It outputs oriented bounding boxes (center, width, height, angle) with class labels and confidence scores via an anchor-free FCOS head on a ResNet-50 + FPN backbone. The unusual aspect is that the same model must handle heterogeneous annotation granularities within a single training run, with most objects in 'labeled' images remaining completely unlabeled.

## Method

SPWOOD uses a teacher-student pseudo-labeling pipeline with two training stages (burn-in on sparse-weak data, then self-training on unlabeled data with EMA-updated teacher). The student (SOS-Student) integrates three learning modules: (1) sparse annotation learning via a modified focal loss that applies an adaptive down-weighting factor ω to high-confidence predictions erroneously matched to background (hard negatives), (2) orientation learning via a symmetry-based angle loss that enforces geometric consistency between original and flip/rotation-augmented views, and (3) scale learning that bounds object size using a Bhattacharyya-distance Gaussian overlap loss (upper bound) and a Voronoi Watershed Loss regressing width/height from single-point annotations (lower bound). For pseudo-label selection, the Multi-level Pseudo-labels Filtering (MPF) mechanism fits a two-component Gaussian Mixture Model to the teacher's confidence scores independently at each FPN level (P3–P7) and uses the EM-derived posterior boundary as a per-layer dynamic threshold, replacing static or class-agnostic thresholds. The paper also introduces an Overall Sparse Method for dataset construction that samples annotations per-category across the entire dataset rather than per-image, preserving the original class distribution.

## Results

On DOTA-v1.0, SPWOOD achieves 57.8 mAP with RBox annotations at the 20-20 sparse-partial ratio, outperforming the best sparsely supervised baseline RSST* (51.0) by 6.8 points. Notably, SPWOOD with only HBox annotations (54.0 mAP) surpasses RSST* with full RBox annotations (51.0 mAP) at the same ratio, demonstrating superior annotation efficiency. On DOTA-v1.5, SPWOOD reaches 52.1 mAP (20-20, RBox), and on DIOR it achieves 45.7 mAP (20-20, RBox), consistently leading all baselines across every tested ratio.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPWOOD outperforms all six baselines across every sparse-partial ratio on all three datasets. Against the strongest sparsely supervised baseline RSST* (simplified SAOD), SPWOOD with RBox gains 6.1–13.2 mAP on DOTA-v1.0 and 3.3–12.1 mAP on DOTA-v1.5. Remarkably, SPWOOD with HBox annotations (54.0 mAP at 20-20) exceeds RSST* with full RBox annotations (51.0 mAP) by 3.0 points, showing that weaker labels combined with the SPWOOD framework beat stronger labels in prior methods. Compared to the semi-supervised MCL, SPWOOD (RBox) leads by 13.3 mAP at 10-10 and 12.8 mAP at 20-20 on DOTA-v1.0. Against PWOOD (partial weakly-supervised), SPWOOD with RBox improves by 5.9–10.5 mAP, while SPWOOD with HBox still surpasses PWOOD with RBox by 2.1–6.4 mAP. On DIOR, SPWOOD leads RSST* by 2.5 mAP (20-20) and MCL by 12.6 mAP, confirming consistent gains across datasets with varying object densities and category counts.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPWOOD unifies three previously separate research threads in oriented object detection—sparsely supervised (SAOD), partial weakly-supervised (PWOOD), and weakly supervised (WOOD)—into a single framework for the first time. It builds directly on the Mean Teacher EMA paradigm for the teacher-student loop, borrows symmetry-based orientation learning from H2RBox-v2 and spatial layout scale learning from Point2RBox-v2 for the SOS-Student, and extends GMM-based pseudo-label filtering (from Consistent-Teacher) into a multi-level variant that accounts for FPN layer-specific confidence distributions. The closest prior work, PWOOD, handles partial weakly-supervised settings but does not address the sparse-annotation challenge of distinguishing unlabeled objects from background, which SPWOOD tackles via a Focal-Loss-inspired hard-negative down-weighting mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPWOOD offers a practically important reduction in annotation cost for remote sensing OOD, demonstrating that a unified framework can handle RBox, HBox, and Point annotations (or combinations) under sparse-partial settings with competitive accuracy. However, the significantly higher computational cost (22.8 GB memory, 40 hours vs. 34 hours for RSST) limits its practicality, and the single-modality constraint leaves open the question of whether multi-modal inputs (spectral, textual) could further reduce annotation needs. A notable open question is raised by the paper's own ablation: the distribution-preserving Overall Sparse Method underperforms the biased Single Sparse Method, suggesting that the common assumption that preserving class distribution is always beneficial may not hold in sparse detection settings.
