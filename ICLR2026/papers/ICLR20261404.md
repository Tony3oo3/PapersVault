## Problem

Existing tabular data generation benchmarks evaluate synthetic data primarily through density estimation, ML efficacy, and privacy, but neglect whether the underlying causal structures of real data are preserved. The only prior structural fidelity benchmark (CauTabBench) is confined to toy SCM datasets because its metrics require ground-truth causal structures that are unavailable for real-world tabular data. Additionally, prevailing benchmarks cover only narrow ranges of generators and datasets, limiting generalisability.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a reference tabular dataset (split into training and test sets) and a synthetic dataset produced by a generator trained on the reference training split; optionally, a ground-truth SCM is provided for CI-based structural fidelity scoring. For each variable in the dataset, an ensemble of nine downstream predictors (AutoGluon) is trained to predict that variable from all others, and the relative performance against the reference data yields a per-variable utility score. The final output is a four-dimensional evaluation profile (density estimation, privacy, ML efficacy, structural fidelity) with both local and global structural fidelity decomposed into CI scores (when SCM is available) and global utility (SCM-free), enabling generator rankings across 29 datasets.

## Method

TabStruct introduces a unified evaluation framework that integrates structural fidelity as a core dimension alongside conventional metrics. Structural fidelity is quantified at the CPDAG (Markov equivalence class) level via conditional independence tests: local CI scores assess only statements involving the prediction target, while global CI scores cover all pairwise CI statements implied by the ground-truth SCM. To extend structural fidelity assessment to real-world datasets without ground-truth SCMs, the paper proposes global utility, which treats every variable as a prediction target, trains an ensemble of nine predictors (AutoGluon) to predict each variable from the remaining features, and computes a normalised relative performance score (balanced accuracy for categorical, RMSE for numerical) against the reference data; global utility is the mean across all variables. The benchmark evaluates 13 generators spanning nine categories (interpolation, GAN, VAE, normalising flows, tree-based, diffusion, EBM, language model, Bayesian network) on 29 datasets (6 expert-validated SCM datasets and 23 real-world classification/regression datasets from TabZilla), using nested cross-validation with 10 repeated splits and ADTM aggregation.

## Results

Across 13 tabular generators on 29 datasets (6 expert-validated SCM + 23 real-world), diffusion models (TabDDPM, TabSyn, TabDiff) consistently achieve the highest global structural fidelity, ranking Top-3 in both Global CI and Global Utility on both SCM and real-world data. The proposed Global Utility metric shows a strong monotonic correlation with Global CI (r_s = 0.84, p < 0.001), validating it as an SCM-free proxy for structural fidelity. In contrast, Local Utility correlates strongly with Local CI (r_s = 0.78) but only weakly with Global CI (r_s = 0.14), confirming that task-specific ML efficacy is an incomplete measure of structural preservation. The benchmark comprises over 150,000 individual evaluations across nine generator categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diffusion-based generators (TabDDPM, TabSyn, TabDiff) dominate the proposed Global Utility and Global CI metrics, achieving near-perfect structural fidelity (Global CI ≈ 0.92–0.99) on SCM datasets and consistently ranking Top-3 on real-world data via Global Utility. SMOTE, while achieving the highest Local Utility (0.82 ± 0.12) and thus appearing strongest under conventional ML-efficacy evaluation, drops to only 0.32 ± 0.08 in Global Utility and 0.30 ± 0.11 in Global CI, revealing that it preserves only task-local structure while violating most inter-feature causal relationships. BN (Bayesian Network) achieves strong density estimation (Shape 0.82) but collapses in structural fidelity (Global CI 0.07 ± 0.05), confirming that high marginal/joint distribution matching does not imply causal structure preservation. GReaT, despite leveraging a large language model, underperforms even simple baselines like SMOTE and TVAE across most dimensions, with the paper attributing this to the fundamental misalignment between autoregressive factorisation and the permutation-invariant nature of tabular data. GOGGLE, which explicitly learns relational structure via a GNN, also fails to achieve strong structural fidelity, consistent with prior findings that causal discovery struggles beyond ~10 features. Energy-based models (TabEBM, NRGBoost) show moderate Global Utility (0.75, 0.09 respectively) but are not competitive with diffusion models on structural fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the structural fidelity evaluation introduced by CauTabBench (Tu et al., 2024) from toy SCM datasets to real-world tabular data, where ground-truth causal structures are unavailable. It builds on the CPDAG-level evaluation methodology from Spirtes et al. (2001) and the 'SCM-inspired and real-data-validated' paradigm from TabPFN (Hollmann et al., 2025) to propose global utility, an SCM-free metric that quantifies global structural fidelity through per-variable predictive utility. The broader benchmark framework departs from prior tabular evaluation suites (Synthcity, SynMeter, Hansen et al.) by explicitly incorporating structural fidelity as a fourth evaluation dimension alongside density estimation, privacy preservation, and ML efficacy, and by scaling to 13 generators across 9 categories on 29 datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes structural fidelity as a necessary but previously underexplored evaluation axis for tabular generators, showing that conventional metrics (especially ML efficacy) can be misleading — SMOTE, for instance, outperforms diffusion models on ML efficacy while violating most underlying causal structures. The practical impact is significant: global utility provides a task-independent, domain-agnostic, and computationally efficient (0.64s per 1000 samples with a small predictor ensemble) way to assess structural fidelity without requiring ground-truth SCMs. Key open questions remain: global utility is an empirical proxy rather than a theoretically provable measure of structural fidelity, and the field still lacks reliable causal discovery methods that can recover governing SCMs from real-world tabular data, limiting the ability to formally validate the metric.
