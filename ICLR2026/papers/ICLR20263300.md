## Problem

Existing image quality assessment methods operate at the whole-image level, implicitly relying on region-level understanding without ever explicitly modeling it. No structured representation exists that grounds pairwise comparative assessment in image regions, and state-of-the-art MLLMs fail at region-level distortion reasoning even when given explicit region cues such as bounding boxes and descriptions. This paper introduces the Distortion Graph (DG) task to fill this gap with a region-first, structured topology for pairwise image assessment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263300/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images (anchor and target) along with panoptic segmentation masks from SAM, requiring one-to-one region alignment across both images (up to 112 regions, mean 18). It outputs a Distortion Graph: a 4-tuple of region node sets for each image, distortion edges (comparative predicates between matched region pairs), and optional scene edges. Each region node encodes a distortion type (15 categories including clean), severity level (none/minor/moderate/severe), and a TOPIQ full-reference quality score in [0,1]; each distortion edge carries one of five comparative predicates (same, slightly/significantly better/worse) derived from score-difference thresholds.

## Method

PANDA encodes both images with a frozen DINOv2 backbone and segments regions via SAM, then introduces a learnable token pool where each region is matched to a token and combined with image features through a Hadamard product and convolution. A Transformer-based degradation decoder performs self-attention on each image's patch features, followed by cross-attention where each region in one image queries the other image's features, enabling region-to-region correspondence learning. Four 3-layer MLP heads (with LayerNorm and GELU) predict the comparative predicate, distortion type, severity level, and quality score per region. Training uses a weighted sum of categorical cross-entropy losses (for the three classification heads) and L1 loss (for score regression) over 30 epochs with AdamW at lr=1e-4, with the final loss weights set to λ_rel=0.1, λ_dist=1.0, λ_sev=0.1, λ_score=1.0 via cross-validation.

## Results

On PANDABENCH Easy, PANDA achieves the best F1 across all four tasks: 0.56 (comparison), 0.79 (distortion), 0.59 (severity), and SRCC 0.79 / PLCC 0.83 (quality score), substantially outperforming the strongest MLLM baseline (GPT-5 Mini: F1 0.26/0.44/0.29, SRCC 0.52). Performance degrades consistently from Easy to Hard for all methods, but PANDA shows the smallest drop (e.g., distortion F1: 0.79→0.52→0.19). On whole-image ranking (KADID10k), PANDA's zero-shot DG-based ranking reaches 78.83% accuracy in 4 minutes, outperforming Q-Insight (69.70%, 8 hours) and approaching GPT-5 Mini (84.72%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PANDA outperforms all 11 baselines on every task across all three PANDABENCH splits. Among open-source distortion-specific MLLMs, Q-SiT and Q-Insight are the weakest (distortion F1 ≈ 0.05–0.07), while Seagull (F1 0.18) and Gemma-3 27B (F1 0.20) are slightly better but still far below PANDA (0.79). DepictQA in zero-shot mode (F1 0.10) performs poorly, but when fine-tuned on PANDASET (DepictQA†) it becomes the second-best method (F1 0.76), confirming that region-first training data is the key factor. Among closed-source frontier MLLMs, GPT-5 Mini (distortion F1 0.44) and GPT-4o (0.44) lead, but both trail PANDA by ~35 F1 points; Gemini 2.5 Pro (0.41) and GPT-5 Nano (0.28) are weaker still. On the Hard split, all MLLMs degrade to near-random (e.g., GPT-5 Mini distortion F1 drops to 0.09), while PANDA retains 0.19. On TID2013 whole-image ranking, PANDA (78.4% predicate / 77.8% score) surpasses mPLUG-Owl2 (48.5%), LLaVA-1.6 (57.0%), and Q-Instruct (55.0%), demonstrating that the DG representation generalizes beyond its training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the intra-image scene graph formalism to the inter-image setting, proposing a Distortion Graph (DG) that treats paired images as a structured topology grounded in regions. It departs from the dominant whole-image paradigm in distortion MLLMs (Q-Instruct, Co-Instruct, DepictQA, Q-Bench, MICBench) by being explicitly region-first and comparative, a combination the authors note is absent from all prior benchmarks. Architecturally, PANDA borrows the DETR-like multi-head prediction paradigm, DINOv2 pretrained features, and SAM panoptic segmentation, but introduces a token-pool mechanism with Hadamard-product region association and cross-attention between regions in one image and global features in the other to learn pairwise relationships.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PANDABENCH exposes a significant and previously hidden gap: even frontier MLLMs (GPT-5 Mini, Gemini 2.5 Pro) perform at or near random chance on region-level distortion understanding, and distortion-specific MLLMs collapse to template responses when queried region-wise. The DG-as-chain-of-thought prompting showing ~15% emergent improvement in GPT-5 Mini suggests structured intermediate representations can unlock capabilities that raw pixel input alone does not. Key limitations include reliance on synthetic distortions and TOPIQ-derived comparative labels (which may inherit perceptual biases), and the absence of human-annotated region-level comparative data at scale, leaving open whether the formalism generalizes to real-world, human-judged pairwise comparisons.
