## Problem

Existing financial QA benchmarks pre-collect relevant context and bypass open-domain search, under-assessing agents' search competence and diverging from real analyst workflows. The only prior end-to-end financial agent benchmark is confined to a self-constructed system and relies solely on static historical data, permitting success via memorization rather than real-time retrieval. No fully open-source benchmark exists that jointly evaluates time-sensitive data fetching, tool use, and multi-source evidence integration in realistic financial search tasks.

## Input / Output

Each question requires at least one external tool call (web search, financial API, or specialized plugin) and has a single objective answer, with 635 questions in English and Chinese spanning Global and Greater China market subsets. Answers are evaluated against expert-annotated tolerance ranges rather than exact matches—e.g., ±0.1% for index changes, intraday high-low range for FX rates, or absolute buffers for commodity prices—accommodating legitimate minor fluctuations across data sources. The LLM-as-a-Judge produces a binary 0/1 score per question, validated at 95% agreement with human-verified labels across ~400 instances per dataset.

## Method

FINSEARCHCOMP comprises three task families mirroring daily analyst workflows: Time-Sensitive Data Fetching (real-time quotes verified via API), Simple Historical Lookup (point-in-time financial data from filings or databases), and Complex Historical Investigation (multi-period synthesis over horizons up to 184 months). Construction involved 70 professional financial experts (50 annotators + 20 senior reviewers from firms like Citadel and J.P. Morgan) with a blind multi-expert verification protocol, multi-source cross-validation, and ambiguity-mitigation guidelines, totaling ~240 expert-hours. Evaluation uses LLM-as-a-Judge with task-specific rubrics: T1 compares against real-time API ground truth with asset-class-differentiated tolerance bands, while T2 and T3 use static answers with predefined error margins. The paper evaluates 21 models (12 web-enabled products, 9 APIs) against a 50-expert human baseline, finding that web search and financial plugins yield average gains of 40.8, 29.0, and 8.1 points on T1, T2, and T3 respectively, and that model origin (US vs. Chinese) significantly affects performance on corresponding market subsets.

## Results

On the Global subset, Grok 4 (web) achieves the highest overall score of 68.9%, outperforming the runner-up GPT-5-Thinking (web) (63.9%) by 5.0 pp, yet still trailing human experts (75.0%) by 6.1 pp. On the Greater China subset, DouBao (web) leads at 54.2%, followed by YuanBao-DeepSeek-R1 (web) (52.5%) and Grok 4 (web) (51.9%), with all models remaining more than 34 pp below human performance (88.3%). Performance degrades monotonically from T1 (Time-Sensitive) to T3 (Complex Historical), with T3 scores dropping to 23.9–51.2% for the top web-based models versus 51.4% for humans. Web-based products substantially outperform their API counterparts (e.g., DeepSeek-R1: 28.8% web vs. 6.3% API), and models without search score 0 on T1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Grok 4 (web) dominates the Global subset across all three tasks, scoring 87.3% on T1, 68.1% on T2, and 51.2% on T3, surpassing GPT-5-Thinking (web) by 10.4, 0.9, and 3.6 pp respectively. On the Greater China subset, DouBao (web) leads T1 at 88.3% (vs. Grok 4's 64.9%), while YuanBao-DeepSeek-R1 (web) edges ahead on T3 (14.8% vs. 11.4%), though Grok 4 still posts the highest T3 score among US-origin models. US-origin models (Grok 4, GPT-5-Thinking, Gemini) consistently outperform Chinese-origin models on the Global subset, while Chinese-origin models (DouBao, YuanBao series, ERNIE-X1) show a marked advantage on Greater China, with an asset-origin ratio below 100% for T1/T2. The gap between web-based products and raw APIs is stark: DeepSeek-R1 drops from 28.8% (web) to 6.3% (API), and Qwen3 from 29.7% to 0.8%, confirming that search infrastructure and financial plugins are critical. Even the best model (Grok 4, 60.4% overall) trails the human baseline (81.6%) by 21.2 pp, with the largest deficit on Greater China T3 (23.9% vs. 76.7%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two existing benchmark families: general-purpose browsing agents (BrowseComp, BrowseComp-ZH) and domain-specific financial QA (FinQA, ConvFinQA, FinanceQA, BizFinBench). Prior financial benchmarks pre-collect relevant context and thus bypass open-domain search and tool use, while browsing benchmarks avoid domain knowledge and long-form synthesis. The closest prior work, the Finance Agent Benchmark (Bigeard et al., 2025), offers end-to-end evaluation but is confined to a self-constructed system and uses only static historical data, permitting success via memorization. FINSEARCHCOMP departs from all of these by requiring open-domain retrieval, time-sensitive data, multi-source evidence integration, and end-to-end agent evaluation in a fully open-source setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FINSEARCHCOMP provides the first publicly available, expert-curated testbed for evaluating whether LLM agents can perform realistic financial analyst workflows end-to-end, filling a gap that has limited the field's ability to measure progress on domain-grounded search. The finding that even the best model (Grok 4 at 68.9%) trails human experts by 6.1 pp on Global and that all models fall more than 34 pp below humans on Greater China highlights a substantial and domain-specific capability gap. Open questions include whether the benchmark's time-sensitive evaluation protocol scales to continuous monitoring, whether the identified failure modes (shallow search, stale evidence, cross-unit aggregation) can be addressed by better tool orchestration rather than larger models, and whether the country-origin effect reflects fundamental retrieval-infrastructure disparities that will persist as models improve.
