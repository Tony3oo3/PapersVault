## Problem

Monocular depth estimation models produce complete but unscaled relative depth, while depth sensors and SfM provide precise but incomplete metric depth. Existing prior-based methods each target only one specific prior pattern (sparse points, low-resolution grids, or missing regions) and fail to generalize to mixed or unseen prior types, limiting practical deployment where multiple measurement sources coexist.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263215/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a depth prior in any form—sparse SfM/LiDAR points, extremely sparse 100-point sets, low-resolution ToF maps, irregular missing areas (range, shape, or object masks), or arbitrary mixtures thereof—and outputs a single dense, complete, metrically accurate depth map at the input resolution. Internally, a frozen MDE model generates a relative depth prediction that is consumed as an auxiliary geometry condition; the depth prior is normalized to [0,1] before conditioning to decouple scene-scale variance. Training images are resized to 518×518, and the frozen MDE backbone can be swapped at test time without retraining.

## Method

Prior Depth Anything uses a coarse-to-fine pipeline that progressively merges metric accuracy from measurements with geometric structure from predictions. In the coarse stage, a frozen MDE model's relative depth prediction is used to pre-fill missing regions of the depth prior via pixel-level metric alignment: for each missing pixel, k nearest valid prior points are found, and an optimal affine transform (scale s, shift t) is solved by distance-weighted least squares to map predicted depth into the metric domain. In the fine stage, a conditioned MDE model (initialized from Depth Anything V2 with zero-initialized condition conv layers) receives the normalized pre-filled prior as a metric condition and the normalized depth prediction as a geometry condition alongside the RGB input, learning to rectify measurement noise and refine geometric structures. The model is trained on synthetic data (Hypersim, vKITTI) with injected outliers and boundary noise using a scale-invariant log loss, enabling zero-shot generalization to 7 real-world datasets across completion, super-resolution, and inpainting tasks.

## Results

On the mixed-prior benchmark (Table 2), PriorDA with Depth Pro+ViT-B achieves an average rank of 1.1 across 5 datasets (NYUv2, ScanNet, ETH-3D, DIODE, KITTI), with AbsRel of 2.01 (NYUv2 S+M), 2.06 (ScanNet S+M), 1.61 (ETH-3D S+M), 3.40 (DIODE S+M), and 3.37 (KITTI S+M). For zero-shot depth completion with sparse points (Table 3), the same configuration achieves AbsRel of 1.96 on NYUv2 (LiDAR/Extreme) and 1.54 on ETH-3D (Extreme), outperforming task-specific methods Omni-DC (2.12/1.88) and Marigold-DC (1.90/2.27). On depth super-resolution with real low-resolution captures (ARKitScenes, RGB-D-D), PriorDA achieves AbsRel of 1.95 and 2.02, significantly beating PromptDA (1.34/2.79) and Omni-DC (2.14/2.09).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PriorDA (Depth Pro+ViT-B) outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and prior combination in the mixed-prior setting, achieving an average rank of 1.1 versus 4.2 for Omni-DC, 5.1 for Marigold-DC, 6.7 for Depth Pro, 7.1 for DepthLab, 7.5 for DAv2, and 8.4 for PromptDA. On zero-shot depth completion (sparse points), PriorDA beats the two purpose-built completion methods: on NYUv2 LiDAR it achieves AbsRel 1.96 vs. Omni-DC's 2.12 and Marigold-DC's 1.90, and on ETH-3D Extreme it reaches 1.54 vs. Omni-DC's 1.98 and Marigold-DC's 2.03. For depth super-resolution on real low-resolution captures (ARKitScenes, RGB-D-D), PriorDA (1.95, 2.02) substantially outperforms Omni-DC (2.14, 2.09) and Marigold-DC (2.17, 2.15), though PromptDA leads on ARKitScenes (1.34) due to its native high-resolution training. On depth inpainting with range masks, PriorDA (16.31 on NYUv2) improves over Marigold-DC (19.83) and Omni-DC (23.24), while all methods struggle on this challenging setting. The paper also demonstrates that swapping the frozen MDE backbone at test time (from DAv2-B to Depth Pro) yields consistent gains without retraining, a flexibility none of the baselines offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Depth Anything V2 as both the frozen relative-depth predictor and the initialization for a conditioned MDE model, and adopts ZoeDepth's scale-invariant log loss for training. It departs from prior prior-based MDE methods (Omni-DC, Marigold-DC, PromptDA, DepthLab), each of which is specialized to a single prior pattern (sparse points, low-resolution grids, or missing regions), by introducing a coarse-to-fine pipeline that first pre-fills any prior via kNN-based metric alignment with the relative prediction, then refines with a conditioned ViT. The key conceptual departure is treating the depth prediction not merely as a fallback for missing pixels but as an explicit geometric guide that narrows the domain gap across all prior types before the learned refinement stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying depth completion, super-resolution, and inpainting into a single zero-shot framework that also handles mixed priors, this work removes the need to select and deploy separate task-specific models in practical pipelines (autonomous driving, AR/VR, 3D scene editing). The test-time model-switching capability—swapping the frozen MDE backbone for larger or smaller variants—offers a practical accuracy-efficiency dial that will evolve as MDE foundation models improve. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions (current training is 518×518 vs. PromptDA's 1440×1920), whether larger conditioned backbones (ViT-L/G) yield further gains, and how well the method handles temporally coherent video depth with sensor noise patterns that differ from the i.i.d. synthetic training perturbations.
