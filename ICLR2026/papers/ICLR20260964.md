## Problem

Inference-time compute scaling via extended reasoning improves robustness to weak text jailbreaks but breaks down against stronger gradient-based or multimodal attacks, and the conditions under which reasoning aids robustness were unclear. This paper identifies why the benefit fades and proposes a principled way to make inference compute more profitable for adversarial defense.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260964/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes adversarially perturbed RGB images (PGD white-box attacks or Attack-Bard black-box transfer attacks) paired with text prompts containing explicit security specifications, and produces class labels, image descriptions, or single-word attribute predictions (shape, color, texture). Unusually, the prompt can include K repetitions of the security specification to naively scale inference compute, and the model response can be pre-filled with specification-consistent tokens; for the reasoning VLM, budget-forcing controls the exact number of reasoning tokens before the final answer is emitted.

## Method

The paper proposes the Robustness from Inference Compute Hypothesis (RICH): inference-compute defenses profit as the model's training data better reflects the components of attacked data, predicting a rich-get-richer dynamic where more robust base models gain more from test-time scaling. They validate this across six VLMs spanning no adversarial training, lightweight FARE finetuning, and full adversarial pretraining plus instruction tuning, under both white-box PGD and black-box Attack-Bard transfer attacks, scaling inference compute via chain-of-thought, budget-forced reasoning, or K-repetition of security specification portions. A key practical contribution is robustifying InternVL 3.5 gpt-oss 20B's ViT encoder via unsupervised adversarial finetuning (FARE), creating the first adversarially robust RL-tuned reasoning VLM that then shows significant robustness gains from scaling reasoning to thousands of tokens. They further demonstrate that reducing the attack perturbation budget ε brings attacked data closer to training data, unlocking inference compute benefits even for less-robust models, and that specification-consistent pre-filling (not random tokens) is required for the security specification to confer robustness.

## Results

On Attack-Bard black-box transfer attacks (30-way multiple-choice), CoT inference-compute scaling yields a statistically significant robustness gain only for adversarially trained models: Delta2-LLaVA-v1.5 improves from 62.0% to 73.0% adversarial accuracy (+11.0 pp, p=4.5e-3), and FARE-LLaVA-v1.5 from 56.0% to 65.5% (+9.5 pp, p=4.6e-3), while non-robust LLaVA-v1.5 gains only 6.5 pp (38.0%→44.5%, p=4.2e-2, not significant). For white-box PGD attacks at ε=64/255, repeating the security specification K=5 times increases the attack's required steps from 25.4 to 63.2 for Delta2-LLaVA-v1.5, but from only 6.2 to 7.4 for LLaVA-v1.5. Large-scale non-robust VLMs (Llama-3.2-Vision-90B, Qwen-2.5-VL-72B) show no significant CoT benefit on adversarial data, confirming that model scale alone does not substitute for adversarial training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaVA-v1.5 (no adversarial training), the paper's key finding is that inference-compute scaling provides no statistically significant robustness benefit on adversarial data (38.0%→44.5%, p=4.2e-2), and adding an explicit security specification actually degrades robustness (attacker loss drops from 6.4 to 2.9 at 100 PGD steps). FARE-LLaVA-v1.5 (lightweight adversarial finetuning, ε=2/255) shows moderate CoT gains on adversarial data (+9.5 pp) and benefits from security specifications, but only when the attack budget is reduced to ε=16/255 (PGD steps increase from 18.8 to 27.2 with K=5). Delta2-LLaVA-v1.5 (full adversarial pretraining + instruction tuning, ε=8/255) dominates all comparisons: it achieves the highest adversarial accuracy (73.0% with CoT), the largest PGD-step increase from inference scaling (25.4→63.2 at ε=64/255), and the only model where a security specification dramatically raises attacker loss (13.5→21.2). Large-scale non-robust VLMs Llama-3.2-Vision-90B and Qwen-2.5-VL-72B show no significant CoT benefit on adversarial Attack-Bard data (27.0→27.5% and 13.0→18.0% respectively), ruling out model scale as an explanation. Compared to o1-v from Zaremba et al. (2025), which retains a 39% attack success rate on Attack-Bard even at maximum reasoning effort, the robustified Delta2-LLaVA-v1.5 + Claude pipeline achieves higher accuracy with far less inference compute, demonstrating that adversarial training of the vision encoder is more effective than scaling reasoning alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Zaremba et al. (2025), who showed that scaling inference-time compute (reasoning) improves robustness to text jailbreaks but plateaus on vision attacks, by proposing the RICH hypothesis to explain *why* the vision-attack failure occurs: the model must first be robust enough to follow instructions on adversarially OOD data. It synthesizes the adversarial training literature for VLMs (FARE by Schlarmann et al. 2024, Delta2 by Wang et al. 2025c) with test-time compute scaling, arguing that compositional generalization (Keysers et al. 2019) is the mechanism by which train-time robustness unlocks test-time specification enforcement. The paper departs from Zaremba et al. by validating the inference-compute-robustness link under white-box gradient-based attacks, with open-source and non-RL-tuned models, and by showing that naive prompt repetition (K-scaling) suffices for non-reasoning models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RICH hypothesis provides a practical decision rule: lightweight adversarial finetuning (e.g., FARE on the ViT) is a cheap prerequisite that makes test-time compute scaling profitable for robustness, rather than a universal fix that works regardless of base model quality. This reframes the train-time/test-time defense tradeoff and suggests that security-critical deployments should invest in modest adversarial training before relying on reasoning budgets. Limitations include testing primarily on smaller VLMs (≤90B), the potential for exposed reasoning chains to increase adversarial risk (Wu et al. 2025), and the known cost that adversarial training can degrade clean-data performance. Open questions include whether the rich-get-richer dynamic persists at frontier-model scale and whether the RICH generalizes beyond vision-language settings to other modalities or task types.
