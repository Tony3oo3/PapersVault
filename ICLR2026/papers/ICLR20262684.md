## Problem

Standard RLVR training (e.g., GRPO) improves Pass@1 by narrowing the policy toward memorized correct solutions, causing policy entropy to collapse and Pass@k to plateau—meaning the model fails to expand its reasoning boundary beyond the base model. The core challenge is that collecting large, diverse problem sets with verifiable answers is non-trivial: human-annotated sets are scarce, and synthetic problems typically lack precise reference answers needed for RLVR's verifiable reward signal.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a mathematical reasoning problem (or code generation problem) and produces a chain-of-thought solution ending in a boxed final answer. During training, the pipeline additionally consumes the policy's own correct solutions as context to synthesize variational problems, which are then fed back as new inputs for the policy to solve. A key structural constraint is that each variational problem must share the exact reference answer of its source problem, so no additional answer labeling is required; group sizes are G=8 solutions per problem and G_v=8 variational problems per correct response.

## Method

SvS is an online self-play augmentation loop integrated into GRPO-based RLVR. At each training step, the policy first solves a batch of original problems; problems whose group accuracy falls in a target underperforming range [acc_l, acc_h] (12.5%–50%) are selected, and the policy's correct solutions to those problems serve as context to synthesize a group of variational problems with rephrased descriptions and structures. The policy then solves these synthetic problems, and correctness is verified against the original reference answers. A critical reward-shaping mechanism (Eq. 4) assigns positive reward to a synthetic problem only if the policy's group accuracy on it falls within a moderate band [1/8, 5/8], preventing the policy from generating trivially easy problems with embedded hints. All three data types—original problem solving, variational problem synthesis, and synthetic problem solving—are mixed into a single GRPO update, enabling the policy to jointly learn problem solving and problem generation in a fully self-improving loop without any external model or distillation.

## Results

On the DAPO-17k training set with Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, SvS achieves absolute Pass@32 gains of +18.3 (AIME 24: 70.8 vs 52.5) and +22.8 (AIME 25: 65.2 vs 42.4) over standard RLVR, while also improving Pass@1 (avg 32) by +10.5 on both AIME benchmarks. Across 12 reasoning benchmarks and model sizes from 3B to 32B, SvS yields consistent average Pass@1 improvements of 1.7–2.9 points over RLVR, with the largest gains on competition-level tasks (e.g., +8.4 on OlymMATH-easy Pass@32, +16.2 on AIME 24 Pass@32 with MATH-12k). The strategy also generalizes to code generation (PRIME-RL) and preserves performance on general QA benchmarks (MMLU-Pro, ARC-C, HellaSwag) where standard RLVR causes degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SimpleRL-32B (Zeng et al., 2025), SvS on DAPO-17k achieves 39.3/40.5 Pass@1 on AIME 24/25 versus 22.1/13.9, and 70.8/65.2 Pass@32 versus 62.0/38.5, substantially outperforming on both metrics. Against ORZ-32B (Hu et al., 2025), SvS leads by 15.1/14.2 points on AIME 24/25 Pass@1 and by 15.1/18.2 points on Pass@32, confirming that self-play augmentation surpasses standard RLVR scaling approaches. Against the Clip-Cov entropy-regulation method (Cui et al., 2025), SvS consistently outperforms in intermediate evaluations on LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct (Figure 14), and the two methods are complementary: combining SvS augmentation with Clip-Cov further improves performance over Clip-Cov alone (Figure 15). The ablation in Table 3 further shows that simply extending RLVR training (Ext) or adding extra rollouts to underperforming problems (Eup) cannot match full SvS, confirming that the key ingredient is online problem diversity rather than additional compute or negative samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the RLVR training paradigm (GRPO, DAPO) and the recently identified entropy-performance trade-off, where standard RLVR improves Pass@1 at the cost of policy entropy and Pass@k. It departs from prior data augmentation approaches (MetaMath-style external rephrasing, SwS failure-case synthesis) by using the policy's own correct solutions as context to generate variational problems that preserve the original reference answers, eliminating the need for external annotation. The self-play framing connects to adversarial self-play for reasoning (Cheng et al., 2024), but the key distinction is the answer-preserving constraint and the reward-shaping mechanism that prevents the policy from generating trivially solvable problems. The approach is orthogonal to entropy-regularization methods like Clip-Cov and is shown to be composable with them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in RLVR training—entropy collapse leading to plateaued Pass@k—by introducing an online, self-contained data augmentation loop that requires no external models or additional labeling. The substantial Pass@32 gains (18–22 points on AIME) and consistent entropy stability across 3B–32B models suggest this could become a standard component of RLVR pipelines. Limitations include observed degradation on open-ended-answer benchmarks when training on integer-only datasets (DAPO-17k), non-trivial computational overhead from the synthesis and solving stages, and validation primarily on mathematical reasoning with only a preliminary code-generation experiment. Open questions include whether the strategy scales to longer-horizon reasoning, whether the underperforming-problem targeting range is optimal across domains, and whether the self-play loop can be extended to multi-turn or interactive settings.
