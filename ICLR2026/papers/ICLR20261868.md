## Problem

Self-Consistency (SC) decoding improves LLM reasoning accuracy by sampling multiple traces and majority-voting, but it must fully expand every trace, incurring substantial token and latency costs. Existing adaptive methods (Adaptive Consistency, Early-Stopping SC) can only detect answer-level agreement after complete traces are generated, leaving the rich structural consensus present in early reasoning prefixes unexploited at inference time. No prior method leverages this prefix consistency as a training-free, inference-time mechanism to prune redundant reasoning paths before full expansion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reasoning question x and a frozen LLM M as input. It first generates N short prefixes (default Lp=256 tokens, sampled at temperature 0.6, top-p 0.9) from the LLM, then conditions full trace generation on the dominant cluster's prefixes. The output is a majority-vote final answer extracted from K expanded traces (K ≤ N), accompanied by token efficiency η, path expansion count (PExp), and clustering overhead kt (typically 2–17 ms). The method is model-agnostic and requires no fine-tuning or external encoders.

## Method

PoLR (Path of Least Resistance) operates in four steps: (1) sample N short reasoning prefixes of length Lp from the LLM; (2) embed each prefix via lightweight TF-IDF bag-of-words encoding and cluster them using Agglomerative Hierarchical Clustering with cosine similarity and a distance threshold of 1.0; (3) identify the dominant cluster C* (largest by cardinality) and expand all K prefixes in C* to full reasoning traces by conditioning the LLM on each prefix; (4) apply standard SC majority voting over the K extracted answers. The theoretical justification separates accuracy preservation (requiring I(Z;Y)>0, i.e., cluster identity weakly predicts correctness) from efficiency magnitude (governed by structural skew κ=|C*|/N, with a formal lower bound η ≥ 1 − (K/M)·κ⁻¹). PoLR strictly generalizes SC (reducing to it when K=N and clustering is bypassed) and is complementary to adaptive methods like AC and ESC, serving as a parallelizable pre-filter that removes redundant reasoning modes before adaptive allocation, achieving up to 60% token reduction and 50% latency savings across 1.5B–32B parameter models.

## Results

Across GSM8K, MATH500, AIME24/25, and GPQA-DIAMOND, PoLR reduces token usage by 40–60% relative to Self-Consistency while matching or slightly improving accuracy (e.g., QWQ32B on MATH500: 91.8%→92.0% at η=51.8%; GPQA-DIAMOND: 68.7%→70.2% at η=53.8%). Clustering overhead k_t is negligible (2–17 ms). On AIME25 with QWQ32B, PoLR drops 10 points (76.7%→66.7%), but this corresponds to only 3 of 30 samples where SC itself succeeds by a narrow margin. Combined with Adaptive Consistency, PoLR+AC achieves ~75% average reduction in path expansions versus SC while preserving accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Self-Consistency (Wang et al., ICLR 2023), PoLR matches accuracy on 4 of 5 benchmarks for QWQ32B (GSM8K: −0.3pp, MATH500: +0.2pp, AIME24: 0.0pp, GPQA: +1.5pp) while cutting token usage by 47–60%; the sole notable drop is AIME25 (−10pp, i.e., 3/30 samples) where SC itself has low consensus. Against Adaptive Consistency (Aggarwal et al., EMNLP 2023), PoLR serves as a complementary pre-filter: PoLR+AC reduces path expansions from 13.54 to 10.53 (DSQ7B, N=31, GPQA) and from 4.87 to 4.67 (QWQ32B, N=51, MATH500) while maintaining or improving accuracy (e.g., QWQ32B GPQA: 69.70%→70.71%). Against Early-Stopping Self-Consistency (Li et al., ICLR 2024), PoLR+ESC similarly lowers expansions (e.g., QWQ32B N=51 GPQA: 16.90→11.13 PExp) with comparable accuracy. Overall, PoLR is not a direct competitor to AC/ESC but a drop-in front-end that compounds their savings, achieving ~75% average PExp reduction versus SC in hybrid configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

PoLR builds directly on Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2023) by inserting a prefix-clustering step before the standard majority-vote aggregation, making it a strict generalization of SC. It departs from prior efficiency methods like Adaptive Consistency (Aggarwal et al., 2023) and Early-Stopping SC (Li et al., 2024), which rely on answer-level agreement observable only after full trace generation, by instead exploiting structural similarity in short prefixes at inference time. The paper also distinguishes itself from UPFT (Ji et al., 2025), which leverages prefix signals at training time via fine-tuning, by showing that the same prefix-consistency phenomenon can be exploited unsupervised and training-free through lightweight TF-IDF embedding and agglomerative clustering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PoLR offers a practical, drop-in 40–60% token reduction for SC with negligible accuracy loss across diverse benchmarks and model scales (1.5B–32B), and it composes additively with existing adaptive methods (PoLR+AC, PoLR+ESC) to push path expansions down by another 30–40%. The clean theoretical separation—accuracy preservation governed by mutual information I(Z;Y) while efficiency is driven by structural skew κ—provides a useful diagnostic for when prefix-based pruning will and will not help. Open questions include whether the method degrades on tasks with highly heterogeneous reasoning paths (GPQA-DIAMOND shows mild accuracy drops at longer prefixes), whether the 10% AIME25 drop for QWQ32B generalizes beyond the 30-sample benchmark, and whether semantic embeddings or multi-cluster expansion could close the remaining gap on less structured domains.
