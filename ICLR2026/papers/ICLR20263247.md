## Problem

Existing data-contamination detection methods were designed for LLMs and rely on text-only perturbations or verbatim memorization checks, which are insufficient for VLMs where visual features carry critical semantic information. The paper shows that these methods either fail outright or produce inconsistent signals across different fine-tuning strategies and contamination levels, leaving no reliable way to flag contaminated VLMs without access to the training corpus.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263247/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes original image-question pairs from visually-grounded multiple-choice benchmarks (RealWorldQA, MMStar) and a black-box VLM. It produces a semantically perturbed image-question pair where the correct answer has been changed and the image regenerated to match, at the same resolution as the original. The final detection output is a performance drop Δ (original accuracy minus perturbed accuracy) per model configuration, yielding a binary contamination flag: a negative Δ on the perturbed set indicates the model memorized the original image-text pair rather than reasoning over visual content.

## Method

The multi-modal semantic perturbation pipeline has three stages: (1) the correct answer is randomly swapped to a different option; (2) GPT-4o generates a dense image caption conditioned on the original question, the original answer, and the new answer, explicitly highlighting which visual elements must change; (3) Flux ControlNet, guided by this caption and Canny edge maps of the original image, synthesizes a new image that preserves global composition while minimally altering the semantics to make the new answer correct. Generated pairs are filtered (manually or via o3) to retain only unambiguously answerable examples. Detection then compares a model's aggregate accuracy on the original versus perturbed benchmark: clean models with genuine visual reasoning perform equally or better on the perturbed set (since difficulty is comparable or lower), while contaminated models that memorized the original image-text pair show a clear performance drop that scales with the number of contamination epochs, satisfying practicality, reliability, and consistency across LoRA, standard fine-tuning, and full-parameter training regimes.

## Results

On MMStar, the proposed multi-modal semantic perturbation method achieves consistent detection across all contaminated model configurations: LLaVA-v1.5-7B shows performance drops (Δ) ranging from −8.29% (LoRA, 1 epoch) to −16.16% (LoRA, 3 epochs) and −8.49% to −13.74% (full fine-tuning), while Qwen2-VL-7B drops range from −7.07% to −32.32% (LoRA) and −29.50% to −43.03% (LLM-only). Clean models consistently improve on the perturbed benchmark (e.g., +31.51% for LLaVA, +16.16% for Qwen2-VL), confirming the perturbed questions are of equal or lower difficulty. The method satisfies all three requirements (Practicality, Reliability, Consistency) across every tested configuration, while all six baselines fail at least one requirement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines in satisfying the three core requirements (Practicality, Reliability, Consistency). **CircularEval** (Liu et al., 2024b) fails Reliability by missing LLaVA-v1.5-7B contaminated with LoRA at 2 and 3 epochs (Δ = −5.05 and +1.22, i.e., no clear drop) and Qwen2-VL-7B with standard fine-tuning at 2 epochs; it also requires a clean model reference, violating Practicality. **Choice Confusion** (Yao et al., 2024) fails Reliability most severely: it cannot detect LLaVA-v1.5-7B under any training strategy or epoch (all Δ values are positive, e.g., +14.75 for LoRA E3), and shows inconsistent signals for Qwen2-VL-7B LoRA (Δ = −1.01 at E2, −2.83 at E3). **Multi-modal Leakage** (Chen et al., 2024a) requires a clean model (violating Practicality) and fails Reliability for Qwen2-VL-7B standard fine-tuning at 3 epochs (Δ = +29.90, indicating the text-only performance actually increases, masking contamination). The three perplexity-based methods — **Shared Likelihood** (Oren et al., 2023), **Guided Prompting** (Golchin & Surdeanu, 2024), and **N-gram Accuracy** (Xu et al., 2024) — all fail every requirement: they require clean-model references, show counter-intuitive trends (e.g., SL p-values increase with more contamination), and provide no consistent detection signal across training regimes. In contrast, the proposed method achieves 100% detection success across all 12 contaminated configurations tested on MMStar, with the performance drop scaling monotonically with contamination degree (e.g., LLaVA LoRA: −8.29 → −13.13 → −16.16 across epochs 1–3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generalization-failure paradigm for contamination detection (established for LLMs by Yao et al. 2024, Mirzadeh et al. 2024, Huang et al. 2025) to the multi-modal setting, arguing that text-only perturbations are insufficient when the model can rely on unchanged visual features. It repurposes diffusion-based image generation (Flux + ControlNet) as a tool for creating semantically perturbed visual inputs, a use case not previously explored in the contamination-detection literature. The paper systematically benchmarks and departs from prior VLM-specific detection attempts (Multi-modal Leakage, CircularEval, BGR shuffling, image masking), showing they fail to meet reliability and consistency requirements across training regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As VLMs become central to production systems trained on opaque internet-scale corpora, a practical black-box detection method that requires no clean-model reference and no knowledge of the training data is genuinely useful for practitioners auditing model claims. The method's simplicity (generate perturbed images, compare accuracy) makes it easy to adopt, and the demonstrated robustness across LoRA/full fine-tuning, paraphrased contamination, and mixed-dataset contamination strengthens its practical value. Key limitations include the requirement for visual dependence in questions, current reliance on diffusion-model quality for text rendering, and the fact that the perturbation pipeline must be run once per benchmark. Open questions include extension to free-form generation tasks, detection of very subtle or pretraining-stage contamination at scale, and whether adversarially trained models could learn to generalize under such perturbations.
