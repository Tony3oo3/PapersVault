## Problem

Autoregressive training of decoder-only Transformers incentivizes the KV cache to retain high-fidelity, step-by-step predictive traces of all prior tokens rather than compressed summaries, which the authors show via Information Bottleneck theory hinders generalisation. Existing auxiliary latent-space computation methods either compress the cache (indiscriminately reducing predictive information) or inject latent tokens, but none implement periodic in-place memory rewrites analogous to biological consolidation and reconsolidation. The paper asks whether selectively reprocessing the KV cache at reasoning-step boundaries can improve reasoning generalisation without reducing memory footprint.

## Input / Output

The model takes a standard text reasoning prompt (e.g., a math word problem) and generates an autoregressive chain-of-thought response ending in a final answer. The unusual aspect is that generation is interrupted at every newline-delimited reasoning step boundary, at which point a Cache Processor rewrites a subset of KV entries in-place before decoding resumes. The Processor operates on the backbone's existing KV cache (no new tokens are emitted), so the output format is identical to a vanilla Transformer, but the internal working memory is periodically restructured during generation.

## Method

The Bottlenecked Transformer augments a frozen SFT backbone with a Cache Processor: a small Transformer with one non-causal block per backbone layer that rewrites KV entries in-place at reasoning-step boundaries. At each invocation, the Processor selects (i) the recent-step window (RSW) of tokens from the just-completed step (consolidation) and (ii) the top-k prior positions by attention mass with the current step (reconsolidation), processes them in parallel without causal masking, and applies a gated residual update to their key and value vectors. The theoretical contribution shows via Information Bottleneck analysis that the KV cache plus final hidden state constitutes the terminal bottleneck in a decoder-only Transformer, and that autoregressive training maximises both I(S₀:ₙ; Ẑ) and I(Ẑ; Sₙ₊₁), motivating a mechanism that selectively compresses input information while preserving predictive information. Training is two-stage: the backbone undergoes standard SFT, then is frozen while only the Processor is trained via next-step cross-entropy with BPTT truncated at step boundaries, so the Processor learns to reshape the cache purely to improve prediction of the next reasoning step.

## Results

The Bottlenecked Transformer achieves pass@1 accuracy gains of up to +6.6 percentage points over the strongest baseline across seven mathematical/logical reasoning benchmarks and four backbones (Llama 3.2 1B, Llama 3.1 8B, Qwen 0.6B, Llama 3.2 3B). Notable gains include +4.6pp on GSM8K (Llama 3.2 3B: 46.78→51.33), +3.1pp on LogiQA (Llama 3.1 8B: 20.74→23.81), and +2.4pp on MATH (Qwen 0.6B: 26.68→29.08). The method wins on the majority of task-backbone combinations, with the principal exception being Gaokao-MathQA (a Chinese-language benchmark) where distribution shift limits the Processor's benefit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the pause-token baseline (SFT + 16 pause tokens after the prefix), the Bottlenecked Transformer wins on nearly all task-backbone pairs, with the pause-token variant showing variable and often lower performance than plain SFT (e.g., consistent drops on Llama 3.1 8B and Qwen 0.6B), consistent with the original pause-token paper's finding that gains require continued pretraining. Against the latent rollout baseline (Coconut-style continuous hidden-state recycling), the Bottlenecked Transformer dominates across the board; the latent rollout baseline is particularly unstable, with Llama 3.1 8B collapsing to near-zero accuracy (e.g., 4.02% on GSM8K vs. 71.87% for the Bottlenecked model). The one notable loss for the Bottlenecked Transformer is LogiQA on Llama 3.2 3B (20.12 vs. 22.12 for SFT), and Gaokao-MathQA is a consistent weak spot across backbones due to the Chinese-language distribution shift beyond the Processor's English-centric training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new sub-direction within the cache-operator family of Auxiliary Latent-Space Computation (ALSC) methods, departing from the dominant compression-oriented approaches (pruning, merging, summarising) by introducing in-place KV rewrites inspired by neuroscientific memory consolidation and reconsolidation. The theoretical grounding draws on Information Bottleneck theory to argue that autoregressive training incentivises the KV cache to retain unnecessary input detail, and that periodic rewrites can improve predictive efficiency without dimensional compression. Architecturally, it builds on the state-space formulation of Transformer decoding and the pause-token/latent-rollout baselines (Goyal et al., 2024; Hao et al., 2024), but replaces token-space or residual-space interventions with a dedicated Cache Processor that operates non-causally on selected KV entries at reasoning-step boundaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean IB-theoretic justification for why the KV cache in decoder-only Transformers is suboptimal as a reasoning bottleneck, and demonstrates that a small auxiliary module can improve mathematical reasoning by up to ~6.6pp over strong SFT baselines. However, the practical cost is significant: Cache Processor training takes ~20× longer than backbone SFT, and inference adds ~45% wall-clock overhead. Key open questions include whether prediction-error-gated reconsolidation (rather than fixed newline triggers) would better mirror biological mechanisms, whether explicit compression objectives or noise-injection schemes could replace the implicit SGD-noise compression, and whether the approach scales to larger backbones or non-mathematical reasoning domains where the current gains are inconsistent.
