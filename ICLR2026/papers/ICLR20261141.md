## Problem

Automated evaluation of text-level creativity across diverse domains remains unsolved: existing methods are confined to single tasks (humor, problem-solving, creative writing), operate at the model or subject level rather than distinguishing individual responses, and when prompted LLMs are used as judges they exhibit poor alignment with human consensus, inconsistency, and high cost. No large-scale, cross-domain benchmark with reliable human labels exists to train or evaluate a general-purpose creativity evaluator.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261141/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CrEval receives a triplet (I, R1, R2) — a shared instruction and two candidate responses — and outputs a three-class judgment: R1 is more creative, R2 is more creative, or they are creatively comparable (tie). The test set comprises ~3,200 human-annotated pairs (ICC(2k)=0.92) drawn from 8 sources across 87 subdomains, predominantly in Simplified Chinese, with pairs labeled distinguishable when score differences exceed 0.3 and comparable when below 0.1. At inference, a reference response can serve as a fixed baseline, enabling win-rate-based ranking of arbitrary candidate responses with minimal additional computation.

## Method

The framework rests on a context-aware pairwise protocol: providing a shared instruction as context raises human annotator agreement from ICC 0.59 (decontextualized) to 0.75, ensuring reliable labels. CreataSet is built in three stages — (1) collecting 113K instruction-response pairs from 8 sources spanning 87 domains, (2) augmenting each instruction with k=5 synthetic responses generated by models of varying capability (Qwen2.5-14B, MiniCPM-2B) under ordinary vs. creativity-oriented prompts, and (3) assigning weakly supervised pseudo-labels using two validated heuristics (stronger model → more creative, 86.6% accuracy; creativity prompt → more creative, 81.4% accuracy), yielding 1M+ training pairs. CrEval fine-tunes Qwen2.5-{7,14}B-Instruct via LoRA (α=16, r=8) with a standard cross-entropy classification loss over the three-way label, augmented by position swapping to eliminate positional bias and negative sampling (random unrelated responses as least-creative anchors) to sharpen instruction-conditioned judgment. The 14B variant achieves 0.762 agreement with human judges, outperforming GPT-4o by 18.7% and all other baselines, and transfers to O.O.D. creative writing and AUT benchmarks.

## Results

On the CreataSet test set (3,196 pairwise samples across 8 domains), CrEval-14B achieves an average F1 of 0.735, Kappa of 0.613, and Agreement rate of 0.762, outperforming all baselines. The 7B variant (CrEval-7B) reaches F1=0.732, Kappa=0.601, Agreement=0.745, surpassing its base model Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (F1=0.614, Kappa=0.403, Agreement=0.574) by 19.2%, 49.1%, and 29.8% respectively. CrEval-14B also achieves the highest consistency rate (94.4%) when swapping response positions, compared to 84.6% for the Bradley-Terry loss variant. On out-of-distribution benchmarks (Creative Writing, WritingPrompts, AUT), CrEval-7B achieves F1 scores of 79.17, 92.46, and 58.22, outperforming frontier models like GPT-4o (70.83, 75.42, 56.57) and DeepSeek-V3 (76.39, 81.05, 57.28).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CrEval-14B outperforms all 20 baselines on average F1, Kappa, and Agreement. Against frontier LLMs, CrEval-14B beats GPT-4o by 18.7% relative in Agreement (0.762 vs. 0.642), o3 by 5.0% (0.762 vs. 0.725), and Claude-3.5-Sonnet by 3.0% (0.762 vs. 0.740), with the closest margin being against Claude-3.5-Sonnet on Kappa (0.613 vs. 0.609). Traditional metrics perform poorly: PPL yields a near-zero Kappa (-0.042), DSI reaches only 0.175 Kappa, and Creativity Index (Lu et al., 2025) achieves 0.231 Kappa, all far below CrEval's 0.613. Among evaluation-centric models, WritingBench-Critic (F1=0.612, Kappa=0.362) and AUTO-J (F1=0.567, Kappa=0.323) are substantially outperformed, while G-Eval with GPT-4o (F1=0.712, Kappa=0.558) is the strongest prompting-based baseline but still trails CrEval-14B by 2.3% F1 and 9.5% Kappa. At the 7B scale, CrEval-7B surpasses its base model Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct by 19.2% F1 and 49.1% Kappa, and outperforms Gemma-2-9B-it (F1=0.704) and LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct (F1=0.621). On out-of-distribution tasks (Creative Writing, WritingPrompts, AUT), CrEval-7B also outperforms much larger models including GPT-4o, DeepSeek-V3, and o3, demonstrating strong cross-domain generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-as-a-judge paradigm (G-Eval, PandaLM, Prometheus, AUTO-J) from general text-quality dimensions (helpfulness, coherence) to the specific and underexplored dimension of creativity, while simultaneously departing from prior creativity evaluation that was confined to single domains (humor, problem-solving, creative writing) and subject-level granularity. The pairwise comparison protocol is borrowed from preference-learning literature, but the context-aware formulation—where a shared instruction anchors both responses—is a deliberate methodological choice to improve inter-annotator agreement (ICC 0.59→0.75). The weakly supervised data construction, using model capability and prompt type as creativity proxies, repurposes the DPO/preference-optimization data pipeline for evaluation training rather than generation alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1M+ pair, 87-domain CreataSet is the first large-scale cross-domain creativity evaluation resource, and the finding that a 7B/14B LoRA-tuned model outperforms frontier LLMs (GPT-4o, o3, Claude-3.5-Sonnet) by 10–19% in human-judgment agreement demonstrates that task-specific training is more effective than raw model scale for this evaluation. The practical DPO pipeline showing CrEval can boost generative model creativity without harming reasoning or increasing hallucination provides a concrete recipe for creativity-aware alignment. Key limitations include the dataset being predominantly Simplified Chinese (limiting immediate multilingual applicability), the reliance on weakly supervised labels whose assumptions (stronger model = more creative) may not hold universally, and the inherent subjectivity of creativity that consensus-based annotation only partially addresses.
