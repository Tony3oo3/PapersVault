## Problem

Autoregressive VLA methods discretize continuous actions into bins, disrupting the continuity needed for precise manipulation, while diffusion-based VLA methods append a separate diffusion head after the VLM that lacks internet-scale pretraining and cannot leverage the LLM backbone as an iterative action-generation expert. The paper asks how to elegantly unify both generation paradigms within a single LLM so they mutually reinforce each other rather than operating as disconnected modules.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view RGB images (resized to 224×224; single-view for pretraining, up to three views for dual-arm fine-tuning), a natural-language instruction, and the current robot end-effector state, which is mapped into the LLM embedding space via a learnable MLP rather than discretized into language tokens. It outputs a horizon of 7-DOF (single-arm) or 14-DOF (dual-arm) SE(3) end-effector poses [Δx, Δy, Δz, Roll, Pitch, Yaw, gripper] normalized to [-1,1], produced simultaneously as discrete autoregressive tokens and continuous diffusion-denoised vectors, then adaptively ensembled at inference based on autoregressive token confidence.

## Method

HybridVLA embeds the Markovian denoising steps of diffusion directly into the LLM's next-token prediction by projecting the diffusion timestep and noisy action into the embedding space as continuous vectors, bracketed by special <BOD>/<EOD> tokens that precede the autoregressive action tokens to prevent ground-truth leakage. A hybrid loss (L_dif + L_ar) jointly optimizes both branches within a unified token sequence, and a structured two-stage training protocol (10-epoch pretraining on 760K trajectories across 35 cross-embodiment datasets, followed by fine-tuning on self-collected demonstrations) stabilizes the joint optimization. At inference, a collaborative action ensemble mechanism uses the mean confidence of autoregressive action tokens (threshold 0.96) to decide whether to average the two predictions or fall back to the diffusion action alone, while a KV-cache design over the denoising steps reduces redundant computation and enables 4-step DDIM sampling at ~9.4 Hz.

## Results

On the RLBench benchmark (10 tabletop tasks, Franka Panda, single-view), HybridVLA (7B) achieves a mean success rate of 78% ± 4%, outperforming the previous SOTA autoregressive VLA (OpenVLA, 41%) by 37% and the SOTA diffusion-based VLA (π₀, 61%) by 17%. In real-world evaluation, HybridVLA (7B) reaches 83% mean success on five single-arm Franka tasks and 71% on five dual-arm AgileX tasks, surpassing π₀ (45% / 55%) and CogACT (61% single-arm). On SimplerEnv (Google Robot, variant aggregation), HybridVLA (7B) achieves 59% mean success versus 49% for π₀. The diffusion-only variant (HybridVLA-dif) runs at 9.4 Hz, comparable to CogACT (9.8 Hz) and π₀ (13.8 Hz), while the full ensemble model runs at 6.1 Hz.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HybridVLA (7B) decisively outperforms all six baselines on RLBench. Against the autoregressive baselines, it beats OpenVLA (41%) by 37 points, OpenVLA-OFT (45%) by 33 points, and ManipLLM (38%) by 40 points, confirming that embedding diffusion denoising into the LLM's next-token prediction recovers the continuity lost by pure discretization. Against the diffusion-based baselines, HybridVLA (7B) surpasses π₀ (61%) by 17 points and CogACT (7B) (60%) by 18 points; notably, even the diffusion-only variant HybridVLA-dif (72%) exceeds CogACT by 12 points and π₀ by 11 points, demonstrating that running diffusion *inside* the LLM backbone (rather than as a separate head) better exploits pretrained knowledge. Against ARP, HybridVLA (7B) leads the four-view variant (68%) by 10 points and the one-view variant (47%) by 31 points. In real-world single-arm tasks, HybridVLA (83%) outperforms CogACT (61%) by 22 points and π₀ (45%) by 38 points; in dual-arm tasks it leads π₀ (55%) by 16 points. On SimplerEnv, HybridVLA (59%) beats π₀ (49%) by 10 points, with the largest gap (10 points) on the Open Top Drawer and Place task requiring strong scene understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

HybridVLA builds on the Prismatic VLM architecture and the autoregressive action tokenization paradigm from OpenVLA, while incorporating the diffusion denoising objective from Diffusion Policy. Its key departure is from prior diffusion-based VLA methods (π0, CogACT, DiVLA) that append an independent diffusion head after the VLM, treating the LLM merely as a feature extractor. Instead, HybridVLA embeds the Markovian denoising steps directly into the LLM's next-token prediction process, using the pretrained backbone as an iterative action expert, and introduces a unified token sequence that interleaves diffusion and autoregressive tokens with specialized markers to prevent information leakage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that diffusion and autoregressive action generation within a shared LLM backbone mutually reinforce each other (validated via PCA and ablations), yielding 17–19% SOTA improvements on simulation and real-world manipulation while maintaining strong generalization to unseen objects, backgrounds, and spatial layouts. A practical limitation is inference speed: the full ensemble model runs at 6.1 Hz, slower than π0 (13.8 Hz), though the diffusion-only variant reaches 9.4 Hz. Open questions include whether the confidence-based ensemble threshold generalizes across embodiments and task horizons, and whether the approach extends to longer-horizon or multi-robot settings where the autoregressive bottleneck becomes more acute.
