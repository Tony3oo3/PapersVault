## Problem

Score-based generative models (e.g., diffusion models) are typically interpreted as learning the full data distribution in the low-noise limit, yet accurately estimating scores at small σ remains notoriously difficult. This paper identifies a previously uncharacterized gap: under the manifold hypothesis, geometric information about the data manifold is encoded at order Θ(σ⁻²) in the score, while density information about the on-manifold distribution appears only at order Θ(1), creating a sharp rate separation that existing convergence analyses do not capture.

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely at inference time on a pre-trained diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion 1.5). It takes a text prompt and the model's learned score function as input, and produces generated images or uniform samples on the data manifold. The only modification is scaling the unconditional score prediction by σ^α in the corrector (Langevin) step of the Predictor-Corrector sampler, with no retraining, fine-tuning, or architectural changes required. For the Bayesian inverse problem application, the input is an observation y and a measurement operator A, and the output is posterior samples supported on the manifold.

## Method

The central insight (Theorem 3.1) is a Taylor expansion of log p_σ showing that the distance-to-manifold term d_M(x) appears at order σ⁻² while log p_data appears only at order Θ(1), establishing that manifold geometry is recovered Θ(σ⁻²) times more robustly than the data density. Building on this, the paper introduces Tempered Score (TS) Langevin dynamics (Eq. 8), a one-line modification that scales the score by σ^α (0 < α < 2) in the corrector step, provably yielding the uniform Riemannian volume measure on the manifold under only o(σ⁻²) score error—orders of magnitude weaker than the o(1) accuracy needed for full distributional recovery. The proof for the general non-gradient case (Theorem 5.2) employs WKB asymptotic expansion of the Fokker-Planck equation, showing via the strong maximum principle that the leading-order prefactor c₀ must be constant on the compact manifold. The framework also extends to Bayesian inverse problems, where using the uniform manifold prior instead of the data distribution as prior reduces the required score accuracy from o(1) to o(σ⁻²) for correct posterior sampling.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion 1.5 with three text prompts, the proposed Tempered Score (TS) method achieves the best CLIP Prompt Similarity (P-sim, quality) on 2 of 3 prompts (Furniture: 30.20 vs. DDPM 29.56, PC 29.40; Car: 26.62 vs. DDPM 26.23, PC 26.30) while consistently achieving the lowest CLIP Inter-Image Similarity (I-sim, diversity) across all prompts (e.g., Architecture: 80.76 vs. DDPM 81.53, PC 81.03). In the synthetic ellipse experiment, standard Langevin dynamics deviates substantially from the target distribution even at σ=10⁻², whereas TS Langevin (α=1) reliably recovers the uniform distribution on the manifold. Table 2 further shows that TS with α=1 (no tuning) outperforms PC in nearly every corrector-step setting (5–30 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) and the Predictor-Corrector (PC) sampler from Song et al. (2021). TS consistently outperforms both baselines on diversity (lower I-sim) across all three prompts, with the largest gap on Architecture (80.76 vs. 81.53 for DDPM, a 0.77-point improvement). On quality (P-sim), TS wins on Furniture (30.20 vs. 29.56 DDPM, +0.64) and Car (26.62 vs. 26.23 DDPM, +0.39), but is marginally below DDPM on Architecture (27.32 vs. 27.36, −0.04). In the synthetic ellipse experiment (Figure 2), standard Langevin dynamics (equivalent to PC's corrector step) produces a visibly non-uniform stationary distribution, while TS Langevin with α=1 recovers the uniform distribution, confirming the theoretical prediction of Theorem 5.2. The improvements are robust: Table 2 shows TS outperforming PC in nearly all corrector-step settings (5–30 steps) with α=1 fixed, indicating the gains are not an artifact of hyperparameter tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the score-based generative modeling framework (Song & Ermon 2019; Song et al. 2021) and the WKB asymptotic analysis of non-reversible Fokker-Planck equations (Bouchet & Reygner 2016), but departs from prior work under the manifold hypothesis in a key way: while earlier analyses (Lu et al. 2023; Lyu et al. 2025) characterized the leading-order singularity of the score as a geometric projection, they subsumed distributional information into a generic bounded remainder. Here, the authors explicitly isolate the higher-order terms involving p_data and prove a sharp quantitative rate separation—geometry at Θ(σ^{-2}) versus density at Θ(1)—that no prior work had established. The proposed Tempered Score Langevin dynamics also extends the uniform-on-manifold sampling literature (Coifman & Lafon 2006; De Santi et al. 2025) by achieving the same target at inference time via a one-line score rescaling, rather than through graph-based normalization or fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The rate separation provides a principled explanation for why diffusion models produce realistic samples even with imperfect low-noise scores: the manifold geometry is recovered orders of magnitude before the on-manifold density, so concentration on the data support is robust to score errors that would be catastrophic for distributional recovery. The practical one-line modification (scaling the unconditional score by σ^α in the corrector step) is immediately applicable to existing diffusion pipelines and Bayesian inverse problem solvers, potentially improving diversity without retraining. Key open questions include whether the L^∞ score-error assumption can be relaxed to L^2 (matching denoising score matching training objectives), whether cumulative error along the full sampling trajectory preserves the rate separation, and whether the continuous-time analysis translates to discretized samplers. The experiments are preliminary (Stable Diffusion 1.5, small synthetic manifolds), so the practical impact remains to be validated at scale.
