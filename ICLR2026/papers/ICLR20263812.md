## Problem

Generalizable bimanual manipulation faces a fundamental dilemma: end-to-end imitation policies demand thousands of demonstrations per task-object combination and full retraining for new scenes, while modular zero-shot methods rely on fragile prompt engineering and lack the structured action priors needed for precise dual-arm coordination. VLBiMan targets the gap of achieving robust, retraining-free bimanual manipulation from a single human demonstration that can adapt to novel object placements, category-level instance changes, and external disturbances.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a concise natural-language task description, a one-shot demonstration collected via kinesthetic teaching (physically dragging dual-arm end-effectors to sparse waypoints, recorded as 14-DoF sequences of 6-DoF poses plus binary gripper states at 10 Hz alongside stereo RGB at 960×540), and a new scene observed by the same binocular camera. It outputs a synthesized dual-arm trajectory (time-indexed 6-DoF poses and gripper commands for both arms) adapted to the new object configuration, with the temporal structure inherited from the demonstration but spatially re-anchored to the new scene.

## Method

VLBiMan decomposes the one-shot demonstration into semantically labeled atomic skill modules via keypose-driven segmentation (velocity discontinuities, gripper transitions), classifying each segment as task-invariant (object rigidly coupled to an end-effector) or scene-variable (pre-contact approach). Variable modules are adapted by grounding VLM-segmented object masks (Florence-2 + SAM2) to task-specific representative points—mask centroids or table-contact points—computing relative position shifts via stereo back-projection and orientation changes via image-moments principal-axis estimation, deliberately avoiding 6-DoF pose estimation. Invariant modules are reused directly, and the full trajectory is composed through progressive IK refinement (iterative spline interpolation toward the goal pose) and dynamic collision compensation (proximal and vertical offsets), enabling closed-loop correction under object displacement. The key design principle is capturing relative spatial relationships between objects rather than absolute poses, so that the same invariant sub-skills transfer across object instances, placements, and even different robotic embodiments without retraining.

## Results

On six primary bimanual tasks with new object placements and same objects (no interference), VLBiMan achieves 85.3% average success rate, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline ReKep+ (63.3%) and all other methods. Under the harder setting of novel object instances, VLBiMan reaches 78.0% vs. 42.7% for ReKep+. With dynamic interference applied, VLBiMan maintains 69.3% (same objects) and 59.3% (novel instances), while the best baseline drops to 38.0% and 25.3% respectively. On four long-horizon multi-stage tasks, VLBiMan achieves 52.0% (same objects) and 41.0% (novel instances) without interference, again leading all baselines by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLBiMan outperforms all four baselines across every evaluation setting by a large margin. Against the strongest baseline ReKep+ (which receives an oracle-level initial grasp label to mitigate perception noise), VLBiMan leads by 22.0 points on same-object/no-interference, 35.3 points on novel-instance/no-interference, 31.3 points with interference on same objects, and 34.0 points with interference on novel instances. The affordance-based Robot-ABC (Ju et al., ECCV 2024) and keypoint-reasoning ReKep (Huang et al., CoRL 2024) both require re-planning the full trajectory for each new placement and cannot reuse invariant sub-skills, resulting in 36.0% and 43.3% respectively (no interference, same objects) versus VLBiMan's 85.3%. The two adapted single-arm one-shot methods—Mechanisms (Mao et al., CoRL 2023) and MAGIC (Liu et al., ICRA 2025)—struggle most with bimanual coordination, achieving only 26.7% and 44.7% without interference, and dropping to 13.3% and 24.7% under dynamic interference. The gap widens further on long-horizon tasks (Table 2), where VLBiMan's modular skill composition yields 52.0% vs. 34.0% for ReKep+ on same objects, confirming that the decomposition-and-reuse strategy is critical for multi-stage generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

VLBiMan carves new ground by repurposing the keypoint/affordance representation paradigm (seen in ReKep, MOKA, RobotPoint) but replacing learned keypoint networks with VLM-based segmentation (Florence-2, SAM2) plus lightweight heuristic anchors (mask centroids, contact points), eliminating the need for retraining or object-specific CAD models. It departs from large-scale VLA pipelines (ALOHA, RDT-1B, π0) that require thousands of demonstrations by conditioning on a single kinesthetic-teaching demonstration and decomposing it into reusable invariant/variable skill modules. Unlike zero-shot LLM-planning approaches (ReKep, MOKA) that re-plan the full trajectory per scene, VLBiMan reuses task-invariant sub-skills and only adapts variable modules via vision-language grounding, bridging the gap between one-shot imitation learning (Mechanisms, MAGIC, Wen et al.) and modular VLA pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path toward scalable bimanual manipulation by reducing demonstration cost from thousands to one and enabling cross-embodiment transfer (validated on a humanoid platform) without retraining, which is significant for deployment in unstructured settings. However, key limitations remain: the system is restricted to rigid objects, lacks runtime anomaly detection and recovery (e.g., slippage, occlusion), and depends on a fixed-base platform without force or tactile sensing. Open questions include whether the modular skill-reuse strategy extends to deformable objects, whether the heuristic anchor selection generalizes beyond the tested household items, and whether the approach scales to mobile or dexterous-hand embodiments where the kinematic assumptions break down.
