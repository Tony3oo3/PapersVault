## Problem

Prior work established that BEoS-stable solutions of two-layer ReLU networks satisfy a data-dependent weighted path-norm constraint, but for the uniform ball distribution this yields a curse of dimensionality that contradicts empirical deep learning success. The missing piece is a principled way to predict which data geometries benefit from this implicit regularization and which do not, i.e., how the geometry of the input distribution controls the effective capacity of GD-trained networks below the edge of stability.

## Input / Output

The theory takes as input a data distribution (isotropic Beta(α)-radial or a finite mixture of uniform distributions on m-dimensional affine subspaces in R^d), a two-layer ReLU network of arbitrary finite width trained by vanilla GD at a fixed learning rate η, and the BEoS curvature condition λ_max(∇²L) ≤ 2/η. It outputs distribution-dependent generalization upper and lower bounds whose rates depend on the radial concentration parameter α (isotropic case) or the intrinsic dimension m and number of mixture components J (anisotropic case), along with a scalar half-space-depth concentration index S_DQ that quantifies data shatterability. Empirically, the framework is validated on synthetic data in dimensions up to 500 and on MNIST (784-d pixel vectors).

## Method

The core mechanism is a half-space-depth quantile partition that splits the input domain into a T-deep region (where every hyperplane through a point leaves at least T-fraction of data on each side) and a shallow complement. In the deep region, the data-dependent weight function g_D is bounded below by a positive constant, so the weighted path-norm constraint reduces to a standard unweighted path-norm ball, enabling classical metric-entropy generalization bounds. In the shallow region, where g_D is tiny and complexity explodes, the analysis abandons function-space covering and instead bounds the contribution by worst-case L∞ amplitude times the probability mass of that region. The generalization rate is then governed by optimizing the trade-off between these two terms over T (or equivalently the annulus width ε for isotropic distributions). For mixture-of-subspaces data, the key additional insight is that a neuron's activation on an m-dimensional component depends only on the projection of its weight onto that subspace, so the effective shatterability collapses from d to m dimensions, yielding rates in n^{-1/(2m+4)} independent of the ambient dimension d.

## Results

The paper establishes a spectrum of generalization rates for two-layer ReLU networks below the edge of stability, governed by a data shatterability principle. For isotropic Beta(α)-radial distributions (Theorem 3.4), the generalization rate degrades smoothly as α decreases (mass concentrates toward the sphere), with the rate exponent transitioning from n^{-α(d+3)/(2(d²+4αd+3α))} for large α to n^{-α/(2d+4α)} for small α. For mixtures of m-dimensional balls in R^d (Theorem 3.10), all BEoS-stable solutions achieve a rate of Õ(n^{-1/(2m+4)}) depending on intrinsic dimension m rather than ambient d. Empirically, on a uniform sphere in R^50, GD interpolates noisy labels to MSE ≈ 1.02 (≈ noise variance σ²=1), while on a union of 20 lines the final MSE is ≈ 0.07, confirming the theory's prediction that low-dimensional structure resists memorization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without direct method-to-method comparisons; the 'competing' scenarios are different data geometries under the same training protocol. The key empirical contrast is between uniform sphere data (highly shatterable) and a union of 20 one-dimensional lines in R^50 (low shatterability): GD on the sphere reaches the noise-level interpolation limit (MSE ≈ 1.02) before epoch 2000, while the low-dimensional mixture resists overfitting with final MSE ≈ 0.07. On MNIST (n=30,000) versus Gaussian inputs of the same dimension (d=784), GD on Gaussian data interpolates within a few hundred steps, whereas MNIST remains in a prolonged BEoS regime with clean MSE well below the noise level for tens of thousands of epochs. The paper also notes consistency with prior results: the uniform-ball 'neural shattering' phenomenon of Liang et al. (2025) is recovered as one special point of the broader α-spectrum, and the spherical limit matches the flat interpolation construction of Theorem 3.6.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the minima-stability / Edge-of-Stability line of work (Mulayoff et al. 2021; Nacson et al. 2023; Qiao et al. 2024; Liang et al. 2025) that connects the BEoS curvature constraint to a data-dependent weighted path norm. Its key conceptual departure is abstracting the 'neural shattering' phenomenon identified by Liang et al. (2025) for the uniform ball into a general half-space-depth-based 'data shatterability' principle, then instantiating it for a spectrum of isotropic Beta-radial distributions and for mixtures of low-dimensional subspaces. Technically, it bypasses the infinite metric entropy of the weighted function class via a depth-quantile partition of the input space, a tool not present in prior EoS generalization analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a single geometric quantity (half-space-depth concentration) that unifies several previously disparate empirical observations—why real data resists overfitting relative to Gaussian data, why mixup helps, why activation-based pruning works—under one theoretical roof. The provable adaptation to intrinsic dimension (Theorem 3.10) gives a rigorous account of why overparameterized networks escape the curse of dimensionality on structured data, a question that has been empirically observed but lacked a clean EoS-based explanation. Key limitations include restriction to two-layer ReLU networks and the concentration index being primarily calibrated for isotropic distributions; extending the framework to deep networks and anisotropic structured data remains open, as does connecting shatterability to optimization speed and the role of normalization layers.
