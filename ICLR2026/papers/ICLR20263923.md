## Problem

Real-world sleep monitoring involves heterogeneous sensor montages across devices and centers, with frequent channel dropout, yet existing PSG models are either task-specific, limited to one or two channels, or rely on reconstruction objectives that fail to enforce a shared semantic space. No prior work has scaled contrastive cross-modal alignment to the full PSG sensor palette or characterized how performance scales with modality diversity and model capacity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263923/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 30-second PSG segments across nine modalities—four high-rate electrophysiological channels (EEG, EOG, EMG, ECG at 128 Hz) and five low-rate cardiopulmonary/interval channels (Nasal airflow, ABD/Thor belt, SpO₂, IBI, RESP at 4 Hz)—each tokenized by a modality-specific MLP into equal-dimension embeddings, alongside demographic metadata (age, gender, recording site) used only for loss weighting. Outputs include a unified 128-dimensional cross-modal embedding per timestep and a global [CLS] representation, which support five-class sleep staging, clinical disease probability, demographic prediction, and cross-modal retrieval under any subset of available modalities.

## Method

sleep2vec pairs modality-specific MLP tokenizers with a shared modality-agnostic RoFormer backbone, projecting hidden states through a common three-layer MLP head into a 128-dim alignment space where timestep-wise contrastive alignment is enforced. The key training innovation is DASH-InfoNCE, a contrastive objective that dynamically weights in-batch negatives by demographic similarity (Laplace kernel on age, binary gender/site factors) and applies a fixed margin to pseudo-negatives from the same subject-night, suppressing cohort-specific shortcuts without using any downstream labels. During pre-training, each mini-batch is constructed around a single modality pair with 15% timestep masking, and the model is trained on 42,249 overnight recordings from five cohorts. For downstream tasks, LoRA adapters (rank 8) are applied to the frozen backbone, and multi-modal features are fused via a learned gating mechanism that adaptively weights each channel's contribution.

## Results

On SHHS five-class sleep staging (W/N1/N2/N3/REM), sleep2vec achieves 88.6% accuracy, 0.84 Cohen's κ, and 79.5% Macro-F1 with full channels, outperforming the strongest foundation model baseline PFTSleep (87.7% Acc, 0.83 κ) and SleepFM (87.0% Acc, 0.82 κ). On the IBI & RESP subset, sleep2vec reaches 83.0% accuracy versus 79.7% (SleepFM) and 80.9% (SleepFounder). Cross-cohort evaluation on unseen APPLES (fine-tuned on SHHS only) yields 78.4% accuracy with full channels, a 7.0-point margin over SleepFM (71.4%), confirming robust generalization under distribution shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest foundation model baseline SleepFM (Thapa et al., 2024/2025), sleep2vec wins on every channel subset: +1.1% Acc on EEG (87.4 vs 86.3), +3.3% on IBI & RESP (83.0 vs 79.7), +4.8% on ECG & ABD (82.7 vs 77.9), and +1.6% on Full Channels (88.6 vs 87.0). Against SleepFounder (Nie et al., 2025), sleep2vec leads by +2.1% Acc on IBI & RESP (83.0 vs 80.9). Against PFTSleep (Fox et al., 2025) on Full Channels, sleep2vec edges ahead with 88.6% Acc and 0.84 κ versus 87.7% and 0.83, though PFTSleep retains a higher Macro-F1 (80.8 vs 79.5) and N1 F1 (48.6 vs 44.1). Compared to specialized non-FM models (XSleepNet1, AttnSleep, SleepTransformer), sleep2vec trails by roughly 0.2–1.4% Acc on EEG-only and EEG+EOG+EMG configurations, but the gap narrows to near-parity on κ (0.82 vs 0.83–0.85). On the unseen APPLES cross-cohort evaluation, sleep2vec's advantage over SleepFM widens to +7.0% Acc on Full Channels (78.4 vs 71.4) and +4.1% on IBI & RESP (73.2 vs 69.1), demonstrating that the DASH-InfoNCE metadata-aware objective provides substantially better distribution-shift robustness than standard contrastive pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrastive pre-training paradigm for physiological signals (SleepFM, SleepFounder) from 1–3 channels to the full nine-modality PSG spectrum, drawing conceptual inspiration from CLIP/ImageBind-style multimodal alignment in vision-language. It departs from reconstruction-based multimodal approaches (Narayanswamy et al., Nie et al.) by arguing that explicit cross-modal alignment, rather than modality-specific fidelity, is the right objective for handling heterogeneous and incomplete sensor montages. The DASH-InfoNCE loss introduces metadata-conditioned negative weighting (age, gender, site, subject-night) that has no direct analogue in prior physiological SSL work, and the paper is the first to chart scaling laws along both modality-diversity and parameter axes for nocturnal biosignals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for sleep medicine: a single pre-trained model that generalizes across arbitrary sensor subsets addresses the fragmented reality of bedside monitors, wearables, and clinical PSG devices, reducing the need for task-specific pipelines per device. However, sleep2vec still trails the best specialized EEG-only models by ~0.3–1% accuracy in in-domain settings, and clinical prediction is evaluated on only four SHHS conditions, leaving open whether the scaling trends hold for rarer diagnoses or longer-horizon outcomes. The modest but consistent cross-cohort gains on APPLES (2–4% over baselines) suggest the metadata-aware objective genuinely helps distribution shift, but the absolute performance gap to specialized models in the full-channel regime limits near-term clinical deployment.
