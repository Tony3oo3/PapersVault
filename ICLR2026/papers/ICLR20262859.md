## Problem

Existing unified visual tokenizers face an inherent conflict between high-level semantic abstraction (needed for understanding) and low-level pixel fidelity (needed for reconstruction and generation). Prior solutions either employ redundant dual encoders, force a single network to optimize competing objectives simultaneously, or rely on frozen VAE decoders that cap reconstruction quality. The paper asks how to efficiently unify both capabilities within a single tokenizer without sacrificing either.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262859/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tokenizer takes RGB images at the native resolution of the chosen vision foundation model (224×224 for CLIP, 256×256 for SigLIP2, 378×378 for DINOv2, or 448×448 for InternViT) and produces a compact 64-dimensional latent code per spatial token via a linear projection of the encoder's last-layer features. Reconstruction is performed one-step (single Euler integration) in a patch-wise manner, where each 14×14 or 16×16 pixel patch is decoded independently conditioned on its corresponding latent token. For downstream MLLM use, visual features are extracted from the second-to-last encoder layer and fed into a language model (Vicuna-7B or Qwen2.5-7B).

## Method

UniFlow combines two key mechanisms to decouple the understanding-generation trade-off. First, a Layer-wise Adaptive Self-Distillation loss aligns a fine-tuned student encoder to a frozen teacher VFM, with per-layer weights that increase with depth (hierarchical prior l/L) and are further boosted for poorly aligned layers via an exponential alignment penalty (temperature β=2), preserving semantic structure in deep layers while allowing shallow layers to adapt for fine-grained detail. Second, a lightweight Patch-wise Pixel Flow Decoder reconstructs each spatial patch independently by learning a Rectified Flow velocity field in pixel space with a 12-layer MLP, conditioned on globally coherent tokens produced by 6 Global Transformer Blocks that process the up-projected latent code. This patch-wise design eliminates the need for a pretrained VAE decoder, avoids grid artifacts through the GTB, and simplifies the data distribution for faster training. The total objective is a simple weighted sum of the distillation loss and the flow matching MSE loss (no GAN, LPIPS, or multi-scale losses), and the entire system is trained end-to-end for only 30 ImageNet-1K epochs with a fixed learning rate of 2e-4.

## Results

On 256×256 ImageNet-1K reconstruction, UniFlow(InternViT) achieves 33.23 PSNR, 0.96 SSIM, and 0.26 rFID — the best among all unified tokenizers and competitive with top generative-only VAEs (e.g., FLUX-VAE at 0.18 rFID). On multimodal understanding, UniFlow-XL (7B, Qwen2.5-7B) reaches 79.09 average across POPE, GQA, TQA, MMVet, MMBench, and MME, surpassing the 14B TokenFlow-XL (73.04) by 6.05 points. For text-to-image generation, the 0.6B UniFlow-based MMDiT achieves 0.65 GenEval and 84.76 DPG-Bench, outperforming both SANA-0.6B and the 7B TokenFlow. On visual-centric tasks, UniFlow(InternViT) attains 82.6% top-1 (linear probing), 59.2 AP (COCO detection), 0.324 RMSE (NYUv2 depth), and 55.4 mIoU (ADE20K segmentation), all setting new SOTA among self-supervised ViT-L methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniFlow(InternViT) outperforms the strongest unified tokenizer UniTok by 0.15 rFID (0.26 vs. 0.41) on ImageNet-1K reconstruction and by 0.09 gFID (2.45 vs. 2.51) on class-conditional generation without CFG, while also beating SD-VAE XL by 0.41 rFID. On multimodal understanding, UniFlow-XL (7B) surpasses the larger 14B TokenFlow-XL by 6.05 points in average score (79.09 vs. 73.04) and outperforms all other unified-tokenizer MLLMs (VILA-U, QLIP, UniTok, SemHiTok) across POPE, GQA, TQA, and MMBench. For text-to-image generation, the 0.6B UniFlow-based MMDiT edges out SANA-0.6B (0.65 vs. 0.64 GenEval; 84.76 vs. 84.30 DPG-Bench) and the 7B TokenFlow (0.65 vs. 0.55 GenEval). On visual-centric transfer tasks, UniFlow(InternViT) beats MAE by +6.8% top-1 (82.6 vs. 75.8), +1.7 AP on COCO detection (59.2 vs. 57.5), and +1.8 mIoU on ADE20K (55.4 vs. 53.6), while also outperforming MoCo v3 and BEiT across all four tasks. The only comparison where UniFlow trails is against the best generative-only VAEs (FLUX-VAE at 0.18 rFID, SD-VAE 3 at 0.20 rFID), which are not designed for understanding and use 8× downsampling rather than 14×.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniFlow builds on Rectified Flow (Liu et al., 2022) for its decoder but departs from prior unified tokenizers in two key ways. Against single-flow unified encoders (VILA-U, QLIP, UniTok) that force a single network to simultaneously optimize semantic abstraction and pixel reconstruction, UniFlow decouples these objectives via layer-wise adaptive self-distillation on the encoder and a dedicated pixel-space flow decoder. Against dual-encoder or frozen-encoder approaches (TokenFlow, EMU2, BLIP3-o) that either introduce architectural redundancy or are capped by a pretrained VAE's reconstruction ceiling, UniFlow uses a lightweight patch-wise flow decoder operating directly in pixel space with Global Transformer Blocks for spatial coherence, requiring only 30 ImageNet epochs to adapt any pretrained VFM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UniFlow provides a practical, general-purpose recipe for converting any existing vision foundation model into a unified tokenizer without sacrificing its understanding capabilities, which is valuable as the field moves toward single-model architectures for both comprehension and generation. The patch-wise flow matching formulation is notably efficient (single-step Euler decoding, no VAE dependency), making it attractive for downstream unified MLLMs. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video tokenization, whether the distillation strategy scales to much larger encoders (e.g., 6B+), and whether the patch-wise decomposition introduces limitations for very high-resolution or long-range spatial tasks.
