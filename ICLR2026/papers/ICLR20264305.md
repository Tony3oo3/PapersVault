## Problem

In continuous-control imitation learning, compounding errors can grow exponentially with task horizon, and prior lower bounds (Simchowitz et al., 2025) prove that no purely algorithmic change to the learning procedure can circumvent this. While action-chunking and exploratory data augmentation are widely deployed in practice, their precise mechanisms for preventing exponential error compounding remained theoretically unexplained, with existing hypotheses (partial observability, multi-round expert correction) not matching the observed benefits in fully-observable, state-based settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n i.i.d. expert demonstration trajectories (state-action pairs over horizon T) and, for Practice 2, a mixture of clean and noise-injected trajectories where noise is added to executed actions but recorded action labels remain noiseless. The output is a learned policy that, for Practice 1, predicts and executes open-loop action chunks of length ℓ (logarithmic in system stability constants), and for Practice 2, a standard Markovian policy trained on the mixture distribution. The theoretical output is a trajectory-error bound that is horizon-free (Practice 1) or linear in T (Practice 2) rather than exponential, expressed as a constant-factor multiple of the on-expert regression error.

## Method

The paper provides the first theoretical guarantees for two interventions that provably prevent exponential compounding error without iterative expert feedback. Practice 1 (action-chunking) shows that when open-loop dynamics are EISS, executing predicted action sequences in open-loop (not merely predicting them in receding-horizon fashion) renders the learned policy's closed-loop dynamics EISS as well, with chunk length scaling only logarithmically in system constants. Practice 2 (noise injection) addresses the harder case where open-loop dynamics are unstable: adding isotropic noise to expert actions during data collection (while keeping action labels clean) provides first-order supervision on the controllable subspace directions identified by the Jacobian linearization's controllability Gramian. The key analytical insight is that compounding error concentrates in the most excitable directions (large Gramian eigenvalues), so simple white noise suffices without requiring persistent excitation or full-dimensional coverage, and a mixture of clean and noisy trajectories avoids the additive drift error that would otherwise scale with noise magnitude.

## Results

The paper's primary contributions are theoretical: Theorem 1 proves that action-chunking with chunk length ℓ > log(1/ρ)⁻¹·log(poly(L_π, C_ISS)) yields a horizon-free trajectory error bound J_TRAJ,T ≤ O_*(1)·J_DEMO,T in open-loop stable systems. Theorem 2 proves that single-shot noise injection (σ_u = O_*(1)) on a 50/50 mixture of clean and noised expert trajectories yields J_TRAJ,T ≤ O_*(T)·J_DEMO,T even when the ambient dynamics are not open-loop stable. Empirically, on HalfCheetah-v5, naive white-noise injection (σ_u = 0.5) achieves performance on par with iterative methods like DAGGER and DART, while on the robot-mimic 'tool_hang' environment, evaluating slightly longer action chunks (beyond receding-horizon ℓ=1) drastically improves success rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its two practices against the iterative expert-querying baselines DAGGER (Ross et al., 2011) and DART (Laskey et al., 2017) on MuJoCo benchmarks. On Humanoid-v5 (Figure 1, right), both DAGGER and DART can be suboptimal: DAGGER suffers from poor learned-policy rollouts that misdirect expert queries, while DART's aggressive noise-covariance shaping over-explores in unhelpful directions; in contrast, the paper's single-shot isotropic noise injection reliably provides the necessary local exploration without iterative rounds. On HalfCheetah-v5 (Figure 3), the paper further shows that using noisy action labels (as a coverage-based RL approach would) is catastrophic, whereas recording clean expert action labels alongside noised execution (as in Practice 2) is essential for good performance. The key empirical takeaway is that a non-interactive, single-pass data collection procedure with fixed isotropic noise matches or exceeds the performance of multi-round interactive methods, validating the theoretical claim that adaptive expert queries are unnecessary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first theoretical guarantees for two widely-deployed practices in continuous-control imitation learning—action-chunking and exploratory noise injection—by building directly on the lower bounds of Simchowitz et al. (2025), which proved that no purely algorithmic change to the learning procedure can avoid exponential compounding error. The analytical framework draws on the EISS (exponentially incrementally input-to-state stable) formalism from Tu et al. (2022) and Pfrommer et al. (2022), but departs from those works by requiring neither stability oracles nor derivative sketching. Practice 2 (noise injection) is explicitly positioned as a distilled, non-iterative version of DART (Laskey et al., 2017), removing the need for multiple rounds of expert queries that DAGGER and DART require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes the rationale for action-chunking from partial observability to control-theoretic stability, and shows that a single round of fixed-scale isotropic noise injection suffices to prevent compounding error—eliminating the need for iterative expert interaction that has been the standard prescription since DAGGER. This has broad practical implications for robot learning pipelines where expert access is expensive or unavailable. Open questions include whether the smoothness assumptions can be relaxed to piecewise-smooth or non-smooth dynamics (e.g., MPC), whether isotropic noise is always optimal in highly dexterous settings, and what marginal benefit iterative interaction provides beyond the single-shot guarantee established here.
