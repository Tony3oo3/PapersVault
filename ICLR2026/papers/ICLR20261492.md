## Problem

Rectified flow inversion suffers from approximation errors that accumulate across timesteps, destabilizing the near-constant velocity field and degrading downstream reconstruction and editing quality. Existing corrections either increase neural function evaluations or fail to address the fundamental trajectory drift into low-density regions of the data distribution.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (and optionally a text prompt for conditional tasks) as input and produces a latent noise representation via inversion, a reconstructed image, or a text-guided edited image. It is training-free and plug-and-play, integrating into existing RF solvers (Euler, Heun, RF-Solver, FireFlow) without adding any extra NFEs, and has been validated on Flux.1-dev, Stable Diffusion 3.5, and SDXL-Turbo at standard latent resolutions.

## Method

PMI (Proximal-Mean Inversion) stabilizes the velocity field during inversion by computing a running time-weighted average of all past velocity predictions and applying a proximal gradient step that pulls the current velocity toward this average, constrained within a spherical Gaussian whose radius is theoretically derived from instability bounds (r_i = √(2n + 3√(2n)) · Δt_i/T + ε). The proximal objective combines an L1 local-consistency term with an L2 global-average term, solved in closed form via a first-order Taylor approximation. For editing, mimic-CFG projects the current velocity onto the running-average direction and interpolates between the projection and the original velocity with weight w (default 0.94), mirroring the structure of classifier-free guidance to balance structural consistency against editing effectiveness. Both components require no additional training or NFEs and can be seamlessly composed with any existing RF solver.

## Results

On PIE-Bench reconstruction, FireFlow+PMI achieves the best conditional PSNR of 30.42 (vs. 29.72 vanilla) and unconditional PSNR of 28.87, while RF-Solver+PMI reaches 30.72 conditional PSNR (vs. 29.17 vanilla, +1.55 dB). On the editing task, FireFlow+Ours attains the highest CLIP similarity (Whole: 25.50, Edited: 22.68) with only 18 NFE, outperforming RF-Solver+Ours (60 NFE) and RF-Inversion+Ours (56 NFE). All PMI-enhanced solvers consistently improve PSNR, SSIM, and reduce LPIPS and MSE over their vanilla counterparts across both conditional and unconditional settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PMI-enhanced solvers against their vanilla counterparts (Euler, Heun, RF-Solver, FireFlow, RF-Inversion) on PIE-Bench. PMI consistently improves all baselines: Heun gains +1.22 dB PSNR, RF-Solver gains +1.55 dB, and FireFlow gains +0.70 dB on conditional reconstruction. On editing, FireFlow+Ours achieves the best CLIP Edited similarity (22.68) with only 18 NFE, while RF-Inversion+Ours improves CLIP Whole from 25.02 to 25.44 at 56 NFE. In cross-architecture validation on SDXL-Turbo (Table B.4), DDIM+Ours outperforms GNRI (PSNR 22.49 vs. 21.96), Turbo-Edit (22.49 vs. 22.26), and Direct Inv (22.49 vs. 22.46) on background preservation, while matching or exceeding their CLIP similarity. The method also generalizes to Stable Diffusion 3.5, where FireFlow+Ours improves PSNR from 20.57 to 20.67 and CLIP Edited from 21.71 to 21.83 on editing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Rectified Flow framework (Liu et al., 2022) and the DDIM/Euler inversion paradigm (Song et al., 2022), positioning itself as a training-free velocity correction layer atop existing RF inversion solvers (RF-Solver, FireFlow, RF-Inversion). Its theoretical motivation draws from the instability analysis of Zhang et al. (2025), which shows that high-dimensional ODE mappings amplify perturbations with high probability; PMI translates this into a concrete spherical Gaussian constraint on per-step velocity corrections. Conceptually, it departs from fixed-point-iteration-based inversion methods (which trade accuracy for additional NFEs) by using a single proximal update toward a running-average velocity, and from editing methods that require auxiliary guidance (masks, attention maps) by operating purely on the velocity field with a CFG-inspired interpolation (mimic-CFG).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, zero-extra-NFE nature of PMI and mimic-CFG makes them immediately applicable to any RF-based pipeline, and the demonstrated generalization to diffusion models (SDXL-Turbo) and other RF architectures (SD 3.5) suggests broad utility. The theoretical grounding via the instability coefficient provides a principled radius for the correction, which is more defensible than heuristic tuning. Open questions include whether the running-average velocity is the optimal reference direction (EMA was tested but not systematically compared), whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation where trajectories are longer and error accumulation more severe, and whether the spherical Gaussian constraint remains tight as model scale and latent dimensionality grow. The moderate but consistent gains across four solvers and two model families indicate a reliable, if incremental, improvement that practitioners can adopt without architectural changes.
