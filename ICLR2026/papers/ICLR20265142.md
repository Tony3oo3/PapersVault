## Problem

Zero-shot diffusion-based image compression methods, while attractive for their flexibility and shared-backbone benefits, remain impractically slow—requiring 10 seconds to several minutes per image due to hundreds or thousands of denoising steps. This paper addresses the speed bottleneck by drastically reducing the number of required diffusion steps while maintaining competitive rate-distortion-perception performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265142/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB image (center-cropped from Kodak24 or DIV2K) and operates in the 16×16×4 latent space of Stable Diffusion 2.1 Base. The compressed output is a bitstream encoding, per diffusion step, the lexicographical index of an M-atom combination from a shared codebook of K=16,384 Gaussian vectors plus M sign coefficients (1 bit each). An optional per-pixel priority mask (downsampled to latent resolution) can be supplied to the encoder for ROI-aware compression without changing the bitstream format. Bitrate is controlled by a single hyperparameter M (ranging 45–300), yielding approximately 0.007–0.27 BPP with near-constant runtime (~1.3–2.8 s round-trip on an A40 GPU).

## Method

Turbo-DDCM replaces DDCM's iterative matching-pursuit noise selection with a closed-form thresholding algorithm that exploits the near-orthogonality of high-dimensional Gaussian codebook vectors. At each of T=30 denoising steps, it computes inner products between all K codebook atoms and the denoising residual, selects the top-M atoms by magnitude, and assigns each a ±1 coefficient (C=1 bit), yielding a normalized noise vector injected into the reverse diffusion process. This closed-form solution is orders of magnitude faster than DDCM's greedy MP, enabling M to scale to hundreds without runtime penalty and reducing required diffusion steps by over 92%. A new bit protocol encodes the order-invariant M-atom combination via its lexicographical index, eliminating M! redundant representations and saving ~40% bits over DDCM's ordered-index scheme. The final N steps use DDIM sampling (no bits transmitted) to improve perceptual quality at low bitrates, with N decreasing as bitrate increases.

## Results

On Kodak24 (512×512), Turbo-DDCM achieves PSNR 25.55 dB, LPIPS 0.09, and FID 22.07 at 0.148 BPP with a round-trip time of 2.53 s, compared to DDCM's 35–88 s and DiffC's 19.4 s at similar bitrates. At the low-bitrate end (0.007 BPP), Turbo-DDCM reaches PSNR 19.01 dB and FID 45.92 in 1.26 s. BD-PSNR on Kodak24 shows Turbo-DDCM outperforming DDCM by +0.76 dB and PSC by +3.35 dB, while being within −0.07 dB of DiffC. Runtime is nearly constant across bitrates (1.26–2.53 s), in contrast to DiffC (3.5–19.4 s) and DDCM (35–88 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDCM (Ohayon et al., 2025), Turbo-DDCM achieves equal or better distortion and perceptual quality while being over 34× faster at high bitrates (2.53 s vs 87.95 s at ~0.15 BPP). Against DiffC (Theis et al., 2022; Vonderfecht & Liu, 2025 CUDA kernel), Turbo-DDCM is ~3× faster at low bitrates and nearly 10× faster at high bitrates, with nearly identical distortion and perceptual quality at high bitrates, though DiffC edges out slightly on FID at low bitrates. Turbo-DDCM clearly surpasses PSC (Elata et al., 2024) in both PSNR and FID across all tested bitrates. Among non-zero-shot methods, Turbo-DDCM is faster than HiFiC (2.53 s vs 19.99 s), DiffEIC (2.53 s vs 5.25 s), CRDR (2.53 s vs 3.89 s), and PerCo(SD) (2.53 s vs 2.76 s), while BPG (0.16–0.24 s), ILLM (0.32 s), and StableCodec (0.37 s) are faster but sacrifice perceptual quality (FID 82–148 for BPG, 28–128 for ILLM) or require per-bitrate specialized models (StableCodec). BD-PSNR confirms Turbo-DDCM is competitive with or superior to all zero-shot baselines and within ~0.5 dB of the best non-zero-shot methods on the rate-distortion-perception plane.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DDCM (Ohayon et al., 2025), which introduced reproducible Gaussian codebooks into the reverse diffusion process for zero-shot compression, by replacing DDCM's greedy matching-pursuit atom combination with a closed-form thresholding solution that exploits the near-orthogonality of high-dimensional Gaussian vectors. The key conceptual departure is that sparse least squares with a quantization constraint admits an efficient non-iterative solution when the codebook is approximately orthogonal, eliminating the iterative exhaustive search that made DDCM's MP strategy scale poorly. The paper also introduces a combinatorial bit-coding protocol that removes factorial redundancy in encoding, and two variants (priority-aware and distortion-controlled) that have no precedent in the zero-shot diffusion compression literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: reducing round-trip compression time from ~65 s (DDCM) to ~1.8 s without custom hardware optimizations makes zero-shot diffusion compression viable for real-world use, while maintaining competitive rate-distortion-perception performance. The priority-aware and distortion-controlled variants address real deployment needs (medical imaging, variable-quality targets) that prior zero-shot methods could not handle. However, the method remains bounded by the SD 2.1 encoder-decoder distortion ceiling at high bitrates, and training-based one-step methods can match quality in a single forward pass, suggesting that a one-step zero-shot variant would be the natural next step. A comprehensive theoretical framework for DDCM-based compression remains an open problem.
