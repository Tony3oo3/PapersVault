## Problem

Most hyperbolic neural networks operate in the constrained Poincaré ball or hyperboloid models, where numerical instabilities arise near model boundaries (gradient vanishing in the Poincaré ball, off-manifold drift in the hyperboloid). The Proper Velocity (PV) model, an unconstrained representation of hyperbolic space rooted in special relativity, offers a natural remedy, but its Riemannian operators—exponential map, logarithmic map, geodesic distance, and parallel transport—had not been derived, preventing its use in neural network construction.

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images (32×32, CIFAR-10/100), graph node features with adjacency structure (Disease, Airport, PubMed, Cora), and DNA sequences (length up to 1000, 4 nucleotide channels, TEB benchmark), and produces class labels, node class labels, or transposable element types respectively. A key practical detail is that because PV space is unconstrained (isomorphic to R^n), inputs can be fed directly as PV coordinates without an exponential-map lift, though the authors also evaluate a variant that applies Exp_0 first. All network parameters reside in Euclidean space and are optimized with standard Euclidean optimizers (SGD or Adam).

## Method

The paper first proves that the PV model and Poincaré ball are Riemannian isometric via the bijection x ↦ (β_x/(1+β_x))x, then derives closed-form PV Riemannian operators (exp, log, distance, parallel transport) by transporting Poincaré operators through this isometry. Building on these operators, the authors construct five core layers: a PV MLR classifier whose scores reduce to inner products ⟨x, z_k⟩ (enabling efficient matrix-multiplication implementation), a PV FC layer with closed-form output y_k = (1/√(−K)) sinh(√(−K)·v_k(x)), a PV convolution that exploits the unconstrained nature of PV space to use standard Euclidean concatenation before a PV FC, a PV activation applied directly in PV space (avoiding exp/log maps), and a PV GyroBN layer that normalizes Fréchet mean and variance using gyroaddition and gyromultiplication. The homogeneity theorem guarantees that GyroBN exactly normalizes batch statistics, and the Euclidean limit K→0⁻ recovers standard MLR/FC layers.

## Results

On graph node classification, PVNN achieves the best accuracy on three of four datasets: Disease (81.15%), Airport (97.96%), and PubMed (74.33%), with the largest margin on Airport (+5.86% over the strongest baseline HNN++). On image classification, PV MLR reaches 95.30% on CIFAR-10 and 78.20% on CIFAR-100, outperforming all prior hyperbolic MLR baselines. On genomic sequence learning (TEB), PVCNN achieves the best MCC on all five tasks, with a particularly strong 9.33-point gain over HCNN-S on SINEs (93.78 vs. 85.45). Numerical stability experiments confirm PV maintains zero failure rate up to r=1000 in FP32, while the hyperboloid model fails at 100% by r=20.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PVNN outperforms all four graph baselines (KNN, HNN, HNN++, LNN) on the three strongly hyperbolic datasets (Disease, Airport, PubMed), with the most dramatic gain on Airport where it beats HNN++ by 9.56 points (97.96 vs. 88.40) and LNN by 22.76 points. On the weakly hyperbolic Cora dataset, PVNN (51.42%) is slightly below LNN (53.34%) and HNN++ (52.06%), suggesting PV geometry is most advantageous for strongly hierarchical structures. For image classification, PV MLR (95.30% CIFAR-10, 78.20% CIFAR-100) surpasses Poincaré MLR (Ganea, 95.09/76.78), Unidirectional MLR (Shimizu, 95.12/77.19), and Lorentz MLR (Bdeir, 95.02/77.96), with the largest margin on CIFAR-100 (+1.42 over Ganea). On genomic sequence learning, PVCNN dominates both Euclidean CNN and HCNN-S (Khan et al.) across all five TEB tasks, with the largest improvement of ~8.3 MCC points on SINEs (93.78 vs. 85.45). Overall, PVNN wins or ties on 11 of 12 main comparison cells, losing only on Cora to the hyperboloid-based LNN.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Ungar's gyrovector space theory to establish the first complete Riemannian toolkit (exponential map, logarithmic map, parallel transport, geodesic distance) for the Proper Velocity model, a representation of hyperbolic space rooted in special relativity. The derivation leverages a newly proven Riemannian isometry between PV space and the Poincaré ball, allowing all PV operators to be obtained from the well-known Poincaré closed-form expressions. The neural layer designs (MLR, FC, convolution, GyroBN) directly extend the parameterization tricks of Shimizu et al. (2021) and the gyro-normalization framework of Chen et al. (2024, 2025) to the PV setting. Notably, Appendix D reveals that standard hyperboloid (Lorentz) layers are algebraically equivalent to PV layers composed with a simple coordinate projection, reframing prior hyperboloid network designs as implicit PV constructions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unconstrained nature of PV space (it is simply R^n) eliminates the boundary numerical instabilities that plague the Poincaré ball and hyperboloid models, a practical advantage confirmed by clean experiments showing zero failure rates up to r=1000 in FP32 versus 100% failure for the hyperboloid. However, the empirical gains over strong hyperbolic baselines are modest (1–2% on most tasks), and the evaluation is limited to small-scale benchmarks (CIFAR, small graphs, TEB) without ImageNet, large-scale graphs, or NLP. Open questions include whether the stability advantage persists at scale, whether PVNNs can be extended to residual architectures and transformers (mentioned as future work), and whether the fixed single-curvature assumption limits expressiveness compared to mixed-curvature approaches.
