## Problem

Research on irregular time series is fragmented across communities (mobility, healthcare, environmental science), each using incompatible data formats and isolated toolsets, with no standardized benchmark for classification on naturally irregular data. Existing evaluations rely on artificially manipulated regular datasets or a narrow set of sources, leaving the generalizability of specialized irregular-time-series methods largely untested.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262839/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts raw irregular time series in any source format, requiring only a user-defined function that yields rows in a long format (instance ID, signal ID, timestamp, value). Internally, data is stored as a sparse COO tensor augmented with xarray for real-valued timestamps and static attributes, distinguishing explicit NaNs (expected-but-missing observations) from implicit NaNs (raggedness padding). For downstream classification, the container is densified via a per-instance dense ranking of timestamp indices, producing a compact tensor X′ ∈ R̊^(n×d×T) where T = max_i(T_i), minimizing raggedness while preserving structural missingness.

## Method

The paper introduces pyrregular, a three-stage framework: (1) preprocessing maps arbitrary raw data into a sparse COO tensor by converting real-valued timestamps to discrete indices; (2) handling stores the tensor within xarray via a custom backend and accessor, enabling array operations, plotting, and direct I/O while natively encoding all three irregularity types (uneven sampling, partial observation, raggedness) and their subtypes; (3) converting applies a per-instance dense ranking to timestamp indices to produce minimally ragged dense tensors compatible with standard libraries (sktime, aeon, pypots, tslearn, diffrax). The paper formalizes a taxonomy proving the five irregularity forms are pairwise independent, then benchmarks 12 classifiers from diverse method families (gradient-boosted trees, kernel methods, RNNs, transformers, GNNs, neural ODEs, inception networks, distance-based) on 34 naturally irregular datasets, finding that the simple regular-time-series method ROCKET outperforms specialized ITS models in bake-off settings, while deep learning methods (e.g., MTSFORMER, MUSICNET) still lead after task-specific fine-tuning on medical datasets.

## Results

In the bake-off benchmark (default hyperparameters) across 34 irregular time series datasets, ROCKET emerges as the clear top-performing model in terms of macro F1, followed by a statistically tied cluster of BORF, LGBM, RIFC, and TIMESNET. SVM is the worst-performing model. In the fine-tuned comparison on the two most common medical datasets (P12 and P19), deep learning specialists dominate: MUSICNET achieves 86.1 AUC on P12 and 86.8 on P19, while MTSFORMER reaches 84.9 and 88.8 respectively. Notably, LGBM remains competitive after fine-tuning, achieving 85.2 AUC on P19 (4th best), while ROCKET drops sharply to 53.4 AUC on P12.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the default-parameter bake-off across all 34 datasets, ROCKET (a regular time series method that ignores irregularity) outperforms all 11 other classifiers, including ITS-specific models like NCDE, GRU-D, BRITS, RAINDROP, SAITS, and TIMESNET. BORF, LGBM, RIFC, and TIMESNET form a statistically tied second tier. However, for partially observed time series specifically, SAITS and BRITS outperform ROCKET, BORF, and LGBM, showing that explicit missingness modeling is beneficial for structured missingness. After fine-tuning on P12 and P19, the ranking inverts: MUSICNET (86.1 AUC) and MTSFORMER (84.9 AUC) surpass all generalist models, while ROCKET collapses to 53.4 AUC on P12. LGBM remains the strongest generalist after tuning, reaching 85.2 AUC on P19 (4th overall), outperforming CONTIFORMER (79.2) and GRU-D (83.9). The paper concludes that simple generalist models excel in bake-off settings due to low-variance inductive bias, while specialist deep learning models win when fine-tuned for specific tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on xarray's labeled-array infrastructure and sparse COO tensor representations to create a unified container for irregular time series, bridging the fragmented ecosystems of pypots (partially observed data), diffrax (unevenly sampled data), and sktime/aeon/tslearn (regular data). Conceptually, it extends the regular time series bake-off paradigm (Bagnall et al., 2017; Middlehurst et al., 2024b) to inherently irregular data, while departing from prior ITS work that typically addresses only one type of irregularity in isolation. The taxonomy of three independent irregularity causes (uneven sampling, partial observation, raggedness) formalizes distinctions that have appeared informally across mobility, healthcare, and sensor literature but had never been shown to be mutually independent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first standardized benchmark and dataset repository for irregular time series classification (34 datasets, 12 models), filling a gap analogous to what UEA/UCR provided for regular time series. The finding that generalist models like ROCKET outperform specialized ITS architectures in bake-off settings highlights a critical research gap: the need for generalist methods that explicitly exploit timestamp and missingness information. Key limitations include the restriction to classification (forecasting, imputation, anomaly detection are deferred), default hyperparameters that may understate specialized model potential, and the absence of emerging generalist temporal models (LLM-based, multimodal pretraining). The framework's extensibility to other tasks and its memory-efficient representation make it a likely infrastructure for future ITS research.
