## Problem

Training LLM routers requires reliable quality supervision, but gold-standard evaluations (expert rubrics, verified labels) are scarce and expensive while preference-based evaluations (crowdsourced, LLM-as-a-judge) are scalable yet systematically biased relative to true quality. Existing routers either rely on a single data source or naively pool both, inheriting the preference bias and degrading routing accuracy, especially in expert domains like healthcare and law where open-ended queries make gold labels particularly costly.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two training datasets: a small gold-standard set D_G of (query, quality-gain) pairs scored by rubric-based expert evaluation, and a larger preference-based set D_P of (query, pairwise-comparison) pairs from LLM-as-a-judge or crowdsourced ratings. Queries are embedded via gemini-embedding-001 into 768-dimensional vectors (optionally PCA-reduced to 20–100 dimensions) and served as tabular features. At inference, a single incoming text query is embedded and passed through the trained router, which outputs a binary routing decision—assign the query to the premium model M_p or the cheaper alternative M_a—by comparing the estimated gold-standard quality gain against a user-specified cost threshold w.

## Method

The core insight is to cast the choice of evaluation mechanism (gold-standard vs. preference-based) as a binary treatment assignment in a causal inference framework, so that the systematic bias between the two evaluation types is exactly the conditional average treatment effect (CATE) Δ(q) = ψ(q) − η(q). The meta-router is a two-step procedure: first, Δ(·) is estimated from the pooled dataset using causal meta-learners (R-learner or DR-learner) that enjoy quasi-oracle efficiency under mild nuisance-estimation conditions; second, the estimated shift is added to each preference-based label to produce pseudo-gold-standard labels, and a nonparametric regression (random forest, XGBoost, or neural network) is fit on the union of true and pseudo gold-standard labels to recover ψ(·). This causal reframing makes the debiasing principled rather than ad hoc, handles heterogeneous bias across the query space, and inherits the double-robustness and sample-efficiency guarantees of the underlying CATE estimators.

## Results

Results are reported as efficiency gain (EG) curves versus primary model usage ratio (PMUR) across GS sample sizes n ∈ {100, 500, 1000} on HealthBench (5,000 queries) and n ∈ {50, 100, 150} on PRBench (676 queries), averaged over 200 Monte Carlo rounds with 500 held-out test queries. The meta-router (R-learner or DR-learner) consistently achieves the highest EG among all non-oracle methods, with the advantage most pronounced at the smallest GS sample sizes. The PB-only predictive router shows essentially no improvement over random routing, while the pooled GS+PB predictive router (following RouteLLM) yields only marginal gains even at n=1000. On PRBench, the DR-learner-based meta-router consistently outperforms the R-learner variant, highlighting superior sample efficiency under very limited GS supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares seven routing strategies: an oracle benchmark (upper bound with full GS labels), two meta-routers (R-learner and DR-learner), three predictive routers (pooled GS+PB, GS-only, PB-only), and a random router. The RouteLLM-style baselines — the pooled GS+PB predictive router and the PB-only predictive router (Ong et al., 2024) — show little to no efficiency improvement over random routing across all GS sample sizes, confirming that directly combining biased preference data with gold-standard data without debiasing is ineffective. The GS-only predictive router improves monotonically with n but remains well below the meta-router at every sample size, demonstrating the value of incorporating (debiased) preference data. The meta-router via R-learner or DR-learner outperforms all non-oracle baselines on both HealthBench and PRBench, with the gap over the pooled-data baseline being largest at n=100 (HealthBench) and n=50 (PRBench). On PRBench specifically, the DR-learner meta-router dominates while the R-learner offers no clear advantage, suggesting DR-learner's doubly-robust property is more critical under extreme sample scarcity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LLM routing literature (RouteLLM, TensorOpera Router, Causal LLM Routing) by reframing the training problem through a causal inference lens: the evaluation mechanism (gold-standard vs. preference-based) is treated as a binary treatment, and the systematic bias between the two data sources is identified as the conditional average treatment effect (CATE). It then imports R-learner and DR-learner from the semiparametric causal estimation literature (Nie & Wager, 2021; Kennedy, 2023) as the debiasing engine, departing from prior routers that either pool GS and PB data naively or rely solely on preference data. The Bradley-Terry-Luce model and LLM-as-a-judge paradigms inform the preference-data generation model, while the routing decision rule follows the cost-quality utility framework established by Ong et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, statistically grounded method for combining scarce gold-standard labels with abundant but biased preference data in LLM router training, which is a practical bottleneck in expert-domain deployments (healthcare, law, finance). Key limitations include the restriction to pairwise routing (multi-model extension is sketched but not fully developed), the reliance on an LLM (GPT-5-mini) as a proxy for true expert gold-standard evaluation, and the common-support (positivity) requirement that may fail in practice. Open questions include the truncation-based extension for positivity violation, semi-supervised and active-learning extensions for adaptive GS data collection, and whether the CATE-based debiasing generalizes to more complex multi-model routing with structural assumptions like BTL ranking.
