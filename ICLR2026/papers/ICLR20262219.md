## Problem

3D vision lacks a scalable rendering paradigm due to two interconnected bottlenecks: a fragmented landscape of incompatible 3D representations that prevents focused architectural scaling, and the high cost of 3D data requiring strict geometric consistency. The paper argues that 3D perception is better understood as visual common sense learned from observation rather than explicit geometric modelling, and that 3D can be treated as a specialised sub-domain of video, enabling transfer of spatial understanding from large-scale video data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262219/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Kaleido accepts an arbitrary number of reference RGB images paired with their 6-DoF camera extrinsics (SE(3) poses) and generates an arbitrary number of target views at user-specified 6-DoF poses, all within a single forward pass. Images are encoded by a pre-trained VAE (8×8 spatial compression, 16 latent channels) and further tokenised at 2×2 patch size (16×16 total compression); the model outputs photorealistic renderings up to 1024px across mixed aspect ratios (1:1, 4:5, 5:4, 16:9, 9:16). The same architecture processes video data by replacing 3D poses with temporal frame indices, requiring no structural modification.

## Method

Kaleido is a decoder-only rectified flow transformer (DiT-inspired with Llama-3 SwiGLU and GQA) that frames rendering as a masked autoregressive sequence-to-sequence task: clean reference latents and noised target latents are processed jointly, with a single timestep t sampled per scene and injected via AdaIN layers. The key architectural innovation is a unified positional encoding that extends RoPE and GTA to represent 2D pixel coordinates, 3D camera poses (SE(3)), and temporal frame indices within a single bounded parameter-free scheme, enabling seamless joint training on video and multi-view 3D data without any task-specific modifications. Training follows a two-stage curriculum: large-scale video pre-training (34M clips at 256px) followed by progressive-resolution 3D fine-tuning (256→512→1024px) on ~3.5M object and scene sequences. A noise-biased shifted-Mode timestep sampler for training and a linear-quadratic schedule for inference concentrate denoising effort on early high-noise steps where coarse scene structure is established, while learnable register tokens suppress massive activations that cause training instability at high resolutions.

## Results

On GSO-30 (10 reference views), Kaleido (3.1B) achieves 33.74 PSNR, outperforming EscherNet (25.90) by +7.8 dB. On Mip-NeRF 360 (6 views), Kaleido reaches 18.03 PSNR versus SEVA's 16.70 (+1.3 dB). Notably, even Kaleido-Small (0.6B) matches or surpasses the larger SEVA (1.5B) and SV3D (2.3B) baselines across all benchmarks. In many-view settings, Kaleido becomes the first zero-shot generative model to match per-scene optimisation methods (Instant-NGP, 3DGS) on NeRF-Synthetic and surpass them on LLFF and Mip-NeRF 360.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Kaleido decisively outperforms all generative NVS baselines: on GSO-30 (10 views) it beats EscherNet by +7.8 dB PSNR (33.74 vs 25.90), and on Mip-NeRF 360 (6 views) it surpasses SEVA by +1.3 dB (18.03 vs 16.70); even Kaleido-Small (0.6B) exceeds SV3D (2.3B) on OO3D (19.77 vs 19.28). Against per-scene optimisation methods, Kaleido matches Instant-NGP on NeRF-Synthetic and surpasses it on LLFF and Mip-NeRF 360 when given all available reference views, while 3DGS remains faster but Kaleido avoids representation-based artefacts. In 3D reconstruction (Table 3), Kaleido outperforms all image-to-3D baselines (Point-E, Shape-E, One-2-3-45, DreamGaussian, SyncDreamer, NeuS2, EscherNet) on both Chamfer Distance and Volumetric IoU, achieving with 2 views what EscherNet requires 10 views to match. In the camera-conditioned video comparison (Table 8), Kaleido achieves dramatically lower rotation error (0.011 vs 0.061 for Wonderland) and translation error (0.026 vs 0.130) than all video generation baselines (MotionCtrl, VD3D, ViewCrafter, Wonderland), while maintaining competitive visual quality (PSNR 18.09 vs 17.56 for Wonderland).

## Relation to Prior Work

Kaleido builds on the DiT/SiT transformer architecture and Llama-3 components (SwiGLU, GQA) for its backbone, and extends RoPE-style positional encodings (via GTA and CaPE) to create a unified representation for 2D image positions, 3D camera poses, and temporal video positions within a single decoder-only rectified flow transformer. It departs from prior generative NVS methods (EscherNet, SEVA, CAT3D) that rely on U-Net backbones, Plücker ray encodings, and separate vision encoders, instead adopting a pure transformer design with no 3D-specific architectural modifications. The key conceptual departure is treating 3D rendering as a sequence-to-sequence problem analogous to language or video generation, enabling large-scale video pre-training to transfer spatial common sense to 3D view synthesis without any task-specific architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Kaleido demonstrates for the first time that a zero-shot generative model can match the rendering quality of per-scene optimisation methods (Instant-NGP) in many-view settings, challenging the assumption that per-scene fitting is necessary for high-fidelity rendering. The finding that video pre-training yields 1.3×–2× efficiency gains in 3D fine-tuning validates the broader hypothesis that 3D perception is a form of visual common sense learnable from unstructured video, potentially reducing reliance on scarce camera-labelled 3D datasets. Key limitations include inference speed (still orders of magnitude slower than deterministic methods like 3DGS) and the current separation of spatial and temporal modelling; a truly unified 4D (space+time) model remains an open direction.
