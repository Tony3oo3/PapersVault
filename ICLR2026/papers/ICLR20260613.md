## Problem

Existing LLM unlearning methods based on gradient ascent and its variants suffer from a loss-of-control issue: they cannot reliably distinguish in-scope from out-of-scope data (leading to either incomplete removal or catastrophic over-erasure), and they produce incoherent or nonsensical outputs instead of meaningful refusals. The root cause is the absence of explicit guidance on what knowledge should be removed and how the model should respond after unlearning.

## Input / Output

TRU takes text queries from unlearning datasets and produces structured outputs consisting of a reasoning trace followed by a coherent refusal response, formatted with special <think> and <answer> tokens appended to the backbone model's tokenizer. The unlearning targets are triplets (x_u, r_rt, s_rt) where the reasoning trace r_rt is a logical analysis identifying the underlying knowledge in x_u and the refusal s_rt is a constructive, explainable rejection. For out-of-scope queries, the model produces normal answers as usual.

## Method

TRU introduces reasoning-based unlearning targets, generated automatically by DeepSeek-reasoner, where each unlearning data point is paired with a reasoning trace that exposes the underlying knowledge and a coherent refusal response. The training objective combines a cross-entropy supervised loss on these targets (teaching the model to reason about scope membership and produce structured refusals) with a GA-based loss (default: GradDiff) for thorough parameter-level knowledge erasure, balanced by a hyperparameter α. The key insight is that reasoning traces capture the semantic knowledge behind individual data points, enabling the model to generalize to rephrased, translated, or structurally varied queries within the unlearning scope while preserving out-of-scope capabilities. Ablations confirm that removing the reasoning component causes the model to learn rigid refusal patterns without discriminative ability, leading to excessive unlearning and retention collapse.

## Results

On WMDP-Bio, TRU achieves Unlearning Quality scores of Rel=6.72, Rej=6.56, Help=7.75, substantially outperforming the best baseline RMU (Rel=2.89, Rej=2.89, Help=0.01) while maintaining Retention Quality (Read=7.13, Spe=2.56, Logic=2.88) close to the base model (Read=5.53, Spe=3.45, Logic=3.64). On WMDP-Cyber, TRU reaches Rel=7.19, Rej=8.81, Help=9.17, again far exceeding all baselines. Across MUSE-Books and MUSE-News, TRU similarly dominates UQ (e.g., Rel=7.55, Rej=8.45 on MUSE-Books) while GA, GradDiff, and KL collapse to near-zero on both UQ and RQ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRU decisively outperforms all five baseline families on Unlearning Quality across every benchmark. On WMDP-Bio, TRU's Helpfulness (7.75) exceeds the next-best PO (0.02) by over 7 points, and its Rejection score (6.56) is more than double RMU's (2.89). GA, GradDiff, and KL all collapse to near-zero on both UQ and RQ, confirming catastrophic model degradation. RMU preserves the best Retention Quality on WMDP-Bio (Spe=6.42, Logic=5.86) but achieves negligible unlearning (Help=0.01), while TRU trades a modest RQ drop (Spe 3.45→2.56) for a large UQ gain. On WMDP-Cyber, TRU's Rejection (8.81) and Helpfulness (9.17) are an order of magnitude above the strongest baseline (PO: Rej=3.76, Help=0.10). On MUSE-Books, TRU leads all baselines in UQ (Rel=7.55, Rej=8.45, Help=9.13) while PO, the closest competitor, achieves only Rel=4.10 and Help=0.08.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient-ascent (GA) family of LLM unlearning methods (GradDiff, NPO, WGA, PO, RMU) by introducing reasoning-based unlearning targets that explicitly specify both the unlearning scope and the desired post-unlearning response. Prior GA-based methods only suppress the likelihood of unlearning data without guiding what the model should produce instead, leading to incoherent outputs and uncontrolled scope. TRU departs from this by combining a cross-entropy supervised loss on reasoning traces (generated by DeepSeek-R1) with the conventional GA-based loss, framing the problem as 'scope unlearning' (Problem 2) rather than mere 'data unlearning' (Problem 1). The conceptual lineage draws from supervised fine-tuning with reasoning datasets (s1, DeepSeek-R1) to endow non-reasoning backbones with discriminative ability over the unlearning boundary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is among the first works to treat unlearning as a controllable, explainable process rather than a blind suppression task, and the reasoning-based target mechanism provides a practical recipe for specifying scope without manual annotation. The robustness results (cross-lingual, jailbreak, relearning) suggest the learned reasoning generalizes beyond the training distribution, which is critical for real-world deployment. Limitations include dependence on an external reasoning LLM for target generation (cost and circularity concerns, though partially addressed), and retention quality still drops noticeably on some benchmarks (e.g., TOFU-Forget10). Open questions include whether reasoning targets can be generated autonomously by the target model, scalability to very large unlearning corpora, and extension to multimodal unlearning.
