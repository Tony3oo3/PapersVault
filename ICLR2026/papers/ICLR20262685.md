## Problem

Post-training quantization (PTQ) methods that work well for autoregressive LLMs degrade sharply when transferred to diffusion LLMs at 2-bit precision. Two specific mismatches drive this failure: timestep-dependent masking shifts activation distributions away from the fully-visible signals assumed by standard calibration, and quantization errors accumulate across denoising steps, becoming most severe at later stages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262685/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a pre-trained dLLM (e.g., LLaDA-8B, Dream-7B) and 128 calibration sequences of 4096 tokens from C4, with no gradient updates or fine-tuning. The output is a set of 2-bit quantized weight matrices, each represented as a sum of K binary matrices modulated by separable row-column scaling vectors (α_r, α_c), where K varies per block (1, 2, or 3) under a strict per-layer 2-bit average constraint. Group size is fixed at 128, and the RC-scaled binary format imposes less memory overhead than conventional zero-point-plus-scale quantization.

## Method

Quant-dLLM is a three-component, training-free PTQ pipeline. First, Masked Calibration Simulation (MCS) constructs timestep-aware calibration batches by sampling visibility ratios from the diffusion schedule and masking non-prefix tokens with Bernoulli(α(t)), producing activation statistics that match the denoising process. Second, the Data-aware Any-order Quantizer (DAQ) approximates each weight matrix as a superposition of K RC-scaled binary matrices, optimized via a Data-aware Objective Reformulation (DOR) that distills the second-moment calibration matrix into a sparse 3σ-outlier importance mask Λ, and Row-column Successive Re-scaling (RSR) that alternates closed-form updates for row and column scales followed by per-entry binary sign search. Third, Adaptive Blockwise Mixed Precision (ABMP) ranks blocks by aggregate importance score and assigns 3-bit to the top-5% and 1-bit to the bottom-5% while keeping the layer average at exactly 2-bit, concentrating representational capacity on the most sensitive regions.

## Results

Under strict 2-bit weight-only quantization, Quant-dLLM achieves an average accuracy of 51.3 across five dLLM models (LLaDA-8B-Base, LLaDA-8B-Instruct, LLaDA-1.5, Dream-7B-Base, Dream-7B-Instruct) on seven general-knowledge benchmarks, surpassing the best baseline Slim-LLM (40.9) by over 10 points. On LLaDA-8B-Base, the average rises from 42.39 (Slim-LLM) to 54.06, recovering 87.7% of the full-precision score (61.46). On math/science and code tasks, Quant-dLLM is the only method that retains meaningful accuracy (>30% on math, >15% on code for LLaDA-Instruct), while all baselines collapse below 12% and near 0% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Quant-dLLM outperforms all three baselines (GPTQ, GPTAQ, Slim-LLM) on every model and every task in the main comparison. Against the strongest baseline Slim-LLM, the average improvement is +11.4 points (51.3 vs 40.9), with the largest gains on Dream-7B-Base (+13.7) and Dream-7B-Instruct (+15.1), where standard AR-transferred PTQ methods degrade most severely. GPTQ and GPTAQ perform nearly identically to each other (within ~1 point) and are consistently 10–15 points below Quant-dLLM, indicating that Hessian-guided error compensation alone is insufficient for dLLMs at 2-bit. On math/science reasoning (GSM8K, MATH, GPQA), Quant-dLLM achieves >30% average on LLaDA-Instruct while all baselines remain below 12%; on code generation (HumanEval, MBPP), Quant-dLLM reaches >15% Pass@1 on LLaDA models while baselines hover near 0%. The advantage is consistent across both the LLaDA family (bidirectional transformer denoiser) and the Dream family (initialized from AR pre-training), confirming the method's generality across dLLM architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RC-scaled binarization framework from DB-LLM (Chen et al., 2024) and the data-aware second-moment objective reformulation from ARB-LLM (Li et al., 2025b) to the diffusion LLM setting. It departs from the recent systematic transfer of AR-PTQ methods to dLLMs by Lin et al. (2025), which showed 4-bit works well but 2-bit collapses, by identifying the root cause as a calibration–inference distribution mismatch introduced by timestep-dependent masking. The key conceptual departure is that standard PTQ assumes fully visible token activations, whereas dLLM inference operates under a visibility schedule that shifts activation statistics across denoising steps, necessitating a diffusion-aligned calibration procedure (MCS) and an importance-guided mixed-precision allocation (ABMP) not present in any prior AR-PTQ method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that 2-bit weight-only quantization for dLLMs, previously considered infeasible, can retain a large fraction of full-precision accuracy (e.g., 87.7% of FP on LLaDA-8B-Base), opening the door to deploying billion-parameter diffusion LLMs on memory-constrained hardware. Limitations include evaluation only on 7B–8B models, weight-only (no activation) quantization, and a single calibration dataset (C4); whether the approach transfers to larger dLLMs, to activation quantization, or to other discrete-diffusion architectures remains open. The finding that a mere 5% block-level precision reallocation yields most of the ABMP gain suggests the sensitivity landscape in dLLMs is highly concentrated, which could inform future structured-sparsity or pruning strategies for diffusion models.
