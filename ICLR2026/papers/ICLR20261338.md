## Problem

Existing copula models impose restrictive assumptions that limit their effectiveness on high-dimensional and multimodal dependencies: Gaussian copulas capture only diagonal symmetric dependence, vine copulas explore an exponential model space, and deep copulas suffer from mode collapse and require expensive MCMC sampling that scales poorly with dimension. No prior copula method can scale to complex dependencies in dimensions exceeding 1000, such as those found in image data.

## Input / Output

The method takes copula observations u ∈ [0,1]^d (obtained by applying known marginal CDFs to raw data) and internally maps them to Gaussian-scale z ∈ R^d via the inverse standard normal CDF. The c_dc model outputs a vector of k class probabilities per input point, from which the copula density is computed as a single probability ratio (first-to-last class) and the copula score is derived from gradients of these probabilities. The reflection copula outputs a learned velocity field v(u,t) that, when integrated backwards from uniform samples at a large terminal time T, produces copula samples. For image experiments, a small Gaussian noise is added to dequantise pixel values before computing copula ranks.

## Method

The paper designs two forward processes that provably forget inter-variable dependence while preserving uniform marginals at all times: (1) an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process on the Gaussian scale that converges to the independence copula at rate O(e^{-2t}), and (2) a reflection process on [0,1]^d where samples bounce off hypercube boundaries with random Gaussian velocities, also converging to independence. Two models then 'remember' the forgotten dependencies: the classification-diffusion copula (c_dc) trains a multinomial classifier to identify the diffusion time of a sample, yielding the copula density as a probability ratio and enabling score-based sampling via Langevin dynamics; the reflection copula learns the expected velocity v*(u,t) = E[v_t|u_t=u] via MSE regression, generating samples by solving the corresponding ODE backwards from uniform initialization. Both models provably recover the true copula at optimality, and the c_dc model's diffusion-based sampling scales more efficiently to high dimensions than the MCMC required by prior deep copula methods.

## Results

On high-dimensional image data (the paper's headline contribution), the classification-diffusion copula (c_dc) achieves the best copula log-likelihood across all three datasets: 13.80 on digits (d=64), 346.70 on MNIST (d=784), and 1470.75 on Cifar (d=1024), substantially outperforming the Ratio copula (334.42 on MNIST) and Gaussian copula (115.84 on MNIST). For sample quality, c_dc achieves the best W2 on digits (6.97) and MNIST (33.64), while the Reflection copula achieves the best FID on digits (5.50) and Cifar (42.14). On scientific datasets (d=10–20), c_dc achieves the best LL (e.g., 18.65 on Magic vs. 6.76 for Ratio) and best W2 (1.33 on Magic), with Frob comparable to the Gaussian copula. The Vine copula fails entirely on Cifar (NaN LL), and IGC produces the worst FID on Cifar (269.68).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The c_dc copula dominates on copula log-likelihood across all six datasets, with the largest margins on high-dimensional data (e.g., 346.70 vs. 334.42 for Ratio and 115.84 for Gaussian on MNIST). The Reflection copula, which only produces samples, achieves the best FID on digits (5.50 vs. 5.74 Gaussian) and Cifar (42.14 vs. 100.04 Vine), and the best W2 on Cifar (32.40 vs. 30.62 Gaussian is actually slightly worse, but 32.40 vs. 49.91 Ratio). The Gaussian copula is the strongest classical baseline on Frob (pairwise Kendall's tau) for scientific datasets but is consistently outperformed on LL and W2. The Ratio copula (Huk et al., 2025), the closest prior method, is beaten on both LL and W2 across all datasets, with particularly large gaps on Cifar (W2: 49.91 vs. 28.67). The IGC (Janke et al., 2021) is the weakest deep baseline, producing the worst FID on Cifar (269.68) and failing to provide density evaluations. The Vine copula, while competitive on Frob for low-dimensional data, fails entirely on Cifar (NaN LL) and is outperformed on all other metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ratio copula of Huk et al. (2025), which framed copula density estimation as binary classification between dependent and independent data, into a multinomial setting where a diffusion-based spectrum of decaying dependence levels replaces the binary dichotomy. The classification-diffusion copula adapts the density-ratio-via-classification technique of Yadin et al. (2024) to copula densities, while the reflection copula repurposes the velocity-prediction framework of Holderrieth et al. (2024) for generative copula sampling on [0,1]^d. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior deep copula work (moment-matching networks, GANs, normalising flows) by designing forward processes that provably preserve uniform marginals while forgetting dependence, ensuring valid copula states at all times rather than relying on optimality for validity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of copula models scaling to high-dimensional (d > 1000) and multimodal dependencies, a regime where vine copulas fail and prior deep copulas suffer mode collapse. The dual contribution of density estimation (c_dc) and efficient sampling (reflection copula) addresses a key limitation of prior deep copulas that could only do one or the other. Open questions remain about architecture design that preserves marginal uniformity for arbitrary parameterisations, the optimal sampling procedure for diffusion/flow-based copulas, and extension to discrete variables. The practical impact is significant for applications in weather forecasting, risk management, and multi-agent systems where copulas are the standard tool but have been limited to moderate dimensions.
