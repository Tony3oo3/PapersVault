## Problem

Newton unlearning, a theoretically grounded second-order algorithm for machine unlearning, fails on trained neural networks and LLMs because the Hessian matrix is severely degenerate near local optima—exhibiting a large concentration of zero and near-zero eigenvalues that render it non-invertible. Standard remedies like pseudo-inverse and fixed damping produce excessively large-norm updates that overshoot local minima, degrading unlearning performance. The paper asks how to automatically identify the optimal Hessian damping factor γ that balances avoiding oversized updates against trivial ones, without manual hyperparameter tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained model's parameters w*, the erased set D_e, and the retained set D_r, and iteratively updates parameters to minimize the retraining loss on D_r. CuRENUS operates Hessian-free via Hessian-vector products (HVPs) computed with Pearlmutter's trick on mini-batches B1 and B2, requiring only O(2d) memory (one gradient vector plus one HVP) rather than the O(dn) of prior Hessian-free unlearning. The output is an unlearned model whose predictions on both D_e and D_r closely match those of a model retrained from scratch on D_r alone.

## Method

CuRENU replaces the standard quadratic Newton approximation with a cubic-regularized approximation (adding an L/6·‖Δ‖³ term), which serves as a global upper bound on the true loss and guarantees convergence to an ε-SOSP in O(ε^{-1.5}) iterations. The key insight is that the dual problem in the trust-region radius α_{t+1} is a one-dimensional convex optimization that automatically determines the optimal damping factor γ = (L/2)α_{t+1}, eliminating manual tuning. CuRENUS, the stochastic variant, approximates the cubic model using mini-batch gradients and Hessians, then solves the inner subproblem via a few steps of gradient descent using only HVPs (no explicit Hessian storage). This yields a Hessian-free algorithm with O(2d) memory overhead and Õ(ε^{-3.5}) convergence, making it scalable to LLMs like Llama-2-7B with LoRA adapters.

## Results

On batch unlearning (CNN × FMNIST), CuRENUS achieves a Tug-of-War (ToW) score of 0.98 (sample-level) and 0.99 (class-level), closely matching retraining (1.00) and outperforming SCRUB (0.94/0.97) and DELETE (0.85/0.99). In sequential unlearning (5 rounds), CuRENUS on Llama-2 × TOFU attains ToW 0.80 and D_e ROUGE 0.455 (vs. retraining 0.390), surpassing SCRUB (ToW 0.72, D_e ROUGE 0.539), while on ResNet-18 × CIFAR-10 it reaches ToW 0.909 vs. SCRUB's 0.144. Critically, PINV-Newton and Damped Newton collapse entirely (ToW ≈ 0.01, D_e Acc. ≈ 9–10%), confirming the Hessian degeneracy failure mode. CuRENUS also runs ~2.6× faster than retraining on Llama-2 × TOFU (340s vs. 901s) with only ~1.3× peak memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SCRUB (Kurmanji et al., 2023), CuRENUS achieves a higher ToW in both batch settings (0.98 vs. 0.94 sample-level; 0.99 vs. 0.97 class-level) and in sequential unlearning on TOFU (0.80 vs. 0.72), while SCRUB retains a marginal edge on D_r accuracy in class-level batch unlearning (92.66% vs. 90.88%). Against DELETE (Zhou et al., 2025), CuRENUS substantially outperforms on ToW (0.98 vs. 0.85 sample-level) and D_r accuracy (86.27% vs. 83.25%), though DELETE achieves a lower D_e accuracy (83.34% vs. 85.93%), indicating stronger forgetting at the cost of over-forgetting on D_r. Against NPO (Zhang et al., 2024b), CuRENUS wins on ToW (0.98 vs. 0.89) and D_r accuracy (86.27% vs. 82.21%), while NPO achieves a lower D_e accuracy (82.57% vs. 85.93%). In sequential unlearning on CIFAR-10, CuRENUS dramatically outperforms SCRUB on ToW (0.909 vs. 0.144) and D_e accuracy (2.32% vs. 0.00%), though SCRUB edges ahead on D_r accuracy (90.70% vs. 90.33%). The Hessian degeneracy baselines (PINV-Newton, Damped Newton) fail catastrophically across all settings with ToW ≈ 0.01, validating the paper's central diagnosis that vanilla Newton unlearning is inapplicable to trained NNs without proper regularization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Newton unlearning (Guo et al., 2020; Golatkar et al., 2020), which leverages second-order information to approximate retraining loss, and on the influence function framework (Koh & Liang, 2017) that connects second-order unlearning to data attribution. The key departure is identifying Hessian degeneracy—the concentration of zero and near-zero eigenvalues in trained NNs/LLMs—as the fundamental reason Newton unlearning fails in practice, and adapting cubic regularization from non-convex optimization (Nesterov & Polyak, 2006; Tripuraneni et al., 2018) as a principled fix. The stochastic variant CuRENUS further builds on stochastic cubic regularization and Hessian-vector product techniques (Pearlmutter, 1994), while improving over the existing Hessian-free unlearning algorithm (Qiao et al., 2025) by reducing memory from O(dn) to O(2d).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By diagnosing Hessian degeneracy as the specific failure mode of Newton unlearning and providing a principled cubic-regularization fix with ε-SOSP convergence guarantees, this work makes second-order unlearning practically viable for the first time on real NNs and LLMs, including sequential unlearning settings where error accumulation is critical. CuRENUS's O(2d) memory footprint and Hessian-free design via HVPs make it scalable to 7B-parameter models, a regime where prior second-order methods were infeasible. Open questions include whether the approach extends to full-parameter LLM unlearning (beyond LoRA), how it behaves under adversarial or multi-round unlearning with overlapping erased sets, and whether the cubic regularization constant L can be estimated more automatically in practice.
