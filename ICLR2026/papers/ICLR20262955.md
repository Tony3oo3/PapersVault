## Problem

Existing hallucination detection methods for LLMs typically address only one source—either data-driven (flawed training knowledge) or reasoning-driven (inference-time instability)—and rely on task-specific heuristics, limiting generalization. No unified theoretical framework formally decomposes hallucination risk into these two components or explains how they emerge and evolve during multi-step generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262955/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt and the LLM's autoregressively generated token sequence, the method computes the NTK Gram matrix over a set of light semantic perturbations applied to the model's hidden states, then extracts three spectral quantities: the determinant of the NTK Gram matrix, the supremum of step-wise Jacobian spectral norms, and the condition number. The output is a single scalar hallucination risk score (HALLUGUARD) that can be thresholded for detection or used as a continuous signal to guide beam search during test-time inference.

## Method

The paper first derives a Hallucination Risk Bound that decomposes total hallucination risk into a data-driven term (bounded via Cea's lemma on the NTK-induced bilinear form, scaling with pretraining-finetuning mismatch) and a reasoning-driven term (bounded via Freedman/Bernstein concentration, growing exponentially with sequence length through cumulative Jacobian products). From this bound, HALLUGUARD is defined as det(K) + log σ_max − log κ², where K is the NTK Gram matrix on embedded perturbations, σ_max is the uniform spectral bound on step Jacobians, and κ is the condition number of K—each term corresponding to representational adequacy, rollout amplification, and spectral instability respectively. Lightweight projection layers act as self-supervised spectral calibration modules, trained offline via AdamW to align NTK spectral properties across heterogeneous backbones into a comparable geometric space, with the backbone fully frozen and zero additional runtime overhead at inference.

## Results

HALLUGUARD achieves state-of-the-art hallucination detection across 10 benchmarks, 11 baselines, and 9 LLM backbones. On the reasoning-oriented MATH-500 (QwQ-32B), it reaches 81.76% AUROC and 79.76% AUPRC, improving over the second-best method (Inside) by up to 8.3% on AUPRC. On the data-centric RAGTruth (QwQ-32B), it attains 84.59% AUROC and 81.15% AUPRC with gains of up to 7.7%. In score-guided test-time inference, HALLUGUARD boosts MATH-500 accuracy to 81.00% (~10% over IO Prompt) and Natural to 70.96% (15.72% over IO Prompt).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HALLUGUARD outperforms all 11 baselines across every task family and backbone tested. The closest competitor is Inside (Chen et al., ICLR 2024), which also probes internal representations; on Math-500 (QwQ-32B), HALLUGUARD leads by 0.96 AUROC but by a much larger 8.27 AUPRC (79.76 vs. 71.49), indicating superior precision under class imbalance. On RAGTruth, the gap widens to 6.87 AUROC (84.59 vs. 77.72), reflecting HALLUGUARD's stronger capture of data-driven factual errors via the det(K) term. Uncertainty-based baselines (Perplexity, LN-Entropy, Semantic Entropy, Energy) and consistency-based methods (SelfCheckGPT, FActScore, RACE) trail by 5–20 points depending on the task, with the largest deficits on reasoning-oriented benchmarks where their single-signal designs fail to capture compounding multi-step errors. In the test-time inference setting (Table 4), HALLUGUARD-guided beam search achieves 81.00% on MATH-500 and 70.96% on Natural, surpassing the next-best detector (MIND at 77.10% and 68.32% respectively) by 3.9 and 2.6 points, and exceeding the IO Prompt baseline by ~10% and ~15.7% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of hallucination detection—data-driven (SelfCheckGPT, FActScore, perplexity/entropy-based methods) and reasoning-driven (RACE, Inside, MIND)—under a single NTK-based theoretical framework. It builds directly on the Neural Tangent Kernel literature (Jacot et al., 2018; Lee et al., 2020b) for analyzing overparameterized network dynamics, repurposing NTK spectral properties (determinant, condition number, spectral norm) as proxies for the two hallucination sources. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the formal Hallucination Risk Bound, which decomposes total hallucination risk via Cea's lemma (data-driven) and Freedman's inequality (reasoning-driven), providing a principled justification for why a single NTK-derived score can address both failure modes simultaneously rather than relying on task-specific heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified theoretical lens offers a principled explanation for why hallucinations emerge and evolve during multi-step generation, filling a gap where prior methods addressed only one source. The practical HALLUGUARD score (det(K) + log σ_max − log κ²) is attractive in its simplicity and requires no external references or task-specific supervision, though computing NTK Gram matrices for billion-parameter LLMs remains non-trivial and the paper relies on lightweight self-supervised projection layers as a bridge. Open questions include whether the theoretical constants (k_pt, Signal_k, ε_mismatch) can be estimated in practice, whether the proxy score degrades on architectures where NTK theory is less well-characterized (e.g., mixture-of-experts), and whether the test-time inference gains generalize beyond the two models evaluated. The paper's acknowledgment that direct step-wise Jacobians are intractable for modern LLMs highlights a significant gap between the elegant theory and deployable practice.
