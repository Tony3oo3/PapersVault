## Problem

Classifier-free guidance (CFG) is a critical practical tool for conditional generation in discrete diffusion models, yet its theoretical mechanism in discrete state spaces remains largely unexplained. Unlike continuous diffusion, where guidance reshapes score-based ODEs in ways that have been partially characterized, the effect of CFG on the reverse transition rates, the resulting sampled distribution, and the convergence dynamics in discrete settings is not well understood. This paper asks two precise questions: how does guidance alter the distribution of generated samples, and how does it affect the rate at which the reverse dynamics converge to that distribution?

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on a finite discrete state space S = {1,...,N}^D with an absorbing (masked) forward process, conditioned on a target class label z from a mixture of M class distributions. The guidance strength w ≥ -1 controls the interpolation: w = -1 recovers the full mixture, w = 0 recovers the class-conditional distribution, and w > 0 amplifies class-specific regions. The output is a fully characterized sampled distribution q_T^{z,w} and the exact reverse dynamics q_t^{z,w} for all t ∈ [0,T], including explicit TV convergence rates. The theoretical results are derived in D=1 and D=2 where closed-form solutions exist, and validated empirically in 1D, 2D, and 5D using a small transformer score model with Tau-leaping (50 steps, log-linear schedule).

## Method

The paper develops an exact analytical framework for CFG in masked discrete diffusion by exploiting the tractability of the absorbing forward process, where each coordinate independently transitions to the masked state. The CFG rate matrix is defined as a geometric interpolation between unguided and class-conditional reverse rates: Q̂_t^{z,w}(y,x) = Q̄_t(y,x)^{-w} Q̄_t^z(y,x)^{1+w}. In 1D, the key result is that the guided reverse dynamics coincide exactly with the reverse dynamics of the tilted distribution p^{z,w} ∝ p(x)^{-w} p(x|z)^{1+w}, yielding a closed-form solution where the generated distribution equals the tilted distribution. In 2D, the guided rates no longer match the tilted distribution's rates; instead, new coefficients c_x and d_x encoding marginal-level steering effects appear, and the generated distribution becomes a region-weighted version of the class distribution where weights depend on the degree of support overlap. A central finding across both dimensions is that the TV distance along the reverse dynamics decays double-exponentially in w (via the α-divergence structure of the normalization constant Z), explaining the sharp transitions and numerical stiffness observed at large guidance strengths.

## Results

The paper's headline theoretical result is that in 1D, the guided reverse dynamics exactly recovers the tilted distribution p^{z,w}, while in 2D the generated distribution deviates from the tilted distribution by a region-dependent multiplicative factor (1/c_{x₁}+1/d_{x₂})/(1/c_N+1/d_N). For both 1D and 2D, the total variation distance along the reverse dynamics decays double-exponentially in the guidance strength w (i.e., −ln TV = exp(Θ(w))·ln(·)). In 5D experiments with overlapping hypercubes, per-state density decreases monotonically from the unique region (k=0: 4.984×10⁻³ at w=1, 5.573×10⁻³ at w=2) to the fully overlapping state (k=5: 0.280×10⁻³ at w=1, 0.200×10⁻³ at w=2), confirming the theoretical prediction that guidance suppresses overlapping regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical analysis paper and does not benchmark against competing guidance methods or generative models. Instead, it validates its closed-form theoretical predictions against empirical results obtained via Tau-leaping simulation. In 1D, the empirically sampled distribution closely matches both the theoretical tilted distribution p^{z,w} and the true class-conditional distribution p(·|z) when supports are disjoint, and matches the tilted distribution (but deviates from p(·|z)) when supports overlap—consistent with Proposition 3.1. In 2D, the ratio of the generated distribution to the class distribution (Figure 3) aligns closely with the theoretical prediction from Proposition 3.3 in both disjoint and overlapping support settings. The empirical TV curve in 1D matches the theoretical double-exponential prediction for small w, though deviations appear at large w due to numerical stiffness of the Tau-leaping scheme. The 5D experiments provide qualitative confirmation that the monotone mass-redistribution pattern observed in 2D extends to higher dimensions, with stronger guidance (w=2 vs. w=1) producing more pronounced suppression of overlapping regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous-diffusion guidance theory of Bradley & Nakkiran (2024), Wu et al. (2024), and Chidambaram et al. (2024) to the discrete setting, building on the CFG rate-matrix formulation of Nisonoff et al. (2024) and the masked (absorbing) discrete diffusion framework of Campbell et al. (2022) and Lou et al. (2023). While prior continuous analyses showed that guided ODEs do not recover the tilted distribution, this paper demonstrates that in 1D discrete diffusion the guided process exactly recovers the tilted distribution—a clean departure from the continuous case—while in 2D explicit deviations emerge that can be characterized in terms of class-support geometry. The work is positioned as the first rigorous theoretical analysis of CFG in discrete diffusion, filling a gap the authors identify between empirical gains (Schiff et al., 2024; Xiong et al., 2025) and mechanistic understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results provide the first quantitative explanation for why intermediate guidance strengths (w > 0) outperform both unguided and strictly conditional sampling in discrete diffusion: guidance reallocates mass from overlapping to class-unique regions while preserving local statistics, and the double-exponential convergence rate in w explains both the sharp quality gains and the numerical stiffness observed at large w. The 2D finding that the generated distribution systematically deviates from the tilted distribution—weighted by a 'privacy' hierarchy of class regions—suggests that CFG in discrete settings leverages marginal-projection geometry in ways absent from continuous analogues. Key limitations include restriction to D ≤ 2 for exact results, assumption of exact scores, and lack of analysis of how score approximation and discretization errors interact with guidance; extending to higher dimensions and incorporating approximation errors are identified as the main open problems.
