## Problem

While anisotropy and rank collapse in transformer embeddings have been well documented, the precise relationship between embedding geometry and autoregressive next-token prediction quality remained unclear. This paper asks whether a simple geometric statistic—average pairwise cosine distance of hidden states—can both predict and improve language-modeling performance, and whether it can be exploited for practical, label-free model diagnostics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261964/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the empirical analysis, the method consumes 512-token text segments passed through a frozen transformer, extracting final-layer (or sub-layer) hidden vectors to compute average pairwise cosine distance; for model selection, it reads only the rows of the output projection matrix (no forward pass, no input data) to compute a 'dispersion gap' between domain-specific and generic token embeddings. Outputs include a scalar dispersion score per text sample, a monotone difficulty ranking of unlabeled queries, a coarse model ranking within a family, a recommended sub-layer for kNN-LM retrieval keys, and—when the auxiliary loss is active—reduced test perplexity. Notably, the kNN-LM layer-selection signal stabilizes after roughly 5,000 tokens of input, and the dispersion-to-accuracy calibration achieves ~1.4–2.2 percentage-point MAE on held-out MMLU.

## Method

The paper defines representation dispersion as the average pairwise cosine distance over a set of hidden vectors (Eq. 1) and demonstrates a strong, consistent negative correlation with sequence-level and last-token perplexity across LLaMA, Qwen, Gemma, Mistral, and Phi models on WikiText-103, CNN DailyMail, and PubMed. Four practical applications follow: (1) dispersion on unlabeled queries monotonically tracks correctness, enabling zero-label difficulty ranking and hard-slice discovery; (2) a 'dispersion gap' G = within(T) + between(T, T̄) computed purely on output-embedding rows ranks model checkpoints by domain readiness without any forward pass; (3) the sub-layer (attention vs. FFN) with higher dispersion in the final block is the better kNN-LM datastore key, identified with ~5,000 tokens; (4) an auxiliary 'push-away' loss that normalizes batch hidden states and adds −d_avg (or cross-domain −d) to the cross-entropy objective, weighted by λ, increases dispersion and reduces perplexity in both single-domain and cross-domain (WikiText + Python) training. A complementary 'squeeze' ablation that minimizes pairwise distance degrades perplexity by up to 27 points, supporting a causal reading of the dispersion–performance link.

## Results

Across LLaMA, Qwen, Gemma, Mistral, and Phi models on WikiText-103, CNN DailyMail, and PubMed, representation dispersion (average pairwise cosine distance) shows a strong negative correlation with sequence-level perplexity, with the correlation strengthening in deeper layers. For the push-away training objective, the auxiliary loss reduces WikiText perplexity by 1–4 points in the single-domain setting (e.g., 166.2→165.6 at LR=5×10⁻⁴, step 500) and by 10–15 points in the cross-domain setting (e.g., Wiki: 268.5→253.3, Code: 33.7→30.5). The 'Squeeze' ablation (penalizing dispersion) degrades perplexity by up to +27.5 points, confirming a causal link. For model selection, the Dispersion Gap achieves Spearman ρ>0.95 with MATH accuracy across nine Qwen checkpoints and ρ=0.90–0.95 across 30 Olmo-7B training checkpoints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external competing methods but makes several internal comparisons that establish the value of dispersion. As a hardness signal, dispersion is strongly monotone with correctness (e.g., MMLU 1B: 0.0425 at 0% correct → 0.0584 at 100% correct), while perplexity is nearly flat (5.058→5.273), making dispersion a strictly superior label-free indicator. For kNN-LM layer selection, the attention sub-layer consistently shows higher dispersion than the FFN sub-layer (e.g., 0.80 vs. 0.68 for GPT2-LARGE), matching the empirically better kNN-LM perplexity reported by Xu et al. (2023). The push-away auxiliary loss outperforms the standard cross-entropy baseline in both single-domain (1–4 PPL gain) and cross-domain (10–15 PPL gain) settings, while the reverse 'Squeeze' ablation degrades performance by 3–27.5 PPL points, providing causal evidence that dispersion is functionally necessary. For model selection, the Dispersion Gap perfectly ranks all nine Qwen checkpoints on MATH without a single mis-ordering, and tracks Olmo-7B training progress with ρ=0.90 (GSM8K) and ρ=0.95 (HumanEval) across 30 checkpoints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the anisotropy and rank-collapse literature (Ethayarajh 2019; Gao et al. 2019; Li et al. 2020), which identified that transformer embeddings occupy narrow cones, and on intrinsic-dimension analyses (Valeriani et al. 2023; Tulchinskii et al. 2023) that linked geometry to downstream behavior. The key departure is making dispersion *actionable*: rather than treating anisotropy as a descriptive pathology, the authors show that average pairwise cosine distance is a predictive, label-free signal for perplexity, example hardness, model selection, and kNN-LM layer choice. The auxiliary 'push-away' training loss is explicitly positioned as a deliberately simplified, unsupervised variant of contrastive objectives (Gunel et al. 2021; Jain et al. 2023), dropping positive-pair structure in favor of a uniform all-pairs repulsive term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a single, cheap geometric statistic that serves multiple practical roles—screening under-adapted checkpoints without forward passes, selecting kNN-LM sub-layers in milliseconds, and ranking unlabeled examples by difficulty—offering a lightweight alternative to expensive task-specific evaluation. The finding that dispersion tracks correctness monotonically while perplexity remains nearly flat (Tables 3–4) suggests geometric separation captures contextual information beyond next-token confidence, opening a path toward label-free model diagnostics. Limitations include the correlational (not causal) nature of the dispersion–perplexity link, reliance on English text and standard benchmarks, and the fact that the push-away loss yields only modest single-domain gains (1–4 ppl points), with its strongest effect in cross-domain settings. Open questions include how dispersion interacts with architectural choices, tokenization, and non-English or multimodal settings.
