## Problem

Existing Concept Bottleneck Models for text classification require training a surrogate classifier to map concept scores to labels, making them infeasible in few-shot settings where labeled data is too scarce to optimize that layer. LLM-augmented CBMs either retain costly LLM calls at inference time or demand large training corpora, and they frame text-concept matching as a supervised objective that precludes iterative concept refinement. The paper asks whether a CBM can operate in a strict few-shot regime with zero LLM cost at inference while remaining fully interpretable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of labeled text samples (as few as 1–10 per class) during an offline construction phase and a single unlabeled text sample at inference time. It outputs a predicted class label together with the specific prototypical and discriminative concepts that drove the decision, providing a human-readable explanation. Crucially, the LLM (DeepSeek-V3) is invoked only during offline concept generation and tuning; the online prediction path uses only a frozen text-embedding model (all-mpnet-base-v2) and cosine-similarity arithmetic, incurring no API cost per inference.

## Method

StructCBM replaces the trained classifier g(·) of standard CBMs with a static, similarity-based two-stage prediction: Stage 1 computes per-class prototypical support scores by averaging cosine similarities to the top-m most relevant per-class prototypical concepts (C_p), selecting a top-k candidate set; Stage 2 computes pairwise relative discrimination scores using fine-grained discriminative concepts (C_d) that contrast each candidate against the others, then combines both signals via a weighted sum to yield the final label. Concepts are generated by prompting an LLM with few-shot examples (shared features for C_p, exclusive features for C_d), validated through LLM-based logical annotation (−1/0/1 scoring), and iteratively refined in a generate-predict-refine loop where misclassifications trigger semantic rewriting of concept descriptions or logical addition of new concepts. A lightweight fine-tuning step aligns the embedding model on sample-concept pairs using a cosine-similarity loss, further sharpening matching without introducing a trainable classifier.

## Results

Under a strict 10-shot-per-label setting, StructCBM achieves 83.90% accuracy on SST2, 85.45% on AGNews, 60.70% on MedAbs, and 77.42% on FinaQuery. These results substantially outperform all prior white-box CBM baselines (e.g., TBM at 45.26% on SST2, CB-LLM at 63.26%) and are competitive with black-box LLMs like DeepSeek-V3, surpassing Deepseek-ICL on AGNews (85.45% vs. 79.00%) while remaining within ~3 points on MedAbs. The full-supervised RoBERTa upper bound reaches 94.62% on SST2 and 95.08% on AGNews, indicating a remaining gap of roughly 10–11 points in the few-shot regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StructCBM decisively outperforms all four prior white-box CBM baselines across every dataset: it gains +38.6 points over TBM on SST2 (83.90% vs. 45.26%), +17.2 over CB-LLM (83.90% vs. 63.26%), +30.0 over C³M (83.90% vs. 53.92%), and +26.6 over SparseCBM (83.90% vs. 57.27%), with similar margins on AGNews, MedAbs, and FinaQuery. Against the black-box LLM baselines, StructCBM wins on AGNews (85.45% vs. 80.47% for DeepSeek-Direct and 79.00% for DeepSeek-ICL) and is within 3.3 points on MedAbs (60.70% vs. 63.95%), but trails by roughly 12 points on SST2 (83.90% vs. 96.10%) and 7.6 points on FinaQuery (77.42% vs. 84.99%), which the authors attribute to the vast world knowledge embedded in LLMs for sentiment and financial-query tasks. Compared to few-shot fine-tuned RoBERTa and DistilBERT, StructCBM substantially outperforms both (e.g., 83.90% vs. 50.08% and 58.59% on SST2), while the full-supervised RoBERTa upper bound remains 10.7 points higher on SST2. The key differentiator is that StructCBM achieves these results with zero LLM calls at inference time, whereas TBM and BC-LLM require LLM access per prediction, and CB-LLM/SparseCBM require large training sets incompatible with few-shot settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Concept Bottleneck Model framework (Koh et al., 2020) into the few-shot regime by eliminating the trained classifier g(·) that prior CBMs require, replacing it with direct sample-concept cosine similarity matching. It departs from existing LLM-augmented CBMs (TBM, BC-LLM, CB-LLM, SparseCBM, C³M) in two key ways: (1) a dual-level prototypical-discriminative concept architecture that decouples recall (candidate selection) from precision (contrastive re-ranking), and (2) a closed-loop generate-predict-refine mechanism that iteratively tunes concepts using classification errors without requiring a separate prediction head. The paper positions itself as the first to combine LLM-driven concept discovery with few-shot CBM prediction, filling the gap between LLM-dependent iterative methods and large-data-trained efficient CBMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-time construction cost versus recurring per-inference LLM cost is a practically significant distinction for deployment at scale, and the 96.43% LLM-human annotation agreement provides empirical grounding for the automated pipeline. However, the quadratic scaling of discriminative concepts (O(|Y|²)) remains a real bottleneck for tasks with many classes, and the performance gap on semantically sparse datasets (SST2: 0.84 vs. 0.96 for DeepSeek-ICL) suggests that world-knowledge-heavy tasks still favor black-box LLMs. Open questions include whether the sufficient-but-not-necessary causal framing can be strengthened via counterfactual reasoning, and whether the similarity-based pre-filtering strategy for large label sets preserves the discriminative power of C_d.
