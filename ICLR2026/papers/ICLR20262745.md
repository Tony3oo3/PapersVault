## Problem

Existing vision-enhancement methods for MLLM hallucination mitigation re-inject all visual tokens indiscriminately into the decoder's feed-forward network, causing background regions and redundant semantics to dilute the model's attention from critical visual cues. This paper identifies that indiscriminate token fusion actively harms grounding and proposes a selective, adaptive reinforcement strategy that amplifies only the most semantically aligned visual evidence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262745/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and a free-form text query as input. The image is encoded into a pool of visual tokens (e.g., K=576 for LLaVA-1.5) and simultaneously cropped into M=12 spatial patches for fine-grained alignment. The output is a standard autoregressive text response (caption or VQA answer) generated with greedy decoding; AIR modifies the internal FFN computation in mid-to-deep decoder layers (e.g., layers 24–32) without altering the input format or output structure.

## Method

AIR operates training-free inside the FFN of each selected Transformer layer and consists of two sequential components. First, prototype-based token reduction computes the mean of all visual tokens as a global prototype, ranks tokens by L2 distance to this prototype, and retains only the Top-Q (Q=100) most distinctive tokens to suppress background redundancy. Second, OT-guided patch reinforcement models the reduced hidden states and each image patch's embeddings as discrete distributions, computes the entropically regularized optimal transport distance via the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm, and selects patches whose OT distance falls below a threshold τ=0.06. The selected patch embeddings are concatenated and re-injected into the FFN alongside the standard computation path. A theoretical result proves that the OT-based selection criterion is strictly more sensitive than uniform cosine averaging for distinguishing well-aligned from misaligned patches, because the adaptive transport plan amplifies low-cost (high-similarity) alignments while the uniform baseline dilutes discriminative structure.

## Results

On the CHAIR benchmark (MSCOCO, 64 max tokens), AIR achieves the lowest hallucination rates across all three MLLMs: CHAIR_S of 18.4 / 18.6 / 11.6 and CHAIR_I of 5.7 / 5.9 / 5.3 for LLaVA-1.5-7B, Qwen-VL-Chat, and GLM-4V-9B respectively, while maintaining BLEU scores within 0.1 of the vanilla baseline. On POPE (LLaVA-1.5-7B, MSCOCO), AIR reaches 89.0% accuracy and 88.2 F1 under Random sampling and 83.9% / 83.6 under Adversarial sampling, outperforming all baselines. Inference overhead is minimal: 2.07 s latency and 13.7 GB GPU memory versus 1.68 s / 13.5 GB for the vanilla LLaVA-1.5-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AIR outperforms all three inference-time baselines on CHAIR across all three MLLMs. Against VCD (Leng et al., CVPR 2024), AIR reduces CHAIR_S by 6.2 points on LLaVA-1.5-7B (18.4 vs 24.6) and 3.2 points on GLM-4V-9B (11.6 vs 14.8); VCD actually *increases* hallucination over the vanilla model on LLaVA and GLM-4V. Against MemVR (Zou et al., 2024), AIR achieves a 3.2-point CHAIR_S reduction on LLaVA (18.4 vs 21.6) and a 1.4-point reduction on Qwen-VL (18.6 vs 20.0), while MemVR shows near-zero improvement over vanilla on Qwen-VL and GLM-4V. Against VAF (Yin et al., CVPR 2025), AIR leads by 2.0 points on LLaVA CHAIR_S (18.4 vs 20.4) and 2.0 points on Qwen-VL (18.6 vs 20.6), and ties VAF on GLM-4V (both 11.6 / 5.3). Against the fine-tuned SENTINEL baseline (Peng et al., ICCV 2025), AIR further reduces Object HallBench response errors from 4.3 to 3.9 and AMBER CHAIR from 2.9 to 2.1 on LLaVA-1.5-7B, confirming that AIR complements training-time methods rather than merely matching them.

## Relation to Prior Work

AIR directly extends the visual token re-injection paradigm introduced by MemVR (Zou et al., 2024), which injects all visual tokens into the FFN during decoding to counteract the progressive text-bias in deeper layers. The key departure is replacing indiscriminate full-token injection with a two-stage selective mechanism: prototype-based reduction compresses the token pool, and entropically regularized optimal transport (via Sinkhorn-Knopp) scores patch-level alignment to retain only well-matched regions. This draws on the broader OT literature (domain adaptation, few-shot learning, distribution calibration) but applies it at inference time as a fine-grained patch-selection criterion, a use not previously explored in MLLM hallucination mitigation. The paper positions itself against training-time fine-tuning (DPO, hallucination-induced optimization), post-processing revisers (LURE, Woodpecker), and other inference-time interventions (VCD, VAF), emphasizing its training-free, architecture-agnostic nature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AIR provides a practical, plug-and-play hallucination mitigation that requires no retraining, no auxiliary models, and only marginal latency overhead (~0.4s), making it broadly deployable across existing MLLM pipelines. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) that OT distance is strictly more sensitive than uniform cosine averaging in distinguishing patches gives the selection criterion a principled foundation beyond heuristic similarity. However, the approach has not been validated on reasoning-oriented MLLMs or agentic settings, and its reliance on a fixed threshold τ and patch count introduces hyperparameters that may need per-architecture tuning. Open questions include whether OT-based reinforcement generalizes to video understanding, cross-modal grounding, or scenarios where the visual input is heavily occluded or adversarially perturbed beyond the mild δ=16/255 tested here.
