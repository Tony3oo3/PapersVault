## Problem

Diffusion models are designed for continuous-valued data, yet real digital signals like 8-bit images are quantized to a finite set of discrete values. Existing approaches either ignore quantization during training or add small continuous noise to make data appear continuous, neither of which guarantees that generated samples converge to the quantized grid, leading to suboptimal density estimation and the need for ad hoc pre/post-processing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261770/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, the model starts from Gaussian noise and produces 8-bit RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/ImageNet-32, 64×64 for FFHQ/AFHQ) whose pixel values are guaranteed to lie exactly on the discrete {0,…,255} grid without any post-processing rounding. For density estimation, the model takes a quantized image and computes a negative log-likelihood upper bound in bits-per-dimension (BPD) via the ELBO, with the key distinction that no dequantization noise or time truncation is needed.

## Method

The core insight is that the reverse SDE solution at t=0 is always a fixed point of the signal predictor x̂_θ(·,0), so constraining the predictor's fixed points to quantized values guarantees convergence to the discrete grid. Concretely, the signal predictor is parameterized as x̂_θ(x_t,t) = softround(x_t,t) + e^{−1/t²}·δ̂_θ(x_t,t), where softround is a softmax-weighted average over quantized levels that converges to exact rounding as t→0, and the residual term vanishes super-exponentially. The maximum likelihood ELBO simplifies under the change of variable u=e^{−1/t²} to E[δ̂_θᵀ(u/2·δ̂_θ − δ_t)] with u~U(0,1), eliminating the t^{-3} divergence that makes standard ELBO training numerically unstable near t=0. The network is a U-Net (following VDM) that receives x_t/√(1+t²) and u as inputs, and sampling uses a custom first- or second-order SDE solver that discretizes in the bounded u-space rather than the unbounded t-space.

## Results

QDPM achieves a test negative log-likelihood of 0.27 BPD on CIFAR-10 and 0.32 BPD on ImageNet-32, dramatically outperforming the previous state-of-the-art (i-DODE with data augmentation at 2.42 BPD on CIFAR-10) by over 2.0 BPD. This is the first diffusion model result to break below 1.0 BPD, approaching the theoretical lower bound of 0.0043 BPD for CIFAR-10. In terms of perceptual quality, QDPM reports FID of 5.60 on CIFAR-10 and 8.89 on ImageNet-32, which remains worse than the best FID scores (e.g., 1.54 from GDD on CIFAR-10), consistent with the known trade-off between likelihood and FID in diffusion models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QDPM decisively outperforms all eight baselines on both CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-32 in terms of NLL. On CIFAR-10, QDPM (0.27 BPD) improves over the previous best, i-DODE with data augmentation (2.42 BPD), by a margin of 2.15 BPD, and over i-DODE without augmentation (2.56 BPD) by 2.29 BPD. On ImageNet-32, QDPM (0.32 BPD) beats i-DODE (3.43 BPD) by 3.11 BPD and MULAN (3.67 BPD) by 3.35 BPD. The gap over autoregressive and VAE baselines (PixelSNAIL 2.85, Very Deep VAE 2.87, Image Transformer 2.90 on CIFAR-10) is even larger, exceeding 2.5 BPD. The only metric where QDPM does not lead is FID: its 5.60 on CIFAR-10 is worse than GDD's 1.54, which the authors attribute to the well-known inverse correlation between likelihood and FID in maximum-likelihood-trained diffusion models. Notably, QDPM achieves these results without any data augmentation, whereas several baselines (i-DODE, VDM) rely on it to reach their best numbers.

## Relation to Prior Work

QDPM builds directly on the continuous-time SDE framework of Song et al. (2020b) and the maximum-likelihood (ELBO) training objectives of Song et al. (2021) and Zheng et al. (2023), but departs from the standard practice of treating quantization as an ad-hoc preprocessing (dequantization noise) or post-processing (rounding) step. The key conceptual departure is a parameterization of the signal predictor via a 'softround' term whose fixed points at t=0 are exactly the quantized grid, combined with a theoretical result (Proposition 1) showing the reverse SDE limit must be a fixed point of the predictor. The efficient SDE solver is closely related to DPM-Solver++ (Lu et al., 2022b), differing mainly in the choice of discretization variable (u = e^{-1/t²} vs. λ = -log t) to handle the unbounded time domain of the VE-SDE formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a simple fixed-point constraint on the signal predictor yields a ~9× NLL improvement (2.42 → 0.27 BPD on CIFAR-10, approaching the 0.0043 theoretical lower bound) is striking and suggests that prior diffusion models were fundamentally mis-specified for quantized data. The approach eliminates the need for dequantization noise, time truncation, or categorical terminal distributions, simplifying both training and inference. However, the FID regression (5.60 vs. 1.54 for GDD on CIFAR-10) highlights the well-known NLL–FID tradeoff, and the method is demonstrated only at 32×32 resolution with a U-Net backbone. Extending the framework to ODE/flow-matching formulations, higher resolutions, and reconciling likelihood with perceptual quality remain open questions.
