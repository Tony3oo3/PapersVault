## Problem

Current text-to-video diffusion models produce outputs that fail to align with authentic user preferences in subject consistency, motion smoothness, and aesthetic quality. Existing post-training approaches rely on large fixed preference datasets that cause overfitting, or on external reward models that suffer from distribution mismatch and become stale as the generator improves, leading to biased or unstable optimization signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262893/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes natural-language text prompts (constructed from a structured pipeline of ~1M diverse captions spanning subjects, actions, and scenes) and produces generated videos. For reward modeling, the VILA-based critic consumes 16 uniformly sampled frames per video along with the prompt, outputting either pairwise rankings or pointwise scores across three dimensions (text-video consistency, content faithfulness, motion smoothness). For each prompt, the T2V generator produces 8 candidate videos, from which the top-1 and bottom-1 by critic ranking form preference pairs (~100K per iteration).

## Method

Dual-IPO alternates two optimization loops: (1) a Self-Refined Preference Optimization (SRPO) loop that iteratively improves a VILA-based reward model using CoT-guided reasoning, multi-path self-consistency voting, and a Preference Certainty Estimator (PCE) that filters pseudo-labels below a 0.5 confidence threshold, weighted into the DPO loss; and (2) a video generation alignment loop that applies Diffusion-DPO (pairwise) or Diffusion-KTO (pointwise) with the critic's updated scores, augmented with NLL regularization on real video data. The key insight is that as the generator improves, its artifacts become subtler, so the reward model must co-evolve via self-labeling on the generator's own outputs rather than relying on a fixed offline dataset. This dual-iterative scheme requires only ~6K human-annotated pairs as cold-start data, after which all subsequent reward refinement is fully automated, enabling a 2B model to surpass a 5B baseline after three iterations.

## Results

On VBench, Dual-IPO consistently improves all tested base models across multiple iterations. After 5 iterations, Wan-1.3B+Ours reaches 88.32 total score (from 84.26), CogVideoX-5B+Ours reaches 86.57 (from 82.01), and CogVideoX-2B+Ours reaches 82.74 (from 80.91). Notably, the 2B CogVideoX model surpasses the 5B variant after 3 iterations (82.74 vs. 81.61 baseline), and the 1.3B Wan model with Dual-IPO (88.32) outperforms the larger Wan2.1 (86.22) and Vidu Q1 (87.41). The reward model achieves 81.33% human preference accuracy, substantially exceeding VideoScore (63.58%), VideoAlign (65.21%), and VisionReward (68.44%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against external SOTA video generation models, Wan-1.3B+Ours (88.32 total) surpasses Wan2.1 (86.22), Vidu Q1 (87.41), Open-Sora-2.0 (84.34), Sora (84.28), CausVid (84.27), and HunyuanVideo (83.24) on VBench total score. CogVideoX-5B+Ours (86.57) also exceeds Sora, Open-Sora-2.0, CausVid, and HunyuanVideo, though it remains below Vidu Q1 (87.41) and Wan2.1 (86.22). The paper's key architectural claim is that a 2B CogVideoX model with Dual-IPO (82.74 after 3 iterations) surpasses the 2.5× larger 5B CogVideoX baseline (81.61), demonstrating alignment efficiency. On the reward model side, the proposed VILA-based critic achieves 81.33% pairwise accuracy on human preference labels, outperforming VideoScore (63.58%), VideoAlign (65.21%), and VisionReward (68.44%) by 13–18 percentage points. In downstream DPO fine-tuning, the proposed critic boosts VBench from 80.91 to 81.54 (+0.67), while VideoScore slightly degrades performance and the other two yield only ~+0.3 gains. Between DPO and KTO alignment strategies within Dual-IPO, KTO achieves a slightly higher total (82.47 vs. 82.42) and semantic score (78.08 vs. 77.97), while DPO leads marginally on quality (83.53 vs. 83.56 is actually KTO leading; DPO leads on quality at 83.53 vs 83.56—rechecking: DPO quality 83.53, KTO quality 83.56, so KTO leads on both total and quality slightly).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO/KTO preference optimization lineage (Rafailov et al., 2023; Wallace et al., 2023; Li et al., 2025) from LLMs and images to text-to-video generation, but its core departure is the dual-iterative paradigm: rather than performing a single offline alignment pass with a fixed reward model, it alternately refines both the VLM-based critic (via CoT-guided self-consistency and a Preference Certainty Estimator) and the diffusion generator across multiple rounds. Prior video post-training efforts (InstructVideo, T2V-Turbo, VADER) addressed reward design or one-shot alignment but did not tackle the reward-drift problem that arises as the generator's artifacts become subtler over training. The SRPO self-refinement loop, which bootstraps pseudo-labels from the current critic and filters them by confidence, is a novel application of iterative self-training to the video reward modeling setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a 2B CogVideoX model, after three Dual-IPO iterations, surpasses a 2.5× larger 5B variant on VBench, suggesting that alignment quality can compensate for parameter scale in video generation. The framework also substantially reduces the human annotation burden by front-loading a small seed set and then relying on automated pseudo-labeling, making preference alignment more accessible to smaller labs. Open questions remain: the paper acknowledges that VBench scores saturate after a few iterations while internal reward keeps rising (risking benchmark hacking), that the optimal number of rounds is configuration-dependent, and that the approach has not been validated on models significantly larger than 5B or on metrics beyond VBench. Whether the dual-iterative scheme generalizes to audio-visual or longer-horizon video generation is unexplored.
