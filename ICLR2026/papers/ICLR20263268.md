## Problem

Prior evidence for implicit planning in LLMs—where models encode representations of future goal tokens in hidden activations—comes from a single closed-source model (Claude 3.5 Haiku) studied with a computationally expensive cross-layer transcoder, making findings neither reproducible nor generalizable. This paper asks whether implicit planning is a universal mechanism across diverse open-weight models and whether it can be assessed with simpler, scalable quantitative metrics rather than qualitative case studies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt (a first line of a rhyming couplet or a question) and the LLM's hidden activations at a single token position (last word, newline, or question mark) across all layers. A steering vector—computed as the mean activation difference between two prompt categories (e.g., rhyme families or noun answers) on a small training set—is added to the residual stream at one chosen layer-position during autoregressive generation. Outputs include the generated continuation (second line or answer) and a suite of planning metrics: fraction of correct rhyme/answer, regeneration rates (last word regenerated without original context), and probability-based measures (top-1 divergence, KL divergence thresholds) comparing steered vs. unsteered token distributions.

## Method

The core technique is mean activation difference steering: for a pair of prompt categories (e.g., rhyme families -ing and -air), the steering vector is the scaled average difference of hidden activations at a target position across training prompts, and is injected at a single token during generation. The authors select the optimal layer-position combination by maximizing steering effectiveness across all layers and three candidate positions. To assess forward planning, they measure whether steering shifts the generated goal token (rhyme word or answer noun); to assess backward planning, they use regeneration metrics (removing the first line and checking whether the second line's last word still regenerates from the correct rhyme family) and probability-based metrics tracking where and how strongly the steered vs. unsteered next-token distributions diverge. Mechanistic analysis on Gemma2 9B identifies two specific attention heads (L30H3, L31H15) that attend to the last word and newline of the first line and carry the rhyme plan to later positions, with subsequent MLP layers converting this into output predictions. The approach is validated across 23 open-weight models (Gemma2, Gemma3, Llama3.1/3.2, Qwen3; 1B–32B parameters, base and instruction-tuned), demonstrating that implicit planning emerges from 1B parameters and is strengthened by instruction tuning.

## Results

Across 23 open-weight LLMs (1B–32B parameters, four families: Gemma2, Gemma3, Llama3.1/3.2, Qwen3), mean activation difference steering on the last word of the first rhyming line reliably shifts the generated rhyme family, with steered rhyming rates comparable to each model's unsteered baseline for all but the two weakest models (base Gemma3 1B and Llama 3.2 3B). All models show successful backward planning: last-word regeneration rates (with the first line removed) exceed chance, and steered regeneration rates closely match baseline regeneration rates, confirming that steering alters intermediate tokens rather than just the final word. In question answering, steering on the question mark or last word shifts both the answer noun and the preceding article (a vs. an) in the correct direction across all 23 models. In Gemma2 9B, patching just two attention heads (L30H3, L31H15) recovers 59–93% of the full steering effect on output logits, identifying a compact rhyme-planning circuit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing steering or interpretability methods in a head-to-head sense; instead it compares unsteered (baseline) generation against steered generation across 23 models and multiple metrics. The key comparison is between the unsteered baseline and the steered condition: for most models, steered rhyme-family fractions match or approach the model's own unsteered rhyming rate (Fig. 2), and steered regeneration rates closely track baseline regeneration rates (Fig. 3), indicating that steering manipulates the same planning circuit the model uses naturally. Newline-token steering is less effective than last-word steering but produces comparable planning metrics for Gemma2 9B and Gemma3 27B (Fig. 5), while for other model families (Llama, Qwen) newline steering is largely ineffective. The paper also contrasts its simple mean-activation-difference steering with more complex alternatives (probe-weight differences, SAE latent steering) in Appendix I, finding that all three methods produce qualitatively similar steered outputs, though the mean-difference approach is more robust and easier to scale. Relative to the prior CLT-based study of Claude 3.5 Haiku (Lindsey et al., 2025), this work extends the evidence to 23 open-weight models from 1B parameters upward, replacing the computationally expensive cross-layer transcoder with a single-vector intervention that requires no additional training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the pioneering qualitative case study of implicit planning in Claude 3.5 Haiku by Lindsey et al. (2025), which used a computationally expensive cross-layer transcoder (CLT) to identify rhyme-planning representations. The key methodological departure is replacing the CLT with simple mean activation difference steering (following Turner et al., 2023 and Arditi et al., 2024), making the analysis reproducible and scalable to 23 open-weight models. The paper also situates its findings within the broader debate on LLM planning capabilities, responding to skeptical positions (Kambhampati et al., 2024; Valmeekam et al., 2023) that focus on explicit planning, by demonstrating that a simpler, heuristic form of implicit planning is widespread. Evidence from unreliable chain-of-thought studies (Turpin et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2025) provides a complementary line of support for implicit forward planning in reasoning contexts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first quantitative, cross-model evidence that implicit planning is a universal mechanism in LLMs, present from 1B parameters upward, and offers a simple, reproducible methodology (mean activation difference steering plus regeneration and KL-divergence metrics) that the interpretability community can readily adopt. The identification of a minimal circuit (two attention heads plus downstream MLPs) implementing rhyme planning in Gemma2 9B is a concrete step toward mechanistic understanding, though it remains unclear whether other architectures share this structure. Open questions include whether the more elaborate newline-token circuit in select models (Gemma2 9B, Gemma3 27B) explains their superior planning, whether planning circuits are truly task-specific or share deeper structure, and what safety implications arise from the fact that planning representations can be externally manipulated. The study is limited to two tasks and 23 models, and the steering intervention, while effective, is a blunt instrument that may not capture the full richness of planning representations.
