## Problem

Greedy maximization of the log-determinant Fisher Information Matrix for instruction-tuning data selection enjoys a (1−1/e) submodular approximation guarantee, yet practitioners observe that marginal information gains collapse far faster than theory predicts. The paper identifies gradient conflicts—misalignment between per-sample gradients—as the missing factor that accelerates this decay, and formalizes the gap via an ε-decomposition linking conflict statistics to submodular curvature.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pool of instruction-response pairs (97.5K total, spanning math, code, and general knowledge) and computes per-sample gradients at a fixed checkpoint using a smaller proxy model (0.5B, same architecture as the 7B target). Selection operates on candidate pools of 120 samples, greedily picking k=12 per cycle with a configurable step interval T∈{1,10,50} between training updates. The output is a 10% selected subset used for LoRA fine-tuning (r=16, α=32) of LLaMA2-7B or Qwen2-7B, evaluated on 8 benchmarks.

## Method

SPICE scores each candidate x at iteration t by score(x|S_{t−1}) = Δ_x(S_{t−1}) − λ·conflict(x|S_{t−1}), where Δ_x is the Fisher marginal gain and conflict is a hinge penalty on negative cosine alignment to the running mean gradient. The theoretical core is an ε-decomposition that splits each marginal gain into a modular baseline and a perturbation term bounded by C·α²Σ_y(g_x^T g_y)²/(1+α‖g_x‖²), showing that gradient inner products control the submodular curvature c and hence tighten the greedy approximation factor from (1−1/e) toward 1 as conflicts diminish. The algorithm supports adaptive early stopping (halt when Δ falls below ω·Δ_first) and uses a proxy model to reduce selection cost to O(k|D|d) time with O(md) peak memory, completing full selection in ~20 GPU-hours versus >100 for prior Fisher-based methods.

## Results

On Qwen2-7B, SPICE with only 10% of the training data achieves an average score of 58.0 across 8 benchmarks, surpassing full-data tuning (56.4) by +1.6 points and all 8 baselines. The largest individual gains are on IFEval (+5.1 over full data) and GSM8K (+2.5), with SPICE achieving the best result on 7 out of 8 benchmarks. On LLaMA2-7B, SPICE similarly achieves 31.1 average (vs. 30.8 full-data), winning on 5 of 8 benchmarks including ARC-C (+1.6) and IFEval (+3.2). The total selection cost is approximately 20 GPU-hours, well below the >100 GPU-hours required by prior gradient-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPICE outperforms all 8 baselines on average across both model families. On Qwen2-7B, the closest competitor is DPP (57.0 average), which SPICE exceeds by +1.0; the largest margin is over LESS (+2.8) and IFD (+2.6). On LLaMA2-7B, SPICE leads LEAD (30.5) by +0.6 and DPP (30.4) by +0.7, with the widest gap over TSDS and IFD (+1.1 each). SPICE is best on 7/8 Qwen2 benchmarks (tied on BBH at 61.0 with LESS, DPP, and LEAD) and 5/8 LLaMA2 benchmarks (losing on BBH, TruthfulQA, and MBPP). Notably, SPICE's IFEval gain of +5.1 over full-data on Qwen2 is the single largest improvement across all methods and benchmarks, suggesting the conflict-aware penalty is particularly effective for instruction-following tasks. All comparisons use the same 10% data budget, and SPICE achieves these results with a total selection cost of ~20 GPU-hours versus >100 GPU-hours for prior gradient-based methods like LESS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends FisherSFT (Deb et al., 2025), which performs greedy maximization of the log-det Fisher Information Matrix for instruction-tuning data selection, by identifying gradient conflicts as the missing factor that explains why empirical greedy selection deteriorates faster than the classical (1−1/e) submodular guarantee predicts. It draws on gradient-alignment literature (PCGrad, CAGrad) from multi-task learning but reframes the problem in the language of submodular curvature and approximation theory rather than training dynamics. The method also leverages AdaFisher's diagonal Kronecker approximation to reduce FIM computation from O(d²) to O(d), and adopts a proxy-model selection schedule similar to LEAD and LESS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ε-decomposition providing a quantitative link between gradient inner products, submodular curvature, and greedy approximation quality is a genuinely novel theoretical contribution that explains a long-observed theory-practice gap in information-based data selection. Practically, SPICE achieves full-data-level accuracy with 10% of the data at ~20 GPU-hours total cost, but the margin over the strongest baseline (DPP) is modest (+1.0 average on Qwen2), and LLaMA2 shows a −3.2 drop on TruthfulQA, suggesting the conflict penalty may not transfer uniformly across architectures. Open questions include whether the framework extends to multimodal or RL settings, whether the proxy-model assumption holds at 70B+ scale, and whether the curvature-based stopping criterion generalizes beyond the 10% budget regime.
