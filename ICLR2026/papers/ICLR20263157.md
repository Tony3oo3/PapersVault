## Problem

Current MLLM post-training paradigms are predominantly text-centric, treating dense visual inputs merely as contextual evidence from which sparse cues are extracted for text-based reasoning, leaving the model's intrinsic visual perception undervalued. Existing approaches to improve visual understanding either require integrating additional visual generative modules or rely on text as an intermediate mediator, raising the question of whether visual understanding can be enhanced without altering the model's architecture or output format.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263157/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For images, the input is a 3×3 grid of shuffled non-overlapping patches (from COCO images with side length ≥ 84 px); for videos, six shuffled temporal clips (from LLaVA-Video, ≥ 24 s, with 5% boundary frames trimmed to prevent frame-matching shortcuts, max 12 frames per clip at 128×28×28 resolution); for 3D, an RGB image with six depth-annotated points (from ScanNet, depth range 0.1–10 m, points separated by ≥ 40 px and ≥ 0.2 m depth difference). In all cases the model outputs a natural-language permutation of K indices (comma-separated) enclosed in <answer> tags, preceded by a reasoning trace in <think> tags, with no pixel-level or generative output required.

## Method

Visual Jigsaw formulates visual understanding as a self-supervised ordering problem: visual inputs are partitioned into K elements via a modality-specific rule (spatial patches, temporal clips, or depth-sorted points), shuffled, and the model must predict the correct permutation in natural language. Training uses GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) with a graded reward that assigns 1 for an exact permutation match, γ·(fraction of correctly placed indices) for a valid but incomplete permutation (γ = 0.2), and 0 for any invalid output, plus a 0.2 format reward for correct <think>/<answer> structure. The key design insight is that jigsaw ordering is a lightweight, verifiable proxy for visual reconstruction that requires no additional generative components and integrates seamlessly with text-only MLLMs. The authors demonstrate that RL post-training (GRPO) substantially outperforms SFT on the same jigsaw task, as RL promotes generalization of the acquired visual skills to downstream benchmarks rather than overfitting to the jigsaw format. Experiments on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and MiMo-VL-7B show consistent gains in fine-grained perception, temporal reasoning, and 3D spatial understanding across 30+ benchmarks.

## Results

On image benchmarks, Visual Jigsaw (GRPO) improves over Qwen2.5-VL-7B by +6.00 on MMVP, +6.06 on MMStar (fine-grained), +5.90 on DA-2K, and +3.75 on HR-Bench-8K, with consistent gains across all 13 evaluated benchmarks. On video benchmarks (32 frames), Video Jigsaw yields +5.52 on AoTBench, +3.20 on AoTBench-group, and +3.13 on Video-MME over the base model. On 3D benchmarks, 3D Jigsaw achieves the largest single-benchmark gain of +17.11 on DA-2K (54.45→71.56) and +15.34 on SAT-Real (48.66→64.00), while also improving multi-view and egocentric video 3D tasks by 1.5–3.3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three RL post-training baselines (ThinkLite-VL, VL-Cogito, LLaVA-Critic-R1), Image Jigsaw achieves the highest scores on 11 of 13 image benchmarks, with the largest margins on MMVP (+5.33 over ThinkLite-VL, +7.33 over LLaVA-Critic-R1) and MMStar fine-grained (+5.86 over VL-Cogito). The only benchmark where a baseline edges ahead is MME-RealWorld, where VL-Cogito scores 47.63 vs. Image Jigsaw's 45.96, and MMBench where ThinkLite-VL (84.19) is marginally below Image Jigsaw (84.45). For video, Video Jigsaw outperforms Video-R1 across all 13 benchmarks at every frame setting (16/32/64), with the largest gap on AoTBench (+6.15 at 16 frames, +5.52 at 32 frames). On 3D tasks, 3D Jigsaw improves DA-2K by +17.11 and SAT-Real by +15.34 over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base, substantially exceeding the 1.5–3.3 point gains on other 3D benchmarks. The SFT variant of Image Jigsaw underperforms the RL variant on most benchmarks and degrades on LISA-Grounding (66.59 vs. 74.54) and OVD-Eval (34.35 vs. 36.49), confirming that RL generalizes the jigsaw skill more effectively than supervised fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the classic jigsaw ordering task from self-supervised pre-training (Noroozi & Favaro, 2016; Ahsan et al., 2019) as a post-training signal for MLLMs, optimized via GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) in the RLVR framework. It departs from prior visual reconstruction approaches (X-Former, Wang et al. 2025a,b) that require additional generative modules, and from the closest prior work Jigsaw-R1 (Wang et al., 2025g) which struggles even on 2×2 puzzles. The key conceptual shift is positioning jigsaw as a vision-centric post-training task rather than a pre-training pretext, leveraging the fact that RL generalizes better than SFT for transferring the acquired perceptual skills to downstream tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a lightweight, architecture-free self-supervised ordering task can substantially improve fine-grained perception, temporal reasoning, and 3D spatial understanding in MLLMs, offering a practical complementary direction to the dominant text-centric RL post-training paradigm. The 3D results (+17 on DA-2K, +15 on SAT-Real) are particularly striking, suggesting that simple depth-ordering signals can unlock significant spatial reasoning gains. Open questions include whether harder configurations (4×4, 8-clip) can be made to work with curriculum learning or higher-resolution data, whether the approach scales to larger models, and whether the 3D variants (view-motion, BEV-pose matching) can succeed on models with stronger 3D priors.
