## Problem

RLVR training for LLM reasoning suffers from a dual entropy dilemma: entropy collapse (premature convergence) and entropy explosion (uncontrolled stochasticity that degrades learning signals). Prior work addresses only collapse via token-level heuristics (e.g., Clip-Higher), which inadvertently induces explosion. The paper identifies the mean-baseline in value-free RL (GRPO/DAPO) as the root cause, showing it systematically mishandles negative-advantage samples under reward outliers, and argues that baseline design—not token-level tuning—is the primary mechanism for stable RLVR scaling.

## Input / Output

The method operates on math-reasoning queries (e.g., AIME/AMC problems). For each query, G responses are sampled from the current policy with binary correctness rewards (1 if the final answer matches ground truth, 0 otherwise). The K-quantile baseline is computed per query group from these G binary rewards, producing a response-level advantage that is zero for roughly 80% of responses and non-zero only for the informative minority (rare successes on hard queries or residual failures on easy queries). Training uses the DAPO-Math-17K dataset with a global batch size of 512 and no KL or entropy regularization terms.

## Method

QAE replaces the group-mean reward baseline in GRPO/DAPO with a right-continuous K-quantile of the group's binary rewards. For binary rewards this reduces to a deterministic threshold on the empirical success rate p(q): when p(q) ≤ 1−K (hard queries), the baseline is 0 so only correct responses receive positive advantage (exploitation of rare successes); when p(q) > 1−K (easy queries), the baseline is 1 so only incorrect responses receive negative advantage (suppression of residual failures). Under first-order softmax updates, the paper proves a two-sided entropy safety guarantee: the K-quantile baseline attains the minimal one-step entropy increment in the low-success regime (explosion-proof) and the maximal increment in the high-success regime (collapse-proof), for any competing baseline b ∈ [0,1]. The resulting objective (Proposition 4.1) replaces GRPO's symmetric √(p(1−p)) query weight with asymmetric monotonic factors √(p/(1−p)) or √((1−p)/p), concentrating updates on the most informative samples. The method is a one-line swap orthogonal to token-level controls (Clip-Higher, CLIP-Cov, KL-Cov) and sequence-level optimization (GSPO), requiring only the choice of K (default 0.4).

## Results

QAE (K=0.4) yields consistent pass@1 gains across all model sizes and RL recipes: the largest improvement is +21.5% on AIME'24 (Clip-Higher, Qwen3-8B-Base, 39.69→48.23), followed by +13.3% on AIME'25 (CLIP-Cov, 33.02→37.40) and +8.6% on AIME'24 (GSPO, Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base, 43.75→47.50). Pass@16 improvements are more modest (0–12.6%), indicating that QAE primarily sharpens the best-of-k selection rather than expanding the solution set. The only declines are minor: −0.5% pass@1 on AIME'24 (KL-Cov) and −0.7%/−2.2% on AMC'23 (GSPO), both within noise for near-saturated benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DAPO with Clip-Higher (Yu et al., 2025), QAE delivers the largest single gain in the paper: +21.5% pass@1 on AIME'24 (39.69→48.23) and +6.7% on AIME'25 (32.71→34.90) for Qwen3-8B-Base, while pass@16 stays within 0.6–2.2%. Against CLIP-Cov and KL-Cov (Cui et al., 2025), QAE adds +13.3% pass@1 on AIME'25 and +8.6% on AIME'24 (CLIP-Cov), and +12.6% pass@16 on AIME'25 (KL-Cov), confirming orthogonality with token-level entropy controls. Against GSPO (Zheng et al., 2025) on the larger Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base, QAE improves pass@1 by +4.3% (AIME'25) and +8.6% (AIME'24) with pass@16 gains of +3.0% and +5.6%, though it slightly regresses on the near-saturated AMC'23 (−0.7% pass@1, −2.2% pass@16). In summary, QAE wins or ties on 22 of 24 benchmark-metric cells, with the two losses confined to already-saturated AMC'23 scores under GSPO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GRPO/DAPO lineage of value-free policy-gradient methods for LLM reasoning, adopting DAPO's Clip-Higher and dynamic sampling as its baseline. It departs from the dominant token-level entropy-control paradigm (Clip-Higher, Clip-Cov, KL-Cov) by identifying the group-mean advantage baseline as the root cause of both entropy collapse and explosion, and replaces it with a group-wise K-quantile (QAE). The discriminative decomposition of the GRPO objective follows DisCO (Li et al., 2025), and the entropy-covariance identity is adapted from Cui et al. (2025), but the key conceptual move is reframing entropy regulation as a baseline-design problem rather than a token-level clipping problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QAE offers a minimal, composable intervention (one-line baseline swap) that simultaneously addresses entropy collapse and explosion—two failure modes the field has typically treated separately—while provably bounding one-step entropy change under first-order softmax updates. The 80% response-level sparsity finding reveals substantial redundancy in mean-baseline credit assignment, suggesting that 'who learns' matters more than 'how much to update.' Open questions include whether dynamic or automatically-adapted K schedules can remove manual tuning, whether the bandit-reduction theory extends to full sequence-level policies, and whether the approach generalizes beyond binary verifiable rewards to denser or shaped reward signals.
