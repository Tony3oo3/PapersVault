## Problem

Existing positional encodings for Transformers—multiplicative rotations (RoPE) and additive logit biases (ALiBi, FoX)—are developed in isolation, with no shared algebraic structure explaining their common properties (exact relativity, norm preservation, streaming cacheability). RoPE in particular fixes coordinate planes and a log-uniform frequency spectrum, limiting cross-subspace coupling and contextual adaptivity, while additive methods lack a principled group-theoretic derivation. The paper asks whether a single group-action framework can subsume both families, recover each as an exact special case, and yield principled learned and contextual extensions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-head query and key vectors (d-dimensional, typically d=128) along with integer position indices n∈ℤ (or continuous t∈ℝ for phase-modulated variants). Multiplicative GRAPE outputs rotated query/key vectors in SO(d) applied via a closed-form rank-2 update requiring only O(d) inner products per token; Additive GRAPE outputs an affine logit bias of the form (j−i)·ω·(λ_q+λ_k) computed in a (d+2)-dimensional homogeneous lift. Both paths feed directly into standard multi-head attention with a streaming KV-cache policy identical in cost to RoPE—cache the encoded key once at arrival, rotate only the current query at each decoding step.

## Method

GRAPE encodes position n as a one-parameter group element G(n)=exp(nωL) and splits into two families. Multiplicative GRAPE uses a rank-2 skew-symmetric generator L=abᵀ−baᵀ∈so(d), whose exponential admits a closed-form Rodrigues-type formula (I + (sin s/s)L + (1−cos s)/s²·L²) enabling O(d) application without materializing the full matrix; RoPE is recovered exactly when d/2 commuting generators act on canonical coordinate planes with log-uniform frequencies, and learned orthogonal bases or compact non-commuting mixtures (via thin Schur compression to rank r) extend this at O(d) or O(rd) cost per head. Additive GRAPE lifts to GL(d+k) with a rank-1 nilpotent generator A (A²=0), so exp(nωA)=I+nωA is unipotent; the paired inverse-transpose scoring in homogeneous coordinates yields an exact relative law and recovers ALiBi (constant slope β_h) and FoX (per-token log-forget gates) as closed-form instances. Path-Integral Additive GRAPE generalizes further by allowing endpoint-dependent edge potentials ψ_h(t,ℓ) computed from token-local positional embeddings, producing causal path-sum biases that remain streaming-compatible and compose additively with the multiplicative rotation in a joint GL(d+2) block-diagonal action.

## Results

On medium models (353M) trained on FineWeb-Edu 100B, GRAPE-AP achieves the best average 0-shot accuracy of 53.25 across 7 benchmarks (ARC-E, ARC-C, HellaSwag, OBQA, PIQA, WinoGrande, SciQ), outperforming RoPE (51.73), ALiBi (52.87), and FoX (52.96). On large models (770M), GRAPE-AP reaches 56.91 average, beating RoPE (55.76), ALiBi (56.44), and FoX (56.30). With KV-shift enabled, GRAPE-AP(w/KV-shift) achieves 53.46 on medium models, the best among all methods in that setting. The paper also reports that RoPE suffers training instability at scale (Figure 3a) while GRAPE variants train steadily.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRAPE-AP consistently outperforms all three baselines (RoPE, ALiBi, FoX) on the medium model without KV-shift, achieving 53.25 average vs. RoPE's 51.73 (+1.52), ALiBi's 52.87 (+0.38), and FoX's 52.96 (+0.29). On the large model, the gap widens: GRAPE-AP reaches 56.91 vs. RoPE (55.76, +1.15), ALiBi (56.44, +0.47), and FoX (56.30, +0.61). With KV-shift enabled on medium models, GRAPE-AP(w/KV-shift) leads at 53.46, ahead of FoX(w/KV-shift) (53.32), ALiBi(w/KV-shift) (53.18), and RoPE(w/KV-shift) (52.89). On large models with KV-shift, FoX(w/KV-shift) narrowly edges out GRAPE-AP(w/KV-shift) by 0.23 points (57.09 vs. 56.86), though GRAPE-AP still beats RoPE(w/KV-shift) by 1.54 points. The multiplicative GRAPE variants (GRAPE-M-ctx, GRAPE-M-nonctx) underperform RoPE on the large model (54.73–54.81 vs. 55.76), suggesting the additive/path-integral formulations are the primary source of the performance gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRAPE unifies two previously separate families of positional encoding—multiplicative rotations (RoPE) and additive logit biases (ALiBi, FoX)—under a single group-theoretic framework: SO(d) one-parameter subgroups for the former and unipotent subgroups of GL(d+k) for the latter. It departs from LieRE, which parameterizes dense skew-symmetric generators requiring O(d³) matrix exponentials, by restricting to rank-2 generators with a closed-form Rodrigues-type formula at O(d) cost per head. The paper also extends RoPE beyond its fixed coordinate-plane, log-uniform-spectrum design to learned orthogonal bases and compact non-commuting mixtures, and introduces path-integral additive biases (GRAPE-AP) that generalize FoX's endpoint-independent forgetting to content-dependent, endpoint-varying edge potentials while preserving exact relativity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified group-theoretic lens gives practitioners a principled design space: any new positional mechanism can be checked for exact relativity, streaming cacheability, and norm behavior by identifying its group action, and the O(d) per-head cost makes learned or contextual variants tractable at scale. However, empirical validation is limited to 353M and 770M models with 4096-token context on a single dataset, and the gains over strong baselines (FoX, ALiBi) are modest (≈0.3–0.6 points average), leaving open whether the learned-subspace and non-commuting extensions deliver meaningful benefits at the multi-billion scale where RoPE dominates. A key open question is whether the path-integral additive bias (GRAPE-AP) provides a genuine long-context advantage over simpler linear biases once context length and model size increase substantially.
