## Problem

Speech LLMs underperform their text-based counterparts on complex reasoning tasks, and naively inserting chain-of-thought reasoning between the user's question and the spoken response introduces unacceptable latency for real-time voice interaction. The paper asks whether a speech LLM can be taught to 'think while listening'—beginning its reasoning trace before the user has finished speaking—thereby recovering the accuracy gains of CoT without the latency penalty.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262455/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes streaming user audio encoded by the Mimi codec at 12.5 Hz (8 codebooks, 2048 tokens per codebook) and produces a spoken response on the system audio stream. On the text monologue stream, it generates a text-based CoT reasoning trace (delimited by <start_cot>/<end_cot> tokens) interleaved with streaming ASR tokens of the user's speech (word-aligned, 6-token/480 ms look-ahead) and the response text, using <switch_cot> and <switch_asr> tokens to alternate between reasoning and transcription modes. Latency is measured in 80 ms audio tokens between the end of the user question and the start of the spoken response.

## Method

The paper fine-tunes the multi-stream Moshi model to generate text-based CoT on its text monologue stream, showing text CoT is as accurate as speech CoT but 2× more token-efficient. To enable early reasoning, the authors introduce a Question Completeness metric ζ(p) = 1 − D_KL(X_N‖X_p)/D_KL(X_N‖X_0), computed via an external LLM, which identifies the inflection point where sufficient semantic information has been received to begin reasoning; the CoT is then left-shifted to this point during SFT so the model learns to emit <switch_cot> at the right moment. Finally, DPO preference tuning on rejection-sampled pairs (correctness-based and length-based) further improves accuracy at equivalent latency and reduces CoT length by ~70% without accuracy loss, pushing the accuracy–latency Pareto frontier.

## Results

On the SRQA benchmark, Moshi+CoT achieves an average absolute accuracy improvement of 29.1% over the Moshi baseline, with most reasoning tasks improving by 2–3× (e.g., ARC-E: 30.2→77.7, SIQA: 22.8→56.1, PIQA: 23.8→56.9). Among speech LLMs of similar size, the method places top-2 on all five reasoning tasks despite being pretrained on only 2.1T tokens (vs. 18T for Kimi-Audio). The streaming user ASR ablation shows that removing aligned user transcripts degrades accuracy by 15–22 points on reasoning tasks while leaving factuality (LLaMA-QS) nearly unchanged, confirming that explicit user understanding drives the reasoning gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Moshi baseline (same 2.1T pretraining), Moshi+CoT wins decisively on every reasoning task: ARC-E 77.7 vs. 30.2, ARC-C 59.8 vs. 21.5, SIQA 56.1 vs. 22.8, PIQA 56.9 vs. 23.8, and GSM8K 16.1 vs. 8.7. Among other speech LLMs, Moshi+CoT outperforms Qwen2-Audio-7B (2.4T pretrain) on all five reasoning tasks (e.g., SIQA 56.1 vs. 21.9, PIQA 56.9 vs. 24.5) and beats Kimi-Audio-7B (18T pretrain) on SIQA (56.1 vs. 32.9) and PIQA (56.9 vs. 34.4), though it trails Kimi on ARC-E (77.7 vs. 83.0) and ARC-C (59.8 vs. 71.5). On GSM8K, the method is competitive with Qwen2-Audio (16.1 vs. 18.1) and Kimi-Audio (16.1 vs. 15.7), but all speech LLMs remain well below text LLMs (LLaMA2: 29.4, Gemma: 43.1). On the factuality task LLaMA-QS, Moshi+CoT (57.8) improves over the baseline (42.8) but still lags behind Qwen2-Audio (64.7), Kimi-Audio (61.7), and text LLMs (69–71%), indicating that CoT fine-tuning primarily benefits reasoning rather than factual recall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends chain-of-thought fine-tuning (Wei et al., 2022) from text LLMs to the multi-stream speech LLM setting, building directly on the Moshi architecture (Defossez et al., 2024) for its decoupled text monologue stream. It departs from concurrent 'thinking while speaking' approaches (STITCH, Mini-Omni-Reasoner) that interleave reasoning with spoken output, instead proposing a 'thinking while listening' paradigm where text-based CoT runs concurrently with incoming user audio. The Question Completeness metric, based on KL divergence between partial and full question likelihoods, is a novel contribution not present in prior CoT or speech LLM literature, and the DPO-based preference tuning builds on Rafailov et al. (2023) and Wu et al. (2025) for alignment in speech models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 70% latency reduction without accuracy loss is practically significant for real-time voice agents, where perceived response delay directly affects user experience. The Question Completeness metric offers a principled, controllable knob for the accuracy-latency trade-off that heuristic word-count shifting cannot match. Key limitations include evaluation on a single architecture (Moshi), a relatively small SRQA benchmark (~9K total examples), and reliance on an external LM (Llama3-8B) to compute QC during training, which limits deployment flexibility. Open questions include whether the paradigm generalizes to single-stream architectures, multi-turn conversations, and whether QC can be computed natively by the model without an external estimator.
