## Problem

Existing single-cell perturbation prediction methods assume cell-level correspondences and optimize pointwise losses that recover only the mean expression profile, ignoring higher-order distributional statistics such as variance shifts and subpopulation proportion changes. Additionally, these methods treat genes as largely independent features, failing to exploit gene-gene regulatory structure and making them vulnerable to the sparse, zero-inflated noise inherent in scRNA-seq data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262459/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a control (pre-perturbation) cell expression vector in log-normalized space, a multi-hot perturbation condition vector (e.g., which CRISPR guides or drugs are applied), and a static gene-gene co-expression graph (KNN=30 from signed Pearson correlations) as structural prior. Rather than outputting a single predicted expression vector, scDFM generates a full distribution of perturbed cells by integrating a learned velocity field from t=0 to t=1 via an ODE solver (Euler, 100 steps). At each training step, only a random gene subset of size s≪G is processed, and the MMD loss compares batches of generated terminal cells against ground-truth perturbed cells in that subset.

## Method

scDFM applies conditional flow matching directly in the high-dimensional log-normalized gene expression space (rather than a reduced PCA latent space), learning a time-dependent velocity field v_θ(x_t | t, c_x, c_p) that morphs a noisy source distribution into the target perturbed distribution along a linear interpolation path. The backbone is the PAD-Transformer, which at each layer injects the perturbation embedding via an MLP adapter, applies self-differential attention (difference of two softmax distributions with a learnable scaling factor) to suppress noisy gene activations, and applies cross-differential attention against the control representation with adaLN-Zero timestep modulation. A gene-gene co-expression graph constrains the cross-attention in the gene encoder so each gene token interacts only with its biologically correlated neighbors. The training objective combines the standard CFM velocity-matching loss with a multi-kernel MMD regularizer (Gaussian RBF mixture with dynamically selected bandwidths via a per-batch median heuristic) computed on one-step endpoint predictions x̂₁ = x_t + (1−t)·v_θ, enforcing both local trajectory consistency and global population-level distributional alignment.

## Results

On the Norman additive split, scDFM achieves the best MSE (0.00315), MAE (0.02155), and Discrimination Score (0.9737) across all baselines, reducing MSE by 19.6% over the strongest competitor CellFlow (0.00392). Under the stricter Systema-style metrics, scDFM attains the highest Pearson Δ̂₂₀ (0.9260) and DE-Spearman ρ (0.5705). On the more challenging holdout split (unseen singles and their combinations), scDFM again leads on L2 (1.6186 single / 2.0309 double), MSE, and DS, while on the ComboSciPlex drug-perturbation dataset it achieves the lowest L2 (1.6567), MSE (0.0028), and MAE (0.0220) with the highest DE-Spearman ρ (0.8289).

## Baselines & Comparisons

scDFM outperforms all six deep-learning baselines on the Norman additive split. Against the closest competitor CellFlow (a flow-matching model in 50-dim PCA space), scDFM reduces MSE by 19.6% (0.00315 vs. 0.00392), improves MAE (0.02155 vs. 0.02207), and raises the Discrimination Score from 0.9321 to 0.9737, indicating better preservation of population-level structure. The simple Additive baseline (linear superposition of single-perturbation deltas) remains competitive on Pearson Δ (0.9024 vs. 0.8853) and Pearson Δ̂₂₀ (0.9244 vs. 0.9260), consistent with recent findings that mean-level prediction can be surprisingly strong; however, scDFM clearly wins on MSE, MAE, DS, and DE-Spearman ρ. Foundation models Geneformer and scGPT underperform substantially: Geneformer achieves a lower L2 (1.9132) but its DE-Spearman ρ (0.3741) and DS (0.8241) are far below scDFM, while scGPT nearly collapses on DE-Spearman (1.07e-5) and DS (0.5404). GEARS and CPA suffer from large pointwise errors (MSE 0.01387 and 0.03435 respectively) and weak distributional alignment. On the holdout setting with unseen perturbations, scDFM's advantage widens, achieving the best DE-Spearman ρ (0.6957 single, 0.5676 double) and DS (0.8914 single, 0.9189 double), while on ComboSciPlex it beats CPA on DE-Spearman (0.8289 vs. 0.7906) and Pearson Δ (0.8933 vs. 0.8150) despite a slightly lower DS (0.8776 vs. 0.8980).

## Relation to Prior Work

scDFM builds on conditional flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022) as its generative backbone, but departs from prior flow-matching perturbation models like CellFlow by operating directly in the high-dimensional gene expression space rather than a reduced PCA latent space. The PAD-Transformer backbone repurposes the differential attention mechanism from Ye et al. (2025) and augments it with a gene-gene co-expression graph mask and per-layer perturbation injection via adaLN-Zero modulation. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against two limitations of prior perturbation predictors: the reliance on cell-level pointwise losses that ignore population-level distributional shifts, and the treatment of genes as independent features that fails to capture regulatory dependencies. The MMD regularization is the key methodological departure, adding a distribution-level alignment objective on top of the standard velocity-matching loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that distribution-level fidelity (via MMD) is a critical component for realistic perturbation prediction, with ablations showing the sharpest performance drop when MMD is removed. However, the evaluation is limited to two datasets (Norman in K562, ComboSciPlex in A549), and the authors acknowledge that the linear interpolant in log-expression space is likely suboptimal for capturing nonlinear biological transitions. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-cell-line, multi-perturbation-type benchmarks like ARC-state or the Virtual Cell Challenge, and whether more sophisticated graph priors (causal discovery, mutual information) could replace the Pearson-correlation-based mask. The framework could serve as a template for distribution-aware generative modeling in other noisy, high-dimensional scientific domains.
