## Problem

Existing RLVR methods for MLLMs optimize solely on final-answer correctness, which the paper shows via McNemar's test does not statistically improve multimodal perception capabilities. Since perception errors (e.g., hallucinating non-existent geometric elements) account for 68–78% of reasoning failures, this reward sparsity for perception becomes a bottleneck that prevents further gains in multimodal reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260913/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image paired with a text question and produces a structured response containing a chain-of-thought reasoning process enclosed in <think> tags followed by a final answer in <answer> tags. During training, each sample is augmented with a set of atomic visual annotations (e.g., 'GE=10', 'GE⊥DF') extracted from reference CoT trajectories, which serve as verifiable perception targets. A separate text-only judging LLM evaluates whether each annotation is accurately reflected in the model's generated response, producing binary judgments that drive the perception reward.

## Method

Perception-R1 extends standard accuracy-only RLVR (GRPO) with a novel visual perception reward that explicitly incentivizes accurate visual perception. Visual annotations are curated by first generating CoT trajectories with Gemini 2.5-Pro on Geometry3K (retaining only correct-answer trajectories), then prompting Qwen2.5-32B-IT to extract atomic visual facts from each trajectory, yielding 1,442 annotated samples. During RLVR training, a judging LLM (Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct) is prompted to assess whether each visual annotation is present in the policy model's response, and the visual perception reward is computed as the fraction of annotations correctly reflected. The final reward combines format reward, accuracy reward, visual perception reward (weighted by γ=0.7), and an N-gram repetition penalty to counteract the increased repetition observed when the perception reward is introduced. This design avoids reward hacking by using verifiable textual annotations rather than a multimodal reward model, and achieves state-of-the-art results across 8 benchmarks with only 1,442 training samples.

## Results

Perception-R1-7B, trained on only 1,442 Geometry3K samples, achieves the best or near-best performance across 8 multimodal benchmarks: 74.2 on MathVista, 54.3 on MathVerse, 28.6 on MathVision, 72.0 on WeMath, 60.8 on MMMU, 42.4 on MMMU-Pro, 64.5 on MMStar, and 27.5 on EMMA. It outperforms the strongest open-source reasoning baselines (Vision-R1-7B with 200K data and MM-Eureka-7B with 15K data) on all benchmarks except EMMA, where it trails by 0.7 and 0.6 points respectively. Statistical significance testing (one-sample t-test) confirms the average improvement over Vision-R1-7B and MM-Eureka-7B is significant with p < 0.01. On vision-only subsets of MathVerse and MMMU-Pro, Perception-R1 achieves 50.1 and 40.3 respectively, surpassing the previous SOTA by +2.5 and +2.7 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Perception-R1-7B (1.4K data) outperforms Vision-R1-7B (200K data) on all comparable benchmarks: MathVista 74.2 vs 73.1, MathVerse 54.3 vs 52.4, MMMU 60.8 vs 55.2, MMMU-Pro 42.4 vs 37.6, and MMStar 64.5 vs 62.6, while using over 100× less training data. Against MM-Eureka-7B (15K data), Perception-R1 leads on MathVista (74.2 vs 72.5), MathVerse (54.3 vs 51.9), WeMath (72.0 vs 65.6), MMMU (60.8 vs 58.0), and MMMU-Pro (42.4 vs 38.3), with only a 0.6-point gap on EMMA (27.5 vs 28.1). Compared to the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B-IT, Perception-R1 gains +6.1 on MathVista, +6.9 on MathVerse, +10.6 on WeMath, +5.6 on MMMU, and +5.4 on MMMU-Pro. The method also generalizes to Qwen2-VL-7B-IT (Perception-R1-Qwen2), where it beats R1-VL-7B on MathVista (64.9 vs 62.7), MathVerse (42.3 vs 40.8), WeMath (60.0 vs –†), MMMU (53.1 vs 52.3), and MMMU-Pro (35.2 vs 29.4). The only benchmark where Perception-R1 does not achieve the best open-source result is EMMA, where Vision-R1-7B (28.2) and MM-Eureka-7B (28.1) slightly edge it out (27.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLVR training paradigm (established by DeepSeek-R1 for LLMs and adapted to MLLMs by Vision-R1, MM-Eureka, R1-VL, and others) by identifying a critical gap: accuracy-only rewards fail to improve multimodal perception, which is a prerequisite for reasoning. It builds directly on GRPO as the optimization algorithm and departs from prior multimodal RLVR work by introducing a visual perception reward computed via a judging LLM that checks consistency between extracted visual annotations and model responses. The empirical finding (via McNemar's test) that RLVR-trained MLLMs show no statistically significant perception improvement over their base counterparts is the key diagnostic that motivates the new reward signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, testable diagnosis (perception is the bottleneck, not reasoning) that reframes how the community should think about multimodal RLVR training, and the visual perception reward offers a practical, low-cost mechanism to address it. The striking data efficiency (1,442 samples vs. 200K for Vision-R1) suggests richer reward signals can substitute for data scale. However, the training domain is limited to geometry (Geometry3K), the annotation pipeline depends on a proprietary model (Gemini 2.5-Pro), and the judging LLM's quality critically affects outcomes (7B judge underperforms the base model), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to diverse visual domains and whether the annotation bottleneck can be alleviated at scale.
