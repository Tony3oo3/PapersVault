## Problem

Camera-centric understanding (estimating camera geometry from images) and camera-centric generation (synthesizing images with specified camera parameters) have been treated as isolated problems in the literature. The core obstacle is a modality gap: camera parameters are numerical and abstract, making them difficult for large multimodal models to interpret or faithfully control, while existing vision-only methods lack the holistic spatial context needed for robust generalization. No prior work unifies both tasks in a single framework capable of reasoning about and controlling camera geometry.

## Input / Output

For understanding, the model takes a 512×512 RGB image and produces a chain-of-thought spatial reasoning text (aligning visual cues with photographic terms) followed by camera parameters (roll, pitch, FoV) serialized as discrete text tokens within an <answer> tag. For generation, it takes a text caption with numerical camera parameters in radians plus a pixel-wise Perspective Field camera map (encoding per-pixel up-vector and latitude angle) as continuous conditioning, and outputs a 512×512 image. Cross-view tasks extend the input with an initial view image and an additional yaw parameter for viewpoint deviation.

## Method

Puffin unifies a geometry-aligned vision encoder (C-RADIOv3-H, distilled from CLIP/SigLIP and DINO/SAM teachers), a Qwen2.5-1.5B LLM, and an SD3-Medium diffusion model within a single autoregressive-diffusion framework. The key insight is 'thinking with camera': numerical camera parameters are mapped to professional photographic terms (e.g., Dutch angle, tilt-up, close-up) that serve as intermediate supervisory signals, enabling the LLM to perform structured spatial reasoning by aligning spatially grounded visual cues (sky regions, foreground composition, depth ordering) with geometric context before predicting final parameters. For generation, a connector module with 64 learnable queries projects LLM hidden states into diffusion conditioning signals, while pixel-wise camera maps provide continuous geometric latents for fine-grained spatial control beyond what discrete tokens can capture. Training proceeds in four stages—modality alignment, supervised fine-tuning, SFT with thinking captions, and instruction tuning for cross-view tasks—on the 4M-sample Puffin-4M dataset of vision-language-camera triplets.

## Results

On the challenging Puffin-Und benchmark, Puffin achieves median errors of 0.41° (roll), 0.74° (pitch), and 1.21° (FoV), outperforming the previous best GeoCalib (0.92°, 2.18°, 5.04°) by 2.2×–4.2×. On MegaDepth, Puffin records 0.32° roll, 1.08° pitch, and 2.42° FoV errors, again leading all baselines. For camera-controllable generation on Puffin-Gen, Puffin reduces median gravity error from 15.34° (PreciseCam) to 3.43° and achieves the best FID of 69.46, compared to 83–96 for GPT-4o, Qwen-Image, and Nano Banana.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For camera understanding, Puffin decisively outperforms GeoCalib (the strongest prior method) on MegaDepth (roll 0.32° vs 0.36°, pitch 1.08° vs 1.94°, FoV 2.42° vs 4.46°) and Puffin-Und (roll 0.41° vs 0.92°, pitch 0.74° vs 2.18°, FoV 1.21° vs 5.04°), with the largest margin on FoV (4.2×). However, Puffin slightly underperforms GeoCalib on LaMAR roll (0.38° vs 0.28°) and FoV (3.62° vs 3.03°), and on TartanAir FoV (7.48° vs 4.90°), which the authors attribute to the fixed 512×512 training resolution and central-cropping evaluation protocol. All other baselines (DeepCalib, CTRL-C, MSCC, ParamNet, SVA, UVP) are consistently outperformed across every dataset and parameter. For camera-controllable generation, Puffin reduces median gravity error by 4.5× over PreciseCam (3.43° vs 15.34°) and by ~7× over GPT-4o (27.39°), while also achieving the best FID (69.46 vs 83–96), indicating superior spatial fidelity without sacrificing visual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously isolated research threads—single-image camera calibration (GeoCalib, ParamNet, UVP) and camera-controllable text-to-image generation (PreciseCam)—into a single multimodal framework. Architecturally, it borrows the learnable-query connector design from unified multimodal models (Pan et al. 2025; Wu et al. 2025c) to bridge an LLM (Qwen2.5-1.5B) with a diffusion model (SD3-Medium), and adopts Perspective Field (Jin et al. 2023) as the pixel-wise camera map for fine-grained spatial conditioning. The key conceptual departure is the 'thinking with camera' paradigm: rather than regressing numerical parameters directly, the model reasons through professional photographic terms (Dutch angle, tilt-up, wide-angle) that align with LMM prior knowledge, effectively treating camera geometry as a linguistic modality. A geometry-aligned vision encoder (RADIOv2.5, distilled from CLIP/SigLIP/DINO/SAM) replaces the standard LMM vision encoder to preserve geometric fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Puffin-4M (4M vision-language-camera triplets with spatial reasoning captions and cross-view pairs) fills a notable gap in multimodal spatial intelligence resources, and the Puffin-Und/Puffin-Gen benchmarks enable standardized evaluation of camera-centric generation where none previously existed. The 'thinking with camera' mechanism offers a general recipe for bridging the modality gap between abstract numerical parameters and LMM semantics, potentially transferable to other geometric or physical reasoning tasks. Limitations include the fixed 512×512 pinhole assumption (no radial distortion), slight underperformance on non-square aspect ratios (LaMAR), and the fact that the understanding gains over GeoCalib are modest on well-structured scenes while the generation gains over general-purpose multimodal models are large but partly attributable to task specialization. Open questions include whether the thinking paradigm scales to 6-DoF pose estimation, video, or multi-view consistency, and whether multi-scale training can resolve the aspect-ratio sensitivity.
