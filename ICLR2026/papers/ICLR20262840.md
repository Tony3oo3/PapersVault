## Problem

RL fine-tuning of LLMs typically assigns a single scalar reward to the entire generated sequence, making step-level credit assignment opaque. Sequential approaches that model generation as an MDP (e.g., OREO, DQO) address this but require training an auxiliary critic network, introducing significant computational overhead and training instability. The paper asks whether step-level value information can be learned implicitly from the policy itself, without any separate value estimator.

## Input / Output

Given a text query x, the method samples K complete responses from a frozen reference policy π_θ_old, scores each with a task-specific reward R(x,y), and updates the policy to better generate high-reward completions. Crucially, the loss is computed over randomly sampled prefix lengths t (where y_t is a token for text generation or a full reasoning step for math), so the policy is trained on partial sequences y_{<t} rather than only complete outputs. The output is an updated autoregressive policy π_θ that produces text responses with improved reward.

## Method

GN-IVO derives a closed-form link between the optimal policy's prefix distribution and a soft value function V(x, y_{<t}) (Theorem 3.1), then eliminates the need for an explicit value network by substituting the policy ratio π_θ(y_{<t}|x)/π_θ_old(y_{<t}|x) in place of exp(V). The final objective (Eq. 9) is a group-normalized weighted cross-entropy: for K sampled responses, it matches a target distribution proportional to exp(R(x,y^{(i)})/α) against the softmax of the K policy-ratio prefix probabilities. Because the partition function Z(x) is constant across all group members, it cancels in the softmax normalization, making the objective fully critic-free. The paper proves (Theorem 3.2) that the minimizer recovers the true value function up to a y_{<t}-independent additive constant, which does not alter the optimal policy (Corollary 3.3). In practice, the exponential reward weights are further normalized within the group for numerical stability.

## Results

On mathematical reasoning with Qwen2.5-Math-7B, GN-IVO achieves 62.5 Pass@1 on AMC 2023 (vs. 60.0 for GRPO and 57.5 for RLOO), 30.0 Pass@1 on AIME 2024 (vs. 26.6 for GRPO), and 13.3 Pass@1 on AIME 2025 (matching RLOO's best). With Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, it reaches 42.5 Pass@1 on AMC 2023 (vs. 35.0 for GRPO/RLOO) and 10.0 on AIME 2024 (vs. 6.6 for both). On text generation with Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, GN-IVO attains 3.370 Avg@1 on Helpful Assistant (vs. 2.013 for GRPO) and 3.347 on TL;DR summarization (vs. 3.181 for RLOO), with a geometric mean of 1.630 across three tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GN-IVO consistently outperforms the strongest critic-free baselines (GRPO and RLOO) on most benchmarks. On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, it leads GRPO by 2.5 points on AMC 2023 (62.5 vs. 60.0), 3.4 points on AIME 2024 (30.0 vs. 26.6), and 10.0 points on AIME 2025 Pass@3 (23.3 vs. 13.3); on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, it beats both by 7.5 points on AMC 2023 (42.5 vs. 35.0) and 3.4 points on AIME 2024 (10.0 vs. 6.6). Critic-based methods (PPO, DRO, OREO) show limited or negative gains, with OREO underperforming DRO on text generation due to inaccurate per-timestep value estimation. On text generation with Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, GN-IVO achieves 3.370 Avg@1 on Helpful Assistant versus 2.013 for GRPO and 1.528 for RLOO, and 3.347 on TL;DR versus 3.181 for RLOO. Even the one-step variant of GN-IVO (treating the full sequence as a single action) outperforms GRPO and RLOO on most text generation tasks, indicating the group-normalized objective itself provides a stronger learning signal than standard policy-gradient estimation. The paper attributes GN-IVO's advantage to combining group-based reward signals with fine-grained sequential credit assignment, a capability absent in bandit-formulated baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

GN-IVO synthesizes three distinct research threads: (1) DPO's key insight of eliminating explicit reward modeling via policy-ratio substitution is extended here to eliminate explicit *value* modeling; (2) GRPO/RLOO's group-based, critic-free advantage estimation is repurposed not for scalar advantage computation but for forming a distributional matching target over sampled responses; and (3) OREO/DQO's sequential MDP formulation for step-level credit assignment is retained, but the auxiliary value network is replaced by the policy itself through the group-normalized objective. The paper thus carves out a middle ground: sequential like OREO, critic-free like GRPO, and implicitly value-learned like DPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is a simpler, lower-memory training pipeline that still provides step-level credit assignment without the instability and overhead of a learned critic. The theoretical guarantee (value recovered up to an additive constant, preserving the optimal policy) gives practitioners confidence that the simplification does not sacrifice optimality. An open question is how much of the gain comes from the sequential structure versus the distributional matching objective itself, since the one-step variant already outperforms GRPO/RLOO on several tasks. The authors note a promising extension to diffusion model fine-tuning, and the method's reliance on K samples per query (shared with GRPO) remains a practical cost that could limit applicability in very low-data or very long-sequence settings.
