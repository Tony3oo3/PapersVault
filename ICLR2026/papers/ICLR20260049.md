## Problem

Existing FSCIL methods that pre-allocate fixed embedding spaces for novel classes suffer from a dual consistency failure: few-shot prototypes deviate from true class centers (feature inconsistency), and rigid structural constraints impose inflexible priors that cause inter-class confusion (structure inconsistency). This knowledge conflict between old and new classes remains unresolved because prior approaches optimize either features or structure in isolation, without a unified mechanism to ensure both are consistent across incremental sessions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (processed through a frozen ResNet backbone) and textual class names (parsed via WordNet for semantic attributes like synonyms, hypernyms, and part-whole relationships). Beyond standard class labels, the framework outputs calibrated class prototypes (blended via a tunable α between raw and MPC-corrected prototypes) and a session-specific geometric structure matrix Δ_t ∈ R^{d_g × N_t} that is updated per incremental session via compact SVD. The NCM classifier then assigns labels by nearest-neighbor distance between projected features and structure vectors, with no class-count prior required at test time.

## Method

ConCM addresses the dual consistency problem through two coupled modules. The Memory-aware Prototype Calibration (MPC) module mimics hippocampal associative memory: it extracts latent semantic attributes from base classes via WordNet, constructs a binary class-attribute association matrix, and trains a meta-learned encode-aggregate-decode network (with cross-attention over semantic and visual similarity) to retrieve and aggregate relevant attribute prototypes, correcting few-shot prototype bias. The Dynamic Structure Matching (DSM) module then constructs a per-session optimal geometric structure by solving a closed-form SVD-based optimization that simultaneously satisfies neural-collapse equidistance (geometric optimality) and maximal alignment with the previous session's structure (maximum matching), requiring no class-number priors. The projector is trained on Gaussian-sampled augmented data using a joint softmax matching loss against structure vectors and a supervised contrastive loss that treats structure vectors as positive anchors, explicitly encoding structural information into the embedding.

## Results

On mini-ImageNet (5-way 5-shot, 8 sessions), ConCM achieves an Average Harmonic Mean (AHM) of 59.78% and Final Accuracy (FA) of 59.92%, outperforming the previous best OrCo (AHM 57.30%, FA 56.04%) by +2.48% and +3.88% respectively. On CIFAR-100, ConCM reaches AHM 59.05% and FA 58.33%, surpassing OrCo (57.12%/52.19%) by +1.93%/+6.14%. On CUB-200 (10-way 5-shot, 10 sessions), ConCM attains AHM 62.20% and FA 62.66%, beating CLOSER (59.80%/62.38%) by +2.40%/+0.28%. On ImageNet-1k, ConCM achieves AHM 51.56%, a +9.30% improvement over NC-FSCIL (42.26%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConCM outperforms all 18 baselines across every dataset and metric. The closest competitor is OrCo (CVPR 2024), which constructs a globally orthogonal embedding space; ConCM surpasses it by +2.48% AHM on mini-ImageNet, +1.93% on CIFAR-100, and +2.59% on CUB-200, while also achieving a higher FA on mini-ImageNet (59.92 vs. 56.04) and CIFAR-100 (58.33 vs. 52.19). NC-FSCIL (arXiv 2023), which pre-assigns an equiangular tight frame as a fixed classifier, is outperformed by ConCM by +7.16% AHM on mini-ImageNet and a substantial +11.16% on CIFAR-100, demonstrating the advantage of dynamic over static structure. On CUB-200, ConCM edges out CLOSER (ECCV 2024) by +2.40% AHM and Mamba-FSCIL by +1.70% AHM. On the large-scale ImageNet-1k benchmark, ConCM achieves AHM 51.56% versus 42.26% for NC-FSCIL and 41.55% for OrCo, a gap of over 9 points. Notably, ConCM also uses less memory (9.57M vs. 24.78M for OrCo/NC-FSCIL) and 11% less training time by avoiding repeated backbone propagation.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConCM positions itself within the feature-space-based FSCIL lineage (FACT, NC-FSCIL, OrCo) that pre-allocates embedding structure for novel classes, but departs from their fixed-structure paradigm by replacing static equiangular tight frames or orthogonal targets with a session-adaptive SVD-based structure update. It also draws on feature-fusion methods (TEEN, PA) that exploit semantic relations between base and novel classes, but reframes this as hippocampal-inspired memory retrieval (attribute separation + completion) rather than direct prototype interpolation. The geometric optimality condition is inherited from neural collapse theory (Papyan et al., 2020), and the maximum-matching objective reduces to a classical orthogonally-constrained Procrustes problem solved in closed form via compact SVD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified 'dual consistency' framing (feature consistency via prototype calibration + structure consistency via dynamic matching) provides a principled decomposition of the knowledge-conflict problem that prior methods address only partially. The closed-form SVD solution for the optimal structure is attractive because it requires no class-count priors and guarantees minimal structural drift across sessions, making it practical for truly open-ended incremental settings. However, the reliance on WordNet for semantic attribute extraction limits generalizability to domains without rich lexical hierarchies, and the authors acknowledge that streaming/active-learning scenarios remain unexplored. The 3–4% AHM gains over strong baselines (OrCo, NC-FSCIL) are consistent but incremental, and the method's advantage narrows in later sessions where accumulated class count dilutes the structural benefit.
