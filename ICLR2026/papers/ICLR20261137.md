## Problem

In RAG systems, LLMs frequently favor their internal parametric knowledge over provided context, producing context-unfaithful hallucinations that are especially dangerous in high-stakes domains like medicine. Existing solutions—citation-based generation and faithfulness fine-tuning—either fail to guarantee content-source consistency or lack explicit attribution mechanisms, leaving the joint challenge of faithfulness and verifiable sourcing unresolved. The paper identifies an underexploited signal: an inverse correlation between response copying degree and hallucination density, suggesting that direct lexical reuse from context can serve as both a faithfulness mechanism and an inherent attribution trace.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language query Q and a retrieved context C as input and produces a contextually faithful answer A that maximizes verbatim reuse of lexical units from C. Copying degree is quantified via two metrics—Copy Coverage (κ, fraction of answer tokens covered by copy fragments) and Copy Density (δ, length-weighted variant emphasizing longer spans)—computed by a copy-fragment detection algorithm adapted from extractive summarization. In the training pipeline, each query-context pair yields roughly five DPO preference pairs, and the final model outputs a Chain-of-Thought response with a strict JSON structure containing both reasoning and a stamped final answer.

## Method

Copy-Paste is a two-stage generation paradigm. Stage 1 (Copy-Paste-Prompting) constructs high-copying candidate responses using three complementary prompting strategies: CP-Order (strict extractive sentence reordering), CP-Link (extractive core plus short generated transitions), and CP-Refine (iterative writer-reviewer loop with a composite copying-score threshold). Stage 2 (CopyPasteLLM) converts these candidates into DPO preference data through an automated pipeline that performs multi-criteria filtering (faithfulness, copying strength, relevance, fluency), an Elo-style LLM-as-Judge tournament diagnosing Twist and Causal hallucination modes, and answer stamping that appends gold answers to the top Copy-Paste candidate while appending incorrect answers to others to create informative negative pairs. Trained with LoRA (r=64) and DPO (β=0.3) on only 365 samples, CopyPasteLLM achieves 12.2–24.5% accuracy gains on FaithEval over the strongest baseline. A Context-Parameter Copying Capturing algorithm then reveals the mechanism: CopyPasteLLM does not enhance contextual representations but selectively recalibrates (suppresses) internal confidence in parametric knowledge, reducing competition from parametric priors during generation.

## Results

On the FaithEval counterfactual subset, CopyPasteLLM achieves 92.8% accuracy on Llama-3-8B, 89.3% on Mistral-7B-v0.2, and 92.6% on Llama-3.1-8B, surpassing the strongest baseline (Context-DPO) by 12.6, 12.2, and 24.5 percentage points respectively, while using only 365 training samples (1/50th of Context-DPO's 18,000). On ConFiQA's most challenging Multi-Conflict subset, CopyPasteLLM reaches 86.8% accuracy on Llama-3-8B and 82.5% on Mistral-7B-v0.2, outperforming even Context-DPO trained on seen data (80.4%). In non-counterfactual settings (Table 3), CopyPasteLLM improves average accuracy from 84.49% to 94.37% on ConFiQA-MR/MC, with a peak gain of 20.67% on Mistral-7B-v0.2 for the MR subset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CopyPasteLLM decisively outperforms all six baselines on FaithEval counterfactual accuracy. Against the strongest fine-tuning baseline Context-DPO (18,000 samples), CopyPasteLLM wins by 12.6 pp on Llama-3-8B (92.8 vs. 80.2) and 12.2 pp on Mistral-7B (89.3 vs. 77.1) while using 50× less training data. Against the prompting baseline Attributed (Zhou et al., 2023), the gap is even larger: 25.7 pp on Llama-3-8B and 27.1 pp on Llama-3.1-8B. The copy-guided decoding method CoCoLex (T.y.s.s et al., 2025) achieves near-perfect copy coverage (κ≈0.989) but suffers from 'blind copying' with only 17.9% Hit Rate on FaithEval; CopyPasteLLM achieves both higher accuracy (92.8 vs. 69.2 on Llama-3-8B) and a rational copying balance (κ≈0.844). Among fine-tuning baselines, Canoe (Si et al., 2025; 10,000 samples) and ParamMute (Huang et al., 2025b; 32,580 samples) trail CopyPasteLLM by 21.4 pp and 24.3 pp respectively on Llama-3-8B FaithEval accuracy. On ConFiQA-MC (the hardest multi-conflict subset), CopyPasteLLM on Mistral-7B (82.5%) even surpasses Context-DPO trained on seen ConFiQA data (80.4%), demonstrating that the 365-sample preference signal generalizes beyond its training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the contextual faithfulness literature for RAG, departing from prior fine-tuning approaches (Context-DPO, Canoe, ParamMute) and decoding strategies (CoCoLex) by reframing faithfulness as a lexical copying problem. It builds on the Knowledge Token Capturing framework (Bi et al., 2024) by extending it from short final answers to full Chain-of-Thought trajectories, and draws on the induction head mechanism (Olsson et al., 2022) to explain why copying mechanically anchors attention to context. The key conceptual departure is using copy-paste as an operational proxy for faithfulness and internalizing it via DPO, rather than directly optimizing attention patterns or suppressing parametric FFNs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50× data efficiency (365 vs. 18,000+ samples) combined with the simplicity of the approach suggests the field may be over-engineering contextual faithfulness solutions, and that a lexical grounding signal can serve as an effective training proxy for semantic faithfulness. The mechanistic finding that CopyPasteLLM suppresses parametric knowledge confidence rather than enhancing contextual representations offers a new lens on how models resolve knowledge conflicts. Key limitations include the assumption that context contains directly copyable evidence (failing on ambiguous 'Negotiation' samples), and the approach's dependence on context quality—over-reliance on copied text risks propagating errors from unreliable sources. Open questions include whether this generalizes to synthesis-heavy tasks requiring multi-source integration and whether the parametric suppression effect transfers across model scales.
