## Problem

On-policy RL algorithms like GRPO suffer from high-variance, unstable updates during early LLM reasoning training, where low-quality rollouts produce noisy gradients and the standard single-shot update per batch underutilizes available gradient information. Simply reusing rollout data off-policy degrades performance as training progresses, leaving an open question of how to exploit multiple gradient steps on the same batch while controlling distributional drift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260543/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts (math or coding problems) and generates G=8 candidate chain-of-thought responses per prompt under the current policy, with context lengths of 4096 or 8192 tokens depending on the base model. Rewards are computed on solution correctness, group-normalized advantages are derived per prompt, and the final output is a single reasoning response evaluated by Pass@1 accuracy across six math benchmarks (Math500, AIME24/25, AMC, MinervaMath, OlympiadBench) and LiveCodeBench for coding.

## Method

SFPO restructures each GRPO training step into a three-stage fast-reposition-slow trajectory: (i) K successive gradient updates on the same rollout batch to stabilize the search direction via cumulative gradient averaging; (ii) a reposition step that interpolates the fast endpoint back toward the on-policy starting point (θ̃ = θ⁰ + α(θᴷ − θ⁰)), acting as an implicit trust-region constraint inspired by the Lookahead optimizer; and (iii) one slow correction gradient step at the interpolated point to align with local curvature. An adaptive entropy-triggered schedule sets α=0 once a z-score on policy entropy exceeds a threshold, reverting to pure on-policy updates near convergence. The design leaves the GRPO objective, rollout generation, and KL/clip regularization entirely unchanged, making it a drop-in replacement that reduces rollouts by up to 4.93× and wall-clock time by up to 4.19× while improving average math accuracy by up to 2.80 points over GRPO.

## Results

On the primary DAPO+Math training set, SFPO outperforms GRPO across all five models, with average accuracy gains of +1.84 (Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B: 40.19 vs 38.35), +0.83 (Qwen2.5-Math-7B: 49.19 vs 48.36), +2.80 (DS-distilled-Qwen-1.5B: 50.53 vs 47.73), +2.57 (DS-distilled-Qwen-7B: 63.04 vs 60.47), and +1.60 (Qwen3-4B-Base: 45.59 vs 43.99) across six math benchmarks. In terms of efficiency, SFPO requires up to 4.93× fewer rollouts and achieves up to 4.19× less wall-clock time to match GRPO's best accuracy. On the larger Skywork-OR1 dataset (105K samples), SFPO also consistently outperforms GRPO on AIME24/25, and when applied on top of DAPO, SFPO further improves average accuracy by +1.26 (DS-Qwen-1.5B) and +1.39 (Qwen3-4B-Base).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), SFPO wins on every model and nearly every benchmark: the largest average gain is +2.80 on DS-distilled-Qwen-1.5B (50.53 vs 47.73), with particularly strong improvements on AIME25 (+7.50 absolute) and AMC (+3.42). The only benchmark where SFPO does not strictly win is AMC on Qwen3-4B-Base (57.23 vs 59.03), a small 1.8-point regression, while all other benchmarks show equal or better performance. Against DAPO (Yu et al., 2025), which already addresses zero-advantage batch variance via dynamic sampling, SFPO still improves average accuracy by +1.26 on DS-distilled-Qwen-1.5B (50.56 vs 49.30) and +1.39 on Qwen3-4B-Base (47.87 vs 46.48), confirming that SFPO's gains are orthogonal to advantage-computation modifications. In efficiency terms, SFPO reaches GRPO's best accuracy with 3.21×–4.93× fewer rollouts and 2.62×–4.19× less wall-clock time, while incurring only ~1.37× per-step compute overhead (K=3 inner updates). On the coding task (LiveCodeBench), SFPO peaks at 21.77 Pass@1 (Avg 4) versus GRPO's 18.91, extending the advantage beyond math reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

SFPO is built directly on GRPO's objective and rollout process, leaving the loss, KL/clip regularization, and data pipeline entirely unchanged; its novelty lies in restructuring the *update rule* itself into a three-stage fast-reposition-slow decomposition. The reposition (Stage II) is explicitly inspired by the Lookahead Optimizer's step-back interpolation, repurposed here as a trust-region mechanism to control off-policy drift in policy gradients. The paper positions SFPO as orthogonal to concurrent GRPO variants: DAPO addresses zero-advantage batches through dynamic sampling, Dr. GRPO removes normalization terms, and off-policy GRPO analyses (Mroueh et al.) modify the surrogate objective—whereas SFPO operates at a higher optimization layer and can be stacked on top of any of these without conflict.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: up to 4.93× fewer rollouts and 4.19× wall-clock reduction to match GRPO's best accuracy, with a plug-compatible design that requires no changes to existing training pipelines. This is significant because rollout generation accounts for >70% of per-step cost in LLM RL, so reducing rollout count directly cuts the dominant training bottleneck. Open questions include whether the one-shot entropy-triggered α schedule (once triggered, α=0 permanently) is optimal versus more nuanced adaptive rules, whether the approach generalizes beyond math/coding to open-ended reasoning, and whether the modular structure can support curriculum or meta-learning extensions as the authors speculate. A limitation is that the theoretical analysis (descent-lemma bound with F(K,α)) remains informal and the method has been validated primarily on 1.5B–7B models.
