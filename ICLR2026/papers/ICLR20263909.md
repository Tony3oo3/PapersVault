## Problem

Lipschitz-based certified robustness provides deterministic worst-case guarantees but has failed to scale beyond small VGG-style architectures (≤32M parameters), with performance degrading sharply on ImageNet. The core bottleneck is that existing methods rely on re-parameterization schemes (Cayley transform, LOT-Orth, Cholesky-Orth) to enforce orthogonality, introducing substantial per-update computational overhead and numerical fragility that prevents low-precision training and further scaling.

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet) and outputs a class label together with a certified ℓ₂ robustness radius. Certification is deterministic: for a predicted class, the radius is the minimum over all one-vs-rest binary sub-classifiers of |f(x)|/K where K is the network's Lipschitz constant (set to 1 by construction), guaranteeing the prediction is invariant to any perturbation within that ball without any probabilistic sampling.

## Method

LipNeXt combines two key innovations. First, a constraint-free manifold optimizer updates orthogonal weight matrices directly on the Stiefel manifold via Riemannian gradient descent, replacing the expensive matrix exponential with a norm-adaptive Taylor truncation (FastExp) that costs at most five matrix multiplications per step; two stabilization techniques—periodic SVD-based polar retraction at epoch boundaries and a tangent-space Lookahead that interpolates accumulated skew-symmetric updates rather than orthogonal matrices—prevent orthogonality drift. Second, a parameter-free Spatial Shift Module replaces depthwise convolutions by partitioning channels into five groups and applying circular shifts along horizontal and vertical axes; Theorem 1 proves this is the unique norm-preserving depthwise convolution, making it the theoretically grounded choice for tight 1-Lipschitz control. Each LipNeXt block applies orthogonal projections R and M around the shift, a β-Abs 1-Lipschitz activation (generalizing MinMax via a tunable nonlinearity ratio β), and the full network terminates with L2 spatial pooling to maintain the 1-Lipschitz bound end-to-end, enabling stable bfloat16 training at billion-parameter scale.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, LipNeXt L32W1024 (64M params) achieves 81.5% clean accuracy and 71.2% certified robust accuracy (CRA) at ε=36/255, outperforming all prior Lipschitz methods. On CIFAR-100, the larger L32W2048 (256M) reaches 57.4% clean and 44.1% CRA at ε=36/255. On ImageNet, scaling to 1–2B parameters yields 41.0% clean and 22.4% CRA at ε=1, improving CRA by up to 8% over prior Lipschitz models (e.g., LiResNet at 14.2%), while maintaining stable bfloat16 training at 17.8 min/epoch for the 2B model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LipNeXt outperforms all 11 baselines on nearly every metric across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny-ImageNet. On CIFAR-10, LipNeXt L32W1024 (64M) beats the closest competitor BRONet (68M) by +0.6% CRA at ε=36/255 (71.2 vs 70.6) and +2.0% at ε=72/255 (59.2 vs 57.2), while the larger L32W2048 (256M) leads all methods in clean accuracy (85.0%) and CRA at ε=36/255 (73.2%). The sole exception is CRA at ε=108/255 on CIFAR-10, where AOL Large (49.0%) exceeds LipNeXt (45.9%/43.3%), though AOL sacrifices 10+ points in clean accuracy (71.6%) and underperforms at smaller radii. On CIFAR-100, LipNeXt L32W2048 leads by +3.9% clean and +3.9% CRA at ε=36/255 over BRONet, and by +9.6% CRA over SLL X-Large (236M). On Tiny-ImageNet, LipNeXt L32W1024 surpasses BRONet by +3.0% CRA at ε=36/255 (32.0 vs 29.0) and +2.8% at ε=72/255 (21.8 vs 19.0). On ImageNet, the 2B LipNeXt model achieves 22.4% CRA at ε=1 versus 14.2% for LiResNet (51M), an 8.2-point gain, and 41.2% CRA at ε=36/255 versus 40.7% for BRONet with diffusion data.

## Relation to Prior Work

LipNeXt departs from the dominant re-parameterization paradigm (Cayley transform, LOT-Orth, Cholesky-Orth, AOL, CPL, SLL) by optimizing orthogonal parameters directly on the Stiefel manifold via a stabilized Manifold Adam optimizer, building on Bécigneul & Ganea's Riemannian Adam and adapting the Lookahead wrapper to the tangent space. Architecturally, it replaces the FFT-based or power-iteration-based convolutions in prior Lipschitz networks (Trockman & Kolter, Hu et al., Lai et al.) with a parameter-free Spatial Shift Module, grounded in a theorem showing that the only norm-preserving depthwise convolution is a spatial shift. The macro-structure and EMMA training loss are inherited from LiResNet (Hu et al., 2023), but the constraint-free optimization and convolution-free design are novel combinations that enable scaling to billion-parameter models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that Lipschitz-based certified robustness can track modern scaling trends with non-saturating gains, directly challenging the long-standing assumption that deterministic certification plateaus at small model sizes. The practical ability to train in bfloat16 (unlike LiResNet's float32 requirement or BRONet's complex32 overhead) means the approach can leverage ongoing hardware advances. Open questions include whether the method extends to image-text pretraining, whether the spatial shift module generalizes to non-grid data (e.g., point clouds, graphs), and whether the FastExp approximation remains stable at even larger scales where learning rates might not be sufficiently small.
