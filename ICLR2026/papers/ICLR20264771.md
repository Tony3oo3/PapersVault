## Problem

Existing feature visualization methods identify a unit's most exciting images (MEIs) but fail to characterize the full manifold of transformations under which a unit's response remains invariant, which is critical for understanding generalization in vision. Prior invariance tests rely on predefined transformations (e.g., affine) or strict representational identity (metamers), and gradient-based approaches cannot be applied to black-box or biological systems where internal representations are inaccessible. This paper addresses the need for a systematic, model-agnostic, gradient-free method to discover the actual axes of image variation a unit tolerates.

## Input / Output

SnS takes a reference image (a MEI computed via XDREAM or a natural image), a target network (ResNet50, ResNet18, VGG16_bn, or ViT), and two layer indices: κ (the representation space to stretch) and ℓ (the target unit's layer). A pretrained generative model (Deep Image Prior, fc7 variant) maps 4096-dimensional latent codes to 224×224 RGB images, which are evaluated by the target network. The output is a set of invariant images (maximally distant from the reference in the κ-layer representation while preserving the ℓ-layer unit's activation) or adversarial images (the reverse), along with quantitative characterizations of the invariance manifold including representational distances across all layers, intrinsic dimension estimates, and human/observer-network interpretability scores.

## Method

SnS formulates invariance and adversarial probing as a bi-objective Pareto optimization problem solved with the CMA-ES evolutionary strategy, requiring no gradients from the target network. The generative model ψ maps latent codes ξ ∈ ℝ⁴⁰⁹⁶ to images, and at each iteration the CMA-ES optimizer proposes a population of 50 candidate codes whose images are scored on two objectives: a stretch loss (maximizing L₂ distance from the reference in the κ-layer representation) and a squeeze loss (minimizing the change in the target unit's activation at layer ℓ). For invariance, stretching is applied at κ while squeezing at ℓ; for adversarial attacks, the roles are reversed. The key design choice is that κ can be set to pixel space (κ=0), a mid-level convolutional layer, or a deep convolutional layer, revealing hierarchically distinct invariance manifolds (luminance/contrast, texture, and pose/semantic changes, respectively). Pareto front ranking is used to handle the trade-off between the two objectives without manual weighting, with different sorting strategies for invariance (prioritizing proximity to target activation) versus adversarial (uniform random to promote exploration) experiments.

## Results

SnS generates effective adversarial examples that suppress readout unit activation by 111% ± 7% at a mean L2 pixel distance of 72 ± 12, and invariant images that preserve 66% of activation (only 34% ± 11% reduction) while achieving a mean L2 pixel distance of 271 ± 32 — substantially exceeding the median distance between ImageNet images and surpassing the pixel displacement of standard affine augmentations while better preserving unit response. A Support Vector Classifier trained on PCA features achieves near-perfect discrimination of invariant images by stretching level for the standard network and >80% for the robust network. Critically, human 12-AFC classification reveals that L2-robust network invariances are more interpretable at shallow stretching levels but become *less* interpretable at deeper layers, while standard network invariances show the *opposite* trend (more interpretable at deeper layers), causing the robust network's interpretability advantage to erode with depth (all p < 0.001).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SnS outperforms predefined affine transformations (rotation, translation, scaling) by discovering invariant images that are farther from the reference in pixel-space while better preserving the target unit's response, revealing that units tolerate variation along axes not captured by standard data augmentations. Compared to single-objective optimization (stretch-only or squeeze-only), the dual bi-objective SnS formulation is essential: optimizing only one loss component produces images that fail to satisfy the other objective, diverging drastically from SnS results. Against the metamer approach of Feather et al. (2023), SnS pushes invariant images to the *boundaries* of the invariance manifold (maximizing representational distance) rather than exploring a narrow neighborhood (minimizing distance), yielding complementary and sometimes opposite interpretability trends across depth. Between L2- and L∞-robustified ResNet50, L∞ robustification produces invariant images whose interpretability by L∞-robust observer networks remains consistently high or even increases with stretching depth, whereas L2 robustification shows the eroding-advantage pattern. SnS also generalizes across architectures (ResNet50, ResNet18, VGG16_bn, ViT) and to sparse representational subsampling, supporting its applicability to neuroscience where only a small fraction of units is recorded.

## Relation to Prior Work

SnS builds directly on the XDREAM framework (Xiao & Kreiman, 2020; Ponce et al., 2019), which used a pretrained generative network and CMA-ES evolutionary optimization to find most exciting images (MEIs) of visual units in a gradient-free manner. The key departure is reframing the optimization objective from single-objective activation maximization to a bi-objective 'stretch-and-squeeze' problem that simultaneously maximizes representational distance from a reference while preserving (or suppressing) target unit activation, thereby mapping invariance manifolds rather than isolated peak stimuli. This positions SnS as complementary to metamer-based approaches (Feather et al., 2023), which minimize representational distance and thus explore only a narrow neighborhood, and to gradient-based invariance mapping (Cadena et al., 2018; Baroni et al., 2023), which are limited to low-dimensional manifolds and require differentiable access to the network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most impactful finding is that L2 adversarial training, while improving human alignment at the pixel level, actually degrades interpretability of high-level invariances relative to standard networks — the opposite of what one might expect from a 'more human-like' training procedure. This reveals a fundamental limitation of current robustification strategies and provides a concrete diagnostic tool for measuring where artificial and biological vision diverge. For neuroscience, the gradient-free and model-agnostic design (validated with sparse subsampled representations) opens the door to probing invariance in biological neurons without requiring a faithful digital twin. Open questions include in vivo validation, scaling to larger models (DINO, ViT-22B), and whether the interpretability gap at deep layers can be closed by targeted training interventions.
