## Problem

Prior convergence analyses of MuON replace its key Newton-Schulz orthogonalization step with an exact SVD-based polar factor for analytical convenience, leaving open whether the actual SVD-free implementation used in practice admits principled nonconvex convergence guarantees. This paper closes that gap by proving convergence for MuON with a finite number of Newton-Schulz steps, quantifying how the approximation error affects rates, and showing how MuON's rank dependence compares to its vector-based counterpart, SGD with momentum.

## Input / Output

The method operates on matrix-valued parameters W ∈ R^{m×n} (e.g., linear layer weights), consuming mini-batch stochastic gradients and maintaining a momentum buffer M_t. Before each Newton-Schulz iteration, the momentum is scaled by α_t = max{1, ‖M_t‖_F} to ensure the spectral norm is at most 1, a prerequisite for the polynomial's convergence properties. The output is an updated parameter matrix W_t = W_{t-1} − η·O_t where O_t is the Newton-Schulz-orthogonalized direction, and the convergence guarantee is stated in terms of the nuclear norm of the gradient (a stricter condition than the Frobenius norm).

## Method

The paper proves that MuON with q Newton-Schulz steps converges to an ε-stationary point at the same iteration rate as the idealized SVD-polar variant, up to a multiplicative factor χ_q that depends on the polar approximation error ε_q. The key technical result is that ε_q decays doubly exponentially in q: the orthogonality residual contracts as δ_{j+1} ≤ δ_j^{κ+1} per Newton-Schulz step (where κ is the polynomial degree), yielding δ_q ≤ δ_0^{(κ+1)^q}. The Newton-Schulz polynomial p_κ is a Taylor truncation of 1/√λ at λ=1 with strictly positive coefficients, and the analysis leverages the monotonicity of τ(λ) = λ[p_κ(λ)]² to establish the residual contraction. The descent analysis decomposes the inner product ⟨∇f, O_t⟩ into an exact-polar term and a Newton-Schulz error term controlled by ε_q, then bounds the momentum mismatch via a true-vs-stochastic EMA decomposition. A secondary contribution shows that MuON sharpens the rank dependence in the leading O(T^{-1/4}) term from r^{1/2} (SGD with momentum) to r^{1/4}, explaining its geometric advantage over vector-based optimizers under the same nuclear-norm stationarity metric.

## Results

The paper's headline result is Theorem 1: MuON with a finite number q of Newton-Schulz steps attains ε-stationarity (in nuclear norm) at rate χ_q · O((rσ²LD/BT)^{1/4} + √(LD/T) + σr/√(BT)), matching the idealized SVD-polar rate up to a multiplicative factor χ_q. Theorem 2 shows χ_q ≤ [1 − δ_0^{(κ+1)^q}]^{−1/2}, so the gap to the SVD oracle vanishes doubly exponentially in q (and improves with polynomial degree κ). Empirically on CIFAR-10 (CIFARNet, ~2M params), MuON with q=1 already outperforms SGD-with-momentum, and q∈{2,3} nearly coincides with SVD-based MuON in train/test loss while reaching a target test loss substantially faster in wall-clock time.

**Table 1: Comparison of convergence rates (nuclear-norm stationarity metric).**

| Method | Convergence rate | Rank dependence (leading term) |
|---|---|---|
| SGD with momentum (Thm 3) | O((r²σ²LD/BT)^{1/4} + √(rLD/T)) | r^{1/2} |
| MUON with SVD (Thm 4) | O((rσ²LD/BT)^{1/4} + √(LD/T) + σr/√(BT)) | r^{1/4} |
| MUON with Newton-Schulz (Thm 1) | χ_q · O((rσ²LD/BT)^{1/4} + √(LD/T) + σr/√(BT)) | r^{1/4} (× χ_q → 1) |

where D = f(W_0) − f*, r = min{m,n}, T = iterations, B = batch size, L = Lipschitz constant, σ² = variance bound. The factor χ_q converges to 1 doubly exponentially in q: χ_q ≤ [1 − δ_0^{(κ+1)^q}]^{−1/2}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MuON with Newton-Schulz against two baselines under the same nuclear-norm stationarity metric. Against SGD with momentum (the vector-based counterpart), MuON sharpens the rank dependence in the leading O(T^{−1/4}) term from r^{1/2} to r^{1/4}, a geometric advantage from aligning updates with the leading singular structure of the momentum matrix; empirically, SGD-M shows a positive slope of ~0.3 in log-log gradient-norm-vs-rank plots while MuON variants are nearly flat. Against the idealized MuON with exact SVD polar steps (the oracle baseline), MuON with Newton-Schulz matches the iteration complexity up to a factor χ_q that converges to 1 doubly exponentially in the number of Newton-Schulz steps q, meaning even q=2 or 3 yields rates essentially indistinguishable from the SVD variant. Crucially, each Newton-Schulz step uses only matrix multiplications (no SVD), so the per-iteration cost is substantially lower, translating the near-identical iteration complexity into a clear wall-clock advantage for the practical SVD-free implementation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MuON (Jordan et al., 2024), which orthogonalizes momentum matrices via a few Newton-Schulz iterations instead of an exact SVD. Prior convergence analyses of MuON (Shen et al., 2025; Li & Hong, 2025; Sato et al., 2025; Pethick et al., 2025a,b) all replace the Newton-Schulz step with an idealized exact SVD-based polar factor for analytical tractability, leaving the actual SVD-free implementation theoretically unanalyzed. This work departs from that line by proving nonconvex convergence for MuON with a finite number of Newton-Schulz steps, quantifying the approximation gap via a doubly-exponentially decaying factor χ_q, and additionally establishing a new nuclear-norm convergence rate for SGD with momentum as a vector-based baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result closes a notable theory-practice gap: it justifies why the SVD-free Newton-Schulz implementation of MuON (used in large-scale LLM training) converges at essentially the same iteration rate as the idealized SVD variant while being far cheaper per iteration, explaining the observed wall-clock speedup. The sharper rank dependence (r^{1/4} vs. r^{1/2} for SGD with momentum) provides a geometric explanation for why matrix-aware orthogonalization outperforms vector-based updates. Open questions include whether the analysis extends to adaptive learning rates, non-smooth objectives, or the full LLM-scale regime where the effective rank and condition number of momentum matrices may behave differently than in the small-model experiments presented here.
