## Problem

Deep neural networks remain overconfident on out-of-distribution inputs, and existing post-hoc OoD scores (feature magnitude, softmax uncertainty) lack a unified geometric explanation for why they work and how they should be combined. The multiplicative coupling of these signals is suboptimal because OoD inputs tend to have both lower feature magnitude and higher uncertainty, causing the two factors to cancel in a product. This paper asks whether Riemannian information geometry can both unify these signals and motivate a more robust additive combination.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image and a pre-trained classifier (ResNet-18/50 or ViT-B/16) in a single forward pass, with no retraining or inference-time modification. It additionally requires a calibration set of ID images (5,000 for CIFAR, 50,000 for ImageNet) used offline to construct two projection matrices: R via LDA on penultimate-layer features and Q via PCA on softmax probability vectors. The output is a single scalar OoD score (higher = more likely OoD) computed as the sum of three weighted geometric components, evaluated against AUROC and TNR@TPR95.

## Method

The paper derives a closed-form expression (Theorem 5.1) showing that the Fisher Information Matrix trace restricted to the final linear layer equals the product of the penultimate feature norm squared and the softmax flatness (1 − ‖p‖²), thereby grounding two common OoD signals in Fisher-Rao sensitivity. It then restricts the FIM to discriminative subspaces via a tensor-product basis P = Q ⊗ R (PCA on the probability simplex, LDA on the feature space), yielding a refined multiplicative score. To overcome the multiplicative cancellation problem, the authors decompose the feature into projected magnitude and orthogonal residual energy via the Pythagorean theorem, then model total sensitivity on a three-factor product manifold with (pseudo-)conformal metrics, producing an additive score U + λ_M·M + λ_y·y. The hyperparameters λ_M and λ_y are set analytically by a variance-balancing criterion (principle of indifference) that equalizes each component's contribution to total score variance under an independence assumption. The final score is invariant under orthogonal transformations, classifier translations, and uniform rescalings of the feature-weight pair.

## Results

On the ImageNet-1K benchmark with ViT-B/16, the Additive FIM Trace achieves AUROC/TNR@TPR95 of 92.61/68.25 (ImageNet-O), 98.82/98.41 (iNaturalist), 89.51/60.17 (Places365), and 93.38/67.98 (SUN), placing it among the top post-hoc methods. With ResNet-50, the same method scores 71.77/22.41 (ImageNet-O), 90.61/54.69 (iNaturalist), 82.93/40.33 (Places365), and 85.76/39.95 (SUN). On CIFAR-10 vs. SVHN, it reaches 91.28/68.88 AUROC/TNR@TPR95, and on the harder CIFAR-10 vs. CIFAR-100 setting, 87.23/54.47. The method requires only a single forward pass with no retraining or inference-time modifications.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Additive FIM Trace is most competitive with ViM (Wang et al., 2022) and GradOrth (Behpour et al., 2023), the two strongest post-hoc baselines. Against ViM on ViT-B/16, the Additive FIM Trace slightly outperforms on ImageNet-O AUROC (92.61 vs. 92.26) and SUN (93.38 vs. 93.32), while ViM leads on TNR@TPR95 for ImageNet-O (71.34 vs. 68.25) and Places365 AUROC (90.83 vs. 89.51). Against GradOrth, the gap is minimal: GradOrth edges ahead on ImageNet-O AUROC (93.22 vs. 92.61) and iNaturalist (98.98 vs. 98.82), while the Additive FIM Trace matches or slightly exceeds it on SUN TNR@TPR95 (67.98 vs. 68.68 is close). The method clearly outperforms simpler baselines: MSP (85.08 AUROC), GradNorm (82.23), ODIN (90.23), and IGEOOD (91.66) on ImageNet-O with ViT-B/16, with margins of 2–10 AUROC points. On ResNet-50, the Additive FIM Trace (71.77 AUROC on ImageNet-O) outperforms all baselines except GradOrth (71.23) and Deep k-NN (72.34), and substantially exceeds Mahalanobis (69.11), ODIN (64.34), and IGEOOD (67.43). The ablation confirms that the additive formulation is critical: the Standard FIM Trace (89.03) and Tensor FIM Trace (90.12) on ImageNet-O are 2.6 and 2.5 AUROC points below the final additive score, validating the product-manifold motivation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of information geometry and OoD detection, departing from IGEOOD (which uses Fisher-Rao geodesic distance) by instead measuring local sensitivity via the FIM trace. It provides a theoretical derivation explaining why gradient-based methods like GradNorm and heuristic scores combining feature magnitude with output uncertainty work: Theorem 5.1 shows the FIM trace for the final layer exactly factorizes into these two signals. The product manifold construction then gives a principled geometric justification for the additive (rather than multiplicative) combination of signals seen in SOTA methods like ViM, moving beyond the empirical observations of Igoe et al. (2022) who noted the combination but lacked a geometric derivation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a unifying theoretical lens that connects information geometry to practical OoD detection signals, offering practitioners a principled framework for understanding why certain heuristic combinations work. The method is attractive in its simplicity (single forward pass, no retraining, no OoD data), but its empirical gains over strong baselines like ViM and GradOrth are modest rather than transformative. Key limitations include restricting the geometric analysis to the final layer only, and the authors acknowledge the specific additive formulation is not unique. Extending the sensitivity analysis to intermediate feature representations and finding a single unified metric that intrinsically captures all geometric aspects are natural open directions.
