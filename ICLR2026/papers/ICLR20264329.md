## Problem

RED-based imaging reconstruction methods are inherently iterative and often require many iterations, yet convergence acceleration has been addressed only through heuristics without theoretical guarantees. The core difficulty is that learned score-based priors render the optimization landscape nonconvex, precluding classical acceleration techniques. This paper closes the gap by providing a principled acceleration mechanism with provable convergence rates under nonconvex priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264329/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes noisy or undersampled measurements y ∈ R^m, a forward operator A: R^d → R^m (linear or nonlinear), and a pre-trained score function S derived from a denoiser (e.g., DRUNet via the gradient-step denoiser technique). It outputs a single reconstructed image x̂ ∈ R^d, selected as an average over the final iterations to ensure a stationary-point guarantee. The score function must satisfy Lipschitz-continuous gradient and Hessian conditions, which are enforced in practice through Lipschitz activation functions in the denoiser architecture.

## Method

RISP extends RED by inserting a restarted inertia mechanism: at each iteration, an extrapolated point z^k = x^k + (1−θ)(x^k − x^{k−1}) is formed to carry momentum, and the gradient or proximal update is evaluated at z^k rather than x^k. A restarting rule resets the inertia (setting x^{−1} = x^0 = x^k, k = 0) whenever the accumulated squared displacement since the last restart exceeds a threshold B², preventing overshooting in nonconvex regions. Two instantiations are provided—RISP-GM (full gradient step) and RISP-Prox (proximal step on the data-fidelity term)—both using the score function S = −∇g directly as the negative gradient of the implicit regularizer. Under Lipschitz-gradient and Lipschitz-Hessian assumptions (no convexity of the prior required), both algorithms achieve an O(n^{−4/7}) stationary-point convergence rate, improving over the O(n^{−1/2}) rate of vanilla RED. A continuous-time analysis further shows that RISP-GM and RISP-Prox correspond to Euler and forward-backward discretizations, respectively, of a restarted heavy-ball ODE, yielding a matching O(T^{−4/7}) rate in continuous time.

## Results

On four linear inverse problems (deblurring, inpainting, SISR, MRI), RISP achieves a faster decay of gradient norm than RED — e.g., in deblurring, RISP reduces the gradient norm by five orders of magnitude within 200 iterations versus three orders for RED — while reaching comparable PSNR in roughly 5× fewer iterations (MRI). On the nonlinear Rician denoising task (CBSD10, noise 25.5/255), RISP-Prox reaches 31.55 dB in 160 ms, whereas RED-GM requires approximately 10× longer runtime for comparable quality. On the large-scale 1024×1024 inverse scattering problem (CytoPacq), RISP-GM and RISP-Prox converge at least 24× faster than RED-GM and RED-Prox, recovering fine cell-wall structures in 20 minutes while RED still produces blurred results after 480 minutes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline family is RED (both RED-GM and RED-Prox variants), which RISP extends with restarted inertia. Across all four linear inverse problems, RISP matches or slightly exceeds RED's final PSNR while converging substantially faster — the gradient norm drops an additional two orders of magnitude in deblurring, and PSNR plateaus ~5× sooner in MRI. On the nonlinear Rician denoising task, where Assumption 4 (convexity of f) is violated, RISP-Prox still converges and reaches 31.55 dB in 160 ms, while RED-GM needs roughly 10× the runtime; visually, RED reconstructions at 160 ms remain visibly noisy whereas RISP-Prox yields near noise-free results. On the large-scale inverse scattering benchmark, the speedup is most pronounced (≥24×), attributed to the high per-iteration cost of the 1024×1024 problem amplifying the benefit of fewer iterations. In no experiment does RISP sacrifice final reconstruction quality relative to RED; the gains are purely in convergence speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

RISP extends the RED framework (Romano et al., 2017) by integrating a restarted inertia mechanism originally developed for nonconvex optimization (Li & Lin, 2023), adapting their O(ε^{-7/4}) complexity result to the composite score-based setting. Prior work on inertia in RED/PnP (Kamilov et al., 2018; He et al., 2019; Wu et al., 2024; Chow et al., 2024) demonstrated empirical speedups but lacked convergence guarantees; RISP fills this gap by proving O(n^{-4/7}) stationary-point convergence without requiring convexity of the score-based prior. The paper further bridges the discrete algorithms to the continuous heavy-ball ODE (Polyak, 1964), showing RISP-GM and RISP-Prox correspond to Euler and forward-backward discretizations of a restarted heavy-ball system, connecting the RED literature to the broader inertial dynamics literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provable acceleration guarantee for RED-type methods with nonconvex score-based priors, moving convergence from O(n^{-1/2}) to O(n^{-4/7}) and translating to up to 24× runtime reduction on large-scale imaging. The practical impact is significant for real-time or large-scale reconstruction (e.g., 1024×1024 inverse scattering), where RED's slow convergence is a bottleneck. Key limitations include the Lipschitz-Hessian assumption (excluding Poisson denoising and some nonlinear forward models) and the need for manual tuning of the inertia parameter θ and restart threshold B. Open questions include whether the Lipschitz-Hessian requirement can be relaxed, whether the framework extends directly to PnP, and whether the continuous-time perspective can inspire parameter-free restart strategies.
