## Problem

Existing retrieval-augmented language models inject external evidence during the reasoning phase but lack a mechanism to keep key information proximal to the final output tokens, causing hallucination to escalate in long-form generation. The paper identifies this as the 'Lost in Lengthy Contexts' bottleneck: redundant retrieved passages obscure critical evidence, and long reasoning chains cause the model to forget earlier intermediate results before producing the answer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265335/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-hop question and retrieves top-5 passages from a Wikipedia knowledge base via an E5-base-v2 dense retriever (FlashRAG). During reasoning, the model issues multiple macro retrieval tool calls and stores answer-aligned facts as JSON key-value pairs in a structured repository (typically 50–150 tokens). The final output is a grounded answer where every retrieved value is enclosed in \boxed{} and must be directly supported by a preceding micro retrieval call from the repository, rather than produced by independent reasoning.

## Method

M²R introduces a two-level retrieve-while-generate framework: during the think phase, macro retrieval gathers coarse-grained external evidence and the model saves answer-aligned key facts into a key-value repository; during the answer phase, micro retrieval queries this repository on-demand, inserting retrieved facts immediately before the corresponding output tokens to enforce evidence-to-output proximity. Training uses GRPO-based reinforcement learning with a two-stage curriculum (first macro retrieval + key saving, then micro retrieval + answer grounding) and customized rule-based rewards combining format compliance, final answer F1, key-information correctness, and consistency between stored facts and the final output. Retrieval-result tokens injected by the environment are masked from the policy loss to prevent spurious credit assignment. The paper further provides a RoPE spectral-decomposition argument showing that attention contributions decay monotonically with token distance, giving a theoretical basis for why proximity enforcement reduces hallucination.

## Results

On the Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct backbone, M²R achieves 44.11% EM and 65.98% LJ on HotpotQA, 48.89% EM and 57.01% LJ on 2WikiMultiHopQA, 24.12% EM and 35.44% LJ on MuSiQue, and 44.56% EM and 56.89% LJ on Bamboogle, outperforming the strongest baseline ReSearch on all eight metrics. On the 3B backbone, M²R similarly leads on most metrics (e.g., MuSiQue EM 20.87 vs. 19.40 for ReSearch), with only marginal losses on HotpotQA EM (38.70 vs. 38.78) and Bamboogle LJ (47.20 vs. 48.12). Gains are most pronounced on MuSiQue, the hardest multi-hop benchmark, where M²R improves over ReSearch by +1.82 EM (7B) and +1.47 EM (3B). Under multi-question long-context settings (HotpotQA-2Q/3Q), M²R maintains stable accuracy while baselines like Naive RAG and ReSearch show rapidly rising hallucination rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M²R is compared against five baselines: Iter-RetGen, IRCoT, COFT, SURE, and ReSearch. Against the two iterative retrieval baselines (Iter-RetGen and IRCoT), M²R achieves large margins across all benchmarks—for example, on 7B MuSiQue, M²R reaches 24.12% EM versus 8.69% (Iter-RetGen) and 6.99% (IRCoT). Against COFT and SURE, which employ coarse-to-fine highlighting and summary-based answer selection respectively, M²R still leads by 7.00 EM on MuSiQue (24.12 vs. 17.12) and 3.25 EM (24.12 vs. 20.87) on the 7B model. The closest comparison is with ReSearch, the strongest RL-based multi-turn retrieval baseline: on the 7B backbone, M²R wins on all eight metrics with gains of +0.59 to +2.36 points (e.g., HotpotQA LJ 65.98 vs. 63.62; MuSiQue EM 24.12 vs. 22.30), while on the 3B backbone M²R is essentially tied on HotpotQA EM (38.70 vs. 38.78) and slightly behind on Bamboogle LJ (47.20 vs. 48.12) but ahead on all other six metrics. Overall, M²R's advantage grows with context length, as demonstrated by the multi-question stress tests where ReSearch's accuracy degrades more sharply than M²R's.

## Relation to Prior Work

M2R extends the retrieve-while-generate paradigm established by multi-turn RAG frameworks (ReAct, Self-RAG, ReSearch) by introducing a second, internal retrieval channel: a key-information repository populated during the reasoning phase and queried during answer generation. Unlike prior methods that inject external evidence only during the think phase, M2R explicitly re-anchors model-generated facts immediately before output tokens, directly targeting the 'lost in the middle' proximity problem identified by Liu et al. (2023). The training recipe builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) with a curriculum-learning schedule, and the RoPE spectral-decomposition analysis in Appendix B provides a theoretical grounding for why proximity matters, connecting the empirical observation to positional-encoding mechanics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The retrieve-while-generate mechanism offers a lightweight, plug-in solution to long-form hallucination that adds less than 10% inference latency and negligible storage (50–150 tokens for the repository), making it practically deployable. However, the method is validated only on multi-hop QA with a single dense retriever (E5-base-v2 over Wikipedia) and rule-based rewards, leaving open whether the proximity principle generalizes to open-ended generation, non-Wikipedia corpora, or learned reward models. The paper itself flags future directions including dynamic proximity optimization and multimodal extension, suggesting the framework is a starting point rather than a final solution.
