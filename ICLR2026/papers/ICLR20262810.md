## Problem

Prior work reports contradictory effects of context length on language model performance: long irrelevant context can hurt accuracy, relevant long context yields scaling-law gains, and in some domains even relevant long context degrades results. No unified theoretical framework explains when and why longer context helps or harms, leaving practitioners without principled guidance on optimal context length for training or inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262810/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes text sequences truncated to varying context lengths (from a few hundred to 16k tokens), extracts final-layer hidden states of the last token from well-trained LLMs (≥10,000 samples per context length), and uses training dataset size as a control variable. It produces a decomposed loss (Bayes Risk + Approximation Loss), a scalar Intrinsic Entropy value per context length (measured via Gaussian-KDE or sum-of-log-eigenvalues in the hidden representation space), and a predicted optimal context length. A key constraint is that the LLM must approximate the Bayes Model well for the linear entropy-loss relationship to hold; outliers where the model fails to approximate (e.g., RecurrentGemma-9B at very short contexts) must be excluded.

## Method

The paper decomposes total Cross-Entropy loss into Bayes Risk H(P, P_l) (the irreducible loss of the optimal model at context length l, which decreases with l) and Approximation Loss D_KL(P_l, Q_l) (the gap between the trained model and the Bayes model, which increases with l due to growing intrinsic dimension). It introduces Intrinsic Entropy S(P_l), measured as the information entropy of the hidden-state distribution in the LLM's representation space, and derives under three assumptions (finite limiting entropy, monotonic increase with context, and a linear mapping to next-token-prediction entropy) that Bayes Risk is linear in Intrinsic Entropy: R_Bayes ≈ −k·S(P_l) + Const. The balance between the decreasing Bayes Risk and increasing Approximation Loss yields a critical point l* (optimal context length) that grows with training dataset size and with task difficulty. Validation is performed on three LLM architectures (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-8B-Base, RecurrentGemma-9B) over OpenWebText, on a GPT-2 124M model trained at multiple dataset sizes, on the RULER benchmark, and on a synthetic 'position-weighted multitask sparse parity' dataset where the Bayes Model is analytically computable.

## Results

The paper validates a linear relationship between Cross Entropy loss and Gaussian-KDE-measured Intrinsic Entropy across three LLM architectures on OpenWebText, achieving correlation coefficients of R = −0.9888 (Llama-3.1-8B), R = −0.9960 (Qwen3-8B-Base), and R = −0.9967 (RecurrentGemma-9B). Bayes Risk is well-approximated by H(P,P_l) ≈ C₀ + C/l^γ across multiple text corpora. On the RULER benchmark, most Qwen3-series models exhibit a clear optimal context length for qa_1, fwe, and cwe subtasks, with larger models (e.g., Qwen3-8B vs. Qwen3-4B) showing a larger optimal context length. On the synthetic Position-Weighted Multitask Sparse Parity dataset, the trained model's CE loss matches the theoretical minimum within ~0.001 nats (e.g., 0.4648 vs. 0.4643 at context length 17), confirming the model approximates the Bayes Model well.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is primarily a theoretical analysis with empirical validation rather than a method-comparison study, so it does not benchmark a proposed method against competing baselines. Instead, it validates its Intrinsic Entropy framework across three architecturally distinct LLMs (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen3-8B-Base, RecurrentGemma-9B), confirming the linear CE-loss–entropy relationship holds universally when the model approximates the Bayes Model well; RecurrentGemma-9B shows outlier points at very short context lengths where it fails to approximate the Bayes Model, and excluding those points restores the linear fit. On the RULER benchmark, the paper compares Qwen3-4B, 8B, 14B, and 32B, finding that larger models exhibit a larger optimal context length (consistent with lower Approximation Loss), and that the vt (variable tracking) subtask shows monotonic improvement with context length because its Approximation Loss is already low for current LLMs. The synthetic data experiments serve as a controlled validation: the trained Transformer's CE loss tracks the analytically computed Bayes Risk to within 0.001 nats, and the eigenvalue-based Intrinsic Entropy measurement shows a strong linear correlation with CE loss, confirming both Point 1 (linear CE–entropy relationship) and Point 2 (validity of entropy measurement in intrinsic space).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the intrinsic-space scaling law framework of Bahri et al. (2024) and Sharma & Kaplan (2022) from the dataset-size and model-size dimensions to the context-length dimension, introducing 'Intrinsic Entropy' as a new bridge between context length and Bayes Risk. While prior scaling law theories (Kaplan et al., Hoffmann et al.) and empirical long-context studies (Xiong et al. 2024, Xu et al. 2024, Levy et al. 2024) have documented mixed effects of context length, none provided a unified theoretical decomposition explaining when longer context helps versus hurts. The synthetic validation draws on the multitask sparse parity construction of Michaud et al. (2024) and Barak et al. (2022), adapted with position-weighted task frequencies to model realistic information distribution across context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled explanation for the empirically observed 'longer context can hurt' phenomenon and predicts that optimal context length scales with training dataset size—a practical guideline for long-context LM training that is not obvious from prior scaling laws. However, the theory rests on the Linear Entropy Relationship and Uniform Information Gain assumptions, which are validated empirically but not derived from first principles; the authors acknowledge that more fundamental explanations for the measured Intrinsic Entropy behavior remain open. The intrinsic-space perspective is also model-dependent (tied to how a specific network represents data), limiting the framework's model-agnostic applicability. Future work could test whether the optimal-context-length prediction holds at much larger scales and across diverse task distributions beyond the RULER-style benchmarks used here.
