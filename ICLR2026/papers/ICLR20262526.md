## Problem

Existing large-scale knowledge editing methods (MEMIT, MALMEN, PMET) modify internal LLM weights and suffer from two fundamental limitations: a hard upper bound on editable knowledge volume due to limited weight capacity, and progressive interference accumulation that degrades Locality as edit scale grows beyond tens of thousands. This paper asks whether large-scale editing can be achieved without modifying the original LLM weights at all, thereby decoupling editing capacity from model size and eliminating cumulative interference.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language knowledge query (e.g., a relation-extraction or counterfactual question) and a frozen pre-trained LLM (Phi-2, GPT-J, or Llama-3). At inference, the query is encoded by a fine-tuned BERT into a CLS embedding, which conditions a DiT diffusion model to generate a single neuron's weight vector (2560²-dim for Phi-2, 4096²-dim for GPT-J/Llama-3). The output is the LLM's response with that one dynamically-weighted neuron attached to a specific FFN layer; irrelevant queries are routed to zero weights by an entropy-based Familiarity Network, so the LLM responds as if unedited.

## Method

MeG attaches a single dynamic-weight neuron to a selected FFN layer of the LLM and uses a DiT diffusion model (v-prediction formulation) to conditionally generate that neuron's weights from the query's CLS embedding, replacing static weight modification with per-query weight generation. Training involves four stages: (1) fine-tuning BERT with InfoNCE contrastive loss on pseudo-equivalent expression pairs to align paraphrase representations; (2) training a 10-class Familiarity Network on the edited knowledge to produce low-entropy fingerprints for relevant queries and high-entropy outputs for irrelevant ones; (3) collecting knowledge-weight pairs by individually fine-tuning the single neuron for each of N knowledge items; (4) training the DiT on these pairs to learn text-to-weight generation. At inference, the Familiarity Network's entropy threshold routes queries: relevant ones trigger 50-step DDIM weight generation, while irrelevant ones receive zero weights, ensuring scale-invariant interference since only one neuron is ever added regardless of edit count.

## Results

On ZsRE with 10,000 edits, MeG achieves a Score (harmonic mean of Reliability, Generality, and Locality under both TF and AG settings) of 82.80 on Phi-2, 83.20 on GPT-J, and 83.90 on Llama-3, substantially outperforming the best baseline MALMEN (45.64, 48.92, 70.03 respectively). The most striking advantage is in Locality: MeG reaches 91.14% (AG) on Phi-2 versus 18.22% for MALMEN and 7.11% for MEMIT, a gap of over 70 percentage points. On COUNTERFACT at 10k edits, MeG scores 69.09 (Phi-2), 71.19 (GPT-J), and 64.45 (Llama-3), again dominating all baselines by 20–60+ points in Score. Reliability remains near-perfect (95–99%) across all models and datasets even at 10k scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeG decisively outperforms all five baselines (FT, MEMIT, PMET, MALMEN, SCEN) on the Score metric across every model–dataset combination at 10k edits, with gaps of 14–67 points. The most dramatic advantage is in Locality: on GPT-J/ZsRE, MeG achieves 83.99% (AG) versus 20.30% for MEMIT (+63.69 pp) and 16.56% for MALMEN (+67.43 pp); on Phi-2/ZsRE the gap over MALMEN is +72.92 pp (91.14% vs 18.22%). On Reliability, MeG matches or slightly trails MALMEN on GPT-J (99.11% vs 96.78% AG — actually MeG wins) and Llama-3 (98.90% vs 87.23%), while on Phi-2 it leads all baselines by 23+ points. On Generality, MeG leads on Phi-2 (59.69% AG vs 41.54% MALMEN) but is marginally below MEMIT on GPT-J (61.69% vs 62.50% AG), a small trade-off for the massive Locality gain. SCEN, evaluated only up to 2048 edits due to prohibitive memory costs, shows high Locality (96.77% on Phi-2/ZsRE-1024) but poor Generality (54.49% AG), and its overhead scales poorly, making it infeasible at 10k. In Appendix A.7, MeG also outperforms the sequential-editing method AlphaEdit at all scales (e.g., Score 82.80 vs 32.49 at 10k on Phi-2/ZsRE), with the Locality gap widening as edit scale grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeG sits at the intersection of two research threads: large-scale knowledge editing (MEMIT, PMET, MALMEN, SCEN) and diffusion-based neural network weight generation (p-diff, HyperDiffusion). It departs from the dominant 'modify internal weights' paradigm by instead attaching a single dynamic neuron whose weights are conditionally generated by a DiT diffusion model, drawing on T-Patcher's single-neuron addition idea but replacing static per-knowledge weights with a learned generative process. The InfoNCE-tuned BERT encoder and entropy-based Familiarity Network are auxiliary components borrowed from contrastive representation learning and overfitting-based anomaly detection, respectively, to address Generality and Locality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The scale-invariant interference property of the single-neuron design offers a principled solution to the Locality degradation that plagues all weight-modification methods as edit counts grow, with the dramatic Locality gains (e.g., 91% vs. 18% for MALMEN at 10k edits on Phi-2) being the most practically impactful result. However, Generality remains the weakest metric (48–61% range), suggesting that contrastive alignment of paraphrases is insufficient for full generalization, and the diffusion model, while mitigated by DDIM to ~5% overhead, still adds architectural complexity to inference. Open questions include whether the approach extends to truly massive (100k+) or sequential editing settings, and whether the single-neuron capacity bound holds for more complex or compositional knowledge updates.
