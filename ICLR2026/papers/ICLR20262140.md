## Problem

Existing speculative decoding methods suffer from serialized execution where the draft and target models operate in strict alternation, creating pipeline bubbles that underutilize hardware. While PEARL introduced parallel drafting-verification, it inadequately handles token rollback: a single rejected token invalidates all subsequent draft tokens, negating parallelism gains—especially for poorly aligned draft/target model pairs where rollback rates reach 66–90%. The paper addresses the fundamental trade-off between increasing draft length for parallelism and the compounding cost of rollback tokens.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262140/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prefix, a small draft model (e.g., 68M parameters), a large target model (7B–70B), and hidden states from the last K layers (K=4) of the target model concatenated with the current token embedding. It produces generated text tokens with a provably identical sampling distribution to the target model, along with per-step adaptive draft length decisions (3-class: all-reject, confidence-gated, all-accept) and branch point selections. The H-RAD predictor is a lightweight 3-layer MLP trained offline in under 5 minutes on a single A100, requiring no modification to either the draft or target model weights.

## Method

SpecBranch introduces two complementary components. First, H-RAD (Hybrid Rollback-Aware Draft Structure) reduces the draft-length prediction problem from a γ-class regression to a 3-class classification by exploiting the bimodal separability of target model features: a lightweight MLP outputs a hard signal (all-reject or all-accept) for the majority of tokens, while a soft signal (draft model confidence threshold) handles the intermediate cases, combining explicit feature-based prediction with implicit confidence-based early stopping. Second, Branch Resampling spawns k parallel speculative branches at identified unconfident tokens via top-k resampling from the draft model's confidence distribution, where k scales inversely with token confidence; these branches share the prefix KV-Cache and are drafted concurrently while the target model verifies the preceding prefix, eliminating the mutual waiting bubbles of serialized SD. A Branch Speculative Sampling algorithm (adapted from EAGLE/SpecInfer) verifies the branch point against the target model's distribution to guarantee lossless output. Theorem 1 formalizes the latency under rollback, showing the optimal draft length γ depends on the acceptance rate α: for misaligned models (α≤0.5), the optimal γ falls below the speed ratio c, while for well-aligned models it approaches c.

## Results

SpecBranch achieves 1.8×–4.5× wall-time speedup over autoregressive decoding across four model pairs (LLaMA 68M/7B, Vicuna 68M/13B, DeepSeek 1.3B/33B, LLaMA-3.1 8B/70B) and multiple benchmarks. On the primary comparison (Table 2), average speedups range from 2.01× (LLaMA 68M/7B) to 3.69× (LLaMA-3.1 8B/70B), consistently outperforming the strongest baseline PEARL by 10–15%. For poorly aligned model pairs, SpecBranch reduces the rollback rate from 66–90% (PEARL) to under 40%, while for well-aligned pairs it improves mean accepted length M by up to 4.14× over vanilla SD on code generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpecBranch outperforms all four training-free baselines (SpS, AdaEDL, Lookahead, PEARL) across every model pair and benchmark. Against the strongest baseline PEARL, SpecBranch gains 15% average speedup on poorly aligned pairs (LLaMA 68M/7B: 2.01× vs 1.74×; Vicuna 68M/13B: 2.10× vs 1.77×) and ~10% on well-aligned pairs (DeepSeek: 3.23× vs 2.93×; LLaMA-3.1: 3.69× vs 3.38×), primarily by reducing rollback tokens by 50% for misaligned models. Against vanilla SpS, the gap is larger (e.g., 3.69× vs 2.28× on LLaMA-3.1), reflecting the combined benefit of parallelism and adaptive drafting. In supplementary comparisons (Table 10), SpecBranch (3.59×) slightly exceeds EAGLE (3.51×) but trails EAGLE-2 (3.78×) on LLaMA-2 7B/70B, though EAGLE-2 requires 1–2 days of draft-model training versus SpecBranch's ≤5-minute MLP predictor. Against training-free tree methods REST, Ouroboros, and SWIFT (Table 11), SpecBranch achieves the highest speedup (3.39× avg) with the longest mean accepted length (18.27 on HumanEval).

## Relation to Prior Work

SpecBranch extends PEARL's parallel drafting-verification pipeline by addressing its critical blind spot: the global invalidation of all subsequent tokens upon a single rejection (rollback). It builds on the foundational speculative decoding verification mechanism from Leviathan et al. (2023) and the feature-reuse ideas from EAGLE (Li et al., 2024a), but departs from prior dynamic drafting work by unifying implicit (confidence-based) and explicit (target-model-feature-based) length prediction into a single hybrid 3-class classifier (H-RAD), and by introducing branch resampling inspired by processor branch prediction to hedge against likely rejections. Unlike tree-based methods (SpecInfer, EAGLE-2) that spawn branches at every token, SpecBranch limits branching to H-RAD-identified uncertainty points, avoiding expensive KV-Cache growth and complex tree-attention verification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical characterization (Theorem 1) of the parallelism-rollback trade-off in parallel speculative decoding, showing that the optimal draft length γ falls strictly below the speed ratio c for misaligned model pairs — a nuance PEARL's static γ=c setting overlooks. Practically, the 50% rollback reduction for poorly aligned models (68M draft / 13B target) and the 43% energy reduction versus PEARL make this relevant for resource-constrained deployments where draft-target capacity gaps are common. Open questions include whether online distillation can close the alignment gap that H-RAD merely mitigates, and whether the parallel branch-parallel framework composes cleanly with feature-level methods like EAGLE in production serving stacks (vLLM, SGLang).
