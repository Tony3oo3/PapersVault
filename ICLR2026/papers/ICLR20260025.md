## Problem

Existing OOD detection methods for language models aggregate token embeddings via mean pooling before computing a Mahalanobis distance, which discards the token-level structure that is often the only signal distinguishing in-distribution from out-of-distribution sequences. This failure mode is particularly acute for autoregressive generative models (e.g., summarizers, translators) that lack an explicit classification head, leaving OOD detection for such models underexplored relative to the classifier-based vision literature.

## Input / Output

AP-OOD consumes a sequence of S token embeddings (each D-dimensional) extracted from a frozen transformer encoder or decoder, and produces a single scalar OOD score per input sequence. The attention pooling mechanism transforms the S-token sequence into a T-token sequence (where T is the number of learnable queries per head), so the method operates on variable-length sequences without requiring a fixed-length bottleneck. In the supervised variant, the method additionally accepts AUX outlier sequences during training, with a hyperparameter λ controlling the interpolation between unsupervised and fully supervised behavior.

## Method

AP-OOD replaces mean pooling in the Mahalanobis distance with learnable attention pooling: for each of M heads with weight matrix W_j ∈ R^{D×T}, it computes an attention-pooled representation Z·softmax(βZ^T W_j) using a matrix-valued global softmax that normalizes over both the sequence and query dimensions. The squared distance between the per-sequence attention-pooled representation and the corpus-wide attention-pooled prototype is summed across heads, and the loss minimizes this distance for ID sequences while regularizing the weight norms via a log-determinant term. The method provably reduces to the standard Mahalanobis distance with mean pooling when β=0 and M=D, establishing it as a strict generalization. For the supervised setting, a binary cross-entropy term is added that encourages large distances for AUX samples, smoothly interpolating between unsupervised and supervised training as the AUX set grows.

## Results

On XSUM summarization (PEGASUS_LARGE), AP-OOD achieves a mean FPR95 of 4.67% in the input OOD setting, reducing it from 27.84% (Deep SVDD, the best baseline) and 39.69% (Mahalanobis), while also attaining the highest mean AUROC of 98.91%. In the output OOD setting, AP-OOD reaches 16.26% mean FPR95 versus 32.33% for Deep SVDD. On WMT15 En-Fr translation, AP-OOD achieves the best mean FPR95 of 75.25% (input) and 70.37% (output), outperforming all embedding-based baselines. On the MIMII-DG audio task, AP-OOD reduces FPR95 from 36.43% (MSP) to 22.35%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AP-OOD decisively outperforms all embedding-based baselines (Mahalanobis, KNN, Deep SVDD) on summarization: in the input OOD setting, it improves mean FPR95 from 27.84% (Deep SVDD) to 4.67% and mean AUROC from 91.60% to 98.91%. The prediction-based baselines (Perplexity, Entropy) are substantially weaker on summarization (FPR95 of 63.21% and 56.30% respectively), which the authors attribute to high aleatoric uncertainty in abstractive summarization. On translation, AP-OOD still leads all embedding-based methods (mean FPR95 75.25% input, 70.37% output), but the gap narrows and Perplexity becomes competitive on output embeddings (FPR95 77.08%), owing to the lower aleatoric uncertainty in translation. On the audio task, AP-OOD (FPR95 22.35%) substantially outperforms both classifier-based methods MSP (36.43%) and EBO (61.86%). In the semi-supervised setting (Figure 3), AP-OOD attains the highest AUROC regardless of the number of AUX samples, while Deep SAD and relative Mahalanobis degrade with fewer AUX examples.

## Relation to Prior Work

AP-OOD directly extends the Mahalanobis-distance-based OOD detection framework for conditional language models introduced by Ren et al. (2023), replacing their mean pooling of token embeddings with a learnable attention pooling mechanism. The method is grounded in a directional decomposition of the Mahalanobis distance (itself from Lee et al., 2018), showing that attention pooling with β=0 and M=D recovers the original Mahalanobis form, while learned weights and multiple heads generalize it. For the supervised setting, AP-OOD follows the outlier-exposure formulation of Ruff et al. (2019) and Liznerski et al. (2022), interpolating between unsupervised and fully supervised training via a binary cross-entropy loss on ID and AUX samples. The attention mechanism is also connected to Modern Hopfield Networks (Ramsauer et al., 2021), and the paper introduces a matrix-valued global softmax for multi-query attention pooling that, to the authors' knowledge, is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical gap: OOD detection for autoregressive language models (which lack explicit classification heads) is critical for preventing hallucinations in deployed systems, yet prior embedding-based methods discard token-level structure through mean pooling. The semi-supervised design is practically valuable because it gracefully scales with available AUX data, which is often scarce in real deployments (e.g., rare industrial faults, low-resource translation). Key open questions remain: how well the learned decision boundary generalizes when the AUX distribution diverges from true OOD inputs, and whether the approach transfers to large language models with their complex multi-phase training pipelines. The paper also highlights that prediction-based methods (perplexity, entropy) remain competitive in low-aleatoric-uncertainty tasks like translation, suggesting that no single OOD signal dominates across all language modeling settings.
