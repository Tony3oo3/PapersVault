## Problem

Current Vision-Language-Action models fail to achieve robust zero-shot generalization in robotic manipulation due to data scarcity and embodiment heterogeneity in training sets. Existing affordance-based methods predict raw coordinates without an explicit reasoning process, limiting their ability to anchor instructions to correct semantic entities and generalize to novel scenes. The paper argues that the key to generalization lies in first performing explicit spatial-semantic reasoning to generate embodiment-agnostic intermediate visual representations before any action decision.

## Input / Output

FSD takes a 336×336 RGB image and a natural-language task instruction as input, optionally augmented by depth from a RealSense L515 camera during execution. It outputs a structured SrCoT reasoning chain followed by one of three visual aids: a spatial affordance box [x1,y1,x2,y2], a set of 8 affordance points, or an ordered 8-point visual trace, all in integer coordinates normalized to 0–999. Special tokens (<ref>, <box>, <point>, <pred>) bind each coordinate to its semantic object, and for execution the 2D trace is back-projected to 3D via the pinhole camera model and depth, then matched to a GraspNet pose and interpolated in SE(3).

## Method

FSD is built on a frozen CLIP-ViT-L encoder + Vicuna-13B LLM with a trainable linear projector, initialized from ASMv2 for grounding and relation comprehension. The core mechanism, SrCoT, operates in two phases: (1) Description, where the model generates an object-centric spatial relationship graph (nodes = objects with coordinates, edges = relative positions), and (2) Reasoning, where it uses that graph as anchors to determine start/end coordinates and iteratively derive intermediate waypoints with explicit logical links. Training is a two-stage SFT: Stage 1 cultivates spatial reasoning on 250K embodied data (Levels 1–3: grounding, spatial relations, multi-hop QA) mixed with 1.4M general VQA samples; Stage 2 trains visual-aid generation on 50K Level 4–5 data using a self-consistency mechanism that adds the inverse task (predict instruction from image + trace) to align coordinate space with visual-textual semantics. A 300K SFT dataset is auto-constructed from BridgeDataV2, RT-X, and Droid via a 5-level weak-to-strong pipeline employing Grounding DINO, Metric3Dv2, WildCamera, and CoTracker, with rule-based filtering validated against manual annotations.

## Results

FSD-13B achieves a leading average rank of 1.3 across 15 spatial reasoning subtasks from 5 benchmarks (CVBench, CRPE, SAT, BLINK, EmbSpatial-Bench), with CVBench Avg of 80.9% and EmbSpatial-Bench of 63.3%, rivaling or surpassing GPT-4o on most metrics. On the proposed VABench, FSD achieves 61.82% accuracy on spatial affordance prediction (vs. 19.09% for RoboPoint and 9.30% for GPT-4o) and RMSE of 78.26 on visual trace generation. In zero-shot SimplerEnv manipulation, FSD achieves 40.6% average success rate, outperforming RoboPoint (17.7%) by 23 points, and reaches 72% success rate across 8 real-world xArm tasks, exceeding the strongest baseline by over 30%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSD outperforms all open-source 13B baselines (LLaVA-1.5, SAT-Dynamic, RoboPoint, ASMv2) across all 5 spatial reasoning benchmarks, with the largest margins on EmbSpatial-Bench (63.3 vs. 57.4 for ASMv2) and CVBench (80.9 vs. 76.2 for SAT-Dynamic). On object/free-space reference, FSD achieves 56.7% on RoboRefIt, surpassing RoboPoint (49.8%) and GPT-4o (15.3%), while matching RoboPoint closely on Where2Place (45.8 vs. 46.0). On VABench, FSD's 61.82% affordance accuracy is over 3× RoboPoint (19.09%) and ~7× GPT-4o (9.30%), and its visual trace RMSE of 78.26 substantially beats the DINOv2 Predictor (128.32) and RoboBrain (121.6). In SimplerEnv, FSD (40.6%) trails the fine-tuned π0-fast (48.3%) and OpenVLA-OFT (41.8%) but significantly outperforms the zero-shot RoboPoint (17.7%) and MOKA (33.3%). In real-world xArm evaluation, FSD's 72% success rate exceeds GPT-4o, MOKA, and RoboPoint by more than 30 percentage points over the strongest baseline, and FSD also achieves the lowest execution latency (10–14 s vs. 12–28 s for OpenVLA and MOKA).

## Relation to Prior Work

FSD builds directly on the LLaVA-1.5 instruction-tuning architecture (CLIP-ViT-L + Vicuna-13B + linear projector) and initializes from ASMv2, which provides grounding and relation-conversation capabilities. Conceptually, it departs from both end-to-end VLA models (RT-2, π0, OpenVLA) that map vision directly to actions, and from prior affordance methods (RoboPoint, Where2Act) that predict spatial targets without an explicit reasoning process. The key lineage is the chain-of-thought tradition in multimodal models (EmbodiedCoT, VoCoT), but FSD reframes visual aid generation as a structured spatial-reasoning task anchored on object relationship graphs rather than free-form textual reasoning, and adds a self-consistency alignment mechanism that has no direct precedent in prior VLA work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a real gap: VLA models struggle with zero-shot generalization due to data scarcity and embodiment heterogeneity, and FSD's reasoning-driven intermediate representation offers a plausible bridge. The introduction of VABench fills a notable evaluation void for visual trace and affordance generation, and the weak-to-strong data pipeline is a practical recipe for building spatial reasoning capabilities from heterogeneous embodied datasets. However, the approach remains primarily open-loop, relies on a depth camera for 3D back-projection, and the SimplerEnv results (40.6%) do not surpass the strongest end-to-end baselines (π0-fast at 48.3%), suggesting the reasoning advantage is most pronounced in truly novel, zero-shot real-world scenarios rather than in simulation benchmarks with limited task diversity. Open questions include whether the 2D-to-3D mapping generalizes to dynamic scenes and whether the fixed 8-point trace representation is sufficient for long-horizon manipulation.
