## Problem

Prior work evaluates RPT models exclusively on tasks from the same domains used for post-training, leaving it unclear whether RPT gains transfer to unseen domains with different reasoning patterns. This paper systematically investigates the cross-domain and intra-domain generalizability of RLVR across structured (math, code) and unstructured (legal, financial, medical) reasoning tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260789/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The evaluation framework takes RPT model and base model pairs and runs them on 16 benchmarks spanning math, code, and knowledge-intensive reasoning, using consistent generation settings (max response length min{16192, context window}, 16 runs for small benchmarks like AIME/AMC, 1 run otherwise). Outputs are aggregated accuracy improvements Δ and Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel odds ratios θ per domain, with statistical significance tested at p < 0.05, enabling direct comparison of in-domain versus out-of-domain transfer.

## Method

The paper employs a two-stage pipeline. The observational study evaluates 18 open-weight RPT models (selected via systematic HuggingFace filtering from 466 candidates) against their corresponding base models across all 16 benchmarks, categorizing each benchmark as in-domain or out-of-domain relative to the model's disclosed RPT training data. The interventional study trains three models from the same base (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B) using GRPO on 40K single-domain problems (math, code, or knowledge-intensive reasoning) with identical hyperparameters (1 epoch, batch 64, context 8192, lr 1e-6, 8×H100), isolating the effect of RPT from confounding configuration differences. Additional ablations test DAPO, 2-epoch training with intermediate checkpoints, and a Llama-3.2-3B base model. A reasoning template analysis using Jeffreys divergence over GPT-4o-tagged reasoning steps quantifies structural similarity across domains, providing mechanistic grounding for the observed generalization patterns.

## Results

Across 18 open-weight RPT models evaluated on 16 benchmarks, the average in-domain (ID) pass@1 improvement over base models is +2.87%, while the average out-of-domain (OOD) improvement is −3.19%, yielding a mean ID−OOD gap of 6.07 percentage points. The odds ratio θ(ID)/θ(OOD) averages 4.46, confirming that RPT gains are concentrated in the training domain. In the controlled interventional study (single-domain RPT on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B), no model achieves statistically significant OOD gains, while in-domain gains are significant (e.g., Math-RPT: +3.13% ID vs. −1.81% OOD; Code-RPT shows significant OOD drops). Cross-domain transfer is asymmetric: math→code transfer is strong and consistent, but structured→unstructured (math/code→knowledge-intensive) transfer is negligible (avg −0.27% on knowledge tasks), while unstructured→structured transfer is surprisingly positive (+21.40% on math, +12.16% on code).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is each RPT model against its own base model (e.g., DeepScaleR-1.5B vs. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B), revealing that RPT consistently wins in-domain but loses or ties out-of-domain. Among the 18 models, 12 show statistically significant ID gains (θ̂(ID) > 1*), while only 5 show significant OOD gains; notably, AZR-Coder-7B achieves a dramatic +30.12% ID gain but a −23.31% OOD drop (θ̂ ratio of 54.6×). In the interventional study, the Math-RPT model (GRPO, DeepSeek base) gains +3.13% on math but drops −1.81% on OOD, while the Code-RPT model shows significant OOD degradation; the Knowledge-RPT model fails to improve even within its own domain. Configuration variants (DAPO vs. GRPO, Llama-3.2-3B vs. DeepSeek-1.5B base) produce nearly identical ID−OOD gaps (4.94–5.23%), confirming the limitation is inherent to RPT rather than algorithmic. Cross-domain, math-trained models transfer positively to code (+4.77% avg) but not to knowledge-intensive tasks (−0.27%), while finance-trained models (Fino1-8B) show severe OOD drops (−27.89%), indicating unstructured domains lack intra-domain consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by being the first to systematically evaluate RPT/RLVR generalizability beyond training domains, where all prior work (DeepSeek-R1, DeepCoder, Skywork-o1, OREAL, etc.) evaluates models only on in-domain tasks. It repurposes the existing RPT model ecosystem (18 open-weight models) as an observational testbed and introduces a controlled interventional protocol (single-domain GRPO/DAPO training) to isolate RPT's effect from confounding factors. The structured-vs-unstructured reasoning taxonomy, validated via Jeffreys divergence on reasoning traces, provides a novel analytical lens not present in prior RPT evaluations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that RPT gains are largely confined to domains sharing similar reasoning patterns (structured↔structured transfers well; structured→unstructured fails) provides critical practical guidance for practitioners deploying RPT models: you cannot expect a math-trained RPT model to improve legal or medical reasoning. The asymmetric transfer (unstructured→structured works, but not vice versa) suggests knowledge-intensive RPT data may serve as a broader 'reasoning substrate,' opening questions about optimal mixed-domain curricula. Open questions include whether these findings hold at larger model scales (>32B), whether alternative RPT methods (RLHF, DPO, process rewards) exhibit different generalization profiles, and whether curriculum or multi-stage training strategies can bridge the structured-unstructured gap.
