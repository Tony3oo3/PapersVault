## Problem

Audiovisual MLLMs for emotion understanding suffer from two distinct failure modes: spurious cue-emotion associations (grounding predictions on irrelevant cues like attire color or ambient noise) and hallucination of audiovisual cues driven by text priors in the language model backbone. Existing benchmarks lack the task diversity to isolate these errors, and many evaluation pipelines depend on proprietary LLMs, making replication difficult.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260481/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes synchronized audio and video clips (sourced from short ~2–10 s emotion-recognition videos) alongside a text prompt, and produces free-form emotion descriptions, discrete emotion labels, and cue-emotion association reasoning. The EmoReAlM benchmark evaluates via multiple-choice question-answer (MCQA) pairs with four-choice options for reasoning tasks and binary Yes/No for modality-agreement and stress-test tasks, enabling reproducible evaluation without external LLMs. The reference architecture pairs Whisper-large-v3 (audio encoder) and LanguageBind (video encoder) with a Vicuna-7B LLM backbone, with LoRA (rank 8, scale 4) applied during preference optimization.

## Method

AVEm-DPO extends standard DPO with three coupled mechanisms. First, Prompt-based Modality Preference (PMP) constructs input-level preference pairs where only the modality referenced by the prompt is altered in the rejected sample (e.g., swapping the video while keeping audio fixed for a visual query), forcing the policy to ground responses in the queried modality. Second, Emotion-based Response Preference (ERP) creates two rejected responses per chosen response—one containing a video-relevant but emotion-irrelevant cue (spurious association) and one containing an emotion-relevant but absent cue (hallucination)—with weighted penalties (β_vr + β_er = 1). Third, Text Prior Debiasing (TPD) subtracts γ_TPD · log π_text(y|x) from the reward, where π_text is the frozen LLM backbone, discounting responses explainable purely by text priors and giving relative credit to audio/video-supported outputs. The final objective L_AVEm-DPO = L_DPO-TPD^y + λ_av · L_DPO^av-prompt is trained for one epoch (lr 5e-7, β=0.1, γ_TPD=0.2, λ_av=1.0) on 41,687 automatically generated preference samples from MAFW and MER2025, yielding 6–19% relative improvements over baselines in zero-shot emotion reasoning and hallucination stress tests.

## Results

On the proposed EmoReAlM benchmark, AVEm-DPO achieves 77.9%/92.5% accuracy on basic emotion reasoning (audio/visual), 60.0 F1 on modality agreement, and 80.9/94.6 F1 on the stress test (audio/visual) for the 'Our base' reference model, representing 6–19% relative gains over the strongest baseline (Vista-DPO†). On existing benchmarks, the method reaches 58.54 UAR / 64.24 WAR on DFEW, 58.66 UAR / 55.48 WAR on RAVDESS, 92.18 F1 on MER2023, and 7.09/6.75 on EMER spurious-association/hallucination scores. Ablations confirm that removing text-prior debiasing (TPD) causes the largest drop on hallucination stress-test samples (97.6→77.8), while removing prompt-based modality preference (PMP) or emotion-based response preference (ERP) each reduce stress-test F1 by ~8 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AVEm-DPO consistently outperforms both Naive-DPO and the adapted Vista-DPO† across all EmoReAlM subtasks for both reference models. On the stress test (the most diagnostic task), AVEm-DPO achieves 80.9/94.6 F1 (audio/visual) versus 73.6/86.7 for Vista-DPO† and 54.8/65.9 for Naive-DPO, indicating that prompt-based modality preference and text-prior debiasing are critical for eliminating spurious cue-emotion associations and hallucinations. Against external MLLM baselines, AVEm-DPO surpasses Qwen 2.5 Omni (76.8/89.2 basic, 33.3 agreement F1, 55.0/56.8 stress F1) and VITA-1.5 (63.1/84.3 basic, 30.2 agreement F1) by wide margins, particularly on modality agreement and stress-test tasks where these models score near chance. On existing emotion recognition benchmarks (Table 2), AVEm-DPO improves DFEW UAR from 56.78 (base) to 58.54 and RAVDESS UAR from 53.59 to 58.66, while also reducing EMER hallucination scores from 5.19 to 6.75 (higher is better here, indicating fewer hallucinated cues). The user study (Table 4) further confirms the advantage: AVEm-DPO is preferred 54.74% of the time for emotion description versus 11.60% for the next-best model (VITA-1.5), and selected least for inconsistencies (4.67% vs. 68.61% for EmotionLLaMA).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2023) from text-only LLMs to audiovisual MLLMs, building directly on Vista-DPO's (Huang et al., 2025b) formulation of multimodal input preferences and the weighted multi-rejected-response scheme. The base architecture follows EmotionLLaMA (Cheng et al., 2024) with upgraded encoders (Whisper-large-v3, LanguageBind), and the preference data pipeline mirrors the captioning-and-verification approach used in prior emotion MLLM training. The key conceptual departures are (i) prompt-conditioned modality preference that selectively corrupts only the modality relevant to the query, and (ii) a text-prior debiasing regularizer that penalizes responses explainable by the language backbone alone—neither of which appears in prior audiovisual DPO work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EmoReAlM benchmark fills a gap by providing a reproducible, LLM-free evaluation protocol for emotion-specific reasoning failures (spurious cue associations, cue hallucinations, modality disagreement) that prior benchmarks either lack or evaluate only via proprietary GPT-based scoring. The text-prior debiasing mechanism is potentially generalizable to any multimodal LLM where text priors drive cross-modal hallucination. However, the benchmark inherits DFEW's cultural and linguistic biases, is limited to short (~2–10 s) clips, and the method still underperforms on ambiguous emotions like disgust; long-video emotion reasoning and robust spurious audio-cue disambiguation remain open.
