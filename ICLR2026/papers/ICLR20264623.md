## Problem

While multimodal foundation models (MFMs) like GPT-4o have shown impressive language and VQA capabilities, their performance on dense, vision-only tasks (segmentation, detection, depth, surface normals) remains unquantified. Existing benchmarks are text-centric, making direct comparison with specialist vision models impossible, and the proprietary, API-only nature of leading MFMs prevents weight-level adaptation to these tasks.

## Input / Output

The framework takes an RGB image and a text prompt (plus an optional query point for grouping) and produces task-specific dense outputs: per-pixel class labels (segmentation), bounding box coordinates (detection), instance masks (grouping), relative depth maps, and per-axis surface normal maps. Critically, the MFM itself only ever outputs text—yes/no presence answers, class names, or pairwise ordinal comparisons—and the prompt chaining pipeline converts these discrete text responses into dense pixel-wise predictions via superpixel flood-filling or optimization-based globalization.

## Method

The paper introduces a prompt chaining framework that decomposes each dense vision task into a sequence of text-solvable classification sub-tasks, exploiting MFMs' relative strength at image classification. For object detection, a recursive 3×3 grid zooming algorithm progressively eliminates empty cells until the object is localized; for semantic segmentation, SLIC superpixels are classified using multi-scale pyramids (superpixel crop + local context + full image) in batches; for grouping, a BFS over the superpixel adjacency graph queries whether neighboring superpixels belong to the same object; for depth and surface normals, randomly sampled superpixel pairs are compared ordinally and globalized by minimizing a weighted objective (repurposed from Zoran et al., 2015) with smoothness regularization. Calibration baselines (Specialist+Chain, Oracle+Chain, Blind Guess) are included to isolate MFM performance from the algorithmic constraints imposed by superpixel discretization and recursive search, enabling fair ordinal comparison with vision specialists.

## Results

Across six standard vision tasks, GPT-4o is the strongest MFM, leading in 4 of 6 tasks (classification, detection, segmentation, grouping), while o4-mini leads in depth and surface normal prediction. On ImageNet classification, GPT-4o achieves 77.20% top-1 accuracy vs. 90.94% for the specialist Model Soups ViT-G. On COCO object detection, GPT-4o reaches AP=31.87 vs. 80.23 for Co-DETR; on semantic segmentation, mIoU=44.89 vs. 65.52 for OneFormer. The gap to specialists is dramatically larger for geometric tasks: on depth prediction, GPT-4o achieves Spearman ρ=0.54 vs. 0.95 for Omnidata, and on surface normals, GPT-4o's ρ_x is negative (−0.14) while Omnidata reaches 0.80.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MFMs lose to every specialist baseline on every task, but the margin varies substantially by task type. On classification, GPT-4o (77.20%) trails Model Soups ViT-G (90.94%) by ~14 points and OpenCLIP H (84.37%) by ~7 points, yet remains competitive on corruption benchmarks (62.46% on 2DCC vs. 66.96% for OpenCLIP). On object detection, GPT-4o (AP=31.87) falls far short of Co-DETR (AP=80.23) and DETR (AP=58.67), though it slightly exceeds the zero-shot 4M-21 baseline (AP=47.71). For semantic segmentation, GPT-4o (mIoU=44.89) lags OneFormer (65.52) by ~21 points but outperforms 4M-21 (54.31). On grouping, GPT-4o (mIoU=59.06) is ~21 points below SAM (80.12) and SAM-2 (80.87). The gap is most severe on geometric tasks: for depth, GPT-4o (ρ=0.54) is ~0.4 below Omnidata (0.95), Lotus (0.93), and MoGe-2 (0.90); for surface normals, GPT-4o (ρ_x=−0.14, ρ_y=0.57, ρ_z=0.40) is dramatically below DSINE (0.76/0.79/0.73) and Omnidata (0.80/0.83/0.78), with several MFMs showing negative correlations indicating systematic directional confusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant VQA-style and text-output benchmarks for multimodal foundation models (e.g., Cambrian-1, BLINK, MMMU) by translating dense vision tasks—segmentation, detection, depth, surface normals—into text-promptable sub-tasks via a prompt chaining framework, enabling direct apples-to-apples comparison with vision specialists using native task metrics. Methodologically, it builds on prompt decomposition strategies (Khot et al., 2022; Wei et al., 2022), the recursive zooming idea from V* (Wu & Xie, 2024), and ordinal regression for depth (Zoran et al., 2015), but unifies them into a single calibrated benchmarking protocol with control baselines (Specialist+Chain, Oracle+Chain, Blind Guess) that prior prompting works (e.g., DetToolChain) did not provide. The key conceptual departure is treating the prompt chain not as a production method but as a measurement instrument, isolating MFM visual understanding from algorithmic discretization artifacts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic benchmark that quantifies how far MFMs are from state-of-the-art vision specialists across the full spectrum of semantic and geometric tasks, providing a reference point for tracking MFM progress on dense prediction. The finding that reasoning models (o1, o3, o4-mini) close the geometric gap substantially while non-reasoning models remain far behind suggests that chain-of-thought reasoning may be a critical ingredient for 3D visual understanding in MFMs. Open questions include whether the prompt chaining granularity imposes a hard ceiling (partially addressed via finer-grained ablations), whether future any-to-any models will render this text-mediated evaluation obsolete, and how to efficiently benchmark at scale given the high per-sample API cost. The framework also raises the question of whether current MFM training on image-text pairs is fundamentally insufficient for geometric reasoning or whether it is merely a prompting/architecture limitation.
