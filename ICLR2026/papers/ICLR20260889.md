## Problem

Diffusion-based samplers for molecular systems suffer from mode collapse, concentrating on high-probability basins while missing rare but thermodynamically essential states, and they are often slower than molecular dynamics for practical molecular sampling. No prior diffusion-based sampler has demonstrated reactive sampling (bond breaking/formation) or accurate free energy estimation across multiple metastable states. The paper addresses this by integrating enhanced sampling via collective variables directly into the diffusion training loop.

## Input / Output

The method takes a potential energy function E(x) (classical force field or universal ML interatomic potential), a small set of low-dimensional collective variables ξ(x) (e.g., backbone torsion angles or bond distances), a reference configuration defining the accessible component, and restraint potentials enforcing chemical isomerism constraints (e.g., chirality). It produces i.i.d. molecular configurations in full Cartesian coordinates, importance weights w(x)=exp[βV*(ξ(x))] for reweighting to the Boltzmann ensemble, and the potential of mean force along CVs recovered directly from the converged bias potential V*(s)=-(γ/(γ-1))V*(s)+const, from which free energy differences between states are computed.

## Method

WT-ASBS augments the Adjoint Schrödinger Bridge Sampler (ASBS) with a well-tempered bias in CV space, updated online via Gaussian kernel deposition inherited from well-tempered metadynamics. The algorithm operates on a two-time-scale scheme: an inner loop trains the ASBS control and corrector networks via adjoint matching and corrector matching objectives with the current biased energy E+V_k∘ξ, while an outer loop projects i.i.d. model samples to CV space and deposits scaled Gaussian hills to raise the effective temperature along CVs by a factor γ. A key advantage over MD-based enhanced sampling is that diffusion samplers generate uncorrelated samples, eliminating the need to wait for decorrelation before bias deposition, which enables simultaneous exploration in multiple CV directions. The paper proves convergence of the bias to V*(s)=-(1-1/γ)F(s)+const, ensuring the sampled distribution matches the well-tempered target and that reweighting recovers the exact Boltzmann ensemble. A practical recipe combines localized MD pretraining (bridge matching) to warm-start the control model, restraint potentials to enforce chemical isomerism, and post-training reweighting or short MD refinement under the biased energy to restore asymptotic correctness.

## Results

On the SN2 reaction, WT-ASBS converges in 0.77M energy evaluations (4.3 h) versus 4.0M (29 h) for WTMetaD, a ~5× speedup in wall-clock time. On the post-transition-state bifurcation reaction, WT-ASBS requires 2.6M evaluations (23 h) versus 6.4M (48 h) for WTMetaD. For alanine tetrapeptide, WT-ASBS discovers all 8 metastable states within 2M energy evaluations and achieves free-energy MAE within chemical accuracy (1 kcal/mol) by 20M evaluations. On alanine dipeptide, WT-ASBS recovers the correct PMF and ΔF between two conformational states, whereas the unmodified ASBS baseline fails to populate the less-occupied state.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ASBS (the unmodified diffusion-based sampler), WT-ASBS clearly wins on mode coverage: ASBS exhibits mode collapse on alanine dipeptide, failing to reproduce the correct distribution in the less-populated φ-state, while WT-ASBS recovers the full PMF and accurate ΔF after reweighting. Against WTMetaD (the classical MD-based enhanced sampling baseline), WT-ASBS achieves substantially fewer energy evaluations and shorter wall-clock times on both reactive systems (5× and 2× speedups, respectively), owing to its ability to generate i.i.d. samples from the biased distribution and batch energy evaluations during training. On alanine tetrapeptide, WT-ASBS discovers all 8 metastable states faster than WTMetaD in the early phase, but the paper explicitly notes it does not achieve a lower final free-energy MAE than WTMetaD, since MD provides efficient local mixing within each basin once barriers are crossed. On alanine dipeptide, WT-ASBS converges ΔF more accurately than WTMetaD at the same bias factor γ. Overall, WT-ASBS wins on exploration speed and computational efficiency, ties or slightly trails on final free-energy precision for multi-state peptide systems, and is the first diffusion-based method to demonstrate reactive sampling at near-DFT accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: diffusion-based neural samplers (specifically ASBS, which casts Boltzmann sampling as a Schrödinger Bridge optimal control problem) and classical enhanced sampling methods (specifically well-tempered metadynamics, which deposits Gaussian hills along collective variables to flatten free energy barriers). The key conceptual departure is embedding the well-tempered bias deposition loop directly into the diffusion sampler's training, exploiting the fact that diffusion samplers generate i.i.d. samples (eliminating the decorrelation wait required in MD-based metadynamics) to drive bias updates more efficiently. The paper also situates itself within the broader Boltzmann Generator literature (Noe et al., 2019; Zhang & Chen, 2021; Vargas et al., 2023a) and addresses the well-known mode-collapse limitation of these samplers by introducing a targeted, CV-space bias rather than global temperature annealing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first demonstration of reactive sampling (bond breaking/formation) with a diffusion-based sampler at near-DFT accuracy using universal ML interatomic potentials is a concrete step toward making energy-based neural samplers practical for real chemical problems where classical force fields are insufficient. The method provides a clean theoretical guarantee (convergence to the well-tempered target) and a practical recipe (pretraining → biased training → reweighting) that is straightforward to implement. Key open questions include: the requirement for manually or ML-selected collective variables (limiting automation for complex systems), the lack of amortization across thermodynamic conditions or related molecular systems, and the acknowledged difficulty of fair benchmarking against the many existing enhanced sampling strategies. The authors suggest that the most effective future workflows will hybridize global diffusion moves with local MD/MCMC refinement.
