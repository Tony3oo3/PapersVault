## Problem

Traditional LR decay schedules (cosine, linear) require knowing total training duration in advance, making continual training impractical. While WSD decouples decay from total steps, it still demands manual configuration of decay start, duration, and function, and cannot be extended once decay begins. The paper asks whether the decay phase can be eliminated entirely by replacing it with a principled checkpoint merging operation that provably emulates any decay curve.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of model checkpoints saved at fixed intervals (e.g., every 25B tokens) during a constant-LR training phase, along with an optional high-quality annealing dataset that replaces the general pre-training corpus after a switch step. It produces a single merged model checkpoint computed as a weighted average of the most recent n checkpoints, where the weights are derived from a target decay schedule via Theorem 3.1. The merge window size n and checkpoint interval jointly determine the effective decay duration and granularity, with the merged model serving as a drop-in replacement for a decayed checkpoint.

## Method

WSM establishes a formal equivalence between LR decay and checkpoint merging: by expressing each checkpoint as the base state minus accumulated gradient updates, a weighted average of k+1 checkpoints reduces to applying a synthetic decay schedule {w_i} to those gradients, where w_i = Σ_{j=i}^{k} c_j. Theorem 3.1 then inverts this mapping, showing that any monotonically non-increasing decay sequence {w_i} (bounded in [0,1]) uniquely determines non-negative checkpoint weights {c_j} via c_k = w_k, c_j = w_j − w_{j+1}, c_0 = 1 − w_1. In practice, WSM trains with warmup followed by a constant peak LR (no decay), periodically saves checkpoints, and asynchronously merges the last n using weights corresponding to the desired decay shape (e.g., 1-sqrt, linear, or cosine). Empirically, merge duration (the token span covered by merged checkpoints) and the use of high-quality annealing data during that span are the dominant factors, with 1-sqrt and mean merging outperforming EMA, and the merged model consistently exceeding WSD by +3.5% on MATH, +2.9% on HumanEval, and +5.5% on MMLU-Pro.

## Results

On a 16.3B-parameter MoE model trained on 10.2T tokens with 400B tokens of high-quality annealing data, WSM (mean-averaging merge) achieves an overall average of 63.95 vs. WSD's 62.67, a +2.04% improvement. The largest category gains are in Professional Knowledge (+4.83%, driven by +5.5% on MMLU-Pro and +3.5% on GPQA) and Math (+2.30%, with +3.5% on MATH). After supervised fine-tuning, the advantage persists: WSM reaches 64.07 overall vs. WSD's 62.90 (+1.86%), with notable gains in Language (+4.51%) and Reasoning (+2.74%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is WSD (Warmup-Stable-Decay, Hu et al. 2024), the standard decay-phase LR schedule adopted by DeepSeek-V3 and ERNIE 4.5. WSM wins on every benchmark category in the base-model evaluation (Table 1), with the most substantial margin in Professional Knowledge (+4.83%) and Math (+2.30%). At the individual-benchmark level, the headline gains are +3.5% on MATH, +2.9% on HumanEval, and +5.5% on MMLU-Pro. The advantage carries through to post-training: after 5 epochs of SFT, WSM still leads WSD by +1.86% overall (Table 2), though WSD retains a small edge on Code Completion (−0.48%). The paper also shows that combining merging with an additional decay phase (either Decay-then-Merge or Merge-then-Decay) yields no further improvement, confirming the two are alternative rather than complementary strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Warmup-Stable-Decay (WSD) scheduling paradigm (Hu et al., 2024) by replacing its decay phase with an offline checkpoint merging operation, building on the Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA) framework (Izmailov et al., 2018) and concurrent work on model merging in pre-training (Li et al., 2025). The key conceptual departure is Theorem 3.1, which formalizes a bijective mapping between any monotonically decreasing LR decay schedule and a set of non-negative checkpoint averaging weights—generalizing prior analyses that focused almost exclusively on EMA as the averaging mechanism. Unlike prior theoretical works (Bergsma et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2024a) that used the decay-averaging connection as an analytical tool to explain convergence, WSM repurposes it as a constructive scheduling strategy that eliminates the decay phase entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WSM offers a practical simplification for LLM pre-training pipelines: practitioners can maintain a constant LR indefinitely and produce annealed models via post-hoc checkpoint merging, removing the need to pre-specify decay duration, function, and start step. The identification of merge duration (rather than checkpoint interval or merge count) as the dominant hyperparameter provides actionable guidance, and the finding that merging and decay are alternative rather than complementary pathways clarifies the design space. Open questions include how optimal merge duration scales with model size and total training tokens, whether the approach extends to multi-stage or curriculum-based training, and whether the modest but consistent gains (+1–3 points) persist at significantly larger scales (100B+ parameters) where decay effects may interact differently with optimization dynamics.
