## Problem

Large reasoning models spontaneously restate the user's prompt at the head of their reasoning traces (the Echo of Prompt, EOP), yet this behavior has remained unanalyzed: prior work either injects task-agnostic thinking tokens or mandates re-reading heuristics without explaining the model's own repetition. The paper asks whether this spontaneous echo carries a measurable probabilistic cost, whether that cost correlates with downstream accuracy, and what mechanistic role it plays in the model's attention dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text math question and, for Echoic Prompting, an already-generated partial reasoning chain to which the original question is re-appended mid-trace. The echo prefix detected by the MLP probe typically spans 200–240 tokens (median 226) of restated question content. ED-SFT training data differs from the normal-SFT baseline solely by the presence or absence of this initial echo segment (175 vs. 136 average tokens), keeping all downstream reasoning and the final numeric answer identical.

## Method

The paper formalizes EOP via a rejection-sampling framework that defines a hypothetical echo-free distribution τ_θ and introduces the Echo Likelihood Gap (ΔL) as a computable per-token proxy for the probabilistic cost of echoing. Layer-wise attention analysis on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B reveals that EOP acts as an attention-refocusing mechanism: correct traces show a ~2.87 pp increase in answer→answer-prefix attention concentrated in middle layers 7–18, while answer→question attention remains nearly unchanged (a negative control). To harness this, Echo-Distilled SFT (ED-SFT) fine-tunes models on teacher-generated traces (from gpt-oss-120B) that explicitly begin with an echo-then-reason pattern, while Echoic Prompting (EP) is a training-free inference technique that re-injects the original question after a partial chain to re-ground the model. An interventional echo-reinsertion experiment on failed completions confirms causality: forcing a short echo before resuming generation yields +10.4 and +7.9 EM points on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B and Qwen3-8B respectively, with no gain on the non-reasoning base model.

## Results

On Qwen3-8B-Base, ED-SFT achieves 94.2% strict EM on GSM8K (+3.4 over base), 58.8% on MathQA (+11.8), and 10.0% on Hendrycks-MATH (+8.2), substantially outperforming the normal-SFT baseline (90.8%, 47.0%, 1.8%). On the instruction-tuned Qwen3-8B, ED-SFT still yields gains of +2.8 (GSM8K), +1.9 (MathQA), and +1.1 (Hendrycks-MATH) over base. Echoic Prompting (EP) consistently outperforms TTTS on both AIME24 and MATH-500 under identical decoding and budget settings, and the causal echo-reinsertion intervention recovers +10.4 and +7.9 EM points on failed DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B and Qwen3-8B traces respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary inference-time comparison is against TTTS (Thinking Token based Test-time Scaling, Qian et al. 2025), which injects generic thinking tokens (e.g., 'So', 'Hmm') to spur reasoning. Echoic Prompting (EP) consistently and substantially outperforms TTTS on both AIME24 and MATH-500 under identical deterministic decoding (temperature=0.0) and compute budgets, demonstrating that re-grounding the model with the task-specific original question is more effective than injecting task-agnostic stimuli. In the SFT setting, ED-SFT is compared against a token-matched normal-SFT baseline (same teacher traces with the echo prefix removed). ED-SFT wins on MathQA and Hendrycks-MATH across all three model families (e.g., +11.8 on MathQA for Qwen3-8B-Base), but on GSM8K strict EM for DeepSeek-Distill-Llama-8B, normal-SFT edges out ED-SFT (80.5 vs. 78.2), suggesting the echo's benefit is most pronounced under distributional shift rather than in-distribution. The causal echo-reinsertion experiment further shows that the non-reasoning Qwen3-8B-Base model gains no benefit from echo reinsertion (10.56% → 10.56%), confirming that the mechanism requires the reasoning priors acquired via RL or SFT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions the spontaneous Echo of Prompt (EOP) as an intrinsic, learned attention-refocusing mechanism, departing from prior work that treats repetition as either a failure mode (the 'repeat curse') or an externally instructed heuristic (re-reading, look-twice prompting). It draws on the test-time compute scaling literature for LRMs and the 'lost in the middle' attention-drift problem, but carves new ground by formalizing the echo's probabilistic cost via a rejection-sampling framework (the Echo Likelihood Gap) and providing a mechanistic attention-based explanation. The practical methods (ED-SFT, Echoic Prompting) are deliberately contrasted with generic thinking-token injection (TTTS), emphasizing task-specific re-grounding over task-agnostic stimuli.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes a widely observed but unanalyzed behavior (initial prompt repetition) into a quantifiable, causally validated cognitive primitive, offering both a theoretical lens (rejection-based conditioning) and two lightweight interventions (SFT with echo-prefixed traces, training-free prompt re-injection) that yield consistent gains across multiple math benchmarks. However, all experiments are confined to 8B-parameter models and mathematical reasoning, leaving open whether the EOP mechanism generalizes to larger models, non-mathematical domains, or multilingual settings. The modest absolute gains (+2.8 to +3.4 points on GSM8K) and the correlational nature of much of the analysis (despite one causal intervention) suggest the effect, while real, may be a small but consistent contributor rather than a dominant factor in reasoning accuracy.
