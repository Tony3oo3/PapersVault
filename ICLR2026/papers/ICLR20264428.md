## Problem

Multi-user contextual bandits with graph-structured users and non-linear reward functions lack a principled framework: the original Gang of Bandits assumes linear rewards, while existing kernelized multi-agent approaches construct user kernels heuristically without a clear regularizer-to-kernel correspondence. This paper bridges the gap by showing that a natural joint penalty combining graph smoothness and individual RKHS complexity is exactly the squared norm in a single unified multi-user RKHS, enabling principled GP-based exploration with tight regret guarantees.

## Input / Output

At each round the algorithm receives a user index u_t, a finite set of arm feature vectors D_t ⊂ R^d, and uses a precomputed regularized Laplacian inverse L_ρ^{-1} (from the known user graph) together with a base arm kernel K_x to form the multi-user kernel K((x,u),(x',u')) = [L_ρ^{-1}]_{u,u'} K_x(x,x'). The output is a selected arm x_t and the GP posterior mean μ_{u,t}(x) and variance σ²_{u,t}(x) over the product space U×D, which jointly encode both arm similarity and graph-based user similarity. The kernel matrix K_t is updated online via recursive O(t²) formulas rather than full matrix inversion.

## Method

The central insight is that the additive penalty PEN(f_{1:n};ρ) = ½Σ w_ij‖f_i−f_j‖²_{H_x} + ρΣ‖f_i‖²_{H_x} equals the squared RKHS norm of a single lifted function f(x,u)=f_u(x) in the tensor-product space H_G⊗H_x, with reproducing kernel K((x,u),(x',u'))=[L_ρ^{-1}]_{u,u'}K_x(x,x'). This unification lets the authors place a single GP prior over the lifted function and derive LK-GP-UCB (optimistic arm selection via posterior mean + β_t·σ) and LK-GP-TS (probability-matching via posterior sampling) with confidence widths that retain the exact log-det term rather than bounding it loosely. Regret bounds of Õ(d̃√T) are proven, where the effective dimension d̃ = log det(I_T + K_T/λ) / log(1+TK_max/λ) captures the spectral interplay between the graph Laplacian eigenvalues and the arm kernel eigenvalues, yielding d̃=O(1) under strong homophily (complete graph) and d̃=O(n(log(T/n))^d) in the worst independent-user case.

## Results

The paper evaluates on synthetic multi-user bandit environments (n=20 users, Erdős-Rényi or RBF graphs) across three difficulty levels (10/20/50 arms, T=1000/3000/5000 rounds) and three reward regimes (Linear-GOB, Laplacian-Kernel GP draw, Laplacian-Kernel representer draw). The primary metric is cumulative regret. In the Laplacian-Kernel (GP draw) regime, LK-GP-UCB and LK-GP-TS are consistently the top-performing algorithms across all difficulty levels. In the harder representer-draw regime, LK-GP methods achieve clearly sublinear regret while most baselines (GP-UCB, Per-User LinUCB, Pooled LinUCB) struggle to achieve sublinear regret. In the Linear-GOB regime (where linear methods are expected to excel), LK-GP methods still beat most baselines with clear gaps, demonstrating robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against **Coop-KernelUCB** (Dubey et al., ICML 2020), which heuristically constructs a multi-user kernel as a product of user and arm kernels, LK-GP-UCB is consistently better across all nine experimental settings, even after the authors empirically tuned Coop-KernelUCB's similarity kernel to its best configuration (inverse Laplacian). Against **GOB.Lin** (Cesa-Bianchi et al., NeurIPS 2013), the original linear Gang-of-Bandits algorithm, LK-GP methods achieve clear regret gaps in both linear and non-linear regimes, with the advantage widening substantially in the Laplacian-Kernel settings where GOB.Lin's linear assumption is violated. Against **GP-UCB** (Chowdhury & Gopalan, ICML 2017), a single-user kernelized bandit that ignores graph structure, LK-GP methods outperform it in all non-linear settings and remain competitive in the linear regime, confirming the value of the graph-aware multi-user kernel. Against **GraphUCB** (Yang et al., AISTATS 2020), a Laplacian-regularized linear graph bandit, LK-GP methods dominate in non-linear settings while remaining competitive in the linear regime. The two linear baselines, **Pooled LinUCB** and **Per-User LinUCB**, are outperformed by LK-GP methods in all settings, with the gap being most pronounced in the representer-draw regime where LK-GP methods achieve sublinear regret while these baselines do not.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: the Gang-of-Bandits (GOB) framework for multi-user graph-structured bandits (Cesa-Bianchi et al., 2013), which was restricted to linear reward functions, and kernelized/GP bandits for single-agent non-linear rewards (Chowdhury & Gopalan, 2017; Srinivas et al., 2009). The key theoretical contribution is showing that the natural joint penalty combining graph Laplacian smoothness and individual RKHS ridge regularization is exactly the squared norm in a unified multi-user RKHS, with an explicit reproducing kernel K((x,u),(x',u')) = [L_ρ^{-1}]_{u,u'} K_x(x,x'). This extends the Smola & Kondor (2003) result on graph Laplacian kernels from scalar signals to vector-valued (function-valued) signals via a tensor product construction, and contrasts with Dubey et al. (2020), who construct a multi-agent kernel heuristically as a product of user and arm kernels without a principled penalty-to-norm equivalence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The principled kernel construction eliminates the gap between the modeling goal (graph homophily + non-linear rewards) and the algorithm, yielding regret bounds that scale with an effective dimension rather than the number of users n. The spectral analysis revealing that under strong homophily (complete graph) the effective dimension becomes O(1) and regret is independent of n is a clean theoretical result with no single-user analogue. Limitations include validation only on synthetic data, the assumption of a known graph, and the restriction to homophilous (monotone spectral filter) priors. Open questions include extending to heterophilous or non-monotone spectral filters, distributed/decentralized settings, and whether the effective-dimension framework transfers to other structured bandit problems.
