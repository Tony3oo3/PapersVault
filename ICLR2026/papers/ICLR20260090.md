## Problem

Existing all-in-one image restoration methods face a fundamental trade-off: degradation-agnostic models cannot leverage degradation-specific priors, while degradation-aware models (PEFT-based or MoE-based) are vulnerable to the inevitable error in degradation estimation, which leads to incorrect expert or prompt routing. This paper addresses how to exploit degradation information for restoration while remaining robust to estimation uncertainty.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260090/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a degraded RGB image (processed at 224×224 for the CLIP-based degradation extractor, with restoration experts operating on 128×128 patches during training and 512×512 inputs at inference) and optionally a task instruction string in 'instruction mode' that prunes the expert pool via binary masks. The output is a single restored high-quality RGB image. In auto mode, no user input beyond the degraded image is required; the system automatically performs extraction, recognition, routing, and restoration.

## Method

UniRestorer first trains a fine-grained degradation representation (DR) extractor based on DA-CLIP using contrastive learning with degree-aware captions (e.g., 'small/medium/large noise'). All training DRs are then hierarchically K-means clustered into multiple granularity levels (e.g., {1, 4, 8} groups), and a separate expert network (Restormer or RetinexFormer) is trained per DR group. At inference, two routers operate jointly: a degradation router G_d selects a path from root to leaf in the hierarchy, while a granularity router G_g—trained with a data-uncertainty-inspired loss that models degradation as a Gaussian whose variance reflects estimation confidence—selects a coarser level. The final expert is the intersection of both routes, so confident estimates route to fine-grained experts for accuracy while ambiguous estimates fall back to coarser experts for generalization.

## Results

On the 7-task single-degradation benchmark, UniRestorer achieves 32.77 dB average PSNR, outperforming the strongest all-in-one baseline MoCE-IR (30.58 dB on 5 tasks) by over 2 dB and the task-agnostic Restormer (28.27 dB) by 4.5 dB. On the CDD-11 mixed-degradation benchmark, UniRestorer reaches 30.90 dB average PSNR, surpassing MoCE-IR-S (29.05 dB) and OneRestore (28.47 dB). In instruction mode, UniRestorer matches or exceeds dedicated single-task models on most tasks, e.g., 41.68 dB on deraining (vs. Restormer 39.60 dB) and 37.45 dB on dehazing (vs. Restormer 33.18 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniRestorer substantially outperforms all task-agnostic backbones (Restormer, NAFNet, SwinIR, MPRNet) by 4–10 dB on the 7-task average, confirming that degradation-aware routing is critical. Among all-in-one methods, UniRestorer beats the strongest PEFT-based baselines (PromptIR, InstructIR, DaAIR, MoCE-IR) by 1.5–5 dB on 7-task PSNR, and surpasses the best 5-task method MoCE-IR (30.58 dB) by 2.2 dB. On the CDD-11 mixed-degradation benchmark, UniRestorer leads MoCE-IR-S by 1.85 dB and OneRestore by 2.43 dB in average PSNR. In instruction mode (Table 5), UniRestorer† matches or exceeds dedicated single-task models on deraining (41.68 vs. 39.60 dB Restormer), dehazing (37.45 vs. 33.18 dB), and low-light enhancement (27.25 vs. 23.66 dB), while remaining competitive on denoising and deblurring. The LoRA variant retains most of the performance (31.81 vs. 32.77 dB 7T) while reducing parameters from 627M to 171M, demonstrating that the multi-granularity MoE design is parameter-efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniRestorer builds on DA-CLIP (Luo et al., 2024) as its degradation representation extractor, extending it with fine-grained degradation degree captions, and borrows the Gaussian negative-log-likelihood formulation from data uncertainty learning (Chang et al., 2020) to derive its granularity estimation loss. Conceptually, it departs from existing degradation-aware all-in-one methods (PromptIR, MoCE-IR, DaAIR, GRIDS) that operate at a single, coarse granularity and route solely on degradation estimation. The key architectural departure is the hierarchical clustering of the degradation space into multi-granularity DR groups, paired with a dual-router (degradation + granularity) that selects experts at the appropriate level of specificity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that an all-in-one model can match or exceed dedicated single-task models (e.g., 41.68 dB deraining vs. 39.60 for Restormer) when given task instructions, a gap that prior all-in-one methods have struggled to close. The multi-granularity routing mechanism offers a principled solution to the inherent uncertainty in degradation estimation, with clean ablations showing ~1.4 dB out-of-distribution gains from granularity-aware routing. However, the full model requires 627M parameters (vs. 26M for Restormer), and the LoRA variant (171M) still carries substantial overhead, limiting practical deployment. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly open-ended degradation spaces beyond the seven tasks studied, and whether the hierarchical clustering step can be made end-to-end differentiable.
