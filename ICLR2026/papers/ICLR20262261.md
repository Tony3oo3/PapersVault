## Problem

Image personalization via test-time fine-tuning of T2I diffusion models suffers from 'concept coupling,' where the model spuriously binds the personalization target to co-occurring concepts in the few reference images (e.g., always generating a girl alongside a backpack). Existing mitigations—data, weight, loss, and region regularization—treat coupling as a symptom of overfitting through indirect heuristics, without directly modeling the underlying statistical dependency. This paper is the first to formalize concept coupling as a tractable statistical dependency problem and identify its two distinct root causes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3–6 reference images of a personalization target (subject, style, or face) along with VLM-generated captions, and produces a personalized image of that target in a novel context specified by a text prompt. It is designed as a plug-and-play module: two additional regularization losses are added to the standard diffusion training objective of any existing fine-tuning method (DreamBooth, CustomDiffusion, LoRA, VisualEncoder, IP-Adapter, etc.) without modifying the architecture or inference pipeline. The losses require four forward passes through the UNet per training step (joint condition, individual conditions, and empty condition) plus CLIP encoder lookups for the PDLoss, increasing GPU memory by roughly 1.5–2× over the baseline.

## Method

ACCORD decomposes the concept coupling discrepancy (Eq. 4) into two measurable components via Theorem 1: a Denoising Dependence Discrepancy (dependency change accumulated across denoising steps) and a Prior Dependence Discrepancy (shift in the learned concept's relational priors relative to its superclass). The Denoising Decouple Loss (DDLoss) upper-bounds the total denoising discrepancy by the sum of per-step changes (triangle inequality) and, via Theorem 2, computes each step's change in closed form from the UNet's outputs under four conditions—(c_p, c_g), c_p alone, c_g alone, and empty—scaled by a linearly increasing weight t/T. The Prior Decouple Loss (PDLoss) leverages CLIP's InfoNCE-trained cosine similarity as a proxy for the conditional probability ratio p(c_g|c_p)/p(c_g|c_s), minimizing |cos(f_p, f_g) − cos(f_s, f_g)| to align the personalized embedding's relational structure with its superclass. Together, the two losses directly minimize the statistical dependency that defines concept coupling, requiring no regularization dataset and no restrictive weight constraints.

## Results

On DreamBench (subject personalization), ACCORD as a plug-and-play module improves DreamBooth by +3.8 to +5.1 on subject fidelity (CLIP-I, DINO-I) and +1.8 on text alignment (BLIP-T), and improves CustomDiffusion by +8.4 to +8.7 on fidelity metrics. Notably, LoRA(SDXL) with ACCORD (92.9M params) outperforms the 3.8B-parameter Omnigen on CLIP-I (77.1 vs. 73.9) and DINO-I (72.4 vs. 68.6). On StyleBench, ACCORD reduces Gram-Distance by up to 13.0% (VisualEncoder) and 10.3% (CustomDiffusion) while improving text alignment. On FFHQ face personalization, ACCORD improves Face-Sim from 14.8 to 16.4 and CLIP-T from 20.0 to 20.7 over IP-Adapter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACCORD consistently improves upon all fine-tuning baselines in a plug-and-play fashion. Against DreamBooth, ACCORD gains +1.0 CLIP-T, +1.8 BLIP-T, +4.6 CLIP-I, and +5.1 DINO-I, substantially closing the fidelity gap caused by regularization datasets. Against CustomDiffusion, the fidelity gains are even larger (+8.7 CLIP-I, +8.7 DINO-I) with only a negligible CLIP-T drop (−0.1). Against LoRA(SDXL), ACCORD improves CLIP-T by +0.7 and CLIP-I by +0.8, and the resulting model outperforms the much larger Omnigen (3.8B) on both CLIP-I (77.1 vs. 73.9) and DINO-I (72.4 vs. 68.6). Against loss-regularization baselines Facechain-SuDe and ClassDiffusion, ACCORD achieves higher fidelity (78.6 vs. 74.3 CLIP-I on DreamBooth; 71.4 vs. 61.3 on CustomDiffusion) while also improving text alignment. Against the zero-shot IP-Adapter on FFHQ, ACCORD improves Face-Sim by +1.6 (16.4 vs. 14.8) and CLIP-T by +0.7, confirming applicability beyond test-time fine-tuning. The only minor trade-off is a −0.2 DINO-I drop for LoRA(SDXL) w/ ACCORD (non-tuned weights), which is resolved to +0.3 with tuned weights.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in image personalization, where concept coupling is treated as a symptom of overfitting addressed through proxy-based regularization (data augmentation, weight constraints, heuristic losses, or spatial attention masking). It builds on the implicit-classifier interpretation of diffusion models (as in Facechain-SuDe) and CLIP's InfoNCE density-ratio structure to derive two targeted losses from a formal statistical dependency decomposition. The key conceptual departure is reframing concept coupling as an explicit conditional-dependence problem with two identifiable sources—Denoising Dependence Discrepancy and Prior Dependence Discrepancy—rather than an overfitting artifact, enabling direct optimization of the coupling metric itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play formulation (two additional loss terms, no architectural changes) makes ACCORD broadly applicable across DreamBooth, CustomDiffusion, LoRA, VisualEncoder, and IP-Adapter pipelines, and the theoretical decomposition provides a diagnostic lens that prior heuristic methods lacked. However, the approach is constrained by the base model's ability to represent superclass–concept relationships, only decouples concepts explicitly present in training prompts, and is incompatible with autoregressive T2I models. Open questions include whether the dependence-regularization principle transfers to video diffusion, multi-concept personalization at scale, or settings where the superclass is ambiguous.
