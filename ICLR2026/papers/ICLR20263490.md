## Problem

Pixel-space generative models have long underperformed their VAE-based latent-space counterparts in both generation quality and training efficiency, primarily due to high computational cost and slow convergence in raw pixel space. Additionally, consistency models (one/few-step generators) have never been successfully trained directly on high-resolution images without relying on pre-trained VAEs or diffusion models. This paper aims to close both gaps by identifying why pixel-space training is difficult and providing a principled solution.

## Input / Output

The model takes Gaussian noise and a class label as input and directly outputs pixel-space RGB images at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution, bypassing any VAE compression. To maintain constant computational cost across resolutions, the ViT backbone uses proportionally larger patch sizes (16×16 for 256px, 32×32 for 512px), keeping the input token length fixed. The diffusion variant generates images with 75 NFEs via Heun ODE sampling, while the consistency variant produces images in a single forward pass.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage framework that decomposes pixel-space generative training analogously to self-supervised learning. In the pre-training stage, a ViT encoder is trained with two NT-Xent losses: a standard contrastive loss on augmented views of clean images, and a novel representation consistency loss that aligns encoder outputs at temporally adjacent points along the same probability-flow ODE trajectory (i.e., matching features of x_{t_n} and x_{t_{n-1}} sharing the same underlying clean image). This reformulates representation learning on noisy images as a generative alignment task, avoiding the representation collapse that plagues standard SSL at high noise levels. In the fine-tuning stage, the pre-trained encoder is paired with a randomly initialized decoder (with residual connections and adaLN-Zero conditioning) and trained end-to-end under either a standard denoising objective (diffusion) or a consistency training objective augmented with an auxiliary NT-Xent loss against a frozen copy of the pre-trained encoder. The key insight is that the encoder's role is to learn temporally consistent high-level semantics across noise levels, while the decoder handles low-level pixel reconstruction—mirroring the SSL encoder/decoder decomposition.

## Results

On ImageNet-256 with CFG, EPG-G/16 achieves a SOTA pixel-space FID of 1.58 with 75 NFEs (class-balanced sampling), surpassing all prior pixel-space methods by a large margin. On ImageNet-512, EPG-L/32 reaches FID 2.35 with 75 NFEs. For few-step generation, EPG-L/16 consistency model achieves FID 8.82 in a single step on ImageNet-256, the first successful pixel-space consistency model without pre-trained VAEs or diffusion models. In terms of training efficiency, EPG-XL/16 reaches FID 2.04 in 139 hours on 8×H200 GPUs, compared to DiT-XL/2's 506 hours for FID 2.27 (~30% of the training compute).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against latent-space methods, EPG-XXL/16 (FID 1.87) outperforms DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27) and SiT-XL/2 (FID 2.06) while using only ~30% of DiT's training compute (139h vs 506h on 8×H200). However, EPG still trails the strongest latent-space methods that leverage external supervision: REPA (FID 1.42) and RAE (FID 1.28), which the authors attribute to a significant training compute disparity rather than a fundamental limitation. Among pixel-space baselines, EPG-G/16 (FID 1.58) decisively beats all prior methods: SiD2 (1.72), JiT-G/16 (1.82), PixNerd-XL/16 (1.93), PixelFlow (1.98), VDM++ (2.12), SiD (2.44), RIN (3.42), and ADM (3.94). For few-step consistency generation, EPG-L/16 (FID 8.82, 1-step) dramatically outperforms iCT-XL/2 (FID 34.24, 1-step) and Shortcut-XL/2 (FID 10.60, 1-step), though IMM (FID 8.05) edges it out by 0.77 FID at 11× the training cost. The paper also shows EPG outperforms pixel-space baselines trained from scratch (FID 59.69 DM / NaN CM), MoCo v3 pre-training (56.26/36.77), and rRCM pre-training (46.51/37.55) in both diffusion and consistency downstream tasks on ImageNet-224.

## Relation to Prior Work

EPG extends the representation consistency model (rRCM, Lei et al. 2025) by replacing its brittle EMA-annealing schedule with a temperature-scheduled NT-Xent objective, and by explicitly decomposing the encoder-decoder roles in diffusion models along the lines of self-supervised learning (MoCo v3, BYOL). The pre-training stage borrows the consistency training formulation and time-discretization annealing from iCT (Song & Dhariwal, 2023), while the fine-tuning stage for consistency models follows ECT's (Geng et al., 2025) continuous-time interval strategy. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant LDM/VAE paradigm by arguing that the encoder in a diffusion model is a semantic learner and the decoder is a task-specific head, making the overall training problem analogous to SSL pre-training followed by end-to-end fine-tuning rather than a monolithic generative objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the VAE bottleneck, EPG simplifies the generative pipeline and removes a permanent capacity ceiling on adaptation to new data, while achieving pixel-space SOTA (FID 1.58 on ImageNet-256) at roughly 30% of DiT's training compute. The first successful training of a consistency model directly on high-resolution images without any pre-trained VAE or diffusion model (FID 8.82, one-step) opens a path to efficient few-step generation that is not tethered to external pretrained components. Key limitations remain: EPG still trails the strongest latent-space methods (REPA 1.42, RAE 1.28), which the authors attribute to compute disparity rather than a fundamental ceiling, and the framework has only been validated on ImageNet class-conditional generation. Open questions include whether the SSL-style pre-training transfers to text-to-image or video generation, and whether leveraging powerful off-the-shelf backbones can close the remaining gap.
