## Problem

Synthetic hand images for 3D hand reconstruction training suffer from two critical misalignment issues: (1) VLM-generated captions introduce irrelevant environmental details through overthinking, causing the diffusion model to shift attention away from hand regions during denoising, and (2) neglecting human body structural information leads to floating hands and implausible poses in generated images. Existing diffusion-based hand generation methods either restrict to lab environments, require expensive optimization for hand feature refinement, or ignore hand shape entirely.

## Input / Output

The method takes a rendered hand mesh image (512×512, cropped centered on the two-hands region) as structural control and a text prompt structured as human behavior semantics (decomposed into pose, action, hand-action, and environment components) as semantic guidance. It produces a photorealistic 512×512 hand image with a naturally attached human body, where the hand geometry matches the conditioning mesh. The text prompts are not raw VLM captions but are refined through a three-stage CoT pipeline to suppress irrelevant details and preserve human-centric context.

## Method

SesaHand builds on Stable Diffusion 1.5 with a ControlNet module conditioned on hand mesh images, introducing two alignment mechanisms. For semantic alignment, a Chain-of-Thought pipeline (Captioner → Extractor → Composer) uses Qwen2.5-VL and LLaMA 3 70B with few-shot JSON-format extraction to decompose VLM captions into four human behavior semantics components, eliminating overthinking-induced irrelevant details. For structural alignment, hierarchical structural fusion extracts multi-resolution self-attention maps (64, 32, 16, 8) from ControlNet's encoding and middle blocks, aggregates them via max-pooling and summation, and applies the result to refine control module features before feeding them to the UNet decoder through zero-convolution layers. Additionally, a hand structure attention enhancement adds a learned bias term (α=2.0) to cross-attention maps at positions corresponding to hand-related verb tokens (identified via POS tagging), efficiently boosting hand-region attention without the slow embedding optimization used by prior work. Training uses AdamW at lr 1e-5 for 50 epochs on a single GPU (~2 days), with the Stable Diffusion backbone frozen.

## Results

On MSCOCO, SesaHand achieves FID 18.63, FID-H 17.77, and KID-H 0.00718, outperforming the closest competitor AttentionHand (FID-H 27.09, KID-H 0.01287) by 34% and 44% respectively on hand-region metrics. For 3D hand reconstruction, finetuning InterWild with SesaHand-generated images reduces MPVPE by 7.0% on ReIH (13.01 mm) versus only 0.3% with AttentionHand (13.95 mm), and DIR achieves a 13.2% MPVPE reduction on ReIH (19.21 mm) versus 8.8% with AttentionHand. User preference scores reach 4.55/5, the highest among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SesaHand outperforms all six baselines on the primary hand-region metrics (FID-H, KID-H) and 3D keypoint accuracy (MSE-3D). Against AttentionHand (the closest competitor), SesaHand reduces FID-H from 27.09 to 17.77 (−34%) and KID-H from 0.01287 to 0.00718 (−44%), while matching its hand confidence (0.966 vs. 0.965) and improving MSE-3D from 1.986 to 1.537. Against ControlNet, the gains are even larger: FID-H drops from 40.32 to 17.77 and hand confidence rises from 0.810 to 0.966. T2I-Adapter and Uni-ControlNet trail further behind on all hand-specific metrics. For 3D hand reconstruction, SesaHand-generated images consistently improve downstream models more than AttentionHand's: with InterWild on ReIH, MPVPE improves by 7.0% vs. 0.3%, and with DIR on ReIH, by 13.2% vs. 8.8%. In the FoundHand comparison (Appendix E.1), SesaHand achieves 8.56 mm PA-MPJPE after finetuning HaMeR versus FoundHand's 13.68 mm, while also producing hand-body aligned images where FoundHand generates floating hands.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ControlNet's architecture for controllable diffusion-based generation and extends the hand-specific generation line started by AttentionHand (Park et al., 2024). It departs from AttentionHand in two key ways: replacing the slow iterative optimization for hand feature refinement with a lightweight bias term on cross-attention maps, and introducing a hierarchical structural fusion that aggregates multi-resolution self-attention maps from ControlNet's encoder and middle blocks. The semantic alignment component introduces a CoT-based pipeline (Captioner → Extractor → Composer) to distill VLM captions into focused human behavior semantics, addressing the overthinking problem identified in prior work that used raw VLM descriptions as prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that diffusion-generated hand images with proper semantic and structural alignment can meaningfully improve in-the-wild 3D hand reconstruction (up to 13.2% MPVPE reduction on ReIH with DIR), validating generative models as a practical alternative to game-engine synthetic data. The 60× training speedup over AttentionHand (0.44s vs 27.25s per iteration) makes the approach far more accessible. Open questions remain around hand-object interaction fidelity (contact-point fusion), small-hand conditioning, and whether the CoT semantics pipeline generalizes beyond the MSCOCO training distribution to truly diverse in-the-wild scenarios.
