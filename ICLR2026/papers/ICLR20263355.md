## Problem

Dataset-level heterogeneity in time series—arising from differences in sampling resolution, physical constraints, and pattern transitions—introduces domain biases that fundamentally degrade the generalization of Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs). Existing federated approaches treat heterogeneity only at a coarse domain level (e.g., climate vs. healthcare) and overlook dataset-specific structural biases, preventing them from producing true general-purpose TSFMs that support zero-shot inference across unseen domains without centralizing data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263355/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes heterogeneous multivariate and univariate time series distributed across N clients, where each client holds a distinct dataset with its own sampling rate, physical domain, and temporal dynamics. Pretraining uses patch-wise masking (75% ratio, patch length 32–64, sequence length up to 3072) on LOTSA (231B time points across 174 dataset-specific clients). The output is a 28.42M-parameter decoder-only Transformer producing domain-invariant temporal representations that, after minimal fine-tuning (one epoch for non-zero-shot tasks), yield forecasted values, imputed missing points, class labels, and anomaly scores across eight downstream tasks.

## Method

FeDaL trains a general TSFM from scratch under a federated protocol using two complementary bias-elimination mechanisms. On the client side, Domain Bias Elimination (DBE) decomposes latent patch representations into trend and seasonal components via a moving-average operator, extracts learnable bias vectors (scaled by γ_t and γ_s), and injects them additively into the reconstruction path so the backbone is forced to encode only transferable temporal structure; an EMA-stabilized alignment penalty (λ‖b − b^g‖²) prevents local bias drift. On the server side, Global Bias Elimination (GBE) applies (i) a SCAFFOLD-style gradient-level dynamic correction that accumulates client-server drift in a state vector and subtracts it from the FedAvg result, and (ii) Core-set Tuning, where each client optimizes a compact set of learnable vectors via gradient matching against a sampled batch, perturbs them in the Fourier amplitude domain for privacy, semantically re-aligns them, and uploads them for server-side fine-tuning with convex fusion (α-weighted) to prevent catastrophic forgetting. The backbone is a decoder-only Transformer (8 layers, 768 dim, 8 heads) trained with self-supervised masked patch reconstruction, and the full pipeline is validated across forecasting, imputation, classification, and anomaly detection against 54 baselines.

## Results

On zero-shot long-term forecasting (Table 4), FeDaL achieves an average MSE of 0.335 and MAE of 0.365 across ETTh1/h2/m1/m2 and Weather, surpassing Moirai-large (0.372 MSE) by 6.2% and FFTS (0.348) by 3.8%, while using only 28.42M parameters versus 84M–2.4B for centralized TSFMs. On federated representation learning (Table 1), FeDaL reduces reconstruction MSE to 0.551 (UTSD-H1), 0.511 (UTSD-H2), and 0.387 (CTSD-DI), outperforming the strongest FL baseline FFTS by 4.16% and 8.86% respectively. On imputation, FeDaL achieves 9/10 first-place counts and reduces MSE by 12.64% over FFTS and 27.62% over FedAvg. On anomaly detection, FeDaL attains the best average F1, surpassing Moment by 5.17% and FFTS by 3.69%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FeDaL consistently outperforms all federated baselines (FedAvg, FedProx, FedPer, FedRep, FFTS) across every evaluated task, with the largest margins over FedAvg (e.g., 27.62% MSE reduction on imputation) and the closest competitor FFTS (3.8–12.64% improvements depending on task). Against centralized TSFMs, FeDaL (28.42M params) achieves zero-shot forecasting MSE competitive with Time-MoE-ultra (2.4B params, 0.337 vs 0.335) while using only 1.8% of the parameters and ~70B fewer training samples, yielding the highest Information Gain per Cost (IGC = 4.545×10⁻¹⁹ vs 4.061×10⁻²¹ for Time-MoE-ultra). On full-shot long-term forecasting, FeDaL secures 9/12 first-place MSE slots across ETTh1/h2/m1/m2, Weather, and ILI, beating task-specific models like PatchTST, TimesNet, and GPT4TS. On short-term M4 forecasting, FeDaL achieves the best MASE (1.489) and OWA (0.818) but trails FFTS by a narrow 0.07% on SMAPE (11.412 vs 11.404). On classification (UEA/UCR), FeDaL surpasses FedAvg and FFTS by 6.4%/6.1% and 9.5%/3.2% respectively, and outperforms centralized Moment and GPT4TS. The only notable losses are on individual zero-shot forecasting sub-datasets where Time-MoE-ultra edges FeDaL by <0.01 MSE on ETTh1 and ETTm2, and on SMD anomaly detection where FFTS scores 89.88 vs FeDaL's 88.46 F1.

## Relation to Prior Work

FeDaL extends the federated foundation model (FFM) paradigm to time series by building on FedAvg's client-server protocol, SCAFFOLD's gradient-drift correction, and Grad-Match's core-set selection, while adapting time series decomposition (from Autoformer) to operate in latent representation space for bias estimation. Its most direct predecessor, FFTS (Chen et al., 2025), also performs federated TSFM pretraining but treats heterogeneity only at a coarse domain level; FeDaL departs by explicitly modeling dataset-level biases (temporal resolution, physical constraints, pattern transitions) through the complementary DBE and GBE mechanisms. The decoder-only Transformer backbone follows the generative pretraining lineage of TimeGPT-1 and PreDcT, but is trained from scratch in a federated setting rather than centrally on a monolithic corpus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FeDaL demonstrates that a 28M-parameter federated model can match or exceed centralized TSFMs with up to 2.4B parameters on zero-shot forecasting, imputation, classification, and anomaly detection, suggesting that explicit bias handling may be a more efficient path to generalization than brute-force scaling. The first systematic study of federated TSFM scaling (data volume, client count, participation rate) provides practical guidance for decentralized deployments where data silos are the norm. Open questions include whether the Fourier-based perturbation offers formal privacy guarantees (the authors acknowledge it mitigates but does not eliminate leakage), whether DBE's decomposition-based bias estimation generalizes to highly non-stationary or event-driven series, and how the framework behaves under adversarial or Byzantine clients.
