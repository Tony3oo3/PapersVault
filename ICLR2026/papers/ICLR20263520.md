## Problem

Existing molecular representations for protein-ligand binding either use atom-level graphs that cannot express higher-order chemical context (stereochemistry, conjugation, lone pairs), or fragment-based methods that partition molecules into disjoint sets, thereby breaking aromatic rings, losing chirality, and erasing ionic states. No prior hierarchical model supports the many-to-many atom-fragment mappings needed to preserve these chemically critical properties simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263520/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a 3D pocket-ligand pair: the protein pocket is represented by residue-level tokens with 3D coordinates, while the ligand is tokenized via OverlapBPE into overlapping fragments (many-to-many atom-fragment mapping) with 3D conformer information. For binding affinity prediction, the output is a scalar free-energy value; for virtual screening, it produces a ranking score per ligand; for HTS, a binary activity label. The joint encoder processes both molecules in a shared 3D frame, and the many-to-many mapping requires bidirectional scatter operations (atom→fragment and fragment→atom) throughout the network.

## Method

The paper introduces two coupled components. First, OverlapBPE is a frequency-based BPE tokenization that iteratively merges the most frequent adjacent token pairs on a token graph, but unlike prior methods it allows fragments to share atoms (overlapping), preserving aromatic ring integrity, chirality (via 3D conformer-augmented isomeric SMILES identifiers), and formal charges. Second, h-MINT is an SE(3)-equivariant graph Transformer designed specifically for this many-to-many mapping: it builds a KNN token graph, expands each token-level edge into atom-level geometric edges (connecting each atom to k-nearest atoms in the neighboring token), and performs bilevel cross-attention where atom-level scores are aggregated into token-level scores and vice versa, enabling bidirectional information flow between the two scales. The architecture stacks equivariant bilevel attention, bidirectional equivariant FFN (with scatter-mean across the atom-fragment mapping), and equivariant layer normalization, and is trained with supervised regression for affinity prediction or contrastive + MSE losses for virtual screening.

## Results

On PDBBind, h-MINT achieves RMSE 1.295, Pearson 0.640, and Spearman 0.625, outperforming the strongest baseline GET-PS (RMSE 1.387, Pearson 0.601, Spearman 0.582) by ~4% Pearson and ~5% Spearman. On LBA, the method reaches Pearson 0.660 and Spearman 0.661, surpassing GET-PS (0.631/0.642) by 3% and 2% respectively. For zero-shot virtual screening on DUD-E, h-MINT attains AUC 84.45% and BEDROC 47.64%, exceeding LigUnity (81.69%/46.01%) by ~3% AUC. On LIT-PCBA, the method achieves BEDROC 6.27% and EF@1% 5.20, substantially outperforming LigUnity (4.34%/3.03%) in early-recognition metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The closest competitor is GET-PS (Kong et al., 2024), which uses Principal Subgraph tokenization with a non-overlapping hierarchical architecture; h-MINT outperforms it by 4% Pearson (0.640 vs 0.601) and 5% Spearman (0.625 vs 0.582) on PDBBind, and by 3% Pearson (0.660 vs 0.631) on LBA, with significance tests confirming p < 0.005. On DUD-E virtual screening, h-MINT beats both DrugCLIP (AUC 81.39%) and LigUnity (AUC 81.69%) by ~3% AUC and ~1.6% BEDROC, while also improving EF@1% from 29.07 to 29.91. On LIT-PCBA, the gap widens in early-recognition: h-MINT achieves BEDROC 6.27% vs LigUnity's 4.34% and EF@1% 5.20 vs 3.03, though AUC is comparable (57.77% vs 57.61%). On PubChem HTS assays, the chiral OverlapBPE + XGBoost pipeline achieves the best average rank (2.625) across 8 assays, surpassing purpose-built chirality models ChiRo (6.375) and MolKGNN (3.250) while using only bag-of-words features. Ablation (Table 7) confirms that GET is architecturally incompatible with OverlapBPE's overlapping tokens, and that h-MINT+PS (non-overlapping) performs similarly to GET+PS, isolating the gain to the overlap mechanism itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GET (Kong et al., 2024) equivariant transformer architecture for 3D molecular interaction learning by introducing a new tokenization scheme (OverlapBPE) that permits overlapping fragments, and a corresponding hierarchical model (h-MINT) that handles the resulting many-to-many atom-fragment mappings. OverlapBPE itself builds on the Principal Subgraph (PS-VAE, Kong et al., 2022b) data-driven fragmentation approach and byte-pair encoding from NLP, but departs from both by allowing atoms to belong to multiple fragments simultaneously, thereby preserving aromatic ring integrity, chirality, and ionic states that disjoint partitioning destroys. The architectural contribution is specifically the bilevel attention mechanism and bidirectional scatter operations that enable cross-scale information flow under overlapping token assignments, which GET's 1-to-1 atom-token design cannot support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and recurring failure mode in molecular representation: disjoint fragmentation breaks chemically critical substructures (aromatic systems, charged groups, stereocenters), which limits the model's ability to capture interactions like π-cation contacts and chiral recognition. The 'fuzzy boundary' framing of molecular substructures is a useful conceptual lens that could inform future tokenization and representation work beyond protein-ligand binding. Open questions include whether OverlapBPE's benefits transfer to molecular generation tasks, whether the 1.32× atom duplication overhead becomes problematic at scale, and whether the approach generalizes to enzyme engineering and docking pose prediction as the authors note as future directions.
