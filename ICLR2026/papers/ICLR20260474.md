## Problem

Standard DPO and RLHF methods optimize alignment at the sequence level, treating all tokens equally and ignoring the differential importance of individual tokens, which destabilizes training through sampling distribution shifts. Existing token-level variants rely on biased probability proxies or overly simplified weighting schemes to identify key tokens, and still inherit DPO's binary good/bad comparison framework, which cannot finely shape generation behavior in a continuous semantic space.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a triplet (x, y_w, y_l) consisting of a text prompt, a human-preferred response, and a non-preferred response, and additionally generates an intermediate anchor response y from the current policy π_θ during training. The output is an aligned LLM policy π_θ that assigns token-level importance weights modulating the implicit reward signal at each generation step. Unlike standard DPO which only requires the prompt and two responses, TI-DPO requires an extra forward-backward pass per sequence to compute gradient attribution scores and an on-policy generation step to produce the triplet anchor.

## Method

TI-DPO introduces two synergistic mechanisms. First, a hybrid weighting mechanism computes per-token importance via L1-norm gradient attribution of the final-token logit with respect to each token embedding, then blends this data-driven signal with a Gaussian prior (centered on the sequence midpoint, σ=T/4) via a convex combination W = λ·I_norm + (1−λ)·P_prior, where the prior counteracts the U-shaped 'lost-in-the-middle' attention bias. Second, a triplet loss uses the policy-generated intermediate response as an anchor, penalizing it if it is not closer to the preferred response than to the non-preferred one by a margin α, thereby providing structured guidance beyond binary comparison. The total objective is L_TI-DPO = L_DPO-w + γ·L_triplet, where L_DPO-w is the standard DPO loss with token-level log-ratios weighted by the hybrid importance scores. Theoretically, the paper proves TI-DPO achieves a strictly tighter loss bound than DPO (Theorem 2) via variance reduction on non-critical tokens, and that its optimal policy achieves higher expected reward under a fixed KL constraint (Theorem 3) by concentrating divergence budget on critical tokens.

## Results

TI-DPO achieves the highest average score of 62.3 across six benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, GPQA, HumanEval, TruthfulQA, IFEval) averaged over three base models (Llama-3.2-3B, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B-v0.3), outperforming the strongest baseline GRPO (62.1) and TIS-DPO (61.1). The method's largest gains are on HumanEval (67.0 vs. 64.3 for GRPO) and IFEval (75.7 vs. 74.0 for GRPO), while it trails GRPO slightly on GSM8K (73.0 vs. 75.7) and GPQA (26.0 vs. 28.0). Compared to standard DPO (57.7 average), TI-DPO improves by 4.6 points overall, with the largest single-task gain on IFEval (+5.7) and HumanEval (+6.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TI-DPO outperforms all 11 baselines on average, with the most significant margins over DPO (+4.6 avg), SimPO (+7.8), and IPO (+8.1). Against the closest competitor GRPO (62.1 avg), TI-DPO wins on HumanEval (67.0 vs. 64.3, +2.7) and IFEval (75.7 vs. 74.0, +1.7) but loses on GSM8K (73.0 vs. 75.7, −2.7) and GPQA (26.0 vs. 28.0, −2.0), reflecting a trade-off between fine-grained token control and holistic sequence-level reasoning. Compared to the token-level baseline TIS-DPO (61.1), TI-DPO gains +1.2 on average, with notable improvements on IFEval (+1.7) and HumanEval (+1.5), while TIS-DPO edges ahead on TruthfulQA (62.5 vs. 62.0). Against sequence-level methods like TPO (60.7) and CPO (59.4), TI-DPO leads by 1.6 and 2.9 points respectively, with the largest gap on IFEval (+3.0 and +4.4). The ablation study further confirms that both the hybrid weighting (gradient attribution + Gaussian prior) and the triplet loss contribute meaningfully: removing the triplet loss drops Math from 80.7 to 79.0 and Code from 33.0 to 31.0, while removing the Gaussian prior reduces Reliability from 86.8 to 82.5.

## Relation to Prior Work

TI-DPO extends DPO's implicit reward mechanism to the token level, building on the gradient attribution framework of Ballout et al. (2024) for importance scoring and the 'lost-in-the-middle' observation of Liu et al. (2024b) to motivate a Gaussian prior that counteracts U-shaped attention bias. It departs from existing token-level DPO variants (TDPO, TIS-DPO, cDPO) that rely on probability-based proxies or uniform weighting, instead combining data-driven gradient attribution with a stabilizing prior in a convex mixture. The triplet loss, borrowed from metric learning, replaces DPO's binary good/bad comparison with a structured three-way objective that explicitly guides an intermediate anchor toward preferred and away from non-preferred responses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded (tighter loss bound, superior expected reward under KL constraints) and empirically validated approach to fine-grained preference alignment, with the added benefit of interpretable token-level weights that make bias sources detectable. However, the ~2× training overhead from the extra backward pass and the modest gap over GRPO (62.3 vs. 62.1 average) limit immediate practical adoption, and the authors acknowledge underperformance on holistic reasoning tasks (MMLU, GPQA, GSM8K) where sequence-level methods retain an edge. Open questions include whether the token-importance mechanism can be integrated with group-based optimizers like GRPO to close the reasoning gap, and whether the sparse-criticality assumption underlying Theorem 3 holds broadly across task types.
