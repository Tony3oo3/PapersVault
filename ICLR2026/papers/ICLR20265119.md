## Problem

While combining Chain-of-Thought (CoT) with reinforcement learning (RL) improves autoregressive text-to-image generation, the underlying interaction between CoT's exploration and RL's optimization remains unclear. Specifically, how these two mechanisms jointly govern token-level uncertainty and generation stability has not been systematically characterized, leaving no principled way to allocate optimization budget across tokens of differing confidence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265119/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt as input and produces a single RGB image as output. Generation proceeds autoregressively in a discrete VQ-VAE latent space: the model first emits a textual Chain-of-Thought reasoning trace, then sequentially decodes image tokens that are reconstructed into the final image. The backbone is Janus-Pro-7B, and training uses 6,786 text prompts from T2I-CompBench with a composite reward combining HPS human-preference scores, GroundingDINO object detection, and GIT VQA signals.

## Method

EG-GRPO modifies GRPO at the token level by partitioning each sequence's tokens into low-entropy (bottom 20%), mid, and high-entropy (top 50%) groups per modality (textual CoT and image tokens independently). Low-entropy tokens are masked out of the reward-driven advantage so that only the KL-to-reference regularizer acts on them, preserving confident knowledge; high-entropy tokens receive an additive entropy bonus λ·sg[H̄_t] on top of the group-relative advantage, encouraging structured exploration where uncertainty is highest. A batch-level calibration λ* scales the bonus to match the gradient mass saved on low-entropy tokens, keeping the overall update magnitude close to vanilla GRPO, and the bonus provably vanishes at GRPO equilibrium so stationary points are preserved. The key empirical insight driving this design is that final reward is strongly negatively correlated with both the mean and standard deviation of image-token entropy, and that textual CoT entropy causally governs downstream image quality.

## Results

On T2I-CompBench, EG-GRPO achieves state-of-the-art scores of 84.11 (Color), 60.88 (Shape), and 77.38 (Texture), surpassing the closest baseline T2I-R1 by 1.53, 2.21, and 0.44 points respectively. On WISE, EG-GRPO scores 49.00 (Culture), 56.00 (Spatio-temporal), and 46.33 (Science), improving over T2I-R1 on Culture (+1.00) and Science (+1.33) while matching or slightly exceeding it on Spatio-temporal (+0.50). The method also outperforms strong diffusion-based baselines such as FLUX.1-dev (74.07 Color) and Emu3 (75.44 Color) on all T2I-CompBench sub-categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its direct baseline T2I-R1 (the GRPO-finetuned Janus-Pro+CoT model), EG-GRPO wins on all six evaluation metrics, with the largest gains on Shape binding (+2.21) and Color (+1.53), confirming that entropy-guided token-level updates improve compositional generalization beyond standard GRPO. Compared to the strong diffusion-based FLUX.1-dev, EG-GRPO dominates on all T2I-CompBench sub-categories (e.g., 84.11 vs. 74.07 on Color) and on WISE Culture and Science, but trails FLUX.1-dev on Spatio-temporal reasoning (56.00 vs. 60.00), suggesting that autoregressive models still lag on certain spatial-temporal inference tasks. Against Emu3, another autoregressive baseline, EG-GRPO leads by a wide margin on every metric (e.g., 84.11 vs. 75.44 on Color, 49.00 vs. 34.00 on Culture). The remaining baselines (PixArt-α, SD-v1.5, Show-o, Janus-Pro-7B) are all substantially outperformed across both benchmarks, with EG-GRPO achieving the highest scores on 5 out of 6 metrics overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on T2I-R1 (Jiang et al., 2025), which introduced GRPO-based fine-tuning with collaborative semantic-level and token-level CoT for autoregressive image generation on the Janus-Pro backbone. The key departure is a token-level modification of the GRPO loss that reallocates gradient budget by Shannon entropy: low-entropy tokens receive KL-only updates while high-entropy tokens get an additive entropy bonus, drawing conceptually on the high-entropy-minority-token insight from Wang et al. (2025c) in the LLM reasoning literature. The systematic entropy-dynamics analysis (CoT expands exploration, RL contracts it; CoT entropy causally governs image quality) is novel for the T2I setting and motivates the loss design, whereas prior CoT+RL T2I works (Visual-CoG, ReasonGen-R1, PromptEnhancer) treated the interaction qualitatively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The entropy-based diagnostic framework gives practitioners a concrete, measurable handle on generation stability that goes beyond aggregate reward scores, and the finding that textual CoT entropy is a direct upstream bottleneck for image quality suggests that improving reasoning confidence may be more impactful than further scaling visual decoders. The method's negligible overhead (+1.35% step time) and equilibrium-preserving properties make it a drop-in refinement for existing GRPO pipelines. Open questions include whether the fixed percentile thresholds (bottom-20% / top-50%) generalize across model scales and prompt distributions, whether the approach transfers to diffusion-based T2I models, and whether the entropy bonus could be replaced by a learned, adaptive schedule.
