## Problem

Existing unsupervised depth completion methods rely on generic regularizers (e.g., local smoothness conditioned on image edges) that are fundamentally 2D interpolation priors and fail to capture 3D scene structure. This limits generalization to novel scenes and robustness to sparse inputs. The paper asks whether predicting regions occluded from the input view—regions that are inherently unobservable and thus require a 3D inductive bias—can serve as a stronger supervision signal for depth completion.

## Input / Output

At inference, ORCaS takes a single RGB image (640×480) and a sparse depth map (~1500 points from SLAM/VIO) and produces a dense egocentric depth map, with evaluation restricted to the 0.2–5.0 m range. During training, it additionally consumes an adjacent view (10 frames forward or backward, ~0.33 s apart) and the relative camera pose between views, but these are not required at test time. The sparse depth is processed following the KBNet pipeline, and the network predicts depth at 1/8 resolution before upsampling via a learned convex combination of neighboring predictions.

## Method

ORCaS encodes the RGB image and sparse depth into 2D features, then broadcasts them into a 3D volume by estimating a softmax probability distribution over D=8 discrete depth planes per pixel and distributing features across planes. The 3D volume is rigidly warped to an adjacent view using the relative pose, leaving 'empty' voxels where the input view was occluded. A Contextual eXtrapolation (ConteXt) block fills these empty voxels using a masked average-pool context descriptor over nearby co-visible features, 3D sinusoidal positional embeddings, and a linear projection conditioned on the occupancy mask. The ORCaS loss enforces L-p consistency between the predicted adjacent-view 3D features and stop-gradient encoded features of the actual adjacent view, trained in an alternating fashion (full network vs. ConteXt-only). At inference, the learned ConteXt positional bias modulates the input view's own 3D features before 3D-to-2D projection and depth decoding, providing the inductive bias that improves fidelity without requiring multi-view input.

## Results

On VOID1500, ORCaS achieves MAE 30.90, RMSE 80.12, iMAE 15.34, and iRMSE 37.19, improving over the previous SOTA (AugUndo) by 7.81% across all metrics. On NYUv2, ORCaS reaches MAE 86.50, RMSE 158.10, iMAE 18.27, and iRMSE 35.39, a 10.01% improvement over AugUndo. The average improvement over the best existing method across both benchmarks is 8.91%. ORCaS also demonstrates superior zero-shot transfer (12.1% on NYUv2, 19.2% on ScanNet) and robustness to extreme sparsity (31.2% on VOID150 with only 150 points).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ORCaS outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric across both VOID1500 and NYUv2. Against the closest competitor AugUndo (Wu et al., 2024), ORCaS improves MAE from 33.32 to 30.90 and RMSE from 85.67 to 80.12 on VOID1500, and from 96.73 to 86.50 (MAE) and 188.70 to 158.10 (RMSE) on NYUv2. The gap over KBNet (Wong & Soatto, 2021) is 22.87% on VOID1500 and 17.49% on NYUv2, while DesNet (Yan et al., 2023) is surpassed by 17.68% and 13.85% respectively. Older baselines show larger margins: VOICED (Wong et al., 2020) is outperformed by 62.34% on VOID1500, ScaffNet (Wong et al., 2021a) by 45.87%, and SS-S2D (Ma et al., 2019) by over 80% in MAE. On zero-shot transfer to ScanNet, ORCaS achieves MAE 68.86 vs. AugUndo's 82.53 (19.2% improvement), and on the extreme-sparse VOID150 setting (150 points), ORCaS reduces RMSE from 239.49 to 163.06 (31.9% improvement), confirming that the occluded-region inductive bias generalizes beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

ORCaS builds directly on the unsupervised depth completion lineage from VOICED, ScaffNet, and KBNet (Wong & Soatto, 2021), which established the framework of photometric reconstruction via rigid warping between adjacent views and calibrated backprojection of 2D features into 3D depth planes. The key departure is the introduction of an auxiliary supervision signal from occluded regions: rather than only reconstructing the input view from co-visible points in adjacent views (as in AugUndo and prior work), ORCaS additionally predicts the features of the adjacent view from the input view, filling 'empty' voxels via a ConteXt pooling mechanism. This repurposes the 3D volume representation (shared with MPI-style broadcasting) not for novel view synthesis but as a training-time inductive bias that, when transferred back to the input view at inference, improves depth fidelity without changing the input requirements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central insight—that forcing a network to predict 3D content it cannot observe (occluded regions) yields a stronger inductive bias than generic 2D regularizers—reframes how unsupervised depth completion should be supervised, potentially applicable to other single-view 3D reconstruction tasks. The 31.2% robustness gain on extremely sparse inputs (150 points) and 15.7% zero-shot transfer improvement suggest the learned 3D scene representation generalizes beyond the training distribution. Open questions include sensitivity to calibration noise (shown to degrade at ±30%), whether the static-scene assumption limits applicability to dynamic environments, and whether the ConteXt mechanism can be extended to handle larger parallax between views or multi-frame sequences.
