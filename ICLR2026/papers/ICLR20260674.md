## Problem

Fully implantable brain-machine interfaces require decoders that are simultaneously accurate, generalizable across subjects/tasks/time, and energy-efficient enough for strict on-chip power budgets. Existing ANN foundation models (POYO, NDT3) rely on large parameter counts and MAC-heavy operations that preclude deployment in fully implantable devices, while existing SNN decoders use architectures too simplistic to serve as cross-condition foundation models. The paper asks whether a lightweight, AC-only SNN can match ANN-level decoding accuracy while generalizing across neural variability in time, subjects, and tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260674/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw binary cortical spike trains (0/1 per channel per 4 ms timestep) from 96- or 192-channel microelectrode arrays, with no rate binning or smoothing required—spikes are fed directly as the input signal. Output is continuous 2D forelimb movement velocity, produced by five Leaky Integrator readout heads that convert discrete spiking activity into smooth kinematic predictions. The architecture is only 4 layers of LIF neurons (~600K parameters, reducible to ~150K after pruning), and all inter-synaptic operations at inference are accumulate-only (zero MACs).

## Method

RAT SNN integrates three mechanisms: (1) Recurrent-LIF neurons with heterogeneous synaptic time constants and intra-layer recurrent connections to capture multi-timescale neural dynamics; (2) a re-parameterized spike-driven self-attention block where Q/K/V are computed via parallel Rep-Conv (multi-kernel 1D conv) and Rep-FC branches with batch normalization during training, then fused into single linear layers at inference to preserve AC-only operations—leveraging the binary spike nature to reduce attention complexity from O(n²d) to O(nd²); (3) a stepwise cross-condition pretraining pipeline (cross-condition → cross-session → single-session → lightweight finetuning with AUB regularization and iterative pruning) that uses subject-specific BN layers to accommodate distributional shifts. The re-parameterization is the key enabler: it allows BN to stabilize training and improve convergence (~3× fewer epochs, +14 R²) while the fused inference graph contains no multiply-accumulate operations, making the model suitable for fully implantable hardware.

## Results

On the NHP dataset (the primary benchmark), RAT SNN-CC achieves R²×100 of 74.06±0.42 (Monkey I) and 70.40±0.68 (Monkey L), averaging 72.23±0.38, outperforming the best SNN baseline bigRSNN-CS (69.79±0.22) and the best ANN baseline POYO-CS (70.31±0.18). The pruned variant RAT SNN-CC-P retains 71.96±0.61 average R² while reducing effective ACs from 65,307 to 21,020 and parameters from 600K to 150K. On the NLB_MAZE dataset, RAT SNN-CC reaches 91.16±0.19, surpassing POYO-SS (94.70) only marginally below but exceeding bigRSNN-SS (90.38) and all other SNN baselines. Cross-condition generalization to an entirely unseen subject performing an unseen task (RTT-joystick) shows RAT SNN-CC converges in ~107 epochs vs. ~304 for from-scratch training, with higher final R² (76.29 vs. 74.84 on Monkey T).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAT SNN-CC outperforms all SNN baselines: it exceeds bigRSNN-CS (the strongest SNN baseline) by 2.44 R² points on average (72.23 vs. 69.79) and SNN3d by 8.93 points. Against ANN baselines, RAT SNN-CC surpasses POYO-CS (72.23 vs. 70.31) and AEGRU (72.23 vs. 69.50), while using only 0 MACs and 65,307 ACs versus POYO's 1,730,507 MACs + 810,339 ACs. The pruned variant RAT SNN-CC-P (150K params, 21,020 ACs) retains 99.6% of the full model's accuracy while using 68% fewer ACs than bigRSNN (42,003) and 1/8 the parameter count (150K vs. 1.2M). On NLB_MAZE, RAT SNN-CC (91.16) beats bigRSNN-SS (90.38) and AutoLFADS (90.62), and on NLB_RTT it reaches 78.70, exceeding bigRSNN-CS (78.61) and POYO-1 (73.78). Against Spikachu (NeurIPS 2025), RAT SNN-CC-P achieves R² of 0.9102 vs. 0.78 on NLB_MAZE and 0.7808 vs. 0.56 on NLB_RTT, while consuming ~4,352 pJ vs. Spikachu's 4,750 μJ (over 1000× less energy) due to maintaining pure AC-only operations without an ANN harmonizer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) spike-driven transformer architectures (Yao et al., 2023a,b) that exploit binary spike representations to reduce attention to AC-only operations, and (2) the re-parameterization paradigm from RepVGG (Ding et al., 2021) that fuses multi-branch structures into single linear layers at inference. The key conceptual departure is transplanting the ANN foundation-model paradigm (POYO, NDT3) — cross-condition pretraining followed by rapid finetuning — into a lightweight SNN setting, replacing POYO's UnitEmbed tokenizer with subject-specific batch normalization that integrates seamlessly into the re-parameterized linear operations. The training framework builds on Stork (Zenke & Ganguli, 2018) but modifies the propagation scheme to reduce latency, and the stepwise pipeline (cross-condition → cross-session → single-session → lightweight finetuning with pruning) is novel in its systematic integration of neural variability at multiple granularities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 600K-parameter SNN with strictly AC-only synaptic operations can match or exceed 1.9M+ parameter ANN foundation models (POYO) in decoding accuracy while consuming a fraction of the energy, which is critical for fully implantable BMIs where external power and compute are unavailable. The controlled experiment (Section D) showing that cross-condition diversity — not merely data volume — drives the generalization gain is a useful methodological contribution. However, the evaluation is limited to monkey M1/PMd/S1 recordings for 2D velocity prediction across ~6 subjects and 3 tasks; open questions include whether the approach scales to human data, higher-dimensional kinematics, or longer deployment horizons where representational drift may exceed what cross-condition pretraining can absorb. The reliance on a single GPU for all training and the NeuroBench-standardized evaluation strengthen reproducibility but also constrain the scope of claims.
