## Problem

Physics-informed machine learning methods (PINNs, neural operators, PIKL) fit models to competing data and physics losses, creating ambiguity in how to measure model quality and select hyperparameters. Existing metrics can indicate strong fits even when the model has silently failed, and without sufficient validation data (common in scientific settings), practitioners cannot reliably diagnose misspecification. The paper asks whether a single, uncertainty-aware criterion can resolve this multi-objective bottleneck.

## Input / Output

The method consumes noisy pointwise observations of the PDE solution, boundary/physics constraint values (which may be zero to enforce Af=0 or nonzero for corrupted data), the governing linear differential operator with its boundary conditions, and a kernel function with tunable hyperparameters (bandwidth, anisotropy angle, scale). It outputs a posterior mean and variance for the PDE solution at any query point, the scalar PILE score for model comparison, and optimal hyperparameter settings. Unusually, a 'data-free' variant of PILE requires only the operator and kernel (no observations) and reduces to a Fredholm determinant, enabling a-priori kernel selection before any data is collected.

## Method

The key insight is that the physics-informed kernel ridge regression (PIKL) objective has an exact GP interpretation: the joint process (f, Af) follows a GP with a block-structured covariance kernel built from the base kernel k and its operator-pushed variants (Id⊗A)k and (A⊗A)k. The PILE score is defined as the normalized negative log-marginal likelihood (Bayes free energy) of this joint GP conditioned on both data observations and physics constraint observations, yielding a single scalar that simultaneously penalizes poor data fit, physics violation, and overfitting. A representer theorem (Theorem A.4) shows the minimizer lies in the span of kernel evaluations at data and quadrature points, making the problem finite-dimensional. In the data-free limit (no observations, only physics constraints), the PILE score converges to the log Fredholm determinant of an integral operator built from (A⊗A)k, providing a data-independent measure of how well a kernel is adapted to a given PDE. Hyperparameter optimization (bandwidth, regularization weights ρ and γ, and even kernel family parameters θ and s) is performed by minimizing PILE via empirical Bayes.

## Results

On the 2D Poisson equation (Ω=(−1,1)², n=m=13, noise σ=1), PILE minimization over bandwidth h, physics regularization ρ, and data regularization γ yields a solution that matches the target function despite high noise and limited samples; PILE divergence accurately flags overfitting at small ρ and γ. On the 1D convection PDE (n=1000, m=400), the data-free PILE score (Fredholm determinant) identifies an optimal anisotropic RBF kernel at θ*≈1.41, s*≈0.5, producing excellent simultaneous physics and data fit, whereas no isotropic RBF bandwidth achieves both (data loss is small only at h≈0.1 while physics loss blows up for h≤0.15). The PILE score successfully diagnoses the isotropic RBF failure by selecting the oversmoothed 'all-zero' solution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include a head-to-head numerical comparison against external PIML baselines (e.g., PINNs, Neural ODEs, or other kernel methods); the `compares_to` list is empty. Within its own framework, the key comparison is between isotropic and anisotropic RBF kernels on the convection PDE benchmark from Krishnapriyan et al. (2021): the isotropic RBF kernel fails at all bandwidths (no single h balances data and physics loss), while the PILE-optimal anisotropic kernel (θ*≈1.41, s*≈0.5) achieves strong fits on both objectives. A second internal comparison shows that PILE-selected hyperparameters (bandwidth, ρ, γ) outperform suboptimal choices, with PILE divergence serving as a reliable overfitting diagnostic. The Fredholm determinant (data-free PILE) is compared across the anisotropic kernel family, demonstrating that kernel selection prior to data acquisition can avoid the failure mode entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Physics-Informed Kernel Learning (PIKL) framework of Doumèche et al. (2024) and Pförtner et al. (2024), which cast linear PDE solving as a kernel ridge regression problem within a Gaussian process prior. The key departure is the introduction of the PILE score—a physics-informed analogue of the standard GP Bayes free energy (marginal likelihood) from Rasmussen & Williams (2006)—as a unified model selection criterion that replaces the ambiguous multi-objective balancing of data and physics losses. A further conceptual contribution is the connection between the data-free limit of PILE and the Fredholm determinant of an associated integral operator, an object previously encountered mainly in random matrix theory and determinantal point processes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PILE score addresses a genuine practical pain point in PIML: the lack of a principled, single-number criterion for hyperparameter selection when data fidelity and PDE residual are in tension. The data-free Fredholm determinant result offers a potentially useful a priori kernel-selection tool before any data is collected. However, the current scope is limited to linear PDEs within a GP/kernel framework, with cubic-time computation and empirical validation on only two small-scale problems (2D Poisson, 1D convection). Open questions include extension to nonlinear PDEs (sketched via Fréchet linearization), to neural network architectures (via NTK or Laplace approximations), and to larger-scale problems where the determinant computation becomes prohibitive.
