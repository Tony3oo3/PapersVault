## Problem

Existing reasoning-augmented image generation methods either restrict reasoning to a single modality (refining text instructions or iterating on images) or require carefully curated reasoning data and model fine-tuning to enable cross-modal reasoning. This leaves a gap for a unified, training-free approach that can jointly reason over both image and text representations at test time to improve generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260742/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MILR takes a natural-language text instruction as input and produces a pixel-level RGB image as output. The instruction is consumed by a unified multimodal understanding and generation (MUG) model (Janus-Pro), which autoregressively generates reasoning text tokens followed by discrete image tokens from a pre-trained discrete VAE; the image tokens are then decoded into pixels. The method operates entirely at test time with no parameter updates, requiring only a single A100 GPU and approximately 32 seconds per sample with an early-stop strategy.

## Method

MILR performs joint image-text reasoning in a unified latent vector space formed by the intermediate Transformer layer outputs, which are modality-agnostic and shared between text and image token positions. Rather than searching over discrete tokens, it optimizes continuous latent representations using the REINFORCE policy gradient algorithm, where the reward is computed by an image-quality critic scoring the compatibility between the generated image and the instruction. Gradients are back-propagated only to the latent vectors (the last Transformer layer outputs feeding into modal-specific decoding heads) without altering any model parameters, making it a purely test-time method. To balance exploration and efficiency, MILR optimizes only the first 20% of text latents and the first 2% of image latents (prefix tokens that govern global structure), then completes generation via standard autoregressive decoding conditioned on the optimized prefixes. An optional ℓ₂ regularizer constrains optimized latents to stay near their initial values, mitigating reward hacking and semantic collapse.

## Results

On GenEval, Janus-Pro-7B+MILR achieves an overall score of 0.95, matching the best training-based model (Flow-GRPO, 0.95) and outperforming the best test-time-scaling methods (ReflectionFlow, PARM, Best-of-N, all 0.91) by 4.4%. On T2I-CompBench, MILR reaches 0.5325 overall, surpassing the strongest training-based baseline T2I-R1 (0.5281). On the knowledge-intensive WISE benchmark, MILR attains 0.63, improving over the base Janus-Pro-7B by 80% and over T2I-R1 by 16.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MILR (0.95) ties with Flow-GRPO (0.95) on GenEval overall, but requires zero training data and no parameter updates, whereas Flow-GRPO needs ~100 GPU-hours on 24 A800s. Against test-time reasoning baselines, MILR outperforms ReflectionFlow (0.91), PARM (0.91), and Best-of-N (0.91) by 4.4%, while also being faster (32.5s vs 52.1s per sample for Best-of-N). Against frontier non-reasoning models, MILR surpasses GPT-4o (0.85) by 12%, BAGEL (0.82) by 16%, and SD3-Medium (0.74) by 28%. On T2I-CompBench, MILR (0.5325) narrowly beats T2I-R1 (0.5281), the strongest training-based reasoning model. On WISE, MILR (0.63) leads T2I-R1 (0.54) by 16.7% and the base model (0.35) by 80%, demonstrating the advantage of joint latent reasoning for knowledge-intensive prompts. Ablations confirm that joint text+image latent optimization is essential: removing either modality drops WISE to 0.61 (w/o image) or 0.56 (w/o text).

## Relation to Prior Work

MILR builds on the Janus-Pro unified multimodal understanding and generation (MUG) architecture and the REINFORCE policy gradient algorithm, but departs fundamentally from prior reasoning-augmented image generation in its search space. Previous methods either refine text instructions (language-space reasoning), iterate on pixel-level images (image-space reasoning), or require curated reasoning data and model fine-tuning (e.g., GoT-R1, T2I-R1, Flow-GRPO). MILR instead searches over continuous intermediate latent vectors shared by both text and image tokens, performing joint cross-modal reasoning entirely at test time without altering any model parameters—a conceptual shift from explicit token-level or pixel-level reasoning to implicit latent-space optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MILR demonstrates that a simple test-time REINFORCE loop over latent representations can match or exceed training-based RL methods (e.g., Flow-GRPO requiring ~2K A800 GPU-hours) with zero training, offering a practical and compute-efficient path to reasoning-augmented image generation. The 80% improvement on knowledge-intensive WISE and the qualitative evidence of temporal/cultural reasoning suggest latent-space optimization can unlock capabilities not accessible through instruction refinement alone. Key open questions include whether the approach transfers to diffusion-based MUG architectures, how to design robust domain-agnostic reward models (the oracle reward still outperforms all non-oracle alternatives), and how to mitigate reward hacking and reasoning collapse without sacrificing quality.
