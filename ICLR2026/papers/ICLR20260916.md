## Problem

For neural networks with general polynomial activations, it was unknown whether the parametrization map has finite or infinite fibers (i.e., whether functions are identifiable up to finitely many parameter symmetries), and the singular locus of the neuromanifold was only formally characterized for linear or monomial activations. This paper resolves both questions for generic polynomials of large degree, extending prior monomial-specific results to the broad class of generic polynomial activations and providing a geometric explanation for the sparsity bias observed in MLPs but not in CNNs.

## Input / Output

The method takes an MLP or CNN architecture (specified by layer dimensions, filter sizes, and strides) together with a generic univariate polynomial activation of sufficiently large degree r (with explicit bounds like r > (6m)^{2(L-1)^{L-1}} for MLPs) and weight parameters. It produces: (1) a proof that generic fibers are finite (MLPs) or singletons (CNNs), implying the neuromanifold dimension equals the parameter count; (2) a characterization of singular points as those parametrized by sparse subnetworks satisfying specific combinatorial conditions; and (3) a critical exposedness result showing subnetworks of MLPs contain loss critical points for a dense set of targets, while no such set exists for CNNs. The finite-dimensionality of the ambient space of polynomial maps is essential and unique to polynomial activations.

## Method

The core technique leverages algebraic geometry—specifically the Zariski topology, the fiber-dimension theorem, and toric geometry—to analyze the parametrization map φ from weight space to the neuromanifold. For identifiability, the authors select a sparse polynomial activation σ(x) = Σ x^{β_i} with super-exponentially growing exponents, which decomposes the network output into a sum of monomial-activation MLPs (Lemma B.1); the fiber is then contained in the intersection of known monomial fibers, and toric geometry (via Smith normal form of a Vandermonde matrix) proves only finitely many rescalings survive. For singularities, they show that at a subnetwork parameter, the chain rule produces tangent vectors whose span exceeds the variety dimension, using the dominance of the activation composition and duality in algebraic geometry. For exposedness, they recast criticality as orthogonality of f_W − u to the Jacobian image, showing the union of orthogonal complements over strict subnetworks has full dimension for MLPs but is dimension-deficient for CNNs due to the regularity and finite-fiber properties of the convolutional parametrization.

## Results

The paper establishes that for generic polynomial activations of sufficiently large degree r > (6m)^(2(L-1)^(L-1)) (where m = 2·max{d₁,…,d_{L-1}}), the generic fiber of the MLP parametrization is finite, resolving the dimension conjecture of Kileel et al. (2019): dim(neuromanifold) = dim(parameter space) = Σᵢ dᵢ·dᵢ₋₁. For CNNs, a stronger result holds: the parametrization is generically one-to-one (Theorem 4.4), meaning almost all functions correspond to a unique parameter choice. The paper also provides a complete characterization of CNN singularities (Theorem 4.6) and proves that MLP subnetworks are critically exposed (Theorem 4.3), while CNN subnetworks are not (Proposition 4.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare methods in the standard empirical sense, but provides a structural comparison between MLPs and CNNs that is central to its contribution. For identifiability, MLPs have generically finite fibers (discrete symmetries from neuron permutations), while CNNs are generically one-to-one — a strictly stronger property. For singularities, both architectures exhibit subnetwork-induced singular points, but CNNs admit a complete characterization (all singularities are nodal/self-intersection type), whereas for MLPs the characterization is only partial. For optimization bias, the key contrast is that MLP subnetworks are critically exposed (Theorem 4.3), meaning they contain critical points of the MSE loss with positive probability over datasets, while CNN subnetworks are not critically exposed (Proposition 4.5). This geometric distinction provides a theoretical explanation for the empirically observed sparsity bias in MLPs but not in pure single-channel CNNs, consistent with findings by Blumenfeld et al. (2020).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neuroalgebraic geometry program by generalizing identifiability and singularity results from monomial activations (Finkel et al., 2024; Usevich et al., 2025; Shahverdi et al., 2025) to generic polynomial activations of large degree, resolving the dimension conjecture of Kileel et al. (2019) in full generality. It builds on the algebraic-geometric framework of Trager et al. (2020) for linear networks and the broader neuroalgebraic geometry invitation (Marchetti et al., 2025), introducing the new notion of 'critically exposed' parameter sets as a geometric formalization of sparsity bias. The paper departs from prior identifiability work (Tanh, Sigmoid, ReLU) by working in the polynomial setting where the neuromanifold lives in a finite-dimensional ambient space, enabling the use of Zariski topology and toric geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a geometric explanation for the empirically observed sparsity bias (lottery ticket hypothesis) by showing that MLP subnetworks are critically exposed (attracting gradient dynamics) while CNN subnetworks are not, aligning with empirical findings that CNNs recover from sparse initializations. Key open questions remain: whether all MLP singularities are subnetworks (only partially shown), the type of critical points at subnetworks (minima vs. saddles), and extension beyond polynomial activations to ReLU or Tanh via approximation arguments. The results are highly technical and require generic polynomial activations of large degree, limiting immediate practical applicability but establishing foundational geometric facts for the neuroalgebraic geometry program.
