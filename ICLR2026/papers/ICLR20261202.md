## Problem

Existing protein generative models treat equilibrium ensemble generation (thermodynamics) and conformational trajectory simulation (dynamics) as separate tasks, and current dynamics models rely on very small timesteps while generalizing poorly beyond their training data. No unified foundation model exists that can simultaneously sample thermodynamically consistent ensembles and propagate conformational transitions across multiple timescales (nanoseconds to microseconds) in a single framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261202/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes protein sequences and conformations discretized into ESM3 structure tokens (4,096 tokens per residue via VQ-VAE), along with an initial conformation for dynamics generation or a pair of endpoint conformations for inpainting. It produces i.i.d. equilibrium ensembles, temporally coherent trajectories at user-specified timesteps (any integer 1–100 ns at inference), and fine-grained 10 ns sub-trajectories recovered from coarse 100 ns intervals. All outputs are discrete token sequences decoded back to 3D coordinates by a paired VQ-VAE decoder.

## Method

ProTDyn is a 1.4B-parameter autoregressive transformer (24 PreLN blocks with RoPE and SwiGLU) built on ESM3's frozen sequence/structure embedding heads, trained with a weighted multi-task cross-entropy loss across three heads: thermodynamics (P(c|s)), multi-timescale dynamics (P(C|s) with δt ∈ {1,10,100} ns and memory kernel M=10), and dynamics inpainting (P(C|c⁰, c^{Mδt}, s)). The key architectural innovation is a dual rotary positional embedding scheme: a residue-level embedding encodes sequence position for both sequence and structure tokens, while an additional temporal-level embedding (applied only to structure tokens) encodes the time offset of each conformational frame, enabling the model to reason about transitions at arbitrary timesteps. Training combines single-structure data (542K AlphaFold Swiss-Prot pairs) with equilibrium MD trajectories (mdCath, Octapeptides, CATH1/2, MEGAsim) so that thermodynamic and dynamic signals are learned jointly. The paper demonstrates that the thermodynamic head stabilizes dynamic propagation—removing it causes rapid structural divergence—while the dynamics head enriches ensemble diversity, making the two objectives mutually reinforcing within one architecture.

## Results

On the CATH1 test set (50 proteins), ProTDyn-Thermodynamics achieves JSD of 0.023 (Rg), 0.012 (RMSD), and 0.155 (TICA), substantially outperforming the BioEmu baseline (0.082, 0.137, 0.293 respectively). The Dynamics (100 ns) head with inpainting achieves JSD of 0.021 (stationary) and 0.055 (transition) in MSM evaluation, matching the quality of 50 μs of reference MD (0.018, 0.049), while the Dynamics (10 ns) head matches 25 μs MD quality (0.040, 0.117). On 10 unseen octapeptides, ProTDyn-Thermodynamics achieves JSD of 0.034/0.020/0.207, closely matching BioEmu (0.031/0.020/0.134) despite those peptides being in BioEmu's training set but not ProTDyn's.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is BioEmu (Lewis et al., 2025), the most recent large-scale protein ensemble generator. ProTDyn-Thermodynamics decisively outperforms BioEmu on all three distributional similarity metrics on CATH1: Rg JSD 0.023 vs. 0.082 (3.6× lower), RMSD JSD 0.012 vs. 0.137 (11× lower), and TICA JSD 0.155 vs. 0.293 (1.9× lower). On the unseen octapeptide test set, ProTDyn-Thermodynamics is competitive with BioEmu (0.034 vs. 0.031 Rg; 0.020 vs. 0.020 RMSD; 0.207 vs. 0.134 TICA), with BioEmu holding a slight edge on TICA—expected since those peptides were in BioEmu's training data but not ProTDyn's. The paper also includes an internal ablation (dynamics-only model without thermodynamic supervision) that degrades sharply (Rg 0.077, RMSD 0.142, TICA 0.315), confirming that the unified multi-task design is critical and that thermodynamic signals provide complementary information for dynamic inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProTDyn builds directly on the ESM3 protein language model architecture (Hayes et al., 2025), adopting its pretrained sequence and structure embedding heads and extending the transformer backbone with a dual rotary positional embedding scheme (residue-level + temporal-level) to encode multi-timescale dynamics. Conceptually, it unifies two previously separate research threads: thermodynamic ensemble generation (Boltzmann generators, BioEmu, Str2Str) and dynamic trajectory prediction (Jing et al., Raja et al., Fu et al.), which prior work has treated as distinct tasks. The key departure is the multi-task autoregressive formulation that jointly learns equilibrium distributions, multi-timescale transition densities, and coarse-to-fine trajectory inpainting within a single 1.4B-parameter model, rather than training specialized models for each sub-task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying thermodynamics and dynamics in one generative framework, ProTDyn provides a practical alternative to expensive MD simulations for both equilibrium ensemble sampling and multi-timescale (ns–μs) trajectory generation, with demonstrated generalization to unseen proteins. The finding that removing thermodynamic supervision causes the dynamics head to diverge rapidly underscores a previously underappreciated coupling between the two tasks. Open questions remain around principled memory-kernel design, rigorous transition path sampling, and enforcing statistical-mechanical constraints like detailed balance via the model's exact likelihood evaluation—capabilities the authors identify but do not yet exploit.
