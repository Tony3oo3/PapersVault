## Problem

A central question in deep learning theory is whether gradient-based training on overparameterized models induces lower-dimensional dynamics on structural variables (e.g., product of weight matrices or path-lifting). Existing implicit bias analyses rely on strong conditions such as commuting parametrizations (Li et al., 2022) that are rarely satisfied in practice, leaving a gap in understanding when the lifted dynamics are genuinely intrinsic Riemannian gradient flows for general architectures. This paper characterizes precisely when such intrinsic dynamics hold, providing both necessary and sufficient conditions that are verifiable for standard network architectures.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a parameter vector θ ∈ ℝ^D, a loss ℓ factorized as f∘φ for an architecture-dependent lifting φ: ℝ^D → ℝ^d, and an initialization θ₀, and produces a Riemannian metric K_{θ₀}(z) on the lifted space such that the trajectory z(t)=φ(θ(t)) satisfies the intrinsic ODE ż = −K_{θ₀}(z)∇f(z). Crucially, K_{θ₀} depends only on z and the initialization (not on the full trajectory θ(t)), and the conservation laws h(θ) that enable this reduction are architecture-dependent polynomials independent of the dataset or loss function. For ReLU networks the lifted dimension d' ≈ D−m (m hidden neurons), while for linear networks with φ_Lin the dimension reduction is much sharper (d' ≪ D).

## Method

The paper introduces a hierarchy of three properties—Intrinsic Recoverability ⊃ Intrinsic Metric ⊃ Intrinsic Dynamic—and proves that the Frobenius property (a Lie-algebraic condition slightly weaker than involutivity) is quasi-equivalent to intrinsic recoverability via a kernel-inclusion criterion (Theorem 2.14, Proposition 2.21). For general DAG ReLU networks, the path-lifting φ_ReLU is shown to satisfy the Frobenius property on a dense open set (Theorem 3.1), yielding intrinsic dynamics for essentially all initializations. For linear networks, the paper generalizes the classical balanced condition to a 'relaxed balanced' condition (U_{i+1}^T U_{i+1} − U_i U_i^T = λ_i Id) and proves it is both sufficient (Theorems 3.8, 3.11) and, in certain configurations, necessary (Theorem 3.9) for the intrinsic metric property. Finally, the analysis extends to the infinite-depth limit (linear neural ODEs), where a closed-form integral expression for the intrinsic metric is derived under relaxed balanced initialization (Theorem 3.13).

## Results

The paper establishes that for general DAG ReLU networks of arbitrary depth, the path-lifting parametrization φ_ReLU satisfies the Frobenius property (Theorem 3.1), guaranteeing the intrinsic recoverability property (and hence an intrinsic Riemannian gradient flow) for a dense set of initializations (Corollary 3.3). For linear networks, the paper introduces the relaxed balanced condition (Definition 3.6), generalizing the classical balanced condition, and proves it is both sufficient (Theorems 3.8, 3.11) and, in certain configurations, necessary (Theorem 3.9) for the intrinsic metric property. For the infinite-depth limit (linear neural ODEs), a closed-form intrinsic metric is derived (Theorem 3.13), with the metric depending explicitly on the initialization through a scalar function λ(s).

| Network type | φ_Lin | φ_ReLU |
|---|---|---|
| Linear network | IMP: only for relaxed balanced θ₀; d′ ≪ D | IMP: for all θ₀; d′ = D−k ≈ D |
| DAG ReLU | N/A | IMP: for all θ₀; d′ = D−k ≈ D |

*IMP = intrinsic metric property; d′ = dimension of the manifold of φ(θ); k = # hidden neurons*

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines; comparisons are made against prior theoretical results. The paper generalizes Arora et al. (2018, 2019) and Bah et al. (2022), who established the intrinsic metric property only under the strict balanced condition (λ=0), to the broader relaxed balanced condition (λ≠0 allowed), and further proves necessity of this condition for 2-layer networks (Theorem 3.9). It relaxes the commuting condition of Li et al. (2022) to the weaker Frobenius property (Proposition 2.21), which is satisfied by the path-lifting for all DAG ReLU networks. The result for 2-layer linear networks (Theorem 3.8) subsumes the special case r=min(n,m) treated by Dominé et al. (2025). For ReLU networks, the paper extends the two-layer involutivity result of Marcotte et al. (2023) to arbitrary-depth DAG architectures via the Frobenius property (Theorem 3.1), and proves completeness of the m canonical conservation laws (Corollary 3.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conservation-law framework of Marcotte et al. (2023) by replacing the involutivity condition with the weaker Frobenius property, and by establishing a clean hierarchy (Intrinsic Recoverability → Intrinsic Metric → Intrinsic Dynamic) with a kernel-inclusion criterion (Theorem 2.14) as a necessary condition. It generalizes the balanced-initialization results of Arora et al. (2018, 2019) and Bah et al. (2022) to the broader 'relaxed balanced' class, and extends the path-lifting parametrization of Gonon et al. (2024) from two-layer to arbitrary DAG ReLU networks. The Frobenius property is positioned as a relaxation of the commuting condition required by Li et al. (2022) for mirror-flow representations, trading the stronger mirror-flow conclusion for applicability to far more practical architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first general proof that path-lifting for arbitrary DAG ReLU networks (including skip connections and mixed activations) admits an intrinsic Riemannian dynamic for a dense set of initializations, closing a gap that was previously known only for two-layer networks. The necessity result (Theorem 3.9) showing that relaxed balanced initializations are the *only* ones yielding the intrinsic metric property in certain linear network configurations gives a sharp boundary for when geometric analyses of implicit bias are valid. Open questions include the case r > max(n,m) for two-layer linear networks, whether warped mirror flows (Azulay et al., 2021) can be established under the Frobenius property, and extending the closed-form infinite-depth metric to non-balanced initializations or nonlinear activations.
