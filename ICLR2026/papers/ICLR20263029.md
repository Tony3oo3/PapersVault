## Problem

Grokking—delayed generalization after training loss converges—has been studied almost exclusively on small models trained for thousands of epochs on clean algorithmic data, leaving it unclear whether this phenomenon persists in practical LLM pretraining (near single-pass, heterogeneous web-scale data, diverse downstream tasks). Moreover, monitoring generalization during pretraining is prohibitively expensive because it requires instruction tuning and benchmark evaluation at every checkpoint, and conventional training/validation loss fails to track the memorization-to-generalization transition once loss plateaus.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only pretraining-side information: expert routing weights extracted from MoE checkpoints at each layer for training samples drawn from the pretraining corpus, requiring no held-out validation set, no test data, and no instruction tuning. It produces two scalar monitoring signals per checkpoint—pairwise pathway edit distance (Levenshtein distance over comma-separated expert-index strings concatenated across layers) and sample-wise pathway consistency (1 minus normalized average cosine similarity of weighted expert embeddings between consecutive layers)—which together serve as a zero-cost, finetuning-free proxy for downstream generalization performance.

## Method

The paper analyzes how routing pathways (the ordered sequence of top-weighted experts selected at each MoE layer for a given input) evolve during pretraining of the 7B OLMoE model. After identifying when individual training samples become memorized (loss below threshold ε and stable within δ of final value), it tracks two pathway-complexity metrics: (1) average pairwise Levenshtein edit distance between samples' pathway strings, which decreases post-memorization as semantically related inputs converge to shared expert routes, and (2) pathway consistency, computed as 1 minus the normalized mean cosine similarity between consecutive-layer weighted expert embeddings, which increases as cross-layer routing transitions become smoother. These metrics show correlations of |r| > 0.92 with benchmark accuracy across math, code, commonsense, and domain-specific QA, far outperforming training or validation loss. A theoretical analysis under the NTK regime for a one-layer MoE with fixed routing establishes a generalization bound whose variance term depends on the effective dimension of the routing kernel, linking structured (low-complexity) pathways to tighter bounds and explaining why pathway simplification drives generalization.

## Results

The two proposed pathway complexity metrics show exceptionally strong correlations with downstream test accuracy across all four domains: pathway consistency (sample-wise) achieves Pearson correlations of 0.92–0.99, and pathway edit distance (pairwise) achieves correlations of −0.93 to −0.98. In stark contrast, conventional training loss correlates weakly and inconsistently (e.g., Pearson −0.04 on Math, 0.64 on Commonsense), and validation loss shows even more erratic behavior (ranging from −0.58 to 0.55). The paper further demonstrates that grokking in LLM pretraining is local and asynchronous: different data domains memorize at different training steps, and the delay between memorization and generalization scales with data difficulty (math/code require memorizing many more samples before generalization leaps compared to commonsense/domain-specific QA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other grokking-monitoring methods but instead benchmarks its pathway metrics against the conventional metrics practitioners rely on: raw training loss, moving-average training loss, and validation loss. Pathway consistency dominates all baselines on every domain, achieving |r| ≥ 0.92 in all settings, while training loss fails catastrophically on Math (Pearson −0.04) and Code (−0.07), and validation loss is unreliable (e.g., −0.58 on Commonsense, −0.33 on Domain-Specific). Moving-average training loss performs moderately on Domain-Specific (0.87) but still lags pathway consistency (0.99) and shows the wrong sign on Code (positive 0.25 when a negative correlation with loss would be expected). The authors also validate generality by replicating the findings on a 55M-parameter nanoMoE trained from scratch, where pathway metrics again show |r| ≈ 0.8–1.0 with zero-shot benchmark accuracy, confirming the results are not an artifact of OLMoE's specific architecture or scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the grokking literature (Power et al. 2022; Nanda et al. 2023; Liu et al. 2022a; Merrill et al. 2023) from small models trained for thousands of epochs on clean algorithmic data to practical 7B MoE LLMs pretrained near single-pass on heterogeneous, cross-domain web corpora. Where prior mechanistic explanations invoked structured representations, subnetwork sparsity, or decision-boundary smoothing, this paper identifies MoE routing pathway dynamics—specifically pathway edit distance and cross-layer consistency—as the mechanism underlying the memorization-to-generalization transition. The theoretical grounding via a routing-kernel NTK generalization bound is novel, connecting pathway complexity to effective dimension in a way not present in earlier grokking analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two pathway metrics offer a near-zero-cost, finetuning-free tool to monitor generalization during pretraining, which is practically valuable for LLM developers who cannot afford repeated instruction tuning and benchmark evaluation at every checkpoint. The finding that grokking is local and asynchronous across data groups (rather than global and synchronous) reframes how we think about capability emergence in LLMs and suggests that data scheduling and curriculum design may interact with grokking timing in non-trivial ways. Key limitations include the study being confined to MoE architectures (dense models lack the discrete routing structure), reliance on a single main model (OLMoE) with only a small-scale nanoMoE validation, and the theoretical analysis covering only a one-layer MoE under NTK assumptions. Extending pathway-based monitoring to dense transformers via 'virtual pathways' is identified as future work.
