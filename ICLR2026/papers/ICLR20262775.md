## Problem

Standard LoRA optimizes its factor matrices (A, B) with Euclidean optimizers that are misaligned with the low-rank constraint: the same adapter ΔW = ABᵀ admits infinitely many factorizations, making training reparameterization-dependent and causing unbalanced learning, fragile hyperparameter sensitivity, and path-dependent solutions. Existing Riemannian LoRA variants either port Adam-like mechanics with ad hoc parameterization choices or use plain SGD on the manifold, failing to inherit the geometry-aligned normalization that makes Muon effective for full-rank matrices.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pretrained weight matrix W ∈ ℝ^{m×n} (from Llama 3-8B MLP/attention layers or Stable Diffusion 2 attention layers) and a task dataset, and produces a rank-r adapter ΔW represented intrinsically as a point on the fixed-rank manifold M_r rather than as a pair of factor matrices. For LLM fine-tuning, the output is a text response evaluated on 8 commonsense reasoning benchmarks; for subject-driven generation, it is a set of images conditioned on reference photos and text prompts, scored by DINO image similarity and CLIP text similarity. The initialization phase requires only 2(q+1) extra backward passes (typically 4) to compute a randomized SVD of the loss gradient, adding ~0.25% wall-clock overhead to the full fine-tuning run.

## Method

Riemannion generalizes Muon to the fixed-rank manifold M_r by replacing the full orthogonalization with a rank-2r orthogonalization (Ortho_r) that preserves the column and row spaces of the tangent-space momentum while setting its top singular values to 1, followed by projection back onto the tangent plane and retraction via truncated SVD. The optimizer uses heavy-ball momentum transported between tangent spaces via projection, with per-step complexity O((m+n)r² + r³) and the same number of backward passes as vanilla LoRA. A Locally Optimal Initialization (LOI) scheme places the starting adapter at the point on M_r whose tangent space maximally aligns with the Euclidean gradient, computed via a randomized SVD of ∇_W L(W) obtained through a single-backward-pass gradient-times-matrix trick that avoids forming the full gradient. All geometric operations (tangent-space projection, vector transport, retraction) are assembled via automatic differentiation over compact QR/SVD factorizations, yielding a reparameterization-invariant update with no prominent overhead at small ranks.

## Results

On the Commonsense Reasoning benchmark (8 tasks, Llama 3-8B, LoRA rank 16), Riemannion achieves 88.1±0.2% average accuracy, outperforming standard LoRA+Adam (87.1±0.6%), DoRA (86.6±0.3%), RPrecAdamW (86.8±0.4%), LoRA-RITE (84.5±0.5%), and Euclidean Muon (83.0±0.6%). Riemannion also exhibits the smallest variance (±0.2) among all compared methods. On the smaller Llama 3.2-1B model with SGD-like optimizers, RiemannLoRA-LOI reaches 73.4±0.3% versus 67.9±0.4% for vanilla LoRA (+5.5 points). For subject-driven generation with Stable Diffusion 2, Riemannion learns concepts in ~600 steps while preserving text-image alignment, converging faster than LoRA at all tested ranks (4, 8, 16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Riemannion consistently outperforms all six baselines on the primary Llama 3-8B Commonsense Reasoning benchmark. Against standard LoRA+Adam (87.1%), it gains +1.0 point; against DoRA (86.6%), +1.5 points; and against the closest Riemannian competitor RPrecAdamW (86.8%), +1.3 points. The gap is largest versus Euclidean Muon applied per-factor (83.0%, +5.1 points) and LoRA-RITE (84.5%, +3.6 points), confirming that per-factor orthogonalization without manifold awareness is significantly suboptimal. On the smaller Llama 3.2-1B model with SGD-like optimizers, RiemannLoRA-LOI (73.4%) beats vanilla LoRA (67.9%) by 5.5 points and LoRA-LOI (70.1%) by 3.3 points, with notably lower variance (±0.3 vs ±1.6). In subject-driven generation with Stable Diffusion 2, Riemannion achieves higher DINO image similarity and CLIP text similarity than LoRA at all ranks (4, 8, 16) and learning rates tested, while requiring only 600 training steps to learn complex concepts like 'robot toy'.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the Muon optimizer (Jordan et al., 2024) from the ambient Euclidean space to the fixed-rank matrix manifold, creating a fully Riemannian optimizer (Riemannion) that is reparameterization-invariant by construction. It departs from prior Riemannian LoRA approaches—RPrecAdamW (Zhang & Pilanci, 2024) and LORO (Mo et al., 2025)—which either port Adam-like mechanics onto the manifold with ad hoc parameterization choices or require explicit Gram matrix inversions, by instead inheriting Muon's geometry-aligned orthogonalization and avoiding such inversions entirely. The locally optimal initialization (LOI) is shown to be directly connected to LoRA-GA (Wang et al., 2024) but is derived from a clean Riemannian geometric principle (maximizing tangent-space alignment with the Euclidean gradient) and computed via a randomized SVD with a single-backward-pass gradient trick from Novikov et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reparameterization invariance of Riemannion addresses a fundamental pathology in standard LoRA training where the update depends on the arbitrary factorization (A, B), manifesting as unbalanced factor learning and hyperparameter fragility. The practical overhead is minimal—O((m+n)r² + r³) per step, matching Euclidean Muon—and the LOI initialization adds only ~0.25% wall-clock time, making the framework drop-in compatible with existing LoRA pipelines. However, the paper explicitly leaves theoretical convergence guarantees open, and the empirical gains, while consistent across LLM and diffusion tasks, are moderate (roughly 1–2 accuracy points over the strongest baselines like RPrecAdamW or DoRA). Whether the approach retains its advantages at very large ranks, in multi-layer joint optimization, or for non-LoRA structured adapters remains to be established.
