## Problem

Existing proactive video MLLMs rely on manually tuned response-probability thresholds (causing either silence or redundant replies) and supervised fine-tuning with precise reply timestamps that are impractical to annotate at frame-level granularity. This paper addresses both issues by eliminating the threshold via a text-based reply/silence decision and replacing timestamp-annotated SFT with multi-turn reinforcement learning that lets the model discover optimal response timing on its own.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260795/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a streaming video as 1-2 frames per user turn (128 visual tokens per frame, sampled at 2 s intervals during training and 1 s during inference) interleaved with optional text questions, all formatted within a standard user/assistant chat template. At each assistant turn the model outputs either a natural-language response or the literal string "NO REPLY"; the timestamp of each turn is implicitly determined by the cumulative frame count, so no explicit time annotation is needed at inference. The entire interaction loop-video ingestion, reply-timing decision, and content generation-is expressed purely as chat messages, requiring no architectural modification to the underlying VLM.

## Method

MMDuet2 is built on Qwen2.5-VL 3B and trained in two stages. First, SFT on ~52k videos (50k web + 2.5k ego-centric) with two dialogue structures-1QnA (one question, multiple scene-aligned answers) and nQnA (multiple questions with immediate summaries and deferred answers)-plus 50k offline video QA/captioning examples to preserve general understanding. Second, multi-turn GRPO reinforcement learning uses a modified PAUC reward (max score S=4, per-turn similarity) that jointly incentivizes correctness and earliness: because the model cannot describe an event before observing it, maximizing the area-under-curve naturally pushes responses toward the earliest feasible frame. Three auxiliary penalties (replication, out-of-span, verbose-prefix) suppress redundant and duplicate replies, with weights omega_PAUC=3, omega_rep=2, omega_in_span=0.5, omega_pfx=2. RL rollouts are performed over short 20-60 s video spans to avoid sparse-reward and credit-assignment problems, using 4 rollouts per step via SGLang and verl on 8 H800 GPUs.

## Results

On the ProactiveVideoQA benchmark, MMDuet2 (RL) achieves a PAUC of 53.3 on [WEB], 33.6 on [EGO], 43.4 on [TV], and 28.9 on [VAD], substantially outperforming MMDuet (38.9/46.0/21.1/27.4) and VideoLLM-Online (25.9/25.0/18.3/25.0) while maintaining a much lower reply duplicate proportion (e.g., 4.2% vs. 81.3% on [WEB]). On the StreamingBench Proactive Output task, MMDuet2 (RL) reaches 34.69 accuracy, surpassing MMDuet (29.44) and Dispider (25.34). Offline video understanding performance is largely preserved: MMDuet2 (RL) scores 67.5/58.1 on VideoMME (w/wo sub), 66.4 on MVBench, and 52.7 on LongVideoBench, close to the Qwen2.5-VL 3B base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MMDuet, MMDuet2 (RL) wins decisively on [WEB] (53.3 vs. 38.9 PAUC), [TV] (43.4 vs. 21.1), and [VAD] (28.9 vs. 27.4), while MMDuet retains an edge on [EGO] (46.0 vs. 33.6 PAUC). However, MMDuet's [EGO] advantage comes at the cost of a 99.4% reply duplicate proportion versus MMDuet2's 8.1%, indicating MMDuet achieves its score by flooding the user with repetitive responses. Against VideoLLM-Online, MMDuet2 (RL) outperforms on every ProactiveVideoQA split by a wide margin (e.g., 53.3 vs. 25.9 on [WEB]). On the StreamingBench Proactive Output task, MMDuet2 (RL) leads all baselines with 34.69 accuracy, beating MMDuet (29.44) by over 5 points and Dispider (25.34) by nearly 10 points. The SFT-only variant (MMDuet2_sft) already reduces duplication to near-zero levels but lags the RL variant in PAUC, confirming that the RL stage is critical for improving response timing and frequency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the proactive video interaction line initiated by VideoLLM-Online and MMDuet, which relied on probability-score thresholds or special tokens to decide reply timing and required precise timestamp annotations for SFT. MMDuet2 departs from this by (1) replacing the threshold-based decision with a pure text-based 'NO REPLY' mechanism that is compatible with standard chat-template frameworks without architectural changes, and (2) substituting timestamp-annotated SFT with multi-turn GRPO reinforcement learning whose PAUC-inspired reward (adapted from the ProactiveVideoQA benchmark) encourages early, correct responses without needing exact reply timestamps. The base model is Qwen2.5-VL 3B, and the RL training leverages SGLang and verl infrastructure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is eliminating the need for precise reply-timestamp annotation, which was a major bottleneck in constructing proactive interaction training data; the RL reward design lets the model discover appropriate timing autonomously. The 52k-video dataset with two dialogue structures (1QnA and nQnA) is a useful resource for the community. Open questions remain: the model still struggles with surveillance-style videos ([VAD]), ego-centric generalization degrades in later RL stages, and the approach has not yet been extended to speech or other modalities. The finding that low-frequency training (2s) combined with high-frequency inference (1s) frames is optimal suggests a broader principle for streaming MLLM deployment.
