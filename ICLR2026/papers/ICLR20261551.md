## Problem

Existing audio-to-video (A2V) generation methods struggle with fine-grained temporal synchronization between audio cues and visual motion, relying on indirect conditioning mechanisms (e.g., amplitude-based modulation or audio-to-text projection) or limited temporal modeling capacity. Moreover, standard MSE-based training objectives provide weak supervision for localized, precisely timed motion changes, making it difficult for models to learn accurate motion onset, offset, and magnitude aligned with audio events.

## Input / Output

The model takes an initial RGB frame (encoded to a VAE latent), a text prompt (encoded via T5/CLIP), and a 16 kHz audio waveform (encoded via DenseAV) as joint conditions, and autoregressively generates video chunks at 380×640 resolution, 24 fps, up to 5 seconds long. Audio features are segmented into local windows aligned to each video latent position, and the model denoises each chunk conditioned on the previously generated chunk, enabling sequential generation of temporally coherent video.

## Method

Syncphony builds on the Pyramid Flow autoregressive diffusion transformer backbone, inserting audio cross-attention layers (with Rotary Positional Encoding for temporal alignment) only into the later 16 transformer blocks (8–23), which govern temporal dynamics, while freezing earlier blocks that control spatial appearance. For training, a Motion-aware Loss multiplies per-frame prediction errors by the ground-truth latent difference between consecutive frames, amplifying supervision at high-motion regions without directly using audio amplitude as a timing signal. For inference, Audio Sync Guidance (ASG) runs a second forward pass with the audio cross-attention layers skipped (an 'off-sync' model) and adds the residual between the full and off-sync predictions back into the output, amplifying audio-driven motion without additional training. The paper also introduces CycleSync, a synchronization metric that feeds the generated video into a pretrained video-to-audio model and computes an IoU between onset peaks of the reconstructed and original audio, enabling evaluation at 24 fps without assuming one-to-one audio-motion peak correspondence.

## Results

On AVSync15, Syncphony achieves the best FID (8.5), FVD (293.1), and CycleSync (16.48±1.28) among all compared methods, approaching the ground-truth CycleSync of 22.15±1.8. On TheGreatestHits, it attains FVD of 166.2 and CycleSync of 16.18±1.26, surpassing even the ground-truth CycleSync (15.99±1.5). In the user study on 150 AVSync15 videos, Syncphony is preferred over AVSyncD in 74% of synchronization judgments, 90% for image quality, and 94% for frame consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Pyramid Flow (I+T, Jin et al. 2024a), Syncphony improves FVD from 550.7 to 293.1 and CycleSync from 14.25 to 16.48 on AVSync15, and from 350.5 to 166.2 (FVD) and 7.41 to 16.18 (CycleSync) on TheGreatestHits, demonstrating that audio conditioning substantially improves temporal coherence and synchronization. Against TempoTokens (T+A, Yariv et al. 2023), Syncphony reduces FVD by over 14× (4187.2→293.1) and gains 3.4 points in CycleSync (13.10→16.48), showing the advantage of image conditioning plus a video backbone over text-only audio-to-video. Against TPoS (I+T+A, Jeong et al. 2023), Syncphony achieves lower FID (8.5 vs 11.9) and FVD (293.1 vs 1227.8), though TPoS reports a slightly higher Image-Audio similarity (38.36 vs 37.02); TPoS does not report CycleSync. Against the closest A2V competitor AVSyncD (I+T+A, Zhang et al. 2024), Syncphony wins on every reported metric: FID 8.5 vs 9.2, FVD 293.1 vs 491.5, and CycleSync 16.48 vs 16.38 on AVSync15, with a particularly large CycleSync gap on TheGreatestHits (16.18 vs 9.89), confirming that the Motion-aware Loss and Audio Sync Guidance together yield both better visual quality and tighter audio-motion alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

Syncphony builds on the Pyramid Flow autoregressive diffusion transformer backbone (Jin et al., 2024a) and departs from prior audio-to-video methods (AVSyncD, TempoTokens, TPoS, AADiff) that condition on audio indirectly via amplitude modulation or audio-to-text projection. Instead, it injects audio features directly through cross-attention layers with Rotary Positional Encoding into the later transformer blocks of a pretrained video model, and introduces two targeted mechanisms—Motion-aware Loss for training and Audio Sync Guidance for inference—that are absent from prior A2V work. The proposed CycleSync metric also breaks from existing synchronization metrics (AV-Align, AlignSync, RelSync) by using a video-to-audio reconstruction loop rather than direct peak-matching between audio and motion signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first A2V system to generate 380×640, 24fps videos with a strong pretrained video backbone, closing the gap between high-quality video generation and fine-grained audio-motion synchronization. The finding that existing metrics (AV-Align, AlignSync, RelSync) can score non-audio-conditioned models higher than audio-conditioned ones exposes a critical evaluation gap that CycleSync addresses. Open questions include whether the Motion-aware Loss can be made selective for audio-relevant motion (vs. all motion), whether CycleSync's dependence on a V2A model introduces dataset-level biases, and whether the ASG and Motion-aware Loss techniques transfer to other modalities such as audio-to-3D animation.
