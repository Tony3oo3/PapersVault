## Problem

All existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods for vision-language models—prompt tuning, LoRA-based, and adapter-based—perform only a single-step adjustment of cross-modal features, which is insufficient for difficult datasets where image and text feature distributions are highly entangled. This paper identifies this one-step limitation as the root cause of PEFT methods' failure to generalize on challenging benchmarks and asks whether multi-step rectification can achieve better cross-modal alignment.

## Input / Output

FMA operates on pre-extracted features in a shared embedding space: RGB images are encoded by a frozen VLM (e.g., CLIP ViT-B/16) into d-dimensional image features, and class names are encoded via handcrafted templates (e.g., 'a photo of {class}') into text features of the same dimension. At inference, a test image feature is iteratively transformed by the learned velocity field over M Euler steps (default stepsize h=0.1), and the resulting intermediate feature is classified by cosine similarity against all class text features. The framework is agnostic to the upstream encoder, so it can be plugged onto zero-shot CLIP or any PEFT-finetuned variant without modification.

## Method

FMA trains a velocity field via conditional flow matching to transport image features toward their corresponding text features through multi-step ODE integration. Three key designs address the challenges of applying flow matching to classification: (1) Coupling Enforcement pairs each image feature exclusively with its ground-truth class text feature, guaranteeing that the learned marginal velocity equals the conditional velocity and thus preserves class correspondence; (2) Noise Augmentation injects time-dependent Gaussian noise (variance t·(1−t)·σ²(x_t)) into intermediate features during training to counteract the data scarcity induced by coupling, preventing the distribution from collapsing to a low-dimensional manifold; (3) an Early Stopping Solver terminates the ODE integration at a fixed number of steps M (selected on validation) rather than integrating to t=1, avoiding accumulated errors that push features toward incorrect text embeddings. The velocity network consists of 6 ResNet blocks with hidden dimensions matching the CLIP feature dimension, trained with AdamW (lr=0.0002) for 200 epochs.

## Results

On 16-shot few-shot classification across 11 benchmarks, FMA (built on CLIP-LoRA) achieves 77.8% average accuracy on difficult datasets (Aircraft, EuroSAT, DTD, SUN397, StanfordCars) and 88.8% on easy datasets (UCF101, ImageNet, Flowers102, Food101, OxfordPets, Caltech101). The gains over the CLIP-LoRA baseline are +1.7% on difficult and +0.6% on easy datasets, with the largest per-dataset improvement being +3.1% on Aircraft (54.7→57.8). At 4-shot, FMA reaches 69.0% (difficult) and 86.5% (easy), and at 1-shot, 59.4% and 83.8% respectively, consistently outperforming all compared PEFT methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FMA (on CLIP-LoRA) outperforms all 8 compared PEFT baselines on the difficult-dataset average at 16-shot: 77.8% vs. PLOT++ (74.1%), TIP-Adapter (71.9%), CoOp (71.4%), ProGrad (70.7%), KgCoOp (65.9%), CoCoOp (64.1%), CLIP-Adapter (62.6%), and zero-shot CLIP (48.9%). The most dramatic gain is over zero-shot CLIP (+20.0% on difficult datasets), confirming that multi-step rectification is essential when cross-modal distributions are highly entangled. Among PEFT methods, FMA's advantage is most pronounced on the hardest dataset (Aircraft: 57.8% vs. PLOT++'s 46.7%, a +11.1% margin) and smallest on already well-aligned easy datasets (e.g., Caltech101: 96.5% vs. PLOT++'s 96.0%). On easy datasets, FMA leads by a narrower margin (+0.6% over CLIP-LoRA, +0.1% over PLOT++), consistent with the paper's claim that one-step PEFT suffices when the distribution is simple. FMA also preserves generalization: cross-dataset transfer accuracy (trained on ImageNet, tested on 10 unseen datasets) remains comparable to the CLIP-LoRA baseline, and the harmonic mean of adaptation and generalization improves from 57.1/84.2 to 58.4/84.5.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Flow Matching (Rectified Flow), a framework originally developed for generative modeling (noise-to-data synthesis), as a discriminative alignment tool for few-shot classification with vision-language models. It positions all existing PEFT methods (CoOp, CoCoOp, CLIP-Adapter, CLIP-LoRA) as 'one-step' approaches that perform a single forward-pass feature adjustment, and argues this is insufficient for datasets with highly entangled cross-modal distributions. The noise augmentation strategy draws on Schrödinger bridge theory, and the coupling enforcement design is grounded in conditional flow matching theory, providing a theoretical guarantee that the learned velocity field preserves class correspondence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic that PEFT methods fail specifically on difficult datasets (low zero-shot CLIP accuracy) because they cannot model complex multi-step transformations is a useful lens for the few-shot VLM community. FMA's plug-and-play, model-agnostic design means it can be stacked on top of any existing PEFT method without architectural changes, making it practically accessible. However, gains over strong baselines like CLIP-LoRA are modest (+1.7 on difficult, +0.6 on easy datasets), and the early-stopping solver uses a fixed step count rather than a sample-specific stopping criterion, which the authors acknowledge as future work. Open questions include whether the multi-step rectification paradigm extends to other cross-modal tasks (retrieval, generation) and whether the velocity field can be made more accurate to eliminate the need for early stopping entirely.
