## Problem

Existing Incremental Object Detection (IOD) methods lack explicit quantitative modeling of inter-task knowledge conflicts: because a single image may contain objects from previous, present, and future tasks, unlabeled future-class objects are learned as background in earlier stages, and standard EWC inadvertently preserves this interference knowledge, violating its conditional-independence assumption and worsening catastrophic forgetting. The paper identifies this gap and asks how to isolate and remove such conflicting knowledge at the parameter level during incremental updates.

## Input / Output

At each incremental stage t, the method receives RGB images (608×928) annotated only for the current task's class subset c_t; objects from previously seen and future classes in those images carry no labels. The old model M_{t-1} is run on the current data to generate pseudo-labels for old classes and to estimate interference regions (background proposals whose IoU with new-class ground truths exceeds a threshold γ). The output is a full detector producing bounding boxes and class scores for all classes learned so far, evaluated via mAP@0.5 (VOC) or COCO-style mAP over IoU 0.5–0.95.

## Method

The IIKC framework combines two modules. (1) IKI-EWC reformulates the Bayesian posterior for IOD by decomposing past proposals into a non-conflicting subset and an interference subset (past background regions that overlap new-class ground truths), then derives a closed-form importance score \tilde{I}_i = I_{1,i}·I_{2,i} / ((1+k)²I_{2,i} + k²I_{1,i}) that down-weights parameters sensitive to interference while preserving those encoding genuine old knowledge; the interference set is estimated at stage t by running the old detector on current data with new-class labels masked. (2) PKC addresses semantic drift in the classification head by training a lightweight linear projection layer on top-K matched old/new feature pairs to map old-class prototypes into the updated feature space, then jointly retraining the classifier on both calibrated prototypes and current-task features. The full training loss at stage t is the standard Faster R-CNN detection loss plus the IKI-EWC quadratic penalty weighted by \tilde{I}_i, with the PKC projection discarded after classifier retraining.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2007 (two-stage), the proposed IIKC framework achieves 75.4, 73.7, 75.7, and 75.6 mAP@0.5 (1-20 average) in the 19-1, 15-5, 10-10, and 5-15 settings, respectively, outperforming the strongest non-rehearsal baseline BPF by 1.3–2.8% and the rehearsal-based GMDP-ABR by 0.5–4.9%. On MS COCO 2017, IIKC reaches 35.9 AP and 55.8 AP50 (40-40) and 37.1 AP and 57.6 AP50 (70-10), surpassing BPF by 1.5% and 0.9% in average AP. In multi-stage VOC settings (Table 2), the method gains up to 23.3% over GMDP-ILOD in the 5-5 split, though it trails in the 15-1 setting where old-class dominance limits plasticity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IIKC consistently outperforms all non-rehearsal baselines across every VOC and COCO setting. Against BPF (ECCV'24), the strongest non-rehearsal competitor, IIKC gains 1.3–2.8% mAP@0.5 on VOC two-stage and 0.9–1.5% AP on COCO. Against the rehearsal-based GMDP-ABR (ICLR'25), IIKC wins by 0.5–4.9% on VOC two-stage without requiring any stored exemplars, and by 2.6–4.3% on multi-stage VOC. The largest margin is against GMDP-ILOD (ICLR'25), where IIKC leads by up to 13.9% (5-15) and 23.3% (5-5 multi-stage), indicating that prototype-based distillation alone is insufficient for severe forgetting. IIKC also surpasses ABR (ICCV'23) by 3.5–6.2% on VOC two-stage, and outperforms older KD methods (Faster ILOD, MMA, PseudoRM, PROB, MVC) by 5–30+ points in the most challenging splits. The only setting where IIKC underperforms is VOC 15-1 multi-stage (67.4 vs. 66.9 for GMDP-ABR and BPF), where the paper attributes the gap to the dominance of 15 old classes limiting plasticity for the single new class.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) from Kirkpatrick et al. (2017) to the Incremental Object Detection (IOD) setting, where the standard conditional-independence assumption between tasks breaks down because unlabeled future-class objects are learned as background in earlier stages. While prior IOD work (ILOD, BPF, GMDP) primarily relies on knowledge distillation to transfer predictions from old models, this paper takes a parameter-regularization route, reformulating the Bayesian posterior to explicitly isolate and suppress interference knowledge at the weight level. The PKC module additionally draws on prototype-based calibration ideas from class-incremental learning (Li et al., 2024) to address semantic drift in the classification head, combining both regularization and calibration into a unified framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and formally addresses a specific failure mode in IOD that prior methods have not explicitly modeled: EWC inadvertently consolidates interference knowledge (future objects encoded as background), which paradoxically worsens forgetting. The theoretical reformulation provides a principled way to decouple genuine old-task importance from interference-driven importance. However, the method is currently limited to two-stage Faster R-CNN detectors, and the 15-1 multi-stage setting reveals a significant plasticity limitation (42.2 mAP on new classes). Extending the framework to single-stage or transformer-based detectors (e.g., DETR) and to longer incremental sequences remains an open challenge.
