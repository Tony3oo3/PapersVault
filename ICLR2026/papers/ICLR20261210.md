## Problem

Logic synthesis requires generating a compact gate-level circuit that exactly matches a given truth table, but existing methods—whether traditional heuristics or learning-based approaches—struggle to simultaneously achieve structural compactness and functional accuracy. No prior method could iteratively improve circuit size while guaranteeing 100% functional correctness, and the NP-hard nature of the problem makes direct optimization intractable for circuits with many inputs and outputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261210/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a truth table (up to 16 inputs and 69 outputs, corresponding to a search space of 2^(69×2^16)) and an LLM backbone (GPT-3.5-turbo, Deepseek-V3, or Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) as input, and produces a compact And-Inverter Graph (AIG) circuit. The intermediate representation is a graph-structured circuit program—a textual format with three parts (IO definition, hierarchical bottom-up structure description, and function definition) that encodes both topological connectivity and Boolean functionality in an LLM-readable form. The final AIG is validated against the truth table and can be further mapped to a standard cell library (mcnc.genlib) for area and delay evaluation.

## Method

CircuitEvo formulates the circuit as a graph-structured program and iteratively evolves it using an LLM within an evolutionary framework. The generator employs four domain-specific evolutionary prompt strategies—two crossover-inspired exploration strategies (E1, E2) that combine pairs of parent programs, and two mutation-inspired refinement strategies (R1, R2) that alter Boolean operators and variable configurations—along with few-shot in-context examples sampled from the current population and pre-identified inter-output logical constraints to reduce the search space. To guarantee functional correctness despite LLM limitations, a structure-aware function optimizer applies a proven completion theorem (F^i = (F_g^i + F_ga^i) * F_gb^i) that appends two auxiliary substructures generated by traditional LS heuristics (ABC) to correct any functional discrepancies. A local program search step identifies the most accurate prefix substructure of the generated program before completion, minimizing redundant logic introduced by the correction. The population is initialized via Shannon decomposition of the truth table, and fitness is defined as circuit size (node count) since all population members are functionally correct.

## Results

CircuitEvo achieves 100% functional accuracy across all 16 benchmark circuits (up to 16 inputs and 69 outputs) with all three LLM backbones (Qwen2.5-7B, Deepseek-V3, GPT-3.5-turbo), outperforming the best learning-based baseline DNAS (99.9% avg) and the LLM-based baseline ICSR (83.5% avg). In terms of circuit compactness, CircuitEvo achieves an average initial size of 470.13 nodes (Deepseek-V3), representing a 6.74% reduction over the best SOTA baseline (DNAS at 532.88 nodes). After technology mapping, CircuitEvo also achieves the lowest area (e.g., 789.67 µm² on Arithmetic vs. 928.00 for DNAS) and shortest delay (e.g., 13.67 ns on Arithmetic vs. 14.07 for DNAS) across all four benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CircuitEvo outperforms all seven baselines on the primary metric of initial circuit size, with an average 6.74% improvement over the strongest competitor DNAS (532.88 → 470.13 nodes). Among learning-based methods, DNAS is the closest competitor, but CircuitEvo still wins on 13 of 16 circuits; the two exceptions (LogicNets1, LogicNets3) show small losses of 1.48% and 6.03% respectively. The LLM-based baseline ICSR is the weakest performer overall (736.75 avg nodes, 83.5% accuracy), trailing CircuitEvo by a wide margin on both size and accuracy. Traditional heuristics SOP (539.38) and BDD (545.81) are competitive on small circuits but fall behind on larger ones (e.g., Arithmetic3: 1424/1490 vs. 1194). In terms of accuracy, CircuitEvo achieves 100% on all circuits, while DNAS reaches 99.9% and all other baselines (Boolformer 91.1%, SPL 91.5%, DSR 89.5%, ICSR 83.5%) fall short. After post-mapping, CircuitEvo also achieves the best area and delay on all four benchmark groups, confirming that the size advantage translates to practical hardware efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

CircuitEvo adapts the evolutionary prompting paradigm from Liu et al. (ICML 2024), which uses LLMs within evolutionary loops for heuristic design, to the logic synthesis domain. It departs from prior learning-based LS methods (Boolformer, SPL, DSR, DNAS) that represent circuits as Boolean expressions or AIGs and rely on gradient-based or search-based optimization, instead introducing a graph-structured circuit program that encodes topological connectivity in a textual format amenable to LLM generation. The structure-aware function optimizer, grounded in a formal circuit functionality completion theorem, distinguishes CircuitEvo from prior LLM-based symbolic regression approaches (e.g., ICSR) that cannot guarantee functional correctness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CircuitEvo is the first LLM-based LS method to simultaneously guarantee 100% functional accuracy and iteratively improve circuit compactness, addressing a long-standing tension in the field where prior methods trade off between the two. The empirical discovery that triangular node-level distributions and increased logic sharing are the primary structural drivers of compactness offers actionable design principles for future circuit generation and initial network architecture design. Open questions include scalability to circuits with more than 16 inputs, whether the function completion overhead diminishes with more capable LLMs, and whether the triangular structure insight generalizes across technology nodes and multi-level logic families.
