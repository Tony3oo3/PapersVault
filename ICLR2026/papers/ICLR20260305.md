## Problem

3D-aware classifiers such as NOVUM substantially improve out-of-distribution robustness by grounding predictions in volumetric object representations, yet their dense Gaussian feature matching remains inherently opaque. Meanwhile, concept-based explainable AI methods provide interpretability but typically neglect OOD robustness. The paper addresses this gap by asking whether a classifier can be simultaneously OOD-robust and inherently interpretable through a small set of geometrically grounded concepts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260305/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CAVE takes a single RGB image as input and produces a class label via bag-of-words matching between backbone features and a sparse dictionary of 3D concepts (D=20 per class, ~98% fewer than NOVUM's ~1130 Gaussians). Beyond the label, it outputs pixel-level concept attribution maps (via NOV-aware LRP) and per-concept importance scores derived from relevance quantiles. 3D object orientation can be supplied as ground-truth pose or estimated zero-shot via Orient-Anything, enabling training without pose annotations.

## Method

CAVE replaces NOVUM's dense Gaussian features on cuboid NOVs with a sparse concept dictionary learned via K-Means clustering on ellipsoid NOVs, formulated as a dictionary-learning problem that minimizes reconstruction error between Gaussian features and a compact set of D concept vectors. Classification then proceeds by matching each L2-normalised image feature to its nearest concept via cosine similarity, summing the top matches per class. To remove the dependency on ground-truth 3D poses, CAVE is trained with estimated orientations from Orient-Anything (weak 3D supervision). A key technical contribution is a NOV-aware LRP that introduces redistribution rules for upsampling-by-concatenation and volume concept-matching layers, preserving the relevance conservation property that vanilla LRP violates on these non-standard architectures. The paper also introduces 3D-C, a part-annotation-free consistency metric that projects concept attributions onto ground-truth CAD meshes to measure spatial coherence across viewpoints and OOD nuisances.

## Results

CAVE (with weak 3D supervision via Orient-Anything) achieves 99.0% accuracy on Pascal3D+, 84.6% on ImageNet3D, 76.8% on OccludedP3D+, and 80.3% on OOD-CV, outperforming all inherently interpretable baselines by 3–13 points in OOD settings. With full 3D pose supervision, CAVE reaches 99.4%, 88.5%, 81.3%, and 84.0%, slightly surpassing NOVUM on ImageNet3D (+0.2%) and OOD-CV (+2.7%) while using ~98% fewer parameters (20 concepts vs. 1130 Gaussians per class). On interpretability, CAVE achieves the best spatial localisation (0.28), object coverage (0.80 vs. 0.56 for LF-CBM), and 3D consistency (0.40 in-distribution, 0.24 on OOD-CV) across all evaluated settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAVE outperforms all five ad-hoc interpretable baselines (LF-CBM, ProtoPNet, TesNet, PIP-Net, MGProto) on every OOD benchmark, with the largest margins on OccludedP3D+ (76.8% vs. 73.8% for MGProto, the strongest competitor) and OOD-CV (80.3% vs. 73.5% for LF-CBM). On in-distribution Pascal3D+, CAVE leads by a narrow margin (99.0% vs. 98.4% LF-CBM), but the gap widens substantially on the larger ImageNet3D dataset (84.6% vs. 83.3% LF-CBM, 84.6% vs. 77.9% TesNet). Against post-hoc concept methods applied to NOVUM (CRAFT, MCD, ICE, PCX), CAVE achieves superior 3D consistency (0.40 vs. 0.28 for NOVUM+ICE on Pascal3D+) and object coverage (0.80 vs. 0.44), while also being model-faithful by design. With full 3D supervision, CAVE matches or slightly exceeds NOVUM's accuracy (88.5% vs. 88.3% on ImageNet3D; 84.0% vs. 81.3% on OOD-CV) while replacing 1130 opaque Gaussians with 20 interpretable concepts. The weak-supervision variant (no ground-truth poses) incurs only a modest 1–4 point drop relative to full supervision, confirming that CAVE remains competitive without expensive pose annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAVE directly extends NOVUM (Jesslen et al., 2024), which introduced Neural Object Volumes (NOVs) for OOD-robust classification via dense Gaussian feature matching on 3D shapes. The key departure is replacing NOVUM's ~1130 opaque Gaussians per class with a sparse dictionary of D=20 concepts learned via K-Means clustering, yielding inherent interpretability while preserving the bag-of-words matching mechanism. For attribution, the paper adapts LRP (Bach et al., 2015; Otsuki et al., 2024) with a novel redistribution rule for volumetric concept matching, and leverages Orient-Anything (Wang et al., 2025b) for zero-shot pose estimation to eliminate the ground-truth 3D pose requirement. The 3D-C consistency metric is positioned as a complement to existing part-annotation-based evaluation (Huang et al., 2023; Behzadi-Khormouji & Oramas, 2023), using CAD meshes as a common reference surface instead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAVE addresses a genuine gap: prior work on 3D-aware robust classifiers (NOVUM) and concept-based interpretable models (CBMs, ProtoPNet, TesNet) have been developed in isolation, and neither line has been evaluated on the other's axis. The 3D-C metric offers a practical, annotation-free way to assess concept stability across viewpoints and OOD shifts, which could become a standard complement to part-alignment metrics in XAI evaluation. Limitations include restriction to single-object, rigid-object settings (12 Pascal3D+ classes), dependence on CAD meshes for 3D-C, and the fact that weak supervision via Orient-Anything still introduces a modest accuracy gap (~2-4%) relative to full 3D supervision. Open questions include extension to non-rigid categories (e.g., humans), multi-object scenes, and whether the ellipsoid approximation suffices for highly variable intra-class shapes.
