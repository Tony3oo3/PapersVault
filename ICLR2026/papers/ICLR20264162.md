## Problem

Hybrid Mamba-Transformer models (e.g., Bamba, Nemotron-H) collapse in perplexity and retrieval accuracy when inputs exceed their training context window, yet no prior work had systematically diagnosed why this failure occurs or proposed a training-free context length extension (CLE) method tailored to the hybrid architecture. Existing CLE techniques target either pure Transformers (RoPE rescaling) or pure Mamba (token filtering, step clamping), leaving the hybrid case unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained hybrid Mamba-Transformer checkpoint (Bamba-9B-v2 at 4K, Nemotron-H-8B at 8K, or Mamba2-2.7B at 2K context) and a small calibration set of 100 sequences (16K tokens each, from the proof-pile ArXiv subset) to profile per-head ERF. It outputs a closed-form rescaling: Δ_t divided by the length-scaling factor n for the top-20% highest-ERF Mamba heads, plus YaRN-scaled RoPE frequencies for Transformer layers. At inference, the rescaled model processes arbitrarily long text sequences with no additional compute overhead beyond the original forward pass.

## Method

UPI identifies the root cause of hybrid long-context collapse as uncontrolled state magnitude growth in a small subset of Mamba heads whose forget gates remain near 1, causing their states to grow far beyond the training distribution and trigger feature collapse via GroupNorm suppression of other heads. The method profiles per-head Effective Receptive Field (ERF) on a small calibration set and selects the top-20% highest-ERF heads (a bimodal split confirmed across all tested models), then divides their discretization step Δ_t by the target length ratio n. For Transformer layers, UPI applies standard YaRN RoPE frequency scaling. The theoretical justification rests on structured state space duality: Mamba's cumulative forget-gate decay is mathematically analogous to RoPE's frequency-based position encoding, so scaling small Δ_t by 1/n is the state-space counterpart of scaling low RoPE frequencies, satisfying both 'forget consistency' and 'input consistency' conditions derived from unrolling the recurrence.

## Results

On PG-19 perplexity at 64K tokens, UPI reduces Bamba-9B-v2 from 127.90 (base) to 18.59 (an 85.5% relative reduction), Nemotron-H-8B from 530.31 to 44.01 (91.7% reduction), and Mamba2-2.7B from 478.21 to 22.24 (95.3% reduction). On RULER at 16K, UPI+YaRN achieves 19.0% average retrieval accuracy on Bamba-9B-v2 versus 11.4% for LongMamba+YaRN and 12.0% for base. On LongBench-E, UPI+YaRN reaches 23.68 average on Bamba and 25.15 on Nemotron-H, outperforming both baselines. Calibration requires only ~3 minutes on 8 A100 GPUs versus ~10.5 hours for LongMamba's parameter search.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LongMamba (Ye et al., ICLR 2025), UPI achieves substantially lower PG-19 perplexity at all extended lengths: at 64K, UPI reaches 18.59 vs. 53.14 (Bamba), 44.01 vs. 78.51 (Nemotron-H), and 22.24 vs. 42.20 (Mamba2). On RULER at 16K for Bamba, UPI+YaRN scores 19.0% average vs. LongMamba+YaRN's 11.4%, with particularly large gains on S2 (40.6% vs. 20.6%) and QA1 (22.6% vs. 9.2%). On LongBench-E, UPI+YaRN outperforms LongMamba+YaRN on both hybrid models (23.68 vs. 22.85 on Bamba; 25.15 vs. 23.14 on Nemotron-H) but trails LongMamba on pure Mamba2 (14.52 vs. 15.77), which the authors attribute to LongMamba being optimized end-to-end for that task family. Against DeciMamba (Ben-Kish et al., ICLR 2025) on Mamba2-2.7B, UPI is slightly worse at short contexts (2K–8K) but substantially better at longer ones: at 64K, UPI achieves 22.24 perplexity vs. DeciMamba's 67.88. A key practical advantage is calibration cost: UPI requires ~3 minutes of forward passes on 8 A100s, while LongMamba requires ~10.5 hours of parameter search on the same hardware.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the context length extension (CLE) literature from its two separate branches—Transformer RoPE scaling (PI, YaRN, LongRoPE) and Mamba state-space modifications (DeciMamba, LongMamba, MambaExtend)—into the hybrid Mamba-Transformer setting, where both components must be scaled jointly. Its theoretical grounding rests on the Structured State Space Duality (Dao & Gu, 2024), which the authors extend to show that Mamba's cumulative forget-gate decay is mathematically analogous to RoPE's frequency-based position encoding, making UPI a direct state-space counterpart to YaRN-style frequency interpolation. The method departs from prior Mamba CLE work by replacing token-level filtering or time-step clamping with a closed-form, head-selective rescaling of Δ_t, and from prior Transformer CLE by identifying that RoPE scaling alone is insufficient when Mamba state magnitudes explode.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As hybrid Mamba-Transformer models (Bamba, Nemotron-H, Jamba, Jet-Nemotron) become the dominant efficient-LLM architecture, a training-free, architecture-agnostic CLE method with <3-minute calibration (vs. ~10.5 hours for LongMamba) is practically critical for deploying these models beyond their training windows. The RoPE-Mamba positional encoding duality opens a principled research direction for cross-architectural CLE transfer, though the paper explicitly leaves deeper exploitation as future work. Limitations include empirical (rather than derived) selection of the 20% head threshold, evaluation capped at 64K tokens, and residual perplexity degradation at extended lengths (18.59 at 64K vs. 8.30 at 4K for Bamba), suggesting the method stabilizes but does not fully eliminate out-of-distribution dynamics.
