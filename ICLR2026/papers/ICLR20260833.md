## Problem

Existing neural network approaches to the functional ANOVA model (e.g., ANOVA-TPNN, NAM) require all components to be specified in advance, and the number of required networks grows exponentially with the interaction order. In practice this limits them to 1–2 dimensional components, making it impossible to detect higher-order interactions without prohibitive computational and memory costs. The paper asks whether a Bayesian framework can learn both the architecture (which components exist) and their parameters simultaneously, enabling efficient detection of higher-order components.

## Input / Output

The method takes p-dimensional tabular feature vectors rescaled to [0,1]^p and a response variable (continuous for Gaussian regression, binary for logistic regression); in the CBM setting, inputs are 5 binary concept labels predicted by a frozen ResNet-18+CLIP pipeline. Outputs include the full predictive distribution (via posterior averaging over MCMC samples), individual functional ANOVA components f_S with 95% credible intervals, and the learned architecture itself (number of basis functions K and the input subsets S_k defining each component's dimensionality). The model is an edge-sparse shallow network where each hidden node k is connected only to inputs in S_k, making the architecture interpretable as a set of variable-group interactions.

## Method

Bayesian-TPNN represents the regression function as a sum of K tensor-product neural network (TPNN) basis functions, where each basis φ(x|S_k, b, Γ) operates on a subset S_k of inputs and satisfies the sum-to-zero condition for unique ANOVA decomposition. The key architectural innovation is treating K (node count) and S_k (edge connectivity) as discrete parameters explored via a custom MCMC algorithm with three sequential updates: (1) K is updated by MH with a novel Stepwise proposal that copies an existing node and adds one edge, encouraging growth toward higher-order interactions; (2) each S_k is updated via Adding/Deleting/Changing moves (RJMCMC) guided by a pretrained variable-importance distribution p_input; (3) continuous parameters (b, Γ, β) are updated by Langevin-dynamics MH for faster convergence. The paper proves posterior consistency of each ANOVA component under regularity conditions, and demonstrates that the Stepwise move and non-uniform p_input are critical for detecting 3rd- and 4th-order interactions that fixed-architecture baselines cannot access.

## Results

On eight real datasets, Bayesian-TPNN achieves prediction performance competitive with black-box models while remaining fully interpretable. On MADELON (500 features), Bayesian-TPNN attains AUROC 0.854, dramatically outperforming the closest interpretable baseline ANOVA-TPNN (0.587) and NAM (0.644), though trailing XGB (0.884). On BOSTON and MPG (regression), Bayesian-TPNN achieves RMSE 3.654 and 2.386 respectively, beating all interpretable baselines and even XGB (4.130, 2.531). In uncertainty quantification (Table 2), Bayesian-TPNN matches or beats BART and mBNN on CRPS, NLL, and ECE across both regression and classification tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ANOVA-TPNN (the closest interpretable baseline), Bayesian-TPNN wins on 6 of 8 datasets, with the largest margin on MADELON (AUROC 0.854 vs 0.587, a 26.7-point gap) and SERVO (RMSE 0.351 vs 0.594), while slightly losing on ABALONE (2.053 vs 2.051) and FICO (0.793 vs 0.802). Against NAM/NA²M, Bayesian-TPNN dominates on every dataset, e.g. RMSE 2.386 vs 2.755 on MPG and AUROC 0.854 vs 0.644 on MADELON. Against the black-box XGB, Bayesian-TPNN wins on BOSTON (3.654 vs 4.130), MPG (2.386 vs 2.531), and BREAST (0.998 vs 0.995), but loses on SERVO (0.351 vs 0.314) and MADELON (0.854 vs 0.884). Against BART, Bayesian-TPNN wins on 6 of 8 datasets including FICO (0.793 vs 0.701) and MADELON (0.854 vs 0.751). Against mBNN, Bayesian-TPNN wins on 7 of 8 datasets, with the largest gap on MADELON (0.854 vs 0.650) and BOSTON (3.654 vs 4.277), losing only on SERVO (0.351 vs 0.301). In uncertainty quantification, Bayesian-TPNN matches or beats both BART and mBNN on CRPS/NLL for regression and ECE/NLL for classification, which the authors note is surprising given its transparent architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

Bayesian-TPNN extends ANOVA-TPNN (Park et al., 2025) by treating the component architecture—which variable subsets define each TPNN basis and how many bases exist—as learnable parameters explored via MCMC, rather than pre-specified hyperparameters. The MCMC design borrows the grow/prune/modify move structure from BART (Chipman et al., 2010) and the node-sparsity prior from mBNN (Kong et al., 2023), but adapts them to the functional ANOVA setting with a novel stepwise proposal that incrementally increases interaction order. This positions the work at the intersection of Bayesian architecture search (S-RJMCMC, Nguyen et al., 2024) and interpretable additive modeling, being the first to combine these for the functional ANOVA decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete bottleneck in interpretable ML: existing functional ANOVA methods (NAM, ANOVA-TPNN) are limited to 1st- or 2nd-order interactions due to exponential growth in the number of required networks, yet higher-order interactions often drive prediction accuracy. Bayesian-TPNN's stepwise MCMC and p_input-guided proposal circumvent this without pre-enumerating all subsets, and the posterior consistency theorem provides rare theoretical grounding for a neural-network-based ANOVA model. Key limitations include the need for an externally specified p_input (which the authors acknowledge is critical for higher-order detection but not yet adaptive), the computational cost of MCMC relative to gradient-based alternatives, and the reliance on a shallow single-layer architecture that may limit expressiveness for very complex interactions.
