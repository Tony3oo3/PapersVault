## Problem

Existing multi-view reconstruction datasets are limited in camera coverage (either ground-only or aerial-only, perspective-only), lack controlled temporal diversity, and are too small or uncontrolled to enable holistic evaluation of large-scale, immersive 3D/4D reconstruction. No prior dataset simultaneously spans multiple elevations, 360° omnidirectional views, four seasons over two years, and twenty buildings in a unified coordinate system, leaving critical challenges like cross-elevation calibration, doppelgänger disambiguation, and multi-appearance consistency unaddressed at scale.

## Input / Output

The dataset comprises 37.7K frames from three consumer-grade devices: iPhone (70° FoV, 7,134 frames), Insta360 (360° panorama split into four 120° perspective crops, 23,260 frames), and DJI Mini 3 drone (82° FoV, 7,334 frames), collected across 20 campus buildings at ground (0 m) and aerial (60/100/120 m) elevations over four seasons. Panorama bottom and top faces are discarded, and all PII is automatically blurred. The pipeline outputs calibrated camera poses in a single campus-wide coordinate system, SfM sparse point clouds, and dense 3D reconstructions evaluated via PSNR, SSIM, DSIM, and LPIPS on held-out ground and aerial test views.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a semi-automated, divide-and-conquer calibration pipeline that avoids doppelgänger false matches: within each elevation, sequential matching constrains intra-sequence pairs while manual front/back bucketing restricts inter-sequence pairs; cross-elevation registration uses transitional drone-to-ground sequences with a manually defined scene graph that excludes direct ground-aerial pairs; and campus-wide alignment extends Procrustes alignment by jointly optimizing over camera centers and back-projected rotation points (Eq. 5–6). The paper then benchmarks ten feature-matching methods (SIFT, SP+SG, SP+LG, LoFTR, RoMa, DaD+RoMa, VGGT, VGGSfM, MASt3R, MASt3R-SfM) and six dense-reconstruction methods (Block-MERF, Splatfacto-W, CityGS V2, Scaffold-GS, Octree-GS, EVER) on cross-elevation and multi-appearance NVS. Key empirical findings include: DaD+RoMa achieves the best cross-elevation AUC@10 but still fails on some buildings; cross-elevation training degrades all dense methods due to insufficient densification; and per-image appearance embeddings in Wild-GS/Gaussian-Wild overfit to specific viewpoints, which a time-based embedding partially resolves.

## Results

For cross-elevation feature matching, DaD+RoMa achieves the highest AUC@10 across most buildings (0.69–0.80), while SIFT, SP+SG, SP+LG, and LoFTR all score 0 on the majority of buildings. For multi-elevation dense reconstruction, Octree-GS performs best overall, reaching 26.488 PSNR and 0.024 DSIM when trained on ground+aerial (GA) images and tested on aerial views, compared to 26.135 PSNR for Scaffold-GS. A key finding is that cross-elevation training (GA) significantly degrades performance versus single-elevation training (e.g., GA→A: 26.488 PSNR vs. A→A: 29.950 PSNR for Octree-GS), and cross-elevation reconstructions contain fewer Gaussians despite having more training data. For multi-appearance reconstruction, replacing per-image embeddings with time-based embeddings matches test-image embedding performance (27.973 vs. 28.133 PSNR for Wild-GS) while eliminating the need for test-view images at render time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In cross-elevation feature matching (Table 3), DaD+RoMa dominates, achieving AUC@10 of 0.69–0.80 on 5 of 7 buildings, while SIFT, SP+SG, SP+LG, LoFTR, VGGSfM, and MASt3R-SfM all score 0 on most buildings; RoMa alone is the only other method with non-zero scores but is highly inconsistent (0.002–0.50). In dense reconstruction (Table 4), Octree-GS outperforms all baselines on the GA→A configuration (26.488 PSNR, 0.024 DSIM), narrowly beating Scaffold-GS (26.135 PSNR, 0.022 DSIM) and significantly outperforming Block-MERF (13.453 PSNR) and CityGS V2 (20.129 PSNR); Splatfacto-W achieves the best aerial rendering from ground-only training (DSIM 0.714 vs. 0.847 for Block-MERF) thanks to its background modeling. For multi-appearance reconstruction (Table 6), time-based embeddings match test-image embedding performance (Wild-GS: 27.973 vs. 28.133 PSNR; Gaussian-Wild: 26.277 vs. 26.528 PSNR) while removing the need for test-view access, whereas using the farthest training image embedding causes a severe drop (Wild-GS: 22.506 PSNR, DSIM 0.061), revealing that per-image embeddings overfit to specific viewpoints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Procrustes alignment framework (Gower, 1975) by incorporating rotation-point constraints to improve cross-elevation coordinate registration, and relies on COLMAP's SfM pipeline (Schönberger & Frahm, 2016) as the calibration backbone. Conceptually, it departs from prior multi-view datasets (PhotoTourism, Block-NeRF, KITTI-360, MatrixCity) by being the first to simultaneously provide multi-elevation (ground + aerial at 60/100/120 m), multi-season (four seasons over two years), and omnidirectional (360° panorama + perspective) coverage at campus scale within a single calibrated coordinate system. The empirical evaluation positions itself as a stress test for the full pipeline—from feature matching (SIFT, SuperPoint, RoMa, MASt3R, VGGT) through scene graph optimization (NetVLAD, Doppelganger++) to dense reconstruction (3DGS variants, NeRF variants)—rather than proposing a new reconstruction algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ULTRA-360 fills a clear gap: no prior dataset combines multi-elevation, multi-seasonal, and omnidirectional imagery in a single calibrated benchmark, making it a natural testbed for end-to-end campus-scale 4D reconstruction. The empirical findings raise important open questions—cross-elevation training paradoxically reduces Gaussian counts and degrades quality (suggesting densification algorithms break under multi-scale positional gradients), no feature matcher reliably resolves cross-elevation correspondences without manual scene-graph intervention, and per-image appearance embeddings overfit to viewpoints rather than capturing 3D-consistent appearance. These results suggest that the field's assumption that 'more diverse training data always helps' does not hold for current 3DGS/NeRF pipelines, and that global (rather than pairwise) doppelganger resolution is needed for truly unconstrained calibration.
