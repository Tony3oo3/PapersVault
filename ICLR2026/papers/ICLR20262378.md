## Problem

While SFT (via distillation from frontier models) and RL (via rule-based verifiers) have each been shown to improve LLM reasoning, a systematic study of their synergy—how SFT quality, scaling, and initialization interact with RL training dynamics—has been largely absent from the literature. This paper investigates whether stronger SFT models consistently yield better RL outcomes, how to set the sampling temperature to balance exploration and exploitation, and when overlong filtering should be applied across different RL stages.

## Input / Output

The model accepts math problem statements or code problem statements (with or without starter code) as text input and produces a long chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a final answer (in \boxed{} format for math) or a complete Python solution (in a code block for code). The base Qwen2.5-Math-7B is modified to support 128K context via RoPE theta adjustment, and training/inference response lengths range from 8K to 32K tokens, with evaluation performed at 32K maximum sequence length using temperature 0.6 and top-p 0.95.

## Method

The training pipeline combines scaled SFT with a six-stage RL curriculum. SFT uses 383K prompts (247K math, 136K code) with responses generated by DeepSeek-R1, scaled along two axes—number of unique prompts and responses per prompt—with training run for 5–6 epochs. RL employs strict on-policy GRPO (G=8 or 16 rollouts per prompt, single policy gradient update) with token-level policy gradient loss and no KL term, progressing through math-only stages at 8K→16K→24K→32K response budgets, a code-only stage at 24K→32K, and a final math-only stage at 32K. Key design choices include setting the training temperature so that temperature-adjusted entropy stays near 0.3 (typically T≈0.85), applying overlong filtering (masking truncated samples) in early short-budget stages but removing it at 32K to encourage token-efficient generation, and using epoch-wise problem filtering in the code stage to maintain difficulty.

## Results

AceReason-Nemotron-1.1-7B achieves 72.6% on AIME24 (avg@64), 64.8% on AIME25 (avg@64), 57.2% on LiveCodeBench V5 (avg@8), and 52.1% on LiveCodeBench V6 (avg@8), setting new state-of-the-art among Qwen2.5-7B-based reasoning models on both math and code benchmarks. Compared to its SFT starting point (62.0% AIME24, 48.4% AIME25), the RL training yields absolute gains of +10.6% on AIME24, +16.4% on AIME25, +8.4% on LCB V5, and +8.3% on LCB V6. The model also scores 95.3% on Math500, 42.9% on HMMT2025 Feb, 69.8% on BRUMO2025, and 84.8% on EvalPlus.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the most direct predecessor AceReason-Nemotron-1.0-7B, the 1.1 model improves by +3.6 on AIME24, +11.2 on AIME25, +5.4 on LCB V5, and +8.0 on LCB V6, demonstrating that the same RL recipe applied to a stronger SFT initialization yields substantial additional gains. Compared to DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (the SFT initialization used in 1.0), the 1.1 model leads by +17.1 on AIME24 and +25.8 on AIME25, confirming that the SFT data curation pipeline produces a much stronger foundation than direct distillation from DeepSeek-R1. Among RL-based 7B competitors, AceReason-Nemotron-1.1-7B outperforms MiMo-7B-RL by +4.4 on AIME24 and +9.4 on AIME25 (though MiMo edges it by 0.6 on LCB V5), and surpasses Skywork-OR1-7B by +2.4 on AIME24, +10.2 on AIME25, +9.6 on LCB V5, and +9.4 on LCB V6. On the SFT-distillation side, the model's SFT checkpoint already exceeds Llama-Nemotron-Nano-8B-v1 by +0.7 on AIME24 and +1.3 on AIME25, and far surpasses Light-R1-7B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B across all benchmarks. The only model reporting a higher AIME24 score is OpenMath-Nemotron-7B (74.8), but that model is SFT-only, reports no code benchmarks, and is not directly comparable as a full reasoning system.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends AceReason-Nemotron-1.0 (Chen et al., 2025), which introduced stage-wise RL on math-only and code-only prompts using strict on-policy GRPO, by integrating a strong SFT foundation and systematically studying the SFT-RL training dynamics. It builds on the broader verification-based RL paradigm established by DeepSeek-R1 and the GRPO algorithm, while departing from the distillation-only SFT approaches (Light-R1, OpenMathReasoning, etc.) by curating SFT data from multiple sources and scaling it along two axes (prompt diversity and responses per prompt). The paper explicitly positions itself as filling a gap it identifies: a systematic study of SFT-RL synergy is 'notably absent from the technical reports of frontier models.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides actionable, stage-dependent guidance for practitioners building reasoning models: the entropy-0.3 temperature rule, the finding that overlong filtering helps at short token budgets (8K/16K) but hurts at 32K, and the observation that SFT benefits from 5-6 epochs of 'overfitting' due to exposure bias. These findings are practically valuable but are validated primarily at 7B scale on Qwen2.5-Math, leaving open whether the same dynamics hold at larger scales or different base architectures. A key open question is whether the SFT-RL synergy patterns (gap narrowing, temperature sensitivity) generalize beyond the math/code domains with rule-based verifiers, and whether the stage-wise curriculum is truly necessary or an artifact of the specific training recipe.
