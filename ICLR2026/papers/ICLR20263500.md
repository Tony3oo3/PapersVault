## Problem

Axis-aligned decision trees must approximate rotated or interaction-dependent decision boundaries through many orthogonal cuts, leading to deeper trees and fragmented regions. Oblique forests capture such boundaries with per-node hyperplane splits but incur substantial per-node optimization cost. The paper asks whether a single global, supervised feature transformation can let standard axis-aligned trees recover oblique-boundary accuracy at near-vanilla training speed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a tabular feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} and class labels (or a scalar target for regression). Internally it first fits a 50-tree surrogate RF to obtain probability estimates, then probes those probabilities via centered finite differences along each coordinate axis with adaptive per-feature step sizes ε_j = α·MAD(X_{:j})/0.6745. The output is a standard 200-tree random forest trained on the d-dimensional transformed features XĤ, producing class probabilities (classification) or scalar predictions (regression) at inference after applying the same linear map Φ(x) = x^T Ĥ.

## Method

JARF computes the Expected Jacobian Outer Product (EJOP) H_0 = E_X[J_f(X)J_f(X)^T] from a surrogate random forest's class-probability gradients estimated via finite differences, then uses H_0 (plus a small γI_d regularizer and trace normalization) as a single global linear preconditioner shared across all trees. The key geometric insight is that an axis-aligned split on the j-th transformed coordinate (x^T Ĥ)_j ≤ τ is equivalent to an oblique hyperplane x^T Ĥ e_j ≤ τ in the original space, and the EJOP's leading eigenvectors span directions where class probabilities vary most—so the preconditioner rotates the feature space to align those informative directions with the coordinate axes. This one-pass, model-agnostic transform requires no per-node optimization, no changes to the tree learner, and adds only ~1.7× the training time of a vanilla RF while matching or surpassing per-node oblique forests (CCF, SPORF, RotF) on 15 classification and 5 regression benchmarks. A dimension-adapted risk bound (Theorem 1) shows that under a ridge-structured regression function with intrinsic EJOP rank r, JARF achieves the nonparametric rate n^{-2/(2+r)} independent of ambient dimension d.

## Results

Across 15 tabular classification datasets, JARF achieves a mean Cohen's κ of 0.810, outperforming RF (0.704), RotF (0.715), CCF (0.715), SPORF (0.723), XGBoost (0.709), PCA+RF (0.692), and LDA+RF (0.697). On 5 regression tasks, JARF attains a mean R² of 0.836 versus 0.776 for RF and 0.810 for XGBoost. JARF wins on 12 of 15 classification datasets and all 5 regression datasets, with the largest gains (≈0.08–0.13 κ) on high-dimensional or interaction-heavy tasks (electricity, magic, letter, higgs, mnist-784). Training time is ~25 s (1.67× RF) versus 44–60 s for per-node oblique forests (CCF, RotF).

## Baselines & Comparisons

JARF outperforms all five primary baselines on mean Cohen's κ across the 15 classification datasets: RF (0.704, +0.106), XGBoost (0.709, +0.101), Rotation Forest (0.715, +0.095), CCF (0.715, +0.095), and SPORF (0.723, +0.087). The gap is largest on datasets with strong feature interactions or high dimensionality (e.g., covertype +0.178 over RF, mnist-784 +0.130, higgs +0.100), while on the already-easy vehicle dataset JARF (0.890) is slightly below RF (0.900). Against the oblique baselines specifically, JARF matches or beats SPORF on 13/15 datasets and CCF on 14/15, and it does so at roughly half the training time (25 s vs. 44–60 s). On regression, JARF (mean R² = 0.836) surpasses XGBoost (0.810), SPORF (0.802), CCF (0.794), RotF (0.786), and RF (0.776) on all five tasks. The simple global projection baselines PCA+RF (0.692) and LDA+RF (0.697) underperform even vanilla RF, confirming that the EJOP-based supervised preconditioning captures predictive geometry that unsupervised or class-separation-only projections miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

JARF repurposes the Expected Jacobian Outer Product (EJOP/EGOP) framework from Trivedi et al., originally developed for supervised dimension reduction and global sensitivity analysis, as a one-pass linear preconditioner for axis-aligned random forests. Conceptually it bridges two previously separate threads: supervised projection methods (SIR, SAVE, LDA) that find label-informed subspaces, and oblique decision forests (OC1, Rotation Forest, CCF, SPORF) that learn per-node hyperplane splits. The key departure from oblique forests is that JARF computes a single global transform shared across all trees and nodes, avoiding per-node optimization entirely, while departing from standard PCA/LDA projections by using probability-gradient geometry rather than moment statistics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

JARF offers a practically attractive middle ground: oblique-forest accuracy at near-RF training speed, with a simple one-pass preprocessing step that is model-agnostic (works with RF, XGBoost, or other learners). The dimension-adapted risk bound (Theorem 1) is the first to formally link EJOP geometry to tree-ensemble sample complexity, providing theoretical grounding beyond the geometric heuristic. Key limitations include reliance on a surrogate model for gradient estimation (though robustness experiments show insensitivity to surrogate quality), the assumption that a single global linear transform suffices (which may fail for highly non-linear or multi-scale boundaries), and the added preprocessing overhead that vanilla forests avoid. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-tabular data, whether adaptive or local transforms could capture more complex geometry, and how it interacts with modern tabular deep learning methods.
