## Problem

Deep neural network–based encoding models predict fMRI voxel activity with high accuracy but remain opaque at the single-voxel level, making it difficult to explain what visual properties a given voxel encodes. Prior data-driven captioning methods (BrainSCUBA, SASC) are constrained by a single captioning model or very short n-gram phrases, limiting lexical richness and the ability to capture fine-grained, multi-concept voxel selectivity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263837/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 7T fMRI voxel responses (1.8 mm resolution, z-scored per session) from the Natural Scenes Dataset (10,000 images at 224×224 px, 4 subjects), CLIP-Vision projection-layer embeddings (512-dim, L2-normalized) as the feature space, and an external pool of ~1.7 million OpenImages-v6 images for optimal-image search. The output is a single concise natural-language sentence per voxel (typically ~11–12 words), composed from five filtered keywords, which is then evaluated by its ability to predict held-out voxel activity via sentence-level (Sentence-BERT cosine similarity) and image-level (FLUX.1-schnell–generated image, CLIP-Vision embedding) correlations.

## Method

LaVCa is a four-stage pipeline that decouples image selection from caption generation. First, voxel-wise linear encoding models are trained via ridge regression on CLIP-Vision embeddings to predict fMRI responses. Second, the top-50 images most strongly activating each voxel are identified by computing inner products between the voxel's encoding weight vector and CLIP-Vision embeddings of ~1.7 million external images. Third, a multimodal LLM (MiniCPM-V) generates brief captions for each optimal image. Fourth, gpt-4o extracts common keywords across those captions via in-context learning; keywords are filtered by CLIP-Text cosine similarity against the encoding weight (softmax threshold), and the surviving keywords are composed into a coherent sentence using MeaCap's Sentence Composer, whose scoring function is modified to include a voxel-relevance term (similarity between each candidate word's CLIP-Text embedding and the encoding weight) alongside fluency and image-relevance terms. This multi-keyword, LLM-mediated design yields captions that are both more accurate and more lexically/semantically diverse than BrainSCUBA's single-caption approach.

## Results

On the NSD dataset (top 5,000 voxels, 4 subjects), LaVCa with 5 keywords and the Sentence Composer achieves sentence-level prediction accuracy of 0.246–0.306 (mean ± SD across subjects) and image-level accuracy of 0.213–0.273, outperforming BrainSCUBA (0.182–0.264 sentence; 0.169–0.250 image) at P < 0.05 (paired t-test). The shuffled-caption baseline yields near-zero accuracy (≈0.01–0.07), confirming that the captions carry genuine voxel-specific information. In terms of lexical diversity, LaVCa captions span 16,922 unique words across voxels versus 3,193 for BrainSCUBA, and each caption averages 11.4 words versus 6.09 for BrainSCUBA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is BrainSCUBA (Luo et al., 2023), an end-to-end method that projects voxel encoding weights into image space via dot-product attention and generates captions with a pre-trained image captioning model (ClipCap). LaVCa outperforms BrainSCUBA on both sentence-level and image-level prediction accuracy across all four subjects (P < 0.05, paired t-test); for example, on subj05 the sentence-level accuracy rises from 0.264 (BrainSCUBA) to 0.306 (LaVCa), and image-level accuracy from 0.250 to 0.273. The improvement at the image level is particularly important because it rules out the possibility that gains are driven solely by non-visual linguistic features such as sentence length or phrasing. A single-keyword LaVCa variant (without the Sentence Composer) performs comparably to BrainSCUBA, indicating that the multi-keyword extraction and sentence composition steps are what drive the advantage. In terms of diversity, LaVCa captions use 5.3× more unique vocabulary (16,922 vs. 3,193 words) and require 219 vs. 123 principal components to explain 90% of CLIP-Text embedding variance, confirming substantially richer inter-voxel semantic coverage. On the BOLD5000 dataset, LaVCa also generalises, achieving significant caption-prediction accuracy in ~40% of voxels with performance proportional to the encoding-model accuracy drop between datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaVCa extends the voxel-level captioning task introduced by BrainSCUBA (Luo et al., 2023) and SASC (Singh et al., 2023), which respectively used an end-to-end image captioning model and short n-gram phrases to describe voxel selectivity. The key architectural departure is a decoupled, multi-stage pipeline: ridge-regression encoding models with CLIP-Vision features identify optimal images, a multimodal LLM (MiniCPM-V) captions them, gpt-4o extracts keywords via in-context learning prompts (following Dunlap et al., 2024), and the MeaCap Sentence Composer (Zeng et al., 2024) synthesizes a final sentence grounded in the voxel's encoding weights. This modular design allows swapping any contrastive VLM backbone and any LLM without task-specific fine-tuning, in contrast to BrainSCUBA's fixed captioning model or SASC's reliance on very short phrases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most significant finding is that LaVCa reveals substantially richer representational content within ROIs traditionally characterized as selective for single categories (e.g., OFA for faces, PPA for places), with most voxels encoding multiple distinct semantic concepts—challenging long-standing assumptions about strict functional specialization in the visual cortex. Limitations include reduced accuracy in face-selective regions (where the MLLM's global captions omit local features like 'eyes' or 'nose') and the inherent constraint of language-based descriptions for capturing fine-grained visual selectivity. Open questions include extending the framework to multimodal voxels (auditory, linguistic, cognitive) and whether the observed ROI-level diversity generalizes beyond the four NSD subjects and the BOLD5000 validation set.
