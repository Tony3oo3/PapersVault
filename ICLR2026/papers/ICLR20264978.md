## Problem

Existing neural operator frameworks (FNO, DeepONet, LNO) rely on global spectral transforms that underrepresent the local spatial derivatives fundamental to PDE dynamics. This causes poor capture of non-stationary behavior, high-frequency content, and fine-scale anisotropic variations, particularly in complex nonlinear regimes. No prior operator-learning method explicitly mixes local spectral derivatives into the operator architecture while preserving physical interpretability and resolution invariance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264978/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes spatial PDE field data (initial or boundary conditions) on bounded domains in 1D, 2D, or 3D, projected into a higher-dimensional latent space via a pointwise lifting map. It outputs the predicted PDE solution field at the same spatial resolution, with the number of Riesz directional channels matching the data dimensionality (e.g., two orthogonal directions for 2D fields). The architecture is resolution-invariant: the spectral multipliers are grid-agnostic, so training and inference can use different discretizations without retraining, unlike FNO+CNN hybrids whose spatial convolutions are resolution-bound.

## Method

RNO embeds the vector Riesz transform directly into the neural operator's integral-kernel layer, replacing the standard Fourier multiplier with a composite operator that couples a global spectral path with d directional Riesz channels (where d equals the data dimensionality). Each Riesz channel applies the fixed multiplier jk_i/||k||, a scale-normalized first-order directional derivative that is energy-preserving, rotation-covariant, and mutually orthogonal across directions. A monogenic-signal-inspired direction mixer fuses the global component with the directional channels using learnable scalar weights constrained by a derived stability bound (ζ·Σω_iγ_i < π/6), yielding a structured low-rank-global plus direction-sparse-local decomposition. The full operator factorizes as CoordToRiesz → M Riesz-domain layers → RieszToCoord, preserving the canonical neural-operator scaffold while injecting anisotropic high-wavenumber detail that global-only transforms suppress.

## Results

On the five canonical PDE benchmarks (Duffing, Beam, Diffusion, Reaction-Diffusion, Brusselator), RNO achieves the lowest relative ℓ2 error across all tasks, with the largest margin on Beam (0.0219 vs. 0.0452 for LNO, a 51.6% reduction) and Brusselator (0.1317 vs. 0.1545 for ONO, 14.7% reduction). On 2-D Navier-Stokes at Re=5000, RNO attains MSE 0.9121, more than 2× lower than the next-best LNO (2.3139). On the real-world ERA5 weather dataset, RNO achieves relative ℓ2 error of 0.0022, roughly 3× better than LNO (0.0062) and 4× better than FNO (0.0093).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RNO outperforms all 11 baselines on every benchmark tested. Against the closest competitors, RNO edges out LSM on Duffing (0.1663 vs. 0.1699, 2.1% gain) and ONO on Reaction-Diffusion (0.0899 vs. 0.0989, 10.3% gain) and Brusselator (0.1317 vs. 0.1545, 14.7% gain). The most dramatic improvement is on Beam, where RNO (0.0219) more than halves the error of the previous best LNO (0.0452), a 51.6% reduction. On Navier-Stokes at Re=5000, RNO (MSE 0.9121) surpasses WNO (2.5914), LNO (2.3139), F-FNO (2.8912), FNO (2.9314), and DeepONet (6.2721) by wide margins. On ERA5, RNO (0.0022) beats LNO (0.0062), WNO (0.0085), FNO (0.0093), and DeepONet (0.0912). Against the high-frequency-specific baselines loglo-FNO and E-FNO (evaluated on Reaction-Diffusion and Brusselator), RNO still leads by 11% and 22% respectively, confirming that its gains are not merely an artifact of high-frequency enhancement.

## Relation to Prior Work

RNO extends the spectral neural operator lineage (FNO, LNO) by embedding the multidimensional Riesz transform—a Calderón-Zygmund operator that acts as a scale-normalized directional derivative—directly within the operator's spectral mixing layer, rather than appending local information as a separate CNN or convolutional module. This departs from prior local-enhancement approaches (Loglo-FNO, E-FNO, FNO+CNN) that break resolution invariance, and from DeepONet's branch-and-trunk decomposition that lacks directional sensitivity. The theoretical grounding draws on the monogenic signal framework (Unser et al., 2009) and the Taylor-expansion/optical-flow perspective to justify why first-order spectral derivatives are the natural local feature for PDE-governed fields, while the Green's function analysis (A.4) shows RNO remains within the same bounded integral-operator class as classical FNO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RNO offers a principled, energy-preserving mechanism to inject anisotropic local dynamics into neural operators without sacrificing resolution invariance or introducing redundant parameters, addressing a recognized gap in the operator-learning literature where spatial locality is either ignored or handled ad hoc. The consistent gains across 1-D to 3-D PDEs, turbulent Navier-Stokes at multiple Reynolds numbers, and the ERA5 climate dataset suggest the directional-derivative representation generalizes broadly. Open questions include whether the fixed direction count (equal to data dimensionality) remains optimal for highly anisotropic or non-Cartesian geometries, whether the approach extends naturally to irregular domains or graph-structured data, and whether the bounded scaling constraint (ζ·Σωᵢγᵢ < π/6) is tight enough for extremely high-frequency or discontinuous regimes. The noise-robustness result (7% degradation vs. 75–390% for FNO/LNO) also raises the question of whether Riesz-based operators could serve as a general denoising prior in physics-informed learning.
