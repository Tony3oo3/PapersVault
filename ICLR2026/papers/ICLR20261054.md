## Problem

Continuous-token autoregressive (AR) image generators consistently trail latent diffusion and masked-generation models at comparable parameter scales. The root cause is heterogeneous variance in diagonal-Gaussian VAE latents, which is amplified by exposure bias and classifier-free guidance (CFG) during multi-step AR decoding, leading to progressive variance drift and collapse. Unlike discrete tokens that are inherently scale-invariant on the probability simplex, no equivalent geometric constraint existed for continuous latents to prevent this scale-channel cascade.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single ImageNet-1K class label as conditioning, consumed as 16 class-conditioning tokens prepended to the causal sequence (with 10% dropout during training to enable CFG). It generates 256×256 or 512×512 RGB images by sequentially predicting 16-dimensional latent tokens in raster-scan order, each constrained to a fixed-radius hypersphere (R=√d with d=16), which are then decoded by a hybrid CNN-Transformer VAE decoder into pixel space.

## Method

SphereAR couples a hyperspherical VAE (S-VAE) with a causal Transformer and a Rectified Flow diffusion head. The S-VAE encoder parameterizes a Power Spherical posterior on the unit sphere via a unit mean direction μ and scalar concentration κ, constraining every latent token to a fixed ℓ₂ norm and eliminating the radial (scale) degree of freedom. The AR Transformer (RMSNorm, FlashAttention, SwiGLU, 2D RoPE, causal mask) feeds a token-level diffusion head trained with Rectified Flow to predict the next continuous token. At inference, all AR inputs and outputs—including those after CFG rescaling—are projected back onto the hypersphere via N_R(z)=Rz/‖z‖₂. A first-order analysis shows the Jacobian of this projection at any sphere point is exactly the tangent-space projector, so radial (scale) perturbations are annihilated before refeeding and cannot accumulate across autoregressive steps, while tangential (directional) errors are preserved.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, SphereAR-H (943M) achieves a state-of-the-art FID of 1.34, outperforming the 2B-parameter VAR-d30 (FID 1.92) and the same-size MAR-H (FID 1.55). At smaller scales, SphereAR-L (479M) reaches FID 1.54, matching MAR-H with roughly half the parameters, while SphereAR-B (208M) attains FID 1.92, matching VAR-d30 with ~10× fewer parameters. At 512×512, SphereAR-L achieves FID 1.67, surpassing MAR-L (FID 1.73) with the same parameter count but half the training epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SphereAR-H (943M, FID 1.34) sets a new state of the art for AR image generation, beating the 2B-parameter VAR-d30 (FID 1.92) by 0.58 FID and the same-size MAR-H (FID 1.55) by 0.21 FID. At the 479M scale, SphereAR-L (FID 1.54) matches MAR-H (943M, FID 1.55) with roughly half the parameters, and substantially outperforms the same-size continuous-token AR baseline LatentLM-L (FID 2.24) by 0.70 FID, as well as diffusion baselines DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27) and SiT-XL/2 (FID 2.06). At the 208M scale, SphereAR-B (FID 1.92) matches the 2B VAR-d30 (FID 1.92) with ~10× fewer parameters, surpasses VAR-d20 (600M, FID 2.57) by 0.65 FID, and outperforms the 1.67B GIVT (FID 2.59) by 0.67 FID. Among discrete-token AR models, SphereAR-B (208M) also beats LlamaGen-L (343M, FID 3.07) and RandAR-XL (775M, FID 2.22), while SphereAR-L surpasses RAR-L (461M, FID 1.70) by 0.16 FID. The paper also shows that at 512×512, SphereAR-L (FID 1.67) outperforms MAR-L (FID 1.73) with the same 481M parameters but only 400 vs. 800 training epochs, and converges to comparable performance in ~20% of MAR's wall-clock training time.

## Relation to Prior Work

SphereAR sits at the intersection of hyperspherical VAEs (Davidson et al., 2018; De Cao and Aziz, 2020) and continuous-token autoregressive image generation (MAR, Li et al., 2024a; LatentLM, Sun et al., 2024b). Prior continuous-token AR models addressed variance collapse during decoding by inflating KL weights or fixing posterior variance, but left the underlying scale heterogeneity intact. This paper departs from both those patches and from post-hoc normalization of Gaussian latents (NextStep-1, Team et al., 2025) by removing the radial degree of freedom entirely: the S-VAE constrains each token to a fixed-radius hypersphere with a Power Spherical posterior, and the AR model's inputs/outputs are projected back onto the sphere at every step, including after CFG rescaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a 208M next-token AR model matches a 2B next-scale model (VAR-d30) on FID, and that a 943M AR model achieves FID 1.34 (new SOTA for AR), suggests that the latent geometry—not the decoding paradigm—was the binding constraint for continuous-token AR. The clean theoretical argument (radial errors are annihilated by the tangent-space projector, so scale cannot cascade) gives practitioners a principled design rule: enforce constant-norm latents whenever multi-step refeeding is involved. Open questions include whether the approach scales to text-to-image and video, whether Riemannian Flow Matching would further align with hyperspherical geometry, and whether the gains persist on larger, more diverse datasets beyond ImageNet.
