## Problem

Reasoning models generate increasingly long sequences during inference, and the autoregressive nature of decoding causes per-token attention cost to grow linearly with context length, making long decoding a major efficiency bottleneck. While sparse attention has been explored for general language modeling and prefill, its application to the prolonged decoding phase of reasoning models remains underexplored, and the key challenge is effectively identifying the intrinsic attention sparsity that exists during token-by-token generation.

## Input / Output

During decoding, the method consumes the current query token's pre-RoPE Q vector and the accumulated KV cache, producing the next generated token along with a set of selected KV block indices per GQA group. The AttnGate operates at block granularity (default block size 64), compressing K via non-overlapping Max/Min/Avg pooling and projecting Q heads down to the KV head count, yielding a per-block score vector that is binarized via top-k (token budget) or threshold selection. A K Compression Cache stores the pooled-and-projected K representations so that the gate only recomputes K once per block of new tokens.

## Method

SeerAttention-R adapts SeerAttention's self-distilled AttnGate for autoregressive decoding by removing the sequence-dimension pooling on Q (since only one query token exists at a time) and introducing a linear projection that collapses each GQA query-head group into a single gate head, enabling shared sparsity decisions across the group. The K branch applies non-overlapping Max/Min/Avg pooling at block size followed by a learned linear layer, and RoPE is reapplied within the gate using block-start position indices. Training is a lightweight post-training self-distillation: a modified FlashAttention-2 kernel directly produces 1D column-wise max-pooled attention scores (further max-pooled within GQA groups) as ground truth, and the AttnGate is trained with KL divergence loss on only 0.4B tokens while all original model weights remain frozen. At inference, a K Compression Cache avoids redundant K-branch computation, and a custom TileLang block-sparse flash decoding kernel with wgmma instructions and split-K scheduling achieves up to 9x speedup over FlashAttention-3 at 90% sparsity on H100 GPUs.

## Results

SeerAttention-R achieves near-lossless reasoning accuracy with a 4k token budget on AIME24/AIME25 and 2k on MATH-500/GPQA-Diamond across Qwen3-4B/8B/14B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B, while Quest fails to reach lossless accuracy even at 8k on AIME and requires ~8k on MATH-500/GPQA. Oracle sparse selection (upper bound) achieves lossless at 2k on all tasks, confirming that attention in reasoning models is inherently sparse. The TileLang block-sparse decoding kernel achieves up to 8.6× speedup over FlashAttention-3 at 90% sparsity (batch size 16, sequence length ≥ 32k) on H100, and 1.7× over the Triton implementation. Training requires only 0.4B tokens (800 steps, ~12 GPU hours for an 8B model) on OpenR1-MATH-220K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Quest (the primary sparse-attention baseline, configured with matching block size 64 and sparse attention in all layers), SeerAttention-R consistently outperforms across every model size (4B, 8B, 14B) and all four benchmarks at every token budget tested. On AIME24/25, SeerAttention-R reaches near-lossless accuracy at 4k tokens while Quest does not achieve lossless accuracy even at 8k; on MATH-500 and GPQA-Diamond, SeerAttention-R is lossless at 2k whereas Quest needs approximately 8k. The gap narrows with model scale but persists: 14B models show the smallest residual gap for both methods, yet SeerAttention-R still leads. Against FlashAttention-3 (the dense-attention kernel baseline), the TileLang block-sparse decoding kernel delivers up to 8.6× speedup at 90% sparsity with large batch/sequence settings, approaching the theoretical I/O-bound upper bound, and maintains a 1.7× advantage over the Triton implementation of the same algorithm. The oracle sparse experiment (not a competing method but an accuracy ceiling) confirms that 2k tokens suffice for lossless reasoning, meaning SeerAttention-R's 4k budget on AIME represents a modest and expected approximation gap from the ideal.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeerAttention-R extends SeerAttention (Gao et al., 2024), which learned sparse attention for prefill via a self-distilled gating mechanism, by removing query-side sequence pooling to support token-by-token autoregressive decoding and adopting GQA-aligned shared sparsity (following SAAP and NSA). It occupies a middle ground between training-free heuristics (Quest, H2O) that estimate block importance from key statistics, and pre-training-based sparse attention (NSA, MoBA, ACP) that bake sparsity into model weights. The kernel contribution builds on FlashAttention-2/3 decoding patterns, re-implemented in TileLang and Triton with block-sparse index traversal. The paper notes a conceptual overlap with DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA), which also uses self-distillation but targets MLA with token-level rather than block-level selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a plug-in gating module trained on only 0.4B tokens delivers near-lossless reasoning accuracy at a 4K token budget with large block sizes (64/128), while the TileLang kernel achieves up to 9× speedup over FlashAttention-3 at 90% sparsity on H100—enabling KV cache offloading and heterogeneous inference. Open questions include adaptive sparsity (the accuracy-efficiency tradeoff varies by task and sequence length), unifying sparse prefill and decoding under a single gating mechanism, and system-level integration with PagedAttention in frameworks like vLLM and SGLang. The evaluation is limited to mathematical reasoning benchmarks and a single non-learned baseline (Quest), leaving transferability to code generation, long-context retrieval, and open-domain QA unvalidated.
