## Problem

Generating portrait animation videos with cross-identity attribute transfer (e.g., transferring a hairstyle or eyeglasses from one person to another) requires paired training data of the same individual with different attributes, which is rarely available at scale. Existing solutions are either limited to single attributes, rely on fixed templates or 3D scans, or require per-frame masks and category labels, making them inflexible and hard to generalize across diverse facial attributes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264205/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a portrait image, one or more attribute reference images (each with a binary segmentation mask localizing the attribute region), and a sequence of F driving facial keypoint images (extracted by Sapiens) as input. It produces an F-frame portrait animation video at 512×512 resolution where the target identity is preserved and the attribute(s) from the reference(s) are transferred. At inference, a lightweight Face Aligner module first aligns the attribute image to the portrait via FLAME parameters to mitigate spatial misalignment, and the complementary masking ensures the attribute image retains only the attribute region while the portrait image has that region removed.

## Method

Durian introduces a Dual ReferenceNet architecture with two separate encoder branches (ARNet and PRNet) that extract multi-scale spatial features from the attribute-only and attribute-masked portrait images respectively, which are then fused into a denoising U-Net via a width-wise spatial attention mechanism that replaces standard self-attention. Training uses a self-reconstruction formulation on unpaired portrait videos: two frames from the same video serve as pseudo attribute/identity pairs with complementary masking to enforce disentanglement, eliminating the need for explicit paired data. An attribute-aware mask expansion strategy (using SDXL+ControlNet to generate variant attribute shapes) and reference image augmentation (affine transforms, FLUX outpainting, color jittering) bridge the domain gap between self-reconstruction training and cross-identity inference. The spatial attention design naturally extends to multi-attribute composition (concatenating multiple attribute feature maps) and attribute interpolation (linear blending of two attribute features) at inference without additional training.

## Results

On self-attribute transfer, DURIAN achieves L1=0.0744, PSNR=18.83, SSIM=0.6527, LPIPS=0.1565, and FID=38.00, outperforming the best two-stage baseline (PbE+LivePortrait: L1=0.1059, PSNR=16.14, SSIM=0.5641, LPIPS=0.2859, FID=40.63) across all reconstruction and perceptual metrics. On cross-attribute transfer, DURIAN scores mCLIP-I=0.9043, mDINO=0.7801, ID-Sim=0.7098, and VFID_I3D=27.15, surpassing the strongest baseline (StableHair+X-Portrait: mCLIP-I=0.8895, mDINO=0.7239, ID-Sim=0.6443, VFID_I3D=28.26) on attribute fidelity, identity preservation, and I3D-based temporal quality. The only metric where a baseline edges out DURIAN is VFID_ResNeXt (2.4052 vs. 1.4672 for StableHair+MegActor-Σ), though the gap is modest.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DURIAN is compared against 12 two-stage baselines formed by pairing four image-level attribute transfer methods (PbE, HairFusion, StableHair, TriplaneEdit) with three animation methods (LivePortrait, X-Portrait, MegActor-Σ). On self-attribute transfer, DURIAN dominates every reconstruction metric: it improves PSNR by 2.3 dB over the best baseline (TriplaneEdit+LivePortrait, 16.52) and reduces LPIPS by nearly half (0.1565 vs. 0.2760). On cross-attribute transfer, DURIAN leads on attribute fidelity (mCLIP-I 0.9043 vs. 0.8895 for StableHair+X-Portrait), identity preservation (ID-Sim 0.7098 vs. 0.6564 for StableHair+LivePortrait), and I3D-based temporal quality (VFID_I3D 27.15 vs. 28.26). The closest competitor overall is StableHair+X-Portrait, which slightly outperforms DURIAN on VFID_ResNeXt (1.57 vs. 2.41), suggesting marginally better ResNeXt-feature temporal smoothness, though DURIAN still wins on all other cross-identity metrics. TriplaneEdit-based baselines perform worst on identity preservation (ID-Sim ≈ 0.28), confirming that 3D-aware GAN editing struggles to maintain identity under cross-identity transfer. In a user study with 100 participants, DURIAN received 76.5% preference versus 19.0% for PbE+LivePortrait and 4.5% for TriplaneEdit+LivePortrait.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the diffusion-based portrait animation line (Animate Anyone, CHAMP) for its U-Net backbone, temporal attention, and two-stage training, and on StableAnimator for its ArcFace+CLIP identity embedding. It departs from prior attribute transfer work (HairFusion, StableHair, Paint-by-Example, TriplaneEdit), which operates on static images and requires paired data or explicit masks, by formulating a self-reconstruction training objective that uses two frames from the same video as pseudo attribute-identity pairs. The Dual ReferenceNet with complementary masking and spatial attention is a novel architectural departure from single-reference designs in prior animation methods, enabling the model to disentangle and recombine identity and attribute streams in a single forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for expensive paired attribute data through self-reconstruction on in-the-wild videos, this work makes cross-identity facial attribute transfer practical at scale, with direct applications to virtual styling (hairstyles, glasses, hats, beards). The emergent zero-shot multi-attribute composition and interpolation capabilities from the spatial attention mechanism open a path toward unified, composable appearance editing without per-attribute training. Limitations include sensitivity to segmentation quality (Sapiens), degradation under severe lighting mismatches, and a residual domain gap between self-reconstruction training and true cross-identity inference that is only partially bridged by mask expansion and augmentation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-facial attributes, longer video sequences, and whether the self-reconstruction formulation can be extended to other disentangled generation tasks.
